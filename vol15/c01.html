Chapter 1: Baramon — Breaking the Gate [568]

第1章　破羅門〔568〕

§ 1

A tale of the far-off Age of the Gods, mighty and swift: To scatter the darkness of the Everlasting Night, The Hamitic tribe, the seed of Noah, Mightiest among them the Baramon, Bearing the divine word from the Land of Tokoyo, Welcomed Ōkuniwake, youngest son of Ōkunihiko, And raised him up as King of the Gods; In Iho, the capital of Egypt, They set the pillars of a shrine Stout and firm, and there proclaimed their creed. Faint though its Kototama was, It crossed the narrow seas of this world, Unto Greece, Italy, and France, And pressing onward at the last to Asia Minor, To Ken'onkyō in Mesopotamia, Where it built and made fast its stronghold, And little by little spread its way, Then crossing Persia from side to side, Advanced toward the land of India, Fording the River Eden.

All the clan of Ham together, With Ken'onkyō as their center, Opened the Baramon Teaching. The god-men of Mount Caucasus, Renowned as the lineage of Shem, Resolving to pacify the Baramon Teaching through the Word And to open the way of truth, Sent Futodama-no-Mikoto, The missionary of Hiromichiwake, To march upon Ken'onkyō. This wondrous tale of the Age of the Gods, At the threshold of the fifteenth volume, Here I begin to tell, and delightful it is.

This Mesopotamia is called by another name the Land of Ripened Ears (Hohokuni), and is the most fertile and peaceful dwelling-region upon the face of the earth. The sheep thrive well, cattle and horses multiply, and the five grains and the fruits yield an unrivalled harvest that changeth not from year to year: it is a heavenly paradise upon the earth. It is said that even when the world was wrapped in dark clouds and the light of sun and moon was dim, this land alone could bring all things to fair growth. To the west the River Eden flows long, to the east the River Izu flows southward, and the two join at the southern end of the land and enter the Sea of Fusa. The Serpent of Eight Heads and Eight Tails and the evil spirits of the wicked fox, having been robbed of their capital upon Mount Caucasus, and Mount Ural and Armenia being in consequence brought into peril, Uralhiko and Ural-hime fled far away to the Land of Tokoyo. Thereupon they caused Ōkuniwake and Shikokuni-hime, husband and wife, descendants of Ōkunihiko the Daijizaiten, to appear in Iho, the capital of Egypt, and to found the Baramon Teaching, a second Ural Teaching. Revering Ōkuniwake under the title of Daijizaiten, they there established a doctrine holding that extreme austerities and mortifications accord with the heart of God, and advancing into Hohokuni of Mesopotamia, they made the Garden of Eden and Ken'onkyō their stronghold. Because of this the Anai Teaching upon the Golden Mountain in the old capital of the holy land of Jerusalem was swiftly eaten away, and the doctrine of Haniyasuhiko and Haniyasu-hime fell into the sorrowful plight of being all but destroyed.

Now Susanoo-no-Kami, abiding upon Mount Caucasus, purposed to cause Hinode-no-Kami and Hinodewake-no-Kami to bring into submission the evil gods of the Baramon Teaching founded by the Hamitic tribe. Futodama-no-Mikoto, missionary of Hiromichiwake—that being another name of Mitsubahiko-no-Mikoto, who had appeared from Mount Ryōjusen—left Matsuyo-hime upon Mount Caucasus and, pressing on night and day, appeared upon the upper reaches of the River Eden, purposing to recover the Garden of Eden as a stronghold and to hold back the inroads of the Hamitic tribe. Futodama-no-Mikoto, together with the three, Yasuhiko, Kunihiko, and Michihiko, built a palace in the Garden of Eden and stood ready against the Hamitic invasion. Yet Ken'onkyō down the river fell at last wholly into the possession of the Baramon Teaching. Thereupon Futodama-no-Mikoto left his daughter Terutae-hime in the Garden of Eden, and leading Yasuhiko, Kunihiko, and Michihiko, set out to proclaim the Way in Ken'onkyō. This Yasuhiko was Yajihiko under a new name, Kunihiko was Yotahiko renamed, and Michihiko was Katsuhiko renamed.

§ 2

The Baramon Teaching was that of Ōkuniwake, son of that Ōkunihiko who had once falsely styled himself Hinode-no-Kami; Ōkuniwake called himself Daijizaiten, held that austerities and mortifications accord with the heart of God, misunderstood the true meaning of Spirit-over-matter, made light of the flesh, and esteemed the soul to the uttermost extreme. Those who believed this teaching would leap stark naked into thickets of thorns, or walk through fire, or dive beneath the waters, or strip themselves naked in the depth of winter, or go to and fro barefoot upon rugged mountain paths; and as the first gate of discipline, they drove nails all over the upper face of high wooden clogs and made men put them on and walk. Therefore the believers of this teaching were covered all over with bleeding sores, like Daruma figures of blood too dreadful to behold, and such was a teaching that boasted of the pains of its discipline. Since the spirits of the Eight-Headed Eight-Tailed Serpent, the Golden-Haired Nine-Tailed Fox, and the evil demon delight in seeing human blood, they made it their sole device, under the fair name of Spirit-over-matter, to possess the souls of men and habitually to work such cruel deeds through them; and so the believers bewitched by this teaching made light of life and much of death, and took as their only joy the hope of being saved into a heaven where they would receive boundless, absolute, beginningless and endless delight. However much one may honor the spirit and slight the body, to forget the heavenly law of the oneness of spirit and flesh, and to despise the flesh—the living shrine of God—as dust, as a goose-feather, is the most grievous violation of the great Way of God, which is generation, transformation, and nurture. Were this teaching to spread throughout the world, all living things upon the earth would of necessity be destroyed by the evil gods. Moreover, the Baramon Teaching set up strict distinctions of souls into three ranks, upper, middle, and lower: those born from the head of Ōkunihiko, the great ancestor of Daijizaiten, however foolish and benighted, would stand above the common people and take the seat of rulers; those born from the belly of the god would stand in the center of all the people above and below, receiving the rank of sub-rulers, and toiling not at all; and it was a doctrine whereby they would wring the sweat and blood of the multitude of the people, said to have been born from the feet of the god, and so live in idle ease. The multitude of the people were forced into all manner of austerities and mortifications; their bodies wasted away or perished; cries of resentment secretly filled the land; and though dwelling in a very heaven and pure land, the multitude of the people could not but continue in a life like hell. The power of the evil gods grew ever more violent, until at length they crossed the land of Fusa and were extending their sphere of power even to the land of India.

Futodama-no-Mikoto, accompanied by Yasuhiko, Kunihiko, and Michihiko, arrived at the ferry that flows to the southeast of Ken'onkyō. Here three demon gods, Tobihiko, Takahiko, and Mozuhiko, with twisted headbands bound about their brows, had set up a barrier upon the riverbank to prevent the entry of men from other lands, and were guarding it closely.

Futodama-no-Mikoto: "Ho, my three companions! When of old I crossed this river, it was a stream of a purity beyond all telling; but since the evil gods of Mount Ural and Armenia, having once fled away to the Land of Tokoyo, have stealthily appeared again in Ken'onkyō and begun to open the false teaching of Baramon, the very wind that blows hither reeks of blood, the mountains, rivers, grasses, and trees have changed their hue, and the flowing river too has become like bloody mire. Since we have come bearing the weighty mission, in obedience to the divine will of Susanoo-no-Mikoto, of restoring the plain of Mesopotamia once more to the paradise of Hohokuni, we cannot retreat a single step, whatsoever demon gods may come upon us. Do ye also face this with that resolve. That vast mansion standing upon yonder riverbank must surely be the barrier of the demon gods. Let one of you three go a step ahead to scout, and open parley with the evil gods of the barrier; and should matters grow urgent, blow the signal flute. Until then we will hide ourselves in this forest and watch how things fall out."

At these words of Futodama-no-Mikoto, Michihiko, stirred with courage, said:

"With all humility, I beg that this task be laid upon Michihiko."

When he had so entreated, Futodama-no-Mikoto said:

"I thank thee for thy pains; go a step ahead and scout for us."

"I have understood."

So saying, Michihiko went forward at his ease toward the barrier upon the riverbank, singing a missionary song. Coming full upon the great gate of the barrier, Michihiko cried in a mighty voice:

"Ho! This Ken'onkyō is a holy land that of old was ruled by Hinode-no-Kami under the title of King of the Southern Heavens, and thereafter was guarded by Onitakehiko and other gods who became Kings of the Southern Heavens, receiving forever the command of the Great God. What evil god then art thou, that hast seized Ken'onkyō and moreover built a barrier upon this riverbank? Open this gate without a moment's delay! I am Michihiko, a missionary of the Anai Teaching!"

And he beat upon the gate as though to break it down. At that moment three men appeared out of the thick trees outside the gate, brandishing keen hand-spears, surrounded Michihiko from three sides, and with glaring eyes and bodies trembling and quivering, said:

"Ho! Art thou a missionary of the Anai Teaching? A summer moth flying into the fire! Taste the point of my spear!"

And all three thrust at him at once; but Michihiko, now to the right, now to the left, before and behind, to this side and that, avoided the spear-points, and struck down one man's spear with a clatter.
