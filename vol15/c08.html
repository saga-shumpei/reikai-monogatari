Chapter 8: The Uranai Cult [575]

ウラナイ教〔575〕

§ 1

The five, beginning with the Sendenshi Yasuhiko, Kunihiko, and Michihiko, together with Takahiko and Mozuhiko, stood before the gate of this spacious mansion, listening intently to hear the conditions within.

Suddenly gazing upon the front gate, they saw upon a nameplate, weathered by wind and rain, faintly written in Jindaimoji, the words 'Headquarters of the Uranai Cult.' Yasuhiko uncertainly read the half-peeled letters and spake:

'Ah, this appears to be the main temple of an anomalous Shinkyō that hath merged the Ural Cult and Ananai-kyō! Yet, even so, the voice of the woman from just a moment ago somehow soundeth familiar. How strange! Oh, Mozuhiko, Takahiko! Do ye quietly climb over this wall, search out the conditions within, and report unto us.'

Mozuhiko and Takahiko nodded with joy; swift as monkeys traveling through trees or sasagani spiders, they nimbly leaped over the wall, concealed their forms in the dense foliage of the garden trees, and began to spy upon the scene.

Appearing to be the leader of the Uranai Cult, a single plump, fat old woman remained in the center, rolling her eyes which resembled ice frozen upon a bucket of dirty water. Seven or eight men and women who seemed to be Sendenshi were all like blind persons suffering from cataracts or amaurosis; though their eyes sparkled brightly on the surface, they appeared to see nothing, groping about to heap large quantities of barley rice and grated yam soup into giant bowls, slurping it down continuously. Two bald old men with kettle-like heads were frantically grinding mountain yams in a mortar. These two also seemed to be blind, working while groping about. Seeing this spectacle, one spake:

'Hey, Mozu! Every single person here seems to be blind. Are they not slurping down barley rice and grated yam soup from those huge bowls? Seeing this, the hangar of my stomach hath suddenly begun to complain of emptiness. What thinkest thou? Taking advantage of their blindness, shall we not quietly go and partake of a bowl?'

Mozuhiko spake:

'That should be most entertaining.'

Saying this, he stealthily crept forward without a sound, appeared before the company, and stood waiting with an innocent countenance. The bald old man filled a bowl with barley rice and grated yam soup, and thrusting it forward, spake:

'Come, come, a second serving is ready, Takahime-san!'

An otafuku old woman of middle age named Takahime extended both her hands forward like a mechanical doll. Right at that moment, Mozuhiko, taking the bowl thrust before his face, altered his voice and spake:

'Yes, thank you for the feast! Please give me another bowl.'

§ 2

The old man handed the bowl to Mozuhiko, and muttering in a low voice, 'It is Takahime-san who eats so well,' he went back toward the kitchen, feeling his way, and devoted himself with all his might to grating yam.

Takahime said, 'Hey, Matsusuke, where did you put it? Will you not hand it over here quickly?'

Because Matsusuke was hard of hearing and blind, without any mercy he was grating yam with all his might while sniffling his nose. Over there and over here, sounds like sniffling running snot were heard continuously.

Mozuhiko and Takahiko, remaining famished as if they would bite into the bowl from both sides, swallowed it down with huge mouths opened wide, slurping with extraordinary suction like a toad pulling in a weasel. At this time, Matsusuke again felt his way, bearing a large bowl of barley rice topped with grated yam sauce, with uncertain footsteps, in a manner like walking through river water, and said, 'Now, now, Takahime-san, your second helping is ready.'

Takahiko again feigned a voice and said, 'Ah, Matsusuke, thank you for your trouble. I ask for another bowl for a second helping.'

'Yes, yes, there are only the ends of the yams left, but if that is acceptable, please eat,' he said, puffing out his cheeks, and returned to the room.

Takahime said, 'Hey, hey, Matsusuke, have you not brought it yet? Where did you put it?'

Takahiko and Mozuhiko instantly finished off the bowl. Beside them, more than ten blind persons set their bowls before them, taking one mouthful and setting it down, enjoying themselves.

Mozuhiko gently placed the bowl of A before B, placed the bowl of B before C, and gently set the bowl of C before Takahime.

A said, 'I thought there was more than half left, but when did it diminish like this? Hey, you finished mine along with yours, did you not?'

B said, 'Do not talk nonsense, you have sent my bowl somewhere. To finish one's own portion and still take and eat another's, is that not too much?' Thus a quarrel between the blind persons began.

The more than ten blind persons, thinking it a grave matter if their bowls were taken, gripped their bowls tightly, not even putting them down, slurping and sucking them.

Takahiko grabbed ash from the brazier and went around gently distributing it, a pinch at a time, into the bowls of the blind persons.

'Oh, what is this bowl's... the taste of the yam sauce seems to have suddenly changed. Someone, thinking others are blind, is making a fool of us; someone has put ash in it!'

Mozuhiko said, 'Yes, yes, just so, just so.'

§ 3

Takahime spoke: 'Hark, it seemeth a fellow with a strange voice hath arrived. Hearken, all of you, take heed! I have felt a suspicion since a short while ago. While I was pretending to be a blind person, some fellows from who knoweth where—two hirottoko rogues—committed an uncalled-for prank upon me. Now, my patience is exhausted! The old man of our house hath a severe lung disease, and right here is a whole bowl full of phlegm that closely resembleth tororojiru. Eat this and begone at once!'

Mozu and Taka, while scratching their heads, said:

'Ah, we beg thy pardon for that!'

Takahime cried: 'Pardon be damned! Hey, Hey, Chiyōsuke, Hansuke! Bind these two men at once!'

'We obey!'

So saying, from the adjoining room appeared large men, who brandished kitchen knives called debabōchiyō and closed in upon the two. Takahime likewise raised her eyebrows in anger, held a debabōchiyō in a reverse grip, and set upon the two from three sides to strike them down. Mozuhiko and Takahiko put donburibachi bowls over their heads and fled toward the front entrance, pattering away. Within the donburibachi that Mozuhiko wore was a whole bowlful of the old man's vomited phlegm. Covered in phlegm from his head downward, he dashed straight toward the front. The two rough men, striding with long paces, pursued the two from behind, but slipping smoothly upon the spilled phlegm, they fell flat upon their backs with a loud crash.

Takahime, brandishing her deba as she dashed out to the front, stumbled over the two rough men; at the instant she fell with a thud, she plunged her deba into Chiyōsuke's belly, whereupon Chiyōsuke gave a single groan and writhed in agony, rolling over seven times and turning eight. Instantly, a great commotion erupted inside the mansion.

Takahiko and Mozuhiko fled with all their might and jumped with a splash into a roadside pond, seeking to wash off the phlegm. Within this water hole, numerous fish were kept. In order to prevent the attacks of weasels and otters, branches of yuzu trees, covered with thorns, had been thrown all over its surface. Unaware of this, the two stripped stark naked and plunged in, only to be pricked by the thorns of the yuzu trees until their entire bodies were full of holes; having barely crawled out, they began to weep bitterly.

The old woman, her eyebrows bristling and two horns about an inch long appearing from between her hair, manifested herself at this spot. Seeing the plight of the two, she laughed merrily, but as she staggered, she plunged back into the pond with a great splash.

'Ouch, ouch! Ouch, ouch!'

Such was the comical sight of the old woman agonizing in pain; the two, remaining stark naked, cried out:

'Serves thee right!'

§ 4

So saying, with their legs shaking and tottering, they ran away along the Tanbomichi. When the three men, Yasuhiko, Kunihiko, and Michihiko, pretending to know nothing, were about to pass by the side of this pond while singing the Sendenka, Takahime joined her palms together from within the pond and eagerly called for help. The three Sendenshi could not bear the pity of the sight, and at long last rescued Takahime. Takahime rejoiced exceedingly, and expressed her gratitude unto the three men for the great grace of Kiumei.

At this time, the two men Mozu and Taka, who had fled away, appeared in this place all trembling while remaining Matsupadaka, and spoke:

"Pray, pray, Sir Sendenshi, we cannot endure the cold. Please obtain suitable Kimono from the old woman of the Uranai Kyō. Come now, old woman, thou too hast had thy Inochibiro thanks to the Sendenshi, wherefore it must be a cheap thing for thee to offer at least some Kimono."

Yasuhiko said, "Nay, Wareware have no Hitsueu of such things as Kimono. We humbly decline."

Kuni and Michi said, "Wareware likewise have no Hitsueu of such as Ifuku."

Mozuhiko said, "Eh, what dull Sendenshi ye are! Doth it not catch your eyes that Koko reside two Okata who stand in need of Kimono?"

Michihiko said, "We seem to have become Shinjiya of the Uranai Kyō, and have turned entirely into blind men. Ahahaha!"

Takahime said, "Ye are insolent rogues who crept stealthily into my parlor, secretly embezzled and ate two or three bowls of yam gruel, and moreover, taking advantage of the large crowd of blind men, grabbed ash and threw it into the yam gruel. I am in no position to give ye Kimono, yet as a token of Kanshiya for having my life saved, shall I give ye the Kimono of the deceased Chō-kō and Ban-kō?"

Michihiko said, "Well, well, did the two of ye steal and eat the yam gruel?"

Mozuhiko said, "Yes, we did the Tororou. In return for that, we met with a terrible ordeal; we were covered on our heads with filthy things. We deceived the blind men and ate a large quantity of yam gruel, and then, being pursued by Chō-kō and Ban-kō, we fled with all our might, tantantan. At the doorway, Chō-kō and Ban-kō tumbled down; thereupon the old woman came flying and fell down, and in the momentum of her fall, she struck the fat belly of Chō-kō. The two men, striving with all their might to cleanse their bodies of the phlegm, forthwith leaped into the reservoir pond; it was painful being pricked by the thorns of the citron. Tantanto, we desire to receive splendid Kimono, do we not, Taka-tan?"

Michihiko burst out laughing, and said:

"Ahahaha! What foul souls ye are. It would be convenient if ye henceforth repent and are admitted into the company of blind men of the Uranai Kyō. Pray, old woman, these two men can by no means comprehend the doctrine of the Anai Kyō, for it is lofty and far-reaching; as they are semi-muddy humans who understand neither good, nor evil, nor foolishness, if thou shouldst employ them even as Sendenshi of the Uranai Kyō, they would be most Tekitau."

The old woman said, "Oh, those are truly grateful words. They are figures most Tekitau for Sendenshi of the Uranai Kyō; for how much wilt thou sell them to me?"

Michihiko said, "Well, as they are mere leftover, unfinished items, I shall discount them to be free of charge. If thou feedest them rice or barley, it will ruin their stomachs, so pray keep and feed them with loaches and frogs according to their souls, ahahaha!"

The old woman said, "Ohohoho!"
