Chapter 22: Peace and War [589]

和と戦〔589〕

§ 1

Kotoyoriwakenomikoto, having explored the Shinkai through Fushigi, returned once more to his Shiyōki, and together with Tamahiko, Izuhiko, and Kusuhiko, spurred their steeds and proceeded steadily toward the Miaraka of Kamususanōnōokami, traversing the Ubusuna Sanmiyaku.

The Miaraka upon the Sanjiyō were all constructed with Marukibashira. The Yōzai consisted of cypress, cedar, pine, fir, and Iroiro of other woods, and there stood many a building with roofs of Warabuki, neither too wide nor too narrow, possessing an indescribable Fūriu.

The party of four arrived before the gate, leaped nimbly from their horses, and with a Daionjiyō cried out,

"We seek audience, we seek audience."

Upon this call,

"Aye,"

responded three or four large men, who flung the gates open to the left and right, and upon beholding the figures of the four, exclaimed,

"Oh, this is indeed a welcome arrival. We have been awaiting you, having heard that you four, as noble envoys, would appear at this residence following an urgent Kifuhau from Takaamahara. Pray, enter, and we shall guide you. I am one who serves as the chief of the deities of Yasotakeru, and my name is Kunitakehiko."

So saying, he stood at the fore and guided them deeper and deeper within, his footsteps causing the earth to resound with a heavy thud.

Standing at the entrance of the mansion, which appeared to be the main residence, Kunitakehiko spoke,

"Ah, Lord Yashimanushi, the party of Kotoyoriwakenomikoto has arrived."

As he finished his words, the sliding doors of the entrance were opened with a soft rustle.

Kunitakehiko guided them, saying, "Come, come, this is the main hall of the Mikoto; pray, enter without reserve."

"Then, with your permission,"

the group replied, and proceeded further and further into the interior. Two beauties of exquisite countenance appeared in that place, and upon closer inspection, they were Princess Aiko and Princess Ikuyo.

Kotoyoriwake said, "Ah, are you not the noble daughters of the deity who resides in Aon-gō, Princess Aiko and Princess Ikuyo?"

"Yes, that is indeed so; how glad we are that you have come."

"I am, as you say, Princess Ikuyo; pray, rest yourselves at your leisure. My father, for the sake of all living beings under heaven, departed from this mansion some ten days ago, stating that he was bound for the land of Tokoyo. It is a pity that you have come all this way, but my father is unfortunately absent; however, as my brother Yashimanushi is remaining in charge as our father's deputy, I pray you will speak with him at your ease."

Kotoyoriwake replied, "Ah, is that so? That is a regrettable circumstance. Nay, I had the honor of beholding your father but a short while ago in the Takaamahara of the earth."

§ 2

Aikohime and Ikuyohime spoke at once, 'Ah, did you meet our father? In what region did this occur?'

Kotoyoriwake replied, 'Yes, I beheld his glorious countenance in the Takaamahara of the earth, thirty-five myriads of years in the future.'

'Ah, is that so? That is indeed a most fortunate occurrence. What did our father say?'

Kotoyoriwake answered, 'Alas, he commanded us that since we have yet divine duties to fulfill in the Genkai, we must return to the past of thirty-five myriads of years ago and complete our divine Shingefu. Truly, we are beings of deep sin, and it is not easy for us to ascend to Takaamahara.'

Tamahiko, Izuhiko, and Kusuhiko spoke together, 'Oh, are you the daughters of the Kamisama? We are but humble servants born in the land of Tokoyo, attending upon Mikotosama Kotoyoriwake. We pray that you will not forsake us, and we humbly request your kind guidance henceforth.'

They offered their greetings with profound courtesy.

'Your greetings are more than I deserve; I am but a woman of little worth, and I pray that you will not forsake me, but remain in kind fellowship with me for all time,' she said, bowing her head.

At this moment, a man with clear eyes, fine brows, a straight nose, and a firm mouth, his face of a peach hue, entered slowly while stroking the mustache above his lip and the beard hanging from his chin; he sat formally before the party and bowed with great respect.

'I am Yashimanushi. You are the renowned Mikotosama Kotoyoriwake; how well you have traveled from afar to reach us. How my father would have rejoiced had he been here,' he said, blinking his eyes and wiping away tears.

The entire company, moved by the demeanor of Yashimanushi, felt a sense of sorrow and wrung their sleeves wet with tears.

At this time, Kikukohime, accompanied by two handmaidens, greeted them, 'Everyone, the meal is prepared; pray, come this way.'

Yashimanushi, the host, and the four others followed Kikukohime into a separate inner chamber.

At the entrance of the inner chamber, Kamehiko, Umehiko, Aikohime, and Ikuyohime sat formally, bowing their heads to welcome the party.

A banquet was held, and the company, their spirits lifted by the fruit wine, sang songs in low voices by turns, while Kikukohime performed a dance with graceful, long sleeves to add to the merriment.

The sun gradually sank into the west, and the voices of various birds announcing the twilight sounded with a sense of loneliness.

Just at that moment, the deity Yasotakeru appeared in haste upon the scene.

I report to you, Yashimanushi Mikotosama, that at this moment, the great Daitōriyō of the Brahmins, Onikumohiko, leading a multitude of Magun with Onitsukami at their head, has surrounded this Tauyakata ten and twenty-fold, raining arrows upon us; furthermore, appearing as a Ketsushitai, hundreds of Aramushiyaotoko have surged forward with a roar, brandishing Chiyōken and Chiyōsau. The Maushiyō of this Tauyakata, Kunitakehiko, has gathered all the Mikata within the walls and is exerting his utmost to Bausen, yet the enemy's strength increases moment by moment, while our Mikata number but Nijifuiuyonin; the enemy's Taigun, relying on their numbers, raise their war cries, and the first, second, and third halls have already fallen into their Senriyō. Kunitakehiko, drawing his Chiyōken, stands against the swarming enemy, and though he fights to defend, slashing and mowing them down in every direction, the enemy is a Taigun beyond measure, and the outcome is manifest; O Master, to remain here is a grave matter for your person, so I pray you, flee at once through the back gate, follow the mountain ridge to the lake of Biwa, and take refuge in the Caucasus Mountains, for the enemy draws near; come, make haste with your preparations.

Upon this report, Yashimanushi, without the slightest agitation, replied: 'Ho, you, spirit of Yasotakeru, manage this as you see fit, for I must attend to my guest from afar; it would be well for you to prepare for Bausen alongside Kunitakehiko.'

'These are indeed the words of my Master, but in this crisis, how can you speak with such nonchalance? The third hall has already been taken by the enemy, and Kunitakehiko is in the midst of a bitter struggle, having suffered several spear wounds; it appears that the greater part of our Mikata have been slain; I beseech you, flee this place with your guest at once.'

'Atsuhahaha, a most interesting turn of events; the wind spirit, watching for the absence of my father and taking advantage of our weakness, is but the limited Onikumohiko and his forces; even if a million or ten million horsemen were to attack at once, with the strength of a single fingertip, Yashimanushi shall drive them to distraction and give them a taste of their own folly; go you to the front and exert your strength to the utmost; ya, ya, everyone, let us make the sight of the enemy's assault the accompaniment to our wine and drink at our leisure; it is a diversion for the moment, a mere amusement; pray, console your hearts by watching the enemy's raid.'

Kotoyoriwake Mikoto said: 'Atsuhahaha, ya, how interesting this has become; if they would but draw a little closer, it would be more convenient for viewing, but as this is surely the eighth hall and four more still stand between us, we are for now in a safe zone; yet, where there is profit, there is harm, and without encountering danger, one cannot see such interesting things, ahahaha.'

§ 4

Kamehiko and Umehiko squared their shoulders and braced their arms, their countenances fearsome and their breath coming in gasps.

"Oh, oh, Yashimanushi-sama, Kotoyoriwake-sama, have you both been seized by madness? At such a juncture as this, how can you be so composed? This is no time for sake. Come, make preparations for defense. We shall stake our lives in fierce battle, cutting down and scattering the attacking wretches one by one, and teach them a lesson they shall not soon forget."

No sooner had they spoken than Umehiko seized a naginata from the nageshi, intending to rush to the front. Kamehiko drew his chiyōken, likewise intending to depart.

Aikohime nimbly cast a rope about the feet of the two and pulled from behind.

Caught in the momentum of their forward rush, their strength concentrated in their jiyōhanshin while their shimohanshin became as empty as a cicada's shell, they were tripped and fell headlong into the center of the zashiki.

Kamehiko cried, "In this situation where one man must face a thousand, what is this mischief? If you delay, it shall be a grave matter for you. Come, Princesses, flee through the back gate as quickly as possible. Kikuko-hime-dono, Ikuyo-hime-dono, come, quickly, quickly! I shall rush to the front from here and fight with all the strength my slender arms possess."

So saying, he rose once more, and with great vigor, sought to go to the front.

Yashimanushi, with an air of iuiu, replied, "Ahahaha, everyone, there is no need to look at the enemy's clamor, for the farce of our own side is more than enough. Yaa, yaa, Kamehiko, Umehiko, first partake of a cup."

He extended a sake cup toward them. Umehiko, shaking his head, said, "Aee, once again, our carefree master. Is this really the time for such things? Come, quickly, whether to flee or to advance, as this is a moment where not a hair's breadth of time can be spared, you must surely have some resolve."

Having said this, the two pointed toward the front and ran off with the speed of Idaten. Already, the enemy had occupied the fifth mansion and was about to advance toward the sixth.

Yatōmitsu-no-kami came running, his face changed in color and blood streaming down his countenance, and said, "I report: the enemy has already closed in upon the sixth mansion. The number of victory and defeat is already decided; I pray you, flee as quickly as possible. We are prepared to fight until our lives end."

Yashimanushi, remaining calm, said, "Yaa, Yatōmitsu, you have labored well. First, it would be best to drink some sake and relax before you work."

Yatōmitsu, gasping for breath, replied, "So, so, so, what are you saying? Is this a time for sake? The rise and fall of the nation is at this very moment; sake will not even pass through my throat."

§ 5

Kotoyoriwake said, 'Ah, ah, ah, Yashimanushi no Mikotosama, your retainers are indeed composed of valiant generals and fierce soldiers; truly, there are no weak soldiers under a valiant general, and I am filled with admiration.'

'Nay, having been told so, I have no words to return; I am truly ashamed to have shown you the unsightly spectacle of their confusion and dismay. As we are those who adopt the principle of non-resistance, leaving ourselves to the enemy's attack, the outcome of victory or defeat is manifest from the beginning; thus, no matter how much one may panic, the result remains the same, so let us first enjoy the fleeting moment. One cannot know what shall befall even an instant hence, ah, ah, ah.'

He was again drinking sake, gulping it down. Tamahiko, Izuhiko, and Kusunohiko, who were usually prone to agitation, appeared somehow composed in spirit since gaining the experience of their journey through the Divine Realm; as if they perceived this disturbance not at all, they took up their chopsticks with composure, smacked their lips at the parting sake, and hummed to themselves in secret. Princess Aiko brought out a single-stringed koto and began to sing in a graceful voice.

'Princess Kikuko, Princess Ikuyo, pray, will you dance? Since it is inexcusable to show our guests from afar such a dreary scene, please show them something splendid, and I shall sing.'

Princess Kikuko and Princess Ikuyo answered, 'Aye,' and began to prepare, then commenced to dance with grace. Outside, the battle of the Asura realm raged. In the inner chamber, it was as quiet as a spring flower-viewing or an autumn moon-viewing, and the sound of laughter leaked out beyond the walls.

Onigumohiko appeared upon the scene like the King of Asura, brandishing a spear stained with blood, and cried out, 'Ha! Since it has come to this, you can no longer prevail. Come, will you commit seppuku honorably, or shall I make you the rust of my spear? Come, come, what is your answer?'

Following Onigumohiko, Onitsukami appeared once more upon the scene and shouted, 'Since Yasotakeru and Kunitakehiko, who were famed as such heroes, have fallen easily at my hands, your hundredth year has come, and you can no longer prevail. Come, will you commit seppuku honorably, or shall I lay my hands upon you? Come, give your answer.'
