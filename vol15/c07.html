Chapter 7: The Relentless Assault [574]

釣瓶攻〔574〕

§ 1

Mozukō and Takakō, dripping profusely with sweat, advanced along the narrow Tanbomichi like a marathon race, proceeding with Daisokuriyoku like a reckless procession of frogs.

Having gone Jifusūchiyō, suddenly before them stood the figure of a Sekizau; Mozukō lost himself in fascination and stood staring at this Sekizau. Takahiko, having caught up from behind, instantly clenched his fist and struck him repeatedly, poka-poka-poka. The Ishijizau slowly stuck out a tongue over Itsushiyaku long, peeled its eyes Shirokuro, thrust its head forward by about Itsushiyaku, and twitched its nose. Takahiko likewise lost himself in fascination, staring intently at the Iyō Sekizau. Mozuhiko again clenched his fist and struck Takahiko upon the Yokozura, poka-poka.

Takahiko said, 'Aitata! The Shiyakukin ought to have been settled with this already, yet you have struck me twice more; what a helpless fellow you are. Wait, wait, I shall presently give you your Henpau!'

So saying, he bound his head with a Nejihachimaki, clenched his fists, and set upon him; Mozuhiko lightly dodged his body, and said,

'Yā Takahiko, I no longer need the Henkin to be made. I shall even remit the Risoku for you,' and ran about escaping.

Takahiko pursued him, saying, 'What! Can a man stand tall if he borrows Shiyakukin from you and does not return it? I shall attach a plentiful Risoku and pay you back!'

Mozukō fled round and round the Sekizau; Takahiko pursued him round and round. Having circled around the Sekizau almost several tens of times, both of them finally became dizzy, and the mountains and fields all at once began to rotate like a motor.

The two clung to the earth and shouted,

'Ah, an earthquake, an earthquake, a catastrophe!'

Four or five rough men who appeared at this place swiftly bound the two with their hands behind their backs, hooked ropes over their shoulders, and ran don-don-don-don along the grass-overgrown path into the forest.

While being dragged along, the two said, 'Ah, a catastrophe, a terrestrial portent! Heaven becomes earth, and earth becomes heaven,' and without noticing that they were bound, shouting all the while, they were dragged beneath a waterfall of several hundred jō.

The four or five rough men instantly drew waterfall water, laid the two upon their backs, and poured it over them like a waterfall without distinction of eyes, nose, or mouth.

From the distress, the dizziness of the two ceased, and as they burst into tears, crying, 'Yā, help us, help us!' the large men raised their voices, faced both of them, and said,

'Ye must be both Mozuhiko and Takahiko, who acted as barrier-keepers of the River of Eden. On this side is Tobihiko, retainer of Onikumohiko; look well upon my face!'

§ 2

Saying thus, he protruded his swarthy face, and rolled his eyes.

Mozuhiko: 'Ho! Kisama art Tobihiko! When on earth didst thou come to such a place? Wilt thou not untie my bonds? That rogue of an Ishijizau, he is insolent in the extreme; having bound both of us with our hands behind our backs, he hath blown us away to a place like this. For the sake of our friendship, do not hesitate, but quickly unloose our ropes!'

Tobihiko: 'What nonsense dost thou chatter? Art thou not the one who turned traitor to Ananai-kyō, and was finally turned into the seaweed-dust of the River of Eden by reason of divine punishment? As the saying goeth that a hated child struts about the world, hast thou come crawling back alive into this mortal Shiyaba, seeking to found Ananai-kyō once again? ... Wait, wait, I too have a thought. ... Now, I shall subject thee to the most rigorous training of Brahmanism. Exhausting the utmost limits of Reishiyutaijiyū, I shall tear thy Nikutai into scattered pieces, and save thy spirit alone into Tengoku; hold this to be Arigata.'

Saying thus, he unloosed their bonds, and attempted to push them into the Takitsubo. Mozuhiko, whilst making a feigned voice, spoke:

'Ah, how vengeful! I indeed became a believer of Brahmanism and served as the barrier-keeper of the River of Eden; yet beyond expectation, Kimo-no-Futotama-no-Mikoto, whose divine power is mighty, appeared on the spot with three brave warriors. I thought to bury the four propagators into the river by means of a strategy; but, ah, how vengeful! Oh, how vengeful! Things went contrary to my intention; the prow of the boat was instantly broken to splinters, and I became the seaweed-dust of the River of Eden and came straying into this world! Hearken, thou rogue Tobihiko! Thou too art a creature that professes the teaching of Reishiyutaijiyū; shall I pull off thy living head and take thee along to the netherworld as a companion? Ho, ho, ho, ho, ho! How vengeful!'

Takayahiko lowered his hands forward, stuck out his tongue, thrust his right hand forward, and uttered:

'Hyū-doro-doro-doro-doro! How vengeful! ...'

Tobihiko: 'Hearken, hearken! What are ye? While alive, ye abandoned the splendid Brahmanism and went astray in Ananai-kyō; I thought ye were ghosts of the mortal Shiyaba, but indeed, even in death, do ye go astray again? This place is a training ground of Brahmanism; it is no place for the dead to come. Hide your forms as quickly as possible! Vanish away! Ah, how disgusting! Shoo, shoo, shoo!'

Mozuhiko: 'How vengeful! I desire the living head of Tobihiko!'

§ 3

Takahiko: 'As a souvenir for Meido, I shall take the head of Tobihiko! Hōi, hōi, hō!'

Thus saying, with a hand-gesture like unto a mantis, he scraped the air while bending his body somewhat backward.

Tobihiko: 'Ha! This fellow is a half-dead, half-living monster; for a ghost, he hath remarkably fine feet. This fellow may perchance be a false ghost! Hey, ye retainers, bind this fellow!'

Mozuhiko and Takahiko: 'Ha! Hold, hold! Where under heaven is there one who bindeth a ghost? Is not thy judgment a little amiss? Hō, hō, hō, hōi!'

Tobihiko: 'Eh! Judgment amiss or whatsoever, now that things have come to such a pass, my mind shall never endure it!'

Speaking thus, he firmly tied a thick rope unto the sashes of the two men.

Tobihiko: 'Now all is secure! Turn the handle!'

Four or five retainers answered, 'Ha!', and began to turn the handle for ascetic discipline round and round. Like unto the buckets of a well, the one soared high above his head, while the other plunged ker-plop into the basin of the waterfall. Next, in reverse, the one above fell into the waterfall basin below, alternating dozens of times; raising them and lowering them, raising them and lowering them, like unto the buckets of a well, the way of their ascending and descending reached the utmost extremity of congestion, the very spectacle of a pilgrimage to Ise for the Okage-mairi. The two men were well-nigh breathless, turning deathly pale, and enduring a peril wherein they had but one chance of life in nine of death. At this moment, a refreshing propaganda song was heard from afar. Tobihiko, together with his four or five retainers, fled at full speed and hid their forms toward the depths of the mountains. Yasuhiko, Kunihiko, and Michihiko innocently appeared at this place, guided by the sound of the waterfall, and were struck with amazement upon seeing Mozuhiko hanging in mid-air over the waterfall basin.

'Ha! This is a terrible plight! We must rescue them without a moment's delay!'

So saying, he instantly drew his double-edged sword and severed the rope. Straightway Mozuhiko plunged ker-plop into the waterfall basin. Yasuhiko stripped himself naked, leaped into the waterfall basin, grasped Mozuhiko's feet, and pulled him up. Again, the hair of the other, Takahiko, was floating upon the surface of the water. Leaping once more into the waterfall basin, he seized him by the hair and rescued him. Both men had swallowed a vast quantity of water and were gasping for breath.

Yasuhiko: 'Ah! Verily these are men with a great affinity for water; how shall we cause them to discharge the water? Since they are still twitching, they may yet be saved if we act now.'

Kunihiko: 'It appeareth they have received a vast feast of water, for their bellies are like unto drums. Shall I perchance pierce their stomach-sacs with this double-edged sword to let the water out?'

§ 4

Michihiko said, 'Speak not such folly; if we do such a thing, life itself shall verily be severed.'

Kunihiko said, 'Whether it be severed or not severed, such a matter pertaineth not unto us. Both life and death rest upon the Mikokoro of Kami. If Kami deemeth to grant us life, He shall preserve us alive. Is it not sufficient for us, by whatever means, merely to bring forth the water? Ahahaha!'

Yasuhiko said, 'Is this a time for jests? It is a moment of one chance of life in ten of death. We cannot leave these two persons to perish before our eyes. We Sendenshi, by virtue of our Shiyokushiyōgara, must succor even our enemies. What were it best for us to do?'

Michihiko said, 'How could there be any other way? There is no path for us save to offer up the Kototama of the Amatsunorito and implore Shinjiyo.'

Yasu and Kuni said, 'Ah, so it was! In the extremity of the moment we were seized with Shiushiyōraubai, and forgot the offering up of the all-important Kototama.'

So saying, they rinsed their mouths in the Takimizu, washed their hands, performed Hakushiyu and Saihai, offered up the Amatsunorito, and sang the Kazūta of Ama with clear and refreshing voices. The two men forthwith vomited forth the water, rose up briskly, and while looking around the Atari with wide eyes, were astonished at the presence of the three in this place, and said:

'Oh, noble Sendenshi, well have ye come unto us. We were upon the point of having our irreplaceable lives taken by that rogue Tobihiko of the Brahman religion over a matter of dice. Ah, how grateful, how grateful we are, to Yasuhiko-san, the parent of our life, and to the living Kami, Kunihiko and Michihiko...'

Whereupon the two prostrated themselves upon the earth, pouring forth tears like a waterfall in gratitude. At this moment, exquisite music resounded from whence no one knew, and the divine protection of Myōon-bosatsu was clearly perceived. The five again clapped their hands and gave thanks for the grace of Myōon-bosatsu.

Thenceforth the five again turned their way, crossed the vast plain, and hastened their steps toward the southeast. Having proceeded several hundred chō, they came upon a village where a dozen or more small houses stood side by side. Within this village there towered a grand building of lofty story. The five Sendenshi hastened their steps toward this mansion as their goal, and as they paused before the gate, the sound of a koto was faintly heard, and the laughing voice of a woman, somewhere familiar, leaked outside the gate in broken fragments. Yasuhiko and Michihiko tilted their heads and said:

'How strange!'
