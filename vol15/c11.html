Chapter 11: The Slaying of the Great Serpent [578]

大蛇退治の段〔578〕

§ 1

"Thus, having retreated, he descended unto the land of Torikami, which is above the river Hinokahakami" (The section on the slaying of the Orochi in the Kojiki).

Izumonokuni signifies the land of Izukumo, representing all the lands upon the Chikiujiyō. Hinokahakami refers to the nation of Japan—the most holy, most severe, most beautiful, and most pure—which preserves the Banseiitsukei Kuōtō, wherein Iuken are in Itsuchi, the Shintoku is Mukiu, and the radiance of the Kuōchō shines forth, expands, and reaches its zenith; where the Kikei is Tōmei, the Tentai and Chitai are Reiteki-ly held in possession, and Shinjin fill the land without Shishiyō, thereby uniting in Kefushin and Rikuriyoku to establish a perfect and flawless divine government.

The land of Torikami signifies the place of manifestation for the ten deities, the sacred ground of divine governance where the Izu-no-Mitama and the Mizu-no-Mitama engage in the divine work of warp and woof, purifying and sanctifying heaven and earth. In short, in order to bring about a great reformation of the world, Susanoo-no-Mikoto traveled throughout the realm, and at last descended upon the most holy ground, the Takamagahara of the earth, which is the geological center of Japan. The descent of the Mizu-no-Mitama as a divine proxy from Mount Takakura to the sacred ground of Ayabe in the autumn of the thirty-first year of Meiji, having retreated from the divine, is of the same significance as Susanoo-no-Mikoto possessing a chosen divine master and arriving at the place of the emergence of the divine age, thereby perceiving it to be the land of divine governance. The prophecy of the Kojiki is consistent from ancient times to the present, and one may verify that it is without the slightest deviation or error.

"At this time, chopsticks came floating down the river."

The spiritual inversion of Hashi is Hi. Hi is the ultimate of great mercy and great compassion. The clear text stating that Hi, the spiritual inversion of Hashi, came flowing down from Hinokahakami contains a truly profound significance. Furthermore, Hashi has the function of conveying all things to one side; thus, a bridge spanning a river and chopsticks conveying food into the mouth are identical in the meaning of Hashi. It is the bridge of divine teaching that causes one to depart from evil and transition to good. The divine teaching that causes the dark society to be improved into a society of light is also a bridge. Therefore, in the divine oracles, it is said that the Omoto of Ayabe is the great bridge of the world, and if one does not cross this great bridge, one shall understand nothing; this signifies the divine teaching of repentance, reformation, and the great renewal. That the chopsticks came floating down from the river of Hi signifies that such magnificent divine teachings for the salvation of the living are heartlessly cast away by evil spirits, and the divine authority is lowered day by day. When we examine this in light of the events of Omoto, it refers to the state where, in the thirty-first year of Meiji, the divine pillar that descended to the sacred ground of the ten deities as the divine proxy of the Mizu-no-Mitama was obstructed by a certain church and its believers, who hindered the unified divine work of the Izu-no-Mitama and the Mizu-no-Mitama, drove away the divine proxy of the Mizu-no-Mitama, and with their faction using the founder as a signboard, concealed the most severe and most grave divine teachings to establish a certain church; this is what is meant by "chopsticks came floating down the river."

『此時しも箸其の河より流れ下りき』

ハシの霊返しはヒなり。ヒは大慈大悲の極みなり。ハシの霊返しのヒなるもの、ヒノカハカミより流れ来たると云ふ明文は実に深遠なる意義の包含されあるものなり。又箸は凡てを一方に渡す活用あるものにして、川に架する橋も、食物を口内へ渡す箸もハシの意味に於ては同一なり。悪を去り善に遷らしむる神の教のハシなり。暗黒社会をして光明社会に改善せしむる神教もハシなり。故に御神諭にも、綾部の大本は世界の大橋であるから、此大橋を渡らねば、何も分りは致さむぞよ云々とあるも、改過遷善、立替立直しの神教の意味なり。その箸は肥の河より流れ下りきとは、斯る立派な蒼生救済の神教も、邪神の為に情無くも流し捨てられ、日に日に神威を降しゆく事の意味なり。是を大本の出来事に徴して見るに、去る明治三十一年に瑞の御魂の神代として十神の聖地に降りたる神柱を、某教会や信者が中を遮り、以て厳の御魂、瑞の御魂の合致的神業を妨害し、瑞霊の神代を追返し、彼等の徒党が教祖を看板として至厳至重なる神教を潜め隠し、某教会を開設したる如き状態を指して『ハシ其の河より流れ下りき』といふなり。

Kokoni Susanōnomikoto, thinking that there must be people at the upper reaches of the river, went up to seek them out, and there were an Okina and an Omina, two persons, who sat with a Kodomo in their midst and were weeping.

Hereupon Susanōnomikoto, the Kiuseishiyu of the Riyōkai of Keniu, thinking that there must be the Hongenchi of the Sekai Keirin where the Shinjin appear at the Takaamahara of the earth, the Chiushin of Nihonkoku at the upper reaches of the river Hi, went up to seek it; and there the Mitama of a Henjiyōnanshi appeared, who, while vomiting Ketsurui in order to pacify the Jiyōtai of the Kokuka, was providing Kyōkai to the Sejin without Kubetsu of Chiuya. The mention of Futari hath the same significance as the Mitama of the male Konjin of Ushitora and the female Mitama of the Foundress Deguchi Nao Ko Toji uniting into one to attend unto the divine work. Hito signifieth the returning and lodging of the spirit; it meaneth the living shrine of God, where the divine spirit of the universe dwelleth within the human physical body to execute the Keirin of heaven and earth. The Okina and Omina, the Futari, signify a Shinjin of female form and male spirit, such as the Foundress Deguchi.

The phrase 'sitting with a Kodomo in their midst and weeping' signifieth that Otome meaneth male and female, referring to the men and women, young and old, throughout the Sekai. It also meaneth the old and the young, and the present people of the Sekai are called the men and women, young and old. Kunitokotachi-no-Ōkami in the spirit world, and the Foundress Deguchi Nao Ko Toji in the manifest world, acting as the Okina and Omina, cannot bear to see the Mitama of the people of the Sekai being defiled and destroyed day by day and month by month by the evil gods; they exhaust all means and travel everywhere to provide rescue, tasting every hardship and suffering, and standing between heaven and earth, they wail aloud; this is what is meant by 'sitting with a Kodomo in their midst and weeping.'

Furthermore, when viewed from the divine eyes of God, all human beings in the Sekai are the children and daughters of God. Therefore, because all the people of the Sekai are the Kodomo of God, just as the parents of the people think of the children they have borne, God is worried for the sake of the people with the feeling of vomiting blood Chiuya; such is the reason it was shown in the Divine Revelations. Moreover, when briefly explaining the Kototama of Otome:

O is the rank of the parent, where parent and child are as one, and it is the activity that enfoldeth the great earth.

To is the perfection of peace and governance, and it is the activity of consistency from beginning to end.

Me signifieth to see through the world, storing power within and not revealing it to the outside.

When these are summarized, it signifieth the instinct of the Japanese soul inherent to Nihon, the Shinjin who possesseth both the flower and the fruit.

When He asked, 'Who are ye?', the Okina replied, 'I am a child of the Kunitsukami Ōyamatsumi-no-Kami; my name is Ashinazuchi, my wife's name is Tenazuchi, and my daughter's name is Kushinadahime.'

In the autumn of Meiji Sanjifuichinen, when the Mizu-no-Mitama was possessed by Susanōnokami in the Kamushiro and descended to the Takaamahara of the land of Ayabe, He faced the Kyōso Deguchi, who was the Gatsutaishin of the Okina and Otome, and asked, 'Who are ye?' At that time, borrowing the mouth of the Kyōso in the Kamushiro of the Izu-no-Mitama, she replied: 'I am the Chiushinjin of the Kunitsukami and the Kami Ōyamazumi. Being the Kami among Kami, I have become Ashinazuchi and Tenazuchi of the Amatsukami, and to protect and assist the Otome under Ten in peace and tranquility, I have performed the Shinji of Misogi at the Kahakami of Hi since seven years ago. Furthermore, the name of the woman of this Nikutai is called Kushinadahime, and her Honshiyugojin is the Great Kami Kinketsu-kaname.' This was the realization of the aforementioned sacred text.

Kushi signifies that the divine wisdom is radiant, being meticulous in all matters, managing all complex and difficult affairs, and cutting through tangled problems like a sharp blade severing hempen ropes.

Na is the activity of the Kami Omoikane, who governs and unites all things and reaches unto every corner.

Da is the ultimate seat of the hempen pillar, the vessel of the Great Creation, the power of duality, and the Kami who binds the ties of lord and subject, master and disciple, and husband and wife.

In short, she is the Kunitamagami of the Great Land of Nippon, the divine country of eternal heaven and earth under the profound protection of Kushinadahime, and is the Ushitora-no-Konjin of the Earth as spoken of in the Divine Revelations.

'Again, when He asked, "What is the cause of thy weeping?" they answered and said, "Our daughters were eight maidens. Here, the Yamata-no-Orochi of Koshi cometh every year and devoureth them. Now is the time when it should come, and for this reason we weep."'

Interpreting the aforementioned sacred text through the science of Kototama: among the daughters, namely the males and females, who inhabit the great earth which I protect, there are various and numerous races and peoples who are the children of the Kami, called the eight sons and daughters; yet with the passing years, the spirituality of the people has deteriorated into the spirit of demons and serpents, and the most pure and refined part of the Kami has been devoured and destroyed. Falling upon the swords of the mouths and tongues of the evil deity called the Yamata-no-Orochi of Koshi, as the years and months pass, they fear not Ten and forget the blessings of the earth; they are naught but those who perform unjust and lawless deeds. Up to eighty percent of the people have become vessels for evil deities, their bodies and souls falling into a state of living as if drunk and dying as if in a dream, where the body rules and the spirit follows; furthermore, they have changed into the tail of the Yamata-no-Orochi and continue in blind obedience. While bringing about the turmoil of the world and the ruin of nations, the time has approached when even the Mitama of the people of the divine country who remain at the last are to be devoured and destroyed; therefore, wondering how to save and assist this world from its miserable state and how to report this to the Amatsu-Ōkami, the heart is broken into a thousand pieces, and reflecting upon the future of the world and the nation, they answered that they weep and lament.

§ 4

The significance of the term Koshi refers to distant lands beyond the far seas, which, from the perspective of Nihon, denotes Shina, Ōbei, and various other nations. From overseas, a myriad of evil ideologies have been introduced. By changing their methods and guises, through Shiukyō, Seiji, and Kyōiku, they have throughout the ages persistently invaded, confounding and seeking to annihilate the Yamatodamashihi, which inherently possesses the Shisei of Keishin, Sonnō, and Haukoku in a Kamunagarateki manner; this state is what is called the devouring by the Yamata no Orochi of Koshi. Furthermore, the Tenchi of foreign lands have for thousands of years been deceived by the schemes of these evil deities, resulting in infinite confusion in both high and low, and leading to the ruin of nations; yet, they continue to pursue these plans vigorously, and have finally invaded even the land of the Nihon Shinkoku, seeking to devour and slay all the Mitama of the Nihon Otome, who are the direct descendants of the Amatsukami; this is now imminent, and only the Otome, who is the Yamatodamashihi inherent to the Shinkoku, remains. Should she be destroyed by the great evil spirits of the evil deities, it would be impossible to save the great Earth which the Tenso and Kuniso have labored to open. I am enduring great hardship and suffering, wishing by some means to save her. It is truly a state of the world where one cannot even weep, and yet one cannot fundamentally save it. This was the reply of the Mitama of the Hensei-danshi, saying that he is at a loss as to what to do, and is weeping aloud toward the Tenchi.

"When asked what its form is like, it is answered that its eyes are like the red kagachi, and upon one body it has eight heads and eight tails. Furthermore, upon its body grow moss, and cypress, and cedar, and its length spans eight valleys and eight peaks, and when one looks at its belly, it is entirely festered with constant blood. (The term red kagachi here refers to what is now the hōzuki.)"

The significance of the phrase 'When asked what its form is like' is the inquiry of Susanoo no Mikoto as to what state the influence of the evil ideologies of the Yamata no Orochi has manifested.

The old man and old woman, who are the Mitama of the Hensei-danshi, replied that the great focus of the evil deities' schemes, as manifested in reality, is that they have eyes like the red kagachi and possess one body with eight heads and eight tails; this signifies that while the essence of the evil deities is one, those who grasp their true intent and participate in the conspiracy to annihilate the world are the eight head-stocks, and these eight head-stocks are advancing their plans on a grand scale everywhere upon the entire globe. In Seiji, in economy, in Kyōiku, in Shiukyō, in industry, in ideology, and in other social enterprises, they are plotting total destruction in secrecy. Consequently, the countless subordinates of the evil deities, who are in the position of the tails, are acting blindly in every direction, participating unknowingly and unconsciously in the great global conspiracy of the one leader and the eight heads, eventually provoking the great world war that has spanned the past five years, destroying the sovereigns of Seiro and others, and inciting laborers to cause great confusion in every corner of the world. The red kagachi signifies the tragic state of cannon smoke, rain of bullets, rivers of blood, and mountains of corpses, and the realization of the red-transformation movement. Truly, the present age is the moment when the great serpent of Yamata has finally bared its great eyes of the red kagachi, having already devoured and slain seven Otome of the world, and is now attempting to devour at once, in both the manifest and hidden worlds, even Nihon, which is the Hino-kawa. In short, the eight heads signify the great leaders who possess the modern great power lurking in strong nations such as England, America, Russia, France, Germany, and Italy, and the eight tails signify the many subordinates who blindly follow the heads. The time is approaching when both the heads and the tails must be severed.

The meaning of the passage, 'Moreover, upon its body grew moss, as well as cypresses and cedars, and its length spanned across Taniyatani and Oyao; and when one looked upon its belly, it was ever entirely inflamed with blood,' is a figurative description of how the various nations upon Chikiujiyō have all been ravaged from the depths of the mountains to the ends of the waters by these Akugami and Dashin, falling into a state of Fuon. This time, I shall utterly cause the guardian deities of the eight tails and eight heads to surrender.' This is a great fact that accords with the grand spirit of this main text.

To briefly explain the Genrei of Mimizura:

Mi signifies that the physical form is fully accomplished, manifesting and determining for each the rank of a subject of the divine land of Japan, attaining the truth, and thereby uniting with the Mizu-no-Mitama.

Tsu signifies preserving true life eternally as a branch spirit of God, protecting the Sovereign and the nation as a physical body, and assisting and protecting God and the people as a spiritual body.

Ra signifies the complete realization of the three matters of word, heart, and action, becoming a subject of consistency from beginning to end and spirit-ruling-matter, manifesting instinct with complete freedom.

When synthesizing the above four Genrei, it signifies becoming a practitioner of the true word and true action of the Japanese soul, and becoming a true child whose soul shall join the ranks of the Gods.

In short, Hayasusanōnomikoto, the Mizu-no-Mitama, was entrusted by the Shinjin who is the founder of divine government as two spirits in one body with the protection and nurturing of man and woman; He manifested a great Daikongau power, transformed and Tatenaho them into the original Japanese soul, and further made them practitioners thereof; this is what is meant by 'transforming that Otome into a Yutsutsumagushi and piercing it into His Mimizura.'

In this manner, it required a corresponding passage of Saigetsu to reform the Mitama of the Banmin of the Tenka and to deliver them unto the Mitama of Ashinazuchi and Tenazuchi. Whereas one might think that person A drinks one gō and collapses in drunkenness, person B drinks one shō and does not become intoxicated, and person C feels not the slightest bit drunk unless he drinks about three shō; such distinctions are proof that reactions differ according to the nature of the Mitama. That one drinks and laughs, another rages, and another weeps, even with the same kind of sake of the same flavor, is truly a wondrous thing, and this is inevitably due to the innate relationship between the body and the Mitama.

『故、告りたまへる随にして、如此設け備へて待つ時に、其の八岐大蛇、信に言ひしが如来つ。乃ち、船毎に、己々頭を垂入て、その酒を飲みき、於是、飲み酔ひてみな伏寝たり。爾ち速須佐之男命、その御佩せる十拳剣を抜きて、その大蛇を切散りたまひしかば肥の河、血に変りて流れき』

§ 8

Thereupon the Mitama of Henjiyōnanshi, in accordance with the will of the Mikoto, brewed a rich and fragrant Miki, and inviting the Shinmei of Tenchi, expressed Kuwanki and offered Kanshiya for the Shinon. The many Kashira and Kenzoku of the Warugami, possessed by the spirit of the Yamata no Orochi, drank up the Ōmiki. Just as the Ningen of the world today rot even their intestines because of sake, impair the Jiyunkuwan of their Ketsueki, their heads becoming heavy and their Kauho becoming unsteady and not Jiiu, falling upon the Chijiyō and losing all sense of Zengo, dallying with ugly women and destroying their houses, clouding their wisdom, and suffering in agony from incurable diseases; this is what is called 'all lay down in drunken sleep.' At this, the Great Kami of Mizu-no-Mitama, unable to endure seeing the misconduct of the people of the world, raised a divine army and cut down and destroyed the Mitama possessed by these evil spirits and serpent-gods. To say that He unsheathed the Totsuka-no-Tsurugi means that He received the imperial decree of the Tōtsu-Kami and manifested the instinct of crushing evil and revealing the truth. Thereupon, the general populace of high and low in Hinomoto, the ancestral land of the world by the River of Hi, reformed their hearts from the depths, becoming of sincere red hearts like blood, and like the same kindred together with the world, the realm was governed in peace and tranquility eternally and without end; this is what is meant by 'the River of Hi turned to blood and flowed.' The meaning of flowing is to be transmitted unto tens of thousands of generations. In the preface of the Kojiki, the desire to let it flow to later leaves bears the same meaning.

"Therefore, when He cut the middle tail, the edge of His august sword was chipped. Thinking it strange, He thrust and split it with the point of His august sword and looked, and there was a Tsumugari-no-Tachi. Therefore, He took this sword, and thinking it a wondrous thing, He reported it to Amaterasu-Ōmikami. This is the Kusanagi-no-Tachi."

The middle tail refers to the subjects of the lower society of Ashihara-no-Nakatsukuni. When He judged those subjects and minutely dissected and inspected their Mitama, He recognized divine persons of truly magnificent adamantine power; this state is described as the edge of His august sword being chipped. Ah, truly a magnificent Mitama of a Savior, beyond all expectation, lies hidden in the lower stratum of society, which is the middle tail of the Orochi. Saying that this is a great find and being deeply moved is what is meant by thinking it strange. To say with the point of His august sword means to shine and see by the light of the ancestral teachings of the Tenso.

そこで変性男子の身魂は命の随々芳醇なる神酒を造りて、天地の神明を招待し、以て歓喜を表し賜ひ、神恩を感謝し給うたのである。八岐の大蛇の霊に憑依された数多の悪神の頭目や眷族共が大神酒を飲んで了つた。丁度今日の世の中の人間は、酒の為に腸までも腐らせ、血液の循環を悪くし、頭は重くなり、フラフラとして行歩も自由ならぬ、地上に転倒して前後も弁知せず、醜婦に戯れ家を破り、知識を曇らせ、不治の病を起して悶え苦しんで居るのは、所謂「飲み酔ひて皆伏寝たり」と云ふことである。爾に於て瑞の御霊の大神は、世界人民の不行跡を見るに忍びず、神軍を起して、此の悪鬼蛇神の憑依せる、身魂を切り散らし、亡ぼし給うたのである。十拳剣を抜きてと云ふ事は遠津神の勅定を奉戴して破邪顕正の本能を発揮し給うたと云ふことである。そこで肥の河なる世界の祖国日の本の上下一般の人民は、心から改心をして、血の如き赤き真心となり、同じ血族の如く世界と共に、永遠無窮に平和に安穏に天下が治まつたと云ふ事を「肥の河血に変りて流れき」と云ふのである。流れると云ふ意義は幾万世に伝はる事である。古事記の序文に、後葉に流へんと欲すとあるも、同義である。

『故其の中の尾を切りたまふ時、御刀の刄毀けき。怪しと思ほして、御刀の端もて刺割きて見そなはししかば、都牟刈之太刀あり。故此太刀を取らして、怪異しき物ぞと思ほして天照大御神に白し上げたまひき。是は草薙太刀なり』

中の尾と云ふ事は、葦原の中津国の下層社会の臣民の事である。其臣民を裁断して、身魂を精細に解剖点検し玉ふ時に、実に立派な金剛力の神人を認められた状態を称して、御刀の刄毀けきと云ふのである。アヽ実に予想外の立派な救世主の身魂が、大蛇の中の尾なる社会の下層に隠れ居るわい。是は一つの掘り出しものだと謂つて、感激されたことを、怪しと思ほしてと云ふのである。御刀の端もてと云ふ事は、天祖の御遺訓の光に照し見てと云ふ事である。

§ 9

The statement, 'Having cut and split it open and looked, there was the Tsumugarinotachi,' signifieth that when the former method of Tenken and Chōsa was changed and a minute Goshinsa was conducted from the Sokumen, it was discovered for the first time that a Daishinjin, whose Kuwatsuyō of the Shikon and Gojiyō was perfect, lay hidden in a corner of the Kasō Shiyakuwai within the middle tail. To interpret the Tsumugarinotachi from the standpoint of Genreigakujiyō, it is the Daikiuseishiyu of the Sanzensekai and the Mitama of Izunome. Therefore, the Mitama of this Tsumugarinotachi, the Daikiuseishiyu, was raised up and, being marveled at as an Isū no Shinjin, was immediately offered as a precious sacred treasure unto Amaterasuōmikami and Her Heugenshin. The Kusanagi no Tsurugi is the name given to that one great Shinjin, the pillar of the nation of Japan and the foundation for the peace of the world, who, like the divine wind blowing and bending the green grass, causeth all people to submit with joy through virtue. In these days when the Yamata no Orochi rageth and destroyeth the Mitama of all people, it is desired that a Daishinjin of true virtue, who manifesteth the Kuwatsuyō of the Kusanagi sacred sword, shall appear as swiftly as possible.

Furthermore, Kusanagi no Tsurugi is another name for the whole of our Japan. The Shinjin who beareth this divine land upon his shoulders is the one who manifesteth the Kuwatsuyō of the Mitama of the Kusanagi sacred sword.

『刺割きて見そなはししかば都牟刈之太刀あり』と云ふことは今迄の点検調査の方針を一変し、側面より仔細に御審査になると、四魂五情の活用全き大真人が、中の尾なる下層社会の一隅に、潜みつつあつたのを初めて発見されたと云ふことである。都牟刈之太刀とは言霊学上より解すれば三千世界の大救世主にして、伊都能売の身魂と云ふ事である。故、此太刀なる大救世主の霊魂を取り立て、異数の真人なりと驚歎され、直ちに天照大御神様、及びその表現神に大切なる御神器として、奉献されたのである。凡ての青人草を神風の吹きて靡かす如く、徳を以て万民を悦服せしむる一大真人、日本国の柱石にして世界治平の基たるべき、神器的真人を称して、草薙剣と云ふのである。八岐大蛇の暴狂ひて万民の身魂を絶滅せしめつつある今日、一日も早く草薙神剣の活用ある、真徳の大真人の出現せむことを、希望する次第である。

また草薙剣とは、我日本全国の別名である。この神国を背負つて立つ処の真人は、即ち草薙神剣の霊魂の活用者である。
