Chapter 16: Shadows upon the Water [583]

水上の影〔583〕

§ 1

The Sannansanjiyo, while chanting the Shinka, advance with purity of heart. Once again, they strike against a Ganpeki.

Takakuniwake said, 'Ah, once more a Ganpeki. Even if one says that the world is in a state of Itsusai Banji Yukitsuma, has the wind of such a deadlock blown even unto this place? We must, by the power of the true Shinriki, open this Ihato and open the world that is in a deadlock. First, let us take Kiusoku and deliberate at our leisure.'

Kamehiko said, 'How would it be to hold a Rinji Gikuwai?'

Umehiko said, 'Ha ha ha, when I hear of a Gikuwai, I cannot help but recall the Gankutsu of the ugly. They say that Rekishi Kurikahe; let us hold a Himitsukuwai in a leisurely manner.'

So saying, they sat upon a rock of Koroahi. The three women likewise sat down, saying, 'Ah, how grateful, how grateful; let us rest here at our leisure.'

Takakuniwake said, 'Eh, how did you all come to enter this Gankutsu?'

Kamehiko said, 'Hearing that the great deity Kami-Susanowo-no-Ōkami had crossed Mount Chikyō and arrived at this secret land of Tibet, we, without delay, followed in his footsteps. Just as we were advancing, we struck against a hill of small weeds, and as the five of us were taking a rest, a goddess appeared from nowhere and left behind the words, "Since Ikutsuhikone-no-Mikoto is exploring this subterranean Gankutsu, you must hasten to follow his trail," and then her form vanished. Looking to the side, we saw a dark hole, and as we were peering into it in wonder, the ground collapsed with a thud. Thinking we had fallen seven or eight ken into the earth, we found this Gankutsu... Since then, we have been exploring the interior of this Gankutsu while chanting the Shinka. Just as we were doing so, we encountered an old well on the path, and when we looked, we saw a mysterious shadow. Just as we were wondering what it could be, we heard your voice from the bottom of the well... such is the history of our arrival.'

Takakuniwake said, 'Ah, that is indeed remarkable. In truth, the moment we fell into that well, it seems we lost consciousness, passed through a vast wilderness, and climbed to the summit of a high mountain. Just as I thought I had encountered five men and women, I awoke with a start, and the moment I looked up at the sky, I beheld the figures of your party... Truly, it is a matter of ten thousand mysteries.'

Umehiko said, 'In my dream last night, I met with you, and the state of us resting here in this Gankutsu at this very moment was vividly before my eyes. Truly, the unity of the manifest and the hidden; this world is a mysterious place.'

§ 2

Suddenly, from nowhere, the voices of Abikyōkuwan resounded throughout.

Takakuniwake stood up abruptly and said, 'Ho, everyone, some Henji is occurring within this Gankutsunai. Come, let us hasten to Tanken.'

So saying, he pushed against the Ganpeki with all his might.

The stone door opened wide.

Beholding the scene, Sūjifunin of Rauniyakunanniyo were all bound with Takate and Kote, while in the Chiuau, four or five Kishin with faces red as cinnabar, carrying Tetsubō, were kicking and tormenting the Danjiyo with the tips of their feet.

Takakuniwake cried, 'Ho, Onōnogata, this place is like the Jigoku of Meido. Ho, all of you, let us leap in and save them.'

Leaping forward, he took the lead.

The five followed closely behind, and in unison, they chanted the Kototama.

The forms of the demons gradually grew faint, and at last, they vanished like smoke.

When they looked at the many Rauniyakunanniyo, it appeared that their being bound with Takate and Kote was but an illusion; each had their Morote joined together, sitting in Tanza before the Gankutsu.

In unison, they prayed with all their might, without casting a glance aside: 'O Great Deity Kami-Susanō-no-Ōkami, manifest upon the earth with all haste and grant us salvation.'

Upon seeing the six figures, the Rauniyakunanniyo turned toward them, and while joining their hands in prayer, they choked with tears of joy, saying, 'Ah, how grateful, how grateful, how unworthy we are; you must be the attendants of the Great Deity Kami-Susanō-no-Ōkami.'

Takakuniwake proclaimed, 'Ho, having heard the situation from before, I see you all praying for the appearance of the Great Deity Kami-Susanō-no-Ōkami; your sincerity has reached the heavens, and even now, the August One is staying at the house of Canaan. Depart from this Gankutsu with all haste and behold the august countenance of the Great Deity of benevolence.'

Hearing these words, they rejoiced greatly, and saying, 'Ah, the return of the Great Deity, how grateful, how unworthy,' they moved their waists in joy, writhed about, and rejoiced.

Takakuniwake faced them and chanted the Heavenly Number Song, whereupon the Sūjifunin of Rauniyakunanniyo, who had been thin and withered until now, suddenly gained flesh, their complexions became beautiful, and their vigor was restored; they rose up at once, clapped their hands, danced wildly in all directions, and thanked the return of the Great Deity from the bottom of their hearts.

Then, all of them, in high spirits, followed the man holding the Ōnusa and walked away in a procession.

Watching them go, Takakuniwake said, 'Ah, what lovable beings. To see so many good men and women unite their hearts and strive in faith gives me a feeling of goodness that cannot be described. The August One must surely be well pleased. Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haese, Kannagara Tamachi Haese.'

§ 3

Takakuniwake and his party advanced deeper and deeper into the interior.

The sun appeared to have set behind Seizan, and the interior of the Ihaya suddenly grew dark.

As the Rokunin proceeded, groping their way, a strange Umekigoe was heard nearby.

Aikohime, with keen ears, heard the voice and said:

"Pray, everyone, do you not hear a strange voice?"

Kamehiko replied, "Ah, that must be your Shinkei. Since sounds echo within the Ihaya, it is likely that the voice of the Norito from before has remained deep within the Naiji, and has begun its Hankiyō Undō."

Aikohime said, "Nay, nay, it is not the voice of the Norito; it is a voice of a woman, pleading in Kumon. In this Ihaya, which is the Sōkutsu of the Akugami, one cannot fathom what manner of Sanji may be taking place. Everyone, pray stop and listen with ears attuned. As we are the messengers of the Kami who shall save the Sekai, we cannot simply let a voice of Kumon pass by and ignore it."

Kamehiko said, "Ah, indeed, it is a voice that sounds most distressed. Pray, Takakuniwake-sama, it is dark, and rather than advancing so recklessly, shall we not seek out the source of this voice?"

Takakuniwake said, "Truly, it is a most strange voice indeed."

As he spoke, the moment he pressed firmly against the rock wall at his side, wondrously, the stone door opened with unexpected ease.

Looking closely, a white shadow was seen lying within the Ihaya, groaning in Kumon.

Kamehiko said, "Ah, this is strange, most strange. In this darkness, something like freshly hewn timber is groaning. This is likely a white serpent."

Umehiko said, "For a white serpent, is it not too short for its girth?"

Kamehiko said, "The fellow is a white serpent that has been severed in half somewhere, and is now in a state of Kumon, having barely escaped death. ...Hey, hey, Master White Serpent, what has happened, what has happened?"

The white shadow said, "Ah, how resentful I am. I followed in the wake of the Himegimi and came as far as this place, but I fell into the schemes of the Magatsukami of the Uranai-kyō, Magatsubetsu, and had my limbs bound and was thrust into this Ihaya, with no means to escape. Ah, what shall I do, how resentful I am."

Kamehiko said, "Ho, I thought it was a great serpent, but it is babbling things I do not understand. Pray, Takakuniwake-sama, investigate it for a moment."

"Nay, I pray you, take the trouble to examine it yourself; it appears to be human."

Kamehiko said, 'Metsusau, how loathsome; how could one toy with such a white figure in this darkness? O Umehiko, you are a man of Daitan nature by Heiso. Why do you not display your Otokogi here and show your Eiyūburi to Ikuyohime?'

Kamehiko said, 'Ah, you have brought Shitsupei to a grave place. What, one should simply think that the Zaimoku is moving... Hey, hey, Zaimoku, who are you?'

The white shadow said, 'Ah, how resentful.'

Kamehiko said, 'W-what is this? Is it the Kyō of divination, or an Iurei? I know not what it is, but I have never heard of an Iurei of Zaimoku since ancient times. Ever since the Ōkami Susanō went into Gotaiin, it is said that even Sansensaumoku have come to Kototo; it seems this Zaimoku is no exception to that selection, as it is somehow performing Kototo... Hey, Zaimoku, will you not rise?'

Umehiko, aiming at the white shadow, stroked it smoothly.

Umehiko said, 'Ha, this is a Ningen, and moreover, she appears to be a Bijin with soft skin, bound with Takate. She has likely been oppressed by the minions of an evil god and imprisoned in this cavern.'

So saying, he deftly untied the bonds. The white shadow stood up straight, and faster than one could grasp a dagger in a reverse grip, she cried,

'Ha, you evil gods of the divination Kyō, you minions of Hinodewake, now that it has come to this, prepare yourselves for my desperate struggle!'

She brandished her short sword at the six faint shadows, raging wildly in all directions.

Kamehiko said, 'Ha, wait, wait, we are the propagandists of the Sangokyō.'

'What? Propagandists of the Sangokyō? That is a blatant lie; feel the power of Asakohime’s desperate, wheel-like movements!'

She flew about like a bird.

Tanakuniwake said, 'Ha, are you not Asakohime, the lady-in-waiting to Aikohime who appeared in Ken-on-kyō? I am Aikohime’s husband, Tanakuniwake.'

Asakohime said, 'Deep-seated schemes of a demon, think you I shall fall for such tricks? Feel the swiftness of Asakohime’s practiced arts!'

§ 5

Once again, she flashed her Tantau in the darkness and began to Arekuru. Aikohime spoke:

"Art thou not Asakohime? Compose thyself, for I am indeed Aikohime, and there is no Machigahi."

Asakohime replied, "Ah, that voice which thou dost Otsushiya is truly that of Aikohime."

Aikohime said, "Thou art Asakohime, whose voice none can mistake, and thy form is known to me even by Yome; how joyful, how joyful it is to meet thee in this unexpected place."

Asakohime, with a voice that had somewhat Ochitsu, and panting for breath, replied:

"Th-th-thou who dost Otsushiya thus art truly Aikohime; how I long for thee."

So saying, she fell weeping upon the spot. At this moment, from Izuku, a single ray of Kuōmiyō shone forth, and the faces of Ichidō became as bright as in the day.

Asakohime said, "Oh, all of you, we have met in a place of Fushigi; truly, you have saved me from a perilous situation. This is entirely due to the profound Goshiyugo of Konohanahime."

With that, she joined her hands in Gatsushiyō, and thereafter could not utter a Hitokoto, her eyes clouded with tears of joy.

To encourage her, Takakuniwake struck Asakohime upon her Senaka three or four times with his Hirate and said:

"Asakohime, be firm. There must be a deep Yōsu to this. We, too, must prepare ourselves greatly hereafter. Since thou hast been Oshiko into this Ihaya, there must be a Sōkutsu of Amata Warugami within this cave; tell us in detail the circumstances of how thou didst arrive here."

As he asked this with an emboldened voice, Asakohime suddenly regained her composure and said:

"There is a deep reason for this; pray, listen to my tale. When Ame no Futodama no Mikoto appeared in Kenon-gō and drove away Onigumohiko, the great leader of the Brahman religion, I, together with Aikohime, was guarding Kenon Castle. Just as Amaterasu Ōmikami hid Herself in the Heavenly Rock Cave, Futodama no Mikoto ascended in haste to Tenkyōzan. During his absence, as Aikohime and I were guarding the castle, the surroundings became dark and gloomy, and we could not distinguish day from night. The raging gods swarmed like flies, and Onigumohiko appeared once again, indulging in his violent power under the cover of darkness.
