Chapter 15: The Deity of the Mountain [582]

山の神〔582〕

§ 1

Obeying the command of the Kami Susanō, who purifies this world, Takakuniwake, of high and noble Miizu, sought to rescue every man, woman, and child who had fallen into the strange Gankutsu, leaving not one soul behind.

He leaped into the cavern of the Chitei and offered his prayers to the Kami, then, with an Ō figure, he followed the direction of a voice.

Arriving at a stone door, he clasped his Riyōte together once more, and finding his Shintai brought to a standstill, he remained for a time lost in Shian.

At that very moment, from Izuko, a Senkuō shone forth, and a Hidama advanced before Takakuniwake.

Takakuniwake placed his hand upon the hilt of his sword, Migama-ing to strike should it draw near.

The Kiyodai Hidama descended with an Onkiyō like the simultaneous Rakuka of Banrai, and as it became Hakuen, wrapping the Atari in a mist where one could not discern even a Shiseki, Takakuniwake steeled his heart, held his breath in his Saikatanden, and Sōjiyō-ed the Amatsunorito; whereupon the fierce smoke, which until then had obscured all, vanished as if wiped away, and a graceful goddess stood there, smiling.

Takakuniwake spoke: 'Ha! Who are you? I suspect you are of the kin of the golden-furred nine-tailed evil fox that dwells in this Gankutsu; I shall strike you down with my double-edged long sword.'

No sooner had he spoken than he struck with the speed of lightning, but the goddess danced into mid-air, dodging his body to the right, to the left, above, and below like a flying bird, and finally transformed once more into the previous fire-bullet, fleeing through the interior of the Gankutsu like an arrow shot, emitting a roar.

Thinking he must not be caught off guard, Takakuniwake looked around the vicinity, when a large man, appearing to be one jō five or six shaku in height, appeared on the scene leading a strange beast, and bowed slightly to Takakuniwake.

Takakuniwake, thinking this must be another attack by an evil Kami, kept his eyes alert and Migama-ed.

The large man opened his mouth wide and laughed loudly: 'Ahahaha, you are a makeshift fake Shinwasha who has descended from Takamagahara; behold my true form.'

He opened his eyes, which were like mirrors, and glared fiercely; Takakuniwake clasped his Riyōte, assumed the posture of Chinkon, and emitted the Kototama with a single cry of 'Un', whereupon the large man instantly changed his form and became the graceful goddess.

Takakuniwake said, 'This is the mischief of a shape-shifting evil Kami; I shall make you reveal your true form now,' and flashing his double-edged long sword, he thrust at the goddess with all his might, intending to pierce even her bones.

The moment the goddess nimbly dodged his body, Takakuniwake, carried by his own momentum, passed seven or eight ken through a tunnel in the Gankutsu, fell headlong into a bottomless pit, and thus Takakuniwake breathed his last, no longer being a person of this world.

Takakuniwake, all alone, proceeds as his feet lead him, chanting the Kamigoto while traversing the Genya where the Ten is azure, the mountains are pure, and the Hiyakukuwaranman flowers are in full bloom, without a set destination.

A high mountain, half-shrouded in a garment of clouds, appeared, standing in the Zenpau. Feeling as though he were drawn to the mountain, Takakuniwake proceeds as his feet lead him. Upon reaching a steep slope, he looks up to see a group of four or five men and women of Senkuwashiyoku, whispering of something with Omoshiro and Oka, and approaching him with Iuiu ease.

Takakuniwake, folding his Riyōte, speaks in Hitorigoto:

"Ah, I thought that, having served in the Otomo of Susanōnomikoto Ōkami and followed the Shiyonin by the Mei of the noble one who rests in the Itsuka of Canaan, I had slipped into a mysterious cavern; yet, having crossed a vast Genya under a clear sky without a single cloud, mist, or speck of dust, it is beyond my understanding that I should now arrive at this mountain pass."

So saying, he looks back at the scenery in all directions, lost in thought. The five men and women appear there, and bowing respectfully in unison, they say:

"Are you not Takakuniwake, the propagandist, the deity Ikutsuhikone? We have come as messengers of the deity Kami Izanagi Ōkami to welcome you; come, let us guide you."

They proceed, taking the lead. Takakuniwake, without suspicion, follows the five with alacrity as they ascend the steep slope. At last, they reach the summit of the slope. The two male and three female deities speak in unison:

"Welcome, Takakuniwake-sama, you must be weary. This is the summit of the Chin-no-tōge; pray, rest yourself here first."

Takakuniwake says:

"Ah, this unexpected solitary journey; I have felt somewhat lonely while traversing these beautiful mountains and fields without a companion to speak with. Yet, to be met by you beautiful people from below this slope—I cannot comprehend it at all. In what land is this mountain called Chin-no-tōge?"

The five reply only with a "Hai," smiling and saying nothing more. At that moment, a cool breeze, beyond description, blows gently, caressing the face of Takakuniwake, making a rustling sound, and passes by while turning over the broad leaves of the trees.

"Oh, it feels as if I were in the Ten-goku Jōdo. The birds sing in the sky, the Hiyakukuwaranman flowers bloom in profusion, the wind is pure and fragrant, and I hear the faint, subtle music. Does such a beautiful land exist even in the Ashihara-no-nakatsukuni, which is so clouded over? Ah, how pleasant it is."

So saying, he sits heavily upon the grass, and with cool words, addresses the group:

"As for this journey of Kefu which I cannot Gaten, to which Kami do you belong? Pray, make known your Nano."

One man, with reverence, replied:

"This is indeed the renowned Lord Takakunowake; to become husband and wife is the destiny of the Mitama bound by the Kami. For Chiyo and Yachiyo, together with me, pray devote yourself to the Kami-waza, hand in hand."

Scattering Momiji upon her countenance, Aikohime placed her gentle hands upon her knees and began to speak. Takakuniwake, unable to discern whether it was a dream, reality, or an illusion, remained for a while in a state of Bauzen, gazing up at the Ōzora and lost in Shian.

Umehiko, feeling impatient, said, 'Takakuniwake-sama, upon what do you ponder? All matters are the Onsada of the God of Union; pray, give your Goshiyōdaku at once.'

'Ah, Arigata, Arigata. An unexpected Mia upon the Sanjiyō, through the Onakadachi of the Kamisama of the mountain; with a mat of grass for a floor, the Tenjiyō of the clouds, the Ongaku of the wind, and the dancing of the Kigi leaves—nay, I am most grateful and I Shiyōchi.'

Thus did Takakuniwake welcome the proposal with an Egaho. From this time forth, the Sezoku came to call a wife the 'God of the Mountain.' Aikohime stood up, and pointing toward the Takayama that towered over the Nanpau Shiyozan, she said,

'The Ōgon mountain beyond the clouds is our eternal home; come, let us all proceed.' So saying, she took the lead and descended the steep slope to the south.

The party descended the slope in the manner of an Abt-style railway, exerting strength with every step. Before they knew it, they had reached the summit of that distant, lofty mountain which stood erect above the clouds.

As the three couples stood upon the summit and recited the Amatsu Norito, the five-colored clouds that had enveloped the mountain parted to the left and right as if a door had been opened.

Beyond the reach of sight lay a vast green field, where towers of white, red, blue, yellow, and purple—triple, quintuple, decuple, and twenty-fold—stood in their hundreds within the villages of the green field below; the splendor was beyond compare. Casting their eyes into the distance, they beheld a vast ocean filled with deep blue waves, where pure white sails, both full and partial, moved to and fro in the calm; it was like a great painting created by the hand of a master, and the party watched the magnificent view, forgetting the passage of time.

At this moment, from within a beautiful shrine on the summit, a single Goddess appeared as the Ōgon doors opened, and accompanied by two handmaidens, she appeared leisurely before the six.

'I am Konohana-hime. Since you are Kami-bito of loyalty, courage, righteousness, supreme benevolence, and supreme love, this sacred domain is the land where you shall dwell eternally. Nevertheless, as there are still tasks to be performed in the Genkai, you must return once more to the Genkai. Henceforth, do not let your hearts slacken. Should you be tempted by the evil wind of Taishu-Reiju, you shall never be able to come here again. Depart now and return swiftly to the Genkai.'

Suddenly the Shihen became Ankoku, and in the moment he felt Kanrei within his Shintai, he returned to life and beheld that Takakuniwake lay fallen at the bottom of a deep Ido within the Gankutsunai.

“Ah, was it but a dream? Nevertheless, it is a matter of Fushin that I was addressed as Ikutsuhikone. Perchance, because I performed Saniha rashly without knowing the Shiyugojin of Wagami, the Ōkami hath taught me through His Gojinji. Ah, Arigata, Arigata.”

Thus he spoke, and joining his hands in Gatsushiyō, he offered up the Amatsunorito in a clear voice. Suddenly, as he looked up toward the sky, there appeared the beautiful Ninansanjiyo, the Shinjin whom he had seen in his dream, standing about the Mawari of the cavern and whispering something as they peered into the Seitei. Takakuniwake felt as though he were dreaming a dream within a dream, and again he folded his Riyōte, unraveling the tangles of his heart. After a short while, Takakuniwake, while looking up toward the sky from the Seitei, spoke:

“Pray, Kamehiko-sama, Umehiko-sama, and the other three ladies, I am Takakuniwake. In order to save the lives of men, I stole into the Gankutsunai of the earth, and by error have fallen into the bottom of such an old Ido. I beseech you, will you not somehow save me?”

“Lo, art thou the Takakuniwake of whom we have heard rumors? Thou must indeed be in distress. We must devise some means to save thee. Nevertheless, in an old Ido within such a Gankutsunai, there are usually stairs. When this Kamehiko once fell into an old Ido of the Gankutsunai of the Shiko in the land of Fusa, I was grieved as to what I should do, but suddenly looking to the side, I found stairs carved therein. Examine it right well.”

“I thank you, but there is neither a handhold nor a foothold. It is exactly as if I have fallen into a bamboo tube.”
