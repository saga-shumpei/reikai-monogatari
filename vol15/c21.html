Chapter 21: Return to the Manifest Realm [588]

帰顕〔588〕

§ 1

As Matsuhiko and his party advanced across the Suganiha, which was paved entirely with Kinsa, Ginsa, and Shinjiyu, a Shinjin appeared, accompanied by two or three Jiyūshiya, clad in a uniform of Ōkin, whose Ganshiyoku was beautiful and whose figure was in every way Iubi and Kaushiyō, and he approached Matsuhiko with a Kuwanji.

"Lord Matsuhiko, your journey has been arduous. First, pray take Kiusoku in the inner chamber. Oh, Tamahiko, Izuhiko, and Kusuhiko, how well you have come."

As the Mihashira looked up, struck by the indescribable Atatakami in this voice, they beheld Kotoyoriwakenomikoto, from whom they had parted by the river.

The three, in astonishment, could only say, "Ah, are you the noble deity, Kotoyoriwakenomikoto?" and stood there, tears of joy falling in torrents.

Kotoyoriwakenomikoto said, "All of you, come this way," and leading the way, he walked slowly up the stairs of a beautiful palace.

The party followed behind, trembling with awe. The four were seated in the very center of a beautiful palace built of cypress. Kotoyoriwakenomikoto commanded many beautiful male and female attendant deities to fill jade cups with sake and to serve rare fruits, urging them to partake.

The party was overjoyed at this unexpected reception, not knowing how to compose themselves, and their hearts were filled with such excitement that they could not settle.

After a brief Kiusoku, Kotoyoriwakenomikoto accompanied the three, leading them through a beautiful corridor fragrant with the scent of wood, deeper and deeper into the interior.

Kotoyoriwake finished clapping his hands and offered a divine prayer, whereupon a white-haired old deity appeared, pushing open a brocade curtain and entering before them with a Kuwanji.

"You, Kotoyoriwakenomikoto, and the three Kami-tsukasa, you have done well to come. Store the majesty of this Takamagahara deep within your hearts, observe the state of this sacred land thoroughly, and remain here for several days to breathe the air of the sacred land and purify your Mitama; then, return once more to the Genkai, fulfill your remaining mission, and thereafter return hither again. I am Kuninotokotachinomikoto."

So saying, with a dignity that was solemn and inviolable yet filled with a smile, he bowed slightly and entered the inner hall.

Kotoyoriwake and the three others, their hearts choked with joy, could offer no response, and remained with heads bowed, unaware of the passage of time as they shed tears of joy.

Once more, in a voice that held both majesty and warmth, the deity spoke: "You, Kotoyoriwakenomikoto, as well as Tamahiko-no-mikoto, Izuhiko-no-mikoto, and Kusuhiko-no-mikoto, your sincerity has reached the Takamagahara of the earth."

"Leisurely observe the state of the sacred land, return again to the Genkai to fulfill your mission, and then return hither once more. I am the divided spirit of Toyokunihime-no-kami, nay, the Mitama of Itsunome, the Kami-Susa-no-o."

§ 2

As the voice proclaimed these words with refreshing clarity, the Ichidō, unbidden, started and raised their heads to gaze upon the Okaho; beholding the Onkanbase of the Sango moon, which possessed a Kedaka beyond all compare, they again started and lowered their heads in that Setsuna, and from afar they worshipped the Ushirosugata as it moved Shizushizu into the Okuden, following the Bimeu Kotoba, and once more, choked with tears of Kiyōkei, Reihai, and Kanshiya, their voices of Norito were dampened only by tears of joy.

At this moment, the Tenshi Matsuhiko of Izen appeared from Izuko, and Matsuhiko said, 'Yea, Minasama, it was Ketsukō. By the Mei of Ōkamisama, I shall now guide you through a part of the Shinkai. Come, let us go.'

So saying, he descended from the Goten and proceeded with quick strides. The Yonin followed after Matsuhiko. Matsuhiko led the Yonin to a Tohe high tower and, pointing to the scenery in the four directions, provided an explanation for each.

A lake filled with waves of gold and silver surrounded the four sides, and ships of five colors with sails of gold and silver were passing to and fro. Pointing to an island covered in lush pines that appeared to float in the far distance, Matsuhiko said,

'That island was, thirty-eight thousand years ago, a paradise called Ken-on-kyō, which the Nanten-ō guarded. Since the inclination of the earth returned to its former state, the lowlands have all become, as you see, a lake, and only the summits of the high mountains reveal their heads, and it has now become the place of rest for Kuninotokotachi-no-Ōkami. That glittering light is the divine crest of the Jūyō.'

Kotoyoriwake asked, 'Thirty-eight thousand years—from what time do you calculate that number of years?'

Matsuhiko replied, 'It is the number of years calculated from the day Susanoo-no-Ōkami was banished from the Takamagahara of Heaven.'

Kotoyoriwake said, 'Ah, then has it already been several hundred thousand years? How strange and mysterious; it is a matter that does not reach my understanding.'

Matsuhiko said, 'In the Shinkai, there is no time. This, too, is a number of years seen from the perspective of the Genkai. Look, look, Minasama, at the four directions. In the era of the Mikoto’s retirement, the area where those waves drift was entirely beautiful mountains. Also, that small black shadow visible slightly to the east is the summit of the ancient Mount Sinai. Thus, the state of the world has changed completely; the land has become a great lake, and new islands have appeared in various places in the sea.'

Just as he was speaking, fourteen or fifteen birds with beautiful wings aligned and soared to this Tohe tower, resting their wings before the five of them.

§ 3

Upon observation, what had been perceived as a bird was a Miayama, a small Ningen endowed with Hane.

Matsuhiko addressed the Ichidō, saying, 'He is a Tsukaigami who traverses between Tenchi and conveys the Okotoba of the Kamigami.'

'As the Sekai of the Chijiyō has become exceedingly plagued by Ennetsu, all Shinjin of light sin are now to Ijiu to the Mikuni of the Sun.'

'For this purpose, Kūchiu Yūbin has been Kaishi, and thus he serves the role of a Yūbinhaitatsufu from the Sanjitsuseiki of old.'

'If you have Goyō for the Mikuni of the Sun, pray write your Tegami here.'

'This Shinden of Tohe is, as it were, a first-class post office that governs the Ōfuku of Bunshiyo between Ten and Chi.'

Kotoyoriwake replied, 'We are acquainted with the characters of the age of the Kamigami, but in this present age, the characters must surely be greatly altered.'

Matsuhiko answered, 'In this present age, unlike the past, there is no need for brushes, pencils, or fountain pens.'

'If one but traces the seventy-five sounds of the Kototama in the Kūchiu with one's fingertips, the deliveryman shall deliver them forthwith.'

'I shall demonstrate an example; this system of transportation was Kaishi at the beginning of the twenty-first century.'

So saying, he traced the seventy-five sounds of Katakana in the Kūchiu with his right finger, forming a word, and laughed, saying, 'There, the Tegami is written; the age has arrived wherein characters emit language.'

The four inclined their ears, striving to hear the voices of the strange characters; the voices of the characters came forth like music.

According to the text, it read: 'At this moment, Kotoyoriwake-no-Mikoto, Tamahiko, Itsuhiko, and Kusuhiko, the four pillars who convey the teachings of the true Kami to the Takamagahara of the Earth, have arrived.'

'They have had an audience with Kuninotokotachi-no-Ōkami and also with Kamisusanō-no-Ōkami, and are now ascended to the high tower of Tohe, gazing upon the scenery of the four quarters.'

'If there be any Goyō for these persons in the Takamagahara of Ten, pray send an immediate reply; farewell.'

The Tsukaigami rolled up the characters of the Kūchiu, tucked them between his Hane, and flew off toward the heavens like a flash of lightning.

Matsuhiko said, 'A reply shall arrive presently; pray wait a while and gaze upon the scenery of the four quarters.'

The three were astonished and said, 'Pray, Kotoyoriwake-no-Mikoto, this is a strange thing indeed; the world has truly opened up.'

'There was an age when the Ningen of the twentieth century claimed to have reached the zenith of civilization, to have probed the secrets of the Kamikai, and to have conquered the age, but looking at it today, it is truly infantile.'

§ 4

As they were thus speaking, the messenger from Izen descended to this place like a flash of lightning. Then, while handing a reply in the Kūchiu to Matsuhiko, he flew away pointing toward the Tōten as if shooting an arrow. The Bunmen stated:

"We send this reply from the Takaamahara of Ten. As Kotoyoriwakenomikoto and the three others who have just sent their Omaoshiko still have many Shingefu to fulfill in the Genkai, we desire that you return them to the Genkai once. We desire that the Jidai be restored to the antiquity of Sanjifugomannen, and that they be caused to Kiken into the Tanisoko of Kajikatauge. We offer this Gohenji. To Lord Matsuhiko, the administrator of the Otozure of the Takaamahara of the earth."

Thus the Gohenji in Kūchiu Moji emitted a voice and was Shizenly telling the Monogata.

Tamahiko said, "Ah, the future age is magnificent. We would also like to remain in the Shinkai as we are, but ah, must we not complete the hardships of Sanjifugomannen before we can come here? Ah, it cannot be helped. Farewell, Lord Matsuhiko, we shall now take our leave."

Matsuhiko said, "Everyone, please wait for a moment. I shall raise the Kūchiu kōtsūki."

At the very instant he inscribed something in the Kūchiu with his fingertip, four sets of things like the wings of a bird, brilliant with golden radiance, descended to this place from nowhere.

"Come, put these on," he said, and before the words were finished, the golden wings adhered naturally to the shoulders of the four.

The four exclaimed at once, "Ah, this is splendid!" and as they attempted to flap them, their bodies soared higher and higher into the Kūchiu, and with the force of a thousand leagues in a single dash, faster than electromagnetic waves, they proceeded toward the western sky.

The moment they looked down and saw their own physical bodies lying below, they returned to themselves, and looking around, they found they were lying in the Tanisoko of the Kajika River.

When they looked to see what had become of the horses they had ridden, the innocent horses were pulling and eating the green grass by the riverside.

Kotoyoriwake said, "Ah, thinking that my consciousness had faded for a while, I am grateful to have been shown the conditions of the Takaamahara and the state of the world tens of thousands of years hence. This is entirely due to the vast and profound blessings of Kuninotokotachinomikoto and Kamususanonomikoto. Come, let us all perform the purification of our bodies here, recite the Amatsunorito, and proceed with purity to the place of the Great God's retirement."

They purified their bodies, rinsed their mouths, and with the sound of brave hand-clapping, finished reciting the Amatsunorito, then nimbly mounted their horses again; as if celestial steeds were galloping through the sky, they ascended the slope with light bodies, heading straight into the pathless mountain depths of small brushwood.
