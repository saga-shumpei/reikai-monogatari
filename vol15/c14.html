Chapter 14: The Cavern of Wonders [581]

奇の岩窟〔581〕

§ 1

Takakuniwake fell with a heavy thud into the Otoshiana. The woman from Izen came pursuing him once more,

"Ho-ho-ho-ho-ho,"

Thus she laughed, and ere one knew it, she lowered a Kinsen, hooked it upon the jaw of Takakuniwake, and pulled him up with a jerking motion as if drawing a Furitsurube. Takakuniwake, his throat being constricted, was brought up again to the mouth of the former hole.

"Ho-ho-ho-ho-ho, oh, what a pale face."

Takakuniwake: "Eh, again and again, thou art a woman who playeth many an Itazura. For one of the Kayowa female Bunzai to cast a rope upon the jaw of an Araotoko and pull him up is Futodokisenban; thou art an Akumade Zūzū wretch. For one of such appearance, thou art a woman of great strength."

"Oho-ho-ho-ho, the rope of Shifuchiyakushin shall never be released. When the Kami hath set a Miko upon a person and cast the rope, it shall not be loosed; this Shigumi of hooking and returning—surely thy gape shall not close."

"By hooking a hard, hard Kinsen upon a man's Agota, my open mouth hath become stuck; behold, such a mark hath been left."

"The mark upon thy throat is set, but how shalt thou set the mark of my fate?"

"Eh, thou art an Urusa wretch; there is no question of setting a mark or not. Kisama and I are neither Fūfu nor Mikata, nor are we Fugutaiten enemies. If thou meddlest overmuch, it shall not be for the good of the Shiyōrai."

"My dear, is thy Mikata not somewhat mistaken? Thou must proceed upon the path of the Kami Akumade with a firm and sincere heart. To refrain from rash and blind actions and to serve the path of the Kami with a prudent attitude is thy duty."

"Eh, whether the Mikata be different or the enemy be different, thou art clamorous. In truth, what manner of being art thou? Thou art a thing that doth not sit well within my mind."

"I know not if I am a thing that doth not sit well within thy mind, but thou art a thing that falleth into holes. Ho-ho-ho-ho, try for a moment to guess; if thou canst not discern my true identity, how canst thou explore this subterranean cavern? As the first step, I pray thee, perform Saniwa well upon my origin."

"Ah, what a nuisance; wilt thou not confess from thy side, 'I am such-and-such of such-and-such'? If thou doest so, I shall perform Saniwa to determine whether it be right or wrong, truth or falsehood."

"Ho-ho-ho-ho-ho, yet, O Saniwa-worker, O searching Saniwa-worker, O bungling Saniwa-san; canst thou not perform Saniwa unless thou hearest my name? The protection of the Kami for thee is zero."

§ 2

What is this? A Saniha possesses the Kennō of a Saniha; why do you not declare your own name when asking things of others? To say, 'Try and guess who I am,' is like a Tabibito standing at a Jifujigai asking, 'Do you know where I am going?' Where is the Rikutsu in such a thing? Will you not declare your name at once?

Ho ho ho ho, you are a Saniha who asks others, 'Where is the place I am going?' just as one might ask a stranger. How could such a Saniha ever explore the Chitei Gankutsu? Until my Sujiyō is clearly understood, I shall cast a rope and pull you back a thousand or ten thousand times, so be prepared, ho ho.

Ah, 'Shintai kore kiwamaru'—this is exactly that state; it is as if I have thrust my foot into a Torimochioke, and I am troubled. Hey, hey, woman, would it not be better to cease this Shiyare? If you obstruct the Gokeirin of the Shinkai, it shall not be to your benefit. How is it? Shall I report your sins to the Shinkai?

No matter what you say, I shall not let you go; nay, I shall not let you move even an inch. If you wish to move, then try to move, ho ho ho ho.

Ah, there is no help for it. Nay, I know not which Kami you may be, but I have reached the end of my endurance. I pray you, despite the many discourtesies and offenses I have committed, look upon me with your vast and profound Ōmikokoro, hear me anew, and grant me your pardon. Even the high-nosed Takakuniwake is ruined in such a state; this nose is not that of Takatoriwake of Tokoyonokuni, but it is utterly flattened.

Ho ho ho ho, this nose, this nose—Master Hikunikuniwake of the Busaikuya-hime, have you come to an understanding?

Oh, you have said it, you have said it; nay, I have truly come to a deep understanding.

Then, try and guess.

Well, well, you are a woman after all. You are a strange and incomprehensible Megami.

What is my name?

Come, I know not what to call you; I have no understanding at all. Perhaps you are the Kami of Jupiter.

The woman shook her head from side to side, 'Wrong, wrong.'

Then, you are the sister of the Kami of Jupiter.

'Wrong, wrong.'

Are you the Megami of Apollo, or the Megami of Athena? Ah, this is a matter that brings no understanding.

You are truly an 'Aporo' (foolish) Otokogami yourself, o ho ho ho.

Ah, when it comes to this, the Saniha of the world is no ordinary being.

§ 3

"Indeed, a sudden Shinjin and a sudden Saniha such as you would surely not discern the Shiyōtai of this Megami."

So saying, she tapped her nose lightly. Takakuniwake instantly prostrated himself upon the Daichi, and spoke:

"This is, indeed, a most grievous Goburei. Nay, as the Baikuwa meets the Shiyunyō and blooms forth in an instant, even as the Ranman Ōkuwa, the darkness of my heart has been dispelled. Anata are the Mikoto of Konohanahime who dwells upon Tenkyōzan."

With these words, he joined his Riyōte and performed Haiki. Suddenly, Bimeu Ongaku was heard in the Kūchiu, and as the Fukuiku scent of Baikuwa pressed upon the nostrils, in the twinkling of an eye, by some mysterious means, the form of the Megami vanished like smoke, and snow began to flicker, mingled with the wind blowing across the Noji.

At this moment, a man appeared upon the scene, breathing heavily, with a Nejihachimaki bound about his head and his sleeves tied with a Jifumonji sash; he lowered his head firmly to the Daichi and said:

"I pray you, I pray you, Kamisama Takakuniwake, a noble personage who is Gotōriu at the house of Kanan has commanded that a great calamity has suddenly occurred, and that I must summon you, O noble deity. Come, follow me and return with all haste. A most terrible thing has come to pass."

"What? Has the Mikoto of Susanoo-no-Mikoto commanded me to return? Truly, this is a matter I cannot comprehend."

So saying, he folded his arms and remained seated in meditation upon the small hill, lost in thought. From beneath the earth, a voice, whose owner was unknown, cried out:

"Yea, yea, Takakuniwake, why do you linger in such hesitation? Will you not enter the cavern beneath the earth with haste? A great catastrophe has broken out within the cavern. To save men is the duty of the Sendenshi; will you stand by and watch dozens of living spirits perish?"

The man cried out:

"I pray you, Takakuniwake-san, it is the command of the Ōkami. Come, come, I beseech you to come quickly. The command of the Kamisama is to return with all possible speed, and he has spoken sternly that if you linger, the bond between master and servant shall be severed. Come, I pray you, come quickly."

From the cavern beneath the earth came the cry:

"Yea, Takakuniwake, is it your intention to let dozens of living spirits perish? How do you conceive of the divine calling of a Sendenshi? It is a matter of life and death; enter the cavern with all speed and save the lives of the people."

"I pray you, I beseech you, return with all haste; it is a matter of great importance to the Ōkami."

"What is this matter of great importance to the Ōkami that has suddenly occurred? Relate it to me in detail."

The man replied:

"The Ōkami is surrounded tenfold and twentyfold by hundreds of Majin of the Uranai sect; the many are too strong for the few, and his person is in peril at every moment. Return with all haste, I beseech you; if the time is lost, it will be a great disaster. Do not, I pray you, hesitate or waver, lest you leave behind a regret that will cause you to bite your own navel."

§ 4

From the cavern of the underworld, a voice cried out: 'Ho, Takakuniwake, is it thy intent to leave these scores of living spirits to their doom? Come hither with haste and save their pitiful lives.'

Takakuniwake, caught between the Shimegi of both sides, heaved a sigh, for he was lost in tears, knowing not what course to take nor how to act. Caught in the straits between loyalty and benevolence, Takakuniwake found that if he would be loyal, he could not be benevolent, and if he would be benevolent, he could not be loyal; his heart was suspended in the void, and he remained immersed in deep contemplation with his hands clasped together.

From the cavern of the underworld, the sounds of Abikyōkuwan grew ever more fierce, striking his ears as if they were close at hand.

'Ah, what shall I do? If I uphold the one, the other falls; if I uphold the other, the one falls; if I would uphold both, my own self cannot stand,' he lamented.

No sooner had he taken up the double-edged sword and bared his chest, than he sought to thrust it deep into his left side; at that very moment, the Goddess who had appeared before suddenly manifested in this place, and instantly restrained the right hand of Takakuniwake, holding it fast so that he could not move. As Takakuniwake struggled and shook his hand free, attempting once more to plunge the double-edged sword into his own side, the Goddess of before leaped upon him like a bird in flight, swiftly snatched away the double-edged sword, and spoke in a solemn voice:

'Thou standest upon the watershed of loyalty and benevolence, and being lost in thy decision, thou hast now sought to slay thyself, which is the height of folly; first, compose thy heart. The Great Ōkami Susanoo-no-Mikoto abides in peace. To test thy sincerity, Konohana-hime-no-Mikoto transformed herself into the man who greeted thee, a divine work intended to make thee firm in thy resolve. Since Susanoo-no-Mikoto possesses divine powers of miraculous transformation, there is no need to trouble thy mind; hasten at once to the cavern of the underworld and save the many living spirits who are being tormented by the evil gods.'

As these words ended, the form of the Goddess and the shadow of the man vanished like smoke. Takakuniwake leaped toward the cavern of the underworld and sprang inside. The cavern was unexpectedly vast, extending forward for countless chō. Takakuniwake quickened his pace and, singing a divine song, proceeded ever onward.
