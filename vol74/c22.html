Chapter 22: Heaven and Earth は曇る [1890]

天地は曇る〔1890〕

§ 1

Here Ikuyohime no Kami had been guided together with Akitsuo no Kami, but feeling that the countenance of Tamanohime no Kami somehow lacked beauty, she concealed herself in the shade of a pine tree, lamented her own foolishness, wept bitterly, and while secretly offering words of apology to the Lord Ōkami, she sang, chanting thus. Her miuta:

'How pitiful am I, even I, who having climbed to the sugadoko, My tamashihi trembles and shudders in fright. Is it that I fail to please the ōmikokoro of the Lord Kami, That my tamashihi trembles within? The sorrow of the heart of Tamanohime no Kami, Knowing it, how can I endure this anguish? I am indeed a strange megami who has troubled The heart of the Mizumitama Kami. Enveloped in strange clouds of what they call love, Have I thus obstructed the kamuwaza? If it be so, there is no place for my body in ametsuchi; Grant me your forgiveness, Lord Ōkami. I have come to yearn for the kamiyama of Manazuru, With the desire to vanish and turn into smoke. Was I a magakami who violated the kamuwaza, Conveyed in the divine words of Tamanohime no Kami? Wondering how I might sweep away this sin and defilement, Though I think, it is in vain; a miko is conceived. The Mizumitama casts not even a single glance, and leaves me behind, Advancing before the mimahe, how fearful it is.

With my murakimo heart rebuked by the Kami, I feel so ashamed that I even think of dying; The dread of polluting the sugadoko— The path I walk has truly been closed off. Resolved never to love, down to the very end of yorozuyo, I have awakened to this, coming to the sugadoko. As the fire in my breast burns on, the way of ametsuchi I have stepped off, and become a body of sin. Was she not a miko destined to give birth nobly? Thinking thus, I am sad; heavy is this body of mine. Conceived without even touching His hands, this mortal shell— I ponder the strangeness of my tamashihi. Without a single time-pillow, how heartless it is; The agony of today's psychological pregnancy. My sighs congeal to form clouds, Covering and hiding the sun and moon of the osora. Alas for me, who lack the kototama to confess In the presence of the Lord Kami. Neither the sunbeams crossing the heaven nor the face of the moon Can I bear to look upon with awe.

When I reflect calmly upon it, the terror of my sin— My tama no o is about to snap. Though the life of my tama no o may well perish, How could the heart that yearns for Kimi ever be lost? My endless yearning—while in ametsuchi I continue to pine for Kimi, the sole pillar. And the Kimi I yearn for, heartlessly led away By Tamanohime, enters the inner chambers. In this heavenly realm where good and evil intermingle, I have no way to untangle my disheveled hair. Guided to the sugadoko of the Tamano Hills, It is sad to encounter such lamentation. I watch the receding figure of the Mizumitama Emerging with Tamanohime, and weep as I see them off. Since there is no shadow of the Kami by my side, all alone, Without holding back, I shall weep my fill. Though the evergreen pines flourish and the white plums Fragrance the air, I am sorrowful and lonely. All alone on the hill of purest white sand, Lamenting the world, I shed my tears.

Thinking of how to wipe away today's hardship, When I ponder it, I only grow sadder still. Repenting that I disobeyed the summons of the Lord Kami And defiled the heart of the Mizumitama, My love, which proved to be a fleeting, fruitless blossom— Conceiving a miko born of such lamentation, Could I ever have imagined being guided to this sugadoko, And lurking in the shade of the pine to weep?'

Just as she was chanting thus, there appeared a kami-man who, waving his ohonusa to left and right, drew near with the crunching sound of his footsteps upon the white sand of the garden. He drew leisurely close to the shade of the pine where Ikuyohime no Kami lay hidden, and once more waving his ohonusa to left and right,

'You who hide in the shade of the evergreen pine, Ikuyohime no Kami, take courage. For I am none other than Chikara-Mitsuo no Kami, Who have hurried here to welcome you. Think of whatever happens as the will of the Kami; Cease your lamentations and take courage. Whatever hardships may befall you, though I know them not, This sugadoko is a land of joy. Upon this hill, which knows neither sorrow nor hardship, Take courage, Ikuyohime no Kami.'

Ikuyohime no Kami chanted her miuta in response:

'How grateful I am for your precious words, Kimi, Yet my sorrow has only grown the more. Upon this pure hill where the Lord Kami sends down His heaven, I weep in fear of my defiled body.'

斯く歌ひ給ふ折しもあれ、大幣を左右左に打振りながら、ザクリザクリと庭の白砂を踏みくだきつつ近寄り給ふ神人あり。生代比女の神の忍ばせる松の樹蔭に悠々近寄り給ひ、大幣を左右左に又もや打振りながら、

『常磐樹の松の樹蔭にしのびます 生代比女神勇み給はれ 吾こそは力充男の神なれば 公迎へむと急ぎ来つるも 何事も神の心と思召せ 歎き止めて勇ませ給へよ 如何ならむ艱みおはすか知らねども この聖所は喜びの国土よ 悲しみも艱みも知らぬこの丘に 勇ませ給へ生代比女の神』

生代比女の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『有難し貴き公の言の葉に 吾は悲しさ弥まさりける 主の神の天降らすこれの清丘に 汚れある身の恐しさに泣く』

§ 2

The Kami Chikaramichio chants a Miuta:

'Though I know not what presence dwells here,

I am the Kami Chikaramichio who shall lend you strength.

The moon crossing the heavens is indeed but a shadow of ruten,

Grieve not, since it is Kamunagara.

Though a body burdened with sin and defilement howsoever hastens,

Can it attain to climb this Sugado?

The lord who possesses the strength to make you climb the Sugado

Has not even a speck of dust of sin and defilement.

Come now, set your heart's steed straight,

And perform your misogi in the spring of jewels.

By the Mikokoro of the Lord Kami, I

Have come in haste now to welcome you.'

『何事のおはしますかは知らねども 力を添へむ充男の神吾は 天渡る月も流転の影ぞかし 歎き給ひそ惟神なれば 罪穢ある身は如何に急るとも この聖所にのぼり得べきや 聖所にのぼらす力おはす公は 罪穢なぞ塵ほどもなし いざさらば心の駒を立て直し 玉の泉に禊給はれ 主の神の御心によりて吾は今 公迎へむと急ぎ来しはや』

生代比女の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『有難し力充男の神の宣 わが魂線はよみがへりたり 死なましとひたに思ひしわが心 公の神言によみがへりぬる 愛善の天津神国に生れ合ひて 歎きに沈みし愚さを思ふ わが心ひがみたりけむ玉野比女の 御顔を畏れちぢみつ』

生代比女の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

力充男の神は、大幣を打ち振り打ち振り老松の蔭に展開せる、玉泉の汀に導き給ひつつ、御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『高天原に生れませる 清けき清水真清水に 朝な夕なを浮びます 月の鏡の弥清く 弥さやさやに照りはえて 紫微天界の神国に 恵の露を降らせまし 百の草木をはごくみて 永久に生かせる真清水清水 斯かる聖所に導かれ わが魂線を洗へよと 宣らせ給ひし有難さ この真清水や主の神の 潔き清しき御心の鏡かも この真寸鏡真寸鏡 玉の真清水うまし水 清しき水よ玉の緒の 生命保たす生き水よ 生ける御神の霊線の 恵の露のしたたりか

§ 3

If I worship this Tamaizumi, / my bodily soul is also moistened, / and has become youthful indeed, / O living waters, O pure waters of Inochi!'

Just as Her Majesty finished chanting this Miuta and solemnly pronounced the Amatsunorito in a clear voice, who should appear here in haste but Machiahasehiko-no-Kami, who had previously served the Mizu no Mitama.

Machiahasehiko-no-Kami chanted a Miuta.

Since the form of Ikuyohime-no-Kami is not here, / I have thoughtfully come to welcome you; / as you are a public person who has served the divine work of Kamiumi, / quickly join our company. / Come now, I shall guide you, make haste, / for the supreme Ōkami is also awaiting you.'

Ikuyohime-no-Kami, filled with unexpected joy and feeling as though she had been brought back to life, chanted a Miuta.

Rather than saying how grateful and unworthy I feel, / I have no words to reply. / Come now, I shall serve before the Mimahe, / floating the Tsukihi upon a pure heart.'

Here, guided by Machiahasehiko-no-Kami and guarded by Chikaramichio-no-Kami, Ikuyohime-no-Kami proceeds quietly, treading heavily upon the white sand of the Hironiha, to advance toward the Ōmiyai.
