Chapter 7: 相聞の Gloom [1875]

相聞の闇〔1875〕

§ 1

Akitsuo no Kami and the Momogamitachi stood upon the summit of Manazuruyama, chanting the Ikukototama in unison, and turned toward the eastern-northeast sky to solemnly pronounce the seventy-five voices of the Kototama. Thereupon, Manazuruyama expanded and spread further and further toward the true north. Thereafter, the Momogamitachi chanted the Ikukototama tirelessly and with all their might for seven days and seven nights toward the north, north-northeast, northeast, east, southeast, east-southeast, south, southwest, west-southwest, west, northwest, and north-northwest. As a result, Manazuruyama expanded and spread in all eight directions, swelling up until it reached the limits of eyesight. Through the expansion of Manazuruyama, the moisture gradually departed from the vast wilderness spanning thousands of ri in all directions, and the earth solidified. Thus, the auspicious land of Manazurukoku wonderfully, marvelously, and elaborately came into being.

The broad marsh enveloping the mountains That continued from this wilderness Gathered into a single place in the southwest, Deep with water, And the marsh spread wide and clear. Around the marsh, evergreen Pines suddenly grew tall, While ever-fragrant white plums Adorned the water's edge, And the auspicious form of the elder flowers, Releasing radiance, was reflected Even to the bottom of the clear marsh, Until it became like a mirror.

Akitsuo no Kami, beholding this state, offered a divine poem.

The seventy-five voices of the Ikukototama spoken by the Lord God Have caused the land to expand. First turning toward the sky of the true north, The Kototama thus chanted caused the land to be born. Next, harmonizing water and fire with the Momogamitachi, The Kototama thus chanted caused the earth to spread. As far as my eyes could not reach, the damp earth Solidified and became a good land. Now once more, when I realize The merits of the Ikukototama, it is noble, for water and fire have come to life. Water and fire having come to life, as long as life lasts, I shall serve In the divine work of the Lord God together with the Momogamitachi. The heavenly realm is an auspicious land, a beautiful Divine august country, with a pure heart. When I look out, as far as the wilderness extends, purple Flowers are fragrant, and the wind is perfumed. The winds of Shinotobe blow moderately, And the scent of plum blossoms envelops all directions. Through the Kototama of the seventy-odd-five voices, The Shibi heavenly realm goes on opening.

Tōmiuo no Kami chanted a divine poem.

Having come to serve as an attendant to the Miuya no Kami, Ah, I meet with today's auspicious fortune. The Kototama is surely the foundation of the life of water and fire. When it brings forth hundreds of spirits in heaven and earth, Even this youthful plain of the country, through the water and fire Favored by the Kototama, has solidified. A beautiful land, a bustling land, a noble land, The land protected by the Miuya no Kami— Born from the divine water and fire of the god who makes souls prosper, The land of Manazuru is indeed beautiful! The light of the morning sun is pure, and the light Of Tsukuyomi is also clear; a beautiful land. O Ikuyo hime no Kami, born in the land Where the water is pure and the wind is also pure! Ikuyo hime no Kami must have been born By the grace of the water and fire of Manazuruyama. The beautiful land of the lovely Shibi heavenly realm, Manazuruyama is Ikuyo no Himegami, Who together with Ikukohiko shall protect The land of Manazuru.

Kuninakahiko no Kami offered a divine poem.

The form of Manazuruyama solidified through the power of Akitsuo no Kami Is majestic. The mountain of Manazuru expands successively And stands high as the pillar of the country. This mountain is the august pillar of the country and the pivot of the world; Gather here, oh deities of the four quarters!

Ikuyo hime no Kami chanted a divine poem.

For how many years did I hide in the earth and wait, And now Manazuruyama has appeared in the world! Awe-struck by the divine words of the Lord God, for a long time I waited for your coming, my lord. Wishing to serve in the noble divine work of the divine land, There is worth in waiting, for you have come, my lord.

Akitsuo no Kami chanted:

You indeed are a fine and wise woman, but after all, You are not the eighty himegami. Meeting with the eighty himegami, I am the official Who serves in the divine work of creating gods. Without the permission of the Lord God, toward Ikuyo hime, I have no way to meet her. You must grant me permission for the work of creating gods; Unless the Great Lord God commands it, Even if you are a fine woman in vain, I should not meet you, For the eighty himegami are waiting for me.

Ikuyo hime no Kami chanted:

生代比女の神は歌ひ給ふ。

§ 2

'How pitiful, the words of the sacred Mizumitama! Hearing them, I feel such sorrow that I wish to die. Rejoicing in the living spirit of the words, the Ikukototama, Have I indeed emerged from the earth? Appearing upon the peak of Mount Manazuru, I never thought such grief would come to pass. Though I lament, the Mizumitama behind the thousand-layered rock Will not move for my Kototama.'

Having sung thus, Ikuyohime no Kami concealed her divine form with clouds and mist in an instant, and black smoke billowed turbulently from the mountain base in a terrifying manner. Suddenly the summit was enveloped in black clouds where even an inch before one's eyes could not be discerned. At this, Tatakumi no Kami, wishing to disperse the dense clouds, chanted a divine poem:

'Hear ye the Kototama of Tatakumi no Kami! O Shinadohikogami and Shinadohimegami, By the power of Tar-tar-tar-tata over this mountain, Disperse the clouds, O Shinadobe no Kami! Has the sorrow and fire-and-water of Ikuyohime congealed To become black clouds and wrap around the mountain? Ah, terrifying things are love and the heart! Has Ikuyohime truly become an ogre? Though she proclaimed her Kototama ever more loudly, How vexing that black clouds still surge forth.'

The living Kototama of Tatakumi no Kami had no effect whatsoever; even the wind of Shinado failed to blow forth, and the hundred deities stood silently upon the mountain peak, each and every one chanting the heavenly norito and awaiting the opening and clearing of heaven and earth.

Here Akitsuo no Kami, with solemn dignity, chanted a divine poem:

'It is not that I am unable to fathom The tender heart of Ikuyohime no Kami. Nevertheless, if it be not in accordance With the divine decree of the Lord Ōkami, what can I do, having no recourse? Soften this innermost heart of mine and Disperse the black clouds, according to the will of Kami. Since I too am one not made of wood and stone, How could I cast thee aside, O delicate maiden? Though I think of thee with tender affection, fierce longing, and yearning, Have pity on me who have no means to act. As I gaze upon thy form, my heart burns intensely, Yet I have resolutely endured it within the Mizumitama.'

Even as he sang thus, from within the black clouds came the voice of Ikuyohime no Kami:

'O the bitter Kototama of my lord! Now I shall become The master of the marsh and yearn after you. The divine work of creating the land and bearing August Children, I shall resent and obstruct for eternity. The eighty himes and Tamano hime are hiding In the groves of Lake Tamano, awaiting your imperial progress. While destroying the divine form of Tamano hime, I shall torment the heart of my lord, I think. The clouds wrapping around Mount Manazuru are the burning thoughts Of my love, which shall never clear away for eternity. Is my lord the one who casts aside my true heart That yearns for him, and meets instead with Tamano no Hime? Since I long for my lord no matter where, I shall now Return my resentment upon Tamano no Hime! In an instant, the lovely face of Tamano no Hime Shall become an ugly hag, and she shall lament.'

Scarcely had she finished chanting thus when she suddenly transformed into a fierce dragon, piercing the curtain of black clouds, flashing brilliant lights, and dashing forth like an arrow toward the waters of Lake Tamano, how wretched it was. Tsuburayahiko no Kami, witnessing this behavior, grew wrathful and chanted:

'Ikuyohime no Kami has been led astray by a crooked deity And seeks to obstruct the Mizumitama. As long as there is life in Tsuburayahiko no Kami, I shall surely subdue her with the arrow of Kototama! Clouding the bright Mount Manazuru, Pitiable is the ugly deity who lurked within the lake.'

Kunitsunakuhiko no Kami chanted:

'I never thought that a crooked deity would run amok In the country fortified by the deity of Mount Manazuru. Come then, by the radiance of the living Kototama, I shall dispel and illuminate the pitch-black darkness. I feel pity for the heart of Ikuyohime no Kami, Who treads a path that is not the true way.'

Minamashihiko no Kami chanted a divine poem:

'Alas, the robe of love of Ikuyohime no Kami Is torn to shreds, with no means to mend it, indeed. In order to pacify the heart of the Mizumitama hime, Pray proclaim the living Kototama! This land-plain, where reeds grow lush and thick, Is an age of the gods where the reeds have not yet settled. Let us forget the distinctions of reeds for a while, And meet face to face with Ikuyohime. Other than my lord, there can be no divine pillar To soothe the wrath of Ikuyohime no Kami.'

Here Akitsuo no Kami, melted by the affection of Ikuyohime no Kami, softened his taut heart and chanted a divine poem:

'Be reeds good or bad, let them be as they may, come what may, Let me meet with Ikuyohime no Kami! O Ikuyohime no Kami, calm thyself, And dispel the heart, clear away the clouds! Though I think this goes against the divine decree, I have no way to cast aside your affection.'

When he sang thus, the pitch-black gathering clouds that had hitherto enveloped the four quarters cleared away as if wiped clean, the azure sky suddenly appeared, the sweet fragrance of white plum blossoms drifted through all directions, and wondrously, it instantly became a state of the heavenly realm.

斯く歌ひ給へば、今迄四辺を包みたる烏羽玉の黒き叢雲は、拭ふが如く晴れ渡り、青雲の空忽ち現れ、白梅の芳香四辺に香り、忽ち天国の状態となりしこそ不思議なれ。
