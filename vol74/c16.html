Chapter 16: The Episode of Truth 言の力 (Part I) [1884]

真言の力（一）〔1884〕

§ 1

The water of Tamanōmi parted in twain, and from the depths a great dragon raised its head. Upon its head stood silently a graceful and ravishing Megami, clearing the path before the party of Akitsuo no Kami. In the twinkling of an eye, they crossed the vast surface of the lake to the opposite shore. The Megami standing atop the dragon's head was none other than the sacred figure of Ikuyohime no Kami. Herein, Ikuyohime no Kami was won over by the profound heart of Akitsuo no Kami, and her feelings of resentment instantly turned into gratitude, transforming into joy and delight, as she was reborn a Megami even more beautiful in appearance than before.

Herein, Akitsuo no Kami was so enraptured by the valiant, gentle, and beautiful divine figure of Ikuyohime no Kami that his very heart and soul were almost carried away. His feeling of deep reverence was unstoppable, but Ikuyohime no Kami quickly perceived his divine heart and, unable to restrain her emotion of complete fulfillment, her joy suddenly condensed and she conceived a divine child within her body. The flames that had burned until now vanished like clouds dispersing, and the sun shone brilliantly across the sky. The moon felt as though it were pouring cool, refreshing air over her entire body, and she was completely liberated. As she ascended the bank, the dragon's body instantly vanished like foam upon the lake's surface, the setting moon cast diagonal light upon the water's surface, and the scene of perfect peace and tranquility became beyond all comparison. Thereupon, Ikuyohime no Kami chanted a divine poem:

'Ah, truly won over by the sincere heart of Kimi, My clouds of love have vanished away! With my whole heart fully satisfied, eternally I am reborn through the true words of Kimi. Thinking I would harbor resentment to the very end, How wondrously I now gaze upon Kimi with reverence! Without crossing or confusing water and fire, with a heart of deep emotion, Within the heart of Kimi, a divine child has been conceived! My heart that yearns for Kimi has condensed to become a divine child, And has been lodged within my womb! From now on, I shall dry up the waters of Tamanōmi, And build up and solidify this land of youth. Forever upon the youthful plains of the true crane, I shall solidify the ground and raise the divine child, I trow. Appearing as the divine spirit of the mountain of the true crane, I shall lend my full strength to you, the deity of land-birth, Tamanohime, who dwells in tranquility within this grove! From this day forth, putting straight the steed of my heart, I shall assist in the divine work of Kimi and Hime. Helped by the living Kototama of the kami, I have indeed been reborn from my serpent form!'

Akitsuo no Kami stood upon the lake shore and, rejoicing that the clouds upon the heart of Ikuyohime no Kami had cleared, chanted a divine poem:

'How awe-inspiring is the majesty of the Kototama! The light of a sincere heart Is precious indeed, for it has saved Hime. Though I performed no special deeds of my own, Verily, the divine child was conceived through a sincere heart! May the child grow as pure as this lake, And as prosperous as the grove of Tamano! Even the dark clouds that welled up upon the mountain of the true crane Have cleared away; how happy is this eve! As the sky of dawn grows faintly bright, The sun has risen in the sky of the heart. Tamanohime, who serves in the divine work of land-birth, I had mistakenly thought to be the deity of deity-birth! Ikuyohime is a deity born from Mount Manazuru; When I reflect upon it, was she not the divine medium sent forth? Though there was no strange mind, though no pledge was exchanged, The divine child of the grand enterprise has been conceived! Now I have come to unravel and attain the endless thread Of the grand enterprise of the Lord God! Without the permission of the Lord God, how could An immaculate conception ever have come to pass? Foolish indeed was I, thinking that the work Of bearing divine children was but a single path! From this day forth, for a while within the grove of Tamano, I shall remain in tranquility and solidify the land. Oh deity of the morning sun, rising while parting the purple clouds, Dyeing the lake in crimson! Ikuyohime's heart grew peaceful, she conceived the divine child, And the heavenly sun has risen in all its abundance. Now indeed have I comprehended in full detail The distinction between the divine work of land-birth and deity-birth!'

Tōmiotsu no Kami could not suppress his admiration and chanted a divine poem:

'The light of the heavenly sun floating upon the surface Of Lake Tamano, which I crossed astride the back of my steed! The form of the dragon god suddenly appeared, And how magnificent is Ikuyohime, standing upon its head! Bound by the deep heart of the august divine spirit, Ikuyohime has been reborn! With a heart of emotion and living Kototama, How precious is Hime who has been reborn! How easily she crossed the deep lake surface, eight fathom deep, Borne upon the living Kototama! Swift are the legs of the steed riding over a thousand folds of waves, Crossing while breaking the moonlight into a thousand pieces! The moon-boat of the grand sky turns pale in the west, While the sun has risen in the eastern sky! The shadow of the morning sun rising and scattering The eternal darkness that enshrouded Mount Manazuru! Today, serving as an attendant to the birth of the land, I have comprehended the profound depths of the divine grand enterprise. My heart, which had wondered why Tamanohime did not come forth to greet us, Has now begun to thaw at last!'

§ 2

Without knowing the distinction between the birth of the land and the birth of the gods, / Alas, we merely suffered in our anxiety! / Faintly mists rise over Tamanōmi, / And the waves have calmed down in peace.

Ikuyohime's flames of resentment burned high, / Oh, how dreadful was that night when the waves raged like churning! / Even the lake surface, where waves raged and winds blew wildly, / Today is tranquil and floats the heavenly sun.

Though thick mists rise, the heavenly sun's / Light is not blocked, and the face of the lake is bright.

I too shall realize the will of the Lord Ōkami, / And help Kimi.

Even I, who rejected Ikuyohime's gracious will, / Have indeed become guilty.

Nothing can obstruct the light of Magokoro, / Difficult though it was not to be caught in a small heart.

On the back of Hayakoma, Asatsuhi shines, / Competing in color with the blue of Kosui.

When I looked at Shirokoma on the kishibe as well, it was blue; / From today I shall name myself Aokoma.

Maruyahiko no Kami chants a miuta.

Wazahahi has vanished without a trace, and upon Ametsuchi / The sun has shone, the moon has set. / Mizumitama, the moon's heart too must have grown calm, / Hidden away in the distant Ōno.

Coming here, I have realized the shigumi of the Kami; / In a pacified heart, a miko has been conceived.

Tamanomori, where evergreen trees grow lush and dense, / Was indeed the august figure of the Lord Ōkami.

Within the clear, pure Ametsuchi, the deep green / Color shines vibrantly in Tamanomori. / That which is fearsome is the heart called love— / This I have realized for the first time.

In the heart called love, Kami are born as well; / Oni and great serpents are also brought forth.

It is a world where good and ill pass to and fro; today I / Have seen the state of the world in Ikuyohime.

Both reeds and rushes grow thick in Wakakunohara; / When I think of it, what distinction is there?

To call something good or to name it bad is simply / The movement of the heart's horse of the Kamis.

Hard teeth precede the soft tongue, / It is a world where there are precedents of perishing. / Hard trees are knocked down by the wind, while the soft / Willow stands just as before.

The figure of the willow growing at the water's edge of Tamanōmi, / Swaying in the wind, how graceful it is.

Though the old pine of the evergreen trees is thick, / It is an imperial reign when it falls in the wind.

The willow that sways even in the softly blowing wind, / An imperial reign where it does not fall for endless ages.

Born within Ametsuchi, with a narrow heart, / Today I have gazed upon my single-minded self.

Tatakumi no Kami chants a miuta.

Illuminated by the power of beautiful love, / Ikuyohime has shone brightly. / Just as the sun and moon clear away darkness and shine, / Ikuyohime's breast has cleared.

Mizumitama Kami's divine words and divine works, / I ponder with awe how unusual they are in the world.

Tamanomori, brought forth by the Lord Kami, / Has begun to shine more and more.

The pure heart of the Himegami who dwells tranquil in this forest / Is seen in the pine.

It is a morning when the refreshing wind of Todokaze / Comes bearing the fragrance of white plum blossoms.

The way the refreshing treetops of the willow growing in a row along the water's edge stroke the lake surface, / While the roots of the willow are sealed and the leaves of the reeds and rushes sway verdantly in the wind,

It is a spectacle of the heavenly kingdom; the plum blossoms fragrant, / And the form of the cranes flying back and forth across the lake surface, / With white swans floating their wings on the waves, / Calmly playing in the morning lake plain.

Last night's storm has cleared away without a trace, and the state / Of the heavenly kingdom is vividly reflected this morning.

Mizumitama is vastly present here, / The merit of creating the land is noble.

How fearsome are the thoughts of love, / Clouded to the point where the efficacy of Kototama is of no avail!

I have realized the greatness of the power called love; / Both oni and great serpents have hidden their shadows.

The mountain ridge of the true cranes, faintly hazy in the distant field, / Shone in the sun.

Ikuyohime, born in the mountain of the true cranes, / Has appeared as the heart of the Kami grew pacified.

The distant Mount Tsururu, to which I whipped my horse and advanced throughout the day, has cleared.

From the cloudiness of Ikuyohime's heart, / Mount Tsururu must have grown hazy.

Ureshishi no Kami chants a miuta.

The waves of Tamanōmi have grown calm, and the lake shimmering with the heavenly sun / Must be the Mizumitama.

The Tamanōmi, deep, wide, purely clear, / Must be the heart of the Mizuo-no-Mitama.

The midnight storm has grown calm, and the heavenly sun rises; / When I look at the dawn sky, how joyous it is!

Ikuyohime must be joyful, Mizumitama / Must be pure, now that she has conceived a miko.

How admirable is the Himegami who changed her form to that of a dragon / And served at the head of my Kimi!

My horse trampled through the waves with a resounding rumble, / Advancing as if traveling across the earth.

Even the deep lake surface, by the august majesty of Kototama, / The horse crossed over with absolute ease.

Since it is a horse born from the water and fire of Kototama, / Crossing over the tops of the waves must have been easy.

I too am born in the Kototama of U; / Since I am a Kami, my body will not be heavy.

Mizumitama was born in the sound of A, and I, born in the sound of / U, am a Kami of joy.

Ruling over the joyful things of this world, / I think I shall bring happiness down to the end of myriad generations.

If one does not have a joyful heart, then whatever the matter, / It cannot be accomplished intricately and thoroughly.

The joy of this morning, when the midnight storm has calmed, / I think I shall transmit for eternity.

Minashihiko no Kami chants a miuta.

Though there is no bridge on the lake, my soul / Has crossed over in peace, protecting Kimi.

To cross the tips of the waves guided by the dragon god / Is also the meritorious deed of Minashihiko no Kami.

To the achievements of carrying through the divine work, / I, Minashihiko no Kami, shall serve.

No matter what difficulties I encounter, as long as Minashihiko / No Kami exists, there is nothing to distress over.

Serving before the august front, I shall protect / The noble divine work of Mizumitama.

In mountains, in seas, in rivers, in valleys, I indeed / Shall become Minashihiko and protect the path ahead.

If not for the water and fire of Minashihiko's Kototama, / How could Wakakunohara ever flourish?
