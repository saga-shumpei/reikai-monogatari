§ 2

The up-gazed sky is purely clear and bright, And the earth is a paradise where green grasses sprout. To serve in such divine works in such an age Is truly the blessed fortune bestowed by Kami.

Though the Kuni is yet youthful, the shadows of the sun and moon Crossing the heavens are bright and clear. Now the wondrous Miko dwells within the sacred womb, How noble to stand as the pillars of the Kuni. Guarding forever the life of the precious Miko, I shall humbly serve throughout eternity.

Filled and overflowing in mountains and fields, the grace-filled dews of the Lord Kami Cause the Kamitachi to flourish.'

Musubiahase no Kami chants the Miuta.

'The Hisakata Amatsutakamiya clears away, And the Kototama of Su resounds. The pure water and fire of Kototama are bound together, And how clear is the born land of Manazuru. Because it is the Aizen Tenkai, even the Yoarashi Is blown to a stop by the living Kototama. The heart of the Himegami deeply enveloped by Kohigumo Clears up in the living Kototama. If Amatsumakoto does not dwell within, the water and fire Of the Kototama grow cloudy and even the dew fails to shine. Temporary soothing words cannot match The water and fire of Hisakata Amatsumakoto. Striking down the soothing words of Mizumitama, It has brought Hime to life in Amatsumakoto. In a Tenkai without even a hair's breadth of falsehood, Words of immediate evasion find no response. Before my eyes I have seen the power and worshipped the light Of the Kototama of Makoto. It is the way of prosperity where Hisakata Amatsumakoto and Kunitsumakoto Are bound together.

In the divine land where the Toyosaka of the Amatsuhi ascends, How could soothing words ever be permitted? It is peaceful because it is an age that rectifies the distinctions of good and evil And guides the Kami of Heaven and Earth. I shall become the Kami of water-and-fire and water-and-fire binding, And guard the divine work of birthing the Kuni and birthing Kami.'

Umasumo no Kami chants the Miuta.

'Proclaiming the Kototama of the voice of Su clearly and brightly, I shall pioneer and solidify this youthful Kuni. Revealed as Umasumo no Kami, I now stand In the Mahayuraba of Manazuru no Kuni. In the Mahayuraba and Tamanomori of Manazuru no Kuni, O Goddess who waits, saying you will birth the Kuni. Until even the white pines of Tamanomori Tokiwa are white, The voices of the nesting cranes ring clear. At the mere echo of a single cry of Manazuru, How the myriad birds fall silent. Crossing over upon the back of the night lake's Koma, I think upon the grace of the Lord Ōkami. In the Kunihara brought forth by the Lord Kami, How could there be the roaring of evil and crookedness? With hearts darkened in the depth of their souls, they spawned evil and crookedness, Truly an age of Kami spawning calamities. It is terrifying when the straying water and fire of the Kamitachi gather And pollute the water and fire of Heaven and Earth.'

Makotogenn no Kami chants the Miuta.

'How stern yet majestic is Mizumitama, A brave heart shining as she has conceived the Miko. The Makoto of Ikudai Himegami has appeared, And has harbored the dew of Mizu no Mitama. Shining unto the very end of myriad generations, Could this not be a most auspicious divine noble word? As both grasses and trees bow to the living Kototama, Flowers bloom in full and the Kuni clears away. How fragrant is the Shinato wind blowing gently, A Kuni blooming full with the flowers of Misogi. In the attire of white plum blossoms, I behold Kimi: What a clear prospect of today's lake shore! As far as the eye can see, the vast plain clears away, And Mount Manazuru towers into the sky. By the merits of Kuninakahiko no Kami, how the ever-green trees Of Mount Manazuru must be flourishing!'

Filled with joy and praising the scenery of Heaven and Earth, the retinue of Kamitachi once again mount the backs of the Koma, and proceed valiantly toward the sanctuary in the forest of Tamano not far away.
