Chapter 20: Pine 下の述懐 [1888]

松下の述懐〔1888〕

§ 1

Tohomio no Kami and His party, repenting that they had desecrated the sacred ground from the foot of the hills of Tamano, rushed out of the forest of Tamano in a flurry with the rapid sound of horse hooves, tethered their horses to the row of trees by the roadside, and becoming barefooted, timidly crept back into the forest of Tamano, stood before the pure spring that shone reflecting the komorebi on both sides of the path, each and every one of them chanted the ikukototama, offered up the Amatsunorito, and advanced while composing poems.

The miuta of Tohomio no Kami:

This umashimizu of the forest of Tamano Where the Lord God descends, Is it a mirror of the moon? Now I regret that I desecrated with horse hooves The pure mashimizu which reflects both moon and sun; May this spring wash my tamashihi, Not to mention the defilement of my karatama.

Maruyahiko no Kami composes a miuta:

Reflecting the shade of the tokihagi For toha, Tsukihi gleam in the mashimizu; Just as this water is pure, I will wash my soul clean and serve Kami.

Tatakumi no Kami composes a miuta:

Unawares my tamashihi grew arrogant And looked upon this mashimizu with indifference; How venerable are the spring and mashimizu Along the roadside leading to the Mimahe of the Ōkami! A mizukagami reflecting moon, sun, and stars, When reflected, how unclean is my soul.

Ureshiho no Kami composes a miuta:

Washing and purifying my tamashihi in the mashimizu, How joyful it is to serve in the divine work! Since the age of the gods, brought forth by the Lord Ōkami, How venerable and pure is this sacred forest.

Musubiai no Kami chants a miuta:

Gushing forth as the august spirit of chihayaburu Kami, How pure this mashimizu must be! While looking directly at the pure mirror, How I neglected the rite of misogi! When I think of washing my spirit in the mashimizu And proceeding to the august presence of the Lord God, I am overjoyed.

Minashihiko no Kami composes a miuta:

Reflecting the azure of the clear sky, A spring clear and blue to its very bottom; When I look upon my own reflection, how ashamed I am, Finding no means to meet with Kami.

Umitama no Kami composes a miuta:

Manifested as Umitama no Kami, not knowing That the umimizu was pure, I passed right by; From the time Tamanohime was born, This mashimizu must be the pristine source of birth.

Tamakibari no Kami composes a miuta:

May this sacred forest where the life-giving spring of tamakibari gushes forth Be eternal like Tokoyo; Morning and evening, making the mashimizu Where tsukihi float into a mirror, I will wash my mitama.

Musubiai no Kami composes a miuta:

Even the crystal clarity of the jewel spring Gushing forth by binding fire and water together-- I have now cast my reflection in the jewel spring And felt shame for my impure heart.

Umashuso no Kami composes a miuta:

Gentle water, sweet water, mashimizu reflecting 22The heart of Umashuso no Kami; Is this water dripping from the breasts Of the Lord Ōkami, a precious jewel-water?

Shingongen no Kami composes a miuta:

Could the sweetness of the mashimizu, Gushing forth endlessly in bubbles, Be the very heart of Kami? Riding a white horse and parched of throat, How I failed to know this mashimizu!

The gods of the party, as they wash their mitama by chanting kototamaic poems one by one at the jeweled springs scattered hither and thither, advance with modesty.

Shingongen no Kami, standing at the head, composedly chants a miuta:

Ah, how grateful, how grateful, In the mid-air between heaven and earth, Standing pure, the tokihagi Of the sacred ground in the forest of Tamano; Following the mizunomitama, I mounted my horse and proceeded onward, Unknowing even of disrespectful deeds, To the foot of the mountain where Tamanohime Permanently dwells in tranquility; When I arrived and looked with high spirits, Tamanohime, by the guidance of mizunomitama And the maiden of the raw generation, Remained in silent mystery; When I reflect upon it deeply and thoroughly, We gods of the party, Whose wisdom and enlightenment are still insufficient, Together with the mizunomitama, Unconcernedly desecrated this sacred ground With the hooves of our horses, And while washing our tamashihi In the jeweled spring, Forgot to diligently perform The divine task of misogi, Realizing for the first time with shame That it was a grievous error Of rebelling against the divine will of the Lord Ōkami; With flushed face we turned back, Repenting our past wrongs, slipped out of the forest of Tamano Onto the path by which we originally came, Tethered our horses to the row of trees, And barefooted, stepping crunchingly On the white sand, we advanced; Along our path, the glittering Right and left jeweled springs Gushed forth purely and freshly, Lodging the reflections of tsukihi; As each and every one of us washed our tamashihi In the eternal spring, And traced our way through The white plum-scented sacred forest, The crane's cry above the pines rang out boldly.

§ 2

Pulling upon our tamashihi,

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara,

In the divine miwaza of the deity's descent,

We who serve, morning and evening,

Chant the Amatsunorito,

Perform misogi in the jewel-clear springs,

And maintain the circumspection with which we ought to advance,

Yet through a brief slackening of our hearts,

We have forgotten it, how deplorable indeed!

Little birds sing and butterflies dance,

In the forest of the deities of eternal Tokoyo,

The wind blowing through is fragrant, too,

And the scent of plum blossoms perfuming the yomo,

So pure, must be the mikokoro of the deities.

While enfolded in the precious mikokoro of the deities,

We, in our foolishness,

Could not comprehend it even a little,

And aligning our carriage-poles with daudau stride,

Oh, the foolishness of advancing into the sacred spot

Where the great Ōkami descends!

Renewing our hearts,

Repenting our sins and transgressions,

Once again engaging in the miwaza of misogi,

We trudge along,

Bathing in the komorebi filtering through the pines,

Performing misogi fully in the springs

Gushing forth kanatakonata,

And gradually we have arrived koko,

Yet the destination ahead is still distant,

And though the steeds of our hearts are eager,

How shall our two feet

Be able to reach the sacred spot?

As the tasogare of this divine forest deepens,

Stars sparkle in the misora,

The evening wind grows chilly,

And pervades our skin.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara,

Tonight, under the thick futomiki of a pine,

Let us all rest together,

Wait out the night until the morning sun ascends,

Perform misogi in the springs once more,

And proceed, Kamunagara,

May your august spirits shine and prosper.'

Chanting thus, they advance.

As their shins sank into the white sand of the vast divine forest, making progress anything but easy, the deities chanted the Amatsunorito and spent a night beneath the pines.

Tōmiotoko no Kami chants an august poem:

'Though the darkness of tasogare presses close, the deity Tsukuyomi

Has shone brightly forth in the misora.

Gazing upon the moonlight reflected purely in the true clear spring,

I look upon it and my face burns with shame.

The rustling of the wind-laden treetops has ceased,

And only the cries of cranes are heard on high.

The moonlight shimmering on the dew of the white plum blossoms

Revives my tamashihi.

On this evening when a hundred birds determine their roosts,

Sunk in sin, how can I find sleep?

How sorrowful the august spirit must surely be,

Seeing the clouded state of our tamashihi!

That there is no such divine pillar, will mykimi

Lament morning and evening?

To think that I served in the miwaza morning and evening

Has turned out to be a dream.

Knowing that my wisdom and spiritual discernment are both lacking,

I reflect with shame upon having served without realizing it.'

Maruyahiko no Kami chants an august poem:

'The mirror of the moon traversing heaven illuminates the august spirit of Maruyahiko no Kami and smiles.

When I look upon the face of the moon

Floating upon the waves of the spring as the night grows late, I feel ashamed.

Nurturing the night's crane-child without even sleeping,

Could I be guarding it out of the strength of my love?

The moon of the misora shines upon the dew of the white plum blossoms,

In the midnight of the jewel field forest, fragrant and pure.

The august spirits left behind in the miwaza gather

And now perform misogi anew.

Knowing full well that the heavenly realm is a divine country where no relaxation of spirit is permitted,

Still I grew negligent.

Because my wisdom and spiritual discernment were lacking, alas,

I forgot the essential work of misogi.

I feel ashamed of my foolishness in having thought

That my tamashihi was purified just the same as the august spirit.

How regrettable it is that I have defiled

The mikokoro of kimi, who utters not a single word.

Is Ikugahime's heart pure as she dwells there,

Having been guided to the hills of Tamano?'

Tatazumikumi no Kami chants an august poem:

'As the night grew late, beneath the shade of the ever-verdant pines,

I lamented my transgressions.

Beneath the moss-grown shade of the ever-verdant pines,

I pass the time weeping tears of penitence.

How foolish my tamashihi must be,

Forgetting the essential miwaza and pressing ever onward!

When I look at the moon reflected in the pool of clear spring water,

Does it smile at my foolishness?

I am utterly astonished at myself

For remaining unaware that my tamashihi was clouded.

Though bearing the official title of Tatazumikumi no Kami,

The foolishness of forgetting such miwaza,

Even the echoes of the wind blowing through the treetops

Sound as though they are laughing at my foolishness.

A true crane alights upon the treetop of the pine,

And with a single cry admonishes me now.

Is my tamashihi inferior even to a crane, if it has come to this?

Tatazumikumi no Kami now feels bitter regret.

From this moment on, I will steady the steed of my heart,

Unite my sincerity, and serve the Way.'

Kaku kamigami wa notsuhaika o nobe, kuigo no namida o ubukaimagara, shinonome no sora ni mukatte reihai nagashou shi, futatabi masagaji o soashi ni kizamimagara, tamano oka o sashite osoru osoru susumase tamahinu.

原文（近代文語）

『小夜更けの松の樹蔭にうづくまり 恥ぢらひにつつ月を仰ぐも にこにこと笑ませる月の面見れば わが魂線を抉らるる如し 国土生みと神生みの神の御供して 岐美をなやませし事を恥ぢらふ 瑞御霊わが魂線のくもれるを 見透し給ひて歎きましけむ 御供に仕へまつると雄々しくも 進みしことの恥づかしきかな さりながらわが魂線の穢をば 早く悟りし事の嬉しさ よき事に曲事いつき曲事に よき事いつく神代なりにけり よしあしの差別も知らに進みてし 宇礼志穂吾の浅間しさを思ふ 時じくに白梅かをる神の森を 蹄に汚せしことの畏き 玉泉右と左に湧きてあるを 禊もなさで進みし愚さ』

産玉の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『大空の青海が原を渡りゆく 月読の舟はいとも美し 冴え渡る御空の月に照らされて 吾恥づかしく打ちふるふなり 真清水の永久に湧く神の森を 禊忘れて進みし愚さ 何事も神の心に宣り直し 見直しませよ吾等の過を 神直日大直日の神聞き直し 見直しまして許させ給へ 主の神は玉の宮居にましまして わが愚なる業覧はすらむ 瑞御霊生代の比女の二柱 淋しみまさむ吾等がくもりに 玉野比女の御顔見るも恥づかしく なりにけらしな凡神吾は 主の神のウ声の言霊鳴り鳴りて 生れ出でたる神吾恥づかし 真鶴の山に言霊奏上し しるしなかりしも宜よと思ふ』

魂機張の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『瑞御霊生言霊の功績を 塞ぎまつりし吾恥づかしも 生代比女神の曇れる魂線を 瑞の御霊は生かし給へり 証覚の未だ足らはぬ吾にして 生言霊のしるしあるべき いや広き玉野の森に小夜更けて 月のしたびに悔い心わく 常磐樹の梢御空をかくさずば ただに月見る顔なかるらむ』

美波志比古の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『恥づかしき吾にもあるか大道に 仕へて禊のわざ忘るとは 禊せよと右と左に真清水の 照れる泉を知らず過ぎけり 魂線のいたく曇りて道の辺の 禊の泉も見えざりしはや 禊より尊きものは世にあらじと 吾は常々語らひ居しを わが駒は榛の並木に繋がれて 主を恋ひつつ淋しみ嘶くらむ 駿馬の蹄そろへて真砂地を やうやう進みし愚なる吾よ 知らぬ神に祟なしとは誰かいふ 汚れし御魂に神はまみえず 小夜更けて淋しくなりぬわが心 あまり曇りの深くありせば 真清水に浸し洗へどなかなかに 魂の汚れの清まらぬかな 天津祝詞時じく宣れど如何せむ わが愚なる魂は洗へず』

結比合の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『瑞御霊御供にはろばろ仕へ来て 吾恥づかしき宵にあふかな わが心いゆきつまりて玉野丘の 麓に歎かひ引き返しける 朝夕に生言霊を宣りつつも 禊の神業忘れ居しはや 国土を生み神を生ませる御供なれば 魂を清めて仕ふべき吾 神業の妨げなせしを今更に 悔いつつ泉に魂洗ふかな しんしんと夜は更け渡り真鶴は 漸く声をひそめ眠れり やがて今東の空はしののめて この神森も明るくなるべし 東雲の空ほのぼのとあからみつ わが魂線もよみがへりけり 東の空にわきたつ紫の 雲美しみ神言宣らむ』

真言厳の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『東雲の空おひおひに明らみぬ やがて天津日昇り給はむ 月にさへ恥づかしきものを天津日の 昇り給はばわれ如何にせむ 村肝の心清めて魂洗ひ 新しき日を拝みまつらむ』

斯く神々は述懐歌を述べ、悔悟の涙を浮べながら、東雲の空に向つて礼拝久しうし、再び真砂地を素足にきざみながら、玉野丘を指して畏る畏る進ませ給ひぬ。
