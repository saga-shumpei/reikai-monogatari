Chapter 2: Field 路の Grass 枕 [1870]

野路の草枕〔1870〕

§ 1

Deeply feeling the achievement of Tatakumi no Kami, who with the living Kototama blew away the dark clouds of the evening sky that covered the wild plains extending over a thousand leagues and purified heaven and earth, Akitsuo no Kami chanted a Miuta.

Akitsuo no Kami: 'Aha, aha! By the Kototama of Tatakumi no Kami, The clouds that enclosed heaven and earth have scattered. All things born from the water and fire of the Kototama Will surely vanish away again into the Kototama! O how clear is the evening wilderness, where the ugly clouds That deeply wrapped our path have cleared away! The starlight of the distant Amatsumisora Now shines with the brilliance of gold and silver. Had there not been the achievements of Tatakumi no Kami, The winds of Shinado would never have blown. Serving in my retinue, you, O Kimi, have ever onward Striven to perform noble deeds. The white clouds that wrapped the summit of Mikasayama Now seem to clear away without a trace. The light of the evening moon has gradually appeared, And the dew on the grass blades has begun to shine. The voices of insects gathering on the grass blades as the moon comes out Have surely grown ever more pure and clear! Though the Amatsuhi hides itself, by the light of the Tsukiyomi Kami, the Kusamakura journey is at peace. Repeating the Kusamakura journey, upon the great field, Ah, how we were enveloped in darkness and clouds! When I see the moonlight shining in the distant sky, My spirit grows ever more clear and bright. Even Hinodewake no Kami in this transient world must be gazing At tonight's Tsukiyomi and longing for me.'

Tatakumi no Kami chanted a Miuta.

Tatakumi no Kami: 'How awe-inspiring yet joyful it is to hear The eulogy of the wondrous spirit; Tonight my heart is filled with gladness, As I serve before the august presence and test for the first time The power of the Kototama! Lest clouds and mists should be upon the path where Kimi walks, I have served in His august presence. Even in the heavenly realm created by Kannagara no Kami, How strange it is that clouds should obstruct the way.'

Akitsuo no Kami chanted a Miuta once again.

Akitsuo no Kami: 'The fire in my breast, which strayed from the path of Aizen And turned into longing, must have gone to receive others; Was it that the fire in my breast, the heart longing for Hinodewake no Kami, Overflowed into the sky as clouds and mists? The thoughts of Hinodewake no Kami in this transient world burned the heavens, And my spirit-line covered the earth. Even when the dark clouds, born from the heart of attachment, Gather in throngs and perplex our path, From today I shall tighten the reins of the steed of my heart, And peacefully and smoothly perform the divine work. Though it be called the heavenly realm, the land That is not yet fully born still generates strange clouds and mists; How awe-inspiring it is that the breath of the Shinado no Kami Sweeps away the clouds and mists that envelop the lands on all four sides.'

Chikamio no Kami chanted:

Chikamio no Kami: 'The clouds that enveloped the great field of divine work, Where the wondrous spirit emerges, have cleared away completely. How pleasant even the smiling face of the moon is, Shining deep within the cleared sky! Taking the wondrous spirit, the Tsukiyomi no Kami, as our strength, We shall humbly serve as offerings for the birth of the land. When I look out, steam rises over the great wilderness; The ground is still young, and not even a single tree is to be seen. As far as the eye can see, an overgrown wilderness of grass, Ah, how distant is the path we must tread! Since it is a land born from the divine spirit of the Lord God, We shall advance in peace, even through the wilderness of ugly fiends. On this journey where Tatakumi no Kami accompanies us, What is there to fear in the rising and disturbing clouds? The achievement of Tatakumi no Kami was to bring forth Shinadewakehiko, the god of the wind. The path of creating the land is distant; I now hear The sounds of evening insects in the wilderness. Though the steps of the steed are swift, as the great field Has no end, evening has fallen upon us. When evening comes, in this field where the voices of insects are pure, Like a mirror, the full moon shines brightly. The sky we gaze up at is cloudless, and in the sea of stars, Even the waves are calm as the moon-boat sails. While watching the moon-boat drifting through the heavenly sky, I find myself yearning for the changing world.'

Maruyahiko no Kami chanted a Miuta.

Maruyahiko no Kami: 'Could I ever have imagined that in the ten-thousand-league wilderness I would be illumined by the wondrous spirit and the moon, and listen to insects? The path of the wondrous spirit is distant, O Maruyahiko; I shall serve Him with enduring patience. Prostrating myself in worship before the distant Amatsutakamiya, I shall distantly proclaim good words and beautiful speeches. Though I proclaim good words and beautiful speeches morning and evening, How strange is my soul, which at unseasonable times becomes clouded!'

Thereupon, Kunitsukahiko no Kami chanted a Miuta.

Kunitsukahiko no Kami: 'How awe-inspiring it is to bring forth the Kunitama no Kami In the boundless and vast heavenly realm of Shibi! How I detest the obstructing dark clouds Amidst the beautiful realm of Shibi! The hearts' clouds of the deities have hardened and congealed, And water and fire have grown turbid, turning into clouds. Morning and evening, by loudly proclaiming the severe Kototama, I shall sweep away and purify the dark clouds of the world. I ponder my own good fortune, born in the midst Of a heavenly realm where the mountains are green and the waters pure. Purifying the water and fire of the Kototama, morning and evening I shall guard The entrusted divine work of the Lord God. Serving as an offering this evening, My Kototama has begun to unfold. Through the Kototama of the heavenly true words of the Lord God, This heaven and earth shall flourish ever more abundantly. The Kototama of pure, crimson, and righteous true words Will cause even raging deities to pacify and submit. Serving as an offering to the wondrous spirit deity, Today I have realized the achievement of the Kototama.'

原文（近代文語）

多々久美の神は、御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『はづかしも瑞の御霊の称言 聞けば嬉しも今宵の胸は 御尾前に仕へ奉りて言霊の 力をはじめて試みしはや 岐美が行く道に雲霧あらせじと 吾御尾前に仕へ奉りぬ 惟神神の生みてし天界にも 雲のさやるは怪しきろかも』

顕津男の神、再び御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『愛善の道をはづして恋となりし わが胸の火ゆくもらひにけむ 比女神を恋ふる心の胸の火は 雲霧となりて空にあふれしか 現世比女神の思ひは天を焼き わが霊線は地を覆へり 執着の心ゆ出し黒雲の 群立ちにつつ行手なやますも 今日よりは心の駿馬綱締めて 安らに平らに神業仕へむ 天界といへども未だ生み終へぬ 国土は怪しの雲霧立つも 科戸辺の神の伊吹に四方の国 包める雲霧払ふたふとさ』

近見男の神は歌ひ給ふ。

『瑞御霊出でます神業の大野原を 包みし雲は晴れ渡りけり 晴れ渡る御空の奥にかがやける 月の面のにこやかなるも 瑞御霊月読の神を力にて 国土生みの御供仕へ奉らな 見渡せば大野が原に湯気立ちて 未だ地稚く一樹だにもなし 見の限り草ばうばうの荒野原 分け行く道のはろけくもあるか 主の神の神霊に生れし国土なれば 安く進まむ醜の荒野も 多々久美の神の添ひますこの旅路 雲立ち騒ぐも何おそるべき 多々久美の神の功は風の神 科戸辺比古を生ましめにけり 国土造る道ははろけし吾は今 荒野の夕虫の音聞くなり 駿馬の足は急げど大野原 果しなければ夕さりにけり

夕されば虫の音清しきこの野辺に 鏡の月は満ち照らひたり 仰ぎ見る御空雲なく星の海 浪も静かに月舟の行く 月舟の御空流るるさま見つつ 移り行く世を偲ばれにける』

圓屋比古の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『思ひきや万里の荒野に瑞御霊と 月に照らされ虫を聞くとは 瑞御霊行手はろけし圓屋比古 吾は気永く仕へ奉らな 久方の天津高宮伏し拝み 遥けく宣らむ善言美詞を 善言美詞朝夕を宣りつれど 非時曇るわが魂あやしも』

茲に国中比古の神は御歌詠み給ふ。

『限りなき広けき紫微の天界に 国魂神を生ます畏さ 美しき紫微天界の国中に さやる黒雲わが憎らしも 神々の心の曇り固まりて 水火濁りつつ雲となりつる 朝夕に厳の言霊宣り上げて 世の黒雲を払ひ清めむ 山青く水又清き天界の 中に生れしわが幸思へり 言霊の水火を清めて主の神の 依さしの神業朝夕守らむ 御供に仕へ奉りてこの宵を わが言霊はひらき初めたり 主の神の天津真言の言霊に この天地は弥栄えまさむ 清き赤き正しき真言の言霊は 荒ぶる神も和ぎ伏すなり 瑞御霊神の御供に仕へつつ 今日言霊の功をさとりぬ

§ 2

The achievements of the Kami Tatakumi are indeed the pure and bright power of water and fire through Kototama.'

Kami Ureshiho sings thus:

'The sky of the trials of the Kusamakura journey has cleared; thinking of this twilight as a field-plain where joy fills heaven and earth, I am Ureshiho no Kami. Awe-struck by the divine words of the Lord Kami, and serving the Kami with the Mizumitama, I am Ureshiho no Kami. The joy of Ureshiho no Kami shall become flowers and also the moon, casting its fragrance across the four quarters. overtaken by darkness, enveloped in gloom and loneliness, the moon that emerged at dusk is Ureshiho. Giving birth to the Miko, filled with joyful courage, Ureshiho no Kami set forth without a moment's rest. Crossing thousands of ri of Arano, abiding with the Kimi of Mizumitama, what an evening of the moon is this!'

Kuninakahiko no Kami sings again:

'Aha, aha! The heavenly sun shines, the full moon glows; flowers are fragrant in the fields, and the voices of insects are clear. In the Kuninaka of the Hisakata Amatsumikuni, how joyful am I, serving in the divine work! Through the achievements of the divine words of the Mizumitama Kami, shall this Hirohara establish a Mikuni? To build the land and give birth to the Kunitamagami, how reverend are the diligent labors! In the Kuninaka of the beautiful heaven and earth, the two deities, Hikohime, have given birth to Miko. As the seed of the eternal Mikuni, the land shall be opened by the Miko born of the Mizumitama. How lofty is the majestic awe of the Mizumitama Kami! Distant are the mountain ridges, and the shadow of Tsukuyomi is bright. Wandering morning and evening, the Mizumitama sheds tears upon the Yaso-hinokami. When I ponder upon this sublimity, morning, evening, and dusk, my soul is wrapped in gloom. On the evening field where Tsukuyomi no Kami shines, together with our horses, how peacefully we sleep!

The Kami must have gone to rest upon the roots of the grass, lulled by the dashing waves of snoring.'

Thus each and every one sang of their reflections and passed the night. When the abundant glory of the heavenly sun rose, led by Chikamenao no Kami, the divine pillar of the Mizumitama whipped their horses and advanced singing sacred songs across the boundless, grassy wilderness—how awe-inspiring it is.

斯く各も各も述懐を歌ひ給ひて、一夜を明し、天津日の豊栄昇りし頃、近見男の神先頭に、瑞の御霊の神柱は、駒に鞭うち、際限もなき草莽々の野を、御歌をうたひながら進ませ給ふぞ畏けれ。
