Chapter 21: Jewel Field Pure 庭 [1889]

玉野清庭〔1889〕

§ 1

That the Tennin experience the Gosui is what the Butsuten reveals. Rightly so, even in the Tenkai where the Kamigami dwell eternally, there are also Eikoseisui, there is Jiyakukiyō and Raukiyō. Therefore, the Kamigami of the Tenkai, rejuvenating and reviving time and again, eternally preserve their youth and live and flourish in their respective Shiyokushiyō. Here, the Kami Tamanohime, in order to fulfill the Kamuwaza of Kamiu, having received the Gosenji of the Lord Kami, had caused a long Toshitsuki to wait, but alas, having passed her Tekirei, she was not Fusaha for the Shinji of Kamiu; once again by the Gosenji of the Lord Kami, having been entrusted with the Sōitsusō greater Kamuwaza of Kuniu, she fixed her eternal Sumika on the Sugaoka of Tamanoyama, while awaiting the time.

When the Kami Akitsuo had gradually arrived at Tamanomori, the Kami Tamanohime, who had waited and lingered for a long Toshitsuki, reverently awaiting the Lord Ōkami who had descended at that very moment, obtaining permission and watching for a fleeting moment, went out together with the two Kami, the Kami Mototsumagoto and the Kami Machiaihiko, to the foot of the hill, and while joyfully welcoming her first encounter with the auspicious Mitama for which she had pined so long, she guided him to the sanctuary.

Tamanohime: 'Awaiting thee, my Lord, long have the days grown, and I now have no way to serve in the work of birthing gods. Yet, by the divine word of the Lord Ōkami, with thee will I give birth to this Land of Reeds. Though the land of Manazuru is still tender, upon the sacred place of Tamanoyama shall I stand and fashion and solidify it?'

The Kami Akitsuo chants a divine poem: 'Leaving the mountains of Takachihoso, and wandering far away, I have come hither wishing to give birth to the land. Since today, when I came to serve in the entrusted work of birthing gods, shall I, together with my lord, give birth to the land? Upon the boundless, tender land of reeds, rising up, the haze is deep and dimly dark. Hearing that the Lord God has descended from heaven, I now, with trembling heart, undergo the divine work. Through a heart of hesitation, have I aged through the years, and lagged behind in the divine work of birthing gods? Receiving the august will of the Lord God, I regret that I gave my heart to the words of common deities; yet keeping the distinction between root and branch, upper and lower, the birthing of lands and the birthing of gods shall be accomplished. Revering the divine word of the Lord God, let us cast away and exhaust the whispers of common deities.'

The Kami Tamanohime chants a divine poem: 'Everything whatsoever is the august Mitama appearing from the august Water and Fire of the Lord Ōkami; reverently will I serve. Though the divine work for which thou didst grant the auspicious Mitama is regrettable, now there is no help for it. I have grown old this year, yet do I not deem the work of birthing the land difficult. If thou, my Lord, art present, even the tender land of Manazuru of the true crane shall be revived in beauty.'

The Kami Mototsumagoto chants a divine poem: 'Serving Tamanohime for a long time, I was the Kami Mototsumagoto who pined and waited for thee, my Lord! Beholding the figure of my Lord who hath deigned to come forth, so profound was the reverence that I was drowned in tears. The tears of joy gushed forth like a waterfall, and shone as the dew of blessing. Though there are hundreds of days, this very day of birth is indeed a felicitous day that brings forth the divine land. Morning and evening praying to the Lord Ōkami, that merit has met the joy of today. Gazing up at the clear moon of the distant heavens, how many years did I wait for thy coming forth, my Lord! This hill is a sacred place brought forth by the very hands of the Lord Ōkami by solidifying Water and Fire. Though it is still a tender land of reeds, upon this mori the pines of a thousand years are entwined. Since it is a Tenkai of thought, the pines of a thousand ages have also been born and come forth.'

The Kami Machiaihiko chants a divine poem: 'Enduring and enduring the Toshitsuki, waiting for thee, my Lord, before I knew it, I have grown old. Guessing the heart of the Kami Tamanohime, gazing up at the moon, how I shed tears! Night after night, gazing upon the shadow of the waxing and waning Tsukiyomi, I suffered in my heart. On the waxing day I thought of my Lord's fortune, on the waning day I thought of my Lord's august body and grieved. Waiting and waiting, welcoming this good day upon this hill, thou, my Lord, art surely a dream, methinks. Subsiding upon this hill which the Lord God hath brought forth, fashion the land. However valiant the Kami Tamanohime may be, as a single pillar deity she would have no way to act. Do ye two, male and female, unite your Water and Fire, and cause to live the tender land of reeds of the true crane!'

原文（近代文語）

天人の五衰ありとは仏典の示す所である。宜なり、神々の永久に住み給ふ天界にも、亦栄枯盛衰あり、若境あり老境あり。故に天界の神々は若がへり若がへり甦りつつ、永遠に其若さを保ちて、各も各もの職掌に生き栄え給ふなり。茲に玉野比女の神は神生みの神業を勤むべく、主の神の御宣示をうけて、長き年月を待たせ給ひけるが、可惜其適齢を過ごし給ひたれば、神生みの神事に相応ず、再び主の神の御宣示により、層一層大なる国土生みの神業を任けられ給ひたれば、玉野山の清丘に永久の住所を定め、時を待たせつつありける。

顕津男の神は漸くにして、玉野森に着かせ給ひければ、永の年月待ち佇び給ひし玉野比女の神は、折から降臨し給ひし主の大神に謹み待りつつ、御許しを得て寸間を窺ひ、丘の麓まで本津真言の神、待合比古の神の二神と共に出迎へ、待ち佗びたる瑞の御霊との初対面を悦び給ひつつ、聖所に導き給ひける。

玉野比女の神『岐美待ちて気永くなりぬ吾は今 神生みの業に仕へむすべなし さりながら主の大神の神言もて 岐美と生まなむこの国原を 真鶴の国土はまだ稚し玉野森の 聖所に立ちて造り固めむか』

顕津男の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『高地秀の山を立ち出ではろばろと 我は国土生むと此処に来つるも 御依さしの神生みの業仕へつる 今日より公と国土生まむかも 果しなき稚国原に立ちのぼる 狭霧深しもほの暗きかも 主の神の天降りますと聞きて我は今 神業をへぬを恐れみ思ふ ためらひの心に我は年を経て 神生みの神業に後れけるかも 主の神の御心うけて凡神の 言葉に心をかけしを悔ゆるも 本と末上と下との差別をば 守りて国土生み神生みは成るを 主の神の神言畏み凡神の 囁き外にいざや尽さな』

玉野比女の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『何も彼も主の大神の御水火より 現れし御魂ぞ謹しみ仕へむ 瑞御霊おくれ給ひし神業の 悔しけれども今は是非なし 吾は今年老いにけりさりながら 国土生みの業を難しと思はず 岐美在さばまだ地稚き真鶴の 国土も〓__GT_13105__怜によみがへるべし』

本津真言の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『玉野比女に吾は仕へて気永くも 岐美待ち佗びし本津真言の神よ いでませし岐美の姿を拝みて 尊さあまり涙にくれける 嬉しさの涙は滝と迸しり 恵の露と輝きにけり 百日日はあれども今日の生日こそ 神国を生ます目出度き日なるよ 朝夕に主の大神を祈りてし 功は今日の喜びにあひぬ 久方の冴えたる月を仰ぎつつ 岐美の出でまし幾年待ちしよ この丘は主の大神の御手づから 水火を固めて生ませる聖所よ 未だ稚き国土なりながらこの森に 千歳の松は繁りあひたり 想念の天界なれば千年の 常磐の松も生れ出でにける』

待合比古の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『年月を忍び忍びて岐美待ちし 吾いつの間にか老いにけらしな 玉野比女の神の心を押しはかり 月を仰ぎて涙せしはや 盈ち虧くる月読のかげ夜な夜なに 仰ぎて吾は心痛めし 盈つる日は岐美の幸思ひ虧くる日は 岐美の御身を思ひなやみし 待ち待ちて今日のよき日をこの丘に 迎へし岐美ぞ夢かとぞ思ふ 主の神の生ませ給へるこの丘に 鎮まりまして国土造りませ 玉野比女如何に雄々しくいますとも 一柱神にてせむすべなからむ 女男の水火合せ給ひて真鶴の 稚き国原生かしましませ』

§ 2

Akitsuo no Kami chants a sacred song:

Upon the hill of Tamano, brought forth by the Lord God, / climbing up, my heart is gladdened. / With a murakimo heart, my spirit thrives and lives vibrantly, / in awe and reverence, I think upon the descent of the Lord God. / Though wisdom and enlightenment are yet lacking, I earnestly desire / to look up to the Lord Ōkami.

Tamanohime no Kami chants a sacred song:

The divine spirit has come hither, and the Lord God / descended first from heaven—how awe-inspiring this day is! / Come then, at the pure spring upon this hill, / let us purify our souls and worship Him.

Himegami, most gracefully, pacing the white sand of the spacious garden on Tamanohime Hill, leads the way into the shadow of the aged pines, where a crystal-clear spring, like a mirror, reflects the filtering sunlight, shining broad, deep, and blue. Tamanohime no Kami stands at the water's edge of the pure spring and chants:

Is not the radiance upon the water's surface / of the Tamaizumi spring, born as the Mitama of the Lord God, majestic indeed? / Morning and evening, in this pure water, washing my tamashihi, / through the years have I passed. / Being one who would behold the Lord God, / there is no day I do not gaze upon this Tamaizumi.

Akitsuo no Kami chants a sacred song:

How awe-inspiring are the onkotoba of the Hime of Tamano! / I assent, and in ritual ablution will I serve.

At that moment, stirred by a cool breeze blowing across, the blue surface of the Tamaizumi brims with ripples like fish scales, gleaming in gold and silver as it catches the filtered sunlight, a profound coolness pressing close upon the body. Gazing upon the lifelike ripples swaying on the water's surface, Akitsuo no Kami composes himself with majestic dignity and chants a sacred song:

This refreshing pure water— / I worship it as the pure heart of Tamanohime. / The deep, clear water brimming so lush and blue, its depths unseen, / is the heart of the public. / The deep water, crystal clear and fathomless, / is indeed the valiant and true heart of the public. / Is this water the dew of the Lord God's grace? / Even a single glance makes the heart revive. / The jewel-clear spring of pure water, locked within the dense thickets of evergreen pines, / how blue and clear it is! / Since Himegami is born from the essence of this water, / well is it that her august figure is so pure.

Tamanohime no Kami chants a sacred song:

This spring is praised as the Pool of Tamano, / and morning and evening have I worshipped it as a mirror. / To build the land of Manazuru, morning and evening, / how I performed misogi in the jewel spring!

Hotsumagoto no Kami chants a sacred song:

Upon the jewel spring of Tamanohime Hill, both moon and sun / float in clarity morning and evening. / Having performed a hundred misogi, I am one who morning and evening / offers sacred food at the shrine of the Lord God. / O Mizu no Mitamakimi, perform misogi seven times / and worship the Lord Ōkami.

Akitsuo no Kami chants a sacred song:

How grateful am I for the sacred words of Hotsumagoto no Kami! / I assent, and in misogi will I serve.

Machiaihiko no Kami chants a sacred song:

Waiting for the sevenfold misogi of Kimimi, / I shall attend as an attendant up to the great palace residence.

Hereupon Akitsuo no Kami performs the sevenfold misogi, and guided by the hand of Tamanohime no Kami, with Hotsumagoto no Kami in the lead and Machiaihiko no Kami bringing up the rear, stepping softly over the white sand garden, they slowly advance toward the sacred hall of the Jewel Palace—how awe-inspiring it is.
