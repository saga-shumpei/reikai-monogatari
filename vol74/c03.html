Chapter 3: 篠の笹 Plain [1871]

篠の笹原〔1871〕

§ 1

Kuninakahiko no Kami took the lead of the company, and wishing to guide the way, advanced from horseback with a clear voice, chanting a sacred poem.

In the midst of the Shibitenkai realm, Though a hundred deities dwell, Particularly wondrous is the Mizumitama, Becoming the seed of all things, Down to the fruits of mountains, rivers, grasses, and trees, Bestowing the water of life, Opening the divine land and begetting deities, Entrusted with noble divine works, To the boundless plains, Without distinction of far and near, The noble Kototama comes forth into birth, Making it live in the way of Makoto, Advancing, how awe-inspiring it is! I am Kuninakahiko no Kami, Serving in today's escort, Even the winding ways of the road, like white clouds, Toward the distant great sky yonder, A journey on the field paths aimed with resolve, Ah, Kamunagara kamunagara, Blessed by the living Kototama,

By the divine commission of the Great Lord Ōkami, May the divine work be without omission or flaw, [...] brilliantly, elaborately, eternally, Cause us to serve! I now, Aiming at Mount Manazuru, which is hazy in the distant great field, Advance parting the grassy fields. Shining brightly across the heavenly sky, The sun is radiant; the moon, like a heavenly torii, Is clear, guarding the broad daylight, Pure white and glowing. The wind of Shinato blows moderately, Caressing the faces of the deities of our company, And sending forth fragrance. From amidst the lush growth of a hundred and a thousand grasses, Visible is the tenderness of the crimson flower's form, Singing of the eternal spring. The voices of insects grow clear, as we travel the journey's grass pillow,

Truly it is a matter of joy, Ah, Kamunagara kamunagara, How precious is the divine grace.

At the end of the vast field, distant as the eye can see, Could that dimly hazy peak be Mount Manazuru? I have heard that a hundred deities gather at Mount Manazuru, And I shall serve as their escort. Come now, whip our steeds and quickening our pace, I shall surely arrive by twilight. O Mizumitama, I shall go a step ahead, Advancing toward Mount Manazuru.'

Akatsuo no Kami, replying to this, chanted a sacred poem.

The divine words and word-raising of Kuninakahiko no Kami, I assent to them, and I shall establish the public principle. Mount Manazuru is far away; whip your steed And make haste until dusk is announced.'

Kuninakahiko no Kami: 'Come now, O Mizumitama, O hundred deities, I shall advance to Mount Manazuru. O hundred deities, protecting the Mizumitama, Come peacefully to Mount Manazuru.'

So chanting, he struck his steed with a whip and dashed headlong out into the grassy field. Watching his brave retreating figure, Chikamio no Kami chanted:

How brave are the divine words of Kuninakahiko no Kami! Rejoicing so, no words even come forth. Kuninakahiko, who stood at the lead and advanced, has already become invisible. Is it because of the swift legs of the steed that his august form is hidden within the surging mist?'

Maruyahiko no Kami chanted a sacred poem.

Will today too draw to a close, the way being long? The shadow of Mount Manazuru is faint. Determining that tonight again we shall sleep in the fields, Let us go quietly through the bamboo grass plain. Up until now we have crossed over the field of pampas grass, And now again we trace our way through the bamboo grass plain. Rustling, the wind rises in the bamboo grass plain, Things are tumultuous, and the heart does not settle. In the bamboo grass plain, which I think is where warped deities lurk, Keep your hearts steady, O hundred deities. The heavenly sun is hidden by clouds, and the wind of the Shinato region Suddenly begins to blow fiercely. O Tatakumi no Kami, raise high your Kototama, And cause the storm of the fields to grow calm.'

Tatakumi no Kami chanted a sacred poem.

I speak to Shinatohiko and Shinatohime: Stay calm, you who have set forth! Setting forth to build the land and beget august children, This is the august progress of the Mizumitama. I am indeed Tatakumi no Kami, the august spirit that governs the wind of Shinatobe.'

When he so chanted, suddenly the wind that had been blowing fiercely across the wilderness quieted its roar, the clouds in the high sky scattered in all directions, and the light of the heavenly sun shone brilliantly.

Ureshino no Kami chanted a sacred poem.

The fierce blowing of the wind across the wilderness has also ceased; In the water and fire of the Kototama of Tatakumi no Kami, The wind that had obstructed the path of the Mizumitama's progress has grown calm in the bamboo grass plain. The shadows of rain clouds crossing to and fro have vanished away, And the sky has become a plain of blue sea. The august light of the moon crossing the blue sea plain Feels as though it shines down to the depths of the earth.

§ 2

While thus chanting and parting the Sasahara, as They advance, a murky torrent surges turbulently, a rather wide river flowing from east to west, blocking the path of the party.

Chikamio no Kami stands on the bank of the murky torrent and chants from horseback:

Even to the depth of eight thousand fathom, it is muddied\nThis muddy river, how shall we cross?\nDid Kuninakahiko no Kami cross this muddy river\nby whipping his horse onward?\nThat such a murky torrent should exist\nin the Shibitenkai protected by the Kami, I would not have thought\nPurifying the water and fire of Kototama, this river\nshall I cause to run clear and cross, as Kamunagara I am\nEvening approaches already, and to have the murky torrent\nbefore us, how lonely is the journey!

Akitsuo no Kami chants a sacred song with solemn dignity:

According to the entrustment of the Lord God, creating the Kuni\nO Mizu no Mitama, quickly grow clear and pure!\nI now appear as Mizu no Mitama\nAnd build the Kuni, away with the turbidity!\nThat water should flow\nthrough the Tenkai where not a single thing should be turbid, is strange indeed\nSasosuseshi, may the water clear and brighten, O Kahamori no Kami\nPurify the river that I cross!

When He thus chants, even the mighty murky torrent gradually fades and finally grows crystal clear, to the extent that even the number of pebbles on the bottom can be seen. Maruyahiko no Kami, marveling at this Shintoku, chants a sacred song:

Indeed, O Mizu no Mitama, the muddy river\nhas been purified by the living Kototama!\nWith the hearts of the Kami growing turbid, the water of this river\nmust have become defiled to this extent\nKuninakahiko no Kami's divine word also caused this stream\nto grow clear as He crossed, no doubt!\nCome now, let us cross over to the opposite bank\nAnd make once more our pillowed grass for a single night.

Here, with Maruyahiko no Kami at the lead, the eleven pillars of Kami aligned the bits of their horses, easily crossed the rapids of the muddy river to reach the opposite bank, and having each watered their horses and finished feeding them grass, each and every one breathed in and out the water and fire of the Kototama of sasosuseshi, puffed out their bellies, and retired to sleep.

原文（近代文語）

斯く歌ひつつ笹原を分けて進み給へば、濁流滔々と漲れるかなりに広き河東西に流れ、一行の行手を横ぎりゐたり。

近見男の神は濁流の河辺に立ちて、馬上より歌ひ給ふ。

顕津男の神は儼然として御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『さすがにも瑞の御霊よ泥河は 生言霊に清まりにけり 神々の心にごりてこの河の 水はかくまで穢れたるらむ 国中比古神の神言もこの流れ 澄ませて渡り給ひたりけむ いざさらば向つ岸辺にうち渡り また一夜さの草枕せむ』

茲に圓屋比古の神を先頭に、十一柱の神は駒の轡を並べて、にごり河の水瀬をやすやす向ふ岸に着き、各々馬に水飼ひ、草を喰せ終り、各も各もサソスセシの言霊の水火を呼吸し、腹をふくらせ寝に就き給ひぬ。
