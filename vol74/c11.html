Chapter 11: 万代の誓 [1879]

万代の誓〔1879〕

§ 1

Hereupon, as the Momogamitachi poured forth their Magokoro into day-and-night prayers, there was a response from the Lord Ōmikami, and a solemn Goshinji came forth from Kūchiu; thus Akitsuo no Kami, filled with awe and reverence, exerted his Daiyūmaushin, whereupon the black clouds of the flames of love of Ikuyohime no Kami that had enveloped Shihen scattered without a trace, and the Saukū became entirely free of clouds, while the light of Jitsugetsu shone brightly once more; perceiving this, the Kamigami pondered upon the Miizu of the Lord Kami and the terribleness of the attachment of love, and each and every one of them chanted Miuta.

The Miuta of Tohomio no Kami.

The Miizu of the Lord Kami is sacred, the Ametsuchi of Tokoyami suddenly cleared and brightened; since it is the Ametsuchi of the Lord Kami, how could the black clouds shroud this Kamikuni? The achievements of the Lord Kami are sacred; now I realize how terrifying the thought of Kohiyami was. Pondering the heart of Ikuyohime no Kami, I think of the suffering that made her spew flames. Pondering the inner Mikokoro of Mizumitama, my tears have still not ceased flowing.

The Miuta of Maruyahiko no Kami.

The Ametsuchi of Hikosuzu cleared all at once, scattering the massed clouds that had enveloped the four quarters; now once more, face to face, I have beheld the achievements of the Lord Ōmikami and am filled with awe. I, now following Mizumitama, have witnessed the functional use of water and fire in the Ametsuchi. I deeply ponder how no easy matter it is to perform the divine work of producing the land and giving birth to the kami. Guarding the august rear of Mizumitama no Kami, I think to assist in the divine work of giving birth to the children. I shall solidify the still youthful kunigara of Mount Manazuru with the true words of Ikukototama. Though I know the sad heart of Ikuyohime no Kami, there is no remedy, since it is Amanogawa. Opening up the kunigara near and far to give birth to children, the activity of Mizumitama is magnificent. Thickly rising steam, out of season, O youthful kunigara enveloping the heavens!

The Miuta of Kuninakahiko no Kami.

Both moon and sun have cleared up through the Kototama of the Lord Ōmikami, how precious is the kunigara! From this day forth, purifying my heart and purifying my body, I shall also purify and clear my own Kototama. Uttering them without knowing the distinction between night and day, my Kototama bore no fruit. Even if I strive to utter clouded Kototama, how could the darkness of the Ametsuchi open up? There is nothing as terrifying as the heart of love; it blocks the Ikukototama and makes it cloudy. Even the flame of the love-heart of Himekami—my Kototama has no means to extinguish it. How shameful is the power of my Kototama, being suppressed by the flames of love!

The Miuta of Ureshino no Kami.

O Sun Ōmikami and Moon Kami who dwell in heaven, pray make and guard our kunigara. Following Mizumitama no Kami, I think to solidify the youthful kunigara of Manazuru. Vast and boundless, the kunigara of Manazuru is youthful, a land of reeds and rushes. Cutting and clearing the reeds and rushes, I shall make the kunigara through the Ikukototama of the Lord Kami. Now I know the achievements of Mizumitama no Kami, the hardships of giving birth to Kuninotokotachi no Kami. Giving birth to children in this kunigara of Aizen, I ponder the achievements of Mizumitama. Since I am a kami of child-bearing, I merely follow the Mikokoro of the Ōmikami. No matter how loud the whisperings of ordinary kami may be, I shall quell them with my Kototama and press forward. Even the swamp surrounding Mount Manazuru has dried up completely through the Ikukototama. Mizumitama, whose achievements are thus illustrious, is indeed the master of the hundred and eighty kunigara.

Since the hundred and eighty kunigara are vast, the Lord Kami has bestowed eighty august mihidai. The preciousness of waiting for the emergence of Tamanohime and Kimi in the distant forest beyond. Except for the fixed mihidai kami, Kimi must not gaze upon those who are unsuited; how awe-inspiring that is! Even if they do not suit well according to the Mikokoro, since they are the kami of the mihidai, you must gaze upon them. Even an undesirable mihidai is endured, and Kimi pours love upon it; how awe-inspiring! Although there are eighty pillars of mihidai, to gaze upon even a kami one does not particularly favor is something I deem awe-inspiring. The heavenly sun illuminates Mount Manazuru while assisting in the divine work of making the kunigara. Tsukuyomi no Kami, who traverses the heavens, is also clouded; is it the shadow of the heart of Mizumitama? Having come from afar to serve in the retinue, you have illuminated the red hearts of the Momogamitachi who served at the august rear to assist in the divine work of Kimi.

The Miuta of Minashihiko no Kami.

Solidifying the muddy ground and building the divine bridge, I serve in the divine work of ground-solidification. Drying up the mud and solidifying the ground, I shall plant and cause the five grains to grow. Looking up, toward the southwest, how blue is the lake of Tamano where the sun and moon float. Through the achievements of Tamanohime no Kami, the water of Lake Tamano has become thus pure. Mount Manazuru rises higher and higher, with clouds playing wide across its skirts. Rising out above the white clouds, this mountain is surely fitting to be the pillar of the land. Softly and gently the wind is fragrant, perhaps bearing the scent of the plum blossoms at the shore of Lake Tamano. Mount Manazuru, which suffered while enveloped in black clouds, has cleared through the Kototama of the Lord. Until the very end of myriad ages, the kunitsubashira of Mount Manazuru shall never move. Tatotsutetsu, perhaps a thousand-winged crane born from the Kototama of Tamanohime!

§ 2

Crane and heron gather upon this sacred mount, / Chanting of a thousand ages in the pine tree tops.

The sacred song of Ubudama no Kami.

The mount of Manazuru is so lofty, so pure; / When cranes build their nests in the ever-green pines, / From the water and fire of the divine Kototama of TA of the Lord God, / The pines instantly sprang forth into growth. / The pines that sprang forth, adding the colors of a thousand autumns, / With greenery growing ever deeper and richer, / In the blowing wind, the pine tree tops are murmuring. / Awe-inspiring is the blessing of the Kototama of U. / The living Kototama of A-O-U-E-I unceasingly resounds in heaven and earth, / Within the noble heavenly realm. / I indeed, wielding the activity of the Kototama of the U sound, / Coming forth into birth, serve in the divine work. / In the achievement of the Mizumitama revolving the four quarters, / Even the youthful land-plain grows firm and solid, / Producing lands and bringing forth gods without end. / The Kimi who revolves this heavenly realm— / The evergreen trees have grown up until the place is crowded, / For there is not a single moment's gap in the living Kototama.

The sacred song of Tamakiharu no Kami.

Guarding the life of Tamakiharu for eternity, / I will serve the Mizumitama. / May the life of the Mizumitama God be preserved everlastingly, / For the sake of creating the land. / Without the life of the jewel-string, how could the birth of lands / And the divine work of bringing forth gods be fulfilled? / Tamakiharu, born from the U sound of the Lord God, / Is a god who guards life. / It goes without saying for the gods, / That to make all things live is precisely through my activity. / To guard the life of the jewel-string for eternity, / I have come, following the Mizumitama. / Fresh and green, forever dwell, O Mizumitama, / Rejuvenating and ever rejuvenating in the world. / Though countless hundreds of millions of years pass, the god of Tamakiharu / Will guard all lives. / Rejuvenating and ever rejuvenating, / I am the one who guards all lives without end. / May the pines of the evergreen trees also flourish for a thousand years, / And may Mount Manazuru likewise prosper forever. / It is also Tamakiharu who brings forth the god of Kunimitama; / There is none to which the divine words of the god do not add. / Without beginning, without end, infinite and absolute, / I indeed guard the life of heaven and earth. / Eternally flourishing and endless, I am the god of life / Born from the U sound of the Lord God. / As far as the eye can see, the land of Manazuru is still youthful; / I will bestow life and make it live. / I swear and leave the vow / To guard the life of the multitudes of people who shall be born in the last age. / It goes without saying for deities and men, I will guard grasses and trees, / Beasts, fish, and insects as well. / Standing upon the mountain of the Kunitsu-pillar of Manazuru, / I swear and leave this vow unto the ten thousand generations.

The sacred song of Musubiai no Kami.

Would one hear the wondrous and noble Kototama, / Or the vow of the god who guards life? / Water and fire, and water and fire bound together, this life thus born, / Pray preserve it for eternity. / The jewel-string of the eighty pillars of Mi-hishiro's gods, / Bound together, may You preserve it forever, / And grant life and prosperity for eternity / To the divine child whom the Mizumitama brings forth. / I myself bind together mountains and rivers, / And bind the water and fire of male and female deities. / Binding together country and country, god and god, / I will guard the Shibi heavenly realm for eternity. / God and man, lord and retainer in amicable accord, / I will bind together with my Kototama.

The sacred song of Mimusu no Kami.

The daily foods of the gods, one and all, / I will grant them delicious and beautiful flavors. / Without flavor, what would the hundred foods be? / They would be no different from stones and pebbles. / The flavor of the gods and the flavor of men, / Verily, it is my divine work to guard the taste of foodstuffs. / Mountains and fields, seas and rivers, of all things / Without exception I will grant them flavor. / When even the Kototama lacks flavor, the hearts of the gods / Will grow rough, and the age of the gods will be disturbed. / If there be flavor in mountains and fields, then grasses and trees / Will flourish luxuriantly in the divine water and fire.

The sacred song of Magoto-no-itsuku no Kami.

The Kototama issuing from the true words of the Lord God / Has created this heavenly realm. / From the U sound of the Lord God I was born forth, / To rule over and guard the Kototama of the world. / If the Kototama were without light, how / Could all things ever be brought into birth? / Clouded words of the gods are false words / That make heaven and earth cloudy and turbid. / Purifying the heart within the breast, purifying the body, / Revering the gods, to utter true words— / The heart of steadfast true words is the foundation / That firmly establishes heaven and earth forever. / Know that even disasters where heaven splits and mountains and rivers roar / Are calmed if the Kototama is pure. / Guarding the daily Kototama of the gods, / I will pioneer and guard it as a beautiful divine land. / If the water and fire of the Kototama are righteous, mountains and fields, / Sun and moon alike will shine alone. / Born from the U sound of the Lord God, / I will guard the Kototama of the world forever. / The activity of the words of men in the last age, too, / As long as I exist, I will open up and guard. / Kuninakahiko no Kami, dwelling upon Mount Manazuru, / Will guard this land-plain eternally. / The auspicious day draws near indeed / When the Mizumitama, having brought forth Kuninitama no Kami, shall emerge.

At this, the hundred deities stood upon the summit of Mount Manazuru, each and every one proclaiming clearly the divine work that had been entrusted to them. Matching their hearts and uniting their strengths ever more firmly, proclaiming that they would guard not only the Shibi heavenly realm but the various heavenly realms of every tier, they left Kuninakahiko no Kami to guard the sacred mountain of Manazuru, and it came to pass that they set forth, taking as their mark the lush and green forest shadows thriving on the shores of Lake Tamano.
