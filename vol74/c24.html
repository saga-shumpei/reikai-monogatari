Chapter 24: Sincerity の化身 [1892]

誠の化身〔1892〕

§ 1

The Kami of Mototsumakoto, judging that the time had come, took the lead and, waving the Ōnusa while purifying the surface of the courtyard, slowly ascended the stairs of the shrine; thereupon the Kami of Akitsuo also followed behind, crouched upon the topmost stage of the stairs, and proclaimed the divine Mikoto.

Awe-inspiring to name even in awe, at the sacred sanctuary of Tamamogaoka where the Lord Ōkami descends, having firmly erected the great pillars of the palace, and in this sacred shrine where the thousand-sectioned rafters reach high into Takaamahara, on this auspicious day and at this auspicious hour, having beseeched and prayed upon the Mikoto of the Ōmikami who descended, we seek to open up and solidify the still immature wilderness of Manazuru. We look up and implore the Lord Ōmikami to grant the august grace of the living Kototama, and to protect and assist in meticulous and thorough detail the divine work entrusted to the Kami of Ōmotoakitsuo, and to cause the Kunitsuhashira of the southern land of Shibitenkai to be born. Awe-inspiringly, though I set forth from the palace dwelling of Shibi to serve in the work of creating lands and birthing kami in the wildernesses of the four quarters, since my own power of the Kototama is from the beginning not complete, it does not accord with my thoughts, nor does the entrusted work progress; I serve in awe and reverence, morning and evening reflecting upon my own person, and praying that you may hear with peace and calm, and grant my petition. Even regarding the divine work of creating the land, teach and guide me surely to the place where my feet tread not, protect and bless me in the protection of the night and the protection of the day, and proclaim and let us hear the great Mikoto of the Ōmikami—thus do I speak, reverencing, fearing, and trembling with utmost awe.

The Kami of Akitsuo prostrated himself flat before the august presence and offered his prayer with awe; yet, how might the august will of the Lord Ōkami be, for no divine pronouncement whatsoever was bestowed, and it was wondrously silent, without even the sound of the breeze blowing through the pines, remaining utterly hushed. Thereupon the Kami of Akitsuo, fearful and reverent, chanted an august poem.

Even to name is awe-inspiring, of this Great Deity / Grant a divine message, for the sake of creating the land / The land of entrusted creation and the birthing of kami, morning and evening / Serving, yet still feeling awe and shame / The work that I shall perform, entirely of the Lord Kami / To serve even according to the august will / Since the land of Manazuru is immature, of the Lord Kami / I think to solidify it with the aid of august water and fire / I pray that you will let drop the rigorous Kototama / And show unto me the path I must walk / The divine message of the Kami of Tamanohime was due to the lagging in the work / Of birthing kami, which is my own sin / While I hesitated, the months and years passed on / And I repent of myself, who neglected the divine work / Blinding the august heart of the Great Deity / Grant forgiveness for my negligence / Since I am a kami devoid of strength, I fear that not even half / Of the commission of the Great Deity is fulfilled / Grant me a single word of the living Kototama / As I bend my knees, prostrate, and beseech and pray

As if a deer folding its knees and prostrating, as if a wild beast / Piercing my neck, I beseech and pray / Little male deer, prick up your ears and listen / To my great norito of land-creation.

Though the Kami of Akitsuo thus put his whole heart into offering various prayers, how displeasure-invoking must the august will of the Lord Ōkami have been, for not even a single word of divine pronouncement was granted; the kami, unable to gauge the state of the august divine intent, prostrated themselves flat upon the stairs, merely trembling with awe. Thereupon the Kami of Tamanohime raised her head, and with the sound of hand-claps clear and crisp, offered her prayer thus:

From the distant heavenly high palace of Hikutsu / The merit of the kami who descended is awe-inspiring / Coming to serve in the palace of Tamano through months and years / How joyous it is to meet this descent to earth / The transgression of opposing the divine work of birthing kami / Forgive me, O Lord Ōmikami / On this day when there is not even a soft-blowing wind / The calmness is such that I cannot gauge the divine heart / Even the sound of Manazuru's wings has ceased / And the sunlight is dull upon the pine treetops / How might I pacify the divine heart of the Lord Kami / I have shattered my heart into a thousand pieces / I pray you, forgive the Kami of Akitsuo / That the divine work of birthing kami unavoidably lagged behind / The heart of hesitation in the end became divorced from the divine work / Of birthing kami and turned into sin / Forgiving the hesitation of the entrusted words of the ancestral kami / Forgive the sin of following the ways of the world / Serving morning and evening in reverence before the august presence / I continually feared that the divine work would fall behind

O wondrous divine declaration of the Lord God of Hitokoto,

We long to hear it, even as we weep and pray.'

Yet despiteaman of Tamanohime's prayer in the living kototama, there was no divine word whatsoever, and the surroundings grew ever more silent. Ikuyohimegami, standing before the divine presence, reverently offered up a sacred song of prayer.

'Appearing as the mitama of Mount Manazuru,

I am Ikuyohimegami, show mercy, O mitama.

Ikuyohime, with utmost awe, has served

In the divine work of kamiu, the birth of deities.

If this be a sin, do thou forgive it and polish

My soul, O Lord Ōmikami.

Within my womb, wherein the Lord God's miiki dwells,

Behold the precious miko thou hast caused to dwell therein.

With trembling awe I have come to worship at this sugado,

Repenting of having been drowned in love.

Through the coagulation of magokoro, a precious miko

Has come to dwell within my body.

Since the kuni of Manazuru is young, I shall nurture and raise

The precious miko and serve him.

I have tended to the aging Tamanohime,

A divine work of kamiu which I have served.

Though the Ōkami gave no divine command, yet forgive me,

Who carry this precious miko.

Ametsuchi remains without even the sound of a gentle breeze,

So profoundly silent is the wonder of kefu.

If the kototama of miiki were not present, all

Deities and trees and plants would surely wither and perish.

I pray that from the miiki of the Lord Ōmikami,

They may be born and grow, to make the kuni live.

If the miiki of the Lord God should cease, Ametsuchi,

Deities, and trees and plants will all come to an end.

Though I concentrate my magokoro and pray, the miiki

Of the Lord God reaches me not, how lonely kefu is.

Though my heart of hearts is hurried, what can I do,

Since there are no words from the Lord Ōmikami?

The ever-verdant pines of Tamanomori and Manazuru

Have begun to wither in this brief moment.

Even the voice of Manazuru is no longer heard.

The miiki of the Lord God having ceased,

Even the pines of the evergreen trees have lost their green hue,

And the surroundings grow ever more lonely.

Though she offered this sacred song and prayed thus, it was as if she were speaking to rock and stone, and there was no echo at all. Thereupon, Machiaihiko-no-kami offered up a sacred song.

'Thinking upon the loneliness of kefu, for which

I have waited through piled-up months and days, I am sad.

Through the years I have served Tamanohime,

And now I meet her in this lonely, pure garden.

In the fleeting moment when the Lord God remains silent,

My soul-line is about to wither away.

To create kuni and give birth to deities, the mizumitama

Has manifested, O protect us today.'

Chikaramitsuo-no-kami offered up a sacred song.

'Though the miiki of the su-sound kototama has ceased,

I shall make the kototama of the u-sound live.

Ū-ū-ū-ū-ā-ā-ā-ā-ā-ā-ā-ā

Return to the su-sound of yore, my kototama.

Ū-ū-ū-ā-ā-ā-ā-ā

Chikaramitsuo-kami will revive through the su-sound.

Sū-sū-sū—quietly, the august doors of the shrine

Open and come forth, O primal parent deity.

In the kototama of the breath of Sasosuseshi deities,

Awaken and revive, O forest of Tama.

Living, living, living endlessly is the heavenly realm,

How could the su-sound ever cease?

Though I have proclaimed my kototama thus far,

It is a wonder that the august doors do not open.

Is the Lord God, desiring to polish our mitama,

Manifested as the deity of Mototsu Magoto?

O my foolish soul-line, was not the Lord God

Indeed the deity of Mototsu Magoto?'

Astonished by this sacred song, Akatsunao-no-kami, Tamanohime-no-kami, Ikuyohime-no-kami, and Machiaihiko-no-kami first bowed deeply to Chikaramitsuo-no-kami, knelt before the presence of the deity of Mototsu Magoto to apologize for their lack of courtesy, and proclaimed a kototama song.

The sacred song of Akatsunao-no-kami.

'O my foolish spirit-line, dwelling close

By the side of the Lord God, I knew it not.

How could a spirit clouded thusly

Perform the divine work of creating kuni and birthing deities?

In the fleeting moment when I realized the Lord God,

I stood in awe of the deity of Mototsu Magoto.

I regret the foolishness of my own body, which knew not

That the deity of Mototsu Magoto appeared before me.

The magoto of Ametsuchi is not far away,

But has shone brightly right before my eyes.

How ashamed I am, who prayed in an empty shrine

Without knowing the deity of Mototsu Magoto.

O Lord God, who art the deity of Mototsu Magoto,

Forgive me for my discourtesy.

From today, washing my spirit and purifying

And clearing my kototama, I shall serve in the creation of kuni.

Without hearing the guiding words of Hitokoto,

My heart is ever more filled with uncertainty.'

§ 3

The Kami of Mototsumakoto sat in majestic solemnity, His sacred countenance shining ever more brightly, and it was a wonder that He did not utter a single word. The Kami of Tamanohime chanted a sacred poem.

Morning and evening, while devoutly worshipping Your sacred form, Alas, I did not realize You were the Lord Ōkami. Forgive me my sins of irreverence until this day; The Kami of Mototsumakoto was indeed the Lord Kami. Manifesting as the Kami of Water and Fire of U-A here now, You have brought forth the Kami of Mototsumakoto. Morning and evening, the Kami of Mototsumakoto labored, Saying, 'I shall build a jewel palace for You.' Without knowing the profound shigumi of the Lord Kami, I foolishly thought of Him merely as a miyatsukasa. That I could not thoroughly accomplish the task of kami-birth, Was because the tamashihi of my soul had grown clouded. Now, realizing why I had grown old in years, Without serving in the work of kami-birth when the time came; Even though young, my chieshiyōkaku being insufficient, So that the Miko of the Kunitsuhashira should be born. The mikoto of the Kami of Ikuyohime is crystal clear; I feel it is right that she is heavy with child. Even the task of bringing forth kami through the yorisashi of the Lord Kami, If the soul is clouded, there is no way to serve.

Did the Lord Kami cause my kototama to return to Ikuyo, Making me realize that it was clouded? Oh, my foolish tamashihi, my clouded Kototama, how sad and how shameful! This day I have been able to realize why The Mizumitama was delayed and why my years have passed.

The Kami of Ikuyohime chanted a sacred poem.

Through the true makoto of that fleeting moment when the Mizumitama yearned for me, The Miko was conceived within me. Enveloped in kohigumo, I too have been revived By the Amatsumakoto of the Lord Kami. Revived by the makoto of water and fire of the Lord Kami, In that fleeting moment, I have conceived the Miko. From this day forth, as I nurture the Miko, with all my heart Shall I apply my mind and put aside all romantic love. Bound by the threads of the shigumi of the Lord Kami, Have I perhaps been deranged for a short while? Now my heart has opened and my soul Must have been revived in the eternal spring. As the Kunitodamakami of the land of Mashirazu, Morning and evening I shall reverently protect the Miko. How awe-inspiring and how precious it is That within my humble body the Lord Kami's mitama is lodged. Serving closely by the side of the Kami of Mototsumakoto, My tamashihi has leapt up in spirited joy. Climbing up to this hill, I have realized That the Lord Kami is none other than the Kami of Mototsumakoto.

Be that as it may, obeying the Mizumitama, I remained reverently silent. The sacred body of the Kami of Mototsumakoto was pure light; All that was reflected in my eyes was merely the moonlight. Feeling as though a true mirror had been hung before me, I stood illuminated, filled with shame. From this day forth, the waters of Lake Tamano shall grow shallow, As my clouded heart grows clear. There is not a single cloud or mist, and my soul Is bright and pure like the sun and the moon.

The Kami of Chikaraitsuomikoto chanted a sacred poem.

Morning and evening purifying myself at the jewel spring of this hill, I knew not the true Kami. Was the Kami of Mototsumakoto indeed the incarnation Of the Lord Kami? How ashamed I am! Serving upon the hill of Tamano, which has become the mitama of the Lord Kami, I lived without knowing the true Kami. The Mizumitama, pure and valiant, Has conceived a Miko upon the shores of Lake Tamano. From this day forth, reverently assisting the divine work Of the Mizumitama, I shall build the land. Since the party of the Kami of Tōmiomikoto are performing purification at the foot of the mountain, I shall go forth to welcome them.

Here for the first time, the Kami of Mototsumakoto chanted a sacred poem with clear and resonant kototama.

How awe-inspiring are the words of the Kami of Chikaraitsuomikoto! Giving my assent, I shall help bring forth and build the land. Come now, we shall return to the Amatsutakamiyaya. O all ye hundreds of kami, be vigorous and well!

Having chanted thus, the Kami of Mototsumakoto, who was the incarnation of the Lord Kami, was suddenly enveloped in deep purple clouds descending from the heavens, and returned high into the distant sky—how truly majestic and venerable it was.

『瑞御霊わがあこがれしたまゆらの 真言に御子は孕みたまひし 恋雲に包まれし吾も主の神の 天津真言によみがへりたり 主の神の水火の真言によみがへる そのたまゆらを御子孕ませり 今日よりは御子育むと村肝の 心くばりて恋を忘れむ 主の神の経綸の糸にしばられて われは暫しを狂ひたるかも 今となりて心開きぬわが魂は とこよの春によみがへりけむ 真鶴の国の国魂神なれば 朝夕御子をつつしみ守らむ いやしかるわが体内に主の神の 御霊やどらす畏さ尊さ 本津真言の神の御側に近く仕へ わが魂線は勇みたちたり この丘に吾登りてゆ主の神は 本津真言の神と悟りぬ

兎も角も瑞の御霊に従ひて 吾はつつしみ黙し居たりぬ 本津真言の神の御身は光なりき わが眼にうつるは月光のみにて 真寸鏡かかりし如き心地して 吾恥づかしく照らされて居し 今日よりは玉野湖水あせむ わが曇りたる心晴るれば 一片の雲霧もなしわが魂は すみきらひたり月日の如くに』

力充男の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『この丘の玉の泉に朝夕を みそぎて真の神知らざりき 本津真言神はまさしく主の神の 化身なりしか吾恥づかしも 主の神の御霊になりし玉野丘に 仕へて真の神知らざりしよ 瑞御霊清しく雄々しくましまして 玉野湖畔に御子孕ませり 今日よりは吾つつしみて瑞御霊の 神業助けて国土造りせむ 遠見男の神の一行は山麓に 禊したまへば迎へ来らむ』

茲にはじめて本津真言の神は、言霊朗かに御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『畏しや力充男の神の言葉 我うべなひて国土生み助けむ いざさらば天津高宮にかへるべし 百神等よ健かにあれ』

斯く歌ひ給ひて、主の神の化身なる本津真言の神は、忽ち天上より降り来る紫紺の雲に包まれて、久方の空高く帰らせ給ひしぞ尊けれ。
