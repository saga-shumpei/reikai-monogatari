Chapter 1: The Asahi-maru [351]

第1章　朝日丸〔351〕

§ 1

East and west and north and south, Upon the tranquil spring sea surface, Departing ships and entering ships, full sails and half sails, While the reflections of men and women who ride on them Are also quietly swayed, Upon the waves whose very water is pure, The ship that carries the missionaries of Hinode-no-Kami Is the Asahimaru, Advancing toward the capital of Chiri in Takasago, Where the divine majesty is exalted.

With the east wind that happened to blow, moving swiftly over the sea surface, avoiding to the right and turning to the left around the countless large and small shadows of islands, the sailors steer skillfully and advance through this matchless scenery under heaven.

The sun, having kicked the waves of the eastern sea and leaped forth, finally submerged its form in the western sky, and the sea surface turned into pitch-black darkness. Countless large and small fishing fires flickered upon the sea surface, and the shouting voices of fishermen were such that one might mistake them for the sound of raging waves. The light of the fishing fires hung long down into the sea, swaying in the waves as though dragons were ascending from the seabed to the water's surface; the spectacle was beyond compare, to the point that one could almost wonder if the Dragon Palace at the bottom of the sea had suddenly lit lamps of spiritual light.

Many passengers gaze at this scene and indulge in casual talk.

Person A: 'Hey, Sarusehiko, how much does your feeling differ between when you crossed Lake Superior and when you cross this sea?'

Sarusehiko: 'From whom did you hear such a thing? That was someone else. How was it for you at Mount Kōhaku?'

Komayamahiko: 'Mount Kōhaku is Mount Kōhaku. Who talks about mountains while crossing over the waves? Shall we have you tell us the story of the mummy monster instead?'

Person C: 'Rather than repeating such useless tales of past failures to each other, how about having a more sensible conversation?'

Komayamahiko: 'Yeah, they say you have become a believer in something called Sango-kyō, and that you are talking about the doctrine of Sango-kyō all over the place, acting pompous like a missionary; won't you let me hear it once, too?'

Person C: 'Preaching is forbidden to worshippers of Uraruhiko and Miyamahiko like you. Moreover, if we start such a talk on the sea, it would be troublesome if we turned into mummies, so perhaps I should refrain. You said to me, "You are now a believer," but do you not know that even a beggar's child turns three years old after three years? Though a believer at first, now I am a fine, indisputable missionary of Sango-kyō. Who says such a rude thing as "Hey, let me hear it" to a missionary of a living deity? We are, with all due reverence, the missionaries of Konohana-hime no Mikoto of Mount Tenkyō.'

Saruyohiko: "That must be so; he must be from the mad Tenkyōzan."

Hei: "How could a mummy know? There are cases where a mummy falls into the sea and transforms into a [fish]. Look, illuminated by the light of that sun. Many of your friends are swimming at the bottom of the sea; when a mummy becomes a [fish], and lice breed on the [fish]'s head, not a single worldly matter can be understood, even if a blind god of a [fish] listens to the doctrines of the Anai teachings. Saying nothing is far better than speaking."

Saruyohiko puffed out his cheeks and glared at Hei's face. That sulking face was illuminated by the fishing fires and could be seen amusingly and distinctly.

"Well, Saruyo, you are swelling up quite a bit."

Being told this, Saruyohiko puffed up all the more. From out of the darkness came the voices of two or three women:

"Oh, we have heard, if we are informed correctly, that you are a missionary of the Anai teachings; we think that crossing this wide sea safely and comfortably like this is all thanks to the divine virtues of the gods. There is no such fine opportunity as this; would you please let us hear the teachings of the Anai religion? We are people of the land of Kumaso."

Thus they earnestly entreated, whereupon the missionary, paying no heed to the two men, said:

"Whoever you may be, since it is a dark night I cannot see your faces, but I am merely a fledgling missionary of the Anai teachings. Since those two men were blowing their trumpets so grandly earlier, I boasted that I was a missionary of the Anai teachings, but I too am a person of Kumaso, and am still an apprentice missionary and a self-styled candidate. I heard by rumor that a splendid missionary called Hinode-no-kami has gone, or is going, to the island of Takasago, so I wish to go to the capital of Chiri in Takasago and try to meet that person."

From out of the darkness came a woman's voice:

"If you are that enthusiastic, you must surely know a little of the doctrines of the Anai teachings. Whoever has heard even one step ahead is a senior, so please tell us at least what you have heard."

Saruyohiko: "There certainly are curious people in the world. I know not who you are, but what could you possibly understand by listening to such a missionary? This man puts on an important face, claiming to be an apprentice missionary, and poses haughtily, but if you look into this fellow's background, he originally resided at the Dragon Palace Castle, was expelled from there, became a dependent guest at Kijōzan, blundered and failed again at Kijōzan, fled the land of Tokoyo claiming to have reformed, and after doing every foolish and evil deed possible in the land of Tsukushi, he is now on his way back to his former haunt. This fellow has a splendid name like Kiyohiko, but in truth he is a man called Nigorihiko, Dorohiko, and Anahiko. Like a snake past the equinox, he is only aiming for holes. You appear to be women, so do not associate with such a fellow. He is an utterly dreadful fellow. Because this fellow has no hole to slip comfortably into, he spouts that he is a missionary of the Anai teachings. Ahahaha!"

...so he ridiculed with all his might, opening his mouth wide.

Kiyohiko said, "Hey monkey, what nonsense are you spouting? You too acted haughty on Mount Kijōzan, putting on airs as the master of Kuniteruhime, but before you knew it, Bōfurihiko stole that position from you, you became a servant to the foolish Miyamahiko, and in the end, deserted by the crowd, you finally left Mount Kijōzan weeping, you foolish Sarusehiko; if you pick at another's hole, your own hole will be exposed. Viewed from any angle, I am an upright man endowed with wisdom, benevolence, and courage, without any hole. And I am an honorable missionary of Ananai-kyō.

From leaving a hole until entering a hole, caring for holes; wondrously amusing is this world of holes.

It would be better not to pry into other people's holes. No matter how much of a Sarusehiko you may be, rather than merely aping men and bringing shame upon yourself, it will be to your benefit to reform from now on and observe seeing no evil, hearing no evil, and speaking no evil, like the attendants of Kōshin-sama. If you grumble and mutter, shall I turn you into a mummy again?"

Thus they were indulging in idle talk. The short spring night dawned, the light of the sun once more reflected in the eastern sky, and vigor shone upon the faces of all, as though the day had dawned upon them as well.
