Chapter 34: The Crow Tengu [384]

第34章　烏天狗〔384〕

§ 1

The moon shone in mid-sky, stars were scarce in the great firmament, and at the sound of the wind through the pines blowing and scattering the clouds, Satsukihime awakened and fixed her eyes upon the face of Odoyamatsumi,

"Well, well, Lord Odoyamatsumi too, when he is walking like that, has a countenance like a splendid and manly deity, but when he forgets all matters and has fallen asleep, his face appears evil. Is this indeed also the color of his heart? Why on earth is this nose of Lord Masakayamatsumi so red? Just when I thought him a handsome man with a straight nose, what is this nose again, an exceedingly ugly face. Oh, oh, a strange color has appeared on Lord Kakatora's face as well; Lord Kakatora, it is written 'the foremost handsome man in the world.' Ho-ho-ho-ho, what an innocent-looking face. Truly this face is like a deity's. Oh, how unpleasant, 'a man who fell for Satsukihime and is very jealous'—ah, how unpleasant, Lord Komayama has come to have such a strange face. Ho-ho-ho-ho."

Kakatora, while listening to the voice of Satsukihime, endured the hilarity, clenched his teeth, and was laughing inside his mouth, 'Koo-koo.'

Komayamahiko sprang up at the voice of Satsukihime, gripped the hair at the nape of Satsukihime's neck firmly, and said:

"Hey, Suheita, what are you spouting? Spouting that Odoyamatsumi's face is evil, that Masakayamatsumi's face is red, and about liking and disliking, and to top it off, saying 'the foremost handsome man in the world'—taking advantage of me sleeping without knowing, this is too much! Do not mock me, saying Komayamahiko is 'a man who fell for Satsukihime and is very jealous'; this is a world where there is no shortage of women. In the world, the number of men is forty percent and women sixty percent; what is with that mug of yours? Women like you are loafing about on this Takasagoshima in quantities enough to be measured by the basketful. Humph, wearing painted eyebrows drawn high, do not make a fool of me! Thinking people are asleep without knowing, staring at Kakatora's face until you burn a hole in it, and babbling that he is the foremost handsome man in the world—what are you spouting? To your eyes in love, pockmarks would look like dimples, and even a missing nose or a bent waist would look charming. Though the moon shines clear and bright high in the heavens, your breast is the darkness of love. Mixing up miso and filth together—do not make too much of a fool of me, claiming who would fall in love with or be jealous over a lowly servant wench like you!"

Kakatora: "Ku-ku-ku-ku-ku, u-ha-ha-ha-ha, amusing, amusing. Hold on there, ku-ku, having my face decorated with mulberries, my face is red! U-ha-ha-ha!"

At this laughter, the two, Odoyamazumi and Masakayamazumi, suddenly sat up,

"Ah, we were sound asleep, yet it is so noisy; my delightful dream has been broken. You are troublesome fellows. There cannot be much time left before dawn. I must take another nap, so it would be best if you too kept silent and went to sleep."

Komayamahiko, "Hey, Lord Odoyamazumi, look, what is on your face? Lord Masakayamazumi, what happened to your nose? It is a bit strange."

Odoyamazumi, "Strange or not, it does not matter. A face is still a face."

Masakayamazumi, "A nose is a nose. Ah, what a noisy fellow."

Kakatora, "Uwahahaha!"

Satsukihime, "Hohohoho!"

Komayamahiko, "How ridiculous! Is this something to laugh at? Criticizing people's faces, and that from a good-for-nothing fellow."

Odoyamazumi, "Now, now, Komayamahiko, remember the Ananaikyō. Won't you rephrase your words?"

Komayamahiko, "Yes, yes."

Meanwhile, the color of the moon gradually faded, and the surrounding area grew faintly bright all over. As if by mutual agreement, various birds began chirping in the treetops. Several dozen crows flew across the sky crying 'Ahō, ahō' over where the five lay resting.

Kakatora, "Hey, you fools, won't you wake up? Even the crows are crying 'Fool, fool!' What an embarrassing sight to expose to the sun! What is that on your faces?"

All of them sat up abruptly and looked at one another's faces,

All together, "Well, well! Look, look! Who on earth played such a prank?"

Komayamahiko, "It is Kakatora, without a doubt!"

"Reflecting carefully upon your faces, this is surely the doing of the crows. Just a while ago, the crows were crying loudly, 'Caw, caw, caw, caw, it might be Kakatora.' Judging by that, in short, namely, it is the prank of a tengu. A tengu is a fellow who plays pranks, even though he has a black face and a bent back."

Komayamahiko burst out laughing,

"You have finally confessed! Look, on your face it says 'The World's Foremost Handsome Man'; you are making a fool of yourself. Somehow my own face also feels uncomfortable, as if the skin of my face were stretching. Lord Masakayamazumi, please take a look at my face for a moment,"

and thrust out his face.

"Well, well, someone has written and written, and in red ink at that: 'A man who is in love with Satsukihime and gets deeply jealous,' hahaha!"

"Ah, now I understand! Lady Satsukihime, I am sorry; I take back what I said. You saw the writing on my face, didn't you? I thought you were speaking ill of me, so I just feigned anger out of courtesy. I am not angry at all from the bottom of my heart. Please forgive me."

Kagatora: "What a soft-hearted fellow! The moment he sees a woman, he narrows his eyes and bobs his fine man's head up and down—what a spineless coward! Ha-ha-ha-ha-ha!"

Satsukihime: "Something red is attached to all of your faces. Is there not something attached to my face as well?"

All clapped their hands, saying:

"Oh, excellent! Heavenly eyebrows! With that, who knows how many times higher your divine rank may have risen!"

Satsukihime,

"Ho-ho-ho-ho,"

she said, hiding her face with her sleeve while laughing. Okotsuyamazumi straightened his collar, adjusted his posture, and, appearing solemn, said:

"Hey, hey, Kagatora! There is a limit to playing pranks. What is this, coloring all of our faces while we did not notice? Our faces are not storybooks; they are not notebooks!"

Kagatora: "I too am covered in dots. Someone like a bent-waisted crow-tengu must have come along and played this prank. Kaminaobi, who created this world, Ōnaobi, broad of heart; In all matters whatsoever of the human world, Look again and listen again with an upright spirit, And confess and correct the errors of oneself."

Komayamahiko: "What a self-serving fellow! The moment things are inconvenient, he immediately sings a propagation song. Truly a troublesome man."

"The actual prankster is clearly known. Right now I shall point clearly with the tip of my finger to show who it is, so you had better watch where the tip of my finger falls. From where did this prankster boy just now come? Did he come from the east? Did he come from the west? Did he come from the south? Did he come, did he come—it is indeed from the north! Like the bent-waisted crow-tengu in the northwest corner, the greatest handsome man in the world, Lord Kagatora, drew them all—this very nose!"

he said, pressing his own nose to show them. Komayamahiko also said:

"I will draw one on you too; will you not lie down there, Kagatora?"

"I shall leisurely hear the rest tomorrow evening."

"Why do you play such a prank?"

Kagatora, rolling up his sleeves and squaring his shoulders, said:

"There is a profound reason for this. When we cross the upcoming Orochi Pass, we will encounter an immense giant serpent, whose girth is an unbelievable eight hundred eighty-eight jō, and whose body length is eight million eight hundred eighty thousand ri. Therefore, Okotsuyamazumi is to show a frightening face, and Masakayamazumi is to thrust out his red nose abruptly to make the giant serpent laugh and roll over. Kagatora is to make the serpent become fascinated, thinking that this is what the world's foremost handsome man is like. Komayamahiko is to make the giant serpent stare intently, as if boring holes through him, wondering if that is what a fawning simpleton's face looks like. And then it will make the serpent gaze at Satsukihime, who is like a heavenly maiden, marveling at what an extraordinary beauty exists. In other words, to captivate it. If Satsukihime is captivated by the giant serpent, she can never be saved."

Komayamahiko: "Ah, even if we speak of washing our faces, there is no water or anything at all. Shall we all proceed as we are until we reach a place with water?"

All: "It cannot be helped; come, let us go."

saying, and girding themselves with straw sandals and leggings, they set out toward the exceedingly steep Orochi Pass.

駒山彦「ああ顔を洗うと言ったって、水も何も有りやしない。ご一同このまま水のある所まで行きましょうか」

一同「仕方が無いなあ、さあ参りましょう」

と草鞋脚絆に身を固め、あれほどに険しい大蛇峠に向かって足を運んだ。
