Chapter 32: The Rosy Countenance of Morning [382]

第32章　朝の紅顔〔382〕

§ 1

In the valley of Uzuyama Pass, whether by divine design or coincidence, they unexpectedly encountered a hot spring, and in the warm waters brimming without ceasing with divine blessing, they purified body and soul for seven days and seven nights; then, once more chanting the divine words and singing propagation songs, the party of five slowly climbed this pass. At length, the party reached the summit of Uzuyama.

Kakatora said:

"Ah, ah, well is it said that within suffering lies pleasure, within pleasure lies suffering, pain and pleasure are not two, and good and evil are one. When we were suffering, dripping with sweat, a splendid hot spring duly welled forth its waters for us, and without saying anything like, 'Come now, everyone, thank you for your long toils; you must surely be fatigued,' it showed us an example of action before words. And having climbed up this slope again covered in sweat, here is such splendid flat land, and a cool wind blows upon us. It is the surplus wind of paradise. Truly, unless one suffers, one cannot understand the taste of ease."

Komayamahiko also said:

"It was truly splendid. It smoothed out the wrinkles of one's testicles. The wrinkles on your face have smoothed out considerably too."

Kakatora said:

"Do not talk nonsense; I never had wrinkles in the first place. You are always a coward, so you scowl at the slightest thing, and thus you are naturally full of wrinkles. Hey, try counting them; there are plenty of wrinkles. Four times eight, thirty-two wrinkles have gathered."

"What a talkative fellow; your mouth must have been born a thousand years ahead of you."

"I am an elder brother who has passed through aeons: a thousand years in the mountains, a thousand years in the sea, and a thousand years in the mouth."

"You are like a giant python. They say after three thousand years it is born as a human for the first time, but when will you become a human?"

"Human indeed; I am a deity."

"Is that so? Taking names like a mosquito, a flea, or a tiger—names like insects or four-legged beasts—and you call yourself a deity? A deity who perches on a person's head and bites their head, or a deity who tears humans apart and eats them, surely."

"Hey, Koma, your tongue has become quite skilled. Sometime or other you have stolen my specialty."

"Naturally; even by name I am Lord Koma. Like a spinning top, the spirit of words rolls freely."

Masakayamatsumi stood up, pointed toward the southeast, and said:

"Lord Odo-Yamatsumi, far over yonder you can see the verdant high mountains; that land is the land of Uzu. I received the strict command from Hinode-no-Kami: 'Protect the land of Uzu.' However, it seemed to me that there was yet another noble land elsewhere, and I could not settle my mind by any means; so climbing vigorously up this pass, I traveled night and day to the capital of Haru to spread the teachings. Thereupon, apparently as a divine admonition, I met with terrible suffering, and only my life was spared. Thinking on this, we must not assert our own ego. I have deeply repented, realizing that we must only obey the orders of our superiors and meekly perform our duties."

Kagatora said, "They must have thought it unrefined to remain fidgeting in such a long and narrow, rare country. Thinking there must surely be another fine country, it is like the greedy hawk whose legs were split, is it not, Masakayamazumi-sama?"

Odo-no-yamazumi said,

"Now, now, Kagatora, you are always like this, and it is troublesome. Why are your kotodama so foul?"

"This is outrageous. Are you obstructing my right to speak?"

"No, it is not so. I only cautioned you because, if you chatter too much, your voice will grow weary, and it will be troublesome if the power of your kotodama weakens in an emergency. Rather, in celebration of reaching the pass, shall we engage in some light idle talk and rest leisurely?"

"Is any kind of talk acceptable, and will you truly never stop my right to speak?"

"Fine, fine, say whatever you wish. Please tell whatever story you like, quietly and entertainingly."

"Can a story be told quietly and entertainingly? You demand the impossible. It is just like an order saying: speak while remaining silent, run while lying asleep, or stretch yourself while biting your own testicles. However eloquent Kagatora may be, that is quite out of the question."

Odo-no-yamazumi said,

"Do not be troublesome by overthinking and getting angry. Anything is fine; a slightly loud voice does not matter."

Kagatora sat cross-legged on the lawn, and said:

"Well, human beings get settled when the time comes to get settled. Once upon a time, there was an incomparable, uniquely rare, exceedingly bizarre, indescribably and stupendously magnificent beauty named Ihaihime. From here and there, eager demands came like a shower of arrows asking to take this princess as a wife or to become her husband, but Ihaihime was vain about her own looks, saying: 'I am the supreme beauty under heaven; I refuse to become the bride of such a person; taking such a man as a husband would be like pairing a paper lantern with a temple bell, or taking a crow as husband to a peacock bride; do not underestimate this beauty so much. I, too, shall become a divine propagation missionary under heaven, accomplish an achievement, and when I have become an eminent person, I will have my pick of splendid and authoritative husbands from throughout the world.' Thus, she fired flat rejections right and left at each and every one. Meanwhile, cherry blossoms do not stay on the twigs forever, and as someone from the Sango-kyō or Ananai-hime or somewhere said: The color of the flowers has faded away in vain, while in empty idleness I passed through the world, gazing at the long rains —gradually fine wrinkles gathered on her face, and her past charm faded day by day and month by month. Even so, the color of an old cherry tree that somehow remained was truly something stupendous. Ihaihime remained haughty, saying, 'Whatever happens, as long as I become an eminent person, the world values first stature, second looks, and third money'; but at last divine punishment struck her, and there is a true story that she married a man named Katoriwake—who had a name very similar to mine—a peerless bald-headed, gourd-faced, craven-waisted man covered in scabs, and became a propagation missionary. If Satsuki-hime does not make up her mind soon, with the rosy cheeks of the morning turning into the white bones of the evening, there will be no one left to look back at her, and even if she stands at the gate like Sei Shōnagon and asks whether anyone will buy her old bones, there will be no buyers left."

§ 3

Komayamahiko burst out laughing and said,

"Ahahahaha, how skillfully done, indeed I am impressed. But Kagatora, do not worry. Just the other day, after you rushed off saying you would fight with a tengu, Lady Satsukihime said, 'Master Kagatora is indeed dark-skinned, but he is a cheerful person. If I were to go on the propagation journey together with him, it would not be bitter in the least. It would be delightful, and I would forget even the fatigue of travel.' She was saying so. Isn't that right, Lady Satsukihime?"

Thus he peered into her face.

Lady Satsukihime placed her sleeve against her face and cast her head downward in embarrassment.

Kagatora said, "Hmph, as the world's most handsome man, my attractive power must be formidable."

Masakayamatsumi said, "Ah, whether in Master Kagatora's eloquence or his courage, one cannot become a missionary of the realm without such traits. If, like us, one goes to the capital of Haru and sings propagation songs, and having no eyes in the back of one's head, is stabbed through by a camel caravan, skewered like potatoes, and thrown into the desert, one cannot function as a missionary at all. From now on, should I follow Master Kagatora's example and train my mettle?"

Komayamahiko said,

"Hey, friend Kagatora, congratulations."

Kagatora replied, "Eh, do not be jealous."

"Even if you say not to be jealous, the sun scorches our heads enough to singe them. What is scorching is the current weather. It is so hot that you have become a little flush and giddy. If there were water, I would pour it over your head, but unfortunately, there is no water at this mountain summit; what a fortunate fellow you are."

"Come, come, everyone, our sweat has mostly dried. Let us now slowly descend the pass,"

So saying, Odotsumiyamatsumi stood at the front and began to walk.

"Ah, ah, how tactless at the crucial moment,"

Kagatora muttered in a low voice, and repeatedly looking back, descended the slope while stealing glances at Lady Satsukihime's face.

「ああ、ああ、肝心の正念場に気の利かないことだわい」

と蚊々虎は小声で呟き、振り返り振り返り、五月姫の顔を盗み見に眺めつつ坂を下る。

原文（近代文語）

駒山彦は吹き出し、

『アハヽヽヽ、うまいのう、イヤ感心だ。然し蚊々虎、心配するな。此間も貴様が天狗と喧嘩すると云つて駆出した後で、五月姫さまが、「蚊々虎さまは本当に色こそ黒いが、快活な人ですね。妾あの人と一緒に宣伝に行くのなら、一寸も苦い事はありませぬわ。面白くて旅の疲労も忘れて了ふ」と言つていらつしやつたよ、ねえ五月姫さま、さうでしたね』

と顔を覗き込む。

五月姫は、顔に袖をあてて愧かしげに伏向く。

蚊々虎『ヘン、天下の色男、俺の吸引力は豪いものだらう』

正鹿山津見『あゝ蚊々虎さまの弁舌といひ、勇気と云ひ、さう無くては天下の宣伝使には成れませぬ。吾々のやうに、巴留の都へ行つて、宣伝歌を歌つて居ると、後に目が無いから、駱駝隊にグサリと突かれて、芋刺と成り、沙漠の中へ放り込まれる様なことでは、宣伝使も何もあつたものではない。これから一つ、蚊々虎さまに傚つて、胆玉でも練りませうかい』

駒山彦は、

『おい蚊々公、お目出度う』

蚊々虎『エー妬くない』

『妬くないと云つたつて、天道様も焦つくほど俺らの頭を焼くではないか。焼くのは此頃の陽気だよ。あまり暑いので、貴様は一寸逆上せ上つたな。水でもあれば頭からブツかけてやるのだが、生憎山の頂辺で水も無し、幸福な奴だワイ』

『サアサア皆さま汗も大分乾きました。これからぼつぼつ峠を下りませう』

と言ひつつ先に立つて、淤縢山津見は歩き出した。

『あゝあゝ、肝腎の正念場に気の利かぬことだワイ』

と蚊々虎は小声に呟き、振返り振返り、五月姫の顔を窃み目に眺めつつ坂を下る。
