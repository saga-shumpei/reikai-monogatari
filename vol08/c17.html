Chapter 17: Friend and Foe [367]

第17章　敵味方〔367〕

§ 1

The storm, fierce enough to twist and fell the trees on the summit, ceased completely, and overhead the burning sun began to shine. Ōdoyamatsumi, descending this mountain ever westward together with Kagatora, said,

"Hey, Kagatora, how are your legs? Have they grown a little lighter?"

"Yes, I am fine now; in this condition, I feel as if I could go through any steep mountain or cliff, even a journey of ten million ri."

"If you do not stay firm, there is a danger that Magatsu will possess you. Say what you will, your reform of heart is still insufficient, and because your soul is not settled in your lower abdomen at all, you are possessed by various Magatsu. That is why your legs become heavy, and you suffer and whine. Magatsu greatly fear our crossing this mountain to go to the land of Haru. That is why they possess you, whose resolve is unsettled, and make you whine. As long as your soul is firm, even if hundreds of millions of evil deities come, they cannot lay so much as a single finger on you."

"Indeed, that is true. Nay, from now on I shall stay firm. I have spoken much ill of you; will you forgive me?"

"There is no question of forgiving or not forgiving; it was all spoken by the secondary guardian spirit possessing you. It was not you who said it."

"Anan-kyō has a sweet loophole, does it not? Even though I intended to revile you so thoroughly, was it still the secondary guardian spirit that spoke?"

"That is right. They were the words of an evil deity or a four-legged beast."

"Even so, I distinctly remember that I myself certainly said it."

"Well, the one that remembers is the four-legged beast; Tora's primary guardian spirit shrank into the background, and the secondary guardian spirit spoke such worthless things. Kagatora and the secondary guardian spirit are, well, pretty much the same."

Kagatora, "Then am I the four-legged beast of a secondary guardian spirit? Is that not too cruel? I have completely lost track of what on earth you are talking about."

"If it were the words of a human being, I might get angry a little or argue reason, but at any rate, it is not worth arguing reason."

"Well, how strange. I cannot comprehend or accept it at all."

"Well, that is fine. You need only do as I say. In time, your body and soul will be polished, and your primary guardian spirit will become active."

§ 2

Forgetting the fatigue of their journey in such talk, the two steadily descended the mountain path overgrown with miscellaneous trees. Beside it hung a fairly large waterfall, scattering spray. Looking, four or five rough men were seated in front of the waterfall, whispering something seemingly in amusement. When the two were about to pass before them, one of the men spread his arms wide and blocked the valley path,

"Hey, wait a moment! Where are you from? This is the land of Haru. It is the domain governed and protected by the administrator Takatoriwake. Not a single person from other lands is permitted to advance past this waterfall. Turn back at once!"

he glared at them. Gagatora, rolling up his sleeves and tying a twisted headband, said,

"What of the land of Haru? What does Takatoriwake matter? Most reverently, this is the passage of Otoyamatsumi, the Great Missionary of the Anai teachings. If you obstruct us, it will not be to your benefit!"

The man blocking the path said,

"I am a man called Arakuma, who guards the barrier of the land of Haru. If you pass without heeding what I say, just try passing. It will not be to your benefit!"

"Well said! When I say it will not be to your benefit, you mimic like a monkey and spout that it will not be to my benefit. Yes, I understand that too. When one receives something from someone, one sometimes gives a gift in return. Hold on there—a word received and a word returned; don't try to be clever! Just what do you take me for? I am entirely different from a four-legged soul like you that has become a vessel for a secondary guardian spirit. I am the genuine, pure Gagatora the wolf, through whom the Primary Guardian Deity acts! Bow down, bow down! If you spread hindrance across the path of the god, it will not be to your benefit. Hey, Arakuma, no more returns are needed!"

"This fellow is an obstinate one! Hey, all of you, come here, won't you? Gather together, the five of you, and strike down this dark fellow!"

"Ahahaha! Gagatora is indeed a lordly tiger! That you cannot do anything unless five of you set upon me alone—whether you are weak or I am strong, I do not understand in the least from root to leaf. Hey, Arakuma's group of five, if you would attack splendidly, just try attacking!"

he said, clenching his fists, thrusting his arms straight forward, and rolling his black eyes round and round.

"Hey, you! I know not whether you are the bone of a horse or the bone of an ox, but you are an exceedingly boastful fellow! Is that all? Roll your eyes more, flare your nostrils, open your mouth, you monster!"

"If I let you speak, there is no telling what you will utter. If you mutter on, with this fist upon your cheek—clatter, clatter, Kagatora is from the land of Haru!"

"Hey, hey! Fall upon him! Fall upon him! Knock him flat! Knock him flat!"

Thus Arakuma gave the command, while Kagatora spat upon both his hands and said,

"Come on, all five at once! One or two of you are too bothersome. All five of you fellows, bunch together, bind together, and attack at once!"

"What, bunch together like split firewood or brushwood to attack? Save that boast for later! Do not wipe your tearful face; late regrets will be of no use!"

With that, they leaped upon Kagatora from front and rear, left and right.

"Well, for all that, he is a fellow with quite some resistance. Hello, pro-pro-prophet, perform the chinkon, perform the chinkon! Do it with all your might!"

"Well, it would be best for you to be thoroughly buffeted. Since your jaw is so remarkably glib, have at least your nose twisted off for you. Ahahaha!"

"That is too heartless; I cannot abide it! Is it not the duty of a prophet to help at such a time? Would you leave a man to die? Hello, hello, there, there, now my arm is being wrenched off! Oww, it hurts, my arm is coming off! Hey, Arakuma, do not be so rough! Cannot you fight more gently?"

"In a fight, what does hard or gentle matter? Eat this iron fist!"

Faster than the words were spoken, he struck him a solid blow. The four men were clinging tightly, sinking their teeth into Kagatora's arms and legs on both sides.

"Hey, you four fellows, do not let go of that! From now on, whether I jab or strike this Kagatora's body is entirely up to me!"

"Hey, jabbing or striking is fine, but do not do anything too cruel! Give a little discount; make an allowance!"

"Even if you speak of giving an allowance, I hate losing a fight! If I were a sawyer, I might saw wood in any way, but I refuse, I hate it! From now on, I will wring off your living head! Ah, how amusing, heave-ho! With a face like yours, it is white-faced indeed! Wahahaha!"

Just as he laughed, he tumbled headlong with a thud from the cliff toward the bottom of the ravine. Surprised, the four men let go of the arms and legs they had seized, whereupon Kagatora regained his vigor and said,

"Now, all is well! I shall bury all of you at the bottom of this ravine too!"

The four men were trembling with fear, clinging desperately to the rock.

"Ahaha! Coming right in front of me, struck down, it seems, by my spiritual radiance, that fellow Arakuma flipped backward down to the valley floor! Hey, henchmen of Arakuma, won't you raise your faces? Shall I flip you over with my spiritual radiance? Everything is fine now. Hello, prophet, aren't you entirely too cowardly? To take the enemy's side instead of siding with your ally shows that you are quite a blockhead! That is precisely why you are called O-do-ya-ma-tsu-mi! You must be overawed by this divine power of Kagatora. From now on, become my luggage carrier!"

『オイ突くのも撲ぐるのもよいが、あまり酷いことをするなよ。ちつと負けとけ、割引せい』

『俺は負けと云つたつて、喧嘩に負けるのは嫌ひだ。木挽なら何の様にも割挽くが俺や止めた、嫌だ。貴様の生首をこれから捻ぢ切つてやるのだ。アー面白いドツコイ、貴様の面ぢや面黒いワイ。ワハヽヽヽ』

と笑ふ途端に崖から谷底目がけてヅデンドウと落込みける。四人は驚いて掴まへた手足を放したれば、蚊々虎は元気づき、

『さあ大丈夫だ。貴様らもこの谷底へみんな葬つてやらう』

四人は慄ひ戦き、岩に獅噛付いて居る。

『アハヽヽ、俺の真正面に来よつて、この方の霊光に打たれたと見えて、荒熊奴が仰向けに谷底にひつくり返つた。オイ荒熊の乾児共、面を上ぬかい。俺の霊光にひつくり返してやらうかい。もう大丈夫だ。もしもし宣伝使様、貴方はあまり卑怯ぢやないですか。味方の味方をせずに敵の味方をするとはよつぽど好い唐変木ですよ。それだから貴方はおーどーやーまーづーみーと云ふのだ。この蚊々虎の御神力に恐れ入つたらう。これからは荷物持ちになれ』

§ 4

So saying, as he blew the great conch and looked around, the form of the missionary had vanished like smoke and was nowhere to be seen.

"Ah, what a weak missionary. I wonder if this fellow too was thrown down to the bottom of the valley; ah, what a pity. Even the brushing of sleeves is karma from previous lives, and stumbling over a stone is the edge of a bond; though he came all this way with great effort, has he been hurled into the bottom of the valley together with Arakuma? Ah, what a pity; ah, human beings are truly unfathomable. Until now he spoke haughtily, bringing up old pretexts like 'Bunbuko, Bunbuko,' and talking so grandly, but what is the meaning of this miserable state? The past is the past, and now is now."

Thus he was carried away, seating the four men before him and spouting his complaints alone. Thereupon, in a fluent voice,

"The deity appears out in the open, Separating good from evil, Kaminaobi who created this world."

such a missionary song was heard coming.

"Oh, another missionary? Who could it be? For the voice of a ghost trapped at the bottom of the valley, it somehow possesses strength. Strange; such strange things can indeed happen."

As he was thus speaking to himself, Odotsuyamazumi climbed up, carrying Arakuma, who had fallen to the bottom of the valley, upon his back, caring for him while singing the missionary song.

"Well, well, m-a monster; it has disguised itself well!"

"Hey, hey, Bunbuko, it is me. I am no monster at all, but a true person. A missionary's role is to separate good from evil. Because you babble such pretexts, I cannot sympathize with you. Rather, I felt sympathy for Arakuma and rescued him from this peril. In the way of the deities, how could there be enemies or allies? The fundamental principle of Anaikyō teaches that among allies there are enemies, and among enemies there are allies. Though you are my ally, you misunderstand the divine will and instead become an enemy. This Lord Arakuma spoke absurdities against us and seemed like an enemy obstructing our passage, but this teaching does not regard an enemy as an enemy, and turns an enemy instead into an ally. How about it, do you understand?"

Bunbuko, with a dubious face,

"Heeh,"

and, making a face as if he had eaten tasteless miso, tilted his head, sucked his finger, and stood dumbfounded on the mountain path.
