Chapter 35: The Realm of Hifumi [385]

第35章　一二三世〔385〕

§ 1

Sent on their way by the singing of countless birds chirping in the trees and the sleepy cries of the large brown cicadas, they climbed panting up the steep slope beneath the blazing summer sun. Their sweat flowed like a waterfall, and their painted faces were so ruined that even red sweat trickled down in an unsightly mess. Wiping away their sweat time and again, the party at last reached the summit. On the mountaintop, rocks of suitable size were scattered in good proportion. The party of missionaries each sat down upon a rock to catch their breath.

Bikutora: "If only it were within my power, I should like to make this cool breeze a gift for my mother."

Komayamahiko: "Hey, Bikutora, you speak as if you were admirable. 'If only it were within my power, I should like to make this cool breeze a gift for my mother'—what a dutiful son you are! For someone so filial, you did nothing but indulge in dissipation, causing your parents worry, and as they say, while no parents weep for lack of children, many weep on account of them; if you possess such a tender heart, why did you leave your parents behind to wander about all over this place? When one's words, heart, and deeds do not agree, it is an offense to the gods."

"Human nature is fundamentally good. What child does not think of their parents? Drifting helplessly upon the waves of this fleeting world, the curtain fell on the tragic parting of parents and children weeping in all directions. As they say, parents and children are for one lifetime, husband and wife for two, and master and servant for three."

Komayamahiko said,

"Hmph, you talk slick! Does that mean your parents do not matter? Claiming that husband and wife are for two lifetimes, intending to remain together even in death and believing you will be husband and wife for two or three lifetimes—how pathetic! No matter if it is Satsuki-hime, who would pledge eternal fidelity to a hunchback like you?"

Bikutora said,

"Gazing upon the sky of my homeland, I wonder / how the parents I left behind in my home province may fare."

Komayamahiko said,

"Oh dear, there he goes again! What is with you, rattling off such admirable words only today, acting like quite the poet in the hope that Lady Satsuki-hime will think, 'Ah, Master Bikutora may look like that, but deep down he is a gentle person; even if his back is bent, his face is dark, his looks resemble a clown's mask, and he is a bit flustered, at the very bottom of his heart shines a gentle and beautiful jewel of devotion to his parents; if I were to become husband and wife with such a person, what a truly harmonious home we would build; if I am to have a husband, I should like to marry such a gentle man'? You are an exceedingly shrewd rascal! Ahahaha!"

§ 2

Odoyamazumi said,

"Ah, admirable! Humans must be so; one who forgets the favors of parents, higher than mountains and deeper than seas, is not human. You are not rotten yet; you are a dependable man."

Komayamahiko said,

"Hey, do not get cocky; since you are a man who easily gets carried away, if you are flattered too much, you will be knocked flat. You are the type to be made to climb as high as the Mountain of Tenkyō, only to be dropped down with a thud and a bump. You value a wife more than your parents, don't you? Quoting sayings like 'parents and children for one lifetime, husband and wife for two lifetimes,' you speak circuitously, implying, 'Lady Satsukihime, if you become my wife, I will treasure you even more than these precious parents,' posing a clever riddle. It is truly ingenious, isn't it?"

Buntora, with a triumphant look, said,

"Hey, Koma, you are an incomprehensible fellow. I shall proselytize to you now, so listen attentively to my precious sermon. As for 'parents and children for one lifetime,' no matter how much of a wicked son bringing tears to parents one may be, even if the parents become disgusted and say, 'Never again shall you step over the parent's threshold; this is a disownment for seven lifetimes,' parents and child remain parents and child after all. Even if you say, 'If you disown me, then disown me; I will take other parents elsewhere,' the parents who gave birth to you are only one after all. That parents and children are for one lifetime means that, whether laughing or weeping, standing or falling, there is only one time. That is why it is called 'parents and children for one lifetime.' It is a bond of parent and child that cannot be severed even if one tries to sever it. Your thinking is probably the logic that one is parent and child while alive, but once dead, one is neither parent nor child, but complete strangers. How can one serve as a missionary with such nonsense?"

Komayamahiko: "You are truly a fellow who fabricates logic for anything; what does 'husband and wife for two lifetimes' mean? You probably have the selfish thought that it is even better than parents, that even after dying you will take each other by the hand to cross the Sanzu River, share the same lotus pedestal, feast on hundred-flavored foods and drinks, and live intimately as husband and wife. The wholesale merchant will not sell so cheaply. Try going to Paradise, shrinking small upon a lotus pedestal, holding each other as husband and wife, and sitting quietly in a muddy pond. Since you are someone who never does any good anyway, while you are exulting, saying things like, 'You are an impregnable fortress; the relationship between you and me, not merely for a thousand or ten thousand years, but even unto the world of Miroku fifty-six hundred and seventy million years later, if you and I remain like this, this is the true Paradise; isn't that right, Lady Satsukihime? When we were in the physical world, we were teased plenty by Komayamahiko's spite, but once it has come to this, we have got it made,' someone remaining in this mortal world who knows your past villainies will surely gossip about you. Every time they gossip, you will sneeze, and at that very moment, the slender lotus stem will wobble, and both of you together will splash down into the muddy pond, shivering and trembling, and end up as drowned corpses. For one who has died once, where can one who has died a second time possibly go? There is nowhere. And then, being reborn into the mortal world again, coming forth with hands hanging downward near the hips, wailing 'Whoosh, rumbling, how bitter and resentful!'—that is surely the punchline. You had better not say such pointless things as 'husband and wife for two lifetimes.'"

§ 3

Kagatora said, "Ah, be quiet! You have snatched away my specialty, and what a fellow you are, chattering glibly like a master swallow. Listen reverently to this sermon of Lord Kagatora. That 'husband and wife are of two worlds' does not mean what you think it means. Husband and wife are Yin and Yang. Do you know of the matter that when He proclaimed, 'Having grown and formed, there is one place that has grown in excess; having grown and formed, there is one place that has not grown together; what if I should plug that place of your body which has grown in excess into that place of my body which has not grown together, and give birth to children?' She responded, 'That will be good'? Husband and wife are the beginning of the world. In anyone's home, if there are no husband and wife, a harmonious household cannot be made. And thus they give birth to children. Those children, in turn, give birth to parents."

"Hold on, wait, wait, do not derail. It happens that children are born from parents, but where does it happen that a child gives birth to parents?"

"You are a fellow who does not understand. A man and a woman who form a household—that is husband and wife. There, when the breaths of the husband and wife harmonize, a child is born crying 'waa-waa.' What is born is none other than a child. Because a child has come into being, the name of 'parent' is attached. A childless couple is neither parents nor anything of the kind. How can you become a missionary without understanding reason of this degree? And consider if, through misfortune, the husband dies or the wife dies prematurely. If it is after a child is born, it might still be acceptable, but if the wife precedes him in death while there is no child, the divine work of childbearing in the creation of heaven and earth cannot be performed, can it? Human beings harmonize the breaths of man and woman, give birth to and multiply people upon this earth as numerous as the stars in heaven, and assist the work of God. Therefore, when one becomes a widower or a widow and cannot perform that divine work, one marries a husband or wife of the second world. This is what is called a wife of two worlds. It is somewhat different from your cunning thought of flirting sufficiently in this world and then flirting again after going to the hidden world. And if the wife of two worlds, again unfortunately, dies beforehand halfway without bearing a child, the husband resigns himself to it, knowing it is heavenly fate. To have a wife as many as three times is a violation of the heavenly laws of the divine realm. Therefore, God forgives, saying that if it is unavoidable, up to a second wife cannot be helped. That is what is called 'husband and wife are of two worlds.' Ah, to make a single missionary is laborious work; my shoulders and arms are creaking!"

Kagatora said, "If you say 'will you not,' then I will tell you. When having someone teach you something, one should indeed humble oneself. For you are having virtue bestowed upon your person. Ahem! As for master and servant for three lifetimes, to give an example: this Master Kagatora was formerly a servant when Lord Odoyamadzumi, who is right here, called himself Shikotsuwake and did nothing but evil deeds. However, thinking that continuing to serve such a master would lead to a terrible future, I thought I would somehow dismiss him. Having done so, however, I could find no good master at all. Right in the midst of searching, a magnificent missionary called Hinode-no-kami appeared. Therefore, this self is serving my master of the second lifetime, Hinode-no-kami. And even if Lord Odoyamadzumi calls me 'Kagatora, Kagatora' and treats me as a servant, my heart is completely indifferent. In contrast, once I have decided on Hinode-no-kami as my master and gone before him, how about that? I observe the duty of a subject faithfully, and if, by some chance, I realize that Lord Hinode-no-kami was an evil deity due to my misjudgment, then indeed, as if casting away worn-out sandals, I shall dismiss my master. And then I will search again for an appropriate master and serve him. That is what is called master and servant for three lifetimes. Even if the third master is an even worse fellow than Shikotsuwake, I can no longer change him. When it comes to that point, I will steel my resolve, saying, 'Ah, it is in accordance with the divine will, it is karmic affinity,' and revere him as my lifelong master. Changing one's master or changing one's teacher up to three times is permitted by the deities in unavoidable circumstances, but anything beyond that is a violation of the heavenly laws. Since master and servant for four lifetimes cannot be, the deities have set the limit, saying, 'Master and servant may be changed up to three times if unavoidable.' How about it, Koma? Has my sermon, explained with utmost care, soaked into your intestines? It surely soaks in with a sizzling sound, does it not? Do you agree? That is master and servant for three lifetimes."

All present raised their voices in unison,

"Ahahahaha, ohohohoho!"
