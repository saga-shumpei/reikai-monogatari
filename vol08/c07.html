Chapter 7: The Octopus Priest [357]

第7章　蛸入道〔357〕

§ 1

Kiyohiko, who suddenly manifested a divine form of radiant brightness in the dark, was endowed with absolute and infinite divinity, and his whole body shone all over so brilliantly that gazing up at him dazzled the eyes.

Saruyohiko and Komayamahiko crouched in awe before this form and held their breath, when Kiyohiko's form vanished abruptly, and a loud voice that seemed to tear the ears was heard coming from the dark.

"Saruyohiko, Komayamahiko, listen well. As you know, until now I was deceived by the soul of the eight-headed, eight-tailed giant serpent, and that I committed every possible wrongful deed is just as you know full well. However, since I heard the teachings of the God of Great Mercy of the Anaikyō, what my own self had done until now became terrifying and shameful to me; following after Hinode-no-Kami, becoming a true human being, in contrast to my past evil, I prayed day and night to God that I might perform only good deeds and show to the world an example that even evil, if reformed, becomes like this; then, the grace of God appeared right before my eyes, and strangely enough, I boarded the Asahimaru, auspicious even in name, met Hinode-no-Kami, and after being bestowed with splendid teachings, my soul was corrected and renewed by Kamunaobi and Ōnaobi, and having now become the pure soul of Kiyohiko, I am the representative of Hinode-no-Kami who illuminates the darkness of the world. You two, taking my reformation as a model, must repent of your evil as soon as possible, in the briefest moment, return to good, be praised as a mirror of the world, and participate in the divine work of Yomotsuhirasaka. According to your reformation, I may meet you again. Separated by the clouds of your hearts, with regret, it has become impossible for me to reveal my form to your eyes. Komayamahiko, Saruyohiko, farewell."

No sooner had he said this than, turning once again into a great fiery light that illuminated all around, he soared into midair and flew away skimming through the sky toward the capital of Chiri.

Saruyohiko: "Hey, Koma-kō, Kiyohiko really went and became Hinode-no-Kami. Shall we stop speaking ill of Kiyohiko from now on? In my anger at him taking us into the depths of the mountain and abandoning us, I hardened my heart and thought I would obstruct him everywhere, but evil cannot last long anyway. Even when you and I gave him all those punches inside the ship, he said his body was made of iron, endured the pain, talked nonsense, and laughed it off—that was no ordinary endurance. When I think about it, we did foolish things. Looking at our behavior at that time, Hinode-no-Kami must have thought in his heart what shameless fellows and what incomprehensible fools we were. When I recall such things, I feel wretched and wish I could disappear."

と言うより早く、またしてもあたりを照らす大きな火光となって空中に舞い上がり、智利の都を指して空をかすめ飛んで去った。

猿世彦「おい駒公、本当に清彦は日の出神になりおったな。もうこれから清彦の悪口はやめにしようか。我々を山の奥へ連れて行きおって、置き去りを食らわせた腹立ち紛れに心を鬼にして、どこまでも邪魔をしてやろうと思ったが、とうてい悪は長続きはしないよ。お前と俺とが船の中で、あれだけげんこつを食らわせてやっても、俺の体は鉄じゃと言いおって、痛いのを辛抱して馬鹿口を叩いて笑いに紛らしていたのは、ひと通りの忍耐力ではないよ。思えば馬鹿なことを我々はしたものだな。日の出神様はあの時に俺たちの行いを見て、何とはしたない奴だ、訳の分からぬ馬鹿者だと心の中で思っていらっしゃっただろう。俺はそんなことを思い出すと情けなくなって消えたいようになってくるよ」

§ 2

Komayamahiko said, "If so, what do you say we do from now on?"

Saruyohiko said, "Well, there is no choice but to reform our hearts. Even if we cannot become such a splendid Hinode-no-Kami as Kiyohiko, at least in some modest way we might become propagators, atone for our past sins, polish our spirits, and participate in the divine work at Yomotsu-hirasaka. In any case, we cannot achieve complete reformation of heart, but at least let us refrain from speaking ill, and go about to save the world. And if we achieve one meritorious deed, Kiyohiko, the Hinode-no-Kami, will surely meet us again. At that time, as splendid propagators, it will be just as we wish: to work as the right-hand men of Ame-no-Mihashira-no-Kami. Shall we decide to propagate each by himself, one by one, from now on?"

Komayamahiko said, "Very well, very well."

Thus the two parted ways here, and went forward, letting their feet take them wherever without a destination, singing propagation songs falteringly. The night faintly began to lighten. Saruyohiko proceeded to the south, and Komayamahiko went ever northwards.

Saruyohiko, with steam rising from his shiny head, sang through the seaside villages, straining his shrill voice with all his might. Upon arriving at a certain fishing town, four or five fishermen looked at Saruyohiko's strange appearance and said:

Fisherman A: "Hey, you are all lamenting that there has been no catch due to the state of the wind recently, but Heaven does not forsake humanity. Look at that; a large octopus is walking this way. What do you think about capturing it alive and cooking it?"

Fisherman B: "Hush! Do not speak so loudly; it is listening! If it hears us, it will flee, it will flee!"

Fisherman A: "How could an octopus hear us? When someone refuses to listen no matter what you say, people say calluses have grown over his ears, do they not? Pay no mind, pay no mind; speak up loudly, speak up! Hey, you octopus priest coming over there! I am a fisherman of this village, and I have been in trouble because there has been no catch recently. Won't you give me your octopus-like head?"

Saruyohiko: "Ah, are you gentlemen the fishermen here? I desire very much to offer you the octopus, but with this irreplaceable octopus head being the only one I have, regrettably I cannot give it to you."

Fisherman C: "What are you grumbling about? If you will not listen, so be it; if you will not give it, so be it. All of us together will gang up and catch this octopus!"

Saruyohiko: "That is fine. Please go ahead and fish individually. I shall pray that an octopus may be caught."

§ 3

Kou said, "You will pray. But can octopuses that cannot be caught like this really be fished up?"

Saruyohiko said, "Why would they not be caught? That is what makes one a deity. If fishing is too troublesome, you too should unite your voices as I say and sing the propagation songs. The octopuses will smoothly crawl up from the sea of their own accord and enter into the bamboo baskets you hold. Then shut the lid and carry them back home."

Saruyohiko blurted out such things on the spur of the moment.

Otsu said, "Hey, Master of Octopuses, if we truly do as you say, will the octopuses come up?"

Kou said, "Look, that is a settled matter. After all, the boss says so; how could his subordinates not come out? That is precisely why I tell you fellows to listen to what this boss says. When I call, sometimes they come and sometimes they do not; you fellows are truly indiscreet rascals. Excuse me, Master of Octopuses, please call the octopuses."

Saruyohiko turned toward the sea surface and, straining his shrill voice, began to sing the propagation song. The fishermen followed after him and sang in chorus. Round whirlpools formed in various places upon the sea surface, and the heads of the octopus-monks popped up one after another. Saruyohiko,

"Come, come,"

beckoned toward the octopuses. As his voice ceased, octopuses leaped boundlessly into the bamboo baskets. This became a widespread topic among the fishermen, and they came to revere Saruyohiko as Hinode-no-Kami. From that time forth, this fishing village was named Takotori Village.

Several tens of chō to the west of Takotori Village, there was a great waterfall called the Arina Waterfall. Saruyohiko built a small hermitage there, and came to propagate the doctrines of the Anana teachings to the people of this region.
