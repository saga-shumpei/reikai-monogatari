Chapter 36: The Back of the Great Serpent [386]

第36章　大蛇の背〔386〕

§ 1

The missionary disciples were either impressed or laughing at the amusing lecture of Kagatora, extolling his eloquence with one voice. Just at that moment, from out of nowhere, a foul-smelling wind blew toward them, rustling with a roaring sound.

Komayamahiko said with surprise,

"Look, it has appeared! That sound must be the noise of a giant serpent. We must prepare ourselves. Shall we tighten our sashes before we go?"

Kagatora said,

"Masakayamatsumi must have told nothing but lies, saying that a monstrous serpent lives in this mountain, intending to test our courage. Speaking of long things, not even a single earthworm has appeared on our way here. Well, let us fully take in this cool breeze, and sing and dance so that the serpent will come. 'They say there lives a serpent on Mount Orochi, a giant, giant serpent, so they say; but it is all a lie, so they say.'"

Masakayamatsumi said, "Master Kagatora, we are, after all, missionaries of the realm; we never speak falsehoods. Giant serpents do not dwell in such densely wooded places. If you descend this pass a little, the mountain is covered only with overgrown grass. Once we reach that grassy place, large ones are moving back and forth, left and right, in great numbers here and there. It will be a grave matter if we encounter the king of the serpents. Let us recite the divine words for the safety of our journey."

"Then, was it merely Kagatora's serpent-suspicion?"

Komayamahiko said,

"Look at you making a pun again! Who says such carefree things while passing through the Serpent Pass? No matter how glib-tongued Master Kagatora may be, when you actually encounter the real thing, you are bound to roll up your banner and retreat!"

Kagatora deliberately put on a dejected face and said,

"Is that so? I do not fear any enemy, but having no experience with giant serpents, I feel somewhat dread-serpent-stricken. Komakō, you go first this time; I shall take the middle of the five."

Komayamahiko said, "Serves you right, you coward!"

Thus arguing, they descended rapidly eastward along the Serpent Pass. While his heart pounded nervously for no clear reason, Komayamahiko feigned courage, singing a missionary song as he went down the Serpent Pass. His voice trembled inexplicably. Kagatora said,

"Hey, guide, what is the matter with that voice? It is trembling, isn't it? Making such a half-crying voice, if the serpent gentleman hears it, he will take you for a woman and leap upon you!"

§ 2

Komayamahiko, shrinking his neck,

"Ah, it has come out, it has come out! It is an immensely huge fellow. If such a fellow lies across this mountain path, passing through is utterly impossible,"

said he, and with a thud he sat down by the roadside. Kagatora said,

"Let me see, let me have a look,"

and putting his right hand to his brow,

"Ah, hey, it has come out, hey, it has come out! It is an exceedingly and boundlessly thick fellow. From the mountain over yonder to the mountain right here, it lies just like a suspended bridge. This is amusing. Hold on, the Serpent Pass with a black tail and a black head! Hey, Master Koma, today is your chance for the glory of the first spear. I usually take the vanguard every time, but being too forward would bring ill fortune. Today I shall concede the vanguard to you. Now, will you not stand up? Ha-ha-ha, have you lost your nerve, O seated missionary Komayamahiko?"

Komayamahiko said,

"S-somewhat my legs have grown heavy and I cannot walk. Friend Kaga, I beg of you. Please go on ahead."

"Hold on, that will not do; a superior man does not approach danger. Like summer insects flying into the fire, just imagine being flicked up by such a long fellow! Then indeed humans like us would go flipping, flipping, flipping toward heaven. In the momentum of being tossed flipping, flipping, flipping, I would reach Mount Tenkyō with a quick fling and arrive safe and sound. As for you people, you are the sort who merely ascend or descend!"

Okitsuyamatsumi said, "What faintheartedness! Kagatora too has lost his nerve. Well then, I shall bear the responsibility and serve as the vanguard,"

and dauntless and unafraid, he strode briskly forward. Okitsuyamatsumi advanced to several dozen paces before the reclining giant serpent, but said,

"Ah, with a fellow so thick lying here, I can neither straddle it nor leap over it; I must give this some thought. Kagatora, do you have any clever idea?"

"Whether there is one or not, whether it can be crossed or cannot be crossed, whether you are afraid or not afraid!"

Komayamahiko asked, "Which is the truth? Crossing or not being able to cross, which is the truth?"

"Just watch the extraordinary feat of Lord Kagatora,"

he said, and striding briskly up before the giant serpent, clenching his fist, and patting the belly of the giant serpent tap-tap,

"Hey, hey, Master Giant Serpent, having received life within the same heaven and earth, why were you born into such an unsightly serpent body? I am a noble and pure missionary who proclaims the salvation of God. It must be painful for you as well to dwell in the depths of the mountains forever in such a wretched form. Suffering the torment of three colds and three heats in a single day, despised by people and feared by people, yours is truly an unfortunate fate. I sympathize with you. I shall now recite the Amatsu Norito for you, so be reborn as a splendid human being as soon as possible. In return, take the five of us upon your back and carry us to a place where the capital of the Land of Uzu can be seen. Do you understand?"

§ 3

The giant serpent raised its sickle-shaped head, shed tears in drops from both eyes, and bowed its head time and time again.

"All right, I understand. You are an admirable fellow. The four missionaries present here are blind, so they do not know my origins in the least, but it seems you have seen through to my true identity. Very well, very well, I shall spare you."

The giant serpent again let tears fall from both eyes, lay prone, and, as if telling them to climb on quickly, waited with its long body arranged in a triangular shape.

Bunbutora, beckoning with his hand, said:

"Hey, you fellows—hold on, you teachers—climb on, climb on quickly! Your feet must be quite weary. Serpent Teacher has pleaded, asking to be allowed to convey our party near the capital of the land of Chin. Come, quickly, quickly, get on! It will be a shame if you miss your ride!"

The entire company was astounded, unable to say a word, standing rooted to the spot in dazed silence. Satsukihime said:

"Everyone, what do you think? Shall we accept the ride?"

Komayamahiko was dumbfounded and said:

"Well, well, what a bold woman! How could we possibly bear being carried on such a thing?"

Bunbutora leaped lightly onto the back of the giant serpent, waved his hands, danced with his feet, and nonchalantly began singing a propagation song. Satsukihime strode forward quickly and leaped lightly onto the back of the giant serpent.

Komayamahiko: "Ah, even a woman possesses such courage! Ah, what does it matter what happens! Everyone, how about it? Shall we ride it?"

Odotsumatsumi: "That sounds fine. Lord Masakayama, what do you think?"

Masakatsumatsumi: "Indeed, I shall ride as well."

And here, the five missionaries leaped onto the back of the giant serpent.

Bunbutora said:

"Now, it is the Serpent Limited Express! Run, run! Borne upon the back of the giant serpent, The wicked deities, deep in suspicious doubts, Worrying about this and worrying about that, Go racing toward the capital of Chin; The toads riding upon the giant serpent, Before long, all the way to the capital of Chin, Are the toads led back home— Is this not truly delightful!"

Bunbutora was singing whatever came out without restraint. The giant serpent, winding along and shaking its long, huge body back and forth, left and right, vigorously descended the mountain.

駒山彦『ヤア女でさへもあの膽玉だ。エイどうならうと構ふものか。皆さま如何でせう、乗つてやりませうか』

淤縢山津見『よからう、正鹿山さま、如何でせう』

正鹿山津見『イヤ私も乗りませう』

と茲に五人の宣伝使は、大蛇の背に飛び乗りたり。

蚊々虎は、

『サア大蛇大急行だ。走つたり走つたり。

大蛇の背に乗せられて じや推の深いじや神らが 如何じや斯うじやと案じつつ 珍の都へ走り行く 大蛇に乗つた蟇蛙 軈ては珍の都まで 引かれて帰る蟇蛙 ホントに愉快じやないかいな』

蚊々虎は出放題に歌ひゐる。大蛇は蜒々と、前後左右に長大なる身体を振動かしながら、勢よく山を下り行く。
