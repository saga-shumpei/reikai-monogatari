Chapter 37: Chinzan-hiko [387]

第37章　珍山彦〔387〕

§ 1

The party of propagators, riding upon the back of the great serpent, descended to the foot of the mountain with the momentum of a thousand leagues at a single dash.

Komayamahiko, with a triumphant face, said,

"Well, 'Try riding a horse, try being with a person, try straddling a great serpent,' as they say. Peaches are easier than apricots. We came quite easily. If it is like this, even if I encounter a great serpent from now on, it will not be in the least frightening. In all our travels ahead, if our Target comes to us whenever we come to a mountain, it will truly be handy."

Kakatoko said,

"Lord Serpent, that is enough; please drop us off [orochi-te] here."

Looking, the five propagators had been set down upon a wide lawn. And the great serpent had become visible in neither shadow nor form.

Komayamahiko: "What is this, was it a dream? Even though I actually intended to be riding the great serpent just now, sitting upon such a lawn as this, Komayama cannot make heads or tails of it at all."

"It is a divine work of supernatural wonder. In the teachings of the Sango, it is surely stated that one must never despise someone, for one knows not what splendid personage may be fallen into ruin. Even though this Lord Kakatoko appears wretched like this, he is a splendid deity! He is merely disguised. Therefore, whether it be a great serpent or whatever it may be, all things in the universe become completely subservient at a single word from this Lord Kakatoko. Rebuking wind, rain, thunder, and lightning, and shaking heaven and earth are also completely at our command with a single snort of the nose!"

Komayamahiko: "There he goes again. Hey, won't you stop boasting already? I am utterly bewildered by your stormy wind."

Otsuyamatsumi, being flabbergasted, said,

"What passes understanding is Kakatoko's divine power. By some chance, this fellow might be a monster."

Masakayamatsumi: "Monster or whatever, isn't it fine? Using such a huge great serpent at will is something that, even if we were to strain ourselves until sparks flew from our eyes, would amount to a stone turtle stamping its feet. It would come to nothing; he is a great personage. Masaka too was impressed."

Satsukihime also said,

"I was truly impressed."

Komayamahiko stepped forward and said,

"'I was truly impressed,' she says! Lord Kakatoko, congratulations!"

Otsuyamatsumi also said,

"Until today we have been calling you Kakatoko, Kakatoko, but this definitely must be declared anew. Shall I bestow some sort of name upon you?"

§ 2

Masakayamazumi was also dumbfounded, saying,

"Well, how would it be if, owing to the divine power that used the giant serpent, we named you Orochihiko?"

"I decline Orochihiko. It is Uzuyamahiko. I would like to be called Uzuyamahiko."

Odoyamazumi also said,

"Ah, that is truly a fine name. In that case, from now on, we shall address you as Lord Uzuyamahiko."

Kagatora: "Quite so, quite so. I shall change the name of Kagatora."

Satsukihime: "Ho, ho, ho, ho, ho, what an elegant, fine name it is. To me, Lord Uzuyamahiko feels much more pleasant than Lord Kagatora."

Komayamahiko pursed his lips, saying,

"Ho, ho, ho, ho, ho! 'What a fine name, I like Komayamahiko better than Lord Kagatora'—did she say that? No, she says, 'I like Lord Uzuyamahiko better.' Humph, she makes a fool of me!"

Masakayamazumi said,

"Everyone, let us hear the talk along the way. Far to the east there is a slightly elevated forest. There, small houses are lined up in great numbers like river snails emptied out of a basket. That area is the Capital of Uzu. Now, just a little further. Would you do me the favor of coming to my house and taking a leisurely rest? In celebration of nearing the capital, let us chant a divine prayer here and proceed while singing a propagation song."

With this, everyone sat upright upon the grass, and having finished chanting the divine prayer, they advanced toward the capital while singing the propagation song.

Masakayamazumi sang:

"Leaving behind the Capital of Haru, Pouring sweat on the summer mountain; Fanned by the cool wind, The heart becomes like autumn. Surpassing even the color Of the autumn foliage on the treetops, The missionary messengers with pure soul and body; Crossing over Uzuyama Pass, Spending the night at the Chibiki Rock; Crossing the pass of Mount Amakumo, So high to look up at, the five deities, Carried upon the ship of the giant serpent, At last have reached the moonlit sky here; This divine island of Takasago, Where Tsukiteruhiko dwells in peace, Is the land of Uzu chosen by the gods; The capital of flowers also drawing near, The steed of the heart is stirred with courage; God appears openly To distinguish good and evil.

Kaminaobi who created this world, Broad-hearted Ōnaobi; Looking left and right at Ōnogahara As we pass through, how pleasant it is; The white walls seen over there Are my dwelling in the Capital of Uzu; In the human world, regarding everything, Re-see and re-hear it in the right mind; Even the name of Lord Kagatora Has been renamed Uzuyamahiko; Noble children of the Heavenly Deities, Appearing as precious treasures, Missionary messengers who open the world: Odoyamazumi and Satsukihime, Komayamahiko of spirited heart, And in the height of summer, Masakayamatsumi no Mikoto, a party of five; Rescued by the God of Sincerity, At last have arrived at the capital; At last have arrived at the capital."

"Whether the morning sun shines or is clouded, Whether the moon waxes or wanes, Even though the great earth should sink, The Ananaikyō taught by the true God Shall save the world; Manifested as the gods of salvation, The five pillars of the Izu-no-Mitama, And the moon's light of the Mizu-no-Mitama— Endless is the grace of God, Endless is the grace of God."

Singing thus with an engaging melody, the party of missionaries at last arrived at the mansion of Mashikayamatsumi.

Komagamihiko said,

"Well, for the residence of a missionary, this is a luxurious structure."

Uzuhiko: "That is only to be expected. He is the guardian of the entire province of Uzu, so it is quite natural."

From within the gate, many servants came rushing out in fluster, saying,

"Oh, Master, welcome home! Everyone has been craning their necks waiting, wondering if you would return today or tomorrow. Come, come, you must be weary, so please rest quickly. And oh, welcome, whoever you noble guests may be, thank you so much for escorting him home. Please drink some hot water at your leisure and make yourselves comfortable."

Mashikayamatsumi said,

"Oh, Kunihiko, you took good care of the house in my absence. Well done. Now, everyone, this is an unsightly, humble dwelling, but please do come in without reserve."

Odoyamatsumi also said,

"Following your words, let us rest without hesitation,"

and following behind Mashikayamatsumi, he sat down comfortably in the inner room.

Kunihiko respectfully boiled some hot water and brought it, saying,

"Ah, honored guests, between the mountain paths and the recent heat, you must surely be exhausted. I hear that my master was also greatly indebted to you. Thank you so much for saving his life. The bath will be ready in a moment, so please take a bath at your leisure and relax."

Having finished his greeting, he vanished toward the other room.

The four missionaries opened their hearts to one another, talking about passing the night upon the rock, having tricks played upon them, and their impressions upon encountering the giant serpent, chattering together merrily and delightfully.

Opening the sliding door, Mashikayamatsumi said,

"It seems the bath is ready. How about it, everyone? Shall we get in together?"

Uzuhiko: "That sounds enjoyable; let us join you."

"Please, this way,"

he said, leading the way. The entire party cast off their clothes beside the bathtub and leaped in all at once with a splash.

と、正鹿山津見の後に随いて、奥の間にドツカと安坐したり。

国彦は恭しく湯を沸かして持ち来り、

『ヤー、御一同様、山道と云ひ、この頃の暑さと云ひ、嘸お疲労でせう。承はれば、主人も偉いお世話になられたさうで御座います。よくまあ生命を助けてあげて下さいました。今お湯がすぐに沸きますから、どうぞ悠くりと湯浴でもして、お寛ぎ下さいませ』

と、挨拶を終つて、部屋の方へ姿を消す。

四人の宣伝使は打ち解けて、岩上に一夜を明かし、悪戯をされた事やら、大蛇に出会した時の感想を語り、面白可笑しくさざめき居たり。

襖を開けて、正鹿山津見は、

『どうやらお湯が沸きました様です。皆さま何うでせう。一緒に這入りませうか』

珍山彦『そら面白からう、一緒に願はうかい』

『どうかこちらへ』

と、先に立つて行く。一同は浴槽の側に衣服を脱ぎ捨て、バサバサと一度に飛び込みぬ。

§ 4

Uzuyamahiko said:

"Well, well, the feeling of getting into the bath is truly exceptional. This is what is meant by taking one's ease in the bath. Silence is far superior to speech. Shall I speak, or shall I remain silent? If I do not speak, the worm inside will speak after all."

Komayamahiko said, "Hey, what are you saying? You are making merry all by yourself as if in high spirits."

"Still, it is delightful. Is there anything as splendid as a bath? What a thing for you to say!"

Thus Uzuyamahiko and Komayamahiko bantered with each other in the bath, washed away their sweat for a while, got out of the bath together with all the others, and when they returned to the original room, they found delicacies and fine dishes from the mountains, fields, rivers, and seas laid out. Expressing gratitude for that kindness, the whole party at length finished the evening meal.

Centered around Masakayamatsumi, facing the altar where the deity of the land was enshrined, they chanted the Amatsu Norito, finished singing the propagation songs, indulged in enjoyable conversation, became utterly exhausted that night, and all slept soundly; awakening at the eighth hour the next day, they were once again absorbed in various conversations.

珍山彦は、

『ヤアヤア、湯に入つた気分はまた格別だね。湯々自適とはこのことだ。ゆはぬはゆふにいや勝る。ゆうて見ようかゆはずにおこか。ゆはな矢張り虫がゆふ』
