Chapter 20: The Paper Tiger [370]

第20章　張子の虎〔370〕

§ 1

Odoyamazumi, together with Arakuma no Takahiko and four others, quietly descended the mountain of Brazil, heading ever westward. Far ahead there was an open plain, where lights twinkled here and there through the darkness of twilight. Looking far into the distance ahead, the light of torches shone brilliantly, and the clamor of a great crowd was heard. The party hurried toward the direction of those voices.

Looking, several hundred people had formed a wide circle around Bunbuntora, shouting something at him. Bunbuntora mounted a high platform in the center, and spoke:

"Though the morning sun shine or be overcast, Though the moon wax or wane, Even though the great earth should sink, The power of sincerity shall save the world. God appears upon the surface, To divide the good from the evil.

Hey, you scoundrels of the land of Paru! The war dividing good and evil is about to begin. The battle of Yomotsu-hirasaka is pressing near before your eyes. What are you glancing about restlessly for? From morning to night, you dote upon the propaganda songs of Uralhiko—pitch black, in which not an inch ahead can be seen—singing, 'Drink and make merry, an inch ahead is dark night, after the darkness the moon will rise,' and you are a lot who do nothing but guzzle liquor until your very entrails rot. He who has graciously set forth from the Golden Mountain, the great propagandist of the realm, Tsuneteruhiko, is none other than myself. Listen closely! I will make you consent and bring you to repentance. Hey! You blind fools, you deaf fools, how about it? Will you repent? If you will, state it like men, resolutely right here to God!"

From among the crowd,

A: "Hey, hey, what is with that fellow, spouting off so pompously? It looks like he desperately wants a drink. Isn't he shouting, 'You listen to Uralhiko's propaganda songs and drink all the liquor, not giving me a single drop'?"

B: "You misheard him. That fellow is a propagandist of the Ananai teachings, shouting at us not to drink liquor, saying that if we drink it our entrails will rot and we will die. His words are somewhat disagreeable, but he jumped alone into this great crowd wishing to save us, and, staking his life as a target, speaks such forceful words. No matter how much courage one may have, unless one casts oneself away, one could never utter such bold things."

C: "What, he is a madman. Could anyone say such ridiculous things with a normal mind? When everyone says first is liquor, second is women, and third is gambling, he tells us to cast away that greatest pleasure. Since he is only going to babble things displeasing to us anyway, he walks about like that staking his life as a target. If we do not listen to him a little, divine favor will be forfeited."

Kō: "I don't know what it was, but the other day when the missionary of Uraruhiko came, he had many gourds at his waist, drank sake in gulps all by himself, and kept urging us to drink; yet however much he told us to drink, we didn't have any sake and couldn't drink it, and licking our lips with pale faces, listening enviously as that missionary drank it deliciously and babbled drunkenly, was hardly clever. Rather than that, like that fellow, refraining from drinking oneself and telling everyone 'don't drink, don't drink' is still better. At least our hearts won't be warped."

From among the crowd, a man dead drunk with sake bared one shoulder, revealing skin like that of a dark-skinned person suffering from jaundice, and tottering step by step, advanced toward the missionary,

The man: "Hey, hey you! Aren't you spouting that one shouldn't drink s-s-sake? Hey, is it bad to drink sake? What a fool. Just what kind of blockhead is it who spouts that one shouldn't partake of something so delicious? Hey! Do you think we can make our way through this world without sake, you fool? In everything and anything, without sake, the dawn wouldn't break and the sun wouldn't set in this world! And you! Sake, oh sake, is there anything in the world as sweet as sake? Do you know that there is nothing more painful than quitting sake, worse than the four hundred and four ailments? There's no talking to a blockhead like you! Go home at once! The homely hag at my place spouts 'don't drink sake, don't drink' day after day; talk about disgusting, I get so angry my bowels boil over! So I get stubborn and force myself to drink sake that I don't even dislike! And on top of that, I don't know where you're from, but what do you mean by telling me not to drink or consume sake, just like my wife? You're truly making a fool of me; if my wife hears something like this, she'll bicker and say, baring her white teeth and slanting her acorn eyes, 'The honorable missionary of Ananai-kyō said that if you drink sake your bowels will rot.' It's not the least bit amusing! Won't you leave quickly before my wife comes out, you bothersome fellow? What the hell are you babbling?"

Paying no heed to the drunkard's words, Bunbuntora raised his shrill voice:

"In the world, there is no fellow as bad as sake; Ruin of the home is on account of sake; Becoming divorced is on account of sake; Quarreling is on account of sake; Discarding one's life is on account of sake; The arising of scolding is on account of sake; Yapping and barking is on account of sake; There is no fellow as bad as sake. The evil fellow who gets drunk on vile sake that rots the bowels And babbles incoherently— Well, well, how pitiable! If you drink sake, drink water instead."

As he begins to sing thus, the thoroughly drunken man becomes increasingly enraged and punches Bimbitora squarely on the side of his face. Bimbitora again raises his shrill voice:

"Hitting a person is also on account of sake; Quarrels between husband and wife are also on account of sake."

Man: "Are you still spouting that? A stubborn fellow. Shall I hit you more?"

Bimbitora closed his eyes and roared "Oon!" at the thoroughly drunken man, whereupon the thoroughly drunken man, swaying unsteadily, tumbled down onto the stony field nearby, struck his forehead, and is bleeding like a waterfall. From among the crowd:

Person A: "Hey, hey, the drunken man has fallen down. Oh, what is that? Isn't he bleeding? Won't someone rescue him?"

Person B: "Even if you say to rescue him, there is not a single person in this vast land of Haru who would deal with such a fellow. He is called Dead-drunk Tora, Limp Tora, Quarrelsome Tora, and is a fellow of terribly bad drunken behavior. If one were to touch even a single finger to him, he would pick a quarrel, slump down, grumble for ten days or twenty days, and drink free sake. If you deal with such a fellow, indeed your house, your storehouse, your mountain, and your fields will all be drunk away. Do not deal with him; leave him alone, leave him alone. If he dies, it will be good riddance for everyone. The people throughout the country might even pound rice cakes and celebrate."

Person B: "Is that fellow the widely rumored heavy drinker, Quarrelsome Tora? Ah, noisy, noisy; you have said it well."

Tora: "Wh-wh-who is it that calls me Dead-drunk Tora, Grumbling Tora, Quarrelsome Tora? Where did I ever babble or pick a quarrel? No, I will not stand for it! Who do you take me for? I am Master Tora, second to none even in this vast land of Haru. What is strange about Master Tora drinking sake? When drinkers get drunk, they all shake their heads, and one and all become papier-mache tigers. Wh-wh-what is wrong with a tiger drinking sake? 乙『彼奴が噂に高い酒喰ひの喧嘩虎か。やあ煩さい煩さい、よう云うて呉れた』

虎『だ、だ、誰だい、俺をグデン虎のグヅ虎の喧嘩虎だと、何処に俺がグヅを巻いたか、喧嘩をしたか。さあ承知せぬ、俺を誰様と心得て居る。俺は広い巴留の国でも二人とない虎さまだ。虎さまが酒を飲むのが何が不思議だい。酒飲みは皆酔うと首を振りよつて、誰も彼も張子の虎になるのだ。虎が酒飲んだのが、な、な、何が悪い。さあ承知せぬ、貴様の家は知つとるから之から行つても家も、倉も、山も、田も、御註文通り飲んで遣らうかい。二人の奴、酒の燗をして置きよらぬかい』

§ 4

...he glared, opening his acorn-like eyes wide.

Kō and Otsu: "Excuse me, Master Tora or whatever you are called, we are truly sorry to have offended you. I never spoke of you. I said that there is reportedly such a person in another country. It troubles me if you misunderstand."

"No, no! Are you trying to fool me? Anything will do; it is fine if you just drink! Hey, the child of the great serpent with eight heads and eight tails is me! Drinking everything up is my trade."

The two were frowning and bewildered. Kakatora appeared on this scene and said:

"Hey, Master Tora, you drunkard, what are you muttering about? Look at my arms! Who do you think I am? I am a missionary of the Ananai Teachings. If you are the Brawling Tiger, this one right here is Kakatora! Shall we begin a match between tiger and tiger?"

"Wh-what, a brawl? I like brawling next to sake. Well now, shall I have a brawl as a snack with sake? That will be amusing."

So saying, Tora stood up and leaped toward Kakatora. Kakatora remained calm and composed, yielding his body to his strikes. At such a moment, piercing the darkness,

"God appears openly, And separates good and evil."

thus the propagation song with a refreshing voice came to be heard.
