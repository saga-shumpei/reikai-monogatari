Chapter 4: The Crow's Bride [354]

第4章　烏の妻〔354〕

§ 1

The waves are at Takasago, the sun shines all across; In the capital of Chiri, the moon is clear.

Thus the boatman began to sing with an amusing melody, exposing his voice to the sea breeze. Just at the moment when Hinode-no-Kami began to patiently explain the exalted virtues of the deities of heaven and earth to the people aboard the ship, the whole sky suddenly clouded over, a hurricane blew violently, the waves began to rise like mountains, and Sarusehiko and Komayamahiko, who until now had been full of vigor, turned pale and were trembling in a corner. The numerous passengers all clung to the bottom of the boat, driven by feelings of anxiety over whether their lives were candles before the wind, and began to murmur prayers for various things.

Inside the ship, the voices of people suddenly stopped completely, and only the small sounds of prayer were heard. The sound of the waves grew ever louder, occasionally showering the vessel with seawater as they raged furiously. At this time, Hinode-no-Kami raised his voice and called out in a mighty voice:

'Ruling over Takamanohara, The divine decree of Ame-no-Mihashira-no-Ōkami I revere, and beneath heaven, To every corner of the lands in all four directions, I proclaim the divine teachings, A missionary illuminating the dark night, The Kashima departure of Hinode-no-Kami. For the sake of God, for the sake of the land, In order to save the people of the world, Across the eight hundred tidal routes, the countless brine ways, Parting the brine, I advance. I am the noble child of God. The sincere hearts of the multitude Who reverently listen To the teachings of Mizu, Pity them, O Amatsukami! Save them, O Kunitsukami! Deities of Shinato, deities of Mikumari, Righteous deities, what is the meaning of this? The wave-paths are high, to become the debris At the bottom of the Dragon Deity, O Naruto Straits!

Even across the swirling expanse of the ocean, With a sincere heart thinking of the divine land, A missionary walking upon the path. My kotodama is the voice of the true God That fills heaven and earth. Ruling over the vast ocean, Where now are the divine decrees Of Unabarahiko and Toyotamahime-no-Kami? Where now are the divine decrees Of Tamayorihime-no-Kami, enshrined as divine? The wind and rain rage, the waves rise high, Threatening these many people; The twisted deities of Ōwatatsumi, Purify them with your breath, blow them away! If you have no power to breathe and blow them away, And if my kotodama is not heard, I shall from this moment, In place of the deities of heaven and earth, Perform the declaration of Sango, O Watatsumi-no-Kami, O Shinatohiko, O Shinatohime!

Swiftly become calm all across, be still! Swiftly become calm all across, be still!'

When he thus proclaimed the pure kotodama toward the wind, the storm that had been so violent and the heavy rain pouring down like axle-trees suddenly subsided; the celestial sky took off its outer garment of dark clouds, revealing its deep blue skin; the sun shone brilliantly in mid-heaven; and the various birds of the sea leisurely spread their wings, raised joyful voices high overhead, and began crying out together. The deep blue sea surface became calm across like a mirror, giving the feeling of having emerged from hell and encountered the spring of heaven; and the people aboard the ship, with complexions as if revived, gathered around Hinode-no-Kami, thanked him for his divine virtue, and proceeded further to reverently listen to the doctrine. The people aboard the ship were moved by the divine virtue of Hinode-no-Kami, gave rise to faith from the bottom of their hearts, and proceeded further to listen to his teachings.

§ 2

Here, Kiyohiko repented of all his sins up to this point, became a disciple of Hinode-no-Kami, and it was decided that he would attempt propagation on Takasago Island. Saruyohiko and Komayamahiko pursued after Kiyohiko, conspired something together, and landed on Takasago Island.

Once again, the flowers of idle chatter began to blossom within the ship.

A: "Dear me, what a terrifying thing it was. We were on the verge of going to the Dragon Palace, but it is well said that there are no demons in this floating world, and that Heaven does not kill a man. If Lord Hinode-no-Kami had not been aboard this ship, we might have become food for the sharks. If such a thing had happened, I would have resigned myself to death as destiny, but how would my wife and children left behind in our homeland spend the months and days? My wife might weep, saying, 'Ah, my beloved Master Tami.'"

B: "Do not brag about your wife in a place like this. Is there anyone who would weep if you died? There may even be those who would pound rice cakes and celebrate, saying the village villain is gone; and since your wife is intimate with Master Shika, she might pound rice cakes and celebrate, saying the nuisance has been cleared away and the lump above her eye has been removed. If anyone weeps, it would only be a crow, or a cicada perched on a persimmon tree crying out. Ahahaha!"

Tami: "Do not make a fool of me; how could there exist anyone in this wide world who rejoices at a death? In all heaven and earth, there is one husband and one wife. When I departed from our homeland, my wife clung to my sleeve, and—ahem!—she wept with that gentle face, saying, 'Please come back even one day sooner; if I cannot see your face, the night will not dawn, nor will the day end; I will watch the sky of Takasago every day and wait for you.' That is what I wanted to show you!"

B: "Do not talk nonsense. As if she could ever be called that gentle wife! A beauty with a bald head, a kagura-mask nose, a wide crocodile mouth, and acorn eyes, possessing an utterly unique and peerless countenance under heaven—not even a crow would look back at her!"

C: "You cannot say that either. Some time ago, when I was out working in the fields, a crow came to the nearby woods and kept calling, 'Kakaa, kakaa!'"

Tami: "Why do you not put an end to such talk and pray to the kami? If the waves rise again, there will be no saving ourselves this time."

D: "The other day, too, an emissary of Omonagi or someone was aboard a ship, and on the way from the island of Tsukushi to Mount Tenkyō, the sea grew rough and the ship struck a reef, breaking to pieces with cracking sounds. And it is said that all the passengers died without exception."

§ 3

A person among the people said, "What became of that Tsuranagi-no-Kami? In such a moment, why did he not display divine virtue like Hinode-no-Kami who is present here? Is not the Official of Tsuranagi the Sendenshi of whom we hear rumors?"

Otsu replied, "Yes, he is a Sendenshi. But because he lacks divine virtue, in a matter of life and death, he is a Sendenshi entirely unable to save people. And so he himself sank helplessly along with them, bubbling down, and blew frothy bubbles, crying, 'Ah, ah, this has become a painful, bitter, and distressful matter.' Hence he is the Official of Tsuranagi. Everyone met with a painful and distressful ordeal, and ended up becoming Tsuranagi-no-Kami. Just as earlier, there are Sendenshi who preach sermons like Lord Kiyohiko, and had Hinode-no-Kami not been aboard this ship, Kiyohiko-no-Sendenshi might have met with the same fate as the Official of Tsuranagi. If so, all of us too would have met with the bitter fate of Tsuranagi. We must not forget the divine virtue of Hinode-no-Kami. Ah, how grateful, how grateful we are!"

Thus they whispered to one another. During this casual talk, Hinode-no-Kami, hearing that the ship carrying the Official of Tsuranagi had sunk, felt his chest leap, and across his face, a shadow of anxiety swiftly drifted.

と口々にささやき合っている。日の出神はこの雑談の最中に、面那芸の司の乗った船が沈没したことを聞いて胸を躍らせ、その顔には、さっと不安の色が漂った。
