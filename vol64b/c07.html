Chapter 7: Tiger 角 [1813]

虎角〔1813〕

§ 1

Yamoriwake and Ohana were in a back room, drinking sake together and flirting, when Tonku and Teku poked their faces in, looking like thieving cats peering into an empty house.

Ohana, having caught sight of the two, said: 'Oh, aren't you two, Tonku and Teku, who came to the Reijiyō with Otora-sama? Do you have some business here?'

Tonku, tapping his forehead two or three times with his right hand, said: 'Well, I am truly sorry. We would like to receive a little bit of sakate, if you please...'

Ohana said: 'Aren't you a servant of Otora-sama? This has nothing to do with me. If you want sakate, go and get it from Otora-sama. How rude of you to come sneaking into someone else's room like a doroneko, and you a blackamoor at that, what is the meaning of this?'

Tonku said: 'It may not have a chiyokusetsu connection to you, Ohanasama, but it has a deep, deep connection to Yamoriwake-sama who is here... My apologies, Yamoriwake-sama, for two boorish blackamoors to come intruding while you are enjoying yourself so much, and I am sure it is a great gomeiwaku, but we would like to receive a little bit of kuchigusuri.'

Ohana said: 'What, you say you want kuchigusuri? Have you managed to grasp some dark shadow of Yamoriwake-sama?'

Tonku said: 'Ha ha ha ha, do not play shirajira. It is precisely because we have mitodo a serious romance that we have come to receive our kuchigusuri. Without beating around the bush, just give us twenty yen each, forty yen in total for the two of us, and be done with it. That is cheap, is it not?'

Upon hearing this, Ohana thought: Could it be that Yamoriwake has been keeping another woman besides Otora? And that this Tonku has discovered it and is holding his jiyakuten over him? What a fickle man he is. ...Thus, a sense of jealousy began to arise, and she said: 'Now, Yamoriwake-sama, you must be up to your usual philandering ways again, must you not? Come, Tonku-sama, tell us plainly. If you do, never mind twenty yen, I will give you fifty or even a hundred yen.'

Yamoriwake said: 'Hey, Ohana, what need is there to give money to such people? Do not pay any attention to them.'

Ohana said: 'That is easy for you to say, but if I were to pay attention to Tonku-sama, it would be a bit inconvenient for you, would it not? Now, now, Tonku-sama, do not be reserved. Please, expose Yamoriwake-sama's romance clearly right here and now.'

Tonku said: 'Yes, thank you. You will surely give us one hundred yen, will you not?'

Ohana said: 'But that is one hundred yen for the two of you. I would be troubled if you misunderstood that.'

Tonku said: 'Yes, that is fine. This Yamoriwake-sama always puts on a face like a master and disciple with Otora-sama and speaks of virtuous things, but in truth, they are intimate in secret. I saw a suspicious scene at the foot of Mount Olive one night some time ago. Isn't that right, Yamoriwake-sama? At that time, you gave me ten yen and said, "You must not tell anyone about this..."'

Yamoriwake said: 'Hmm, I certainly remember doing that, but what of it? Even if you say such things in front of Ohana-sama, they are not worth three mon. Ohana-sama already knows about the relationship between me and Otora-sama up to this day, you see.'

Tonku said: 'Even so, if you were to announce such things to the world, you would be in a bit of trouble too, would you not?'

Yamoriwake said: 'How ridiculous. Tonku-sama, if that is all it is, I do not need to hear it. I wanted to hear whether or not this Yamoriwake-sama had a suspicious relationship with another woman. Even without solid proof, it would be enough to know at whose house I was drinking sake or flirting; that is all I wanted to know.'

Tonku: 'Heh, if you give me fifty yen, I will tell you. In the back of a cafe in an alleyway of Jerusalem, Ohanahime-sama and Yamoriwake-sama were having a drink together, making marriage promises, pinching each other's cheeks, and hanging onto each other's shoulders; it was a truly unsightly spectacle that one could hardly bear to watch. I am not the only one who saw it; the maid here is a witness too. And that was today, this month. So, fifty yen each, one hundred yen in total. How is that? It is cheap, is it not?'

Teku: 'Fu-fu-fu-fu, this is interesting. Why don't you have a drink?'

As he pushed a glass toward her, Ohana bristled her eyebrows and raised her voice in a sharp tone:

Ohana: 'Hen, how ridiculous. Acting so pretentious, what is that to me? If I had money to give to the likes of you, even fifty ryō, I would rather throw it into the Jordan River.'

Tonku: 'Very well. If that is your intention, I shall report this to Otora-sama immediately.'

Ohana: 'Please, go ahead and report it. And report to Otora-sama in detail about the intimate affection between Yamoriwake-sama and this Ohana, and show off your loyalty. This Ohana has already cut ties with Otora-sama, and with Yamoriwake-sama, I am now a husband and wife before the whole world, inseparable forever. Please tell Otora-sama that the couple, Yamoriwake-sama and I, send our regards. And please announce to the city of Jerusalem that we have held our wedding ceremony, though I cannot offer much of a tip.'

Tonku: 'Eh, how utterly boring. Very well, I shall go and stir up Otora now.'

With that, he staggered away with Teku, left the cafe, and snuck off toward Otora's spirit castle to make his report.

Otora, having been driven to a state of frenzy by the sudden change in Yamoriwake and Ohana's cold indifference and heartless behavior, had collapsed on the dirt of the garden for a while. When she finally regained consciousness and looked around, the box brazier had been smashed to pieces, and the area was filled with a cloud of ash, turning the tatami mats so black that not even a single weave could be seen. While grumbling to herself, she managed to sweep away the ash with a broom that had become a naginata at the tip, and just as she sat down with a heavy thud, Tonku and Teku came in, staggering and humming a tune in a drunken mood. Tonku opened the conversation first:

Tonku: 'Well, well, Ikumiyasama, you must be very lonely all by yourself. Since you ran out in pursuit of Yaku, we thought your situation was dangerous in the crowded streets where people and horses pass by, so the three of us split up to search everywhere. We could not find you, so we thought we would at least catch Yaku and show him to you, and we even searched as far as the back tenements of Jerusalem, but in the end, for better or worse, we lost sight of him. Then, we stopped by a cafe in the alleyway and were having a glass of wine, Teku and I, when—should I call it a heaven-and-earth upheaval? With earthquakes rumbling and lightning flashing—no, it was truly a massive incident that has broken out concerning your situation. So, without a moment's delay, we have come to report it as our first act of service as your disciples.'

Otora: 'Well, thank you very much. It is because it is you that you came to report it. Is it not that Burabāsa is plotting to send this Otora back to her country by using some gang of thugs?'

Tonku: 'No, heaven forbid. Could it be such a small matter? There could be no greater disaster for you than this, could there, Teku? Even watching from the side, it makes one sick, does it not?'

Teku: 'Truly, I am so angry that my teeth are grinding. That state of affairs is beyond reason or logic. I truly feel sorry for Otora-sama.'

With her Mayuge Sakada, her eyes bloodshot, still wearing her headband and with her sleeves tied back with a sash, she rushed out in a fit of rage without thinking of the consequences. Tonku and Teku, saying 'Hey, this is a serious matter,' followed behind Otora, keeping out of sight, and at a crossroads, they encountered Yamoriwake and Ohana, who were coming along unsteadily in a tipsy mood, with Yamoriwake holding Ohana's hand. Otora just let out a gasp and fainted on the spot. Yamoriwake and Ohana, thinking it would be a serious matter to get involved, pretended not to notice and fled toward Mount Olive.
