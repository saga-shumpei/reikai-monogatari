Chapter 13: The Aching Belly [480]

転腹〔480〕

§ 1

Right in the midst of Matsuyohime singing songs in her joy at meeting her younger sister Umegakahime, a man wearing a black hood and holding a jutte in his hand appeared at the front gate, and while peering at the state inside the gate,

Ko: "Hey, we too received orders from Banko Shin'o of Uralhiko of Armenia and headed here as pursuers, but the situation inside is somehow suspicious. It seems that Gujyagujya is not alone; at any rate, the voices of five or six people are being heard. Even with just one Gujyagujyahime, it is unmanageable unless five rough men like us come forward, so if there are five or six of them, we cannot make a move easily; no matter how much she is Gujyagujyahime, she might not easily yield."

Otsu: "What are you spouting? How could she be Gujyagujyahime? She is Kushyakushyahime."

Hei: "Do not talk nonsense, she is called Kyushakuhime. Your wife also lives in a dilapidated house of nine shaku by two rooms, in a row tenement, spouting 'nine shaku, nine shaku,' but would she be something like that?"

Tei: "Well, if you speak ill of her as kushyakushya, right about now your Onabe might be sneezing, 'achoo, achoo,' and turned into a Kushyamihime."

Inu: "What clueless fellows. She is called Shakuhime; because she has a ladle-like face, she is Shakuhime, and what terribly slow-witted fellows you are. Dealing with the likes of you annoys me to no end; did you hire some serving maid from somewhere and make her your wife? A senryu composed by someone also says, 'Onabe serving rice with a ladle face,' so she must be such a piece."

Ko: "Don't be a fool, Gujyagujyahime is said to be the foremost beauty under heaven. The moment one is glared at by her, whoever it may be ends up crushed into mush. That is why she is called Gujyagujyahime. Hey, hey, just listen; they are saying things like dipping into sanbaizu vinegar, boiling and eating, or that it must be delicious. If you dawdle, there is no telling if you will be devoured by the monster woman Gujyagujyahime."

Hei: "Hey, if we give in to cowardice and fail to accomplish our important mission, there is no telling what our superior will say and how he will snap at us when we return."

Tei: "Whether we are bitten here or bitten upon returning, either way there is no chance of being saved; it is a wolf at the front gate and a tiger at the back gate. Let us summon our courage and proceed to break in."

§ 2

Bo said, "Hey, barging in is all well and good, but that one is a limp princess, so she is a nyū (monk)."

Kō said, "What do you mean, a nyū?"

Bo said, "Still, a nyū is a nyū. She is an old raccoon-dog monster-monk. If she casts her eight-mat-sized testicles over us like throwing a casting net, we won't be able to bear it. We'll be eaten up at once and exposed as bleached bones. That is why people often say: testicles in the morning, bleached bones in the evening."

Otsu said, "Thick-faced (kōgan) applies to you! You are truly a brazen fellow, so at times like this it is convenient for you to lead the way. Stop hesitating; come on, get inside, get inside, starting with you! Hey, Matsu-kō, Ume-kō, why are you dawdling? If you act timidly, Master Take will give you a taste of his fist next!"

Inside the gate, Tokikō heard these voices,

Tokikō said, "What rude fellows! They are mocking Princess Matsudai as 'Matsu-kō,' Princess Umegaka as 'Ume-kō,' and speaking of 'Take-kō.' If they keep spouting nonsense, I will crush them with my fingers!"

Princess Umegaka said, "Look here, Master Toki, you are troublesome precisely because of that. Cease such violent talk as picking people up and throwing them out at every other breath. People will turn up their noses in disgust."

Tokikō said, "Even if they turn up their noses, to let them say such things makes me less than a man; is it not useless to remain silent? In short, in sum, that is to say, it is like being bewitched by a fox. At any rate, shall I go take a peek at the gate?"

Princess Umegaka said, "It is fine to go and look, but be gentle and do not engage them. It is fine to lift them up toward the path of sincerity like the gods (kami), but you must not do violent things like an eagle clutching a sparrow."

Tokikō said, "Yes, yes, I understand. I shall lift them up, toward the kami (god / above)."

So saying, he swung his shoulders and headed toward the gate.

Tokikō said, "Now, I have Lady Umegaka's permission. If I am to lift them up, she said toward the 'kami' (above). Shall I grab and hoist them above my own hair (kami)? My arms have been itching for action for a while now. Well, this will surely relieve my pent-up feelings."

So muttering to himself, he rattled the gate wide open. Still clutching their jutte, the five constables wore faces like doves struck by peashooters at the sudden opening of the gate, staring intently at the gigantic figure of Tokikō.

Tokikō: "Hey, hey! Who is it that stands before the gate of Lady Kujakuhime, the peerless through all ages, unique under heaven, the incomparable beauty, and babbles complaints? You appear to be a constable of the Ural religion. What is that jitte? Bring it here! Bearing such a thin thing like a hemp stalk—shall I bunch a hundred or a thousand of them together and snap them to pieces for you? Hey, you, shall I snatch you up too, like catching a snake?"

Matsukō: "Ah, just as you say, I am an official constable of the Ural religion. Surrender quietly and put your hands behind your back."

Tokikō: "I am the boss of snake-catchers on Mount Altai, the Great Snake-Catcher. Come, spin your eyes meekly! Ah, before I even finished speaking, his eyes went dizzy, and there he lies fallen! A fellow with a weak spine—no, weak eyes! If you do not repent, at the critical moment you shall spin like a top and be seized with dizziness!"

Just at this point, Katsukō came along once again.

Katsukō: "Oh, this fellow is amusing! Black boy, coward, impudent rascal—you seem to be an official constable, but won't you capture him quickly and return home? If you dawdle, I will seize you up into the way of the gods! For such a weakling, a hero like me would not find enough to eat. Shall I have Hachikō and Kamokō boil you up, chew you, and eat you? Since you are shivering with quite terrible cold, if we boil you, fry you into tempura, and eat you, it might warm us up a bit! Lord Toki, how about snatching them up one by one while we are at it, and showing them to Lady Kujakuhime?"

Tokikō: "That is amusing! I will grab four of these fellows with both hands and lift them toward the upper side, so you just snatch up one and bring him along!"

So saying, Tokikō of matchless strength gripped the four men at once in both hands, and while lifting them high above his head:

Tokikō: "Ah, the gate is in the way! What a nuisance—it is a small gate, a low gate, and besides, these fellows are weak!"

So saying, he shut the gate with a snap. In the act of shutting it, the bolt dropped with a clatter. Katsukō called from outside:

Katsukō: "Hey, hey, won't you open it, won't you open it? This fellow is quite a tough one! Hey, hey, send help!"

Tokikō: "Hey, hey, Hachi, Kamo, Katsukō is foaming at the mouth outside! You go and reinforce him too!"

Hachi and Kamo: "Right you are!"

And the two opened the bolt and rushed out to the front gate. With great difficulty, they carried one constable on their shoulders and came inside.

と言ひながら、ピシヤリと門を閉めた。閉めた拍子にガタリと枢はおりた。勝公は外から、

勝公『オイオイ、開けぬか開けぬか。此奴は中々手強い奴だ。オイオイ、助け船だ』

時公『オイオイ、八、鴨、勝公が外で泡を吹いて居る。お前も加勢に往つて来い』

八、鴨『よし来た』

と二人は枢を開けて表門に駆け出した。やつとの事で一人の捕手を担いで這入つて来た。

§ 4

Tokikō: "Come, come, master captor, even a stumbling stone is the edge of affinity. Come, partake of a cup of sacred sake. Poison is by no means in it; I will taste it for you."

So saying, he brought down the sacred sake from before the altar,

Tokikō: "Pardon me for going ahead of you,"

So saying, he gulped down a cup himself,

Tokikō: "Now, as you see. I have drunk it, I have drunk it."

Matsukō: "Well, well, this is unexpected. Just when I thought I was to be killed, am I to receive sacred sake? It is my favorite thing above all."

Takekō: "It is like a botamochi in a dream."

Umekō: "It is sake in hell. Come, Harukō, Akikō, you take a cup as well."

Matsukō: "Oh, well, well, maid of the cup!"

Kamokō: "Unfortunately, there is no maid of the cup here; shall Lord Kamo pour it for you? Drink a cup with duck meat as an appetizer, and afterwards, let me hear a rare song from the missionary hymns."

Takekō: "Thank you for treating us to an unexpected feast and saving our lives. Ah, being an arresting officer no longer makes sense; let us retire completely starting today."

Tokikō: "The post of arresting officer is your calling. If you quit it, what do you intend to do?"

Takekō: "I am inherently unskilled, with no work worthy of note."

Tokikō: "Indeed; a fellow who lives doing nothing in this world is a good-for-nothing, an encumbrance upon the earth. The post of arresting officer is your calling, so you must not quit. Take me back to Armenia and make it your achievement."

Matsukō: "A-absurd! If we were to take back a person such as you, an enormous uproar would surely break out."

Tokikō: "Hahahaha, what weak captors you are!"

Umekō: "Ah, no matter what you say, I have grown sick of the captor's role. There is no telling when one's life will be lost. To become an arresting officer, a job shunned by others despite having much work—what a fated birth this is!"

So saying, the alcohol gradually took effect on him, and he began to weep.

Katsukō: "Uhahaha, amusing, amusing! A crying drunk has appeared."

Tokikō: "Just now you said you would quit the role of captor because your life was in danger, but people do not die so haphazardly. Living and dying are both entirely the will of God. No matter how much one wishes to die, one cannot die without God's permission. Even if you think 'I will not die, I will not die,' if God says He will take you to the spirit world, you must die, even while drinking sake. Human life is such that one knows not what will happen even while eating a meal. You had better entrust everything to God and faithfully perform the duties of the day."

Here, the five captors were moved by the unexpected hospitality, and all became fervent believers of the Ananaikyō.

Matsushirohime's party, while having their hair combed by the cold wind, joyfully and courageously pressed forward, crunching across the snowy road.
