Chapter 14: The Vessel Kagamimaru [481]

鏡丸〔481〕

§ 1

Matsuyohime and Umegakahime, accompanied by Tokikō and the others, traveled for many days across the snow-covered plain that stretched as far as the eye could see, and at last reached the shore of Lake Biwa. Because the wind and waves on the lake were fierce, they were forced to spend two or three days in vain on this shore, but finally they boarded the ship. The name of this vessel was the Kagamimaru. Several dozen passengers vied with one another to board the Kagamimaru, and the party of five, as if at last relieved, also became passengers aboard the vessel.

Matsuyohime said, "Oh, that was quite severe snow, was it not? At this rate, it must have piled up considerably on Mount Caucasus."

Tokikō said, "No, there is no need to worry. If a fair-skinned missionary like you appears, the snow itself will be discreet and melt away. Ahahaha, if a fellow like Katsukō were aboard here as well, it would be convenient for our mission; does boarding a ship not bring that to mind, Lady Umegakahime?"

Umegakahime said, "Hohohoho, if we were to sing a song, someone from the Ural faith might be aboard, and another scene might begin."

This lake was far broader than Lake Akashi, and accordingly the waves were high and the voyage required a number of days. To pass the boredom, the passengers formed groups here and there, chattering away and indulging in idle gossip.

Man A said, "Strange things truly happen in this world. On Mount Altai, there is an evil god with four eyes called Hebitsukami who reportedly devours giant serpents and eats humans, while in Kusunogahara a one-eyed monster appears and giant serpents swallow people; it is an exceedingly dangerous world."

Man B said, "Is there anyone who does not know that? Such a widely known story! There is an even more remarkable tale than that. It has surely not reached your slow-to-hear ears yet."

Man A said, "Do not speak nonsense! I have eight ears. Rumors of the world reach these ears first. Moreover, my eyes are sharp, my nose is sharp, and my mouth is sharp; my tongue can judge the quality of sake, my hands and feet are nimble, my arms are strong, and my intimidations are effective; and on top of that, my spirit is unyielding. If you keep mumbling nonsense, I will not stand for it!"

Man B said, "Ahahaha, with that triangular face like a mantis, glaring with your eyes rolling wide, do not talk so big!"

Man A said, "Then what is it? Shall I hear it?"

Man B said, "See there? Listen carefully. Lately, in Kuronogahara, an evil spirit named Kujakuhime has appeared—like a Snow Hag, yet like a translucent, clear gem, an exceedingly beautiful maiden—and it is said that everyone who passes through that place is devoured, one and all."

Kō: "Yeah, is that so? That is news to me. They say that heaven has a mouth and walls have ears, but the house where I live, just like my own body, has lost all the flesh from its walls and is nothing but bones, so even the walls did not let me hear it. Well then, shall I take the trouble to hear that rare story through your wall?"

Chin-kō: "Hey, hey, what are you fellows talking about such an old story at this late hour? There is a far, far newer, singularly rare story. For this Master Chin has sharp ears."

Kō: "Hey, Chin-kō, you line up nothing but preambles; won't you say what comes next?"

Chin-kō: "I will tell you right now, so prick up your ears like a young stag and listen with awe and reverence."

Kō: "Won't you say it quickly?"

Chin-kō: "Do not be so clamorous. If I sell it so cheaply, it has no value. They say a deity has appeared with a name like your wife's backside."

Kō: "What do you mean, like my wife's backside? Did a mortar monster appear?"

Chin-kō: "It is not a mortar; you are quite a blockhead too. They say a deity named something like the great Danjirihime has appeared."

Otsu: "Ahahaha, humph, what are you babbling? Catching mere scraps of hearsay so arrogantly, putting on a knowing face when you know nothing at all—even my backside is appalled."

Chin-kō: "Oh, 'backside' reminded me. It is Ōgetsuhime."

Kō: "And what of that Ōgetsuhime?"

Chin-kō: "Just listen quietly. The word is that she loves delicious food, wants to wear beautiful clothes, builds a clean and magnificent house, employs many men and women, and lives in luxury and splendor."

Kō: "Anyone naturally loves delicious food. Preferring the soft clothes of the silkworm over garments that scratch and tear the skin is the same not only for Ōgetsuhime, but even for the Ōjirihime at my house. Is there anyone in the whole world who dislikes delicious food? Humans find joy in wearing clothes and eating; what are you babbling about as if it were something novel?"

Chin-kō: "Saying obvious, settled things is precisely what is rare. The present world is one where anyone who speaks of incomprehensible, unsettled things is universally praised as clever, a scholar, or smart. It is a world where everyone makes a fool of whoever speaks the true, straightforward truth. This is what is called an upside-down world."

時公『竹野姫と云ふ御方と違ふか』

珍公『アー、その竹野姫だ。その女が雪の降るのに只一人、月は照るとも虧るとも、雪は積むとも解るとも、大直日だとか、大気津姫だとか、見直すとか、斬り直すとか、偉さうに云つて山へ登つた限り、雪に鎖されたのか、大気津姫にしてやられたのか一向その後の消息がわからぬといふことだ。女だてらに豪胆にも、彼んな猛獣や大蛇ばかりの山へ往くから、そんな目に遭うのですな』

§ 4

Tokikō: "Well now."

Umegakahime: "Pardon me, elder sister, might not the story just now be about our middle elder sister? If that is so, what should I do?"

Matsuyohime: "No, do not worry. Wherever one goes, one is accompanied by the gods. Since upon this earth all is the sacred ground protected by Kuniharutachi no Mikoto, Kinkatsukane no Kami, and Susanō no Mikoto, Takenohime, who is surely performing the service of the gods, will never meet with anything untoward."

Umegakahime: "Elder sister, you are right. Being too worried, I inadvertently fell into doubt. Shall we go as soon as possible to this Mount Caucasus or whatever it is called, and give strength to elder sister?"

Matsuyohime: "You still worry. Such groundless anxiety is unnecessary."

Umegakahime: "Ho, ho, ho, ho."

Tokikō: "Pardon me, sisters, somehow it seems to me that this is likely about Takenohime. I, Tokikō, shall run up Mount Caucasus and do a good turn. Just watch me."

Matsuyohime: "What will you do?"

Tokikō: "If that wretch called Ōgetsuhime repents, well and good; if she does not repent, shall I borrow the mighty power of Susanō no Mikoto, slash and slash, cut them all around, and make a blood sacrifice of all her henchmen without exception?"

Umegakahime: "Once again, Toki-sama, do you utter such violent things? Correct your speech."

Tokikō: "I have just changed ships from the Meishimaru to the Kagamimaru."

Umegakahime: "Well, well, that is fine. Calmly compose yourself so as not to do rough deeds, and let us pacify them through the propagation songs of Ananai-kyō."

Tokikō: "Is Ananai-kyō called the external teaching? Among all who hear these songs, there is no one who does not approve; it is indeed a hugely popular teaching that is well received. That is why they must say God appears outwardly. Shall I now run up Mount Caucasus and divide good from evil?"

The voyage of five days and five nights ended safely, and at last the vessel arrived at the western shore. The passengers vied with one another to disembark. Leisurely singing songs, the five once more directed their course toward the northwest and proceeded onward, aiming for Mount Caucasus.
