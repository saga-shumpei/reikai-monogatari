Chapter 19: The Vessel Shiohimaru [486]

第19章　汐干丸〔486〕
§ 1

Matsuyo-hime stood straight and upright and sang:

"Vast is the heart of the Lake of Biwa, deep is the grace of the Lake of Biwa. Upon the waves floats Matsushima, where ancient pines flourish verdantly with divine spirit. All at once blooms Umeshima, while on crowded Takeshima the slender bamboos are tossed in the wind. Though rough gales sweep strongly, and though they bend under deep snow, through a thousand and eight thousand generations the young bamboos know no distress in their bravery.

I am Matsuyo-hime of the Divine Age, admonishing the luxury of Ōgetsu-hime who disturbs this world, scattering the false blooms of her vanity. With our sacred words bestowed by the heavenly deities, swift to blossom is the spirit of Umegaka-hime. To aid Takeno-hime shut inside the rock cavern amid boundless grief, the time to advance has truly arrived! While worldly sorrows and pains abound, how could I dwell alone in comfort, building a palace in the deep mountains where stags call to their mates? Together with Umegaka-hime, Matsuyo-hime will cleanse and dispel the perverse deeds of the ugly crooked spirits who surpass tigers and wolves. Delightful is the spring when blows the long-awaited seasonal wind! Aboard this ship we unexpectedly heard news of Takeno-hime: do not starve, do not perish! Though a woman, Takeno-hime dedicates herself in true sincerity to the divine country. Appearing as a deity of salvation, the three sisters will converse together; though the night be brief, let your enduring rough spirit wait bravely, beloved sister! Our souls of pine and plum that care for you shall reach the boulder of a thousand hands, by the side of our sister inside the cave!"

Having sung, she returned to her seat.

Ushikō lowered his voice: "Hey brothers, did you hear that chanting just now?"

Umakō: "How could you call that chanting? She was just babbling on and on in a sluggish, incomprehensible murmur!"

Shikakō: "Hey, that's not it. That is what people call a poem!"

Ushikō: "Ah, is that so? She was warbling some suspicious poem. That woman might be a beggar poet! What she sang was quite strange."

Umakō: "What are you jabbering about? That is the very person we were talking about! If we join forces and tie her up tight, we've got it made. Didn't she confess to being Matsu and Ume?"

Shikakō: "Still, taking a look at her, she seems quite bold and fearless. She doesn't look like someone two or three of us can handle. Besides, she has that giant fellow attached to her; no matter what ambition you harbor, it will probably come to nothing."

Torakō: "Indeed, indeed, and she is a formidable one! If we fumble clumsily, we'll end up completely humiliated!"

Umakō: "What are you spouting?"

Shikakō: "It truly depends on the situation."

Ushikō: "This is a timely windfall. Let us capture them firmly!"

Umakō: "Stringing our names together, you spout clever jokes!"

Ushikō: "Now that it has come to this, we can't just leave them in the corner of the stable!"

Shikakō: "Indeed, right in the center of the parlor, dwelling in the palace on Mount Caucasus!"

Torakō: "That would be splendid indeed!"

Ushikō: "Stop punning! All you do is ape others—let's drop this chatter."

Umakō: "Because it's absurd. One rarely encounters a windfall or a monster."

Shikakō: "Still, they say many monsters gather on Mount Caucasus. Aren't there plenty of sweet propositions?"

Torakō: "Tigers, lions, wolves, bears, foxes, badgers, dogs, cats, ladles, gourds, sake lees, Caucasus—they say only cunning, selfish rascals gather there! From now on, Tiger will become a bit cunning, feign innocence like a cat, and turn into a tiger-cat—a meow-velous idea, wouldn't you say?"

Tokikō: "Hey, ox, horse, deer, tiger! I've been pretending to sleep like a badger listening to your whispers, and what bold rascals you are! Ushikō's scheme for profit, Umakō's sweet calculations, Shikakō's cunning schemes, Torakō's feigned innocence—even Ōgetsu-hime would be appalled! Come on, say it once more before Toki-sama!"

Yakkō: "Look here, you four beasts, you four-legged creatures! I am Lord Yatsu (Eight)! Do you know that eight is double four? If you keep babbling, I'll tear you into eight pieces!"

Kamokō: "Hey you lot! Why do you care about Matsu or Ume? Leave them alone! I am a missionary of Ananai-kyō. Because I know everything (kamo), I am called Lord Kamo! I have clearly seen through your evil scheme. Well, will you confess everything?"

Ushikō: "Moo, moo! We confess everything!"

Umakō: "Please wait patiently (umaku)!"

Shikakō: "Please don't scold us (shikaranaide) with such a stern face!"

Torakō: "We will tuck our tails before we are captured (tora-erarenai)!"

Kamokō: "Very well. From now on, you had better watch yourselves in everything!"

Tokikō: "Aha-ha-ha-ha!"

時公「おいおい牛、馬、鹿、虎、俺がさっきからタヌキ寝入りをして貴様らの囁きを聞いていれば、ふてぶてしい奴らだ。牛公の儲け話、馬公のうまい算段、鹿公のずるい思惑、虎公の猫かぶり、大気津姫も呆れるわい。さあ、もう一度時さまの前で言ってみろ。」

八公「こら四人の獣、四足、俺は八さまだぞ。知ってるか、四の倍が八だ。ぐずぐずぬかすと八つ裂きだぞ。」

鴨公「やい貴様たち、松がどうだの梅がどうだのと何をかまうのだ。構うてくれるな。此の方は三五教の宣伝使だ。何もかもご承知だから鴨さまと呼ぶのだ。貴様の悪だくみはちゃんと見破っているのだ。どうだ、何もかも白状するか。」

牛公「モー、モー、何もかも白状いたします。」

馬公「うまく待ってください。」

鹿公「しかつめらしい顔をして叱ってくださるな。」

虎公「お前さまたちに捕らえられぬ先に尻尾を巻きます。」

§ 2

Tokikō stood up straight and began to sing a missionary song:

"Loud are the wave-sounds across the Lake of Biwa, sounding the spirit of words here and there. Looking toward spring returning again, there is Matsushima, and Takeshima amid high wind and waves, and Umeshima which looks formidable to behold. How delightful the floating scenery! Briefly passing beyond the offshore waters of the three islands, catching the wind in the full sail, we arrived here at the appointed hour.

Packed tightly in the ship, the passengers foam at the mouth crying of misery: Ushikō with his horns up; Umakō telling petty fibs to cover his rear; Shikakō with his sour grimace entangled in greed and drink, with a heart like autumn leaves where deer bray; and Torakō whose rough courage was taken away. When Yakkō, hearing the tales of Mount Caucasus that would astound Ōgetsu-hime, fired off his petty logic like flatulence, it became an empty exchange without marrow or substance. Kamokō, with his bald head like an octopus blowing ink, began to swagger about.

Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, Toki-sama appears before the crooked gods of Mount Caucasus, losing no time to explain the true path of sacred words and untangle the knotted hearts of those crooked gods blinded by greed. Spurred by sweet talk that even cow dung could rule the world, they carelessly blabbered foolish chatter aboard this ship. While it is easy to scold them, Torakō had no substance to grasp! Correcting sight, hearing, and speech with the Naohi spirit, we advance aboard this vessel.

Not only you four—ox, horse, deer, and tiger—but all people aboard this ship: just as chisels, planes, and saws perform their work, from this moment cut away the crookedness of your hearts, plane off the defilements, and conform to the teachings of the Ananai God! Do not imitate Ōgetsu-hime who lives in pomp and luxury; reform your hearts with obedience and quickly transfer to God's ship—the divine ship that saves this world, the rescue vessel of Menashikatama! Amid a world of turbulent wind and waves, without fear of drowning, Matsuyo-hime drifts upon the surging waves, while Umegaka-hime blossoms all at once with divine teachings. Listen well to the missionary songs of these two noble ladies and reform yourselves!

What serves as our strength not only in this life but in the life to come is God's sacred law. Aboard the companion ship of the law, growing neither old nor dying through countless ages, it rescues and bears people toward the divine realm of the heavenly sun. Transfer without a single day's delay to God's vessel of salvation! Board it, board it, board God's ship! Proclaim anew your sacred words, casting away defilement. God is with you! Ah, how wondrous is God's grace, how precious is God's virtue! Reflecting deeply upon this, manifest the pure color of the heart bestowed by God. Though Mount Caucasus is towering, compared to God's grace it cannot approach even His feet. Though the rough Lake of Biwa is deep, compared to His boundless grace it is beyond comparison. Bow before the teachings of the awe-inspiring God, O all ye people! In this mortal world fraught with perils, it is difficult to pass one's days with ease if separated from God. Praise and exalt the divine virtue; pray before God! Wrapped front and back, left and right in the divine fire and water, the living human body must not part from God nor be abandoned. Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara! May the divine spirit bestow happiness and polish the souls of all mankind! Push open the rock door of the heart, and let the pure sun and moon illuminate brightly! Let the pure sun and moon illuminate brightly!"

Having finished singing, he returned to his seat.

(March 3, Taisho 11 [1922] • Lunar February 5 • Recorded by Hisako Fujitsu)

此処まで来たる時も時　ぎうと詰まつた船の客
うしや苦しと泡を吹く　角の立ちたる牛公や
尻の始末に馬さまが　豆屁の様な法螺を吹き
欲と酒とにからまれて　心は紅葉鹿の鳴く
しかめ面した鹿さまや　荒肝とられた虎さまの
コーカス山の物語　大気津姫が呆れたと
屁を放る様な小理屈を　やつとかました八公の
骨も身もないかけ合ひだ　墨を吹いたる蛸の様な
禿ちやま頭の鴨公が　かもかかもかと威張り出す
朝日は照るとも曇るとも　月は盈つとも虧くるとも
コーカス山の曲神を　この時さまが現はれて
時をうつさず言霊の　誠の道を説き分けて
欲に迷うた曲神の　心のもつれ解いてやる
牛の糞でも天下取る　うまい話にのせられた
船の上にてうつかりと　ほざいた鼻鹿物語
叱り散らすは易けれど　とらまへ処のない虎公
直日に見直し聞直し　宣り直し行く船の上
牛馬鹿虎のみならず　この船中の人々よ
鑿や鉋や鋸の　働らく如く今よりは
心の曲をきり払ひ　垢を削れよ三五の
神の教にまつらうて　栄耀栄華に暮らし居る
大気津姫の真似をすな　従順に心改めて
早く乗り換へ神の船　この世を救ふ神の船
目無堅間の救ひ船　浪風荒き世の中も
溺れる案じあら波の　浪に漂ふ松代姫
神の教の一時に　開く梅ケ香姫の神
此の二方の宣伝歌　確り聞いて改めよ
この世ばかりか先の世の　力となるは神の教
教の友船幾千代も　老いず死らず天津日の
神の御国へ救ひ行く　神の救ひの御船に
一日も早く乗り直せ　乗れよ乗れ乗れ神の船
醜の言霊詔り直せ　神は汝と倶にあり
嗚呼有難き神の恩　嗚呼有難き神の徳
とつくり思案した上で　神に貰うた生粋の
心の色を現はせよ　コーカス山は高く共
神の恵みに比ぶれば　足元さへも寄り付けぬ
琵琶の荒湖深くとも　深き恵みに比ぶれば
たとへにならぬものぞかし　畏き神の御教に
まつろひまつれ諸人よ　禍多き人の世は
神を離れて易々と　くれ行く事は難しい
ほめよたたへよ神の徳　祈れよ祈れ神の前
前や後や右左　神の御水火に包まれて
生きて行くなる人の身は　神に離れな捨てられな
アヽ惟神々々　御霊幸ひましまして
世の諸人の身魂をば　研かせ給へ研きませ
心の岩戸押しあけて　清き月日を照らせかし
清き月日を照らせかし』

と歌ひ終つて元の座に就きけり。

（大正一一・三・三　旧二・五　藤津久子録）
