Chapter 2: The One-Eyed Phantom [469]

第2章　一目お化〔469〕

§ 1

The missionary of the god Azumahiko-no-Kami, accompanied by Tokikō, sang a propagation song as he advanced across the boundless plain of Kusu-no-gahara.

"The missionary of the Ananai teachings, Following the divine words of the Amatsumikami, Subdues by persuasion the many Shikome and Sagume of the eighty Magatsu, And to save the hundred and eighty deities and the myriad people Unto the great way of divine truth, Has crossed oceans and mountains And at last reached the plain of Kusu; Though not the god Kitamitsuhiko-no-Kami, Frightened by the one-eyed evil spirit, With peaceful dreams shattered, Rising and gazing in all four directions, There are cries of tigers and wolves, And the sound of the wind sweeping across the withered moor; At that moment of trembling in the cold, There was the unexpected Toki-san's Jest fitting for the occasion, And by the wind blowing like a great conch, Awakened from sleep yet again, Here the two lay resting,

Becoming close companions In the divine teachings of Ananai, Advancing cheerfully Across the desolate moor is a delight; Whether the morning sun shines or clouds gather, Whether the moon waxes or wanes, Even if the wind, the wolves of the wilderness, The lions, the great serpents, and tens of millions of Evils approach all at once, Why should I fear them? As a missionary of the divine teachings of Shikishima, Blowing them away with the divine water and fire, Scattering them with my kotodama, Crossing the Lake of Seimeishi, And once again facing the wild plains, Walking on foot across moors where tigers lie, Whipping the steed of the heart, Riding the boat of divine salvation Upon the mirror-like Biwa Sea, With a heart as firm as a rock-camphor

Ship relied upon to face Armenia, The capital of evil; Taking as our staff the divine teachings Of Kaminaobi who created this world, Of Ōnaobi with a broad heart, And of the Sunrise God and Konohana Of the Naobi spirit, Izunome, Advancing without hindrance Even to the depths of the road is a delight."

Tokikō said, "Thanks to you, missionary, we have advanced unaware across this wilderness where people seem never to have passed. But since my legs seem quite exhausted, shall we give them a drink of water?"

Azumahiko said, "Come, let us keep going. For with each step we draw closer to Armenia. Even the Sun Deity does not rest for a single minute, so it is a waste to rest."

Tokikō said, "Drawing closer to Armenia with each breath is fine, but recently, in the words of the missionary Ishikoridome, it was said that as we work like this for the world, we are drawing closer to the grave with each breath. Hearing such a thing makes human existence seem so frail, and my legs grow weary so that I have no desire to go on. Long are the months and days, but short is life. Shall we rest for a moment?"

§ 2

Azumahiko said, "Human beings draw closer to the graveyard with each and every breath, and then go to the land beyond the graveyard. Our purpose is to cross beyond the graveyard."

Tokikō said, "Approaching the grave, you say—is this fleeting world transient or grave-bound? From root to leaf it is not going well at all. I feel it is utterly ridiculous."

Azumahiko said, "The purpose of human life lies there. People are reborn and die by turns, growing young again and again, entering and departing the spiritual realm and the physical world, sustaining life that is eternal and boundless. For thousands and tens of thousands of years, one is ageless and deathless. You too were born into this world, and have been swaggering about calling yourself Lord Toki for merely some forty years, but your true self was born tens of thousands of years ago."

Tokikō said, "Wait a moment."

So saying, he sat down heavily upon the grass by the roadside, moistened his eyebrows with spittle, and

Tokikō said, "Hey there, Sir Monster, won't you have a smoke to keep me company? They say that smoking does not pass for monsters and devils, and indeed you have shown your tail."

Azumahiko said, "You have suddenly begun to speak vulgarly again."

Tokikō said, "It is only natural. If I were to speak politely and involve myself with a monster like Akumahiko, there is no telling what kind of ordeal you might subject me to. Just as the sun is beginning to set, you have revealed your true nature."

Azumahiko laughed, "Ahahaha!"

Tokikō,

"You are an incomprehensible fellow,"

tilted his head in doubt.

"While a human being dies once and never dies again, once born, does anyone ever get born a second time? Those who live and die repeatedly are foxes or tanuki. Seven transformations for a fox, eight for a tanuki; let your mask of disguise be stripped away!"

So saying, while poking Azumahiko's buttocks slightly in half-doubt and half-belief,

"Shall I strike him? Yet if he is the real thing, divine punishment will fall upon me, which would be troublesome, and he is a fellow whose true identity is completely beyond comprehension. As dusk falls, in contrast to my morning vigor, I become worn out from walking and my legs turn stiff as poles, so that no matter how enduring Lord Tokikō may be, I come to desire a smoke; yet that he only grows more spirited as night approaches is one strange thing. A monster is indeed a creature that makes merry when night falls."

So saying, while thrusting his iron rod into the ground, he glared at Azumahiko's face to an offensive degree.

"Haha, you had better keep your eyes sharp and examine my face thoroughly. For it would be troublesome if you were to misunderstand me after night falls."

§ 3

Tokikō tilted his head slightly,

"Three parts human and seven parts monster, eh—three parts person and seven parts apparition; when night falls, it must become one part person and nine parts apparition. Spouting nonsense like a magician or an actor about dying and being born, being born and dying—if you do not confess honestly, you will find there are bones in Master Toki's arm. Here, look at this!"

So saying, he stretched out his left arm, rolled up his sleeve to reveal an arm covered with knotted, bulging muscles, and struck his left arm with his right hand to show him.

Azumahiko said, "Ahahaha! Amusing, most amusing! Having been treated to quite the splendid actor in such a lonely place, I have completely forgotten the fatigue of my journey. You might say again that I am a monster frolicking at nightfall, but I truly feel as though I have come back to life, and my vigor is ever more flourishing."

Tokikō said, "Clever though you may seem, you are indeed a Magatsukami after all. You have finally confessed! You must be the ghost of an ogre. Proof that you are dead lies in your blabbing just now that you felt as though you had come back to life! Well, how about that? Since things have come to this and you confessed of your own accord, there is no way of escape for you. Wandering stubbornly into this world... you one-eyed monster!"

Azumahiko laughed, "Ahahahaha!"

Tokikō said, "Thinking of you as a dead soul, somehow things around there feel all eerie, and I am beginning to feel sick."

Azumahiko said, "Lift smoothly the curtain over your eyes, remove the covers from your ears, and listen carefully to what I say; make no mistakes in seeing or hearing. There is such a thing as looking again and listening anew through the Naobi. If you do so, you will come to restate all the vile and violent words you have prated until now entirely into good and beautiful words."

Tokikō said, "Hmph, do not mock me! It is not as if I were a one-eyed monster; I am a two-eyed fellow! As if I would lower a curtain over my eyes or put covers on my ears like a monster! You have only picked up fragments of the missionary songs of the Ananaikyō, so do not make a fool of me by telling me to look again and listen anew!"

At this moment, an indescribable fragrance filled the surroundings, and resonant music came floating from nowhere.
