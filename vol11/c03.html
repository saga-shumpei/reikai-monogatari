Chapter 3: Contemplation of Life and Death [470]

第3章　死生観〔470〕

§ 1

Enveloped by the clear, ringing sounds of music and the sweet fragrance of blossoms, Tokikō suddenly fell into a state of spiritual ecstasy and, looking up toward the heavens, gazed in the direction of the sounds.

Whether of plum, cherry, or peach, the fluttering, lovely petals fell like snow. The fragrance grew ever sweeter, and the music grew ever more serene and clear, on the verge of entering the realm of the divine and penetrating the utmost mystery.

Higashihiko said, "Oh, Toki-sama, the veil over your eyes must have risen, and the lids covering your ears must have come off. Your nose must have been revived as well."

"Ah, my mind has suddenly opened up, as if lotus flowers had burst into full bloom all at once."

"Do you still think me a monster?"

"A monster is still a monster, yet you are a rather good sort of monster, are you not? With this alone, Tokikō cannot understand it at all; please explain the reason why, as you said earlier, I was supposedly alive tens of thousands of years ago."

Higashihiko said, "Listen seriously this time. Human beings are born from the water and fire of God. God lives continuously through all eternity. The divided spirits of that God become humans. And the physical body is the vessel of the human. This physical body is a shrine of God, not the human itself. Humans must use this physical body to carry out their work as children of God. The physical body undergoes vicissitudes and cannot remain in full bloom forever. Yet its true essence dies and is reborn repeatedly; that is, it renews the body and enters it, and when the body becomes old and useless, it sets out anew and comes to dwell in a new body. That a human dies is not something to be grieved over at all. It merely means passing beyond the graveyard to change into another new physical body beyond. Originally, in God there is no distinction between life and death, and humans who have received that divided spirit are the same. Present humans regard dying as something utterly disagreeable, but for the true essence of man, it is nothing of consequence."

Tokikō said, "If that is so, it seems I should be able to understand that I have been alive since tens of thousands of years ago, yet I do not understand it in the least. If it is as you say, it seems I ought to remember as what sort of person I was born in the previous world, where and how I lived, and through what procedure I came to be born. And if it is such a wonderful thing, why is it that, until the very moment of death, one feels that dying is so disagreeable?"

東彦「今度は真面目に聞きなさい。人間というものは、神様の水火から生まれたものだ。神様は万劫末代まで生き通しだ。その神様の分霊が人間となるのだ。そして、肉体は人間の入れ物だ。この肉体は神の宮であって、人間ではないのだ。人間はこの肉体を使って、神の御子たる働きをしなくてはならない。肉体には栄枯盛衰があって、いつまでも花の盛りでいることはできない。しかしその本体は生まれ変わり死に変わり、つまり肉体を新しくして、それに入り、古くなって役に立たなくなれば、また出直して新しい体に宿ってくるのだ。人間が死ぬということは、別に憂うべきことでも何でもない。ただ墓場を越えて、もう一つ向こうの新しい肉体へ入れ替わるということだ。元来神には生死の区別がない、その分霊を受けた人間もまた同様である。死ぬということを、今の人間は非常に嫌なことのように思うが、人間の本体としては何でもないことだ」

時公「そうであれば、私が何万年前から生きていたということが、自分にわかりそうなものなのにちっともわかりません。あなたのおっしゃる通りなら、前の世には何という者に生まれ、どこでどうしていて、どういう手続きで生まれてきたかということを覚えていそうなものです。そしてそんな結構なことであるなら、なぜ臨終の際まで、死ぬということが嫌なような気がするのでしょうか」

Azumahiko: "That is where Kami is so gracious. If you were to remember that you did such things in the previous world, or that there were such splendid things in the spiritual realm, you would feel, 'Ah, rather than staying in such a world of bitter strife, I want to return quickly to the original spiritual realm; it would be better to die,' and you would be unable to fulfill your duty in human life. That all humans have come to this world endowed with physical bodies is for the purpose of fulfilling a certain mission from Kami. The reason why one feels reluctance to die is that, in short, so that one may remain in this world even one day longer and serve Kami's purpose even once more, the spirit that fears death has been bestowed upon us. To tell the truth, there is no telling how much more enjoyable and interesting the spiritual realm is than the physical realm; eventually, a thousand years from now, you and I will enter the spiritual realm, and we will laugh together, saying, 'Oh, Lord Azumahiko,' 'Oh, is that Tokigimi?,' 'How have you been?,' 'When did you die?,' and 'Was it so? Indeed, indeed, it seems as though I died only recently.'"

Tokigimi: "Ah, is that so? In that case, rather than living a long life and committing sins like me, it would be happier instead to die quickly before committing sins."

Azumahiko: "There, because people come to feel that way, we cannot tell them about the spiritual realm. There is no place so splendid as this world. Hoping to live even one day longer, fulfilling one's duty as one born a human during that time, doing even one good deed, and serving Kami, until the angels come to welcome one, saying, 'This is enough now, return to the spiritual realm,' one cannot arbitrarily leave this world. No matter how much one may wish to die of one's own accord, one cannot die unless Kami permits it."

Tokigimi: "Let me ask one thing: when I was a child, I did not know east from west. If we possess the divine souls of Kami that have lived unceasingly from ancient times, one would think that from childhood we would have understood everything a little better, yet it seems to me that we were taught little by little and gradually gained wisdom. Why on earth is this so?"

Azumahiko: "Because a child's physical body is frail, a soul only of a degree corresponding to it resides in it. It is only after becoming a full-fledged body that the entirety of the person's soul transfers into the physical body and operates. Until then, one is reborn little by little."

Tokikō: "Then, does the true form of a human remain in the womb for ten months, and after that, not fully reincarnate until another twenty years have passed?"

Azumahiko: "Well, it is something like that. However, no matter how splendid the spiritual world is said to be, if one dies without fulfilling life's mission, speaking evil words and doing nothing but evil, one can never return to that original splendid place. Indeed, one will receive the infinite torments of the Root-Land and the Bottom-Land. Therefore, during this life, one must do at least one good deed."

Tokikō: "I understand quite well. If you teach me all at once, I will forget, so please give it to me little by little again."

Azumahiko: "Come, let us go; a journey by night is rather interesting."

Tokikō: "Eh, walking in the wilderness all day long and walking even into the night—is that not pushing oneself a bit too hard? Even the Sun lowers a black curtain and rests at night; that is too much."

Azumahiko: "A night journey means sleeping. Come, to repose means to rest, ahahaha. Tonight again, we shall sleep on a bedding of reeds with an elbow for a pillow, beneath a blanket of clouds. To thank for the grace of the dew of God's blessing, let us chant the divine words, sing a propagation song, and go to sleep."

Tokikō: "I do not know the new propagation songs at all. Will anything do?"

Azumahiko: "First, I shall chant a propagation song, so you shall sing trusting to your own kotodama."

Saying this, Azumahiko stood up immediately,

Heaven and earth are eternally The continuous living of Yin and Yang; Born from God's water and fire, Man is the child of God, the shrine of God. Living and dying are the same thing; If one likens this to something, The world of God is one's homeland, The house where beloved parents dwell. Human life, born into this world, Is a grass pillow on a bed of dew, Just as a traveler setting out on a journey Traces his way through fields of grass; Tracing on and on until twilight, Seeking a house somewhere, The time he lodges there Is none other than the time he leaves this world. Departing from the one-night lodging, When he sets out on a journey once again, That is the time he is born again as a human And performs the work of God.

Born, one works for a day; Dying, one rests again for a night. Dying is not the end Of the human world; it is the time When the living spirit lays down its burden and rests, The time it plays before God, The time the flower of prosperity blooms, The time when joy is fulfilled. Once again, at the command of God, Departing from the lodging of the divine world, One makes the journey of human life once more. A journey is a sorrowful thing, a painful thing; Yet even amidst the pain, there is one thing: The flower of limitless joy Leading to the capital blooms and gives off fragrance. The human person, being the child of God, Is born, dies, and is born again; Dies, is born, and is born again; Dies, is born, and is born again, Endlessly, no matter how far.

Tokikō: "What do you mean, fool? I have chanted it over, I have chanted it over!"

Higashihiko: "To say 'chant it over,' what a shrewd man you are."

The night deepened profoundly. Amidst the violent autumn tempest, the two put on their woven hats and went to sleep comfortably.
