Chapter 20: The Cavern of Fiends [487]

第20章　醜の窟〔487〕
§ 1

Umegaka-hime arose and sang:

"Looking across the mountains and plains in all directions, draped in a mantle of snow, it is a silver realm pure to behold. Concealing the world's crookedness, rubbish, and filth, the modern world glitters only on its surface, reaching a dead end everywhere! Only the waves of the sea are green. The missionaries who illuminate the teachings of the Great Deity Kamu-izanagi who appeared at Aokigahara, and of Konohana-hime, travel aboard this vessel with many companions. The words whispered from mouth to mouth, fanned by the wind, cast a cloud over their hearts like dark night. Yet by the command of the Rising Sun Deity who illuminates the darkest night, how did Takeno-hime—who faced the snow-clad Mount Caucasus where raging evil deities and Ōgetsu-hime abide—come to be captured inside a rock cavern? Ah, receiving the mighty light of God upon ourselves, can we leave our elder sister, clouded with sorrow within the pitch-black cave, without rescuing her?

What is the mountain gale of Mount Caucasus to us? Our party advances, carving out the path of God! The time has arrived, the hour is now! Pushing open the heavenly rock door, we shall win over by word the hundreds of evil spirits assembled on Mount Caucasus! The Kamunaohi of the broad-hearted Heavenly Deity who brings peace through speech, the dew of grace in Ōnaohi, will straighten the crooked spirits, enabling the missionaries to see, hear, and speak anew with righteousness. Revering the marvelous deeds of God in the ever-transforming spiritual realm, five companions cross the pure Lake of Biwa. Takeno-hime, drawing strength from God, praise Him with divine prayers and wait a little longer! Though the starlight is momentarily hidden, once the clouds withdraw, the flowery sky appears. Though the moon waxes and wanes, the three sisters must never be broken! Just as the peach fruit manifested as Ōkamuzumi and won renown in the Netherworld battle, we shall once more serve the divine work of opening the rock door before the Sun Goddess Amaterasu! Until then, protecting our pure lives as we slip through the brutal blades of tigers, wolves, lions, and bears, O Golden Deity, Susanoo-no-mikoto, Kunihurutachi-no-ōkami, and all the myriad deities protecting Ananai-kyō, guard well the destiny of Pine, Bamboo, and Plum!

Exacting blood from the people below, squeezing out their marrow, Ōgetsu-hime alone exhausts every luxury; around Princess Ural who manifests her spirit coil eight-forked serpents, foul foxes, and eighty crooked spirits. Now is the appointed hour to subdue them all beneath God's breath! Ah, Toki-sama, Yatsu-sama, Ushikō, Umakō, Shikakō, Torakō, Kamokō! Advance bravely! Blow away in a single breath the foul winds sweeping Mount Caucasus, and cleanse the sacred land! For God and country, unite your strength and give your all; cast aside chisels and planes, tread only the path of God, and receive the divine favor of Kannagara! Advance, advance, onward advance!"

Having sung, she concluded her song.

Tokikō: "Yatsu-sama, Kamo-sama, what do you think? The four beasts—ox, horse, deer, and tiger—who were clamoring so noisily, seem to have surrendered before Toki-sama's missionary song! This too is the power of time (Toki). You always mocked me, saying I was 'Toki-sama' because I went off the rails from time to time, but look: I am Toki-sama who achieves noble deeds from time to time!"

Yakkō: "What are you boasting about so pompously? Whether time or noble, isn't it the same thing? Look at the miserable sight you made at Kusunogahara when Umegaka-hime teased you, and when you met that one-eyed priest! Even if you blow your trumpet thinking I'm ignorant, Lord Yatsu has investigated everything with his clairvoyance. When people speak of Lord Eight-Ears, they mean me! Any scoundrel tucks his tail before this Lord Yatsu!"

Tokikō: "You may be 'Yatsu', but you're a stubborn guy (yatsu), an evil guy, a lying guy, and a troublesome guy!"

Yakkō: "Hey Toki-sama, that's only four guys, not eight!"

Tokikō: "Four guys plus the four legs of your soul makes eight! What a blockhead you are!"

Kamokō: "Aha-ha-ha! That feels refreshing! Because this fellow makes a fuss over everything, the villagers were utterly disgusted with him and treated him as a nuisance—he really is a terrible fellow!"

Ushikō: "Hey Yatsu-sama, you're being squeezed tight like a cow!"

Umakō: "You fool, serve you right!"

Shikakō: "He keeps getting scolded (shikarare-tōshi)!"

Torakō: "He keeps having his legs pulled (torarete-bakari)!"

Tokikō: "It's all pleasant fun for the moment."

Amid such conversation, the vessel touched the shore. The passengers disembarked and set out along the snowy paths in their respective directions. Joining the five members of Matsuyo-hime's party with the ox, horse, deer, and tiger made nine companions. Singing missionary songs, they aimed for Mount Caucasus, advancing along the valley following travelers' footprints.

Heavy snow blanketed the mountains, and from valleys near and far came the frequent cracking sounds of trees snapping under the weight of snow.

Kamokō: "Well, dusk is approaching. The snow makes everything appear bright, so before we realize it, night falls. If there is a hunter's shack around here, let's take a rest, and if possible, spend the night."

Yakkō: "Indeed. I have been looking for an inn myself. If we walk another league into the interior, there are hundreds of splendid houses, so let us push on."

Tokikō: "Wait, wait! Once we reach that point, we are inside enemy territory. Let us spend the night before reaching there, rest our weary bodies, and restore our energy for tomorrow."

Ushikō: "I travel this area frequently and know the mountain well. If we descend slightly to the right in this valley, there is a rock cavern. Shall we spend the night there?"

Tokikō: "What do you say, Matsuyo-hime?"

Matsuyo-hime: "Yes, that sounds fine. Shall we lodge in a rock cavern tonight for the first time in a while?"

Deciding by consensus, they followed Ushikō's guidance toward a small valley. Just as Ushikō had said, there was a spacious rock cavern where twenty or thirty people could sleep comfortably. There the party laid down straw raincoats, ate the dried figs they had brought, and settled in for the night.

§ 2

Umegaka-hime: "Ah, what a convenient cavern this is! Seeing this cave reminds me of my elder sister. Would the cave where she is confined look something like this?"

Ushikō: "Heaven forbid! It's nothing so fine as this! In the deep mountains there are seven caves known as the Seven Holes. The interior is nothing like this flat room. I peeked inside once: the cave is pitch-black and bottomless, said to connect all the way down to the Dragon Palace! If you were thrown in there, you couldn't even sit down comfortably. Both sides are sheer rock walls; you have to plant your feet on jagged edges and spread your legs across the narrow chasm. If you take a brief nod of sleep, you plunge into the bottomless abyss!"

Tokikō: "Are there seven such holes?"

Ushikō: "Indeed. Not long ago, a fierce fellow named Odoyamazumi came along declaring he would make Ōgetsu-hime surrender. When a vast crowd attacked him, he hid inside that hole. The crowd rushed up, slammed the stone lid shut with a bang, and locked it from outside! It has been about a hundred days since, with no word at all. Rumor says he probably fell to the bottom and died. A while later, a tall, slender missionary named Takeno-hime came singing her songs. When night fell and she had nowhere to stay, she slipped into the hole of her own accord! There are fools of all kinds, aren't there?"

Umakō: "Hey, don't speak so foully! Her sisters are right here!"

Ushikō: "Ah, that's true! That missionary lady Takeno-hime seemed troubled by the falling snow and quietly entered the hole. Then the evil gods under Lady Ōgetsu-hime lay in wait, saying 'Now she is in!' and slammed the door shut from the outside. Lady Takeno-hime cried out in a shrill voice from inside. From outside, the evil gods said, 'Now your fate is sealed! Either fall into the bottomless pit and perish, or starve to death—take your pick! Now our worries are gone and we can rest easy!'"

Umakō: "Hey, it's fine to be polite, but you're being excessively polite! You can speak politely of Lady Takeno-hime, but make proper distinctions for the evil gods!"

Ushikō: "If I were so flexible, would I be doing rough carpentry or acting as an overseer for the Ural sect?"

Tokikō: "Ushi-san, you are a remarkably candid fellow!"

Ushikō: "In this world, they say roll with the long, obey the strong, sip the sweet juice and spit out the bitter; it's a human virtue to follow the times!"

Tokikō: "This is the first I've heard of it, but you just said you were an overseer for the Ural sect, didn't you?"

Ushikō: "No, no, that's not it! It was just a slip of the tongue—I take it back!"

Tokikō: "No, that can hardly be so."

Ushikō: "Quite so, quite so—it can hardly be so!"

Matsuyo-hime: "Everyone, shall we sleep now? Let us first recite divine words, and all together chant a missionary song."

Tokikō: "That would be good. However, I have an idea today: let us draw lots, and whoever draws the first lot shall lead the chanting."

Matsuyo-hime: "As you wish, Toki-sama..."

Tokikō: "Come, come, we shall draw lots! The ladies are exempt; the seven men will draw. The one who pulls the longest straw will lead!" Tearing off strands of straw from a raincoat, he made long and short pieces and thrust them before the six men: "Come, draw, draw!" The six competed to draw.

Tokikō: "Aha! Ushikō drew the longest one! Well Ushikō, you lead the missionary song. You've heard it taught aboard the ship and along the way, so surely you can recite it!"

Ushikō: "Yes, indeed, I certainly can! I have ears of the underworld that never forget once heard, so I remember everything! Well then, gentlemen, today I am the master of ceremonies; follow along after me!" With that, Ushikō began to sing:

"God appears out in the open, setting apart tables and rice bowls! In this world, delicious is warmed sake, a delightful divine liquor! Truly, in all things human, sake and women are the very best! Drink and revel, for an inch ahead is darkness; and after the darkness, the moon will rise!"

All: "Aha-ha-ha-ha!"

Kamokō: "Hey Ushikō! You truly belong to the Ural sect! Spouting nonsense about darkness ahead—correct your words! What do you mean by setting apart tables and bowls? You speak nothing but tall tales!"

Ushikō: "Naturally! I am a retainer of Ōgetsu-hime, so there is nothing besides eating, drinking, and dressing! Besides, it comes out of a mouth that eats and drinks, so it is only natural to speak of food and drink! Now then, Kamo fellow, if you have another word to say, let's hear it!"

Tokikō: "Good grief, what a hopeless fellow! Punning in a place like this won't do. As the organizer, I shall lead the missionary song, so you follow along!" He began to sing the missionary song, and all followed after him. At that moment, the footsteps of hundreds of people could be heard outside the cavern. Peeking through a crack in the rock door, Ushikō blurted out:

"Aha! They have arrived, they have arrived! With this many allies, no matter how strong Tokikō may be, we are safe!"

Tokikō: "This is trouble!" And he struck Ushikō with a body blow. The ox collapsed on the spot with a groan. Outside, the footsteps gradually receded into the distance.

(March 3, Taisho 11 [1922] • Lunar February 5 • Recorded by Kyūtarō Iwata)
