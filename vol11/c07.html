Chapter 7: The Dewdrop Inn [474]

第7章　露の宿〔474〕

§ 1

The missionary deities Azumahikonokami and Takahikonokami, together with the missionary messengers Umegakahime, Tsuki, Yuki, and Hana, a party of six people in all, parted the withered fields of Aratamahara, singing missionary hymns from their respective lips, and advanced pointing in the direction of Lake Akashi.

The sun gradually sank in the west, and the sound of a bell announcing dusk faintly echoed.

Azumahiko said, "Ah, after a long time I have heard the sound of a bell. It appears that human habitation is already close at hand. However, as the day has also gradually begun to darken, shall we, fortunately, spend the night in the shade of the forest visible over there?"

All together said, "Let us do so."

So saying, setting the forest as their goal, they hastened their tired legs and advanced. It was a dense forest shade, where thousand-year-old aged trees were moss-grown to the tips of their branches and dark even in daytime.

Azumahiko said, "Ah, inside this natural house where neither moonlight nor starlight can be seen, let us rest leisurely tonight after a long time."

While saying this, he recited the divine words as usual, and began to sing the missionary hymn.

"God appears outwardly And separates good and evil; Kaminahobi who created this world, Ōnahobi broad of heart. Should the crooked deeds of the Hiko of Ural Be left uncorrected? Should they be left without being made to speak anew? Having finished singing thus with one voice, laying out their straw raincoats, the two male and four female missionaries easily fell asleep.

Four or five men who had appeared from the shade of the forest approached stealthily before the missionaries and, observing their breathing in sleep,

First Man: "Hey, every single one of them is utterly exhausted and collapsed. How about it, while they are like this, why do we not quietly put a rope around their necks, drag them off, and take them to the chieftain?"

Second Man: "Wait, wait! If, in our haste, it turns out they are missionaries of the Ural religion, we will catch an enormous scolding. Rather than that, shall we calm down and investigate first before taking action?"

First Man: "Even so, I heard a missionary song just a moment ago, and its melody seemed to be that of the Sango religion. Did they not say something about the moon shining or becoming clouded?"

Third Man: "That is only natural! Look at tonight's moon. Is it not shining and clouding over? Even without being missionaries of the Sango religion, when we look at the moon, we too know enough to say whether it is shining or clouding over. If you assume they are missionaries of the Sango religion over such a thing and bungle it, it will be disastrous."

Fourth Man: "Well, there is no need to make such a fuss. The other party has six people. We are five; even if we grapple with them one-on-one, one more still remains. Just think carefully. There is the saying that the few are no match for the many."

First Man: "What nonsense are you spouting? We are five rough men, while the other side has two men and four women. Even I alone, as far as the women go..."

Fourth Man: "Your strength is only that much. However, if you think they are women like your Ofuku at home, you will miss the mark entirely. Without the strength to seize a man or two and cast them aside, how could they travel across the realm? Even if we were to grapple one-on-one, the other side will have one person with free hands. If that one remains and thumps the heads of us while we are grappling, that will truly be like being bitten by a dog. That is precisely why it is said: the outnumbered are no match for the many, and the few cannot oppose the multitude."

First Man: "Ah, the many, is it?"

Fourth Man: "Do not make light of it. If we dither about and they wake up, it will be trouble. While we can, let Mokubē, Hachikō, Yoshikō, and Genkō, and every fellow in the village whose legs and hips can stand, all gather together, surround them from a distance, and force them into utter submission."

と歌いながら、悠々としてこの場を立ち去ったのであった。
