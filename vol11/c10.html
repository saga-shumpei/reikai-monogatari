Chapter 10: 立聞 [10]

立聞〔10〕

§ 1

Escorted by Tokikō, Umegakahime boldly advanced across the Suzanoshironogahara plain where cold winds raged. A group of four or five men, led by Katsukō, also followed along behind. Spotting a boar hut by the wayside, the entire group came to take a rest there.

Katsukō said, "Without any trace of sake, it has somehow grown chilly. As far as the eye can see, there is not a single green thing; heaven and earth are a pure white world, as though spread thick with cotton. Looking at it this way, the only black things between heaven and earth are merely the faces of Hachikō and Kamokō."

Hachikō said, "That is because your face is white."

Katsukō said, "Naturally. I am still a novice as a missionary of the Sango Teachings, so it is only natural that I am white."

Kamokō said, "Feigning innocence all of a sudden without knowing even half of what an ignorant person knows! Even if a brazen white egret imitates a peacock, it cannot suddenly produce a jewel."

Katsukō said, "Oh, that peacock reminds me: if we go a little further ahead, there is a place called Kuronogahara. Now it is all Shironogahara covered in snow, but in that place lives an enormous monster called Kujakuhime, and rumor has it that whoever passes through there is sucked in completely. Whether they belong to the Ural teachings or the Sango teachings, once Kujakuhime glares at them for even a moment, they are all deceived, and not a single soul has ever returned."

Tokikō said, "Oh, is that true? Since she is called Kujakuhime, she must be quite a beautiful woman. What on earth does she eat?"

Katsukō said, "That goes without saying. She eats humans. Even a dark-skinned human like me is eaten if caught by Kujakuhime, so they say."

Tokikō said, "That is amusing; to have a beautiful face and yet devour humans—she may well be a demon maiden. It is just like the verse: 'With such a voice, do you eat lizards, O cuckoo?' When the world becomes so turbulent like this, nine-tailed golden foxes and Yamata-no-Orochi serpents run rampant here and there, and such demons appear everywhere. A fellow like me, with a frightening face that looks as if it could devour a person, has no real evil within. Rather, there are far more people with beautiful faces who commit murder or deceive others. In short, devils put on the mask of goodness to throw the world into disorder."

Katsukō said, "Lord Toki, shall we throw caution to the wind and investigate the true form of Kujakuhime? There is no telling what has disguised itself."

Tokikō said, "Since you are a man like the persimmon called 'monkey-uneaten' beside a privy, there is hardly any worry that even Kujakuhime would eat you, for you would taste foul. A persimmon called 'monkey-uneaten' is astringent, filthy, small, and completely inedible."

§ 2

Katsukō: "Hey, hey, Toki, correct your words."

Tokikō: "Shall I go to Lake Biwa again and board a pretty boat anew? A man of flavor like me is somewhat hazardous. Say, Lady Umegakahime, when I told you to eat Takahiko at Kusunogahara, you said, 'No, no, Toki is more manly, richly dark in complexion, fleshy and plump, and looks sweet, so please let me eat Toki,' and such; I truly want Umegaka to eat me."

Katsukō: "Do not play the fool in such a place. If you play the fool too much, look, that teacher of a serpent will come sauntering along again."

Tokikō: "I do not care whether it be Orochihime or Meishimaru or anything else; I am a shrewd elder brother who thinks slowly and deliberately. I am somewhat different from a dullard like you."

Katsukō: "What do you mean by dullard? Correct your words. If you curse a person, there are two holes."

Tokikō: "Could I bear having holes, whether two or one? Ah, what a thankful missionary of the Anaikyō!"

All: "Ahahaha!"

Umegakahime: "You are all lighthearted people. Walking together with you is just like a journey in Heaven."

Kamokō: "That must be so. For a shrewd, moderate man who reaches every itchy spot like me has joined in, being a single dot of black amidst the snow along the way."

Tokikō: "Ahaha, you make me laugh. Knowing that Lady Umegaka is present as the solitary red dot amidst ten thousand green thickets, this fellow has smoothed out the wrinkles on his face and is making merry."

Katsukō: "Shall we put an end to such chitchat and get going? From now on, we are to behold the face of Kujakuhime, who is many times more beautiful than Lady Umegaka. If we chatter away with such silly talk, the sluggish one will come along again. Even in this world there is said to be the change of limits and renewal; so let us cease this endless talk and at last carry out the renewal and reconstruction on shank's mare. Come, advance, advance. Everywhere it is heavy snow and an impasse; it is not only my place that is jammed and dull. When the year-end comes, those who cram into a trifling house like mine are bill collectors alone. Come, come, double time, double time. Since I have nothing to pay even when the year-end arrives, shall I brush away the snow as we go?"

Katsukō led the way and cleared the path. Treading firmly on the snowy road, tumbling and falling, Umegakahime's party drew near before the hideout of Kujakuhime.

Tokikō: "Hey, Katsukō, come on, come on, from here on is the battlefield. You shall take the vanguard and be eaten first of all by Princess Kujaku."

Katsukō: "Well, I am like the uneatable monkey-stool beside a privy; even if someone like me went, Princess Kujaku would turn me away. It would be like stamping a mark shaped like a drum. Kamokō: "Let us have Katsu-san, who is a match for a thousand, go. It is no use worrying ourselves sick over someone else's election."

While chattering nonsense thus, they arrived right in front of Princess Kujaku's mansion. Princess Umegaka restrained everyone with hand gestures, pressed her ear against the door of the gate, listened to a faint voice leaking from inside, and tilted her head suspiciously.

Katsukō: "Excuse me, Lady Preacher, what are you pondering? Does it sound as though someone's bones are being gnawed?"

Princess Umegaka: "Well, I cannot quite comprehend it; in a voice that seems familiar, she appears to be singing a propaganda song of Uralism."

Katsukō: "Well, that is strange. As expected, she must be a magatsukami under Uralhiko's command; let Katsusama have a listen. Lady Umegaka, please step aside for a moment."

So saying, he pressed his ear to a knothole in the gate, and:

Katsukō: "Ah, I heard it, I heard it! Hey, Hachikō, Kamokō, she is saying an astounding thing! What an extraordinary thing a magatsukami is! They know everything and anything thoroughly!"

Kamokō: "What is she saying? Let us hear a little."

Katsukō: "How can I let you hear it or not? That Princess Kujaku has her pointed beak set, spreads her wings wide, and is prating: 'Ka-ka-Ka-Mo-mo-mo, Kamoko Kamoko, no, no, Hachikō or Kamo? Past two in the afternoon, my stomach is empty. Shall I eat Kamo and Hachi together? Heh-heh-heh-heh-heh!' The moment the gate creaks open right now, it will be the end of you two: the extinction of suffering brings bliss, instant attainment of Bodhi—a truly pitiful state of affairs!"

Kamo and Hachi: "An-an-an-an, on-on-on-on."

Well, well, one must simply wait for the season. A peacock princess for a crow's bride! Ahahaha!"

Tokikō: "Now then, let me listen for a moment."

Tokikō: "Well, this is splendid! Like a bush warbler, elegant and seemingly cool—it is not disheveled hair, but an unspeakable scene inside the gate that cannot easily be unraveled. Indeed, it is only natural that anyone passing in front of this gate should be unconsciously intoxicated and drawn toward it."
