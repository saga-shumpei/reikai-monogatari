Chapter 5: The Grand Wrapping Cloth [472]

第5章　大風呂敷〔472〕

§ 1

Azumahiko, rising and parting the skies of dawn; Takahiko, parting the blue clouds, shining purely in midair, propagators of the will of Kami, with Umegaka in the spring of her sweet sixteen, Kanahiko of Kanatani Village, and Tokikō, who guards the iron gate, singing the propagation song with clear voices, singing as they march forward. The wind blowing over Arayagahara, is it the Eight-forked Serpent, or crooked spirits, or the breath exhaled by ugly hags and spy-women? Without even taking a breath, briskly and with fierce vigor they march onward. Ah, delightful, delightful! Though serpents, spy-women, and ugly deities should come by tens of thousands, against the divine power of our kotodama bestowed by the ancestral Kami who created heaven and earth,

There shall be none that can rival it. The enemy is not a thousand leagues away; that which lurks unawares within one's own heart is our true foe. Having blown away, purified, and cleansed the demons and crooked gods of the heart, upon our bodies, as children of Kami, the light of the sun and moon shines everywhere, and the jewel is like a clear mirror. Surpassing even the mirror is the Yamato spirit; drawing and holding the precious sword, we chop into pieces the wicked serpent that obstructs our way, and in the brief span of raising our shout of victory, we soon arrive at the plain of Kusu, which has now become the plain of Aratama.

Takahiko: "Now, everyone, let us catch our breath here for a moment and finally begin our preparations for battle. First, let us ask Tokikō, the peerless hero of mighty strength, to take the vanguard. Umegakahime and I shall form the main force, while Azumahiko and Kanahiko shall form the left and right wings, advancing toward the enemy's headquarters in a triangular formation."

Tokikō: "Excuse me, if the strongest man goes first and, by some chance, is bitten in one gulp by that serpent, it will all be over. There will be no reserve left behind. In all warfare, the rear reserve is the most crucial part."

Takahiko: "Though he talks tough, he is a weak man. What a boaster, quite skilled at empty bravado. Well, at any rate, shall we offer up the divine words, then take our time to determine our battle plan, arrange our ranks, and advance in grand style with banners flying and drums beating toward the enemy's stronghold?"

Tokikō: "Wait, everyone, please hold on a moment. I forgot an important matter that I must report to my master. Oh, Kanahiko-sama, please return home immediately."

Kanahiko: "What is it?"

Tokikō: "Never mind what it is; if only you return, you will understand."

Kanahiko: "Listen here, even if you say I will understand, how could I turn my horse around for no good reason at such a critical moment? Speak clearly!"

Tokikō: "Well, to tell the truth, it is hard to speak of, but around midnight on the second day after you departed the mansion in a party of three with Azumahiko, the propagator, the wind was roaring, the rain was pouring down, and the raindrops were falling with steady drops; it was an exceedingly lonely, pitch-black night, as if monsters might appear. At that time, without saying 'Oh, how smelly!' or anything at all, the mistress went to the privy. But just as the rain-soaked, cold branches of a weeping willow, protruding from beside the privy, brushed roughly against her face along with the wind, the mistress, whether it should have happened or not, only cried 'Ah!' and was scared out of her wits, falling flat on the spot. Thinking it a grave emergency, even the gatekeeper Tokikō, with his chest pounding like a tree frog, approached to look, and what did he see? Though I called and shouted, there was no response whatsoever, like a stone thrown into water, leaving her in a pitiful state, with her hands and feet turned cold. 'Ah, what should I do? Mistress, please speak once more! While the master has gone out infatuated with the propagators, for you to meet such a fate during his absence—how can this Tokikō ever face the master again?' I cried and was lost in tears."

§ 2

Kanahiko: "Ah, has Kanahime died?"

Tokikō: "Yes, she has completely breathed her last, and says not a single word. Now that it has come to this, if only we search for the master's whereabouts and hold a splendid funeral for her, at least, at least, at least..."

Kanahiko: "What, speak clearly! Has the mistress really died?"

Tokikō: "Yes, she passed away. Thereupon the mistress said, 'Hey, Tokikō, this is no time to dilly-dally. Go quickly and call the master back.' So this Tokikō said, 'Mistress, understood,' and so tucking up my skirts in a seven-to-three ratio, crossing my sash in a cross, tying my headband crisply behind, and carrying a large-bladed spear, I cried: 'Hear ye, hear ye, all of you! Those far away, hear the rumor; those nearby, come close and see with your own eyes! Be not surprised to hear my name, renowned in Kanatani Village as a hero! Those who know, know; those who know not, know not at all! A real man among women, Lord Tokikō is none other than myself!'"

Kanahiko: "Hey, hey, Tokikō! What are you saying? Speak seriously!"

Tokikō: "Yes, I was so overjoyed that my head turned a little. The mistress once stopped breathing and became completely cold. While we were in an uproar, in utter turmoil over what to do, what a blessing it is to have children! As soon as that gentle Lady Kiyohime sang the missionary songs of Ananaikyō and played what they call the divine koto, the mistress immediately bounced back like a koto string and revived; in exceptionally good spirits, she made a special request: 'Hey, Tokikō, call the master back as soon as possible. It is helpless with only women; human life is uncertain for both the old and young. Should such a thing happen again, it cannot be undone. Pursue him as swiftly as possible, relate the whole matter in fine detail, explain the reason clearly to the two missionaries, and come back.' I could not refuse, and came chasing after you at top speed. If by some chance the master were swallowed by the giant serpent of Kusunogahara, how could this Tokikō ever offer an excuse to the mistress?"

Kanahiko: "We have made our resolve for the sake of the gods, and are to pacify the demons of Armenia by word for the sake of the world; therefore, until that is accomplished, we cannot concern ourselves with small household matters. Moreover, I am accompanying the precious missionaries of Ananaikyō. If you are afraid, you may return, but even if I were swallowed by a giant serpent, I will never, never return until the business of the divine world is finished, so convey that message to the mistress."

§ 3

Tokikō said, "Oh, Master, you do speak on and on. It is not really any big deal, so why not defeat the great serpent before having us return?"

Umegakahime said, "Ho-ho-ho-ho-ho, once again, once again, Toki's grand tall talk is amusing."

Tokikō said, "Ah, that is it! I shall wrap that great serpent all around in this giant wrapping cloth, hook it casually onto the end of a stick, carry it back to Kanatani Village, and scare them saying, 'Come now, this is Toki's souvenir; gather around, everyone, whether you fling it or strike it, boil it and eat it, it is no big deal [a great serpent]!' I wonder if I should frighten them like that."

Kuruhiko said, "Stop the jokes already; this is a critical moment of one warrior against a thousand."

Tokikō said, "What talk of a thousand warriors or searching! We shall attempt an assault in a single breath. Now, Azumahiko, Azumahiko, Lady Ume, I shall serve as the vanguard. One warrior against a thousand—I will do no clumsy thing like losing the labor of a thousand cubits for lack of a single basketful of earth. To freely subdue and pacify them with the propagation songs of the thousands of songs of the Anaizekyō is within our expectations; it is easier than worrying about it. Come, let us go."

The entire company,

saying, "How amusing,"

rose from their seats, laughing uncontrollably at Tokikō's disjointed, impromptu propagation songs, and advanced deeper and deeper into the recesses of Aratamahara.
