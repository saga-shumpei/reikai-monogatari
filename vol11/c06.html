Chapter 6: The City of Wonders [473]

第6章　奇の都〔473〕

§ 1

The party pushed through the grass across the winter plain of Arenogahara, which was deep enough to conceal their bodies, advancing toward what appeared to be the lair of a giant serpent. Here stood an exceedingly large rectangular rock, protruding high and overtopping the plumes of the miscanthus.

Kanahiko said, "Beneath that rock visible over yonder, there is an exceedingly large cave, where water like bloody grease drifts in abundance, and it is said to lead all the way to the Dragon Palace. A thick creature always thrusts its head out of that hole and swallows the lions, tigers, wolves, human beings, and others that pass through this wilderness. Because of this giant serpent, there is no one to till this fine plain, leaving the grass to grow as it will. If we were to subdue this serpent through speech, open up the grassy fields, sow the seeds of the five grains, and build a capital, who knows how greatly the people of the world would rejoice."

Azumahiko said, "That is fascinating. Come, let us at last sing the propagation song here."

With that, everyone stepped up close to the rock, chanted the divine words, and began to sing the propagation song in unison. As the propagation song ended, the crag, roughly one hundred ken from north to south and fifty to sixty ken from east to west, began to groan with a tremendous noise. At this sound, one could only wonder whether heaven would shake and the earth split open. Tokiko squared his shoulders, thrust out his elbows, and said,

Tokiko said, "Well now, Rock, you have spat something out! Hey, Rock, I thought you were just a silent stone, but you groan rather well! If you have enough kotodama to groan like that, Master Toki permits you, so spit out whatever you will!"

Wondrously, as soon as Tokiko finished speaking, the formidable loud noise stopped dead. Tokiko, turning triumphant and raising his nose in the air, said,

Tokiko said, "Well, everyone—nay, Lord Propagators—Toki-san's kotodama is, roughly speaking, just like this; if I were to exert my ultimate power, I would blow away rocks of this size with the tip of my nose, even if there were a hundred, a thousand, or ten thousand of them! How strong the power of faith is!"

Azumahiko said, "Indeed. It is said that the power of faith can move even mountains. If you could not accomplish at least this much, serving as an attendant to the propagators would be impossible."

At this moment, purple smoke rose countless fathoms toward heaven from the rock cave, making a hissing sound. The surroundings of the rock were entirely enveloped in the purple smoke. Looking closely, three maidens were standing upon the rock. Holding fans with the rising sun design in both hands, the three maidens formed a three-way whorl and began to dance gracefully upon the rock while singing something in low voices. Princess Umegakahime tilted her head in wonder and gazed fixedly at the dance of the three women.

Tokikō: "Well, that serpent fellow does things skillfully. When they dance in such gentle figures, even a serpent comes to seem lovely. Come, come, dance, dance! Hello everyone, they say a woman is a demon, so beware."

Azumahiko: "Nay, we are all right, but Master Toki, if you do not steady yourself, it will be dreadful if you think them beautiful women. Look, the tail of a serpent is visible at your feet."

"Eh?"

saying, looked at his feet and was searching where, where. The figures of the three abruptly vanished. When Tokikō fixed his eyes upon the rock again, the figures of the three had neither shadow nor shape.

Tokikō: "Well, it has transformed again. What is it this time? Hey, serpent, I make a request; won't you perform the dance of the peach fruit of Yomotsuhirasaka? Attention, attention! The tayū, Orochihime, who now appears upon the rock, will henceforth present to your view the dance of the peach fruit of Yomotsuhirasaka."

Azumahiko: "Ahahaha haha!"

Umegakahime: "Hohoho."

Just at the moment they were laughing thus, the three maidens, parting the withered miscanthus reeds, quietly appeared before the five people and put both hands to the ground,

The three: "Oh, you are the missionary apostles of the Anaisankyō; for the sake of the Way and for the sake of the people of the world, thank you for your labors."

Umegakahime: "Oh, you who speak thus, are you not the three daughters—Tsuki, Yuki, and Hana—of the land of Hazama?"

The three, looking at the face of Umegakahime all at once,

The three: "Oh, Lady Umegakahime, we have met you in a wondrous place. This is entirely the bringing together of the gods; how well and safely you have been performing your sacred duty. Are your elder sisters, Lady Matsushirohime and Lady Takenohime, in good health?"

Umegakahime: "Yes, thank you. The three of us sisters have divided our tasks, and for the sake of the Way, we are traveling around as missionary apostles."

Tokikō: "Well, what is this? It has come to where I cannot make any sense of it at all. Look here, look here, monster of the rock, Lady Ume, what is all this? Even if you try to deceive me, this Toki will not be deceived!"

The four women, all at once,

"Ohohoho!"

The three men, all at once,

"Wahahaha!"

Tokikō puffed out his cheeks, folded his hands, hung his head, and put on a thoughtful face.

Umegakahime: "The person who is here now is Lord Azumahiko, the missionary apostle of Shinonomewake no Mikoto, and Lord Takahiko, the missionary apostle of Aokumowake. Also, the person who is over here is the chieftain of Tetsudani Village, called Tetsuhiko. The servant who is here is Tokikō, the gatekeeper of Master Tetsuhiko. Allow me to introduce them briefly."

Akizukihime said, "Oh, my, as I did not know, I have been very rude. Are you Azumahiko? And are you Takahiko? As we are mere female missionaries, we ask for your kind support. And my, Kanahiko, how wonderful it is that you have come."

Tokikō said, "What is this? Things have become incomprehensible. That Umehime exposed such beautiful women by calling them gatekeepers or servants; what a terrible person. She could have shown at least a little tact."

Takahiko said, "Ah, let me ask the three of you—Tsuki, Yuki, and Hana: is this place not the den of the giant serpent?"

Akizukihime said, "Yes, it is so. Two or three days ago, we three sisters wondrously met in this field and heard that there was a den of a giant serpent causing various calamities. Thinking that our duty as missionaries would not be complete unless we pacified it with words, the three of us joined our strength, recited the divine words together, chanted the propagation songs, and pressed it persistently. Thereupon, the giant serpent revealed its huge form, shed tears in drops, and vowed that it would henceforth lurk beneath the earth and never come out here again; thus, through spirit-pacification, we sealed it so that it would not emerge again. The soil in this Kusu Plain and Aratamahara is quite fertile. If we now set fire to this wilderness and cultivate it, a great harvest will be gained, and many people will surely rejoice."

Azumahiko said, "Ah, you have anticipated us in achieving renown. In any case, it is splendid. Come, let us set fire to this wilderness."

Saying this, he took out his fire-striker and set fire against the wind blowing at that moment. The fire spread in all directions before their eyes, and the vast wilderness became a burnt-out field.

From this point on, the missionaries ordered Kanahiko to clear this wilderness, and Tokikō gladly obeyed Kanahiko's orders, engaged in land reclamation, built houses, and lived there. The five grains bore well, the vine crops were abundant, and eventually, a very prosperous capital was created. This is called the Capital of Kusu.

The party of missionaries raised a song of triumph, bade farewell to Tokikō, and, singing the propagation songs once more, proceeded ever westward.

と火打を取り出し、折から吹き来る風に向つて火を放つた。火は見る見る四方に燃え拡がり、さしもに広き荒野原は焼野ケ原となつて仕舞つた。

これより宣伝使一同は、鉄彦に此原野の開墾を命じ、時公は喜んで鉄彦の命に従ひ、開墾に従事し家屋を造つてこれに住んだ。五穀は良く実り蔓物は豊に、遂には大変に繁華な都が出来た。これをクスの都と謂ふ。

宣伝使一行は凱歌を挙げ、時公に別れを告げ、又もや宣伝歌を歌ひ乍ら、西へ西へと進み行くのであつた。
