Chapter 17: The Episode of Ōgetsu-hime (Part III) [484]

第17章　大気津姫の段（三）〔484〕
§ 1

"Therefore, the things born upon the body of the slain deity were: silkworms were born on her head; rice seeds were born in her two eyes; millet was born in her two ears; red beans were born in her nose; wheat was born in her genitals; and soybeans were born in her buttocks. Therefore here Kamimusubi-no-mioya-no-mikoto caused these to be taken and made them into seeds."

The phrase "the slain deity" signifies that the defiled customs of clothing, food, and shelter that violated the divine laws of the Great Deity were fundamentally abolished and eradicated.

That "silkworms were born on her head" signifies the rulers who stand above all the people. In the science of the spirit of words, regarding silkworms (Ka-hi-ko): "Ka" has the function of covering and clothing, meaning apparel; it also denotes radiant light, clear brightness, and ethereal transparency. "I" means conforming to and completing the physical body, and moving the body's limbs—which is likewise the function of garments. "Ko" represents the cerebral core of Heavenly Sincerity and the activity of the child. Therefore, leaders destined to stand above all people must first empty themselves like the silkworm, becoming sacrifices to serve the state. Clarifying heavenly principles and human morality, receiving divine wisdom and discernment, they offer pure and sincere loyalty upward to the Sovereign of countless chariots, tenderly care for the people below, resolve to be practitioners of Heavenly Sincerity, make governance flawless, and weave the divine loom of brilliant brocade in the great Way of universal stewardship. This auspicious state of the world is what is meant by "silkworms were born on her head."

"Rice seeds were born in her two eyes" signifies that the eyes govern upright balance. They are the divine mechanism for discerning everything in the world, distinguishing good from evil, beauty from ugliness. The "two eyes" refers to the left and right eyes: the left represents the upper classes, and the right represents the lower classes. Nothing in all creation lacks these eyes. Yet if upper society knows only upper society, and lower society sees only lower society, they are one-eyed. Today's statesmen and educators are mostly one-eyed, lacking the full utilization of both eyes, causing the world under heaven to become increasingly unenlightened, dark, and shrouded in perpetual gloom. Furthermore, those who can clearly perceive both the visible and spiritual worlds possess the illumination of both eyes.

Regarding rice seed (Inadane): "I" is the spirit of words for fulfillment, immense adamantine power, and foundation. "Na" is the spirit of words that governs and harmonizes all things, extending thoroughly everywhere. "Ina" is also called "Ine" (rice plant), which is the master and eye of the five grains. The spirit-return of "Ine" is "E" (food/nourishment), and the return of "Kome" (rice) is "Ke," which is the "Ke" of Ōgetsu-hime. It is also called "Yone." The return of "Yone" is likewise "E," and the return of "Kate" (food) is "Ke." The eyes of human beings sleep at night, hence called "Yone" (night-sleep / rice), and because one rests in sleep, it is called "Ine" (sleep / rice plant). Furthermore, because it resembles the human eye in its small shape, calling it "Kome" (small eye / rice) is another fascinating interpretation in the science of the spirit of words. Whenever one eats grains, one's blood naturally becomes purified, clear, and perceptive, penetrating both visible and hidden realms, discerning high and low, and fully comprehending the trends of the world. Therefore, rulers who stand at the head of all people must purify their blood, equip themselves with divine wisdom, and govern the realm. When this fundamental heavenly principle is understood by those who stand at the head, when defiled food is abandoned and replaced by the righteous grain food native to the Imperial Land, bringing forth virtuous and benevolent rule, this state is called "rice seeds were born in her two eyes."

For religious leaders, clearly perceiving the truth that the visible and spiritual worlds are one, fully understanding the divine workings of passage between life and death, and edifying the populace—this too is called "rice seeds were born in her two eyes." Attaining the realm where visible and hidden unite, high and low become one, yin and yang harmonize, sovereign and subjects live in peace, internal and external are friendly, and god and human are united—this state can truly be designated as "rice seeds were born in her two eyes."

現代日本語訳

「ゆえに殺された神の身に生じた物は、頭に蚕が生じ、二つの目に稲種が生じ、二つの耳に粟が生じ、鼻に小豆が生じ、陰（ほと）に麦が生じ、尻に大豆が生じた。そこで神産巣日御祖命がこれを取り集めさせて、種となし給うた。」

「殺されたまえる」ということは、大神の御法則に違反した汚らわしい衣食住の方法が根本的に撤廃されたという意味である。

「神の身に生れる物は頭に蚕生り」ということは、頭とはすべて国民の上に立つ支配者（指導者）のことである。蚕は言霊学上、「カ」はかぶせ覆う活用であって衣服を意味し、また光り輝き、晴れ渡り、気体が透明である言義である。「イ」は身に従い成ることであり、身にそくして動かすことである。これも衣服の活用である。「コ」は天津誠の脳髄であり、子の活用である。ゆえに万民の上に立つべき役員（指導者）は、第一に蚕のようにその身を空しくし、犠牲となって国家のために尽くさねばならない。天理・人道を明らかにし、神智・神識を感受し、それによって上は一天万乗の大君に純忠の至誠を捧げ、下は人民を愛撫し、それによって天津誠の実行者たる覚悟を持ち、政治は完全無欠、錦繍綾羅の神機を織り出すという、天下経綸の大道に奉仕するに至るめでたい世のありさまを称して、「頭に蚕生り」というのである。

「二つの目に稲種生り」ということは、目は正中（真ん中・公正）をつかさどるものである。世界の一切を見極め、善悪・美醜を判明する神機である。二つの目とは左右両眼の意義で、左は上流を代表し、右は下流を代表する目である。万有一切みなこの目のないものはない。しかし上流社会は上流のことのみを知り、下流社会は下流のことしか見ないとすれば、いわゆる片目である。現代はおおむね皆、片目の政治家や教育家ばかりであって、二つの目の活用が足りないので、天下はますます無明・暗黒・常闇となってくるのである。また顕幽両界（現界と霊界）を達観しうる人は、いわゆる二つの目が照るのである。

稲種の「イ」は成就する言霊で、大金剛力であり、基盤である。「ナ」は万物を兼ね統べる言霊にして、よく行き届くことである。「イナ」はまた「イネ」といい、五穀の主であり、眼である。イネの霊返しは「エ（餌）」となり、米の返しは「ケ」となる。大気津姫の「気（け）」である。また「よね」ともいう。「よね」の返しもまた「エ」であり、「糧（かて）」の返しは「ケ」となる。人の眼は夜分に寝る（いねる）をもって「夜寝（よね・米）」といい、寝る（いねる）をもって「寝（いね・稲）」という。人の眼に似て形が小さいがゆえに「小目（こめ・米）」というのも、言霊学上おもしろい解釈である。すべて穀食をする時は、心血が自然に清まり、明朗で敏敏となり、顕幽を達観し、上下を洞察し、天下の趨勢を知悉することができるのである。ゆえに万民の頭に立つべき治者は、心血を清め、神智を備えて天下に臨まねばならないのである。この原理・天則が頭に立つ人々に分かってきて、汚穢の食を廃し、皇国固有の正食に改め、それによって善政良治を布くに至ることを「二つの目に稲種生り」というのである。

また宗教家ならば、第一に顕幽一本の真理を達観して生死往来の神機を知悉し、万民を教化するに至ったのを「二つの目に稲種生り」というのである。顕幽一致、上下合一、陰陽和合、君民和平、内外親睦、神人合一の境地に入る真相を称して、また「二つの目に稲種生り」ということができるのである。

§ 2

"Millet was born in her two ears" signifies, as stated before, the left and right: the left representing upper society and the right representing lower society. The contraction of the spirit of words in "mimi" (ear) is "Mi." "Mi" represents agriculture, industry, and commerce; it denotes actual productive enterprise, bodily completeness, and the physical form. In short, all organs of production are collectively called "mimi." Therefore, the left ear represents capitalists and large landowners, while the right ear represents laborers and tenant farmers. Furthermore, looking at the practical function of the ear, its core is "Kiku" (to hear / to function). One speaks of the hand functioning, the ear functioning, the eye functioning, the nose functioning, the tongue speaking, the belly functioning, the palate tasting sake, and the hips functioning—these are called the "eight ears." Similarly in the spiritual realm, divine sight, divine hearing, heavenly speech, and the rest constitute the eight spiritual ears. Taking this significance of the sixteen ears of visible and hidden realms—completely flawless in both spirit and body—to institute the Imperial Chrysanthemum Crest of sixteen petals was a design of the most profound divine wisdom. That Kamiyatamimi-no-mikoto, Hikoyatamimi-no-mikoto, Oshihomimi-no-mikoto, or Prince Shōtoku being called Yatsumimi-no-mikoto (Eight-Eared Prince) are all named from this very principle.

In "millet (Aha) was born," the spirit of words in "A" is the Master of All Things, the earth, the physical realm, and the foundation. The spirit of words in "Ha" denotes expanding, flowering and bearing fruit, and abundance. In summary, "millet was born" means that both material and spiritual realms developed harmoniously, bringing forth national welfare and the people's happiness, realizing a sacred age where citizens tap their bellies and strike the earth in joyful contentment. In the Divine Revelation, it is written: "Because the people of today are all blind and deaf, no matter how magnificent a truth is held right before their eyes, they cannot see a single thing, and one step ahead is absolute darkness. God desires to make the world good, bringing joy to high and low alike to create an easy and peaceful Divine Age, making flowers bloom and fruit ripen, re-establishing the Age of Pine, the Divine Age of Miroku (567). Since 1892, God has proclaimed and called out in diverse ways, but because the people's ears have grown calloused, they say 'What is that crazy old woman blabbering about?' Even when fire is burning beneath their own feet, they pay not the slightest heed. Just watch: soon the blind will see and the deaf will hear; but once that happens, even if people suddenly panic and wish to heed what God says, it will be too late. If you are to listen, listen now; afterwards, regret will be in vain. Cruel events where neither eyes nor nose can open will soon emerge, yet not a soul in the world listens to God's true warning. Though it is a troubling situation, unless God explains it and puts it into their ears, the divine mission cannot be fulfilled. Therefore, God will persistently warn until people tire of hearing it; so you had better clean out your ear canals thoroughly." This passage expresses God's deep intent to cause millet to spring forth in their ears.

"Red beans (Azuki) were born in her nose" means reforming ostentatious attire and establishing uniforms suited to practical duties: setting official attire for ministers, distinct dress for junior officials, sacred robes for Shinto priests, vestments for Buddhist monks, uniforms for the military, working dress for farmers, artisans, and merchants, and distinct apparel for masters and servants, so that one can instantly recognize officials, religious figures, farmers, masters, servants, or laborers by their clothing. Today, lacking strict standards of dress, priests and monks wear Western suits, while servants swagger boldly down main avenues wearing crest-bearing haori and silk tabi. Such behavior is frivolous in the extreme and becomes a cause of national downfall. In "Azuki," "A" denotes radiant light, representing noble textiles such as terutae and nigitae. "A" also represents true persons in positions of prominent honor; hence it is the clothing that ministers and priests should wear, not ordinary subjects. The witty phrase "Kinumono (silk) is kinu-mono (things not to be worn)" should be taken to heart as a practical admonition. The "zuki" of Azuki means "tsuki" (wearing); determining that such garments are restricted to governing ministers, high officials, and Shinto priests is what is designated by "red beans were born in her nose." The nose is the gateway of respiration for the human body, the vital organ for human life; therefore, the chief ministers who bear the security of the nation are likened to the nose. The Divine Revelation also prophesied: "When this matter is accomplished, the nose of the Golden Deity of the Northeast will reach not only to China and India, but all the way to heaven." This signifies that the world will hold him in awe and reverence. In worldly parlance, "raising one's nose" upon achieving a great merit similarly carries the meaning of towering above ordinary persons. In contemporary times, for high ministers of state and priests who serve the spotless deities to wear clothing made from the pelts of beasts instead of wearing azuki is truly an act that violates heavenly law.

『二つの耳に粟生り』と云ふ事は、二つは前に述べた通り、左右の意義であり、左は上流、右は下流社会なる事は勿論である。耳の言霊の約まりは【ミ】である。【ミ】は農工商の三種であり、実業であり、形体具足の言義であり、身体である。要するに、一切の生産機関を総称して耳と云ふのである。故に左は資本家や、大地主を意味し、右の耳は労働者や、小作人を意味するのである。また耳は一方よりその活用を調ぶる時は【キク】と曰ふ事が主眼である。手が利く、耳が利く、目が利く、鼻が利く、口を利く、腹が利く、舌で酒を利く、腰が利く、これを八ツ耳と曰ふのである。また霊的方面に於いても同一に、神眼、神耳、天言等やはり八ツ耳である。斯の如く霊体共に完全無欠なる、幽顕十六耳の意義を取りて十六菊の御紋章を制定されたのは最も深遠なる御慮の御在します所である。神八井耳命、彦八井耳命、忍穂耳命、または聖徳太子を八ツ耳命と申すなぞは、みな前述の意義から、名付けられたものであります。

『粟生り』の【ア】の言霊は大物主であります、地であり、顕体であり、大本である。【ハ】の言霊は、延び開く也、花実也、数多き也の活用である。要するに『粟生りき』と云ふ意義は、物質、霊界共に円満に発達し、国利民福を招来し、鼓腹撃壤の聖代の、出現せし事であります。御神諭に、『今の人民は盲と聾計りであるから、何程結構な誠をして、目の前に突出してやりても一つも見えず、一寸先は真の暗であるぞよ。神は世界を良く致して、上下揃へて人民を歓ばして安楽な神世に致して、花を咲かし、実を結ばして、松の世、五六七の神世に立直して与らうと思ふて、明治二十五年から、色々と申して、呼ばはりて聞かしても、耳が蛸に成りてをるから、狂婆が何を吐かすと申して、我が身の足下に、火が燃えて来て居りても、少ちつとも耳に入れぬが、見て居じやれよ、今に盲が目が明き、聾が耳が聞こえる様に成りて来るが、さうなりてから、俄かに周章て神の申す事を聞く気に成りても、モウ間に合はぬぞよ。聞くなら今の内に聞いて置かぬと、後の後悔間に合はぬぞよ、目も鼻も開かぬ如うな、惨い事が今に出て来るが、神の申す誠の警告を聴く人民は、世界にないぞよ、困つたものであるなれど、是れを説いて聞かして、耳へ入れさして置かねば、神の役が済まぬから、嫌になる所まで、クドウ気を付けるから耳の穴を能く掃除致しておくが良ぞよ』云々とあるのは、耳に粟を生り出でしめむとの、神様の深き思召であります。

『鼻に小豆生り』と云ふ事は華美なる衣服を改め、実務に適する制服を改定されると云ふ事である。大臣は大臣の服装、小臣は小臣、神職は神職、僧侶は僧侶、軍人は軍人、農工商は農工商の制服を定め、主人は主人、僕婢は僕婢の制服を一定し、一見してその官吏たり、宗教家たり、農夫たり、主人たり僕婢たり、労働者たる事の、弁別し易き服装を制定さるる事を『鼻に小豆生り』と曰ふのであります。現代の如く服制に厳格なる定規なく、神職や僧侶なぞが洋服を着用したり、僕婢が紋附羽織を着流し、絹の足袋を穿ち大道を憚らず濶歩するが如きは、実に不真面目の至りにして、亡国の因となるのである。【アヅキ】の【ア】は光り輝く事で、照妙、和妙なぞの、高貴なる織物であります。【ア】は顕誉の地位に在る真人である。故に大臣とか、神官神職とかの、着用すべき衣服である、其の他の臣民の着用すべきものでないのだ。絹物は着ぬ物也との滑稽語は、実際の戒めとして服膺すべき言葉である。【アヅキ】の【ヅキ】は着キと云ふ事であつて、治者たる大臣高官および神官神職に限りて着用すべきものであると云ふ事を、決定されたのを『鼻に小豆生り』と曰ふのであります。鼻は人体に取つては呼吸の関門であつて、人民生息の主要点である。故に一国の安危を背負つて立てる国家の重臣を鼻と云ふのである。神諭にも、『此の事成就致したら、艮の金神の鼻は、カラ天竺は愚か、天まで鼻が届くぞよ』と予告されてあるのも、世人が尊重畏服するとの神意である。世俗が一つの功名手柄を顕はしたる時に於いて、鼻が高くなると謂ふのも人の上に卓絶したる意義である。今日のように国家の重臣や、清浄なる神明に奉仕する神官等が、小豆を着用せずして、獣畜の毛皮を以て作れる、衣服を着用するなぞは、実に天則違反の行為であります。

§ 3

"Wheat was born in her genitals" signifies that Westerners take wheat as their staple food. Because Japan and other Eastern nations are situated in the realm of Yang, it is the natural principle of the land to take rice—a Yang food—as their daily staple. Because the West is situated in the realm of Yin, it is the natural principle of that land to take wheat—a Yin food—as their daily staple. Therefore, Westerners eat bread made from wheat, whereas Easterners, especially the Japanese, are endowed by nature to eat rice. However, among modern Japanese, the higher their social rank, the more Western-worshipers appear; putting on airs of civilized sophistication, they proudly spread butter on bread and eat bland things as if they were delicious. Yet in Japan, wheat was historically designated as feed for cattle and horses. Therefore, Japanese eating rice and peoples born in the Yin realm of the West eating wheat is what is meant by "wheat was born in her genitals."

"Soybeans were born in her buttocks" refers to regions like Hokkaido, which constitutes the "buttocks" (northern extreme) of Japan. Since soybeans are plants rich in fats, people dwelling in cold climates inevitably need to consume soybeans. If one eats soybeans, one will not suffer ill health even in cold lands. However, this does not mean eating soybeans alone; one should mix them with rice, roast them, or grind them into flour. The province named Shiribeshi in Hokkaido carries the meaning of the buttocks (shiri); and reading the province of Chikugo as "Michinoshiri" likewise signifies the extreme border of the country. In summary, this passage of the Kojiki records that the dietary system corresponding to each respective land was first ordained by the divine realm.

"Therefore here Kamimusubi-no-mioya-no-mikoto caused these to be taken and made them into seeds." Takamimusuhi-no-mioya-no-kami is the Ancestral Deity of the spirit lineage, while Kamimusubi-no-mioya-no-kami is the Ancestral Deity of the material physical lineage. That he "caused these to be taken" means that the dietary system outlined in the preceding sacred text was adopted, immediately enacting Susanoo-no-mikoto's proposal regarding food. That he "made them into seeds" means establishing a divine policy for reforming heaven and earth based upon this dietary system. Therefore, when human beings violate these heavenly laws and indulge in gluttony, they ruin their physical bodies, which are sacred shrines of God, and become unable to fulfill their allotted lifespan. Hence, human beings must pay the utmost attention to their daily nourishment.

(January 17, Taisho 9 [1920] • Lecture recorded by Mayu Tanimura)

「陰（ほと）に麦生り」ということは、西洋人は麦を常食とするという意味である。日本およびその他の東洋諸国は陽の位置にある国土であるから、陽性の食物たる米を常食とするのが国土自然の道理である。西洋は陰の位置にある国土であるから、陰性の食物たる麦を常食とするのが国土自然の道理である。ゆえに西洋人は麦で作ったパンを食べ、東洋人、特に日本人は米食をするのが天賦の本性である。しかるに今日の日本人は上流になるほど西洋崇拝者が多く現れ、文明人らしい顔つきをして自慢げにパンにバターなどをつけて、まずいものを美味そうに平気で食べているが、麦は日本では牛馬の食べる物と決まっていたのである。ゆえに日本人は米を食べ、陰所たる西洋に生まれた人種は麦を食べるようになるのが「陰所に麦生り」というのである。

『陰に麦生り』と云ふ事は、西洋人は麦を常食とすると云ふ意義であります。日本および其の他東洋諸国は陽の位置にある国土であるから、陽性の食物たる米を常食とするのが、国土自然の道理である。西洋は陰の位置にある国土であるから陰性の食物たる麦を常食とするのが国土自然の道理である。故に西洋人は麦で作つたパンを食ひ、東洋人殊に日本人は米食をするのが天賦の本性である。然るに、今日の日本人は上流に成るほど西洋崇拝者が多く現はれ、文明人らしき顔付をして、自慢でパンに牛酪なぞを附けて無味ものを美味さうに、平気で喰つて居るが、麦は日本では、牛馬の喰ふべき物と決定つて居るのである。故に日本人は米を喰ひ、陰所たる西洋に生れた人種は、麦を喰ふことに成るのが『陰所に麦生り』と云ふのであります。

『故、是に神産巣日御祖命、茲を取らしめて、種と成し賜ひき』高御産巣日御祖神は霊系の祖神であり、神産巣日御祖神は、物質界体系の祖神である。『茲を取らして』と云ふ事は、前記の御本文の御食制を、採用されてと云ふ事で、素盞嗚尊の食物に関する御定案を、直ちに御採用遊ばした事であります。『種と成し玉ひき』と云ふ事は、この食制を基として、天地改良の神策を樹立し玉うたと云ふ事であります。故に人間はこの天則に違反して、暴食する時は大切なる神の宮居たる身体を毀損するやうな事になつて、天寿を全うする事が出来ぬやうに成るのであるから、人間は日々の食物には、充分に注意を払ふ可きものであります。
