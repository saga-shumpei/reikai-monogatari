Chapter 11: 表教 [11]

表教〔11〕

§ 1

The beauty, who opened the gate door a crack and peeked out for a moment, beamed with smiles, extended her fair, gentle hand, and, while beckoning toward everyone, had already hidden her figure within the gate.

Tokikō said, "Well, hearing the name Kujakuhime, I expected she would be a beauty, but she is many times more beautiful than I thought. She is a peerless beauty of whom it is said that a single glance can topple heaven and earth. Most people, once caught by those eyes for a moment, would have their hips and everything else turn completely limp. Somehow, my own hips have started to feel strange, too."

Umegakahime smiled serenely, as if she possessed some inner certainty.

Katsukō said, "Well, I thought the missionary Umegakahime was pretty, too, but her smile far surpasses your smile. When she turned this way for a moment and grinned, I too thought, 'Shitashita!' It is shitashita-batabata."

Kamokō said, "Hey, Toki-sama, have your hips become strange too? Mine feel strange as well."

Tokikō said, "Your hips have given out. Unfortunately, there is not even a herring, which is a nuisance."

Hachikō said, "He speaks as if I were a cat. With my hips giving out like this, I cannot move one way or the other."

Tokikō said, "We shall leisurely meet with Kujakuhime and enjoy a fine reception, so you had better just calmly sit down, heave a sigh, and look upon the snow all around, imagining it to be Kujakuhime's fair face. Come, Lady Umegakahime, please enter. It is a thatched hut just as it appears, but this is my humble dwelling; make yourself at home and stay as long and leisurely as you please."

Katsukō said, "Hey, Toki-sama, what are you saying? How disgraceful! Even if you repeatedly bow your hips and purse your big mouth into a tiny pucker, she will not cling to you."

Tokikō said, "Well, do not be so noisy. I am playing the part of Kujakuhime."

At this moment, from inside the mansion,

Drink and make merry, for just ahead lies darkness; After the dark, the moon will rise.

a woman's clear voice came echoing.

Katsukō said, "Well, this is bold! It is indeed the Ural teachings after all; now that it has come to this, the Anai teachings will not hold much sway. Hey, Hachikō, Kamokō, be at ease! This is the Ural teachings, a place where you fellows can have influence. Yes, Lady Umegakahime, what a pity, it is not the Anai teachings, but the spiteful Ural teachings. Hmph, pardon me, but please stand outside the gate. Come, Hachi, Kamo, there is no need to worry, come forth, come forth!"

§ 2

Hachi and Kamo drew strength from this voice, recovered from their fright,

Hachi and Kamo, "What, the Ural religion? That is interesting,"

and, squaring their shoulders, they advanced through the gate.

Tokikō, "Ahahaha, what self-serving fellows! That is why I say your repentance cannot be relied upon."

Katsukō, "Praying to the gods only when things go wrong."

So saying, he vigorously opened the front door,

Katsukō, "Pardon me! I am Katsukō from Kitano village, an inspector for the Ural religion. I have just captured missionaries of the Anai religion alive; they are rather tough customers, so I ask for your reinforcement. If we miss such a prize, there will be no undoing it."

Princess Umegaka, "Ho-ho-ho-ho. Lord Katsu, do not worry. We shall neither run nor hide. Through my kotodama, I shall show you how I make them submit splendidly."

Katsukō, "Hmph, listen to you talk!"

Saying this, he strode boldly deeper into the house as if he were returning to his own home.

The party followed behind Katsukō. It was not a very large house, but there was a neat and tidy parlor. There sat the person in question, Princess Kujaku, with a one-stringed zither placed before her. The moment Princess Umegaka took one look at her,

Princess Umegaka, "Ah, you are, ma...!"

she began to say, and suddenly covered her mouth with her sleeve.

Katsukō, "Now, now, Lady Umegaka! What a rude thing to say to Princess Kujaku of the Ural religion, crying 'You are ma—'! She is no evil spirit or anything of the sort, but a fine deity."

Tokikō, "Well now, permit me to ask this person called Princess Kujaku: according to rumors heard on the road, it is said that you eat up each and every person who passes through here, but do you truly devour them?"

Princess Kujaku, "Yes, to passersby, without distinction of age or sex, I tell them as if I chew and swallow them down, and..."

Hachikō, "Eh, what? Chewing and swallowing, scalding them in hot water just like boiling an octopus, is that it? Thrown into hot water and boiled, made just like deep-fried fritters, and then chewed and swallowed—are you really the incarnation of the eight-forked serpent, the guardian deity of Uralhiko?"

Princess Kujaku, "Ho-ho-ho-ho."

Katsukō, "You must belong to the Ural religion. I am an inspector of the Ural religion, a petty fellow like this, but please keep me in mind and kindly make good use of me."

Princess Kujaku, "I do not belong to the Ural religion."

§ 3

Katsukō: "Eh, what, if it is not Uralism, then what teaching is it?"

Kujakuhime: "It is the Omote Teaching."

Katsukō: "Aha, that is a name like a tatami mat; I am constantly thinking—omote—that the tatami is worn out and I want to re-cover it, and I too am thinking—omote—that my soul has somehow become strange, so I want to have it renewed; then, what kind of relation does it have with Uralism?"

Kujakuhime: "It is front and back—ura and omote; in short, it is the opposite teaching. If people of Uralism appear, we will chew and suck them, from one end to the other..."

Katsukō: "Excuse me, I too shall join the Omote Teaching; up until now, I was just thinking that Uralism was not interesting."

Tokikō: "Hahaha, what a mercenary fellow you are."

Katsukō: "It is a change of heart, a fleeting moment's impulse; I have never felt such distress. Contrary to expectation, front and back, root and leaf, I have completely lost track of what is what."

Tokikō: "Excuse me, Umegakahime, you seem somehow to bear a close resemblance to the master of this place. You also seemed to fail somewhat in your transformation at Kusunogahara, but are you different from that demon?"

Umegakahime: "Well, well, that is fine."

Tokikō: "Saying 'Well, well, that is fine' means the timing—ma—is bound to be bad; having a poor head—atama; if we hesitate in such a place, there is no telling what kind of plight we might encounter."

Umegakahime: "There are no ogres in this world. First of all, calm yourself."

Tokikō: "Umegakahime, you were making somewhat strange glances and doing something like telepathy with the evil god of this place, were you not?"

Umegakahime: "Ho-ho-ho-ho."

Katsukō: "This is strange; hey, Hachikō, Kamokō, this fellow may be one of the Anai Teachings."

Hachi and Kamo: "Neither Hachi nor Kamo belongs to the Anai Teachings; we belong to the Omote Teaching."

Tokikō: "Even if you suddenly turn your hand and switch allegiance, claiming to belong to the Omote Teaching, there is no fear of your being immensely popular."

Hachikō: "Is it not said that God appears on the surface—omote—and separates good and evil? This might well be a faction of the Anai Teachings."

Umegakahime stood up straight and began to sing a song of propagation.
