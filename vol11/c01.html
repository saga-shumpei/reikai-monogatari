Chapter 1: The Plain of Camphor Trees [468]

第1章　クス野ケ原〔468〕

§ 1

The missionary of Aokumo-wake-no-Mikoto, who clears across the plains of heaven, Having changed his name to Takahiko, The divine priest of Amenokoyane, Who forever established great merits before the Rock Cave of Heaven; Seeking to subdue Ural-hiko, Ural-hime, And the eighty Magatsu deities, Who shut themselves up in the mountains of Ural and Armenia as their demonic strongholds, And to thoroughly clear and illuminate The Toyoashihara no Mizuho no Kuni, He departed from Golden Mountain, Crossed Ame-no-manai, Was swept by waves and soaked by rain, His hair combed by the blowing wind, Crossed over many mountains and rivers, And with divine majesty, crossed the Uchinogawa River Falling from the Altai Mountains, Arriving here at the Plain of Kusu.

Upon the grassy plain stretching a thousand leagues in a single view, He has advanced as the days and months piled up.

Parting the fields of pampas grass as far as the eye could reach, and blown by a particularly cold winter gale, he trudged on his weary feet, using the howling voices of tigers and wolves as his guide, proceeding while singing the missionary songs.

The sun drew near to twilight, and the gloomy night air pressed upon his body. The voices of hundreds of birds ceased abruptly, and the cries of fierce beasts were heard louder with every passing moment.

The missionary Takahiko, intending to pass the night here, took the withered plain as his bedding, covered his face with his woven hat, and donned his straw raincoat; as he slept, an unease arose from nowhere in his chest. Suddenly opening his eyes, he saw an immense monster towering before him, a single mirror-like eye shining in the center of its forehead, its nose like that of a Kagura lion, its mouth torn open to its ears, with an indigo-blue face, glaring down at Takahiko.

Lying on his back, Takahiko remained silent, fixing his gaze upon the one-eyed monster without turning away. The monster stretched forth its hairy, pitch-black hand, pressing closer to seize Takahiko's chest in a single grasp.

Takahiko quietly chanted the missionary song. Emitting a strange voice, the monster began to spin wildly forward, backward, left, and right. Takahiko chanted the missionary song ever louder. Little by little, the monster diminished in volume, eventually turning into a ball resembling white smoke and fading away. Looking up into the midair, a mysterious black shadow rose, parting the pale, fish-scale clouds.

Takahiko: 'Ah, that demon of the Ural Mountains has disturbed my peaceful sleep, just when I was resting my fatigue and sleeping so well. I had heard that this Plain of Kusu is a place where one cannot let down one's guard. Well, there is still some time before dawn breaks, so I shall have another sleep before setting off.'

So saying, he once again rolled over and lay down, falling into a deep sleep thereafter, his snoring echoing around the surroundings like thunder.

At this time, out of nowhere, someone aimed at Takayamahiko's body and struck him with a staff with all his might. Takayamahiko was startled and stood up straight,

"Insolent fellow!"

Thus he bellowed, whereupon a large man said,

"Ba-ba-ba-ba, you monster, do not make a fool of me! I will not fall for that trick! Who do you think I am? I am none other than the gatekeeper of Kuroganehiko, chief of Kuroganedani Village. Though I have grown a bit older now, I am Master Toki, renowned in this neighborhood, who in his youth even took part in minor sumo wrestling! What is this? Just a while ago, you turned into a one-eyed monster, opening a big, clumsy mouth and threatening this honorable self with a pale face, but what a sorry sight you were, fleeing like clouds and mist before the kotodama of Master Toki's propagating song! This time, changing your trick, you put on the disguise of a propagating envoy of Ishikoridome, lying here and snoring away! I will tolerate it no longer! You must be a lackey of Hebitsukami from the Altai Mountains. Even your boss, Hebitsukami, feared the mighty prestige of Master Toki, turned into a pale light, let his unsightly loincloth hang down, and fled back to a country called Armenia! Come on, glare with your eyes, stick out your tongue! Master Toki is not one to be startled by such things! Do not underestimate me so much!"

Takayamahiko: "Well, Master Toki or whatever your name may be, we are not monsters."

Tokikō stuck out his tongue a little, jutted his chin, and said,

"'Well, Master Toki or whatever your name may be, we are not monsters' ...is what you dare to say! That trick won't fool me! If you keep blabbering nonsense, this staff shall pay you a visit! You eyeball monster!"

Takayamahiko: "Dear me, you mistake us for monsters; we are by no means such creatures. We, too, have just encountered that one-eyed boy. While we were sound asleep, we were awakened in the middle of it, and being sleepy, the condition of our eyes is..."

Tokikō: "Whoa... spare me that! Stop talking about eyes, stop it! It is somewhat bothersome to me as well..."

"Because I have not had enough sleep, I have developed a fever and my tongue is tangled..."

"Hey, hey, do not mention that tongue anymore! Even I was startled by that tongue!"

"Because I am exhausted from a long journey, to your eyes I may not look human, and my face probably looks pale. Therefore, that you suspect me is..."

時公『ザマ見やがれ。白状しよつた。アクマ彦奴が。蛙は我と我が口から白状したが、もうアクマと知つた以上は、俺は善にも強ければ悪にも強い時さまだ。あくまで打ちこらしてやる。俺の顔を冥途の土産に穴のあくまで見ておけ。根の国底の国へ行つてもこの時さんのやうな強いお方は滅多にありやせぬぞ』

と言ひながら、携へた鉄棒をもつて打つてかかる。東彦は笠をもつて、その棒を右に左に避け乍ら、時公の足をさらへた。時公はズデンドーと仰向けに倒れた。東彦は、

『ウン』

と一声霊縛をかけたるに時公は、

『オイ、目の玉、アクマ彦、何うしよるのだ。貴様わりとは悪戯けた事をしよる。身体はアルタイ山の鬼の化石のやうになつちやつたが、目と口と耳とはしつかりしてをるぞ。貴様は一つ目だ。俺は二つ目だ。睨み殺してやらうか』

東彦『アハヽヽヽ、やあ、時さまとやら、私を信じて下さい。私もつい最前のこと、その一つ目小僧に出会つたのだ。が、お前さんも途中で出会つて来たのか』

時公は俄に調子をかへて、

『ハイハイ、一目見るよりビツクリ仰天せむとせしが、待て暫し、アルタイ山の蛇掴みでさへも、この時さまの鼻息で吹き散らしたのだ。何だ、一つ目の化物位と思ひ直してここまでやつて来たが、何だか膝頭がこそばくて、笑うたり泣いたりしやがつて、時さんは怒る、膝坊主は泣き笑ひする。酒も飲まぬに、一人で三人上戸を勤めて来ました。私の主人は鉄彦というて、それはそれは余り偉うない豪傑ですが、三五教の宣伝使のあななひをしてな、アナ有り難いとか何とか言つて石凝姥の宣伝使と、何でも名は忘れたがスイスイ、粋な名のつく別嬪の宣伝使と三人連れで、クスの原を向ふへ渡ると言つて出かけました。さうしたところが俄に奥さまが、病気になつたものだから、オイ時公、お前は天下無双の豪傑だ、一つ目小僧の百匹や千匹はビクともようせぬ奴だから、御苦労だが主人を呼んで来てくれと、奥様が手毬のやうな涙を、こぼして頼むものだから、ヨシきた、たとへウラル彦の軍勢、幾万来るとも、この時さまが腕力をもつて、縦横無尽に打つて打つて打ちまはし、木端微塵に砕いてやるは瞬くうちと尻ひつからげ、クスの荒野を韋駄天走り、生かじりの宣伝歌を、処々歌つて足拍子をとり乍らやつて来たところ、向ふに怪しき影がある。ハーテ訝しやな、この荒野ケ原に現れ出づる怪物は何者なるぞ、尋常に名を名乗れとやつて見せたり、と思つたが何だか、向ふの舌が長うてこつちの舌が捲かれたか、負たか知らないが、こわばつて一寸も時さまの言ふ事を聞きやがらぬので、今度は目の御用だと、クルクル鏡の如き両眼を開いて見せた。流石一つ目の怪物も、時さんの勇気に辟易し、褌下げて西南の天を指して逃げ散つたり』

§ 4

Azumahiko said, "Ahahaha, what an amusing fellow you are."

Tokikō said, "Whether it is amusing or not I do not know, but I find it not amusing at all. Even lying on my side and talking like this, I have no influence whatsoever. Why do you not also come beside me and lie down? Shall we have a leisurely pillow-talk together?"

"Ahahaha, what a thorough braggart you are through and through! You are a fellow who hates to admit defeat. If so, shall I also lie beside you and keep you company until daybreak? This is why they say parents who have mischievous boys have a hard time. Well then, heave-ho!"

Saying this, he lined up his pillow with Tokikō and lay down with a thud.

Tokikō said, "Well now, you are quite a sensible fellow, Akumahiko! However, I feel sorry for you, for though it would be fine if I were Lady O-Toki, with Lord Tokikō I doubt I suit your taste. Even so, my heart goes toki-toki somehow."

Azumahiko said, "Ah, I too was troubled by boredom. Not that it is the Reikai Monogatari, but shall we lie snugly on our backs here and share a pillow-talk?"

The missionary Azumahiko, resembling the morning sun rising and parting the dawn sky, rose up briskly, released Tokikō from his spiritual binding, and while indulging in divine tales along the way, the two proceeded west and ever westward across the boundless great plain.
