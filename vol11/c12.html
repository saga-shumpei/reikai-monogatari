Chapter 12: 松と梅 [12]

松と梅〔12〕

§ 1

Umegakahime: "In the battle of the Island of Yomi, the missionary of pine, bamboo, and plum, who appeared as the fruit of the peach tree, ran up Mount Tenkyō, reverently obeyed the Divine Word, blew away the clouds and mist in all four quarters, and seeking to shine upon the reign of the sunrise, the missionary of Umegakahime-no-Mikoto, whose very name is auspicious like the tree flowers blooming all at once that withstand the frost and give off fragrance, straddling the back of a camel, crossed the Desert of Ashi, and advancing to make even the evil deities of the Plain of Kusunogahara submit to the divine words of the Amatsu Norito, came across the three pillars of Moon, Snow, and Flower, the officials of the Ananai teachings possessing high divine majesty, on the Plain of Aratamahara,

rejoicing in meeting here, Shinonewake-no-Azumahiko and Toki-sama of Tetsutani Village met as three, and the pure-hearted party of six pillars, trampling through the snow in the northern forest, advanced ever westward. The children of the Kami, purer than snow, divided to the right and left to drive away the evil deities of Mount Caucasus, and just as I advanced all alone to Lake Akashi, assisted by Toki-sama's attendant deity who followed after me, and came seeking to guide the evil deity Peacock Princess to the Great Way of the Kami with just a single word and save her, to my astonishment, the Peacock Princess,

shone with the divine teachings, and how joyful it was when I realized this to be the dwelling of Matsudirohime-no-Mikoto in the reign of Miroku! Yet even so, where is the elder sister, Takenohime-no-Mikoto, now? Does she wander here and there over the snow-covered fields? How painful it is! Ah, elder sister, elder sister! Like the opening of the Heavenly Rock Cave, the sky of today clears the heart; having subdued the eighty evil spirits, she restored the divine capital of Armenia of high merit, and before Nodatihiko, Nodatihime, and Takateruhime who dwell on the Mountains of Heavenly and Earthly Teachings, Matsudirohime, wondrous in the color of an evergreen tree that achieves great deeds, in the era of Kami, the era of pine, the era of Miroku,

I joyfully celebrate the deeds of my elder sister, linked to the divine era of pine."

Having so sung, she took her seat.

Hachi said in a low voice:

"Hey, hey, Katsu-kō, Kamo-kō, it seems the wind has changed somehow. You were blabbing inside the Akashimaru about a wind god taking advantage of weakness, or about catching a cold or not, but this might be another strange wind god."

Katsu-kō: "Barely as it is, this is certainly suspicious; just when I thought she was a beautiful missionary, she turns out to be the Peacock Princess's younger sister. She blabbed that if we keep looking around, she would boil us up and chew and eat us just like before, and that might actually be true."

§ 2

Tokikō said, "Now then, Katsukō, how is it? Shall I hear your momentary feeling? What about turning over a new leaf?"

"Far from turning over a new leaf, my nerves are in a state of excitement. Hey, Toki-sama, for the sake of our travel companionship, won't you please decline on our behalf?"

Tokikō said, "A refusal is a refusal, but such a thing is flatly refused from Toki-sama's side. At any rate, you are a fellow who tormented the missionary of Ananaikyō in the northern forest, so this is repayment for that. You had better prepare yourself. For a human, the heart of the moment is important. No matter how painful it may be, resign yourself to it."

Katsukō said, "The first one who tormented him was Kamokō, next was Hachikō, and this Katsukō was only the third. Excuse me, Princess Matsushiro, Princess Kujaku, I know nothing of it at all; if you are going to boil and eat something, the eight-legged octopus or the duck tastes good. Even if you eat someone like me, it will be hard and tough, like biting a rock, and hardly delicious at all."

Tokikō laughed, "Hahahaha, ahahaha!"

Princess Umegaka laughed, "Ohohoho!"

Princess Matsushiro said, "Oh, everyone, welcome; you have come well. This is quite convenient. Bonito, duck, octopus—you possess names that sound truly delicious."

Tokikō said, "Please tell them to chew well before eating. Otherwise, he is quite a stubborn man and will not easily go down into the belly."

Katsukō said, "Hey, Toki-sama, do not give them ideas by speaking needless things; how could I ever end up entering a monster's belly? With you around, it is dangerous and nerve-racking; you infuriating fellow. Ah, ah, whether dream or reality, demon or serpent, purify and cleanse the disaster coming upon my body!"

Tokikō said, "Uhahahaha! Excuse me, Princess Kujaku, shall the three of us—you, Princess Umegaka, and I—each take one? If a peacock eats a duck, it would be birds eating birds, which is not amusing, so Toki-sama will take the duck; Lady Ume has a slightly sour name, so she can eat the eight-legged Hachikō dipped in sweetened vinegar; and Princess Matsushiro, please eat Katsukō, for the fish of the pine is bonito. It is a menu perfectly made to order. Ahahaha!"

Katsukō said, "Hey, Toki-sama, what is this really? Do you truly intend to eat us? I thought you were somewhat strange fellows, but the two of you tricked us and dragged us to such a den of demons. Since it has come to this, it is now desperation. Come, Tokikō, there is not a moment to delay. This fellow might be an evil being dwelling in the Altai Mountains. Shall this Katsukō strike your head with a clunk? It cannot be helped now; I am driven to reckless abandon. Hachi, shall we tear this fellow into eight pieces, or shall we bite them from our side instead? Hey, Kamokō!"

Tokikō said, "What a fool you are; it is all a lie. In any case, judging from the song of the propagation missionary earlier, she seems to be our elder sister; look carefully and compare her face. Though her body appears slightly larger, her eyes, nose, and mouth are an exact double. Well, set your mind at ease; there is little fear of being eaten."

Katsukō said, "What an ill-natured man, turning one's pluck upside down! Hey, Hachikō, Kamokō, we are safe now."

Tokikō said, "It was not your pluck that was overturned; it was the jewel of your true mind that was overturned by your momentary impulse. Hey, shall you overturn it once more and take refuge in Ananai-kyō so that it will never be overturned for all eternity?"

Katsukō said, "I will, I will; I will grind miso and grind mortars. From now on, should I become a miso-grinding servant here and be put to work?"

Matsushiro-hime stood up smiling and began to sing a propagation song.

Matsushiro-hime sang: "To establish the immovable, Firm and everlasting reign of Miroku, Guiding by the divine teachings Of the God of Sunrise and of Konohana, The propagation missionaries of pine, bamboo, and plum Go forth clearing the barren wilderness. To subdue the ugly rampages Of the evil god, Ural-no-hiko, Takenono-hime has set out, Heading for the deep mountains of the Caucasus. For a while, clearing through the snow Of the Peacock Plain, To illuminate the darkness of the hearts of worldly people, Matsushiro-hime of passing travelers, Treading through the path Of the brilliantly radiant spear, Has now disguised herself here as the Princess of the Peacock, Saving the green human herbs one and all Into the divine land of God, The heavenly rock cave of perpetual dark night

Matsushiro-hime of the evergreen rock opens it; The waiting has been worthwhile, To Umegaka-hime whom I yearn for, now here, Showing a healthy form, the time has come To illuminate the divine work of the three peaches, The great spirit of Ōkamuzumi. O evergreen and firm Toki-sama! O Katsu-sama, who says that evil spirits flourish And triumph over Heaven! When Heaven is settled, it triumphs over man; The country of eight islands with eight island roots, Hachi-sama and Kamo-sama together, Illuminate your hearts with the teachings Of the Moon of Ananai, Clap your open hands and praise the Great God! Tokikō said, "Well, with this, everything—both the spring snow and our doubts—has melted away. Matsushiro-hime decided to head for Mount Caucasus together with Umegaka-hime. And Katsukō decided to remain in this mansion and sing the propagation songs of Ananai-kyō with all his might to pacify the evil gods with words.

Tokikō, Hachikō, and Kamokō followed the two propagation missionaries and decided to head vigorously toward Mount Caucasus.
