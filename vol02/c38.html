Volume 2 • Chapter 38 (Cumulative 88)

Rejoicing of Heaven and Earth

第三八章　歓天喜地〔八八〕
§ 1
Having been parted by death from his beloved wife without even the leisure to give her a solemn burial, Kiyoteruhiko received the inescapable, severe divine command from Kototamawake-no-Mikoto, and immediately set out toward Mount Kohaku; with feelings akin to swallowing a sword and severing his own bowels, he was thrust into the desperate extremity of being forced to destroy his own biological parents. Yet having no way to decline the divine mandate, he upheld supreme righteousness and launched a tragic battle wherein blood washed blood.

§ 2
Arakumahiko and Lady Arakuma managed to cut a path through the encirclement and narrowly escaped with their lives. At that moment, had Kiyoteruhiko pursued them immediately, it would have been effortless to destroy them. Yet though they were enemies in arms, swayed by flesh-and-blood love, he deliberately let them escape; bowing to their receding shadows in his heart, he worried over his parents' future, fervently praying that wherever they might hide, they would spend the remainder of their lives in peaceful retirement. In terms of parental and filial affection, such conduct was only natural.

§ 3
Gathering his scattered remnants, Arakumahiko, still unchastened, advanced upon Rome Castle and fought with desperate determination. Yet destitute of heavenly fortune, Arakumahiko's strength was exhausted, and he was captured by Ōshimahiko; husband and wife were secretly imprisoned together, passing many cheerless and dreary months.

§ 4
Hearing rumors carried upon the wind that his parents had been defeated at Rome, imprisoned, and were suffering in chains, Kiyoteruhiko found no peace of mind, passing solitary and cheerless days in agonizing grief. Because Kiyoteruhiko was a divine commander deeply devoted to loyalty and filial piety, the torment in his heart was beyond imagination. On rainy mornings and windy evenings, he gazed up at the sky with heavy sighs: "Knowing the miserable plight of my parents, I cannot bear to sit idly by; yet if I attempt to rescue them, I will disobey my lord's command and commit the heinous crime of high treason. Alas! My beloved wife and my parents—one has departed to the unseen spirit world, while the others are captive in an enemy citadel! However ruthlessly a child might harden his heart, this is too grievous to endure. Rather, let me take my own life, and by dying fulfill the supreme righteousness of both loyalty and filial piety!" Just as he reached this resolve, a dispatch arrived:

§ 8
Leaving behind these words, she transformed once more into a jewel of dazzling brilliance and vanished into the high heavens. In her wake, Kiyoteruhiko felt as though he were dreaming within a dream, and bewildered by the heavenly maiden's words, doubted himself, wondering: "Have I not finally lost my mind from excessive grief and suffering? Or did my yearning for my parents and wife become so intense that a delusion condensed before my eyes?" Yet somewhere deep within his heart, he perceived a single ray of radiant light. In any event, he thought, "If I take my own life here and now, who will comfort the spirits of my parents and wife?" Composing his spirit, he resolved to endure and wait for the appointed time, uncertain though it seemed.

§ 9

Yet the sky of Kiyoteruhiko's heart remained overcast; though flowers bloomed, though birds sang, though the commanders rejoiced, to him all he saw and heard seemed to mock and curse his soul; his tears of grief found no drying, and day and night his sorrow only deepened.
