Volume 02 • Part 4: The Land of Tokoyo

Chapter 26: The Albatross

第二六章 信天翁〔七六〕
§ 1
Tokoyohime's apparent flight into the Rockies disguised as an eight-tailed golden vixen had been nothing more than an illusory demonic trick; in reality, she had remained concealed within the deepest chambers of the citadel. Now appearing in regal splendor before Ryūseihime and Takekumahiko, her followers rejoiced as if the dead had been resurrected, and the castle erupted in festive jubilation like blossoms blooming upon withered boughs. In stark contrast, Kojimawake, Takeshimahiko, and Matsudayohime—having allowed Kototamawake-no-Mikoto to slip through their fingers—were paralyzed with dread, agonizing over how to face Waka-sakurahime-no-Mikoto with faces pale as ashes. Unable to contain her amusement at their misery, Ryūseihime burst into laughter and launched into another satirical song and dance:
現代日本語訳
元照彦の猛攻に屈して金毛八尾白面の悪狐に化けて逃げ去ったように見えたのは、すべて常世姫が仕組んだ幻惑の魔術でありました。常世姫は実際には城外へ逃げておらず、依然として城奥深くに身を潜めていたのです。奪還に成功した城内に、美麗な正装を纏った常世姫が悠然と姿を現すと、竹熊彦らは死人が蘇ったかのように狂喜乱舞しました。城内は枯れ木に花が咲いたかのように一転して華やいだ空気に包まれました。これとは対照的に、言霊別命を取り逃がし失態を重ねた小島別、竹島彦、松代姫の三柱は、本国に帰って稚桜姫命にどう弁明すべきか思い悩み、青息吐息で真っ青になっておりました。その惨めな姿を見た竜世姫は笑いを堪え切れず、またしても面白おかしく戯歌を歌い踊り始めたのであります。
原文（近代文語）
元照彦の攻撃に進退きはまり、金毛八尾白面の悪狐となりてロッキー山の方面に雲をおこして逃げ帰りしと見えしは、まつたく常世姫の魔術であつた。常世姫は依然として城内の奥深く潜んでゐた。常世姫は盛装をこらし悠然として竜世姫、竹熊彦らの前に現はれた。竹熊彦は死者の蘇へりし如く狂喜した。諸神司もともに歓喜の声をあげて勇躍した。城内はにはかに、枯木に花の咲きしがごとく陽気となつた。これに反し小島別、竹島彦、松代姫は稚桜姫命にたいし、この失敗をいかにして陳謝せむやと、思案にくれ、顔の色までかへて青息吐息の体であつた。竜世姫は可笑しさに堪へかねて失笑だした。さうしてまた面白く歌を唄つて踊りだした。
§ 2
The lyrics of her ditty ran: 'Looking at Takeshimahiko's grim visage, / Like King Yama swallowing bitter incense! / What ails thee to wear such a sour face? / Is this hell, or is it paradise? / Is this not the castle of Tokoyo? / Shall we summon the Bodhisattva Jizō to pour thee wine? / And glancing at Kojimawake's countenance, / Is it an unripened gourd or dried calabash? / A morning melon, duck melon, or pumpkin? / Or perhaps a stunted, twisted squash? / Though this is no vegetable patch, / There he gasps like a withered yam! / Pondering thy deeds with sober eyes, / Guilty of plots kneaded out of lies, / Thou hast blushed crimson with shame like persimmon stalks! / Never meddle in clumsy plots; / Like the night owl scheming at twilight, / Deprived of supper, how pitiful! / Behold the true albatross (ahōdori—foolish bird)! / Thy only gain is weeping in bed; / Wait for roasted beans to blossom! / This humiliation is thy own karma, / Karmic retribution manifest before thy eyes! / Rage and lash out as thou wilt, / I wear a face of utter innocence! / Whether thy honor is saved or lost, / Whether thy spiky chestnut head splits in two, / The useless ones are none but ye! / Cut thy three daily meals down to two, / Suck thy thumbs and go to bed! / Yoi, yoi, yoi, yoito-sassa! / Quickly flee home to Ryūgū-jō! / When ye return, Waka-sakurahime will slap thy heads, / What a comical sight! / Mocked and laughed at, lose not thy temper, Kojimawake! / Good fortune enters the smiling gate; / Bide thy time and reform thy ways! / Yoi, yoi, yoi, yoito-sassa!'

§ 3
The officials of Tokoyo consoled Kojimawake and Takeshimahiko with reassuring words. Tokoyohime bestowed rare gifts upon the two, gifted radiant gems to Ryūseihime, and prepared magnificent treasures as tribute for Waka-sakurahime-no-Mikoto. Thus the four deities set forth on their return journey across oceans, mountains, and rivers to Ryūgū-jō. Appearing before Waka-sakurahime-no-Mikoto without a trace of apprehension, Ryūseihime presented the tribute, recounting the comical blunders of Kojimawake and Takeshimahiko in witty, vivid detail. Waka-sakurahime-no-Mikoto erupted in fury:

§ 5
She commanded in a thunderous tone. The three culprits cowered like mice pursued by cats or fleas scurrying for cover, trembling with terror. Pitying their wretched state, Kototamahime sought desperately to soften the sovereign's anger; taking the cue, Ryūseihime improvised a hilarious ditty while Kototamahime danced with eccentric pantomime, moving Waka-sakurahime-no-Mikoto to uproarious laughter to diffuse the wrath. The song ran:

§ 6
The lyrics of this delightful ditty ran: 'Like a toad pursued by a giant serpent! / Had they known it would come to this, never would they have gone on the embassy! / Deceived by every trick, tormented by high-spirited deities, / Obstructed by Moto-teruhiko, quarreling over good and evil, / Trading dark suspicions back and forth! / Wagging their tails before the crafty fiends of Tokoyo, / Wiggling their hips and losing their nerve, / Stripped of their litters and forced to carry the palanquin, / Sweated and squeezed by Kototamawake-no-Mikoto they carried, / Forced to massage his aching hips into the night! / Crawling back from Koshi with spineless faces into Ryūgū-jō, / Looking like salted greens or leeches sprinkled with salt! / Like cuckoos coughing up blood in agony, / Dragged before the throne without a breath of rest! / What excuse can such clumsy rough-hewn men offer? / Departing with towering bluster, but returning in such a pitiful state, / Hiding their faces and weeping in dark corners! / Behold the bewildered cuckoo of deep mountains, / Staring blankly, hiding its face but showing its tail! / Surpassing all fools under heaven, / Like the crow croaking foolishly at dawn: "Fool! Fool!" / We beseech thee, grant them thy royal pardon! / Though three heads together are said to match Monju's wisdom, / These three deities have grown old without growing wise; / Weak-kneed like limp sea-kelp, babbling without looking ahead! / Like three monkeys scurrying abroad to reap humiliation in Tokoyo, / And returning home only to scratch their heads in shame! / Rather than weeping like monkeys fallen from trees, / Leave this courtly presence for now! / Yoi, yoi, yoi, yoito-sassa!' Such was the comic song. By the wondrous Kotodama of this song and dance, Waka-sakurahime-no-Mikoto's wrath was softened, and the three culprits were graciously forgiven for their failure.
