Section 1

Divine Vestments & The Spiritual Aura (Reii)

神界における神々の御装束と霊衣の相

Outlining the sacred garments worn by primordial deities and revealing the vital distinction between righteous and evil spirits through the thickness and radiance of their spiritual robes.

太古神界の神々の高貴なる御装束の基準を明かし、霊衣（オーラ）の厚みと光輝によって正邪を見分ける奥義を説く。
§ 1
I feel it necessary to state in advance a broad outline regarding the sacred attire and vestments of the deities in the Heavenly Realm. As it would consume immense time and labor to dictate individual descriptions for the garments of every single deity appearing throughout this narrative, I shall provide a general overview here. First, a deity of supreme nobility such as Kunitokotachi-no-Mikoto (here provisionally named Kuniharutachi-no-Mikoto) generally wears garments of the finest silk: an outer robe of unpatterned royal purple, undergarments of immaculate pure white, and an inner robe of vivid, solid crimson—vestments reflecting the utmost divine majesty. Next, Kunihirotachi-no-Mikoto wears an outer robe of solid azure blue, an inner robe of red, and lower attire of solid white. Wakazakurahime-no-Mikoto wears an outer garment of light sky-blue woven with diverse exquisite patterns, customarily appearing in a magnificent twelve-layered ceremonial robe (j&umacron;nihitoe) adorned throughout upper, middle, and lower layers with auspicious motifs of pine and plum blossoms. Celestial messengers such as Tenshi &Omacron;yashimahiko-no-Mikoto and &Omacron;taruhiko wear solid black silk outer robes, crimson inner robes, and solid white undergarments. Other divine generals wear solid-colored robes in accordance with their divine rank and station—such as blue, red, scarlet, sky-blue, white, yellow, or deep navy—distinguished strictly by materials ranging from silk to hemp and cotton.
現代日本語訳
神界における神々の神聖なる御装束・御服装につきまして、あらかじめその大略を述べておく必要があろうかと思います。今後の物語において登場する神々お一人お一人の御服装に関しまして、その都度いちいち口述して説明することは大変な手間と時間を要しますので、ここで全体的に概括して述べておきますと、まず国治立命（くにはるたちのみこと／国常立尊）のような最高位の高貴なる神は、多くの場合において上等な絹物（絹織物）をお召しになり、上衣（うわぎ）は高貴な紫色の無地、下衣（したぎ・袴など）は混じりけのない純白色、そしてその間に着用される中衣（なかぎ）は鮮やかな紅色の無地という、至高の威厳を備えた御装束であります。次に国大立命（くにひろたちのみこと）におかれましては、青色の無地の上衣に、中衣は赤色、下衣は純白の無地をお召しになっております。また稚桜姫命（わかざくらひめのみこと）は、上衣は爽やかな水色に種々の華麗で美しい吉祥模様が織り出され、多くは上衣・中衣・下衣のいずれにも松や梅などの雅やかな文様が施された、絢爛たる十二単衣（じゅうにひとえ）の御装束をお召しになっておられます。さらに天の使いである天使・大八洲彦命（おおやしまひこのみこと）や大足彦（おおたるひこ）などの御装束は、上衣は格式ある黒色の無地、中衣は赤色、下衣は純白無地の光沢ある絹の服となっております。その他の神将（武神・軍神）たちにつきましては、それぞれの神格・位階の序列に応じて、青、赤、緋（あけ）、水色、白、黄、紺など、いずれも単色の無地服を着用し、その身分や神役に応じて絹・麻・木綿などの素材の違いによって厳格に区別されているのであります。

§ 2
Furthermore, the sacred crowns (kanmuri) assume diverse shapes with varying lengths of rear pendants (ei), indicating spiritual rank; these are worn by exalted divinities of the rank of the Eight King and Eight Head Deities (Hachi&omacron; Hatt&omacron;shin) and above. Deities of lower ministerial rank wear eboshi caps, clad in n&omacron;shi (informal court robes) and kariginu (hunting garments). Goddesses generally wear immaculate meii robes, utilizing graceful hues such as blue, red, yellow, white, and purple, while their pleated hakama skirts are divided into the five cosmic colors. The martial divine generals wear ketteki robes (slit-sided military court dress) with crowns, all uniformly attired in solemn black. The divine soldiers (shinsotsu) wear horizontal flat kasa hats (“ichinoji” hats) upon their heads, their garment hems tucked up short for swift movement, with wrists and ankles bound securely with purple cords—presenting an exceptionally gallant and dignified bearing. If one were to expound upon these in meticulous detail there would be no end; what I have outlined above is the general attire of the deities in the primordial Heavenly Realm prior to the divine retirement of Kunitokotachi-no-Mikoto.

§ 3
However, as stars revolved and moons shifted through immense cosmic epochs, the garments of the Heavenly Realm gradually underwent transformations. In modern times, numerous divine officials have appeared who wear sacred robes closely resembling the formal ceremonial dress worn by humans in the physical world; furthermore, among the myriad Konjin and Tengu realms occupying the lowest tier of divine emissaries, spirits have come to conduct their activities attired in all manner of fashionable contemporary garments popular in human society today.

しかしながら、星が移り月が改まるように悠久の歳月が経過するにつれて、神界における神々の御装束も次第に時代とともに変化してまいりました。今日では、我々現界（地上人間界）の人々が冠婚葬祭などの儀式で着用する礼服・礼装に酷似した神服を身にまとう神司（神官）も数多く現れるようになり、さらに神々の使いの最末端に属する八百万（やおよろず）の金神界（こんじんかい）や天狗界（てんぐかい）の諸霊におきましては、驚くべきことに現代の人間社会で流行している多種多様な最新の服装を着用して活動するようになっております。

Moreover, entities of the realm of evil spirits likewise disguise themselves in magnificent garments identical to those of the great righteous deities according to their respective ranks, deliberately impersonating divine beings; hence, even when observing with clairvoyant spiritual sight (reigan), one may at first glance easily become confounded as to whether a spirit is righteous or wicked.

§ 5
The crucial distinction lies in this: the supreme deities of perfect goodness are enveloped in a spiritual aura—their “spiritual robe” (reii)—which is extraordinarily thick, radiating an intense, dazzling luster that almost pierces the eyes; whereas evil spirits possess spiritual robes that are exceedingly thin, murky, and devoid of true luminescence, by which contrast one can ultimately discern good from evil. Nonetheless, formidable ringleaders of darkness such as Hachi&omacron;-no-&Omacron;kami (Tokoyohiko) and Tokoyohime possess spiritual robes that are relatively thick and emanate considerable supernatural brilliance akin to genuine righteous gods, making it exceedingly difficult at first glance to pierce their disguise.

Section 2

Spirits of the Living & Dead, Astral Underworld & Rejuvenation by Chinkon

幽界の精霊と現界人の霊衣――生死の徴と鎮魂・言霊による蘇生

Spiritual inspection of the Netherworld; comparing the circular robes of the living with the triangular robes of the dead; life-and-death prognosis and healing through Chinkon and the Heavenly Prayer.

幽界に籍を置く現界人の実態、生者（円形）と死者（三角形・足なし）の霊衣の相違、および鎮魂秘法と天津祝詞による奇跡の蘇生神理。
§ 6
Furthermore, when I explored the spiritual realm and inspected the Astral Underworld (Y&umacron;kai), I witnessed countless spirits clothed in garments of diverse hues. These colors are strictly differentiated according to the gravity of sins and spiritual debts accumulated by each soul. However, the inhabitants of the Astral Realm do not consist solely of deceased souls. Even among living human beings actively laboring in the physical world today, there are those whose spiritual registry (reiseki) is already anchored in the dark Underworld. Such individuals, even while walking the Earth, suffer day and night in miserable destitution and distress as the agonizing torments of the Netherworld reverberate directly into their physical bodies and minds. The sole means of escaping these horrific afflictions is, while one still possesses a mortal physical vessel, to maintain earnest faith in the Divine, perform virtuous deeds, aid one's fellow beings, render every possible social service, receive the supreme grace of God, and wash clean all sins and spiritual defilements.
現代日本語訳
また私（出口王仁三郎）が霊界を探険し、冥府・幽界を視察・遍歴いたしました折にも、実に多種多様な色彩の衣服を身に着けている無数の精霊（霊魂）を目撃いたしました。これらの精霊の衣服の色は、各人が生前に犯した罪穢れ（つみけがれ）の軽重や霊的段階の違いによって、厳然と色彩が分かれているのであります。しかしながら、幽界に存在しているのは決して死者（亡者）の霊魂ばかりではありません。現在この現界（物質世界）において肉体を持って活動し働いている生きた人間の精霊であっても、すでにその霊籍（魂の籍）が幽界の暗黒界に置かれてしまっている者が少なからず存在するのであります。そうした人々は、現界で日常生活を送っていても幽界における霊的苦痛が肉体や精神に直接反映・感応してくるため、日夜にわたり病気や災難などの悲惨極まりない苦悩の生活を強いられることになるのであります。これらの絶望的な苦痛を免れるための唯一の方法は、現界において生きた肉体が存在している間に、至誠をもって神を深く信仰し、善行を積み重ねて万民を慈しみ助け、できうる限りの社会的奉仕に身を捧げて神の大いなる御恵み・御加護をいただき、自らの魂に染みついた罪穢れを洗い清めておかねばならないのであります。
原文（近代文語）
また自分が幽界を探険した時にも、種々の色の服を着けてゐる精霊を目撃した。これは罪の軽重によつて、色が別れてゐるのである。しかし幽界にも亡者ばかりの霊魂がをるのではない。現界に立働いてゐる生きた人間の精霊も、やはり幽界に霊籍をおいてをるものがある。これらの人間は現界においても、幽界の苦痛が影響して、日夜悲惨な生活を続けてをるものである。これらの苦痛を免るる方法は、現体のある間に神を信仰し、善事を行ひ万民を助け、能ふかぎりの社会的奉仕を務めて、神の御恵を受け、その罪を洗ひ清めておかねばならぬ。
§ 7
Now, when one examines through spiritual vision the spirits (astral bodies) of living human beings on Earth, they possess astral vessels of identical form to mortal humans; yet in comparison with deceased spirits, one can at a single glance clearly and distinctly tell the living from the dead. The astral body of a living person is enveloped completely in a harmonious, spherical, circular spiritual robe (reii/aura), whereas the astral body of a deceased spirit is pointed like a mountain peak at the head and wrapped in a sharp, triangular spiritual robe. Furthermore, this deceased robe is worn only from the waist up, with no spiritual vesture existing below the waist—the common popular saying that “ghosts have no legs” is rooted precisely in this spiritual reality. In addition, the spirits of deeply virtuous and noble individuals possess spiritual robes that are exceedingly thick, expansive, and brilliantly radiant, casting a piercing luminescence that commands natural respect and endows them with the profound capacity to lead and govern others. In modern society, individuals blessed with such magnificent spiritual robes are so few that true great leaders can scarcely emerge. Modern humans have steadily allowed their spiritual robes to wither thin, devoid of radiating luster, resembling the ragged tatters worn by low-tier fiends of the evil realm, devoid of all spiritual authority and riddled with holes. When observing critically ill patients, their spiritual robes become thinned to the utmost extremity, and I have witnessed countless cases where the robe around the head was already tapering into a triangular mountain-shape. Whenever I visit the bedside of severe patients, I judge their fate based upon the thickness and circularity versus angularity of their spiritual robes, and my prognosis has proven unerringly accurate in one hundred cases out of one hundred. Even if renowned physicians have abandoned all hope and given up on a patient, if I observe their spiritual robe and find it still thick and illuminated by divine light, that patient will without fail make a complete recovery. Conversely, even if celebrated doctors and medical professors declare that a patient's life is out of danger, if their spiritual robe has begun to assume a triangular shape and has become thin as paper, that patient will inevitably perish.
