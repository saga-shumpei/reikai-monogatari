Appendix: Travelogue Poems of the First Pilgrimage to Mount Takakuma

附録　第一回高熊山参拝紀行歌
Onii, Deguchi Onisaburo（王仁　出口王仁三郎）
Translator's Introduction & Literary Note:
On December 3, 1921 (Taisho 10), shortly after completing the dictation of the first volumes of Reikai Monogatari, Master Deguchi Onisaburo led a grand collective pilgrimage of two hundred believers to Mount Takakuma (near Kameoka, Kyoto Prefecture). Mount Takakuma is the holy mountain where Onisaburo underwent his historic spiritual ascetic retreat in February 1898 (Meiji 31), during which the mysteries of the Three Worlds (Divine, Astral, and Physical) were first revealed to him.

To commemorate this ascent, Onisaburo composed this extraordinary kikōka (travelogue poem) in classical 7-5 meter. Woven into the verse is an intricate orikomi-uta (acrostic name-incorporation poem), embedding the personal names of all 199 pilgrims (including Aikido founder Morihei Ueshiba) and a concluding postscript party of 22 believers into the literal narrative of mountain scenery, pine trees, rock caves, and spiritual revelation. In the English translation below, the travel narrative is rendered in rhythmic blank verse, with each pilgrim's embedded name noted in brackets.
Section 1 • Verses 1–14

Rending asunder the massive, boulder-bound crags of a thousand folds, The valiant sons of Yamato stand firm as stouthearted heroes [Ishiba Kaoru]; And noble women of radiant grace and fragrant virtue Strive to forge a sturdy pillar of salvation across all nations of the world [Morikuni Mizo]. From east and west, north and south, The diligent people of the Land of the Rising Sun Summon devout men and faithful women together [Nishide Zenryū], Assembling in joy at the sacred Dragon Palace Citadel. Rejoicing in this sheltered land of peace as an eternal, blessed abode, They proclaim one after another the celestial revelations of God [Hozumi Keijirō]. The sacred word-spirits (Kototama) they sound abroad Resemble a heavenly dragon soaring into the clear, cloudless expanse, Exalting the supreme majesty of our homeland, the Land of the Sun [Fujimoto Jūzaburō]. Counting one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, and ten— Through all the three and ten passing years, A pure, radiant light shall now sweep across the fields, Parting the lingering clustered clouds in all four quarters [Kon Kiyo]. Encompassing the six realms of heaven and earth, our divine land Beholds the vast, peaceful ocean of the sacred Manai waters [Tamura Kentarō], Where the crossings lie calm, and the eternal peace of Urayasu Brings ninefold splendor to the sovereign imperial realm [Doi Yasuto]. From the jeweled imperial capital and thriving regional towns To the most secluded recesses of the mountain ranges [Koyama Sadayuki], Loyalty, courage, benevolence, righteousness, and unswerving filial piety Shine forth brightly, and the true Divine Way governs all in tranquility.

Section 2 • Verses 15–30

In the thirty-first year of Meiji, during the second moon, On the ninth day when the fragrance of plum blossoms filled the air, Setting the western moon behind, Master Deguchi entered deep Into the sacred mountain heights of Takakuma [Takai Jūzaburō]. Praying fervently for the ten-thousand-year longevity of the Emperor, Upon the bed of three-leaved azalea branches, He sat in erect, pure meditation with an immaculate heart, Listening intently to the divine teachings of Kototamahiko [Uemura Kiyohiko]. Beneath the pine trees, he heeded the heavenly voice, Unmindful of the piercing winds that swept the slope [Uemura Matsu]. At peace before the sacred mountain cavern, The clustered clouds that had hovered in his soul [Uemura Kan] Vanished along with the fading morning mists, And his inner spirit was reborn into pristine renewal [Nakamura Shinkichi]. Basking thus in the supreme grace of the Almighty, What an auspicious day of grace this was! "Even though I must expose my corpse upon the wilderness wastes, If it be for the Great Deity Kunitokotachi [Yoshihara Tsunezaburō] And the teachings of the Three Sacred Souls, Why should I hesitate to tread the frost-chilled pampas plains? [Suzuki Nobukichi]" Though extending his footsteps into the remote mountain heights, How delightful and crisp was the pure spring water issuing from the rocks! [Mito Tomiharu] A land that needs no barred doors, flourishing in joyous peace, Prospering under the benevolent reign of our Sovereign. The waters of the Isuzu stream flow pure and crystalline, At every river mouth, large and small [Ōkawa-guchi Toku]; The waters overflow, while the mud-stained shogunate rule Of the fallen Tokugawa clan came to its ruin, And feudal lords great and small, down to the village headmen [Shōji Kitsu], Collapsed in weeping and scattered to the four winds— A pitiful spectacle indeed of worldly transience.

Section 3 • Verses 31–49

Such ancient examples bear witness: at the pristine origin, Ushitora no Konjin, the Golden Deity of the Northeast [Arihara Ushitarō], Manifested in this world to expound the primordial relationship Between the Creator Kami and human souls in the bygone age [Maeda Michika]. "The white plum blossoms that bloom across the three thousand worlds All at once in fragrant spring!" At the foot of the sacred mountain, He offered up most auspicious divine oracles before heaven and earth [Yamane Kikutatō], Vowing valiantly to accomplish the reconstruction of the three thousand worlds. How heroic was that sacred pledge! At the foothills of Shibi and Hongū [Yamane Chikahe], The divine loom weaves the cosmic warp and woof with brocade threads [Yamane Kinuedako], Blessed by the divine grace of Heaven! The white crane Calls with a ringing voice from the secluded marsh [Arita Kyūkō], While the green-haired tortoise climbs upon the bank, Embodying longevity spanning ten thousand years [Takagishi Toshie]. Ebisu and Daikoku, the deities of abundant fortune, Dance in joyous play above the pure sacred well of Manai [Inoue Aya]. In this wondrous, holy garden of God, without divide between land and sea, The gods of mountain, plain, river, and ocean Reveal the bright path of mutual assistance [Toyama Sukeaki]. Along the roadside turf, planted and cultivated far and wide, The blossoms thrive in glorious, towering splendor [Ueshiba Moritaka]! In the fragrant evergreen groves of the Divine Sanctuary, Embracing good and evil alike within supreme benevolence [Mori Yoshihito], God's compassionate grace remains forever changeless. Beneath the royal bamboo gardens of our imperial land, The divine glory shines resplendent across the high celestial plain of Ayabe, Where the Northeastern Golden Deity [Higashio Yoshio] Is reverently enshrined with heroic majesty, And the teachings flow forth like broad rivers through this land of virtue [Hirose Yoshikuni]. The rising sun ascends high like a towering bridge [Takahashi Tsuneyoshi], Its boundless vigor enduring eternally through all ages— How truly auspicious is this unfolding age of dawn, Centered upon the noble, celebrated Pavilion of Auspicious Omens (Zuishōkaku)!

Firming the foundations of the sacred dwelling, Both in distant provinces and neighboring hamlets [Tsumura Tōtarō], They quell the raging spirits of distortion (Magatsu) [Shimada Ei]. Across the broad expanses of the Dragon-fly Islands of Yamato, Where thousands of rich ears of golden grain ripen in abundance, In the midst of the villages nestled under fertile rice ears [Kimura Ken'ichirō], They teach that polishing the soul is of foremost importance [Fujii Takehiro]. How vigorous is the flourishing wellspring of spiritual truth! As they propagate the sincere faith far and near, Clearing away the clustered clouds gathered across the northern skies [Kitamura Takamitsu], The glorious light of the sun ascends in majesty! Upon Mount Hongū and beside the jeweled spring of Tamanoi [Miyai Itsuko], How enviable are the souls who bathe in such radiant divine virtue [Urayama Sen'ichi]! Devoting oneself entirely to spiritual ascetic training, Penetrating deep into the mountain wilderness, The sacred cave of Takakuma where enlightenment was attained Now thrives in bustling devotion [Yamasa Iwa]. Using the rising and setting sun as his canopy, Reciting sacred norito and chanting divine poetry [Hikasa Ginzō], The pilgrim advances under the protection of the Three Sacred Souls.
