The Cat's Eye Jewel

第四九章　猫の眼の玉〔九九〕
§ 1
Spurred by Tokoyohime's ferocious battle-cry, regions throughout the world were plunged into vortexes of warfare: demonic clouds blackened the heavens, murky torrents inundated the earth, and the roars of wild beasts and monstrous serpents locked in deadly combat resounded with ever-increasing fury.
現代日本語訳

原文（近代文語）
常世姫の雄猛びにより、世界の各地はほとんど戦乱の巷と化し、天に妖雲みなぎり、地に濁流あふれ、猛獣悪蛇の咆吼する声、呑噬の争ひはますます烈しくなつてきた。
§ 2
Neither Banko-Daijin nor his supreme general Hachiō-Daijin could devise any effective strategy to stem the catastrophe. Watching this tragedy from above, Daijizaiten could not bear the spectacle and searched desperately for a means of pacification. Unable to restrain his own consort Tokoyohime, Hachiō-Daijin had initially dismissed the disturbance as a localized flare-up; yet unexpectedly the fire had spread until it threatened to reduce the entire cosmos to cinders. Confounded by this turn of events, Hachiō-Daijin repented of his past arrogance, realizing that there was no other path to save the universe than to restore peace through righteous virtue.

§ 3
While gazing up at the northeastern sky from Tokoyo Castle, Hachiō-Daijin beheld a brilliant beam of celestial light shooting into the heavens. Straining his eyes, he discerned within the radiance the forms of numerous goddesses of peace playing melodious harps and flutes, dancing gracefully with fans bearing the crimson sun disk (Hinomaru). Filled with wonder, he reported this apparition to Banko-Daijin; then, mounting clouds and winds, he hastened in pursuit of the radiant light, which radiated directly from Earthly Takamanohara.

Takaowake made courteous bows and pleaded that the divine stratagems for restoring universal peace be revealed unto him. § 15
Leaving Kamikuniwake-no-Mikoto and his officers to rest at Tokoyo Castle, Takaowake proceeded directly to the mansion of Banko-Daijin, arguing that the only strategy for achieving universal peace lay in submitting to the Law of Kuniharutachi-no-Mikoto. Banko-Daijin offered not a word of reply, listening to Takaowake's proposals with a faint, enigmatic smile. Frustrated by this silence, Takaowake reasoned bitterly: 'To serve such an indecisive sovereign in times of global crisis was my gravest error! Better to submit wholeheartedly to Kuniharutachi-no-Mikoto!' Expressing this resolution to Matsuyamawake and Kojikahiko, divine retainers of Daijizaiten, he asserted that Banko-Daijin was suited only for times of tranquil peace and lacked the mettle to master global turbulence.
