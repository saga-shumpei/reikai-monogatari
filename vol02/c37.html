Tragedy atop Mount Chōkō

第三七章　長高山の悲劇〔八七〕
§ 1
At the fortress of Mount Chōkō, Kiyoteruhiko, Lady Sueyohime, and Mototeruhiko were waiting together for the return of the envoys dispatched to Mount Kohaku. Just then, the first and second messengers skimmed through the vault of heaven and returned together.
現代日本語訳
長高山の城塞においては、清照彦、末世姫、そして元照彦が肩を並べ、高白山へと派遣した使者たちの帰還を今か今かと待ちわびておりました。そこへ、第一と第二の使者が大空をかすめ飛ぶようにして、揃って同時に帰城してまいりました。
原文（近代文語）
長高山の城塞には清照彦、末世姫、元照彦とともに、高白山に遣はしたる使者の帰還を待つてゐた。そこへ第一、第二の使者は天空をかすめて一度に帰つてきた。
§ 2
Anxiously asking how matters stood, Kiyoteruhiko immediately ushered the envoys into his living chamber. The messengers reported that the rebellious intent of Arakumahiko and his wife had grown even firmer, that Lady Tokoyo's pressure was intense, that Komayamahiko would not easily submit, and that their resolve to continue resisting Kototamawake-no-Mikoto was unyielding.

原文（近代文語）
様子いかにと待ちかまへたる清照彦は、ただちに使者を居間に通した。使者は荒熊彦夫妻の反逆心ますます強く、かつ常世姫の圧迫はげしく、駒山彦は容易に従はず、やむを得ず、言霊別命に反抗を継続するの決心確なりと報告した。

For a time Kiyoteruhiko hung his head in silence, sighing heavily and lost in profound thought. Over the face of Lady Sueyohime, too, drifted a dark cloud of sorrow.

Before his words had even finished echoing, he shook free of her grasp on his sleeve and turned to commence his departure for battle. Lady Sueyohime instantly slipped into an adjoining chamber, gripped her dagger in reverse, severed her own throat, and collapsed upon the floor. Startled by the ominous noise, Kiyoteruhiko rushed into the chamber, and beheld to his horror Lady Sueyohime bathed in crimson blood, writhing in agonizing torment.

§ 18
Such was the solemn command. However, since Kototamawake-no-Mikoto was a deity of boundless mercy and supreme benevolence, he had no inward desire whatsoever to make Kiyoteruhiko slaughter his own parents; rather, it was his profound divine design to lead the parents to repentance through Kiyoteruhiko and save them through the hands of their most beloved child. Unaware of this profound divine will, Kiyoteruhiko prioritized universal righteousness, and with tears streaming down his face, resolved to attack his divine parents. In truth, Kiyoteruhiko's inward anguish was more agonizing than swallowing molten iron. Nevertheless, the divine command could not be refused, and he reverently answered that he would obey.
