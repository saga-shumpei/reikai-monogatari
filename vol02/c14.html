The Essence of Mercury

第一四章　水星の精〔六四〕
§ 1
Here, Tayorihiko and Nakasakihiko sought to construct a magnificent garden to comfort Wakazakurahime-no-Mikoto; leading hosts of deities unto the upper reaches of the Jordan River, they quarried colossal boulders to build a vast stone garden. Suddenly, Wakazakurahime-no-Mikoto was seized by violent bodily convulsions and wracked by excruciating abdominal agony. Struck with alarm, the divine ministers assembled, offering prayers unto Heaven and presenting rare medicines, yet all their efforts proved entirely in vain. Thereupon Kojimawake stepped before Kototamawake-no-Mikoto, entreating him to divine the spiritual cause of their sovereign's critical affliction and obtain celestial revelations. Astounded, Kototamawake-no-Mikoto immediately offered holy prayers to seek divine guidance. According to the oracle of the Heavenly Deities:

§ 2
'In the upper reaches of the Jordan River resteth an eboshi-shaped sacred stone of brownish hue and oblong form, born from the essence of Mercury. The divine ministers excavated and brought it hither, placing it upon defiled ground and piling countless boulders atop it. Unable to endure such agony, the spirit of Mercury inflicted illness upon Wakazakurahime-no-Mikoto to warn the ministers of their transgression. If thou swiftly remove the heavy boulders, purify the sacred stone with golden spring water, and erect a sacred shrine to venerate it, her affliction shall instantly be healed. It was Nakasakihiko who unearthed it, and he likewise who defiled it. Though Tayorihiko and the other ministers also deserve divine retribution, the burden of responsibility hath fallen upon their sovereign Wakazakurahime-no-Mikoto. Therefore let the ministers repent before the God of Mercury and revere the stone with deepest reverence.'

原文（近代文語）

§ 4
Hearing this divine oracle, Kojimawake was filled with awe and executed the commands faithfully. Miraculously, the moment the sacred stone was purified and enshrined within the sanctuary, Wakazakurahime-no-Mikoto's excruciating agony vanished completely, as though wiped away.
現代日本語訳
小島別はこの天津神の尊き神託を聞いて大いに恐れ慎み、その神命の通りに迅速に取り計らった。実に不思議なことには、霊石を黄金水で洗い清めて恭しく神殿に鎮座して奉斎すると同時に、稚桜姫命の激しい病苦はまるで拭い去るかのように跡形もなく全快平癒されたのである。
原文（近代文語）
小島別はこれを聞きて大いに恐れ慎みてその命のごとく取計らつた。不思議なるかな稚桜姫命の病苦は、霊石を洗ひ清めて恭しく神殿に祭るとともに拭ふがごとく癒えたのである。
§ 5
Because this eboshi-shaped sacred stone born of Mercury's essence had originally resided upstream, the Jordan River had flowed broad, deep, and tranquil with crystal-clear waters like pure glass; yet ever since the stone was excavated, landslides poured from the mountains, choking the riverbed and transforming it into a muddy torrent. Nakasakihiko lost his mind and threw himself into the river; his soul turned into a venomous serpent, drifting into the Dead Sea where it mutated into a ferocious demon. As for the Shrine of Water erected for Mercury's essence, Kototamawake-no-Mikoto was appointed chief priest, ministering day and night in solemn devotion.
現代日本語訳

§ 6
Though Wakazakurahime-no-Mikoto had temporarily recovered after the enshrinement of the sacred stone, her health remained fragile, and she often relapsed into bed. Thereupon Tokoyohime penned a secret message, bound it to the leg of a golden eagle, and dispatched it unto Mamichitomo-no-Hiko, the eldest son of Wakazakurahime-no-Mikoto. Reading the message, his countenance changed in blazing fury; rushing into Ryūgū Castle, he assembled Kamikuniwake-no-Mikoto, Hanamorihiko, Matetsuhiko, Kojimawake, and other ministers in urgent council. Their conspiratorial finding was presented unto Wakazakurahime-no-Mikoto, who flew into intense indignation, confronting Kototamawake-no-Mikoto:

'I hear that under the guise of venerating the stone of Mercury, thou art using its mystical power to torment and curse me, plotting to usurp my throne! Thy rebellious heart is beyond all excuse! § 9
Hopelessly outnumbered, Kototamawake-no-Mikoto swallowed his bitter tears, appealed unto Heaven, apologized unto the sacred stone, and reverently raised it above his head before casting it down upon the emerald lawn. To one as devout as Kototamawake, it was like being forced to swallow molten iron. Instantly a violent whirlwind erupted from the stone, striking Wakazakurahime-no-Mikoto as she stood upon her high tower; swept from the terrace, she crashed unto the ground, fracturing her hip and writhing in agony. The ministers rushed in panic to carry her into the inner sanctum, tending her with desperate care. Though she eventually recovered sufficiently to attend to governance, her injury left her permanently lame, suffering severe pain with every step.

§ 10
Kototamawake-no-Mikoto cut a bough from a double-flowering plum tree in the courtyard, fashioning a walking staff and presenting it unto Wakazakurahime-no-Mikoto. This was the ancient origin of the aged walking with a staff. Revering the terrifying majesty of the divine, Kototamawake quarried colossal stone monoliths to construct a magnificent stone sanctuary, enshrining the sacred stone of Mercury forever as a revered subordinate deity of Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto.
