Preface

序（じよ）
English Translation • §1

In this work is chronicled the narrative of how Onisaburo, having been commanded by the Divine Realm to undergo one week of rigorous, severe ascetic discipline from the ninth unto the fifteenth day of the second lunar month in the thirty-first year of Meiji (1898), and upon returning home was further commanded to undergo one week of bed-binding discipline, during which his soul journeyed freely through the Spirit Realm and was granted firsthand vision of diverse conditions across both the Astral Realm and the Divine Realm. Within the Spirit Realm, time and space are entirely transcended; there is no distinction between far and near, great and small, light and darkness, and the occurrences of the spiritual realm throughout all antiquity and across all four directions reflect upon the spiritual eye as if upon a single plane. Therefore, seeking out the proper thread of guidance, I have dictated this account with the chief aim of making it as readily comprehensible to readers as possible.

English Translation • §2

Worldly scholars and individuals unversed in the realities of the Spirit Realm will doubtless read my Reikai Monogatari and laugh it away as a children's fairy tale. Some may mock it as a comical tale in the vein of Don Quixote. Others may dismiss it without a backward glance as a mere fantasy of dreams. Still others may view it merely as an allegorical moral fable. Yet I care not how it is criticized. The essential point is that people take it into their hands to read it even once, gain a glimpse into a portion of the Spirit World, and come to understand in some small measure the activities of the Deities; if they do so, then the true purpose of my dictation shall be fully achieved.

English Translation • §3

That which this volume recounts is, in general, the sacred battle of the assault upon Mount Zion, narrating the circumstances wherein the Ancestral Great Deity of the Land (Kunitokotachi-no-Mikoto) instituted the Law of Heaven and Earth, and wherein Wakahimegimi-no-Mikoto, having committed the transgression of violating celestial statutes, was the first to retire into the Astral Realm. Those who cannot place faith in this book may read it simply as a fairy tale or as a clumsy novel. If through reading this there are those who can achieve even the slightest measure of spiritual deconstruction and renewal (Tatekae Tatenaoshi), for Onisaburo it shall be a blessing far exceeding all expectations.

English Translation • §4

The divine revelation declaring, “The plum blossoms that bloom across the three thousand worlds all at once; the age of Ushitora no Konjin has arrived! Sitting upon Mount Sumeru, the Golden Deity of the Northeast guards and protects!” was the grand lion's roar for universal salvation delivered by the Golden Deity of Ushitora upon His first divine possession of the Foundress (Nao Deguchi), who may truly be called the soul that established the Divine Age. Ah, what a magnificent and sublime divine proclamation! Does not the phrase “blooming across the three thousand worlds all at once” represent a supreme, merciful utterance bestowing vibrant life upon all myriad things in the cosmos, and granting unto every living creature across the world the divine key to ultimate peace of mind and enlightened life (Anshin Ritsumei)?

English Translation • §5

Each time this dictating servant reads this divine word, I am overwhelmed by the boundless and absolute, beginningless and endless Great Primal God's immense Divine Plan and the grandeur of His sublime aspiration; it feels as if the moon of Suchness (Shinnyo) shines brilliantly upon the ocean surface of the human heart, and the sun of compassion casts its fair, impartial illumination across the universe, dissipating all darkness throughout the myriad realms.

English Translation • §6

Furthermore, in expressing the cosmic plan vertically and with absolute completeness through the words “blooming across the three thousand worlds all at once,” and then condensing it at the close of the verse into “plum blossoms”—just as opening a white folding fan stirs a cool breeze, unified at its base by the tiny pivot (kaname) of the plum blossom—this revelation explains the true reality of the activation of divine authority, being supremely vast without exterior and supremely minute without interior; such an accomplishment is utterly beyond the reach of worldly intellectuals or scholars.

English Translation • §7

Moreover, following this comes the revelation: “Sitting upon Mount Sumeru, the Golden Deity of Ushitora guards and protects!” Ah, what a magnificent expression of the supreme divine stature! What a masterwork of divine literature! It is entirely beyond the contrivance of petty human intellect. Furthermore, as the fruit of the Great God's spiritual descent, this revelation gives warning with a heart of boundless divine compassion toward all three thousand worlds—the Divine Realm, the Astral Realm, and the Manifest Realm—not only unto heavenly and earthly deities, but unto all Buddhas and all humankind, manifesting future events with exhaustive, tender care, truly standing without precedent or parallel throughout all of history.

English Translation • §8

That such a sacred divine revelation of the Great God should be difficult for ordinary mortals to grasp is indeed a natural truth; consequently, misunderstandings were exceedingly prone to arise. This dictating servant constantly lamented this reality, yearning with all his being to offer even a partial interpretation of the Sacred Oracles (Ofudesaki/Shin'yu) and to clearly demonstrate where the true intent of the Great Deity's Will lay; for nearly twenty-three long years I wrestled with this anguish in my heart. Yet because the Divine Realm did not permit its public disclosure, down to this very day I had never explained so much as a single word or half-phrase concerning the inner meaning of the texts of the Sacred Oracles.

English Translation • §9

However, on the eighth day of the ninth lunar month of this year (1921), the divine decree suddenly descended upon this servant's soul, bringing the divine command: “Now at last, proclaim unto the world the tidings of the Spirit Realm that God revealed unto you in the second month of Meiji thirty-one!” Hearing this sacred instruction, I resolved to publish the narrative of the Spirit World stored within my breast for twenty-four years. Yet since this past spring, this servant had suffered from an affliction of the eyes and severe pain in the brain, being deprived of the freedom to write; whenever I attempted by sheer force of will to take up the brush, excruciating pain instantly seized my eyes and head, rendering it impossible to proceed, causing me immense distress of heart and mind over how to manage this task. Ten days after that divine revelation descended, on the morning of the eighteenth day, another divine command was granted, saying: “You need not write with your own hand; for God shall borrow your mouth to dictate these words. Summon the four individuals—Toyoji Toyama, Akiko Katō, Shigeo Sakurai, and Masaharu Taniguchi—and have them transcribe the divine words that issue from your mouth!”

English Translation • §10

Thereupon I finally made my firm resolve: retiring into seclusion at the Shōunkaku villa in Namimatsu to serve as a medium (reibai-sha), I began to transmit the divine revelations through oral dictation. The tidings of the Spirit World kept secret in my heart for twenty-four years now opened like the seasonal spring breeze, like the plum blossoms blooming across the three thousand worlds in the evergreen pine age of God's unfolding plan; a wondrous narrative that leaves the mouth agape in awe, wondering whether it be dream, reality, phantom, or divine omen. Below flow the serene clear waters of the Shirase River, the Otonase and Yura Rivers, the Wachi River, and the Kanbayashi River, with the Ogumo River in their midst, where rows of ancient pine trees cast their reflections along the riverbanks; with pure waters and pure winds at the foot of Mount Hongū in Namimatsu, within the newly built study of Shōunkaku, five souls joined together—one speaking from the mouth, four holding the brush, all five brimming with valiant vitality—to dictate and publish the divine revelation precisely as granted.
