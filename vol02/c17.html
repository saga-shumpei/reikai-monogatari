Sagahime's Righteous Death

第一七章　佐賀姫の義死〔六七〕
§ 1
In truth, Kototamawake-no-Mikoto did not harbor the slightest intention of rebelling against Waka-sakurahime-no-Mikoto. Rather, for the noble purpose of rectifying corruption and restoring heavenly order to the divine realm, he deliberately drafted a defiant epistle and delivered it into the hands of the envoy Kojimawake.
現代日本語訳
言霊別命には、もとより稚桜姫命に対して叛逆し敵対しようとする悪心など毛頭ありませんでした。しかしながら、乱れた神界を正しき道へと復興させる（反正撥乱）という至大なる目的のために、あえて反抗的な文面の信書をしたためて、使者である小島別に手渡されたのでありました。
原文（近代文語）
言霊別命は素より稚桜姫命に反抗するの心は毛頭なかつたのである。されど命以下の神司にたいし、反正撥乱の目的をもつて故意に反抗的信書を認め、使臣小島別に渡した。

'How could I begrudge this fleeting thread of life itself? / If laying it down will illuminate the true path of divine sincerity.'
現代日本語訳
『玉の緒と喩えられる儚きこの命であろうと、どうして惜しむことがあろうか。この一身を投げ打って神の誠の道を明るく照らし出すことができるのならば』
原文（近代文語）

§ 3
As Waka-sakurahime-no-Mikoto had already been enveloped in clouds of doubt regarding the intentions and deeds of Kototamawake-no-Mikoto, she took the blistering epistle at face value, concluding that he had risen in genuine rebellion. She immediately summoned the celestial angel Ōyashimahiko-no-Mikoto and commanded him to lead an expedition to subdue Kototamawake-no-Mikoto. Discerning the deeper reality, Ōyashimahiko-no-Mikoto pleaded illness and refused to appear, firmly barring his doors, severing all intercourse with the shrine dignitaries, and patiently awaiting the providential hour when truth would be severed from falsehood.
現代日本語訳
稚桜姫命は、言霊別命の真意や日頃の行動に対して、かねてより半信半疑の疑念の暗雲に包まれておられたところでしたので、その強硬な信書を一目見るや、『やはり本心から叛旗を翻したのだ』と断定されました。そして天使・大八洲彦命を召し出し、言霊別命を武力討伐せよとの厳命を下されたのであります。しかしこの時、事の真相を見抜いていた大八洲彦命は、病気（疚）と称して御前への出仕を固辞し、居宅の戸を固く閉ざして引き籠もり、諸々の神司たちとの往来を完全に絶って、正邪善悪の黒白が自ずと判明する時機をじっと待たれたのでありました。

§ 4
Waka-sakurahime-no-Mikoto was thus compelled to confer the supreme command of the punitive expedition upon Kojimawake. Kojimawake instantly received the imperial commission, appointing Tayorihiko and Yasukawahiko as vanguard generals; mustering a vast celestial army, he threw his entire strength into crushing the ostensible rebels. As a result, Kototamawake-no-Mikoto was plunged into an excruciating predicament, attacked on one flank from all sides by Kojimawake's divine legions, and assailed on the other by the demonic hordes of Miyasahiko and Kuniteruhime serving Tokoyohime.

§ 5
Kototamawake-no-Mikoto established his primary base in the metropolis of Rome, appointing Hanazonohiko and Oshimahiko to command the city's defense forces, while dispatching Masa-teruhiko and Mizogawahiko to oversee the northern bastion of Moscow. Disguising their appearances, Kototamawake-no-Mikoto and his brother Moto-teruhiko personally scouted the movements of both Kojimawake's forces and Tokoyohime's demonic hosts, devising elusive, divine stratagems. Presently, Kototamawake-no-Mikoto arrived at the camp of Sagahiko, who was garrisoned upon Mount Bombay, using every persuasive argument to urge him to unite in this righteous struggle.

§ 6
Sagahiko was an official whose very life had once been rescued from mortal danger through the benevolence of Kototamawake-no-Mikoto. § 8
This severe order had been circulated to all shrine officials far and wide, including Sagahiko. Sagahiko was trembling in fearful apprehension. What was more, Tayorihiko had already arrived in secret, delivering the imperial injunction and arranging an ambush so that should Kototamawake-no-Mikoto ever set foot there, concealed troops would instantly spring forth and slaughter him.

§ 9
Unsuspecting of such treacherous machinations, Kototamawake-no-Mikoto followed Sagahiko's courteous invitation into the inner chambers to rest and outline defensive countermeasures. In truth, Sagahiko's loyalty had utterly defected from Kototamawake-no-Mikoto, having pledged secret allegiance to Tayorihiko. Kototamawake-no-Mikoto's life now hung like a flickering candle in the gale. At that critical instant, Sagahiko's noble wife, Sagahime, observing Kototamawake-no-Mikoto taking a solitary stroll through the garden, purposefully crossed his path and intentionally dropped a folded slip of paper from her bosom before swiftly vanishing into the halls.
現代日本語訳
言霊別命はそのような暗殺の罠が張り巡らされているとは夢にも知らず、佐賀彦の勧めるままに奥殿へと通されて休息を取られ、今後の迎撃作戦について指示を出されました。しかし佐賀彦の心はすでに恩義ある言霊別命から離反し、敵将・田依彦と密かに内通（款を通ず）していたのであります。言霊別命の尊い命は、まさに風前の灯火の危機に晒されておりました。その時、佐賀彦の貞淑な妻である佐賀姫は、言霊別命が庭園に出て静かに散策しておられる隙を見澄まし、命の御前を急ぎ足で横切りながら、胸元から一枚の紙片をわざと足元に落とし、素早く館の奥へと姿を消しました。

§ 10
Perceiving something amiss, Kototamawake-no-Mikoto quickly retrieved the slip and unfolded it; therein was written: 'By the treacherous conspiracy of Tayorihiko and Sagahiko, thy precious life is in imminent danger this very instant. Flee through the rear gate without a moment's hesitation!' Just as Kototamawake-no-Mikoto stood contemplating this alarming warning, a chilling, agonizing shriek rang out from the inner sanctuary. It was Sagahime taking her own life. Caught in an agonizing dilemma where saving her divine benefactor would betray her husband, yet abetting her husband would destroy all righteousness, she sacrificed her own life to uphold unwavering honor. The encampment on Mount Bombay erupted into tumultuous chaos; Sagahiko, deranged by the shocking death of his wife, screamed madly as he summoned his warriors. His frantic cries pierced clearly through the air to Kototamawake-no-Mikoto's ears.
現代日本語訳

§ 11
Every commander encamped upon Mount Pompeii rushed toward Sagahiko's agonized cries. In his frenzy over his wife's sudden suicide, Sagahiko completely forgot the presence of Kototamawake-no-Mikoto. Disguising his garments amid the chaos, Kototamawake calmly walked out through the main gate, joined Mototeruhiko who stood waiting outside, and fled swiftly toward the fortress of Moscow.

The stronghold of Moscow was securely guarded by Masateruhiko and Mizokawahiko. At that hour, Kusakahime, elder sister of Tayorihiko, disguised herself and infiltrated Moscow, claiming to be an urgent messenger from Kototamawake-no-Mikoto: 'Kototamawake hath been trapped on Mount Pompeii by Sagahiko and Tayorihiko, and our army faceth total destruction! § 14
Mizokawahiko accepted the falsehood, evacuated Moscow, and marched toward Rome. In reality, Rome stood triumphant with soaring morale, untouched by enemy forces. Meanwhile, advancing toward Mount Pompeii, Masateruhiko encountered Kototamawake-no-Mikoto and Mototeruhiko on the road, learning with horror that he had been duped by Kusakahime. In frantic haste, fearing for Moscow's safety, he abandoned the march and galloped back toward the citadel.
