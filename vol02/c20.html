Chapter 20: The Questionable Love Letter

第二〇章 疑問の艶書〔七〇〕
§ 1
Kototamahime, the consort of Kototamawake-no-Mikoto, was the fifth daughter of Waka-sakurahime-no-Mikoto, while the scheming Tokoyohime was the third daughter. With the triumphant return of Kototamawake-no-Mikoto to the castle, the dark clouds of suspicion hanging over Ryūgū-jō were completely dispelled, leading to an auspicious and joyous family reunion among parent and daughters, siblings, and spouses. The sly, deceitful Tokoyohime feigned cordial congratulations on the surface, while the assembled commanders rejoiced from their hearts that peace had been restored. For a season, Ryūgū-jō enjoyed profound tranquility and serenity.

§ 2
Thereupon Tokoyohime, posing as the great mediator, concentrated the favor of Waka-sakurahime-no-Mikoto and the commanders upon herself, her courtly influence swelling day by day. In contrast, Kototamawake-no-Mikoto's prestige was initially diminished, regarded askance by many as a former rebel commander. Nevertheless, through the subtle, unyielding advocacy of Ōyashimahiko-no-Mikoto and Masumihime—who were deeply trusted by Waka-sakurahime-no-Mikoto—Kototamawake-no-Mikoto's honor was steadily restored, allowing him once more to hold reins of divine administration. Kamikuniwake-no-Mikoto continued to preside over governance, and the concord between Kototamawake-no-Mikoto and Kamikuniwake-no-Mikoto was harmonious beyond measure, resembling an affectionate couple. Seeing this, Tokoyohime once again enlisted Magahiko and Magahime as her chief confidants, scheming tirelessly to undermine and destroy the prestige of these two loyal deities.

§ 3
As Kototamawake-no-Mikoto's renown recovered with every passing day, the insidious craftiness of Tokoyohime became increasingly apparent to discerning shrine officials. Yet the faction comprising Kojimawake, Tayorihiko, Yasukawahiko, and Takehiko remained blindly devoted to her. Even Waka-sakurahime-no-Mikoto eventually discerned Tokoyohime's self-serving duplicity, and her royal favor waned considerably compared to former days.

§ 4
A clear polarization emerged at court between the supporters of Kototamawake-no-Mikoto and the cabal loyal to Tokoyohime. Tokoyohime's faction was small and isolated. Recognizing that her court intrigue could not succeed at present, Tokoyohime chose to bide her time. Masking her resentment behind an amiable smile, she sought leave from Waka-sakurahime-no-Mikoto, claiming that an unexpected crisis had erupted in the Land of Tokoyo demanding her immediate return. Waka-sakurahime-no-Mikoto, harboring her own misgivings, granted permission without hesitation. Flanked by Magahiko and Magahime, Tokoyohime imparted clandestine instructions to Kojimawake, Tayorihiko, and Yasukawahiko, and then departed openly for the Land of Tokoyo.

§ 5
Following Tokoyohime's departure, Kototamawake-no-Mikoto's ascendancy surged like the morning sun ascending the celestial dome. Bent upon tarnishing Kototamawake-no-Mikoto's immaculate reputation, Tayorihiko and Yasukawahiko summoned a maiden of peerless beauty named Kazukohime into the palace, appointing her as a personal handmaiden to Kototamawake-no-Mikoto. Kazukohime served him with consummate attentiveness, catering to his slightest need with exquisite charm. Pleased by her apparent sincerity, Kototamawake-no-Mikoto made it his custom to have her accompany his retinue whenever he ventured outside the castle.

§ 6
One day, a letter was found deliberately dropped within the palace corridors. Yasukawahiko swiftly snatched it up, pocketed it, and delivered it to Kojimawake, who unsealed it and brought it before Waka-sakurahime-no-Mikoto. It was an amorous, scandalous letter ostensibly written by Kazukohime unto Kototamawake-no-Mikoto. Its text claimed that they had engaged in clandestine intimacies on multiple occasions, but bitterly reproached Kototamawake-no-Mikoto for subsequent heartless coercion, lamenting her transgression of celestial morality and declaring her intention to drown herself in the Jordan River to atone in death. Stunned and appalled, Waka-sakurahime-no-Mikoto secretly summoned his consort, Kototamahime, and presented the incriminating epistle before her.

§ 7

Utterly unaware of this vile intrigue, Kototamawake-no-Mikoto was resting peacefully in his chamber when, in the dead of night, a shadowy figure pushed open the sliding screen. Feigning deep sleep, he observed the figure; it was his beloved consort, Kototamahime. Seating herself beside his pillow, she whispered poignant words, bowed thrice in tearful reverence toward his sleeping form, and departed. Sensing calamity, Kototamawake-no-Mikoto leaped up and pursued her on noiseless feet. He found her standing upon the precipitous bank of the sacred Heavenly Well of Manai; having offered solemn prayers and chants to Heaven and Earth, she was about to plunge into the abyss. Leaping forward, Kototamawake-no-Mikoto caught her in his arms, desperately demanding the cause. Weeping bitterly, she disclosed the scandalous letter and cried out:

§ 15
Thereupon, every suspicion against Kototamawake-no-Mikoto was completely cleared. Following his expulsion, Yasukawahiko fled directly to Mount Kijō, enlisting in the demonic fortress of Kuniteruhime.
