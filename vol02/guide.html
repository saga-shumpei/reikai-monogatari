凡例（はんれい）
Item 1: Cosmic Chronology and the Mind of a Newborn Infant
English Translation • §1
1. From Volume 1 through Volume 4, the narrative treats of the age prior to the sacred descent of the two deities Izanagi-no-Mikoto and Izanami-no-Mikoto. It is only in Volume 4 that the divine retirement of the Ancestral Deity of the Land (Kunitokotachi-no-Mikoto) is recounted, and in Volume 6 that the dual deities Izanagi and Izanami descend unto Ashihara-no-Nakatsukuni (the Central Land of Reed Plains, Earth). Therefore, one must not view this work through an overly narrow lens, becoming bound by the contemporary institutional form of Oomoto. For instance, where the text speaks of the “Holy Land of Jerusalem,” it by no means refers strictly to the town of Ayabe. Master Zuigetsu (Onisaburo Deguchi) has issued particular caution on this point, and we request that all readers bear this deeply in mind. In essence, it is most vital to approach and read this work with the pure, unprejudiced heart of a “newborn infant.”

English Translation • §2

Item 3: Cautions against Premature and Superficial Criticism
English Translation • §3
3. Along with the publication of Volume 1 of this work, diverse worldly criticisms appear to have arisen; however, by merely reading Volume 1 or Volume 2, it is utterly impossible to comprehend the whole. In any case, we ask that critique be postponed until the entirety of this work has been published. As the Divine Oracle explicitly declares: “The craftsmanship has its various ways; pray wait to see the finished work.” To indulge in critical remarks having glimpsed only a single fragment is not only exceedingly rash, but threatens to inflict severe hindrance upon the sacred divine enterprise.

Item 7: Master Zuigetsu's Esoteric Decipherment: Fulfillment of the Kingdom of God
English Translation • §7
7. When we inquired of Master Zuigetsu concerning this mystery, he immediately revealed its profound spiritual significance. The mysterious characters upon the pillar of the five-story pagoda total the sacred number seventy-seven; moreover, seventy-seven reads as seventy-seven whether counted from above or from below—a perfect symmetry uniting heaven and earth, above and below. The number seventy-seven is a cipher for the fulfillment of the Divine Oracle: seven signifies “Fulfillment (Naru),” ten signifies “God (Kami),” and seven signifies “Nation (Kuni)”—in sum, “The Fulfillment of the Kingdom of God (Sei-Shin-Koku).” The three circles in the cipher denote the Three Sacred Jewels, signifying that the Savior Soul, the Mizu no Mitama (Spirit of Mercy / Zuigetsu), was concealed until the appointed time. K&omacron;b&omacron; Daishi was fully aware of this truth, but intentionally kept it veiled. Master Zuigetsu deciphered the verse thus: [Master Zuigetsu's Decipherment] • Getsu ken ch&umacron;-gan — The Moon hangs suspended over the central bank • Kan-kyo dare o ka matsu — Quietly dwelling in seclusion, whom does it await? • Tsuyu wa y&umacron;-tai ni shiworu — The dew withers upon the secluded moss • Doku-shin ko-jaku tari — A solitary soul in lonely solitude • H&omacron; wa hajime habakarazu — The Law at first does not spread unchecked • Tsuzumi ni shitagaite hikari o masu — Following the divine drum, its light increases • Michi wa hitori shite hirogorazu — The Way cannot expand by a single person alone • Ji-setsu wa hito o matsu — The appointed hour awaits the chosen people Truly, this is a marvelous, mystical tale foreshadowing that the light of the Moon (Zuigetsu / Mizu no Mitama) shall dispel the dark gloom of all the myriad realms, and that the long-awaited season for the fulfillment of Divine Governance has at last arrived.

Item 8: Closing Comic Verse (Yohakuka) upon the Doubters and Critics
English Translation • §8
Sour or sweet, all passes out through the rear like a blast of wind! Let the arrogant proud-nosed hypocrites with foolish countenances have their pretenses plucked clean; if they dismiss this work as mere ramblings of foxes and badgers and refuse to take it seriously, let them be! It is like the hypocritical warrior who sneaks into the privy to gobble down sweet dumplings, while proclaiming to the world: “A warrior never goes back on his word!”—such arrogant pontifications are worth no more than rolling a piece of dried snot between one's fingers; brush them aside and laugh them off! In any case, read it through but once beneath the dusk sky; no false words are these, unlike the deceitful or fickle moon, but the true Reikai Monogatari proclaimed by Zuigetsu! With deepest reverence, with deepest awe.

酸いも甘いも皆尻の穴、おならの如くに音を立てて抜けて行く。間抜けた顔をして威張り散らす天狗の鼻高が、尻の毛を抜かれ眉毛の数を数えられて化けの皮を剥がされ、「狐狸の妄言（うわごと）だ」と相手にせぬならせぬで一向に構わない。雪隠（便所）に隠れてこっそり饅頭を頬張りながら、「武士に二言はない」などと大口を叩くような偽善者の能書きなんぞは、鼻くそを丸めるように軽くひねり潰して聞き流し、馬鹿にしておけばよい。何はともあれ一度は読んで見よ、暮れの空に昇るきょろ月、まご月、嘘月のような空言ではない、これぞ真実の瑞月が語る『霊界物語』であるぞ。穴かしこ、穴かしこ。
原文（近代文語）
酸いも甘いも皆尻の穴、おならの如くにぬけて行く、間抜けた顔の鼻高が、尻毛を抜かれ眉毛をよまれ、狐狸のうさ言と、相手にせねばせぬで良い。雪隠で饅頭喰ひつ武士、武士の言葉に二言はないと、こいた誤託の鼻の糞、ひねつて聞いて馬鹿にして、一度は読んで暮の空、きよろ月、まご月、嘘月の、空言ならぬ瑞月霊界物語穴かしこ穴かしこ。
