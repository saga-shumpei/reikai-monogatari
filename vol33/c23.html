Tōsuke, wondering what to do, sprang straight up, and letting his voice ring out clearly to echo across the surroundings, began to chant:

Together with the divine minister of the heavenly administration, Tsuranagihiko, / Appeared in the rich land, / Discovered Tōsuke's father, Takateruhiko, / And commanded him as the governor of the realm. / Born as a precious child of Takateruhiko, / Becoming one of the three who emerged, / I am Tōsunowakenokami. / Receiving the command of my father, / Crossing over the vast ocean, / Ascending the river of Eden, / To the city of God, Jerusalem, / Advancing to the foot of the Mountain of Gold, / Even as I came, a graceful maiden traveling alone, / At the very moment I was passing her by and leaving her behind, / Suddenly a cry was heard: / What manner of crookedness has appeared,

Forgetting the mission of my father, / Since I am one who heaped up sins, / Though I thought the woman pitiful, / I abandoned her and fled from the hut, / Advancing from there into the land of Fusa, / Traversing the land of the moon, / Crossing the mountains and rivers and traversing the wastes of Tibet, Siam, and Cathay, / Advancing and advancing, / Just as I was crossing over to the island of Onogoro, / My boat shattered by the wind, / At a moment when my life was in peril— / Whether by the grace of heaven or the favor of earth, / A divine being endowed with wings / Grabbed my body abruptly, / Soaring high into the great sky, / Upon the ocean of the strait of life, / Floating wide upon Awaji Island, / At the summit of Mount Akatsuki, / Upon a soaring pine tree,

Oh, the wonder of being carried along! / The island people of the land of Awaji, / Looking up at my figure lingering in the treetops of the pine, / Reverently welcoming me as a divine being descended from heaven, / Rejoicing from the bottom of their villager's hearts, / Raised up as the chieftain of the island of Awaji, / And in this island, / Choosing Ohyuri, whose fame as a virtuous woman was ever high, to be my wife, / Changing my name anew to Tōsuke, / I came to pass the days and months; / Yet drawn by the cord of grace of the supreme deity of Ssangyong, / I have now traveled forth to the sacred ground, / Following the princess Eiko, / And have become a teaching minister.

Forgive our deep sins, / and advance us / upon the august path of the Gods, / Kamunagara! / We pray before the august presence of the Gods,'

So singing to an end, he sat down upon his seat with composed dignity.

Takahime for the first time realized that Tōsuke had been her lover thirty-five years ago, and blushing with maidenly shyness, she kept her head bowed for a little while, but as if resolved, she turned toward Tōsuke and:

Ah, were you that gentle man of those days, Azumanowakenokami-sama? Not knowing this, I have committed various discourteous acts. Sazosazo, you must have been utterly disgusted by the strength of my ego. Spoken of as the time of the shipwreck at Ahajishima, or as the derailment escapade at Ejima, you must be laughing at me as such a disgraceful woman. Thinking that if I spoke of such old tales carelessly, you might despise me, wondering if the woman of those times was this very Takahime, ah, it would have been better for both of us if I had remained oblivious just as in the past; this too must have been caused by the Gods making me vomit up the old dirt. Please do not cast me aside, and treat me with affection just as before,'

She drew close to Tōsuke's side and tried to take his hand, but Tōsuke sternly pushed her away, repulsed her, straightened his deportment, and with a solemn tone addressed Takahime:

Takahime-sama, please restrain yourself a little. The past is the past, and now is now. If there were to be a repetition here once more of the one hundred and eight earthly desires that we had gone to such lengths to forget, an explanation would not stand before the Gods. You are also a person who has a splendid husband, and as for me, as you well know, I am a chieftain of the island of Awaji with a wife named Oyuri, and above all, as long as I perform the duties of the General Affairs Officer of the Izutsu-no-miya, I cannot afford to do such a thing even by accident. From now on, let us conduct our interactions even more strictly than with strangers. However, I will only acknowledge the fact that Kunitsukuni-no-wake, the Kami-governor of the land of Kumaso, is the son born between you and me. Even if you wish to meet that son, it should be quite well if you meet him freely. I certainly do not interfere up to that point. Yet, as this Tōsuke, no matter how beloved a child he may be, I cannot bring myself to meet him. First of all, I shall make a request to Eikohime-sama, and after receiving permission from the Kami-governors Tamateruhiko and Tamateruhime, I will confide the circumstances to my current wife, Oyuri, and after obtaining Oyuri's permission—and if the opportunity arises—I might perhaps meet him. Please forget the dreams of the past as of today, and since this Tōsuke will adopt an even stricter attitude toward you from now on, please do not think ill of me. I declare my attitude publicly before everyone.'

Before his expression, which would not budge an inch no matter what arrows or guns were fired, Takahime shrank back, unable even to raise her face, and fell weeping alongside her tears.

Takahime chants desolately:

Compared to my heart now when seeing you again, / how the past has grown dear to me!

Tōsuke: 'Rather than calling to mind the old days and lamenting, / rejoice in the unfolding august reign of Izutsu! / That one becomes a parent and is born as a child, / whether you know it or not, is the deep bond of the age of the Gods. / Having passed through thirty and five years, / I hear now of the reliance upon my child!'

Takayamahiko: 'The karma of this world is like a revolving cart, / alas, how pitiful is the single-wheeled carriage! / Yet even so, I too am a child of past sins; / save me, O Gods of Izutsu!'

Kurohime: 'Crossing mountains and seas, seeking my husband, / the joy of meeting my beloved child!'

Tamajiwake: 'Without knowing the father and mother who bore me, / oh, the foolishness of a life spent in vain!'

Akihiko: 'The grace of the Gods of Izutsu is deeper still; / today is the day I realize the whereabouts of my child!'

Tomohiko: 'Parents and children are bound together / by the cord of the unbreakable bonds of the Gods!'

Tellersthime: 'At the shrine dwelling at the foot of Mount Okenfuse, / oh, this evening of hearing such auspicious things!'

Natsuhiko: 'Residing in place, to know fully the whereabouts of one's child, / how venerable is the lord who knows!'

Sadahiko: 'Realizing the husband of old, knowing the wife, / how auspicious is the person who has met his child!'

Yuzurihime: 'Today indeed the flower of the divine grace blooms, / and even the heart is fragrant in the autumn firmosphere!'

Otama: 'The Kami-governor Tamajiwake-no-kami / must be feeling joyous to have met his father and mother.'

Ryūkokuwake: 'The joy of meeting one's parent / is something I too have tasted at least once.'

Though congratulatory poems by Tsunehiko, Haruhiko, Sunko, Yoshiko, Fusako, and others were chanted without number, as it would become overly tedious, it was decided to omit them. What kind of activities will Takahime perform from now on?
