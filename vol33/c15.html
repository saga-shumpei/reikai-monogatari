Chapter 15: 婆論議 [930]

婆論議〔930〕

§ 1

Takahime and her party of six parted ways with Taruchīru, Kiyokohime, and Terukohime, and proceeded toward the port of Hara.

Incidentally, Kiyokohime, together with her younger sister Terukohime, by the command of Kotoyoriwakenomikoto, proceeded to the mountain stream of Mikurayama, visited the shrine of the land-spirit Tatsuyohimenomikoto, taught and guided numerous people of the land, and from there passed through the lands of Hiru, Karu, and Aida, crossed over to the land of Tokoyo, advanced to Mount Rotsukī, reached Mount Kijiyōzan, and being protected by white fox deities led by Onitakehiko and others, propagated the Way of Ananahi. Kiyokohime, by the command of Hinodenokami, became the wife of Kahedewakenomikoto of the capital of Hiru, departed from the mansion of Hiru, and together with her younger sister Terukohime, facing this waterfall, built a small pavilion upon the rock standing upright in the center of the jewel pond in the mountains, intending to perform ascetic practices for a hundred days and a hundred nights. Meanwhile, Terukohime, through the matchmaking of Kunioyakenomikoto, became the wife of Ishikuma, who had been the leader of Brahmanism, and established the Ananahikyō in the mansion of Takaharahama. Furthermore, Ishikuma received from Kunioyakenomikoto the divine name Mitsukuniwake, and husband and wife lived in harmony, serving in the great divine work of subduing the great serpents.

Although the demons of the Amazon River and the fierce beasts of the Forest of Shigure were all saved into the divine path through the efforts of Takayorihime and others, malicious deities of the lineage of Yamata no Orochi still inhabited a part of the land of Hiru and the forests of the land of Karu, wielding violent might and tormenting the people. Therefore, the two couples Mitsukuniwake and Kahedewakenomikoto obtained divine power and, as a result of agonizing trials, were finally able to pacify the malicious deities through kototama. And Hayahiko, who had accompanied Takahime, together with Taruchīru, crossed over the land of Tokoyo, advanced far into the snowy country of the north, and achieved outstanding military fame. This tale shall be recounted again at a later date.

Takahime and her party boarded the Takashima-maru once more from the port of Hara under favorable circumstances, and set out to return toward Jitōtō. Upon the ocean the waves were calm, and even the vast Atlantic lay tranquil like a mirror. To console the tedium within the ship, casual conversations were beginning in every corner.

Takahime and her party quietly listened with interest to the idle talk of various people. Three men occupying seats near Takahime sipped liquor from a gourd bit by bit while indulging in casual chat.

A: 'The other year, on the way back from the island of Ryūgū to the land of Teru, I returned aboard this very Takashima-maru, and something strange happened then. An old woman and two stalwart men came aboard in a small boat, rammed a reef with their vessel, and having no other choice, with supreme audacity began walking upon the waves. When the waves grew high due to a sudden storm and they were in distress, the Takashima-maru happened to pass by and rescued them. At that time, I remember the name was something like Takahime, but she plunged headlong from above the boat into the sea, was rescued by her accompanying man, and conversely, a nagging old hag who talked back was on board. Aren't there truly strange old hags in this world?'

B: 'It is not limited to Takahime; old women anywhere are terribly malicious. That is why people dislike what is called maternal solicitude. Not a single old hag is any good. Even in my household, ever since the wife came, my mother, who had cherished me until now, suddenly underwent a complete change of heart, and even toward me, her only son, she says this and that and bears down harshly upon us. They say that once the name of daughter-in-law is attached, even one's own child is hateful, and an old hag's twisted disposition is truly something beyond any help. Since our old woman treats the bride so harshly and persecutes her relentlessly, I too could not bear to watch and so pleaded: Mother-sama, she is still a young bride, so it is no wonder she lacks experience. From now on I will gradually train her to suit the family ways and to please you, Mother-sama, so please close your eyes to what the wife does for a while, even if it is annoying... When I begged her thus, oh, how angry Mother-sama became! Instantly turning her eyes vertical, she blurted out: Until now you have been exceedingly filial to me, but ever since that Kame came, your disposition has suddenly turned bad, and you have become unfilial. And this, never because you are at fault; that Kame wretch treats this old age as a nuisance and offers prayers to the gods every morning hoping this old hag will soon die. There is no fear of such an unfilial child being born into my lineage; Tsurukō is naturally a gentle and filial person. At any rate, the wife is to blame... and so forth, spouting arbitrary logic, and actually I am troubled as well. When people become old hags, why do their wicked natures turn out so foul?'

Kō: 'Such unreasonable hags are nothing rare. Throughout the village, about nine tenths of them are hags of that sort. Still, since hags are treasures of the household, one must first show them respect; but this so-called Takahimebaba whom I am talking about is a specially crafted article, not some wishy-washy hag like that. She is said to be a dreadful hag, something like Yamata no Orochi, Kinmaukiubi, or a moraband. They say she is a cormorant-like hag who swallows and vomits various splendid jewels, such as the adamantine wish-granting jewel and others. She, you see, came all the way to Takasagojima, encountered the moraband in the forest of Shigure, wrestled with it, took away its tail-sword, and is an absurd, outrageous wicked old hag of an onibaba who intends to swagger about on Onokorojima or somewhere, you know.'

Tsurukō: 'Hey Matsukō, the world may look wide, but it is a small place. If you go talking like that, and if the person herself should happen to be riding on this boat, you will never hear the end of it. Why don't you be a bit careful?'

Matsukō: 'Since only humans are riding on this boat anyway, there must be one or two hags among them. I have a feeling that someone with a face resembling Takahime is right in my neighborhood. Because of that, the spirit of Takahime's double has possessed me, making me think of Takahime, and I had no choice but to talk about it. Even Kototamanokami-sama, without playing around half-heartedly, could reform her disposition if he let her swallow that jewel, you know. Even tigers and wolves, when their bellies are full, do not snap at people recklessly. Much like a lean dog that instantly bites at you the moment it sees a person and yelps as it vomits, if you just fill its belly, it will immediately wag its tail and turn docile; even Takahime, if only she stuffs the adamantine wish-granting jewel into her belly, will become docile and surely won't chase after that Kototamawake-sama and the like like a yaksha all the way to a place like this.'

Tsurukō: 'Aren't you just comparing human beings to four-legged beasts? If Takahime hears that, she will probably get angry, saying you are making a fool of people.'

Matsukō: 'Nonsense, Takahime too is just a four-legged sack. Merely because she holds an elegant title called a propagandist, she just seems like some unusual person. Strip off her propaganda robe and look at her stark naked. She is only older than my Otake, has no worth, and to top it off, she is unsightly. Not a shred of feeling of respect arises. Whether man or woman, unless you strip them naked, their true value cannot be known. Because they talk grandly as generals or leaders and wear splendid furoshiki over themselves, they look like generals or leaders, but if you make them naked, they are no different from us anywhere at all. I do truly wish a world of sincerity would come, where only naked human beings live.'

Tsurukō: 'There is a rumor that a hag called Takaiwohime also spoke sweet words at the waterfall of Arina, gathered many jewels, and in the very end, pilfered the golden jewel dedicated from the village of Tena in the land of Hiru, that four of her kind pretended to be Zaitokuji in the dead of night, had the fat squeezed out of them mercilessly by the kami at the wasteland of Kayanohara, finally repented or found peace, and became the king of rabbits on the banks of the Amazon River; have you heard of this, you?'

Matsukō: 'I have heard of it. No matter how large it may be, becoming the head of a chicken is better than being the tail of an ox, which is the ideal as a human being, so I am always impressed that Takaiwohime is quite a remarkable person. The story goes that that hag too escaped from Onokorojima due to Takahime's unreasonable demands. Truly, this person called Takahime is an outrageous, irritating wicked old hag even when others hear of her, you know.'

Tsurukō: 'Hey, do not speak in such a loud voice. Are not the two old hags behind you making strange faces and starting to cry?'

As they were thus situated, Telistan strode up directly to them,

Telistan: 'Matsukō, Tsurukō, or whatever you are called, your hag-debate is quite lively, is it not?'

Matsukō: 'Oh, it is lively indeed. We are exposing the rumors of that exceedingly arrogant Takahime and hurling abuse at her. That hag sitting right over there gives me the feeling that she somehow resembles Takahime whom we saw on this ship last year, but after all, she did not have such a white face. Are you by any chance a creature connected to that Takahime?'

Telistan: 'Connected, not quite; not connected, not quite either. Since you fellows were telling a strange story so interestingly, I thought I would like to hear it too, and since my ears are a bit hard of hearing, I drew closer.'

Tsurukō: 'Come, have a cup. Without sake, a long journey like this is impossible to endure.'

So saying, he held out a gourd.

Telistan: 'That is kind of you. However, much as I appreciate it, I am a teetotaler, so I do not touch a single drop.'

Tsurukō: 'Nonsense, what is this? This is no place for teetotalers. It quite spoils the fun, why!'

Telistan: 'If it were a nice story, the taste of sake might be sweet as well, but with old hag stories, sake could hardly ever taste sweet, could it?'

Matsukō: 'I know it is not sweet, but there is something I must say so that it is loud enough for Takahime—oops, watch out—for the old hag over there to hear.'

Tsurukō: 'Such a thing, who asked you to do it?'

Matsukō: 'Matsuwakahiko—oops, watch out—this very Matsu has hated old hags ever since he was young, you see. Since I saw one fall into the sea of hags on this ship last year, today the sight of that time came floating back into my memory, and before I knew it, I plunged deep into old hag talk with a splash. Come now, let us put an end to this hag talk right here.'

Telistan: 'Hey Matsukō, you are a spy for the Reko, are you not?'

Telistan: 'Hey Matsukō, you are a spy for the Reko, are you not?'

Matsukō: 'Do not talk nonsense. As for being a spy, you are the one whose mind is full of suspicion. You are a bit different from a crafty fellow like Matsukō who does sumo using another man's loincloth... Hey Tsurukō, you know my usual self, do you not?'

Tsurukō: 'You put on clothes and boast of being a propaganda emissary of the Capital of Uzu, but once you drink sake you are utterly hopeless. It seems that your origin is the Ural teachings after all, so that habit cannot be corrected, why!'

Matsukō: 'Hey, is there any fellow who exposes another's secrets? Retract your words.'

Tsurukō: 'Excuse me, to the person who looks so very much like Takahime-sama and the rest of your honorable party, I meant to say that this Matsukō is a propaganda emissary of the Izumo teachings, but once he drinks sake his true nature is revealed and he is hopeless; however, at Matsukō's request, I retract those words anew, so please think that he is certainly not a propaganda emissary. Ha ha ha ha ha!'

Telistan: 'Aha! As I thought, you are a propaganda emissary after all. Where on earth are you planning to go? No matter what you say, it is no longer any use hiding it. Come on, let us have the truth; I am also a person who has come all the way here from the Capital of Uzu, and I am a mere fragment of a propaganda emissary of the Izumo teachings myself.'

Matsukō: 'If that is so, I shall make a clean breast of it. The truth of the matter is that, receiving orders from Matsuwakahiko-sama, I came following behind your party. I did not come with any wicked thoughts whatsoever. By the inner command of the Great Deity Lord Susanoo, since we wish to have Takahime-sama, Takaorihime, and Ryūkokubake make the sincere service of the wish-granting jewel of Mani, I was chosen and came from among a great crowd to ride on the same ship and give you all sorts of indirect teachings so that Takahime would not become arrogant.'

Telistan: 'Is that so? That is truly much obliged to you for your trouble. However, that cannot be all of it. You must be carrying some other earnest and important mission. Or rather, you must be holding the key to a secret. Would it not be possible for you to whisper that to me right here?'
