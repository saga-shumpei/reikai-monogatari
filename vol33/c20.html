Chapter 20: 昔語 [935]

昔語〔935〕

§ 1

In the mansion of Kurohime, adorned with a scenic view overlooking the Kokumogaha River at the eastern foot of Mount Okefuse, Kurohime herself along with Takayamahiko, Tōsuke, Takahime, Akihiko, Tomohiko, Princess Theles (Teerusu hime), Natsuhiko, Sadahiko, Otama, Takayorihime, and Tatsukuniwake were invited to a banquet for a family reunion, quietly sipping sake and indulging in various conversations.

Takahime: 'Kurohime-sama, thank you for your arduous journey to the island of Tsukushi for a long time. Your primary purpose was to follow in the footsteps of Takayamahiko-sama, but I wonder what kind of divine plan (shigumi) this turns out to be. The essential object of your quest, Takayamahiko-sama, was hiding away in the back parlor of Iseya right under the lighthouse where it is pitch dark at the base, and you knew nothing of it. Despite putting yourself to the extreme trouble of crossing distant waves to come here, which I thought was rather pitiful, through a miraculous bond it has now become clear that Tamajiwake is your own child. Indeed, this is a wondrous divine arrangement; one never knows what kind of convenience things will turn out to be.'

Kurohime: 'Oh, I am truly filled with joy. I never dreamed in my dreams that my son would become such a splendid missionary (sendanshi). Truly, it is an undeniable manifestation that the gods say it is a gathering of those with karmic ties... "If you repent, I shall assign you the service of the spirit alone; if you do not repent, the meeting of parent and child will become impossible"—so it appears in the divine revelations (ofude). But because the clouding of my own soul (mitama) was excessive, I lived until now encountering my own child without recognizing him. There is nothing as joyful as this. As people say, when you grow old, whatever you may say, your children are your strength. Parent and child are said to be joined by a deep bond in a single lifetime that cannot be severed no matter what. Speaking of which, they often say that husband and wife are joined for two lifetimes, but compared to the relationship between parent and child, the way of husband and wife is quite aloof and distant. Sometimes husbands say unpleasant things and conceal their figures like Takaya-sama did, causing severe worry to their wives.'

Takayamahiko: 'Please, I ask you to stop that conversation. It interferes with the political public order of the household...'

Kurohime: 'Hohahaha, what a convenient way for you to talk. Being of such a mature age and carrying on one or another affair with a maid of Iseya... I do not know the truth of it, but you are a fine gentleman who had rumors spread about you during my absence, so you truly are a distant husband. Ah, but I will say no more. Since we are before my splendid son, I am starting to feel embarrassed.'

Takayamahiko: 'Seeing that you met your true son and became so happy, you have suddenly grown boisterous and become extremely cold toward me, your husband. Seeing things come to this, I too have started to wish for a child. However, with an old woman like you, there is ultimately no hope of bearing a child, and there is nothing to do but give up. Tamajiwake-sama is your child. And you are my wife. If so, I am not entirely a stranger either. There is nothing to do but rely on Tamajiwake-sama's care. However, when on earth and with whom did you become a husband and wife and give birth to Tamajiwake-sama? If it is no trouble, since everyone is present, would you tell us about it?'

Kurohime: 'This too is to atone for my sins; I must expose my shame so that the gods will take away my sins, and therefore I shall speak of it for the sake of repentance.'

Saying this, she drew a hikinokoto (zither) closer to her and began to chant in song:

In the village of Kashiwai in the land of Persia, famed as the officials of humanity, born and raised as the eldest daughter of Ubayamahiko and Ubayamahime, Kurohime the wayward maiden,

at the twilight hour near the foot of the bridge flung across the Kashiwai River, all alone, buffeted by the river breeze, looking up into the empty sky, right at the moment when she beheld the divine figure of Tanabata-hime on the east and west of the heavenly riverbed, from ahead there came a gallant young man of around sixteen or seventeen, clad in a stylish yukata, swaying tipsily and humming a tune as he advanced,

whose voice was like that of a bell cricket, pine cricket, field cricket, or katydid, as a sudden chilly evening wind—a demonic wind, a wind of love—blew sharply.

Faces meeting in Aiohi, Truly a noble man, you are— Thinking thus within this place, He too—rubbing, tangling, intertwining, With the deep colors of pine and plum, Secretly sharing a dew-like pledge, Stealing into the shade of the surroundings, The darkness is dark, even the shadows of the stars Of the ubatama are sealed away, Upon the grass in the shade of the forest's trees, White arms like ahayuki, Embracing bare the youthful throbbing breast, Stroking, fondling, true tamade tamade, Coiling round, long as the thigh, Just as we lie together, shameful! As I wander about in all directions, Once again I happen to meet my father, Returning once more to my own home, In the lap of strict parents, Spending the days, a lapse of ten years, Fretting over the abandoned child, Morning and evening burning with anxiety, Pass the days spending in anxious worry, though not a single clue remains, Spending days in weeping tears, In the Ken'ongō of Mesopotamia, Demon-Cloud-Prince (Kigumuhiko) appears And opens the Brahmin teachings, Hearing this, I, stealing past the eyes Of my parents, from afar Come visiting to Ken'ongō, Listening to the teachings of the gods, Yearning for my child, my lover, The clinging attachment of longing in my heart, Not yet cleared away, in my suffering, Takahime-sama establishes

And in the Uranai teachings I lean my body for support, Morning and evening receiving the grace Of sea and mountain, through three-fives, Entering the path of truth, Seeking and wandering for the whereabouts Of the golden jewel, seeking the footsteps Of my husband Takayamahiko, Coming to the province of fire and its capital, Having the deity official of Takakunikuni Call himself Takayamahiko, Oh, the preciousness of his having you dwell there! Blessed by the grace of the gods, Here declaring my name alongside my child, Guided by Tamajitowake, Crossing over the sea at last, Coming to the port of Yura, Unexpectedly Takahime-sama Has returned from Takasago Island, Celebrating each other's safety, By the unexpected divine work of the Mani Treasure Jewel,

Having returned to the sacred ground, This joy—in what age Shall I ever forget, to the limits of my spirit? Tamajitowake's preaching messenger, Kurohime, whose spirit was clouded, Not humbling her body, forever May the affection of parent and child continue ever deeper, Adorning the Will of the Gods (Imakuni). Prostrating before the presence of the gods, With all sincerity I earnestly pray. Ah, Imakuni, gods, gods, May your divine spirits flourish and prosper!'

Tamajitowake began to chant, following after Kurohime. 'Thinking back on the past in the land of Fusa, At the foot of Mt. Takai, Foster parents of child-humans, whose names are high indeed, Takayorihiko and Takayorihime, Nurtured by a couple's affection, Even up to the spring of my fifteenth year, Nurturing me like their own child with tender care, How precious was their upbringing! As it chanced, right around the dead of night, Faces covered by hoods, in black garments, Five figures kicked open the front door, Invaded the living quarters, Without letting us say yes or no, binding both parents With hands and arms tightly bound, Returning in triumph as they played the song of victory. I, with a child's puny strength, Higher than the mountains, deeper than the sea Was the grace I had received, In the suffering of watching over the perilous distress Of my foster parents—'
