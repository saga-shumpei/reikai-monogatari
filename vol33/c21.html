§ 1

Takahime: 'Listening to you now, Kurohime-sama truly has followed a strange destiny. When you were young, you were surely an iris with countless suitors, much dotingly fussed over by young men, I suppose. From somewhere about you, a graceful scent of flowers still lingers. Ohohoho... However, there is no such auspicious occasion as this. I too have come to feel as joyful as if meeting my own child. ...Takayamahiko-sama, if you too had caused children to be born in your younger days, by now you would surely, by the grace of the gods, be able to experience a parent-child reunion and find it most delightful, wouldn't you?'

Kurohime: 'Yes, it is truly an embarrassing matter. Awe-inspiring... Even though I abandoned the child given to me by the gods, and even with such a sinful person as me, the gods have forgiven me and granted me such a joyous parent-child reunion. I went wandering hither and thither doing as I pleased, thinking not very much of my parents, and kept inquiring only about my husband and my child. Whether my parents are still in this world or not, I know not, but just as I yearn and pine for my child, my parents must surely be worrying a great deal about me. Truly, one cannot fathom just how deep a parent's heart's boundless affection is. Coming to this pass, I also feel anxious about the plight of my parents, and furthermore, my son Tamajiwake is rejoicing at having finally met his mother, but he is surely wishing to know the whereabouts of his father, without a doubt. Whatever the case, entirely due to my own thoughtlessness, I am causing even my single son to experience painful feelings. Ah, Tamajiwake, please forgive me. I will surely seek out your father even if I must part the grass, and have you meet him...'

Tamajiwake: 'Please do not say such undeserving things. In this wide world, no matter how long one searches, it is unlikely to be found easily. If the gods intend to make us meet, they will surely make us meet... So do not distress your heart over such matters, and let us single-mindedly, mother and child together, humbly serve the divine service of the gods.'

Kurohime: 'That is indeed so. Mother and child, holding hands, let us perform the divine service of the gods.'

Tōsuke clasps his hands, hangs his head, sighs deeply from time to time, and without uttering a word, gazes intently upon this scene.

Takayamahiko began to chant:

Aged parents, following the decree Of the deity Akitsuhime, Fled away to Armenia, Hiding deep within the mansion, Guarding morning and evening The teachings of the deity of Ural, Guiding the people of the world.

To such a place, a woodcutter Appeared and came, and my person Carefully and multifariously tended, And though my perilous life Was saved, the woman I loved, Her hometown's shadow appeared not, Swept away by the raging current of Fukatanigawa, There was no help for it; Deeming it useless to linger any longer in this world And live alone any further, Coming and looking at the foot of the bridge Spanned across Kashiwaigawa, Though by night's eye it could not be known for sure, It closely resembled Osato's face. Gazing, straightway the thread of love,

As it becomes entangled, stepping into the nearby

Forest, in the midst of...

Hearing the startling footsteps

Of someone coming forth clearly to the ears,

Starting up with a sudden bound they part,

And flee away like clouds and mist.

From then on, throughout the lands of Fusa,

I wandered hither and thither,

Thinking, might not my village home in Utsushiyo

Still be surviving and enduring?

Thus searching and seeking to the utmost,

With unreliable deliberations like grasping the clouds,

I sent days and months passing by,

Yet now, hearing the tale of Kurohime,

My heart within is startled.

Could the woman met upon the bridge of Kashihaigaha be Kurohime?

If so, Tamaharuwakenokami,

Truly my precious Miko,

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara!

The grace of Kami is thought to be higher than the mountains,

And ever deeper than the sea,

So that the tears of gratitude turn to rain,

Pouring down upon this very evening,

O Tamaharuwake, O Kurohime,

Takayamahiko is your father,

And he is your husband of old.

The bond of parent and child is thus,

Manifest like the sun and moon,

And this being so, from now on,

Uniting parent and child hearts together,

Before the august presence of the Palace of Brocade,

Offering sincerity morning and evening,

You must devote yourselves to the utmost limits of your strength.

The sins of old having come round again,

Enduring the varied and miscellaneous painful eyes of the world,

And wandering astray,

The relationship of husband and wife is also

Perhaps the chastisement of the whip of grace of Sumekami.

Now hearts too are melted and open,

And the heavenly expanse of Amatsumisora especially

Grows ever brighter, while above the earth

It has become ever more pure and clear.

The wind blowing past—wherever have the sorrowful sounds

Of hitherto gone?

The voices of joy celebrating a thousand reigns,

And the flowing of small clouds gently and softly,

Are heard as if celebrating

The future course of us, parent and child.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara!

May your august spirits be prosperous and blessed!['

Kurohime and Tamaharuwake, startled twice by the tale of Takayamahiko, cling to each other, saying, 'Was that youth at that time Lord Takayamahiko... was he my father...?' and their immersion in tears of joy appears truly heart-rending.

Takahime: 'Kurohime, auspicious events indeed pile up when they repeat themselves. It appears that your own sins and obstacles of up to now have completely been removed, and the Kami have caused the reunion of parent and child. And though Lord Takayamahiko spoke of a bond like the dew, he was the person you loved in your youth—what a sweet story, just like eating a rice cake in a dream! Yet for all that, this Takahime still seems not to have the permission of the Kami, and bears a heavy trouble within her heart. Ah, somehow, may the clouds of this trouble clear away even for a moment, and like today's sky in clear weather, may it become bright and clear. Ah, Kamunagara samayou tama sakiyahaimase.'

So saying in a tearful voice, she joins both hands and prays.

Kurohime: 'I heard a brief account of this in the course of our conversation some time ago, but you made an abandoned child, did you not? Even a resolute person like you still worries about it, after all.'

Takahime: 'The feeling called the love of parent and child is the same for anyone. The older one grows, the more one yearns for one's child. Ah-ah, upon Kurohime witnessing the reunion of the original parent-child and husband-wife, matters of the past are recalled all the more, and I cannot bear it, wanting to meet my child once. That husband of mine—where and how might he be living now... Today, even though we were to meet that husband, it would not do to become husband and wife, but at the very least... I should like to try calling out to each other: Were you the wife of that time, the husband, or the child...'

So uttering, she weeps and sinks down. Kurohime, smiling gently as if with firm conviction,

'Takahime, being somewhat careless, I do not fully remember your story, but did you not put a cross mark in pearls upon the guardian dagger for the child you abandoned, and put the characters for "East" and "High" upon the hilt?'

Takahime: 'What, Kurohime! I have no recollection of having stated such detailed matters, but might I have stated such a thing?'

Kurohime: 'Was not the name of that child Kintarau, and is he not, just like myself, reaching thirty-five years of age this very year?'

Tōsuke's facial complexion suddenly changed upon hearing this. Takahime's face also changed abruptly; her eyes grew round and the tips of her mouth began to pucker.

Takahime: 'My word, how very well-informed you are about such details. I do not recall ever speaking of that matter up to this point, so how on earth do you come to know such particulars? There must be some reason behind this. Pray, tell me plainly.'

Kurohime answered this with a song:

Pursuing the footsteps of Takayamahiko, Facing toward the island of Tsukushi, Leaving behind the port of Takehi, Over the great mountain pass of Tsukushigadake, And climbing up the Takayama pass, When she reached its very summit, There beside her stood five fierce men, Namely Tamakō and Torakō among them; Having heard the rumors of men, As the wife of Takenohime, The daughter of the divine official of the land of Kumaso, Not knowing whose abandoned child The highly renowned divine decree of Kenkokubake might be, Until this thirty-fifth year of his life, He has been inquiring after the whereabouts Of his birth parents, as I have heard; Whereupon, making her way from afar to his mansion, She met with the deities who are husband and wife, Wondering if perchance this might be her own child, And recounting his past history, While she examined and investigated various matters, Kenkokubake spoke thus: 'Of what manner of person am I the child? In the sorrow of still not knowing, Morning and evening, serving the thirty-five deities, While seeking and inquiring after the whereabouts Of my parents, How sorrowful are the days that pass! Conveying the divine teachings that thy life is far and near, Since thou art one who goeth forth, if thou shouldst happen To make me meet with my parents, Pray let it be known to my dwelling-place As early as even a single day. His childhood name, a joy even to hear, is Kintarau; A figured brocade attached to his own body, His name inscribed upon his amulet bag, And drawing out the cross sign with pearls Upon the guard of his sacred sword, When one gazes intently at the base of the hilt, There are the characters of 'East' and 'High' marked thereon; Nurtured by human kindness, He at last grew into an adult; If it be true that his genuine birth parents Are living in this world, He wishes to meet them even if it were for a single glance,' As she heard him thus lament, Even this Kurohime felt her heart swell, and thought Shall I reveal my name and make myself known? Having said just that, she hung her head for a brief moment and let out a sigh. Tōsuke likewise changed his facial expression and stared intently into Takahime's face as if to pierce holes through it.
