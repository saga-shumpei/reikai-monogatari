Chapter 19: 報告 Festival [934]

報告祭〔934〕

§ 1

In the Yahirodono of the Palace of Brocade in the Sacred Land of Aya, the sound of the Shichigosan drum was heard. Today the wind was exceptionally pure, the gloomy mists cleared away from early morning, the azure sky grew ever higher, the sun revealed its majestic figure from the edge of the eastern mountains, and cast golden light upon the earth. This morning the sound of the drum rang out somehow with unusual clarity, and an atmosphere drifted about as if the evil spirits of the nether world had been driven away beyond ten thousand leagues.

Tōsuke and Takahime, beginning with Akihiko, Tomohiko, Lady Teles, Natsuhiko, Sadahiko, Otama, Takayamahiko, and other senior officials, attired in solemn ritual vestments, were conducting the Haukokusai. It was a commemorative Haukokusai combining the joy that Takahime had returned after a long absence from the island of Takasago, that Kurohime and Tamajiwake had journeyed from afar to return to the capital from the island of Tsukushi, that the propaganda envoys Takaayehime and Ryūkokuwake had safely returned home to successfully serve the divine work of the Manihodama, and gratitude for the fact that Kurohime's own true child had been discovered after thirty-five years.

Along with the resounding musical tones of the Ichikōgoto and Nikōgoto, the grand festival concluded successfully. Led by the Supreme Priestess Yoshikohime, the divine officiants Tamaterehiko and Tamaterehime, together with Murasakihime, advanced deep into the sanctuary and participated in this festival. As soon as the festival ended, these four pillars headed toward the residence of the Supreme Priestess, leisurely escorted by four or five believers as they returned.

Behind them, a lively banquet of Naorae was held. Declared to be an unprecedented great celebratory event of all ages, the Yahirodono of the Palace of Brocade broke away from its usual strictness, and for this single day, everyone was freely permitted to partake of the Naorae wine to their heart's content, singing, dancing, and dancing wildly with abandon. As the intoxication of the wine began to take effect, casual conversations gradually started to arise.

Speaker 'A': 'Hey Torakō, times indeed must be awaited, you know. Takahime the general and old Mother Kurohime, whether sleeping or waking, kept crying out for jewels and made such a tremendous jewel fuss, giving Imperial Prince Kotoyoriwake and a great multitude of people a hard time, but didn't they finally carry out a single intent and brilliantly accomplish the service of the jewels? To top it all off, it was Kurohime who brought back a jewel from the island of Tsukushi. Truly, they played a sweet game, didn't they?'

Speaker 'Torakō': 'Hey, why don't you speak in a lower voice? Look over there, Takahime-sama and Kurohime-sama are sitting formally in place.'

Speaker 'A': 'I wonder if I too should set out right now to go jewel-hunting.'

Speaker 'Torakō': 'Even if you do not go out to search, you have plenty of them at home, do you not? Think about it well. Your cat is called a jewel, is it not? And the color of its fur is pitch-black Kurohime, oops, a black cat. Moreover, your wife is named Osumi, a self-explanatory pitch-black creature, and she has a face like a medium-low ladle. And she possesses a tiny bit of courage, and lets two eyeballs dangle like acorns. Your testicles are luxury goods fit for the eight-tatami mat tanuki to come and collect taxes on. With such an abundance of wish-granting jewels and golden orbs in your possession, if you were to make a fuss over jewels on top of this, everyone would not be able to bear it, so you had better give up while the going is good. Isn't that right, Yasukō the tanuki?'

Speaker 'Yasukō': 'Hey you alley cat, what nonsense are you spewing? Instead of talking about others, sweep away the bees from your own hive first. Mine is not an eight-tatami mat thing, but a brocade Shingen pouch. You tanuki bastard, you talk as though you are the deity Tajikarao-no-Mikoto; I was born in the year of the tiger, yet since I am a domestic animal kept in human homes, I am far greater than the self-proclaimed Ushitora no Konjin—or so you blabber, but where on earth is there such a thing as a cat-tiger Konjin? What a fine fool you are.'

Speaker 'Torakō': 'Tiger, what are you blathering about? A fellow called the ox is one who is left in the corner of the garden, covered in dung, and spends his days eating grass. A fellow called the cat is one who climbs onto the master's lap, crawls under the same footwarmer, and becomes the flower of the household to be treated with special courtesy. That is why the cat is the greatest. That is why the Cat-Tiger Konjin is superior in rank to the Ushitora no Konjin, so I say.'

Nekotora: 'With the threads of Misuji's trickery, she pulls and slays loaches and catfishes…'

Do you not know such things? No matter how much beard a loach or a catfish may sprout, even a Zenitolumen can pull them about freely, possessing almighty power, so it is quite a feat. It is none other than the Konjin-sama of Nekotora.…

Konjin of Nekotora appears and protects down to the gods of the Sanzensekai, the Buddhist rituals, the people, birds, beasts, and the insect tribes.…

He laughs. Yasukō stands up, looks around here and there, and scans his surroundings with drunken, hazy eyes to see if there are any friends who have come to aid him. The friend who catches his eye there is one called Takekō. Yasukō says,

'Hey Takekō, come here for a moment, reinforcements, reinforcements!'

At this voice, Takekō makes his way unsteadily like a golden plover amidst the throng, stumbling over a sake bottle, crushing wine cups underfoot, stumbling onto a person's head, and pressing down on shoulders, finally arriving before Yasukō. He is a peasant man from the neighborhood who has begun a faith because of somewhat poor eyesight. With eyeballs that look as if murky water were spilled over pearls, glittering from the depths, Takekō says,

'Hey Yasukō, what are you going to help with? Even if you talk of helping, these days my pockets are completely withered by winter. Not even a single leaf do I have rolling about, so on the contrary, can't you lend me a tiny bit over here? I too have been given this joyous sake today, but since I am an Edoko-spirited elder brother who eats today what he earns today, I cannot earn anything today.

Two or three ghouls that become the neck-shackles of the three worlds are waiting at home with the missus washing pots, so whenever I think of that, the sake I drank with such anticipation is likely to sober up right away.

Instead of spitting out such nonsense to a fellow like me, how about having this Nekotora lend to you?'

Torikō: 'Hey Takekō, isn't Nekotora going a bit too far?'

Takekō: 'Isn't it a name grander even than the Konjin-sama of Ushitora? You were just saying so and boasting about it just now. That is why this Takekō respects it with all his might and calls it Nekotora, so what is wrong with that? Don't say such things, just lend a tiny bit to me.

Today is a joyous day, so there is no reason you should refuse flatly when asked by people, and neither will I do such a clumsy thing as to reject it in one breath when asked to lend and refusing to lend.'

Yasukō: 'Hey Takekō, if you were to borrow money from a fellow like this, that truly would be a serious matter. Every time you meet, he keeps saying he lent it, lent it, without caring whether it is before people or anywhere else, and he makes you feel ashamed forever, so leave it be, leave it be, it bodes ill for the future.'

Thanks to that Fudesaki, Nekotora has completely thrown away his hitherto attachment and returned to a good heart, even giving me one hundred ryo in cash on top of having many debts. Otohime-dono of Ryūgū was truly a deeply greedy Kami, but this time, when the Ushitora no Konjin-sama manifested visibly and took charge of the three-thousand-fold world, Otohime-sama also acknowledged that this would not do, and handed over all the treasures she had accumulated at the bottom of the sea without exception to the Ushitora no Konjin-samā, becoming the right arm for this service. The people too, just like that, accumulating in greed and clinging to the notion that it is mine for all eternity, since all things of heaven and earth belong to Kami, they must be returned to Kami. I will make it a world without ups and downs of fate, and put the people of the world at ease. Those who reform their hearts early will become all the more blessed... such Fudesaki is a great gospel from heaven for us. Even Nekotora nearly became Otohime-sama of Ryūgū, you know. Utsuhuhu, what a windfall, what a windfall, could there be such a profitable thing in the world. That is why they say you do not know the taste of faith.'

Thus acting triumphant and chattering away, just as he was speaking, a man named Isuke, a relative of Takekō, came running up abruptly, shouted out a loud roar of 'Idiot!', and struck Takekō across the face.

Takekō: 'Ouch, hey Isuke, by whose permission did you hit my face? You reckless brat. Since I will bring you plenty of money shortly to express my thanks, think of it that way.'

Isuke: 'Come and give your thanks soon, I will be waiting.'

So saying, he pushed his way through the crowd and dashed out to the front. Tōsuke appeared upon the elevated seat, raised his voice loud and strong, and declared:

Tōsuke: 'Everyone, thank you very much for your trouble. With this, we will close the banquet, so I ask you to leave the premises for the time being.'

At Tōsuke's single word, the several thousand people turned toward the shrine, clapped their hands and bowed twice, and each headed home in high spirits.
