Chapter 6: 皮肉 Song [921]

皮肉歌〔921〕

§ 1

Takahime stood up and began to sing a celebratory song. The song went:

From the beginning of the Heavenly Rock Cave, / It is only by women that the night breaks. / In the land of the gods, called the land of the country, / In matters of women, there is no oversight. / Kuniyoriwake, the Kamutsukasa, / Since the days of Munehiko and Okatsu, / Has possessed formidable arms. / The disordered course of this world, / He has swung about at his own free will, / And has made his name resound. / Nevertheless, by the precious Mioshihe of the Ananahi God, / He has repented from the bottom of his heart, / And though he has become a newborn Akago, / To the place of Anshin, / As for me, I have not yet reached. / Nevertheless, the pure and noble Oboshime of Kamususanōnōokami, the Mizumitama, / To the Mikokoro of the Sueko Hime, / A fortunate person who has fulfilled it, / The Sendenshi of Kuniyoriwake, / Using the jewel as a pretext, Takahime, / To the Chikubu Island where the Mizumitama appears, / The Goshinriki that sent me from afar, / I truly feel with gratitude. / Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara.

Leaving it to the Kami, Takahime has decided that, / From now on, regarding this marriage proposal, / I shall say nothing more. / Now, now, Kuniyoriwake-sama, / You are by nature a carefree person, / Born with a talent for teasing. / From now on, straighten out the horse of your heart, / Which is your village-spirit, / And quickly become serious. / The Argentine Kamudate, / The pillar of the Kami and the lord of the country, / You, who bear a heavy burden, / Please be firm. / Parent and child are for one generation, husband and wife for two, / The rule says master and servant for three generations. / Your marriage is for two or three generations, / It is not for five or six generations. / If you judge by the rules of heaven and earth, / There is no one as violating as you. / Nevertheless, Kamunagara, / As a redemption for the Mizumitama, / The Kamunao-hi who created this world, / The Ōnao-hi with a broad heart, / Simply, all things are the sins of men, / Looked at and listened to by Nao-hi, / Being corrected, the Sendenshi who is the end result of Munehiko, / Kuniyoriwake, strangely enough, / Has married the Sueko Hime, who is a pure crystal Mitama, / And has climbed to a high rank.

That he should face the people of the world universally, / I cannot understand where the calculation went wrong. / Kuniyoriwake-sama, / If you think that Takahime is spitting out hateful things, / You are mistaken. / From now on, be humble, / Respect the Sueko Hime, / Do not cast your eyes on other women, / Revere her as Tsuma-daimyōjin, / And serve her with the utmost care. / Do not let your chronic illness recur, / And do not reach out to anyone you see, / And trouble the heart of the Hime. / Now, now, Sueko Hime-sama, / The person called Kuniyoriwake, / As I have just said, / Is a handsome man who cannot be let out of your sight. / Be careful morning and evening, / And as for maidservants,

If you place them by your side, / Be sure to keep only those who are plain, / Like an Okame. / You must never let your guard down. / This is the grandmotherly heart of Takahime, / Because I think of the Way, think of the country, / And think of you as precious. / The living palace of the Hinodewake, / Oops, wait, that is wrong, / The splendor of the Hinode in the great sky, / The Masukagami that shines across, / The sincerity reflected in the heart, / I shall exchange it for a mirror and present it to you. / Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. / Without fearing the noble presence of Kamususanōnōokami, / The bitter words of Takahime, / O Kamunao-hi, / O Ōnao-hi with a broad heart, / Please look upon them again, O Ōkami.

Kyōshu-sama of the Kototama, / Maidservant-sama of the Sutego Hime, / Tsukasa-sama of Matsuwakahiko, / What this Takahime has said, / You must never forget. / Everyone likes New Year's words, / But to line up the Kototama that people dislike, / I do so from my heart, / Because I think everyone is precious. / It is no use to give advice, / After having been clumsy and then rambling on. / Be careful in all things ahead. / The Kami appears in the front, / And distinguishes between good and evil. / The Hinode Kami clearly, / Like a mirror, illuminates good and evil, / From the bottom of the heart, / O Kami who judges. / On this auspicious banquet of today, / Where the wood flowers bloom all at once,

I have lined up, / As a feast of bitter Kototama, / That everyone does not like. / Do not just dismiss them as such crude items, / But taste them well and eat. / Bitter words are medicine for the stomach, / They become medicine for the spirit. / There is a person in the corner, / Laughing, saying 'medicine, medicine.' / Why are you so amused, Ishikuma-sama? / Your name is solid, / But in your heart, on the contrary, / You are likely a follower of Kuniyoriwake. / To seek one another with the same spirit and heart, / And to pity each other for the same illness, / Since I hear that is the principle of heaven and earth, / Because today is an auspicious occasion, / I will not blame you too much, but...

§ 2

Takahime: 'From now on, in this public place, if such a Futsugafu occurs, I, Takahime, shall not accept it. You are all looking at my face with Kimeu Kahotsuki, staring until it seems you would bore a hole through it; I cannot fathom your intent. Ah, your Kahotsuki is as if a Hatodori has been struck by a Mamedetsupau. Why are you feeling Shinpai? Since the ceremony has been completed in a Mede manner, you should all open up to one another and reveal the depths of your hearts. As I, Takahime, say, for the sake of Kuniyoriwake, it is the truth that you must be careful. Ah, Kamunagara-kamunagara. If I use the Kototama for too long, you all yawn and say, "Ah-ah-ah-ah-ah-ah." Truly, your Kahotsuki is unsightly.'

Takahime: 'Are you acting with an "Afun"? Your mouths, opened with such effort, remain agape as if they cannot close; it is just like looking at a Chinretsujiyō of Gohiyakurakan. Ah, Kamunagara-kamunagara. I have only spoken of things that do not please you, rambling on in a Benben-darari manner, and I am sorry for causing you worry. From this day forward, I, Takahime, shall say nothing at all regarding Kuniyoriwake. O Kami of Kuniyoriwake, please be at Anshin. Because of this, your Heizei conduct is Kanjin. If you are put to shame before Hitobito in a time of crisis and have to show a red face, let this be a good mirror for you all.'

Takahime: 'You must guard the Mioshihe of Ananai-kyō well, and as husband and wife, you must not stray from the path of Fūfu, but keep it with caution. Ah, Kamunagara-kamunagara. It is only because I think it is Kamunagara that I, too, shall hold my tongue regarding this Kondo Endan, in deference to the words of the Lord Susanoo-Ōkami. To the eight million Amatsukami, to the eight million Kunitsukami, and to Ryūzehime of the Kunitama-kami, before the presence of the divine command, I, Takahime, pray. I, Takahime, pray.'

Having sung this ironic song, she sat down in her original seat, looking cold and aloof.
