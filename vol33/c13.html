Chapter 13: 帰途 [928]

帰途〔928〕

§ 1

In the land of the gods of Argentina, leaving the capital behind, Tatsukuniwake, Takayorihime, Takahime, Terisutan, Karinsu, and Tsunehiko—a party of six—were seen off by Kuniyoriwake, Suekohime, and Matsuwakahiko.

Wishing each other well for the journey ahead, they returned toward the Shimane of Onokoro, their hearts emboldened and their footsteps light, feeling the joy of returning through the scorching, burning heat of the summer sky.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. Receiving the blessings of the Kami, they cast aside their attachment to worldly treasures as if they were worn-out sandals, and their spirits reached the foot of the pass at Mount Teru.

At the foot of the slope, in a forest of camphor trees, they took shelter from the rain for a night.

Awakened by the cry of a crow, they purified their bodies in the Hosotanigaha, finished their morning meal, and whipping their horses, they set out to climb the steep, rugged, and rocky slope.

As they climbed, they sang the songs of the Ananahi teachings, their voices full of courage, and before they knew it, their steps brought them at last to the pass of Mount Teru, where the wind blew fiercely.

There, the party of six finally rested their feet.

Tatsukuniwake: 'Everyone, while bathing in this cool breeze, shall we rest for a while and bid farewell to the land of Uzu?'

All: 'That would be well.'

With no objections, they expressed their agreement, and while exhaling rough breaths, they sat down in the shade of an evergreen tree standing with interesting branches at the summit to catch their breath.

Tatsukuniwake: 'This mountain is a famous place where, when Momogamihiko-no-Mikoto-sama settled in the capital of Uzu with Gogatsuhime and served in the divine work, the sisters of Pine, Bamboo, and Plum, who appeared as Ōkamutsumi-no-Mikoto during the battle of Yomotsuhirasaka, climbed up to this point at the time of the first battle of the propagandists, looked up at the sky of the capital of Uzu, and sang a song of farewell. It is also a place of deep historical significance where Suekohime-sama, accompanied by Sutegohime, Karu, and Ishikuma, rested for a while, sang songs, and then proceeded to the capital of Uzu. We, too, must each sing a song to pass it on to future generations. Takahime-sama, since you are the leader of this party, would you not sing something for us?'

Takahime: 'Without needing to be asked, I was just thinking of singing something. Since it will be an impromptu, improvised piece, you must not laugh at me.'

With this preamble, she began to sing while looking down at the capital of Uzu.

Looking ahead, there lies the land of Uzu, The divine mansion of Argentina. Upon Aonogahara, glittering and bright, Shine the white walls. Kuniyoriwake, the minister of the Kami, Together with Suekohime, Must be living in harmony at this moment, With no one to disturb them, just the two of them, Looking into each other's faces, Smiling and beaming with the faces of Ebisu. How they must be enjoying themselves, That scene is so vivid, It is as if I can see it with my own eyes. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. Whether it is the plan of the Kami or not, Traveling this far through the scorching heat, Gasping and panting, striking at my chest, Climbing the steep slope, Squeezing out sweat and fat, For the sake of the people of the world. When I compare it, There is truly a difference like that between clouds and mud. In a world where there are those above and those below, In a world where there are those below and those above. While saying it is hot or cool, With fans rounded and widening at the ends, Opening the fans and flapping them, Creating a breeze, the two of them, Must be resting in perfect peace. And what of us, What a fated nature we have. Climbing the pass of Mount Teru with effort, Breathing roughly, Bathing in the mountain breeze that blows only slightly, Saying how cool it is, But compared to the command of the Kami of Kuniyoriwake, It is like comparing the moon to a turtle, or snow to charcoal, Even if we say it is cool, the coolness, Is as different as heaven and earth. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. May the spirits be blessed, Until we descend the pass of Mount Teru, Raise the clouds to hide the shadow of the Heavenly Sun, And blow a cool breeze. Ryūzehime, the Kami of the Land, Please protect us. Needless to speak of the divine offices of Tatsukuniwake and Takayorihime, Even for Terisutan, Karinsu, And Tsunehiko, who are foaming at the mouth, It is pitiful to even see them for a moment. Illuminated by the heart of benevolence, Can one bear to look at this with open eyes? Everyone, you must be truly hot, Even if it is hot, please be patient. If you think of it as the warm blessing of the Kami, The heat will also become cool, Burning within the heart.

§ 2

If Kuwanetsu also, by the grace of the divine Mitama, were to pour down the rain of blessings, perhaps even the fire would become cool. Yet, Takahime wonders, why is it so hot as this? Thinking of Kuniyoriwake and his young Wakafūfu, who open the cool northern window and whisper words that even water could not penetrate, she is seized by a sense of Senbau toward the sky of Uzu. If this Takahime were but a little younger, she might somehow find a way to welcome a Ritsupa husband in the late blooming of her remaining flowers, and in the Sanroku of Yotsuo mountain, where the Yahirodono stand in rows, she would Mainobo morning and evening, and what a joy it would be to become a Fūfu who serves in the Miyazuka in such cool comfort.

Until this moment, her heart had been stolen by the Niyoihotsushiyu, and she had never even in a dream thought of a husband. Now that she has awakened from the dream that troubled her heart over the jewel, a single desire has welled up within her. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara, by the Mimegumi of the God of Binding, I pray to the Amatsukami and the Kunitsumikami before their Onmahe, with reverence and awe, that you grant to aged Takahime a person who will be a friend to her heart. I pray with reverence and awe.

Tsunehiko: 'Ahaha, Takahime-sama, the heat is so severe that you are becoming delirious. However, your recent Gojiyutsukuwai might be the Hontau. If it were possible, there is a lonely bird fallen here too, but then again, the Tohiya does not sell so easily.'

Takaorihime: 'Otsuhohohoho.'

Takahime: 'Hmph, Tsunehiko, do not make a fool of me. No matter how much of a Takahime I may be, who would ever take a man like you, who lacks wit, as a husband? You should have some limit to your vanity.'

Terisutan: 'Ahaha, Tsunehiko, you have been struck down. If you were to be struck by a young nice like Suetsuhime-sama, it would be one thing, but to be given the elbow by a cold person like a tree at the beginning of winter with half her teeth fallen out, you no longer have a face to show to the world as a man.'

Tsunehiko: 'Do not say foolish things. I was not talking about myself. I was thinking of taking care of Tatsukuniwake-sama, so I just tried to test the waters. Because Takahime-sama jumped to conclusions and took it the wrong way, it has been misunderstood like this. Tell me, Tatsukuniwake-sama, do you have any intentions?'

Tatsukuniwake: 'I am grateful for your kindness, but a person like me would never be to Takahime-sama's liking. And since I do not like her either, ahaha.'

Takahime: 'Hey, Tatsukuniwake-sama, in this heat, you should stop making a fool of me. If we are people who do not like each other, is that not just right? You should not speak unnecessary words.'

Tatsukuniwake: 'This is an unexpected outburst of anger; please, I pray you look upon this and hear this again with Kamunao-bi and Ōnao-bi.'

Takahime: 'Was it not you who initiated this by saying why do I not sing something? Come, try singing one. Since it is you, I am sure a Ritsupa song will come out.'

Tatsukuniwake begins to sing:

'Leaving the mansion of Chin, the party of six finally climbed to the summit of the Teru mountain pass. Whether it is the wind of paradise or not, the coolness that brushes against our faces is a joy to the heart. The peach fruit of the pine, bamboo, and plum stands, and we cherish the memory of the Father God who dwells in the capital of the land of Uzu. Looking far into the distance from this pass, where the fame is high, the waves of the Atlantic Ocean are high, and below us lies the land of Teru. The mountains and rivers are clear, the fields are green, and the Amatsumikara and the ocean are like a clear mirror. The God who governs the capital of Uzu dwells there eternally.

Kuniyoriwake and Suetsuhime, and the hundred other officials and believers, stand on the pass of Teru mountain, the six of us together, lamenting the parting once more. Even if the morning sun shines or clouds over, even if the moon waxes or wanes, even if the earth should sink, we have crossed to Takasago Island. The history of having exhausted our bodies for the sake of the Way in various ways shall be told and passed down until the end of the Miroku age, and shall not be forgotten. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara, may the divine Mitama bless us, and while protecting the future of Tatsukuniwake, may you cause great achievements to be established for the ages to come. Kamunagara, the grace of the God is deep.

As we leave the land of Uzu, with all our hearts and all our bodies, we offer our sincerity and pray with reverence and awe. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara, may the Mitama bless us.'

Having finished singing, he sits down on the grass again. From here on, there are songs by Takaorihime, Teru, Ka, and Tsunehiko, but as it would be too tedious, I shall omit them.

Takahime's party descends from this pass, stops by the Inui waterfall where Suetsuhime rescued Ishikuma when he was bewitched by a great serpent and suffering, and each performs Misogi here, recites the Amatsu Norito, sings several songs, and spends a day and a night here. The six of them descend the steep slope, finally advance to the port of Hara, wait for a ship, and it is decided that they will board the Takashimamaru again to return to their country.
