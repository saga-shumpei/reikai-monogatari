Chapter 5: Kami 寿言 [920]

神寿言〔920〕

§ 1

The marriage Mondai of Suekohime and Kuniyoriwake, having obtained the Fudōiteki Reukai of Takahime, was finally to be Toriokona. An altar was set up in the Ōhiromahe of the mansion of Chin, Kotoyoriwakenomikoto served as the Saishiyu, while Matsuwakahiko and Tatsukuniwake served in other Shinmu, and thus, the Saiten of the Shinzen Ketsukon was Medeta completed. Moving on to the Nahorahi banquet, they exhausted Jifunibun of Kuwanki, singing songs, dancing, and performing, to celebrate the Keiji of Kefu.

Kotoyoriwakenomikoto bowed respectfully to the Shinden, and while still wearing his Reifuku, began to sing in a refined voice:

Looking up at the high Hisakata, Parting the Yahekumo of Ame, Descending from heaven to the Aokigahara, Of Tachibana in Hyūga of Tsukushi, Meeting around the pillar of Tsu, Binding the vow of husband and wife, Lord Izanagi-no-Ōkami, Lord Izanami-no-Ōkami, Following the divine path of those ancient times, Appearing as the spirit of Mizu, Suekohime, who is the child of Lord Susanoo-no-Ōkami, With a heart of crimson, A true minister of God, Descending to the land of Chin, Making the teachings of God her shield, The merit of governing the people of the four quarters, In peace and tranquility, Is surely that which fulfills the heart of the Great Imperial God.

The minister of the Ananaikyo, Kotoyoriwake, leaving behind the land of Hidezuma, Of Onogoro, As a missionary of pure heart, Together with Kuniyoriwake, To spread the teachings of God, Crossing the waves to the land of Teru, Crossing the famous Teru-yama pass, Of Takasago Island, And coming to the capital of Uzu, Where the wind blows five times and the rain falls ten times in order, And the five grains ripen and the fruits, Ripen abundantly in this land of God, Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, Blessed by the grace of God, The divine power of Suekohime, Shines with the sun and moon, At such a time, the divine Lord Susanoo, From far away, At the Saien-kan of Mount Ubusuna,

Came out and arrived here, Meeting Suekohime, At the moment of rejoicing and courage, Looking upon Kuniyoriwake, Whom Kotoyoriwake had brought along, Making him the husband of Suekohime, And commanding him to protect this land of God, How noble was that proclamation, Kotoyoriwake, in awe, To Matsuwakahiko, Sutegohime, And many other people, Proclaimed and transmitted the words of the Great Imperial God, And all of them, Rejoicing and taking courage, celebrated this, Keiji together. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, The bringing together by the God of Union, The communion of soul and soul, In the fleshly palace where the soul resides, Even though there be high and low, noble and humble,

If one seeks the source, It is the divided spirit of the same God, Led by the times and seasons, Born high and again, Born low, Is the profound and mysterious divine providence of the spirit world, Dividing the lineage of soul and soul, This bond tied purely, For a thousand generations and eight thousand generations without limit, As long as Takasago Island exists, In the age of the pine that flourishes forever, May its color never fade for eternity, With waves calm, the two pillars, Warm in the quilt of mandarin ducks, Floating and advancing on the sea of Wada, How deep is the heart of the people, How deep is the grace of God, The sun and moon are clear and bright, In the evening of Kefu when the sky clears, My heart is courageous and my body is courageous,

This joy, from here, I shall not let go outside, I swear with my true heart, Before the presence of God, I celebrate and revere, I celebrate and revere.

Having finished singing, he returned to his seat. Matsuwakahiko stood up, opened his silver fan, and while singing a celebratory song, danced himself.

The land of Mizuho of Toyoashihara, The eighty islands and eighty lands of the islands, Saving all without exception, Born as the youngest child of Lord Susanoo-no-Ōkami, To Suekohime, a woman of excellence, Saving the people of the world who are sinking in the floating shoals and suffering, The missionary of the Ananaikyo, The minister Kuniyoriwake, Crossing the eight hundred roads of the tide, Manifesting his miraculous merit far and near, And now here, Coming out to the capital of Uzu, Respecting the words of Lord Susanoo-no-Ōkami, On this evening of Kefu, binding the vow, Of husband and wife with Suekohime, Shining across the heavenly sky, The sunlight is bright and the moon is clear,

Even the shadows of the stars are twinkling, The color of the sky changing as always, On this auspicious day of heavenly signs and earthly omens, The minister Kotoyoriwake, Becoming the Saishiyu, at the Shinzen, Performed the Ketsukon-shiki, And now finally, Binding the path of husband and wife to protect, This land of God forever, Today has become the beginning, From now on, the Uzu-kan, With the sun and moon lined up, brilliantly, Shall become the age of Tokiwa, Of Takasago, where you shine, Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, Expressing the true heart of Matsuwakahiko, Celebrating the Medeta day of Kefu, Praying for the long life of the spirit of Mizu, Lord Susanoo-no-Ōkami, For a thousand generations.

と歌ひ終り、元の座に着きぬ。松若彦は立上り、銀扇を開いて祝歌を歌ひ且つ自ら舞ひ踊りける。

§ 2

Fūfu pray for happiness before the Mimahe of the Sumekami.

Whether the Asahi shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, Kuniyoriwake and Suekohime, a wise woman and a refined husband, stand side by side. Since they have appeared, Takasagojima shall be praised forever as a precious Mikuni. In the eternal spring of Tokoyo, flowers bloom in profusion and birds sing. The Yamakaha are clear, the wind is pure, and the fields are lush and Aoao. The Aohitogusa multiply like the stars in the Ōzora, and the Mizumitama, before the Kami, offer the purity of Kototama to the fullest, wishing with awe and reverence, wishing with awe and reverence.

Having finished singing, they took their seats. Suteko Hime stood up, opened her Ginsen, and sang and danced herself, celebrating the Keiji of Kefu. Her song:

Leaving the Hisakata Takaamahara, you have traveled through the lands of the four directions. The Yamataorochi and the hideous Kami, the Magatsumi who rage fiercely and torment the common people, the Great Kami of infinite mercy, through the divine power of the living Kototama, has brought them to submission and harmony. Having tasted a hundred sorrows, and having traveled through the islands said to be formed by the congealing of the bitter sea foam, you have bestowed the dew of your grace. Under your divine authority, to which even the trees and grasses bow, the sky of Takaamahara is clear, the bottom of the great ocean is bright, and the lands floating on the waves are pure, clear, and lush. The myriad things of the three thousand worlds, fearing the divine virtue of the Lord, serve you with such nobility.

How noble it is that the Suekohime, the youngest daughter of the eight maidens, born as the precious child of such a magnificent Great Kami, even before she has reached maturity, for the sake of the Kami and the world, desires to bestow the sincere grace of the Kami even unto the single leaf of the grass. Appearing in the Aenkyō, she entered the mansion of Onigumohiko of the Brahman religion, and while she waited for the hideous demon to submit to the Kannagara path of sincerity, the Tatemikami appeared. Onigumohiko quickly raised clouds and fled. Suekohime, having no choice, together with her elder sister, crossed the clear-flowing Eden River and opened the path of the Kami in the four directions.

At the very moment she was opening the path, she was sniffed out by the subordinates of Onigumohiko, and I, too, was placed in a half-rotting fishing boat and heartlessly cast out into the Wadano-hara. What suffering it was. Suekohime, who receives the brave and pure spirit of the Kami Susanoo Ōkami, was not in the least bit surprised, and she encouraged my heart. Crossing through the raging waves of the ocean, we finally landed at the port of Hara in the land of Teru, where the divine majesty of the Kami shines, and crossing the Teru mountain pass, she manifested the power of the Mitama.

She brought the two divine officials of the Brahman religion, Ishiguma and Karu, to submission and harmony. Climbing up and down steep slopes, she proclaimed the Kototama of divine majesty against the Magakami who target the lives of the people, and having brought them to harmony, she emerged at the divine mansion in the land of Uzu. My joy and happiness at being able to appear here, following the Hime, are beyond measure. At the moment when she became the ruler of the land and was guiding the people, the divine official of the Misokyo, the Kami-hito Kuniyoriwake, parted the clouds and descended.

He stayed here and opened the teachings, but the Mizumitama of the Kami Susanoo Ōkami caused the Suteko Hime to manifest in this physical body, and proclaimed that she should bring the hundred Magakami to submission and harmony through the divine majesty of the Kototama, to save Takayohime and Takahime who were facing danger while heading toward the Amazon River. The Kami-hito Kuniyoriwake, revering that divine word, without delay followed the attendants, left the capital, and headed for Mount Boshi. Just as he was looking down at the Amazon River and smiling, the propaganda envoy Kuniyoriwake, pulled by the threads of the divine plan, followed by four attendants, came climbing up here.

When the two, filled with the divine majesty of the jewels of Ryu and Kyu, shot at the hideous Magatsu in the forests spread out to the north and south of the Amazon River, the divine majesty of the Kami was manifest before their eyes, and Takayohime and Takahime, yearning for the light, gathered at Byōbuzan and Mount Boshi. That the truth-man Kuniyoriwake, who bears such noble divine virtue, appeared in the capital of Uzu and became the husband of Suekohime, and that they have pledged their bond for all eternity, is truly noble beyond measure.

Moreover, the Mizumitama, the Kami Susanoo Ōkami, crossed the distant waves and attended this celebration. The gratitude of having the bond of parent and child, and husband and wife, bestowed upon us. Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara.

The grace of the Kami is before our eyes. Suteko Hime, who has served long, has finally found peace of mind, a feeling like meeting the spring when flowers bloom on the winter trees of a snow-covered mountain. Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara.

May the Kami of union keep the bond between the two of you harmonious forever, without change. May you remain as calm and peaceful as the deep color of the pine on the tail of Takasago, for a thousand years of the crane, and for the ten thousand generations of the turtle's age. May you two pillars remain in tranquility. From now on, Suteko Hime, encourage your heart even more, and as long as your strength lasts, taking sincerity alone as your shield, serve the Kami and the Lord.

Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara. May your Mitama be blessed.
