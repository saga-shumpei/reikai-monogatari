Chapter 4: 楽茶苦 [919]

楽茶苦〔919〕

§ 1

Before the break of dawn, Sutekohime had begun the preparations for Suekohime's Ketsukon, and was extremely busy with the Yōi. Matsuwakahiko came running in haste and requested an Annai. Takakō heard the nature of his business at the Genkuwan and immediately ushered him into the Ima of Sutekohime.

Sutekohime: 'Matsuwakahiko-sama, your appearance is so flustered; has some Henji occurred?'

Matsuwakahiko, while gasping for breath,

Matsuwakahiko: 'I truly congratulate you on this day. Now, just a moment ago, Takahime-sama came to my mansion with a terrible Kenmaku. In short, she is engaging in a wheel-like Undō with great Netsushin to jam this upcoming Ketsukon. As I had heard the general situation from Kotoyoriwakenomikoto-sama last night, I thought this must surely be that matter; and indeed, I was told that if I would only say "Un"... and nod my head, everything would be resolved. This is a Taihen matter; if I were to have cold water poured on this now, I would have no excuse to offer to Lord Susanoo-Ōkami or to Suekohime-sama. I thought that even if I were to argue with the eloquent Takahime-sama, I could not keep up, and that not dealing with her was the best strategy, so I escaped through the back door. When I looked back, Takahime-sama was disheveled, chasing after me with the force of a Yasha. Fortunately, I dropped the single-plank bridge to block Takahime-sama's path and finally managed to rush here. Since she will likely come to your house later, I wanted to consult with you thoroughly before then and ensure that the Hime-sama also maintains a firm resolution, which is why I have intruded upon you.'

Sutekohime: 'Ah, is that so? I am very sorry for the worry this has caused you. As for the Hime-sama, since last night, Kotoyoriwakenomikoto-sama and I have spoken to her in various ways, though we are inadequate, and at this point, her resolution is so firm that she will not budge an inch, so no matter what Takahime-sama may say, she will no longer be shaken. Please be at ease.'

Matsuwakahiko: 'Hearing that, I am somewhat relieved. However, if such messy matters were to reach the ears of Kuniyoriwake-sama, who is a man of simple character... he might shake his head and say, "I have had enough of such messy marriage talks," and if that happens, we would have no excuse to offer to the Ōkami, which is my primary concern.'

Sutekohime: 'Regarding Kuniyoriwake-sama, I quietly accompanied Takakō to visit him last night and heard his various opinions, and he was a little annoyed by Takahime-sama's interference. However, he showed a very firm resolution, saying that this time, because it is the will of the Ōkami, he cannot refuse. Please be assured of this as well.'

Just then, Kotoyoriwakenomikoto appeared all alone, and while having Takakō guide him, he walked briskly into the inner room,

Kotoyoriwakenomikoto: 'Ya, Matsuwakahiko-sama, so you were here; I am glad to have met you at a good time... Sutekohime-sama, you must be very busy.'

Matsuwakahiko: 'I have only just arrived a moment ago.'

Sutekohime: 'Whatever one may say, it is a joyous occasion. I can well imagine the trouble you have taken, running about for this matter.'

Kotoyoriwakenomikoto: 'By the way, Matsuwakahiko-sama... regarding Takahime-sama, a sudden illness occurred, and she once fell into a state of unconsciousness, but thanks to the prayers of us, as well as Karl and Haruhiko, she has finally regained her breath. However, her Kototama has ceased, and no matter what is said, she gives no answer, only blinking her eyes and shaking her body. She must be displeased with tonight's Ketsukon, but in this matter alone, no matter what, we cannot use only the opinions of Takahime-sama; I wish to make this grand ceremony a complete success at all costs, so I visited your house, but Tsunehiko-sama was away, and I was told that she had likely gone to Sutekohime-sama's house, which is why I have intruded upon you.'

Matsuwakahiko: 'No matter what you say, it can no longer be changed. Come, let us go up to the palace and begin all preparations.'

Kotoyoriwake: 'If that is the case, Sutekohime-sama, Matsuwakahiko-sama, let us proceed with haste.'

As they were about to depart, Takahime, with her hair in disarray, stood blocking the entrance; the suspension of the Kototama had been lifted before anyone knew it.

Takahime: 'Kotoyoriwake, Matsuwakahiko, Sutekohime-dono, I have business with you. Open this gate quickly.'

She was shouting thus. From behind, the two, Karl and Haruhiko, came running in a flurry, finally catching up at the entrance and smoothing their chests in relief.

Kotoyoriwakenomikoto and the others, hearing Takahime's voice, thought, 'How tiresome, she has come again...' though they did not say it aloud, they expressed it on their faces; Matsuwakahiko opened the front door.

Matsuwakahiko: 'Takahime-sama, we are currently occupied, so please return home. Let us meet again leisurely tomorrow.'

Takahime: 'Do you intend to treat me as a nuisance once again and turn me away at the gate? Both the I and the Sute are here as well, are they not? I know not how busy you may be, but if we do not settle this matter of tonight, it will not do to leave Sutekohime as she is. Each of you must be careful; if you do not consider this marriage of the Princess even down to the youngest daughter, and if you do something like throwing a precious daughter into a cesspool, you will have no excuse to offer to the Mizu no Omita-sama, and I, this Takahime, will have no excuse to offer first and foremost. What I say may not be to your liking, but you must show great magnanimity and take up what this old woman says, or else you will fall into a troublesome situation where even biting your navel later will be of no avail.'

Matsuwakahiko: 'It is unsightly at the entrance like this; please, do come inside.'

Takahime: 'Even if you do not tell me to pass, do you think I shall not pass? If what I say does not pass, I will make it pass until it does. There is a saying that the peach and the plum do not speak. Once I, this Takahime, have passed, you must let my opinion pass without a word. You have all gathered together and are wasting the days in various Odawara-hyōjō deliberations, but no matter what you say, how can this match be settled when your spirits are as different as snow and charcoal, or the moon and a turtle? Try placing your hand on your chest and considering the consequences. Truly, even if one were to say you are insulting the Gods, there could be nothing more severe than this. Everyone is only thinking of currying favor with Suetsukohime-sama and does not care one bit about the importance of her own person, so it has come to pass that I, the aged Takahime, must sally forth again and again.'

Karl: 'Takahime-sama, that is indeed so. Since you are one of the elders of the San-Go-Kyō, it is only natural that you should fly out at such times. Even when a cabinet is being formed, do the elders not always fly out? However, in this day and age of constitutional government, the public is complaining quite a lot, saying that the emergence of elders is anachronistic or what have you. Why don't you close your eyes and be quiet for a change? In this day of rapid progress, if you go around brandishing such old things, the divine strategy of adapting to the times will not be carried out, and this land of Uzu will end up being treated like a stepchild by the civilization of today.'

Takahime: 'Be silent... This is no place for the likes of a Karl-mouth to be prattling on. I have come to offer my opinion to Kotoyoriwake-sama and the other distinguished persons. Why are you, who are less than a comma, sharpening your beak against this great problem?'

Karl: 'This is why it would have been better to leave things as they were. When I was seeing Kotoyoriwake-sama off part of the way... 'Hey Karl, so that Takahime's Kototama will work, go back once and chant the Kototama twice, 'U... A...', then she will be able to speak freely,' the good-natured Kotoyoriwake-sama said, so I thought it would be troublesome if she were to speak now, but having no choice but to disobey the leader's order, I went back and enabled her to speak, and now that I have done so, it is always like this, which is a bother. I wonder if there is some way to stop her once more.'

And she is shaking her head.

Takahime: 'Hmph, do not trouble yourself. Takahime has not been mute since birth. I have received the Kototama from the Gods of Tenchi. If you can stop it, try and see. Since you have always served as an attendant to Suekohime-sama, if you have the sincerity that a servant should have, it is your sincerity to expose the faults of Kuniyoriwake without covering or hiding them, to make her lose interest, and to lead her to break off this engagement. The aged Takahime has gone to the trouble of visiting here for that very reason, so please, I beg you, do not let Takahime's coming here be in vain...
