§ 1

Karl stood up and began to sing.

Karu: 'Of the Ananahi that we believe in, / Of the Lord Kamususanōnōokami / Who reigns as the ruler of the Kami, / As the precious Onko, / Suekohime of gentle appearance, / With the Kokonmusau Shinriki / Endowed in her spirit, / Shall hold the Konrei ceremony / With Kuniyoriwake-sama; / Thus, for Chiyo, Yachiyo, / And even unto Yorozuyo, / Never to change, / They pledge the path of Imose. / How noble is this Kamuwaza! / Regarding this Gokeiji, Takahime / Speaks intolerant words, / Running about in all directions, / Agitated and restless in various ways, / To the pure Suishiyōmitama, / To the Hime-sama of the true Mitama, / She brings the Onnagoro and the Goketafu.'

Karu: 'The Babakaka, the destroyer of homes, / A person who will not budge for anything, / If she were to force a Ketsukon / With Kuniyoriwake as her husband, / The divine nation of Argentina / Would fall into a great upheaval of natural disasters. / This world would become muddy and dark, / A world of eternal night. / If this engagement is not / Interrupted by any means, / She stands up with eyes glaring / Toward the mansion of Matsuwakahiko, / And when she spits out a word or two, / Matsuwakahiko quickly / Opens the back door and, with a clatter, / Runs out; then Takahime, / Raging like an Asura, / With hair disheveled, chases after him. / What intensity of a marathon race! / Matsuwakahiko, at the wooden bridge,'

Karu: 'Using his strength, knocks it down, / And while stroking his chest saying "Phew," / He runs out of breath / To the mansion of the abandoned Hime. / With her momentum excessive, Takahime / Falls from the high embankment, / Into the bottom of the river, drawing the shape of a large "da." / The two who were chasing her / Are startled and, / Blowing breath into her, help Takahime. / "Kore-kore, mōshi, Takahime-sama, / It was a dangerous thing," / They say with great kindness; / But Takahime-sama, contrary to what one might think, / Responds with perverse logic: / "To be an opponent to such a person / Until the end of time, the day will end. / Yet, while it is troublesome, / I cannot just leave her as she is."'

Karu: 'Karl and Haruhiko / Carry the Baba-sama and, saying "Heave-ho," / Send her back to the mansion, / But she, with her mouth to the extreme, / Once again begins to curse with a hateful face. / Haruhiko, unable to contain his anger, / Suddenly grabs a brazier, / And as a result of the scuffle, / The three men and women are covered in ash. / Just as they were grinding their teeth, / Kotoyoriwake-sama / Paid a visit, and because of that, / The quarrel finally subsided. / Takahime-sama, feeling angry, / Without saying a word, flies off / To the mansion of the abandoned Hime / Like a Yasha. / Haruhiko and Karl, / Fearing she might commit a discourtesy, / Follow her to see, and find,'

Karu: 'Takahime, whose beginning was grand and end is weak, / With a look of having nothing to do. / "The momentum of the moment was unavoidable, / And I yielded once, / But because of the unpleasantness, just now, / At such a celebratory occasion, / You said such strange things. / I do not know if your mouth is a Karl, / But can I let this pass in silence? / I will say this for the sake of the future. / Takahime of the Ananahi-kyō, / You have also passed the slope of fifty, / Stop such foolish logic / As a mischievous brat would say. / Your dignity as a Hime will be lowered. / The Vajra-indestructible wish-fulfilling jewel, / And the other Mani-jewels, / It is no wonder they disliked you, ran away, / And went into hiding. / Even if it were not a jewel, I myself,'

Karu: 'Every time I see your face, / Come to want to hide. / It is a world where, / Depending on how one holds one's heart, / One is loved by many people / Or hated. / Takahime-sama, oh Takahime, / I never intended to / Speak of such things in front of many / And put you to shame. / I am worried about your future, / And because it is something truly unpleasant from the bottom of my heart, / I endure and tell you. / Please correct your heart. / I have never once thought / That I hate you. / If I thought you were hateful, I would not say it. / It is only because you are dear to me / That I must chatter on / About bitter things; / Please try to understand the karma of my body."'

Karu: 'May the Kami of heaven and earth also witness this, / And may this Karl’s / Deepest, pure heart / Be reflected in the true heart / Of Takahime-sama. / Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase. / May the Mitama of the Kami bless us, / May today’s joy be eternal, / Unchanging, like the pines of Takasago, / With the color of a thousand years deep, / Flourishing and prospering, the land of Uzu. / May the great path of the Kami be ever wider, / May the virtue of the teachings reach everywhere, / And may it open like the wind of Shinato / That sways the grass and trees. / Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase. / I pray before the Kami. / I celebrate before the Kami."

§ 2

Tsunehiko also stood up and began to sing.

Tsunehiko: 'The Kamuzukasa of Takahime-sama, Kotoyoriwake and Kuniyori, chasing after the Mabito, set out to cross to Takasagojima, accompanied by both Tsunehiko and Haruhiko. In the islands of Riukiu, they foamed at the mouth, and in the great ocean, they split their ship. Rescued by the Takashimamaru, they finally arrived in the land of Teru. Abandoning us two, treating us as nuisances, hiding in the shade of trees, and muttering to themselves—the anger I felt when I heard that! Takahime-sama is so distant. To bring us all the way here and then to cast us aside is too much. Had I known she was such an Okata, I would never have followed her. Even if I regret it, there is no help for it.'

Tsunehiko: 'Meeting again on the way, facing the Mirror Pond, carried to the Kakehashi Goten, and having our lives saved once more, while we were spouting spiteful remarks and being despised, we ran out of the mansion, crossed the mountains of Arina, and in the wilderness of Argentina, under the poplars, we encountered the incarnation of the god of tree flowers. There, she underwent a complete conversion, crossed the river, traversed the wilderness, and went over the sea. To save Takayorihime, we wandered through the endless forests and, in Morubando, encountered a great monster. In the midst of trembling with fear, shining down from Boshigatake,'

Tsunehiko: 'By the spiritual light of Riu and Kiu, our lives were finally saved. We crossed over to the southern forest, and on the summit of Boshigatake, we met the great divine beings, Kotoyoriwake and Kuniyoribake. We faced the mansion of Uzu, and upon hearing of the marriage between the youngest princess and the divine Mikoto of Kuniyoriwake, we suddenly became angry. Boasting of the lineage of our own Mitama, we spoke down to the teacher of Kotoyoriwake as if he were an inferior. We visited Kuniyoriwake and the other executives, and though we interfered with various trifles, in the end, we broke our pride and were allowed to join this banquet, where you have sung for us.'

Tsunehiko: 'Nevertheless, everyone, please do not think of Takahime as having a wicked heart. The sincerity of Takahime-sama is solely for the sake of the way of the Lord Ōkami and for the sake of this world; that is why she said such things. I have followed her for a long time, and I know the heart of Takahime-sama well, as if reflected in the mirror of Naozumi. I do not know if you will be displeased by my saying this, but I, this Tsunehiko, am neutral, and I state the Kototama of fairness and selflessness before you all, without fear or hesitation. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, may the spirits be blessed, and may you see, hear, and correct all things directly.'

Tsunehiko: 'Takahime-sama is single-minded, a lump of pure sincerity, and that is why she speaks as she does. Oh, everyone, please correct your pronouncements with a broad and great Naohi. I, Tsunehiko, here represent the heart of Takahime and swear to state that there is no mistake in this. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, may the spirits be blessed. The Kamuzukasa of Kuniyoriwake, you are holding the auspicious ceremony of union with the youngest princess. Please, forever and ever, remain in the capital of Uzu, let the divine majesty of the gods shine, and protect the people in peace and tranquility. Kannagara, I pray before the presence of the gods.'

Tsunehiko: 'I offer my congratulations before the presence of the gods.'
