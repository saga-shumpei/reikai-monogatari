Chapter 26: 若の Bay [941]

若の浦〔941〕

§ 1

Autumn has gradually grown high, / and upon the mountains in all directions, / while listening to the voices of the stags of Sahohime / weaving brocade, / a couple whose hearts are deeply attuned, / Kunitamawake and Tamanohime, / together with Komahiko-sama, / seen off by a great number of believers, / holding aloft the sacred sphere of the jewel, / leaving behind the mansion standing / at the foot of Mount Futatabisan, / cherishing a slight lingering regret, / passing through the forest of Ikuta, / day upon night, they arrived at Waka no Ura in Kii Province.

Waka no Ura was formerly called Toyomi no Ura. Since Kunitamawake-no-mikoto offered up the sphere of the jewel, and enshrined the Mitama of Wakahimegiminomikoto by hanging it upon the sphere of the jewel in an island of Wadanaka where camphor trees grew lush and dense, Toyomi no Ura came to be renamed Waka no Ura. This island was named Tamatsumejima.

The significance of calling it Tamatsume is the meaning of burying the jewel firmly in the ground, building a shrine upon it, and protecting it eternally. Now this island of Tamatsumejima has become connected to the mainland, and is renamed Tamatsukishima.

Around this area, extremely huge cedar trees and camphor trees grew thick and lush all over the earth. When it came to thick camphor trees, there were some whose trunk circumference measured over a hundred jo. Cedars with trunk circumferences of thirty or fifty jo also grew without number. Since it was the country where the most gigantic trees grew lush among all the islands of Jitentōshima, it had been called Ki no Kuni (the Country of Trees) since the Age of the Gods.

It is said that deities such as Ōyakihiko no Kami were born from the crotch of such a large tree. Also, the deity of the Age of the Gods called Kimata no Kami is a divine person who appeared from the spirit of a large tree.

In modern times, very large trees have decreased, but in ancient times, extremely huge trees grew in great numbers not only in Ki no Kuni but also in various regions. Just as plant fibers produce insects through the action of fermentation, and those insects hatch to generate beetle-like beetle tribes, in ancient times it was not uncommon for human beings to be born through the fermentation action of wind, water, and fire using the fibers of large trees. Also, monkeys and the like were generated in quite large numbers.

Calling tengu tree spirits means that they are the souls of trees, and they are a kind of animal generated from the vital spirits of trees. Some tengu resemble the human body, and some resemble bird tribes. Even down to modern times, gigantic trees are called perches for these tengu, and in some regions they are feared greatly. Even in the present age, in the large trees of great forests, quite a large number of kinds of tengu are being generated.

When relating such things, modern pundits and botanists will likely dismiss them with a laugh as the dream tales of a foolish person and pay no heed, but nevertheless, everything between heaven and earth is mysterious. I assert definitively that the spiritual power of the gods can by no means be understood by the intellect of today's so-called civilized persons.

Now, Kunitamawake and Tamanohime built a shrine on this island, offered and enshrined the precious sphere of the jewel, and served and worshipped it as the Great Deity Wakahime, and built a spacious hall nearby, from where they expanded the teachings of Izuruhaya across the whole of Ki no Kuni, needless to say, as well as to Ise, Shima, Owari, Yamato, and Izumi provinces.

Kunitamawake built a palace, enshrined the jewel, chanted the Amatsu Norito, and sang a sacred song. That song:

Kunitamawake: 'The divine land where the morning sun shines straight, / the wondrous land illuminated by the evening sun, / at the very ends of Onogorojima, / amidst the rough waves dashing and coming, / the wondrous island floating within, / the bedrock at the bottom is deeply rooted, / reaching even to the bottom of the Ryūgū Palace, / made firm with the myriad-fathom rocks, / this wondrous island is the firm and unchanging foundation / of the divine land, indeed! / By the sacred word of Sumerookami, / the sacred sphere of the jewel / which appeared from the Ryūkyū Islands, / now here firmly erecting the great pillars of the palace, / with the chigi raised high in Takamagahara, / serving and worshipping it, / to enshrine it eternally for this joyful day! / In this divine land, as long as this jewel / remains tranquil and abides, / Onogorojima will become even firmer,

and the waves will grow calm and settle, / and the common people, month by month and day by day, / like the stars in the heavenly august sky, / and numerous as the sand grains on the beach, / will go on prospering, oh divine land! / Whether the morning sun shines or is clouded, / whether the moon fills or wanes, / whether the great earth is immersed in mud, / this wondrous island hiding the sacred sphere of the jewel / will never drown in water nor burn in fire, / becoming the protector of the land, / Kunitamawake and Tamanohime, / may the achievements of our service / endure for a thousand and eight thousand generations! / Ah, wondrous deities, / may your august spirits shine and prosper, / the Izuruhaya protected by Sumerookami / without a single dark corner in all the lands under heaven.'

原文（近代文語）

秋も漸く高くして 四方の山辺に佐保姫の 錦織出し小男鹿の 妻恋ふ声を聞き乍ら 心あうたる夫婦連れ 国玉別や玉能姫 駒彦さまと諸共に 数多の信者に送られて 球の玉をば捧持しつ 再度山の山麓に たちたる館を後にして 少しは名残を惜しみつつ 生田の森をくぐりぬけ 夜を日についで紀の国の 若の浦へと着きにける。

若の浦は昔は豊見の浦といつた。国玉別命が球の玉を捧じ、樟樹鬱蒼として茂れる和田中の一つ島に稚姫君命の御霊を球の玉に取りかけ斎祀つてより、豊見の浦はここに若の浦と改称する事となつたのである。此の島を玉留島と名づけられた。

玉留といふ意義は玉を固く地中に埋め、其上に神社を建てて永久に守るといふ意味である。今は此玉留島は陸続きとなつて、玉津島と改称されてゐる。

此辺りは非常に巨大なる杉の木や楠が大地一面に繁茂してゐた。太い楠になると、幹の周囲百丈余りも廻つたのがあつた。杉も亦三十丈、五十丈の幹の周囲を有するものは数限りもなく生えてゐた。自転倒島に於て最も巨大なる樹木の繁茂せし国なれば、神代より木の国と称へられてゐたのである。

大屋比古の神などは此大木の股よりお生れになつたといふ事である。また木股の神といふ神代の神も大木の精より現れた神人である。

近代は余り大木は少くなつたが、太古は非常に巨大なる樹木が木の国のみならず、各地にも沢山に生えてゐたものである。植物の繊維が醗酵作用によつて虫を生じ、其の虫は孵化して甲虫の如き甲虫族を発生する如く、古は大木の繊維により風水火の醗酵作用によつて、人が生れ出た事も珍しくない。又猿などは随分沢山に発生したものである。

天狗を木精といふのは木の魂といふ事であつて樹木の精魂より発生する一種の動物である。天狗は人体に似たのもあり、或は鳥族に似たのもある。近代に至つても巨大なる樹木は之を此天狗の止まり木と称へられ地方によつては非常に恐れられてゐる所もある。現代に於ても大森林の大樹には天狗の種類が可なり沢山に発生しつつあるのである。

斯の如き事を口述する時は、現代の理学者や植物学者は、痴人の夢物語と一笑に付して顧みないであらうが、併し天地の間はすべて不可思議なものである。到底今日の所謂文明人士の智嚢では神の霊能力は分るものではない事を断言しておく。

さて国玉別、玉能姫は此島に社を造りて、球の宝玉を捧按し、之を稚姫君の大神と斎祀り、傍に広殿を建て、ここにありて三五教の御教を木の国一円はいふも更なり伊勢、志摩、尾張、大和、和泉方面まで拡充したのである。

国玉別は宮殿を造り玉を納めて天津祝詞を奏上し、祝歌を歌ふ。其歌、

国玉別『朝日のたださす神の国 夕日のひてらす珍の国 自凝島のいや果てに 打寄せ来る荒波の 中に浮べる珍の島 下津磐根はいや深く 竜宮の底まで届くなり 千引の岩もて固めたる 此珍島は神国の 堅磐常磐の固めぞや 皇大神の御言もて 琉球島より現れし 球の御玉を今ここに 大宮柱太知りて 高天原に千木高く 仕へまつりて永久に 納むる今日の目出たさよ 此神国に此玉の 鎮まりゐます其限り 自凝島はいや固く

Iyu, the divine man who has traveled widely,

Being extremely peaceful and calm,

Rejoicing in the reign of Miroku,

To say nothing of birds and beasts,

Even down to a single blade of grass,

Let each and every one attain its proper place.

The living spirit of Wakahimegimi,

Entrusted to the round jewel,

Settling in the divine land of trees,

The surging waves of hostility,

Blow them away with Ibukihara.

May Onokorojima forever,

Flourishing and prospering more and more,

Be a paradise of peace where the divine man

Rejoices and delights,

Preserving the state of the heavenly pure land

Everlastingly and eternally,

Flourishing for a thousand generations like green pines,

In the rising and lying of bamboo joints in this world,

Cleansing our hearts and purifying our bodies,

May we serve as a wedded couple.

May Komahiko's heart be pure,

Submitting before the presence of Kami,

Plum blossoms whose blessings also unfold,

A favorable wind scented all at once,

The foundation of the divine age of pines,

Even firmer than the roots of camphor trees,

Straight and upright like a grove of cedars,

Protect us, O Kamunagara.

I pray before the presence of Kami.

I pray before the presence of Kami.'

At last becoming the princess of Tamano,

By the august words of Kototamawake,

Offering precious jewels from the islands of Ryukyu,

Auspiciously and for the second time

At the divine hall at the foot of the mountain,

Returned to the forest of Ikuta.

Here, the husband and wife in the blessing of cohabitation,

Bathed in grace, morning and evening,

Firmly guarding the divine treasures of Ryu and Kyu while residing,

By the august words of Tamateruhiko and Tamateruhime,

Of the noble deities,

Handing over the hall of the forest of Ikuta

To the ministry of Takahime,

Leaving the jewel of Ryu behind,

And holding the divine treasure of Kyu,

Crossing over to the divine land of trees,

Upon the everlasting and unchanging bedrock

Drifting in the great ocean plain,

Building a palace residence solemnly,

Commanding us to serve

As the august spirit of Wakahimegimi.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara,

The august words of Kami cannot be disobeyed.

Leaving behind the hall where we had grown accustomed to living,

Looking back and coming far away

To this island country, when I look,

It is a wondrous country beyond imagination.

The colors of the trees are lustrous and beautiful,

Fields and mountains weave brocade looms,

The flow of the rivers murmurs softly,

Playing the music of nature,

It is like the heavenly pure land.

Especially in this venerable island,

Building a wondrous shrine,

To protect it everlastingly,

Is truly the feeling of meeting the spring

When the udumbara flower blooms.

Now once again realizing

The depth of the august grace of Sumerōkami,

Tears of gratitude fall gently,

Oh, the joy that cannot be uttered with the mouth.

O Wakahimegimi Ōmikami,

May your august soul settle in this island

Everlastingly and eternally,

And protect the body and soul of all people universally, Kamunagara.

Earnestly before the presence of Kami,

Tamanohime prays.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara,

May your august spirit be blessed and tranquil.'

So chanting to the end, it came to pass that she would settle in this island forever and devote herself to the divine work.
