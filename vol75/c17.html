Chapter 17: 西方の Journey [1911]

西方の旅〔1911〕

§ 1

From ancient times, when literary men have attempted to explain the historical facts following the Tenchikaibiyaku, they have employed two methods: the one was the shiyanson, and the other was the roman. Furthermore, the shiyanson was an intersection of history and Kūsau, while the roman was a historical element embellished with a more dense Kūsau. Such as the poetry of Dante, or the Taketorimonogatari, they all took the form of the roman. Among them, the shiyanson seemed to occupy the great majority, but reaching down to Kōsei, scholars translated them into Sanbuntai and came to publish them widely. However, needless to say regarding Wagakuni, even among the historical tales widely circulated in Taiseishiyokoku, many deal with heroes, Kami, and Akuma; and books dealing with the Kototama, like our described 'Tenshō Chizui', are utterly nonexistent. In short, Kototama learning is profound and subtle, completely impossible for the brains of mediocre scholars to chew and digest, and being a wondrous academic principle which one cannot even glimpse in a dream, it has been neglected until today. With a brain of superficial knowledge, a shiyanson or roman taking Kototama learning as its subject is an absolute impossibility. Boldly and fearlessly, based upon the functioning of the Kototama which is the ultimate origin of the great cosmos, from the formation of the universe to the divine activities of the Kami, I borrow the form of the shiyanson and now intend to state its outline here.

Being a tale of an era when the heavens and earth were still vast and dim, and the brilliance of fixing and completing the creation had not yet shone, intended to explain the world of will and thought arising from the wondrous utility of the Kototama, it must be that most things will be felt as mysterious by the eyes and ears of modern people. All scholars say that man was created in the image of Kami. Therefore, there are many people who imagine that Kami all possess the shape of human beings. Nevertheless, it is a clear truth that due to the stirring of will and thought, the primordial world cannot maintain a fixed and immutable form. There are Kami of dragon bodies, there are Kami of beast bodies, there are also Kami taking the form of mountains, and there are serpent bodies possessing more than ten heads, being of a thousand postures and ten thousand forms. Why is this so? Because it is the manifestation of the form of will and thought itself. It is for this reason that the human countenance is called the index of the spirit. However, today, since the human form has become fixed, one's body is not changed by will and thought. It has only come to pass that changes are brought to the countenance, making it no longer possible to easily know one's character by looking from the surface. The rampant running of Akuma, such as having the exterior like a bodhisattva and the interior like a yaksha, gives convenience to Akuma since both good and evil have come to possess the identical form. When one pays careful attention, although the form is human, in its countenance, in its voice, and in its action, it is something that displays the state of Akuma, yet from the eyes of ordinary human beings, it has come to pass that the good or evil of their spiritual state cannot be easily glimpsed. From the eyes of a true human being who treads the path of righteous Kami, purifies the soul day and night, and has attained wisdom, illumination, and perception, it is possible to easily see through this, but failing not to fall into the proverb of the world of a thousand blind men and one seeing man, the majority of people are deceived and fall into misfortune. Here, the great Lord Ōkami, having lowered the divine pillar of Miroku to the ground, spreading the righteous teachings throughout the realm, awakening the eyes of humanity, making them shine, annihilating the rampancy of Akuma, and thereby intending to establish the divine age of Miroku, is truly a matter of wondrous and precious gratitude.

Here Taigen Etsu no Kami At the sacred place of Tamamo-no-Yama Leaving behind two female Kami Offering parting words at the Tamano Palace Having finished proclaiming, leisurely Straddling a steed, the sound of bells So valorous, by a hundred Kami Guarded at His august rear Descending step by step, following The slope paths right and left Falling down from the waterfall of a thousand strands Undergoing misogi in the clear waters of the valley Giving water to the horse, and into the wilderness Proceeding valiantly, He goes forth Oh, the valor of His august figure! A hundred Kami Serving at His august rear From His august back where the divine light shines In awe and reverence, they go forth Creating the lands of the Shibi Heavenly Realm

原文（近代文語）

古来文学者等が、天地開闢以後の史実を説明せむとするに当り、二つの方法を用ゐて来た。其一つは史詩であり、其一つは伝奇物語であつた。而して史詩は歴史と空想との交錯であり、伝奇物語は史的要素を、より濃厚な空想で賦彩したものである。ダンテの詩の如き、又は竹取物語の如きは、総て伝奇物語の形式を取つたものである。中にも史詩は其大多数を占めて居たやうであるが、後世に至つて学者達が散文体に翻訳し、之を広く発表するに至つたものである。併しながら我国は言ふも更なり、泰西諸国に流布さるる史的物語にも、英雄、神、悪魔等を取扱つて居るもの多く、吾が述ぶる『天祥地瑞』の如く、言霊を取扱つた書籍は絶無である。要するに言霊学は深遠微妙にして、凡庸学者の脳髄に到底咀嚼し能はず、又夢にも窺知するを得ざる玄妙なる学理なるが故に、今日迄閑却されて居たのである。一知半解の頭脳をもつては、到底言霊学を題材とする史詩又は伝奇物語は絶対不可能である。私は大胆にも不敵にも、大宇宙の極元たる言霊の活用に基づき、宇宙の成立より、神々の御活動に就いて、史詩の形式を借り、弥々茲にその大要を述べむとするものである。

未だ天地茫漠として修理固成の光輝かざりし時代の物語にして、言霊の妙用より発する意志想念の世界を説明せむとするものなれば、現代人の目より耳より不可思議に感ずる事最も多かるべし。人間は神の形に造られたりと、総ての学者は言つて居る。故に神は総て人間の形をなしたるものと想像して居る人々が多いのである。然れども意志想念の情動によりて、最初の世界は一定不変なる形式を保つ事の出来ないのは明瞭なる真理である。竜体の神もあれば獣体の神もあり、又山岳の形をなせる神もあり、十数箇の頭を有する大蛇身もあり、千態万様である。何故なれば、意志想念其ものの形の現れであるからである。人間の面貌は精神の索引なりと称ふるも此理由である。併しながら今日にては人間の形態定まりたれば、意志想念によりて其体を変ぜず。唯面貌に変化を来すのみとなつたので、表面より見ては其性格を容易に知る事が出来なくなつて居る。外面如菩薩、内心如夜叉の如き悪魔の横行するのも、善悪共に同一形態を備ふるに至りしより、悪魔に便宜を与へて居るのである。細心に注意する時は、形態は人間なれども、其面貌に、声音に、動作に、悪魔の状態を現ずるものなれども、一般の人間の目よりは、其精神状態の善悪を容易に窺知する事が出来ないやうになつたのである。正しき神の道を踏み、日夜に魂を清め、智慧証覚を得たる真人間の眼よりは、容易に之を観破する事が出来るのであれども、盲千人目明き一人の世の譬に漏れず、大多数の人は欺かれ禍ひにかかるものである。茲に主の大神は、ミロクの神柱を地上に下して、正しき教を天下に布き施し人類の眼を覚させ、光らせ、悪魔の跳梁を絶滅し、以てミロクの神世を樹立せむとし給へるこそ、有難き尊き限りなれ。

§ 2

The tale of the journey of the birth of the august child,

Sitting in solemn posture within the Suimeikaku,

Causing the Shinonomeshiyain to hold the brush,

With a leaping and eager heart I go on to recount it.

Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara,

Blessed by the divine Mitama,

May this Monogatari become ever more vast,

Ever more detailed, to serve as a mirror for future ages,

To serve as salt,

To serve as flowers, bestowing light upon the Mitama of the people of the world,

I humbly pray that you guard and bless us.
