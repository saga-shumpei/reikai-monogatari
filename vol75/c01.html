Chapter 1: 禊の Kami 事 [1895]

禊の神事〔1895〕

§ 1

In our Shinkoku, there exists the Shinji of Misogi transmitted from the sacred ages down to Konnichi, originally brought forth in the Taikiyochiu by Amanominehio no Kami, who was born from the Kototama of Su. This Shinji is a primary matter that reaches unto Konnichi, without the Kamigami of Shibitenkai neglecting it even for a single Ichinichi. Broadly speaking, Misogi becomes the governance of the state and the pacification of all under heaven; narrowly speaking, it is the basis of cultivating the self and regulating the family. Thus, various methods of Misogi have also been transmitted. Gojin intend from this point to briefly describe various kinds of Misogi.

Among the events concerning Misogi, the most essential Shinji is the event of Furutama. Although there are various methods for this, in ordinary Baahi, one combines both palms in the shape of a cross in front of the region of the navel, and while invoking the name of Kami with all the strength of one's entire body, being self-aware of one's fundamental spirit, it is an event of shaking and moving the whole body vigorously, fiercely, and continuously for tens of minutes to even several hours. In the Misogi of the age of Kami, all the Kamigami respectfully invoked the august name of Amanominehio no Kami, but in modern times, in our Misogi, we respectfully invoke the august name of Amenominakanushi no Ōkami.

By means of this event of Furutama, while suppressing and locking down delusional thoughts and evil desires inherent in the mind, one controls the oppositional and isolated activities of each part of the body, and performs a unified activity of the whole body centered upon one's fundamental spirit. During Misogi, one reduces daily meals, eating nothing at all except for a bowl of gruel, three pickled plums, and a small amount of sesame salt in the morning and evening; this is a preparation entirely to diminish the whole body's resistance against one's fundamental spirit (the true guardian deity) and to conveniently plan for the unification of body and mind. However, there is no worry that the body will become fatigued, diseased, or fall into hardship on account of that reduction of food. Because the internal fundamental spirit increases the degree of excitement and tension, vigor instead fills the entire body, the head becomes calm and lucid, the whole body feels refreshed, and the mood of Kami drifts about. Since there is no sin or impurity to be ashamed of upon introspection, even if tens of millions of formidable enemies were to appear, one does not fear them; the spirit to breakthrough oceans and high mountains and to swallow and spit out the universe bursts forth, and even though one eats nothing besides a bowl of gruel, one feels no further hunger. It becomes just as if one's heart has revived in the days of old in the age of Kami, leading one to clearly perceive the true nature of the Japanese people.

Next, there is the Shinji of Misogi called the Heavenly Bird-Boat. This is an event wherein, while yearning upon the grand heroic plan in which the Kamigami of the age of Kami rode upon the Heavenly Bird-Boat and crossed the great ocean plain, one puts strength particularly into the vicinity of the navel, and together with the breath-spirit, repeats the very action of rowing the stern a hundred or a thousand times; not only is the exercise itself valuable, but through it the practice of breath-spirit arts can also be accomplished, and it is a Misogi in which the hearts of the multitude harmonize as one before one even knows or is conscious of it.

Next, there is the Misogi of Yūken, which is an event of holding a posture of standing firm while brandishing Amenonuhoko, chanting the divine names of Ikutama, Tarutama, Tamadumarutama, and Ōkotoninushi no Mikoto. Namely:

Firstly, standing upright, grasping the belt firmly with both left and right hands, inserting the thumbs into the belt, thrusting the abdomen forward while filling the entire body with strength and chanting 'Ikutama', and arching the torso backward,

Secondly, chanting 'Tarutama', filling the entire body with strength and lifting both shoulders, and thereafter, putting sufficient strength into the hips, abdomen, and both feet to lower both shoulders,

Thirdly, chanting 'Tamadumarutama', further filling both feet with strength to stand upright on the tips of both toes, and thereafter strongly putting strength into the entire body to lower both heels.

Fourthly, stepping the left foot one step diagonally forward, the left hand grasping the belt just as it is, the right hand straightening and pointing the second and third fingers side by side while bending the other three fingers (symbolizing Amenonuhoko) and holding it up to the crown of the head, this being an event holding courage and resolve surpassing that of a serious duel. In short, the Misogi of Yūken is the posture of the association of Kami and self as one.

Next, there is the Misogi of Yūdzume. Yūdzume refers to the vocalization of conquering Magatsumi and guiding and deifying them as good, with Kami and self being as one. Yūdzume is performed by uttering the sound 'Iets', stepping down with the right foot onto the left foot, and at the same exact time, slashing the air diagonally with Amenonuhoko brandished at the crown of the head, striking it straight down to the left side of the waist; no sooner is this done than one further utters the vocalization 'Eits', and while keeping the right elbow pressed against the chest side, holds the forearm upright, and thereafter further holds Amenonuhoko at the crown of the head, repeating this continuously three times through front and back. Striking down with 'Iets' as Kami and self being as one is the protocol of overawing and chastising the devils in the surrounding four quarters, and striking up conversely with 'Eits' is for the purpose of making the devils repent and be resurrected. Namely, to cause even ogres to turn into Kami and disasters to turn into blessings, absorbing and assimilating them so as to make them together one with Kami and self, this is the instruction of the Great Ancestral Deity, and is the expansive grand ideal of Kami and man.

原文（近代文語）

我が神国には、大虚中にスの言霊より生れ出で給ひし天之峯火夫の神の、聖代より今日に至るまで伝来せる禊の神事あり。此の神事は紫微天界の神々と雖も一日も怠り給ふ事なく、今日に及べる主要の事柄なり。抑禊は大にしては治国平天下となり、小にしては修身斉家の基本たり。而して禊にも種々の方式伝はれり。吾人は是より諸種のみそぎに就て略述せむとす。

禊に関する行事の内にて最も至要なる神事は振魂の行事なり。之には種々の方式あれども、普通の場合には、両掌を臍あたりの前方に於て十字形に組み合せ、渾身の力を籠めて神名を称へながら、自己の根本精神を自覚して、盛んに猛烈に数十分乃至数時間連続して全身を振ひ動かす行事なり。神代の禊には神々何れも天之峯火夫の神の御名を称へ奉られたるが、現代にては吾人の禊には天之御中主之大神の御名を称へ奉るなり。

此の振魂の行事に由りて、精神内包の妄念邪想を鎖鎮すると共に、身体各部の反対的孤立的の活動を制御し、自己の根本精神を中心としたる全身の統一的活動を為すなり。禊の間は日々の食事を減じて、朝夕に一合の粥と三粒の梅干、小量の胡麻塩以外一切を食せざるも、全く自己の根本精神（本守護神）に対する全身の抵抗力を減殺し、偏に心身の統一を計るに便ずる用意なり。然るに身体は其減食のために、疲れ又は病み困難に陥るといふ心配はなし。内部の根本精神が興奮緊張の度を増し来る故に、却て元気全身に充足し、頭脳は冷静明快となり、全身爽快にして神の気分漂ふ。内省して疚しき罪穢もなければ、仮令百千万の強敵現はれ来るとも恐れず、大海高山を突破し、宇宙を呑吐する気概勃発して、一合の粥以外に何物をも食せずと雖も、更に飢餓を覚ゆる事なし。恰も自己は神代の昔に蘇りたる心地となり、日本民族の自性を明瞭に感得するに至るなり。

次に天の鳥船と称する禊の神事あり。之は神代の神々が天の鳥船に乗り給ひて大海原を横ぎり給ひし大雄図を偲びつつ、渾身特に臍の辺りに力を込め、気合と共に艫を漕ぐままの動作を百千回反復する行事にして、運動夫れ自身に価値あるのみならず、之に依りて気合術の練習も出来、不知不識の間に衆心の一和する禊なり。

次に雄健の禊あり、生魂、足魂、玉留魂、大国常立之尊の神名を唱へつつ、天之沼矛を振りかざして直立不動の姿勢を構ふる行事なり。即ち、

一に直立して左右の両手を以て帯を堅く握り締め、拇指を帯に差し『生魂』と唱へつつ、力を全身に充足して腹を前方へ突き出し、体躯を後方に反らせ、

二に『足魂』と唱へつつ、力を全身に充足して両肩を挙げ、然る後、腰、腹、両足とに充分の力を込めて両肩を下し、

三に『玉留魂』と唱へつつ、更に力を両足に充足して両の爪先にて直立し、然る後強く全身に力を込めて両の踵を下すなり。

四に左足を一歩斜前方に踏み出し、左手はそのまま帯を握り締め、右手は第二第三指を並立直指し、他の三指は之を屈し（之を以て天之沼矛に象る）之を脳天に構へ、真剣以上の勇気と覚悟とを持する行事なり。要するに雄健の禊は、神我一体聯想の姿勢なり。

次に雄詰の禊あり。雄詰といふは神我一体として、禍津見を征服し、之を善導神化する発声なり。雄詰は「イーエツ」といふ声を発すると共に、右足を左足に踏み付け、同時に脳天に振りかざしたる天之沼矛を斜に空を斬つて、一直線に左の腰元に打ち下すや否や、更に「エーイツ」と発声すると共に、右肘を胸側に着けたる儘前臂を直立し、然る後更に天之沼矛を脳天に構へ、前後に通じて続けさまに三回反復して行ふなり。神我一体として「イーエツ」と打ち込むは、四囲の悪魔を威圧懲戒するの作法にして、之を反対に「エーイツ」と打ち上ぐるは、悪魔を悔悟復活せしむるが為なり。即ち鬼も神と化し、禍も福と化し、之を吸収同化して共に神我一体たらしめむとするが、大祖神の垂示にして、神人の膨脹的大理想なり。

Next, finishing the okorobi, immediately place both palms at the position of the navel, join them vigorously into a cross shape, and thereafter perform abdominal deep breathing three times.

And swallowing the entire final inhalation without exhaling, this is called the divine ritual of ibuki.

At present, the ritual of misogi has lost its origin and its true significance is not transmitted, yet in the main it remains among a portion of Shintoists in such a form.

Even in the Shibitenkai, the misogi ritual was established as the foundation of all things, and Ōmotoakitsuo no Kami and the myriad deities gathered together at the jade spring of Tamanōka to perform misogi, and served in the work of purification (shiubatsu).

Akitsuo no Kami and the other various deities, each fixing their seats at the water's edge of the sacred spring of Tamanōka, chanting sacred poems as they sought to perform the misogi ritual.

The sacred poem of Akitsuo no Kami.

The moon and sun crossing over are reflected in the jade spring; Is not this purity the mind of Kami?

In the jade spring where even the fine sand at the bottom of the water shines, I will wash away all my sins and defilements without a trace.

Understanding the holiness of the divine work of creating the land and giving birth to the children of Kami, I will perform misogi.

By the misogi of the shaking of the spirit (tamafuri), even the fine sand at the bottom of the water Has been shaken forth according to the will of Kami.

How clearly the misogi of the shaking of the spirit of the deities Is reflected here upon the water's bottom!

To solidify the youthful land plain of Manazuru, I will first perform misogi in the jade spring.

The treetops of the ever-verdant pines are reflected green at the bottom of the water, And the waves are calm.

Tracing ripple patterns upon the surface of the wave, what bubbles up Is the breathing of the small fish dwelling at the bottom of the water?

In this pure jade spring, forever How refreshing the fish tribes dwelling there must be!

Being one who descended from the southwestern sky, I think I should like to live in this pure spring.

In days of old, serving as an eel, I yearn for the bottom of the spring's water.

Returning as an eel in this water, forever It seems I have come to wish to dwell.

Repeating things that cannot be attained, how terrifying it is To turn my back upon the divine commission of the Lord God.

Enduring various sufferings and torments, Now I perform misogi in this spring.

My august spirit has grown cloudy, ever so cloudy, and turbid; Would that I might be revived in this pure spring.

Though the work of creating the deities has been finished in its early stages, Is there something weighing upon my heart?

The sacred poem of Tamanōhime no Kami.

Appearing from the fruit of the ever-blessed fragrant tree (tokijiku no kagu no konomi), I am the kami of the tachibana at the water's edge.

May the august spirit (mizumitamashii) be at peace; the spirit of my lord (kimi) is as pure as the jade spring.

Thinking to solidify this land plain by performing misogi, I hold my lord, who so desires, in supreme reverence.

The fragrant fruit upon which the august water and fire of the Lord God fell Became the eighty-fathom-pillar goddess (Yosohashiramine no Kami).

Added to one of the eighty-pillar deities, I regret having lagged behind in the divine work.

Bringing forth one national soul (kunidama) in one land, O Lord God, who opens the age of the gods eternally!

The heart of the Lord Great God, who established one august mihedashiro in one land, Is truly noble.

Being the mihedashiro of this land, I now regret Having fallen behind in the divine work;

The fault of having lagged behind in the divine work of creating the deities Was the clouding of the spirit-line (tamashisen) of my soul.

How could the deity of the national soul (kunidamagami) be born Within the mihedashiro of my clouded spirit-line?

The birth of children by the divine pronouncement of Ikurashiruhime no Kami Must be the grand plan of the Lord Great God.

If Ikurashiruhime no Kami were not present, the national soul deity of Manazuru Would surely not have been born.

I will not resent the august spirit, nor will I resent Ikurashiruhime no Kami, since it is the way of Kami (ainagara).

It was the sin of holding in contempt Those divine works which the Lord God had commissioned.

Amidst my arrogance as the mihedashiro, How cloudy my living soul (ikutama) must have become!

The sacred poem of Ikurashiruhime no Kami.

Appearing as the august spirit of the mountains of Manazuru, I served the divine work without knowing.

Since I am one whose august spirit was born of water and fire, My spirit-line has cleaved unto my lord.

How many times have I reflected upon my heart, Thinking I must give up this forbidden love?

Bound by the thread of affinity of the spirit-line, I conceived a child through the august water and fire of my lord.

Without once touching his august hands or his august skin, Through the true words (makoto) of my lord, I conceived through imagination.

Pardon me, Tamanōhime, for my heart, Morning and evening trembling in awe before the public.

I am ashamed that my thoughts flared up and became dark clouds, Locking away the august sky.

From now on, performing misogi in this spring, I will wash away all my sins and defilements, countless as the grains of sand.

If I am born as a child of the Lord God, my breath and body Will become smoke and ascend to the heavens.

O Tamanōhime no Kami, the child of the gods that is born, Do thou nurture him as thine own child!
