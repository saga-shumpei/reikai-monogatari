Chapter 15: The Episode of Crane の訣別 (Part IV) [1909]

鶴の訣別（四）〔1909〕

§ 1

The Kami of Tamakiharu causes the parting miuta of nagori to be chanted.

Though stretching far away I served as mitomo and now here, how sorrowful to part from the august mitama of Mizu! Not yet content, my heart thinks even now of having served Kimi, and that we part today, though it was joyful. Knowing beforehand that it would be thus, even now I linger, loth to part and full of nagori. Bow down and worship the thriving misugata of Kimi, how full of nagori and sorrowful is today when we part! Parting now, for the sake of Kami, for the sake of the land, from today I think I shall serve in loneliness. Even for one who thinks not to lament and bears it in resignation, now it has become indeed hard to endure! Ah, how delightful! From today onward, O misugata of Kimi, there are surely no more ways to yearn for you! Thinking upon the future of the land of Manazuru, I feel sorrow in a age of Kami without Kimi. May the blessings of the living kototama of Yashimaguni make the yukute of Kimi peaceful! Parting afterwards at Tamamoyama and setting out on tabida, I feel an extra measure of sorrow for Kimi. Though thousands of generations pass, I shall never forget the days when I was embraced by bountiful Kimi. Where Kimi is not, even the yamakaha of the land of Manazuru, down to the tips of the kusaki, do indeed wither! Even the shirakumo today, hesitating at the yamasuso, wander and send forth the miyuki of Kimi. The colors of the yachigusa too seem changed to sight, today when the august mitama of Mizu goes forth on its journey. The land of Nishikata is far away; upon the path that Kimi walks, may even the kumade be free from harm, I pray. Descending from the Hisakata Amatsutakamiya, though Kimi's misugata is no more seen from today, the pure-watered chisuji no taki also roars, as if hushing itself to send Kimi off. Shaking the fifty-hundred-ringing suzu, both kami and koma are today sending off the miyuki of Kimi. Bearing the flawless figure of Kimi upon its back, the heavenly steed neighs with brave spirit. Awe-inspiring is Kimi, who created all things of the visible world and finished the making of the land. Though the kunihara is still youthful, where Kimi walks the hoofprints will surely cause flowers to bloom and fruit to ripen. The sound of the hooves of the advancing koma rings clear, and the noble kototama that echoes forth— Opening the curtains of the shirakumo, Tsukiyomi has revealed her pale misugata. Since Kimi was born into the mitama of Tsukiyomi, the world of nubatama shall be forever no more. Upon the youthful kunihara that we tread, the mochichigusa will hold up flowers and wait for Kimi. Gathered thickly and growing in luxuriant strength, the trees and grasses of the ashibiki mountain fields also look withered. Parting from a person bound by ties and setting out on tabida, I think upon the manly strength of Kimi's heart. Entrusting the noble miko to the Kami of Amatama, go forth in peace, O Kami of Akatsunoo! Both grass and tree change the color of their leaf-tips, as if lamenting the departure of Kimi. The number of taduru crying with the sound of the matsukaze blowing refreshingly across the mountain ridge. In the pure courtyard the iewadori cries, and the blossoms of the shirayume have scattered in the spring mountain wind. For my Kimi whom I served morning and evening, the thoughts of today when we part are far-flung. I pray for the divine achievements of the Mizu Mitama, which returns to the Hisakata heaven.

The Kami of Yuihiai causes the miuta to be chanted.

Alas, alas, does the august mitama of Mizu make this day the very limit, and set forth upon a journey? Even the departure of Kami, which is awe-inspiring to even speak of, I reverently celebrate and revere! Enduring various sufferings, Kimi who created the land of Manazuru—how awe-inspiring is He! To the very limit that the power of reisen exists, Kimi, who exhausted it to give birth to the land of the Kami— even if I were to weep, there was no way to stop it! I will simply send Kimi off with a brave heart. While the flower-scented spring of Yayoi deepens, the kamiyama is lonely as we part from Kimi. The voice of the kalavinka is heard as though veiled in haze, and even the wind blowing through the pines is muffled. Today, which announces an eternal parting at this sacred place upon the mountain top, is sorrowful! As for the future growth of still-youthful Chiyozuruhime, I shall guard her; let your heart be at peace. Living on and on, in the endless life of the heavenly realm, may the life of Chiyozuru flourish ever more! If Kimi goes, the land of Manazuru will be lonely; yet I trust in the newly born miko as my strength. Wrapped morning and evening in thin garments of shiratae, her life will grow and flourish day by day. Without placing the growth of Chiyozuruhime in your deep-seated heart, set out upon your journey! I too will urge my koma onward to the border of the land of Nishikata, and follow and serve you. I have heard it said that at the border of the land of Nishikata, the Hinodenkawa flows wide. Crossing over the roaring, rushing rapids of water, Kimi will go, crossing mountains and crossing fields! Since they are koma bound together by the might of fire and water, the river rapids of Hinode will also be crossed in peace! Whipping the isukwashi white steed of heaven, I think upon the brave figure of that day when you will cross. The fish tribe too will float upon the surface of the water to welcome the koma of Kimi in the river of Hinode. When I view the land, the land of Nishikata is veiled in the depths of clouds and cannot be seen; my heart is far away.

§ 2

The vast and boundless sky, pure and clear, / How well it fits the August Progress of Kimi. / Both moon and sun shine brilliantly, illuminating / The forefront of Kimi as He descends upon Mount Tamamoyama. / Dispelling the darkness of nubatama and advancing, / Truly, Kimi is the God of Light. / Treading across the reed-grown plains of Yoshiashi, / May there be no misfortune on the path He shall advance! / The sounds of insects ring clear on both sides of the road, / Let us revere and praise the August Progress of Kimi. / Going forth, the day when He shall stand / Upon the riverbanks of Hinatagaha is truly awaited! / The Hinatagaha River lying below / The distant sky where floating clouds play— / To solidify the land unto the end of a myriad years, / Kimi is He who expends His sacred heart. / When I think of the eight-cloud-risen Shibi-Tenkai as the foundation / Of Sumeramikuni, how noble it is! / The Lord God, unto the end of a myriad years, / Shall eternally protect the land of the Lord.

Tautau flows the rapid current of the Hinatagaha River, / And Kimi shall soon cross its rapids, how valiant! / Since it is a divine age where good subdues evil, / O Kimi, advance with a tranquil heart! / The journey of Kimi, who permanently secures and vitalizes / The childish plains of Moromoro— / Swelling and expanding without end, the vast land— / How awe-inspiring is the divine work of Kimi in securing it! / As far as the eye can see, eightfold clouds rise and crowd; / Wicked gods, I hear, swarm into the endless land. / O burdened Kimi, serving in the divine work / Of land-creation to solidify the eternal foundation! / The joy of having been born into such an age / Is that I am one who serves in the divine work of land-creation. / O sacred mountain, from this day forth, I have no way / To gaze upon the august figure of Kimi, who is so majestic! / Unto the ends of the land of Koshu, the divine exploits of Kimi / Shall shine across through the changing moon and sun. / Above and below, right and left, aligning in unison, / Kimi is He who opens up the peaceful land of Urayasukuni.

With the life of the eternal living Kototama, / He is Kimi whom hundreds of gods protect. / Fields and mountains are especially bright today, / Under the moon and sun that guard the departure of Kimi. / Destined to become the renown of all ages, the Mizu-no-Migami's / Heartfelt concern of this very day— / The voices of a hundred birds have grown valiant, / As if they had resigned themselves to the departure of Kimi. / Not even knowing the distinction between night and day, He advances; / The exploits of Kimi are a mirror to the world. / When I think of the famed solemnity / Of Amatsu-Takamiya, Kimi becomes all the more noble. / Intricately binding together the water and fire of heaven and earth, / How majestic is the Mizu-no-Migami!'

The Goddess of Umasu sings an august poem: 'Having completed the birth of the beautiful land, the Mototsu-Kami-no-Kuni, / There is no way to stop Kimi who now departs. / Though I think to resign myself to it, I cannot bear it, / How sorrowful I am at this departure today! Kimi's radiant face has grown clouded, / Now I shall not speak of it again and repeat my words. I desire a god who will become the help / Of Tamanohime, who serves eternally at Tamanomiya. The far-reaching sky is pure and clear, / And I send off Kimi's departure in this purity. Thinking to serve as long as the jewel-string of life, / Kimi, whom I relied upon, is now about to set forth! With my whole heart, unto the furthest ends of endless ages, / I had thought to dedicate myself and serve. Though spring departs and summer gradually approaches, / How lonely I find myself to be! Sending Him forth as far as the Hinatagaha River, / Which lies at the border of the western land— The sound of the wind blowing through the pines is also still, / Do even the pines regret the departure of Kimi? Sweeping away the clouds and mists blocking the four quarters, / The sun and moon shine as Kimi departs. Leaving behind the august wife of young grass, / I muse upon Kimi who is about to set out on a journey. The sound of the bells rings clear, and the steed / Gallops forth in eager readiness! At the summit of this high mountain where the pheasant cries, / I think of a thousand years and shed tears. How regrettable it is to part with Kimi, / Who sets forth to the distant plains where white clouds trail! Feeling my strength suddenly fail me, / As I send Kimi off to the border of the land— Since I hear that in the western land there are haunts of wicked gods, / My heart is troubled all the more. Though the flow of the waterfall's rapids is fierce, / Do not fear it, O Kimi of majestic divine majesty! Unto the end of thousands of generations, / Today shall become a tale for the gods to recount. Down to the very last of the descendants born, / We shall pass on this parting of today by telling of it.'

§ 3

By the blessing of the wondrous Ikukototama, the land of Manazuru has become bountiful and fertile; blessed by the pure and radiant Kototama of Mizu, the land of Manazuru has solidified. With not even the shadow of Tsumikega upon my lord Kimi, there is nothing to hinder the path wherever he goes. Warming Ame and Chi through the interaction of Iki and Iki, the Mizumitama has given birth to Miko. How noble it is that Futahashira, uniting Iki and Iki, gave birth to the Kunitama Kami! Purple clouds, overlapping one upon another, have enfolded the mountain of Tamamo. Is it a dream? One hundred days have passed since the day the Mizumitama appeared. It has become a lovely mountain, and the bottom of Tamanōmi has dried up to become the Nazohe. How the merit of Kimi, who assists with such noble Kototama, is a treasure of Mikuni! The journey of my lord Kimi, who advances treading through the yoshiashi, shall surely be filled with great blessings.

I too was born into the Kototama of U, serving humbly the Mizumitama. Appointed as the master of the birth of Ametsuchi, how magnificent is the virtue of the Mizumitama! Through the Kototama of the seventy-five voices of the Mizumitama, he created the Manazurukuni. The land of Manazuru, which had swollen up while the earth was still tender, has gradually begun to solidify. Glorious is the age of the gods, where even to the unseen edges of the land, all is moistened by the blessings of the Kototama. The Kamigami rejoice and make merry, celebrating the thousand-year reign of Manazurukuni. I shall serve so as not to defile the land of Manazuru, which has been solidified thus far. Even if I grieve, it is of no avail, for Kimi is one who assists in accordance with the Mimune of the Lord God. From the day the Mizumitama appeared, trees and grasses alike have increased their light and begun to flourish. Do they celebrate the flourishing age of the gods? Manazuru too sings clearly like the pines of Tokiha.

Now already the voices of the Kariyōbinga, of Manazuru, and even the crowing of domestic fowl ring out clearly. All the birds too, realizing the departure of Kimi, must be taking courage for the sake of the divine nation. Protecting every corner of the distant and near lands, how noble is the achievement of Kimi! You hundred flowers, shed your fragrance along the path of Kimi, who departs on his journey at the close of tranquil spring. It is the glory of all eternity of Mount Tamamo; today's parting is for the sake of the creation of the land. The hundred birds sing of a thousand autumns, and the myriad grasses celebrate the age of the gods, rustling in the wind. Serving as attendants in the divine work of creating the land, today is indeed a sad parting! Parting the clouds of the vast sky, may the heavenly sun illuminate the path of Kimi.'

Akitsuwo no Kami, from atop his horse, turns toward the assembled Kamigami and chants a divine waka: 'How awe-inspiring are the hearts of the hundred gods; today upon this departure I feel deeply humbled. Though my form is not seen here, my spiritual thread shall forever remain tranquil and protect the land. Shall I send down the kami who serves morning and evening at the shrine of Tamano to serve as a memento? O Tamanohime, let your heart be at peace; for your sake, the assisting kami shall surely descend now.'

Thus finishing his song, while sorrowing at the parting with the Kamigami, he whips his horse and descends quietly, turning right and left along the slanting slopes of Mount Tamamo. The hundred gods, each wishing to attend him as far as the border of the land, mount their horses and serve before his august presence. However, Kuninakahiko no Kami, protecting Ikushirohime no Kami and wishing to nurture Chiyozuruhime no Kami, leaps upon a swift horse and turns back, while Tamanohime no Kami remains on Mount Tamamo to serve intimately in the august shrine—how awe-inspiring it is.

奇びなる生言霊の幸ひに 真鶴の国土はうまらになりぬ 澄みきらふ瑞の言霊幸ひて 真鶴の国土は固まりにけり 罪穢れかげだにもなきわが岐美の 行く先き先きにさやるものなし 天と地の水火と水火とに温めて 瑞の御霊は御子を生ませり 二柱水火を合せて国魂の 神を生ませしことの尊さ 紫の雲は次ぎ次ぎ重りて 玉藻の山を包まひにけり 夢なれや瑞の御霊の現れましし 日より百日の日はたちにけり 美しき山となりけり玉野湖の 底はかわきて傾斜面となりぬ 斯の如尊き言霊もたす岐美の 功は天界の宝なりけり 葭葦を踏み別け進ますわが岐美の 旅はまさしく幸多からむ

吾も亦ウの言霊に生れ出でて 瑞の御霊に仕へ奉りし 天地の生みの司と任けられし 瑞の御霊の功美はし 瑞御霊七十五声の言霊に 真鶴国土を生みましにけり 地稚くふくれ上りし真鶴の 国土はやうやく固まり初めたり 目に見えぬ国土の果まで言霊の 幸にうるほふ神世は尊き 神々はゑらぎ楽しみ真鶴の 国土の千歳を祝ふなるらむ 斯くまでも固め給ひし真鶴の 国土汚さじと吾は仕へむ 惜しむとも詮術なけれ主の神の 御旨にしたがひたたす岐美なり 木も草も瑞の御霊の現れし日ゆ 光を増して栄え初めけり 栄えゆく神世寿ぐか真鶴も 常磐の松にさやかにうたふ

今ははや迦陵頻伽も真鶴も 家鶏鳥の鳴く音も冴え渡りけり 百鳥も岐美の出で立ちあきらめて 神国のためと勇むなるらむ 遠き近き国土のことごと守ります 岐美の功ぞたふとかりけり 長閑なる春の終りを旅立たす 岐美の行手に匂へ百花 万世のほまれなりけり玉藻山の 今日の別れは国土生みの為と 百鳥は千歳を歌ひ百千草は 神世寿ぎて風にそよげる 国土生みの神業の御供仕へつつ 今日は悲しき別れするかも 大空の雲かき別けて天津陽は 岐美が行手を照らさせ給へる』

顕津男の神は、馬上より諸神に向ひ御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『百神の心かしこしわれは今 今日の門出にかたじけなみ思ふ わが姿ここに見えねど霊線は 永久に鎮めて国土を守らむ 玉野宮に朝な夕なに仕へ奉る 神をくだして形見とやせむ 玉野比女心安かれ汝が為に たすくる神のいまや降らむ』

斯く歌ひ終り、諸神に名残を惜しみつつ駒に鞭うち、玉藻山の傾斜面を右に左に折れ曲りて静に下らせ給ふ。百神は各自国境まで御供に仕へむとして駒にまたがり、御尾前に仕へ給ふ。さり乍ら国中比古の神は生代比女の神を守りつつ、千代鶴姫の神を育くまむと駿馬に跨りかへらせ給ひ、玉野比女の神は玉藻山に残りて、大宮に親しく仕へ給ふぞ畏けれ。
