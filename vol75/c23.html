Chapter 23: Demon の Forest Woods [1917]

魔の森林〔1917〕

§ 1

Transforming His figure into Suyuatoguru, Ōmagatsumi, who made the tenchi of the western kuni his wagamono and enshrouded it in jiyaki, welcomed Asakahime no Kami descending from the miya of Takachiho, hailed Her as the Kami of Akitsuo Himself, caused the mikō to be born, and plotted without the slightest negligence to completely senriyō the western kuni; yet when He perceived the true Kami of Ōmotoakitsuo drawing majika unto Him, surprised and wishing to annihilate the entourage of Mizu no Mitama tochiu, He gathered the jiyashintachi of His buka, and as a result of various hiyōgi, dispatched an ugly fox into Kashiwagi no Mori—a fox most kaukuwatsu, evil in seishitsu, skilled in lying, and cleverly plotting only for its own jiko—giving it various wicked stratagems to carry out this task. This fox is called the Kami of Shikome.

Hiding Her form near the edge of Kashiwagi no Mori, the Kami of Shikome spoke in a faint voice near the hooves of the horses of the entourage of the Kami of Akitsuo:

Shikome no Kami: 'Right you go, and certain to win; Middle you go, and certain to lose; Left you go, and surely to ruin; Such is the teaching of the Lord God.'

Hearing the song of the Kami of Shikome with keen ears, the Kami of Akitsuo smiled and chanted an imperial waka:

Akitsuo: 'I have heard the ugly words of the ugly kami; Even if I go to the left, I shall not perish. I will tread the middle path of Mitsuguri. The Kami of Makenokachi, who knows not ruin, Said that going right would win—that ugly voice Is a falsehood meant to deceive me. This forest is deep and dimly dark; I shall trample it to pieces with the hooves of my horse. If I go right, into the deep traps of Magatsu I would fall, and a hundred kami would perish. Come now, I will take the left path, which was said to lead to ruin, And advance upon it with the hooves of my horse.'

At this waka, the seven attendant kami, with slightly anxious expressions, each chanted their waka.

The waka of the Kami of Minashihiko:

Minashihiko: 'The forerunner of the ugly Magatsu of Suyuatoguru, It must be the stratagem of the Kami of Shikome. I, Minashihiko, will advance, obtaining the permission Of Mizu no Mitama, and take the left path. Fearing the light of my kimi, has Suyuatoguru Created the trap of this stratagem in this very place? I hear that the ugly fox serving under Suyuatoguru Bears malice toward this forest. Opening a way across this forest, I think To advance to the dwelling place of Magatsu of Suyuatoguru. Be that as it may, I shall serve before the Great Presence; O Mizu no Mitama, continue to lead me.'

Chanting thus, trampling underfoot the words of the crooked kami that going left would lead to ruin, deeper and deeper the entourage of eight pillars advanced, majestic upon their horses, chanting waka.

The waka of the Kami of Akitsuo:

Akitsuo: 'Though Kashiwagi no Mori is darkly overgrown, I shall not fear the dwelling place of demons either. Sealing the great sky and illuminating the dimly dark forest Of Kashiwagi, I shall proceed onward. The peak where Suyuatoguru Ōmagatsumi lies in lurk Soars into the sky yonder. From all four directions and eight quarters, mysterious sounds echo; I shall open up the forest of Kashiwagi. However strong the forces of the crooked kami may be, I will forge ahead, opening the way with living kototama. Illuminating the light of aizen in all directions, Magatsu, who bears malice toward me, shall perish. How joyful it is to go into the forest By the left path, which the ugly kami said would lead to ruin! The clouds of the great sky are torn asunder, and the light Of the heavenly sun has quietly begun to shine. The crooked kami has stirred up clouds to shroud the august sky, And blocks even the light of the moon and sun.'

The waka of the Kami of Utsutoyohi:

Utsutoyohi: 'A strange sound is heard at unaccustomed hours; Kashiwagi no Mori is the dwelling place of Magatsu. Now, what is there to fear, O Mizu no Mitama, Since the kimi of light has revealed Himself? The cry of the Kami of Shikome that we would perish— How joyous it was for me, walking this path! How valiant is the kimi of light, who illuminates And advances along the path where we are bound to perish! If it be so, whatever ugly obstacles may bar our way, What is there to fear, since my body has undergone misogi? Since I am one who has performed misogi and purification according to the will of kami, There can be no way for the ugly ones to harm me. May you guard the path that I shall tread, Even to its winding corners without fault, O Great Ancestral Kami! Since I am one born from the u-sound of the Lord God, Should I be moved by the words of the Shikome? In the western kuni, where the land is yet young, here and there Magatsuhi lurk and commit acts of malice. From this day forth, this plain of kuni is a heavenly country, a sanctuary newly born.'

§ 2

The divine miuta of Ōmichishirio-no-Kami.

I, the deity Ōmichishirio, walk in Kamunagara,

Thinking there is no disaster on the left-hand path,

While the shikomegami lurks on the right,

Plotting with all her might to bring calamity.

This forest is the dwelling place of the magatsuhi,

Tenri wide from east to west, and thirty-fuhachiri from north to south.

Most of the deities under the command of Sūyatogoru,

Are said to be lurking within this shinrin.

Be that as it may, let us cause the shikogami of the Kashihagi forest,

To kotomukeyaha as we advance, O Kimi.

Though we go on and on, the forest's end is unknown,

And the wind blowing through the kokage is chilling.

The iki of the magakami congeals and thickens, so that even the wind,

Soaks deeply through the bones of our bodies.

Illuminating the iki of kototama, we shall tread down this forest,

Beneath the hooves of our horses as we go forth.

Though the wicked schemes of the magakami are thoroughly deep,

They possess no sokojikara.

Though they appear strong only upon the uhabe, the magakami,

Shall vanish when they encounter the ikukototama.

O land where the kototama prospers, O land where the kototama shines like the sun,

O fearless land!

Until kefu we were attacked by the magatsuhi-no-kami,

But now we have attained the power of makoto.

Our souls, having undergone misogi and become purified,

Are a hundredfold stronger than kinofu.

The divine miuta of Ushibaki-no-Kami.

Though I, Ushibaki, am in this region, I have never once set foot,

Within the forest of Kashihagi.

Until kefu I have abandoned it as a strange forest,

But now I shall tread upon and break the dwelling place of the magatsuhi.

With a strong heart, escorted by the deity of light,

Today as I go through the forest where the magatsuhi dwell,

The majin of the Sūyatogoru peaks, too,

Hearing of the destruction of this forest, will instantly vanish.

This boundless, gloomy, and foul forest,

Is truly befitting as a dwelling place for the magatsuhi.

The myriad trees are half-withered, each and every one,

Spitting forth evil breath and polluting the world.

This forest where the evil breath of magatsumi gathers,

We shall purify and open up with the ikukototama.

Hearing that ugly foxes dwell in this forest,

No deity has ever ventured in until now.

Of the kunitsukami who have strayed into this forest,

Not a single pillar has returned alive.

Serving the mizunomitama Kimi, we advance,

And no evil breath dares to attack us.

My horse advances through the forest where evil breath gathers,

Neighing as it goes.

Startled by the august awe of Akitsuo-no-Kami,

Have the ugly magatsuhi fled away?

How easily the eight pillars of deities cross over,

Parting the dense thickets of the ma-no-mori!

The divine miuta of Usuzōno-no-Kami.

When you, Kimi, go forth, the forest of Kashihagi grows bright,

And begins to shine; where are the magatsuhi now?

When I look up, the mountain ranges of Sūyatogoru,

Have receded further and further away one after another.

Before the light of Akitsuo-no-Kami, the magatsumi,

Seem to flee in terror, lifting up the mountains.

Since this forest is bound by the ikukototama,

Even the magatsuhi cannot move it.

May the shikomegami dwell in this shinrin eternally,

While continuing to act as enemies of the land.

The eight million deities subordinate to the magatsumi,

Lurk within this forest of Kashihagi.

I think to kotomuke all the countless magakami,

And purify them in the sacred place of the kami.

The left-hand path, which the magatsuhi declared must perish,

Is peaceful in the light of the kami.

Both moon and sun push aside the clouds of the august sky,

And shine above the forest of Kashihagi.

Illuminating those shining august spirits one by one,

Upon the path we go forth, there is no figure of the magatsuhi.

Through the merit of the Hinonegawa misogi ritual,

We shall cross safely over the forest of Kashihagi.

The magakami conceal their forms and remain silent,

With only the sound of the wind blowing through the treetops.

Karyōbinga sings as if seizing the opportune moment.

The sun of Takama-ga-hara shines even upon the forest of magatsumi,

And the hundred flowers, bearing dew in the forest shadows,

Rival each other in beauty as they bloom forth,

Looking just like a spread-out tapestry of flowers.

The path beneath the trees in the forest where we go,

The forest where the shikogami dwell, instantly

Becomes fragrant with flowers while small birds sing,

And the sounds of insects and the voices of small birds ring out clearly,

As if singing of the spring of the heavenly realm.

Seeing flowers bloom so fully in such a forest of Kashihagi,

One cannot imagine that magatsuhi live here.

Along the winding paths where you, Kimi, walk, there are no magatsuhi,

Only flowers blooming fragrantly and birds singing.

Do they come from the land of Manazuru? Several thousand

Cranes have begun to dance in the august sky.

If it is like this, then this ma-no-mori is a heavenly realm,

And the shikomegami must have fled away.

How valiant is the journey of Kimi, as both mountains and fields,

Down to the very grass and trees, are brought back to life!

The crying voices of thousands of cranes

Must be singing of the myriad ages of the western land.

Advancing without mistaking the right and left paths,

Today's journey is truly peaceful.

Had I gone to the right, I would have fallen into the plots

Of the shikomegami and suffered.

While turning the tables on the schemes of the magakami,

How manfully Kimi has gone forth!

I too, by the august grace of the luminous Kimi,

Journey through the ma-no-mori with a peaceful heart.

Here and there are springs brimming with clear water,

Reflecting the image of the heavenly sun as they flow clear.

Though this pure water had remained turbid until kefu,

It has been revived by the ikukototama.

If it is like this, even if I cup it in my hands, it will not be soiled,

And will it not become provisions for long life?

§ 3

The Divine Song of the Kami of Uchiirui.

Following the divine mitama of the Kami of Tsukiyomi, With a peaceful heart we walk through the forest of demons. This forest, the dwelling of the foul ōmagatsumi, Has been purified by the august progress of Kimi! Trembling with dread, the Sūyatoru mountains Along with them have fled far away. This forest, said to be inhabited by countless ugly foxes, Has become a divine misono where the sun and moon shine! The blowing wind also clears, grows sharp, and shines; The treetops whisper of a thousand ages. Even the foul black clouds that enfolded the great vault of heaven Have scattered without a trace, and sun and moon shine bright. If it be thus, the land of the West, yet young in its creation, Will flourish, and the grasses and trees will sprout. Where in the ends of the fields have the Amatsushikome fled, Not even their forms remain! One after another, the light of Kimi shines brightly, And there is nowhere for the foul magami to hide. Though they soar through heaven or lurk beneath the earth to flee, How could they ever escape the eyes of the Kami?

Into the youthful kunihara of the West, step by step, Has the Kimi of Light now appeared! If the Yasohimegami were not present in this land, Ah, what a specially disordered dwelling of maga was this! Taking the absence of the Yasohimegami as a fine opportunity, The magakami raged and ran wild; Yet the divine august might of Yasohime, whom the Lord God appointed, Is the pacification of the land! Had but a single pillar of Yasohime been in this land, Then maga would not have raged thus. At last, gazing upon the advent of the Kami of Light, The kunihara is now coming back to life. Though we revere the divine word of the Lord God and traverse the kunihara, Still the maga could not be destroyed. Though there are eighty days, this very day of birth Is truly the auspicious beginning of the land!

Thickly rising, black clouds drifted, and even at midday They illuminated the dim land. By the stern august water and fire of my Kimi, the clouds That enfolded the great vault of heaven have also scattered and vanished.

As the black clouds transform into purple mists, This kunihara shall be born anew. How Kimi shines, illuminating the western land From where the eight-fold billowing black clouds rise up! The Kimi of Light, for whom we waited and waited, has appeared; Surely this is the fruit of our prayers to the Kami. From this day forth, guiding the kunitsukami, Let us make life thrive through the sacred rites of misogi. Every quarter was enfolded in clouds and mists, A land where even the moonlight was not seen.

When I look up, the light of the Amatsuhi and the light of the Moon Shine brilliantly in the azure of the august sky. The sky is azure, the earth is green, with trees and grasses sprouting, And the land thus born must surely be a divine land! The mountains of Takahara rise soaring to the east, And purple clouds can be seen welling up. Until this day, even the summit of Mount Takahara Remained enfolded in clouds and could not be seen.

The Divine Song of the Kami of Hatsumusubi.

Strong is the neighing of swift steeds, and even in the august sky Today the clear-ringing cries of cranes echo far. In the awakening of my soul by the pure brilliance of the sun and moon, Which for the first time we behold in this land, With the sky closed by black clouds, the kunitsukami Were beholding the sun and moon for the very first time. Dancing and singing in the empty sky of the true crane, They celebrate the land whose rock-door has been opened. The clouds that enfolded the great vault of heaven ten and twenty fold Have drifted away, and the august sky is high. Across the azure sea of heaven, whose depths are unknown, Quietly drifts the shadow of the moon-boat. To what shall I compare this joy? A new land Where hundreds and thousands of flowers bloom and shed their fragrance, In the mountains and in the fields, a hundred and a thousand flowers bloom in abundance, A land where even the blowing winds are fragrant.

The Divine Song of the Kami of Aumino.

With the heavenly realm and the pillar of the Kami of Aumino already cleared of clouds, The land grows bright and serene. Upon the advent of the bright mitama and the Kimi of Light, A new land has surely been born. The mountain ranges where the Sūyatoru magatsumi lurk Are receding far away from this moment on. Subdue and harmonize the magakami, O western land, And forevermore worship its sun and moon. Grasses, trees, little birds, and insects from this day forth Shall raise songs of praise to the august might of Kimi. The auspicious day has come at last, overflowing with the august might Of the bright mitama and the Kimi of Light. Clouds and mists rose on every side, the wind grew cold, And it was a land where the growth of trees and grasses was meager. By the kototama of the Kimi of eternal light, This youthful land shall be revived. In the western land, the Yasohimegami were not present, And magatsumi took on smug faces and raged furiously. The creation of the land by the Kimi of Light, for whom we so long waited, The kunitsukami shall joyfully welcome!

Thus the Kami, each singing the miuta of living kototama, pierced without any hardship the forest of kashiwa wood said to be the dwelling of the magakami, set their horse-bits in a row toward the Sūyatoru mountain range, and proceeded forward with majestic composure—a matter of the utmost awe.
