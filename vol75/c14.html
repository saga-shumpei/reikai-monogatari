Chapter 14: The Episode of Crane の訣別 (Part III) [1908]

鶴の訣別（三）〔1908〕

§ 1

Kuninakahiko no Kami chants a Miuta.

'Having firmly established the Nakatsumikuni of Manazuru, Now you set forth, O Mizumitama, To the Mizumitama whom I served morning and evening, Alas, how I lament this day of parting! A gentle divine wind blows, and upon Tamamoyama The ever-firm pines have begun to whisper. Against the bamboo leaves, pattering like falling hail, The Kunitamamiko has been born. Though no father of milk remains, like the hirasoba Beneath her mother, Chiyotsuruhime shall grow. This very day when I send off Kimi who sets out on a journey, Is both glorious and sorrowful. With tears, I welcomed you, my Kimi, with joy, And today I am about to send you off with tears. On the ridge of the spring-deep mountains, with the Mizumitama The morning of parting scatters white plum blossoms. The land of Manazuru has been firmly established, region by region, And a hundred creatures have gushed forth. Having given birth to Yahoyorozu no Kami and causing a thousand myriad Beings to live, O Kimi who sets forth on a journey! Parting with the Kototama of your young-grass wife, How valiant is the journey of Kimi! Pray, in those respective times, fondly remember The divine form of the awe-inspiring Ikuyo no Hime. Do not forget, even in your dreams, the sincere heart of Tamanohime, Who waited for Kimi and served with enduring devotion. When I gaze upon the figure of Tamanohime, purer than white plum blossoms, I, too, feel sorrow. What a feeling of exhausted strength is mine, from today I part with Kimi and shall create the realm. When I think of the distant journey ahead Of the Mizumitama who will stand forth in the land of the West, I am sorrowful. Even the Kami who eternally dwell In the high heavenly palace of Hikitatsu will surely rejoice. As the Mizumitama departs today, leaving Manazuru, The skies have grown cloudy. The water and fire of the hundred Kami have grown turbid, becoming clouds And turning into mists that shroud the skies. Though the flow of Isasagawa's waters should run dry, I shall never forget the merits of Kimi! In this present world, the yomi world, and also the heavenly realm, Within the water and fire of the Lord Ōkami, Though there are many lands called lands, there is none That is not illuminated by the living life of the Lord Kami. The Lord Kami, desiring to firmly establish the heaven and earth evermore, Has descended Kimi. Meeting with Kimi, who was born as the Mitama of Tsukiyomi no Kami, My heart is bountiful. Parting with bountiful Kimi and all alone Opening up the land of Manazuru is lonely.'

Ureshishiho no Kami chants a Miuta.

'Having created a beautiful land and setting forth on a journey, How joyful was the departure of Kimi! Both the land and the Kami make the flowers of joy and delight bloom, And now the Mizumitama sets forth! Though the white plum blossoms, born of the water and fire of the Lord Kami, Scatter, their fruition is joyful. Tsukiyomi no Kami, upon the white plum blossoms of the garden, Causes the dew to lodge and shines brilliantly. There was no darkness like that of nubatama, so long as the light Of Tsukiyomi no Kami continued to shine. My blocked heart is also opened, O beloved Kimi, Adorned by the blossoms of white plum blossoms! The water and fire that had intertwined, solidifying together, The child to be born is the Mikoto, Chiyotsuruhime. Being a child whose skin is whiter than snow, She has come to dwell with exceptional purity. The child born through the power of Umutama no Kami Remains purer than a jewel. Rejoicing, set forth on your journey, O Manazuru, Its land shall not be moved unto ten thousand generations! The Mizumitama, born as the Kami of the present world, Sets forth on a journey, making today the limit. Tamanohime, parting from her husband Kimi and alone in Tamamoyama, Shall serve in the palace, how pitiful! Radiantly upon the treetops of the pines, the heavenly sun Shines forth and has firmly established the land. Both mountains and fields, through the Kototama of the Mizumitama, Shall flourish ever more, this expanse of the land! I, as a merely joyful, joyful-ho Kami, Send off the departure of Kimi with a smiling face. Both the mountains and fields of the awe-inspiring land of Manazuru, Blooming and fragrant with a hundred and a thousand flowers, And to the very ends of the great plain where Kimi goes, with a thousand flowers Blooming and fragrant, I shall offer solace. The white plum blossoms have gradually begun to scatter, Thinking of the later fruition, I am happy. Manazuru goes without saying, a hundred and a thousand birds Sing in the forest and send off Kimi. The land of the West is vast and without limit, O Kimi who holds endless hopes! Having completed the vows of right and left, and given birth to the child, I think of the merits of Kimi who is about to set forth. Filled to overflowing, without even a gap, the living power Of the Kototama is truly mighty! Valiantly the horse neighed, the form of Kimi Who now sets forth is peerlessly valiant. May there be manifold auspicious blessings for the Kunitama Kami Of Manazuru, where water and fire congealed and solidified! O precious land of Amakuni, the land of Manazuru, As the sun and moon illuminate the young plains of the country, The heavenly sun shines without a single shadow, and the trees and grasses Of the foot-dragging mountains and fields flourish day by day. May this flourishing increase more and more, I pray morning and evening. In the southern land guarded by Toomiwo no Kami, The wondrous land of Manazuru, Having chanced to be born in a heavenly realm pleasant to dwell in, How could my heart ever grow turbid? Revering the pointing Kototama, O Mizumitama, I shall serve through a thousand and eight thousand reigns!'

§ 2

The deity Mihashihiko chants a sacred poem.

Guard well the departure of the Mizumitama today, And I shall cast a grand bridge across the path ahead. Even the light of the sun shining in heaven is at times A world of gathering clouds; go forth with a watchful heart. As we race beneath the overcast skies, I pray the stride of our steeds will be sure and safe. Surmounting various sorrows and pains, This is a journey to bring forth the Kunitamagami. Serve to the utmost limit of your jeweled-thread life, Guarding the august bridge of Kimi's imperial progress. The voice of the weeping crane is heard as if dimmed in haze. Today's sacred garden from which Kimi departs, Though spring has deepened and the white plum blossoms have scattered, Are the myriad flowers blooming in Kimi's heart. The land of Manazuru is vast; even a day or two Of urging our steeds onward leaves much to spare. Spurring our horses to run through seven days and seven nights, We shall surely arrive at the western land at last. How heavy indeed are the achievements of Kimi, Who gives birth to mountains, hardens the earth, and departs on this journey! I too, before the august prow of the Mizumitama, Shall serve and safely guard the august progress. Adorning the tail of the steed with the clear, small bells of Ihosuzu, Clinking merrily, let us walk the rustic paths. From today onward, on Kimi's journey, upon the backs of our swift steeds, We shall ever adorn and revere the bells. To the end of ten thousand generations, hanging bells upon the steeds, If we make them a memento of Kimi's august progress, Even the white steed, stirred with courage by the sound of Ihosuzu, With light hooves will prance and advance eagerly. Like unto the voices of insects hiding and crying Among the grasses left and right is the sound of the Ihosuzu. How pure, the suzumushi and matsumushi crying in the fields Where Kimi travels, to comfort the wayfarer! While the phoenix dances and sports in the august sky, The steed shall neigh upon the earth and press forward. How valiant is the advent of the A-sound-born Akitsuwo deity Of the Kototama! I too, meeting birth in the U-sound of the Kototama, Rejoice in sending forth today's imperial progress. The sky is high, the road is distant across the great moor; How valiant is Kimi, urging the steed onward!

The deity Usubutama chants a sacred poem.

Forever the lingering sorrow will not end, as the gentle deity Of the Mizumitama sets forth upon this day. Parting from Kimi of deep affection, I shall serve in the land Of Manazuru, guarding the august child. The divine water and fire of wondrous Kimi appearing, How exquisite is the august figure of Princess Chiyozuru! Nurtured and embraced in the bosom of her mother, Ah, the pitiable life of Princess Chiyozuru! Serving the august child through the passing years, I shall guard the foundation of a thousand years in the land of Manazuru. The auspicious months and days when the growing august child shall thrive, I shall await together with Kimi's spirit. How lonely Ikudaohime must feel, When she thinks of parting in life from the Kimi of her back! Upon the sacred site atop the peak of Mount Tamamo, I shall assist Princess Tamano and serve there. Mount Tamamo and Mount Manazuru, day by day and night by night, Soaring through the heavens, I shall ever protect them. As Kimi departs upon the journey across the endless land of children, I ponder Kimi's valiant heart. Grieving to part and regretting the end of ten thousand generations, It is sorrowful indeed to think upon the Mizumitama. Yet since it is the august will of the Lord Ōkami, Whatsoever I might do, there is no art to change it. The Lord Kami, desiring to secure the imperial land, Entrusted these duties to the Mizumitama. From the great empty void where naught existed, bringing forth the heavenly land, How revered is the Lord Kami! Without form, the raw Kototama coagulated and thickened, And thus the manifest heavenly realm was born! When I ponder the august majesty of the Kototama, how awe-inspiring it is; Living for ten thousand generations, I shall recompense the Kami. Do white clouds shroud the foothills of Mount Tamamo in deep folds, Lamenting the departure of Kimi? Layer upon layer of rising clouds above Mount Tamamo Swell upward and obscure the heavens in gloom. Even the cascading waterfalls of a thousand threads are hidden from sight, Enveloped in white clouds, and the wind is stilled. When I look out, the land below is shrouded in rising mists, So that even the iris cannot be distinguished. Though the voices of a myriad birds are heard, their forms Are lost within the mist—such is the present view. Standing upon the ridge of Mount Tamamo and looking far across, This land below is a very sea of mist. As the clouds of the great sky scatter, the azure Heavens reveal themselves one after another. From the breaks in the clouds that enfolded the vast sky, The light of the heavenly sun has begun to pierce through.
