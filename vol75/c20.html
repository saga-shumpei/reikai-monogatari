Chapter 20: The Episode of 岸辺の出迎 (Part I) [1914]

岸辺の出迎（一）〔1914〕

§ 1

Sūyatogoru is the righteousness of the sacred mountain. Here the malevolent vapors of heaven and earth coagulated and solidified, becoming a twelve-headed giant serpent deity; yet suddenly transforming its shape, it became the mountain range of Sūyatogoru, towering high in the northwestern region of the Hinatagaha River, generating malevolent vapors day and night, clouding a part of the Shibitenkai, and afflicting numerous kamigami. Sūyatogoru is a false name, and in truth, the evil spirits of the deity Ōmagatsumi and the deity Yasomagatsumi are entrenched there.

The deity Akitsuo, desiring to open up the lands of the West, first sought to bring Sūyatogoru, the avatar of the evil deity, into submission, and when he crossed over to the northern bank of the Hinatagaha River, the deity Teruo came forth to welcome him, accompanied by seven pillars: the deity Uchitsuyutahi, the deity Ōmichishirio, the deity Ushibaki, the deity Usutsukurio, the deity Naiyoi, the deity Ubusuna, and the deity Aimio, and having finished expressing greetings after a long separation and celebrating his good health, recited a waka poem.

The waka poem of the deity Teruo.

Waiting in yearning for so long, the deity Akitsuo has come to the land that I guard. The lands of the West are blocked by crooked deities, blowing forth malevolent vapors day and night. How shall I govern these crooked deities, my heart was shattered into a thousand pieces. From this day when the deity Akitsuo goes forth, the sun and moon of the lands of the West shall shine clear. Raising black clouds out of season and making rain fall, the wind blows fiercely on the mountain of Sūyatogoru. The mountain of Sūyatogoru exhales unseasonal black smoke, scattering malevolent vapors in all directions. Even the grasses, trees, fruits, and the five grains have stiffened in dismay because of these malevolent vapors. Things that should grow cannot grow, withering away month by month in the clouds of Sūyatogoru. Meeting by chance with the Kimi of the light of the Kototama, the high beating of my valiant heart never ceases. The waters of the Hinatagaha River falling from the mountain of Takateru have also grown muddy with the malevolent vapors of Magatsumi.

Robbing the kamigami of their lives, and oppressing even the growth of grasses and trees. From now on, by means of the water and fire light of the Kototama, do thou repel Sūyatogoru.

The deity Akitsuo recites a waka poem.

Through the achievements of the deity Teruo, both moon and sun, the lands of the West shall be bright. I have now finished creating the land of Mazuru, and have crossed the Hinatagaha River. Though I know not the state of the lands of the West, I think to proceed according to thy guidance. O deity Teruo, be at peace of mind, for I have received the living Kototama of the Lord Kami. However fierce ugly Magatsumi may be, since I possess manifold preparations, having merely crossed the Hinatagaha River, the condition of the Western land is yet unknown to me.

The deity Teruo replies once again with a waka poem.

Powerless am I, and to my shame, when the water and fire of the Kototama grow turbid, Magatsumi grows fierce. Transforming his body into the pure sacred mountain of Sūyatogoru, Ōmagatsumi has disturbed the land. Morning and evening, the colors of the water and fire blown by Magatsumi become black clouds and wrap around the four quarters. I, having now made seven pillars of deities follow me, welcome the imperial progress of Kimi. The divine words of the seven pillars of deities are the deities born from the living Kototama of the sound U. Fearing the divine words of the Lord Kami, from the sound U the seven pillars of deities were born. Leading the seven pillars of deities, I now welcome the imperial progress of Kimi.

Here, one of the seven pillars of deities, the deity Uchitsuyutahi, congratulates with a waka poem.

Descending from the lofty mountain of Takachifu, Kimi is beheld for the very first time. Meeting now with Kimi of the light of the Kototama, my heart has surely grown bright. Awaiting and welcoming you, revering the divine work of the Misu-no-Mitama who shall produce the land and give birth to imperial children, accompanying the deity Teruo through moon and sun, I came to face the Hinatagaha River. The waters of the Hinatagaha River suddenly grew clear, touching the water and fire light of Kimi. In the very instant that the Misu-no-Mitama appeared, my soul line expanded. That there should be a deity of such venerable light, I never thought in dreams or in reality. Verily am I the deity Uchitsuyutahi; protect me in the water and fire of the Kototama. From this day forth, guarding the august front of Kimi, I shall serve in the work of creating the land. The deity Mimashihiko appeared on a previous day, and has climbed up to Sūyatogoru.

原文（近代文語）

スウヤトゴルは、聖なる山の義である。ここに天地の邪気凝り固まりて、十二頭の大蛇神となりけるが、忽ち姿を変じ、スウヤトゴルの連峰となりて、日南河の西北方に高く聳え、邪気を日夜発生して、紫微天界の一部を曇らせ、数多の神々をなやませて居たのである。スウヤトゴルは偽名にして、その実は大曲津見の神、八十曲津見の神の悪霊が割拠してゐるのである。

顕津男の神は、西方の国土を拓かむとして、先づ第一に悪神の化身なるスウヤトゴルを帰順せしめむと、日南河を北岸に打ち渡り給へば、ここに照男の神は内津豊日の神、大道知男の神、宇志波岐の神、臼造男の神、内容居の神、初産霊の神、愛見男の神の七柱を従へて出で迎へ給ひ、別れて程経し挨拶を述べ終り、その御健在を祝しつつ御歌詠ませ給ふ。

照男の神の御歌。

『気永くも待ちわびにける顕津男の 神は来ませりわが守る国土に 西方の国土は曲神塞がりて 日に夜に邪気を吹きまくるなり 如何にしてこの曲神ををさめむと 心を千々に砕きけるはや 顕津男の神の出でます今日よりは 西方の国土の月日冴ゆらむ 時じくに黒雲起し雨降らせ 風吹き荒ぶスウヤトゴルの山 スウヤトゴルの山は時じく黒煙 吐きて四方八方に邪気を散らすも 草も木も木の実も五穀さへも この邪気のため伊竦みにけり 育つべきものも育たず日に月に しをれ行くかなスウヤトゴルの雲に 言霊の光の岐美にめぐり会ひて わが雄心の高鳴りやまずも 高照の山より落つる日南河の 水も濁りぬ曲津見の邪気に

神々の生命を奪ひ草や木の 生ひたちまでも虐げしはや この上は言霊の水火の光にて スウヤトゴルを退けたまはれ』

顕津男の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『月も日も照男の神の功績に 西方の国土は明るからむを 我は今真鶴の国土を造りをへて 日南の河を渡りつるかも 西方の国土の有様知らねども 汝の案内に進まむとぞおもふ 照男神心安かれ主の神の 生言霊を受けし我あれば 如何ならむ醜の曲津見猛ぶとも われには諸の備へありせば 日南河渡りしばかりの我なれば 西方国の状はわからず』

照男の神は再び御歌もて答へ給ふ。

『力なきわれ恥づかしも言霊の 水火の濁れば曲津見は猛ぶ スウヤトゴルの清き神山に身を変じ 大曲津見は国土を乱しつ 朝夕に曲津見の吹く水火の色は 黒雲となりて四方を包めり われは今七柱の神従へて 岐美がみゆきを迎へまつりぬ 七柱神の神言はウの声の 生言霊ゆ生れし神ぞや 主の神の神言畏みウ声より 七柱の神は生れましにけり 七柱神を率ゐてわれは今 岐美がみゆきを迎へまつりぬ』

ここに七柱の神の一柱、内津豊日の神は御歌もて寿ぎ給ふ。

『久方の高地秀の山ゆ下りましし 岐美を初めて拝みけるはや 言霊の光の岐美に今あひて 心明るくなりにけらしな 国土を生み御子を生まさむ瑞御霊の 神業たふとみ待ち迎へゐし 月も日も照男の神の御供して 日南の河に立ち向ひける 日南河水は俄に清みけり 岐美の御水火の光にふれて 瑞御霊現れませしたまゆらに わが魂線はひろごりにけり かくの如尊き光の神ますとは 夢に現に思はざりしを われこそは内津豊日の神なるよ 守らせ給へ言霊の水火に 今日よりは岐美の御尾前を守りつつ 国土生みの業に仕へまつらむ 美波志比古の神は先つ日現れまして スウヤトゴルに登りましける

§ 2

Mihashihiko: 'The tidings from Mihashihiko-no-Kami have indeed ceased, / Has He been captured by Ōmagatsumi? / Entrusting everything to the heart of the Kami, / I think I shall await the auspicious day and the favorable hour.'

Ōmichishirio-no-Kami chants a Miuta:

'I am now serving as a companion to Teruo-no-Kami, / And have come to welcome Kimi of the Light, / Thanks to the achievements of Teruo-no-Kami, both moon and sun, / The calamities of Ōmagatsumi are few. / Yet this youthful Kuni and wide sky, / How shall the Hitohashiragami endure it? / The Kunitsukami, awaiting the coming forth of Kimi, / Have gazed upon the sky and lamented. / Awe-inspiring indeed at the coming forth of Kimi of the Light, / Even the Kahamizu has grown clear. / Magatsumi turns into mountains and rivers, and turns into boulders, / Leading the Kamigamitachi astray. / The appearance of Mount Sūyatoguru is pure, / Yet what a craft of Maga is that which decorates the surface! / While becoming the shape of a beautiful mountain, / Day and night it spews forth Jiyaki. / The Kuni of the West is overgrown with good and ill, / There are few places where the Kami should dwell. / The Kami dwelling at the foothills of Mount Sūyatoguru, / Have always become the prey of Magami. / From time to time Yasomagatsumi, in the middle of the river, / Becomes a boulder and dams the stream. / By the Kototama of Kimi of the Light of Mizunomitama, / The boulders of Magatsumi were shattered! / Because the fierce rage of the ugly ones of Magatsumi is strong, / Even the trees and grasses of the wild fields have withered and drooped. / From today, serving before the August August of Kimi, / I shall sweep away the Jiyaki of the Kuni of the West. / Gratefully and reverently I think of the Kuni of the West, / To the Kuni descended the August Spirit of Muzu. / Even at the neighing of the spirited steed, in all four directions, / The clouds that blocked the way have begun to scatter. / Because of Kimi who is filled with eternal light, / The black clouds of the ugly ones have begun to scatter. / From today, both the sun and the moon of the heavenly august sky, / Shall shine with clear and bright light.'

Ushiwaki-no-Kami chants a Miuta:

'I myself am the Kami of Ushiwaki of Wakakunohara, / Yet even so my strength is insufficient. / Upon the coming forth of the Kami of Light whom we awaited and awaited, / The mountains and rivers simultaneously clear up. / Leaving the distant heavenly Takamiya behind, / Kimi of the Light has come hither! / To illuminate the Kuni of the West of eternal darkness, / How valiant is the figure of Kimi who goes forth! / Were the Kami of Hīrō not present, / The Magatsumi of the Kuni of the West would surely not yield. / The Eightfold Clouds of Izumo rise up and overlap, / A Kuni where even the moon and sun cannot be gazed upon distinctly. / The Magatsumi, blocking the light of the moon and sun, / Water and fire congealed to become black clouds. / Clouds welling up one after another fill the heavens, / And impair the water and fire of this Kuninohara. / Even I, born from the U-voice of the Lord Kami, / Though my strength is insufficient, am at my wit's end.'

Thus the four pillars of Kami, rejoicing at the descent of Akitsuo-no-Kami, chant celebratory songs, and turning toward the heavens, they join their hands in worship for a long while—how awe-inspiring it is.

Just at that moment, with a thunderclap as if splitting heaven and earth, lightning flashes, a heavy torrential rain arrives, and in the twinkling of an eye, the Hinodewake River swells with muddy waters, swallows the banks, and begins to sweep giant boulders from the riverbed downstream like balls.

かく四柱神は、顕津男の神の御降臨を喜び給ひて、寿ぎ歌を詠ませつつ、天に向ひて合掌礼拝久しくし給ふぞ畏けれ。

折しもあれや、天地も割るるばかりの雷鳴轟き、稲妻走り、大雨沛然として臻り、みるみる日南河は濁流漲り、岸を呑み、河底の巨巌を鞠の如くに下流に流し初めにける。
