Chapter 19: Day 南 River [1913]

日南河〔1913〕

§ 1

A Russian proverb says that fairy tales are fabrications, while legends are things that actually happened, which essentially speaks to the certainty of legends. The Monogatari that we preach is neither a fairy tale, nor a legend, nor a Roman, but an epic poetry seen from the certain Kototamagakujiyō. Legends are transmitted to the mouths of people in later ages, and their facts are gradually exaggerated, or piled up with degrees of density to become interesting, but this Reikaimonogatari has not been transmitted to anyone, and is simply explained based on the Meuyō principles of the water and fire filling Tenchi, from the state of the creation of Uchiu, regarding various phenomena.

In writing this Monogatari, since it is something that participated in the pivot of the divine realm day and night, and disclosed even up to the historical facts of the generation of all things in the universe, it is natural that it should seem strange to the eyes and ears of modern scholars.

Being a universe Monogatari of profundity and subtlety never yet seen, never heard, and never transmitted, and since it is a phenomenon from before recorded history, there should be no room for anyone to add criticism of good and evil. If by any chance there should be anyone who adds criticism to this Monogatari, that would be the height of foolishness, and something without value to be discussed.

Shinjin who submit to the obligations of Kanagara morality, serve the heavenly realm, restrain themselves, and are magnanimous to others besides themselves, possess spiritual freedom in their reality, and for the sake of the public in all things, there is nothing that cannot be accomplished. Contrary to this, Shinjin who do not reflect upon the obligations of Kanagara morality, cling only to their own desires, are lenient to themselves, and cruel toward others, are truly bound in the hands of fate.

The Shibi heavenly realm, where the deity Amanominehio reigns as the imperial deity, is still a world of spirits and Kototama, and since things with form are only those of condensed vapor, it can be called nothing other than a world of will and thought. Therefore, it is a natural law that virtuous will and thought manifest the figure of a virtuous shinin, and ugly will and thought manifest the ugliest form. There is the deity Yamata no Orochi, there is a demon deity with twelve heads, there is a half-demon, and there are many gigantic deities such as those who walk carrying a great mountain nestled in their bosom, and deities that take the form of mountains, the shape of rivers, and manifest the shape of rocks, all of which are figures manifested by will and thought. In the Monogatari that I preach, too, there are times when various deities and animals appear, but you should know that they are by no means enough to be wondered at.

Akitsunoo no Kami, repeating a journey of seven days and seven nights, arrived safely at the south bank of the Hinodewake River, where turbulent currents surged wildly and the width was broad and the waterbed deep; at this time, Amatsuhi no Kami had already ascended to the position of thirty degrees, shining brilliantly and brightly, coloring the waves of the rapid current of the Hinodewake River in gold and silver.

Akitsunoo no Kami dismounted his steed on the riverbank of the Hinodewake River, gazed upon the torrential current, and chanted a sacred poem.

Days aligned, crossing the desolate plain, We have arrived at the riverbank of Hinodewake. Upon the crests of the waves of the rushing, flowing water, The golden color of the shining sun is indeed so. Hinodewake River, gold, silver, and purple, Mingling its waves, flows eternally. Hazy on the far distant shore across the vista, Is the mountain of Suyuatogoru where the great serpent dwells. The dark clouds rising up over the mountain of Suyuatogoru, Are the water and fire of Magatsubi clouding the heavens. Though the steed neighs and grows valiant, Hinodewake River, Alas, there is no way to cross the current. Nevertheless, if there is light in my Kototama, Even this river water shall recede for a while. Days aligned, continuing a journey of day and night, The myriad deities too must have grown weary, alas. Shall we rest the legs of the steed and for a short while, Await the time when the river water shall recede? The foot-dragging mountains are hazy yonder, Clouds rise up as if burning.

Crossing over the wide plain of the land of Manazuru, Now we shall advance toward the land of the western region. The crags soaring up, blocking the waves in the midst of the river, Must be the incarnation of Magatsu. Come now, if we sweep them away with our Kototama, The crags as the incarnation of the hideous great serpent.

原文（近代文語）

ロシヤの俚言に、お伽噺は作り事にして、伝説は実際あつた事なりと言つてゐるのは、要するに伝説の確実性を言つたものである。わが唱ふる物語は、お伽噺でもなく、伝説でもなく、伝奇物語でもなく、確実なる言霊学上より見たる史詩である。伝説とは後世の人々の口に伝はり、其の事実が次第々々に誇張され、又は濃厚の度を重ねて面白く出来上つてゐるが、この『霊界物語』は何人にも伝はつたものでなく、只単に天地に充満せる水火の水火の妙用原理にもとづき、宇宙創造の状態より、諸般の事象に就いて説示したるものである。

この物語を著すに就いては、日夜神界の枢機に参じ、宇宙万有発生の歴史的事実に到るまで開示したるものなれば、現代学者の耳目には怪しく思はるるは当然である。

未だ見ざる、聞かざる、伝はらざる幽玄微妙の宇宙の物語にして、有史以前の事象なれば、何人も善悪の批判を加ふる余地はなかるべし。万々一この物語に対して、批判を加ふる者あらば、そは迂愚の骨頂にして、論議すべき価値なきものである。

惟神的道徳上の義務に服し、天界に奉仕し、自己を制して自己以外に寛大なる神人は、其実際に於て精神上の自由を有し、一切万事公共の為、何一つ成らざるはなきものである。之に反し、惟神的道徳上の義務を省みず、自己の欲望にのみ執着し、自己に寛大に、他に対して残忍である所の神人は、其の実運命の手に縛られてゐるのである。

天之峯火夫の神の、皇神として君臨し給ふ紫微天界は、未だ霊と言霊の世界にして、形あるものは気体の凝れるもののみなれば、一に意志想念の世界と称しても他なき事である。故に善良なる意志想念は、善良なる神人の姿を現じ、醜悪なる意志想念は、最も醜悪なる形を現ずるも、自然の理である。八岐の大蛇神あり、十二の頭を持つ鬼神あり、半鬼あり、大山を懐に包みて提げ歩く如き巨大なる神あり、山の姿を為し、河の形を為し、岩石の形を現ずる神等数多あるも、意志想念の現ずる姿なのである。我説くところの物語も、種々の神、動物の現るる事あれども、決して怪しむに足らずと知るべし。

顕津男の神は、七日七夜の旅を重ねて、濁流滔々と漲る、幅広き水底深き日南河の南岸に着かせ給ひけるが、この時早くも天津日の神は三十度の位置に昇らせ給ひ、晃々と輝き渡りて、日南河の速瀬の波を、金銀色に彩らせ給ひける。

顕津男の神は、日南河の岸辺に駒を下り立ち、激流を眺めて御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『日並べて荒野が原を渡り来つ 日南の河の岸辺に着きぬ 滔々と流るる水の波頭に かがやく太陽の黄金色はも 日南河黄金白銀紫の 波を交へて永遠に流るる 目路はるか彼方の岸に霞めるは 大蛇のすめるスウヤトゴルの山 スウヤトゴルの山に立ちたつ黒雲は 曲神の水火か天をにごせる 駿馬は嘶き勇めど日南河 流れを渡る術もなきかな さりながらわが言霊に光あれば この河水も暫しは引かむ 日並べて昼夜の旅をつづけつつ 諸神も亦つかれけるかな 駿馬の脚を休めて今暫し 河水引かむ時を待たむか 足引の山はあなたに霞みをり 燃ゆるが如く雲立ち昇る

真鶴の国の広原渡り越えて 今や進まむ西方の国土へ 河中に波せき止めて聳え立てる 巌は曲の化身なるらむ いざさらばわが言霊に払はばや 醜の大蛇の化身の巌。

§ 2

One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, a hundred, a thousand, ten thousand, countless living Kototama Kamigami, descend hither and purify the Mitama of the Shikogami! This Magakami, who obstructs our journey of creating the Kuni and begetting Mikō, is a hideous serpent defying the divine work of the Lord God. Guard us, O Kamigami! With the Kototama of the pristine Mitama, we earnestly pray, pouring our pure hearts into it. Ah, Kamunagara, grant us the blessings of the Mitama!

As this was chanted, the towering Chibiki giant rock that dammed the torrential river suddenly sank into the water, and in the twinkling of an eye, it turned into a gigantic serpent-body, galloped up the northern bank, instantly stirred up a tempest, rode upon black clouds, and fled in a single dash.

Ever since this giant-rock monster retreated, the river water gradually decreased its volume, and before long, when the water had receded enough to allow crossing to the opposite bank, Akitsuno-no-Kami lightly leaped astride his steed and chanted a sacred poem facing the river:

By the aid of the living Kototama of the Lord God, how brightly the water and fire proclaimed by me have shone! The Magakami changed its shape into the Chibiki boulder and stood obstructing the path I go. Even unto the bounds where my sight reaches not, the flow of the ever-widening river has indeed dried up! Now anew I have realized the sublimity of the light of water and fire through Kototama. From this place on is the sacred yet foul land dominated by Magatsuhi; with my heart purified, I shall advance. The four pillars of kami have valiantly guarded my rear and escorted me hither. O four pillars of kami, return hence from now on, to open up the Manazuru land! Now then, let us part and go forth; the Kuni of the West seems to press close at hand!

Here Ureshiho-no-Kami, taking hold of the reins of the sacred horse ridden by Akitsuno-no-Kami, chanted a sacred poem:

Though I knew you to be the noble Kimi, I never thought you would shine thus far! In the sorrow of now parting from Kimi, rains fall unknown to the skies. Now then, do fare well, and serve diligently in the divine work of Kuni-birth, O Kimi! The river waters of the ever-widening Hinodewake River too have indeed dried up at Kimi's words!

Tamakibari-no-Kami chanted a sacred poem of parting:

Having served Kimi for eighty days, to think of parting here at this present spot filled me with sorrow. Safely crossing over the folds of the mountain paths, we are about to part from the radiant Kimi. This riverbank, whence the Manazuru aligned their wings to escort you, is the boundary of the Kuni. By the living Kototama of my Kimi, the boulder of the Shikogami was shattered and the river dried up. Thinking of the path ahead of Kimi, who thus fills the place with the light of water and fire, fills my heart with deep emotion! Holding boundless thoughts, we shall return to Tamamozayama and Manazuruyama. Serving Toomiwo-no-Kami, from this day forth we shall guard the Kamikuni; think of it with peace of mind!

Yuihiai-no-Kami chanted a sacred poem:

Escorting Kimi from afar, to think of parting here at this present spot—why is it so sad? Though there is no sorrow in the Land of Aizen, now my tears flow without ceasing! In both joy and sorrow, the tears welling from my eyes—how strange they are! Kimi goes forth to pacify and harmonize through Kototama the Magakami lurking in Mount Suyatogoru. If mountains, rivers, and boulders are all incarnations of the Shikogami, go forth with a vigilant heart! To think that this river flows from the ravines of Mount Takahirateru makes it deeply venerable. Whenever I gaze upon the Hinodewake River, I think of the day when Hinokoromo-no-Himegami departed!

The sacred poem of Mamasu-no-Kami:

The water of the Hinodewake River falling from the mountain of Takahirateru has dried up by the living Kototama. The rock-clusters of the riverbed are visible, the water is shallow, and large and small fish-tribes are leaping about. Since the fish-tribes are also Mitama born from the water and fire of the kami, they cannot be neglected! Now then, let us part from Kimi, and return afterward to govern the Kuni of Manazuru. Nevertheless, until you arrive safely on the opposite shore, I shall linger and gaze upon you to the very end!

Akitsuno-no-Kami responded to the various kami and chanted a sacred poem:

Surmounting various distresses, I joyfully appreciate the merit with which you escorted my journey. Convey to Tamano-himegami, Ikuse-himegami, and the other kami my crossing of this river.

Hereupon Akitsuno-no-Kami bade farewell to the various kami, applied the whip to the horse's back, and leisurely traversed the dried-up riverbed, ascending the distant shore in the twinkling of an eye. The four pillars of kami heaved sighs of relief, lightly leaped astride their steeds, retraced the path they originally came by, and hastened toward the two sacred grounds.
