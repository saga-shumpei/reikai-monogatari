Chapter 6: Plum の初 Flower [1280]

梅の初花〔1280〕

§ 1

Hatsuwakahime, in order to save the Mitama of Ōkuronushi dwelling in the capital of Haruna and to remove the evils of Tenka, obeyed the Mei of Kamususanōnōokami and set out on a Seito alone without any attendants; having hidden herself deep in the residence of Mokusuke for over Hiyakunichiiuyo to study the Oshihe of Kami well and to read the Seigen with devotion, she finally requested an audience with Yashimanushi of the Iso Mansion to bid him farewell before setting out on her Seito. This Hatsuwakahime was supposed to set out on her expedition at the same time as Terukuniwake, Tamakuniwake, Harukuniwake, Ōgonhime, and Kiyoteruhime, but because the Meirei of Kamususanōnōokami was difficult to ignore, she was ordered to undergo training at her Jitaku for over Itsupiyakuiuyonichi.

Hatsuwakahime proceeded to the Shinden in the depths of the Iso Mansion, served before the Ōmahe of Kamususanōnomikoto, and visited to pay her respects to Yashimanushi-kami. Yashimanushi gladly came out to welcome her, invited Hatsuwakahime into the living room, listened to the general outline of the plan Hatsuwakahime intended to adopt for the subjugation of the demons, and smilingly rejoiced, saying:

Yashimanushi: 'Hatsuwakahime-sama, as you are now going to the capital of Haruna, which is separated by thousands of ri, it would surely be inconvenient for you to be single at your age. Unless you decide on a person to be your husband while you can, you will likely encounter difficulties on your way.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'As I am still young, I have no desire to take a husband, and besides, I cannot find an ideal husband.'

Yashimanushi: 'As long as a human is in the world on earth, one cannot live a single life no matter what. Also, no matter how much you may think of finding an ideal husband, things will not go so well as to please you. A husband and a wife are those who endure each other's shortcomings, and only then can they create a harmonious home and participate in the divine work of the Ōkami. The desire to seek an ideal husband or to obtain an ideal wife is something that can never be hoped for in the manifest world. There is no other way than to live in harmony as husband and wife, following the Mei of Kami in all things. Ideal husbands or wives are impossible for physical humans who are swayed by the natural world, unless it is in heaven. However, when the Oshihe of the San-kyō spreads completely throughout Tenka and the hearts of humans are improved ideally, the pattern of heaven will be reflected on earth as it is, and humans will be able to have ideal marriages.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Then I shall wait until the world of Miroku comes to the earth. What is the state of holy marriage between a celestial being and a celestial being in Takamagahara like?'

Yashimanushi: 'It will be difficult for an ideal world to appear in five or ten years. Until the Yamata-no-orochi perishes, heaven will not come completely to the earth. If I may speak a word about marriage in Takamagahara, the marriage between a celestial man and a celestial woman is like the marriage of both male and female sexes taking place in the world on earth. And there are points of difference and points of agreement between marriage in Takamagahara and in the world on earth. In the first place,

1. Marriage in Takamagahara is what is called the union of intellect and will into one heart; that which acts through the union of intellect and will is called one heart. The husband represents the part called intellect, and the wife represents the part called will.

1. This union arises internally from the beginning, and when it belongs to the spiritual body, one perceives and senses it, giving birth to what is called love. This love is called the love of marriage. The love of marriage is generated where the intellect and the will of both parties unite to become one heart. Therefore, celestial beings are one in body as male and female, and a pair of husband and wife is not two celestial beings but one celestial being.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Is the affinity between husband and wife, as you have just described, derived from the true cause of the creation of man and woman?'

Yashimanushi: 'Since a man is naturally intellectual from birth, all his thinking comes from intellect. Conversely, since a woman is naturally emotional from birth, her thinking also comes from will. This is a clear fact, whether viewed from the sexual conduct of men and women or from their physical forms. When viewed from temperament, all of a man's actions are rational, while a woman's are emotional. Even when viewed from their forms, a man's face is not as graceful and soft as a woman's. A man's body is strong and healthy, while a woman's is soft and delicate. Therefore, there is a similar distinction between intellect and will, and between emotion and thought, in the relationship between men and women. There is also a distinction between truth and goodness, and between faith and love. This is because faith and truth belong to the intellect, while goodness and love belong to the will.'

§ 2

Hatsuwaka: 'Are there distinctions between Seinen, Seijin, Shiyojiyo, and Fujin in Tengoku?'

Yashima: 'When speaking from a Reiteki Igi, we designate Seinen and Seijin as those who manifest the wisdom to fully attain Truth, and we call those who manifest the emotion toward Good, Shiyojiyo and Fujin. Furthermore, viewed from the emotion toward this Good and Truth, a Seijiyō or a Kyōjiyō is sometimes called Fujin, or called Shiyojiyo, or called the Mitama of a Henjiyōniyoshi.'

Hatsuwaka: 'Your Osetsu that Danshi act only with Chisei and Jiyoshi act only with Ishi does not make Gaten to me at all. It seems to me that Jiyoshi also possess Chisei, but...'

Yashima: 'Without distinction between Danjiyo, they both possess Chisei and Ishi. It is merely that Danshi take Chisei as their primary, and Jiyoshi take Ishi as their primary. What determines a person's Seikaku must depend on how they treat that which they take as primary. However, in the Konin within Takaamahara, there is no imbalance. That is to say, the wife's Ishi is the husband's Ishi, and the husband's Chisei is the wife's Chisei. Because Danjiyo mutually think what the other thinks and aspire to what the other aspires, the thoughts and Ishi of both mutually resonate, harmonize, and become one body. Since this harmony is a harmony in actual practice, the husband's Chisei enters the wife's Ishi, and the wife's Ishi enters the husband's Chisei. And this harmony arises especially when they see that aspect within their mutual relationship. In Takaamahara, because there is a traffic of thoughts and Jiyōdō, and especially because the mutual love between husband and wife is deep, the degree of this traffic is even more concentrated and intimate. Seeing this, has it not become clear how the Konin state of the heavenly beings in Takaamahara is established? It is clear that this love in Tengoku is a heart that wishes to give everything one possesses to the other, offering all.'

Hatsuwaka: 'I have been able to clearly perceive the state in which the Chisei of Danshi and the Ishi of Jiyoshi harmonize to become one heart and one body, and how the Konin of Tengoku is performed divinely. However, I ask you to show me once more what things Chisei receives, and what things Ishi is able to receive in Tengoku.'

Yashima: 'As long as there is such harmony and unity between Danjiyo who have performed a divine Konin, those heavenly Danjiyo dwell in the love of Konin, and at the same time, they dwell in wisdom, perception, happiness, and joy. This is because the divine Good and divine Truth, which are the sources from which all wisdom, perception, happiness, and joy must come, flow primarily into the love of Konin. Therefore, the love of Konin is the very plane into which the divine nature flows. This is because it is, at the same time, a Konin of Truth and Good. The harmony of Truth and Good is like the harmony of Chisei and Ishi; Chisei receives divine Truth and thereby perfects its Chisei, and Ishi receives divine Good and thereby perfects its Ishi nature.'

Hatsuwaka: 'What distinction is there between the harmony of Chisei and Ishi, and the harmony of Truth and Good?'

Yashima: 'In the end, they are the same. The harmony of Truth and Good perfects the heavenly being, and also perfects wisdom, perception, happiness, and joy. For the degree to which a heavenly being is a heavenly being lies in the extent to which his Good has harmonized with Truth, and his Truth has harmonized with Good. In short, the Konin is performed according to the degree to which his love has harmonized with faith, and his faith has harmonized with love.'

Hatsuwaka: 'From where does the cause of the harmony between Good and Truth come?'

Yashima: 'It originates from the divine love that the Taigen-shin possesses toward all things in Takaamahara and the world of earth. From this divine love, divine Good is produced, and this divine Good is what heavenly beings and those who dwell in divine truths receive. Since there is no vessel other than Truth to receive Good, those who do not dwell in Truth cannot receive anything from the Taigen-shin and Takaamahara. Therefore, in so far as the various truths in humans harmonize with Good, they harmonize with the Taigen-shin and the heavenly realm. The source of the love of Konin lies here. Therefore, this love is the very plane where the divine nature flows. Furthermore, the state in which Good and Truth are harmonized in Takaamahara is called heavenly Konin.'

§ 3

Hatsuwaka: 'I have heard that the Fūfu of Takaamahara are in the form of Nikoitsutai, that is, Ichitennin, but I request a more detailed Shiyōsai Setsumei once more.'

Yashima: 'In the Tennin or among the Ningen on Chijiyō, the Good and the Truth that have achieved Wagafu are one and not two. The reason is that the Good originates from the Truth, and the Truth originates from the Good. This Wagafu is like the Wagafu that is formed when a person thinks of what they aspire to, and aspires to what they think of; at this time, their Saunen and Ishi become one, reaching the point of forming an Itsushin. This is because the Saunen follows what the Ishi desires, creating an image and manifesting it upon the Keishiki, and the Ishi bestows upon it the emotion of Kuwanki. It is for this reason that in Takaamahara, the marriage of both a man and a woman is called one Tennin, and not two Tennin.'

Hatsuwaka: 'In the Hajime, He who created man created them as man and woman. For this reason, a man shall leave his Fubo and be joined to his wife. The Futari shall become Itsutai. Therefore, they are not two, but Itsutai. What Kami has joined, let no man separate. Is the passage written in the Holy Word, "This saying cannot be accepted by all men, but only by those to whom it is given," referring to the marriage of the celestial realm where the Tennin dwell?'

Yashima: 'It is the marriage of Tennin in the celestial realm, and this is the marriage of the Good and the Truth; a marriage joined by Kami cannot be separated by man. In short, it means that the Good cannot be separated from the Truth. Through this, one can see from where a true marriage begins. That is, first the inner heart of those who enter into marriage is formed, and from there it is transmitted down to the physical body, where there is perception, and feeling this, it becomes love. This is because everything that the physical body feels and perceives draws its source from the spiritual power of man.'

Hatsuwaka: 'Thank you for your various explanations. However, having heard that, I feel even less inclined to enter into marriage on Chijiyō. Nevertheless, as I have already told my father, I promise that after my duties in the capital of Haruna are finished, I will be under your care and marry a suitable husband. I do not mean to say that I will persist in being single forever. No matter what, I am still young and have a long future ahead, so I wish to engage in independent activities.'

Yashima: 'If you say so, I shall not insist. To tell you the truth, I must confess that Lord Susanoo-Ōkami commanded me to test your spirit, which is why I said such things. If that is your determination, you will surely be able to Kototama-wasure the evil spirits of the capital of Haruna. If you had a husband, you would not be able to engage in actual activities. Even among the Hachinin-Otome, those who have husbands are like those who cannot engage in free and unrestricted activities as a housewife. Eikohime-sama and Etsukohime-sama, who are still single, are attempting great activities just as you see. That is also thanks to being single. By the way, Hatsuwakahime-sama, why did Mokusuke-sama not see you off on your departure?'

Hatsuwaka: 'My father is not such a weak-hearted person. When I said, "Father-sama, I am going all the way to the capital of Haruna for my duties, so please stay healthy," my father immediately raised his voice and said, "Never worry about Mokusuke. You have your own duties," and he did not even see me off to the gate. Truly, the love of a parent is deep. I, too, must engage in activities for the sake of the Great Kami and for the sake of the people of the world, in response to my father's brave heart.'

Yashima: 'Naruhodo, like parent, like child; I am truly impressed. If Susanōnōokami-sama were to hear of the Goseishin of you and your child, He would surely be filled with Gomanzoku. Please, may you have a fortunate Seito and be on your way.'

Yashimanushi: 'Well then, let us part ways here. Hatsuwaka... Yashimanushi-kun, may you dwell in peace.'

Having finished singing, the two Kami-jin parted ways here. Although it was called spring, Hatsuwakahime, with the sleeves of her robe fluttered by the still-cold wind, lingered with regret over the fragrance of the plum blossoms that had begun to bloom on Mount Ubusuna, and while alone, she set out on a long journey she was not accustomed to, dressed lightly in Waraji, Kiyahan, a straw raincoat and hat, and leaning on a Kongō-zue, she advanced with courage.
