Chapter 3: 地鎮 Festival [1277]

地鎮祭〔1277〕

§ 1

Three hundred and fifty thousand years ago, Kamususanōnōokami constructed a heavenly kingdom on earth at the summit of the Ubusuna Sanmiyaku in Perushiya, descended there in divine presence, opened the Ananai-kyō, and trained numerous Sendenshi to bestow the virtues of Aizen and the light of Shinshin upon the many Ningen dwelling in the Kokudo of the earth. It is truly of infinite nobility that the Lord God descended to earth and continued His physical activities, intending to build a heavenly kingdom on earth, to purify the Shinkon of Yamataorochi, Shikogitsune, and Jiyaki, and to open the age of Miroku where there is not a single point of Kiyogi or Zaiaku between Tenchi. At this time, in the capital of Haruna in the country of Tsuki, the Brahman priest Daikokunushi, whose Shinkon had been occupied by the evil spirits of Yamataorochi, led numerous Sendenshi, holding a sword in his right hand and the Koran in his left, and was attempting to propagate the teachings of Daijizaiten Ōkunihiko-no-Mikoto throughout the world, forcing them to submit to his path by the sword. Furthermore, the creed of Brahmanism is a heretical religion of darkness and injustice that slights life, honors death, and makes the torment, injury, destruction, and bleeding of the physical body the essence of ascetic practice. As many Ningen were tormented by this religion, and the voices of agony and lamentation filled the mountains and fields, the Great God of supreme benevolence and love descended a portion of His divinity to earth, appeared as Kamususanōnō-no-Mikoto, and in order to save the spirits and Ningen lost in the Chūyūkai and Jigokukai, He built this heavenly kingdom on earth. In addition to this, He opened spiritual kingdoms in the Caucasus Mountains, Jerusalem in Turkey, the sacred grounds of Aya on Jitentō Island, Tenkyōzan, and other sacred mountains, and sent down Sendenshi to take charge of these duties. Tamakuniwake, serving the command of the Great God, left the sacred grounds of Mount Ubusuna as a Sendenshi, accompanied by three followers, Dōkō, Itakō, and Junkō, and crossed the steep slopes of Kajika Pass. Just as they were sheltering in Futokoro-dani from a gale, they were attacked by a troop of mountain monkeys and suffered eye injuries, but finally reached the forest of the shrine, where they encountered the party of the Sendenshi Kuniwake, and it was decided by a special mission that they would construct the divine hall of the Great God until the eye disease was healed. In the forest of the shrine, cedar, cypress, pine, and other magnificent timber stood in a Kannagara manner. This Kajika Pass is always windy, and as the mountain is covered in rock, large trees do not grow, with only thin, withered old trees of two or three shaku dotting the spaces between the rocks. However, the forest of the shrine, which is a part of Mount Kajika, lies in the bosom between valleys, so there is not much damage from violent winds, and the soil is relatively fertile, thus the trees grew thickly to form a reasonably large forest.

By the divine decree of Kuniteruhime, it was decided to construct the divine hall of the Great God, and under the general supervision of Tamakuniwake, Yoshikohime, Imakohime, Dōkō, Junkō, Itakō, and the former Brahman soldiers Iru, Iku, Sāru, Yoru, Teru, Haru, as well as Harukō, Chin-hiko, Shizuko, Kaede, and others, regardless of day or night, used sacred hoes and sacred axes to fell trees, level the ground, and solidify the foundation to begin preparations for the construction of the divine hall. At this time, General Ranchi and General Katahiko, who were at the camp in the forest of Ukiki, and others had all submitted to the Ananai-kyō, and as the many soldiers had scattered in all directions and peace had finally returned to this area, the people of the country rejoiced, saying this was entirely due to the grace of the God of Ananai. To repay this divine favor, people gathered from all directions to contribute money or labor for the construction of the divine hall in the forest of the shrine. This truly lonely valley was filled with the sound of hoes, the sound of sacred hoes and sacred axes, as well as stone-pounding songs and the voices of human joy, and beasts such as wild boars and monkeys fled far away, leaving not even a shadow.

Dōkō became the chief of civil engineering and took charge of construction supervision. However, as mentioned above, Tamakuniwake was the general supervisor. The stone-pounding song resounded vigorously through the forest spirits. The song:

Manifesting in Takamagahara, By the words of the Imperial Great God, Kamususanōnō-no-Ōkami, At the sacred grounds of Mount Ubusuna, Built the hall of the sacred garden, To save the people of the world universally, Opening the precious teachings Near and far, How grateful it is. Tamakuniwake, the Sendenshi, Having received the divine words, In the cold early sky of winter, Enduring the rain and wind without complaint, When he came to Kajika Pass, The wind was fiercer than he had heard, Unable to advance even a single step, Taking shelter in Futokoro-dani, While waiting for the wind to pass, By an unexpected mountain monkey, His right eye was torn, How sad it is to suffer.

The noble Mitsukahi of Kami, encountering such hardships, wondered if it were entirely a warning from Kami, or perhaps some Shigumi. Looking at each other, unable to fathom the heart of Kami, there were moments when they wandered in the Chiū. Then, the Sendenshi of Harukuniwake appeared and declared, 'Sendenshi of Tamakuniwake, you are being made to encounter these hardships by the Shigumi of Kami; pray, keep your heart peaceful and calm, as is the Oboshime of Kami.' Even as he spoke, there were times when he comforted them.

Isokohime and Imakohime came from afar to this place. The Kototama transmitted through the Kamugakari of Kuniteruhime were: 'In the forest of the shrine, serve the Miaraka of the Sumekami. Build the Miyahashira in Takaamahara firmly, and protect the Shinro of the people of the world from the Magakami, and save them all.' Fearing those Kamigoto, they rammed and solidified the Uhatsuihane, rammed and solidified the Shitatsuihane, cleared the stones, and leveled the earth. The Mamehito gathered together, forgetting both heat and cold, and served with their bodies untiringly.

This Arisama was as if the Tenbito of Tengoku were rejoicing and delighting in their work. If one returns to the origin of the age of Kami, though it is said that the heavens are high, the heavens become extremely near. In the age of Miroku where heaven and earth are in harmony, Kami and man will rejoice together, and welcome the eternal spring of Tokoyo.

'Polish your Mitama quickly,' thus declared the divine decree of the severe spirit of the three-five. Now, appearing before our very eyes, it is truly noble beyond measure. Ah, people of the world, people of the world! Having been born as humans in this world, there is no merit greater than sweating and serving in the divine work of the noble Kami. Living, one becomes a Kami of the earth; dying, one shall join the precious group of the pure heavenly realm and become a Mitama that enjoys a life of supreme joy and supreme happiness.

When I think of it, when I think of it, how grateful I am. Even though there are countless beings living upon this earth, we were born as people who, having been made in the image of Kami, perform the divine work in place of Kami. Rejoicing that we are truly the primates of all creation, we must love the true Kami well, accumulate the virtues of goodness, and while yearning for the Kami Susanoo-no-Ōkami, who has descended filling the divine character of the Sumekami, as the Kami of salvation, we must devote ourselves to sincerity alone.

Strike, strike, strike, strike well! Strike until the bottom of the Shitatsuihane, until the cauldron of the Ryūgū breaks, until the bridge of hell falls. The cry of victory, rejoicing and brave, echoes well through each group of the Tengoku of Takaamahara. A hundred Tenbito rejoice, and in order to serve this stone-ramming completely, they have descended until the place is crowded. Heaven and earth, Kami and man, are in harmony, and while serving in this divine work, we shall fulfill the heart of Kami. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase.

Thus he led the chant, and began to ram the wide site from all four directions with a thudding sound.

Reflecting the form of Tengoku in the mansion of Iso, the Kamubashira, 'Untokoshiyo, dokkoishiyo,' the Lord Kamususanō, who has appeared as the God of salvation, 'Yattokose, dokkoise,' the Sendenshi sent by His Jinjimugen Mikokoro, 'Yoitosa, yoitosa,' the Murakumo filling the Tenchi shall also clear away completely with this, 'Dokkoishiyo, dokkoishiyo,' we who have served the Brahman Kyō, by the grace of Kami, 'Dokkoishiyo, dokkoishiyo,' helped by Ananai-kyō, in the Gofushin of the forest of the shrine, 'Yattokose, yattokose,' the joy of being used, the happiness of being used.

A, dokkoishiyo, dokkoishiyo, yoitose, yoitose, yoiyasā, yoiyasā, strike, strike, strike, strike them hard, until the cauldron of Jigoku breaks, until the castle of Riugū shakes, dokkoishiyo, dokkoishiyo, yoitose, yoitose.

With such Itsushiyōkenmei, the Renchiu of the Brahman faction became Yakuki and worked at the Ishitsuki without sparing their Honemi. At last, after Mitsukamiyosa, the Kisokōji reached its complete Kuwanketsu.

From this, the Ichidō, as a celebration of the Ishitsuki, and also to express gratitude for the Shinon from the Hitobito of the Yomo, opened a celebratory feast, laying out sake, bread, and other rare fruits offered as gifts until there was no space left, and gave thanks for the Shinon.
