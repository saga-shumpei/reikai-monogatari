Chapter 4: 人情 [1278]

人情〔1278〕

§ 1

The Ishitsuki was finally completed safely, the Jichinsai was over, and they moved on to the Nahorahi banquet. Today, having obtained the permission of Tamakuniwake, the Brahmin group—Iru, Iku, Saru, Teru, Haru, and Yoru—who were such great Shiyugau, felt as if they were ascending to Ten, singing songs, striking stones with a clatter, and dancing wildly as if a levee had broken. When any person is dead drunk, they become like a Kodomo. Furthermore, they tend to spill out all the Fuhei they have held in their hearts since ordinary times.

Iru: 'Hey, Iku, Saru, what do you think? When we were at Kiyoharuyama, we drank Tarafuku of sweet sake from morning till night, and we even treated the Shinki guest, Itakō-sama, with hospitality, breaking down the barriers between friend and foe, didn't we? And yet, since surrendering to this ridiculous Ananai-kyō, we have not been allowed to drink even an Itsuteki until today; I am truly lonely, and I thought that the original Brahmin religion was Yotsupodo better after all. Because you Kisamara have been dragging your feet in a place like this, I have had no choice but to stick around. Truly, the leader here is a stingy fellow. What is this, acting all high and mighty as a supervisor, that Dōkō? I feel sick just looking at that face of his. Eeen!'

So saying, his sub-consciousness was triggered, and he poured out his true feelings.

Iku, fearing that Dōkō the supervisor might hear Iru complaining and making a scene in such a loud voice, was doing his best to cover his own ears with both hands. Then, leaning his mouth close to Iru's ear,

Iku: 'Oi brother, you should not complain in such a loud voice. It is a sacrilege. What will you do if it reaches the ears of Dōkō?'

Iru: 'Na, what is it? What is a sacrilege? Are you that afraid of it reaching Dōkō's ears? What is that hand for, covering your ears?'

Iku: 'Even so, because you are spouting abuse in such a loud voice, I am plugging my ears so that it does not enter the supervisor's ears.'

Iru: 'What are you doing? You are a Baka. What is the point of plugging your ears like someone trying to steal a bell? Everything this Iru says is meant to reach his ears immediately, no matter how deaf he may be. Those who blindly follow, like leading a thin horse of skin and bone over the Kajika Pass, saying "Hee-hee, Hai-hai," are nothing but spineless octopus-men. This Iru will not do such a cowardly thing. Complain in an even louder voice. Nay, let us unleash a torrent of resentment and criticism. Tell me, Saru, you are a Saru-fellow too, so you must surely feel the same as me.'

Saru: 'Don't talk like a Baka. You have been served excellent sake at this auspicious Jichinsai, so why are you grumbling? Do you not have a bit of sense?'

Iru: 'Na, what is auspicious? What is so excellent about it? Think about it carefully: Kiyoharuyama was destroyed, the camp at Ukiki-no-mori was made a mess, and how can we, the Brahmin warriors, hold our heads high? On top of that, to be made to help with the shrine that worships the god of Ananai-kyō, and to be forced to drink sake that we do not even want, looking happy—what is there to be grateful for? It is a sacrilege. Is it not shameful? One who takes off his helmet to the enemy, accepts the enemy's hospitality, and sheds tears of gratitude is not a human being. Even though I have surrendered on the surface like this, I have not surrendered from the bottom of my heart like you. I have mingled among the crowd like this, intending to scout the situation and then perform a great feat. Did Bo Yi and Shu Qi not live by eating ferns on Mount Shouyang and refusing to eat the millet of Zhou? Without that much backbone, can one be called a Brahmin warrior? Eeen!'

Saru: 'Ahahaha, if you dislike the food and drink of Ananai-kyō that much, why did you eat and drink until you were so drunk you lost all sense of your surroundings? You are always a nuisance when you drink bad sake. If you are not disciplined a little, it will be troublesome for us to be suspected of things we did not do. We are not two-faced warriors like you; when we say we have surrendered, we have surrendered from the bottom of our hearts.'

Iru: 'I have submitted to Matsuhiko and Harukuniwake from the bottom of my heart. It is not that I have submitted to such Sendenshi as Tamakuniwake or Michikō. First of all, that is what I cannot accept. Think about it carefully; where is the Shintoku in a person who is supposed to be a Sendenshi of Tenka, acting as if he were having his eyes gouged out by four hands? I feel disgusted whenever I see the face of that Tamakuniwake. If he were a Sendenshi like Harukuniwake-sama, even a Brahmin like me would submit to him.'

Michikō, seeing the three of them huddled in a corner and chirping in loud voices, thought that they might be quarreling, and if it were a quarrel, he must mediate and settle it happily, for he could not bear to have the essential Jichinsai marred, so he advanced toward the three of them with a tipsy, staggering gait.

Michikō: 'Hey Iru, Iku, Saru, why are you talking so noisily? Is there some interesting story?'

Iku: 'Yes, there is something interesting. This fellow Iru has finally spilled his Honne and is saying things that are beyond help.'

Iru: 'Hey, hey, Iku, even if you are drunk, you must not say things that are of Daiji. You must never tell the Kantoku Michikō that I have come to dislike Tamakuniwake, that I am not satisfied with this Fushin, or that I said the Brahmin religion is better after all. That is the friendship between us.'

Michikō: 'Ahahaha, oh Iru-sama, I will not insist on hearing it. But I have understood everything, you see.'

Iku: 'See that, Iru has finally vomited out the trash in his belly due to the Shintoku of the sacred sake. Michikō-dono, please, whatever Iru says, he is just saying what the vice-guardian is telling him, so please just let it pass.'

Saru: 'Kantoku-sama Michikō, Iru is such a fellow. But he is a relatively honest man, so please do not tell Tamakuniwake-sama and forgive him. He is truly a hopeless fellow. Un-ungā, ah, I am drunk, I am drunk, I have truly received such excellent sacred sake, and not only the main guardian deity, but even the primary and secondary guardian deities are extremely pleased.'

Michikō: 'Hey, all three of you, do not worry. I am not such a fool as to take seriously what a drunkard says.'

Iru: 'Indeed, hearing that, I have come to like you, Michikō-sama. I have also come to like Tamakuniwake-sama for having such a clever servant. Please let me offer you a cup. Today, being drunk on the sacred sake, I have vomited out all the trash in my belly. It was never Iru's body that said such things. It was the kin of Daikoku-nushi who was in my belly whispering, so I am truly troubled.'

Michikō: 'That must be so. Well, do not worry. Come, let us have a cup.'

With that, Michikō cheerfully gave cups to Iru, Iku, and Saru, poured for them himself, and sat down there himself, drinking sake in a lively and amusing manner while singing a song.

Iru said, 'Brother, one more cup,' and offered a cup to Michikō and began to sing.

Iru: 'Michikō-sama of the Sangokyō / From morning until night, you go pon-pon / It is fine that you clap your hands / Yoitosa, yoitosa / But you catch me, Iru-sama / And it is troublesome when you say pon-pon / Yoitosa, yoitosa indeed / Ahahaha, well, pour me a cup, Michikō-sama, you remember the saying that a drunkard's true nature does not change, right?'

Michikō sings:

'That I, Michikō, said pon-pon to you / There is a reason for it / You drink sake from morning until night / Because I thought you were a gourd / And then, your face is thick-skinned / Like a drum / And because your belly is empty / I thought you were a drum and went pon-pon / I tried tapping you, Iru-sama / It is a misunderstanding for you to get angry at me / It is my duty to go pon-pon / I have to pound the stones / Think of it as me giving a signal / Yoitose, yoitose / Yattokose, yōiyana / Arewaise, korewaise / Come now, yōitose.'

Iku and Saru clapped their hands.

Iku: 'Yay, pom-pom! Sweet, sweet! While pom-pom and while kan-kan, I, Iku-sama, shall sing one song. Ahem! Hey, Iru, clap your hands and accompany me a little. If the accompaniment is poor, the song will not go well at all.'

Iru: 'Very well, I shall accompany you. Come, say it, say it!'

Iku: 'The Kami of the forest of the shrine, / Bonten, Taishiyaku, and Jizaiten, / When I thought they were Ōkunihiko, / My expectations were completely off, / For they are Ōkuniharutachikami-sama. / Until now, I had thought / That there was no one as great / As the Kami of the Brahman religion, / But what is this? / My expectations were off, and I fell into the plight / Of having to rejoice and worship / The unpleasant Kami of Ananai-kyō. / Fushiyōbushiyō, morning and evening, / Pretending to be a believer, / The fact that I had lived until now / As a hypocrite, / I realized, and placing my hand upon my chest, I pondered. / The end result was that / I learned the Oshi of Ananai-kyō is truth, / So the worries of my heart cleared away, / And now I have become one who completely believes / In the Kami of Ananai.',

'Truly, it is by the state of one's heart / That a person's body can become anything. / People are children of Kami, palaces of Kami, / And when I was taught / That we are the masters of the Tenchi Keirin, / At that time, / I thought it was something strange, / And despised it in my heart / As a teaching tainted by arrogance. / But as expected, Kami does not lie. / In the Brahman religion, they say we are / Like lumps of dust and dirt, / Or like failures of creation, / But as expected, people are children of Kami. / When I think of this, even the sake I drink / Tastes all the better. / For today's Ishizuki celebration, / I have received plenty of sake, / And my soul is floating, / Feeling as if I am playing in the Misono of Tengoku.',

'Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. / Reverencing the grace of the spirit, / I offer my gratitude here. / Come, let us have a cup!'

Thus, the four became a group and were exchanging cups of sake. On the other side, the three of the Brahman group, Yoru, Haru, and Teru, were sitting cross-legged in a triangle, grumbling.

Yoru: 'Hey, look at that Iru. That fellow has been eating and getting drunk on excellent sake since earlier, and is he not exposing the trash in his belly that cannot be helped? Whenever that fellow drinks sake, he exposes everything. Something strange must be possessing him.'

Teru: 'That is so, it is a pitiful thing. Since he is a sluggard in the village and hates work, it cannot be helped. He is always wearing rags, and if he stands at someone's gate and drinks sake, he sits down with a filthy appearance, so everyone is troubled; they give him sake and a little money to send him home. That is the mercenary who was hired for cheap money in this war. Since he is an upstart who was originally a loafer, once you show him pity, he gets carried away and becomes unbearable. Is it not too much that Michikō-sama is in such a good mood and exchanging cups with a guy like that? It would not be a loss for him to let me have a cup, and it is not acceptable to be looked down upon more than a guy like that.'

Haru: 'Hey, Teru, if you do not take care, a cloud is forming on your forehead. That it is clouded tells you that something inauspicious is coming to your future. In short, it is teaching you that you will die. It is dangerous if you do not refrain from heavy drinking. Even if your body is fit, if you only speak ill of others, you will be hated and there is no telling what kind of disaster you will buy. You had better be a little cautious.'

Teru: 'If you say such things, the drunkenness I went to the trouble of achieving will fade away, will it not? My complexion is bad from the start, so do not worry about me. If I hear such things, I start to feel unwell myself.'

As they were talking, Harukō came forward carrying a warmed sake bottle in one hand and a cup in the other.

Harukō: 'Yoru-sama, Haru-sama, Teru-sama, the Ishizuki was quite grand, but thanks to you all, it ended safely, and there is nothing as auspicious as this. Please let me pour you a cup in celebration.'

ハル『オイ、テル、貴様は気をつけないと額際に曇りがかかつてゐるぞ。そこが曇つて居るのは貴様の未来に取り不祥なる事が来るのを教へて居るのだ。つまり死ぬと云ふ事を教へて居るのだ。深酒を呑まぬやうにせぬと危ないものだ。たとへ身体がピンピンして居ても人の悪口ばかり云ふて居ると、憎まれてどんな災難を買ふやら分らないぞ。些と慎むがよい』

テル『そんな事を聞くと折角の酔がさめて仕舞ふぢやないか。俺は平常から顔色が悪いのだ、気にかけて呉れるな。そんな事を聞くと何だか俺迄気分が悪くなるからな』

斯く話す所へ片手に燗徳利を下げ、片手に盃を持ち進んで来たのは晴公であつた。

晴公『ヨルさま、ハルさま、テルさま、石搗は大分大層でしたが、先づ貴方方のおかげで無事終了し、斯んな目出度い事はありませぬね。お祝ひに一杯つがして下さい』

§ 4

He held out the sake cup. Yoru, looking delighted, had Harukō pour for her, then took a sip, tapped her forehead with a 'pon' sound, and spoke.

Yoru: 'As expected of Harukō-sama. Being trained by Harukuniwake-sama, you are far more considerate than Michikō-sama. Harukō-sama, I rely on your kindness. Since we are so-called foreigners who converted from Brahmanism, I cannot help but feel that we are alienated in every way. Perhaps this is just a distortion of my heart. Humans are strange creatures; when you treat us like this, I feel as if I can open up from the very bottom of my heart, and somehow I feel more energetic in my work. Hey, Haru, Teru, isn't that so?'

Teru: 'That is true. If those who stand above others are not very careful, those below cannot bear it.'

Haru: 'Because Harukō-sama also has the name Haru, even if it is just a case of having the same name but being different people, our Mitama are in harmony after all. That is why Harukō-sama has come to us. Yoru, Teru, you must thank Harukō-sama, and at the same time, you must thank this Haru-sama too.'

Yoru, Teru: 'Hmph, what are you spouting? I will twist your nose off.'

Harukō: 'The Yoru of eternal darkness has cleared away, in the great sky / The moon shines and the stars sparkle. / The starlight of Teru and Yoru, where the sky has cleared / Appears so clearly and far, does it not.'

Yoru: 'Hey, Teru, Haru, both of you rejoice. I am Yoru-sama, you are Teru, and Haru is the other, and since it is Harukō-sama, he has composed such a celebratory poem for us. The virtue of kindness and Jiai clears even the cloudy sky, and the moon of a cloudy heart also shines, does it not.'

Harukō pressed his hands together, and thinking of something, he recited the sacred prayer with a voice full of gratitude and tears, saying Kannagara Tamachi Haemase. The three of them also clapped their hands and chanted in unison, 'Kannagara Tamachi Haemase, Kannagara Tamachi Haemase.' Thus, the Naorai banquet came to a complete close, and everyone, having enjoyed themselves to the fullest, went to sleep.
