Chapter 11: 水呑同志 [1285]

水呑同志〔1285〕

§ 1

Harukuniwake took up his position at the Nagadoko near the old shrine in the forest of Kuru, and together with Ranchi, Katahiko, Gariya, Kesu, Mankō, Otora, Imoriwake, Otami, Tatsukō, Matsuhiko, and others, commenced a lecture series lasting one hundred days. He instilled in them the general principles of the Ananai-kyō, and finally, having made Otora the first candidate for a Sendenshi, he wrote a letter of introduction and sent her toward the mansion of Iso. As previously mentioned, Otora stopped by at Kogitayama, took Magahiko as her companion, and while singing the Sendenka, proceeded to climb the Kajikatauge.

Though the morning sun may shine or cloud, Though the moon may wax or wane, Though the great ocean may dry up, Though the earth may turn to mud, The light of the one sincere God of Ananahi Shall save the world. As a Sendenshi of Harukuniwake, Appearing in the forest of Kuru, During those one hundred days and one hundred nights, How joyful it was to receive The precious Mioshihe of God. Otora, chosen here, Determined as a candidate For the Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, Having been handed a joyful letter of introduction, Now, with eager heart, toward the Iso mansion, Toward the sacred ground, she advances. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, The grace of God is not shallow. My own body, which had hitherto Exhausted itself in the way of 'body-master, spirit-servant', Was pitied by the Great God, And through the hands of Harukuniwake, And through his mouth, in detail, How grateful I am to have been taught The pure and precious Mioshihe. Having bathed in such precious divine grace, Should we not serve the Great Imperial God By grinding our bones and shattering our bodies? The wind has gradually softened, The spring air rises, On the plum branches, one or two flowers Have begun to bloom in the spring sky. Sent off by the leisurely voice of the bush warbler As it opens the door of the valley, How joyful is my advancing self. I, this Otora, who was known in the surroundings As a chivalrous person of the Ukiki forest,

Now, completely forged By the teachings of the Ananahi God, The demon has changed into a god, The dream of ego has fully awakened, And how light-hearted I feel, having become A child of the spirit from the bottom of my heart. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, Great God Susanoo-no-Ōkami, And the divine officials who follow him, Polish my spirit, which was clouded by evil, And make it a divine pillar. I worship from afar the precious sacred site Of my birthplace, and with reverence and awe, I pray to Thee. Though the Kajikatauge is steep, And though the blowing wind is cold, My body, filled with the divine love of God, Feels as if it were spring, Flowers bloom in the garden of my heart, Small birds sing and butterflies dance.

I have become a person of leisure. How foolish I was, struggling on the mountain of Kogita, Manipulated by nameless gods, Clouding my soul with gold and lust. Magahiko, from now on, you too, Together with Otora, engrave in your heart And dye in your breast The pure and precious Mioshihe of the Imperial God. Stand firm in sincerity alone, And do not become a captive of evil. With heart encouraged and body encouraged, Feeling rejuvenated, I know not why, My thoughts, which can even crush demons, To the limit of my strength, to the end of my body, How joyful is my self, ascending the path of God Which I must exhaust to the end. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, May your spirit be blessed.'

While singing thus, Otora, followed by Magahiko, finally arrived at the foot of the ascent of Kajikatauge. The flow of the mountain stream sent forth spray from the turbulent pools, and its murmuring seemed to wash the heart and soul. The tips of the trees had felt the arrival of spring and were swelling with buds, and the air was filled with the spirit of spring warmth, I know not why. The two descended to the mountain stream, washed their hands, rinsed their mouths, recited the Amatsu-Norito, and while gazing at the magnificent view of the mountain stream for a while,

Otora: 'How pure is this valley water running over the rocks, May the divine spirit of the Mizu be likewise. I feel as if my heart is being washed, By the sound of the water falling in the valley.'

Magahiko: 'Seeing this scenery, I cannot help but think Of the majestic figure of the divine spirit of the Mizu. Just the two of us, leaving the mountain of Kogita behind, We have come forward, following the will of God.'

Otora: 'Though the Kajikatauge may be steep, My body, longing for God, is at peace. Even the mountain of Kajika, where lions, bears, wolves, and jackals rage, Is according to the will of God. This slope I climb Is somehow joyful, somehow happy.'

Magahiko: 'The heart with which I scoop the valley water feels like the ancient days, It makes me think of the old age of the gods. How joyful it is to drink this valley water, Into which the command of the God Kototama-wake fell. This is the valley water that the God Kototama-wake scooped, Intending to go to the Amatsu-Mikuni. The mountain wind of the Kajikatauge, heard by reputation, Seems to be brushing away the dust of my body. Thinking that the blowing wind is the breath of the saving God, How happy is today's journey.'

魔我彦『この景色見るにつけても思ふかな 瑞の御霊の雄々しき姿を。 唯二人小北の山を後にして 進み来りぬ神のまにまに』

魔我彦『谷水を掬ぶ心はいそのかみ 古き神代の思ひせらるる。 言依別の神の命の落ち入りし この谷水を呑むぞ嬉しき。 言依別天津御国に往かむとて 掬びたまひしこれの谷水。 音に聞く河鹿峠の山颪 吾身の塵を払ふべらなり。 吹く風は救ひの神のみいきぞと 思へば嬉し今日の旅路よ』

Otora: 'Now then, while lingering with regret at the water, let us proceed to the mansion of Iso.'

Magahiko: 'Eagerly following you, Otora-dono, I too shall proceed to the mansion of Iso.'

Having finished singing thus, they set out walking once more. As Magahiko climbed the steep slope, he began to sing.

Magahiko: 'Come now, from here it is a mountain path. Otora-dono, take care. On the road, there are high stones. Untokodotsukoi, dotsukoishiyo. The flow of the valley is sōsō, playing the koto and blowing the flute. It sounds as if it is celebrating the path of us two. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. Following Takahime-sama, I built the head temple in Kitayamamura and opened the teaching. Even I, Magahiko of the twisted heart, was saved by Matsuhikotsukasa at the sacred ground of Kogita-yama. The clouds of confusion have cleared entirely, and having returned to Ananai-kyō, I proceed for the first time to the Iso mansion. The sins and transgressions I have committed against Kami until now, even if they were as high as a mountain, since I heard that the Ōkami of infinite mercy and compassion will look upon them with Nahohi, and listen with Kikinaho, and save me, I, though I have wounded feet, rely upon Kami for itsusaibanji and proceed. Even the moonlight crossing the Ōzora, though it may be covered by clouds and hide its form, when they clear, it is the same moon as before. Just as it illuminates the mountains and fields in all directions, the sun and moon in the heart of Magahiko, who has awakened to Kami, shine here once again. Understanding the true Kami well, and forgiven by Matsuhime-tsukasa, I go to worship at the sacred ground of Chin. My own lot is indeed joyful. Whether the morning sun shines or is cloudy, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if the earth should sink, the power of sincerity saves the world. No matter how steep this slope may be, since there is the protection of Kami, how could I not be able to proceed? Untokodotsukoi, haa haa haa. Although my breath has become labored, if I were to give up at a place like this, how could I serve the divine work? Otora-dono, you are, after all, an old person. Even though I, Magahiko, who am still young, am this out of breath, you are climbing steadily with a calm face. It is truly a mysterious thing. When I think of this, perhaps my repentance is still insufficient. Untokodotsukoi, haa haa haa.'

Magahiko: 'The slope is steeper than I thought. On one side, cliffs stand sheer, and on the other, a deep valley of a thousand fathoms. If I were to make a mistake with one step and lose my footing, and fall to my death—that vital life—what would I do? Although there is nothing left for me in this world, if I were to be born as a human with such effort and yet, without serving the divine work of the noble Kami, go to the Yūkai so easily, it would be the regret of my whole life. Please protect me, Ōmikami. Let me proceed without incident even through the corners of the path, Kamunagara. I pray before the presence of Kami. Untokodotsukoi, dotsukoishiyo. The slope has gradually become steeper.'

Magahiko: 'Otora-sama, please do not let me fall as you keep the pace. I am worried. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. I pray for the protection of Kamisama. Untokodotsukoi, dotsukoishiyo. Haa haa haa haa, I am out of breath. Shall we take a rest around here?'

Upon his saying this, Otora looked back.

Otora: 'Magahiko-sama, for one so young, there is a limit to how much you should complain. With such an attitude, can you serve the divine work of the Kami of heaven and earth? Is this slope, which an old person like me climbs without difficulty, so painful for you? It is a thing I cannot understand. You must surely have a Magakami lurking at the bottom of your belly, fearing to go to the sacred ground and writhing in agony. Quickly correct your heart, drive that deputy-guardian fellow out of your body as soon as possible, and become a spirit of supreme purity and refinement. Become a spirit like the clear water flowing through this valley, and from your heart, revere the Mizu-no-Mitama, and with one heart, recite the Amatsu-Norito, and stop singing such trivial songs. You are always noisy, a person with only a wide mouth. When climbing a steep slope, hold your mouth shut, hold your breath, keep your eyes on the step ahead, and go quietly, quietly, as if climbing the stairs of a two-story house. When you do that, no matter how steep the slope, you can climb it easily before you know it. This is the secret for a traveler walking the first mountain path. If you go about one ri, the sacred ground of the forest of the shrine will be waiting for us two. There must be many pure and noble Kamitsukasa there. Just one more breath, Magahiko.',

Otora: 'Let us whip the horse of our hearts and proceed with courage. If we climb while relying on the Kami of the forest of the shrine, any steep mountain path will be as if we are walking through a plain, and we will be able to reach the sacred ground easily and before we know it. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. Ōkami Susanoo, please bestow your noble grace and grant this Magahiko the strength to climb. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. May your spirit be blessed and prosperous.'

Singing thus, they barely managed to reach the sacred ground of the forest of the shrine. Here, many men and women, young and old, were gathered, and the sound of them all reciting the Amatsu-Norito in unison echoed through the spirits of the trees in the valley. Otora, together with Magahiko, was led to the front of the shrine by the guidance of Yoru, the person in charge of reception.
