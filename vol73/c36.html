Chapter 36: 荒 Field の駿 Horse [1867]

荒野の駿馬〔1867〕

§ 1

At the Great Palace of Takachihoyama and residing within the Palace of Takahi, tended by numerous Kami, the brilliant Kamutsukasa, fulfilling the divine command of the Lord Ōkami to perform noble miwaza, endured a hundred sufferings, leaving the mikoto of the Hime of Mitama behind, and hastening forth with lingering regret for the journey's end, escorted by Itsuhashira no Kami, crossing the turbulent current of the vast Himukagaha, and gradually arriving in Shinonomekuni, drawing close to Giyokusenkiyō, causing the mikoto of the Hime of Himuka to be born within the austere sanctuary, today setting out upon a lonely journey, straddling a shinme with rhythmic hoofbeats echoing with valor.

Heading toward the south, preceded by the hime of Yukue, separated in life from the hime of Yotsukasahime, leaving his beloved miko behind, resolved to serve in the miwaza according to the sacred decree of the Lord Ōkami, without distinction between day and night, buffeted in the face by storms, his departing figure is truly valiant.

To the right and left alike extend the wild plains; as far as the eye can reach, nothing is seen save the kayakusa swaying in the wind. The Mizu no Mitama, gazing upon this scene from atop his horse, deeply lamented within himself that to open such a desolate plain and give birth to Kami was no easy miwaza, and having traversed a thousand leagues of field-paths, he arrived at the wide river flowing across this place.

Alighting his horse upon the bank, he rested his breath for a while. Ah, the awe-inspiring Kami! The miwaza of creating the land and birthing the Kami in the distant age of the gods are feats of difficulty utterly beyond the imagination of modern people.

Compared to the Himukagaha he had crossed earlier, it was about one-twentieth of the flow, yet it was considerably wide, with deep rapids, flowing somewhat turbidly toward the west. Akitsuo no Kami stood upon the bank and chanted:

Akitsuo no Kami: 'Wishing to create the Kami who build the land, I have now come upon this Yokokawa; if the Kamamori Himegami should appear, I pray she will stay these rapids. Lonely in my core is my heart as dusk falls, standing here all alone on this riverbank. How shall I cross this current? Though I have a horse, the rapids are fierce; even my horse is startled and troubled by the sound of the waterfall raging like thunder. As I linger on the riverbank at dusk, the river wind rustles the bamboo grass; blowing and swirling, shaking the small bamboo violently, the wind is strong and ominous. Alone at the river's edge at dusk, I am quite helpless; I shall wait impatiently for the dawn sky. This heavily turbid current is ominous, appearing as though an ugly serpent lurks within. The journey I undertake, keeping the teachings of the Lord Kami to give birth to the Kami, is truly painful.

The Himegami of Yotsukasa must even now be ascending the lofty hall and calling out my name. Guessing the heart of Ōmononushi no Kami, it must have grown lonely by now. Surely it is mysterious how the sound of the rapids changes from time to time in this current.'

Even as he thus chanted these divine poems, Chikamiwo no Kami, following numerous Kami and riding a white horse, appeared and arrived here, respectfully welcoming the Mizu no Mitama and chanting a divine poem in response.

Chikamiwo no Kami: 'Knowing full well that the bright Mizu no Mitama has set forth, I come to welcome you. Yet how awe-inspiring it is that you are alone by the riverbank as night deepens; I pray you, mount this horse. The horse carrying the Mizu no Mitama is weary and ill-fitted to cross this swift river.'

At this divine poem, Akitsuo no Kami was emboldened, and directly responded with a poem.

Akitsuo no Kami: 'You who have come to welcome me, distressed upon this riverbank as the night grows late, the current of Yokokawa is turbid indeed, and I deem it as though a serpent deity lurks within. All alone by the riverbank where the wild wind sighs, I have grown lonely as the night wears on.'

Chikamiwo no Kami chanted:

Chikamiwo no Kami: 'Setting forth from the mansion of Giyokusenkiyō, I have pacified the raging Kami. The Kami who follow and arrive here with me were all hitherto raging Kami, yet subdued by the austere power of Kototama, they have become Kami who serve in the miwaza. I too, alone upon the back of a white horse, have come hither. Thinking that this river had not yet been crossed and that a serpent dwells here, I hesitated until today. Seeking and probing the ocean expanse where the waters run swiftly, I have led forth this valiant horse and arrived. Mount this horse, which neighs ever ahead; it is a steed born from the sacred water and fire of the Lord.'

近見男の神はうたひ給ふ。

§ 2

Akitsuo-no-Kami replies and chants:

'Since this is a steed born of the Lord God's Miiki, it is no ordinary horse but shall be a Kami. The Mitama of the Lord God, the Miiki, has condensed and condensed to become this steed, so that I may cross. No matter how high the current of the Yokogaha may be, this Shinme shall cross it with ease.'

Chikamio-no-Kami chants:

'Come then, Mizumitama and Momogami, follow me and cross over.'

No sooner had he finished chanting than he drove his steed toward the Gekiriu with a splash. Akitsuo-no-Kami and the Momogami, determined not to be left behind, tightened their Tazuna and applied their whips, and as if crossing the Gekiriu of the Tairiu, they ascended the southern bank Matsushigura.

Akitsuo-no-Kami, looking back at the current he had crossed, chants:

'How easily we crossed, thanks to the Mikoto of Chikamio-no-Kami and the protection of the Lord God. Thinking that this steed is the condensation of the Kototama of the Lord Ōkami, it is truly sacred. I have deeply realized at this Kahagoe that all things come into being through the power of Kototama. The Momogami have crossed the Hayakaha without a single one falling behind; how valiant they are.'

Chikamio-no-Kami replies and chants:

'The Kotoa of the Mizumitama-no-Kami makes me feel ashamed. I have merely invited this Hayakoma for the sake of Kimi, revering the Mikoto of the Lord God. From this day forth, I shall serve by your Misoba and assist in your noble Kamuwaza.'

Akitsuo-no-Kami chants:

'Though I came alone and lonely across the Ōnohara, now I meet you and it is lively. From now on, accompanied by the eleven Kami, I intend to open up the southern plains. No matter what mountains or rivers may lie across our path, since we have this steed, we shall cross with ease.'

Here, among the eleven Kami, a Kami of exceptionally tall stature advances his steed close to his side, and while raising his left hand toward the heavens and pointing his right hand toward the back of the horse, he chants a song before him:

'I am he who was born of the Kototama of A, and I shall serve in the retinue of Maruyahiko-no-Kami. How I have suffered morning and evening for the sake of the Majin while trying to build this land. Since the appearance of Chikamio-no-Kami, my Kamuwaza has begun to unfold. How many years and how many days have I waited to serve in the wake of the Mizumitama-no-Kami? I pray you, use me in your retinue, O sacred steed that shines with a pure heart.'

Akitsuo-no-Kami chants:

'Is Maruyahiko-no-Kami, of whom I have heard, a public figure? Your appearance is valiant and courageous. You must have appeared, Maruyahiko, to assist in the work of building the land and giving birth to the Kami. Rejoicing that this is the will of the Lord God, I shall permit you to join our journey.'

Maruyahiko-no-Kami chants with solemnity:

'I am grateful. As I hear the Kototama of Kimi, my soul dances forth. With a red, pure, and righteous heart as my shield, I shall serve by the side of Kimi.'

In the lead, Chikamio-no-Kami advances while trampling the grass, followed by Taigen Akitsuo-no-Kami, and then Maruyahiko-no-Kami, who leads the nine Kami, all riding with their bridles aligned, advancing through the great wilderness where no divine traces yet exist, while proclaiming songs of Kototama. Chikamio-no-Kami, riding gracefully, chants:

'The pampas grass field that knows no end, in the midst of this wilderness, together with the Mizumitama, to give birth to the Kunitamagami, how valiant it is to advance. Maruyahiko-no-Kami and Momogami, guard the Mizumitama well and advance with all your hearts. No matter how strong the storm, no matter how thick the ugly weeds, no matter if great serpents lurk here and there, how could I fear? Protected by the strict Kototama of the Lord God, even the winding paths shall be without mishap, and for a thousand or ten thousand ri, we shall advance straight and with ease. Though I know not what obstacles may lie in our path, the Kototama shall illuminate the Miiki and remove them, and the Kamuwaza of the divine mission shall be accomplished in every detail, until we report back before the presence of the Amatsu-Mi-Oya. Without flagging, without yielding, we shall advance. The Amatsu-Norito, the Great Norito, shall resound in the heavens to become the moon, and become the stars, shining and illuminating the path ahead. We are Kami, beings born of the majesty of Kototama. How could we fear the crooked? Ah, Kannagara, the strict Kototama is sacred.'

Maruyahikogami chants. The song is:

'From the Mitama of the Lord Ōkami, The sound of A was born, Water and fire solidified and emerged, Here is Maruyahikogami. To assist in the Miwaza of the Kamiu, Of the Mizu no Mitama, I hide my body in the Ōgahiogahi, And proclaim the Ikukototama. Just at that moment, the Shikogami, Upon the mountain ridges and river rapids, Obstruct and cause a hundred calamities, Upon hearing this, I wonder, How to kotomu and pacify them, While my heart is troubled, Descending from the Takahi no Miya, Chikamio no Kami appears, And while speaking the Makoto to each other, Uniting our hearts and Shinriki, To serve in the Miwaza, Of the Kuniu as one. While I was troubled in thought, Since hearing through the wind, Of the appearance of the Mizu no Mitama, Together with Chikamio no Kami, Leading the nine Kami, To serve as Mitomo, I came with joy and courage. Even if wandering in some Arano, As long as the Mizu no Mitama exists, No Kami shall fear, Planning and deliberating, we served, As Mitomo for the Miwaza. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, May the Itsu no Mitama be blessed, Use us, the Tohashirakamitachi, Forever without change, In your Miwaza, I pray earnestly, I pray earnestly.'

Here, the party of twelve, including the Mizu no Mitama Akitsuo no Kami, align the bridles of their white horses and proceed south, ever south.
