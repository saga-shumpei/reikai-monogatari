Volume 73 • Chapter 33

Chapter 33: 四 Horse の Distant 乗 [1864]

四馬の遠乗〔1864〕

§ 1

At this time, Ōmononushi-no-Kami served close to the side of Akitsuo-no-Kami, remaining in Giyokusenkiyō, while Chikamio-no-Kami, Masumi-no-Kami, Teruo-no-Kami, and Akeharu-no-Kami each took charge of the east, west, south, and north of Shinonomekoku, and served in the divine work of nation-building.

Akitsuo-no-Kami summoned the five Kami close to his side and gave his command:

Ōmononushi-no-Kami, remain in the palace and assist in my divine work. Akeharu-no-Kami, go forth to the east and guide the divine Miko. Teruo-no-Kami, travel through the western lands; bring life to the Kami and illuminate the land. May you open up the northern lands, for both heaven and earth, through the noble achievements of Masumi-no-Kami. May the form of Takateru-no-Yama be clear, and Chikamio-no-Kami, guard the southern region.

Thereupon, revering the Mikoto of Akitsuo-no-Kami, the five Kami each prepared to head toward the directions commanded and chanted their Miuta.

The Mikoto of Ōmononushi-no-Kami is the Mikoto of the Hikogami; revering it, I shall serve nearby. Until the beautiful Miko is born, I shall not move, for I have set my heart, O Ōmononushi. Even though the Mizumitama may have its waxing and waning, I shall not change, serving in the Way. The land of Shinonome is refreshing, and the blowing wind is gentle and clear, and the fruits of the trees ripen. Ōmononushi-no-Kami, who grows and stands in the Kunihara, I shall guard your prosperity until the end of Ikuyo.

Thus did Ōmononushi-no-Kami reply with a Miuta.

Thereupon, Akeharu-no-Kami chanted a Miuta.

The eastern sky is here, and I, Akeharu-no-Kami, shall advance to build the nation. The glorious Kami declared that I should open up the east of the land of the Shinonome-no-Kami. Come then, I shall ascend pointing toward the east. May there be divine fortune on the journey I take. To repay the command of the Hikogami, I shall devote my heart morning and evening. O Mizu-no-Mitama, shining in this palace of Giyokusen, dwell in peace. The mountain of Takateru shall be hidden in the clouds. As I head toward the far eastern land, following the clear flow of the Hinata River, I shall advance to the eastern land. I shall ride upon the white steed bestowed by Kawamori-hime and travel a long journey.

Akitsuo-no-Kami chanted:

O, the valiant words of Akeharu-no-Kami! Even the Lord Ōkami shall be stirred to courage. The figure of Akeharu-no-Kami, setting out astride the back of a steed, is truly valiant.

Yotsugi-hime-no-Kami chanted:

The Kami who leaves the Giyokusen palace behind and whips his horse on its back is truly valiant. Akeharu-no-Kami shall surely whip his steed and ride across the wilderness.

Ōmononushi-no-Kami, while gazing far off at the figure of Akeharu-no-Kami as he set out, chanted:

The pace of the steed is swift; the figure of Akeharu-no-Kami has blurred into the mist of the fields. He has set out, whipping his steed; valiant is the figure of Akeharu-no-Kami. If Akeharu-no-Kami travels around the east, heaven and earth shall surely clear up more and more. Revering the Mikoto of the Hikogami, he sets out; tears well up in my heart. For his figure as he sets out so valiantly, I have no words of praise.

Chikamio-no-Kami, intending to open up the southern land according to the command, mounted a white horse and chanted with a valiant voice:

Akitsuo-no-Kami and Hime-gami, come then, I shall make my departure for the south. I shall not forget the words of the Hikogami's command even for a moment, and I shall open up the land. While being sent off by the spring breeze, I am valiant as I head toward the southern land. From today, there is no way to gaze upon the moon reflected in Giyokusen, and it is with lingering regret. I am leaving this palace where the moon met the moon and conceived the Miko. O two pillars, and first of all, Ōmononushi-no-Kami and Kawamori-hime, dwell in health. Even if I am far away in the southern Kunihara, I shall not forget you, Kimi, even for a short while. Come then, Ōmononushi, serve morning and evening and nurture the Miko. May the Mikoto of Kawamori-hime-no-Kami be guarded closely by Yotsugi-hime-no-Kami.

As he chanted this, he leaped lightly onto the back of the white horse, and with the sound of hooves clicking and his mane fluttering in the spring breeze, he departed. While watching his valiant figure go, Akitsuo-no-Kami chanted a Miuta:

What a valiant departure it is; Chikamio-no-Kami sets out for the Kunihara, whipping his steed. I am reminded of the divine work of guiding the Kami as he crosses to the southern land. The Kototama of the noble Chikamio-no-Kami shall ring and ring, and all things shall surely be revived.

原文（近代文語）

茲に大物主の神は顕津男の神の御側近く仕へ、玉泉郷に留り給ひ、近見男の神、真澄の神、照男の神、明晴の神は各も各も東雲国の東西南北を受持ち、国造りの神業に仕へ給ひぬ。

顕津男の神は五柱の神を御側近く招きて依さし給はく、

『大物主神は館に留りて わが神業を補ひ奉らへ 明晴の神は東に出でまして 神の御子等を導き給へ 照男の神は西の国をば経廻りて 神を生かせよ国を照せよ 北の国を拓かせ給へ天も地も 真澄の神の貴の功績に 高照の山の姿も明らけく 近見男の神は南方を守らへ』

茲に顕津男の神の神言畏みて、五柱の神は各々依さしの方に向はむとして御歌詠み給ふ。

『大物主神の神言は比古神の 神言かしこみ近く仕へむ 美し御子生れます迄は動かじと こころ定めし大物主よ 瑞御霊月の満干はありとても 吾は変らじ道に仕へて 東雲の国は爽けし吹く風も やはく清しく木の実はみのる 国原に生ひ立つ大物主の神 栄え守らむ幾世の末まで』

と大物主の神は御歌もて答へ給ひぬ。

茲に明晴の神は御歌詠まし給ふ。

『東の御空はここに明晴の 神は進みて国造りせむ 東雲の神の御国の東を 拓けと宣りし畏き神はや いざさらば東をさして上るべし 吾行く旅に神の幸あれ 比古神の依さしに報い奉らむと われは朝夕こころ尽さむ 玉泉これの館にかがやける 瑞の御霊よ安くましませ 高照の山は雲井に隠るべし 遠く東の国に向へば 日向河清き流を伝ひつつ 吾は進まむ東の国へ 河守比女賜ひし白き駿馬に 跨り行かむ旅路はるけく』

顕津男の神は謡ひ給ふ。

『明晴の神の雄々しき言の葉よ 主の大神も勇み給はむ 駿馬の背に跨り出で立たす 明晴の神の姿雄々しも』

世司比女の神は謡ひ給ふ。

『玉泉館を後に駒の背に 鞭ち給ふ神ぞ雄々しき 明晴の神はいそいそ駿馬に 跨り荒野を鞭たすらむ』

大物主の神は、明晴の神の出立ち給ふ御姿を遥に打眺めつつ謡ひ給ふ。

『駿馬の足並速し明晴の 神の御姿野辺に霞みつ 駿馬に鞭たしつつ出でましぬ すがた勇し明晴の神 明晴の神の東に廻りませば 天地ますます晴れ渡るべし 比古神の神言畏み出で立たす 神の心に吾涙湧きぬ 勇しく出で立ち給ふ御姿に 称への言葉吾無かりける』

近見男の神は依さしの儘に南の国を拓かむとして、白馬に跨り声勇しく謡ひ給ふ。

『顕津男の神比女神よいざさらば 吾は南に鹿島立ちせむ 比古神の依さしの言葉片時も 吾は忘れず国を拓かむ 春風に送られながら南の 国に向はむ吾ぞ勇し 玉泉写らす月も今日よりは 拝むよしなし名残惜しくも 月と月見逢ひて御子を孕ませる これの館を吾は去り行く 二柱初め大物主の神 河守比女よ健かに坐せ 南の国原遠くさかるとも 岐美は忘れじ束の間さへも いざさらば大物主よ朝夕を 仕へて御子を育み給へ 河守比女神の神言よ世司の 比女神近く守らせ給へ』

と御謡詠ましつつ白馬の背にひらりと飛び乗り、蹄の音もカツカツと鬣を春風に靡かせ給ふ。其雄々しき御姿を見送り見送りつつ、顕津男の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『勇しき出立ちなるかも近見男の 駒にむちうち立たす国原 南の国に渡らひ神々を 導き給ふ神業偲ばゆ 近見男の貴の言霊鳴り鳴りて 総てのものは甦るらむ』

§ 2

Ōmononushi-no-Kami also sings:

Chikamio-no-Kami has departed for the south / Whipping his silver steed / Across the great plain where the Tokijiku of white plum blossoms scent the air / How he whips his steed, Chikamio-no-Kami.

Yotsukasahime-no-Kami sings:

How valiant is the figure of Chikamio-no-Kami / The shadow of his swift horse has vanished from sight / The great plain is veiled in purple mist, and the white plum blossoms / Shall scent the four quarters to welcome him / From now I shall know the Isao of Chikamio-no-Kami / Who has acted so valiantly and bravely / I shall remain in this mansion with Hikojigami / And pray for the fulfillment of the Miwaza.

Kahamorihime-no-Kami sings:

Up to the Himukagaha, I have guarded this Kunihara / Until this Kefu / Hearing that Chikamio-no-Kami is to open the south / I am joyful and my Mitama is at peace / The Kunihara of the four directions / Shall be peacefully opened by the Yohashira-no-Kami / From this Kefu, I shall make the Yohashira-no-Kami my strength / And serve in the mansion with a peaceful heart.

Thereupon, Teruo-no-Kami, intending to depart as he was guided, mounted his white horse, stood in the Teijiyō, and sang a farewell Miuta:

To Hikojigami, who is born as the Mitama of Tsukiyomi / Now I must part, may you dwell in peace / I go forth to open the Kuniguni at the foot of the Takateru mountains / Tamaizumi, may you remain clear and pure for Tokoshihe / O you two Kami, may you dwell in clarity / O Kototama of Ōmononushi-no-Kami, now farewell / May you be blessed as you serve in the mansion / Yotsukasahime-sama, may your heart be at peace / And may you bear a child and prosper evermore / Upon the swift horse bestowed by Kahamorihime-no-Kami / I shall mount and head toward the west / The moon and the sun float morning and evening in Tamaizumi / I depart, leaving this Kefu as a memory.

As he finished singing thus, he nimbly mounted the horse, applied the whip two or three times, and galloped off toward the direction of Mount Takateru with a resounding sound.

Akitsuo-no-Kami, watching the valiant figure of Teruo-no-Kami from afar, sings:

Is it the swiftness of the swift horse upon the great plain / That has hidden Teruo-no-Kami in the mist / The Kami at the foot of Mount Takateru / Shall also prosper in peace, now that this Kami is present.

Yotsukasahime-no-Kami sings a Miuta:

O the valiant figure of the Kami, the swift horse / Has already become invisible to the eye / Galloping across the great plain that mists as far as the eye can see / The sound of the horse's hooves echoes / Mount Takateru wears white clouds as a belt / To welcome the Kami of the white horse.

Ōmononushi-no-Kami sings a Miuta:

The swift figure of the swift horse has vanished / The valiant journey of Teruo-no-Kami / Leaving behind a thousand memories, valiantly / Teruo-no-Kami has set forth / If Teruo-no-Kami is present at Mount Takateru / Even the monstrous serpent shall not be able to lurk there.

Kahamorihime-no-Kami sings a Miuta:

The moon and the sun clearly shine, Teruo-no-Kami, the ruler / Has departed for the western land / Mounted upon the horse I gifted as my Mitama / His departing figure was truly valiant / My Mitama shall dwell in the horse and, together with the ruler / Peacefully guard the western land.

Masumi-no-Kami, intending to open the northern land, announced his departure to the various Kami, and while mounting his horse, he held his left hand over his head and sang a Miuta:

In such an age, O Taigen Akitsuo-no-Kami / Now, now I shall depart, I, Masumi-no-Kami / You have surely completed the Miwaza of bearing the divine child / The Isao of the Kami is truly noble / Because you carry the precious child, my heart is at peace / And I depart / Yotsukasahime-sama, morning and evening, take care / And guard the child in your womb / O Kototama of Ōmononushi-no-Kami, guard the two Kami / And may you be safe / Through the true words of Kahamorihime-no-Kami, it has become an age / Where the precious child is born / I shall open the northern land in the midst of the Himukagaha / And make it a pure place of heaven and earth, Masumi / Now farewell, O Kami who guard the mansion / I shall advance to the northern land.

Having finished singing, he straightened the horse's head, gave it a lash of the whip, and set out with the sound of bells ringing valiantly, shanko-shanko.

Akitsuo-no-Kami, lamenting the parting, sings a Miuta:

Valiantly, Masumi-no-Kami has departed / My heart remains with the Kami of the mansion / The greater part of the Kami who guarded me morning and evening / Have now departed / The Yohashira-no-Kami have departed far away / Why is this mansion so lonely / If Masumi-no-Kami departs to the northern land / The mountains and the great plains shall suddenly prosper / His figure as he whips his swift horse / Resembles the rapids of the Himukagaha / Not disobeying my words, the Yohashira-no-Kami / Have vied to be the first to depart.

Yotsukasahime-no-Kami sings a Miuta:

This mansion, from which the Yohashira-no-Kami have departed / Is lonely, and the wind has calmed / Soon, having finished creating a beautiful land / The day they shine is awaited.

Ōmononushi-no-Kami sings a Miuta:

Following Hikojigami, the five Kami who came / The Yohashira-no-Kami have departed / Parted from the Yohashira-no-Kami, I now / Shall serve closely the two Kami / The land of Shinonome shall from this Kefu, in ever-increasing prosperity / Open and flourish without end.

Kahamorihime-no-Kami sings again:

The Yohashira-no-Kami have valiantly departed / Intending to open this Kunihara / Settling abundantly and most abundantly in this mansion / Create the land, O Mizu-no-Mitama.

原文（近代文語）

大物主の神も謡ひ給ふ。

『近見男の神は南に出でましぬ 白銀の駒に鞭たせつつ 白梅の非時香る大野原 鞭たすかも近見男の神は』

世司比女の神は謡ひ給ふ。

『勇しき近見男の神の姿かな 駿馬のかげ見えなくなりぬ 大野原紫にかすみ白梅は 四方に香りて迎へ奉らむ 勇しく雄々しくませる近見男の 神の功績を今よりぞ知る この館に比古遅の神と籠り居て 神業のなるを祈り奉らむ』

河守比女の神は謡ひ給ふ。

『日向河限りに此の国原を 今日まで吾は守りこしはや 近見男の神は南を拓かすと 聞けば嬉しも吾魂安し 西東南北なる国原も 安く拓けむ四柱の神に 今日よりは四柱の神を力とし 心安けく館に仕へむ』

茲に照男の神は依さしの儘に出で立たむとして白馬に跨り、庭上に立ちて別れの御歌詠まし給ふ。

『月読の御霊と生れます比古遅神に いざや別れむ安くましませ 高照の山の麓の国々を 拓かむとして吾は行くなり 玉泉いや永久に清らけく 澄みてましませ二柱の神 大物主神の神言よいざさらば 幸くあれませ館に仕へて 世司の比女神心安らけく 御子生みまして弥栄えませ 河守比女の神の賜ひし駿馬に われ跨りて西に向はむ 月も日も朝夕浮ぶ玉泉 今日を名残と吾は行くなり』

斯く謡ひ終るや、ひらりと馬背に跨り二つ三つ鞭を加へて、トウトウと高照山の方向さして駆け出し給ふ。

顕津男の神は、照男の神の勇ましき姿を遥に見送りながら、

『駿馬の足はやみかも大野原 靄にかくれし照男の神はも 高照の山の麓の神々も 安く栄えむ此の神いまさば』

世司比女の神は御歌謡ひ給ふ。

『勇ましき神の姿よ駿馬の 早くも見えずなりにけらしな 目路の限り霞む大野を駆けて行く 駒の蹄の音ひびくなり 高照の山は白雲帯にして 白馬の神を迎へ奉らむ』

大物主の神は御歌謡ひ給ふ。

『駿馬の早御姿は隠ろひぬ 照男の神の勇ましき旅 千万の名残を後に勇ましく 照男の神は出で立ちにけり 高照の山も照男の神まさば 醜の大蛇も籠り得ざらむ』

河守比女の神は御歌謡ひ給ふ。

『月も日も清く照男の神司 西の御国に出でましにけり 吾魂と贈りし駒に跨りて 出でます姿雄々しかりける 吾魂は駒にいつきて西の国を 安く守らむ神司と共に』

真澄の神は北の国を拓き給はむと、諸神に暇をつげ立出むとして駒の背に跨りながら、左手を頭上に翳しつつ御歌詠まし給ふ。

『かかる世に太元顕津男の神よ いざいざ立たむ真澄神吾 御子生みの神業を確に終へ給ふ 神の功績ぞ尊かりける 貴の御子宿らせ給ふと村肝の 心安らぎ吾は出で行く 世司の神よ朝夕心して 御腹の御子を守らせ給へ 大物主神の神言よ二柱の 神を守りてまさきくありませ 河守の比女の真言に貴の御子 生れます神世となりにけらしな 日向河中に拓かむ北の国 天地真澄の清所となさばや いざさらば館を守る神々よ 吾は進まむ北方の国へ』

と謡ひ終り駒の頭を立て直し一鞭あてて鈴の音も勇ましくシヤンコシヤンコと立ち出で給ふ。

顕津男の神は別れを惜しみて御歌詠まし給ふ。

『勇ましく真澄の神は出でましぬ 館の神に心のこしつ 朝夕に我を守りし神々の 其大方は出で立ちましぬる 四柱の神は遥けく出でまして 何か淋しき此の館かも 北の国に真澄の神の出でまさば 頓に栄えむ山も大野も 駿馬に鞭ち立たす御姿は 日向の河の早瀬に似たるも 我言葉反き給はず四柱は 先を争ひ出でましにける』

世司比女の神は御歌詠まし給ふ。

『四柱の神の立たせし此館は うら淋しもよ風もしづみて やがて今美しき国を造り終へ かがやき給はむ日こそ待たるる』

大物主の神は御歌詠ひ給ふ。

『比古神に従ひてこし五柱の 神四柱は出でましにけり 四柱の神に別れて吾は今 近く仕へむ二柱の神に 東雲の国は今日より弥栄に 拓け栄えて果しなからむ』

河守比女の神は又謡ひ給ふ。

『四柱の神勇ましく出でましぬ 此の国原を拓かむとして 此館に豊に太豊に鎮まりて 国造りませ瑞の御霊よ』
