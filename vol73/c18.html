Chapter 18: 佐田の辻 [1849]

佐田の辻〔1849〕

§ 1

Takateruyama towers high into the Unpeu, piercing the Sora, with no corner of its ridges or mountain skirts left untouched. The Tokihagi are lush, and the Momobana compete in brilliance, weaving spaces between them as if they were brocade. The Harukaze blowing gently scatters the Kuwaben in the Yomo, while the Haukau fills the air with increasing fragrance. The Kariyōbinga sings clearly of the spring in the Tengoku.

The earth is a Hanamushiro in its entirety, and Goshiki butterflies flutter, playing among the flowers of the Haruno. The Saukuwai is boundless. The retinue of the Kami Akitsuo proceeds leisurely, riding upon Hakuba and the backs of Kirin. Hōō dance wildly in the Sora, and the hundred Kamitachi, one after another, chant the Amatsunorito and proclaim the Kototama of Iwa, offering them up.

The Sora and the earth face each other, and they advance as if flowing toward the sacred grounds of Takateruyama. The departure of the Kami Taigen Akitsuo is indeed pure. Resolving to create the land from this point on and to give birth to the Kunitamagami, he exerts his heroic spirit, advancing while gazing back at the Higashinomiya and Sakaishiro-yama. The heart of the Kami is truly valiant.

In the clear, vast Sora, the light of the moon crossing over is pure, and the cool breeze brushes against the face in the night fields, even at an unusual hour. There is nothing to hinder them. Ah, Kannagara-kamigami, the departure of the Kami Akitsuo on his kusamakura journey is pure.

Due to the urgent report of the Kami Ōmimoya, the Kami of the Keirin gathered at Takahinomiya, a sacred site at the foot of Takateruyama, waiting impatiently for the descent of the Kami Akitsuo. Among the Kami gathered at Takahinomiya, there was one chosen to wait and welcome them by the roadside; the name of this Kami is the Kami Menachio. He is also called the Kami of Eyes. The Kami of Eyes, accompanied by the Kami Ajitoyo and the Kami Teruo, waited at the crossroads in the wilderness where flowers were blooming in profusion, awaiting the arrival of the retinue.

Watching the heroic figure of the Kami Akitsuo advancing under the guidance of the words of the Kami Ōmimoya, the Kami of Eyes sang with joy and courage:

Keganaku mo ware machiwabishi kai arite / Akogare no Kimi wa ima ya kitamaseru / Ōmimoyagami no kamigoto ni shitagauite / Kono jūjiro ni Kimi machimukau

The Kami Akitsuo, from upon his horse, replied:

Nanji koso wa Menachio no Kami nare ya / Waga na o yoku mo satori imaseru / Higashinomiya o tachide kunitsukuru / Kamiwaza osore ware kitsuru kamo / Tada hitori tabi ni tatsu mi to omoishi o / Ware o mukahuru kimi zo tōtoki / Nushi no Kami no kamiwaza ososhi ware wa ima / Kuni tsukuran to koko ni idekoshi / Ōmimoyagami no kamigoto no michibiki ni / Yashima no kawara mo yasuku watarinu / Yashimakawa no kiyoki ga gotoku kunihara ni / Chiri-hokori sae nakare to omou

The Kami of Eyes stood upright before the horse, correcting his posture with dignity:

Nushi no Kami no tsukurashetamau shibi no kuni mo / Kimi nakariseba osamemu yoshinashi / Momogami no minikusa no sayari wa shigekutomo / Nanji ga Kototama ni nabiki fusu beshi / Takateru no yama wa ososhi Kimi imasaba / Kami no mi-itsui wa yomo o hikarusamu

The Kami Akitsuo, from upon his horse, calmly recited a poem:

Hisakata no ame ni jitsugetsu kagayakite / Hikari shitamau hamu Takateru no yama / Murasaki no kumo no tsutsumishi Takateru no / Yama o akashite kamiyo o osamemu

The Kami Nyokohime, from upon her horse, said:

Harubaru to Kimi o mukaeshi me no kami no / Tōto no kō ni kansha shi-tatematsuramu / Ware wa mo yo onna ni shi aredomo Takateru no / Yama no kumokiri harawamu to zo omou / Akitsuo no Kami no chikara to narimashite / Kuni-hara o hirake me no kami / Ware mo mata Akitsuo no Kami to morotomo ni / Tōto no kamiwaza tsukaemu to omou

The Kami of Eyes replied to this:

Ōoshiku mo norashetamau koto no ha yo / Nyoko no Kami wa tōto no masuraome / Iza saraba omotomo ni tsukae-tatematsuru beshi / Koma wa nakeredo hizakurige nite

Singing thus, they waved the masakaki back and forth, clearing away the evil spirits of the mountains and fields, and pushed into the great highlands of Takateruyama as if an arrow were flying. The cheers of the myriad Kami were louder and clearer than before, and the Kami, without distinction of good or evil, high or low, knelt on both sides of the road to welcome the retinue.

斯く謡ひて真榊を打振り打振り山野の邪気を払ひながら、高照山の大高原を矢の進むが如く分け入り給ふ。万の神の歓呼の声は以前に勝りて益々高くさやけく、神は善悪上下の区別なく道の両側に跪きて一行を迎へ奉る。
