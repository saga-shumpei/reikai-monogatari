Chapter 7: High 鳴 [1437]

高鳴〔1437〕

§ 1

Though the flowers of words bloom in double and triple layers, not even a single fruit is borne upon the Yamabuki, serving merely as mimic grass of teachings. While placing the domicile of its mitama in the deepest bottom of the inferno, clinging to the single pillar of truth of the Ōkami of Sokotsuihane, Takahime, who firmly believed this to be the mandate of heaven to save this world and the living shrine of Hinodewake, rejected one by one every noble teaching because it did not attune to her own soul. Selecting and picking out the teachings written by the living mitama of Henjiyōnanshi as if sorting through a mandala, she interpreted them arbitrarily from her own clouded mind, letting both body and soul be clouded by the magatama possessing her.

To exhaust one's murakimo heart for the sake of God alone and for the sake of the yobito with all one's might is a pathetic thing indeed. Having her mitama extracted by Mokusuke of Eugenbau, she fell perilously from the midair into the wilderness of Calcuta in India where weeds grew thick and overgrown. Her spirit successfully detached its body and arrived at the Yachimata barrier station, where two gatekeepers, one red and one white, released her out of pity. Wandering around far and near the Yachimata of heaven, she built a hermitage at the foot of a rocky mountain, and acting like the Datsueba of the Sanzu River, she inspected the spirits coming to the netherworld, leading them back one by one to her hall.

While spitting foam from the corners of her mouth, she preached disjointed doctrines with undivided and chaotic intensity. Her zeal was of a force that might scorch the heavens and burn the earth; yet even the Ōkami of supreme compassion, having no means to save her, left Takahime alone at her own will for three years, closing His eyes and waiting for the time when she would awaken of herself. Not being able to comprehend such an awe-inspiring Ōkami's august will, the spirit possessing her—believed by her to be the supreme and pure divine spirit, the mandate of heaven of Hinodewake, and the Ōkami of Sokotsuihane, though deceived by the spirit of crookedness—was truly a pitiful thing to trust in.

In the very middle of the Yachimata crossroads, she happened to meet a group of four people, whom she lured with clever words and led back to her hall. Anxious to save them for heaven with all her heart and exerting every effort, Takahime emptied her mind while the Brahmins Hell and Kelina resisted, glaring at each other with eyes fixed, and huddled in a small room with their white and black eyes glaringly rolled up. Just at that moment, was it a water rail or a thief pounding on the front door, or was it the sound of a storm passing by? Whatever it might be, wishing to reveal herself at the threshold and probe the truth or falsehood while glaring at the four men and women, she stepped down into the garden and slid open the front door—how bewildering!

Hearing that a runaway samurai of Brahmanism was the boss of a band of thieves, Takahime widened her eyes, determined in her heart to teach the truth through the kototama of God's teachings and save him, she invited him in and led him before the four people. Ah, Kannagara kamigami-kami! May the august mitama of God bestow blessings so that Takahime and the other five spirits may be made to submit to the true teachings of the Ōkami and be saved as swiftly as a single moment, we humbly pray. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if the great earth should sink, the power of truth saves the world. However Takahime, full of falsehood and erring from the path of truth, may spread her teachings, as long as the righteous Kami exists, how could her purpose ever be achieved?

Nevertheless, since good people abide in the virtue of love and goodness and bathe in and serve the radiance of truth and faith, good and evil, right and wrong instantly flutter and shift across the sun and moon of the heart's sky; yet the spirits whose hearts are clouded by the Makatsumi treat evil conversely as good, misunderstand falsehood as truth, and go mad all the more—how pitiful! How could the Puritans of Izanagi-Izanami and the fully saved spirits ever lend their noble ears to the scandalous words of crookedness? Their eyes blinded, their ears blocked, and their spirits defiled, spirits gather by the affinity of spirit to spirit, just as ants swarm to sweetness, rejoicing and rushing together to conviction that the teachings of falsehood and unrighteousness are supreme, and rejoicing in adoration.

Ah, Kannagara kamigami-kami! Measuring the august heart of the great mercy of God, my myriad bolts of tears flow like a river. Down below this river appears the Sanzu River as Datsueba, and it is a pitiful thing to cross the flow of the Jordan where the spirits of the visible and invisible worlds wash their hearts. That Mizuho Ōnisaburo respectfully reveres and offers his loyal heart before the august presence of the Kami of Izanagi-Izanami, praying earnestly that this tragic situation be saved with all possible speed.

§ 2

Takahime turned toward the man who had just arrived, gazing fixedly and piercingly into his face, and said,

Takahime: 'Ha! Your countenance is overflowing with a murderous aura. I daresay you are up to some thievery, aren\'t you?'

Beru: 'Well, I never! To be called a thief the moment I step in here is quite a shock. Indeed, as you are pleased to say, we are not thieves by origin. Accompanying the general of Oniharuwake, retainer of Ōkuronushi who manifested in the capital of Haruna in the Moon Realm, right in the midst of our advance to the mansion of Iso, the general\'s subordinates Katahiko and Kumehiko were utterly crushed by the Kototama of Harukuniwake, the proselytizer of Ananai-kyō, and having retreated to the Forest of Ukiki, we divided the army into two here, one led by Oniharuwake and the other by Ranchi, each commanding three thousand riders, ravaging the land of Biku, next setting up camp on Mount Ikarashi, and just as we were making our martial might shine in all directions, we were shattered once more by the divine army of Harukuniwake, whereupon both Generals Oniharuwake and Kumehiko surrendered to Ananai-kyō, and we met with the misfortune of disbanding, and now we are going about here and there doing thievery of stripping things away without even wanting to. But surely there is no reason for you to blame me by calling me a thief! A thief does his utmost as a thief and strives for the prosperity of his trade, so won\'t you please stop calling us names? If I am a thief, the four persons here are also thieves. Besides, there is not a single person in this world who does not possess the nature of a thief, though there may be differences between direct and indirect. Nay, not only is there no one without the nature of a thief, but there can be no one, whoever they may be, who would not steal even a single piece of straw-matting.'

Takahime: 'Ho ho ho ho! They say even a thief has three-tenths of an argument, so arguments can be made for anything after all, but I have never heard of anyone boasting about being a thief like you do. Have a little shame! That is why the Gods say, "The people of today are the thieves of Heaven, it is an age of thieves." If you were doing it timidly and stealthily, there would be something rather cute about it, but when you begin to swagger about and shout loudly that you are a thief, it is truly the end of the world. That is why the Great Deity of the Deepest Roots is going to make the Great Renewal this time, so that there will be neither ogres nor great serpents nor thieves. If you do not reform yourself reasonably as well, the future is terrifying, you know.'

Beru: 'Ha ha ha ha! There is a saying about "the monkey laughing at its own red bottom," but even if we are thieves, we merely steal money and goods. More than that, a great thief—nay, a thief of Heaven—seems to be right here. This Beru has no desire whatsoever to listen to an ogre\'s prayers!'

Takahime: 'If you say that a thief of Heaven is dwelling right here, whose business is that? Since you glared at my face and called me a thief of Heaven, you must have mistaken this genuine living palace and called it a thief of Heaven, mustn\'t you?'

Beru: 'Of course I mean you, just think about it carefully. Isn\'t it that the living palace of Henshiji-Gensei no Mitama tightly clasps the divine revelation transmitted by Ōkunotokotachi no Mikoto deep within its belly and embezzles it as its own? And then to grow arrogant, claiming to be righteousness itself in Heaven or the living palace of the Great Deity of the Deepest Roots—truly, there can be no greater sin, evil, or falsehood that Heaven and Earth will not tolerate than this. Therefore, where could there be any mistake in this Beru calling you a great thief and a thief of Heaven? If you have any objections, let me have an explanation before Beru.'

So saying, he pulled his folding stool closer and pressed her sharply with cutting words.

Takahime: 'Ho ho ho ho ho! What an utterly incomprehensible man you are! To call a thief the living palace upon which the ancestors of this very world—the root of the root of a single, pure, genuine Japanese spirit—descend, shows the height of ignorance. As long as blind and deaf creatures like you block the mundane world, the accomplishment of the Divine Government can never be achieved! No matter what you say, since your spirit has fallen into hell, it seems that while dust on others\' eyes is visible, the beam in your own eye is not; now look at these two men, Sharu and Rokuzō, and reform yourselves. This is the crucial moment, you know. As subjects, what on earth are you talking about, babbling about good and evil? Does not the teaching of Ananai-kyō also indicate that "the God will appear to the fore and clearly separate good and evil"? There is none other than the God who separates good and evil. Furthermore, there is none who separates them other than the fundamental Miroku-sama; lesser gods cannot do it. Therefore, what this Takahime, who performs the service of the fundamental God, says is the mind of the Great Deity, so even if it does not accord with your heart, if you simply obey precisely as Takahime says and reform your conduct, you can perform wondrous services in the phenomenal world, the divine world, and the nether world alike.'

Takahime: '...'

Rokuzau: 'Takahime-sama, no matter what you say, I simply cannot trust you at all; I have been observing your facial expression since a while ago, and not a single divine trait is manifesting in it. Outwardly you smile and pretend, but deep down, indescribably ominous aspects and looks of hatred are appearing. It is said that "the human countenance is the index of the mind," and no matter what, one cannot conceal the inner state; it is bound to manifest outwardly, you see.'

Takahime: 'Ah, ah, not a single person understands the spirits in any of this. The Kamisama surely spoke thus: "If there are three sincere people, the rebuilding and direct renovation of the Sanzensekai can be accomplished." Remembering those words now, truly there is nothing to do but marvel. I too have long worked with such devotion for the sake of Kamisama and for the sake of Yobito, exerting myself with bone-crushing and body-shattering efforts, yet can I still not find even a single Chiki? How sad, how pitiful; truly I have grown sick of this Ukiyo.'

Sharu: 'I say, Takahime-sama, I believe your words through and through. There is no mistake that you are the living shrine of the true primordial Ōkamisama. Would you not please make me your disciple?'

Takahime: 'Ho ho ho ho! Indeed, I anticipated from the very beginning that you were a clever fellow from parts unknown. As expected, the eyes of the God of Hinode are not mistaken. Listen, you pack of thieves, even if it is something Takahime says, if sincerity alone resides in your heart, it will sink into your bellies just like this Sharu's does. That you do not understand is simply because your hearts are clouded. Reform your minds a little, lest a bird starts up right from under your feet.'

At that moment, from out of nowhere, the sound of a conch shell drawing near echoes like a mountain resonance, boo-u, boo-u; ah, Kannagara Tamachihau Mase.
