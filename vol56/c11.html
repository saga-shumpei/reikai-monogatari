Chapter 11: 惚 Mud [1441]

惚泥〔1441〕

§ 1

Kiudaukoji, together with Heru and Princess Kelina, proceeds through the overgrown wilderness toward the mansion of Okuniwake of Mount Telumon.

Human traffic is scarce, weeds grow luxuriant enough to submerge one's body across the uniform plain, and clusters of briars are interspersed here and there, preventing the path from making the expected progress.

Various flowers bloom and emit fragrance all over the wilderness, and from time to time vipers appear at one's feet, making walking extremely dangerous.

The sun has completely set. The moon has begun to peek from among the thickets in the east.

To the north, the lofty peak of Mount Telumon stands in majestic solemnity.

Wafted on the evening wind, the sound of the evening bell echoes mournfully as the transience of all things.

Crowes with white and red spots seal the sky and hurry on their return journey toward the direction of Mount Telumon while cawingly crying.

The three of them advanced, making the moonlight their guide.

Nevertheless, it is utterly impossible to evade the danger of the vipers crawling at their feet.

No matter how high the moon climbs, it is blocked by the long, slender weeds and moreover is not as clear as day; if one should mistakenly step even on a viper's tail, there is the danger of being bitten instantly and losing one's life on the spot.

Kiudaukoji advances while chanting Kannagara Kamusakiwasese and also reciting the Heavenly Number Song; Heru and Princess Kelina, considering their divine virtue still insufficient, refrain from chanting the number song, and Heru follows behind the two while reciting the Brahman sutra.

If one encounters evil rakshasas, poisonous dragons, and various ghosts, by reciting the power of Guanshiyin, at that moment all will dare not harm.

If evil beasts surround with sharp fangs and claws to be feared, by reciting the power of Guanshiyin, they will gallop away to boundless regions.

Vipers, as well as vipers and scorpions, with breath poison and flame fire burning, by reciting the power of Guanshiyin, following the sound they will naturally turn back.

Clouds and thunder beating and emitting lightning, sending down hail and pouring heavy rain, by reciting the power of Guanshiyin, in accordance with the time they will be dissipated.

Heru: 'Sensei, a suspicious shadow just passed over yonder, what on earth could that be?'

Kiudaukoji: 'Hmm, that looks like a thief. He probably came to rob travelers with some evil purpose, but since the other party is one person and we are three, he must have thought it was no use and strayed from the path. Ah, what a pitiful man. There are so many enterprises to be accomplished in this world, why would anyone do such a thing as thieving? I would somehow want to help him, but he has already gone off somewhere.'

Heru: 'Sensei, stop trying to help thieves already. Look at that Bell, he's just like that. Even though he received a lot of money from General-sama and said he would never steal again, his spirit hasn't been cured yet, so it's no use.'

Heru: 'That's true. I myself actually received money from General-sama and thought to stop thieving once and for all and take up an honest occupation, but simply because of evil companions my hard-won resolution faltered, my evil deeds grew more and more, and in the end I killed a person and was sent all the way to the checkpoint of the other world as divine punishment; yet even a thief is not necessarily incapable of reforming now that he has finally repented and come to accompany you. However, since we must escort Princess Kelina today, please try not to engage with thieves even if we encounter them on the way.'

Heru: 'No matter how much a ningen is said to be a child of Kamisama, as long as one exists between heaven and hell, it is utterly impossible to get along in the world on goodness alone. Heru: 'Indeed, what you say is very reasonable. However, while you are in the status of a shiyugenjiya, did you not say that you possess ichimanriyō of money? I cannot help feeling that what you say is contradictory, you see.'

I somehow think I want to throw this away, but it seems my sins and karmic obstructions are not yet full, so I am unable to discard it. What I call my ichimanriyō is that an evil dragon consisting of stubbornness, arrogance, conceit, anger, and spiritual pride remains. Because I must somehow throw away this ichimanriyō, even if I become a bikshu, I cannot help feeling ashamed before heaven and earth, so I am wandering in a state that is neither a propaganda emissary nor a secular person, and have become a shiyugenjiya. I somehow wish to receive divine virtue and become a candidate for a propaganda emissary, but it is by no means an easy matter, and because of that, I am actually troubled.'

Heru: 'I firmly believed that you had actual money amounting to ichimanriyō in your bosom. Sensei, you make yourself look detached and devoid of desires with your mouth, but I thought in your heart you were a mammonist; yet you do not possess even a scrap of tangible treasure? With that, my doubt has also cleared up. When I think that Beru truly believed you possessed cash and followed you all the way to a place like this, is he not rather piteous?'

Kerina: 'Hohohohoho, is Heru not also piteous? You too came along intending to play a staged fight with Beru, cleverly deceive the shiyugenjiya, and take the ichimanriyō of money. Both Sensei and I saw right through such things, you know. You must have been thinking of conspiring around here and ripping us off.'

Heru: '...'

Kiudau began eagerly chanting the heavenly number-songs (tensu-kazu-uta). Both Heru and Beru showed not the slightest tonchaku, and taking up the two stout wooden cudgels that Beru had brought, they set upon him from both sides. Kerina clung to a white poplar tree and trembled.

The ascetic Kiudau fought defensively with all his might while remaining matsupadaka. However, Kiudau, who was matsupadaka and possessed not even a single piece of wood, was beaten down by the two and ended up dying on the spot. Laughing coldly, the two said,

Beru: 'Un, that is also one method. Transcending good and evil, beauty and ugliness, it would be best if you turned toward the one your true guardian deity is content with. For there are manly men whose style is ugly yet whose hearts are kirei, and no matter how good their style is, there are also base men whose hearts are filthy, so it would be well for you to make your selection so as not to err there.'

Kerina: 'That is certainly so. No matter what, I like a manly man, one who has a warm-hearted and gentle disposition no matter where you look; someone who would beat a person to death and not even bury them is an absolute dislike of mine.'

I was careless because I was too caught up in the selection of a wife. Since this too is because my love for you is deep, please never think ill of it.'

Heru: 'Saying Baka things—what is the use of a love that the Gohonnin does not Shiyōdaku to? Ainikusama to you, ihihihihi.'

Kerina: 'Hohohohoho, what a matching pair of fools you are; who on earth would ever surrender themselves to a Bakamono like you? Unubore to a reasonable Kagen, please.'

Beru: 'Oi, no matter how much of a Bijin you may be, after being Bujiyoku'd to this extent, it is simply too ridiculous to make you my Niyōbau, isn't it? Shall I dismember this Koitsu along the way as well?'

Heru: 'Mm, that is right. Since she speaks to me with such Aisau-zukashi, it cannot be helped. There are Ikura women in the Sekai. If we leave this woman alive, she might tell people that we killed the Shiyugenjiya, which is a bit Kennon, so let us finish her off.'

Beru:

'Understood.'

So saying, he immediately takes up a club and strikes at her. Kerina uses a poplar tree as a shield and tries to escape. Heru again takes up his club and brings it crashing down squarely on the top of her head. Pitiful Kerina utters a piercing shriek and collapses on the spot. At this moment, there was a great ball of fire scorching the sky as it descended; the two of them, terrified, vanish into the forest like clouds and mist. The fireball suddenly descended right in front of where the two had fallen. This was Tsukiteruhiko no Mikoto, dispatched from the First Spirit Realm under divine command to rescue the two from their peril. The two finally notice the sound of the fireball's landing, and looking around, they see a lovely angel dressed in pale pink thin silk standing there. The seeker bowed twice with clapping hands and gave thanks for the grace of saving their lives. Kerina also worships the angel, unable to utter a single word, blinded by tears of joy. The angel speaks quietly to both of them,

Angel: 'You two did not stray from the great path of God, and your determination to sacrifice your bodies for the sake of the Path is admirable indeed. Pardoned by your resolve, I have descended from the Spirit Realm to save you. I am the god Tsukiteruhiko. It would be best for you to be very careful as you return to Mount Terumon. Before then, you will likely face another test. Since you cannot save the world if you cling to your own lives, it is best to leave everything to God and open up the path of salvation. Farewell.'

Leaving these words behind, the angel mounts a purple cloud and returns toward the eastern sky. The two prostrate themselves before the receding figure, lost in tears of gratitude, and head back toward Mount Terumon while chanting the heavenly Kazu-uta.

A mulberry bow that pierces through with a sincere heart— Will it not reach the divine land of God?
