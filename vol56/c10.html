Chapter 10: 十字 [1440]

十字〔1440〕

§ 1

Having been hauled up onto the bank of the Erushina River and saved by the ascetic Kiudaukoji of Mount Bikutoru, the three—Beru, Heru, and Kerina—ascended the mountain stream of the Erushina River and, weaving through the shade of the pines, emerged onto the somewhat spacious Aonogahara.

Here, various beautiful flowers were in full bloom.

The group sat down to rest their breath upon a flat rock by the roadside.

While turning his jiyuzu beads between his fingers, Kiudaukoji chanted an incantation repeatedly, saying,

Kiudaukoji: 'Tenriuko, Ōmiyō, Shō-[_GT_04943_], Daisuijitsu. Tenriuko, Ōmiyō, Shō-[_GT_04943_], Daisuijitsu.'

Heru: 'I say, Shiyugenjiyasama, thanks to you we were saved from a place where our lives would have been lost; though I do not quite understand the sutra you just recited, it permeates my head and I feel as though it is deeply grateful. Would you be so kind as to explain that incantation to us?'

Kiudaukoji: 'Ah, very well, very well. This incantation has become the mystery of Brahmanism.

That I called you back when you were drowning in the water and losing your lives is entirely thanks to this secret art of the cross.

If you were to always chant even just this, there would be not the slightest chance of encountering such disasters.

It is a mystical incantation that revives from the dead and wards off all calamities.

Listen as I give you the explanation one by one.

Tenriuko, Ōmiyō, Shō-[_GT_04943_], Daisuijitsu. Tenriuko, Ōmiyō, Shō-[_GT_04943_], Daisuijitsu.

This secret transmission of the cross manifests divine virtues of marvelous and unfathomable mystery, allowing any desire to be fulfilled and any misfortune to be removed.'

Heru: 'I would truly ask you to instruct us regarding the virtue of those characters.'

Kiudaukoji: 'Very well, very well, it is a grateful thing to hear of their origins.

It is an incantation of divine transmission which says that to speak of it repeatedly makes it yet more grateful, so you would do well to fold it deeply into your hearts.

Now then,

Ten (Heaven) is what one writes when appearing before a noble high official.

Also, it is fine to write this when making a sea voyage or crossing in a boat; if you do so, your own will is perfectly communicated to the high official, and you escape the disaster of a shipwreck.

Ryū (Dragon) is something one writes and carries when crossing the sea, a river, or a pontoon bridge.

Also, if you write this when setting out facing a great wind and rain, you will escape all perils of seas, rivers, wind, and rain.

Tora (Tiger) is what one writes when wishing to go into broad plains, wildernesses, or deep mountains.

Also, if you write it at the time of mountain hunting or when encountering bandits, you will escape that peril.

Ō (King) is something one writes and carries when confronting evil people and the like.

Also, it is fine to write this when responding to emergencies and at the time of trials.

You will surely have the divine protection.

Mei (Life) is also fine to write and carry when offered strange tea, sake, food, or drink at someone's house, and it is also fine to write and carry when facing an enemy.

You will surely escape calamity.

Shō (Victory) is written at the time of military camps as well as myriad victories and defeats, and it is also good to write at the time of buying and selling.

[_GT_04943_] is what one writes when intending to go to a house where there is illness, or when intending to go to places where various wicked people gather.

Divine virtue is surely manifested.

Dai (Great) is what one writes when setting out to a place deemed uncanny or a lonely place, or when visiting a person with a malignant disease or infectious disease.

Mizu (Water) is good to write when going to a house whose layout you do not know, or when attending a banquet, when bracing oneself, at times of purification, or when there is a water dispute.

Hi (Sun) is something you can receive the divine virtue together with your partner if you write and carry it when relating to myriad wedding ceremonies and auspicious joys, or when visiting a sick person.

Since this is the most secret among secrets of Brahmanism, there is a fear of its abuse if rashly transmitted to people, so it is strictly forbidden to pass it on to others.

These crosses mentioned above are called the Great Dharma of the Brahman Cross.

To perform this, men use the left hand and women use the right hand to write in the empty air with the sword-seal mudra.

Also, it is splendid to apply the sword-seal to an inkstone, write it on white paper, and keep it in your bosom.

However, there is something even more revered than this.

Nevertheless, because it is too overwhelmingly sacred to speak of with the mouth, I go about my spiritual training while writing the incantation of the cross in the empty air or chanting it, commensurate with my own body and soul.'

§ 2

Heru: 'Is there any matter more arigata and precious than this? Dōzo, let me hear it in the course of things. Since I wish to help the people of Sanzensekai in order to return this gōn, having been saved by you, Anata.'

Kiudau: 'If you have the magokoro not to monopolize goshintoku, but wish to help the people of Sanzensekai, I will denjiyu it to you. What is called the ichiban precious thing is called the kazūta of Ama, chanting: "Ichi, ni, san, shi, go, roku, shichi, hachi, ku, jū, hyaku-sen-man." This is a shinmon in which the Kamisama of mugenzetsutairiyoku created this tenchi from the very beginning of tenchikaibiyaku, filled shintoku throughout sekai, and bestowed the virtue of aizen and the kuōmiyō of shinshin upon us ningen. And afterwards, you chant: "Kamunagara a, hi, ra, ke, ma, se." Since there is no kotoba more precious than this in Sanzensekai, listen well and keep it in mind.'

Heru: 'Iya, how very arigata it was. Thanks to you, I have received magnificent goshintoku. Thank you, thank you.'

Kiudau: 'If you have time to speak idle words, you should chant this shinmon. If you do so, you will be able to endure any hardship, and you can be used for the honorable service of Kamisama. However, it is no good to chant it with the feeling of singing a song, so you had better chant it with deep reverence.'

Heru: 'Thank you, thank you.'

Beru: 'Ahahaha, what nonsense. You spoke so solemnly about dragons, tigers, badgers, and weasels, and then right after that, you line up numbers like a merchant or a carpenter, talking about planes and chisels—what a laughingstock. Ahahaha, in a sekai where human culture has advanced to this extent, I cannot understand the mindset of a shugenja who goes around uttering such rambling, delirious words. Ufufufu.'

Heru: 'Look here, Beru, the shugenja Kiudau Koji-dono was originally our colonel and emishi-sama, yet he chanted a magnificent jumon and saved us, so how can you say such an awe-inspiring thing? Isn't it a terrible sin?'

Beru: 'Hahahahaha, you too have grown soft. No matter what anyone says, those who have a lifespan are bound to die. Didn't they say at the crossroads, "You still have life, so go back"? It is not the power of a shugenja at all. You returned to life because you still possess the power of survival in this sekai. Do not say such foolish things. Rather than that, who knows how much more profitable it would be to apply yourself to business? From now on, straddling the world, setting up a worldly base, and applying oneself to the thief's trade will bring immediate divine favor. No matter how much you chant the secret art of the cross, or count "Ichi, ni, san, shi," not a single mon of money will fall from the sky, will it? Such things are the work of a hermit. Since we must struggle through our daily hardships of living, we cannot afford to talk about such cheerful things. As long as the flesh exists, we must also take food, and since the human body is a real substance, a real physical substance is above all things essential. What use is an empty, boundless, formless jumon? How foolish you are.'

Kiudau: 'Hahahahaha, Beru, it seems you simply do not understand. And didn't you say to the general-sama, when you received all that money, that you would enter upon an honest calling? Despite that, are you still going to start thieving from now on?'

Beru: 'Since bread is essential for human beings above all else, someone without assets like me has no choice but to do things like thieving. No matter how much one prays to Kami, not a single piece of bread will well up. As for whether Kamisama exists or not, such things cannot be relied upon at all.'

Beru: 'In that case, are you going to advocate for atheism to the very end? What a troublesome fellow you are. You know, do you not, that not even a single leaf of a tree can be made by human power alone? If so, there must be a prime mover that fashioned the mountains, rivers, plants, and trees. Without something other than human beings, this tenchi could never have been made. You had better reflect upon it well.'

Beru: 'It is understood that humans cannot do it. Everything in the universe is made by the power of nature. Do youanatagata call that nature God? Anatagata are atheists. If not, it is natural worship. I can never believe that such a thing as God exists in this world, you know.'

Kiudau: 'What a hopeless fellow you are. You had better calm down, put your hand on your chest, and think quietly.'

Heru: 'Hey, Beru, humans cannot live in this world for even a single day apart from God. It is all thanks to Kamisama. Without saying such impious things, do you not feel like worshiping Kamisama? This is the turning point where you will either be saved into Tengoku or fall into Jigoku, so why don't you listen closely to Kiudausama's words and think about it?'

Beru: 'Hmm, in that case, let me ask Kiudausama one thing: just how many Kamisamas are there between this Tenchi?'

Kiudau: 'There are countless hundreds of millions of Kamisamas, referred to as eight million Amatsukami and eight million Kunitsukami. Since each of them fulfills their respective duties and protects this world, humans must have faith in Kamisama.'

Beru: 'If there were that many Kamisamas, wouldn't the world become ungovernable instead? Your argument simply does not make sense to me. By that logic, this world would be completely buried under nothing but Kamisamas, leaving no place for humans to dwell. Such polytheism is far too outdated to enter the ears of us who have received modern education.'

Kiudau: 'Kamisama was originally only one pillar. That Kamisama is named Ōkunitachirutsu-no-Mikoto, the Primordial Great Kamisama who presides over all things in the universe; therefore, various heavenly beings born from the water and fire of this Kamisama have become the eight million Kamisamas and protect us. That is why, when rolled up, it becomes one God, and when unfurled, it becomes many Gods. So-called Kamisama is one God yet many Gods, and many Gods yet one God. Even we ourselves are ultimately a part of the divine body of Kamisama.'

Beru: 'It has become more and more incomprehensible. Just when I thought what youasama say was monotheism, it turns out to be polytheism. Just when I thought it was polytheism, it suddenly turns into pantheism. I politely decline to worship such an uncertain Kamisama. Ah, listening to this kind of talk makes me feel sick. Rather than that, I want to do something that realistically grants divine favors. Come on, it is my turn now.'

Saying this, he pulled his twisted headband tight, thrust his lump-covered arm straight forward,

Beru: 'Hey, Shugensha, where do you think this place is? Impiously enough, this is the turf of the great thief Beru-sama. Come on, hurry up and throw down all your belongings and leave. You must have received a hefty share at Inakurayama, so you should still have some. Hand that over here without reservation. Out of mercy, I will spare your clothes.'

Kiudau: 'Ahahahaha, what a troublesome fellow. I still have about ten thousand ryo here, but this is a material treasure intended to help troubled humans in the world. I do not carry a single mon of money to give to a thief like you. Since you are the kind of scoundrel who, even if given a large sum of money by the Shogun-sama, would only become a worse villain, I cannot hand over even a single rin if I wanted to help you. Rather than that, why don't you receive the formless treasure and become a true human?'

Beru: 'Ahahahaha, no matter what you say, faith cannot be given without bread. If you want to lead me onto the path of faith, give me bread first. Hurry up and give that money here... I won't say all of it, so hand over about five thousand ryo. If you do, I wouldn't mind becoming a believer while this money lasts.'
