Chapter 3: 仇 Flower [1433]

仇花〔1433〕

§ 1

Satsuki matsu hanatachibana no ka o kageba Mukashi no hito no sode no ka zo suru

A grass of deep thoughts, where the road of love is profound, wondering how her beloved husband, bound even in dreams, might fare.

Passing through the black list of various flowers, she is enchanted by the blooming unohana, kakitsubata, and the color and fragrance of yamabuki dyed in purple, walking alone, stumbling along Aonogahara. Although the flowers smile along the path of her advancing body, not a single voice visits her, nor even their footsteps can be heard; and though myriad birds cry out in the surrounding mountain forests, her tears are not yet exhausted. Truly, the color of inexhaustible love, the seeds of myriad flowers—green, crimson, white, red, yellow—the sorrow of blooming in full brilliance is deep. The deep lily, the deep-view grass of Kami's grace, as she advances with her heart set, her dear husband is like white frost on the twigs of the white dew; even if frost should lie upon them, she remains ever constant. How pure is the awakening grass of the tachibana! Even if there is no event of any trouble befalling your august person, where are you residing? The grass that yearns for loves of old, turning into spring haze, the wild geese flying away while abandoning the sky through which they rise—could they have learned to dwell in a village devoid of flowers? Her heart is filled with empty doubts. Abandoning the sprouting flowers blooming in full glory in the Shinen of Mount Telmon, it has been a year already; how heartless of her husband, who does not cast a glance back. Even if this body were to expose its corpse to the fields, with the stubborn intent of a love pushed to its limit, her body yearning for a love forbidden by her parents—the butterbur mistakenly opening in the wind of late spring, the azalea catching the moment of a night-reveller's plucking, amidst the dream of a startling spring—were the dalliances of butterflies and their fragrant charms merely a deceptive flower of the heart, when she reflects upon it now? Now her body is like the marsh lilies mingling with the damp mushrooms among summer grasses, the pitiful state of one whom no man plucks. Ah, my dear Lord Kamihiko, where do you reside? Yearning to meet just once, even in the sound of the blowing wind, chasing and pursuing while tormenting her heart, she proceeds further and further to the north.

To such a place, coming from ahead,

A single man walking stumblingly,

Upon seeing the woman, he pauses,

And gazes intently with a suspicious air.

The woman, upon seeing him, raises a voice of astonishment,

Kerina: 'Ah, are you not my dear Kamihiko-sama? Why, pray tell, came you to be in such a place? You left the hermitage in the Elsina Valley, saying you would go do a bit of trade and earn some money, and after you set off leading a camel, it has been a dwelling for one with no visiting soul. From morning to evening my solitary slumber was startled by the voices of fierce beasts, and hearing the sound of insects on autumn evenings plunged me into tears of sorrow. Grief piled upon grief, my heart grew increasingly sentimental, and finding the world unbearable as I felt I could neither live nor die, I thought I had thrown myself into the Elsina River, yet I have wandered into such a place of unknown name. How joyous it is! I never even dreamed I would lay eyes upon you in such a place. How nostalgic it is.'

When she clings to him, Kamihiko shakes her off,

Kamihiko: 'Kerina-hime, you too must have suffered greatly, and I am truly sorry. However, though I had not yet concealed it nor spoken of it to you, before I wed you, convinced it was the enmity of love, I neatly tricked a man named Berujī, threw him into the abyss, and took his life. Thanks to that, I became a happy companion to you, and hiding from our parents' eyes, we built a hermitage in the Elsina Valley. Just as we pledged to grow old together and share the same grave, night after night the vengeful spirit of Berujī appeared, glaring with a terrifying face, so that I could not even remain by your side. Moreover, your face began to look like Berujī's, and it was so terrifying I could bear it no longer. Feigning peddling, I led camels away, parted from you, and as I wandered here and there, I encountered a trio of thieves, was plundered of all my belongings, and left stark naked, thrown into the Lion River. As punishment for my sins, I am now wandering at the crossroads of the underworld. Pray, put me out of your mind, for unless you do, I can never go to heaven. You do not seem to be a spirit who should come here yet. Somehow or other, make haste and turn back, make apologies to your parents, and take a suitable husband to spend your life with. That is my earnest request.'

§ 2

Folding her palms together, she worships amidst tears.

Kelina held a breast of doubt toward Kamahiko's words, tilted her head, and sank into thought for a short while. After a while, she raised her face,

Kelina: 'Moshi Kamahiko-sama, hearing your words for the first time just now, I was surprised. Did you kill that lovely Berusī-sama? Why did you do such a bad thing? That Berusī-sama is, in truth, my older brother. Our father impregnated a secret woman, hid it from our mother, and sent him to the house of Shiyuda, you see. Since I heard about that matter from my father, there have been times even until now when Berusī-sama, not knowing me to be his younger sister, approached me with love many times, but I refused him gracefully. When I think of it, when I think of it, could my beloved husband be my brother's enemy? Hearing that story, whether to feel you hateful, or whether to feel you dear—even to myself, my own heart has grown strange.'

Kamahiko: 'I must once present myself to Berusī-sama and offer my apologies. Therefore, since coming to the Reikai, I have searched everywhere and in all directions for his whereabouts, and I am in a hurry to receive a single word of forgiveness; but hearing the rumors, it seems he has somehow gone up to the Tengoku, so I cannot even meet him, and I am truly at my wits' end. If you are brother and sister, please, in place of your elder brother, say just a single word that you forgive me. If you do so, this Kamahiko too will have his sins absolved and be able to spend a life in the Tengoku. Please, in return for our past friendship, I wish to receive the words that you forgive me just this once.'

Kelina: 'I do not have the qualifications to say that I forgive you. Moreover, the sin of my becoming your wife without knowing that you were my brother's enemy is surely not an easy thing either. This soul, which must certainly fall to the very bottom of the jigoku, how could such a thing be done? Ah, it is regrettable.'

So saying, she collapses in tears.

Kamahiko crosses both hands, sits down with a thump upon the grass-grown ground, and is lost in tears of penitence. As this happened, the three of Beru, Sharu, and Heru—with black garments and masked hoods, hanging long swords at their hips—came trudging along. Beru, seeing Kamahiko's figure, is surprised,

Beru: 'Yaa, were you not the traveler who led a camel and came on a trading journey to the riverbank of the Raion River?'

Kamahiko: 'Un, that's right. Those thieves at that time were the three of you, aa. At last your heavenly fate ran out and you too were sent to the Meido, weren't you.'

Beru: 'Don't talk nonsense. Have you gone mad? This place is not the Meido. We are right in the midst of walking along the foot of Mount Terumon, doing our thieving trade. Once we made you tumble into the raging current of the Raion River, I thought you had already gone to the Meido, but what a life-blessed fellow you are. Rescued by someone somewhere, you are wandering about to a place like this. Ahahaha, ... Ah, are you not the nice girl called Kelina? When on earth did you come out to such a place? Come, finding you here is good fortune, you shall become my wife.'

Kelina: 'Hohohohoho, well, well, master thief, while you fell into the Erušina River and wandered together with us to the Meido, are you still going to try and do thieving? Reform yourself to a decent degree.'

Beru: 'Hate na, hearing you say that, eh, I thought we began a struggle from the entanglement between these two fellows and you, and fell into the abyss, so did I die right then as well? Well, it is a thing that defies comprehension. The man I thought I killed is standing right here. And the nice girl is here too. Moreover, the scenery all around does not seem particularly changed either, it is an incomprehensible matter. Oi, Heru, Sharu, what do you take this place to be?'

Heru: 'Un, anyhow, to us it looks neither like the Genkai nor the Yūkai.'

漸くにして八衢の関所に着いた。白と赤との守衛は例の如く儼然として控へてゐる。鎌彦は何時の間にやら姿は見えなくなつてゐた。赤の守衛は四人の姿を見て、まづ第一にベルを呼び出し、一々生前の罪状を取り調べ、

ベル『成る程、私は仰せの如くよからぬ事を致して来ました。併し乍らコレも不知不識の過ちで厶いますから、何卒許して頂きたう厶います。神様は愛を以て御本体となさるぢやありませぬか。何処迄も悪人を悪人として罰せず、地獄の苦しみを課せず、天国に救つて下さるが神様だと思ひます。悪人を罰するのならそれは決して愛とは申されますまい。愛の欠けた神は最早神ではありますまい』

赤『お前は直に地獄へ行くべきものだが、今此処でエンゼルが御説教をなさるから、夫に依つて悔改め、エンゼルの御言葉が耳に入り、心に浸潤したならば屹度天国へ救はれるだらう。併し乍らお前の造つた悪業では、エンゼルの御言葉は耳に入るまい。人間が霊肉脱離して霊界に来り八衢の関所を越えて伊吹戸主の館に導き入れられた時には、エンゼルが冥官の調べる以前に一応接見して、大神様や高天原及び天人的生涯の事をお知らせになり、諸々の善や、真実を教へて下さるやうになつてゐる。併し乍らお前の精霊が世に在つた時に、神は屹度八衢に於て善悪の教をなし其心の向けやうに由つて或は天国へ、或は地獄へ自ら行くと云ふ事は生前より既に承知し乍らも心の中に之を否んだり、或は之を軽く見てゐたから、何うしてもエンゼルの言葉を苦しくて聞く事は出来まい。エンゼルの御面が怖ろしくなり胸は痛み、居堪たまらず悦んで自分の向ふ地獄へ自ら飛び込むであらう。神は決して世界の人間の精霊を一人も地獄へ堕さうとは御考へなさるのではない。其人が自ら神様に背を向け光に反き地獄に向ふのである。其地獄はお前が現世に居つた時既に和合した所のもので、悪と虚偽とを愛する心の集まり場所である。大神様はエンゼルの手を経たり、且高天原の内流に依つて各精霊を自分の方へ引寄せむと遊ばすけれども、素より悪と虚偽とに染み切つたお前達の精霊は、仁慈無限の神様の御取計らひを忌嫌ひ、力限り之に抵抗し、自分の方から神様を振り棄て離れ行くものである。自分が所有する処の悪と虚偽は鉄の鎖を以て地獄へ自ら引入るるが如きものである。謂はばお前等が自由の意志を以て自ら地獄へ堕落するものだから神様は之を見て愛と善と真との力を与へ、一人も地獄へ堕そまいと焦せつて厶るのだ。どうぢやこれからエンゼルの御話を聞いて、神様に反いた悪と虚偽とをスツカリと払拭し天国の生涯を送る気はないか』

原文（近代文語）

ベル『ハイ、兎も角人間は意志の自由を束縛される位苦しい事は厶いませぬ。天国へ行つて自分の意志に合はぬ苦しい生活をするよりも、一層の事地獄へ行つて力一杯活動して見たう厶います』

赤『ウン、さうだらう。お前はどうしても地獄代物だ。各所主の愛に依つて精霊の籍が定まるものだから、どうしても助けやうがないワ。併し乍ら此生死簿には未だお前は此処へ来る精霊ぢやないから、此関所は越ゆる事は出来ない』

ベル『ハテ、合点が行かぬ事だなア。生きて居るのか、死んで居るのか、自分には少しも合点が行かぬ。どうも死んだやうな覚えもなし、だと云ふてエルシナ川へ飛び込んだ事は確だし、其間に人に救はれて生きてゐるのか、或は死んでからも残つて居る意志がハツキリしてゐるのか、どうも其点が私には分りませぬがなア』

赤『ウン、そらさうだ、わかるまい。仮令肉体は亡ぶるとも、人間の本体たる精霊は意志想念を継続してゐるなり、又生前と同様の肉体を保つて居るのだから、合点の行かぬのは無理もない。併し乍ら此処は幽冥界だ。霊肉脱離後の人間（即ち精霊）の来る処だ。サア、早く此処を立去れ。やがて誰かが迎へに来るだらう。モシ迎へに来なかつたならば、お前の好きな地獄へ行くだらう。サア早く立てツ』

と金棒を以て突出せば、ベルはヨロヨロとし乍ら、傍の茫々たる草の中に倒れて了つた。
