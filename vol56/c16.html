Chapter 16: 不臣 [1446]

不臣〔1446〕

§ 1

As soon as the worship at the Shinden was concluded, Michihiko was invited into Okunihime's Ima, where he was entertained with Sakuwa and Chōhan, and was relaxing.

When breakfast was over, the two Jijiyo withdrew from the place, and Okunihime appeared with a sorrowful expression on her face.

Hime: 'An Burakku-sama, thank you ever so much for coming. There is a matter I would like to ask of you from the bottom of my heart, will you not listen to it?'

Michihiko: 'Yes. If it is something within my power, I shall accept any Goyō whatsoever. Please speak without any Goenriyo.'

Hime: 'Thank you very much. I will make my inquiry without further ado; as you are well aware, this Tauyakata is the ancient site of the origin of Brahmanism, where the Kamisama of Brahmanism, the Daitōriyō Ōkuronushi, designated this place as the primary Seijiyō back when he was still called Onikumohiko. The names of we husband and wife were Kunihiko and Kunihime, but having received our names from Onikumohiko-sama, we are now called Okunihiko and Okunihime. Accordingly, the wish-granting jewel, which is the priceless treasure of this Tauyakata, has been lost and its whereabouts remain unknown to this day, and unless this jewel is discovered within one hundred days, we husband and wife have fallen into a destiny where we must die to make amends. My husband has worried over this and fallen gravely ill, and we have reached today when his life hangs by a thread. To make matters worse, three years ago, a younger daughter of ours named Kelina ran away from home with a wicked man, and her whereabouts are still unknown; the anxiety of the two of us is simply beyond description. Can you not somehow use your divine virtue to let us know the whereabouts of the wish-granting jewel?'

Since Michihiko's Tengantsū was not working in the slightest, even when presented with such a problem, he could not answer a single word. Nevertheless, thinking desperately on the Ōkami while trying somehow to muddle through the situation, he answered casually.

Michihiko: 'Hearing your story, I truly feel deep sympathy. Please do not worry at all. Now that I have come here, it is surely without a doubt that the divine tether of the Kamisama has pulled me here. I shall pray for one week and inquire into the whereabouts of the jewel.'

He offered such vague and evasive greetings. Like the proverb that a drowning person will clutch even at a single straw, Okunihime made Michihiko's words her sole reliance, and greatly delighted, with a smile spreading across her face,

Hime: 'Thank you so much for your kindness. I earnestly ask you for whatever can be done. And though it is a bold request, how might my husband's illness be?'

Michihiko: 'Let us first spend a week devoting our mind and soul to prayer. If the Kamisama thinks it necessary no matter what, He will probably save his life, and if He thinks there is some Goyō for him in the spirit world, He will probably take his life. The matter of life and death alone is something no one can do anything about. There is probably no other way than to leave this to the Kamisama.'

Hime: 'Just as you say, I always preach to believers that regarding the matter of life and death, it is not something within human control; but now that it concerns my own situation, foolish complaints slip out and I become bewildered, which is quite embarrassing. Then, though it is another hard thing to mention, it is about the whereabouts of my daughter. Is that girl still safely remaining in this world? Or could she have been killed by some evildoer? That alone worries me beyond endurance.'

Michihiko could not give haphazard replies to any of these. Ah, come what may, resolving to risk it all,

Michihiko: 'Do not worry about the matter of the daughter-sama. She will certainly return safely before long by the grace of the Kamisama.'

Hime: 'Yes, thank you very much. And in what country might my daughter be living around this time? I would like to hear about that just for a moment.'

§ 2

Michihiko was taken aback for a moment, yet he put aside his hesitation,

Michihiko: 'She is hiding somewhere very close by. Please do not worry. She will be returning soon, though I cannot speak further without first inquiring at the shrine.'

Hime: 'Indeed, that must be so. When you are quite at ease, pray inquire of the divine oracle once.'

Michihiko: 'Yes, I understand. I shall go and ask immediately now. However, I ask that no one else comes along.'

So saying, he left those words behind and proceeded toward the shrine.

Michihiko advanced into the shrine, lowered his voice, and chanted the Amatsunorito; upon finishing, he spoke:

Michihiko: 'I have stumbled upon a most difficult problem. Yet, as a messenger of Ananahi, I cannot speak carelessly. If I were to speak carelessly and my disguise were exposed, it would stain the name of Kamisama and I could not face my master; therefore, I ask that you let me hear a clear answer in my ear within this week, or else let me know even through a dream. And if possible, I also ask that you show me the whereabouts of my master.'

As he prayed thus and closed his eyes for a while, suddenly his back began to swell out heavily, and feeling a weight as though something like a dog had leaped onto his back, though no figure was visible, someone whispered in his ear in a slightly raspy voice. This was the spirit of Sumāto advising and protecting Michihiko. And its revealed words were as follows.

Spirit: 'Michihiko-dono, you are worrying greatly, but Lord Tamakunowake and his party will surely be able to meet you at this mansion before long. And as for this mansion's treasure, the wish-granting jewel, it is being hidden by the steward's son named Wakkusu for a certain purpose, so this too shall appear right now. I am Sumāto, who protects the personal safety of Hatsuwakuhime, but you must by no means tell Princess Oguni that Wakkusu is hiding it and so forth. However, tell her not to worry, for I shall make it appear directly. Also, as the master of this house, Ogunihiko, has only a short while left to live, you would do well to tell her to resign herself to that. Furthermore, her daughter, Princess Kerina, will be saved by an ascetic of the Ananahi faith and will return in the near future. Inform her of this too so that she may be at ease. Is that all the things you wished to inquire about?'

A small voice could be heard. Michihiko thought that his divine clairaudience had opened for the first time, and rejoicing greatly, he gave thanks to the Ōkami, and with a smile returned to the parlor of Princess Oguni. Princess Oguni saw Michihiko's expression, which was somehow brimming with vigor,

Hime: 'Well now, this must surely be promising.'

She grasped it quickly, and looking delightfully pleased,

Hime: 'My, my, Anburakku-sama, you have had a hard time of it. You, whose divine virtue is high, must surely have heard the oracle of Kamisama directly. I beg you to show it to me.'

Michihiko: 'Oh, I am humbled to be praised thus. Whatever I might say, ever since I entered the Brahman teachings, though I was suddenly promoted to become a sendenshi, I can scarcely chant the sutras properly. It is only because I was made a sendenshi on the grounds of having firm faith that I do not know the doctrines of Brahmanism in the least, but through the power of faith I have been granted the divine clairvoyance and divine clairaudience. Because of that, any matter whatsoever is shown to me as clearly as if reflected in a mirror.'

Hime: 'Oh, that is splendid. The sendenshi of today only speak of difficult subtle reasonings, spending their days from morning till night studying sutras, and since they lack true faith, even while acting as go-betweens for Kamisama, they do not understand the will of the Ōkami in the least. Whatever one may say, deeds without words are splendid. And what did Kamisama say?'

Hime: 'Ann Burakku-san, just as the steward has said now, my husband is waiting, so I will go for a moment, please rest at your ease.'

So saying and leaving her words behind, she departs from this place looking busy.

Ōrusuchin turns to Michihiko-san.

Ōrusuchin: 'Propagandist-sama, thank you very much for your trouble. Michi: 'Do not worry. The precious jewel of the Niyoihotsushiyu is by no means lost to the outside. Ōrusuchin: 'What, what did you say? Do you say that someone of this household is hiding the precious jewel of that Niyoihotsushiyu? And who might the son of the prominent officials who comes and goes to this mansion be? For reference, I would like to ask to hear his name...'

Michi: 'Since my ascetic training is still insufficient, I cannot distinctly state even the full name of the person who hid it. Only that he is a round-faced, fair-complexioned man is precisely what I know.'

Ōrusuchin: 'Well now, I hear a strange thing. However, whoever may have hidden it, unless this is searched out, the explanation of Okunikohiko-sama will not hold, and even the officials of this mansion must receive severe punishment from Daikokusuma. And will that jewel appear in the near future?'

Ōrusuchin: 'Indeed, as you say. If such a matter leaks to the outside, it is a momentous affair; please pray that it appears even a moment sooner, and that peace of mind may come to the master even a moment sooner.'

Michi: 'Yes, I understand.'

To such a place, Okunihime appears once more.

Hime: 'I say, propagandist-sama, my husband's condition has become extremely bad, so will you not please perform a prayer for him?'

Okunihiko was feverish and uttering usagoto. And from time to time, he groaned, 'Wakkusu, Wakkusu.' Wakkusu was the son of the karei, Ōrusuchin. Hearing this, Ōrusuchin suddenly stroked his chest, bowed his head, and fell into deep shian. Raising her voice slightly, Okunihime spoke,

As Ōrusuchin left the mansion and walked the road back to his house, he sighed ikutabi, tilted his head as though something weighed on his mind, tapped his staff, and trudged back home. The inaba in the tanbo were rustling bravely, stirred by the wind. Swallows, darting to and fro like flying shuttles, revealed white plumage from beneath their black wings, and flew high and low, performing great wheel-like movements above the rice fields. The sleepy voice of an owl echoed hō-hō from the woods behind the house. When Ōrusuchin stealthily returned to the gate of his house, the voices of two or three people could be heard loudly. With anxious mind, Ōrusuchin strained his ears, leaned against the gate door, and stood eavesdropping on the conversation.

原文（近代文語）

三千『それはお困りです。然らば参りませう』

と云ひ乍ら家令と共に主人の居間に通つた。

小国彦は熱に浮かされて囈言を云つて居る。そして時々、ワツクス　ワツクスと呻いて居る。ワツクスとは家令のオールスチンが息子である。オールスチンは之を聞くよりハツと胸を撫で、俯向いて思案に暮れて居る。小国姫は少しく声を尖らし乍ら、

姫『これ、オールスチン、今旦那様が夢中になつて「ワツクス　ワツクス」と仰有るのはお前の悴の名に違ひない。何か旦那様に対し、御無礼の事をして居るのではあるまいか。よく調べて下さい。此宣伝使様にお尋ねすれば直分るだらうけれど、斯んな事まで御苦労になるのは畏れ多い事だから、お前、心に当る事があるなら包まず隠さず、ワツクスの事に就いて述べて下さい』

オールス『ハイ、心当りと申しては何も厶いませぬが、兎も角宅へ帰りまして悴を調べて見ませう。暫くお待ち下さいませ。然らば奥様、旦那様をお大切にして下さいませ。アンブラツク様、左様ならば一寸宅まで帰つて参ります。何卒宜しうお願ひ申します』

と言葉を残し急ぎ吾家を指して帰り行く。

オールスチンは館を出でて吾家に帰る道すがら幾度となく吐息をつき、何事か心に当るものの如く首を傾け乍ら、杖を突きトボトボとして吾家に帰り行く。田圃の稲葉は風に煽られてサラサラと勇ましく鳴つて居る。燕は前後左右に梭をうつ様に黒い羽根の間から白い羽毛を現はし、或は高く或は低く大車輪の活動を稲田の上にやつて居る。寝むたさうに梟の声はホウホウと家の後の森林から聞えて居る。オールスチンは秘かに吾家の門口に帰つて見ると二三人の人声が盛に聞えて居る。心にかかるオールスチンは耳をすませて門の戸に凭れ話の様子を立聞きし居たりけり。
