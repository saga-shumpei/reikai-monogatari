Chapter 13: 不動滝 [1443]

不動滝〔1443〕

§ 1

Along the mountain ridges of Mount Terumon, all over the mountain grow lush, giant trees thick in Mount Suga, sealing off the skies so that the valley is dark even at midday and thick with mist, with no distinction between summer and winter, a secret land where fog rises. Like a piece of white cloth bleached from heaven, the great waterfall is called the An Black Waterfall, with the terrifying sound of water echoing as though a hundred thunders were roaring at once. To such a place, walking briskly night after night, comes a woman, a priestess of Brahmanism, who has built a mansion on Mount Terumon and propagates her teachings, the beloved eldest daughter of Okuniwake, Princess Debisu, wishing to save her father from his heavy illness, parted from her sole younger sister, forgetting herself in that sadness, advancing to pray for the illness of her father and the safety of her younger sister. The moon shines brightly across the empty sky, illuminating the lower world without a single shadow, yet this waterfall alone has its shadow blocked by the branches of aged trees, and only the pale white of the waterfall water catches the eye. Princess Debisu suddenly casts off her clothes, plunges into the waterfall basin with a splash, and chants the Brahman spells with single-minded devotion, offering her prayers; how admirable is that heart of hers! To such a place, walking briskly and running in a flurry, come mysterious shadows only two in number, stealing close with muffled footsteps, gazing upon the white skin emerging in the darkness and whispering to each other, waiting for Princess Debisu to come up from the waterfall basin. These are none other than both Bell and Heru, who, terrified by the divine majesty of the divine spirit of Tsukiteruhiko, abandoned ascetic Princess Kerina and fled for their very lives, making their way thither casually by the guidance of the sound of water in the valley, having searched it out.

The valley of Mount Suga is a dense forest, not overly high, where large trees of twenty or thirty rod trunk diameters stand out even in this neighborhood, sealing the sky and making it a terrifying place dark even in midday. Furthermore, gathering all the valley water of Mount Terumon here to form a great cataract reaching several hundred rods in height, it appears as though a white cloth were suspended from heaven. This spot is a place strictly forbidden to kill, and An Black Myōō is enshrined beside the waterfall. Nevertheless, no countryman has ever dared approach this waterfall basin, out of fear. Various kinds of fierce beasts and vipers inhabit it in great abundance, and it was feared that anyone who set foot into the forest of Mount Suga even for a single step would never return alive. Bats resembling spread umbrellas fly about in pitch-black swarms around the vicinity of the waterfall, hiding their bodies in the rotting holes of great trees by day, and beginning to stir slowly from around dusk.

Princess Debisu, notwithstanding the fact that she was a gentlewoman born into a house of pure practices, made her own life a target, walking thither night after night to pray for the full recovery of her father suffering from an old age illness, and furthermore to pray before An Black Myōō that Princess Kerina, who had concealed her form three years prior, might return safely, taking risks as she walked thither night after night and casting her body into the deep waterfall basin to perform severe austerities. Just as the ascetic is about to strike Princess Kerina to death and conceal her form, an angel of the deity Tsukiteruhiko appears from one direction of the heaven as a great flash of light, and the ascetic resurrects Princess Kerina. Both Bell and Heru are startled out of their wits by the sound of this fire-ball exploding, and though they are well aware of the terrifying nature of the valley of Mount Suga, they lose their place to escape from excessive astonishment, running up the mountain and barely escaping to the foot of this waterfall. The sound of the waterfall water is roaring and noisy, and even the voice of Princess Debisu praying cannot be distinguished.

Since both Bell and Heru never imagined that a delicate woman would come to such a deep forest at midnight to perform severe austerities, they cannot restrain their suspicions, wondering if she might be a specter or an apparition, and though they approached near the waterfall while making the roots of their teeth rattle, they clung to each other in dread and trembled, feeling an eerie chill.

Because Princess Debisu was praying with all her heart and soul, she did not even dream that two men had come close, and wiping away the moisture of her drenched body, she changed into fine clothing and walked briskly back along the familiar path. Not failing the proverb of wanting to see something frightening out of curiosity, the two men followed her footsteps while keeping a distance of about ten rods. The woman finally emerged into a grassy plain shone upon by the moon. Here there is a natural rock shaped like a rectangle of about one ken square, called the Heaven Worship Stone. Princess Debisu sat down properly in the very middle of that rock and once again concentrated her prayers. Both Bell and Heru bent their hips, sneaking while creeping through the vast grassland, drew softly close, hid their bodies in the thicket of grass, and watched how things were.

Debisuhime brought her hands together facing the moonlight and began to pray.

Devisubuhime: 'Namu Daijizaiten Baramon Ōkamisama, today precisely I have safely completed the twenty-one days of rigorous ascetic training. I humbly pray that by some means the wish-granting jewel of the Nyoi-hotsushiyu, which my father was entrusted with by the kamutsukasa of Ōkuronushi, may be discovered even one day sooner, and that the divine displeasure of Ōkuronushi-sama may be forgiven. Furthermore, my father, deeply distressed since my younger sister's whereabouts became unknown, has fallen onto a sickbed of grave illness, and his life approaches its evening and dawn. I earnestly beg You to pity my heart, heal my father's illness completely, and let me meet my beloved sister. And I fervently implore You to bestow Your divine grace so that the sacred treasure of the Nyoi-hotsushiyu may return to the mansion through the hands of someone even for a single moment sooner. As I gaze upon the divine figure of the Moon Ōkami, the sacred treasure of the Nyoi-hotsushiyu, resembling that full and round appearance, comes to my mind. When that sacred treasure is absent, the divine mansion of Mount Terumon is no different from pitch darkness. I care not if You take away my life, so I earnestly beseech You to grant these three prayers...'

Thus she was pouring her heart into her prayer with single-minded devotion. Beru and Heru, the two of them, heard this woman's identity for the first time, stroked their chests in relief, and once again stealthily stirred up avarice, plotting to strip the woman bare and get their hands on a considerable fortune. On Debisu's head and body, precious gems such as diamonds, coral beads, lapis lazuli, agates, and giant clamshells were adorned, shining like flowers, reflected in the opportune moonlight. Gazing at this, the two men, like showing a bonito to a cat, purred in their throats, drew their mouths close to each other's ears, and said,

Beru: 'Hey, Heru, isn't this a remarkably extraordinary nice girl? And those jewels shining from her head down to her body must be quite expensive things. Shall we, as a final act of evil here, strip her bare, rob her of all her possessions, take them back to our home country, and return in glory to our birthplace? If we do that, there will be no cause to be mocked that the Baramon army was disbanded and dismissed. People don't matter; as long as you succeed, people will praise you, you know. There will hardly be another chance as good as this.'

Heru: 'Somehow my body has begun to tingle and move. I am quitting being a thief anymore. No matter how expensive things are, I don't want them. It has come to feel terrifying, as if the bright Moon Ōkami high above our heads is watching over our actions. If you want them, how about opening up the situation to that nice girl and asking her?'

Beru: 'Eh, what a coward. That's why you are called a mud-fool. If that's the case, you, watch my skill right here. In exchange, if I plunder them, I won't distribute a single one to you, so you agree, don't you?'

Heru: 'Yeah, I agree. But Beru, if you don't think it through thoroughly, you never know what kind of trouble you might run into. Isn't a divine light shining from her body from nowhere? I somehow feel like she is like a god, and my body seems to shrink in awe.'

Beru: 'The fact that it is shining is its value. If we manage to get our hands entirely on her, they are things of unknown value worth hundreds of thousands of ryo. You are quite a hopeless coward. Are you going to deliberately throw away the treasure hanging right before your eyes? Ungrateful wretch, if so, crouch down there for a while like a viper.'

Saying thus, he stepped forward briskly and stood right before Debisuhime's praying figure,

Beru: 'Hey, I don't know what kind of lady-in-waiting you are, but while making a long journey, you encountered bandits, had all your money completely stolen, and now you are making your journey like a beggar out of sheer necessity. You have to return from here to the Land of the Moon. Won't you please hand over two or three of the things shining on your head to this side?'

Davis was startled by this voice, stopped his Kiguwan, and peering intently through the Tsukikage, he saw a rough-looking man standing and blocking his way slightly to the side where he was sitting.

Davis: 'I do not know what kind of Tabibito you are, but you seem to have said just now that you wanted the things on my head; however, there is no way I can give them to you. The Hauseki I wear all over my Karadajiu are a Majinahi to ward off Akuma, and I still have a Michinori of about Niri to travel before I return home. It is Kiken to travel the road at night, so if you insist, please come another time. My home is the Kamuyakata on Mount Telmon.'

Bell: 'What? Are you saying you are the daughter of the Reichi Okuniwake-sama of Mount Telmon? Well, that is a strange coincidence. Even though I look like this, I am the Seiitaishiyōgun of the Brahman army, General Oniharuwake.'

Davis: 'Ho ho ho ho, are you not General Oniharuwake-sama, who usually travels in Daudau style with a Takusan Guntai? What did you say just a moment ago... that you were on a Chiyōto journey, met a Dorobau, and had all your money stolen, so you asked for the things on my head... is that not what you said? How could someone who is supposed to be Oniharuwake walk alone or have his Mochimono stolen by thieves? You must be a petty thief. Come, try to take them if you can; even though I am a woman, I have confidence in my arms.'

Bell: 'The truth is, there is no mistake that I am Oniharuwake. The army of the San-Ikkyo, ten thousand horsemen strong, pressed upon my camp, and my side had only a little over three thousand, most of whom were suffering from beriberi, with only about two or three hundred actually able to stand on the battlefield. No matter how brave Oniharuwake may be, facing ten thousand enemies with only three hundred soldiers means that, by the laws of heaven and earth, my side was inevitably wiped out, and I, saved by the help of the gods, barely escaped with my life and fled to this place. There is no mistake that I am Oniharuwake.'

Davis: 'If there is no mistake that you are Oniharuwake-sama, then please, by all means, come to my mansion. The Hauseki I wear on my body are nothing worth mentioning. There are mountains of various treasures offered from all over, and my father would surely be happy if Oniharuwake-sama were to come. I truly wish for you to come with me.'

Knowing he was an impostor, she deliberately tried to draw him out. Davis thought in an instant that when they reached near her mansion, she would order her subordinates to capture this Dorobau, punish him, and make him repent. Bell, thinking that if he went to the mansion his disguise would be revealed immediately, became desperate.

Bell: 'Eh, the truth is, I am a thief known throughout the world. Come, I will take everything attached to your body right here. How about it? If you keep babbling, I will even take your precious life.'

Hell, without thinking, called out from the grass:

Hell: 'Oi, Bell, do not say such unreasonable things. If you want them that much, why not just ask for one?'

Bell was startled and tried to cover it up with a loud cough, 'Ahem, ahem,' to drown out Hell's voice. Davis immediately realized that another cowardly thief was hiding outside.

Davis: 'Ho ho ho ho, what a spineless Dorobau, saying things like "ask for one"—what a pathetic, stingy thing to say. If you want my life, I shall give it to you. If you want the Hauseki, I do not mind giving them either. However, since I am a living being, I will move a little, so please be careful not to be blown away.'

Bell: 'Eh, it is no use anymore. Hey, you Hell, come out here too. We will split the spoils of war.'

Hell: 'I do not want to do such murderous things anymore; what if it shines from heaven again? Diamonds or whatever, I have had enough of things that shine.'

ヘル『オイ、ベル、そんな無茶な事云ふない。それ程欲しけりや一つ丈頂戴したらどうだ』

と呼んでゐる。ベルはハツとし乍ら、

『アハン　アハン』

と大きな咳払に紛らし、ヘルの声を消さうとした。デビスは早くもまだ外に一人の卑怯な泥坊が潜んでゐる事を悟つた。

デビス『ホツホホホホ、腰抜泥坊だこと、一つ丈頂戴せいなどと、何した情ないシミツタレた事をいふのだらう。命が欲しけりや命もやらう。宝石が欲しければ与らぬ事もない。併し乍ら此方も生物だから、チツと許り動きますから、跳飛ばされぬやうになさいませや』

ベル『エー、モウ駄目だ。コラ、ヘルの奴、汝もやつて来んかい。戦利品は山分けだ』

ヘル『俺モウそんな殺生な事はしたくないワ、又天から光つて来たら何うする。ダイヤモンドでも何でも、俺モウ光るものには懲々だ』

§ 4

Debis: 'Ho ho ho ho, you are all nothing but weak-kneed thieves gathered here. However, you trembling coward of a thief over there, you are a pathetic fellow. It is a waste to give it to such a guy, but I will give you jewels if it is you, and I will leave your body to you if you want your body, so instead of crouching there in the grassy field like a grasshopper, step right out here quickly.'

Heru is robbed of his wits by the bold and fearless woman's words, loses strength in his hips, and trembles while lying flat with a thud.

Beru: 'Hey, you coward, you only blurt out cowardly things, you are a fellow who only hinders the business instead of being of any help. No matter how much a mere woman's hands are capable of, it is well known. Hey woman, if you hate handing them over, I will snatch them away directly, so behave yourself.'

So saying, he stretched out his long arm like a monkey and made to grab the jewel shining on her head with a firm grip. Debis firmly grasped that hand, and using the jiujitsu techniques she had trained in daily, threw him about threeken away into the grassy field. Beru became like a dead warrior and tried to bite into the woman and strangle her throat. Beru's hands were also capable just a little bit, but he is no match for Debis after all. However, blinded by the jewels, forgetting even his own peril, he was thrown and grappled, thrown and grappled with all his might, tossed almost twelve or thirteen times until he was utterly limp. Even so, without learning his lesson yet, he tries to bite, aiming at the light of the jewels on her head and body. Debis said, 'What a bother,' leaped down from the rock, firmly grabbed the breastplate of the charging Beru, and choked his breath. Beru is struggling with his hands and feet, kicking and thrashing about. As expected, Heru could not go on trembling and lying prostrate in the grass forever either, so he found a half-rotten stake that had fallen nearby, shining in the moonlight, and struck down with all his might aiming at the crown of the head from behind as Debis was strangling Beru's neck. His hand slipped and he struck her fiercely from the ear across the cheek with a heavy thud. Alas, Debis uttered a single cry of 'Ah!' and easily fell right then and there. The night wind blows past making a sound without hesitation or reserve.

原文（近代文語）

デビス『ホホホホ、腰の弱い泥坊許り集つたものだなア。併し乍ら其処辺に慄つてる腰抜泥坊、お前は可愛想な奴だ。こんな奴にやるのは惜いが、お前になら宝石もやらうし、体が欲しけら体も任してやるから、そんな草原に螽斯の如うにスツ込んでをらずに、トツトと此処へ出て来なさい』

ヘルは大胆不敵の女の言葉に度肝を抜かれ、腰をぬかしてバタリと平太つたまま慄うてゐる。

ベル『エー、腰抜奴、気の弱い事許りぬかしやがつて、助けになる所か商売の邪魔許りする奴だ。タカが女の一人、何程手が利いてると云つても知れたものだ。オイ女、渡すのが厭なら俺が直接に奪つてやる、神妙にしろ』

と云ひ乍ら猿臂を伸ばして、頭に光る宝石をグツと掴みかけた。デビスは其手をグツと握り、日頃鍛えし柔術の手を以て、三間許り草つ原へ投げ付けた。ベルは死武者になつて、女に喰ひつき喉を締めようとした。ベルも少し許り手は利いてゐたが、到底デビスには敵はない。併し乍ら宝石に眼眩んで、自分の危い事も忘れ、一生懸命に放られては組み付き放られては組み付き、殆ど十二三回も投げられ、グタグタになつた。それでもまだ性懲もなく、頭や体の宝石の光を目当に喰ひつく。デビスは『エー面倒』と岩を飛下り、武者振りつくベルの胸倉をグツと取り、息を詰めた。ベルは手足を藻掻きヂタバタとやつてゐる。流石のヘルも何時迄戦慄して草の中に伏艇してる訳にも行かず、傍に落ちてゐた半朽ちたる棒杭が月に照らされて光つてゐるのを見つけ出し、デビスがベルの首を締めてゐる背後から、脳天目蒐けて力一杯打下した。手許外れて耳から横つ面をウンと云ふ程撲りつけた。愍やデビスはアツと一声叫んで脆くも其場に倒れて了つた。夜嵐は遠慮会釈もなく音を立てて通つて行く。
