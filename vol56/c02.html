Chapter 2: 恋淵 [1432]

恋淵〔1432〕

§ 1

At the Yamaguchi of the Erushina Valley along the mountain continuation of Mount Terumon on the border between Fusa and Tsuki, even the smoke of lonely hearths has ceased, and the long days of spring are exceedingly difficult to pass; in a dilapidated house where the pillars sway and the beams rot, where even the firelight has faded on a long autumn night, a woman of exquisite beauty capable of deceiving even flowers, wrapped in tattered garments within the humble and desolate hut of a peasant, sits all alone, plunged in sorrow and lamentation. Once, Onikumohiko built a temporary lodge there, and there were Okuniwake and Okunihime, the two who guarded the traces of the divine lodge of Mount Terumon where the Brahman teachings had been founded. These two husband and wife had two beautiful daughters named Princess Debisu and Kerinahime.

Kerinahime had fallen in love with a young man named Kamahiko from the neighborhood, and hiding from the eyes of her parents, she slipped away from the lodge on Mount Terumon, built a broken house in a valley of the Erushina Valley, and lived there playing at being husband and wife. However, her husband had gone away a year prior holding a certain purpose, leaving Kerinahime behind in this broken house, and without a single message since; the princess left behind was at her wits' end, unable to return to her parents' home nor able to go anywhere else, eating tree roots or foraging for meager fruits and the like to barely pass the days, while awaiting tidings of her husband Kamahiko. The house grew increasingly poor, with neither friends nor acquaintances, and the only visitors were the peak's tempest and the sound of rain.

The sorrow of one who narrows the wide world of her own accord and hides away from the world deep in the mountains is unbearable; with no human footprints the paths are narrow and choked with daylilies, and the summer blazing sun feels somehow lonely, while from time to time the voices of fierce beasts pierce her ears and cause her soul to fly into the void, more times than she can know. The figure of her husband, who fails to return no matter how many days pass, made her wonder if she had been utterly abandoned by the divine grace and was destined to expose her dew-like life upon the wild fields; lost in thought and hesitating this way and that, she parted the grasses guided by the moonlight, and finally emerged into the human thoroughfare at the mouth of the valley.

Here a considerably large mountain river flows, sending fierce rapids and splashes flying. This is called the Erushina River. Despairing of the world and without a thought for before or behind, Kerinahime leaped into the water beneath the cliff, aiming for the depths. The moon hid behind the western peak, and the darkness of the night grew increasingly intense. At this time, at the edge of the mountain river, three men with long swords girded at their waists and masked cowls were whispering about something in low voices.

First Man: 'Hey, brothers, the three of us received six thousand ryo of gold from General-sama in the northern forest, and with his instruction we intended to return to our home province as quickly as possible to make our parents, wives, and children rejoice, but it has ultimately not gone as we desired; the gold of grace has been entirely taken away, and is it not impossible for us to return home to the province in this state? I want to make one more effort somehow, and taking that as an opportunity, to completely give up being thieves, return to our home province, and settle down to an honest occupation.'

Second Man: 'Hey, Beru, you and the rest of us were each distributed two thousand ryo, but now we are already left with nothing; to say that what you took gets taken away is the reason of heaven and earth, so ill-gotten wealth does not stay with its owner, and how about completely giving up on it now? I already disliked this trade from the beginning, but I have come to detest it all the more. If the gold we stole with such effort is taken away from us again, it amounts to nothing at all, does it not? For all that remains is sin.'

Third Man: 'Hey, Beru and Heru, both of you, what is this about being taken away? Did not all of us dote on courtesans and drown ourselves in wine, spending it all? Since we had such a pleasurable time to that extent, it was by no means stolen. Rather than that, if we go about ten ri from here, there is the divine lodge of Mount Terumon. Why not make a pilgrimage there, entirely reform our hearts, and like General-sama, become yamabushi and go around blowing conch shells? If we do that, perhaps our sins up to now will be wiped away and we can make a pilgrimage to paradise.'

Beru: 'Agh, good-for-nothing! Fellows who blow such weak whining sounds might as well throw themselves into this mountain river and perish. As for me, I intend from here on to further my studies in the art ofthievery and make the treasures of the realm entirely at my own free disposal.'

Hei: 'But, didn't that sound rather strange? It can't possibly be a suicide by drowning, can it?'

Beru: 'Well, it certainly looks like a drowning suicide. However, if some wretch wants to die, they might as well die as they please. We have such a strong attachment to life that we intend to keep on living for a thousand or ten thousand years.'

Hei: 'If I hear of a drowning suicide, I cannot simply sit by like this. We must do something to save them, surely. Right, Heru?'

Heru: 'Aye, that is so. Just as Sharu says, shall I strip stark naked right now and dive into the mountain river to rescue them? If we dilly-dallied, even in a pool with such a slow current, the corpse might disappear.'

Sharu: 'Oh, that is right,' he said, flinging off his clothes and plunging with a huge splash right into the heart of the darkness of the pool. The very moment he jumped in, something seized fiercely upon his own legs with all its might. Startled to the utmost, Sharu cried out at the top of his lungs, 'Save me! Save me!' Heru plunged in with another big splash. When he looked, a man and a woman were rising and sinking in the pool, clutching at one another. In the gloom, Heru seized them by the hair and dragged them up to the shallows. Both of them had swallowed a vast amount of water and were all but breathless. Raising his voice to the highest pitch, Heru cried out,

Heru: 'Hey, Beru, this is terrible. It looks like even Sharu has gone and died. Come quickly and help me make them spit up the water.'

Beru, maintaining an air of utter detachment from above, said,

Beru: 'Hey, you Heru, has Sharu died already? Since wretches who love to die are better off left alone, why not just leave him be? And as for the other person, is it a man or a woman? If it is a woman, I suppose it is fine to save her.'

Heru said,

Heru: 'Ah, what a cold-hearted wretch you are!'

So saying, he dragged the two of them up onto the fine sand and, tending to them in various ways, made them spit out the water. At last, the two breathed life back into their bodies.

Heru: 'Hey, Sharu, that was a close call. Well, I am glad above all that you have come to your senses. But whoever's young lady this might be, she has somehow managed to revive. Thanks to this, our desperate efforts were not in vain. Ah, how joyous, Great Jizaiten-sama!'

Thus he clapped his hands in prayer. From above, smoking his tobacco with puffs and puffs, Beru said,

Beru: 'Hey, you three denizens of this ephemeral world, you failed-suicide wretches, what are you dawdling about for? Why don't you come on up in good time?'

Heru: 'Hey, you Beru, it is no use when it is this dark. Won't you kindle a fire somewhere around there and light a torch for us? We cannot find the way up, you see.'

Beru: 'Stop making such a noisy fuss. Fire is forbidden to thieves. Fortunately, since it is summer, it should not be cold, so you might as well crouch down right there until daybreak. I hear there is even a torpedo boat around, so the timing is just right. A pool of love into which one has fallen deep; no doubt some foolish woman existed and committed double suicide out of devotion to a worthless man. Where on earth would you find someone who goes to rescue such a wretch? You are a remarkably optimistic fellow. Ha ha ha ha ha!'

Fuming at Beru's cold attitude in failing to show any friendship, Heru and Sharu managed at last to rescue Princess Kerina, making their way up by following the sheer, wall-like rocks. Hearing that it was a woman, and wishing to test whether she was beautiful or ugly, Beru gathered withered grass and set it ablaze with a flash. When he looked closely, despite the fact that the woman was a breathtakingly fair-skinned, high-bred beauty of tender years, her attire was a truly wretched, ragged garment.

Beru: 'My, oh my, whoever's young lady this might be, though I do not know, what a remarkably pretty person you are. Like a golden jewel wrapped in rags is your appearance, noble lady; no doubt there must be some deep reason behind your committing suicide in such a place as this. Whatever the case may be, thinking that we must needs save you, I had my retainers Heru and Sharu go to your rescue. Come now, tell me the circumstances in full detail.'

Sharu: 'Ha! What a fine thing you are saying. When we said we might as well save her, didn't you blurt out that if someone wanted to die, they might as well be left to die as they pleased? Hey, Heru, just looking at this nice face of his and hearing him blurt out that sort of thing right away leaves me too astonished for words, doesn't it?'
