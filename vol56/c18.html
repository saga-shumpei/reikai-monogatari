Chapter 18: 寛恕 [1448]

寛恕〔1448〕

§ 1

Okunihime enters a room together with Michihiko, looking worried and speaking in whispers.

Hime: 'Moshi, Ambrucq-san, ever since you arrived, the karei's taido has somehow seemed rather restless, so I cannot help wondering if even his musuko might have hidden the jewel. Are they not trying to trouble us wareware, plunge us into kiuchi, make our daughter Davis-hime their niyōbau, and harbor ill omens? I have always thought them suspicious from the start, but whatever is said, he is the karei's musuko, and I have found it difficult to bring up, which distresses me sincerely. What is your okanga on this, I wonder?'

Michi: 'Moshi anata, supposing the karei's musuko were indeed hiding the niyoihotsushiyu jewel, what is your intention to do about it?'

Hime: 'If such a thing becomes clear, no matter how much he is the karei's musuko, we surely could not forgive him.'

Michi: 'Here, we must settle the matter smoothly no matter what. Firstly, because it would become a shame for the master of the house... and once it breaks out into seken, there will be no shikata for it, so how would it be if we settle it as much as possible in naishiyō?'

Hime: 'If only the jewel comes back, without it turning into buchōhafu for us fūfu, and if everyone is saved, I should not wish to bring it out too much into the open. However, since we do not yet know this with hatsukiri certainty, I came to your oide, right in the midst of nursing my husband, because I wished to ask you, anatasama.'

Michi: 'If your okanga is to settle any and all crimes privately, then I shall speak. Actually, as you suspect, the karei's musuko Wax, along with Ex and Hermann—three young men—have stolen the sacred jewel and are hiding it for a certain purpose.'

Hime: 'Ah, that makes sense of it. I had indeed thought Wax's taido was restless. Since the karei Austin is an extremely loyal and honest person, there is no fear of him doing such a thing, but as the saying goes, bodies are created, yet souls are not created; thus, it is a frequent habit in seken for fools to be born to heroes of great might, or rebels to be born to loyal vassals and righteous men. Therefore, the karei rushing back home after hearing your words must also have been because something struck his heart. Regarding that, I had my servant Elle go to observe how matters stood, but for some reason, he has not yet returned.'

Michi: 'Ah, he will be returning presently. When he does, the truth will come to light. We must make as little a grand affair of this as possible.'

Just as they are speaking thus, the servant Elle returns in a flurry, panting for breath,

Elle: 'Moshi okusama, tata, it is terrible! A mutual slaughter has begun.'

Hime: 'Nani, a mutual slaughter has begun... were they fighting somewhere?'

Elle: 'Me-me-memeshi! If you call it mutual slaughter, it is not just a fight, you see. It is a fight with a hair or two more added, you see. Solé, it is a matter of taking lives, you see. How frightening, how frightening, earthly and heavenly changes, earthly and heavenly changes! Choking throats, throwing ornaments, shouting, escaping over the mountain from the back door, slipping into the storehouse—solé, solé, it was a grand affair!'

Elle, if you speak like that, how can I understand? Where on earth is this place?

Elle: 'Hei, it is fixed, you see. It is a matter inside the house, you see.'

Hime: 'Whom do you say fought with whom?'

Elle: 'Men and men wrestled for each other's lives. Eh, what a hard person to understand you are!'

Hime: 'I am asking you who on earth is fighting whom!'

Elle: 'Eh, how stifling! Are there only a certain Hyōbee or that Hyōbee here? If we dawdle, lives will be lost, you see. Ah, how frustrating this is, wai!'

§ 2

Hime: 'If we keep talking about such incomprehensible matters forever, won't it get nowhere? I am getting impatient, you see. Are you still not going to the Karei's mansion? You must have been watching a dog fight or something, most likely.'

El: 'Yes, about that Karei, though. He was awfully, terribly angry! He stood up blue veins on his large forehead, you see...'

Michihiko: 'Ahahaha, well now, El-sama or whatever you are called, I understand. Because you are flustered quite a bit, what you say comes out all jumbled and hard to make out, but you went to the Karei's residence and saw the four people fighting, didn't you?'

El: 'Yes, exactly as you say. Now then, I am going to proclaim it throughout the village. This is a terrible crisis, a terrible crisis!'

Just as he tried to leap out, Okunihime grabbed him by the back of the neck and pulled him back firmly.

Hime: 'Hey there, El, you must not go anywhere. And you must not blabber anything.'

El: 'W-well, even if you tell me such things, can I possibly keep quiet about this? If we dawdle, who knows, the Karei's life might be lost, you see.'

Michihiko: 'El-sama, please calm down for a moment. The Karei will be all right, so you must not say a single thing.'

El muttered 'Yes,' and shrank into himself.

Michihiko: 'Okusama, it seems that somehow what Wax had hidden was seen through by Kareiidono, and a disturbance has arisen. Please leave this to me. I will definitely bring back the Niyoihotsushiyu and show it to you. And please leave the Karei and his child to me. Since we have discovered this all thanks to the divine grace of the Ōkami, after all.'

Hime: 'Whatever happens is as you, whose divine virtue is high, command; I leave it entirely to you.'

Just as they were talking, the Karei Ōlsutin, while leading his child Wax, wrapped the jewel of Niyoihotsushiyu heavily in multiple layers, appeared at this place, prostrated himself completely with both hands on the ground,

Ōlsutin: 'Okusama, I have truly done an inexcusable thing. My foolish son was instigated by bad friends and plotted various rebellions; realizing this at last, I have attached a waist-rope to my son and come here to apologize. Since my son is a capital offender against life, please deal with him to your heart's content. If I thought such a person could emerge as my son, I could show my face neither to our master nor to the world...'

Even before he finished speaking, he drew his pocket dagger, and at the very instant he was about to thrust it straight into his own belly, Michihiko leaped down, snatched away the dagger, and raising his voice,

Michihiko: 'Ōlsutin-dono, compose your heart. Everything must be the providence of the Ōkami. Since this matter has been entrusted entirely to me by Okusama, there is no need for you to rush so much first. Who knows how much more sincerity will prevail if you prolong the life about to die now and devote your loyalty to your master? And it will be good for you to rest assured, since Michihiko has also taken your son, Wax-dono, into his custody. To tell the truth, Ambrak is a provisional name for me; in fact, I am called Michihiko, a missionary of Hinodekyo. Knowing that this mansion belongs to Brahmanism, I intentionally disguised myself as a missionary of Brahmanism and came to save you. I ask you, starting with Okusama, and everyone present, to forgive my crime of having falsified my true name until now.'

Hime: 'Eh, what are you saying? Were you a missionary-sama of Hinodekyo? This is immensely rude of me. However, you have well and truly helped us out of a critical situation. I thank you. Through your divine virtue, the whereabouts of the jewel has become known, and there is nothing more joyful than this.'

Michihiko: 'Whether we speak of Hinodekyo or of Brahmanism, tracing them to their origin, they are one single Ōkami; therefore, there can be no superiority or inferiority in the teachings. Only, according to the heart of those who uphold the Way, there arise differences of magnitude and hierarchy in the manifestation of divine virtue.'

Aorus: 'Ever since the first time I had the honor of meeting you, I thought you were somehow an unusual person, but were you a Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō? Please excuse my profound rudeness. I am truly ashamed to expose such a drunken and disorderly scene to your eyes. Since we parent and child have smeared mud upon the face of Brahminism, please be so kind as to let us die. This alone is my earnest prayer. And if, by means of my suicide, you will make my son's crime somewhat lighter, taking that as a parting gift for the netherworld, I shall bravely march to my death. Namu Daijizaiten Ōkunihikonomikoto-sama...'

So she clasped her hands, showing a resolute determination to die.

Michihiko stood up and began to chant a Sendenka.

'A Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, I am Michihiko the Kamutsukasa, Manipulated by the divine sacred cords, Unwittingly appearing at the foot of Mount Terumon, Crossing over the clear stream, At the time of proceeding toward this place, The Kamutsukasa of Princess Okuni, Accompanied by two handmaidens, Most kindly invited my very self Back to her mansion; Hearing all manner of varied worries, Declaring them without hiding or concealing, And listening to how she dispatches them, Unable to restrain tears of sympathy, At the august presence of the Great Ancestral Kami, When praying with the utmost sincerity, Smart, the avatar of the Kami, Spoke a word close to my ear, And the whereabouts of the wish-granting sacred jewel,

Showing it fully and in complete detail, While delivering numerous prophecies such as Princess Kelina and others, Returning by parting the paths of clouds, My heart too became courageous, Facing toward Princess Okuni, Explaining and preaching the principle of Heaven and Earth, Only urging that whatever happens is Kaminaobi, And the vast-hearted Ōnaobi, Beseeching her to look again and see, Prostrating myself before the presence of the Kami, To tie up this difficult situation with extreme ease, I distributed my village-liver heart into a thousand pieces. Just at that moment, Lord El came in, hurriedly entering, Reporting that a murder had suddenly broken out At the chamberlain's mansion, And hearing such a sudden uproar, That it would be a major crisis if it leaked outside,

Thinking what on earth should be done, at that moment, Came the arrival of Aorus-tin, Who auspiciously caused the precious treasure of wonders To be transported right here, Rejoicing that this auspicious sign Is an auspicious sign for the mansion of Terumon That will remain unchangeable for ten thousand generations, Declaring anew all crookedness, And solely leaving all matters to the judgment of the Great Kami, Man is the child of Kami, the palace of Kami, And from the beginning is not a wicked thing, Clouded by the crooked spirits of Yamata no Orochi and ugly kami, As they had unwittingly fallen away into the demonic path, Chanting forth the stern Kototama granted by the Great Ancestral Kami, And blowing away all manner of miscellaneous crimes and sins In the wind of Shinato,

Purifying them so that no dust remains Upon the body or spirit, Like the flow of the swift river, Thus Michihiko prays reverently Before the presence of the Great Ancestral Kami; Princess Okuni, Aorus-tin, And Waxu the Kamutsukasa, be at ease in your hearts And be assured, for since Michihiko Has appeared here, How could any criminals be created? Rest your hearts at ease, trust in Kamunagara, Pledging to the Kami and proclaiming it abroad, Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, Whether the moon waxes or wanes, Even if the Great Earth should sink, If one entrusts one's body solely to the teachings Of Makoto-hito's Ananai, There is nothing whatsoever to fear From the raging of any crookedness; Revere and honor the divine virtue

Of the Great Ancestral Kami of Ananai, Protecting the Brahmin teachings, The august majesty of Ōkunihiko, Ah, Kamunagara-kamikami, Grant us spiritual peace and prosperity.'

Through Michihiko's song, the chamberlain Aorus-tin and Waxu at last felt at ease, shed tears, and expressed gratitude for the divine grace, and holding the wish-granting jewel, together with Princess Okuni and Michihiko, they proceeded softly to apologize for the crime in the sickroom of Okuniwake.
