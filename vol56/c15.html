Michihiko: 'Manifesting as the august spirit of rigor, O Great Deity of Takamimusubi, Manifesting as the august spirit of harmony, O Great Deity of Kamumimusubi, Manifesting in the wondrous water and fire, The Great Deity of Ananai-kyō, Decreeing the divine life of Haniyasuhiko and Haniyasuhime into the world, Purifying the souls of a hundred deities of heaven and earth, And wishing to save them in the pure sacred place of Tengoku, Keeping this deep in mind, Entrusted that divine work to the Great Deity Kamusanō, Thereupon the Great Deity of Harmony, As the two pillars of Kamurogi and Kamuromi, Offered the divine decrees of Kami to heaven and earth as an Ananahi pillar, Ruling the world of Tokoyami peacefully and placidly, To make it an august age of pine, Wishing to return to the protection of Hinode, Trained a hundred officials, Traversed the Toyoashihara Nakatsukuni, Through eighty countries and eighty islands of the land, Visiting every remaining nook and corner, And caused the gospel of the heavenly pure land to spread. Preying upon the wild play of Ametasuhiko and Habahime, The Magatsuhi gods who disturb this world, Yamata no Orochi, ugly foxes, And wicked fiends, throughout the realm, Possessed the officials who govern the land And a hundred other people, Boldly and unrestrictedly committing all manner of evil deeds, Soiling the world day and night, Fierce and outrageous are these ugly sports! Becoming disciples of the propaganda officials of the Saien Mansion and Tamakunibake, With the sincere thought in their hearts To transmit the teachings of Kami to the countries of the four quarters, Michihiko crossed the Lion River, Spending the days amidst the vast wilderness, An unavoidable lodging of dew where he sleeps, At the very moment when he follows a dark path, Innumerable defeated warriors of the Brahman religion, Carrying weapons in their hands, Enraged to slaughter the propaganda officials of Ananai-kyō, Surrounded our party's surroundings ten and twenty fold, Brandishing swords and throwing stones, Attacking with fierce momentum. At the very moment when our master teacher Tamakunibake And Masumihiko struck forth the Kototama with all their might And fought defensively, Itahiko's thigh was pierced by the tip of a charging spear of the enemy, And he fell heavily to the ground upon that spot. Seeing this with shock, Masumihiko Clasped Itahiko under his arm, Cut his way through the enemy's heavy encirclement, And fled away to parts unknown. Our master teacher too, by the multitude, Was surrounded and concealed his figure somewhere. Michihiko, who remained behind, Suddenly found his Kototama choking up, With no strategy left, in his sorrow, Risking his life to break through the encirclement, Little by little searching for the traces of our master, Has advanced and come as far as this. Ah, O Kami, O Kami, How venerable is the divine protection, Manifesting above our master, That the mission received from Kami May be completely and thoroughly fulfilled, Grant us the dew of grace. Where is Masumihiko now? Has the spear wound of official Itahiko Healed already, or else, Troubled by a deep wound, is he hiding in the deep mountains Nurturing his illness? Though he longs to hear of it, We whose spirits are clouded Have no way to inquire of Kami, Anxious about the progress of the way, We have unknowingly arrived Near Mount Terumon, where the Brahman religion is entrenched. Before the untrustworthy enemy, Numerous traps of scheming pits Have been prepared, and we hear That they wait with bated breath for the officials of Ananai-kyō to come. Ah, O Kami, O Kami, Before the venerable presence of Kami, Beginning with our master teacher, We respectfully and reverently pray for the good fortune of our party.'

Chanting this secretly, he arrived at the riverbank of the An Black River flowing down from Mount Terumon. It was the middle of summer, and a semicircular moon hung in the western sky, casting a sharp light like a reaping hook. Taking advantage of the sunset, Michihiko sat down on the river lebank, lowered his voice to intone the Amatsu Norito, and upon finishing, spoke to himself in a soliloquy,

Michi: 'Ah, the flow of water and the destiny of man, how things change when they change. Since I became the companion of our master teacher Tamakunibake, leaving the Saien Mansion in the winter of last year, we have known ups and downs, various and sundry hardships and labors. Among them, our master teacher had his eye torn out by a monkey in Huai Gu, was forced to hole up in a shrine grove, and in accordance with the divine decree, constructed the shrine palace. Together with us three disciples, we bravely advanced to fulfill the divine message to the capital of the moon world, Haruna. At that very moment of sudden rain, the great torrent of the Lion River, crossing the river of a width where the eye could hardly reach astride a water horse, risking our lives to cross over to this side, when we let the sun set and slept for a night in the vast wilderness, numerous remnants of the Brahman religion attacked us. My friend Itahiko was stabbed by the enemy's sharp hand-spear, and whether he lives or dies is uncertain. Where have our master teacher and Masumihiko gone now? With no tidings whatsoever in this summer night, my tears turn toward the moon, the dew on my sleeves has no way to dry—have pity, O God Tsukiteruhiko.'

Expressing his jiyutsukuwai, he is praying itsushiyōkenmei.

Michihiko finally fell asleep upon the aogusa on the riverbank. Takusan mosquitoes, catching the scent of a human, gathered around with a curious air, raised a buzzing and unpleasant sound, and piled heavily all over Michihiko's body, biting into him. At this moment, a sudden, record-breaking river wind blew up, rolled Michihiko's body, which had been sleeping upon the bank, like a ball, and blew him straight into the surrounding dorota. Michihiko tried to start up in alarm, but the mud was deep and his body sank in up to the waist; unable to do anything whatsoever, his body sank into the dorota with a stinging sensation, and before long he had sunk up to his neck. If he were left as he was, his whole body would sink into the mud, and Michihiko's seimei had already fallen into a unmei like a lamp before a storm. Michihiko prayed at the top of his voice, offering up the amatsunorito itsushiyōkenmei, earnestly pleading that even if his nikutai were buried and perished within the dorota, his seirei might be saved in tengoku. To such a place, the shadow of a black four-legged beast appeared from izuku no tomo shirezu, brushed away the doro surrounding Michihiko's body, bit hold of his mud-stained kimono, and—while even itself sinking more than hanbun of its body into the doro—finally rescued him up onto the bank. Michihiko knew not what kind of beast it might be, but thinking that the one who had saved him could surely be none other than a messenger of the kamisama, he pressed his palms together, worshipped the black beast itsushiyōkenmei, leapt into the shallow waters of the river with his mud-covered kimono still on, and slowly began to wash it. The beast of the black shadow plunged into the midst of the river with a splashing sound once more and was washing its own body.

Michihiko finished washing his clothing hastily, and since it was summer, he dried his kimono upon the white, sun-baked gravel of the riverbed; as for himself, in order to ward off the mosquitoes, he resolved to spend the night with his entire body immersed in the water. The shadow of the beast had disappeared before he knew it. Having barely seen out the summer night and looking closely at his clothing, he saw that disgusting leeches were biting all over the kimono as if hair were growing upon it. They were leeches of strong adhesive power that would not easily come off; when he tried to remove them with his hands, they bit into his hands and would not let go no matter what. Turning over in his mind whether to think, 'Ah, come what may, shall I throw this kimono away into the river and set off on a barefoot journey, going as far as I can?' or 'No, no, wait, wait; when evening comes again, I shall be unable to ward off the attacks of the mosquitoes, yet if I put on a kimono with this many leeches all over it, my blood will be sucked once more—alas, what had I better do?', he lamented his misfortune in life, climbed back up to the bank, and was drowning in tears.

From far away in the direction of Mukōgata, the black beast seen the previous night came running toward this place as if shooting an arrow. This was because First Princess had foreknown Michihiko's distress, instructed Smart smoothly, and sent him forth on a rescue mission. Smart had brought in his jaws a splendid robe of a Brahman missionary. And before Michihiko, barking 'wan, wan' two or three times, he wagged his tail and showed a demeanor as if urging him to wear it. Choking back tears of emotion, Michihiko muttered to himself:

Michihiko: 'Ah, you are cleverer than even a brute beast, what a fine dog, you have rescued me well. Surely you must be a messenger of the kamisama without a doubt. That being the case, I will gladly accept this robe. However, it is the missionary robe of Brahmanism. Surely there must be some profound divine will in this as well. Taking advantage of this, shall I disguise myself as a Brahman missionary and cross this Mount Telumon toward Mukōgata?'

muttering thus, he swiftly wrapped the robe around his body. When he cast a glance down at his feet with a sudden start, the shadow of the dog was already gone. The shadow of the dog running up the bald mountain in the distant Mukōgata could be seen looking no larger than a cat. Michihiko crossed the shallows to reach the western shore, and deliberately assuming the air of a Brahman missionary, he proceeded onward while chanting sutras.

An old crone of about sixty with grey-flecked hair, accompanied by two maidservants and leaning upon a staff, advanced toward this place. Just as Michihiko stopped in his tracks at the side of the road, muttering to himself, 'Well now, what a mysterious crone she is. Unlike the Vaishyas or Shudras, she possesses a noble air from no one knows where. Could this most likely be the mistress of Okunowake?', the three of them quickly approached him,

姫『あ、左様で厶いますか、貴方のお名はアン・ブラツク様でしたか、何と目出たいお名で厶いますなア、此アン・ブラツク川は昔から濁つた事のない清川で厶いますが、其名を負はせ玉ふ宣伝使に出会うとは、何といふ結構な事でせう。之でテルモン山の館も、万世不動の基礎が固まるでせう。実の所は夢のお告に「アン・ブラツク川の岸辺に行け、さうすればお前を助ける真人が現はれる」との事で厶いましたので、信頼ない夢を力として参りましたが、矢張り神様のお告げと見えて、尊い名の宣伝使に会ふ事が出来ました。ああ有難い有難い』

と嬉し涙をたらし乍ら合掌する。三千彦は真面目な顔して、

三千『ハイ承知致しました。然らばお世話に与りませう』

姫『早速の御承知、満足に存じます。……コレ、ケーや、セミスや、宣伝使のお荷物を持たして頂きなさい』

ケー『ハイ何でも持たして頂きますが、別に何もお持になつてはゐないぢや厶いませぬか』

姫『それでもお背に沢山の荷物を負うてゐらつしやるぢやないか』

ケー『奥様、あれは荷物ぢや厶いませぬ、宣伝使様が猫を負うてゐらつしやるのですよ。なア、セミスさま、さうぢや厶いませぬか』

三千彦は何時の間にやら背中にブクブクとした瘤が出来てゐたが、背中の事とて少しも気がつかなかつた。

三千『アハハハハ、猫に見えますかな、どうで犬に……』

と云ひかけて俄に口をつぐみ、

三千『犬か猫のやうな霊ですから、仕方がありませぬ。まアさう仰有らずに可愛がつて下さいませ』

小国姫は『サア参りませう』と先に立つて行く。三千彦は半安半危の面持にて門内深く進み入り、小国姫と共に直ちに神殿に至つてバラモン教の経文を称へた。三千彦は只聞き覚へに経文のそしり走りを知つてゐる許りで、余り大きな声を出し、間違つた事を言つては、忽ち看破さるる事を恐れ、ワザと小声になり、教服に添へてあつた数珠を爪繰り乍ら、一生懸命に念じてゐる。何時の間にやら三千彦の猫背は元の通りに痕跡もなく直つてゐた。これはスマートの霊が三千彦を無事に館内に送り且つ其身辺を守らむが為であつた。スマートは館の床下に隠れて守つてゐる。
