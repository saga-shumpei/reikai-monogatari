Chapter 25: 万歳楽 [1500]

万歳楽〔1500〕

§ 1

Teku, feeling as though he had become the Bantōgashira along with Akisu and Kāru, tied his Nejihachimaki, stripped down to his loincloth, carried out sake from the storehouse, knocked out the bung of a barrel, and continued to chatter aimlessly after taking a hearty gulp from the mouth of a Hishiyaku.

Hundreds of Satobito, regardless of Kisenrauniyaku, came rushing in—old women with rheum in their eyes, old men with runny noses, the Hanamaga, the deaf, and even the blind—and they were having a riotous celebration in the spacious Teinai, knocking out the mirrors of sake barrels and singing and dancing with Itsushiyōkenmei.

Teku, having reached the Chiyōjiyō of his Tokui, said:

Teku: 'Hey, Akisu, Kāru, pull yourselves together! Keep a close eye on the Yōsu of the guests, and make sure there is not a single Rakugoshiya who has not received Miki, so that there are no oversights. Since those who are not of a Shiyōjiyōketsupaku spirit like mine cannot notice things in every Sumizumi, you must be the kind of fellows who pay attention to everything—from the places where people run, to the inside of the Setsuchin, and even the corners of the Monōki—otherwise you cannot fulfill your true Goyō. Ah, this is sweet sake. I am a Buka of this house, and even if I drink enough to topple a Shiyōjiyō, it is only natural, for this is the greatest Gauka in the land of Izumi, no matter what anyone says. As they say, "If you take shelter, choose a great tree; when it comes to chopsticks and masters, bigger is better." So, I am quitting being a spy from today and have decided on my own to become the head Bantōgashira for Bāchiru-sama, so you must follow my orders from now on. Do you understand, Akisu, Kāru? Tell me, Akisu, Kāru, that is fine, is it not? Yoitosa no yoitosa, good sake, good sake, drinking and drinking, delicious and drinking, the intoxication is spinning, drinking, sweet and drinking, yoiyoi no tekuteku. If you speak of Shiyōjiyōhiko-sama of the four hands, you are speaking of me. No matter how much you call me Teku-sama, my hands are not prone to thievery, so please keep that in mind.'

Akisu: 'Hmph, do not act so grand just because you have suddenly put on the face of a Bantōgashira. However, since today is a celebratory day, I shall refrain from fighting. Since you are the Bantōgashira for just this one day, you may blow as much hot air as you like. Right, Kāru?'

Kāru: 'Yeah, that is right. It is not Teku who is speaking; it is the celebratory sake that is speaking. They say sake is "mad water," but it really is an interesting thing. Even for light drinkers like us, one gulp makes my Kimochi feel sharp and clear.'

Akisu: 'Hey, you said just one gulp, but you have been hitting it quite a lot since a while ago, have you not? It was as if a cow or an elephant was drinking slop.'

Kāru: 'That is a given. While a gulp from a five-sho sake bottle is only a small amount, when I tried it bit by bit, a faint red flower began to bloom on my cheeks, ehehehe.'

Akisu: 'Hey, did the mistress not tell you that because you have such a round face, you look like a full moon or that you must love rice cakes? People who love rice cakes do not drink sake, but you are a connoisseur of mountains and rivers. You said you would not drink a single drop of sake, yet you had a red face and breath that smelled like overripe persimmons; so you were drinking in secret, you revealed your true colors, you transformed tiger!'

Kāru: 'That is a given. I am always made to guard the sake storehouse, so I would drill a hole in a barrel, stick in a straw, and drink with a "chiu-chiu" sound like a mouse. And then I would stick a nail in the hole afterward. You have been in service with me for a long time, yet you are such a slow-witted fellow.'

Akisu: 'However, I always wondered why this Dannasama has made so much sake since long ago without selling it, and why he keeps sake stored in a twenty-house storehouse, but it seems he was preparing for a time like this after all. Is he not truly a great man?'

アキス『オイ、たつた一口と云ふたが、最前から随分沢山引つかけたぢやないか。まるで牛か象かが、雑水を呑むやうだつたよ』

カール『定まつた事だよ。五升徳利に一口ぢやほんの少々ながら、ごしよごしよとやつて見たら、俺の頬べたにほんのりと紅の花が咲きかけたのだ、エヘヘヘヘ』

アキス『オイ貴様は奥様から、余り円い顔をして居るので望月とか餅が好きだとか云はれたぢやないか。餅の好きな奴は酒を呑まぬものだが、貴様は山川道楽だな。俺は酒は一滴も呑まないと云ふて赤い顔をしたり、熟柿臭い息をしたりしたのは内証で呑んで居たのだな、たうとう化を現はしやがつたな、化虎め』

カール『定つた事だい。いつも酒倉の番許りやらされて居るのだもの、樽に錐穴をあけ、麦藁を突つ込んで、チウチウと鼠鳴きをして居たのだ。そして其後へ釘を突つ込んで置くのだ。貴様は長らく俺と一緒の奉公しておきながらノロ作だなア』

アキス『併し此方の旦那様は、昔から酒許り沢山作つて売るでもなく、二十戸前の倉に酒を蓄へて居るのは不思議と思つて居たら、矢張斯う云ふ時の間に合さうと思つて準備して居たのだな。ほんとに偉い人ぢやないか』

Karl: 'However, Akisu, is it not the case that the Shiyōjiyō of the Forest of the Tenō have possessed Dannasama and Okusama? I was listening a little from the next room, and it is truly a fushigi matter. What do you think?'

Akisu: 'Well, would that be what is called the spirit of shiyōjiyōmuku? Whatever one may say, both Dannasama and Okusama pity the jinmin, so their uke among the satobito is good; when I think of why such jihi-deep people went to a hanarejima and suffered kurau for sannen, I wonder if there are no Kami and no Buddhas in this world. But well, well, they have returned, and not to mention Okusama, one cannot know how much the satobito of Suma will rejoice. Oh, how very noisy it has become toward the gate. Shall we go check it out?'

With that, he ran out to the omoteguchi.

Looking out, Teku was standing in the mannaka of a large crowd while shouldering an ōbishiyaku, dancing insanely by himself. The large crowd was chanting in accompaniment.

Teku: 'Ohhh, how mede it is! At this mansion, yoi-yoi, After sannen, the Goshiyujin Has made a medeta return. Daiichi of all, the joy of Okusama, Next, the young master and the bantō-sama, Beginning with Akisu and Karl, Yoi-yoi! In the village of Suma in the land of Izumi, Rauniyakunanniyo have gathered together, And the sake cellar of nijitsutomahe, Yoi-yoi, He opened up and threw open to everyone, Telling them to drink! How thick-hearted he is! Yoi-yoi! This Teku-sama, from today on, Is the bantō-sama of the Bāchiru Mansion! Having completely quit Being a spy for Brahmanism, I am the supervisor of the sake cellar! Everyone, please be spirited! Yoi-yoi! From now on, as long as Teku is here, The rice, barley, and Sake of this mansion, I shall give to everyone as much as desired! No matter how much treasure there might be, It is of no use if there is no life! Yoi-yoi! The master whom we thought dead Has medeta returned home, And as a sign of celebration to everyone, He treats us to sake! The tightly bound rice balls, Eat them until your stomachs swell And become gourds! Yoi-yoi! Truly medeta, how medeta! The Kami-sama of the Forest of the Tenō Has bestowed protection, and medeta, Brought the husband and wife face to face here, Causing them to shed plentiful Tears of joy—how thankful we are! Yoi-yoi! Thanks to that Kami-sama, Sweet sake such as we have never Tasted once since being born, Drink your fill, and courageously Dance, go wild, and leap, Is the direct divine command of the Kami! Could there be anything happier than this? Yoi-yoi! Bantō-sama Akisu and Karl Were originally small-hearted, And having such a vast amount of sake, While holding the position of bantō, Did not drink it themselves, nor did they Treat others to it, but left it abandoned— What divine retribution against those ignorant of grace! Yoi-yoi! Since Teku from now on Becomes the number one bantō of this house, I shall not do stingy things! Without distinction between high and low in the whole village, Harmonize, be friendly, and Thankful for the dew of the Kami-sama's grace, Receive plenty of sacred sake! Could there be anything as medeta as this? Yoi-yoi! Shiyōjiyōnohiko and Shiyōjiyōhime Have met through a fushigi fate, Borrowing the bodies of the master and mistress; The husband and wife of this mansion Have two feet and six hands— The couple combined have four feet, Clapping twelve hands, Before the Kami-sama of Mount Azumosu, How sublime they are, Uttering the great solemn prayer of thanksgiving! Yoi-yoi! The propagators of Anai-kyō— The propagator of the Eyeball Country, Kunibetsu, Masumihiko whose heart is pitch black, Itahiko whose head was struck, And Sanseihiko with three thousand barrels of sake, With his beautiful wife, Debisuhime— Whether she be Debisu or Ebisu, I know not, But the deity of good fortune, Daikoku-sama! This house that has suddenly sprung up And the people of the land of the sake izumi Must surely be rejoicing! Everyone, line up and clap your hands! Yoi-yoi! Teku-sama, whose drunkenness has circulated, Has become short of breath! Everyone, from now on, your hidden talents, Without hiding or concealing, let them out, And for today's medeta celebration, Please bring forth a bloom of flowers!'

'Yoi-yoi!'

With Itsushiyōkenmei, they lead the Ondō, and hundreds of Rauniyakunanniyo are holding hands and dancing wildly. To this place, Chiruteru of the Baramon army, who guards the Sekishiyo of Kiyo harbor, leads Sūjifunin of his Buka, straddles his horse, and advances slowly toward the Monnai. Standing on his horse, he shouts in a Daionjiyō:

Chiruteru: 'Hey, hey! I am Chiruteru, the captain guarding the Sekishiyo of the Baramon-kyō. According to a spy's Chiushin, the Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō, Tamakuniwake and Ika, are hiding in this Tauyakata, so I have come for their Meshitori. Hey, hey, Murabito, guide me to the Arika of the Sendenshi!'

Upon hearing this, Teku, Akisu, and Kāru fill a Hishiyaku to the brim with sake and thrust it before the Hanasaki of Chiruteru. As soon as Chiruteru hears the word sake, he cannot restrain himself, leaps nimbly from his horse, and begins to drink it down with a gulping sound, with the intensity of a Gaki drinking water. The Amata Buka who followed him also mingle with the Gunshiu and begin to drink greedily, not to be outdone. Forgetting their Kanjin Shimei, they each tie on headbands and, shaking their hips, become absorbed in dancing the suteteko. In the mid-air, the auspicious birds—the Kalavinka, the phoenix, and the peacock—dance wildly with their wings spread wide. Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haema Se.
