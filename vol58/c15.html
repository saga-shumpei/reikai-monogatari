Chapter 15: 哀別 [1490]

哀別〔1490〕

§ 1

The party of Tamakuniwake brought the Hatsuwakamaru to the shore of Shiyōjiyōjima, and when they looked closely, they saw a remarkably large Shiyōjiyō holding a child that could not be distinguished as either human or ape. Nearby stood a person with a shaggy beard, who was also indistinguishable as human or ape. Itahiko addressed the man:

Itahiko: 'Hey, are you a human standing there? If you are a human, please speak to me.'

Bachiru had not seen a human face or heard a human voice for three years, and in his nostalgia and joy, tears streamed down his face. Then,

Bachiru: 'Yes, I am a human. Please, I beg you to save me. I drifted ashore on this island three years ago, and I have been living a lonely life together with this group of Shiyōjiyō just like this.'

Itahiko: 'Well, that is a strange story; there must be a deep reason behind it. In any case, I would like you to tell us about it in detail. Now, Sensei, shall we land and take a look? It might even be an island of jewels, you never know.'

Tamakuni: 'Hmm, in any case, let us land and investigate the situation. Come, everyone, please go ashore.'

Saying this, he leaped and landed on the shore. Following him, the rest of the party, leaving the boatman behind, all came ashore, driven by curiosity. The Shiyōjiyō, seeing many imposing men approaching, became somewhat intimidated; the King of the Shiyōjiyō held the child and retreated seven or eight ken, tilting its head and pondering the situation. Many small apes gathered together, chattering and blinking their eyes, staring intently.

Tamakuni: 'Ah, where are you from? How is it that you have been living on such a remote island with Shiyōjiyō? Please tell me your story. I am a missionary of the Sangokyō, and my duty is to help people. I am certainly not a person for you to worry about. Please, feel at ease and tell me your story.'

Bachiru: 'Yes, thank you very much. I am a Shuda from the village of Suma in the land of Izumi, a farmer named Bachiru. Because I am very fond of fishing, I ventured out into the rough sea with my servant three years ago to fish far out in the lake, when we were suddenly caught in a storm. The ship was swallowed by the waves, and thanks to that, I reached this island and have been able to preserve my life until today, helped by the King of the Shiyōjiyō. My wife back home must be very worried about me. I beg you, please help me.'

Tamakuni: 'Indeed, that must have been a great hardship. Now that it has come to this, do not worry any longer. We shall rescue you and take you back on this ship.'

Bachiru: 'Yes, I humbly ask for your kindness. Since I have been on this island for three years, I have been helped by the Shiyōjiyō and have not lacked for food, but no matter what I say, the other party is a beast, and I have been troubled because we cannot understand each other's language.'

As he was speaking, the King of the Shiyōjiyō appeared on the scene holding the baby, pointing at the child and crying out 'kya-kya' in a way that could not be understood. Itahiko looked at the child intently and said,

Itahiko: 'Ah, this child is a hybrid of a human and an ape. My, what a strange thing. Tell me, Bachiru-sama, this is not the bond formed between you and the Shiyōjiyō-sama, is it?'

Bachiru: 'Yes, it is truly embarrassing to say, but thanks to becoming a husband to that King of the Shiyōjiyō, I have been able to preserve my life until today. A seed of karma was planted, and such a child was born. It is truly a troublesome matter. Although I say it was born from the womb of a beast, I truly feel a lingering attachment. However, I cannot take such a thing back with me, nor can I take the Shiyōjiyō wife back with me. Truly, even though they are beasts, they are kind. I want to return to my country more than anything, but you might laugh at me for saying this, but I truly feel sorry for that Shiyōjiyō.'

Tamakuni: 'I truly sympathize with you. This is a pitiful situation. If I take you back, your wife at home will surely be happy, but I can perceive the heart of your second wife, the Shiyōjiyōhime. I wonder if there is any way we can take her back with us.'

Bachiru: 'Yes, thank you, but the Shiyōjiyō will never leave this island. The reason so many Shiyōjiyō are able to live like that, without being swallowed by the Orochi that appear from time to time, is because they have that King. If I were to take that Shiyōjiyō back with me, I would have to let its Kenzoku face Migoroshi. Furthermore, the Shiyōjiyō would not follow us if it meant letting its own Kenzoku face Migoroshi, for they are truly affectionate Dōbutsu.'

Ita: 'If they have lived as Meoto for three Sannen, even if one calls them Chikushiyō, it is natural that such Miren would remain. Ah, the Shinrijiyōtai of Ningen is truly something unfathomable.'

Tamakuni: 'Whether it be Ningen or beasts, there is never any change in Aijiyō. Moreover, Ningen are the sort who will cast away their Niyōbau or abandon their husbands if they are even slightly displeased, but Chikushiyō are superior in that regard. Even among birds that fly in the sky, if one is taken by a person or dies, they will not take a second male or female, even for the sake of appearances. Thinking of this, Ningen seem to be inferior to birds and beasts.'

Ita: 'Indeed, it is admirable. Now, Michihiko-sama, you must take the words of Sensei to heart and never let Davis-hime go. Furthermore, even if your Oku-sama were to pass away, you must not take a second Oku-sama. And Oku-sama, you too; no matter what happens, you must never take a second husband or be unfaithful.'

Michi: 'Hahaha. Thank you for everything. I shall never do anything to go against your instructions, so please be at ease.'

Ita: 'I mean it, you must never take the words of Itahiko lightly. Oh my, I am truly shedding tears at this story.'

Tamakuni: 'Well, there is no use in grieving forever; in any case, Bachiru-sama, please board this ship. It would be best to return for now and put your Oku-sama at ease.'

Bachiru: 'Yes, thank you. I humbly ask for your favor.'

The small monkeys approached one after another while making chattering sounds. The three members of the Brahman group, Yakkosu, Hāru, and Sabōru, were running about wildly while chasing the group of small monkeys for amusement. In the meantime, the ship left the three behind and moved about seven or eight ken away from the shore. The King of the Shiyōjiyō stood on the shore, letting out a shrill cry, holding out its baby, and gazing at Bachiru's face while tears streamed down. Though it did not speak, it expressed through its countenance, 'Is this child not dear to you? Is it not cruel to abandon your wife and return?' and stamped its feet in frustration. From time to time, it attempted to threaten them by showing as if it would tear the small monkey apart from its crotch. However, everyone hardened their hearts, and as it was a situation where there was no other choice, they began to row the ship. The King of the Shiyōjiyō, before their very eyes, tightened its grip on its own child's throat to kill it, and having tied a heavy stone to a wisteria vine, threw itself into the sea with a splash.

Seeing this tragic scene, the party of Tamakuni-wake was overcome with tears of sorrow. The three, Yakkosu, Hāru, and Sabōru, were surprised to see the ship departing and rushed to the shore in a flurry.

Three: 'Hey! Hey! Wait, wait! Are we not still left here? Turn that ship around!'

They shouted while stamping their feet. Mēto and Daru stood at the bow, making strange gestures, jutting out their chins, and striking the air repeatedly with their fists.

Mēto and Daru: 'Ihihihi, ufufufu. Hey, you three villains, it seems you were plotting to capture all of us at the port of Kiyo, but the missionaries of the San-go-kyō are well aware of such things. That is why we are leaving you three behind here and returning. Well, you should become the King of Sarushima, become Meoto with the monkeys, and plan for the prosperity of your descendants. Farewell, you poor things, take your time, and goodbye.'

With all manner of mockery, he feigned ignorance of the three standing upon the shore, and catching the breeze that blew at that moment, he hoisted the sail and glided away toward the southwest.

The boatman began to sing in a cool voice while gently plying his oars.

Boatman: 'The boat rowing off the coast with a Yansa-monsa, / Why do you approach the shore when the courtesan beckons? / Yansa, even if the courtesan beckons, / Do not approach the shore, / Alas, the courtesan is a monster, a daytime fox, / Yansa-yo. / A trio of thieves, a trio of thieves, / Even if they beckon until their voices are hoarse, / Yansa, do not approach the shore. / They are thieves, daytime foxes. / If you arrive at the port of Kiyo, / Yansa, Ensa, / Yanya-no-ya. / Conspiring with the Metsuke wretches, / Leading hundreds of henchmen, / Yansa, korewai-sa. / The propagandists of Tamakuniwake, / And all the other living gods, / To capture them all in one net, / They wait with Tegusune drawn.

Will you fall for that trick? / Yansa, Ensa-no, / Enyara-ya. / Living in seclusion on the island of Shiyōjiyō, / Taking the Shiyōjiyōhime as your bride, / Giving birth to children, covered in hair, / Live your life crying Kya-tsu Kya-tsu. / Is this the Miseshime of this world? / Truly, you are a great fellow. / Become the king of the island of Shiyōjiyō, / And live a life of extraterritoriality, / Forever and ever. / This too is the rust upon your own body. / Though you were saved with such effort, / Yansa, Ensa, / Enyara-sa. / With evil schemes in the depths of your heart, / The Sendenshi who perceived them, / We know it all completely. / Truly, you are a pitiable one.

The moon shines, the cool breeze blows, / The waves are calm and rustling. / Pleasantly, joyfully, and purely, / Without arriving at the port of Kiyo, / To the shore of Suma in the land of Izumi, / We shall put the Metsuke of the Brahman religion to shame, / And give them a great surprise. / Ensa, Ensa-no, / Enyara-ya.'

With these words, waving his hands and feet to mock the three, he gradually drifted away toward the island.

Bachiru: 'How joyful it is today that the Savior God of the vast heavenly skies has descended. / Yet, how pitiful is the princess who has been my wife for three years. / The heartache of leaving to die the child of my blood that resided within the Shiyōjiyōhime. / To become husband and wife is a deep bond from a previous life, here upon the white waves. / This boat rowing over the white waves carries a hundred sorrows. / I long for the hearts of the three men left behind on the desolate island where no one visits. / Was it because they were tormented by the demons of their own hearts that they became such wretched beings?'

Masumihiko: 'The vast sky and the depths of the water are clear and serene, / Yet my heart is sorrowful. / As I witness the pitiful end of the Shiyōjiyō, / My tears are unbearable.'

Mēto: 'I am happy to leave the three villains on the island and return. / By now, Yakkosu must be weeping and collapsing on the shore, gazing at my boat.'

Daru: 'Everything is the seed sown by the sins of the heart, which must have sprouted on the island of Shiyōjiyō. / A minor fault would be one thing, but the sins of the crooked gods are truly terrifying.'

Mitsuhiko: 'The sorrow is such that even in the jar of tears, there is no way for Mitsuhiko to draw out the sadness of this day.'

Devisu-hime: 'The three ugly ministers must also be living in peace under the thick protection of the Imperial Gods.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Now that we have reached the water's edge of the shore of Suma, I shall use my own people to go and rescue them.'

As they each expressed their reflections, they glided right and left upon the lake, riding the current. A small island shadow, floating like a mist, caught their eyes far ahead. Iru, sharp-eyed, saw it and said,

Iru: 'Ah, it is finished; the boat has finally drifted into the sea of demons. If we go around that way, it will be a detour of thirty or forty ri, but once this lake has caught that current, we cannot change our direction. However, as long as there are no hidden reefs, there is rarely any danger. Everyone, please be at ease. It is quite a detour if you wish to arrive at the port of Suma, but please accept that this is the way things must be.'

Tamakuni: 'It must be the will of some god. Leave it to the waves and be very careful as you proceed. There might be some great prize waiting for us.'

Iru: 'Yes, thank you; I am relieved to hear that.'

Tying his headband, he commanded the eight sailors and began to row with all his might, stripped to the skin. The boat drifted on, lingeringly, at the mercy of the waves.
