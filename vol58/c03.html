Tamakuniwake of pure heart, Accompanied by two disciples, Masumihiko of the summer Misora, And Itahiko whose foot was injured, Even under the Amatsuhikage, To the front entrance of the rare hall of Terumon, Guarded smartly by the divine messenger, They step forward and enter inside. Surprised by the shouting voices of old and young, men and women, And the sound of arrow-yells, Michihiko who appeared At the entrance of the front gate, Met again his longed-for master And two friends; Overwhelmed with emotion from excess joy, He could do nothing but cry without any answer. Gradually recovering his composure, Quietly to an inner room, He leads and proceeds inside with the three, Beginning with Okunihime,

As well as Princess Devis and Princess Kerina, The priests of Brahmanism, Beginning with Captain Nicholas, Kiudaukoji and Hel the priest, Maris, Rebena, Luikin, Polito, Together with four or five servants beginning with Bat, Wait in attendance. As soon as Okunihime sees the figures of the three, Rejoicing, She steps down from her seat, supports her hands on the floor, And says: 'Welcome indeed have you come. You are Michihiko the Sendenshi, the master of the savior Kami. Well, well, please come this way.' Tamakuniwake nods in greeting, And obeying her words, Takes the prepared seat.

Okunihime: 'This place is the divine hall of Brahmanism, the birthplace of Ōkuronushi-sama. We husband and wife who guard this hall were visited by various kami of calamity, and in the very midst of our distress and agony, your disciple Michihiko-sama arrived and rescued us all from our hardship. For his courage and chivalrous spirit, we are shedding tears of gratitude. Please do not abandon us hereafter and grant us your favor. Also, this Kiudaukoji was formerly a colonel of Brahmanism; moved by the words of Chikunikawake-sama, he became a monk, and on his way to open the path of the Anai teachings, he was our benefactor who saved my two daughters from danger. I have no words to express my thanks.'

Tamakuni: 'Well, well, you have received wonderful divine virtue. I offer my congratulations. And are you Kiudaukoji-sama? How well you have entered the faith.'

Kiudau: 'Yes, I am a colonel of the Brahman army named Emishi. Following Generals Kishunwake and Kumehiko, I marched as far as the Forest of Uki-ki, and due to the defeat of our allies at Kajika Pass, I retreated to the country of Biku. Departing from here again, I constructed a fortress in the cave of Mount Ikura, boasting of its impregnability, when Chikunikawake-sama arrived and admonished me in the way of the Kami. Thereupon I suddenly realized the truth, and now I have become a believer of the Anai teachings, allowed to serve in the divine work. I, an unworthy person, beg for your deep guidance.'

Tamakuni: 'Let us pull each other's hands and allow ourselves to be of service in the divine duty.'

Kiudau: 'The seven gentlemen visible here are Captain Nicholas of the Brahman army, and the other six are all non-commissioned officers. To investigate the change of mind of General Kishunwake and the subsequent conditions, they came to this hall just a short while ago, and upon hearing our talk, they have finally agreed. We earnestly beg for your guidance.'

Tamakuni: 'By all means, let us ask for each other's favor. Ah, are you Nicholas-sama? Ah, gentlemen, I meet you all for the first time. In the world there are neither enemies nor allies. We human beings who are nurtured by the same Kami must be on good terms with one another.'

Nicholas and the other six bow deeply,

Nicholas and others: 'We beg for your favor by all means.'

Michihiko stood up briskly and went out to the front gate to see what was the matter. Smart is solemnly guarding the gate. You have come all this way to the Kamuyakata, and by saving the house from its Kinan, clearing away the Kurokumo of this mansion, and restoring it to Tenkataihei, you have earned the gratitude not only of all in Miyamachi, but of all the Kokumin, which is beyond words. Michihiko: 'Ah, that is well. Has the misunderstanding regarding us been resolved? From now on, I pray that we may join hands, and for the sake of the mansion and the country, cooperate in unity and dedicate our sincerity. However, if so many of you enter the mansion, it may cause trouble for Oguniwake-sama, who is currently ill, so let us meet at the riding ground in front of the gate.'

He led Smart out of the gate, descended the stairs, and stepped out onto the green grass of the riding ground, where he saw fifty soldiers standing in orderly ranks, guarding Wax and the other four who remained bound. It appeared that the military side and the Akuyōkai side had somehow made peace, as they were smiling at each other. Tanku climbed onto his donkey again, stopped his horse in front of Wax and the other three, and began to deliver a speech in a loud voice. Michihiko-sama sat down on the green grass, which was filled with delicate red, white, yellow, and purple flowers, and while stroking Smart's head, he waited with a smile.

Tanku: 'The townspeople of this Miyamachi, / Have guarded the sacred ground of Mount Terumon, / Firm and everlasting, / And to repay the blessings of the gods of heaven and earth, / We only strive with all our might / In our sincere devotion. / The ignorant townspeople, / Were caught in the trap planned by Wax, / The son of the house steward, / And how foolish it was to raise their blades / Against the god-man of Shinrikimusau. / Wax, skilled in evil, / To cover his own crimes, / Devised a scheme here, / Organizing the Akuyōkai, / Claiming to crush the weak and help the strong, / And forming a group of over several hundred people, / To torment the true god-man, / And plotting to occupy the Kamuyakata, / Such an evil scheme is truly terrifying.'

Tanku: 'Heavenly punishment has struck immediately, / And by the very Kuwaiin he led, / He has been bound hand and foot, / Exposing his shame for all ages / Here on this riding ground, which is truly laughable. / Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, / How long would the true God / Allow such evil schemes to persist? / We are men called Tanku, / Chosen by six hundred townspeople / To be their leader, / And now, having been made the head of the Akuyōkai, / Representing the townspeople, / We have come before the divine messenger of Ananai, / To manifest our sincere hearts, / And to serve for the sake of the country, / Leading the masses, we have come.'
