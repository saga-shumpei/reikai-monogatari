Chapter 11: 欵乃 [1486]

欵乃〔1486〕

§ 1

At last, the moon sank into the waves of the west, the stars hid themselves one by one, and a sharp light began to shine from the waves of the east. The distant sky, where one could not distinguish between cloud and wave, or heaven and sea, gradually turned crimson, and the voices of the hundred birds crossing over the lake were heard across the entire space above, as if a band of thousands were playing music all at once. The shadows of the water birds, fluttering their white, blue, black, green, yellow, and red wings as they flew to and fro, were truly a magnificent sight.

Tamakuniwake appeared at the gunwale, clapped his hands toward the eastern sky, offered the Amatsunorito, and prayed for the safety of their voyage. Following this, Michihiko and all the others imitated Tamakuniwake and bowed toward the east. The huge sun with its clear outline began to peek out from within the lake water. The sea on a summer morning is something exceptionally refreshing.

Tamakuniwake: 'The God of the Sun, who floats, parting the curtains that bind heaven and earth, whether they be clouds or waves. I pray with a pure heart that this sea voyage may also be fortunate today. The hundred birds, gliding over the waves, seem to be guarding the ship upon which I ride.'

Michihiko: 'The summit of Mount Terumon, faintly floating upon the waves, grows bright.'

Masumihiko: 'Even the lake, where nothing else enters my eyes, shows only the form of Mount Terumon. Looking up at Mount Terumon, I wonder how Kerinahime-sama is faring.'

Itahiko: 'Once again, you are only concerned with the princess; the heart of a teacher is a strange thing.'

Masumihiko: 'A sincere heart that thinks of a delicate woman is what makes a man among men.'

Itahiko: 'You, who take the worldly proverb that "nothing goes right without a woman" so literally. If only women were to exist in this world, how could the space between heaven and earth ever flourish?'

Michihiko: 'Could it be that a brave man's deep-seated sincerity would not reach a woman's heart?'

Itahiko: 'You are truly infatuated, Michihiko; the very position of your eyes and nose has changed.'

Debisuhime: 'Mixing among the brave men, I follow behind, suffering in my heart all alone. No matter how much strength men may possess, it would be hard to contend with the eyes of a woman. If I but shoot a clear glance from my eyes, the spring ice cannot withstand it for even a moment.'

Masumihiko: 'Pressed by the love of you two, my words have failed me. On this ship where I am so astonished I cannot even speak, I feel for the heart of Shio-Michihiko.'

Yakkosu: 'I envy you all, who can exchange such carefree words and laugh. It is sad to be one who commits evil while fearing the demon in one's heart morning and evening. There was never a time in my life as a thief when I could spend even a single day with a peaceful heart. However, since I heard the teachings of God, my heart has become peaceful.'

Hāru: 'I, too, feel as though the demon in my heart has fled somewhere. Is there any fool in the world who does as much evil as I did, living in constant fear whether sleeping or waking? Now, thanks to the grace of God, I have come to realize the taste of the true path.'

Sabōru: 'I am Sabōru, who spent my days being mocked by the people of the world as a sluggard. With the moon shining in the sky of my heart, even the curved spirit has cleared, and my own body has become broad and at peace.'

Mēto: 'Before I could even think of whether I was making a terrifying journey, I was saved. Saved by God from my path to the underworld, from now on I shall raise my meter on the path of truth.'

Daru: 'My hands and feet have withered and decayed, and I, Daru, have been sustaining my meager life on crabs and shellfish. Having been given delicious bread for the first time in a long while, we starving ghosts have been revived.'

Tamakuniwake: 'The grace of the God of heaven and earth is like the vast ocean, the depths of which are unknown beneath the wide waves. Come then, let us steady the horses of our hearts and proceed toward the land of God.'

Debisuhime stood at the gunwale, gazed at the scenery of the lake surface, and began to sing in a low voice.

Debisuhime: 'Looking up at the eternal heavenly sky, and gazing out at the vast ocean, when I look well, the works of the God of heaven and earth are revealed before my eyes. Oh, how noble, how awe-inspiring! Oh, how clear, the light of the heavenly sun rises, illuminating the sea, and bestowing its radiance upon the four corners of heaven and earth. It dwells even in the dew on the single leaves of the hundred grasses and trees. The great majesty has calmed the waves of the four seas, and the green of the evergreen pines grows straight and lush. When spring comes, a hundred flowers and a thousand blossoms bloom and fragrance, and the land of God opens and advances before my eyes. How brave is the ship rowing through the waves.'

§ 2

Growing tall on the opposite shore, the messenger of the God of reconciliation, the Sendenshi, prays that Masaki may prevail. Though the morning sun may shine or cloud over, though the moon may wax or wane, though the stars may fall from the sky, the path of the Ananahi Oshihe of sincerity alone is the way to save the world. Leaving behind the presence of my father and the side of my mother at the Kamuyakata of Okuniwake on Mount Terumon, crossing over the sea of a thousand ri, and setting out on a journey of ten thousand ri, I, though a woman, follow my brother Michihiko, who is a man among men, a Kamutsukasa of Tamakuniwake, with a heart clear as the sky, and with joy in my breast, I ascend the path of the Seito.

How joyful is my lot! The Ōmikami Kuniharutachi, Toyokuninushi, the Kamubashira, and Kamususanō, who created this world, have manifested in the sacred ground of the precious land of Caucasus, in the hall of the Iso of Mizumitama. They distribute their holy hearts to save and assist all things under heaven, intending to bestow the infinite Kuwanki of the age of Kami. They appoint a hundred officials and strive to rule the eighty islands of the Ashihara no Nakatsukuni and the eighty lands forever and ever, manifesting their hearts of benevolence. How awe-inspiring, how they strive! How awe-inspiring! Ah, Kannagara Kami-Kami.

May your spirits be blessed. Ah, Kannagara Kami-Kami. I pray for the divine favor of the spirits.

The boatman Iru, while plying his oar, began to sing in a cool, clear voice.

Iru: 'Departing from the shore of Kitami, I head toward the south. The blowing wind is gentle, washing my sweat-stained face. Is this the Pure Land or Heaven? The waves are calm and peaceful. The bottom of the boat beats a drum in silence. Emerging from the waves and entering the waves, the light of the sun and moon is pure. Without distinction between night and day, the surface of the lake casts its light. This boat, traveling through the cloudy sky, crosses the heavenly riverbed. It is the boat of the Kami of Menashikatama. Birds chirp happily in the mid-air. The waves beat like drums, don-don. Floating the boat on the heavenly riverbed, Tanabatatsume-hime rows across. Here is the surface of the waves of Ryūgū.'

Iru: 'Otohime-sama must surely be watching this boat heading toward the sky, looking on with a smile. Crossing the sea of a thousand fathoms, is this the heavenly sky or the mid-air? The sun, moon, and stars shine below. The moon shines between the waves, and the shadows of the stars lie on the bottom of the sea. The heavenly riverbed lies across. The boat of jewels rows across the sky. No matter how the rough waves may rage, this boat under the protection of the Kami is a boat of salvation of the Itsunomitama, a boat of protection of the Mizunomitama. Crossing over the waves of a thousand ri, with a peaceful heart, we go to the port of Kiyo, to a land where the water is purple, the fields are green, and the flowers bloom and scent the air.'

He continued to row and advance, singing in a smooth voice with a tone accustomed to the sea.
