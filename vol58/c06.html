Chapter 6: 茶袋 [1481]

茶袋〔1481〕

§ 1

Michihiko takes the lead, descending the mountainside of Mount Terumon toward the south. It is a relatively steep slope, and he cannot take his eyes off his footing for even a moment. Relying on his Kongauzue, he concentrates the weight of his entire body onto the tips of his fingers, descending while keeping a steady rhythm.

Michihiko: 'As a Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, I fear no enemy, yet this slope, steep as a standing board, is slippery and sliding, which is truly vexing. Tamakuniwake-sama, my teacher, and you, Princess Debisu, please take care and do not fall. I am a Sendenshi of peerless divine power, heading toward the Moon Capital, but if I were to slip and fall at the very start of this journey, it would weigh heavily upon our future. Though I do not carry a Gohei, today is a vital departure, a journey that feels no different from a battlefield. Without looking up at the Amatsumisora, I fix my eyes upon my toes for a while, keeping my focus—Untokodotsukoi, zūzūzū.'

No sooner had he spoken than his feet slipped, and he landed with a thud on his backside. This fall, as if to celebrate our future, was protected by the Mizu-no-Mitama of the full moon shining in the sky. Believing our future to be auspicious, I see it anew and declare it anew with Naohi. My legs tremble as I descend the slope, laughing at my own shins in a comical way. The bravery of today's departure—whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, whether the cicadas cry in the treetops, or however much sweat may pour down, even if the Lord Sun shines down with scorching heat from above, this body is entrusted to the Gods. My soul, harder than rock, is like the lush growth upon the rock of the Tokiwa pine.

Untokodotsukoi, dotsukoishiyo. What a slippery slope this is. The red clay is polished and gleaming, and because it is damp, it is all the more slippery. I do not know who passed this way before, but it has been polished to a remarkable degree. Even with such grace, looking at how they descend while keeping their bodies tilted and moving with a clatter, as if on four legs, it is a truly difficult slope. Beyond this lies the lake, famous in the legends of Terumon. We, the party of Sendenshi, must cross this lake, or we shall never reach the Moon Kingdom. Untokodotsukoi, zūzūzū. Truly, a dangerous, slippery slope.

It is a footing like rubbing a bald head with a gourd. It is a terrifying path where one careless step leads to a fall, leaving the divine boat of the Tengu mask or Otafuku exposed to the elements. Everyone, please take care. Untokodotsukoi, dotsukoishiyo. It seems the slope has become gentler. We must not let our guard down here. This is a place where the evil spirits of Brahmanism seem to be lying in wait, threatening the future of our party. No matter how many enemies may come, now that I have confidence in my own strength, my Yamato-damashii shall never falter. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, I descend according to the will of the Gods.'

Masumihiko also sings.

'The southern slope of Mount Terumon, a path as slippery as an eel. Michihiko-sama takes the lead, calling out the names of our teacher and the young grass, Princess Debisu, the wife of the Mikoto. Why is it that he does not call out the names of Masumihiko or Itakō? You are truly distant. Calling the name of our teacher, Tamakuniwake-sama, and offering kind warnings was but a facade; in the depths of your heart, you are surely worried about the well-being of Princess Debisu, who is on her honeymoon, and you called out our teacher's name out of duty or perhaps jealousy. Ahahahaha-tsuha, ahahahaha. You are a man of extreme pragmatism.

Because of this, if a young man takes a wife while on a mission of propagation, his soul becomes scattered and he cannot fulfill his true purpose. He becomes distracted by a woman and may neglect his vital, essential mission. However, Michihiko, as a messenger of the Gods, is an exception. Though there is no need to worry, he was distracted by a woman first, his soul scattered into the Chūu, and he failed to keep his eyes on his essential footing, slipping and sliding two or three times. If Michihiko, our guide who stands at the very front, slips and falls, there must be some reason why we are able to descend this slope safely.'

§ 2

Reflect upon this, Michihiko.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara.

I shall take care in place of Kami.

Itahiko sings again.

Itahiko: 'Untokodotsukoi, dotsukoishiyo. Even if Akuma appear or flee, and even if Tsukimono all run out, once one is on this Sakamichi, the body inevitably grows weary. Nevertheless, Michihiko, who is accompanied by his beloved Niyōbau, must be having a truly joyful time. His Genki is a hundred times greater than usual, and with high spirits, he squares his shoulders. He treads the Rokupau and walks the Sakamichi just like a Yatsukosu. Enyara, enyara, enyara. Is that style a Kamakiri? Or perhaps the Teodori of a frog? If that is wrong, it is like the Steteko dance of a Yamazaru. It is a truly strange style. Ahahahaha, zuuzuu zuuzuu. Oops, dotsukoi, I slipped my foot.'

He struck his buttocks with a thud. Thanks to the Okage of Kami, the Tengu mask and the Chiyabukuro are safe. The two balls flew up completely into the stomach and went on a Daikifukau to Yōkau. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. It is a steep slope where one's Medama might pop out. Though I am not a Zugani, I walk sideways; I cannot walk straight forward. Michihiko-sama and his wife may not care if they stumble or fall, but my teacher, Masumihiko-dono, please take care and descend this slope safely. The voice of the cicadas chirping in the summer trees sounds sleepy, singing as if in their sleep. Is it really such a cheerful matter? This Sakamichi is a matter of life and death.

How could one sleep through this? The calves of my legs have become swollen like sake bottles. Zuuzuu zuuzuu, ouch, ouch. I have learned for the first time that descending a slope requires one to keep one's mouth shut and look downward. Because Michihiko-tsukasa took the lead and spoke such trivial songs, both Masumi-no-hiko and Itahiko were lured into speaking carelessly to pass the time, but that became the cause of my own misfortune, and I struck my big buttocks. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. If I had known it would come to this, I should have attached a copper washbasin to my rear, just as when Wax-sama received a flogging at the riding ground. It is no wonder people say that the knowledge of a low-ranking person comes after the fact. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. Now then, Davis-no-hime-sama, you too should join us. While descending this slope, sing a song of the downward slope. Do not just cater to the mood of Michihiko-sama; comfort the hearts of our party a little. Display the true nature of the Goddess of Peace, Kamunagara. Itahiko-tsukasa, the messenger of Kami, requests it. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. May the spirits be blessed.'

Davis-hime: 'I am Davis-hime, who has Michihiko-sendenshi as a husband. I have heard that for one who serves the path of Kami, having a spouse makes it impossible to perform the essential duties. Although it is said so, I am a person who has had my life-thread saved by a divine official of great benevolence. Remembering that bravery as he advanced into the midst of an enemy army like clouds and mist, crossing the wilderness where Akuma rages, how could I, though a woman, remain still and stay in the mansion? A wife should follow her husband, exert her strength, and protect his body. When a crisis arises, I am prepared to stake my life to ensure my husband fully accomplishes his mission.'

'I have resolved that I must fulfill the way of a woman. Looking back at my comfortable mansion from afar, I set out on a journey with a pillow I am not accustomed to, prepared for hardship. How could such a thing be possible for a delicate woman, as if it were a lighthearted whim? There is a limit to your teasing. My heart is serious. Whether people laugh or ridicule, once I have entrusted both my soul and body to my husband, I will never falter halfway. Though I am a woman, I will achieve merits not inferior to yours and show them to you, so that you may call it magnificent. Masumi-no-hiko-no-kami-tsukasa, teacher of Tamakuniwake.'

'Itahiko-tsukasa, and all of you, please realize the purity of my heart with sincerity. It is never for the sake of passion or love that I, a sendenshi who has formed a chrysanthemum stone, would fall for Michihiko-sama. No matter how ugly his face may be, his essential soul is a hundred times more beautiful than the figure of the three-five moon. It is reflected in the mirror of the heart. My sincere heart, which has solidified into the journey of today, thinking only of serving the world and serving Kami by joining forces with such a brave warrior, is not something to be laughed at. Even though I am a delicate woman, please forgive me to the end.'

'Please lead us, our couple, and let us advance, Kamunagara. I swear my sincerity before Kami and make this confession. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. May the spirits be blessed.'

Having finished singing, they descended the slope, which was surprisingly lively, laughing and enjoying themselves, half in jest. At last, they crossed the steep three-ri slope and arrived at the shore of Lake Telmon. The sweat wrung out on the slope was blown away by the cool breeze blowing over the lake surface, and they drifted into a feeling of indescribable refreshment. The great lake, one hundred ri from east to west and two hundred ri from north to south, was filled with waves like the scales of gold and silver fish, lying quietly and vast.
