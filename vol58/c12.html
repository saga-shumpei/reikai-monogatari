Chapter 12: 素破抜 [1487]

素破抜〔1487〕

§ 1

A sense of boredom began to drift across the two-hundred-ri sea route from north to south, and various idle conversations began to unfold. Here and there, people were punctuating their yawns or marking time with farts, laughing and carrying on in a senseless manner. To console the tedium of the ship, Daru began to sway his bone-and-skin, ghost-like body back and forth and side to side, reeling off a string of nonsensical gibberish.

原文（近代文語）

南北二百里の航路にはそろそろ退屈気分が漂ひ、いろいろと腮を解く雑談が初まつて来た。彼方にも此方にも欠伸に節をつけたり放屁を刻んだり、他愛もなく笑ひ狂ふて居る。ダルは船中の無聊を慰むる為め、骨と皮との餓鬼のやうな体を前後左右に揺り阿保陀羅文句を並べだした。

§ 3

Having been exposed by Daru, Yakkosu and the other two changed their ganshiyoku, breathing heavily with their shoulders rising and falling, and sat huddled in the funazoko.

Itahiko stepped forward in front of the sannin.

Itahiko: 'Oi, Yakkosu, why are you three looking so depressed? Are you feeling ill from that kotsukei-mixed ahodara? Who would really do such a thing? Even if you were mistakenly the akunin that Daru described, I would not do anything cruel. If I were to do such a thing, I would have left you behind on the island of sin. We take a shinshiteki taido. We will never do anything hikefu. Rather than that, why don't you sing a song and cheer yourself up? Even Tamakuniwake-sensei is not the type of person to be concerned about such things at all.'

Yakkosu: 'Hai, arigata-gozaimasu. Hearing that, I feel relieved. That fellow Daru hates me to such an extent that he makes such zangen. Dōzo, I beg for your gosuiriyō.'

Itahiko: 'Yoshi, yoshi. Everyone knows that there are no blind hearts here, and we understand everything down to the very bottom of the belly. Even if, hypothetically, you were plotting what Daru said, there is not hitori who would be startled by such a thing, so you had better be at anshin. Why don't you sing a song to console the bureu of the senchiu?'

Yakkosu: 'Hai, arigata-gozaimasu. In that case, I shall take you at your kotoba, and since it is a long voyage, I will sing despite my lack of skill.'

Itahiko: 'Yoshi, yoshi. It does not matter what it is; just cheer up and sing for me.'

Yakkosu: 'Hai, arigata. Gomen-kudasai.'

Saying this, he walked straight up to Daru, grabbed both of Daru's ears with his hands, thrust his face forward, and began to recite an apologetic ahodara-kyo, jerking his neck with every line he sang.
