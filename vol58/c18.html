Chapter 18: Ship 待 [1493]

船待〔1493〕

§ 1

There were two men who had set up an agura on the aoshibagusa of the Hamabe in Suma, and were indulging in zatsudan while gazing at the waves of the kosui and yawning. These two men were Akis and Karl, servants of the house of Bachil.

Akis: 'Hey, Karl, for three days from yesterday to today, we have been gazing only at the surface of the lake like a kagashi or something, with our hohobeta bitten by buto, and isn't this waiting getting to be a bit much? It is as if I am dreaming. I am already sick of this. I am completely fed up, Akis.'

Karl: 'No matter what you say, the master has been missing for three years, and we do not know when he will return, or if he will return at all. Even if there is a mistress, if the master is not there, it is just as you say, Akis. But well, since no one is targeting the mistress, it is ketsukō. Lately, the mistress has been in a state of kōfun, and because she says with her tail on fire that the master is coming back and that we must go to meet him, we have come here, but I wonder if the mistress has seen a dream. They were truly a couple with a good relationship, but ah, when one becomes a yamomedori, one feels lonely for no reason, and I wonder if her mind has become a little strange. After the master went fishing and did not show his face even after ten or twenty days, taking the head clerk Anchi with him, the mistress finally held a solemn funeral on the day he left, and even went so far as to build a stone monument, so I thought she had concluded that the master would never return, but lately she is all excited, saying the master is coming back, the master is coming back, and it is truly troublesome.'

Akis: 'Even so, it has been three years this year, and it is truly admirable that she has not taken a new husband and remains chaste and virtuous. No matter what, she is the wealthiest person in Suma, so many men approached her out of lust and greed, and in the beginning, there were many sinful fellows who came and went, saying she must be lonely or offering to look after the house, but everyone was sent packing, and it is a stroke of luck that lately, they have concluded it is no use and we do not even see the shadow of a wicked man. However, is she not extremely nervous? When the memorial service for the first anniversary was held, we also performed the rites in front of the master's stone monument, and when the mistress cried with all her might and pleaded, even we who were by her side shed tears. I was impressed by what the affection of a married couple is like. She is truly a chaste woman.'

Karl: 'Yes, that is right, I also shed tears at that time. The third-year memorial service is coming up, but I hope there will not be a farce like that time again. When the mistress was pleading with all her might to the stone monument, saying, "My dear husband, you abandoned your beloved wife and child, and even though I tried to dissuade you, you would not listen, and you went on killing living things with the head clerk without learning your lesson. Is it divine punishment, or what should I call it, but in the end, did you not have your precious life taken by the fish? If you have a spirit, please show me some proof," and she cried without caring who was watching, it was truly painful for those of us by her side. Then, the master's stone monument began to rattle. I was so surprised that my soul felt as if it were flying into the void. Even so, the mistress was calm and composed, and when she cried and pleaded, "My dear husband, it seems you are thinking of me even after becoming a stone monument; the fact that the stone monument just moved must mean that your spirit is present. Please, say one word to your wife. I beg you," it was truly pitiful and unbearable, was it not? Thinking about it later, the stone monument moved because there was an earthquake at that very moment. At that time, both the house of Keru and the house of Taku-kō were smashed to pieces, were they not? At that time, I thought the master's stone monument had really moved, and I do not know how much I was terrified. Thinking about it later, it was truly a farce.'

カール『うん、さうさう、俺もその時には本当に涙が零れたよ。やがて三年祭が出て来るのだが、あの時の様な滑稽は今度はあるまいね。奥様が一生懸命に石塔に向つて仰有るのには、「もし、旦那様、貴方は可愛い妻子を振り棄てて私がお諫め申すのもお聞き遊ばさず、番頭と一緒に性凝りもなく殺生をしておいで遊ばした。その天罰と申しますか、何と申すか知りませぬが到頭貴方は魚の為に尊い命を奪られたぢやありませぬか。もし貴方に霊があるのなれば何とか証を見せて下さい」とそれはそれは人目も憚らずお泣き遊ばした時は本当に側に居るのも苦しい様だつた。そした所、旦那様の石塔がガタガタと動き出した。俺は吃驚して魂が宙を飛ぶ様になつて居た。それでも奥様は泰然自若たるもので、「旦那様、貴方は石塔になつてからも私の事を思ふて下さいますと見えまして、只今石塔がお動き遊ばしたのは、全く性念がお在りになるので厶いませう。何卒一言女房と云つて下さい。お願ひで厶います」と泣いて口説かれた時は本当に気の毒で堪らなかつたぢやないか。後から考へて見れば石塔が動いたのは丁度その時、地震があつたのだ。あの時、ケルの家もタク公の家もメチヤメチヤに壊されて了つたぢやないか。その時は本当に旦那様の石塔が動いたのかと思つて、どれ丈け俺は肝を潰したか分らなかつたよ。後で思つて見れば実に滑稽だつたな』

Akisu: 'Yes, that is what happened. However, it is too pitiful, and since one cannot say that it was the Jishin that caused it to move, and Okusama believes with all her heart that the Sekitafu moved because it felt her sincerity, she is truly a pitiable person. But I wonder, will Dannasama really return? Since about sunset yesterday, seven or eight Kaizokusen passed by in the distance and went to the port of Kiyo, and since then, not even a Giyosen has passed by, and no ship-like things have come here, have they? According to what Okusama says, she claims that he will return riding a magnificent ship and be sent off by many people, but is that not a story like trying to grasp a cloud? Being exposed to the sun in this Enten is unbearable. If there were even the shade of a tree, I could Shinbau, but to be on this beach where there is not even one tree as far as the eye can Miwata, that is enough for me, Akisu. Why don't we go somewhere like a friend's place, take a Kiusoku, and then return?'

Karu: 'Even if you say that, if by any chance Dannasama were to return, you would have to face a Taihen Medama. Since Okusama speaks so insistently as if she were a Kichigahi, I think there must be no Machigahi.'

'I also have a Kimochi that he might return, and a feeling that he might not, and I have come to have a strange feeling. I wonder if I might be losing my mind. It is Oka, as my head has started to feel like it is ringing because I have been exposed to the Enten too much.'

To such a place, a man came walking with a staggering gait, his face bright red, and said,

Man: 'Hey, damn it, why are you sitting here like a fart in this heat every single day, and what the hell are you doing? No matter how much you gaze at the lake, there is no fear of fish jumping in front of you. It is unlikely that a bird flying in the sky will fall flat in front of you. What is it, you idle fellow, just yawning every single day without any business, you are a dull guy. Lately, you seem to be a bit delirious. Because the mistress of your house says strange things, the Kichigahi has become contagious, and you come here every day like a scarecrow, what is it? Leave it, leave it, it is more interesting to come to my place and drink a cup of sake. This Teku-sama has been receiving generous compensation from the Brahman-sama from morning till night, searching for propagandists and believers of the Sangyōkyō. It is said that they will arrive at the port of Kiyo within today or tomorrow, so why don't you try to catch one of those guys? You can receive a wonderful reward and drink plenty of sweet sake. Won't you come out to my place, have a cup of sour sake, and do some work? It is not clever to be sitting here like a fart.'

Akisu: 'Hey, Teku, you are drunk on sake every single day, and when I wondered how you get that money, I see you are acting as a spy for the Brahman. Then it is no wonder you can drink spy-sake. However, one who is born a human should not do things like a dog.'

Teku: 'What, what do you mean by dog? Do not make a fool of me. If you say such things, how about I report you to the checkpoint, saying "With all due respect..." that you are a believer of the Sangyōkyō?'

Akisu: 'Hmph, am I the kind of guy who would take such a threat? My master is a magnificent believer of the Brahman religion. I am the one who is in service at that house. Since the propagandists of the Brahman religion always come to chant difficult sutras before the spirit of Dannasama, do you think they would take up such a thing even if you said it?'

Teku: 'Ah, that was a failure. Then it is no use. However, I will tell you this, but if by some turn of the wind a guy from the Sangyōkyō drifts to this rocky shore, come and secretly let me know. If you do that, I will receive a large reward and we can drink delicious sake to our heart's content, you see.'

Akisu: 'I dislike sake. I am a Geko by nature, after all. Even if I were to do things that people despise to earn money and drink sake, it would only rot my intestines and yield nothing, so I shall beg to be excused.'

Teku: 'Hmph, cut it out. You Tauhenboku, you have no taste for sake and a tongue like a herring roe, so there is no point in talking to you. How ridiculous; I shall wander along the beach, find a good bird, and earn some Sakate. You lot had better just stay there and dry the backs of your Sakegame at your leisure.'

Karu: 'Do not bother yourself with us. If I want money, I receive as much as I like from the mistress. My Kiyōgū is a bit different from yours, you see.'

Teku: 'W-w-what? You old Furugitsune borrowing the tiger's prestige, what does it matter how rich your Aruji is? You are a pathetic fellow, a Shiyuda who bows your head for five Gotobei of rice, being worked to the bone by your master while saying "Yes, yes, right away." I am an independent master of my own, supporting ten mouths every ten days. I have the skill to support such a Daikazoku, you know. Hmph, since my Mitama is manufactured differently, you had better not look down on me too much.'

Akisu: 'Hahahaha, a man living a single life claiming to support a Daikazoku of ten mouths—what kind of Baka are you spouting? I support a hundred mouths every hundred days. A family of a hundred is a much larger household than a family of ten. You sake-guzzler, leave this place. You smell like overripe persimmons and it is enough to bend my nose; if you were to open a greengrocer's shop, I could not bear it.'

Teku: 'Eh, hanging around with such a fool who understands nothing will not earn me a single coin. I, Teku-sama, shall go teku-teku and wander about here and there; though I am no dog, perhaps I shall stumble upon a stick. What a fellow who knows neither sake nor anything else.'

So saying, he spat out insults and went walking with unsteady feet, swaying and staggering along the beach toward the port of Kiyo.

Akisu and Karu watched the back of Teku as he departed, clapping their hands and laughing at the passing amusement.

Akisu: 'The stick-man Teku comes along, / With tongue rolling and slurring in a drunken stupor. / Staggering with a chidori-ashi along the beach, / He goes off hunting for a side dish for his sake.'

Karu: 'Whenever the stick-man Teku acts as a spy, / He drinks his sake with a "spy"-like air. / I, a Brahmin, mock him as a spy, / And he blusters about, letting out spy-farts.'

Akisu: 'My lord and master whom I wait for in vain does not return, / How shall I answer when I return home? / Waiting in vain for my lord who has no destination, / How pathetic I am, suffering in this heat.'

Karu: '"My lord will surely return," / So says the mouth of the mistress. / Even if she says he will return, / There is no shadow on the lake that looks ahead.'

Akisu: 'Today again, like an albatross waiting in vain, / My heart is drawn even as I flap my wings. / The two of us wait with our necks tilted like cormorants, / Hearing only the sound of the sea breeze.'

Just as they were singing thus, a small white sail appeared in the distance on the water, visible through the haze. Akisu clapped his hands in joy.

Akisu: 'How grateful I am, the white sail visible over there, / Must surely be the ship of my lord and master.'

Karu: 'Seeing a ship and assuming it is your lord, / That joy is but a bubble on the water.'

Akisu: 'For three days now I have not seen the figure of the ship, / Whatever the case, I yearn for him.'

Karu: 'Gazing at a hopeless ship, my lord, / How pathetic to think he will return.'

Akisu: 'Seeing my heart leap for some reason, / I am convinced it is my lord and master.'

Karu: 'If my lord were truly on that ship, / My fatigue would be healed at once. / Yet, like a man trying to catch stars with a pole, / How pitiful is the one who dreams such a hopeless dream.'

Akisu: 'For some reason, the shadow of the white sail is dear to me, / My heart leaps as I think of it.'

アキス『何故か白帆の影は懐しく 思はれにけり心勇みて』

§ 4

Kāru: 'This lake, where Maho and Kataho are raised and pass through, is heard to be a lake of demons beyond measure. It seems the ugly Magami is plotting, inferring our wavering hearts.'

Akisu: 'When I gaze upon the form of the ship I have waited for so long, half of my heart is set at ease. If my Aruji is not aboard, I shall give my head to you.'

Kāru: 'That is an Omoshiro and confident statement. Shall I receive your Jiyamakusa head?'

Akisu: 'This head is my treasure that I use for my Itsushiyō. Would there be any Baka who would hand it over so easily? Since I knew the ship my Aruji is boarding, I have made a firm vow.'

Kāru: 'If that is true, I too shall rejoice, clap my hands, and dance the Suzumeodo for you.'

Akisu: 'When I see the shadow of the ship drawing near, my heart becomes joyful and grows more so. I do not mean to say anything rigid, but surely my Aruji-no-kimi is aboard that ship. If this turns out to be wrong, I am prepared to give you my head as promised.'

Kāru: 'Truly, I am amazed by your strong Jigashin and cannot find words to say. If the Aruji is indeed on that ship, I shall pound Mochitsuki and hold an Ōiha.'

Akisu: 'I shall distribute sake, side dishes, and rice cakes throughout the Murajiu, and continue the celebration for Tōka or Hatsuka. The Okusama told me firmly that the face of the Aruji is like the god of the full moon.'

Thus, the two were driven by feelings of Hanshinhangi, gazing at the approaching ship, stretching their necks like cranes, and waiting impatiently. The shape of the Shiraho grew Shidaishidai, and they drew near enough to recognize even the shadow of the person standing at the prow with the Nikugan. The two held hands and danced around on the Isobata, their hearts leaping and dancing for no apparent reason. In the sky, a Kiyochō spread its wings in a straight line, cutting through the breeze, and was performing exercises like an automatic airplane, flying with great composure back and forth and left and right.
