Chapter 19: Dance 踏 [1494]

舞踏〔1494〕

§ 1

The Kokunetsu Taiyō shines down as if pouring fire from the Nauten. The Koshibagusa on the Hamabe of Suma, unable to endure the Shiyonetsu, thirsts, and every leaf curls tightly, becoming like needles. Akisu and Kāru, by the Meirei of Sāberu-hime, while bathing in the Enten sun, spend their time from morning until night gazing at the offing, waiting in a state of Yumeji for their master, whose whereabouts are unknown, to return.

A Shiraho appeared as a speck in the distant Okiahi. The Futari, wondering if this was indeed the ship of their Shiyujin returning or perhaps the Kaukaisen of a Tanin, waited with Hanshinhangi yet with a glimmer of hope. The Shiraho drew closer Kokukoku. The Futari held hands and danced on the Shibakusa of the Isobe, rejoicing at the Kikoku of their master, who was yet unknown to them. Perhaps it was a trick of the mind, but the Morodori flying Zujiyō seemed to join in their dance, as if celebrating the master's return, and they presented a Kuōkei of Jiyōgeitsuchi and Benbujiyakuyaku, fearing no one.

The Futari, covered in Asemidoro, panted for breath, and after resting for a Shibaraku, looked out at the offing. Suddenly, seven or eight ships appeared from the offing, raising sails stained with grime, and in the blink of an eye, they surrounded the Shiraho ship from front, back, left, and right. There were seven or eight brown sails and one white sail, and they began to maneuver on the calm lake like butterflies dancing, chasing and being chased by one another.

Akisu: 'Hey, Kāru, it is no use, it seems to be different again. That ship with the white sail seems to be carrying the missionaries of the San-go-kyō. And those ships with the grime-stained sails are the ships of the Brahman police. If it were the ship our Danna-sama was aboard, since he is a Brahman believer, they would hardly be chasing him. What do you think?'

Kāru: 'Does it not seem incomprehensible? Since we have gone to such lengths, why not call it a day and crawl into the shade of the trees on Mount Adsumosu to escape the heat? If we stay here forever, we shall end up suffering from heatstroke. Ah, look there, the white sail has disappeared, has it not? Surely it has not sunk. It is hot, so shall we not return to the shade of the trees on Mount Adsumosu?'

Akisu: 'Even so, I cannot disobey the master's orders. Do they not say, "If you extinguish your mind, even fire itself is cool"? Let us compose a poem here to refine our hearts and gather our spirits. As a set topic, let us start with a summer poem, and I shall begin.'

Akisu: 'Summer is / not a time for humans / to rest / rather it is a time / to work / even more vigorously / Behold / the sun shines in the heavens / the thunder roars / and the grass and trees / surrounding humanity / grow and flourish / abundantly at this time / Can humans alone / remain idle / and spend these good days / in sloth / indulging in summer retreats / while doing nothing? / The great ancestor Ōkami / from the beginning of creation / until today / has worked for us / without resting / for a single day / a single moment / or a single second / Truly / for those who perceive / this fierce / and supreme spiritual energy / flowing between heaven and earth / labor is / rather the supreme and only rest / Indeed / true rest / is like that of a fetus / communicating its breath / with the mother's body / through the umbilical cord / it is a life / that conforms to the spiritual movement / of the natural universe / and exists only within activity.'

Kāru: 'Indeed, that is interesting; summer, when all trees and grasses flourish so diligently, is a good season. Humans are troublesome creatures; when summer comes, they wish for winter to arrive, and when winter comes, they hope for summer to return. I, too, shall compose a summer poem, Summer days / are not hot at all / it is the fire of desire / for fame, profit, and physical pleasure / burning in our hearts / that is hot / again and again / The poisonous flames / of greed, anger, and ignorance / that burn in our hearts / from birth / are what are hot / By receiving / the cool breeze of the Holy Spirit / that blows / forever through the four seasons / the stubborn walls of the heart / that are tightly locked / and prevent the extinguishing / of those fires of desire / and poisonous flames / turn what should be a cool summer / into a burning hell'

遙の沖合に白帆が、ポツと目に映つた。二人はこれこそ主人の帰り来る船であらうか、但しは他人の航海船だらうかと、半信半疑ながらも稍望を属して居た。白帆は刻々に近より来る。二人は手を繋いで磯辺の芝草の上に、まだ分らぬ主人の帰国を、喜びながらダンスをやつて居る。心のせいか頭上に飛ぶ諸鳥も二人のダンスに和して、主人の帰国を祝する如く思はれ、上下一致抃舞雀躍の光景を誰憚らず呈して居る。

アキス『それでも主命に背く訳には行かない。「心頭を滅すれば火も亦自ら涼し」と云ふぢやないか。一つ此処で歌でも詠んで心を練り直し、元気をつけて見ようかい。先づ兼題として夏の歌で、先づ俺から初めて見よう』

I make it felt. I am but to advance into the spring of Tengoku, blown by the cool breeze of the Seirei.'

Akisu: 'Ahahaha, what a nostalgic song, truly fitting for summer. However, though you speak strong words with your mouth, when it is hot, it is indeed hot. This Shibusagusa seems to have finally wilted, and has it not shriveled up like an awl? It is as if, having rolled up the flags and sheathed the spears, pursued by the Ennetsugun, it has taken refuge in the Sansai. Truly, Sensei of the Natsugusa, I, Akisu, sympathize with you. I, too, feel as if a Kifuseitaikutsuen is about to break out suddenly. Well, to refresh my mood, I shall try composing a poem about autumn.

The cool autumn has come

And from nowhere

It is lonely

In the far and near mountains and fields of Yomo

The figure of Sahohime who has woven the brocade

Is shining with Manmoku Kuōeu

And the heart's horse

Is strangely Ochitsu

The crimson and Moegi colors are gorgeous

The maples

Display their beauty day and night

Resembling the drying of Banjiyō robes

In the deep mountains, the voice of the small stag

Calling its mate

Sounds like Kairō-rō, yet

There is a sorrowful tone somehow

The sound of the Sayuginuta also becomes sparse

Enduring the frosty night

The voice of the Hinadori singing

Drifts with golden waves

Boasting of the wealth of the imperial reign

It has become the harvest season

For the farmers who took up the hoes

Makes its light increasingly stronger

Its love and heat wane

The brilliance of the autumn frost and the sun of the solstice

Diagonally

Tormenting and exhausting the myriad trees and grasses

It is a force that will not rest

Ah, even if the plants and trees on earth

Are distanced from the heat

And harmed by the light

And wither and decay

Even if the many evening insects

Join their voices and lament the world

In a forlorn manner

We, who have been given the divine light

Filled with the Aizen and divine heat

And warmth of the noble Ōkami

To the fullest extent

Are what is called

The spiritual head of all things

Blooming and fragrant forever

We are the flowers of the heavenly realm and the earth

The living shrines of God

The predecessors of the Tenjin

Nay, we are the spiritual bodies of the Tenjin

And to us, who are the corresponding gods of the flesh of the natural world

There is no autumn

And winter does not come either

Only forever blooming and fragrant

With birds singing and butterflies dancing and playing

It is the spring day

And the time when all trees and grasses flourish and prosper

It is nothing but a heavenly summer

Therefore, for us

Neither autumn nor winter is a burden

As long as we are living spirits blessed with the inner-flowing divinity of the Lord God

We are forever and eternally

The flowers of Tengoku and the earth

The frost pillars that hold up swords and press upon all existence

And the terror of the cold moon shining in the sky

Seeing this tragic spectacle

I feel it as the skin

That contains the white, soft

Warm affection of the Tenjin

Also, the sound of the shrill winter wind blowing 'pyuu, pyuu'

I hear as the sound of the shō played by the Amatsuotome

Ah, how interesting

The spring of Tengoku

The autumn of the human world.'

Karu: 'Indeed, that is great horsepower. You speak sweet things. However, I wonder if you have that much resolve; it is a bit suspicious.'

Akisu: 'Because I am Akisu, I just tried composing an autumn poem for the time being. All poets draw fantasies and tell lies skillfully; even Tōchūken Kumoemon of 350,000 years in the future speaks of things like encouraging Bushido or rewarding good and punishing evil, but the reality is that he stole his master's wife and stands on the podium calmly, so the moralist teachers who have begun to raise their heads like bamboo shoots after the rain lately, and the missionaries of the Brahmins, are all just half-baked when you enter their inner side. Rather, I do not know how much more correct the secular people are. It is a dark world where hypocrites and villains are respected, so even I might be a fellow hypocrite if you tap the bottom of my stomach.'

Karu: 'Hmm, if so, am I also a hypocrite? Somehow, I have come to hate my own heart.'

Akisu: 'Hey, look at that; before we knew it, many ships have disappeared, and one ship is rowing busily toward us, is it not? That might be the ship of our master after all.

When I went out to the beach wondering if it would come

A ship that gladdens the heart is coming.'

Karu: 'A white sail is visible between the waves off the shore, yo

That is the ship where the master is. Ah, hey, hey.'

Suddenly recovering their energy, the two began to dance once again. The ship approached while rowing with eight oars, raising white waves at the bow, accompanied by the sound of a propaganda song.
