Chapter 1: Kami Wind [1476]

神風〔1476〕

§ 1

The summer breeze blowing over the green leaves, the pure Sendenshi Otohiko, the pure-hearted Tamakuniwaketsukasa, and Masumihiko, whose very sky is pure, accompanied by Itahiko-tsukasa. In the rice fields where the Inada rice plants sway, lotus flowers bloom in profusion here and there, and the scent, so fragrant, comes drifting, which is truly charming. The three traveled together, seeking the whereabouts of Michihiko-tsukasa, who served as a companion Kami, their waraji sandals dampened by the rice field paths. Leaping over this and that, they finally arrived at the foot of Mount Terumon. Looking up at the sky, on the mountainside of Mount Terumon, the purple Yahetanagumo clouds drifting across were perhaps a symbol of the gathering of Shinjin, or perhaps the appearance of Kami.

Be that as it may, it was the birthplace of Ōkuronushi of the Brahman. Thinking to stop by and explore the situation, they braced themselves and, leaving the plains behind, climbed the steep mountain path with a steady gait, gasping for breath, until they finally reached the banks of the Anburakku River. Here, too, was a plain; numerous rice fields spread out widely to the east and west, and lotus flowers here and there fluttered in the gentle breeze, giving the impression of advancing through the highlands of the Heavenly Pure Land. Here, the three Sendenshi crossed the Anburakku River, and as they finally arrived at the sacred ground, a war cry suddenly arose. Before they could even hear it, hundreds of rough men appeared, brandishing clubs and throwing stones, attacking the three. The scene was truly fearsome.

As the party of Tamakuniwake crossed the Anburakku River and advanced a few chō, the chairman of the Akuyōkai, Wax, led a multitude of ruffians and gave orders, waving his baton: Wax: 'The companions of the San-kyō sorcerer Michihiko have appeared. If we do not destroy him at this very moment, this sacred ground will be trampled by the San-kyō. Now is the time for one to face a thousand. Advance, advance!'

Ekisu and Heruman, who had been confined within a rocky cave, also mounted donkeys and deployed their allies on three sides, singing songs and surrounding the mere three enemies like a crane-wing formation, closing in from afar. Then, they grabbed small stones and threw them at each individual. The rain of stones fell upon the three as if it were pouring, and the danger was indescribable. The party of Tamakuniwake hid themselves in the shadow of a protruding rock, chanting the Amatsu-norito with all their might, and sang the Heavenly Kazauta in unison. While the group surrounded them from afar, they continued to rain stones down as before. Itahiko was struck by a sharp stone on his shin, and before he could even cry out, he collapsed on the spot. Seeing this state, the enemy gained even more momentum, throwing stones like rain and hail with all their might. Tamakuniwake remained calm and composed, continuously chanting the Kazauta. Masumihiko, while tending to Itahiko, hid Itahiko's body in the shadow of the rock.

The hundreds of rough men beat drums and rang cymbals, approaching little by little like a closing dragnet. Itahiko, in too much pain, grimaced and suffered without even being able to let out a voice. The enemy pressed in closer and closer. Even the steadfast Tamakuniwake found himself at an impasse, and leaving his fate to Heaven, he had Masumihiko carry Itahiko on his back. Risking danger, he resolved to break the enemy's heavy encirclement, open a bloody path, and escape, charging into the midst of the enemy like a wild boar. The few could not withstand the many, and in an instant, the three were beaten down.

Just then, the fierce dog Sumāto came flying through the air, and as soon as it barked twice or thrice, 'Woo, uwa, uwa,' the group, having had their courage stolen by Sumāto, cried out, 'These are formidable enemies!' and abandoned the three, rushing headlong toward the mansion to seek reinforcements from the soldiers.

Tamakuniwake expressed his gratitude to Sumāto, patting its head and stroking its back to thank it for its kindness. Sumāto wagged its tail happily, and upon seeing Itahiko's wound, it immediately crept up and licked the injured area. Instantly, the bleeding stopped, the pain vanished, and he was finally able to stand. From here, the three, together with Sumāto, headed toward the Kami mansion, climbing the steep slope while singing the Sendenshi song in high spirits.

Wakkusu: 'Gunjinsama of the Brahman army, I have a humble request to make. Just now, three magicians of Ananai-kyō have appeared once more. And since ferocious, mad dogs are running rampant in this sacred ground, I beg you to kindly use your might to make them retreat—... nay, arrange for their utter extermination. Not once or twice has a single magician of Ananai-kyō snuck into this Kamuyakata since days ago, committing all sorts of miscellaneous evil deeds and tormenting us Brahman believers. However, now that three magicians have crossed the Amuburakku River and come over here, there is no telling what kind of mischief they might pull off. Before things get out of hand, please annihilate them by the power of the military somehow. All of us members of the Akusuikuwaiin will follow behind you and render full support.'

The Brahman army sergeant Îru immediately answered with consent, and without even obtaining the consent of Nikorasu, he led fifty soldiers, descended Mount Terumon together with Wakkusu's troop, and headed toward the banks of the Amuburakku River.

Singing thus, they advance steadily toward the mansion as if walking through an uninhabited field.

They arrived at the bell tower situated at the intersection in Miyamachi. Suddenly, arrows were shot at them from the houses on both sides, and the three, finding their advance and retreat blocked, stood still in the street, singing the counting song with all their might. From ahead, Brahman soldiers advanced, handling long spears. From behind, a crowd of old and young, men and women, rushed forward, raising a battle cry with loud clamor. Sumāto roared fiercely, 'Uwatsu! Uwatsu!' His voice, like that of a wolf, shook the sliding doors and paralyzed the courage of everyone present, whereupon the arrows abruptly ceased. The battle cries also died away for a time. What the soldiers of the Brahman army were thinking, one could not tell, but they fled at top speed toward the training ground of the mansion.

Sumāto concealed his form once again, knowing not where. Tamakunigewake and his party pursued the rear of the Brahman army.

Wax again mounted his donkey, raised a loud voice, whipped and urged on the Akusui-kaiin, and came galloping after the three. A great collision began in front of the training ground, and finally, without distinction between friend and foe, they became hopelessly intermingled, resulting in a fierce battle between the Brahman army and the Akusui-kaiin led by Wax.

Suddenly, cries of agony and torment, the sound of swords and halberds, presented a miserable scene too painful to behold. Tamakunigewake, together with the two missionary envoys, leisurely passed through the front gate, leaving this conflict behind them as they advanced inward. Sumāto reappeared from somewhere and stood at the doorway, shouting at the top of his lungs, 'Uwatsu! Uwatsu!'
