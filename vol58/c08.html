Chapter 8: 孤 Island [1483]

孤島〔1483〕

§ 1

In the center of Terumon Kosui, there lies a single island drifting upon the waves. The entire island is encased in crags, where a small amount of Sasagusa grows here and there in patches, a desolate island of Tsumi. With a circumference of one Ichiri, the Isobata is always bitten by Aranami, filled with a truly Seisan spirit. This island is a Jigoku where Zainin are cast away to die, left without even Shiyokumotsu until their Inochi ends. The pitiful people who appear on this Onijima leap into the Sakama waves of the Isobata, gathering shellfish and catching crabs, barely sustaining their Romei, until they are reduced to skin and bone, looking exactly like Gaki.

At that moment, four or five Yūshi of the Brahmin, rowing their boat, passed by the nearby offing. Suddenly, an Arakaze blew, and in a Muzan turn of events, the boat struck an Anshō, and the Sentai was instantly Uchikuda into Kotsupamijin. In their sorrow, the five men swam until they finally reached the Tsumi island as their Meate, while two were swallowed by the water and disappeared into the unknown. The three who remained were Haru, Yakkosu, and Saboru, rough-and-tumble ruffians who were famous even in the Brahmin religion. When they finally arrived here and looked around, they found Daru and Meto, two men who had been abandoned on this Tsumi island for the crime of murder.

Hiding their bodies in a cave of crags, naked without even a garment, with beards overgrown like monkeys and hair tangled like a kite's nest, they lived in the cave. The three men, who had drifted here after their boat was broken, first searched the Isobata here and there, hoping to find food. However, they were daunted by the dangerous state of the Aranami that surged and receded. Clutching their empty stomachs, they wandered around the island, searching and strolling until they finally discovered this cave. No sooner did they see the figures of the two men than the three, with a sinister look at one another, leaked a laugh that was truly terrifying, intending to kill these Zainin and make them their immediate prey.

The three men had finally reached this island with their lives barely intact. Ships rarely passed, and there was no prospect of returning to the continent no matter how long they waited. The mountain was covered in rocks, making it impossible to dig for roots to eat, and although they tried to fish for shellfish and crabs on the Isobata, the waves in the lake were particularly rough here, and they could not obtain even a single crab or a speck of shellfish. The three fell into a plight where they had no choice but to starve to death. Desperate for any prey, they searched every corner of the small island and finally noticed the two Zainin, gaunt and hiding inside this cave. Unable to bear their hunger, the three conceived a terrifying heart to make these two men their immediate prey; they walked straight up to the two, and without warning, grabbed one of them, attempting to tear him apart with all their might using both arms from either side. One man wielded a sharp stone knife he had hidden in the cave and stood up to save his friend. Terrified by this display of ferocity, the three let go, and in an instant, they fled up the steep rocky mountain like rabbits, picking up pieces of rock from above and throwing them at the three. The three had no choice but to hide themselves inside the cave where the two had been lurking to avoid this danger. The two men thought that the three man-eating humans must have all fallen, struck by the many rocks they had thrown down, and that they would finally be able to eat their fill of warm human flesh after a long time to regain their strength; they took a roundabout path, descended the slightly gentler mountainside, and followed the Isobata to approach the side of the cave where they lived. When they looked, the three were sitting in a tripod formation inside the cave without a single injury, sitting on their heels and talking about something. Upon seeing this, the two said, 'Ah, they are still lively. This is terrible,' and fled for their lives, following the edge of the shore. Startled by the sound of their footsteps, the three said, 'Come, let us catch them now and fill our bellies,' and chased after the two with all their might. The two fled while picking up and throwing small stones. The three chased them with all their might, saying, 'You wretches, I will not let you escape; it will be terrible if you get up the mountain.' Meto and Daru were driven in front of a rock that stood like a flute at the edge of the shore, and with no way to advance or retreat, they became desperate and grappled with the three. Here, the three hungry men and the two men, who had built up their strength with crabs and shellfish despite being thin and were accustomed to starvation, grappled with each other, performing a great grand drama of Gaki against Gaki on the edge of the shore.

§ 2

The ship on which Tamakuniwake was riding was sailing about four or five chō offshore, with its white sails catching the wind. Itahiko, who had been standing at the bow, noticed a mysterious island floating in the middle of the lake, and upon looking closely, he saw four or five men on the rocky shore who, though not clearly visible, appeared to be engaged in some sort of struggle. He immediately ducked into the reed-thatched cabin.

Itahiko: 'Sensei, there is a mysterious island floating over there. And it seems as though some figures, likely human, are having a great brawl on the shore; please take a look for a moment.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Hmm, we must have approached the side of the famous Island of Sin. It is said that great criminals are confined there; what a pitiful sight.'

Itahiko: 'What do you think? Shall we bring the ship closer and, since they are criminals, all the more reason to preach and teach them the Oshihe of the Ōkamisama and save them? It is surely not the duty of us missionaries to pass by when we see the Nangi of people in the present world.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Yes, that is right. I want to save them. Hey, boatman, would you please bring the ship to that island?'

One of the boatmen: 'Yes, if you command it, I will bring the ship as close as I can, but there are only terrible villains there, and if we were to carelessly bring the ship near, we would be devoured. It is said that those who are sent there one after another have no food, so the weak are eaten by the strong, and those who remain are nothing but human-demons who have no regard for anything. I will bring the ship close, provided you do not go ashore.'

Itahiko appeared once again at the gunwale.

Itahiko: 'Hey, boatman, it looks like a great spectacle is being performed. Why don't we watch it?'

Boatman: 'In that case, I will bring it close. It is a very frightening place, though.'

So saying, he rowed the eight-oared boat, bringing it toward the vicinity of the island as if shooting an arrow. For about forty or fifty ken near the island, the waves were strangely high and the area was dotted with reefs, making it an extremely dangerous place. The boatman skillfully navigated through the reefs and finally approached to within about ten ken of the shore. The five men were all exhausted, lying there with their breath coming in gasps.

Tamakuniwake: 'Ah, boatman, I am sorry to trouble you, but please moor the ship gently. It seems that as a result of the five men fighting each other, they are at their last gasp. We must do something to save them.'

Boatman: 'If you please, customer, if we were to save such people, it would be a disaster; we would become criminals ourselves, and we would be sent to this island by many soldiers and abandoned forever. It would be best if you stopped that.'

Tamakuniwake: 'What, I do not care about that. We cannot overlook the Nangi of others. Now, hurry and moor the ship. If by any chance you become a criminal because you moored the ship, I will explain it for you. I will certainly save them, so please be at ease and bring it to the shore.'

One of the boatmen: 'In that case, I will follow your orders and moor the ship, at any rate.'

He brought the ship to the shade of a rock where the waves were low, in a slightly bay-like area. There, the boatman tied the ship firmly to the rocks on the shore, and so that there would be no fear of it being swept away by the waves and drifting, he pulled several ropes and lashed it securely.

The party of Tamakuniwake walked along the shore, jumping over the rough stones to reach the side where the five men were lying. The five men looked at the figures of the missionaries with puzzled expressions.

Itahiko first approached the side of the five men.

Itahiko: 'What are you five doing on such a remote island? If I look, you are all bleeding from your faces, are you not? Now that you have been exiled to such an island for your sins, why don't you live together in harmony?'

Yakkos of the Brahman army rose up, and while wiping the blood from his face with his hand, he said:

Yakkos: 'I was entrusted with a mission by the Ōkuninushi-sama of the capital of Haruna to cross this lake, but as I was heading north, I encountered a violent storm and collided with a reef, and the hull of the ship was smashed to pieces. My two colleagues were swallowed by the surging waves and their whereabouts are unknown, and we three finally drifted ashore on this island. We are not suspicious people, so I beg you to save us.'

Meto: 'You appear to be a messenger of Kamisama, so please, I beseech you, save us two. These three people drifted ashore here about two futatoki ago, and they have been chasing us to take our lives and make us their ejiki; we lost our place to flee and fought back in a shinimonoguruhi, but now our strength is exhausted and our hands and feet no longer obey us. I beg you to take pity on us and save our lives. And please, take the three of them away somewhere. We cannot endure it if such hitokuhijinshiyu remain on this island.'

So he pleads sorrowfully, with tears streaming down from his repulsive, protruding eyes and mizubana dripping from his nose.

Tamakuniwake took out bread stored in the senchiu, gave it to the gonin with gentle words, and the whole group cared for them with all their hearts. The gonin were given bread, and for the first time in a long while, Daru and Meto were given mamizu; weeping with joy, they pressed their palms together and bowed down in itsushiyōkenmei prayer.

Michihiko and Davis-hime tended to the gonin with gentle words, leading those with weakened legs by the hand or carrying them on their shoulders toward the side of the ship they had arrived on, and each of them helped carry the bodies of the gonin into the ship, laid them down, and once again hoisted the sails to proceed south, ever south.

Masumihiko stands at the prow and sings a song. Michihiko and Itahiko strike the gunwale to keep a graceful rhythm.
