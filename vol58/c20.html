Chapter 20: 酒談 [1495]

酒談〔1495〕

§ 1

Hatsuwakamaru, with the white sail still furled, finally arrived at the shore. Akisu and Kāru, the two of them, dancing with joy, tucked up their skirts and ran splashing through the shallow sea, unable to wait any longer; they clung to the boat and peered inside, only to be struck with surprise at the sight of two men with thick, overgrown beards, along with a handsome Sendenshi and a beautiful woman on board, and they cried out in loud voices,

Akisu: 'Ah, Dannasama, oh, Bantōsama!'

Having said that, they were already overcome with tears of joy, and could not utter another word, but went around to the back of the boat and pushed with all their might, taking advantage of the shallow water. At last, the whole party, with Tamakuniwake in the lead, landed one after another.

Tamakuni: 'Ah, Iru-sama, and all the rest of you, thank you for your hard work. Come, this is but a token of my appreciation. Please use it to buy some sake or the like.'

He took a small amount of money from his bosom and attempted to hand it over.

Iru: 'Dannasama, please, I beg you, do not trouble yourself. We have already received a generous wage from Hatsuwakahime-sama, so to accept more would be to invite misfortune; I gratefully accept your kind intentions. I pray you, please take it back.'

Tamakuni: 'Once a Sendenshi has offered something, no matter what is said, I cannot take it back. I pray you, please accept it.'

Iru: 'If that is the case, it would be rude of me to decline further; I shall gratefully accept it.'

He held it up to his forehead, then immediately turned toward the sea,

Iru: 'Ryūjin-sama, thanks to you, we have been brought here safely. As the journey back is long, I pray for your protection so that we may return to the port of Kita. I offer this as a fee for the sacred offerings.'

No sooner had he said this than he tossed it into the lake, bade farewell to the group, and with a graceful stroke of the oars, rowed away while singing a boatman's song.

Tamakuni: 'Ah, boatmen are truly people of strong faith. They are truly honest. I am deeply moved by their lack of greed and their detachment from worldly desires. Seeing the pure heart of that boatman makes me feel ashamed of our own hearts. Alas, our spiritual cultivation is still insufficient. Oh, God, I have received a most grateful lesson. Kannagara Tamachi Haemaese.'

Weeping with gratitude, he celebrated their safe arrival at the port.

Bāchiru took the hands of the two, Akisu and Kāru,

Bāchiru: 'Were you my servants? Thank you for coming to meet me. How is the household?'

Akisu: 'Yes, they are in good health. The master is also doing extremely well.'

Kāru: 'For the past two or three days, he has been acting as if possessed by a spirit, and though he says strange things from time to time, I was truly impressed. He kept insisting so urgently that I must go to the beach of Suma because the master was returning, that I have been standing here waiting for three days. I am so glad you have returned. I am overjoyed.'

Bāchiru: 'Ah, is that so? That is a mysterious thing. In any case, I shall accompany this teacher, return home as quickly as possible, and take a good rest. Come, lead the way.'

The two of them replied at once,

'Yes, then we shall guide you, everyone.'

They immediately started walking in the lead. From the other side, a man came running with his arms spread wide,

Man: 'Hey, wait, wait! These fellows are Sendenshi of the San-go-kyō. They are sorcerers of divine wonders. Your master is a follower of Brahmanism, yet he is a traitor who brings back sorcerers of the San-go-kyō. He is a Protestant against Brahmanism. Yes, he is a Bāchirusu. Hey, Bāchiru, I have something to investigate. Come with me to the checkpoint at the port of Kiyo for a moment.'

Akisu: 'Aren't you Teku, the drunkard? If you keep spouting nonsense, I will treat you to this iron fist!'

Teku: 'Hah! My body is covered all over with the Mitsuba-aoi crest. If you dare to lay even a single finger on me, just try it!'

§ 2

Tamakuniwake opened the mouth of the Heutan he was carrying, thrust it toward the tip of Teku's nose, and while smiling, said,

Tamakuniwake: 'Your Yakume is truly Gokurau. Why not have a little to help with the Shiyokibara?'

Teku: 'Hehehehe. Even a Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō can talk a little, I like it very much. If the world were not like this, Ningen could not get by; as they say, "Without sake, what kind of a Sendenshi am I?" First of all, I shall accept a cup.'

He said this while beaming, and without pausing for breath from the mouth of the gourd, while making his throat rumble, he performed an emergency Kuwangai into the tank of his stomach; then, turning the gourd upside down and making it dance on his palm, he licked the two or three dripping drops of sake with Goteinei, changed his Taido suddenly, and said,

Teku: 'Oh, my, having received a Jiyōtō Daigomi, I am truly in the mood for Kanpai. With this gourd, I feel a happy Kokoromochi, like a dream on a Kantan pillow, wondering what kind of Hiyōshi or gourd, or what kind of Sokotan or Kontan it is. I shall reform from this moment on, become your Deshi, and I wonder if it might be possible, if it might be possible, for me to be allowed to drink this sweet, sweet Heutanzake, lots and lots, little by little, again and again. You are a truly clever teacher, yes, I like you, teacher. The Ananai-kyō of four-class equality and four-class equal human love is far, far, far sweeter than the despotism of the Brahmins. It has plenty of delicious flavor. Speaking of sweetness, I would like to have a lot of the sake I just drank, even if it is just a little, just a Chiyotsuto, just a little, even if it is only a little, I want a lot of it. As long as you let me drink sake, as long as there is the spirit of sake, this Teku is a gentle, mild, soft, obedient, wonderful, and happy Ningen, like a cat. I am a truly happy Ningen. Hehehehe.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Ahahahaha.'

Bachiru: 'Listen, Teku-sama, since I have returned to my happy home after three years, I am going to hold a great banquet, and I do not care if the family fortune disappears for about a week, so could you take care of the sake for me? I would like you to order tens of koku of Jiyōtō sake and prepare the menu.'

Teku: 'Certainly, certainly, ten million times reasonable; like a boat at the landing, a sail for the pursuer, a man for a woman, sweet sake for Teku, Teku for sake, Teku for sake, is it sake that is Teku, or Teku that is sake? I am a Teku born from sake; yes, indeed, I have understood. I have certainly accepted. Ah, ah, ah, when I hear the word sweet sake, my throat, that cat of a throat, begins to growl. My saliva comes out to the gate to welcome it before even seeing the face of the sake. Yes, saliva, you too, wait a little. I will supply you immediately. No, I will irrigate you. You should wait just until the steam pump equipment, no, the installation, can be done. No, although I say that, I have also become unable to wait. Hey, teacher, do you not have another Heutan, a gourd, a gourd, a gourd, that you are carrying for emergency use?'

Michihiko: 'Ahahahaha. You look like quite a tank; in that case, I shall offer you my share as well. I will go to Bachiru-sama's house later and refill it with new ones... This sake is a bit tired from crossing the waves, but please bear with it.'

Teku: 'Oh, that is grateful; Heutanzake is better the older it gets. It has flavor. Sake that has been shaken for three or four days is sticky and thick, and it has a good mouthfeel. Oh, grateful, grateful.'

Saying this, he snatched it from Michihiko's hand as if pulling it away, accepted it, raised the gourd up to his forehead, looked at the bottom, and...

Taking the lead, singing songs energetically along the way, and dancing the Yakko dance, they returned through the scorching summer fields. At the southern foot of Mount Adsumosu, a forest with thick, old trees could be seen. That was the vast mansion of Bachiru.
