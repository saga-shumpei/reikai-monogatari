Chapter 9: Lake Moon [1484]

湖月〔1484〕

§ 1

Emerging from the waves and entering the waves, the Giyokuto's Tamakuniwaketsukasa, riding in the Menashikatama boat bestowed by Hatsuwakahime, proceeds across the Unabara of unknown end, saving five men while singing the Funauta high and pure above the waves. The light of the stars sparkles, and in the depths of the sunken lake, both the Amatsumisora and the Sentei are inlaid with Kinshiya and Ginshiya; crossing through that Chiukan, the Ama no Kahara flows clearly and endlessly to the Nanpoku, and the Tsukikage, as if washed clean, flickers deep in the Umizoko like a Ginriu. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, the Sendenshi of the Kami's divine guidance.

Beginning with Otohiko of the clear Namioto and Tamakuniwake, the stars reflect the figure of Michihiko in the Masumizora of the Misora; separated by one Ichimai, Itahiko and the thousand-fathom deep water hell, together with the princess of Davis, sing the Sendenka with clear voices. Singing and singing, they proceed, poling toward the Ama no Kahara, rowing the boat on the Eden River, as if ascending to the Gen-on-kyo, advancing with cool and brave hearts, unaware and unthinking. The summer breeze wipes away the sweat, there is no scent of unpleasant tide, the sails swell as if shooting an arrow, the mast bends like a bow, and they possess the bravery to ride out and overcome the rough waves that arise at that moment.

The party of Tamakuniwake, having traveled the land route for a long time, felt their spirits rise for no reason, their bodies clear and full of energy; while admiring the scenery of the gulls of the offing and the Amboina flying to and fro, they sped along, leaving it to the wind as if ascending to the heavenly pure land. Dal and Met, whose precarious lives had been saved, finally recovered their energy, and raising their hoarse voices, began to sing cheerfully.

Met: 'I was born in the land of the moon, at the southern foot of Mount Telmon, I am the only son of Metel, born as a Shuda. As a Sendenshi of the Sangokyo, I asked most earnestly to cross these lake waters.'

Even when it was proclaimed, knowing that we would break the law, Dal and I set out the boat, and when we arrived safely at the port of Kiyo, the inspectors of the Brahman religion, Kanna and Heru, caught us without a word, took us to the checkpoint of Kiyo, inflicted three thousand five hundred lashes, and in the end, put us on a boat and sent us to the island of Tsumi floating in the lake. Watching them flee quickly after sending us off, I spent my days in tears of grief; how must my parents, whom I left at home, be faring? Though I wish to let them know of this state, even in a dream, what can I do, a body without wings?

Unable to find a way to send word, sobbing and choking, Dal and I embraced, lamenting the world, and while sending the days relying on the sound of the waves crashing against the terrifying shore, catching crabs and shellfish on the beach, and barely sustaining our dew-like lives, at the moment we were praying to the Kami of heaven and earth that the rescue boat would come as soon as possible, a party of three came drifting by. Hearing their words, which were more terrifying than anything, that they would beat us to death and make us their prey the moment they saw our figures, we stood up from the narrow rocky cave, and while escaping the poisonous hands of the enemy, we climbed the steep rocky mountain like monkeys, and taking stones of a handy size in our hands,

We crushed the man-eating race to death, and while racking our brains to escape the danger to our own bodies, we thought the three man-eaters had easily lost their lives, struck by the rock fragments I threw down; but when we peeked and approached, who could have guessed? The three were sitting upright in the rocky cave, and while rolling their white eyes, they found the figures of us two, and came chasing with all their might to kill us again. We, thinking that we must not let our precious lives be taken, ran away breathless, and stopped dead at the rock of Sho; trapped here, the cornered mouse bites the cat, and with our lives as the stake, we turned to counterattack.
