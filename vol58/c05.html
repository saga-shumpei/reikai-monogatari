Chapter 5: 潔別 [1480]

潔別〔1480〕

§ 1

At the Kamuyakata of Mount Terumon, in the garden where green leaves grow in abundance, the Hanaayame are blooming in full pride, bluish-purple, white, and Kiiro; with Kakitsubata crowded without space, how enchanting is their fragrant bloom! The branches of the pine trees, blown by the Riyōfū, play Shizen's music, and while performing a Butafu, they manifest the Zuishiyō of Shichitaihei, which is truly cause for rejoicing. The evergreen pine trees are brightly green, their verdure stretching forth, and the jewel-like dew drops, with every blowing of the wind, fall with a patter upon the Kinshiya and Ginshiya. The Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, while being showered with the dew of grace, advancing forward, led by Tamakuniwake-no-mikoto, and the Kiudaukoji in the guise of a Biku,

together with the captain of Brahmanism and the Kashikuwan brought along with him, twining their knees together in the inner room, while letting the cool breeze in, they rejoice in the grace of Tenchi, removing the barriers of their hearts; that they begin to speak is itself according to Kami; for other than the teachings of the true path, the words are like the harsh wind crossing over the Aono; as if beholding the true reality of the Heavenly Pure Land right before their eyes right now. Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara! The grace of Kami granting good fortune, erasing the distinctions between the teachings of Ananai-kyō and Brahmanism, the morning sun also cleanly shines at the foot of Mount Terumon, where they have begun to build a paradise, how venerable! Here, the Sendenshi Michihiko,

having expelled Waks, Ekis, Hellman, and the official of El, who committed enmity against the Kamuyakata of Kami, and taking Tank and Tonk along, as he leisurely returns and takes his seat, Tamakuniwake calls out to him: 'Thou Michihiko, Kamitsukasa, in the riding ground before the Kamuyakata, the shouts of men and women, old and young, and the bonfires burning the sky— the full details of that story I urge thee to proclaim in detail,' whereupon Michihiko, placing both hands on the floor, most respectfully answered.

○

'The great pillar of Kami, Ōkuninushi, who manifested in the Capital of the Moon, resided here in ancient times, and as a memorial of the sacred place where He opened the teachings of Brahmanism, He enshrined the Nyoi-hōju, a precious treasure; He commanded the two masters of the Kamuyakata to act as masters of the teaching and strictly guard it; yet Waks, the foolish and dull son of Allstin, in order to satisfy his ambition, instigated the two men, Ekis and Hellman, to sneak into the inner palace and steal the jewel, covering it with soil to hide it deep beneath the floor of my house— how hideous is that inner heart! The master of the Kamuyakata felt sorrow in his heart,

and having become a person of severe illness, with his life pressed at dawn and dusk, I revered the command of Kami and entered into this Kamuyakata; requested by Ogunikohime-sama, I searched for the location of the jewel, and while rescuing the Kamuyakata from its distress, there was a moment I stayed for a short while; then, the Kyōshi Waks, who was lost in lust and greed, beginning with him, and one hundred other villains besides, spread rumors that Michihiko of the way of the teaching was a sorcerer, and instigating the men and women who dwelt eternally in this sacred place, they organized the Akuyo-kai and came attacking with the Kamuyakata as their target; how dreadful was their force! I, with only my single body,

confronted the wicked ones who attacked, and fought to the limit of my strength; yet my ally was but one, while the enemy army had the momentum of clouds and mist, so I was helplessly captured by the enemy and thrown into the Anblack River, falling into a state where life and death were uncertain, how miserable! To such a place, Smart appeared and kindly uttered the strict Kototama, and holding pure water in his mouth, he called my living soul back to life and restored me once again to my original body. My courage multiplied a hundredfold beyond ordinary days, and eager to remove the disaster from the Kamuyakata of Kami, burning my body and exercising my heart in manifold ways, I finally captured the villains,

and applied the whip according to the rule; under the lash of the two persons, Tank and Tonk, the villains fled away like clouds and mist. Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara! The precious grace of Kami, my precarious life was saved, and the severe illness of the master of the Kamuyakata moves toward recovery day and night; Takahime-sama of Davis and Takahime-sama of Kerina have auspiciously returned here; and while there was a moment of joy having completed the meeting between parents and children without hindrance, the master Tamakuniwake, accompanying Masumihiko and Itahiko, came joyfully, and the meeting between master and disciples was accomplished. Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara! Before Kami, we offer our true hearts and render our deep gratitude.

Whether the morning sun shines or is clouded, whether the moon waxes or wanes, whether the stars fall from the sky and are lost, whether the ocean of a thousand fathoms dries up, will I not exhaust my earnest devotion to the divine commission? Understanding the heart of Michihiko, may the master depart from this Kamuyakata, and together with us, to the Country of the Moon, the capital of Haruna, smoothly advance, according to the will of Kami! Under today's sky that permits no hesitation, differing from yesterday, mist hangs in all four directions, the wind blows fiercely, and rain is even mixed in on this summer day; the human body whose destination is uncertain should not waste even a single moment, and I pray that thou advance for the sake of Kami and the world.' Takahime-sama of Davis stood before me—

§ 2

Having declared the hundred Kototama, and in a heart-rending negotiation to pledge the way of Imose, I could not return a word, and while I hesitated, my Master appeared. Taking this as a blessing, I quickly left the mansion, thinking to advance toward the Land of the Moon as soon as possible. Masumihiko and Itahiko, accept the Ikukototama of Michihiko, the minister of the Kami's Oshihe, and together with my Master, whip the chestnut horse, ride across the Aono Plain, fight the heat, bathe in the rain, and comb your hair in the wind. Let us advance, come, quickly, quickly, quickly! he urged.

Masumihiko stood up, faced the Daishinzen, bowed respectfully, and clapped his hands. Then, facing Tamakuniwake, he lowered his head and spoke in a low voice: 'As the Sendenshi of Tamakuniwake, who serves with my Master, I accept the words of the minister Michihiko. Let us leave this place as quickly as a moment passes and ascend the Seito of the Akuma. No matter what kind of evil may come to attack, how could we fear the way of the Kami? Crossing over the Senri of mountains and rivers, crossing the lakes where the Namikaze rage and the great rivers where the Dakusui swell, let us cross by the grace of the Kami, climb the steep slopes, and while converting the Akuma along the way, let us advance, Kannagara. I pray that you grant us permission.'

Tamakuniwake: 'The storm on Mount Terumon has also subsided. Come, let us depart for the imperial land of the Moon.'

Michihiko: 'Departing in accordance with the declaration of my Master, the path ahead shall surely be peaceful.'

Debisuhime: 'Michihiko, minister of the Kami, please take your young wife and proceed.'

Michihiko: 'Departing in accordance with the declaration of the Lord God, how could the path of the minister Michihiko be painful? If my Master grants permission, I shall take her with me to the imperial land of the Moon.'

Debisuhime: 'I speak to the command of the Kami Tamakuniwake. Please take me with you to India.'

Tamakuniwake: 'If there is permission from the parents, I shall take you. Everything is solely in accordance with the Kami.'

Ogunihime: 'Debisuhime, as the wife of the minister Michihiko, please take her with you, minister of the Kami.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Since there is permission from the mother, how could I refuse a companion on this journey?'

Masumihiko: 'Michihiko, my lord, with the joy of the spring and autumn, where the eternal flowers bloom, in your heart.'

Itahiko: 'You are wise, my lord, to take the innocent Debisuhime as your wife and set out on a journey.'

Michihiko: 'Even so, she is a companion not yet dyed into my heart, until we fulfill the mission of the Kami.'

Masumihiko: 'It is amusing to see you taking a young wife while avoiding her, adorning the patterns of your words.'

Kyudokoji: 'The heart of the minister Michihiko, the Kami, is like the moon of the fifteenth night. Come then, I shall remain in this house and serve the two parents faithfully.'

Nikoras: 'I speak to the command of Tamakuniwake. How shall I govern this mansion?'

Tamakuniwake: 'Brahman, without distinction between the San-go-kyo, serve the Kami of Omoto. Nevertheless, as for this mansion, it will not do to abandon the Kami of the Brahman. Serve the Kami of the San-go, and serve the Great Imperial Kami of the Brahman faithfully.'

Nikoras: 'Following your words, my lord, which make no distinctions, I shall serve the hundred Kami. From today, I shall depart from the lord of the Brahman army and serve the Kami faithfully.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Come then, hundred ministers, be at peace and spend your days with sincerity.'

Ogunihime: 'Though I have met my beloved lord of the Oshihe, the pain in my heart at the thought of parting now.'

Kerinahime: 'Tamakuniwake, who conveys the way of the Oshihe of the Imperial Kami, please depart in peace.'

Tamakuniwake: 'The time has passed quickly, Kerinahime. Serve your parents and remain pure.'

Thus, they sang songs of parting to one another, and in a harmonious atmosphere, they exchanged sake cups. Tamakuniwake, Masumihiko, Itahiko, Michihiko, and the party of Debisuhime entrusted all future matters to Kyudokoji, and while singing songs of propagation, they descended Mount Terumon to the south with joy, disappearing into the shadows of the green leaves. Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haemaese.
