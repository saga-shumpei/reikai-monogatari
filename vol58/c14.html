Chapter 14: 猩々 Island [1489]

猩々島〔1489〕

§ 1

At the northern end of the land of Tsuki, in the village of Suma in the land of Izumi, which one crosses the Kosui of Terumon to the south, there lived a wealthy farmer named Bachiru. Although Bachiru lived a life of Nanifujiiu, he made it his sole pleasure to float his boat upon the Kosui and fish whenever he had leisure. His wife, Saberu, would always remonstrate with him, saying, 'Stop this Kiken Giyogefu and stay quietly at home to attend to the Seiri of our Zaisan,' but he would not listen to her at all. Thinking that his wife had fallen into a Neshizu, he went out to the Hamabe and, as usual, cast his nets and dropped his lines. Finding it so Omoshiro, he drifted further and further offshore before he knew it, until he had reached a point from which he could Tautei not return in Ichinichi. The night he had set out his boat dawned, and that day too passed into darkness; forgetting the passage of time, he was so captivated by the good catch that he worked with all his might alongside one servant.

Suddenly, Manten was filled with Kurokumo, and not even Itsuten of Hoshikage could be seen. The Amekaze became fierce, the waves rose high, and it became clear that it would be Tautei impossible to escape Kiken while carrying such a Takusan of fish in the boat. However, he could not bear to throw away the fish he had worked so hard to catch into the Kochiu, yet he knew that if he did not lighten the load, the capsizing of the boat would Tautei be unavoidable. His servant, Anchii, trembled and shook, and with a weeping voice, pleaded:

Anchii: 'Danna-sama, what shall we do? In this darkness and with such a great storm, we shall surely not survive. Please, throw every one of these fish you have worked so hard to catch into the lake. If you do so, the boat will become lighter, and somehow or other, we may be able to reach some island and preserve our lives. I beg you, please let me throw every single one of them away.'

Bachiru became angry at his servant's words and, in a sharp voice, replied:

Bachiru: 'Listen here, Anchii. How dare you say such a rude thing, telling me to throw away all the fish I have risked such Kiken to catch? If you say the fish are too heavy and there is a fear of the boat capsizing, then if a heavy, good-for-nothing fellow like you were to jump into the lake, this boat would become much lighter and the fish would be saved.'

Being a man half-mad, he had eyes and ears for nothing but the fish. He spoke words of rebuke to his servant that were truly merciless.

Anchii: 'Danna-sama, I am a servant who has been raised in your house since I was a child, so if there were any matter involving your life, I am prepared to be your substitute. However, no matter how much of a servant I may be, I am still a human being. Fish can be caught in any quantity, but there is only one body of this Anchii in the world, so please do not say such merciless things and let me throw these fish away quickly.'

Bachiru: 'You just said that you would save your master from danger. Why do you not jump into the lake to reduce the weight, so that you may save these fish that your master has taken such pains to catch? Is that how you perform your duties as a servant? You are an incomprehensible creature.'

Anchii: 'Danna-sama, is my life so much cheaper than dead fish? Is that not far too heartless?'

Bachiru: 'Do not talk like a fool. If I take the fish home, I can enjoy them myself, and I can serve them as sashimi, simmered, or grilled to the people of the village. No matter how fat you are, your body will not turn into sashimi. There is no one who would be happy to receive that. If you wish to show loyalty to me, why do you not jump in yourself quickly? If you dawdle, will the boat not capsize? We are at the brink where we must decide whether to lose your life alone or lose both our lives. Come, be quick and jump in.'

Anchii: 'This is another outrageous thing to say. You are not yourself. Has what you are saying suddenly become strange?'

と半狂の男とて魚にかけたら目も鼻もない。僕に対して実に無慈悲極まる叱言を云つた。

アンチー『これは又怪しからぬ事を仰有います。貴方はどうかして居りますな。俄に仰有る事がへんになつたぢやありませぬか』

§ 2

Bachiru remained looking down in thought for a while, but suddenly, in a voice that sounded surprised, he spoke.

Bachiru: 'Ah, that is indeed so. I was so startled that I mistook the relative importance of the fish and a human being. Ah, forgive me, I have said a foolish thing. Perhaps because I was so preoccupied with the fish and worried in my head, I have conjured up a strange hallucination. Now, I shall do as you say. Come, come, let us release the fish, release them.'

With that, the master and servant began to grab the fish they had caught with such great effort and throw them into the sea, and just as they thought they had released about half of them, they were swallowed up by a great tsunami that came attacking with a sound like a roaring tiger, and they were swept into the waves along with their boat. The two fell into the rough sea in the dark of night, and being unable to do anything more, they left their fate to Heaven.

In the village of Suma in the land of Izumi, Bachiru, a wealthy farmer of Shiyuda, would not listen to his wife's warnings. On a night when the sea looked ominous, saying that at such times the catch was always greater than usual, he took his servant Anchi and set out in a boat from the shore of Suma. Losing himself in a bountiful catch such as he had never known before, he drifted far out to sea without realizing it. From the time he spent the night there until the following dawn, and even until the twilight passed, he fished with undivided attention. When it came to be around the middle of the night, the whole sky suddenly clouded over, and it became so dark that one could not distinguish black from white. Suddenly, a storm attacked. The waves rose, and the white crests shook their manes, threatening to bite at the fishing boat.

The terror of the roaring tiger, the dragon's cry, and the lion's fierce growl were overwhelming, along with the howling of the wind and waves. Without yielding or flinching, they cast their nets and fished for various large fish, but there were moments when they were truly in danger. The fishing boat was tossed about up and down and left and right like a falling leaf, and as their situation became perilous, the master and servant pair, while arguing about this and that, threw all the fish they had caught with such effort into the sea, hoping at least to save their lives. Just as they were rushing to do so, the boat was suddenly swallowed by waves like mountains, and mercilessly, they became mere seaweed in the water.

Anchi, the servant, ended up missing, and though called, he would not return from his journey to the land of the dead; it is a pitiful story to hear. Bachiru, who had been washed up on the shore of Shōjō Island, where people feared to approach, was tossed about by the waves, and just as he was on the verge of death, the King of the Shōjō appeared, quickly rescued him to the land, and by putting fresh water into his mouth, finally saved his life. It is truly an interesting story. Riding in a rectangular boat floating on the waves of tatami mats, smoking Shikishima tobacco, up to this point, the tip of the brush of Takamitsuhiko of Kitamura has written.

Wishing to copy this and pass it down for a thousand generations, he presses the nib of his fountain pen onto the manuscript paper, and roughly records it here. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase. May the spirits be blessed.

When Bachiru suddenly came to his senses, the night had already broken, and he was lying on the shore of an isolated island whose name he did not know, with many Shōjō gathering around him, crying 'Kya-kya' and surrounding him like ants. Nearby, one exceptionally large Shōjō looked at his face, tilting its head and shedding tears as if it could not bear the sympathy it felt. Looking closely, he realized it was the island of the Shōjō he had heard rumors about.

Bachiru: 'Ah, have I drifted to such a terrifying island? Because my ego was too strong, I did not listen to my wife's warnings and snuck out to fish; it was the greatest mistake of my life. And what has become of my servant Anchi? Ah, what a terrible thing has happened. Even if I think of returning, there is no boat; am I to end up as food for the Shōjō?'

Driven by fear, he trembled in terror.

Unexpectedly, the large ape, who seemed to be the King of the Shōjō, turned its back to Bachiru in a friendly manner, indicating by its gestures the intention, 'Carry me on your back.' Bachiru thought, 'Now that it has come to this, there is no choice but to resign myself to my fate. I shall leave myself to their will,' and fearfully clung to the Shōjō's back. The Shōjō, with him on its back, climbed the steep rocky mountain with great ease and quickly carried him into a deep cave bored into a rock that looked like a standing folding screen at the summit. Bachiru was led into the cave, and as his heart pounded, the Shōjō had a small monkey peel some rare fruit and offered it to him, saying, 'Eat this.' At the unexpected attitude of the Shōjō, he felt relieved, and since his body was exhausted and limp like a rope, he lay down to rest for a while. The Shōjō spread thick, half-dried leaves, held Bachiru, and using its own arm as a pillow, stroked and rubbed his whole body as kindly as a mother nursing a baby, devoting itself to his care. In this way, Bachiru came to spend three years on this Shōjō Island. This Shōjō was female and the king of the animals on this island. Besides the Shōjō, deer and rabbits lived there. However, the rabbits and deer also sometimes came to the side of the Shōjō King and played together harmoniously.

§ 3

In the second year, a strange half-human, half-beast baby was born between the Shiyōjiyō and Birchil. The King of the Shiyōjiyō cherished the child as a jewel in his palm, making Birchil hold it with a boastful air, or licking its face and feeding it milk himself, as if waiting for the child to reach adulthood. Although Birchil wished to return to his country, there was no essential boat, nor could he return by hiding from the eyes of the Shiyōjiyō King. Birchil lived with the Shiyōjiyō for three years, and he had come to be able to communicate his will mostly through facial expressions. Feeling an irresistible urge to go out to the beach, he gestured to the Shiyōjiyō King, asking if he might go to the beach to play. The Shiyōjiyō King nodded happily, and while holding the child and followed by many small monkeys, he guarded Birchil's surroundings, descended the steep rocky mountain, and went out to the rocky shore, where they chased crabs, dug in the sand, and enjoyed various amusements, happily playing on the summer shore.

Suddenly, a ship with white sails raised passed about two or three chō offshore. This vicinity was a dangerous area dotted with reefs where ships could not easily pass. Upon seeing this ship, Birchil waved both his hands, signaling that a human had drifted ashore on this island. Eer, the boatman, saw this figure and exclaimed in a voice that sounded surprised,

Eer: 'Ah, everyone, please take a look for a moment. That island is called Shiyōjiyō-ga-shima, and it is inhabited only by Shiyōjiyō, but strangely enough, something that looks like a human is standing on the rocky shore, surrounded by many Shiyōjiyō, and waving their hands. There are quite a lot of Shiyōjiyō, you know.'

Ita: 'What, an island of Shiyōjiyō? That sounds interesting.'

No sooner had he said this than he crawled out from the thatched roof to the bow, and looking closely, he saw that, just as Eer had said, something that looked like a human was waving its arms incessantly.

Ita: 'Excuse me, Sensei, it appears that a human has drifted ashore on that island. Shall we try to bring the boat closer and investigate?'

Yakkosu: 'There are very wicked Shiyōjiyō there. If you go to such a place, you will all have your eyes gouged out and your lives taken. You must not go to such a dangerous place. I am telling you this for your own good. Please stop.'

Ita: 'Sensei, shall we bring the boat close to the side and try to investigate? There is no danger as long as we do not actually land. In any case, we must check carefully whether it is a human or a beast, and if it is a human, we must help them. I certainly want to dock the boat.'

Tamakuni: 'Indeed, you are right. It certainly looks like a human. They must be living with beasts on such an uninhabited island. We might be able to hear some interesting stories. At any rate, it is only two or three chō away, so Boatman-sama, could you please bring the boat in for a moment?'

Eer: 'Yes, I have received a large sum of money from a person called First-Waka-sama, and I have been requested to do as the missionary says, so I shall follow your orders.'

Tamakuni: 'Oh, that is grateful. Then I leave it to you.'

'Yes,' replied Eer, and he turned the bow, considered the pilotage, and finally arrived at the rocky shore.

と云ふより早く苫屋根の中から舳に這ひ出てよくよく見れば、イールの云つた通り人間らしいものが頻りに腕を振つて居る。

伊太『もし、先生、どうやら、あの島に人間が漂着して居る様子です。一つ何とかして舟を寄せ調べて行かうぢやありませぬか』

ヤッコス『あれは大変な悪い猩々が居るのです。あんな処へ行かうものなら、皆両眼を刳り抜かれ命を取られて了ひます。そんな険呑な処へ行くものぢやありませぬ。決して悪い事は申しませぬ。お止めなさいませ』

伊太『先生、側まで船を寄せて調べて見ようぢやありませぬか。別に上陸さへせなければ危険はありませぬ。兎も角人間か獣か、よく調て、人間ならば助けてやらねばなりますまい。是非とも船を着け度いものですな』

玉国『成程、お前の云ふ通りだ。どうも人間らしい。あんな無人島に獣と同棲してるのだらう。何か面白い話が聞けるかも知れない。兎も角僅か二三丁の処だから、船頭さま、一寸船をつけて呉れまいか』

イール『はい、初稚様と云ふお方に沢山なお金を頂き、又宣伝使の仰有る通りにして呉れとのお頼みで厶いますから仰せに従ひませう』

玉国『や、そりや有難い、そんなら頼む』

『はい』と答えてイールは舳を転じ水先を考へ乍ら漸くにして磯辺に着いた。
