怪物〔1492〕

§ 1

Hatsuwakamaru crossed the heubeu unaji and finally arrived at the island of Fuku, which was about nijifugochiyō in shiui. It was a dainankuwan where the hijiyō aranami bit at the shore, making it kennon and impossible to approach. Looking closely, a very large gankutsu was naturally bored into the chiufuku of the mountain, and something appeared to be moving within it. As soon as Itahiko caught a glimpse of it,

Itahiko: 'It is a shiyōjiyō, a shiyōjiyō! This might be a goshinrui of yours, Bachiru-sama. I would like to find a way to row to the island and investigate its shiyōtai.'

Upon seeing it, Bachiru gasped and nearly collapsed.

Itahiko: 'Ah, this is fushigi. Bachiru-sama, you gasped and nearly collapsed when you saw the figure of that kuwaibutsu; what is it exactly? Do you have some kokoroata about it?'

Bachiru: 'Yes, I cannot say for certain, but it looked like the figure of a man I once saw, so I cried out unintentionally.'

Itahiko: 'It is just as I thought; having lived in a betsusekai, you have become shinkeikuwabin. Still, it is fushigi that something like a ningen should live on such a remote island. Perhaps it is someone who met with a nanpasen and was washed ashore on this island. My curiosity is aroused and I cannot help myself; even if it is dangerous, let us go up and investigate its shiyōtai. Sensei, would you not entrust the exploration of this demon's den to me?'

Tamakuni: 'Hmm, it would be well to try. Bachiru-sama is quite accustomed to islands, so he should be fit for the exploration.'

Bachiru: 'Yes, please grant me permission as well. I feel that I must investigate this no matter what.'

The kuwaibutsu, upon seeing this boat, hurriedly emerged from the gankutsu and climbed down the steep rocky crags like a monkey, exposing its hairy face, and beckoned with its hands, saying,

'Oi, oi.'

At this moment, the boat had advanced to within about one chō. Finally, they brought the boat to the rocky island protruding into the sea, and with great difficulty, the four of them—Itahiko, Bachiru, Meto, and Daru—rushed onto the island. It was a truly dangerous feat. The falling waves, rising like a ball of one jō and ascending like a dragon to heaven, could not help but strike them with a sense of misery. The four did not falter, but ran to the side of the man and peered into his face with a sense of fushigi. The mysterious man gazed intently at the faces of the four, and said,

Man: 'Ah, are you not my master? How wonderful that you have come.'

Bachiru: 'Oh, were you my Anchī? How did you survive in a place like this? I thought you had been swallowed by those aranami and turned into foam on the water, and that I would never see you again in this world. How wonderful that you are alive. I am also on my way home after being rescued by this boat, and due to the currents, I happened to come to this place. Was this also an arrangement by the gods?'

Anchī shed tears that fell in drops from his bearded face and began to weep like a man.

Itahiko: 'Now, Anchī-sama, why are you crying? Pull yourself together. Come, I am going to take you home now, so there is no need to be anxious; follow me with peace of mind. But somehow, my own tears are also leaping from both eyes without permission.'

And with that, he was already sobbing.

Wiping his tears with his hands, Anchī said,

Anchī: 'Danna-sama, I was swallowed by the waves with you, fell into a state of unconsciousness, and was washed ashore on this island. Then, a beautiful goddess called Hatsuwakahime-sama came and cared for me in various ways. Thanks to her, I have preserved my life until today. Fortunately, as you know, there are many birds on this island, and a few fruits also ripen here, so I was able to obtain enough food for one person.'

Ita: 'Well, are you not saying something that does not make sense? Hatsuwakahime-sama passed by here only yesterday. And yet, whether she left by boat, or whether she came riding on a dog, it is a thing that does not make sense.'

Ita: 'Indeed, I had heard that Hatsuwakahime-sama was an ikigamisama, but she is truly great. They say she is a tennin from the Daiichitengoku, and if that were not so, she could not perform such a feat. Thinking of this, our shishiyō still has much shifugefu to do, or it will be dame. Regardless of anything else, since the wind may grow rough at any time, let us leave this kikenkuiki as quickly as possible.'

Shaking his mane, he slipped through the raging shiranami, and here the gonin safely became people of the ship, hurriedly turned the prow, manipulated the oars, and followed the tidal current, proceeding toward the southwest. Inside the ship, various flowers bloomed from Anchie's tale of drifting.

Ita: 'If I may, everyone, what a fushigi thing this is. It seems this person was a servant of Bachiru-sama. Is it not truly the strangest of wonders that we were shipwrecked three years ago, both master and servant kept our lives on a desert island, and were both saved by our ship at the same itsutoki?
