Chapter 21: 館帰 [1496]

館帰〔1496〕

§ 1

Akisu stood at the head of the party and began to sing with great vigor.

Akisu: 'Ah, how grateful, how grateful. Since that day when the master, who pined for love, and the head clerk together lost their way, it has been almost three full years. Even the peaceful household, as expected, has ended up with the master absent. Though spring comes, the flowers do not bloom; the summer grove is prone to withering; the autumn gale blows fiercely; and the treetops shiver as if stripping off their feathered robes. For no reason, the inside of the house has lived in cold and sorrow. Saber-hime-okusama, taking her young son as her strength, while yearning for her husband, Bāchiru, who vanished into the white-capped sea, not knowing when he would return, has been performing prayers and mourning with tears in her hand.'

She has been performing memorial services, and from early in the morning, she offers worship before the Kami of the Brahmins. Praying for the repose of her master's soul, at sunset she offers sacred flowers and water at the grave of the On-hakaba on Mount Azumosu. She offers the delicacies of mountain, field, river, and sea with all her heart, and by manifesting the depth of their marital bond to the very end, she has been praised in all directions as a mirror of a virtuous and chaste woman. We, who have served the master's house since long ago, feel our hearts tighten and heave with sighs every time we see the face of our mistress, measuring the unfathomable sorrow.

Having endured it little by little for three years, the Ōmikami, taking pity on this state of affairs, descended into the house of Bāchiru. Possessing Saber-hime, the Lord caused her to solemnly proclaim that the master would return. How noble it was. Overwhelmed by the event, we, too, wrapped in the clouds of half-belief, braved the burning sky and stood on the shore of Suma, stretching our necks to wait for the master's return. Just then, the shadow of a white sail appeared beyond the white waves. Is it the master, or is it someone else? We, being but mortals, unable to discern, sat ourselves down on the grass of a summer day.

Waiting impatiently, wondering if the beloved person will return before us, the opening of the dark heart was illuminated. The Kami of the Sun rose over the edge of the eastern mountain, casting light upon the lower world, and the time of joy arrived. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara. How joyful it is that he returns from the uninhabited island, saved by the party of Tamakuniwake, together with Anchī-sama, and that he transmits the teachings of the San-go-kyō in all directions. It goes without saying for Saber-hime, but even the five-year-old boy, leaping with joy, will surely perform a lively drama of joy and sorrow in the inner room.

Ah, how grateful, how grateful. It goes without saying for the Bāchiru family, but if the villagers who received their favor hear that the master has returned home safely after three years, the old men and old women will surely come with their grandchildren to offer their congratulations. The gate will suddenly become a market, and the voices of joy will be like the incoming tide. The surroundings of the mansion will build a mountain of people, and the flowers of joy will bloom. Watching the bustle of the spring of Tokoyo and the auspicious signs, I feel as if I am seeing it before my eyes. My heart is encouraged, my chest leaps, my body feels as if it is standing in the void, and my heavy feet advance lightly without my knowing.

Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara. Blessed by the grace of the Kami, this mansion, which was sinking in sorrow, and the scene that was like hell, will instantly change into a heaven of eternal spring. It will be a great joy of drinking and singing. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara. Reverencing the grace of the Kami, I offer my thanks from afar. Even if the morning sun shines or clouds over, even if the moon waxes or wanes, even if the earth should sink, helped by the grace of the Kami, my master has returned safely, healthily, and with a smile. I shall never forget that high favor in any age. We who serve as servants, rejoicing in the grace of the Kami,

shall drive out the demons of the heart, and on the one path of sincerity, with all our hearts, morning and evening, we shall serve for the sake of the master. With all our strength and to the limit of our bodies, we shall serve with sincerity. O Great Kami, please protect us. I pray before your presence.'

Kāru sings again.

Kāru: 'Though it is said that the transience of life is the way of the world, it is pitiful. The master, known as the greatest man of wealth in the village of Suma, enjoyed nothing but fishing morning and evening. Whenever he had leisure, he would row his boat, cast his net into the vast ocean, catch many fish of all sizes, and give them to the villagers without a hint of regret. Hearing the voices of the villagers clicking their tongues and saying it is delicious, he forgot his family business, thinking this was his only pleasure. His technique of fishing, which had become solidified, developed more and more, and he was nicknamed the Kami of Fishermen. He would startle the fish of the purifying sea like a demon.'

§ 2

Becoming exceedingly proud, he applied himself more and more to fishing, until at last, bewitched by an Akuma, he encountered a record-breaking Shike. Swallowed up along with his boat by the Aranami that surged in like mountains, he drifted, sinking and rising, until he reached Shiyōjiyōjima. Saved by the protection of Kami, he endured three years of sorrow, and was sent back home by the officials of the Ananai-kyō. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. How grateful I am for the blessings of Kami. My master, Bāchiru-dono, from now on, please reform your heart. Even if they are but the lowest of the Giyozoku, do not ever cause suffering to the Ikimono that enjoy the blessings of Tenchi and live in Iuiu. It goes without saying for Kinjiuchiugiyo, and even down to the Mushikera and Kusaki, all are Mitamamono in which Kamisama dwells and bestows Ikumitama. Do not commit Mueki Setsushiyō. I, your servant Kāru, humbly and with sincerity, offer this remonstrance for the sake of your house. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. May your spirit be blessed.

Approaching the forest of the mansion, the treetops were lush and green, waiting for the return of the master. In the spacious garden, the peony flowers offered their charms and smiled, while the peonies with their jaws unhinged moved their tongues, flapping them in the wind, waiting impatiently for the celebratory sake, their throats rumbling in anticipation. The flock of sparrows that had built their nests between the roofs chirped, celebrating the prosperity of the house.

The Kakitsubata flowers blooming at the eaves were fragrant, and the Shōbu swords fluttered, brandishing their blades as they stood guard. Truly, this day is auspicious, for the master has returned from the Yūmeikai, resurrected. Just as Urashima, who traveled far to the Ryūgū-jō and was brought back on the back of a turtle, received the Tamatebako from Otohime-sama and returned home, there is no way to compare the joy of this moment. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. I offer my gratitude to Kami.
