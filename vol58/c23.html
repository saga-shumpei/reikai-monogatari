Chapter 23: 昼餐 [1498]

昼餐〔1498〕

§ 1

Tamakuniwake and his party received a courteous reception, exchanged cups of Miki, and as host and guests relaxed, they mutually expressed gratitude for the Shinon and enlivened the gathering by singing Shiyukuka. Tamakuniwake took up his cup, offered it to his host Bachiru, poured the sake himself, and sang with a Yukuwaige air.

Tamakuniwake: 'In the forest of the Kami of Mount Azumosu, where the Chigi stand high upon the Shitatsuihane, and the Ōmiyabashira are firmly set, the Sushin who dwells in stillness, the Jizaiten who is the Lord God, the Ōkami of Ōkunihiko, in the forest where He is enshrined, Shiyōjiyōhiko and Shiyōjiyōhime have appeared as messengers of the Kami. By what Suguse or Innen it came to be, the deep Shisai is unknown, but the Inochi of the precious Tama-no-o, belonging to your father, Bachiru-sama, was taken away. Having lost the Jiushiyo of their Seirei, they entered the body of the heir, Bachiru-sama, and settled there in eternal firmness. Loving the people of the village of Suma from the bottom of their hearts, and praised as noble persons, they live purely in this world. Guided by the living spirit of Shiyōjiyōhiko and the Seirei of Shiyōjiyōhime, they boarded a boat without a shelf on the vast ocean, and whenever they had leisure, they enjoyed the work of fishing. Drawn by the rope of Innen, they drifted to the Island of Shiyōjiyō, where the spirit couple met, grew intimate with each other, and spent three years on the uninhabited island—what a mystery it is.'

The party of Tamakuniwake, the propagandists of the San-go-kyo, are the pillars of the Kami of Ōkuninushi who appeared in the capital of India. Following the imperial command of the Ōkami of the Mizumitama, the Kami Susanoo, to guide and harmonize the land of India and rebuild it as a divine land where the morning sun shines, they endured a hundred troubles and traveled far. While stopping at various sacred sites and establishing merits, they unexpectedly and unknowingly encountered the divine mansion of Terumon, where they met Sanzenhiko. There, they rejoiced and celebrated the reunion of teacher and disciple, and happily gave thanks before the presence of the Imperial Kami.

The party of five descended the steep slope and finally reached the northern beach. Just as they were gazing at the waves at the edge of the pier of the vast, drifting sea, they entrusted themselves to a boat that came sliding in as if shooting an arrow, and as they proceeded far along the sea route, Wax, who harbored resentment, emerged from the bottom of the boat with many comrades, pressing in to take revenge.

At the critical moment of 'Swa!', as the party tightened their belts, they were saved by a boat that came racing, sliding over the waves. Naming it Hatsuwakamaru, they followed the path of the Great Mahito who had gone forth riding on the back of a dog upon the waves. As they proceeded day and night upon the sea, the Island of Sin lay to their left. Looking closely, they saw five men at the edge of the shore, and seeing them arguing, they thought there must be some Shisai, so they rowed the boat closer to land to investigate the situation.

While rescuing the five men, they hoisted the true sails again and proceeded toward the south. As they did so, a pirate ship appeared in their path, forming a horizontal ladder formation and surrounding Hatsuwakamaru. The sight was terrifying. Yakkosu, whom they had rescued into the boat, faced the pirate ship and cried out, 'I am your leader, the inspector of the Brahman religion, Yakkosu-tsukasa! Retreat at once!' It was amusing how he cleverly staged such a sham performance. Thereupon, the many pirate ships turned their prows to the north and fled to parts unknown. Avoiding the reefs that dotted the sea route to the right and left,

The boat drifted into the current and flowed steadily toward the southwest. Wondering if it were clouds, mist, or perhaps a mountain, they were suspicious of the black shadow visible in the distance, questioning if it were the Island of Shiyōjiyō. As they gradually approached and looked, Bachiru-sama was standing there, surrounded by many small monkeys, together with Shiyōjiyōhime. Recognizing this as truly the child of man, they rescued him onto their boat. Just as they were returning leisurely, Shiyōjiyōhime, seeing the infant she held in her arms, cruelly strangled it, and throwing herself into the sea, she pitifully vanished like sea foam. We all looked back, mourning the emotions of the princess, and spent our time in tears of sorrow.

Leaving Yakkosu, who was the inspector of the Brahman religion and also a pirate, along with Haru and Saboru, those three men, on this Monkey Island, we returned while singing boat songs loudly, drifting with the waves. Again, there was an island on the left. Without knowing it, our boat approached the shadow of the island. When we looked up and gazed carefully, a natural cave was bored into the center of the steep rocks, and there, the shadow of a strange person was beckoning toward us, as if crying for rescue. Overcoming the surging waves, we rowed the boat close to the spot. When we approached and looked, it was Anchi-sama, the servant of the house, whom Bachiru had relied upon as his trusted dagger.

§ 2

They leaped out in joy, rescued Hatsuwakamaru, and while hoisting the true sails to the favorable wind, they steered the bow of the ship toward the northeast, setting their sights on the shores of Suma.

Just as they were returning, the pirates who called themselves priests of Brahman led eight ships and blocked their path with persistence.

Wishing to scatter them with the power of Kami, they recited the Amatsunorito and unleashed the Kototama of rigor; perhaps fearing that divine virtue, the pirates broke their ranks and fled.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara.

With the divine wind blowing at that moment, they steered the oars and paddles with strength, and upon arriving at the shore of Suma, they saw Akisu and Karu, the two of them, waiting with great joy.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara.

The wonder of the divine Shigumi is such that the Shiyōjiyōhime had already arrived at this mansion before them, possessing the body of the Princess Saber, who resides as the mistress of the house.

She awaits the return of Shiyōjiyōhiko, who is her husband in the realm of spirit, to his living shrine.

Such a precedent is a miracle unheard of even in the age of the gods, when the heavens were shaken.

With spirits and spirits, flesh and flesh, the two pairs of husband and wife were reunited, settled safely in the mansion, served well in the great path of Kami, loved the villagers, and let the dew of blessings fall forever; Kamunagara, I pray, swearing to Kami.

Bachiru: 'Taking the place of the people of the age of the gods, when the heavens were shaken, the first marriage of humans is now performed. Since I hear that all people are descendants of the Shiyōjiyō, it feels exactly like the age of the gods.'

Itahiko: 'Humans are humans and beasts are beasts; there has been a distinction in their physical bodies since ancient times. Nevertheless, the soul received from Kami must be the same for both humans and the Shiyōjiyō.'

Saberhime: 'I shall serve in all things according to the will of Kami, for I am a body saved by Tamakuniwake.'

Tamakuniwake: 'It is not the achievement of the priest of Tamakuniwake, but entirely the protection of the Sumerakami.'

Masumihiko: 'Well then, let us cut short this banquet and hold a divine festival before the presence of rigor.'

Tamakuniwake: 'You, the master of the divine priest's lodging, serve together in the festival of rigor.'

At last, they finished the banquet, purified their bodies, changed their sweat-stained clothes, and began preparations for the festival of thanksgiving.
