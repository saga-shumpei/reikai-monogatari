Appendix: About the Reikai Monogatari

附記　霊界物語について
Zuigetsu, Deguchi Onisaburo（瑞月　出口王仁三郎）
English Translation • § 1

The Reikai Monogatari (Tales of the Spirit World) is projected to be completed in a grand total of one hundred and twenty volumes. However, to believe that one cannot possibly comprehend the divine workings and intricate spiritual realities across the Three Realms—the Divine Realm (Shin), the Astral Realm (Yū), and the Manifest Realm (Gen)—without reading through the entirety of such a vast and monumental body of writing is indeed a most grievous misconception. In reading and deciphering sacred scripture, if one but thoroughly savors, meditates upon, and takes deeply into one's very soul the single opening section, the entire spirit of the teachings can be clearly and fully comprehended. No matter what manner of person it may be, one can discern their true inner substance and heart from the very first glance. With a sword, if one but eases open the scabbard mouth (koiguchi) by a single inch to peer at the blade, one can instantly tell whether it is a treasured masterpiece or a blunt instrument; and with a serpent, if one sees but three inches of its form, one can already gauge the nature of the entire creature. Just as in the Chinese Classic of Poetry (Shijing), all subsequent chapters are encompassed within the opening “Zhounan” (Shūnan) section, and the “Zhounan” section itself is wholly embodied within its very opening line—“Guan-guan cry the fish-hawks, on the islet in the river”—so too with this book: if one reads with depth but a single focal point within this First Volume, the foundational spirit of all the volumes ought to be understood. Indeed, from merely the three stanzas of the Fundamental Propagation Song presented at the beginning of this book, the grand spirit of the whole can be fully realized. Even in the ten thousand volumes of the Ofudesaki (sacred automatic writings) left behind by the Foundress (Nao Deguchi), in that very first revelation given at the inception:

English Translation • § 2

“The plum blossoms that bloom across the three thousand worlds all at once—the age of Ushitora no Konjin (the Golden Deity of the Northeast) has arrived! Sitting upon Mount Sumeru, the living God of the origin of the world manifests outwardly to guard and protect the three thousand worlds. God comes to the fore and levels the measure so that there is no bias of high and low, fortunate or unfortunate; reforming the souls of all gods, Buddhas, and peoples of the world, God shall reconstruct and renew the cosmos into the Age of Miroku (Maitreya), presenting it before heaven and earth... and so forth.”

English Translation • § 3

Through this single supreme divine revelation, the entirety of the profound Divine Plan (Go-keirin) and the sublime will of the Great God governing the universe can be completely comprehended. Even in the Christian Bible, if one but takes deeply into one's soul the fundamental scriptures—“God created the world; and in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was God, and the Word was with God, and all things were made by Him”—the entire spirit coursing through the Bible can be readily understood in the same manner. For example, when a single morning glory hangs upon the tokobashira alcove pillar inside a tranquil tea room, depending on the beholder's perception and spiritual intuition, not only the interior of the tea room but the entire universe between heaven and earth is filled with the sacred beauty and spiritual essence of that flower, such that the whole cosmos transforms and embodies the morning glory. All phenomena and all things are such that through the individual part, the universal whole can be fully absorbed and comprehended. What the Buddhist Avatamsaka Sutra (Kegon-kyō) proclaims as “Ten Billion Realms in a Single Flower” bears precisely the meaning that within a single particle of dust, the entire three thousand worlds are fully encompassed. When standing from such an exalted perspective (the realization that the one is the all, and the all is the one), no matter how immense and voluminous this work of Reikai Monogatari may be, it is in truth nothing more than an extended commentary upon a single chapter. When one attains the state of the celestial beings dwelling within the Inmost Heaven (the supreme heavenly realm), their wisdom and enlightened intuitive perception far surpass those of celestial beings in lesser realms; thus, truths that celestial beings in other realms cannot fully comprehend even after reading vast treatises numbering millions of words, the beings of the Inmost Heaven immediately realize in all their profound, wondrous, and subtle reality from a mere word or two. In essence, it is for the sake of those mortals whose spirituality has not yet attained the state of the celestial beings of the First Heaven that, in obedient accordance with the Divine Will, I have authored such a lengthy, monumental narrative. That all readers may graciously comprehend this underlying divine purpose, I offer this concluding word here.
