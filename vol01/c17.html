Chapter 17: Journey through the Divine Realm (Part 4)

第一七章　神界旅行の四〔一七〕
English Translation • § 1

Just as the scene of the Divine Realm suddenly transformed, I found myself standing once again by the foot of the original great bridge. All of a sudden, the sound of the Great Purification Prayer (Oharae-no-Kotoba) echoed from out of nowhere. Puzzled by this, I walked about two or three hundred yards, where I saw an old man of about fifty and a woman of about forty stuck together back-to-back, writhing in struggle unable to separate. The man was shouting the sacred name of 'Tenchi Kane no Kami' at the top of his lungs, while the woman was desperately joining her palms in prayer to Inari. In the sky toward which the man was praying, a long-nosed tengu appeared in the clouds, beckoning the old man. Looking in the direction the woman was worshiping, foxes and badgers were beckoning frantically from within the mountains. Whenever the man tried to go forward, his back was suctioned tightly to the woman’s back so he could not proceed; whenever the woman tried to go, she squirmed and struggled, yet could not advance because their backs were stuck fast together. Taking two steps in one direction only to step back, then taking two steps in the other direction only to step back again, they were hopelessly lost in delusion due to their conflicting faiths. I stepped forward to them, prayed to the Kami 'Kamunagara Tamachihaemase' (Yield Thine divine grace in accordance with the divine will), and recited a norito. At that moment, I felt as though an exceedingly cool, pure voice flowed forth from within myself.

So they promised. Soon, the man, in his physical body, sought to ascend to the earthly Takama-ga-hara and take part in the divine work. However, because he was inherently of a covetous nature and the possessing tengu spirit had not dispersed, he ultimately became a vassal of Banko-Daijin (Pan Gu) and plotted to seize earthly Takama-ga-hara; consequently, his spirit suffered divine condemnation and fell into Hell, and his physical body perished two years later. English Translation • § 7

So he offered. But saying, 'Being in spiritual training for the Divine Realm, there is no reason for me to ride in a carriage,' I firmly refused and walked steadfastly westward on foot. Crossing over three or four extremely steep mountain passes, I eventually arrived beside a broad, crystal-clear river. Pure, clear water flowed in the river, and ancient green pines lined the banks, forming a truly magnificent scenic vista. I felt that this indeed was the Divine Realm, and that I would love to stay in such a place for a long time. Trudging forward alone, I came upon a small town. Looking to the left, there was a small hill, with purple mountains and the river flowing like a silk sash; whether to describe it as atop a lotus pedestal or as the center of Takama-ga-hara, I was captivated by its scenery for a while, hesitating to depart.

English Translation • § 8

Descending the mountain and advancing a little to the north, I found a small house. Drawn as if by electricity, I was immediately standing at the gate. There, wondrously, the Great King from the Astral Tribunal had transformed into the appearance of a very young woman to welcome me, and presently guided me into a small sitting room. Rejoicing at this reunion with the Great King, I was listening to various rare and wondrous stories, when suddenly a sound like the roaring of tigers and the howling of wolves could be heard. Straining my ears, I realized it was the chanting of the Amatsu-Norito and the Oharae-no-Kotoba! Along with those voices, the surroundings gradually darkened; dense clouds rolled in like a thick fog, sunlight became completely invisible, and a gale abruptly arose—a horrific scene as if threatening to topple houses and blow away all things upon the earth. From within those churning black clouds appeared an old-faced demon named 'Ashi' (Foot). Accompanied by an ancient fox named 'Kuro' (Black), he glared down haughtily at the lower realm. At that moment, the river waters roared thunderously, and a colossal dragon emerged from the midst of the river, while from who knows where, indescribable demons appeared. The Great King’s sitting room and its vicinity became entirely pitch-black, dark to the point where one could not even see an inch ahead, and the figure of that gentle Great King was swallowed up into the dark. All that met the eye was a single faint light flickering as if about to be extinguished in the ferocious wind. Inwardly realizing that now was the time to pray to the Kami, I single-mindedly concentrated on 'Amaterasu-Ōmikami' and the 'Ubusuna-no-Kami', serenely chanting a norito in a clear and refreshing voice. Instantly, the whole heaven cleared, leaving not a single speck of cloud.

English Translation • § 9

Norito are sacred divine utterances that pacify the hearts of the Kami and bring harmony between heaven, earth, and humanity. However, only when their kotodama (spiritual power of words) is harmonious, pure, and clear can they dispel all defilement and evil. When chanted from the mouths of demons, the world on the contrary becomes all the more disordered and degenerate. This is because the kotodama used by demons has no power to cleanse the world, and being contaminated by evil passions such as covetousness, jealousy, hatred, envy, and wrath, it wounds the sacred hearts of the Kami of heaven and earth. Therefore, even though Japan is the land blessed by kotodama, only when a person whose body and soul have become truly pure uses that kotodama can they cleanse society. In contrast, if a person whose soul and body are defiled uses kotodama, since that utterance contains all manner of evil elements, the world is plunged into even deeper darkness.

English Translation • § 10

Now, when I returned to the Crossroads of Heaven (Yachimata), the evil spirits of the demon, fox, and giant dragon from earlier pursued me. The demon 'Ashi' brought along numerous subordinates this time, attacking me from all eight directions, and spewed countless hundreds of thousands of needles like a mist from each of their mouths! English Translation • § 11

Here, let me add a brief note of explanation. The demon named 'Ashi' was clad in eboshi (court cap) and hitatare (ceremonial robe), dressing just like someone in divine service. Although he inherently possessed an extremely ferocious countenance, he had disguised himself in an apparently respectable appearance. Moreover, the dragon that had risen from the river quickly transformed into a beautiful woman. This Dragon Maiden had received a grave mission from the Dragon Palace Realm and was a soul destined to participate in the Great God’s providence of world reformation; yet, transforming into a woman of beautiful flesh, she entered into a physical relationship with the demon 'Ashi', utterly ruining her divine mission. That physical body into which the Dragon Maiden had transformed is currently surviving, serving the Kami across the river. As proof that she is a Dragon Maiden, three dragon scales have formed upon her thigh. Because the providence of the Divine Realm governs all three realms, its retribution unfailingly manifests; therefore, violating a Dragon Maiden charged with a divine mission incurs the eternal condemnation of the Kami, both in the spirit world and the manifest world. By that divine punishment, one of 'Ashi's sons was born deaf, and a daughter developed ammonite-like scars (chrysanthemum stones) all over her face, leaving traces like an ugly crawling dragon. Soon, the daughter died first, followed by the death of the son; for his sins, he was kicked down into a ravine by Kunitokotachi-no-Mikoto, shattering his sternum, so that both spirit and body perished. Thus the mortal body of 'Ashi' also received divine chastisement: wasting away day by day, falling into severe financial destitution, he contracted pulmonary tuberculosis and died in agony.

English Translation • § 12

The aforementioned son and daughter were children from 'Ashi's former wife; however, a son was also born between the Dragon Maiden and the demon 'Ashi'. Having lost two of his children, 'Ashi' sought to establish this boy as his heir. The Dragon Maiden, too, was anxious to make him the heir to her own household. On the other hand, the Dragon Maiden had strict parents, who refused to let go of the boy, wanting him to be the heir to their family line. Trying to seize him by force, the demon 'Ashi' tore the single child's body into two halves and killed him! In the manifest world, the child who had thus been torn apart and died in the spiritual realm agonized between his parents, thinking: 'If I side with father, it is unfair to mother; if I side with mother, it is unfair to father,' and as a result of that deep torment, contracted pulmonary tuberculosis and died. In this manner, the demon 'Ashi' suffered the complete annihilation of his family line in both spirit and flesh, while the Dragon Maiden still lives a lonely widow's life today without any heir.

English Translation • § 13

By nature, a Dragon Maiden is one who undergoes a thousand years of ascetic discipline in the freezing cold and boiling heat of the ocean, another thousand years amidst the mountains, and another thousand years in rivers before finally being born into the human realm. In the very first human life after reincarnating from a dragon body, she must either become a nun or dedicate herself to divine service—in either case, severing all sexual relations with men and leading a sacred, pure life. If this taboo is violated, three thousand years of arduous discipline dissolve like bubbles on water, and she falls back into a dragon form once more. Consequently, Dragon Maidens take no pleasure in relations with men, are remarkable beauties, have piercing eyes, and occasionally retain the traces of a few scales somewhere upon their bodies. If, through human sentiment, obligation, or personal sympathy, one forces such a Dragon Maiden into violation or causes her to be violated, such people incur the deep wrath of the Dragon Deity and cannot escape vengeance. Typically, whenever a Dragon Maiden is violated, that marital union will never endure in safety: the husband usually dies prematurely, and however many times the woman changes spouses, she repeats the same tragedy, while the violator must suffer the curse and retribution of the Dragon Deity unto generations.
