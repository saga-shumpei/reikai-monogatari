Volume 1 • Part 5: Contention for the Sacred Jewels (御玉の争奪)

Chapter 43: The Red-Crowned Crane

第四三章　丹頂の鶴〔四三〕
English Translation • § 1

Ever since acquiring the red jewel of the Golden Water, Tsuruwaka's faith had grown unshakable; climbing Mount Zion in secret, he underwent years of arduous spiritual training—now plunging into the waterfall of Zion, now purifying his body in the mountain torrents—until he could manifest supernatural powers at will. True to his name, Tsuruwaka could transform into a crane soaring through the skies, ascending and descending between heaven and earth, wholeheartedly pursuing his severe austerities in his quest to become an angel of the divine realm.

現代日本語訳
鶴若（つるわか）は黄金水の精である赤色の玉を手に入れてより、信仰の信念がいよいよ強固となり、密かにシオンの山に登って多年の厳しい修行を積みました。ある時はシオンの滝壺に飛び込み、ある時はシオンの渓流で禊を行って、ついには神通力を自由自在に発揮できるようになりました。鶴若はその名の通り鶴の姿に変じて大空を飛翔し、天地の間を自在に往来して、神界の天使となろうと一意専心に苦行を重ねていました。

English Translation • § 2

Meanwhile, Takekuma's wicked faction plotted tirelessly to rob Tsuruwaka of his supernatural power and bring him down to earth. Soaring across the skies with lightning speed, Tsuruwaka flew across various lands; before him loomed a high mountain draped in purple clouds, its snowy summit rising pristine above the cloud banks. Drawn irresistibly to that peak, Tsuruwaka alighted upon the mountain crest. Just then, radiant beams illuminating the four quarters burst from the purple clouds on the mountainside, rising toward the summit and condensing into a single crimson jewel. Tsuruwaka had now resumed his form as a majestic divine man. Before his eyes, the jewel parted to the left and right, and an ethereal maiden emerged. As Tsuruwaka gazed entranced by her beauty, the maiden cast tender glances at him and silently approached his side. This mountain was Mount Altai, and the maiden was named Tsuruhime. Tsuruwaka and Tsuruhime pledged their marital vows; at that very instant, Tsuruwaka lost his supernatural power and could no longer fly through the skies.

English Translation • § 3

In the mountainside lay a colossal cavern. The two made this cavern their dwelling, gathered inhabitants from the surrounding mountains, and established a kingdom with this sanctuary as its center. Enshrining the red jewel deep within the cavern, they revered it as their peerless sacred treasure. Loving and cherishing one another, they spent long years upon Mount Altai.

English Translation • § 4

However, as years passed, their youthful vigor faded; their bodies withered, their hair turned white, and an unspoken loneliness crept over them. They began to yearn intensely for a child to succeed them.

English Translation • § 5

Thereupon Tsuruorihime, an agent of Takekuma, transformed into a fair angel and ascended Mount Altai, unleashing thunder and torrential rain. The cataract-like deluge washed away a flank of the mountain, flooding the front of the cavern; when the rain ceased, a soft, lovely jewel of flower-like blossom hue was left lying before the entrance. Tsuruwaka picked it up, finding it as soft as freshly pounded mochi. Seeing this, Tsuruhime was seized by an overwhelming craving to eat it; attempting to snatch it from Tsuruwaka's hands, the jewel was torn in two, and each half slid naturally into their mouths and down into their stomachs. Thereupon carnal desire was awakened within them; Tsuruhime conceived, and when her months were fulfilled, she gave birth to a jewel-like baby girl, whom they named Tsurukohime.

English Translation • § 6

The two gave birth to Tsurukohime, showering her with unbounded devotion; with all the doting tenderness of parental affection—hoping the crawling babe might soon stand, and once standing, take her first steps—their hearts stirred at every tear and smile she made, ready to sacrifice everything without regret for the sake of their child. Under the loving nurture of her parents, Tsurukohime gradually grew and, beginning to utter speech, cried out for the first time: 'Ta-ta!' Her parents were troubled by this cry and racked their minds over what 'Ta-ta!' could possibly mean, yet they could not fathom its significance at all. Next, Tsurukohime cried out: 'Ma-ma!' What this could mean was likewise completely incomprehensible. After a while, Tsurukohime began to cry out: 'Tama, tama (Jewel, jewel)!' Hearing this and wondering if she meant eggs (tamago), her parents ordered their retainers to gather the eggs of various birds; however, Tsurukohime shook her head vigorously from side to side, refusing to suck on the eggs. Day and night the parents sat knee to knee, tilting their heads and pondering what on earth Tsurukohime could mean by 'Tama,' yet they could not figure it out no matter how they tried. At length, the parents gathered tens of thousands of retainers and conducted a solemn festival at the Shrine of the Jewel (Tama-no-Miya), where the Red Jewel was enshrined, offering fervent prayers for Tsurukohime's safe growth. On that occasion, Tsurukohime attended the sacred festival grounds held tenderly in Tsuruhime's embrace. Thereupon Tsurukohime broke into a bright smile for the first time, rejoicing with delight and saying: 'Akatama, akatama (Red jewel, red jewel)!' Seeing the smile of their beloved child Tsurukohime—whom they loved so dearly she would not hurt even in their eyes—her parents felt their hearts swell with supreme joy; such was their love that if it were Tsurukohime's desire, they would gladly forfeit their own lives without the slightest regret. The festival concluded without mishap, and the two returned to the living quarters of their vast cavern. The tens of thousands of retainers grew merry on the sacred banquet wine, shouting cheers of 'Banzai!' again and again as they dispersed to their respective dwellings. Afterward, the family of three retired into an inner chamber and fell peacefully asleep. In the middle of the night, however, Tsurukohime suddenly began crying out: 'Tama, tama!' Hearing this, Tsuruhime realized the meaning for the first time, perceiving that Tsurukohime's cry of 'Tama, tama' could mean nothing other than a longing for that sacred Red Jewel; she whispered this revelation to Tsuruwaka. Tsuruwaka started up from his couch, crossing his arms in deep contemplation, hanging his head without uttering a single word. Tsurukohime's cries grew increasingly frantic, piercing her parents' hearts as though tearing them apart. Unable to bear it any longer, heedless of the midnight hour, Tsuruwaka arose and entered the Shrine of the Jewel; reverently carrying the sacred Red Jewel in both hands, he solemnly placed it upon the table in the living chamber. Instantly Tsurukohime's weeping ceased, turning into joyful laughter; touching the jewel with her little hands, she danced and hopped about it in radiant delight. The parents left the sacred jewel resting upon the floor just as it was, turning it into a plaything to humor Tsurukohime's whim.

English Translation • § 7

Tsurukohime grew rapidly; then one day, she suddenly transformed into a ferocious black dragon, seized the jewel, summoned black clouds and thunderbolts, and with a deafening roar abandoned her parents, vanishing high into the western skies! Left behind, the parents were stunned with horror and wept without end. Though they realized Tsurukohime was an incarnation of an evil spirit and resigned themselves to the loss of their child, they could never reconcile themselves to the loss of the sacred red jewel. Having once endured Takekuma's abuse and defended it at the risk of their lives, to have lost it to the enemy left them without an excuse before Ōyashimahiko-no-Mikoto; weeping bitterly to heaven and earth, their anguished grief condensed upon their crowns, leaving a scarlet stain on their heads. This was the origin of the red-crowned crane (Tanchō-no-Tsuru). That there is no love surpassing that of cranes for their offspring originates from this profound causality.

現代日本語訳
鶴子姫はこうして成長しましたが、ある日突然その姿を黒竜へと変じ、その玉を咥えるや否や黒雲を巻き起こして雷雨を呼び、轟音とともに父母を捨てて西の空高く姿を消してしまいました。後に残された二人は驚き呆れ、失われた玉と愛児の行方を見つめて嘆き悲しみました。二人は鶴子姫が悪神の化身であったことを悟り、我が子については諦めがついたものの、どうしても諦めきれないのはあの赤玉でした。かつて竹熊らの迫害に耐え、命に代えて守護した神宝を敵に奪われては、大八洲彦命に対して何の申し訳も立たないと天地に向かって号泣しました。その痛切な一念が凝り固まり、頭上に赤玉の痕跡を留めるに至ったのです。これこそが「丹頂（たんちょう）の鶴」の起源であります。「夜の鶴」「焼野の雉子」と称され、子を愛すること鶴に勝るものなしと言われるのも、実にこの因縁によるものなのであります。
原文（近代文語）
鶴子姫はかくてだんだんと成長したが、ある日たちまち其の姿を黒竜と変じ、その玉をとるや否や、黒雲を捲きおこし雷雨をよび、大音響とともに、父母を捨て、西方の空高く姿を隠してしまつた。後に残りしふたりは驚き呆れ、かつ玉と愛児の行方を眺めて長嘆止まなかつた。ふたりは鶴子姫が邪神の霊の変化なりしことを悟りて、姫の身については断念せるものの、断念め切れぬのはかの赤玉である。かつて竹熊らの侮辱圧迫にたへ、生命にかへて守護したる、かの宝玉を敵に奪はれては、大八洲彦命にたいして一言の申訳なしと、天地にむかつて号泣し、その一念凝つて、頭上に赤玉の痕をとどむるにいたつた。これを丹頂の鶴といふのである。焼野の雉子、夜の鶴、児を愛すること鶴に優るものなきも、これが縁由である。
