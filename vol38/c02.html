Chapter 2: 吉崎仙人 [1039]

吉崎仙人〔1039〕

§ 1

In a small village called Oyogi in Higashiyatamura, Ikarugagun, Tanba, where Mount Misenzan is located—the mountain that enshrines Konohanasakuyahimenomikoto, who has deep Innen with Ōmoto—there lives a mysterious man named Kanekichi Yoshizaki, who calls himself Tsukumosennin.

Ever since he encountered a strange old man with white hair in the mountains at the age of seven and was taught various Shinpi, his Genkau changed suddenly; day and night, he writes Fudesaki in a Kanakugiriu style using wood chips or the ends of bamboo, and he considers it his lifelong heavenly vocation to receive commands from Ōkamisama at the first horse-riding ground of the heavenly shrine and to propagate the divine edicts of Ōkamisama to the gods of heaven and earth, though he is regarded as a madman by his relatives, brothers, and villagers, and no one will deal with him; yet, undeterred, the Sennin says that the Fudesaki he writes is not for the sake of preaching to people of the present age who do not understand its meaning, but for conveying the heart of Ōkamisama to the gods of the universe, so there is no reason why a person, in their capacity as a human, should be able to understand even a single sheet of what he has written.

From around the age of twenty-five or twenty-six, he left his hometown of Oyogi and hid in the deep mountains of Kuchitanba village, making his living as a woodcutter; he would work only enough to feed himself, and as soon as he had saved even a little rice or salt, he would seclude himself in a hut in the mountains until it was mostly gone, writing divine edicts with a brush made by crushing the tip of a bamboo stick against a split board, and he would choose a sunny spot, stand them diagonally facing the vast sky, and leave them to be exposed to the sun.

The Fudesaki written by that Sennin has a very interesting connection when compared with the Ofudesaki of the founder of Ōmoto.

The gist of those Fudesaki is roughly as follows: 'The world until today has been a world where we, the evil gods and the like, could act freely and run rampant, but now that the heavenly fortune has cycled and the world of our Great Freedom sect has come to an end, we must flow the world toward Ōmoto in Ayabe and hand over all rights of the divine world to Ushitora no Konjin.'

Many things to that effect are written in them. Also, the Innen of the founder Deguchi from the ancient age of the gods is roughly described.

The spirit of this Tsukumosennin appeared at the Yūsai training ground in Uetani, possessed eighteen-year-old Haruzō Shikata, and made him take up the brush to write: 'Since all matters of the divine world of this world must be handed over to Ōmoto in Ayabe, have the teacher of spiritual science who has appeared this time, Mr. Adachi, and Haruzō Shikata come here immediately.'

Thus, he wrote a letter of request fluently through the hand of Shikata. Kiraku thought he would study it for the sake of reference in spiritual science and intended to travel to the Sennin in the deep mountains of Kuchitanba the very next day, but as an urgent matter arose in Ayabe, he had to return. While he was doing so, on the third day, past noon, an old man named Yūsuke Shikata came flying in a panic from the training ground in Uetani to Ōmoto.

Yūsuke: 'Ueda-sensei, a terrible thing has happened. Just now, Mr. Adachi and Haruzō-san conspired, hiding it from you, Ueda-sensei, to go and meet Tsukumosennin to have all the secret arts of the divine world bestowed upon them, and they said, "When we return, we will make Ueda-san dumbfounded. In any case, unless we receive ten-tenths of divine power, we cannot throw Ueda out. This is a great secret, so you must never let Ueda know..." and the two of them hurried off. Those two people going off on their own, hiding it from you, sensei, cannot be for any good. They must be plotting something bad again. Sensei, it will be terrible if you dally, so come, I will guide you to the entrance of the mountains of Kuchitanba, so please go and chase after the two of them now. Come, hurry, hurry!'

此九十九仙人の精霊が、上谷の幽斎修行場へ現はれて来て、当年十八才の四方春三に神懸し筆を取らして、

とスラスラと四方の手を通じて依頼文を書いた。そこで喜楽は霊学上の参考の為、一つ研究して見ようと思ひ、其翌日直様、口上林の山奥の仙人の許へ出張する筈であつたのが、折ふし綾部に急用が出来たので、帰らねばならなくなつた。さうしてゐると三日目の正午過に、上谷の修行場から四方祐助といふ老爺サンが慌だしく大本へ飛んで来て、

祐助『上田先生、大変なことが出来ました。今の先足立サンと春三サンが諜し合せ、上田先生にかくれて、九十九仙人に会見に行き、一切の神界の秘術を授けて貰ひ、帰つて来て、上田をアフンとさせてやらう、兎も角十分の神力を受けて居らねば、上田を放り出すことが出来ぬ。これは大秘密だから、決して上田には知らしてはならぬぞ……と云つて、二人があわてて出て行かはりました。あの人達二人が、先生に隠れて勝手に行くといふのは、何れ碌な事ぢやありますまい。又一つ何かよからぬことを企むのでせう。先生、グヅグヅして居つては大変ですから、サアこれから私が口上林の山の口まで御案内致しますから、今から二人の後を追つかけて行つて下さい、サア早よ早よ！』

He was urging him on. Thereupon, Kiraku immediately had an audience with the Kyōso, and when he reported the matter just as he had heard it, the Kyōso said, 'In that case, please go and meet the Sennin without a moment's delay, though it may be a hardship for you.'

Guided by Iusuke-san, he was seen off to a point about one ichiri short of Sugiyama, where the Sennin was said to reside. From there, he was taught the geography in detail by Iusuke-san, and after parting ways, he climbed up the winding, narrow path overgrown with weeds all by himself.

He followed the rough map he had drawn on a single sheet of paper, climbing and stumbling along the grass-deep, steep slope that he did not know the way through, while his heart pressed forward ahead of him. When he thought he had climbed about half a ri, there was a small, humble hut in the woods by the side of the road, and the voices of two or three people could be heard from within. As Kiraku stood still by the hut to catch his breath, not intending to listen, he heard the voice of an old man of over sixty years, 'If you are people who perform the work of the Lord God, why do you not know the obligations and human feelings of the world? How can you save the masses with such an attitude? With sincerity that is only lip-deep, your hearts and your actions are complete opposites. Far from saving the masses, you cannot even save yourselves. Are you begrudging a mere one or two sen? You are likely just religious merchants who think only of taking money from believers every single day, saying sweet things with your lips. Do you not know how to reward the labor of this old man? Once I have said that if the money is that precious to you, I do not want it, then even if I were to starve to death in these deep mountains, your money is filthy!'

The voice grew louder and louder as he scolded them. When he listened closely to the other, smaller voice, he realized it was the voice of Adachi Masanobu-san, whom he recognized.

Adachi: 'Hey, old man, do not tell such absurd lies. Even if you ask for money for the repair of the mountain path, how could that ever be done? Where has any repair been made? There is not a trace of a single weed having been cut, nor is there any sign that even a single patch of earth has been moved. Just a moment ago, I cut my foot on the pampas grass by the roadside, and I tripped on a high stone and tore my quick. To ask for repair fees from a traveler who has suffered this much is quite something. Unless I were a madman with money to spare, I would not give a single sen to a mountain bandit-like fellow, a fake old man of these mountains, even if I had money to throw into the abyss. There is a limit to how much you can make a fool of the people of the world. You are at a good age, so why do you not reform yourself? What is the meaning of acting like a beggar?'

He was mocking him right to his face. When Kiraku looked at the entrance of that humble hut, he saw a sign written:

'I am a pitiful, lonely person with no wife, children, relatives, or family. I am sixty-seven years old, and for the sake of those who travel to these deep mountains, I live here all year round, repairing the mountain paths and ensuring the convenience of those who pass by. If there are those who have compassion, please throw in a token of your heart, even if it is only one sen or half a sen, thinking of it as giving to a beggar... To the philanthropists of the world... Date... Master of the humble hut.'

Seeing the aforementioned sign, he was able to roughly infer the reason for the argument inside the hut that he had heard earlier. Kiraku was overjoyed to have met Adachi and Shikata in such a place, and he entered the hut directly.

Kiraku: 'Excuse me.'

He called out and entered, and to the old man, Kiraku said, 'Thank you for your hard work,' and gave him one ten-sen silver coin.

The old man did not look particularly happy, but looking at Kiraku, the old man said, 'Hmm, very well, pass through with your head held high.'

Having said only that one word, he stared at Kiraku's face as if to pierce a hole in it, but soon he slapped his own knee and nodded repeatedly, saying, 'Hmm, hmm.'

This old man was truly a mysterious being. Since this is a true story without fiction or embellishment, I would like the reader to pay attention here. In short, it was later understood that the guardian deity of the Kujūku Sennin had temporarily possessed this old man and tested the hearts of the three men.

Adachi and Shikata, the two men, were somewhat taken aback to see the figure of Kiraku, whom they had thought would surely not follow them, appear right before their eyes.

Adachi: 'Oh, is that you, Uheda-san? Where are you going all alone on this mountain path? I am in a bit of a hurry to go to a certain house in Kanbayashi, so why don't you just rest here at your leisure and listen to some interesting stories from the old man? What an old man says will be for your own good.'

Leaving these parting words behind, he hurriedly left the small hut and went up the mountain path with Shikata.

Just as Kiraku was about to follow them immediately, the old man pulled his sleeve.

Raujin: 'Wait a moment, I shall guide you through a shortcut.'

He puffed hard on his pipe for a puff or two, and said,

Raujin: 'Come, come, follow me this way.'

He took the lead, and contrary to his appearance as an old man, he walked with light steps, guiding Kiraku to the side of a mountain stream at the foot of Sugiyama where the Sennin was hiding.

Raujin: 'Now, cross this stream, take the right path, and proceed for a chō or two; that is where the Sennin's hideout is. Farewell...'

Having said this, he returned in haste.

Kiraku crossed the slippery, rapid mountain stream and hurried toward the woodcutter's hut. By the time he thought he had climbed five or six chō, the Tsukumo Sennin was standing in the middle of the slope waiting for him. Turning to Kiraku, his expression softened, and looking quite pleased, he said,

Sennin: 'Ah, Sensei, you have done well to visit me from so far away on this mountain path. Come, come, sit here and have a smoke.'

He guided him into his small hut, drew hot water from a black earthenware teapot, and offered it to him, spending the entire night explaining and instructing him in various secrets of the divine world. Kiraku recalled the facts he had been shown from the divine world during his first and second periods of ascetic practice on Mount Takakuma, and feeling as if they matched like tally marks, his conviction grew ever stronger.

Kiraku had arrived safely at the hut of the Tsukumo Sennin thanks to the kind guidance of the old man at the small hut, and he was extremely satisfied after hearing various beneficial divine revelations. However, beginning to worry that the two men, Adachi and Shikata, had not appeared here even after a day had passed, he asked the Sennin,

Kiraku: 'The two of them should surely have come to visit here, but I wonder why they have not yet shown their faces. Could it be that they have wandered off into the deep mountains?'

The Sennin laughed and replied,

Sennin: 'Hahaha, they are black-hearted individuals who harbored great ambition and tried to get ahead of you to seize the important key to the mystery; therefore, they have been fenced off by the divine world so that they cannot possibly arrive here today. When tomorrow morning comes, they will likely arrive with their tails between their legs. There is no need for concern. The Father of the First Baba of the Heavenly Palace, the Father of the Second Baba of the Heavenly Palace, and the Kokuzoku Musashi Yoshizaki Kanekichi of the Third Baba of the Heavenly Palace are all protecting your body, and because it would be a hindrance if those two were present to receive this mystery, they are being delayed on purpose.'

He said this with a smile. Kiraku, having heard every word of the Sennin's speech, was astonished at how perfectly it matched the writings of the founder. He became prepared for the fact that he was shouldering a great responsibility toward the divine world, and his conviction became increasingly firm.

Meanwhile, the two men, in their attempt to get ahead of Kiraku, instead lost their way on the mountain path, misjudged their direction due to thick fog, and tumbled into the bottom of a deep ravine. They suffered scratches on various parts of their bodies, and after wandering about, they finally arrived back in front of the old man's hut. This time, they were yelled at by the old man until his eyes bulged, and while trembling, they apologized for their previous rudeness. Eventually, the old man's anger subsided, and thanks to his benevolent guidance, they finally arrived at an inn at the foot of Sugiyama around eleven o'clock at night. They stayed there for the night and climbed the mountain early the next morning. The two of them...

仙人『アハヽヽヽ、大変な野心を起し、お前さまを出しぬいて、大切なる神秘の鍵を握らうとした、腹の黒い人物だから、今日も到底ここへは来ることが出来ぬやうに、神界から垣をされてゐるのだから、明日の朝になつたら、ヤツとの事で来るであらう。憂慮するには及ばぬ。天のお宮の一の馬場のお父様も、天のお宮の二の馬場のお父様も、天のお宮の三の馬場の国族武蔵吉崎兼吉も、皆お前の体を守り、此神秘を伝へむ為に、彼等両人が居ると邪魔になるから、ワザとに遅れさして居るのだ』

といつて微笑して居る。喜楽は仙人の言を一伍一什聞き終り、余り教祖の筆先に符合せるに驚き、益々神界に対して一大責任の身にかかれることを覚悟し信念はますます堅くなつた。

一方の二人は喜楽の先を越さうとして、却て山路にふみ迷ひ、濃霧の為に方向を誤り、深い谷底へ転落し、身体の各所にすり傷さへも負ひ、迷ひ迷うて漸く又元の老人の小屋の前に到着し、今度は老人に目が剥けるほど呶鳴りつけられ、ブルブル震ひ乍ら、先の無礼を陳謝し、漸く老人の怒りも解け、老人の好意的案内に依つて、夜の十一時頃漸く杉山の麓の一軒の宿屋に着いた。其夜はそこで一泊し、翌日早朝登山して来たのである。二人は、

'I wonder if Kiraku-san thinks that because I have made such a great misunderstanding, the Shinkai has given me a Kizuke, but perhaps this too is some Shigumi of the Kamisama.'

As he had a nature that was strong in Makeoshi, he pretended to be Heumen-heiki, but on his face could be seen an Fuan-ketsusau that he could not hide. The Sennin turned toward Adachi and said with Genzen:

Sennin: 'Satsuki is appearing on your Menbu. Your Shinchiu is somehow Fuon. Return to the path of Kamunagara as soon as possible, and cease your unattainable Kito. If you do not Kaishin now, you will invite your own Hametsu.'

He said this strongly and decisively, and then turned again toward Shikata Haruzau:

Sennin: 'You are protected by the spirit of Banko. Your great ambition is very Omoshiro-kunai; it is just like a monkey trying to catch the moon in the water. If you do not change it now, you will surely come to ruin your own body. From this very moment, return to your conscience and serve the Shinkai with all your heart and sincerity. If you do so, you will be forgiven for the deep sins of your spirit from long ago, and you will be used for the glorious work of the Shinkai. However, with your current heart, it is no good. If you do not change quickly, the ominous signs of disaster that will soon befall you are appearing on your face. Do not ever doubt what this Sennin says.'

He said this in a reprimanding tone. The two men had pale faces and could not answer a single word, their bodies trembling violently. The Sennin spoke once more:

Sennin: 'The time has finally arrived, and my role ends today. From tomorrow, I shall descend to the human world, follow the duties of the human realm, and spend the rest of my life. My work in the divine realm ends today, so even if you come to visit me again, it will be no use. Farewell...'

Having said this, he threw his large saw over his shoulder and disappeared deep into the mountains, never to emerge again, so the three of them had no choice but to head home.

From this point on, Shikata Haruzau was possessed by the evil spirit of Banko, his wicked heart grew day by day, and he schemed to exclude Kiraku and win over many officials and believers to perform the work thereafter, making various miscellaneous plans. However, one year later, just as the Sennin had said, he suffered a terrible divine punishment and died in agony. Truly, what one must guard against is arrogance and misunderstanding.

Kamunagara Yaosakae Mase.

原文（近代文語）

『余り心得違を致したから、神界から、お気付をされたと喜楽サンは思はれるか知らぬが、これも何か神様のお仕組でせう』

と負惜みの強い性質とて、表面平気を装うてゐたが、其顔には隠し切れぬやうな不安な血相が見えてゐた。仙人は足立に向ひ厳然として、

仙人『お前の面部には殺気が現はれてゐる。何となく心中不穏だ。一時も早く惟神の道に立帰つて、及ばぬ企図を止めなさい。今改心せなくては身の破滅を招きますぞよ』

と言強く言ひ放ち、又もや四方春三に向ひ、

仙人『お前は盤古の霊が守護して居る。甚面白くない、お前の大望は、丁度猿猴が水の月を捉へむとするやうなものだ。今に改めなくてはキツと身を亡ぼすことが出て来るぞ。今日只今限り良心に立ち復り、一心に真心を以て神界に仕へなさい。さうすれば昔からの霊の深い罪科を赦された上、天晴れ神界の御用に使つて貰へるであらう。併し乍ら今の心では駄目だ。早く改めないと、災忽ち其身に至る凶徴が、お前の顔に現はれて居る。此仙人の云ふことをゆめゆめ疑ふこと勿れ』

ときめつける様に言つた。二人は真青な顔をして一言もよう答へず、体をビリビリと震はせて居た。仙人は更めて言ふ。

仙人『いよいよ時節到来して、自分の役目は今日を以て終りをつげた。明日からは人界へ下つて、人場の勤めに従ひ、余生を送りませう。神場の用は今日で終結だから、再び訪ねて来て貰つても最早駄目だ。左様なら……』

と云ひすて、大鋸を肩にひつかけ、山奥深く其姿を没した限り、出て来ないので、やむを得ず、三人は帰途に就いた。

これから以後の四方春三は盤古の悪霊に憑依され、邪心日に日に募りて喜楽を排斥し、其後の御用を勤めむと数多の役員信者を籠絡し、いろいろ雑多の計画を立てて居たが、一年たつた後に、仙人の云ふた如く、大変な神罰を蒙りて悶死するに至つた。実に慎むべきは慢心と取違とである。

惟神霊幸倍坐世。
