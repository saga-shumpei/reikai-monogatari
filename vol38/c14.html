Chapter 14: 沓 Island [1051]

沓島〔1051〕

§ 1

From Mahizuru in Tango and Obama in Wakasa, and from Miyazu where it is said that one's striped Saifu becomes empty, there lies an island in the Okinaka, exactly Jifuri away, where an ancient legend and superstition has long persisted: 'Men must visit this place at least once in their lifetime, but never twice; for women, it is strictly forbidden, and should a woman ever make a pilgrimage, she shall incur the wrath of Otohime-sama of the Riugū, causing the Kaijiyō to grow tempestuous, various Eukuwai to emerge, and many Orochi to appear and swallow the woman whole, and furthermore, her descendants for generations to come shall suffer Shinbatsu.' Breaking through these ancient legends and superstitions, the Kyōso and his party successfully completed a pilgrimage to Kanmurijima on the eighth day of the sixth month of the old calendar in the thirty-third year of Meiji, and this time, intending to cross to the sacred Kutsushima, where no human foot has trod since ancient times, and to pray for the peace of the world and the great victory of the Imperial Army by enshrining the Tenjin Chigi and the Ikigami Ushitora no Konjin, they departed from the Hongū again on the eighth day of the seventh month of the lunar calendar, and the party of nine, as they had done previously, hired a boat at Ōniuya and began to row toward Kutsushima, gliding over the calm sea surface.

The lanterns on the pier reflected in the seawater, their colors red and beautiful, the tide at the harbor gate was green, and the bay was as calm as a polished mirror, with hundreds of birds flocking and flying, the shore quiet, and green pines lining the beach, drooping their branches; while gazing at the indescribable beauty of the moonlit night, they boarded two small boats at eight-thirty in the evening, and the boatmen, Hashimoto Rokuzō and Tanaka Iwakichi, as they had done last time, took charge, rowing leisurely while singing boat songs in cool, clear voices. The entire sky was pear-colored with shining stars, the waves were extremely calm, and the sea surface was as if oil had been poured upon it, with the stars reflecting and twinkling; one could even clearly see jellyfish floating by, and it felt as if one were sitting on a bed of silver sand, making for an extremely safe voyage. By the time they reached Bakuchigamisaki, the half-moon of the eighth day was crossing to the west beyond the sea, and the lighthouse at Kyōgamisaki appeared, flickering in and out of sight in the wake of the waves; above their heads and beneath their feet, the Milky Way stretched across, and the moon, bright as a sharp sickle, flowed quietly through its center, making one wonder if it would meet the horizon of the sea. According to the boatmen:

Speaker: 'There has been no sea as calm as this tonight in the last three or five years. It must be the divine protection of the gods of Kanmurijima and Kutsushima. Truly, it is grateful and awe-inspiring.'

They sang boat songs with interesting rhythms, full of joy and courage, wearing only red loincloths and completely naked. Beyond the vast, rolling waves, hundreds of fishing fires floated upon the water, and the voices of the sturdy men competing in their boats to catch sea fish could be heard high above the sound of the waves; looking at these fishing fires, it was as if one were seeing an illumination on the sea. The boat was rowed relentlessly toward the northeast, and the two boatmen called out loudly to two or three fishing boats passing by just a few chō away; as the boatmen were on familiar terms with one another, their greetings were extremely rough, such that anyone hearing them for the first time would suspect they were quarreling. They purchased about twenty mackerel, each about a foot and two or three inches long, from these fishing boats as offerings for Kanmurijima and Kutsushima. The eastern sky began to brighten little by little; the boatmen, down to their loincloths and dripping with sweat, rowed with all their might and to the limit of their strength, and at eight-thirty in the morning, they arrived safely at the shore of Kanmurijima. 'Well, that is a relief,' they said upon landing, and after the Kyōso and the other eight had finished reciting the Tenshin Norito before the shrine, they left Kinoshita Keitarō, Fukubayashi Yasunosuke, Shikata Yūsuke, and Nakamura Takezō on Kanmurijima, ordering them to clean the shrine grounds and intending to visit again on the return trip, while the Kyōso, along with Deguchi Kaichō, Deguchi Sumiko, Shikata Heizō, and Fukushima Toranosuke, departed immediately for Kutsushima. Fukushima Toranosuke, having been terrified by the giant waves between Kanmurijima and Kutsushima, clung to the bottom of the boat, groaning and occasionally appearing to be on the verge of a seizure; it seemed he had learned his lesson, as he declared he would never visit Kutsushima again.

原文（近代文語）

丹後の舞鶴からも若狭の小浜からも、縞の財布が空になると云ふ宮津からも、丁度十里あると云ふ沖中の一つ島で、昔から『男は一生に必ず一度は参れ、二度は参るな、女は絶対に禁制万一女が参拝しやうものなら、竜宮の乙姫さまの怒りに触れて海上が荒れ出し、いろいろの妖怪が出たり大蛇が沢山現はれて女を丸呑みにする、さうして子孫の代迄神罰を蒙る』と云ふ古来の伝説と迷信とを打破して、教祖の一行は恙なく明治三十三年旧六月八日冠島参拝を遂げ、今度更に古来人跡なき神聖なる沓島へ渡つて、天神地祇を初め奉り、生神艮の鬼門大金神を奉祀して天下の泰平や皇軍の大勝利を祈願せむと、陰暦七月八日再び本宮を出立、一行九人は前回同様大丹生屋で船を雇ひ、穏かな海面を辷り乍ら沓島に向つて漕ぎ出した。

埠頭の万灯は海水に映じて其色赤く麗しく、港門の潮水は緑色をなし、海湾浪静にして磨ける鏡の如く、百鳥群がり飛んで磯端静に、青松浜頭に列なり梢を垂れ得も言はれぬ月夜の景色を眺めつつ、午後八時半二隻の小舟に乗り、舟人は前回の如く橋本六蔵、田中岩吉の二名これに当り声も涼しく船唄を唄ひ乍ら悠々として漕ぎ出した。満天梨地色に星輝き、波至つて平穏に、恰も海面は油を流した如く、星が映つてキラキラと光つて居る。海月が浮いて行くの迄が判然と見える。銀砂を敷いた上に居る様な心持がして極めて安全な航海である。博奕ケ岬迄行つた頃は、八日の半絃の月は海の彼方に西渡き経ケ岬の灯台は明々滅々浪のまにまに漂ふて見える。頭の上にも足の下にも、銀河が横つて其真中を敏鎌の様に冴えた月が静かに流れて、海の果で合するかと疑はれるばかりであつた。舟人の話によれば、

『茲三年や五年に今夜位静穏な海上はない。大方冠島沓島の神様の御守護でありませう。ほんに有り難い、勿体ない』

と喜び勇み乍ら、赤い褌を締め真裸となつて節面白く船唄を唄ひ出した。

万波洋々たる海の彼方には、幾百の漁火が波上に浮み、甲艇乙舸競ふて海魚を漁りする壮丁の声は波の音を掠めて高く聞えて来る。此漁火を打見やれば、恰も海上のイルミネーシヨンを見る様である。舟は容赦なく東北さして漕ぎ出された。二三の釣舟が二三丁ばかり傍に通りかかるのを、二人の船頭は大声で呼びとめる。船頭同志は互に分け隔てなき間柄とて、極めて乱雑な挨拶振り、初めて聞いたものは喧嘩ではないかと疑ふばかりである。此釣舟で一尺二三寸ばかりの鯖を二十尾ばかり買ひ求めて、冠島沓島への供へ物とした。東の空はソロソロと明くなり出した。舟人は褌一つになつて、汗をタラタラ流しつつ力の極み、根限り漕ぎつける。午前八時半無事に冠島の磯際についた。『まあ一安心だ』と上陸し、神前に向つて教祖以下八人は天津祝詞を奏上し終つて、木下慶太郎、福林安之助、四方祐助、中村竹蔵の四名を冠島に残しおき、神社境内の掃除を命じおき、帰途に改めて参拝する事とし教祖を始め出口海潮、出口澄子、四方平蔵、福島寅之助の五人は直に沓島に向つて出発する。福島寅之助は冠島から沓島へ行く間の巨浪に肝を潰し、舟底に喰ひつき時々発動気味になつて唸つて居る。それきり同人はコリコリしたと見え沓島へは再び参らないと云つて居た。

§ 2

Now, the group left on Oshima was engaged in great activity, raking away the bird droppings that had piled up more than an itsushiyaku deep all over the garden to cleanse it, and sweeping up dead trees and fallen leaves into the forest beside the shrine. Suddenly, Nakamura Takezau was struck by a fierce stomach ache and began writhing in agony. Convinced that it was truly a divine punishment, the whole group was struck with fear and offered their apologies, carrying the refuse even further away into the forest. Whether the divine spirits had granted their request or not, the pain ceased suddenly, and even the stubborn Nakamura seemed moved by the divine virtue. The Kyōso's party finally rowed to Meshima. As it was a Kamishima that people had feared and been unable to approach since ancient times, it was vastly different in character from Oshima. Despite the fact that it was an exceptionally calm sea that day, swells like mountains were incessantly crashing against it. Gulls, albatrosses, and cormorants were perched all over the rocks as if sesame seeds had been sprinkled upon them, looking down at the party with a curious air. Tens of thousands of seabirds were floating and diving on the waves, playing leisurely. It was a place of renowned precipitous cliffs, and no spot could be found to row the small boat ashore. Just as they were deliberating to circle the island and find a suitable landing point, the Kyōso insisted that they must moor the boat at Tsurigane-iwa. When they rowed to the base of Tsurigane-iwa in obedience to the command, they found it to be a precipitous cliff like a man's back, which was impossible to cling to by any means. If they dangled about, there was a risk that the violent waves would cause the boat to collide with the rocks and be destroyed, so there was no time to hesitate even for an instant. Kaichō, casting aside his fear as if to say it was 'a single leap over hell,' tied an eight-fathom rope around his waist, and watching for the split second when the boat was struck by a wave and drew near the rock, he aimed for the cliff face and leaped. Fortunately, the rock was rough and his hands and feet did not slip; there was a small flat area about a jō and four or five shiyaku above. When he threw one end of the rope toward the boat from there, the boatmen quickly caught it and tied it to the boat. 'It is safe now,' came the voice from above, and Kaichō of Ueda pulled the rope with all his might. From below, the true Kaichō pushed the boat carrying the Kyōso, and as it came, he carried it over with great effort. The Kyōso, clinging to the rope, finally landed safely. The three others followed and climbed up. They unpacked the portable shrine they had brought, assembled from Ayabe, and the boatmen tied each pillar with ropes, while Yotsukata and Fukushima pulled them up. They finally made the flat rock, about the size of two tatami mats at a height of nearly a hundred shiyaku, the site for the consecration, and after spending over an hour, they finally erected the shrine. They enshrined the Ushitora no Daikonjin Kunitokotachi no Mikoto, Ryūgū no Otohime, Toyotamahime no Kami, Tamayorihime no Kami, and the eight million gods of heaven and earth, and after offering rare treasures of the mountains, fields, rivers, and seas, the Kyōso sat quietly before the shrine and recited the norito for the peace of the world and the great victory of the divine army in a clear voice.

Although the story has jumped back and forth a little, Kaichō first recited the norito for the transfer ceremony with awe and reverence, and finally, the whole group joined together to recite the Ōharae no Norito. The flocks of birds on the island seemed as if they were listening to the norito. Since it was an island exposed to the open sea as far as Vladivostok in Russia to the north, the violent waves and raging billows of the Sea of Japan all struck this Tsurigane-iwa of Meshima, washing it clean; thus, this side was nothing but rock, and one could not find even a hint of soil even if one wished to see it. The waves, as large as mountains, that came surging from the offing from time to time collided with this Tsurigane-iwa, and a fierce sound like a hundred thunders ringing out at once—'gon-gon, dodon-dodon'—pierced the ears. Even with the waves that the boatmen called the calmest they had seen in years, the sound was this intense; thinking of how fierce it must be on a rough day, a feeling of awe began to stir within them. According to the boatmen, it was a famous island where, during the Bunroku era, a man named Sanshu Shirōzaemon led several hundred subordinates, used Oshima as a base to build a camp, and in order to collect military funds to usurp the world at that time, he plundered ships passing by and made a living as a pirate. He hung a bell on the top of this rock to signal scouts and maintain contact with Oshima, and victims continued to appear until they were too numerous to count. Hearing of this, the renowned hero Iwami Jūtarō, known for his bravery, could not leave it be; he used a stratagem to disguise himself as a dry goods merchant, lured them one by one to Maizuru, threw them into prison, and wiped them out completely. Since then, it was called Tsurigane-jima or Kimon-jima, and it was said that no one had ever come to this Meshima. However, because the Kyōso had now come across for the sake of all the people of the world for the first time, overcoming a hundred hardships, enshrined the gods, and offered prayers for the peace of the world, it was an unprecedented and magnificent feat, and it was serialized in various newspapers, including the Fuji Shimbun in Tokyo and the Santan Shimbun in Fukuchiyama.

原文（近代文語）

さて冠島に残された連中が一尺以上も堆高く積つて居る庭一面の鳥糞を掻き浚へ、お庭を清める、枯木や朽葉を集めて社の傍の林の中に掃き寄せる等、大活動をやつて居た。忽ち中村竹蔵が激烈な腹痛を起し七顛八倒する。全く神罰が当つたのだと一同は恐れ入つて謝罪をなし、塵芥を一層遠き林の中へ持ち運んだ。神明聴許遊ばしたか、俄に痛みも止まつたので頑固一辺の中村も、其神徳に感激した様であつた。教祖の一行は漸くにして沓島に漕ぎついた。流石に昔から人の恐れて近づき得ない神島だけありて、冠島とは大変に趣が違ふてゐる。今日は格別穏かな海だと云ふに拘はらず、山の如きウネリが頻りに打ち寄せて来る。鴎や信天翁、鵜などが岩一面に胡麻を振りかけた様に止まつて、不思議相に一行を見下ろして居る。波の上には数万の海鳥が浮きつ沈みつ、悠々と遊んでゐる。音に名高き断岩絶壁、小舟を漕ぎ寄せる場所が見つからぬ。兎も角も此島を一周して適当な上陸点を探らうと評定して居ると、教祖が是非に釣鐘岩へ舟を着けよと云はれる。命のまにまに釣鐘岩の直下へ漕ぎつけて見ると、恰も人の背中の如く険峻な断岩で如何しても掻きつく事が出来ない。愚図々々してゐると、激浪のために舟を岩に衝突させ、破壊して了ふ虞があるから、瞬時も躊躇してをる場合でない。海潮は『地獄の上の一足飛び』と云ふ様な肝を放り出して腰に八尋縄を結びつけたまま、舟が波にうたれて岩に近づいた一刹那を睨ひすまして、岩壁目蒐けて飛びついた。幸にも粗質な岩で手足が滑らぬ、一丈四五尺程の上の方に少しばかりの平面な処がある。そこから舟を目蒐けて縄の片端を投げ込めば、舟人が手早く拾ふて舟に結びつける。最早大丈夫だと岩上からは上田の海潮が一生懸命に縄を手繰り寄せる。下からは真正の海潮が教祖を乗せた舟を目がけて押し寄せ、来るや来るや母曾呂々々々に持ち渡す。教祖は手早く縄に縋り乍ら漸く上陸された。続いて三人も登つて来た。綾部で組み立てて持つて来た神祠をといて、柱一本づつ舟人が縄で縛る、四方と福島がひきあげる。漸く百尺ばかりもある高所の二畳敷ほどの平面の岩の上を鎮祭所となし、一時間あまりもかかつて漸く神祠を建て上げ、艮の大金神国常立尊、竜宮の乙姫、豊玉姫神、玉依姫神を始め、天地八百万の神等を奉斎し、山野河海の珍物を供へ終り、教祖は恭しく祠前に静座して声音朗かに天下泰平神軍大勝利の祈願の祝詞を奏上される。

話は一寸後前になつたが、第一着に海潮が遷座式の祝詞を恐み恐み白し上げ、最後に一同打揃ふて大祓の祝詞を奏上した。島の群鳥は祝詞を拝聴するものの如くである。何分北は露西亜の浦塩斯徳港迄つつ放しの島であるから、日本海の激浪怒濤は皆此沓島の釣鐘岩に打かるので一面に洗ひ去られて、此方面は岩ばかりで土の気は見たいと思ふても見当らなかつた。沖の方から時々寄せ来る山の様に大きな浪が此釣鐘岩に衝突して、百雷の一時に鳴り響く様に、ゴンゴン　ドドンドドンと烈しき音が耳を刺戟する。舟人は今日は数年来に見た事のない穏かの波だと云つた浪でさへも、これ位の音がするのだもの、海の荒れた日にはどんなに烈しからうと思へば、凄い様な心持がして来た。船人の語る所によれば此釣鐘岩には、文禄年間に三種四郎左衛門と云ふ男、数百人の部下を引率れ冠島を策源地として陣屋を構へ、時の天下を横領せむと軍資金を集むるために海上往来の船舶を掠め海賊を稼いで、此岩の頂上に半鐘を釣り斥候の合図をし冠島との連絡をとつて居たので、被害者は数ふるに暇なき迄続出したので、武勇の誉高き豪傑岩見重太郎がこれを聞いて捨ておけぬと計略を以て呉服屋に化け、一人一人舞鶴へ引寄せ牢獄に打ち込み、悉皆退治したと伝ふる有名な島で、其後は釣鐘島、鬼門島と称し、誰も此沓島へは来たものはないと云つてゐた。然るに今回初めて教祖が世界万民のために、百難を排して渡り来られ、神々様を奉祀し、天下無事の祈祷をされたのは実に前代未聞の壮挙であると云ふので、東京の富士新聞や福知山の三丹新聞を始め其他の諸新聞に連載された事がある。

§ 3

Now, having circled this island and admiring the strange rocks and precipices, they rowed the boat back to Oshima, where the party of nine gathered to bow before the shrine and, having finished offering the gifts, decided to circle Oshima as well. It is said that the circumference is over forty-odd chō, and that the seeds of all plants and trees in the world are gathered on this island. In the past, upland rice grew here naturally, but because the farmers of Ōuramura defiled it by applying fertilizer, not a single stalk of rice has grown since, and the island has come to be densely overgrown with weeds; as the two spoke of this while rowing to the Peering Rock, they found a natural tunnel bored through dozens of jō of rock. Rocks standing like folding screens and rocks piled up like books stand in rows, and mysterious rocks that look like dragons flying and tigers running stand in the sea. When they advanced the boat a little to the northwest, the sheer cliffs that make one lose one's spirit at a single glance and the blue tide that makes one's heart thunder at a single look revealed schools of sea bream swimming vertically and lurking horizontally, their emerald and crimson colors intermingling in confusion just like brocade, and they were so lost in admiration that they forgot to return. Playing here for a while, it feels as if one's life span is extended by ten years. A sense of having wiped away all the worldly dust of the world wells up in one's breast. In any case, regardless of gender, it is a place that believers should by all means visit at least once.

On the evening of the ninth, they returned to Maizuru without incident, and on the following tenth, the party took a commemorative photograph in Kyōguchi-machi, Maizuru, and it was decided that they would return to the Hongū in celebration.

原文（近代文語）

さて此島を一周りして、奇岩絶壁を嘆賞しつつ冠島へ再び舟を漕ぎ寄せ、一行九人打揃ふて神前に拝礼し、供物を献じ終つて又此冠島も一周する事となつた。周囲四十有余丁あり、世界の所在草木の種子は皆此島に集まつてあると云はれてある。昔は陸稲も自然に出来てゐたのを、大浦村の百姓が肥料を施して汚したので、其後は稲は一株も出来なくなり、雑草が密生する様になつたのだと二人が話しつつ覗き岩迄漕ぎつけて見れば、数十丈の岩石に自然の隧道が穿たれてある。屏風を立てた様な岩や書籍を積み重ねた様な岩立ち並び、竜飛び虎馳る如き不思議の岩が海中に立つてゐる。少しく舟を西北へ進めると、一望肝を消すの断巌、一瞻胸を轟かすの碧潮に鯛魚の群をなして縦に泳ぎ、緯に潜み、翠紅、色交々乱れて恰も錦綾の如く、感賞久しうして帰る事を忘れるに至る。此処に暫く遊んでゐると、十年も寿命がのびる様である。世の俗塵一切を払拭し去つた様な観念が胸に湧いて来る。兎に角男女を問はず信徒たるものは一度は是非参詣すべき処である。

九日の夕方、恙なく舞鶴へ帰着し翌十日舞鶴京口町で一行記念の撮影をなし、目出度本宮へ帰る事となつた。
