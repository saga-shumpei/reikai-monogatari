Chapter 23: Fox 狸々々 [1060]

狐狸々々〔1060〕

§ 1

In August of Meiji 38, Nishida Genkyō, after experiencing various hardships and trials, had gathered several dozen believers in Uji of Yamashiro and was vigorously preaching, renting a guest room from a person named Ibaraki Seijirō; however, being excessively busy, he sent a postcard asking me to come once to support him, so I quietly slipped out of Ayabe before dawn, carrying a bag, and by the time I climbed Shiyuchiyamatauge, the sun had risen. Then, crossing Enokitauge and Kuwanontauge with all my might, I dropped by the Sonobe branch for a moment, took a believer named Sai Kōtarō as a load-bearer, passed through Kameoka and Ōji on foot, took the right road from Kutsukake, and when I arrived at Fushimi, the sun was already only one or two ken above the western mountains. When I dropped by the house of a believer named Yasuda Shōtarō in Fushimi, it was a tiled-roof house where they were right in the middle of building a fire in the hearth, making it impossible to talk at leisure, and since I was a man who had become the right-hand hand of Nakamura,

Yasuda: 'Kaichō-han, why are you not in Ayabe? Has your illness broken out yet again? The Foundress says that everything Kaichō does is nothing but going backward, yet are you going again? Come, please return home, or else, since we are right in the middle of building a fire in the hearth, we cannot even talk, so please stay tonight. I will talk to you later...'

he said.

'Has this person not yet awakened? It will be dreadful if I dawdle...'

so urging Kōtarō, I promptly parted ways with him, crossed Bungobashi in Fushimi, went up the long embankment of the Uji River, and after a journey of twenty-four or twenty-five ri from Ayabe, I finally arrived at Ibaraki's house around eight o'clock at night. When I went inside and looked, it was packed full of people. Nango Kunimatsu, Ibaraki Seijirō, Okada Kumajirō, Hasegawa Senkichi, and seven or eight other organizers had been established, and they were holding the monthly festival with tremendous momentum. Both inside and outside the house were packed full of worshippers. Upon seeing Kaichō, numerous believers shed tears and rejoiced. Then, without informing the people of Ayabe in the least, I engaged in propagation and proselytization at Seijirō's house, and every day fifty or sixty to about a hundred worshippers came, and as various sick people returned having received divine grace, the town of Uji, excluding only the priests and doctors, became entirely believers. And even from neighboring villages three or four ri away, daily worship took place in extraordinary prosperity. Because I was concerned about the hall of Konishi Matsumoto of Utsu, I reluctantly urged Nishida Genkyō, who had said he would never go to Konishi's place in his life, and sent him as an envoy together with Sai Kōtarō for an inspection. Thereupon, because his own house was small, Matsumoto had borrowed the spacious shrine office of the local tutelary Hachiman Shrine, enshrined the Deity there, and was engaging in propagation and proselytization with tremendous momentum. And seeing that Nishida had come, Konishi,

Konishi: 'How rare, you have managed to come without forgetting.'

he said arrogantly. Thereupon, because the state of Konishi's divine possession was extremely suspicious, when I performed a quick saniwa from the shadows, it seemed as if a badger was possessing him, so when I opened the sliding door and looked, five or six old Buddha statues whose hands had fallen off were carelessly shoved inside. Thereupon, Nishida,

Nishida: 'Konishi-san, how about floating such worm-eaten Buddha statues down the river? Because a badger is surely guarding these, and that fellow is possessing you, your divine possession has become strange and no longer matches in the least.'

when he said this, Konishi became extremely angry,

Konishi: 'Do not speak nonsense. You must have been sent here being manipulated by the tip of Kaichō's fox tail.'

he uttered abuse, reviling him severely before a large crowd of believers, so Nishida became furious, left that place, went around to Miyamura, crossed Kiriutauge, and keeping Kifune Shrine on his right, traversed Kyoto and finally returned to Uji, muttering complaints. Thereupon, when the next day came, Nishida's face turned pale and he began to shiver violently. Upon examining him closely, he had contracted ague. "I am the fox spirit who protects the Buddha statue in the nagatoko of the Hachimansama shrine of Utsu. Because koyatsu uttered that my precious Gohonzon should be washed away into the river, I shall take his inochi."

Thus he stubbornly persisted, increasingly torturing the karada, so I placed a suribachi upon Nishida's head, put a hitotsuka of moxa on top of it, and burned the moxa; whereupon he began to cry out, "It is hot, it is bitter!" and at last was cast out. After that, on the very next day, nothing happened; but at the same hour on the mitsukame, Nishida—

Nishida: 'He has come again, that fellow. What the hell!'

—was straining himself. Nevertheless, the spirit possession of the fox attacked with violent force, making him tremble again with ague and torturing him. This time I placed a wet tenuguhi upon Nishida's head, set the suribachi upon it, attached about a hiyakume of moxa, and while fanning it with a fan, performed chinkon, until with great effort the spirit was cast out. After that, he came back casually nisanpen, but in the end he retreated completely, and Nishida regained his original genki and engaged in missionary work.

To return to what happened earlier, upon seeing Nishida's letter I departed from Ayabe and stopped by the branch office in Sonobe, and then while taking a brief rest at the residence of Tai Gihei in Koyama, the sound of the steam whistle of a train entering Sonobe from the east somehow possessed a tone of shock and sorrow, so Kaichō spoke toward Tai Gihei:

Kaichō: 'That sound of the steam whistle doubtless means someone was run over and killed.'

Tai: 'Indeed, it is a fierce voice, different from usual.'

And as he looked outside, many people who were in the fields were running toward the railway with all their might. Since it was also my way to Uji, I could not linger here slowly, so I went to the side of the railway to look, and there a man with a small, pale face was cut into two from the waist and dragged about five or six ken, lying dead with a completely pale face. His face was so similar that I thought Nishida might have come to meet me and been run over to death, but when I approached and looked closely, it was not so. In the meantime, police officers arrived and were conducting various investigations. Upon the sand at the place where he was run over, "So-and-so of Shinjō Village" was written in the soil with the tip of a stick. Hearing later, I learned through the newspaper that this man had overspent a mere one yen and fifty sen of his master's money, came to his relatives in Sonobe to borrow it, was refused, and suffered a run-over death. Now, Sai Kōtarō's face suddenly began to look like that dead man, and feeling unbearable discomfort, I nevertheless forced him to carry the baggage as far as Uji. Sai Kōtarō again began to tremble with ague from time to time, and when I conducted saniwa—

Sai: 'I am the man who was run over to death upon the railway in Koyama. Because you were the very first to look at my face, I possessed you.'

—he said. Then, by moxa upon the suribachi once again, I barely managed to bring him to complete recovery and sent him back to Sonobe. While things were going on thus, apparently having heard from Yasuda of Fushimi, a henchman of Nakamura named Mimaki Jisaburō came to Uji and told various and sundry bad things about Kaichō and Nishida to Nangō Kunimatsu, Hasegawa Senkichi, and other officers—

Mimaki: 'They are fellows who have opposed Ushitora no Konjin-sama, so do not deal with them.'

—and at last, on the morning of New Year's Day in the thirty-ninth year, a large crowd gathered and threw me out. Returning as far as Mr. Tai's residence in Koyama without a single mon of travel expenses, many believers gathered, wept for joy, and gave me about four or five yen of spending money. With that, I returned to Ayabe for the first time in a long while. Then, on the fifteenth of that month, Nishida fell into the plot of Mimaki Jisaburō, Nangō, and others, had all his baggage taken away, and was thrown out; he went to Fushimi together with Oyuki as husband and wife, Oyuki entered a certain thread-spinning factory as a female worker, while Nishida practiced anma and engaged in missionary work in the Fushimi region alongside his trade; but after I returned to Ayabe in the forty-second year and came to build the Ōhiromae and shrines, he gradually returned to Ayabe and came to preach enthusiastically.

Prior to this, Nishida and Mimaki, at the request of a local boss in Uji named Hashiguma, had been frequently performing Mitamamatsuri at the houses of his kobun during the night. It so happened that those Mitamabako would occasionally leap about, and when Sonahemono were offered, they would make a clattering sound and turn toward the fish offerings or even descend the Kaidan; thus, it was rumored that spirits were truly formidable things. A reputation spread that Nishida-san and Mimaki-san were truly great, and people everywhere competed to request Mitamamatsuri from them. Hashiguma, being the oyabun, came to make a special request, saying he wished for Kaichō himself to perform the rite at his own house; thus I went and completed the Mitamamatsuri, and while I was taking an itsupuku, Hashiguma spoke with a strange expression.

Hashiguma: 'Say, Sensei, is the Mitama-sama of my house not yet settled? While the Mitama-sama of other houses all move, why is it that only mine does not move? Despite being the Sensei, is your spirit not effective?'

As he spoke with such dissatisfaction, I could not very well reveal that it was foxes entering and moving the boxes.

Kiraku: 'Since today is my first time performing Mitamamatsuri, I do not know the ways of this house; Mimaki-san is jiyōzu at it, so please have him do it.'

I said this politely to excuse myself. Thereupon, when they requested Mimaki and had him perform the Mitamamatsuri anew, the box began to move violently, and Mimaki's credit rose; however, since it would not move at all whether Nishida or I performed it, I eventually lost the trust of those superstitious people and was the first to be cast out, with Nishida being driven away shortly thereafter. In the village of Gōnoku in Tsuzuki District, there was a sister of a sake brewer named Asada Yasuji, a woman named Otsuru who suffered from epilepsy. Having been asked to heal her illness, I traveled far to Gōnoku and performed Chinkon, whereupon the epilepsy ceased for about a month. However, when the epilepsy broke out again inside the sake cellar, Kaichō's credit began to wane; just then, in that village, there was a sick girl about twenty years old whose legs had long been paralyzed. I had returned to Uji and was proselytizing at the house in Nangō when a request came again, so this time Mimaki and Kotake went to perform Chinkon. Then, that girl spoke out:

'I am the grandmother of this house who died three years ago, and I secretly lent such-and-such amounts of money to various people in the village.'

She spoke so plausibly that when they checked, the sum amounted to about a thousand yen; thus the brother of the sick girl walked all around the neighborhood, saying:

'The Mitama of our grandmother-san says she lent money to your place, so please return it.'

This caused a great uproar in the village, and when they investigated, asking:

'Who on earth said such a thing?'

It was discovered that the girl began to speak when Mimaki performed Chinkon, and that a man named Kotake and another were attending her; consequently, the police arrived and various troubles began. Then, a messenger came from Gōnoku to fetch me, and when I went to see, the girl was persistently using the grandmother-san's voice, stubbornly insisting, 'I definitely lent the money.' After sending Mimaki and Kotake back to Uji, I stayed for one night to consider the situation; finding it extremely suspicious, I borrowed a sword from the master of the house and, while reciting Norito, slashed through the air, whereupon seven small foxes jumped out from beside the chest of drawers in broad daylight. Simultaneously, the girl's illness was completely healed. Then, far from thanking Kaichō, they argued against me, saying:

'You must have used disciples like Mimaki to possess our daughter with foxes and cause such an uproar in the village!'

They shouted at me:

'You fox-beast, leave at once!'

Thus I was ultimately branded as a fox-possessor and, swallowing my resentment, returned to Uji. Thereupon, Nangō and Hasegawa joined with Mimaki and decided to cast me out on the morning of New Year's Day in the thirty-ninth year. It is a troublesome thing when dealing with people who do not understand the affairs of the spirit world; the more one explains the reasons, the more they doubt, making them impossible to handle. After that, I became utterly disgusted with performing Chinkon for the sick and abandoned my training in divine possession. However, in the fifth year of Taishō, when I visited the house of Asano-san in Yokosuka, I demonstrated the practice of Yūsai again for reference, which became the catalyst for Asano-san to begin researching spiritology with great devotion.

と其処ら中へ歩いたので、村中の大騒動となり、

『一体誰がそんな事云ふたか』

と調べて見ると、三牧が鎮魂して其娘が喋り出し、小竹と云ふ男と二人がついて居ると云ふので、巡査がやつて来たり色々と悶錯が初まつた。そこで郷の口から自分を呼びに来たので行つて見ると、其娘は頻りに婆アサンの声色を使ふて、『如何しても金を貸した』と意地張つてゐる。それから三牧と小竹を宇治へ帰し、自分が一晩泊つて様子を考へた処が、非常に怪しいので刀を一本主人から貸して貰ふて祝詞をあげ乍ら空を切つて見ると箪笥の横から昼の真中に七匹の豆狸が飛び出した。それと共に其娘は病気が癒つて了つた。さうすると海潮にお礼を云ふ所か、

『お前サンは三牧の様な弟子を使ふて宅の娘に狸を憑けて、こんな村中の騒動をさしたのだらう』

と反対に理屈を云ひ、

『ど狸奴が、早うかへれ』

と呶鳴りつけられるので到頭狸憑けにしられて了ひ怨みを呑んで宇治まで帰つて来た。さうすると、南郷や長谷川が三牧と一つになつて、三十九年の正月元日に朝つぱらから自分を放り出す事となつたのである。霊界の事の分らぬ連中になると困つたもので、訳を云へば云ふ程益々疑ふて始末におへぬものである。それから自分も病人の鎮魂がサツパリ嫌になり、神懸の修行も断念して了ふた。が大正五年に横須賀の浅野サンの宅へ行つた時、参考のために又もや幽斎の修行をして見せたのが元となつて浅野サンが霊学を熱心に研究し始める事となつたのである。
