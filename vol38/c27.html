Chapter 27: 仇箒 [1064]

仇箒〔1064〕

§ 1

It was in the second month of Meiji Sanjifuhachinen. Shikata Heizau, Nakamura Takezau, and ten other leaders of Ōmoto were blowing horns full of derailments, claiming that the Tatekahetatenahoshi of the world would be completed within this year and that the world of Miroku would appear.

As it was in the midst of the Nichiro Sensō, they were excited and agitated, raising a great meter as the Shinji of the Fudesaki was realized. One like Nakamura Takezau took as a shield the phrase in the Fudesaki that said to walk straight in the Mannaka of the path, and having memorized the phrase about the world of darkness, he would light a candle in a lantern bearing the Toeu crest in the middle of the day, and walk down the Mannaka of the main road with a composed face.

He would not move aside even if an oxcart came, holding the lantern in his left hand and a folded fan in his right, swinging his elbows as he walked. When the oxcart moved aside for him, he would say, 'The power of the Kami is truly something. If one walks in the Mannaka of the path of sincerity, even such a person will move aside...', and getting more and more carried away, he would stride down the main road, not caring about the inconvenience to traffic, loudly reading the phrases of the Fudesaki; such was his agitated state.

Once, while he was walking around selling farm tools loaded on a cart, he went as far as the Moto-Ise area for propaganda. Seeing the moon of the second day of the lunar calendar, he cried, 'Oh, this is a crisis! I have worshipped the crescent moon since long ago, but to be able to worship the moon of the second day is truly a sign that the world is about to change. I cannot dally!' He threw his gold wallet, luggage, and cart into the Yuragawa River, ran back to Ayabe with all his might, and while repeatedly shouting that it was a crisis, he went before the altar enshrined on the second floor and prayed with all his might.

When he suddenly lifted his face, he saw that clear water had been drawn and offered in a large water bowl. Because the window on the second floor was open, a sparrow had flown in and dropped a piece of excrement onto the water bowl, which was floating in the water in an amusing way. He gazed at it and cried out, 'Ah, the Tatekahe has finally begun! An Udumbara flower is blooming in the water!' Hearing this, Shikata Heizau and the other leaders gathered, advanced to the front of the Kami, gazed at it, and became even more grateful, their voices filled with tears, as they recited the Norito many times.

Coming out before Kiraku, who was in the six-mat room, Nakamura Takezau puffed out his elbows and said with an air of great composure: Nakamura: 'Hey, Chairman-san, you had better repent to a reasonable degree, have Komatsubayashi-san leave, and quickly become the Hitsujisaru no Konjin-sama to serve as the wife, for the time is pressing. Just yesterday, when I returned from Moto-Ise, I was able to worship the moon of the second day, and today, an Udumbara flower has bloomed in the water offered to the Kami. You cannot afford to dally; repent quickly.' As he said this as if scolding in a loud voice, Kiraku replied: Kiraku: 'There is nothing particularly rare about worshipping the moon of the second day. We have lived in Anō and have worshipped it from time to time. You are living in such a narrow place surrounded by mountains that you make a fuss about being able to worship the moon of the second day, but if you tell such foolish things to people, they will think you are insane, so please, at least do not say that.' Before he could even finish, Nakamura pursed his lips and said: Nakamura: 'Hey, Komatsubayashi, there is a crescent moon since long ago, but is there such a thing as a moon of the second day? Saying such wild things as having seen a moon of the second day in Anō, it is useless to try to cloud the spirit of the crystal. The time has come when the spirit of the Ushitora no Konjin Hensei Danshi has appeared as the Great Ōdeguchi Kami, and with the help of the Hinodewake and the Ryūgū-sama, is performing the Tatekahetatenahoshi. If you, Komatsubayashi, do not repent quickly, leave the body of the Chairman, and settle in Naitō of Sonobe to become the fleshly palace of the Hitsujisaru no Konjin-sama, there is no telling how much the Kami, Buddhas, and people of the world will suffer. Hey, Komatsubayashi, if you think that is a lie, come to the altar on the second floor for a moment. An Udumbara flower is blooming in the crystal water. If you see that, even a Komatsubayashi like you will surely have to surrender.'

As he was speaking boastfully, thinking it was a strange thing to say and likely nothing good, I went up to the second floor immediately to see, and there, bird droppings were floating on the water, hanging down white, looking exactly like an Udonge flower. Kiraku scooped it up with the tip of a wooden chopstick, held it close to Nakamura's nose, and said,

Kiraku: 'Hey, this is not an Udonge, it is bird droppings.'

When he said this, Nakamura made a strange face and fell silent. Then, the other Yakuin, with a troubled look, pleaded,

Yakuin: 'Please, Kaichō-san, do not tell this to anyone, for you will be laughed at.'

The absurdity of this pleading was remarkable.

Prior to this, Kikuko, who had been Nakamura's wife, had offered her opinion many times because Nakamura would not conduct business day after day and went around saying things full of Datsusen, but eventually, Nakamura became angry, started saying, 'An Akurei of Komatsubayashi has possessed O-Kiku,' and cast her out. He had been thinking of taking a wife from the Kyōso's relatives, and he had schemed to bring back Fukushima Hisako from Yagi to make her his wife, but he was hindered by Kiraku and could not achieve his purpose; from then on, Nakamura and Hisako began to add even more intense obstruction to everything Kiraku did.

Around the 38th year of Meiji, four or five years later, Nakamura was finally ordered by the Kyōso to take a wife, so the Yakuin gathered and introduced various daughters of believers to Nakamura, but no matter what, he shook his head and would not consent. This was because Nakamura was constantly working in the shadows to take Sumiko's sister, Ryūko, as his wife. Ryūko also thought in her heart that she might become Nakamura's wife, reform him, and make him listen to what the Kaichō said. However, Nakamura had the ambition to make Ryūko his own wife and to force Kiraku and Sumiko into retirement so that he could act haughtily. Since the Kyōso had issued an order to choose Ryūko's husband from among the three men, Nakamura Takezō, Takehara Fusatarō, and Kinoshita Keitarō, Shikata Heizō and other executive members finally decided to make him Nakamura's wife. Even now, Kiraku and Sumiko were receiving this much pressure and obstruction, and thinking that it would be unbearable if Nakamura became the sister's husband and started making a clamor, they said to the Kyōso,

Kiraku: 'Please, divorce me from Sumiko as of today; I will return home.'

When he said this, the Kyōso was also greatly perplexed, called Shikata Heizō, and said,

Kyōso: 'Please, regarding this matter of the Kaichō-san, leave it to my opinion.'

As he said this, it was suggested that Kinoshita Keitarō, who listened a little to what Kiraku said even among the executives, should be adopted, so it was decided to have Ryūko establish a separate house and to adopt Kinoshita. And they made a woman named Nakamura Komatsu, who had become the right-hand person of Fukushima Hisako of Yagi and had been going around obstructing Kiraku's missionary work by disguising herself as a second-hand dealer, into Nakamura's wife. After that, as a result of Nakamura being completely disappointed and discouraged, he became prone to madness, and eventually, he began to confess his old conspiracies indiscriminately.

Because Nakamura opposed the Kami so much, I was shown in a dream by the Kami on the night of April 3rd in the 37th year of Meiji that he would receive divine punishment and die by falling into a cesspool, and I had written it down in the guide of the path, but eventually, in the 39th year of Meiji, it happened just as in the dream, and he died in madness.

Nakamura and Hisako of Yagi constantly went back and forth, and responding to each other internally and externally, they continued the movement to exclude the Kaichō. Until recently, Hisako had continued the opposition movement for twenty-four years, still viewing Kiraku as an enemy.

In this state, because the superstitious group would not use anything that the Kaichō preached, saying that such teachings found in books and learning were teachings of evil, and not a single person would listen, as a result of consulting with Sumiko, I went out for re-propaganda. Since Murakami Fusanosuke had finally begun to wake up, I took Murakami with me and went as far as Yagi, but they were vigorously attacking, saying that square characters must not be used at all and that it was a foreign way of doing things. Even though they had made me burn the Shingō that Kiraku had written, at the shrine in Yagi, a pair of lanterns with 'Ushitora no Konjin Kunitokotachi no Mikoto' written in thick square characters were hanging, and all other sacred implements also had square characters written on them. Thereupon, Kiraku...

Two or three months later, when they stopped by Yagi, Fukushima was Yaseotoro, reduced to skin and bones, and the Fūfu were waiting in tears. Today was exactly the completion of the hundredth day, and Fukushima Toranosuke said that he had been assigned the role of ascending to heaven to turn the chaotic world into crystal, so he had just performed a parting water-cup ceremony with his wife, and he felt as if his body were floating and he was about to rise into the sky by himself.

Kiraku, without making it too obvious, told him a story that had appeared in the newspapers at the time: a certain teacher of the Maruyama Church had built a high platform of three jō and three shaku on a rooftop in Nagoya, claiming he would ascend to heaven, and about two hundred believers were praying 'Tenmeikaiten, Tenmeikaiten' from below the platform. Fukushima: 'It was the time to ascend to heaven at twelve o'clock today, but because the evil Kami of Komatsubayashi came, I have postponed the arrangement by one hour. Quickly, make Komatsubayashi, the wife of the household, leave!'

However, he was so worried that he rested at a teahouse in Yagi and thought about Fukushima's condition from afar, as he intended to go and stop him if it looked like he might climb a pine tree.

Then, the guardian deity of Fukushima postponed the arrangement due to circumstances, then postponed it until the next morning, then until the day after, and so on for about a week. Because he had given such nonsensical oracles, Fukushima also realized the truth. While Hisako was away on an errand to the town of Yagi, he gathered all the O-fudesaki he had spent four or five years writing day and night, poured kerosene on them, and burned every single volume without leaving one behind.

福島『今日の十二時に天上をさす所であつたけれど、小松林の悪神が来たによつて、一時間仕組をのばしたぞよ。早く家内の久どの小松林を去なせよ』

と呶鳴つてゐる。喜楽は福島久子に余り気の毒でたまらぬので、曲津がだましてるのだといふ事も出来ず、大方霊が天へ上るのだらうから、肉体に気をつけて、松の木へでも登りそうだつたら止めなされ……と忠告をして帰つたものの心配でたまらぬので、八木の或茶店に休んで、福島の様子を遠くから考へて居た。もし松の木へでも上りそうになつたら止めに行かうと思うたからである。

そしたら福島の守護神は……都合に依つて仕組を延ばした、明日の朝まで延ばした、明後日まで延ばした、と一週間程延ばした。出鱈目な託宣をしたので、福島も気がつき、久子を八木の町へ買物にやつた不在の間に、四五年もかかつて昼夜せつせと書いた自分のお筆先を一所によせ、石油をかけて一冊も残らず焼いて了ひ、

といきなり御神前をひつくりかへし、神さまの祠を残らず外へ叩き出して了うたといふ面白い物語もあつた。

それから又綾部より役員が出張して、神さまを斎り直し、盛んに会長の攻撃を続行してゐた。会長は澄子と相談の上、嵯峨京都伏見の支部などへ気をつけてやらうと浦上を伴れて、綾部を立出で、園部に一泊してそれから八木へ立寄ると、福島寅之助が矢庭に奥から飛んで来て、

福島『ヤア四足が来よつたシーツシーツ』

The two picked up broken straw sandals, attached them to their feet, and went up and down the mountain paths until they finally reached the mountain pass of Suchiyama. Someone was selling konnyaku there, so they bought three pieces for one sen and five rin, and it tasted sweeter than anything else, having walked twenty-four ri from Uji while holding their hunger. After that, they returned to Ayabe. Urakami, having had enough, started a rice cake shop, and Kiraku worshipped the gods for him, which led to great prosperity. Then, gradually, Urakami became arrogant, began to speak ill of the gods and even criticized Kiraku's whereabouts, and started to oppose him incessantly. Finally, he opened a poultry farm, which invited a great loss; after losing all the money he had earned without a single mon remaining and incurring a large debt, he caused damage to his relatives and others.
