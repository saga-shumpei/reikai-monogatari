Chapter 8: 三ツ巴 [1045]

三ツ巴〔1045〕

§ 1

It was on the fifteenth day of the tenth month in the thirty-second year of Meiji. Adachi, Shikata, and Nakamura, the three of them, having held a secret council at the training ground in Uhedani as a united group of Kamugakari, returned to Kinmeikuwai with faces as if nothing were amiss, acting with the authority of plenipotentiary ministers, with the intention of advising Kiraku to withdraw from Ayabe; they lured him out to the summit of Hongūsanjiyō with clever words, and Shikata Haruzau, being the first to speak, opened his mouth and said:

Shikata: 'I speak to Uheda-sensei, but last night at my house in Uhedani, all the officers of Kinmeikuwai gathered, and as a result of our consultation, it has been decided that we must have you leave Ayabe as soon as possible, even if it be but one day sooner. Since all the officers requested us three to negotiate with you, we had no choice but to come, so please listen well. As a disciple, it is truly heart-wrenching and painful for me to tell you, my teacher from whom I have long learned Reigaku, to leave so coldly; however, as long as you remain in Ayabe, it hinders the teachings of the First Kyōso-sama and the divine plan cannot be accomplished, causing the hearts of the officers and believers to become divided and impossible to unite, so please return to Anao for about a year. After that, if there is a divine connection again, we shall consult among ourselves and come to welcome you from this side. To tell you the truth, your arrival in Ayabe was a year or two too early.'

Thus he was making his demand for withdrawal without a shred of shame. Kiraku remained silent, offering no reply, and as he stared at Haruzau's face until it seemed he would bore a hole through it, smiling slightly, Haruzau looked uneasy, his face turning pale as he hung his head and shook it incessantly. Then, Adachi Masanobu, wearing the face of a plenipotentiary commissioner, spoke:

Adachi: 'The reason I have come to speak to you today, Sensei, is not merely my own personal opinion. First and foremost, I have come as a representative of all the officers and believers, including Ushitora no Konjin-sama, and you must listen to me with that understanding. Originally, there was a splendid Konkō church in Ayabe that opened the path of Tenchi Kane no Kami-sama, but Deguchi Onao-sama became agitated and secretly consulted with Shikata Heizō-san, and without a word of consultation with us officers and believers, invited a teacher of Reigaku from a different sect, and eventually caused the Konkō church to be completely destroyed; as you know, the Konkō religion is a properly recognized church under the direct jurisdiction of the Shintō Honkiyoku, and it is a path that engages in missionary work without fear of the world. If I may say so, the Kinmei Reigakkai that you, Uheda-san, have established has not received any official approval, and even if you preach with such an air of importance, it will ultimately come to nothing. No matter how much you, Deguchi Onao-sama, and Shikata Heizō-san exert yourselves, you will be crushed by the authorities in an instant. If that happens, you will have no face to show everyone, so while the ground is still visible beneath your feet, leave as soon as possible. You may call yourself a founder or a president now, but if you trace the origins, you are nothing but an uneducated old woman who used to buy waste paper or a milkman, and no matter how hard you try, it is beyond discussion, so pull out of here while you still have some dignity. As for Onao-sama, we will take good care of her at the Konkō church, so please make up your mind and give us a definite answer now. Are you so reluctant to leave Ayabe even though you are hated by everyone here? It seems you are a troublesome wanderer with nowhere else to go. Does this make you angry? If it makes you angry, then just to show you, why don't you try to exert yourself and build a church in the center of a grand city, not in a countryside like this? Well, since one cannot know the end of a person, even someone as incomprehensible as you might, as the saying goes that even a dog walking might hit a stick, eventually become someone great, though I cannot say for sure.'

He pressed him with a mocking tone. Kiraku, overwhelmed by the excessive insults and abusive language, had no answer, and remained silent with his head bowed. Adachi, with a pleasant smile on his face, glanced at Kiraku sideways and walked down the narrow slope, swinging his arms. Nakamura Takezau, while grinning, said:

Nakamura: 'Uheda-san, if you trace your origins, are you not a peasant who cuts frogs, and having risen in the world a little, became a milkman? Furthermore, what is this about Reigaku or Saniha, coming to this deep, rural countryside to deceive people? You cannot keep your tail hidden forever. Even if they say Ayabe is a mountain village, there are those among us whose eyes are open. A peasant's son becoming an audacious Shindauka—such rebellion will not work. After all, the frog-leaping of a worm-cutter always has a certain earthy smell to it. Even in a small town like Ayabe, there is no fool who would be as freely manipulated as you are and still be happy. Rather than doing something that does not suit your nature, return to Anō as soon as possible and go back to being a peasant. No matter how much a frog's child, a tadpole, resembles a catfish's child, once it begins to grow a little, hands sprout, feet sprout, and before you know it, the tail falls off, and it cannot help but become a dung-frog, just like its ancestors. It is the law of heaven and earth that it can never become a catfish. Even I, having studied the path of the Lord God of Heaven and Earth for nine years, and having read the Fudesaki of the Ushitora no Konjin from morning till night for eight years, still find it so difficult that I cannot yet preach to others satisfactorily; yet you, who were leaping frogs and milking cows until last spring, to say now that you will become a Saniha and teach others about divine possession—the time is a bit too early for that. Go somewhere else as soon as possible and study the path of the Lord God much, much more. If you complete your training and become a fine person, perhaps I will be of service to you again. In Ayabe, we have the number one divine medium in Japan, such as Shikata Haruzō-san, and furthermore, a living god like Great Teacher Fukushima has appeared as the time has arrived. You must know it too, but did you not receive a letter just two or three days ago from your mother in Anō, saying that you should return as soon as possible to help with the farming, and that you are not at an age to be wandering around forever? If you return willingly now, you will be able to apologize to the Lord God of Heaven and Earth, and the many officials and divine mediums will be overjoyed; above all, it is an act of filial piety to your mother in Anō. No matter how much the Kyōso-sama pulls you along, and no matter how much you are disliked by so many people, do you still want to stay in Ayabe? You are a cowardly and irresolute person, quite unlike your appearance. Everyone is saying behind your back that you are a complete coward.'

He exhausted every possible insult and mockery, devising means to make Kiraku angry and drive him away. Kiraku's heart was boiling over. At the very moment when the string of his patience was about to snap, Deguchi Sumiko came climbing up to Hongū-san, and said:

Sumiko: 'Sensei, the Kyōso-sama has been saying for some time that he cannot see you, and he is very worried, so Heizō-san and Yūsuke-san are searching everywhere for you. I thought you must have gone up to Hongū-san, so I came to meet you. Come, let us return quickly, for the Kyōso-sama is waiting for you; please come and eat your meal together.'

Taking this as a good opportunity to leave, Kiraku left Shikata and Nakamura behind and descended from Hongū-san. The two watched his retreating figure, clapped their hands firmly, and...

Kyōso: 'Wahai wahai, it is a fitting match, long live the Gofūfu!'

They were teasing them thus. At that time, Sumiko and he were not yet a Fūfu or anything of the sort; they were in a Mukuwankei relationship. Nevertheless, the Riyōnin were turning their thoughts to a strange matter and laughing. About an Ichijikan later, the three from before returned to the Hiroma with composed faces.

Jibun was sitting in Tanza in a Hitoma, tilting his head and lost in Shian as to whether he should Taikiyo from this place for the time being. However, reflecting upon the spirit of Nahohi... no, no, he could not Misu the Motsuka state of the Kinmeikuwai Yakuin and their Kamugakari Jiyōtai and simply leave. If he were to return now, everything would surely fall into chaos. He thought he would endure with Nintai upon Nintai, suppress his Munen once more, and after calming the Seishin of Karera, decide his course of action. Just as he was thinking this, the Kyōso, together with Heizō-san, quietly pushed open the fusuma and entered, taking a seat before him. The Kyōso spoke first:

Kyōso: 'Sensei, you seem to be contemplating returning to Anō, but that will not do. Since you are a person drawn here by the convenience of the Lord God, you cannot leave Ayabe no matter what happens. I am sorry to trouble you, but unless you endure for the sake of the Lord God to the very end, the essential divine plan will not be accomplished. Even if the Yakuin and believers oppose us and not a single person comes near, as long as Deguchi Nao and you, Sensei, remain in this Hiroma, the divine plan of the Lord God will be magnificently accomplished, as the Ushitora no Konjin says. Therefore, no matter what hardships arise, and no matter how much opposition there may be, you must not leave this place. Heizō-san, please be firm. As I have just said to Sensei, the Lord God will not let you go no matter what. So, Heizō-san, pull yourself together, listen to the teachings of Sensei, and do not be led astray by what other Kamugakari or Yakuin say. Those who wish to open the teachings of Konkō-sama may do so as they please. We three must maintain an unmoving resolve to the end, so please keep that in mind. Sensei, I beg of you, truly.'

Having said this, she withdrew to her own room. From then on, the attitude of Shikata Heizō changed completely, and he came to protect Ueda both in shadow and in light, and at last, the foundation of Ōmoto began to solidify.

Tsuchida Yūkō, who had been a teacher at the former Konkōkyō, was moved by the power of Jibun's spiritual studies and went up to Kyoto. He gathered old friends and others to open a branch of the Kinmeikuwai at the residence of Taniguchi Fusajirō, south of Shio-kōji Shichijō. After a joint consultation, they sent a man named Taniguchi Kumakichi to Ayabe for the purpose of training. Due to the earnest teachings of Jibun, after two or three weeks, he had learned the spiritual arts in a general way, and first of all, his clairvoyance began to function. Thereupon, the person himself became extremely arrogant, thinking that there was no one who had reached the level of spiritual arts that he had, that Shikata Haruzō and his like were not even worth counting, and that the reason he had become able to see through the past, present, and future must be due to his own heaven-sent spiritual ability. Triumphantly, he appeared before the Kyōso and, with brazen face,

Taniguchi: 'This Taniguchi is a soul with a predestined connection chosen by the Lord God, and I am surely the one who should become the Kyōshu of Ōmoto in the future. If it were not so, I could not have improved so much with only two or three weeks of training. It must surely be the result of a connection from the past and divine assistance. From this day forward, though I am unworthy, I intend to serve the divine work, manifest the magnificent Ushitora no Konjin-sama to the world, and perform the duties of the Kyōshu. Therefore, it would be best if you gave Ueda-san a suitable amount of money as a token of gratitude for his help until now, and sent him back to Anō.'

Having been requested to retreat in reverse, Taniguchi, whose calculations had gone completely awry, turned pale, left the presence of the Kyōsosama with a look of failure, immediately rushed to Uhedani, and began a great movement regarding his second strategic plan.

In the Fudesaki of the Kyōsosama, it was written repeatedly,

This man was originally a feudal retainer of Yodo and had worked as an elementary school teacher, but a Konkuō Kyōkuwai branch had been established there; he would visit that church whenever he had leisure, throw logic at the teacher in charge, cause various obstructions, and finally, he was a man who had beaten that church into a mess. Sugita Masajirō, the head of the Kyoto Shimabara branch of the higher-level church, had foolishly taken him under his own wing, paid him a proper salary, and used him as a clerk.

When the Deguchi Kyōsosama first became Kamugakari, Mr. Shikata Heizō and his wife were staying in a place called Tsuchida village in Minami-Kuwata. At that time, because they were listening to the teachings of Konkuō-kyō from a teacher named Ōhashi Kamejirō of the Konkuō Kyōkuwai in Kameoka, they tried talking to Ōhashi in Kameoka about the Kyōsosama. However, the Deguchi Kyōsosama could not be satisfied with such things.
