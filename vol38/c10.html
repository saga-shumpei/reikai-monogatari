Chapter 10: The Episode of 思ひ出 (Part I) [1047]

思ひ出（一）〔1047〕

§ 1

It is a story from the time when the Saidan of Ōmoto was on the second floor of the Kiuhiromahe, in Jifuguwatsu of Meiji Sanjifuyonen. The police would come almost every day, insisting that they would not permit Fukyō unless it received Ninka as a Shiukyō. Ever since the freedom of Shinkyō was granted by the promulgation of the Kenpafu of Meiji Nijifuninen, even if one argued that such a thing should not be the case, they would not listen at all. In the end, they even went so far as to station a Jiyunsa on guard duty in front, and it reached the point where even the Shinjiya were repelled and would not come. Finding this troublesome, the thought arose to boldly reorganize into a Hōjin Soshiki called the Kuōdaukuwai, and to go to a person named Nagasaha Yūtate in Shizuoka to consult. However, the Kyōso-sama inquired of the Kami, and the instruction was that no matter what the police might say, one should not care and should leave it as it was; yet, as the interference of the police became increasingly intense, it became impossible to simply leave it alone. Therefore, keeping it a secret from the Kyōso-sama, they took Kinoshita (Deguchi) Keitarō and set out for Shizuoka.

During their absence, the Kyōso-sama heard of this matter and declared that the conduct of Ueda Kisaburō (the former name of Mizugetsu) was an outrageous act contrary to the Shinchoku, and equivalent to the conduct of Lord Susanoo-no-Mikoto in the age of the Kami, and hid behind the Iwato within the shrine of Hikohohodemi-no-Mikoto located on the mountainside of Misen.

The two, not dreaming that such a thing had occurred, consulted in Shizuoka and returned, intending to stop by Kyoto-fu to complete the procedures, but because one seal was missing, the procedures could not be completed; thus, they had no choice but to order Kinoshita to return to Ayabe to fetch the seal, while they remained in Kyoto and engaged in Fukyō.

The executives of Ōmoto at that time were truly in a state of chaos; the simple-minded fellows fell into superstition, while the ambitious ones utilized this, thinking that if there were an opening, they would exclude him. And among those ambitious ones, there was also constant infighting. Kinoshita, who had returned to fetch the seal, was like a stone thrown into a pond, with no word from him at all. Then, the Shinjiya who were not waiting came and began to say that they should visit one by one the spirit mediums, like the Inari-sage who were numerous in Kyoto, and test their spiritual power. Having no choice, they went around visiting them one after another, and went about binding many Inari-sage.

明治三十四年十月、大本の祭壇が、旧広前の二階にあつた頃の話である。警察署から毎日の様にやつて来て、宗教として認可を受けなければ布教を許さないと云つて頑張るのである。明治二十二年憲法の発布によつて信教の自由が許されてから、そんな筈はないと理窟を言つて見ても堂しても承知しない。仕舞には巡査を前に張番させると云つた様な訳で、信者までが嫌つて遣つて来ない様な始末だ。是では困るから思いきつて皇道会といふ法人組織に改めやうとして、静岡の長沢雄楯と云ふ人の処へ相談に行かうと考へたところが、教祖様は神様に伺はれて、仮令警察から何と云つて来ようと構はぬから、其儘に打捨てて置けと云ふお話であるが、警察の干渉は益々激しくなる一方なので、如何しても打捨てて置くといふ訳には行かなくなつて来た。そこで教祖様へは内密にして、木下（出口）慶太郎を連れて静岡へ出掛けたのである。

留守中に教祖様は此事を聞かれて、上田喜三郎（瑞月旧名）の所業は神勅に反く怪しからぬ所業だ、神代の須佐之男尊の御行跡と等しきものだと云つて、弥仙の中腹にある彦火々出見命のお社の内へ岩戸隠れをされて仕舞つた。

そんな事とは夢にも知らぬ両人は、静岡で相談をして帰り、京都府へよつて手続をしようとしたのであつたが、印形が一つ足らぬので手続が出来なくなり、止むを得ず木下に命じて印形を取りに綾部へ返し、自分は京都に滞在して布教に従事して居た。

其頃の大本の幹部は実に混沌たるものであつて、愚直な連中は迷信に陥つて仕舞ひ、野心家はそれを利用して、隙があつたら自分を排斥しやうといふ考へであつた。そして其野心家の間にも亦絶えず暗闘があつたのである。印形を取りに帰つた木下は梨の礫で一向消息がない。そして待つて居ない信者の連中がやつて来て、京都に沢山ある稲荷下げの様な交霊術者を一々訪問して、霊力を試して見やうと云ひ出した。仕方がないから片端から廻つてあるいて、沢山な稲荷下げを縛つて歩いた。

§ 2

I heard a rumor that there was an Inarisa named Aoshiba Tsuyu in Yokōchi, Fushimi, who was worshipped by the people of Fushimi, so I went to visit. Thinking that if I went in a very daudau manner I would surely be turned away, I disguised myself as a hiyakushiyō. My dōgeushiya were a group called Matsui, Matsūra, Tanaka Toku, Tokida, and Mimaki. When I asked her to consult the Kamisama as to where Uheda Kisaburō, who had left home and whose whereabouts were unknown, might be, she cleared her throat in a motsutai manner and said that the person had taken one hundred ryō of kinsu and absconded, and had gone in the direction of the southeast. When I said that in that case I would like her to kanashiba him, she said that seven ryō of kinsu would be required to bind him. Then I stepped forward and said that I was actually suffering from an illness and would like her to consult as to what kind of illness it might be; she tapped a short gohei with a clatter and said that there was a large snake in my abdomen, and that a large snake which had been at the storehouse in the northwest direction of my residence had entered my abdomen. When I said that there was a pond in the northwest of my residence but no storehouse, she began to say things that were beyond reason, telling me not to say rude things to the Kamisama, so Tokida revealed the disguise and began to harass her, saying, 'Do not talk nonsense; this person is Uheda Kisaburō himself, and he is not suffering from any illness or anything of the sort. We have come to gather material for a newspaper, so put today's events into writing and certify them as the truth.' Since it was the day after the festival of the local deity of Yokōchi, which fell on the ninth of the tenth month, the young men of the village were gathered around in groups, perhaps due to the aftermath of the festival. Once they heard this, it was unbearable; they began saying things like, 'An outsider has come to our Odaisama's place to cause trouble, finish him off, make him drink the water of the Yodogawa River,' and it became a great uproar, with them calling in a ruffian turned monk named Okada Ryōsen and police officers rushing to the scene. Seeing this, my five companions seemed to value their lives, for they sided with the enemy and cowered. Thinking back on it now, it was fortunate that the five companions sided with the enemy, for the crowd completely mistook me for Okada Ryōsen, beat the five companions and Ryōsen mercilessly, and, thinking I was Ryōsen, said, 'Please, come this way,' and led me into the house. Not knowing when my identity would be exposed and feeling uneasy, I slipped away and had the police station protect me. I heard later that at this time at Ōmoto, the large water jars and earthenware vessels before the altar suddenly broke, the sacred sake bottles overturned, and even at the homes of believers in Kyoto, items on the kamidana fell and broke, causing an uproar as to what could be happening. It is said that my real mother, who possessed tennantsū, saw the figure of Kiraku sitting calmly in the midst of being surrounded by a large number of people, and was thus at ease. When I left the police station, the case of mistaken identity was cleared up and they began to make a fuss again, but a police officer escorted me as far as Fushimi, and the believers in Kyoto, worried, came to meet me as far as Fushimi, so I arrived safely in Kyoto and settled at the home of Nishimura Eijirō in Nishinotoin. Just when I thought I was safe, the group of five would not accept it. Setting aside the fact that they themselves had sided with the enemy, they lined up their grievances about being beaten mercilessly and pressed me, saying, 'If you have spiritual power, why did you not stop the violence of hundreds of people? It must be that you are a guardian deity of the four-legged evil for having let the five of us be beaten instead. Quickly reveal your true form, heal the painful places where we were beaten, and if you cannot heal us, commit seppuku.' When I said that I would commit seppuku at any time if it were for the sake of the nation, but that my life was not so cheap as to commit seppuku here, they said, 'You coward, because your conduct is bad, the Kyōso-sama has gone into Iwato-gakure. There is no longer any need to go to Ōmoto, so go wherever you like.' When I said that if that was the case, I had no choice but to go elsewhere, they said, 'If you are going elsewhere, apologize, go around three times and bark like a dog,' and other such unreasonable things. I thought that this was a time to be patient, recalling the story of Han Xin crawling through the legs, and I endured it. Then I apologized to them, saying, 'I have done a terrible thing, please forgive me.' Just then, a Kyoto chivalrous man named Iroha Kōtarō happened to arrive, and he had his subordinate Yamada Jūtarō accompany me, so I was able to return to Ayabe safely.

Upon returning, I found my Nimotsu had been Hitsukuku and Katazu, and Nakamura Takezau and Shikata Heizau were bullying me again, saying, 'Such a four-legged Shiyugojin who disturbs the world must not be allowed to remain; get out.' While things were thus, a summons for Kyōsosama arrived from the Keisatsushiyo; when I asked what it was about, I was told that it was a Kisoku Ihan for Kyōsosama to have broken the Jiyōmahe of the Jinjiya on Misenzan and secluded himself there, and that he must pay a Batsukin. Kyōsosama had been secluded in the shrine on Misenzan, quietly writing his Fudesaki, but upon hearing the sound of villagers approaching the Shiyazen, he had peeked out. Since a Hakuhatsu Rauba had suddenly appeared from an unexpected place, it was no wonder the Murabito were startled, and it became a Heuban that a Hihizaru was living inside the Jinjiya on Misenzan. Although the trees have been cut down and it is bright now, the Misenzan of that time was a sacred mountain where ancient trees grew thick and it was dark even in the daytime. The rumor that a Hihizaru was there grew, and in the end, it was decided that the whole village would go to exterminate the Hihizaru. It became a great uproar, with people carrying bamboo spears, police officers joining in, and the priest coming along. When everyone rushed to Misenzan, Ushino Ichitarō, who happened to be bringing a bento to Kyōsosama, was there, so it was understood that it was not a Hihizaru. When everyone surrounded Kyōsosama and asked, 'Why are you here?', Kyōsosama replied calmly, 'The world is in darkness, so I have hidden myself.' When everyone said in unison, 'Are you an idiot from the Tenrikyo of Ayabe? Get out quickly!', Kyōsosama replied with composure, 'Today is the seventh day since I began my seclusion, so even if you all tell me not to come out, this is the day I am coming out; while you are at it, please escort me to Uesugi.'

When I went to Uesugi and arrived at the police box, they were just about to put Kyōsosama into the interrogation room. I said that I would enter the interrogation room myself, so please do not put Kyōsosama in, and then I underwent the interrogation. When they asked, 'Why did a woman enter a place where women are forbidden?', I retorted by saying, 'The barrier has been lifted by the proclamation of the fourth year of Meiji, so what is this about women being forbidden?', and they could not say a word. Then, they asked, 'Do you not think it is illegal to break a lock and enter a building managed by others?', and although I was a bit troubled by this, I told a lie, saying, 'There should be no problem, as it is stated in the shrine laws that those who enter for the purpose of faith are an exception.' Since I did not have the shrine laws at hand, they could not check, so they let me go home safely. However, Kyōsosama had asked the priest in advance, and the priest had secretly allowed him to seclude himself there.

The uproar over Kyōsosama's seclusion on Misenzan was settled safely with this, but what was not settled was the state of mind of the leading members. Because they could not accept my actions, they claimed that this was the work of a four-legged evil Shiyugojin called Komatsubayashi, and they kept cursing that evil Shiyugojin. If that were the case, they should have just driven Kiraku out, but because Kiraku's physical body had become one that must exist for the sake of divine work according to Kyōsosama's Fudesaki, they could not drive him out, and since they were looking at my one body in two different ways, it was unbearable. Eventually, I was put into a six-mat room and my every move began to be monitored. When they came near me, they would sprinkle salt, so I would tease them by saying, 'Are you sprinkling salt to purify yourselves because your bodies are dirty before you approach me?', which would make them turn bright red and get angry. 久子『先生如何して改心が出来ませぬか、さういふ御心得だから、教祖様が岩戸隠れなどをされるのだ。早く改心をして下さい……』

などと熱心に云つて来る。教祖様の岩戸隠れなども、年寄の吾儘だから仕方がない……と答へて、何の斯のと面倒だから、腹が痛いと云ふと……それ見なさい、改心をしないから、腹が痛むのだといふ。

喜楽『よし腹の痛いのは改心をしない証拠か、それなら証拠を見せてやる』

と云つて鎮魂をすると、久子サンが俄かに腹痛を起して、痛い痛いといつて大苦しみに苦しむで、五日間も呻吟した。教祖さまが『早く先生に謝罪せよ』と云はれたものだから、四方平蔵が謝罪に来た。

喜楽『いや代人では無効だから本人をよこせ』

と云つてやつたので、とうとう久子サンが謝罪に来た事もあつた。さうすると幹部の連中が又やつて来て、

『先生は何といふ悪い事をする人であるか、是は如何しても悪魔だ、術を見せろ、裸になつて見せろ』

と云ふ。五月蠅いから近寄つて来る中村竹蔵をウンと睨んでやると、又腹痛を起して……痛い痛いと云つて苦み出した。斯う云つた様な次第で、……会長（瑞月）は悪魔だ、会長のいふ事を聞くな……と云つて、幹部の連中が切りに方々へ云ひふらして歩行いたものである。
