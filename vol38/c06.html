Chapter 6: 曲の猛 [1043]

曲の猛〔1043〕

§ 1

Shikata Gennosuke and Nishimura Bunuemon, the Riyōshi, continued their conversation while drinking tea offered by Kiraku.

Shikata: 'In the Ohiroma of the Kinmeikuwai, many Konjinsama and Riujinsama who had fallen into the world since Senjitsu are now, as the Ushitora no Konjinsama is graciously manifesting in this world, to be raised up to this world at this time to save their Mitama; for if they are not saved now, they will never be able to rise for Mangofumatsudai. Therefore, when the Saniha of Uheda returns to Ayabe, he causes various obstructions in the Shinkai, such as sealing them away, driving them off, or casting Reibaku, calling them gods of the Jiyashinkai. Because of this, it is regrettable, but the Lord said that He would prevent Uheda from returning to Ayabe for a while. Just as Kyōsosama said, a sudden heavy rain fell, the Wachi River rose by one shō and two gō, and the great bridge at Ayabe was washed away; so I believe that Uheda-san being unable to return to Ayabe is entirely due to the vast and boundless divine virtue of the Kami of Deguchi. Since the Kami greatly dislike the teacher, even if you return to Ayabe this time, unless you cease to assert your ego as you have until now, and follow the words of the Kami of Deguchi and the Kamigakari-san in all matters, things will always go awry, and it will become impossible for the teacher to remain in Ayabe. We have come to offer our advice quietly because we hold the teacher dear. As they say, the nail that sticks out gets hammered down, so it is for the peace of the world and for your own benefit, teacher, not to antagonize any Kami.'

They offered this advice. Kiraku, who was well-educated and even served as a town councilor in the village, was so stunned that he did not even know how to respond, thinking that such people would say such things. After offering various opinions to Kiraku, the two departed.

Just as they were leaving, hearing that Kiraku had returned to Uetani, Deguchi Sumiko came running in secret.

Sumiko: 'Teacher, while you were away, Shikata Haruzō-san, Murakami-san, Kuroda-san, Shiomi-san, and others returned to the Ohiroma and engaged in reckless Kamigakari, saying nonsensical things, jumping and leaping, and in the end, even running outside while naked. They said that Uheda is the great enemy of the Shinkai, so even if he returns, he must not be allowed into the Kinmeikuwai, and that everyone should gather and throw him out. They claimed that this is the origin of the three worlds, and that having just this much is enough, and that Uheda is the guardian deity of evil gods and has the spirit of an ogre. Saying they would perform an ogre-exorcism, Haruzō-san drew a picture of a horned face on your likeness, teacher, and hammered nails into it, beat it, and spat upon it. They are very much bothered by you and speak only evil things, so all the believers take it to heart and say one after another that if that is the kind of teacher you are, you should leave. I had no choice, so I thought I would tell Kyōsosama about it, but when I spoke to him, Kyōsosama just looked calm and said, "Leave everything to the Shinkai," and remained silent. I do not understand what is going on at all, so I want you to return as soon as possible to open everyone's eyes, and I ran here alone to inform you, hiding it from the officers and believers.'

She reported this with an agitated expression. Thereupon, Kiraku entrusted the subsequent training of the practitioners to Shikata Tōtarō-san and was about to return to Ayabe for the time being, when Yūsuke-jii-san, as usual, came running, and while weeping with his hands on the ground, said:

Yūsuke: 'I must tell you something, teacher. From yesterday evening until noon, it was so noisy that people from all over town gathered as if to watch a play, and the gate and the road were crowded like a mountain, and many came to mock us, which was very troublesome. However, thinking they were just blind people who knew nothing, the officers and believers were trying their best to practice the Yūsai without engaging them. Then, in the evening, a rickshaw puller from Nishihata named Koman was pulling an empty rickshaw through a side alley... and shouting in a loud voice, "Konjin, Konjin, you stupid Konjin, you crazy Konjin, you dream Konjin, you beggar Konjin, you are a god that no decent human would ever visit!" He listed various evil things while passing by, making many spectators laugh, and when he overturned his rickshaw into a muddy field, a person who happened to be passing by saw it and said, "You spoke too much ill of Konjinsama, so you have received divine punishment." The rickshaw puller Koman got angry and tried to strike the person, who was so surprised that he fled toward the west as fast as he could. Then Koman shouted, "What is this talk of punishment for a believer of Konjin just because someone is covered in mud? Drag the fellow who spat that out here right now!" He stormed into the Ohiroma, and seeing Zentarō-san of Nishihara who was visiting, he claimed he was the "gaki" wearing a white yukata and that he must be the one. He climbed onto the sacred altar with his shoes on, went on a rampage, and smashed the sacred implements of the Kami one after another. Many townspeople found it amusing and cheered him on, saying, "Go on, conquer the Konjin!" and mocked us, with not a single person intervening. After speaking ill of the Kami to his heart's content, he left around twelve o'clock that night. All the believers are trembling with fear. Even though the police are nearby, they do not protect us, saying that Adachi-san's conduct is always bad. This old man is truly, truly sorry and cannot bear it.'

Then, he began to cry, letting out a voice slowly.

Kogarashi ya inu no hoetsuku kabe no mino

Wiping away his tears, Iusuke-jii-san began to lament slowly once again.

Iusuke: 'Sensei-sama, please listen to me well. The Deguchi-no-Kamisama took the pension and annuity of Seikichi-san, who passed away in Taiwan during the Nitsushin-Sensō, and saying that this was money exchanged for his Inochi, kept it without spending even a single Ichimon. He pulled it out from the Ginkau and bought two koku of precious Hakumai for us, but because everyone comes out and eats it every day, there is Mohaya not even an Isshiyō left. So, the Deguchi-no-Kamisama again took money from the Ginkau and bought Hakumai and oil for us, but these days, even for Taneabura alone, five or six shiyō are needed in one Ichinichi. Even so, because the Jiyashinkai is still dark, He says through the Kamugakari of the three-san, "Light the Tōmiyō," so we light twenty or thirty candles at a time. It is a tremendous expense, but they do not try to offer even one sen of money, and everything is treated as a good thing. They only gnaw at the hands and feet of the Deguchi-no-Kamisama, and there is not a single sincere believer who shows concern or care. It is truly pitiful, and this old man also feels apologetic to the Kamisama. Shihō Heizō-san says he is skilled at Ten-gantsū, and becoming one with the three-san, he assumes a strange posture as if looking through a telescope, and when the three-san say, "Heizō-dono, look at that," Heizō-san closes his eyes and says, "Yes, yes, I have worshipped, I have worshipped, a great Ryūjin-sama has appeared," and because he is absorbed in it, he does not care about the accounting at all. Nakamura no Take-san, from morning until night, and from night until midnight, becomes like a fool, and with the Ofudesaki, he reads and reads it to exhaustion with a melody like an Ukare-bushi, laughing "Aha, aha, oho, oho," and is of no use at all. The Deguchi-no-Kamisama is only doing the work of the Ofudesaki, and even though there is such a great uproar, He remains with an indifferent face. I am also very worried about what will happen, so my bald head, which was not thick to begin with, has become even balder, and on top of that, it has become shiny black from the smoke of the hearth. All the officers tease me, saying, "Your hard work is black, Iusuke." Ah, it is ridiculous. If it were not for the sake of the Kamisama, I could live in comfort in my retirement; who would do such a thing?'

So saying, he stroked his black face mixed with tears with his black hands, and while spraying saliva from his mouth filled with tooth-dirt onto the face of Kiraku, he was speaking with all his might. Then, Kiraku ran his brush over a sheet of Hanshi that was nearby,

Hakutō torii mo kami mo naki mama ni / Kurō kurō to guchi o Iusuke

and when he wrote and gave it to him,

Iusuke: 'Aha, aha, aha, this is grateful.'

He accepted it with joy, tucked it into his bosom, and returned to Ayabe at full speed.

Then, on the third day, this old man came walking briskly again, took something like a letter out of a Furoshiki bundle as if it were very important, and said,

Iusuke: 'Sensei, this is, with all due respect, an Ofudesaki from the Ushioni-no-Konjin-sama to Ueda-sensei regarding his attention, so please look at it with great care.'

When Kiraku opened it immediately to look, it was written in an irregular and messy hand,

"The Ushioni-no-Konjin will pay a little attention to Ueda. Because the Kami has His reasons, you must take all the practitioners and return. It is scary if you disobey this command of the Kami, and so on..."

It was written thus. To awaken Iusuke-jii-san from his delusion, Kiraku tore the letter to pieces right before his eyes. The old man was startled and said,

Iusuke: 'Ah, Sensei, that is Motsutai. If you do such a thing, the divine punishment will strike you immediately.'

§ 3

He becomes Yakuki. Kiraku says to Iusuke-san,

Kiraku: 'What is there to worry about? You should just tear up that fan you say you received from the Ushiudo Konjin. You will never be struck by divine punishment or anything of the sort.'

Encouraged by this, Iusuke-san, an Ahaushiyōjikimono who follows whatever anyone says, tears it up right there with a ripping sound. Seeing that neither his hands nor his feet seem to be twisted, Iusuke-san slowly begins to stamp his feet in a Jidanda.

Iusuke: 'Have I, an old man with a bald head like this, been deceived by a Murakami who is only eighteen or so? Ah, how Zannen, Shigoku, and Kuchio this is!'

Throwing that fan onto the Daichi, he stomps and kicks it; the Yōsu of it was so Oka and pitiful that it was beyond description.

Then, accompanying Iusuke-san, he returns to the Hiroma of the Kinmeikuwai. The Ohiromahe is overflowing with Shinjiya, and outside, Kenbutsunin are piled up like a mountain, mocking the interesting Kamugaka of the Jiyashin.

As soon as Kiraku Hai into the interior, he sees that someone has possessed Murakami Fusanosuke, who is running wild, deceiving many Shinjiya and toying with them by telling them to go here and there. The Shinjiya, knowing nothing of the reason, think he is a god and, being afraid, are doing exactly as they are told, saying 'Hei-hei, hai-hai.'

Upon seeing his face, Murakami says,

Murakami: 'Oh, Ueda, you have returned well. This is Komatsubayashi-no-Mikoto. You are a great fellow who listened well to the orders of the Ushiudo Konjin. As a reward for that, I shall bestow this upon you, so you had best preserve it with care.'

As he presents a large bone fan with something crumpled written on it, acting as if it were precious, Kiraku, thinking this is a good opportunity to awaken the many officers and Shinjiya, takes it and, before he can even hold it properly, strikes Murakami on the head three or four times with that large fan. The Shinjiya each look on with puzzled faces, staring only at Kiraku's face.

From the direction of the Oku-no-ma, the voices of the aforementioned three people cry out in thin voices,

'Ueda-dono has just returned. Ōkami-sama, please strike him with divine punishment quickly.'

Kokoro naki yojin no soshiri nani ka sen / Kami ni makaseshi wami nariseba
