Chapter 26: Day の出 [1063]

日の出〔1063〕

§ 1

In the summer of Meiji Sanjifuninen, while Kiraku, the Saniha, was in the midst of Iusai Shiugiyō at the Shiugiyōba in Uhedani, Komatsubayashinomikoto descended upon him in Kamugakari.

'No matter what persecution or Atsupaku may occur, you must not leave Ayabe. At any rate, you must endure in Ayabe until the fifteenth day of the first month of Meiji Sanjifugonen.'

Such was the divine instruction. Therefore, Kiraku endured all persecution and Bujiyoku, waiting for Sanjifugonen while diligently practicing the way of the Kamisama.

When the fifteenth day of the first month of Sanjifugonen finally arrived, Kiraku asked, 'How shall I proceed from now on?'

Upon receiving the Shinji which stated, 'From tomorrow morning, quietly set out toward the Sonobe region,' he prepared light gear, told only Sumiko of his intention, and set out on the path of missionary work.

Sumiko was pregnant for the first time and was already in her final month. It was a situation where she might give birth at any moment. As it was the birth of his very first child, he was deeply concerned and could not help but worry.

However, having once entrusted his life to the Kamisama, he resolved that he could not neglect his divine duties for even half an hour for the sake of his wife, and after consulting with her, they departed.

First, he settled at a general store called Okumura in Sonobe Honmachi, and through the kind care of the master and his wife, he was allowed to use their detached house for free, and was provided with all necessities of life by Okumura, while he engaged in propagation day and night.

Mr. Okumura held a considerable position and reputation in Sonobe and possessed wealth as well. He was a gentleman known for his integrity and purity. Because Mr. Okumura took the lead and enthusiastically engaged in propagation alongside his business, the local gentlemen competed with one another to join the faith.

On the night of the twelfth day after settling in Sonobe, he had a dream that Sumiko, whom he had left behind in Ayabe, had given birth, so when he asked the Kamisama, the answer was that she had indeed given birth, so he should return for a while.

Thereupon, he informed Mr. Okumura of the matter and asked him to look after things during his absence, ordered an old woman named Asai Hana to serve at the altar, and set out all alone, walking briskly to the home of an enthusiastic believer named Minosuke Sakahara in the Sakahara of Hiyama, where he was served lunch and was taking a rest, when an old man named Yūsuke Yotsukata from Ayabe came looking for him and said from the gate,

Yūsuke: 'Is Kaichō-san at home?'

Sakahara Minosuke had become disgusted with the methods of the Nakamura faction in Ayabe and believed only in the teachings of Kiraku, so he had come looking for him, thinking that Kiraku must surely be there.

Kiraku, who had been reciting Norito in the inner room, heard this voice and quietly went out to the front, saying,

Kiraku: 'Ah, Yūsuke-san, you have come all this way, please have a cup of tea.'

As he said this, the old man stood firmly in the garden and said with a harsh voice, as if glaring at him,

Yūsuke: 'Look here, Kaichō, what are you dawdling for? A terrible thing has happened in Ayabe.'

Kiraku, feeling a bit anxious that some incident might have occurred regarding Sumiko, asked,

Kiraku: 'Yūsuke-san, did Sumiko give birth safely?'

Yūsuke shook his head vigorously and said,

Yūsuke: 'Eh, birth or whatever! While your own wife is in her final month, you hide from the Kyōso-sama and wander about here and there, what are you doing being so calm and relaxed? A terrible thing has happened in Ayabe. That is why the Kyōso-sama says not to go anywhere. If you disobey the orders of the Kyōso-sama, it always turns out like this.'

He shouted these incomprehensible things while panting for breath. Kiraku, wrapped in increasingly anxious clouds, said,

Kiraku: 'Sumiko must have had an easy delivery. And was it a boy or a girl, tell me quickly.'

Before he could even finish, Yūsuke shook his head again and said,

Yūsuke: 'Eh, how would a common man like me know such a thing? Kaichō-san, you have the power of divine vision, don't you? If you asked Komatsubayashi, you should be able to figure out that much. If you cannot even understand that, you should not be called a master or claim to have divine possession. Ayabe is in such an uproar. Because you have been turning a deaf ear to what the Kyōso-sama always said—that if you do not repent, such things will happen—this unfortunate event has occurred. Since it is her first birth, the Kyōso-sama is also extremely worried, and I, Yūsuke, do not know how much I have worried as well. You have no face to return to Ayabe so brazenly. If you do not want to return, you need not return. Farewell.'

原文（近代文語）

明治三十二年の夏、上谷の修行場にて幽斎修行の最中審神者の喜楽に小松林命神懸せられ、

『如何なる迫害や圧迫があつても綾部を去つてはならぬ。兎も角明治卅五年の正月十五日までは綾部で辛抱をせよ』

とのお諭しであつた。それで喜楽はあらゆる迫害と侮辱を隠忍して卅五年を待ちつつ、神妙に神様の道を修行して居た。愈卅五年正月十五日が来たので、

喜楽『今後如何しませうか』

と伺つて見た所、

『明日の朝からソツと園部の方面を指して行け』

との神示が降つたので軽装を整へ、只一人澄子に其意を告げ布教伝道の途に上つた。澄子は初めての妊娠で已に臨月であつた。何時出産するかも知れない場合である。自分も大変に初めての子の出産であるから気にかかつて仕方がない。けれども一旦神様に任した身の上、妻の為に神務を半時でも悤にする事は出来ぬと決心し、夫婦相談の上出立したのである。

先づ園部本町の奥村と云ふ雑貨店へ落ちつき、主人夫婦の懇篤なる世話によつて其家の別宅を無料で貸して貰ひ、且つ衣食万端を奥村から支給され日夜宣伝に従事しつつあつた。奥村氏は園部に於て相当の地位名望もあり財産もあつた。さうして清廉潔白の聞えの高い紳士である。其奥村氏が先頭に立つて商業の傍、熱心に宣伝したので、地方の紳士連中は先を争うて入信した。園部へ落ちついてから十二日目の夜に、綾部に残してある澄子が出産した様な夢を見たので、神様に聞いて見ると出産をしたから一先づ帰つてやれと云ふ事であつた。そこで奥村氏に其旨を告げ留守中を頼みおき、浅井はなと云ふ婆サンに神前の御給仕を命じて只一人スタスタと檜山の坂原巳之助と云ふ熱心な信者の宅へ立寄り昼飯を供せられ一服して居ると、其処へ慌ただしく綾部から四方祐助と云ふ爺サンが尋ねて来り門口から、

祐助『海潮サンはお内に居られますか』

と尋ねてゐる。坂原巳之助は綾部の中村一派のやり方に愛想をつかし、喜楽の教のみを信従してゐたのだから、屹度喜楽は此処に居るだらうと思つて尋ねて来たのである。奥の間で祝詞を奏上して居た喜楽は此声を聞いて静かに表へ出て、

喜楽『あゝ祐助さん、能う来て呉れた、まあ一服しなさい』

と云ふと爺サンは庭に立ちはだかつた儘、

祐助『これ海潮、何をグヅグヅして居るのだ。綾部は大変な事が出来て居りますぜ』

とゴツゴツした声で睨めつける様に云ふ。喜楽も何か澄子の身の上に就て変事でも出来たのではないかと、少しく不安の念に駆られて、

喜楽『祐助サン、澄子は機嫌よく出産しましたかな』

と尋ねると、祐助は首をブリブリと振つて、

祐助『えー、出産も糞もあるものか。自分の女房が臨月になつてゐるのに教祖様に隠れて其処ら中をうろつき廻り、悠々閑々と何の事ぢやい。綾部には大変の事が出来ましたぞ。それだから教祖様が何処へも行くでないと仰有るのだ。教祖様の命令を背くと何時でもこの通りなりますのぢや』

と息を喘ませて諒解し難い事を呶鳴りたてる。喜楽は益々不安の雲に包まれて、

喜楽『澄子は安産しただらうなア。そして男か女か何方が出来た、早く知らして呉れ』

と云はせも果てず、祐助は又もや首を頻りに振つて、

祐助『え、凡夫の俺がそんな事分つて堪るものか。海潮サンは天眼通が利くぢやないか、小松林に尋ねたら、それ位の事は分りさうなものぢやないか。それが分らない様な事で偉さうに神懸ぢやの、先生ぢやのとは云ふて貰ひますまい。綾部は何どころの騒ぎぢやないわ。改心をせぬと、こんな事が出来ると何時も教祖さまが仰有つたのを尻に聞かして居るものだから、こんな不都合な事が出来たのぢや。初産の事とて教祖さまも大変な心配、此祐助も何れ丈心配したかしれませぬぞや。お前サンも綾部へヌケヌケと帰つて来る顔がありますまい。帰るのが嫌なら帰らいでも宜しい左様なら』

§ 2

Saying this, he started to walk out of Sakahara's house with a brisk pace. Kiraku, becoming anxious, asked in a small voice:

Kiraku: 'Excuse me, Iusuke-san, is it good or bad? Just tell me that much.'

Iusuke, muttering to himself, 'What use is a Tengantsū if it cannot even tell such things?' ignored the details and ran off, leaving behind a parting remark:

Iusuke: 'You had better think it over well.'

He was already on his way back to Ayabe. Kiraku, flustered, was sent off by Mr. Sakahara and chased after Iusuke with all his might. Upon doing so, Iusuke sat down at a teahouse where the Sannomiya shrine was located, and confident in his own judgment, said:

Iusuke: 'If I say that, he will surely come back.'

It turned out later that after the Yakuin and Shinjiya in Ayabe had consulted, Iusuke had been chosen as their representative to come and fetch him, thinking that if he caused Kiraku to worry by saying, 'In any case, the birth was safe, and a girl was born,' and then saying nothing more, Kiraku would not return, so he would surely come back if he were worried. It was on the very day that he had the dream in Sonobe that the Nahohi was born. That day was the seventh day of the third month, the twenty-eighth day of the first month in the old calendar. When he returned to his home in Ayabe with Iusuke, the sound of a baby crying could be heard in abundance. When he heard his own child's voice for the first time, he felt something that could not be described. However, thinking that while the child was healthy with that voice, he wondered how Sumiko's body was, he stepped over the threshold and found both mother and child to be in excellent health. Then, the Kyōso-sama scolded him severely:

Kyōso-sama: 'Your wife is in her final month and you do not know when the child will be born, yet you dash off everywhere without listening to the command of the Lord God; is that not too indifferent?'

He apologized and remained in seclusion in Ayabe for thirty days until his pilgrimage to the shrine was completed. Then, letters arrived almost daily from Okumura in Sonobe saying, 'Many believers are waiting, so please come quickly,' so on the third day of the fourth month, he dashed out of Ayabe again and continued his missionary work in Sonobe. During his absence, the five hundred volumes of books he had written from the time he was in Anao in the thirty-first year of Meiji until the end of the thirty-fourth year were all burned by the instigation of Heizō Shikata and Takezō Nakamura, who claimed it was for the sake of the Lord's work. After that, using Sonobe as a base, he opened a missionary line to Osaka and, in cooperation with a retired army officer, Lieutenant Colonel of Artillery Kiyotoshi Mizoguchi, engaged in propaganda in Osaka city. A man with a slender back, a refined appearance, and a slightly shiny head, who used to visit the Lieutenant Colonel's house comfortably, was persuaded by Lieutenant Colonel Mizoguchi and became an ardent believer. Gradually, believers of the middle class and above emerged, and it reached the point of planning to buy ten thousand tsubo of land in the vicinity of Tennōji to establish the headquarters of the Reigakkai. The man who had been frequenting Mizoguchi's house was Mr. Masateru Naitō, the head of the Namba branch in Osaka. Mr. Masateru Naitō, Lieutenant Colonel Mizoguchi, and Kiraku, the three of them, toured the Inari-sage churches in Osaka city and performed various spiritual arts competitions, making that their sole pleasure. They neglected their missionary work and spent their time searching for spirit-possessed people in about three hundred and sixty houses in the city, performing spiritual bindings, and boasting of their own spiritual arts, feeling proud of themselves. Thinking about it now, he feels he did truly ridiculous things with pride. However, considering that he gained extraordinary experience regarding spirit possession during this time, perhaps this too was the will of the Lord God. After that, he and a man named Buntatsu Yamada, who had been the president of a bank in Hokkaido, Naitō, and Eitarō Matsubara of the Kyoto Farm, set up a synthetic milk company in Kyoto, Osaka, and Sonobe. They planned to raise several thousand yen to start a business and use it for propaganda expenses. While they had already begun, a certain Takamatsu from Kyoto, under the direction of Takezō Nakamura, was hired as a carpenter for the factory repairs of the company and spoke ill of Kiraku in every way. This became the basis for Eitarō Matsubara, a certain Wakabayashi, and Buntatsu Yamada to change their minds, and they tried to expel Kiraku and Naitō, the initiators who had gone to such trouble to assemble it. Nakamura thought that if he did not somehow hinder Kiraku's activities in the Keihan region, cause him to fail, force him to flee back to Ayabe, and confine him to a single room so he could not act, it would be like letting a tiger loose in the field, and he would surely take over the Ōmoto teachings. This was the result of the consultation among all the Yakuin. Therefore, Kiraku had no choice but to withdraw from the milk company and, with Masateru Naitō, enshrined the Lord God at the foot of Aizen-zaka and began missionary propaganda. There was an ardent believer of Konkōkyō named Makutarō Sugimura, a gynecologist in Namba Shinchi, as well as a religious instructor of Ontakekyō named Megumi Sugimoto, Toshitarō Takaya who worked at the Osaka Ōbayashi District Office, and a tinsmith named Daizō Ikeda, with whom he plotted and carried out a great propaganda campaign. The Osaka chivalrous men, Kumadan and Kahei Yamada, also became believers and began great activities, and he finally saw the dawn. Toshitarō Takaya said he would go to worship in Ayabe once as a representative of everyone, but when he went, Nakamura spoke ill of Kiraku with all his might, lined up deviant words, and swept Mr. Takaya out with a broom, saying, 'People who wear Western clothes are not allowed to come here.' Mr. Takaya returned to Osaka and was indignant, a crack entered the Reigakkai that they had gone to such trouble to assemble, and while things were in a mess, Jisaburō Mimaki came to visit under the secret order of Takezō Nakamura. This man abused Kiraku with the utmost language, so he had no choice but to leave Osaka and return to Ayabe around November of the thirty-sixth year of Meiji. Then, the Yakuin gathered together, stripped off his Western clothes, tore his hat, shoes, and suit to pieces, and threw them into the latrine:

Yakuin: 'There, we have skinned the four-legged beast. With this, Kaichō will surely repent and become docile. It is written in the Ofude that whatever is done against the Lord God will be overturned at ninety-nine percent, so Kaichō-san will surely realize it now.'

While they themselves had obstructed him with all their might, they acted as if it were the work of the Lord God. After that, he again secluded himself in the six-mat room of the annex, and once again hiding and enduring, he began to study classical literature and started writing 'Fude no Shizuku' and 'Michi no Ōmoto', deciding to stay put in Ayabe and wait for the time until August of the thirty-eighth year of Meiji.

原文（近代文語）

と云ひ乍らスタスタと阪原の家を出て行かうとする。喜楽は益す気になつて、

喜楽『これ祐助サン、吉か凶か、どちらか、それだけ聞かして呉れ』

と小さい声で尋ねて見ると祐助は、

祐助『そんな事の分らぬ様な天眼通が何になるものかい』

とブツブツ囁き乍ら委細構はず駆け出し、

祐助『よう思案するがよい』

と捨台詞を残して早くも綾部へ帰つて行く。喜楽は慌て阪原氏に送られ一目散に祐助の後を追ひ駆けた。さうすると祐助は三の宮のある茶店で腰をかけ、

祐助『あゝ云つておけば屹度帰つて来るに相違ない』

と高を括つて居る。

『兎に角安産した。そして女の子が出来た』と云つたら、『あゝさうか』と云つたきり喜楽は帰らぬから心配さしたら帰つて来るだらうと、綾部で役員信者相談の結果祐助が代表者になつて選まれて来たのだと云ふことが後になつて分つた。丁度自分が園部で夢を見た其日に直霊が生れたのである。其日は新三月七日旧正月二十八日であつた。祐助と三人連れで綾部の宅に帰つて見ると、盛に赤児の泣き声が聞えて居る。初めて自分の子の声を聞いた時は何とも云はれぬ感じがした。然しあの声で子は達者であるが、澄子の身体は如何だらうと案じ乍ら門口を跨げて見ると母子とも至極壮健であつた。それから教祖さまに、

『自分の女房が臨月で何時子が出来るか分らぬのに、神様の命令も聞かずに、そこら中に飛び出すのは余り水臭いぢやないか』

と散々叱言を云はれ、謝り入つて三十日の間、宮詣りのすむ迄綾部に蟄居して居た。さうすると園部の奥村から『沢山の信者が待つて居るから早く来て呉れ』と云ふ手紙が毎日の様に出て来るので、四月の三日再び綾部を飛び出し園部で布教をつづけて居た。其留守中に自分が明治三十一年、穴太に居つた時から三十四年一杯かかつて執筆しておいた五百冊の書物を、四方平蔵、中村竹蔵の発起で立替の御用ぢやと云つて悉皆焼いて了つたのである。それから園部を根拠として大阪へ教線を開き、陸軍砲兵中佐の溝口清俊と云ふ休職軍人と心を協せて大阪市の宣伝に従事して居た。此中佐の宅へ心安く遊びに来る、背のスラツとした、人品のよい少し頭の光つた男が溝口中佐に説きつけられて熱心な信者となり、追々中流以上の信者が出来て天王寺の附近に一万坪の地面を買ひ、霊学会の本部を設置する段取とまでなつてきた。さうして其溝口の宅へ出入りして居た男と云ふのは、大阪の難波分所長の内藤正照氏であつた。内藤正照氏と溝口中佐と喜楽の三人は大阪市中の稲荷下げの教会を巡歴して種々と霊術比べをやり、それを唯一の楽しみとして布教は、そつちのけに三百六十軒ばかり市中の神懸を探し廻つて霊縛をしたり、色々と自分等の霊術を誇り得意になつて居た。今から思へば実に馬鹿らしい事を得意になつてしたと思ふ。然し此間に神懸に対する非常な経験を得た事を思へば、これも矢張御神慮であつたかも知れぬ。それから北海道の銀行の頭取をやつて居た山田文辰と云ふ男や内藤や、京都牧場の松原栄太郎等と人造精乳会社を、京都、大阪、園部に設置し、数千円の金を醵出して一つの事業を起し宣伝の費用に当てやうと目論見、已に着手して居る所へ、京都の高松某が中村竹蔵の指図によつて会社の工場修繕の大工に雇はれ散々に喜楽の悪口をなし、それが基となつて松原栄太郎、若林某、山田文辰等が変心し出し、折角組み立てた発頭人の喜楽や内藤を放逐せむとした。中村は何とかして喜楽が京阪地方で活動するのを妨害し、失敗の結果綾部へ逃げ帰り一間へ押し込めて活動の出来ないやうにしてやらねば此儘にして置いては虎を野に放つやうなもので、大本の教をとつて了ふに違ひないと役員一同が相談の結果、かう云ふ手段をとつたのであつた。そこで喜楽は已を得ず精乳会社を脱退し、内藤正照と愛善坂の麓で神様を祀り、布教宣伝に着手して居た。難波新地の婦人科の医者で杉村牧太郎と云ふ金光教の熱心な信者があり、又杉本恵と云ふ御嶽教の教導職や大阪大林区署に勤めて居た高屋利太郎、並びに錻力職の池田大造らと図り大宣伝をやつて居た。大阪の侠客の団熊や山田嘉平等も信者となつて大活動を始め、漸く曙光を認め、高屋利太郎は一同の代表者として一度綾部へ参拝して来やうと云つて詣つて来た所、中村が一生懸命に喜楽の悪口をついて且脱線的の言葉を並べ『洋服を着た様な連中は此処には来る事ならぬ』と高屋氏を箒で掃き出したので、高屋氏が大阪へ帰つて来て憤慨し折角組み立てた霊学会へひびが這入り、ゴタゴタして居る所へ中村竹蔵の内命で三牧次三郎が尋ねて来て、此男が口を極めて喜楽を罵倒したので止むを得ず大阪を立ち綾部へ帰つて来たのは明治三十六年の十一月頃であつた。さうすると役員が寄つて集つて自分の洋服を剥ぎとり、帽も靴も服も引裂いて雪隠へ突つ込んで了ひ、

『さあ、これで四ツ足の皮を剥いでやつた。これで海潮も改心をして、おとなしくなるだらう。神に背いて致した事は何事も九分九厘でグレンと覆るとお筆に出て居りますが、これで海潮サンも気がつくだらう』

と自分等が極力妨害しておき乍ら、神さまの業の様にすまし込んで居る。それから自分も再び離れの六畳に蟄居して又もや隠れ忍んで古典学を研究したり、筆の雫や道の大本等の執筆にとりかかり、明治三十八年の八月まで綾部に尻を据えて時を待つ事としたのであつた。
