Chapter 12: The Episode of 思ひ出 (Part III) [1049]

思ひ出（三）〔1049〕

§ 1

A man named Nanbu from Kiyōto, whom I had taught Chinkon, began to pose as a living Ikigami, calling himself Konkuōsensei, and people like Takamachi became obsessed and began to worship him. Taking advantage of this, he eventually committed Kutsutsu with Takamachi's Saikun, and claiming that one was a Henjiyōnanshi and the other a Henjiyōniyoshi, they sat in a place one Ichidan higher, while Takamachi prostrated himself outside the Ketsukai one Ichidanshita to serve them. When I asked Takamachi what on earth had happened, he said, 'Because the Kamisama (Nanbu) says he has a Gonifuyō, I offered my Kanai to him.'

Kiraku: 'Baka, how could such a thing be? You are being completely deceived.'

When I told him this, he became swayed again, and though he once confronted Nanbu and demanded the return of his wife, when his Kanai threatened him, saying, 'You bald old man, do not ever dare to take up what that four-legged creature from Tanba says, lest you incur divine punishment,' he became unsteady again and ended up bowing in submission. Since the material was interesting, a man named Bakahachi of the Kiyōto Ōtora-za theater turned it into a farce and even prepared an exaggerated pictorial signboard. However, as I could not bear to have such things done in a farce, I was asked by Takamachi to find a way to stop it. Having no other choice, I asked the chivalrous man Iroha Kōtarō to intervene, paid fifty yen for the signboard, and finally put an end to it.

When my first child was born in the first month of the thirty-fifth year, the founder said that since this child was a seed of crystal, as written in the Ofudesaki, no vaccination could be given. While I was away in Ōsaka on business, both the town office and the police kept pressing for the vaccination, but as I had left it as it was, I was finally summoned by the police in the sixth month and sentenced to a fine of twenty sen for the offense of not having the vaccination done. Soon after, the regulations were amended, and the twenty-sen fine jumped to a twenty-yen penalty.

At that time, I could not afford to pay twenty yen, and furthermore, the group of leaders would not agree to pay the fine, claiming that this was the arrival of the time foretold in the Ofudesaki and that it would overturn the world. Even though I told them that the arrival of the time was not about such things, they would not listen. Having no choice, I consulted with my Kanai for the next vaccination period, emptied our chest, and finally managed to pay the fine. The fine was paid, but what would not settle down were the leaders; once they found out that I had paid the fine, they began to make a commotion as if a beehive had been overturned. Then, all of them rushed to the police station wearing straw raincoats and hats, and began to negotiate, demanding that the paid fine be returned.

They began to throw a tantrum, saying, 'Since paying a fine for not being vaccinated would mean that Japan has lost to a foreign country, it is not that we begrudge the money, but we want it returned; if you do not return it, we will not move an inch from here.' It was obvious that the police would not return a fine once it had been collected, so they would not deal with them at all. Having no other choice, they went to the town office next. They troubled the office by saying unreasonable things, such as, 'If you say it cannot be helped because it is decided by law, then amend the law; if you do not listen to the command of God, the government office will collapse, so what do you say?' but since they would not engage with them, they finally went to the public prosecutor's office and demanded the return of the fine paid to the police. The prosecutor's office was also troubled and threatened them, saying, 'If you ignore national law and say such incomprehensible things, we will send the military to crush Ōmoto, what do you say?'

Then, Shikata Heizō became indignant and began to boast, saying, '...Interesting. Let us have a trial of strength to see which is stronger, God or the military. Send as many tens of thousands of soldiers as you like.' However, since no one would take them seriously, it ended in tears.

原文（近代文語）

京都の南部といふ男、是は自分が鎮魂を教へてやつた男であるが、一廉の活神様気取りになつて、金光先生などと称へ、高町の如きは夢中になつて信仰し出した。それを良い事にして、とうとう高町の細君と野合いて、一人は変性男子、一人は変性女子と云つて、一段高い処へ坐り込み、高町は一段下の結界の外に平伏して仕へて居る。高町に……一体如何したのかと尋ねると、神様（南部）が御入用と仰有るから家内を差上げたのだ……と云ふ。

喜楽『馬鹿な、そんなことがあるものか、お前はすつかり騙されて居るのだ』

と云つてやると、又其気になつて、一応は南部に向つて、嬶を返せと迫つては見るものの、家内から、

『禿ちやん老爺、決して丹波の四つ足の云ふ事なんか取上げて神罰に触れまいぞ』

とおどかされると、またグラグラと其気になつて畏まつて仕舞ふ。材料が面白い材料だから、京都大虎座の馬鹿八と云ふ男が、俄芝居に仕組み、業々しい絵看板まで拵へた。併し俄芝居などでこんな真似をされては叶はぬから、止めさす工夫はなからうかと云つて、高町から泣き込まれた。外には仕方がないから、是は侠客いろは幸太郎に頼んで、看板代を五十円出して、とうとう止めさせて了つた。

三十五年正月直霊が生れた時、教祖様はお筆先通り、此子は水晶の種だから、種痘は出来ぬと云はれた。自分が所用で大阪へ行つて居つた留守中、役場からも警察からも切りに種痘を迫つて来たが、其儘にうちやつてあつたのでとうとう六月に警察から呼出しがあつて、種痘をしなかつた罰で二十銭の科料に処せられた。間もなく規則が改正されて、二十銭の科料が一足飛びに二十円の罰金になつた。

当時の自分の手前では迚も二十円の支出は出来ぬ許りでなく、幹部の連中が罰金を出す事を如何しても承知しない、是が御筆先の時節到来で世を転覆すのだといふ。時節到来はこんな事ぢやないと云つて聞かしても、中々承知しない。仕方がないから、次回の種痘期には家内とも相談して、箪笥を空にして漸う罰金を支払つた。罰金は納まつたが、治まらぬのは幹部の連中で、自分が罰金を支払つたといふ事が分つたものだから、蜂の巣を崩した様に騒ぎ出した。そして一同蓑笠で警察署へ押掛けて、支払つた罰金を返して呉れといつて談判を始めた。

種痘をしなかつたと云つて罰金を出しては日本が外国に負けた形になるから、出した金を惜むのではないが返して貰ひたい、返して呉れねば此処一寸も動かぬ……と云つて、駄々をこね出した。警察では一旦取つた罰金を返さぬのは知れ切つた事で、如何しても相手になつてくれない。仕方がないから今度は役場へ行く。法律できまつて居る為に仕方がないと云ふのなら、法律を改正せよ、神の命令を聞かねば役所が潰れるが如何だ……などと無理な事を云つて、役所を苦めたが、相手にならないので、仕舞には検事局へ行つて、警察へ出した罰金を返せと云つて迫つた。検事局でも手古摺つて、国法を無視してそんな訳の分らぬ事を云へば、軍隊をさし向けて大本を叩き潰すが如何だ……といつておどかした。さうすると四方平蔵がムキになつて、……面白い、神様と軍隊と何方が強いか、力比べをせう、何万の軍隊でもさしむけろ……と云つて威張るといふ始末だ。併しどこでも取上げてくれぬので其儘泣寝入りになつて了つた。

§ 2

Such being the Shidai, I kept the scheduled payment of the Batsukin from the Yokunen strictly Himitsu, ensuring that the Kanbu-Renchiu remained completely unaware of it. Dreaming nothing of the truth, the Kanbu-Renchiu were Daitokui. They rejoiced, saying that since the Kamisama had finally triumphed, no one was coming to collect the Batsukin.... Had I left things alone, all would have been well; yet because the Kanbu-Renchiu were so exceedingly Tokui, I eventually informed them that in truth I Jibun had paid it Mainen. Thereupon, another enormous Ōsawa erupted, and they resumed calling me a four-legged beast.

One day, Aoshiba Senkichi, Tanaka Senkichi, Uhenaka Gitarau, and Tsuji Fude from Sonobe came to me, asking me to attend the Hatsukuwaishiki of their Shibu. Accompanying them, as we paused for an Itsupuku by the side of the Kuwannonsama pond on the way, I was Totsuzen shoved into the pond; as I attempted to crawl out in Raubai, they held bamboo poles and pushed me under once more. And then, weeping, they admonished me:

Aoshiba Senkichi: 'Depart, depart, evil guardian spirit of Komatsubayashi! We are sorry to the Master, but Komatsubayashi will not do. We must expel the foreign soul.'

Against such foolishly honest superstitious fools, I had no recourse whatever. Crawling out with great difficulty, I proceeded to Sonobe, only to discover that the Hatsukuwaishiki of the Shibu was an absolute lie. In the middle of the night, four of them held me down, placed huge moxa cones upon my back, and said:

Kanbu: 'Hey, four-legged guardian spirit of Komatsubayashi, what do you think you are doing? This honorable person is not someone to become a vessel for the likes of you; depart, depart at once!'

Thus they proceeded to apply huge moxa burns all over my back. Next, turning me face up, they tickled me from my chest down to my sides. When I could bear it no longer and burst into laughter, they cried out:

Kanbu: 'There are still more, there are still more within him!'

And as they tickled me, they tormented me, demanding: 'Will you not depart? Will you not depart?' Upon investigating the matter by degrees, I discovered that two or three Kanbu, including Nakamura, had come out and were secretly issuing directions from behind the scenes.

If things were to continue in this manner indefinitely, I would be unable to accomplish any work whatsoever; yet even when I thought of going elsewhere, they declared that if I left, they would cut their bellies in earnest, leaving me with no means of action. On one occasion, Nishida came secretly by night, and after making arrangements, went on a mission to Kitakuwata.

In the thirty-seventh year, I went to Kitakuwata. As there were the festival of the tutelary deity and the festival at the residence of Fukushima-san at Yagi, I traveled Dōkau with Yomo Heizō, Nakamura Takezō, and my wife's elder sister, O-Ryu-san. My intention was to take this opportunity to commit a night escape. Pre-arrangements had been made with Nishida to have him wait at the blacksmith's in Hiyama. As my mother would worry if I vanished Totsuzen, I wished to whisper in her ear not to be concerned; yet because everyone remained at my side, I was unable to proceed ahead. Having no choice, I caused O-Ryu-san to develop a stomach pain. Everyone requested that I perform a chinkon for her, but I replied:

Kiraku: 'I am a four-legged beast; how could the chinkon of a four-legged beast be of any effect?'

So saying, I walked ahead rapidly and spoke with Nishida at my leisure. Afterwards, no matter how long I waited, the others failed to arrive. Listening later, I learned that they had been walking round and round in circles along the same road. At length, they arrived divided into two groups. As Yomo came from the new road, I called out to him.

We proceeded to Yagi, completed the festival of the tutelary deity, and on the Yokunen day dropped by Anao for a moment. Watching for a moment when Nakamura and Yomo were distracted, I whispered in my mother's ear and returned to Yagi. I planned to escape that night after finishing the festival of Fukushima-san, but everyone was watching so closely that I could not flee. Having no choice, I behaved impishly, demanding that they rub my feet and rub my shoulders; when I had them rub me until late at night, everyone became thoroughly exhausted and fell fast asleep. In the meantime, I quickly made my preparations and wrote an impromptu farewell note.

'Tired from an occasional journey, fast asleep they drop; A layman masseur twists the shoulder without stop. They sleep, they sleep, senseless and deep; While they sleep, the eyeballs slipped away, No way to search, left to weep and stay. A mere hole in the tunnel of the bedding, Such a thing unknown while slumbering and treading, Hard to tell the Founder upon returning home. A flannel muffler, flannel drawers below, From the sky the rain of flannel starts to flow. Burning hot, burning hot, the fever at the Fukushima gathering spot, A day too late for Yomo and Nakamura at the scene.'

原文（近代文語）

こんな次第であるから、翌年からの予定の罰金納めは余程秘密にして、幹部の連中には知れない様に済ませて来た。そんな事とは夢にも知らぬので、幹部の連中は大得意だ。とうとう神様の方が勝つたから罰金を取りに来ないのだ……と云つて喜んで居た。よせばよかつたのに、幹部の連中が余り得意がるから、実は毎年自分が払つたのだと話しをしてやつた。さうすると又大騒ぎになつて四つ足呼ばはりをやり出した。

或日園部の青柴仙吉、田中仙吉、上仲儀太郎、辻フデなどがやつて来て、支部の発会式をするから来てくれと云ふ事であつた。同行して途中の観音様の池のそばで一服やつて居ると、突然池の中へ付き落とした、狼狽して這ひ上らうとすると、竹を持つて居て、又突込む。そして、

『小松林の悪の守護神去ね去ね。先生には済まぬが、小松林が不可ぬ。外国魂を除けねばならぬ』

と泣いて意見をする。愚直な迷信家にかかつては手の出し様がない。やうやう這ひ上つて園部へ行くと、支部の発会式なんて全然嘘なんだ。夜中に四人でおさへつけて、背中に大きな灸を据ゑて、

『コラ小松林の四つ足守護神、何と心得て居る。此御方は貴様等の容物になる様な御方ぢやない、早く去んだ去んだ』

と云つて大きな灸を据ゑ廻しにする。今度は仰向けにして、胸から脇のあたりをくすぐる。たまらなくなつて笑ふと、

『まだまだ居る居る』

と云つてくすぐり乍ら……去なぬか去なぬか……と責めつける。だんだん調べて見ると幹部の中村などが二三人出かけて来て居て、蔭から差図をして居るのであつた。

何時まで経つてもこんな風では、何も仕事が出来ぬから、他処へ行かうと思つても、行けば本気で切腹をするといふのだから、如何することも出来ぬ。西田が夜隠れて来て、打合せをして北桑田へ布教に行つた事もあつた。

三十七年に北桑田へ行つた。八木に氏神の祭礼と福島サンの内のお祭とがあつたので、四方平蔵、中村竹蔵、家内の姉のお竜サンなどと同行した。是を機会に夜逃げをしやうといふ考へであつた。予て西田と打合せがしてあつて、檜山の鍛冶屋に待たしてあつた。突然姿を隠しては母が心配するから、決して心配せぬ様に耳打をして置いてくれと云ひたいのであるが、皆が側に居るので、先に行く事が出来ない。仕方がないからお竜サンに腹痛を起さした。皆が鎮魂をして上げてくれと頼んだが、

喜楽『自分は四つ足だ、四つ足の鎮魂なんか利くものか』

と云つてドンドン先へ行つて、ゆつくりと西田と話をした。それからいくら待つて居ても皆やつて来ない。後から聞くと同じ道をクルクル廻つて居つたのださうな。仕舞には二手に分れてやつて来た。四方が新道からやつて来たから声をかけてやつた。

八木へ行つて氏神の祭礼をすませ、翌日一寸穴太へ寄つた。中村と四方の隙を見て、母に耳打をして又八木へ帰つた。福島サンの祭をすませて、其晩逃げやうと考へたのであるが、皆が見張つて居て逃げる事が出来ぬ。仕方がないから足を揉め、肩を揉めとヤンチヤを云つて夜更までもませたら、皆草臥れて寝て了つた。其間に手早く仕度して、即興の書置をかいた。

『たまたまの旅につかれてグツと寝る 素人按摩が肩ひねる ねるは　ねるは　たわいもなしに 寝る間に抜けた目の玉は 尋ねる由も泣寝入り 夜具のトンネル穴許り 寝てる間に知れぬとそんな事 帰つて教祖に云ひかねる ネルの首巻ネルパツチ 空から雨がフランネル ヤキヤキと熱を福島会合所 跡の祭で四方中村』

§ 3

Finding it funny even to myself, I chuckled, and Oryu-san woke up and signaled with a gesture to go silently. When I went outside, Nishida was there waiting. From there, we ran in one breath to Sonobe. Then, we hid on the second floor of an inn called Minatoya. It seems that once it was known that I had fled in the night, everyone made a great uproar and searched all over Sonobe and Anao. It was a repetition of the second volume of Reikaimonogatari, Reishiyutaijiu, just as it was.

I stayed at the house of Kuwankichi Uchida in Miyamura for two days, went to Utsu, then to Yamakuni, and after staying two nights in Yamakuni, I went as far as Uji. From there, I took a train to Kameoka, and just as I was about to go to Ōji, Heizau Shikata came along. I stopped by Yagi because he said they were worried there. Although they told me to sleep since I would not run away tonight, they kept watch without closing their eyes for a moment, remaining on guard. In this manner, I returned to Ayabe again. Then, I was confined to a six-mat room for about another half a year. They took turns standing guard, and I was not allowed a moment of my own freedom.

When I tried to study the Kojiki, they would take it away, saying that such beggar's learning and imitating Shakyamuni were unnecessary. If I then said I would read the Nihonshoki, they would say that the Japanese chronicles might be acceptable. While I was reading it, they would look at the book, say that this was nothing but square characters, and take it away to burn it. Having no other choice, I borrowed a copy from a Shinto priest in Fukubayashi, covered myself with a futon, and studied by the light of a bean lamp. It was a situation where I had to hurriedly extinguish the light whenever I heard footsteps, and the effort involved was beyond measure. Later, due to circumstances, I served as a Shinto priest at a certain government-shrine until the forty-first year.

The executive members had misunderstood that the Tatekae-Tateraoshi would occur during the Russo-Japanese War of the thirty-seventh or thirty-eighth year, but because that was not the case, one by one they dropped out; the ambitious Takezau Nakamura died, and by around the forty-second year, besides the Kyōso-sama, only Yohei Shikata and Zenkichi Tanaka remained, and all the others were gone. Thinking that it was now safe, I also returned and engaged earnestly in missionary work, and thus the foundation of today's Ōmoto began to be built little by little.

原文（近代文語）

自分乍ら可笑しくなつてクスクスと笑ふと、お竜サンが目を覚まして、黙つて行けと手真似で知らす。外へ出ると西田が来て待つて居る。それから園部迄一走りに走つた。そして港屋といふ宿屋の二階に隠れてゐた。自分が夜逃したといふことが分つて、皆で大騒ぎをして園部穴太をスツカリ捜し廻つたさうな。『霊界物語』霊主体従第二巻其儘を繰返したのである。

宮村の内田官吉の内に二日居つて宇津へ行き、山国へ行き、山国で二晩とまつて宇治まで行つた。それから汽車で亀岡に行き、王子へ行かうとすると、四方平蔵がやつて来た。

八木で心配をしてゐるからと云ふので八木へ寄つた。今夜は逃げぬから寝よといふけれど、警戒をしてマンヂリともせずに見張つて居る。こんな風で又綾部へ帰つて来た。そして更に半年程六畳の間に押込められた。代る代る張番をして暫時の間も自分の自由にはならぬのである。古事記を研究しやうとすると、そんな乞食の学問なんか、釈迦の真似などする事は要らぬと云つて取り上げて了ふ。それぢや日本書紀を読まうといふと、日本の書紀ならよからうといふ。読んで居ると其本を見て、日本の書紀などと云ふが、是は角文字ぢやないかと云つて取上げて焼いて了ふ。仕方がないから一部福林の神官から借りて、蒲団を被つて豆ランプの火で調べた。人の足音がすると、あわてて消すといふ様な次第で、骨が折れる事、一通りや二通りではなかつた。其後事情があつて四十一年迄某官幣社の神職を勤めた。

幹部の連中は立替立直しは三十七八年の日露戦争だと誤解して居たのだが、さうでなかつた為に、一人減り二人減り、野心家の中村竹蔵は死に、四十二年頃には教祖様の外には四方与平、田中善吉のみが残つて、後は皆ゐなくなつた。モウ大丈夫と考へたから自分も帰つて来て、熱心に布教に従事し、今日の大本の土台がだんだん出来て来る様になつたのである。
