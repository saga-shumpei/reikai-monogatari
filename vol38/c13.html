Chapter 13: 冠 Island [1050]

冠島〔1050〕

§ 1

The I-Ching states that writing cannot exhaust words, words cannot exhaust ideas, and ideas cannot exhaust the divine; yet, without words, ideas cannot be manifested, and without writing, words cannot be recorded. Indeed, the words of Seiken, the deeds of Ijin and Kunshi, the great Ikiyo of Chiushin and Gishi, and the Biden of Teijiyo and Setsupu are all transmitted through Monji. For me, Kiraku, a Ningen of ordinary Shiyōrai, to attempt to describe the Shingenkau of the Kyōso, who is Donkonretsuki, Shigu, and Shichi, is the height of Senetsu. However, even if it be but an Ichishōbubun, if I do not transmit the Shingen that the Kyōso Jitsukau, it would be as if the Shingen of the Kyōso were buried in the Dochiu; therefore, I intend to relate here the gist of the Jijitsu of the Kyōso’s first journey to Oshima, undertaken in obedience to the Shinmei.

Sima Xiangru once said that there must first be an extraordinary person, and only then an extraordinary event, and after an extraordinary event, there follows an extraordinary achievement. I believe that Deguchi Naoko, whom I am dictating about here, is also an extraordinary person. Nay, I firmly believe that she is the representative of the noble Kami, and it is for this reason that I intend to tell a part of her story.

After the Sino-Japanese War, the German occupation of Jiaozhou Bay and the Russian lease of Port Arthur and Dalian greatly stimulated the spirits of the Great Powers. As a result of each hoping for a share, Britain occupied Weihaiwei and France occupied Guangzhou Bay. Following the example of Russia obtaining the right to build railways in Manchuria, they vied to acquire railway construction rights in various parts of Qing China, each establishing their so-called spheres of influence. This quietly fostered a situation of the partition of China, and it was thought that the crisis in the Orient had never been more severe than at this time. Furthermore, a group of Chiushin and Gishi who did not understand the trends of the times, known as the Boxers, were deeply indignant at this. They finally raised the banner of 'Revive the Qing, Destroy the Foreign,' rose up in various parts of Shandong Province, massacred Christians, and destroyed railways. In early April of the 33rd year of Meiji, they advanced into Zhili Province, suddenly supporting Prince Duan of the Qing imperial clan and making General Dong Fuxiang their backbone. From May onwards, their influence became increasingly rampant, and by June, Beijing had fallen completely into a state of siege. The German Minister Ketteler was the first to be brutally murdered, and a certain Sugiyama, a secretary at our legation, was also killed. Japan and the other powers finally appointed the German Field Marshal Waldersee as commander-in-chief, organized an Allied Army, and were on the verge of advancing from Dagu to press upon the Boxers in Beijing.

It was a glorious war, which could be called the greatest trial of strength in the world since the creation of the universe. Under the divine command that it was necessary to stand upon this grand stage to manifest the bravery of the divine nation and the divine army, and to prevent the contempt of the Great Powers, the Kyōso rose from her old body of sixty-five years. She set out for Oshima, a deserted island off the coast of Tango where no woman had ever crossed before, to pray for the peace of the Orient and the great victory of the Imperial Army. On the eighth day of the sixth month of the lunar calendar, she took with her four people—President Ueda, Deguchi Sumiko, Shikata Heizō, and Kinoshita Keitarō—and walked a distance of five and a half ri. Around dusk, they arrived at the Otaniuya, a shipping agent in Maizuru, and hired a boatman to cross to the island. Just as they were about to set sail, the blue sky, which had been clear and extremely calm until then, suddenly clouded over. The whole sky looked as if ink had been spilled, the wind blew against the sea surface, and the sound of the raging waves could be heard growing more intense by the moment.

The master of the Otaniuya said, 'This weather is surely the approach of a typhoon, so I suggest we postpone the voyage tonight. Moreover, to go to a single island in the middle of the rough open sea ten ri away in such a small fishing boat is Tautei impossible to do safely. If one thing goes wrong, you will have to throw away your precious lives. Please wait for tomorrow's dawn, observe the weather, and pray only after it has been decided that it is safe.'

He kept trying to stop them. The boatmen also spoke with one voice, insisting that it was Tautei impossible to cross the sea safely. Not only did they refuse to set sail, but one by one they slipped away and fled somewhere. The Kyōso,

Kyōso: 'Because it is the Gomeirei of Kamisama, I cannot allow even a Hitotoki of Iuyo by saying such things. Whether it is difficult or not, I want you to prepare a boat and set out from now. The reason the sea is rough this Konban is that Riugūsama is coming to welcome our party with joy and courage, which is why the fierce wind blows and the rain falls. She spoke with Otake and showed no sign of listening to the words of the master of the Sendō. The master of the Ōniyuya and the rest of the boatmen whispered in small voices with a mocking air:

'Money is desirable, but life is more important. We cannot bear to deal with such a mad old woman who is a fool without regard for her own life.'

Not a single person responded. The party of five could do nothing, and while praying with palms pressed together on the bridge, they kept asking for the arrangements for the departure to be made as quickly as possible. Kinoshita, having come back after persuading the two fishermen, Tanaka Iwakichi and Hashimoto Rokuzō, who were known for their skill in handling boats, said:

'Please take us to Kanmurijima from now.'

When he asked again, the two opened their eyes wide and said:

'No matter if it is the command of Kamisama, we cannot go in this weather. We have lived on the sea for a long time, thinking of the boat as our home, so we would row out for a moderate storm, but in this Keshiki, it is absolutely impossible. Where on earth are you people from? You are truly unreasonable people.'

They stared at the faces of the party in astonishment. Kyōso urged them repeatedly.

Kyōso: 'Quickly, quickly.'

She pressed them. The Sendō did not answer. Thinking that it would not end this way, Kinoshita said to the two Sendō:

'Even if it is only one chō or half a ri on the sea, I will pay the fare to Kanmurijima, so please go with the intention of returning from halfway.'

The two Sendō said:

'If you people insist so much, it must surely be the teaching of Kamisama; without conviction, you would never have the spirit to go in this Keshiki. I will also ask Kanmurijima-sama for a moment and decide.'

While saying this, they stood on the new bridge, faced toward Kanmurijima, and while praying with palms pressed together, they drew an impromptu mikuji and looked at it, saying:

'As it says that Kamisama tells us to go, we shall row as far as we can go anyway.'

With a feeling of being half-anxious and half-dangerous, they revealed their consent, immediately made preparations, took the five people on board, and gracefully left Maizuru Port behind. They steered the small boat without a roof, disregarded the rain and wind, carried seven living souls including themselves, and rowed out with a loud boat song.

Around the time they thought they had rowed about two ri on the sea, the folding umbrella that Kyōso had been holding was, by some mechanism, suddenly snatched by a wave and dropped. While it was seen, the boatman Iwakichi, who was standing at the bow and operating the oar, noticed it sharply and picked it up. Sumiko, who had been sleeping fitfully with her elbow on the bottom of the boat as a pillow, suddenly woke up.

Sumiko: 'Ah, I was startled. I thought that just now, Heizō-san had accidentally fallen into the sea, and as his life was in danger, a Kamisama with a dignified and awe-inspiring appearance appeared from behind Kyōso-sama, rescued Heizō-san, and blew breath into his mouth to revive him, but was it just a dream after all?'

She spoke with a mysterious air. The party felt even more strange, and when they examined the folding umbrella that Kyōso had brought, it was indeed Heizō-san's umbrella. The Great Kami of supreme benevolence and love must have had Kyōso-sama drop Mr. Heizō's umbrella into the sea as a substitute to save him from that danger, cleanse his sins, and let him be reborn as a new human being... while each of them sighed with admiration, they sat quietly in the narrow boat, knees touching, and while the whole group recited prayers of gratitude, they rowed out into the rain with courage.

If one were to state such things, those without Shinkau would perhaps call it Kiyōmō, slander it as Meishin, or laugh at it as a Gūzen Angafu. They would likely dismiss it with an Itsushō, refusing to consider that there is any Dauri for Shinireikuwai to exist in this world. Nevertheless, that all things between Tenchi are Maka Fushigi is as Motōri Norinaga sang in his Tamahokohiyakushiyu.

Ayashiki o hiji to iu wa yo no naka no / Ayashiki shiranu shiregokoro kamo / Shirubeki mono naranaku ni yo no naka no / Kushiki kotowari kami narazu shite

As the meaning of the above poem suggests, all things between Tenchi are Kikuwai and beyond the reach of Jinshinshōchi to fathom. Yet, since the appearance of those called Seijin from the Chiuko period, who preached not to speak of Kuwairikiranshin or claimed that there is no Fushigi in the Shiyōhafu, spreading Herikutsu with an enlightened air, the hearts of Yobito have gradually inclined toward Mushinron. They have come to Mushi Shinrei and Reikuwai, failing to realize the truth of the Uchiu, and have arrived at a state of not knowing the divine nation, which is supreme, noble, and unparalleled in all lands. Furthermore, it is written in the Tamahokohiyakushiyu:

Karatama no sakashiragokoro utsurite zo / Yobito no kokoro ashiku narinuru / Shirubeshi to shiko no monoshiri nakanaka ni / Yokosa no michi ni hito madowasu mo

That this is sung is truly a reasonable matter. Meanwhile, the boat, rowed with all their might by the boatmen, arrived quickly at Bakuchi-ga-misaki. As the Kyōso had declared, upon reaching this point, the rain suddenly cleared, the wind calmed, and the waves grew still. The light of the stars filling the heavens rested deep in the bottom of the sea, coloring the ripples in silver; the space above the clouds and the bottom of the sea were like a matching mirror. Recalling the words of the boy of old,

The oar pierces the moon upon the waves, / The waves envelop the boat in the midst of the sea

I was struck by a truly exhilarating feeling. Recalling even the poem by Ki no Tsurayuki,

When I look at the reflection, the moon is at the bottom of the waves / I am lonely, rowing across the sky

and feeling the inspiration deepen, just as we proceeded, a black shadow in the distance of the sea obscured the moon. When the boatman shouted,

Kammurijima-sama can be seen!

the joy of everyone was not that of a seagull on the open sea, but as if they were about to take flight; it felt as though this were the joy of a Ryūjin finding the moment to ascend to heaven, and we were struck by an indescribable feeling of refreshment.

After a while, the eastern sky shone brilliantly with a crimson glow, and from above the mountains of Wakasa, as if holding up a golden orb, the Amatsuhi no Kami shone in Toyosaka-nobori. Kammurijima was already so close it felt as if one could reach out and touch it, blocking the view before our eyes, and the chirping of a hundred birds was such that one might suspect a hundred thousand orchestras were playing in unison. It is said that this is the Ryūgū-jima where Urashimako of the ancient tales rode a turtle to go to Ryūgū and returned with the Tamatebako received from Otohime-sama; it is also the Ryūgū-jima where Abe no Dōjimaru was granted various divine treasures and mysterious arts; and it is also the palace of the sea god where Hikohohodemi no Mikoto, as recorded in the Kojiki and other texts, went, taught by Shiotsuchi no Okina, to search for the fishhook lost in the sea. With such legends, a feeling of having advanced into the realm of Shinsen arose from nowhere.

Seeing that it is written in the Shōzōmatsu Wasan:

Since it is a time when the practice and realization of sentient beings in the Mappō-godaku are not fulfilled, the legacy of Shaka has all entered Ryūgū. / In the three periods of Shōzōmatsu, the original vow of Mida has spread; in this world of the Jōki-mappō, all virtues enter Ryūgū.

it seems that even the Buddhists highly value Ryūgū. To such a joyous Hōraijima, the boat arrived without incident.

Beside the Torii where the emerald trees were lush and dense, an old pine tree stood out, and a famous mulberry tree with a trunk circumference exceeding three jō, like a ladder to the clouds, towered at the summit of Mount Kammurijima. The voices of hundreds of thousands of birds seemed to welcome the party of the Kyōso. It is truly a scenic spot of beautiful mountains and seas, and excellent scenery, worthy of the name Ryūgū. Immediately upon landing, the Kyōso performed Misogi at the water's edge. Everyone followed suit, performed Misogi, and proceeded quietly before the Kami of the Rōtō-jinja, where the divine power is awe-inspiring. We knelt and bowed in reverence, offered the various delicious foods of the mountains and rivers prepared from Ayabe, and poured our prayers into the Chigoku-heitenka-anmin. The sound of the Norito reached the nine heavens, and the sound of the clapping of hands felt as if it purified the six directions. With this, the purpose of the pilgrimage to Kammurijima was achieved. On the return trip, the waves were calm, and on the evening of the ninth, we returned to Ōniuya in Maizuru Port. On the following tenth, we returned on foot, welcomed by many believers, and were able to return happily to the Ayabe Hongū. Ah, Kamu-nagara-tamachi-ha-e-mase.

右の歌の意の如く、天地間は凡て奇怪にして人心小智の伺ひ知るべき限りではない。然るに中古より聖人などいふ者出で来りてより、怪力乱神を語らずとか、正法に不思議なしとか悟り顔に屁理屈を振りまはしてより、世人の心は漸次無神論に傾き、神霊霊怪を無視し、宇宙の真理を得悟らざるに至り、至尊至貴万邦無比の神国を知らざるに立到つたのである。又玉鉾百首に、

からたまのさかしら心うつりてぞ 世人の心悪しくなりぬる しるべしと醜の物知りなかなかに よこさの道に人まどわすも

とよまれてあるのも実に尤もな次第である。却説舟人の一生懸命にこぐ舟は早くも博奕ケ岬についた。教祖の言あげせられた如く、ここ迄来ると、雨は俄に晴れ、風はなぎ波は静まつて、満天の星の光は海の底深く宿つて、波紋銀色を彩どり、雲の上も海の底も合せ鏡の如く、昔の男の子の、

棹はうがつ波の上の月を、 波はおそふ海の中の舟を

を思ひうかべ実に壮快の気分に打たれた。

影見れば浪のそこなる久方の 空こぎわたる我ぞわびしき

といふ紀貫之の歌まで思ひ出され、一しほ感興深く進む折しも、ボーツと海のあなたに黒い影が月を遮つた。舟人は、

『あゝ冠島さまが見えました』

と叫んだ時の一同の嬉しさは、沖の鴎のそれならで、飛立つ許り、竜神が天に昇るの時を得たる喜びも斯くやあらむと思はれ、得も言はれぬ爽快の念にうたれた。

暫くあつて東の空は燦然として茜さし、若狭の山の上より、黄金の玉をかかげたる如く、天津日の神は豊栄昇りに輝き玉ひ、早くも冠島は手に取る許り、目の前に塞がり、囀る百鳥の声は百千万の楽隊の一斉に楽を奏したるかと疑はるる計りであつた。かの昔語にとく所の浦島子が亀に乗つて、竜宮に往き、乙姫様に玉手箱を授かつて持ち帰つたと伝ふる竜宮島も、安部の童子丸がいろいろの神宝や妙術を授けられたといふ竜宮島も亦、古事記などに記載せられたる彦火々出見命が塩土の翁に教へられて、海に落ちたる釣針を捜し出さむと渡りましたる海神の宮も皆此冠島なりと云ひ伝ふる丈あつて、どこともなく、神仙の境に進み入つたる思ひが浮かんで来た。

正像末和讃にも

末法五濁の有情の行証叶はぬ時なれば、釈迦の遺法悉く竜宮に入り玉ひにき。

正像末の三時には弥陀の本願広まれり、澆季末法の此世には諸善竜宮に入り玉ふ

とあるを見れば仏教家も亦非常に竜宮を有難がつて居るらしい、かかる目出たき蓬莱島へ恙なく舟は着いた。

翠樹鬱蒼たる華表の傍、老松特に秀でて雲梯の如く幹のまわり三丈にも余る名木の桑の木は冠島山の頂に立ち聳え、幾十万の諸鳥の声は教祖の一行を歓迎するが如くに思はれた。実に竜宮の名に負ふ山海明媚、風光絶佳の勝地である。教祖は上陸早々、波打際に御禊された。一同も之に倣うて御禊をなし、神威赫々たる老人島神社の神前に静かに進みて、蹲踞敬拝し、綾部より調理し来れる、山の物川の魚うまし物くさぐさを献り、治国平天下安民の祈願をこらす、祝詞の声は九天に達し、拍手の声は六合を清むる思ひがあつた。これにて先づ冠島詣での目的は達し、帰路は波もしづかに九日の夕方、舞鶴港の大丹生屋に立帰り、翌十日又もや徒歩にて、数多の信者に迎へられ、目出度く綾部本宮に帰ることを得たのである。あゝ惟神霊幸倍坐世。
