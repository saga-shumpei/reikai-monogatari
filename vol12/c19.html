Chapter 19: The Ocean of Kure [515]

呉の海原〔515〕

§ 1

The divine priest Tokiokishi was offering silent, fervent prayer toward the surface of the sea where Ushikō had plunged. After a while, with a broad smile beaming across his face, he calmly and leisurely returned to his seat. Uma-kō lowered his voice and spoke:

Umakō: 'Hey Deer, Tiger, did you see that?! Even the missionary of the Anai teachings, seeing Ushikō jump into the sea, didn't make the slightest move to save him, but just stood there smirking and chuckling with immense satisfaction!'

Shikakō: 'Aye, indeed! No matter how grandly they proclaim themselves to be missionaries who save mankind, it's nothing but empty talk! At any rate, in a world like this, it is just as God's teaching says: "Men of sincere truth are not even enough to make medicine of!" If we chatter in loud voices, who knows what dreadful retribution we might suffer! Be quiet, be quiet! If you want to say something, speak silently!'

Umakō: 'How on earth can anyone speak silently?! I'm not a conjurer or a magician!'

Torakō: 'Of course you can! That is telepathy and mind-to-mind communion! There is a saying: "The eyes speak as eloquently as the mouth!" In the Reikai Monogatari too, does it not say that unless one sees with ears, hears with eyes, eats with nose, and smells with mouth, one can never understand God?!'

Umakō: 'None of that matters now! Let's just keep quiet! Look, look—Tokiokishi has opened his huge eyes wide and started glaring around sharply! It looks like our turn has come!'

So saying, he began hurriedly unfastening and spinning off his sash.

Shikakō: 'Hey Umakō, what are you planning to do by untying your sash?!'

Umakō: 'Shut up, don't talk! There is a secret to this! It's advance preparation!'

Torakō: 'What do you mean, advance preparation?!'

Umakō: 'Jumping into the sea with all one's clothes on like Ushikō did is so clumsy and miserable! If Tokiokishi comes shouting "Hey you!", I will beforehand become the deity of untied garments, stripped stark naked, and plunge headlong into the sea—ker-splash! You fellows prepare yourselves too, get ready!'

Gazing around in all directions and catching wind of the trio's whispered conspiracy, Tokiokishi stepped forth:

'Well now! We have not met in a long time! Are you not Ushikō's fellow inspectors of the Ural sect—Horse, Deer, and Tiger?!'

Torakō: 'N-no, that's mistaken! Pray look carefully and clearly! I am by no means such an honest man as would artfully deceive people!'

Tokiokishi: 'Aha ha ha ha! What is the meaning of such utter confusion?! Why, you have stripped stark naked!'

時置師神は四辺キヨロキヨロ見廻しながら、三人の囁き話を聞き、

時置師『アハヽヽヽヽ、其狼狽へ様は何だ、裸になつて居るぢやないか』

§ 2

Umakō: 'When naked, there is nothing one can drop or lose! Since losing one's singular precious life would be an utter loss, I stripped naked as a precaution for any dire emergency! If a violent storm were to blow and capsize the ship, it would be simply unbearable!'

Tokiokishi: 'You are undoubtedly Ushikō's comrade! A human being ought to be honest!'

Umakō: 'Yes, yes, pray forgive me! If one speaks the plain truth nowadays, one's life won't last! In today's times, if one speaks the truth, one is condemned as an evil villain and subjected to atrocious cruelty! In a world where lies become treasure—"truth born of lies" and "lies born of truth"—whether lie or truth, whether rain or wind, steering through the middle with opportunistic agility is the proper art of living today! I am by no means an evil wretch who would ever resist this world! Following the good way of yielding to the times, I shall obey Master Tokiokishi in all things!'

Tokiokishi: 'Aha ha ha ha! Utterly dyed in the philosophy of the Ural sect through and through!'

Shikakō: 'Indeed, indeed! By such-and-such circumstances and karmic affinities, sharing the same fate aboard the Kunitakemaru—"even brushing sleeves is a bond from previous lives," "a stumbling stone is an edge of destiny," "eating dumplings on the hearth-frame"...'

Tokiokishi: 'Hey, hey! What nonsense are you spouting?! It is troublesome how your speech continually derails!'

Shikakō: 'Right, right! Suiting the occasion, this is my entertainment for Master Toki! Times and seasons are fearsome things; in this vast world, having committed merely one or two wicked deeds, I never dreamed I would run straight into Master Toki! Ah, vast though it seems, how narrow is this world; far narrower still is the inside of this boat; and narrower yet is the mortal belly!'

Tokiokishi: 'What an extraordinarily glib tongue you possess!'

Shikakō: 'They say: "Far more than the belling deer, the silent firefly burns with inner passion!"'

Tokiokishi: 'What a hopeless fellow! Why are you trembling so violently?!'

Shikakō: 'I am shaking off the crooked spirits obsessing my body! Pray look anew and relisten to cleanse my past sins cleanly; and if it suits your convenience, it would be extraordinarily convenient for us if you would kindly transfer aboard some other ship!'

Tokiokishi: 'You are an amusing rascal—nay, a dark-faced fellow! A man just like oiled persimmon-tannin paper! Your face has that grim astringency, making you perfectly suited for an inspector or constable!'

Torakō: 'Pardon me, Master Toki! Shikakō was saying plenty of things earlier! Terrible, awful things, I assure you!'

§ 3

Tokiokishi: 'What was he saying?'

Shikakō: 'In tranquil ease, I was expounding upon the gracious blessings of God! In the Anai teachings, there are magnificent doctrines and a host of splendid missionaries; and among them all, if one speaks of a missionary who is exceptionally merciful, possessed of tremendous divine power, handsome, and truly like a living deity, there could be none other than Master Tokiokishi—thus was I praising you to high heaven!'

Torakō: 'Hey, Shikakō, don't spout such barefaced rubbish! O Missionary, Shikakō's words are not "truth born of lies", but "blatant lies born of evil intent"!'

Shikakō: 'Silence, you villainous Tiger! What an utterly cowardly scoundrel you are! Do you think you alone should be spared while the two of us are abandoned to whatever fate?! Very well, then I have my own countermove! O Missionary, this rascal Torakō received handsome bounties from the Eight Kings of Mount Caucasus, and in truth trailed right behind you to spy upon you! As proof, he is concealing a dagger within his bosom!'

Tokiokishi: 'Whether he conceals one or not is of no consequence!'

Torakō: 'O Missionary, pray understand my true intentions!'

Shikakō: 'What drivel are you babbling?! Of course he will understand! He will clearly understand that you are an evil villain stalking and scheming against the missionary, carrying a lethal weapon in your bosom! As the saying goes: "The frog is swallowed by its mouth," and "A dagger cannot be concealed!"'

At that very moment of heated dispute, just as a colossal spout of water seemed to soar up toweringly before the bow of the vessel, borne upon the back of a gigantic sea turtle, Ushikō came bobbing up joyfully unto the surface of the sea! Horse, Deer, and Tiger cried out in unison:

'Aaaah! Ushikō... is saved!'
