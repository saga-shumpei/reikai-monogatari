Chapter 11: The Twelve Earthly Branches [507]

第11章　十二支〔507〕

§ 1

The divine missionary of Ananaikyō who illuminates the gloom of the human heart: Priestess Iwaihime, not dreaming that the lord husband of her mortal body was present in the adjoining chamber, wept pitifully in secret concealment within the innermost room of chieftain Natsuyamahiko's hall, wiping away her cascading tears that surged like the turbulent rapids of the Shirase River, rewinding the spool of her inner heartstrings again and again upon the one-string koto with restless agitated grief. Whether warned by some intuitive premonition, the noble Lord Natsuyamahiko too felt the sky of his inner soul unsettled by the midnight storm blowing down from Mount Sinai; he sank into brooding contemplation with heavy sighs, finding no way to untangle the knotted cords of unrequited love. Though it was a world of perpetual darkness, the cocks crowed to herald the dawn, and through the fourth and fifth watches the skies slowly brightened. Although mortals say that 'no demons wander the floating world,' in this present age ugly waves and storms rage fiercely. 'This very night, when genuine, enlightened divine mortals have come to visit, I must blow away the cloud clusters weighing on my heart, re-establish myself straight and upright in the divine mind, and dispel the winds of demons and passion through the divine teachings of Ananaikyō!'—thus resolving, he sat bolt upright, stepped before the altar, rinsed his mouth, and with the solemn, awe-inspiring clap of hands, proclaimed the solemn Norito before God.

§ 2

Natsuyamahiko: 'God manifests openly upon the world to sever good from evil! Especially agonizing is the darkness of passion; though I know full well what is good and evil, the tangled threads of love cling tenaciously in my breast, shattering my very soul! Ah, Sovereign Deities, Sovereign Deities! At the earliest day, in the swiftest instant, remove the crooked evil spirits lurking within my chest, and make me a valiant pillar of the Gods of Ananai! Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara! Bestow blessings upon my spirit, bestow blessings upon my spirit! Unable to disperse even the single cloud over my own self, the sky of Natsuyamahiko's heart is enveloped in the gloom of the fifth month where the cuckoo cries, unable to distinguish black from white! Uttering thousands and myriads of cries like spitting blood, Priestess Iwaihime displays a courage beyond her tender years, crossing desolate wildernesses, treading wild waves, enduring freezing frost to propagate divine teachings for the sake of the world; compared to her valor, though I am a noble pillar of the gods, I drown shamefully in unrequited tears! O night storm that knows compassion, blow and purify the clouds of love clinging to my person! Swiftly fling open the dark doors of layered love-clouds and shine the light of the Heavenly Sun into my breast! Shine brightly within my breast! Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara! May the divine spirit bestow fortune, dispelling the anguish of my heart and the gloom of my breast in the blast of the Shinado wind—purge and purify, O Heavenly Deities and Earthly Deities, eight hundred myriads of gods! Dust and rubbish alike, O Iwaihime, blow a wild wind of pitiless severity against my frame, and blow away the tangled web of love! Blow away the tangled web of love!'
