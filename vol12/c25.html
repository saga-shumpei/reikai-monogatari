Chapter 25: The Kotohiramaru [521]

琴平丸〔521〕

§ 1

The missionaries Takamitsuhiko and Tamamitsuhiko, accompanied by the deity Tokiokishi, departed from Tachibana Island and landed at the port of Kure; singing missionary chants as they traversed the vast, gloom-shrouded wilderness, they arrived at the shores of Lake Biwa. At that time, winds and waves raged so violently that departures had been halted for seven days and nights, and the waiting passengers whiled away the time in idle chatter.

Kō: 'What a wretched, troubled world it has become! This Lake Biwa has always been such a calm body of water, its gentle wavelets sounding like celestial maidens strumming the lute—renowned above all for absolute safety in maritime transit! Yet to see it churn so wildly, with boats unable to sail for five or six days on end, is something unprecedented in all memory! What on earth could be the cause?!'

Otsu: 'It is obvious! In these dark times when heaven is dim and earth is pitch-black, vegetation has lost its vital hue and devils strut haughtily across the world in perpetual gloom! Since Lake Biwa sits right between Heaven and Earth, it is only natural that it should be somewhat rough! More importantly, have you heard about the colossal battle that took place in the Inland Sea?!'

Kō: 'Nay, I haven't heard a word. What sort of battle was it?'

Otsu: 'They say there was an enormous quarrel! A great big quarrel on a grand scale is what we call a war!'

Kō: 'Saying merely that explains nothing unless you recount the details!'

Otsu: 'What is there to understand or not understand?! A fight is a fight! It is just like the jealous brawls you are always having with your wife! You slap your old woman across the face—whack! Enraged, she sinks her teeth into your arm! "Curse you, woman, what are you doing?! Let go, or I'll bash your head in!" you roar, raising your fist! With her teeth clamped onto your arm, weeping bitterly, she cries, "Kill me if you will! Even in death I won't let go of this arm!" Then Hachikō next door comes running out to mediate: "Come, come, Tsurukō and Lady Okame, what are you fighting about?! The crane lives a thousand years, the turtle ten thousand—even dogs won't eat a marital quarrel! For a married couple meant to live in loving harmony for thousands of years, what disgraceful conduct is this?!" Whereupon you, Tsurukō, puff out your chest: "Stay out of this, Hachi! I cannot stomach this creature; I am divorcing her as of today!" Then your wife Okame drops on all fours and weeps: "Master Hachi, pray leave us be! I am utterly disgusted with this man! Drinking, gambling, chasing after vile harlots—a useless, good-for-nothing old wreck! It is a blessing to be divorced! We may part now, but three years hence I will bear two children by a splendid gentleman, and leading him by the hand I shall return to say: 'Master Tsuru, I am immensely obliged to you for three years ago! Thanks to that, I have won a magnificent husband and borne splendid children! Living with a foolish old dunce only makes me foolish too! Even if I bore children to you, they'd be nothing but dim-witted brats with faces like cheap copper coins! Thank you for divorcing me!'"—it is just like that! At any rate, it is a lovers' spat! Between "Izu" and "Mizu", cold and distant as can be, even the deities had a mighty battle!'

Kō: 'Listening to your drivel, you blab my private household embarrassments before this whole crowd—what an outrageous scoundrel you are!'

Otsu: 'Well, since it is a brawl over a sacred island or an Okame island, how could I not be reminded of your fights with your wife?!'

Hei: 'Spouting half-baked rumors before everyone is utterly disgraceful! As if there is anyone nowadays who doesn't know the real story!'

Otsu: 'Don't talk so grandly! In that case, let's hear you tell it step by step!'

Hei: 'I am a man thoroughly enlightened through eyes, nose, ears, and mouth—as clear and unobstructed through and through as a brand-new pipe!'

Tsurukō: 'What do you mean by a brand-new pipe?'

Hei: 'It means a man of thorough insight, well-ventilated through and through!'

Tsurukō: 'What rubbish! If it's a completely new pipe with no draw, it just means an unobstructed dunce of a man! Aha ha ha!'

Otsu: 'Asking such a raw pipe only bores us to tears! Isn't there someone here who knows the real details?!'

Tei: 'In a crowd of ten thousand, there is bound to be at least one! They say even in a dung-heap a crane may land—though this Tsurukō is an exception, being a crane whose wife flew away!'

Hei: 'Then who is this "crane in the dung-heap"?!'

Tei: 'It's obvious; anyone could tell just by looking at his face! A man with dignified lips, refreshing eyes, and a straight, noble nose!'

So saying, he tapped his own nose.

Tei: 'I speak in dead earnest, so listen solemnly! Long ago upon Mount Caucasus, there was a goddess with massive hips named Lady Ōgetsuhime no Mikoto. Gathering numerous underlings like the Eight Kings and Biccos, she enshrined exalted deities and built a capital—when there arrived missionaries with delicious-sounding names like Matsutake, Shiitake, and Kanpyō, who ran rampant everywhere, so that even the broad-hipped goddess hoisted sails on her backside and fled post-haste to Armenia! Thereafter, those mushroom gentlemen enshrined the sake-loving Susanō no Mikoto, making swords and turtles their sacred objects! Maiden deities named Moon, Softshell Turtle, and Flower served the shrine; but as the world grew overcast, they grew weary of Mount Caucasus, transformed into three gigantic serpents, and soared into the sky on gathering clouds! One serpent nested on Tachibana Island in the Ocean of Kure, disguised as a dazzling beauty; another descended upon Takeshima here in Lake Biwa as a great dragon! And the other maiden goddess, named Softshell Turtle or Snow, became a white serpent dwelling upon the singular island in the Inland Sea, appearing as a stunning beauty surrounded by many retainers! Thereupon, dispatching Amenohohi no Mikoto as an envoy of the Henjō-Nanshi from Mount Tenkyō with a horde of formidable warriors, they surrounded the island of Sardinia, torched it, and burned it to the ground! What a tremendous affair that was!'

Tsurukō: 'Don't talk nonsense! Sardinia was no brawl! The men arrived in warlike ferocity, but the maiden goddess prepared abundant feasts, saying, "Welcome, welcome! We have little to offer, but pray drink your fill of sake!" and refused to fight! When one side refuses to fight, it is no brawl!'

Tei: 'Stop spouting logic, it was still half a fight!'

Tsurukō: 'If a man leads a huge army to pick a fight with a woman and she simply smiles and serves him wine, he looks like a fool! It is like arm-wrestling a fabric curtain—all his fighting spirit evaporated!'

Beside them, the deity Ishikoridome, who had been quietly listening with closed eyes, exclaimed:

'Hark, this is a matter of profound gravity! Though it be street gossip, truth often resides even in common prattle! It seems the divine covenant (ukehi) between the Izunomitama and Mizunomitama has begun! A single misstep could plunge the world into the confinement of the Heavenly Rock Cave! Lord Tokiokishi, Lord Yukihirawake, I bid you farewell here! I must ascend Mount Alps at once to forge the Eight-Hand Mirror in the sun's likeness! The deity Amenomahitotsu has likely departed already! Cross over unto Takeshima and ascertain the safety of Lady Akizukihime! Farewell!'

Casting aside these parting words, he vanished like mist toward Mount Alps.

Tokiokishi: 'Well, the missionary Ishikoridome bears a grave mission, so it cannot be helped! Parting here feels somewhat lonely, but it is splendid as part of the divine work! Come, our boat is about to depart! Let us converse at leisure aboard!'

So saying, they advanced toward the vessel. Over a hundred passengers vied to board the Kotohiramaru. Hoisting its sails full in the wind, the ship parted the calm lake waters, the waves strumming a lute against the hull as it glided swiftly northeast.

In one corner of the vessel, four or five shrewd men sat in a circle, absorbed in diverse gossip. The missionaries Tokiokishi and Yukihirawake sat beside them with innocent expressions, listening intently.

Kō: 'Recently, because the world had fallen into incorrigible disorder, the benevolent deities ascended unto heaven or gathered in the Dragon Palace, leaving the earth a dark realm of devils; Susanō no Mikoto of Mount Caucasus went unto Takamanohara, and ever since, earthquakes have rumbled, tsunamis surged, plagues spread, rivers dried, plants withered, and crops failed—catastrophes everywhere! Thereupon, Amaterasu-Ōmikami of Takamanohara, suspecting that Susanō harbored treasonous ambitions, became enraged, preparing bows, arrows, swords, and halberds to slay Him! Susanō proclaimed: "I harbor no such foul or treacherous ambitions! Despairing of this earthly realm, I wish merely to return unto the Moon realm of my Mother Deity; before departing, I came only to behold my elder sister's face once more!" Yet the Elder Sister Deity was deeply suspicious and would not be easily persuaded! In the end, placing the Heavenly River of Yasu (the Pacific Ocean) in between, they agreed upon a divine trial and covenant at the Heavenly Well of Amenomanai (the Sea of Japan)—a momentous affair!
