Chapter 9: The Prophetic Dream [505]

第9章　正夢〔505〕

§ 1

To disperse the gloom of the perpetual night and succor the cherished children of the Sovereign God, Takamitsuhiko of towering majesty, and Tamamitsuhiko reflecting the jewel of Izunome received from God, and Kunimitsuhiko floating like a jellyfish upon the vast ocean—these three water-spirits (Mizu-no-Mitama) rested their heads upon temporary pillows beneath the aged trees at the outskirts of the capital city of Iho, avoiding the night dew. Hiding themselves behind the shrine dedicated to the Kunitama Deity, just as they dozed off, shadowy forms of people gathered from parts unknown to offer sacred lamps and holy wine, lamenting the perpetual darkness. Even the Chief of Iho, Natsuyamahiko, sought means to save the mortals, chanting solemn Norito before the shrine in a voice exceedingly pure. Haru-ko opened his storehouses to distribute grain, swearing before God to relieve hunger and thirst—an easy task indeed; yet he agonized over spiritual nourishment to feed their souls. Gathering the populace before God, he instructed them in divine teachings, striving to awaken degraded souls whose only care was food, clothing, shelter, and wine, to revere the divine, respect their elders, and restore their original sincere spirits so that all might fulfill their duty as children of God. Like light snow melting in spring, the divine wrath dissolved; flowers blossomed, birds sang in the joyful Divine Age, and the sun and moon shone in the skies, washing clean all demons and Amanosagume in the waterfalls of the Nile. The Chief's sincere tears for humanity flowed like a torrent on the Shirase River in Tsukushi. As if between dream and reality, Katoriwake's pure missionary song resonated through the dark night; just as Hatsu-ko bellowed drunkenly in furious madness, struck by the Kotodama of the missionary clasping his hands, the listeners' souls were polished, awakening to the deep grace of the Sovereign Deities. Heartened with boundless joy, the four divine missionaries, escorting Hatsu-ko whom they had just met, advanced along the pitch-black road beneath the cloud-shrouded sky toward the estates of Haru-ko and Natsuyamahiko. Drowsiness overcoming them on the road, the five sat down upon the dewless grass; seized by slumber, they lay fast asleep. Waiting for dawn in the forests along the Shirase River by the Nile waterfalls to purify the evil spirits entrenched at the Six Waterfalls starting from Shūgetsu Waterfall, resting beneath the dark canopy of trees, a solitary light suddenly manifested, illuminating the hearts of the five travelers. Falling from the back of the giant serpent and stunned breathless for a fleeting moment, the resonant footsteps of Natsuyamahiko leading his multitude came striding loudly near.

§ 2

Katoriwake: 'Well, well, what a terrifying nightmare that was! Unawares, I had grown conceited, mounted upon the back of a giant serpent and dragged high above the clouds! There is a saying that "a blind serpent fears nothing"; thinking myself fully sighted, I strutted about boasting that I was the missionary of the realm who would open the eyes of the deaf and blind of the world! What a frightful dream indeed! This was assuredly a prophetic spiritual warning. Ah, how easily does vanity creep into the soul! Pride is the fountain of grave ruin, as the adage goes; while preaching it constantly, I was like the bamboo-hat seller caught bareheaded in the rain. Whenever I thought others wicked or foolish, it was all reflecting myself! Henceforth, I must thoroughly temper and reforge my soul! Oh, thank You, dear God! You have warned me most timely!'
