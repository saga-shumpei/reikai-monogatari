Chapter 15: 四天王 [1760]

四天王〔1760〕

§ 1

Kunishiu and Asashiu, the two of them, arrived at the great hall of the Kakehashigoten at Arina Falls, which Kunitamayoriwakenomikoto oversees, at around ten o'clock in the morning. Kunitamayoriwake and Tamatatsuhime, the married couple, were dressed in Saifuku and, together with many Shinto, had finished the monthly Saiten and were chanting the Sendenka.

Kunitamayori: 'The water of Arina Falls is clear, / In the depths of this valley, / Wrapped in the blessings of Kami, / Morning and evening at Kakehashigoten, / I offer my Magokoro and serve. / I am a Sendenshi, / Serving the Kami of Kunitamayoriwake, / Together with Tamatatsuhime, / The Mioshihe of Sumeōkami, / High in the sky of Mount Arina, / Far across the Arano of Teru, / Beyond the sea, I convey. / Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, / Though the world has become a place of eternal darkness, / Since the distant age of the Kami, / The blessings of Kami remain unchanged, / Blessing the people of the four quarters. / Overcoming the rough winds and ugly rains of the world, / Living peacefully in this world, / The land of Teru is truly magnificent.'

'The morning sun is clear in the land of Teru, / The evening sun is also clear in the land of Teru, / The moon is vivid in the firmament, / Traversing the heavens, the land of Teru. / Like the countless grains of sand on the beach, / The stars in the sky are also of the land of Teru. / The mirror pond where the imperial Kami, Tsukiteruhiko, / Appeared and manifested, / Illuminating the darkness of the eternal night, / The land of Teru shines with majesty. / By the grace of Amaterasukami, / The fields and mountains are green and the water is clear, / The waves crashing from the great ocean, / Are calm, and the work of fishing, / Is abundant, and the plains of the land, / Are rich with rice, wheat, beans, and millet. / The people who live upon the earth, / Rejoice morning and evening, / There is no one who does not revere the Kami. / Truly, worthy of the name Takasago.'

'The land of Teru at the base of the rocks, / The Kunitama who possesses it, / Before the holy presence, like a deer, / Bending the knees and prostrating, / I serve and revere the great majesty. / Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, / I offer thanks for the blessings of the spirit. / One, two, three, four, five, six, / Seven, eight, nine, ten, and more, / Millions of people of the land, / Morning and evening before the great presence, / Gathering together to repay, / One part of a thousand of the blessings. / The moon of three-five shines across, / On this day of life, the monthly, / Sacred festival I serve. / The delicious things of the sea, rivers, mountains, and fields, / I place in abundance at Yokoyama, / And offer my Magokoro to serve.'

'Though the morning sun may shine or cloud over, / Though the moon may wax or wane, / Though the Atlantic may dry up, / Though Mount Arina may crumble, / Though the flow of the falls may cease, / The people who are moistened by the dew, / Of the divine grace that never ends for a thousand generations, / The color of their hearts is beautiful, / The flowers of their hearts are beautiful. / The creator of this world, / Infinite, absolute, without beginning or end, / The ancestor of the land with strong divine virtue, / Kuninotokotachi no Ōmikami, / Who saves the people of the world widely, / Before the noble presence of Kami Susanoo no Ōkami, / I revere and praise, / The great incantation of the heavenly prayer, / How joyful it is. / Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, / I offer thanks for the blessings of the spirit.'

Having finished singing, they clapped their hands four times and withdrew from before the Kami; the two pillars, while smiling and bowing to the many Shinto, proceeded into their own living quarters.

Kunishiu and Asashiu, the two of them, had been mingling among the Shinto and participating in this Saiten. Asashiu leaned his mouth toward the ear of Kuniteruwake and said,

'Is that not a solemn Sendenka? And although the Kamitsukasa here seems quite elderly, does he not produce a clear and pure voice like that of a youth of seventeen or eighteen? When I hear that voice, I become so moved that I tremble.'

Kuni: 'If one's heart is pure and white, the Kototama will not become turbid, so that is why such a beautiful voice comes out. We, too, should purify our souls from now on so that our voices do not grow old. I have heard from my old man about this place in the past; it is said that a Sendenshi named Ryūkokubake, a friend of my old man, performed a play of Tamayose here together with his mother and disciples. At that time, the mother and child of Ryūkokubake secretly stole a golden ball and went all the way over Mount Arina to the field of Chin, but they encountered the admonition of the Kami and repented. It is a shrine where the history remains that next, a strong-willed Sendenshi named Takahime came, and because of that same ball, she was squeezed by the Kami and reformed. Since the golden ball that was taken away from Ryūkokubake by the Kami in the middle of his journey has become the Goshintai and is enshrined at this shrine, as long as we are believers of the San-Go-Kyō, we are not entirely without connection. How about it? Let us hold an all-night vigil here tonight to receive the divine virtue, be struck by the water at Arina Falls, and then slowly head to our destination.'

『何と荘厳な宣伝歌だありませぬか。そして此処の神司は随分老耄の様だが、其言霊は十七八の若者の様な涼しい清らかな声を出すだありませぬか。あの声を聞くと私はふるひ附く程好になりました』

Asa: 'That would indeed be most splendid. If I may say, Oyabun, rather than going to such a distant land, separated by mountains and rivers for hundreds of leagues, crossing just one mountain brings us to the country where you were born. So would it not be all the better to settle down here for a while? Even without going all the way to the land of Hiru, one can still become a chivalrous man.'

Kuniteruwake: 'Once a man has made up his mind, he cannot give up halfway. Even if confronted by a precipice, or if a cold blade glimmers above his head, a man cannot be called a true man unless he executes what he has once declared. How much less can we—who are praised by the world as a man among men and who harbor the great ambition of saving the world through chivalry—do such a weak-willed thing? If you dislike it, you may remain here stuck fast for as long as you wish, but I shall go on alone.'

Asa: 'I shall accompany you wherever you go. However, how about staying secluded here for three or four days to rest your bones and rest your feet?'

Kuniteruwake: 'First, let us be struck by the waterfall for two or three days to purify our bodies, receive a divine oracle from the Kami of Kagami-no-ike, and then set off little by little.'

Asa: 'Ah, that relieves my mind! In that case, shall we go to the waterfall now?'

Kuniteruwake: 'All right! First of all, let us perform the purification.'

Even as he spoke, clapping his hands and bowing twice, reciting the Heavenly Number Song deep within his throat, fourteen or fifteen men disguised as believers scattered from all sides, front and back, left and right, surrounding them, gripping the necks of both men firmly, and pinning them down with brute force. Asakō was astonished and cried out:

Asa: 'Ouch, ouch, ouch! Wh-what are you doing! Hey, aren't you believers who have faith in the Kami? What do you intend to do, grabbing a person's neck? O-ouch, it hurts! What is this? Twisting a person's arms behind his back... Did I do something bad? ... Hey, Oyabun, h-help me!'

Kuniteruwake, held down by brute force, remained facing downward, holding his breath of A-un, watching for an opportunity. Gauging the rhythm of his breath, he bounded up with a burst, instantly throwing four or five large men. The giant man holding down Asakō was also startled and let go of his grip. Asakō instantly stood upright in the middle of the room, spreading his arms wide, spitting on his hands, and roared:

Asa: 'Come on! The famous white-wave man, Asakō-sama of the capital of Chin, is none other than me! Do not meddle needlessly and come to regret it. Even I, a mere follower, am like this; what do you take my Oyabun for? He is Kuniteruwake-sama, the very pillar of the country of Chin!'

The fellow who seemed most like the leader among them sat politely and neatly upon the tatami mats, placing both hands down, and said:

Man: 'I offer my sincerest apologies. Although we are humble officials serving under Chief Councilor Isahiko, the news that Prince Kuniteruwake dropped his status and went incognito in the capital of Chin spread throughout the castle town. Many people made arrangements, but since your whereabouts could not be known by any means, thinking that you might possibly flee to another land, I led a dozen or more subordinates, disguised ourselves as believers in this mansion at one of the entrances, and were observing the situation. Today, most unexpectedly, your Highness has arrived. It is truly awe-inspiring, but we shall accompany you, so please return to your home country.'

Kuniteruwake: 'You have indeed carried out a troublesome duty. Returning at your request would be an easy thing, but since I have already made up my mind, I cannot turn back even a single step. Please give up and go home. In any case, I am not abandoning the country of Chin forever. I have my own thoughts regarding this matter, so it would be best for you to return obediently.'

Man: 'I am Komaji, an inspector from the Fukamizo Office. Please do not say such things, and return at once. The castle town of Chin is in a great uproar, so if you do not return once, the living beings will fall into the agony of extreme misery. If you possess a sincere heart that loves the living beings, please allow me to accompany you, and pray return from this very place. If you do not return, we cannot possibly go back to the capital again.'

Kuni: 'Is there any need for you to return to the capital? I shall provide for your Seikuwatsu, so how about it? I have become a Kefukaku called Kunishiu and have Genzoku, so why do you not become my Kobun and sell your names as the Otokodate of Tenka? Then, after I have honed my skills, I intend to return to my Kokoku and become the Mahashira of the nation. At that time, I will promote you as well and appoint you to the rank of Ōtorishimari, but this is a moment for Shian. How about it? If you agree with what I say, throw those Jitsute into this Tanigaha immediately, without any Iyaō.'

Komaharu thought to himself... 'Perhaps I should just become a Kefukaku. No matter what, he is the Goseishi of the precious Itsukoku. Since he has lowered himself in such a way and is attempting to save the world by becoming a Shiranamiotoko, even if I remain a mere Yakunin forever, the future is clear. Perhaps I should Kausan gracefully...' and he had already reached a Ketsushin. However, he could not immediately submit in front of his Ōzei subordinates, so he was quietly observing their expressions.

Kuni: 'Hey, all of you, from today you are my Kobun. Even if not as Kefukaku, I am the future Kokushi of Takasago Castle. In that case, you are all my Kobun. How about it? There should be no Iyaō. Do you not have the heart to snap those flimsy Jitsute and throw them into the Tanigaha?'

Komaharu: 'Please, make me your direct Kobun. I will obey your orders in any matter. The proof is as you see here.'

He threw the Jitsute into the bottom of the valley below. The other arresting officers were lost in their decision, their eyes rolling in confusion as they stared at Komaharu's face, but except for the two men, Ichikō and Umakō, they kept their hands on their Jitsute, formed a line, and with a fearful look, fled the mansion and ran back toward Mt. Arina. Watching them go, Kuniteruwake said,

'Ha ha ha ha ha! Komaharu, Ichi, and Uma, it is often said that the true men may become three... three is the origin of the world, excellent, excellent. You three new converts and new Kefukaku, when added to us two, make five; the spirit of the strict. The bright moon of three and five. Ah, how auspicious, how auspicious! Come, let us now offer our thanks to the Kami.'

Komaharu: 'Goseishi-sama, if that is the case, from today, though it is truly rude of me, may I call you Oyabun?'

Kuni: 'That is settled. Call me Oyabun Kunishiu-sama. That goes for Ichi and Uma as well. To abandon the stifling work of an official and become a man of one's own, with only one's shins and one's spear, is the true heart of a man. You too have been saved by this. It seems your spirit is good, for you seem to have understood my heart. Ah ha ha ha ha!'

Komaharu: 'Eh, Oyabun, I must tell you, if we do not leave this place quickly, those thirteen men who just returned will go back to the capital and will surely report to the Rōjū Isahiko. If that happens, more arresting officers will come, and it is dangerous, so we must find a way to hide ourselves.'

Kuni: 'Nah, do not worry. It takes five or six days to climb up and down these steep slopes, cross the wide fields, and return to the capital. Even if they come from there, it will take another five or six days. Well, for the next ten days, we are safe. We shall leisurely perform Misogi, receive the Shinchoku, and then decide our own policy thoroughly. If you are fretting and worrying your head over such things, you will never be able to become a Kefukaku. Ah, how interesting, how interesting! I have now gathered four Kobun, have I? Warriors of the Shitenno, I rely on you.'

Asa: 'Excuse me, Oyabun-sama, we must establish an order among the four of us, but who is the eldest brother among us? Surely it must be me, right?'

Kuni: 'In terms of time, you are the elder brother. However, in terms of courage and physical strength, that is questionable. In any case, let us go to the waterfall for Misogi. One, two, three, four.'

Saying this, they left the Kakehashi Palace behind and the group of five arrived at the side of a waterfall where the sound of water roared and echoed. What does the heartless waterfall water speak of? It roars and thunders, shaking the earth, and the sunlight reflects in the countless droplets, raining down like indescribable jewels.

駒治『何卒私を貴方の直参の乾児にして下さいませ。如何なる事でも御命令に服従致します。証拠は此通で厶います』

と十手を、眼下の谷底へ投込んで了つた。他の捕手連中は去就に迷ひ、目を白黒させて駒治の顔を見つめてゐたが、市公、馬公の両人を除く外、十手に手をかけたまま、列をつくり、駆足の姿勢で、怖さうに館を逃げ出しアリナ山を指して逃げ帰りゆく。後見送つて国照別は、

『ハヽヽヽヽ駒治、市に馬、誠の者は三人になるかも知れぬぞよ……とはよく言つた事だ、三人世の元結構々々だ。お前達新帰順新侠客が三人、俺達二人を合すれば五人となる、厳の御霊だ。三五の明月だ。ヤ、目出たい目出たい、サア是から神様に御礼を申上げやう』

駒治『御世子様、そんなら今日から、誠にすみませぬが、貴方を親分と申しても宜しう厶いますか』

国『きまつた事だ、親分国州さまと云つてくれ。市も馬も其通だぞ。窮屈な取締をやめて脛一本、沼矛一本の男一匹になるのは男子の本懐だ。汝も之で救はれたのだ。ヤツパリ霊がいいとみえて、俺の心が分つたと見えるワイ、アツハヽヽヽヽ』

駒治『エー、親分に申上げますが、早く此場を立去らないと、今帰つた十三人の奴、都へ帰り、伊佐彦老中へ報告するに間違ひありませぬ。さうすりや捕手がやつて来る、険呑ですから、何とか身隠しをせななりますまい』

国『ナアニ、心配するな、此急阪を登り下りして、それから広い野を渡り、都へ帰るにも五日や六日はかかる。それからやつて来た所で、又五日や六日は時日が要る。マアここ十日位は大丈夫だ。ゆつくり身禊でもして神勅を受け、それから自分の方針を徹底的にきめるのだ。そんな事に齷齪して頭を痛めてゐるやうな事では到底侠客にはなれないぞ。ヤ面白い面白い、俺も〆て乾児が四人出来たか、四天王の勇士、しつかり頼むよ』

浅『モシモシ親分さま、四人の中で順序を立てておかねばなりませぬが、誰が此中では一番兄貴になるのですか、キツと私でせうね』

国『時間に於てはお前が兄貴だ。併し乍ら胆力と腕力に於ては怪しいものだなア。何は兎もあれ、お滝へ身禊に行く事にせう、一二三四』

と言ひ乍ら懸橋御殿を後に、水音轟々として響きわたる瀑布の傍に一行五人辿りついた。無心の滝水は何を語るか。囂々鼕々として地をゆるがせ、無数の飛沫には日光が映じて、えも言はれぬ宝玉の雨を降らしてゐる。
