Chapter 1: Great 評定 [1746]

大評定〔1746〕

§ 1

Spanning across Taihei, Taisei, and Riyōyō, and blocking the waves of Tokoyo, the Nanbeitairiku, which hangs like the severed leg of a Togiu, is an ideal Tengoku that knows the seasons of Shiyunka but not those of Shiutō. The Seifū blowing incessantly from the Taiyō contains Enbun, making the Tochi Masumasu Hōzeu; Jintōdai Konomi ripen in Zuishiyo in Hōjiyuku, and Amata flowers, enough that Gojin could sit amidst them and still have space to spare, bloom in profusion in all Shihau, while Sūmanshiyu of Yakusau grow in Haukau and Hanmo in the Sanya everywhere. In the thousands of tributaries flowing into the Amazon River, hundreds of thousands of species of fish dwell, and the mountains contain a rich store of minerals such as gold, silver, copper, iron, and coal, with the production of coal in particular being unparalleled anywhere else in the world. However, as the methods of mining are not yet sufficiently equipped at present, it is a case of leaving a precious treasure to the earth in vain.

The Andes mountains tower high into the clouds, with highlands ranging from fourteen or fifteen thousand to thirty thousand feet above sea level. And upon the summits of the mountains, plateaus extend, ranging from ten ri to several tens of ri in width. The number of trees is quite large compared to our country. Furthermore, the Amazon River, which flows through the country of Brazil, is the greatest river in the world, so vast that even if the entire nation of Japan were thrown into it lengthwise, there would still be room to spare. Particularly in the wilderness of Peru, Brazil, Argentina, and elsewhere, cotton trees resembling the persimmon trees of Japan grow naturally here and there, and cotton retaining natural colors such as blue, yellow, red, purple, and white hangs from the branches all year round. Also, things like bamboo grow in clumps like the pampas grass of the Japanese mainland, and they are thick enough that if cut across, one could make a coffin or a water bucket. There are even butterbur-like plants so large that ten people can gather under a single leaf to shelter from the rain. Cattle, horses, sheep, and pigs are left to graze in the boundless wilderness, yet each has its own owner. Delicious strawberries, bananas, and figs grow in such abundance in slightly lower lands that one becomes weary of them. And monkeys, deer, and wild boars approach near human dwellings in broad daylight and play without concern. Butterflies like hawks and bats, insects like bees, bees like sparrows, and flies like fists sometimes come flying in swarms at the mercy of the wind. Everything is continental, and from the eyes of a Japanese person, it is nothing but things that make one's liver chill. However, as Zuigetsu has merely stated what appeared to his eyes while lying on the third floor of a hotel at Dōgo Onsen in Iyo, it may perhaps be mistaken. If a person well-versed in the circumstances of South America reads this story, they will for the first time understand its truth or falsehood. I have merely stated what was reflected in my spiritual eye.

Ever since Kuniyoriwake-no-Mikoto, a missionary of the San-shin-kyō, by the command of the Lord God Susanoo-no-Ōkami and Kototama-yoriwake-no-Mikoto, married Suetsukohime, the youngest daughter of the eight maidens of the Great Spirit of Mizu, and became the governor of the capital of Chin in Argentina, the world was at peace, the land was fulfilled, and the four classes of people were harmonious; they built the Tengoku of Chin for eternity, and the people, yearning for the benevolence of the governor, came to look up to and serve them as the heaven-sent Lord, Master, and Parent. However, the ideology of the Ural-kyō from the land of Tokoyo, along with the development of transportation, was imported before anyone knew it, and as days followed days and months followed months, the country gradually began to show signs of demonic obscuration. Arguments for the abolition of the Seike and the abolition of the equestrian class erupted everywhere, people formed parties and fought over factions, and Argentina, which had been so peaceful, finally reached the point of brewing a state of affairs like tangled hemp. Kuniyoriwake grew old, coming to use a staff even for walking within the castle, his head covered in frost, and his forehead began to shine almost like an electric lamp. Suetsukohime also grew old and became a middle-aged lady. Between Kuniyoriwake and Suetsukohime, there were a son and a daughter named Kuniteruwake and Harunohime. Kuniteruwake inherited the free-spirited and bold disposition of his father, Kuniyoriwake, and from his youth, he made chivalry his guiding principle in life. And he detested the Seike lifestyle, thinking that if there were a gap, he would slip out of the castle and lead a simple life as a commoner.

§ 2

Matsuwakahiko and Sutekohime, who had been faithfully assisting the Kokushi, had gradually grown old, and had raised two children, Matsuyoriwake and Tokihahime. Furthermore, Isahiko and Ihaharuwake held the important posts of Saiu under Matsuwakahiko, assisting him in his administrative duties.

Kamususanō Ōkami, desiring to carry out the Keirin of Sumeōkami, looked back at Isoyakata from afar, boarded the Ama-Ihakusufune, and descended to the capital of the precious land of Argentina. He appointed Suekohime, the youngest of the eight maidens, as the administrator of the land, and designated the Kamuzukasa Kuniyoriwake as her husband. After having them perform the Gafukin ceremony, he stood up with courage and returned to the Iwa-no-yakata of the Ubusuna of Fusa. Thirty-three years have passed since then, and the capital today is a great metropolis unparalleled in South America, with high mansions standing in rows and houses of the people spread out dozens of times more than before.

Transportation systems have been completed, numerous government offices stand in rows, and large stores are lined up like the teeth of a comb. There is no one who is not surprised by the prosperity that has changed so completely that one wonders where the traces of the past have gone. Kuniteruwake and Harunohime, born between Kuniyoriwake and Suekohime, surpassed the masses in beauty and were renowned throughout the South American nations as the moon and flowers of the capital of the precious land, stirring the emotions of young men and women and making their blood boil. As the story of the distant age of the gods lies upon the bedding, and as the Kototama wheel rolls along, it is told in an amusing and comical manner. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi, may the spirits be blessed.

In a separate room of the council chamber within Takasago Castle in the capital of the precious land, the elder Matsuwakahiko, along with the senior councilors Isahiko and Ihaharuwake, gathered their heads together to hold a secret meeting. The sky was overcast, and for some reason, it was sultry, with a strange and unpleasant atmosphere enveloping the room. Matsuwakahiko faced the two senior councilors, and while sniffing his runny nose, he thrust his red-black, skin-and-bone arms forward, and with a gesture like a beckoning cat, he broke the silence with a trembling voice from his toothless mouth.

Matsuwakahiko: 'My two lords, thank you for coming to the castle so early in the morning despite your busy schedules. I have invited you today because there is something I would like to consult with you about, and I wish to express my determination without reserve and obtain your assistance.'

He said this while gulping down a mouthful of coffee and wiping the dew dripping from his beard with a thick towel two or three times.

Isahiko: 'We are truly grateful that you, despite your advanced age, always dedicate your body and life to the important affairs of the state. I do not know what the matter is for which you have invited us today, but if it is within our power, we shall exert all our efforts for the sake of the Kokushi and for the sake of the precious land.'

Matsuwakahiko: 'No, hearing that, Matsuwakahiko feels relieved. Ihaharuwake-dono, you too must feel the same as Isahiko-dono, I presume.'

Ihaharuwake: 'Indeed, that is so. We are those who naturally dedicate our bodies and lives to our lord and country, so there is no reason for us to disobey your words in the slightest. However, the world today has changed greatly. A spirit of innovation is overflowing. Therefore, as we are those who lament the world and worry about the country, depending on your words, we may have to disagree when considering the future of the state. I ask that you be aware of this in advance.'

Matsuwakahiko: 'Indeed, as you say, today's society is not the society of the past. It is a world situation that knows almost no bounds in its daily progress. Therefore, what Matsuwakahiko wishes to consult you about is, as you know, that I can no longer endure the duties of my old age, and I wish to resign from the post of Grand Elder, hand over my position to you two, who are new and spirited, and retire to a life of leisure to enjoy the clear wind and the bright moon. Would you two not accept my wish and agree to be my successors?'

Isahiko: 'This is an unexpected request from you, my lord. Are you not the pillar of the precious land? With the lack of unity between the upper and lower classes, and the fierce struggle between the noble families and the masses today, if you, the cornerstone of the state, were to retire in such a case, it is the most obvious logic that the already chaotic state would fall into further strife and open the way to endangering the nation. I beg you to reconsider this matter at least.'

Matsuwakahiko: 'While your recommendation is reasonable, it is unbearable for me to remain in a position of national importance while being physically unable to fulfill the duties of my advanced age, thereby blocking the path of my successors and causing the atmosphere of the nation to decay further. I implore you to accept my wish and take on the position of Tairau between the two of you.'

Ihaharu: 'Indeed, as Matsuwakahiko-sama says, for an old man with little time left to govern the nation is to hinder the progress of the state most severely, and if it is contrary to the Great Way of Kamunagara, then please retire as you wish. In truth, I have been expecting these very words for several years. Your wisdom is truly admirable, and I am overcome with gratitude.'

Isahiko spoke with indignation, his voice raised:

Isahiko: 'This is outrageous, both of you. With such weak-willed attitudes, you will never be able to govern the people. Is it not the sacred duty of a Tairau to demonstrate a spirit of sacrifice for the sake of the nation until the very end? Ihaharu-wake-dono, you have forgotten to offer counsel to Matsuwakahiko-sama, and your ambition to sit in his seat and usurp the authority of the Kokushi-sama is clearly revealed. With such an ambitious official at the top, the lower ranks will fall into further disorder, eventually leading to a chaotic age that cannot be salvaged. I firmly hope that Matsuwakahiko-sama will remain in his post.'

Ihaharu: 'This is an absurd statement from Isahiko. I harbor no such ambition whatsoever. Think carefully. Matsuwakahiko-sama is already of advanced age; in such times, only a young man of spirit and vigor can support the nation and hold the hearts of the people. As expected of the wise Matsuwakahiko-sama, he has perceived the situation and voluntarily chosen the path of resignation; I can only praise him as a pillar of the nation. Do not worry, for I am capable. I shall become the successor to Matsuwakahiko, and before the Kokushi and the people, I will implement a government of supreme truth, purity, beauty, and love, and restore the land to the heavenly paradise of old that the Great Lord Susanoo-Ōkami once brought down.'

Isa: 'Be silent. Although you hold the position of Rōjū, it is recognized by all, high and low, that you are incompetent, without policy, and utterly incapable of bearing the heavy responsibilities of the state. You constantly spout grandiloquent words, establish private academies to harbor delinquent youths, and are the very devil who cultivates the roots of national subversion; you are not a person of character to influence the politics of the castle. Even so, there is no other suitable candidate, and though it may be a burden, we must ask Matsuwakahiko-sama to exert himself once more, lest a non-nationalist like you seize political power. This would be the most terrifying catastrophe for the nation. If you have even a shred of sincere patriotism, leave the position of Rōjū gracefully, return your rank, go down to the fields, observe the conditions of the people thoroughly, and then offer your opinions. As long as I, Isahiko, am here, I will never let you achieve your desires.'

Matsuwakahiko thought to himself... He knew full well that the world today could not continue as it had been. However, if he handed power to Ihaharu-wake, who held reckless ideas, the foundations of the nation would surely be overturned; and if he handed power to Isahiko, who truly cared for the nation, he would spread outdated conservatism and open the way for further alienation of the people's hearts. 'How troublesome this is,' he thought. He could not retreat, yet he could not advance. Looking at the state of the nation, he realized that neither rising nor falling was within his own power. There was no reason for the politics of noble families or cliques to last forever... No, the political power of the ruling class was already nearing its end. He wished to renew the atmosphere of the nation, save the people from their weariness, alleviate the deterioration of thought, and establish a new government where high and low were united. 'Ah, what should I do?' He sniffled, crossed his arms, and tears even dripped from his eyes. The three men all kept their mouths shut, watching each other's faces.

伊佐『おだまりなされ。貴殿は老中の地位に在りと雖、無能無策、到底国家の重任に堪へざるは、上下一般の認むる所で厶る。常に大言壮語を吐き、私立大学を創立して不良青年を収容し、国家顛覆の根源を培ふ悪魔の張本、到底城中の政治を左右する人格者では厶らぬ。それだと云つて外に適任者はなし、御苦労乍ら松若彦様に今一度の御奮発を願はなくては、忽ち貴殿の如き非国家主義者が政権を掌握さるる事となつて了ふ。之れ国家の為に最も恐るべき大事変で厶る。貴殿にして一片報国の至誠あらば体よく老中の地位を去り、爵位を奉還し、野に下つて、民情をトクと視察し其上更めて意見を進言なされ。此伊佐彦のある限り、どこ迄も貴殿の欲望は遂げさせませぬぞ』

松若彦は心の中にて……到底今日の世の中、今迄通りではやつて行けないことは、百も千も承知してゐた。されど投槍思想を帯びた岩治別に政権を渡せば、忽ち国家の根底を覆すであらうし、真に国家を思ふ伊佐彦に政権を渡せば、時勢おくれの保守主義を振りまはし、益々民心離反の端を開くであらう、ハテ困つた事だなア、退くには退かれず進むにも進まれず、国内一般の民情を見れば、上げもおろしも、自分の力ではならなくなつて来た。到底清家政治や閥族政治のいつ迄も続くべき道理がない…否斯の如く乗馬階級の政治的権力は最早最後に瀕してゐる。何とかして国内の空気を一新し、人心の倦怠を救ひ、思想の悪化を緩和し、上下一致の新政を布きたいものだ。あゝ何うしたら可からうかな、……と水ばなをすすり、腕をくみて両眼よりは涙さへ滴らしてゐる。三人は何れも口を噤んで互に顔を見守つてゐる。

§ 4

It was Kuniteruwake who came along, humming a tune with a Sanjiyakuobi wrapped carelessly around his Yukata.

Kuniteru: 'Yo, is this a meeting of the puppets? In any case, with those dried-up old heads, no decent Saudan will ever be concluded... Oh, Matsuwakahiko, are you crying? Is it because you have grown old, too? You have become quite tearful, haven't you? ...Oh, Isahiko the conservative Raujiu and Ihaharuwake the Nageyari Raujiu, are you? ...How interesting. Why don't you have a Daigiron and let me hear it to pass the time? In truth, I am sick of this Seikaseikuwatsu and am thinking of flying off somewhere, but no matter what I say, I am like a bird in a cage; with the Ibutsu of the Kiujidai, such as Kinji and Eji, hanging around me, I cannot do a thing. This is, in short, the Sashizu of the Tairau with his old-fashioned head. My father would never like such stifling things. Hey, why don't you drink some sake and cheer up? Tears are forbidden within the Takasagojō walls, you know.'

Matsuwakahiko, hiding his tears out of embarrassment, shuffled his seat back about two or three ken, and while pressing his head to the tatami, said,

Matsuwakahiko: 'Oh, oh, is that you, Young Lord-sama? I have been terribly rude. Please, I beg of you, by the power of Kamunao-bi and Ōnao-bi, look upon me again, hear me again, and forgive my Muzafusa.'

Kuniteru: 'Hey, old man, what are you doing? I hate such empty, formalistic things. Why don't you be a bit more lively, stand at attention, and give a simple salute? Isn't it all too tedious?'

Matsuwakahiko: 'I am humbled. However, there are rules within the castle, so I cannot break the Yūsoku-kojitsu. It is said that without etiquette, there is no order; since etiquette is the primary requirement for governing a nation, I must ask you to forgive me for this, no matter how much you may dislike it. This is the very foundation of politics, which should be called the national essence of the Chin-no-kuni. Without etiquette, the nation will immediately fall into disorder, the order between young and old will be broken, and the path of lord and subject, father and child, and husband and wife will perish.'

Kuniteru: 'Hmm, is that so? That is fine, but do you think the Yūsoku-kojitsu will be preserved even if you change countries and are no longer here? The hearts of people today do not like such tedious things. We live in an age where it is popular to settle everything quickly. The world has changed from the days of old when people would compose poems and wander about in long sleeves. Since the administrative work has become a hundred or a thousand times more complicated than in the past, such gloomy things will never last.'

Ihaharuwake: 'I am truly moved by the words of the Young Lord-sama; I, Ihaharuwake, am truly overcome with emotion. It is only by having such a Young Lord-sama that the nation of Chin can hope for eternal prosperity and the flourishing of the state. Your father, the Lord, is already of advanced age, and in this situation where we do not know when he might depart for the heavens, hearing the wise heart of the Young Lord-sama, I, Ihaharuwake, am filled with such great joy that my courage is surging forth. With such a subject for such a young lord, although we have been added to the ranks of the Raujiu, our hearts have not yet grown senile. I beg you, Young Lord-sama, please keep this humble subject in your heart and give your direct orders to me regarding important matters. Matsuwakahiko-dono has just expressed his desire to resign, saying he is no longer fit for the duties of his old age.'

Kuniteru: 'Hmm, is that so? It is probably fine for Matsuwakahiko to step down now. Isahiko also has a very old head, so he is no good either, and while Ihaharuwake seems a little too advanced for today's era, there are also parts where he is lagging behind; in any case, perfect politics cannot be done by your hands. I am thinking of advising my father to make you retire, put you into a simple life as commoners, and let you spend a peaceful remainder of your lives, so why don't you all abolish the Tairau and Raujiu and live a life of ease in the countryside? I am quite prepared for this, you know.'

原文（近代文語）

そこへ浴衣の上へ無雑作に三尺帯をグルグル巻にして鼻唄を唄ひながらやつて来たのは国照別であつた。

国照『ヨー、デクさまの御集会かな、到底、干からびた古い頭では、碌な相談もまとまりはしまい、…ヤアー松若彦、お前は泣いてゐるのか、お前もヤツパリ年が老つた加減か、余程涙つぽくなつただないか、……ヤア保守老中の伊佐彦に投槍老中の岩治別だな、…ヤ面白からう、一つ大議論をやつて退屈ざましに僕に聞かしてくれないか。僕も実の所は清家生活がイヤになつて、どつかへ飛出さうと思つてゐるのだが、何をいつても籠の鳥同様、近侍だとか、衛士だとか旧時代の遺物が僕の身辺にぶらついてゐるものだから、何うすることも出来やしない。之も要するに頭の古い大老の指図だらう。僕の親爺は、決してこんな窮屈なことは、好まない筈だ。オイ酒でも呑んで、いさぎようせぬかい。高砂城内で涙は禁物だからのう』

松若彦は手持無沙汰に涙をかくし乍ら二三間許り座をしざり、畳に頭をすりつけ乍ら、

『コレハ　コレハ、若君様で厶いますか、エライ御無礼を致しました。何卒々々神直日大直日に見直し聞直し、無作法をお赦し下さいませ』

国照『オイ、爺、ソリヤ何をするのだ。左様な虚礼虚式的な事は、僕は大嫌ひだ。モウちつと活溌に直立不動の姿勢を執つて、簡単に挙手の礼をやつたら何うだ、余りまどろしいぢやないか』

松若『恐れ入りました。併し乍ら城内には城内の規則が厶いますから、有職故実を破る訳には参りませぬ。礼なくんば治まらずと申しまして、国家を治むるには礼儀が第一で厶いますから、之計りは何程お気に入らなくても許して頂かねばなりませぬ。之は珍の国の国粋とも申すべき重要なる政治の大本で厶います。礼儀なければ国家は直にみだれ、長幼の序は破れ、君臣父子夫婦の道は亡びて了ひます』

国照『ウンさうか、それも結構だが、お前が若しも国替をして、居らなくなつても、有職故実は保存されると思うてゐるのか、今日の人間の心はそんなまどろしい事は好まないよ。何事も手つ取早く埒をつける事が流行する世の中だ。昔の様に歌をよんだり、長袖を着てブラブラと遊んで居つた時代とは世の中が変つてゐる。昔の百倍も千倍も事務が煩雑になつてゐるのだから、そんな辛気臭い事は到底、永続すまいよ』

岩治『実に痛み入つたる若君様のお言葉、岩治別、実に感激に堪へませぬ。斯の如き若君様を得てこそ、珍の国家は万代不易、国家の隆昌を期する事が出来るでせう。親君様は最早御老齢、何時御上天遊ばすかも知れぬ此場合、賢明なる若君様の御心を承はり、岩治別、イヤもう、大変な喜びに打たれ、勇気が勃々として湧いて参りました。此若君にして此臣あり、老中の仲間に加へられたる吾々なれど、未だ心まで老耄はして居りませぬ。何卒若君様、微臣を御心にかけさせられ、重要事務は微臣に直接御命令下さいませ。松若彦殿は老齢職に堪へずとして、只今吾々の前に辞意をもらされました』

国照『ウンさうか、松若彦もモウ退いても可いだらう。伊佐彦も随分古い頭だから、此奴も駄目だし、岩治別は少し許り今日の時代に進みすぎてるやうでもあり、又遅れてるやうな所もあり、到底完全な政治はお前たちの腕では出来相もない。僕が親爺に勧告して退職をさせ、簡易なる平民生活に入れてやり、安楽な余生を送らせたいと思つてゐるのだから、一層のこと、お前たちも大老や老中なんか廃して、安逸な田園生活でもやつたら何うだ。僕も大に覚悟してゐるのだからな』

§ 5

The Sannin stared at the face of Kuniteruwake in Mugon, as if stealing glances. Kuniteruwake, in a Muzafusa manner,

'O Okimono of the alcove of Takasagojiyō Castle, O Mushinkeishitsu Kotsutōhin-dono, three gathered together possess the Chie of Monjiyu. You should thoroughly devise Shinsankibō to Yōgo the Heiwa and Kaufuku of the Shiyujiyō and to Zendau the Shisau of the Jinmin. Ah, looking at those difficult Shiwakuchiyazura has made my shoulders stiff. Well now, shall I ride a horse and go galloping around the Baba?'

Throwing out these words, he strode with heavy Ashioto toward the Okuden. As he Mioku his departure, Matsuwakahiko shed tears once again,

'If the Wakagimisama, who is the Kanjinkaname Kōkeishiya, has such thoughts, then Mohaya the Kokuka of Chin has no choice but to face Metsubō. Ah, things have come to a troublesome pass, has it not, Isahiko-dono?'

Isahiko turned pale, his lips trembling violently as he smoothly stroked his bald head,

'As your Excellency says, it is indeed a troublesome matter. How ought we to devise a method to bring tranquility to the Shiyujiyō of Chin and cause our house to prosper eternally? When I raise my head from my pillow at midnight and ponder the future of the Kokuka, I truly cannot endure the feeling of anxiety.'

Iwanji: 'Ahahaha! To circulate through this deadlocked modern age, to establish a benevolent government wherein the Shiyujiyō are intoxicated with the heavenly joy of drumming on their bellies and stamping the ground while enjoying their respective trades, is a matter of no difficulty whatsoever. With all due respect to both of your honors, it is likely because you are stubborn and bigoted, failing to understand the age. If we only Zendau the current of the times, the Shiyujiyō of Chin will yearn for the virtue of the Kokushi, and it is a clear fact that a heavenly society will instantly unfold. At any rate, I consider your retirement to be the primary means for the progress of the Kokuka. If you blindly adhere to old customs, suppress the hearts of the Shiyujiyō, and hinder the progress of society, who knows what grave event may erupt from beneath your feet at any moment. I do not argue in order to gain rights for myself, nor to monopolize political power. It is because I believe my thinking to be the best method to save the Kokuka. Even regarding both of your honors, if you promptly remove your colored spectacles and discern my sincere heart, your doubts will naturally be resolved.'

Matsuwakahiko: 'Your inner heart seeks to accomplish your own ambition through specious rhetoric—never, never shall Matsuwakahiko fall for such tricks! Inept though I am, I am the pillar of the country of Chin; since matters have come to this, worry no further. As long as life remains in me, I shall strive and exert myself for the sake of our lord and country, despite my old age. Therefore, Isahiko-dono, from this very day and hour I relieve Iwanjiwake of his office of Rōjū, so pray consider it so as well. And henceforth, let us consult on all matters together.'

Isahiko beamed with delight on his entire face, displaying an attitude as if to say, "Ah, the nuisance has been ousted!"

'Your Excellency's words are most reasonable indeed; for the sake of the Kokuka, I respectfully offer my congratulations. Iwanjiwake-dono, surely you will not disobey the words from the Tairō. Come, leave this place at once!'

He berated him in an overbearing manner.

Iwanji: 'These are outrageously improper words from both of you! I am not one appointed by the likes of you. Considering the merit of my long years of laborious service in state affairs, I was added to the ranks of Rōjū by the Kokushi himself. For you, holding merely the position of Tairō, to pronounce my dismissal upon me—is this not utterly outrageous? Even if you are called those who ignore divine authority and privatize state affairs, you will have no words to escape the charge. Traitorous ministers and rebellious subjects are precisely what you two are!'

He glared at them in an overbearing manner, his voice growing rough.

Matsuwakahiko and Isahiko exchanged glances, quietly rose from that place, and proceeded into the palace of the Kokushi, Kuniyoriwake.

Afterwards, Iwanjiwake folded his hands, and as thoughts of the past and future crossed his mind, he wept tears of grief over the world and concern for the nation. Just then, the young master Kuniteruwake hurriedly entered all alone,

Kuniteruwake: 'Hey, Iwanjiwake-dono, escape through the back gate as quickly as possible! To capture thee and throw thee into prison, those two dotard old men have summoned the Ōmetsuke and are making arrangements. Come, once time is lost, there will be no undoing it; hurry, hurry!'

He urged him on. Iwanjiwake raised his hand in salute, left behind only the single word, 'Dankon', and taking advantage of the evening shadows, transformed his appearance and vanished through the back gate into parts unknown.

The full moon illuminated the edge of the eastern mountains, smilingly gazing down as if oblivious to the commotion within Takasagojiyō Castle.

原文（近代文語）

三人は国照別の顔を無言のまま、盗む様にして打守つてゐる。国照別は無雑作に、

『高砂城の床の置物、無神経質の骨董品殿、三人よれば文珠の智慧だ。トツクリと衆生の平和と幸福とを擁護し、人民の思想を善導すべく神算鬼謀を巡らしたが可からう。あゝ六かしい皺苦茶面を見て肩が凝つて来た。ドーラ、馬にでも乗つて馬場でもかけ廻つて来うかな』

『閣下の仰、御尤も千万、国家の為、謹んで祝し奉ります。岩治別殿、大老よりのお言葉、ヨモヤ違背は厶るまい。サア速に此場を退出召され』

と居丈高になつて罵つた。

と居丈高になり声荒らげて睨めつけた。

松若彦、伊佐彦は目配せし乍ら、ソツと此場を立つて国依別国司の御殿に進み入る。

後に岩治別は双手を組み、越方行末のことなど思ひ浮かべて、慨世憂国の涙にくれてゐた。そこへ若君の国照別はあわただしく只一人入来り、

『オイ、岩治別殿、一時も早く裏門より逃れ出でよ。汝を捉へて獄に投ぜむと、二人の老耄爺が大目付を呼び出し手配りさしてゐる。サア、時遅れては取返しがつかぬ、早く早く』

三五の月は東の山の端を照して、高砂城内の騒ぎを知らぬ顔にニコニコと眺めてゐる。
