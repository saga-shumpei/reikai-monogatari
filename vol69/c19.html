Chapter 19: 老水 [1764]

老水〔1764〕

§ 1

Akiyamawake and the elder Morisu, bearing the responsibility for having failed to notice the escape of Kunichikawake, the Seishi of Takasagojiyō, declared that they would resign from their precious Chii to take responsibility Heumenjiyō, and submitted their Jiheu. Upon receiving the merciful Shingen from Kahedewakenomikoto, saying, 'That is unnecessary; from now on, be careful and strive for your Chiukin to the Kokuka,' they finally felt relieved. They settled back into their beloved positions and assumed that Tenkataihei was secured, but then, feeling that the thoughts of Imōtogimi Kiyokahime were somehow strange, they became deeply worried. Fearing that if they made a mistake again, they would have to commit Setsupuku to explain themselves, the two elders paid close attention to the surroundings of the Hime despite it being Yahan. Nevertheless, they were met with a surprise as if a kettle had been stolen on a moonlit night, and being beside themselves, they thought that if other officials discovered this, their own Chii would be in danger. Fortunately, as there was still some time before dawn, they decided to search for the whereabouts of the Hime within the night and quietly welcome her back into the castle. Relying on their canes, stumbling and falling, they wove through the grove of the riding ground from the back gate, and with a style like a frog jumping, crying 'Untoko dottsukoi, dottsukoi,' they chased after her, gasping for breath.

Akiyamawake sings while keeping time with his feet.

'Haa haa untoko dottsukoi shiyo. Respected by the fellows of the world as a senior retainer of Takasagojiyō, I grasped the greatest authority and sat in the position of a Tairō. Having been outsmarted by the young lord Kunichikawake, dottsukoi shiyo, my bald head is ruined, my nose is twisted, and how can the face of a Tairō stand? Having no choice, I submitted my resignation and request for retirement to the surface. The joy of being told by the infinitely merciful Kokushi-sama that it was never necessary! I felt relieved and, thinking of my duties as important, watched over things morning and night while steaming my kettle. Though it passed peacefully for a while, a demon god aiming for a gap appeared in the mansion again, and must have lured the precious Hime-sama with sweet words and pulled her out. Since there is still time before dawn, how can I leave without finding her whereabouts? Oi oi, Morisu, pull yourself together. Today is the brink of life. Untoko dottsukoi, haa haa haa. My throat is sticking and I am breathless. To have to endure such hardship at my age, the two of us, truly a fated birth. Untoko dottsukoi, dottsukoi shiyo. Be careful in all directions, and if you see a human-like shadow, grab it and investigate. Without saying yes or no, bring her back. If we do not take the fat of the two of us and remove it quietly, it will not be a lesson for the future, dottsukoi shiyo. My reading glasses are clouding up, and I cannot see even an inch ahead. If I take off my glasses, I see even less. My advancement and retreat are stuck here. Untoko dottsukoi, dottsukoi shiyo. Aita tatatsuta, I tripped on a tree stump and bruised my shin. Un un un un, ah, it hurts. Even my hip bones are creaking. I started saying useless complaints. Aita tatatsuta, aitatsuta.'

Morisu lifts up the fallen Akiyamawake, but if he keeps nursing him, he will lose sight of the Hime. However, he cannot simply abandon his friend, so he runs a ken forward, then returns, repeating his movements back and forth.

Akiyama: 'Oi, Morisu-dono, what are you doing? If the first line is broken, is it not the secret of military strategy for the second line to act? Do not worry about me, and depart quickly. In this situation where there is no time to spare, please go quickly. I, Akiyama, will become the rear guard and search the tree shadows and thickets around here as I go, so hurry, hurry.'

Being urged on, Morisu says:

Morisu: 'Indeed, I shall leave the rest to you. Untoko dottsukoi, dottsukoi shiyo. The sins of the past have come around, and I am suffering because of a woman again. Even though it is not my own love, a wicked fellow must have flown in and lured Hime-sama away, saying come, come, come. Untoko dottsukoi, dottsukoi shiyo. If I dawdle, dawn will break. I must quickly find her whereabouts, grab her, and return her to the original sheath; otherwise, our great faces will be completely ruined. We will likely have to cut our wrinkled bellies. There is nothing more unavoidable than serving the court. Untoko dottsukoi, dottsukoi shiyo. Eighty years since cutting the umbilical cord, can there be anything as painful as this? The coward Akiyamawake...'

Hey, hey, Harukohime! If you are around there, please consider our feelings and reveal your form at once. Hey, hey, Ohime-sama! I shall never scold you, so if the photograph of Ichigau, Nigau, Sangau, Shigau, or Gogau does not please you, I will search for one, even if it takes one or two shō. Oh, hey, Ohime-sama! There is a saying that a sparrow does not forget the cock until it is a hundred. No matter how stubborn Morris may be, I have experience in love. I will not speak of anything that would be to your disadvantage, Anata. Please set your Murakimo heart at ease and appear before me at once. The great disturbance at Takakurajiyō, the great Daimondai of the nation of Hiru—to abandon your beloved father and Hahauhe to leave is unfilial. Incidentally, I am also unfortunate. I am following in the footsteps of Akishū. I tripped on a tree stump and grazed my shin on a sharp stone. Groan, groan, groan, groan, ouch, ouch! Ouch, ouch, ouch, ouch!'

Having said this, his breath grew thin and he collapsed on the spot.

Harukohime had caught up to the Hime in a thicket of bushes to the side and was resting her breath, but seeing this state, she felt pity and said in a low voice,

Harukohime: 'Hime-sama, is that not Morris who has collapsed there? If we leave him like that, he will surely perish. Shall we nurse him and save him?'

Sayaka: 'Ah, if I do not save him, it is wrong, but if I do save him, my purpose will not be fulfilled. What should I do? I cannot simply run away while letting an old retainer die before my eyes. This is a difficult situation. Haruko, you, please go and nurse Morris little by little. If you revive him too quickly, I will have no time to escape, so time it well and save him slowly so that he does not perish. In the meantime, I shall escape, you see.'

Harukohime: 'That is indeed a good idea. No matter how many officials like Morris may come, I will hold them back so that they do not go one step further south, so please be at ease.'

Sayaka: 'I rely on you in every way. Farewell...'

With these words, she started running, using her Vajra staff for support. The night cleared up completely, and the voices of small birds could be heard from all directions. Haruko called out,

Harukohime: 'Hime-sama, I will surely follow you later.'

Sayaka-hime nodded two or three times and hid her form within the dense forest. Haruko approached Morris's side and saw him moving his body twitchingly, breathing faintly. She immediately had him take water from her canteen into his mouth, patted his back three or four times, prayed to the Great Gods of the Three and Five, and recited the Heavenly Number Song: 'One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, hundred, thousand.' After about five minutes, Morris let out a groan, lifted his head slightly, opened his aged eyes, and said,

Morris: 'Ah, Akiyama-wake, you have saved me well. Because I am old and my legs are fragile, I have met with such a cruel fate. Ah, my eyes are dizzy. Well, I must rest my breath here for a while. Sayaka-hime-sama would not do such a reckless thing, but that maid, Haruko, she must be the ringleader. Hey, Akiyama, I cannot scold the Hime-sama, so as a future punishment, we must throw that Haruko into a prison and make her suffer, groan, groan, groan.'

Upon hearing this, Haruko took a towel from Morris's bosom, covered his eyes with it, tied it tightly around his head, and, knowing Morris's life was safe, ran off with all her might to search for the Hime.

Akiyama-wake, with his legs trembling, finally arrived at Morris's side.

Akiyama: 'Ah, are you not Morris-dono? You seem to have suffered a great injury. What is that hachimaki you are wearing?'

Morris: 'Is this not the hachimaki that you tied for me? My life was already in peril, and I cannot thank you enough for saving me. A colleague is indeed a person to rely upon. Thanks to you, the pain in my leg has also been greatly reduced.'

Akiyama: 'By no means; I did not save you. I merely arrived here with great difficulty. Judging by the situation, you must have been rescued by someone else.'

He removes the hachimaki as he says this.

Morris: 'I thought your hands felt soft. In that case, was it not you who saved me? In any event, it is a relief to have escaped death.'

Akiyama: 'Now that the day has dawned, there is no way to conduct a search. For a Tairau to be wandering about without any attendants would only invite suspicion; shall we not devise a zengosaku?'

Morris: 'That is so. In the line of duty, I cannot simply abandon this, but even if I say that, with the sun shining like this, we cannot search for the Hime. At any rate, let us return to my house quietly via the nukemichi. Akiyamawake-dono, unlike me, you must be filled with deep emotion. Since it is as if you have let the jewel in the palm of your son, Kikuhiko-dono, escape, I can well imagine your grief. Is it not best that we two resolve from this point on not to enter the castle? We are both old, and we are treated like antiques or ornaments on a shelf, handled like machines; is there any point in struggling on? If we two were to resign, the political policy might worsen, but at any rate, the public sentiment might change completely, which might actually be for the good of the country. What do you think, Akiyama-dono?'

Akiyama: 'Having failed not once, but twice, how can I, as a Tairau, face the Kokushi? Furthermore, I have no excuse to offer the people. As you say, I shall return to my own mansion, submit my resignation, and clarify my responsibility. If I were to cut my wrinkled belly in seppuku, it would be painful, and there is no reason to lose a precious life; no matter how important the position, it cannot replace one's life. Hahaha!'

Morris: 'I am satisfied with your prompt agreement, Akiyama-dono. However, with my leg in such pain, I cannot do anything. If we go back about one chō, there is valley water flowing there. I shall drink some of that water, catch my breath, and slowly return to my mansion.'

Akiyama: 'As I grow old, the mockery of others grows frequent / The agony of this day, when my legs and waist cannot stand.'

Morris: 'Even if the body grows old, the spirit / Remains beautiful, youthful, and flourishing.'

Akiyama: 'What are you saying, when your legs and waist cannot even stand? / Drink some clear, cold water and catch your breath.'

Morris: 'That is true, but no matter how energetically I speak / I cannot fight against the slope of my years.'

Akiyama: 'Even if I become bent like a shrimp and jump about with vigor / If no one buys me, I am but goods left unsold in the shop. / Once again, we have been escaped by the Hime of clear fragrance / And here we two are, foaming at the mouth.'

Humming this, they finally turned back about one chō and arrived at the side of a pool of clear water flowing from the mountain stream.

Morris: 'Clear water that moistens the spirit of my old body. / This clear water must be saving a person's life.'

Akiyama: 'I too shall cup the clear water on this summer morning. / Clear water that becomes sweat and becomes strength. / Clear water that even stretches out the wrinkles of an old man. / Beyond this, I shall return and find nothing but the rock-spring water.'

Morris: 'Escaped by the cold-hearted Hime, I drink the clear water. / Harukohime saves me and runs away.'

Akiyama: 'Come, let us return home quickly, the two of us together.'

Humming this, the two old men, using their staffs for support, walked away feebly and slowly from the nukemichi of the castle's horse-riding ground.

モ『身体はよし老ゆる共精霊は いと美はしく若やぎ栄ゆ』

秋山『脛腰も立たぬ身乍ら何を云ふ 清麗の水でも呑んで息せよ』

モ『そらさうだ何程元気に云ふたとて 争はれない年の阪路』

秋山『海老腰に、なつてピンピンはねたとて 買うてくれねば店曝しかな。 又しても清香の姫に逃げられて 二人はここに泡を吹くかな』

かく口ずさみ乍ら、漸くにして一町許り引返し、谷川から流れてくる清水の溜の側へと着いた。

モ『老の身の霊うるほす清水かな。 此清水人の命を救ふらむ』

秋山『われも亦清水むすばむ夏の朝。 汗となり力ともなる清水かな。 年寄りの皺迄伸びる清水かな。 此の上は帰りて何も岩清水』

モ『水臭い姫に逃げられ清水呑む。 春子姫吾を救ふて逃げて行く』

秋山『サア早く家に帰らむ二人連れ』

かく口ずさみ乍ら両老は杖を力に城の馬場の間道から、力なげにトボトボと帰つて行く。
