Chapter 5: 性 Bright [1750]

性明〔1750〕

§ 1

At the edge of the town in the Capital of Chin, at the Kurumachiyōba in Fukamizochiyō, three Hikiko named Kuni, Ai, and Asa were raising a Daikien when a pale and emaciated Kautsūtorishimari-Sensei, having overheard the three talking, walked in with an indifferent air. He was rolling his unpleasant eyes. The three felt a slight Dōeu in their Kiyōbu, fearing that their recent conversation had been overheard by this Torishimari, but they sat up straight with proper Giyōgi and feigned ignorance.

Asa: 'Hey, Danna, what is your business? Is it an inspection of the Shiyatai? This is a healthy Shiyatai that we just had checked at the Oyakushiyo a few days ago, so there is no fear of us Metsuta overturning a customer on a Doromichi. Please go home quickly. If you stay in the shop for long, customers will be afraid, thinking that perhaps we are playing Bakuchi or receiving another Kogoto, and they will not come near. If that happens, our livelihood will dry up in an instant. We are already heavily taxed, ordered to repair our Shiyatai, and on top of that, having our business obstructed is more than a Hikiko can bear.'

Torishimari: 'No, this is not a Shiyatai inspection or anything of the sort; I am a Kautsūtorishimari. I heard that political discussions were becoming quite popular, and I came in because I wanted to hear one myself. You have raised quite a grand Daikien. I am a dropout from Inada University, so I am in the same boat as you. First, please set your mind at ease, open your heart, and let me hear your aspirations, ahn.'

Asa: 'Hmph, the Torishimari of today are all the same; they say things to please the masses, get them to spill their guts, and then secretly report it to their superiors, thinking only of receiving a reward, so I cannot easily talk about politics. No matter how much of a Torishimari you are, you eat thanks to our sweat and toil. If anything, we are your Danna, so please do not speak so arrogantly. Even as I am, if the universal suffrage is implemented, I will be a representative of the world.'

Torishimari: 'Ha-ha-ha, you are starting to show your true colors. Ah, interesting, I agree. If you run for office, please buy my vote. I have the right to one vote, after all.'

Asa: 'Hmph, what is your one vote worth? If I am to pay money to get people to vote for me, I will have someone more respectable vote for me, hmph, excuse me.'

Torishimari: 'Whether it is a great elder, a noble family, a millionaire, me, or you, the right is still one vote. The vote of a street-food eater and our votes have the same effect. Do not look down on us so much.'

Asa: 'Hmm, is that so? If that is the case, then when universal suffrage is implemented, we and the street-food eaters will be equals. Good, that is excellent. In that case, I shall win the workers to my side and give it a try. I intend to organize a major political party, become its leader, take the cabinet, and carry out great governance for the sake of the masses, it-hit-hi. When that time comes, I will not even let you remain a mere Kautsūtorishimari. I will promote you to a high-ranking inspector, so count on it.'

Torishimari: 'Ah-ha-ha-ha, interesting, interesting... By the way, are you not Kuni-sama and Ai-sama? Why are you doing such a job in such a place? It is no use hiding it anymore, so I want you to tell me everything.'

Kuni: 'I am not Kuni-sama or anything of the sort; I am Kuro-sama. That is why I am working as a clerk for the lower class and suffering. Would you mind not mistaking me for someone else? Rather than that, why do you not go stand at the crossroads quickly? If there is another collision between a cart and a child's vehicle, you will be branded as male or female in an instant, and you will end up in a situation where you must be thrown into a place full of smoke.'

Kunichikawake: 'In truth, I am not an ordinary human either. However, I must ask you to wait for me to reveal my real name. I never know when I might be betrayed. But you may rest assured, for this Kunichikawake-sama is neither a member of a deceptive society that helps the strong and crushes the weak, nor a petty employee filled with suspicion and jealousy.'

Torishimari: 'Ya, I understand. Even without hearing your name, I have already grasped what kind of Tanuki or Kitsune you are, judging by your appearance and your words. If you do not wish to name yourself, you need not. Well, in any case, let us open our hearts to each other and cooperate for the sake of the present time.'

Kunichikawake: 'In truth, it is fine to say "the present time" over and over like a chicken, but it would be more appropriate for the era to say it is for the sake of love for humanity.'

Torishimari: 'The truth is, I am a person called Ikukō, a retainer of Matsuwakahiko, but it is not very clever to be a lantern-bearer for the Horse-Passing Tribe forever; I had been thinking that I must wash my hands of it and awaken to the times. Well then, Kunichikawake-sama, I do not know what noble lineage you are from, but for now, let us associate as Kunichikawake and Aishiu. When you become a great elder, I shall use honorifics again, wa-ha-ha-ha!'

Kunichikawake: 'Yosh, I like it. Then Ikukō, come, let us shake hands.'

Ikukō: 'I will shake hands as much as you like. After all, it requires no capital.'

Kunichikawake: 'Secondly, do not say such stingy things about capital or whatever. Whenever I hear words like capital or wealthy families, my ears ache, I feel sick, and I feel as if a disturbance is breaking out in my stomach. The capitalists of today are, so to speak, "four-legged" creatures.'

Aishiu: 'Just as Ikukō was able to sniff out our true identities, now that even the cold dogs have begun to have a keen sense of smell, the rickshaw pullers are no longer of any use. Surely others will come sniffing around, so how about this: if we become chivalrous outlaws from now on, it might be convenient for our future plans.'

Kunichikawake: 'Sora, that is interesting. But who will be the boss?'

Kuniteru: 'Geimushiyo is a sanatorium for baidoku. Furthermore, since fellows who cannot be managed by hitosujinaha or futasujinaha are manipulating those with long noses using misuji strings, importing such shiromono would be a great meiwaku to Haisuikuwai.'

While indulging in such zatsudan, the yonin finished off a sho of sake by drinking it ratsupanomi.

Just as this was happening, the fierce ringing of a shinbunhaitatsu's bell echoed sharply, chirin chirin.

Kuniteru: 'Hey Asa, go buy a gauguwai; a totsupatsu henji must have surely occurred.'

Asa, genka, tucked up his skirts and, chasing after the gauguwaiya while shouting 'Oi! Oi!' like Kumagai, ran after him. Behind them, the sannin waited impatiently for Asa to return.

Kuniteru: 'It is neither a jishin nor likely a seihen, but I wonder what that gauguwai is about. Somehow we cannot help feeling kigaka, wā.'

Aishū: 'That is true, koitsu is no futsū matter. Could a major problem concerning our itsushinjiyō have arisen? Hey Ikushu, you must have eavesdropped on our conversation and blabbed something to some shinbun kisha fellow or another.'

Ikushu: 'What, I did not blab anything. A corrupt shinbun kisha was standing under the eaves here letting his pen fly, so thinking koitsu strange I drew near to look, whereupon the kisha fellow wore a strange expression and hid his figure somewhere. Since then, I only heard a little bit of what followed. Because we were talking in too loud a voice, he might have put out a gauguwai with a headline like "Kuniteruwake Lurks in the Office." If that is the case, it is terrible; the Ōmetsuke fellow might be startled, order a crackdown on his subordinates, and come to arrest us. Come, let us hide our bodies; getting caught just as we begin our lives as kyōkaku would be a bad omen...'

The faces of sannin changed color in a flash. The one who came running back as if tumbling into the place was Asakō. Asakō shouted from the doorway,

Asakō: 'Oi, ta-ta-terrible! Ku-ku-Kuniteruwake's se-se-son, Kuniteruwake, is lurking in the office here, so the authorities' hands have spread all across the capital—it is written in the go-go-gauguwai, wā. Ku-ku-If Kuniteruwake is caught, o-o-I am a kobun too, so I am an accomplice. Come, let us somehow flee from this place, shall we not?'

Kuniteruwake, remaining completely calm:

Kuniteruwake: 'Ha-ha-ha, interesting, interesting; with this, indeed, our prayers are fulfilled and the time has arrived. Hey Aishū, Ikukō, Asa, follow me. Shall we move on to our second plan?'

So saying, leisurely through the back door, the four members took advantage of the dusk's darkness and concealed their figures somewhere, nobody knew where.
