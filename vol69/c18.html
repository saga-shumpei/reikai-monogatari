Chapter 18: 老狽 [1763]

老狽〔1763〕

§ 1

Kiyokahime: 'Since the ancient times of the majestic Kamiyo, When Hinodenokami, the divine pillar, Descended from the mountain of the heavenly teachings, He guided my Oyagami And opened the Ananahi teachings To purify this world. Since then, the divine Miizu Has spread without omission or leak To the four quarters of the lands, To every cape of the islands and every nook of the shores. Beneath the heavens, there is no obliquity; The Aohitogusa bind together in harmony From the very depth of their Murakimo hearts, Mirroring the true form of the Amatsumikuni and Tengoku In this land of Hiru. Revered as the descendants of Inca, Our ancestors built a wondrous Miyai Upon the Sokotsune rock, firm and everlasting as Kakihatokiha; And like sturdy divine pillars, They flourished exceedingly. There is no one among the people who does not look up to them. Yet in recent reigns, evil teachings have spread from Tokoyokuni, Clouding the heavens and defiling the earth, Turning the green mountains into Karayama, And causing the hearts of the world’s people to become utterly desolate. The divine land as it was of old Is now about to become a realm of demons. Raising my head from my pillow in the deep of night, As I peer into the future of this world, Divine revelation declares that in the capital of Hiru Lies the dwelling of hideous ogres; The eight-headed serpent, wolves, Tigers, lions, and fierce bears Conceal their claws and lie in wait. As I listen to this oracle of the Lord God, My chest aches and my heart is thrown into turmoil. Ah, why was I born as the child of the governor? Had I been raised in the countryside, I would not have known such anguish. Wrapped in this abominable royal robe of the Seike house, I suffer in utter immobility. Pity me, O Kami of heaven and earth! Keeping the words I pledged to my brother, I depart from the castle of Hiru. Stealing away into the night, I tread step by step along the mountain path. May Thou protect me peacefully, Leaving no shadow upon the path ahead! O clan deity who guardeth this castle of Mount Takakura, O Kakuritama-no-Kami who holdeth dominion Over the realm of Hiru eternally, Before Thy divine presence, I play the eight-fold harp of clouds, My heart refreshed as I pluck the strings; With two strands of thread and a single, sincere heart, I pray solely unto Thee. Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara! Grant unto me the grace of Thy divine spirit.'

Just as she was singing thus, the pitch-black night descended. Kiyokahime secretly prepared her personal belongings and waited for the hour of the Rat to arrive.

The lights inside the castle were extinguished, and an air of quiet solitude drifted over the surroundings; only the sound of rats running across the ceiling could be heard faintly—shito, shito, shito. Thinking the time was right, Kiyokahime was about to slip secretly out of her living room, when her handmaid, Harukohime, approached with stealthy footsteps.

Harukohime: 'Princess, are you not yet asleep?'

Surprised by this voice, Kiyokahime feigned innocence and said:

Kiyokahime: 'Ah, is that you, Harukohime? Are you still unable to sleep?'

Harukohime: 'Yes. For some reason, tonight of all nights my eyes are wide awake, and being worried about your well-being, Princess, I simply cannot sleep.'

Kiyokahime: 'Are you unable to sleep as well? I too cannot sleep in the least.'

Harukohime: 'Princess, shall we compose poetry together to pass the night until dawn?'

Though troubled, Kiyokahime replied:

Kiyokahime: 'I am sure I will be able to fall asleep soon, but let us compose a verse or two before we part.'

Harukohime: 'Yes, thank you very much.'

So saying, Harukohime took a seat close beside the princess.

Harukohime: 'Standing at the front of Takakura, the iron gatekeeper— His bloodshot eyes cannot see. The back gate where the full moon of the fifteenth night peeks through Is exceedingly quiet, without even a breeze.'

For the first time, Kiyokahime realized that Harukohime had deduced her escape tonight, and was grateful, thinking that she had instructed her to flee through the back gate.

Kiyokahime: 'Illuminated by the moonlight of departing spring, The first plum blossoms fragrance so purely. None know that deep within the valley, the white plum Burns in a figure so majestic.'

Thus exchanging poems with one another, Kiyokahime said:

Kiyokahime: 'I shall go stroll for just a little while in the moonlit garden, so Harukohime, please play this koto and wait for me.'

Saying so, she slipped away toward the back door. At the back door, straw raincoats and hats, arm-guards and leg-warmers, a walking stick, and all other necessities for travel were neatly prepared. Harukohime, tears brimming in her eyes, said:

Harukohime: 'Princess, I will never let you travel alone; please be at ease.'

§ 2

When she spoke thus in a low voice, Kiyokahime turned around,

Kiyokahime: 'Wherever I go, I am together with Kamisama; do not fret your heart so.'

Leaving these words behind, thinking it would be a terrible matter if she were discovered, she went out to the Uraguchi, swiftly prepared her attire, slipped quietly out from the Uramon, passed beneath the Kodachi of the Banba, and hurried southwards and further south. Afterwards, Harukohime took up a Nigenkin, locked the sliding door that separated the rooms, and played the harp while singing.

'This place is the nightless land of Hiru, The central land of the capital of Hiru. The Miizu of Kami is also of Takakurayama, The rare castle built upon the Ihane. Since the ancient times of Hinodenokami, The rare castle where they have reverentially worshipped The Ōkami of Ananai-kyō. Be that as it may, as the stars shift And as the Tsukihi flow onward, The hearts of people gradually Drift away toward improper places, And the world falls into confusion like Karigomo. To cleanse this truly wretched Tenchi, The Sumekami deeply comprehended in His Mikokoro, And the brave Kashimadachi of the Wakagimi and the Hime; When I think of it, when I think of it, my tears Flow as deep pools and rapids without ceasing. So long as Kami exists in this world, The Wakagimi-sama and the Hime-gimi Shall establish great merit, And before long as the pillars of the gods of Hiru, They shall be looked up to as lords of salvation; Not to speak of this realm, Subjugating every corner of Takasagoshima All unto that virtue, The prosperity of Inca changing from of old; With the pine trees also auspicious, of Takasago, The long-lasting reign governed by the old man and the old woman Is eagerly awaited. Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara, By the grace of the Sumeōkami, The whereabouts of Hime-gimi-sama, May You send her peacefully and safely to the land of Mezurashi, To the sacred land where her elder brother Mikoto abides; Harukohime, who serves close by her side, Offers her true heart and prays.'

Akiyamawake and Morris had returned to their own homes, but because their hearts were inexplicably uneasy, wondering if some accident had befallen Hime, the two came without prior agreement through the front gate after the hour of the Rat had passed, entered their respective offices, and strove to perform their duties of surveillance. From the living quarters of Hime, the fluent sound of the harp could be heard. Akiyamawake and Morris felt relieved for the time being upon hearing the sound of the harp, and the two began to converse cheerfully in loud voices.

Akiyama: 'Morris-dono, for a person of your advanced age to be out in the dead of night, you have taken immense trouble. Has some urgent business arisen?'

Morris: 'There is no particular urgent business, but my heart was inexplicably uneasy, thinking that perhaps a sudden emergency had occurred concerning Hime-sama within the castle grounds; unable to wait even to grab my belongings, in the middle of the night, without bringing any attendants, I slipped out quietly and came. And as for your good self coming to the castle in the darkness of night, did you also feel something prompting you?'

Akiyama: 'We also, just like your thoughts, felt our hearts inexplicably uneasy, and being unable to help worrying whether any untoward event had occurred to Hime, we came hither. However, when we approached near Hime's living quarters to probe the situation, there was the extremely fluent tone of the harp; feeling relieved, we returned here and are now resting. It seems Hime-sama has taken a liking to the matter; unable to wait for tomorrow to come, she plays the harp without sleeping a wink, which is something that has never happened before. Is this not a truly joyful and auspicious sign?'

Morris: 'Indeed, just as you say, we also feel as though we have become young again. It makes one feel like becoming a young person of old times once more. Ha-ha-ha-ha!'

Akiyama: 'By the way, Morris-dono, which number do you suppose Hime-sama desires?'

Morris: 'According to that poem, numbers one, two, three, and four will probably not do; she will most likely adopt number five. Akiyamawake-dono, congratulations. Is that not your son?'

Akiyama: 'Indeed, my lad Akihiko is a fortunate fellow as well. You and I underwent great hardship regarding the maiden Benaihime-sama of this castle, and in the end, that result occurred; though we say it was the extremity of youthful folly, we suffered a great embarrassment. But for my lad to surpass his father and meet Hime-sama's favor, how the world has changed! Oh-ho-ho-ho!'

She is inside the Etsubo.

Meanwhile, Harukohime thought, 'By now, the Hime-sama must have escaped; surely no pursuers will come. Come, I must follow her and protect the Hime-sama's person.' On the single path of the Terukunikaidau, at the hour of the tiger just before dawn, she could not afford to linger. Tightening her ashishiyōzoku, she fled from the uramon in a mad dash.

The barking of the Western dogs within the castle echoed incessantly, 'Wau, wau, wau.' Akiyamawake and Morris pricked up their ears at this sound.

Akiyama: 'The dogs are barking in a way they never do; this cannot be a simple matter. Above all, I am anxious about the Hime-sama's safety.'

Saying this, he rushed to the front of the Hime-sama's ima, but the sound of the koto had stopped completely.

Akiyama: 'Hime-sama, pardon me.'

Saying this, he slid open the sliding door with a clatter and peered inside, but to his surprise, the master of the koto had vanished like a cicada's shell. Thinking she might be in the benjiyo, he searched all around, but there was no sign of the Hime-sama. Thinking to wake Haruko and ask her, he went to Haruko's ima, but that too was a cicada's shell.

Akiyama: 'This is a disaster. However, I cannot report such a blunder to the Kokushi and his wife. If it is said that we let the young lord escape before, and now we have let the princess escape, there will be no way for us two but to cut our bellies to apologize. Fortunately, no one knows yet. Morris-dono, shall you and I search for them quietly?'

Morris: 'Akiyamawake-dono, indeed that is so. Since it is our great responsibility, they cannot have gone too far before the people in the castle find out; let us search. The omotemon will attract attention; let us start from the uramon.'

They hurried toward the uramon. The door of the uramon was left wide open, and a woman's hanhaba had fallen there. Morris quickly picked up the hanhaba and, holding it up to the moonlight before dawn, saw the mark of Haruko upon it. 'Without a doubt, Haruko must have conspired with the Hime-sama and absconded,' he said, and the two went down the stairs outside the uramon, clattering as they went, stumbling and falling with the help of their staves, and chased toward the thicket of trees in the baba.
