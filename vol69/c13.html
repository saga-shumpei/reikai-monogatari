Chapter 13: 国別 [1758]

国別〔1758〕

§ 1

Kuniteruwake: 'I am like the lonely winter moon, / Shaking high in the Misora, / In the sorrow of having no way to open / The cloud-doors that block the Chiukū. / Even when I suffer and writhe, / The Tokitsukaze suddenly blows, / Sweeping away the clouds that wrap / In ten layers and twenty layers, / Finally opening the path to descend to the Chijiyō. / While borrowing a cold lodging / Where frost settles on the Kakiha of the grass, / My own person, descending and leaving the capital behind, / Is truly reliable. / When I look out far over the Chijiyō, / In the Arenohara where tigers, wolves, lions, and bears / Rage and run wild, / Righteous people are trampled and stepped upon / By the feet of the Shikogami, / Raising a Himei and crying out. / The crooked people, Yōyō, / Like butterflies dancing in the Haruno, / Leaving the troubles of the Chijiyō to Yoso, / Are immersed in Kabu and Ongaku. / Truly, it is a Tenchi of Mujiyun. / As the Kami finally appear, / Bringing the Sanzensekai into alignment, / Down to the Kakiha of the grass, / Moistening them with the dew of blessing, / The time to save them has drawn near. / Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara. / I am Kuniteruwake-tsukasa. / This clouded Kunitsuchi, / With the Mioshihe of the Ananahi moon, / I shall illuminate and purify, and for Tokoshihe, / Make it the reign of Kuniteruwake. / Harunohime, who also blesses the plants and trees, / The brother and sister of moon and flower, / The precious land granted by the Kami, / I shall pull it back to the ancient age of the Kami, / And the people who suffer in sorrow, / I shall save and help, that is my vow. / For the sake of achieving this, I set out on my Kashimadachi. / Please watch over me, Kamunagara. / I pray before the presence of the Kami. / Even if the blowing wind is rough, / Even if the falling rain is strong, / Even if the earth shakes and the thunder / Roars above my head in this world, / How could I ever fear? The holy soul of Kuniteruwake / Of Shikishima. / No authority or worldly desire / Has the power to sway it. / It goes without saying for the precious land, / And for the land called Takasago-jima, / I shall twist back all the affairs of the land / To the teaching of the Ananahi Kami, / And as a living, true Kami, / To descend is truly courageous. / Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara. / I pray for the divine favor of the spirit.'

While singing this, he arrived at the summit of the pass of Mount Arina. Looking down far over the wilderness to the east, Kuniteruwake said, 'Ah, I will not be able to see this precious land for a while. In return, when I return next time, this vast Arenohara will surely transform into a paradise on earth, decorated with the seven treasures such as gold, silver, agate, lapis lazuli, giant clam, and crystal. Leaving behind the castle with its deep clouds, abandoning my parents, siblings, and retainers, descending to the countryside, and now unable to live even in my own castle town—though I say it is of my own will, leaving my birthplace makes me feel lonely in a way I cannot describe. Ah, no, no, if I am so weak-hearted, how can this divine work be accomplished? The ruler of the precious land had the heavenly duty of enlightening the people with the teaching of Ananahi. It was governed naturally without needing to pay too much mind to politics and the like. However, as it is today, various isms, ideologies, and useless academic studies have flowed in from abroad, and it has become impossible to govern the country with only the simple faith of the past. Nevertheless, the world can never be governed by the power of knowledge or learning. First and foremost, there is no other way to govern than by virtue. In order to cultivate that virtue, I slipped out of the castle, joined the ranks of the lowliest rickshaw pullers, explored the circumstances of the lower society, and now, becoming a chivalrous man, I appear and disappear in the streets of the town, trying to make my soul a diamond-like, indestructible wish-fulfilling jewel. Yet, no matter how I fret and struggle, what can I do? Raised in the castle as a young master, with a weak body that is troubled even by a slight rough wind, how can I bring peace to the sentient beings? No matter what is said, I am the heir to the precious land. I do not desire a life of luxury or a prominent position in the least, but if I am no longer in this country from now on, it is the same as losing the center of faith, the target of respect, and the true pillar of thought; it would be difficult for any wise man or person of virtue to govern it easily. Thinking of that, I must polish my soul as quickly as possible, receive the true divine virtue, and return to this country again. As the vast wilderness of the precious land leaves my sight now, I have somehow come to yearn for the mountains, rivers, plants, trees, and the people of my country. However, I cannot overturn my soul once I have made up my mind. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara. Lord Kunitokotachi-ōkami, please accept the sincere heart of Kuniteruwake, and not to mention the precious land, please twist back the Tenchi of Takasago-jima into the paradise of the ancient age of the Kami. Also, I humbly pray that you will bestow special divine grace upon my parents, my younger sister Harunohime, the other old retainers in the castle, and my friends, and lead the nation of the precious land to peace and prosperity. As I face parting from the precious land, I offer my humble thanks before the presence of the Kunimitama-kami. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara.'

§ 2

With an expression of deep emotion, he heaves a heavy sigh. Looking down upon the wilderness of Chin, Asakō says,

Asakō: 'Oyabun-sama, how vast the land of Chin is, and somehow, the mountains, rivers, grass, and trees of the land of Chin... they make me feel as if they are beckoning me, saying, "Asakō, do not go, do not go, return to the beginning." This feeling makes the journey ahead seem burdensome, and I feel a strange lack of joy. Looking at your expression now, Oyakata, tears are falling one by one from your eyes. It seems that no matter how much of a chivalrous hero one may be, human sentiment remains the same.'

Kuniteru: 'Hmm, one cannot help but feel a longing for the land of one's birth. It is, so to speak, the true mother who has raised us for so long. Since an Osanago leaves the bosom of its mother and sets out into the skies of a foreign land, it is only natural that even I should be overcome with tears of deep emotion. A human without tears is a demon. It seems I have at least managed to avoid the fate of a demon. Hahaha!'

He suddenly tries to mask his feelings with laughter. Asakō also joins in the laughter, his voice mixed with sobs, 'Ahahaha!'

Kuniteru: 'Asakō, from here on, it is, in short, a foreign land. From the perspective of the Kamisama, it is all the land of Kami, with no boundaries or discrimination. However, because the humans on the surface have arbitrarily set boundaries—saying this is the land of Chin, that is the land of Teru, or the land of Karu, or Hiru, or Haru—and are competing for power, if you do not keep that in mind, you will make a terrible mistake. Within one's own country, a chivalrous hero may hold sway, but in a foreign land where one does not know the circumstances, things will not go that way.'

Asakō: 'They do say that a dog that does not bark is not a dog, after all.'

Kuniteru: 'Oi, Asakō, is it not cruel to compare me to a dog? Haha. Come, let us descend from here; I hear there is a place called the Kakehashi Palace. We shall pay our respects there, borrow lodging for the night, and proceed at our leisure.'

Asakō: 'Hai, I shall accompany you. Ah, I wonder if this is the last time I shall see the sky of my homeland...'

Asakō: 'Unable to leave, I look back at the land of Chin, / Though I am one without wife or child. / Somehow, I have grown to love the sky of Chin, / And as I part now, tears begin to fall.'

Kuniteru-wake: 'Thou too art a child of man, and knowest the pathos of the world, / Well hast thou realized it, deeply hast thou awakened. / The sky of Chin, where thy dear parents dwell, / I gaze up at it as I depart. / My dear friend of the land, how shall thou, / Act in the days after I have gone? / My friend, wait a while, Kuniteru-wake, / Until I appear as Kami and return. / I do not go to abandon the Imperial Land, / But to make it the true land of Kami. / I do not go to cause my parents suffering, / But to comfort the Great Divine Heart. / I do not go to abandon the people, / But to save them in the Heavenly Pure Land.'

Having finished singing, he uses his Kongo-zue as a support and descends the steep slope.

Kuniteru-wake: 'The Arina Mountain of Kami's grace, / I descend, leaning on my staff. / Whether the morning sun shines or clouds gather, / Whether the moon waxes or wanes, / Even if the great earth should sink, / My soul, entrusted to the one true / Three-Five Kami, / May there be fortune for this body of mine / That devotes its sincerity to Kami and the country, / I pray morning and evening with courage. / Leaving the sky of my homeland behind, / I advance, climbing and descending / The mountain paths I have never trod. / Ryūzehime of the Land-Spirit Kami, / Please protect me, Kannagara. / I humbly revere and pray to Thee. / O Great Kami who governs the land, / Who created this world, / And who teaches and leads the people of the world, / O Great Kami of the Mizu-no-Mitama.',

Kuniteru-wake: 'Sweeping away the dust of this world, / Thou protectest us as the wind of Shinato, / Or as rain, or as snow. / Relying on Thy noble power, / I leave behind my irreplaceable / Parents and beloved, / Who have no equal in heaven or on earth. / The rain of tears falls upon my sleeves, / And my eyes are blurred by the sky of this day. / Grant me Thy grace, Kannagara, / I pray to Kami with all my heart.'

Singing thus, Kuniteru-wake takes the lead, and Asakō follows behind, taking steps in rhythm while leaning on his staff along the stone-strewn road of ninety-nine curves.

Singing this mountain-descending song in broken fragments, they finally reached a slightly flat, gentle slope. The fog grew deeper and deeper, making it impossible to distinguish even a few feet ahead; the sun seemed to have hidden behind the western sky, and the curtain of darkness began to wrap around the two, bit by bit. The two had no choice but to spend the night here. Ah, Kamunagara Izuchi-no-Mitama-no-Sachi-Hae-Mase.
