Chapter 2: 老断 [1747]

老断〔1747〕

§ 1

Matsuwakahiko and Isahiko, the Tairau and Raujiukabu, commanded a great number of Metsuke to capture the radical Raujiu Ihaharuwake, but he had somehow caught wind of it, slipped out of the Jiyōnai, and vanished; their Shinpai grew ever deeper, and as they let out sighs of Hanmonkunau, the two sat once again at a Tēburu in the Hiyōjiyōshiyo, sipping coffee while discussing a Zengosaku.

Matsuwakahiko said in a voice of Hitsū, 'Isahiko-dono, has the Kokuka not reached a Kiki truly akin to a Tomoshibi before a Baufū? Thinking him a man of slightly advanced Shinpo, I recommended that Ihaharuwake as a Raujiu and sought to have him participate in the Sūki of Kokumu, but when I invited him and probed his opinions under the pretext of my own retirement, I found that Ihaharuwake, a traitor to the Kokuka whom heaven and earth cannot tolerate, harbors great ambitions; how might we place this nation, now in such a state, in the security of Mount Tai?'

So saying, he was already shedding tears of blood that dripped profusely from both eyes. Isahiko, while heaving a deep Toiki, said, 'Indeed, as your Excellency says, it is truly unbearable to contemplate. However, now that it has come to this, you and I must exert every effort to save the Kokuka from collapse, console the heart of the Kokushi, and open a path for the peace of the people. Yet, since that Ihaharuwake, agile as he is, foresaw that his crimes would reach him and has hidden himself in the shadows like a dove or a rat, he is surely lurking at the ends of some land, gathering the Sanpeisha, laborers, and Taimeisha to plot the overthrow of the Kokuka, and it is to be expected that he awaits his time to achieve his desires and return with renewed strength; we must study some method of prevention... or rather, a means to annihilate him. To leave such a traitor, whom heaven and earth cannot tolerate, at large in the country is more dangerous than releasing a fierce tiger into the fields. I trust that your Excellency surely has some brilliant plan or strategy...'

He peered at Matsuwakahiko's face through his spectacles with a look of Shinpai, stroked his shiny head two or three times with his right hand, and wiped the bottom of the kettle with a handkerchief.

Matsuwakahiko: 'It is truly a troublesome matter. Now that it has come to this, mild measures will likely be of no use. We must proclaim a security ordinance throughout this castle town, increase the number of Metsuke and spies, and arrest anyone suspicious in every corner of the slums without allowing them to say a word; unless we show the authority of the Tairau, it is unlikely that we can ever hope to win over the hearts of the people. In a society like the present, where the corruption of the human heart has reached its peak, decoctions and liquid medicines will no longer suffice. There is likely no other way but to perform a surgical operation, exterminate these ugly creatures from the roots, and prevent a national crisis before it happens. Fortunately, we hold the power of the Metsuke in our hands, and we possess the special privilege of commanding the Daimyo and samurai in times of emergency; in our current position, neither conciliation nor benevolent government will work anymore.'

Isahiko: 'Indeed, what you say is most reasonable, but... I think we should first grant the masses the right to petition for what they desire, and advocate for policies that appeal to the popularity of the masses, such as a Tokuseian, thereby preventing the volcanic crater that is about to erupt; by dragging things out for a long time, we can weary the people whose spirits have risen, drain their bones, squeeze their blood, and exhaust their energy, and only then, by showing absolute and infinite authority, even the most intense movements of the masses, the throwing-spear ideology, and other evil thoughts will have no way to raise their heads and will perish of their own accord. What is your Excellency's opinion on this?'

Matsuwakahiko: 'I am well aware that it is a sign of my own incompetence to recklessly mobilize the pillars of the Kokuka or to suppress the masses with Metsuke and spies, but in today's situation, where we are pressed by the urgency of a burning head, I believe there is no other way. Luring the hearts of the people with the bait of a petition plan is one strategy, and a Tokuseian will likely serve as a temporary sedative. Today, one can no longer succeed by being honest. Politicians like so-and-so were too honest, and because of that, their inner armor was always seen through, they repeated failures, and in the end, did they not bring about the split of their party? In extraordinary times, extraordinary measures are necessary. Isahiko-dono, what do you think?'

§ 2

Isa: 'Naruhodo, for today's jikiyoku, to say things like seirenketsupaku or shiyōjiki is harmful and yields no benefit. Just as you say, dealing with gendai through kenbōjiyutsusū or dakefuseiji is perhaps the most kenmei yarikata.'

As his voice gradually rose, the riyōnin clenched their fists, pounded on the table, and scattered the hanabira of the yamabuki flowers stuck in the kuwabin all over the surface. Entering lightly dressed, Kuniteruwake appeared once again,

Kuniteru: 'Ha-ha-ha, both of you are worrying your minds for kokuka, and Kuniteruwayake finds himself at a loss as to where to place his kiyōku. No matter what is said, it is a gathering of the daiichiriu daiseijika kiyotō of the country; divine and miraculous plans have surely been churned out.'

He began to tease them. Unable to get angry at being made fun of by the wakagimi, and whispering a secret 'Tch' while clicking their tongues, they deliberately left their chairs with a solemn attitude, stood straight with both hands hanging vertically down to about below their sashes, and while offering a standing bow,

Matsurawakasa: 'Yoshikohime-dono, how well you have come in. Since we also have secret consultations regarding state affairs, please return to the inner chambers for a while, if you please.'

Isa: 'As the great elder says, since this is a case requiring the most urgent consultation regarding state affairs at this very moment, we humbly ask you to withdraw for a short while.'

Kuniteru: 'Ha-ha-ha, since Iwachidewake's dart-throwing old minister has vanished, a harmonious and thorough deliberation will surely be forged. Ah, I consider this to be of the utmost celebration for the kokuka. However, there is something I would like to ask the two elders.'

Matsurawakasa: 'High, I am honored. Please ask me about anything whatsoever.'

Kuniteru: 'If one listens just now in the corridor, didn't you say that so-and-so was too honest, and therefore fostered internal strife within the party and failed? To say one is too honest—well, what on earth does that mean? In short, honesty is fine, but you mean to say that one must practice a little deceit as well, and that today's political situation cannot be maintained without employing kenbōjiyutsusū. If I failed because I was too honest, like so-and-so, wouldn't that be fulfilling one's true aspiration? Where is the need to seize political power by deceiving people generally, high and low? I want you to let me hear the meaning of being too honest.'

Thus cornered, the two were at a loss for a reply, flushed their faces, and with a simple 'High', kept their heads bowed down.

Kuniteru: 'Ha-ha-ha, surely you cannot make a reply. Because it is a world where politicians who are too honest are not employed, your influence is likely thriving. And there is one more thing I want to ask... Didn't you say just now that since you were discussing the essential affairs of kokuka, I should withdraw to the inner rooms? Why is it that I cannot participate in the pivotal machinery of politics? Is it because you look down on me as a youth, or do you perhaps not trust me? While respecting me as wakagimi-wakagimi on the surface, do you already fail to acknowledge me in your hearts? Come, let me hear that reply.'

Struck by this second arrow, the two could not utter a single 'goo', and stood trembling with bowed heads.

Kuniteru: 'Atsu-ha-ha-ha, hey, you two, isn't the kettle leaking? It's pathetic, you know. Rather than that, both of you should abandon your senior official posts for the sake of kokuka, step down into the alleys of the city, and run a udon shop or something. That might prove to be far more beneficial for the kokuka. As Iwachidewake did, tucking up your skirts and retreating promptly—there's no telling how much better the common people's reception would be. If you cling on forever like a spoiled sardine stuck to a fire-poking chopstick, the time will come when no one turns back to look at you at all. Maintaining state governance with power as number one and violence as number two as your sole weapons—how on earth do you think the royal way and benevolent government will be promulgated? The politics you practice are so-called expedient ways, the way of hegemony, the politics of demons attempting to support the strong and overwhelm the weak. I pre-informed Iwachidewake of your conspiracy, had him make a backdoor escape, and turned the tables on your schemes. With such a lack of understanding, how can either of you serve as a great elder of the nation? To say that monkeys wearing crowns is an expression that vividly portrays the state of a so-called demo-elder, atsu-ha-ha. Goodness me, what miserable-looking... anxious-looking expressions you wear. That face of yours certainly won't recover in two or three years. Recovering like the earthquake of Jitentōshima won't be easy, atsu-ha-ha.'

Matsuwakahiko straightened his posture.

Matsuwakahiko: 'This, this is an unreasonable statement to make, even if it is the Gojiyō of Wakagimisama. Is it not outrageous to treat this Raushin as if I were mere Giuba? I am one who has managed the Kokusei for generations since the Miyo of Masakayamazuminokami, who returned this Kokudo to Onhahauhe Suekohime-sama, and who welcomed Onchichiuhe to serve him as Kokushi. I am somewhat different from other Shinka. Even if you are the Wakagimi of the Kokushi of Chin, there is a Fubunritsu that it is impossible in Jijitsu for you to set the Shiseihaushin freely while setting me aside.'

He dragged out his ancestry and, while looking indignant, returned a single arrow.

Kuniteruwake remained calm.

Kuniteruwake: 'Ha ha ha ha, dragging out ancient history and speaking of being a descendant of generations of Kokushi—such Brahmin-like Kotoba were used in an era decades past. It is because of such an old brain that the nation cannot be governed. The reason the hearts of the people of the land of Chin are desolate today is, in short, not the fault of the Kokushi. As long as this land is protected by the gods, it shall never perish. However, as long as a person of no common sense like you remains at the top, the world cannot be expected to be peaceful for long. It is the great responsibility of people like you that has led the land of Chin to its current state. Both of you should place your hands on your chests, reflect upon yourselves, repent, be ashamed of your incompetence, realize your ignorance, awaken to the times, fear the will of Heaven, and make the best decision. I cannot remain as Wakagimisama forever, after all...'

Matsuwakahiko: 'That may be so, but the Kokushi-sama is of advanced age, always prone to illness, and one never knows when he might ascend to heaven. If that happens, the Wakagimi will be the pillar of the nation; you cannot remain a child forever. Therefore, you must keep what this old man says in your ears.'

He adjusted his posture and spoke with a grave air. However, what Kuniteruwake had just said—that he could not remain as Wakagimisama forever—was a sudden slip of the tongue regarding his intention to leave his life in the Seike, descend to the common people, and thoroughly reform society. Yet, as neither of the two men were gods, they did not dream that the Wakagimi held such thoughts, and thus the situation passed without incident.

Kuniteruwake walked back to his living quarters with loud footsteps, wearing a cold smile. Behind him, the two men huddled together and spoke in low voices.

Matsuwakahiko: 'Isahiko-dono, with Wakagimisama having such a spirit, is it not the case that we cannot possibly remain in our positions? Would it not be better to resign gracefully and retire to a quiet life?'

Isahiko: 'I do not recognize those as your own words. You were born into a family that should assist the Kokushi; you cannot think in the same way as those of us with humble origins. Even if you were to retire, the Kokushi would surely seek your counsel when organizing the internal cabinet, and the spineless politicians would likely make pilgrimages to your door to seek your instructions. You cannot possibly escape the political sphere of the land of Chin. That is your loyalty to the land of Chin, after all.'

Matsuwakahiko: 'Indeed, it is not that I have not thought of that, but it is so excessive that my heart is truly wavering. Ah, never become a politician in life... that was well said.'

He let out a weary sigh.

Isahiko: 'I sympathize with your distress. However, you must not let your intention to resign slip, even in jest, in front of the Kokushi-sama. It would not be the duty of a Shinka to cause worry to the elderly Kokushi-sama. I will warn you of that much, even at the cost of my life.'

Matsuwakahiko: 'Indeed, as you say. However, unable to advance and unable to retreat, it has truly become a troubled state of the world. Ah, what should I do?'

と顔の居ずまひを直し、仔細らしく述べ立てる。併し今国照別が………何時迄も若君様ではをられない……と云つたのは、近い内清家生活から放れ、民間に下つて徹底的に社会を改造せむと考へてゐた事をフツと漏らしたのである。併し乍ら両人は若君にそんな考へがあるとは神ならぬ身の夢にも知らなかつたので、此場は無難に済んだのである。

国照別は冷笑を泛べ乍ら、足音高く吾居間に帰つてゆく。後に二人は首を鳩め、声を低うして、

松若『伊佐彦殿、若君様があゝいふ御精神では吾々も到底職に止まることが出来ぬでは厶らぬか。一層のこと潔く辞職を致し、閑地については如何で厶らうかな』

伊佐『貴方のお言葉とも覚えませぬ。貴方は国司を補佐すべき御家柄の生れ、吾々の如き氏素性の卑しき者と同日に考へることは出来ますまい。仮令御退隠遊ばしても、内局組織の時には国司からもキツとお尋ねもあるだらうし、又腰抜の政治家共がお百度参りをしてお指図を仰ぎに行くでせうから、到底貴方は珍の国の政治圏外を脱することは出来ますまい。それが貴方の珍の国に対する忠誠で厶いますからな』

松若『なる程、それも思はぬではないが、余りのことで実は心が迷ふのだ。あゝ人生政治家となる勿れ……とはよく言つたものだなア』

と青い吐息をつく。

伊佐『御苦心御察し申します。併し乍ら国司様の前で、貴方は仮りにも辞意をお洩らしになつてはいけませぬぞ。御老齢の国司に御心配をかけては、臣子たる者の役がすみませぬからなア。それ丈は伊佐彦が命に替へても御注意を申上げておきます』

松若『いかにも貴殿の言はるる通りだ。併し乍ら進みもならず退きもならず、実に困つた世態になつたものだなア。アヽ何うしたら可からうかなア』

§ 4

From the next room, a young voice is heard shouting,

'The only path for Tenkai is simply that of resignation.'

The two are startled and listen intently, lost in thought.

After a short while, pushing open the dividing sliding door unceremoniously, the one who appeared in just a summer yukata was Harunohime.

Harunohime: 'You two elderly ojii-sama, do you still not understand Brother's warning to that extent? You truly have old-fashioned heads, don't you.'

Rather than looking upon the face of Harunohime, the two suddenly straightened their deportment, and while lowering their heads,

Matsuwaka: 'We are humbled. In any case, our office is heavy and our virtue insufficient, and truly causing distress to the august mind of Kokushisama, we have no words of excuse.'

Harunohime: 'Ho ho ho, are you old men not just telling lies? If you have attained the awareness that your office is heavy and your virtue insufficient, why do you not quickly resign your posts?'

What you just said to me is no doubt merely a diplomatic phrase to adorn the surface.

'The office is heavy, and though we possess virtue and wisdom, upon the advancement of the era there are no further means to be applied, and if we are thus, even though other junior ranks should come forth and take up that office, they would ultimately be unable to accomplish government superior to us.'

'They would instantly plunge the nation into the abyss of ruin.'

'If I do not step forth, what shall be done with this nation and the common people?'

It must be that you are driven by such bottom-of-the-heart self-conceit, is there any mistake about that? Ho ho ho.

As the station of a young woman, I have spoken rude things to you deeply experienced ojii-sama.

By Kamu-naobi and Ō-naobi, looking anew and hearing anew, quickly forgive me, please.

Pardon my great rudeness, ho ho ho ho.'

So saying while laughing, she briskly walked along the corridor and advanced into the inner palace.

The two, after pondering deeply for a short while, took each other by the hand and proceeded toward the living room of Kokushi, intending to make some kind of presentation.

Suddenly is heard the sound of a warning bell tolling wildly, and when they look down upon the castle town from the high railing of the corridor, in the distant direction black smoke scorches the heavens, and a considerably large fire has broken out.

松若『恐れ入ります。何分任重くして徳足らず、実に国司様の御心慮を悩まし奉り、申し訳が厶いませぬ』
