Chapter 12: 悪 Plain 眠衆 [1757]

悪原眠衆〔1757〕

§ 1

Matsuwakahiko sat in the inner room of his mansion together with Sutekohime, wearing a severe and troubled expression, muttering complaints, and grumbling.

Matsuwakahiko: 'Hey Sutekohime, because your upbringing is far too lenient, our son Matsuyoriwake disguises himself day after day and frequents the Ashiharaiukuwaku pleasure quarters, while our daughter Tokihahime has become a tomboy, claiming it is for the Hime's errands and staying away from home. With recent conduct like this, the authority of the Seika cannot possibly be maintained. Won't you pull yourself together and stop causing trouble? Since I am busy with affairs of state, I cannot possibly attend to the education of children. That children go bad is entirely because the mother's upbringing is poor.'

Sutekohime: 'Needless to say, I have provided sufficient education, but I consider that I have not raised them in a way that is shameful as the son and daughter of the Seika.'

Matsuwakahiko raised his voice shrilly,

Matsuwakahiko: 'You raise him in such a way that he visits the Ashiharaiukuwaku pleasure quarters night after night, and you still say it is fine? There is a limit to being devoid of common sense!'

Sutekohime: 'Although our son is at a marriageable age, an age when we must have him married before long, you always say things about how the household should be, or how his status should be, talking about old-fashioned things, so out of the result of heartbreak, our son has also become desperate. The woman whom our son loves is the person you also know, the udon shop daughter named Ofuku. Did you not say that her guardian deity did not match in status, and using your retainers, did you not oppressively force them to break off their engagement? Because of that, as the result of heartbreak, worrying about what kind of thing he might commit, I could not sleep a single wink at night. There are various customs in the world; the ends of heartbroken people vary, such as throwing themselves into a deep river to commit a double suicide out of desperation, stepping in front of trains, or disemboweling themselves with a gun, and hanging. Therefore, worrying about what our son would do, I prayed to the great deity of Misuso, and it seems our son was also able to see things anew and hear things anew through Kaminasuki Oonasuki, so perhaps he has come to boldly frequent the Ashiharaiukuwaku pleasure quarters. As the result to which a heartbroken person should go, I am filled with admiration that he chose the best method.'

Matsuwakahiko: 'Hey Sutekohime, well, old dame, are you saying such a thing in your right mind? A son who damages the family name—a fellow not enough even if put to the sword—and you seem to agree with him, isn't that outrageous? Our household has been entrusted with the proxy rights of the Chini country since the era of Masakayama-tsumi no Mikoto, and has been transmitted to this day as a prestigious family of power and influence. If someone stains that family lineage, no matter how precious the son is, can I possibly forgive them?'

Sutekohime: 'That must be the code of morals from decades ago. In a world where morals, politics, religion, and human feelings and customs advance day by day, such moldy thoughts probably do not pass muster today. Although you are the prime minister of a country, with such an old head, you always wonder why the masses are satisfied, which I always find strange. Fortunately, since our son or daughter was born with souls suited to the times, I take that as the joy of my old age, and spend days filled with discontent.'

Thinking whatever she thought, Sutekohime also grew somewhat desperate today, and without cowering or shrinking, she carried it through. Matsuwakahiko had never even dreamed in his dreams that the obedient wife who had been with him for decades would say such a resolute thing, and because it was the first time, he began to worry whether she might have gone mad or something, and deciding first of all that not opposing her was paramount, and that until her fever cooled down just a little bit, he would do as she said, he put on a cringey voice and stroked her back while saying,

Matsuwakahiko: 'Hey Sutekohime, just as you say. My, what a sharp brain! Since you seem a little agitated, I won't say anything more today. It would be good for you to go quietly to the inner rooms and rest peacefully.'

§ 2

Sutekohime had already read the mind of Matsuwakahiko. Taking advantage of his belief that she was frantic, she resolved to blurt out all her own pent-up opinions right here and urge Matsuwakahiko to make his decision, deliberately feigning ignorance,

Hohahaha, what a difficult face you are making there, Gozensama. Since I am going to throw myself into Fuchikaha from now on for a long farewell, please grant me a leave of absence. Even if you say you will not grant it, once I have made up my mind, I will bite my tongue and show you I can die. Oh, I want to die, hohahaha. I wish to cross the boundary of spiritual-physical detachment, ascend to Tengoku as quickly as possible, and enter a second life of purity and joy. Look, look, an angel is opening a golden fan and beckoning to me, saying come, come. Ah, how I wish to go quickly.

Matsuwakahiko was even more astonished, and thought... Ah, this creature is stark mad. There is nothing to be done, I must first act so as not to displease her...

Aiya Sutekohime-dono, just as you say, since this Matsuwakahiko will listen to anything you ask, please do not go to such places as Tengoku. If my wife precedes me in old age, it will be lonely, you see.

Suteko: 'Will you say yes, yes, to what I say, and listen without a single word of contradiction?'

Matsuwaka: 'Un, I will listen to anything. You had better speak without reserve.'

Suteko: 'If so, I will tell you. First of all, please return the office of Chief Minister (Tairo), and descend together with me to the common folk, escaping the resentment of the living beings. And if you hand over the political power to the living beings, the living beings will surely establish a splendid administration centering on the Kokushi family.'

Matsuwakahiko frowned with an expression of annoyance, but Ah, however, if I oppose her and she has a fit, I can bear it. Anything is fine, since it is what a mad person says, I need only say un, un... and so he harbored a crafty thought,

Matsuwaka: 'Un, yoshi yoshi, I intend to return it at any time.'

Suteko: 'Ah, how joyful! As expected of Matsuwakahiko-sama, that indeed makes you my husband. Please, while your imperial/honorable intention does not change, draw up the letter of resignation for the post of Chief Minister and stamp it with your official seal. If you do not do so, I will die and go to Tengoku.'

Matsuwaka: 'Chie, what a troublesome madwoman. If I do not write it for her, she will not be pacified. Once I have written it, I simply need not submit it.'

And taking out writing paper from the writing desk, rubbing the ink and loading his brush, he smoothly wrote out the resignation as Chief Minister, and pressed his official seal before Sutekohime,

Matsuwaka: 'Saa, Sutekohime, you are convinced by this, aren't you?'

Suteko: 'Hai, I am convinced. Please hand that letter of resignation over to me.'

Matsuwaka: 'Iya iya, if I keep it this way, I can submit it at any time. If I were to let you hold it and you dropped it somewhere, it would be a disaster, so let us stop at just handing it over.'

Suteko: 'Then you must be deceiving me. You must be clinging fondly to political power and high office, are you not?'

Matsuwakahiko, stung to the quick,

Eh, noisy, it is a settled matter. Today's position was by no means acquired by this Matsuwakahiko. So to speak, it is the same as acting as a proxy for my ancestors. How could such a thing be done lightly by my own single judgment? I must wake up my ancestors from beneath the earth and receive their permission. You must be possessed by the Great Orochi of Yamata. How filthy, go over there!

he shouted in desperation. Sutekohime thought... It is also a pity to make the old man too angry, this is about the place to bring down the curtain... and obediently lapsed into silence. While Matsuwakahiko was walking a few steps outside to see the flowers at the edge of the garden to dispel his gloom, leaning on his cane, the servant Shinkō hurriedly came running back,
