Chapter 14: Darkness 枕 [1759]

暗枕〔1759〕

§ 1

Kuniteruwake and his follower were forced to spend the night on the mountainside of Mount Arina. Just as the dense fog, which made it impossible to distinguish even a few feet ahead, pressed in upon them in a gloomy and oppressive manner, the sky was suddenly filled with black clouds, and the rain of a summer shower struck their garments like gravel, followed by a wind that seemed to blow them away. In the loneliness and suffering of the deep fog, haze, heavy rain, and strong wind, which pressed upon them one after another like a revolving lantern, even the stalwart Kuniteruwake felt the hardships of travel for the first time; he knelt and bowed to the heavens from the bottom of his heart, praying to behold the light of dawn as soon as possible. However, no matter how much he prayed, the power of time could not be swayed, and the night only deepened and wore on, while the surroundings grew ever darker until they could no longer even see each other's whereabouts.

Kuniteru: 'Exposed to rain and wind, wrapped in fog, / My own Mitama wanders, lost on the path ahead.'

Asakō: 'Those are the words of a faint-hearted master; / How long could this darkness possibly last?'

Kuniteru: 'May you cherish the living Kototama of Asakō, / And may the morning sun grace the sky. / Crossing the fields of morning reeds, now here / I rest upon the peak of sincere Arina. / Fearing the night rain and the storm on the peak, / Are the treetops trembling?'

Asakō: 'I thought the master and servant were trembling, / But it is the treetops, so I am relieved. / If the master trembles so, / There is no way to traverse this world where evil spirits run wild.'

Kuniteru: 'The Kototama of trembling that I speak of / Is a riddle for shaking off all the dust of the world.'

Asakō: 'O Kuniteruwake-sama, you who have an unyielding spirit, / Take care in the shade of the lacquer trees. / Unable to see right, left, front, or back, / The dark mountain path is all the more silent.'

Kuniteru: 'Asakō, to say it is silent must be a lie, / Is it not perhaps speaking of the loneliness in your heart?'

The two were struck by a sense of desolation, and they sat with their knees touching, exchanging foolish banter. From somewhere, a thin, lonely, thread-like voice was heard. Asakō clung to Kuniteruwake's waist, trembling violently.

Asakō: 'O-oh, Master, i-it has appeared!'

Kuniteru: 'Hmm, it has appeared, has it?'

Asakō: 'What shall we do?'

Kuniteru: 'Whatever happens, it is fine; leave it to the will of the Gods. It is surely a trial from the Gods. Perhaps the demon of Mount Arina, thinking that if I take a coward like you along, you will be a hindrance to my hands and feet, has taken the initiative to appear in order to finish you off. Ha-ha-ha! Still, it is truly dark. If I am mistaken for you and get bitten, it would be a disaster, so hey, Asakō, let us speak from a distance of two or three feet. It is so dark that even a monster would have no way of seeing. If we keep making noise, it will likely target that, ha-ha-ha!'

Asakō: 'Master, you say such cold-hearted things. Is it not the duty of a master to help his henchman in times of distress? Are you trying to have your henchman eaten by a demon just to save yourself?'

Kuniteru: 'Of course. When you became my henchman, what did you swear? "If there is a crisis for the master, I will cast aside my life to serve. My life is dedicated to the master," you said, and you even cut your little finger to give to me, did you not? Your efforts are appreciated, ha-ha-ha! A man should indeed have a henchman. If you were not here, I would be the one eaten because there would be no substitute. Thanks to Asakō, I can keep my life intact. Namu Asakō Daimyōjin, please be killed, please be eaten; since that cannot be, please bring me spiritual happiness, he-he-he-he!'

Asakō: 'Th-that is, th-that is different. If I were to lose my life accompanying you when you are fighting or helping the weak against the strong, it would be worth dying for, but to be eaten by some unknown monster in the depths of such a lonely mountain is truly a dog's death.'

Kuniteru: 'As you say, it is indeed a dog's death, a dance under the floorboards. However, that is what is called a sacrifice. I made you my henchman so that you could be a sacrifice in my time of need. Even I do not want to see my only henchman eaten by a demon, but still, it is easier to bear than losing my own life, ho-ho-ho-ho!'

§ 2

The sound, which seemed like a strange whistle from a moment ago, was heard drawing a line in the air about seven or eight feet above the ground like a thin ribbon.

'Hyu-hyu, hee-yu.'

In reality, it was the sound of a gale passing through the treetops. However, to Asakō, it sounded like nothing but a monster. Kuniteruwake had known from the beginning that it was the sound of the wind, but because Asakō was so frightened, he tried teasing him for fun. Asakō trembled and cried out in a loud voice,

Asakō: 'Kuniharutachinōokami-sama, Mitamanōokami-sama, I beseech you, please drive away the strange Kami that has just appeared. My Oyabun is precious, but my own body is also precious. I would not mind in the least if I were eaten in place of my Oyabun... but if it is all the same, please save both the Oyabun and his Kobun. If I am eaten here now, it would be a pity for you, Oyabun-sama, to suffer such hardships and toil on a solitary journey in a foreign land where you know no one. I do not wish to die in a place like this either. Kamunagara Chihahe Mase, Kamunagara Chihahe Mase.'

He continued to pray. The darkness grew deeper, and a lukewarm wind licked at his waist.

Kuniteruwake: 'In the Arina Mountains where many Magatsu-kami dwell, who feed upon the lives of men, wrapped in a shroud of darkness, here lie two upon a stone pillow. Before they can sleep, a strange man-eating Kami appears. When I trace the source of that weeping voice, it is the fleshly palace of Kuniteruwake. Even at a glance, it looks delicious. As for the fellow Asakō who follows him, his body is somehow unappetizing and looks filthy. Even if I were to eat such a yakuza fellow, it would not provide strength to my belly. If I upset my stomach, I would have to writhe in agony tomorrow night. Therefore, I shall refrain from eating the flesh of Asakō. What I truly desire to eat, what makes my throat ring, is the scent of the flesh of the Oyabun, Kuniteruwake. Yet, because his divine virtue fills his entire body, I am troubled that my teeth cannot sink in, and so I leave this place to return. From now on, I shall feed the Kobun of Asa plenty of delicious things to fatten their flesh, and once they are covered in fat, I shall come to see them again. Lord Kuniteru, Lord Asa, farewell. I shall take my leave now.'

As the demon sang this, he left his geta behind and departed. 'Hey, Asakō, if you do not pull yourself together, you will not be saved.'

Asakō: 'Oh, Oyabun, did the demon really say such things? That must be a lie.'

Kuniteruwake: 'It must not have reached your ears, but when I translate the Kototama of the demon, that is what it amounts to. Just as the language used daily by the people of the land of Chin differs from that of the land of Teru, the language of humans and demons is also different. Even birds and beasts have their own languages and communicate their intentions to one another, you see.'

Asakō: 'If that is the case, Oyabun, you are like a Kami. Born in a magnificent castle, holding the status of a governor in the land of Chin, I thought it was a whim beyond measure that you would be served by a Kobun like me, but I am truly impressed that you can understand the language of demons. It feels presumptuous even to call you Oyabun anymore.'

Kuniteruwake: 'In any case, the demon said your body was filthy, tasted bad, and could not be eaten, so you may rest easy. The dangerous one is me. It seems that because I was raised in luxury and splendor from a young age, my body is soft, and the demon said it wanted to eat me. But thanks to the divine virtue I possess, it retreated in defeat. However, it said to feed Asakō sweet things and let him get plenty of fat, and that it would appear again then to devour him with a crunch. You must be very careful. Therefore, if there is any sweet food, give it all to me, and if you only eat the dregs, you will not gain fat, and the demon will leave you alone, understand? If you do not value your life, then by all means, work hard to eat gourmet food. Ha-ha-ha!'

Asakō was an unexpectedly honest man. He swallowed Kuniteruwake's words whole without a second thought.
