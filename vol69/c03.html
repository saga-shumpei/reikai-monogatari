Chapter 3: 喬育 [1748]

喬育〔1748〕

§ 1

Kuniyoriwake was by nature open-hearted and magnanimous, never fretting over petty matters and remaining unconcerned with everything. Thus, ever since he was appointed Kokushi of the realm of Mezu, he entrusted all political affairs to his senior minister, Matsuwakahiko, and merely held an empty title in actual practice. Therefore, all political matters, great and small, of the realm of Mezu were held within the hands of Matsuwakahiko and other clannish factions. Kuniyoriwake did nothing but bow in worship morning and evening before the Sumeōkami, enjoying the beauties of nature, remembering the easygoing days when he was a Sendenshi, occasionally sighing, letting Matsukohime pour his sake, and inviting musicians into the castle to soothe his melancholy feelings with song, dance, and music. Furthermore, toward his biological children, Kuniteruwake and Harunohime, he forced no troublesome home discipline upon them, leaving them to mature naturally. Consequently, the relationship between parent and children was harmonious like that of brothers, without the slightest discrimination, creating a home filled with gentle harmony like a spring breeze. Through the divine virtue of the Tama-no-Tama, Kuniyoriwake clearly foresaw the course of all worldly events. For that reason, he intentionally refrained from participating in politics until the proper time came, knowing well that even if he applied petty makeshift expedients, it would end almost entirely in futile effort unless the hour had arrived. Thus, as a temporary stopgap, he had delegated actual governance to Matsuwakahiko's faction for many long years.

Opening the round window of the inner room to let the summer breeze enter, he leaned against his armrest, absorbed in composing poems. Thereupon, sliding open the sliding door quietly and entering was Matsukohime. Kuniyoriwake's mind was consumed by his poem composition, and he did not notice that Matsukohime had entered. Matsukohime placed both hands on the floor and, in graceful words,

'My lord-sama, how is your health...'

She repeated this four or five times. Kuniyoriwake, keeping his eyes fixed on the poem card,

'Seeking to face the dawn, heaven and earth Tremble and shudder morning and evening. Though the moon shines in the vast sky, passing clouds Enfold it deeply, making it unseen upon the earth. Enduring the snow to bloom while waiting For the spring of Kinoene, the fragrance of plum blossoms wafts. The time is now; heaven and earth are dark, Tangled in wild confusion before the dawn. Because the grace of the gods of heaven and earth is deep, An earthquake arrives to protect the world.'

He was reciting softly to himself. Raising her voice even higher, Matsukohime,

'My lord-sama, how is your health?'

She repeated. Kuniyoriwake started in surprise,

'Ah, Matsukohime, is there something you need?'

Matsuko: 'Yes, I have an urgent matter to consult with you about, and I apologize for startling you while you were studying.'

Kuniyori: 'Nonsense, it is no study at all. I was merely twisting together a clumsy thirty-one-syllable poem.'

Matsuko: 'You must have composed a splendid poem. Won't you please let me hear it once?'

Kuniyori: 'Nonsense, it is no masterwork worth hearing. My mind was so frustrated that even the poem turned out all jumbled up. Today it is unusually poorly done.'

Matsuko: 'Your poems become better as time passes. Pardon me for saying so, but even if I think "what a poem" when you first compose it, when I read it in later days, all your poems turn out to be records of prophecy. Matsuwakahiko also said that one cannot carelessly overlook your poems, for every single one is a prophecy.'

Kuniyori: 'Whether it is a prophecy, a five-syllable verse, or a strange rumor, it is no big deal. At any rate, it is a poem I composed for myself, ha-ha-ha!'

Matsuko: 'Oh, what are you saying? You must be speaking in riddles again. Are you saying there will be an earthquake in the near future?'

Kuniyori: 'Yeah—earthquake, thunder, fire, father—and nowadays one more thing has been added to the list: the so-called old lady, ha-ha-ha-ha-ha!'

Matsuko: 'My lord-sama, please do not use such vulgar words as "old lady" in a high-class household. If our son or daughter should hear it, they might imitate it, which would be troublesome.'

Kuniyori: 'What, whether you call her Okusama, Kōshitsu, Goreishitsu, Yama-no-Kami, or Mama, she is still just a Niyōbau. Is there any need to be so particular about the names that Ningen have attached?'

Sueko: 'Just now you mentioned Jishin, thunder, Kuwaji, and Oyaji... surely that must be a profound mystery as well. Your words always contain such terrifying meanings within their Kotsukeishiyadatsu, so I cannot easily let them pass by.'

Kuniyori: 'Ha ha ha ha, saying Jishin and thunder means that Kuniyoriwake himself is a Kami. Since your Jishinriyoku is as strong as a Kamisama, you too are a Jishin-Kami.'

Sueko: 'Ho ho ho ho, you are playing at being evasive; that cannot be the meaning. What do you mean by Kuwaji and Oyaji? Please, tell me that.'

Kuniyori: 'You heard the sound of the Keishiyōranda just now, didn't you? It is a mystery about how the Oyaji-ren, Matsuwakahiko and Isahiko, were in the midst of discussing national politics or some such thing with their kettle-heads, when a Kuwaji broke out; the Oyaji was startled, fell from the high railing, bruised his waist, and was carried to my room to be cared for by Mama, ha ha ha ha.'

Sueko: 'Oh my, from whom did you hear that Matsuwakahiko fell from the high railing?'

Kuniyori: 'Such things are clearly understood through my spiritual eye. That is why I said that Kuniyoriwake himself is a Kami. He is a Kuwaji-Oyaji because the Oyaji was startled by the Kuwaji and fell.'

Sueko: 'Speaking of that Matsuwakahiko, the reason I came to ask you just now also concerns him. Fortunately, since Sutegohime was in attendance, he was immediately carried to his own living quarters and is receiving care from Sutegohime. I felt so sorry for him that I visited his sickbed, but Matsuwakahiko is extremely indignant.'

Kuniyori: 'I suppose he is indignant because he is relieving himself in the latrine. Even I go on a pilgrimage to Kōya once a day to relieve myself, ha ha ha ha.'

Sueko: 'There is a time and place for making jokes. You must listen to what he has to say at least once.'

Kuniyori: 'It is not that I will not listen. He was teased by his son and daughter, let off steam like a kettle, and was injured after being startled twice by a Kuwaji, is that not it? Well, it is fine; it is about time for Matsuwakahiko to withdraw as well.'

Sueko: 'Please, I am being serious today, so listen to me earnestly. Matsuwakahiko was complaining that you are a troublesome lord, always slipping away from the point of your Kototama like trying to catch a catfish with a gourd, and that you are as elusive as trying to tie the wind with a rope. Indifference is fine, but for what purpose did you become the governor of the land of Chin?'

Kuniyori: 'For no purpose at all; it is only because the Lord Ōkami and Kotoyoriwake-sama told me to become your husband, so I became the husband of you, who never take no for an answer. You know this too, but did I not press the Lord Ōkami and Kotoyoriwake-sama for a final confirmation at that time? ...I said, "Even if you make such a good-for-nothing Ningen—a family-ruining, widow-toppling, womanizing vagabond—the husband of Suekohime-sama, it will be of no use..." and when I refused, the Lord Ōkami said that was exactly what he liked about me, did he not? Even so, I am suppressing my surging courage and obeying the divine command while enduring plenty of stifling circumstances. It is cruel to press me further than this. Politics is something for the vulgar. Does the Laozi not say, "The highest ruler is one whose existence is barely known"? It is enough if the people just know that there is a ruler in this country. If I were to meddle and attempt even clumsy politics, the name of Kuniyoriwake would instantly fall into disrepute, and it would even go so far as to defile the name of the Lord Ōkami.'

Sueko: 'Your explanation is reasonable enough, but Taijiyō belongs to the remote past, an era when human intelligence was yet uncivilized; knowing simply that there was a king in the land was enough to keep the minds of the people at peace, but the state of society today cannot go on in that fashion.'

Kuniyori: 'Does it not say in the Raushikiyō: In the highest antiquity, the people knew only that there was a ruler; next, they praised him; next, they feared him; next, they reviled him; next, they insulted him? As the world gradually progresses and virtue declines, it comes to pass that people talk of benevolence and salvation, and one must humor the myriad masses. Because alms are thus bestowed upon the myriad masses, the supreme leader is praised as a benevolent person and a ruler of the golden age of Geushiyun; as for the next stage, of fearing him, this is precisely how it is: growing too accustomed to the supreme leader's benevolence, the living beings become self-willed and, as a result of their arrogance, plot injustices, commit such grave evils as robbery, murder, and arson, and begin to disrupt the order of the world. Thereupon, the supreme leader establishes strict laws and regulations to reward good and punish evil. It is just like serving as a guard at a busy crossroad. That much might still be acceptable, but as the world progresses further, the next stage is that of insulting him. At last, the hearts of the living beings become defiled, and foolish fellows arise who proclaim things like: What kind of seed is a supreme leader after all? In short, it is sufficient if the name of being the supreme leader stands at the center of the nation as the representative of the gods. Doing all sorts of petty, elaborate handiwork will no longer do. Therefore, this Kuniyoriwake is revered as the provincial governor by the living beings of the land of Chindokuni, but as for myself, I am neither a provincial governor nor anything of the sort; I am simply and solely the original Kuniyoriwake, the former Munehiko. Who... how could anyone show their face in public with such a grandiose air, it is ridiculous... Ahhh.'

With this, he gave a great yawn, clenched the fists of both hands, and thrust them high above his head.

Sueko: 'There is simply nothing to be done. You are always like that, so my foolish words are brushed aside in a single breath, aren't they? Nevertheless, My Lord, you are always talking about natural education and spontaneous education, and because you left your two children to themselves in their own willful way, Matsuwakahiko and Isahiko turned upon the elderly retainers and uttered outrageous and arrogant words, saying... such antique junk as you should retire as quickly as possible for the benefit of the state or the happiness of the living beings... or something of the sort, so I have heard. No matter how good laissez-faire education is supposed to be, wouldn't you be in trouble if you did not give them even a tiny bit of moral instruction?'

Kuniyori: 'The education of children lies with the mother. You are in trouble precisely because you are what the world calls a good wife and wise mother; true education cannot be achieved without being a wise wife and good mother. Since Kuniyoriwake is neither an educator nor a babysitter nor a home tutor, such a thing is completely outside my field. Still, did they urge both son and daughter to retire to those old-fashioned fogies? As expected of my child, ah, admirable, admirable. Like father, like son; this may truly be called Kuniyoriwake finding a kindred spirit, ah-ha-ha-ha.'

Sueko: 'Ah, what a troublesome situation this has become. As for My Lord, any admonition whatsoever is like driving a wedge into bean curd or a nail into rice bran. If we leave things neglected like this, I cannot help worrying that both son and daughter, taking a fancy to novelty, will abandon their equestrian life and their parents, and run away to parts unknown. Matsuwakahiko also says he cannot help worrying about that, you know.'

Kuniyori: 'Whether son or daughter, they will surely grow to dislike the equestrian life and leave sooner or later. No matter what, they are children who carry my blood. Even though they are now caught up in the nominal title of provincial governor of Chindokuni, putting on airs and maintaining a gilded authority, when you trace their origin, they are the very end product of Munehiko, who used to go on pilgrimages with Okatsu, yes, they are his son, so it is only natural. Since a child who does not resemble its parents is said to be an ogre's child, it seems I truly have a genuine child after all, ah-ha-ha-ha. Hey, Princess Sueko, education is of the utmost importance for human beings. Depending on the direction education takes, a person may become great or small, you see.'

国依『子供の教育は母にあるのだ。お前は世の所謂良妻賢母だから困るのだ。賢妻良母でなくては本当の教育は出来ないよ。国依別は教育家でもなければ子守でもなし、家庭教師でもないから、そんなことア畑違ひだよ。併しあの時代遅れの親爺連に、悴も娘も引退を迫つたといふのか、流石は俺の子だ、あゝ感心々々。此父にして此子あり、国依別知己を得たりと云ふべしだ、アツハヽヽヽ』

末子『あゝ困つたことになつたものだなア。丸で吾君様に向つては如何なる箴言も豆腐に鎹、糠に釘だワ。此儘にして放任して置かうものなら、悴も娘も新しがつて乗馬生活を捨て、両親を捨て、どこへ逐電するか分らないと気が揉めてならないのですワ。松若彦もそれが心配でならないと云つてゐましたよ』

国依『悴も娘も乗馬生活を嫌つて何れは出るだらう。何と云つても、俺の血を受けてる子供だからな。今こそ斯うして珍の国の国司の仮名に捉はれ、鍍金的権威を保つてすまし込んでゐるものの、元を糾せば、お勝と巡礼をして居つた宗彦の成れの果だ、其悴だもの当然だよ。親に似ぬ子は鬼子と云ふから、俺もヤツパリ誠の子を持つたと見えるワイ、アツハヽヽヽ。オイ末子姫、人間は教育が肝心だよ。教育の行方によつて、人物が大きくもなり小さくもなるのだからな』

§ 4

Sueko: 'Ho ho ho ho ho, it makes me laugh to hear you talk about education. Is not the character for kyō in your education written with the beast radical and the king character for madness?'

Kuniyori: 'Certainly, even a beast is fine if it becomes a king, but however, the kyō of the education I speak of is not like that. It is the kyō of tall trees soaring and piercing the clouds amidst the forest. With the state of education as it is nowadays, though one can make potted bonsai to decorate an alcove, good timber to serve as pillars cannot be made. Wild cedars, cypresses, and pines grow just as they are in the way of Kamunagara without adding the slightest artifice, which is why they become splendid pillars. As it is today, ignoring the capacities and genius of children, administering oppressive education and cramming education, winding wires around young trees that are trying hard to become large trees, pinching their hearts, and putting braces on them so that they end up being put into small pots, not even a single decent human being is ever produced. Leaving things to Kamunagara, letting children develop as they wish, and extending their intellect is true education. Large fish do not dwell in small ponds, and my son too seems to have refined his personality considerably, making this narrow Takasagojō seem cramped to him. That truly makes him a world-class figure, no, a venerable person of character; oh, truly, how grateful I am for the divine grace, and I offer my thanks.'

So saying, he claps his hands and bows toward the shrine. Sueko-hime was utterly dumbfounded by the absurdity of it all, and did not even know what words to reply.

原文（近代文語）

末子『ホヽヽヽヽ、教育が聞いて呆れますワ。貴方の教育の教は獣扁に王の狂でせう』

国依『無論獣でも王になれば結構だが、併し俺の云ふ教育の教はそんなのではない。森林の中に雲を凌いで聳え立つ喬木の喬だ。現代の如うな教育の行方では、床の間に飾る盆栽は作れても、柱になる良材は出来ない。野生の杉檜松などは、少しも人工を加へず、惟神の儘に成育してゐるから、立派な柱となるのだ。今日の如うに児童の性能や天才を無視して、圧迫教育や詰込教育を施し、折角大木にならうとする若木に針金を巻いたり、心を摘んだり、つつぱりをかうたりして、小さい鉢に入れて了ふものだから、碌な人間は一つも出来やしない。惟神に任して、思ふままに子供を発達させ、智能を伸長させるのが真の教育だ。大魚は小池に棲まず、悴も余程人格を練り上げたと見えて、此狭い高砂城が窮屈になつたとみえる。それでこそ世界的人物だ、否崇敬すべき人格者だ、てもさても神様の御恵み有難う感謝致します』

と拍手し乍ら神殿に向つて拝礼する。末子姫は余りのことに呆れ果て、返す言葉も知らなかつた。
