Chapter 7: Holy 子 [1752]

聖子〔1752〕

§ 1

Since Ihaharuwake, who had earned the Adana of Shinporaujiu or Nageyariraujiu within Takasagojiyōnai in the capital of Chin, vanished like the wind to Izuko, various Riugenhigo spread wildly: that he was hatching some grand Taimau, colluding with Shiyugishiya to make a Kendojiurai and force the Riyōrau Matsuwakahiko and Isahiko into Intai in Itsukiyo, sweeping clean the Kūki of the Tenchi of Chin, and that the world would undergo a Tatenaho back into the holy Seidai of Masakayamazuminokami; thus the Jinshin grew Kiyōkiyō and uneasy, and whenever people gathered, it was trumpeted aloud on every Ōji, in every Uramachi, and at the Idobatakuwaigi of the wives of the Nagaya. Matsuwakahiko, deeming the situation serious, roused his aged body and stationed Kenpei and constables throughout every corner of the city, or let plainclothes officials patrol the crossroads, arresting without exception all who uttered suspicious words; so that when night fell, not a single person ventured outside for fear, and the once bustling capital avenues took on the aspect of a wilderness, growing desolate as though the lights had gone out.

Ihaharuwake had in reality changed his name to Iwakō and was living deep within the mansion of the chivalrous boss Aishū as an ordinary chivalrous rogue. Because several hundred daredevil youths were gathered tightly like a beehive, even the formidable Kenpei and constables hesitated to lay a single finger upon this mansion. The boss Aishū understood everything regarding Iwakō being the elder Ihaharuwake per his earnest plea, yet he kept it secret deep within his heart and uttered not a word to his other henchmen. Consequently, the numerous henchmen foully abused and harshly ordered about the senile old man Iwashū, creating a pitiable sight in the eyes of onlookers.

To turn to another matter, in the inner palace of Takasago Castle, five figures—the provincial governor Kuniyoriwake, Suekohime, Harunohime, Matsuwakahiko, and Isahiko—were seated around a table, holding a vital conference regarding some matter.

Kuniyori: 'Matsuwakahiko-dono, despite holding an advanced age, thy sincerity in bustling with state affairs day and night without thought of food or sleep is truly admirable to the utmost. Both Suekohime and I constantly express our gratitude for thy most sincere actions, talking of thee before all else. Yet that today, upon thy request, I have come to deliberate with both of you alongside my wife and child is, after all, divine providence. Today I shall listen seriously, so state your opinions without reserve, both of you.'

Matsuwakahiko wore a pleased smile.

Matsuwakahiko: 'Hearing my lord's unprecedented words, this aged body feels as though it has been revived for the first time. Though it is truly difficult to speak of, as the whereabouts of the heir Kuniteruwake-sama have become unknown, I, a humble servant holding a position of great responsibility, have no excuse before my lord, nor any face to meet the common people. Therefore, though I am deeply sorry to my lord, I secretly dispatched searchers throughout the city to conduct a search, yet to this day his whereabouts remain unknown. Truly, the crime of inadequate supervision deserves ten thousand deaths; thus, taking this responsibility upon myself, I, Matsuwakahiko, wish to resign from the post of Great Elder and cede all government affairs to Isahiko-dono. I pray thee grant your gracious sanction to this matter, for which I shall be deeply grateful.'

Kuniyori: 'Ha-ha-ha-ha-ha! I have known for a long time that my son concealed his whereabouts. There is no need to worry particularly. However much one is a parent, one cannot restrain even the thoughts of one's child. Do not worry about that matter in the least.'

Thus he laughed with utter composure. Contrary to Matsuwakahiko's expectation that he would incur the governor's wrath and be thundered at like lightning, he was utterly dumbfounded by such an exceedingly calm attitude, and could find no words to reply.

Isahiko: 'My lord, that such a grave problem has erupted is entirely due to the offense of us humble servants; we humbly beg for strict punishment.'

Kuniyori: 'He who leaves is not pursued, and he who comes is not rejected. No matter how much one tries to detain them, those who think only of fleeing are hopeless. It seems my son has become a relatively great man, as he has grown weary of this cramped birdcage. And while the two of you request permission to resign, if both of you become ghosts, what do you intend to do about the country's governance afterward? Recommend successors, make preparations so that there will be no hindrance to future state affairs, and only then request your resignation. Have you finished selecting your successors? That is the foremost prerequisite problem.'

話替つて高砂城の大奥には、国依別の国司を始め、末子姫、春乃姫、松若彦、伊佐彦の五柱が卓を囲んで、何事か重要会議を開いてゐた。

国依『松若彦殿、爾も老齢の身を持ち乍ら、昼夜寝食を忘れて国務に鞅掌する其誠実は実に感歎の至りだ。予も末子姫も常に爾の至誠至実なる行動について時々感謝の意を表し、何よりも先に爾の噂をしてゐるのだよ。併し今日爾の請求に依つて、予は妻子と共に爾両人と何事か協議する運びに至つたのは、要するに神の摂理であらう。今日は真面目に予も耳を傾けるから、忌憚なく両人とも意見を吐露せよ』

松若彦は嬉しげに笑を泛べて、

松若『常になき吾君のお言葉、此老体も始めて甦つた如き心地が致しまする。誠に申上にくい事乍ら、御世子国照別様は御行衛が分らなくなりましたので、大責任の地位にある微臣、吾君に対して申訳なく、又衆生に対しても会はす顔が厶いませぬ。夫れ故吾君様には済まぬ事乍ら、内々探りを市中に配り、捜索致させましたなれど、今にお行衛が分りませぬ。誠に監督不行届の罪、万死に値致しますれば、松若彦は此責任を負ふて、大老職を拝辞し、一切の政務を伊佐彦殿に譲らうと存じまする。何卒々々此儀御聴許下さいますれば有難う存じまする』

国依『ハツハヽヽヽ、悴が行衛を晦ましたのは、予はとつくに存じてゐる。別に心配する必要はない。如何に親なればとて、吾子の思想迄束縛する訳にも行かない。其事については決して心配致すな』

と平然として笑つてゐる。松若彦は国司の怒に触れ、雷の如くに叱咤さるるかと思ひの外、余り平然たる態度に呆れ果て、返す言葉も出なかつた。

伊佐『吾君様、斯かる重要問題が突発致しましたのは、全く微臣等の罪の致す所、何卒厳重なる御処分を願ひたう厶ります』

国依『去る者は逐はず来る者は拒まずだ。何程引止めようとしても、逃げよう逃げようと考へてゐる者は駄目だ。悴も比較的大人物になつたと見えて、此狭苦しい鳥屋の中が厭になつたと見えるワイ。そして爾等両人骸骨を乞はむと申出て居るが、爾等両人が幽霊となれば後の国政は何う致す考へだ。後任者を推薦して、向後の国政上支障なき迄に準備を整へ、而して後骸骨を請へよ。後継者の物色は済んだのか、それが先づ先決問題だ』

§ 2

Matsuwaka: 'Yes, I am humbled. However, looking from the eyes of us who are ignorant in this land of Chin, there does not seem to be a single person suitable for the important posts of the state. Every official is struck by a high-collar-like mood, and I cannot find a single person who thinks of the state with sincerity. Truly, the future of the state is something I cannot help but feel anxious about.'

Kuniyori: 'Ah, so you are saying there is no suitable person for a successor? You two are truly people of extreme disloyalty and injustice. Get down!'

He let out a thunderous voice and scolded them severely. The two men shrank back, holding back their tears, and spoke in unison,

Both: 'Though we are unworthy, we are devoting our lives and risking our bodies for the sake of the master's house and the state, handling state affairs. Yet, regarding your words just now, we cannot accept being called disloyal and unjust. Even if you are the Kokushi, we cannot step back from this place even one step unless you clarify this matter to us.'

Kuniyori: 'Are you not deceiving me? While saying you cannot bear the duties of your old age or that you are resigning to take responsibility, did you not say there is no person who is a pillar of the state? To offer your resignation while knowing there is no successor is to threaten the Kokushi house entirely. No, it is to make me feel perplexed. Holding such a mentality, what is wrong with me calling you two disloyal and unjust?'

He shouted even more strongly, and the two men shrank back completely, unable to withstand it. Suetsuhime-sama saw this state and felt pity for them,

Suetsuhime-sama: 'My Lord, please wait a moment. Because those two think so much of the state, they must have resorted to such sophistry as this to urge you to make a decision. No matter what is said, they are the pillars of the Chin nation; please forgive their minor faults.'

Kuniyori: 'Although this is a situation that cannot be forgiven, if it is the mediation of my beloved Suetsuhime-dono, I have no choice. I shall blindly follow for now, ha-ha-ha-ha!'

The previous angry voice was gone, and he began to laugh with his mouth wide open as if he were at ease. The two men caught their breath and finally began to stretch out their shrunken testicles.

Matsuwaka: 'I am humbled to have said something inappropriate to you, my Lord, despite my age. I beg you to reconsider and listen to my words with your broad and benevolent heart, and I wish to be employed as before.'

Isa: 'I, your humble servant, likewise request that you continue to employ me.'

Kuniyori: 'Hmm, very well, very well. If you understand, I have no further complaints. From now on, do not scatter resignations that are not in your heart. Though, in your hearts, you two are likely still clinging to political power and cannot cast off that attachment, are you not?'

Both: 'Ha!' They turned red and hung their heads in silence.

Suetsuhime-sama: 'By the way, my Lord, the reason why the two elders requested your presence today is, as you are already aware, because the whereabouts of the heir, Kuniteruwake, are unknown. Therefore, the two elders have proposed that we make Harunohime the successor to stabilize the hearts of the people, high and low, and they wish to obtain your approval. Please think it over carefully, and we would like to follow whichever command you find convenient.'

Kuniyori-wake said indifferently,

Kuniyori-wake: 'Hmm, that is right. If Harunohime herself approves, that is fine. I cannot forcibly bend the will of the princess. It would be a shortcut to ask the princess herself about this problem.'

Suetsuhime-sama: 'Is it not unheard of for a woman to succeed to the throne? We must adopt someone, must we not? If we do that, will the Kokushi house, which should be eternal for ten thousand generations, not be severed?'

Kuniyori: 'Does the teaching of the San-go-kyo not show that a woman's reign is good? If you keep it as a female succession, it is passed from womb to womb, so there is not the slightest mistake in the lineage. If it were a male successor, in the case where the wife on one side had created a second husband without the husband knowing, it would become impossible to tell whose child the born child is. Therefore, a woman is actually more certain. In fact, even with Kuniteruwake, I do not know if he is my true seed or if Suetsuhime-dono secretly created a second husband and conceived that seed, ha-ha-ha-ha!'

Suekohime began to weep, and said:

Suekohime: 'Such heartless words from you, my lord. Do you truly have so little faith in me? Are you implying that I have committed adultery with someone? It is truly regrettable.'

She prostrated herself upon the ground and wept.

Kuniyori: 'Ha, ha, ha! It is a lie, a mere lie, I only used it as a metaphor. You are the goddess of the home, respected even as the deity of chastity. I do not doubt your conduct even by the width of a hair. On the contrary, I am deeply grateful to you. Come now, do not worry, it was only a metaphor.'

He stroked her back two or three times. Suekohime gradually regained her composure, and while wiping away her tears and smiles together with a handkerchief, she said:

Suekohime: 'Ho, ho, ho! I am relieved to hear that. In that case, my lord, you will trust me forever, will you not?'

Kuniyori: 'As the saying goes, a sparrow does not forget its nature until it is a hundred, and though we have now become a wrinkled old couple, I sometimes picture your elegant figure from the past in my heart and find myself in a state of bliss. Only at those times do I truly feel a sense of splendor.'

Suekohime: 'That must be different. Is it not Okatosama who is the true jewel that makes you recall the past and feel such splendor?'

Kuniyori: 'Hmm, Okato is also one of those I recall. However, the one that remains most vividly in my impression is still your beautiful and elegant figure at the time of our marriage, ha, ha, ha, ha!'

In front of their daughter and old retainers, the couple began to engage in innocent, playful banter. Matsuwakahiko and Isahiko were also drawn into the conversation, loosening the strings of their chins, and drool with strong viscosity hung down seven or eight sun, like a spider descending from the ceiling.

Kuniyori: 'Harunohime-sama, you have been listening to everyone's conversation from the beginning and must generally understand, but how about it, do you have no desire to become the successor?'

Harunohime: 'I do not want to. There is a proverb that says one should not become a person of great wealth in life. I do not wish for such unhappiness as being enshrined within a cramped cage, showered with insincere flattery, treated with cold respect, and spending my life as a puppet for two or three politicians. Watching the circumstances of you, Father, and Mother, I am truly moved to tears of sympathy for your pitiable situation. Thinking that my brother might also follow in your footsteps, Father, I felt unbearable pity for him. As expected of my brother, he said that being installed as a puppet for two or three politicians is not clever for a human being, so he caught the wind, fled somewhere, and is now in the happy position of roaming freely in the world of liberty. He is truly a wise brother. I am his sister, and I do not wish to know such a cramped and unfree status myself. I would like to ask for your pardon on this matter alone.'

Kuniyori: 'Ha, ha, ha! Is that so, is that so? Your father had also been prepared for this. It is heartless to forcibly suppress someone who dislikes it. It is not what a parent should do. It would be best to do as you like.'

Suekohime: 'Wait, my lord. If my brother Kuniteruwake has left home, and my daughter Harunohime says she does not want to be the successor, will the Kuniyori family not become extinct here? What kind of thinking do you have to say such carefree things? Even if it is pitiful, you must firmly persuade Harunohime, and for the sake of preserving the pillar of the nation, you must have her become the successor, whether she likes it or not.'

Kuniyori: 'Hmm, it is not limited to Harunohime. Since Matsuwakahiko also has a son and a daughter, how about I adopt Matsuwakahiko's son, Matsuyoriwake, and have him succeed? That, too, cannot be helped but to leave it to the person's own will. Matsuwakahiko, what do you think?'

Matsuwakahiko was surprised and said:

Matsuwakahiko: 'This is, this is... words I would not expect from you, my lord. There has never been an instance where a subject was made a lord. Please do not say such things, and I would like to beg Harunohime-sama to become the successor. Furthermore, how could my foolish son ever perform such a duty? On this matter alone, I must humbly decline.'

§ 4

Kuniyori: 'Leave everything to Kamunagara.'

Sueko: 'You always say Kamunagara, Kamunagara, and try to bury every problem, but such an important Jiueujiken will not proceed by Kamunagara alone.'

Kuniyori: 'Ah, that is exactly where Kamunagara lies. Even if we do not use the Chisuji of the Kuniyoriwake, whose Mitama are clouded, as an Okimono for the bed, there are surely three or five good-natured people among the Sanzenmannin who would want to become an Okimono. You need not worry about such things. ...Does it not appear in the Goshinyu that "one knows not what kind of Mitama has been cast down"? Whether we issue an Okimonoshōshifurei through Kuwanpau, or call a Shinbunkishiya to have it printed in the Kuōkokuran, there are any number of Hauhafu. If that does not work, we shall search for a person of good spirit through the power of Kami.'

He says this with a carefree air.

Sueko: 'That will not bring peace to the world. Even if a Hitsupugerau suddenly rises to a high position, the Shinyō of the Kokumin will not be maintained.'

Kuniyori: 'The Kokumin do not respect us as you think they do. Nor do they pay us respect from the bottom of their hearts just because you are a daughter born of our own flesh. Because we wrap ourselves in a Brahmanic hue, they pay us a sense of awe because they have no other choice. If you think such things, you will be mocked by the masses as a fool who is blind to the times, hahahahaha.'

Matsu-waka: 'Whatever you may say, now that it has come to this, we must beg Harunohime-sama.'

Haruno: 'I hate it, I hate it, please spare me.'

Isa: 'We must by all means beg the Hime-sama.'

Haruno: 'Ah, what a disagreeable old man. I said no once, and that means no, ...now Mother, Father, it is a sin to restrict a person's will, you know.'

Sueko: 'Whatever you may say, in this case, please display a spirit of sacrifice for the sake of the Kokushi family and the nation, and become the heir. It is your mother's lifelong request...'

Haruno: 'I have a request, and if you will grant it, I do not mind becoming the heir.'

Sueko: 'I will accept any demand you have, so you will become the heir, will you not? And what kind of request is this condition?'

Haruno: 'First, that I may enter and leave the castle freely, regardless of whether it is inside or outside.', 'Second, that I shall not be accompanied by ladies-in-waiting or strict warriors at my side.', 'Third, that I shall select my own husband.', 'Fourth, that depending on the circumstances, I may resign as heir and live an ideal life.', 'Fifth, that you shall grant pardon for crimes.', 'Sixth, that I shall have the real power to appoint and dismiss the Tairō, Rōjū, and those below them.', 'I would like you to ask my parents to agree to these conditions, roughly speaking.'

Kuniyori: 'Interesting, interesting, one must say you have grasped my intent. As expected of Harunohime, she is great. Even the two elders must have been defeated by this, hahahahahaha.'

Matsu-wakahiko and Isahiko, both reluctantly and having no other choice, accepted Harunohime's conditions and designated her as the heir. Sighing, they offered prayers of gratitude at the shrine, greeted the Kokushi couple with courtesy, and returned toward their own mansion.

On this day, there was not a single cloud in the blue sky, the light of the sun was exceptionally clear and red, a cool breeze blew gently, the voices of a hundred birds sounded very refreshing, the atmosphere all around was somehow exhilarating, subtle music echoed from the heavens, and fragrance permeated in all directions, as if one were in the Shihikyu of the First Heaven. Ah, Kamunagara Kamu-nagi-mae-se.
