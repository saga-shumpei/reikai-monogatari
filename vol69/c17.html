Chapter 17: 琴 Jewel [1762]

琴玉〔1762〕

§ 1

Under the divine grace of the Lord, the main castle of Takakurayama in the land of Hiru, a name itself auspicious, stood majestically upon the Kakihatokiha at the center of the capital, rooted deep in the Shitatsuihane; with the teachings of the Ananahi, the nation prospered ever more, and the clear stream of Higurashigaha flowed purely through the heart of the capital, providing convenience for traffic and transport, until it was truly called a paradise on earth.

Kahedewakenomikoto and Kiyokohime had one son and one daughter, Kunichikawake and Kiyokahime. Their ancestor, Kiyohiko, having received the divine virtue of Hinodenokami, had established what was known as the Inka nation (meaning descendants of the Sun God), becoming the object of reverence for all living beings. The people looked up to Kahedewakenomikoto as the governor, revered him as a great master, and felt close to him as a parent; with the upper and lower classes in harmony and without overly burdensome regulations, the nation flourished in extreme peace and tranquility. However, as transportation facilities developed from the Tokoyonokuni, various evil ideologies began to circulate, and this divine nation, which had manifested the unparalleled auspicious signs of paradise, now saw the hearts of its people gradually shaken; the teachings of individualism developed, many idle people appeared, and bad elders, bad middle-aged people, and youths filled the land from top to bottom, forgetting righteousness and running toward profit, until it became just like the state of Tokoyonokuni, where the governor was slighted, officials were despised, the hearts of the people deteriorated, and an atmosphere of anxiety filled the country. Kahedewakenomikoto and Kiyokohime prayed to the gods morning and evening, supplicating at the palace of the national spirit for the prosperity of the nation and the peace of the people. Before anyone knew it, the heir, Kunichikawakenomikoto, hid himself and went missing. Starting with the couple Kahede, the two elders Akiyama-wake and Morris furrowed their brows and urged the subordinate officials to search every corner of the country, but there was no clue to be found. Thus, having no other choice, they held a grand council, which resulted in the decision to make the younger sister, Kiyokahime, the heir to the land of Hiru.

Kiyokahime, like her brother, knew that the times were day by day becoming unfavorable to the bourgeois class, and she was racking her brains on how to save her nation. However, her parents, who were of the old school, and the old retainers, who were blind to the times, opposed Kiyokahime's opinions one by one, and they were never adopted. Thinking of the future of the nation, Kiyokahime could not sleep well at night, and she prayed to the gods, devoting her heart only to sweeping away the evil clouds hanging over the nation and opening a new heaven and earth, while her body grew thin and weak day by day.

The old retainers Morris and Akiyama-wake were huddled together in the council chamber of the castle, whispering about something with worried looks.

Akiyama: 'Morris-dono, what do you think of the Princess's condition these days?'

Morris: 'Indeed, I am worried that it might be a sickness of the mind, judging by appearances. I suppose your thoughts are the same, that it is a sickness of the mind?'

Akiyama: 'Indeed, that must be it. Thinking back now, you and I have a history of falling in love with Benihime-sama and Erina-sama, and eventually meeting with the disaster of the Season River; so it is only natural that a beautiful woman of marriageable age would suffer from lovesickness. We must welcome a suitable adopted son as soon as possible to comfort the Princess's heart. The Princess always tells us that we are dull old men, but now that I think about it, it might have been a riddle meaning that she wanted us to find her a husband quickly, and that we were dull fellows... unlike us who suffered in love, it was truly a foolish oversight on our part.'

Thus, the two of them, without a second thought, turned their attention to such a strange matter.

Akiyama: 'Even so, we must choose a suitable adopted son, but how about we ask the Princess directly?'

Morris: 'Surely, we cannot ask her, "Who would you like to have as your husband?"... that would be too rude, would it not?'

Akiyama: 'However, why don't we scout two or three candidates, take their photographs, and leave them scattered in the Princess's living room? If the Princess likes them, she will likely tuck them away in her desk drawer, and given her temperament, she will surely tear up or ink over the photos she does not like. Then, after testing the Princess's heart, let us try to probe her indirectly; I believe this is the essential duty of an old retainer.'

Morris: 'Naruhodo, then I shall proceed to collect photographs of the sons of the Seikaren, who are raised in respectable families among my subordinates. What a splendid idea I have hit upon. Kamunagara Chihahe Mase.'

With this, he stands up with enthusiasm, and as the sun has gradually set, the two old men return to their own homes.

Changing the scene, Kiyokahime is strolling through the garden within the castle with her maid, and while plucking two or three dahlia flowers to hold in her hand, she returns to her own room. Upon looking, she sees four or five photographs of stylish, high-collar men lined up on her desk. The moment she sees them, Kiyokahime sends the maid away, seals the sliding doors, and upon looking closely, finds them to be portraits of the sons of the Guwanmeikorōha Seika. Kiyokahime inspects each photograph one by one, and writes a poem in black ink over each of them.

'The more I look at this figure, the more repulsive it seems; it must be a ghost from the land of the abyss.'

On another photograph:

'A face that looks like it was made with a mallet and chisel; I wish to strike it now and tear it to pieces.'

Facing yet another photograph:

'Born with the form of a man, yet how pathetic it is to resemble a woman.'

On another photograph:

'Looking at any of them, I feel sad, thinking that not even one possesses a true soul.'

On the final photograph:

'Though you seem slightly more masculine, you are not the soul that shall become my husband.'

Having written these lampoons, she puts them into an envelope, writes 'To the elders Akiyama-wake and Morris' on the front, and claps her hands to summon her maid. The maid, Haruko, quietly pushes open the sliding door.

Haruko: 'Kiyokahime-sama, is there some business for which you have summoned me?'

Kiyokahime: 'Haru, I am sorry to trouble you, but please take this and deliver it to Akiyama-wake and Morris. And there is no need to wait for a reply, so once you have handed it over, come straight back.'

Haruko says, 'Hai, I understand,' and departs with quick steps. Afterward, Kiyokahime seals the room, takes out a two-stringed koto, and sings her reflections with a calm heart.

'I am the daughter of the governor of Takakura Castle, in the land of Hiru where there is no night. Born as Kiyokahime, raised alongside my brother, the Mikoto, cherished like the moon and flowers, my life was free of any hardship on summer evenings. Clad in a cool yukata, enjoying boat trips on the Shigure River, such is the karma of one raised in the upper-class society where nothing is lacking. Like a bird blind to the world, unable to clearly perceive the state of the times, I feared that the land of Hiru would eventually become a dark road of night. Thinking of the future of my dear father and the fate of my mother, my brother and I illuminate each other's hearts to save them. Following the currents of the world, to save the endangered nation,'

'While praying to the gods and waiting, how joyful it is that the time has come. My brother, the Mikoto, has quickly escaped from this mansion, treading upon the frost morning and evening, tasting the hardships of the world. I, a weak woman, in place of my brother, though I rack my brains a thousand times to protect this land of the gods alone, the sentiments of my father and mother, who cannot shed their old ways, and the old retainers like Akiyama-wake, and the Guwanmeikorōha Morris and his kind, treasure the inorganic matter they call Seika as the highest thing. The politics of the country decline day by day like ice in the sun, and it is sad to see the land of Hiru, entrusted by the gods, being buried away.'

'And whose mischief is this? A hateful man with a dark spirit, like white clouds, has captured my heart and mind, and has displayed these strange figures, lining them up on my writing desk. The terror of such ugly schemes, I suspect, must be the work plotted by Akiyama-wake and Morris. If it has come to this, I cannot live in this mansion for even a moment. To be caught in the hands of the tempting demons would be a great disaster. When shall I ever accomplish the divine work I vowed to my brother? Taking advantage of the darkness of this evening, I shall prepare everything, and without taking even a maid, I, a lone woman, shall proceed to the land of Chin. The mountains are steep and the rivers are deep, the storms are strong and the rain is heavy.'

'Even if there are many demons, my three-five Great God will surely love the sincerity that cares for this world, and will surely establish the hopes of my brother and me. How fleeting is my body as I leave this mansion, making this evening the limit. Ah, my dear father and mother, please remain safe and wait for the completion of the divine work of my brother and me. After I am gone, the Guwanmeikorōha old retainers will surely be in a panic and uproar. Seeing that state before my eyes, the pity flickers in my mind, and the autumn sky, wrapped in the darkness of the eternal night, makes such sad thoughts arise.'

'Ah, Kamunagara-Himesama, May the Mitama grant blessing, And may the declared words of Kiyokahime Be fulfilled most peacefully and calmly, Thus I pray. Though the morning sun shines or clouds over, Though the moon waxes or wanes, Unmoved should remain the land of Hiru. Though the earth quakes, mountains split, rivers overflow, And high tsunamis attack, This castle, built eternally Upon the deep bedrock below, Shall never be shattered for a thousand, for eight thousand generations. Ah, yet nevertheless, nevertheless, What shall be done with these shiyujiyō? The kōzui of shisau overflows, And the teibau of the Higurashigaha Is on the verge of hōkuwai. Even while beholding this sanjiyō, They still remain waiting in taizen calm. Oh, the foolishness of the raushin! Were it not for us otodoi, The capital of Hiru and the shiyujiyō Would instantly plunge into the abyss of Jigoku Of Shiyura and chikushiyō. Protect us, O Kamunagara, Thus I pray unto the Kami with all my heart.'

Thus she was singing with all her might. There, from outside the sliding door, both Akiyamawake and Morris called out at once, 'Himesama-Himesama!' The Hime hurriedly stopped playing the koto, and with a feigned innocence,

Kiyokahime: 'Is that voice not Akiyamawake and Morris-dono? I do not know what business you have, but open the sliding door and enter.'

The two of them, overjoyed by the Hime's words as if finding a boat at a crossing, rubbing their hands together, opened the sliding door and entered, making a polite bow, yet fidgeting as they hesitated to say whatever it was they had to say.

Kiyoka: 'You surely received the items I had Haruko bring to you a moment ago, did you not?'

Akiyama: 'Yes, we have certainly viewed them. Regarding that, there is something we wish to ask Himesama: those five photographs are the famed men of Hiru, wise men renowned as the Five Selected Men of the land of Hiru, whether in status, lineage, learning, or handsome appearance. Knowing Himesama is at a ripe age, we thought it inappropriate to speak too bluntly, so after consulting with Morris, we quietly gathered the photographs to seek your pleasure. However, Himesama, you carelessly wrote poems in pitch-black ink on the front of the photographs, and we cannot grasp their meaning at all. Therefore, if you would kindly tell us your true intentions without restraint, we two shall handle the matter in whatever way you wish.'

Kiyokahime, knowing that she had to escape this place smoothly tonight no matter what, quickly realized that making them angry and tightening the guard would be disadvantageous, so she intentionally played dumb and feigned ignorance,

Kiyoka: 'Ho-ho-ho-ho-ho! How embarrassing! Please let me think about it slowly, won't you?'

Akiyama: 'Thinking about it is fine indeed, but if the marriage issue is not settled as quickly as possible, the duty of us old vassals will not be fulfilled. I have numbered them on the back as Number 1, Number 2, and so on, so could Himesama not just tell us from your own mouth which number it is?'

Kiyoka: 'Well now! Number 1 is fine, Number 2 is fine, Number 3, Number 4, or Number 5 are all fine; any of them is fine! Ho-ho-ho-ho-ho!'

Morris: 'Himesama, it troubles us if you give such an ambiguous reply! Please state clearly which number it is!'

Kiyoka: 'Ho-ho! In deciding a matter of a lifetime, five numbers are not enough, are they? Please gather about five more numbers, and then I will give you my answer, won't I?'

Morris: 'Himesama, are you saying this is still not enough? These are already first-rate! The rest will be second-rate, so they will certainly not be to your liking!'

Thus he spoke, just like a haberdasher setting up his shop.

Kiyoka: 'At any rate, since it was too sudden today to decide, I will pick the right one within tomorrow and give you my answer. I rely on both of you to handle things well before my father and mother!'

Akiyamawake and Morris, thinking, "Oh, how wonderful, now we are relieved," put on smiling faces, flattering profusely to curry favor, and scratching their heads two or three times,

Both: 'Himesama, if that is the case, we eagerly await your answer as soon as possible.'

Leaving behind those words, they quickly departed from the scene. Kiyokahime smiled knowingly, pulled the koto close once more, and began to sing her full heart out.

秋山『ハイ、確に拝見致しました。それに就て姫様に御伺ひ致したいので厶いますが、あの五枚の写真はヒルの国に於ては、地位といひ門閥と云ひ、学問といひ器量といひ、最も選抜された、ヒルの国の五人男といはれてゐる賢明な名を取つた名物男で厶ります。姫様も良い年頃、余り露骨に申上げるも如何と存じ、モリスと相談の上ソツト写真を集めて御意を伺つた次第で厶います。然るに姫様は無造作に、写真の表に墨くろぐろと歌をお書きになりましたが、一向其意を得ませぬので、どうぞ御心の在る所を忌憚なく仰せ聞け下さらば、吾々両人が如何様とも取計らふで厶りませう』

清香姫は何と云つても今晩は都合よく此場を逃出さねばならぬのだから、余り怒らして警戒を厳にさせては却て不利益と早くも合点し、ワザと空呆けて、

清香『ホツホヽヽヽ、恥しいワ、どうかゆつくり考へさして頂戴、ねえ』

秋山『御考へなさるも結構で厶いませうが、一時も早く結婚問題をきめなくては、吾々老臣の役が済みませぬ。私が裏に一号二号と番号をつけておきましたから、姫様のお口から、一寸何号だといふ事を仰有つて下さいませぬか』

清香『さうだなア、一号でもよし、二号でもよし、三号でも四号でも五号でもよしだ、どうでもよしだ、ホヽヽヽヽ』

モ『モシ姫様、そんなアヤフヤの御返辞をされちや困るぢやありませぬか。何号なら何号とハツキリ言つて下さいませ』

清香『ホヽヽ、一生（升）の事を定めるのに、五号（合）では足らぬぢやないか、モウ五合許り集めて来て下さい、そしたら返辞をするからねえ』

モ『姫様、之でまだ足らないと仰有るのですか、之はモウ第一流ですよ。後はモウ第二流になりますから、迚もお気に入りませぬワ』

と、丸で小間物屋が店出しをしてるやうな事を云つてゐる。

清香『とも角、今日は余り咄嗟の事で決まらないから、明日中に、之といふのをきめて御返事をする。両人共、お父さまお母さまの手前、宜しく頼んだぞや』

秋山別モリスの両人は、ヤレ嬉しや、之で一安心と笑顔をつくり追従タラタラ機嫌を取り乍ら、頭を二つ三つ掻いて、

両人『姫様、左様ならば、一時も早く御返事をお待ち申上げます』

と言葉を残してスタスタと此場を去つて了つた。清香姫はニタリと笑ひ、又もや琴を取よせて思ひの丈を歌ひ始めけり。
