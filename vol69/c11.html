Chapter 11: 気転 Messenger [1756]

気転使〔1756〕

§ 1

In the very Mannaka of Akagirekōen at the Chiushin of Miyakōoji, a Furaukaikifu Daienzetsukuwai had begun. Amata of Torishimari secured the Deguchi and Iriguchi, and Kakusode turned out by the Sūsennin in a Wariahi of roughly one to every Hitori of the Chiyōshiu, exhibiting a Monomono display of Keikai. Bulldog, the Kuwaichiyō of the Furaukaikifu Enzetsukuwai, with a voice like the Haukau of a Shishi,

'What are the Torishimari? What are the Hafuki?'

—with a spirit as if to roar thus, he was commencing the Ketsushiteki Daienzetsukuwai.

Bulldog: 'O Shiyokun, O Shiyokun, listen well! The world hath become Tokoyami; Though Jitsugetsu in the sky Kuōkuō Shine across, what can be done? Gathered clouds block the Chiukū, And while wrapping and concealing the light That shineth so Kuōkuō, The world, endlessly like harvested rushes, Falleth into miserable disorder. If one asketh where the cause Of such disorder lies, Though I think it a matter Which Shiyokun already know, Here and now I shall unleash my speech. The evil deities who have blocked The divine land of equality for all four classes— With Matsuwakahiko and Isahiko at the head, And other various officials and rich men— They, in order to satisfy their position and self-interest,

Are blind fools who do not comprehend Even in a dream the troubles of all living beings; Using authority as a shield, while wringing The sweat and fat from all living beings, They fill their storehouses—how hateful! We, the living beings, cry in hunger, Freeze in the cold, and having not even A thatched cottage wherein to lay our head, As we stand and sleep by the roadside, The arrogant Torishimari, Spitting out words of violating Hafuki, Throw every last one of us into prison, Inflicting infinite humiliation—how hateful! Yet even a winter night of falling frost and piling snow Is not an eternal thing; Surely flowers will bloom and breezes grow fragrant, The waters will warm, and of trees and plants A hundred, a thousand blossoms will bloom.

Just as the joyful spring cometh, Surely upon our bodies A rain of grace must fall. Nevertheless, unless we cross The desolate wild plain where the winter blast bloweth wildly, How could we bathe in the exceedingly lovely Warm light of the spring fields? Clear away the trunks and cut the branches Of the deeply rooted tall trees! Because there are ugly tall trees That cover and hide the light of the sun and moon, The various grasses swarming upon the ground Have their dew of grace cut off, And having the divine light hidden from them, Are forever dwellers in the shade. Though growing upon the same ground, We who have found no place Are useless wretches all our lives. To break through this difficult barrier,

The sole hope is to chop off and discard The branches and leaves of the tall trees Standing to seal off the heavens; There can be no other means. Rouse yourselves, arise, O all people! No matter what oppression may come, Though Jutte whips attack in great numbers Like miscanthus reeds, We who have cast away our lives as a target— How could we be afraid? I, Bulldog, who transmit unto thee The divine voice of Heaven— To put it another way, the Savior— Lend thy ears to my Kototama, Hear it out completely and in full detail, And awaken thy sleeping eyes! In a world so clouded as this, What are the gods and Buddhas doing? As I surmise, even what is called God And what is called Buddha

Are nothing more than temporary expedients for the masses. We shall no longer face the world Holding up gods and Buddhas at the forefront; Absolute atheism! We recognize neither god nor Buddha; We take only the physical strength we possess As our sole power. Take courage, take courage, O all people! Assenting to my Kototama, Those who wish to participate In the work of saving this world, Without faltering or fearing, Ascend the platform and express your conviction! If this world is left as it is, We, the weak of the world, Have no choice but to perish. All final solutions Depend solely on luck, tenacity, and dull persistence!'

So he declaimed. The Torishimari shouted loudly, 'We order you to stop and disperse!' Speakers one after another ascended the platform ignoring the restraints of the Torishimari, each venting their heat. The Torishimari attempted to drag them down. Instantly, a grand brawl was enacted between the Sūsennin of Torishimari and the Sūsennin of Chiyōshiu, and by whose mischief it was unknown, thick black smoke rose billowing from towns here and there, and the sound of alarm bells ringing wildly—chan, chan, chan—resounded. Both the Torishimari and the crowd reached the extremity of panic, scattering right and left, knowing not what to do. Thereupon, riding on horseback, Aishū, Genshū, Heishū, and Tōshū commanded Amata of subordinates, organized a fire brigade, extinguished every last bit of the flaring fires, blew their trumpets, and leisurely withdrew. The fires being ended and the disturbance somewhat settled, the fire pump of the Torishimari office arrived, protected by Amata of Torishimari. The crowd that had once fled and scattered gathered again in Akagirekōen, and the Enzetsukuwai was held once more. Speakers took turns delivering impassioned speeches, spouting their own heat demanding the overthrow of the Isahiko cabinet, the extermination of the Mochimaru class, the destruction of the Seike class, and the like. The crowd increased in number by the moment, clamoring and roaring continuously, until the speakers' voices could no longer be heard. The Torishimari also gradually increased in number, surrounding them tenfold and twentyfold, producing here a second scene of carnage. This conflict reached the absolute extreme of violence; cries of Avīci hell arose in all directions, presenting a spectacle almost like a battlefield, with no telling when it might end. Both the Kenpei and the Torishimari had their weapons taken by all living beings and were driven into a tight corner, trapped without any way of escape. Just then, riding a white horse and wearing a veil, there came forth a woman singing a song of propagation in a cool, clear voice.

原文（近代文語）

『取締何者ぞ、法規何者ぞ』

と云はむ許りの勢を以て、決死的大演説会を始めてゐる。

と述べ立てる。取締は「中止解散を命ず」と大声叱呼する。弁士は次から次へと取締の制止を聞かず登壇して各自に熱をふく。取締は引摺り落さうとする。忽ち数千の取締と数千の聴衆との間に大格闘を演じ、何者の悪戯か、彼方此方の町々より、黒煙濛々と立上り、チヤン　チヤン　チヤンと警鐘乱打の声聞え来る。取締も群衆も狼狽の極に達し右往左往に散乱して為す所を知らなかつた。其処へ馬に跨つて、愛州、源州、平州、藤州は数多の部下を指揮し、消防隊を組織して、燃え上る火災を残らず消しとめ、喇叭を吹いて悠々として引あげて了つた。火事もすみ、騒動も稍おさまつた所へ、取締所のポンプが数多の取締に保護されてやつて来た。一旦逃げ散つた群衆は又もや赤切公園に集まり来り、再び演説会が開催された。弁士は代る代る熱弁を揮ひ、伊佐彦内閣倒壊、持丸階級の討滅、清家階級を打破せよなど勝手な熱を吹き立てる。群衆は刻々其数を増し、ワイワイとどよめき亘り、弁士の声も遂に耳に入らなくなつて了つた。取締も亦次第々々に其数を増し、十重二十重に取まいて、茲に第二の修羅場を演出した。今回の闘争は最も激烈を極め、阿鼻叫喚の声四方に起り、殆ど戦場の如き景況を呈し、何時果つべしとも見当がつかなかつた。賢平も取締も武器を衆生に取られ進退維れ谷まり、逃場を失ひ困つて居る。そこへ白馬に跨り、被面布を被り乍ら、声も涼しく宣伝歌を歌つて出て来た女性がある。

§ 2

Matsuyohime: 'Departing from the Oreon Seiza, descending to the Nakatsukuni of Toyoashihara, to the capital of Chin, I am the messenger of Kami, Matsuyohime. To save this world, I have appeared astride a white horse, to enlighten all Shiyujiyō. Be silent, all of you, be silent! You are all the precious children of Chin, wrapped in the dew of the Sumekami's grace. What kind of misunderstanding is it for brothers to fight within the fence? When I look down upon this world from far above in Ten with holy eyes, neither those who stand above nor the Tamigusa who dwell below are good. You persist in your own stubbornness, thinking of Meiri and Butsuyoku above all else, and without reflecting upon your own souls, which is the Kanjin, you have manifested a miserable Shiyurajiyō here. All of you, calm your hearts and listen to the Oshi of Kami. By the Mikoto of Konohanahime, who has appeared on Tenkyōzan, I have now come to save the Shiyujiyō of the Mikuni of Chin and lead them to the Tengoku Jiyōdo. The policies of Isahiko-shi, starting with Matsuwakahiko, are reckless in the extreme, never considering the times. The state of mind of the lower masses is also the retribution for forgetting Kami and forgetting the whereabouts of their own souls, which is the Kanjin. Humans are the children of Kami, the palaces of Kami; spirits descend from the Amatsukuni, fearing the heart of Kami, and are born as people of this world. While possessing such physical bodies, what is this state of affairs? This is not the conduct of human beings. Are you tigers, wolves, bears, or boars? Or are you the descendants of the eight-headed serpent? The wretchedness of exposing such indescribable ugliness to heaven and earth! Repent, all people. Without distinction between high and low, without separation between noble and base, unite your hearts and strength, and protect the spiritual land that Kami has bestowed upon the country of Chin, making it firm and eternal. Now, now, farewell, now, now, farewell! I shall now part the Yaekumo and ascend to Ten. If there are any Shiyujiyō who defy the words of Kami, the punishment of Kami shall descend upon them instantly. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, I have now finished conveying the heart of the Sumekami.'

Saying this, she whipped her horse and rode off, turning clouds and mist toward the summit of Mount Asahara. The female warrior who had just appeared, calling herself Matsuyohime, was in reality Tokiwahime, the daughter of Matsuwakahiko. Tokiwahime had conspired with Harunohime, and had appeared by employing a clever ruse, disguising herself as a messenger of Kami, in order to pacify the temporary disturbance.

Kenpei, the authorities, the crowd, the impostors, those who believed in Kami, and those who did not, were all stunned by the appearance of the beautiful woman. Their fierce momentum was sapped, they ceased their fighting, and simply watched in a daze as the mysterious woman rode off toward Mount Asahara.

To such a place, there came a woman dressed in straw rain gear, a hat, and leggings, wearing a cloth over her face, singing a song of propaganda with a clear voice as she advanced.

Harunohime: 'Kami has appeared in the world, bringing order to the three thousand worlds. All of this world is the paradise created by Kami of heaven and earth. The people born on this land, without distinction between noble and base, should be intimate and friendly with one another, and the treasures bestowed by Kami should be shared and distributed equally among all; such is the divine law. If one side piles up treasures high, the other side will inevitably lack and become low. If there are those who enjoy themselves on one side, there will be those who suffer on the other. This cannot be called peace. Now, the fate of Ten has turned, and deep within the Takasago Castle, the saving Kami has appeared. I am Harunohime. To save the suffering of these Shiyujiyō, I have cast aside all treasures morning and evening, showing an example, wearing straw gear, and touring the towns to preach sincerity. However, the Seike-kaikyu Mochimaru are blinded by greed and do not awaken. They have stopped their ears, and have reached the point where they cannot even understand the screams of the Shiyujiyō's lamentations. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, there is no other way now but to rely on the hands of Kami and open the world of dawn. Awaken, awaken, people of all classes, high and low! Fearing the Mikoto of the Sumekami, I have descended from the Amatsukuni and become the child of Kuniyoriwake. I was raised in the castle until now, but the time of Ten has finally come, and I have appeared as a pillar of Kami to preach and teach you Shiyujiyō. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, there are no two words in the language of Kami. Repent and reform.'

Having said this, she hid her figure like smoke, no one knew where. The crowd, with one voice, spoke of Harunohime and could not cease their words of admiration. The injured officials and the Shiyujiyō alike returned to their homes without a single word of complaint. Will the hearts of the Shiyujiyō truly be calmed by the appearance of Harunohime from now on, and will the roots of the conflict between the authorities and the Shiyujiyō be severed?

と云ひ乍ら、馬に鞭ち浅原山の山頂目がけて雲を霞みと駆り行く。今現はれた松代姫と称する女武者は其実松若彦の娘常磐姫であつた。常磐姫は春乃姫と諜し合せ、奇智を弄して天使と化け込み、一時の擾乱を平定せしめむが為に現はれたのである。

賢平も取締も群集も酒偽者も、神を信ずる者も信じ無い者も、麗しき美人の出現に胆を潰し、猛り切つたる勢を削がれ、争闘の手を止めて、只茫然と浅原山を指して逃げ行く怪しき女の姿を見送つて居た。

斯かる所へ蓑笠草鞋脚絆の扮装にて、被面布を被り乍ら、声も涼しく宣伝歌を歌ひつつ進み来る女がある。

と云つたきり、煙の如く何処とも無く姿を隠した。群衆は異口同音に春乃姫と聞いて感歎の言葉を絶た無かつた。負傷した役人も衆生も、一言の叱言も云はず各家路に帰り行く。果して今後は春乃姫の出現に依りて衆生の心が鎮静し、取締と衆生との争闘の根が断たれるであらうか。
