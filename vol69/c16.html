Chapter 16: Waves 動 [1761]

波動〔1761〕

§ 1

Kuniteruwake and Itsukau plucked the fruits of the surroundings, endured their hunger, practiced the misogi of three days and three nights, paid homage to the sacred and time-honored sanctuary of the Mirror Pond, and chanted the Sendenka in clear voices.

Kuniteruwake: 'A - The morning sun is pure and bright, I - Shining through the land of Teru, U - Leaving the sorrows of the floating world behind, E - The birth-destined Mitama, O - Reside in peace within the Kakehashigoten, Ka - Quietly and peacefully, Ki - The Kiko-shimesu sincere hearts', Ku - Kuniteruwake's prayer, Ke - Radically reforming the humble and crooked paths, Ko - To the prosperous and inexhaustible Land of Treasures, Sa - Presided over by Kunitamaōmikami, Shi - Chanting the cool and sacred Futonorito, Su - For the sake of the world, Se - Rescuing the culled Mitama, So - Saving and quickening them, Ta - The pine teachings of Takasago flourishing for a thousand autumns, Chi - Without a cloud in the midair, Tsu - Shining and clearing away the darkness, Te - Saving and leading all the way to the eternal land. Na - Floating upon the waves, Takasago's Ni - Land of Treasures to the west and east, Nu - Even my soul, which should become the master, Ne - Until it removes the sorrows of the bottom country, No - There is nothing to do but act decisively; Ha - Swiftly renewing the Mitama, Hi - Manifesting the frost of vast and noble divine grace to the world, Fu - And governing the people in peace, He - The land of treasures of high renown, Ho - With the light of the sole true Ananahi path casting no shadows, Ma - Restoring it to the divine age of old, Mi - With the dew of grass rich in divine grace, Mu - Countless kami and mortals rising up in courage, Me - Peaceful, joyful, and forevermore, Mo - Flourishing in life for the sake of the country, Ya - And for the sake of saving the gloomy underworld, Yu - Praying that eternal and infinite life may be granted. Yo - Before the great front of our Kototama, Wa - If we approach and shine through, Wi - Scooping away the clouds of the floating world, We - Behold the faces of the kami filled with smiles, Wo - Pray with reverence, Kannagara. Kuniteruwake, reflecting the state of the world's good and evil in the Mirror Pond, Before the sacred dwelling of the jeweled shrine, Awe-struck and trembling with reverence, we offer up our prayer.'

Thus they finished chanting, clapped their hands, sat down upon a nearby boulder, and shifted to tales of ancient history.

Kuniteruwake: 'Hey, boys, this Mirror Pond is also called the Kototama Pond, and it is the most mystical sacred site on Takasago Island. They say that if you shoot forth Kototama from here with a sincere heart, the kami will surely answer you with Kototama. What do you say? Since we've just purified our bodies at the waterfall, shall we ask the kami?'

Asakai: 'That sounds utterly delightful. Let us ask the kami here to determine the ranking of the boys. That would be fair and leave no lingering grudges between us, you know.'

Kuniteruwake: 'That is also splendid. In that case, Asakō, why don't you pray to the kami first?'

Asakai: 'Yes, understood.'

So saying, he clapped his hands and bowed twice,

Asakai: 'Kannagara, if the kami of old still dwell here, Show me and manifest my prayer. Since this is a consecrated site known by the name of the Kototama Pond, Answer me in the leaves of my words.'

Suddenly the Mirror Pond began to bubble up with strange, unusual foam.

Kuniteruwake and the others straightened their collars at this prompt response, holding their breath in awe. Asakai also began to feel uneasy, but unable to retreat now, cold sweat streaming from his brow,

'Ah, how wondrous, how awe-inspiring! O living kami of the Mirror Pond, Asakō the chivalrous man has come early in the morning, a fool bustling about and offering up an unworthy prayer; yet please, without stirring up trouble, simply and roughly as it may be, make your divine virtue manifest!'

From the Mirror Pond came a voice:

'Ha ha ha ha! Asakō, you shallow-witted fool, your open mouth won't even close!'

Asakai: 'I-I-I can't stand this! Where in the world is there a kami who scolds people right from the very start? U-u-u-un, without finding it annoying, please listen to my prayer in earnest! It's enough if you just let us know who is to be above and who is to be below among Lord's boys.'

Having groaned this, the voice ceased, and not another word was spoken.

Komaji: 'Kami of the Mirror Pond, thank you very much. What you have said is most fitting. You have spoken exactly as I thought. Kannagara Tamachi Haemaese.'

Asa: 'Oi, Kunishū, this Kami requires a bit of samani. It is likely that your deputy guardian or someone of the sort jumped out and spat that out. Eh, it is disgusting; no matter what anyone says, I am the first branch.'

Komaji: 'It is precisely because you are the first branch that you are useless. Look at a pine tree; the first branch withers and the second branch forms, the second withers and the third forms, and one after another, more magnificent branches grow out, do they not? Well, in any case, let us leave it as the Kami has said.'

Kuni: 'Ha-ha-ha, first of all, if you doubt the divine decree of the Kami to this extent, try testing your jitsuriyoku. Since it will not do to have you both scarred by a kenkuwa, let us hold a sumō match before the Kami and make the winner the primary kobun. Asakō, you are satisfied with this, are you not? Considering what happened the other night, you cannot be too arrogant, can you?'

The two of them wrestled a match, and it was finally determined that Komaji was the first branch of the kunishū, and in high spirits, they descended the mountain and emerged onto the coast of Takatori Village.

§ 3

Kuniteruwake and his party rested for a breath on the coast of Takotorimura, gazing at the scenery of the boundless unabara, and sang cheerfully.

Kuniteruwake: 'Is that a cloud, a mountain, or the surging waves? The salt waves of the boundless senri, with the shallow eyes of ningen, I face the ocean of Taisei. Ten jifuri, twenty, thirty jifuri, though my shisen barely stretches, what can I do, beyond the sea, the shape of the drifting country is not seen, how sad! White sails crossing to and fro are like hanabira, amidst the waves, shining purely. I am now standing on the isobe, in the kuōdaimuhen tenchi I shrink in kiyokuseki, and the body of ningen, so utterly fugahi, I deeply, deeply lament. Ah, I now, leaving my well-used kokoku, for the first time meet the waves of this vast kaiyō. Though the strange country is vast, can it equal this unabara? Where the clouds of the ōzora and the waves of the unabara ahisetsu, surely there must be a lovely haukoku. Ah, when I think, when I think, oh feeble body of ningen! The magnificent phenomena of tenchi, the providence of the universe, now anew my breast begins to throb. Where on earth is the capital of Hiru that I aim for? Might it be right beneath thoae distant crimson clouds? Or perhaps, surpassing even that, far, far away beneath the low clouds, is it located? When I think of it, my future is extremely ryōan. Leading four attendants, through the limitless wilderness I go. Oh, the height of the waves in my heart! Nay, the tumult of my breast! Calm them down, O Kunichiratsu Ōkami, who art the ruler of tenchi, and O Kami Susanoo-no-Mikoto, who possessest the land of Arakane! The sails are white, the waves are high, the sky is wide, the clouds are low, protect the future of us five!'

Having finished chanting thus, Kuniteruwake took the lead, and while loudly singing the ancient traditional propaganda song, advanced further and further north along the Teru Country Highway.
