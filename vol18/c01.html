Chapter 1: Spring Field の Journey [629]

春野の旅〔629〕

§ 1

Warm is the wind, and layered is the mist, Haruhi and the aunt seem about to darken, Enfolding in crimson the Yayohizora; The hazy moon is in the Chiuten, Neither shining nor clouded, but dimly suspended, At such evening dusk of a Yamaga; The flow of the river is Sōsō, Resounding across the village of Wachi; Stand in a line, sealing the sky, Along the path beneath the Raujiyu, Trudging along comes the Sendenshi, Unto the furthest ends the Oshihe of Kami To convey, at the bridge of Higo in the Yamaga; Taking the moonlight as strength, Taking the grace of Kami as a staff, Where crows sleep in Takanosu, Unto the village by the Kahabe they advance; In face and form Yoshikohime, As the flow of the water Otohiko, And ere long to come, Natsuhiko.

Along the Tsukumomaga Yamamichi, with bent waist, trudging so as almost to crawl, while rubbing his feet in his Waraji, Kamehiko came unto the foot of the Mikosakabashi, at which very moment, from the opposite direction, Futari came walking briskly, whispering something in low tones, and peering through the Yome at the Itsukau, gazing upon them with apparent intent.

"Pray, excuse me; I would ask you Chiyotsuto. Hearing your voices from Saizen, it appeared that you were walking along singing propagation songs; might you perchance be the Sendenshi-sama of Anagyo?"

Kamehiko replied:

"Oh, you who speak thus, I feel as though your voice is somehow familiar to me. As it is an Oboroyo, I cannot clearly distinguish your face, but who might you be?"

"I am a Sendenshi of Anagyo named Kamehiko, and the other person is Eikohime, the daughter of Susanoo-no-Mikoto."

"Ah, how I have longed to see you! Were you Eikohime-sama? I am Yoshiko. We have met in a most welcome place. Since I parted from you at the foot of Mount Kentsuru, I paid homage at the sacred throne of Manaigahara, and thence pacified by speech the rock cave of Mitake; together with four or five comrades, I launched a siege by means of Kototama upon the rock cave of Mount Onigajo where Onikumawake held sway; thereafter, crossing Ikuno and Osadano, I subjugated the evil kami of Mount Kamichichi, faced Mount Takagi, turned my steps once more, and returned toward the holy land along the stream of Wachi; desiring to meet with you and to consult regarding the course we should henceforth take, I have braved the night from afar and traveled unto this place."

Eikohime, rejoicing, said:

"Ah, is that so? Since I parted from you, in accordance with the command of Kami, I ascended the deep mountain of Misen bearing a certain mission; now again, by the revelation of the divine spirit of my father the Ōkami, accompanied by Kamehiko, I am on my way to Mount Ibuki. Ah, we have met in a most welcome place. I know not who your companion may be, but doubtless one of Anagyo. Listening to the sound of this river, let us take advantage of our meeting to rest at leisure."

"That is my very wish. I also was thinking that I would like to take a rest if there were some good place. ... Here present are the Sendenshi Otohiko and Kamehiko, and the one person is a man named Natsuhiko, who was for a while converted to the Uranai-kyō."

"I am Kamehiko. Are you Sir Kamehiko, whom I met before the gate of Akiyamahiko, the ruler of Hitogo at the harbor of Yura? Well, well, we have met in a strange place. And as for Sir Otohiko, are you not my old friend with whom I parted in the province of Fusa?"

"Indeed, I am that Otohiko."

"Your coming from afar to this Onokorojima must surely be due to some deep reason."

"Concerning this, there are various strange tales, but I shall tell you at leisure later. Come, everyone, shall we all gather together upon this lawn and rest our weary bones?"

"That would be well."

Thus all of them unbent their hearts in the shade of the old tree, and stretching out their arms and legs, took their rest.

Here are six Sendenshi; The winter wherein the six flowers scatter hath passed away, And bathing in the fragrance Of the mountain cherry blossoms scattered upon the wind, They make flowers bloom upon a hundred tales of bygone days, Unawares letting the time pass by.

"I say, Princess Hideko, according to your words just now, you stated that you climbed Mount Misen bearing a divine mission. Otohiko also desires by all means to climb that sacred mountain once. It must be quite a steep place, I suppose."

"As you guess, it is indeed a steep and deep mountain. It is dark even in the daytime, and dense old trees seal the heavens, so that one can by no means behold the light of the sun and moon. However, if you should climb it, there is a very convenient thing. By the divine decree of my father, I have executed a certain divine plan. Please go there once."

"What kind of duty was that divine plan? Will you not tell us beforehand? Otohiko must also prepare his mind for it."

"Even if I do not speak of it now, when you go... 'Aha, so it was this!'... it will naturally become clear to you. Let us for a while reserve this talk as something to look forward to."

Kamehiko said, "Oh, Princess Hideko, you should not be so grudging; please speak of it plainly."

Princess Hideko said, "Nay, nay, there are no double words in the speech of a Sendenshi. That which I have once declared I will not say, I can by no means divulge."

"Though you resemble a Kirei Megami, you speak in a Zuibun ungracious manner. Refusing to grant the very first Kame no Onega instantly, do you thus discharge a Krupp-style Haudan to Gekitai our Yokukiu? Now then, do you know the Teigi of grace?"

"Oh dear, you bring forth such difficult Giron. Pray, ascend the mountain step by step. A Shigumi is prepared there, Goza, that shall truly make one gasp in astonishment."

"She is a Hime who subtly employs Jiyunjiyun and Kiben. With Kiben-banjiyō, Kame is utterly invincible, ahaha!"

"Now, now, Kamehiko-sama, you must not speak so rudely and without Buenriyo. Will you not exercise a little composure?"

"Yes, yes, let us exercise composure, Yoshiko-sama. Between Burei, Kenson, Enriyo, composure, and various other Gagau that exist in Takusan, I am almost at my wit's end trying to choose among them."

Otohiko frowned and said:

"Ah, what an ominous thing to say! Do not speak such foolishness as being at your wit's end. Because you dabble in such unfilial and impolite studies as philosophy and moral doctrine, you are beyond control; it were best to obey meekly and say 'yes, yes' as Eiko-hime-sama says. Among the six of us, she is the most noble, so to speak, our teacher. It is said that one should not tread even six feet behind a teacher's shadow."

"You too speak of ominous things. While reproving Kame for mentioning being at his wit's end, you yourself talk about the shadow of death and such; is that not binding yourself with your own rope?"

Otohiko burst out laughing and said:

"Hahaha, you are a man who well kneads inexplicable, lumpish logic!"

"It is you who speak lumpish logic! Ours is rice-cake logic. A gnat pounds the rice-cake, Kamehiko kneads it, and Otohiko makes a popping sound—is that not how the saying goes? Ahaha!"

Kamehiko stood up and said:

"Now, everyone, no matter how long we talk, there will be no end to it. From jesting comes leisure, and from a gourd comes a horse. Let us whip the horse and set out toward our destination as soon as possible, shall we not, Eiko-hime-sama...?"

"Just as I felt glad to meet you at last, the divine command of the Divine Realm leaves us no choice. Eiko too shall depart Sugusama. Farewell, everyone; surely an opportunity to meet again shall arise Goza."

Thus he offered his greeting, and forthwith stepped out.

Yoshikohime, while offering her greeting, said:

"If that be so, my Lady, may you fare in good health. Kamehiko-sama, though I doubt there shall be any negligence on your part, pray take heed of the Lady's person."

"I, Kamehiko, have fully understood. By no means, by no means let your heart be troubled. Now, from here on, let us pour oil into the compass and advance. Yoshikohime-sama, Otohiko, Kame the Sendenshi-dono, Natsuhiko-sama, if that be so, farewell..."

"May you two fare happily, and we pray that you may display preeminent and glorious deeds of merit; Ariyōsu."

Thus they parted ways on both sides. Only two white staves go ascending up the mountain path toward the riverhead beneath the hazy moonlit night.

The spring night broke open in the twinkling of an eye, and pushing through the misty sky, the Ōkami of the Amatsu Sun offered His perfectly round, warm face, and shone upon the heads of the four persons. Amid the pleasant spring wind, the mountain cherry blossoms fall and scatter so as to narrow the road; Yoshikohime, who puts the flowers to shame, walking along the mountain path in her refined charm, is like a person in a painting.

Otohiko, looking up at the steep slope, said:

"Ah, it is a quite steep slope indeed. That Hidekohime-sama kept complete silence was surely out of her solicitude lest, there being many such chest-striking steep slopes, we might conceive fear, and our spirit, though raised with great effort, might turn back into a journey of weak will and feeble action; nay, I am truly filled with awe. The mind of a Sendenshi who seeks to lead and elevate people is indeed exceptional, is it not?"

Yoshikohime said:

"Nay, by no means, by no means could Hidekohime-sama's heart be so; there must surely be a somewhat deeper meaning. Even in the fragments of Hidekohime-sama's words, I instantly felt the deep, deep meaning touch the chords of my heart. Well, well, we shall not know until we go as far as we can go."

"Is that so? A simple and honest person like Otohiko does not notice such minute points; after all, I am a man like a rough-hewn Niō, ha, ha, ha, ha, ha."

Kamehiko said in a loud voice:

"Hey, hey, Otohiko-sama, are you not saying something clever? Walking so close together with Yoshikohime-sama along this narrow road, talking about matching or not matching—look, what are you saying? The demons will laugh, ah-ha-ha-ha-ha! Hey, Natsuhiko, are you not dripping with sweat as if in distress?"

Kamehiko: 'Ah, so it was! Though Shiyakuwaigaku is as yet mikuwansei, if it be a matter of the Kamisama, I shall bring kaiketsu to whatever it may be. Truly, Hidekohime-sama is grand as well. Kamehiko: 'Now, now, old man! This fellow was trained to blow big talk from morning till night for ten years by a creature named Kurohime of the Uranai religion who is clever only with her mouth; therefore, what he speaks is oranges, kumquats measured by the peck—he is a fellow who understands nothing at all!'

Old Man: 'Even though this old man has been pleading with all my heart since a short while ago, do you people make light of this old person? Ah, surely the grand person of whom Hidekohime-sama spoke cannot be this company! Ah, how foolish! Had I known it would be such a thing, this old man would not have dragged these heavy feet here…'

§ 11

Otohiko said, 'Hello, old grandfather, please do not be so angry. Since these two are relatives of skylarks and swallows, please tell me whatever you desire. To the best of my strength, I shall inquire of the Kamisama and give you an answer.'

The old man said, 'Ah, is that so? I thought that you were somehow a firm and steady man. Since we cannot talk here, please come alone to my house, where my old wife and my daughter are waiting.'

Otohiko said, 'Grandfather, I am grateful for that, but here is Yoshikohime, whom I look up to as my master and teacher. Since this lady is our Daisensei, I cannot abandon her and go by myself.'

The old man said, 'Ah, that must be so, that must be so. If so, then you and Yoshikohime's teacher come; it would be fine to leave such young men waiting here.'

Otohiko said, 'However, an unwritten law has been established that the four of us must never be separated, so we cannot abandon these two men here. All four of us shall go. If that is not to your liking, it cannot be helped, and we have no choice but to decline.'

The old man said, 'Ah, if so, then please come. Hey, you two young ones, I do not mind if you come to my house, but do not speak so noisily, for it would be troublesome if it affected my daughter's body...'

Kamehiko said, 'Hey, Natsu, if you do not treat us to a cup, it will be bad Miyōga! You are the eldest among us, yet were you not called a "young one"? This too is because you were seen as young owing to the virtue of my handsome face. That is why the old man set up a defensive line, saying it would be troublesome if it affected his daughter's body... He must have taken us for dangerous goods, ahahaha!'

Otohiko said, 'Now, Yoshikohime, though it may be a trouble, please drop by the old man's house for a moment.'

Yoshikohime said, 'If such an ungraceful person as I will suffice, grandfather, I shall go.'

The old man said, 'Ah, I am grateful, I am grateful! I shall give my thanks later. The old man's house is just around that grove over there. From my sitting room, your resting here was so clearly visible that it seemed within reach of my hands, so it is just a very short way around.'

So saying, he took the lead and totteringly guided them toward his own home.
