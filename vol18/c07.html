Chapter 7: Kami か Demon か [635]

神か魔か〔635〕

§ 1

The mountains of the four directions are clad in autumn leaves, Where Sahohime, weaving brocade, Adorneth her raiment beneath the autumn sky. Down the mountain slopes where the sunbeams grow short, Cometh the Uranai, A clumsy priest of the Way of the Road.

Seven or eight Araotoko, while resting in the shade of the trees at the foot of Fukafutauge, were engrossed in idle Zatsudan.

A: 'Of late, are there not a great many Sankeishiya at Hinumanai? The Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō have at last occupied that Seichi, and in the end, even Aohiko, who was celebrated as the very Futokorogatana of Takahime-sama of the Uranai-kyō, hath finally fallen to Ananai-kyō. Otohiko and Kamehiko are also strange fellows; there is no telling when they shall fall. Of late, the displeasure of Takahime-sama and Kurohime-sama is beyond description, is it not? Why do they thus become so prickly and glare with such sharp eyes at even the most trivial matters?'

B: 'It is a settled matter. Though we perform roadside propagation day after day, we bring not a single gift in return; since it is a vain thing for anyone to maintain so many idle consumers such as ourselves, it is only natural that even Kurohime-sama should fall into a foul mood. Doth she not always say? If each one of you bringeth but one believer, then ten men shall produce ten believers. If those believers then increase by one each, the line of the teachings shall naturally expand even without Sendenshi. Yet, though we thus catch the passers-by and engage in propagation day after day, there is not a single soul who submitteth. At Hinumanai, a Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō appeareth but once in three months. Nevertheless, the believers increase naturally. Therefore, in short, there must be something in us that is not yet thorough. If we do not somehow manage to make even one convert and return this day, we shall have no face to show them.'

C: 'Even so, it is of no use to forcibly drag back those who will not come. Unless we bestow the feeling from the depths of the heart that the Uranai-kyō is a truly gracious deity, we cannot lead them to true faith, can we?'

A: 'That is also true, but I wonder if there be not some good method. We exhaust our words with all our might and study and expound the doctrines, yet for some reason, everyone fleeth when they are ninety-nine percent won over. When we think a believer is finally made, they immediately run to Ananai-kyō, just like Aohiko and O-setsu. It is truly strange indeed.'

§ 2

Tei said, 'There is a saying that the Mokuteki justifies the Shiyudan. What if we were to use Takumi to bring the Sankeishiya of the Hinu no Manai into our Nifushin?'

Otsu asked, 'Hast thou any good Meuan or Kisaku?'

Tei replied, 'Indeed I have, yet as thou and the Mahetachi are such Bakashiyōjikimono, it is a Geitau that ye could Tautei not perform; therefore, I shall withhold the Hatsupeu for the present.'

Hei said, 'Even so, we cannot return today without an Emono, and the day hath already faded into dusk; shall we not be unable to face our households and partake of our Mugimeshi? Whatever the Shiyudan may be, if thou hast a good Hauhafu, pray teach it to us.'

Tei said, 'Will ye do exactly as I say? If ye employ my Meuan and Kisaku, the Seikō is beyond doubt. The Ten hath a mouth and the walls have ears; I cannot speak in a loud voice, but in truth, the Shiyudan is thus and so.'

He drew close to the Mimimoto of the Ichidō and whispered something. The Ichidō cried out, 'Gatsuten, Gatsuten!'

They groaned, and the Gonin lay across the center of the Daidau, blocking the path, and sprawled out as if intoxicated with sake. Kō, Otsu, and Hei remained hidden in the nearby forest. The day had fully set, and the faces of any could no longer be discerned.

From the distance, a man and a woman approached, whispering to one another; having no way to know of such Takumi, being but mortals, they walked with uncertainty, using the stars in the sky, the shapes of the mountains, and the branches of trees as their guide.

As one of them stepped firmly upon the side of a man, the man cried out in surprise, and as they attempted to flee, the two stepped upon the bellies and legs of two or three others, slipped, and fell to the ground.

Ushikō cried, 'Who goes there? To tread upon my testicles... thou dost mock me!'

Torakō said, 'And I, who is this that hath trodden upon my loins?'

Tatsukō cried, 'Aita! To tread upon my belly... Hey, who art thou, to trample upon the bodies of others with thy feet? What is the meaning of this? Hey, all of you, will ye not rise? This fellow appears to be with his wife. I can no longer endure this; let us all gather, beat them until they are stretched out, bind them, and cast them into the sea of Miyazu.'

'Agreed, agreed,' they all cried in feigned voices, and from all directions, they struck out with reckless abandon.

The man pleaded, 'Oh, this is truly inexcusable. It is so dark that I could not see where I stepped... I pray thee, have mercy upon us.'

Torakō exclaimed, 'What! Talk of forgiveness or such filth? Do you know who you are dealing with? I am Onitora, the foremost kobun of Onikumohiko of Ōeyama. Now that it has come to this, no matter what is said, I shall beat you into submission, drag you back to the honjiyō of Ōeyama, tear off your teashi to serve as an accompaniment to my sake, or else cast you into the sea; it must be one of the two. Hey, kiyōdai, this is a piece of work that has offered us insult; kill these two and be done with it.'

'Very well.'

And once again, with unrestrained and reckless fury, they struck, trampled, and kicked, inflicting every manner of chiyōchiyaku. The woman raised a himei, crying, 'Hitogoro! Hitogoro!'

Ushikō retorted, 'What! It is you who are the hitogoro; did you not attempt to trample me to death with your reckless haste? Had things gone slightly awry, it is we who would have been slain. Either we kill or we are killed; one or the other must perish. Now that it has come to this, no matter what is said, I shall exact my zonbun of seibai upon you.'

The man pleaded, 'Though your anger may be justified, it was a buchōhafu committed in ignorance; I beseech you, for this one time, to minoga us.'

Torakō replied, 'Ha! No matter what you say, once I have declared that I shall not riyōken, I shall not riyōken even to the depths of Konrinnaraku. Hey, all of you, bring the spears and swords, and be quick about it! You wretched couple, I knew you would come, and from the beginning, I have had dozens of my subordinates lying in wait, hidden in the surrounding forest.'

With these words, he struck at the man and woman as if wielding a flail. The couple raised a himei, crying out at the top of their voices, 'Help us! Help us!'

Suddenly, two or three large men appeared upon the scene.

'Ho, ho! We are the messengers of the Uranai faith. You minions of Onikumohiko of Ōeyama, even if hundreds of you were to attack at once, by the divine power of the God of the Uranai faith, I shall destroy you, a host of demons, one by one. Prepare yourselves!'

Torakō sneered, 'How insolent! No matter how much divine power you claim to possess, you are but two or three insignificant warriors, while our allies number nearly a hundred. Cease your prattle and depart from this place at once! We are about to exact seibai upon these two who have offered us insult. If you hinder our actions, I shall bind the teashi of you all together, drag you back to the honjiyō of Ōeyama, and hack your bodies to pieces, to be served as an accompaniment to my sake. Prepare yourselves!'

The large man laughed, 'Ha, ha, ha, ha! What are you spewing? No matter how many of Onikumohiko's minions there may be, even if a million or ten million were to attack at once, with the divine power of the Uranai faith, I shall, with but a single fingertip, cut and mow you down at will, and turn you into the seaweed of the sea. Prepare yourselves!'

The man said, 'Yes, Arigata-gozaimasu. I am one called Ayahiko, who dwells at the foot of Misenzan, and this one is my Niyōbau, O-Tami. We set out to make a Sankei to the Manai-gahara of Hinu, but the day was spent upon the Tochiu, and thinking to reach the port of Yura to take lodging, we came as far as this place, where we were Torikako by the Oni of Ōeyama. We were at the point of having our Inochi taken, both husband and wife, in a Kikifu-sonbau Baahi, when you saved us; could there be such an Arigata thing in Ten or on Earth? How shall we return this Gōn?'

O-Tami said, 'Truly, truly, in that perilous place, you have picked up our Inochi for us... You are the parent of the Inochi of us Fūfu. For the return of your Gōn, pray command us in any matter whatsoever. We, as man and wife, shall perform your Goyō to the utmost of our strength.'

Ayahiko and O-Tami, with voices of tears at once, said: 'Yes, Arigata-gozaimasu. Since it is now the case that we have had our Inochi, which was not, picked up for us, we shall follow your commands; pray command us in any manner of thing.'
