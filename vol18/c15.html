Chapter 15: Distant 来の客 [643]

遠来の客〔643〕

§ 1

A Nukaame, which causes the Beika to Aga, reveals a red Jiyabara in the sky.

Takahime, the Atamakabu of the Uranai Kyō who is Hiyōi by Yamataorochi, with a Hanatakadaka air, causes the Ama no Ihabune to roar high in the Tenkū, leaves the land of Fusa behind, crosses over the Ōunabara, and makes a Chiyakuriku at the port of Yura.

Accompanied by two attendants, she rushes with vigor into the Kengo Gankutsu where Kurohime has established the Iba of the Kyō in Makutsugahara, near the mountain of Ōe, and where Eujin is maintained.

Umekō, observing the figure of Takahime with Mezato eyes, bows with Teinei Eshiyaku and says:

"Ah, this is indeed Takahime-sama; have you been Tatsushiya? You have come flying from such an Enpau place; you are most welcome. Kurohime-sama will surely be delighted; pray, proceed further within."

Takahime, looking around the surroundings with restless eyes, says:

"Ah, how greatly the surroundings have changed; this is surely the manifestation of your daily devotion, for everywhere is thoroughly cleaned and kept in such a state of purity."

Umekō replies, "Nay, that is far from the truth; to be praised so is truly a matter of great embarrassment; pray, let me guide you within."

Takahime asks, "Is Kurohime-sama at home?"

Umekō answers, "Yes, Takayamahiko-sama is also here; both of them spend their days from morning until night in a manner so harmonious that it is truly enviable."

At this moment, Kurohime appears abruptly and says:

"Oh, Takahime-sama, you are most welcome. Please, do rest at your leisure."

Takahime says, "Kurohime-sama, it has been a long time; I congratulate you that Takayamahiko-sama also appears to be in good spirits."

Kurohime replies, "Yes, I thank you; he is a stubborn person, and I am troubled by it."

Takahime says, "Ah, a human must be stubborn; in any case, an honest person is always stubborn, for unless one possesses a spirit of the Hensei-danshi type, the divine work can never be accomplished. By the way, Kurohime-sama, you are surely aware that every day, near this mountain of Ōe on the Jitentōtō, purple clouds rise and a sacred, great personage has appeared?"

Kurohime replies, "Yes, yes, I am fully aware of all the details."

Takahime says, "Even if you are aware, have you made the arrangements without fail to draw that person called Tamateruhime into the Uranai Kyō?"

Kurohime replies, "Without your needing to command me, everything is as secure as the strings of a haori, and I have it all firmly in my heart. Ohohoho."

Takahime says, "Ah, I am relieved to hear that; if we are sluggish, we cannot bear it if she is taken by the side of Susanoo-no-Mikoto. That alone has been weighing on my mind, and I have flown my aircraft through my busy schedule to come here specifically for that. And so, have you already obtained the essential object?"

§ 2

Kurohime said, 'Nay, I am even now advancing step by step; regarding that matter, it is by such and such means.'

Drawing near to her ear, she whispered, revealing also the plan to use the couple Ayahiko as hostages, and her face shone with triumph.

Takahime replied, 'Good deeds should be done with haste. Though I doubt not your discretion, it must be accomplished as soon as possible; until that success is attained, I shall be in great anxiety. I shall wait here; as soon as Tamateruhime is secured, we shall place her upon the Hikauki and return to the land of Fusa.'

Kurohime said, 'Takahime, pray rejoice; Aohiko, who had once fallen into the Ananai-kyō, has surrendered to the Goshinriki of the Kamisama and has returned.'

Takahime asked, 'What is this you say? Has that Aohiko returned? That is indeed a most excellent deed. Truly, you are one who has experienced the merit of Sengunbanba; alas, you must have labored greatly. Even the Hinode-no-kami is moved by your skill as a Binwanka. But tell me, what has become of Natsuhiko and Tsunehiko? It seems they are not here.'

Kurohime replied, 'Alas, they have finally become obsessed with the Ananai-kyō. However, this too is but a matter of time; I shall surely summon them back. It must be a design of the Shinkai; perhaps by entering the Ananai-kyō in such a manner, they may return, like Aohiko, bringing many followers with them.'

Takahime said, 'I cannot be so optimistic, yet since the Ushitora no Konjin-sama is a deity who overlooks nothing, there must surely be a deep and profound design.'

Kurohime said, 'There is one whom I wish for you to see; she is a lady like unto Kanzeon, possessing the thirty-three marks, flawless in her manners, her beauty, her knowledge, and her speech. She has become a believer and is currently in training as a propagandist. I am laboring daily, hoping to mold her into a magnificent propagandist to please you. Pray, meet her once. Fortunately, she, Aohiko, and the deer-person brought by Aohiko, as well as a person called the horse-person who is truly a most manly figure, are all present. They are truly a great discovery; they shall surely become the pillars of the Uranai-kyō.'

Takahime replied, 'That is most excellent.'

At the very moment they were speaking, Takayamahiko appeared upon the scene, attired in haori and hakama, and said,

'Hail, Takahime, it has been a long time. You have come from afar; you must be weary. Pray, make yourself at ease.'

Takahime said, 'Ah, Takayamahiko-sama, how old might you be? You appear remarkably young. Since your wife’s hospitality is so excellent, you naturally grow younger. I, on the other hand, have aged as you see, my teeth have fallen out, and I am but a wretched old crone. Ohohohoho.'

Takayamahiko replied, 'Takahime-sama, I pray you, do not tease me. I am no longer a young man. Nay, though I may appear thus, age is a thing that does not lie; my spirit alone remains vigorous, while my body grows sluggish of its own accord.'

Takahime said, 'Because your wife’s hospitality is so good, and you are always staying at home, your body must naturally be growing heavy. I, too, wish to become a person of leisure like you. Ohohohoho.'

Kurohime said, 'Today, Takahime-sama has come from afar. Regarding this, shall we gather Aohiko, Murasakihime, and all the others, and partake of a cup of Miki to celebrate your arrival and hold a welcoming gathering?'

Takahime said, 'Please, do not trouble yourselves on my account. However, if you would offer Miki to everyone as a token of my visit, that would be even better.'

Kurohime stood up abruptly and called out, 'Umekō, Umekō.'

At this voice, Umekō came running in haste.

'What is your command?'

Kurohime said, 'Today, Takahime-sama has come after a long absence, and since everyone is to partake of Miki, please make the preparations.'

Umekō said, 'Yes, I understand. Everyone will surely be delighted.'

So saying, he hurried away and hid his figure toward the storehouse. Murasakihime appeared on the scene together with Aohiko, and placing her hands upon the floor in a respectful manner, she spoke.

Murasakihime said, 'Is this truly Takahime-sama? Despite your noble station, you have traveled all the way from such a distant place. I am a person of the capital; when I went to worship at the Moto-Ise-sama accompanied by two servants, I witnessed the state of Kurohime-sama’s fervent faith. Since then, I have suddenly become grateful, ceased my faith in the Sangōkyō, and have been under her care. I pray you, do not forsake me henceforth, and I humbly request your guidance.'

Aohiko said, 'I am the Aohiko you know. I have been nothing but clumsy; I forgot the words of you, to whom I owe a great debt, became addicted to the Sangōkyō, and piled up grave sins against the Ōkami-sama. As the divine realm became fearful to me, I apologized once more to Kurohime-sama and was granted permission to return. I humbly request your favor.'

Umeko said, 'Excuse me, the preparations are complete. Everyone is waiting. Please, everyone, come to the inner hall.'

§ 5

The Izumitama of Tamateruhime, which appeared beyond the mountains, now manifests here; the time has finally drawn near when the Sanzennen Onshigumi shall open the age of the Tokiha pine, and the long-held thoughts of Takahime are cleared, for the Kahi of waiting has been fulfilled.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara; the Mitama Sachihahe-mashite, and the Mizumitama, stubborn in its ways, which hinders the path of truth, has come to an age where the Henjiyōniyoshi must undergo Kaishin. Though the moon may wax or wane, though the morning sun may shine or be clouded, and though the Daichi itself may sink, Takahime, who has firmly grasped the Ichirin Himitsu of the Kami no michi of the Uranai-kyō, has opened the lid of the inner Shigumi and swallowed the Niyoihotsushiyu into her belly, causing the light of the jewel to shine vividly upon the Sanzensekai.

Illuminating the Sanzensekai, she brings about the restoration of the Oni, the Orochi, the Magatsukami, and the Sangokyō; having thoroughly caused the Ōkami of Kanakatsuyō and the Ikumiya of Konohana-hime to undergo Kaishin, Takahime, having boarded the vessel at the moment of their weakening, cries out, 'Come now, how is it? Come, how is it?' She, the Futobashira who has grasped the depths, declares that if they attain Iyoiyo-kaigo it is well, but if they do not yet understand, shall she cut the ledger? Being of the lineage of the Henjiyōdanshi, yet becoming a pillar of Kami, how shall things fare if they remain thus? If they do not understand the Makoto no koto, it is better to withdraw their ranks quickly.

Thereupon, Takahime, having boarded, shall surely bring the Ichirin no Onshigumi to a glorious completion. Until this Henjiyōniyoshi is chastised, there is one thing that must be done: having obtained Tamateruhime, who appeared on Mount Misen, and using this as a seed to press the attack, even the stubborn Henjiyōniyoshi, who plays the role of the woof, will surely surrender and doff their helmet. There is not a hair's breadth of error in the Kami no michi; though the Sangokyō and the Uranai-kyō appear as two separate teachings of Kami on the surface, when one traces them to their origin, they are but a single stock. Just as rain, hail, snow, and ice change their forms, the water to which they return is the same.

Once the Mitama of the Henjiyōniyoshi, which flows down the same valley, has been brought to surrender, the submission of the Kami of Kanakatsuyō, the Kami Konohana-sakuya-hime, and the Shinrei of Hikohohodemi will be an easy matter. The only things that remain as obstacles are the guardian deities of this world who play the role of the woof and have caused disorder; these alone are my concern. Ah, yet and yet, the time has come, it has truly come; the Kaminaohi who created this world and the Ōnaohi of vast heart—in the world of men, all things are to be seen anew and proclaimed anew through the Naohi. The divine decree of the Kami no michi of the Sangokyō and the Uranai-kyō shall be fulfilled when Takahime appears gloriously in the Takamagahara.

Expounding the path of truth, she shall establish the long-lasting service of the sutras for the Kami of Miroku, destroy the protection of the woof, discover the secrets of the world through the Ikutama of the Tokoyo no hime, and by the divine power of the Hinodewake and the Otohime-sama of the Ryūgū, the time has truly come to build the age of the Kataha-tokiha pine. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara; may the Mitama be Sachihahe-mase.

Kurohime, becoming somewhat intoxicated and in high spirits, raised her deep voice and began to chant.

Crossing the distant seas, mountains, and the Kahano, / I return hither from afar. / The foundation of the Uranai Kyō, / The essence grasped by Takahime, / How gracious that you have deigned to appear. / In the days of old, the ancient past, / The Great Ōkami Kuniharutachi / Did devise a grand design. / To bring that work to fruition with all haste, / To raise up all the Kamigami who have fallen into this world, / And to gather them all here, / That the Tamikusa of the Sanzensekai / May know no distinction of Jiyōge or Unpu, / Drawing the lines of the measure to make them equal, / To advance the great work of the Shinsei achievement, / How noble is this day of your appearance. / Whether the Asahi shines or is clouded, / Whether the moon waxes or wanes, / Even if one were to search the Tenchi,

Is there such a magnificent Nikutai / As the living shrine of the Hinodewake / Anywhere else in the world? / And the Princess of the Ryūgū, / Having possessed her, by the divine plan / Of the Ushitora no Konjin, / Gives her aid without sparing her own flesh and bone. / Is there such a true deity / Anywhere else in the world? / Ah, Kannagara Kamigami. / Until now, in various ways, / I have fretted over this and that / Regarding the divine path, / But the season has arrived, and like parched beans / That bloom and bear fruit, what joy it is.

Just as she chanted this, a faint and eerie sound was heard from the direction of the stone door outside.

Alas, Lady Hana no Takahime, and the dark-complexioned Kurohime with her long, rambling, crooked song; the clicking of Aohiko's tongue, the graceful voice of Lady Murasaki, and the many others—Bakō, Kakō, Ume, Asa, Iku, Ushi, Tora, Tatsu, Tobi, Tsuru, Kame—all intoxicated by wine; having been shown this scene in my spiritual vision, and as divine punishment for overtaxing both my spiritual and physical eyes, I have suddenly become sleepy. I shall break off here for a moment.
