Chapter 16: 返り討 [644]

返り討〔644〕

§ 1

Umekō, being in a state of mild intoxication, heard a clamorous noise outside and ran out accompanied by Shikakō and Umakō, only to behold Tsunehiko, together with Takikō and Itakō, those three, gasping for breath and shouting at the top of their voices,

"Bring out Aohiko and Murasakihime to this place!"

They were shouting thus. Umekō, in a coarse tone, replied:

"What is this filth you are spewing? Do you know where this place is? This is the sacred throne where the living palace of the Hinode-no-Kami and the living palace of Otohime of the Riugū are enshrined; it is no place for rebels such as you to appear. Begone at once! Hey, Tsunehiko, what are you? You ungrateful wretch, you beastly creature, have you not been under the care of Kurohime-sama for so long, only to kick sand at her as you departed? How dare you show your face here with such brazenness. Hear me, I shall not allow you to enter even an inch from here; begone, return at once!"

Tsunehiko replied, "Are you not Umekō? How dare you speak thus to your senior disciple? Without further prattle, go into the inner chambers and tell Aohiko and Murasakihime that Tsunehiko-sama has arrived, and that they should come out here for a moment."

Umekō replied, "What foolishness are you uttering? You have come to abduct Murasakihime and Aohiko once again, have you not?"

Tsunehiko replied, "That is a matter of course. Can I stand by and watch my friends wallow in such a heretical sect? Do not spew nonsense; you need only convey my message as I have commanded."

Umekō replied, "Ha! Tsunehiko, you just called yourself my senior disciple to my face. What senior disciple? You were my senior while we were in the Uranai sect, but... how heartless... you fled as you pleased, and for one who carries earth for the Sangokyō, there is no such thing as a senior disciple or anything of the sort. Hey, do you still think of this Umekō as a snot-nosed boy? Today, I am the Chief of Staff to Kurohime-sama, second to none. Takihime-sama has come from afar today, and we are celebrating auspiciously... and you have come to cast a pall over it."

Tsunehiko replied, "What, Takihime is here? That is most convenient. I shall negotiate with her directly; do not hinder me, move aside!"

He attempted to advance. Shikakō and Umakō gripped both of Tsunehiko's hands firmly from the left and right, saying,

"Hey, Tsunehiko, what do you think this place is?"

Tsunehiko replied, "Ha! You are the two servants of Murasakihime, the horse and the deer; if you continue such foolish acts, it shall not be to your benefit."

§ 2

Today is a day of celebration; I pray you, for the sake of a horse and a deer like me, return home, for the sake of the wine which will otherwise lose its spirit.

Umekō said, 'Ha! You, Takikō and Itakō, why have you returned so brazenly? Did you not go to Takashiroyama, get cast out by Matsuhime, and wander about as beggars? And now you have come crawling back, thinking to deceive me with your glib tongues... that trick shall not work upon me. Come, come, you heartless wretches, you three, begone! I alone am sufficient to deal with such as you. Ho, Umakō, Shikakō, go within and report this matter.'

'Very well, understood.'

So saying, the two flew into the inner chambers. Umekō was engaged in a dispute with the three, but Tsunehiko, unheeding, said with firm resolve, 'Come, Itakō, Takikō, follow me!'

Seeing them all appear at the place of the banquet, Takahime said, 'Ha! Are you not Tsunehiko?'

Kurohime said, 'You, Takikō, Itakō, where have you been wandering? You are men of fickle minds. Yet, I shall overlook and listen anew to all that has passed. Come, drink a cup of wine, and since Takahime-sama has arrived, listen well and tell us of the truth.'

Tsunehiko said, 'I have already heard everything there is to hear of the Uranai-kyō; there is no need to hear it again. Furthermore, no matter how much you may entreat me, once I have made my resolve, I shall not easily return to the Uranai-kyō.'

Aohiko said, 'Hey, hey, Tsunehiko, Takikō, Itakō, what is this? You have lost your senses to the Sangokyō... would it not be better if you repented in good measure?'

Tsunehiko said, 'Aohiko, you are the coward indeed. After fighting so valiantly at Onigajō, to think you would return wagging your tail to the Uranai-kyō... you are a spineless knave. Yet, consider this well: is not the Sangokyō deeper in its mysteries? And it is a teaching of silent action; you should know that well. Why have you returned to such a place as this?'

Aohiko said, 'Leave me be. I have entered the Uranai-kyō by my own right of freedom. I simply cannot abide the fellows of the Sangokyō. No matter what anyone says, what fool would enter the Sangokyō? Would it not be better if you, too, gave it up in good measure?'

Tsunehiko said, 'Murasakihime, and even Umakō and Shikakō, have all become dazed; what a shameful spectacle this is. Come, follow me. I have come to welcome you out of the affection of a friend, for I pity your plight. If you linger here idly, there is no telling what fate may befall you. I speak for your own good; come, follow me and return.'

§ 3

Murasakihime: 'Ho, ho, ho, ho. Regarding what you, Tsunehiko-sama, say, I know not how to express it, but for some reason I do not like the Ananai-kyō. I have already cast my body and soul entirely into the Uranai-kyō. I pray that when you have the opportunity, you will tell Yoshikohime-sama that I have sent my regards.'

Tsunehiko: 'Truly, this is the Makutsugahara. To have come even this far, and for all of this to have been ensnared—ah, what a regrettable thing I have done.'

So saying, he clasps his hands together, sheds tears, and remains lost in deep contemplation.

Aohiko: 'That Kurohime-sama, without the slightest anger at your opposition, bids you return to your old nest is a thing no ordinary Ningen could do.'

Tsunehiko: 'Say what you will, even to the Konrinnaraku, even if the Daichi should sink, I shall never return to the Uranai-kyō. Say, Aohiko, would it not be well for you to awaken?'

Aohiko: 'What is it you are saying?'

No sooner had he grasped his club than he struck Tsunehiko three or four times. Tsunehiko, suffering wounds in several places upon his body, let out a Himei and Kakeda toward the outside. Taki and Ita, the two of them, followed after him in flight. Bakō and Rokō, with all their might, snatched the club from Aohiko. Meanwhile, Tsunehiko and the other two crawled up to the doorway and ran off from the Makutsugahara toward some unknown destination. Behind them, Takahime and the others opened their mouths wide and laughed aloud.

Kurohime: 'Oho, ho, ho, ho. They have returned at last, but it seems their hearts are troubled, for they could not remain. Nevertheless, Aohiko, you must not commit such violence henceforth.'

Aohiko: 'It was merely the influence of the sake... I was so incensed that I did it.'

Takahime: 'Yet, because of that, Aohiko... our doubts about you have been completely dispelled. After all, in a certain sense, you have received a splendid divine virtue.'

Aohiko: 'I know not of any divine virtue, but he is truly a violent fellow, and it is troublesome. It seems that when one enters the Ananai-kyō, one immediately becomes such a rascal. Ah, the more I think of it, the more I come to dislike the Ananai-kyō.'

Takahime: 'Hear me, everyone. This is the crucial point. The devil will change his tactics and methods to lead us astray, so be on your guard.'

Kurohime: 'Hear me, Aohiko, Murasakihime. I ask a favor of you: would you go to the residence of Tamateruhime? If it be not you, no other person could possibly grasp the essentials. In return, if you successfully deliver Tamateruhime, I shall return both Ayahiko and Otami to you...'

§ 4

Aohiko said, 'I understand; however, the old man named Toyohiko on the other side appears to be a quite stubborn fellow, so he will surely not listen to mere words. Since the whereabouts of Ayahiko and O-Tami, upon whom he relied, are unknown, Otama and Tamateruhime are now like the lifeline of the old couple; even if I go to negotiate, it will likely be in vain. If I were to bring both Ayahiko and O-Tami and have an audience with the old man, he might perhaps return them in gratitude.'

Kurohime replied, 'That may be so; I entrust everything to Aohiko, so please, I pray you, go and return.'

Aohiko, together with Murasakihime, accompanied Ayahiko, and the party of five, including the horse and the deer, said:

'Then, Takahime, Lord Takayamahiko, Kurohime, and all others, please, we beseech you, wait for us.'

As they said this, O-Setsu and O-Tami suddenly returned, and said, 'Oh, is this not Kurohime?'

Kurohime said, 'Oh, O-Setsu and O-Tami, you have returned at a good time; is Matsuhime faring well?'

O-Setsu replied, 'Matsuhime and everyone else are safe and are diligently engaged in the divine service.'

Kurohime said, 'Ah, that is most excellent. Fortunately, the Great General of the Uranai-kyō, Takahime, is here; come, offer your greetings.'

The two replied, 'Yes,' and turning toward Takahime, said, 'Is this the Takahime of whom we have heard? How glad we are that you have come. I am O-Setsu... and this is one called O-Tami; we are greatly indebted to Kurohime, and we pray that you will favor us hereafter as well.'

Takahime said, 'You are both admirable persons, unlike what one would expect of your young years; as there is a place where I wish for Aohiko and Ayahiko to go... it is a most opportune moment for both of you as well, so please accompany them.'

O-Setsu replied, 'Yes, yes, we thank you; if it is for the sake of the Way, we shall go anywhere...'

Here, the four, Murasakihime, Aohiko, the horse, and the deer, accompanied by Ayahiko, O-Tami, and O-Setsu, offered their greetings to Takahime and the others, and departed from that place with composure. Ah, will Murasakihime, Aohiko, and the rest of the party return once more to the Uranai-kyō?
