Chapter 6: Truth か偽か [634]

真か偽か〔634〕

§ 1

Murasakihime, arrayed in purple, prostrates herself before the rare Seichi of Misenzan, the mountains of Yotsuo and Okefuse; leaving the Nishisakatauge behind, she proceeds along the spring path accompanied by Wakahiko, amidst the rustling young leaves, and the faithful Umakō and Shikakō. As the mountains gradually press closer, they traverse the narrow valley paths; viewing the village of Kaumori to the Yunde and Funaokayama to the right, the sun at last reaches the hour of the Rooster. The veil of darkness being lowered, the Itsukau lose their way; as they stop to rest at a small shrine of Kami by the wayside, suddenly there is the sound of a woman's cry.

Murasakihime rises and, having inclined her ear to listen, dispatches the three—Wakahiko, Umakō, and Shikakō—toward the direction of the voice; when they search and investigate the state of affairs, they find her beloved Manamusume unexpectedly bound in the dark forest. Rescuing her from the midst of her gasping agony, the three return, their hearts illuminated by the suddenly rising Tsukikage. When asked from whence she came, the young woman tells her Monogatari; the astonished Wakahiko and all the others, while mutually caring for and protecting one another, take the moonlight as their strength and, paying Chiui to the Atari, head toward the rare Seichi at the foot of Kensakiyama.

The party of three men and two women finally managed to arrive safely before the Great Shrine where the Sumera-Ōkami is enshrined, along the mountain path through which the moon filters. The night gradually deepens into the hour of the Rat and the hour of the Ox; overpowering the sound of the murmuring mountain stream, the voices of prayer are heard, bearing a weirdness—a turbidity of Kototama where many spirits of demons, giant serpents, and Magatsumi draw near, an incongruous harmony for the pure flow of the valley river.

Murasakihime: 'Everyone, according to the revelation of Kami, I must receive the Divine Decree within this shrine for about half a day; in the meantime, please perform the Misogi in the valley waters of the riverbed at the Ubagama and Ubadarai, and wait while chanting the Kamigoto.'

Wakahiko: 'I have understood the details. Come, Umakō, Shikakō, and Lady Osetsu, let us go.'

Thus finishing the worship before the Kami, they proceed to the place designated by Murasakihime, below the Heavenly Rock Dwelling. The night begins to break faintly. Looking toward the opposite bank of the valley, a woman, together with two who appear to be attendants, is scooping up the water of the Ubagama and Ubadarai with a ladle, pouring it over her head, and with all her might squeezing out a withered voice to chant the propaganda songs of the Uranai religion. As the four step closer, the old woman continues to perform the water purification, utterly unaware that the four have come.

Umakō: 'I say, I know not what manner of old woman thou art, but coming to this Seichi, recklessly pouring the august font of Kami over thy head and singing strange songs—what art thou doing? Have a little more discernment.'

§ 2

The old woman, while being drenched with water, said:

‘I know not from whence thou comest, but by the Meirei of the Shiyugojin of the true Suishiyōdama, of the pure Yamatodamashihi, who stands firm with Itsushin of sincerity for the sake of the Kamisama and for the Sekai, I am purifying my Mitama with this Ketsukō water and proclaiming Ketsukō songs to the Kamigami of the Uchiu; what is it that thou sayest? Canst thou not hear the Ketsukō Kototama?’

Umakō: ‘I hear them not at all; somehow, when I hear those Kototama, it seemeth as though Akuma are drawing nigh.’

Shikakō: ‘Ho, Umakō, speak not such Yabo things; though it be not the hoof of a cow, is it not understood from the beginning? The Taishiyō of the Akuma, intending to gather the Kobun of the Akuma, is exerting Zenriyoku and performing Shiyarin-like Kuwatsudō; one must not perform Baugai against another’s Shiyōbai.’

Umakō: ‘I do not particularly intend to perform Baugai, but when such a voice is let out, it somehow toucheth a nerve, and I feel as though Hedo shall come forth. Ho, old woman, would it not be well to cease now in good Kagen? This birth-tub is not thy sole possession; how would it be if thou didst retreat in good Kagen?’

Old Woman: ‘I know not what manner of youths ye are, but an old person is undergoing training at the risk of life for the sake of the Sekai and for the sake of the Way. It seemeth that my Kototama greatly offendeth thy mind, but that is no wonder; the Akuma possessed of thee is in fear. Endure that and listen reverently to my Kototama for a while; then, taking my method of training as a model, wash away the filth from the tip of thy head to the soles of thy feet until not a fraction of a grain of impurity remaineth; if thou doest so, I shall permit thine initiation into the Ketsukō, Ketsukō Way of the Kamisama of the Uranai-kyō. The youths of these days desire only that which is new in everything, knowing not the affinity or the nature of the original, fundamental Kamisama of old; when told the truth, they are but those who make light of it and chuckle. They are but those who shall be cast down into the Root-Country and the Bottom-Country of the hundred billion worlds, meeting such a fate that they cannot rise for ten thousand ages and all eternity; because that is piteous and I cannot bear to see it with open eyes, this is the Ketsukō Way that renovateth and reconstructeth the Mitama of the people of the Sekai, and saveth them by explaining everything from the affinity of the Great Ancestors to the matters of the karmic hindrances of the Mitama. It is because thou hast a connection that thou hast been allowed to see this Ketsukō practice of mine. Even if thou feelest a little unwell, endure and listen.’

Umakō: ‘That is a great kindness, and I thank thee. I also was originally born in the capital, but while I was on my way intending to worship at Mount Manai of Hinu with the daughter of my master, I was deceived by the Kobun of the ogre of Mount Ōe and cast into a cavern, where I met with a terrible fate. Then a great personage appeared and rescued me; having heard that a Ketsukō Kamisama is Goza in this vicinity, I have come to make a pilgrimage of thanks.’

§ 3

The old woman, while drenching herself with water with all her might, did not even look toward them but directed her voice toward them, saying:

‘Is it not so, is it not so? Far from receiving blessings by visiting Manaisan, thou hast been lured into the Ihaya of a demon. To call it Manaisan is a slip of the tongue; that is called Magaisama, a mere imitation of the Kamisama. Under the name of the three Mitama of Henjiyōniyoshi, a formidable Akugami mimiceth the original and pure Kamisama of the Yamatodamashihi of Henjiyōnanshi, performing evil while appearing as good. Wait a while; I shall teach thee of a Ketsukō matter. The teaching called Ananai-kyō claimeth to be the Ananahi moon, but if it be the Ananahi moon, it must be the Mangetsu. What can a teaching of only the Katawa moon of Henjiyōniyoshi avail? Hidden by clouds, here is but Hanbun. If thou desirest to hear the true Shigumi, come and follow me. Depending upon thy Shinkau, I may not refuse to let thee hear the Shigumi of the Ichirin, born of three thousand years of long Kurau and Kannan. Now, crouch down in Sokora and wait until my ascetic training is finished.’

Thus the old woman again drenched herself incessantly with water. The two men, as if shadows following a form, drew up water and splashed it over her black body with a sound of zabu-zabu. The old woman finally finished her water austerity, wiped away the moisture completely from the tip of her head to the soles of her feet, meticulously adjusted her appearance, donned her crested haori, and accompanied by the two men, crossed the stones of the mountain stream with a gaze like a serpent eyeing a frog, leaping lightly like a rabbit. Oshizu bent her waist and rubbed both hands together, saying:

‘Great Teacher of Kurohime, it hath been a long time since I last beheld thee; I congratulate thee on thy health.’

Kurohime: ‘Ha, so it was thou, Oshizu. No matter what is said or done, even if by binding or seizing, the single-mindedness of a woman will not rest until she hath heard. With a heart of great mercy and compassion, I sent the two, Taki and Ita, to pursue thy tracks, but where hadst thou been wandering? Come, come, follow me. Ha, art thou not Aohiko? Art thou still dazed by Ananai-kyō and not yet awakened?’

Wakahiko: ‘Yes, thank thee; by thy grace, my eyes have clearly awakened.’

Kurohime: ‘Is it not so? The young are ever prone to change their minds, wandering hither and thither to no avail. Thou knowest not how much I have labored, desiring to save thee. Now, come slowly with Oshizu to my place. Ananai-kyō also speaketh things that seem somewhat plausible, but in the end the gilding shall peel away, and even for one such as thou, Kintarō, thy patience must have been exhausted. By slighting the main house of the essential Mitama of Izu and being dazed by a half-baked Ananai-kyō, even if thou triest to eat, there are bones in the rice and it shall not pass thy throat. One or two bowls may be novel and thou mayest swallow them whole without touching the throat, but from the third bowl onward, it becometh sticky and grate-like against the tip of the tongue; if thou forcest it down, the condition of thy belly shall worsen, inducing diarrhea, and in the end, thou shalt stroke thy belly crying Hannya-haramitta, and even the condition of thy backside shall worsen so that thou must perform a thousand pilgrimages to the privy, until thy backside seemeth to chant the Dharani: On-abokya-birushanabutsu-makamodaranibutsu-jinraba-harabaritaya. Thus, one knoweth not until one hath eaten. Having long eaten the rice of the well-tempered Uranai-kyō, and in an excess of luxury peeling the skin of the rice cake, thinking there might be yet something sweeter, thou didst enter to see if there were novel food in Ananai-kyō, but there is neither taste nor savor. It is not Ananai-kyō, but a tasteless teaching. Ah, thou hast performed a good training. Surely thou shalt not turn back now.’

Wakahiko said, 'Nay, how could I ever believe in such a thing as Ananai-kyō? Henceforth, I am prepared to follow your Ishi and spare no labor, even as a Kenba.'

Kurohime replied, 'That is Ketsukō. Osetsu, that those stubborn old man and woman have undergone Kunigahe is both a sorrow and a joy; that you have come to be able to live at ease with your beloved Aohiko is entirely thanks to the Uranai-kyō. Ah, what a well-matched Wakafūfu they are.'

With this, she changed her demeanor, regained her Kigen, patted Aohiko on the Senaka, and laughed.

Umakō said, 'Having been permitted to Haiken such a place that is not easily seen, I am filled with Senbaumanbau.'

Shikakō said, 'Is this not strange? Here, there are what are called Ubugama and Ubudarahi, where two pools of water for a Meoto spring forth beautifully like Megane. Across the river are Osetsu-san and Wakahiko, and—oops, pardon me—Aohiko-sama; what a fine Haigafu! I, too, wish to be wed through the mediation of someone soon. Is that not so, Umakō...?'

Kurohime said, 'You are one I see for the first time, but you are a disciple of Aohiko, are you not? And what is your name? I have been listening for some time, and you call each other by names that sound like those of four-legged beasts; pray, let me hear your true names. They are likely the names of your deputy guardian deities. At a glance, you have a foolish face. No matter how much you say you desire a fine wife, with that style, it will not do. I shall cast out your four-legged guardian deities through the Uranai-kyō, and I shall replace them with the guardian deities of the attendants of the magnificent Princess of the Dragon Palace. How is that? Are you happy? You hang your head like a man in shame; how faint-hearted you are. Yet, that is your good point; you are gentle. Humans, too, must not forget the sense of shame. Come, come, all four of you, approach me. These two men also—one of them was a great devotee of the deity Konohanasakuya-hime of Mount Misen, but he has realized the existence of another great deity, the Hinode-no-kami, the Princess of the Dragon Palace, and thus he is now devoted to the faith with all his might.'

Aohiko said, 'Ah, is that so? That is indeed a fervent matter.'

Umakō said, 'Obā-sama, pray wait a moment; I have a companion with me.'

Kurohime said, 'That is settled; is your companion not a deer?'

Umakō said, 'Nay, nay, there is yet another; there is a magnificent person named Murasaki-hime, who was originally my master.'

Kurohime: 'Where is he dwelling? Make haste and summon him hither.'

Umakō: 'He has but recently become a Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō, and this day he has made his first Gosanpai to Ōkamisama. Even now, he is engaged in Gokinen within the shrine.'

Kurohime: 'Ah, is that so? Hearken, Aohiko, as a token of your Kaishin and return to the Uranai-kyō, go and bring that Murasakihime to Kijiyun. Since she has also been Hai into the Ananai-kyō for a time, you must know well both her Chiyōshiyo and her Tanshiyo. Relate to her the Keireki of how you grew weary of the Ananai-kyō, and bring that Murasakihime here with all speed.'

Aohiko: 'I shall Ukea it with certainty and bring her to Kijiyun; I beseech you, will you not employ us as you did of old?'

Kurohime: 'I shall indeed employ you; nay, it is not I who employ you, but the Otohime of the Riugū who deigns to employ you.'

At this juncture, it was Murasakihime who approached with Shizushizu steps.

Murasakihime: 'Wakahiko-sama, Umakō, Shikakō, oh, I have kept you waiting. Come, let us Gekau.'

The Ichidō replied with a 'Hai,' their voices sounding as if they were Tamerahi, from no certain place.

Kurohime: 'So, you are the one called Murasakihime? It is said you are a Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō; I thank you for your labors on behalf of the divine realm. I pray you, exert yourself to the utmost for the sake of the world.'

Murasakihime, with a countenance of joy, replied: 'Ah, you are the living shrine of the Otohime of the Riugū; it is a fortunate place to have met you. As it has been but a short time since I became a Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō, I know nothing; I beseech you, wise lady, to grant me your instruction.'

Kurohime: 'Ah, very well, very well; the Ananai-kyō is also acceptable. Whenever you hear my words, you shall surely cast off your helmet and must needs become of the Uranai-kyō. Until your heart is fully convinced, it is well that you retain the title of Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō; I do not ask that you enter the Uranai-kyō by force. Ōkamisama says that unless the spirit opens, it shall not open, and that one must not be dragged in by force; for without a Kaishin from the depths of the heart, there is no blessing.'

Murasakihime: 'Even at a brief glance, I can perceive the noble divinity of your person; even to a foolish woman such as I, it is evident that the path you serve is superior. I humbly request your guidance.'

Kurohime: 'Ah, what a wise lady you are! How is it that one who understands such things so well came to enter a teaching such as the Ananai-kyō? Since such persons are hidden here and there in the world, we must search for them to the ends of the earth and gather the people of truth. To draw only those of truth and accomplish the great divine plan—these are the words of Ōkamisama. Ah, I am humbled. Spirit of the Hensei-danshi, spirit of the Hensei-joshi who is the root of truth, come, let us all offer our thanks to the gods.'

With these words, Kurohime, in high spirits, chanted the Norito, her face filled with triumph, her nose twitching, her shoulders swaying, and her gait altered from the usual; with a light heart, she took the lead along the rugged mountain path, and the party of eight proceeded toward the hidden dwelling in the Makutsu-ga-hara.
