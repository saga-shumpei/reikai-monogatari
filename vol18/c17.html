Chapter 17: Jewel 照姫 [645]

玉照姫〔645〕

§ 1

The first of the Reichi of Onokorojima, renowned throughout the world as the unique Shinsakuchi, is the hidden place of the Zuino-Mitama.

Natsuhiko, where the green leaves rustle, and Kamehiko, who celebrates the eternal and immovable Zuishiyō, work together with their four limbs, laboring morning and evening, guarding the residence in the absence of the Lord.

As the evening storm of the mountain of Yotsuō beat against the Amado, a Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō came seeking, breathless and hurried.

Tsunehiko, whose countenance was ever-changing, scattering Momiji upon his face, while Takikō with a rustling sound and Itakō, whose heart was pained, knocked upon this hermitage and called out, beseeching an audience.

Answering with an 'Oh,' Kamehiko slid open the Amado and spoke: 'What god is it that visits this solitary house in the middle of the Mayonaka? Is it an Oni, a great serpent, or a Magakami? If I am mistaken, it is Tsunehiko; the Kototama of a single word, the affection and the greeting, are blown away by the evening storm, and as I gaze through the evening moon, I soften my heart at the sight of a figure I recognize, though I know not why.'

He asked, 'Who is the person visiting this solitary house in the midst of the forest? Pray, declare your name,' and as he spoke the Kototama with great courtesy, Tsunehiko tilted his head, bent his waist, placed both hands upon his knees, and said, 'We parted at Onigajō.'

'I am the Sendenshi Tsunehiko; are you Kamehiko and Natsuhiko? I have matters of detail I wish to state. Murasakihime and Aohiko have grown weary of Ananai-kyō, and even the Amatsumikora is enveloped by the foul demonic winds of Kurohime, having strayed from the path of Makoto and become captives of the Akuma; to save the Mitama of my friends, I have traveled from afar, day and night, to visit you here.'

Upon hearing this, Kamehiko exclaimed, 'What, have Murasakihime and Aohiko sunk into the Uranai-kyō? That is grave; first, pray, enter... Oh, and two strangers as well.'

Takikō and Itakō spoke at once: 'We are newcomers who have come accompanying Tsunehiko-sama; we humbly request your favor.'

Kamehiko replied, 'Ah, very well, let us be at ease with one another. Natsuhiko, Great General, why do you linger? A great catastrophe of celestial and spectral change has occurred.'

Natsuhiko emerged slowly from the inner chamber and said, 'Ah, Tsunehiko-sama, it has been a while; you are most welcome. Pray, come up. Shall we speak openly and at leisure?'

Kamehiko said, 'Now, now, Great General Natsuhiko, this is no time for such cheer.'

Natsuhiko replied, 'There is no need to be so flustered. All things are the work of the Kami. Ah, Tsunehiko-sama, you must never, never worry. Before long, Murasakihime and Aohiko will return to this house in high spirits.'

§ 2

Tsunehiko said, 'I do not know if that is the case, but at this present moment, the situation is of an extraordinary intensity. I, too, in order to save Aohiko and Murasakihime from their Daraku, intentionally infiltrated the enemy's Honjiyō and offered them my Chiukoku. Yet, upon doing so, the Jinkaku of Aohiko deteriorated completely, and in the end, he resorted to Ranbaurōzeki, striking our Shintai indiscriminately with a Konbō. Deeming that there is no longer any Shiyudan to save such a Ranbaumono, I, without a moment's delay, brought these two with me and returned here, thinking to borrow some good Chie from you, beginning with Yoshikohime-sama. One cannot be so Rakukuwan about this.'

Kamehiko replied, 'Alas, that is a grave matter. To leave such a sudden incident neglected while Yoshikohime-sama is absent, having followed Eikohime-sama to Chikubushima, is the height of disloyalty. Come, Tsunehiko-sama, let us without delay ride into the main camp of Kurohime in Makutsugahara and launch a great offensive of Kototama warfare.'

So saying, he made as if to tuck up his robes and rush out with haste.

Natsuhiko laughed, 'Ah, what a hasty fellow you are. This is why the young are troublesome. Come, it has been a long time; let us partake of some Omiki and devise a strategic plan. Haste makes waste.'

Kamehiko retorted, 'If we do not hasten, the matter will not be in time. Let us partake of the Omiki at our leisure only after we have achieved a glorious victory. The arrows of abduction of the evil spirits of the Uranai-kyō, which seep into the depths of the heart moment by moment, will surely grow more fierce day by day. I have lost all patience with the senile Natsuhiko and his wavering. Because it was the command of Yoshikohime-sama, I have hitherto closed my eyes and followed blindly, thinking I was serving the Princess, even through any foolish arguments or poor strategies; but that was in times of peace. In this moment of crisis, one must take measures according to the exigencies of the situation. You may be a suitable prime minister for times of peace, but at this juncture where the fate of the nation hangs by a single thread, even if it be the command of my superior Natsuhiko, I am not bound to obey. Come, come, Tsunehiko and the others, follow me, Kamehiko...'

Natsuhiko laughed, 'Ha, ha, ha, the stamping of a stone tortoise; no matter how much you clamor, it is to no avail. You would do well to calm yourself and be at ease. I have intuitively perceived the intentions of Murasakihime-sama, so there shall be no reckless outcome. There is some profound thought behind this. Should it come to pass that even one person, starting with Murasakihime, Aohiko, or others, is ensnared by Kurohime of the Uranai-kyō, I, this Natsuhiko, shall offer my one and only head—or as many as you desire—to Kamehiko and Tsunehiko-sama.'

§ 3

Kamehiko said, 'On this day of all days, Natsuhiko the Taishiyō is composed, is he not? Behold, it is passing strange; might it be that the Akurei of Kurohime has found Hiyōi within him? There is surely ample Yochi to perform a Genjiu Saniha Shikau.'

Natsuhiko brought his mouth close to the ears of the Futari and whispered something.

Kamehiko said, 'Ah, is that so? Then I am at Anshin. Now, Tsunehiko, because you would not tell us the Kanjin matter, we have fretted over nothing, have we not?'

Natsuhiko said, 'So long as there is even a speck of cloud upon the Mitama, Kami shall not reveal the true Shigumi until this very moment. If you desire to hear the truth, humble your pride, become as an Akago, and polish your Mitama until it is as Suishiyō. Kami desires to let you hear the truth, but to the Shiyugojin in whom the Mitama of evil is mingled, the Jitsuchishiyōmatsu cannot be spoken... thus it is revealed in the Fudesaki.'

Kamehiko said, 'Ya, if that be the case, Tsunehiko-sama, we two are not yet counted among the number. How difficult a thing it is.'

Natsuhiko said, 'In any event, offer your thanks to Kami-sama and take your rest here for a day or two. In the meantime, the whereabouts of Murasakihime-sama and Aohiko shall surely be made known.'

Kamehiko said, 'As expected of our Taishiyō. Nay, from this day forth, I shall follow you blindly in all things. Yet, should you be mistaken, as promised, Tsunehiko and Kamehiko shall take the head of Natsuhiko... you are prepared for this, are you not?'

Natsuhiko said, 'Ahahaha, it is certain, it is certain.'

Thus, while absorbed in their discourse, the host and his four guests laid their heads side by side and retired to sleep that night.

The sky of the fifth month, which had been overcast day and night, was this day clear and bright with the fair weather of a Nihon-bare; Natsuhiko and the other four opened the window and gazed with delight upon the scene where, in the scorching firmament, scores of Korean swallows, carrying mud in their beaks, flew to and fro in every direction.

Kamehiko said, 'They have been active indeed. We, too, must emulate the swallows and strive for even greater soaring and activity... Ya, yonder, a white hat comes swaying into view.'

Even as he spoke, advancing with vigor toward them, undulating through the green leaves, came a manju-shaped hat, three diamond staves, two black legs, and four white legs.

Kamehiko said, 'Hark, Natsuhiko the Taishiyō, it appears that Aohiko is returning in triumph. Shall we raise a Banzai three times? Or shall we fire a salute?'

Before the words had even ceased, it emitted a sound, 'Putsu-putsu-pū,' and radiated.

Natsuhiko said, 'Ah, it smells of Enshō; it is Kennon to set off Hanabi indoors. Please go outside to do so.'

Kamehiko replied, 'The Kototama of the inner realm is now exhausted of Zaireu; from here on, it is the Kototama of the outer realm... Urō-urō.'

He was shouting this all by himself. Three men and women approached.

'Hail, Kamehiko-sama, you are in high Genki!'

Kamehiko said, 'Indeed, I am in high Genki. Murasakihime, Aohiko, and the other one... is likely Osetsu, I presume. You have returned well. Come, come, report the Jiyōkiyō of your Funsen in Tsubusa to the Ontaishiyō, Natsuhiko.'

'Aha-ha-ha-ha!'

One man took off his hat.

Kamehiko said, 'Oh, are you Otohiko-sama? ...Ah, this is unexpected, Yoshikohime-sama... Ah, what, Isokohime-sama... Oh, Otohiko-sama, congratulations. If Yoshiko-Jiyōu were not present, your life as a married couple would surely have been most pleasant... Ah, the world does not go as one wishes, does it?'

Yoshikohime said, 'Oho-ho-ho-ho.'

Kamehiko said, 'The Yoshiko River that divides the middle, alas; poor Koga-no-suke, yearning and yearning, unable to have his way even while looking upon her face... such is the motion picture of comedy and tragedy... Ah, in any case, please enter.'

Otohiko said, 'Then, pray, excuse us.'

Kamehiko said, 'Thanks to your journey with the Hime-gozen, the Kototama has greatly improved. ...Come, Natsuhiko-sama, from this day forth you are our colleague; you cannot serve as a substitute for the Jiyōu forever. ...Come, Yoshikohime-Jiyōu, please proceed deep within.'

Yoshikohime said, 'Kamehiko-sama, Natsuhiko-sama, you have kept watch most admirably. It seems you have been very attentive, for elegant summer grasses have grown thick all around the house. It looks as though even small snakes might emerge. Oho-ho-ho-ho.'

Kamehiko, scratching his head, said,

'Nay, already... eh, I left the outside to Kannagara, while within, I devoted myself with all my might to the enrichment of the content. This is what is called the dominance of the inner and the subordination of the outer.'

Yoshikohime said, 'Ho-ho-ho-ho, indeed, outside, the beautiful grasses are growing wildly thanks to the sun. Inside, it is informal, with many scraps of paper scattered about, just like a garden for viewing flowers.'

Kamehiko said, 'Nay, since the other day, Natsuhiko, the temporary Ontaishiyō, caught a chill and a cold, so he left nose-papers scattered everywhere. ...Please wait a moment. I shall sweep them away with a broom now; if you were to step on them carelessly, they would stick stickily to the soles of your feet. ...Hey, Natsuhiko, Ontaishiyō of the nose-papers, why are you dawdling? This Kamehiko has followed everything blindly. How is it? If I sweep these nose-papers away with a broom, will there be no scolding?'

Natsuhiko said, 'Oh, Princess Yoshikohime, I am truly sorry that you have returned in the midst of this Susuhaki. Pray, for a while, there are beautiful flowers blooming in the forest at the rear; the Ayame are in full bloom, so please, all three of you, go and view them for a time. Kamehiko replied, 'You stand there idly without lifting a hand, using only your chin... how can one do all these things at once? I am not Senjiyukuwannonsama; is it possible to draw water, use the Saiharahi, and use the broom all at the same time? Why do you not exert yourself as well? Learn a little from the activity of the swallows outside the gate.'

And putting on his sash, he said, 'I shall clean the house. You, please clean the garden...'

Suddenly, there was a clattering and a rattling...

Natsuhiko said, 'Oi, Tsunehiko, the boards, the waterfall, will you not help me? ...Ah, he has gone off somewhere.'
