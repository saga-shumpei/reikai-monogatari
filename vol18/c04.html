Chapter 4: 四尾 Mountain [632]

四尾山〔632〕

§ 1

The Iki of the Kami of Ame and Tsuchi, Abundantly and fully throughout Ōyashima, A single Hitoshizuku from the Ame-no-Nuhoko, The Mahoraba of Onokorojima, Encircled by the Aogakiyama, The noble garden of the Shitatsuihane. Governing this world from Maruyama, The Miizu of the Kami is Yotsuōyama, Hiding its power within Okefuse, The Kami-hill of rounded form. Where the Kogane jewel is hidden, In the noble Seichi eternally, The Kamubashira of the unmoving Miyo. Kunitakehiko forevermore, Residing therein and soaring through Ame, Concealing the Shinriki that traverses the land, Here stands Misenzan. The Ikumitama of Konohana-hime, Established by the divine Mikoto, Haniyasuhiko and Haniyasuhime.

The capital of the Kami shall in time, Open and flourish like the plum blossoms. In the Miroku-no-Miyo of the fragrant pine world, The old pines flourish by the riverside of Kogumagawa. Deep in the bottom of the pure stream, The Shirasegawa washes the spirits, Of the Kami and people of the four directions. By the divine design, the Ikutagawa, Flows with the renown of Yura. Enduring the sorrows of Ikuta, Kami-Susanoo-no-Ōkami, Establishes the Kamubashira of Ame and Tsuchi, Upon the eternal and everlasting rock. Dispelling the darkness, Hanakohime, And Kamehiko who celebrates ten thousand ages, Upon the pine branch where the crane nests, Solidify the foundation of a thousand years. Subjugating the Magatsukami who disorder this world, And the demon Kumohiko.

The gathering clouds that block the four directions, Are blown away by the divine breath. Purifying the Higo Bridge of Yamaga, Unto the front of the Mikozaka Bridge, The Sendenshi comes with steady steps. Looking toward the distance through, The moonlight hanging in the haze, As if warned by an insect, somehow, The spring mist weighs upon the heart. Though not clearly seen, amidst the heat haze, Without a moment's blink, the Sendenta, Comes to be heard as if piercing the ears. Suddenly stopping by the wayside, While observing the state of things, Though not clearly discerned by night eyes, One feels the presence of Etsukohime. When calling out to the graceful shadow, As expected, it is the Asabashira, The divine official Etsukohime, And Otohikohiko whose voice echoes in the river shallows.

Though the season has not yet arrived, Named Natsuhiko and Kamehiko, The following shadows are a company of four. Buffeted by the heartless floating world, The tedium in the depths of the heart, Taking the Tsurezuregusa as a bedding, They share their tales with one another. The divine virtue shines upon I-I-U-U-Mi-Yo, The company of six with the five spirits. Seven, eight, nine, ten, A hundred, a thousand, ten thousand times, Synchronizing the breath of Kame and Kame, Appearing and disappearing in the two worlds of Ken and Yū, Until the arrival of the 5-6-7 Miyo, Tightening the belt of the heart firmly. Exhausting their efforts by the wayside of Yamaga, Leaving behind deep thoughts, At the parting of the ways to East and West, Upon the mountain slopes of accumulated prayers, With the voices of farewell, farewell.

Parting ways is indeed manly. Etsukohime with steady steps, Accompanied by three brave men, Following the Munatsuki slope, The clouds floating in the sky of the heart, The words of Hanakohime, Savoring them as if they held deep significance, Feeling as if treading through the mist, They run forward most gallantly. The old trees of the mountain seal the great sky, Concealing the sun and moon, Enveloping the deep design. Azalea flowers here and there, Bloom in various distractions of the breast. Enshrining Sakuyahime, The divine mountain fragrant with Konohana. Coming to the foot of the mountain, Of the high-graced Shumisen, Ah, is it Heaven or is it Paradise, Nestled between mountain and mountain.

Fields of green barley and flowers of rapeseed, And the blossoms of Genge are in full bloom, A carpet of flowers most pleasant to the heart. In the Shinen where butterflies dance and play, Like unto the descent of a red-crested crane Whose heart is likewise crimson, Beholding such Keshiki, from a humble dwelling A thin trail of smoke rises, while Toyohiko Allows his snow-surpassing Shirahige To be toyed with by the Harukaze blowing at that moment. Tap-tapping, leaning upon his staff of goosefoot, Whether a messenger of Kami or a Shinjin, Unlike a person worn by toil, An Okina-dono possessed of Ifū. 'I pray thee,' the words he uttered Were fanned by a sudden gust of wind, And the heart of Yoshikohime swayed in response. As if drawn by the Kami, When she came before the Fuseya and looked,

Upon the Sanguwatsumitsuka, at the gate Which might be mistaken for Hishimochi, While in surprise, yet with a Nanige air, Otohiko, who opened the Omotedo, Accompanied the Yoshiko Hime. At the entrance of the cramped Koya, Just as they rested, an old couple, Raufūfu, who had nurtured with utmost care, Were telling a story that weighed upon their hearts Concerning the Itatsuki of their daughter Otama, Their very Inochi and the jewel within their Shiyōchiu. As they spoke thus fully and in detail, Yoshikohime, the incarnation of benevolence, Stretched forth her jewel-like hands And stroked the daughter Otama. Inclining her head this way and that, She faced the old couple and said: 'Toyohiko, Toyohime, and Otama-sama, Pray, do not be anxious. This swelling sickness, incurable for a lifetime,'

'And the concern for her Inochi, Pray, clear away your bitter tears. The Ōkami of Izunomitama Has established the foundation of the reign of Miroku, And has solidified the Water and Fire of the Kami. Borrowing the body of Otama, The three spirits have harmonized, And the divine child has been conceived. It is not a soul of much cloudiness Solidified by the breath of man, But the Mitsu-mitama of crystal jewels. 'Hogyaa, hogyaa,' came the cry of birth. Needless to speak of the grandfather and grandmother, Even Otama, whose pain had vanished, Eighteen months since the pregnancy began, By the grace of Kami, without ailment, Has given birth; Otohiko and Kamehiko, who celebrates for ten thousand ages, Knowing not where their hands dance or feet tread, Leaped for joy in great excitement. 'Auspicious, auspicious, most auspicious!' Like a spiny lobster for a thousand or eight thousand ages, Natsuhiko of the bent waist, While adding a hundred years to his age, Leaps about with great vigor. This Mitsu-mitama of good omen, By the arrangement of Yoshiko Hime, Was named Tamateruhime. The greetings offered were brief, With voices choked by tears, While enduring the rain of tears,

Four bamboo hats depart the gate. Four staves strike the earth, Enveloped in the spring mist, The hats sway through the air. The night falls dark as a leopard flower, And within the forest they pass the night. Together with the cries of crows, Again the four staves advance. Upon the summit of Mount Maisen, Safely upon the four-legged grass sandals, Placing their bodies of over seven feet, The voice of Kami within the hats, Before the Izunomitama, most pure. Even demons and giant serpents, They descend toward the valley of the compass. Here, the party of four, Sent off by the storm of the peaks, Through the valley path where old trees grow thick, Swiftly along the stream, They advance and proceed toward Hiyama.

The flowers of the wood of Divine grace Open all at once in Umezako; Leaving behind the village of Uhesugi Where the path is straight, unto Mikatabara. The deep Shigumi is the Shirasegaha; Otohiko, whose sound of waves is high, Accompanying Kame and Natsu, While protecting the Princess Yoshiko, Cometh up to the Seichi of Aya. Within the breast of Princess Yoshiko, Who hath received the precious Mikoto, The bitterness that cannot be disclosed Is a Shigumi that none in the world Save for Kami can fathom. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, May the Mitama bestow grace.

Princess Yoshiko, at the foot of Mount Yotsuōzan, having received the Taimei of Kami, commanded Kamehiko, Natsuhiko, and Otohiko to cause a small house to be built; there she enshrined the two Kami, Kuniharutachinomikoto and Toyokunihimenomikoto, and caused Kamehiko and Natsuhiko to guard them. Just as she herself, accompanied by Otohiko, was about to depart for Chikubushima in Afumi where Kamususanōnōokami dwelleth in concealment, four men and women came seeking the whereabouts of Princess Yoshiko; at the hour of twilight when the sun had gradually sunk into the western mountains, they knocked upon the door of the gate.

'Pardon me, I ask of you. Is this house not the manor of Princess Yoshiko?' spake the gentle voice of a woman.

Kamehiko: 'Ha, it is a voice that I seem to have heard somewhere. ...Ho, Natsuhiko, open the front and see.'

Natsuhiko: 'In this solitary house at the foot of the mountain where trees grow in profusion, one who cometh seeking and calling in a woman's voice after it hath grown dim is surely no genuine person. ...Kamehiko, it is a trouble, but will you not open it? For I should be distressed if a voice like that of Mount Misen were to sound again.'

Kamehiko: 'Eh, what a weak-spirited man. One who frolics lively when it becometh day, and becometh dejected when it becometh night, is Kamehiko... Nay, stay, he is a man like Natsuhiko.'

Natsuhiko: 'Hahaha, ho, Kamehiko, is the calculation not slightly mistaken? If you perform such reckoning, before a year hath passed you shall fall into the fate of bankruptcy and receive the disposition of total loss of fortune.'

Kamehiko: 'What, for a moment, by means of Shinreijutsu, I performed an exchange of personality. Since Kamehiko is possessed within your body, and your spirit is possessed within the body of Kamehiko, that which is called your opening the door is, in the end, Kamehiko opening it. ...Ho, representative of Kamehiko, Natsuhiko commandeth, ...will you not open it quickly?'

Natsuhiko: 'Eh, saying this and that, he thinketh only of evading responsibility. ...Ah, it cannot be helped; if it be so, shall the associate Kamehiko open the door?'
