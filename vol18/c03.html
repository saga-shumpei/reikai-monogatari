Chapter 3: Kami 命 [631]

神命〔631〕

§ 1

Leaving behind the humble Fuseya, the party of Yoshikohime trod up the Munatsukizaka, ascending step by step as if climbing a ladder. The sun inclined toward Seizan, and the Muragarasu, coloring the Shinzan dark where even the daytime was dim, flew madly about to the left and right in search of their roosts, cawing ga-ga-ga. In the sky looked up to, hawks and kites spread their wings straight as the character Ichimonji, showing an attitude of commanding the air calmly and leisurely.

Kamehiko: 'Oh, this is Taihen! That fellow, the kite, dropped dung upon my Taisetsu Itsuchiyōra Sendenshifuku! Truly it is the height of Funman. Hey, fellow kite, Futsugafu-senban! Pull back Kotsuchi for a moment; I shall preach and admonish thee upon the Dauri of Tenchi!'

Natsuhiko: 'Ahahaha, ufufu.'

Otohiko: 'Thou art a man who cannot contain his wrath unless he speaketh something. No matter how great a human being may say he is, he hath not the divine power to soar freely in the sky; thus it is wise to swallow thy tears and remain silent.'

Kamehiko: 'Ah, infuriating! Even Lord Otohiko sideth with the kite—what a cold-hearted person! Foully infuriating! Ah, stink of nine years and eighteen years!'

Otohiko: 'Hath it not become Taihen dark around here? Lest we be lost upon the road, let us proceed while maintaining a distance of about two feet.'

Kamehiko: 'No matter how dark it may be, we are Sendenshi who share joy and sorrow together; at such a time, it is Taihen convenient for Kamehiko. For the divine vision to see the invisible hath not opened unto me! Hey, Natsuhiko, grasp my waist and follow. Since thy body is small, if perchance thou shouldst come under a butterbur leaf, thou mightest become lost to sight!'

Natsuhiko: 'Even so, art not thou shaking thy body greatly? Although the whole body shaketh whenever one walketh, the manner of thy shaking is a bit strange; art thou not trembling?'

Kamehiko: 'It mattereth not! Hold fast to my waist and do not let go!'

Natsuhiko: 'Hahaha, what an unyielding man thou art! Thou art afraid, art thou not?'

Kamehiko: 'Afraid, yes, afraid! The skin of my heels is Taihen hard!'

Otohiko: 'Lady Yoshikohime, since the curtain of darkness hath fallen so completely, is there nothing to be done? In the night, human beings who cannot see are helpless.'

Yoshikohime: 'Shall we pass the night in some suitable place? It seemeth we have already climbed about half the mountain, but in any case it will be impossible to finish our worship and return today.'

Kamehiko: 'Ah, how pitiable are the Ningen who cannot see; well is it said that the world is filled with a thousand blind men. Even for the one sighted Kamehiko, there is naught to be done; I shall set down my Mikoshi here for the sake of our Otsukiahi.'

Natsuhiko: 'Ho, Kamehiko, what is it thou sayest? Dost thou in Jitsusai possess the power of sight?'

Kamehiko: 'I see, indeed I see; is not he whose eyes behold nothing a blind man?'

Natsuhiko: 'Haha! This fellow is surely a Yotsuashi Mitama; for those whose eyes see in the darkness are the fox, the tanuki, the weasel, or the cat, or else such Shiromono as the tiger, the bear, and the wolf. Ho, Yotsuashi Sensei, thou mayest Iba to thy heart's content this Kefu; if there be a rabbit thereabouts, go thou, seek it out, and perform a Marugu upon it.'

Kamehiko: 'Any man may see, yet that is after a fire is kindled or when the night hath dawned, Ahahaha!'

Natsuhiko: 'It seemeth the Yotsuashi hath Daibun permeated thee; if it be so, I shall, with Tokubetsu Jinji, grant thee Kaijiyo from the Shiyōgau of Yotsuashi from Tadaima henceforth.'

Kamehiko: 'Much obliged for thy trouble; we are Tanin now, so leave me be. When we become Goshinrui once more, I shall humbly crave thy favor, Ohohoho!'

Princess Etsuko: 'Pray, Kamehiko-sama, Natsuhiko-sama, if you speak so clamorously, there are many great serpents upon this mountain; I beseech you, be a little more quiet.'

Kamehiko: 'Yea, yea, whatever may come, it mattereth not; by nature I am of a bold and fearless disposition. Even should the long Sensei come by the thousands or tens of thousands in a bundle, I shall not be the least bit Orochi-frightened.'

Otohiko: 'Eh, thou art clamorous! Silence, silence!'

Hereupon the four laid themselves down to quiet sleep upon the lawn, where the withered grass of the past year was mingled like the tresses of a middle-aged woman, bending their legs and curling their bodies into the shape of the character "Sa" with their right sides downward.

Troops of monkeys, numbering I know not how many hundreds, moved through the treetops, adding to the loneliness with their ruinous cries of "Kya-kya" from before and behind, and from left and right. Though no wind blew, there came from far and near a sound like "Zaa-zaa," as of a great serpent crossing the grassy plain, and strange voices like the growling of tigers and wolves; at the sound of great trees being twisted and broken, and a terrifying noise as of great rocks collapsing all at once, Kamehiko awoke, and bringing his mouth to Natsuhiko's ear, spoke in a low voice:

'Ho, Na-Na-Natsu, Natsu, Natsuhiko, I say!'

His teeth went kachi-kachi-kachi.

'O, wilt thou not wake? That sound—didst thou h-h-hear it?'

Natsuhiko: 'Speak not so clamorously; thou wilt hinder the peaceful slumber of Princess Etsuko. Such things are common when one cometh to such deep mountains. That voice like a woman being strangled, crying "Kya-kya," is but a troop of monkeys. The sound of "Zaa-zaa" is the sound of the great activity of the great serpent corps. That which growleth "On-on-on" is the Sensei of the wolves and bears in their excitement. The sounds like the splitting of tree branches and the collapsing of rocks are the mischief of the long-nosed ones. Recite "Kannagara Tamachihaemase" within thy heart and go to sleep quickly.'

Kamehiko: 'Even if thou speakest of sleeping, we cannot enjoy an anmin in such a gruesome place, can we?'

Natsuhiko: 'Thou seemest to be a shiromono laden with deep zaishiyō and heavy sins. The fukurodori groweth noisy when it becometh yabun, and by day it shrinketh upon a leafy branch, opening wide its great eyes and trembling. But thou art the seihantai of that; when day cometh, thou dost metsuta yatara rush about and make a clamor, and when night cometh, thou art weakened like a leech made to swallow salt, or a mukade spat upon; thou art a man whose hāmonii of strength and weakness is ill-balanced. Well, well, hang onto my body and go to sleep while trembling.'

Kamehiko: 'I pray thee, I pray thee! Yet, if thou shouldst speak of such a thing to Otohiko or Yoshikohime-sama, I shall be troubled. Keep it gokuhi and put it away within thy belly.'

Natsuhiko: 'Very well, very well, I have shiyōchi-ed it. In return, if thou makest a muchiyakuchiya clamor when day cometh, I shall sutsupa-naku it, so be prepared for that; say what thou wilt, it is as though I hold the handle of the sickle and thou holdest the side that cutteth, Ahahaha!'

Kamehiko: 'Do not laugh with such a loud voice! It will become a baugai to our anmin. Come, come, let us sleep.'

Natsuhiko was already snoring a takaibiki. Kamehiko was pressed in the chiuau of the sannin, waiting impatiently for the night to dawn. The mysterious voices were heard without kandan, and moreover becoming kiyōretsu jijikokukoku.

The spring night easily broke, and the curtain of darkness was imperceptibly rolled up into the sky. The voices of the momodori began to twitter noisily. At the same time, the hitherto great voices and strange sounds ceased utterly. Kamehiko regained his spirits once more, saying:

'Ah, a spring night is indeed a short thing; even as I thought I had just fallen down there, the night hath already dawned. Come, come, Otohiko-sama, open thine eyes, use water to wash thy hands, and let us ascend the mountain; having slept so soundly as this, the weariness of the day must have been rested away. I too have slept so thoroughly that I was almost crushed.'

Natsuhiko: 'O, indeed, we all slept well! However, among us there is one faithful person who served as a watchman without sleeping; Yoshikohime-sama, I pray thee see to it that they are not left out of the rewards for their distinguished service.'

Kamehiko: 'Yea, yea, even so! The fellows of the sparrows and the fellows of the crows were crying chiu-chiu and gā-gā when we went to sleep. As might be expected, they seem to have served as watchmen for our sake, and as ever they display their loyal devotion, crying chiu-chiu and kā-kā. It is not one or two birds, nor three or four; it is a number of birds of which how many thousands there be is unknown.'

Natsuhiko: 'Hmph, though there be none to listen, it is like the ablution of a widower; I shall perform the Yūto alone, ha ha ha ha.'

Yoshikohime: 'Yea, a new Hikari hath been granted. Come, come, let us use the Temizu with the Shimizu springing forth here, and offer the Norito unto the Kamisama.'

Thus she rinsed her mouth and washed her hands in the nearby Shimizu. The Sannin followed her like shadows, repeating the same. The Sōjiyō of the Norito having concluded without incident, the Yonin took forth parched rice from the Kahazutsumi at their waists and finished a Tegaru Asage; then, with Yoshikohime at the Sentō, they marched aiming for the Chiyōjiyō. Before long, they reached the Chiyōjiyō.

Through the thickets of the aged Raujiyu that had passed many years, the sea of Tanabe glittered brilliantly, and the Shiraho of the ships flickered Uōsaō.

Kamehiko: 'What a Zetsukei this is! Yoshikohime-sama, Otohiko-sama, shall we set down the palanquin until the sweat drippeth dry, compose our breath, and thereafter apply ourselves to the prayer?'

Ere he had finished speaking, he sat down with a thud, stretched out his legs, and began rubbing his kneecaps. Yoshikohime advanced slowly before the deity, and was fervently offering secret prayers and silent meditations.

Kamehiko: 'When I cast my gaze afar, a thousand mountains and ten thousand peaks lie folded one upon another without end, each as if boasting of its form and displaying its character. Those near adorn their mountain shapes in green or cobalt; the azure sea shines beneath my eyes; the heavens have cast off their raiment of thin clouds, revealing the true metal even unto the innermost depths. In the center thereof, the famous man Kamehiko lieth in the shape of the character for "great," glaring upon the myriad phenomena of the universe. The scene of this place is just like a single cosmic living painting. The heavens are vast without limit, the sea is expansive without end; how should the swallow or sparrow know the will of the great phoenix? Hey, you swallows, you brats of sparrows, you are clamorous; will you not keep silence for a while? It interfereth with my peaceful sleep—oops, it interfereth with the secret prayers and silent meditations of Yoshikohime-sama.'

Otohiko: 'Ho there, Kamehiko! It is thou who art the interference. Wilt thou not restrain thy Kototama a little?'

Kamehiko: 'Yes, yes, I shall immediately command a power failure of the Kototama.'

Yoshikohime: 'Come, come, everyone, let us descend.'

Kamehiko: 'Wait, wait, pray tarry a moment. Having gone through such toil and hardship to reach the Chiyōjiyō, if we descend before even offering the Norito or praying, I know not for what purpose we have come this far; I pray thee, grant a brief delay.'

Yoshikohime: 'A grave divine decree hath just been bestowed upon me. I cannot delay for even a single moment. I shall descend a step ahead with my weak womanly feet. You all, offer the Norito at your leisure and overtake me later. Ariyōsu.'

Kamehiko: 'Ah, it cannot be helped; there is no hope if one is forsaken by the essential queen. In a world that prizes brevity in all things, I shall omit the Norito of tedious formalities and descend while offering the Norito along the way; for the economy of time, it shall be killing two birds with one stone.'

Thus in great trepidation he pursued Yoshikohime, and while chanting the Norito with his mouth, he ran down the slope making the earth resound.

Otohiko and Natsuhiko leisurely offered the Amatsu-Norito and the divine words, and having finished the ceremony of solemn twice-bowing and clapping, they set out on the path of descent.

At about the fifth station of the mountain, the party's steps were joined. From here, Kamehiko stood at the Sentō and proceeded toward the holy ground of Aya while chanting the song of propagation.'

悦子姫『妾は今重大なる御神勅が下りました、一刻も猶予をする事が出来ませぬ、一足お先に女の足弱、下向致します。貴方方は悠くり祝詞を上げ、後から追ひついて下さい、アリヨウス』

加米彦『エヽ仕方がない、肝腎の女王に見限られては浮かぶ瀬がない。何事も簡単を尊ぶ世の中ぢや、繁文縟礼的の祝詞は略しまして、道々祝詞を奏上しながら下向致しませう、時間の経済上一挙両得だ』

と周章狼狽き悦子姫の後を追うて、口に祝詞を称へながら、下り坂を地響きさせつつ駈け下る。

音彦、夏彦は悠々と天津祝詞を奏上し神言を上げ、恭しく再拝拍手の式を終へ下向の途につく。

山の五合目辺りにて一行の足は揃ひたり。これより、加米彦は先頭に立ち綾の聖地を指して宣伝歌を謡ひながら進み行く。
