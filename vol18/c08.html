Chapter 8: 蛙の口 [636]

蛙の口〔636〕

§ 1

At the foot of Mount Misen, famed as the Peak of Kogane, Toyohiko, who endured this world, was troubled by his daughter Otama. Since the autumn of the previous year, her Hoteibara had swollen day by day, causing her breath to labor. Toyohiko and his wife, in their distress, offered prayers to the Kami of the Konohana, yet in a dream, they received a divine oracle from the awe-inspiring Ōkami. The oracle commanded that Ayahiko and Otami be sent with all haste to the Mimoto of Toyokunihime, the Ōkami who governs the Ametsuchi. As the vision revealed the Mikokoro of the noble Mitama, the dream was suddenly shattered by the sound of rain beating against the Amado.

Amidst the clamor of autumn leaves falling in the cold wind, the night slowly gave way to dawn. Toyohiko commanded his son Ayahiko and his wife Otami to depart without delay and present themselves before the precious Mei of Toyokunihime. Being honest and devoted children, they regarded the Kotoba of their parent as weightier than the Daichi itself. Clad in the attire of Waraji and Kiyahan, the Fūfu crossed Mount Wakakusa and advanced through the valley paths, passing Ippongi, Tanabe, Maruhachie, and the Yura River. They reached the Monju-dō at Kireto, breathless, with Amanohashidate on their right, never once uttering a word of complaint against the command of their parent, as firm as the rocks of the abyss.

Passing through the wide plains to Goka no Shō, they traversed the ridges of the Hijiyama Pass. They paid homage at the sacred site of Hinu no Manai and the Mizu no Hōza, enduring many nights with grass for their pillows. As they returned with haste to the foot of the Fukō Pass, the autumn sky suddenly darkened. Black clouds hung low, and their hearts were filled with dread in the gloom. Upon the path of their return, they unexpectedly stumbled over the shadow of a man. Suddenly, five fierce men rose up, surrounding them from all sides, shouting that they would slay them, striking and kicking them. Ayahiko and Otami cried out with all their might, begging for salvation, when suddenly, in the darkness, a great man appeared.

By the Kototama of the Uranai faith, he scattered the evildoers. Taking Ayahiko and Otami with him, he returned in high spirits to the cavern of Makutsugahara. On the way, they encountered the group of five, who were boasting of their exploits, returning to the hideout of Kurohime with many spoils.

Asa and Iku, the two, removed the stone lid of the cavern as usual and slid inside together with the party of ten.

Asa-kō said, 'Lord Takayamahiko, Lady Kurohime, we have just returned.'

Kurohime replied, 'Oh, is that you, Asa-kō? And Iku-kō, Ume-kō, are you not very late? What have you been doing? If you wander about after sunset, you will be mistaken for the kin of the demons of Mount Ōe, so you must return immediately when the sun sets. You are very late today. Oh, and there are two people here I do not recognize. What is the meaning of this? Their hair is quite disheveled; is there some circumstance behind this?'

Ikukō spoke: 'Regarding this, there are various tales of hardship; it is for this reason that we, the entire company, have arrived late today. As the propagandists of the Ananai-kyō are leading many people astray, we set out to find these propagandists of the Ananai-kyō, intending to preach the Dauri of Tenchi and bring them to Kijiyun, and thus we descended the Fukafutafuge. The darkness was profound, the wind blew with a whistling sound, and the surging Unabara roared like a Taiko, presenting a truly fearsome Kuōkei. Suddenly, hearing a tumultuous Monōto, we strained our ears to discern what Nanigoto might be occurring, when we heard a loathsome voice crying, "Hitogoro! Hitogoro! Save me!" Ah, this is a Taihen matter; to save others is the Kyō of the Kami of the Uranai-kyō. I care not what becomes of my own person; even if I were to become the Ejiki of the demons of Ōeyama, we who serve the Kami cannot hear this Himei and simply abandon them. Believing that to save others is the duty of a Sendenshi, we risked our Inochi and crept toward the source of the voice, only to find, as we had suspected, nearly one hundred of the kinsmen of Onigumohiko, disguised in masks and hoods, brandishing spears, naginata, clubs, and man-catchers, their blades flashing in the darkness as they surrounded the victims in ten or twenty layers. In the center, four or five men were capturing a man and a woman, committing acts of violence and brutality, beating and kicking them, with the design of dragging them back to the fortress of Ōeyama to offer them as a sacrifice to the Kami of the Brahman-kyō. Perceiving this, we could not restrain ourselves; with all our might, wringing out sweat like boiling water, we prayed in silence, exerting our Sakimitama to the utmost: "O Great Kami of the Uranai-kyō, we beseech Thee, let this traveler’s Inochi be saved, even if our own Inochi be lost." Then, a wonder occurred: the Amatsukami, Kunitsukami, and the eight million Kami suddenly descended upon our bodies, with the Hinode-no-Kami at the lead, followed by the Princess of the Ryūgū, all three of us possessed by them. With the voice of Kototama resounding through Tenchi, without even finishing the count of one, two, three, four, the mighty and peerless henchmen of Onigumohiko, like dew vanishing in the morning sun, had their souls snatched away and their bones shattered, and for the sake of their own Inochi, they wept and begged for mercy. Though I thought them hateful wretches, even they are originally bunrei of the Kami, and it is the mercy of the Great Kami to aid the good and forgive the evil. As I reconsidered and rectified my heart, the more than one hundred heroes, unable even to utter a sound, stole away with hushed footsteps, vanishing like the wind, like demons, like foam into the darkness. Thereupon, we eight, employing our prearranged strategy—"Otto! Dokkoi!"—dealt the hammer of Kototama upon the demons who sought to torment the traveler, admonishing them for the future, and having rescued this couple, we have returned in triumph.'

Kurohime replied: 'Yea, that was a labor of great merit. When the moment of crisis arrives, the Kami will surely make use of one; there is a saying that "even a rotten rope has its use." I had thought of you as a good-for-nothing whom I have kept in large numbers, a useless fellow whom I could neither discard nor abandon, like a slug clinging to a radish leaf. Yet, if you can manifest such divine power in the moment of crisis, you are not entirely without worth. Yea, you have labored well. Come, come, take a rest. However, what have the Ox, the Tiger, the Dragon, and the other two men been doing until now? Asa, you too must exert yourself a little like Iku, or you will not be able to face the Kami.'

Torakō spoke: 'At the moment we were offering our prayers before the Goshinzen—nay, toward the Mukei Shinden—the sudden Jiken at Fukafutauge was reflected in our Tengantsū. Alas, the Kahaisau of the two Tabibito; though our hearts were filled with Ase, wishing to save them with Shinriki, we were in a distant land, and what could we say? Ah, there is no Shikata. Just as we thought to execute the Shinrei Chiushiyahafu, what was reflected in our Tengan once more were the three, Asa, Iku, and Ume. Thinking these to be good Mitamashiro, the five of us at once possessed the Karada of the three, and fought against the swarming Magun, exhausting not only the Shinriki of the Kototama but every spiritual power; the enemy scattered like spiders' offspring, and as if a storm had struck flowers, they vanished into mist.'

Asakō laughed: 'Ahahaha.'

Kurohime said: 'In any case, it is as if a quack doctor has achieved a feat; it is the same as taking the head of an Oni with a spatula. Today, it would be well to receive much Omiki in the detached room and sleep soundly. Now, you travelers, have you understood the preciousness of the Kami?'

Aya and Tami replied: 'Yes, we are so grateful that we have no words to express it; we can only do this...'

The husband and wife joined their hands, bowed their heads before Kurohime, and shed hot tears in abundance.

Kurohime said: 'You are truly a fortunate couple; you have received a magnificent Goshintoku. As they say, even the brushing of sleeves is a bond from a previous life, and even the stone one stumbles upon is the edge of a connection; to be saved by the mediation of such magnificent teachings of the Kami shows that your deep, ancient fortune has manifested. I, too, do not feel as though I have met you for the first time. From now on, cast aside all worldly attachments and engage in the activities of a thousand-cavalry-one-rider for the sake of the Shinkai. Furthermore, since this is a path of the Kami where one cannot serve while remaining a couple, you shall separate tomorrow and perform your service. Though it is painful to part in death, if you live in this world, you will still be serving in the same path of Uranai, so there will be many opportunities to meet. What are your names?'

'Yes, I am called Ayahiko. I am O-Tami.'

Kurohime said: 'Ah, is that so? Ayahiko shall serve by my side. O-Tami shall go to the branch of the Uranai-kyō at a place called Takagiyama; there, the stubborn... yet not evil Matsuhime is waiting. O-Tami, you shall go to the side of Matsuhime and perform your service. If you consent, I shall have Ikukō send you tomorrow.'

O-Tami said, 'Yes, if it be for the sake of Kamisama, I shall not say nay to any matter; yet, I pray you, would you not permit us to remain together for but three or four days? For we, as husband and wife, desire to hold counsel together...'

Kurohime replied, 'Ah, that counsel is what is forbidden; what availeth it to fret with Ningengokoro? In all things, one must entrust oneself absolutely to Kamisama. Is it painful for husband and wife to part? That is but natural for those of your young estate. Even I, an old woman, hold that husband and wife are beings who ought to be together. Yet, the aged are an exception, for our end is near... For the young, there are many opportunities to meet throughout the long Tsukihi. That creature called the Kiten, though they be a couple of good Fūfunaka, never dwell together. If the male dwelleth in the hollow of a tree upon the eastern mountain, the female surely dwelleth in the hollow of a tree upon the western mountain, separated by the valley, and they keep watch over one another from the west and from the east. It is said that if a Refushi should approach the foot of the Taiboku where the female dwelleth, the male, who watcheth from afar—though the Tōdai be dark at its base—perceiveth this, and sendeth a Denpa to warn the female; and when danger approacheth the male, the female, who watcheth from afar, likewise informeth the male by means of a Denpa. Even so, you too shall separate, and the wife shall think of the husband, and the husband of the wife; the joy of your meeting by chance shall yield a savor beyond expression. Since there are so many young people here, if I were to keep a young Fūfu such as you together, the young ones would surely kindle the fires of Shura and cause discord, and it would be painful for you as well; therefore, O-Tami, go straightway to Takashiro-yama tomorrow and perform your service.'

'Since I have entrusted all things to Kamisama, I pray you, use me according to your will.'

Kurohime said, 'Ah, is that so? Excellent, excellent. You are truly one who understandeth. Come now, go to the inner chamber and sleep this night. I, too, am greatly wearied, so let us sleep now. Yet, listen, you young ones: join your hands in prayer to Kamisama and offer your thanks before you sleep. You must never, never turn your backs or your feet toward the direction where Kamisama is enshrined.'

The couple replied, 'Yes, we thank you. Then we shall retire to sleep.'

§ 5

With their eyes fixed upon the inner depths, the Riyōnin advanced in a Shizushizu manner.

Kurohime lamented, 'Ah, the world does not go as one desires; though I have just issued a command to the Wakameoto that is like rending a Namaki, when I reflect upon it, they are truly Kahaisau creatures.'
