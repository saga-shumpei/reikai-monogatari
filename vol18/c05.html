Chapter 5: 赤 Bird 居 [633]

赤鳥居〔633〕

§ 1

Binding Ten, Fire, Water, and Earth, Mixing deeply the Blue, Red, White, and Yellow, Amidst the Ashibiki mountains dripping with green, Enfolded by peak upon peak, Along the flowing stream of Yura, Advancing toward the purple smoke Rising in the distance, The Sendenshi of Murasahihime, Wakahiko, where even the Kusaki sprout, Urging the horse with a whip, the Hizakurige, Stepping firmly like a deer, step by step, Shoulders like Ikaruga, through the flying sky, Upon the summit of Kasanishisaka, The Yonin at last arrived.

Wakahiko: 'Murasakihime-sama, shall we rest our breath while observing the scenery of the Yomo from this point of beautiful Fūkei?'

Murasakihime: 'I also was thinking thus; the mountain of Misen revealeth its purple Sugata, pressing down upon the Renpō and protruding its head high into the Unpeu; it is indeed of an indescribable Yūdai, is it not?'

Wakahiko: 'It is so; the Misenzan of spring is indeed Kakubetsu. The Shinzan of Yotsuo seen over there, the luxuriant growth of trees, and Okebuse-yama is also visible by a glimpse; indeed, the Yotsuo-yama seen from afar seemeth to increase its sense of sublimity. When we visited the mansion of Etsukohime-sama at the foot of that mountain last night, I did not think it such a magnificent mountain, but as expected, with great things, rather than seeing them up close, a distant view seemeth to touch the truth far more.'

Murasakihime: 'Ah, struck by the beautiful Keshiki, I have spent time without thinking. Shall we depart soon?'

Umakō: 'How would it be to rest a little longer? From here, if we go into the interior, there are only mountain and mountain, desolate and difficult paths as if squeezed from both sides. Shall we not sufficiently yearn for the holy ground from here, feel the regret of parting, and finish our farewell greetings to Yotsuo-yama? There are quite steep slopes ahead...'

Murasakihime: 'Well, shall we rest a little more?'

Shikakō: 'Ah, please do so; let us sufficiently nourish our spirits. Since we approach Ōeyama step by step... let us sufficiently nourish our vast spirit in this safety zone. However, already Onikumohiko of Ōeyama hath fled, and thereafter the followers of Onitakehiko are providing protection; the rock cave of Mitake hath become a shambles, and since the retreat of Onikumawake, Onigajō likewise retaineth no shadow of the enemy. Furthermore, that you have brought us to this direction without even telling us your business is somewhat difficult to understand. What in the world do you intend to do? If you were to leak even a little to us, we would seldom disclose it to others.'

Murasakihime: 'Nay, as it is the Goshinmei of Ōkamisama through Yoshikohime to "keep Itsusai in Himitsu," even between Anata and myself, this alone I cannot make Hatsupeu; in time, the Shinsau shall surely be known.'

Shikakō: 'Murasakihime-sama, the two of us are originally Anata's Shimobe; it truly fills us with awe that you should use such Teinei Kotoba. Henceforth, I pray you, please call us Shika and Uma.'

Murasakihime: 'Nay, nay; were I the self of former days, I might have assumed the air of a Shiyujin through the Shifukuwan of the Kiyokutan Kaikifu Seido, but since being saved by Ananai-kyō, I have utterly caused the barriers between high and low to vanish from my mind. To adhere blindly to the irrational Kaikifu Seido created by men would, on the contrary, violate the divine will of Ōkamisama. For originally, we are the divided spirits of the same one stock of Ōkamisama.'

Shikakō: 'Yea, I am grateful. If it be so, henceforth let us remove the barriers of master and servant, and in our private relations, let us aim for an equal intercourse. Nevertheless, regarding matters of doctrine, I desire to maintain the relationship of master and disciple to the very end; this alone, I beseech you to acknowledge.'

Murasakihime: 'When it is said that I am your master, I cannot help but feel a tickling sensation even unto the soles of my feet.'

Shikakō: 'Henceforth I pray you to proceed with that intention. However, do Anata not desire to return to the capital of flowers?'

Murasakihime: 'Being human, my desire to return to my old home is as vast as the mountains, yet I must completely fulfill the commands of Ōkamisama. Until then, I have entirely severed the thought of my old home from my mind; I pray you, henceforth speak not of my old home... Now, Wakahiko-sama, let us go.'

And thus she valiantly ran forth.

Umakō said: 'Hello, Hello, Wakahiko-sama, why do you stand so Bonyari? What of that voice? Is it not the sorrowful voice of a maiden seeking Kiuen?'

Wakahiko said: 'It is a voice that I cannot at all Gatsuten.'

The cries were heard coming Masumasu fiercely.

Murasakihime said: 'Everyone, thank you for your Gokurau, but as I shall perform Gokinen and wait before this shrine, though the road be dark, will you not be careful, search out that voice, and investigate its Jitsupi?'

Wakahiko said: 'Yes, I have understood. Since it would be unpardonable to leave Anata Hitori waiting Koko, I shall leave Shikakō beside you.'

Murasakihime said: 'No, no, by no means have Goshinpai. From now on, I must become a Sendenshi and execute a Tanki Shingeki into the midst of whatever Magami there may be. Dōzo, pay me no mind, and proceed towards that voice even Itsukoku faster.'

Wakahiko said: 'Isai Shiyōchi. I shall have the Senkiyō reported Jijikokukoku. Now, now, Umakō, Shikakō, come, it is Shiyutsujin!'

Umakō said: 'Here is a horse, a famed steed of a thousand leagues. Pray mount upon it; it would be quite wonderful to display the martial prowess of a glorious general towards the enemy.'

Shikakō said: 'If a horse is not to your liking, a deer is also here. Did not Koyane-no-Mikoto ride upon a deer? If possible, it would be splendid if you would grant me your gracious order, but...'

Wakahiko said: 'Umakō, Shikakō, is there time for such foolery? Come, let us go quickly.'

Uma and Shika said: 'Eh, how foolish, charge, charge, one-two, one-two!'

And so, raising their voices, they advanced along the dark road. The previous voice ceased abruptly. Just as the three were lost in perplexity, having lost their direction in the dark night, a human voice was heard in the darkness.

First Person said: 'Come, now it is safe with this. If we leave her bound with a gag under the pine tree like that, there is no fear of her escaping. Well, taking advantage of the darkness, shall we not at least take a rest at leisure?'

Second Person said: 'Even if you say to rest, it has suddenly grown dark and is in absolute darkness; is it not just as if we were covered by a kettle?'

First Person said: 'If one were covered by a kettle, one should not be able to see the stars in the sky. Look there, through the parting of the clouds, the light of stars is faintly twinkling here and there, is it not?'

Second Person said: 'What, everywhere heaven and earth are pitch-dark; it is the sadness that not even a single star can be seen.'

First Person said: 'When the stars are visible so magnificently as this, where on earth are you looking again? Ahahaha, you have been struck, haven't you, on your eyes!'

Taki and Ita cried out, 'Lo, they have appeared! This is more than we can bear,' and they fled in a reckless Muchiyakuchiya manner. By misstep, the Futari fell headlong with a splash into the nearby Tanigaha.

Umakō said, 'Ah, what a thing to be Arigata; with this, we are at peace. Come, let us hasten; Murasakihime-sama is waiting expectantly; let us go.' Thus taking Osetsu by the hand, the four finally returned before the shrine where Murasakihime was offering her silent prayers.

Shikakō said, 'Murasakihime-sama, behold the glorious deed and merit of this fellow Shika; pray, bestow your praise upon me. The object of our quest hath been successfully secured.'

Murasakihime spake, 'Ah, truly, you have labored well. I know not from whence she came, but it was indeed a perilous situation.'

Osetsu spake, 'Yea, I am grateful. Having been parted by death from my grandfather, upon whom I relied, and likewise parted by death from my grandmother, I am now a lone woman living without support. While I was journeying toward the Holy Ground, following after my betrothed husband, I lost my way and passed through Makutsugahara. Then, from behind, came a man's voice crying "O-i, O-i!" and regardless of all else, thinking them suspicious fellows, I fled with all my might along the long path unto this place. Unluckily, I stumbled upon a rock on the way and fell prostrate; thereupon, the two men who had pursued me fell upon me, bound me securely, and took me to the foot of a pine tree, where they committed acts of violence, striking and kicking me. I cried out with all my strength, thinking that if anyone should pass by, they might save me. Then the two Warumono forced a Sarugutsuwa into my mouth; and just as I closed my eyes in resignation, thinking all was lost and my senses grew dim, I received this unexpected succor. This favor I shall not forget even in death; you have all saved me well.'
