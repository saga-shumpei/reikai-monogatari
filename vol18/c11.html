Chapter 11: 相見互 [639]

相見互〔639〕

§ 1

Falling and ceasing, the sky hangs low; the Atari is dark and Tasoga descends. The Yamahototogisu, in the Ochikochi, cries 'Honzau-kaketa-ka, kaketa-ka,' spitting blood with Hatsusenyakoe; the Satsukizora is dampened by its voice. To guide the Hitobito suffering in sorrow toward the Ōmichi of the Kami, Tsunehiko leaves Onigajiyōzan behind. His feet unsteady, he crosses the Yura river, passes Hebiganahana and the village of Hase, traverses Ikuno, and goes beyond Hinokiyama, Shiyuchi, and Komono. A Hitoritabi, he strikes his steed with a whip. At the Chiyōjiyō of Kuwanontauge, under a sky of drizzling rain, he looks out toward the Anata; Tenjinyama, Komukiyama, and the flowers of Sonobe lie beneath his eyes.

As Yokota and Kizaki unfold, and Takajōyama reveals its form above the clouds at the break of dawn, he gazes upon the fields below. In the rice paddies, the Saotome, planting the seedlings of life, form groups of three and five, their voices singing of the prosperity of the imperial reign; it is truly the image of the age of the Kami.

Tsunehiko leans against a rock at the summit of the pass, feeling the fatigue of his nocturnal journey; while worshipping the rising sun, he is unwittingly overcome by the spirit of slumber.

Aiming for the Chiyōjiyō of Kuwanontauge, two figures in the guise of beggars ascend from the east.

The first says, 'When a man is reduced to such destitution, there is truly no help for it, is there? Even if they say a man is worth a hundred kan in his nakedness, wearing but a single tattered undershirt, wandering in this drizzling Satsukizora—no matter which house I seek, they slam their doors shut, and everyone is out in the fields. There is not a place to be served even a cup of tea; if one scoops and drinks the water of a mountain stream, though it has salt, it soon upsets the stomach. While one loses nothing in nakedness, even if one wishes for something, without at least a garment, no one will treat with you. We are misunderstood as mere beggars. It is truly regrettable.'

The second replies, 'Even Matsuhime of Takajōyama, of the Kyō of divination, who speaks so grandly of saving the world and the true path, has changed her attitude completely from before, and treated us with cruelty as if brushing flies from a meal, has she not? This is, after all, because our own wisdom is lacking. To be tormented by rain, buffeted by wind, and on top of that, attacked by mosquitoes—that a man of seven feet should have no place to dwell in this vast heaven and earth is, in short, because our wisdom does not turn. That fellow Ume-kō and the likes of Matsuhime are indeed insidious things, but they have cleverly ingratiated themselves with Kurohime and now live in luxury. Is there no way for us to become subordinates of Kurohime once more?'

§ 2

A: 'Once a Danshi has uttered a Kuōgen and has been dismissed from this place, how could he return, wagging his tail in submission? There is a saying that a hawk, even when starving, will not peck at ears of grain. Do not utter such Yowane; does not the moon rise after the darkness?'

B: 'It appears that the Unmei of Ningen is ordained. Kurohime, Takahime, and Matsuhime, wherever they may be, possess round and abundant faces; it is said that there is no sorrow in a Marugaho, while there is sorrow in a Nagagao. As for us, if we but have gold, we are called the spitting image of society and are treated as a Shiromono to be welcomed, yet in a state such as this of Kefu, I appear to myself increasingly Hinsau. Who would cast an eye upon a Butsusō Nikutai that causes one to lose all Aisau for oneself, no matter how many Baka there may be in this world? Ah, there is no Shikata. Shall I not attempt to settle the Itsushinjiyō Shiyochi? To commit the Nūsū method would be to heap sin upon Shinkai and sow the seeds of suffering for ten thousand eons; indeed, to say so, suicide is a sin, and thus I cannot even die, which is a troubled state. How might this agony and suffering be resolved? Or rather, could it be forgotten entirely?'

A: 'It is a matter of how one holds one’s heart. One must enjoy the heart of the moment.'

B: 'You still speak of such carefree things, but it is said that when clothing and food are sufficient, one knows propriety. For three days now I have eaten nothing, and my stomach demands the entirety of my assets. When I have become unable to take even a single step, how could I enjoy the heart of the moment? Does it not merely increase the pain in every passing moment? Ah, reflecting upon this, I have come to understand the grace of Kurohime anew.'

A: 'Hey, do not speak such pathetic words. Look, there stands a missionary of the Sangokyō.'

B: 'Now that it has come to this, there is neither Sangokyō nor any fortune-telling faith. It is the sorrow of not eating. When pressed by starvation, what room is there for shame or anything else?'

And so, approaching the place where Tsunehiko stood, B said:

B: 'Excuse me, are you not a missionary of the Sangokyō?'

At this voice, devoid of strength, Tsunehiko suddenly awoke and said:

'Ah, it seems I was exhausted from my night journey and fell asleep without knowing it. ...Hey, you appear to be a beggar. What is your business?'

B said nothing, but pointed to his mouth and belly, indicating that he was pressed by hunger.

§ 3

Tsunehiko said, 'Ah, I thought you were alone, but you are a pair. Fortunately, there are rice balls remaining here. It is a discourtesy, but will you not partake of them?'

Otsu replied, 'Yes, that is most grateful. We shall accept them at once. ...Hey, Takikō, a rescue ship has arrived; a commissariat has been established. Come, offer your thanks.'

Takikō said, 'Ah, if that is so, shall I accept? It is a shameful thing. It is the first time since I was born that I have received and eaten a traveler's lunch.'

Thus, the four rice balls were distributed, and they devoured two each as if pouncing upon them.

Otsu said, 'Ah, with this, I feel somewhat like a human being again. ...Nay, Sendenshi-sama, I thank you. ...However, what should we do from this point forward?'

Takikō said, 'It is a heart of the moment. Again, Kamisama will assist us. Do not worry. ...I know not who you are, but I thank you. With this, we have finally managed to secure our position.'

The moment he looked up, he was startled and hid his face.

Tsunehiko said, 'Ah, pardon my rudeness, but are you not Takikō-sama of the Uranai-kyō? Ah, and you are Itakō-sama; how did you come to be in such a state? There must be some circumstance. If it be no hindrance, I pray you, tell me.'

Itakō said, 'We have met your eyes in a shameful state. In truth, that it has come to this is rust born of my own self; there is no way to express it, but in reality, because the effect of our Senden was not rising, I did a trifling thing. That became the beginning of our sinking into this abyss of great ruin.'

Takikō said, 'It is truly the height of blushing shame; despite my lack of wisdom, I imitated others and performed a great failure, falling into the bottom of the valley of darkness, meeting with an experience where my life was barely spared, and in the end, not only did I offend the mood of Kurohime, but we were also intruded upon by Aohiko and Osetsu, and we fled with all our might. Thereafter, we two went to Mount Takagi, and intending to bare our backsides before Matsuhime and expose the inner workings of the Uranai-kyō, we acted with coercion; however, she is a formidable one, and the farce we squeezed out from our intellects gave not even the dew of a rabbit's hair of threat to Matsuhime, and having received a counter-attack, we came to this place with our lives barely intact. However, there is a proverb that says when one is in distress, one becomes disordered, but we are those who once heard the path of Makoto; even if we were to starve to death, we absolutely detest stealing the property of others, and now, at the brink of life, at the great mountain pass of our existence, we encountered you and were given a lump of bread, and at last, we felt like human beings. If this had been a favor from a complete stranger, it would be regrettable, but thankfully, to have been saved by you, with whom we were once acquainted, is proof that the Heavenly Way has not yet abandoned us, and somehow, courage has welled up within me.'

§ 4

Tsunehiko: 'In your words just now, you said that Aohiko and Osetsu have gone to the place of Kurohime; is that the truth?'

Takikō: 'Yea, yea, it is the truth of truths, without a single grain of Kakene. By this time, Kurohime may have been deceived by Aohiko, Osetsu, and two or three other men and women, her Daujiyō destroyed, and she may have fled even unto the land of Fusa; for the Yōsu of Matsuhime of Takashiroyama was somewhat strange...'

Itakō: 'Nay, Kurohime is no such creature. Surely Aohiko and Osetsu are as rats in a bag, and they must be cleverly beguiled by the tip of her tongue. Rather, that which I deem regrettable are the Lady Murasakihime, and the young men called Umakō and Shikakō. Without doubt, they have suffered Chinbotsu into the Uranai-Kyō.'

Tsunehiko: 'How strange; as you both know, we also were fervent ones of the Uranai-Kyō, but because the Naiyō was not Jiyūjitsu, and because Kurohime lacked the Itsuchi of Genshinkau, which point we could not fathom, and moreover, as we had no words of excuse to offer to the many believers as subordinate evangelists, we thought, "Ah, the future of the Uranai-Kyō is already visible. It hath crumbled from the very foundation. With such a state, how can the cultivation and purification of the world be accomplished?" Those intoxicated by faith may maintain their life-pulse for the present, but even as the wine of intoxication shall eventually clear, it is the natural conclusion that faith also shall gradually cool; thus, foreseeing the future, and realizing that it is indeed the Sango-Kyō that shall save the world, we immediately entered the faith of Sango-Kyō, set forth to subjugate the evil deities of Onigajō, and have since been traveling and preaching in various places. Even so, that which I cannot comprehend is the case of Aohiko, Osetsu, and the Lady Murasakihime, whose determination was so firm. In this, there must be some deep Yōsu. Lo, we cannot remain thus. We must go to Makutsugahara as swiftly as possible to ascertain the truth or falsehood of the matter, and thereafter determine our plan of operations. Ah, a troublesome thing hath come to pass.'

Thus he foldeth his arms and heaveth a deep sigh.

Takikō: 'Concerning this, Lord Tsunehiko, have you any thought? If it may be so, we also desire to be allowed to serve for the sake of the Sango-Kyō together with you; yet, whatever we may say, in this ruined state, it may affect your honor...'

Tsunehiko: 'Lo, what is it that you say? Raiment can be changed at any time. Your souls, which have been forged through the experience of your past failures, are not easily obtained. At any rate, let us go together. Furthermore, if there be a convenient place, I shall purchase raiment for you. In any case, we must head to the rescue of Aohiko and those below him. Come, Takikō, Itakō, let us go.'

§ 5

The Futari said nothing, and while blinded by tears of joy, they followed behind Tsunehiko, pointing toward the Seihoku; their demeanor, which had until now been filled with the spirit of Suimauhaizan, was suddenly as if flowers had bloomed upon a withered tree, and they followed him with light and eager steps.

As if an ancient tree leaning against the Santōkangan had met the warmth of spring and put forth buds of deep Shinriyoku, their pale faces were suddenly transformed into the color of Sakurairo, and while offering their Zetsutai obedience and Shisei to Tsunehiko, they crossed the blooming Karekitauge, saw the salvation of Kami behind the Kifuhan of Enoki-tōge, unclasped their clenched fists, crossed the Nobe of ferns swaying in the Natsukaze, entered a certain Chiyamise, and having again prepared their stomachs, they grew plump in an instant, and at the summit of the Shiyuchiyamatauge, where they came walking along, they received the cool summer breeze blowing over the Aoba, and sat upon a rock by the side.

Tsunehiko said, 'Ah, how swift the time passes; in but one Hitoiki more, we shall arrive at the Seichi. It is said that the Keirin-jō of Etsuko-hime-sama has been established at the foot of Yotsugi-ōyama. I wonder if I should stop by for a moment. I might be able to discern the state of Aohiko... No, no, this time I shall pass through, confront Aohiko, and if he may be saved, I shall devote my sincerity to the utmost to offer him counsel, and only then shall I visit the hermitage of Etsuko-hime-sama. If, fortunately, Aohiko and his followers have repented and returned to the Sangyō-kyō, it would not be to Aohiko's benefit for me to have whispered strange things beforehand. As a matter of friendship, it is surely the right path to keep it a Zetsutai secret.'

Taki-kō said, 'There is surely no concern that Aohiko-sama has become a follower of the Uranai-kyō.'

Ita-kō said, 'I cannot guarantee anything; it was so sudden that we were also startled, thinking we had come to purge the rock cavern, but upon reflection, when I consider the countenances of Kurohime, Aohiko, Murasakihime-sama, and the others, there was not the slightest strange hue upon them. Perhaps we were cleverly deceived by the sorcery of Kurohime at the foot of the waterfall on Kensenzan; in any case, having entrusted ourselves to Tsunehiko-sama and become his disciples, we must save the party of Aohiko-sama and lead them back to the original path.
