Chapter 13: 救の Kami [641]

救の神〔641〕

§ 1

Torawaka, Tomihiko, and Kikuwaka, these three, concentrated their prayers for the fulfillment of their desires with undivided attention before the Misen Jinshiya at the Chiyōjiyō of Kinpusen, and at last, they saw the night through.

Torawaka said with a voice like that of one scratching a sore, 'Ah, I have offered up a daibun takusan of Kamigoto, and it appears that the sauko of my voice has signaled kiubafu; it has begun to grow hoarse.'

The other futari also spoke with similar hoarse voices.

Torawaka said, 'There is no shikata; since I have kennafu all the Kototama I possessed, I am almost muichibutsu as far as a voice is concerned; it is as if I have become a naked akago of voice; surely, having become a newborn akago to this extent, the Kami will grant whatever guwanbau I may have.'

Thus, he was finally hatsushiya a hinjiyaku Kototama that sounded like a takebauki brushing over withered grass. Torawaka drew a short sword from his kuwaichiyū with a flourish, shaved a nearby tree, and toward it, he took out a brush shaped like a long triangle, resembling a bamboo leaf, which he had struck from a yatate, and began to write something in a crumpled manner.

Having finished writing, he pointed at the characters on the tree with a look of triumph, grunting like a mute, telling them to look; when the two drew near and read it down, it was written:

'The brush-tip of Konohana-hime no Mikoto: Today is the end of the seventy-five-day mourning period, and the Kami is mindful of Otama, who is supposed to come for worship without fail. Tamateru-hime, whom I have bestowed upon you, is no ordinary human child. Because she has entered your flesh and been reborn in order for the Kami to use her for divine service, you shall now repent here, dedicate yourself to the Uranai-kyō, and serve the purpose of the Kami; this is the shikumi of the Kami. If you consent, place a hikage-kazura upon your head as a sign and return to your home. If you do not consent, you may return as you are, and the Kami shall later show you a sign of warning.'

Kikuwaka, with a hoarse voice, said, 'Ahaha, excellent, excellent; say, Tomihiko, he is indeed an elder brother of song, is he not?'

Torawaka said with a hoarse voice, 'I am an elder brother of song, am I not?'

The three, before long, at the hour when Otama would come for her morning worship and ascend, slid down from the mountain behind, stole around, hid their forms in the thicket of shrubs on the mountainside, and waited for Otama to descend. Otama, all alone, leaning on a cherry-wood staff, finally reached the Chiyōjiyō, offered words of gratitude before the shrine, and when she suddenly looked at a large tree by the side of the shrine, some characters appeared there. Thinking, 'How strange,' she drew near to look, and it was the aforementioned text; for a while, she glared at the tree, folded her arms, and was lost in thought. After a short while,

With these words, the three men braced themselves for battle. Otama, feinting to the right and then to the left, struggled and grappled with them like a flying bird. The moment Torawaka bit into Otama's leg, she fell backward, and aiming for the valley two or three ken away, she and Torawaka rolled over and over, performing an acrobatic feat three or four times, now on top and now beneath. Kikuwaka and Tomihiko, using the Fuji-rope they had prepared in advance, sought to bind her hands behind her back and gag her. At this moment, a white hat came swaying up from below.

Torawaka: 'Hey, some suspicious fellow is coming up. It is likely the old man Toyohiko.'

Kikuwaka: 'For an old man, his pace is remarkably swift. Let us bind her quickly, hide her thereabouts, and wait for him to pass.'

So saying, they hurriedly bound Otama and concealed her body in the thicket of shrubs. A man came up to that place.

'Hey, you are of the Uranai sect, are you not? I would ask you a small matter. Has the beautiful woman named Otama, daughter of Toyohiko of Oyogi, passed this way? I see that you are all clad in fox-sashes; has something interesting occurred?'

Torawaka: 'Nay, nothing in particular has occurred; we have not seen the lady Otama at all.'

So saying, he deliberately cast his eyes in the opposite direction from where they had hidden Otama.

The man said, 'It is the Jikoku when we must be here, yet... I have been watching from Achira for a Chiyotsuto, and it seemed as though the shadows of four people were moving.'

Torawaka replied, 'Yes, did it appear so? That is likely because it is Hirunaka, and perhaps it was the Kagebōshi that cast them.'

The man said, 'In this Mitsurin that seals the Ten, it is strange that shadows should be cast; I shall have a Tabako here... if I may. I seem to hear the breath of a woman. Ha-ha-ha-ha, you are not hiding someone, are you?'

Torawaka replied, 'Metsusau, to speak of hiding someone in this Hirunaka... what Hitsueu could there be to hide anything? Knowing that if one does thus, it becomes so, it is what we call the Yamatodamashihi that one cannot suppress, just a Chiyotsuto...'

The man said, 'Truly, the Sannin are all trembling; wait, wait, I shall perform an Akurei Chiushiya to provide further amusement and subject them to Reibaku.'

Kikuwaka replied, 'Metsusau, Metsusau, this is already Takusan, I pray you.'

The man uttered a single 'Un.'

With that single Hitokoe, he administered the Reibaku. From the waist down, the three remained braced like the legs of a Kurakake, unable to move a fraction as if they had grown from the earth like trees, while from the waist up, they shook with a Binbafu, their eyes rolling in a manner most Oka.

The man said, 'Ah, I must now proceed to rescue O-Tama-sama.'

He untied the ropes of O-Tama, who lay fallen within the nearby Kuwanboku, and removed her Sarugutsuwa.

The man said, 'Traveler Jiyochiu, nay, O-Tama-sama, you have met with a terrible ordeal; come, compose yourself, for you are Daijiyōbu now, as I have placed them under Reibaku just as you see.'

O-Tama looked around, gazing at the man's face.

O-Tama said, 'Yea, you must be of their Dōrui; I am not such an O-Tama as to be deceived by such a Yahochiyō.'

The man replied, 'This is Meiwakusenban; I am a man called Tanshiu, and a Chiki of Toyohiko-sama.'

O-Tama Jiyukushi the man's face.

O-Tama said, 'Yea, are you indeed Tanshiu-sama who came to visit the other day? This is truly a fortunate arrival; come, let us return.'

Tanshiu said, 'Pray, be at ease; he is the Kami who, though his feet walk not, knows all things under the Ame, the one known as Kakashihiko, whose other name is the Kami of Sofuto, appearing in three manifestations; aha, aha, aha.'

O-Tama said, 'Truly, what a magnificent Kami of Kakashihiko it is.'

Tanshiu said, 'Since he is one who knows all things in the world, shall we try to inquire of him?'

O-Tama said, 'That would be interesting; nay, it would be most interesting.'

Tanshiu said, 'To speak of inquiring of a Kami as interesting... is there such a disrespectful thing? Pray, restrain your Kototama a little.'

O-Tama said, 'Ho, ho, ho, I shall certainly restrain myself.'

Approaching slowly before Torawaka, O-Tama said, 'Ha, this Kami is staring with eyes wide open; I wish to offer something, but I have nothing; Tanshiu-sama, what shall we do, for he is holding his mouth wide open...'

Tanshiu said, 'How would it be to stuff him full with dumplings of earth or stone? Aha, aha, aha.'

O-Tama said, 'That would be economical and well; shall we offer them to all three so that there may be no dispute?'

Tanshiu said, 'Yea, your hands will become soiled, so let us refrain; now, now, you six-legged ones, I shall release the Reibaku; it would be best for you to flee with all haste, report this matter to Takahime, Kurohime, and Takayamahiko-no-Mikoto without concealment or omission, and receive your reward.'

Upon the single 'Un' and the release of the Reibaku, the three fled in desperation, tumbling as if a pile of stones had been cast down a slope.

Tanshiu returned with O-Tama to the house of Toyohiko in Oyogi around twilight. Toyohiko and his wife heard with tears the full account of O-Tama's distress and the manner in which Tanshiu had saved her, expressing profound gratitude; Tanshiu was treated with great respect as the savior of her life, and from then on, he dwelt for a time at the home of O-Tama. Yet, the relationship between Tanshiu and O-Tama was entirely free of any suspicious connection, remaining most pure.

原文（近代文語）

男『真に三人共慄つてるな、まてまて今一つ退屈覚しに悪霊注射でもやつて霊縛してやらう』

菊若『めゝゝゝ滅相な、もう之で沢山で御座います』

男『ウン』

と一声、霊縛を施した。三人は腰から下は鞍掛の足の様に踏ん張つたまま地から生えた木の様にビクツとも動かず、腰から上は貧乏ぶるひをやり乍ら目許りぎろつかせて居る可笑しさ。

男『アーア、お玉さまを之から助けて上げねばなるまい』

と傍の灌木の中に倒れて居るお玉の綱を解き猿轡を取り外し、

男『旅のお女中、否お玉さま、えらい目に会ひましたね、サ、しつかりなさいませ、もう大丈夫ですよ、あの通り霊縛を施して置きました』

お玉はキヨロキヨロ男の顔を見廻し、

お玉『ヤア、其方は同類であらう、そんな八百長をしたつて欺される様なお玉ではありませぬよ』

男『これは迷惑千万、私は丹州と云ふ男、豊彦さまの知己ですよ』

お玉は男の顔を熟視し、

お玉『ヤア貴方は先日お越し下さいました丹州さまで御座いますか、これはこれはよい処へ来て下さいました、サア帰りませう』

丹州『マア、ゆつくり成さいませ、足は歩かねども天の下の事悉く知る神なりと云ふ案山子彦又の御名は曽富斗の神が御三体現はれました、アハヽヽヽ』

お玉『ほんに、マア見事な案山子彦の神さまですこと』

丹州『何でも世界の事は御存じのお方だから、一つ伺つて見ませうか』

お玉『それは面白からう、いやいや面白いでせう』

丹州『神様に伺ふのに面白いなんて、……そんな失敬な事がありますか、ちつと言霊をお慎みなさい』

お玉『ホヽヽヽ、屹度慎みませう』

と寅若の前に徐々と現はれ、

お玉『ハヽア、此神様は目ばかり剥いて居らつしやる、何かお供へしたいが何もありませぬ、丹州さま、如何でせう、大きな口を開けて居らつしやいますが………』

丹州『お土かお石の団子でも腹一杯捻込んであげたら如何でせう、アハヽヽヽ』

お玉『それは経済で宜しいね、お三方とも勝負のない様にお供へしませうか』

丹州『ヤア手が汚れますから措きませうかい、こらこら六本足、霊縛を解いてやる、一時も早く立帰り此由を高姫、黒姫、高山彦の御前に包まず隠さず注進致して、御褒美に預つたが宜からう』

『ウン』と一声霊縛を解くや否や三人は一生懸命ガラガラガラと坂道に石礫を打ちあけた様に転んで逃げて行く。

丹州はお玉と共に於与岐の豊彦の家に黄昏ごろ帰つて来た。豊彦夫婦はお玉の遭難の顛末より丹州が助けて呉れた一条を涙と共に聞き非常に感謝し、丹州は生命の親として鄭重に待遇され、それよりお玉の宅に暫時同棲する事となつた。されど丹州とお玉との両人の仲は一点の怪しき関係も無く極めて純潔であつた。
