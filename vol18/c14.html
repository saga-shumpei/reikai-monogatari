Chapter 14: 蛸の揚壺 [642]

蛸の揚壺〔642〕

§ 1

In the Chikashitsu of Makutsugahara, Kurohime, who is acknowledged by herself and others as one of the Sōheki of the Uranai Kyō and who possesses talents far beyond those of a Shirōto, is sharing a drink with Takayamahiko in an intimate manner.

"Hearken, Takayamasama, the Jisetsu is something that must be awaited. While I have pledged Kairaudōketsu with you, I have feared the Dōeu of the Wakarazuya Buka, the Sendenshi, and the Shinjiya, who are like those who rely upon a Koboku or a Kangan and find no warmth in the Santō, and thus I have exercised Kiga; you went to the Honzan in the land of Fusa, while I crossed over to this Onokorojima, where I have exhausted every Arayuru Saizen Doriyoku for the sake of the Kamisama and the path, and have engaged in Senden with Itsushiyōkenmei. Yet, no matter what I say, the years draw on, and struck by the sense of Mujiyōjinsoku, I have felt a nameless loneliness in my heart, and have wished to live openly as husband and wife; however, having upheld the principle of celibacy until now, I could not perform an act that would be like turning over my palm, and how many times have I spent my days and nights in tears, gazing at the clouds passing in the sky and wishing for a message from the wild geese? Nevertheless, no matter how much I say it is for the sake of the country and the path, it is of no avail to sacrifice the pleasures of a lifetime and persist in a stubborn pretense. At first, they praised me, saying that Kurohime is great and that her words and deeds are consistent, but in the end, they began to say that it is natural for one who acts as an intermediary for the Kamisama to live a celibate life, even if one is a woman, and that there is nothing to admire in it, or that perhaps I have some defect in a part of my body and am living a celibate life out of a refusal to admit defeat. Ah, how absurd. If I am to be spoken ill of even after enduring this much, I thought it would be far better to take the husband I wish to have and live openly, so I finally resolved to carry it out; at first, the stubborn faction, including Natsuhiko and Tsunehiko, gradually withdrew, which left me somewhat bewildered, but as they say, giving birth is easier than worrying, and before I knew it, the matter of our marriage ceased to be a topic of conversation among the Shinjiya, and lately, even wise ladies such as Aohiko, Osetsu, and furthermore Murasakihime have come to submit and enter the faith, and truly, we have moved toward a splendid opportunity. From now on, Takayamasama, you need not hesitate in the slightest; do not merely give orders to me, but as the husband of the illustrious Kurohime, I pray you exercise your rights."

§ 2

Takayamahiko: 'Ah, indeed. As they say, if one waits, the winds of Kairo shall blow. There is nothing so formidable as the power of Jisetsu, yet nothing so Ketsukō.'

Kurohime: 'At this time, Torawaka, Tomihiko, and Kikuwaka, the Sannin, have been gone for Shigonichi since they departed from here, yet they have not returned. I fear that some strange event may have befallen them upon the road. My heart is heavy with Shinpai, and I know not what to do.'

Takayamahiko: 'Do not be so troubled. All things are governed by the power of Jisetsu.'

At the very moment he spoke these words, the Sannin slid in, having quietly opened the stone door.

Kurohime: 'Ah, speak of the devil and he shall appear. Torawaka, were you not exceedingly late? How was the Shiyubi?'

Torawaka: 'Indeed, I shall relate the Isai and Yōsu at my leisure, perhaps even on the morning of Asu. Tell me, Kikuwaka, Tomihiko, did we not encounter a most dire situation?'

Kurohime: 'Though the path was not so distant, why have you returned only now? Is this not the Nanukame? Whenever things go well, you return with loud voices, shouting from the Kadoguchi, yet today you have crept in, cowering and small. This cannot be a tale of great Ketsukō. What mean you by saying you shall tell me on the morning of Asu? Since that time, I have been counting the Hinichi and Mainichi, waiting for your return. Come, speak the truth of the matter quickly, without concealment or reservation.'

Torawaka, scratching his head vigorously, spoke as if with great difficulty:

Torawaka: 'Well, how shall I say it? It is a most grave matter, and I know not how to make my report. However, as for material spoils, the time was premature, and I have reserved them until the moment is ripe; yet, spiritually, we have gained a great harvest.'

Kurohime: 'Again and again you speak of a spiritual harvest, but that is merely your customary excuse. This Kurohime is weary of such talk of spiritual harvests. State clearly before your Queen whether you have succeeded or failed.'

So saying, she sharpened her voice, rounded her eyes, and glared at them.

The Sannin shrank back in fear, and said:

'Alas, since it has come to this, we shall report the Isai in its entirety. As we journeyed toward the foot of Mount Misen, purifying our hearts and bodies and chanting the songs of Kototama, wondering what manner of Kami would appear in the purple-clouded heavens of the Northeast, the mother of the renowned Tamateruhime, Otama-no-kata, fearing the majesty of our Sannin, raised clouds and summoned rain with the infant upon her back, fleeing toward Mount Misen. The earth trembled, thunder roared, and the mountains emitted a sound as if they were crumbling, creating a scene that none could face. Even the three brave generals, Torawaka, Tomihiko, and Kikuwaka, hesitated for a moment, but suddenly your spirit and the spirit of Takayamahiko descended upon us. With our courage increased a hundredfold, we charged straight toward Mount Misen. At that moment, the minions of the Sangokyō appeared from the mountainside, each wielding weapons, and attacked our small band of three with terrifying force. Yet, Kurohime-sama, how could we, possessed by the spirits of Kurohime and Takayamahiko, ever falter? We charged and attacked the swarming enemy like lightning, scattering the sparks of Kototama as we fought. Nevertheless, we possessed but a single blade of nine sun and five bu. The enemy swarmed in the hundreds of thousands, filling the entire mountain. Though we fought to defend ourselves, it seemed that even the divine power of Kurohime-sama could not prevail against such numbers. Thus, you used our bodies as you willed, opening a path of blood, and we retreated toward this place like a rushing waterfall, with the speed of a thousand leagues in a single stroke. It was a feat of valor that would deceive even the gods.'

Kurohime said, 'Hearken, Tomihiko, what Torawaka has just spoken, there can be no error in it, can there?'

Tomihiko replied, 'Indeed, indeed, how could there be any error? You constantly pour your heart of Jinji upon our persons; that Ichinen has brought forth fortune, and your Gobunrei descended in an instant, and that we waged Shishifunjin Ōsen against such a formidable Kiyōteki was entirely due to the Shintoku of you both. If by Manmanichi there had been no Goshiyugo of the Onmitama of you Futakata, no matter how brave we might be, it is certain that we would have been instantly crushed into Kotsupamijin. Yet, that we three alone fought so well and caused the enemy's gall to freeze, regardless of the Keitaijiyō, in the Seishinjiyō, the degree to which we caused the enemy to feel Ikuwaku is beyond measure. Pray, be Oyoroko.'

Kurohime said, 'That is, for the present, Ketsukō. However, what has become of Otama and Tamateruhime?'

Tomihiko said, 'Hey, Kikuwaka, from here on it is your turn. Speak firmly.'

Kikuwaka said, 'Yes, yes, I shall speak. Well, despite all that was said, the opponent was but one woman.'

Kurohime said, 'What, one woman?'

Kikuwaka said, 'Thinking it was but one woman, they came and came from the shadows of things on all sides, as if ants were changing their nests, zoro-zoro-zoro, rushing toward us. The three of us were subjected to the spiritual binding of Tanshu, and our bodies instantly became rigid.'

Kurohime said, 'What, you three?'

Kikuwaka said, 'No, no, heaven forbid. That fellow called Tanshu aimed at us three, and when he attempted to apply spiritual binding and make us rigid, as expected of Kurohime-sama and Takayamahiko-sama, there was no way he could do anything to us three upon whom your divine spirits dwell, and the enemy pressed forward with all their might, exhausting their mortal strength. We three, thinking, "Ah, how interesting, how interesting," had our courage increased a hundredfold, and just as we were about to challenge and fight, the command of the spirits dwelling within us three came: "It is a better strategy for you to withdraw for now, wait for the opportune moment, and prepare for a comeback." As expected of the divine strategy and demonic cunning of Kurohime-sama and Takayamahiko-sama, we could not defy the command of your spirits, and we abandoned the enemy before our very eyes and fled back at full speed.'

Having finished speaking, he wiped away his cold sweat.

Kurohime said, 'Listen here, listen here, while I have been listening like a fool, you, what kind of tall tale are you blowing? It is all a lie, is it not? You were frightened by one or two petty warriors and fled back at full speed, were you not? How could my spirit dwell in such base souls as yours? Do not make a fool of me.'

Torawaka: 'If that be so, could it be that Yotsuashi or some other entity has possessed you, assuming your name?'

Tomihiko: 'That may well be. Did not the elder Toyohiko say as much?'

Kurohime: 'Behold, I told you so. You went to the house of Toyohiko, were made to eat the dust, and returned having offered your apologies, did you not? Oh, what helpless men you are. You, who were chosen and brought all the way from the land of Fusa by Takayama-sama, are you not supposed to be a leader of men? What a spineless display this is.'

Torawaka: 'Say what you will, for in the land of Fusa I am well acquainted with the geography; yet here in Onokorojima, I am a stranger to the terrain, and thus could not wage battle as I had intended. Furthermore, the atmosphere is drowsy, and so I could not perform the activities I had envisioned, nor could I achieve anything of substance. However, one should not be so disheartened by a single failure. One accumulates experience with every failure and eventually attains success; thus, this failure is, in the end, but the threshold to success.'

Kurohime: 'Oh, be silent. Is it not said that a defeated general should not speak of war? Do not wag your tongue with such insolence in so loud a voice. Go into the inner room, eat your fill of barley rice, and rest. You have even caused the sake, which we were enjoying so pleasantly, to lose its warmth. Oh, will you not go to sleep at once?'

With that, she struck the brazier with her long pipe so violently that it nearly broke. The three men clutched their heads and slunk away into the shadows of the inner room.

Kurohime: 'Takayama-sama, pray retire for the night. I have matters to inquire of Ayahiko, and if you remain by my side, I shall feel constrained and unable to speak freely, which would be a trouble. As I am a woman, I shall ask him directly, so please, go to sleep at once.'

Takayamahiko: 'Very well, very well. I shall be a hindrance, I suppose. If that is the case, I shall beg your leave and depart first.'

Kurohime: 'Pray, remember this: it is you who would be the hindrance. Go to the side of Murasakihime or elsewhere, and spend the night in leisure.'

She said this with a cold and haughty expression.

Takayamahiko: 'Ha, ha, ha, the situation has become exceedingly precarious. Come now, I shall retreat before the thunder strikes. Ah, Kuwabara, Kuwabara.'

Leaving these parting words behind, he walked slowly and heavily toward the inner chambers.

Kurohime: 'Though he may appear so, Takayamahiko is, after all, a man of such pitiable honesty. Somewhere within his spirit and soul, he possesses a truly good quality.'

§ 5

Shaking her shoulders, and once again replenishing her Nagagiseru with Tabako, she called out, 'Ayahiko, Ayahiko.'

At this voice, Ayahiko appeared upon the scene, placed his Riyōte upon the floor, and asked, 'Was it I whom you summoned just now?'

Kurohime replied, 'Ah, yes, it is so, it is so; there is something I have been wishing to ask you earnestly for some time past. It has been quite a while since you came here, but tell me, where is your Kunimoto? I have had others ask you various things, but you will not say.
