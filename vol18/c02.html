Chapter 2: 厳の Flower [630]

厳の花〔630〕

§ 1

Between the mountains closing in, upon the spring field of flowers to the right and left, white, purple, and gold, male butterflies and female butterflies flutteringly dance and play in the spring of the Eternal Land; upon the field path at the foot of the mountain where the genge flowers bloom in full, the scenery is most wondrous unto Yoshikohime; the water sound of the valley flows murmuringly, echoing as a distant sound unto Otohiko, and Kamehiko together with Natsuhiko, along with the white-haired old man Toyohiko, slowly towards the humble hut, crossing the stone Dengakubashi, treading upon and disturbing the dandelion flowers, at last arrived at the front entrance of the half-collapsed thatched house.

Toyohiko knocked upon the door of the entrance, which had become shaped like the Third-Month hishimochi, and said:

"Hey, hey, old woman, here are guests; wilt thou not open quickly?"

The old woman said: "Is that Master Toyohiko? Pray wait a moment; both the sill and the lintel have become tilted, biting the door so that it will not open in a trice. Otama's body is not free to move, so, old man, pray add thy strength from the outside as well. Ah, ah, when one suffers poverty, even the door distorts itself in an ill-favored manner, and the walls reveal their bones perchance because our fortune hath grown lean; 'tis a pitiful thing, even if such splendid guests come to such a thatched hut as this, there is no place for them even to sit down."

Toyohiko, together with the old woman, exerted the adamantine strength of old age from within and without, and the moment he jerked it forcibly to the left, the half-broken old door came off its sill and collapsed with a thud into the inside.

Toyohiko said: "Ah, what an unthinking old woman, a true door-overturner; now, now, guests, pray pass right into the inner room."

Kamehiko said:

"Grandfather, though thou tellest us to pass into the inner room, where is any inner room? As soon as one enters the entrance, is it not already the back door? In the Uranai teachings, in the innermost of the inner rooms there are yet further inner rooms, but what a narrow house like a box-pillow this is!"

Otohiko, feeling sorry, said:

"Hey, hey, Kame, thou art making merry again; wilt thou not keep silent a little? How rude!"

Kamehiko said: "Yes; indeed, this fellow the deputy guardian does not obey the orders of Kamehiko, so it is troublesome. Hello, old man, pray do not let it offend thy feelings; it was spoken by a guest who hath entered my humble hut."

"It must be so; he is one of the guests who hath entered my humble hut; if thou speakest so noisily, it will affect the maiden's body; pray be a little quiet."

Kamehiko, lowering his voice, said:

"Yes, I have understood; however, do not despise me overmuch; though I appear thus, I shall do no such insolent thing as would affect the maiden's body."

Toyohiko said, 'Here now, old woman, wilt thou not bring forth a Zabuton? Wilt thou not serve tea? Prithee, my Hime, pray sit thee down.'

The old woman said, 'Every one of you, ye are right welcome. Though it be sudden, I would fain ask of you: we husband and wife are of a truth most unfortunate. We brought a wife for our one Musuko, and sent them on a Sanpai unto Manaisan of Hinu; yet on their way, they were carried off by the subordinates of an Akunin called Onikumohiko of Ōeyama, and we know not whether they be alive or dead. To this day, no tidings have reached us. Furthermore, our one Imōtomusume hath been strange of body for about a year and a half; she began to say she wished to eat sour things, and her belly hath swelled day by day, and from day to day. Though eighteen months have already passed, she suffereth from an incomprehensible Gofubiyō, being neither Chōman nor yet appearing to be with child. In such a single house deep in the mountains, the daughter being by nature an Okubiyōmono, who until her present Jifuhatsusai hath never parted from her parents' side even for half an hour, there is no reason why a child should dwell within her. Yet when we press her belly, a great lump moveth about with a rumbling sound, so that we know not what is what, and for the sake of our only daughter under heaven and earth, we aged Fūfu spend our days in tears of weeping. And what is beyond our comprehension, a little while ago a lady called Eikohime-sama, a preacher of the Three-Five Religion, accompanied by splendid servants, turned aside unto this thatched hut, looked intently upon the daughter's condition, and spake words saying that this is pregnancy, wherefore we should take great care of her. Were it pregnancy, it ought to have been born long ago; yet though it hath been eighteen months, there is no sign nor tidings. What Eikohime-sama spake was that within four or five days she would send a splendid preacher, wherefore we should rely upon him to have the child born safely. Could such a thing exist since ancient times, that a child should be conceived without a partner?'

Etsukohime said, 'Ah, how anxious ye must be! Let me look upon her for a moment.'

So saying, she drew near to the side of Otama, stroked her belly, and said,

'Ah, this is indeed pregnancy. Nevertheless, it is by no means a child born of the breath between human and human. Is there not anything that cometh to thy mind?'

Toyohiko said, 'Hearing thee ask thus, it cannot be said there is nothing. In the beginning of the autumn of the year before last, in my dream, a white-haired old man of strange appearance visited this thatched hut, gave me five jewels that could not be likened unto splendid crystal or lapis lazuli, and spake saying, "I give these unto thee, wherefore make thy daughter swallow them." Thereupon I asked, "I have understood. Nevertheless, can such hard things be swallowed?" Whereupon that person spake saying, "I shall make her swallow them; they are by no means hard to swallow." So saying, he firmly embraced the body of Otama, and just as I thought he forcibly pushed them near her chest, I awoke. Then, because my daughter Otama was groaning heavily in a nightmare, I shook her awake, and the body of Otama was drenched all over with sweat; she had seen a dream identical to the dream I had seen, and she said that thereafter her body had somehow become painfully agonizing beyond endurance. Saying that at any rate it was a matter of a dream, so her pain would likely be healed by tomorrow, we slept that night. When the dawn broke, Otama was still groaning as before. Ever since, unto this day of eighteen months, her belly hath merely swelled by degrees, nor can she move her body freely. Strange things indeed exist; might it not be the doing of some evil god?'

Yoshikohime said, 'Ah, do not be anxious; far from an Akugami, the noble Mitama of a magnificent Kamisama is accommodated within her. Surely the Ōkami of the Izuno-Mitama is about to be born. I shall now offer up prayers to Kamisama.'

Even as she was intensely offering up prayers in a low voice, speaking I know not what, Otama gave a single cry, saying, 'Un!' and for the first time sat upright, as if in a dream, saying,

'Ah, I am thankful; now I too shall be saved. The white-haired Kamisama who appeared to me some time ago declared that this shall be the birth of the Izuno-Mitama, the foremost among the seven women. Prithee, make ready for the delivery of the child; strong labor pains have come upon me.'

Toyohiko and his wife were astonished, saying,

'Ah, that is a gravity indeed! We must boil hot water at once.'

Yoshikohime said, 'Grandfather, Grandmother, please remain quietly right here. Come now, Kamehiko and Natsuhiko, boil the hot water quickly.'

Kamehiko replied, 'Yes' (in a strange voice), 'Now, Natsuhiko, I thought nothing good would come of this; to come into the depths of such mountains and be made to boil water even for a child-birth—is this not an unexpected honor?'

Natsuhiko said, 'Thou shouldst not utter such profane things! A splendid Kamisama is about to give birth; to be employed in the margins of such service must be due to no ordinary karmic affinity. How could such a duty be commanded us? Ah, how thankful, how auspicious!'

Otama groaned, 'Un, un!'

Otohiko said, 'Come, quickly, Kamehiko and Natsuhiko, will ye not boil the hot water for her?'

'Yes, yes!'

So saying, they poured water into a broken pot, set the wooden lid securely upon it, snapped firewood into pieces, blew upon the embers of the brazier to light them, raised a breeze with a cushion, and poured all their strength into boiling the water. Suddenly, the voice of the newborn baby was heard,

'Hogyaa, hogyaa, hogyaa!'

'Ah, auspicious, auspicious! Come, let us bind the belly-band for her.'

So saying, Yoshikohime briskly went behind Otama, firmly bound the belly-band, and said,

'Come, now all is safe with this. Grandfather, Grandmother, be at ease.'

The old man and old woman said, 'Yes, yes, we are thankful. That ye have even performed the delivery fills us truly with awe.'

'Come, the water seems to have boiled. Let me see, I shall bathe the child. Ah, because of having been inside the womb for so long a time, what a magnificent child it is!'

§ 4

Thus Otohiko took the Akago into both his hands, and having adjusted the temperature of the warm water, he proceeded to bathe the child together with Yoshikohime. Inside the washbasin, the Akago raised its voice and wept, its face turning scarlet as though its Karada were scorched by fire.

Yoshikohime, full of joy and alacrity, spoke:

"Ah, what a magnificent and Jiyōbu child this is. Grandfather, Grandmother, Otama-sama, pray be at Goanshin."

The Sannin remained Mokunen, shedding tears and bowing their heads down.

"Is this not a most Fushigi thing indeed, nay, Natsuhiko? That a child should be born after eighteen months is a thing unheard of in Zendai. Whenever the year-end season cometh, we ever weep over our Tarahi (lack), yet this tiny baby, warmed through and through, likewise weepeth in a Tarahi (washbasin), ahaha!"

Natsuhiko spoke: "Now, now, Kame, if thou utterest such a loud voice again, what shalt thou do if it bringeth harm to the Karada of Otama-sama?"

"As for bringing harm, Kame is of a different office; is not Yoshikohime-sama touching her? Ahaha!"

Otohiko spoke: "Now, now, ye Riyōnin, wilt thou not be quiet?"

"Yea, we humbly obey."

Otama spoke: "Minasama, I have received your great Osewa. Is the child that was born a male or a female?"

Otohiko spoke: "Oh, indeed, indeed; in my exceeding joy I forgot to examine. Ah, regrettable though it be, it is divided."

Toyohime spoke: "Eh, is it a female again? It seemeth that my house cannot be governed unless by an adopted heir. Even when I thought to take a bride for my son that he might succeed us, as I stated a short while ago, his whereabouts are unknown; thus an adopted heir must needs be taken for the younger sister, Otama. The eldest child born this time hath likewise come to take an adopted heir."

Kamehiko, making merry once more, spoke:

"Grandfather, congratulations! By this the fortune of thy house shall also open. It is said that if adopted heirs continue for three generations, one becometh a wealthy man. Pray rejoice; I too am glad and joyous, knowing not where my hands dance nor where my feet tread. Dockoisho, dockoisho!"

Thus leaping up, he missed his footing on the Dengaku Bridge and fell splash into a small ditch.

Kamehiko spoke: "Alas, I have soaked my precious clothes!"

Natsuhiko spoke: "Haha, what a flurried fool thou art!"

Yoshikohime spoke: "Ye need no longer worry over anything; all is well. Shalt thou bestow a name upon the child?"

Toyohiko spoke: "Indeed we are most grateful; as this is a child who hath received the care of your ladyship, pray be pleased to grant her a name."

Yoshikohime spoke: "I have understood. Then I shall bestow a name upon her; let her be named Tamateruhime."

Toyohiko, Toyohime, and Otama, all in one voice at the same time, spoke:

"We offer our heartfelt thanks."

Yoshikohime spoke: "Now, now, I shall henceforth go to worship at the mountain. On my return I shall call upon ye at leisure. Farewell."

And so saying, she crossed the threshold swiftly. The three, saying nothing, joined their hands in prayer and bowed down toward Yoshikohime. Otohiko spoke:

"Now, Kamehiko, Natsuhiko, it is time to depart for the front!"

And following after Yoshikohime, he turned back upon the path by which they had formerly come; the four of them once more crossed the step-ladder of tree roots on the steep slope that crept out onto the path, ascending deeper and deeper into the interior.

悦子姫『サアサア私は之からお山へ参拝を致して参ります。又帰りがけに悠くり伺ひます、左様なら』

と早くも門口を跨げる。三人は何も云はず手を合して悦子姫の方に向つて拝ンで居る。音彦は、

『サア、加米彦、夏彦出陣だ』

と悦子姫の後に従ひ旧来し道に引返し、四人は又もや道に這ひ出た急坂の木の根の段梯子を渡つて奥へ奥へと登り行く。
