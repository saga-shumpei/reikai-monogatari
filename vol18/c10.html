Chapter 10: 赤面黒面 [638]

赤面黒面〔638〕

§ 1

Having completed the Misogi in the Tanigaha, Umekō and his party returned once more to the mansion in the Chitei.

Umekō spoke: 'Takayamahiko-sama, Kurohime-sama, thanks to you, the Akurei of Ōeyama have been completely Taisan. It is as if all around has become light without cause, and my Meiseki Zunau has become Masumasu Meiseki; now, the Kotsupon of the Daiuchiu, in the Genkai, Shinkai, and Iukai, has become as clear to my Shinkiyō as if held in my hand, and all Bantan are reflected therein. Truly, what is called Misogi is a Ketsukō thing.'

Kurohime replied: 'Is that not so, is that not so? It is always thus, and that is why I have told you to receive the water morning, evening, and noon, whenever there is a moment to spare. The Gōn of fire, water, and earth is Daiichi. You have grumbled and complained, saying that to be always doused in water is the practice of a frog, but today, you must have Gaten, must you not?'

Umekō replied: 'Ah, I have understood it thoroughly. It was a grateful thing that Hinode-no-Kami-sama and Ryūgū-no-Otohime-sama deigned to possess my physical body; it was most Ketsukō.'

Kurohime replied: 'Now, now, Umekō, what arrogant and insolent things do you say? Hinode-no-Kami-sama will not possess any but the Mitama of the pure and original Kotsupon of the Makoto-Suishō, and furthermore, Ryūgū-no-Otohime-sama would not possess just anyone, for she possesses only those Mitama with the proper Inen. As the Mitama of the Hensei-Danshi has said: "From of old, the Kami have not spoken. At the turning point of the world, the Kami must possess someone to make known certain things to the people of the world; thus, the Kami possess the Mitama of Inen to explain in detail the matters from the beginning of the world to the end, and the Inen-Seirai of the Mitama, in order to cause the people of the world to repent. While an Inari may possess anyone, the true Ōkami does not possess a Negi or a Miko." How could such Ketsukō Kami possess the Mitama of a base person? Could there be two living shrines of Hinode-no-Kami, or could living shrines of Ryūgū-no-Otohime be created here and there? You, at the slightest success, immediately become conceited and full of pride, and at the slightest scolding, you immediately turn pale and become utterly dejected. That is because your abdominal band is not yet tightened. Since you are to be given service appropriate to your Mitama, do not be conceited, do not become puffed up, and do not fall into disarray. Beneath the lighthouse it is pitch dark; you cannot see the ink upon your own face. If you think to walk through the world looking only at the sky, you shall stumble upon a high stone and be forced to turn a somersault. It is well for each to be mindful, that you may not be left with your mouth agape and unable to close it.'

§ 2

Umekō said, 'Ah, I understand. However, as the Gokaurin was temporary, there is no help for it.'

Kurohime replied, 'What is it that you are saying? In Shinpeu, there are two Shiyurui: Kōhiyō and Shihiyō. Among them, what is called Shihiyō is that which does not possess one unless it be according to the depth of the Innen of the Mitama. Kuniharutachinomikoto is the Nikutai of the Henjiyōnanshi, the Hinode-no-Kami is the Nikutai of the Hitsupō, and the Otohime of Riugū is likewise the Nikutai of that Hitsupō; it is firmly established thus. It is not like those Jidaraku Kami who, being called Kōhiyō, possess the Mitama of the higher, the middle, and the lower realms in an Rinki-Ōhen manner. What do you take the Otohime of Riugū to be? Read a little of the Fudesaki. If you but bind the Fudesaki to your belly, at the Masaka moment, She will grant you whatever Shinriki may be required. You all merely utter sophistry, saying the characters are poor, or difficult to read, or tedious, or lacking in Shiyubi-ikkan, or that the Nikutai are mixed, or that this part is a divine oracle and that part is a human teaching; that is why you end up understanding nothing at all. You err because you attempt to Saniwa the Fudesaki written by the living shrine of the Hinode-no-Kami. From now on, you must Itada with the conviction that it is absolutely sacred.'

Umekō said, 'Am I not a Mitama with the Innen to open this path?'

Kurohime replied, 'That is a settled matter. Without Innen, could you have been drawn to this magnificent Uranai-kyō?'

Umekō said, 'Among those with the Innen of the Mitama, I must be one with the deepest Innen. The name Takahime-sama has an Innen with Takamagahara, and Kurohime-sama has an Innen with the Fudesaki which speaks of the flower of the mass of suffering blooming; am I not a name that appears in the first Fudesaki of the Henjiyōnanshi, which speaks of the plum blossoms of the three thousand worlds opening all at once? Say what you will, a plum is a plum, and the role of opening all at once must be the duty of the guardian Kami of Umekō.'

Kurohime replied, 'In this vast world, you are not the only one who bears the name of plum.'

Umekō said, 'Even so, did you not just say that you have drawn only those with the Innen of the Mitama? Is there even one person among those drawn to the Uranai-kyō who bears the name of plum? Matsuhime-sama, who has an Innen with the pine and is said to govern with the pine, wields such influence at Mount Takagi; why is it that I, Umekō, am so disparaged by you?'

Kurohime said, 'Engage in your Shiugefu a little more. Then, it may be that you shall come to stand shoulder to shoulder with Matsuhime. However, as today I shall directly perform the Senden to Ayahiko and Otami through the Nikutai of Otohime of Riugū, you also must listen firmly. And, having fastened the manner of this Nikutai’s Senden well into your belly, you must open the Sekai with Makoto.'

Umekō replied, 'That is Arigata Gozaimasu. We, too, have obtained the honor of Bauchiyō...'

Kurohime said, 'Here, here, one should not speak too much... for a man... the mouth is the gate of calamity. Be silent and Kinchiyō.'

While suppressing him thus, she assumed a slightly bent Koshitsuki and, raising a voice that was Sokobuto, began to sing:

From the ancient past, that ancient past, From the beginning of the distant Kamiyo, Kuniharutachi no Ōkami, Bearing the Chikura no Okido, Hid the world in the Ushitora. During those Sanzennen, Enduring Kurau and Kannan, To open this world for Toha, Disguising His body in various ways, He performed the protection from the shadows, And awaited the Miyo of Miroku. When the time arrived, like parched beans, The Miyo changed and flowers bloomed, The Mitama of the Hensei-nanshi, Used as a tool from the ancient times, Seeing through to the very end of the end, He brought forth the precious Ofude, Raising the gods who had fallen in the world, Together at this time, Bestowing upon each their name, And causing them to stand for the service of God.

The time having come, Otohime-sama of Riugū, Being a god of keen perception, Cast aside all the gold, silver, jewels, and coral, That she had stored as her life until now, And offered them to Ōkami-sama, And having cast away her impure heart, Flowing it into the sea, Kurohime appeared, As a Mitama of destiny, And as the organ for the achievement of Shinsei, How noble is Her manifestation! Until now, the Mitama-take of Otohime of Riugū, Which had been unsightly, Was possessed by a Mitama of the lineage, But she performed repentance and was the first, To be a clever one who underwent Kaishin.

The gods who had fallen into the world of the Sanzen-sekai, Should take Otohime-sama of Riugū as their model, And with all their might, polish their Mitama, And upon being of service to this great plan, Receive their respective names, And be enshrined as magnificent gods. The Ume-no-hana of the Sanzen-sekai, Opening all at once means this: Otohime-sama of Riugū, who long dwelt, At the very bottom of the sea, Has settled into the temple of flesh, And to the Tamikusa of the wide world, Reveals the work of the one mechanism of Makoto, Accompanied by the God of Hinode, Becoming the land for this service, Achieving the great ambition of God, And showing to the three Ōkami-sama, Who dwell in Heaven, that the world of earth, Has become thus.

It is a Shigumi of great Tegara. This Shigumi is written in the Fudesaki of the Henjiyōnanshi, as if to contain the Ananahi Oshihe of the Kami and the Izumitama. The Mizu-Mitama, blind and lacking in the manner of reading that Fudesaki, the Henjiyōniyoshi, mixes and reverses it. Like a procession of frogs facing the Mizu, they find fault with the Fudesaki of the Hinode-no-Kami, appearing as the Mizu-Mitama and acting with Erasau, even mocking the Kurohime as Baka. Yet, Kochira has come forth enduring and enduring to the end; but as it is not understood until Itsu, Zehi, I must cut the ledger, reveal the mirror of evil, and leave it written for Mangōmatsudai. The Henjiyōnanshi is Hitori.

The Henjiyōniyoshi is also Hitori. What is this about the Mitsu-Mitama? How can there be three? This is where the Machiga begins. The Henjiyōnanshi is the role of the warp, and the Henjiyōniyoshi is the role of the weft. It is a Shigumi to weave a brocade loom by bringing the warp and weft into Wagafu. Do you not know that the Hinode-no-Kami becomes the earth, and with the Tetsuda of the Riugūsama, the Kondo Ichiban Goshiyutsuse will be performed? By all means, do not be led astray by the weft Oshihe of the Ananahi. With the warp at Shichibu and the weft at Sanbu, if it is not thus, the magnificent true loom cannot be woven; yet the Ōmoto of the Ananahi-Oshihe has gradually fallen into disorder, and the warp is at Sanbu and the weft at Shichibu.

The Henjiyōnanshi runs ahead, and the Henjiyōniyoshi is the Miroku-sama; they line up selfish arguments and wrap things in smoke. If this were left abandoned as it is, the long years of hardship and toil of the Henjiyōnanshi would become bubbles on the water. Thinking that I shall not let it become bubbles on the water, the Hinode-no-Kami appears, selects the Mitama of the lineage, and writes down the true Fudesaki. Though I urge them to read it once, the stubborn Henjiyōniyoshi, thinking how could this ever be read, raises their voice in anger and throws it away. I told them that if they dislike it so much, they need not read it, and not to cry later, but the stubborn one who understands not the reason takes the grace of the Kami as an enemy.

No matter how much kindness is exhausted, Takahime laments that there is no longer any way to save them, and always sheds tears, for such is her heart of mercy. Following that, the one who does not understand is the Ōmikami of Kanakatsu-no-Kami; this physical body is also stubborn, and there are those who are even more stubborn. Even if the earth should sink, or the cord of life should be severed, they reject the Fudesaki of the Hinode-no-Kami, saying it is not to be relied upon. Whether it be a lie or the truth, the man and woman who have appeared as the Ikimiya of Konohana-hime, whether fire turns to a serpent or whatever may happen, I shall not leave them until I make them repent. From the vertical, from the horizontal, and from all eight directions, I shall send the Sagume and the Shikome.

Though much labor is expended, she is by nature dull and foolish; it is as if one were to offer a sermon unto a stone Jizau, for she will by no means listen. Unless the Nikutai of Hanahime be brought to Kaishin, the Shigumi of Kami shall never be accomplished, nor shall the Uranai-Kyō prosper. Come now, from this time forth, follow the Sashizu of Kurohime, disguise thy form, and move with Senpenbankuwa; pray that the Mitama of Innen may be brought to Kaishin as swiftly as even a single Hitohi. Kurohime also, for Hiyakunichi and Hiyakuya from this day, shall purify her body with the birth-waters of the Tanigaha of Kensakiyama, and offer Kinen with Itsushinfuran. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara!

May the Mitama grant favor and blessing.'

Thus he spoke, dripping with sweat, his entire Shintai emitting steam in rising plumes.

'Ah, what suffering. ...If one desires to save the people of the world, it is no ordinary task; the hardships of the Henshei-Nanshi and Takahime-sama pierce my very soul, and I feel such pity for them, oh, oh, oh,' he cried, breaking down in tears.

Kurohime trembled her lips, glared with eyes upturned, and gnashed her teeth, consumed by wrath.

Takikō spoke, 'Pray, Lady Kurohime, there is a saying that one should not give way to anger. Are you not indeed enraged? I pray you, smile a little.'

Kurohime replied, 'Umu, umu.'

She continued to gnash her teeth and stare with wide, glaring eyes.

Murasakihime addressed them, 'You there, who call yourselves Taki and Ita, I pray you, calm yourselves to a reasonable degree. Last night, we, along with three others, witnessed you tormenting Lady Osetsu. Lady Kurohime is by no means the one who did this. A man of my company, named Shika, took advantage of the darkness to mimic the voice of Lady Kurohime. It is most pitiable of you to claim that Lady Kurohime was the culprit; Lady Osetsu has come into this inner chamber and is sleeping soundly.'

'What is this you say? Lady Osetsu is... that is a grave matter indeed.'

Murasakihime replied, 'Do not be so troubled, for Lord Aohiko has also arrived.'

Takikō exclaimed, 'Alas, if we are careless, we know not what fate may befall us. They have come for vengeance, to purge this cave. Hey, Itakō, never mind Lady Kurohime. Life is the seed of all things; let us retreat from this place as quickly as possible before we are discovered.'

Taki then rushed out. Ita followed, saying, 'Hey, I understand.'

And they pursued him.

Kurohime cried out, 'Hey, you there, Takikō, Itakō, wait, wait! I have something to say.'

The two, Taki and Ita, peered from outside the cave into the interior and said, 'Lady Kurohime, farewell. It would be well for you to be squeezed of your fat by Aohiko and Osetsu at your leisure. Goodbye! Ha ha ha! Ufu fu fu!'

Having said this, they vanished, and from that time forth, they were seen no more within the Uranai-kyō.

Murasakihime, feeling pity, said, 'I pray you, Lady Kurohime, though you must be angry, I beseech you to forgive the words of these young men. As the saying goes, to employ others is to employ suffering; I truly sympathize with your great distress.'

Kurohime replied, 'You have spoken with such kindness. It is troublesome that they speak such nonsense. Yet, this is by no means, never by any means, the doing of Taki or Ita themselves. They are not men who would commit such evil deeds, but the kin of the evil spirits of Susanoo-no-Mikoto have possessed them, seeking to torment us, and thus they speak and act in such a manner. One must not be off guard for even a moment. Takikō and Itakō, used by the evil spirits, are truly pitiable beings. On, on, on.'

She then feigned weeping.

Murasakihime said, 'Lady Kurohime, would it not be well for you to rest now? It seems the night has grown quite late.'

Kurohime answered, 'Yes, thank you. In that case, I shall take my leave first. Tomorrow, I shall have you listen to the fundamental story at our leisure.'

Murasakihime replied, 'Yes, thank you.'

And so, they both retired to sleep.
