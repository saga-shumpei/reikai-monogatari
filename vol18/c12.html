Chapter 12: Great 当違 [640]

大当違〔640〕

§ 1

The moon wanes and yearns for the mountain; they say that when a man grows old, he speaks of the Way in vain. At the foot of Mount Misen, Toyohiko of the humble Fuseya, a Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō, having had his daughter Otama saved by the retinue of Hime Yoshiko, gazed upon the face of his first grandchild, a child of excellence beyond the world, and the Raufūfu cherished her as if she were a butterfly or a flower, preaching and instructing in the teachings of Kami.

This matter spread far and wide, as if by the wind, at all times; the old man of the village of Oyogi, together with Mount Misen, became high in name. Rauniyakunanniyo came in a continuous stream, as if ants gathering to sweetness, revering the Kyōji of the old man Toyohiko as if he were Kami.

Day and night, from dawn until dusk and throughout the night, they came to worship, seeking salvation.

Among them, three Ōotoko stood out, wearing the garments of a Sendenshi, and shouted while standing at the gate:

"Pardon us. We are those called Tomihiko, Torawaka, and Kikuwaka. Having visited the shrine of Mount Misenzan this time, we heard by the divine decree that a person named Toyohiko has appeared in a house at the foot of this mountain, and because he transmits the true teaching, you three shall stop by on your way home, invite that Toyohiko to the Uranai-kyō, and bring back the daughter Otama and the newly born Tamateru-hime to the main mountain... such was the clear divine revelation. Since the words of Kami cannot be doubted, while searching here and there at the foot of Mount Misen, we asked those passing by and heard that the house beyond the forest of Oyogi is the residence of Toyohiko-sama, and thus we have come here by divine decree."

They were shouting while standing at the gate. Fortunately, there were no visitors today, and the Raufūfu, the daughter, and the grandchild, the four of them, were in the midst of kneeling and praying in silence before the sacred altar where the divine spirit of Misen was enshrined. Toyohiko finished his prayers, approached near the gate, and said:

"I know not who you are, but it seems there is someone at the gate asking for something. Whoever you may be, first open the door and enter."

"Yes, thank you," said Torawaka.

He slid open the rain shutter that was set diagonally with a clatter, and Torawaka said:

"Well, this is quite a splendid house... Hey, Tomihiko, Kikuwaka, come in... There is a beautiful young lady who does not suit this filthy house. Shall I call her an iris blooming in a sewer, or a mountain cherry at the bottom of a valley? If she were transplanted to a better place, she would surely become a magnificent person."

"Now, now, as you say, it is a wretched thatched hut, but even so, this is my only place of rest... Is that not too rude?" said Toyohiko, as he stepped down to the entrance with heavy footsteps, treading roughly upon the torn tatami mats, and glared at the faces of the three men.

§ 2

Torawaka said, 'Ah, Grandfather, I have truly committed a Shitsugen. I did not mean it ill at all. I have spoken only what was reflected in my heart, honest and Shiyōmei, without the slightest adornment; therefore, I pray you do not take it amiss. To call a crooked house a magnificent Jiutaku would, on the contrary, be an act of Chōrō. It is like catching a Kame and telling her she is a Betsupin; she would be angered, thinking she was being treated as a Baka. In any case, those who serve the way of Kami have not a particle of Shinshiyaku or Jiyōzu. If I have offended you, I pray you, forgive me.'

Toyohiko replied, 'That is as you say, yet to criticize our Jiutaku suddenly, without even a proper Aisatsu, is not something that makes one feel at ease. You said you were a Sendenshi, but one would think you might know whether such words would offend a person's Kanjiyō or not.'

Torawaka said, 'What I just said was not Torawaka at all. It was the Kenzoku of the Ōkami who dwells in Misenzan, the Tengu Torawaka, who spoke. Since those called Tengu have fallen in the world and do nothing but the Shitabatara of the Kamisama, they possess neither manners nor knowledge of etiquette, and there are even drunkard, dissolute Tengu; I pray you, forgive me. Because my Mitama is polished too highly, I am sensitive and easily possessed, which is a trouble, ha ha ha.'

Toyohiko said, 'And what is the nature of the divine decree? Let us hear it quickly.'

Torawaka said, 'As you know, I have a very obedient Mitama, and various Kami possess me, so one must perform strict Saniwa; however, this Sendenshi named Tomihiko is a truly Ritsupa person. In truth, there was a divine decree for Tomihiko. Come, Lord Tomihiko, inform Grandfather of the particulars of the divine decree.'

Tomihiko, folding his hands and assuming an overbearing posture, said, 'Here, I am Konohanasakuya-hime, who guards Misenzan. That I have sent Tamateru-hime, the spirit of Konohana-hime, to your house this time is a matter of deep design; since all things are caused by Kami, she is my child, yet she is not my child. This Tamateru-hime, who was conceived within the body and born in the tenth month, was caused to perform service as your daughter, after I sought out a Mitama with ancient connections to serve the purpose of Kami. Come, from now on, it is well that you set this Tamateru-hime to the service of Kami. If you do not consent to what Kami says, I shall cause you to consent by making you consent. What is your reply, Toyohiko? I shall hear it.'

§ 3

Toyohiko, with a composed countenance, smiled with a grin and gazed at the faces of the three men with rolling eyes.

"Ha-ha-ha-ha, you fellows, you perform your tricks with great cleverness. Thinking me a mere rustic old man, you have come plotting to deceive me, but though I may appear thus, this old man is not one to be easily gulled by vinegar or konniyaku. Since I am seven or eight ranks above you as an actor, I shall not be taken in by such schemes. Ah-ha-ha-ha! Indeed, the children of men may be born in ten months, but the child of this place is of a different seed from such merchandise. The Kami, too, are unreliable, are they not? If in truth the Ōkami has possessed you and orchestrated this, then you should know when this Tamateruhime was conceived, in which month she was delivered, and who it was that acted as the midwife. Come, pray tell me that."

Tomihiko, sweating profusely and with a face turned deathly pale, spoke:

"Yea, that which was called the Ōkami was in fact a Kenzoku. ...Ke-ke-ke, the Kenzoku shall now withdraw. This time, the true Ōkami-sama shall possess me, so you must not be rude. Umu."

Having said this, he fell flat, then once more waved his hands, straightened his posture, and said:

"This time it is the authentic Kami. Your heads are too high; bow down, bow down, lower yourselves..."

Toyohiko replied, "Hmph, again? In any case, it is likely no decent Kami. It is probably a Tengu without wings or a fox without a tail. It is no small feat that you perform such tricks without reward in this heat. You are a person of deep compassion; on that point alone, this old man is greatly grateful. Two or three others came this morning, and it was quite amusing to have a wild Tengu-possessed fellow like you come and blow your conch shell. If you are a missionary of the divination faith, you must undergo further training. With such conduct, you cannot even dream of saving the sentient beings."

Tomihiko retorted, "How dare you be so rude to the Ōkami! Do you mock this Kami? You shall not be forgiven."

Toyohiko said, "Ha-ha-ha-ha, it is I who shall not forgive you. Come, I shall perform a Saniwa upon you. ...On what day was my daughter Otama's pregnancy? How many months was she with child? Speak clearly. It is somewhat different from the ordinary, crudely manufactured goods that are made in about ten months. Tamateruhime is one who was specially prepared by the Kami world, with thought upon thought, and forging upon forging. Come, come, try to guess."

§ 4

Tomihiko said, 'It is twelve months. There can be no mistake. Because this Otama stepped over the rope of an ox, it has taken twelve months. How is it? Are you humbled?'

Toyohiko replied, 'Ah, ha, ha, ha! You there, Mr. Tomihiko, pray cease such antics. You are sweating quite profusely.'

Tomihiko said, 'The Ōkami, desiring to speak and impart matters of great excellence, has possessed this Mitama of Innen to make it known; yet this old man is stubborn in his self-will and will not perform Kaishin in the slightest. Therefore, the Kami has no choice but to cut the account and withdraw. I shall warn you to take heed, that you may not regret it later.'

Toyohiko replied, 'Yes, yes, I am most grateful. Whether you be a badger or a fox, I know not, but though it may appear thus, this house is a magnificent shrine of the Kamisama. Hey! This is no place for a four-legged creature to enter. It is unclean; pray, depart. Tamateruhime-sama is in a very foul mood. Come, come, be off with you, be off with you!'

So saying, he took up a broom and began to sweep him out. Tomihiko, feeling Temochibusata, stood wiping his face with a Tenuguhi.

Torawaka said, 'Hey, old man, is that not going too far? Is there any law that says one should sweep a person out with a broom as if they were dust or some such thing? You are of a good age; would it not be well to learn a little Giyōgi and Sahafu?'

Toyohiko replied, 'Hey, hey! For a four-legged creature that comes sauntering into the shrine of the Kamisama, what need is there for Giyōgi or anything of the sort? Giyōgi is something necessary only between humans, or between humans and the Kamisama who are above them. If you dally and prattle, this broom shall descend upon your head.'

Kikuwaka firmly grasped the broom that the old man had raised aloft.

Kikuwaka said, 'Pray, excuse me, Grandfather, please calm yourself. Impatience is a loss. If you disparage Kamigakari as you do, no discourse can be held. There, there, be calm, be calm.'

Toyohiko replied, 'I have no ears to listen to sermons from the likes of you. Though I may appear thus, I, Toyohiko, having received the divine virtue of the Kamisama, belong to no sect, but walk an independent path, serving the Kamisama directly. To save others is the way of the Kami; if you yourself would perform Kaishin and humble yourself, I would teach you any matter of excellence, but with such an attitude, I cannot allow you to remain for even a single breath. Come, come, be gone, be gone!'

Otama said, 'Grandfather, it would be well not to say such cruel things.'

Toyohiko replied, 'Here, here, Otama, you must remain silent. For if you are burdened with Innen by such fellows again, it will be troublesome...'

Torawaka: 'Ah, Lady Otama, you can speak; if you could not, you would not be a woman. Truly, the flower of Shiyakaukai is woman. Aisatsu is the Ujigami of the hour; pray, take upon yourself the labor of Atsusen in this matter. Behold the Okimono upon your alcove. The moment we passed through the gate, did not the image of Gehō bow its long head and offer a polite Aisatsu, saying, "Welcome, you have come well"? Even such a mindless Gehō, in the presence of our Goisei—nay, our Shinkaku—is moved to reverence and sits in awe. Furthermore, this old man is far too Gaujiyō. Since it is the Kataiji of a Toshiyo, he will not consent even if Watashitachi speak to him; therefore, I pray you, Lady Otama, speak to the old man, who has neither eyes nor nose for his daughter, in such a way that his heart may soften.'

Otama: 'Ho, ho, ho, ho.'

Toyohiko: 'Eh, if I tell you to return, will you not return?'

So saying, he performed a Shiko with such force that the floorboards trembled. The Sannin replied:

'Eh, Grandfather, we shall see you again. As the Tenkō is Taihen bad today, we shall consider the Hiyori and come to visit you again without being a Jiyama.'

Toyohiko: 'Eh, do not mumble, but return quickly, for I should be troubled if Lady Tamateruhime were to be in a foul mood. Hey, old woman, bring the salt. I must purify this place, for there is a scent of four-legged beasts that I cannot endure. Aha, ha, ha, ha.'

The Sannin, looking bewildered as if they had been thrust out, departed from the house and stepped onto the steep slope of Mount Misen.

Kikuwaka: 'Hey, shall we take a rest here for a while?'

Torawaka: 'I feel so ill that I have no face to show Lady Kurohime. I have no heart to rest. Even the butterflies and dung-frogs here seem to look upon our faces and mock us. Shall we go somewhere where there are no frogs or butterflies to take a rest?'

With a hurried demeanor, as if pursued from behind, they climbed the steep path until they reached the foot of the three cypress trees.

'Ah, here is a good place to rest. A clear spring also gushes forth. Let us drink some water and set our hands to the second strategic plan at our leisure.'

The Sannin spent their time in idle talk while letting the cool breeze blow beneath the trees.

Kikuwaka: 'Even this Toyohiko, who has become so renowned, has that infant called Lady Tamateruhime, and the fact that she reveals various things has become the talk of the town; thus, like the peach and plum trees that do not speak yet form a path beneath them, believers gather from here and there every day. Every morning, when Lady Kurohime rises and performs her ablutions, purple clouds drift in the eastern sky; she said that it must surely be in the direction of Mount Misen, and commanded me to go and scout it once. When I came to the foot of this mountain alone the other day, the popularity was immense. The source of the purple clouds is undoubtedly that thatched hut. And every night, a most beautiful star has begun to shine in the eastern sky. It is said that a brilliant star always appears in the heavens upon the emergence of a great person; convinced of this, I returned at once to report it. I thought that a girl from such a thatched hut would offer herself to the Uranai-kyo with a ribbon on her head at a moment's notice, but judging by their attitude today, they are not to be moved by ordinary means. Furthermore, Torawaka, my teacher, performed a clumsy spirit-possession from the start and was glared at by the old man; Toyohiko, who appeared as the second line, was glared at by the old man's discernment of spirits and, reeling, was forced into a defensive posture—though it was clever of him to start over by saying, "This was indeed a wild Tengu," he appeared again as a Great Deity, only to be soundly defeated and left with no hope of recovery, finally being swept out with a broom in such a disgraceful manner. If Lady Kurohime or the others were to hear of this, even by chance, it would be so ridiculous that we could not walk anywhere. We must rack our brains and find a way to wash away the shame of Kuaiji. Is there no good idea?'

Torawaka said, 'There is no particular Hauhafu or Shiyudan, but first, it must be the Umekōshiki. That is the Saigo no Shiyudan.'

Tomihiko replied, 'If one fails to perform the Umekōshiki, it becomes the Takiitashiki, and it would be a Taihen matter if one were to be cast out in the Shimahi. Koitsu truly leaves ample Yochi for Jiyukushi and Jiyukukau.'

Torawaka said, 'Nani, the Mokuteki chooses no Shiyudan. Since it is done for the sake of good, there should be no reason for it to become a sin. Shall we not Ketsukau it?'

Kikuwaka said, 'Ah, Ketsukō, Ketsukō, Ketsukō-kedarake, neko-haidarake. It is said that the Ōkami of Misenzan has the cat as a Tsukahashime; let us by all means wear a cat mask this Kondo and perform the Umetakiriu.'

Tomihiko asked, 'What is this Umetakiriu?'

Kikuwaka replied, 'It is to go through the Mannaka. The way of performing by Umekō at the Fukafutauge was very convenient because there were Hachinin of the Mikata. The Takiita play performed near Funaokayama was exposed because there were too few actors. However, with the three of us, what can we do? Even if it is said that three people together have the wisdom of Monju, no suitable plan comes to mind. What a troublesome matter this is.'

Torawaka said, 'I hear that tomorrow is the end of the seventy-five days of mourning for Otama, and she is to make a pilgrimage to Misenzan. How is this? Let the three of us cover our bodies with clubmoss, threaten Otama on her pilgrimage route, bind her tightly and gag her, then lead her away along the mountains. Then, send the others to the old man's house and say, "It is said that your precious Otama has been abducted by evildoers. It is a pity, but we shall exert all our strength to search for her; in return for that reward, will you not lend us Tamateruhime-sama, even for three or four days?" There is no other way but to deceive them thus. What say you, do you agree?'

'Ya, it is not much of a brilliant plan, but if it has come to this, there is no help for it. Well, let us endure with such a measure. But I wonder if it will go well.'

'In any case, let us go and worship the Ōkami of the heavens so that it may go well once. Tonight, the three of us shall pray with all our might before the spirit-fraction of Konohana-hime-sama, praying and praying until we collapse. If we do so, even the Ōkami... will surely say, "It is annoying in the Shimahi... eh, there is no help for it, I shall grant it once." If it were not so, how could we return to the Uranai religion? It would be shameful before Aohiko-sama and Murasaki-hime-sama.'

Saying this, they advanced toward the mountain top. They spent the night before the shrine at the summit, concentrating their prayers for the fulfillment of their desires with all their might. Will the Ōkami indeed grant their petition?
