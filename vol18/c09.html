Chapter 9: Morning の一 Wonder [637]

朝の一驚〔637〕

§ 1

The long night of Banshiu had passed unawares, and in the southeastern sky veiled by Asakiri, the Taiyō hung low and hazy. Takayamahiko rose at last, and with Kurohime, who held a Nagagiseru in her long, slender hand that seemed touched by the shadow of the setting sun, he sat in intimacy, smiling with pleasure as if recalling the dream of Yūbe, amidst a luxury ill-suited to such a Fuben place—the Asaburo, the Tanzen, and the Nagahibachi.

To this place came a newly arrived Fūfuzure, who respectfully placed both hands upon the ground, and Ayahiko spoke: 'Oh, you two, good morning; we were greatly indebted to you last night, and we were allowed to sleep warmly, we two. Pray, though it is your kind Oboshimeshi, we ask that you grant us leave from today.'

Kurohime, with a Kegen look, replied: 'You have only arrived last night, have you not? After speaking so firmly, why do you waver and change your heart in the space of one Hitoyosa? Perhaps it is that you both, as a young couple, are displeased that O-Tami is to be sent to Takashiro-yama; ah, that is not unreasonable for a young couple, yet this is where endurance is required, and as I said the previous night, without a single hardship or worry, the true flower of humanity shall not bloom.'

'We thank you truly for your repeated teachings; however, once we have entrusted ourselves to the Kami, even if we, as a couple, were to be parted in life as it is, we hold no attachment to such things. Yet, having heard last night in your conversation over sake that the eight of you were performing a fixed game to draw me into the Uranai-kyō, and that you had suddenly staged a play, a fool as honest as I cannot possibly live together with those people, so please, I pray you, grant us leave.'

Kurohime said: 'Are you not saying something strange? In the Uranai-kyō, which is of absolute sincerity, how could there be such a fixed play? It is likely that due to the fatigue of your journey, you have seen such a dream.'

Ayahiko replied: 'No, no, it is by no means a dream. When I greeted you last night and thought to go to sleep, as I passed through the corridor, all of you were drunk on sake, and the interesting conversation, heard whether I would or not, resounded in the ears of us two like thunder; it was the boastful tale of the bitter scheme at Fukō-tōge, told in every detail. I felt both anger and terror; the faith I once thought grateful vanished like smoke, and in sheer vexation, we two did not sleep a wink, but waited for the dawn while weeping. It is certainly not a dream; I beseech you, grant us leave.'

§ 2

Kurohime, wearing a look of wonder, said, 'What is this incomprehensible thing you speak of? Are you not perhaps beside yourself? To the young ones here, the Way of Truth is preached daily as if chewing and feeding it to them. There is not one among them who would utter a lie even as thin as a cormorant's feather; they are so honest that they are simpletons, mere items of no use in this present age. Such a thing could never be; it must be some sort of illusion.'

'Nay, nay, it is by no means an illusion or anything of the sort,' replied O-Tami. 'I certainly heard the gentleman speak of it in his boastful tale last night.'

Upon hearing this, Kurohime grew suspicious and said, 'Wait a moment. You speak of strange things; I shall investigate this now. Come here, Asakō, Ikukō, Umekō, Torakō, Tatsukō, Tobikō, all of you! I have something to ask; come forth.'

She shouted this with a voice slightly trembling. At this call, the eight of them appeared one after another, and each sat in a crouched posture, saying, 'For what purpose have you summoned us? We have been of no use at all, and having received such a feast for merely helping a person on occasion, it is far too much to be granted further commands of grace; it is beyond our merit. We have not repaid a single favor, and we are truly ashamed. Ah, you are the person from last night. Well, well, that was fortunate. This is entirely due to the grace of the God of the Uranai faith, and next, due to our own efforts. You must never forget this debt of gratitude.'

Ayahiko gave an indecisive reply: 'Hee.'

O-Tami replied: 'Hin.'

Asakō said, 'Now, you called O-Tami-sama, what is that reply? To make a face like a gaunt horse and say "hin"—are you mocking your savior? Nay, you are taking a defiant attitude, are you not?'

O-Tami replied: 'Hen.'

Kurohime said, 'You eight, will you tell me the story of last night once more in detail?'

Umekō, shrugging his shoulders with a look of triumph, said, 'Ah, you mean the tale of our meritorious deeds from last night? I am glad you asked. I felt that a single telling was insufficient, as if the hardships and toils we endured—or rather, the divine strategies and strange ruses—had not fully penetrated your heart, leaving me unsatisfied. There were, indeed, a great many of the demon's underlings...'

As he rattled on, exaggerating the small into the great, Kurohime said, 'Ah, that is not a lie, is it?'

"Nay, never, never have I uttered a falsehood, nor has the head of a Bōzu, since the hour of my birth; this is a Shiyōshinshiyōmei Monogatari of the net price."

"Yet, didst thou not, Yazen, partake of sake, employ Shiyujiyu Shiyudan, and perform Yahochiyō to compel this side into Shinkau, and didst thou not alternate in recounting thy Tegarabanashi of having dragged them hither? While dwelling within the path of the one true Kami Kyō, what manner of deed is this? Ayahiko and his Fūfu are in great Zannen and are requesting their leave from this moment. No matter how much one may speak to instruct you, you Mahera are Dame; come, from this Tadaima, I excommunicate Asami, Ikugai, and the Rokunin. Oh, how filthy! The Kami-sama have declared that those who destroy the Uranai Kyō shall not come from without, but shall emerge from within the Uranai Kyō itself, so take heed. Even as a Tanbaguri, though fortified with needles of the most Kengo Eugai, bursts asunder from within, you are the Bakuretsudan of the Uranai Kyō, the Tsurayogo of the path of the Kami-sama. Ah, how filthy! Depart at once, and return with all haste."

"What is it that you Otsushiya? Though I am no apprentice of an umbrella-maker, it does not suit the abacus of Ume-kō and his fellows to labor in vain only to be rebuked."

"Yet, as it is said that the frog is undone by its own mouth, hast thou not confessed with thine own mouth even now?"

Ume-kō, feigning ignorance, replied:

"Ah, is that what you mean? Yazen, having partaken of much sake, my spirit was loosened; thereupon, the spirits of the demons of Ōeyama descended to assault us, seeking to obliterate the Kōmyō Tegarabanashi of us eight. They possessed me and all the others; they did not allow me to drink much sake, but the evil spirits consumed it all, and in the end, they borrowed our mouths to employ the stratagem of sowing discord and self-inflicted injury. Truly, evil spirits are not to be taken lightly, ahaha. Hey, Asami, Ikugai, Tora, Tatsu, Tobi, and Taka-kō, if you do not tighten your belly-bands, you too shall be assaulted by demonic spirits as I was last night, and become vessels for demons."

Tatsu-kō replied, "Do not speak so loftily; is it not the case that a demon is possessed within thee as well?"

Ume-kō said, "That is indeed so. We are all the same; in an evil equality, they have possessed all eight of us. Ah, I feel somewhat unwell; I pray you, Takayamahiko-sama, Kurohime-sama, perform the Chinkon of Kami-rei injection with vigor, that the demons may enter no more."

Kurohime replied, "Ohohoho, ah, was that so? I had surmised as much. Take heed from this time forth; I shall perform the Chinkon for you later, so go to the valley stream and purify your bodies thoroughly."

Umekō cried out, 'Hey, you of the seven dwellings of the demons of Ōeyama, come, it is time for purification. Before the demons come again, O Sentakuba, O Sentakuba!'

So saying, he tucked up his skirts, passed through the narrow Ihato, and ran toward the Tanigaha, aiming for Mega. Behind him followed the four: Takayamahiko, Kurohime, Ayahiko, and O-Tami.

Kurohime said, 'Alas, these are evil spirits against whom one cannot be off guard; even as the couple Ayahiko seek to be saved in the path of righteousness, the persistent spirits of the demons come to attempt to drag them down. Takayama-sama, if you do not remain firm, we know not when the evil spirits may strike.'

Takayamahiko replied, 'Indeed, that is so. Come now, you two, do not be anxious; as you have just heard, there are no such evil things in the Uranai-Kyō, so be at peace.'

Ayahiko said, 'Are evil spirits such things as these? Truly, one cannot be off guard.'

Kurohime said, 'They have made arrangements as if spreading a spider's web from corner to corner, so you cannot be off guard for even an instant. As you are new to the Uranai-Kyō, you may not understand when I speak of spirits, but pray with faith for a while, and all things will become clear to you; then your doubts shall melt away like ice.'

O-Tami said, 'Ah, is that so? I have caused you such worry, and I have no words to express my apology. I humbly ask for your guidance.'

Kurohime said, 'Ah, that is well, that is well. Go into the inner chamber and offer your thanks to the Kami for this resolution.'

The two bowed their heads respectfully and proceeded quietly into the room of the altar.

Umekō and the other seven, having barely escaped the assault of the low pressure that had suddenly appeared upon the countenance of Kurohime, felt as though they had escaped the jaws of a tiger in the nick of time, and ran toward the Tanigaha for the sake of misogi. Along the way, Umekō chanted:

Ah, how terrifying, how terrifying, Feeling as though crossing a sword, I wrung out wisdom where there was none, And devised a strategy of deception and bitter sacrifice, At last the purpose was achieved, And I returned in high spirits, Before the presence of Kurohime-sama, Playing the part of a loyal retainer to report, And the face of Enma finally relaxed, Before the eyes of the husband who is like Fukurokuju, Takayamahiko, I spoke at length of my exploits, And Kurohime, too, Relaxed her expression and expressed her admiration, And as a reward, she gave me plenty of sweet sake to drink, Therefore, as the proverb says, Take off your hat when you meet, Forgetting all else, I smacked my lips, And went about intoxicated by the sake.

Whether it be the Fukushiyu or some other, driven by Kōmiyōshin, they spoke of a plot with Utsuka. Yet, the Ten hath a mouth, and the walls have ears. Before they knew it, the Tenmatsu had been overheard in its Itsusai by Ayahiko and that wretch O-Tami. Ignorance is bliss, they say, yet the Kamigokoro remains. In the midst of Shirakahayobune, snoring and crossing the Yumeji, they awoke and looked at one another, saying, 'We have done it well, we have done it finely. My Chishiki is such that even Monjiyubosatsu would go Maruhadashi. There is no other Shiyudan to gather Shinjiya from this point forward.' While they secretly boasted, thinking they had learned a fine thing, and while they stood with Katahiji raised, the high voice of Kurohime-sama rang out.

Thinking this was surely a Gohaubi, the Hachinin rejoiced and took courage, lining up their Suikuwaatama. Then, like Denkuō and Sekikuwa, a voice as terrible as thunder roared. Their Kimotama were taken, their navels extracted, their nails removed, and they were put to shame. They tilted their heads, wondering how they might overcome this Nankuwan. At that moment, the Shiyugojin appeared. The Fukushiyu blew his Hora, and with the eloquence of Furuna, he wrapped Kurohime in smoke, shifting the great responsibility by claiming it was the work of the evil spirits of Ōeyama. With this sudden wit, Ume-kō was deceived by the sweet logic, and the face of Enma instantly transformed into the face of Jizō, a swap between demon and Buddha.

Having barely escaped excommunication, and by the command of Kurohime-sama, they were taught to perform Misogi in the valley stream to cast out the evil spirits that had not even possessed them. They stroked their chests in relief and smoothed their wrinkles. The rise and fall of the nation was still foolishness; it was the management of their own lives in this crisis that mattered. Feeling as though they had been washed away in the river, they finally arrived here. 'Ah, how amusing, how amusing! We shall devise strategies superior to Sun and Wu without limit, and like killing lice, we shall draw all people, leaving not one behind, into the path of the teachings of Uranai. To fall into the laughing-pot of Hanataka-hime and Kurohime—is that not our superior judgment? Oh, Asakō of shallow Chishiki!'

'Oh, Ikukō of weak spirit! If you take even the nails of the hands and feet of Ume-kō, who brings forth wise schemes, and decoct and drink them, you shall become great. Torakō, Tatsukō, Tobikō! From now on, you shall do well to listen to what I say.' The general, Kurohime-sama, exhausted her mouth, speaking at length and with all her might to lead them into the path of Uranai, but as you see, execution is better than debate. Direct action is the only way. Nevertheless, everyone, you must never speak of such a scheme, even in a dream, to Takayama-sama or Kurohime. If it should ever be known, then each and every one of you, under your noses...

In the great drought and the great panic, the frog speaks from its mouth; be mindful when you drink sake.
