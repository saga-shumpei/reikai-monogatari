Chapter 45: Dialogue on the Soul's Departure [245]

第45章　魂脱問答〔245〕

§ 1

In the divine land that preserves true longevity, under the vast heavenly sky of the world, white-naped cranes fly back and forth to east and west exchanging felicitations, singing of the world's auspicious omens, while a green turtle cheerfully dances up toward the heavens at Mount Manju of auspicious longevity, appearing as the Lord God, and as Iwakusuhiko—whose very name is auspicious, said to protect this beautiful divine land forever and ever—in the Land of Eight Islands proclaimed the great liturgy of divine salvation without distinction between far and near, tread across Mount Morokoshi, and while buffeted by mountain storms of the Rocky Mountains floating in the clouds, singing a truly valiant propaganda song, with light heart clad in straw raincoat, straw hat, straw sandals, and gaiters, on a long journey without a set destination, at last approaching the Castle of Tokoyo, which has now drawn near; as the missionary of Iwakusuhiko, the priest named Iwatowake, pushing through the snow and reaching a dead end in the new spring of this morning, seeking to open the blocked path, parting the crimson veil of the sun like autumn maple leaves pushing through, how noble is his pure, sincere heart! The Castle of Tokoyo, once firm and enduring, where the eight kings of Banko-ōkami exerted their evil violence, retains only its name, and now the Castle of Tokoyo has merely fallen into the possession of the evil, vile man Ōkunihiko; having styled himself Tokoyo Shin'ō in place of the flourishing Jizaiten, and beholding the golden crest of the cross shining upon the castle that radiates brilliant light without shadow, sighing deeply, the missionary worn by wind and rain, who is none other than Iwatowake, the fruit of the god Iwakusuhiko, though the rock-cave of his heart is open, seeks to open the yet unopened land of Tokoyo and dispel the darkness of Tokoyo, spurring on his own feet on shank's mare, at last traversing here this immensely vast continent, standing on the seashore where raging waves surge tumultuously under heaven, seeking to place the clamoring myriad divine people, like the urasu birds and beach plovers, upon the ark of divine salvation and cross over to Ryūgū, having piled up nights on the grass pillow, he now arrives at the port.

The god Iwatowake finally arrived at Crimson Port on the western coast of the land of Tokoyo. Here, four or five boatmen had moored their boats and were indulging in various idle talks.

A: "Hey, isn't the weather strange these days? Heavy rain falls continuously day and night, rivers overflow, houses are washed away, and on top of that, mysterious thudding rumbles of the earth shake without ceasing. At first, we thought it was the sound of waves, but apparently it is not waves either. We were worried thinking it might be a portent of an earthquake, yet the rain has continued for thirty days as of today, and there is no sign of an earthquake at all. The other day, someone calling himself a missionary came by and said that earthquakes, thunder, and rain of fire would fall, and in the end it would become an ocean of mud; perhaps it might turn out just like that."

So saying, he tilted his head anxiously.

B: "What? Rain of fire falling? How could there be such nonsense? Rain is when all water rises into the sky, cools in the heavens, and descends again as original water; rain of water has fallen often since ancient times, but has there ever been an instance of a rain of fire falling?"

"Even so, the other time when the Etna volcano erupted, didn't a rain of fire fall?"

"Don't talk nonsense; that was rocks of fire falling. The missionaries of Mount Manju or somewhere were preaching boastfully as if they had descended from heaven, but isn't this also called the opening or closing of the rock-door falling from heaven?"

"It is not impossible that a rain of fire might fall. The other day, the moment I bumped into a pillar in the darkness, a rain of fire fell; I clearly saw it, so I cannot say it does not fall."

"That is just you bumping into a pillar and sparks flying from your eyeballs. It did not fall; you struck it. As for earthquakes and thunder, we divine humans are descendants of the gods, so we ourselves are gods. That is why it is said, 'Self is God.' And so you, who are 'Self is God,' made a rain of fire fall from your eyes. Well, one could almost say there is no such thing as wonders, monsters, or true things in this world."

Hei: "That talk does not matter, but wasn't there a terrible war across the sea the other day?"

Tei: "Yeah, I heard such a thing. That must be the sound from that time, banging and booming day after day."

"Even after the battle ended, the booming sound can still be heard, so there must be some cause for it. They say that on Ryūgū Island or somewhere, treasures called the Heavenly Masumi Jewel and the Tide-Ebbing and Tide-Flowing Jewels have been hidden since ancient times, and the minions of Uralhiko of Mount Ural built many boats to steal those jewels and attacked under cover of darkness. Then the one called Ōunabarahikono-kami of Kutsujima raised the seas and lost his temper, trying to afflict the enemy with that Masumi Jewel. However, that jewel was of no use, and in the end it was taken by the enemy, so they say. And on Kanmurijima, also known as Ryūgū Island, the Tide-Ebbing and Tide-Flowing Jewels were hidden. The minions of Uralhiko attacked to steal those as well. The guardian deity of this place, intending to afflict the enemy's assault, brought out the jewels called Tide-Flowing and Tide-Ebbing to defend. These too were completely useless, and in the end both Kanmurijima and Kutsujima were taken by the enemy, so it is said. Even if they talk of jewels, they amount to nothing at all."

"That is an obvious matter; consider it carefully. Is it not called the Pearl of Masumi? If one says Masumi, it means the pearl where evil spirits reside. Since many evil spirits gather and try to seize it, it fits just right—a milt for a harelip, clappers for a cleft-lip, a sweet rice dumpling falling into an open mouth, like a man and a woman meeting each other. No matter how much Umabarahiko-no-kami, who stirred up the ocean waves and direct rage, faced enemies from within and without and was attacked from within and without, it was impossible to withstand. Moreover, I heard that the Pearl of Shiomitsu and the Pearl of Shiohi were also useless, but consider it carefully: salt is originally salty, and honey is sweet. Even if one puts salty things and sweet things together, it is natural that no harmony can be achieved. Furthermore, what is called the Pearl of Shiohi means salt to a leech. Gathering such sworn enemies together to call them the Pearl of Shiomitsu or the Pearl of Shiohi makes no sense at all, does it? It is natural to be defeated. As for the core nature of those pearls, long, long ago in ancient times, there was a formidable deity called Izu-no-Mitama, who hid it on the summit of a mountain called Mount Sinai. A villain named Takekuma tried to snatch it away and met with a terrible fate. However, the gods of the holy ground acted pompously and boasted of the secret of the single flower and the divine plan of the single flower, but at last the secret of the single flower was exposed; Uralhiko caught wind of it, snatched the pearl of Ryūgū Island first, and attacked with all his might to immediately drag out the sacred spirit of that mountain. At that time, an official named Kijiwake, who was guarding Mount Sinai, displayed the divine virtue of that sacred spirit from above the enemy army and rained down rocks upon them. Uralhiko's vassals finally lost heart at this, did nothing, and fled back, so it is said. That is why it is called Mount Shinai, meaning the mountain that suffers not a bit of attack."

Kō: "Do not mock us; you tell people comic stories, so shall we be going now? If we keep dealing with such a fellow, the sun will set. Look, look, another tremendous voice has been heard. While it is still light underfoot, let us flee somewhere."

Otsu: "Even if you say let us flee, the very ground we are standing upon is shaking, so would it not be the same wherever we flee?"

The rain grew ever fiercer, and the rumbling of the earth became increasingly intense with each passing moment. Just as they all turned pale, cast their eyes in all directions, and were suddenly enveloped in clouds of anxiety, a missionary lightly dressed in a straw raincoat, woven hat, and gaiters parted the dense grove and raised a clear, cool voice:

"Though the morning sun shines or clouds over, though the sea of a thousand fathoms dries up,

Though the world be submerged in mud, the power of sincerity shall save the world,"

Such a missionary song began to be heard. All of them strained their ears and listened intently to that missionary song.

『そりや定まつた話だよ、よう考へて見よ。真澄の珠と云ふぢやないか。マスミつたら、魔の住んで居る珠だ。それを沢山の魔神が寄つて来て奪らうとするのだもの、合うたり叶うたり、三ツ口に真子、四ツ口に拍子木、開いた口に牡丹餅、男と女と会うたやうなものだ。ナンボ海原とか向腹立とかを立てた海原彦神でも、内外から敵をうけて、内外から攻められて、お溜り零しがあつたものぢやない。また潮満とか潮干とかの珠も、役に立たなかつたと聞いたが、よう考えて見よ、塩は元来鹹いものだ、そして蜜は甘いものだ。鹹いものと甘いものと一緒にしたつて調和が取れないのは当然だ。また潮干の珠とか云ふ奴は、塩に蛭といふ事だ。ソンナ敵同士のものを寄せて潮満の珠とか、潮干の珠だとか一体わけがわからぬぢやないかい。負けるのは当然だよ。その珠の性根とやらを、どつと昔のその昔に厳の御霊とかいふどえらい神があつて、それをシナイ山とかいふ山の頂上に隠しておいた。それを竹熊とかいふ悪い奴がをつてふんだくらうとして、偉い目にあうたといふこと。しかしながら、聖地の神共は勿体ぶつて、一輪の秘密とか一輪の経綸とかいつて威張つてをつたが、とうとうその一輪の秘密がばれて、ウラル彦が嗅ぎつけ、第一番に竜宮島の珠をふんだくつて、直にその山の御性念を引張り出さうと一生懸命に攻めかかつた。その時シナイ山とやらを守つてゐた貴治別とかいふ司が、敵軍の頂辺から、その御性念の神徳を現はして岩石を降らした。ウラル彦の幕下はとうとうこれに屁古垂れよつて、何にもしないで、逃げ帰つたと言ふことだ。それで攻撃を一寸もシナイ山といふのだ』

甲『馬鹿にすな、人に落話を聞かせよつて、もうもう行かうかい。コンナ奴に相手になつてゐると、日が暮れてしまふワイ。それそれ、またど偉い声が聞えてきた。脚下の明るいうちに何処なと逃げようぢやないか』

乙『逃げようたつて、吾々の乗つてゐる大地が動いてをるのだもの、何処へ逃げたつて同じことぢやないか』

雨は益々激しく、地鳴りは刻々に強烈になつて来た。一同は真青な顔して、四方八方に眼を配り、忽ち不安の雲に包まるる折しも、林の茂みを別けて、簑笠脚絆の軽装をした宣伝使が涼しき声を張り上げて、

『朝日は照るとも曇るとも　千尋の海は干くとも

世界は泥に浸るとも　誠の力は世を救ふ』

といふ宣伝歌が聞え始めたり。一同は耳を澄ましてその宣伝歌を聞き入りにける。
