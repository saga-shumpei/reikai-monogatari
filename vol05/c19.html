Chapter 19: The Morning Sun Pierces the Dark [219]

第19章　旭日出暗〔219〕

§ 1

Uralhiko did not understand the heart of Banko-shin'ō, who was wise and discerning and understood from the bottom of his heart this warning arising from the divine will of heaven and earth; possessed by the evil spirit of a serpent and the wicked spirit of a golden fox, his arrogance grew ever more extreme, he let the proclamation of the Divine King pass like wind blowing through empty air, and dispatching the deities in all directions to search for Kotobure-no-kami, he finally cast him into the prison of Mount Ural. And then, in order to dispel the confusion of the deities, he composed a song and had it widely propagated in all directions. That song was:

'Drink and make merry, for an inch ahead is darkness,

After the darkness the moon will rise,

Though the cuckoo's voice is heard, its form is not seen,

Is the unseen form an evil spirit or an ogre?'

Even the precious propagation of Hinode-no-kami's 'Three thousand worlds... plum blossoms' was almost completely wiped out on account of this song.

Banko-shin'ō heard the clamorous voices outside the palace, and when he stepped out from inside the palace to the front gate wondering what was the matter, with Uralhiko in the center, numerous deities were dead drunk with sake,

§ 2

"Drink and make merry,"

Surprised by the riotous disorder of those singing this song and dancing madly, he appeared in the center of the banquet,

"The three thousand worlds, and so forth,"

and began to sing this nursery song, raising his voice.

As soon as they heard this voice, the drunkenness of the majority of the deities suddenly cleared completely, and some even turned pale and began to tremble violently. Banko Shin'ō still continued to chant this song. The majority of the deities shrank back all the more and fell to the ground, with some even collapsing flat upon the earth.

Uraruhiko, seizing this moment, once again cried,

"Drink and make merry, for one inch ahead is darkness; after the darkness, the moon will rise."

—he sang out in a loud voice. In response to that voice, the deities stood up once more, and having recovered their vigor, danced wildly. Banko Shin'ō once again,

"The plum blossoms... of the three thousand worlds,"

began to sing. The deities, who had at last recovered their vigor, fell flat upon the earth once again.

Uralhiko and his wife, shouting at the top of their voices from both sides of the grounds, clapping their hands and dancing,

"Drink and make merry, for an inch ahead is darkness,"

began to sing this song. Once more the deities raised their heads and danced wildly. At this moment, from one side of the grounds, an indescribably beautiful and solemn voice was heard. At that voice, the deities agonized once again as if their chests were pierced, and fell to the earth. Banko Shin'ō proceeded forward, guided by that voice. Strangely, that voice was heard from inside the prison.

"Suspicious."

『不審』

§ 4

Saying this, the God-King advanced before the prison, accompanied by four or five attendants.

"The plum blossoms that open at once across the three thousand worlds."

Thus it began to be heard once again. Banko Shin'ō felt such agony as if his head were being crushed with an iron hammer and his chest pierced with red-hot iron, and instinctively prostrated himself on the spot. The four or five attendants likewise collapsed abruptly all at once, falling like dominoes.

When the divine people finally raised their heads and looked, it was that Kotofure no Kami. Astonished, they immediately opened the door, rescued him, took him back to the inner palace, entertained him courteously, and respectfully begged for his teachings. Hinode no Kami courteously conveyed the true intentions of Nodatehiko no Mikoto, urged reform and submission, and explained the immense benevolence of heaven, earth, sun, and moon. The God-King revered this propagator as if he were a living god, never neglecting an attitude of piety. Immediately, by order of the propagator, he newly built a splendid palace atop Mount Ural, solemnly enshrined the God of the Sun, the God of the Moon, and the God of the Earth, and did not neglect worship.

In contrast, Uralhiko and Uralhime, who were enchanted by the body-master-spirit-servant giant serpent and golden fox, felt displeasure toward this act of the God-King, became exceedingly reckless toward the divine people, held banquets day and night, had them feast fully on abundantly ripened fruits, advocated atheism, and sang:

"Drink and make merry, for an inch ahead is darkness; after the darkness, the moon will rise. Yoito-sa, yoiyasassa, yoiyasassa,"

stubbornly dancing in frenzy, indulging only in pleasures day and night, until they came to forget the divine administration.

At this moment, no sooner was a roaring sound heard in heaven than an extremely intense light radiated over the ground. The divine people spontaneously looked up at the sky all at once. It was an intense light, dazzling the eyes. That light was once again rotating through midair in the east, west, south, and north, as though a searchlight were being cast serenely in all four directions from the Floating Bridge of Heaven. Moreover, some appeared who became blind due to this intense light. From the tip of the Floating Bridge, tens of golden stars were discharged, falling upon Banko Shin'ō's palace on the top of Mount Ural.

Banko Shin'ō deeply gave thanks for this as the grace of the Great God, gathered those jewels one by one, reverently enshrined them in the sanctuary, offered sacrifices day and night, solemnly performed rituals, and devoted his utmost sincerity to god-reverence. From then on, atop Mount Ural, purple clouds drifted, heavenly men and maidens occasionally descended to dance in midair and play exquisite music, the wind blew warm, the flowers were fragrant, and the fruits of the trees became delicious in taste and ripened abundantly.

The God-King appointed Hinode no Kami as the chief priest, having him serve here. From this point forward, the deepest chasm was dug between Banko Shin'ō atop Mount Ural and Uralhiko and his wife.
