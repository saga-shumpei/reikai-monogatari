Chapter 5: The Blind Turtle and the Drifting Wood [205]

第5章　盲亀の浮木〔205〕

§ 1

Having cast herself into the river of Eden, Shioharuhime passed through the water and reached a shallow downstream. Here lay a single huge tree stump. Thinking it heavenly aid, the princess clung to the large tree stump and caught her breath. Though she had thought it a tree stump until now, in an instant a neck like that of a horse appeared, and next limbs appeared. The stump was completely transformed into a great turtle.

The princess mounted the turtle's back, and when she looked upstream, two deities came drifting down, raising their heads bubbling as if gourds were tied to them. Looking closely, they were Tamaharuhime and Tsuneharuhiko. Spontaneously she raised her voice and called out to the two. Rejoicing, the two clung to the turtle. Thereupon the three deities straddled the back of the great turtle, and leaving it to go as it pleased, went down the great river of Eden without distinction of day or night.

On both banks of the river, rocks stood steep like walls, making it impossible to approach. A little further downstream, a white sandbar became visible. When the three deities left it to the turtle's will, the turtle crawled sluggishly toward that sandbar and climbed up. Here, seemingly holding a festival, many divine beings had come out to the riverside, drinking sake, singing and dancing, beating various pieces of wood and stone to keep time, and making merry boisterously.

The turtle mercilessly advanced through the midst of the thronging divine beings while carrying the three deities. Exposed to the sun, the clothes of the three deities had completely dried before they knew it. Seeing this sight, the many divine beings dead drunk with sake clapped their hands in unison and cried out, "Uro, Uro!" Breaking through this place and advancing further and further north, there again a group appearing to be somewhat higher-ranking deities was deeply drunk with sake, clapping their hands and reveling. The turtle advanced sluggishly through their midst without any hesitation. At this time, from the head of the banquet, a deity resembling a commander, wearing a golden crown and endowed with a physique and frame far superior to the crowd, appeared. Then he stood blocking the front of the turtle. The turtle appeared to whisper something to this deity.

To the north, lofty green mountains encircled the area, and mountain ranges extended to the east and west like the spread wings of a crane, forming a topography just like a horseshoe shape. And facing the great river to the south, various beautiful flowers bloomed in profusion, and rare fruits ripened on the treetops. It was a paradise differing not in the least from the Garden of Eden. The unifier of this place was called Nanten'o and was revered by the many divine beings like the ancestor of the land. All the deities ate tree fruits, drank clear spring water, and relished naturally growing wild yams and the like, not feeling the slightest hardship of food, clothing, or shelter, just like a heavenly pure land. Nanten'o was in truth Omichiwake. This land was called Ken'onkyo. Nanten'o gathered the many divine beings and had them welcome the rare guests atop the turtle as an auspicious omen of peace throughout the realm. The three deities thanked the divine beings for their unexpected hospitality, and at last became intoxicated with the rare wine made from fruit and began to chant delightful songs. The divine beings of this land were all relatively broad-headed and short in stature, having faces just like crabs. The appearance of the three deities there was a scene just like a crane alighting upon a rubbish heap.

§ 2

These kamigami held awe, reverence, and obedience toward Nanten-o as their foremost duty, regarding him as a divine person who had descended from heaven. However, in contrast to Nanten-o's divine caliber, physique, and other qualities that excelled the multitude, the kamigami of this land were relatively short in stature, dwarfish in body, and hideous in feature, so that at this very time, the kamigami were unanimously grieving that there was no deity suitable to be made his consort. Thereupon, seeing the appearance of two goddesses and one god who resembled heavenly maidens, all of them thoroughly believed that they were superior deities who had descended from the heavenly skies. Accordingly, after consultation, the kamigami intended to report to Nanten-o and make those two goddesses the king's consorts, and because the one god had large horns growing on his head, they believed him to be truly a genuine deity. Therefore, with respect to the two goddesses, there was no one who took the slightest consideration of their being the wives or consorts of this god.

The three deities, Tsuneyahiko, Shioyahime, and Tamaharuhime, taking advantage of not being understood through the kotodama of the kamigami of this village, were able to converse freely in various ways. Then numerous kamigami gathered, weeping and kneeling in worship, carried a palanquin over, and forcibly urged Tsuneyahiko by hand gestures to board it. Tsuneyahiko thought they were extending him great hospitality, displayed a feeling of joy in his heart, and nodding two or three times, entered the palanquin in good spirits. The kamigami gathered around and lifted up the palanquin together. In this Ken'on-go, a legend had been handed down from ancient times that a horned deity would descend, ward off celestial disasters and earthly portents, and protect a lifespan of ten thousand years. Now, falling upon the birthday festival of Nanten-o, his coming down from upstream riding a tortoise said to possess an age of ten thousand years caused them to rejoice and take heart from the bottom of their hearts, convinced that he was undeniably a divine person descended from heaven.

The sacred palanquin was carried step by step, crossed the mountain valleys of the northeast, and was placed along with the sacred palanquin atop a standing rock. This rock was an oblong, pillar-shaped rock, like an erected cylinder. And the kamigami retreated afar, clapped their hands, simultaneously praised and rejoiced calling "Uro, uro," and shed tears as they bowed in worship.

When Tsuneyahiko, sensing something strange from within the palanquin, opened the door a little and looked, the palanquin he was riding in had been set atop a pillar-shaped rock so long it seemed almost to pierce the heavens. Unable to go out and unable to climb down, he was at a loss and cried out with all his might, "Oi, oi!" Hearing that voice, the numerous kamigami rejoiced while shouting "Oi, oi," and, believing they had heard the voice of a heavenly god for the first time, danced about in frenzied excitement. Tsuneyahiko said:

"Lower the palanquin!"

といつた。コラコラと二つ重ねていふ時は、頭を下にし足を上にして手で歩き廻ることである。神人らは天の尊き神の御命令を固く尊信し、先を争うて倒さまになり、前後左右に這ひ廻り、廻り損なつて谷に落ち傷つく者も出来た。中には、

『こいつは真の神でない、吾々を苦しむる悪神である』

とつぶやく者もあつた。何処よりともなく傍の山の中腹に塩治姫、玉春姫の女神の姿が忽然として現はれた。白き尾のやうな領巾を前後左右に振つてゐた。この郷の神人らはその白き領巾を振るとともに、雪崩をうつてもとの平地に帰つてしまつた。常治彦は横槌の柄に乗せられた亀のやうに手足をもがき、

『塩治姫ヤーイ

玉春姫ヤーイ』

と声をかぎりに叫び、つひにはその声さへ出なくなつてしまつた。
