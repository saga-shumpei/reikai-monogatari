§ 1

The party vied with one another to grope in the dark and ran up onto the island. At the mountain peak, a single ray of light pierced the darkness like a searchlight, shining thin and long upon the sea surface. This island was a solitary island in the Mediterranean Sea called Ushijima, and was also called Kamijima and Hōrakujima. At present, it is called Sardinia Island. Also, this sea is called by another name, the Sea of Seto.

Of the twelve jewels that had once emerged from the spirit of the Golden Water, when as many as ten were snatched away from the divine beings of Ryūgū Castle—who fell victim to the divisive and deceitful stratagems of the evil god Takekuma's faction—the cautious Takasugiwake ordered his retainer Sugitaka to secretly break through the rocks at the summit of this island, deeply conceal one of them, the lapis-lazuli-colored jewel, plant a marker pine tree above it, and cause Sugitaka to steadfastly guard it.

However, upon the eruption of Mount Tenkyō, eleven precious jewels emitting beautiful radiance and shining from the sky fell into this Sea of Seto, and numerous sea gods searched deep beneath the ocean floor, retrieved them, and offered them to Sugitaka, so that now all twelve precious jewels were assembled on this single island. Such a miraculous phenomenon occurred entirely because the Primordial Ancestral Great Deity was moved by the sincere reverence of Sugitaka, who maintained his austere fidelity on this solitary island, complied with the divine commands of heaven and earth, never took his eyes off them or slackened his heart through rainy mornings and snowy evenings, and strictly protected them; thus the Great Deity bestowed the eleven jewels, completing the total of twelve precious jewels, and thereby showed favor to the supreme sincerity of Takasugiwake and Sugitaka. From this point on, Sugitaka, together with Takasugiwake, held these jewels aloft to serve the great divine work of the reconstruction of heaven and earth, transmitting their honorable names to ten thousand generations. I shall describe this fact in detail on a later day.

On this pitch-black night where one could not distinguish close distances, the divine beings who had barely been washed ashore on this island were seized with joy and gratitude, as if meeting a Buddha in hell, as if a blind turtle clinging to a floating log, as if the dead reviving from the underworld, as if blossoms blooming on a withered tree, or as if the peach blossoms blooming in the garden of the Queen Mother of the West after three thousand years; and as Hahuribe-no-kami stood in the darkness,

even the divine beings who had loathed like snakes and scorpions the sound of his voice singing this song found that the spiritual authority of their original guardian deities was activated here, so that it sounded like the music of heavenly maidens and resonated like the loving voice of an affectionate mother.
