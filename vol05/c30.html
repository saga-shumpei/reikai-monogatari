Chapter 30: Full Sails and Reefed Sails [230]

第30章　真帆片帆〔230〕

§ 1

At the ferry landing of the Mediterranean Sea—where the scene of heaven and earth, which had been so gloomy, changed completely, the brilliant light of the heavenly sun brightly illuminating the lower realm in the sky, while on the earth the east wind gently blew to stir ripples on the deep blue sea surface, upon which the sunlight was reflected so that the glittering waves resembled a surface paved with sea bream scales—a missionary appeared, out of breath. It was now the moment when the ship, hoisting its mainsail to the calm breeze, was about to set sail toward the southwest.

The missionary who appeared here briskly slung a thick bamboo upon which teachings were written diagonally from the left shoulder to the right armpit, firmly tied it near the breasts with a purple cord, leaned on a staff with one hand, wore a purple face-covering cloth, occasionally turned this face-covering cloth up to the forehead with the left hand, pushed up the bridge of the nose with the right hand, smoothly rubbed across from the right eyelid to the outer corner of the left eye, rubbed beneath the nose again with the back of the hand, and pressed for boarding while covering the face again with the left hand.

Many passengers opened their eyes wide in wonder at this bizarre attire. Funado-no-kami cheerfully raised his right hand, beckoned, and gave a sign to board quickly. The missionary approached the boarding point with brisk strides and jumped toward the ship. At that impact, the ship shook violently, tracing unseasonable wrinkles of waves upon the sea surface that lay like tatami mats. The long and thick trees along the shore cast their reflections upside down toward the seabed, and as if dragons were ascending to heaven along with the waves, their trunks meandered to the left and right, rising toward the land.

There was not a single cloud in the sky, and the windless sea surface was as if crossing a glass mirror; the sail sagged, the ship's pace was sluggish without progress, and drifting upon this sea lasted for several days. The divine beings spent their time in idle gossip of this and that, consoling their boredom.

The sun sank into the western mountains, and various birds flying across the sea each returned to their nests in search of a roost. The half-moon hung in the western sky, casting light like a sharp sickle upon the sea. The sky gleamed all over in the aventurine lacquer hue of heavenly writing, giving the impression that the moon floated along the Milky Way and was sinking at the edge of the sea.

Starlight shone across the bottom of the sea, the Milky Way spanned the heavens, and a radiant galaxy also flowed beneath the waters—two rivers and two moons—while struck by a refreshing feeling as if resting oneself upon a lotus pedestal, the ship quietly advanced toward the southwest.

現代日本語訳

あれほど暗澹としていた天地の光景はここに一変し、空には燦然とした天津日の光がうららかに下界を照らし、地には東風がおもむろに吹いて紺碧の海面にさざ波を立て、これに日光が映り輝いて波がきらめく様子はあたかも鯛の鱗を敷き詰めたかのようである地中海の渡し場に、息を切らして現れた宣伝使がいた。今や船は静かな風に本帆を揚げ、西南に向かって出帆しようとする時である。

ここに現れた宣伝使は、太い竹に教示を記したものをかいがいしく左肩から右の脇の下へ斜めに背負いながら、紫の紐で胸のあたりにしっかり結び、片手に杖をつきながら、紫の被面布をかぶり、時々左手でこの被面布を額のあたりまでめくり上げ、右手で鼻筋を押し上げ、そのまま右のまぶたから左の目尻にかけてつるりとなで、再び鼻の下を手の甲でこすり、左手で再び被面布を顔に覆いながら乗船を迫った。

多くの船客は、この異様な扮装に怪訝な目を見張った。船戸神は快く右手を挙げて手招きし、早く乗れとの合図を与えた。宣伝使はつかつかと乗り場に近づき、船を目がけて飛び込んだ。その響きに船は激しく揺れ、畳のようである海面に思いがけない波の皺を描いた。海辺の長くて太い樹木は海底に向かって逆さにその影を沈め、波につれて竜が天に昇るかのように、樹木の幹は左右に曲がりくねりながら、地上を目指して上ってくるのであった。

空には一点の雲もなく、また風もない海面はあたかも玻璃の鏡を渡るかのようであり、帆はしおれ、船足は遅々として進まず、この海上に漂うことは数日に及んだのである。神人らは四方山の無駄話に時を費やし、退屈を紛らわせていた。

日は西の山に沈み、海上を飛び交う様々な鳥はねぐらを求めてそれぞれ巣に帰って行く。半月は西の空にかかり、鋭い鎌のような光を海上に投げかけた。空は一面に天書の光の梨地色に輝き、月は天の川を流れて海の果てに沈むかのような感があった。

海の底には一面に星の光が輝き、天にも銀河が横たわり、海底にもまた燦爛たる銀河が流れ、川が二つ、月が二つあり、実に蓮華の台に身を預けたかのような爽快な思いに打たれながら、静かに船は西南に向かって進んでいる。

海の底には一面の星光輝き、天にも銀河横たはり、海底にもまた燦爛たる銀河流れ、河二つ月二つ、実に蓮華の台に身を托したる如き爽快の念に打たれつつ、静かに船は西南に向つて進んでゐる。

§ 2

The ship crossed the sky of the sea bottom, the pole pierced the stars of the sea bottom, and the sight of countless tens and hundreds of jellyfish drifting on the waves gave the impression that suddenly scores of moons from the heavens had descended to support and protect the ship.

In contrast to the disastrous spectacle of heaven and earth yesterday, today's tranquility—whether fair weather after an evening shower or calm after a storm—left heaven and earth silent without a sound, as if even the whispering of earthworms might reach one's ears.

Due to the consecutive days of sailing, all the divine beings aboard the ship suffered from boredom, and at the four corners of the ship,

"Ah, ah,"

divine beings appeared who opened their mouths wide and yawned. All were infected by these yawns, and clenching their fists with both hands and extending them high above their heads, they opened their mouths wide,

"Ah, ah,"

and saying so, began yawning as if not to be outdone by one another. For a time, it felt as though it were a contest of yawning. Eventually, the urge to yawn was exhausted, and in a corner of the ship, perhaps mindful of their surroundings, a divine being appeared who even sang a hum in a low voice. Infected by this, another began to sing some short song in a low voice again. At last, growing bolder, they raised their voices, stood up in the ship without hesitation, and, placing both hands on their cheeks,

"Drink and make merry, for one step ahead is darkness, After the darkness, the moon will appear. If the ship floats, let the heart float too, If the heart sinks, the ship sinks. Come now, float and float and be buoyant, Whatever becomes of this fleeting world, let it be. Though they say this fleeting world does not go as one wishes, I am floating along with the times. Though the voice of the cuckoo is heard, its form is not shown; Is the hidden form an evil spirit or a demon? If that demon fellow should come forth, Bind its limbs and break its horns, Beat it, roast it, and eat it up. Even cattle, tigers, wolves, and lions, Or the weak goat, Or a thousand monkeys, ten thousand monkeys, Rake them together, bind them tightly, And cast them cleanly away into the western sea, Cast them cleanly away, Yoi yoi, yoi to sa no, yoi to sassa."

The divine beings joined their voices in unison and began singing with amusing rhythm, clapping their hands.

The missionary silently and indifferently watched this commotion without really looking. After a while, the divine beings appeared to feel exhausted; the surface of the waves, which had been so clamorous, immediately returned to stillness as if water had been sprinkled, and it became completely overcome by a tranquil air in which not even the sound of the wind could be heard.

§ 3

The missionary quietly raised himself, appeared on a slightly elevated place in the boat, raised his clear voice, and sang:

"Looking down from a high mountain into the valley bottom, how sorrowful, the sea of mud in the abyss. The plum blossoms open at once throughout the three thousand worlds; they open, fall, and bear fruit. Know the grace of the sun, moon, and earth. The living deity who saves this world appears on Mount Tenkyō. The living deity who teaches this world has appeared on Mount Chikyō. Though the morning sun may shine or be clouded, though the moon may wax or wane, even though the great earth may sink, the power of sincerity saves the world. The power of sincerity saves the world."

Thus, clapping his hands and stamping his feet with a rumbling sound, he danced in frenzy.

The divine persons, feeling drawn out by this voice, stood up all at once, clapped their hands, stamped their feet with a rumbling sound, and, riding the mood single-mindedly, danced in frenzy.

At this time, from a corner of the boat, a giant divine person with a bitter expression stood up straight and glared intently at the missionary. That sight was as if King Enra had appeared with his angry hair thrusting into the heavens. Ah, what manner of being could this divine person be?
