Translator's Critical Overview:
The prefatory matter to Volume 5 of Reikai Monogatari, composed in January 1922 at the Iwai Hot Springs in Inaba, establishes the critical turning point of the entire work:
1. Author's Preface (序文): Master Onisaburo reveals that the original scope of the spiritual tales was meant to finish in five volumes, but expanded vastly into a detailed universal chronicle; he gives stern warning against fraudulent spirits who imitate the divine teachings.
2. Editorial Notes (凡例): Explains that before the descent of Izanagi and Izanami, the tales encompass the universal cosmos before the earthly name of Japan even existed.
3. General Overview (総説 嵐の跡): A masterly spiritual treatise exploring the cosmic aftermath of Kunitokotachi-no-Mikoto's retirement, the true nature of Christ's sacrificial atonement, the necessity of building the Kingdom of Heaven within the human soul, and fortitude in adversity.

Author's Preface • The Infinite Scope of the Tales and Warning Against False Deceit 第一節　著者序文（因幡岩井温泉晃陽館・王仁識）

This Reikai Monogatari (Tales of the Spirit World) was originally intended to conclude in its entirety within five volumes. However, in accordance with the request of the scribes who wished for as detailed an account as possible, the editorial policy was slightly altered from around the fourth volume onward, so as to narrate the events with maximum detail.

Consequently, it has become utterly impossible to bring the narrative of the divine and astral realms to a close within the originally planned fifth volume. At the opening of the present volume, I had initially intended to describe how the two deities, Izanagi and Izanami, descended upon the Floating Bridge of Heaven in order to repair and solidify this drifting country, which floated like a jellyfish. Yet this plan was once again completely disrupted, and it is only in the sixth volume that we shall finally reach the passage where the two deities stand upon the Floating Bridge of Heaven to probe the vast ocean.

Between July 1899 and August 1900, I took up my brush and composed this narrative, keeping the manuscript secretly treasured and permitting only two or three earnest believers to peruse it. However, certain individuals who had read it thoroughly ended up conceiving various pernicious notions, transformed into vessels for monstrous enchantments, and passed away into the hidden realm; others gathered together and tragically burned and discarded the entire work of over five hundred volumes.

Thereafter, I contemplated resuming the manuscript, but divine permission was by no means granted from the heavenly realm, so that I was unable to commence the oral dictation until the eighteenth day of the ninth month of the previous lunar year. The spirits of the evil deities who had possessed those few directors at that time had completely memorized the contents of this Reikai Monogatari. Now infiltrating the physical vessels of those belonging to the lineage of the Foundress, they claim that none other than themselves truly comprehends the truth of the divine brushstrokes revealing the foundation of this present world, or proclaim themselves to be the living spirit of the Deity of the Rising Sun or the spirit of Princess Tokoyo. In this manner, these pernicious spirits are attempting once again to compose incoherent tales and disrupt this divine teaching. I myself was repeatedly urged by a certain individual to read their purported divine brushstrokes at least once.

Yet, for some reason, the worm within my belly rumbled in refusal and forbade me from reading it. I perceive this, too, as profound counsel from the divine realm. Even regarding the sacred brushstrokes penned by the Foundress herself, there are many in the world—and indeed among newly joined believers—who look upon them with skeptical eyes, declaring that Zuigetsu must have composed them in advance and had the Foundress transcribe them, for an old rural woman could never otherwise have written anything so profound. If I were to read even a single volume of the brushstrokes produced by that certain person under the sway of evil spirits, there might well emerge believers who misjudge the situation, claiming that I derived my source material from those very writings.

In truth, the guardian spirit possessing that individual read and memorized the tales of the spirit world that I had previously written, accessing them through a certain physical body here and there; claiming to know everything by its own virtue, it has thoroughly deluded even the mortal vessel of that person. Moreover, I cannot help but be astounded that there are distinguished individuals who rejoice in and thirst after these writings, revering them as golden maxims and precious dictums. It is entirely natural, therefore, that divine names such as Princess Tokoyo and the Great Deity Hachio, along with various similar appellations, should appear in the brushstrokes of that individual's possessing spirit. Yet, if one examines them through the active application of the heaven-bestowed wondrous soul (kushimitama), it will become clear that there lies a disparity as vast as that between heaven and earth regarding their righteousness or wickedness, their authenticity or falsehood, and their dissonance with divine reason.

Ah, I did not wish to utter a single word of such matters in this preface. Nevertheless, for the sake of the righteous souls who remain unacquainted with the conditions of the spirit world, I must state these words by way of warning.

In the divine oracles of the Foundress, it is revealed: 'Since the Way of the Gods is purely one of sincerity, no discrimination shall be made between parent and child, siblings, relatives, or strangers.' Thus, in accordance with the teachings of her sacred brushstrokes, I offer this word of caution. Furthermore, while I hear that distinguished scholars have offered diverse critiques regarding this Reikai Monogatari, I leave such matters entirely to the freedom of their own scholarly pursuits.

I simply give shape to the divine revelations and dictate them exactly as they are imparted.

January 14, 1922 (December 17 of the Tenth Year of Taisho by the Lunar Calendar) Written by Onisaburo at Koyo-kan, Iwai Hot Springs, Inaba Province

Composed by Onisaburo: An atrocious fiend appears, wearing the mask of goodness, Defiling the path of sincerity with artful, deceptive tongue. Do not thoughtlessly fall for it, O believers! Its outward countenance is like a Bodhisattva, yet its inner heart is that of a Yaksha. In this realm of devils masquerading as gods, let not yourself be enticed by flattering tales.

現代語訳

この『霊界物語』は、当初は全五巻で語り終える予定でございました。しかし、筆録者から「できるだけ詳細に書き残してほしい」との要望があったため、第四巻あたりから幾分その方針を改め、できる限り細やかに物語ることにいたしました。

そのため、予定していた第五巻をもって神界や幽界の物語を完結させることは、到底不可能となってまいりました。本巻の冒頭においては、かつて海月のように漂っていたこの国土をととのえ固め成すべく、伊邪那岐・伊邪那美の二神が天の浮橋へと御降臨あそばすところまでを述べるつもりでおりましたが、またしても計画は大きく外れ、第六巻に至ってようやく二神が天の浮橋に立たれ、青海原を探り給う段に到達することとなります。

この物語は、去る明治三十二年七月から三十三年八月にかけて、一度筆を執って著し、これを秘蔵した上で、ごく二、三の熱心な信徒にのみ閲覧を許しておりました。ところが、これを読了した某氏らは、やがて様々な悪しき企てを起こし、妖怪変化の器と化して帰幽（他界）したり、あるいは示し合わせて五百余巻に及ぶ草稿を焼き捨ててしまったりしたのであります。

それゆえ再び筆を起こそうと考えたものの、どうしても神界からの許しが出ず、昨年の旧暦九月十八日に至るまで口述を再開することができませんでした。その当時、二、三の役員に憑依していた悪神の霊は、この『霊界物語』の内容をことごとく記憶しており、今では開祖の血統を引く者の肉体に潜入し、「現世の根本を説き諭すというお筆先の真理を正しく解する者は自分をおいて他にない」とか、「日の出の神の生魂である」「常世姫の身魂である」などと詐称し、ふたたび邪神が支離滅裂な物語を書き散らして本教を攪乱しようと企てているのであります。私はその某氏から、「一度その筆先を読んでおくように」と幾度となく勧められました。

しかし、どうしたことか腹の虫がグウグウと鳴って拒み、決してそれを読ませようとはしないのであります。これもまた神界の深慮による警告であると推察しております。世間において、否、新しい信徒の中にあってさえ、「開祖の書かれたお筆先ですら、瑞月（王仁三郎）が前もって創作し、それを開祖に書かせたものに違いない。そうでなければ、あのような田舎の老いた女性が、これほど深遠な事柄を書ける道理がない」などと言って、お筆先を半信半疑の目で見る者が少なくありません。それゆえ、万一にも邪神の産物たる某氏のお筆先を一冊でも私が読んだとすれば、「またもやその材料を某氏の筆先から盗用したのだ」などと誤解する信徒が現れかねないからであります。

実情を明かせば、某氏に憑依している守護神は、かつて私の著した霊界の物語を特定の肉体を通じて各所で読み覚えており、あたかも万事を自らが知り尽くしているかのように語って、某氏の肉体そのものまでをも惑わしているのであります。また、それに歓喜渇仰して金言玉条と崇め、憧憬を抱くような立派な立場の人々がいることには、呆れざるを得ません。それゆえ、某氏の憑依神による筆先の中にも、常世姫や八王大神などといった、幾多の類似した神名が現れるのも当然の理であります。しかし、天から授かった奇魂を活用して見定めるならば、その正邪、信憑性の有無、そして理法との齟齬において、天地霄壌の懸隔があることが自ずとご理解いただけるはずであります。

ああ、私はこのようなことを序文において一言たりとも述べたくはありませんでした。しかし、霊界の実相を知らない正しき人々のために、どうしても注意を促すべく書き記しておかなければならなかったのであります。

開祖の神諭にも、「神の道はまこと一つであるから、親子、兄弟、親類、他人の分け隔ては一切いたさぬぞよ」と示されておりますゆえ、そのお筆先の教えに従い、一言の忠告を申し述べておく次第です。また、この『霊界物語』に対しましては、高名な学者諸賢による種々の批評があるとのことですが、それらは各自の自由な研究に委ねることといたします。

私はただ、神示のまにまに整え、口述を続けるのみであります。

Editorial Notes • The Universal Horizon and Polishing the Soul 第二節　凡例（編者識）

We had anticipated that this volume would be the final installment of the Tales of the Spirit World, comprehensively encompassing both the divine and the astral realms. However, the venerable Master Zuigetsu's profound erudition regarding the spiritual realm has far exceeded all imagination, such that the narrative has not yet arrived even at the halfway mark of the whole. Moreover, the events herein still precede the descent of the twin deities Izanagi and Izanami, belonging to an era before the very name of the nation of Japan had ever been bestowed.

Therefore, the descent of the Heavenly Grandchild to the land of Japan and the subsequent governance and ordering of the realm will, in all likelihood, appear in far later volumes.

When it is said that the divine realm, the astral realm, and the manifest world are of a common nature, it signifies that the principles of good and evil, righteousness and falsehood, are shared among them. We are informed that it is an utter misconception to suppose that phenomena in the divine realm are directly and immediately realized in the manifest world precisely as they occur.

Rather, since the mind of the deities in the divine realm and the heart of human beings in the manifest world are fundamentally of the same essence, it is sufficient for us to reflect upon the lessons gleaned from the Tales of the Spirit World, measure them against our own inner conscience, and utilize them as materials for the spiritual and physical purification of ourselves.

January 14, the Eleventh Year of Taisho (1922) Recorded by the Editor at the Koyokan, Iwai Hot Springs, Inaba Province

General Overview (Part 1) • Traces of the Storm: Divine Atonement and Responsibility 第三節　総説　嵐の跡（上：国祖の忍耐と贖罪の至徳）

In deep reverence, Kuninotokotachi-no-Mikoto, the Divine Earthly Ancestor, even when subduing the rebellion of the adverse deity Tokoyohiko and his faction, strictly observed the cosmic laws of Heaven and Earth. Wielding only the halberd of benevolence, He eschewed deceptive stratagems and divine machinations. He profoundly disdained quelling tyranny and chaos through ruthless martial arts, such as King Tang banishing Jie or King Wu chastising Zhou in ancient human history.

In stark contrast, Tokoyohiko-no-Mikoto and his followers, enslaved by evil spirits, deployed the esoteric and deceptive tactical formations, denying the Earthly Ancestor any opportunity for self-defense or vindication, and ultimately compelled Him into an inevitable divine retirement.

Even base predators such as wolves and otters occasionally show gratitude for divine favor by offering their prey of beasts and fish to worship the Heavenly Kami; even the raven, though reviled for its unclean feeding, possesses the filial virtue of regurgitating food to feed its elderly parents. Yet, the myriad deities who had long been bathed in the boundless mercy of the Great Ancestor and had received His exceptional grace, discarded that divine benevolence. Duped by the treacherous military tactics of malevolent spirits, they surged like a raging tide toward whichever side seemed momentarily advantageous, casting the Earthly Ancestor into the sorrowful solitude of being a solitary deity forced to conceal His divine presence.

The kaleidoscopic and covert activities—such as Onitakahiko of Mount Oe disguised as Moritakahiko during the council at Tokoyo Castle, together with Omichiwake, Yukinarihiko, Takakura, and Asahi, who employed cunning maneuvers to uproot the insidious plots of the wicked spirits—were by no means known to or sanctioned by the Great Ancestor. Although these were ad-hoc expedients and brilliant stratagems devised by the divine mortals of the Holy Land against their adversaries, and proved undeniably effective, the cosmic laws of Heaven and Earth contain the strict commandment: 'Thou shalt not deceive.' Because such deeds were inadmissible for truly sacred and upright divine mortals, the ultimate accountability fell upon the Great Earthly Ancestor, thereby tarnishing His supreme position and divinity. Indeed, the supernatural stratagems of Omichiwake, Moritakahiko, and Onitakahiko were not direct imperial commands from the Earthly Ancestor; His sole divine intent was to bring the evil deities to genuine repentance through the spiritual power of utmost benevolence and righteousness, harmonizing all through divine words. However, the deities of the Holy Land, brimming with impetuous vigor and fierce loyalty, lacked the composure to heed the cosmic laws. They answered violence with violence, met treason with deception, inadvertently defiled their own divine stature, and failed to divine the profound heart of the Earthly Ancestor. Ah, yet infinite was the Earthly Ancestor's benevolence, and tremendous was His sense of responsibility. Assuming entirely upon Himself the transgressions committed by His subordinate deities, without offering the slightest syllable of excuse, He gallantly relinquished His exalted station and withdrew into seclusion—a spectacle of sublime, peerless majesty. Striving constantly to uphold the sublime will of the Earthly Ancestor, I, too, frequently encounter disasters and afflictions arising from the misconduct and misunderstandings of certain individuals. Yet, my spirit remains ever vast like the ocean, resolute like the mountain, unshakeable and undisturbed, taking it as my true calling to shoulder all blame and guilt alone. Anyone who leads others must deeply comprehend people, trust them, entrust them with duty, discern their virtue or malice, their wisdom or folly, and grant each their proper place. If, through unavoidable circumstances in appointing subordinates, errors were committed, I reflect upon my own shortcomings of ignorance, weak will, and lack of execution. Without uttering a single murmur of complaint, I accept this with gratitude as the disciplinary whip of the Great Kami's supreme benevolence.

Ah, how noble it is to bear the heavy penalty of the thousand tables of offerings and to enact the sacrificial atonement of the Cross! I perpetually chant the prayer for divine favor and spiritual flourishing, engaged in the joyous divine work of delivering this present age from ignorance and dark delusion. Under heaven there exist two supreme difficulties: the first is ascending to the heavens, and the second is finding true human talent. Ah, just as the Great Earthly Ancestor endured every hardship in attempting to establish the Kingdom of Heaven upon earth, yet failed to secure the ideal divine master builder, so am I unable to escape this same destiny. The Great Earthly Ancestor constructed a pure, exalted, and beautiful palace in the Holy Land, founded a divine governance of supreme peace, and rejoiced for a mere few centuries in viewing the auspicious age wherein the myriad deities dwelt in security. Eventually, however, even the benevolent deities deteriorated over time into vessels for evil spirits, destroying the initial grand purpose of the Ancestor. This is analogous to one who builds an exquisite villa atop a hill, delighting in the majestic and refined vistas seen from the parlor, but who, finding few trees surrounding the house to shield it from harsh winds, plants various necessary saplings. At first, the trees being short, they do not obstruct the view in the least; but as the years pass, they grow and flourish with dense foliage until not only is the distant view completely obscured, but the mighty trees catch the gale, collapse onto the roof, demolish the house, and inflict severe injury upon the master himself. Many similar occurrences must surely befall those deemed successful in this world. In truth, I myself experienced a dream wherein trees planted around a house blocked the distant horizon, shrouded everything in darkness, and ultimately crushed the dwelling, causing me grievous wounds. Yet, I am convinced that this nightmare shall break, and an opportunity shall arrive to rebuild an even more magnificent mansion upon level ground.
