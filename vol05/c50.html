Chapter 50: The Sacred Rock-Camphor Boat [250]

第50章　磐樟船〔250〕

§ 1

Although it is said that all living things must die, those who meet must part, and prosperity and decline are the ways of the world, this versatile deity, who once held the post of angel alongside Ōyashimahiko no Mikoto in the divine capital of the holy land Jerusalem, now gazes at the moon of his place of exile; in this painful misery, the ruined end of Ōtaruhiko no Kami appears as the deity Tarumahiko, concealing himself in this world, and to quell the wild rampages of the raging evil spirits, he whips the steed of his sincere heart that cares for the divine land, running through mountains, fields, rivers, and seas, transmitting the divine revelations of Mount Tenkyō in all directions, bold in his pine-like evergreen spirit. Tarumahiko,

"Plum blossoms blooming all at once across the three thousand worlds, blooming, scattering, and bearing fruit. The cuckoo's voice is heard, yet its form cannot be seen."

he walks along here at last, wandering in all directions with a faint voice concealing himself from the world.

Like the Asuka River changing between deep pool and shallow rapid in yesterday and today of this fleeting world, he deeply bears in mind the world of Miroku crossing to the other shore and the fulfillment of the divine administration of Miroku, an awesome herald of God.

This was the Village of Kurenai in the Land of Tokoyo. In the Mansion of Kurenai, there was a righteous deity named Minohiko who governed this region.

Tarumahiko appeared before the gate and passed by looking lonely, chanting this propagation song in a low voice.

A little further ahead, the sound of trees being chopped incessantly could be heard. Tarumahiko was unconsciously walking toward the direction of the sound.

Dozens of deities were intently covered in sweat, wholly absorbed in felling camphor trees in this mountain forest. A dark-skinned deity of gigantic stature, who appeared to be the master deity, spoke toward the deities:

"Hey, all you deities, isn't it fine to rest now?"

he shouted. At this authoritative command, the many deities dropped their tools on the spot, gathered in one place, and sat down on the felled logs, their diverse conversations flourishing.

Party A: "Hey, everyone, felling this camphor mountain every single day, which has been called the specialty of the Village of Kurenai from ancient times—what on earth is this for? Has Minohiko no Kami gone a bit mad lately, I wonder?"

he began, making gestures as if wary of his surroundings and looking around nervously.

Party B: "What did you say? There is no need to murmur. How could the mind of Minohiko-sama be understood by mere mortals? You all just need to keep silent and work looking down as commanded. Murmuring and criticizing what the Great Deity does is called Minohiko—whoops, hold on—not knowing one's own station."

「三千世界に一度に開く梅の花、開いて散って実を結ぶ。ほととぎすの声は聞こえても姿は見えない」

と世を忍ぶかすかな声で四方を巡り歩きながら、ようやくここに歩んで来る。

淵や瀬と変わる仮の世の昨日や今日の飛鳥川のように、彼岸へと渡す弥勒の世、弥勒神政の成就を深く心にかける、恐れ多くも尊い神の宣伝使である。

ここは常世の国の紅の郷である。紅の館には蓑彦というこの地方を治める正しい神人がいた。

足真彦は門前に現れ、この宣伝歌を小声で歌いながら、寂しそうに通り過ぎた。

少し前方に当たって、しきりに木を伐る音が聞こえた。足真彦は知らず知らずその音のする方へと歩みを運んでいた。

数十柱の神々は一生懸命に汗まみれになって、この山林のクスノキの伐採に余念がなかった。棟梁の神と思われる体格の非常に大きい色の黒い神は、神々に向かって、

「おーい、神々の皆さん、もう休息してもよいではないか」

と怒鳴る。あまたの神々はその鶴の一声に、道具をその場に放り出し、一カ所に集まり、倒した木に腰を掛けながら、よもやまの話に花を咲かせた。

甲「おい皆の者、昔から紅の郷の名物と言われたこのクスノキの山を、毎日毎日伐採するなんて、一体、これは何のためだろうな。蓑彦神様も、近頃は少しどうかしてはおられないかな」

と辺りをはばかるような手つきをして、そこらをきょろきょろ見回しながら口を開いた。

乙「何だと。くすくす言う必要はない、凡夫の身分で、蓑彦様のお心が分かってたまるものか。ただお前たちは黙って仰せの通りにうつむいて働いていればよいのだ。大神様のなさることをくすくす批評するのは、みのひこ、おっと、どっこい、身の程知らずというものだよ」

§ 2

Hei stood up with a composed face, and said,

"In the first place, this mountain, from the ancient times of the distant age of the gods—"

Tei: "Hey, hey, what are you spouting? Distant or not, damn it all! As if a feigning coolie could understand things from ancient times. I know the real truth. Lately, look, there are those who cross the sea in rapid succession to the eastern country like a procession of ants, aren't there? Those fellows, terrified out of their wits by what the so-called missionaries said, those tiny ant-like gods are going to a capital with some strange name like Yurusareru or Jerusalem to be saved, so they say. Lining up in great numbers like a procession of ants, there is nothing grateful about it at all. Rather, the divine humans who possess many ships collect plenty of boat fares and truly consider that a blessing, so whether Lord Minohiko, or vinegar, or konjac—whoa, hold on, truly magnificent, clever, deeply wise, shrewd for profit, tactful, wise, agile..."

Kō: "What are you spouting? Piling up only the same words, cut it short quickly and say what comes next!"

Tei: "Even if you tell me to cut it short and speak, can such a great tree be chopped down so quickly?"

Kō: "Fool, I am telling you to hurry up and say what comes next!"

Tei: "What comes next is what comes next, is it? And so, Lord Minohiko, thinking of his own gain, intends to build many splendid ships, take aboard many ant-like commoners, and suck up their fares. We are made to fell trees day after day sweating profusely; it is truly inconsiderate."

Bo: "Talk sense, Minohiko is not such a greedy deity. He is a divine human without greed."

Tei: "It is bad precisely because he has no greed. If you say such things, soon, just like Katsuhiko, you will have your eyes popped out in horror again, shrink back in fright, and face a situation where you have to apologize with a tear-streaked face!"

Otsu: "The truth of the matter is that unless you too hold firm, the Great Deluge will break out. In order to save us, Lord Minohiko is felling this mountain treasured from ancient times to build splendid camphor-wood ships, and his spirit is to save you as well when the Great Deluge of the world arrives. About that, there is no mistake whatsoever. We believe it firmly, most firmly. Speaking of firm, it is like a deed carved in stone, a seal stamped upon a rock."

§ 3

Tei: "I wonder if such a great flood could actually happen. For some time now, I too have somehow felt anxious. Day after day, with the rain pouring down for some forty or fifty days up to today, the embankments of large and small rivers bursting, and all the houses in low places being washed away—if it continues to rain like this for two or three years, it will be a grave matter indeed. Surely the mountains and everything else will sink. Well, well, let us work as hard as we can in accordance with Minohiko-sama's command."

All: "That would be best; that would be best."

So saying, they stood up again, and just as they were about to begin cutting down the camphor trees, a divine person came forth, singing a propagation song in a low voice:

"Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, Though the darkness of the Eternal World may not clear, Though the earth be soaked in mud, The power of sincerity saves the world."

Singing thus, he advanced toward them. The divine people listened to the song of this missionary. From behind, once again,

"Halloo! Halloo!"

Calling out thus, a dignified man came chasing after the missionary. This was, in fact, Minohiko. All knelt upon the ground.

Minohiko courteously greeted the missionary, and expressing gratitude for his supreme sincerity in braving myriad hardships for the sake of the divine people on earth, journeying through the world to give warnings, he accompanied him back to the Vermilion Hall.

Tarumahiko, guided by Minohiko, entered the hall and advanced deep into the inner sanctuary.

There lay a gigantic rock of untold fathoms, upon which the forms of many divine people appeared naturally.

When he looked closely, Oni himself had, before he knew it, awakened from the spirit in front of the cavern of Mount Takakuma, and with both eyes wide open, he was gazing at the cavern.
