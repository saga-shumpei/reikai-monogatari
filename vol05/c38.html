Chapter 38: The Joy of Rejuvenation [238]

第38章　回春の歓〔238〕

§ 1

Seeing Takasumiwake on the carriage burst into a sorrowful, manly weeping, Hafuribe no Kami knitted his brows and,

"I have an absolute, immense, profound hatred for a fellow who cries and wails despite being a man,"

he said nonchalantly, yet in his heart feeling wholehearted sympathy for Takasumiwake's present plight, deliberately and purely trying to cheer his spirit, he made another amusing song, took hands with Sugitakahiko and Hafurihiko to form a tomoe-like whirlpool, and began spinning around counterclockwise. The song ran:

"Carp and all kinds of fish may stop when halted, Yet the path of love will not stop even if halted. Dokkoisho, dokkoisho. There surely should be no distinction of high and low in love; If there is a distinction, it is not love. What everyone desires is the deep shark of lust; Hips trembling like crucian carps, she is a woman's sashimi, Delighting and rejoicing as a true crucian carp; The red ark shell of a woman, clams day and night, The mussel of this world is unhindered; Shedding it cleanly, Thrusting into the conger eel, Letting other gods give their opinions, Knowing nothing at all, whether mullet or sand lance; To the ocellated octopus and the blunt sea cucumber, Chattering away with careless mouths, Do not get so angrily furious as a yellowtail at your tongue's pleasure; Without even peeling an eye, Swallowing the young yellowtail whole, If it is for the sake of love, even the sand lance's

endurance is the dried squid's part to play. Though being a person no longer of an infant's age, To the blue crab, the reasoning is a soft-shelled turtle's Absurd logic that does not fit into the intervals; If things are so bad, Please forgive me, as sweet as tilefish and trout. If you do not want a mahi-mahi, you had better take a mud mussel; For you, the greenling's head of herring roe Is just as it is to be sought; Once you die, you will not die twice, All the more like a black sea bream; Even if a small crucian carp lived on in the floating world, Because there is no reliance, one's life to the Spanish mackerel Is not to be begrudged. Whelk, whelk! Burning icefish is better than dolphins, Yet no door can be shut against a fish skewer; Dried sea cucumber is also embarrassing before the neighbors, Wrinkling the nose at the rotten fish That smells strongly, at the rotten carp,

Takasumiwake, whose fried bean curd was snatched by the fox of Kasuga, With his haggard figure and barracuda-like face; Because he arrogantly, haughtily speaks like a salt sea bream that there is no distinction of high and low in love, Disliked by the gods who say that fine things are troublesome, Keeping away, saying that touching no god Brings no curse, retreating like a young Spanish mackerel, With a dreadful look as if having seen a Heike crab; No matter how well-handled the young Spanish mackerel was, Because the treatment was not good, One must speak words of love; If so, turban shells and scattered hairtails; Kasuga is sashimi, Takasumi is a fond cut; Now Hafuribe no Kami strikes; Take a taste of the weasel's dying fart! Stinky, stinky in the evening moonlit night! Was it not a stinking, suspicious relationship Of lovers who curse the moonlit night? Ah, what a nuisance, what a nuisance! The anus of a forty-nine-year-old!"

§ 2

Thus, with boundless comicality and jest, they danced madly while singing. Takasumiwake on the cart, his soul enraptured by this amusing song and perhaps infected by the strange gestures and footwork, completely forgot his crippled state, instantly stood up on the cart, and together clapped his hands, stomped his feet resoundingly, and danced madly.

Seeing this sight, Kasugahime broke into tears of joy and prostrated weeping. Takasumiwake realized for the first time that his feet were standing, and again he raised his voice and began to weep tears of joy. Hafurino-kami once more,

"One who weeps, I hate, hate, hate, utterly hate!"

began to chant toward him.

"Wait a moment,"

Kasugahime said, hurriedly covering her mouth. Hafurino-kami placed a fist on his nose, placed the fist of his left hand on top of it, and successively, alternating by turns, withdrew one and stacked it, withdrew one and stacked it, mimicking the long-nosed god, and

"The cripple has stood, his feet have stood; stood, he stood just now! Come, come, leave this place with all speed; let us depart standing toward the sacred land!"

he danced madly again in high spirits, and slipping his foot into a narrow ditch beside him,

"Aitatatta, aita aita no tatta!"

as he danced madly while lying fallen in the ditch, appearing lighthearted as ever, the five kamitsukasa burst out laughing and rolled over all at once.

Here, at last, the rock door of the heart began to open, and that the feet of the cripple with such an intractable disease were able to stand was also the effect of laughter and cheerfulness. In the divine revelation as well,

"Live cheerfully and laughing,"

it is indicated. Good fortune enters the smiling gate. If living in tears, gloom, and regret is a lifetime, living in laughter, cheer, revering the gods, and taking joy in this world is also a lifetime. As long as all things between heaven and earth are governed by the spirit of words, human beings born as the lord of all creation must never curse this world, grieve, or retain the calamitous heart of anger, sorrow, and distress; no matter what great disaster they encounter or persecution they face, they must never regret or mourn. They have been brought into existence as human beings destined to be endowed with divine virtue in proportion to their cheerfulness.
