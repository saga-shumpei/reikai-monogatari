Chapter 26: The Dual Spirits of Ushitora and Hitsujisaru [226]

第26章　艮坤の二霊〔226〕

§ 1

From the summit of Mount Tenkyō, which suddenly erupted with a roaring, massive sound, the numerous starlights launched toward heaven fell respectively upon various parts of the world.

This was the explosion toward the crater of Mount Tenkyō, wherein the spirits of the deities—who had fallen into the great fireball that is the central axis of the earth, namely the Land of the Bottom (Nesoko no Kuni), as shown in Volume Four, and had tasted various hardships and tribulations—found their appointed time, and through the divine virtue of Nodatehiko no Mikoto, opened the subterranean cavity (the Heavenly Rock Cave). This is what is commonly referred to as the opening of the lid of hell's cauldron. Furthermore, the term "Opening of the Heavenly Rock Cave" is also sometimes used to refer to these events.

The starlights scattered over the earth, refined through many years of toil and suffering, were transformed into heaven-bestowed true spirits, and came to manifest divine virtues corresponding to each soul as particularly beautiful divine humans upon the earth; referring to this whole sequence of events, it is called:

"Plum blossoms opening all at once throughout the three thousand worlds,"

and pointing to the state in which each spirit is born as a beautiful divine human and participates in the divine work, it is said:

"Opening, scattering, bearing fruit, and nurturing the seed of Su."

現代日本語訳

轟然たる大音響とともに突然爆発した天教山の頂上から、天に向かって打ち上げられた数多の星の光は、世界の各地にそれぞれ落下した。

これは第四巻に示す地球の中軸である大火球、すなわち根底の国に落ちて、種々の艱難辛苦をなめた各神の身魂が時を得て、野立彦命の神徳により地中の空洞（天の岩戸）を開き、天教山の噴火口に向かって爆発したのである。俗に地獄の釜の蓋が開くと言うのはこのことである。また「天の岩戸開き」と言うのも、これらを指して言うこともあるのである。

地上に散布された星の光は、多年の労苦に洗練されて天授の真霊魂に生まれ変わり、ことに美しい神人として地上に各自身魂相応の神徳を発揮することとなった。これらの顛末を称して、

「三千世界一度に開く梅の花」

と言い、また各身魂が美しい神人と生まれて、神業に参加する状態を指して、

「開いて散りて実を結び、スの種子を養う」

§ 2

So it is said.

Thus, as the forerunner of the renewal and reconstruction of the world, Nodachihiko-no-Mikoto opened the rock-dwellings at once to rescue the righteous deities who had fallen into the world, opened the lid of the cauldron of hell, and made them the divine seeds for the reformation of the world—such was this most profound divine plan.

Now, Konohanahime-no-Mikoto, accompanied by Tsukiteruhiko-no-Kami and various other deities, descended to Aokigahara on the mountainside of Mount Tenkyō. Here, numerous deities who had crossed that Silver Bridge had gathered, and as they looked up at the mountain summit and saw Konohanahime-no-Mikoto descending at the head with numerous accompanying deities, they clapped their hands all at once, raised shouts of triumph, cried "Urō, urō," and welcomed them dancing wildly.

The divine people, gazing afar at this spectacle, rejoiced greatly, scrambled to ascend out of breath to Aokigahara, celebrated and rejoiced that the divine pillars of the three levels—upper, middle, and lower—for the fulfillment of divine government had all gathered, opened fans made of tree bark in their respective hands, matched the clapping of hands and stamping of feet in front, behind, to the left, and to the right, and danced wildly while crying "Urō, urō." Their manner was just as if male and female butterflies were fluttering in a spring field, sporting among the rape blossoms. The voices of the deities clapping their hands all at once and chanting norito resounded above in heaven and shook all things of the earth below.

In this manner, on Mount Tenkyō, the divine pillars of the upper, middle, and lower souls assigned their respective posts and toured as proclaimers for the salvation of the earthly world; moreover, the souls previously scattered across the earth emerged into the world as beautiful divine people and appeared in various regions, and hearing the teachings of these proclaimers, they rejoiced and yearned for them with reverence. When the speaker and the hearer are in accord, the correct teaching of the deities permeates the depths of the soul. A close relationship can be established, just as a magnet attracts iron. These are called the affinities of souls.

Therefore, no matter how precious the benevolent words of the Great Deity or the doctrines may be, souls without affinity repel each other just as water and oil do, and no effect is achieved at all. In the words of Shakyamuni, who was born in India in later ages:

さて木花姫命は、月照彦神以下の諸神を従え、天教山の中腹である青木ケ原に下り着かれた。ここにはかの銀橋を渡ってきた神々が数多く集まり、山上を見上げながら、木花姫命を先頭に数多くの供の神とともに下りて来るのを見て、一斉に手を打ち喊声を上げ、ウローウローと叫びながら、踊り狂って歓迎した。

ゆゑにいかに尊き大神の慈言といへども、教理といへども、因縁なき身魂は、あたかも水と油のごとく反撥して、その効果は到底あがらない。後世印度に生れた釈迦の言に、

§ 3

"Sentient beings without affinity are difficult to save."

Saying this is also based upon this principle. Therefore, those who possess a karmic affinity with the Ōkami, regardless of its depth or shallowness, thickness or thinness, are inevitably bound by a kind of subtle thread of divine affinity and cannot change their faith.

Also in the Shin'yu,

"Ōmoto of Ayabe shall draw together spirits with affinities from of old and have them perform divine work according to their affinities."

The reason this was divinely revealed is also that they are bound by the inseparable cord of divine affinity from the distant age of the gods.

"The spirits of affinity onto whom God has once cast a cord shall never be let go, no matter what. Try to do whatever you will in disobedience to what God says; you will only end up backtracking."

§ 4

This divine revelation means that, because one is tied to the cord of divine karma, even though one takes independent action and things go favorably for a time, when it reaches the point of ninety-nine percent and that cord of karma is pulled by the deity, one is destined to return once again to the original Great Bridge.

In the Divine Revelations, this is

"The Mechanism of Hooking and Drawing Back,"

as it is indicated.

Now, receiving the proclamation of Konohanahime-no-Mikoto, Tsukiteruhiko-no-Kami, Tarumahiko-no-Kami, Sukunahikona-no-Kami, Ōta-no-Kami, Hahuribe-no-Kami, Hirokohiko-no-Kami, and the other deities boarded the Silver Bridge that had come rotating again just at that time, and after circling once through midair, descended safely once more to Mount Himalaya where Nodatehime-no-Mikoto had appeared.

On Mount Himalaya, numerous divine persons worked day and night, felling trees below the eighth station of the mountain, making numerous large stakes, and carrying mallets, vigorously driving them into the ground. Tsukiteruhiko-no-Kami and his party, not knowing what this meant, bowed politely to the divine persons and, in solemn formation, proceeded toward the shrine of Nodatehime-no-Mikoto upon the mountain.
