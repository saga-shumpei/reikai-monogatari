Chapter 22: The Ark of Divine Revelation [222]

第22章　神示の方舟〔222〕

§ 1

Oe-no-kami was revered and respected by the deities as the God-King of the Little Heaven, and his commands were executed to the fullest extent.

Here, he promoted Kaniwaka to be the Guardian of the Left, and singled out Toen-o, appointing him as the Guardian of the Right; this single small precinct was truly as though a heavenly paradise had appeared.

Oe-no-kami climbed Mount Toen, employed his subordinate deities to dig out iron, and manufactured saws, axes, and other metal tools. Then he had the large trees that bore no fruit in Toen-kyo—such as cedar, cypress, and camphor—felled, and taught them to build numerous arks.

The deities did not know what this meant; they merely followed the command, dripping with sweat, and joyfully engaged in the construction of the arks and the manufacture of metal tools. Among the deities, some asked the Guardian of the Left for what purpose the arks were to be used. But the Guardian of the Left,

"For what purpose they are truly intended, I have not inquired even half a word of the God-King. We have only to obey the command of the God-King. Since he is a God-King who descended from heaven to provide for our safety, there is no reason for him to command useless things. You, too, must simply obey according to his command and devote yourselves whole-heartedly to the construction of the arks,"

declared.

All deeds wrought by God are not things that humans can fathom.

"Until this very moment God does not say a thing; if the people simply do as God says, there will not be the slightest fault,"

just as shown in the Divine Revelation, we must simply abandon trivial quibbles and act purely according to the divine command.

Nevertheless, among people there are many who act clever, saying that unless they inquire into everything from root to leaf, and from turnip to rapeseed, they cannot believe in God or perform divine service. However talented or learned one may be, it is impossible to comprehend the vast and boundless divine will and divine plan. Furthermore, even if God should attempt to convey this in detail to human beings, humans of body-primary spirit-secondary, struck by the three poisons of greed, anger, and delusion, could never truly consent to it. One need only believe in the words of God, polish one's body and soul, and engage in the divine work as commanded.

The deities of Ken'on-kyo had no worries about food, clothing, or shelter; their minds and souls were simple and without the slightest suspicion; they were innocent and free, possessing none of the cunning cleverness of modern people. For that reason, they were able to obey the divine command with submissiveness. In the Bible too,

顕恩郷の神人らは衣食住の憂ひなく、心魂ともに質朴にして少しの猜疑心もなく、天真爛漫にして現代人のごとく小賢しき智慧も持つてゐなかつた。そのために従順に神の命に服従することを得たのである。聖書にも、

"I give thanks that God does not reveal Himself to the strong and the wise, but reveals sincerity to the weak and the foolish,"

as it is said, nothing is more calamitous than the incomplete knowledge and intellectual talent of petty human beings.

Thus, through the day and night devotion of the divine people, three hundred and thirty-three splendid arks were completed. And into these vessels were loaded all fruits without exception, and they were also fully laden with livestock and seeds of plants and trees.

The pillar rock standing among the mountains in the northeast corner of Ken'on-kyō, which had been peaceful until now, suddenly roared and began to rotate front and back, left and right. And the stone statue of Onitakehiko gradually began to extend upward toward heaven. This is called the Heavenly Reversed Halberd.

The tribe possessing monkey-like features and the crab-faced tribe held hands with each other in mutual friendship, came beneath this reversed halberd, offered fruit wine, chanted prayers, and prayed that Ken'on-kyō might remain safe for ever and ever without end. At this time, a voice sounded from the Heavenly Reversed Halberd, saying:

"Clouds gather over the moon, storms strike the flowers; In heaven there are changes of wind, rain, and thunder; On earth there are disasters of earthquakes, floods, and fires; Unto divine people also come afflictions of disease and demons. Rosy cheeks in the morning, white bones in the evening; Mutable change is the way of the world. In this world that changes like deep pools into shallows, O divine people, do not slacken your hearts. Everlasting and rock-firm, through the reversed halberd, Establish and hold fast to a steadfast heart. Even though hardships of heaven and earth arise, Entrust it to God and do not be alarmed. Today's sky changes from yesterday's; Be prepared for this uncertain world, And never forget, even in dreams, The grace of sun and moon, earth, and God. Kannagara tamachi haimase, Kannagara tamachi haimase."

Resounding thus, the reversed halberd finally fell silent.

The divine people, in unison with uncertain words,

"Kantama, kantama,"

chanted.

When heaven and earth trembled, and the earthly world was engulfed in a great flood, the divine people of this village, without a single soul remaining behind, boarded these vessels, fled the disaster to Mount Himalaya, and became the ancestors of humanity the second time. Therefore, there are races that inherit the bloodline of the divine people of this village and retain their facial features to this day.

It is no wonder that modern biologists and anthropologists claim that humans evolved from apes. Furthermore, descendants of the crab-faced divine people survive in various parts of the world today, preserving their bloodline in the so-called Tsuchigumo race, which has short heads and flat faces.
