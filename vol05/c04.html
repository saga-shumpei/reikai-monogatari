§ 1

The purport of Banko Daijin's letter was as follows:

"Our eldest son Shiomitsuhiko has indulged in the love of your daughter Tamaharuhime, and their bond has already become inseparable as glue and lacquer; though we expound and admonish him with reason and righteousness, it is said that love knows no distinction of high and low, so we cannot do anything about this; we pray that you would send your daughter Tamaharuhime to us,"

so it ran.

Tokoyohiko, stating that it was the request of none other than Banko Daijin and also the advancement of his daughter, consulted with Tokoyohime and came to consent to this. In compensation for it,

"May you grant your daughter Shiojihime as a wife to our eldest son Tsuneharuhiko,"

he earnestly requested.

§ 2

"Thou shalt become the wife of Tokoharuhiko, the eldest son of Yatsuō-ōkami,"

so he strictly commanded. Shioharuhime was so astonished and dumbfounded as if to faint, and merely fixed her glaring eyes upon the faces of her parents. Her mouth convulsed, rendering her unable to utter a single word, and tears like a waterfall trickled from both of her eyes. Seeing this state of their most beloved daughter, Banko-ōkami and his wife felt as if nails and clamps were being driven into their breasts.

The demands of Yatsuō-ōkami grew more urgent day by day.

"If by any chance thou wilt not grant Shioharuhime, return my most beloved daughter Tamaharuhime to the sacred ground as swiftly as possible,"

he thus pressed with an inescapable and unyielding demand. Shioharuhime wept and cried out for seven days and seven nights, until at last she could no longer raise her voice. Meanwhile, Tokoharuhiko, donning a deep and large crown to cover his horns, crossed the great river of Eden, accompanied by four or five attendants; exasperated by the indecisive reply of Banko-ōkami, he advanced inside to negotiate directly himself.

At this time, Shioharuhime, unable to bear the coercion of her parents, escaped from within the gates and ran to the riverside intending to hide herself somewhere. At this moment, Tokoharuhiko conveniently encountered Shioharuhime at the riverside. Yet, deceived by the princess's utterly haggard appearance, he took her for someone else and advanced into the Castle of Eden.

Tokoharuhiko immediately requested an interview with Banko-ōkami and his wife, and earnestly pleaded that they grant Shioharuhime to be his wife. At this time, within the palace of Eden, they were startled by the disappearance of the princess, and numerous attendants were in the midst of searching every corner of the vast garden, rushing this way and that. "Because I myself came hiding my body on which horns have grown like an ogre, the princess disliked me and concealed herself, and the many attendants must be making such a commotion in order to strike and kill me. Lingering is dangerous; I shall return for now to the sacred ground, lead a great army of kami, and attack and destroy the palace of Eden,"

he deeply resolved in his heart, abruptly turned on his heel, left the palace behind, and returned to the riverside of Eden.

When he came to the riverside, many divine people were in a commotion on both banks of the river.

"What has happened?"

he asked. The divine people said in unison,

"Just now, Princess Shioji-hime, the daughter of Banko-no-Ōkami, cast herself into the river, and since even her figure cannot be seen, we are making a commotion trying somehow to save her,"

they answered.

§ 4

Upon an urgent report, Banko-daijin, accompanied by numerous deities, ran to the riverside and wept aloud, gazing at the river. Shiomitsuhiko and Tamaharuhime also followed behind and appeared at the scene. There stood her elder brother deity, Tokoharuhiko, staring blankly at the river. Tamaharuhime

"Elder brother,"

called out to him. Tokoharuhiko, startled by his younger sister's voice, turned around and said,

"Oh, is it you, Tamaharuhime? Return with me to the Holy Land!"

No sooner had he spoken than, clutching the princess under his arm, he plunged with a splash into the river, and their forms vanished.

Ah, what became of these three deities?

Just as Shiomitsuhiko, having lost his beloved wife, was beside himself with grief, looking up to heaven and lamenting his misfortune, suddenly, no sooner was a white cloud seen arriving before him, than Tamaharuhime, who had drowned in the water, smilingly appeared, firmly grasped the Mikoto's hand, and gallantly led him back to the palace.

Banko-daijin and his wife also rejoiced greatly upon seeing this sight, returned to the palace, and gave thanks to the deities of heaven and earth. Ah, who could this Tamaharuhime, who had just appeared, truly be?

In the palace of the Holy Land of Jerusalem, Hachiō-ōkami Tokoyohiko came out before the great gate, yawning at Tokoharuhiko's late return. Sent off from ahead by numerous deities, Tokoharuhiko, holding hands with Shiojihime, returned smilingly and looking most harmonious. Ah, of what deities could these two gods be the incarnations?
