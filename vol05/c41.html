Chapter 41: Non-Duality of the Way and God [241]

第41章　道神不二〔241〕

§ 1

Thousand-year-old pines and cedar groves, maples and mixed trees covered in moss; standing in divine solemnity upon Mount Seiun, the Golden Bridge glitters in the sky. Its color shining in the morning sun illumines the dark fleeting world in the pitch-black darkness of the eternal night; the jeweled shrine served by Tamamorihiko. The sky is clear and serene, and above the clouds where the distant stars shine keenly, this mountain rises steeply, the divine majesty ever higher, praised as Takamanohara, where high and low harmonize well, intimately keeping the way of the deities. That the divine majesty may shine in the four quarters with the three-thousand-year divine light, the great norito is prayed morning and evening, the resonance of the kotodama roaring with valor through heaven and earth and the four quarters.

The propagator Tentōwake, said to admonish the way of heaven and earth, crosses the valleys and tramples over the roots of rocks and trees as he climbs up this mountain that is so very high; his manly figure closely resembles the pure form of Kamisasanoo no Ōkami, who came into being as the guardian of the land of Wadatsumi where wild waves rage, unrivaled in the world. Ah, were it a reign of great peace, Kamisumihiko, the living deity revered by the world as one of the Eight Kings of Mount Seiun, is now like a fleeting blue cloud with an uncertain destination; Tentōwake, sharing the same heart and wishing to save the deep afflictions of all the deities and mortals under heaven, enters through the front gate of the Golden Shrine without any announcement.

Even though the world becomes perpetual darkness, the divine mind remains firm as enduring rock; the moonlight shining within his heart displays the valor of the morning sun deity illumining the universe. Clad in a thin garment of radiant cloth, buffeted by the raging wind as he parts the cloud paths and climbs up, no sooner does Kamisumihiko behold this figure of Tentōwake than he leaps up with high spirits, and accompanied by Kamisumihime, Azumahiko, and Azumanohime, he comes forth to receive him with utmost courtesy. Led by their guidance, the propagator, the living deity Tentōwake, advances deep into the inner sanctuary, and recounting the innumerable painful karmic circumstances since their parting, they converse together until the night deepens.

Their words are merely rustling, like autumn leaves whispering as they are blown by the winter gale.

Kamisumihime knelt reverently before the Golden Shrine, respectfully chanted the Amatsu-norito, arranged sacred food, sacred sake, sacred water, and various rare delicacies from the seas, rivers, mountains, and fields abundantly upon eight-legged tables like horizontal mountains, and with a clear voice in her norito, respectfully reported Tentōwake's arrival before the Golden Shrine; having finished, she returned to the living quarters, and here the pure feast of the naorai was delightfully opened.

Tamamorihiko, the chief priest of the Golden Shrine, holding a golden offering wand in his right hand and carrying a bell in his left hand, appeared at this feast and began to sing a congratulatory song to the propagator. That song:

"Pushing open the clouds that choke the distant heavenly sky, pushing aside the path of the heavenly plain and descending, comes the missionary of Amadinowake. The divine virtue—higher than Mount Seiun where Kamusumihiko-no-Kami-no-Mikoto of pure heart eternally rules—flows and flows, like the Yangtze River pouring into the vast thousand-fathom sea, divine teachings washing clean the five seas without a shadow. The Kotodama of Amadinowake breathes away the clouds and mists in the four directions of heaven and earth; together with the court official of Tamamorihiko, purer than the moon of later ages, whose heart clears like an autumn sky, and together with Kamusumihiko-no-Kami-no-Mikoto, who is purer than snow,

to illuminate the darkness of heaven and earth, the divine virtue of Nodatihiko-no-Kami has appeared on Mount Tenkyō. The merits of Konohana-hime-no-Mikoto, blooming all at once, fill Aokigahara to overflowing, and overflowing, the waves of the vast ocean settle calmly, a propitious sign of a tranquil reign. Ah, but, ah, but, with gathering clouds in the sky and mud drifting upon the earth, to save this land of deep anguish like a rough coast, and the divine people of the four quarters like white waves, his limbs are wounded upon the rocks like autumn leaves dyed in blood; setting aside even black and white, the true heart of Amadinowake-no-Kami-no-Mikoto will surely be favored by the gods of heaven and earth. This world approaches its end,

and tigers, wolves, lions, and giant serpents rage fiercely, seeking to swallow the land of Yasushima in a single gulp. Nodatihiko-no-Ōkami, seeking to save this world of eternal darkness and to rescue the multitude of divine people, does not forget them even for the space of a single trembling leaf of grass or tree; his blessing is higher than the peak of Mount Seiun, and his great merciful heart is deeper than the sea of the Dragon Palace. Those who draw from it strive to level this rough floating world of turbulent waves and restore it into a most peaceful divine age; how noble are the hearts of the officials, the missionaries who reveal the heart of the Sumera-kami, Amadinowake and Kamusumihiko! How noble are the hearts of the officials! ○ The plum blossoms of the three thousand worlds

bloom all at once, and waiting for the pine era of enduring, unchanging evergreen is no longer an ancient dream; now before our eyes, in the pine land of the divine people of Yamato-shimane, they shall prosper for a thousand ages, for eight thousand ages through the blessing of Mount Tenkyō. Ah, yet in the islands said to have formed from coagulated brine, demons and giant serpents run rampant, leaving no means of rescue—a grief of weeping, a drizzling rain of tears. Shining clear and serene like the cold moon that clears after the drizzle, sharpen your hearts, O hundred gods and attendant gods of Morokoshi! The departure of Amadinowake is the opening lord of the Heavenly Rock Cave of heavily clouded eternal darkness; the missionary Kamusumihiko,

the deep thoughts of the Sumera-kami dwelling in the divine age throughout the lands of the four quarters, upon the mountain ridges, in the river shallows, and to the furthest corners of the wilderness, blow across them, divine virtue; blow across them, O divine virtue!"

Swinging their long sleeves and holding the large purification wand and bells in both hands, they finished singing with an interesting melody, adding splendor to this banquet. From this moment, the married couple Kamusumihiko-no-Kami and Azumahiko-no-Kami, together with Amadinowake-no-Mikoto, reciting the divine revelations and missionary songs of Mount Tenkyō, left Mount Seiun behind and departed for parts unknown in order to save the turbid world.
