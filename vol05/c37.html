Chapter 37: The One-Wheeled Chariot [237]

第37章　片輪車〔237〕

§ 1

The party of missionaries that had appeared on the plains of Egypt, where a violent wind was raging, parted ways here to the east and the west. Pointing toward the east, Tsukiteruhiko no Kami walked with loud footsteps, and, braving the wind with his long hair disheveled, finally disappeared into the shade of the trees. Accompanied by Sugitakahiko and Hahorohiko, Hahorobe no Kami hurried along the road toward Jerusalem, singing songs of propagation.

Even that fierce wind and heavy rain cleared completely away, and the rustling of the grass also ceased. From far ahead, an emaciated, bedraggled woman came advancing toward the north, pulling a cart made of cross-cut rounds of a large pine tree. Looking closely, a male deity, who appeared to be crippled in the legs, was riding upon the cart. It was the very image of Hatsuhana pulling the crippled Katsugorō upon a cart over Mount Hakone.

Straining her faint voice and singing something, the woman pulled the cart slowly along as if it were heavy.

On rainy nights and on windy nights, Since leaving Ken'onkyō, Across the rapid shallows of the River Eden, Husband and wife taking each other's hands, Crossing the fleeting rapids of this world, Floating and sinking to the southern shore, No sooner had they arrived than, in Tōenkyō, By people resembling apes, While their wounded bodies were being pursued, Entering deep into the recesses of the mountains, Crowned with stars and treading on moonlight, At the countless cries of the apes, Their hearts aching and their breasts crushed, Barely escaping the mouth of the ogre, Leaving behind the dwelling place of the giant serpent, Whether by heaven's blessing or earth's grace, Our dark selves, like white clouds, Pitiable even to the eyes of onlookers, The married couple, deep in the mountains, Fighting against hunger and cold.

In contrast to yesterday's splendor, Today the end of a rotting tree; Knowing neither how to advance nor how to retreat, Deep in the valley of the mountains, Despairing over our falling selves, Wringing sleeves soaked with tears, Husband and wife embracing one another, Spending dark nights weeping till dawn, How many times have we made pillows of grass? Stumbling over stones and wounding our feet, A tattered, desperate pair traveling together; My husband's illness, day and night, Pain and suffering unbearable, Kasugahime sinks into despair; When I recall, long ago in Moscow, As the most beloved daughter of Hachiōshin, The divine grace of being born; Higher than mountains, deeper than an ocean Of eight thousand fathoms was my parents' favor, Yet forgetting my parents, I went to the Eternal World;

Wandering lost in the darkness of love, Following after Takasumibewake, The outcome of trials and tribulations: The Hachiōshin of the Eternal World, Tokoyohiko and Tokoyohime, The benevolence of that divine couple Deeply permeating, for many years Wandering in the darkness of the Eternal World, That divine punishment is before our very eyes; Once, by the grace of the Kami, Being saved in Ken'onkyō, I became the wife of Nanten'ō, Gathering upon myself the reverence Of all gods and men, boasting of splendor; The couple joined like moon, snow, and flowers, Straying from the Way of heaven and earth, Wandering into crooked ways, How terrifying is the end of such a life; To help the body of my husband, A cripple who cannot even walk,

§ 2

Karma revolves like a small wheel, In Egypt of revolving wheels, Fiercely fighting the autumn tempest, Like autumn leaves in the cold gale, How wretched is my scattering self. How many times have I received The sword of frost upon my frail soul, Enduring and enduring until now, Though I arrive here, what does not end Is my karma and past sins. This heavy burden of accumulated sins and evils— In what age will it be blown away By the wind of Shinado? Gazing intently at the sky, The sun and moon shine in the heavenly sky Just as they did in days of old, Illuminating the trees and grasses on every side, Yet unilluminated is my own misfortune; Though my heart is shattered into a thousand pieces Yearning to return to my original old nest,

With this illness of my beloved husband, Even if the Sun Deity should rise In the western sky, Or the very depths of the Ryūgū sea dry up, Could I ever abandon the beloved husband-deity To whom I swore long into the future? My life is not worth living; Casting myself into the rough sea of life's strait, With disaster raining down on me, The end of my self is truly terrifying. Ah, in heaven and earth, to save the world, Is there no deity, does no deity exist? Ah, in heaven and earth, to save the world, Is there no deity, does no deity exist?"

Singing thus mournfully, she came advancing toward this side.

Seeing the figure of this woman, Hahuribe-no-kami was so astonished that he almost fell over. Without saying a word, Hahuribe-no-kami gazed intently at the visage of this emaciated woman, tilted his head, and seemed like one lost in thought over something. As if unable to bear it, the woman clung to Hahuribe-no-kami's sleeve, pressed her gaunt face tightly against the area around his navel, shed tears like a waterfall, and her sobs could even be heard.

With a pained expression, Hahuribe-no-kami stroked and rubbed the woman's back repeatedly. The woman finally raised her face and,

"In this shameful condition of my present self, to meet you in an unexpected place leaves me without words to say. As you know, I served at Tokoyo Castle, and am Kasugahime, the eldest daughter of Michinukihiko, one of the Eight Kings of Mosukō, who was celebrated alongside Yashimahime as the Moon, Snow, and Flowers at the Tokoyo Conference. Are you not the unforgettable Iwashirohiko, one of the Eight Kings of Tenzan?"

she asked.

Wary of their surroundings, Sasuraigami placed his hand over Kasugahime's mouth. Kasugahime understood his intent and,

"I have said something rude. My spirit is weakened and my eyes are clouded from the exhaustion of a long journey; please forgive this unexpected carelessness and discourtesy,"

she feigned ignorance. Hahuribe-no-kami said afresh,

"I do not know what woman you are, but seeing your appearance, I perceive you to be the offspring of no ordinary person, but of a divine being. In accordance with the command of Konohanahime-no-Mikoto residing on Mount Tenkyō, prior to the demolition and reconstruction of the world, I am a branch deity who proclaims the gospel of salvation far and wide to the divine beings on earth. I am not a person of such noble origin as you speak of,"

he said, intentionally putting on a feigned look of ignorance. Looking at the divine being upon the cart, Hahuribe-no-kami said,

"Oh, are you..."

raising his voice eccentrically,

"Why are you riding in a cart? It makes no sense,"

he asked, rounding his eyes, pursing his lips, and rubbing his nose.

The man upon the cart let tears flow sorrowfully, covered his eyes with both hands, and hung his head.

Ah, what will the outcome of this be?
