Chapter 18: The Divine Messengers [218]

第18章　宣伝使〔218〕

§ 1

Here, the deities who gathered by the invitation of Konohanahime no Mikoto, who was enshrined on Mount Tenkyō (also called Mount Shumisen), were:

Ōyashimahiko no Mikoto (also known as Tsukiteruhiko no Kami), Ōdaruhiko (also known as Darumahiko), Kototamawake no Mikoto (also known as Sukunahikona no Kami), Kamikuniwake no Mikoto (also known as Hiroyasuhiko no Kami), Kuninaohime no Mikoto (also known as Kuniteruhime no Kami), Ōmichiwake (also known as Hinode no Kami), Iwakusuhiko (also known as Iwatowake no Kami), Tokiyohiko (also known as Hahuribe no Kami), Ōshimawake (also known as Ōta no Kami), Onitakehiko (also known as Ōe no Kami), Tsukihimyōjin who was the combination of the two deities Takakura and Asahi,

and other deities.

Those deities assembled at the sacred ground of Aokigahara halfway up Mount Tenkyō, obeyed the divine decree of Nodatehiko no Mikoto, became prophets in order to awaken the deities throughout the realm, and were dispatched to various parts of the world. The words of that prophecy said:

"The plum blossoms that bloom at once throughout the three thousand worlds; know the grace of the sun, moon, and earth; the savior deity of single-hearted devotion appears on Mount Tenkyō."

While chanting these divine words, the aforementioned deities, making pretext of nursery rhymes, theatrical entertainments, or music, propagated the prophecy and warning of the Land Ancestor in secret amidst great hardships.

However, the deities who were completely seduced from the bottom of their hearts by the evil spirits of the great serpent and the golden fox almost entirely belittled this prophecy, merely letting it pass as a popular song at drinking feasts; and those who, immediately humming it upon touching upon affairs and meeting with things, sought to investigate the true meaning of the warning, give thanks for the divine grace of the sun and moon, and polish their body and soul, were scarcely even one in a thousand.

Tokoyo-shin'ō tilted his head in wonder upon hearing the children's song of Tsukihimyōjin, who came singing delightfully in tune before the gate, "The plum blossoms that bloom at once throughout the three thousand worlds, etc.," had Ōtakawake accompany Tsukihimyōjin and invite him into the palace, and had this song sung in the presence of all the assembled deities.

§ 2

Tsukihimyōjin matched the clapping of hands and the tapping of feet delightfully, and began to sing and dance gracefully. Everyone marveled at the exquisite skill and watched in fascination.

The deities rejoiced as if listening to the music of heavenly maidens, and some rose of their own accord to sing the song and danced ecstatically with elegance alongside Tsukihimyōjin. The interior of the hall was filled with the joyful voices of the deities, resembling spring. Tokoyo-shin'ō alone, in contrast to the deities who were innocently rejoicing and dancing wildly, held his head in both hands with an expression of unbearable anguish, kept his head cast down throughout, shed tears from both eyes, showed a look of fear and trembling, and appeared somewhat restless.

Perceiving this state of affairs, Ōtakawake bowed respectfully before Tokoyo-shin'ō and said:

"Why does the Divine King appear so gloomy and distressed while watching such delightful singing and dancing? I cannot comprehend it, and wish to know your true intention; if it is within my humble power, no matter what difficulty it may be, I will spare not my life to serve the Divine King."

Thus he advised, with utmost sincerity showing upon his face.

Yet, Tokoyo-shin'ō merely bowed his head, uttered not a single word, and did nothing but sigh repeatedly. Ōtakawake quietly inquired again of his true intention. Tokoyo-shin'ō spoke but a single word:

"Entertain Tsukihimyōjin with great care, and treat him with hospitality as the chief guest of this castle,"

and having left these words, quickly concealed himself within the inner palace.

§ 3

Tsukihi-myōjin turned to the multitude of deities, and said:

"The end of the world has drawn near; apologize from the bottom of your hearts for the sins of your souls to the deities of heaven and earth."

Even as she spoke, her form vanished like smoke.

After a while, Tokoyo-shinnō turned to Ōtakawake and said:

"The nursery song sung by the one called Asahi-myōjin is not a song made by ordinary deities, but is a prophetic warning from the exalted deity dwelling in heaven; therefore, we must repent of our past errors as swiftly as possible, thank the great benevolence of the sun, moon, and earth, and enshrine the divine spirits of heaven and earth. For this, we too require a great determination. Promptly send back Shiojihime, the daughter of Banko-shinnō, and Tamaharuhime, the daughter of Uraruhiko, to the sacred capital of Armenia with abundant courtesy, erect a temporary shrine upon Mount Rocky without delay, and swiftly commence preparations for relocation."

Thus he strictly commanded. Ōtakawake, unable to comprehend the true intention of the divine king and feeling it foolish within his heart, nevertheless caused numerous deities to send the two women back to Armenia according to the command, leveled the earth upon the summit of Mount Rocky, and came to construct a mere semblance of a temporary shrine.

In the sacred capital of Armenia, Banko-shinnō, as well as Uraruhiko, felt relieved, regarding this as a sign that Tokoyo-shinnō had suddenly repented of his past errors and submitted from the bottom of his heart, and while their disdain increased, their spirits soared to the skies.

§ 4

"Plum blossoms opening all at once across the three thousand worlds."

Thus sang a Kotoburenokami (propagator) who passed by. Banko-shin'ō pricked up his ears at this voice, pressed his chest, and prostrated himself on the spot. This was because, as soon as this voice entered his ears, he felt as if his head were splitting and his chest were being torn apart.

Uralhiko and his wife, seeing this state of the Divine King, could not bear their suspicion, and hurriedly ran up to tend to him. The Divine King raised his right hand, waved it from side to side, and, while exhaling painful breaths,

"Listen to the voice of the Kotoburenokami just now,"

he said. The two deities replied,

"He is a wanderer who travels throughout the realm seeking food from divine men; that you should trouble your mind and body by believing the words of such a divine person seems unlike the words of the Divine King, who is usually wise and daring. Are you not suffering from a nervous affliction?"

they asked, with a faint sneer.

The Divine King, in a manner as though the words of the two did not even enter his ears, clasped both hands together, now bowing to heaven and now bowing to earth,

"Know the grace of the sun, the moon, and the earth; know the grace of the sun, the moon, and the earth; forgive the sins of the divine men of the world, and deliver our clan from this great disaster,"

thus, sweating profusely, he concentrated on praying in complete absorption.

Uralhiko and his wife, seeing this spectacle, could hardly contain their amusement and were on the verge of bursting into laughter, but out of deference to the Divine King's presence, they shed tears of laughter from both eyes and withdrew from the spot. And the Kotoburenokami who had appeared on this spot was Hinodenokami.
