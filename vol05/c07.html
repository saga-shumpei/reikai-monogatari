"Please wait a while,"

Nanten'ō said,

"I certainly sympathize with your feelings, Princess. However, your father Ōshimawake has gradually grown old, and has repeatedly requested me to search for your whereabouts and send you to him; yet, deeming that the time had not yet come, I have not disclosed this to you until today, but please open and read this letter,"

he said, taking out a sealed letter from a vessel beside him and handing it to Yashimahime. With a dubious expression, Yashimahime took the letter in her hand, and when she looked at it closely, it was unmistakably her father's handwriting. Her heart was beating as if a rapid bell were being struck. Calming her throbbing chest, when she quietly broke the seal and looked, the following message was written in jet-black ink. The text said:

"As an Eight King of Nankōzan, I had been favored with the trust of Kunitsu-no-Ōkami, and though it has now become the divine government ruled by Banko-no-Ōkami, the deeply benevolent Banko-no-Ōkami has appointed me as an Eight King just as before. However, there is no telling when the Prime Minister deity, Hachiō-no-Ōkami Tokoyohiko, might change his heart, strip me of my office, and destroy us. You, Yashimahime, return to our main castle as soon as possible, become husband and wife with the loyal and valiant attendant Tamazumihiko, and succeed me. Ah, yet Tamazumihiko, after the conference at Tokoyo Castle, went after you, searched around various places in the world, and even now his whereabouts are unknown. If, fortunately by the protection of Kunitsu-no-Ōkami, you are reunited with Tamazumihiko, at that very time, become husband and wife through the mediation of Hinode-no-Kami, promptly return to the castle at Nankōzan, and comfort your father's heart."

Such was the letter. Upon seeing this, Yashimahime, while blushing all the more, respectfully returned the letter to Nanten'ō's hands along with tears of gratitude.

§ 4

Here, the two deities held the wedding ceremony. In her heart, Yashimahime seemed once again to bow her head and be lost in thought, thinking how greatly her only, aged father would rejoice if he were present at such an auspicious and happy wedding seat.

Here, Nanten'ō turned to Tamasumihiko and,

"Since thou hast no father here now, I shall become both matchmaker and father, and attend this ceremony,"

he said. And then,

"As for Yashimahime, since her father is alive, I shall have them meet face to face here now,"

he declared. Yashimahime could not comprehend it at all, thinking that even if her father dwelling far away on Mount Nankō possessed divine and miraculous power, there was no reason for him to appear swiftly in this place now. Thinking it strange, the moment she raised her bowed head, wondrously, her father Ōshimawake was seated in a higher place than Nanten'ō. Here, Ken'onkyō was filled with the unexpected blossoms of joy from the reunion of parent, child, husband, and wife; all together in one voice thanked the divine favor, and were deeply moved by the abundance of that heavenly grace.

Until now this village had been called Kawakitagyō, but following this event, it was named Ken'onkyō. Tamasumihiko then secretly fled back to Mount Nankō by night with his father, and became the eight kings of Mount Nankō. In the palace of Ken'onkyō, the white fox Asahi still transformed into Yashimahime and served near Nanten'ō. Nanten'ō summoned the numerous divine people of this village into the palace, and proclaimed that he would yield the position of great king to his son Takasumiwake. Without the slightest hesitation, the divine people clapped their hands in celebration and revered Takasumiwake as the great king.

Then, Hinode-no-kami, the former Nanten'ō, slipped away under cover of night and set forth somewhere for the divine work of heavenly administration. The divine people firmly believed without the slightest doubt that the former Nanten'ō had returned to heaven in the dead of night. Mourning the former great king's return to heaven, the divine people arranged rare delicacies from the mountains, fields, rivers, and seas upon the rocks, offered them, raised voices of gratitude, grew drunk on fruit wine, clapped their hands once more, and made merry, singing and dancing. Takasumiwake donned the royal crown, went beside Bōiwa together with Kasugahime, and held a celebratory banquet. The divine people presented cups of wine by turns to the two deities.

When Tsuneharuhiko, who had been placed upon Bōiwa, opened the door and looked down, it appeared as though his most beloved Yōjihime had harmoniously become husband and wife with Takasumiwake and was receiving the celebratory cups of the divine people; Tsuneharuhiko ground his teeth in vexation and rampaged back and forth, left and right, within the palanquin. This Bōiwa, which wobbled even in a slight breeze, shook violently from Tsuneharuhiko's roar, and at that very instant plunged headlong into the valley along with the palanquin.

What would be the outcome of this?
