武志の宮〔663〕

§ 1

To clear the darkness of Tokoyo, the divine Miizu of Kami becomes the rain of dew and snow, deep within the quiet Ihaya of Takakuma, to wash and illuminate the ugly world of Utsusemi. Asahiko, shining in the sky, and Tamateruhiko, with his powerful light, receive the Mei of Izu. Escorted by the Shinrei of Kototeruhime and many other Kami, Matsuhime, with a heart full of joy, crosses the mountain slopes of the Miroku Kamiyo. Along the Tamaboko road, wide and vast, she reaches the peak of Yotsuōzan, the eastern face, where the colors of Momiji shine. At the foot of Terayama stands a temporary palace, where Yoshikohime-hime, in awe of the Mikoto of Kami, keeps watch.

From the Miiki of Konohanahime-hime, the Kami Tamateruhiko emerges and, with courage, comes forth to escort them. Murasakihime and Wakahiko, united with the fire, water, earth, and heaven, rejoice and welcome the Hiko-Kami. With all their hearts, they serve the spouse of the Hime-Mikoto of Tamateru. The great Kami Susanoo sends Eikohime to serve the hundred-fold plan, the foundation of the Miroku Kamiyo. The great Kami Kuninotokotachi appears as Kunitakehiko, hiding his form at Okebuseyama as a pioneer to illuminate and purify the world that has grown dark in its final age.

Together with the golden jewel, the Miizu shines in all four directions. Kotoyoriwake-no-Mikoto leaves the hall of Saien, pushes through the clouds, and arrives at the sacred land of Aya. Rejoicing in the divine spirits of the Hiko-Mikoto and Hime-Mikoto of Tamateru, who are the divided spirits of the Imperial Kami of the warp and weft, he proclaims and transmits the Sangokyō far and wide, forevermore.

Kotoyoriwake-no-Mikoto, obeying the command of the great Kami Susanoo, served the noble shrines of the great Kami Kuninotokotachi and the great Kami Toyokunihime between the mountains of Terayama and Okebuseyama, enshrining their majestic spirits. He made Tamateruhiko and Tamateruhime serve the palace, establishing the foundation for the divine work of world governance. He cut trees from the mountains and fields, near and far, to build the sacred palace. Thinking of the path of the Kami and men, and the world itself, their sincere hearts coalesced, and the magnificent, unparalleled sacred palace was built in an instant. It is called the Palace of Brocade. Tamateruhiko and Tamateruhime, from their youth, excelled over many other Kami, possessing high divine virtue and superior divine status, eventually gathering the respect of all people under heaven as the saviors of the Kamiyo.

Kotoyoriwake-no-Mikoto, obeying the command of the great Kami Susanoo, took the Palace of Brocade as his base to hold the supreme authority of the Sangokyō on the island of Jitentōtō. Together with the Caucasus Mountains and the hall of Saien, he expanded the divine work of world purification throughout the universe. It goes without saying that the missionaries of the Sangokyō were active, and Takahime, Kurohime, and Matsuhime, who had strongly resisted the Mizu-no-Mitama by establishing the Uranai-kyō, turned their hearts as if waking from a dream, dedicating their lives to the Sangokyō, and traveled throughout Jitentōtō and overseas countries to expand the divine virtue. Here, the reincarnation of the spirit of Mototeruhiko, Amanomau-ra, was born at the foot of Mount Odaigahara. He lived his days as a woodcutter, but hearing that the Palace of Brocade had been built in the high heavens of Aya and that its divine virtue was shining in all directions, he abandoned his trade as a woodcutter and traveled far to visit the sacred land, where he was granted an audience with Kotoyoriwake-no-Mikoto and became a new missionary. Amanomau-ra was greatly overjoyed, and without distinction between day and night, he sang divine songs while attempting the divine work of proclaiming divine virtue, starting with Jitentōtō. Leaving the sacred land alone, he pushed through the sea of fog and followed the wind, arriving at the western foot of Hitono-o Pass. The powdery snow, so thick that one could not even see the people walking, fell like a waterfall, and before his eyes, it piled up about a foot on the ground. The sun set, the road grew longer, and Amanomau-ra lost his way. Unable to move forward or backward, he stood on the road wearing his straw raincoat and hat, repeating the Amatsu-Norito in a low voice over and over again. Dozens of shadows of strange beasts formed a line and descended from the mountain. However, Amanomau-ra's eyes were blocked by the snow falling like a waterfall, and he did not know they were approaching until they were at his feet. He only heard the sound of someone's footsteps crushing the snow and voices gradually drawing near. Amanomau-ra muttered to himself,

'Until now I was raised in a warm country, and I had never seen such deep snow. Having entered the path of the Kamisama for the first time, and having completed one hundred days and one hundred nights of Shiugiyō, I was finally permitted to become a Sendenshiho and have come this far as my feet allowed... Ah, I have reached a dead end. Kotoyoriwakenomikoto-sama commanded me, "Thou shalt henceforth cross the Hitono-o Pass and attempt thy Hatsusenden in the village of Uzu where the river waters are pure." However, with this great snow piling up, and moreover in this valley where trees grow thick, the sun is beginning to set... Ah, being a Sendenshi is a painful thing. The snow piles up more and more; if things go wrong, my body will be buried in the snow and will surely become cold. To be at a standstill is exactly this situation.'

Just as he was murmuring despondently, the previous footsteps gradually drew closer. Looking, he saw a pack of several dozen bears, running vigorously toward Maura's feet. Maura was startled and avoided the path in the snow. The pack of bears, without any hesitation, formed a line and went running toward the west.

Maura breathed a sigh of relief.

'Ah, how grateful I am; many bears appeared and trod through the snow path, opening a fine passage for me. This too must be entirely the divine virtue of the Kamisama... How grateful, how grateful.'

While giving thanks, he climbed up, treading in the footprints of the bears. He noticed that there was a single thatched hut by the side of the road. Inside the house, a faint firelight was flickering. The snow spray brought by the wind became increasingly violent, and the new path in the snow that the bears had gone to the trouble of opening was blocked in an instant. Inside the house, there were voices laughing and chatting lightheartedly. Maura, hearing these voices laughing pleasantly, thought:

'Truly, it is an enviable thing. Although I follow a divine command, in contrast to my suffering on this snow path, the pain of extreme cold where even the thread of life is about to snap, and the hardship where the blood flowing through my veins is about to freeze, there is the sound of cheerful laughter inside this house... Truly, there is nothing so subject to luck as a person's circumstances. However, I am also in the first campaign of a Sendenshi who spreads the way of God; to be afraid of such snow and try to enter a person's house to ask for a night's lodging is somehow shameful. Ah, what shall I do?'

Just as he was hesitating, a man's voice came from inside the house.

Man: 'Hey, I do not know what kind of beggar you are, but what are you loitering for in this falling snow? Why don't you come into my house and rest a bit? There is hot water boiling. There is a large fire burning, too.'

松鷹彦『俺は神様に対して不言実行、暗祈黙祷を行つて居るのだ。どつか其辺らに不言実行者が、モウ出さうなものだ。アイタヽヽ』

真浦は赤裸となり、裏の川にザンブと飛び込み、御禊をなし、一生懸命で何事か祈願し始めた。爺イの足の痛みは不思議にもピタリと止まつた。

松鷹彦『真浦様、有難う。御神徳を頂きました。サアどうぞ此方へ来て下さい。火を焚いてあたらしてあげませう』
