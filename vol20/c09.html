Chapter 9: 童子 Teaching [671]

童子教〔671〕

§ 1

In the Kokonohe capital of flowers, in Yamaushiro, during the awe-inspiring Kamiyo, along the Afumiji, the colors are Wakasa and Tanba. Across the three provinces stands the Sangokugatake. The dense thickets of trees block out the sun and moon of the Ōzora, creating a Monosugo atmosphere. Even in the daytime, the Daikauhō is dark. Whether it be an ogre, an Orochi, a demon, or a human, one old woman, terrifying even to behold, dwells there. Upon a towering rock base, she has built a temporary hermitage, catching and eating snakes and frogs to pass the days in a state of terror. At the foot of this mountain stand thirty or forty small, dilapidated huts, a Betsusekai separated from the Ukiyo. The Rauniyakunanniyo fish for crabs and frogs in the Tanigaha, making them their Ejiki to survive the days.

Looking up to the Onibaba as their Tōriyō, they sneak out into the night, extending their reach to Yamashiro, Tanba, Afumiji, Wakasa, and even Noto. Searching for the cries of Akago, they seize them whenever there is an opening, fleeing back along the mountain ridges to offer them as Ejiki to the old woman. Who is this Magatsumi? To pacify and harmonize, to dispel the sufferings of the world and save it, Kotoyowake, who appeared in the Takamanohara, revering the words of the Lord Susanoo Ōkami, was commanded to go forth. When he entrusted this mission to Munahiko, whose heart was pure and who had ascended to the sacred ground, Munahiko rejoiced and accepted without hesitation. Declaring this the first battle of his mission, he took leave of his parents and siblings.

Crossing over the Akashi Pass, he reached the solitary house in Kumada on the road. He blew away the confusion in the heart of Harahiko, who was suffering from illness, and manifested a miraculous divine power. He caused all the Rauniyakunanniyo of the village to return to the great path of the Sangyō, leaving not a single one behind. Resting his feet for a short while, he was accompanied by three believers: Harahiko, Tagosaku, and Tomekō. Crossing the single-log bridge in the village of Yamaguni on the plains surrounded by the Aogaki Mountains, they left Miyamura and Hanase behind, and the four of them finally arrived at the foot of Sangokugatake.

The Daikauhō, spanning the three provinces, is indeed such that the cedar groves are dense and pregnant with wind, and the roaring voices sound as if hundreds and thousands of lions and wolves are howling at once. Although it is summer, the air drifts with a chill that penetrates to the core, and the sound of the Tanigaha strikes the ears as if demons were whispering. Groups of monkeys leap through the treetops, crying and screaming 'kyatsu-kyatsu.' The four of them sat down heavily on the banks of the deep Tanigaha flowing at the foot of the mountain, resting from the fatigue of their journey and indulging in hushed conversation.

Tomekō: 'This is a truly eerie valley, is it not? Ever since we crossed the log bridge in Yamaguni, we have not encountered a single human soul. From time to time, strange voices come from the dense forests on both sides; this place seems to be a den of demons even worse than Ōeyama. Hey, Tagosaku, although I came here with joy and courage, my neck is shivering, and I have ended up in a state of mind I cannot even describe.'
