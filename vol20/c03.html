Chapter 3: Mountain River 不尽 [665]

山河不尽〔665〕

§ 1

Tomekō, making the ground rumble with a thudding sound, once again trampled the Akago in the potato field with great care, and without learning his lesson, said, 'Hey, you potato bastard, you have exerted an influence on me; even a potato is not to be underestimated. Well, now that it has come to this, what is good, what is bad, what is scary, what is cute, what is difficult, what is happy, or what is sad? I shall trample you, you Sandoimo bastard, to the end with my six fingers. How potato-like, how potato-like!'

He was treading and crushing them to his heart's content, putting strength into his feet. The one who came running there was the Sendenshi of Maura, who, seeing this state of affairs, said:

Maura: 'Tome-san, what are you doing?'

Tomekō: 'Oh dear, you have discovered a terrible scene. However, please, I beg you, spare me from the Sendenka at least, for I feel as if the number of heads I have is branching off into a Bunke...'

Maura: 'Very well, if you dislike it, I shall remain silent; but is that not a potato you are trampling now?'

Tomekō: 'Yes, in these days of rising Butsuka, if Akago increase in such Takusan numbers, the Daiichi concern is that the Kokumin will suffer for Shiyokuriyō. Since they are called Sandoimo, the kind that gives birth to children three times a year, Mrs. Sanger has come for the sake of birth control and has just begun a great campaign here. Please, I beg you to overlook this and not refuse them landing, ahahaha!'

Maura: 'Is it not troublesome to do such a thing? It is the way of the Lord God that we must increase people as numerous as the stars in the heavenly sky and give birth to Akago as numerous as the sands on the beach; is it right to ignore the great path of creation and development and commit such violence?'

Tomekō: 'I am convinced that this is the best method, most suited to the will of God, whether viewed from the perspective of the national economy or from the academic theory of human coexistence. Please, listen to my speech just once; it is very thorough, you know.'

Maura: 'The speech is cancelled; no, I absolutely order its dissolution.'

Just as this was happening, the man from before came running, carrying a hoe, his hair standing on end with rage, and said:

Man: 'Hey, hey! Are you killing the precious, precious Akago again?'

Tomekō: 'Oh, I have made up my mind to conquer the Akago even if I have to swap them for Brahmanism, so no matter what you say, I will not stop. Well, you had better resign yourself to the fact that this is also a fate from a previous life and give them a respectful funeral.'

The man, his anger reaching the peak of his heart, raised his hoe straight above his head and struck down, aiming for Tomekō's head. Tomekō nimbly dodged, and the hoe missed, slicing off the little finger of Maura's foot. Maura grimaced, quickly picked up the severed finger, and pressed it against the wound. The finger adhered just as it was. It seems he was so flustered that he attached the tip of the little finger inside out. Until this point, the name Morihiko had been attached to Maura, but it was here that the name Maura was created for the first time.

Man: 'This is, this is a most Shitsurei thing I have done, Dōzo forgive me. Motsutai, to cut the finger of a Sendenshi... how should I atone for this sin? I have committed a Buchōhafu against the Shinkai that can never be Torikahe.'

He sinks into weeping.

Tomekō: 'He is a Sendenshi of the Ikigami who saves the Sekai. Even if one finger or half a hand were lost, if one were to weaken over such a thing, one would not be a Sendenshi. More than that, the Inochi of the Akago in your care has met a most Muzan fate, has it not?'

Man: 'After ruining the Imodane that I have put so much Tansei into, do you still spit out Gofutaku? Eh, my Kanninbukuro has burst, prepare yourself!'

He brandishes his hoe again and presses upon Tomekō. The Sendenshi firmly Uketo the handle of this hoe,

Maura: 'Now, now, wait a moment; Tanki is Sonki. Potatoes are Taisetsu, but human Inochi is also Taisetsu.'

Man: 'From morning till night, I have treated them like my own Jibun-born children, applying fertilizer, weeding, and caring for these precious potato children in so many ways, only for them to be trampled and crushed... how could their foster parent remain silent? Potatoes have their own spirits dwelling within them. They must surely be suffering. Poor things... to whom can these Akago appeal this resentment? If I do not get angry on their behalf, these Akago will surely not be able to find peace... Ah, my potato children, you poor things, but now that it has come to this, there is no help for it. I shall pray for your happiness in the next world, so depart for the Yūmeikai without regrets and live in peace, an-an.'

He cries out with a feigned, loud wail.

Tomekō: 'Ahahaha, that is why, Tagosaku, I say you are a fool. If they are such precious potatoes, why do you boil them in a pot or take a kitchen knife to them to eat them when they grow large? Because you say such contradictory things, you are called a madman. Tell me, Sendenshi-sama, does this logic not fail to hold together?'

He says with a look of self-satisfaction.

Tagosaku: 'That may be so, but... I cannot help but feel they are pitiable. To think that one would so cruelly massacre weak Akago that know neither west nor east; a potato should have its own lifespan. When autumn comes and the vines wither, that is when its lifespan has ended; if one eats it then, the potato would surely be content. You were born into such misfortune, poor thing.'

And again, he is filled with tears.

Tomekō: 'Hey Tagosaku, which is more important to you, a human life or a taro root? Which do you hold as the master?'

Tagosaku: 'That is a settled matter. If I were to compare you to a taro, you are just about ready to be eaten. You are an object for which there is no longer any use in this world, so I would not regret losing you, nor would it be a loss to the nation. On the contrary, it would be like cleaning up the dust and filth of society.'

Maura: 'Ahahaha, I have been treated to a truly interesting taro-logic. However, since the spirit of Kamisama dwells in all things, there is a corresponding Mitama in everything, without distinction between high or low, old or young, plants or tools. Because the precious Mitama of Kamisama dwells in all of creation, you must not treat even a single leaf of a tree with disrespect.'

Tagosaku: 'Look at that, Tomekō. Is it not a bit strange that even what a crazy fool says is, after all, in accordance with the truth of heaven and earth?'

Tomekō tilts his head, folds his hands, sits upright on the green grass, and is thinking intently about something. Finally, he raises his face.

Tomekō: 'Ah, everything has become clear. Tagosaku-dono, please forgive me. From now on, I will never do such a thing again...'

Tagosaku: 'No matter what you say, since it has come to this, there is no help for it. Be careful from now on. It is not just taros; beans and wheat are all the same, you know.'

Tomekō: 'Yes, I understand. I will keep that in mind.'

And he greets him politely, unlike before.

Maura: 'Ah, with this, everything is resolved. With the corpse of the taro, peace is restored at last. Now, shall we go to meet Tomohiko-san of the Brahman religion and hear what he has to say?'

As he attempts to leave, Tomekō holds him back.

Tomekō: 'Excuse me, Sendenshi-sama, please wait a moment. It is dangerous for you to go all alone. I know the situation well, but the Sendenshi is staying in my detached room, and Kamisama is enshrined there as well. However, there is a wide, deep ditch dug around the house, and if one were to carelessly try to cross it... that would be a disaster... you would lose your life.'

Maura: 'Is that a true story?'

Tomekō: 'It is indeed true. It is my house, after all; why would I say anything mistaken? Just like the proverb about lending the eaves and having the main house taken away, the Sendenshi Tomohiko, who first came acting like a beggar, now behaves as if my parlor and main house are his own with great force. I am treated like an apprentice; there is nothing as extreme as this reversal of host and guest. At first, I thought he was a truly splendid Sendenshi and was captivated, doing whatever he said, but the inconsistency between the words and actions of this Sendenshi lately is truly beyond words. However, because I have the reputation of having taken the lead in recommending him to everyone in the village, I cannot now, for the sake of responsibility, confess that this Sendenshi was a fraud, and I have been truly troubled. But just before, when I went as a messenger to the house of Matsutakahiko, I heard a Senden-ka being sung in the inner room by what sounded like hundreds of people. The terror of that voice was truly endowed with infinite power. I have lost my affection for the Brahman religion and wish to convert to the Sangyō-kyō, but I would be in trouble if I were to have such head-splitting Senden-ka sung at me. What should I do?'

と敵やら味方やら訳の分らぬ歌を謡ひ首をすくめ、糞垂れ腰になつて、左右の手を胸の四辺にかまきりがすくんだ様な手付し、ピリピリ慄ひ乍ら左右の足を一所にキチンと合せ待つて居る。その可笑しさに友彦も、跟いて来た田吾作も、思はず声を上げて笑ひ転けたり。
