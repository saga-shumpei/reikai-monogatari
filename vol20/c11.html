Chapter 11: Ogre 婆 [673]

鬼婆〔673〕

§ 1

The night gradually broke, and escorted by the voices of the Morotori chirping among the trees, the Sannin proceeded, leaving their feet to lead the way. Against the background of a Daigankutsu, thirty or forty houses with Kayabu Yane stood with their eaves aligned.

Tagosaku: 'Well, at last the great city of the Onibaba of Mikunigadake has come into view. It is a grand city, with a number of households beyond counting, some thirty-odd myriad, and a population of almost eight hundred myriad lies. Since our feet have become quite strange, surely there must be elevated Tetsudau, and the facilities for Jidōshiya and Denshiya must be perfectly complete in the city; I wonder if I should try riding one.'

Harahiko, shaking Tagosaku's shoulder,

Harahiko: 'Hey, Tagosaku-sama, from here on is what is Kanjin; why are you acting senile now? Pull yourself together. Is it not just thirty or forty straw huts leaning against the Gankutsu? When you say such mad things, I too begin to feel lonely.'

Tagosaku: 'Ahahaha, you are quite foolish here. I just advertised it in an exaggerated way to liven things up. It is a den of half-beast, half-ogre creatures that eat snakes and frogs. From now on, I shall refrain from foolish talk and proceed to act without words.'

Munehiko: 'Since you two are finally heading to the battlefield, I will be in trouble if you do not pull yourselves together. Also, you must never commit any violence, so please be cautious. If you raise your eyes or twist your mouths just because you have been struck on the head three or four times, you will not be able to fulfill this mission; in any case, keep the character for endurance in your hearts. The character for endurance is a heart beneath a blade. You must pass even beneath the enemy's blade with a sincere heart to make the enemy repent, so please keep this well in mind.'

Both Tagosaku and Harahiko answered 'Hai, hai' in low voices as they advanced. About two hundred men and women, old and young, formed a single village. And every one of the people here had become mute. It was because the Onibaba, Mukade-hime, had poisoned their tea to enforce a gag order instead of issuing one. It was just as if they had come to a land of mutes. Tagosaku, not knowing this situation in the slightest, jumped into one of the houses,

Tagosaku: 'Excuse me, I am asking for information, but which way should I go to reach the mansion of the Baba?'

From inside, four or five men and women ran out to the doorway in a sluggish manner, and with puzzled faces, all of them opened their mouths agape, saying 'Aaaa' like mutes. Tagosaku raised his voice,

§ 2

Tagosaku: 'I am asking you where the old woman's place is.'

The group, having had their divine Genreiki and Chiyōseiki destroyed, understood nothing of the matter and could only open their mouths and cry out, 'Ah, ah, ah, ah.'

Tagosaku: 'Excuse me, Sendenshi-sama, no matter what I say, they give no reply; they are just like mutes, only opening their mouths and saying, "Ah, ah, ah." Shall we go to the next house and try asking there?'

Munehiko: 'I leave it to you, so please, do go door-to-door so that I may be elected, and ask for their pure votes politely; since bowing requires no capital, I beg of you.'

Tagosaku: 'Even if you say it requires no capital, my head will ache if I keep bowing like that. Do not speak of things like voting; how can I endure it when I am suffering from a headache over someone else's election? No matter how hard I try, once the order for Kaisan is issued, it will truly be back to Mokuami.'

Munehiko: 'In any case, I leave it to you.'

Tagosaku: 'I understand. Please do not interfere with the election, thinking I am from the Zaiyatau. Hello, people of this house, where does the old woman live? Won't you tell me? I am certainly not a vote-beggar, so please rest assured and tell me.'

From inside the house, four or five men and women appeared at the gate with puzzled faces, only opening their mouths to say, 'Ah, ah, ah.' Thinking, "Ah, these ones are useless too," Tagosaku went on to the next. No matter where he went, he was met with the 'Ah' torture and could not grasp the situation at all. Finally, he finished the door-to-door survey of every single house without incident. However, he gained nothing, and the old woman's figure was nowhere to be found.

The three of them, having no other choice, looked for a convenient rock to sit on, sat down with a thud, and rested for a while. A woman holding a baby in her bosom, along with dozens of others, appeared before the three with curious looks, opening their mouths and repeating the sound, 'Ah, ah, ah, ah.'

Tagosaku: 'What a strange place this is. It seems that the area around Mikunigadake is a barbaric and uncivilized land, so they must have no language.'

As they were talking, a woman of excellent appearance appeared, bowed to the Sendenshi, and while also repeating, 'Ah, ah, ah,' pointed toward the northern valley and ran off. This woman was Otama, the biological mother of Tamateruhime. She had been kidnapped by the old woman's subordinates and brought into these deep mountains.

§ 3

The old woman's plan was to first successfully abduct Otama, the biological mother, and bring her here as a means to seize Tamateruhime. Since the three of them had never seen Otama's face, they did not even dream that such a secret lay hidden, and they set off quickly, following in Otama's tracks.

After proceeding along the valley to the left for about four or five chō, they found countless giant rocks, like upright walls, standing in rows in the valley. Beckoning with her hand, Otama slipped through a hole in a rocky cave and hid herself. The three of them followed after her, quickening their pace, and advanced about five or six ken into the cave. This was the second operational base on Onokorojima for Mukadehime, the wife of Onikumawake, who had entrenched herself at Onigajiyōzan. The meat of snakes, frogs, mountain crabs, and other beasts was well dried and hung in countless numbers inside the cave.

Tagosaku: 'Ah, I beg your pardon, is this the dwelling of the Ogre-hag of the Brahman religion?'

Old Woman: 'This is the dwelling of the Ogre-hag Mukadehime.'

Tagosaku: 'Ah, is that so? My apologies. What a magnificent mansion this is; with this, one would be safe even from wind, rain, thunder, lightning, and earthquakes. I would like to live in such a splendid mansion for at least half a day, if nothing else.'

Old Woman: 'Where are you from, and why have you brought two companions along with you?'

Tagosaku: 'Yes, the truth is, I have been appointed as a preliminary missionary for the Sangokyō, and today is my first campaign. As you see, I have brought along these two stingy fellows named Munehiko and Harahiko. They say they are very hungry, so please, spare them some dried frogs.'

Old Woman: 'Since you have come all this way, I shall treat you to these frogs, though they are precious. These frogs are the specialty of this mountain, known as Lord-frogs; they serve as medicine for insects and are a miraculous cure for all kinds of illnesses. They are somewhat different from the green frogs or dung frogs that go hopping about in the rice fields, so taste them with that in mind. You are a frog-leaping, earthworm-cutter, after all.'

Tagosaku: 'Hey, you are making a fool of me!'

Harahiko: 'Hey, hey, you fake missionary-san, it is troublesome if you say things like that, isn't it?'

Tagosaku: 'I asked you to be troubled. They say that long ago, when Takekuma invited the envoy from the Dragon Palace, he served a feast of centipedes, lizards, and slugs, don't they? It is fine as long as you eat them thinking they are flounder or dried squid. You are not a picky eater, but you are a frog-hater, so that is the problem. Hahaha!'

Baba: 'Do not stand there, all three of you; come, wash your feet and step up. Let us talk at leisure tonight. You said that you are a Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō on your Uhijin, did you not?'

Tagosaku: 'Yes, I said so; that is exactly the case.'

Baba: 'That is all the more Ketsukō. Those moss-covered Sendenshi who are half-baked and stubborn are difficult to bring to Kaishin. Since you are still fresh, you surely have not heard the full doctrine.'

Tagosaku: 'I have just left my hometown, but you say such strange things.'

Baba: 'Ha ha ha ha, you appear to be quite a Mugakushiya. By Kyō, I do not mean your hometown; I am asking what the essence of the Ananai-kyō is.'

Tagosaku: 'I was just feeling bewildered, so I do not understand the essence or anything else.'

Baba: 'Ah, I thought so, I thought so. If you understood the essence, it would be too Ahau for you to remain in the Ananai-kyō. It is Ketsukō that you do not understand the essence. Now, you shall perform the silent practice here for about a hundred days, and after that, you shall open the Kototama and become a Sendenshi of the Brahmanism. You must have seen many houses on the road here, did you not? They are all under the silent practice.'

Tagosaku: 'Does that not mean they will become unable to speak? They seem to be deaf.'

Baba: 'Being deaf is all the more Ketsukō. If you perform one more Aragiyō, you will end up unable to see as well. However, I have left their eyes alone, as it would be a Fujiyū, so I have looked at it with Ōme. There are those for whom a hundred days of practice is good, and those for whom ten days is enough. Once the Shiugefu is complete, I have arranged it so that they can speak and hear properly.'

Tagosaku: 'Baba-san, that is not a silent practice. They do not speak because they cannot, do they? If they had mouths that could speak and chose not to, or ears that could hear and chose not to, that might be a practice, but to be unable to speak or hear because they have no choice—is that not hardly a practice at all?'

Baba: 'You should not speak such Rikutsu; Rikutsu is forbidden on the path of Shinkau. As a human being, you should not rattle on with petty Rikutsu like a monster of a bell.'

Tagosaku: 'Hee.'

The man tilts his head.

Munehiko: 'I am a Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō. The man who was just called a Sendenshi is still an egg, so he does not know what he is saying.'

Baba: 'Ah, I thought as much. You are a person who concocts logic that lacks Mabeushi. When a Ningen attains Taigo and Tetsutei, they come to understand the Kuōdaimuhen Goitoku of the Kamisama, and they reach a state where they cannot say anything, remaining silent and coming to Kaishin. If you are a Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō, you must know those Wakarazuya called Aohiko, Murasakihime, Tsunehiko, Kamehiko, and Yoshikohime, must you not?'

Munehiko: 'No, no, we three are people from Uzuyamamura; we have visited the holy land only once and practiced for a while, but we have never met such people. Perhaps they are out somewhere on a mission of propagation.'

Baba: 'How about it? Why don't you quit the Ananai-kyō and become my disciple?'

Munehiko: 'I am grateful for the offer, but everyone sees their own sect as the best. I have come all this way thinking that I would like you to undergo Kaishin and submit to the Ananai-kyō. How about it? Why don't you listen to what I have to say, and then, upon that, decide to join the faith?'

Baba: 'Ah, no, no, who would be the person to join a foolish religion like the Ananai-kyō? Asking me to undergo Kaishin—what are you saying? I am an Onibaba with a soul as clear and free of dust as this; how could there be any need for Kaishin? It is you people who should be undergoing Kaishin.'

Munehiko claps his hands and begins to recite the Amatsu Norito. The Baba is startled and says,

Baba: 'Hey, hey, everyone, the Norito is fine, but if the frog meal I went to the trouble of preparing is not to your liking, you do not have to eat it; but at least, since it was offered to the Kamisama, please partake of the Omiki and the tea. Then you may recite the Norito at your leisure. I shall join you in the recitation as well.'

She tries to force them to stay.

Munehiko: 'As for our bento, we have bread here, so please just give us some tea.'

Baba: 'You like Omiki, don't you? I have some delicious sake brewed from wild yam. How about having a cup?'

Munehiko: 'No, tea is quite enough for us.'

Tagosaku: 'Baba-sama, let us suspend this debate for now and have some warm tea. Tonight, let us leisurely begin a battle of Kototama, neither side yielding to the other. And shall we agree that the one who loses will follow the other?'
