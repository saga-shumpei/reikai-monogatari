Chapter 7: 再生の歓 [669]

再生の歓〔669〕

§ 1

Matsutakahiko, leaning on his staff of goosefoot, concerned for the trail of Tagosaku and Oharu who had rushed out in a flurry, entrusted the house to the couple with unsteady steps and departed. Left behind, the couple was immersed in tears of both joy and astonishment.

Okatsu: 'Pray, Munehiko-sama, will you not grant me my leave?'

Munehiko: 'Is that truly what you desire, you?'

Okatsu: 'Why would I say something that is not in my heart? I have felt something for a moment, so I beg you to sever our ties as of today.'

Munehiko: 'Haha, I understand. You must have thought my father was someone more distinguished, and now that you know that old man is my parent, you have suddenly come to loathe him, have you not?'

Okatsu: 'No, no, how could that be? How could I ever come to loathe him? On the contrary, I have grown all the more fond of him.'

Munehiko: 'If that is so, then all the more reason for us to live together in marital harmony. I have only just met my father and have not even had time to rejoice, so how could I face my parent if my wife were to leave me? If it were until yesterday, I might have granted your request if it were unavoidable, but how could I do such a thing now? I wish you would perceive my heart, even if only a little.'

Okatsu: 'That is indeed so, but there are circumstances regarding this that I cannot bring myself to speak of.'

Munehiko: 'Between a husband and wife who should have no need for reserve or hesitation, there should be no secret that cannot be told. Come, let me hear that secret.'

Okatsu: 'If I were to tell you that secret, you would surely be astonished. This alone I cannot tell you, even if I were to die.'

Munehiko: 'Haha, so that is it. You must have made some secret contract with Tagosaku-sama behind my back, did you not? The way you and Tagosaku exchanged glances was quite suspicious.'

Okatsu: 'How can you say such heartless things? I would even cut open my belly to show you, for this body of mine is heavy with sin and must die eventually.'

No sooner had she said this than she drew a dagger from her bosom and attempted to plunge it through her thin garment of katabira. Munehiko, startled, gripped her hand firmly.

Munehiko: 'Wait, wait!'

Okatsu: 'No, no, please do not stop me. Let me die with dignity. I shall cut my belly, and in my final moments, I will speak one word to offer my apologies to the gods and to you.'

Munehiko: 'Did we not say we would share life and death, and did we not descend to the wretched appearance of pilgrims, wandering through mountains, fields, rivers, and seas, hand in hand, seeking our parents? You must be resentful because I have reunited with my parent. No, you must be disappointed and discouraged. The time for us to meet will surely come, so do not be so rash. Your husband is kneeling before you, see, like this.'

§ 2

He grasped Okatsu's arm with one hand, thrust his other hand before his eyes, and worshipped Okatsu with tears.

Okatsu: 'Aa, Motsutai. If you think of me to that extent, I shall stop from dying. Please be Anshin. In return, I wish to request a divorce Mudeuken from this moment on.'

Munehiko: 'If I do not grant a divorce, you say you will die; if I grant it, I cause Oyaji Shinpai. Aa, caught in the Shimegi of gratitude and love, can there be such a bitter thing in the world? Now, Okatsu, Dōzo, just for a while, please remain my Fūfu. I shall grant your wish and Rien later when the time comes.'

Okatsu: 'If that were a matter that could be waited for, why would I make such a request? Where could there be such a Fugafuri as a wife asking her husband for Rien? Anata must think of me as a Sazosazo Futekusa woman, but inside my heart, it is Senmanmuriyō, as if Yakigote were being applied.'

Munehiko: 'Aa, how could I escape this bitterness? The more you say you want a divorce, the more the Kusemono called love in my heart leaps out, and it is as if my body has fallen into Shōnetsujigoku.'

He heaved a deep sigh. A curtain of indescribable Hisan clouds descended between the couple. Just then, Matsutakahiko returned in high spirits.

Matsutakahiko: 'Ya, Munehiko, Okatsu, are you crying? Is there such a thing as a couple crying together at such a Medeta time? If you want to cry, you may cry to your heart's content slowly in the middle of the night. The village people are coming there now, so come, come, quickly cheer up.'

Munehiko: 'Oyaji-sama, I was so happy that tears of joy overflowed. Please do not worry.'

Matsutakahiko: 'Aa, that must be it, that must be it, it is no wonder. However, it seems Okatsu was also crying. Are your eyes not swollen? Now, now, Okatsu, it is unsightly, please do not cry.'

While stopping them, Matsutakahiko began to cry himself.

Munehiko: 'Oyaji-sama, it is a difficult thing to say, but my wife says she wants a divorce from this moment. That is why, in truth, the two of us were arguing.'

Matsutakahiko: 'Young people are helpless, aren't they? I have memories of that too. Since it is Tenka Gomen, please do your best with a quarrel that even dogs would not eat.'

Afterwards, they sobbed with tears of joy.

§ 3

At this moment, the Sendenshi of Ame no Maura, along with Tagosaku, Tomekō, and Oharu, came walking briskly together with some Satobito from Shigo.

Tagosaku: 'Excuse me, Grandfather, please rejoice; the Sendenshi of Maura certainly has a plum crest vividly appearing under his left arm. He is surely your eldest son, Matsu-sama, just as you said a moment ago. Isn't that right, Maura-sama?'

Maura: 'Yes.'

He said only this, and his reply was somehow restless.

Matsutakahiko: 'Excuse me, Maura-sama, I ask you a rude question, but where was your home country?'

Maura: 'Yes, I was born in Kumano, in the province of Ki.'

Matsutakahiko: 'Are your father and mother doing well?'

Maura: 'No, both my father and mother have gone missing, and as for my three siblings, I do not know what has become of them. Since we were separated when I was very small, I do not even know their faces, and I am all alone in the world with absolutely no relatives. The only thing I rely on is the Kamisama of infinite benevolence. I have dreams often, and I think my father looks very much like you. However, since this is just a dream, it cannot be relied upon. Please do not take it to heart.'

Matsutakahiko: 'You say that you have a plum blossom birthmark under your left arm; is that true? If it is true, please let me see it for a moment. As for my children, both brothers had the plum crest birthmark—the elder on the left and the younger on the right—which is a mysterious thing; I have heard that they are reincarnations of Kamisama. My one daughter had a mole like a triangular star above her navel.'

Maura: 'This is a strange thing to hear. Come, please examine it.'

He bared his skin. Matsutakahiko made his eyes shine and gazed at it intently.

Matsutakahiko: 'Indeed, he was my son beyond any doubt. Grateful, grateful; this is all the arrangement of Kamisama. If the old woman were alive, how much she would rejoice. I have done a pitiful thing. Day and night, she and I would remember our three children and weep, speak of them and weep; she spent her days weeping and weeping so pitifully, and in the end, she became desperate, fishing in the river and committing only the sin of taking life to console her troubled heart. Ah, how pitiful she was.'

Wrapped in the clouds of affection for his wife, he sank down and wept powerlessly on the spot.

Even if heaven and earth should change, the bond between parent and child shall never change. Parent and child are for one life, husband and wife for two lives. Parent and child, who cannot be severed even if one tries to cut them, have been separated for a long time in life. Meeting here is like the Udonge flower, the plum blossom of the spring that blooms. Do not scatter after opening, but bear fruit. Blooming and fragrant in seven layers, eight layers, nine layers, ten layers, and twenty layers, it is the auspicious sign of the teachings of Kami. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. May your Mitama be blessed, and the sins of the body of Munehiko and his wife, committed unknowingly, may the Ōmikami of Ananai-kyō look upon them with Nahohi and Minaho, and wash away all sins and impurities in the deep currents of the Ara-kawa river, and purify them. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. May your Mitama be blessed.

Unlike a Heukin man, he chanted seriously and danced seriously, limited to this day. The gathering, which had been on the verge of losing interest, suddenly became cheerful, and all changed their expressions, showing smiles like cherry blossoms blooming in the spring breeze.

Munehiko: 'Ah, Okatsu, I thought you were saying something suddenly that I could not understand, but ah, you were my younger sister. Why do you hold back? From the beginning, we did not violate the laws of heaven knowing we were brother and sister; since it was a transgression committed unknowingly, the Kami will surely forgive us. Please do not worry. However, now that it is known we are brother and sister, I shall grant you your leave as you wish.'

Matsutakahiko and Maura were struck with astonishment, wondering if it was a dream or reality, whether they were parent and child or brother and sister, looking at each other's eyes, stunned and only able to weep.

Ama no Maura stood up and chanted:

'Among the people born between heaven and earth, especially we, parent and child, manipulated by the Kami of destiny, parent and child were scattered in all directions, in the sky of a journey where we knew not where we were going. The parent, seeking the whereabouts of her child, exposed her body to wind and rain, and while shedding tears of maternal love, crossed mountains and rivers and traversed wildernesses. The heart of the parent, who wandered here and there forgetting the sorrows of her aged body to follow our tracks, is higher than the mountains and deeper than the sea, a noble blessing. We three brothers and sisters were like chicks separated from their parent, like a small abandoned boat on the shore with nowhere to turn, drifting here and there, abused by heartless people.'

'Enduring a hundred hardships, the sadness of searching morning and evening, without aim, for the whereabouts of my mother. If there are Kami in heaven and earth, we prayed to the Kami, wishing that they would clear away this sad feeling of ours and let us meet as soon as possible, even for a moment. Blessed by the awesome divine power, the unexpected reunion of parent and child today, how can I express it? Even the Kototama of gratitude to the Kami of heaven and earth is not enough. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara.
