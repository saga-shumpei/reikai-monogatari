Chapter 14: 慈訓 [1118]

慈訓〔1118〕

§ 1

In the Gankutsu of Takateruyama, guarded by wolves, five guests are sitting with their knees touching, lost in some conversation. As Kitateru no Kami is a master Kaji among the Sendenshi, he manufactures many elaborate Kikai whenever he has Kanka, and by forging chisels, hammers, Tsuruhashi, and hoes to pierce the Gankutsu, the Yata Ōhiroma has now been excavated countless times, becoming a Nankōfuraku Kinjiyōtetsupeki. Furthermore, hundreds and thousands of wolves, submissive to the Oni of Kitateru no Kami, protect his Mei as well as any Kahiinu, and have come to understand Jingo. In one Hitoma of the Gankutsu, the Ame no Mahito no Kami sits in the Jiyōza, and to his Migigaha sits Takenohime, with Yasudarahime, Ryūun, and the leader sitting slightly lower, all sipping hot water while lost in myths.

Ryūun: 'Kitateru no Kami-sama, I too was possessed by a wicked and lawless demon, and having piled up the sin of disloyalty against King Sagaren, my soul was on the verge of being cast into the land of the roots; yet, thanks to your divine and kind teachings, the dream of greed, anger, and ignorance has awakened, and you have finally made me a true human. Compared to the suffering I felt when I rode the tide of power and wielded authority, usurping the place of King Sagaren, the peace and joy of today are as different as heaven and earth. It is surely not only Ryūun who, driven by the desires of the body-master and spirit-servant, has unknowingly cast his body and soul into hell. I wish to somehow awaken them from their delusions and bring peace to their bodies and souls.'

Kitateru: 'Since you have toured the land of the moon, you must already understand the human sentiments of the world well. There must be a great deal in this world that is worthy of pity, is there not?'

Ryūun: 'Yes, as you say, no matter which country I visit, the miserable state of people grinding their teeth against one another, entangled in religious strife and the desire for fame and profit, is the very appearance of the hell of hungry ghosts and beasts. The prototype of Ryūun in the former Ceylon Island can be seen scattered everywhere. However, strangely enough, in regions where the San-iku-kyō has even the slightest influence, I witnessed that the hearts of the people are extremely calm, their desires few and their minds indifferent to fame, with superiors and inferiors loving one another, and a small heaven being built; this was the most delightful thing I felt.'

Kitateru: 'Among the seven thousand and more countries you have traveled, which ones did you think were relatively difficult to govern?'

Ryūun: 'Yes, there are so many that I cannot mention them one by one. However, first of all, the country of Karumata, and second, the country of Iruna, seem to be on the verge of a great upheaval. The country of Karumata has Mount Chikyō in the northeast, and on Mount Chikyō, the divine pillar of the San-iku-kyō protects the path of truth and educates the nearby people. There, Tsunoyami-hiko of the Ural-kyō has appeared and established a base, plotting to swallow Mount Chikyō if there is any opening. Recently, because the power of the Ural-kyō is so great, frequent reports have arrived that Daikokunushi of the capital of Haruna is sending General Ōashiwake to lead many troops to attack, and the hearts of the people are in a state of great anxiety. Next, in the country of Iruna, the jealousy and antagonism toward King Seran increase day by day, and the righteous faction and the unrighteous faction are constantly engaged in secret strife; it is rumored among the people of the castle town that the Minister of Right Protection is planning to rely on the power of Daikokunushi to banish the King of Iruna and take his place, and it is said that the capital of Iruna may turn into a scene of war at any moment. Wishing to prevent this tragedy before it happens, I, Ryūun, wandered through the capital and sang songs of propaganda; when the Kārutin of the Right Protection was being pressured by his subordinates and was about to have his life taken, strangely, a pack of wolves appeared in broad daylight from nowhere, roaring and shouting to scatter the enemies, and then hid their figures like smoke. Watching for that opportunity, I fled the capital at full speed, and while I was returning, crossing the Teruyama Pass, I met Yasudarahime-sama and her retinue by the side of the Hasukawa River, saved the two of you from danger, and while following and leading, appearing and disappearing, I escorted you to the foot of the Teruyama Pass, where I met the mother and daughter of Koganehime-sama of the San-iku-kyō, and upon hearing the words that I must go to Takateruyama immediately because it is the order of Kitateruhiko no Kami, I accompanied the princess here without a moment's delay. Truly, the world has become extremely dangerous.'

'Indeed, that is a gokurau. As this mansion is guarded by a multitude of the kenzoku of maujiu, it is the safest chitai in all tenka. Yasudarahime-dono, pray be at goanshin.'

'Hai, arigatō gozarimasu. I am overwhelmed with awe and know not how to express my gratitude for your saving me, a woman who has strayed from the path, without even a word of reproach. I humbly beseech your continued gokyōyō from this day forward.'

'Zuibun, Yasudarahime-sama, you too have suffered as a gisei to the yokubau of akunin. You were wed to a husband with whom your mitama did not harmonize, and you must have felt fuyukuwai in your goshinchiu day after nichinichi. I truly sympathize with your feelings.'

At these compassionate kotoba, Yasudarahime felt a sense of relief, and while wiping tears of joy with her sleeve, she spoke:

'Arigatō gozarimasu for your unexpected and goshinsetsu okotoba. I shall hide nothing; I was born as the eldest daughter of Kurinsu, the samori of the capital of Iruna, and was the betrothed of King Sēran. However, I was thwarted by Karuchin, the migimori who fawns upon the Daikokunushi of the capital of Haruna, and after being subjected to various false accusations, I was made the wife of a Bisha of the land of Teruman, and have spent these days in misery. As you, noble deity, have said, perhaps our mitama do not harmonize, for not a single feeling of love has arisen in me for my husband Shiaru, and he too is extremely cold toward me; like strangers meeting on the roadside, I have never tasted the warmth of a couple even in my dreams. Although it is the way of a wife to offer love to her husband, for some reason, that heart does not well up within me. It is a shameful thing to admit, but day and night, the image of my betrothed, King Sēran, flickers before my eyes, his voice echoes in my ears, and I have continued to pile sin upon my heart by longing for the King in both dreams and reality. At that time, Tēnahime, the wife of the migimori, appeared at my husband's mansion as a messenger of the migimori, cast unreasonable demands upon me, intimidated my husband Shiaru, and finally built a prison to throw me into, inflicting terrible abuse upon me. I had resigned myself to the fact that my fate had reached its end, and just as I was drowning in tears, on a night of fierce wind and rain, this loyal servant Ridā broke down the prison, carried me on his back, and managed to bring me here, hidden by the darkness. This is entirely thanks to the grace of the deities and the protection of Ryūun-dono. I no longer have any desires in this world, but I wish to meet my father Kurinsu and my younger sister Sērisuhime at least once. Also, if it be possible, I wish to behold the figure of the King even for a single glance. If only that could be done, I would have no resentment even if I were to die now.'

With this tale of her life, she shed tears and bowed her head.

'I have now understood the whole situation. Before long, not to mention your father and siblings, I shall let you meet King Sēran. And since you are truly a couple whose mitama harmonize, I shall protect you so that you may become a couple in the flesh as well, and be celebrated as the flower of the capital of Iruna. This arrangement is already being carried out here under the divine shinto, so pray be at anshin.'

'Could such a wonderful thing truly come to pass? If such a thing were possible, I cannot say how happy I, my father, and my siblings would be. The King would surely be most satisfied as well.'

Takeno: 'Yasudarahime-sama, you too must undergo a great deal of gokurau for the sake of the deities from now on. When I was young, I too was separated from my parents and spent lonely days, but hearing through the wind that my father was in Takasagojima, my three sisters and I endured various hardships and sufferings. Just as we left the capital of Chin and were proceeding toward the riverbank of Eden, we were surrounded by evildoers. While we were in dire straits, a servant of a mansion called Teruhiko, who is an incarnation of Tsukiteruhiko-sama, chased after us and saved us from our crisis. From there, the four of us, master and servant, visited the capital of Chin where my father resided. No sooner had I thought, "How happy I am," than I was guided by the deity Chin-yamahiko, an incarnation of Konohana-hime-no-Mikoto, and left behind the capital of my beloved father. In the land of Teru, I parted from Teruhiko, and from there I boarded a ship and landed at the port of Ataru. The three sisters separated to spread the teachings in the lands of Hiru and Karu, and while being chased by the metsuke of Takayoribake, a demon of the Ural religion, I was hidden in the mansion of the compassionate Haruyamahiko and finally escaped the crisis. I participated in the battle of Yomotsuhirasaka, but I encountered various trials from the deities. From there, I crossed to Ajiya, and while traveling from place to place to spread the teachings, I held a wedding ceremony with the deity of the Northern Light at Mount Caucasus through the matchmaking of the Great Deity Kamususanō-Ōkami. Since then, we have not lived together as a couple for a long time, continuing our activities for the sake of the Way, and only recently have we finally been able to work together like this as a couple. My husband has now grown old, and I have ended up as such an old woman. Ohohoho!'

She hides her tears and masks them with a smile.

Yasudarahime, moved by the words of Takenohime and ashamed that her own hardships were insufficient, blushes and speaks with trepidation:

Yasudarahime: 'Is that so? It is truly no easy matter for a human being to pass through a lifetime. When I think of you, I have no words to speak. I, who lacked even a little patience and escaped from my husband's prison, returning shamelessly to seek my parents, siblings, and the husband I long for—my heart is so impure and shameful that I am filled with deep embarrassment.'

Takenohime: 'Yasudarahime-sama, do not worry. From now on, you shall act for the sake of the Shinkai. A human life is like climbing a steep mountain path while carrying a heavy burden. One never knows when one might encounter a dangerous situation or fall. It is through the Goshinriki of the Kamisama that one is saved and can cross the rough seas of this world with ease. While protecting yourself, you must teach and guide the Banmin, who are the precious Miko of the Kamisama, in the path of truth, save them for the heavenly realm, and grant them Anshin-Ritsumei in both spirit and body; this is the mission of you who have been chosen by the Kamisama, so no matter what hardships or sufferings you encounter, you must never be discouraged or resentful. In this world, not even a single leaf falls except by the will of the Kamisama. Since everything is controlled according to the divine will of the Kamisama, you must leave all things to Kannagara, no matter what may come to pass, and humans need only offer their best efforts as humans. Even this Ryūun-sama, at first, harbored quite selfish thoughts and had his days of triumph, but his dream was suddenly shattered, and he fell into a valley of a thousand fathoms, ending up as a wretched beggar; yet here, he was struck by a sudden realization of the truth of heaven and earth, traveled through many lands, and has now become a perfect Kamitsukasa, having attained Goshinriki within himself. Therefore, no matter what, a person cannot become a true Kamibashira without undergoing hardship. When this Hokkō no Kami forges the sword of Toyukari, he throws iron and steel into the raging fire, places them on an anvil, and hammers and stretches them repeatedly with a mallet, finally forging them into a brilliant, radiant masterwork; humans, too, are useless without undergoing the tempering of the Kamisama. Just as a sword that has been struck many times has a better cutting edge and a more beautiful luster, a human must also be struck and tormented sufficiently, and pass through water and fire to be of any use. From now on, this Hokkō no Kami will have you meet King Seran, whom you love, but you must never let your guard down. Like us, a husband and wife, you must work together and devote yourselves to the path of truth. As long as you are young, in this time of Tenka-jōran, you cannot simply enjoy the comforts of married life. Shoja-hitsumetsu, Esha-jōri—the pain of separation is something that not only humans but all things cannot escape; I ask that you fully understand this point. Before long, King Seran will arrive here, guarded by two or three loyal servants.'

Yasudarahime: 'Yes, thank you very much. I shall engrave your kind teachings, from beginning to end, deep into my heart and never forget them. Nay, I shall obey the commands of the Kamisama in all things and am prepared to be tempered into a magnificent Kamibashira.'

Takenohime: 'Hearing the words of Yasudarahime-sama, tears well up in my eyes. Since hearing your courageous words, the heart of Takenohime also shines.'

Yasudarahime: 'Grateful am I to Hokkō-shin and Takenohime, I shall serve the path according to your words. Hearing that King Seran-sama is coming here now, my heart thunders. Compared to the heart I shall have after we meet, how happy I am even now.'

Hokkō: 'King Seran-sama is now visiting the dwelling of Hokkō-shin here. Guided by the hand of the Kannagara-kami, go through the valley of the Imose mountains.'
