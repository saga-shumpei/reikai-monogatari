三番叟〔1120〕

§ 1

Kitateru no Kami, leading Takenohime, Riūn, Thames, Leader, and others, tactfully withdrew into another room. Left behind, King Seran and Yasudarahime maintained a curtain of silence for a while. Yasudarahime, exerting Kongauriki to calm the beating of her heart, let a flush of Momiji spread across her face, and in an Obotsuka tone, said,

Yasudarahime: 'King Seran, it has been a long time. I am overjoyed to see your Gosauken face.'

Having said only this, she hung her head in embarrassment, hiding her face. King Seran, blinking his eyes, said,

King Seran: 'You must have endured such painful thoughts as well. I, too, have gazed at the sky over the land of Telman and sent my thoughts with the migrating geese more times than I can count. My Magokoro must have reached your Seirei.'

Yasudarahime: 'Yes, there is not a single Hitoyo that I do not see the King in my Oyume. Meeting you here today feels like a dream. I wonder if we are both dreaming. If it is a dream, I wish it would never wake, no matter where it leads.'

King Seran: 'It is certainly no dream; it must be Genjitsu. Yet, the space between us has been more Haka than a dream. I am currently at a loss, having received various understandings from Kitateru no Kami-sama, and am pondering what I should do from here on.'

Yasudarahime: 'Would it not matter even if we broke the heavenly laws? If I could satisfy my instincts for even one minute, I would not care if I died or rotted away. Even if the two of us were cast down to the Root-land or the Bottom-land, if I am holding hands with you, would that not be enough?'

When it comes to the critical moment, she is a bold woman. Yasudarahime has already forgotten all of the Kami's teachings, and in a desperate state, she is inciting and urging the King to make up his mind.

King Seran: 'Indeed, it is natural that you should feel that way. My heart, which thinks of you, is by no means inferior. However, to endure that is the duty of a human. Clouds over the moon, storms over the flowers; that things do not go as one wishes is the way of this floating world. Whatever happens is the providence of the Kami-sama. That we, a man and woman who thought we would never meet again in this life, have met and are speaking our hearts out even for half an hour is the deep compassion of the Kami-sama. Even if I can never meet you again in this life, I will never resent the Kami-sama or lament the world.'

Yasudarahime: 'Your love is truly tepid. Are you satisfied with that? Oh, how pathetic. I never dreamed your heart was like that. I tried everything to meet you, confided in each other about the seas and mountains, endured all sorts of hardships and obstacles, and even in the depths of the mountains where wolves and tigers howl, I was encouraged by a Yatake-gokoro to fulfill the true purpose of love as a couple. I crossed the dangerous wilderness and was saved here by the guidance of the Kami-sama while fleeing back to the capital of Iluna. Please do not say such weak things; show the great courage of Kongaufue and let us plan to achieve our goal. Since you have your beloved wife, Samarihime-sama, waiting for you, I suppose it cannot be helped. No, I, too, was lost. Your heart must no longer be the heart of the past. I have said something truly unforgivable. Please, live in marital harmony with Samarihime-sama for a long time. I shall go to what is called the Yūkai and watch over your life as a couple.'

So saying, she burst into tears and collapsed onto the King's lap. The King was suddenly bewildered, and fearing that the sound of her crying might reach the ears of Kitateru no Kami-sama, he stood up abruptly to push open the partition door to check if anyone was around. Yasudarahime, misinterpreting this as the King abandoning her to flee, used all her strength to pull the King's hand back sharply. The moment he was unexpectedly pulled by the Hime, the King stumbled back two or three steps, tripped over the Hime's lap, fell flat on the spot, struck his head against the rock wall, let out a single groan, and fell unconscious.

'Ah, what shall I do? What shall I do?'

She ran about the Shitsunai like one in a state of Kiyōki, placed her hand upon the head of the Ōsama, and said:

Yasudarahime: 'Moshi, Ōsama, please forgive me. I never intended to kill you. It is an accident. I shall never kill you alone. I, too, shall surely follow after you.'

While saying this, she smoothly drew the Kuwaiken from its scabbard, gazed at it intently, and said:

Yasudarahime: 'Knowing that this fleeting Ukiyo is but a dream, I never thought I would face such grief. As I think of meeting you, my beloved, does this become a parting in this world, weeping and crying? Sorrow fills my small chest, and I am one who cannot even shed tears. Forgive me, Kamuzukasa of King Seran; soon I too shall serve as your Mitomo. Do not despise me, O Inochi of the Kitateru Kami, for the baseness of Yasudarahime’s heart.'

As she said this and was about to thrust it into her own throat, the Leader, who had been standing in the Kogai watching the Yōsu, rushed in frantically, snatched the Kuwaiken from the Hime, raised his voice, and said:

Leader: 'Yasudarahime-dono, have you been seized by Kiyōki? In such a Shinsei Reijiyō, what is this talk of Murishinjiu? Do you not understand that it is a Daizai of Tensokuihan? Not knowing that such was your Okokoro, I protected your Onmi, escaped the Terman country at the risk of my Inochi, and while accompanying you here, having finally crossed the wilderness where wild beasts roar, what a pathetic heart you have to kill the Ōsama and commit suicide yourself here! You must have been possessed by the Yamata no Orochi or a golden-furred nine-tailed evil fox to have such an evil heart. Now that it has come to this, I, this Leader, will not stand for it. I must avenge the Ōsama.'

He said this as if scolding her, his voice trembling. The Ōsama groaned, 'Un, un,' and sat up holding his head, and said:

Ōsama: 'Ah, Yasudarahime, were you there? Why are you crying? Hey, who are you? Do you intend to threaten the Hime with a weapon? You insolent fool, I will not forgive you. Do not move from there.'

As he raised his voice and glared, the Leader, in his confusion between the joy of the Ōsama’s revival and the fear of being misunderstood, said:

Leader: 'Me, that is preposterous. Why would I kill the Hime-sama whom I have accompanied all this way? If you misunderstand me like that, I, this Leader, have no standing. I thought the Hime-sama had gone into a state of Kiyōki and was going to kill you, Anata-sama, and commit suicide herself, so I rushed in, just now snatched the short sword from the Hime-sama, and was offering my opinion.'

Yasudarahime: 'Ōsama, how happy I am that you have regained consciousness. This Leader is by no means a bad person. Please, I beg you to forgive him.'

Ōsama: 'Ah, was that so? I am truly sorry. Leader, it was a complete misunderstanding, so please forgive me.'

Leader: 'Hai, thank you. If you understand, there is nothing more fortunate than this.'

Ōsama: 'It would be terrible if this commotion were known to the Kitateru no Kami, but I wonder if they might already know.'

Leader: 'He-he, they know everything. I think that the Kitateru no Kami, Takenohime-sama, and Ryūun-sama are listening to the conversation of you two in the next room... though I do not say that for certain.'

Ōsama: 'Eavesdropping is the height of immorality. Why would such Kami-bito do such a thing? Yasudarahime, you should be at ease.'

Yasudarahime: 'The Kitateru no Kami are Ikigami who have attained the power of Tennantsūriki; no matter how far away they may be, they see us as if it were in their own hands. Also, they must be hearing our words without missing a single syllable with their expert Tennijitsū. Ah, this has become a shameful matter.'

Ōsama: 'If you know of the Tennijitsū and Tennantsūriki of the Kitateru no Kami, why did you say such a bold thing?'
