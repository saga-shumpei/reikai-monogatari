§ 1

In the inner chamber of Iruna Castle, the three, Ōgonhime, Kiyoteruhime, and Serisuhime, are huddled together, engaged in a lively and clamorous debate.

Serisuhime: 'My, Kiyoteruhime-sama, how beautiful you are; you are the very image of Yasudarahime-sama. Even though you have never seen her face, you have managed to create such a likeness.'

Kiyoteruhime: 'I met her at the foot of Teruyamatauge. Since I created you by keeping that face in my memory, it is as if you were captured in a photograph. As this is an age where women are asserting themselves in all things, Kiyoteruhime has somehow ended up becoming the new Yasudarahime-sama. Ohohohoho.'

Serisuhime: 'But does Ananai-kyō not say that a new world is being, or has been, constructed?'

Ōgonhime answers with a knowing look.

Ōgonhime: 'A new heaven and a new earth shall now appear through the divine power of Ananai-kyō. The heaven and earth of until now have already passed away this very day. The time has approached when the new Jerusalem, which is the Holy City, shall descend to the earth, and Kuniharutachinomikoto shall descend to cause all the people under heaven to be reborn anew. The city of Jerusalem is four-square, and its length and breadth are the same. It is said that Konohanahimenomikoto-sama sent Izumohimenomikoto from Tenkyōzan to measure the city of Jerusalem with a rod, and it measured twelve thousand furlongs. The length, breadth, and height of the city are all equal, and its walls are one hundred and forty-four cubits; the walls are built of jasper, the city is made of pure gold like clear glass, and the foundations of the city walls are adorned with all manner of precious stones. The twelve gates are made of twelve pearls, and the buildings, all made of transparent gold, are so dazzling that they blind the eyes.'

Serisuhime is astonished and says,

Serisuhime: 'For a new heaven and a new earth to appear—is that not a tremendous matter? One must say that natural disasters and celestial upheavals have reached their limit here.'

Ōgonhime: 'The new heaven and earth refer to a new church; in short, it points to the fact that the deities Haniyasuhiko and Haniyasuhime have opened the dojo of Ananai-kyō.'

Serisuhime: 'What on earth does the Jerusalem city descending from heaven refer to?'

Ōgonhime: 'It refers to the sincerity of heaven and earth, and the teachings of Ananai-kyō, as shown by the Savior God, Haniyasuhiko-no-kami.'

Serisuhime: 'What is the meaning of your saying that its length, breadth, and height are equal, each being twelve thousand furlongs?'

Ōgonhime: 'It is an expression combining the truth, goodness, and beauty within the teachings of Ananai-kyō. Furthermore, the walls of the city refer to the ministers of God who protect and propagate this teaching. That it is one hundred and forty-four cubits is a praise that enumerates all three aspects of truth, goodness, and beauty of Ananai-kyō, and it speaks of the pure nature of those who are the missionaries. Also, the twelve gates made of pearls refer to the truth of the path of ability. The foundations of the walls made of precious stones refer to the various knowledge that stands upon hearing those sermons. The gold resembling clear, transparent glass of which the city is built refers to the virtue of supreme benevolence and supreme love. For the teachings and their truth, goodness, and beauty become increasingly transparent through the power of love, you see.'

Serisuhime: 'If that is the case, should the divine oracle of Ananai-kyō which says "Heaven and earth shall be turned upside down" also be interpreted in the same manner?'

Ōgonhime: 'Among the missionaries and leaders of Ananai-kyō, there are those who still think that heaven and earth will realistically overturn, and there are those who think that the twelve gates of pearl will realistically be built on the sacred ground of the divine plan, and that is why it is troublesome. Serisuhime-sama, you likely think so as well, do you not?'

Serisuhime: 'Hey, hey, I had indeed thought that a magnificent palace would realistically be built and a castle would be constructed.'

The Umori no Tsukasa glares with wide eyes at Kiyoteruhime. The mammoth is trembling like a grandchild of jishin. Princess Seris and Yufutesu remain heizen and continue in chinmoku. It is Samarihime who comes running soko.

Umori no Tsukasa: 'Sonata began a fūfugenkuwa some time ago, and since the wakai has not yet been achieved, it would be best for you to return to my mansion and wait until the matter is settled.'

Yasudarahime: 'Ya, how rare, you are Samarihime-dono; I am Yasudarahime, who was driven away to the land of Teruman and could not even stay with my ihinazuke husband because of you. The time to clear my higoro grudge is now, at this moment; you have appeared in a good place. Now, kakugo!'

She then crossed her tasuki and struck a pose. Samarihime is startled, clings to the waist of Karuchin, and while trembling, says:

Samarihime: 'Moshi-moshi, Father, what shall I do? Please help me.'

Umori no Tsukasa: 'Un, wait a moment; I will bind Yasudarahime and remove the obstacle for you.'

Saying this, he takes a whistle from his pocket and blows it with a shrill sound; immediately, more than ten captors appear in this place, scattering about, and as they rush toward Kiyoteruhime, Kiyoteruhime spreads her arms and stamps her feet, saying:

Kiyoteruhime: 'Iya, omoshiro, omoshiro! Now is the time for Yasudarahime to manifest her bravery; I shall punish these petty warriors, leaving not one remaining. Sa, come, come!'

She takes a stance. At the imposing demeanor of the beautiful woman, the captors are bewildered and, without laying a hand on her, surround her from a distance. From the next room, separated by a door, comes the voice of King Seran:

King Seran: 'Aiya, Umori no Tsukasa, I am King Seran. Samarihime, be quiet. If you offer resistance toward Yasudarahime, I shall no longer forgive you. Samarihime, if you do not heed my words, I shall sever the bond of marriage as of this moment. Is that acceptable?'

That he shouted this is, needless to say, the feigned voice of Koganehime, who was hiding in the adjacent room. To Karuchin, the voice sounds a bit too aged for the King. However, he concludes that his body must be weakened by illness and his voice is trembling, so he suddenly softens his tone:

Karuchin: 'I am truly sorry for causing you worry while you are ill. I humbly ask for your forgiveness.'

Samarihime: 'Ōsama, please forgive me.'

She sobs. Yasudarahime says:

Yasudarahime: 'Ōsama, I am Yasudarahime, your ihinazuke wife who has come all the way from the land of Teruman, longing for you. Please sever your bond with Samarihime and make me your wife. And please, let me see your noble face just once.'

She intentionally sheds tears and looks down.

From the next room, again in the feigned voice of the King:

King Seran: 'The great leader of Brahmanism, Ōkuninushi-sama, is a proponent of polygamy. Since he has shown us the example by driving away his first wife and taking the second, Ishinōhime, as his principal wife, there is nothing to hesitate about. Make Samarihime your principal wife as before, and I shall use Yasudarahime as your second wife to supervise the upper maids, so be at ease. Umori no Tsukasa, you have no objection to this, do you?'

Umori no Tsukasa: 'Hai, your command is logically clear; I shall never object. Will you love Samarihime as your principal wife to the end?'
