I feel it is necessary to state a few words here regarding Ninjiyutsu.

There are some who think that Ninjiyutsu is a form of magic where one can appear and disappear at will, vanishing like smoke before an opponent's eyes and cleverly concealing one's tracks, but Ninjiyutsu is by no means such a mysterious thing. Ninjiyutsu means the art of endurance; when spying on the enemy's situation, it requires considerable training to be able to remain in an attic for a week or ten days without food or drink, without coughing, holding one's breath while observing the situation, or regardless of the heat or cold, to submerge one's body in water until the objective is achieved—even for ten or twenty days—breathing with only the nose and eyes above the water surface, covering one's face with algae to observe the enemy, or to move across a wide pond without creating waves or making a sound in the water. Furthermore, one must be able to perform high-speed travel on foot for over a hundred ri in a single night. The way of walking is to keep the left shoulder forward, moving through the road while minimizing exposure to the air, and when walking sideways like a crab, one can walk three or four times the distance without making a sound. The goal of Ninjiyutsu is to achieve a certain objective at a location in the early evening and to have arrived at and settled into a location a hundred ri away within the same night. Also, those who use Ninjiyutsu keep various cloths of black, white, blue, red, and other colors hidden in their robes; when standing before a white wall, they bring out a white cloth to hide their body, when standing near a black object they cover themselves with a black cloth, and in a blue place they bring out a blue cloth to cover themselves, considering this deception of human eyes to be the secret principle of Ninjiyutsu. In short, they act like a chameleon changing its color according to the surrounding plants and trees. They also wear black attire from top to bottom and carry a towel about four or five shaku long, which they use as a hood or apply as a rope to descend from high places. They also carry a single, extremely sturdy short sword about one shaku long called a Yorōtōshi, which is unsigned. They use an unsigned sword out of caution so that if it is lost by mistake, the owner cannot be identified, and they carry no personal items with any markings. They tie a long, sturdy cord of three or four ken to the short sword, and when crossing walls or the like, they tie a suitable stone or weight to the end of the cord, throw it over the branch of a garden tree, fasten the rope, stand the short sword on the ground, place one foot on it, use the cord to leverage their body to jump up onto the wall, retrieve the short sword by pulling the cord, and then use it to slide down from the tree branch as if it were easily untied, creeping into the room.

They keep two or three gō of water in a leather bag, pour it gently onto the threshold, and creep into the dark night without making a sound when opening the door, which was the duty of a Ninjiyutsu-tsukai. When they creep into the enemy's bedroom, they approach from the head side. This is because they fear that if they approach from the feet, the enemy might wake up and recognize their figure the moment they rise. Approaching from the head side is most convenient for cutting down the enemy from behind as they wake up in surprise. They also carefully observe the state of the room and strive to hide behind folding screens or lanterns. Before entering the room, to test whether the enemy is fast asleep, they keep two mice they have tamed in their robes, and first release one into the room. When a mouse goes into an unfamiliar house, it becomes flustered and runs around the room making a clattering noise. If the enemy wakes up at the sound of the mouse, they are not yet fast asleep. In their sleepiness, the opponent might scold the mouse, saying 'shoo, shoo,' and then fall back into a deep sleep. When the Ninjiyutsu-tsukai peeks through a hole in the shōji, the mouse sees their face and returns to their robes. This time, just to be sure, they release the second mouse into the room, and again the mouse becomes flustered and runs around with a rustling sound. If the enemy still sleeps without noticing, the Ninjiyutsu-tsukai can then safely achieve their objective.

Kūrinsu had an audience with King Sēran, and after engaging in a secret consultation to prepare for the conspiracy of Kāruchin, the Minister of the Right, he finally returned home around the hour of the first watch, exhausted, and fell into a deep sleep. You were asked by Kāruchin to come and assassinate the Minister of the Left, were you not?'

Manmosu: 'I was never, never asked by Kāruchin. I just had a strange dream and flew in here without knowing it. I have no particular thoughts in mind, so please forgive me for my rudeness.'

Uirusu: 'Do not talk like a fool. Since you have come wearing such ninjutsu attire and prepared for every eventuality, it is no longer any use to hide it. Will you not confess?'

Saying this, he used Manmosu's short sword to tickle him around the chest. Manmosu, laughing and crying from the ticklishness and pain, said, 'Ahahaha, ihihihi, it hurts, it hurts! I will tell you, I will surely tell you, so please stop.'

Uirusu: 'There is no need to confess anymore; the evidence is clear. Rather, shall I tickle you a little more and make you laugh?'

He again poked and prodded him around the chest. Manmosu: 'Ahahaha, ihihihi, it hurts, it hurts, please stop. In truth, I was asked by Kāruchin-sama, the Minister of the Right. He said that the Minister of the Left is a thorn in his side that hinders his great ambition, and if I could finish him off with my specialty, ninjutsu, and return, he would make me the Minister of the Right when he becomes the King of Iruna. I was overcome by greed, and though I knew it was a bad thing, I thought it was the path to success, so I sneaked in. I will surely take heed next time, so please, I beg for your pardon.'

Uirusu: 'Danna-sama, how shall we deal with this?'

Minister of the Left: 'Hmm, leave it to me.'

He said this while glaring intensely at Manmosu's face, and continued, 'Though you are an unforgivable villain, I shall let you go today. Return to the mansion of the Minister of the Right as quickly as possible; Kūrinsu is becoming restless. If you do not repent quickly, calamity will befall you right before your eyes. Tell him well that he should repent while there is still time.'

As he untied the ropes, Manmosu escaped the crisis of a lifetime, and filled with joy, he hurried away into the darkness, fleeing at full speed from the back gate of the mansion.
