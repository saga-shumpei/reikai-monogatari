Chapter 1: 入那の Field 辺 [1105]

入那の野辺〔1105〕

§ 1

The autumn sky where the Kogarashi blows, / The grasses of the field, and the Kamuzukasa / Of the divine Mikoto Ōgonhime, / With Kiyoteruhimemikoto in the four directions, / Leading Rebu and Karu, / Proceed toward the Shōtokuwai of the land of Iruna, / The capital of night, where the God of the Amatsu sun resides in Seizan.

The curtain of darkness is lowered, / And the small birds of the forest chirp noisily; / The sound of their cries is lonely on the Tanbomichi, / As the four of them walk with eager hearts at their Ashimoto.

Suddenly, from the shade of the forest, / A black shadow appeared, / Seizing the mother and daughter, / And vanishing into the darkness, nowhere to be seen, / Moving like an arrow with hushed footsteps, / Instantly hiding its form.

Rebu and Karu, / From the moment the figures of the mother and daughter vanished, / Were struck with surprise and moved with stealthy steps, / Searching here and there with hushed voices, / Peering through the evening darkness, / Seeking them with tears of distress.

Rebu: 'Hey, Karu, this is troublesome. Having lost sight of the two of them in a place like this, how can we ever justify ourselves to Onikumawake-sama of the capital of Haruna? We must part the grasses to follow their trail, find their whereabouts, and return them to our master, must we not?'

Karu: 'That is just as you say. We cannot simply stand here with our hands tied and leave it to Kannagara. Even during our journey through the spirit world, Mikoto Imotohime-sama warned us sternly against falling into the trap of Kannagara-poisoning.'

Rebu: 'Hey, Karu, when I think about it carefully, Ōgonhime-sama and Kiyoteruhime-sama are beings of a divine rank many tens of stages above us, and they possess the direct divine power of the Kami, so I do not think there is much need for worry. However, we cannot simply leave them to their own devices, so let us exert our best efforts. That black shadow looked like a pack of wolves or lions, but if that is the case, we have nothing to fear. The two of them are being protected by wolves, so they are safe.' (Wolves and lions are all metaphors; they signify shadow warriors or the strong.)

Rebu: 'In any case, let us offer Norito to the Kami and pray for the safety of the two of them.'

Karu: 'Ah, that would be good.'

With that, the two knelt in the darkness of the forest shade, concentrating their prayers to the deities of heaven and earth, and decided to wait for the dawn of the night. From nowhere, a voice came from the air:

Voice: 'Rebu, Karu, there is no need for you to worry. Because of the convenience of the Kami, Ōgonhime and Kiyoteruhime are being used for divine tasks in the spirit world for two or three days, so you shall depart from here at dawn tomorrow and proceed to the capital of Iruna one step ahead. The mother and daughter will catch up later, so you two should spend the night here without worry.'

Hearing a voice like thunder, the two were convinced that this was truly the voice of heaven, a divine revelation. Rejoicing, they sat down at the roots of a great tree in the forest, spent the night talking about various matters, and let the flowers of aimless conversation bloom. Then, Rebu began to tease Karu.

Rebu: 'Hey, Karu, I have heard rumors, however faintly, that after your precious wife showered you with a barrage of elbow strikes, you were eventually robbed of her by that hunchback Semu in the neighborhood. What happened after that? It seems you just swallowed your tears, but that does not speak well of your manliness, Karu. Why did you not file a lawsuit for violation of chastity?'

Karu: 'How could a man possibly do such a thing? Is it not the woman who files a lawsuit for violation of chastity?'

Rebu: 'The era when only women could file lawsuits for violation of chastity is the era of the subordination of spirit to body in the twentieth century, more than three hundred thousand years from now. Today is the age of the gods, more than three hundred thousand years in the past from the twentieth century. Is there any reason why a man cannot file a lawsuit for violation of chastity? Are you lost in the laws of the future, forgetting the laws of the present, eh?'

Speaker: 'Even so, in Baramonkyō, there is such a thing as a woman's chastity, but I have never heard of a man's chastity.'

Speaker: 'In Baramonkyō, since the Kyōshiyu, Ōkuronushi-sama, follows the principle of polygamy, women are treated as if they were mere machines. It is because a woman must have the right to file a complaint for the violation of her chastity, or else she could not bear it. However, in Ananai-kyō, which upholds the path of monogamy, if a wife abandons her husband like your wife did, has an affair with another man, or blinds her husband, then the man has also had his chastity violated. It is only natural for the man to file a lawsuit against his unfaithful wife for the violation of his chastity. The idea that only women have the right to sue for the violation of chastity is a system that will be practiced in the world of the future, the twentieth century. But Baramonkyō seems to be a civilized and progressive religion, as all its rules and practices have been advancing for three hundred and fifty thousand years. Hahaha!'

Speaker: 'If that is the case, then why does Onigumohime-sama not file a lawsuit for the violation of her chastity? She has endured hardships and difficulties with her husband, Ōkuronushi-sama, for so long, built the foundation of the Baramon up to that point, and just when she thought she could finally rest, she was cast out by Ōkuronushi-sama, and a splendid young Ishiuohime-sama was taken in as his wife in her place. Even after being subjected to such cruel treatment in her old age, why does she not file a lawsuit for the violation of chastity?'

Speaker: 'That is the way of the world, where the strong prey upon the weak. There is no office higher than Ōkuronushi-sama, and no law to control him, so there is nothing to be done about this. Since Ōkuronushi holds the three great powers of judiciary, administration, and legislation, there is no one with the right to control or punish him except for Ōjizaiten-sama. When you think about it, those below are insignificant. Onigumohime-sama endured wind and rain, running east and west for the sake of the path, and finally built such a foundation, only to be cast out when she was just one step away—is that not too cruel? That is why they say Baramonkyō is a lawless religious group. If this were [a certain] religion, would it not be a catastrophe? The subordinate propagandists and believers would not stand for it.'

Speaker: 'Even so, is not the divine pillar of Ananai-kyō, Lord Susanoo-no-Mikoto, a polygamist? Were there not eight girls of similar age?'

Speaker: 'Lord Susanoo-no-Mikoto is the Great God of the Moon. Naturally, he has no wife. The eight maidens were not his children of the flesh. He gathered eight beautiful maidens of spirit from here and there, and by breathing his own strict breath upon their spirits, he made them his own children. It is different from how we create children in the dark as husband and wife.'

Speaker: 'In that case, those eight maidens must have had biological parents, right?'

Speaker: 'That they did, but since he picked up all eight maidens as abandoned children and raised them as his own, even though their parents are known to the Lord, the eight maidens seem to think of him as their true father. Since they have been granted the essential spirit, even if they are not children of the flesh, they become children closer and dearer than those of the flesh. We are also like the grandchildren of Lord Susanoo-no-Mikoto. Until now, I was a grandchild of Ōkuronushi, but this time I have finally become a grandchild of the Lord. You might even have become a great-grandchild of the Lord since yesterday.'

Speaker: 'Is that so? How grateful I am. Kannagara Tamachi Haese, Kannagara Tamachi Haese.'

Rebu and Karu were awakened by the voices of various birds beginning to chirp in the forest, and startled by the fact that it had become bright around them, they said:

Speaker: 'Oh, Karu, it is already dawn. We were so exhausted that we had a good sleep. Come, let us join forces and search for the whereabouts of the ladies.'
