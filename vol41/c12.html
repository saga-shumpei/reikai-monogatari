Chapter 12: Capital 入り [1116]

都入り〔1116〕

§ 1

Ōgonhime and Kiyoteruhime sent the party of three to Takateruyama, and feeling as though a heavy burden had been lifted from their shoulders, they climbed the steep and rugged slope with a rhythmic, labored gait. At last, they reached the summit. Here, a man of unparalleled disloyalty named Yūfutesu, who served as the chief retainer to the Umoritsukasa, had come up with a few of his household followers, having learned of the arrival of Ōgonhime and her daughter through a divine revelation, intending to serve as both guide and greeter. Yūfutesu approached them as they stood upon the mountain pass, gazing at the scenery in the four directions and catching their breath, and bowing his head respectfully, he spoke.

Yūfutesu: 'I beg your pardon for asking, but I am Yūfutesu, who serves as the chief retainer at the mansion of the Umoritsukasa of the capital of Iruna. If I am not mistaken, are you not the party of the Ananai-kyō missionaries, Ōgonhime-sama and Kiyoteruhime-sama? As the capital of Iruna is a land governed by the teachings of Brahmanism, you may perhaps think it strange that I, who serve the state, have come to welcome you, the missionaries of Ananai-kyō, but I have not come to welcome you with any impure heart. I humbly ask that you please identify yourselves.'

Ōgonhime: 'Ho, are you Yūfutesu-dono, who serves at the mansion of the Umoritsukasa of the land of Iruna? That is much appreciated. As you have surmised, I am Ōgonhime, and this is my daughter, Kiyoteruhime. How fares the King?'

Yūfutesu: 'Yes, thank you. At this very moment, he is safe, but we are on the brink of a crisis where, should a great storm pass through, even the famous castle of Iruna might be destroyed. Truly, dark clouds are gathering over the capital of Iruna, and as a torrential rain is about to fall, there is no man of truth to lock the windows against it. It goes without saying for the King, but even those men of truth who are eager for loyalty cannot sleep soundly at night, and their hearts are pained. The spies sent out by the Umoritsukasa are rampant, moving about in all directions, and the situation is such that one cannot even speak properly in a loud voice. I implore you to consider this and use your divine power to save the land of Iruna from its peril.'

Ōgonhime: 'Since this is a den of villains who seek to achieve their outrageous ambitions by employing the strategies of sowing discord and self-sacrifice, one cannot simply speak in a loud voice. Although we are on a mountain summit, the spirits of evil gods are still circling us from afar, so I cannot speak of complicated matters. Everything is within my heart, so please be at ease.'

Kiyoteruhime spoke gracefully.

Kiyoteruhime: 'So you were Yūfutesu-sama. Thank you for your trouble. Is there still a long way to go to the capital from here?'

Yūfutesu: 'Yes, it is less than ten ri from here, so if we hurry a little, we should be able to arrive by the hour of the horse tonight. It would be safer for you to enter the castle in the middle of the night.'

As they were talking, five horsemen came climbing up from below the slope, calling out, 'Oi, oi.' The three wondered what was happening, and while sitting on a stone by the pass, they intentionally exchanged trivial small talk. Yūfutesu was dancing while singing in a humorous manner.

Yūfutesu: 'Looking from the high mountain to the bottom of the valley, Pumpkins and eggplants are in full bloom. Though I say so, this is a lie, Now it is the end of the autumn of colored leaves, It has become the border of winter, The tips of the trees are scattering, The falling leaves are like rain, The autumn leaves of Takateruyama, Have shed their clothes and are stark naked, Old trees and young trees alike are shivering, How pitiful they tremble and shake, Though it is called Teruyama Pass, Beaten by the rain of leaves, Not one remains, truly stark naked, Teruyama Pass has suddenly, Become Nakiyama Pass, Dokkoisho, dokkoisho, dokkoisho.'

As he sang this, the five horsemen climbed up and, looking down at the three from above, began to speak.

'Who are you?'

As he shouted this, Eufetes deliberately feigned ignorance, cupping his hand to his ear and tilting his head.

'Hey, what are you saying? Are you asking if this downhill path is rough? It is indeed a very steep slope, sir.'

Kishi: 'It seems you are deaf. Well, it cannot be helped. I shall ask that woman there: did a beautiful woman of marriageable age and a manservant not pass by here just now?'

Ōgonhime put on a deliberately foolish face and said,

'Yes, just before this mountain pass, I met three incredibly beautiful women and one man. But the moment they saw me, they cursed me, calling me a filthy beggar, and climbed this slope at full speed. No matter how fallen a beggar may be, we are still human beings, are we not? There is no need to be so contemptuous.'

'What? Three women and one man? That does not make sense. Surely, only one woman and one man should have passed. Are you not telling me a lie?'

'If you think it is a lie, you may think so as you please. To this old woman's eyes, there were certainly three women and one man. And they were wonderful beauties, too. Tell me, where are you going and from where? You are riding such a magnificent horse; my, how strong you look.'

'Oh, Mother, the beautiful woman who just passed by seemed to be saying something like Yas or Dara.'

'What, did she say Yas? Then it must surely be Princess Yasudara. To cover her tracks, she must have hired some beggar woman somewhere. Hey, you women, well done. Come, everyone, let us give them a whip. This will surely satisfy Lord Shar.'

He attempted to proceed down the slope while still mounted on his horse. Eufetes said,

'Oh, excuse me, if you try to go down such a slope on horseback, that will be the end of you. If you do not value your life, go ahead and ride.'

'What, is such a steep slope as this a problem? We are a group of masters of horsemanship. How could this mission be accomplished if we were to dismount just because the road goes downhill? Come, let us go.'

Saying this, he tightened the reins and rode down, shouting, 'Hi, hi, hi!'

'Mother, the Lord God makes things work out well, does He not? A little more and Princess Yasudara would have been captured by that party. Oh, what a fortunate person she is.'

'Ah, that is true. That is why the divine power of the Lord God is so precious and never to be forgotten.'

Eufetes: 'Did you meet Princess Yasudara? Why would the Princess have come to such a place? Since she is married into the home of a wealthy man named Shar in the land of Telman, if she were returning, she should have had many attendants with her. Could some emergency have occurred?'

Ōgonhime: 'There is a reason for this. However, I have guided her to a cave on Mount Takateru, and since the wolves are guarding it, you need not worry.'

'What are you saying? To guide Princess Yasudara to the den of wolves on Mount Takateru, which people fear and avoid—did you essentially send her to her death?'

'Ohohoho, as a messenger of truth who saves people, how could such a thing happen? Even wolves are extremely docile if you approach them with sincerity. As for me, even though I am just standing here, if I clap my hands once, fifty or a hundred wolves will appear here immediately. Ohohoho.'

Eufetes turned pale, his legs trembling, and said,

'W-w-what are you saying? Do you command wolves?'

'Ohohoho, you are trembling greatly. I am of the party of the Wolf Crone and the Wolf Maiden, so if you ever grow tired of this world and wish to die, do not worry at all; be happy, for I shall have the wolves devour you.'

Oganemihime: 'How strange is the man who says he would cast away even his life for the sake of the Lord and the Way. There is also a weak man who loses his nerve, startled by the howling of wolves. O official of the Kami, Yūfutesu, strengthen your heart and rise up.'

Kiyoteruhime: 'My mother has faced Yūfutesu-tsukasa and uttered words of Kototama that are harsh. Yet, Yūfutesu-tsukasa, listen well, for this is a test of your spirit. A wicked official is hiding ahead, waiting to capture the three of us. My mother’s plan was to ensure your life would not be led astray by being startled at that time. Do not think ill of it, for it is because she wishes to temper your spirit. Since I am one who serves the Kami in accordance with Kannagara, how could I take the life of a person? Yūfutesu felt relieved after hearing the song of the two, put strength into his waist with a grunt, and stood up using his staff for support.

Yūfutesu: 'My courage has moved to some other land, and now I have become but an empty shell. Having lost my nerve and had my courage taken, how shall I walk the Way? Had I known you were such frightening persons, I would not have come all the way to welcome you. My legs and waist will not stand, though I hasten to flee, and I shed tears with no remedy in sight.'

Oganemihime stroked Yūfutesu's waist two or three times, recited the Amatsu-Norito, and chanted the Heavenly Counting Song two or three times. Wonder of wonders, Yūfutesu's waist suddenly became strong, the trembling of his legs ceased, and now, through the response of the divine spirit, a courageous heart that would not fear even a million enemies welled up from his lower abdomen. Yūfutesu realized Oganemihime's heart for the first time, bowed his head many times, pressed his palms together to thank her for her kindness, felt his energy increase a hundredfold, followed behind the two, and began to sing while keeping time with every step as they descended the steep slope.

§ 4

Umori no Tsukasa's Karlchin, having bitten sweetly into the throat of Tena Hime and climbed up like an eel to become a chief retainer, was served by Yufutesu, a child of the Karlchin Tsukasa's house. Serving with all his body and all his heart for his master morning and evening, Yufutesu's heart changed instantly upon the visit of Serisu Hime, the noble Mikoto whom he had adored morning and evening. While serving the Umori no Tsukasa with an outward appearance of loyalty, his heart was, as expected, double-faced. Appearing as the father of Serisu Hime, the Kamuzukasa, Samori no Tsukasa's Kurinsu, was deceived by the Umori no Tsukasa in private, as if to help him.

Knowing it to be a sacrifice of love, he came saying 'Untokosiyo'. 'Yatsutokodokkoi', how embarrassing. Hey, hey, Umori no Tsukasa-dono, do not be careless. Just as one descends this Sakamichi, one never knows where an Akuma might be lurking or when things might change in a flash. The human heart is unfathomable. Thinking of this, the most terrifying thing in the world is woman. With but one soul of a woman, even a Kokonmusau Gauketsu or Yufutesu, who was known as a Chishiya, instantly 'dokkoi' fell from grace. Truly, the path of love is terrifying. Though one says so, 'dokkoishiyo', it is too late now. To repent is 'dokkoishiyo', whether it is good or bad, 'untokosiyo'.

I have lost my bearings. If I think it over carefully, opposing Seran-ō, who has appeared as the pillar of the country, must surely be evil. If that is so, even if I, the Umori no Kamuzukasa, rebel, 'dokkoishiyo', the divine punishment of the Brahman Kami should not fall upon us. If I think of it that way, I am at ease. Hey, hey, you two, please watch your footing. Teruyama-tōge is the steepest Sakamichi in the whole country. It is a difficult pass that even a lion would not cross, known to the world as 'dokkoishiyo'. It is a place of distress where one cannot go even if one wishes. Without knowing the way, whipping the horse's belly haughtily, the five knights who came from Teruman-koku,

must by now have 'dokkoi' fallen into a thousand-fathom valley along with their horses, breaking their heads and arms, and must be groaning in pain. When I think of it, when I think of it, it is pitiful. Oh, Kami of the Brahman religion, they have no sin. From now on, when they come to oppose us, 'dokkoishiyo', do not help them, for I would be a little troubled. 'Untokodokkoi dokkoishiyo'. I hear that the five knights who just went by are hiding in this mountain pass at the words of Kogane Hime, waiting for us. 'Untokodokkoi', how impudent. If they do such a thing, Yufutesu, having received divine power,

will fire Kototama and strike them all down without leaving a single one, and send them on a journey to the land of the roots, 'untokodokkoi'. If they do not oppose us, please help them. Bonten, Taishakuten, Jizaiten, 'otsutodokkoi', before the presence of the Kami no Mikoto who establishes the governance of the land, I humbly and reverently pray.
