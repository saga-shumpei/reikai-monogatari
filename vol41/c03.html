§ 1

Having been met with an Itsukatsu from King Seran, Yūfutesu, the chief retainer of Karuchin, the Umori whose power in the castle was such that he could even bring down flying birds, fled back to the castle in a Shōzen state, and was now sitting in the inner room of his Jitaku with his arms folded, lost in Shian.

Just then, the Bantō Kōru came rushing in and said,

Kōru: 'Excuse me, Dannasama, a Sutekimetsupafukai Bijin has appeared at the Monzen, and she asked me to deliver this Tegami to you. She says she wants a Gohenji Sugusama. With a grin, he handed a letter to Yūfutesu.

Yūfutesu: 'Kōru, what Shitsurei things are you saying? You had better have some Kokoroe.'

Kōru: 'Hai, Shiyōchi. I shall keep it in mind, Kōru, hehehe.'

Laughing softly, he crouched and waited.

Yūfutesu opened the Shinshiyo Tebaya and examined it, and read:

'I am Princess Sērisu, who has received many kind letters from you, Anatasama. I have long wished to send you a reply, but no matter what I say, I have been separated by the barrier of human eyes, and I have endured until today, hiding my burning feelings; however, I am now so scorched by the flames of love that I can no longer stand or sit still. Therefore, though I know it may be a nuisance, I have come to pine for you, hiding from the eyes of others. I pray you, though it may be a nuisance, will you not let me see you once? I shall wait at the front gate until I receive a reply. I await a favorable reply as soon as possible. Anakashi.'

Upon seeing what was written, Yūfutesu suddenly loosened the knot of his grim face, and with a collapsed expression like a rice cake dropped in a sand field, while wiping away the saliva flowing from his mouth with the back of his right hand, he narrowed his eyes and spoke in a wheedling voice,

Yūfutesu: 'Oh, Kōru, you have done well to come as a messenger. Tell her that although Dannasama is very busy, he will set aside all obstacles and meet her for a while, so tell her to come in quickly.'

Kōru, being a little hard of hearing, held his right hand to his ear, listened to Yūfutesu's words, and with a look of bewilderment, said,

Kōru: 'Eh? What are you saying? You say you are busy and cannot meet her, and that she should come back another time? Is there any reason to turn away such a heavenly maiden who has descended from heaven so coldly? It is not the Omoto-kyō, but why don't you try to re-examine, re-hear, and re-proclaim, and meet her just once?'

Yūfutesu: 'Eh, there is no helping a man who is deaf. It would be terrible if I were to have my lover, who has come all this way, driven off because of a misunderstanding. It would be best to write a letter to avoid any mistakes.'

Yūfutesu took paper from his writing box, dipped his brush in ink, and while biting the tip of the brush and spitting out two or three drops of black saliva, he wrote something fluently, sealed it strictly, and said,

Yūfutesu: 'Oi, Kōru, since you are hard of hearing, I am worried there might be a mistake, so deliver this letter to that woman. Now, go quickly with this.'

He said this loudly, leaning close to his ear.

Kōru immediately understood and said,

Kōru: 'Hai, Shiyōchi.'

He puffed out his shoulders and ran toward the front gate, stepping like a sumo wrestler, and said,

Kōru: 'Hey, nice, aren't you a bit slow on the uptake? A white-necked woman like you coming around in broad daylight like this makes Dannasama very uncomfortable and troubled. Now, you had better go home quickly. He is busy and has set aside all obstacles to write you a letter of refusal, so you should read this and give up and go home. You really are a dull woman. Even if you try to capture Yūfutesu-sama or trick him with such clumsy methods, it won't work, ahahaha.'
