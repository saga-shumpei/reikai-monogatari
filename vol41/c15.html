Chapter 15: 難問題 [1119]

難問題〔1119〕

§ 1

King Seran, accompanied by the three, Thames, Rebu, and Karu, arrived at the cavernous mansion of the Kami Kitateru, the sound of their horses' hooves ringing out valiantly at last. Riūn came out to the mouth of the cavern to welcome them.

Riūn: 'Are you King Seran-sama? I have been waiting for you. Please wait a moment, for I shall go and report to my master.'

King Seran: 'I pray you, be so kind. We have followed the instructions of Ōgonhime-sama and Kiyoteruhime-sama, and taking advantage of the dark night, have slipped away from the castle of Iruna. I beg you to kindly intercede for us with the Kami Kitateruhiko-sama.'

Riūn: 'Yes, I understand. I ask that you wait a while.'

With that, he turned on his heel and proceeded deep into the interior, and bowing before the Kami Kitateru, he spoke.

Riūn: 'It was indeed King Seran-sama who arrived. Where shall I show him to?'

The complexion of Yasudarahime changed in a flash with joy, embarrassment, and surprise. The Kami Kitateru, filled with joy, spoke.

The Kami Kitateru: 'Since I shall meet him now, show him to Room Number Three. And please serve him some hot water and ask him to wait for a while. I am going to the Shinden now to offer my thanks to the Kami. Come, Takenohime, Yasudarahime-dono, let us go to the inner chambers.'

So saying, he took the lead and advanced to the room before the Kami. Riūn ran out toward the front to welcome King Seran. Leader, left behind, folded his arms and spoke to himself.

Leader: 'What a strange thing this is. When I came to the mountain of wolves with fear, the many wolves were as docile as dogs. And here, a one-eyed old man like the Kami Kitateru, and a wife who could put flowers to shame—what a strange contrast. Thinking I had come here escorting Yasudarahime-sama, to find that King Seran-sama has arrived—what a mysterious thing this is. Surely, when the Princess sees the King's face, she will make a strange expression, caught between surprise and joy... (In the style of Jōruri) Though it is said that all living things must perish and those who meet must part, and that this is the way of the floating world, we were betrothed by our parents, separated by strange clouds, divided between the country and the capital, and I have spent my mornings and evenings worrying about your well-being. What a lucky day this is. To meet the person I have yearned for so, and in a cavern on Mount Takateru where wolves dwell of all places—a thing no mortal could have known, I have done nothing but weep. When I think of it, I am filled with gratitude; it is a divine intervention, as if the Heavenly Rock Cave itself has been opened, and my heart has been set at ease. How happy I am, how nostalgic I feel... clinging to each other without shame before others' eyes, taking each other's hands, and weeping aloud—how lovely it is. Chan, chan, chan... that is the moment. I, too, would like to taste such a romance once. A man and a woman burning with the blood of youth—how happy they must be. A man and woman who have yearned for each other meeting in a place they never expected—how could this not be a cause for joy? Ahahaha, how auspicious, how auspicious, how very auspicious. The Kami Kitateru is a man of experience, so he is quite stylish. If I am to serve a Senden-shi who understands such things, I would not mind any hardship. A vow of a thousand years, firmer than rock, inside a cavern of a thousand-pulling rock—it is a pun on 'katame' (to harden/to betroth), not just the eyes of the Kami Kitateru, isn't it? Ufufufu.'

Riūn, who had led King Seran's party to Room Number Three and had come running to report to the Kami Kitateru, saw Leader laughing all by himself.

Riūn: 'Hey Leader, what are you laughing at? Guests have arrived. Since there are only the two of us, husband and wife, in this mansion, we are short-handed; why don't you hurry and draw some hot water for the guests and attend to them? You are a man with no wit.'

Speaker: 'Eh, it cannot be helped, then from now on it shall be Fugenjitsukau.'

Speaker: 'Moshi-moshi, Riūn-sama, you just said that you would practice Fugenjitsukau, but does that not already break the principle of Fugenjitsukau?'

Speaker: 'Eh, you are noisy, mine is a special-made quasi-Fugenjitsukau.'

Speaker: 'Seiran-ō-sama, I am sorry to have kept you waiting after you have come all this way. However, I have only just arrived myself, and my seat has not even warmed up yet. Speaker: 'Yes, thank you, has the Kamisama of Kitateru not arrived yet?'

Speaker: 'He will be here soon. Please wait for a while.'

Seiran-ō: 'Having climbed the steep mountains of Takateru, I rest my body within the rock cave. Wishing to meet the command of the Kamisama of Kitateru, I have visited following the will of the Kami.'

Riūn: 'Wait a while longer, Kami-tsukasa. Soon the Kamisama of Kitateru will be here. Takenohime, whose Ikimitama is pure and upright, and his wife the Mikoto are also with him. The time is drawing near when you shall meet the Himegami whom your Mikoto has longed for. Yasudarahime-ki no Mikoto has been longing for you and praying morning and evening.'

Seiran-ō: 'How mysterious, how is it that Yasudarahime is hiding in this rock cave?'

Riūn: 'Everything is in accordance with the will of the Kami; the human body is in a world of dreams, being protected as it goes.'

Speaker: 'I am Kitateruhiko. You have done well to enter this rock cave. Are Koganehime and Kiyoteruhime-dono doing well?'

Speaker: 'I meet you for the first time. You are the Kamisama of Kitateru, whose name is high in Ananai-kyō; having seen your figure even once, my heart has been strengthened as if I have met a true Ikigami. I humbly ask for your guidance from now on. As you know, the castle of Iruna is in a state of life or death, so following the instructions of Koganehime-sama, I have come here in disguise, though I know it is cowardly and weak.'

Speaker: 'Ahahaha, please do not worry at all. There is no other way but to leave everything to the Keirin of the Kamisama. We shall now apply our Kamisakura, so please calm your mind and take it easy. As you know, this is the den of wolves, and no matter what hero or great man, they cannot spy into this rock cave. Please be at ease. However, there is one thing I must ask you. It is not a matter of others, but you have a consort called Samarihime, do you not? What do you intend to do with that consort from now on?'

The King was struck dumb by these words, his heart troubled over how to answer, and he remained silent for a while, looking down.

Kitateruhiko: 'You intend to take Samarihime, whom you have once decided upon as your wife, with you wherever you go and share your hardships with her, do you not? If, by any chance, a splendid woman whom you truly love were to appear here, would you follow your own will and make that woman your wife? Or will you continue to love the Samarihime whom you do not favor, no matter what? I would like to hear your answer to that. Even the god Kitateru has some thoughts on the matter.'

The King, lost in thought as to how he should answer, finally replied,

King: 'Yes, I shall leave everything to Kamunagara. We, whose hearts are clouded, cannot judge what is right. I humbly wish to hear your thoughts.'

He skillfully diverted the words and cast the resolution of the matter back upon the god Kitateru.

Kitateruhiko: 'Otsuhohoho, you are a King not to be underestimated. I thought you would be troubled for an answer if I, Kitateru, spoke thus, but instead, you have cast a great problem upon me, and Kitateruhiko is somewhat perplexed.'

King: 'It is useless to hide anything before you, who know all. Furthermore, I do not wish to say things that are not in my heart. You may scold me, but in truth, no matter how I think of it, I cannot help but dislike Samarihime. There was some scheme involved; the Minister of the Right drove away my betrothed and forced his daughter upon me, so in short, it is a marriage without love. I feel that such a marriage of false love is an offense against the Kami. On the other hand, looking at it from another perspective, Samarihime seems to be harboring feelings of love for me from the bottom of her heart at this moment. Therefore, I am suffering day and night, lost in thought as to what I should do. Since it is a matter concerning the rise and fall of the castle of Iruna for the sake of Samarihime alone, I am unable to make a decision.'

Kitateruhiko: 'What a weak-willed person you are. Why did you not act like a man and refuse firmly at the beginning? You were clinging to your position as King of Seran and consented to a marriage that was not in your heart. You should have had a betrothed permitted by your parents; why did you not fulfill that prior promise? Furthermore, you are fundamentally mistaken. To ignore a betrothed permitted by one's parents and change course halfway is a failure in the way of filial piety. Regardless of the circumstances, why did you not obey the command of your father the King and risk your life to contend for it?'

King: 'Hearing those words, I repent my own weak will and lack of resolve more than ever. Even if it were a violation of the heavenly law, if I could become husband and wife with Yasudahime, I would have no regrets even if I were to cast away the throne.'

Kitateruhiko: 'Ufuhohoho, you have finally spoken your true heart. That is your sincere heart without falsehood. However, if that Yasudahime-sama had already shared intimacy with a husband even once, what would you do? Would you still be happy to become her husband? If she had kept her chastity for Yasudahime and had not shared intimacy even once, that would be one thing, but if you think that it does not matter as long as you can be together, then you are no longer human, but a slave to love.'

King: 'Yes, as you say, if such a thing were true of Yasudahime, I would give her up. However, I believe that in her case, such a thing could not be.'

Kitateruhiko: 'There is someone I want you to meet now. Please do not be surprised.'

King: 'Is that Yasudahime? Somehow, such a feeling has come to my mind.'

Kitateruhiko: 'Ahahaha, as they say, a snake knows the way of a snake.'
