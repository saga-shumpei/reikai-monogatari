§ 1

Having surmounted the Forty-Eight Slopes of Ojika Pass at last, the company of four advanced across a boundless wilderness overgrown with rank weeds, trusting unto their feet. Suddenly their way was barred: they arrived beside a roaring mountain torrent that dashed spray aloft and surged in swirling eddies.

Yaji: 'Alas! We have surmounted the Forty-Eight Slopes of Ojika Pass and tramped across this boundless moor, yet here our way is brought to an abrupt halt! A mighty river lieth across our path! To cross unto the capital of Fusa and reach Mount Caucasus is a long journey beset with many perils; yet where in the world do those unending throngs of pilgrims—men and women, young and old—pass by? Folk say the high roads are teeming with travelers, yet along our path not a single mortal soul is to be seen! Could it be that on descending Ojika Pass we strayed from the true road and have marched in the opposite direction?'

Yota: 'Between this gloomy weather and the eerie surroundings, 'tis for all the world like a journey into the Nether Realm! 'Tis just as when we plunged into the abyss, our souls wandering in midair, until we reached the Crossroads of the Six Realms and met the Copper Tree Statue! Could this stream not be a brother of the Sanzu River? An uncanny wind bloweth! Behold this marvel: those mountain peaks that stood sublime and unyielding till a moment ago have vanished into thin air! 'Tis exactly like the Sanzu River! What thinkest thou, Yajihiko?'

Yaji: 'Awed by the divine power of our Kototama, the mountains have fled like clouds and mist! What mighty fellows we of the Sango Faith are, ha-ha!'

Katsu: 'Hark! Could this not be the Sanzu River that floweth through the Netherworld? Its atmosphere seemeth passing strange compared unto rivers of the mortal sphere!'

Yaji: 'Though its atmosphere be strange, have no fear! This Yajihiko is an experienced traveler who hath explored the Netherworld twice already! When first I came with Yotakō, the Sanzu River was clear as crystal; yet when I came the second time, a foul, choking stench pierced the nostrils, and a filthy brew of urine, dung, black blood, snot, and phlegm flowed in place of water! At that time, the ferry-crone and clothes-stripping hag told me: "Because mortals of the mundane world commit foul deeds, this pure river hath become thus defiled!" Seeing that this filthy mortal world cannot turn pure overnight, wert this the Sanzu River of the dead, turbid waters would still be flowing! Yet look! This is a stream of exquisite, pristine purity! Wherefore it cannot possibly be the Sanzu River!'

Roku: 'Gentlemen, look yonder! At the foot of that pine of shapely boughs, doth not a small cottage appear? That may well be the principal residence of the demon-crone of the Sanzu River!'

Yaji: 'Nonsense! That is roofed with tile! The mansion of the crone is a magnificent structure beyond compare, resembling a storehouse of gold!'

Yota: 'A storehouse of gold, sayest thou? When I journeyed with thee previously, was it not a wretched hovel resembling a privy?'

Yaji: 'Ha-ha-ha! Dull-witted rogue! Because it resembled a privy, in modern fashion I rectified the Kototama and termed it a "Storehouse of Gold"!'

Yota: 'What drivel! Yet it smelleth exceedingly suspicious! Shall we visit it and see what creature dwelleth therein?'

Yaji: 'Hold awhile; 'tis a matter for deliberation! There is ample room for careful reflection!'

Yota: 'I care not! Sink or swim! Whether good or evil, false or true, old man or crone, peerless beauty or ugly hag—I, Yotaemon-no-Jō Mutekatsu, Chief of the Imperial Police, shall conduct an official inspection!'

Yaji: 'Ha-ha-ha! He hath begun to cut capers again! If thou frolickest so, when the Sun Deity appeareth, thy skinny arms will be scorched to cinders!'

Yota: 'What sayest thou? If scorched, I need only plunge into this river! "Adopt the long to supply the short; supply water where water lacketh!" Worry not! I am a candidate envoy of the Sango Faith traversing heaven and earth in perfect freedom!'

So saying, he stepped briskly toward the hut, assuming a bizarre posture like a praying mantis with knees bent and swaying hips, peering through the sliding lattice-window of the solitary cottage:

Yota: 'Prithee, good grandmother, grant me lodging for the night! "That is easily done, yet open not this chamber!" Hearty thanks unto thee; tonight I shall stretch my weary legs and sleep in comfort! The crone puckers her lips, saying: "Open not this chamber!" So saying, she taketh her pail to fetch water from the glen, leaving Yotahiko all alone! What the crone meant by forbidding me to open this door—surely a fair maiden lieth within to share my couch! Be that as it may, let me open it! Sliding the screen with my left hand, I peer inside—heavens, what horror! In yonder corner lie severed arms; in this corner lie severed legs!

Wert I to lodge in this house tonight, limbs and bones would be chopped to mincemeat with a carver, salted, and devoured by the old hag! Foaming at the mouth in terror, tucking up my skirts toward the rear gate, off I scurry—heigh-ho, heave-ho, off we sprint, ha-ha-ha!'
