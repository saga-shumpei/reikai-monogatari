Chapter 6: A Comfortable Retirement [556]

第6章　楽隠居〔556〕

§ 1

The three—Yaji, Yota, and Roku-kō—climbed Ojika Pass with perplexed countenances. When they deemed they had ascended some seventeen or eighteen chō, their eyes were caught by a considerably vast cavern looming beside the path.

Roku: 'Master Yaji, Master Yota, what an exceedingly narrow path this hath become! On one side yawneth a sheer precipice, and the torrent far below splasheth violent foam—truly a harrowing scene that maketh one's skin crawl at a single glance! And upon this narrow ledge, an enormous cavern jutting out before us! 'Tis suspicious beyond measure; mayhap knaves of the Ural faith are lying in ambush for us at this perilous pass?'

Yajihiko: 'Aha! Verily upon a ridge narrow as a horse's spine, a cavern protrudeth abruptly! This is somewhat suspicious; shall we toss stones from here to sound it out?'

Yotahiko: 'A capital plan; let us take a sounding!'

So saying, each seized suitable stones, and like a rapid-fire battery, hurled them together clattering against the mouth of the cavern. From within the cave came a shout:

'Ho there, look out! What manner of mischief (tengo) are ye about?'

Yajihiko: 'Thinkest thou pebbles striking a cavern reinforced with solid rock could harm thee? We merely woke thee from thy slumber! Who art thou lurking in there?'

Voice from within: ''Tis I, 'tis I! Who art thou?'

Yajihiko: ''Tis I too! A fellow human being!'

Voice: 'Art thou of the Ural faith or the Sango faith?'

Yajihiko: 'Gentlemen of the Sango faith!'

Voice: 'Oho! Art thou certain? I too am of the Sango faith! Captured by the rogues of the Ural faith, I was locked up in such a dungeon! Wilt thou not rescue me?'

Yotahiko: 'Hold, Yaji, we must be exceedingly wary! There is no telling what insidious trap an evil spirit may have contrived! Ho, thou creature within the cave, if thou art truly of the Sango faith, what is thy name? Declare thyself!'

Voice: 'Tell me thy name first! For if thou art of the Ural faith, all is lost!'

Yotahiko: 'Aha! He doth indeed sound like a true believer of the Sango faith! The tone of his voice is remarkably resolute. To maintain such vigor while cooped up in such a hole is the true Sango spirit! Were he an Ural knave, he would surely whine like a professional mendicant: "Alas, alas, noble travelers passing up and down, have mercy upon this wretched beggar; pity my sore affliction! Good master passing by, spare an alms for a poor blind man!"—whimpering mechanically. Yet his words possess a distinct potency of Kototama. To preserve such spirit while confined in a cramped cave showeth that even were he of the Ural faith, he hath backbone! Shall we banter with him a little from outside?'

§ 2

Yajihiko: 'That should be diverting! Ho there, venerable hermit of the cavern, thou must needs feel exceedingly bored!'

Voice from within: 'Nay, not at all! There is a small aperture facing outward, and when I peer out from time to time, the scarlet and emerald hues of Kojika River mingle in rich spring splendor, foaming and splashing along the torrent—verily an unrivalled spectacle under heaven! If thou too, when old age cometh, desirest to retire, thou shouldst select such a hermitage and enshrine thyself upon eternal, unshakable rocks!'

Yajihiko: 'What an amusing rogue! Say, Master Hermit, what is thy venerable age?'

Man in the Cave: 'Mine age? By hook or by crook, I am thirty by calendar count!'

Yajihiko: 'Is not thirty far too youthful for retirement? At thirty, a man is in the prime of his manly vigor! Some five hundred thousand years in the future, sages named Qiu (Confucius) or Meng (Mencius) shall appear in China and declare that "At thirty, a man standeth firm!" To retire at this hour is a waste of good timber! Who in the world art thou?'

Voice from within: 'I was formerly a believer of the Ural faith, but when the Preaching Emissary Ishikoridome journeyed toward Mount Caucasus, I encountered her at the mansion of Princess Kujaku; thereupon I repented and embraced the Sango faith! Yet having achieved not a single merit for God, and desiring to be of some holy service, I feigned outward reconciliation with the Ural faith and joined their troop of constables. Presently, an Emissary of the Sango faith with two companions wandered into our barrier-station. While our fellows were dressing a wild boar with blood-stained cleavers, seeing the Sango men arrive, a rogue beside me muttered, "Shall we dress them up roughly with our blades?" Unable to endure such cruelty, I gave the Sango Emissary a discreet wink; whereupon they flung open the sliding door and fled headlong! Thereupon my comrades shouted, "Thou art a traitor! The stink of the Sango faith hath not left thee; thou didst make a suspicious sign! As punishment, thou shalt be confined here without term!"—and so from yesterday I have been locked within this cave! I am Katsukō of the Sango faith! Pray hasten to open this Heavenly Rock Door and deliver me!'

Yajihiko: 'Hold, hold! We shall now perform the opening of the Heavenly Rock Door! Ho, Yotahiko, thou shalt wield the purification wand (ōnusa); I shall chant the Heavenly Prayer; and as for Roku, having no offerings, pluck leaves from the boughs to serve as holy food!'

§ 3

Katsuhiko: 'Pah! This is no time for frivolous jesting! Wilt thou not open quickly?'

Yajihiko: ''Tis the way of nature: when dawn openeth (akereba), dusk falleth (kureru)! "Open for me, open for me!"—and so day after day dusketh away! Ha-ha-ha!'

Katsuhiko: 'Ugh, how exasperating! Cease tantalizing me!'

Yajihiko: 'Tantalize thee I will! Because thou art cooped up in such a cramped hole looking so pitiable, I tantalize thee out of sheer spite! Ha-ha-ha!'

Yotahiko: 'Ho, Yaji, talk not such foolishness! Open the door for him quickly!'

Yajihiko: '"Opening the casket, remorse ensued! Had I known it would come to this, 'twere better left unopened! Longing to meet, longing to see from dawn to dusk..."—if we are treated to such a tragic theatrical weeping-scene, it would be intolerable!'

Roku: 'Ugh, what a fellow for spouting worthless jests! Release him cleanly and completely!'

Yajihiko: 'Ah, yea, indeed so! Let us open it! Alas, what an imposing padlock they have set upon it! Unluckily, I possess no skeleton key, so Master Katsu, there is no help for it! Pray tarry here in peace until the appointed season arriveth!'

Katsuhiko: 'Smash the padlock with a stone from the roadside and set me free!'

Yajihiko: 'To destroy such a splendid padlock wantonly would be a shameful waste!'

Katsuhiko: 'Waste be damned! 'Tis a padlock of the Ural faith; smash it into smithereens and deliver me!'

Yajihiko: 'Hold, hold! To destroy what was painstakingly crafted requireth reflection! To act like violent extremists would offend the Sun Deity! Can we not devise some means to open it while preserving its original shape intact?'

Roku: 'Look here, gentlemen! There is such a contrivance upon the wall! This must assuredly be some secret trigger!'

So saying, Roku pressed a slender projection of rock shaped like a little finger; upon which, the stone portal swung open without the slightest resistance!

Yajihiko and Yotahiko: 'Ha-ha-ha! What a trivial mechanism, like gluing a target with nasal mucus—truly the Ural style throughout! Ho, Master Katsu, come forth quickly!'

Katsuhiko: 'On such a long spring day, there is no need for such haste! Pray enter within at your leisure; let us exchange tales and while away the pleasant spring hours!'

Yajihiko: 'Behold, what an extraordinarily easygoing soul! "Birds of a feather flock together," as the proverb rightly saith! Horses go with horses, and oxen with oxen! Now our four souls are complete! O Master Katsu, pray grant us your hearty friendship! I am Yajihiko, a jester by nature; this fellow is the world's master of foolishness, so he is named Yotahiko; and that other beast is named Roku (Six) because he is a thoroughly worthless (roku-demonai) fellow! Ha-ha-ha!'
