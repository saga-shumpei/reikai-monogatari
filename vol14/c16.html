§ 1

As an envoy of the Sango Faith overcoming all crooked obstacles, Katsuhiko, clad in three haori cloaks, trudged along in bizarre attire, surmounting the Eighteen Slopes and ascending the mountain passes of Nineteen and Twenty with youthful vigor. Yajihiko and Yotahiko spewed elusive sophistries, trembling like lean nags—buzz, buzz, buzz!—with unsteady wobbly hips, blowing trumpets from their mouths and loud horns of flatulence from their rumps, until Thunder himself fled barefoot in terror! Reaching the Twentieth Mountain Pass, they rested awhile, and set forth once more on Shanks' Mare. Just then, Karasu Kanzaburō of cawing voice appeared, leading a band of many minions.

Before the path of Katsuhiko and his four companions, the masked band shouted in eerie guise, spreading their arms crying, 'Hold! Ye shall not pass!' Brandishing weapons, they barred the way. Though our heroes seemed reckless like frogs facing snakes, through the grace of the Mizu-no-Mitama, when Katsuhiko chanted the majestic Kototama toward Heaven and Earth, even those ferocious ruffians lost their wits and surrendered, prostrating upon the highway like crawling dogs! True to his name of Katsuhiko—victorious over all defeats—he marched triumphantly at their head. Crossing five more passes, they rested upon the veranda of a modest thatched cottage, meeting a blind grandmother.

Hearing the tragic tale of the aged couple, Rokukō realized his past sins; with his former misdeeds exposed, he could scarce reply for weeping. A rekindling of old love like fire catching upon charred embers—putting aside the aged couple, Rokukō's rejoicing was even as the opening of the Heavenly Rock Cave! Uplifted in spirit, resolving that after worshiping at Mount Caucasus the sword should return unto its former scabbard, they bade farewell and climbed unto the Twenty-Third Pass of famed magnificent prospect. Buffeted by gales, gasping for breath, they stood atop the Twenty-Fifth Pass.

Tarrying there, clumsy rites of Chinkon suddenly opened a scene of wild bedlam! Tumbling into the ravine, the four swooned away, and hastening unto the crossing of the Itto River in the Nether Realm, what met their eyes? Peering through the lattice-window of a cottage nestled beneath a pine grove, born jester Yotahiko engaged in fierce dispute with a crone. Forcing the door and entering, amidst tumultuous strife the hags brandished carving knives, striving to wrench away the souls of the Sango Faith! Two grotesque crones—one crying, 'I am Kotsune-hime, younger sister of the Demon Crone of the Sanzu River!'

§ 2

'Come on, let us fight!' cried Tokoyo-hime, brandishing her carver to strike at the four! The four envoys darted like birds, clashing blades and dodging like butterflies fluttering in spring fields; and hard-pressed by their valiant combat, the hags fell back struck dumb! Yet scarce had they rejoiced, when lo—whether dream, reality, or illusion—at the sound of rushing waters they awoke upon the river bottom! An incomprehensible marvel! Skilled swimmer Rokukō unaccountably drowned and turned into a floating corpse! The three left behind were plunged in despair, having not even incense to burn in his memory!

Night's curtain fell in pitch-black gloom where white and black were indistinguishable; through the night they wept, and at dawn marched dejectedly across the vast plain. Reaching a rushing stream, Yotahiko shuddered with panting breath, crying: 'Ro... ku... I am Roku!' Declaring that Rokukō's living spirit had arrived, striking awe into their hearts—even as he spoke, an enormous man strode forth! Looking closely, 'twas Karasu Kanzaburō, carrying Rokukō's body upon his back! A proof that the threads of divine affinity never sever; through the virtue of Kototama, Rokukō instantly revived!

Peering around in bewilderment, Rokukō cried: 'Katsuhiko-san? Yota-san? And thou art Yajihiko the farting bug! Is this the Netherworld or the mortal sphere?' Shaking his head in doubt, he realized at last: 'Am I truly alive?!' Rejoicing, the four followed Katsuhiko down steep slopes, passed safely through the capital of Fusa, and worshiped at the glorious shrine of Mount Caucasus! Joyfully returning unto Koyama Village to Otake's home, with Katsuhiko as matchmaker, their broken bond was rekindled; and the old man, grandmother, Roku, brother Matsu, his wife, and Otake rejoiced in boundless gladness!

The wondrous tale of their wedding ceremony—auspicious beyond compare!

Otake's parental home in Koyama Village was named Harunoya (Spring House). The grandfather was Tsurusuke (Crane), the grandmother Okame (Tortoise), the son Matsukō (Pine), and his wife Oume (Plum). With the bridegroom added unto the family of Crane, Tortoise, Pine, Bamboo, and Plum, six souls dwelt together; and Roku's wedding was solemnized with Katsuhiko the divine envoy as matchmaker! Yajihiko and Yotahiko joined as Roku's companions in the feast! Together with the one true teaching of Sango, ten precious spirits celebrated the joyous banquet! Fragrant as white plum blossoms was Otake; noble as evergreen pine was Matsu and his bride; praying that like the crane's thousand years and tortoise's ten thousand ages their love might endure into the Miroku Age, before the Deity of Union they chanted the Amatsu-Norito, exchanging the sacred three-times-three cups!
