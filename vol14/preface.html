§ 1. Introductory Hymn (序歌)

Summoned into the Hall of Miroku (567), with hearts made pure, we evoke with awe the name of Ame-no-Minakanushi, the Supreme Sovereign Deity;
Followed by Takamimusuhi-no-Ōmikami and Kamumusuhi-no-Ōmikami;
And the Primeval Ancestral Deity of the vast Earth, Kunitokotachi-no-Ōmikami, and Toyokuninushi-no-Ōmikami;
And the Sovereign Sun-Deity who ruleth over the Divine Realm of Light, Amaterasu-Sume-Ōmikami;
And Kamu-Susano-o-no-Ōmikami, and Suseri-no-Hime-no-Ōmikami;
And the Sovereign Deity Tsukiyomi traversing the celestial heavens;
Together with the eight hundred myriads of Heavenly Deities (Amatsu-kami) and eight hundred myriads of Earthly Deities (Kunitsu-kami);
Whose divine majesty permeateth the Great Eightfold Isles, the eighty islands, and eighty realms,
Dwelling eternally within every grand shrine and humble sanctuary across all provinces—
With all the myriad attendant deities who serve Them;
And all the Chihayaburu deities from the distant Divine Age who descended into the mortal world and endured manifold bitter trials, without a single one omitted;
And those who govern the affairs of the Hidden Realm: the deities of Yahoyone,
And Ōkuninushi and Ōmononushi manifested in the grand sanctuary of Kitsuki;
And the divine soul of Sukunahikona, who blesseth the arts of healing and medicine and the sacred science of holy enchantments;
And the Great God of the primordial Ōmoto enshrined upon Mount Okefuse of the four-crested sacred summit of Hongū;
Not to speak of the branch and leaf deities, and the hermits and sages of Kara and Yamato who soar across the heavens and traverse the earth—
Yea, all the righteous and pure spirits across all the world, without omitting a single one!
Protect this Reikai Monogatari (Tales of the Spirit World)!
Imparting the pure Kushimitama (Wondrous Spirit) unto the righteous souls of mortals,
Wash and cleanse their spirits, transmuting them into luminous gems radiant as crystal,
And grant that they may comprehend, wide and deep, the grand design of the Divine Realm!
O Amakatsu-Kunikatsu-Kushimitama, Chiyorihiko-no-Kami, Sohodo-no-Kami, also named the divine spirit of Kuebiko!
Bestow the blessing of the divine spirits upon the devout gathered here in Ōmoto, and upon every mortal across the world!
Causing exalted spirits to descend upon each soul,
May the sins, faults, defilements, and transgressions of mortals—who unknowingly and recklessly treated lightly the divine decrees entrusted by God—
Be re-viewed, re-heard, and absolved through the spirit of Naobi (rectitude),
That they may profoundly comprehend the divine will of the Deities,
And preserve the sacred words spanning the Divine, the Astral, and the Manifest!
Before the Ancestral Deities of the Divine Land, I offer my humble and reverent prayer:
Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! May the divine spirits grant their blessing and grace!
To elucidate, without omitting the slightest detail, the divine teachings of Foundress Nao Deguchi,
This Tales of the Nyoi-Hōshu (The Wish-Fulfilling Sacred Jewel) is dictated by Zuigetsu,
Who, praying with wholehearted devotion for the nation and the world,
Biddeth mortals savor with joyful hearts in every leisure hour the wondrous achievements of the Deities of heaven and earth.
In awe of the divine command of Kunitokotachi-no-Ōmikami, the two deities Izanagi and Izanami,
Seeking to repair and solidify the drifting, floating earth,
Thrust down the Jeweled Spear of Heaven, and stirring the brine with a curdling sound (kōro-kōro),
Brought forth Onogoro Island, establishing it as the afterbirth (placenta) of the sacred land;
And setting up the Heavenly Pillar and Earthly Pillar, and fashioning the Eight-Fathom Palace,
The two deities united in eternal wedlock,
Bringing forth the Great Eightfold Isles, myriad islands and lands,
The primeval ancestors of humanity, and the ten thousand things.
To cause all divine mortals to dwell in peace upon the earth,
They brought forth the diverse deities of the Sun, the Earth, and the Moon,
Entrusting unto each their sacred tasks, inaugurating and opening all things,
Laboring ceaselessly without end—how great and wondrous is their grace!
Amaterasu-Sume-Ōmikami, taking the heart of the Great Ancestral Deity of the land as Her own heart,
Graciously blessed, cherished, and loved all the green-grass mortals (humanity),
Causing them to multiply, flourish, and achieve prosperity;
And taking upon Herself the grand divine mission, governed the Heavenly Realm;
And beholding the seeds of the five grains, proclaimed:
"These precious seeds are the nourishment whereby the green-grass mortals of the manifest world shall live!"
And planted them without omission throughout the four quarters of the world.
Even so, may the divine spirits grant their blessing,
That this Tales of the Wish-Fulfilling Jewel may become the spiritual sustenance of humanity,
Opening and spreading abroad unto all lands and islands across the four seas!
Kamunagara, beneath the divine protection of the exalted Deities,
May the Kototama of God grant blessing,
Sweeping away and purifying every turbulent, raging evil spirit,
Silencing even the murmuring leaves of rocks, roots, and noxious weeds that once disputed,
And causing all to yield single-heartedly unto this sacred doctrine!
Before the Deities of Heaven and Earth, I offer this humble prayer!

§ 2. The Albatross (4) (信天翁四)

"Rivaling Kyokutei Bakin of old, 'tis a rambling, endless tale of long sleep-talking! Its true substance merely taketh this or that officer of Ōmoto as a target, attaching divine titles in forced contrivance; borrowing renowned figures of the modern world and dressing them up as 'the God of So-and-So,' it depicteth the past, present, and future of the Ōmoto faith, inserting Onisaburo's own wild delusions and decorating them beyond recognition! Cleverly fishing out raw material from Ehara Koyata's writings on the Old and New Testaments and Mahayana sutras, 'tis nothing but a secret contraption known to none save the top leaders—a ridiculously long Ahodara-sutra of a tale!"
—In such fashion hath an impostor newspaper appeared, heaping cheap abuse upon us!
Among its accounts is an amusing marvel: it asserteth that "the manuscript, written in gigantic characters visible from a furlong away, amounteth by their own claim to twenty-two volumes and twenty-six thousand seven hundred sheets... etc." How could any mortal utter such utter nonsense?
Such a manuscript could not possibly be found though one searched the entire globe! Even for folly, there is a limit!
No matter how ignorant the reporter (one Iida) may be of a newspaper's public responsibility, this is a monstrous derailment!
Hath the changing season caused blood to rush to his head? Even for irresponsibility, there is a limit! The sheer absurdity of it leaveth me struck dumb without words!
Not only that, but packing his pages with fabrications concerning Zuigetsu's personal life, pouring forth every manner of slander and foul invective, and shouting in gloating triumph—verily, even for inhumanity, there is a limit!
Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! May the divine spirits grant their blessing and grace!
Swiftly cleanse and purify the foul, ugly evil spirits lurking within their hearts, and deliver them into true sincerity of soul!
Before the exalted Deities of Heaven and Earth, I offer my humble and reverent prayer!

October, Taishō 11 (1922)
Recorded by Onisaburō

などと難癖をつけ、ケチをつけているインチキ新聞が現れた。
その新聞の記述には、思わず吹き出してしまうような笑止千万な記事がある。曰く、
「一町（約百メートル）ほど遠くからでも見えるような巨大な文字で書いた原稿が、全二十二巻、二万六千七百枚に及ぶと自称している……云々」
と、よくもまあそこまで莫迦げた出鱈目を言えたものである。
そんな巨大な文字で書かれた原稿など、世界中どこを探したって存在するはずがないではないか。莫迦を言うにも程がある。
いかに新聞の社会的責任を知らない無知な記者（飯田某）だからといって、これはまた酷い脱線・デタラメぶりである。
季節の変わり目で頭に血が上り逆上でもしたのか、無責任にも程がある。あまりの荒唐無稽さに呆れ果てて言葉も出ない。

ああ、神ながら神ながら、みたま幸わい給いて、彼らの心の奥底に潜んでいる醜悪な邪神（まがつかみ）を一刻も早く祓い清め、真心に立ち返らせて救い給えと、天地の尊き神の御前に謹み敬いて祈願し奉る。

§ 3. Editorial Notes (凡例)

1. The narrative in this fourteenth volume, reckoned consecutively from the First Volume, concludeth with Chapter 567 (Five Hundred and Sixty-Seven)—the most auspicious number symbolizing Miroku.

2. Because a certain newspaper in Ayabe published a bizarre and slanderous article against the Reikai Monogatari, the "Albatross" hath once again taken flight to reply.

3. The Afterword (Epilogue) at the end of this volume treateth in detail the inner meanings of the Sanzu River, the Sanzu Hag (Datsueba), and the Ichizu (Single-Minded) River; and furthermore provideth an indispensable exposition upon the relationship between the Ōmoto Shin'yu and Buddhist terminology, which every reader ought to peruse.

October, Taishō 11 (1922)
Recorded by the Oral Author

§ 4. General Introductory Song (総論歌)

Deep indeed is the Divine Way when we reflect upon it: in the great and righteous tenth year of Taishō, the power of God was revealed, and His wondrous doctrine began to be proclaimed around noon on the eighteenth day of the ninth month. Nakano Tamahiro gathered the teachings like dew softly dripping from the pine needles of Takasago, bathed in divine grace; and Deguchi Onisaburo, having spent four-and-twenty years veiled in obscurity, stood forth like the eternal pine under the supreme majesty loftier than Mount Okefuse. Drawing forth the tempered ten-span sword, he smote the stratagems of the crooked spirits; and through the grace of the Miroku Deity, the brush moved with lightning speed, overcoming all obstacles. In the spring of the Mizunoe-Inu year (1922), narrating the august tales of the Divine Age, he completed the dictation of five hundred and sixty-seven chapters in honor of the Miroku God.
Further opening the eternal highway at four o'clock on the twenty-seventh day of the second month, the devout gathered: Tanimura, Matsumura greeting the flowering Divine Age, Kitamura marching hither amid today's glorious sunshine, while the mists upon Toyama cleared away; and Katō with loyal heart, Tachibana offering sincere devotion—how fragrant the everlasting Tachibana blossoms of the Anai teachings!
In particular, this fourteenth volume recounteth how Otohiko, attending upon the high priest Hinode-wake, the divine pillar sustaining the three thousand realms of the Divine, the Manifest, and the Astral, was encircled in the forest of Saruyama Pass together with Yajihiko and Yotahiko by the Chief Inspector of the Ural Cult and a legion of captors. Carving a bloody path to Kojika Pass, they were once more hemmed in fore and aft; and having no choice, leaped into the abyss of a thousand fathoms!
Lying unconscious, they traversed the paths of the Netherworld unto the Sanzu River, encountering the Hag of Clothes-Stripping (Datsueba). Engaging in a hilariously witty exchange of ten thousand words, crying "Come what may!", they splashed across the river stark naked. Striking against a sheer mountain, they blew so colossal a boast that even the mountain vanished in astonished clouds and mists! From the earth where they fled, an iron-wood idol sprang forth, sputtering foam, blowing snot, and discharging urine, throwing the three into choking confusion, and smiting its crown against the sun in heaven, fled away.
While Otohiko, Yaji, and Yota stood dumbfounded, they were awakened by Hinode-wake's company, only to find themselves lying at the bottom of Kojika Pass!
Then, swept into heaven by a record-breaking gale upon the Eighteen Passes, Yajihiko once more drifted to the banks of the Sanzu River; and here is told the tale of haughty Katsuhiko, undefeated in spirit though beaten again and again; the two crones of the Ichizu (Single-Minded) River wailing "I want it, I want it!", brandishing butcher knives in rapacity and pride; the endless disputations between man and woman on the long journey; the romance of Roku and Otake portrayed in vivid detail, rekindling lovers' vows—a volume dictated in merry banter, whirling in bewildering mists between dream, reality, and phantom!
How awe-inspiring! How delightful!

📖 現代日本語訳

思えば神の道は深遠であり、偉大にして正しき大正十年、神の神力が現れて、奇しき御教えを九月十八日（旧暦八月十八日）の正午頃、信徒の中野玉拾が書き集め、高砂の松葉の雫がしとしとと滴る神恵に潤いながら、神の出口王仁三郎は二十四年間の光陰を雲に覆い隠されていたのを脱し、桶伏山よりも高い神の大神威を照らす常盤の松の世としていよいよ現れ出で、鍛え上げたる十握の剣を抜き放ち、邪悪なる曲津神の企みを斬り払ってゆく。
みろくの神の御加護により、言霊の玉の緒、筆の運びも極めて速く、あらゆる妨害を退け、壬戌（大正十一年）の春、口述するも畏れ多い神代の物語を、みろく（五六七）の神に因んだ五百六十七の章節として語り解き、書き終えた。
永久不変の大道をいよいよ開く二月二十七日の午後四時、神の集う谷村、花咲く神代を松村氏、ここまで歩み来た北村氏、めでたく今日の日本晴れ、外山の霞はいつしか晴れて喜ばしい今日の空、心も加藤、道の信徒の誠を尽くす立花氏、時ならぬ橘の香りを放つ三五教よ。
とりわけこの第十四巻は、神界・現界・幽界の三千世界を支える霊柱たる三五教の宣伝使・日の出別の神司に従い仕える音彦が、猿山峠の森林において弥次彦・与太彦の二人とともに、ウラル教の大目付率いる数多の捕手に包囲され、血路を開いて小鹿峠にかかる折から、またしても敵に前後を囲まれて止むを得ず千尋の谷底へ飛び降り、気絶したまま幽界の冥土を辿って「三途の川」の脱衣婆（だつえば）に出会い、千言万語を尽くして面白おかしく掛け合い、ままよとばかりに素っ裸のまま三途の川を渡り、山に突き当たって大法螺を吹けば山も呆れて雲霞となり、逃げ去る跡の地中からヌッと湧き出た銅木像（どうもくぞう）が泡を吹き鼻をかみ小便を絞り出し、三人を一度に煙に巻き、空にかかる太陽に頭をぶつけて逃げてゆく。
音彦、弥次彦、与太彦が呆気にとられているところへ、日の出別の一行に呼び醒まされて気がついてみれば、小鹿峠の谷底に倒れていたのであった。
さらに十八峠の坂道で新記録破りの猛烈な暴風に吹き上げられて天へ舞い上がり、再び三途の川のほとりに迷い込んだ弥次彦や、いくら負けても鼻息荒い勝彦の物語、一途（いちづ）の川の二人の鬼婆が「欲しい、欲しい」と泣き言を垂れ、強欲と高慢に出刃包丁を振り回し、男と女の争論果てしない長旅、六公とお竹のロマンスを克明に描き、比翼連理の恋物語を蒸し返して面白おかしく語り立て、夢か現か幻か、とりとめもなく吹きまくり、煙に巻いて読者を翻弄する奇想天外な巻である。
ああ尊し、ああ面白し。
