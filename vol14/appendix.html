Appendix: Epilogue (Batsubun)

附録　跋文

§ 1 The Divine Mandate and the Great River of the Three Crossings

Having received with holy reverence the divine decree of God, I began to dictate these wondrous Tales of the Age of the Gods within the Spirit World. Not merely of the Divine Age do I speak, but of the Astral Realm and likewise of the Manifest World; in unbroken accord with the divine will, my speech hath rolled onward like a celestial chariot.
Standing upon the mountain pass that boundeth the Three Realms of the Manifest, the Astral, and the Divine, beside the Three-Rapids River—at the foot of the Mitsuo Pass and the peaks of Yotsuo, reclining within the Sanctuary of the Master shaded by the pure Golden Pavilion—while the waters of the Sanzu (Three Crossings) flowed in surging torrents, the running brush of the Mizu-no-Mitama hath recorded all things. And now, at the very close of this Fourteenth Volume, its ultimate truth shall be revealed.

§ 2 The River of Sanzu and the Manifestations of the Ferry-Keeper

The River of Sanzu (Three Crossings) is the judgment tribunal wherein the eternal destination of the spirits of all mortal souls is decreed—whether unto the Divine Realm, the Manifest Realm, or the Astral Realm. 'Tis a holy precinct hailed likewise as the Yasu Riverbed and the River Jordan.
When an evil spirit approacheth, the guardian of that river transformeth in an instant into a terrible hag; stripping from it every garment, she casteth it down in nakedness into the Land of the Roots (Ne-no-kuni) and the subterranean astral abyss. But when a righteous spirit arriveth, the guardian changeth swiftly into a fair and radiant maiden, uttering words of tenderness; divesting it of its worn earthly vestments, she clotheth it in robes of shimmering brocade, and bestoweth upon it the holy seal and badge that granteth entrance unto the Paradise of Takama-ga-hara.
And when souls of undecided good and evil draw near, the guardian transformeth once more into an old crone, brandishing a bamboo broom to drive them back unto the earthly mortal realm (Saha). Knowing neither morning nor evening, even as the river’s currents ever shift, she unfoldeth her manifold workings through everlasting ages. Strictly severing good from evil and righteousness from crookedness, verily this is the Great Watershed River of mortal spirits, flowing onward for a thousand generations without end!

悪霊がそこへ通りかかる時は、その川守はたちまち恐ろしい鬼婆へと姿を変え、その着物を剥ぎ取って丸裸にし、根の国や底の幽界（地獄）へと突き落とし捨てる。反対に、善なる御魂がやって来る時は、川守はたちまち麗しい美女となり、優しい言葉をかけながら、現世の古き衣服を脱がせて輝く錦の衣へと着替えさせ、高天原の楽園へと登るべき証印（印綬）を授けるのである。

§ 3 The Three Rapids and the Destiny of Spirits

Now as for the waters of this river, at times they flow in crystal purity, and at times in murky foulness. This unfixed state of clarity or turbidness is reflected from the inner state of the souls of all mortals who gather there; 'tis a wondrous, sacred, and incomparable stream, unique in all the cosmos.
Those who cross the torrential Upper Rapids (Uwatsu-se) are solely those spirits destined to be reborn into the Manifest World, or souls that are brought back unto earthly life.
Those spirits who cross the sluggish and dismal Lower Rapids (Shimotsu-se) are none other than the mournful souls doomed to plunge into the Land of the Roots and the subterranean deeps, into the world of primordial darkness and spiritual ignorance.
And those who cross the Middle Rapids (Nakatsu-se)—whose waters flow tranquil yet strong, crystal-clear, warm, and beautiful—are the blessed souls ascending unto Heaven and the Pure Land, where blossom the flowers of supreme joy and supreme bliss. Each soul, drawn onward by the cord of its own spiritual causality (Karma), advanceth upon its path; how awe-inspiring and sublime is the inviolable law of God!

§ 4 The River of One Crossing: Good and Evil Paths

Such is the tale of the River of Sanzu (Three Crossings); yet beside it, there existeth also the River of Ichizu (A Single Crossing).
Now, as for the destiny of this Single Crossing: those divine spirits who, having once been born into the mortal world, upheld the teachings of the Supreme, All-Good, and All-True Kami and Buddhas, trod the path of righteousness, and fully accomplished their holy vocation as children of God—these advance and ascend unto the Heaven of Great Sages and Fair Beings by way of the Yasu River. Though their spirits were already purified, here they are cleansed yet once again as they cross onward. Verily, rare indeed are the mortals who cross this River of Life, this single path of pure goodness!
Conversely, those perverse spirits who, having once been born into the mortal world, piled up mountain-high karma of evil under the sway of Matter-over-spirit (Taishu-reijū)—they receive not even the privilege of a formal trial; but plunging headlong into the murky, polluted torrents that cascade into the darkest abyss of the underworld, they cross while drowning and agonizing. Such are the Single-Crossing Rivers of Good and Evil—verily, a fearful and portentous flood!

§ 5 The Eternal Light of Divine Oracles and Prayer

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! May the divine spirits grant their blessing and grace!
Concerning the River of Sanzu and the River of Ichizu, I have dictated this tale mingled with humour and parody; yet may God cause the souls of those who read it with deep contemplation to awaken within themselves the steadfast faith of repentance and reformation! May they be guided to walk the pilgrimage of mortal life in purity, joy, and hope!
Before the throne of the God of Heaven and Earth, beneath the crystal-clear expanse of the vaulted sky, shineth Zuigetsu (the Auspicious Moon); illuminated by the all-pervading radiance of the Great Deity who shineth from Heaven (Amaterasu-Ōkami), how immense is my joy in elucidating and making plain the eternal destiny of the souls of all mankind!
Though the morning sun may shine or be veiled in clouds; though the moon may wax or wane; yea, even though the vast earth itself should sink beneath the waves—the holy teachings of the True God shall endure unto ten thousand kalpas and through all generations! As long as Heaven and Earth endure, never shall they alter, never decay, never perish throughout all eternity!
Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! May the divine spirits grant their blessing and grace!

§ 6 The Position of Maitreya and the Teachings of Ōmoto

In the Ōmoto Shin'yu (Divine Oracles), it is declared: "We shall transform this world into the Age of the Pine, the Age of Miroku (Maitreya), the Age of the Gods, and so forth." It is taught that Miroku signifieth Supreme Benevolence and Supreme Love, and is the Parent, the Master, and the Lord of all things throughout the universe.
Among our readers, there are those who, comparing this with Buddhist scriptures and the discourses of Shakyamuni, remark: "Maitreya is merely one Buddha among the Seven Past Buddhas (or the Buddhas of the successive ages); he is by no means an exalted deity or Buddha possessing so supreme a status as depicted in the Ōmoto Shin'yu." From the perspective of one who hath studied only ordinary Buddhist texts, this may indeed be considered a most natural and reasonable opinion.
Therefore, taking this occasion at the conclusion of the present volume, this Onisaburo desireth to set forth herein the position of Maitreya as revealed within the Buddhist scriptures, and offer it for the reference of our readers.

原文（近代文語）

神諭に『松の代　弥勒の代　神世に致すぞよ云々』とあり、弥勒は至仁至愛の意にして、宇宙万有一切の親也師也主也と説きたまへり。
読者の中には、仏教の教典に由りて釈迦の説と引き合せ、ミロクは七仏出生説の中にある一仏にして、大本の神諭にある如き尊き位置にある仏又は神にあらずと云ふ人あり。仏書のみを読みたる人の意見としては、最も至極なる見解と謂ふべしである。
王仁は、序を以て本巻の末尾に於て仏典に現はれたる弥勒の位置を茲に掲載して、読者の参考に供して見ようと思ふ。

📖 現代日本語訳

大本神諭に『松の代、弥勒（みろく）の代、神世に致すぞよ云々』とある。弥勒とは「至仁至愛（この上ない慈悲と愛）」を意味し、宇宙万有・森羅万象すべての「親」であり「師」であり「主」であると説かれている。
しかし読者の中には、仏教の経典に照らし合わせて釈迦の教説と比較し、「ミロクは過去七仏（あるいは歴代の仏）の系譜の中に現れる一仏に過ぎず、大本の神諭に書かれているような最高無上の位置にある仏や神ではない」と言う人がいる。仏教書ばかりを読んできた人の意見としては、極めてごもっともな見解と言うべきであろう。
そこで私（王仁三郎）は、この機会に本巻（第十四巻）の末尾において、仏典に現れた弥勒の真の位置をここに掲載し、読者の参考に供してみたいと思う。

§ 9 The Unity of Kami and Buddhas in the Ofudesaki, and Postscript

Viewing the matter in this light, one can clearly perceive that although the sacred Ofudesaki (Brush-writings) of the Foundress of Ōmoto embody the Way of the Gods (Shinto), from the very beginning, by virtue of the divine truth that Kami and Buddhas are fundamentally One, Buddhist terminology was deliberately employed so as to be readily understood by the ears of contemporaries.
On the first day of the First Month of the 25th year of Meiji (1892), when the Great Deity Ushitora no Konjin possessed Foundress Nao Deguchi for the very first time, His mighty roar resounded:
"The plum blossoms open all at once across the Three Thousand Worlds; the Age of Ushitora no Konjin hath arrived! Seated upon Mount Sumeru, Ushitora no Konjin shall safeguard the world, and so forth."
Here, "Three Thousand Worlds" (Sanzen-sekai) is a term belonging unto Buddhism, nor is "Ushitora no Konjin" an orthodox Shinto expression. "Mount Sumeru" (Shumisen-zan) is the supreme sacred mountain held in highest veneration by Buddhist scholars. Furthermore, that such terms as Miroku Bodhisattva, the Dragon Palace (Ryūgū), Dragon Deities (Ryūjin), Heavenly Devas (Tenshi), and Kings (Ō) appear throughout the text is entirely because they were proclaimed by borrowing the familiar language of Buddhism.
Therefore, the "Kings" mentioned in the Ofudesaki are abbreviations referring unto the Eight Great Dragon Kings and the diverse Buddha-Kings; and "Tenshi" (Heavenly Sons/Devas) naturally referreth unto Myōgetsu Tenshi, Fukō Tenshi, Hōkō Tenshi, the Four Heavenly Kings, and other diverse celestial devas and heavenly kings. There exist manifold Buddhas bearing the title of "King"—such as Jizai Tenshi, Dai-Jizai Tenshi, and Bonten-ō; and it is expounded that Buddhas and Kami are of one identical essence, springing originally from a single root. Moreover, seeing that Dai-Jizai Tenshi is accompanied by his retinue of thirty thousand devas (tenshi), these "Tenshi" are none other than what Shinto termeth Kamiko (divine children) or Shinshi (divine messengers). In essence, they signify devout and exalted believers who excel in the Way of the Gods and the Way of the Buddhas. Furthermore, in Christianity these Tenshi are spoken of as Angels.
Because the Ofudesaki of Ōmoto was revealed from the very inception of the Foundress's spiritual awakening through Buddhist terminology, unless one elucidateth it through its intimate relation with Buddhism, grave misunderstandings will inevitably arise.
Upon concluding the dictation of the five hundred and sixty-seven sections (the 567 Kototama number of Miroku) associated with Maitreya Bodhisattva, this Onisaburo hath set forth the true position of Miroku Bodhisattva as revealed within Buddhism, and hath simultaneously explained herein that the Ofudesaki was revealed primarily through the medium of Buddhist vocabulary.
Ah, Kamunagara, may the divine spirit bestow boundless blessings!
November 4, Taishō 11 (1922)
(Revised by Onisaburo at Chiayi Public Hall, March 17, Shōwa 10 [1935])

このように見てくると、大本教祖（出口なお）の『筆先』というものは、根本においては「神の道」を説いたものでありながら、最初から「神仏一体」という宇宙の深遠なる神理に基づいて、当時の世の人々の耳に入りやすく理解しやすいように、仏教の用語をも用いて説かれたものであることが悟られるのである。
