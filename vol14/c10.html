第10章　牡丹餅〔560〕

§ 1

Scrambling up from the ravine, the four companions—Yajihiko, Katsuhiko, Yotahiko, and Rokukō—at length reached the sloping road of Ojika Mountain Pass.

Yaji: 'Aha! Here is the very stage where we performed the opening scene of our drama! That Wind Deity truly subjected us to an extraordinary ordeal!'

Roku: 'Ye brought that upon yourselves because ye wore three haori cloaks, and moreover donned garments meant for the upper body upon your lower halves!'

Yaji: 'Even so, the Gods declare: "Upper and lower united shall govern the world!" Furthermore, seeing I am clad in splendid garments bestowed by six divine envoys, there is no reason why the Gods should inflict punishment!'

Roku: 'Still, wearing a haori cloak in place of hakama trousers is somewhat questionable!'

Yaji: 'Yet we could hardly journey in the nude, so there was no helping it!'

Roku: 'Since record-breaking tempests blow across this pass from time to time, we must proceed with exceeding caution. When we descend this slope, next lies the Nineteenth Great Pass; before reaching it stretches a vast wilderness of some two or three leagues, where many dwellings stand clustered together. Shall we proceed thither and take our rest?'

Yaji: 'Let us do so! Yet to pass through a populated village in such bizarre attire would look passing strange. Canst thou devise no remedy?'

Roku: 'Well, I am but an attendant, whilst thou art a noble envoy of the faith. How would it be if we exchange our garments before traversing the village?'

Yaji: 'If thou art willing, 'twould be splendid! ...Bless me, Roku, hast thou stripped naked already? What an impatient fellow thou art!'

Roku: ''Tis the principle of "the crane's single cry—words and deeds matched in an instant"! If we dally, someone might pour cold water upon our resolve, ha-ha-ha!'

Thereupon Yajihiko exchanged garments with Rokukō, while Rokukō draped the three haori cloaks fore and aft like hakama trousers, binding them tightly around his waist with a vine-cord like a gourd.

Roku: 'Behold! A quick change worthy of a star actor of a thousand ryō! Yet though we speak of actors, the play is finished! Come, shall we proceed?'

So saying, they sped down the slope at a brisk run, arriving at last at the village at the foot of the mountains.

Katsu: 'The worms in my belly have begun clamoring mightily with hunger! If there be any eatery hereabouts, I would fain enter and fortify my stomach!'

Yaji and Yota: 'If such a place exists to our wish, 'twould be marvelous indeed!'

Roku: 'Nay, have no fear! A little farther ahead stands a modest tavern named Matsuya; and there, I assure ye, dwells a wondrous beauty!'

Yota: 'Splendid! 'Tis but one more sprint thither! Somehow my vigor hath suddenly revived!'

Speaking thus, the four quickened their pace and dashed forward.

Yaji: 'Aha! Here is Matsuya! At last we have safely reached our destination, ha-ha-ha! After such a spell, we may even feast our eyes upon Benzaiten (a goddess of beauty)!'

Yaji: 'Never mind Benzaiten—I would fain lay hands upon food speedily! At this pass, appetite thoroughly conquers romance!'

At the entrance of Matsuya a maidservant appeared.

Maid: 'Pardon, gentle guests! Are ye pilgrims unto Mount Caucasus? Ye must needs be weary from that steep ascent; prithee take tea and rest awhile!'

Yota: ''Tis unnecessary to urge us! Our party of four hath graced this threshold with the express purpose of resting at Matsuya! We shall take our ease; hast thou not some dainty morsel to eat?'

Maid Otake: 'Yea, yea! We have all manner of dishes! All dependeth upon your desire—and your coin!'

Yota: 'Tush! How loathsome that folk instantly harp on this! Coin, coin, ever coin! The corrupt atmosphere of the Ural Faith lingereth here indeed! Yet there is no help for it, for an empty stomach fighteth no battles!'

So saying, Yotahiko took the lead and bounded inside.

Maid: 'Mercy on us! Those two gentlemen yonder—ho-ho-ho-ho—what extraordinary attire ye wear!'

Yota: 'Bizarre or not, we ask thee not to fall in love with us, nor do we beg thee to lend us robes; so bridle thy tongue and make haste to serve something delicious!'

From the rear of the shop an elderly matron tottered forth with jerky steps.

Crone: 'Welcome, welcome, honorable guests! Thank you for favoring us with your company! Speak your desires and command whatever ye please!'

Roku: 'Hast thou any botamochi (sweet rice dumplings)?'

Crone: 'Yea, yea, we have plenty! We have just prepared dumplings for the Equinox festival—piping hot, plump and steaming, shaped so bountifully as to overflow from the hands!'

Yaji: 'I wager the first batch thou didst knead is black as pitch!'

Crone: 'Heaven forbid! They are pure beyond pure, dumplings of absolute cleanliness! For those who relish not sweet dumplings, we have rice balls here. Ye seem travelers from a distant province, yet exceedingly swift of foot—stout walkers sturdy even as dumplings! Pilgrims unto Mount Caucasus pass daily, yet few are the dumpling-guests; rice-ball guests are far more numerous, o-ho-ho!'

Yaji: 'Old dame, what meanest thou by dumpling-guests and rice-ball guests? Art thou mocking me, implying my face is shapeless as a dumpling?'

Crone: 'O-ho-ho! 'Tis but a parable! A rice ball is round, whilst a dumpling hath somewhat uneven corners. Once upon a time, a rice ball and a sweet dumpling ran a marathon race. Now, the round rice ball ought by rights to have won, yet halfway through it collapsed utterly; wherefore the dumpling at last safely reached the finish line and claimed the glorious championship banner! Then came a steamed bun and asked, "Friend Dumpling, at first glance thou art so heavy and clumsy that thou seemedst slow of foot; how camest thou to win the victory?" Unto which the dumpling replied: "Because I am clad in azuki beans (I have azuketa—entrusted my safety unto heaven), my journey was free of anxiety!" O-ho-ho!'

Yaji: 'Well, of all things! To spin a witty jest whilst our stomachs are completely empty—what a care-free old crone thou art!'

Crone: 'By telling such a tale I beguile the time for our guests while their hunger sharpens, and in the meantime I boil the rice dumplings to serve you—such is my crafty scheme! Wait but a moment longer; the rice pot is now boiling over, and presently I shall clothe them in bean paste and let you feast in boundless abundance!'

Yota: 'Aha! Hearing of dumplings, the worms in my throat have begun rumbling with fierce demand! Serve them quickly, no matter how!'

Yaji: 'Hark, Yotakō! Yonder maiden is an exquisite beauty! How sayest thou? Doth she strike thy fancy?'

Yota: 'Look ye, seeing there are nations that have nationalized women, why should not we four, through a four-power concert, make her our joint property? Might we not take turns each evening to embrace (empress) that rare jewel and take our delight?'

Katsu: 'If thou attemptest such an embrace, seeing that popular maiden is the pride of this village, thou wilt surely become the focus of all villagers' wrath! And yet, viewed whether from the side or front, she is an impeccable maiden possessing the thirty-three marks of beauty! Having been born a man, I am not without the urge to embrace her at least once; yet being austere envoys of the Sango Faith, we can do naught! It feeleth truly like entering a mountain of treasures and returning bare-handed!'

§ 4

Crone: 'Come, come, gentlemen! The sweet dumplings are ready! Prithee partake!'

Yota: 'Thank you, thank you indeed! Let us dine at our ease!'

Crone: 'Now, let me serve you!'

Yota: 'Aye, aye, aye! That is exceedingly gracious, yet if it be all the same, if that fair maid Otake were to dish them up, methinks they would taste all the more exquisite!'

Crone: 'Ho-ho-ho-ho! Thou art quite a worldly connoisseur! If my astringent-bark-like hands dish up the dumplings, they will scarce suit thy fancy! If my serving doth not please thee, I flatly decline to let thee eat a single dumpling!'

Yaji: 'Hold, hold, pray wait! 'Twas but a jest; take it not so earnestly!'

Crone: '"From jests spring steeds—from gourds leap horses"! With a face like a scorched green gourd, complaining that an old crone suits thee not, babbling of soft-shelled turtles—what insolence is this? Despise not the aged! Mortal man gains true value only as years advance and moss gathers upon him!'

Katsu: 'Indeed, indeed, entirely right!'

Crone: 'Then help yourselves as ye please!'

So saying, the old crone retired inside with a huff. The four stretched forth hands like rakes, devouring them ravenously like famished tigers.

All Four: 'O wondrous feast! Such delicious dumplings have we never tasted since our navel-cords were severed! For such fare, we care not if our bellies burst! Even a hundred or two hundred, the worms in our throats would swallow them with cries of "Well done, well done!"'

In a single breath they devoured the mountain of dumplings piled high upon the platters.

Maid Otake: 'Gentlemen, what prodigious appetites! Ye will upset the rice market! Would ye have another helping?'

Yota: '"A dispute in a mochi shop—mochiron (of course)"! Bring them forth speedily, bring them forth!'

Otake: 'Mercy on us! Ye gentlemen are like horned cattle behind the stable bar—mō, mō, mō (utterly, utterly) astounded am I!'

Yaji: 'Never mind that, make haste to bring them! Though the worms in our belly seem appeased, our eyes and tongue are still driven by envious longing! They belong to the very same body; to pamper only the belly while casting eyes and tongue outside the pale would prove us devoid of compassion as divine envoys, ha-ha-ha!'

§ 5

Otake: 'Here, here! Another helping is ready! Prithee enjoy them at your leisure!'

Forming an allied army of two pairs of chopsticks in double column, the four fell once more upon the bomb-like dumplings, devouring them with gusto.

Yota: 'Hark, Otake! Take us not for fools! Thou didst serve exquisite samples at first, yet these taste exceedingly poor! Wilt thou not serve such as the first batch? Arranging sweet ones on top while stacking inferior dumplings below—my discerning tongue seeth through thy deceit!'

Otake: 'What nonsense art thou babbling? Delicious or poor, they were all prepared of the very same batch! They tasted wondrous because thou wast famished! Once thy belly is stuffed, nothing tasteth good! If thou utterest such complaints, thou hast no right to eat; thy palate is regrettably paralyzed! Come, having eaten thy fill, pay the reckoning!'

Roku (drawing coin from his bosom): 'Here, take it! No change is needed—keep the surplus for thy pin-money!'

Otake: 'Hearty thanks unto thee!'

Yet the moment she raised her eyes to look upon his face:

Otake: 'Good heavens! Art thou Roku-san?!'

And tumbling in utter shock, she vanished toward the back door!

Yota: 'Hark, Roku, thou art a rogue steeped in guilt! There is surely some secret behind this! Wherefore didst thou insist so stubbornly on visiting Matsuya? What an elusive hypocrite thou art! Flaunting thy past romance before us—make not fools of us!'

Roku: 'Never mind that! Retreat, retreat!'

Rustling his haori-hakama skirts, he bolted headlong at full speed. The three had no choice but to shout:

Three: 'Hold, Roku! Wait!'

Glancing back over his shoulder, Rokukō cried:

Roku: '"Wait (matta)" or "Matsuya"—what care I? Wilt ye not come along at once?'

And he ran on, panting for breath. The three muttered:

Three: 'Alas, what an inexplicable sudden uproar! There is no help for it; let us follow leisurely!'

And shaking their bellies distended with sweet dumplings, they gave chase in the wake of Rokukō.
