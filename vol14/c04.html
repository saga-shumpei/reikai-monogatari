Chapter 4: Bitter Vituperation [554]

第4章　馬詈〔554〕

§ 1

The divine Hinodewake, leading the five Preaching Emissaries and Roku-kō who had been resting upon the summit of Saruyama Pass, rode valiantly into the gorge of Ojika Pass, their horses' hooves sounding spiritedly as they advanced abreast.

Here upon the fine sand at the bottom of the ravine, Otohiko, Yajihiko, and Yotahiko lay side by side, unconscious. The Preaching Emissaries took turns scooping river water into their mouths and blowing it across the faces of the three. Otohiko groaned deeply and sat up:

Otohiko: 'Hark, where in the world is this place? I thought we had crossed the Sanzu River and advanced unto the Eight Crossways of Heaven; when did this river appear? Hath the Sanzu River moved its residence hither?'

Iwahiko: 'Look here, Otohiko, thou hast been lying unconscious! This is the bottom of the ravine at Ojika Pass! Come, gather thy senses!'

Otohiko: 'Ah, was it so indeed? By a hair's breadth I was on the verge of taking a nether excursion and exploring the infernal realms! How wondrously ye have arrived to deliver us! It seemeth I still have sacred tasks to perform in the mortal world.'

Iwahiko: 'Yea, indeed thou hast! How could we endure thy removal unto another realm at such a time? Rouse thyself! Henceforth we must press forward afar to the capital of Fusa and proceed toward Mount Caucasus. Shouldst thou collapse by the wayside, it would be an ill omen for our journey!'

Otohiko rubbed his eyes and said:

Otohiko: 'Ah, Lord Hinodewake and all our companions, we meet in a strange place indeed! Yea, we are deeply indebted for your deliverance!'

Iwahiko: 'Otohiko hath at length revived, but the other two have not yet returned their souls. Brethren, let us join our voices together and chant the soul-calling incantation!'

The assembly raised their voices in unison, chanting the sacred enumeration: 'One, Two, Three, Four, Five, Six, Seven, Eight, Nine, Ten, Hundred, Thousand, Ten Thousand...' four or five times. Thereupon Yajihiko and Yotahiko stirred and began to move.

Otohiko: 'Look alive, Yajihiko! Open thine eyes, Yotahiko!'

Yajihiko: 'Thou knavish Copper-Wood Statue, though thou changest thy tricks and disguises to beguile us, thinkest thou I shall fall into thy trap? Hearken, rogue Gengorō, I am Master Yaji, who took the very place of the demon hag by the Sanzu River! Wilt thou not repent in good earnest?'

Iwahiko: 'Look here, Master Yaji, come to thy senses!'

Iwahiko: 'Look here, Master Yota, come to thy senses!'

Yotahiko: 'What babblest thou, thou phantom monster of Gengorō?'

Otohiko: 'Ho there, Yajihiko and Yotahiko, this is no Netherworld! This is the gorge of Ojika Pass!'

Yajihiko: 'Bah, make no fool of me! At Ojika Pass we long ago performed an aerial leap and successfully passed into the spirit realm! Thence we crossed the Sanzu River, and have but now routed the Copper-Wood Colossus at the Heavenly Eight Crossways! However much of a ghost one hath become, who ever heard of tumbling back into the mortal world? I live in the present moment; I despise turning back a single step!'

§ 2

Otohiko: 'Alas, what a vexing plight! It appeareth they are still immersed in the frame of mind of departed ghosts! Look here, Yaji and Yota, ye are by no means dead! Ye have returned alive!'

Yajihiko: 'Utter no nonsense! Is there any precedent for a dead man dying twice? What talk is this of returning or rebounding to life? Cast away thy delusive attachments of the Asura realm, and let us journey across the myriad Buddha lands! Having crossed into this Nether Realm where the manifest and hidden worlds diverge, however much thou mayest long for mortal life, we must needs travel as far as our path leadeth! Now we dwell in the Intermediate State (Chūu); presently the State of Rebirth (Shōu) shall arrive. Until then, obey the regulations of the Netherworld and travel meekly! What sayest thou, Yota?'

Yotahiko: 'Look here, Yaji, is there not something strange? Somehow the air smelleth rankly of the mortal world! The divine Hinodewake is present before us, and a multitude of Preaching Emissaries are assembled! Wilt thou not open thine eyes in good season?'

Yajihiko: 'Talk not such foolishness! Hinodewake and his party departed on their nether journey even before us! Did they not appear just after that monstrous Copper-Wood Statue bumped its head against the sun and fled away?'

Iwahiko: 'The water we blew upon him was clearly insufficient! What if we plunge his whole body into this river like an eggplant pickled in brine?'

Yotahiko cried out with a weeping wail:

Yotahiko: 'I pray you, worthy Emissary, after we have been saved with such pains, if ye do such a deed, he will turn into a drowned corpse! For pity's sake, forgive him that! Ah, why is Yajihiko such a blockhead? My excessive affection for him hath turned to bitter exasperation!'

And Yotahiko twisted Yajihiko’s nose with all his might.

Yajihiko: 'Ouch, ouch, ow! Even after reaching the Netherworld, wilt thou not repent, twisting my nose like this? Thou shalt surely be condemned to the nose-torture of Hell!'

All: 'Ha-ha-ha-ha!'

Yajihiko: 'What is this? While a man is suffering from having his nose wrenched, how can holy Emissaries of God laugh so merrily? I would gladly take thy life as a companion on the road to the other world, but seeing thou art already dead, there is no life to take! Bah, what a vexation! If I meet a demon, I shall report all your misdeeds!'

Yotahiko: 'Ugh, what a hopeless fool! Failing to revive properly, his soul hath moved out of its wits and he hath gone clean mad!'

So saying, he clenched his fist and struck Yajihiko squarely across the cheek. With the force of the blow, Yajihiko staggered unsteadily, tripped over a stone, and tumbled flat onto the ground.

Yajihiko: 'Ouch, ouch, ow! I can still feel pain after all! Can it be that I am still in the mortal world? Why, Lord Hinodewake, Taka-san, Iwa-san, Ume-san, Koma-san, and Oto-san, Yotahiko, and yet one other beast...'

Otohiko: 'Alas, that is thy chronic failing! The moment thou comest to thy senses, thou referrest to a fellow man as a "beast"—what a foul-mouthed rascal thou art!'

Yajihiko: 'Aha! So it was true after all! We were at the bottom of the gorge at Ojika Pass!'

All: 'He hath awakened! He hath recovered! Come, let us recite prayers in thanksgiving!'

And with that, stripping themselves naked, they plunged into the stream, performed sacred ablution (misogi), and recited the Heavenly Prayer.

Hinodewake: 'Lo, we have spent unexpected time! The divine ministry at Mount Caucasus is urgent! We shall take our leave ahead of you; pray follow us at your leisure!'

So saying, he gathered the reins of his divine steed, and with bells and bits jingling gallantly, ascended straight into the firmament, ringing 'shan-ko, shan-ko' as he soared away.

Iwahiko: 'Behold, as expected of the divine Hinodewake! Such a miraculous feat as a heavenly steed coursing through the sky is far beyond the hope of feeble emissaries like us! Brethren, let us hasten unto the capital of Fusa! Yajihiko, Yotahiko, and that other gentleman, pray follow slowly behind us!'

The six Preaching Emissaries drew their reins abreast, preparing to gallop away. Yajihiko seized the horse's bit firmly:

Yajihiko: 'Hold, hold! What will ye do with us two naked men?'

Iwahiko: 'Shall we each take off an upper garment and lend them unto you?'

All: ''Tis well indeed!'

And stripping off their robes one by one, they spoke:

All: 'Ye three gentlemen, pray follow slowly at your leisure! Ye walk upon two legs while we ride upon four; ye can by no means overtake us! We shall await you in the capital of Fusa!'

And whipping their swift steeds, they vanished like dust and clouds.

Yajihiko: 'Ah, how strange is the world! That demon crone of the Sanzu River seized us naked wretches and demanded that if we had no garments we should yield up our hereditary skin-coats; yet truly as expected of Sango emissaries, they stripped off their own splendid garments and bestowed them upon us without a trace of regret! Hark, Yota, what use is it to receive naught but haori coats? We have neither sashes nor trousers, and unluckily possessing neither needle nor thread, we cannot even tailor them! Ah, this is why bachelor life is such a vexation! Though I have a wife shaped like a green gourd, unless I return unto Takatori Village I cannot see her face; had we but a telephone I might ring her up and summon her hither, but alas, there is no help for it!'

Ah, shall I perform an act of charity by crossing the Forty-Eight Slopes, making the Wild Horse Gorge a reality, and leaving my name unto future generations?'

Yajihiko: 'This is outrageous! We cannot carelessly mount such a beast!'

The Horse: 'As the proverb hath it: "Live with a man to know him; ride upon a horse to try him!" Come, mount quickly! Night after night in the dark, dodging thy wife's watchful eyes to filch money, thou wast locked up in the lantern-storeroom, and ended up bringing home a "horse" (failing to pay thy debts)! To be escorted by a horse is the very fulfillment of thy desires! Neigh, neigh, neigh!'

The Three: 'Silence thy noisy chattering! If thou pleadest so earnestly, we will mount thee!'

And they sprang forward, leaping onto the horses.

Yajihiko: 'Aha! My calculation was wrong—this fellow hath testicles (a stallion)! If I must ride, shall I change to a mare? As the proverb hath it: "Mend (remount) thine errors!"'

The Horse: 'Heave-ho, not so fast! 'Tis a horse embarked upon, like a launched ship! Now that thou art mounted, thou art wholly in my power! Like Gengorō of the Iron Grasp, when we reach the steep precipice I shall rear my forelegs, turn a somersault, and crush thy belly beneath my back! Aha, what sport, what glorious sport!'

Yotahiko: 'Mine is a mare! She seemeth reasonably gentle!'

Roku: 'Mine is a mare too!'

Yajihiko: 'Hold thy noisy tongue! While the horse neigheth hin-hin and I am distressed at drawing the unlucky short straw (poverty ticket), chatter not thou too with such neighing—it lacketh all dignity (hin)!'

The host of horses raised their voices in unison:

'Neigh, neigh, neigh! Neigh, neigh, neigh!'
