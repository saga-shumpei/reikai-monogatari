Chapter 11: The Kappa's Fart [561]

第11章　河童の屁〔561〕

§ 1

With Katsuhiko the divine envoy at their head, Yajihiko and Yotahiko pursued Rokukō and at length arrived at the foot of the Twentieth Pass.

Yota: 'Now we enter the danger zone once more! The hour approacheth when we must pierce the enemy's defensive lines, fight valiantly, employ our secret arts, and manifest the mighty power of the Divine Army!'

Katsu: 'Indeed, let each gird his loin-sash firmly and climb the slope watching in every direction. Yet whither hath that fellow Rokukō vanished? With only three of us, the balance of our conversation is quite undone; he is ever such an impetuous scatterbrain!'

Yaji: 'The moment he beheld Otake's face, the two scattered in opposite directions like startled cats after mating! The comical sight of it! I warrant some tangled romance lieth concealed beneath!'

Yota: 'What? Romance? How could such a creature ever have a romance?'

Yaji: 'Despise him not so! "Affinity is a wondrous and mysterious thing!" Presently Rokukō will appear leading Otake, and she will cling unto him weeping, crying: "O Roku-san, where hast thou been? What meaneth this strange hakama, and these wondrous robes?" Whereupon Rokukō will reply: "Ah, Otake, whatever betide, resign thyself that 'tis all bound by karma! Today's skies differ from yesterday's; our sorrowful path of love escapeth not the shifting ways of this fleeting world—alas, Heaven itself is deaf!" Thus enacting a tragic scene along the path, they will at length reconcile, take each other by the hand, ascend unto the Twentieth Pass, and boast: "Behold the realm's foremost gallant! Pledged as birds with wings intertwined, our love can never be severed!"—swaggering forth with outstretched necks, ha-ha-ha!'

Yota: 'Uff-fu-fu! Cease uttering such foolishness! My belly is convulsed with laughter, and my knees are tottering under me!'

Yaji: 'Master Katsuhiko, heed not this fellow whose knees are trembling with laughter; let us two press forward in harmony! A party of three leaves one man awkward and empty-handed, most inconvenient for walking in conversation!'

Yota: 'Humph! 'Twould delight thee to leave me behind and forge ahead together! Being a journey of four-legged beasts, make haste and nibble the roadside grass, or piddle against a stone Jizō!'

Yaji: 'Hark! Insult us not! Telling us to graze like cattle or horses, or piddle like dogs—carry not thy mockery too far!'

Yota: 'Aha! What a faint-hearted creature thou art! If thou takest offense at such trifles, how canst thou serve as an attendant unto a divine envoy? Thou phantom of the mortal sphere, if thou jabberest further, thou wilt slip from the pine bough once more and dislocate thy hips! This is Ojika Pass, but hereafter men will name it "Yajihiko's Hip-Dislocation Pass"! Forward, Master Coward, proceed as thou wilt! Between thee and me, diplomatic relations are severed as of this moment! I shall issue thee a passport; board the train and return post-haste unto thine own country!'

Yaji: 'Ha-ha-ha! Yota, what a pompous face thou makest! Glaring with acorn eyes like a night-hawk (yotaka—street-walking courtesan) and sharpening thy beak—what an unsightly visage! Henceforth thou hadst best change thy name from Yota to Yotaka! A night-hawk is a creature who drapes a straw mat over her head and stands at dark crossroads!'

Yota: 'Ah, "so ka" (dost thou call me a sōka—harlot)? What insolence!'

Yaji: 'The situation between thee and me hath grown precarious! Come, before the exalted presence of Katsuhiko the divine envoy, let us conclude a treaty for the restoration of peace!'

So saying, he hitched up his skirts, crying:

Yaji: 'Come, let us break wind—a fart of peace!'

Yota: 'Ha-ha-ha! A fart is ever accompanied by a smiling face! Yet as for thy fart, having stored away such an enormous cargo of sweet dumplings, gas hath generated with terrifying fury, discharging a bizarre, revolting stench so thick one must needs turn away—an odor foulest among the foul! Ugh, how repulsive! To live near thy rump is like serving a life sentence inside a privy; I am brimming with grievance!'

Yaji: 'Nay, mine is the supreme masterpiece of odors! It billoweth like fragrant musk! Stench is the very soul of a fart; an odorless fart hath already forfeited all qualification as a fart! Farts devoid of smell like the fart of a Kappa (water-goblin) are merely rotten, stale farts! If thou breakest wind, break a living fart; a dead fart bringeth ill omen!'

Yota: 'Thy fart is a pilgrimage fart unto the Grand Shrine of Ise! Discharging a hard fart, 'tis "Ishibe"; its roar is "Ōtsu"; its stench is "Kusatsu"; and in truth, 'tis full of vigor ("Ise"), ha-ha-ha!'

Yaji: 'A master of martial arts and grand strategist—that is Yajihiko! Presently I intend to erect an artillery arsenal, manufacture cannon, deploy grand batteries, and commence battle preparations, ha-ha-ha!'

Yota: 'Hark, Yajibei! Henceforth I bestow upon thee the honorable titles of Heisuke (Soldier-boy), Bunsuke, and Kyūsuke! Receive them with grateful reverence!'

Yaji: 'Aye, aye, hearty thanks! I accept them with reverence! Matters standing thus, between us twain, even as the wind looseth the bow and looseth the stern, pushing forth a colossal ship resting upon the shores of Ōtsu into the boundless ocean, the gentle breeze of Heiwa (peace—fart) softly caresseth the spring sea, bringing Great Peace (taihei) under Heaven, and at last completing the realm of Peace! At length we behold the dawn of restored peace! Even if one farteth at eighty-eight shrines of Shikoku, chanting Namu (no matter how many times), 'tis Daishi Henjō Kongō, ha-ha-ha!'

Yota: 'Hark, Katsuhiko-san! Thou art exceedingly grave of countenance! Doth such merry jesting move thee not in the least?'

Katsu: 'Find ye such worthless, fart-like trifles amusing? To stir up a petty quarrel like a fart bubbling underwater, and then talk of restoring peace—enveloping folk in a smoke of flatulence, I am utterly struck dumb and bowed in embarrassment!'

Yaji: 'This Yotakō is a faint-hearted fellow with wobbly hips; once we go a little farther, he will surely collapse in surrender, ha-ha-ha!'

Yota: 'Thou art Yajirobei himself from the Tokaido Shanks' Mare—a farting old simpleton! If thou ridest thy high horse too far, thou wilt become a public nuisance; thou hadst better shut the gate of thy muzzle, ha-ha-ha!'

Katsu: ''Tis as though I journeyed with a weasel, a horse, and a stink-bug! O-ho-ho!'

Yaji: 'Look ye! Engrossed in our talk, we have arrived at the summit unawares! Here too a smooth road openeth out. Gazing afar, a boundless plateau stretcheth beyond the reach of sight—truly a realm of Great Peace under Heaven, ha-ha-ha!'

Katsu and Yota: 'Uh-ho-ho!'

Yaji: 'Bless me, my belly hath begun to feel somewhat strange! Pray excuse me for a moment yonder! Sit ye down here and wait awhile; pardon me!'

And with scampering steps, he vanished into a thicket of trees.

Yota: 'Ha-ha-ha! He hath gone to plant a sweet potato somewhere—a colossal one, ha-ha-ha! Ugh, my stomach turneth; those sweet dumplings we devoured feel ready to come up!'

From the forest on one side arose groans of "Ugh, ugh!" and from the thicket where Yajihiko had hidden arose likewise groans of "Ugh, ugh!"

Yota: 'Mercy on us! This is intolerable! 'Tis as though we were pincer-attacked by enemies from right and left! Who are those wretches groaning "Ugh, ugh"?'
