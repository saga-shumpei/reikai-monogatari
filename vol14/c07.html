第7章　難風〔557〕

§ 1

Up the steep ascent of Ojika Pass, the company of four—Yajihiko, Yotahiko, Katsuhiko, and Roku-kō—tripped over rocks, caught their feet in tree roots, tumbling right and staggering left, singing improvised propagation songs full of rustic flavor as freely as wine pouring from a bottle. Along the path gouged out by rainfall, stumbling on unsteady, reeling legs, gasping and panting they climbed. "Dust amassed maketh a mountain"; with unremitting steps ascending one foot at a time up the Forty-Eight Slopes, Katsuhiko, who boasted this climb as his pet specialty (his eighteenth favorite tune), at length clambered to the summit. Wheezing like a soft-shelled turtle chewing smartweed, having no mother near yet panting "haa-haa" (mother-mother), he staggered forward.

Katsuhiko: 'Brethren, at this vantage point of glorious prospect, what if we halt our compasses (legs) awhile to nurture our noble spirit (kōzen-no-ki)?'

Yajihiko: 'Indeed, without hesitation or apology to anyone, let us rest openly! As the proverb saith, "Empty not the heavens of their boundless spirit!" Yet while we rest, shall we load coal into our boilers (break bread)? Such a convenient harbor is scarce to be found ahead; our engine oil hath well-nigh run dry!'

Katsuhiko: 'Being cooped up in such a rigid depot (the cavern), my storehouse is utterly barren! What can we swallow? Possessing no raw materials whatever, there is no help for it! The worms in my belly have charged up to my throat, sounding their steam whistles incessantly! I would fain take on water to rinse my throat, but unluckily the ravine is deep, and our belly's plight is brought to a standstill in the gorge! Hast thou any suitable design (belly-scheme)?'

Yajihiko: 'Aha! Here are two miserable rice balls, colored as though scorched without fire—a metaphysical luncheon received as a keepsake from the demon crone of the Sanzu River! It requireth neither the toil of chewing nor the burden of troubling the five viscera and six entrails! Shall we feast on these by swallowing our saliva and fancy we have dined?'

Yotahiko: 'Ugh, Yajihiko, how repulsive! Thou seemest still unable to shake off the delusions of the mortal world—nay, hold, the delusions of the Netherworld! Chattering about that hag and her snotty rice balls, what an unclean fellow thou art! Wilt thou not cast it off in good measure?'

Yajihiko: '"Though trees to fell upon the mountain abound, there is no wood (resolve) to cut it off!"... That hag's loathsome countenance, with teeth encrusted with grime jutting forth, clamoring, "Hand over thy hereditary skin-coat!"—how could I ever banish such a visage from my thoughts? Every time I swallow rice, I recall those rice balls and my gorge riseth!'

Yotahiko: 'Thy Yaji-style is incurable! If she was so frightful a hag, why didst thou enact such an unseemly, bizarre romance with her, promising to be reborn upon the same lotus and keeping half thy seat vacant for her? Thou art an inscrutable blockhead!'

Yajihiko: ''Tis obvious; for I am the peerless gallant of the realm! Young or old, male or female, noble or mean, rich or poor, every Tom, Dick, and Harry, weasels, turtles, snails, slugs, horses, and cattle—all succumb before Master Yaji! Ah, what a wearisome burden it is to be such a popular gallant! Even in the Netherworld, that merciless Clothes-Stripping Hag beamed intense electromagnetic waves upon me; an irresistible gallant is truly a singular being! Katsuhiko: 'Ha-ha-ha! What a comical porter—nay, hold, what an astounding popular gallant we have encountered!'
