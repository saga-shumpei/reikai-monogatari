§ 1

Swept from the crest of the Twenty-Fifth Pass by a furious hurricane and plunged into the glen, Katsuhiko's company revived, arose, and gazed into one another's visages.

Katsu: 'Aha! Here is the bottom of Ojika Glen! Fancying I had been battling the evil spirits of Tokoyo-hime and Kotsune-hime in a solitary thatched cottage by the pine grove of the Itto River—was it but a dream after all?'

Yaji: 'Master Envoy, didst thou behold that dream as well? I saw it too! What a hideous hag! Face purple and swollen from brows to nose—a repulsive spectacle of Tokoyo-hime reclining, which chilled me to the marrow! And that middle-aged hag in her light-blue robe marked with the star-crest—the memory maketh my flesh creep! And Yotakō peeping through the lattice, cutting capers like a strolling actor—'twas comical, terrifying, sickening, and infuriating all at once, until the spirit of the Sango Faith fled, leaving no room to re-see, re-hear, or re-rectify! Hark, Yotakō and Rokukō, how was it with you? Ye were actors in that dream!'

Yota: 'Mine was much the same! The hag was babbling of the Itto River and being resolute in desires! A thoroughly demented old crone indeed!'

Roku: 'When the demon hag charged with her carving knife, seeing we were empty-handed while she held a blade, my heart leapt into my mouth—and at that instant I awoke! What a foul dream! Since men speak of this fleeting world as a dream, somewhere such reality may well exist!'

Yaji: 'A dream is a sacred thing! It occurs when we cast off all worldly bonds and abandon ourselves innocently unto the workings of our Guardian Deities; wherefore what is shown in dreams surely cometh to pass whether in the past, the present, or the future!'

Roku: 'Could it be so? Is it of the past, or of the future?'

Katsu: 'The fulfillment of this dream belongeth unto tens of myriads of years in the future! When that era of rampant demons known as the Twentieth Century arriveth, the dark spirits of the Eight-Headed Serpent and Nine-Tailed Golden Fox will stir anew, seizing bodies prone to possession by Tokoyo-hime and Kotsune-hime! Inquiring through divine clairvoyance, toward the northeast from here, upon the parkland of the Gods, the Male and Female of our dream shall manifest to commence the works of the Miroku Age; wherefore evil spirits will possess vessels of the henjō nanshi lineage, masking themselves in virtue to devour the children of the faith and snatch away their jewels of life! Alas, how piteous are mortals of the Twentieth Century! One cannot but contemplate the heart-rending agony of the Izunome, Mizu-no-Mitama, Kin-katsu-kane, and Konohana-hime! Kamunagara Tamachihaemase, Kamunagara Tamachihaemase!'

Yota: 'For the salvation of all living beings across past, present, and future, seeing we have fallen beside this pristine stream, let us perform misogi, chant the sacred words, and pray for the fleshly shrines where abide Kin-katsu-kane, Konohana-hime, and the Male and Female who are the grand pillars of the Miroku Divine Reign! That this world may endure for myriad kalpas, blooming with flowers of unalloyed good!'

Katsu: 'I heartily approve! Brethren, let us immediately act upon Yotahiko's proposal!'

Yaji and Roku: 'We agree as well!'

Casting off their garments by the riverbank, they plunged headlong into the stream with a resounding splash. While the four were immersed, purifying their flesh, Rokukō sank beneath the surface with a bubbling murmur and vanished into the depths. Clasping their hands with wholehearted devotion, Katsuhiko and the other two completed the Amatsu-Norito facing upstream; yet when they glanced aside, Rokukō was nowhere to be seen!

Katsu: 'Heavens! Whither hath Roku-san gone? O-ho! Roku-san, where art thou?!'

Though they shouted and called, no reply returned, save the roaring turbulence of dashing waves. Yajihiko cried:

Yaji: 'Disaster! Rokukō hath sunk out of sight! We cannot stand idle; we must search for him! If he swalloweth water and drowneth, 'twill be past all remedy! Hark, Yotakō, what shall we do?'

Yota: 'What can we do? He must be playing at being a submarine, lying submerged on the river bottom! He is a master of swimming; there is no fear of his drowning! When thou wast caught in the pine tree, he swam across this very torrent in full clothing without turning a hair! He is merely trying to startle us in jest!'

Yaji: 'No matter how skilled a swimmer, one cannot be careless! Men say, "He who swimmeth well drowneth well!" In my judgment, he hath surely changed his name!'

Yota: 'Changed his name? What meanest thou?'

Yaji: 'He hath surely renamed himself Kawasoko Dozaemon—a drowned corpse at the river bottom!'

烏勘三郎『はい、左様でございます。六さんを連れて参りました！』

弥次彦『それはそれは有難い、ご苦労だった！ 六さんは物を言いますかな、いや、まだ生きておりますか』

烏勘三郎『物も言わず動きもしませんが、身体の一部に温もりがありますので、火でも焚いて温めたなら息を吹き返すかも知れぬと考え、プカプカと流れて来るのを我々一同が命がけで川へ飛び込み、拾い上げて参りました』

勝彦『それは有難い。たった今、六公がここへやって来て、「今にお目にかかる」と言っておりましたよ』

烏勘三郎『妙ですなあ。先ほどここへ来ただなんて合点がいきません。そうするとこいつは六さんじゃないのかなあ、大方化け物でしょう。ええい、大変な苦労をさせおって、この古狸めが、叩きつけて踏み躙ってやろうか！』

弥次彦『まあまあ待った、待った！ そんな手荒な事をしてどうなるものか、それこそ本当に死んでしまうわい。そっとその辺りに下ろしてくれ、これから魂呼びの神業だ』

烏勘三郎『ああ何だかさっぱり訳が分からなくなって来たわい。まあ仕方がない、下ろそうかい』

と芝生の上にそっと下ろした。

弥次彦『おお六公、貴様は幸せ者だ。待て待て、今に魂返しをやってやろう。さあ宣伝使様、天の数歌を始めましょうか』

§ 7

Clapping his hands in silence, Katsuhiko chanted the Heavenly Counting Song with serene calm twice: 'Hito, Futa, Mi, Yo, Itsu, Muyu, Nana, Ya, Kokono, Tari, Momo, Chi, Yorozu.' Thereupon Rokukō's body began to stir, and sitting upright, he rubbed his eyes and peered into their visages:

Roku: 'Aha! Yajikō, Yotakō! Master Envoy, what a strange spot to meet! Having failed to cross the Sanzu River, I was on the brink of my second journey to the dead; yet Karasu Kanzaburō and his disciples plunged into the flood, fished me out, and carried me on his back unto this log bridge! Are ye still journeying through the Nether Realm? I fancied I was alone, yet what faithful companions ye are to follow me even in death! True friends are treasures indeed! Kamunagara Tamachihaemase, Kamunagara Tamachihaemase!'

Yajihiko gave Rokukō's back three or four resounding smacks with his open palm.

Roku: 'Ouch, ouch! What art thou doing? I knew thou wast a ruffian in the mortal world, but hast thou not reformed even in the Netherworld?!'

Yaji: 'This is not the Netherworld! 'Tis the plain beneath the Twenty-Fifth Pass! Thou wast drowned in the torrent and on the brink of death; yet through divine providence, Kanzaburō's men rescued thee and brought thee hither! Pull thyself together!'

Rubbing his eyes, Rokukō surveyed the surroundings with fresh wonder:

Roku: 'Aha! It seemeth indeed the mortal sphere! Good friends, forgive the sorrow I caused you, and accept my heartfelt gratitude! And Karasu Kanzaburō and all thy brethren—ye are the saviors of my life; never shall I forget this debt so long as I live!'

Kanzaburō: 'We rejoice that thou hast revived! Let us give thanks unto the Gods!'

Chanting the sacred words and singing propagation songs, the four envoys expressed their profound gratitude unto Kanzaburō and his troop; and crossing the log bridge, they pressed forward toward the Twenty-Sixth Pass.
