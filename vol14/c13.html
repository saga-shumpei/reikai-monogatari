Chapter 13: Quiet Sanctuary on the Mount [563]

第13章　山上幽斎〔563〕

§ 1

Overcoming the foul demonic tempests and diverse temptations of the world, Katsuhiko the divine envoy, accompanied by Yajihiko, Yotahiko, and Rokukō, traversed the hamlet of Koyama, surmounted three slopes beyond the Twentieth Pass, and at length climbed to the summit of the pass.

This mountain crest, unlike all the passes they had crossed before, unfolded into an exceedingly broad plateau. Below in the valley, the stream of Ojika River dashed against boulders like bleached white linen, scattering radiant spray. The mountain slopes were mostly sheer rock, while shrubs grew lush and green here and there, coloring the contours of stone with noble and sublime beauty.

Yaji: 'Having climbed the Twenty-Third Slope of Ojika Pass at last, nowhere have we found a plateau so vast as this summit! Far and near unto the four quarters, mountains and boundless plains lie beneath our single gaze; the breeze is pure, and somehow a breath of spring drifteth in the air. Shall we rest leisurely here awhile?'

Katsu: 'Indeed, that is well pleasing! Gazing in all directions from this height, the soul feeleth grand and exhilarated, as though grasping the very cosmos in one's hand! Let us speak comfortably of the Divine Realm; in such a pure place as this, no matter what sacred mysteries we utter, scarce any foul deities could ever launch an assault!'

So saying, he seated himself upon the green turf, and the three companions likewise reclined upon the lawn.

Yota: 'Ah, what a delightful sensation! Attaining a mountain summit is ever a singular joy, but today's felicity is beyond compare! Wert we to commence spiritual exercises of silent contemplation (Yūsai) at such an hour, surely benign deities would vouchsafe divine communion! ...Prithee, divine envoy, wilt thou not bestow upon us all the sacred rite of Chinkon Kishin (Spirit-Calming and Divine Return)?'

Katsu: ''Twould be splendid, yet unhappily upon this high plateau there is no water to wash our hands, rinse our mouths, or perform cold-water ablutions... this is our prime obstacle!'

Yaji: 'Yet the divine oracle saith: "The grime of the flesh may be washed away in the bath-tub, but that which cannot be cleansed is the defilement of the soul!" Even without water, could we not seek divine pardon and have our spirits laundered? Water cleanseth merely the dross of the body, whereas Chinkon purgeth the stains of the soul; wherefore, leaving the flesh aside for today, let us receive a cleansing of our spirits alone! Is it not so, Yotahiko and Rokukō?'

Katsu: 'Because spirit and flesh are one, and the visible and invisible worlds are undivided, in strict theory, wert the spirit cleansed, bodily ablutions might be dispensable; yet into an unclean vessel of flesh, how could a pristine divine spirit ever descend? Wert we to conduct Yūsai heedlessly in such a spot, we know not when evil deities protecting the Ural Faith might possess us! Lately in the Spirit Realm, the treacherous demons Tokoyo-hime, Kotsune-hime, Kuchiko-hime, and Yaso-magahiko—who of old fiercely rebelled against the Great Deity Kunitokotachi and forced Him into retirement—are seeking to possess persons of any repute to plot the overthrow of the Divine Realm! Having not a drop of water to cleanse our flesh, let us rather seek a waterfall or pure mountain brook to perform misogi before commencing our spiritual exercises!'

Yaji: 'Alas, what an inflexible soul! Would the Omniscient and Omnipotent Primal God entertain such narrow notions? Good and evil intermingling, beauty and ugliness combining—is it not thus that all creatures in heaven and earth generate power and begin their activities? In this world there is neither absolute good nor absolute evil! Though our spirit be clear as crystal, it must dwell within human clay wrapped in foul vapors; washing the exterior with water cannot cleanse the five viscera and six entrails! Thinking too deeply paralyzeth both hand and foot! Through the rectifying grace of Kamunaobi and Ōnaobi, re-see, re-hear, and re-declare, and commence this holy rite without fail! My nerves are stirred, and I yearn for divine possession beyond all endurance!'

Yajihiko's floating form circled in midair in rhythm with Katsuhiko's spinning finger, revolving in whatever quarter his finger pointed. Reaching forth his arm like a pair of compasses, Katsuhiko traced sweeping circles in the heavens; and Yajihiko's body soared and plunged, soared and plunged, enacting a breathtaking spectacle of aerial navigation!
