§ 1

The three mounted the fiery wild steeds, and bombarded by barrages of flatulence, galloped headlong up the precipitous slope of Ojika Pass.

Yajihiko: '"Even a long whip reacheth not the horse's belly!" Bah, 'tis a nuisance; a whip is utterly useless!'

So saying, he tossed his whip away from horseback without the slightest regret.

The Horse: 'Ignorant fool! What need hath a fellow who is three parts human and seven parts monster of a whip? Thou gluttonous captain of swilling and gorging, shall I rear upright here and now?'

Yajihiko: 'Thou knavish nag, cease thy insolent chatter! Dost thou not know Master Yajihiko, the peerless equestrian master of all ages? If thou babblest further, I shall squeeze thy ribs and choke thy breath!'

The Horse: 'Neigh-hee-hee-hee! How laughable! I feel not even the touch of a mosquito upon my back, thou vessel of foxes!'

Yajihiko: 'Ho, brute beast, thy tongue is far too insolent! Dost thou not know it is treasonous insolence against thy master?'

The Horse: 'Neigh-hee-hee-hee! Fine words indeed! Who ever heard of a man riding a horse without even putting on a bridle? Thou art truly an exquisite simpleton (tonma)!'

Yajihiko: 'The simpleton is thine own self!'

The Horse: 'Yajihiko, thou doltish knave! When Otake forced thee to drink urine at her inn, how comical thou wast, tilting thy horse-head in perplexity wondering if it were not horse piss! Thou art a knave who committeth blunder (he-ma) upon blunder! Small wonder they call thee a foolish horse-wit!'

Yajihiko: 'Ugh, what a prating fool! Having no bit or bridle, I am in somewhat of a fix!'

The Horse: 'If thou hast no bit, mount not! A wooden rocking-horse is good enough for thee! Nowadays men become meek enough if only one fitteth them with a golden bit (a bribe)! How sayest thou? Hast thou any gold?'

Yajihiko: 'Being a dunce like thee, of late my purse is utterly empty and pinched with poverty (hin-hin)! Ho, Yotahiko, how fareth thy mount?'

Yotahiko: 'My ride is smooth and delightful! I feel as though I were astride a gentle maiden-steed!'

Yajihiko: 'Alas, there is no help for it; 'tis my misfortune to have boarded such a frantic jade! Ho, Roku-san, how is it with thee?'

Roku: 'Mine is tolerably pleasant too; the ride is exceedingly fine! 'Tis an indescribably agreeable sensation, as though I were straddling the golden or silver dragon!'

Yajihiko: 'Ugh, vexatious (horse-vexing) indeed! I have drawn the short straw!'

The Horse: 'Hark, Yaji, seeing thou art a busybody (yaji-uma), thou hast only thyself to blame! Come, take an aerial glide from my back, crash upon the rocks, crack thy skull, and pay another visit to the myriad Buddha realms! After that, thou mayest well be fit to ride a decent horse!'

Yajihiko: 'Ugh, what a brazen-tongued rascal!'

§ 2

The horse shook its mane, glared with furious eyes, and opening its cavernous jaws, snapped fiercely to bite Yajihiko's arm.

Yajihiko: 'Hold, what art thou about, thou unruly jade? Thinkest thou I would let myself be bitten by the likes of thee?'

The Horse: 'Shall I stand upright on two legs? Since thou art a vessel of four-legged beasts, 'tis well that thou shouldst try riding a horse standing upright like a human for once!'

Yajihiko: 'Hark, behave as a horse ought! For a four-legged creature to walk upright is a violation of heaven's natural law!'

The Horse: 'Neigh, neigh, neigh! That applieth unto thine own self! While thou hast the soul of a four-legged beast, thou struttest haughtily on two legs—why shouldst thou marvel at a horse standing upright? Neigh, neigh, neigh!'

Yajihiko: 'Ugh, what a hopeless brute! I dismiss thee from my service! Henceforth I shall travel upon my hereditary shanks' mare! Huge in frame yet skilled only in breaking wind, thou simpleton nag! Halt, halt, I shall dismount!'

The Horse: 'I will not let thee dismount, nay, not here! 'Tis the wrong spot for dismounting! Tarry yet a little; ahead lieth a tremendous precipice whose bottom is covered thick with prickly smilax brambles! Unless I carry thee thither and cast thee down, the sport hath no flavor whatever!'

Yajihiko: 'This is a horse-plague of boundless annoyance; I bow my head in speechlessness (heikō-tonshu)!'

The Horse: 'Presently thou shalt drop dead in speechlessness (heikō-tonshi)!'

Yajihiko: 'What babblest thou? Cast me down if thou darest! Clinging fast unto thy mane, thou too shalt be buried together in the briars! When we reach the point where thou canst move no more like a dead nag, I will generously bestow upon thee a lavish tip!'

The Horse: 'Now the dance beginneth: One, two, three!'

Yajihiko: 'Hold, ramp not so wildly! Be quiet! If thou ragest, I will bite into thee and suck thy blood—how wilt thou like that?'

The Horse: 'I thought thou wast but a puny human, but thou art verily like a flea! The horse-radical joined with a flea maketh the character "commotion" (騒)! Well then, here beginneth a mighty commotion! Prepare thyself!'

Yajihiko: 'Alas, there is no help for it! Astride this horse, shall I partake of food and drink in farewell to this earthly life? Ramp, horse, ramp! One step ahead is total darkness! A flea biting for food and drink—this is true gorging and swilling (gyūin-bashoku)! Shall I gnaw thy neck a little?'

The Horse: 'Neigh, neigh, neigh! What a miserable wretch, destitute of all dignity!'

Yajihiko: 'What? Doth a mere beast dare grade the dignity of a Preaching Emissary?'

The Horse: 'Now matters have reached desperation—I am a wild jade!'

Quicker than words could tell, it shook its mane and reared upright, leaping two or three fathoms into the air to the left, right, fore, and aft, bucking in frantic fury.

Yajihiko: 'Hold, hold! Quiet down! My eyes are spinning! Not only mine eyes, but the mountains and roads are all spinning round! Everything hath begun revolving like a gyrocompass! Cease in good measure!'

The Horse: 'Nay, I shall rage until thou tumbling fallest! Presently I shall dispatch thee unto the Netherworld!'

Yajihiko: 'Earthquakes and volcanic eruptions be damned! I have grown weary of this earth that spinneth so dizzyingly! It seemeth the divine reconstruction of the world is unfolding at desperate speed! Ah, what a whirling world it is!'

The Horse: 'How wretched is thy wandering in karmic transmigration! Thou art ever boasting that thou art participating in the heaven-turning and earth-shaking divine work—how sayest thou? Can any heaven-turning, earth-shaking enterprise surpass the activity of this horse? Art thou not filled with awe at my miraculous feat, spinning swifter than a gyrocompass at eight thousand revolutions a minute?'

Yajihiko: 'I submit! Forgive me, I pray thee!'

The Horse: 'Look at thy disgraceful posture! Whom in the world dost thou take me for?'

Yajihiko: 'Art thou not a brute beast of a nag?'

The Horse: 'Ignorant fool! I am an avatar of Princess Konohana—the Vitup-horse-ive Avalokiteshvara, the Horse-Headed Goddess of Mercy (Batō Kannon)!'

Yajihiko: 'Merciful heavens! Forgive my insolence, I pray thee have mercy upon me!'

With a whistling sputter like a fart, the horse vanished into thin air like smoke.

Yajihiko: 'Ah, what an ordeal! What a dreadful trial I was put through! Ho, Yota, Roku, what hath become of your horses?'

Yotahiko and Roku: 'What meanest thou by horses? Who ever rode a horse?'

Yajihiko: 'Did not ye ride comfortably upon mares all the way hither but a moment ago?'

Both: 'Talk no nonsense! We walked all the way on our hereditary locomotion (our own feet)! And thou too wast trudging along with a strange grimace, thine eyes shut tight! Thou wast dreaming while walking! What an easygoing fellow! Ah, how unreliable to have such a companion on the road!'

Yajihiko: 'How uncanny! I cannot fathom this scene—whose doing can this be?'

Yotahiko: 'Ha-ha-ha! Rolling thy huge eyes, even if thou enactest a play in such a spot, no audience will ever appear! Belching out a discordant croak and revolving thy acorn-eyes, thou ham actor, what farce art thou mimicking? The spring air hath made thee dizzy with fever! Ha-ha-ha!'

Yajihiko: 'Can it be that it was indeed a dream?'

弥次彦『どうしたと言うのだ。夢を見たのではないか。歩きながら夢を見る奴がどこにあるか！』

与太彦『何をほざくか！ 歩きながら夢を見るのは貴様が元祖（教祖）じゃないか。俺も張り合って夢を見てやったのだ。なあ六公、貴様もろくでもない夢を見たのだろう』

弥次彦『アハハハハ、神様は偉いものだ！ 俺が夢を見たと言ってボロクソに笑いおったから罰は覿面、貴様らも同じように歩きながら夢を見せられおったな。これだからアルコールの抜けた甘酒のような低脳児と同行するのは困ると言うのだ』

この時、山岳も崩れんばかりの怪音が轟いてきた。三人は驚いて目を覚ませば、あにはからんや、小鹿峠の道端でコクリコクリと居眠りをしていたのであった。

三人『ああ、大層な夢を見たものだなあ。夢の中に夢を見たり、何が何だか有名無実、曖昧模糊、五里夢中だ。ああ小鹿峠だ、しっかりと腹帯でも締めて行こうか！』
