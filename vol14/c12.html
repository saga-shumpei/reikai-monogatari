Chapter 12: Discourse on Reconciliation [562]

第12章　復縁談〔562〕

§ 1

Having pacified the company of Karasu Kanzaburō through Kototama, Katsuhiko the divine envoy, accompanied by Yajihiko, Yotahiko, and Rokukō, crossed several passes in high spirits and arrived at yet another settlement.

This was a small hamlet of some twenty or thirty dwellings scattered hither and thither, named Koyama Village.

Yaji: 'Aha! Here too a little world hath formed itself! Let us find a convenient cottage and ask for leave to rest!'

Taking the lead, he peered this way and that, searching for a suitable dwelling. At the entrance of a modest thatched cottage stood an elderly woman.

Yaji: 'Pardon, good grandmother! Wouldst thou graciously grant us leave to rest awhile?'

Crone: 'I am sightless, wherefore I cannot discern your visages. Whither are ye traveling, and hast thou companions with thee?'

Yaji: 'Aye, aye, we are a company of four. Having traversed many rugged mountain passes, our legs have turned to rigid sticks, and our bodily senses seem to have moved away to parts unknown, so that our limbs utterly refuse our commands! Wilt thou kindly lend us thy veranda a moment? We are pilgrims on our way to worship at Mount Caucasus.'

Crone: 'Ah, is it so? How admirable is your pious faith! I too am one who worshippeth the Gods of Mount Caucasus. Were ye of the Ural Faith I should flatly refuse, but being pilgrims unto Mount Caucasus, ye must surely belong to the Sango Faith. Pray rest at your ease!'

Yaji: 'Sango Faith indeed—pure and genuine to the core!'

Katsu: 'Good grandmother, I am an envoy of the Sango Faith named Katsuhiko.'

Yota: 'And I am a believer named Yotahiko. We beseech thy gracious favor!'

Crone: 'Ye spoke just now of being four, yet methinks I hear the voices of only three! Whither hath the fourth companion gone?'

Rokukō feigned an assumed, humble voice:

Roku: 'I am a humble rogue named Roku, wherefore I beseech thee to grant me thy kind acquaintance!'

Crone: 'Though thou bidst me look upon thee, I am blind! I have no choice but to know thee through thine auditory voice, ha-ha-ha-ha!'

Yaji: 'In so spacious a cottage, art thou entirely alone, grandmother?'

Crone: 'Nay, my old man was stricken with paralysis and hath lain bedridden in the detached room behind these three years past, to our great grief.'

§ 2

Yaji: 'Hast thou no children, grandmother?'

Crone: 'Two children have I: the elder brother took his wife and set out for Mount Caucasus some days ago to pray that my blindness might be healed; he should return in two or three days. And I have a younger daughter, yet she hath met with cruel misfortune. The husband to whom she was wedded suddenly turned into a constable of the Ural Faith, guzzling wine, gambling, running after wanton women—an utterly incorrigible wretch! My daughter Otake, being a pious devotee of the Sango Faith, suffered endless domestic strife, until at last she fled in the dead of night and returned home. No matter how we urged her, she declared, "Even unto death, never will I return unto such a profligate rogue!" Wherefore we had no choice but to send her into domestic service at a tavern named Matsuya in Yamada Village at the foot of the Nineteenth Pass. In mine old age, I have borne immense sorrow on her account! If thou art an envoy of the Sango Faith, wilt thou not offer prayer unto the Gods on her behalf?'

Yaji: 'Aye, aye, very willingly! We shall offer prayers for thee. And is thy daughter under long-term contract, or is she serving merely on a temporary footing?'

Crone: 'Seeing she must needs be wedded if a suitable match appear, she hath signed no term-contract. If ye travel the wide world, pray find her a good match! 'Tis not for a mother to boast, yet Otake is a woman of deep piety, honest and tender of heart! Both mine old man and I vow that until Otake's husband is settled, we cannot die in peace! Prithee pray earnestly unto the Gods that she may find a blessed match!'

Yota: 'What was the name of Otake-san's former husband?'

Crone: ''Twas an ill-visaged rogue of crooked nature named Roku! A good-for-nothing knave! By what strange karma such a gentle maid as Otake was yoked unto that worthless Rokosuke, detested like a poisonous millipede—daily both the old man and I lament that the Gods were somewhat unfeeling! Alas, how fares Otake now? Poor, piteous child! Boo-hoo-hoo!'

Yajihiko glanced at Rokukō's face with a nudge of his chin:

Yaji: 'Hark, Master Roku! How sayest thou? Wilt thou not also offer prayers for her?'

§ 3

Roku: 'A-a-aye, I shall o-o-offer prayer, shall I?'

Yaji: 'Ha-ha-ha! What a comical squeak!'

Crone: 'Otake hath had ill fortune with husbands. Even before that Roku, she was wedded once, yet that fellow too was a drunkard and a notorious thief who was cast out by the village, wherefore weeping bitter tears she returned home to pass sorrowful days. Then came a matchmaker who whispered sweet flatteries unto blind me, promising mochi growing upon trees, and wedded her unto Roku's house; but that Roku, as I said, was an utterly incorrigible scoundrel, so that my daughter is truly piteous! The doctrine of the Sango Faith prescribeth: "Unto the second time, remarriage may be tolerated through divine forbearance; but unto a third, it violateth the ordinances of Heaven!" Because it is an unbending commandment, my piteous daughter hath resolved to remain a young widow; yet as a mother, even wert it to break the laws of Heaven, I would gladly lay down mine own life if she might only have a worthy husband! Ye who are of the Sango Faith, what think ye? Wilt thou not inquire of the Gods?'

Yaji: 'Aha! This is a knotty problem beyond our power to untie! Master Katsuhiko the divine envoy, wilt thou not grant us a solution?'

Katsu: 'The teachings of the Sango Faith declare: "Parent and child endure for one existence; husband and wife endure for two." Concerning this, many believers fall into perplexity; yet to explain it clearly, it is an unwritten law that whether husband or wife, one cannot change one's spouse more than twice.'

Crone: 'If that be so, suppose one's former husband or wife dieth. Compelled by circumstance, one welcometh a second spouse. Then, after passing away, doth not one find two husbands or two wives? Men debate endlessly, wondering with which spouse one should dwell in the Divine Realm; how dost thou regard this matter?'

Katsu: 'Wedlock is bound by the affinity of spirits; yet a soul may divide into multiple manifestations when entering this mortal world. While divine souls of mighty spiritual power divide into four souls (the Shikon), ordinary human beings manifest primarily as two souls—the Aramitama and Nigimitama. Wherefore, up to two marriages, spouses linked by the very same spiritual affinity are guided by the Gods to unite unawares. A third spouse, however, possesses no spiritual accord, rendering divine service impossible and causing chaotic confusion in the Divine Realm, wherefore it is strictly forbidden. Furthermore, spouses who enter the Spirit World harbor not the slightest carnal lust; their wedlock is of soul unto soul. Though possessing spiritual bodies, they have neither need nor desire for mortal congress, dwelling in pristine purity. While certain earthbound spirits of clinging attachment obsess living animals to indulge their passions, such are base exceptions. In the Spirit Realm, husband and wife are even as loving brother and sister. Carnal wedlock exists to sustain the mortal lineage, whereas spiritual wedlock in the Divine Realm fulfills the divine mission of warp and weft (経 and 緯) in spiritual service.'

勝彦『夫婦というものは無論、身魂の因縁で結ばれるものではあるが、身魂というものは幾つにも分かれてこの世へ生まれて来ているものなのだ。しかし余程神力の備わった神の身魂であれば「四魂」と言って四つにも分かれて生まれて来るものだが、普通の人間はまず荒魂や和魂といった二魂が現れて来るのが普通だ。それだから二度までは同じ身魂の因縁の夫婦が、神のお引き合わせによって知らず知らずのうちに縁を結ぶことになる。それだから三人目の夫や妻は身魂が合わないから、どうしても御神業が務まらないばかりか、神界の秩序を乱し身魂の混乱を招くことになるため厳禁されているのだ。また霊界へ行った夫婦には肉体の欲は微塵も無い。心と心の夫婦だから幽体はあっても、この世の人間の交わりのような行いは全くする必要もなく、欲望も起きないから極めて清らかなものなのだ。中には執着心の強い身魂が、この世の生きている動物を使ってあれこれと悪さを働く例もあるが、こんなものは例外だ。ちょうど幽界へ行ってからの夫婦というものは仲の良い兄弟のようなものだ。肉体の夫婦は肉体の血統を繋ぐための神命であり、神界の身魂の夫婦は神界における縦と横（経と緯）の神業を果たすのが夫婦の身魂の使命なのだ』
