Chapter 3: The Restoration of Takahiko [553]

第3章　鷹彦還元〔553〕

§ 1

Takahiko, Umehiko, and Kamehiko, rejoicing in the conversion of the once-stubborn Iwahiko, spurred their steeds alongside Komahiko and rode majestically up Ojika Pass. Guided through the nocturnal path by divine providence along the steep mountain trails, they speedily reached the summit.

Takahiko: 'Thanks to the divine grace, we have conquered Ojika Pass! Brethren, shall we rest our horses here awhile ere we proceed?'

All present unanimously assented to Takahiko’s proposal and nimbly leaped down from their steeds.

Iwahiko: 'Brethren, ye must all be weary from such a steep ascent. Yet, Preaching Emissary Otohiko is nowhere to be seen!'

Umehiko: 'Ah, indeed so! Whither can he have gone? Hath his mount collapsed along the road? Surely, since we shouted with such a loud voice, he could not have failed to hear, nor is there any reason he should not awaken. Hath he perchance fallen from his horse? Neither are Yaji nor Yota to be seen!'

Iwahiko: 'Nay, have no fear! Were he alone, we might well be anxious, but since they are a party of three, there is scarce any danger of them going astray! Ha-ha-ha!'

Kamehiko: 'Somehow I cannot help but feel uneasy. May they not have encountered the Inspectors of the Ural faith on the way and be locked in bitter combat? Preaching Emissary Otohiko is meek and full of fortitude, so he might easily surmount any adversity; yet I am in constant anxiety lest the newcomers, Yajihiko and Yotahiko, have displayed unneeded bravado and stirred up a tumult!'

Komahiko: 'Friend Kame, dost thou also think so? I am of like mind; my heart is filled with apprehension. By all accounts, this vicinity is a stronghold of the Ural religion. We hear that fierce captains from Mount Ural and Armenia have gathered in great numbers in the land of Fusa, so we cannot afford negligence. Takahiko, though it trouble thee, wilt thou not manifest thy divine gift, restore thyself momentarily unto a sacred hawk, and reconnoiter the land? Though one may say all is well, we cannot be fully at ease.'

Takahiko: 'I consent with pleasure. Pray wait here awhile.'

And forthwith he transformed into a sacred hawk, soaring high into the vault of heaven and vanishing from sight.

The remaining four recited divine prayers upon the height and lost all sense of passing time as they conversed of past journeys. The eastern sky flushed with crimson, and the radiance of the Great Sun Deity arose above the peaks of the Takao mountain range.

Iwahiko: 'Ah, day hath already dawned! What of Takahiko? Can it be that the hunter of mummies hath himself become a mummy?'

残された四人はここで神言を奏上し、これまでの物語を語り合ううちに時の経つのも忘れていた。東の空が茜色に染まり、日の大神の御光がタカオ山脈の頂から昇って来られた。

岩彦『ああ、もう夜が明けた。鷹彦さまはどうしたのだろう。ミイラ取りがミイラになったのではあるまいかなあ』

§ 2

Umehiko: 'Nay, that could hardly be! Seeing it concerneth Oto-san, we shall surely receive tidings (a pun on Otohiko / otozure) of him ere long!'

Komahiko: 'Truly, lacking Oto-san's companionship is just like having no tidings at all; our pilgrimage feeleth solitary and desolate!'

At that moment, four masked men climbed the pass from the west, whispering among themselves:

First Man: 'Aha! There they are! And four Preaching Emissaries of the Sango religion to boot! Ho, Hachikō, run quickly and report unto General Gengorō that he may dispatch a multitude of captors! Meanwhile, we shall keep watch here to ensure these rascals do not escape!'

They spoke in hushed whispers.

Iwahiko: 'Ho there, are ye not the captors of the Ural religion coming yonder? Brethren, we thank you for your early morning toil! Pray sit down and enjoy a pipe at your ease; let us converse together of mountains and seas!'

Umehiko: 'To view the four quarters from this mountain pass and exchange casual talk is by no means an unpleasant matter! Instead of acting so restlessly and nervously, pray sit down comfortably with us and take a smoke!'

First Man: 'Aha! Ye are undeniably the Preaching Emissaries of the Sango faith! Even as ye discern, we are constables of the Ural faith! Now that matters have reached this pass, your doom is sealed! Even if ye should employ miraculous divine arts to fly through the heavens or burrow into the earth, we too possess our own supernatural power! Come, fold your arms behind your backs like good fellows!'

Iwahiko: 'Ha-ha-ha-ha! How boldly thou speakest! Setting thy face stiff and grim like a Guardian King cast in lead, one should not be so unbending! We are all children of the same Heavenly Deity—supporting and supported by one another, helping and being helped, living and prospering in this world to serve the True God as precious human beings! Come, take a quiet smoke!'

Second Man: 'At this critical hour, I have no ears to hear thy useless preachments! Thou art Magatsu deities propagating an evil heresy named the Sango religion across the realm! If thou hast such enlightenment, why dost thou place faith in such a creed? Full of plausible tongue and fawning countenance, though thou seekest to kill us with thy mouth like a beggar crushing lice, this Roku-san is not so foolish as to fall into thy snare! Cease thy babbling and fold thy hands meekly, or shall we drag thee bound unto the fortress of Mount Ural?'

Komahiko: 'Ha-ha-ha! Come, come! On such a long summer day, if thou waxest so agitated from the very dawn, thou wilt become exhausted! Pray calm thyself and take a pipe! We shall gladly perform a solemn soul-pacification (chinkon) for thee!'

§ 3

Roku: 'Wh-what babblest thou? 'Tis that very soul-pacification which offendeth me! If thou prattlest further, thy life shall sink beneath the sod in an instant!'

Kamehiko: 'What a doctrine is this Ural religion, to employ such violent language! One might easily mistake thee for a highway palanquin-bearer! In this world blessed by the Kototama, would it not be better if thou didst use slightly politer language?'

Roku: 'Hold thy noisy tongue! 'Tis leagues apart from the hypocritical creed of hypocrites like thee, who affect a posture as if they would not harm a flea, yet harbor hearts like demons, serpents, wolves, lions, or wild dogs! In this world, there is naught more perilous than a villain harboring evil designs beneath a gentle countenance! Outwardly like Bodhisattvas, inwardly like Yakshas! Prepare thyself, for I shall soon strip off thy disguise of fiendish Rakshasas! As for our folk of the Ural faith, viewed from without we may resemble rough-hewn Guardian Kings, yet as for the purity of our hearts, we possess souls so fair that the Maiden of the Dragon Palace or Princess Konohana of Mount Tenkyō would flee barefoot in awe! Pray do not mistake us!'

Iwahiko: 'That is indeed a splendid creed. Nevertheless, as the unity of spirit and flesh (reiniku-itchi) teacheth, the color of the inner soul ever manifesteth itself without. If the heart be gentle and pure, a person's words and actions must likewise be gentle and pure. There can be no reason why a jewel of pure gold should be wrapped in filthy rags.'

Roku: 'Ugh, what a fellow for kneading lumpish sophistries! In this present world, men have cultivated only their tongues; even men use effeminate words and oil their hair to appear fashionable! As for men like us, guileless and innocent as raw nature itself, thou couldst not find many though thou shouldst search with gongs and drums! Devils ever deceive men by donning the mask of virtue! Ye too must be of that stamp. To preach what cannot be practiced is the way of false creeds. Babble no more; accept that matters have come to this pass through karmic fate, and submit to my command!'

Iwahiko: 'Ha-ha-ha-ha! Verily thou expoundest the doctrines of the Ural faith with exceedingly wondrous skill! Truly, one cannot know without hearing; alas, mortals in this world are plagued by prejudice without tasting! If what thou sayest were true, we too would believe in the Ural faith. Nevertheless, the Ural religion is a false teaching whose actions contradict its words. To speak the plain truth, we ourselves were formerly Preaching Emissaries of the Ural faith! Having crossed unto the Solitary Isle of the Dragon Palace to propagate it, we served as Ural missionaries until but a single month ago! Yet when we heard the Sango faith and compared it with the Ural doctrine, we realized from the bottom of our souls that there lay a difference as great as between Heaven and Earth! In short, no matter how noble a doctrine appear, if practice faileth to follow, it will only pour poison into society. The Sango faith is a religion of silent execution. However glorious a faith be, if it lacketh worthy emissaries, it is as if a priceless golden jewel were buried in the mire! How say ye? Would it not be for your future salvation to submit unto the Sango faith here and now? Nay, in this very mortal flesh ye may bask in boundless tranquility and divine glory! The Sango faith is a doctrine of blessings both in the present and the future life!'
