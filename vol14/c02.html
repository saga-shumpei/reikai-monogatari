Chapter 2: The Copper-Wood Statue [552]

第2章　銅木像〔552〕

§ 1

The four travelers—Otohiko, Yajihiko, Yotahiko, and Gengorō—bade farewell to the Clothes-Stripping Hag of the Sanzu River and pressed westward across a boundless wilderness overgrown with tangled weeds. Suddenly they came to a dead halt before a barren mountain; hemmed in by precipitous peaks on three sides, they lost all trace of a path and stood in bewildered dismay.

Otohiko: 'Alas, we are brought to a sudden standstill! Our pilgrimage through the Netherworld hath struck a dead end. We must needs devise some manner of counsel!'

Yajihiko: 'O Preaching Emissary, come, invoke the Kototama of the Heavenly Prayer (Amatsu-Norito)! Is not such a dire extremity the very occasion for putting Kototama into active service?'

'Ah, yea, indeed so!'

So saying, he clapped his hands and solemnly recited the Heavenly Prayer. Thereupon, the barren mountain that had blocked their way vanished into the distance like a fleeting cloud, fleeing with headlong speed tens of leagues away.

Yajihiko: 'Ah, how wondrous is the majesty of Kototama! Verily I have heard it said that the power of faith can move even mountains. Truly thou art a worthy Emissary; the vigor of thy faith is unquestionable! Having gained such an experience, whether it be the Scorching Hell or the Eight Freezing Hells, what cause have we to fear? We shall penetrate unto the deepest depths of Hell, and our company shall carry out a grand reformation of Nether administration!'

Otohiko: 'Hark, be not overly negligent. If thou growest careless, pride and conceit will unawares steal into thy soul, and there is no telling what unexpected blunders thou mayest enact. We must advance with utmost prudence into every nook and cranny.'

While they were thus conversing, suddenly an enormous Colossus of copper and timber, towering so vast that one could scarcely behold its crown, rose abruptly out of the ground.

Yajihiko: 'Behold, out it cometh! Verily the Netherworld is the Netherworld—it hath its own peculiar wonders! Ho there, monster of the Nether Realm! Thou impudent rogue who darest obstruct our passage, withdraw, withdraw! Far different from the phantoms of the mortal world, nether apparitions are wrought in a vastly novel style! Ho, monster, wilt thou not answer, thou knavish Copper-Wood Statue?'

'Gwah-hah-hah-hah! Goo-foo-foo-foo! Gyeh-heh-heh-heh! Gwi-hee-hee-hee!'

'What is this? Belching forth naught but harsh, guttural sounds like "gah-gah" and "gih-gih"—whom dost thou take us for? I am Master Yajihiko, who brought even the Clothes-Stripping Hag of the Sanzu River to her knees!'

'I am a mechanical monster wrought of copper and wood! If thou babblest further, I shall swallow thee down whole from the crown of thy head!'

Yajihiko: 'Ha-ha-ha! So thou art a serpentine monster! I would gladly let thee swallow, yet unluckily I possess naught at all. Shall I give thee to drink some of that urine-tea which Otake forced us to swallow at Tago Station?'

§ 2

The Copper-Wood Statue: 'Nay, I care not to drink tea! I desire to swallow a naked wretch!'

Yajihiko: 'Oho, is it so? Well, well, just as thou hast ordered, here is a fellow named Gengorō of the Ural faith, who hath arrived stark naked! Verily he must be the one thou wishest to swallow.'

The Statue: 'Gah-bah!'

Yajihiko: 'Truly, what a splendidly delighted countenance thou hast! Come, Gengorō, my lad, with all due labor, pray let him swallow thee!'

Gengorō: 'The naked one is thine own self! Those garments are all mine own! Hearken, Sir Copper-Wood Colossus, Yajihiko and Yotahiko were stripped stark naked on the hither side of Ojika Pass! They are scoundrels well accustomed to journeying in the nude. I was robbed by these two at the ferry of the Sanzu River; therefore, pray swallow down these two thoroughly!'

The Statue: 'Gwi-hee-hee! Yea, indeed so! Yajihiko and Yotahiko seem far more savory to the taste!'

Yajihiko: 'Silence, Gengorō! Thou art the genuine, stark-naked one! I wear these garments with the sanction of the Clothes-Stripping Hag. Look here, Sir Copper-Wood Colossus, art thou not laboring under a misunderstanding?'

The Statue: 'I am a machine! Henceforth I shall utter not another word. Go ye freely into my belly and operate the machinery as pleaseth you. Once ye enter my belly, various mechanical contrivances are fully equipped, and the method of using each is duly inscribed. When one pulleth those ropes, this Copper-Wood Statue will enact a mighty performance! Gah-hah-hah-hah!'

Yajihiko: 'Oho, this is delightful! Enma must have fashioned such a byproduct to beguile our boredom, thinking we would journey across a hundred thousand million Buddha-lands. Behold, the air of civilization hath truly penetrated everywhere! Shall I venture inside and try operating this machine?'

Gengorō: 'Ye stripped me of my garments, so your sins are aggravated; ye have no right whatever to use this machine! Since I am naked, I will slip inside this Copper-Wood Statue in lieu of clothes and give it a trial. Why do not ye take me on with all your might? A disputation between the Grand Inspector of the Ural religion and the Preaching Emissary and believers of the Sango faith might well prove exceedingly diverting!'

Yotahiko: 'Hark, Yajihiko, why not let Gengorō have his say and do so? Say, Lord Otohiko, would that not be fine?'

Otohiko: 'It shall be well. In that case, friend Gengorō, pray enter within.'

§ 3

Gengorō: 'This is truly grateful! I shall manipulate it freely like a puppet! Where is the entrance? Aha, it hath wide, gaping nostrils. Shall I clamber roughly inside through here?'

So saying, he began climbing the torso of the Copper-Wood Colossus, leaning on his staff like a woodsman ascending a mountain peak.

Yajihiko: 'Look, how fascinating! He appeareth tiny, just like a fly clinging to an ox. What a prodigiously immense Copper-Wood Statue it is!'

At last, Gengorō crawled inside through the monster's nostril.

Yotahiko: 'Lo, he hath finally vanished inside! Now the sport beginneth. Ho, hurry up and start thy performance!'

The Colossus: 'Ooh-ooh-ooh! I am Gengorō of the Iron Grasp, Grand Inspector of the Ural faith! Come, come, petty Preaching Emissary of the Sango religion! Now that matters have come to this pass, I am invincible—I am Gengorō clad in the cloak of the Copper-Wood Colossus! Oo-foo-foo!'

Yajihiko: 'Behold what an uncanny Colossus! Putting on the airs of Gengorō—how outrageous! Ho, Copper-Wood Statue, play not such witty pranks!'

The Colossus: 'Witty or not witty, what care I?'

And with that, it rotated its eyeballs—vast as bull's-eye umbrellas—round and round at furious speed.

Yajihiko: 'Behold, what a bizarre trick! At best it is merely a machine; he must be revolving the eyeballs by means of some rotary press! Ho, if thou rollest thine eyes so wildly, thy sight will turn into a motor and burn out!'

The Colossus: 'Art thou terrified of these eyes? On this journey through the Netherworld, I have first rolled my eyes to awaken yours! Shall I flare open my nose for you into the bargain?'

Yajihiko: 'Flare it if thou wilt! I shall sit here at ease, fanned by the spring breeze, and enjoy the flower-viewing. Lo, it wriggleth its hill-like snout—what an unsightly snout! Ha, it flared and gaped! What a nose, resembling a great ventilator, pointing its nostrils upward as if sniffing the ceiling! It mistook the ceiling for burning incense! Ho, monster statue, take a little thought and consider!'

The Colossus: 'Shall I blow thee away with the blast of my breath? Prepare thyself, for this ventilator shall belch forth a tempest of dense smoke!'

Yotahiko: 'Ha-ha-ha! How times have altered! In this era when machines speak, this too must be a gramophone of novel design! Ho, Sir Gramophone, spin quietly so that thy record doth not crack! If thou abusest it too harshly, thou wilt shorten its lifespan!'

The Colossus: 'Come, now the true sport beginneth! If I stamp my right foot, Ojika Pass will shake and rattle; if I stamp my left foot with might, ye shall bounce into the air towards the heavens, and plunge headlong into the muddy rice paddies with a thud and a screech—a swift end to your journey through the Netherworld! Aha-ha-ha!'

Yajihiko: 'What, thou even recountest the matters of Ojika Pass? Ho, Gengorō, wilt thou not come out in good time? Now it is my turn!'

The Copper-Wood Statue stood up heavily, stamping its feet with a thundering tread; striding over the towering barren mountain in a single pace, it ambled lumberingly forward.

'Lo, the sun is so low that my head keepeth bumping against it; there is no help for it! Yonder also the moon hangeth low; shall I bend my loins and pass beneath?'

Roaring like thunder, it strode away toward the west.

Yajihiko: 'Alas, that monster statue hath departed somewhere, carrying Gengorō inside its belly! Ah, there is no help for it; we are stark naked! Will not someone come along? If we do not procure garments ere reaching Enma’s court, we shall be locked up again if we encounter the police!'

Yotahiko: 'Look, look over yonder! Preaching Emissary Hinodewake cometh leading the way, accompanied by Takahiko, Iwahiko, Umehiko, and Kamehiko! The sound of their prayers can be heard!'

Yajihiko: 'Lo, they come! I wondered whither that Preaching Emissary had gone, but it seems he too was swallowed by a serpent and hath been journeying through the Netherworld! Yet having companions maketh it lively and good!'

Hinodewake and his party advanced toward them, the hooves of their horses clicking clatteringly upon the ground.

Yajihiko: 'Pray, sir, my head is covered all over in urine! Wilt thou not blow water upon it and cleanse me?'

Hinodewake: 'O Takahiko, O Iwahiko, draw water from the mountain stream and pour it over them all!'

Takahiko and Iwahiko: 'We obey with reverence.'

Descending into the ravine, they filled their mouths with water and blew a purifying breath onto the faces of the three. With a deep groan, when they came to their senses with a start, the three found themselves lying unconscious on the riverbank at the foot of Ojika Pass.

Hinodewake: 'Splendid, splendid! Had our arrival been delayed but a little, ye would verily have made the final journey to the Netherworld! Above all, how wonderful that your lives are spared! Come, let us now repair to the capital of Fusa, and thence advance toward Mount Caucasus!'
