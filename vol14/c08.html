第8章　泥の川〔558〕

§ 1

Unto a boundless, withered moor, where the grace of the gods was met by raging tempests, by the edge of a river thoroughly turbid and foul, the two did at last arrive.

Yaji: 'Well, what is this? Another backward journey into the Nether Realm! Lo, this is none other than the River of the Three Crossings—the Sanzu River! Katsuhiko, I wager somewhere around here a most wondrous beauty of my acquaintance ought to be living in comfortable retirement!'

Katsu: 'Master Yajihiko, this place seems altogether severed from the mundane world. Whilst we were storming the heights of Ojika Pass amidst that hurricane, I felt my senses reel, and the moment that befell, the entire scene instantly transformed into this boundless, withered wilderness. I have no recollection of boarding an airplane, yet how in the world did we arrive in such a place as this? Could it be that we have engaged in that sleepwalking whereof philosophers so frequently speak? Unless someone brings forth a master of mesmerism to awaken us speedily, if any misstep occurs, this might well turn into a journey unto the Nether Realm!'

Yaji: 'Speak not of "might turn into"! It is undeniably a journey unto the Nether Realm! This very place is the crossing of the Sanzu River!'

Katsu: 'If that be so, where is the old crone of the crossing?'

Yaji: 'In these times of soaring prices, it seems her income failed to balance her expenses, and so she must have gone out of business. But far more important than that, I have a fair lady of old acquaintance sheltered here; shall I grant thee an audience with her?'

Katsu: 'Thou art ever incorrigibly Yaji-like! Of all places, what an eerie spot! How could anyone ever keep a mistress's abode beside the Sanzu River?'

Yaji: 'Even so, since she was the one who sought to be so kept, it cannot be helped. Her eyebrows are dark like the crescent moon; her eyes shine with deep verdant luster; the shaping of her nose, the contour of her mouth, and her cheeks faintly tinged with the blossom of the peach—she is indeed an incomparable masterpiece beyond all description!'

Katsu: 'Oho! Doth such a masterpiece truly exist? Wilt thou not grant me a glimpse of this rarity?'

Yaji: 'Make no trivial puns! From now on it shall be one warrior against a thousand! We must do battle with myriads of demons of every hue—blue, black, red, white, and olive! Ah, ha-ha-ha-ha!'

Katsu: 'No matter what manner of beings appear, we shall be entirely safe if we employ the divine, miraculous weapons of Kototama! More than that, wilt thou not make haste to present that wondrous masterpiece for my exalted viewing?'

Yaji: 'Very well, very well, marvel not! She is a beauty of no ordinary kind! Once thou hast gazed upon her countenance, never shalt thou forget it unto the end of myriad kalpas, even unto the eternal reign of Miroku! A beauty so dreadful and fearsome as to strike awe into thy soul! Follow me a little way. Look yonder! Where I fancied there was a neat, tasteful dwelling with a screening pine—alas, 'twas but a dream! In what resembles a thatched privy-hut, she is right now in direst poverty!'

Katsu: 'What strange speech is that? What meanest thou by direst poverty?'

Yajihiko peered through a chink in the door of the straw hut.

Yaji: 'Well met, and how fares thy noble health! Lo, I have come once more! Behold, thy husband from a former life, the unrivaled, dark-complexioned hero of the realm—Yajihiko! Make haste and open the door!'

From within the straw hut came a voice:

Voice: 'Well, well, hast thou come yet again? What a straying, famished ghost thou art! Although this river is the Sanzu River which none can cross a second time once passed, for what purpose dost thou wander hither from the mortal world into the realm of the dead, thou phantom of the mortal sphere?'

Yaji: 'Hold, hold! Husband and wife are not such distant strangers! Since it is called the Sanzu—the River of Three Crossings—it is only natural to cross it three times! Even with one's daily meals, unless one eats three times a day, the sun doth not set upon that day! To call me a phantom of the mortal sphere—what nonsense art thou babbling?'

Crone: 'A phantom of the mortal world thou art indeed! Sticking thy neck into phantom companies, brandishing phantom stocks, and whenever thou hearest of some profitable share, thy greed stretches taut like skin, defiling thy spirit, causing thy wife and children hardship, bringing affliction upon the folk of the world! Wondering how thou canst stand upright in the mundane world, thou art a flimsy shadow wriggling about in midair like a creature without waist or legs! What wonder is there in calling thee a phantom of the mortal sphere? In this Nether Realm, there is not a single deceased spirit so wretched as thou, thou chieftain of pedantic ghosts!'

Yaji: 'Hearken, thou old hag! What manner of insolence art thou spewing? Doth a wife ever heap such filthy abuse upon her husband? Knowest thou aught of wifely fidelity, thou insolent, defiant crone?'

Crone: 'Insolent and defiant, sayest thou? Insolence is not confined to women alone; among men, too, there are insolent rogues in plenty! What hast thou been doing? Living in the mortal world, piddling here and defecating there, beguiling thy neighbor's wife, seducing maidens of the neighborhood, and whenever thy wife grew old, casting her aside even as a horse-trader barters his horses or cattle—treating human beings as though they were beasts or machines—thou prime instigator of insolence! Though I appear thus, ever since Hell was first established, I have faithfully discharged my duty beside this riverbank, observing all the statutes! I am vastly different from such fickle trash of a human as thou, who shifteth incessantly like a cat's eye, crying that wages are too high or too low, or that this post is too grueling or that one too easy! Time and again thou comest to burden this old woman! Make thy retreat at once; it is far too early for thee to come hither! Those who come to this place are souls who have atoned for their sins in the mortal realm. Seeing thou hast accumulated an immense debt of sins and transgressions, thou must first undergo far more agony before coming forth! To leave thy debt of iniquities behind in the mortal world and flee unto such a place as this is utterly cowardly! There are bounds even to weak-willed sloth! What manner of place dost thou deem this Sanzu River to be? Is it a laundry to wash thy defiled spirit? In celestial terms, it is a place sacred even as the Heavenly River of Yasu!'

Crone: 'Is that so indeed? If the mortal world be so magnificent, why dost thou not abide therein and perform thy spiritual asceticism? No matter how enlightened it may have become, the sun doth not rise in double or triple spheres, nor can every night be blessed with a full moon! That which hath remained unchanged throughout fifty-six hundred and seventy million years is sincerity alone! What of thee? Dazzled by material cravings and what thou callest civilization, thou hast lost thy vision! Devoid of capital, even wert thou in the mortal world, thou couldst not establish a single company; though thou boastest of buying stocks, thou couldst not purchase a single share! Thou boastest of the twenty-fourth century, yet canst thou not comprehend that it hath already passed into history some one hundred and fifty thousand years ago? We are now in the one-hundred-and-eighty-thousandth century, thou antiquated simpleton!'
