第9章　空中滑走〔559〕

§ 1

Though the mountain squall, so exceedingly fierce, had subsided and the calm after the tempest had settled upon heaven and earth, the turmoil in their breasts was by no means stilled. Yotahiko and Roku parted the fallen leaves to search through the glens of Ojika Pass; yet the boughs were dark, and the underbrush grew in such tangled thickets that no path remained, so that the day at last drew to a close. Just then, parting the clouds in the eastern sky, the rising moon shed its radiance, illuminating all four quarters around them.

Thereupon the two shouted at the utmost pitch of their voices:

'O-ho! O-ho! Yajikō! Katsukō!'

Yet their shouting voices at length grew utterly hoarse, and now their hearts were withered and wrinkled, devoid of voice and sound.

Piteous indeed it was to see them wandering to and fro, straying in search of word concerning their two companions.

Yotakō and Rokukō arrived at a ravine where ancient trees stood dense and gloomy even at high noon.

Yota: 'Hark, Rokukō! Seeing we cannot find them though we have searched thus far, there is nothing for it but to abandon hope!'

Roku: 'Searching a mountain so vast with only one or two men for a day or two is indeed in vain. Yet we cannot unfeelingly abandon our comrades in their calamity and return home; so long as breath remaineth in us, we must ascertain their whereabouts, and even if they be corpses, we must grant them honorable burial!'

Yota: 'After searching so much, if they were alive it would be splendid; but if unhappily Yajihiko and Katsuhiko have rung down the curtain on their frail lives and lie prone as lifeless corpses, it would truly be unrewarded labor!'

Roku: 'Master Yota, is this a time to utter such idle twaddle (yota)? Prithee become earnest and sincere!'

Yota: 'Earnest? I am earnest to the very core! But I am so thoroughly exhausted that my legs are tottering—wobbly Master Yota indeed!'

Roku: 'It is truly troublesome that though we have called so loud, there cometh neither whisper nor sigh in reply. Struck by so mighty a tempest, could it be that Yajihiko and Katsuhiko have had their eardrums ruptured, so that our sad voices filled with friendship cannot penetrate their ears?'

Yota: 'What? Would a tempest of that measure rupture eardrums? The cord of their life's harp hath snapped! It hath severed completely, beyond all doubt—a grievous thing! Moreover, in this overgrown, deep mountain wilderness, searching is exceedingly laborious. Why is it that no matter how we shout and scream, not the slightest sound returneth? I would fain fire off salvos of Kototama unto the farthest recesses of this valley, but my voice is hoarse and my precious Kototama hath suffered a sudden blackout, so there is no help for it! How could I ever make excuses unto the Deity Hinode-wake, saying, "We mislaid two of our comrades upon the road"? Alas, what a terrible plight! Had I known this would happen, I should never have followed them! Repeating my grumbling lamentations to ease my heart—verily, since the dawn of creation, never have I heard of a dead man returning in answer to calling or shouting!'

Roku: 'Yotakō, alas, what an ominous chatterbox thou art! Concerning two men whose life or death is yet unknown, hast thou already resolved in thy mind that they are dead?'

Yota: 'Those who neither hear nor speak are dead men! Alas, "the iris of the sixth day, the chrysanthemum of the tenth"—too late for repentance! Where and how fare my beloved Yaji-san and Katsu-san? How miserable, how heartbreaking! Of our four travelling companions, to meet with such half-dead, half-alive affliction—how could we refrain from weeping? "Alas, slay me together with them! I would fain die!"—bred as a samurai who prizes not his life! Clang, clang, clang!'

Roku: 'Hold, Yotakō! There is a limit even unto thy twaddle! Canst thou not become more serious?'

Yota: 'Whether I become serious or not, 'tis all the same! Never hath there been an instance where a dead man returned! Because I have grown so lonely and mournful in heart, I am uttering foolish nonsense out of sheer caprice! Where in the world is there anyone who rejoiceth at a friend's calamity? The divine teachings instruct us: "Indulge neither in anxious forebodings nor in lingering regrets; live with high courage, for the spirit of the instant moment is paramount!" That very instant is the realm where we may act freely according to our will. One minute ago hath already vanished into past dreams, never to be recalled; and that which lies one minute ahead is not subject to human freedom. All things unfold according to the divine will of the Gods! Even while we speak thus, the demon of the merciless wind ceaselessly stalks our steps! Our comrades, Yaji and Katsu, swept away by an utterly pitiless tempest, are of uncertain fate; and if we should chance to meet them and say, "Ah, thou art here! How splendid, how wondrous! Sound in limb and hale in health, to gaze upon thy noble visages is our supreme felicity!" and they should reply, "Lo, is it Yotakō and Rokukō? Ye have searched out my abode and surmounted rugged paths to find me! Well done, well done! I am well pleased! As a reward, I bestow this upon thee," fancying they would grant us a wonder-working pill—yet if, on arriving, our calculations fail and expenditures exceed all budget, so that our two friends have filed for bankruptcy and suffered confiscation of goods, abandoning beloved wives and children to migrate unto the realm of the Dead, ten myriads of millions of lands away—then indeed, the cord of our braced heart and the thread of hope would snap, and we should be struck by a tragic curtain beyond all tears and regrets! At that moment, no matter how we despaired, it would be in vain! Rather than brooding thus, through the power of constant faith I forcefully summon the spirit of the present moment, comforting the steed of mine own soul! Thinkest thou I babble out of giddy levity? "More than the chirping cicada, the silent firefly consumeth its own flesh!" Alas, yet I yearn to gaze upon lively Yajihiko's face once more! And Katsuhiko as well—hardly half a day after being rescued from the cavern, to be seized by a ruthless wind, scattered like autumn leaves, and vanish like smoke—what an ill-fated birth was his! The more I ponder, the sadder I become! My spirit of the present moment seemeth about to collapse in defeat! Boo-hoo-hoo! Ugh, ugh, ugh!'

Roku: 'Though the Sun Deity smyleth radiantly, ascending the mountain crest to shine upon our heads, our hearts are shrouded in dark clouds, and rain of tears falleth like a summer shower—what an indescribably forlorn grief! How frail and fleeting is mortal man! Until yesterday, those two comrades, brimful of vigor capable of crushing demons, made merry in high spirits; yet now we know not if they be dead or alive, and must needs resign ourselves that ninety-nine percent they have crossed unto the other world! Until yesterday I was an envoy of the Ural faith, and scarce had I pledged allegiance unto the Sango faith when I was parted from Yajihiko and Katsuhiko! Yet though we met but yesterday, I cannot feel we are recent companions; it seemeth rather as though we had been friends for ten or twenty years! Even I, who have known them not three full days, am so deeply grieved; how much more must thou, Yotakō, their comrade of many years, be pierced to the heart!'

Yota: 'Alas, there is no help for it! Without fainting in weakness here, let us mount one more grand search! If divine affinity remaineth, the Gods will surely grant that we meet! Even if they be corpses, we must look upon them once more and witness their final end! Ugh, what an eerie cawing of crows! It maketh me ever more uneasy!'

Roku: 'Ah, truly, truly! Upon the boughs of a great tree in yonder glen, a flock of crows hath perched and are cawing loudly! Hold, this is passing strange! Doth not something resembling human garments appear upon the branches? Look yonder!'

Yotakō fashioned rings with the thumbs and index fingers of both hands like spectacles, fitted them closely to both eyes, and peered fixedly at the branches where the crows had gathered.

Yota: 'Lo! Those are unmistakably the garments of Yajikō and Katsukō! That goblin Tengu must have torn them from the crotch and hung the clothes upon the tree! How infuriating! Yet even clothes will do—we may count them as mementos of our friends! Let us go unto the foot of that tree and search; it may well be that they lie beneath as lifeless corpses! If they were alive, since they could hear the crows, our Kototama should surely reach them! Shall we shout with all our might?'

Roku: 'Though we would fain shout, the raw material of our voices is exhausted! If yonder be crows (karasu), here we but dry our throats (koe-wo-karasu)! Rather than trading idle words here, let us make haste to reach the base of that tree and investigate! If by any chance there be corpses of one or both under that tree, I shall act the part of their wives: "Prithee, dear Yaji-san, dear Katsu-san!
