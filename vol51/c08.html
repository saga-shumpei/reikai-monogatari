Chapter 8: 曲輪 Castle [1323]

曲輪城〔1323〕

§ 1

Born in the land of Tokoyo, claiming herself to be the reincarnation of the Hime of Tokoyo, Takahime, who dwells in the Kiyōtaukai—the second Jigokudau that is neither Jigoku, Chiū, nor Shiyabasekai—is an Akurei of the Kinmaukiubi and one of the hundred Magakami. Watching for an opportunity when her parents were away, she slipped out, passed through Armenia, and headed toward the capital of Jerusalem. In the prime of her youth, Takamiyahime unexpectedly fell into a strange relationship with Azumanowake, and having even borne a child, this love was obstructed by Kitaterugami, leading to a parting of the living. Forced to wander here and there, Takamiyahime's fate was thus cast.

The result of this was that, having heard bits and pieces of the teachings of the Brahman, the Ural religion, and the Ananai-kyō, and due to her own petty cleverness, her entire body was swayed by the Seirei of the Nikutaikai. Drifting and flowing, she arrived in the land of Fusa, where she built a training ground for the teachings of divination in Kitayama Village. Calling together her trusted right-hand woman Kurohime, Magahiko, and other disciples, day by day she deceived the people of the world by claiming to be the living shrine of the Hinodewake and the lineage of the Hensei-danshi. Finally, she crossed over to the island of Jitentō, and after various hardships and sufferings, she was moved by the infinite benevolence of the heart of Kami Susanoo-no-Ōkami.

Squeezing out tears of gratitude, she changed her heart and was saved by the Ananai-kyō, becoming a minister of Kami. However, deceived once again by evil spirits, she became obsessed with grasping the golden jewel—the Nyoi-hōju that is indestructible like diamond. With her monkey-like mind and horse-like will unrestrained, the pathos of her madness began. Wandering around the eighty islands and the eighty countries, including Takasago-jima and Ryūgū-jima, she finally repented of her ego and pride, becoming like a cat once more. She served at the sacred site of Aya and propagated the path for a while, but after learning that her childhood sweetheart was the chieftain Tōsuke of Awaji, the heart of Takahime wandered in darkness.

Leaving her child and the jewel entirely out of her mind, she yearned for her lover and traveled far and wide, crossing mountains, seas, and rivers. Day and night, she arrived at the Saien-kan on the great plateau of Ubusuna, the Takamagahara where Kami Susanoo-no-Ōkami resides. Though she was exhorted by Azumanowake on the principles of heaven and earth and repented once, her horse-like will went mad again. Agonizing over how to recover from this unrequited love, and while weeping tears of love, the persistent old woman, now over fifty, braced herself with courage. As the wind blew fiercely and lions, bears, tigers, and wolves roared, she climbed up and down the Kajika Pass with heavy, thumping steps.

When she finally arrived at the forest of the shrine, she wondered, 'What is this? What is this?' Seeing the palace pillars built upon the lower foundation stones and the Chigi rising high, she gazed upon the sacred dwelling of the Imperial Kami who resides there. Secretly nodding and smirking, she thought, 'This is the famous Kajika-yama, the throat of the Saien-kan. Here, I shall unfurl my banner, make it flutter in the blowing divine wind, and strike down the many officers and believers like falling dominoes. I shall build the Takahime Kingdom, oppose the Ananai-kyō, and make a name for myself.' Thus, Takahime, who appeared as the lineage of the Hensei-danshi and the righteous heaven-sent Hinodewake, stood up with resolve.

Striving with the conviction that she was the soul and spirit of the original lineage that had grasped the source since the beginning of the opening of heaven and earth, even unto the end of many eons, she used her clever words to persuade Tanehiko and the other hundred ministers of the shrine forest, displaying her hateful tyranny. Just then, Yōgenbō appeared as the right arm of the Yamata-no-Orochi of the Kiyōtaukai. Quickly discerning the evil heart of Minister Takahime, he changed his form and appeared as Mokusuke of the Saien-kan. He skillfully deceived Minister Takahime, and there they pledged their marriage. Settling in the shrine forest, they exerted all their power to obstruct the divine government of the Miroku-kami.

Just as she was exerting all her efforts to assist the great ambition of Ōkuninushi-no-Ōkami, she poisoned the uncooperative Tanehiko and his wife. Having become a person who feared no one, she plotted to carry out her original intention. Just then, Hatsuwakahime, a missionary of the path of the Ananai-kyō, appeared. She saw through the evil plot and, humbling herself, tried to obstruct the wicked desires of the two villains and save them by restoring them to a good heart. Though she exhausted her sincerity, the ugly soul, stained with the filth of the Magatsu-mi, feared the exposure of its true nature and, chased away like a dog, fled down the Kajika Pass with a clattering sound.

§ 2

Fleeing with all his might, Magatsu Eugenbau once again deceived Takahimetsukasa. Climbing to the sacred ground on the mountain of Kogita, he thought to perform a single scheme here, and plotted various evil things. Illuminated by the spiritual light of Tsukiōkami, he was suddenly struck with terror, tumbled onto the rugged rocks, and having sustained injuries, he slunk away. At the very moment he fled from this place, he lost the treasure of the Magawa. Leaving the mountain of Kogita behind, he crossed the fields where spring grasses were budding, and until he reached this side of the forest of Ayashi, he stumbled over a stone by the roadside and fell flat. When he searched his bosom, the Shinadama used for the magic of Eugenbau's transformation had somehow become an empty shell, and in the sorrow of not knowing where his form had gone, he sat heavily on the earth with his legs crossed, lost in thought with his arms folded.

To such a place, Takahime, who had been following behind, caught up with the two, Hatsukō and Tokukō. While they rested their breath for a while, they heard the lamentations of Eugenbau, who said that he had dropped the essential treasure on the mountain of Kogita. Upon hearing this, Takahime became irritated and strictly ordered the two, Hatsu and Toku, to go and retrieve the treasure of the Magawa. Having no reason to refuse the master's command, they secretly returned to the sacred ground of Kogita and looked to see how the reception was going.

As they listened to the blind old man Bunsu-sama, who was drawing pictures while telling the story of the origin of the Bunbun-dama, the two tried to use various clever words to get it back. However, Bunsu-sama was firm, and his reluctance to hand it over easily was frustrating. The two decided here to make their move, suddenly knocked the old man down, snatched the ball in the meantime, and with their painful feet dragging, they arrived at the forest of Ayashi like clouds and mist. When they received words of praise from Eugenbau and Takahime, there was a moment of joy, but suddenly their wounds began to ache, and since they could not take another step, they decided to spend the night here.

The four reached a consensus, and Hatsu and Toku suddenly fell into a deep sleep, dreaming in the midst of their slumber. Takahime, who had listened to the loud snoring heard all around, urged Eugenbau on, and taking advantage of the darkness, they headed toward the village of Ukiki. At the entrance of the village of Ukiki, they stopped by the Tamadaki waterfall, where the sound of water was high, and washed the ball of the Magawa. When they looked, the Magawa began to shine brightly, and Takahime was blinded and dizzy, falling down on the spot. Eugenbau quickly took advantage of the moment when Takahime had fainted, and using his sorcery, he created a pavilion here.

Suddenly, a mirage appeared, the Jerusalem of the capital of Chin. Compared to its magnificence, it was a building that was dozens of times more astonishing, and it was mysterious that he had manifested it by employing many evil gods. Eugenbau laughed, 'With this, my plan has finally reached its beginning.' No matter how much magic one uses, it is an ugly act that cannot be done without using humans with strong hearts, created in the image of God. Here, one can understand the pride of the evil god who has captured the foolish Takahime. Eugenbau scooped up the clear water of the waterfall for Takahime, made her take it into her mouth, and called out to her with all his might.

She regained her breath and came to her senses, looking around in wonder. Staring at the mysterious light that was brighter than the day, she clicked her tongue in amazement, 'Ah, how mysterious, how mysterious.' She bowed her head, lost in thought, wondering if the Hinode-kami of the Tianjin-hi had suddenly appeared, which was a comical sight. Eugenbau laughed, 'Ahahaha, Takahime! I am the god-official Mokusuke. Now that the treasure of the Mizu-no-Mitama is in my hands, I have turned the darkness into day, manifested the power of God, and instantly transformed this field where the spring breeze blows into a castle. How brave it is to have drawn this out!'

He rubbed her back and called out, 'Rejoice and celebrate, Takahime!' Takahime was suddenly moved and said, 'Whether Mokusuke-kami-tsukasa is good or evil, I do not care anymore. Please look at the finish of the work. Starting with the Sumeru-ōkami of heaven and earth, and all the other hundred gods, the duty of the Hinode-kami is now heavenly. From now on, I will work with Mokusuke for the sake of God and for the sake of the people of the world. I will build the reign of Miroku before my eyes, make the people under heaven rejoice, and surprise the god-official of the San-go-kyō, the Mizu-no-Mitama, as well as Higashinowake and the other hundred officials, and make them say 'Afun!''

Suddenly regaining her energy, she took Eugenbau's hand, passed through the front gate of the pavilion that had just appeared, and advanced toward the inner palace. The Magatsu-mitama is truly detestable.
