Chapter 1: Spring の菊 [1316]

春の菊〔1316〕

§ 1

The mountains of Ashibiki in all directions are showing signs of spring, and even the branches withered by winter have begun to bud in the spring scenery.

The heat haze has begun to shimmer, flickering here and there, and the Misono of Tengoku seems as if it is about to open now, upon Kogitayama.

Small birds sing and butterflies dance, and the wind blowing in feels somehow soft and gentle.

Stepping out into the tranquil garden, the two, Okiku and Ochiyo, play in the shade of the blooming Shiramomo, where butterflies flutter and turn their sleeves.

Born of the same womb, they are as close as sisters, loving and respecting each other, and the eyes of others who see them are also very pure.

They are truly enviable.

To such a place, a man and a woman climb the Kifuhan with a brisk step.

A rugged man, tall enough to pierce the clouds, leading a middle-aged woman, appears, pointing toward the Hironiha of the Ōmon.

Seeing Eugenbau no Mokusuke and Takahime chasing butterflies under the peach tree where Okiku and Ochiyo are playing together in a friendly manner,

Takahime: 'Hey, are you two coming to worship at this mansion?'

Okiku: 'I do not know what kind of auntie you are, but you have come to worship well. Come, I shall guide you.'

Takahime: 'Even if you do not guide me, I am not blind. I know the reception well enough... However, what I asked is whether you are a believer here, or perhaps the daughter of some official; that is what I wish to hear.'

Okiku: 'Still, auntie, that big man, is his head not wrapped up? I thought perhaps my eyes were bad again. You should not catch a girl and talk logic like that, acting so important, old as you are. Everyone who comes to worship here is a gentle person. You are the first person I have ever met who suddenly points their mouth and says things so full of logic. Truly, what a disagreeable auntie you are. Oh, how foolish. Ochiyo-sama, shall we leave her alone?'

Ochiyo: 'Even so, Okiku-sama, did Magahiko-sama not say that we must treat everyone who comes here with courtesy?'

Takahime: 'What, Magahiko? So he has been smoldering here after all. Let me see, I shall investigate. In any case, there will be no one of any worth. This is the mansion given by the God to the Ikimiya of Hinodewake. Come, Mokusuke-sama, follow me.'

Appearing at the reception, Takahime, with an arrogant face and a voice tinged with slight contempt, said:

Takahime: 'Hey, excuse me, I wish to ask something for a moment. Who exactly is the leader here?'

Bunsuke, who was busy drawing pines on the Hinode at the reception, stopped his brush, tilted his head slightly with dim eyes, and peered at her face:

Bunsuke: 'Your voice sounds as if I have heard it somewhere, but who might you be?'

Takahime: 'What do you mean, who? It is Takahime of the Ikimiya of Giritenjō Hinodewake. Are you not Bunsuke? Well, well, I am glad to see you are in good health.'

Bunsuke: 'Oh, was it Takahime-sama? This is indeed a long time since we last met. I heard that you have been visiting the mansion of Saien recently, and that you have achieved a great promotion. Oh, congratulations. Thank you for stopping by. And where are you going?'

Takahime: 'I did not just stop by; I have come as the founder of Kogitayama by the order of Giritenjō. Come, come, from now on, you shall listen to everything I say.'

Bunsuke: 'My, that is a strange thing to hear. Since this mansion is entirely under the jurisdiction of Yashimamuchi-no-Mikoto of the Saien mansion, if you were to come here, there should have been a prior notification. Also, if you are coming as the founder of this mansion, we must make appropriate preparations for your welcome, but what a sudden matter this is. I doubt even the founder, Matsuhime-sama, knows of this. In that case, I cannot just sit here. I must report this. Please wait a moment. I shall go and tell the founder of this matter...'

Speaker: 'What, Matsuhime as the Kyōshiyu? She is my kerai, and as you know, I had her looking after Takashiroyama, but since she is a weak-willed creature, she was deceived by Tamanohime of Osechi and surrendered to Ananai-kyō, abandoning the Uranai-kyō.'

Speaker: 'Hmph, do not make a fool of me; I am a little different from a human like you. I am the general manager of the Saien-kan, the god known as Tokiokishi-gami, also called Mokusuke-sama. Now, hurry and welcome us; if there is any rudeness, it will not be to your benefit.'

Yōgen: 'I am Mokusuke, one of the famous three crows of Ananai-kyō. Please remember me, and I hope we shall have a good relationship.'

Speaker: 'Yes, I have heard your self-introduction and have had the honor of seeing the noble Mokusuke-sama. Please forgive me if I am mistaken.'

Takahime: 'Hey, Bunsuke, what kind of extremely rude things are you saying? Mokusuke-sama has caught a slight cold lately, and that is why his voice has changed. Even you cannot recite the norito properly when you have a cold, can you?'

Speaker: 'No, I am truly sorry. In that case, I will guide you to the Kyōshiyu-kan from here. Please offer your worship at the shinden. I will tidy up the room and make preparations in the meantime.'

Takahime felt an inexplicable fear about going to the shinden, as if her inner thoughts were being seen through, and she was not inclined to go. So, she began to employ her usual sophistry.

Speaker: 'Hey, Bunsuke-sama, as I said before, the Giritenjiyō Hinodewake-sama is a Tennin of the spirit realm; performing festivals and offering worship is what the Tennin of the heavenly realm do. Furthermore, it is for yatsumado humans like you to offer worship and perform rites in order to pray for help and salvation. Our duty is to transmit the oshihe. Since our tasks differ according to the innen and seirai of our mitama, that is how it is.'

Speaker: 'Even so, Anata, until now you have been performing rites with all your might and offering prayers from early in the morning, have you not?'

Speaker: 'That is a settled matter. Try to think about it carefully. Tadpoles, which are the children of frogs, and the children of catfish, look the same when they are small, do they not? When this Takahime was a tadpole, I had to offer worship like other people, just like the children of frogs. However, as the days pass, even among tadpoles of the same shape, depending on the seirai of the spirit, they separate into those that grow hands and feet and become frogs, and those that become large catfish. To give an example, you are a frog that has succeeded as a tadpole. This Takahime is a catfish, you see. A catfish lives at the bottom of the earth, and if it flicks its tail, this great earth shakes. Since that innen has become clear, if you think of me as the same Takahime as before, your understanding is a little off. Right, Mokusuke-sama? Since there is such an ignorant receptionist in Ananai-kyō, it is a troublesome thing.'
