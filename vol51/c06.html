Suddenly, the Shinsen began to dance, and the fruits offered and the like were dancing wildly in the Kūchiu like gnats, and the Ninjin, Daikon, mountain yams, Mikan, and Kahauo also began to spring and dance. Takahime tilted her head and was Hijiyō impressed.

Takahime: 'My, what a magnificent thing it is when one with high Shintoku performs the Goyō. It seems the Kamisama are in such high spirits that the offerings have soared into the Chiuten and are all dancing. Tell me, Matsuhime-sama, this is truly a grand sight, is it not? Look there, pray. The Kamisama are hanging like bells upon the trees all around, are they not? Eh, can you not see them? It cannot be helped for those whose Shiyugefu is insufficient. Giritenjiyō-sama telling you to cast Matsuhime out... no, to send her to the forest of Ikuta, must have been because of such a Shigumi as this. Ah, the Yahoyorozu no Kamisama are appearing in the Chiukū with such vigor. The navel oranges and such are dancing up to places beyond the reach of the eye, just as you see. Truly, one cannot dispute the power of Shintoku.'

Mokusuke wore an indescribably sour expression, enduring the pain in his head. Takahime, becoming Masumasu carried away by her own Chōshi, was blowing her own Hora.

Matsuhime: 'Now, Takahime-sama, since the Kamisama are in such high spirits, shall we for once open the doors of Yurarihiko, the Moon Ōkami, and the Sun Ōkami-sama?'

Takahime: 'Ah, that is the most essential thing. You, please open them, for I shall speak directly to the Kamisama. Surely the Kamisama will be satisfied to be served by Takahime and attended to by Mokusuke-sama. Ōkami-sama, Mokusuke of the Saien mansion and Giritenjiyō Hinodewake shall take care of You from this day forth.'

Matsuhime stepped forward to the altar and threw open the doors of the inner shrine. Mokusuke and Takahime both cried out, struck by the spiritual light, and the moment they staggered seven or eight steps backward, they tumbled headlong from the sheer cliff onto the jagged rocks, and while letting out strange cries of 'Ugh, Kya!', they endured the pain.

Takahime: 'Mokusuke, come!'

With all their might, they fled up the slope. Seeing this, Hatsukō and Tokukō forgot the pain in their own posteriors and followed the two, running away with all their might. Just then, they collided with Bunsuke, who was tottering up the stairs; Yōgenbō scratched Bunsuke's face and threw him to the bottom of the slope, then dashed off like a cloud into the mist. Takahime, letting out a piercing shriek with her hair in disarray and shaking her massive backside, thinking it would be Taihen if she let her dear man escape, kept a distance of about one chō, crossed a single-log bridge, and fled toward the forest of Ayashi.

About one chō behind them, Hatsukō and Tokukō, shouting 'Oi! Oi!', chased after them along the field path filled with green grass shoots. From the summit of Mount Kokita, the voice of Smart, like thunder, reached the ears of Mokusuke the Yōgenbō: 'Woo-woo-wow, wow, wow.'

From this point on, Smart, with Matsuhime's reply tied around his neck, returned toward the forest of the shrine to report to Hatsuwakahime.
