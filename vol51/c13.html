Chapter 13: 槍襖 [1328]

槍襖〔1328〕

§ 1

Takahime arrived at the Genkuwanguchi and said, 'Excuse me, Goriyōninsama, please do come up. This is my father's Hontaku.'

Ranchi: 'Oh, Arigata. My, the Atari is decorated with nothing but dazzling Shitsupau; it is as if I am looking at the Saugon of the Tengoku Jiyōdo.'

Katahiko: 'Indeed, that is so. Since I was born, I have never seen such a mansion. Even the Reishōden in the capital of Haruna feels like a Hijiyō inferiority when compared to this mansion.'

Takahime: 'Futarisama, it is an embarrassing Abaraya, but please do proceed to the back. Takako, Miyako, go quickly to the back and tell Father and Mother that guests have arrived.'

Takako and Miyako: 'Hai.'

Having answered, the two Jijiyo hid their figures behind the Tsuitate. The painting on the Tsuitate, which was decorated with Shitsupau, depicted a Tsukiyo Kaimen. By whose hand it was painted, one could not say, but from the moment one saw it, one was cast into a mood of Yūgen and grandeur. As the two followed Takahime timidly, walking through the long corridor with a sense of shame, Ranchi turned to Katahiko and said,

Ranchi: 'Katahiko-dono, it is truly like a Ruri-no-Miya, is it not.'

Katahiko: 'Indeed, there are no words to describe it. Shall I say, "This is, this is, the flowery Yoshinoyama"? Led by a Senken Yōchō beauty, we two are proceeding through a precious palace decorated with the Shitsupau of gold, silver, lapis lazuli, coral, agate, giant clam, and crystal; I wonder if we are dreaming.'

Whispering such things, they proceeded further and further into the back. At the point where they reached the end, something like a double-door storehouse stood. From there, seven or eight maidens of twelve or thirteen years, who could deceive the flowers themselves, appeared one after another, and the eldest-looking maiden among them bowed politely and said,

Maiden: 'Ojōsama, where have you been? Your parents were very worried. I was just thinking of going to search for you, when Takako-sama and Miyako-sama said that you were returning with guests, so I came to welcome you. Oh, I am so glad you have returned.'

She said this politely.

Takahime: 'Are you not Satsuki? That was hard work. This person is a Senden-shi of the Sanshinkyō. Now, please guide them to the back. To the room of Hatsuwakahime-sama.'

Satsuki: 'Hai, I understand. Now, guests, I shall guide you.'

Ranchi: 'Oh, this is truly humbling.'

Katahiko: 'If that is the case, I shall accept your offer without reserve.'

Just as they were about to pass through the double doors, Takahime said,

Takahime: 'Excuse me, guests, I will go and meet my parents for a moment, so please wait in the reception room. Satsuki, treat the guests with great courtesy.'

Satsuki: 'Hai, I understand.'

Katahiko: 'Please, Hatsuhanahime-sama, do not trouble yourself for us.'

Ranchi: 'If that is so, we shall be waiting; please show us your face soon.'

Satsuki: 'Hai, I understand. Excuse me for a moment.'

Saying this, she opened the door and vanished from sight. This was a magnificent room in the Taniana given to Takahime. Katahiko and Ranchi, led by the eight maidens, passed through the double doors and entered. The furnishings in the room were neatly arranged, and five chairs were lined up around a round table in the center. The two sat down as Satsuki suggested. It was an indescribably pleasant feeling. The door was firmly locked before they knew it. The two fell into a dream, nodding off.

After a while,

Voice: 'Excuse me.'

Something tapped them on the shoulder. When the two suddenly awoke, there were sake, sushi, and fruits served on beautiful vessels they had never seen before on the table. And a woman of indescribable, exquisite age had jewels inlaid all over her clothing, and their light reflected off the lamps, shining even more brilliantly. The lights of red, purple, blue, crimson, yellow, white, olive, and deep purple were overflowing from her entire body. The two were surprised, thinking it must be a dream. Then, seeing that the lamps were lit in the room, they thought they must have been asleep for quite a long time.

§ 2

Ranchi: 'Ah, I am truly sorry. I was brought into such a magnificent mansion, and I have committed the rudeness of falling asleep. I pray that you will not blame us for our lack of manners and will forgive us.'

The three women all possess features as perfect as if they were carved from eggs, with matching appearances. However, the woman sitting in the center appears to have a certain air of nobility, and looks to be about two years older, a figure of about eighteen or sixteen years of age. The beauty in the center turns to the two men,

Hatsuwakahime: 'I am Hatsuwakahime. I have heard that you are General Ranchi and General Katahiko. It is truly remarkable that you have entered into the faith of the Ananahi. I am on my way to the capital of Haruna to propagate the teachings under the command of the Ōkami, when I was discovered by Niyoiōsama and have stopped here for a while. The person to my right is Akikohime, and the one to the left is Toyokohime. Though they are still young, they are ladies whom the King brought along from the Kōran country as handmaidens. I pray that you will cooperate with us from now on.'

Ranchi: 'Were you the renowned Hatsuwakahime-sama? This is indeed a strange place to meet. As you have said, I am Ranchi. I am a sinner who once became a subordinate of General Kiharuwake and, serving the command of Ōkuninushi, dared to attack the mansion of Saien. However, by the grace of the Ōkami, I have completely repented, received long-standing instruction from Chirikuwake-sama, and while on my way to the mansion of Saien with a letter of introduction, I met Hatsuhanahime-sama, who guided me here. I pray that you will guide us in all things, as we are still lacking.'

Katahiko: 'I am Katahiko. Having followed the same path as Ranchi-dono, I have now become a disciple of Chirikuwake-sama, and having been guided by the Princess at this gate, I have just arrived here. I pray for your kind guidance in every way.'

From this point on, Hatsuwakahime ordered Akiko and Toyoko to serve the two men plenty of sake. The two men, entranced, became intoxicated by the sake and the beauty of the two women, and they became so drunk that they could hardly tell whether they were in heaven, on earth, in the sea, in the fields or mountains, or in the palace. Thus, the doctrines of the Ananahi and the teachings of Chirikuwake were all lost from their minds, and now, only the face of Toyoko for Ranchi, and the face of Akiko for Katahiko, floated before their eyes. Ranchi and Katahiko, led by the hands of the two women, staggered along the corridor and were guided into a beautiful room, where the two men and two women lay down side by side to sleep.

After a while, the two regained consciousness and looked around to find themselves lying on a stone floor inside a room paved with stone. There was no exit anywhere. The four sides were enclosed by smooth marble, as if made of single boards. The two suddenly changed color,

Ranchi: 'Ah, this is terrible. Katahiko-sama, what do you think? I thought I had fallen asleep while being led by a beauty, but have we not been thrown into a stone prison like this?'

Katahiko: 'Indeed, this is a predicament. What should we do?'

Ranchi: 'Even if you ask what we should do, there is nothing to hold onto, and since there is neither an exit nor an entrance to push or strike, there is no help for it, is there? It is precisely at a time like this that we should recite the Amatsu Norito.'

Katahiko: 'Indeed, that is so.'

Though the two were anxious to recite the Amatsu Norito, for some reason, not a single word would come out. Any other words would come out, but it was a mystery of mysteries that only the Amatsu Norito would not come out at all.

Ranchi: 'Ah, it is no use. Katahiko, it seems you are also unable to do it.'

§ 3

Katahiko: 'It is truly a matter of extreme regret. Shall we not shout with all our might? If we do so, someone might hear our voices and come to rescue us.'

Ranchi: 'That would be well.'

The two began to chant the A-O-U-E-I sounds repeatedly, one after another. However, this room, which was about ten tsubo in size and enclosed by stone paving without the slightest gap, could not possibly let any sound escape; the voices echoed back completely, and in the end, both men tore their throats and could only produce raspy sounds.

Ranchi: 'Ah, it is hopeless; there is nothing to do but become mummies here.'

Katahiko: 'Let us resign ourselves to the fact that this is the retribution for our own sins, and perhaps commit a double suicide as men.'

Ranchi: 'There is truly no other way.'

He muttered this in a voice that sounded as if it were stuck in his throat. Suddenly, from beneath their feet, there was a sound as if sharp chisels were crushing rock, and then, sharp spears like bamboo shoots appeared, thrusting through the stone floor.

Ranchi: 'Ah, this is perilous.'

As he recoiled, there was another clatter, and the tips of spears surged forth. In an instant, three, four, five, ten spears stood in a row, piercing through the stone floor without any gaps. From the stone walls that formed the sides, spear tips also emerged from all directions, each making a clattering sound, protruding about three shaku. Before long, the two were completely surrounded by spears, unable to stand straight, lie down, or do anything at all. The spear tips suddenly transformed into snakes, and as they raised their heads in unison to lick the bodies of the two, some spat fire from their tongues, others spat water, and others spat black smoke; they gradually extended and bound the bodies of the two men tightly. The two could not even raise their voices, and they looked at each other's faces. Suddenly, their faces grew haggard, their expressions of resentment became ghastly, and they instantly took on the appearance of hungry ghosts from hell.

At this moment, a voice like a drum was heard from nowhere. When the two strained their ears to listen carefully:

'A-A-A: You have been deluded by the Sangokyo, which deceives the world with the teachings of demons and heretics,

I-I-I: You have disobeyed the orders of Daikokunushi, who resides in the capital of Haruna in India, and abandoned your military duties,

U-U-U: Because of your sin of having foolishly defected to the Sangokyo and fallen into superstition,

E-E-E: Having received permission from the court of Enma, I shall cast you both into the mountain of swords, the chamber of snakes, and the prison of flames,

O-O-O: I shall destroy your cowardly spirits and flesh, and cast you into the very bottom of hell.

Ka-Ka-Ka: It would be well if you repented and apologized to the gods at once; if you persist in your stubbornness,

Ki-Ki-Ki: I shall cast you into the hell of awls and tear your necks apart with the blades of saws,

Ku-Ku-Ku: I shall drive you to the depths of suffering and feed you excrement; how about that?

Ke-Ke-Ke: You insolent ones.

Ko-Ko-Ko: If you say you will repent here and now, I shall spare you this pain, but if you continue to serve the Sangokyo, this is your final hour; what is your answer?'

A voice like thunder echoed.

Ranchi: 'I am a warrior who has commanded three armies. Once I have submitted to the Sangokyo, I shall never bend my convictions. Come, do whatever you will with me. Even if my flesh is destroyed and I meet with any torture, my spirit shall leave this body, ascend to the heaven of the Lord God, lead the divine army, and crush your demonic army into smithereens. Do whatever you wish.'

Once again, a great voice came from nowhere:

'Well, well, you are a stubborn thing indeed.

Shi-Shi-Shi: If you persist in your defiance, I shall crush your spirit and flesh, and instead of ascending to Takamagahara, I shall use the forces of the Third Heaven to count your evil deeds and destroy you upon the tips of my spears.

Su-Su-Su: It would be for your own sake to repent honestly and follow what I say.'

何処ともなく又もや大きな声、

『さてもさても合点の悪い代物だなア。

シシシ強太う致して我を張りよると、汝が霊肉を粉砕し、高天原へ上る所か、第三天国の軍勢をもつて、汝が悪業を数へ立て、槍の穂先に亡ぼし呉れむ。

ススス素直に改悪致して、此方の云ひ分についたが汝の身の為であらう。

§ 4

Se-se-se-senaka ni hara wa kaeraremai.

So-so-so-sobani tachi-noboru sono Kensaki, ima ni honoo o haite nanji o yakitsukusu darau. Katahiko mo dōyō da zo. Ichidō shian o sadamete hentafu o itasu ga yokarau.

Katahiko: 'Ta-ta-ta-tatakuna tatakuna, akuma no keiriyaku ni jōzerarete, tatohe kono nikutai wa horoburu tomo,'

Chi-chi-chi-sasatomo osore wa itasanu.

Tsu-tsu-tsu-tsukuna to kiruna to katsute ni itase.

Te-te-te-tengo o itasu to, yagate Ananahi no Ōkami araware-tama-hi, nanji o basshi-tamau beshi.

To-to-to-tohou ni kurete, ika ni Tochimenbō o furu tomo, kesshite sonata wa yurusaremai zo.'

From above the head, again a mysterious voice,

Na-na-na-nani o gotegote to yomahigoto o hōsu ka.

Ni-ni-ni-ninin tomo Ōkuronushi-sama ni hanki o hirugaeshi,

Nu-nu-nu-nutsukeriko to shisetsu o yaburi Ananai-kyō no michi ni,

Ne-ne-ne-negahe utsu ta ōchiyakumono,

No-no-no-nozomi to araba, kono yari no hosaki o kuwaiten sase, nodo to iwazu, atama to iwazu, hara to iwazu, tsuite tsuite tsukimakutte yarau ka.'

Ranchi: 'Ha-ha-ha-hara nari to nodo nari to,'

Hi-hi-hi-hiji nari to senaka nari to katsute ni,

Fu-fu-fu-fusoku no nai yau ni, saa tsukei, gappuri tsukei.

He-he-he-heta na koto o itashite Jigoku no kurushimi o ukena.

Ho-ho-ho-boke yarau-yatsu, kono Ranchi wa, nanji gotoki akushin ni hekotareru yau na yowamushi de wa nai hodo ni, koi wa manaita no ue ni noserarureba kesshite hanemo ugokimo itasanu. Bushi no hana to utaware-taru kono Ranchi, Katahiko ryōnin wa issun mo ugokaba koso, dai-banjaku-shin da, katsute ni itashita ga yokarau.'

Thus, they resisted, emitting raspy, intermittent voices.

Strangely, the spears stood up like a forest, and fire burned in flames. Snakes and centipedes gathered all over their bodies, but they felt neither pain nor itch. Both men, believing this to be entirely the protection of the gods, prayed to the Ōkami and focused only on ascending to heaven as quickly as possible.

Ah, how shall these two be saved?
