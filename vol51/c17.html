狸相撲〔1332〕

§ 1

As it was nearing dawn, Okiku thought she would return once to Kogitayama, which was not too far away, so she picked up twenty or thirty stones lying in the darkness, and while counting one, two, three, she threw them toward the place she thought they were, saying to herself, 'Ah, with this I have taken my revenge on Bunsuke-sama. They are only stone balls like bullets in the dark, but it would be even more interesting if one of them hit.'

While saying this to herself, she returned nimbly. The two men had their noses and foreheads struck by the pebbles Okiku had thrown in the Ayashi forest, and they continued to groan for about three days, surviving on pieces of bread from their pockets until they could finally move their hands and feet, so they proceeded with their staffs as strength, determined to find the whereabouts of Takahime and Mokusuke and to take revenge.

The great trees of the Ukiki forest, such as the zelkova, fir, and pine, began to catch their eyes as they stood in dense, wide clusters. All around this area, both inside and outside the forest, was filled with miscanthus grass that would swallow one's body. Furthermore, thickets of Shinodake and Kodake were scattered here and there. However, as the army of General Lunch had been stationed there, at least a reasonably wide path remained open.

As the two came along, dragging their feet, they saw Takahime at the base of a camellia tree, covered in mud and wearing her haori inside out, with two large tanuki accompanying her, knocking down camellia flowers and sucking on them as if they were sweet. Takahime was stuffing grass and grabbing horse dung to twist into a hole in a piece of rotten bamboo, working with all her might, eyes fixed on her task, muttering something to herself as she packed the bamboo tube.

Hatsu: 'Hey, isn't Takahime being deceived? Look at that, aren't those two large tanuki shaking the camellia tree and sucking the flowers? And there is Takahime, wearing her clothes inside out; she might be getting tricked by them.'

Toku: 'Indeed, indeed, they are large tanuki. It is punishment for them escaping after hitting our heads in the dark, and now she is being toyed with by old tanuki. Let them be, let them be, it is a good show to watch.'

The two hid themselves in the miscanthus and watched without taking their eyes off them, wondering what Takahime would do and where those tanuki would go. The tanuki held camellia leaves in their mouths, covered their heads with the flowers, and after shaking their bodies three or four times, they turned into beautiful maidens of fourteen or fifteen years of age. Then, Takahime, led by the hands of the two maidens, was pulled toward a certain magnificent fire lookout tower while keeping her eyes closed and shaking her head.

Seeing this, the two were greatly impressed by the tanuki's skill in transformation.

Hatsu: 'Hey Toku, isn't Takahime being pulled into that magnificent fire lookout tower?'

Toku: 'Yes, she certainly went in. But those tanuki, they transform so sweetly. Takahime probably thinks one is Mokusuke and the other is Imoriwake-dono. Let us follow them and see what is being done to Takahime.'

Hatsu: 'That sounds interesting, come, let us go.'

Toku: 'We must go without making any footfalls, or it will be no good if the tanuki notice us; quietly, quietly.'

The two walked on tiptoe, approached the side of the fire lookout tower, and peeked through a knot hole in the door. When they looked, the tanuki, who had been transformed into beautiful women, had revealed their true forms again, grabbing mud to throw at Takahime, pulling leaves, and doing various things. Finally, they brought a large pile of cut miscanthus, wrapped Takahime's body in it, and set it on fire all at once. Takahime was enveloped in flames, let out a painful voice, and shouted, 'Help me, help me!'

When it came to this, no matter how hateful Takahime might be, as a matter of human feeling, they had to save her. Thinking they would rescue Takahime and catch the two tanuki, they kicked open the door and jumped in, only to fall into a cesspit, where they were bathed in gold from head to toe and became living Buddhas of a golden-yellow color.

Speaker: 'Yes, that person is a Sumaukichigahi, so we have granted him a Tokubetsu permission. Speaker: 'How would you fare against him?'

Speaker: 'Indeed, it would not be a match at all. However, as I am feeling Temochibusata just acting as a Miyaku like this, if you request it, I do not mind taking on those two.'

Speaker: 'Oh, is that so? In that case, I shall consult with the Toshiyori. Please wait a moment.'

The Giyōji ran to the seat of the Tōdori, whispered something in a 'busha-busha' manner, then flew back to the east seat to whisper with the Tōdori and the Toshiyori, and finally appeared before Case, saying:

Speaker: 'Well, the Tōdori and the Toshiyorishiu are in Sansei. Please, by all means, try a Toriku. And your name seems a bit too long; could you perhaps give us a simpler name?'

Speaker: 'Marishiten, Niōgadake, or Goliath—would any of those not suffice?'

Speaker: 'In that case, how about you take the name Ukiki-no-mori?'

Speaker: 'Hmm, that is excellent. Please, I leave it to you.'

Speaker: 'Yes.'

The Giyōji replied, ascended the Dohyō, waved his paper fan, and shouted: 'East, Ukiki-no-mori! West, Oetayama and Korobeyama! Both at once, the mountain-opening of the day, Ukiki-no-mori will finish them!'

The spectators clapped their hands like rain and hail, 'pishya-pishya-pishya,' and each beat their bellies like tanuki, shouting and cheering loudly. Hatsu and Toku, the two, whispered to each other:

Speaker: 'Ha, interesting, a newcomer has come. We two must throw him out of the sixteen-straw-bale Dohyō and receive great applause. How ridiculous, for both of us to take him on together; one is enough.'

Whispering thus, they ascended the Dohyō. First, Hatsu-kō appeared on the west side, stomped his feet, scooped up sand in his hands to rub onto his body, and from both sides, they measured their breathing like cats stalking prey. The Giyōji pulled his fan and shouted, 'Yatsu!' They grappled, but somehow Oetayama's body was slippery and smelled so foul it was unbearable. However, having boasted so grandly, Case felt that if he did not defeat this fellow, he would lose his honor, so he pushed with all his might. As the dung-smeared body slipped like a loach or an eel, he was tricked, fell face-down into the center of the Dohyō, stuffed his mouth full of sand, and blood seeped from his teeth. The Giyōji raised his fan toward the west. The spectators shouted 'Waai, waai' all at once. Case, unable to bear his frustration, became desperate, pulled out the four pillars, and attacked Oetayama and Korobeyama in every direction. The two also pulled out pillars, rampaged back and forth, and finally, their strength exhausted, all three collapsed on the spot. Here and there, the sound of 'pon-pon-pon' echoed like drums; these were the voices of many mame-danuki, beating their bellies and laughing as they returned to their old nests.
