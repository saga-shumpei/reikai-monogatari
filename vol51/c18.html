Reciting this poem composed by the Kiuseikyōshiyu, and bathed in the light of the Oborozukiyo, there came one dressed in Waraji, Kiyahan, Minokasa, and Kongauzue; it was Gariya, who had been an aide to General Ranch. By the side of the road, many new graves were lined up. While in the camp, Gariya had been adored by a beautiful young woman named Marie of the Ukiki Forest, and during his stay, they had continued to meet in secret whenever the opportunity arose. His relationship with Marie had begun when General Ranch issued an order to capture all women in the vicinity, regardless of age, and bring them to the camp to perform cooking and other military duties. At that time, Gariya, being in charge of that task, broke into Marie's house and said to the elderly couple,

Gariya: 'All the women in this village must be conscripted for military service. As there is a fear that they will not only be made to cook but also be violated by many fierce and wicked soldiers, all the women must flee before tonight.'

Marie's father was the village headman. He immediately passed the word to the village, and following mountain paths they knew well, or carrying bread as they pleased, they hid themselves in the mountains and forests. Marie, believing that all the women in the village had been saved from danger through Gariya's kind arrangements, thought to herself,

Marie: 'I do not mind if I represent the women of the village and offer myself as a human sacrifice. Besides, even though he is a Brahmin soldier, where else could such a gentle warrior be found? If I were to have a husband, I would wish to be with such a warrior...'

She developed a strange sympathy, and ignoring her parents' attempts to stop her, she waited alone in the house, hoping that the Brahmin army would come to capture her soon. And fortunately, this house of Marie's was designated as Gariya's quarters. Gariya, worried about what the General would think if he alone kept a woman while his colleagues had none, hid her in the storehouse and continued to meet her in secret whenever he could. However, Marie's body grew thinner and weaker day by day, and eventually, she entered the register of ghosts. Gariya then secretly buried Marie's remains in this graveyard at night and erected a marker. As there was no suitable marker readily available, he stood another tombstone upside down, intending to give her a proper memorial when the time came, but just then, he surrendered to Chikunowake. Gariya had received one hundred days of moral training in the Forest of Kuru, and after that, he was granted a second round of training at Thames Pass; he was on his way to the Hall of Saien to study with Case, having obtained a letter of introduction from Chikunowake. He felt he must visit Marie's grave once to mourn her, but he thought it would be inconvenient to do so while in the company of Case, so he said,

Gariya: 'I am going to bury some potatoes just around here; you go on ahead, and I will catch up with you at the Ukiki Forest later...'

Having successfully shaken off Case, he entered the valley, passed the time by playing with the water or chasing crabs, and taking advantage of the dim moon that had risen in the eastern sky, he arrived at this spot. Gariya approached Marie's grave and, with tears in his eyes, lamented,

Gariya: 'The water-colored moonlight flows / The graves stand in a row, deep blue / Ah, but Marie, my love / Thou art not a mermaid of passionate flame / Wishing for death, thou didst embrace the grave / And leaving me behind, thou hast gone far away / I, who remain behind / Shed tears silently / The silk of thy heart / Circles around Gariya's heart / And soon a peach-colored atmosphere / Wraps around the surroundings / Yet the blue graves / Do not move even a shadow upon the earth / Only thy figure, like a phantom, trembles in the moon.'

Singing thus, he came step by step to the shade of the trees where camellias were blooming.

Gariya sat on a natural stone bench under a camellia, took out a flint, lit his tobacco, and while exhaling, he was leaking a breath of deep emotion while gazing at the other side of the forest of Ukiki.

Gariya: 'The transience of the world is the way of the world; things change as they change. Only four months ago, there were many barracks-style camps built in rows, but before I knew it, it seems a great castle has been built. Well now, whose residence could it be? It is a matter I cannot comprehend.'

He muttered to himself, closed his eyes, and fell into deep thought. Somehow, human voices could be heard from nearby. Gariya stood up abruptly and approached, searching while peering through the moonlight. When he looked, three men were stark naked in the midst of the overgrown cogon grass, crawling on all fours, and chirping about things as they pleased.

Gariya: 'How strange. Even though it is spring, the night is still cold. And what is this, three rough men in such cogon grass, what on earth are they doing? Ha, they must have been bewitched by the tanuki of the forest of Ukiki. I must be careful.'

He thought, but then reconsidered.

Gariya: 'Wait, wait. I must hear what the three of them are saying, and after I get a rough idea of who they are, I cannot know what kind of disaster I might encounter. First of all, knowing the opponent is the most important thing in any encounter.'

He reconsidered and leaned on his staff, peering and thinking.

Naked One: 'Hey, Tōdayama, that fellow called Marishiten is a weakling who is all talk. When he got caught in my grip and was stretched out in the very center of the sumo ring, his attitude was something else, wasn't it?'

Naked Two: 'You call yourself Maketayama, but weren't you strangely strong? Perhaps he was overpowered by your strength.'

Naked One: 'Don't talk nonsense. I was strong and he was weak. It is a rule that has been set for tens of thousands of years that the weak lose. However, didn't that fellow get startled and run away like clouds and mist?'

Gariya:

'Haha, Case, you were fooled by a tanuki and were doing Sumau. And it seems you were grappling with someone who had fallen into a Kusotsubo. It has started to stink somehow. I must shout out loud to wake you up, or it will be no good.'

Saying this, he gathered his breath in his lower abdomen and shouted, 'Uuu!' The three of them, misinterpreting it as the attack of a wild beast, crawled out sluggishly while stark naked, and as if they had agreed, they all gathered at the root of the camellia.

Gariya: 'Oi, are you not Case? I am Gariya. What is this, becoming stark naked in a place like this...'

Case: 'Un, brother, you should have come a little earlier. The tanuki Sumau was very lively, ahahahaha.'

Hatsu: 'Ehehehehe.'

Toku: 'Stinking, stinking, stinking, utsufufufu, it is not interesting at all. We have been turned into dung.'

Case: 'You are all stinking fellows, every one of you.'

Gariya: 'Ahahahaha, ah, the day has broken.'

The three of them looked around with mud-covered faces. Their dung-covered Kimono emitted a strange stench, hanging on the branches of the nearby green tree like a dead crow that had been soaked in water. When they peered into the clear spring under the camellia... it was a Kusotsubo filled with stench. Golden flies buzzed and attacked the four men's faces as targets, crawling into their eyes, lurking in their nostrils, and busily fussing over the corners of their mouths.

'This is unbearable.'

With that, Gariya and the three naked men ran away to the north, to the north, with all their might.

ガリヤ『オイ、お前はケースぢやないか。拙者はガリヤだ。何だ、こんな処へ赤裸になりよつて……』

ケース『ウン、兄貴か、もう一足早く来ればよかつたにな。大変狸相撲がはづんでゐたよ、アハハハハ』

初『エヘヘヘヘ』

徳『臭い臭い臭い、ウツフフフフ、糞面白うもない。糞にされて了つた』

ケース『揃ひも揃つて臭い野郎だな』

ガリヤ『アハハハハ、ああもう夜が明けた』

三人は泥まぶれの顔をして其処等を見まはした。糞まぶれの着物は異様の臭気を放ち、傍の青木の枝に烏の死んだのを水に漬けた様な形になつてブラ下つて居る。椿の下の清泉は……と覗いて見れば臭気紛々たる肥壺であつた。金色の蠅がブンブンと四人の顔を目標に襲撃し、目をせせつたり鼻の穴へ潜伏したり、口の角などを頻りにいぢり出した。

『此奴ア堪らぬ』

とガリヤ及び三人の裸は一生懸命に北へ北へと逃げて行く。
