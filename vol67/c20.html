Chapter 20: Crooked Spirit の陋呵 [1722]

曲津の陋呵〔1722〕

§ 1

Inside the Jiyōnai castle of Tarahan, before the throne of King Karapin, Samori and Umori along with numerous jiushin began a council of Odahara, racking their dried-up brains and squeezing out unhelpful chie as a daichinji had occurred where the taishi had hidden himself from the dennai and vanished without a trace, leaving steam rising from their yakuwanatama.

Karapin: 'By the way, Samori-dono, my crown prince, who has been sunk in gloomy iūtsu for the past three days, still has not returned; what on earth shall we do? Can you not think of some good idea?'

Samori: 'Ha, I am truly filled with awe and deeply regret this. Though I am in the post of denchiu kantoku, this raushin has no other way to apologize to the daiō than with my very own death.'

Karapin: 'Does not your son also remain unreturned? I imagine that he, who is habitually favored by the taishi, has wandered off with your son into some deep mountain recesses, or something of the sort.'

Samori: 'That undisciplined good-for-nothing son of mine, spoiling himself on the taishisama's kotoba, always behaves most fearfully as if he were an iujin. I have constantly lamented his insolent behavior, inflicting various reprimands and giving warnings upon him, yet on the second score, he indulges in abuses and ridicules such as calling people yakuwanatama, antiques, or decorative ornaments in the alcove, and taking advantage of the taishisama's profound affection, he refuses to listen to what his parents say. He is truly a troubled, unfilial, and disloyal fool. If by good fortune my son should return this time, I am resolved to confine him in a secret room, explain the principles of things thoroughly to him, and if even then he refuses to listen, though he is my only son, for the sake of the royal house and for the sake of the nation, I shall take his life with my own hands, ending his days to remove the calamity of the country. I beg you for some temporary forbearance. In due course, without fail, he will safely return to the castle.'

Karapin: 'Ah, your son too has received this new education and seems to have grown considerably worse in character. However, my taishi is also a taishi. He utters nonsensical chatter incompatible with our national polity, such as plebeianism or egalitarianism, grumbles that aristocratic life is not to his liking, displays a discontented visage day and night, lets my cautions pass like the wind past a horse's ears, and has become a figure quite beyond control. This is entirely the retribution for my having been temporarily charmed in spirit by evil forces in the past and having committed the atrocious sin of not being tolerated by heaven and earth, and now in my old age, I am made to suffer unbearable heartaches. Ah, whatever may come to pass is the karma of past lives. Do not trouble your head overmuch anymore, Samori-dono. As for the matter of the taishi, I abandon all hope as of this very moment.'

Samori: 'Your awe-inspiring words, Your Highness—what manner of apologies we your vassals should offer, I know not; I am truly filled with the utmost fear and trembling.'

Umori-dono: 'If the taishi does not return, we must surely devise some remedial measures. I desire to hear your opinion. In the case of such a momentous emergency, there is no need for any hesitation whatsoever, so please lay bare the bottom of your heart without reserve.'

Umori: 'Ha, I am deeply awed. Ever since the taishisama's secret departure, I have dispatched members of the household in all four directions to search for His Highness's footsteps, yet to this moment we have obtained no auspicious news. For three days and three nights, this Umori has also grieved his heart, tormented his breast, and been unable to take even a proper meal. Turning our gaze back to domestic affairs, we find the voices of popular discontent in every place, reaching a situation where a momentous crisis might erupt at any moment. In addition, rumors that the Brahman army is launching an invasion rage furiously, the minds of the people are filled with apprehension and anxiety, mountains, rivers, grasses, and trees lose their color, and the situation is precisely about to manifest a hell of agonizing screams. Under such times of many disasters and troubles for the nation, the departure of the taishi lord is, shall we say, like a sickly child given a lotus root, or like bees stinging a swollen face; it is truly a fearful circumstance. The condition of the Tarahan country is like a candle in the wind; to save the nation before it collapses and to prop up the great mansion as it threatens to fall is utterly beyond the power of a single tree or a single pillar. In any case, on this occasion, high and low must be of one accord, a hundred million people with a single mind, dedicating the utmost sincerity within our power to sacrifice ourselves for the national crisis, without which we cannot succeed. In such a time of critical peril and ruin, Arina, the son of Samori-dono who seduced and coaxed the taishi lord and lured him away from the dennai, is a traitorous and wicked vassal whom neither heaven nor earth will pardon. First, to govern the nation and the people's minds, we must cast aside feelings of closeness and distance, abolish the distinctions between high and low, make truth truth, falsehood falsehood, and evil evil, clarify rewards and punishments with an impartial and unselfish attitude, and display a model of good government to the world. Awed though I am, I believe that as our very first step, we must execute the disposition of Samori's son Arina. Following that, I consider it the most appropriate and best course of action that his father, Samori-dono, should perceive his responsibility at this juncture, apologize for his crime before the imperial steps, and, for the sake of offering an excuse to the subjects below, voluntarily beg to lay down his bones. Nay, I am convinced it is what the teachings of state law prescribe. Your Highness, I earnestly desire that by your wise and brilliant decisive judgment, you will rigorously restore official discipline, reject stubborn and shameless officials, and thereby make it universally known to the people that Your Highness is an enlightened ruler.'

King: 'Well, Umori's words are plausible enough, but today the whereabouts of the Crown Prince are still unknown, and the location of Alina is also unknown; since it is a time of chaos, Samori's punishment need not be rushed in such a manner.'

Umori: 'Though such are Your Highness's gracious words, at a time of national crisis and imminent peril, I consider that such sluggish disposition will rather endanger the state. Umori respectfully submits that Your Highness resolve this with imperial decision, achieving a solution with the swiftness of wind and thunder, and boldly cutting through the tangles like a master swordsman.'

King: 'You, Umori Sakurens, think of the royal house and think of the state; that sincerity is truly praised by me. However, our state reaches down to me as the fifteenth generation, and with the imperial lineage continuing unbroken without the slightest blemish, it has become the center of the nation's respect, maintaining the state of Tarahan even if small. Yet now, the Crown Prince dislikes aristocratic life and has come to dash out from within the palace, so at last neither royal government nor autocratic rule can possibly be sustained forever. Even if the Crown Prince should return to the castle, he would likely not care to succeed me and reign over the Tarahan state. Rather than that, I think I would wish to make the princess Banna the successor, introduce a suitable adopted son, and have them inherit the royal house; what do Samori, Umori, and the other senior vassals think about this?'

Samori: 'Grieving Your Highness's sacred breast, as a subject I cannot bear to idly prolong my worthless life and sit watching Your Highness's worries. As Umori says, whether as a subject or as a vassal, since I am an enemy of the royal house and a destroyer of the state, in order to apologize, right here in your honorable presence I shall rip open my wrinkled belly and make amends for a crime of ten thousand deaths. Lord Umori, please by all means strive in your loyal service for the sake of the state. Your Highness, if it must be so.'

Before he could even finish speaking, with the prepared short sword he drew it from its scabbard and was about to plunge it into his left flank, when Princess Banna hurriedly ran out from within the bamboo blinds,

Princess: 'Samori Gandī, do not be hasty. Now why do you not prolong your fleeting life and devote your sincerity for the sake of the royal house and the state? Do you think that by dying you become loyal? Do you think your excuses will hold? There is a limit to how deluded you can be.'

At this word like the cry of a crane, Samori placed both hands flat on the floor as if startled, pressed his white-haired head against the tatami, shook his voice, squeezed out tears, and with his spoken words broken and disjointed,

Samori: 'Ah, I truly have no excuse to offer for presenting such an unseemly and flustered appearance. I humbly beg for your gracious pardon.'

Umori: 'Ah-ha-ha-ha, Lord Samori, is this not cowardice? Once a man has made the resolute decision to die, do you begrudge death and yearn with attachment to life simply because of the princess's words? With such an effeminate spirit, how well you have managed to fulfill the office of Samori until now. Do you know a shred of shame?'

Thus the perverse and lawless Umori Sakurens was urging Samori to commit suicide. Until ten years prior, when Samori Gandī had been serving as Umori, he had been promoted to household steward, and as Umori advanced in glory to Samori, he too was promoted and attained the heavy post of Umori. That he obtained his present position was entirely due to the mediation of the current Samori. Yet the foul-hearted Umori treated Gandī, who had rendered him great favors, as a nuisance, took advantage of this latest failure to force Samori to commit ritual disembowelment, intended to take his place and supplant Samori to arbitrarily disturb state affairs, wait for the opportune time to have Princess Banna marry his younger brother Yale, manipulate the state through his own clan, and become a maternal relative so that his power would shine throughout the land and he might achieve his long-cherished ambition.

King Karapin did not dream in his wildest nightmares that Umori Sakurens harbored such ambitions, and at a moment of life-or-death crisis, believing that none among the many senior vassals could save the state other than this Umori, he deepened the degree of his trust all the more.

Even so, he could not bring himself to openly voice raising his younger brother Yale to the throne, having him stand alongside Princess Banna to inherit the royal house, and reigning supreme over all affairs of state. Therefore, he deliberately babbled on with the following sorts of things in a rambling manner at the privy council meeting, attempting to probe the minds of the king and the senior vassals first.

Umagami: 'I declare to Denka, "Speak your mind without hesitation or reserve for the sake of the state today." With this imperial command in mind, and for reference, I shall now express my opinion to Denka and all the senior officials present. Whether you choose to adopt it or not is of no concern to this subject whatsoever. As I calmly contemplate the situation of the realm, the kingdoms of the world are falling one by one, and it is the contemporary trend that all are shifting toward popular politics and republican regimes. In addition, the crown prince, who is of the utmost importance, seems to favor democracy and is constantly advocating communism. Ever since the opening of the country, I have offered day and night prayers with the desire that this royal house, which has endured for fifteen reigns, might solidify the foundation of unchangeable eternity and that its prosperity might shine forever with the sun and moon in a corner of the moon's land; yet now, at the present day, I feel a sense of uncertainty.'

At these unexpected words, the King, along with Samori and other senior retainers, fell silent as if struck by cold water, not even drawing a deep breath. After a while, King Karapin tensed the muscles of his face and said,

King: 'These are words of utmost unexpectedness from Umagami. To abolish our royal house, which has reigned by heavenly mandate, and to establish republican politics is the height of disloyalty and infidelity. Umagami, it seems you too have been infected by the evil trends of the times, and the foundation of your conscience has begun to wobble. With such a spirit, how can you possibly support our state? Reflect well upon this.'

At these words, the elderly retainers seated in a row opened their troubled brows slightly, and all together voiced their agreement with the King's declaration. Samori stood up in indignation, tears welling in both eyes, drew close to Umagami's side, placed his hand on the hilt of his short sword, and said with a trembling voice,

Samori: 'Thou, Umagami the Sakurensu, vainly wields deceitful arguments, puts on the front of a loyal retainer and righteous warrior, but harbors the claws and fangs of a jackal in thy heart—an outrageous, lawless, disloyal, and unrighteous scoundrel! To retract the eternal imperial rule and transform it into a republican regime is utter nonsense and the height of disloyalty and infidelity! Since matters have come to this, I, Samori, driven to desperation, shall sever thy very life and eradicate the root of calamity for the state. Prepare thyself!'

Before he had even finished speaking, he attempted to leap toward Umagami. Princess Banna spoke out once again,

Princess: 'Samori, wait a moment. You are in the presence of the King. Violence with a blade within the palace is strictly forbidden by state law. Have you gone mad? Have you lost your composure? Samori, calmly distinguish between good and evil, right and wrong.'

Raising his voice, Samori said,

Samori: 'Even though this is the strict command of the Princess, I, this Samori, being inherently disloyal and unrighteous, will die a thousand deaths to atone for my crime. As for the fear of breaking the law, there may be none; but since leaving this Umagami alive would make him a great traitor who destroys the royal house and ruins the state, my intention is to cut off Umagami's life. Please, I beg of you, forgive this action.'

And once again he slashed forward. Taken aback, Umagami, acting just like Moronao in the pine corridor, cried out repeatedly,

'Lord Samori, we are within the palace! We are within the palace!'

and ran this way and that to escape. The senior retainer Halchin, acting just like Kakogawa Honzō, gripped Samori tightly from behind with brute strength, embraced him, and immobilized him in a bear hug. Samori cried out,

Samori: 'Hey, let go! Do not interfere! This is a momentous crisis for the royal house. Now is the time to banish the root of calamity for the state!'

Though he struggled and flailed, embraced by the powerful Halchin, he gnashed his teeth in deep mortification and dropped his short sword onto the floor with a clatter. Seizing this opportunity, Umagami fled cowardly and miserably like a wisp of cloud or mist.

Amidst this uproar, the Crown Prince, together with Alina, calmly passed through the castle gate. Umagami the Sakurensu, who had just fled for his life with hair disheveled, bumped squarely into Alina's chest right at the gateway in his agitation, and both tumbled down from the steps in front of the gate onto the street about two or three ken below. Fortunately, Alina sustained no injuries, but Umagami the Sakurensu broke his shin, crawled on all fours like a clumsy beast, and fled back toward his home for his very life like a rat chased by a cat.

原文（近代文語）

右『殿下に申上げます。「今日は国家のため遠慮会釈もなく言上せよ」との御令旨、参考のために、殿下を初め一同の重役達に吾意見を吐露致します。御採用下さらうと、下さるまいと、それは少しも臣の意に介する所では厶いませぬ。倩々天下の情勢を考へまするのに、世界の王国は次第々々に倒れ、何れも民衆政治、共和政体と代り行く現代の趨勢で厶います。加ふるに肝腎要の太子の君は平民主義がお好きでもあり、常に共産主義を唱道されてゐるやうで厶います。開国以来、十五代継続遊ばした此王家をして万代不易の基礎を固め王家の繁栄は日月と共に永遠無窮に、月の国の一角に光り輝くべく日夜祈願をこらしてゐましたが、最早今日となつては、どうも覚束ないやうな気分が致します。殿下を初め奉り、諸君の御意見は如何で厶いませうかな』

此意外なる言葉に王を初め左守、その他の重臣は水を打つたる如く黙然として大きな息さへせなかつた。暫くあつてカラピン王は顔面筋肉を緊張させ乍ら、

王『意外千万なる右守が言葉、天の命を受けて君臨したる我王室を廃し、共和政治を布かう等とは不臣不忠の至りだ。右守、汝も時代の悪風潮に感染し、良心の基礎がぐらつき出したと見える。左様な精神で、どうして我国家を支へる事が出来るか。よく考へて見よ』

此言葉に並ゐる老臣等は稍愁眉を開き、一斉に口を揃へて王の宣言に賛意を表した。左守は憤然として立上り両眼に涙を浮べ乍ら、右守の側近くニジリ寄り短刀の柄に手をかけ、声を慄はせ乍ら、

左『汝右守のサクレンス、徒に侫弁を揮ひ、表に忠臣義士を粧ひ、心に豺狼の爪牙を蔵する悪逆無道不忠不義の曲者奴、万代不易の王政を撤回し共和政体に変革せむとは何の囈言、不臣不忠の至り、もう此上は左守が死物狂ひ、汝が一命を断つて国家の禍根を絶滅せむ、覚悟致せ』

と云ふより早く右守に向つて飛びつかむとする。王女のバンナは又もや声をかけ、

王女『左守、暫らく待て、王様の御前であらうぞ。殿中の刃物三昧は国法の厳禁する所、血迷ふたか、狼狽へたか。左守、冷静に善悪理非を弁へよ』

左守は声を励まして、

左『王女様の厳命なれども、もとより不忠不義なる此左守、死して万死の罪を謝し奉る。ついては御法度を破る恐れは厶いませうが、此右守を残しておかば王家を亡ぼし国家を亡ぼす大逆者で厶れば、右守の命を絶つ考へで厶います。何卒此儀はお許し下さいませ』

と又もや斬つてかかる。右守は打驚き松の廊下の師直よろしく、

『左守殿、殿中で厶る　殿中で厶る』

と連呼し乍ら彼方此方へ逃げまはる。重臣のハルチンは加古川本蔵よろしく、左守の後よりグツと強力に任せて抱きかかへ羽抱絞めにして了つた。左守は、

左『エー、放せ、邪魔召さるな。王家の一大事だ。国家の禍根を払ふのは此時で厶る』

とあせれど藻掻けど、強力のハルチンに抱きつかれ無念の歯噛みし乍らバタリと短刀を床上に落した。右守は此隙に乗じて雲を霞と卑怯未練にも逃げ出して了つた。

かく騒ぎの最中へ太子の君はアリナと共に悠然として城門を潜つた。今や生命からがら髪振り乱し、逃げ出して来た右守のサクレンスは狼狽の余り門口にてアリナの胸にドンと許りつきあたり、二人は共に門前の階段から、二三間許り下の街道へ転げ落ちた。幸ひにアリナは何の負傷もせなかつたが、右守のサクレンスは脛を折りノタノタと四這ひとなり生命カラガラ吾家を指して猫に追はれた鼠よろしく逃帰り行く。
