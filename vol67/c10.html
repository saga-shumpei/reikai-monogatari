Chapter 10: スガの Long 者 [1712]

スガの長者〔1712〕

§ 1

The lights of the houses in the port were reflected in the lake, making it look just like a city that never sleeps. The sky shone clear, starlight twinkled across the heavens, and an indescribable, fresh atmosphere drifted in the air. Hundreds of passengers rushed eagerly to cross the pier, some returning to their homes and others seeking lodging, creating an extraordinary bustle for a time.

As Umekō and his party were about to disembark from the vessel, the ship's captain, Arī, suddenly knelt before Umekōtsukasa, shedding hot tears and speaking as follows:

Sendenshi-sama, through an unexpected divine connection, I have been blessed to hear the gospel of the heavenly kingdom, and my habitual delusions have completely cleared away. From this moment on, instead of avenging my father's death, I will cherish and protect the travelers passing by as if they were my own children, returning entirely to the path of righteousness. Please pray over us and guard us so that peace, joy, and fortune may dwell upon our lives. If it were not for this ship, I would wish to accompany you no matter where you go, but since today's circumstances do not permit it, I must bid you farewell with great regret. I pray morning and night that your divine mission along the way will succeed safely and that you will make a triumphant return to the mansion of the Saien. Next, Dariya-sama, Iruku-sama, up until now I have stalked your household as my sworn enemy, but now that this day has come, blown away by the divine wind of Isuzu, I feel a spirit that has returned to a pure and undefiled spiritual body without a single particle of dust lingering in my heart. Since there is no longer any hatred, jealousy, or anger within me, please rest easy. Furthermore, Dariya-sama, you were born from the same mother's womb as I; you are, so to speak, my younger sister, so I sincerely hope we can associate closely from now on. Please also convey my warmest regards to your father.

Iruku: 'Everything must be a matter of karmic connection. I, too, am relieved to hear your words just now. The truth is, having heard rumors from various people that you had been plotting against my father, I was secretly worried about it; but now that I hear these words of yours, I feel as though the world has suddenly become much wider. Since you are an elder brother to Dariya, and I am also a brother to Dariya, shall we three become brothers and sisters, embrace each other warmly under the blessings of the Gods, and journey through this world together?'

Ari: 'Yes, thank you very much. Such great joy is something I have never tasted even once since I was born. Please live together harmoniously as parents, brothers, and sisters. By the way, Dariya-sama, I shall be coming to this port once a month, so please come and visit me again.'

Dariya: 'Yes, thank you very much. When you arrive at this port, please be sure to visit my home. I shall regard you just like a true elder brother.'

Arī: 'By the great and merciful grace of the Imperial Great Deities, The clouds and mist upon my heart have cleared away.'

Dariya: 'Parting from one whom once I deemed a source of fear, And once I thought with tender longing dear. Since I have heard thou art my noble brother's grace, Like mist swept by the wind, all shadows leave this place.'

Arī: 'Though well I knew thou wert a sister not of common blood, By cords of passionate desire my heart was bound and stood. Ah yet, so awe-inspiring are the teachings of the Gods, No path forbidden could I tread against such righteous odds.'

Umekō: 'The stars of the vast heavens brightly shine, And light up both your spirits pure and fine.'

Yorikohime: 'Now then, farewell, I must depart from Lord Arī; Sail safely on the peaceful waves across the lake.'

Arī: 'O Yorikohime, your precious words of Kototama so serene, Have struck deep through my very soul, transforming all within.'

Hanakaohime: 'Since first I trod the divine path of Misogi and of awe, So many varied colors of the universe I saw.'

Thus singing their parting poems to one another, they proceeded through the streets of the port of Suga, where the eaves-lights were glowing, heading toward Iruku's mansion while chanting missionary hymns.

The mansion of Arisu, reputed to be a millionaire of the port of Suga, was surrounded by vast earthen walls, with dozens of splendid residences and storehouses standing in rows. Lush garden trees stood thick here and there, blocking out the sky and forming a natural forest. At the front gate, two guardsmen, troubled by the delayed homecoming of the young master and the princesses, were drinking sake and whispering softly to one another.

Kō: 'Oi, Aru, Jiyōsama has been gone for about half a month now, yet she still has not returned. I wonder what on earth has happened to her. Since she was carried off by pirates on her way back from her excursion to the remote island and there has been no news or tidings whatsoever, the very look of Ōdanna's face lately, full of anxiety, is pitiful to behold. Moreover, Wakadanna went off ten days ago saying he would go search for Jiyōsama's yukuhe, and there is no news of him either, is there? It is just as if a miira-taker had become a miira himself, ah.'

Aru: 'No matter what you say, it is because the metsukeyaku are munō. What is more, if one were to even mention that she was captured by pirates, they would turn matsusao in the face and tremble with fright, so it is simply more than one can bear, na wa. Cats that do not catch mice need not be kept, though, ah.'

Karu: 'That is truly as you say. It was in the spring of kiyonen, when a dorobō stole into this uzuyakata, and I rushed off at once to the metsukeyaku and said to him, "A dorobō has broken in just now, so please come immediately and bind him!" The metsukeyaku fellow made a matsusao face and did nothing but quiver violently like the grandchild of a jishin, and would not come out at once. Right around there seven or eight subordinate metsuke were nodding off, bobbing their heads like a yobune, but when they heard my voice saying that a dorobō had entered, they opened their eyes wide in astonishment, glaring with owl-like eyes, and asked things they had no need to ask—such as what the dorobō's physiognomy was like, how many companions he had, about how old he was, from where he had entered, what the striped pattern of his kimono was like, whether he was a man or a woman, an old person or a child, a cripple or one-eyed—wasting time with dallying, and their calculation was to come strutting along with a clatter of tin just around the time the dorobō had safely gone back. Just as expected, when they did come back, it was after the dorobō had finished his work completely and gone home. It is truly the state of a blind metsuke. We simply cannot live with peace of mind in a world where such dorobō run rampant; those who are called metsuke are people who are of no use, ne.'

Aru: 'Could that be so as well? Receiving a meager salary, being driven about both night and day, and being told to do life-risking work—anyone at all would shrink back. The fellows who become metsuke, every single one of them, are nothing but social dropouts, you see. If there were anyone with even a tiny bit of backbone, who would ever serve in such a trivial office? They lack the learning to be elementary school teachers, they have no capital to go into commerce, and they loathe to labor—in short, fallen shosei-agari take up service solely because hunger is distressing, so you must look upon them with a generous eye, nah.'

Karu: 'However, even granting that metsuke have low salaries and thus should be looked upon generously, the head metsuke fellow puts on a grand air of a commander while carrying himself with self-importance, yet upon hearing of a dorobō he is frightened almost out of his wits—is that not something to be amazed at? Though there is likely no such thing as a decent fellow among modern actors, when the metsukeyaku, whose duty is the protection of the people, are in such a state, the realm will only grow increasingly chaotic. Since the world is one where fish that swallow boats break through the legal net and escape, while small fish and minnows are all securely bound and groaning in prison cells, we cannot possibly live in untroubled ease relying upon the regulations. Is there any way other than to organize a jishudan and protect ourselves?'

Thus they were speaking ill of the metsukeyaku. At that moment, Iruku and Dariya, brother and sister, were sent off by the missionary and returned leisurely.

Aru, both of them, overwhelmed with wild joy as if it were a dream,

Aru: 'This is, this is it, Wakadanna-sama, Jiyōsama, we have been waiting for you with longing.'

Karu: 'Now, now, now, you have returned safely and well. With this, the wrinkles of our testicles have also been smoothed out. The wrinkles on the face of Ōdanna-sama will doubtless also become carefree from today onward. Oh, you have a great many companions with you, indeed.'

Irukū: 'Surely my father must have been waiting for us. Come, guide me quickly. Nay, tell my father that the two of us have returned safely. Meanwhile, I shall wash my feet.'

Leaving Karu at the gate with the words, 'Very well, I understand,' Aru hurried away into Arisu's living room. The master of the house, Arisu, having finished praying to the god Uraruhiko in the innermost room, sat puffing on a tobacco pipe, tilting his head and speaking to himself.

Arisu: 'Ah, is there anyone in this world whose character is as wretched as mine? Inheriting abundant wealth from generation to generation, I feel not the slightest difficulty in daily life, but my essential wife, having given birth to Irukū, suffered a bad postpartum recovery and became a guest of the netherworld of Kuōsen. For three years I kept the empty bed and mourned my wife's memorial rites. When I reflect upon it, the wife of the medicine peddler who has frequented my house since ancient times is the exact spitting image of my deceased wife; thus, suddenly chased by the hound of earthly desires, and knowing full well it was an improper path, I considered the master's absence by the wife's side and approached her time and again, yet she stubbornly refused me no matter what. The flame of love blazed up as if to scorch my very body, and I could endure it no longer; knowing it to be wrong, by using cunning means I dragged that man's wife, Anna, into my mansion, locked her up in the storehouse, forced her to become my wife against her will, and finally she gave birth to my younger sister Dariyū. Yet Anna, too, like my first wife, had a bad postpartum recovery and passed away on the anniversary of the first wife's death. And her husband, mortally grieved at having his wife taken from him, threw himself into the waters of Lake Haru and died. When I think of it, when I think of it, even the bad luck of my own fortune must be the work of Heaven. My two beloved children, whom I relied upon as my cane, my strength, and my pillar, have once again gone missing; even if I remain in this world hugging boundless wealth all by myself, there is no joy in anything, and yet, even if I wanted to die I cannot die. As a reward for the sins I have committed in the present life, after death I shall surely be cast down to the very bottom of hell. When I think of that, lonely though I am, there are moments when I feel I would like to linger in this world even for a single day. Ah, what can I do to escape from this suffering? While praying to the god Uraruhiko, I feel as though the bottom of my heart is being seen right through by the god, and somehow it feels terrifying. Even coming before the god makes me timid and shrinking. Ah, what a lonely thing it is. By now, there can hardly be any likelihood that my two children will return safely. Ah, what on earth should I do?'

So saying, he drooped his head and was lost in tears of regret. Just then the gatekeeper Aru appeared hurriedly with a broad smile upon his face,

Aru: 'Great Master, please rejoice. The two young masters have returned safely.'

Arisu: 'What, the two have returned? And are both of them in good spirits?'

Aru: 'Yes, they are in high spirits. It seems there are about four companions accompanying them. I do not know the details, but I imagine the young master and the young lady returned because they were helped by those people. Since they are washing their feet right now, they will surely come here shortly.'

Arisu: 'That is the happiest news of all. From now on I shall offer my thanks to the god of Uraru, so you people tell that to the head clerk and the maidservants. Make preparations for the evening meal quickly.'

Aru: 'Yes, I understand. If so, Master,'

he said as he hurried toward the kitchen. Arisu turned toward the sanctuary and offered a prayer of thanksgiving.

Arisu: 'Before the august presence of Uraruhiko, the Great Lord and Supreme Ruler of Heaven, Earth, and all creation, I, Arisu, master of the apothecary in the village of Suga, bow down with reverence and offer words of thanksgiving. Thou hast not cast aside even our bodies and souls, which are akin to demons, having piled up sins day and night, and hast allowed my dearest son and my beloved daughter to return safely to our home. For thy vast and profound grace and virtue, I offer my grateful thanks. Until this day, my body has been smitten by the three poisons of greed, anger, and foolishness, wandering in the alleys of the five rebellious acts and ten evil deeds, paying no heed to the poverty and distress of others, running solely after selfish profits, and causing suffering to the countless people who are the august children of the Great Deity. In addition, I have stolen the wives of others, exhausting every measure of wickedness and lawlessness. For thy boundless benevolence, in that thou didst not cast aside even me, a sinner of the utmost gravity, but bestowed thy mercy upon me, my gratitude is inexhaustible. Ah, God, from this day forth I repent my former wrongdoings, dedicate all the wealth handed down from my ancestors unto Thee, select a pure land at the foot of Mount Suga, construct a solemn shrine, atone for even a ten-thousandth part of my sins, and pray that I may be granted blessings in the world to come. I earnestly beseech Thee to graciously and peacefully accept my prayer, and to grant Thy divine protection so that our descendants may flourish eternally and partake of the Great Deity's grace forevermore. Kamunagara Tamachihau Mase.'

アリス『あゝ私程型の悪い者が世にあらうか。親代々から沢山な財産は譲られて、生活上の困難は少しも感じないが、肝腎の女房はイルクを生んだまま、産後の肥立悪く、冥土黄泉の客となり、三年が間空閨を守つて妻の菩提を弔うてゐた。思ひまはせば吾家へ古くより出入する売薬行商人の女房が自分の亡くなつた妻に其容貌そつくりなので、忽ち煩悩の犬に逐はれ、道ならぬこととは知り乍ら、女房の側へ主人の不在を考へて、幾度となく言いよつてみたが、どうしても頑として応じてくれぬ。恋の炎は吾身をこがさん計りに燃え立つて、到底こばり切れないので、無理と知りつつ彼の女房アンナを手だてを以て、吾館へ引張り込み、倉の中へ閉ぢこめておいて、無理往生に女房となし、遂に妹のダリヤを生んだが、又もやアンナは先妻と同様産後の肥立が悪く、先妻の命日に亡くなつて了つた。そして彼の夫は女房を捕られたのが残念さに、ハルの湖水に身を投げて死んで了つた、思へば思へば自分の運の悪いのも天の為す業であらう。杖とも力とも柱とも頼む二人の愛児は、又もや行方不明となり、只一人巨万の富を抱へて、此世に残つてゐても、何一つの楽みもなく、それだと云つて、死ぬにも死なれず、現世に於て犯した罪の報ひによつて、死後は必ず地獄のドン底に落されるだらう。それを思へば淋し乍らも、一日でも此世に永らへて居りたいやうな気もする。あゝ何うしたら、此苦患から逃れる事が出来るだらう。ウラル彦の神様を念じ乍ら、心の底を神様に見透かされるやうな気がして何だか恐ろしいやうだ。神様の前へ出るのでさへもオヅオヅして来る。あゝ淋しい事だ。最早二人の吾子は、無事に帰つて来る気遣ひはあるまい。あゝ何うしたら可からうかなア』

と首をうな垂れて、悔み涙にくれてゐた。そこへ門番のアルが慌ただしく、ニコニコとして現はれ来り、

アル『大旦那様、お喜びなさいませ。お二人様が無事お帰りになりました』

アリス『ナニ、二人が帰つたか。そしてどちらも機嫌ようしてゐるか』

アル『ハイ、シヤンシヤンしてゐられますよ。何だか四人許りお伴れがあるやうで厶います。詳しいことは存じませぬが、若旦那様もお嬢様も、あの方々に助けられてお帰りになつたのぢやなからうかと思ひます。今お足を洗つてゐられますから、軈てここへお出でになりませう』

アリス『それは何より嬉しい事だ。私は之からウラルの神様へ御礼を申上げるから、お前たちは番頭や下女にさう云つて呉れ。早く夕飯の用意をせよ』

アル『ハイ、畏まりました。左様ならば旦那様』

と云ひ乍ら、勝手元をさして急ぎ行く。アリスは神殿に向ひ感謝の祝詞を奏上してゐる。

アリス『天地万有の大司宰神たるウラル彦の大神の御前に、スガの里の薬屋の主人、アリス謹み敬ひ、感謝の辞を捧げます。日に夜に罪悪を重ね来りし、悪魔に等しき吾々が身魂をも見すて給はず、最愛なる吾倅、吾娘を安全無事に、吾家に帰させ給ひし、其広き厚き御恩徳を、有難く感謝致します。今日迄、吾身は貪瞋痴の三毒にあてられ、五逆十悪の巷に迷ひ、人の貧苦困窮を意に介せず、利己一片の利に走り、大神の御子たる数多の人民を苦めまつり、加ふるに人の妻女を奪ひ、悪逆無道の限を尽して参りました。極重悪人の私をも見すて給はず、御恵み下さいました其広大無辺の御仁慈に対し、感謝にたへませぬ。あゝ神様、私は今日より前非を悔改め、祖先より伝はりし一切の財産をあなたに奉り、スガ山の山元に清浄の地を選み、荘厳なる社殿を営み、吾罪悪の万分一をつぐない、来世の冥福を与へられむ事を祈り奉ります。どうか吾願を平けく安らけく、相うべなひ下さいまして、子孫永久に立栄え、大神の御恩徳に永久に浴し得る様、御守護あらむ事を、ひとへに願ひ奉ります。惟神霊幸倍ませ』

§ 4

Shedding tears of Kuwaigo, he offers prayers of Kanshiya and Aiguwan and Kiguwan. Led by his elder brother Ilku, Dariyahime, Umekō, Yorikohime, Hanakahime, and Shigō—a party of six—walked in leisurely.

Arisu: 'Ah, are you my son... or my daughter? How wonderfully you have returned safe and sound. For the past half month, your father has not slept properly even for a moment at night, clinging in prayer to Kamisama. Thanks to that, today I have been blessed to see your safe faces. Now, even if I were to leave this world this very moment, I would have no Kokoronoko. ...I know not who you might be, but thank you so much for escorting and bringing my children back. I respectfully offer you my gratitude.'

Umekō: 'Meeting you for the first time, I am one called Umekō, serving in the entourage of a Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, but aboard the ship Namikirimaru, I became Jitsukon with Lord Ilku, and begged for a night's lodging. I kindly ask for your warm hospitality.'

Arisu: 'That is most wonderful of you to have come. As you know, it is an Abaraya, but since the house is spacious, please stay overnight, however many of you there may be.'

Dariya: 'Father, it was this Kamutsukasa who saved me, you see. It is truly thanks to the Goshintoku of this person that I was rescued from a perilous place, so please offer your thanks. And then, this beautiful person is in the entourage of the Sendenshi-sama, known as Yorikohime-sama and Hanakahime-sama. Also, the person with white hair is a sudden Dōshin-sama called Shigō-sama, but because he is truly a person of fine spirit, wishing also to share in the joy of returning safely to our home, I accompanied them back thinking I would have him perform a house prayer.'

Arisu: 'Oh, oh, to all you Katasama, you are most welcome indeed. Now please, make yourselves completely at ease. You need not hesitate in the least.'

Umekō: 'Yes, thank you very much. Obeying your words, as I am naturally a free-spirited person, I will make myself entirely at home. Now everyone, since the master has given us his permission, please lie down to heal the weariness of your bodies.'

Dariya: 'Father, these people are truly living Kamisama, so we must not be careless. Will you please leave the care-taking entirely to me?'

Arisu: 'Very well, very well. Seeing you all return, my body suddenly feels as though it has grown weary with Kuirō. Though it is rude to you all, I will go to the annex and let myself rest peacefully, so make sure there are no omissions and no rudeness in your hospitality.'

So saying, striking his bent-like-a-prawn waist two or three times with the palm of his right hand,

Arisu: 'Everyone, farewell, I will excuse myself.'

Leaving just this single word, he concealed his body in the tatami room of the annex. At that moment, a clamorous sound was heard coming from the southern sky. When Umekō stepped out into the garden with Yorikohime and gazed in the direction of the sound, the vast sky was engulfed in a tremendous cloud-glow. This was the great flame of the army of General Ōasuwake attacking Barugan Castle and burning down the city, which was dyeing the color of the sky.

と悔悟の涙をこぼし乍ら、感謝と哀願の祈願をこめてゐる。そこへ兄のイルクを先頭にダリヤ姫、梅公、ヨリコ姫、花香姫、シーゴーの六人連れ、悠々として這入つて来た。

アリス『ヤ、其方は倅か、……娘か、能うマア無事で帰つて来た。父はここ半月の間、夜の目もロクに寝ず、神様におすがり申してゐた。其おかげで、今日はお前達の無事な顔を見ることを得たのだ。モウ私はこれつきり、此世を去つても心残りはない。……何れの方かは知りませぬがよくマア吾子を送つて来て下さいました。謹んでお礼を申上げます』

アリス『それは能うこそお出下さいました。御存じの通り、茅屋で厶いますが、家は広う厶いますから、幾人さまたり共お泊り下さいませ』

アリス『よしよし、私もお前達の帰つたのを見て、俄に体がガツカリと草労て来たやうだ。皆さまに失礼だけれど、離室へ行つてゆつくり休まして頂くから、手落のないやう、御無礼のないやう、おもてなしをしておくれや』

と云ひ乍ら、エビの様に曲つた腰を右の掌で二つ三つ打ち乍ら、
