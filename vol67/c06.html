Chapter 6: 浮 Island の Monster 猫 [1708]

浮島の怪猫〔1708〕

§ 1

Namikirimaru is proceeding very gently, beating drums upon the gunwale, across the vast and boundless surface of the lake, pointing toward the southwest. The weather is clear and serene with the gentle air of spring drifting, and sea gulls spreading wings either white, black, or red form flocks of a hundred or a thousand, uttering strange cries to soar through the midair, or rest leisurely among the waves, floating and sinking while fishing for fish. Amboina is spreading its great wings of seven or eight shaku, performing an aerial glide in a straight line. That tranquil mood felt like wandering in the paradise of heaven. Ahead, there is a tall mountain constructed solely of rocks, the very first of Lake Haru's waters. The people of the country call this island mountain the Peak of the Floating Island. It is also called the Rock Mountain of Night Radiance. The ship has advanced mercilessly to about one ri short of this rock mountain. The passengers are all casting their gazes toward this rocky island, attempting to criticize one another regarding the ancient legends and origins concerning this island.

Kō: 'Everyone, please look. That rocky island standing lofty, surpassing the clouds ahead, is the highest mountain in Lake Haru, a sacred mountain harboring various mysteries. Formerly called the Rock Mountain of Night Radiance, a light like the sun and moon shone upon the summit of the rock, and it was respected as a lighthouse for navigation on moonless nights. Speaking of the condition of that mountain figure piercing straight through the clouds, and speaking of its indestructible diamond appearance solidified entirely by rocks, it is an eternally immovable sacred mountain. Those who cross these waters cannot say they have crossed the waters without seeing this mountain.'

Otsu: 'Indeed, the more I look at it, the more magnificent a mountain it is. However, even now at night, does it emit radiance just as it did in ancient times?'

Kō: 'It is so much called the Lake of Haru that there was no darkness. However, perhaps due to the gradually clouding momentum of the world, the light has grown faint with the years, and now it hardly shines at all. And although it used to tower at the center of the lake, before one knew it, it has moved from that center to the east. The eternally immovable rock mountain seems to lack roots and looks like a floating island, and since the west wind appeared to have been extremely fierce, it shifted little by little from the center to the east.'

Otsu: 'Indeed, they say culture moves eastward, so perhaps a cultural wind blew. However, while the sun, moon, and stars all move westward and westward, to have that rock mountain alone move eastward—is that not somewhat contrary to the principles of heaven and earth? If it is an island that floats and moves according to the wind like duckweed, no matter how much it is solidified by rocks, one never knows when it will sink, so one certainly cannot approach it carelessly.'

Kō: 'Please look at the summit of that mountain. You can see twisted, tiny trees that are almost about to wither, barely maintaining their life force in the crevices of the rocks, can you not? A mountain is not respected because it is tall, but respected because it has trees... or so they say, and no matter how high a mountain is, if it is solidified by useless junk rocks and lacks the essential trees, it can hardly have the qualifications of being a mountain. If it could at least serve as a lighthouse, it might retain its value as a mountain, but an occupying rocky mountain with a large surface area and no performance whatsoever makes no sense at all. Also, if it had the function of illuminating dark nights like in the past, protecting passing ships, and allowing them to reach the other shore safely, a rock mountain would be fine, but by today it is already a useless surplus item. In the past, the large rock standing upright like a Niō, particularly noticeable at the summit of that mountain, was called the Akeharu Rock, and the populace respected it as the guardian god of the country. By today, it has not the slightest light, and to make matters worse, that rock has a large vertical crack, making it impossible to know when it will break, and people now call it the Daikoku Rock. Even looking at this, the world will surely not continue just as it is. Since it is said that the heavenly gods manifest wonders on earth to show the transition of the world, judging from this, the rule of Ōkuninushi will probably not last very long either.'

Oto: 'Do you suppose that some fierce beast or other lives on that rocky mountain?'

Kō: 'It is said that all sorts of strange monsters inhabit it. And as for those creatures, they have webbed feet, and they can swim about freely over the surface of the water, while their speed in scrambling up the mountains is simply like a balloon flying through the air... or so the story goes. In ancient times, the two deities, the Sun Goddess and the Moon God, descended from heaven and gathered the eight million deities, radiating a brilliance like the sun and moon. They illuminated not only this lake but the entire country of India, dispelling the vapors of evil spirits, bringing peace to all the people under heaven, and treating that rocky mountain as the sacred body of the Kami. The people of the land used to revere it, but gradually the world has fallen into the degenerate age of the Latter Law, and before anyone knew it, that brilliance has lost its light. Now it has completely become a habitat for fierce beasts that are hard to describe whether as tigers, wolves, nine-tailed golden foxes, or giant serpents. Because of this, people say that if we humans were to set even a single foot on that island, we would instantly fall into the claws and fangs of those savage and wicked beasts; our blood would be sucked, our flesh eaten, and our bones burned to ruin. Fearing this, no one goes near it. If rough winds and waves were to cause a boat to crash against that rocky island, that would truly be "Nirvana and Bliss," and it would be impossible to return alive. Lately, people have been whispering and calling that island the Devil's Island. However, if anyone were to say such a thing in a loud voice, the monsters might catch the sound and there is no telling what kind of mischief they would do. Therefore, everyone holds back, closes their lips regarding stories about Daikoku-iwa, and prays for safety and peace. Because that island exists, whenever there is even a slight gale, it stirs up tremendous high waves, and small boats always meet with the disaster of capsizing, you see. It would seem likely that somehow some great heavenly artisan will come forth, wield a great iron hammer, smash it to pieces, and appear as a great divine general to protect our safety.'

Oto: 'What an authoritative rocky mountain it is, is it not? In other words, standing proudly and defiantly upon the surface of this lake, glaring down at the scattered islands, it carries itself with a fierce and intimidating air, doesn't it?'

Kō: 'That rocky mountain sometimes causes a great rumbling roar, spews forth and scatters volcanic smoke, and wraps the lake's surface in darkness. Since that smoke contains a toxic gas, those attacked by it immediately contract baldness, or are afflicted with eye diseases, become deaf, and lose the movement of their tongues. And the emptiness of their stomachs and the thirst of their throats are said to be beyond the ordinary. Anyone who happens to encounter such a demonic wind has a terrible misfortune.'

Oto: 'It is a terrifying and disgusting rocky mountain, just like a centipede, a snake, or a scorpion. Why on earth do the gods of heaven and earth, out of their love for the people, not remove this great pest from the lake? It is of no use while present, and a huge problem while absent; if it were gone, we could navigate freely and conveniently, but it seems that even the gods cannot have things entirely their own way up to a certain point in this world.'

Kō: 'Everything is a matter of the power of the times. Since that great high rocky mountain, which was said to have thrust up from the nethermost depths of the underworld, has come to sway with the slightest shift in wind direction and drift eastward and eastward, its foundation must already be wobbly. If a single record-breaking major earthquake should break out, it would sink to the bottom of the lake without a struggle. Oh, look, look over there, please. Have not the husband-and-wife rocks at the summit begun to move in some strange way?'

Oto: 'There can be no reason for the thousand-chou rock to move automatically when the wind is not even blowing. The boat must be moving, making the rock appear to move.'

Kō: 'Nonsense, that can hardly be the case. If the boat were moving and making the rock appear to move, the entire floating island would have to move. The countless other large and small islands scattered about would also have to move in the same way. For only the summit of the rocky mountain to start moving can be neither the effect of the boat's swaying nor the effect of visual distortion or hallucinations. Surely this must be some kind of portent.'

B: 'Hearing you thus, it is indeed moving. Look, look, have not the Meutoiha rocks begun to walk from the top toward the bottom?'

A: 'Indeed, it is strange. Are they not coming down gradually? Does it not begin to look as though a tiger is crawling if you think it a rock?'

B: 'Indeed, it is a giant tiger. Look, look, the entire mountain has begun to tremble. If this fellow were to sink, the volume of water would increase to that extent, giant waves would rise, and our boat would also receive a severe impact. It has come to be a dangerous affair, alas.'

As they spoke thus, the Namikirimaru advanced close to the rock mountain of the floating island. The surroundings of the island had high waves for some reason. The transformation of the rock that had seemed to be a tiger descended to the water's edge. Looking very closely, it was a tiger cat like an ox. Glaring at the Namikirimaru with angry eyes, the tiger cat crossed the lake surface as if fleeing, paired together, floating and sinking as it fled toward the west. Suddenly the floating island began to rumble, and front, back, left, and right, the entire mountain began to shake. Bit by bit the volume of the mountain grew smaller and lower, and within about half an hour it sank its shadow into the water surface and was gone. Because the manner of the sinking was so gradual, no terrifying rough waves arose, and it ended up merely shaking the Namikirimaru back and forth and left and right. The passengers all gazed at this spectacle, their complexions all turning pale, and they merely repeated over and over how strange and mysterious it was. At this time, the missionary Baikō, who had been lying down at the bottom of the boat, awoke from the slight swaying of the boat and came up to the deck swaying unsteadily. The once-famous giant rock mountain of Taka had disappeared without a trace, leaving only water bubbles. And the passengers were talking one after another about the memorial place of the cave-in. Baikō turned to one of the passengers and said,

Baikō: 'Even though there is no wind, the waves are terrible. Has some island not sunk?'

A: 'Yes, honorable sir, did you not witness that great event? It was quite a sight. The Meutoiha rocks on the summit, which had shone like the sun and moon since ancient times, suddenly began to sway, and in the end they became a great tiger; when they descended to the water's edge they became a cat, and thinking that they were fleeing toward the west, floating and sinking among the waves, bit by bit the island began to sink, and at last it disappeared completely. I have never seen such a thing since ancient times. I wonder if this is some warning from Heaven.'

Baikō: 'How mysterious indeed. However, although it seems like a terrible event from the perspective of human beings, from the eyes of the God who created the universe and all things, it is probably like swatting and killing a single mosquito that has bitten our cheek. Nevertheless, we must look upon this, take warning ourselves, and attain realization.'

B: 'You seem to be a missionary of some religion, but what on earth will become of this world? We cannot bear the anxiety. It is such an ominous world where that rock mountain, which stood majestically towering in the middle of the lake until just an hour ago, has so fragilely sunk to the lake bottom.'

Baikō: 'Today, the spirit of evil and depravity fills the upper and lower classes of the country, beautiful customs such as benevolence and righteousness are swept from the earth, evil winds of falsehood and vice blow wildly, the world sinks deeper and deeper into the abyss of pitch darkness, sages hide in the fields, fools ascend to high places and privatize the governance of the country, goodness is oppressed and evil prospers—such is the lawless society, so heaven and earth respond to this, and various wonders presumably erupt. The human beings of today all sink into the abyss of depravity, only base desires raise their heads, talented and capable human persons are not born, and the world has ended up as an everlasting darkness of the Latter Day of the Law; therefore, we serve the divine pillars of the Saien Hall and the great divine work of salvation of the Lord God, clear away the dark clouds of the world, and in order to save the multitudes sunk in the abyss of sorrow into a peaceful and safe utopia, we endure all sorts of hardships and sufferings wherever we are, travel throughout the land, and transmit the divine teachings. The world will not settle down yet with just wonders of this sort. Within these ten years, a global and once again great war will surely erupt. Today, a war between the Ural religion and the Brahman religion is about to begin, but such a thing is equivalent to mere child's play, and a truly terrifying catastrophe lies in wait for the future of the world. Therefore, we respectfully receive the divine messages of the Great Deity, who is filled with the virtue of love and goodness and the light of true faith, and in order to save all the common people throughout the world universally, we make our bed on the grass, use the starry night sky as our quilt, and take the roots of trees as our pillows, thus dedicating our dust-like bodies for the public good of the world.'

A: 'Naruhodo, the more I listen, the more it seems that the atmosphere of Konnichi's world is drifting with fuan. People today ignore the kōdaimuhen goitoku of Shinbutsu, and bowing down and katsukau before Ōkuronushi who takes bauriyoku and atsei as his yuiitsu buki, they are under the gokai that there is nothing in the world more precious than Ōkuronushi of the capital of Haruna. Ume: 'As I stated earlier, the Ōkamisama of the Saiin Mansion opened up the Izumo religion. And I became a follower of a person called Terukuniwake-sama, a missionary of the same religion, and set out on a journey of proselytization. Therefore, to your inquiries, I can by no means give a splendid answer. However, Kami is not someone detached from Ningen, aloof like the ancients said. Only by entering the state of union of Kami and Ningen can the work of Kami being Kami and Ningen being Ningen be accomplished. Therefore, in the Izumo religion, Ningen are called the children of Kami and the palaces of Kami, and they are carrying out self-sacrificing grand activities for the sake of all people under heaven.'

A: 'Regarding your teachings, would it be possible to make me understand the relationship between Kami and Ningen in an extremely simple and clear manner?'

Ume: 'Yes, since my own training is still immature, I cannot state things definitively, but since there is one maxim I was taught from my lord, the missionary, I shall let you hear it.'

Kami's Power and Human Power

1. The origin of the universe is active power, namely Kami.

2. All things are the manifestation of active power and are fragments of Kami.

3. Ningen is the subject of active power and the ruler of the governance of Tenchi. Active power is kōdaimuhen and is the source of religion, politics, philosophy, ethics, education, science, laws, and the like.

4. Ningen is the child of Kami and the living palace of Kami. And they are also those who can become Kami.

5. Since Ningen are Kami, they should learn from Kami, act ably, believe in themselves, believe in others, and must not arouse a spirit of reliance.

6. Those who do not mind hardship for the peace and happiness of the world's humanity, and who act and execute for the sake of truth, are Kami.

7. Kami is the ubiquitous living spirit of all things, and Ningen is the subject of the divine enterprise of governance. Spirit and body being unified, they here display infinite and boundless authority, and establish the basis for all ages.'

A: 'Oh, thank you. Hearing your instruction, I choked with a rapturous joy as if having transferred from hell to the Pure Land of Gokuraku. Naruhodo, Ningen are branch streams of Kami, so to speak, small Kamis. Compared to the doctrines that have been praised in the Ural religion up to now, in their content, in their preciousness, and in the point of the thoroughness of truth, there is a difference as vast as the clouds and mud. I am a small merchant at the port of Suga, but at my home I enshrine the Kami of Uralhiko. However, since this has been worshipped traditionally since my ancestors, to put it mildly, I am merely becoming a Ural believer for the convenience of funerals and the like. Established religions have already lost their life force, merely retaining their wrecks. We people were starving for shinkau, wandering in the path of the spirit, and could not spend this world in peace for a single day. The old morals have already faded in the world, new morals do not arise, and great new religions do not spring up, so I was lamenting day and night, but I never even dreamed in my dreams that such a sublime and great true religion had arisen. To unexpectedly encounter such an emissary of a precious Kami amid the ship of Nagakirimaru, and to be made to hear the life-restoring divine teachings—how happy I must be. My home is truly cramped, but it is a small merchant shop known to a greater or lesser degree near and far, called Iruku at the port of Suga. Please bend your lotus steps to my home as well, and I ask that you bestow the precious teachings upon my family altogether. And I do not intend to monopolize this splendid divine virtue, but thinking to propagate divine virtue to all people to the utmost of my ability, I humbly and kindly request your favor.'

Umekō: 'Truly splendid is your state of mind; this too must be the divine arrangement of the Great Lord of Great Compassion and Great Mercy. Taking this as a bond, when my ship arrives at the port of Suga, I shall drop by your home.

Proceeding while dividing the rough waves of the world.'
