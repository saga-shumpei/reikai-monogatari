Chapter 19: 絵姿 [1721]

絵姿〔1721〕

§ 1

Having barely escaped from the prison of eighteen years of mercy, together with his favorite retainer Arina, and attempting a clear-eyed excursion through the mountains and fields to his heart's content, the Prince of Tarahan Castle forgot his return route in the midst of such joy and delight, completely forgetting everything, abandoning himself to the steed of his heart, and wandering deep into the recesses of the unknown deep mountains; while Samori's son Arina, who had in turn catered to the Prince's wishes to guide him through the mountains and fields, only to lose his direction, seek the return path, and while being bathed in moonlight upon a mountain side overlooking valley rivers where range upon range of mountains were piled one upon another, had his very soul and gall stolen away by the roar of a lion, suddenly seized by a sense of terror, his complexion turning pale, without even the sensation of being alive, and having an earthquake within his body erupt—having finally recognized the faint gleam of a single pillar of fire at the foot of the mountain, just like someone standing at death's line and encountering a savior god, suddenly grew a hundredfold in courage, leading the Prince, parting the dwarf bamboo and brushwood, and at last arriving at a single hidden dwelling; and granted a night's lodging in a thatched, dugout-style dwelling that was mere form by the master's kindness, after various tales, realizing that [the master] was a senior retainer who had served King Karapin ten years prior, while alternately rejoicing and being astonished, his heart finally settled down, and laying down his body exhausted to the consistency of cotton, here the youth fair as dripping dew fell into a lodging of dews, knowing neither before nor behind. Ah, what manner of dream paths could this master-servant pair of youths have traversed that night? Struck by bizarre destinies, tossed about by the winds of joy, anger, sorrow, and pleasure, immortals suddenly descend to the earth, while earthworms lurking beneath the soil sprout wings and sing of spring upon the branches of tall trees. The seven transformations of human life—reciter, scribe, and reader alike, with keen interest, desire to hear the unfolding of the dream of the master-servant pair.

Neither the phoenix beating its wings high above the clouds, nor the red-crowned crane floating in the misty sea of heaven, nor the tiny insects lurking beneath the soil, make any distinction whatsoever when it comes to love; to sink and float seven or eight times in the abyss of affection is the way of the world. The Prince within Tarahan Castle, who is difficult to compare to either flowers or the moon, and Samori's son Arina—whose height is slightly short and whose complexion is tinged with a faint redness, yet whose facial features are so strikingly similar that one might mistake him for another—are engaged in a rare romance wrought with all their dead strength. While reclining as usual in Shōunkaku under the frosty moon's sky of the Rat year of Taishō, speaking skillfully, writing skillfully, and gazing upon the dawn sky where mountain colors connect with the clouds, while equipping the Kototama carriage with a fountain-pen machine gun, the four souls of Deguchi, Matsumura, Kitamura, and Katō gather together to part the misty ocean of Tamba's famed scenery.

Shakanna, a rare guest, making only the moon and sun of the sky his friends, and the sighing of the pines as the tidings of the world, together with his most beloved daughter, casting away all plans, abandoning his aspirations of long standing, and resolved to finish his days deep in the recesses of the mountains with his daughter as his strength, was visited in his dream hermitage by the beloved child of his lord of three generations of shared benevolence together with Samori's son who had treated his own person harshly; rejoicing yet astonished, the animosity he had felt to retaliate for the cold treatment which Samori Gandī of ten years ago had shown toward his own person upon meeting the Prince was smoothly forgotten for the sake of loyalty; unable to sleep even a wink of the night's eye on account of this unexpected rare guest, from the hour demanding a bonfire while it was still early morning, together with Princess Subaru, he set about industriously preparing the morning meal, determined to repay even a ten-thousandth part of the old benevolence with scanty materials to the limit of his strength, not even ordering his servant Colton, but cooking and presenting it himself, exhausting the utmost of his heart, he exerted his entire strength in the preparation of the breakfast. Colton, limited only to this morning, was excused from the task of cooking, and with a broom made of tree branches swept and purified the surroundings of the thatched hut and the edge of the garden, sweeping it thoroughly from corner to corner like the day before a local deity's festival or a rustic New Year's Eve garden, with neat sweep-marks, just like a pair of torn straw sandals.

Korton: 'Ah, what a strange thing has come to pass. A month ago, those damned octopus-like priests called Genshinbau and Tenshinbau came barging into the cave on Mount Taniguku, along with a woman resembling a goddess, gorging on sake. In that drunken gap, they played hopeless, mischievous tricks on me—turning me into a dog, a monkey, a frog, a cat-carrying nursemaid, and whatnot—while that beautiful Nice no Shian had spoken gently to me and cast subtle amorous glances my way, stripping me bare. The expressions of the three of us at that moment still remain vividly etched before my eyes. Like gazing at that pink crepe through white, thin silk, the complexion of that beauty, and from the hands of the beauty whose delicate, fine-grained skin seemed forged from white jade—though not deer-hair scrolls—she made me grind ink with a handmade brush crafted from palm-tree bristles, leaving the commemorative letters of "monkey" and "frog" upon my forehead before departing. I prayed endlessly for this commemorative mark to stay upon my forehead forever, wishing it would bite even deeper into my flesh, and as for the trace of the beauty's brush of affection, whether they called it a monkey or a frog, I cared nothing for such things. Yet, in vain did I pray that it would never fade away; before I knew it, legs sprouted out smoothly, and I was subjected to the unfeeling plight of being called a monkey and a frog. Though I, a man of worldly passions, created a slight sin, as the days piled up, the dog of earthly desires fled somewhere, and I came to return to my true mind. And then, once again, on the same fifteenth night, when two white-faced youths who looked as though they might even be heavenly maidens descended to this doorway, my astonishment was something I had never experienced since birth. From my utter shock, wondering if they might perhaps be the grandchildren of tengu, I contrived various words and tried my utmost to refuse and send them back. What do you think that turned out to be? Upon hearing that they were the former master of the boss-in-chief, the crown prince of Mount Tarahan, and the son of the governor, I was shocked a second and third time. My internal organs turned completely upside down, and out of a sense of nameless terror and awe, it felt too sacrilegious to rest upon my bedding last night. Crawling on all fours upon the earthen floor, I resigned myself to the fate of a dog and a frog, barely managing to see out the night. I had been worrying whether I would receive a scolding from the boss-in-chief, but as they say that peaches are cheaper than apricots in this world, fortunately, even the blazing glare of the boss's eyeballs and his Kototama, sharp as autumn frost and summer heat, somehow or other seemed to have been pardoned. Nevertheless, our young lady, though still a child, is already welcoming the spring of her fifteenth year. Moreover, according to evening's talk, the boss appears to have been the governor under King Karapin until ten years ago. True to her birth in a prestigious lineage, the more one looks at her, the higher her nobility, and she is an unsurpassed beauty. Might some strange development transpire between the prince and the young lady? It somehow strikes me as suspicious. Though not petty jealousy, a somewhat angry and comical feeling has begun to well up within me. Up until now, I too had won the boss's favor and, waiting for the young lady to come of age, intended to somehow become his adopted son, but now that things have reached this day, it has somehow become dubious. My routine displays of loyal service and my kindness toward the young lady are about to vanish like thin clouds over Mine Peak. Does a hundred days of preaching not produce even a single fart's worth of effect? Ah, this is the height of regret. The rainy mornings and windy evenings, waiting for her to come of age, living out days hoping to pluck the famous flower of Mount Taniguku—have those too utterly become a dream? Ah, what regret and vexation! No matter how clever I may be, on one side is the king's crown prince, who is moreover my former master, and on top of that, a youth glowing like a jade; hence, he is by no means an opponent I can match. Speaking from status and fame, it is already hopeless. Ah, how embarrassing! What is the need to suffer in a place like this and continue a wretched life? Even the broom I hold in my hand naturally feels as though my hand is growing weary and letting go. Ah, even the voices of small birds sound this morning as though they are making a fool of me. The tree leaves rustling under the gentle breeze look this morning as though they are mocking my unrequited love. Even the sound of the murmuring valley stream, which until yesterday sounded as joyously as the music of celestial maidens, sounded this morning somehow like the mournful tones of a ruined nation. Compared to my everyday life filled with hope, today's me, having plunged into the abyss of disappointment and dejection, feels as though heaven and earth, the great boss-in-chief, and the pitiful young lady have all abandoned me. Ah, how absurd. While there is still time, I shall hide my figure in the deep forest and resolve to live as a vagabond in some empty sky. Oh, yes, indeed, how utterly disgraceful!'

Muttering thus, he turned his full-breathed discontent toward the broom, and crying out, 'Ah, you bastard!', he hurled it with all his might toward the valley stream, hauled up his black backside, struck himself two or three times, bent his body into the shape of a hiragana 'ku', thrust his chin forward, bared teeth like a pond snail, hiccuped 'hn, hn, hn' two or three times, and swiftly concealed his form from this place.

As the sun shone high overhead, Taishi and Alina, lord and servant, finally opened their eyes.

Taishi: 'Ah, old man, thanks to you I was able to sleep comfortably last night. It seems my vitality has recovered with this, and I feel like a human being again. Won't you give me a cup of hot water?'

Sha: 'Wakagimisama, are you already awake? Please rest at your leisure.'

Tai: 'Nah, this is already enough.'

Alina: 'Last night, thanks to you, I shared in Wakagimisama's hospitality and was able to sleep comfortably. I will never forget this kindness anywhere.'

Sha: 'Hey, Koruton, go draw some hot water for the guests. What is Koruton doing?'

No matter how many times he called, Koruton gave no response. Nor did his gourd head show itself. Just then, Princess Subaru changed into somewhat neat clothing, untangled her disheveled hair, and with a smile on her flower-like, lovely face,

Subaru: 'Wakagimisama, good morning. Honorable retainer, did you sleep last night? Since this is a thatched hut as you know, I was worrying that both of you might find it hard to sleep. Come, please partake of some bitter tea.'

So saying, with trembling hands and somewhat turning her face away, she reverently poured tea and offered it to Taishi. Taishi thought, ...what a charming person, what a lovely thing..., while quietly reaching out his hand, receiving the tea extended by Subaru, blowing on it two or three times with a fū-fū sound, and quietly drinking it down.

Suba: 'Wakagimisama, how about one more?'

Taishi: 'No, don't bother, I'll take it as I please.'

Sha: 'Kora kora, Koruton, where have you gone? Will you not quickly greet Wakagimisama?'

Suba: 'Father, Koruton leaped out a little while ago. There is no longer any worry of him returning.'

Sha: 'What, do you say Koruton has run away? Why did you not stop him at that time?'

Suba: 'Father, I thought a good centipede had run away, so I didn't stop him. He always says strange things and looks at me with disgusting eyes. Somehow, every time that happens, I feel as if I am being attacked by a demon, and my chest was always trembling. With this, it is just parent and child alone together, and there is nothing as comfortable as this. Father, even if Koruton is not here, I will do all the cooking work, so please rest assured.'
