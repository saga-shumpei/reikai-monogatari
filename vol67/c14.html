Chapter 14: 獣念気 [1716]

獣念気〔1716〕

§ 1

At a point ten ri due north away from the city of Tarahan, a lofty mountain named Mount Taniguku towers into the sky. The three directions of south, north, and west are encompassed by steep and precipitous mountains, and with only a narrow entrance on the eastern side, the murmuring valley water flows out from this east mouth. At the foot of Mount Taniguku, a natural grand cavern has been pierced through. Dissuading King Karapin, causing Azuma to be slain, and escaping from the narrow-escape peril where he himself was also about to become the rust of a blade, bearing upon his back the then six-year-old daughter Subaruhime who had even been tacitly arranged as the consort of Prince Sudaruman, leaving the capital behind and lurking in this cavern, gathering outlaws from far and near, making himself the ringleader of mountain bandits, driving away the traitorous fellows of Gandī who had served as the Minister of the Right and his subordinates Sakuretsu, and working to his utmost day and night to purify the side of the sovereign, he was Shakanna, the Minister of the Left who had served King Karapin. Praised since ancient times as the lair of lions, not a single person had ever set foot into this mountain depths. Along with the years, Shakanna saw his numerous subordinates multiply. Then, sending those subordinates secretly by night into the castle town of Tarahan and various other places, targeting the houses of the wealthy to plunder wealth and goods, he was making preparations in order to subjugate Gandī. Subaru, the daughter whom he had brought along when she was six years old, had finally welcomed the spring of her fifteenth year this year. Sitting with legs crossed broadly in the inner chamber of the cavern and leaning against an armrest, Shakanna was listening to the reports of his numerous foster-children.

Shakanna: 'Hey, Barugi, of late your squad is surely lacking in work, isn't it? If you do not pull yourselves together a bit, even the military supplies that we have taken such pains to store up will end up gone, and I wonder just about when you will ever reach your objective—almost no clue can be seen at all, can it?'

Barugi: 'Ha, as you instruct, of late the Brahman army is running riot and swaggering about, so satisfactory work cannot be done, and houses that look even slightly as if they might have goods are all messed up by the Brahman army, and taking barely twenty or thirty subordinates along, it will not do to make that great army our opponents and fight either, so while regretting it I am considering the timing until the troops make their retreat. To a slight extent, it seems of late we are running short of provisions, but if you wait a little while, I will surely show you a grand activity. I am also doing my utmost to spur on my subordinates, but no matter what one says, it is work by night alone, and it does not make headway as desired. Since we must steal along this ten-ri mountain road in disguise, set out for the city of Bargan, and come back again during the night, the vital time for working is a mere half hour or quarter of an hour, so the foster-children are also in great distress.'

Shakanna: 'Ah, it cannot be helped, then. Well, anyhow, tell them to work to their utmost. And how is the state of Gandī's mansion? Has it become clearly known?'

Barugi: 'Ha, of late they say a Brahman army is making a raid, so they have made the walls high, and seventy or eighty sleepless guards are patrolling and protecting the front and back gates, so we cannot approach.'

Shakanna: 'Is that so? That also cannot be helped. Shall I prolong the plan for a little while longer?'

Barugi: 'If you can be so pleased as to wish that, it will be splendid.'

Today is the anniversary of my wife's throwing away her life at the hands of the Great King within the castle, so I told Koruton to seek out a good shugenja somewhere and have him perform memorial services, and I dispatched him two or three days ago, but if he does not return today, it will by no means be in time. It is a troubled matter. However, since Koruton is a fellow steadfast in righteousness, he will surely search out a shugenja somewhere. Regarding that, make preparations for a welcoming reception. And today, it will be well to let the two hundred foster-children eat their fill of a feast and treat them to sake to their hearts' content.'

Baru: 'Yes, this morning I spurred on the subordinates, and the preparations for the feast and the arrangements for the festival are neatly completed. Please rest assured on that point alone. However, if Colton does not return, it stands to reason that the painstaking feast will come to naught. If he does not return, what shall we do?'

Sha: 'Such worry is unnecessary. If he does not return, I have the mind to helplessly chant the sutras before the spirit altar myself and finish the tenth-year memorial service. Rather than the chanting of a maisu priest, my own direct chanting might perhaps be all the better for the deceased. During the daylight hours he too will likely hesitate to return. In any case, it will be a matter of the night.'

Baru: 'Let us decide to wait until the fourth hour of this evening. If he does not return by then, please give up the thought already, and you yourself, Ontai, perform the duties of a biku. Though lacking in ability, this Barugī too has the experience of having served as a novice in the Ural religion when I was a child, so I remember bits and pieces like knowing the sutras by heart...'

Sha: 'Today my body somehow feels as if it is tired. Until Colton returns, I will rest, so you pay close attention to the fellows who are subordinates and make sure not to neglect supervision.'

So saying, pointing to the cavern which was his bedroom, he concealed his form.

Around the time dusk was passing, Colton returned in high spirits, accompanied by a single ascetic of majestic air and a seventeen or eighteen-year-old beauty who looked like a heavenly maiden—whether she was his wife or daughter he knew not—along with two or three subordinates, with his shoulders puffed up and with the determination of a triumphant general, walking leisurely. As soon as Barugī saw Colton's figure, he came out in a fluster to greet him,

Baru: 'Oh, brother, well done, well done. What a splendid biku you have brought along. The Oyabun-sama has been waiting for you very much. All preparations are neatly completed. The late Okusan-sama will surely rejoice as well. Such things are truly left to you alone.'

Colton: 'Oh, if you praise me so, I won't be able to speak. However, running about hither and thither and searching everywhere for famous monks and men of wisdom, lately due to the invasion of the Brahman army, both bikus and ascetics had hidden their shadows somewhere, and they were not easily to be found. And if I relied on a priest who owned a temple, there was the fear that this hiding place would be exposed to the world, so thinking I wanted a wind-blown traveler with strong divine power and high virtue, I spent three days searching as of today, and finally this morning, I happened to meet such a splendid ascetic—nay, an incarnation of Tentei—and when I explained the circumstances, he readily consented, so I brought him along.'

Barugī: 'Ah, that was very convenient. Now, go to the inner rooms and rest. ...This, this is an incarnation of Tentei, I have the honor of meeting you for the first time. I am one who serves as the head clerk of this establishment, and my name is Barugī. Since it is by all means in such mountain depths, we cannot offer full hospitality and it is nothing but inconveniences, but I ask you to kindly overlook and reconsider that with a magnanimous heart, and we desire the memorial rites for our late Okusan-sama. Excuse me, but what might your honorable name be? Since there is also the circumstance of reporting to my master, I would like to hear it.'

The ascetic, loftily throwing back his shoulders,

Ascetic: 'Ho, you must be Barugī-dono, the head clerk of this house. I have heard well from Colton-dono that you are a man of talent. However, this house is surely no ordinary home. Swept away by Colton-dono's persuasive tongue and led away into these mountain depths, when I look at the surroundings, it looks somehow like the dwelling of mountain bandits. You must be a minor boss serving the chief leader.'

Baru: 'Yes, I am humbled. Indeed I am completely won over by your sagacity. Now that it has come to this, it is useless to conceal it. We are serving as minor bosses of mountain bandits. Our great head boss is named Shakanna, and he is a great hero. Excuse me, but I would like to ask your honorable name once again...'

Shu: 'Ahahahaha, what will you make of it upon hearing my name? If I were a human, I would have a name and a meuji. As for this setsusō, taking Ten as my father and Ji as my mother, the seiki of Tentei condensed, manifesting a jintai here, and performing shiyujiyōsaido—if I must state my name outright, I shall name myself an incarnation of Tentei, ahahahahaha.'

Baru: 'Indeed, whether viewed vertically or horizontally, you are possessed of dignity, a sacred goshinkaku, and an incarnation of Tentei. Ah, what a kaufuku person the okusama must be. And is the gofujin lingering there the okusama, or perhaps a musumego?'

Shu: 'Ahahahaha, I have neither wife nor daughter; this onkata is Tanabatahime-sama, who has descended this time from Tengoku Shibikiu for the sake of banminsaido.'

Baru: 'Indeed, hearing it told thus, she does not look like a gojin who cut her umbilical cord upon the earth. With an unspeakably noble gokihin and an okirei figure, upon glancing at her once my eyes seem to grow dizzy. Now then, please proceed to the inner chambers, since my shiyujin is waiting impatiently.'

Shu: 'If so, lead the way. Meeting the shiyujin, I shall fully impart unto him the principles of Ten and Ji.'

Baruyuki muttered inwardly... It would be a terrible disaster to have the principles of Ten and Ji preached to the head of a band of thieves. An incarnation of the deity whose principle is Aizen (Love and Goodness) could hardly hit it off with a great boss who threatens people and extorts money and goods. Why on earth did Colton lack sense, and why would he drag along an incarnation of such a deity... Thus muttering within his mouth, he guided him into the splendid rocky cavern where Shakanna nested.

Baru: 'Lord Husband, Colton has just now returned, bringing along a shugenja—no, more, more, more—a noble, noble, and great personage-sama. This appearance is not that of a ningen. It is said to be an incarnation of Tentei, and the other personage is Tanabatahime-sama. Please be careful so that there may be no impropriety and no discourtesy.'

Sha: 'What, did Colton bring along an incarnation of a deity? Ah, that is most auspicious. First, let us try meeting this so-called incarnation of a deity.'

Baru: 'I have just now accompanied them here. Please wake up. It is discourteous.'

Shakanna sprang up with a start, straightened his posture, and while gazing intently at the face of the man who called himself the incarnation of Tentei, broke into a grinning laugh,

Sha: 'Iyō, how well you have disguised yourself. If a monk wears such a splendid robe and puts on a haughty air, even the eyes of an amateur would suppose him to be an incarnation of Tentei. Yet to my eyes you appear as nothing more than a yamago-bōzu, ahahahahaha. Hey, tanuki-bōzu, where on earth are you from?'

Shu: 'This is outrageous. To address this setsusō, who am the savior sent from Heaven and the incarnation of Tentei, as a tanuki-bōzu—is this not excessive insolence? I have not traveled all the way to such a deep mountain to receive such greetings. If it does not please you, this setsusō shall simply return as I am.'

Sha: 'Ahahahaha, hey bōzu, do not get so angry. What is the meaning of this—that one who harbors a great ambition to wrap a body of excrement and urine in brocade, to disguise the heart of a yaksha in the provisional robes of a bodhisattva, and to perplex all the people under heaven, should be frightened by a single remark of mine and make haste to prepare his flight? Hey bōzu, you put on a bold and mighty front, puffing yourself up, but deep within your heart you must be trembling with fear. Thinking you had found a sweet bird, you were neatly taken in by that scoundrel Colton, and upon coming here, you found an unexpected hard-boned old man—you must be quite astonished, surely.'

Shu: 'More and more outrageous words! I dwell in the virtue of Aizen, am filled with the light of Shinjin (True Faith), and am undeniably the incarnation of Tentei, across whom the brilliance of Chie Shōgaku (Wisdom and Enlightenment) shines. Growing old, your faculty of sight has grown dim, and you cannot recognize the goshinkaku, countenance, and majesty of this setsusō. Go wash your hands and face for a little while. To attempt to view a divine person with sleep-blurred eyes—is that not entirely unfitting for your station?'
