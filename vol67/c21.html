Chapter 21: 針灸思想 [1723]

針灸思想〔1723〕

§ 1

Samori's son Arina had been ordered to one month of confinement as a result of the deliberation, and was locked up in his father's mansion. Gandī, the Samoritsukasa, though receiving no particular reprimand from the King, bore the responsibility of having disturbed the Denchiu and engaged in a ninjiyō with Umori, and had closed his gates of his own accord to observe confinement.

Gandī: 'Hey son, what an outrageous thing you have done! Because you always egged on the Prince, breathing into him such new-fangled ideas as kiyōsanshiyugi, jinruiaizen, and haikara-style thoughts, he became possessed by such a mindset, came to dislike inheriting the Bandai and Fueki Ōtō, rushed out of the Dennai, caused anxiety to everyone from Daiō Denka at the top down to this father and the raushin, and threw the jiyōge into commotion—the sekinin for that is not at all small. It is well if you reform your mind from now on; if you remain with your previous riyōken, you will not be permitted to serve as the Prince's sobazuki. Nor can you be kept in our house. Try to understand a parent's feelings even a little. Have you not troubled the Emperor's august mind, caused worry to the raushin, and disturbed the Dennai?'

Arina: 'Yes, indeed, as you say, Father, it is a fact that I have caused worry to the Daiō, startled the raushin, and disturbed the Dennai. However, even if I disturbed the same Dennai, I did not commit such violence as a ninjiyō like you. Father, if you are going to give me your opinion, I ask you first to wipe your own bottom, brush away the bee that has settled on your own face, and earnestly give me your teachings. Like father, like son; that father and son alike have agreed in troubling the august mind of Daiō Denka and disturbing the Dennai must be some kind of innen.'

Gandī: 'What! Do not talk back and utter sophistries without knowing the reasons. Even if you and I both disturbed the Dennai, the reasons are different. There is a difference as vast as heaven and earth, black and white, or a moon and a turtle. That fellow Umori's lackey Sakurensu plotted such outrageous, traitorous schemes as abolishing the imperial house's autocratic government and establishing a republican government, thereby troubling the Emperor's august mind; therefore, making my life my stake, I slashed at that Umori to punish the treacherous villain. It cannot be compared to your wicked and lawless acts of instilling various haikara ideas into the precious Prince, confounding the Prince's mind, and finally bringing about a grave crisis for the state. You would do well to firmly steady your wits and listen to your father's words. The Daiō, holding a body of kinshi gyokuyō, undergoes august hardships that are not meant for your sake alone. How could I, Gandī, the father of such a son, brazenly continue to live? If you do not somehow repent and turn the Prince's heart, I have reached the pass where this father must commit suicide to make amends. You are the very epitome of disloyal and unrighteous wickedness. How on earth could such an evil wretch have been born from my seed?'

ガン『エヽ、ツベコベと訳も知らずに屁理窟を云ふな。お前と俺とは同じ殿内を騒がしたにしても訳が違ふのだ。天地霄壤、黒白、月鼈の差違が有るのだ。彼れ右守のサクレンス奴、王家の専制政治を廃し、共和政治を立てようなどと、大それた国賊的機略を弄し、殿下の宸襟を悩ませ奉つたによつて、俺は命を的に奸賊を誅伐せむと彼右守に斬りつけたのだ。貴様のやうに、大切な太子に種々のハイカラ的思想を注入し、太子の精神を惑乱し、遂には国家の一大事を惹起せむとするやうな悪逆無道の行為とは比べものにならぬのだ。確りと性念を据ゑて父の言葉を聞いたらよからうぞ。大王様は金枝玉葉の御身をもつて、汝一人の為に有るにあられぬ御苦心遊ばして厶るのだ。その悴の父たるこのガンヂーが、どうしてノメノメと生て居られやうか。お前がどうしても悔い改めて、太子の御心を翻さぬに於ては、もはや此父は自害して申し訳を立てねばならぬ羽目となつて居るのだ。不忠不義の極悪人とは貴様の事だ。どうしてまアこんな極悪人が俺の胤から生れたものだらうなア』

§ 2

Ari: 'Ahahahahaha. Father, please calmly reflect upon your own actions up to now. Just like that, you cannot grandly offer opinions to me. Up until I was in my early childhood, Father, you served as Umori no Kami. At that time, a Samori no Kami named Shakanna, possessed of loyalty peerless, managed the affairs of state, did he not? The late Ōhisama was bewitched by Akuma, day and night her cruelty grew, at last she oppressed the muko min, sliced open the bellies of pitiful ninpu to pluck out their taiji, boiled them whole, placed them upon her shiyokuzen, and clicked her tongue in relish, did she not? Despite all this, knowing not how to resolve to die and offer chiyokukan, instead you fawned and flattered the ōhi, causing her zanninsei to grow ever more augmented, did you not? Did not the white-feathered arrow of the kokumin's ensa fly in from one direction of the heavens while the ōhi was out hunting, pierce through her forehead to cause her to expire on the spot, and upon hearing this did not the kokumin instead rejoice and secretly hold a celebratory gathering? After instigating that ōhi who had become the target of the kokumin's ensa, you poured all manner of evil wisdom into the Great King himself... and fired the white-feathered arrow of heavenly punishment through subordinates of the Samori... placing groundless guilt upon them, you used the hand of the Great King to strike down and slay the Samori's wife, Harisahime, and as though even that were not enough, attempting to take the life of the Samori though you failed, in the end you replaced him yourself and serenely assumed the post of Samori, did you not? Even aside from that, you confiscated the entire colossal fortune of the Samori's household, and in order for yourself to bind the kokumin's trust—without losing a hair of your head or feeling a twinge of pain in your belly—you bestowed that fortune upon the kokumin, donning the mask of virtue while executing evil deeds, making you a kyokuaku akunin of the highest order, did you not? Because of you, Father, did not Shakanna set out on a journey of wandering the realm together with his pitiful daughter, and his very whereabouts remain unknown to this day? Before you, everyone without exception exhausts every manner of ayu hensēi tsuishō, acting merely as officials seeking to flick the dust from your mustache, but they are moshi fukuhai, and behind your backs, whenever they turn away, they stick out their tongues, curse your monstrous wickedness to the utmost limits of language, and moreover detest you. That the Talahan Kingdom has fallen into such disorder as it is today is entirely your responsibility, Father. Through laws unsuited to the times that are convenient for oppression, coercion, and despotism, and by the power of military forces, police, and prisons, have you not until now suppressed the heads of the kokumin, oppressed their thought, and behaved with all the selfishness you could muster, did you not? Did not Father's vices rebound upon his children, finally extending their retribution to the royal house, resulting in the tragic state of affairs today? It is simply that I would wish Father to listen to my opinion, turn about in sudden repentance, and return to the red heart of loyalty and the practices of ai-zen. For me to hear opinions issue from Father's mouth is just like a demon of hell striking a surigane while reciting nembutsu, and it is so comical I cannot bear it. Nay, rather, it is the height of belly-aching hilarity, ahahahahaha.'

Gan: 'Now you impudent brat, what kind of loose-mouthed things are you uttering? For you, if only as a child, to discuss a father's conduct and rattle off absurd opinions is nothing if not a complete inversion of heaven and earth. How do you understand the maxim that "one should consider parents and masters to utter unreasonable things"? No matter what anyone says, am I not your father? Regardless of good and evil, right and wrong, anyone who rebels against a parent is an unfilial wretch throughout the realm. You too are already eighteen; know at least a little bit of what filial piety is. No, you must understand the path of loyalty!'

Ari: 'Father, do you oppress me under the name of being a parent? If one becomes a child, do you think that no matter what unreasonable demands you blow my way, reason will somehow hold firm? Such ancient moralism is already three hundred years in the past. If affairs of state are handled with this style, one can only sympathize with the troubles of the many officials and the kokumin. I desire to become what Father calls an unfilial person, a disloyal person... Did not the sages of old say... Great filial piety resembles lack of filial piety. Great loyalty resembles great treason... and so forth? There is also the proverb that great righteousness destroys one's parent. For the sake of the great righteousness of clear duty, I cast away my parent. From the moment you refuse to change that spirit of yours for all time, you are neither my parent nor am I your child. I shall now disown you from my side!'

ガン『これや悴、何と云ふ口巾の広い事を申すか。苟にも子として父の行為を云々し、くだらぬ意見口を叩くと云ふ事は、天地転倒も甚だしいではないか。「親と主人は無理を云ふものと思へ」との格言を何と心得て居るか。何と云ふても親父ぢやないか。善悪正邪に拘らず、親に反抗する奴は天下の不孝者だ。貴様も最早十八、些とは孝行と云ふ事を知れ。否忠義の道を弁へねばなるまいぞ』

アリ『お父さま、貴方は親と云ふ名の下に私を圧迫するのですか。吾子になればどんな無理難題を吹きかけても、それで道理が立つと思ひますか。そんな古い道徳主義は三百年も過去の事ですよ。こんな流儀で国政に当られては、数多の役人や国民共の迷惑が思ひやられます。私はお父さまの所謂、不孝者、不忠者になり度う厶います。……大孝は不孝に似たり。大忠は大逆に似たり。……と古の聖人も云つたぢや厶いませぬか。大義親を滅するとか云ふ諺も厶います。私は大義明分の為には親を捨てます。何時迄も其精神をお変へ下さらぬ以上は、親でも無ければ子でもありませぬ。私の方から貴方に向つて勘当を致しますよ』

§ 3

Gan: 'Listen, you rascal, if I let you keep talking, you grow more and more arrogant and utter such outrageous words, not even looking upon your parent as a parent; I shall deal you summary justice!'

Ali: 'Father, please be reasonable and stop raving. What are you panicking about? Alina's body is no longer at your disposal. My body is the staff and pillar of Taishi-sama, and I would have you rely upon it; it is an indispensable national treasure of Tarahan Castle. If you intend to execute summary justice upon me, please do so only after obtaining the permission of Daiō Denka and Taishi Denka. Surely even Samori cannot dispose of Taishi-sama's favorite retainer at will. That precisely would make you a traitor guilty of disloyalty and filial impiety.'

Gan: 'What outrageous words to speak of disloyalty and filial impiety! You yourself are the wicked and lawless villain who attempts to overthrow the unchanging imperial house of ages eternal. You are a traitor guilty of disloyalty and filial impiety. You are a traitor who has instilled communism and plebeianism into Taishi-sama day and night; be silent, you traitor!'

Ali: 'Father, it is my freedom and right to exercise the organ of speech granted to me by Tentei. Do not even the laws you yourself made, imperfect to the extreme as they are, recognize the freedom of speech and assembly? If you utter such uncomprehending things, you will be called a doting old man, and you shall have no excuse to offer. Contradictory, chaotic, and self-contradictory—one must say this has reached its absolute peak. You are attempting to altogether ignore the dignity of the individual, but as citizens and as individuals, must we not fully develop our individuality, fully manifest our workings as the divided spirits of heaven and earth, and fully exercise the right of freedom? And yet, for you to attempt to obstruct this through suppression and intimidation—you must be called a senile lunatic blind to the times.'

Gan: 'Because you are young in years and have never participated in the pivot of politics, you concoct such petty sophistry. Yet, theory and practice are vastly different. Look at the politicians of today: when they are out of power, they raise all manner of opposition against the measures of the current government, incite the populace, and finally seize the government. And then, when they come to administer state affairs, their tone suddenly changes; the doctrines and principles they roared when out of power are coolly discarded—nay, abandoned as they are bound to be. That is why in this world, there is a vast divergence between argument and practice. Without knowing the circumstances behind it, for you as a mere greenhorn to make a fuss and chirp like a frog in a paddy field will not settle anything. Above all, the secret of politics lies in suppression.'

Gan: 'You do not understand at all, Father. Excuses can be made in any way whatsoever. What of Slavic lands, which were governed using tyranny and suppression as their sole weapons? What of China? Have they not already perished? When you look at the trend where countries across the world vie to make republicanism the principle of statecraft, and monarchies fall one after another, is not the tidal current of the times advancing toward republicanism with rapid force? If you ignore individuals one by one, how can you ever govern a state? The wise Taishi-sama has already perceived this point early on, and he desires to leave the throne, become a commoner, and live a life befitting a human being as an individual. Come, Father, you too should beg for your skeleton in good measure. The longer you manage state affairs for even a day, the more the state heads toward ruin. Many of the citizens are saying... "If the obstinate and foolish Samori leaves this world even a day sooner, it is a day's gain for the state."'

Gan: 'You speak constantly of individuals and individuals, wildly spouting individualism; but the more individualism develops, the more autocratic politics becomes necessary, does it not? Once complete individualism has developed and the power to sustain life has been created, it is merely like a tiny grain of sand. It is as though twenty thousand citizens became twenty thousand grains of sand. Grains of sand separated one by one can never have cohesive power, no matter how firm they are. It may be fine for individuals, but as a state and organization, it is truly pitiful. Therefore, a large leather bag called the Karapin imperial house is necessary. Only by putting twenty thousand grains of sand into this leather bag, binding the mouth of the bag tightly, and striking it hard with a mallet can a single state organization be solidified. A bag with a torn leather pouch is the so-called disorganized and powerless thing. You are the arch-criminal who attempts to tear it. The wise Denka could not possibly fail to understand such a principle, but because you constantly instill evil thoughts, he has come to have such an evil spirit. So to speak, you are the origin of the demons who would overthrow the country of Tarahan. Ah, there is no help for it now. Unable even to die as one might wish, how painful a position it must be when my child is so obstinate and spiritually dead, failing to understand the times and knowing nothing of politics!'

Ali: 'Father, I sympathize with your present circumstances of anguish and suffering; but after all, it depends entirely upon how one holds one's heart. Take a little walk in the suburbs and behold the art of heaven and earth; if you do so, your chest will open up a little, and new thoughts will surely arise.'

Samori, breathing heavily and at his wit's end, spoke:

Samori: 'Ah, ah, whether by this means or that, turning into the smoke of incense, I wonder if the state of Tarahan is destined to perish!'

ガン『お前は年が若いから政治の枢機に参加した事が無いから、左様な小理窟をこねるのだ。併し乍ら理論と実際とは大に違ふものだ。今頃の政治家を見よ、野にある時は時の政府の施設に対し、どうのかうのと極力反対を試み、民衆を煽て上げ遂に政府を乗つ取り、扨て国政を執つて見ると俄然と調子が変つて来て、野にあつて咆哮した主義主張もケロリと捨て、否放擲せなくてはならぬやうになるものだ。それだから世の中は議論と実際とは大に径庭のあるものだ。其間の消息も知らずに、青二才の分際として小田の蛙の鳴くやうにゴタゴタ云ふても納まらないぞ。総て政治の秘訣は圧迫に限るのだ』

アリ『どこ迄もお父さまは解らないのですな。理窟はどんなにでもつくものですよ。専制と圧迫を唯一の武器として治めて居たスラブはどうです。チヤイナはどうですか。既に已に滅亡したではありませぬか。世界各国競ふて共和主義をもつて治国の主義となし、次から次へと王政が亡びてゆく趨勢を見ても、時代の潮流は共和主義に向つて、急速力を以つて進んで居るぢやありませぬか、個人、個人を無視するやうで、どうして国家を治める事が出来ませうか。賢明なる太子殿下は早くも此点に気付かれ、王位を去つて庶民となり、個人として人間らしい生活をやつて見度いと望んでゐらつしやるのですよ。もうお父さま、貴方も好い加減に骸骨をお乞ひなさい。貴方が一日国政を料理さるればさるるだけ、それだけ国家は滅亡に向ふのです。国民の多くは……頑迷固陋の左守が一日も早く、此世を去れば一日丈国家の利益だ……と云ふて居りますよ』

ガン『お前は個人々々と云ふて盛に個人主義をまくし立てるが、個人主義が発達すればする程専制政治が必要ぢやないか。完全なる個人主義が発達し、生活し得る力が出来た所で、ほんの小つぽけな砂のやうなものだ。二十万の国民が、二十万粒の砂になつたやうなものだ。個々別々になつた砂は何程堅固でも団結力は有るまい。個人としてはよからうが、国家及団体としては実につまらぬものぢや。そこで、カラピン王家と云ふ大きな革袋が必要なのだ。この革袋に二十万粒の砂を入れ袋の口を固く縛り横槌などで強く叩きつけてこそ初めて一つの国家団体が固まるのぢやないか。革包の破れた袋は所謂支離滅裂何の力もない。それを貴様は破らうとする極重悪人だ。賢明なる殿下のそれ位の道理のお解りにならない筈は無いのだが、貴様が常に悪い思想を吹き込むものだから、あのやうな悪い精神におなりなされたのだ。云はば貴様はタラハン国を覆へす悪魔の張本だ。あゝもう仕方がない。死ぬにも死なれず、悴は何程説き聞かしても頑迷不霊にして時代を解せず、政治を知らず、何とした苦しい立場であらう』

アリ『お父様、煩悶苦悩の今日の境遇、私も同情致しますが、併し乍ら心の持ちやう一つで厶いますよ。些と郊外の散歩でもして、天地の芸術を御覧なさいませ、さうすれば些とは胸も開けて新しい思想が生れて来るでせう』

左守は青息吐息しながら、

『あゝあゝ兎やせむ角や線香の煙となつて、タラハンの国家は滅ぶのかなア』
