Chapter 13: Mountain 中の Fire Light [1715]

山中の火光〔1715〕

§ 1

While gazing down with wondrous eyes upon the expansive plains in all directions, and being blown by the wind of the mountain ridge, the Taishi presses onward step by step as his heart desires. The sun sinks completely into the western mountains, and the curtains of darkness gradually descend. Countless birds seek lodging on the treetops of ancient trees, chirping with voices like bells. Meanwhile, the rising moonlight is exceptionally beautiful, illuminating without a single shadow the great sky, which is utterly devoid of even a speck of cloud. This place is known as the famous Mount Toride in the land of Tarahan. Seated peacefully upon a great rock standing straight up at the summit of Mount Toride, the Taishi gazes upon the scenery of the sky, yearning for its grand and natural form.

Taishi: 'The moon rising through the great sky, holding up the wish-granting jewel, is the land of yearning. The great sky, scattered with gemstones, must be the manifestation of the divine power. The sound of the pine wind crossing the ridge is also clear, and surely it speaks of some mystery. When I look down, the plains in all directions are bathing in the dew of the moonlight, glowing wondrously. Now, here upon the peak of Mount Toride, I draw the pure water of eternal life. The dew of the moon dampens my body and soul, and indeed it feels as though I have been revived.'

Alina: 'When I follow and see behind the wake of the Young Lord, Mount Toride is particularly magnificent. The rock upon which the Young Lord stands shall leave a name that remains unchanged for a thousand ages. At this very hour, my father must surely be searching for the Young Lord's whereabouts and causing a stir. Picturing the stern divine heart of the Great Lord, I become soaked in tears thinking of my father.'

Taishi: 'Gazing upon the form in which heaven and earth are born, I do not wish to return to the dead mansion. If I return to my dwelling, the fence of separation from my father will surely grow higher. Flowers are fragrant and tree fruits ripen abundantly; I truly wish to live upon this sacred mountain.'

Despite the midnight hour, the Taishi stands up once more, relying on the light of the moon to descend into valleys or cross peaks, pressing onward without knowing a destination. Muttering in a low voice out of sheer helplessness, Alina follows, keeping an interval of five or six paces so as not to lose sight of the Taishi's figure. The moon of the fifteenth night has already hidden behind the high mountains, and a great mountain shadow approaches. Having come without even carrying bread, moistening their throats with fruits across several ri of mountains and fields, the two became exhausted as ropes and fell fast asleep, lying down beneath the dense trees. When the two naturally opened their eyes, it was the hour of the eight-shaku of the afternoon of the following day.

Rubbing his eyes, the Taishi speaks, Taishi: 'Oi, Alina, what on earth is this place called? Having become so exhausted, it seems I slept without knowing front or back, and is it not already the hour of the eight-shaku on the following day? Father, Samori, and Osamori must surely be astonished and making a commotion at not seeing my form. Shall we not return for the time being?'

Alina: 'Hai, I understand. We must return even if it takes but a single moment. If we return to the palace, I am certain to incur the wrath of the Great Lord, my father, and the like, but I shall bear all responsibility myself, so please do return.'

Taishi: 'What, it would not do for me if I made you bear the responsibility. No matter what anyone says, I am the Taishi of a country. I shall apologize to Father as I please. And I will swear upon my life to explain so that no blame comes upon your person, so be at ease.'

Alina: 'It is no use causing the Crown Prince worry. Since it is entirely my fault, come, let us set forth.'

This time, Alina becomes the guide and sets off on the homeward path. Whether they lost their way somehow, go as they might, the return path is nowhere to be found. Mountains tower here and there by the thousands and hundreds, and looking down at the valley bottoms, bluish water flows gently.

Alina: 'Crown Prince, we have come to a dreadful place. Since I am also seeing the mountain paths of these parts for the first time, I have no idea toward which way we should walk to return. I have truly done a regrettable thing.'

Taishi: 'What, do not worry. If we do not know the path, living a mountain life will do just fine, will it not? Although I have grown quite hungry, the amount of food for the two of us, you and me, will surely hold out even if we gather and eat tree fruits. In all things, it is best to leave it to Kanagara.'

ア『殿下大変な所へ参りました。私も此辺の山路は初めてで厶いますので、何方へ歩んだら帰れるやら、見当が取れませぬ。誠に済まない事を致しました』

太『何、心配するな。道が分らねば山住居をすりやそれで好いぢやないか。余程空腹にはなつたが、余とお前と二人の食料位は木の実を取つて食つて居ても続くだらう、何事も惟神に任すがよからうぞ』

A: 'Ah, yes; but if we stray into such a deep valley in the midst of such mountain recesses, we can scarcely even make out our direction. At any rate, shall we try setting up this stick, and decide to proceed in the direction in which it falls?'

Tota: 'Ha, that is one plan too. What need is there to have any worries? The mountains are green, the valley water is clear, the birds sing, the fresh greenery is luxuriant, rare flowers compete in brilliance here and there and diffuse fragrant aromas, the air is warm—is there anything as delightful as this? For my part, I should like to wander about in the mountains for ten or twenty days more, ah-ha-ha-ha!'

A: 'How carefree Your Highness is. A timid person like myself has already reached the limit of endurance.'

Tota: 'Ha-ha-ha-ha-ha. You are a fellow who readily blows a cowardly tune. Look at those grasses and trees. While being tossed by fierce winds in such a steep mountain, do they not open their unseasonal flowers and set fruit with absolute composure, enjoying nature? The birds cheerfully sing of spring, and mountain monkeys gather on the treetops just like that, playfully enjoying themselves with delight. Even if small, are we not human beings? Wherever we may be, there is no reason why we cannot sustain our livelihood. Embraced within the bosom of divine grace, intimately communing with nature, with heaven and earth as our father and mother, and being thus without any reserve or constraint toward anyone—are we not placed in a truly fortunate condition? What is there to regret? What is the meaning of that worried look of yours? Why don't you pull yourself together a bit, summon your vigor, and cheer up? As human beings who boast of being the lords of all creation and the flowers of heaven and earth, do you not feel ashamed before the mountains, rivers, grasses, trees, birds, and beasts?'

Tota: 'Ah-ha-ha-ha-ha, hey, Alina, do not utter such useless things. Kingship over India and the like are not within my purview. Rather than that, I wish to become a mere mortal fragment of the universe, travel widely through the realm, freely and spontaneously draw close to the grace of heaven and earth, and lead a truly human life. As human beings endowed with spirits born from heaven, to be hung behind blinds like puppets, enshrined by the multitude, respected, and worshiped—what joy is there in that? What honor does it become? I am thoroughly sick of the ways of modern humans filled with falsehood and pretense. I will certainly never commit such a foolish act as returning to the palace again. What do you say to weaving grass to make a rain cape, plaiting tree leaves to make a sedge hat, and setting out from now on with you on a penniless journey? Do you have that much sincerity? That is what I would like to hear. You would probably say you are incapable of enduring even that much hardship.'

A: 'I would endure any hardship if it were with Your Highness, but with a person of your status above the clouds, to whimsically imitate beggars and speak of a penniless journey goes far beyond mere amusement. I will say nothing that is ill-intentioned. Please think about it calmly. Considering even a little of the human feelings in the nation of Tarahan, the mind of the Great King, and the hearts of the subjects, I earnestly beg you to return to the castle for the time being.'

Futa: 'I do not say that we shall never return to the castle. However, the more I think of returning, the deeper I wander into the mountains, and do not the return routes become incomprehensible? That is why I believe this entirely to be the mandate of Ten, and have resolved upon a penniless journey. Ahahahahaha, what a faint-hearted fellow you are to the very end!'

Arina: 'Nay, I too, encouraged by the brave okokorozashi of Denka, have entrusted itsusaibanji to Tenchishinmei. I understand it as Tenmei whether we are able to return safely to the denchiu, or whether we wander deep into the mountains and become the ejiki of tigers and wolves. Come, let us proceed in the direction the staff has fallen.'

So saying, he took the staff he had brought along, stood it straight, and let go with a snap. The staff fell with a flap toward the left where Arina was standing.

Arina: 'Denka, it is thus. Let us proceed toward the left. Since this must also be a notification from Kamisama,'

Futa: 'Very well, trusting the fallen direction of the staff both as our staff and as our strength, let us make hitoiki of batsushō once more. Ah, how omoshiro, how omoshiro!'

So saying, the Taishi went ahead, hastily running left and left through the chiufuku of the mountain. Though he went and went, mountain upon mountain, as for the haugaku, it was beyond knowing, and that very day had completely grown dark and finished. The shiyujiyū futari folded tree leaves to serve as shikimono, and while holding their kūfuku, waited for the night to clear. From the zenpau valley, the voice of a lion echoed through the mountain peak's echoes and was heard in a terrifying manner. Arina trembled at this terrifying lion's voice, dyed his lips purple, and with an ash-white face exposed to the moonlight, shivered and shook.

Arina: 'Mo-mo-if, de-de-Denka, ta-ta-it has come to a terrible thing. Ko-ko-tonight, do-do-somehow we might be eaten, who kn-kn-knows. If this is said to be so, it is entirely because I am bad. De-de-if a matter should occur that brings hardship upon the august body of Denka, toward the Daiōsama, the multitude of okeraushū, and further toward the kokumin as well, mo-mo-apologies cannot be made. Do-do-please forgive my great crime.'

Already he was weeping. Sudaruman Taishi remained calm and without the slightest agitation,

Futa: 'Ahahahahaha, oi, Arina, what manner is that? Is the lion that frightening? Is that fellow not a beast? For a human being, who may even be called the living palace of Kami, to tremble and quake in fear before a mere lion, tiger, or wolf—what kind of matter is this? That lion fellow, upon seeing my figure, is on the contrary trembling, fearing, and raising a cry of lamentation.

The living palace of the gods of heaven and earth stepping forth, Beholding this, the lion must be roaring. What could lions, bears, tigers, or wolves signify, Since we are in the bodies of humans, the august children of Kami? Receiving the deep grace of heaven and earth, What do we fear in the voice of a beast?'

Arina: 'If I am with the young prince, the roaring of the lion and bear Did not seem a thing to be feared at all. Yet every time I hear the roar of the lion, My body naturally strikes and trembles.'

The voices of the fierce beasts were heard from all directions like a hundred thunders. When he looked at the valley bottom on the left hand, how marvelous, a single pillar of fire light was flickering through the gaps in the trees. The Taishi, being quick-eyed, saw this, and while patting Arina's back two or three times with the flat of his hand,

Futa: 'Oi, captain of cowards, teacher of Arina, be at ease. Behold that fire light. It is surely not the fire of an apparition. That is certainly sunlight. It seems somehow that humans are dwelling there. Shall we seek out human dwellings with that fire light as our guide and partake of food and drink?'

Arina raised his head at the words of the Taishi and, looking down in the direction he pointed, saw a truly powerful light of fire flickering. Suddenly recovering his spirit, and with a courageous voice,

Arina: 'Ya, truly, just as Denka commanded, the fire light is visible. It must be entirely the august grace of Ten. Let us descend, taking that fire as our guide, even a moment sooner. Come, since I shall open the spider's webs, please do follow behind me.'

Futa: 'Un, very well, you too seem to have suddenly grown strong. I too have grown strong in heart because of that.'

So saying, he parted the thicket of overgrown shrubs and descended.

太『オイ、弱虫の隊長、アリナの先生、安心せよ。あの火光を見よ。決して妖怪の火でもあるまい。あれは確に陽光だ。どうやら人間が住ひをして居るらしい。あの火光を目当に人家を尋ね飲食にありつかうぢやないか』

アリナは太子の言葉に頭を上げ指さす方を瞰下せば如何にも力強い火の光が瞬いて居る。俄に元気恢復し、声も勇ましく、

ア『ヤ、如何にも殿下の仰の通り火光が見えます。全く天の御恵で厶いませう。一時も早くあの火を目当に下りませう。サア私が蜘蛛の巣開きを致しますから、どうか後について来て下さいませ』

太『ウンよし、お前も俄に強くなつたやうだ。俺もそれで心強くなつた』

と云ひ乍ら灌木茂る木の間を分けて下り往く。
