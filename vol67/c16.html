Chapter 16: 酒艶の Moon [1718]

酒艶の月〔1718〕

§ 1

Genshinbau felt that Dariyahime had suddenly grown gentle and seemed completely infatuated with him, which made him all the more proud; with a relaxed expression and lost to himself, he carried his cup from his left hand toward a mouth with teeth like jagged river stakes.

Genshin: 'Oi, Dariya, it is not right to pour wine only for your husband. Hey, think a little of what others around us might say. Well, will you not pour a cup for Oyakata as well?'

Dariya: 'Ho ho ho ho ho, how hateful of you to say such a thing. Important though Oyakata may be, how could I ever put the husband to whom I have entrusted my life for eternity second? I am also a little tipsy on wine, so you may think I am speaking impolitely, but I beg you to view it as the foolish talk of a woman and forgive me.'

Genshin: 'Un un yoshi yoshi, to see you hold the warmed sake flask in your white hands is truly peerless under heaven. Ehe he he he he. Getting drunk is good, it tastes good, my spirits are so bright they are good, it satisfies my belly so it is good, from the early evening of yoi yoi yoi until the break of day, for a husband and wife to drink deeply together and nourish the vast flowing spirit of kōzen is a joy under heaven like no other. What a wondrously fortunate fellow I am to be loved by you, a beauty who might be likened to the moon, the snow, or the flowers. Well now, Shakanna-dono, are you not envious, eh? For this night only, I command the Benzaiten of Azuma to be your drinking companion, so I am sure you will buy this humble monk's good will. Geh, afu fu fu ah aa. What a potent wine this is, considering I intended to have drunk no more than one or two cups yet.'

Shakanna: 'Aha ha ha ha, I too am heavily intoxicated. While gazing upon the face of Dariyahime-sama, who lies to the flowers, to toss back mellow wine—there is no knowing how much more refreshing this feeling is than spring flower-viewing, or autumn moon-viewing, or maple-leaf viewing, or a banquet of snow-viewing that reveals a silver world spanning all of earth.'

Genshin: 'Indeed, so it must be. This too is the fruit of my goodwill toward you. You must give thanks, ba ha ha, divine retribution will strike you otherwise.'

Shakanna: 'Nay, having now gazed upon this most auspicious scene to my heart's content, Shakanna is well satisfied. As I look upon this spectacle, the spirit of my departed wife must surely be rejoicing before the spiritual altar. Namu Yūrei Tonshō Bodai... A drinking Amida Buddha of fine wine, a chewing Amida Buddha. Atsu ha ha ha.'

Dariya: 'Otsu ho ho ho ho, what delightfully amusing men you both are, calling one Oyakata and the other my husband Tenshinbau-sama. I simply adore men like this. How is it that I am able to be matched with such a lighthearted, good-natured, and splendid man? And with an imposing, fierce countenance graced with whiskers like Shakanna-sama's, I feel a joy as though I had gathered all the happiness of heaven and earth into my own single person. Oho ho ho.'

Shakanna: 'Ha ha ha, truly admirable. Dariyahime-sama is quite the socialite. If you were made a diplomat, you would surely display astonishing skill. It is only a pity that you are a woman, so it cannot be helped.'

Dariya: 'Ho ho ho, what a thing for Oyakata-sama to say. There is no reason why a woman cannot become a diplomat, you know. In the world today, if women do not exert themselves, how can their husbands make their way in the world? Those officials today called minor lords or great lords all attained their positions because their wives are skillful at diplomacy. When a woman quietly slips in through the back door, murmurs a few gentle words to the superior's wife, lavishes flattery upon her, and presents even a single bolt of cloth, her husband's official rank is promoted within a month without fail. No matter how much strength or wisdom a man may possess, it is useless if his wife lacks diplomatic skill. Ne, Tenshinbau-sama, what do you think?'

Gen: 'Utsufuhuhuhu, you are just as you say. Not looking your age, you are a splendid woman. If I thought you were only capable, you seem to have considerable wits and arms as well. More and more, I have come to yearn for and love you. "Such a husband and such a wife," is truly well said. Gen: 'Nay, I am in love more and more. I will surely hand you a bonus, I will lay it on thick.'

Dari: 'Ah, I will give it today, I will give it tomorrow, I will give it after a while longer—you must be thinking of dragging it out endlessly with delays and procrastinations, stretching it out limply and sluggishly to put people off. Such an inauspicious kototama must be declined.'

Gen: 'Good grief, what a difficult missus you are. If so, how much should I give you?'

Dari: 'Do not go about calling me missus, missus, is it not far too unseemly? I am not the wife of a rickshaw puller or a stablehand, am I not the okugata of Tenshinbōsama, the incarnation of the Heavenly Emperor? If you are going to give me a bonus, please throw out toward this okugata all of your sincerity.'

Gen: 'Ah, this is troublesome, if you come at me with all of my sincerity, I cannot help being taken aback a bit. My sincerity is such that I would like to give even ten billion yen, but my purse does not allow it. Since it is the okugata's very first request, I want to give with all my might, but somehow, with the deadlock in the economic world, an autumn wind is blowing a little bit in my purse. I would like you to endure for today with around three hundred yen or so.'

Dari: 'Hohoho, is not a mere three hundred yen as pillow money too cheap for the okugata of Tenshinbōsama, the incarnation of the Heavenly Emperor? It is altogether too heartless, hey, Boss Shakanna?'

Sha: 'Hahahaha, that is the way of a husband and wife. Lay it on thick and bear with it. Indeed, in today's world there are no softies who fork out money for their wives. Even today, when all commodity prices have soared to extremes, the cheapest thing is a wife. Raised like butterflies and flowers from atop straw, and if it is a middle-class family or above, after pouring in plenty of money through elementary school, girls' school, and women's university, they prepare chests of drawers, clothing chests, triple boxes, this and that until the ridge of the house is warped, saying, "She is a clumsy woman, but please treat her well," and hand her over for free in this world—so no matter what you say is cheap, there is nothing as cheap as the market price for a bride. Therefore, Dariisama, if you receive three hundred yen, you are the greatest hero under heaven. Because Tenshinbōsama has fallen head over heels and neck-deep in love with you, he says he is going to throw out a hefty three hundred yen. Ah, I am drunk, I am drunk. Somehow I feel as though I am floating in the sky. At a time like this, if I had a wife, I would gladly use her knee as a pillow and sleep, but I cannot possibly borrow the knee pillow of Tenshinbōsama's okugata, oh, what an enviable thing.'

Dari: 'Hohoho, please tease me just enough. However, thanks to the boss's words, I have truly realized that I am the happiest woman in the world. My, what a splendid person Tenshinbōsama is. Hearing that, I have come to cherish you more and more, hey, Tenshinbōsama. Saying things like, "You have grown old, so I no longer need you," and doing such a heartless thing as throwing away a torn shoe—I would hate that. ...Until a hundred bowls, until ninety-nine chopsticks, until both coats of vermilion paint peel off... You will stay with me harmoniously, won't you? And please see to it that I do not have to worry myself like this. Because I fret easily, you see.'

§ 4

Baru: 'Ya, I am a geiko. I am merely sitting at the table for the sake of company. I do not wish to drink any sake.'

Genshinbau had drunk himself into a stupor and fallen fast asleep, utterly unaware of his surroundings. Dariyahime quietly slipped her knee out, propped a wooden pillow against a roasted sesame bun, and while pouring a cup for Koruton with a slender, cool voice,

Dariya: 'How truly lovely is a man who drinks sake, And even lovelier when he gets drunk and spins yarns. Now, now, Koruton-sama, please pass your time like a man. As you see, Tenshin-sama has also fallen asleep. It seems the oyabun-sama has fallen asleep as well. From here on, I shall drink you under the table, anata. Barugī-sama is a geiko, and the two oyabun-sama have fallen asleep; now it is just a world for me and Koruton-sama.'

Koruton: 'Ehehhhh, why, thank you; this too is thanks to drinking sake. To exchange cups with a beautiful hime who looks as though Otohime of Riugū were coming to collect the unjiyō is just like a dream. Now, hime, I too am a man. No matter how much you give me, I will not back down. Please watch my way of drinking.'

Dariya: 'My, what a splendid way of drinking, truly like a man. I would love to become the wife of such a person. But because I made a naiyaku with Tenshinbau-sama, it has already become impossible to back out.'

She said this in a purposely low voice. Koruton truly believed that the hime had fallen for him, and becoming all the more full of himself, he displayed his prowess as a gauketsu, drinking down ungodly amounts of sake he did not particularly care for, just to keep up the pace and win the hime's heart over to his side.

Dariya: 'Ohohhhh, how magnificent. No matter how much you partake, you do not get drunk in the least. That is a man's true worth. If a man were to collapse just from drinking a little bit of sake, he would be of no use in an emergency.'

Koruton: 'That is indeed so. I am a famous sakagauketsu of whom it is said that even if he drinks from the corner of a sho box, he does not budge an inch.'

In this fashion, Koruton was thoroughly defeated by Dariya, and with his eyes rolling back, he continued to endure, but unable to bear it any longer, he collapsed pathetically right on the spot. In the barracks-style hut, a large crowd of kids were spreading straw mats, chattering noisily in a clamor, thoroughly drunk, crying, laughing, throwing punches, and indulging in wild, disorderly revelry to the utmost limit.

Dariyahime, having ascertained that all three of them were completely drunk and collapsed, wrapped her white arms around Barugī's neck and lowered her voice,

Dariya: 'I say, Barugī-sama, I have truly done a terrible thing to you. Since you do not partake of sake in the least, your decorum truly remains unbroken, and it is charming. I truly love a person like you. Look at that. All three of them, drunk on sake, foaming at the mouth, drooling, are truly a sight not fit to be seen. I say, Barugī-sama, do you think of me as hateful?'

Barugī, struck by the unexpectedness, drooled happily while hastily fumbling with both hands. Then, lowering his voice,

Baru: 'Hime-sama, please stop treating me as a mere toy to a reasonable extent. I too am a fraction of a man. You must be quite drunk on sake.'

Dariya: 'Even I am a human being, so if I drink sake, I get a little drunk. However, I am not drunk to the point of losing my true nature like those three-sama. Do you doubt what I am saying? Oh, how hateful!'

So saying, she pinched Barugī's cheek hard.

Baru: 'Aitahhhh, please, Hime-sama, I do not doubt you at all; I take you in earnest. Please let go of that hand. My face is going to get distorted.'

Dariya: 'Do you truly believe what I am saying?'

§ 5

Baru: 'I believe everything, so please rest assured.'

Dariya: 'If so, you will surely understand the feelings I hold so deeply within my heart, won't you? Please, will you not take me and run away? Since my home is a millionaire in the village of Suga, it is far more sensible than doing something like thieving. And please become my husband.'

Dariya: 'Then my husband-sama, no matter how far, please let me accompany you.'

Baru -gy hastily prepared foot-attire and gave waraji to Dariya as well, making her ready for flight. ...The three of them, in bodies that were not gods, had collapsed in simple-minded drunkenness. Dariya dipped a brush in ink, inscribed 'Nenneko' upon Tenshinbau's forehead, inscribed 'Mō, inu' upon Shakanna's forehead, scribbled 'Saru, kaheru' upon Koruton's forehead, leaving behind a laugh of 'Ho ho ho ho ho', and guided by Baru-gi who knew the secret paths, successfully fled from this place.

A moonlit sleepy crow perched upon the branches of the great trees all around, crying 'Ahō ahō'. Inside the cavern, amidst snores like thunder, the night grows deeper and deeper. The innocent moon smiles as it illuminates the two people's escaping path.
