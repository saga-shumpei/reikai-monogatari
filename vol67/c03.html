Chapter 3: Beauty 人の腕 [1705]

美人の腕〔1705〕

§ 1

As the starlight of the full heavens was brilliant, like sinking gold and silver sands into the surface of Lake Haru, though there was no moonlight, the sky over the great lake, true to its name, was red, and the Milky Way flowed purely from the peak of Mount Aura toward the sky of Castle Vargan. When one looked up at the eastern sky, the Pleiades seemed to advance high overhead, heavily towing the Orion constellation. The morning star, now making its lodging in the shade of the old trees on the peak of Mount Aura, was peering through the tree leaves with a twinkling sparkle. The sails were lean and silent, and the progress of the vessel was so slow that it was a stillness as if it had almost stopped. Countless large and small islands scattered in the lake floated quietly and densely, enveloped in pine trees. From time to time, only the voices of sea birds, like the crying of infants, and the flapping of the wings of Amboinan struck the earlobes of everyone. The heavens were silent without a voice, the sea surface calm and not breathing, feeling just like traveling upon a sleeping sea that had lost its vitality. All the passengers emerged onto the deck, bathing in the night dew, and scattered faint sparks from the bowl of their tobacco pipes hither and thither. At this time, rowing eight oars, a single boat that had come biting towards the Namikirimaru like an arrow and like a fierce dog carried fourteen or fifteen pirates. Suddenly throwing a rope ladder onto the gunwale, before one could say ah-ya, the one who climbed up with a great sword drawn was a leader called Cōse, feared as a fiend pirate upon this lake. He stood defiant on the deck, and together with his fourteen or fifteen subordinates, with swords still drawn, he was shouting.

Cōse: 'We are the masters of the waters of Lake Haru, I am the lord of the pirate chiefs, Cōse. Now that I have appeared, no words are needed. Whatever possessions any of you may have, without distinction of high or low, poor or rich, offer them up before me. If you say this and that and refuse, I shall tear off every single one of your necks, thread them on a rope to make a rosary, and offer them to Mount Daiun in the capital of Haruna. How is that, let me hear your answer.'

Thus he began to heap up overbearing extortion. It was a tragic sight indeed to behold: among all the passengers, some trembled in fear and hid at the bottom of the ship, some raised shrieks and ran hither and thither on the deck, and some trembled without even managing to raise a voice. At the very moment when Cōse ordered his subordinates to strip the passengers completely bare one by one, took out prepared ropes, gathered the plunder into a bundle, and was about to throw it into his own ship, a natural beauty appearing as if drawn by a master painter emerged abruptly from the bottom of the ship, trembling her cinnabar lips while making her white arms like raw wax shine amidst the dim dark,

Woman: 'You are remembered as Cōse, famed as the master of the waters of Lake Haru. I shall chastise you.'

Even quicker than the speaking, she grabbed the lapel hair of the rough man like Niō, shouted a single 'Ei!' and threw him toward the sea surface; caught off guard, even the mighty Cōse spun skillfully through the air two or three times, dropped with a thud onto his own boat, and sat heavily on his rear. The other lacklings panicked and fled back and forth, but... 'Ah, leaf-like fools, how troublesome'... grabbing and throwing them in a domino effect, grabbing and throwing them, in the twinkling of an eye she finished the grand cleanup of the deck. Revealing her white teeth, the beauty laughed faintly with a look of utmost delight, 'Oh-ho-ho-ho', leisurely descended the stairs, entered her bedroom with an innocent face, and fell into a dream of deep sleep with her arm as a pillow. All the passengers merely whispered to one another with a clamor only of the rumor that a strange woman had appeared from nowhere and thrown the villains into the sea. Baikō was sleeping drowsily, but startled awake by the whispering voices of the people, and listening with ears strained to hear what could be the matter, the voices of various people—that a saving god had appeared... or that an incarnation of the sea dragon god had saved them... or that she was the daughter of the tengu of Mount Aura... or that she was a woman of the sea...—were indulging in all sorts of miscellaneous speculations. The captain, as though finally relieved, fearfully surveyed the sea surface from the top of the deck; though Cōse's ship was not seen, a dozen pirate ships in the distant shadow of the island ahead seemed as though waiting in readiness, blocking the onward path of the Namikirimaru. Captain Alley clasped both hands and thought thus: If we take the present sea route, we shall surely be threatened again by Cōse's underlings. Though it is a detour, let us turn the prow to the east and proceed while drawing close to the shore... Giving orders to his subordinate sailors, manipulating the oars, and paddling with all his might while sketching sudden ripples upon the mirror-like sea surface, he dashed forward with all his strength. The ship suddenly struck a reef, and the bottom of the ship made strange sounds like rattle-rattle-rattle and crack-crack-crack, crushing the hearts of all the passengers. Suddenly, cries of shrieking agony—'Help us, oh help us'—came echoing from the entire front of the ship. Baikō suddenly appeared at the bow, chanted the heavenly number song, and with a clear and resonant voice offered a song of prayer for salvation to the gods.

§ 2

Calm are the waves on the lake of Haru, / The heavenly sky is pure and the water is clear; / Beholding the heavenly river in the firmament, / The sacred boat that rows along / Is the jewel-boat wherein the god of Tanabata was revered. / Protect us, O vast expanse of Wada! / O Riujin, who rulest thy domain, / While we fall into slumber, / Wandering in the pure sacred garden of Tengoku / Where flowers bloom in fragrance, / Before the august presence of Sumeōkami, / We commit our bodies to Namikirimaru, / The divine child who purifies the soul, / Crossing the boundless clouds above. / In this boat traversing the great sky, / Can there be any hardship of reefs? / Though we know not what divine planning this may be, / The Sendenshi who transmits the teachings of Sumekami,

Having descended from heaven to grant protection, / How could any obstacle of evil prevail? / Riding upon the ship of divine salvation, / Going forth to transmit the way of salvation, / For the august sake of the gods of heaven and earth, / May Umekō, who devotes his sincerity, / Behold today's distress, / And through the power of our Kototama, / Cast down the breath of the god Shinatobe from Amatsusora, / Awaken the slumber of the lake! / May the hippocampi shake their manes, / And the Riujin lurking deep in the ocean floor rise up, / Stirring up violent waves and surging billows, / While lifting high into mid-air this our boat / Which had run aground upon a hidden rock, / Save us from the peril of the reefs!

Though tens of thousands of pirates / Should follow their subordinates and attack us, / We have the divine protection; / Yet because the common people / Are mixed with impure souls, / They may invite the wrath of the gods. / Ah, Sumekami! Sumekami! / Shouldst thou summon my life thread of jewels, / I would not shrink from it; / But pray save the lives of the people, / O Kamunagara! / This is the one and only prayer of Umekō of the San-Go-Kyō, / Offered with reverence and awe. / Ah, Kamunagara, O gods! / May your august spirits flourish! / Whether the great sun shines or is clouded, / Whether the moon waxes or wanes, / Whether there be an age when the sea dries up, / For the august sake of Sumeōkami, / Illuminating the wondrous spirit to the world,

Through all eternity without limit, / In the three worlds of visible, invisible, and divine, / Going out and coming in, / I, a child of God, shall protect my life. / Summon my body, O Lord; / Even though the physical life should perish, / As long as the life of the soul remains, / I shall humbly serve the divine work / Of the divine dwelling. / Save the people, / Set this boat afloat!

Thus he chanted in a loud voice, when suddenly a mass of black clouds rose in the northeastern sky, expanding before one's eyes to swallow the starlight of the full firmament, and next swallowed the lake of Haru. With the roaring sound of a hurricane and the noise of billows, Namikirimaru was instantly lifted high into mid-air like a withered leaf by the rising waves; at that very moment, its hull left the reef, half-swallowed by the waves, and was unexpectedly carried to a fairly large island shadow. Just as they were floating the boat in the calm shadow of the island out of the wind and taking a breath, suddenly the entire sky cleared as if wiped clean, the wind calmed and the waves grew still, and no one could help marveling as if in a dream at the excessive swiftness of the change.

By the august grace of Kamunagara's divine breath, / The jewel-boat has verily been saved. / My soul flew into mid-air, while my boat / Floated and sank in the valleys of the waves. / Not in vain did I pray with all my heart, / And Namikiri's ship caught a breath at last. / Where have the evil gods fled, pointing to what place, / Leaving not even a shadow upon the great ocean? / The curtain of darkness that swallowed heaven and swallowd the island, / The joy of its clearing without a trace. / The voices of a hundred birds could at last be heard / After the roaring of the waves had subsided. / The pale faces of the people gradually / Began to look up once more upon the night lake.

The god who pacifies the raging winds and waves / That we feared would tear the heavens and shatter the earth. / As I behold the wondrous power of the gods, / I am ashamed of the smallness of human strength. / My master is heading toward the castle of Bargan, / And is waiting for me at Melaonna.

When Shakanmela shook its mane and bit into our boat, / I too trembled. / Shakanmela, standing and shaking its mane, / Must have entered the bottom of the great ocean. / Shakanmela vanished like bubbles of foam; / Protect the divine boat until we reach the land of God.

From somewhere came the sweet singing voice of a woman.

The joy of the time when we dispelled the evil / That torments the travelers coming and going on the lake of Haru. / By the wondrous power of Sumekami, Yoshikohime / Has saved people for the very first time. / The regret of having committed sins hitherto / By engaging only in foul deeds. / Wondering in terror if the god of the sea / Was punishing me because of my sinful self. / By the Kototama of Umekō's wondrous office, / Wondrous salvation has been manifested. / Kamunagara, come, from today onwards / How joyful it is for my body to advance onto the Great Way!

Namikirimaru, which had mistakenly run aground on a reef while trying to avoid the peril of pirates, had its wedged hull floated free as the ocean waves surged, moved by the compassionate pity of the gods at the earnest prayers and Kototama of Umekō, and was thus at last spared the peril of sinking and destruction. The captain and all the passengers knelt before Umekō to praise his divine virtue and offered their sincere gratitude to the Great Deity. At length the night dawned faintly, and the water surface, once more filled with fish-scale waves and tinged with blue, slipped on toward the south, ever southward.
