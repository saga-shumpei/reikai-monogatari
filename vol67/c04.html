Chapter 4: 笑の座 [1706]

笑の座〔1706〕

§ 1

As Koshin shook the mane of Hakuba, raising fierce waves and roaring billows with a force that seemed almost to swallow the sky, the wildness of the lake suddenly subsided as if a fit of epilepsy had ceased, calming down completely like a blatant lie, so that the water surface wrinkled like a tatami mat or crepe silk. Pushing out its bow into the shadow of the islands, the Namikirimaru drifted forth with rich funauta, turning its prow toward the south.

As a custom of this region, whenever things were peaceful and quiet, a gathering known as the 'Seat of Laughter' would sometimes be held to console one's boredom. Those who participated in the Seat of Laughter all wrapped their facial features in black cloth so that no one could tell who they were, and gossiping about everything from the high-ranking nobility down to servant maids and menservants, the current state of the nation, and the subtle nuances of human feelings, they indulged in ridicule and mockery with entertaining hilarity, laughing heartily and using it mutually as a compass for self-cultivation and family regulation. Even the immensely powerful Daikokunushi could not lay a single finger upon this Seat of Laughter alone. Instead of commoners participating in national politics, the Seat of Laughter unwrittenly permitted them to vent their grievances and pent-up frustrations, or even to speak out eloquently and unreservedly before anyone at all about the rights and wrongs, justice and injustice of politics, and the advantages and disadvantages of the state.

The sun was mild, the wind warm, the waves calm, the progress of the boat leisurely, and heat haze shimmered in the sunlight across the distant lake surface. That appearance seemed just as if the crepe-like wrinkles of the water's surface were reflected in the air. Out of joy at having escaped the terrifying dangers of pirates, the afflictions of violent storms and raging billows, and the peril of sunken reefs that had nearly brought the Namikirimaru to shipwreck, everyone worshipped the gods of heaven and earth, offering words of gratitude for about half an hour, after which, gathering in threes and fours to form a circle upon the deck, the Seat of Laughter was opened.

Kō: 'Gentlemen, what do you say to holding a seat of laughter, exempt from the law of the land, to console our traveller's sentiment by gazing upon this tranquil lake surface?'

Otsu: 'Ah, that would be interesting indeed. I was just thinking it might be well to let operate the heavenly jeweled spear, which acts as an organ of public opinion. I would love to hear some interesting talk.'

Kō: 'Everyone, please put on your black cloths. For this too is an unwritten rule established since the Age of Gods in this country. In return, I might utter abusive words and vituperation against you who are right before my eyes... but as a rule of the Seat of Laughter, I ask you beforehand not to take offense, hahaha.'

Otsu: 'Come now, since one cannot see the stains upon one's own face, people are apt to think that they are great and wonderful, while the folks of the world are fools, blockheads, or cowards. Once a person comes to understand oneself, they are a full-fledged person of character, but as the saying goes, it is dark right under the lighthouse, and since one cannot understand one's own affairs, please criticize without hesitation whatever you happen to notice. For that will become my sole source of strength in mastering life.'

Kō: 'Very well, once the bung of the inverted sake cask is removed, eccentric theories and turbid doctrines that make you spew out unpleasant cloudy sake and lead you into a drunken, dreamlike death may well gush forth endlessly.'

Otsu: 'Come, please, I beg of you. If one were a person who could see one's own head and face, or see one's own neck and back, one's own faults would surely become plainly clear; but we humans, being imperfectly created, inevitably have a dark side. To have that dark side exposed and brought to attention by a close friend must be the supreme happiness. However, I shall expose your dark side as well, you know—are you prepared for that?'

Kō: 'That is give-and-take. Then I shall launch the spark first... Eh, you were recently granted a tremendously grand official title by Lord Daikokunushi, but tell me, how do you feel about it in general? Speaking of a Takejigwan official, unlike a common low-ranking clerk, going to your office must require considerable preparations such as a horse or a carriage. You must find it exceedingly pleasant, don't you?'

原文（近代文語）

湖神白馬の鬣を揮つて、激浪怒濤を起し、殆ど天をも呑まむとする勢なりし湖上の荒びも、癲癇が治まつたやうに、まるつきり嘘をついた様にケロリと静まつて、水面は恰も畳の目の如く、縮緬皺をよせてゐる。島影を漕出した波切丸は、欵乃豊かに舳を南方に向けていざり出した。

此地方の風習として、人々何れも閑散な時には無聊を慰むる為に、笑ひの座といふものが催される事がある。笑ひの座に参加する者は、何れも黒い布で面部を包み、何人か分らぬやうにしておいて、上は王公より下は下女下男の噂や国家の現状や人情の機微などを話し、面白く可笑しく、罵詈嘲笑を逞しうして、笑ひこけ、互に修身斉家の羅針盤とするのである。流石権力旺盛なる大黒主と雖も、此笑ひの座のみには一指を染むる事も出来なかつた。笑ひの座は庶民が国政に参与する事のない代りに、其不平や鬱憤を洩らし、或は政治の善悪正邪や、国家の利害得失迄も、怯めず臆せず何人の前にても喋々喃々と吐露することを、不文律的に許されてゐたのである。

日は麗かに、風暖かく、波は静に、舟の歩みもはかばかしからず、遥の湖面には陽炎が日光に瞬いてゐる。其有様は恰も湖面の縮緬皺が空中に反映したかのやうに思はれた。さも恐ろしかりし海賊の難や暴風怒濤の悩み、殆ど難破に瀕したる波切丸の暗礁の難を免れたる嬉しさに、何れも天地の神を礼拝し、感謝の辞を捧ぐる事半時許り、其あとは三々伍々デッキの上に円を描いて、笑ひの座が開かれた。

甲『諸君、何うです、此穏かな湖面を眺めて、旅情を慰むる為に、天下御免の笑ひの座を催したら何うでせう』

乙『イヤそりや面白いでせう。チツト許り、言論機関たる天の瓊矛を運用させても宜しからうかと考へてゐた所です。何か面白い話を聞きたいものですなア』

甲『皆さま、黒布をお被りなさい。之も此国の神世から定まつた不文律ですから。其代りに目の前にゐる貴方方の悪口雑言を云ふかも知れませぬが……笑ひの座の規則として御立腹のなき様に予め願つておきますよ、アハヽヽヽ』

乙『サアサア自分の顔のしみは見えないものだから、俺は偉い偉い、世間の奴は馬鹿だとか、間抜だとか、腰抜だとか思つてゐるものです。自分が自分を理解する様になれば、人間も一人前の人格者ですが、燈台下暗しとか云つて、自分の事は解らないものですからな。どうか忌憚なく、お気付になつた事は批評して下さい。それが私に取つて処世上の唯一の力となりますから』

甲『宜しい、倒徳利の詰が取れた以上は、味の悪い濁酒を吐出して、諸君を酔生夢死せしむる様な迷論濁説が際限もなく迸出するかも知れませぬよ』

乙『サアサア是非願ひませう。自分の頭や顔面が見え、又自分の首や背中が見える様な人間ならば、自己の欠点が判然と解るでせうが、不完全に造られた吾々人間は、到底暗黒面のあるのは、止むを得ないです。其暗黒面を親しき友から、破羅剔抉して注意を与へて貰ふ事は、無上の幸福でせう。併しお前さまの暗黒面も素破抜きますが、御承知でせうな』

甲『それは相身互です。そんなら私から発火しませう。……エー、貴方此頃大黒主様から大変な偉い職名を与へられたといふ事だが一体どんな御気分がしますか、竹寺官と云へば腰弁とは違つて、役所へ通ふのにも馬とか車とか相当な準備も要るでせう。随分愉快でせうな』

§ 2

Otsu: 'Actually, I have been promoted to a shitsuji in a certain yakushiyo. However, having graduated from Akamon and swimming in the kuwankai for almost jifugonen, somehow or other I have risen to the rank of shitsuji. While our gakuiu have mostly risen from shōmiyō to daimiyō and nagonkifu ranks, I dislike such kaui as ayu, tennei, tsuishiyō, and teitōheishin, so despite possessing sautau jitsuriyoku, I have barely managed to become a shitsuji in a certain yakushiyo. Truly, even after spending jifugonenkan shishi kotsukotsu diving through the gates of the yakushiyo, living in a shiyakuyazumai up to now, and receiving various gagau, not a single itsusen of money besides my monthly salary wells up, nor is a single handful of rice produced, and I am entirely like a higher-level ronpen. Even so, because the other renchiu happily go about hanging labels with gagau attached to formal places, though it does not sink into my heart, I somehow feel as if I am losing face, and so I reluctantly go and paste on a label. Ahahahaha!'

Kō: 'You said you go while pasting what you dislike, but surely as for your true heart, it cannot possibly be that you are thoroughly loathsome and cannot bear it. If a hateful, hateful hairy caterpillar were clinging to your chest, anyone would surely brush it off. Therein lies your dark side, what is called hypocrisy. Peerage, what is it? Authority, what is it? Wealth and honor, what are they? These are probably decorative words meant to make people think that we are the loyal retainers of the realm. You probably make empty rituals and empty decorations your sole way of life, and believe them to be weapons in social intercourse. While such persons are floating in the upper classes, the fulfillment of the divine administration of the gods will probably also be utterly hopeless. Every time I see a thing called a rice-pounding grasshopper, a feeling of aversion somehow wells up in my chest. However, please pardon me for an overstatement. After all, these are words from the seat of the gathering of laughter, you see.'

Otsu: 'My, you are quite the critic too! Actually, I do not want to become a rice-pounding grasshopper either. If I quit this, my wife and children would immediately be cast out onto the streets and threatened by the difficulty of survival, so I bend my knees and bow my waist to the elders, and while sinking into the tragic plight of a grasshopper or a phonograph, I endure in the shadows and restrain myself, sending away the precious days and months. There is nothing quite as base, foolish, narrow-minded, and intensely proud as today's rice pounding. If there were some good trade, I would like to resign like a man. And I do not know how many times I have been drowned in tears of gnashing teeth, clenched fists, righteous indignation, and bitter grief, wanting to throw my letter of resignation right in front of the chief. Base, foolish, and bootlicking pettiness can steadily become shitsujis, shōmiyōs, and daimiyōs, and can flourish everywhere; but when it comes to a backbone man like me, the upper-class fellows attach various titles to me—saying that fellow is obstinate, stiff-necked, inflexible, an ambitious person, an extremist, a rebellious person, impertinent, cheeky, or whatever—and not only do they suppress my head, but if I dawdle, I am thrown out of the cold sea, so in human life, there is no one quite as miserable as a rice-pounding insect. Truly, those whose sorrow is extreme are those in official life. Hahahaha!'

Kō: 'On the whole, the people of the Land of the Moon, even if the country is large, consist only of petty persons, and it is probably impossible to eternally maintain the honor of ranking among the world's great powers. Diplomacy has become an utter mess, absorbed solely in watching the breathing of great powers; internal politics suppresses the free will of the people, and any human being with even a bit of backbone is thrown into prison on some pretext or another, employing only heaven's men as if they were nothing, making them ascend to prominent positions, and elevating only those who flatter and fawn upon themselves, while only fools and base persons writhe about in high places, so is not the very existence of the state also precarious? Encountering the present time, it must not do unless true heroes and martial champions who think of the state, or great true persons filled with the virtue of love and goodness, appear, must it?'

Oto: 'That is so; according to my thought, within these two or three years, a great national calamity from the Moon Kingdom will likely launch an attack. The ōbantō and other Nagon are always putting their heads together on the turf, whispering that something is terribly suspicious, and conferring with sighs and gasps, but they all say there is no way to apply a countermeasure. No matter what is said, the current condition of the world treats religion as a nuisance, pushes material instinct to the extreme, and misunderstands that everything in the world can be resolved as long as one has gold; therefore, national education has also entirely leaned toward materialism, the foundation of national faith has wobbled, and we are on the verge of near-spiritual bankruptcy, so there is hardly any hope of ever reversing this downward trend. Presently, the world is becoming seven major powers, and after a decade or more, the trend is that the world will divide into two major powers; somehow I would wish for the land of India to also become one of the two major powers, but under the administrative policy of today's leading figures, there is no path other than national ruin. Prices are high, officials are numerous, the populace is relatively large day by day, livelihood difficulties arrive daily, disturbances such as robberies and murders and unprincipled acts break out everywhere, humanity and morality have fallen to the earth, the human heart has grown as desolate as wolves and tigers, and even between parents and brothers, for the sake of interests, human feeling is no different from sworn enemies; the power of education and the power of religion are completely at zero. No, religion increasingly nurtures evil men, economics plunges the state and its people into poverty, the law alienates good men and employs the clever, medicine shortens human life, morality becomes a tool for evil men's fraudulent lives, commerce becomes an open swindler, and not a single thing can be found that maintains the state and advances national power. Because of this, I too wish to rouse myself and prevent the destruction of the nation beforehand, and I am feeling anxious, but in any case, being chased by food, clothing, and shelter, there is no way for me to lend a hand. If I utilized the position of a rice-pounding insect and took bribes relentlessly, or when I resigned from the cold official post, I might gain some surplus to be active in society, but that is a feat utterly impossible for me. Wondering whether to do this or that, thinking of the nation's future, I break my lungs and liver day and night, but it is to be deeply regretted that my heart alone is impatient and I cannot set foot upon its execution.'

Kō: 'Just now you said that if you resign from government office, you will immediately be troubled for food, clothing, and shelter, and therefore, though confronting great national affairs in the future, you cannot be active; but that is what one calls your weak will and faint heart. Merely gnashing your teeth, wringing your wrists, and drowning in tears of righteous indignation will yield not an inch of effect for society. Since you are a person with enough ability to become a Nagon, no matter what enterprise you undertake having descended to the private sphere, you will surely gain a fair profit and also succeed. For a person, the single character of decisive action is vital. Even birds flying through the empty sky make no savings whatsoever, yet the gods of Heaven and Earth nourish them safely, do they not? Why do you not quit the cramped and unpleasant life of a lowly official, and righteously and freely carry out some enterprise? Activity will surely generate food, clothing, and shelter. Why suffer and need to stubbornly cling to your precious life for the sake of government expense?'

Oto: 'Your explanation is plausible enough for once, but the sad thing for us is that, gnawing at our parents' knees, we crossed the routine educational path of primary school, middle school, and university, spent our days solely in a school-window life, remained unacquainted with the general conditions of society, and have never experienced hardship; now we are like worn-out riding horses, and once we leave the cold official post, there is not a single job with which we can stand and work in society. We are nothing more than helpless nuisances with nowhere else to go, perhaps becoming newspaper reporters, or like those overgrown pettifoggers.'
