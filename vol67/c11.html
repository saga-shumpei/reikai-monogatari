Chapter 11: Darkness Fox 苦 [1713]

暗狐苦〔1713〕

§ 1

To the southwest of the Kaugen plateau lies a realm named the Tarahan country, boasting a population of twenty thousand, with fertile soil and abundant products. The sovereign is called King Karapin, who erected a magnificent castle in the city of Tarahan, situated at the center of the country, and upholding the Ural religion, continued for over a dozen generations. The name of the king at that time was King Karapin, and his queen consort was called Princess Mondoru. Between the two of them, they had raised two children, the Crown Prince Sudarman and the Princess Banna. The Queen, Princess Mondoru, was possessed by the spirit of a silver-furred, eight-tailed evil fox, took on a cruel nature, constantly ripping open the bellies of pregnant women, gouging out their fetuses, roasting them whole, and smacking her lips in delight. The voices of the people's lamentation and resentment filled the realm, creating a situation where an uprising might break out at any moment. Nevertheless, King Karapin was so infatuated with the Queen's beauty that he was completely besotted, readily approving any unreasonable demand whatsoever if it was the Queen's word. Shakanna, the Minister of the Left, grieved that the hearts of the people were day by day drifting away from the state, and concerned by the situation where the fires of revolution might ignite at any moment, constantly resolved to die as he remonstrated directly with the King and Queen. Yet the King did not heed the words of the Minister of the Left in the least, and eventually, upon seeing the Minister of the Left arrive at the castle, he would hide deep within the inner chambers to avoid meeting him. Gandī, the Minister of the Right, was an unscrupulous fool who constantly instigated the Queen, pushed aside the Minister of the Left, and aspired to take his place, attain the post of Minister of the Left, and seize the sovereignty of the Tarahan country into his own hands. Through the internal backing of the Minister of the Right, Gandī, the Queen's wicked and lawless acts added increasingly to their cruelty, the hearts of the people became ever more alienated, and peasant uprisings began to erupt in various places.

On a certain occasion, Princess Mondoru, accompanied by her favorite retainer, the Minister of the Right Gandī, along with his wife Anchī and over a dozen maids-in-waiting, attempted an excursion to explore the scenery of Mount Karumon. From out of nowhere, a white-feathered arrow flew and struck Princess Mondoru squarely on the forehead; she uttered a shrieking cry, tumbled down the valley floor, and finally breathed her last. When this news spread far and wide, the people were in a mood of secretly raising large cups to celebrate the light of the country's future.

King Karapin found the attachment of his heart toward the Queen impossible to cast off, and eventually grew like one deranged, putting his close retainers to the sword, and—just like the Queen—once again began ripping open pregnant women, roasting fetuses whole, and smacking his lips. Shakanna, the Minister of the Left, determined to die for the grave crisis of the imperial house and the nation, advanced deep into the royal palace together with his wife, Princess Harisuta, pressed the King to reform his heart, and explained that the voices of the people's lamentation were clamorous and an insurrection might break out at any moment. Having lost his most beloved queen, King Karapin's heart was utterly hardened, and he could by no means bring himself to listen to the loyal remonstrance of the Minister of the Left. Suddenly drawing his great sword with a fierce look upon his face, he spoke to the Minister of the Left:

King: 'The untimely death of Princess Mondoru must certainly be the deed of your subordinates. It is vengeance for the Queen, prepare to meet your doom. I shall cut you down.'

Saying this, he attempted to slash at the Minister of the Left like the Asura King. Princess Harisuta, the wife of the Minister of the Left, planted herself right between the King and the Minister of the Left, and pleaded with tears dripping from both eyes:

Princess Harisuta: 'If I may speak with all reverence to Your Majesty, to slay a loyal vassal is the same as cutting off your own arm. Without the pillars of the state, how can the Tarahan country be maintained? Please compose your mind first and consider this; if the Minister of the Left must be killed no matter what, then please set me up as his substitute.'

The King spoke with his hair standing on end and piercing the heavens:

King: 'Bah, the meddling of a clever woman, I have no ear to listen. If you want to be killed, I will kill you. Not only you, Shakanna too shall rust my blade together with you. Prepare to meet your doom.'

So saying, with a sharp, fine sword, he struck Princess Harisuta down on the spot with a single slash diagonally across from her left shoulder to her right flank. Next, he pursued the Minister of the Left like the Asura King, intending to strike him dead. The Minister of the Left fled for his life out of the back gate like clouds and mist, carrying upon his back young Subaru, who had turned six years old that year, and concealed himself somewhere unknown. Gandī, the Minister of the Right, became the Minister of the Left, and spent triumphant days together with Arina, his only son born of his wife Anchī. He then promoted Sakurensu, the steward of the House of the Minister of the Right, and appointed him to the post of Minister of the Right.

原文（近代文語）

デカタン高原の西南方に当つてタラハン国と云ふ、人口二十万を有する地味の肥た産物の豊かな国土がある。国王はカラピン王と云ひ、国の中心地点なるタラハン市に宏大なる城廓を構へ、ウラル教を信じて十数代を継続した。その時の国王の名をカラピン王と云ひ王妃をモンドル姫と云ふ。二人の中には太子スダルマン、及び王女バンナの二子を挙げて居た。王妃のモンドル姫は銀毛八尾の悪狐の霊に憑依され、残忍性を帯び、常に妊婦の腹を裂き、胎児を抉り出して、丸焚きとなし舌皷を打つてゐた。国民怨嗟の声は国内に充ち溢れ、何時騒動の起るやも知れざる形勢となつて来た。然し乍らカラピン王は王妃の容色に恋着し、王妃の言ならば、如何なる無理難題も二つ返事で承認すると云ふ、惚け方である。左守の司のシャカンナは民心日に月に国家を離るるを憂ひ、且つ何時革命の狼火のあがるや知れざる形勢を憂慮し、常に死を決して王及び王妃に直諫した。されども王は少しも左守の言を用ひず、遂には左守の登城するを見るや、奥殿深く身を忍んで面会を避けた。右守の司ガンヂーは心よからぬ痴者にて常に王妃を煽動し、左守を退け、自分が、とつて代つて左守の職につき、タラハン国の主権を吾手に握らむ事を希求してゐた。右守のガンヂーが内面的応援によつて、王妃の悪逆無道の行為は益々残虐の度を加へ、民心益々離反して所々に百姓一揆が勃発して来た。

或時モンドル姫は寵臣の右守ガンヂー及その妻アンチーと共に十数人の侍女を伴ひ、カルモン山の風景を探るべく遊覧を試みた。何処ともなく白羽の矢が飛んで来てモンドル姫の額に命中し、姫は悲鳴を挙げて谷底に転倒し、遂に絶命して了つた。この事四方に喧伝するや、国民は窃に大杯をあげて国家の前途の光明を祝すると云ふ勢であつた。

カラピン王は王妃に対する愛着の念去り難く、遂には狂乱の如くなり、近臣を手討になし、王妃の如く又もや妊婦を裂き胎児を丸焚きにして舌皷を打つやうになつて来た。左守の司のシャカンナは王家及国家の一大事と死を決して、妻のハリスタ姫と共に王宮深く進み入り、王に改心を迫り、且つ国民の怨嗟の声喧しく、いつ擾乱の勃発するやも知れぬ事を説明した。最愛の王妃を失ひ、心の荒びきつたるカラピン王は到底忠誠なる左守の諫言を耳にするに至らなかつた。忽ち大刀を引抜いて形相凄じく左守に向つて云ふ、

王『モンドル姫の横死は必ず汝が手下の処為ならむ。王妃の仇だ、観念せよ。手討にして呉れむ』

と阿修羅王の如く左守に斬りつけむとした。左守の妻ハリスタ姫は王と左守の間に立ち塞がつて、

『恐れ乍ら王様に申上げます。忠臣をお斬りになるのは御自分の片腕をお斬り遊ばすも同様で厶います。国家の柱石なくして、どうしてタラハン国が保てませうか。まづまづ心静かにお考へ下さいませ、もし左守の司を、どうしても殺さねばならぬのならば、どうか私を身代りに立てて下さい』

と涙を両眼に滴らし乍ら陳弁した。王は怒髪天を衝いて云ふ、

王『エー、さかしき女の差出口、聞く耳もたぬ。殺して呉れなら殺してやる。汝のみならず、シャカンナも共に刀の錆だ。観念せよ』

と云ひ乍らハリスタ姫の左の肩から右の脇へ袈裟掛に、切れ味のよい銘刀にてスパリと其場に斬り倒した。次いで左守を打殺さむと阿修羅王の如くに追掛ける。左守は一生懸命に裏門より雲を霞と逃げ出し、当年六才になつたスバール嬢を背に負ひ、何処ともなく身を隠した。右守のガンヂーは左守となり、妻アンチーの仲に生れた一人息子のアリナと共に得意な日月を送つて居た。さうして右守家の家令サクレンスを抜擢して右守に任じた。

§ 2

The new Samori, Gandhi, obtained the position of Samori and advocated for administrative reform, confiscating the immense wealth inherited from the former Samori's household, bestowing charity upon the impoverished within the nation, and worrying solely about raising his own reputation; thus, gradually, the country of Tarahan attained a measure of calm.

King Karapin entrusted all matters of state to Gandhi the Samori, while he himself turned his mind to the ceremony of tea, haikai poetry, and the like, making a life of refined indulgence his sole pursuit.

Prince Sudarman of King Karapin welcomed his eighteenth spring, and Princess Banna welcomed her sixteenth spring.

Prince Sudarman shut himself deep within the palace, feeling somehow mentally depressed and unable to take joy in anything; needless to say regarding his father's words, even toward the Samori, the Umori, and other senior retainers, whenever they begged an audience he wore an unpleasant expression, uttered not a single word, and, gloomy and despondent, confined himself to his study.

The anxiety of King Karapin to start with, as well as the senior retainers such as the Samori and Umori, was beyond measure.

Day and night they prayed to the gods and buddhas, or strummed delightful instruments to console the Prince, and as a result of consultations to cure his melancholia, they summoned the beauties of the land and made them attend upon the Prince's palace.

The hundred-odd graceful and slender beauties were like brilliantly blooming cherry blossoms or butterflies, and their beauty was as if nothing could compare to it.

However, the Prince did not cast even a single glance at these beauties, and merely shut himself up yet more deeply in the inner palace, sinking further into melancholy.

The only one pleasing to the Prince was Alina, the son of the Samori, and him alone.

Therefore, Alina was constantly summoned by the Prince to be a companion in conversation, and from time to time they strolled about within the castle, comforting the Prince's heart.

That which most comforted the Prince's heart was drawing pictures together with Alina.

Both the Prince and Alina daily played with paintbrushes, and when drawing figures and the like, they improved almost to the point of equaling reality.

At one time the Prince turned toward Alina and said:

Sudarman: 'Hey, Alina, how is it? Today, why don't you and I go somewhere and do some sketching?'

Sudarman: 'Within this narrow castle, the subjects for sketching have already run out.'

Hinting at a rare desire to go out, Alina resolved:—'This opportunity must not be missed; while His Highness's intent does not change, if I accompany the Prince and let him amuse himself with sketching the mountain and river scenery he loves, perhaps his daily melancholy will be healed.'

Alina: 'For the royal house and for the nation, there is nothing quite so wonderful as this.'

Propping his hands on the floor and letting a smile brim across his face, Alina spoke:

Alina: 'Your Highness, it is an invitation I could not even wish for; if you would allow me to accompany you, it would be my supreme honor.'

Alina: 'Scenery of mountain streams where the waters are blue and clear and spray is scattered gives one the feeling of having tasted boundless coolness in one's breast.'

Alina: 'In that case, I shall report this to my father the Samori and have him make preparations for our escort.'

Alina: 'Even if we call it a secret journey, since Your Highness is the prince of a country, an escort of twenty or thirty men would surely be necessary to preserve your dignity.'

Shaking his head from side to side with a very displeased look, the Prince said:

Sudarman: 'In this castle, there is no one who pleases me more than you alone.'

Sudarman: 'Aside from that, I hate taking even a single person as an attendant.'

Sudarman: 'If you are going to make such a grand affair of it, I will stop my walk to the suburbs altogether.'

Sudarman: 'My illness arose because I grew to detest such a stifling palace life.'

Sudarman: 'Like ordinary commoners, I want to take a walk with you just the two of us, very casually.'

Alina: 'If you say so, then there is no help for it.'

Alina: 'However, to have secretly taken Your Highness out to the suburbs—though I do not know how great the anger of the King, to begin with, and my own father will be, I care not even if I are disowned by my parents for Your Highness's sake.'

Alina: 'If Your Highness's heart is put at ease even for half a day, I am satisfied with that.'

Alina: 'If so, tomorrow at daybreak let us secretly slip out from the back gate and accompany you on a half-day of clear amusement.'

Sudarman: 'Ah, with that I am satisfied.'

Sudarman: 'My illness will probably be completely cured, too.'

Sudarman: 'Sick of aristocratic life, I also want to see the actual conditions of commoners working in the mountains and fields, and taste heavenly blessings amidst natural scenery.'

Sudarman: 'Then Alina, I am counting on you for certain.'

Alina: 'Yes, I obey with reverence.'

Alina: 'Then I shall make all the preparations.'

Feeling as though a starving demon of hell had been saved in paradise, the Prince awaited the dawn of the next day with feelings as though a single day were a thousand autumns.

原文（近代文語）

新左守のガンヂーは左守の地位を得て国政改革を標榜し、前左守家伝来の巨万の富を没収し、国内の貧民に慈善を施し、吾声名のあがらむ事にのみ焦慮し、漸くタラハン国は小康を得た。カラピン王は一切の政務を左守のガンヂーに一任し、自分は茶の湯、俳諧などに心を傾け風流三昧を事として居た。

カラピン王の太子スダルマンは十八才の春を迎へ王女バンナは十六才の春を迎へた。太子のスダルマンは宮中深く閉ぢ籠もり、何となく精神憂鬱として楽まず、父の言葉は云ふも更なり、左守右守、その他重臣に対しても、拝謁を乞ふ度毎に面白からぬ面を現はし、只一口の言葉も発せず鬱々として書斎に籠つてゐた。カラピン王を初め左守右守の重臣連の憂慮は一方でなかつた。日夜神仏を念じ、又は面白き楽器を弾きならして太子を慰め、憂鬱病を治さむと、相談の結果、国内の美人を召集し太子の御殿に侍らしめた。百余名の嬋妍窈窕たる美人は燦爛と咲き乱れたる桜花の如く、蝶の如く其美はしさ、譬ふるに物なきが如くであつた。されど太子は之等の美人に対し一瞥もくれず、益々奥殿に閉ぢ籠り深く憂鬱に陥るのみであつた。只太子の気に入るのは左守の悴アリナ一人のみである。それ故アリナは常に太子に召されて話相手となり、時々城内を逍遥し、太子の心を慰めて居た。

太子の最も心を慰むるものはアリナと共に絵を描く事であつた。太子もアリナも日々絵筆を弄び、人物等を描く時は殆ど実物に等しきまで上達した。或時太子はアリナに向ひ、

太『オイ、アリナ、どうだ今日はお前と何処かへ行つて写生でもやらうぢやないか。狭い城内では、もはや写生の種もつきて了つたから』

とツヒにない外出の意を、ほのめかしたので、アリナは…此機逸すべからず、御意のかはらぬ内、太子のお伴をなし、太子のお好きな山水の写生でも遊ばしたら、日頃の憂鬱症が癒るかも知れぬ。王家に対し、国家に対し、これ位、結構な事はない…と決心し、両手を支て、満面に笑を湛へ乍ら、

ア『太子様、願ふてもなき御催しで厶います。どうか私もお伴さして頂けば無上の光栄で厶います。山青く水清く飛沫をとばす谷川の景色等は胸に万斛の涼味を味はつたやうな気が致しますよ。さすれば父の左守に申伝へましてお伴の用意を致させませう。何程微行と申しても一国の太子様、二三十人の護衛は威厳を保つ上に必要で厶いませうから』

太子は頭を左右に振り乍ら、さも不機嫌な顔にて、

『此城中に於てお前一人より、余の気に入るものはない。その外に只一人たりとも召使をつれる事は嫌だ。そんな大層な事をするなら、もう郊外の散歩は止めにする。余の病気は、かやうな窮屈な殿中生活が嫌になつて、其為め起つたのだ。普通人民の如く、極手軽にお前と二人散歩して見たいのだ』

ア『左様仰せられますれば是非は厶いませぬ。併し乍ら太子様を窃に郊外にお連れ申したとあつては王様を初め吾父の怒りは、いか許りか分りませぬが、私は太子様の為に、仮令親に勘当を受けても構ひませぬ。半時でも太子様のお心が安まればそれで満足で厶います。然らば明日払暁裏門より窃に脱出し、半日の御清遊にお伴を致しませう』

太『ア、それで満足した。余の病気も全快するだらう。貴族生活に飽き果てた余は庶民の山野に働く実況も見たいし、自然の風物に対し、天恵を味ひ度い。それではアリナ、屹度頼むぞ』

ア『ハイ、畏まつて厶います。それでは一切の用意を致しておきます』

太子は地獄の餓鬼が天国に救はれたやうな心持になつて翌日の未明を一時千秋の思ひで待つてゐた。
