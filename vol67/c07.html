Chapter 7: 武力鞘 [1709]

武力鞘〔1709〕

§ 1

Yorikohime stands upon the kanban, gazing out over the peaceful komen, and sings with a clear voice.

High into the hisakata ōzora soars / Mount Ōra, hazy in the mist / The darkness of tokoyo becomes the spring lake / The sound of striking sazanami is pure / Beating its drums against our funabata / A lake of ripples, a thousand waves and ten thousand waves / Upon the extending Namikirimaru / Blossoms blooming higher than ten / While listening to the sacred minori / We advance toward the distant shore / Yorikohime, whose heart was once clouded / Her inner darkness swept away by the tokitsukaze / Dispersing the murakumo / Over the crystal-clear expanse of the lake / Kotori sing of chiyo / Spreading their wings, over Amboina / Fluttering back and forth above the divine gleaming zujiyō / Zengosaiu / As if guarding our yukute / Such a sight is charming upon the waves.

Large and small shimajima / Nourishing the shade of pine trees / Drifting here and anata / Today's journey with a pure view / Suddenly comes a yoarashi / Its fury lifts the ship into chiuten / Escaping the perils of anshō / Preserving the fragile thread of my life / Serving the Izumo Shinshikyō / The grace of Lord Uaikō / Manifesting the divine awe right before my eyes / How venerable it is / The ship sails on, the waves stand in quiet rows / The scales of fish shimmer gently / Lodging the rays of the sun in heaven / Singing of eternal peace in the world / Guided by the gentle words of our peerless master / Leaving behind the land of roots.

To the august country where the eternally blooming gods of heaven / Reside in deep peace / To proceed thither—the feeling thereof / Born into this world in my present body / The tears of unprecedented joy / Have moistened my sleeves / Ah, Kannagara Kamigami / This ship guarded by the gods / Is crossing to the port of Suga / Truly pure and refreshed is my heart / There is nothing to which it may be compared / In the full bloom of spring at twice eight / The storm of the heart blew furiously / Hand in hand with unrighteous men / Plotting evil and ugly deeds / The fortress of Mount Ōra / Fixed as a dwelling for chiyo / Ah, the regret of piling up sins / The grace of the gods is not shallow / My body's fortune not yet exhausted.

To advance and enter such a wondrous divine teaching / I revere it as the grace of the gods of heaven and earth / Morning and evening without neglect / Giving thanks for their virtue / The sky of my chest cleared wide / The great sun shines brightly across / The moonlight is clear and serene / The twinkling of the stars is most wondrous / The wind plays continuous music / Revealing the deep divine majesty of the gods / As it happens, the Great Takashima / By what divine contrivance / Was it said to be thrust deep at the roots of the lower rocks? / But before long, without any hardship / It vanished like bubbles leaving no trace / And upon its summit, eternally / Standing side by side, the husband-and-wife rocks.

Suddenly changing their bodies into beasts / Breaking away from the high rock seats / Like sheep being dragged to the slaughterhouse / Pitiful figures trudging along / Making their way through the crevices of the rocks / Tearing their bodies on sarutoriibara / Or tumbling and falling once more / With their heads down and tails up / At last descending to the rocky shore / Crossing the waves and escaping out of sight / Ah, Kannagara Kamigami / To forever deceive the gods upon Mount Ōra / To cheat and distress the people of the world / To extend my might in all directions / Thus I had thought to do / Reflecting upon it now, I realize / What a foolish enterprise it was / From the distant age of the gods / Everlasting and firm, of this lake / To become the light and to become the flower.

Revered as the lord of the lake / Becoming the target of the longing of all people / The rock island that flourished far and wide / When the divine time suddenly arrives / Has thus vanished so mercilessly / Thinking upon this, the body of man / Must be all the more ephemeral / Though the might of Ōkuninushi possesses power / That pierces heaven and earth / Should the divine admonition descend / Like frost melting in the morning sun / Like summer ice thawing / It will vanish fleetingly, Kannagara / How fearsome is the power of the gods / The governor of Seegō has fortunately / Advanced and entered the great path of the gods / Overcoming the barrier of crookedness / Now leading to the floral garden of Takama / It has become a journey of communion / My dear sister Hanakahime.

Escorted by the lord of the teachings / Crossing the stormy billows of a thousand leagues / Trekking across the vast plains of ten thousand leagues / For the sake of the gods, for the sake of the world / I have become one who dedicates my body / Both I and my sister, Kannagara / Saved by the venerable gods / To the divine country where the morning sun strikes straight ahead / How joyous it is to advance with a brave heart / Ah, Kannagara Kamigami / Revering in the presence of the gods / Praying that there be good fortune in our future / Facing heaven and earth, we offer our prayers / With awe and reverence, we humbly pray.

Hanakahime: 'The wings of the birds flying across the surface of the lake, I mistake for flowers or butterflies. Seeing Ōtakajima sink to the bottom of the lake without even a sound, I perceive the changing of the world. In the changing Miyo, I await the spring Hanaka that flourishes with the wide-spreading tips of a fan.'

Shīgō: 'The Shimajima float upon the waves in Taizenjijiyaku, yet how pitiful and fleeting is Ōtakajima. The Ihajima that troubled the passing ships have also vanished helplessly, becoming Minawa. The evil that nested in my heart has also vanished like Minawa upon seeing the pathos of Ihajima. I think of this ship crossing the center of Ametsuchi as the Mihashi of the salvation of Kami. The Shimayama that illuminated the darkness as I went to Tokoyo has now sunk to the Nesoko. It is the way of the world to fall from the high to the low, and I too have descended from the mountain of Aura. This ship, rowing across the Suiheisen, must be the Misugata of Sumeōkami. The figure of Namikirimaru, gliding over the Suiheisen in Kamunagara, is valiant.'

Umekō: 'When I look out, the Mihikari glittering between the waves must be the Mikage shattering into Chiji. The waves of the Shikai have calmed down most peacefully, and there is not even a shadow of Ōtakayama. Ōtakayama floated high in the Kumoma above the waves, troubling the people. It is joyful that neither the sun nor the moon are obscured by the peak of Ōtakayama, and there is no suffering.'

Umekō, Yorikohime, Hanakahime, and Shīgō descended the stairs, entered their respective cabins, and lay down with their elbows as pillows. Various idle chatter began on the deck. A large man with round, dark eyes and a look of having some peculiarity sat cross-legged among the dozen or so passengers, boasting of his martial arts in an overbearing manner.

Barack: 'Excuse me, you appear to be a man of great valor at first glance; are you a sumo wrestler, or perhaps a martial artist?'

Drack: 'Me? When I was young, I did quite a bit of sumo. And I once held the title of Yokozuna, the champion of the land. It was quite interesting during the grand sumo tournament in the capital of Haruna. I did not touch the ground for ten days of matches, and I was immensely popular. To say that I made the blood of tens of thousands of spectators dance was a reputation unprecedented in history. You must have heard of it; the champion Drack-yama is me. Look at this; my arms are like iron. No matter how strong a man is, once I grip him tight, his breath is stifled, his chest is constricted, and he turns blue. And then he cannot speak. Because my strength is so great, no wrestler could stand a chance against Drack-yama, so in the end, I had no opponents left. Since I could not wrestle alone without an opponent, I had no choice but to retire from sumo, and now I have become a master of both kendo and jujutsu.'

Bara: 'Indeed, those are arms that look truly strong. However, since you are so strong, why did you not capture the pirate leader Coz when he attacked? Is that not what one calls a waste of talent?'

Drack: 'At that time, I was having a pleasant dream in my own cabin, so I did not know a thing about it. I am a man whose arms itch, whose blood boils, and who is always longing for an opponent; if I had heard that the pirate leader was attacking, how could I have let him escape? Later, when I truly heard the rumors from others, I regretted it in my heart, thinking it was an irreparable loss for generations to come.'

Bara: 'If I am on the same ship as a man of such valor as you, I too shall feel at ease on this voyage. I cannot help but be grateful for this as a blessing from Kami.'

Drack: 'Hmm, you need not worry about anything. As for swordsmanship, there is probably no one in the world today who can beat me. In sumo, even if Raiden Tameemon, Onogawa, Tanikaze, Umegatani, and Hitachiyama were all bundled together, they would be no match for me at all. And when it comes to kendo or jujutsu, even if Goliath, Miyamoto Musashi, Tsukahara Bokuden, Nomi no Sukune, Hanawa Dan'emon, Iwami Jūtarō, and Araki Mataemon were bundled together, they could not even approach my feet, so it is quite a feat. However, it is also lonely to be too strong and have no opponents. I want to clash with a strong opponent somehow, but to tell you the truth, a mere pirate leader does not even provide a challenge.'

§ 3

Barack is struck dumb with astonishment, making a perplexed face and clicking his tongue in wonder. The great crowd of passengers take Dorak's tall tales completely seriously, puffing up their chests, taking a keen interest in the tales of supreme valor, inching their knees closer tick by tick, and before anyone knows it, they surround Dorak, gathering around him like a Western chrysanthemum flower made of seashell art.

A merchant-like man named Check asks timidly toward Dorak,

Check: 'Pardon me, Sensei, if you are a person of such immense strength, there can surely be not a single thing in the world to be feared.'

Dorak: 'That is just so. Whether it is bow and arrow, rifle, cannon, or anything at all, it is useless against me. If I were to give this mast a single mighty whack with this fist, it would snap cleanly right from the base. That is why it is said that I have no enemy under heaven. Well, you folks can rest easy too. As long as I am riding this ship, even if a thousand or ten thousand pirates come, a single fart will blow them all away. For even upon hearing the name of Dorak, they shrink up in terror.'

Check: 'Oh my, how fortunate we must be. To be on the same ship with such a strong and masterly martial artist guest, it is truly through the guidance of our ancestors. We shall return to our homeland in peace. Truly, you are a person like a living deity. However, when the leader of Kozu came up to this ship last night, a delicate woman appeared from out of the dimness, threw all the terrifying pirates into the lake, and helped us take back our clothing—that was truly a thankful matter. Might that person be one of your disciples?'

Dorak: 'Hmph, all those fellows who have gained even a little bit of skill have received my training. Since they are such an overwhelming number of disciples, I do not remember all their faces and names, but it would be no problem to say that the martial artists of the seven thousand and odd countries of the Moon Kingdom are all my subordinates. After all, the arrest squads of every regulatory office studied swordsmanship and jujutsu entirely under me. And as for who that woman is, you people surely know, do you not? Did you hear her name?'

Barack: 'The general rumor is that some goddess who suddenly descended from heaven must have saved us from our peril. No matter how accomplished one is in martial arts, as long as one is human, a woman by her very nature could not perform such an extraordinary feat.'

Check: 'Even so, Barack-sama, after a little while, a woman with the exact same style as the beauty who appeared in the darkness came up onto the deck, calling herself Yoshikohime or something, and was chanting as if she herself had rescued us.'

Barack: 'Bah, this is an age overflowing with fellows who want to snatch away other people's achievements, so it was a cunning trick to say such a thing and try to win our trust. The fellows in the world today are nothing more than creatures who utter splendid and formidable things with their mouths, but once it comes to Kamakura, their hands and feet rattle, their hearts thud, their lips tremble, and they turn into jelly-spined cowards scurrying about in flight. At any rate, it is an era when big talk and boasting are all the rage. And today is different from ancient times... Even though peaches and plums do not speak, they naturally create a path below... no one is pondering such tedious matters anymore. It is an era that heavily practices self-advertising, so unless we witness your skills with our own eyes, nobody can possibly trust you, ha ha ha ha!'

Dorak: 'Now now, Barack-sama, how could you utter such slander right in front of me? You must think that what I said just a moment ago was mere big talk and boasting, do you not? Since we uphold the unity of word, mind, and deed, we never tell lies. If you think it is a lie, lend me that arm for a moment, and I shall show you by gripping it.'

Barack: 'Oh dear, I am quite humbled. It is by no means that I do not trust you, honorable sir. An honorable sir like you, whose word, mind, and deed are one, is truly admirable. Today's world is entirely filled with people whose mouths and minds are completely opposed, so if it were at least the unity of word and mind, that would be one thing, but it is a wicked society where deceit and arrogance are rampant.'
