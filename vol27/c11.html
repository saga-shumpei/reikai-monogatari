Chapter 11: 茶目式 [793]

茶目式〔793〕

§ 1

Following Kotoyoriwake, they climbed up Mount Haris; Kuniyoriwake, on the way, seeing the Taiheigaki bearing heavy and beautiful fruits with drooping branches across the valley, suddenly had his appetite burst forth, and to distract his hunger, laid out plantain leaves as a seating mat by the roadside. Sitting in leisurely meditation just like Mirokuniyorai, placing his left hand lightly upon his thigh while clenching the fist of his right hand, thrusting out his index finger, he silently pointed at the Taiheigaki in the bottom of the valley. Following that, he repeatedly pointed at his own mouth and belly, as if saying: If there be any skilled tree-climbers among Charles, Base, or the others, let them cross the valley, climb up, and offer one or two of those sweet-looking persimmons to me. Though he spoke no word, by his gestures he eagerly showed such a comical sight. The Taiheigaki made the prey of the dragon god of Mount Haris—if people, rousing wild ambition, should take even a single one, it is rumored to be a most perilous fruit whose touch instantly causes the abdomen to swell with divine wrath, finally giving birth to snake pups and ending one's life. The appetite of Kuniyoriwake arose so fiercely that, unable to endure it even for a second, he urged Tsunekusai to have them plucked for him. Tsunekusai, feeling uneasy yet forced by necessity, commanded his attendants, Charles and Base, paying heed to fulfill the demand of Kuniyoriwake.

Charles and Base, having no way to refuse the oracle of the living god, descended by vines, went down the cliff into the valley, hopped and skipped across the rock heads floating amidst the raging current to reach the opposite side, and at last crossing over, embraced the root of the persimmon tree and looked up at the sky, where a sweet fragrance came wafting to pierce their noses. Instantly the two resolved in their minds, climbed up like monkeys, and like silkworms devouring mulberry leaves, plucked the red ripe persimmons from their little mouths, opening their big mouths without a word to stuff them in—oh, the comical sight of it! From the excessive sweetness, both forgot their own duties, plucked them with all their might, and blindly crammed them into their mouths, whereupon their bellies swelled up like bulging bags, and panting with a swish-swash breath, they turned into bizarre figures resembling a blowfish climbing a tree.

Kuniyoriwake looked up and cried out for them to drop even just one sweet fellow down to him, but the two turned a deaf ear and ate on regardless of their lives. Kuniyoriwake thought to himself: The teachings of the God of Anagama say, never rely upon others; the affairs of one's own body must be done by oneself. So thinking, he stood up from his plantain-leaf seat, descended the precipice like a monkey using vines for strength down to the bottom of the valley, and reaching the opposite bank, embraced the great persimmon tree, climbed up to the first branch, and while resting his breath there, looked down to find it immensely high, with the water spray of the raging current flying and the roar of the water terrifyingly majestic, enough to knock the very soul out of one. The two overhead flew to and fro like left and right monkeys, eating on recklessly until their guts and internal organs were about to split. Kuniyoriwake, unable to bear it any longer, thinking that unless he climbed to a perilous place he could not eat sweet ripe persimmons, caught hold of a slender branch at the risk of his life and gradually plucked a single ripe persimmon; when he took it into his mouth thinking it an incomparable delicacy, instantly his swelling belly made his breathing gradually painful.

When he looked down at the root of the persimmon tree, a great number of large snakes bearing tortoise-shell patterns came climbing up with glaring eyes, their appearance so terrifying that advancement and retreat were here blocked. Kuniyoriwake made up his mind, leaving his fortune to heaven, transcending beyond life and death, and aimed for the blue abyss of the roaring waterfall spray as a death-defying feat; the moment it was seen as splashing water spray rising, the form of Kuniyoriwake vanished like foam. Charles and Base, seeing the snakes climbing up, trembled with fear and leaped toward the blue abyss where the precious life of Kuniyoriwake had plunged, whereupon their forms likewise vanished.

Tsunekusai and four or five native attendants who had been gazing upon this state of affairs with their own eyes were astonished and flustered, crying out in a hubbub as they ran along the valley stream, shouting whither had the three figures gone, running back and forth in frenzy as could not be helped. The sound of the wind crossing the valley blew fiercely and terribly. Kotoyoriwake and Wakahiko, knowing nothing of such matters, sang the propagation hymn of Anagama with cool voices, quickening their steps along the valley as they advanced deep into the mountains.

§ 2

Tsunekusu could not but hesitate here. He had to participate in the vital Goshingefu, and yet unless he helped Kuniyoriwake and the others, the way of Ningen could not be upheld...

Tsunekusu: 'Ah, what should I do? Must I abandon this Goshingefu, which comes but once in an age, yet still save Kuniyoriwake and the rest? That being said, the life of Kuniyoriwake is also singular. If I dawdle, it will become an irretrievable matter. If I devote myself to that side, I cannot save this side. If I would save this side, I must abandon the precious Goshingefu. The command of Kamisama is most weighty, and human life is also truly precious.'

Fretting whether to do this or that, for a while he paced back and forth over a distance of four or five ken, bewildered as to what measure to take.

Tsunekusu: 'Ah, if I dilly-dally, on the other side the breath of life will cease entirely. As for the Goshingefu, it is not as though it cannot be fulfilled if delayed by half an hour or an hour. Oh, that is so. Saving Kuniyoriwake is surely the right thing.'

Murmuring thus to himself, he sought the direction whence the voices of the dojin came, traced the mountain stream, parted the brushwood, and descended.

At a point four or five cho downstream, four or five attendants raised their voices to the utmost,

Attendants: 'Look there! Look there!'

they clamored. When he looked, there were three black shadows, now sinking, now floating, being swept down by the fierce current. In any case, both banks were like sheer walls, and it was impossible to approach easily. Tsunekusu raised a loud voice,

Tsunekusu: 'To the downstream! To the downstream!'

crying out thus, he dashed off at breakneck speed toward the downstream for about ten cho.

Here the mountain stream spread out somewhat wider, the water became considerably shallower, the flow also grew milder, and even the small pebbles at the riverbed were clearly visible. Tsunekusu and the four or five attendants splashed straight into the river, and waiting by forming a horizontal ladder formation to pick up the bodies of the three persons drifting down.

The one who finally drifted ashore was Kuniyoriwake, and following him, the other two also safely drifted to this place. Dividing the task of handling the physical body of each person between two men, they hauled them onto the bank, made them vomit water, and after applying various forms of artificial respiration, Tsunekusu, raising his hoarse elderly voice, repeatedly and endlessly chanted the Hankonka. Kuniyoriwake at last began to move his hands and feet. Tsunekusu's face suddenly began to shine. Chanting the verses of the Hankonka once more toward the two persons, at last the two also revived. The joy of everyone was beyond all comparison.

By the command of Tsunekusu, Kuniyoriwake and the others were sent to the hollow of a zelkova tree in the natural hotel and left in the care of the dojin, while Tsunekusu, thinking that delay would mean a great crisis, dragging his tired aged steps and taking the tara-wood staff for his strength, climbed up once more toward the valley of Mount Harris.

Kuniyoriwake, Charles, and Base finally recovered their vigor. However, perhaps as divine punishment for eating the persimmon of the dragon god, their bellies gradually swelled up until they looked like women in their final month of pregnancy. Charles and Base, rolling about with their whole bodies curled up round like balls, writhed and suffered,

Charles: 'I say, Kuniyoriwake Kamisama, please do something about this.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Now wait. I must heal my own belly first.'

Charles: 'Originally it was because of you that we met with such a plight, so you must help us or we are done for. Somehow inside my belly it feels as though the babies of a giant snake are wriggling about, and it is so agonizing I cannot endure it, oh. The moment the infant giant snakes are born, we will die on the spot without distinction of sex, so they say. Since it hurts this much, it would be better to die, but even dying is useless. At home my wife and children remain. If you do not somehow save us quickly, it has grown gradually more painful.'
