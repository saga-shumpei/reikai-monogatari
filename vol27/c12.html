Chapter 12: Lake 上の Monster 物 [794]

湖上の怪物〔794〕

§ 1

Kotoyoriwake, together with Wakahiko, unknown even in dreams that such a strange event had befallen Kuniyoriwake on the way, chanted the divine words with a mind absolute and undisturbed, and gradually arrived at the ravines where the rocks of Senzeujiki lined up here and there; casting his eyes abroad, he beheld an azure abyss spreading boundlessly into the valley between the mountains, where, though there was no wind, high waves rose and surged.

At a single glance, one felt as though assailed by truly dismal and ghastly spirits.

Kotoyoriwake looked back behind him,

Kotoyoriwake: 'Wakahiko-sama, is this not the dwelling place of the dragon god who possesses the precious jewels of Ryu and Kyu?'

Wakahiko: 'Yes, indeed it is so. Today the waves are raging terribly. I cannot help feeling anxious, wondering if perhaps on the way Kuniyoriwake and Tsunekusu did something that failed to accord with the divine will. ......Look, please look over there. Have not the waves grown more and more violent in this windless zone? Look, look, waves like mountains are beginning to rise.'

Kotoyoriwake: 'Indeed, the mood of this lake water is quite different. Judging even from the manner in which these waves rise, it is clear that the noble dragon god lies in concealment. However, what can have happened to Kuniyoriwake, Tsunekusu, and the other persons? Are they not exceedingly late?'

Wakahiko: 'On the way, the Taihei persimmons, which are said to be guarded by the dragon god, were bearing fruit with their branches sagging heavily, and I am filled with anxious foreboding that perhaps Kuniyoriwake-sama plucked and ate those very persimmons, thereby drawing the wrath of the dragon god upon himself and starting up a commotion.'

Kotoyoriwake: 'That man is of a mischievous temperament, possessing no other art than the specialty of mockery. Yet since he is simple, honest, and an amusing fellow, perhaps he put on a show of false bravery, thinking he would try to pick and see the persimmons that people fear. Old man Tsunekusu is a truly earnest person, so he must have been taken in by Kuniyoriwake's smooth talk and is now suffering the sacrifice. Be that as it may, let us offer prayers here that everyone may be safe.'

So saying, joining their hands, the two persons chanted the Amatsu Norito toward the lake surface, raised their voices in the songs of heaven, and spent a little time.

Filtering through the trees, a sedge hat came swaying. Looking closely, Tsunekusu was there all by himself, climbing up out of breath, and prostrating himself before the two,

Tsunekusu: 'I have kept you waiting for a long time. You must have been terribly bored. There is a slight reason for this, which wasted some time. I beg your forgiveness.'

Kotoyoriwake: 'Could it be that Kuniyoriwake gathered and ate the dragon god's persimmons?'

Tsunekusu: 'Yes, because of that a terrible rare event suddenly broke out, and though my heart was once in a flurry, I have now become somewhat able to feel at ease, and without a moment to lose, I hurried up to this place.'

So saying, catching his breath time and again, he told the tale painfully.

Kotoyoriwake advanced his knees and, moving from one detail to the next, inquired minutely. Tsunekusu narrated all the things that had happened, concealing and hiding nothing.

The three once more celebrated the safety of Kuniyoriwake, and were again chanting the norito of thanksgiving and prayer. Just then, singing the previous song, Kuniyoriwake appeared before the three in high spirits, leading Chāru, Bēsu, and five others in tow, and scratching his head,

Kuniyoriwake: 'Ah, truly, I must apologize for keeping you waiting so long. Since the circumstances have no doubt been heard fully from old man Tsunekusu, I shall say nothing further. Through this, the mischievous spirit, my sub-guardian deity, has completely dispersed, and I have been reborn as a truly sincere, frank, pure, faultless, valiant, and active person.'

Wakahiko: 'Ahahaha, Kuniyoriwake-sama, has the mischievous spirit not... become all the more fierce?'

Kuniyoriwake: 'They say that when a lamp is about to go out its light is especially bright... and so forth, but that sub-guardian spirit of mine is now letting out the pensive, agonizing cries of the agony of death at its destruction. He is truly a painful, vexing guardian deity, and Kuniyoriwake finds him the height of a nuisance. Both Chāru and Bēsu as well have their bellies swollen like blowfish, presenting an appearance as if a wife in her full term were carrying triplets, groaning and screaming on and on, and to top it all off scratching ringworm, and on top of that suffering from children's eczema, and groin ringworm besides...'

Wakahiko: 'Kuniyoriwake-san, you have strayed from the subject again. Will you not drive away Chiyamebō in good time?'

Kuniyoriwake: 'No matter how much I think of driving away Chiyamebasa, he clings to me and thinks of me as the parent of his life, so Fukushiyu cannot leave me, and I can do nothing about it... Hey, Chiyamebasa, as Wakahiko-sama says, there is no need for you to be my Fukushiyujojin anymore, so get away quickly.'

Tsunekusu: 'Ohohoho, what a carefree thing to say when facing the battlefield.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Facing a formidable enemy, playing the flute, and remaining calm and unhurried in his attitude—without this, it would be impossible to participate in the true Kototamasen and win a great victory. Ahahehehe.'

At this time, a gust of violent wind blew up from the water's surface, reaching a momentum that whirled even gigantic rocks into the air. 'Hey, this is terrible,' Kuniyoriwake cried, clinging to the trunk of a large tree, praying with all his might and even with a trembling voice. For some reason, Kotoyoriwake, Wakahiko, Tsunekusu, and everyone else remained seated in their places, their hems not even blown by the fierce wind.

Kotoyoriwake: 'Kuniyoriwake-sama, facing a formidable enemy, your attitude of absolute composure was truly impressive to me.'

Wakahiko restrained his laughter and burst out blowing, 'Kyu-kyu-kyu-pu.' Tsunekusu waited with a serious face.

Kuniyoriwake: 'I am not composed from the bottom of my heart. Please look upon me and listen with Great Naorahi and Direct Naorahi. Please pray so that this fierce wind will stop. If such a huge boulder were to fall upon our heads, our five bodies would surely be ground to dust. Somehow, the muscles of my body have begun to work in minute detail.'

Wakahiko: 'Kuniyoriwake-sama, where is the fierce wind blowing? I could use a little wind. It is so hot... Does it look to you as if the wind is blowing?'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Ah, no matter what... Hey... Something is wrong with me. Truly, after all, the wind is not blowing. Most likely, the fierce wind's devastation from the past or future transcended time and space and was reflected in my eyes.'

Wakahiko: 'You are thorough to the end in your own way, ahahehehe.'

he laughed.

Kotoyoriwake-mikoto solemnly said,

Kotoyoriwake: 'Now, Kuniyoriwake-sama, from here on is the decisive moment. Tonight, we must gaze upon the lake water of this valley, concentrate our prayers, and receive the precious jewel of the Dragon God; this is an important task. From now on, you must become serious and devote yourself entirely to virtuous words and beautiful expressions; please be careful.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Hai.'

and gracefully, as if waking from a dream, he pressed both hands down, made a serious face, and lowered his head. Maintaining a distance of about three ken, everyone concealed themselves in the shadows of the rocks lined up along the lakeshore of the valley, deciding to wait for the passage of time while silently praying in the dark.

The night gradually deepened. The valley between the mountains, extending from west to east, saw the moon, like a twenty-third-night sickle, gradually push aside the clouds and rise. As everyone concentrated their prayers toward the moonlight, a rain of pebbles fell sparsely, scattered like stones. Looking at the lake surface, countless dents were marked on the water like arranged water basins, and the circular ripples overlapped one another, coming to look like the texture of a pocket watch lid. After a while, the large drops of rain stopped. At the bottom of the lake, something indescribable, like a twisting pillar of fire, began to lie horizontal and shine. Everyone held their breath and watched this spectacle. In contrast to the subtle music, a strange and desolate ominous sound echoed all at once from the valleys and mountains on all sides. The earth began to rumble and shake, and even the bodies of everyone began to vibrate with a tingling sensation. Suddenly, the surroundings became pitch dark, reaching the point where one could not distinguish an inch ahead.

国依別『八釜敷う云ふな。俺が竜若彦に直接談判をやつて、見ん事受取つて帰る。……コラコラ竜若彦とやら、汝は三五教の宣伝使に向つて、礼儀を知らず不届きな奴だ。種々と化様もあらうに、其方の失敬千万なる顔は一体何だ。人に対する時は最も美はしき顔色を以て、笑顔を十二分に湛え、挨拶するが神の礼儀なるに、鬼面人を驚かすと云ふ、其方の遣り方、国依別中々承知仕らぬぞ。これに返答有らば承はらう。……又竜神の柿を採り喰ひしを、汝は非常に罪悪の如く今申したが、彼の柿なるもの、竜神の平素食す可きものなるや返答聞かう。柿は人間の喰うべきもの、人間に次いでは猿、烏の食す可き物だ。人にも喰はさず、棚にも置かず、あたら天与の珍味を毎年木に腐らし、天恵を無視する大逆無道、国依別…サアこれより言霊の神力を以て、汝等は申すに及ばず、大竜別命、大竜姫命を言向け和し、天晴、琉、球の玉を奉らせ呉れん。此方の言に向つて一言の弁解あるか……一二三四五六七八九十百千万………』

と国依別は自暴自棄になり、背水の陣を張つて力限りに言霊を奏上した。竜若彦命と称する怪物は、次第々々に容積を減じ、遂には豆の如くになつて消えて了つた。国依別は、

国依別『アハヽヽヽ、コレ若彦さま、御心配御無用になされませ。これより国依別、飽迄も言霊を以て奮戦し、目的の琉、球の宝玉を受取つて見せませう。最早吾々に渡す可き時機が到来したのだ。さうでなくては大神の直司なる、玉照彦様、玉照姫様が何しに教主に御命令あるものか。此竜神執着心未だ晴れやらず、小さき事にかこ付けて、すつた揉んだと一日なりとも永く手に持たんと、吝嗇な奴根性から申して居たのである。………ヤアヤア湖底にある竜神、よつく聞け。三五教の神の司言依別命、国依別命、若彦、常楠の四魂揃うて玉受取りに向うたり。時節には叶ふまい、速かに我前に持来り目出度く授受を終れツ』

と大喝した。此時の国依別の顔面は、四辺を射るが如く崇高なる権威に、何処となく充されて居つた。
