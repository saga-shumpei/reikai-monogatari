高姫館〔783〕

§ 1

The keirinchi of the Kamiyo of Miroku, encircling Aogakiyama, upon the Rengedai of the Reizanejiyō, at the eastern foot of Okebuseyama, catching the morning sun, looks down upon the clear stream of Kokumogaha.

Upon a rocky foundation of sublime scenery, with pillars of round logs and a roof thickly thatched with bamboo grass, stands a sacred hermitage with a quietly erected wicket gate, paved all around with natural stones and encircled by a moderately spacious garden of plums and pines.

The built mansion is of a four-room structure, and the single building in the inner detached quarters is the study room of Takahime.

Weaving together small twigs of bush clover, a writing desk has been made, and using a natural stone inkstone much like an okonabe grinding miso, wearing down a burnt wooden stake with a crisp turning motion just like a pestle, and while soaking ink into a brush made with a bamboo spatula, upon a bluish dried banana leaf, unawares and smoothly she is sometimes seen writing things down.

Upon seeing the figures of Takayamahiko and Kurohime opening the gate and entering, the manservants Katsukō and Ankō, viewing this as the arrival of the Dragon King's entourage, bow their waists very politely and express their reverence.

When Kurohime urges them saying, 'Is Takahime-sama at home? We, a married couple of Takayamahiko, have come; please report this to the inner rooms,' Katsukō replies, 'Hai, hai,' and saying, 'Hey there, Ankō, guard the gate strictly,' he leaves it behind and scurries eagerly to the inner quarters.

After a little while, Katsukō bows his waist before the two and speaks: 'According to Takahime-sama's command, "Like the hototogisu of Machikaneyama, both of you, please proceed quickly to the inner rooms." She is in an extraordinarily good mood,' upon which Kurohime flutters her wings, and Takayamahiko, brushing the dust from his teachings-uniform, leisurely making his way along the narrow corridor, stealthily enters toward the inner rooms.

Takahime emerges from the study of the detached building, coming down the corridor without even putting on her hakama, making the wooden veranda creak loudly, looking somewhat skyward as she appears in the inner rooms, seating herself squarely upon a thin cushion resembling a rice cracker before a brazier carved out of a tree stump,

Takahime: 'Well, well, Takayamahiko-sama and Kurohime-sama, how harmonious you two are. If you display such a joyful scene before a single Takahime like myself, I simply cannot bear it, ho ho ho ho! Now, now, there is no need for reserve; please pass right on through to the inner quarters. ... If you hold back with such reserve, the most essential matters cannot be seen, nor can your faces be heard; it is extremely inconvenient, you see.'

With this, she intentionally asserts that her face cannot be heard and matters cannot be seen, displaying her skill at derailing, hinting to the couple of Takayamahiko that with the great sun shining, isn't it rather a derailment for a married couple to come visiting so leisurely in broad daylight?

Kurohime's face changes abruptly, and she gently pulls at Takayamahiko's sleeve, secretly signaling him that he should cleverly understand and go home.

Takayamahiko: 'Now, Kurohime, you are always tugging at people's sleeves, but you are not mute; why can't you say it clearly? I hate such fox-and-rat-like hand gestures and stealthy methods of mind-to-mind communication.'

Kurohime: 'Oh, how dense... what a Hyōroku grandfather you are. The words Takahime-sama uttered just now, how did you hear them? Just as you said even at Chikubushima, it is a Path where official duties cannot be accomplished if one has a spouse, yet she stated quite plainly that ever since she took Takayama-sama, my mind has become vacant, didn't she?'

Takayamahiko: 'Ho ho ho ho, oh dear, I am quite humbled. This Takayamahiko is also in full agreement with Takahime-sama's spirit.'

Kurohime, setting her eyes in anger and letting a little foam ooze from the corners of her mouth,

Kurohime: 'If you are so fond of whatever-her-name-is, please go ahead and do just as you like. No matter what, always as you say, the bride is a black crow, and the bridegroom is a crane with a long neck and legs; they do not match at all. Humph... With the sky and a man's heart nowadays, though I went to the trouble of intruding, I hereby excuse myself. Takayamahiko and Takatorihime-sama, a union of sentiments between a hawk and a falcon, I too abandon this here. Who on earth would like such a troublesome old man and wish to make him a husband? Thinking it was an introduction by Takahime-sama, for the sake of the Path and for the sake of the country, I have endured until now. A widower swarms with maggots, a widow blossoms with flowers, humph... I may be sorry, but even I... hmph!'
