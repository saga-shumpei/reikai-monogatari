Chapter 18: Kami 格化 [800]

神格化〔800〕

§ 1

Kiyohiko, Kiyokohime, Teruhiko, and Terukohime, the two couples, here celebrated their auspicious wedding ceremony. Reporting this to their father Tsunekusu, who dwelt in the cave of the Zelkova tree, and wishing also to exchange the parental cup of pledge, they set forth from this rock cave, committing themselves to the alligator boat together with M. and Semu, and four or five attendants to begin with, crossed the broad Riukiunuma, parted the boundless grassy fields, and barely reached the cave mansion of Tsunekusu at that day's dusk.

Tsunekusu was in the very midst of praying before the altar with four or five natives for the happiness of Kiyohiko and Teruhiko, and also praying for the safe ocean journey of Kotoyoriwake's party. The two brothers returned here in high spirits, accompanied by their two fair new wives and a host of retainers. Tsunekusu had become utterly absorbed, single-mindedly intent upon his prayer. The brotherly couples sat upright beside him and offered up words of thanksgiving and prayer. When Tsunekusu finished offering the norito and looked back, Kiyohiko and Teruhiko were sitting politely with two beauties of graceful countenance.

Kiyohiko: 'Father-sama, we have returned safely just now.'

Teruhiko: 'You must have been waiting for us anxiously indeed.'

Tsunekusu: 'Ah, you have returned even sooner than I thought. Ah, were not you two the Kiyokohime and Terukohime who departed from this place a short while ago? Looked at from the front or looked at from the side, you are spitting images, beautiful women without a single hair's breadth of difference, how could this be? This Tsunekusu, too, was beside himself with worry. Well, well, safely above all else, most auspicious.'

Kiyokohime: 'Are you the father-sama of Tsunekusu, renowned in rumor? I have become the wife of Kiyohiko-sama. Please cherish me forever and ever.'

Terukohime: 'I am the wife of Teruhiko-sama. Father-sama, for the first time... no, I meet you again. How well you have remained safe. Please love me as your child forever and ever. Since I am an incompetent person, please do not hesitate to scold me, and I beg that you will employ me for all eternity.'

While holding back tears, Tsunekusu said,

Tsunekusu: 'Ah, both of you have spoken so well. This Tsunekusu, too, has no remaining regrets now because of this. Husband and wife, please get along well and fulfill your divine work perfectly.'

Kiyokohime and Terukohime lowered their heads simultaneously with a start, and are lost in tears of joy and nostalgia. Expressing his celebratory feelings and wishing to reveal his own identity, Tsunekusu unfolded a silver fan, and unlike his aged self, began to chant in a clear voice.

Tsunekusu: 'In that ancient age of the gods, when the deities shook the thousand rock-doors, Jerusalem of the divine capital, Kunitachinotokami no Ōkami Permanently and forever tranquil, Ruling over the world at that juncture, Kunihiko of the distant ancestral deities, Together with Tsunekohime, Offering the hemp pillars of divine festivals, To no avail despite having served him, Ashizumi no Takebi's might being so strong, Descendants scattered in all directions, My father and mother, Tamahiko and Tamahime, those two, concealing their forms on the island of Jitōtō, While giving birth to me and nowhere to be found, Concealed their pure forms. Tsunekusu, a fledglings separated from its parents, Drifting to the shores of reliance, Touring far and near around Jitōtō, Came to Kinki. While sending up thin wisps of smoke, Passing through the merciless floating world, As though encountering the great upheaval of the Heavenly Rock Cave, Father and child are blown about by the rough waves of the world, Passing through life separately. Just around that time, through the wondrous workings of the divine, That intertwined parental reunion, Beginning with Akihiko and Komahiko, With hearts as pure as Kiyohiko and Teruhiko, meeting these four by chance, Rejoicing in ties that never end, As the days and months passed by, At the purification grounds of Kumano waterfall, When the time came to purify the heart With Wakahiko of the Misogakyō, Konohanahime having appeared, Both Tsunekusu and Wakahiko, Having received the divine treasures of Ri and Kyū, Upon the island of Riukiyu, the secret land, Concealed forever and ever, Receiving the jewel in the dragon's jaw, Proclaiming with stern words That they should present it to Kotoyoriwake No Mizu no Mikoto, Awed by that divine decree, Beating across the eight-hundred paths of the tide, Bathed in rain and combed by the wind, Enduring the spray of the vast ocean, Swallowed by waves, and gradually, Ascending to this island of Ri and Kyū, When one looks upon climbing up, Deep mountain paths where human footprints had long ceased, In the valley is a pure sea of jewels, Tsunekusu, now aged and rusted, Along with Tama no Seikatsu Wakahiko, Ascending day by day and night by night, Chanting the heavenly norito, And of Ōryūwake and Ōryūhime...'

§ 2

Having turned Chindin-no-mikoto to pacify and submit, and offering the precious jewels of Ryū and Kyū to Kotoyoriwake of Ananai-kyō, the heart of the dragon god, whose murakimo heart was firmly resolved, opened at last—even such a time arrived. Parting the waves and crossing over, Kotoyoriwake, the great high priest, together with Kuniyoriwake, appeared in this cave which they had fixed as their temporary dwelling.

Here four kami-ministers, advancing eagerly and cheerfully through the valley of Mount Harris, without any mishap obtained the precious jewel of the dragon's jaw into their hands, and oh, the joy of their return! Kiyohiko and Teruhiko, his sons, by what divine guidance had they visited his dwelling?

Lady Kiyoko and Lady Teruko, the four of them, already residing in the temporary lodge, oh, the wonder of it! Kotoyoriwake, the great high priest, accompanied by Kuniyoriwake, declaring that he would cross the waves and go forth to the island of Takasago, made this Wakahiko, whom this Tsunekusu had brought along over the waves, carry the precious jewels of Ryū and Kyū, causing him to return afar to the island of Onokoro, while he designated this Tsunekusu as the guardian deity of the islands of Ryūkyū, and made his sons Kiyohiko and Teruhiko the gods of the left and right guards, and crossed the waves to go forth. Both Kiyohiko and Teruhiko, having formed the bond of younger sister and elder brother so auspiciously here with Lady Kiyoko and Lady Teruko to protect this floating island forever, the things they had thought became mere bubbles of water.

Lady Kiyoko and Lady Teruko, blending into the darkness, from the time they hid their forms somewhither, the suffering within the hearts of both Kiyohiko and Teruhiko, how great it must be; and my grief as a parent is doubly intense. There is indeed avail in having prayed morning and evening with sincerity to the gods of heaven and earth; today, oh, the joy of meeting again with Lady Kiyoko and Lady Teruko as young brides! Ah, Kannagara kamugami-gami, may your august spirits prosper; may the bond of husband and wife be harmonious, for a thousand years, for eight thousand years, and eternally.

May the pledge of the mandarin ducks never change no matter when, anywhere; may the bond of husband and wife remain unfading, deep as the color of the evergreen pine! Already I have nothing left in this world; from here I will ride across the ridge of the peaks of Mount Harris, and in order to protect this sacred island eternally for myriad ages, extending my life, I will join the throng of mountain people, become their leader, and protect the world eternally. You, Kiyohiko, and Lady Kiyoko, and Lady Teruko radiant with light, with hearts pure as Teruhiko, go forth everlastingly and forever to the southern islands, and serve in the divine works.

Though the morning sun shine or grow cloudy, though the wax and wane of the moon take place, even should the great earth sink, as long as the spirit of my body remains on this island, blessing it as the peaceful land of Urayasu, may you cause the dew and rain of divine grace to fall everlasting and firm as bedrocks! Already there is nothing left in this world; whichever it may be, farewell,'—almost before he had finished speaking, chanting the heavenly number-song and clasping his hands in prayer, Tsunekusu instantly became pure white like snow over his whole body, and just as he stepped one or two paces out of the entrance of the wooden round palace, instantly his form became white smoke, and the sound of the wind blowing over the shore was heard loudly only in the sky.

The brothers and their wives, astonished, ran out of the wooden round palace, gazed up at the sky and joined their hands, and the voices calling out, 'Father, oh Father,' were blocked by the blowing wind, leaving no way to search for him save to weep. Gazing up at heaven and prostrating upon the earth, this pathetic parting of parent and child, how pitiful it is to see them lamenting! Ah, Kannagara kamugami-gami, may your august spirits prosper.
