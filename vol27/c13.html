Chapter 13: Dragon の解脱 [795]

竜の解脱〔795〕

§ 1

Floating amidst the vast ocean, the isle of Riukiu, famed and high in renown, where the jewel lies hidden, is a sacred island. The plum blossoms of the three thousand worlds open all at once as the time arrives. Setting up the pillar of the palace in the patterned sacred ground, erecting the chigi high, abides in serene solemnity the august Izumitama. Receiving the divine oracle of the auspicious Mizutama fully and in detail through the two deities of Tamaterugami, and having Kotoyoriwake, a divine descendant of the Mizutama, take charge of and convey the divine mandate to seek out the rare treasures of the tide-flowing and tide-ebbing jewels, the grand preceptor himself, leading the ministry of the teachings of Kuniyoriwake, crossed far over the path of the waves, and when they arrived and looked about in this place, behold how noble it was that prior to them, starting with Wakahiko and Tsunekusu, having truly received the divine imperial proclamation once more, had swiftly come and were residing there.

In the forest of zelkova and camphor trees standing in rows that seal the heavens, with extraordinarily thick zelkova trunks, within a natural cave of spontaneous formation, both Wakahiko and Tsunekusu appeared as the deities of the tree forks, pacifying and reconciling the island people to the august majesty of the Ōkami while awaiting the coming of the time. In the joy of the arrival of Kotoyoriwake of the pure Kototama, the grand preceptor of the august spirit, together with Kuniyoriwake, Wakahiko and Tsunekusu, full of valor, deep in the mountains of Mount Harris, with hearts courageous upon their spirited steeds, whip on and advance deep into the valley, arriving before the expanse of lake water. Surrounded on all sides by darkness, with a driving rain of gravel falling incessantly, at such a terrifying moment, when the curtain of darkness was drawn open, gazing upon the mysterious shadow walking and advancing over the surface of the waves, its beard hung shaggy down its breast, white hair mocking the snow hung long down its back, eyes shone like mirrors, the color of its face splashed with vermilion, its crocodile mouth split wide to the ears peeling back golden fangs, while holding upright upon its forehead a golden horn some four or five inches long, raising a rattling voice and thrusting out its strange tongue, it accosts the entourage of Kotoyoriwake with complaints.

The gist of the complaints is that it is a crime for Kuniyoriwake, however awe-inspiring he may be, to have stolen and eaten the Taiping persimmon which the dragon god was reputed to guard. The demon god of obsessive attachment reviled with all its might, stretching ropes to prevent them from passing and vowing to cut down effortlessly the barbed wire strung by the evil deity. With the shelling of truth launched by the Kototama of Kuniyoriwake, the unconstrained divine minister, even the mighty demon god was abashed, gradually shrinking its form until it ended up no larger than a bean, and at last vanished powerlessly away. 'Ah, Kami, may the Mitama bless us and abide! Against the diamond-indestructible wish-granting jewel, and the power of the Kototama hidden secretly within the lower abdomen of Kuniyoriwake, there is no shield that can oppose. Taking up the spear of justice, how could I fail to shine across the great path of the gods of heaven and earth, the divine land of Takamanohara, the fruitful reed-plain land of Mizuho, and down to the very bottom of the great ocean? The Kototama of Kuniyoriwake echoes like the plain of blue trees of Tachibana in Himuka of Tsukushi. The divine Izanagi no Ōkami has become the precious breath; the four deities of the Misogi-do, Lord Seoritsuhime, Lord Ibukidoji, beginning with the precious Ōkami, together with Lord Hayakitsuhimekamitand Lord Hayasasurahimekami,'

'Here four deities as the messengers of proclamation have appeared, becoming the living shrines of these deities. Oh dragon gods of the precious deities, Great Ryūwake and Great Ryūhime, this heaven and earth is a land saved by the Kototama, a living land, a land where the Mitama are blessed and abide. In the present age, when the heavenly rock cave is flung wide open, and even the nethermost land shines clear and bright, what benefit is there in forever holding the two jewels of the tide-flowing and tide-ebbing? The Mizu-Mitama saving this world, unto the two entrusted by the gods, without grudging, without concealing, unbendingly reveal your form and offer them swiftly now, according to the way of the gods! The gods are with us together,'

'Though you wrap and conceal them for ever and ever at the bottom of the water a thousand fathoms deep, since we of the Mihikari teachings have come forth and arrived here, do not hesitate for a single moment, nor for a single breath. Ah, Kami, may the Mitama bless us and abide! One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, tarari, to guard the hundred, thousand, and myriad divine beings in peace of heart within the land of Urayasu, firm and immovable, this journey entrusted by the gods, grant it swiftly!'

As they proclaimed thus, fast and fast, the black clouds that had hitherto enveloped everything cleared away completely on all sides, illuminating the waves, and a group of firelight gradually revealed the two.

Approaching the place where they stood, the Futahashira suddenly transformed. Appearing as noble Megami, with faces full of smiles, they folded their hands before Kotoyoriwake and Kuniyoriwake. From the earth emerged Tamatebako, and offering one to each of the two, they turned their Reura sleeves. Riding upon purple clouds that arose in an instant, they ascended high into the Hisakata Ōzora, to the Wakamiya of the Ama, the sun.

The two Kami, Ōtatsuwake and Ōtatsuhime, who had been deeply bound by Shifuchiyakushin, had now completed their Sanzennen of Sankan Sannetsu Kugiyō. Saved by the grace of Kami, they now sat at the Onmigi of the noble Amatsukami, the Sumeōkami, and encountered the Tokoyo spring of the pure Kamikuni. It is a truly noble Monogatari; I am happy to recount it this evening. Lying down in Shōunkaku on the second day of the sixth month of the lunar calendar, with a fan in one hand to keep the rhythm, I set this down in detail. The one holding the brush is Mr. Kitamura. I record this as a joy beyond expectation, for it shall serve as a guide to the teachings of the San-Ikkyō, where the divine authority of Kami shines brilliantly. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase.

By the Kototama of Kuniyoriwake, the monster known as Ryūwakahiko instantly vanished. The reappearing Ōtatsuwake and Ōtatsuhime respectfully presented to the hands of Kotoyoriwake and Kuniyoriwake the Tamatebako, which contained the jewels of Ryū and Kyū. Having thus concluded their three-thousand-year Sankan Sannetsu Kugiyō and cast off all attachments, they rode leisurely upon purple clouds, ascended to the Wakamiya of the Amatsubi, and became Ōkami who sit at the right of the Ōkami and regulate the rainfall as the Ame-no-Mikumari-no-Kami.

The pure and righteous Kototama is also called the indestructible Nyoi-hōju. This heaven and earth is a land that lives and works, helped by the blessings of Kototama. The most precious treasure in the universe is the Kototama of seventy-five sounds, which has voice but no form, is non-existent yet existent, existent yet non-existent, and possesses the free and unfettered application of life and death. When referred to spiritually, it becomes the indestructible Nyoi-hōju. In the divine decree of Amaterasu-Ōmikami, it is written, 'Kotomuke-yawase, norinaose,' which is clearly indicated in the divine classic, the Kojiki. That which governs the lands of the four quarters under heaven is the Itsutsu-misu-maru-no-tama; when this jewel is active, there is no famine, no disease, no war, and no disaster under heaven, nor is there any fear of wind, water, or fire calamities, or natural disasters, and all in the universe is governed in peace and safety.

Furthermore, the two treasures of Ryū and Kyū received here from the two dragon Kami, Kotoyoriwake and Kuniyoriwake, are divine vessels that regulate wind, rain, water, and fire, receive and accept all things, or subdue them, and perfectly complete the divine work of never abandoning those they have taken in.

Here, Kotoyoriwake-no-Mikoto and the others faced the lake, recited the Amatsu-norito, sang the Ame-no-Kazauta, and while singing the Sendenka, they descended the path they had originally come, deciding to return for the time being to the cave of the Keyaki tree.

The party of Kotoyoriwake left the dragon lake behind, and while traversing the strange and magnificent scenery where the Senjō-iwa rocks were arranged like a go-board, they descended as their feet led them. The descent, unlike the ascent, was faster than they had thought. Before they knew it, they had returned to the vicinity of the Taihei-gaki, which is said to have been guarded by the dragon Kami. Tsunakusu suddenly stopped, looked back at the party, and said, 'Kyōshu-sama, Kuniyoriwake-kami-sama was attacked by a swarm of great snakes, leaped from the top of the Taihei-gaki, jumped down into the blue abyss with a splash, fell into a state of suspended animation, and was carried away by the whirlpools.'

Tsunakusu continued, 'This is the sacred ground of his repentance. The divine official Kuniyoriwake, with his strong and unyielding spirit, conversely fed Ryūwakahiko a counter-argument so magnificent that he was humbled. How admirable! Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase. Now that it has come to this, even I, Tsunakusu, cannot understand the heart of Kami. How should I distinguish the standards of good and evil, right and wrong? I beg for your judgment.' When he said this, Kotoyoriwake laughed and said, 'The Kototama of Kuniyoriwake accords with the principles of heaven and earth. If one falls into good, it becomes evil, and the extreme of evil becomes good. This truth of the unity of good and evil—place your hand upon your heart and reflect, and through Naohi, look again and listen again.'

Kotoyoriwake continued, 'With the small wisdom of man, is there any reason that the standards of good and evil, right and wrong could be understood? If it accords with the heart of the Ōkami who created this world, then all becomes the path of supreme good; if it does not accord with that divine heart, then it becomes the path of evil. As a human being, one is not given even a fraction of the right to judge one's fellow man. It is best to leave all things to the hands of Kami.' As he explained this in great detail, Kuniyoriwake, Wakahiko, and the elder Tsunakusu were encouraged, and with joyful hearts, the party returned to the extremely vast natural hotel in the forest of Kusunoki and Keyaki at the beginning of the evening as the twilight passed. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase.
