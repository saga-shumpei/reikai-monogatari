Chapter 10: 太平柿 [792]

太平柿〔792〕

§ 1

On the shores of Kumano in Kishiu, proclaiming morning and evening the divine teachings of the Gods of Tenchi, Wakahiko of Ananai-kyō, together with Grandfather Tsunekusu, went on pilgrimage to the waterfall of Kumano. In the very midst of their Misogiharahi, the Himegami who suddenly appeared was the guardian deity of the mountain of Hachisu, where the flowers of the heart open in bloom. Konohanahime appeared abruptly at the edge of the waterfall, and speaking with a tranquil voice proclaimed thus: 'Thou, by this, together with Tsunekusu, shalt arrange thy travel gear and board a boat, setting forth from the coast of Kumano to reach the sacred site where the twin treasures of Liu and Qiu, forever and eternally settled, rest upon the floating Takerajima in the intervals of the waves. Pacify through Kotomu the Aragami dwelling in the mountains of Harisu, receive the pearl of the dragon god's jaw, and offer it up before the noble august presences of Tamateruhiko and Tamateruhime, the Kamuzukasa of Ananai-kyō. Starting with Kotoorowake from the sacred site of Takamanohara, the missionary of Kunitorowake shall come forth from behind. Thou shalt arrive at this sacred island in advance thereof, and in the deep valleys of Mount Harisu, pacify the dragon god dwelling there through the divine breath of Kototama. Konohanahime shall attend closely upon thee, both before and behind, and shall surely cause thee to build achievements; advance even a single day sooner.'

No sooner had the words ended than Her August Presence instantly vanished away, and afterward a wondrous fragrance, rich and mysterious, pervaded all around with poignant sweetness. The profound and elegant music harmonized beautifully with the wind of Shinotobe crossing the tree-tops, delightful to the ears. Wakahiko, his ears growing youthful, leaving behind the forest of Kumano where ancient camphor trees covered the sky, accompanied by Tsunekusu, and revering the divine words, issued forth at the whim of the waves. Making the hollow of a giant zelkova tree at the foot of Mount Harisu their temporary dwelling, and day by day unceasingly pacifying the dragon god, for that very purpose he was attended by numerous natives, ascending and descending steep mountain slopes, and with all his heart and utmost sincerity serving the divine work. Today, peculiarly, the appearance of the dragon god was delayed and took much time, and by the time they returned to the provisional dwelling in the giant zelkova tree, it was around midnight. Numerous bonfires were glowing brightly, and as he drew near to their cave and looked inside from without—whether it were a wolf or a bear, a demon or a snake, or perhaps the avatar of the dragon god—a strange shadow suddenly appeared, and the howling voice resounded all around with great thunderous tones of 'oo-oo'.

Wakahiko, while his liver was being crushed with dismay, lowering his voice and finishing the recitation of the number-song, from within, in a strange voice lacking pitch and tone, responded in return with: 'One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, tarari, a hundred, a thousand, ten thousand.' Wakahiko threw himself flat upon the earth, pressing down his throbbing chest, and cried out, 'Are you a wolf, a bear, a snake, or truly the true Kami? Make known your name!' When he called thus, Kunitorowake changed his voice and said, 'The dragon god of Mount Harisu is bestowing the precious jewels of Liu and Qiu upon the divine officials Kotoorowake and Kunitorowake. Have no doubt whatsoever.' Wakahiko, who heard this, being of a straightforward and single-minded nature, rejoiced, believing it to be a true Kami, and remained immersed in tears of gratitude. Grandfather Tsunekusu felt suspicious, and though he knew not if it were a bias of the heart, there were certain points where he could hardly think this was the Himegami who was the avatar of the dragon.

'Kunitorowake-sama or the missionary of Kunitorowake, do enter this cave and rest your breath. This Himegami is surely none other than the Kamuzukasa of Kunitorowake of Ananai-kyō displaying his prankishness. Hey there, Kunitorowake-sama, reveal your true form quickly!' Before he had even finished speaking, Kunitorowake, unable to endure the perceptive words, unwittingly let out a burst of laughter. Instantly the transformation was revealed, and here the three pushed their way into the dark interior of the cave. Wandering about in the darkness, strike the flint sparks click-clack as they would, for some reason, on this day alone no fire came forth. Enveloped in the darkness, the three were peopling the fence of the blind Kami, and while they were groping all around, two persons holding torches appeared and entered this place. Placing the bright fire upright there, they suddenly dashed out to the front.

Kunitorowake arose, and looking through the forms of the three, while celebrating their unexpected reunion along with tales of distant mountains, the night broke into dawn. Ah, how wondrous, how divine! The guidance of the noble Kami. The four souls gathered together, and the divine humans, while bathing in the morning sun, with four or five natives following behind, pushing their way through the thickets where palms and rosewoods grew lush, trod upon the soft, spongy mountain path where their feet sank deep, and went climbing up.

Wakahiko: 'Grandfather Tsunekusu-sama, look at that!'

Tsunekusu: 'Oh, indeed, Wakahiko-san, how magnificent! Truly a divine realm.'

§ 2

Though it is winter, there is neither snow nor frost; in the summer-like warmth of Onokorojima, sweating, I climb with unaccustomed feet the boggy, ash-like path that sinks the shins.

Kuniyoriwake, unable to bear his hunger, peels a single banana leaf from the roadside, folds it into four, and, using it as a mat, sits down with a thump by the side of the path, placing his left hand palm-up on his knee, clinching his right hand with only the index finger thrust straight out in front, and seeing the Taiheigaki looking sweet and ripening on the steep precipice, sits in silence making a rattling sound in his throat. Kotoyoriwake and Wakahiko are standing seven or eight ken ahead. Tsunekusu, Charles, Base, and other natives who followed behind Kuniyoriwake could not dispel their suspicion at Kuniyoriwake's attitude, and stared fixedly at his face. Kuniyoriwake raises and lowers the left hand placed on his knee once or twice, points to the persimmon across the way with the index finger of his right hand, next points to his own mouth, and then points to the persimmon and again to his mouth.

Tsunekusu: 'My dear Kuniyoriwake-sama, this Tsunekusu may be old, but my ears are sharp, so why don't you speak with your mouth instead of doing things like that?'

Kuniyoriwake points to his mouth and then to the persimmon, and finally shows his belly by pointing to it.

Tsunekusu: 'Judging by appearances, are you saying you want to eat that persimmon? If so, I will have them feed you now. Now, now, Charles-sama, is there anyone here good at tree-climbing who will cross this valley to the other side and pluck two or three of those sweet-looking persimmons for him? The god in Kuniyoriwake's throat is commanding that those persimmons be offered.'

Charles: 'Yes, I understand. However, those persimmons remaining over there are called the Dragon God's persimmons and are not things for human beings to eat. If anyone were to eat even one, man or woman alike, his belly would suddenly swell, his navel would finally burst, the child of a great snake would be born, and the parent would shift his residence away for good—such is the perilous persimmon. Therefore, no one has ever plucked them, nor has anyone ever eaten them. Consequently, no one knows their taste. Is the Dragon God possessing you here, I wonder? If so, shall I pluck them with the intention of offering them to the Dragon God?'

Base: 'Hey, hey, Charles, you shouldn't make such rash promises. No matter how much Tsunekusu-sama is a god descended from heaven, he cannot do as he pleases with the Dragon God's persimmons. And even if he is possessed, since that is a spirit, there is no way it would munch and eat them. If it were to eat them, this physical body of his would eat them, and that would be a huge problem indeed. Come on, let's go. Wakahiko-sama and Kotoyoriwake-kamisama are already out of sight.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'You two, Charles and Base, that argument is reasonable. However, this person here is the true incarnation of the Dragon God; if he were in his original form, he would descend to the valley, raise his snake-head sharply, and thrust his tongue out wigglingly, and they would easily and smoothly enter his mouth, but while he is transformed into a human like this, he cannot pluck them like an ordinary person. The god commands, Charles, Base, go and pluck them quickly. You will suffer no harm.'

Charles: 'Yes, I understand.'

Base: 'You said we would suffer no harm, didn't you? Of course it's so. Sitting stock-still cross-legged on a banana leaf, making human beings go through a painful time to pluck those persimmons, and receiving a meal served without lifting a finger—what hardship is there in that? It is an easy thing.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Stop grumbling and go pluck them quickly to present them. Due to his hunger, Kuniyoriwake can no longer walk a single step, and is on the verge of attaining peaceful entry into Nirvana right here.'

Tsunekusu: 'Otsuhohoho.'

Charles and Base descend the precipice like monkeys, leaping over the tops of rocks scattered in the fairly deep valley stream, cross to the other side avoiding the current, cling to the persimmon tree, and climb up.

§ 3

Persimmons looking so sweet as if water were dripping from them hung in countless numbers beneath the shade of the leaves. Their size was surely about that of a bull's kintama. The two, Chaale and Base, unable to endure the indescribably sweet fragrance, forgot their shimei and, with itsuyōkenmei, picked and ate, picked and ate the sweet-looking ones, smacking their lips in itsuzuzumi.

Tsunekusu called out from below,

Tsunekusu: 'Hey there, you two, Chaale and Base, won't you drop the persimmons?'

At this voice Chaale suddenly came to his senses,

Chaale: 'We will drop them now. However, if we drop such soft persimmons, they will be smashed. Unfortunately there is no container, so I will carry them down inside my belly, please wait.'

Tsunekusu: 'The Riujin-sama is waiting impatiently here. Even if they are a little hard, it is fine, so pluck them and throw them over this way.'

Chaale: 'Hard ones are astringent and cannot be eaten, you know.'

Tsunekusu: 'Ah, there is no shikata for it, eh.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Ah, being like this, when I see people eating with such a sweet look, my belly feels as if it were getting extra empty. Ah, there is no shikata, the Kamisama said not to make people one's strength, and not to use the master as a walking stick. Even if you think to pick sweet persimmons with someone else's strength and obtain virtue, it is no dame. Come now, for one's own affairs, it is best to settle them oneself.'

Holding his peko-peko belly and bending into a futahegoshi, he slid down the dangai, leapt hither and thither over the heads of rocks protruding from the tanigaha, and barely reached the base of the persimmon tree on the taigan.

When he looked, the two had devoured the red, sweet ones down to the last bit like silkworms eating mulberry leaves, and further down, green and astringent ones were hanging.

Kuniyoriwake, looking up at the sky,

Kuniyoriwake: 'Hey, you two, Chaale and Base, leave a little of the red ones for me. I am climbing up now...'.,

Placing hands and feet on the knotty, scarred burls of the persimmon tree, he finally caught hold of a first branch and looked down, whereupon he was assaulted by the terrifying spirit of the tanigaha with its gekitanhimatsu, and owing to his empty stomach, he began to feel as though his eyes were swimming.

Kuniyoriwake barely managed to draw his body close to a slender branch on one side,

Kuniyoriwake: 'Ah, how dangerous this is. If this branch should snap even once, it is instantly jakumetsu iraku. However, the Kamisama said that unless one goes to a scary place, one cannot eat ripe persimmons. Delicious ripe persimmons are indeed to be found in scary places, aa.'

Muttering thus, he barely plucked one good-looking one, and crammed it into his mouth on the spot as if pouncing upon it.

With an indescribably delicious taste his eyes involuntarily narrowed, and wrinkling his face he savored it.

Presently his belly swelled moment by moment like Hotei's bag.

Kuniyoriwake: 'My, this fellow is unbearable, I wonder if a great snake has taken up residence in my belly as Chaale says. Somehow the inside of my belly has come to feel all kure-kure. Will it not end up with my body being master and spirit being servant, just like in the ancient days of Amatsu-tsu and Hoba? Since it is said to eat and partake of the Takayama ibori and Toyama ibori, dividing the persimmons, surely no divine punishment will befall. Ah, if I dawdle and dally my belly will grow so large that I won't be able to climb down. Ah, Kamnagara kamunagara kamunagi mimase.'

He attempted to climb down the tree.

A considerably dark, large great snake, making its tortoise-shell patterned markings gleam, came writhing one after another, aiming for the persimmon tree—how repulsive it was.

Kuniyoriwake with itsuyōkenmei chanted the heavenly number song of hitotsu, futatsu, mittsu, yottsu.

Kuniyoriwake, disconcerted by the fierce and rapidly climbing wicked snake, spread both arms from the treetops and, scratching at the empty air, plunged with a plop right into the blue-tinted deep abyss of the tanigaha.

Caught in the surging waves, for a while his figure vanished entirely from sight.

With extreme speed and in countless numbers, the snakes climbed up the persimmon tree.

The two, Chaale and Base, aiming for the blue pool into which Kuniyoriwake had plunged, once again jumped in with a plop and a plop.

Along with water splashes that suddenly rose, the figures of the two vanished once again.

Ah, what has become of the fates of these three?

と柿の節だらけの瘤に手をかけ足をかけ、やつと一の枝に取りつき下を見れば、激潭飛沫の谷川凄惨の気に襲はれ、空腹の上の事とて目も眩む様な感じがして来た。国依別は漸くにして一方の細き枝に身を寄せ、

国依別『アヽ危いものだ。この枝が一つペキンと折れようものなら忽ち寂滅為楽だ。併し怖い所に行かねば熟柿は食へんぞよと神様が仰有つた。美味しい熟柿は矢張り怖い所にあるものだナア』

と呟きながら辛うじて美味さうな奴を一つむしり、飛びつくやうに矢庭に頬張つて見た。何とも云へぬ美味で思はず目も細くなり、顔に皺を寄せて賞翫した。忽ち腹は布袋の如く刻々に膨れ出した。

国依別『ヤア此奴は耐らん、チヤールの云ふやうに大蛇が腹に宿つたのかなア。何だか腹の中がクレクレとして来たぞ。天足、胞場の昔のやうに体主霊従になつて仕舞ふのではあるまいかなア。高山の伊保理、低山の伊保理を柿わけて食し召せと云ふからは、強ち神罰も当るまい。アヽグヅグヅして居ると腹が大きくなつて下りられないやうになる。あゝ惟神々々霊幸倍坐世』

と樹を下りんとする。相当に黒い大きな大蛇、亀甲型の斑紋を光らせながら絡繹として柿の樹目蒐けて上つて来る嫌らしさ。国依別は一生懸命に一二三四と天の数歌を唱へた。

国依別は追々登り来る勢猛き悪蛇に僻易し、樹上より両手を拡げて空中を掻きながら、谷川の蒼味だつた深淵の上にドブンと落ち込んだ。逆巻く浪に捲き込まれて暫くは其姿も見えなくなつて仕舞つた。蛇は急速度を以て数限りなく柿の木に上つて来る。

チヤール、ベースの両人は、国依別の飛び込んだ青淵目蒐けて又もやドブンドブンと飛び込んで仕舞つた。パツと立つた水煙と共に二人の姿は又々消えて仕舞つた。あゝ此三人の行方は如何なつたのであらうか。
