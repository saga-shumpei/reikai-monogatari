Chapter 15: 情意投合 [797]

情意投合〔797〕

§ 1

Abu and Hachi stopped over at the forest of Ikuta, had an audience with Komahiko, and hearing the news that the Kyōshiyu of Kotoyoriwake, together with Kuniyoriwake, had assumed their sacred duties on the island of Takasago, departed hastily from the sacred site, and headed southwest across the sea of Seto—upon hearing this, the aforementioned Takahime caught wind of it, and a party of three consisting of Haruhiko, Tsunehiko, and herself set off in pursuit of Kotoyoriwake, whereupon Abu and Hachi, without a moment to lose, entrusted their bodies to a single light boat, thinking it impossible to tell what manner of obstruction Takahime might inflict upon the Kyōshiyu, and with all their might they rowed out in search of Takahime's wake, and as they approached the coast of Kojimahantau, they discovered a single boat stranded upon a reef, and peering through the starlight to see who they might be, found them to be Kiyokohime and Terukohime, the two who had served upon the sacred throne of Manai in Hinu.

Hereupon they rescued the two into their own boat, abandoned that half-broken vessel, and rowing vigorously against the rough waves, finally arrived safely at the port of Naha in Ryūkyū, where the party of four felt as though they were being drawn by some mysterious force, and around the evening of that day they reached the front of the zelkova tree cave which Tsunekusui and Wakahiko had made their temporary dwelling.

Lord Abu had already been granted the name of Kiyohiko by Kotoyoriwake-no-Mikoto, and Lord Hachi had been granted the name of Teruhiko, and they were serving in the office of associate missionary. The two rejoiced beyond measure at having been unexpectedly promoted by Kotoyoriwake-no-Mikoto, and in order to requite that master's grace, they had resolved that toward Kotoyoriwake-no-Mikoto they would grudge no hardship, even were they to cast away their own lives and bodies.

Although they had lost sight of their intended objective, Takahime's party, upon the sea, the fact that they had saved Terukohime and Kiyokohime from distress felt to them entirely as the divine providence, leaving them somewhat satisfied.

The ancestors of these Terukohime and Kiyokohime were Yukinarihiko-no-Mikoto, and they corresponded to his fourth-generation grandchildren. Having received a divine oracle, they appeared in Hinumanai prior to the emergence of Toyokohime-no-Mikoto, built a hermitage on Mount Hijiyama, and waited for the time. There they encountered Kurohime of the Uranai teachings, were preached to and expounded upon various doctrines of the Uranai teachings, half believing and half doubting it; yet no matter how much eloquence Kurohime used to preach, Kiyokohime and Terukohime never once set foot in the mansion of Kurohime at Magatsuhagahara, nor did they meet with Takahime and others. They merely refrained from refuting Kurohime's words, taking and discarding good and evil, and maintained a surface-level obedience and nothing more. This attitude of the two women toward Kurohime was unavoidable given the circumstances of the time, and they were unable to adopt a better attitude than this.

At that time, the divine oracle of Toyokohime-no-Mikoto descended upon these two, they received the Mani treasure pearl from Tamayohime of Lake Suwa, and when Umekohime and the rest of the party returned to the mansion of Akayamahiko at the port of Yura, and heard that Kami Susanoo-no-Ōkami and Kunitakehiko-no-Mikoto had appeared, the two prepared their travel attire and came out to the mansion of Akayamahiko at the port of Yura; but by that time the Mani treasure pearl had already been sent to the sacred site, and since it was after the fact when the whereabouts of Kami Susanoo-no-Ōkami and Kunitakehiko-no-Mikoto had become unknown, the two delayed not a moment, headed by land toward the sacred site, paid homage to the shrine priests Tamateruhiko and Tamateruhime at Nishikinomiya, and in order to cross over to the islands of Ryūkyū, they once again departed from the sacred site, secluded themselves on Mount Takakuma—the place of appearance of Tamateruhiko-no-Mikoto—performed ascetic practices anew for three weeks, received the divine teaching of Konohanahime, crossed over mountain and slope in high spirits, stopped over at the forest of Ikuta, set sail from the port of Hyōgo to head for Ryūkyū, and whether by divine design or otherwise, unexpectedly ran aground on a reef just before Kojimahantau, where, into this most perilous place, they were rescued by the boat of the Mitsugoshi new missionaries, Kiyohiko and Teruhiko, and finally arriving safely as a party of four at the port of Naha, they had advanced right up to the front of the cave of Tsuki.

While making their long-distance voyage in a small boat, the four men and women naturally found their wills and feelings united before they knew it, and each had deeply fixed their hearts upon the person of their desire in their minds. Kiyokohime placed her hopes in Kiyohiko, and Terukohime placed her hopes in Teruhiko. Yet conversely, Kiyohiko placed his hopes in Terukohime, and Teruhiko placed his hopes in Kiyokohime, and they wished to become husband and wife in the future and participate in the divine work. Kiyohiko was in his vigorous prime at forty-four or forty-five years of age, Teruhiko at forty-two or forty-three, while Kiyokohime had reached twenty-five and Terukohime twenty-three. There was an age difference of about twenty years. Nevertheless, Kiyohiko and Teruhiko, having received divine virtue and understood the true path, were overflowing with a refreshing spirit throughout their bodies, had good complexions, and looked relatively young, so that as a husband and wife they did not at first glance appear overly mismatched.

§ 2

The four persons, wishing to spend the night here, advanced deep into the interior of the cavern. Rustling reed-mats were spread across the earthen floor, and tableware and the like were neatly arranged.

Kiyohiko: 'Ah, I know not whose dwelling this is, but in this island abounding in cave-dwelling races, to have such a natural mansion of tree stumps is a magnificent thing. Whatever else, this must be the dwelling of the chieftain of these parts, I suppose. If we carelessly sleep peacefully in such a place, and they return with numerous kinsmen and fly into a rage, there is no telling what kind of thing might suddenly break out. The entrance is one-sided; if we dally, we shall be caught in a bottleneck siege and suffer. Come, shall we each take a turn standing watch at the entrance, and if some suspicious fellow comes along, make a signal?'

Teruhiko: 'That is also true. Nevertheless, first let us finish eating these strawberries we picked along the road as our evening meal, and even after that, it will not be too late. It seems that round about there it is gradually growing dark.'

Taking out a fire-striker from his bosom, he set fire to the split pieces of fat pine piled up here and there, lit a lamp, ate their evening meal, and with the feeling of having returned home, the four sat in peace deep within and indulged in various talks of impressions.

Kiyohiko: 'Thus the four of us, men and women, having crossed over to this island, for any of us a single life is an inconvenient thing. It is just as if Izanagi and Izanami descended upon the self-pivoting island. If we designate this great tree as the heavenly pillar of the spear, and... saying, "Ah, how delightful, oh maiden..." or... "oh man..." and so forth, begin the divine work of Kannagara, how would it be?... Teruhiko-sama, how would it be if I were to become the matchmaker and hold the wedding ceremony with Kiyokohime-sama? Now, Kiyokohime-sama, you too cannot go on living in such a barbaric land while remaining single until the end of time.'

Kiyokohime: 'Yes, thank you very much. Nevertheless, I should like to be allowed to think it over a little.'

Kiyohiko: 'Kiyokohime-sama, do you not care for Teruhiko-sama?'

Kiyokohime: 'No, good heavens, it is not any such reason.'

So saying, she looked down with a tearful expression.

Teruhiko: 'Now, now, Kiyohiko, your kindness is appreciated, but pray say no more of marriage. Kiyokohime-sama does not care for this Teruhiko. Even if we push it through by force, a couple whose horses do not match will surely have to face the lament of a broken mirror later on, so I would have you stop this talk. Regarding that, I would like to ask you to take care of Terukoike-sama as your wife, how about it?'

Kiyohiko: 'That is truly appreciated; nevertheless, I would like to hear Terukoike-sama's opinion. Without that, I cannot give any reply.'

Terukohime: 'Teruhiko-sama's kindness is appreciated, but I somehow... it is not that there is anything specific, but Kiyohiko-sama, I do not like bugs. To speak bluntly, the person in my heart is Teruhiko-sama. If it is you, I should like you to bind the pledge of growing old together and sharing the same grave anywhere.'

Teruhiko: 'This is indeed a great trouble. The truth is, this Teruhiko wishes to bind a marriage promise with Kiyokohime-sama. And yet Kiyokohime-sama says this and that, in a posture as if she dislikes me.'

Covering her face with her sleeve, Kiyokohime said, 'Ho-ho-ho-ho-ho,'

Kiyokohime: 'I truly wish to become a couple with Kiyohiko-sama and perform the divine service of the spirit world. Becoming a couple with Teruhiko-sama gives me a feeling as if our souls do not match.'

Kiyohiko: 'With our objects thus complicated on both sides, it cannot be helped. Well, this is troublesome. If one says this side is liked, the other says it is disliked; if one says this side is disliked, the other says the opposite is liked. This fellow cannot be decided by human power, it seems. If only Izotowake no Mikoto were here, he would make a judgment and settle it for us, but considering that no one is in such a splendid cavern mansion, it appears that the God Izotowe is after having obtained the treasures of Ryūkyū, already departed. Well... how troublesome.'

The four looked at each other in utter dismay and breathlessness, when the voices of two or three people were heard at the entrance of the cave.

Kiyohiko, sharp-eared, heard this and spoke.

Kiyohiko: 'Ah, that voice sounds like Takahime's. I will go and check for a moment, so please wait here together, the three of you.'

Thus he stood at the entrance of the cave already.

Outside, Takahime, Haruhiko, and Tsunehiko were peering fearfully into the cave.

In the moonlight, the faces of the three were clearly visible.

However, from Takahime's side, Kiyohiko's figure could not be seen at all.

Kiyohiko picked up a small stone from nearby, held it in both hands from within, and made a clicking sound as he struck them together.

Takahime: 'It is a terribly large and hollow cave, but I have a feeling as though some beast or other lives right inside here... Tsunehiko, why don't you go inside and check for a moment?'

From within the cave, Kiyohiko went 'Click, click, click.'

Tsunehiko: 'Ha, ha! It looks as though this is a cave where the old raccoon dog of Mount Kachi-Kachi dwells... Hey Haruhiko, why don't you become the scout and go on an exploration?'

Haruhiko: 'The order was given to you. It is only natural that Tsunehiko should enter the raccoon dog's lair. Well, a gentleman stays away from danger. To face danger and fail without even receiving an order would be like having one's efforts eaten by dogs.'

Takahime: 'Haruhiko, you must go in and explore together with it.'

Haruhiko: 'Considering how small this cave is, there is probably no need for two people to go in.'

Takahime: 'Ah, is that so? In that case, since one person is enough, Haruhiko-sama, you are a bold person, so please go in.'

Haruhiko scratched his head,

Haruhiko: 'Heh... Yes.'

And he remained fidgety.

'Click, click, click, click, ooh,' a groaning sound could be heard.

Haruhiko: 'Moshimoshi Takahime-sama, this fellow simply cannot go alone. Just listen to that voice, dozens of fierce beasts are lurking firmly inside. If we dawdle, we will lose everything and end up catching neither game nor bees.'

Takahime: 'That mention of bees reminds me, I hear that fellow somehow received the names Kiyohiko and Teruhiko from Kototama no Ichiwake no Mikoto to become a propaganda emissary, and that he intends to maintain an attitude of opposition toward us to the very end; I wonder where and how he is doing right now.

I hear that Kototama no Ichiwake no Mikoto crossed over to this Ryukyu, obtained the precious jewels of Ryu and Kyu, and with the seven jewels he had hidden himself, plans to carry them over to Takasagojima and become the king of Takasagojima.

In Jitentōjima, the living shrine of the Hinode no Kami, Takahime, stands like a thorn in the eye and prevents her purpose from proceeding smoothly, so with the idea of executing her ambition at a place where Takahime is not present, she stole the precious jewels and left Jitentōjima; therefore, even if Kototama no Ichiwake were to soar through the heavens or burrow through the earth, we must search him out even by parting the grass, take back the precious jewels, and then peel off his mask and make him repent from the bottom of his heart, or else our duty as our lineage will not be fulfilled.

Ah, now that I have grown old, must I once again, over and over, cross the waves of ten thousand ri of ocean and suffer hardships?

This is entirely because an evil guardian deity is possessing the physical body of Kototama no Ichiwake.

...Ah, Kannagara Tamashii Sakiwaese.

Please make the subsidiary guardian deities of Kototama no Ichiwake retreat as quickly as possible, return to the true Yamato spirit, and obey the commands of the Hinode no Kami.'

With a half-weeping voice, she sniffled, put her palms together, and prayed with all her might.

Seeing this behavior, Kiyohiko suddenly found it funny and burst out with 'Pūtsu, pūtsu,' and finally raising his voice,

Kiyohiko: 'Watsuha-ha-ha-ha!'

He laughed and rolled over.

Takahime: 'Who is it? While the living shrine of the Hinode no Kami is praying with all her heart for the sake of the divine realm, who is the one laughing, saying ufu-ha, aha-ha-ha...

Surely it cannot be a raccoon dog.

Who is it?

Well, since it has come to this, Takahime will not let this pass.

If I do not smoke this entrance out with green pine needles and send them to their deaths, I will not let it rest.

...Hey, Tsunehiko-sama, Haruhiko-sama, bring some green things from around there.

Hey, there is a dreadful, aged raccoon dog living here.

When a four-legged creature ages, it starts to use human speech, you see.'

With a reluctant air, taking the hand of Tsunehiko, as if dragging him, together with Takahime she slipped out of this cave, running with all might toward the beach where coral rocks overgrown with strawberries and stone pines lined up while chanting Norito along the ways, committing themselves to the boat they had ridden in, and with all their might headed south and rowed out into the great open sea.
