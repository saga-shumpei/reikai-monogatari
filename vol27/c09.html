Chapter 9: 女 Kami 託宣 [791]

女神託宣〔791〕

§ 1

Kuniyoriwake stands at the entrance of the Kūdō, gazing at the hitokage approaching kokukoku and the light of the kagaribi, and hitorigochi:

Kuniyoriwake: 'That giyōgiyō light of the taimatsu and the ashioto of amata people cannot be a tadagoto. If by manichi this is an invasion of mauaku dojin, no matter how much we apply the shinriki of Kototama, it will not be yōi to make them kaufuku. Although kenbōjiyutsusū is not permitted by Kami, I must take advantage of the oyasumi of Kotoyoriwake-sama, employ a playful trick, use the back way, startle the tens of thousands approaching with a sudden shout, and strike terror into their hearts. Oh, yes, that is it, that is it.'

While nodding, he stands in the darkness of the entrance as if floating vaguely. They have already drawn near. Even in the darkness, it has become possible to distinguish between men and women. Looking at the figure of the person standing at the lead, it is certainly a white-haired old man. Kuniyoriwake suddenly lets out a roar from within the cave like a tiger or wolf howling, and with all his might,

Kuniyoriwake: 'Uts!'

He bellows. The voice echoes inside the cave and becomes an even greater sound. Two or three men outside whisper in low voices,

Man: 'Ya, this is terrible. While we were away at Mount Harisu for the conquest of the Ryūjin, it seems some suspicious tiger, wolf, or perhaps a remnant of the Ryūjin has gotten ahead of us and occupied our natural hotel. If we carelessly enter, it will be a disaster. Oi, what shall we do? Shall we chant the number song and try to conquer it?'

Kuniyoriwake, keenly catching a fragment of their whispering,

Kuniyoriwake: 'Ya, interesting. They are saying it is a tiger, a wolf, or a remnant of the Ryūjin. Very well, I have my own resolve.'

He says to himself, and while filling his whole body with breath, he deliberately shouts back from his side with a voice that sounds as if it contains 'One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, hundred, thousand.' With this voice, the taimatsu that had been flickering among the trees until now go out as if by agreement, and the inside and outside of the cave become pitch black. The attackers were startled and extinguished the fires so that the demon would not be able to find their location. From outside, a fluent voice chanting the heavenly number song could be heard. The whole group joined in, and the voice, echoing through the trees of the forest, seemed to reach even the heavens. From among the crowd, one man advances slightly closer, and while waving a tamagushi to the left and right,

Man: 'Ya, is the one who has taken up residence while we were away a great serpent, a kyōki, or a fierce beast? If you are one who understands language, answer quickly. Or if you are a beast, leave this place as soon as possible. If you are a Kami, state your name.'

Kuniyoriwake felt as if the words were somehow familiar. However, there is no reason why our acquaintances should be on this remote island of Ryūkyū. That voice was certainly a man's. And somehow, the Kototama was sharp. This is certainly nothing to worry about. Now is the time to seize the initiative... thinking this in his heart, and taking advantage of the darkness,

Kuniyoriwake: 'Aru, shiaito, chīchiyābando, jyanjyahēru, sāchiraito, pakkusu, ēru, shīē, pittsu, hōsu.'

Man: 'Ya, this fellow is a dojin from the South Seas who has drifted ashore. He was saying something that sounded like the heavenly number song. Most likely, a believer of the Sanshūkyō from the land of Jyan-na has drifted to this island, found this cave, and is hiding inside. A troublesome fellow has come. I cannot understand the dojin's language, so I wonder what I should say.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'I am the great Ryūjin who has resided on Mount Harisu since the distant age of the gods. Because Kotoyoriwake-no-Mikoto has come from Takamagahara by divine decree to receive the jewels, it has come to pass that I must reluctantly hand over the two jewels of Ryū and Kyū, which I have preserved with great care until now. The divine decree is difficult to defy; I intend to cast aside my attachment and hand them over completely. Once these two jewels leave Ryūkyū, I do not know what will come to pass. If you respect me as the true Kami from now on, offer this cave as my residence, and provide me with the delicacies of the mountains and seas, I shall spare you from disasters of earth and heaven, grant you all peace, happiness, and longevity, and allow you to maintain the joy of the heavenly kingdom forever. What is your answer?'

If Kuniyoriwake is, for example, gold, then this is like copper. Kotoyoriwake: 'I said, can you see or can you not see in the darkness? I am asking whether they are here or not.'

Both men: 'Ha, thank you very much.'

Saying this timidly, they went out to the outer mine. The interior of the mine was illuminated like day by the two torches. Knot-hole windows were open here and there. Smoke appeared to escape through those holes, and not a bit of smokiness was felt.

Kotoyoriwakenomikoto got up, sat properly and straight on a rush mat, looked at the faces of Tsunekusu and Wakahiko, and said, 'Yaa.'

Wakahiko: 'Well, well, Kyōshiyusama, you have graciously come to visit.'

And already he was overcome with tears of joy.

Kotoyoriwake: 'Wakahiko-dono, you have had a hard time. Is this old man the much-talked-about Tsunekusu-okina, a relative of Akihiko and Komahiko?'

Wakahiko: 'Yes, that is correct.'

Tsunekusu: 'Kyōshiyusama, I had been wishing to pay my respects to you once, but speaking of a distant place and being an old man, walking the mountain paths became a hardship, and I ended up neglecting to do so. Thank you for taking such warm care of my sons; I express my gratitude. This time, thinking of it as my final activity like a thousand cavalry in a single horseman, and wishing to repay one-ten-thousandth of the divine grace, I accompanied Wakahiko-sama and came to this island the other day, and we have begun a Kototama battle against the dragon god of Mount Harris. Thanks to that, the violent winds that used to blow furiously every day and every night have also calmed down, and because of that, the natives respect both of us very much, almost like Kami. Wherever we go, just as the light of Hinodewake shines, the divine virtues of the Great Kami extend everywhere, and I cannot suppress my utmost gratitude. As I am a senile old man in every respect, I earnestly beg that you will not abandon me and will command me in whatever is needed.'

Finishing saying this, he wiped his tears of joy with his sleeve.

Kotoyoriwake: 'Just as the Kami showed, with this all four souls are now gathered. The divine powers of Tamaterohiko-sama and Tamaterohime-sama are something one can only be in awe of, now as ever. With our desires finally fulfilled, the acquisition of the precious jewels of Ryūkyū will surely be right before our eyes.'

Tsunekusu: 'By now, up to ninety-nine percent, the dragon god has surrendered. If only one more attachment can be removed, she will probably hand them over. Wakahiko-sama and I have done the absolute best we could to the limit of our abilities, but from now on, there is no other way than to borrow the power of Kyōshiyusama.'

Wakahiko: 'No matter how much it is the divine arrangement, up until this very moment, in a place like this, I never imagined as a non-divine being that I would meet Kyōshiyusama. Ah, human beings are fragile things, indeed. Why, I was even deceived by Kuniyoriwake-sama who is right before my eyes.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Kutsukutsukutsu, utsuputsu.'

Bursting into laughter.

Kotoyoriwake: 'Kuniyoriwake-sama, since you have come to this island, if you do not maintain a quite solemn attitude, they are formidable opponents, so this is no time for prankster ways.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'That is true. Even if I play the prankster in a place like this, it is totally a prank, so from this moment on I will treat such things as a prank, please do not worry.'

Kotoyoriwake: 'What an incorrigible, funny man.'

Wakahiko, shaking his shoulders while holding back his amusement,

'Kiyūkiyū'

he was saying. Tsunekusu remained serious and reserved with an attitude as if to say, 'What is so funny, young fellows are amused even by a chopstick falling over...' At last the night broke. Kotoyoriwakenomikoto, leading Charles, Base, and four or five other natives besides the three of them, proceeded along the valley path of Mount Harris under Wakahiko's guidance.
