Chapter 8: 琉と球 [790]

琉と球〔790〕

§ 1

By the divine word of Kamususanōnōokami and Kunitakehiko, the Daikyōshiyu of Ananai-kyō, the Kamuzukasa Kotoyoriwake, receiving the profound mission of the senmanmuriyō Shinkai, purposely exchanged the jewels, bore the responsibility entirely upon his own single body, and fled from the holy land. Accompanied by Kuniyoriwake, amidst the autumn sky where even the maple leaves glowed, blending into the darkness, he crossed the pass of Nagamiya, climbed over valleys, slipped among the stands of cedar trees, and advanced pointing toward the south. Leaving Tanba Sasayama behind, he bowed in worship toward Takaharuyama, and in a fleeting moment, passing Ikeda and Itami, at last arrived at Akashi. Stopping by the house of a fisher, he purchased a single boat, and both Kuniyoriwake and he, manipulating oars and sculls leisurely, upon the calm waves of the Seto Sea, taking advantage of the dark night, drew near to the roots of the pines where the wish-granting jewel of indestructible vajra, the purple sacred gem, was firmly and eternally embedded off the shore of Takasago. After praying in the dark and meditating silently for a short while, the empty sky suddenly brightened, and in the twinkling of an eye, three small goddesses appeared, and with solemn voices proclaimed thus: 'Thou, the god Kotoyoriwake, as we three goddesses are guarding morning and evening the precious jewel buried earlier, do not trouble your heart concerning this island! By the earliest dawn, advancing purely by the grace of the gods over the vast ocean, set forth until the port of Teru, according to the will of God! The precious divine plan shall be realized and understood hereafter! Ah, may the august spirits be blessed and prosperous, Kamagara! Mimasu!' As one looked upon their speaking, instantly their forms vanished, and white smoke lingered over the ridge of the mountain. Suddenly heavenly music was heard, surrounded on all sides by sweet fragrance, producing an incomparable exhilaration. Kotoyoriwake paid homage to the three radiant goddesses, entrusted his body together with Kuniyoriwake to the boat in which they had come, and leisurely crossed over the great ocean where the waves like fish scales drifted.

Rowing across following the whims of the waves, crying out the Great Spell to the god Wadatsumi at the limit of his voice, 'Ah, may the august spirits be blessed and prosperous, Kamagara!', while parting the raging waves, before he knew it, riding past the rough sea of Seto, he advanced through Uma no Seki, Toshima, Oshima, and the spring islands, crossed Kiyoshima, arrived at the port of Naha in Ryukyu, tied the boat at the seashore, and advanced according to the divine will.

This island is a vast floating island, the foremost in Ryukyu. Compared to that era, in modern times it has almost sunk into the sea, leaving barely one-tenth of its area, but in this era it was an exceedingly vast island. The two men felt somehow as if the secret of divine work was concealed herein, and advanced at the discretion of their feet across the boundless wilderness.

The sun was gradually tilting toward the western mountains, and only the summit of Mount Haris was shining slightly with sunlight.

Kuniyoriwake: 'Daikyōshiyu-sama, somehow when walking on this island, the soles of my feet feel rather numb, you see. I have heard from the elders that there is some mysterious jewel in this place, but that we originally had the thought of going to Hawaii, and yet without knowing it, we came to such a place—might this be for some divine convenience, I wonder?'

Kotoyoriwake: 'Indeed we have business in this island. It cannot be spoken of with too loud a voice, but here the jewel of Ryu and the jewel of Kyu are eternally concealed. That is why it is called Ryukyu. The pearl from the dragon's jaw is in this island. Unless we acquire both of these jewels successfully and cross over to the island of Takasago, true divine work cannot be accomplished.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Heh, that is a serious matter. I wonder if such a thing can indeed be obtained. And do you know the location of those jewels?'

Kotoyoriwake: 'For the most part I know. I have received orders from Kunitakehiko Ōkamisama. The jewel of Ryu is the tide-flowing jewel, and that of Kyu is the tide-ebbing jewel. Carrying each one of these to tour the world, they are sacred treasures such that any demons whatsoever will instantly submit in awe. Look at the summit of that mountain. Although the sun has already set in the western heaven, and the curtain of dusk has already been drawn thickly moment by moment, does that place alone not shine like midday?'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Indeed, hearing it thus, so it is, you see. Why on earth does only that place shine? Could it be that the Hinodewake god has gone ahead and kindly informed us that this is the place?'

此島は琉球一の広大なる浮島である。現代は其時代に比ぶれば殆ど海中に陥没して其面積殆ど十分の一しか残つて居ないが、此時代は随分広大な島であつた。二人は何ともなしに、此処に神業の秘まれあるかの如く感じ、茫々たる荒原を、足に任せて進み行く。

日は漸く西山に傾いて、ハーリス山の頂のみ日光が少しく輝いて居る。

国依別『教主様、何だか此島を歩きますと、足の裏がボヤボヤする様ですなア。何でも此処には不思議な玉があると云ふ事を故老から承はつて居りましたが、布哇へさして行く考へだつたのが、知らず識らずにこんな方へやつて来ましたのは、何かの御都合でせうかなア』

言依別『確かに此島に御用があるのだ。余り大きな声では云はれないが、此処には琉の玉と球の玉とが永遠に隠されてある。それで琉球といふのだ。竜の腮の球と云ふのは此島にあるのだ。此玉を二個共うまく手に入れて、高砂島へ渡らなくては本当の神業は出来ないのだよ』

国依別『ヘー、それは大変ですな。果して左様な物が手に入るでせうか。さうして其玉の在場所はお分りですか』

言依別『大抵分つて居る。国武彦大神様より命令を受けて居るのだ。琉の玉は潮満の玉、球の方は潮干の玉だ。各一個づつ之を携へて世界を巡れば、如何なる悪魔と雖も、忽ち畏服すると云ふ神器である。あの山の頂きを見よ。太陽は既に西天に没し、最早黄昏の帳は刻々に厚く下ろされて来たにも拘はらず、あそこ計りは昼の如く輝いて居るではないか』

国依別『成程、さう承はればさうですなア。どうしてあこ計り光るのでせう。日の出神さまが、先へ廻つて我々に此処だとお知らせ下さるのでせうか』

§ 2

Kotoyoriwake: 'Well, it must be something like that. Since the day has grown quite dark and our bodies have also grown tired, let us take lodging here for the night and set out to climb anew tomorrow morning.'

Saying this, he walked briskly beneath a zelkova tree with a thick trunk of unknown height. Kuniyoriwake followed him in silence as well. Looking closely, at the base of the zelkova, a hollow about five feet high and three feet wide was open. Being an excessively old tree, it was reduced to mere bark, and when one looked inside, it was entirely hollow, with straw-woven mats and the like scattered here and there. The hollow of this tree was nearly fifty tsubo or so in spaciousness. As they advanced deeper and deeper, beautiful straw mats were spread out with a fragrant aroma.

The two of them took off their straw sandals there and lay down; upon looking, they were dry mats that made a rustling sound.

Kuniyoriwake: 'It is a remarkably large tree, is it not? However, since the mats are spread out this neatly, someone must be dwelling here. Because it is dark, I cannot see clearly, but it makes me think that perhaps this place is a human dwelling.'

Kotoyoriwake: 'This place is the hiding place of the King of Ryukyu. Today, depending on circumstances, he has taken numerous retainers and gone out, but if it becomes around the middle of the night tonight, he will surely return, so please try not to be too startled. He is by no means an evil person for us.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Oh, is that so? How is it that you know such a thing?'

Kotoyoriwake: 'All matters are being made known by Kunitakehiko-no-Kamisama through Tamateruhiko and Tamateruhime-no-Mikoto. Something very interesting is going to come forth. Come, from now on let us all together recite the Amatsu Norito, chant even a propagation song, and go to sleep.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Yes, thank you very much. It is somehow a pleasant place, is it not?'

Saying this, he lay down with a thud. The two of them drifted into deep sleep, instantly becoming beings wandering and delighting in the realm of dreams.

Around the hour thought to be the dead of night, when Kuniyoriwake suddenly awoke, a noisy voice was heard striking the outer side of the entrance.

Kuniyoriwake: 'Moshi moshi Kotoyoriwake-sama, terrible footsteps have sounded. Come, please wake up.'

Kotoyoriwake appeared to be fast asleep, and

Kotoyoriwake: 'Kuniyoriwake, won't you go to sleep quickly without making a fuss? Muniya muniya muniya muniya, un un un.'

With a roll, he turned over and began snoring like thunder again. The footsteps were approaching moment by moment. Kuniyoriwake hurriedly stood alone at the entrance and gazed out at the exterior. Countless lights flickered as they wove through the trees, and human voices were noisy and clamorous as they drew near toward this direction.

Kuniyoriwake: 'Ha ha, this fellow appears to be a considerable number of people. We cannot just stay like this. I must devise some plan or other.'

Standing as he was at the entrance, he crossed his arms, tilted his head, and fell into deep thought.
