Chapter 18: 一心同体 [1251]

一心同体〔1251〕

§ 1

The tennin of the Reigoku and Tengoku of Takaamahara are able to communicate their intentions simply and clearly in a mere two or three minutes, far surpassing the eloquent speech for which ningen spend several hours. Moreover, things which ningen cannot adequately express even in manuscripts of several tens of pages can be clearly manifested in their meaning within the space of just a single page, and the tennin who hear or read them are likewise fully able to comprehend them. Since all the language of the tennin is filled with elegance, peace, aizen, and shinshin, no akuma whatsoever can offer any resistance to their words. This is because all the language of Tengoku is filled with zengenbishi. Furthermore, the divine law known as 'interpreting everything in good will, looking anew, listening anew, and proclaiming anew' is practiced there. Next, in the language of the tennin of the Sun realm, which is Tengoku, there are many of the major vowel sounds 'u' and 'o', whereas in the language of the tennin of the Moon realm, which is Reigoku, it is rich in the major vowel sounds 'e' and 'i'. And within all these sounds, there is the emotional movement of love. Words and characters containing goodness mostly use 'u' and 'o', and sometimes slightly use 'a'. Words and characters containing truth have many 'e' and 'i' sounds. And the tennin all possess a uniform language; there is no such inconvenience as that seen among ningen of the present world, where differences arise in their language as they are separated by the East and West, or where there are various dialects from region to region. Nevertheless, the point that differs slightly here is that the language of those who are filled with shōkaku is entirely internal, rich in changes of emotional movement, and contains the greatest number of conceptual elements in thought. The language of those with little shōkaku is external and not so sufficient. As for the language of foolish and honest tennin, it is often external, and just as among ningen mutually, there are times when its significance must be inferred from within the phrases. There are also words made through facial expressions. This language is such that it emits sounds of modulation, pauses, and inflections according to concepts, and there are also those that conclude with it. There are also words that harmonize the representations of the heavenly realm with concepts. There are also words made so that one sees concepts within oneself. There are also words performed with gestures resembling emotional movements. These gestures manifest things similar to those that appear in their phrases. There are also words employing the general original meanings of various emotional movements and various concepts. There are also words like the rumble of thunder, and various other miscellaneous adjectives are employed.

Harumodewake and Ryūkō were guided by the controller of the association and were able to enter a fairly beautiful residence surrounded by various flowers and plants. This place corresponds to the center of this association, and unlike other tennin who live in closely packed houses, it stands isolated as a single dwelling. Speaking in terms of the present world, it is the home of a tennin who serves a role just like a town or village mayor. When the two are guided and proceed to the inner room, a hanging scroll bearing the words 'Truth, Goodness, and Beauty' is displayed, and in the alcove, the transom is decorated with seven treasures, glass and crystal tea utensils and the like are neatly and properly arranged, and a golden kettle is hung over a coral bead brazier. This is an association of tennin whose shōkaku is the most excellent even within the Second Tengoku, and the facial expressions and garments of the master and his wife are particularly superior compared to those of other tennin. Harumodewake is guided timidly into the inner room and sits down neatly in silence. The name of this tennin was Uzuhiko, and his wife was called Uzuhime. Through his acute shōkaku, Uzuhiko perceived the moment he welcomed Harumodewake into his living room that Harumodewake still had a physical body in the present world and had come to tour Tengoku as a spirit by the permission of the gods, for the purpose of ascetic training. Here, Uzuhiko for the first time employed a language within the scope known to Harumodewake, and came to exchange various conversations.

Harumodewake-sama: 'You are still spirits, are you not. I truly thought you were those who had been resurrected in Tengoku, and I treated you with that thought, so you must have been terribly troubled, I suppose.'

'Yes, the truth of the matter is that, bearing the command of Ōkamisama from the mansion of Iso, while on my way to set out in order to bring to peace through Kotomukeyaha the evil spirit of Yamataorochi lingering in the capital of Haruna in the land of Tsuki, on account of my own carelessness at Ukimogahara I fell into the crafty scheme of General Ranch, was dropped into a deep and dark pit, and my spirit became detached from my physical body, wandering before I knew it into Yachimata, guided by the manifestation of Ōkami, and was permitted to have a glimpse into a part of Daisantengoku, and then, guided by Konohanahime, I have climbed up all the way to this place. In any case, being a person lacking in both goodness and truth, and deficient in Chieshiyōkaku, I could not understand the language of the Tennin and was terribly bewildered. It was truly a journey of a mute. Ahahaha! Ta-da!'

'Please, rest comfortably here at the mansion of Uzuhiko. Today happens to be the festival day of Ōkamisama, so before long a group of Tennin will gather together at my mansion. At that time, limited to this group, I shall announce that your spirit has come to dwell here. If I do so, the Tennin of our group will converse with you with that understanding in mind.'

'Yes, thank you very much. In any case, since I am a foolish and dull human being who knows nothing of the ways here...'

'Well, well, Uzuhiko-dono, I have become greatly indebted to your care. Please look after our teacher in every way.'

'No, no, it is by no means I who have taken care of you. Moreover, to be thanked as if I had placed you under obligation causes me great trouble. Whatever we do, we act mechanically, just as we are commanded by Ōkamisama, so if there is even a single thing for which we should give thanks, please thank Ōkamisama directly. All of us merely work through the inner flow of goodness and truth from Ōkamisama. As Tennin, how could we possibly save even a single insect by our own strength?'

Harukuni: 'Indeed, as befits a Tennin of heaven, I have nothing to do but admire how bright you are regarding the truth.'

‘Receiving the inner flow of the divine character of God, we Tennin are truly sent to lead a joyful existence.’

Ryūkō: 'Tell me, Uzuhiko-dono, the Tennin of this group are all young people, are they not? And whichever face one looks at, do they not truly resemble one another remarkably well?'

'Just so. The human countenance is a mirror of the mind, and since those filled with the goodness of love seek out their own kind and dwell together in flocks, those whose inner nature is the same will accordingly have an outer appearance that is also similar. Therefore, in the groups of heaven there are not many unusual persons. Because their hearts are one, their countenances and forms are likewise made in the same mold.'

'I see, I understand that now. However, while there seem to be plenty of children, elders with countenances over thirty are not to be found at all; are they accommodated in some nursing home in heaven, or something?'

'The human spirit is ageless and immortal. Since a Tennin is simply a human being whose development has advanced upward, the human heart becomes for the most part completely perfected around thirty years of age for a man and around twenty years of age for a woman, and although the physical body may grow old and frail, that heart will surely never weaken anywhere along the way. Nay, it undoubtedly becomes ever more precise and clear. Since heaven is entirely a world of thought and all things are spiritual, no matter how much of an elder one may have been in the manifest world, once one becomes a resident of heaven, one bears such countenances and fleshly forms as about thirty years of age for men and about twenty years of age for women, just as you see. For that reason, it is called ageless and immortality in heaven, and the suffering of the hateful old age, illness, and death simply does not exist.'

‘Indeed, I am truly impressed. Even if we were somehow to easily detach our physical bodies, it is a difficult thing for us to become residents of heaven. We are bound to be Yachimata humans lingering forever in Chūū. Truly, illumined by your brilliance, Harukuwake cannot bear his shame.'

Uzuhiko: 'Aha-ha-ha-ha.'

Harukuni: 'Utsu-hu-hu-hu.'

Tatsukō: 'Oho-ho-ho-ho.'

'Hey Tatsukō, making an Oho-ho-ho-ho sound, pursing your lips like that and making a woman's voice, isn't that rather unsightly?'

'Due to the Gonairiu of the Goshinkaku of Konohanahime, by the correspondence of good and truth, I was instantly Shinkakukuwa'd, and although Tatsukō knows nothing of it, the internal Shinon simply manifested externally. Otsu-ho-ho-ho.'

Attracted by the laughter of the three, Uzuhime, who had been in the Katsutemoto, appeared at this place, served her hands before the three, and said,

'Enrai guests, you have come so well. I am called Uzuhime, the wife of Uzuhiko.'

Harukuni: 'I do not know what kind of Goaisatsu to offer, as I am completely unacquainted with the state of Tengoku; yet, as I have just heard from Uzuhiko-sama, since similar qi seeks one another, we are in such a state of harmony; if so, your heart is my heart, my heart is your heart, I cannot make formal Goaisatsu either, and if you are the same as myself, I do not know how to make Goaisatsu toward myself either, and truly I am at a loss.'

'Yes, I am exactly the same. Let us stop worldly false courtesies and false formalities, and becoming ten-thousand-year confidants, nay, of one mind and one body, let us open our hearts to one another and let me hear your wondrous stories.'

'Really, stories of the earthly world are filled with sin, falsehood, and filth, and having come to such a pure Tengoku, I have grown sick of even placing them on my lips. Rather than that, I wish to hear stories of Tengoku.'

'Yes, if we obtain the permission of Izanagi, no matter how much you insist it is noisy, I cannot tell what kind of things I might utter. Like an arrow released from a bowstring, it will likely not stop until it hits the target it is meant to hit, hotsu-ho-ho-ho.'

Tatsukō: 'Listen Uzuhime-sama, except for your clothing being different from Uzuhiko-sama's, aren't your faces exact duplicates? Surely you were not born as twins in the earthly world, were you?'

'Hey Tatsukō, what a rude thing to say. Please restrain yourself a little.'

'Even so, it simply floated into my mind. Isn't Tengoku a place where one says what one thinks and does what one aims for? Making such outward pretenses and decorating with falsehoods in the earthly-world style would surely find no use in Tengoku. Because Tengoku takes the truth of trust as its light.'

'Oh, I am humbled, aha-ha-ha-ha, once you come out to Tengoku, Harukuniwake is full of failures too. When it comes to this, a pure and innocent Tatsukō-sama is truly a precious thing.'

'Ah that is just so, truly a pure thing it must be. Hotsu-ho-ho-ho.'

'Is this again the Gonairiu of the Goshinkaku of Konohanahime?'

'This is the Guwaibun of Tatsukō's deputy guardian. Listen Uzuhiko-sama, pray if there is any way my present words might pollute Tengoku, I will correct my pronouncement right away.'

'If you take it as burlesque, even harsh words and violent language will instantly transform into good words and beautiful phrases through that laughter, so do not worry. Is there ever a case in Tengoku where burlesque, jest, and cheerful voices cannot be spoken? Burlesque, jest, and cheerful cries are the flowers of Tengoku.'

'Oh wonderful, Sensei, with this I too can breathe a little easier.'

'Mm, that is true, somehow my body feels as though it is tightening up, and no matter what I do I cannot become of such a carefree mood as you.'

'That is indeed so, for attachments to Shiyaba still remain within you. You must have a desire to return to the Nikutai once more. Do you remember what I said in Daisantengoku—that I no longer wish to return to Shiyaba and desire to stay right here? Even if I were to return to Genkai once more, I am of the Setsunashin, you see. I lament not the kuwako, hope not for the mirai, and have attained the truth of Ananai-kyō that this very shiyunkan is the bunsuirei of zenakuseijiya, you see.'

'It is truly a horidashimono, is it not? Sensei could not have imagined that Tatsukō's shiyōkaku had opened up to this extent. That is precisely why people say even in Genkai that you cannot judge a person by their appearance.'

'Yes, arigata, I humbly ask for your kind favor in all matters.'

'Merely saying it with your lips won't do, you know. Do you arigata think it from the bottom of your heart, or does a slight feeling of keibu toward Tatsukō still flash in the depths of your heart?'

'Ah, I am filled with awe; you must surely be the Ōkamisama.'

'I am not the Ōkamisama. The divine character of the Ōkamisama fills my spirit, and through the mouth of Tatsukō, it is admonishing Harukuniwake, you see. By the way, Uzuhiko-sama, you and your wife bear facial features as strikingly similar as twins; I should like you to explain the reason for that, if you please.'

'Husbands and wives are established through the harmony of love and trust. That is to say, the husband's intellect enters deeply into the wife's will, and the wife's will enters deeply into the husband's intellect, and here for the first time the marriage of heaven takes place. So to speak, husband and wife are of one mind and one body, so the similarity of facial features is an unavoidable state according to the principle of correspondence. The marriage of Genkai people is contracted based on status, fame, worldly prestige, or the amount of property, so to speak, it is a false marriage. Since all marriages in heaven are spiritual marriages, husband and wife are in a state of inseparable closeness. Therefore, in heaven, a husband and wife are not counted as two, but as one. Unlike in Genkai, there is no trouble of counting so many males and so many females in a population registry. If one simply speaks of one or two, one understands that as one couple or two couples. Therefore, when they speak of a hundred people in heaven, there are two hundred heads. This is the point of difference from Genkai. The true reality of ruler and subject in unity, husband and wife as one body, and superiors and inferiors in harmony can surely never be truly witnessed anywhere but in heaven. When Harukuniwake-sama and Tatsukō-sama descend to Genkai, I would earnestly ask you to make the earthly world create a heavenly mood, even if only in some part.'

Harukuniwake: 'Yes, to the extent that my feeble power allows... No, no, I shall have my very self used by the divine character of the Kami. Ah, Tamakushimare-futatabi-masu.'

As they conversed thus, a man appeared and came forth from the entranceway,

'Uzuhiko-sama, preparations for the festival are complete; come, everyone is waiting. Please proceed to the shrine.'

'Ah, thank you for your trouble. Let us go immediately. Both of you, what do you say to joining the festival of heaven from here on and offering your worship...'

'We shall accompany you.'

'The festival of heaven must surely be magnificent. I wonder if Tatsukō may be permitted to accompany you as well.'

'Yes, please do so.'

Harukuniwake: 'If it were not permitted, would it not be fine to worship through the inner flow of Konohanahime's divine character, haha-ha-ha!'

Tatsukō: 'Oo-oh-ah.'

Uzuhiko: 'Tatsukō-sama, please do accompany us.'

Tatsukō: 'Yes, arigata.'
