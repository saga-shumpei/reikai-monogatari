Chapter 2: 黒士会 [1235]

黒士会〔1235〕

§ 1

Harukuniwake, having suffered an unexpected Fukaku, while tending to Tatsukō, said,

Tatsukō: 'Oi Tatsukō, you were not injured anywhere, were you? I have suffered a terrible Fukaku and have fallen deeply.'

Tatsukō: 'Hai, Arigata-u gozaimasu, I have not sustained any Kega anywhere, but having been lured into such a deep scheme and dropped into a deep hole, I feel nothing but Fukuwai. Aha-ha-ha-ha.'

Harukuniwake: 'Ufu-fu-fu-fu, you too have become quite Ananahikyōshiki. No matter what kind of Kannan you encounter, it is no good unless you maintain that Taido.'

Tatsukō: 'Ana arigata-ya, Ana totoshi-ya, the Kamisama of Ananai-kyō; as expected, Brahmanism is the opposite of Ananai-kyō, it is Anaarikyō, is it not.'

Harukuniwake: 'Oi, is it not useless to remain in Chitsukiyo in a place like this forever? Why do you not try to devise a way to crawl up now that you have had enough Kagen?'

Tatsukō: 'That is true. Fortunately, they have set up a Takusan of spears, and as they have spread a net to wrap around our Karada like a Kumo no su, shall I pull out all the spearheads, tie them together, and make a Hashigo of spears to climb up? If we dawdle, that fellow Ark will bring a Takusan of Kobun, and if he makes it rain spears from above, we shall be in trouble.'

Harukuniwake: 'Naani, at that time, since there are this many spears, we can just make it rain spears from below to above. Let us rest for a while at the bottom of this empty well where the wind does not blow and then climb up. By the way, is there not someone at the edge of the hole?'

Tatsukō: 'That fellow is a man called Tar. He is quite a fool, but since he is a good-natured human, he is a creature that leans whichever way. How would it be if we were to Kototama-influence him?'

Harukuniwake: 'Try doing it for your first battle; I, Harukuniwake, will watch Tatsukō's Kototama battle from here...'

Tatsukō said,

Tatsukō: 'Hai, Arigata-u.'

And while saying so, he looked up at the sky and said,

Tatsukō: 'I, Tatsukō, secretary to General Katahiko of the vanguard of Brahmanism, now formally command you, Tar. As you know, this Tatsukō, having been crushed in one defeat by Harukuniwake at Kajika Pass and fleeing with the entire army, could not bear to see the pathetic remnants of his allies, so I devised a plan. Together with Matsukō, I feigned surrender to Harukuniwake and led him here. Lower a rope ladder or something immediately and rescue me. If you do, I shall report to General Lunch that you are a man of greater merit than Ark. How is that, Tar? You must be in awe of my divine strategy and demonic schemes, are you not?'

Tar: 'Hai, hai, not knowing that was the case, I have truly been very rude. Come, please come up. Fortunately, there is a rope ladder here, so I will lower it now. Please, only you come up. And what has become of Harukuniwake?'

Tatsukō: 'There is no longer any need to concern yourself with Harukuniwake. You only need to lower the rope ladder.'

Tar: 'That is truly a pitiable, or perhaps not so pitiable, situation. Have you been turned into an Imo-sashi? Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haema Se.'

While saying this, he lowered the rope ladder into the dark pit. Harukuniwake climbed up the rope ladder, tonton, one step after another.

Tar: 'Yaa, Tatsukō-sama, ah, how wonderful, how wonderful, it is best of all that you were not injured. Please, forgive my rudeness.'

Harukuniwake: 'You called Tar, I am not Tatsukō. I am Harukuniwake.'

Tar: 'Yaa, this is, truly, I cannot say anything at all. Man-man-man, congratulations. And what has become of Tatsukō?'

Harukuniwake: 'Un, Tatsukō has become well-situated. Well, he is safe.'

Tar: 'That is, well, a pitiful thing to have happened. A Takusan of blood must have come out, I suppose.'

Harukuniwake: 'Un, before long he will become a Yūrei, light a blue fire from the bottom of the well, and come along saying hiyū.'

§ 2

At this moment, Tatsukō was already climbing up towards the mouth of the well, having ascended about nine-tenths of the way. Overhearing the conversation of the two, he suddenly turned into a ghostly mood, rolling his eyes round and round, opening his mouth wide, letting his tongue hang out, making his hips limp, and thrusting his hands forward powerlessly and limply,

'How resentful I am!'

he squeezed out in a strange voice. Tāru let out,

'Eek!'

falling flat on his rear on the spot, and with his mouth wide open, crying,

'Ah, ah, ah, ah, ah!'

he was trembling.

'Hahaha, hey, Tāru-san, it's a lie, it's a lie. Tatsukō is just playing a trick. Hey Tatsukō, even a ghost doesn't play from early in the morning, you know!'

'Hey, Tāru, to tell the truth I am sorry, but everything I said from the bottom of the well was a lie. Since I was dropped into a place like hell, I spoke of pretenses suitable for hell. Now, compared to the bottom of the well, we have climbed up to this flat ground which is comparable even to heaven, so I cannot speak lies and pretenses. Come, from here on, guide me towards General Ranch's mansion.'

'Ah, because of that I too am relieved for just a moment. However, rather than going to such a place, would it not be possible for me to accompany you as well and have you help Sendenshi-sama escape? Oh my, Harukuniwake-sama and whatever your name is, I am truly saying nothing bad; before long Ark will come leading a large army, and without a doubt he will come to capture you. Ah, please turn back quickly. In exchange, I will let myself accompany you as well.'

'Hahaha, there is no such thing as seeing the enemy, rolling up the flags, putting away the spears, and retreating; the Misrokai has no retreat towards its objective. There is only moving straight ahead.'

Just as they were talking thus, the one who came riding astride a horse and taking the lead was Ark. Leading dozens of knights, Ark rode abreast and scattered to surround Harukuniwake's party,

'Harukuniwake of the Misrokai, or whatever your name is, now that it has come to this, you can be no more. Come, straightforwardly put your hands behind your back and submit to the bonds. I shall lead you before the presence of General Ranch,'

he shouted in a loud voice.

Harukuniwake remained calm and said,

'Well, Ark, or whatever your name is, thank you for the welcome; Harukuniwake has come forward of his own accord to meet General Ranch face to face, even without your demand. Do not worry at all, I shall neither run nor hide.'

Saying such things, you intend to make us let down our guard, watch for an opening, and make a run for it. That trick won't work on me. Hey, men of my command, capture the rebels, starting with Harukuniwake, and put ropes on them together with Tatsukō,'

he commanded. As Harukuniwake calmly chanted the Heavenly Number Song, the knights of the entire company had their bodies stiffened and were utterly unable to do anything, and with a patter-patter they began to tumble to the ground from horseback like camellia flowers falling in the rain. Ark also fell headlong from horseback, lying flat on his back in the shape of the character for 'big' at the feet of Harukuniwake, and stretched out flat. Harukuniwake turned to Tatsukō and had him chant the Heavenly Number Song. Feeling somewhat anxious in his heart, Tatsukō did his utmost and chanted the Heavenly Number Song about twice. Wondrously, the knights of the entire company suffered not the slightest injury, their stiffened bodies returned to normal, and nimbly mounting their horses once more, whipping their steeds, with all their might they fled back towards the encampment like a gale. Remaining behind was Ark alone; somehow or other, he had no freedom in his body.

'O God, I thank You humbly. That a scoundrel like me should chant the precious number song and immediately manifest its miraculous efficacy is entirely, I believe, the grace and majesty of God. It is by no means the power of Tatsukō. I earnestly beg that from now on You will use my body ever more deeply. In this connection, this Ark alone, having yet to receive the divine virtue of Kototama, remains in this state of stiffness. I earnestly beg that You will save even me through my mouth. Ah, Almighty and Omnipresent Deities, bless us!'

With itsushiyōkenmei, he gatsushiyō. No matter how much one prayed or performed kazūta, Āku's kiyōchiyokujiyōtai did not return to its former state.

Harukuniwake-sama: 'What shall we do, I wonder if the Kamisama do not forgive Āku hitori?'

Harukuniwake-sama: 'Well, this Āku intended to inflict kigai upon Harukuniwake, so if setsushiya does not perform kiguwan, it will likely be dame.'

So saying, continuing ankimokutau for a while, waiting for the time when the invasion and jiuitsu of shinkaku filled the zenshin... as he pronounced a single word: 'Forgive'... strange to say, Āku's karada returned to its former state. Āku knelt before Harukuniwake, shedding tears, and apologized for his jiyūjiyū burei.

Āku-dono: 'You have gone through such taihen honeo. However, thanks to you, Harukuniwake is not bearing even a single scratch, so setsushiya holds no feeling of resentment toward you. Nay, rather, I think of it as gokeikoku from the Kamisamisama and am kanshiya. The Kamisama must have used your hand to show this Harukuniwake that yudan is taiteki. If so, you are the sole dōshi to me. I am greatly kanshiya. Now, Āku-dono, you seem to be a personage holding a fairly considerable position within the Brahman army as well. You must surely have business in the camp. Hurry back and tell Harukuniwake that he will visit General Ranchi for an interview forthwith.'

Āku: 'Hai, there are no words to express it. However, from here on, obeying your command, I shall appear before General Ranchi, state the doctrines of Izuwake, and arrange for him to surrender before you at the earliest possible moment. Then, please excuse me.'

Quicker than it is said, he straddled his horse, gave it a single whip, and headed back toward the camp like clouds and mist.

Yotahiko: 'Ha-ha-ha-ha, at last, General Āku has caved in. But my word, he went off talking big about making General Ranchi repent, but that fellow is dame too. 『貴様にはテンデ話が出来ないワ。国士会と云つたら、国家を憂ふる志士の団体だ』

§ 4

Whether it is a lion, a tiger, a wolf, a leopard, or a rat, I know not, but surely it is not a decent band of fellows gathering together; when I hear that Āku is the hotsutōnin, is it not that one can hardly place any shinyō in it? And has some shiyuishiyo of the society even been formed?

First, Āku-sama, the chiyōhonnin of the Fuheitau, is the shiyushiyōshiya, and we are the sanjiyoin. Just haidoku this shiyuishiyo for a chiyotsuto.

Thus speaking with tokuige, he torida a small insatsubutsu from his bosom and showed it. Tatsukō took it in his hands, and when he read down the shiyuishiyo, the following bunshiyō was written:

Shiyuishiyo

Though the kokuji is not in a critical day, there is no kenishiya in the world whom tenkaichinin should chiyōjiyū; the ihayuru men of gaisei, whenever they open their mouths, speak of the henkuwa of shisau, and preach that to deal with shisau one must use shisau. Although their words are not fuka, such manzen chiushiyōron has no sunkau at this juncture. Much less when kōtau and public figures lead each other on to deceive the world, to deceive themselves, and, in their haste only to protect themselves, do not hesitate to expose the inferiority of their mental disposition, thereby increasingly encouraging the henkuwa of shisau. We subjects would rather think that the standing of one man of chivalry is preferable to a hundred debaters. Now, to rush into peril is the true character of a man of chivalry; in the large, the perils of the realm and state, and in the small, the perils of a single region and a single individual, the men of our party will by no means shirk, but desire to fling their bodies forward and rescue them. If there should be a case that the men of our party once stand up and fail to resolve, that would be nothing but the disgrace of the chivalrous way. Why is this? Because our Kokushikai does not stand unless the name is righteous; as shown in the ten oaths, we act entirely guided by the chivalrous way, and we hereby boldly proclaim this to the world.

Year Month Day

Kokushikai

Ten Rules

1. A man of chivalry prides himself on being a Brahman male.

2. A man of chivalry takes rushing into peril as his true character, provided that it depends on time, place, and station.

3. A man of chivalry swears never to proclaim an unnamed war. However, if it is the command of General Ranch, he dares not decline.

4. A man of chivalry expects to be loyal by planning on behalf of his opponent. However, this shall not apply to the Sanjuu Teaching.

5. A man of chivalry possesses no original enemy, and therefore transcends victory and defeat. (The action of our army in the Kototama warfare at Kajika Pass is a fitting example thereof.)

6. A man of chivalry does not regret a single promise even if it is worth a single death, provided it is limited to the woman he loves best.

7. A man of chivalry places spirit as primary and form as secondary. However, while in the midst of the Brahman army, there may be times when this is not applied.

8. A man of chivalry does not pursue the past but believes in the future, except that the end of Daikokunushi, the great ridgepole of the Brahman teaching, is not necessarily bright.

9. A man of chivalry is not ashamed before heaven for a meaningless day, except that at the time of a drinking feast, even if it be for three or four days, he feels no shame therefor.

10. A man of chivalry is satisfied if he possesses a single bosom friend, provided that if it is of the opposite sex, it is deemed most excellent.

"Oh my, you are lining up such splendid things, but are not the proviso clauses utterly hopeless? This is why they say the Brahman style cannot be depended upon. After all, they hoist a sheep's head and sell dog meat."

"This is the most excellent means of the art of worldly survival in modern times. It is something that pierces to the true marrow of the Brahman teaching; without this, one cannot cross through the world."

"Ahahahaha, say, Sensei, how is it? Does not the Kokushikai also say quite eccentric things?"

"Mm, it is splendid. It is an unfeigned confession of the Brahmans. Ah, I am truly impressed."

"If it were me, I would not enter such a society. I would say, 'Excuse me...' and be off."

"Hahahahaha, Dora, let's go. Shall we have Taru-sama guide us? Nay, honored sanjiyoin of the Kokushikai, I pray you lead the way."

Tatsukō: 'Long live the Kokushikai members, ahahahaha.'

Laughing and making merry thus, the two, Chigokuwake and another, pointing toward the jinya of the Floating Tree Village, singing a propaganda song, tread upon the morning dew and vigorously press forward.

趣意書

国事日に非なれども、天下一人の聴従すべき権威者なし、所謂慨世の士、口を開けば思想の変化を言ひ、思想に対するには思想を以てせざる可らざるを説く、其言や不可なしと雖も、漫然たる抽象論は此際寸効なし。況んや公党公人相率ゐて世を欺き、己を欺き、只自ら守るに急にして、心術の陋劣を暴露して憚らず、益々思想の変化を助長しつつあるに於ておや。吾々国民は寧ろ百人の論客よりも一人の志士の立つべきを思ふ。それ難に赴くは士の本領なり、大にしては天下国家の難、小にしては一地方一個人の難、吾党の士は苟くも辞せず、身を挺して之を救はむことを欲す。もし吾党の士一度立つて解決せざる案件あらば、そは士道の汚辱たらむのみ。何とならば吾国士会は名正しからざれば、断じて立たず、誓約十則に示すが如く、悉く士道に率由して行動すればなり、敢て天下に宣す。

国士会

十則
