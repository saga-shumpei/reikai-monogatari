Chapter 15: 公義正 Way [1248]

公義正道〔1248〕

§ 1

In the innermost first heaven, the thoughts, emotions, and speech of the heavenly beings dwelling therein are by no means perceptible to the heavenly beings of the middle heavens; for why is this? Because all things pertaining to the beings of the innermost heaven surpass and transcend the phenomena of the middle heavenly realm. Nevertheless, when it pleases the heart of the Ōkami, the heavenly beings of the middle heavens look up and gaze high into the upper heaven, beholding high in the firmament a radiance like unto a flame. Furthermore, the thoughts, emotions, and speech of the heavenly beings in the middle heaven are as if they were of the nature of luminous light, such that they may be beheld even by the heavenly beings of the lowest strata.

That radiance shines forth, assuming a myriad of miscellaneous colors, and at times appears as clouds. Beginning with the patterns of those clouds and that radiance, both above and below, and reflecting upon their forms, one is able to remotely comprehend to a certain degree the state in which the various heavenly beings in the upper heaven are discoursing. The uppermost heaven is perfectly and amply endowed, with divine light shining and pervading throughout; compared to the middle heavenly realm, the degree of its perfection is vastly higher. Next, when descending to the lowest heaven, upon reaching it, that degree adds a yet lower measure. Moreover, the form of the first heaven (Kōten), by means of the internal flow issuing from the Kami, perpetually exists for the sake of the second heaven (Oten).

To comprehend the form of Takamanohara down to its minute details, or to apprehend how this form operates in what manner of condition and how it circulates, is that which even the heavenly beings currently in heaven are unable fully to accomplish. If one were to compare this, when a person of utmost wisdom, richly endowed with the divine intellect, inspects the forms of various matters within the human body and reflects upon them by way of deduction, they might perhaps be able to comprehend the general outlines concerning that form of Takamanohara. The overall form of Takamanohara appears to resemble a single human body. Moreover, all things within the human body correspond universally to the things of Takamanohara. Therefore, how difficult the form of Takamanohara is to comprehend and explain as a human being may be roughly perceived if one looks upon the nerves and fibers that connect the various human parts. What indeed are these nerve fibers, and how do they operate and circulate within the brain? No matter what medical doctor he may be, they are not things that can be seen with the naked eye or through a microscope. Within the human brain there are innumerable fibers, and judging from the manner in which they cross and where they gather, they indeed resemble a single soft and connected mass; yet it is a matter of course that individual and separate activities arising from volition and intellect are all carried out by means of these fibers. That all these fibers exist within the physical body and how they mutually bind together and become active is clear when one observes various central organs, for example, the heart, lungs, stomach, intestines, and other things. Also, in medical science, if one looks at the bundles of nerves referred to as ganglia, numerous fibers come from their respective local parts to gather here, intersect here, and after being connected in various ways, they depart from here once more, and existing outwardly, each fulfills its own faculty. And such a phenomenon does not cease with a single occurrence. This is likewise the case with the various viscera, various limbs, and various muscles. When one examines these matters and their numerous wonders with the eyes of an enlightened seer, there is nothing to do but marvel in awe at their profound, subtle, and delicate operations. Yet the above is merely a most meager, partial observation visible to the naked eye. As for things hidden within the interior of the natural world and lying beyond the reach of human vision, they enshroud a yet greater degree of wonder. That the aforementioned bodily form corresponds to the form of Takamanohara is clear even from seeing that the intellect and volition which exist within that form and work thereby make their outbreak in relation to all things. For if a human being has something he resolves in his will, it all spontaneously breaks forth upon this form. Furthermore, if a human being whatsoever has something he reflects upon, his thoughts cannot but circulate from the initial point of outbreak to the extremities upon the nerve fibers, and from this there arises that which is termed sensation. And since this form is presently the form of thought and will, it may well be said to be also the form of wisdom and enlightened perception. Therefore, it can be known that the form of the heavenly realm corresponds to the activities of all the faculties in the human body. Moreover, one can know that the emotions and thoughts of the heavenly beings all follow this form and spontaneously extend themselves, and as long as those heavenly beings remain within this form, they are in a state of wisdom and enlightened perception. However, the form of Takamanohara is such that even its general principles cannot be sufficiently investigated; it was for the sake of making the scientific omnipotence-believers of the natural world aware of this that the human body was introduced by way of example.

高天原の形式を、其細目に亘つて了解する事や、又此形式が如何なる情態に活動し、如何に流通するかを会得するのは、現在天国にある天人と雖も能くし得ざる所である。これを譬ふるならば、最も聰明にして神の智慧に富んだ人が、人体に於ける種々の事物の形態を検査し、これより推して考へる時は、高天原の其形式に関して、或は其大要を悟り得る事が出来るであらう。高天原の全体の形式は、一個の人身に似て居る様である。又人身中に於ける万の事物は総て高天原の事物に相応するものである。故に高天原の形式が如何に人間として解し難く、又説明し難きかは、人間各部を連結する所の神経や繊維を見たならば、略察知する事が出来るであらう。是等の神経繊維は抑何物なるか、又如何にして脳髄中に活動し流行し居るかは、如何なる医学博士と雖も、肉眼を以て、或は顕微鏡をもつて見得るものではない。人間の頭脳中には無数の繊維があつて、交叉する様や其集まれる所より見れば、実に柔かき連絡した一つの固まりに似て居るけれど、意性及び智性よりする所の個々別々の活動は、皆此繊維によつて行はれて居る事は無論である。総て是等の繊維が肉体中にあつて、如何にして相結束し活躍するかは種々様々の中枢機関、例へば心臓肺臓胃腸、其他のものを見れば明かである。又医学上に於て、神経節と呼ばれて居る神経の束を見れば、数多の繊維が各其局部より来つて此処に集まり、茲に交雑し、又種々に連結したる後、再び此処を出で往き、外にあつて各其官能を全うするものである。而して斯の如きもの一再にして止まらない。又各臓腑や各肢体各筋肉にあつても此通りである。証覚者の目を以て是等の事物と其数多の不可思議とを考査する時は、唯々其幽玄微妙なる活動に驚嘆するの外はないのである。併しながら以上は肉眼にて見得る所のほんの僅少の部分的観察に過ぎないのである。其自然界の内面にかくれて、吾人の視覚の及ばない所にある物に至つては、更に一層の不可思議を包んで居るのである。以上の身体上の形式の、高天原の形式と相応すると云ふ事は、其形式の中にあり、之によつて働く所の智性と意性とが、万般に対し発作するを見ても明かである。人間が其意に決する所があれば、皆自らにして此形式の上に発作するからである。又人苟くも何事か思惟する所があれば、其想念は最初の発作点より末端に至つて神経繊維の上に環流せざるはなく、是よりして茲に感覚なるものがある。さうして此形式はやがて想念と意思との形式である故に、又智慧と証覚との形式なりと云つてもよいのである。故に天界の形式は、人体に於ける総ての諸官能の活動に相応するものなる事を知り得らるるのである。又天人の情動と想念とは悉く此形式に従つて、自ら延長するものなる事を知り、彼等天人はこの形式の内にある限り、智慧と証覚とに居るものなる事を知り得らるるのである。併しながら高天原の形式は、其大体の原則すら充分に探究すべからざる事を、自然界の科学万能主義者に知らさむために、人間の身体を例に引いて見たのである。

§ 2

Just as there are three degrees in Takaamahara, so too are there three degrees in the life of each tennin. Those who dwell in the highest, first Tengoku and Reigoku have their third degree—that is, the innermost degree—opened, while the intermediate tenkai and lowest tenkai are closed. Those who dwell in the intermediate tenkai have only their second degree opened, while the upper ten and lower ten are closed. Those who dwell in the lowest-layer tenkai have only their first degree opened, while the intermediate tenkai and upper tenkai remain closed. Therefore, if a tennin of the upper tengoku were to look down upon a collective body of the middle tengoku and converse with them, the third degree possessed by the upper tennin would instantly become closed. Along with that closure, even their spiritual discernment would perish. The reason for this is that the spiritual discernment of a tennin of the upper tengoku dwells in the third degree and does not reside in the first and second degrees. As it is shown in the sacred words of Izu-no-Mitama:

1. Let him who is upon the housetop not go down to take what is in his house. Let him who is in the field not return to take his garment.

2. In that day, if a man is on the housetop, let him not go down to take it even if his utensils are in the room. Likewise, let him who is in the field not return.

What is thus indicated are the words revealing the secret meaning above. Furthermore, from the lower-layer tenkai to the upper-layer tenkai, there is no influx of divine character. This is because it opposes the order of God. One might well praise God under the name of Order. Therefore, from the upper tenkai toward the lower tenkai, there is an influx. And the spiritual discernment of a tennin of the upper tenkai exceeds that of a tennin of the lower tenkai in a proportion of ten thousand to one. This is also the reason why a tennin of the lower tenkai cannot converse with a tennin of the upper tenkai. Even if a tennin of the lower tenkai should look up and yearn, they cannot see their form at all; it merely appears as though clouds were still hanging over the head of the upper tenkai. Contrary to this, a tennin of the upper tenkai can see a tennin of the lower tenkai. However, they cannot converse with them. If there were ever an occasion to converse with a lower-tenkai person, they would instantly lose their spiritual discernment. Although the tennin among the various collective bodies in Takaamahara all dwell equally in goodness and truth, there are various degrees in their spiritual discernment; therefore, as an inevitable reason, a system of governance is also established in Takaamahara. No matter what, the various tennin must observe that order. And all matters concerning order can by no means be disrupted. Furthermore, the system of governance in Takaamahara is by no means uniform. Individual systems are established in each respective collective body. A kind of governing system is likewise established in the collective body that constitutes the spiritual land—that is, the Land of the Moon—governed by the Great Deity of Izu-no-Mitama. As the official duties of each collective body differ, it is inevitable that there are also discrepancies in their systems. However, in Takaamahara, apart from the system of mutual love, there is no other thing that can be called a system.

The governing system in Takaamahara is called the True Way. Everything that is practiced while abiding in the virtue of love and goodness toward the Great Deity is called the True Way. This governing system belongs solely to the Great Deity. As for the various truths—which may also be called principles of public righteousness—whereby the Great Deity personally guides the tennin of the various tenkai and teaches them the affairs of life, they are so clearly memorized in the hearts of each tennin that there is no tennin who does not know them, perceive them, and feel them. Therefore, matters concerning public righteousness do not become the seed of disputes; however, matters concerning the True Way—that is, matters concerning practical execution by each tennin—sometimes become subjects of doubt. When such matters concerning the True Way arise, those with less spiritual discernment correct them through tennin superior to themselves, or else ask teachings directly of the Great Deity to settle their outcome. Therefore, for the tennin, to subsist purely in accordance with the True Way and just as the Great Deity guides them constitutes their own tenkai, and is their supreme and secret joy and delight. Next, the system in the Reigoku—that is, the Moon's August Land—of the Great Deity is called public righteousness. This is because the various tennin of Reigoku dwell in spiritual goodness. Spiritual goodness refers to the virtue of benevolence toward one's neighbor. And its reality is truth. Truth belongs to public righteousness, while goodness belongs to the True Way. To have spoken of the Moon's Land here does not mean the lunar globe currently seen by human beings on earth. It is a spiritual realm constituted by the divine character of God. Although the various tennin dwelling in this realm are likewise guided and governed by the Great Deity, because it is not direct, a ruler comes to be established here. The number of such rulers is provided according to the needs of their respective affiliated collective bodies. Moreover, laws are enacted here, and the various tennin live together in groups in accordance with them. The rulers are tasked with regulating numerous matters by means of those laws. Furthermore, since these tennin all possess spiritual discernment, they understand those laws well; and should there be the slightest doubt, the Great Deity descends and gives them a clear interpretation. The governance conducted through goodness, such as that found in Tengoku—that is, the Sun's Land—is called the True Way, and the governance conducted through truth, such as that found in Reigoku—that is, the Moon's Land—is called public righteousness. The rulers of the respective collective bodies of Tengoku and Reigoku are by no means putting on airs of self-importance like the rulers of various nations in the modern age; rather, they are filled with condescension and the virtue of humility. Furthermore, they place the welfare of their collective body and the affairs of their neighbor first, and put their own welfare last. Nevertheless, those rulers possess extraordinary honor and glory. These rulers are self-aware that the glory and honor given to them are entirely bestowed by the Great Deity, and knowing that it is solely due to the divine majesty of the Great Deity that other tennin submit to them, they naturally possess the virtue of humility and do not act haughty.

原文（近代文語）

高天原には三つの度ある如く、各天人の生涯にも亦、三つの度があつて、最高第一の天国及霊国にあるものは、第三度即ち最奥の度が開けて居り、中間の天界と最下の天界とは塞がり、又中間天界に居るものは、第二度のみ開けて、上天と下天とは塞がれ、又最下層の天界にあるものは第一度のみ開けて、中間天界と上天界とは塞がつて居るのである。故にもし上天国の天人にして中天国の団体を瞰下して、之と相語る事あらむには、上天人が有する第三度は忽ち塞がつて了ふのである。而して其閉塞と共に証覚迄も亡ぶのである。何故なれば、上天国の天人の証覚は、第三度に住し、第一及び第二の度に居らないからである。瑞の御霊の聖言に、

一、屋上にあるものは、其家のものを取らむとて下るなかれ。田に居るものは、其衣を取らむとて帰るなかれ。

一、其日には人屋上にあれば、其器具室にあるともこれを取らむとて下るなかれ。又田畑にあるものも帰るなかれ。

と示されたるは右の密意を示されたる言葉である。さうして下層の天界より、上層の天界へは神格の内流なるものがない。それは神の順序に逆らふからである。神は一名順序と讃へ奉つてもよいものである。故に上天界より下天界に向つては内流がある。さうして上天界の天人の証覚は下天界の天人に勝る事万と一とに比例するのである。是亦下天界の天人が上天界の天人と相語る事の出来ない理由である。仮令下天界の天人が仰ぎ望む事あるも、更に更に其姿を見る事を得ず、唯上天界は尚雲が頭上にかかつて居る如く見えるばかりである。これに反し上天界の天人は、下天界の天人を見る事が出来る。併し乍らこれと相語る事は出来ない。もしも下天界人と相語るやうな事があれば、忽ち其証覚を失ふものである。高天原に於ける諸々の団体中の天人は、善と真とに居る事何れも同様なれども、其証覚には様々の程度がある故に、必然の理由として高天原にも又統治の制度が布かれてある。諸天人は何うしても、其順序を守らねばならぬ。さうして順序に関する百般の事項は、どうしても破壊する事は出来ぬ。それから高天原の統治の制度は決して一様ではない。其団体々々に於ける個々の制度が布かれてある。瑞の御霊の大神の司り給ふ霊国即ち月の国を構成する団体にも亦一種の統治制度が布かれてある。各団体の職掌の異るにつれ、其制度にも亦不同あるは止むを得ない。併し高天原に於ては、相愛の制度を外にしては別に制度なるものはないのである。

高天原に於ける統治制度を称して正道と云ふ。大神に対する愛善の徳に住して行ふ所を総て正道と云ふのである。この統治制度は唯大神のみに属するものであつて、大神が御自身に諸天界の天人を導き、又之に処世の事物を教へ給ふ公義上の理法とも云ふべき種々の真理に至りては、各天人中の心中に明かに記憶さるるをもつて、天人として之を識り又之を知覚し、又之を感得し得ないものはない。故に公義上の事件に就いては争議上の種とはならないけれども、正道上の事件即ち各天人が実践躬行上の事件のみは時々疑問となる事がある。斯の如き正道上の事件の起つた時には証覚の少きものより是を自己より勝れたる天人に正し、或は之を直接大神に教を請うて、其結着を定むるものである。故に天人は唯正道に従つて、大神の導き給ふが儘に生息するのをもつて自分等の天界となし、又極秘の歓喜悦楽とするのである。次に大神の霊国即ち月の御国に於ける制度を、公義と云ふ。霊国の諸天人は霊善に居るからである。霊善とは、隣人に対する仁の徳を云ふのである。さうして其実性は真である。而して真は即ち公義に属し、善は正道に属するものである。今茲に月の国と云つたのは、現在地球上の人間が見る月球の事ではない。神の神格によつて構成されたる霊的国土である。この国土に住める諸々の天人は亦大神の導き給ひ、統治め給ふ所なれども、直接ならざるが故に茲には統治者なるものが出来て居る。其統治者の多寡は、各其所属団体の必要によつて設けらるるものである。又茲には律法が制定せられて諸々の天人は之に従ひて群居して居るのである。統治者は其律法によつて数多の事物を統制するの任務に当つて居る。さうして、是等の天人は何れも証覚あるにより、その律法をよく解し、万一疑ふ所あれば、大神が下らせ給うて、之に明白なる解釈を与へ給ふ事になつて居る。天国即ち日の国にあるが如き、善によつて行はるる統治を正道と云ひ、霊国即ち月の国にあるやうな真によつて行はるる統治を公義と云ふのである。天国、霊国の各団体の統治者は現代に於ける各国の統治者の如く、決して自ら尊大振るものでない、却て卑下し且つ謙譲の徳を充たして居るものである。さうして其団体の福利と隣人の事を第一に置いて、自己の福利を最後におくものである。けれども其統治者は非常なる名誉と光栄とを有して居る。是等の統治者は自分に与へられたる光栄と名誉は全く大神の与へられたるものたる事を自覚し、他の天人が自分に服従するのは、これ全く大神の御稜威なる事を知つて居るから、自然に謙譲な徳が具はり尊大振らぬのである。
