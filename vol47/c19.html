Chapter 19: 化相 Kami [1252]

化相神〔1252〕

§ 1

Conducting the festivals of the Tengokujin constitutes one of the important duties of the Tengoku community. Because the Tennin of Tengoku abide in the good of love, they love the Ōkami and love their fellows, solemnly enshrine the God who is the Creator of the Uchiu in accordance with the law of Tenchi Kamunagara, offer various rare Kumotsu, and thereafter bathing in the love of Kami constitutes their sole Kuwanki and their sole Kamuwaza. Furthermore, the Tengokujin never become angels nor do they become Sendenshi. Becoming angels or Sendenshi is entirely the Ninmu of Reigokutennin. This is because Reigoku has many who are filled with the truth of faith, whereas Tengoku is a Kokudo having many who are filled with the good of love. When the festival is concluded, angels or Sendenshi are dispatched and come from Reigoku to preach the good of love, exhort the truth of faith, and endeavor to make the wisdom and enlightenment of the harmonious Tennin increasingly harmonious. Moreover, the Tennin listen to their preaching to make their own personality harmonious and seek to attain welfare in worldly life. As for the Tengoku communities, large ones gather as many as one hundred thousand, while small ones include communities of fifty or sixty people. This is because communities are formed according to the principle of similarity and correspondence, depending on how the emotional movement of thoughts arising from love and faith is.

Harukunewake and Ryūkō, accompanied by Uzuhiko, were led to a wooden hall called the House of Kami, where the Tennin conduct festivals and Reigoku Sendenshi preach. The scent of wood remains fresh and fragrant forever, and because it is built according to the thought held at the time of its new construction even after the lapse of many years, it never decays or becomes old.

Uzuhiko and his wife wore red garments filled with luster and proceeded leisurely into the House of Kami. The Tennin of the community, down to infants, gathered here, and wishing to partake in the festival, waited with countenances filled with indescribable Kuwanki. Although these Tennin have some differences depending on their wisdom and enlightenment, for the most part they bear similar countenances. Compared to the formal festivals of the phenomenal world, it is a state of affairs beyond comparison, whether one calls it solemn, elegant, or magnificent. Within this community, those excelling most in enlightenment served in various roles pertaining to the purification rites, offerings, and festivals, and Uzuhiko came to serve as the chief priest as the community leader. Since all of Tengoku adheres to the principle of cleanliness, the principle of unification, the principle of progress, and the principle of optimism, it is filled with an indescribable good feeling. Through this festival, Kami and man reach the utmost limit of harmony and are intoxicated with Kuwanki and delight. And while it goes without saying that the festivals of Tengoku fulfill the path of expressing gratitude and repaying virtue to Kami, on the other hand they are also for praying for the harmony of the community, tasting the Kuwanki of each Tennin, and becoming intoxicated with delight. Therefore, unlike the festivals of the phenomenal world, they are not rigid, have no Ogasawara style, and proceed emotionally with absolute smoothness, absolute freedom, and according to the thoughts springing from the good of love, so that an indescribable, complete festival is performed. There is law without law, and there is ritual without ritual, a spectacle that can by no means even be dreamed of in the phenomenal world. And as for the Norito, just as in the phenomenal world, they chant the Amatsu Norito and Kamigoto to comfort the divine will, gladden the hearts of each of the Tennin, and wipe away all sins, defilements, and faults—such is the Kamuwaza. Even in Tengoku, it cannot be said that one is never filled with a little sorrow at times, or that encountering sadness and surprise is completely non-existent; therefore, the Tennin fix days to perform solemn festival rites and pray to obtain welfare for their existence.

The festival rites finally concluded, and a banqueting feast was held in the Yahirodono. For the most part, these festivals are conducted in the morning. However, in Tengoku there are no such things as time and space, and consequently there are no distinctions such as morning, afternoon, day, or night. Nevertheless, depending on changes in emotional movement, the sensations of morning or evening arise. Now, morning corresponds to the love of the sun and harmonizes with the good of love of Tengoku. Evening corresponds to the truth of faith and corresponds to the moon. Therefore, the festivals of the Tengokujin are conducted in the morning, and the Sendenshi dispatched from Reigoku—that is, the land of the moon—customarily begin their preaching upon reaching the afternoon. The fact that the rituals and ceremonial matters of various religions in the contemporary world are all conducted in the morning, while preaching and the like are conducted in the afternoon, reflects unconsciously the state of Tengoku upon the earth.

治国別、竜公は珍彦に伴はれ、神の家と称する、天人が祭典を行ひ霊国宣伝使が説教を行ふ木造の殿堂に導かれた。いつまでも木の香新しく薫り、幾年経ても新築した時の想念に依つて建てられてあるから、決して腐朽したり或は古くなつたりするものではない。

珍彦夫婦は光沢にみちた赤の装束をつけ、神の家に悠々と進み入つた。団体の天人は赤子に至る迄此処に集まり、祭典に与らむと、えも言はれぬ歓喜に充ちた面貌を表はして控へてゐる。この天人も智慧証覚の如何に仍りて、幾分か差等はあれども、大抵は相似の面貌をしてゐる。現界の形式的祭典に比ぶれば、実に荘厳と云はうか、優美と言はうか、華麗と言はうか、譬方のない情態である。此団体中にて、最も証覚の秀れたる者が、祓戸や神饌係や祭典に関するいろいろの役目をつとめ、珍彦は団体長として斎主の役に当ることとなつた。凡て天国は清潔主義、統一主義、進取主義、楽観主義であるから、何とも云へぬ良い気分に充たされるものである。此祭典に依つて、神人は和合の極度に達し、歓喜悦楽に酔ふのである。而して天国の祭典は神に報恩謝徳の道を尽すは言ふも更なれど、又一方には其団体の円満を祈り、天人各自の歓喜を味はひ、悦楽に酔ふ為である。故に現界の祭典の如く四角張らず、小笠原流式もなく、実に円滑に自由自在に、愛善より来る想念の儘に情動するのであるから、何とも云へぬ完全な祭典が行はれる。法なくして法あり、式なくして式あり、到底現界にて夢想だもなし能はざる光景である。而して祝詞はやはり現界の如く天津祝詞や神言を奏上して、神慮を慰め、且天人各自の心を喜ばせ、一切の罪汚れ過失を払拭する神業である。天国に於ても時に或は少々の憂ひにみたされ、悲みや驚きに遭遇することは絶無とは言へない、故に天人は日を定めて、荘厳なる祭典式を行ひ、其生涯に対して福利を得むことを祈るのである。

祭典の式も漸く済み、八尋殿に於て直会の宴が開かれた。大抵此祭典は午前中に行はるるものである。併し天国に於ては時間空間など云ふものはなく、従つて午前午後昼夜などの区別はない。併しながら情動の変異に依つて、朝たり夕べたるの感覚が起るものである。而して朝は太陽の愛に相応し、天国の愛善に和合するものである。又夕べは信真に相応し、月に相応するものである。故に天国人の祭典は午前中に行はれ、霊国即ち月の国から出張し来る宣伝使は午後に至つて説教を初むるのが例となつてゐる。現代に於ける各宗教の儀式も祭事に関することは凡て午前に行ひ、説教などは午後に行はるるのは、知らず知らずに天国の情態が地上に映つてゐるのである。

Each Tennin sings songs of their own choosing, dances, and plays music; after the Saitengo, they forget all else and play, reveling in comical Chiyaban and Kiyōgen, oblivious to the passage of time.

The situation of the Tennin's songs and Enbu shall be omitted here and recounted at a later date when the time is right. Now, as the Nahorahi banquet concluded safely and the emotions of each Tennin first reached a sensation corresponding to the afternoon, a Sendenshi of the Reigoku, accompanied by Riuriyō music coming from nowhere, advanced toward the house of Kami while illuminating the Atari. The Shiyotennin welcomed this Sendenshi, pouring forth voices of love with cries of 'Wo-wo.' The Sendenshi, with a Kuwanji and looking most joyful, offered a silent greeting to the Shiyotennin and, led by the guidance of Uzuhiko, who was the group leader, sat leisurely upon the stage constructed in a semi-circle. Beside him sat those whose light of Shōgaku was slightly inferior; these were the attendants of the Sendenshi. The Shiyotennin took their seats in a half-moon shape before the stage, never taking their eyes off the Sendenshi, and listened to his teachings with delight. The sermon of the Sendenshi in the heavenly realm serves no purpose other than to ensure a thorough understanding of the divine nature of the Lord God, to explain in the utmost detail what love, goodness, and true faith are, and to strive to help the Tennin achieve convenience in their life in the world.

Chikunowake and Ryūkō could not understand the language of the heavenly realm, so they sat silently in a special seat, inclining their ears and striving to grasp even a single word of the teachings. However, as these two individuals did not yet possess the wisdom and Shōgaku even to distinguish the language of the Tennin of the second heavenly realm, it was impossible for them to hear or understand such profound and beautiful sermons intended to instruct these Tennin. Consequently, they could not comprehend them. Like wooden dolls, they could only vaguely discern what was being spoken by considering the ingenuity of the beautiful tone of voice, the modulations and inflections of the language, the movements of the facial muscles, and the gestures. When they thought about an hour had passed, the Sendenshi announced the conclusion of the sermon. Each Tennin applauded repeatedly and, while praising him and crying 'Wo-wo,' departed from the house of Kami to return to their respective dwellings. Uzuhiko and his wife stood at the head of the Sendenshi, pointing toward their own mansion to welcome him back. Chikunowake and Ryūkō also followed behind and entered the mansion of Uzuhiko.

It is the custom for the Sendenshi to proceed to the inner room, remove his crown, change into his ceremonial robes, and relax while the host and guest sit opposite each other to indulge in a short conversation. Uzuhiko and his wife arranged rare fruits and poured wine to offer to the Sendenshi. The Sendenshi, while turning his countenance filled with glory toward Uzuhiko, drank a gulp and offered it to Uzuhiko. Uzuhiko, with reverence and worship, received it, drank it down, and offered the remainder to Uzuhime. Uzuhime likewise received it, finished drinking it, and returned it to the Sendenshi, who then gave the cup to the two attendants and poured the wine with his own hands. The two attendants began to sing a song that was difficult to understand. Chikunowake and Ryūkō were dazzled by the noble, graceful, and light-filled countenance of the Sendenshi and could not easily face him. This was because there was an extraordinary difference in the degree of their wisdom and Shōgaku. In a hurry, Chikunowake and Ryūkō took out their face-covering cloths and covered themselves with them. And when they looked closely at the face of the Sendenshi, to their surprise, it was Gosankō, the disciple of Chikunowake from whom they had parted in the forest of the field. The Sendenshi, Gosankō, had seen through at a glance that Chikunowake and Ryūkō were here, but he had intentionally not revealed his name until the two recognized him naturally. Chikunowake thought to himself:

Chikunowake: 'Ah, how similar, how very similar; he is the spitting image of my disciple, Gosankō. It is just that his skin tone appears translucent, and there is no place where he differs at all. Should I reveal my identity? No, no, how could someone like Gosankō become a Sendenshi of the Reigoku? It would not do to ask questions rashly and be rude.'

Thinking this over and over, he continues to be troubled by anxiety. Tatsukō too, while examining the Sendenshi's figure from beneath the Himenpu, tilts his head for cutting, or shaking it to the right and left,

Tatsukō: 'Well now, they look a lot alike, how strange, or perhaps they really are different after all? No, no, they cannot possibly be different. Whatever else may be said, it is a most Fushigisenban matter. No matter what I do, the worm of Gaten will not accept it. Even if one says that in Tengoku there are Sauji Menbau, surely a face resembling this to such a degree does not exist a Nido.'

and so on, regardless of his surroundings, speaking out from a Muku heart without any Enriyoeshiyaku. The Sendenshi turns his glowing Menbau toward both men, smiling cheerfully. Without minding, Tatsukō continues,

Tatsukō: 'Moshibensei Sensei, please look at the face of that Sendenshi. With those Mikazukimayuge, dimples on the cheeks, the shape of the eyes, the form of the nose, and that tiny pouting mouth, is he not the exact spitting image of Isokō?'

Harukuniwake opens his mouth for the first time,

Harukuniwake: 'He looks very much alike, doesn't he?'

Tatsukō: 'Sensei, is there any question of whether he resembles him or not? It is the Honmamono Isokō himself. Hey, Isokō, why so Erasau? It is me, it is me! Because I am wearing the Himenpu you probably do not recognize me, but I am Lord Tatsukō. And the other person is your precious teacher, Harukuniwake-sama. Will you not step down from your seat and offer your greetings? Heh, Eenh! When on earth did you rise in the world to such an extent?'

The Sendenshi smiles all the more cheerfully.

Sendenshi: 'My, my, Konohanahimenomikoto-sama! How wondrous that you became a Totei of one like myself, and through the art of Kashingō have guided this foolish Harukuniwake up to now so well. I gratefully thank you.'

The Sendenshi opens his mouth for the first time,

Sendenshi: 'Harukuniwake-sama, Tatsukō-sama, please forgive my rudeness. However, I am Kototamawakenomikoto, serving at the side of the Moon Ōkami. This time, by the command of the Ōkami, I descended to the earth, filled the spirit of Isokō, and for the achievement of the divine nation, I have been acting together with you. Therefore, I and Isokō are completely separate human beings. At the same time, because the entirety of my divine nature has filled the spirit of Isokō, even our Menbau probably resemble each other well. In the future too, I shall occasionally descend into the spirit of Isokō and act through Kashingō in order to construct Tengoku on earth; therefore, I ask you to treat Isokō as before, utilizing him as your Totei.'

Harukuniwake is struck with astonishment at this tale,

Harukuniwake: 'Hai.'

uttering only this, he lets fall tears of gratitude at the vast and boundless grace of the Kami.

Tatsukō: 'Moshi Sendenshi-sama... No, Kototamawakenomikoto-sama, when I return to the phenomenal world, should I maintain our usual way of interacting toward Isokō-sama as before? If I do not ask this now, it will cause inconvenience for the future, you see.'

Sendenshi: 'If I appear through Kashingō, he is still Isokō. I ask that you interact with him just as before.'

Tatsukō: 'Ah, Isokō, I understand. When I think that you will become such a grand person, I too am your friend after all. No matter how much Chie shōkaku one has, once we go out into the phenomenal world, I am perhaps a little grander than you, or perhaps just slightly inferior. It is as if I have one shō and you have nine gō, or you have one shō and I have nine gō. Hey, Isokō, is that not so?'

Kototamawake suddenly strips off his outer robe. Before long, he becomes indistinguishable in appearance from the Isokō seen in the phenomenal world, and his speech too has grown familiar and friendly.

Kototamawake: 'Shall we take off the divine robes of Tengoku and for a while comfortably make a journey to Tengoku with you once again? Harukuniwake-sensei, from now on, not to mention the Second Tengoku, I shall guide you even to the First Tengoku.'

Harukuniwake: 'Please be so good as to guide us.'

Tatsukō: 'Hey Sensei, you are a person who lacks flexibility. Well, Isokō, why do you not say that you grant us to accompany you? Hey, Isokō, is that not so?'

Kototamawake: 'Un, once the mask of transformation is stripped off, I am still the original Isokō. Ah, Urazuhiko-san, Urazuhime-san, thank you very much. Since we shall now make a circuit of inspection through Tengoku, let us part with you here.'

The couple Urazuhiko are utterly dumbfounded by this unexpected dialogue, and silently stare into the faces of the three. Where the two attendants of the Sendenshi have gone, neither shadow nor form was any longer to be seen.

Here Harukuniwake, accompanied by Isokō and Tatsukō, expresses profound gratitude to Urazuhiko and Urazuhime, and chanting a Sendenka while following the guidance of Isokō, proceeds heading toward the south.

宣伝使は始めて口を開き、

『治国別様、竜公様、失礼を致しました。併し私は月の大神の御側に仕へまつる言霊別命で厶います。此度大神の命に依り、地上に降り、五三公の精霊を充たし神国成就の為に、貴方と共に活動をしてゐた者で厶います。夫れ故私と五三公とは全く別個の人間です。併しながら私の神格の全部が、五三公の精霊をみたしたる為、面貌までが能く似てゐるのでせう。今後に於ても時々五三公の精霊に下り、地上に天国を建設する為、化相を以て活動を致しますれば、五三公はヤハリ貴方の徒弟としてお使ひを願ひます』

治国別は此物語に打驚き、

『ハイ』

と言つたきり、神の恵の広大無辺なるに感謝の涙をこぼしてゐる。

竜公『モシ宣伝使様……否言霊別命様、私が現界へ帰りました時は、五三公さまに対し、今迄の通りの交際振をやつて居ればいいのですか、之を伺つておかねば、今後の都合が厶いますからなア』

『化相を以て現はるれば、ヤハリ五三公です。従前の通り交際を願ひます』

『ヤ、五三公、承知した。お前がこんな偉い者になることを思へば、俺だつてヤツパリ友達だ。何程智慧証覚があるといつても、現界へ出れば、俺とチーと偉いか、少し劣つた位なものだ。俺が一升でお前が九合か、お前が一升で俺が九合か位なものだ。なア五三公、さうだらう』

言霊別はパツと上衣を脱いだ。忽ち現界で見た五三公と、風体まで変らないやうになり、言葉もなれなれしくなつて来た。

『天国の法衣をぬいで、暫く気楽に又お前と天国の旅行をしようぢやないか。治国別の先生、これから五三公が第二天国は云ふも更也、第一天国まで御案内を致しませう』

『何卒宜しく御願致します』

『コレ先生、融通の利かぬ人だな。ヨシ五三公、お供を許すと何故仰有らぬのだ。なア五三公、さうぢやないか』

『ウン、化の皮をぬいだら、ヤツパリ元の五三公だ。ヤア珍彦様、珍姫様、有難う厶いました。之から天国の巡覧を致しますから、之でお別れ致しませう』

珍彦夫婦は意外の此問答に呆れ果て、黙然として三人の顔を見つめてゐる。二人の宣伝使の侍者はどこへ行つたか、影も形も見えなくなつて居た。

茲に治国別は五三公、竜公を伴ひ珍彦、珍姫に厚く礼を述べ、宣伝歌を歌ひながら、五三公の案内につれて、南方さして進み行く。
