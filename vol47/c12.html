Chapter 12: 天界行 [1245]

天界行〔1245〕

§ 1

The Reigokutennin and the tennin of Tengoku who dwell in the various groups of Takaamahara make love their life, and because they broadly love itsusai, they exhaust arayuru kōgi even for the seirei who leave human nikutai and ascend, impart kontoku kyōkun, or sing omoshiro songs, perform bukiyoku, play ongaku, and the like, thinking of nothing else than wishing to guide as many as possible, even if it be hitori, to the groups of Takaamahara; this is what is called the saikausaigo kuwankietsuraku of the tennin. Nevertheless, if a seirei made the nikutai of a man its dwelling, and had not spent a shiyōgai such as would cause it to enter the flock of zenrei, namely seishiyugojin, while it was in gense, or a shiyōgai such as would lead it to the flock of tennin, namely honshiyugojin, then those seirei desire to depart far away from these heavenly zenrei. In this manner, unless the seirei finally herd together with the seirei that match the shiyōgai they had when they were in gense, they will not halt this transition anywhere.

Upon discovering what closely conforms to its own shiyōgai in the previous life, that seirei intends here again to spend it together with that which resembles its shiyōgai in the world. Indeed, the laws of the spiritual world must be called wondrous things.

As has been stated before, within the body of every human being, two kinds of seirei, good and evil, lie latent. And human beings, through zenrei—that is, honshiyugojin or seishiyugojin—harmonize with the various groups of Takaamahara, and through akuryō—that is, fukushiyugojin—harmonize with the groups of Jigoku in accordance with the principle of correspondence; these seirei are registered in Chūukai, namely the seireikai, which is situated midway between Takaamahara and Jigokukai. When these seirei come to human beings, they first enter into their memory, and next invade their thoughts. And fukushiyugojin infiltrate among the evil things existing in memory and thought, while seishiyugojin come to invade the very best things existing within their memory and thought. Yet the seirei themselves do not know in the least that they enter into the body of that human being and dwell together. Moreover, when the seirei are together with a human being, they believe all the memory and thought of that human being to be the possession of the seirei themselves. Furthermore, those beings that are seirei do not see human beings. The reason is that those who are in the actual solar world do not become the objects of vision for those seirei. Ōkami uses His grand divine heart profoundly in order to make these seirei not know that they accompany that human being. The reason is that if those seirei should know this thing, they would be able to converse with human beings, and then the evil spirits that are fukushiyugojin would think of destroying human beings. Fukushiyugojin, namely evil spirits, are those that harmonize with the various evils and falsehoods of the root country, and therefore, aside from desiring single-mindedly to destroy human beings, lead them to Jigokukai, and make it their own achievement, they have no other business. And fukushiyugojin desire to destroy not only the psyche of human beings—that is, their faith and love—but even raise and destroy their nikutai as well. Therefore, if those evil spirits do not converse with human beings, they do not know that they are within the human body, and thus they never inflict harm. Those evil spirits do not know whether the things they think and speak to one another truly originate from human beings or not. For although the speaking among those seirei actually originates from human beings, they firmly believe it to be originating from within themselves. And since every person extremely respects what belongs to themselves and moreover passionately loves it, even though the seirei do not know this of themselves, they come to be in a position where they must naturally love and respect human beings. This was entirely brought about by the great benevolent heart of Mizu-no-Minochi-no-Ōkami, who so arranged it that the seirei are not made to know that they are together with human beings.

§ 2

As stated before, both the seirei communicating with the dantai of Tengoku and the seirei communicating with Jigokukai accompany ningen in the same manner. Furthermore, among the seirei communicating with the dantai of Tengoku, the purest ones are called shinrei or honshiyugojin, those slightly inferior are called seishiyugojin, and the seirei communicating with Jigoku are called akurei or fukushiyugojin. However, when ningen are born, they are inevitably destined to fall into the domain of evil. Therefore, one may even say that their initial shiyōgai resides entirely in the hands of these seirei. If ningen were not accompanied and guarded by seirei similar to themselves, ningen would not be able to live as nikutai. Nor would they be able to depart from various evils and return to goodness. It is by means of akurei, namely fukushiyugojin, that the nikutai of ningen maintain their seimei, while at the same time, it is by means of zenrei, namely seishiyugojin, that they are able to detach themselves from this evil. Because ningen maintain a state of equilibrium through the virtues of both of these, they possess freedom of ishi. By means of this free ishi, they are able to cast away various evils and turn to goodness, and furthermore, they are able to plant and cultivate goodness within their heart. When ningen are not in such a state of freedom, they can never achieve the fruits of reforming their faults and practicing goodness. Yet, on the one hand, there is the activity of akurei flowing from the root country, and on the other hand, there is the activity of zenrei flowing from Takamanohara; standing between these two, ningen must be wedged between the pressures of both Tengoku and Jigoku, and thus freedom of ishi should by no means exist without this.

Furthermore, when ningen have no freedom, they cannot attain seimei. Nor can goodness be forced upon others; goodness that has been forced upon one by someone else never remains within the internal reikon, and no matter what, it can never soak into the depths of the heart. Only that goodness which has been freely and spontaneously received casts deep roots upon the ishi of ningen, and comes to make that goodness appear as if it were entirely their own thing.

Facing all spiritual and manifest existence, unless one infers from natural things, the common failing of modern people, who are incapable of thinking, is that even spiritual or divine personality is conceived as something physical or natural; therefore, when looking at the conclusions of those fellows, they advocate that if God is indeed a single personality, the size ought to be equal to the entire great cosmos; and they conjecture that if God truly governs and arranges heaven and earth, God must employ numerous officials just like monarchs in the world above.

Truly, this is the height of foolishness! To such foolish human beings, even if the spirit realm of Takamanohara is instructed that there is no spatial extension as in the actual world, they will not be able to comprehend it directly and easily. For the reason that those who think, having established the natural world and natural light as their sole standard, except for the extension recognized right before their eyes, can by no means contemplate anything else whatsoever. The extension of Takamanohara is entirely opposite in circumstance to the extension found in the world. Because the extension belonging to the natural world possesses a certain kind of limitation, it can be easily measured and known, yet

the extension of Takamanohara, possessing no limitation from the very beginning, is not a matter that the shallow wisdom of the human heart can easily measure and know. Why, no matter how far the horizon of human eyes reaches, even the sun, moon, and stars, which have extremely distant distances, can be easily recognized, as everyone knows well. Furthermore, if one sinks the heart a little deeper and reflects, our internal visual power, that is, the visual power of the thought world, reaches even further into the distance, and advancing still further, at the extreme limit reached by our inner sight, we know that the horizon should be yet far grander. Harukuniwake and Tatsukō, both of them, had been resting for a while at the gatehouse when a ball of fire, illuminating the eastern sky, drew near tracing a streak through the air, fell before the two, and instantly transformed into the figure of a beautiful heavenly being. Before they knew it, the two had been dragged along by their thoughts and had ascended to the Third Heaven. When they looked closely at the divine being's form, who would have expected it, it was none other than the missionary of Misoshirugi, Ioriyoriwake no Mikoto. Harukuniwake, struck with astonishment and joy, bowed his head abruptly and quietly began to chant the heavenly numeration song.

Ioriyoriwake-san: 'It is exceedingly convenient, Harukuniwake-sama. I had been thinking of guiding you around the various organizations of Takaamahara at some point, but finally, I had not obtained the opportunity. Fortunately, your physical body was tormented by Brahmanism, and your spirit was able to detach itself from the flesh and at last come here. After you have thoroughly examined heaven, you must return once more to the manifest world and do yet another round of work for the sake of the gods and for the salvation of all living beings.'

Harukuniwake: 'Meeting you, Noble Deity, in such an unexpected place, I am so overjoyed that words fail me. At the same time, I have heard that if a human physical body passes twenty-four hours, it already rots and putrefies and cannot once again become a vessel for the spirit, yet I feel as though I have already spent almost ten hours or so since coming here. With only some thirteen or fourteen hours remaining, can I possibly tour heaven in such a short time?'

Ioriyoriwake-san: 'Do not worry; one day in the spirit world corresponds to one year in the manifest world. Not even a minute has passed for you yet as viewed from the spirit world. That it felt as though ten hours had passed is likely the reflection of the actual world. Moreover, in the spirit world there is neither time nor space. Much less is there autumn or winter or night in heaven; there is only the change of emotion. Since the entire spirit world is a world of thought, time and the like are not an issue. Come, it would be best for you to follow me leisurely and tour the various organizations of heaven.'

Harukuniwake: 'Yes, thank you very much; then, following your instructions, I shall attend you as an escort.'

Tatsukō: 'Hey, Teacher, please let me accompany you as well.'

Harukuniwake: 'Hmm, that's true. I wonder if I should ask Ioriyoriwake-sama.'

Ioriyoriwake-san: 'Since Tatsukō-sama is not yet equipped with enough goodness, faith, and the wisdom and perception to tour heaven, touring is ultimately impossible. Fortunately, however, by the command of the Great Deity, I have been appointed as a mediating heavenly being, so with special exception, I shall permit you to accompany us.'

Harukuniwake: 'Yes, thank you very much; I humbly ask for your favor in all things.'

Tatsukō: 'Ah, having received such special patronage, it is an honor that exceeds my worth.'

Ioriyoriwake-san: 'Tatsukō-sama, because you are still someone registered in the spirit realm, if you go to heaven, you will surely be blinded, grow short of breath, and ultimately be unable to endure it. Since I have a face-covering veil here, put this on; if you do so, however things turn out, you will be able to accompany us.'

Taking a black face-covering veil from his bosom, he hurled it directly at Tatsukō's face, and wondrously, the veil was placed neatly upon Tatsukō's face.

Ioriyoriwake-san: 'Now, with this, I shall first guide you from a certain organization of the Third Heaven.'

Harukuniwake: 'Yes, thank you.'

Harukuniwake and Tatsukō followed behind, advancing with cautious trepidation.

Suddenly, exquisite music came echoing, an intoxicating fragrance enclosed all four sides, and they found themselves filled with an indescribable sense of exhilaration. Kotoyoriwake led the two onto a certain small hill, sat down upon a beautiful rock, and taking upon himself the labor of explanation, looked down upon the Aonogahara plain below.

Kotoyoriwake-sama: 'Please look over toward the east yonder. Do you see a small, elevated hill over there, with a great many houses built upon it? That is a certain partial community of the Third Tengoku, a community where the tennin who excel in love and faith dwell. And in the direction corresponding to this true west as well, there is likewise a community, is there not? That is a community where tennin whose virtues of goodness and truth are somewhat thinner, and whose light is also slightly dimmer, reside. Compared to the eastern community, the western community has all its scenery greatly inferior, does it not? This is because, according to the thickness or thinness of the virtues of love-zen and faith-shin of the tennin within those communities, such distinctions are thus inherently attached in a divine manner. As it is said that like attracts like, those of the same will and thought gather together through the virtue of love. Therefore, compared to the eastern community, the western community is considerably inferior.'

Harukuniwake: 'Even among tennin, do not those who dwell in the eastern community and those who dwell in the western community experience a tremendous difference in fortune and misfortune? Is there not such a thing as the western community envying the group on the eastern side and migrating thither?'

Kotoyoriwake: 'By no means, by no means is there any such notion. Since each person's destiny is determined entirely according to the emotional movement of love itself originating from divine character, even if the western community were to go envying the radiance of the eastern community, their own virtue would be insufficient, and they would find it painful and unbearable to remain there. Therefore, the tennin of individual communities never freely move to other places; throughout Takamanohara, order is regarded above all else. Those who mistake this order cannot possibly hope for the life of Tengoku. Since the divine character of the Ōkami places order in the highest position, there is never, like in the world of the manifest, even the slightest dream of such a danger as disorder and confusion. For this reason, peace is preserved eternally in Tengoku.'

Harukuniwake: 'Indeed, even in the divine pronouncements of the Spirit of Strictness, it is indicated that virtue corresponding to one's soul is granted; this is truly an awe-inspiring matter, indeed. And what do the tennin do day by day?'

Kotoyoriwake: 'Human beings of the manifest world imagine that the tennin of Takamanohara indulge in song, dance, and music year in and year out, intoxicated in merrymaking, but even in Tengoku, there is no one who passes their days idly in debauchery and sloth. The God created the universe for the sole purpose of having a purpose. Therefore, humans without a purpose are not supposed to be permitted existence in either the spiritual world or the manifest world; those tennin enjoy their respective divine vocations, labor diligently to participate in the divine work, and carry out the occupations of samurai, farmers, artisans, and merchants. And there are official holidays three times a month, at which times the tennin gather at the house of God, enjoy merrymaking to the utmost of their strength, praise and extol God, and moreover bathe to the absolute full in the grace of God.'

Harukuniwake: 'Indeed, it is truly a magnificent divine administration that has been made.'

Kotoyoriwake: 'Like the manifest world, such things as clashes between labor and capital, labor problems, tenant-landlord disputes, ideological issues, political issues, and economic issues never arise even in a dream; it is truly a peaceful and happy existence. If a person of the manifest world were to see the condition of Tengoku just once, they would grow reluctant to return to the manifest world ever again.'

Harukuniwake: 'Indeed so, we too have come to desire to spend the life of Tengoku just like this.'

Yotahiko: 'Sensei, please beg Kotoyoriwake-mikoto-sama not to let us be chased back to this mundane world; truly, this is a wonderful place, indeed.'
