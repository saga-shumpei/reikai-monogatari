Chapter 6: Beauty 人 Grass [1239]

美人草〔1239〕

§ 1

Covered by the softly falling snow fluttering down, the three of them—Kō, Waku, and Emu—were building a tiny fire in the mysterious shadow of the woods, sitting in a circle, and indulging in idle chatter to comfort their boredom.

Kō: 'As long as the Shiyōgun is establishing a semi-permanent camp in the forest of Ukiki, for the next year or so there will hardly be any inclination to advance toward the Iso mansion. When they said it was lonely without women in the camp and tried executing a woman hunt in the village of Ukiki by the Shiyōgun's order, every last one of them turned out to be mere apparition-like stuff, nothing but monstrous oversized pumpkins that you couldn't bear to look at twice. So the Shiyōgunsama gave a secret instruction to the great Metsuke of the extracts, saying that if a splendid woman is found, she must be presented to him, and if she is, he would use her in an important position—so I heard he said, but somehow or other I really want to capture a beauty, don't I.'

Waku: 'Even so, in such a turbulent world, women have all hidden themselves away like moles in the earthen caves of the deep mountains, and there is little likelihood of them coming out easily. Still, if we wait right here like this, it's not as though they might not come along, you know.'

Kō: 'That woman called Omin, whom Imoriwake chased after, was quite a nice one, wasn't she. Since I was born a man, I certainly want to pair up with such a woman at least once. Despite being a woman, she has strength and a well-composed courage; it was just like the descent of a heavenly maiden. I was completely won over by that Omin's attitude. Ha ha ha ha ha!'

Waku: 'Since ancient times, as for praising and extolling the beauty of a woman's mind and body as a man, no matter how great an artist or poet, none have yet fully succeeded. The gods who kneaded clay to fashion human beings, or monkeys and dogs, probably do not feel it quite that much, but at least the charm of women reflected in our eyes is a tremendous thing. Supple, long hair arranged into various shapes in interesting and beautiful adornments; a flesh neither bony nor sinewy because of subcutaneous fat; even the torso alone, or even just smaller parts, seem to have enough value for us to worship. Even those smooth cheeks without beards are splendid. Even the plumply stretched breasts are fine. Even the wrists, into which dimple-like hollows enter at the base of the fingers that become ginkgo nuts when clenched and ume plums when opened, are splendid. Even the feet are soft and pleasant. On top of that, since women possess the beautiful and gentle instinct of devoting time and soul to adorning their bodies without looking back, the jewels only increase their luster all the more. The vocal cords being stretched in a high pitch also convey a pure resonance pleasing to a man's ears. Generally, from a passive disposition, their deportment is quiet and graceful, and even their words have no sharp edges and are modest—these are also a woman's virtues. A woman seems to be without a single spot to criticize anywhere. No matter what genius appears since the creation of heaven and earth, no matter how much we grow eager and speak of the elegance of a woman's mind and body which has never been fully praised and extolled to the end, it is to no avail. There is nothing to do but reverently respect them and revere them as the guardian deities of eternal and infinite peace. Ah, Kannagara Tamachihachimaseshi. Aha ha ha ha!'

Emu: 'Hey Waku, are you chosen as an advisor to the Women's Rights Extension Society or something? Aren't you spouting off quite a defense of women's rights? From my perspective, a woman is strangely all show and no substance, and there is nothing quite as clumsy and troublesome. Once praised, they get full of themselves; if scolded, they cry; if killed, they haunt you—aren't they a terribly troublesome lot? No matter what they are made to do, they are ultimately no match for men. Defending men a little won't bring much punishment upon us.'

§ 2

Bah, have there ever been any decent fellows among men since ancient days? Are there not plenty of women who have accomplished great works, such as the three sisters of Matsutakyōme, Izumohime, Hafurihime, and Konohanahime of Tenkyōzan? Though my knowledge is limited, I keep hearing of the emergence of many more heroine figures. Even Tokoyohime and that old lady Otora of the Uranai faith who just passed by, when viewed from our standpoint, are they not magnificent persons, eh? I admit that even compared to me, my wife handles the needle far more dexterously. After all, children cannot be born by men. No matter how you look at it, women are the flowers of the shiyakaukai. Beyond that, even regarding tasks once thought to be the exclusive domain of men, they stride right in and carry them through. For instance, when we consider how, upon visiting members of parliament and chattering away about something or other, most matters end up passing safely, I truly admire them, feeling that my wife's political brain is far more developed than someone like me. Such being the case, one simply cannot dismiss and bury women away by calling them clumsy in a single breath.

Indeed, in the aspect of deftly pulling off just about anything, women might perhaps be superior. Yet, strangely enough, has there ever appeared since the creation of the world a single extraordinary woman of such towering stature that no man could hope to rival her, even if he stood on his head? In fields like music, there are certainly those who go quite far up to the first rank, but there has never been an instance where they reached the very pinnacle of the first rank. Even Jeanne d'Arc, if brought alongside Ōkuninushi, would be merely second or third rate. Even if Murasaki Shikibu or Sarah Bernhardt are said to be great, they are not quite of the absolute first rank. Even in pursuits that have come to be thought of as women's work by shifting perspectives, the positions deserving to be called the pinnacle of the first rank are all without exception occupied by males. Even in cooking and needlework, the top-tier artisans are still men. If you think about it just a little, it would be hard to object even if it is said that women are clumsy. Moreover, as for matters like romance, I should think women would take the lead and plunge into it headfirst with profound depth, but if you ask me, when it comes to romance of the absolute highest order, a woman is always led by the hand by a man, following step by step behind him. Since even the fire of love had to be fetched for them by Prometheus, must it not be said that woman is truly an eternal idol?

Now, you must not despise women. A man whose wife has died can become a first-rate mother, but he can never become what should be called a mother of the absolute highest order. This is the one point where, no matter how a man may stand on his head and strain himself, he cannot prevail. This matter alone is an indisputable fact.

Whether woman at the beginning was the sun or a female ape matters not, but it is undeniable that human beings are viviparous animals and that women possess a magnificent baby-making apparatus called the womb. If you dislike it, you need not bear children, and that is as you please, but if the work they do is clumsy and their love is shallow, where on earth is the worth of a woman's existence? Ultimately, women are meant to be subjugated to men, you see.

I cannot think that women are subjugated to men, and at the same time, I do not think they are independent. That is precisely the same as saying that men are neither subjugated to women nor independent. However, I would never attempt to grasp women's freedom within the palm of a man's hand. I am one who prays that women may be all the more free.

Women's freedom—bah, how impudent! For a woman to cry out for freedom, is that not outrageous? It is a rebellion attempting to make thought, emotion, customs, and livelihood her own; it is an effort to tear herself away from man's meddling. It is an ambitious desire to snatch every right over to the women's side. She is simply loathing to become womanly.

§ 3

The modern woman's sentiment of trying to establish women's own shisau, kanjiyō, shifuzoku, and seikuwatsu—such things—manifests that women have awakened to the times, so are not modern women exceedingly dependable?

However, to try to take those back from danshi, the sight of women's champions charging into the midst of danshi and sinking more and more slidingly among danshi is painful to behold. To think that danshi have taken things away from women and are concealing them within gijiyō, the sentiment of jizen and aikoku, upon hikauki, within daigaku, the name of kachiyō, or inside noriahi jidōshiya, and are making them private, must be said to be somewhat of a kentauchiga sensaku. Whether from such places the true jiiu of women will be taken back or not, well now, you had better try acting fully for the sake of jiyokenkuwakuchiyōkuwai. If by any chance danshi have taken anything from women, it is merely that because whatever women do is clumsy and completely unwatchable, danshi have been doing it in their stead. Yet I am not talking about occupations. If only women would splendidly manage shisau, kanjiyō, shifuzoku, seikuwatsu... such things with their own hands, danshi would be helped by just that much. Danshi could use that much labor and effort advantageously toward more masculine directions. Danshi will by no means begrudge returning anything to women. In what the new women or awakened women of recent times are doing, there are only contradictions. There is a shape to it like leaving strangers whose ways are unknown to work in one's own house, while going off to help with thin arms of no use in the spring-cleaning of the neighbor's house where there are neither giri nor hechima. It is truly pitiful that they do not realize it is an unwelcome nuisance where things at home are unattended to and at the neighbor's house it is only in the way. When incited even a little by mischievous gangs, they take upon themselves the persons of noble ladies with no hardships whatsoever, stand at street corners, and even peddle artificial flowers. When it comes to this, it goes past being foolish and becomes rather stylish. Even if a woman is dissatisfied with being a woman or feels ashamed of it, unless she is recast, neither the Kamisama nor humans nor anyone at all can possibly do anything about it.

Hey, Emu, that is not how it should be said. Even women are capable of anything. One could manage about as much as a prime minister.

It is only natural that there is no reason why a woman cannot become about as much as a prime minister. In fact, though it is not lofty, even Katō Akiko has become one, has she not? If it is the prime minister of recent times, even a blockhead can perform it splendidly, you see. However, even being the same blockhead, danshi do it a little bit better. Therefore, since such things must be vexing, you had better leave them to danshi for a while, and even if you stand on reverse-spears against danshi, you had better put your energy into aspects where women cannot imitate. That is none other than a woman being a mother. If you have an excess of energy to the extent that you feel life is not worth living with just that, you should strive to be a first-rate mother. Even if you are still not satisfied after that, it would be good if you strived to be a first-rate among the first-rates, an ideal mother. For it is not work that is so leisurely that even a single child is boring, nor is it back-breaking work, nor is it work without such worth in toiling, since it is a nursery institution of heavenly beings. It is not work as easy as scattering handbills to the passersby on the highway.

Hey, you must not make a mistake, what I called a first-rate among first-rate mothers is not the sort commonly called a good wife and wise mother in the world. If it is such a thing, even danshi can do it accordingly.

Then what do you say it is?

Well, that is the point where even if danshi stand on reverse-spears they cannot catch up. You had best leave it entirely to the ladies, you see.

Kō: 'No matter what anyone says, the beauty of a woman's heart and body is something that cannot be praised enough. A tender, deeply thoughtful, and modest heart; a heart that is unveiled yet pure; a small, lovely heart that—despite holding many malicious thoughts—takes all of them together in the end; a heart that is a liar and yet immediately repents; however you think of it, they are good. Emu: 'Since women are fools by nature, they are creatures who can be shaped into anything. If you want to make a woman happy, rather than wasting a million words and praising her mind, it is far more effective to praise just a single word—her hair, the shape of her nose, the look in her eyes, or the luster of her nails. Aha, ha, ha, ha!'

Kō: 'No matter what anyone says, I praise, worship, and adore a woman's heart and her body, revering her as the goddess of peace. That is the moral duty of a man. No matter what, women are eternal idols.'

Waku: 'Aha, ha, ha, ha!'

Emu: 'Ehe, ha, ha, ha!'

As they were laughing and making merry thus, two maidens of tender age with snow-white faces drew near, raising cool voices and singing poems while heading toward the forest on this side. The three of them quickly spotted this, and pulling each other by the eyes and sleeves, Kō said in a low voice,

'Hey, Waku, Emu, isn't my Kototama a mighty thing? The moment I was praising women, haven't gorgeous and beautiful maidens suddenly appeared before us? Speak of the devil and he appears; truly, they are marvelous creatures. Shall we somehow cleverly capture them, lead them before the Shogun-sama, and achieve great fame and fortune, the three of us?'

Emu: 'How interesting! Let us be firmly resolved. Waku, even as an opponent of women, you surely cannot have a single word to say against that beauty. Eh!'

'Indeed, my head feels all dizzy as if struck by divine light. They are marvelous things.'

Even as they talked thus, the two beauties had already approached.

First Woman: 'Pardon me, I would like to ask a brief question—where does one go to reach the camps of General Launch or General Katahiko?'

Kō: 'Hey, with what intention are you ladies inquiring about the whereabouts of the Generals?'

'It is enough if we just meet them. Once I have met them, I cannot tell at present whether it will rain, blow the wind, thunder, or cause an earthquake. At any rate, please guide us.'

'If I were to guide you carelessly without even hearing your errand, it would be a terrible thing. Waku, Emu, what shall we do?'

Waku: 'Just look at you! You were praising women with all your might, but now that the time has come, what is that cowardice of yours? That is why I said women are no good. If we were to take the likes of them along, who knows, they might turn into explosive shells for the Brahmin army!'

Second Woman: 'Ho, ho, ho, ho, ho! Please do not worry, gentlemen. No matter what, at most we are women; even if two women were to go among nothing but splendid men, what could possibly be done? A woman facing a man—no matter what, unless the opposite sex is added, the true power of a man can never be brought forth!'

Kō: 'Is that so? Ah, I understand. My, with those crescent-shaped eyebrows, those bright and clear eyes, fair skin, and glossy hair, they are just like heavenly beings!'

'Ho, ho, ho, ho, ho! You should not tease such simpletons in that manner. Please guide both of us to the Shogun's camp.'

Kō: 'Yes, yes, I will guide you indeed. However, not knowing your names, there is no way for me to guide you. I beg of you to give your names.'

The two women, Kafu and Jiyo, bowed politely, and with smiling faces, stepping in the footprints of the three men, boldly and fearlessly advanced toward Ranch's encampment.
