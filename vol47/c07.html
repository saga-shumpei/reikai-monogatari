Chapter 7: 酔の八衢 [1240]

酔の八衢〔1240〕

§ 1

Even as the sun and moon shining in the heaven, when obscured by dark clouds, suddenly lose their light, so did Harukuniwake, the Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō who possessed wisdom, benevolence, and valor, suddenly have his Reigan crossed by Eūn, fall headlong into the trap of the cruel and lawless General Lunch, and plunge directly into the dark and pitch-black underground dungeon; that he should have fainted was inevitable.

The respiration of the lungs gradually became faint, the Jiyōdō ceased entirely, the heartbeat stopped, and Harukuniwake, together with Tatsukō, came to wander through unfamiliar mountains and fields. Advancing without any particular destination as the flow of his own thoughts directed him, he strayed into a narrow ravine flanked on one side by towering mountains and on the other by gigantic rocks. This place is the entrance to Chūkai. Chūkai is the gathering place of good spirits and evil spirits. It is also called Seireikai.

Tatsukō, struck by the wondrous scenery on all sides, pulled at the sleeve of Harukuniwake and spoke.

Tatsukō: 'Moshibe-san, where on earth might this place be? If one thought we were drinking sake in the inner parlor of General Lunch, the situation suddenly underwent a complete change, and into such a valley bottom—just when on earth did we arrive?'

Harukuniwake: 'It is indeed strange. It remains faintly in my memory, but as I recall entering a splendid parlor guided by Katahiko, I suddenly had the feeling of having fallen into a dark hole. If by any chance we have cast off our physical bodies, could it not be that only our spirits have wandered here?'

Tatsukō: 'The air feels somehow slightly different. However, there is no help for it even if we remain in such a place. Shall we advance as far as we are able to go?'

Harukuniwake folded his hands for a moment, tracing his faint memories while nodding twice or thrice, and spoke.

Harukuniwake: 'Mm, mm, exactly so, exactly so; we fell smoothly into the schemes of General Lunch and Katahiko-shiyōgun, and our lives have been taken from us. Ah, what a troublesome thing we have done.'

Tatsukō: 'Moshibe-san, do those whose lives have been taken live on like this, both of us? Do not utter such strange things.'

Harukuniwake: 'Spoken from the human world, it is what is called having one's life taken. However, human beings have their registry in the spiritual world. The physical body is merely a training ground for the spirit. Spoken from the spiritual world, we have not died; we have resurrected. Come now, from here there must surely be a place where the good and evil, righteousness and wickedness that we have accomplished in the manifest world during our lifetime are inspected. There we shall undergo inspection, and either ascend to heaven or be cast down into hell.'

Tatsukō: 'Oh, that is a serious matter. Might there not be some contrivance by which we can return to this world just once more?'

Harukuniwake: 'Since all things are according to the divine will of the Great Deity, Lord Susanoo, whether one wanders astray in the spirit world, resurrects to heaven, or returns backward to the phenomenal world is not a matter that we human beings can control. Now that things have come to this, there is no other path than to leave it entirely to the deities.'

Tatsukō: 'I had heard from you that there is a world after death, but I would not have thought that the life after death would continue with such distinctness. I had considered that we would retain a gaseous body and wander vaguely through midair, but now, as things are, my sense of touch, my perception, my thoughts, my emotions, and my heart of love all feel as though they have become a layer more precise and exact than during my lifetime. Is it not truly wondrous? I had indeed heard that a world after death exists, but I would not have thought it to be so clear a world.'

Harukuniwake: 'The human physical body is what is called the vessel of the spirit. Among the spirits, some ascend to heaven and become heavenly beings, while others fall into hell and become demons. The spirit that is to become a heavenly being is referred to from the aspect of the physical body as the Main Guardian Deity (Moto-shugojin), the benevolent spirit is called the Right Guardian Deity (Sei-shugojin), and the evil spirit is called the Subordinate Guardian Deity (Fuku-shugojin).'

Tatsukō: 'Within the human body, are there thus three distinct personalities divided into main, right, and subordinate?'
