Chapter 17: 天人歓迎 [1250]

天人歓迎〔1250〕

§ 1

Aided by Konohanahime, Harukuniwake and Tatsukō, with joyful hearts, advanced eagerly toward the east without any fixed path through the vastly wide heavenly realm of Chiukan, amidst the glowing colors of gold, silver, agate, giant clam, lapis lazuli, glass, and crystal standing among the trees, while singing the Sendenka and stepping in unison.

Harukuniwake: 'In Takaamahara dwell the eight million venerable Kami. The two pillars of Kamurogi and Kamuromi, by the divine words of Sumeōkami, rule the divine land of the sun. When the great deity Kamuizanagi performs the misogi and purification at the clear spring of Aoki in Odo of Tachibana in Himuka of Tsukushi, the four noble deities of the Harahido who are then born—I pray you to purge away all the sins, defilements, and transgressions that I have committed in my own body, quickly, and make me pure, I humbly beseech. I pray that you hear my words of Kototama, pricking up your eight ears like a little stag. O Sumeōkami, who art born as the primordial root of the universe, protect our entire company, O my heart, purify our hearts! Ah, Kannagara, Kami, Kami! May your divine spirits prosper and bless us!'

Harukuniwake respectfully offers these divine words before the hundred deities of the heavenly kami and earthly kami: 'In the precious divine land, the land of Kami, in Takaamahara dwell the eight million venerable Kami. The great ancestors who govern this world, the two pillars of Kamurogi and Kamuromi, revering the stern divine words, appeared as the Kami of awakening. The Kami who thinks upon this world, Omoikane no Kami, gathered the hundred and thousands of myriads of kami together at the riverbed of Ano. Having gathered them, they held a divine council, and the ruling Kami commanded and entrusted them, saying: 'The Luxuriant Land of Reeds of Fresh Rice Ears, as a peaceful land of great tranquility, do you rule over it!''

Thus having been entrusted, the wild and fierce kami were questioned through divine questioning, swept away through divine sweeping, and by asking and telling, even the single leaves of rooted trees, standing trees, and grasses were caused to cease speaking; and majestically parting the heavenly rock-seats and splitting the eightfold clouds that block the skies into a thousand parts, the divine protection descends from heaven—the land of the four quarters. Hearing that it is that true realm of Mahayura, they determined the Great Land of Yamato Hinotakami to be the land of Urayasu. At the base-rock below, they erected the palace pillars mightily, and with the chigi rising high in Takaamahara, they served the divine palace of the Lord Kami who dwells in pure tranquility. Covered by the heavenly divine grace and the grace of the sun, they dwell in hiding. In the land where they rule in peaceful tranquility, the Masahito who are born, committing transgressions and various sins they have created—may you recite and redress them swiftly, O Kannagara! Before your precious presence, we humbly pray.'

The heavenly sins are: breaking down ridges, filling up ditches, releasing irrigation pipes, sowing seeds repeatedly, setting skewers, skinning while alive, skinning backwards, and the filth-gates and numerous such sins of transgression—the heavenly sins which are thus distinguished. The earthly sins are: cutting the living skin and cutting the dead skin, the sin of defiling one's own mother Kugumi the fair-skinned, the hundred sins of oppressing and defiling one's own child, the sin of defiling mother and child together, the sin of defiling beasts, the hideous disasters of insects flying across the land, the hideous disasters of high deities called land-flying birds, the hundred disasters, the sin of striking down beasts and creating witchcraft—even more numerous sins will issue forth. If such manifold sins issue forth, with the divine words of the heavenly norito, cutting and cleaving the roots and tips of the heavenly sakaki tree, placing them all upon a thousand offering-seats, cutting and trimming the roots and tips of the heavenly sugaso, splitting them into eight needles, and taking and tearing them apart, proclaim completely and in detail the heavenly country's grace-giving Kototama and great norito bestowed by the imperial kami.

Having thus proclaimed, the heavenly kami push open the heavenly rock-door, and splitting the eightfold clouds that block the skies into a thousand parts, hear them with peaceful hearts. The earthly deities climb the peaks of high mountains and the ridges of small mountains, and cleanly parting the thickets of mountains high and low, they hear a hundred prayers. Having thus heard them, though there be no sins whatsoever called sins, like the breath of the Shinato wind blowing away the eightfold clouds, like the morning mist or evening mist being delightfully blown away by the breath of the Shinato wind,

like large and small boats moored at the wave-beaten shores of Ootsu having their bows cast off and their sterns unmoored and being pushed out and released into the deep ocean plain of a thousand fathoms, like the roots of trees growing yonder being struck down with a burning sickle or a sharp sickle forged by a blacksmith—even though not a single trace of dust or sin remain, the act of purifying and washing them away is made stronger from the tips of high mountains and the tips of short mountains, down to the rushing torrents and swift rivers where dwells the Kami Setsuoritsuhime.

She shall carry them out into the ocean plain. When she thus carries them out, the Kami Hayakitsuhime who dwells in the currents where the eight-hundred paths of salt and myriad paths of salt of the rough salt meet in eightfold layers—

While singing this Sendenka, which is an expanded Norito, they arrived at a section of a certain heavenly dantai. Numerous tennin, without distinction of danjiyo, formed orderly lines on both sides of the road of sūjifunin, raising up the Kototama filled with love and goodness, shouting 'Oo, Oo,' as if welcoming the arrival of the futari.

Here I find it necessary to state a few words concerning the clothing of the tennin and the state of its transformation. Now, since the garments of the tennin correspond to their wisdom, all those who are in Tengoku wear respective clothing according to the degree of their wisdom. Among them, the attire of those possessing the highest wisdom appears remarkably more brilliant compared to the clothing of other tennin. Moreover, the clothing of the especially prominent ones shines forth like flames, or illuminates the four quarters like a brilliant light. The garments of those whose wisdom is slightly inferior have a radiance and appear pure white, yet they look somewhat dim and hazy, lacking a blazing brilliance; furthermore, those whose wisdom is next to this wear clothing corresponding to it, and its colors are also various, by no means uniform. However, the tennin who exist in the highest and deepest heavenly spiritual realms never use clothing or the like. Because the clothing of the tennin corresponds to their wisdom, it also corresponds to truth. Why is this so? Because all wisdom comes from the divine truth. Therefore, rather than saying that the clothing of the tennin depends upon wisdom, it might be more appropriate to say that it depends upon the degree of divine truth. Thus, the color shining like flames corresponds to the goodness of love, and its brilliance corresponds to the truth originating from goodness. The fact that their clothing is sometimes bright yet pure white, sometimes lacks radiance, and has various colors that are not uniform, corresponds to the fact that the light of divine goodness and divine truth shines upon it but little, and tennin whose wisdom is still insufficient receive and retain it in ways that are various, mixed, and not uniform. Furthermore, the reason why tennin residing in the highest and deepest heavenly spiritual realms do not use clothing is due to the purity and undefiled nature of their spiritual bodies. To be pure and undefiled means precisely that it corresponds to being stark naked. Moreover, tennin possess many garments, sometimes taking them off, sometimes putting them on, keeping those that are unnecessary stored away for a while, and when the time for their use arrives, wearing them again, and these garments are all bestowed by the Lord God. There are various transformations in their clothing: when they are in the first and second states, it shines brightly, white and pure; when they are in the third and fourth states, it appears somewhat cloudy. This arises from the law of correspondence, because according to the degree of wisdom and spiritual perception, there are respective changes in the state of the tennin. By the way, I shall describe the clothing of those who are in the hell realms.

Those who have fallen into the lowest depths of the land are also permitted to wear a kind of clothing, but since their evil spirits have escaped outside of all truths, the garments they wear, owing to the degree of their madness and falsity, are either torn, frayed, or look wretched like rags, and their defilement is to such a degree that it is utterly unbearable to look upon. Yet they are truly unable to wear any clothing other than this. Moreover, when evil spirits in the hell realms wear clothing of beautiful luster, because it runs counter to the law of correspondence, their bodies suffer, their heads ache, and they feel as though their bodies are being squeezed, so that they cannot wear it at all. Therefore, the reason why the Great Kami permitted them to wear clothing corresponding to their evil state is His great benevolence to prevent their evil appearance, falsity, and defilement from being exposed in stark nakedness.

Various Tennin, clad in diverse ifuku, knowing by the senji of the Ōkami that Harukuniwake and Tatsukō were entering this dantai, had prepared the kuwangei and were waiting eagerly, thinking it was still not soon enough. Among the amata Tennin, one Tennin, wearing the most brilliantly shining and seijiyō irui, advanced close to the side of Harukuniwake, and saying 'Ūō,' displayed the sentiment of kuwangei from the bottom of their heart. Harukuniwake, feeling both surprise and joy at the unexpected taigū, made a teinei eshiyaku, firmly grasped the hand of the Tennin, and tried to say something, but for some reason his kōzetsu grew kauchiyoku, and he was unable to utter even a single ichigon. Therefore, Harukuniwake decided to express his sentiment of gratitude through the expression of his facial features. The amata Tennin gathered around the front, back, left, and right of Harukuniwake, shouting 'Uō uō,' while singing, dancing, and going wild in a frenzy, forgetting themselves in order to comfort his travel-fatigue and devoting all their strength to his reception. Tatsukō, with his mouth open from excessive joy, stood dumbfounded and merely shouted 'Ā ā.' However, in the celestial realm, the sound 'A' signifies the expression of joy, so this single utterance by Tatsukō, contrasting with the speechlessness of Harukuniwake, was most respected by the Tennin and became the object of high praise. Whenever Tennin converse with humans at all, the Tennin never use their own language, but use the language of the other party and the language known to the other party, and never use any language unknown to that person. When a Tennin speaks with a human, they transform themselves toward the human and harmonize with them. This harmonization causes both parties to enter into similar thoughts and emotional states. However, because Harukuniwake did not think that the Tennin in the dantai were spirits possessing a physical body, he used the language of the Tennin, which is why Harukuniwake was taken aback.

All human thoughts adhere to memory and become the source of that language, so it may be said that both are within the same language. Moreover, when Tennin and spirits approach a human, if they transform themselves toward the other and reach the point of harmonizing with that person, all memories of that person appear before the Tennin. When a Tennin converses with a human, harmonizing with that person is essentially harmonizing with the person's spiritual thoughts; however, because that spiritual thought stream flows into the natural world's thoughts, adheres to its memory, and does not depart, the person's language appears like that of a Tennin, and the person's knowledge appears like the knowledge of a Tennin. Such things are due to the special blessings of the Ōkami, causing harmonization to exist between Tennin and humans, making it as if the celestial realm were cast into the interior of humanity. However, the emotional state of modern humanity lacks the view of celestial humans as in ancient times, and harmonization with Tennin is also difficult. Instead, they have come to the point of harmonizing with evil spirits outside the celestial realm. The spirits do not believe that the one telling such a story is a human, but firmly believe that it is none other than themselves existing within this person.

Here Harukuniwake tried to announce that he was a spirit still possessing a physical body, and to explicitly state that he had not yet advanced to the realm of Tennin, but for some reason he could not utter even a single word. The reason was because the love and goodness, true faith, wisdom, and clear realization appropriate for the Tennin of the Second Heaven were not equipped within him. Here, Harukuniwake was welcomed by the various dantai of Tennin, continued his mute journey, came to barely utter only the five primal father-sounds of a-o-u-e-i, narrowly avoided putting himself to too great of shame, and was able to successfully tour this single dantai while leaving a relatively favorable impression upon the Tennin and departing from this place—which was truly wondrous if one were to call it wondrous. From this point on, Harukuniwake, once again through the guidance of Yoshikohime-no-Mikoto, was granted wisdom and clear realization, toured each dantai of the Second Heaven, and the story of his proceeding further to the highest First Heaven and the Spirit Realm will be narrated orally from the next section.

Kannagara Kamutsunagi Masase.

原文（近代文語）

種々さまざまの衣服をつけたる諸々の天人は、治国別、竜公両人の此団体に入り来ることを、大神の宣示に仍つて前知し、歓迎の準備を整へて、今や遅しと待つてゐたのである。数多の天人の中から、最も美はしく光り輝いてゐる清浄の衣類を着用した一人の天人は、治国別の側近く進み来り、『ウーオー』と言ひながら、心の底より歓迎の意を表示してゐる。治国別も意外の待遇に且つ驚き且喜びながら、叮嚀に会釈をなし、固く天人の手を握りしめて、何事か言はむとしたが、何故か口舌硬直して、一言も発することが出来なかつた。茲に於て治国別は其顔面の表情を以て、感謝の意を示す事としたのである。数多の天人は治国別の前後左右に群り来り、『ウオーウオー』と叫びながら、且歌ひ、且舞ひ踊り狂うて、其旅情を慰めむと吾を忘れて其優待に全力を尽してゐる。竜公は余りの嬉しさに口をあけた儘、ポカンとして、只『アーアー』とのみ叫んでゐる。併し天国に於ては、『ア』といふ声は喜びを表白する意味であるから、竜公の此一言は治国別の無言に引替へ、最も天人間から尊重され、且賞揚の的となつたのである。天人が総て人間と相語る時は、天人は決して自らの言語を用ひず、其相手の言語及相手が知れる所の言語を用ひ、其人の知らざる言語は一切用ひない事になつてゐる。天人の人間ともの云ふ時は、自己を転じて人間に向ひ、これと相和合するものである。此和合は両者をして相似の想念情態中に入らしむるものである。併しながら、治国別は天人の団体に於ては、これを肉体のある精霊とは思はなかつた為に、天人の語を用ひたから、治国別が面くらつたのである。

凡て人間の想念は記憶に附着して、其言語の根源となるが故に、此両者は共に同一の言語中にありと云つても良いのである。且又天人及精霊の人間に向ひ来るや、自ら転じて彼に向ひ、彼と和合するに至れば、其人のすべての記憶は、天人の前に現出するものである。天人が人間と談話するに当り、其人と和合するは、人の霊的思想とつまり和合するものであるけれども、其霊的想念流れて、自然界想念中に入り、其記憶に附着し離れざるに仍り、其人の言語は天人の如く見え、又其人の知識は天人の知識の如く見ゆるものである。斯の如きは大神の特別の御恵に依つて、天人と人間との間に和合あらしめ給ひ、恰も天界を人間の内に投入したるが如くならしめ給ふに仍るのである。併しながら現代人間の情態は、太古に於ける天的人間の観なく、天人との和合も亦難かしい。却て天界以外の悪精霊と和合するに立至つたのである。精霊は斯く物語る者の、人間なることを信ぜず、この人の内にある自分共なりと信じ切つて居るのである。

茲に治国別は自分が未だ肉体のある精霊なる事を告げて、未だ天人の域に進んでゐない事をあから様に告げようと努めたけれど、何故か一言も発することが出来なかつた。其故は第二天国の天人に相応すべき愛善と信真と智慧と証覚とが、備はつてゐなかつたからである。ここに治国別は天人の諸団体に歓迎され、唖の旅行を続けて、只アオウエイの五大父音を僅に発する様になり、辛うじて余り大きな恥をかかず、此一つの団体を首尾良く巡覧し、且つ天人に比較的好感を与へて此処を去る事を得たのは、実に不思議と云へば不思議な位であつた。是より治国別は再び木花姫命の御導きに仍つて、智慧と証覚を与へられ、第二天国の各団体を巡歴し、進んで最高第一天国及霊国に進む物語は次節より口述する事とする。

惟神霊幸倍坐世。
