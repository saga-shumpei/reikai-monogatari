Chapter 20: 間接内流 [1253]

間接内流〔1253〕

§ 1

To divide the Tenkai of Takaamahara into two, namely Tengoku and Reigoku, is as stated previously.

Generally speaking, the land of the sun, that is Tengoku, when compared to the human body, corresponds to the heart and the entire body, and corresponds to all things that should belong to the heart.

Also, the land of the moon, that is Reigoku, corresponds to its lungs and the entire body, and corresponds to all various organs that should belong to the lungs.

Thus, the heart and the lungs are two domains in man that should be compared to a shōuchiu and a shōtenchi.

The heart by means of arteries and veins, and the lungs by means of nerves and motor fibers, become the rulers within the human body, and wherever power emanates and wherever action takes place, one never fails to recognize the cooperation of these two rights.

Even within the naibun of each person, that is, within the Reikai which forms the spiritual personality of man, there are also two domains; one is called the domain of will and the other is called the domain of intellect.

The will governs the two domains of the human naibun through affection toward good, and the intellect through affection toward truth.

These two domains also correspond to the two domains of the lungs and the heart within the physical body.

Therefore, the heart is Tengoku and corresponds to the domain of will, and the lungs are Reigoku and correspond to the domain of intellect.

In Takaamahara, too, there is a correspondence such as the above.

Tengoku is the will-power of Takaamahara, governed by the virtue of love, and Reigoku is the intellect-power of Takaamahara, governed by the virtue of faith.

Therefore, the relationship between Tengoku and Reigoku completely corresponds to the relationship between the heart and the lungs in man.

In sacred words, to indicate the will by means of the heart, and also to indicate the virtue of love, and to indicate the truth of intellect and faith by means of the respiration of the lungs, is because it is based upon this correspondence.

Also, though affection is not in the heart nor comes from the heart, to attribute it to the heart is in order to be based upon the principle of correspondence.

The correspondence between the above two domains of Takaamahara and the heart and lungs is the general correspondence between Takaamahara and man.

And for the various limbs, various organs, and internal organs of the human body, there is a correspondence that is not general in this manner.

Now, let us explain by comparing the entirety of Takaamahara to a giant.

That which is in the head of the giant, namely Tenkai, dwells in love, peace, innocence, perception, and wisdom, and accordingly dwells in joy and happiness; thus throughout Tenkai there is nothing that can be compared to the good virtue in this head.

The divine virtue flows into the human head and all things belonging to the head, and corresponds thereto.

Therefore, the human head is to be compared to the highest Tengoku and Reigoku of Takaamahara.

Next, that which is in the chest of the giant, namely Tenkai, dwells within the good virtues of benevolence and faith, and flows into the human chest, corresponding thereto.

1. That which belongs to the lumbar region and generative organs in the giant, namely Tenkai, dwells in so-called conjugal love; this is the state of the second Tengoku.

1. That which is in the legs dwells within the lowest virtues of Tenkai, namely natural and spiritual good virtues.

1. That which is in the arms and hands dwells in the power of truth that comes forth from within good virtue.

1. That which is in the eyes dwells in wisdom, that which is in the ears dwells in attention and obedience, and that which belongs to the nose and mouth dwells in perception.

Also, that which belongs to the mouth and tongue dwells in speech coming forth from intellect and perception, that which belongs to the kidneys dwells in various truths that research, investigate, analyze, and correct, and that which belongs to the liver, pancreas, and spleen excels in variously refining good and truth.

In all instances, the divine divinity flows into each local part analogous within the human body and corresponds thereto.

Since the inner flow from Tenkai enters into the workings and uses of the various limbs, and thus manifests concrete results, it is here that the correspondence comes to be realized.

1. People call one who has wisdom and discernment one who has a head, or say that his brain is dense, or praise him by saying he has a good head; they also call one rich in benevolence a friend of the chest, or say his heart is beautiful, or he is a person of good spirit, or his spirit and disposition are good.

They call one superior in perception one who has an acute sense of smell, or say his nose is high; they say of one excelled in prudence that his visual sense is sharp, or call him a demon's eye.

They say of a powerful person that his hands are long, or say that he is capable; and they call one who determines what he aims at based upon love and the heart one whose action comes from the heart, or comes from the bottom of his heart, or has deep sympathy.

The party of Harukuniwake is now in the midst of touring the most central part of the Daini Tengoku, which corresponds to the heart in the human jintai.

Thus, the organization of Tengoku is divided into three sections of upper, middle, and lower in the Saikautengoku, the intermediate Tengoku is likewise divided into three sections of upper, middle, and lower, and the lowest Tengoku is also divided into three sections.

Each section of Tengoku is constituted by individual groups, and according to the degree of the virtue of aizen and the wisdom of shōkaku, hundreds of them stand separately and individually, so that it is utterly impossible to calculate them clearly.

The Reikoku is similarly divided; according to the good virtues of faith and wisdom and the degree of wisdom and shōkaku, the Reikoku is broadly divided into three sections, and furthermore, innumerable groups standing independently and individually are formed.

Moreover, even within a single group, depending on the degree of love, faith, and wisdom and shōkaku, some occupy seats in the center while others occupy seats on the periphery, by no means being uniform.

Such shifts in the love, faith, and shōkaku of the heavenly beings exist because the natural law wherein the so-called victors guide the inferior and the inferior follow the victors is made in a kannagara way, so that all people are at ease according to their respective fundo, without the slightest anxiety, resentment, or dissatisfaction arising, and they spend a life of extreme peace.
