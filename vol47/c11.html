Chapter 11: 手苦駄女 [1244]

手苦駄女〔1244〕

§ 1

The physical body of a ningen is the so-called vessel of the seirei. And it becomes a training ground for tennin, or alternatively serves as the lair of jaki and akuki. Thus, the reason the same ningen undergoes various transformations is that, depending on how the emotion of love—which the ningen takes as primary—manifests, one either becomes a tennin, strays into the seireikai, or becomes an eukuwaiteki jinbutsu of jigoku. Furthermore, while a ningen is living in the gense, all shisaku are shizenteki; therefore, as a seirei which is the hontai of the ningen, one does not join the dantaichiu of those seirei. However, when that saunen keizenly ridatsus the physical body, while in that interval, being among the seirei, one sometimes appears within the dantaichiu to which kakuji shiyozoku. At this time, one whose seirei observes him can easily distinguish him from all other moromoro seirei. For a seirei possessing a physical body, like the seirei of all people mentioned earlier, appears sunk in thought, wandering silently back and forth, right and left, heeding not others, looking precisely as though he were blind. If a seirei attempts to speak with him, that seirei suddenly vanishes like smoke. As for how a ningen ritsus the physical body and enters the seireikai, a ningen at this time is in a peculiar state, neither in sleep nor in wakefulness, and when in this state, that ningen merely thinks only that he is fully awake. And the various sensations at this time are clear and crisp, differing not in the slightest from the time when the physical body is at its most awake. The sensations of the five senses and the tactile feelings of the limbs and body become especially exquisite, a realm which the sensations and tactile feelings at the time of physical awakening cannot possibly reach. When in this state, upon seeing tennin and seirei, one can recognize their seiki actively gleaming with majestic vigor, and furthermore one can clearly hear their language. What is even more wondrous is that one can intimately come into contact with those tennin and seirei. The reason for this is that nothing belonging to the ningen physical body mixes in during this interval. Calling this state in the reikai the time of bodily separation, from the standpoint of the genkai this is termed death. At this time the ningen does not perceive himself to be inside that physical body, nor does he perceive himself to have stepped outside that physical body. The reason a seirei is a ningen in its naibun, namely its spiritual lifetime, is spoken of thusly by viewing it from the standpoint of matters belonging to its saunen and intention. For matters during this interval are the naibun of man, acting in accordance with the law of reishutaijū, and this is the very reason why man is made man. Since man cannot go outside his naibun, the seirei is precisely the ningen. The physical body of man may also be called the house or vessel of the ningen. When the physical body of man—namely the active organ of the seirei—reaches a point where it can no longer fulfill all its various sensory faculties in the natural world, in accordance with the various saunen and diverse jiyōdau possessed by the seirei which is its own hontai, viewed from the physical body this is called death. There is an inner communication between the seirei and the breathing as well as the heartbeat. This is because the saunen of the seirei communicates mutually with the breathing, and the emotion coming from its love communicates with the heart. Therefore, the very time when the activities of the lungs and heart completely cease is the time when the spirit and the flesh suddenly separate. The breathing of the lungs and the heartbeat of the heart are the lifeline connecting the seirei itself, which is the hontai of the ningen; when these two faculties are destroyed, the seirei instantly returns to itself, independent, and able to be resurrected.

Thus, the physical body—namely the shell of the seirei—was separated from that seirei, and therefore gradually grew cold, ultimately reaching the point of putrefaction and dissolution.

人間の肉体は所謂精霊の容器である。そして天人の養成所ともなり、或は邪鬼、悪鬼共の巣窟となるものである。斯の如く同じ人間にして種々の変化を来すのは、人間が主とする所の愛の情動如何に依つて、或は天人となり、或は精霊界に迷ひ、或は地獄の妖怪的人物となるのである。さうして、人間が現世に住んでゐる間は、すべての思索は自然的なるが故に、人間の本体たる精霊として、其精霊の団体中に加はることはない、併しながら其想念が迥然として肉体を離脱する時は、其間精霊の中にあるを以て或は各自所属の団体中に現はるることがある。此時或精霊が彼を見る者は容易に之を他の諸々の精霊と分別することが出来るのである。何とならば肉体を持つてゐる精霊は、前に述べた万公の精霊の如く、思ひに沈みつつ、黙然として前後左右に徘徊し、他を省みざること、恰も盲目者の如くに見ゆるからである。若しも精霊が之とものを言はむとすれば、彼の精霊は忽然として煙の如く消失するものである。人間は如何にして肉体を脱離し、精霊界に入るかと云ふに、此時の人間は睡眠にも居らず、覚醒にもあらざる一種異様の情態に居るものであつて、此情態に在る時は、其人間は、只自分は充分に覚醒して居るものとのみ思うて居るものである。而して此際に於ける諸々の感覚は醒々として、恰も肉体の最も覚醒せる時に少しも変りはないのである。五官の感覚も、四肢五体の触覚も特に精妙となることは肉体覚醒時の諸感覚や触覚の到底及ばざる所である。此情態にあつて、天人及び精霊を見る時は、其精気凛々として活躍するを認むべく、又彼等の言語をも明瞭に聞く事を得らるるのである。尚も不可思議とすべきは、彼等天人及び精霊に親しく接触し得ることである。此故は人間肉体に属するもの、少しも此間に混入し来らないからである。此情態を呼んで霊界にては肉体離脱の時と云ひ、現界より見ては之を死と称するのである。此時人間は其肉体の中に自分の居る事を覚えず、又其肉体の外に出て居ることをも覚えないものである。人間は其内分即ち霊的生涯に於て精霊なりといふ理由は、其想念及び意思に所属せる事物の上から見てしか云ふのである。何とならば此間の事物は人の内分にして即ち霊主体従の法則に依つて活動するから、人をして人たらしむる所以である。人は其内分以外に出づることを得ないものであるから、精霊即ち人間である。人の肉体は人間の家又は容器と云つても可いものである。人の肉体にして即ち精霊の活動機関にして、自己の本体たる精霊が有する所の諸々の想念と諸多の情動に相応じて、其自然界に於ける諸官能を全うし得ざるに立到つた時は、肉体上より見て之を死と呼ぶのである。精霊と呼吸及心臓の鼓動との間に内的交通なるものがある。そは精霊の想念とは呼吸と相通じ、其愛より来る情動は心臓と通ずる故である。夫だから肺臓心臓の活動が全く止む時こそ、霊と肉とが忽ち分離する時である。肺臓の呼吸と心臓の鼓動とは、人間の本体たる精霊其ものを繋ぐ所の命脈であつて、此二つの官能を破壊する時は精霊は忽ちおのれに帰り、独立し復活し得るのである。

斯くて肉体即ち精霊の躯殻は其精霊より分離されたが故に次第に冷却して、遂に腐敗糜爛するに至るものである。

§ 2

The reason why the human seirei has naiteki kautsū with respiration and the shinzau is that, regarding the activities concerning the seishi of a human, it is generally and individually based upon both organs of the lungs and the shinzau. Thus, although the human seirei, namely the hontai, is after bunrigo, it still remains within that body for a shibaraku, and waiting for the kodō of the shinzau to cease entirely, it escapes in zenbu. And this is a genshiyō produced according to how the shiin of the human is. In some baahi the kodō of the shinzau continues for a long time, and in other baahi it may not be long. When this kodō stops completely, the seirei, which is the hontai of the human, is able to fukukuwatsu directly into the reikai. However, this is what the Ōkami of Mizu no Mitama brings about, and is not something that human beings can accomplish by themselves.

And the reason why the seirei is not separated from its nikutai until the kodō of the shinzau ceases entirely is because the shinzau corresponds to emotional agitation. All emotional agitation belongs to love, and love is the hontai of human life. Because human beings rely upon this love, there is the warmth of each life, and while this harmony continues, there is a corresponding existence, so the life of the seirei is still within the nikutai.

The seirei of a person preserves the final thought held at that moment upon reaching the departure period of the nikutai, namely the final time of death, for a short while after death; but as time passes, the seirei returns into the various thoughts that it ordinarily held when it existed in the original world. Now, these various thoughts come from the general emotional agitation of that seirei, namely the emotional agitation of love which serves as its principal. The inner aspect of a person's heart, namely the seirei, perceives and senses as though it were being pulled from the nikutai, and almost as though it were being extracted. The saying of the ancients that the final single thought draws forth the life after death is an error. Since the ordinary emotional agitation of love inevitably governs this in any case, a human being must purify their mitama from ordinary times, speak goodness, do good for the sake of goodness, and obtain wisdom and realization beforehand.

Now, Harukuniwake and Ryūkō, with an extremely cautious attitude, listened intently with their deer-like ears raised, determined not to let even a single word escape concerning the questions and answers between the red and white guards and the seirei coming forward here. Those who came sauntering thither were two seirei, male and female. The red-faced guard turned toward the two persons and roared:

Akaminejinsei: 'Hey, you two, wait a moment. This is the checkpoint of Yotsutsuji. It is necessary to examine you regarding your actions while alive.'

The two looked timid and simply said:

Toku and Kanoe: 'Yes.'

And squatting down right on the path, they stopped there.

Akaminejinsei: 'What manner of persons are you two?'

Toku: 'Yes, I am Toku, the chief clerk of a draper's shop.'

Kanoe: 'I am a geisha called Kanoe.'

Akaminejinsei: 'Hm, I thought so. You two committed a shinju and have come this far in high spirits, hand in hand, behaving so flirtatiously. Well, well, what a carefree pair you are!'

Toku and Kanoe: 'Yes, we truly have no face to show you. Far from being carefree—not at all, not at all—just ahead of here, after crossing the Sanzu River and having our grease squeezed out thoroughly by the old grandmother, and after being put to all sorts of shame, we have managed to escape and reach this place by the skin of our teeth.'

Akaminejinsei: 'You called Toku, come over this way for a moment and wait. I shall examine the woman first.'

Toku and Kanoe: 'Yes, please, together, if possible... we would like you to examine us. For we came having made a firm vow that through two or three worlds, even to the end of the fields or the depths of the mountains, wherever we may go we will never part, so we cannot be separated even for a single minute.'

Akaminejinsei: 'Do you think such selfish matters will pass in the reikai? Wait aside for a little while... Hey, Shirosama, please throw this Toku into the pigpen for a while.'

Shirosama: 'Understood.' (Natsuhiko: '...' / Kamehiko: '...')
