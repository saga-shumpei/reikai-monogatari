Chapter 13: 下層天国 [1246]

下層天国〔1246〕

§ 1

In the communities of the east and the west in the heavenly realm of Takaamahara, the Tennin who dwell there reside in the virtue of Shinai.

Those of the east clearly and distinctly perceive the virtue of love, whereas those of the west dwell only in those who perceive it somewhat dimly.

The communities of the south and the north issue from the virtue of Aishin, and those who dwell in Chieshiyōkaku abide there everlastingly.

Among them, those who dwell in the south possess Shiyōkaku and Kuōmiyō clearly, while the north is inhabited only by those in whom Shiyōkaku shines dimly.

The Tennin who exist in the Reigoku of Takaamahara and the Tennin who exist in Tengoku all alike observe the foregoing order.

The point of slight difference is that the one advances according to the virtue of love, and the other forever dwells according to the light of faith issuing from the virtue of goodness.

The love existing in this Tengoku is love directed toward Kami, and the Shinkuō proceeding therefrom is entirely Chie and Shiyōkaku.

Moreover, the love existing in the Reigoku is love directed toward the Rinjin, and this is called Jinai; the true light issuing from this Jinai is Chie, or else this is called faith.

In the divine land of Hisakata no Takaamahara, there is no distinction of Jikan, Kūkan, and Shiyunkashiutō.

There is only the change of state for each of the Tennin.

Just as in the manifest world, there is continuity and progression in all things of the heavenly realm, yet the Tennin have no concept of Jikan and Kūkan.

In Hisakata no Takaamahara there are no years, no months and days, and no time; there is only the transition of states.

Where there is a transition of states, there is nothing but states themselves.

All people of the manifest world are unable to break away from that concept called time.

If the Tennin all contemplate this from the perspective of states, whatever comes from time within human thoughts will all entirely become thoughts of states when entering among the Tennin.

Spring and morning become thoughts concerning the love, goodness, and state of Shiyōkaku where the Tennin existing in the first state dwell.

Summer and noon become thoughts concerning the love, goodness, and state of Shiyōkaku where the Tennin in the second state dwell.

Autumn and evening become thoughts concerning the love, goodness, and state of Shiyōkaku where the Tennin in the third state dwell.

Winter and night become thoughts concerning these states held by the spirits who have fallen into hell.

Kototamawake-mikoto, turning toward Kunikuniwake, continues to explain the heavenly realm communities.

Kunikuniwake: 'Indeed, a place called Tengoku is a splendid land with an order beyond our imagination; it seems unlikely that sinful human beings like us will ever ascend to this land in the future.'

'By no means is there any such reason; please be at ease. This place is the lowest Tengoku, and yet above this there is an intermediate Tengoku and also a highest Tengoku. Furthermore, besides these, there is what is called Reigoku, where Tennin appropriate to it continue their lives.'

'The Tennin who can ascend to that highest Tengoku must have accumulated extraordinary good virtues and excel in Chieshiyōkaku, otherwise they surely cannot go there.'

'As stated in the sacred words of Izunonomitama as well, if one but returns to the pure and innocent heart of a newborn babe, one immediately corresponds with the first Tengoku, and through the inner flow of divine dignity, one can ascend surprisingly easily.'

'Indeed, yet we cannot become like the heart of an infant no matter what, so we are troubled. However, in Tengoku as well, will a sun like that of the natural world appear, I wonder?'

'Look, it is shining in the eastern sky just as you see. Can you not worship it?'

'Yes, it seems that I am not yet equipped with the visual power merely to worship the sun of Takaamahara, regretting it as I do.'

'That is likely so. Since you still have remaining duties toward the actual world, you cannot worship it like the sun seen from the manifest world. The sun of Tengoku is the divine dignity of Izunonomitama being manifested and appearing here as the sun. Therefore, it is very different in flavor from the sun of the manifest world. In Reigoku, it appears as the moon of the Great Deity of Mizunonomitama, and in Tengoku, it appears again as the sun. Then, the moon of Reigoku shines like the light of the sun seen from the manifest world, and the sun of Tengoku has a brilliance magnified sevenfold compared to the light of the sun seen in the manifest world. And the sun manifests true love, the moon manifests true faith, and the stars manifest knowledge of goodness and truth. Therefore, in the sacred words of Mizunonomitama,'

言依別命は治国別に向つて尚も天国団体の説明を続けて居る。

『成程、然し吾々は如何しても赤子の心にはなれないので困ります。然し天国にも矢張り自然界の如き太陽がおでましになるのでせうな』

§ 2

1. 'The light of the moon shall be as the light of the sun, and the light of the sun shall be sevenfold, as the light of seven days.'

2. 'When I shall destroy thee, I will cover the heaven, and make the stars thereof dark, and cover the sun with a cloud, and the moon shall not give her light.'

3. 'I will make the shining light of heaven dark over you, and set darkness upon your land.'

4. 'I will darken the sun when it riseth, and the moon shall not cause her light to shine.'

5. 'The sun shall be darkened like a maufu, and the moon shall become as the earth, and the stars of heaven shall fall unto the earth.'

6. 'After these tribulations, straightway the sun shall be darkened, and the moon shall not give her light, and the stars shall fall from heaven.'

It is so written. These holy words were surely given to reveal the state in which love and faith have utterly perished. In today's genkai, the sun and moon of the shizenkai shine across the tenkū; yet because they have destroyed the love comparable to the sun, the faith comparable to the moon, and the knowledge of goodness and truth comparable to the stars, the genjitsukai, which is the reflection of tengoku, has fallen into such utter disorder. It is of this that the holy words speak: ...When these shiyotoku perish, these shiyotentai shall be darkened, and losing their light, shall fall from heaven... We may infer how great and of what nature the shinai of Ōkami is by comparison with the sun that shines in the genkai. That is to say, we may perceive that the love of God is exceedingly fervent. If a ningen is truly able to believe this, he will understand that the love of God-sama is a degree stronger still in comparison with the fervent heat of the sun in the genjitsukai. Moreover, Ōkami-sama does not shine directly in the middle of the sky of Takamanohara like the sun of the genjitsukai; as His shinai descends step by step, the degree of its fervor gradually softens. This degree of softening forms a kind of zone shining around the heavenly sun, and in order that various tenjin may not harm themselves by the inner flow of this sun, they appropriately cover their bodies with spiritual garments like thin clouds. Therefore, the positions of the various tengoku in Takamanohara become distant from or close to the august presence of Ōkami, according to the degree to which the tenjin dwelling there receive the love of God. Furthermore, the tenjin dwelling in the high places of Takamanohara, that is, the highest tengoku, dwell near the august side of Ōkami, who is manifested as the sun. However, those in the lowest tengoku communities dwell in the virtue of faith, and therefore they are the furthest removed from Ōkami, who has manifested Himself as the sun. Because this place is the lowest part even of the lowest third tengoku of Takamanohara, to behold the august light of Ōkami, who has manifested Himself as the sun, is extremely distant, and it is not clearly understood as one beholds the sun of the genjitsukai. And those who are most unrighteous, such as those dwelling in the hell of the dark realm, are extremely far removed from the presence of Ōkami-sama and turn their backs upon the light of the sun. Moreover, the degree of separation from God in that dark realm is proportionate to the degree of turning away from the path of goodness. Therefore, the wickedest ones can ultimately see not even the slightest light, and fall into the lowest hell of pitch darkness.'

Yotahiko: 'Ah, thank you very much. Since we still lack the wisdom, consciousness, and enlightenment that stem from goodness and truth, we suppose we cannot behold the august form of God.'

Speaker: 'When God manifests Himself directly as the sun before the tenjin of the third tengoku, their eyes are blinded, their heads ache, and they cannot endure the bitterness. Therefore Ōkami-sama descends as a single tenjin to the communities corresponding to goodness, corresponding to truth, and corresponding to wisdom, consciousness, and enlightenment, and intimately bestows His teachings.'

Yotahiko: 'Well, I am greatly enlightened. When I return to the genjitsukai from now on, with this understanding I shall act for the sake of goodness and for the sake of truth. Ah, Kamunagara Tamachihachimase.'

'Now I shall guide you to the dantai of the tennin.'

Harukuniwake and Tatsukō:

'Yes, thank you,'

expressing their kanshiya, followed behind Kotoyoriwake and proceeded isoiso.

As they went down the hill for about nisanchiyō, suddenly transforming into a kuwakuō firelight, Kotoyoriwake sank his figure toward the tenkū. The futari felt as if they had been deprived of a lamp in anya, and kneeling upon the daichi, choking with kanshiya:

'Ah, arigata, motsutai! Because the virtue of our love and zen is not complete, because the light of our shinshin and shiyōkaku is not bright, and consequently because the light of chie and shiyōkaku is weak, awe-inspiringly Sumeōkami did not manifest as the taiyō of tengoku, but manifested His body as Kotoyoriwake-no-mikoto and guided us right up to koko—is that so? Ah, arigata, arigata, oh the divine virtue of the Ōkami of infinite jinji!'

they were drowning in tears of kanshiya.

'Say, Sensei, what shall we do from now on? If we are abandoned in a place like this, do we not understand at all how we should go? Did not the communities of the tennin of east, west, north, and south, which were so clearly visible just before, slip out of our line of sight before we knew it?'

'They say that a lion casts its cub into the bottom of a valley after three days; this is entirely the providence of infinite jinji of the kami. That is why the sacred words of Hinomoto-Kyō also state, "Do not lean on the master as a staff, do not make human beings your strength, act solely according to the will of the kami." It was our mistake that we indulged in Kotoyoriwake-sama guiding us, let down our guard, and leaned upon him. That is why the kami have distanced themselves from within our thoughts. Our love and shin are still not thoroughly developed. Ah, Kannagara Tamashii Sakiwamae Tamae!'

they clasped their hands, choked with tears of emotion, and concentrated their prayers upon the Lord God.
