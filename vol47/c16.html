Chapter 16: Spirit 丹 [1249]

霊丹〔1249〕

§ 1

Residing upon Tenkyōzan, as the divine transformation of Konohanahime, she guided them and leaving behind the third heavenly world, treading apart the five-colored clouds, heading toward the east they ascend.

Harukuniwake and Tatsukō, how indeed must it have been for them; their eyes grew dizzy, their heads ached, their legs failed, their chests thudded, and both hands lost their strength, trembling and shivering—there was nothing else to be done.

The divine transformation of Konohanahime, like a small boat of the shore hoisting its full sails before a favorable wind advancing, without any trouble—how wondrous—ascends tap, tap, tap, tap.

Harukuniwake and Tatsukō, even their breathing growing faint and intermittent, squeezing out voices at the very limit of life:

Harukuniwake: 'Hey, hey, I say, O Konohanahime no Mikoto no Kamutsukasa, pray wait a while for us! Though I know not by what reason, unawares my eyes grow dizzy, consciousness wanes, strength fails, advancement here is brought to a dead stop, and already I cannot advance a single step. I beseech you out of compassion, lead us two once more back to the rear, and grant us your salvation; this I earnestly implore. Whether the Asahi shines or clouds over, whether the moon fills or wanes, even though the great earth should sink, when shall we ever forget the precious divine grace? Now the existence of heaven is constituted by the high virtue of the divine love, truth, and goodness, Filled and overflowing with love and goodness, it must be the divine land. Since you too are a precious deity, you surely will not cast aside and forsake the sufferings of us two. Make us turn back, O Kannagara, Before the august presence of Konohanahime, We pray with the very limit of life! Ah, Kannagara, O deities, May your divine spirits flourish and be blessed!'

So singing, their voices too growing broken and scattered, they came right up to the entrance of the second heaven and collapsed flatly upon the ground with a thud. Tatsukō could not utter a single word, leaving his mouth agape like an idiot while faintly moving his fingertips intermittently. Konohanahime, without even looking back, transformed into a giant light and concealed her form in one quarter of the heavens. Harukuniwake looked back and gazed upward,

Harukuniwake: 'Ah, I have erred! Ah, I have erred! How foolish of me, controlled by the desire of self-love, to have sought the rescue of Konohanahime-sama amidst the sufferings of my own body. Have I forgotten the divine teaching which says, "Do not lean on your master as a crutch, do not rely upon men," just when the time of true victory had arrived? Ah, what a wretched thing man is! Tatsukō is already at his last gasp; in such a heaven as this, must we throw away even the lives of our refined spirits? Ah, what had I better do? O Great Deity Kuninotokotachi, Great Deity Toyokuninushi, Great Deity Kamususanowo, pray, pray, save us from this desperate plight once more!'

His complexion turning pale, becoming almost like a dead person, the two hands he clasped trembled and shook violently, and he had come to present a truly pitiful appearance in the extreme. Though he begged, though he prayed, though he called, though he shouted, not a single heavenly being entered his eyes, nor could the divine voice be heard; all around was lonely and desolate, and he could no longer remain either standing or sitting. When he looked back at Tatsukō, pitifully, like a frog dashed against the ground, he had stretched out his hands and feet and become almost like a corpse. Yet Harukuniwake followed the deity to the very end, relied upon the deity, believed in the divine character of the deity, and even in such a case held not a particle of complaint or resentment toward the deity. Harukuniwake firmly set the navel of his determination,

Harukuniwake: 'Ah, whatever comes to pass is the divine will, not something we mortals can do anything about. O Deity, please dispose of us according to your divine will. Harukuniwake, who has piled up sins, has come up undeservedly into this pure and precious heaven, and for the sin of lawless rudeness in not knowing his own station, and our sin of throwing the order into confusion, pray, O Kaminahiroki, O Ōnahiroki, look upon us anew and grant us your fitting disposal!'

Even the voice with which he prayed grew faint, and it had come to pass that they were in a desperate extremity. At that moment, suddenly the heavenly door opened, and a dazzlingly brilliant deity clad in golden garments descended from the heavens, attended by two side retainers, riding upon a cloud, and leisurely descended before the two of them. Taking out from his bosom a heavenly medicine called Reitan, he made the two take it in their mouths, and wondrous to say, the two returned to their senses, courage was suddenly added, their emaciated bodies grew plump as of old, their complexions changed to the color of fresh flowers, and they became filled with an indescribable, refreshing feeling. When the two timidly raised their faces, there stood smiling before them a gentle heavenly being whose majestic appearance was stern, yet hard to distinguish whether male or female. Harukuniwake involuntarily clapped his hands,

'Ah, how grateful, how grateful! The great mercy of the Ōkami has so graciously saved us poor sinners. I am truly filled with gratitude.'

Looking closely at the face, it was Konohanahimenomikoto, from whom they had parted previously, accompanied by two jijiyo.

'Yaa, could you, Anata, be Konohahimenomikoto-sama? Truly, truly, I cannot express the depth of my gratitude for your mercy.'

'Kamisama, how graciously you have saved us! Tatsukō was already on the verge of dying a wretched death in Tengoku. A place called Tengoku is truly a painful place, naa.'

'Since the order corresponding to good and truth is firmly established throughout Tengoku, it is naturally agonizingly painful if one opposes this order. If one simply leads a life appropriate to one's mitama, this world is truly a place of peace and comfort. It is much like how fish that live in water will immediately lose their inochi if they go up onto dry land.'

'Indeed, that is most gomotsuto. Without reflecting upon my station of being registered at Yachimata, I indulged in Kamisama's kotoba, gave rise to manshin, and took it into my head to make a jiyunran of Tengoku. This was the crime of our fukaku and fudyōhō, and I earnestly beg to offer my apologies to the Ōkami.'

'Kunikunibakewake-dono, since you came to make a jiyunran of Tengoku through a mediator, no one would ever call it entirely unbefitting your station. However, because you dropped the absolute essence of a sended-shi—the wish-granting jewel—along the way, that is why you suffered so. Since you appeared almost to have stopped breathing, I hurriedly ascended to the palace of the Ōkami of the Moon, received the spiritual elixir, and reappeared here to restore your inochi. It was truly fortunate, naa.'

'Hai, Konohanahime-sama is our parent of inochi, and this great kindness shall never be forgotten.'

'I am not the parent of your inochi. The parent of your inochi is the Ōkami of the Moon. I merely acted as an intermediary. When you thank me in such a manner, it somehow feels as though I were usurping the divine virtue of the Ōkami, and it makes my heart somehow troubled. Everything in the universe is embraced within the divine character of the Ōkami of the Moon. While we can transmit divine virtue, we cannot connect inochi or bestow divine virtue. Henceforth, no matter what happens, even if you perform a small act of goodness or pour forth love, I would be troubled to ever have you express gratitude. Please offer your thanks directly to the Kamisama.'

'Hai, your teaching is as clear as reason itself. Even stubborn Kunikunibake seems to have awakened as if from a daze through your preaching, Anata. Ah, Kunihiritatsu-no-Ōkami, the Ōkami of the Moon, Amaterasu-Ōmikami dwelling in the highest Tengoku, I gratefully and thankfully acknowledge right here that you have bestowed upon us inochi, glory, and joy through the person of Konohanahime-sama.'

'Do you still not recall what you dropped along the way? The wish-granting jewel, that is.'

'Hai, unlike Takahime-sama, I have never once beheld such a thing as the wish-granting jewel, nor have I ever been allowed to touch it, and therefore I have no reason to have dropped it either. Could this not be some sort of riddle? For dull-witted Kunikunibake, this riddle simply cannot be solved.'

'The wish-granting jewel that gave rise to Takahime-sama's possessive attachment is a precious orb possessing the form of the natural world. Since all phenomena and objects in the celestial realm are composed of spiritual entities, it is a wish-granting jewel created from the realm of thought. First, calm your mind and reflect upon it. It is an easy thing for me to tell you, but if you cannot understand even this much, you will ultimately be unable to meet the celestial beings of the middle Tengoku and exchange even a single word. Illuminated by the love of Kami and the faith of Kami, receiving the inward flow of the divine character, and obtaining wisdom and realization—it is nothing at all.'

Harukuniwake answered, 'Hai,' with his hands folded together and eyes closed, remaining lost in thought for a while.

Even Harukuniwake, famed for possessing a keen intellect, found himself almost like a person with dementia upon coming to the Reikai, unable to easily solve this riddle no matter how much he turned over his thoughts.

Tatsukō clapped his hands from the side and let out a cheerful, joyous voice, saying, 'Well now, Sensei, that which is called the Niyoihotsushiyu of the Reikai is the Kototama of Zengenbishi.

On the way up to the Chiukan Tengoku, because we forgot the sōjiyō of the Amatsunorito and Kamigoto, Himenomikoto-sama gave us a helpful warning.'

'Indeed, oh, I had completely forgotten.

Konohanahime-sama, thank you very much.

Ah truly, Tatsukō-san, you are my sensei, ah, truly impressive, impressive.'

'Sensei, if you say such things, it causes me great trouble.

It was certainly not spoken through Tatsukō's chie.

It was made to well up and occur to you through the internal flow of the Shingkaku.'

'In the Genkai, Tatsukō-san is perhaps the disciple of Harukuniwake-san.

However, in this Tengoku, those excelling in chie shōkaku stemming from aizen and shinshin occupy the highest positions.

The deeper one's faith in Kami, the thicker the internal flow of the Shingkaku becomes.'

'Ah, I am truly humbled.

Even upon coming to Tengoku, to have stubbornly maintained the false hierarchies of the Genkai was my repeated mistake.

Ah, Tsukino Ōkami, Hino Ōkami, through the fleshly shrine of Konohanahime-sama, and also through the fleshly shrine of Tatsukō-san, I gratefully thank you for bestowing precious chie upon foolish Harukuniwake.'

'Now everyone, from here on, while chanting the Kototama of the Amatsunorito, please walk around the second Tengoku.

If so, I will take my leave here.'

Both Harukuniwake and Tatsukō bowed their heads, saying, 'Hai, thank you,' and in the very instant they expressed their gratitude, accompanied by resonant music, the divine form of Konohanahime vanished high above the clouds.

Ah, Kannagara Tamashii Sakiwaese.
