Chapter 13: Mountain 口の別 [1182]

山口の別〔1182〕

§ 1

Harukuniwake and his party saw Uzuhiko, parent and child, all four, safely off as far as the ascent of Kajikatauge, and there the party sat upon the boulders by the roadside, and in place of parting greetings, sang.

Harukō's song:

Appearing in the Caucasus mountains, Serving as a subordinate of Ōgetsuhime, Entering the ranks of Yatsukosugami, Even my father, who once flourished gloriously, Retreated from the Caucasus mountains, And the place where he fell away was Armenia; The Ural Hiko and Ural Hime, The Ural Kyō which they opened, Praised the great deity Shihonagahiko, As Bankoshinō, And while they went forth spreading the teachings in all directions, Accompanied by numerous officials, And flourished in triumph, Being utterly destroyed by the supreme leader of Brahmanism, Along with the subordinates of Onikumohiko, The divine officials and the believers alike, Fled and scattered in all directions like clouds and mist. At that time I barely managed, To slip away and escape amid the gloom of night, Wandering about hither and thither, And when there were times I sought the whereabouts of my parents and younger sister, Assisted by the divine official of Ananai-kyō, Kamike Hikosan, Guided to the mansion of the sacred garden, Taught the precious divine teachings, Morning and evening, And becoming their companion, climbing Mount Kajikata, While bathing in the fierce winds, At last crossing over and coming to the shade of the trees in the forest of Yamaguchi, Whether an insect warns me or whatever it may be, Unable to sleep, quite alone, Recalling the living Kototama uttered by my master, the lord, When there were times I thought it through to the end, A faintly visible light of fire, How joyous, how joyous, that the divine power of my Kototama, Appeared, To illuminate this forest enveloped in darkness, Without leaving a single corner, how grateful, Thinking that the divine power of my Kototama Too had now manifested itself, When there were times I entered into a smile of triumph, The light of fire drawing closer by degrees, When I looked closely, what could this be, With an appearance truly dreadful, Making the millet-flesh rise upon my skin, Startled by the figure of an ogress, Words failing me in every way, Trembling and shaking, when there were times, By the gracious instruction of my master, the lord, The mysterious woman was my younger sister, Oh, the joy when I understood this! My spirit grew brave with the excess of joy, Unable to sleep, while pulling the hand of my younger sister, Stadstad down the small path outside the forest, As we advanced about three chō, Hastening our steps and coming at a run, Hanging a lantern bearing the crest of the three-leaf hollyhock, Coming towards this direction, Thinking this a momentous affair, the brother and sister, Drew their bodies close to the shade of a large tree, While they peered into the state of things, The three scout soldiers of Brahmanism, Aku, Taku, and Teku, Enticed by coward-winds, Began to chatter about worthless matters, And at last came to chatter unconsciously Even of the whereabouts of their parents. Before there was even time to be startled and say ah, The mountain palanquin coming from behind, Was truly to be recognized as my parents, And the astonishment at that time, With what could it be compared, there was nothing. By the deep grace of the great deity of Ananai-kyō And the power of the living Kototama of my master, the lord, Parent and child, brother and sister met by chance, Talking together of former times, Sank into tears of joy. Ah, how truly divine, how divine! Not shallow is the grace of the gods. Meeting face to face with a safe countenance My parents whom I had yearned for day by day And my younger sister, Parent and child, brother and sister, spiritedly rising, Made a pilgrimage of gratitude to Mount Ubusuna, Permitted by my master, the lord, Are secluded in the forest of the shrine. Tamakuniwake and Isogohime, Advance before the official. How joyous indeed is my own personal lot! Though the morning sun shines, though it clouds over, Though the moon waxes, though it wanes, Though the stars fall from the sky, Though the mountains split and the seas run dry, Shall I ever forget in a dream or in reality The grace of the imperial great deity? Oh my master, the lord, come now, let us part, Receiving the grace of the gods, Promptly make your way to Castle Haruna, Direct the Kototama towards Daikuronushi, And establish mighty achievements! Await praying before the great deity, Counting on your fingers the auspicious day To make your triumphant return address Before the august presence of the imperial great deity. Ah, how truly divine, how divine! May your august spirit prosper happily.

Sent off by the grace of the deities of Ananai, Easily may we cross Mount Kajikata. Swiftly let us advance to the shrine palace Where dwells the divine official Tamakuniwake. Having finished building the august dwelling of the imperial deities, We shall illuminate the divine majesty in all directions.

Harukuniwake: 'Imitating the gods of old, let parent and child Protect the people of the world for a thousand and eight thousand reigns. However steep the mountain pass of Kajikata may be, By the grace of the gods we shall cross easily. If you meet with the command of Tamakuniwake, Report that Harukuniwake is well. Tell them that Matsuhiko, Mankō, and Isuzukō are likewise without illness, And devote themselves to the Way.'

§ 2

Harukō: 'The words of my revered teacher, folded within my breast, I shall never forget. To save the life of my tarachine parent, and to let me meet my younger sister—how revered is the Kami! From this day forth, parents, son, and daughter, living in harmony together, shall we serve the Great Way of the Kami.'

Uzuhiko: 'The spring of the age of the Kami, of mighty Chihaya, has come round, and parents and children meet in the spring of a thousand ages! Now I know the grace of the Ananahi Kami, through the mouth of Harukuniwake. As I climb and go upon Kajikayama, my very self do protect, O Kami of Ametsuchi! The teacher of the master, who received the grace of Umiyama, morn and eve as a Kami shall I revere.'

Shizuko: 'Even upon my body, which has received a myriad sorrows, a joyful journey do I make today! The Kami and master who saved parent and child— the grace thereof, though I die, I must not forget. Even if it be so, even if without meeting you, my master, my soul shall cleave unto you. When I gaze upon the omokage of my revered teacher, filled with yearning feelings, I sink into the sky of early winter. Even the mountain path where the cold wind blows wildly, when I think upon the Kami, is not painful at all.'

Kaede: 'The joy of meeting again my yearning father and mother, and of meeting also my elder brother the master! May the Sumekami and my revered teacher, unto all eternity, bless us parents and children! Miraculously we met in the dark shade of the trees, and we are immersed in the grace of the Kami. Should Toshihiko my elder brother return with his life, how anxious my revered teacher must be! Nevertheless, my revered teacher is a living Kami, for he is not a child of man defiled by sin. Praying for the forward path of my revered teacher, morn and eve shall I serve the Kami.'

Harukuniwake: 'Kaedehime, set your heart at ease, since I have the protection of the Kami. As a Kami-tsukasa transmitting the way of the Sumekami, how could interfering malevolent deities prevail?'

Mankō: 'Toshihiko, cherish your two parents, and also treat your younger sister with tenderness. To become a parent and to be born as a child is an unbreakable bond of the age of the Kami, so I hear—how felicitous! Though people may have a hundred practices, other than filial piety there is no good way. Morn and evening, reverence the Kami, serve your tarachine parents, and pass through the world. Pity your young younger sister, and rear her in the true way.'

Harukō: 'How grateful is the command of Mankō-sama, folded within my breast, I shall never forget. Now have I realized the deep feeling of a comrade, and the warmth and tenderness of your heart.'

Isakō: 'Harukō, cherish your parents, treat your younger sister with tenderness, and reverence the Kami. I now, following my revered teacher, shall press forward along the Great Way of the Kami. If you have leisure, remember the matter of Isakō, and pray for me before the presence of the Kami. Yet it is not to say that, leaving parents and siblings behind, you must pray for me.'

Harukō: 'Isakō-sama, do remain cheerful and bright, for I will not forget about you. When a sneeze escapes me, I shall think that Isakō is speaking ill of me, and I shall rejoice.'

Isakō: 'To have a single sneeze escape you, think of it as being praised, and do not grow angry. For two sneezes to escape you is to be spoken ill of, yet it is so, and I pay no heed. For three sneezes to escape you is to be laughed at, yet it is so, and I laugh not. For four sneezes to escape you is to catch a cold, thinking upon this, take good care of yourself. You know full well, do you not, that Isakō is not a man to speak behind people's backs?'

Harukō: 'That is truly so; that you, of all people, would speak such words—even more do I not imagine it.'

Tatsukō: 'Through the divine bond of the Kami my heart is set at ease, and yet to part now is so sorrowful! Harukō-sama, cherish your two parents, and on occasion think of me as well. In return, morn and evening shall I pray for the happiness of Harukō's body.'

Harukō: 'Tatsukō-sama, I earnestly beg of you, until the day we meet again.'

Matsuhiko: 'The mountain pass of Kajikayama is steep and the wind fierce, and since monkeys dwell there, go with care. Though it is not the shrine official of the Tamakuniwake Kami, beware lest you fall under the displeasure of those monkey fellows. Go swiftly to the grove of the shrine, and serve within the sacred dwelling. To my comrades Michikō, Itaikō, and Junkō, give my warm regards and convey the message.'

Harukō: 'Even if Matsuhiko were not to say such things, I shall speak appropriately on your behalf. To the divine official of the command of the Harukuniwake Kami, I express my thanks for a grace higher than the mountains. Come now, henceforth we four, parents and children together, shall climb and ascend the steep slope. Harukuniwake and the other three officials, advance in peace under the grace of the Kami.'

Kaede: 'Come now, unto our revered teacher who is as the parent of our life, with lingering regret let us part.'

Harukuniwake: 'Parents and child, three and four, living in harmony together, climb and ascend the Great Way of the Kami.'

Thus mutually exchanging songs of parting farewell, they separated their sleeves to the south and to the north. Ah, May the Divine Spirits of Ametsuchi prosper us!
