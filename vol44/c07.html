Chapter 7: Mountain 口の Forest [1176]

山口の森〔1176〕

§ 1

Isokō walked down the steep slope, and taking advantage of the slightly gentler gradient, he stood at the very front and sang:

In the spirit land of Takaamahara, Lords the God of the Universe who manifests, To purify the spirits of the tamigusa Beneath the heavens, and to let them taitoku The way of aishin, Opening the Ananai-kyō, Illuminating the hearts of hitobito Darkened by evil with the jitsugetsu of shinniyo— How arigata it is! Isokō, of high renown at the mansion of Iso, Was chosen to become the companion of Harukuniwake, Crossing without difficulty the Kajikayama mountain pass Where the retsupū blows fiercely, Scattering the crooked armies, Crossing the exceedingly steep descent yasuyasu, And reaching at last the grove of the shrine Where the divine mei of Tamukuniwake is enshrined, Spending two nights there,

Kotomu and pacifying amata of the people of the Brahman teachings, Setting out once again on his hizakurige, His heart's steed also surging with courage, Mounting his own body to advance further, Ah, kannagara kamigami, To the precious divine blessings, The heavenly calling born as a human being Completely and thoroughly fulfilled to the end, And once he has reported back Before the august presence of Sumeru Ōkami, Through the minori of Takaamahara, Without letting them fall into the yatsuzaka Where spirits stray, or into the land of the roots, Guiding this very body and self Into the land without night of the heavenly kingdom, Protect for a long time this second self of mine, Kannagara, Kami is together with us, Born as the divine children,

The dependability of one's own lot, Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, Whether the moon waxes or wanes, Even if the great earth should sink, Making the kototama protected by the imperial gods of Ananai-kyō The supreme and sole weapon, All of the crooked teachings Where the Yamata no Orochi coils, Kotomu and pacifying them upon the earth, Without opening the paradise of Takaamahara above the ground, How could we leave it be? Though I am a humble person of a body, Illuminated by the light of kami, Having established wondrous merits, Being chosen and appointed as a propaganda envoy, Serving them will not be far off either, No matter how strong the military forces Of General Lunch Katahiko may be, If we strike forth our kototama,

Even a large army like clouds and mist Scatters like tree leaves in the wind, Like a small sparrow meeting a hawk, Trembling, clamoring, and fleeing away, Kotomu and scattering them right before our eyes, Ah, kannagara kamigami, As for the party of Tamukuniwake left behind in the grove of the shrine, Blessed by the grace of kami, Widening the valley and shoveling the earth, Doing all good and exhausting all beauty, With thick main pillars of the great palace, Chigi standing high into Takaamahara, Serving the august dwelling of izu, Open it up as a sacred site for the pacification of the nation, Kannagara. Isokō, with sincere heart before the august presence of kami, prays: Ah, kannagara kamigami, May your august spirits be blessed and flourish,

The renowned Armenia, where the kami of Ural is enshrined, Ever since the clan of Ōkatsuhime Was chased by the divine humans of the Caucasus mountains and fled to Tokoyo, The Brahman teachings took advantage of the void, Led amata soldiers, Set fire around the castle, And in the sadness of burning it all down, Seeking to find his one older brother, Armenia, lost in the darkness, Started out and wandered through the lands in all directions; At that very moment when he was wandering about, Found in an unexpected place by the pursuers of the Brahman teachings, His perilous life saved, Watching for an opening and barely escaping the jaws of death, he ran out, Traveled through the lands of the moon, And searching for the sacred ground of the teachings of Ural,

Wondering if his brother was there, As two or three years passed, He was discovered by Yale and Oos, the divine priests of the Brahman teachings, Promoted, and rose even to the position of secretary to Katahiko, Receiving the mei of Ōkuninushi, And mingling within the army attacking the mansion of Iso, Crossing the Kajikayama mountain pass straddling a steed, and when he looked, He was tormented by the kototama Of Harukuniwake, the divine priest of Ananai-kyō, Meeting with miserable and disastrous experiences, Katahiko and his subordinate soldiers all together Fled and scattered like clouds and mist. We who remained behind, Fearing even the ears of susuki grass, Stepping softly and coming before the front of the mountain kami's shrine,

Who could have ever expected it— His older brother, Kamehiko-sama, Was a propaganda envoy who had attained the wondrous power of kototama, And meeting again with Harukuniwake, Unable to suppress his tears of wild joy, Being treated with hospitality by Todokō-sama, Meeting face-to-face with Harukuniwake, And asking him to reveal his name, you who serve the Brahman teachings, Have no place in my memory as a younger brother, So shaking his head sideways, With greetings devoid of any warmth, The rope of reliance also snapped completely, Clutching at straws and weeping bitterly, Unless I somehow return the captured Itahiko to the august presence of Tamukuniwake, There is no way we can ever declare our brotherhood,

Quickly realizing it in my breast, / Accompanied by Tatsukō-sama, / To the grotto of Kiyoharuyama / Arriving, saving Itakō, / And at last my elder brother's anger / Dissolving—oh, the joy of that time! / Furthermore, and still more joyous, / Coiled in the capital of Haruna, / In the subjugation of Yamataorochi, / You have graciously deigned to let us participate— / The sacred grace of Kami, / Even unto thousands of ages I shall not forget. / Even though Ametsuchi should overturn, / My soul eternally, / Like a boulder, I shall not let be moved. / Surviving in this brief present world, / As much strength as there may be, / Exhausting it completely, in the divine body's / Joy of the land without night, / Oh, the gladness of bathing and serving therein!

The sendenshi of Harukuniwake, / Hear fully and in detail / These words of Matsukō. / Before the august presence of Sumeōkami, / Pray ye, Kamunagara, / Reverently imploring with divine invocation."

Thus chanting as he went was Matsukō, the younger brother of Harukuniwake.

Tatsukō chants again.

"Whether the Asahi shines or clouds o'er, / Whether the moon fills or wanes, / Even though Daichi should sink, / The evil gods of Brahminism / Making submissive and pacified, and the glory / Of Kamikuni making to shine in Sekai, / Living, to become the kami of this world, / And dying, to become a tennin of Takama, / And the spring of the flowers of Tokoshihe / Rejoicing and enjoying as a spirit— / Pray, deign to advance us, Kamunagara. / We are the children of Kami, the palace of Kami; / Shedding this fleshly palace, / When going to the land without night, / My generation's exploits and fame / Kami will deign to approve, / Sending forth innumerable maidens, / While playing dance and music, / Making fragrant scents to waft in the four directions, / Until in the realm of glory and permanence / They graciously welcome and greet us— / With all our hearts, with all our bodies, / Goodness and truth upon the earth / Making to shine across, so that / We may fulfill the august will of the three-five Kami, / Guard us, in the august presence / Of the awe-inspiring Kami of Ametsuchi, / Tatsukō humbly, / Solemnly and simply prays. / The Kami Naobi who created this world, / With a heart spacious as Ōnaobi, / If, however, in the world of men, all things / Are things with many errors, / With a spacious heart rectifying the pronouncement, / And again looking anew and listening anew, / Grant forgiveness, Kamunagara. / We supplicate before the august presence of Kami."

Chanting thus, at last they arrived at Yamaguchi. The mountain stream turned to the left, and even the sound of the water ceased to be heard. Here there was a forest grown with considerably large, ancient trees. This is called the forest of Yamaguchi. The six travelers, seeking to brave the night dew, advanced while feeling their way in the dusk of evening. It seemed to be the site of an old shrine, with only the foundation stones remaining. Here the party spread their straw raincoats and spent the night.

Harukuniwake: 'Coming to the shigure forest of Yamaguchi, / The silence of the darkness where even birds do not sing. / Beyond this point, the warriors of Brahminism / Are lying in wait to strike us down. / However fierce the attack of the fierce fiends may come, / With my kototama I shall blow it away.'

Mankō: 'Under the dark trees where even moonlight is absent, / My master's party passes by at daybreak. / Kajikayama has at last descended here, / Taking a breath in the grace of Kami. / Though the dew of divine grace shines around, / In the moonless midnight there is no way to see. / Soon again illuminating the eastern sky, / The great deity of the moon will surely ascend.'

Harukō: 'Though the starlight shines like fine sand, / It has hidden itself in the leafy thickets. / The achievement of the moon deity who illuminates the world / Is not inferior to the sun-illuminating Kami. / By the grace of the moon and sun deities traversing the heavens, / Men go without saying, and all things flourish.'

Isakō: 'The tranquil presence within the sanctuary of Susanoo— / The auspicious divine grace, now I yearn for it.'

Matsukō: 'Is not the sincere heart spent for the Way and for the world / Like the moon illuminating the dark night? / Illuminating the sky of dawn, crimson / Clouds pushing aside, the sun kami ascends. / In the crescent moon shining in the twilight sky, / I think the rock door of heaven has opened. / Looking at the moon, a hundred sorrows fade away, / As the auspicious spirit washes them clean. / The path that all men should walk is Kamunagara, / For the sake of fulfilling the heart of Kami. / Spending a night in this forest, tomorrow again / We shall seek rain-shelter in the forest of Ukiki.'

Tatsukō: 'Wherever the wicked commanders of Brahmā may be, / Be mindful lest they come to spy. / I hear that the watching kami of Katahiko / Wander about the four sides of Yamaguchi.'

Harukuniwake: 'Entrusting everything to the heart of Kami alone / Must be the great highway that man should walk.'

Hereupon the party spread their straw raincoats, braved the night dew, and peacefully fell asleep.

斯く歌ひ乍ら漸くにして山口についた。谷川は左にそれて水音さへも聞えなくなつて来た。此処には可なり大きな老樹の茂つた森がある。之を山口の森といふ。一行六人は夜露を凌がむと宵暗の中を足さぐりし乍ら進み入る。古い祠の跡と見えて台石ばかりが残つてゐる。此処に一行は蓑をしき一夜を明す事となりける。
