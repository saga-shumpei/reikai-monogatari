§ 4

Aku said in a loud voice on purpose,

"What, do you mean that if we approach closely in the dark, Ananai-kyō might thrust at us with a kuwaiken? It does not matter whatever is thrust; just let them thrust. Since friend and foe alike are told to take down their shiyōheki and associate intimately, being thrust is ketsukō. Being thrust at is also ketsukō. Before long, the kurokumo will be pushed aside and the moon will likely come out, too."

Taku pulled his sleeve and said,

"Hey, Aku, you shouldn't open your mouth so wide. We couldn't bear it if Taku's taku-declaration were laid bare in such an ōgoe, could it?"

"An ōgoe is better. They say an ōgoe does not enter the riji. On the contrary, if you whisper in secret, people hear it."

As they were talking thus, from out of the dark came a colorless, pale-blue ball of fire, dragging a long loincloth, floating along at a place some five or six shiyaku above chijiyō.

Matsuhiko turned toward the ball of fire and gave the gaurei,

"Turn to the right,"

and upon doing so, the ball of fire followed Matsuhiko's kotoba, suddenly turned its head, and wheeled around to the right. Then, stroking around Matsuhiko's forehead with its tail, it passed straight through, groaned with a buzzing sound in the chiuau, and made its tail chiyokuritsu like a towering kuwachiu.

Mankō said in astonishment,

"Matsuhiko-sama, please say 'Turn and return.' Isn't a most disgusting thing coming near?"

"Ahahaha, that is a tanuki. Since the kototama of you, who called it a tanuki from aiazen, has manifested in jitsuchi, if you do not make shiyochi of it, who will? Look, isn't the ball of fire drawing closer to your side?"

"Hey, ball of fire, your honke is not Mankō. It is Aku-sama. Go right along to Aku-sama's side. There is a limit to playing tamado. Hey!"

The ball of fire pressed closer and closer, aiming right at Mankō. Mankō became itsushiyōkenmei, folded both his hands, and assumed a posture of pacifying the spirit with groans. The ball of fire only swelled thicker and longer more and more, and before one's eyes the face of an ogress appeared, with three candles glowing upon her head. On her chest she wore a mirror. She was the exact spitting image of Kazurahime from the night before. Mankō closed his eyes, plugged his ears, and squatted down. The three men, Aku, Taku, and Teku, merely uttered a gasp and stretched out flat upon the earth. With their eyes rolling wide and their mouths agape, they lay there stunned.

The monster stuck out her long tongue licks and licks, and in a disgusting voice said,

"Mankō, Gosankō, Aku, Taku, and Teku—you five heroes and brave warriors, I have come to welcome you from Mount Daiun. Come, follow along behind me. (Loud voice) If you dare to disobey, shall I bite you to death?"

"Y-y-y-y-y-you tanuki monster bastard, wh-wh-wh-what are you spitting out! Wh-wh-who is the fellow who would go as far as Mount Daiun, you st-st-stupid idiot, do you not know the divine power of Gosankō?"

he shouted while breaking out in a cold sweat. The monster's figure vanished with a thud just like an elephant passing gas. The moon, having climbed to the mid-heaven, shone brightly as before. Looking about on all sides, a single white animal was dropping its thick tail and making off clumsily toward the depths of the forest.

"Ahahahahaha, we got had again!"

"Hehehehehe,"

they exchanged a laugh together with faint chuckles from everyone. From this time onward, Matsuhiko led the five—Gosankō, Mankō, Aku, Taku, and Teku—and waiting for daybreak, set forth heading toward the forest of Ukiki.
