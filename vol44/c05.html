Chapter 5: 不眠症 [1174]

不眠症〔1174〕

§ 1

Harukuniwake and Tamakuniwake's party stepped out from in front of the shrine, and spread their straw rain capes to spend the night in the shade of the former forest in Jiyōhau.

Isokō, who had been stationed as a watchman before the shrine, came running up cheerfully,

Harukuniwake: 'If I may say so, Harukuniwake-sensei, some guests have suddenly arrived. What shall we do about this?'

At this voice Harukuniwake suddenly opened his eyes,

Harukuniwake: 'It is no use welcoming guests in the middle of this mountain. However, what kind of people are these guests? They are probably outcasts of the Brahman religion, I suppose.'

Isokō: 'Yes, just as you suspected, three Brahman sensei have come along. Moreover, being clever fellows, they have brought along a mountain basket packed full of plenty of wine. Thinking perhaps some poison might be put inside, I took off the stopper and made the man who brought it taste it, but it is all right. Anyway, one is a gleaming-headed, elderly-councillor-like fellow named Teru, one is a fellow with his fan spread out wide like an upper-class head named Haru, and another is a wobbling-legged fellow called-sama like Yoro or Yoru. They certainly say quirky things. Please meet with them, I pray you.'

Harukuniwake: 'Won't you keep your voice down and speak quietly? Won't it disturb everyone's sleep? And have you asked what kind of business they have?'

Isokō: 'I have not asked yet, but the details should be known by the three of Iru, Iku, and Sāru. Since they are the ones who dragged them along. There is hardly any fear of it turning upside down, so please trust the three of Iru, Iku, and Sāru first of all.'

Harukuniwake: 'At any rate, meeting here would interfere with everyone's peaceful sleep, so I suppose I shall step out as far as the front of the shrine.'

Isokō: 'Yes, thank you for your trouble and I kindly ask it.'

Before he had even finished speaking, Isokō bounded down the mountain path of the night like a flying bird, turned toward the six people waiting in front of the shrine, gasping and panting for breath,

Isokō: 'Hey, Iru, Iku, Sāru, Teru, Haru, Yoru, you half-dozen human beings-sama, Isokō-sama's negotiation committee is a great success. Harukuniwake-sama says he will grant you the exceptional honor of dangling before your eyes. Come, since he is stepping out right here presently, straighten your collars, fix your postures, and welcome them with reverence, you'd better do so.'

Teru and the others: 'That is indeed prompt news, how grateful we are! Will he even meet with such a one as Yoru? This is entirely thanks to the gods.'

Isokō: 'Even if you say this is partly thanks to Isokō-sama, it's not a tale that cuts very deep though, ahahahaha.'

While he was laughing thus, the one who appeared before the seven people, carefully watching his footing quietly, was Harukuniwake.

Harukuniwake: 'Are you gentlemen the guests who came out from Brahman?'

Yoru stepped forward timidly, stroking his head two or three times,

Yoru: 'Yes, I am called Yoru, enjoying the favor of General Ranchi, but the truth of the matter is that I was greatly moved, and having a matter of petition for you-sama of Isanago, I have come all this way.'

Harukuniwake: 'What matter of petition might that be?'

Yoru: 'In truth, hearing that at Tamayama Pass I was awed by the Kototama of Isanago, and again that even in the Forest of the Cross we fled before the power of the Kototama, and that even the vanguard force of General Katahiko was smashed quite easily, the foundation of my faith began to wobble, and this means that surely the god we believe in is not the god of the universe's origin. Since the war we fight for the god continues to lose to this extent, there must be some cause. Pondering deeply that this was a time to think things over greatly, I resolved to surrender to Isanago. Regarding that, I have made Teru and Haru—who are the most zealous toward Brahman and whose divine spirits are known to be stubborn—surrender among the subordinates of General Ranchi of the Brahman religion, and have dragged them along as a souvenir for you. I earnestly beg that you will pardon the crime of having resisted Isanago until now, in exchange for this merit, and add me to your disciples.'

§ 2

Ananai-kyō's losing or winning, and Brahmanism's losing or winning, are all the divine providence of Kamisama. To test the power of Kami through one or two military contests is an act of sheer presumption. If people enter the faith under such a weak foundation, then if Ananai-kyō should unfortunately lose from here on, they would surely say that the god of Brahmanism is greater still, turn on their heels, and reverse their course. Since persons whose minds change so easily do not exist in Ananai-kyō, you see.

My, what a difficult Kyō this is. We are certainly not such fickle and frivolous people. There are various motives for this. Merely talking about the war was done for reference, and I have only stated a part of the reasons. First of all, taking the conversion of an obstinate fellow like Teru or Haru as proof, we earnestly ask for your permission to enter the faith.

Ah......

I say, Sendenshi-san, what this Yoru says cannot be relied upon at all. The truth of the matter is that while this Haru and I were on guard duty, we found it so cold and tedious that, although we thought it a breach of duty, we were having a cup of drink when this Yoru-sama came patrolling with a terrifying face and said, 'Now then, you two fellows have been speaking ill of the Brahman god and Daikokunushi-sama's affairs, and praising Ananai-kyō, haven't you? Since you are outrageous scoundrels, I will bind you both and drag you before the presence of General Katahiko,' becoming fierce and imposing, and glaring at us in such a terrible, terrible way. When we two successfully lured him with sake and made him spill the truth, it appeared that this fellow also had the paradise of Ananai-kyō open at the bottom of his heart, and as the liquor went to his head, he disparaged Brahmanism. Thinking this fellow was harmless, we loaded the completely drunken Zubaroku-sama into a palanquin and came up to this place. This Teru here is by no means a bad person such as Yoru describes. Moreover, since he is not all that enthusiastic about Brahmanism, please be entirely at ease. Right, Haru, there is no mistake about that, is it?

Haru took up the thread, saying:

There is not even a fraction of a hair's difference from what Teru-kou said. Please think that you have gained more mischievous ujiko, overlook this with a generous eye, and pick us up, if you please.

Harukuni-wake: 'To build fences between enemies and allies and fight / Must be the work of birds and beasts. / The imperial deities who created heaven and earth / Surely rectify such ugly falsehoods. / Since there is no way we can coldly turn away / Those who seek and come to the path of Ananai. / To the three pillars of Teru, Haru, and Yoru who come invited, / I shall transmit the pronouncements of the living Kototama. / From now on, become children of the true Kami's clan / And devote yourselves for the sake of the people of the world.'

Yoru: 'How grateful, the flowers of the heart now bloom / Under the rigorous Kototama of Harukuni-wake. / From today, wiping away the dirt of the heart, / Peacefully and joyfully I shall serve the Path.'

Haru: 'Haru has passed, summer departed, autumn too has gone by, / And I have received the winter of the mitama. / Hoping to receive the divine grace of the imperial Kami, / I have managed to preserve this dewy life.'

Iru: 'Advancing into the path of salvation of the great Kami, / I have become a mitama full of joy.'

Sāru: 'Tightening my bellyband firmly now once more, / I will devote my sincerity for the sake of the people of the world. / The falsehoods that disturb the world are turned and pacified by the divine words of Sāru; / How noble is the lord who does so. / Shut up within the stronghold of Mount Kiyoharu, / The path of Ananai that I was able to comprehend. / Under the divine teachings of Matsu-kou and Ryū-kou-sama, / The Brahman clouds have completely cleared away.'

Harukuni-wake: 'I shall now go to Haruna / Where the eight-forked serpent dwells, upon the very path. / Yet, the land of Haruna is extremely far away, / Since a hundred mountains and rivers lie across it.'

Yoru: 'Day and night, to the limit of my heart and the limit of my body, / Without knowing it was error, I have devoted myself. / From today, straightening up the steed of my heart, / I enter the right path of the great imperial Kami.'

§ 3

The scene shifted as Michikō and Itakō, the two who had been sleeping in the shade of the forest, woke up and rose.

Michikō-san: 'Hey, Michikō-sama, isn't a lively action play starting leisurely in front of the shrine once again? How about going over secretly to take a look?'

Itakō: 'That's true, Itakō. It seems somehow noisy. Still, if there is any urgent business for us, Sensei will surely call us down. Well, it would be best to stay put for now.'

Itakō: 'First, I will go on a reconnaissance, so won't you wait here, Michikō?'

Michikō: 'I beg to be excused from that. If I were taken off to Kiyoharuyama again like last time, it would be a great nuisance for us... If this Michikō finds anything suspicious, I will pull out my unique laughter-maker and laugh them to scorn just as before. Now wait. I will go.'

Itakō: 'When you thought you laughed them to scorn, wasn't he the younger brother of Sendenshi-sama? Since Itakō has already suffered a painful blow once, he is paying full attention, so it is quite all right. Even I can manage a bit of laughing them to scorn.'

Michikō: 'If so, Michikō will act as your guide. You are hopeless because the mitama of a captive is still possessing you. Follow behind me at a distance of about threepaces. If there is anything suspicious, run back with all your might.'

Itakō: 'You have certainly lost a great deal of trust. However, since I have the trust of the Gods, I am at ease. Since this must undoubtedly be none other than Chikokuwake-sama starting right here, how about shouting out to see?'

Michikō: 'Don't talk nonsense. If you raise a loud voice, everyone will wake up. Chikokuwake-sama... when Michikō woke up and listened, I thought I was sleeping, and he was whispering together with Gosankō; one never knows what might have come to what.'

Itakō: 'Does that mean Michikō was awake while pretending to be asleep?'

Michikō: 'I am suffering from insomnia or something that is fashionable these days; at night my eyes become sharp and my nerves are excited, so I cannot sleep for even a little while or quietly. Michikō is truly a pitiable creature. Aha-ha-ha-ha!'

Michikō: 'Hey you two, you must go quietly. It would be a pity if Junkō-sama were to wake up.'

Itakō: 'Aha-ha-ha-ha! What are you talking about? Aren't we talking precisely because we are awake?'

Itakō: 'Junkō's physical body is asleep, but I am just having a dream.'

Michikō: 'Whether it is a dream or reality, whether it is foolish or clever, it is a thing whose measure cannot be taken by even a hair's breadth.'

Michikō: 'Though it is not quite like Kunikachitachi-no-Mikoto-sama, since it is a magnificent mitama that becomes a void and completely clears up, how could the wisdom of Junkō-sama ever be measured by human wisdom alone? Things that can be measured by humans are no longer gods or anything else at all. There is a fixed, settled market price for them. In a place where one cannot tell whether it is a fool or a dunce, the divine character of Junkō-sama is active freely in all directions. That is why I remember Ikozohime-sama praising this Junkō-sama once upon a time, wondering where she could possibly put him out of mind.'

Itakō: 'That must have been mostly a dream. Hey Michikō, isn't Ikozohime-sama also a bit questionable for praising such a man? If not, Junkō-sama himself might have been dreaming.'

Michikō: 'Human beings are dreaming dreams within a dream. To generally think that one is truly seeing such a thing is foolish. How could one ever discern the right and wrong, wisdom and foolishness of people? Much less with these physical eyes, which are like fallen pearls covered with thin ice, is it impossible to observe even the exterior. How exceedingly so regarding the pure, undefiled, and active spirit within the body!'

Mankō: 'Hey, you bunch of junk, what are you making such a noisy fuss about? Why don't you go to sleep properly? As for me, Mankō—not to mention Harukō-sama—I am a source of great trouble to Ikozohime-sama, Imahikohime-sama, and Tamakokuwake-sama. Come, come, sleep and sleep. Since Chikokuwake-sama has honored us with his presence, everything is well. What good would it do for paltry child-warriors like us to remain awake? Behave yourselves and go to sleep until the bugle blows for rising.'

§ 4

'Well, it cannot be helped. That is indeed so; Michikō, Sumikō, Mankō, shall we sleep? There can hardly be much time left until dawn, and since the present moment is something that can never be recovered for all eternity, let us make up our minds and go to sleep; for Itakō is also sleepy, you see.'

Isokohime: 'The torment by the command of Tamakuniwake Kami Has already calmed, and the moon shines brightly. Bathing in the dew of the grace of Sumekami, How joyful it is to sleep, blown by the wind.'

Tamakuniwake opened his eyes,

'How noble to advance, dividing the lands While the jewel of the moon shines across the vast sky. Is the command of Harukuniwake Mikoto valiantly Instructing the commander of the ugly fiends? Since I too have been chosen as an official of Kami And have come here, how could I leave them unsaved? The torment in my right eye has also ceased, Ever since I bathed in the light of the moon Mikami.'

As he thus chanted, the seventeen-day moon faded in the western sky, the voices of the magpies echoed clearly with caws, and the myriad birds began to chirp at the top of their voices.
