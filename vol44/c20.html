§ 1

Matsuhiko watched the backs of Otora and Okiku receding,

Matsuhiko: 'Mankō has run into grandmother Otora, / And countless bitter grudges, oh Princess Okiku! / Supposing it was not a thing to simply cast aside, / Isokō's mouth calculated thus. / By the river's edge, he washed away the old stains, / Sins and faults that Mankō will never shake off to the end of his days. / The raging torrent of the fierce Kajikagaha, / The peril of crossing the log bridge. / Setting forth from the wooded grove where monkeys cry, / It is Mankō's wretched fix with the old hag.'

Mankō: 'Grandmother Otora, bringing Okiku to the river's edge, / Mankō comes, and here is Matsuhiko. / Pouring forth once more the village scandals for eternity, / Flowing down to the gravel beds of Kajikagaha.'

Iso: 'With the oil of my body taken, on top of which comes the scolding, / Oh, the pain in the ears of Princess Kikuko. / In such a grand fashion this destination cannot be spoken of, / For the body of Mankō who knows the village inside out.'

Mankō: 'Hey, Isokō, it is not just me, after all, / You are also a fellow whose rear end smells. / A mountain monkey who knows not that his own backside is red, / Is meddling in the affairs of others.'

Iso: 'Scratching his shame, scratching his head, sniveling and weeping, / Tossed about like a temari ball by grandmother Otora. / Ha, ha, ha, even the open mouth refuses to close, / If the ro of romance is said to be ro [the mouth]. / Field mice and moles ravage the daikon patch, / Are you aiming even at the holes of the village yams?'

Mankō: 'Behold the honorable holes, the holes of the teaching that says there are no holes, / Even the propaganda emissaries have younger sisters, you know.'

Mankō: 'Fellow who digs yams and plucks turnips to eat, / You are a makeshift actor of the Izanami teaching.'

Mankō: 'Talk no nonsense, right in the footprint where the turnip was plucked, / There is a hole like a temari ball. / Who said this was the teaching of the God of Thirty-Five? / There is an escape hatch upon your face as well. / While leaving your escaped mug gaping wide open, / How smoothly you blurt out about the Izanami teaching. / If one searches out Isokō's romance, / It is perhaps the unrequited love of an abalone on the shore. / No matter what anyone says, Mankō is a dashing man, / He was thought well of by the village maiden. / A man of valor who, being thought of, returns that thought, / There are also fools who know not that this is authority.'

Aku: 'Spoken to with wicked tongue by the foul-mouthed hag, / Mankō's face has shriveled up in dismay.'

Mankō: 'Hey, you wicked wretch, noisy without knowing a thing, / This is no place for your foul mouth to spout off. / For one with a face like a mountain monkey such as you, / Could romance ever be understood by any chance?'

Aku: 'Remembering the affair of the village maiden he put to death, / He smiles with a ho, ho, ho. / Again and again, ho, ho, ho, within the woods, / Lost in the gloom, his dead lover yearns for him. / What a funny fellow, no matter how much she pines and yearns, / You surely cannot sleep holding a ghost.'

Mankō: 'Hey, Aku, what on earth are you calling a ghost, / There is a limit to speaking so rudely.'

Taku: 'This is becoming interesting once again, / The village maiden comes stepping forth right out of her grave.'

Mankō: 'Taku, you rascal, making a great fuss without knowing anything, / How could a child understand romance?'

Taku: 'Lord Taku has taken unto himself princesses on Mount Taku, / Only peerless, nice maidens.'

Mankō: 'What are you blathering with a face like a lizard, / There was never any such thing as nice or crap.'

Teku: 'Hey, Taku, seize that arrogant fellow, / Make him not your match, people will laugh at you.'

Taku: 'Even if they laugh, what do I care about being laughed at, / When he is Mankō, whose oil has been taken.'

Teku: 'Mankō of the way of the Izanami teaching, / Has been devoured by the hag and the maiden, hasn't he?'

Mankō: 'Even you, Teku, what are you chattering on and on about, / Do you know what is in my heart? / Mankō may be losing right now, / But just wait and see when Okiku grows up.'

Iso: 'When Princess Okiku grows up, you will try again, / Are you banking on that one-in-a-million chance?'

Mankō: 'Hey, Isokō, are things ever accomplished by hurrying? / Just wait and see the spring three years hence.'

Iso: 'Do not harbor any such wild ambitions once again, / Next time your head and torso will part ways.'

Mankō: 'By the time three years have passed, grandmother Otora / Will have already gone on her journey to the Netherworld. / Since everything will be up to Mankō, it's Okiku he'll choose, / Or so he seems to be casting amorous glances even now.'

Matsuhiko: 'Chanting nothing but broken-backed songs, / They exhausted every imaginable bizarre deed. / Come now, Mankō, Isokō, Aku, Taku, and Teku—the five of you, / Shall we leisurely climb up the mountain now?'

Mankō: 'That's fine, let's leave old Granny Tora be, since that wench Okiku is waiting for me.'

Iso: 'I thought she was a wench with deep attachment, but I never imagined it was to this extent.'

Mankō: 'Are you astonished or dumbfounded, Isokō? People are not always what they seem. Nevertheless, I walk the path of sincerity, and since I am Mankō, I have cast aside romance. Please do not worry yourselves about it, for I hardly think of Okiku at all.'

Iso: 'Is that so? No matter how much she might think of you, if the other party is unwilling, it cannot be helped.'

Aku: 'This has gotten interesting again! There is no greater solace on a journey.'

Taku: 'Treading along and coming to the riverbank, chased by the old hag, Plop! into the water they fall. How fortunate that our strong-fisted fellow was saved, hearing a tale so rare in this world.'

Mankō: 'If Master Man had been there, you fellows too would have drifted into a smile of joy. Isn't spiritual nourishment called joy? Revere me as your parent of life.'
