Chapter 18: 一本 Bridge [1187]

一本橋〔1187〕

§ 1

Matsuhiko and his party leave the Nonaka forest behind and proceed toward Ukikegahara, singing the Sendenka.

Here lies the lower stream of the Kajikagaha.

This river is said to flow into the Lion River.

Across a fairly wide river, a single-plank bridge is built, anchored on natural rocks in the middle of the river.

Two women were returning, having crossed the bridge.

One is a middle-aged woman, and the other is a young girl of fifteen or sixteen.

The party of six stood at the bridge approach, looking at the clear, rushing current and catching their breath.

Mankō turned to the two women and said,

Mankō: 'It is quite a violent current, yet as women you managed to cross such a single-plank bridge so well. Tell me, where on earth have you come from?'

Woman: 'Yes, we are from the village of Ukiki, but recently a large number of soldiers occupied our village, and they drafted every woman house by house to do cooking and subjected us to all sorts of insults, so everyone fled. As for me, being an old woman, they would not pay attention to me, but as the women gradually dwindled down, they said they did not care if it was an old woman or a girl, they would drag away anyone who was female, so I fled my village, crossed this bridge, and went into hiding in the sanctuary of the deity of Mount Kobakiyama; but there were far too many women and no place to sleep, and we were turned away, so the two of us, mother and child, came back this far.'

Aku spoke hurriedly,

Aku: 'Hmm, about how many tens of women in all are hiding in Kobakiyama?'

Woman: 'Yes, about a hundred have gathered, but since I went later, every single room was packed like vinegar barrels and there was no place to sleep even under the eaves. That is why we came back. I am at my wits' end wondering what to do from now on. The crest on your straw hat is the Jūyō crest, but strangely enough, the deity of Mount Kobakiyama also had the Jūyō crest on it.'

Aku: 'And what kind of deity is enshrined there?'

Woman: 'Yes, I heard it is Kuninotachiritsu no Mikoto-sama.'

Aku: 'Well now, it is hard to believe that Kuninotachiritsu no Mikoto-sama is enshrined there. Could it not be a faction of the Izunome-kyō?'

Woman: 'I do not know what it is, but we came here calling it the deity of Mount Kobakiyama. You cannot quite tell from the outside, but look over there, a thin smoke is rising, is it not? That is where the deity is enshrined. And there is a gate, and many deities are enshrined as well; I do not remember all their names, but they are all deities with complicated names.'

Itsukau: 'Matsuhiko-sama, isn't this old woman's story rather encouraging? She said the deity Kuninotachiritsu no Kami is enshrined there and that the Jūyō crest is attached to it, didn't she? By any chance, could the teacher Chikunikubetsu have gone there?'

Matsuhiko: 'It may not be so, but I too would like to drop by that place called Mount Kobakiyama for a moment and see how things are.'

Itsukau: 'If so, shall I accompany you? Hey, Gosan-sama, Mankō-sama, Taku, Teku, you guys agree too, don't you?'

The four all shouted in unison, 'Agreed, agreed.'

Aku: 'Ah, thank you very much on Aku's part for approving my proposal unanimously.'

Itsukau: 'Ha-ha-ha-ha-ha, Aku-sama, do you intend to leave these two women to die? Isn't it pitiful not to take them along somehow? Surely there should be room for two among a place with a hundred people. Didn't this old woman commit some kind of petty theft or something?'

Aku: 'That is right, she must have done it. After all, there are some people with very sticky fingers even among women.'

Old Woman: 'Now, now, you people, you called me a person with sticky fingers, but since you say it so boldly, do you have some kind of proof? Come on, let me hear it. Hearing a thing like this, no matter how much one says I am a woman, I cannot let it pass. Being branded with the name of a thief, what excuse do I have to my ancestors? I cannot even show my face to my daughter. On what grounds do you say such a thing?'

Frowning fiercely, he glares at him.

Aku: 'Ah, this is a blunder! I am truly and excessively sorry for my slip of the tongue. My mouth just happened to slip, you see.'

Yoshikohime: 'Can an excuse be made with such an excuse as a slipped mouth or slipped feet? Come now, how are you going to dry the false accusation you pinned upon me? You seem to be a person who goes about guiding the people of the world, but how can you perform the sacred service of Kamisama with such ways?'

Aku: 'Ah, I am truly struck dumb with shame, my mitama has been completely beaten!'

Yotahiko: 'Hey, Aku-sama, look at your posture! If you use the kototama too much, things happen like a man over seven shiyaku tall being made a fool of by a woman. Ha-ha-ha-ha-ha, what a worthless thing you are!'

Kamehiko: 'So you say your name is Aku? No wonder you have the face of a man who commits manbiki. Oh, how terrifying, how terrifying! It would be a taihen if we were to be robbed by ohihagi in a place like this. Come, Kiku, a nagai stay is to be feared, let us hurry back.'

Okiku: 'Mother, if we return to the village of Ukiki, we will be robbed by Brahmin gunjin or subjected to a nenbutsukau, so we cannot endure it. Shall we instead throw ourselves into this place and die? Even if we go to Kogitayama, we are cast out; if we come here, we are made victims of ohihagi. If we return home, we are scolded by gunjin. Is there anything at all that we can do?'

Yajihiko: 'Now, now, Mother and daughter goriyōnin, I am one called Isokō, and I am certainly no dorobō. I am a companion of the missionaries of Hirokai. Even if you asked me to do so, I would never do such things as tormenting people or acting as ohihagi, so please be at ease. To end a precious life in a place like this is a bad intention. There is a limit to being short-tempered. Now, Okiku-san, this uncle is not such a scary person, so please set your mind at ease.'

Okiku: 'No, no, you are a dorobō! The three persons sitting right over there are the very ones who came out to my village the other day and came to snatch people away, saying, "Female conscription!" I remember their faces. Even if you say such things, I will not accept it. Right, Mother, is that not so?'

Otora: 'Indeed, those three men over there are the very men who even came to my house. The ones who tried to snatch away neighbor Okame are those three. They acted all proud, saying they were inspectors of the Brahman religion. Listen here, you three wretches! Though this old woman may look like this, she is Otorabō of Ukikigahara, a female robber who in her youth opened gambling dens. Now that my daughter has resolved to throw away her life, this Otora has no encumbrances and can act with all her might. Come now, you brats, this Otora will throw you into the river and avenge the villagers. Now, what do you say about that?'

Lifting her eyes and glaring with a fierce expression, she stared them down. The three men, Aku, Taku, and Teku, were daunted by Otorabō's momentum, stepped back, and scratched their heads.

Yotahiko: 'Ha-ha-ha-ha-ha! Hey, Aku, you three have been talking big, but you have done quite a few bad things, haven't you? It is time to pay your yearly tax. Why not try being thrown into this torrent together with the old granny? If you like, I wouldn't mind lending granny a hand either, since Mankō is the mirror of good for all eternity.'

Aku: 'Now, now, Grandmother, you will make things difficult for us if you get that angry. I, Aku, went out on female conscription because it was my official duty. Just think of it as my duty and reconsider, please.'

Otora: 'No matter what you say, Otorabō is desperate—do you think I will forgive you? Hey, you called Mankō, you must be of the same kind too. Now, Okiku, since you have made up your mind to die, it is a waste to die all by yourself. Why don't you throw all six of these people into the river without exception, make a great show of activity, become a splendid warrior, and boast of your heroic name when you go to the underworld?'

Okiku: 'Mother, if that is so, I too shall make a desperate effort. Unless I take even one person along with me as a companion, my anger will not be appeased, you see.'

Matsuhiko opened his mouth for the first time,

Matsuhiko: 'Now then, Otora-san, Okiku-san, first of all calm down; truly we are not bad men at all. Even within Brahmanism, good people are mixed in occasionally, you see. It is true that these three went for the women's levy, that much is a fact. But today they have already repented and are walking along accompanying the missionaries of Ananai-kyō, so please forgive them.'

Otora-san: 'Just because you have a somewhat clever-looking face, you speak of somewhat sensible things. Though an unforgivable imp of a fiend, I will let you off with a blind eye today. In return, unless I make the three imps become dog-crouching and offer apologies saying "We were truly bad," I will not consent, you know. I will spare only your lives.'

Matsuhiko: 'Hey, Aku, Teku, Taku, all three of you are completely pale in the complexion, aren't you? With such a state trembling because of being overwhelmed by one or two women, how can a man's face stand? Considering this, bad things must not be done, must they. It is a disgrace for ten thousand generations to the end of time. Ha ha ha ha!'

Aku: 'Not at all, I have no reason to apologize to this old woman. I haven't even touched a single hair of the old woman or her daughter's body; I merely went as far as the neighboring house. Hey old woman, isn't it old-fashioned to call it a vendetta for the neighboring house? You too are quite old-fashioned in your head, aren't you.'

Okiku: 'Heh! Since the fellow just now snaps and chews up blue covers and crab letters, he spits out such petty logic. If you are bold and will not apologize, it is fine even if you do not apologize. Since we too have our resolve.'

Aku: 'Ha ha ha ha, what an obstinate old woman. You are trying to strike an Edo enemy in Nagasaki. Hey, let us three cross this single-plank bridge to the other side, and so that the old woman cannot come, let us drop this bridge, shall we? Taku, Teku, come now!'

And rolling up his skirts, trying to cross the single-plank bridge recklessly, Aku in his panic plunged plump into the swirling raging torrent. Both Teku and Taku managed to cross to the other side. Otora and Okiku raised both hands, bustling about with cries of "Uwai, uwai."

Matsuhiko, astonished,

Matsuhiko: 'Hey, Mankō, Gosankō, we cannot just stay like this. The old woman is an old woman, but we must help Aku; come, let's cross.'

So saying, Matsuhiko took the lead and began to cross the single-plank bridge. Following him, Gosankō also started across.

Mankō: 'Helping Aku is strange, isn't it. If it were me, I'd help the good, you know.'

He was spouting off. From behind, Otora grabbed Mankō firmly by the nape of the neck, while Okiku swept out her legs, knocking him down with a thud onto the riverbank.

Mankō: 'Ba-ha-ha-ha, old woman, what-what are you doing? Don't-don't I know nothing in the slightest?'

Otora: 'Whether you know it or not is fine. Since you are a remnant of the enemy, mother and child gather together and we will take your life.'

Mankō, startled,

Mankō: 'Hey, Matsuhikosama, Gosankokosama, it's murder, save me!'

He cried out at the top of his lungs. Drowned out by the sound of the raging torrent, it could not be heard on the opposite bank. The four were running about in confusion, hither and thither, trying to rescue Aku. As for Aku, somehow swimming and reaching the bank two or three chō downstream, he became completely naked and began to squeeze his wet clothes.

Matsuhiko: 'Ah, it's safe now; as expected, Aku is a great fellow. Matsuhiko is impressed. Having strong bad luck must be this very thing, ha ha ha ha.'

Okiku: 'Now, Matsuhikosama, isn't Mankō missing?'

Matsuhiko: 'What, Gosankō, is Mankō not there?'

So saying, when he looked at the opposite bank, he was being pinned down by two women and struggling.

Matsuhiko, hurriedly,

Matsuhiko: 'Hey, both Teku and Taku, go toward Aku's side and take care of him. Gosankō, I am sorry to trouble you, but cross the single-plank bridge and go save Mankō.'

Gosankō: 'Hey, I understand. But what are you intending to do?'

と云ひながら向ふの岸を見ると、二人の女に押へられ藻掻いて居る。

松彦は言せはしく、

『オイ、タク、テクの両人はアクの方へ往つて世話をしてやつて呉れ、五三公は御苦労ぢやが一本橋を渡つて万公を助けて来い』

『ヘイ承知致しました、併し貴方はどうなさるお積りです』

§ 4

I am the deputy of the Sendenshi, so I intend to take a seat first in the center and critique the combat styles of both armies. Come on, won't you hurry and go?

Ah, it can't be helped.

Isokō crossed the Itsuponbashi bridge once again,

Hey there!

As he shouted, Otora and Okiku remained completely unconcerned,

Hey you, what are you interfering for? Isn't the Sensei waiting over yonder? Hurry on over there right now. This fellow is called Mankō, the rogue who deceived my daughter. He's the scoundrel who lay in wait where Okiku's older sister Osato went out to the fields, ambushed her, and finally lived together with her in my house for about a year as if they were a married couple. Osato's belly swelled from an evil bond, and as a result of a difficult birth, she ended up dying. When that happened, this heartless man ran away kicking sand back behind him. I had been searching everywhere to find where he went, but fate cannot be evaded, and we happened to meet right here. He is my daughter's enemy, and no matter what, I won't be satisfied unless I kill him. Mankō thinks I have bad eyes and puts on an innocent face, but I'm not an old woman who doesn't understand such things. Taste this iron fist, you enemy of my daughter!

So saying, she raised her clenched fist and struck him repeatedly, tap, tap, tap.

Ouch, ouch, ouch, ouch! Mankō-Mankō-Mankō, please pardon me!

Enemy of my sister, I won't let you off!

So saying, she clenched her fist again and struck him, tap, tap, tap.

Hey, Isokō, won't you help me? I'm not a man who gets weak over three or four women, but Mother Otora is a hundred-dan master of judo, so once you're grabbed tightly, there's nothing at all you can do.

Ho, ho, ho, ho! Now, you called Isokō, if you lay even a single finger of yours on this old woman's body, I won't let you off.

Well now, even Isokō has no way to intervene. There probably won't be any chance of taking your life, so just endure the beatings with all your might. Now, Grandma, please hit him firmly yet softly, I beg you.

Mother, it's no use beating such a spineless man. Shall we forgive him already? Rather than that, it might be more worthwhile to return to Ukigahara, plunge into the camp of General Lunch, and die a glorious death by fighting and being cut down.

That's right, it's no use taking two or three such blowflies as opponents; let's forgive him. What a lucky fellow who has been spared his life. From now on, you must surely reform yourself, you scoundrel Mankō.

Yes, I shall be careful.

Always remember what I say, listen well to what that Sensei says, and return to a good heart. Now, you are a creature set free in the three thousand worlds, go wherever you please, Mankō.

With a sudden release, she let go of the hand she had been grasping. Mankō stood up with a sluggish heave,

Grandma, thank you very much for your trouble. Thanks to you, my stiff shoulders have been cured.

Leaving behind those parting words, he ran away.

What a hopeless man. That fellow still hasn't straightened his loach bones, it seems. I'll chase after him from behind and administer one more reprimand, come on, Okiku.

Trying to cross the Itsuponbashi bridge, Isokō spread both his arms and said,

Grandma, please, please wait. Since I will reason with him thoroughly, please forgive him this once and for all. Since you yourself said you forgave him, please let it go just this once. Isn't it fine if you don't chase after him with such deep grudge?

Even though he is a hateful fellow, he is a man whom my dear daughter cherished even if only for a year, so just to lecture him thoroughly, punish him, and turn him into a proper man, we mother and child acted so roughly. The reason we struck Mankō was still because we are fond of him. Why would we hate him and strike his head if we didn't care?

So saying, she wiped away her tears with her sleeve. Okiku also hid her face and softly wiped her tears.

Ah, how precious a parent's grace is! Farewell, Grandma.

With that parting remark, Isokō hurriedly crossed the Itsuponbashi bridge once again and hastened toward the sacred ground of Kitagata.
