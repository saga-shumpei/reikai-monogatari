§ 1

Matsuhiko gathered his five attendants near a huge rock standing abruptly by the mountain path, resting their breath and engrossed in conversation.

Aku-sama: 'It was quite a dangerous affair, wasn't it? Well, it turned out all right.'

Run by the old woman, I missed my footing the moment I started running and met with a rather chilly experience. However, since I am good at swimming, I was saved. If it were the two of you, Taku and Teku, you would be utterly hopeless.'

That's right. Well, it was good. Mankō-san, you were thoroughly beaten by the mother-and-daughter pair, weren't you? What a remarkably weak man you are.'

Heh-heh, I am Mankō, weak to evil and strong to good; if it weren't for the non-resistance principle of Ananai-kyō, I was about to throw the old woman into the river, but I kept the non-resistance principle firmly by correcting my view and listening anew as much as possible to the nahohi. When I did so, the old woman and her daughter gave me a massage. Kneading my shoulders, massaging my waist, pulling my legs—thanks to that, my body became comfortable.'

Isokō burst out laughing,

Ahaha! You are a man who hates to lose. Weren't you screaming and crying?'

Nah, that was because they were massaging a ticklish spot, so I was laughing. Does it sound to you like I was crying?'

Still, didn't you say, 'Murderer, help me'?'

Well, I just said it as a bit of a joke. As proof of that, didn't the old woman and the daughter cry? I didn't cry a bit; a tough guy like me wouldn't let women make him cry.'

What, you pried out a secret? That's interesting. What kind of thing is it, let me hear it if there is no hindrance.'

Koriya koriya Isokō, there is no immorality like exposing other people's secrets, shouldn't you restrain yourself?'

If that's the way it is, it can't be helped; shall Isokō also remain silent? It would be distressing if your true colors were known, you see.'

Koriya, please don't talk about my true colors. You shouldn't expose a close friend's matters in front of the public.'

Matsuhiko-sama, since he begs like that, Isokō will also preserve friendship and hold it in reserve until a certain time. In exchange, when Mankō fails to obey my orders, I will expose it. Hey Mankō, shall we keep silence for a while with that condition?'

You are a miser, a backbiter, a fault-finder, cunning, sly, immoral, a master of scheming, greedy, as if you were licking soy sauce dregs on a Chandala's backside. If I have my secret grasped by such a guy, I will never be able to lift my head up for a lifetime, so I'd rather make it public from my side in front of Matsuhiko-sama, so please don't mind it. Hey Isokō-sama, you gave me a grand worry. It's not as if I pilfered someone else's things, and since it's a delicious and interesting story that makes your mouth water when you hear it, it won't be much of a disgrace either. Anyone has some sort of romance, after all. While wearing a face as if to say women make them sick, there are many guys who get bound by women's yumaki cords and drool where people don't see them, so having some love story is rather a boast. With a blockhead like you, flowers won't bloom even if spring comes.'

You may blather however you please, that's fine. I won't interfere anymore. In exchange, even if you fail, Isokō will watch from the high ground, so you had better think of it that way.'

Matsuhiko-senseisama, please listen to what this old woman has to say. This man called Ban is an animal with whom neither vinegar nor konniyaku will work at all. Was it the winter before last, around the dusk when the cold wintry wind was blowing with a whistling sound, when my servant came to inform me in the inner rooms that a miserable beggar was shivering at the front gate of our house; since I too believe in the God of Kogitayama, and thinking that helping people is the divine service of God, I took a single rice ball and went out as far as the threshold to see, and lo and behold, wasn't there a wretched beggar wrapped in a straw mat, wearing tattered brocade like a procession of wakame seaweed or a boss of Shimeshi, shivering all over? Thereupon, thinking it so pitiful that he is a human being born alike from the breath of God, and that helping him is filial piety towards God, I placed a single rice ball on a tray, and with great politeness to that filthy beggar, saying, 'Well, you must be extremely hungry; come, eat even this and go back,' that beggar, sticking his eyes out from a face black as black, and blurting out, 'Oh, there are no demons in the sekai, I am truly grateful, I will never forget this favor,' bowed down with his waist bending back and forth like a rice-pounding grasshopper a hundred times or so, didn't he? I too felt my pity redoubled and was thinking of somehow finding even some secondhand yumaki and putting it on him. Though I placed the rice ball on a tray and held it out, he made no move to take it, and only kept bending his waist back and forth. Finding it tedious beyond endurance, when I asked him, 'Hey, don't you like this rice ball?', what that beggar said was that since he had just finished eating his fill of leftovers from a funeral nearby, he did not want a rice ball, but would like to have a single cup of warm tea; and since I am a female chivalrous person who took the nickname of Otora of the floating-wood village, I could not help but help him either, so I took his moss-covered hand, led him into the inner rooms, brought out tea that was boiling hot, and presented it to him with a teacup attached, saying, 'Now, partake of this.' And when I went into the inner room and peered through a tear in the shoji screen, it seemed he was a beggar by birth, and oh, his manners are bad. Swallowing boiling tea right from the spout of the earthenware tea-kettle with a gulp, scorching his throat, rolling his eyes round and round, and frothing at the mouth, wasn't he convulsing in agony? Thinking, 'Oh, how creepy to have dragged in a vile beggar,' when I went over in a panic, the blue earthenware kettle that I had cherished was smashed into two halves, the tea that had been boiled with such care was spilled on the tatami mats, and the tatami mats, without saying whether they were a feast or anything, licked it up with a nonchalant air, and wasn't he glaring with numerous narrow eyes lined up? Ho ho ho ho, when I looked at the place where that vile beggar had fallen flat on his back, he was lying with a loincloth hanging down that looked as if it had been simmered in soot, and exposing something pitch-black like a dried persimmon. Now, it was a major crisis; the whole household gathered around, made him drink water, and as a result of various ministrations, he finally revived his breath. However, because he had scorched his tongue, both his tongue and mouth were swollen up, and even when trying to ask his region or his name, he could not speak, so there was no way to ask either; and even when bringing writing materials and telling him to write his name, this fellow appeared to be totally blind and could not write a single character, so with no other choice, we had to hire a quack doctor, came in from the back door, gave him an enema, and finally made it so he could speak. After that, we poured oil on his clothing infested with lice, burned it all up, put the oldest clothing of our deceased grandfather on him, and since they said he was a creature with nowhere to go, I employed him as a servant and put him to work in the fields.'
