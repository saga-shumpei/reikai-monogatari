Chapter 14: 思ひ出の Song [1183]

思ひ出の歌〔1183〕

§ 1

Harukuniwake and his party parted from the family of four at the foot of the pass of Kajikatauge, and hastening their steps once more, headed toward the forest of Yamaguchi. Mankō beat time with his feet along the way and sang a marching song.

Mankō: 'When the Kami appear in the world To divide good from evil Ever since this Mankō was a child Saying that fellow is a fine man Praised by the villagers Acclaimed as a prodigy and boy wonder Both my parents rejoiced Saying a family treasure has been born Surely he will raise up the house As a peerless hero Bound to achieve worldly success Flattering and praising me unceasingly Raising me like flowers and butterflies Even if put into both eyes Cherishing me to the point of not hurting Biting, spitting, embracing me Carrying me on their back up the mountain paths Helping me and spoiling me night and day At last, coming and going, they raised me Into such worthless junk as this! Disliked by the villagers like centipedes Spat upon with phlegm Like a dog, drawing water in a ladle To be chased away—such a pitiful Good-for-nothing delinquent son they made of me! Not that I harbor resentment against my parents, But because their way of cherishing was wrong The sweet tea they made us runny-nosed brats drink Turned into poison Turning me into a stubborn, unruly fellow Who will not budge for the life of me Indulging in women, gambling at dice Though I do not take other people's property A liquor-thief and a wild rascal They ended up making me a public nuisance! Ah, Kamunagara Kami kami! Even so, the revered Kami-sama Do not abandon a good-for-nothing wretch like me Extending hands of infinite benevolence They cherished me. More than the love of two parents How many fold greater is the grace of Kami Incomprehensibly precious! Ever since I heard the teachings of benevolence My warped and twisted soul Was fundamentally reformed And now, as a glorious Sanjū Propagator, whose very name is auspicious, Accompanying Harukuniwake Mounting up to conquer the demons I have become a revered personage! Izanagi and Izanami who made this world, Great Naorahi whose heart is vast as well, Seeing straight and hearing straight anew, The great Kami who saved us— Whenever I recall Their grace, There is never a lull in the dew of tears! I too, from now on, shall polish my sincere heart With all my might, Chosen as an official of Kami Whom none can push aside or shake, Not to mention the august names of my ancestors, Even the august names of both my parents Manifesting them, to repay That great debt of rearing and nurturing— Even the time of eating rice I will not forget! Is it what they call the medicine of age? Lately I have grown homesick. Wanting to put them at ease quickly, My feelings are overflowing in my breast, My soul is beside itself! When the time comes to show filial piety to one's two parents, Parents are no more! Even if one thinks that, upon a stone pagoda One cannot even dress them in warm quilts, No matter how sweet food and drink One offers, without even saying whether it is sweet Or saying anything at all, If they are gone, what shall I do? To the Kami of the Sanjū-kyō I will do anything whatever! Oh, please prolong the lives of my two parents Until Mankō achieves brilliant feats! Leave them alive, I beg you. That is my foremost Morning and evening prayer! When Harukuniwake, under the shade of the forest, Met with his beloved parents, My heart at the moment I beheld My joy at meeting my younger sister— So overjoyed I could almost fly away, I could not remain calmly composed! Even if laughed at as a flusterer, Even if mocked as a buffoon, Faced with that scene, is there any time To worry about such things? Ah, Kamunagara Kami kami! Both parents who raised me Morning and evening joining their hands to Kami Praying that Mankō may reform even a moment sooner, Become a full-fledged valiant man, And return in triumph, wearing brocade To his native village, They are surely praying thus! Higher than the mountains is a father's grace, Deeper than the seas is a mother's compassion. Surpassing even those, yet higher And deeper is the grace of Kami! Ah, Kamunagara Kami kami! Grant this sincere heart of Mankō And purely guard my soul! Following my master's lord, With flags flying and drums rolling, the way of Kami Now we advance and march forth! Whether the morning sun shines or is cloudy, Whether the moon waxes or wanes, How could I ever forget the grace of my parents? How could I ever forget the grace of Kami? Kami are together with us, Man is the child of Kami, the shrine of Kami. Having heard such joyful teachings, Not even for a single day Can I let the days pass in vain. Ah, Kamunagara Kami kami! I humbly pray for this divine grace.'

§ 2

Harukuniwake sings along the way.

Where is Takaamahara, The pure and righteous land of Kami, The paradise of eternal glory, Where the people of pure Mitama Cast off their garments of Utsushiyo And thrive in Tokihakakiha, Oh, the wondrous land of Kami!

Where is Takaamahara, Where the Lord Onkami resides, The pure land of Kami without night, Where Tsukihi shine bright at dawn, And even the shadows of the stars sparkle, Brighter by many a hundredfold Than the worlds of Chijiyō, Those who dwell in this paradise Are all praised as Tennin, Placed within the boundaries of Furau and Fushi, Believing and loving the Sushin, Unfolding supreme Shiyōkaku, Thriving evermore throughout Tokoshihe, Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara, Reverend is the sacred land of Kami, In the heavenly realm of Takaamahara, There are two distinct divisions here, The first is called the Reigoku, The realm where Kami abides, The second realm is called the Tenkaku,

Called the land where pure Mitama and others, Having forsaken Chijiyō, Are people who have become Tennin, Rejoicing, taking courage, and playing, Reigoku and Tenkaku together, Where the sun and moon of love and faith Shine without night or day, Gold, silver, agate, lapis lazuli, giant clam, Crystal or coral palaces, Trees flourish in the fields, Like gems of peerless clarity, Tennin, men and women, forever Hold hands and labor and serve In the sacred tasks of Kami, The sole divine land of the universe, Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara, May the grace of Mitama be bestowed, Without defiling the Mitama of us Who were born upon Chijiyō, When having finished all the matters of Utsushiyo,

We become Mitama and ascend To Tenkaku, Enveloped in the precious grace of the Lord Kami, Living peacefully, joyfully, and eternally, O heavenly Kami, Lord Kuninotokotachi, Before the presence of the Kami of Miroku, We humbly and reverently pray, Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, Whether the moon waxes or wanes, We who have entrusted ourselves to Kami, How could we ever be stained by crookedness? The utmost purity, Kamunagara, Our Mitama bestowed by Kami, Nurtured in faith and love, This very body, just as it is, placing our divine register In the sacred land of Tenkaku, Grant it, we pray, Kamunagara, We pray before the presence of Kami, Infinite in benevolence and mercy, the Ōkami,

Sowed the seeds of humanity upon Chijiyō, Using the palace of flesh as the placenta To nurture the pure Mitama, Once they have grown to maturity, Welcoming them to the heavenly realm where the Sushin dwells, If You have granted them To serve in the divine tasks of Tenkaku, Then the child of Kami born as a human, If they strive in goodness, avoid evil, Believe in Kami and love Him, too, And become a Mitama that fulfills Their duty as a child of Kami, How could You ever cast them aside? When we think and think upon it, the human form Is truly a wondrous and precious thing, Oh Mankō-san, oh Gosankō-san, Oh Ryūkō-san, absorbing the benevolent heart Of the imperial deities, Well guarding the teachings of Kami,

Do not be caught in petty desires And defile the Mitama, If you become Mitama who can protect Azusaigusa upon the earth, Serving as the people of Tenkaku Who preserve boundless life and thrive, Then as a human, you are one Who is already without blemish, The Kami of Miroku has appeared, And the time has come when He establishes And divides the three realms of Utsushiyokami, We who have been born Into such a precious and great reign Are truly blessed and fortunate beings, Rejoice and celebrate the grace of Kami, Praise the virtue of Kami, Kami is with us, Man is the child of Kami and the palace of Kami, Never defile it, Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara,

We pray for the divine favor of Mitama."

Having sung thus, the four members of Harukuniwake's party cross the wilderness where the winter gales blow fiercely, and receiving the brilliant light of the sun upon their faces, in high spirits, they once again approach the forest of Yamaguchi. Mankō says,

Teacher, we have arrived once again at yesterday's theater of living arts. We certainly had good prey yesterday. Let us borrow lodging here again tonight. This time, who knows, perhaps the Hiyudorodoro might come crawling over.

Ha-ha-ha-ha, it would be troublesome if they transformed into konjac ghosts so quickly as yesterday. This forest is dangerous; come, let us whip our Hizakurage while it is still day and make a long ride as far as Ukikinohara.

How many miles is it as far as the forest of Ukiki?

Well, roughly about fifty miles or so.

That is terrible indeed. How on earth can our compass hold out?

Mankō has started blowing faint-hearted notes again. If we borrow the power of Kami, fifty miles or so is just one breath; come, let us go.

Making a brief bow to the Yamaguchi forest, paying no heed to details, they press on decisively, pointing their way toward the south.
