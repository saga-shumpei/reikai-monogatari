Chapter 2: Moon の Shadow [1171]

月の影〔1171〕

§ 1

Harukuniwake, while listening to the innocent snores of Mankō and Harukō, folded his arms, bowed his head, and remained lost in meditation for a while.

Isokō was the one who came hurriedly, panting as he climbed the steep slope of the forest.

Isokō: 'If I may say, Sensei, Okusama has arrived. Now, please hurry and descend to the front of the shrine!'

Harukuniwake: 'What? The wife has arrived—what on earth has she come for? I have no business with the wife. Tell her that meetings are not permitted and that she is to turn back immediately.'

Harukuniwake wore a displeased expression.

Isokō: 'No matter how much you say you have rights, Isokō does not think you have any such right of command toward the wife of Tamakuniwake-sama!'

Harukuniwake: 'What? Is it Isokohime? You said it was the wife, so I thought perhaps Kikukohime had chased after us again, and I considered her an outrageous wretch.'

Isokō: 'Indeed, she is the outrageous one! Please look upon Isokohime-sama. Anxious and distressed over the personal situation of Tamakuniwake-sama, and disregarding even her womanly status, she sought out this mountain slope through day and night. And as for the wife of fellow sendenshi, Kikukohime-sama—is she not truly the outrageous one? Isokō thinks that things like the affection of a husband and wife should not be so cold and distant.'

Harukuniwake: 'Aha-ha-ha-ha! The human heart is a thing that differs from person to person. Since I have set forth to propagate the teachings in this manner, I have forgotten my wife, forgotten my home, and forgotten even my own life!'

Isokō: 'How very cold-hearted you are, indeed. If Kikukohime-sama were to hear this, she would surely be disappointed and disheartened. Without knowing at all that her irreplaceable husband in heaven and earth has such a (joruri) cold and distant heart, ever since parting in the capital—in the rains of morning and the winds of evening—she has not had a single moment when she forgot him, even for a transient while. When she thinks of your heartless nature—ah, what shall I do, oh, what shall I do...—to lament thus is like pressing a seal upon a stone monument of testimony. Since you are a sensei who forgets his own essential wife, it is no wonder at all that you would forget disciples like us. To be left behind and abandoned halfway—that truly... is so cold of you, truly so cold of you.'

Harukuniwake: 'Aha-ha-ha-ha, what a strange fellow you are; it looks as though the spirit of a monkey from Kajika Pass has possessed you. Heh, what troublesome human coolies I have brought along. Better yet, when Isokohime returns, I should put Isokō inside my sleeve, tie up the tip of the sleeve with a vine so he cannot crawl out, have him taken back, and thrown away in the corner of a privy! Aha-ha-ha!'

Mankō and Harukō had hitherto forced their sleepless eyes shut against Harukuniwake's stern commands, deliberately snoring loudly and feigning sleep, but at the sheer amusement of it all, both of them burst out at once:

Mankō and Harukō: 'Gyatsu-ha-ha-ha! Gyutsufu-ha-ha-ha!'

Harukuniwake: 'Mankō, Harukō, you were pretending to be asleep before Harukuniwake all this time—what impossible fellows you are.'

Mankō: 'It cannot be helped if there are only men indeed; it is such a bleak scene. Please look at the vicinity of that shrine. There are Isokohime-sama and Imakohime-sama—there is plenty of lively charm there! Come now, Mankō, Harukō, let us give up playing the substitute raccoon-dogs, accompany Sensei, and go enjoy the spring-like atmosphere in front of the shrine! Ever since we left the hall of Saien, we have not even smelled the fragrance of the opposite sex, and it has been nothing but bleak scenes—in a place where heart and mind are utterly withered, a supreme beauty drifting with the aura of spring sunlight has arrived, so I cannot help feeling in high spirits. Come now, let us leave the forest and wander around thereabouts with Isokō!'

Mankō: 'It somehow sounds to Mankō's ears as though the voices of laughter are welling up near the shrine, and I cannot bear it.'

Mankō: 'That is why junior says that the best way to get along in human society is to laugh; laughter is the shortcut that opens the gates of heaven. Come now, Sensei, let us go together with Isokō. Even if you meet Isokohime-sama after all this time, you will probably not feel too ill about it. Is she not your sister-in-law? Before long, Matsukō-sama will likely bring Itakō back as well, so a reunion of brothers is a matter drawing close at hand. Come now, quickly lift your palanquin. No matter how grave the dignity of a sendenshi may be, if your hips are that heavy, you will never be able to perform myriad-transforming activities!'

Harukuniwake: 'Aha ha ha ha! In that case, supported by three subordinates, I, Harukuniwake, shall set out for the dangerous zone as well.'

Isokō: '(In a theatrical tone) As you are so promptly aware, for my part as Isokō, I consider this the utmost of honors. Well then, I shall take the vanguard and act as your guide. Now, Mankō, Harukō, guard the front and rear of the Sendenshi Harukuniwake and follow in my footsteps. Down, down, down!'

Sweeping the surroundings with his staff, he advanced downwards toward the shrine.

Harukuniwake finally advanced before the shrine, and after finishing his obeisance,

Harukuniwake: 'Tamakuniwake-dono, how is your condition? My, my, Yoshikohime-sama, you have gracefully come to visit us. Ah, with this I too am completely at ease. Truly, Tamakuniwake-dono, you have suffered terribly.'

Yoshikohime: 'Harukuniwake-sama, my husband has received your profound and various cares, and I have no words to express my gratitude. It must be that God has awakened our eyes for the repentance of us husband and wife. When I think of it, when I think of it, we have truly received wondrous divine grace.'

Imokohime: 'Imokohime-sama, it is Harukuniwake here. You have had a hard time of it. You must have grown quite exhausted, no?'

Yotahiko: 'Ehe ha ha ha! Women are certainly convenient creatures, aren't they? To Tamakuniwake-dono, a brief, conventional, and simple greeting; then toward Isokohime-sama of the opposite sex, a tender attentiveness that leaves no stone unturned; pouring the surplus of that charm generously over Imokohime-sama—ah, what a flawless method of our sensei! Isokō, if you had been born a woman, you would have spoken just a bit more gentle words to me, wouldn't you? It is just a mistake between Isokohime and Isokō—can the treatment by society change this much because of that?'

Isokohime burst into laughter and said,

Isokohime: 'Oho ho ho ho! How amusing! Harukuniwake-sama has brought along a company, so surely the journey must be filled with a Yaji-Kita mood and be most delightful.'

Harukuniwake: 'At any rate, there is nothing as joyful as acting for the sake of God. In connection with this, an indescribably wondrous thing has suddenly occurred to me here.'

Hearing this, Isokohime wore a look of astonishment and said,

Isokohime: 'That is more wonderful than anything. And what might this happy event be? Please let me hear it quickly.'

Harukuniwake answered 'Hai' and lowered his head.

Isokohime: 'In place of our sensei, I, Isokō, shall assume the duty of reporting. Harukuniwake-sama has achieved a reunion with his brother. My, what a splendid younger brother you have! Moreover, since he is the secretary to General Katahiko, he is quite a distinguished person. Both his personal appearance and his countenance are absolute twins to our sensei.'

Harukuniwake: 'What, you had a reunion with your brother? That is indeed a most auspicious matter. However, is it not rather strange that the secretary to the Brahman General Katahiko should be such? Humans seem to be tossed about in the palm of the hand of God called fate. Is it somehow not possible for him to return his allegiance to Izanagi-no-Mikoto's teachings, and for you brothers together to devote yourselves to the divine work?'

So saying, she gazed into Harukuniwake's face with a somewhat worried expression.

Harukuniwake: 'Through the divine providence of Amenomakanushi-no-Ōkami, He will surely make things go favorably. We went seeking the whereabouts of Itakō-sama, so before long, my younger brother will return here after pledging his allegiance.'

Isokohime: 'Where has Itakō-sama gone? I fear he may have been captured by the hands of the Brahmans, has he not?'

Seeing Isokohime's clouded complexion, Harukuniwake replied,

Harukuniwake: 'Hai. Itakō was captured by Brahman soldiers and is confined in a rock cave on Mount Kiyoharu. As a result of my younger brother's repentance and allegiance, he went to rescue Itakō-sama to begin his service to God. Until I successfully bring Itakō-sama back, I, this Harukuniwake, am resolved not to allow myself to be called his brother.'

So saying, he grew tearful.

Just as they were speaking thus, voices of four or five people could be heard coming from the lower part of the valley path. Tamakuniwake strained his ears to those voices, his heart leaping with anticipation as he wondered if it was an attack by the remnants of the Brahman teachings. Just as this was happening, those who returned while escorting Itakō were Matsukō, Ryūkō, and several others. Matsukō performed gasshō before the front of the shrine, finished chanting the prayer of thanksgiving, turned respectfully toward the company to make a bow, and stepped forward before Harukuniwake.

Harukuniwake changed his tone,

'From today, Matsukō is the younger brother of Harukuniwake, and Tatsukō-sama is my brother-in-law. By all means, I want you both to get along well together with everyone and devote yourselves to the divine work.'

Both Matsukō and Tatsukō choked with tears of joy at the word, unable to even raise their heads, and prostrating themselves upon the great earth, kept their faces turned downward.

'With the permission of everyone, will you not tell me in detail the situation in Armenia after Harukuniwake parted from you? And by what procedures did you enter Brahmanism? I want to hear the motivation behind it.'

'After my elder brother crossed over to the single island of the Dragon Palace as a missionary from the holy city of Armenia, we were attacked by a faction of Brahmanism, and the four castes starting with Kshatriya and Chōgyō, as well as Vaishya and Shudra, were scattered in all directions, and a horrific disaster that was too dreadful to look upon suddenly broke out. It being a time shortly after Ōgetsuhime-sama had fled back from the Caucasus Mountains in a state of ruin, when the wounds of exhaustion had not yet fully healed, the holy city suddenly lost its defensive power; Uraruhiko and Uraruhime-sama's lineage concealed their forms and escaped to the land of Tokoyo, the residences of officials and commoners were burned and destroyed, and only a few samurai mansions remained near the Uraru River. The withered grass on the vast plains and piles of white bones intertwined in the thickets, the shadows of the capital of yore could no longer be seen, and from the distinguished personages of the scholarly and high gates down to the Vaishya caste, countless numbers threw themselves into the Uraru River and became the sport of the waters, while among them, some drifted away to distant lands and humbled themselves as lowly rustics, or hid away in deep mountain hamlets, living lives of remorse in thatched huts with bamboo doors, how pitiful the fates of those noble lords. Their night garments were thin, the dawn frost was cold, the smoke of their morning meals ceased, and not a few died upon Mount Shouyang. Amidst it all, I encountered the sorrow of living separation and death separation from my parents and siblings, and while wandering without a fixed destination across the wide realm, I was discovered by Katahiko of Brahmanism, and against my heart's desire, with the sole purpose of searching for my elder brother's whereabouts, I have passed the days until today. Ah, the wondrous guidance of the great Gods, there is nothing so joyful as this.'

And he squeezed his tears into his sleeve.

Everyone listened to Matsukō's tale, struck with emotion, and some even wept softly. The night grew progressively deeper, the moon was wrapped in dark clouds, and suddenly the curtain of darkness was deeply lowered on all four sides. The cries of mountain apes echoed desolately from valleys here and there.

『貴方の壮健なお顔を見て治国別も安心致しました。お蔭で弟に公然と対面が出来るやうになりました。あゝ惟神霊幸倍坐世』

と合掌する。

『もし先生、道公の一行はもとの森蔭へ転宅致しませうか。兄弟御対面につきまして何れ海山の話がありませう。貴方が五十子姫様と御面会の時も治国別様は気を利かしてあの森蔭に待つて居て下さつたのですから、此方も其返礼にしばらく此幕を切り上げようぢやありませぬか。ねえ五十子姫様、さうでせう』

『旦那様、私が手を曳いて上げますから、あの森蔭迄遠慮致しませう』

『別に男と男との兄弟が久し振りに巡り合うたのですから、又夫婦の御面会とは模様が違ひます。何卒御遠慮は要りませぬから、ここに居て下さいな』

『伊太公お前は如何だつた。随分困つたらうな。玉国別の言ふ事も聞かずに血気の勇を揮つて飛び出すものだから皆のお方に心配をかけたのだよ。これからは気をつけて貰はねば困るよ』

『はい、誠に申訳も厶りませぬ。至つて至らぬ伊太公、此後は屹度心得、自由行動は今日限り鼬の道切れといたちます』

『アハヽヽヽ何処迄も気楽な男だなア、道公の私もあきれて了ひました。先生、此奴はもう脈上りですよ。こんながらがらを旅につれて歩くのは一つ考へ物です。奥様のお帰りの時に懐へでも入れて持つて帰つて頂いたら如何でせう』

『五十子姫だつて、さう二人も軽い男を懐に入れて帰るのは困ります。ねえ今子さま』

『あの五十子姫様の弱い事を仰有りますこと、今子は歯痒ゆくなりましたワ。男の三人や五人は髪の毛一筋あればつないで帰れるぢやありませぬか。現代の男はまるで屁の様なものですからな。ホヽヽヽヽ』

『此奴あ怪しからぬ。斯う女に侮辱されては伊太公も男子を廃業したくなつて来たわい』

治国別は言葉を改めて、

『今日より松公は治国別の弟、竜公さまは義理の弟、何卒皆さまと一緒に仲良うして神業に尽して貰ひ度い』

松、竜両人はハツとばかりに嬉し涙に咽び頭も得上げず大地に踞みて俯向き居る。

『皆さまに御免を蒙つて治国別が其方と別れし後のアーメニヤの状況を詳しく聞かして呉れないか。さうして其方は如何云ふ手続きでバラモン教に這入つたのか。その動機を聞かして貰ひ度い』

『兄上様がアーメニヤの神都より宣伝使となつて竜宮の一つ島へ渡られた後、バラモン教の一派に襲はれ刹帝利、浄行を始め毘舎、首陀の四族は四方に散乱し目も当てられぬ大惨事が突発しました。大宜津姫様がコーカス山から敗亡の体で逃げ帰つて来られてから間もない疲弊の瘡の癒え切らない所だから、忽ち神都は防禦力を失ひ常世の国へウラル彦、ウラル姫様一族は其姿を隠し玉ひ諸司百官庶民の住宅は焼き亡ぼされ、ウラル河の辺りに武士の館が少し許り残されたのみ。離々たる原上の草、累々たる白骨叢に纒はれて、ありし昔の都の俤も見えず蓮府槐門の貴勝を初め毘舎の族に至るまでウラル河に身を投じて水屑となつたものも沢山にあり、中には遠国に落ち延び田夫野人の賤しきに身を寄せ或は山奥の片田舎に忍び隠れて桑門竹扉に詫住居する貴勝の身の果敢なさ。夜の衣は薄くして暁の霜冷たく朝餉の煙も絶えて首陽に死する人も少からず。その中にも私は父母兄弟に生別れ、死別れの憂目に会ひ、広い天下を当所もなく漂流する内バラモン教の片彦に見出され、心ならずも兄様の所在を探るを唯一の目的として今日まで日を送つて参りました。アヽ有難き大神様の御引合せ、コンナ嬉しい事は厶りませぬ』

と袖に涙を搾る。

一同は松公の物語を聞き感に打たれてすすり泣きするものさへありき。夜は段々と更け渡り、月は黒雲に包まれ、忽ち四面暗黒の帳は深く下ろされぬ。山猿の叫ぶ声、彼方此方の谷間より消魂しく響き来る。
