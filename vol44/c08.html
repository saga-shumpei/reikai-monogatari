Chapter 8: Light と熱 [1177]

光と熱〔1177〕

§ 1

Descended from the spirits of Tenōsei, Jisaiten, with Ōkunihiko as the principal deity, made plans to cause the teachings of Reishiyutaijiu to shine brilliantly throughout Udai and to save and protect Yobito; thus the teachings of Brahmanism that were established were originally not evil.

However, failing to understand the truth of Geniu and vainly preaching the path of Keiseijiushi, falling into Yūgenfujitsukau, people on Chijiyō endure Kannan and, shedding Ikichi while making Sonahemono to Kami, mistaken in thinking that this is the way to satisfy Mikokoro, unconsciously deceived by the crooked deity Yamatanōrochi, instead of saving people, for that very purpose lead people astray into the root country and the bottom country.

Pitying that tragic Sanjiyō, the great deity of Izumitama who appears as the lord Kami of Takaamahara, Kuniharutachi no Ōkami, making no distinction between Tenjiyō and Chijiyō, raises up the spirits of Ōmitakara into eternal and infinite salvation, and day and night devotes His heart to leading them into the righteous path of compassion and faith and bestowing upon them the divine grace—truly a most precious state of affairs.

Tokoyohikokami and Tokoyohime, themselves also bewitched by demons, established the teachings of Ural and, looking up to Pangu Shiwang as the lord Kami, went forth to open up the world; how formidable is that momentum!

How could the Kami of supreme benevolence and love ever forgive this? Desiring to wash and purify the bodies and souls of Hitokusa, who are the children of Kami, making them pure and beautiful, and to open the imperial garden of heaven, descending the divine pillar of Izumitama and the august pillar of Mizunomitama into thiskonoyo, changing and transforming in various and miscellaneous ways, Haniyasuhiko and Haniyasuhime appeared as the imperial commands of Kami, establishing Sogo no Oshihe, not to mention Koganeyama, Ubusunayama, Manjuyama, Caucasus Mountain, and Mount Griddhakuta, and crossing over to Onogorojima, reflecting the form of heaven upon the sacred land of Aya, saving Yobito into the true great path—how precious it is!

Kami Susanoo no Ōkami, who has arisen as Mizunomitama, with the desire to save without exception the bodies and souls of the three realms of Geniukami, brought down His precious august body into the world, burdened with Chiza no Oketo, appearing in the form of man without a single corner left throughout the under-heaven, braving wind and rain, facing ice and snow, becoming the heat of thiskonoyo, becoming light, becoming salt, appearing as the flower of fruition, and saving all the bodies and souls lost in darkness—truly this is of the utmost preciousness.

The propagation emissary of the teachings of Kami, the party of Harunotowake, receiving the august words of Izumitama, left behind the mansion of the sacred garden where the original Daijin is enshrined, crossed over the rugged mountain slopes of the wild moor where the wild wind blows, arrived at the grove of the shrine, unexpectedly met the party of Tamakuniwake, spent two nights there, and reunited with his younger brother Matsukimi and others whom he had parted from some time ago; suppressing the tears of joyful surprise, leading five companions in accordance with the divine oracle, singing the most auspicious propagation song as he climbed the steep slope of the Kajika Pass, he finally arrived safely and securely in the shade of the grove where old trees grow thick in Yamaguchi. Ah, indeed, Kami be praised! The spirits of Kami blessing them, the party of Harunotowake executed the divine mission without mishap and, so as to speak of the return words before the august presence of Kami, while bathing in the morning sun of Mizuzuki as she lay horizontally in the Ryūgū Mansion stretching out with her pillow to the east, ah, indeed, Kami be praised! For the precious divine grace, let this story proceed without delay, which we reverently pray before the august presence of Kuniharutachi no Ōkami, who has appeared as the original ancestors of heaven and earth, Toyokunihime no Ōmikami, and Kami Susanoo no Ōkami.

The party of Harunotowake arrived at last at twilight at the Yamaguchi grove on the southern foot of Mount Kajika, where old trees grow dense, and it came to pass that they spent a night in this grove, which is dark even by day. Here, only the foundation stones of the site of an ancient shrine remained. In former times it was called the shrine of the mountain deity, and Ōyamatsumi no Kami had been enshrined there. Exposed to the natural wind and rain and left to ruin, having suffered the calamity of the god of fire on account of the bonfires of beggars, without the opportunity for reconstruction nor any zealous believers, it retained only its pitiful and desolate remains. Therefore, almost no one knew what deity had been enshrined in this grove. However, this grove was quite large, and no one had ever set foot within it. Those who mistakenly ventured deep into the forest never returned, and so it was also called the Demon's Grove. It was even rumored that a gigantic snake lurked there, swallowing men, and it was feared by the people.

The grove of the shrine at Kajikatauge is actually dedicated to Daijisaiten, but somehow it came to be called the shrine of Yamakami. It was confused with the god of this Yamaguchi grove. It seems that most cases where the Saijin of ancient shrines become Fumei are due to reasons like this.

Harukō: 'Come now, Matsukō-san, have no worries. By the divine power of Harukō's living Kototama, we shall drive Orochi and demons a thousand ri away, clear the heavenly expanse as clear as crystal, and show it to you; then you too will clear away your doubts, right?'

Mankō: 'Your Kototama is also a dubious thing. The teacher's Kototama immediately manifests divine power, but as for Harukō's Kototama, it is a hoarse, croaking voice; far from clearing up, even our hearts become gloomy, and my ears hurt so much that I feel as if I were falling down to the nethermost country—at least this Mankō-sama does.'

Harukō: 'Come now, whether it is the teacher's Kototama or Harukōsama's Kototama, is there any difference in Kototama? Does not the Kami say that there is no distinction of old and new in faith?'

Mankō: 'If so, why don't you use the Kototama to make this darkness even a little bit bright? That is the proof of reality, and after seeing that, this Mankō-sama will also be satisfied.'

Harukō: 'Is there any real difference between the word Aoueiya uttered by the teacher and the word Aoueiya uttered by Harukōsama? It seems that when one grows old, one lacks strength in the belly after all. Hey teacher, isn't that so?'

Since not the slightest efficacy of the Kototama comes forth, no matter what you say, a kobushi-musha is a kobushi-musha after all! Ah-ha-ha-ha!'

Harukō, becoming fired up and puffing his nose a little:

Harukō: 'Hey Manshū, do not make too much fun of me. People are not always what they seem. There is no telling what hidden arts one might possess. Wait, wait; I will manifest the divine power of the Kototama and make that fellow Mankō burst into repeated cries of wonder and astonishment. Tie a twisted headband so your head does not split, rearrange your navel so it does not shift, and fasten your bellyband tightly. It is a Kototama like firing a forty-eight-centimeter giant cannon, so make preparations so as not to damage your listening apparatus. Now, it is the opening of the barrel for the Kototama emission. One, two, three!'

Light is the shinshin of Sushin.

Thought and language do not possess power in themselves,

However, the shinshin has a power of its own within it, and Tenkai is created precisely by this.

The world of chijiyō and all things within it together are also things created by this.

That such a noble divine power exists within the shinshin,
