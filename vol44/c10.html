Chapter 10: 奇遇 [1179]

奇遇〔1179〕

§ 1

Mankō, Harukō, and Tatsukō finally breathed sighs of relief, their faces freed from the malaria-like chill, and gazed at the woman's face with a sense of wonder. Matsuhiko treated the woman with a vague yet tender kindness, offering various words of comfort. Harukuniwake bowed his head out of sheer pity, blinking his eyes and quietly wiping away his tears, and spoke thus:

Harukuniwake: 'If I hear aright, a tragic curtain has fallen upon your household, hasn't it? And yet, because you listened to the teachings of God from Ōgonhime-sama and chanted the norito of Ananai-kyō, you were captured by the Brahmans—how truly pitiable a thing that is. Nevertheless, do set your mind at ease. Your parents surely have no danger to their lives. I shall somehow rescue them from this very moment and hand them over to you.'

The woman wiped away her tears of joy, and spoke thus:

Kaede: 'Oh, thank you so much for speaking to me with such kindness; I am truly grateful. I feel as though I have encountered God Himself. I beseech you, have pity upon my wretched circumstances and save them. Will my parents surely be saved, I wonder?'

Harukuniwake: 'I shall surely show you that they are saved. Do not trouble your heart. Now tell me, what are the names of your parents?'

Kaede: 'Ah, the name of my father is Uzuhiko, and my mother is called Shizuko. And my name is called Kaede.'

Harukuniwake: 'And what might the name of the elder brother you seek be called?'

Kaede: 'Ah, my brother's name was called Shun. Simply because I longed to meet that brother again, the three of us—parent and child—wandered through the vast land of Fusa, and at last came as far as the banks of the Lion River... My parents grew old, and their footsteps upon the road were no longer steady, so they settled their dwelling right there.'

Hearing this woman's tale, Harukō drew a heavy breath, pressed his mouth into the shape of the character for 'he', closed his eyes, and while incessantly emitting groans and sighs, sank deep into some profound reflection.

Mankō spoke with great vigor:

Mankō: 'Hey Harukō, what is this, making such a solemn face—isn't this the nice girl you gave birth to? When I thought you had uttered some useless kototama and given birth to an ogress, it turns out to be none other than a peerless nice girl. Why don't you make a bit of noise? A time like this is precisely when you should be boasting.

That phantom which I thought in my heart to be an ogress,

Is a nice girl beyond all comparison—

just like that. Truly, you are the man of distinction in tonight's kototama battle. By discovering this woman, we have gained a foothold, and the momentum has turned toward utterly overturning General Ranch's camp from its foundations and manifesting divine power. Why are you groaning and sighing? Pull yourself together, won't you, eh!'

Harukō spoke feebly:

Harukō: 'Ah... I am sorry. What on earth should I do?'

So speaking, he let fall teardrops the size of beans, patter-patter. Just at that moment, the moon of the eighteenth night began to rise, grazing Mount Kajaka. Even though it was within the forest, the surroundings gradually grew faintly bright all around. The candle flame was replaced anew.

Mankō spoke with high spirits:

Mankō: 'What is this, Harukō, you are weeping, aren't you? What kind of seed of an Ananai-kyō propagation envoy are you, that you should whimper and howl like that? Let me give you a jolt of life, so brace yourself.'

So speaking, he clenched his fists and struck Harukō's back two or three times in succession.

Mankō: 'Now, that elder brother whom Kaede just spoke of—that is me, this very Harukō!'

Mankō: 'What, you are the older brother of that nice girl? Oh, now that I hear it so, there does indeed seem to be something about you that resembles her from nowhere in particular. My dear teacher, such strange things do happen in this world, don't they? This too must surely be an auspicious drawing-together brought about by God. When I saw Harukō staying up without sleep to raise those useless kototama, I wondered at him as a bizarre fellow and tried to sleep, yet my guardian spirit told me the truth, so I could not sleep after all. The souls of brother and sister must have communed with one another, I suppose. Oh, Harukozama, congratulations! Kaedesama, congratulations! I offer you my felicitations. My teacher here, too, has had a long-awaited reunion with his brother in this very Matsuhiko-sama. How unpredictable human destiny is, my dear teacher—is it not truly marvelous?'

'Indeed, if there are such mysterious things, here they truly are. Generally, those who become sendenshi are only those who have been separated from their parents and kiyōdai by fate, or who have fallen into an unnopposable unmei to stand in the world again, and they are saved by the grace of Kami and are performing goyō; thus, if anyone washes and exposes their raireki, they are all nothing but hisan ones. If we were to return to ningengokoro and think it out, we could not ease our hearts even for ichiji, but since we are illuminated by love, faith, and the light of Kamisama and are forgetting the sorrows of chijiyō, ah.'

He says, looking down shōzen. Mankō, with a voice sounding like namidagoe yet purposely trying to act genki,

'Sensei, if you are shiyoge like that, what are we to do? Did you not always say that human shinrei exists for the sake of kuwanki? It seems to you are in a state where kuwanki departs and hiai arrives. Please pull yourself together a little.'

'No, for me this is hiai born of an excess of kuwanki. In other words, I am drowning in tears of gratitude. Because these are tears of suijiki, thanking the grace of Kamisama more than ever, do not worry about me so much.'

'I too am overflowing with tears of kuwanki, an-an-an! On-on-on, O Harukō—no, Toshisama, you too must be overflowing with tears of joy. If they are tears of kuwanki, it would be fine to let them flow until the dikes break. An-an-an, I am crying so much from happiness that I am having my fill of it!'

Harukō, again in a namidagoe,

'Harikunowake-sensei-sama, thank you very much. I humbly beg you to treat my younger sister's circumstances kindly.'

'Un, I am satisfied too, but you must be quite satisfied as well.'

'Were you my older brother? I am Kaede. How wonderfully you have remained safe. Please, save the lives of our father and mother. If I can only let my elder brother know of this, even if Kaede dies right now, I will have no lingering thoughts left in this world. Ah, Kamunagara tamashii sakihai mase.'

'My younger sister, you must have suffered a great deal. Even as for me, there has not been a single moment when I forgot about my parents and kiyōdai. Gazing at the morning rain and the evening wind, looking out at the skies of Armenia, wondering how our parents were and how my sister was, I do not know how much I wept until the seeds of my tears were exhausted. Picked up by the sendenshi of Harikunowake, I entered the path of Kami, bathed in the rain of kuwanki, always distracting my mind by thinking, "Let us not dwell on things that cannot be returned," and though I live on the surface with three-bu-go-rin of this floating world just uttering foolish banter, the binding ties of deep love and affection can by no means be severed. Sister, I too wanted to meet you.'

Without minding the eyes of others, he holds Kaede's body in his arms, unable to utter a single word, and collapses in tears. Wishing to raise their spirits, Harikunowake stands up and, with a clear voice, begins to chant:

'Though the morning sun shines or clouds over, Though the moon fills or wanes, Even if the great earth sinks, Though the deities of Magatsuhi rage, Yet the teaching path of Makoto, Of the single Sango, saves the world. Kami appears in the manifest realm, Distinguishing upright kami from wicked ones. Naobi, who created this world, Oomainaobi of expansive heart, Whatever happens in the world of men, Look upon it straight, listen to it straight, Correct and reform the disasters of the body. O Harukō and Kaede, both of you, With peace of heart and tranquility, Entrust yourselves to Kami! By the grace Of the awesome and noble Kami, Parents, brothers, and sisters meet again, And to receive and hold the joy Of the heavenly pure land is before your eyes. Harikunowake, relying upon the power Of the Kami of Sango, Shall surely grant and fulfill The desires of you two. Before the august presence of Kami, Kamehiko Appears as Harikunowake, And earnestly prays, Ah, Kamunagara, Kami, Kami!'

晴公『三五の恵の露に浴しつつ 浮世の夢を覚しけるかな。 妹と聞くより心飛び立ちて 抱きつきたくぞ思ひけるかな。 バラモンに捕へられたる父母の 身の行末を果かなくぞ思ふ。 さりながら神の恵は垂乳根の 身を隅もなく守りたまはむ。 垂乳根の父珍彦よ母の君よ 今兄妹が救ひまつらむ。 さは云へどか弱きわれの力ならず 産土山の神の恵みに。 治国別神の司に助けられ 吾垂乳根を救ふ嬉しさ。 曲神の如何程せまり来るとも 神の力におひ退けやらむ。 妹よ心安かれ三五の 神は吾等を見捨てたまはじ』

楓『有難し兄の命の言の葉を 胸にたたみて守りとやせむ。 アーメニヤ恋しき家をふり捨てて 逍ひし親子の身の果なさよ。 黄金姫神の司に助けられ またもやここに救はれにけり。 何事も皆神様の御経綸 見直し見れば憂き事もなし。 憂き事のなほ此上に積るとも 何か恐れむ神のまにまに』

松彦『月も日も大空に照る世の中は 曲のかくらふ隙はあらまし。 ランチてふ軍の司の前に出て 生言霊をたむけてや見む。 愛信の誠の剣振りかざし 曲のとりでを切りはふりなむ。 面白しあゝ勇ましき門出かな 神に仕へし軍司の』

万公『世の中に吾子に勝る宝なし 珍彦静子の心しのばゆ。 珍彦よ静子の姫よ待てしばし 救の神と現はれゆかむ。 ゆくりなく廻り会ひたる山口の 森は結びの神にますらむ』

竜公『常暗の森を照らして進み来る 怪しき影に驚きしかな。 さりながら世にも稀なるナイスぞと 悟りし時の心安けさ。 今となり身の愚かさを顧みて 顔の色さへ赤くなりぬる。 吾胸に醜の曲津の潜むらむ 正しき人をおぢ怖れけり。 村肝の心に潜む曲神を 払はせたまへ三五の神。 治国別神の司に従ひて 言霊戦に向ふ嬉しさ』

治国別『山口の森に休らひ兄妹の 名乗りあげたる事の床しさ。 片彦やランチ将軍何者ぞ 彼は人の子人の身なれば。 吾こそは神の御子なり神の宮 いかで恐れむ人の御子らに。 さりながら心高ぶる事勿れ 言霊戦に向ふ人々。 たらちねの親子兄妹廻り会ひ 抱き喜ぶ時の待たるる。 夜や更けぬ月は御空に上りましぬ いざいねませよ百の人達』

晴公『神司宣らせたまへる言の葉も 守るよしなき今日の嬉しさ。 村肝の心勇みて森の夜の 明けゆく空を待ちあぐむなり』

楓『なつかしき兄の命よ治国別の 神の司の御言守りませ さりながら妾も心勇み立ち ねるに寝られぬ今宵ばかりは』

治国別『兄妹の心はさもやあるべしと 直日に見直し聞き直しおく いざさらば万公五三公竜公よ 松彦共に一ねむりせよ』

松彦『吾兄の言葉にならひ人々よ よくねむりませ寅の刻まで』

斯く歌ひて治国別一行は、やすやすと眠りについた。晴公、楓の兄妹は嬉しさの余り一睡もせず辺りを憚り、ひそびそと長物語を涙とともに語り明かさむと此場を立出で兄妹は月光を浴びて森の外を逍遥する真夜中、初冬の月は皎々として満天に輝き、此森の外面は白く光つて居る。
