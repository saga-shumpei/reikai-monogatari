Chapter 1: 笑の Blessing [1170]

笑の恵〔1170〕

§ 1

Listening closely to the whispering voice of a maiden, he wondered with a start how strange it was as he listened.

The innocent moon shone brightly, illuminating Harukuniwake's head through the leaves of the trees.

In the winter wind that blew up suddenly, the withered leaves of the trees fell scatteringly like hail.

Suddenly, the woman's whispering was blocked by the blowing wind, and while being heard in fragments, finally, like the aftermath of a dream, it stopped abruptly, and only the sound of the winter wind crossing the treetops could be heard echoing through the surroundings.

Harukō: 'Teacher, Mankō cannot understand it clearly, but can you not hear the unexpected voice of a woman in this mountain? The source of the voice somehow seems to be near the shrine, and is not that fellow Michikō putting on a carefree voice like that, playing some kind of farce for Tamakuniwake-sama, and offering it as entertainment?'

Isokō: 'No, no, it is surely not like that. Blocked by the sound of the wind, it cannot be heard clearly, but it seems somehow to be a woman's voice. No matter how skillfully a voice is disguised, a fake is after all a fake. There is somewhere that is different. In the voice just now, there seems to be mixed, inexplicably, surprise, joy, and sadness. Might it be that, by some chance, Isokohime-sama has come on a visit of inquiry to the ailing Tamakuniwake-sama, guided by the manifestation of the Kami?'

Mankō: 'Indeed, if it is heard that way, it may well be so. If so, we cannot remain at ease in a place like this. Come, Teacher, let us go together with Mankō to offer our greetings.'

Isokō: 'There is no need to go in a hurry. Since Tamakuniwake-sama's business will be finished, and Michikō is with him, he will surely let us know somehow or other. Until then, since there might be various circumstances, it is better to stay back. For in this world, there is such a thing as unwelcome kindness, you see.'

Mankō: 'Indeed, as expected of the Teacher. You have thought of everything so thoroughly. This Mankō is also filled with admiration. To the place where they are having the pleasure of a precious meeting with their beloved wife, for an unrefined man to burst in would be desolate indeed, you see. This Mankō will also send his wholehearted sympathy and decide to hold back for a while.'

Isokō: 'Un, that would be best. How about taking a nap around here? If you do not rest reasonably, it will become a nuisance by disturbing our sound sleep, you see.'

Mankō: 'Hehe...'

Harukō, from beside them, spoke:

Isokō: 'No matter what you say, since he is the master of sparrows, it cannot be helped, you see. Though they chirp chyun-chyun chaa-chaa during the day, the moment evening falls, not a single chyun or syutsu do they utter—that is the very nature of a sparrow. But this fellow does not know the time, so he chirps endlessly, causing great trouble to us as well.'

Mankō: 'Oi Harukō-san, if I am a sparrow, you are a swallow. Or if not yet that, a bell cricket. Are you not a noisy and unsettled fellow whose voice alone goes gachya-gachya gachya, and whose very movements are restless? You are an article just like a clapper in an autumn field, my friend.'

Harukō: 'Hen, Haru-sama is after all spring. A clapper in an autumn field—still, what a fine nickname you have given me. But since a clapper is something that chases away sparrows, well now, attsuha-ha-ha-ha!'

Isokō: 'Both of you, how about sleeping to a reasonable degree, pray? Is Harukuniwake not troubled by you?'

Mankō: 'Please look at this. On every single leaf dew is gathering, and the moon is lodging there, shining brilliantly, as if enveloped entirely in lapis lazuli light. My eyes have forgotten how to sleep, longing for the light of lapis lazuli, and they simply will not blow the horn of a truce. Please overlook this Mankō for a little while longer.'

'Un, I ask you to be as quiet as possible.'

'Sensei, may we not be getting down about now? Even Tamakuniwake-sama's eyes are bad, and Michikō alone may have gone to the Okusama, and being made to listen to various scenes of lamentation, he might be troubled. From here, shall Isokō and Sumikō go together and see how things are?'

'Since I do not know what kind of circumstances there might be, if you go, it is better to go stealthily, hiding your footsteps and slipping away. Leaving the sparrows and the rattleters here with me, ah ha ha ha!'

'Indeed, the escaping little sparrow—if it be possible, let us go ahead as the sparrow, step by step. Now, Sumikō-san, you too follow along behind me and advance forward, O Isokō-sama. Your face is also quite dark, you see.'

'Ah ha ha ha, a second generation of rattleters and sparrows has been made. Thinking upon this, it appears that in this world there is not a single thing that perishes and disappears. If this side keeps silent, then the other side makes a din again; if you press down one side, the other swells up. In the end, there is nothing to do but leave it to Kamunagara, why!'

He muttered to himself. Mankō and Harukō held their breath, and attempting to fall asleep suddenly, they manufactured their own snoeches and strove to soar high into the realm of dreams.

The wind gradually grew calm, and the voices of people in front of the shrine came clearly to be heard.

The two, Isokō and Sumikō, moved with tiptoeing steps like thieves peering into someone's house on a dark night, and when they gradually approached the back of the shrine and looked, the figures of Tamakuniwake's wife, Ikohime-sama, and Imakohime-sama appeared as if dimly floating in the moonlight. So Isokō said in a low voice,

'Oi, Sumikō-sama, as this Isokō surmised, it is just as expected. As expected, it really is as expected, eh! Isn't it a splendid thing? Isn't it a kind thing? Isn't it wonderful? Isn't it envious? Truly, doesn't it make you burn with jealousy? Eh, how unpleasant, in the end it makes me feel sick to my stomach. Now, Sumikō-san, this surely cannot end just like this as Sumi-kō, ne!'

'Do not speak so loudly. What will you do if you are heard? She is Sumikō-sama's master. Do not look down upon her too much as being unpleasant. I am glad. I feel a fond yearning, it is wonderful, I am humbled, it is awe-inspiring.'

'Even so, isn't Michikō's fellow devoid of tact? In the midst of the husband and wife, he puts on a face like a coroner's official and pushes himself forward. That fellow came down a little short, you see. Look at my Sensei, isn't he quite full of verve? Surely Tamakuniwake-sama and Ikohime-sama too, in the depths of their hearts... Ah, it was the master... was it my wife? How kindly you have come, I wanted to meet you, I wanted to see you—they ought to be drowning in tears of joy embracing and clinging to one another. Yet Michikō, that fool who blocks the loving path of husband and wife and lets no one pass, it would have been good if he had shown just a tiny bit of tact, wouldn't it!'

Before he knew it, a loud voice erupted. Thereupon Sumikō, now driven to desperation, raised his voice to his utmost strength and said,

'Michikō's fellow, there is a limit to not knowing the Way, why! Though the way of husband and wife is also exceptional, ne. Either way, he is a troubled article. Ah ha ha ha!'

He shook his shoulders and, driven to desperation, burst out laughing. Startled by this voice, Michikō hurriedly advanced behind the shrine and came, saying,

'Oi, Sumikō, do not be a fool. The master is ill, and the Okusama has come to comfort him. What is there funny about that? Isn't it rude to Sensei? Come, from now on you are going to apologize for the crime of your impoliteness. There is a limit to lacking tact, isn't there!'

'Henh, the one lacking tact is you. Why do you not go together with Imakohime-sama to Harukuniwake-sama's side? Even though a husband and wife have graciously been granted an interview, what is the meaning of this? Can a disciple be served with such ways? Now Isokō, isn't that so?'

『コレ、今子さま、俯むいてせせら笑ひを致しましたなア。其笑ひ声といひ、月にすかして見た笑顔と云ひ、中々以て意味深長、イヤもう此五三公も、貴女の笑顔には恍惚と致しましたよ。一瞥城を傾けるといふ千両目許で眺められた時の心持と云つたら、胸をさされる様で厶いました』

今子姫は顔に袖をあて、恥しげに俯向いてゐる。五三公は世間慣れのした磯端の団子石の様な円転滑脱なあばずれ者、宣伝使の伴となつて、ここ迄伴いて来たものの、物の機みにはチヨイチヨイと生地が現れる厄介至極な滑稽男である。五三公は右の手を額口のドマン中にチヨンと載せ、カイツクカイのカイカイ踊りをし乍ら、治国別の休らふ森蔭をさして、

『オイ道公、純公、粋を利かせよ』

と呼ばはり乍ら登つて行く。

『ハツハヽヽヽ、玉国別もおかげで余程頭痛が軽減して来たやうだ。目も何うやら、一方はハツキリとし、一方の方は薄明りが見える様だ。笑うといふことは実に霊肉の薬になるものだなア』

§ 4

'It is said that the laughter of joy opens the gate of Tengoku and the path of Michikō, you see.'

'Before long, Sensei's eyes will likely open as well.'

'Please laugh with all your might.'

'Laughter is a fine thing, but such hasty laughter cannot be conjured up, and since I am, after all, the unrefined Tamakuniwake, the raw material for laughter is currently declaring a shortage, you know.'

'Sensei, your body is overflowing with laughter.'

'Shall Michikō make you laugh for just a moment?'

So saying, he goes behind him and tries to tickle his armpits with his fingertips.

Tamakuniwake springs up and dashes away before Michikō's hand can touch him, already laughing.

Michikō tucks up his skirts, bends at the waist, deliberately assumes a crocodile-legged posture like a dancing stone turtle, and gives chase to Tamakuniwake.

Tamakuniwake and everyone else involuntarily burst into a roar of laughter, holding their bellies.

Tamakuniwake, too, chased by Michikō, runs around the shrine over and over again while laughing.

原文（近代文語）

『歓喜の笑声は天国の門平安の道公を開くと云ひますからなア。軈て先生の目も開くでせう。どうぞ力一杯笑つて下さいませ』

『笑ひは結構だが、さう俄作りの笑ひも出来ず、何分無粋の玉国別のことだから、笑ひの原料が欠乏を告げてゐるのだからなア』

『先生、貴方の御身体には笑ひが充満して居りますよ。一寸道公が笑はして見せませうか』

と云ひ乍ら後へまはり脇の下を指の先でくすぐらうとする。玉国別は道公の手が障らぬ内にツと立ちて逃出し、早くも笑つてゐる。道公は尻を捲り、腰を屈め、ワザと鰐足になつて石亀の踊つたやうなスタイルで玉国別を追つかける。玉国別始め一同は思はずドツと吹出し、腹を抱へて笑ふ。玉国別も道公に追はれて、何回となく祠の周を笑ひ乍ら逃廻つてゐる。
