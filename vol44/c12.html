Chapter 12: Great Joy [1181]

大歓喜〔1181〕

§ 1

At the kototama of Harukuniwake, everyone was startled and woke up, and Mankō, rubbing his eyes,

Mankō: 'Sensei, you suddenly projected a kototama, but has something unusual appeared?'

Harukuniwake: 'Mm.'

Mankō: 'Hey, Harukō, isn't Lady Kaede's figure missing? She hasn't been swallowed by the great serpent, has she? Hey, Isokō, Tatsukō, what are you dawdling for? Come on, let's search, let's search!'

He panics. Isokō, Tatsukō, and Matsuhiko also roll their eyes around while looking all about the surroundings, startled by the absence of the two figures.

Harukuniwake: 'Pardon the trouble, but all four of you, go outside the forest and carry the kago here.'

Mankō: 'To carry kago, you say such a strange thing again, ah.'

Harukuniwake: 'You will understand once you go. Harukō and Princess Kaede are waiting. Come on, all four of you, go quickly, go quickly.'

Mankō: 'Hey, come what may, it is the sensei's gomeirei. Let's go and see.'

The three say, 'Understood with a yo-yo,' follow behind Mankō, and run out toward the outside of the forest.

Behind them, Harukuniwake clasps his hands together, and speaking to himself,

Harukuniwake: 'Ah, how arigata, the divine ohikiaha; it seems the reunion of parent and child, brother and sister, has somehow been permitted. Since last night, I was lost in thought and could not sleep, thinking that from now on I must put my shoulder to the wheel, but how wonderfully the Kamisama has arranged things. This must be thanks to the divine power of singing the kototama propagation song last night. The sacred word that the Way is with the Gods and all things are created by it is felt now more than ever as truly arigata; ah, how great is the divine power of the Gods and the efficacy of the kototama!'

While exclaiming this in admiration, he turned toward the east, recited the Amatsu Norito, chanted the heavenly counting song, and reverently proclaimed even the kamigoto to the end. Thereupon, carrying two kago, the party of six returned.

Harukuniwake:

Harukuniwake: 'Yaa, congratulations. Harukō-sama, Kaede-sama, the divine virtue of the Kamisama is a grand thing, ah.'

Mankō: 'Sensei, thanks to you, Harukō was able to have a ta-ta-reunion with his parents.'

Saying this, he already has a clouded voice. Kaede clasps her lovely hands like maple leaves, turns toward Harukuniwake, and uncertainly, amidst weeping, repeats Kannagara Tamashii Sakiwaba Se over and over again.

Mankō: 'Sensei, oh my, there is simply no way around it, wai. It is a grand thing, it is a big thing, eeh, there is no such splendid thing as this anywhere. I am truly happy, I do not know what kind of greeting to offer, and words do not come to Mankō right away, wa.'

Harukuniwake: 'Yaa, splendid. Mankō, come on, quickly bring the two out here.'

Mankō: 'Mankō-mankō-mankō accepts. Look here, Harukō-sama, Kaede-sama, what are you flustered about? Won't you help too, kora Isokō, Matsuhiko, Tatsu, what are you dawdling for? In this case of one thousand riders against one, this is no time to be sitting idly. Come on, it is a reunion, it is a reunion, it is the kototama, it is the kototama, it is the land where the kototama prospers!'

Mankō spins around the periphery of the kago countless times, like one making the hundred-fold shrine pilgrimage. The elderly couple calmly stand up from the kago, press their hands together before Harukuniwake,

Elderly Man: 'The living Kamisama of Izusan, thank you very much. I am one called Tarehiko.'

Elderly Woman: 'I am his wife, Shizuko. My gratitude is thus.'

Joining their hands together, they shed tears of joy like a waterfall. Both Harukō and Kaede are dazed, speechless from excessive joy, and only watch their parents' faces from the side.

Mankō: 'How grand this is, eeh. Truly, sincerely jumping for joy with dancing hands and knowing not where one's feet tread—this is what is meant. Some dance around in circles from sheer happiness, while others dance around in circles from fear, and such can happen; do trust in the divine, trusting in the divine becomes a staff in times of unexpected trouble... such must be the sacred word, ah, the complete and utter satisfaction of Mankō.'

Everyone, shall we not?

Ah, arigata, arigata,

Tears of gratitude are overflowing.

Yatsukodotskoi, dotskoishiyo,

Dotskoidotskoi, korewashiyo,

Yoyoiyoi no yoitosaa,

Dotskoidotskoi, dotskoishiyō

With these words, lost in ecstasy, he flies around the open square. Harukuniwake speaks in a calm voice,

Uzuhiko-dono, you must have undergone terribly trying times. I sympathize with you deeply. Shizuko-sama must also have been exceedingly worried, I imagine.

Uzuhiko wipes away his tears and says,

Yes, thank you very much. Due to the great upheaval in Armenia, our family was separated in various directions, each going our own way. At last, I sought out the whereabouts of my daughter, and the three of us, hand in hand, set out to discover the whereabouts of my older brother Toshihiko. Enduring all manner of hardships and sufferings, we arrived at the banks of the Lion River, which flows at the foot of Mount Thames. Whether it was due to the fatigue of old age, mysteriously, both husband and wife lost the use of our limbs. While our two parents were being cared for by our single daughter, suffering unbearable hardships, Koganehime-sama happened to pass by along with a beautiful maiden. She graciously let us hear various wonderful stories, and thanks to her, our spirits became refreshed and our physical ailments gradually healed. As the three of us, parent and daughter, were living in a small thatched hut we built, a house by the riverbank, subordinates of General Lunch came by, heard the sounds of our husband and wife's norito chanting... saying, 'You are a spy of Ananai-kyō...' they forcibly bound us hand and foot, placed us on horses, and hauled us off to the encampment of General Lunch. It matters not what becomes of the two of us. Though our lives are of little worth, we were worried about our daughter and my older brother. Whether sleeping or waking, the two of us were held captive in the frost-chilled encampment, wringing out tears of bitter regret.

Saying this much, he burst out weeping bitterly in a loud wail.

Harukuniwake looked dismayed and consoled him, saying:

'You have undergone terribly severe hardships with your elderly body. However, please set your mind at ease now. As long as we are here, you are quite safe.'

Uzuhiko simply said 'Hai,' and wept convulsively once more.

Shizuko, again wiping her tears, spoke:

'Though the mere telling of it is a seed of tears, my husband and I were dragged off to General Ranch's camp, interrogated from morning till night by demon-like guards about things we knew nothing of, whipped regardless of our bodies, and suffered truly bitterly. And from time to time we were brought out before General Ranch... "You must be Mokusuke of Ananai-kyō. You must be Kurohime; confess. And the girl living with you is undoubtedly Hatsuwakahime. Come, where did you hide her, reveal her whereabouts..." Thus subjected to severe torture, I had resigned myself that my life was utterly at an end. Though I did not care about this dying body of mine, I thought that if I could not somehow manage to reunite with my two children, I could not die even if I died. Thinking it would not do to tell lies, yet as they kept questioning, my husband answered that he was Mokusuke..., and I blurted out that I was unmistakably Kurohime, and that our daughter was without a doubt Hatsuwakahime... and so I carried it through. Upon doing that, the interrogation became increasingly stern; we were questioned about various unimaginable things, such as how many missionaries of Ananai-kyō had set out toward the capital of Haruna, and when in the agony of suffering I gave random answers off the top of my head, saying they should send us to the Saiin Mansion, they tied our hands behind our backs, threw us into mountain palanquins, had their servants carry us, and brought us this far. My husband and I's hearts ached wondering what would become of us, but we have met with your unexpected help, my lord, and on top of that have been allowed to meet our two children we had so yearned for—there is nothing in heaven or earth as joyous as this. You are our living deity and parent of our lives!'

With this, she pressed her hands together once more, weeping and prostrating herself with a loud wail.

Harukō drew close to Uzuhiko's side and said:

'Father-sama, it has been a long time. I am so glad to see you are alive. I am Toshikō; in my youth I caused you all sorts of worry, but as I listened to the teachings of Ananai-kyō, I remembered my parents' benevolence, and morning and evening there was not a moment I did not pray that I might somehow be allowed to meet my parents again.'

Saying this, he wrung out his tears once more. Sniffling, Uzuhiko stretched out his wrinkled hand, stroked Harukō's head all around, and said:

'Ah, Toshikō, you have spoken well. Hearing those words, even if I were to be transferred to another province right now, nothing would remain for me in this world. Ah, how thankful I am. The children one should have are one's own offspring. Now, Toshikō, rest assured; although I am aged, my body is vigorous, so nothing will happen to me for the next two or three years.'

'Hai, thank you very much; from now on I will strive to my utmost in filial piety. Please forgive my sins up to now.'

Even these words were mingled with tears.

Kaede firmly grasped Shizuko's hand and said:

'Mother-sama, you must have been terribly troubled. I do not know how much I wept. Pilgriming to the Saiin Mansion of Mount Ubusuna to find out the whereabouts of my parents, I disguised myself and went as far as the mountain pass of Kajika Pass. When I heard from the talk of passersby that the army of the Brahman religion was blocking the valley paths, I thought, "Ah, it cannot be helped; now there is nothing left for me to do but pray for my parents' safety and the destruction of our foes." Hearing that deep within this fearsome demon forest there was a serpent's cavern, I thought that if I hid here, the Brahman pursuers would rarely come looking; so I concealed myself in the terrifying cavern and performed twenty-one days of night prayers disguised as a demon. Seeing that my heartfelt wish had been granted, at the end of the twenty-one days I met with Elder Brother, and furthermore was allowed to meet Father-sama and Mother-sama. Please be fully at ease; now that you have been held in the bosom of such a great missionary, you are already quite safe.'

Consoling her with tears, Shizuko bit into Kaede's back, drowning in tears of joy.

From this time onward, by the command of Harukuniwake, Uzuhiko, Shizuko, Kaede, and Harukō—these four people—were made to carry a letter to the forest of the shrine where Tamakuniwake was secluded, and the missionary entourage escorted them as far as the mountain pass. The four family members had an interview with Tamakuniwake, assisted in the construction of the shrine, the couple eventually became attendants at the palace, Kaede became a lady-in-waiting to Itokohime, and after the completion of the shrine they returned to the Saiin Mansion, studied the teachings of the God, and ultimately became splendid missionaries, living as those who repaid the benevolence of the God.

『それは御老体の身を以て、エライ御艱難をなさいましたな。併し乍ら最早御安心をなさいませ。吾々のついてゐる限りは最早大丈夫ですから』

珍彦は「ハイ」と云つたきり、又もや泣きじやくる。静子は又もや涙片手に、

『お話申すも涙の種乍ら、ランチ将軍の陣営へ夫婦は連れ行かれ、鬼のやうな番卒に朝から晩迄、身に覚えもないことを詰問され、身体所構はず鞭たれ、実に苦しう厶いました。そしてランチ将軍の前に時々引出され……其方は三五教の杢助であらう。汝は黒姫であらう、白状致せ。そして其方の同居してゐた娘は初稚姫に違ひなからう。サアどこへ隠した、所在を知らせ……とエライ拷問、到底命はなきものと覚悟致して居りましたが、死ぬる此身は厭はねど、どうぞして吾子二人に廻り合はねば死ぬにも死ねないと思ひまして、嘘を言つては済まないと存じ乍ら、向うの尋ぬるままに、夫は杢助で厶いました、……と答へ、私はまがふ方なき黒姫だ、そして娘は初稚姫に相違厶いませぬ……と言つてのけました。そした所がますます詮議が厳しくなり、三五教の宣伝使はハルナの都へ向つて、何人ばかり出張したかとか、いろいろと存じもよらぬことを詰問され、苦しさまぎれに口から出任せの返答を致しました所、斎苑の館へ送つてやらうと云つて、吾々夫婦を後手に縛り山駕籠に投込み、家来に舁がせてここ迄つれて来ました。吾々夫婦はどうなることかと胸を痛めて居りましたが、思ひもよらぬ貴方様のお助けに預かり、其上焦れ慕うた二人の子に会はして貰ひ、斯様な嬉しいことは、天にも地にも厶りませぬ。命の親の活神様』

と又もや手を合はしてワツとばかりに泣伏しにける。

晴公は珍彦の側に寄り、

『父上様、お久しう厶います。よくマア生きてゐて下さいました。私は俊彦で厶います、若い時はいろいろと御心配をかけましたが、三五教の教を聞くにつけて、親の御恩を思ひ出し、何卒両親に会はして下さいませと、朝夕祈らぬ間とては厶いませなかつたので厶ります』

と又もや涙を絞る。珍彦は鼻を啜り乍ら、皺手を伸ばして、晴公の頭を撫でまはし、

『あゝ俊彦、よう言うてくれた。其言葉を聞く以上は此儘国替をしても、此世に残ることはない。あゝ有難い。持つべきものは吾子だ。コレ俊彦、安心して呉れ、私は年はよつてゐても体は達者だから、ここ二年や三年にどうかうはあるまいから』

『ハイ有難う厶います、これから力限り孝行を励みます。今迄の罪は許して下さいませ』

といふ言葉さへも涙交りである。楓は静子の手をシツカと握り、

『お母アさま、随分お困りでしたらうねえ。私、どれ丈泣いたか知れませぬよ。ウブスナ山の斎苑館へ参拝して、御両親の所在を知らして貰はうと、身をやつして、河鹿峠の山口迄参りました所、道行く人の話に聞けば、バラモン教の軍勢が谷道を扼してゐるといふことを聞きましたので、あゝ是非がない、モウ此上は両親の無事を祈り、かたきの滅亡を祈るより、私としての尽すべき途はないと思ひ、此恐ろしい魔の森の奥に大蛇の岩窟のあることを聞き、ここに忍びて居ればバラモンの捕手も滅多に尋ねては来まいと思ひ、恐ろしい岩窟に身を忍び、三七廿一日の夜参りを、鬼に化けて致して居りました。心願が通つたと見えて、三七日の上りに兄さまに巡り会ひ、又お父さまお母アさまに会はして頂きました。どうぞ御安心下さいませ、斯様な偉い宣伝使様の懐に抱かれた以上は最早大丈夫で厶います』

と涙交りに慰める。静子は楓の背に喰ひつき、嬉し涙にかきくれる。これより治国別の命に依つて、珍彦、静子、楓、晴公の四人を玉国別のこもつてゐる祠の森へ手紙を持たせてやることとした。そして山口迄宣伝使一行は送り届けた。親子四人は玉国別に面会し、神殿造営の手伝ひをなし、夫婦は遂に宮のお給仕役となり、楓は五十子姫の侍女となつて、神殿落成の後斎苑館に帰り、神の教を研究し、遂には立派なる宣伝使となつて神の御恩に報ずる身とはなりにける。
