Chapter 12: Bruised and Battered in the Fray (Shōtō Rangaku) [12]

焦頭爛額〔12〕

§ 1

Around 3:00 a.m. on the following fifth day, having returned from Hōten with the machinery, we immediately set about the repair of the automobiles. As the damage was severe, the repairs could not be completed easily. Hideo and Okazaki, while having Ōjiyudau drive one automobile, arrived safely at the residence of Ōshiyōshin in Daishikashi, north of Shiyōto, at the twenty-shiri mark by 7:30 a.m., left the automobiles deep within the gate, and waited for about an hour for the automobile of Moritaka and the others. As they were quite late, they unloaded all the luggage from the automobiles, and Ōjiyudau took one driver and returned to the cheap hotel at Shiyōto No. 3. Just as the repair of the severely damaged automobile was completely finished and the luggage had been loaded, a member of the Japanese consulate arrived for an inspection, bringing two or three policemen with him. Then, together with the automobile of Ōjiyudau, they entered the gate of the No. 3 shop. While the Japanese official proceeded directly into the No. 3 shop and was conducting an investigation, Ōjiyudau accelerated to full speed, drove the automobiles out, and headed straight for the residence in Daishikashi where Hideo was waiting, arriving with both vehicles.

The residence of Ōshiyōshin was home to a large family of over ten people, and they possessed an air of high refinement. The family here, declaring that the savior of Japan had arrived, welcomed them warmly and treated them to a feast of boiled kaoliang, Chinese rice porridge, and chicken eggs. Everyone rejoiced in their mutual safety and bade farewell to the household; however, after traveling about ten shiri across the vast, desolate wilderness, they encountered a troop of about two hundred Chinese soldiers. Ōjiyudau, ignoring everything, aimed for the line of soldiers and drove on at full speed. When they had been driving for almost thirty minutes, the machinery suffered damage once again, and they spent about an hour engaged in repairs.

Proceeding along difficult paths, aiming for the northwestern sky without regard for hills, fields, or rivers, their bold and fearless actions caused travelers and horses to be startled and flee in all directions. Herds of horses pulling large carts were frightened and tumbled into ditches. The sight of oxcarts and carriages coming one after another, piling up and overturning, was truly both comical and pitiful. With the swaying of the vehicles, Hideo and his companions struck their heads and hips time and again; at times, the rear automobile collided with the front one, and the glass of the vehicle body shattered, leaving Moritaka bathed in a rain of fragments, suffering injuries around his eyes and bleeding profusely. Thus, it was around 3:30 p.m. when they finally arrived safely at Old Shipeijie. As the automobiles had suffered major damage again and could no longer move, they had no choice but to hire two horse-drawn carts, and it was around 5:30 p.m. when they arrived safely at the residence of the merchant Mikizō Okumura in New Shipeijie, while being exposed to the cold wind.

At this residence, Hideo and his companions were treated to Japanese food for the first time in a long while, and they enjoyed themselves while gathered around a pot of chicken. Since eating breakfast in Kaiyuan, Hideo's party and the four drivers had not eaten properly, day or night, yet their spirits remained increasingly high. They stayed the night at the Okumura residence, and it was decided that they would head toward Zhengjiatun from Shipeijie Station under the guidance of Shintarō Hirama, the chairman of the Japanese residents' association in Taonanfu. At this house, they bathed in a Japanese bath for the first time in a long while, washed away their sweat and grime, and became passengers on the train early the next morning from Shipeijie. The weather that day was extremely warm, so they took off their Chinese-style jackets one by one, opened the windows, and proceeded while exposed to the wind of the vast, boundless wilderness. At 6:50 p.m., they alighted at Zhengjiatun Station and it was arranged for them to stay one night at the residence of Kumano Yamamoto. The four men—Okazaki, Hirama, Moritaka, and Ō Genki—held a drinking party at a Japanese restaurant called Azumaya, became quite intoxicated, and with the help of a plump waitress and a geisha who facilitated the flow of cups and dishes, they greatly displayed their heroic spirit; however, the next morning, they entered the Yamamoto residence where Hideo was staying and immediately rushed to the station. It was exactly 6:30 a.m.

原文（近代文語）

翌五日の午前三時頃奉天から機械を持つて帰つて来たので、直様自動車の修繕に着手した。何分破損が甚だしいので容易に修繕が出来なかつた。日出雄と岡崎とは七時半一台の自動車を王樹棠に操縦させ乍ら、二十支里の地点なる昌図の北、大四家子の王昌紳の宅に安着し、自動車を門内深く入れおき、守高等の自動車を一時間許り待つてゐた。あまり遅いので自動車の荷物を全部下し、王樹棠は一名の運転手をつれて再び昌図三号の木賃ホテルに引返した。恰度大破損した自動車の修繕を全く終り、荷物を積み込んだ所へ日本領事館員が警官を二三名引連れて臨検に来た。そして王樹棠の自動車と共に三号店の門へ這入つた。日本官吏は直ちに三号店内に進み入り、調査して居る間に、王樹棠は全速力を出して自動車を駆け出し、日出雄が待つてゐる大四家子の館を指して驀地に二台ともやつて来た。

王昌紳の宅は十数人の大家族で何処となく気品の高い風貌をしてゐた。此処の家族は日本の救世主来れりと云つて、大いに歓待し、高粱や支那米のお粥や鶏卵等を煮て饗応した。一同は互に無事を喜び当家に別れを告げ、茫漠たる大荒原を十支里許り疾走すると、二百名許りの支那兵の一隊に出会した。王樹棠は一切構はず兵隊の列を目あてに、一目散に駆り行く。殆んど三十分許り駆け出した時に、又もや機械の損傷を来し、一時間許り時間を費して修繕に取掛かつた。

丘陵や畑や川の区別なく、西北の空を目あてに難路を進み行く豪胆不敵の行動に、旅行く人馬が驚いて右往左往に逃げ廻る。大車を曳いてゐる馬の群は驚いて溝の中に顛倒する。其中に次から次へ来る牛車や馬車が折重なつて顛倒する有様は、実に可笑しくもあれば気の毒でもある。車の動揺につれて日出雄等は幾度となく頭を打ち臀を打ち、時々後の自動車が、前の自動車に衝突したり、車体のガラスが破壊して守高は破片の雨を全面に浴び、眼辺に負傷し、ダラダラと血を流してゐる。かくして漸く旧四平街に安着したのは午後三時半頃であつた。自動車が再び大破損を為し、最早や動く事が出来ぬので、止むを得ず荷馬車二台を雇ひ寒風に曝され乍ら新四平街の貿易商奥村幹造氏の宅に安着したのは午後五時三十分頃であつた。

此処の宅で日出雄等は久し振りで日本食を饗せられ、鶏肉の鍋を囲んで舌鼓を打つた。途中開原で朝飯を食つたきり今日まで日出雄一行も車掌四人も昼夜の区別なく、碌に飯も食はなかつたが元気は益々旺盛であつた。当夜は奥村方に一泊し洮南府の日本居留民会長平馬慎太郎氏の案内で、四平街駅から鄭家屯に向ふ事と決つた。当家にて久々にて日本風呂に入浴し、汗や垢を落し翌早朝四平街より列車の客となつた。此日は非常に陽気暖かく支那服の上着を一枚二枚と次々に脱ぎ、窓を開けて茫々たる大原野の風に当りつつ進んで行く。午後六時五十分鄭家屯駅下車、山本熊之氏方に一泊する事となつた。岡崎、平馬、守高、王元祺の四人は、日本の東屋と云ふ料理店に於て、牛飲馬食会を開き大変なメートルを上げ、お多福仲居や、豚芸者が盃盤の間を斡旋し、大いに豪傑振りを発揮したが、翌早朝日出雄が泊つて居る山本方に入り来り直ちに停車場へと駆けつけた。正に午前六時三十分である。

§ 2

To the northwest of Gakotoneki station, the Tsuchimanjiugata Hōrizan stands in solemn composure, breaking the silence of the vast Daigenya. It is a mountain that looks as if a manjiu has been placed upside down, and it is said to be one of the Mōkoshichizan. This Taunantetsudau had its first trial run last Ichiguwatsu, and the tetsuro had only just settled; in particular, the kishiya consisted entirely of old Mantetsu equipment, which caused damage to the kikuwan along the way, leading to a tachiōjiyō of about shichijikan just before Morineki station. Okazaki, engaging four Shina shiyōkau in conversation, spoke with such vigor that he displayed the style of Cho Hakkai following Sanzauhofushi, wrapping Hideo in a cloud of smoke. Even the famous Mōko yellow dust seemed as though it would flee barefoot before the force of Okazaki's breath.

Suspicious of the kishiya's mid-journey stop, several dozen Mōko people of all ages gathered around, looking on with curiosity. They were puffing away at tobacco with long pipes, looking quite carefree. Seeing this, Hideo sank into meditation, tracing history back and thinking that the Japanese age of the gods must have been just as carefree. Hideo composed several dozen waka while in the carriage. A portion of them is recorded below.

I advance through the boundless great wilderness, how my soul does soar.

Heaven and earth become one, the Kunitsukami spreads a mat upon the wide field and awaits me.

At last I have passed through the Ataka barrier, and now I run through the wilderness of Mōko.

I feel as if I have already grasped the land of Mōko, and my spirit pierces the heavens.

The wind is clear, the sun is serene, the withered field takes on the look of spring, and the heat haze burns.

If it be possible, I shall summon my brethren and save them in a new paradise.

After five and five days have passed, at last I have entered the land of treasure.

In the midst of the boundless wilderness, Mōko dwellings are seen flickering here and there.

The vast sky, without a single cloud, is seen descending upon the horizon.

The Mōko people, who can see neither trees nor grass, must surely see the moon, stars, and flowers of the sky.

The sun shines upon the frozen snow, and the great wilderness looks like a great ocean.

I suspect it is only because the sea tide is shining that the snow on the great wilderness reflects the sun.

In the midst of the boundless great wilderness, dwellings like earthen chambers stand here and there, and when viewed from the train window, it is as if boats are floating on a great ocean of land. Casting my eyes far into the distance, the standing yōryū trees break the monotony of the Daigenya, forming a dark, dense forest. At Taiheigawa station, the kishiya stopped once again, and about an hour or more was wasted in a messy fashion with water supply and the like. Okazaki began to roar, spitting foam from the corners of his mouth:

Okazaki: 'Hey, this piece-of-junk kishiya is just like a slug heading to Edo.'

Hideo, while laughing, said:

Hideo: 'Okazaki-san, if the kishiya will not move, there is nothing to be done, so why not be clever and set out on foot, wait for the kishiya at the next station, and transfer? You would likely reach Taunan station that much sooner. Ahahaha.'

He spoke such foolish words. A Shina merchant looked at Okazaki and asked with a look of wonder:

Merchant: 'Where are you going, and for what purpose are you traveling?'

Okazaki: 'Don't talk nonsense! Do you think someone with no purpose would ride a kishiya and travel? If you meddle in things that don't concern you, I'll slap you! I am not a merchant like you; I am a high-ranking official of the Three Eastern Provinces with a gold-leafed status.'

He scolded him with tremendous force.

Hideo: 'The Japanese are like this in everything toward the Shina people, so no matter how much we call for Japan-Shina friendship, it is useless.'

Hideo muttered to himself.

The arrival time at Taunan is 4:20 PM. However, the kishiya is still stuck at Taiheigawa station.

Moritaka: 'Masumiwake and his party are probably waiting for us like fools at the cold station. I am planning to see the crescent moon for the first time upon arriving at Taunan, so I am casting a little spell to stop the kishiya. Ahahaha.'

It was Moritaka who was saying such foolish things.

The interpreter, Ō Genki, is lacking his usual energy from somewhere. He lies down with a pale face, scratching and stroking his nose while groaning loudly, 'Un, un.' After a while, he opens his eyes wide again, looking out the window with a dissatisfied expression. This was because his chronic orchitis had recurred.

原文（近代文語）

臥虎屯駅の西北の方に土饅頭形の宝裏山が、大原野の寂寞を破つて端然として立つて居る。饅頭を伏せた様な山で蒙古七山の一なりと云ふ事である。此洮南鉄道は去る一月初めて試運転を行ひ漸く鉄路が固まつた所で、殊にその汽車は満鉄の古物許りで途中機関に損傷を来たし、茂林駅の手前で七時間許りも立往生をした。岡崎は支那の将校四人を相手に談論風発盛んにメートルを挙げ、三蔵法師について行つた猪八戒式を発揮し、日出雄を煙に巻いた。蒙古名物の黄塵万丈も岡崎の鼻息には跣足で逃げ出しさうであつた。

汽車の途中停車を怪しんで、附近の村落より蒙古人の老若男女が数十人許り物珍しさうに集まつて来た。そして呑気さうに長い煙管で煙草をパクついて居た。之を見ても日本の神代は斯くの如く呑気であつたらう等と、歴史を遡つて日出雄は冥想に耽つた。日出雄は車中に於て数十首の和歌を詠じた。その内の一部を左に録する。

際しなき大野ケ原を進み行く吾魂の勇みけるかな

天も地も一つになりて国津神広野の蓆敷きて吾待つ

漸くに安宅の関をくぐり抜け今は蒙古の広野を走る

早や已に蒙古の国を握りたる如き心地し意気天を衝く

風清く日はうららかに枯野原春めき立ちて陽炎燃ゆる

事ならば我同胞を招き寄せ新楽園に救ひ助けむ

五五と云ふ日数重ねて漸くに宝の国に入りし吾かな

際限も知らぬ原野の真中に蒙古の人家チラチラ見ゆる

一点の曇りさへなき大空は地平線上に下りて見ゆる

木も草も見る事を得ぬ蒙古人は空の月星花と見るらむ

積む雪の凍れる上を日の照りて大野ケ原も大海と見ゆ

海の潮光ると許り疑はる大野ケ原の雪に日は照り

際限もなき大荒原の中に土室の如き人家がポツリポツリと建並び、車窓より眺れば陸の大洋に舟の浮んだ様である。遠く眼を放てば楊柳の立木が大原野の単調を破つて、コンモリと黒ずんだ森をなしてゐる。大平川駅にて又もや汽車は停車し、給水やなんかでゴテゴテと約一時間余を費した。岡崎は、

『エー、此ボロ汽車奴、まるで蛞蝓の江戸行見たやうだ』

と口角に泡を飛ばして怒り出した。日出雄は笑ひ乍ら、

『岡崎さん、汽車が動かなけりや仕方がないから気を利かして徒歩と出掛け、次の駅で汽車を待つて乗り換へたら、それ丈け早く洮南駅に着くだらう。アハヽヽヽ』

と馬鹿口をたたく。支那の商人が岡崎を見て、

『貴下は何処まで行かるるか、何の用があつて旅行されるか』

と不思議さうに問ふ。

岡崎『馬鹿を云ふな、用のない者が汽車に乗つて旅をするか、余計な世話を焼くと張りとばすぞ、貴様のやうな俺は商人ではないぞ、金箔付の東三省の高等官だ』

とエライ馬力で叱り飛ばす。

『日本人は支那人に対し、凡てがこんな調子だから何程日支親善を叫んでも駄目だなア』

と日出雄は独語した。

洮南着の時間は午後四時二十分である。然るに汽車はまだ大平川駅に焦げついてゐる。

『真澄別一行は寒い停車場に自分等を阿呆待ちしてゐるだらう。僕は洮南着で初めて三日月を見る積りだから、一寸まじないをして汽車を止めてゐるのだ、アハヽヽヽ』

と阿呆口を云つてゐるのは守高であつた。

通訳の王元祺は何処ともなく元気がない。青白い顔して横になり、鼻を掻き撫でてはウンウンと大声に唸り、暫くしては又キヨロリと目を開け、窓外を不足相な顔をして眺めてゐる。持病の睾丸炎が再発したからであつた。

§ 3

The Taiyō approaches the Chiheisenjiyō, yet Taunan remains far away.

As we ride the sluggish Kishiya racing across the Kuōgen, pearls of Akubi come in Renpatsu.

Kuwaiko on the Shiyajiyō:

Kishiyahahakuwaisushiyōtogai Kokukokuwagatainisemaru Jiyunkeiheishinichikeikuwan Kanōkagahiteitsukaukifunitonkuwaisu

Incidentally, the party of Masumiwake boarded the train departing Hōteneki at eleven-ten in the evening on Sanguwatsu Mitsuka, bound for Chiyōshiyun, arriving at Shiheigai at five-thirty in the morning on Yotsuka, where they partook of Chōsan at the Uehanriyokuwan, departed Shiheigai at eight-thirty that same morning, arrived at Teikatun before noon, and put up at a hotel. Then, on Sanguwatsu Itsuka, they headed for Taunan on the six-thirty morning train. Shortly after departing Sanrin Station midway, the locomotive suffered a breakdown, and the train came to a standstill in the middle of the wilderness. The staff ran about in all directions, eventually pulling a rescue locomotive from Teikatun, and it finally began to move. At that time, it was four in the afternoon; the train proceeded slowly amidst strict vigilance against the attacks of mounted bandits along the route, and arrived at Taunan Station twenty minutes past midnight. The party was met by a boy from the Taunan Ryokan, split into two Chinese carriages, and escorted by soldiers with fixed bayonets, passed through a specially opened city gate to lodge at the Taunan Ryokan.

車上の懐古

汽車破壊昌図街 危険刻々我隊迫 巡警兵士日警官 窺間一行急遁晦

因に真澄別一行は、三月三日午後十一時十分奉天駅発長春行列車に搭乗し、四日午前五時半四平街着、植半旅館にて朝餐を喫し、同日午前八時半四平街発、正午前鄭家屯着、ホテルに投宿した。そして三月五日午前六時半発列車にて洮南に向つた。中途三林駅を発し間もなく、機関車に故障を起し、列車は荒野の真中に立往生した。係員は東奔西走して遂に鄭家屯より救援機関車を引張つて来て漸く進行し始めた。時に午後四時、沿道の馬賊の襲来に対する警戒物々しき中を列車は遅々として運転し、夜十二時を過ぐる二十分洮南駅に到着した。一行は洮南旅館のボーイに迎へられ支那馬車二台に分乗し、銃剣をつけたる兵隊に護られつつ特に開かれたる城門を潜つて洮南旅館に投じた。
