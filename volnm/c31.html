Chapter 31: The Forced March (Kyōkōgun) [31]

強行軍〔31〕

§ 1

Just as the vicinity of the Gankutsu began to brighten with the dawn, it was Hagihara who came galloping on horseback.

Hagihara: 'I thought of turning back immediately upon receiving the notice from Masumi-san last night, but for some reason, the path was pitch black and my horse would not advance even an inch, so I have only just arrived. Since there were four or five households last night, it was rather crowded and chaotic, so it was actually more convenient that you did not come.'

Sakamoto: 'As expected, the Kamisama can see the future.'

Hagihara: 'Okazaki-san was extremely indignant and was venting his anger at Rosenkuwai. Furthermore, Nadahiko-san was troubled by illness and felt quite helpless, but it seemed he was slowly walking and turning back.'

Hideo: 'That is pitiful. Hey, Pairinku, take the horses and go meet Nadahiko-san.'

Pairinku immediately mounted his steed, led Moritaka's horse as a mount for Nadahiko, and rode off. In exchange, five or six horses came galloping in a line; it was Rosenkuwai and his adjutants. Rosen immediately went to Hideo's side, bowed his head, and explained something. Unfortunately, there was no one present who could interpret his Chinese, which was spoken with a Shanxi accent, but the meaning seemed to be: 'I hurried to select a suitable place for camping, so I am truly sorry for having left without permission. Since the army must be reorganized and it is crowded, I ask you to understand my intentions and proceed with me.'

Hideo left only a single word:

'Your efforts are appreciated.'

He then accompanied Masumiwake and Moritaka to the top of the rocky mountain, performed a joint recitation of Norito toward the eastern sky, had Hagihara take a commemorative photograph, and leisurely ate breakfast. When Rosen approached Hideo's side again and expressed his plea, Hideo nodded and mounted his horse.

Masumiwake: 'Sensei, are you going to proceed further? Shall we speak well and take a separate path?'

As he tried to stop him,

Hideo: 'Since Rosen is pleading so earnestly, let us go to where everyone is and decide on the matter then.'

He hurried the departure. Nadahiko was to ride in a sedan chair with Yamada and proceed to the headquarters station.

Masumiwake: 'Tsk, are we being led by Rosen-shi like a pilgrimage to Zenkoji?'

He muttered this while watching Hideo, urged on by Rosen, kick up dust and hasten his horse. He rode side by side with Chief Medical Officer Ino, who had come to meet them halfway, and they proceeded slowly while talking about something.

Ino: 'Ni-sensei, is it not worrisome for the future to rely on Rosenkuwai? Okazaki-san said that the current situation is too dangerous to bear, so he left a while ago, riding in the same sedan chair as the Bao commander, saying he would devise some method.'

Masumiwake: 'In any case, since the first command from the Kamisama was given to Rosenkuwai, was it not the activity of Rosenkuwai that allowed us to enter Mongolia safely?'

Ino: 'The soldiers seem to be decreasing gradually since last night... Rosen's orders have no authority at all. I cannot help but feel uneasy when a unit of this size cannot be unified. I think it would have been better to utilize Zhao Ti and Zhao Jie, whom Okazaki-san had originally planned to use to prepare a residence for Hideo-sensei in Fengtian, but what do you think? Okazaki-san was also saying that repeatedly.'

Masumiwake: 'There is a great difference between the thoughts of the Kamisama and the thoughts of humans; in reality, humans do not even have the qualifications to judge good and evil, or right and wrong. In short, Rosenkuwai has his mission as Rosenkuwai, and Liu Shengshan has his mission as Liu Shengshan, and they are participating in the campaign, so the truth will not be known until the very end. Well, we shall go as far as we can go.'

Ino: 'It is truly as if Liu is the one commanding Rosen. Liu's unit has the largest number of people and is well-equipped with weapons. I feel a sense of danger, so I feel like returning to Taonan once; what do you think?'

と出発を急ぐ。名田彦は山田と共に轎車に便乗し、司令部駐屯所迄進む事となつた。

と呟き乍ら、日出雄が盧に促され砂煙りを立てて馬を急がすのを見送つた。途中まで出迎へに来た猪野軍医長と轡を並べ、何事か語り合ひつつボツボツ進み行く。

猪野『二先生、盧占魁を力にして居ては前途心細い事はありますまいか。岡崎さんは、現状では危くて仕方がないから、何とか方法を講じて来ると云つて、包団長の轎車に同乗して先程出発しましたよ』

真澄別『兎に角神様からの第一命令が盧占魁に下つたのだから、安全に入蒙出来たのは盧占魁の活動ぢやないか』

猪野『昨晩から段々兵隊も減る様だ……盧の命令は少しも権威がありませぬ。これ位な部隊の統一が出来ない様では不安で堪りませぬ。ヤハリ最初岡崎さんの計画で奉天へ日出雄先生のお住居まで用意して居つたと云ふ趙倜や趙傑をお利用になつた方が良かつたらうと思ひますが、何うでせう。岡崎さんも切りにさう言つて居られましたよ』

真澄別『神様の思ひと人間の想ひとは大変な相違のあるもので、実際人間には善悪正邪を批判する資格もないのだから、要するに盧占魁は盧占魁としての使命があり、劉陞山には劉陞山としての使命があつて従軍してゐるのだから、最後迄行かなきや其真相は分るものでないよ。マア行く所まで行くのさ』

猪野『全く劉が却つて盧に命令する様な傾向ですよ。劉の隊は人数も一番多いし武器も揃ふてますからなア。私は何だか危険味を感ずるので、一度洮南へ帰つてみたい様な気が致しますが如何でせう』

§ 2

Masumiwake: 'You must ask Daisensei and decide that. As for me, I cannot recommend one way or the other. I believe Daisensei himself is Kami, so even if Daisensei says or does something that differs from my own thoughts, I know that the plot of each stage must be understood by Kami alone; therefore, I will follow Daisensei, obeying his command. Although I am reluctant to advance from the cave we were in just before, Daisensei is advancing together with Lu, so I will leave it to Kami and go.'

Ino: 'Is that how it is?'

He followed with a look of dissatisfaction on his face. The headquarters' garrison at this time was a private house in the northernmost part of Rehe, and the fact that the recent base of operations for the Taainzuru was in the vicinity of the aforementioned cave must also be a matter of some karmic connection. Now, the headquarters where Hinodeo's party arrived was in utter confusion due to the reorganization of troops, and Okazaki had already left for Fengtian with Hōkinzan to organize a support army. Here, the formation was renewed, and those whose horses and firearms were not in order were given travel expenses and sent on their way home. Lu's younger brother Lu Bingde, Natahiko, Yamada, Kobayashi Zenkichi, and two other Chinese men boarded two carriages that had come from Taonan, loaded their personal belongings that would hinder a forced march, and set out on the road to return to Fengtian, heading toward Taonan, which was said to be over four hundred Chinese li away. This party was later captured by Chinese authorities at Tuquan; Lu Bingde was shot to death in Taonan, and the three Japanese were handed over to the consulate.

In a room of a private house, Masumiwake, acting on Hinodeo's intentions, was exchanging written notes with Liu Shengshan. The meaning is as follows.

Masumiwake: 'Where is this unit supposed to go from here?'

Liu: 'They say we are going to winter in Suiyuan, where supplies are abundant, so we will likely head toward Chahar first. Regarding that, we must fight a battle with the troops of Kailu after traveling about three hundred Chinese li from here, so we have planned to reduce our burdens as much as possible here.'

Masumiwake: 'Do you intend to share the same fate as Commander Lu no matter where he goes?'

Liu: 'Generally speaking, I joined without knowing anything. After Li Jinglin, the First Division Commander of Fengtian, sent a letter to Brigade Commander Kan of Zhengjiatun, Lieutenant General Kan also said he would protect you, and told me to go to Solun quickly and join Lu Zhankui's army. So, in truth, I did not hear anything about the purpose of Lu's army, but since it is a path I like, I immediately led my own men and joined. However, I intend to center everything on Daisensei and support him to the end. Oh, the Commander has appeared there too.'

Lu entered smiling at this moment.

Lu: 'With this, we now have five hundred cavalrymen carrying weapons. There is no point in staying stationed in a place like this, so let us go to a place where we can obtain more provisions. I have decided that Daisensei shall ride in a carriage starting today.'

He immediately made preparations for departure. Liu Shengshan's unit took the vanguard, Hinodeo rode in a carriage loaded with his own personal effects and Lu's valuables, Lu Zhankui himself drove the horses, Shougao and two or three Chinese officers accompanied and guarded Hinodeo's carriage, Masumiwake appeared and disappeared at the front or the rear to watch over the entire army, Hagiwara flew about freely with his camera on his shoulder, and thus a forced march toward the southwest was commenced. However, in the case of camping, the Japanese all gathered at Hinodeo's side and never forgot to remain as one group.

Until they arrived at the Lama temple within the Rehe region on the morning of June 11th (the tenth day of the fifth month of the lunar calendar), it was a forced march that continued day and night, with only occasional rests centered around a few private houses. The food brought from Solun had already run out, and even the sharing of a single cigarette had come to an end. Lu and other opium addicts had their complexions grow haggard, their courage suddenly waned, and they fell into a desperate situation where horses died or men dropped out one after another. At last, they were able to obtain a supply of parched rice at the Lama temple and bought sheep from nearby houses, allowing everyone to fill their stomachs. Lama temples in the interior of Mongolia are generally built on small hills or hillsides, with numerous monks' dwellings lined up, modeling the main mountain in Tibet, with the main hall as the highest center; when viewed from afar, they have the appearance of a great castle. Depending on the region, the beauty and grandeur vary, but they contrast sharply with the thatched huts or sheepskin tent dwellings of the general populace, and there is a great lack of harmony. Of course, since this appears as the crystallization of the faith of the Mongolian people, it should not be criticized. Also, parched rice is like roasted Japanese millet, and it has a fragrant taste even if eaten as is. It is the only grain in Mongolia that is even easier to eat if tea or milk is poured over it. From this day on, the direction was changed once more, and they began to advance toward the southeast. The situation unfolded, and they had to cross either small deserts or sand dunes where grass and trees grew only sparsely.

真澄別『それは大先生に伺つてお定めなさい。私としては何れとも御勧めする訳には行かない。私は大先生自身を神と信じて居るので、仮令自分の考へと違つた言行が大先生にあつても、何事も其舞台々々の筋書は神様でなくては判らぬから、大先生に対し維れ命維れ従つて行くのだ。何だか最前の岩窟から前進するのは厭で仕方がないけれども、大先生がああして盧と一緒に進まれるのだから神に任せて行くのですよ』

猪野『そんなものですかなア』

と腑に落ちぬ顔色で従ひ行く。此時の司令部の駐屯所は熱河の最北部に在る民家で、輓近大英子児が活動の根拠は、右の岩窟の附近だといふのも何等かの因縁事であらう。さて日出雄一行の到着した司令部は兵員整理の為如何にも混雑中で、岡崎は包金山と共に応援軍組織の為奉天に向つた後であつた。茲で陣容は一新され、乗馬や銃器の調のはざるものは、それぞれ旅費手当を給与して帰還の途に就かしめる事となつた。盧の実弟盧秉徳、名田彦、山田、小林善吉其他支那人二名は、洮南より来れる二台の轎車に分乗し、強行軍に邪魔になる様な携帯品をも積み込み、四百余支里の距離と称せらるる洮南に向つて帰奉の途に就いた。此一行は後に至り突泉にて支那官憲の手に捕へられ、盧秉徳は洮南に於て銃殺せられ、日本側三名は領事館渡しとなつたのである。

真澄別『一体此部隊は之から何方へ行く事になつてゐますか』

劉『物資の豊かな綏遠で冬籠りをするのだと云つてゐますから、先づ察哈爾へ向ふのでせう。それに就てはこれから三百支里程行つた所で、開魯の兵と一戦せねばなりませぬから、此処で可成り手足纏ひを少なくする様に計つたのです』

盧は此時微笑し乍ら入り来り、

盧『これで武器を携帯した騎兵のみ五百騎となりました。こんな所に駐屯して居ても仕方がありませぬから、今少し兵糧の得られる所まで参りませう。大先生は今日から轎車に乗つて戴く事に致します』

とて直ちに出動の用意を整へた。劉陞山の部隊は先鋒に立ち、日出雄は自分の手廻り品と盧の貴重品を積み合はした轎車に乗り、盧占魁自ら馬を馭し、守高並に二三の支那将校は日出雄の轎車に附添ひ護り、真澄別は或は先頭に或は後方に出没して全軍を見守り、萩原は写真機を肩にして自由に飛び廻り、茲に西南に向ふて強行軍が開始せられることとなつた。但し宿営の場合には、日本人一同日出雄の側に集り一団となる事は忘れなかつた。

六月十一日（陰暦五月十日）の朝、熱河区内の喇嘛廟へ到着する迄は、時に数戸の民家を中心として休息する外殆ど昼夜兼行の強行軍で、索倫より携帯せし食料は已に尽き、巻煙草一本の喫み廻しも元が切れて了ふ。盧其他阿片の嗜好者は顔色憔悴して勇気頓に衰へ、馬の斃るる者或は落伍する者漸次増加の窮境に陥つた。漸くにして喇嘛廟において炒米の供給を得、附近民家より羊を購めて全員腹を充たす事が出来たのである。蒙古内地の喇嘛廟は概して小高き丘上又は山腹に建立せられ、本堂を最上中心として数多の僧坊が、それぞれ西蔵本山を模して羅列し、之を遠望すれば宛ら一大城廓の観がある。地方に依りて美観壮観に程度はあるが、一般民家の茅屋若しくは羊皮天幕住居に対照して、調和の取れない事夥しい。尤も之れは蒙古民族信仰の結晶として現はれてゐるのだから批判の限りではあるまい。又炒米は日本の粟を煎つた様なもので、其儘食べても香ばしい味がある。お茶若しくは牛乳をブツかければ猶更喰べ易い蒙古唯一の穀物である。此日より更に方向は一転されて東南指して進む事となつた。局面は展開して、或は小砂漠、或は砂山の僅かに草木の生ひ茂れる所を横断せねばならなかつた。

§ 3

On the thirteenth day of the sixth month (the twelfth day of the fifth month by the lunar calendar), they were able to stay at a Lamasery once again, but as the direction remained toward the southeast and it appeared they were approaching the sphere of influence of Hōtenshiyō, Masumiwake asked Ro, 'If we do not go to a place with many Minka, the Hiyōrau and Bariyō will be insufficient, and we shall be unable to do anything.' He merely answered in a dispirited manner.

At last, near the dusk of the fourteenth, they entered a district said to be that of the clan of the Taaraihanōfu. There, a Lamasery and an Ōfu, which seemed to deserve the Keiyōshi of being adorned with Shiyuranhekiguwa and possessing the beauty of Rinkuwan, stood facing each other about ten Jitsuchiyō apart, and more than ten Minka of Chinese-style architecture stood in a row outside. When Commander Ro sent a messenger to the Ōfu to request an audience, the retainers who remained behind gave a truly surly greeting, saying the King was absent. Ro, while feeling suspicious, found Minka in a valley to the southwest and designated them as the lodging for the whole party. The fact that the Lamasery here had all its doors locked and was so silent that not even a single cat was to be found caused everyone to knit their brows significantly.

On this night, in a corner of the dimly lit encampment, Hinodeo was whispering something to Masumiwake, but only the following was heard at the end as the voice of Masumiwake: 'I had heard from the Goshinchoku of Tōnan that the money necessary for this undertaking was one hundred thousand yen, so I believe that any amount beyond that is, to put it plainly, dead money. And the fact that you, Sensei, were pressed by Mr. Ōishi at Kamikikyokushi and wrote a request for a remittance to be sent to Yano-san was truly a form of coercion, was it not?' After this, the night deepened into silence, save for the shrill barking of dogs.
