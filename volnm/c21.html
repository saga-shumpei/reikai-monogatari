Chapter 21: Main Camp at Solon (Soron Hon'ei) [21]

索倫本営〔21〕

§ 1

At Sōronzan, Mokukiyokushi was once occupied by Rokoku, which established a government office called Mokukiyokushiyo for the purpose of collecting taxes on timber, and many Tābīzu settled there in an iuzen manner.

This chiten is the rensaten of Kokuriukōshiyō, Netsuka, and Guwaimō, and it was named Sōronzan after it was occupied by Rokoku.

It is a shimiyaku of the Kōanreisanmiyaku, and the area within a radius of one hundred nitsuponritei is called the Sōronchitai.

Later, a taichiyō of the Mōkobazoku drove out the Rohei, established a konkiyo here, and was preparing for the independence of Mōko.

After that, when the issue of the Qing Dynasty restoration arose in the seventh year of the Republic, the houses were almost destroyed by the ravages of war.

It is now under the jurisdiction of Kokuriukōshiyō, and is guarded by more than ten officials and several dozen soldiers.

In the future, with the increase in population, it is intended to establish Sōron-ken.

Mandōkan had been the great leader of the horse troops in this area, leading several thousand bazoku in the vicinity and consolidating his konkiyo here, but his subordinates have gone out to Guwaimō and the special districts to make preparations for the current Mōko rescue army, and only a few dozen subordinates remain to guard the person of their leader.

Hinodeo and Ro Senkai established a temporary headquarters at this chiten, which is called a stronghold and impregnable, and planned the organization of the army in a leisurely manner, because they had chosen the advantage of the terrain.

The wilderness northwest of Tōnan is a dangerous area where fierce bazoku groups appear in many places and even the Chinese cannot enter, but since they advanced with the great leader of the Mōko hero bazoku, the extremely dangerous bazoku welcomed them with the utmost sincerity as if the sun had appeared in the dark night and followed the army, so the entry of Hinodeo and his party into Sōron was extremely easy.

As Ro was to serve the great savior of the land of Hinode and raise the Mōko rescue army, the people from top to bottom rejoiced greatly, and the popularity was wonderful.

The kings, lamas, and horse troops of Mōko heard the rumors one after another and gathered, and as they led their subordinates to participate, the following organization of executives was completed in an instant.

索倫山木局子は、一時露国が占領して採木の税金を取る為め木局署と云ふ役所を作り、多くの大鼻子がここに悠然と割拠したのである。此地点は黒竜江省と熱河と外蒙との連鎖点であり露国が占領してから索倫山と命名されたのである。興安嶺山脈の支脈であつて、日本里程一百里四方の間を索倫地帯と称してゐる。その後蒙古馬賊の隊長が露兵を追払つて、ここに根拠を構へ、蒙古独立の準備をしてゐた。その後民国七年清朝復辟問題の起つた時、兵燹に罹つて家屋は殆んど滅亡したのである。今は黒竜江省の管轄となり、十数人の官吏や数十人の兵士が守つてゐる。行ゆくは人口増加と共に索倫県を置くと云ふ事である。曼陀汗は此辺の馬隊の大頭目として附近数千名の馬賊を率ゐて此処に根拠を固めてゐたが、其部下は今回の蒙古救援軍の為に、外蒙や特別区域の方へ手分けして準備のために出て行つて了ひ、僅かに数十名の部下が御大の身辺を守つてゐる。

日出雄及び盧占魁が、要害堅固にして難攻不落と称へらるる此地点に仮本営を構へ悠々として軍の編成を図つたのも、地の利を選んだ為めである。洮南より西北の曠野は到る所に慓悍なる馬賊団が沢山に出没して、支那人でさへも這入る事の出来ない危険区域であるが、何と云つても蒙古の英雄馬賊の大巨頭を引連れて進んだのだから、危険至極なる馬賊は暗夜に太陽の出た如く歓迎の至誠を尽して従軍すると云ふ次第であるから、日出雄一行の索倫入りは極めて易々たるものであつた。

此度日出の国の大救世主を盧が奉戴して蒙古救援軍を起すと云ふので、国民は上下を挙げて大に歓喜し、素晴らしい人気であつた。蒙古の王、喇嘛及び馬隊等が次から次へと噂を聞いて集り来り、部下を率ゐて参加するので、瞬く間に左の如き幹部の編成が出来上つた。

§ 2

From Sōronzan, respectfully, April 29th.

To the Commander, for your inspection. I, Rosenkuwai, respectfully report that on the 9th of the month, I led my subordinates north. Having received your extraordinary care and attention, I am filled with gratitude beyond words. On the 27th, I arrived at Sōronzan. At present, the personnel accompanying me number over five hundred. Throughout the journey, we have strictly maintained order and have not committed the slightest offense. After Lieutenant Zhao departed, Liu Singsan immediately sent men to Sōron to report that he had already recruited a force of seven or eight hundred men, who departed for the north on the 22nd of the month and will arrive at Sōron in about three to five days. Regarding the Mongol banners, the Gōrusu Public Association has sent trusted representatives to Sōron, promising in person that each of the ten banners under their jurisdiction will provide one hundred soldiers. However, their firearms are quite incomplete. I have also consulted with the Mongol Prince De, who agreed that each of the ten banners under his jurisdiction will provide two hundred soldiers, with firearms and horses fully prepared. Once the men and horses have all arrived and the organization is settled, we shall immediately proceed westward. Firearms and horses are generally sufficient, but ammunition is not very abundant and is difficult to purchase. I fear that a lack of supply will become a major difficulty in the future.

I humbly pray that the Commander will grant extraordinary attention and help us devise a way to purchase it. As for the necessary funds, I will take responsibility for them myself, and I earnestly request that you negotiate with the Great Master to have them issued so that the military needs may be met. If the great undertaking is successful in the future, it will all be due to the Lord Commander's fostering care. I respectfully submit this report and humbly request your inspection.

Rosenkuwai, respectfully reporting, April 29th.

(2) From Rosenkuwai to Sōsangi Yang (Three Eastern Provinces)

Respectfully presented.

To Sōsangi Yang, for your inspection.

From Kokuryūkō, Sōronzan, respectfully, May 8th.

To Sōsangi, for your inspection. It has been a long time since we parted, and my admiration has grown deeper. I have already sent a letter reporting the situation regarding the arrangements, and I trust it has received your kind attention. At present, there is a force of over five hundred men, with firearms and horses fully equipped, and we are just beginning training at the defense site. There are still over one thousand men who will surely arrive at Sōron in about five or six days. However, at this moment, firearms and ammunition are urgently needed. I request that you select trusted personnel to transport the weapons and ammunition to Sōron as soon as possible for our use. I have no choice but to rely on your seat to devise a way to maintain this, hoping to achieve complete results to match my inner sincerity. As for the trouble caused to your noble spirit, I shall engrave it upon my heart and never forget it. Furthermore, I have selected fifty or sixty men to go to the hot regions to purchase horses in preparation for organizing the troops. Regarding how to organize the troops after they have all arrived, I request your full support to facilitate progress. I will continue to report on how matters are handled from time to time. I respectfully request your peace and await your reply.

Rosenkuwai, bowing, May 8th.

(3) From Rosenkuwai to Chiyōhosui of Kichirinshiyō

Kichirin Military Governor's Office.

Presented to Chiyōhosui, for your inspection.

From the river province, respectfully sending this letter.

To the Commander, under your command, though we are far apart, my thoughts of you are constant.

Seeing the Dragon Gate in sight, my admiration is boundless. I have recently heard the news that you have gloriously assumed the post of Kichirin Governor, protecting the frontiers, and that your great talents are being displayed. I admire you from afar and offer my congratulations from afar. I, Rosenkuwai, have received your favor and am deeply moved. Regarding the recruitment of troops after coming north, I have already reported the outline. The matter is in its initial stages, and the threads are tangled, but it now has a rough shape, and the number of organized men is already five or six hundred. There are still over one thousand men who have not yet arrived at Sōron, and they will surely arrive at Sōron within about a week. After the recruitment is finished, I humbly ask for your guidance on how to organize them so that I may follow it. I also hope you will spare no effort to devise a way to maintain everything. The completion of the organization in the future will depend entirely on your grace. In the future, I hope you will not withhold your golden words, and I will listen to your instructions on every matter. I trust that one who treats me with such kindness will not abandon me.

I respectfully submit this report and humbly request your peace, and I pray for your glory and happiness.

Rosenkuwai, respectfully, May 8th.

I respectfully inquire again: since I arrived at Sōron at the beginning of last month, more than a month has passed, and I have had no way to inquire about the news in the provincial capital. Yesterday, I read in the newspaper that there have been changes in the province, but I do not know if it is true.

Furthermore, is Mr. Xu in the capital, and has there been any definite news? Regarding the position of Commander Xu, has it been decided? We are separated by a thousand miles, and I am filled with anxiety. I beg you not to withhold your words and to show me the compass from time to time to correct my ignorance.

Rosenkuwai, postscript.

(4) From Rosenkuwai to Lieutenant Chiyō of the Three Eastern Provinces

Presented in person.

To Lieutenant Chiyō, respectfully opened.

From Kokuryūkō, Sōronzan, respectfully sending this letter, May 8th.

To my elder brother, your Excellency. I trust you have read my previous letter, so I will not trouble you with trifles. At present, the internal organization has been roughly completed, and the scale is as it is. The recruited troops number five or six hundred. At this moment, we are in the midst of organization and training. There are still over one thousand men who have not arrived, and they will surely arrive at Sōron within about five or six days. However, while the number of men is complete, military equipment is even more urgent and essential, and we are waiting to use it, so it cannot be delayed for a moment. I hope you will quickly devise a way to transport it to Sōron. I pray that you will speak to the Sōsangi in gentle words, and the sooner the better, so as not to make your younger brother wait in the distance and cause his heart to waver. I trust that one who loves me can understand my inner feelings. Furthermore, I earnestly request that you take care of the matter of supporting the families of my subordinates, which I reported when I was in the capital, as I have already received your golden promise. I fear that the personnel accompanying me north this time may also need your help. I hope you will take care of them as well, which would be reasonable and avoid the criticism of being short-sighted. I am attaching a list of personnel with this letter and pray for your early approval and implementation. I am deeply grateful. I respectfully request your peace.

Your younger brother, Rosenkuwai, bowing, May 8th.
