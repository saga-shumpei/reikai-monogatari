Chapter 14: Clouds over Taonan (Tōnan no Kumo) [14]

洮南の雲〔14〕

§ 1

The houses in this region are remarkably different compared to those in Naichi. Every Minka is surrounded by a high Dobei to prepare for the Shifurai of Bazoku, and the Okunai is structured as an Itsupauguchi, with only one Iriguchi for each room. The construction is such that one locks the door from the inside before sleeping. The rooms are partitioned by thick walls, some over an Itsushiyaku in thickness, and the Madori is Hosonaga, resembling an eel's sleeping quarters. During the Tōki, fire is kept in the Ondoru throughout the Chiuya, so the Shitsunai is warm. Conversely, if one steps Itsupo outside, the Kanki is severe and presses upon the body; if one is careless, one will immediately harm one's Inkō. Furthermore, looking at the Bashiya on the road, one does not see the usual Shinashiki rickshaws with low floors and long Kajibō, but rather unsightly oxcarts and horse-drawn carriages racing about on ash-like roads, and one cannot travel even a step ahead without wearing sand-proof goggles. Every house has red paper pasted on the Iriguchi, inscribed with auspicious characters such as wealth, happiness, or good omens. From the evening, the castle gates are firmly closed, and it is forbidden to go to other regions at night. Since the thousands of soldiers and numerous patrolmen within the castle are mostly former Bazoku, as soon as the curtain of night falls, they calmly commit theft while still in their military uniforms, so there is absolutely no guarantee for life or property. Moreover, since one-third of the castle is occupied by Bazoku leaders and petty thieves who operate various large and small shops with feigned innocence, there is no story more extremely dangerous than this. The Bazoku groups in this vicinity enter villages in small parties of thirty or fifty, stay in the village for about three days, eat well, drink well, rape any woman they see regardless of age, and when food and drink run out, they leisurely move on to the next village to repeat the same, a band of thieves so carefree that the Mongolian residents truly cannot sleep in peace. Furthermore, the reputation and credibility of the so-called Living Buddhas in the East Mongolia region have fallen completely to the ground, and the faith of the Mongolians has begun to waver. In fact, the Living Buddha of Paintara lost at mahjong, incurred a debt of over one hundred thousand, had his vast lands swindled by the Chinese, and having lured women and children, is now suffering from syphilis. The Living Buddha of Wangyemiao also has various unpleasant rumors circulating about him. As it was the first anniversary of the day when a certain Kamimura of the South Manchuria Railway was beaten to death by Bazoku in this region on the fourteenth of March last year, a memorial service was held among the Japanese. Kamimura was a master of kendo, but it is said that he was struck on the crown of his head with a club from behind in the dark of night and fell without a chance to resist.

The cold wind blows fiercely, and a funeral procession passes through the streets filled with ten thousand jō of yellow dust, accompanied by various musical instruments. Looking from within the window, about twenty lamas, dressed in yellow and red robes, carrying many interesting flags, and bearing a corpse on a palanquin carried by poles as long as five or six ken, pass by while making a chirping sound, followed by a long procession of several carriages with pigs and sheep tied to them. It is a spectacle like the procession of a portable shrine at a festival for a local deity. Such a magnificent funeral is said to be for a person of considerable fame in this land.

当地の家屋は内地に比して非常に変つてゐる。何れの民家も皆家の周囲に高き土塀をめぐらし、馬賊の襲来に備へ、屋内は室毎に入口のみあつて一方口である。中から鍵をかけて寝る構造となつてゐる。一尺以上もある様な厚い壁で間を仕切り、そして鰻の寝所のやうな細長い間取になつてゐる。冬季は昼夜温突に火を入れてあるから室内は暖かい。之に反し一歩屋外に出づれば寒気厳しく身に迫り、うつかりしてゐると、直ぐに咽喉を害して了ふ。それから道行く車馬を見ると、例の支那式の床の低い梶棒の篦棒に長い人力車は見られないが、不恰好な牛車や馬車が灰の様な道路を駆け廻り、防砂眼鏡をかけねば一歩も先を通行することが出来ない。何れの家も入口に赤い紙を張り、富貴だとか幸福だとか、瑞祥だとか、目出度さうな文字を誌してゐる。そして夕方から城門を固く閉し、夜分は他の地方へ出られない事になつてゐる。当城内にいる数千の兵士も数多の巡警も大部分馬賊上りだから、夜の帳がおりると同時に、平気の平左で、軍服の儘泥棒をやると云ふのだから、生命財産の保証などは到底駄目である。そして城内の三分の一迄は馬賊の頭目や小盗児連が大小各店を開いてそ知らぬ顔してゐるのだからこれ程危険極まる話はない。此附近の馬賊の団体は三十人或は五十人の小勢で村落に入り来り、三日間位其の村に逗留して、能く食ひ、能く飲み、女と見れば老若の別なく強姦をなし、飲食物がなくなると、悠々として又次の村へ行つて同じことを繰返すといふ呑気千万な泥棒団が横行し、蒙古の住民は実に枕を高くする事が出来ないと云ふ有様である。それから東蒙古地方の俗称活仏の名望と信用は全然地に墜ち、蒙古人の信仰が動き出したといふ。現にパインタラの活仏は麻雀に負けて、十万余の負債が出来、広大な土地は支那人にボツたくられ、且つ婦女子を小口から引つかけて、今は梅毒に罹り苦しんでゐるといふ有様だ。王爺廟の活仏も又いろいろ面白からぬ評判が立つてゐる。昨年の三月十四日満鉄の上村某が当地にて馬賊に擲り殺された一周忌に当るといふので、其追悼会が日本人間で行はれた。上村は剣道の達人であつたが、暗夜に後から棍棒で脳天を擲りつけられて一堪りもなく斃れたとの事である。

寒風烈しく吹きまくり、黄塵万丈の巷をいろいろの鳴物入りで葬式の行列が通つて行く。窓内より眺むれば喇嘛僧が二十人許り、黄や赤の衣を着け、面白い旗を沢山押立て、死骸を輿に載せ、五六間もあるやうな長い棒でかついで、チワチワさせ乍ら、馬車数台に豚や羊などを縛りつけて長い行列を作つて通る。恰も氏神の祭礼の神輿渡御の様な光景である。斯る立派な葬式は此土地でも余程名の売れた人士だと云ふことだ。

§ 2

Okazaki received the movement funds from Hideo's hands and, while smiling, invited five or six Chinese officials along with an officer who was a relative of the Taunanfuchiji, engaged in reckless spending, and furthermore gave them pocket money to buy their favor, exerting himself to the utmost in his movement as a preparation for any emergency. In the same residence as Mr. Heimashi, where Hideo was staying, a certain Renchiyō of the Taunanfu was living. Okazaki became intimate with this Renchiyō and they visited each other. Every time the Renchiyō couple started a great quarrel—shouting about dying, running away, asking for a divorce, or killing each other—and a Rinkigenkuwa broke out, the Gejiyo would be startled and come to call Okazaki, until they became such close friends that they eventually became sworn brothers. Okazaki, looking Tokuizen, would speak in a loud voice without regard for his surroundings, eventually even asserting that they should do something to the Taunanfu, and because this reached the ears of the Chinese authorities or the Japanese authorities, the Japanese side became extremely worried. Even without this, they were lurking in the Taunanfu where anti-Japanese sentiment was intense, so if such things, even if spoken in jest, were heard by the Chinese authorities from the mouth of a Japanese, there was no telling what might happen; thus, when Okazaki's slip of the tongue was conveyed to the comrades in Fengtian, Karadewake, Sasaki, and Ōkura changed color, panicked, and sent a letter via Sakamoto Kōichi requesting that they be asked not to let Okazaki-kun return to Fengtian. They also requested that Hideo-sensei pull Okazaki away and take him to the Gōyafu as soon as possible. Okazaki was not in the least concerned about such things, blowing them off with a scoff, saying, 'We are devising the best methods for the sake of the nation and society. Generally, those fellows Sasaki and Ōkura are cowards with no guts, worrying about nothing; with such an attitude, how can such a great undertaking be accomplished? Hmph, how ridiculous.' Hideo cautioned Okazaki, saying, 'In the present case, you should maintain as much secrecy as possible and not speak of important matters,' and Okazaki, who would listen to no one else, kept a prudent silence for two or three days. Then, one day, Mr. Okumura Mikizō of Sipingjie arrived in a flurry. Because Okazaki's big talk had backfired and seemed to have reached the ears of the Japanese and Chinese authorities, he had come out of kindness to inquire after the well-being of Hideo's party. Okazaki then entered and spoke triumphantly, saying that he had gone to a brothel the previous night with various Chinese officers, played mahjong and spent money together, and had completely bought them off, so there was no longer any cause for worry. Okumura was struck with surprise at seeing Okazaki's calm face.

As Hideo's party was about to enter the depths of Mongolia, they decided to rent a Chinese house under the name of Wang Yuanqi for an annual rent of 150 yen for the sake of convenience in all matters. It was a four-room house with a Kang, which they named Chōeigō, and on the surface, they made it appear to be a trading firm, using it as a relay station for military arms and provisions.

While Hideo was reading in Mr. Heimashi's residence with Moritaka, Mr. Tsukikawa Samon of the Japanese Consulate arrived. He and Ino Toshio exchanged various conversations for a long time, and they indulged in discussions on the management of Manchuria and Mongolia, concluding that there was no other way to make the relations between Japan and China smooth than to demonstrate Japan's real power. Hideo was listening to their conversation from the next room. After a while, Chinese officers arrived, surrounded a table, and while laughing happily, played the game of gambling called mahjong until late at night. Futaba-ko, the wife of Mr. Heimashi, was also indulging in mahjong with them. Hideo, who possessed a literary taste, not only felt no pain even if he remained shut in one room for several days, but also said that various fountains of thought would spring forth, and he spent his days pleasantly and happily, indulging in poetry and the like. Whenever I gaze upon the moon of the twelfth night, I wonder how that midnight was when I was born.

Having departed from Hinomoto, I behold the moon of the twelfth night once again in the skies of Mōko.

The moon trembles in the Ōzora and the wind is cold, yet my body rests in the bosom of Kami.

The sound of bells is heard most vividly; perhaps I am traveling the road of a bashiya once more.

This house where the Senriu hides must surely be a mansion of the deep Shigumi of the Shinkai.

On Sanguwatsu Jifurokunichi (the thirteenth day of the second month by the old calendar), a rumor reached their ears that a certain Yamazaki, an employee of the Mantetsu, had informed the Japanese Kenpeitai in Shiheigai that Hideo and his party had arrived in Taunanfu, and that the authorities on the Shinagaha side had begun to take action; the party was troubled by a feeling as if they were treading upon thin ice. Meanwhile, the Shiyokisei Tsuki-kaha and a certain Satō of the Mantetsu were taking turns spying on the residence of Mr. Heima.

Hideo recited, 'Having come to Taunan, I have no time to feel at ease, and once again I am filled with deep worry.'

However, he spent his days relying on the golden rule that a bird in distress seeking refuge in one's bosom will not be killed by the hunter, and trusting in the power of Kami, believing that remaining in a dangerous house might actually be safer. In the memoirs of Hideo from that time, there is a passage as follows:

Hideo: 'Even though I am taking actions that are upright and honorable for the sake of all people under heaven, without shame before heaven and earth, the fact that I must hide my body and conduct secret activities like this is, in short, because there are cowardly politicians above. It is out of consideration so as not to cause the slightest worry to the Japanese diplomats and cabinet members who are strong internally but weak externally, and who only watch the breath of foreigners with submissive compliance. Yet, the Japanese authorities are quite fierce and oppressive toward Shinagaha, Chōsen, and Russia, while toward the British and Americans, they are nothing but spineless cowards who cannot utter a single word of complaint even when spat upon with green phlegm. They claim the influence of Kōdō Ōmoto is great, and thus they impose pressure everywhere, eventually forcing false charges upon a great thinker of pure loyalty and unmatched sincerity, destroying the shrines of the Great Kami of heaven and earth, and inflicting various miscellaneous misgovernment and atrocities; it is truly appalling that they treat a righteous organization with such wickedness and lawlessness. And yet, they are insufferable because they are conceited enough to call themselves the gentleman's nation of the East, the Urayasu-koku. That they should be in such a state of panic, starting a search with a bounty of one thousand yen, just because I have calmly broken through their security lines and come from afar to carry out the divine governance of the Shinkai, is truly pitiful. But please, do not worry; I will not do anything so clumsy that it would not be for your benefit, for this is to execute and realize the great ideal of world peace and co-prosperity. With your level of intellect, what do you think you can accomplish in today's world, and how do you think you can protect a nation of one continuous lineage for all ages? Unless you, who are the source of injustice and sin, wake up even a little, it is impossible to build a secure nation in the East; indeed, what is this current state of affairs...'

Regarding Zhang Zuolin, he also commented as follows:

Hideo: 'Zhang Zuolin of the Three Eastern Provinces is also quite a formidable man for a Shinagaha person, and he is quite skilled at weaving secret schemes. And it is insufferable that he does not put up the essential capital himself, but makes others suffer while he secretly tries to reap the sweet rewards. However, business without capital will likely end in failure. Profit lies in the foundation. The capitalist is the final victor. Will Lu truly remain content to be ordered about by Zhang forever? The tug-of-war between the Zhili and Fengtian cliques has been going on for a long time, but I am sure that before long, some kind of drama will be staged. And so on.'
