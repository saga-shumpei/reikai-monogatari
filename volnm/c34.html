Chapter 34: The Tribulation of Longkou (Ryūkō no Nan) [34]

竜口の難〔34〕

§ 1

Prior to this, Hideo, accompanied by his attendant Inōhe Kanekichi, Rosenkuwai, and over ten others, and guarded in front and behind by twenty Shinakuwanpei soldiers, set out for Paintara, which was fifty Shiri away. He was heading toward Paintara with high spirits, resolved to complete the transfer of weapons there, return to Nitsupon for a time, and then depart again, all in order to appear at the Ōmotosaiban on the twentieth of Shichiguwatsu.

They arrived at a place about thirty Shiri away where the Heieibuntai Chiutonjiyo of Tsūreuken was located (Tsūreu is the Shinamei for Paintara). After resting for a while at this barracks with the Shinakuwanpei officers, Rosenkuwai held a brief consultation with the commander of the barracks and then proceeded eastward once more. Just as the night was about to break, several hundred cavalrymen advanced from the front, and after negotiating something with Rosenkuwai, they turned back toward the original military camp. At this time, Rosenkuwai turned to Inōhe and said,

Rosenkuwai: 'Please take good care of the Great Teacher.'

He repeated this again and again as he left. Hideo, together with Inōhe, dismounted from their horses and let them graze on the nearby green grass, but after about an hour, the chief of staff of the Tsūreu brigade arrived with over ten cavalrymen and said to Hideo in Japanese,

Chief of Staff: 'You are a Nitsupon person; please flee as quickly as you can, please flee.'

He gestured with his hand, pointing toward the south. Hideo said,

Hideo: 'To flee while leaving Masumewake and the other Nitsupon people behind is a disgrace to a Nitsupon man. In any case, whatever happens is the divine providence of the Lord God. I would rather charge into the barracks myself and negotiate the receipt of the weapons; in the meantime, Rosenkuwai will likely appear, and I will also be able to learn the whereabouts of the Nitsupon people left behind.'

He whipped his horse once more and galloped toward Paintara. Secretary-General Mō followed him with one attendant, and the party of four riders rode side by side, advancing boldly toward the west gate of Tsūreuken.

As they reached the front of the city gate, the sun began to rise from the grass with a red face. Several heavily armed soldiers stood guard on both sides of the gate, collecting a transit tax of fifty sen per person. When Hideo and Inōhe asked for the location of the brigade, four guards escorted them to the barracks, walking in front and behind them.

Hideo entered the barracks, greeted what appeared to be a high-ranking officer through Inōhe's interpretation, and requested,

Hideo: 'I wish to rest at this camp until Rosenkuwai arrives.'

The officer very courteously provided them with a beautiful room, served tea and sweets, laid out soft blankets, and encouraged them, saying,

Officer: 'Please, first take a rest.'

Hideo and Inōhe, exhausted from the forced march through the night, thanked him for his kindness and fell asleep, unaware of what was to come. When they had slept for almost three or four hours, the staff officer, accompanied by four or five soldiers, pointed their guns at them, shook Inōhe Kanekichi awake, confiscated the ten-shot Mauser and six-shot pistol he carried in his pocket, searched his belongings, and then bound his hands behind his back with hemp rope before waking Hideo. Hideo, having been roused from a peaceful dream, rubbed his eyes and looked around to see that Inōhe was already bound. The staff officer drew hot water into a metal basin, soaked a towel in it, and said kindly in Japanese,

Staff Officer: 'First, please wash your face with this.'

Hideo said,

Hideo: 'Yes, thank you.'

He wiped his face with the towel soaked in the hot water. The soldiers took turns soaking and wringing out the towel, handing it to Hideo, and even washed his neck and hands. Then, they searched Hideo's belongings, examined the 'Tengoku' sword, the Nyoino-hōgyoku, the platinum watch, and the eight hundred and seventy yen in cash right before his eyes, and said,

Staff Officer: 'I will keep these in custody until Rosenkuwai arrives.'

They then took them away. Looking into the next room, he saw Secretary-General Mō and one Shinakuwanpei soldier bound just like Inōhe. Hideo, surprised by the unexpected turn of events, asked the staff officer,

Hideo: 'Why have you bound Inōhe?'

Staff Officer: 'Inōhe was carrying a weapon, so we recognized him as a Bazoku and tied him up. Those two Shinajin were also tied up because they are Bazoku. And until Rosenkuwai arrives, we shall tie you up as well, albeit merely as a formality.'

Upon hearing this, Hideo replied:

Hideo: 'Do as you please.'

He then moved his hands behind his back. The Staff Officer tied his hands very loosely as a formality, had him sit in the barracks along with the two Nichijin and two Shinajin, and exchanged various conversations with Hideo in Nitsupongo. Then, he offered comfort, saying:

Staff Officer: 'Since you are not carrying a weapon and are a religious person, you shall be released immediately.'

Here, Hideo parted from his beloved horse with tears. It is said that the horse also shed tears, as if it understood Hideo's heart.

Around four o'clock in the afternoon of that day, Rosenkuwai, accompanied by more than twenty cadres, was sent by the Shinaguntai and arrived at the brigade headquarters where Hideo was being held. As a result of negotiations between Rosenkuwai and the Chief of Staff, the bonds on Hideo and the other three were removed, and tea and refreshments were brought, and they began to entertain them kindly once again.

Chief of Staff: 'I would like to hold a welcome banquet for you all tonight, so please stay at the barracks.'

He urged them. There, Rosenkuwai and He Zenhō arrived, invited Hideo to the brigade commander's office, and wrote down in a brush conversation:

Rosenkuwai: 'The time has finally come when disarmament is unavoidable. However, the brigade commander here is my sworn brother, and I have many of my own subordinates here, so it is fine. Please be at ease. Even if an emergency occurs, our lives will not be in danger, and you Japanese can be even more at ease. I will change my name to Ō Shōgi and bury the name Rosenkuwai as of today. If I were to be killed, your life would likely be at an end as well. In any case, let us go to Hōten tomorrow.'

Hideo replied:

Hideo: 'Hmm, hmm, hmm, hmm.'

While nodding his head two or three times, he entered the Chief of Staff's office again and said:

Hideo: 'I am quite tired tonight, so I would like you to guide me to a good Japanese hotel somewhere, as I wish to eat Japanese cuisine for the first time in a while.'

Then the Staff Officer replied:

Staff Officer: 'Until about the year before last, there was a Japanese inn, but now there is none. There is a first-class Shinajin inn called Kōhinkan, so I shall have you guided there.'

He ordered a horse-drawn carriage with a young soldier who understood Nitsupongo as a guide, and Hideo and Inōhe were delivered to Kōhinkan. The streets of Hakutōtara were crowded with people, watching Hideo's passage with curiosity. Just then, several thousand soldiers returned, leading the remainder of Rosenkuwai's troops, with the sound of bugles blowing bravely. Masumiwake, Moritaka, and Sakamoto were riding in a carriage loaded with Hideo's luggage, and they encountered Hagiwara returning on horseback. Hideo called out from the carriage:

Hideo: 'I am staying at the Kōhin Inn tonight, so please come to the hotel with Hakuryōkaku.'

As he called out, he arrived at the inn around sunset and rested in a room.

About an hour later, the group of four including Masumiwake, along with one Mongol and one Shinajin, arrived at the inn. This Mongol was the deputy commander, the right-hand man of Chō Kirin. Although he had received a bullet in his chest, he sat with a calm face. A Shinajin military officer wearing the shoulder insignia of a major displayed great charm and welcomed Hideo's party. Everyone clicked their tongues in delight at the greasy Shinajin cuisine. However, Hideo did not feel like it on that day, and he did not even touch his chopsticks to the meal. Just as he was about to go to sleep, Rosenkuwai visited Hideo with two adjutants and began a brush conversation:

Rosenkuwai: 'Tonight feels somewhat suspicious, yet as I said earlier, I believe it is fine. I intend to escape with my subordinates to the special zones of Netsu, Satsu, and Sui, and plan for a comeback. This is to help with your travel expenses to Japan...'

So saying, he presented Itsupiyakuen, but Hideo firmly declined and did not receive it. Then Rosenkuwai exchanged a handshake with Hideo, wiped away his tears, and departed. That night, at the brigade barracks, they invited dozens of prostitutes, performed plays, and did various interesting things to entertain Rosen and his subordinates, serving them opium and providing a great deal of food and hospitality. The many officers and soldiers, thinking themselves 'safe for the time being,' drank sake, ate the feast, watched the plays of the prostitutes, enjoyed themselves to the fullest, and went to sleep.

Around the middle of the night, while they were sleeping in only a single layer of undergarments, they were dragged out one by one outside the barracks gate, and the shooting began from the edge with machine guns.

The Chinese Major: 'I wished to guide you to the bath this evening, but it is too late and the water is dirty, so I shall guide you to a new, clean bath tomorrow morning. How about a haircut? Your hair has grown quite long; shall I call a barber?'

He spoke thus kindly. Thereupon, the two men, Hagiwara and Inoue, had the barber called and received haircuts. Hideo and the others said, 'We will do it tomorrow,' and fell asleep. At the front gate, several government soldiers and several policemen were keeping strict guard. This Major was a man like a honeybee, containing sweet juice in his mouth while hiding a sharp sword in his tail. When it became about one o'clock in the middle of the night, just as the sound of many footsteps was heard inside the room, soldiers suddenly burst into the room; first of all, they shook Hagiwara awake, and five or six soldiers pointed pistols at him.

Soldiers: 'Surrender meekly to the ropes!'

Next, in the order of Moritaka, Inoue, Sakamoto, Masumiwake, and Hideo, while trembling and shaking, the six Japanese and two Sino-Mongolians were finally bound. Looking closely, the Major who had been tossing about kind flatteries when the party stayed at this inn was the one conducting the command. Hideo questioned the Major.

Hideo: 'Why do you do such a thing?'

The Major only shook his head from side to side.

Major: 'I know nothing, nothing.'

And more than ten soldiers with leveled guns stood in readiness. Finally, they were dragged out to the garden in front of this inn and made to stand in a line. In pairs of Hideo and Masumiwake, Inoue and Hagiwara, and Moritaka and Sakamoto, they were bound with ropes; the Major and his subordinate soldiers scrambled to plunder all the belongings of the party without exception, leaving only the lama robes to be placed on a cart and taken somewhere. Amidst this commotion, Inoue, who was fluent in the Chinese language, heard the whispers and curses of the Chinese soldiers and shouted to Hideo with perfect composure.

Inoue: 'Master, the Chinese soldiers are saying just now that they will shoot us all. There is no help for it now.'

Responding to this voice, Hideo repeated two or three times.

Hideo: 'Yes, it seems so. The Chinese fellows likely intend to cowardly commit a foul murder through this policy of hospitality; then the time has finally come for me to become a Christ and ascend to heaven. Since I shall take you all and Rosen's subordinates to the Kingdom of Heaven, you should ensure that your spirits do not become separated.'

He then preached on the magnificence of the world after death. Inoue and Sakamoto replied to this.

Inoue and Sakamoto: 'Please, we humbly entrust ourselves to you. We shall accompany you even to the bottom of Hell.'

The other four remained silent and composed. At this time, Hideo chanted three times.

Hideo: 'Kannagara Tamachihaemase.'

Masumiwake began to chant the Heavenly Counting Song in a loud voice. The Chinese soldiers were startled and scolded them.

Chinese Soldiers: 'Do not speak so noisily!'

Therefore, the two lowered their voices to a medium tone, and while singing propaganda songs amidst the many soldiers who were making a 'wai-wai' clamor, they were paraded through the long streets of Bain-Tala and sent again inside the gates of the barracks. Meanwhile, the two Sino-Mongolians were separated from Hideo's party and sent to the execution ground. As the six of Hideo's party again exited the gate of the barracks and were led further and further north, there were many of Rosen's subordinates lying on both sides of the road, fallen in the shape of the character for 'great' and stained with blood. Soldiers had brought large carts and were transporting them. Hideo proceeded while examining the corpses on those large carts one by one by the light of the electric lamps, and before long, they were lined up in a row.

と二三回繰返し、且つ死後の世界の壮厳なる事を説いた。井上、坂本は之に答へて、

『どうか、宜しくお願ひ致します。仮令地獄の底へでもお伴を致します』

と答へた。他の四名は平然として沈黙してゐた。此時日出雄は、

『惟神霊幸倍坐世』

と三唱し、真澄別は天の数歌を大きな声で唱へ出した。支那兵はビツクリして、

『八釜しく云ふな』

と叱りつけたので、両人は更に中声になつて、『ワイワイ』と騒いでゐる沢山の兵の中を、宣伝歌を歌ひ乍ら、白音太拉の長い町を引廻され再び兵営の門内に送られた。其中支蒙人二名は日出雄一行と離され、銃殺場へ送られる。日出雄一行六人は再び営所の門を出で、北へ北へと引かれて行くと、道の両端には盧の部下が大の字になつて血潮に染まつて倒れてゐるのが沢山にある。そして大車を持つて来て兵士が運んでゐる。日出雄は一々其大車の死骸を電燈の光に査べ乍ら進んで行くと、やがて一列に並べられた。

§ 4

Masumiwake, Hideo, Hagihara, Inōhe, Sakamoto, and Moritaka were lined up in order, and just as it seemed the bullets of the machine gun would fly into the chests of these Nitsuponjin, the shooter fell backward from the recoil of the gun, which required several minutes to rectify. Hideo spoke toward all the Nitsuponjin.

Hideo: 'Now that things have come to this, the time for ascension has arrived. From here I shall ascend to Tengoku, become a Reigokutennin, and intend to protect not only Nitsupon but the whole Sekai. You all, follow me, and let us die being shot like men. I do not wish to perform any cowardly mane that would stain the name of a Yamatōnoko.'

He spoke as if to admonish them. Sakamoto, letting out a namidagoe, spoke.

Sakamoto: 'Please, I beg you, do not abandon me.'

Masumiwake interrupted Hideo's words and spoke.

Masumiwake: 'Sensei, there is absolutely no worry that your life will be taken. You said just now that you are going to Tengoku, but the current reconstruction of the world cannot be accomplished without a physical body. If your life were to be taken here, it would mean that the Kami-sama has deceived mankind. I believe you will surely be saved...'

He asserted this as one who possessed conviction.

Hideo: 'Even so, Masumiwake-san, no matter how much one serves the Way of God, Hideo's body is a physical one. If a bullet hits, it is natural to die. If you are hit by a single shot while feeling relieved thinking you will not die, your soul will contrary to your expectations wander in the intermediate realm. Therefore, is it not better to be prepared to die?'

Thus he admonished him. Masumiwake stubbornly refused to bend his view.

Masumiwake: 'No, no, by no means can I have you die, Sensei. If your life were to be lost, the Kami-sama would surely set me up as your substitute. Since I have come from the beginning under the title of your substitute, there is no need for such worry.'

Hideo spoke.

Hideo: 'I am not worried about anything. I have resigned everything to Kannagara. Even upon a tatami mat, when it is time to die, one dies. It is also pleasant for a Yamatōnoko to expose his remains upon the fields of Mongolia. However, the life after death is the vital matter...'

Thus he concentrated his heart solely on saving the souls of everyone. Then Hideo composed a death poem.

Hideo: 'Even if this body should rot upon the wild moors of Mongolia, I shall not let the dignity of a Yamatōnoko fall.'

He waited for the gun bullets to fly into his chest. Then Inōhe Kanekichi shouted.

Inōhe: 'Will you not shoot quickly? Shoot my chest! If you shoot poorly, you will waste extra bullets and suffer a loss; kill me splendidly with a single shot!'

Hideo again composed death poems up to seven times, and shouted three cheers for the Great Empire of Nitsupon and Oomoto.

Hideo: 'Now, farewell; I shall gallop up into the High Heavenly Realm, and protect not only the Land of the Rising Sun but the whole Sekai.'

Hideo: 'Leaving the Land of the Rising Sun far behind, I shall now become a deity in the skies of Mongolia.'

Masumiwake was offering a prayer.

Masumiwake: 'If it is unavoidable, please save the Great Sensei and Inōhe. Inōhe will surely be able to lead Sensei to the destination. I will become the substitute.'

While this and that were happening, the execution by shooting was halted, and once again the soldiers dragged Hideo's party away and took them to the prison attached to the Tongliao Public Office. They fitted each person with sturdy leg irons and placed manacles on their hands, chained them in pairs, and further bound the six together with hemp rope, tying them to the timber outside through the window, treating them with the strictness accorded to prisoners on death row.

原文（近代文語）

真澄別、日出雄、萩原、井上、坂本、守高と順に並べられ、今や機関銃の弾丸が此等日本人の胸先に飛んで来ると思ふ矢先、射手は銃の反動を受けて後方に倒れた為数分を要した。日出雄は日本人一同に向つて云ふ、

『最早かくなる上は昇天の時が来たのだ、自分は之から天国へ上り、霊国天人となつて日本は云ふに及ばず、世界の守護をする考へだ。君達も俺について来い、そして男らしう討たれて死なうぢやないか。日本男子の名を汚すやうな、卑怯な真似はしともないからのう』

と諭すやうに云つた。坂本は涙声を出して、

『どうか、よろしくお見捨てなきやう』

と云つた。真澄別は日出雄の言葉を遮つて云ふ、

『先生、決して貴方は生命を取られる気遣はありませぬよ。貴方は今天国へ行くと云はれましたが、今度の世の立替は肉体がなくては出来ないのです。若し貴方がここで生命を取られるやうな事があれば、神様が人間を騙したことになります。私は屹度お助かりになると思ひますから……』

と確信あるものの如く主張する。

『それでも真澄別さん、何程神の道に仕へてゐると云つても、日出雄の体は肉体だ。鉄砲が中れば死ぬのが当然だ。あんたも死なないと思つて安心して居る途端に一発食つたならば、あなたの霊魂は予想に反して中有に迷ふだらう。それだから死ぬものと覚悟して居ればよいぢやないか』

と諭す。真澄別は頑として其説をまげず、

『イエイエどうあつても先生は死んで貰ふ事は出来ませぬ。もし貴方が生命が無くなる事があれば、神様は私を身代りに立てられるでせう。私は初めから貴方の身代りと云ふ名義で来て居りますから、そんな心配は要りませぬ』

と云ふ。日出雄は、

『何も心配はして居ないよ。何事も惟神と諦めてゐるのだ。畳の上でも死ぬ時は死ぬのだ。日本男子が蒙古の野辺に骸を曝すのも愉快だ。然し死後の生活が肝腎だから……』

と一同の霊魂を救ふべく、それのみに心を集注してゐた。それから日出雄は、

『よしや身は蒙古のあら野に朽つるとも日本男子の品は落さじ』

と辞世を詠み、銃弾の吾胸に飛来するを待つた。それから井上兼吉は、

『早く討たぬか、俺の胸を討て、下手な打ちやうをすると弾丸が余計いつて損がいくぞ、見事一発で俺を打殺せ』

等と怒鳴つてゐる。日出雄は又、

『いざさらば天津御国にかけ上り日の本のみか世界を守らむ

日の本を遠く離れて我は今蒙古の空に神となりなむ』

等と辞世を七回迄詠み、大日本帝国万歳、大本万歳を三唱した。真澄別は、

『どうか止を得ざれば大先生と井上とを助けて下さい。井上は屹度先生を目的地へ連れて行く事が出来ませう。私が身代りになります』

と祈願してゐた。さうかうするうち銃殺は止めになつて、又もや兵士が日出雄一行を引立てて通遼公署付属の監獄へ連れて行つた。一々堅固なる足枷をはめ、手には手枷をはめ、二人づつ繋いで尚其上に麻縄にて六人を一つに縛り、窓を通して外の材木に括りつけ、厳重な死刑囚の取扱ひをした。
