Chapter 8: The Sacred Hero and the Worldly Hero (Seiyū to Eiyū) [8]

聖雄と英雄〔8〕

§ 1

Leaving the Japanese Concession in Hōten, where the cold moon shone brightly and a fierce wind blew, two automobiles sped straight toward the official residence of the Tōsanshiyō Army Lieutenant General, Rosenkuwai. It was a meeting between Minamoto no Hideo, nicknamed the Great Monster and former assistant to the leader of Ōmoto, and Rosenkuwai, a Lieutenant General, hero of Mōko, and a great chieftain of the bandits. Masumiwake, Okazaki Tetsushiyu, Karakuniwake, Sasaki Yaichi, Ōkura Goichi, Yōsuitei, Moritaka, and Nadahiko sat in a circle around a stove placed in the center of Rosenkuwai's residence, leaning back in their chairs, and indulged in heroic deliberations regarding an advance into Mōko. As Sasaki was proficient in the Chinese language, he served as an interpreter between Hideo and Rosenkuwai.

Sasaki pointed to Rosenkuwai and said, 'Sensei, this is Rosenkuwai-san.'

Hideo: 'Indeed, even at a glance, he is a heroic figure with a sharp gaze. If it is this man, there is no need to say anything; I shall entrust everything to him. Masumiwake-san, what do you think?'

He looked back at Masumiwake. Masumiwake smiled and replied, 'That would be fine.' Rosenkuwai said to Hideo:

Rosenkuwai: 'Ten years ago, I led seventy thousand elite soldiers and occupied the city of Daikurin. I was twenty-nine years old at that time. Since then, I have taken Xinjiang and campaigned as far as Yunnan. I have also participated in the Hōchoku War. When I was in Shanghai, I met Sun Yixian and heard of your great reputation, Sensei; I have always longed for an opportunity to meet you. It seems the time has ripened, and to be able to meet you at my humble home today is the greatest honor for me. Please, use me under your command. I shall surely act in a way that fulfills your objectives and show you the results.'

Hideo: 'Let us work together with one heart and one strength for the survival and development of East Asia.'

The exchange between the two ended there. That the hero of the religious world and the hero of the bandit world should share their innermost thoughts and plan a project unprecedented in history seems to carry a novelistic flavor.

Hideo, Moritaka, and the interpreter Ō Genki decided to stay at Rosenkuwai's residence, while the others headed toward the Mizunari Shōkai and other locations. The moonlight of the eleventh day of the first month was tilting toward the western sky.

On February 16th, a meeting of patriots was held at Rosenkuwai's residence regarding the organization of a relief army for Inner and Outer Mōko. Around eight o'clock in the evening, Karakuniwake, Masumiwake, and other patriots reported the outline of the conference. The result of the conference was, first, to obtain the understanding of Zhang Zuolin, to purchase weapons, and to establish the Ōmoto Lamaist religion; Hideo would become the Dalai Lama, Masumiwake would become the Panchen Lama, and they would enter Mōko with Rosenkuwai in their retinue.

Originally, Mōko is a dependency of China. And the one hundred and six Mōko kings perform alternate-year attendance in Beijing. It is not permitted by treaty for Japanese people to open a Japanese religion in Chinese territory. However, he was a high-ranking missionary of the Five Great Religions, a new religion of China. Therefore, it was his public right to propagate religion in Mōko. While there are many Buddhists and Shintoists from Japan who have crossed into China to conduct missionary work, it is limited to Japanese residents. Propagating religion to the Chinese people is not permitted. And the extent of it is merely instilling religion in a portion of the Japanese residents. Because he, Hideo, was a missionary of the Five Great Religions, he was in a position to easily conduct religious propagation, which was most convenient for this current enterprise. As a result of the deliberations, at the command of Rosenkuwai, Yōsuitei rushed to Beijing to prepare Lamaist robes and accessories.

The Japanese man Inoue Kanekichi, acting on the orders of Rosenkuwai and others, went out toward Suiyuan and Guihuacheng to report Rosenkuwai's mobilization for the entry into Mōko to the remaining leaders of the Gelaohui, such as Yō Seigyō, and to seek their support. Yō Seigyō was a major leader of the Gelaohui, possessed seventeen or eighteen thousand subordinates, and swore to provide the utmost support for Rosenkuwai's current grand undertaking.

Iushi were running about in preparation for the advance into Mōko, and a great vitality surged forth as some rushed to Pekin, some served as envoys to Mōko, and others returned to Nitsupon.

Passing into the twenty-eighth day of the second month, the twenty-fourth day of the first month of the thirteenth year of the Mingoku, the order for the expedition into inner and outer Naiguwaimōko was finally issued by the Commander-in-Chief of the three eastern provinces, Chiyōsakurin, to General Rosenkuwai.

The comrades were filled with joy and excitement, and as if it were a new opportunity, they flew in all directions, eager to set about the various preparations at once.

It was decided to organize the army into ten brigades, to use the emblem of the Ōmoto Kōshikai, with the sun, earth, moon, and stars dyed upon it, as the banner, and to have military flags and command flags embroidered with the numbers from the first to the tenth brigade.

Furthermore, it was determined to prepare sacred flags without characters, dyed with the sun, earth, moon, and stars, as the Ōmoto Lamaist flags.

In every instance, their spirits soared to the heavens, and they felt as if they had already swallowed up the heavens and earth of Man-Mō.

While he, Minamoto no Hinodeo, was staying at the official residence of Rosen, he composed a poem as a trial.

Zuirei attained enlightenment at the age of twenty-eight, manifested at Riyōshiuzan in Nitsushiu, and at the age of thirty, reappeared at Misenzan, manifesting as the thirty-three-fold Konohanasakuyahime, and as the Shiurei of Tenkyōzan, and as Saishiyōmeuniyorai, preached the Kushifumetsudau at Maruyama at the age of fifty-two, and revealed the Dauhofureisetsu. At the age of fifty-four, he manifested in Mōko, where the Butsuen is deepest, corrected the Jiyakiyoku of Gendaibutsupofu, established the Shinsei Butsukyō, and caused all sentient beings to dwell in peace in the heavenly pure land, with the spirits of Ananda and other disciples of the Buddha following him. 私の脳裡の 暗黒から明るみへ 勇ましく雄々しく 煙の様な 期待が流れて ぐるぐる廻る 走馬燈のやうに 聖地母上妻子 弟妹愛児 数多の信徒 あゝそれは 私の過去の断片だ 半世の俤だ 現世の縮図だ さて今日から 張り替へる 走馬燈は 随分世界の 見物だらう。
