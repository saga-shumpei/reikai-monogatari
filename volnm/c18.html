Chapter 18: The Mongolian Character and Spirit (Mōko Kishitsu) [18]

蒙古気質〔18〕

§ 1

The religion of Mōko is entirely Ramakyō, and in every household, a Butsudan is worshipped with Teichiyō. Ramadera are all built in the Chibetsutoshiki, and unlike the Waishō and Hinjiyaku Mōkojin, they stand Kizen, towering into the clouds; when gazed upon from Enpau, they appear as if rows of Ritsupa Yōkuwan are standing there. In one Ramameu, the smallest has Sanbiyakunin, and the largest has seventy or eighty thousand Rama, who, with the temple as their Chiushin, have built Ritsupa Kiyotaku that differ from Futsūminka, forming a Daishigai. In the case of the Ramameu where the living Buddha of Daikurin, who rebelled against the Shinaseifu Sennen ago and declared Dokuritsu to become the Mōkokuōtei, resides, three hundred thousand Rama monks have lined up many dwellings and live there. At present, the rank of the emperor and the authority of the great living Buddha have become entirely nominal, and the Red Army of Russia is behaving as it pleases. Furthermore, Daikurin has a population of one million seven or eight hundred thousand, and it is said that several Japanese also reside there. In addition, about twenty thousand people from England, America, France, and Russia live there, and while churches of the Yasō religion have been built, it is said that there is not a single Mōkojin believer. What is called Ramakyō is a popular name; Rama means monk in the Mōko language, and in truth, it is correctly called the Buddha religion. In Mōko, the living Buddha holds a higher position than the kings of various regions, and the trust and respect of the people are far higher than for the kings. Mōko is so called the land of the Rama that more than one-fourth of the total population are Rama. All of them are merely ignorant and uneducated peddler-monks, and even the living Buddha, who is the object of respect for the Mōkojin, often uses his position to commit adultery with many women, suffers from syphilis, and spreads the virus.

The general Mōkojin have a strong sense of chastity, and there is not a particle of shameful misconduct such as adultery. Furthermore, although it is polygamy, many wives live together in one small house, and not the slightest jealousy or quarreling occurs. Perhaps due to the climate and the influence of simple food, the Mōkojin seem to be people who do not have much interest in sensuality.

In contrast to this, the high-ranking monks who are in a position to save all living beings are actively engaging in misconduct and have become the ringleaders of the corruption of public morals. However, the Mōkojin do not blame the living Buddha for his misconduct in the slightest. They rather seem to welcome it, saying that a daughter who has been touched by the living Buddha can marry a Ritsupa husband due to her Buddhist connection.

Hideo had come to the depths of Mōko, thousands of miles away, and was respected by its people as if he were a god, receiving the utmost hospitality, and he rejoiced that it was entirely thanks to the Lord God. The divine virtue of Hideo was brilliant, like the rising sun, and he spent interesting days surrounded by Mōkojin from near and far. For a time, there was a tendency for him to be treated somewhat coldly out of fear of the Shinaseifu of the old In-kun and others, but this was limited only to the old In-kun and two or three other officials serving in the Kōyafu, and he did not receive any cold treatment from the general public. Also, compared to the cunning of the Naichijin and the Shinajin, the Mōkojin are truly innocent and artless; their temperament is like that of children, like people of the age of the gods. Thinking that there is such a heaven in an evil world like the present one, one feels happy that there is still living life in the world.

Now, on April 14, as the commander-in-chief of the Northwest Autonomous Army, Ro Senkai led two hundred of his own soldiers, rode in a palanquin, and arrived safely at Kōyafu. Ro immediately entered the temporary headquarters and visited Hideo's lodging on his way. When Hideo heard that Ro had come and went out to the gate to welcome him, Ro Senkai embraced Hideo in front of many soldiers and wept with joy. Tears of deep emotion were also welling up in Hideo's eyes. Then, Ro Senkai presented Hideo with a pure white riding horse sent by the Chinkokukō, and also offered many sweets and fruits to comfort his traveler's heart.

蒙古の宗教は皆喇嘛教で戸毎に仏壇を鄭重に祀つてゐる。そして喇嘛寺は凡て西蔵式に建てられ、矮小な貧弱な蒙古人に似ず、巍然として雲に聳へ、遠方より凝視すれば恰も立派な洋館が立並んだやうに見える。さうして一つの喇嘛廟には、最も少いのが三百人、多いのになると七八万人の喇嘛が廟を中心として、普通民家とは変つた立派な居宅を構へて大市街をなしてゐる。先年支那政府に背いて独立を宣言し、蒙古皇帝となつた大庫倫の活仏が住んで居る喇嘛廟の如きは、三十万の喇嘛僧が沢山な住宅を並べて住んでゐる。現今では皇帝の位＠も大活仏の権威も全然有名無実になつて了ひ、露西亜の赤軍が自由自在に我儘を振舞つてゐる。さうして大庫倫には一百七八十万の人口があつて、日本人も数名住つてゐると云ふ事である。それから英、米、仏、露の人間が二万許り住居し、ヤソ教の教会堂も建つて居るが、蒙古人の信者は一人もないと云ふ事である。喇嘛教と云ふのは俗称であつて、喇嘛は蒙古語の僧侶といふ意味で、その実は仏陀教と云ふのが正当である。蒙古では各地の王様よりも活仏の方が上位に居り、国民の信用も尊敬も王様に比して非常に高い。蒙古は喇嘛の国と云はれる程あつて、総人口の四分の一以上は喇嘛である。何れも暗愚な無学な売主坊主計りであつて、蒙古人の尊敬の的となつてゐる活仏でさへも、自分の地位を利用し、沢山な女を姦し、梅毒に悩んで、病毒の伝播を行つて居るのが多い。

一般の蒙古人は貞操の念強く、有夫姦等の忌はしい醜行は微塵も無い。さうして一夫多妻であり乍ら、狭い一つの家に沢山の女房が一所に暮して居て、少しも悋気喧嘩が起らないのである。気候の故と淡白な食物の影響であらうが、蒙古人は余り色情等には趣味を有たぬ人間らしい。

それに引替へ衆生済度の地位にある高僧連は盛んに醜行をなし、風俗壊乱の首魁者となつてゐる。然し乍ら蒙古人は活仏の醜行に対しては少しも咎めない。活仏のお手が掛つた娘は仏縁に依つて立派な夫に嫁しづく事が出来ると云つて寧ろ歓迎してゐる風である。

日出雄は数千里を隔てた蒙古の奥へ来て、其人民からは神の如くに尊敬され、心限りの待遇を受けて、全く大神様のおかげだと喜んで居た。日出雄の神徳は赫々として旭日昇天の如く、遠近の蒙古人に取囲まれて面白き月日を送つてゐた。一時老印君等の支那政府に憚つて稍冷遇をされた傾きがあつたが、之は老印君其外公爺府に仕へて居る二三の役員のみに限つたので、一般人からは少しも冷遇は受けなかつた。又内地人や支那人の狡猾なるに比べて蒙古人は真に天真爛漫、その性情は子供の如く、神代の人の如くである。現代の如き悪化した世の中に、こんな天国があるかと思へば、まだ世の中に活きた生命のある事を楽しく思はれるのである。

さて四月十四日、西北自治軍総司令上将として盧占魁は二百人の手兵を引率し、轎車に乗つて無事公爺府に到着した。盧は直ぐ様仮司令部に入り、其足で日出雄の宿舎を訪ねて来た。日出雄は盧が来たと云ふので門口に出迎へると、盧占魁は大勢の兵士の前で日出雄に抱きついて嬉し泣きに泣いた。日出雄の目にも感慨無量の涙が浮んでゐた。それから盧占魁は鎮国公から送られた純白の乗馬を日出雄に送り、且つ沢山の菓子や果物をすすめて旅情を慰めた。

After that, Masumiwake took over and it was decided that all administrative matters would be discussed with Lu. Hideo would compose songs, write poems, keep a diary, and preach the teachings of Kami to the Rama and the villagers in his uncertain Mōkogo.

There was a small house near the Konefu, and its master was named Utsutaarita, who had two wives. He had one son and two daughters, and the eldest daughter was named Utsutagura. When Hideo passed by the front gate, the master would beckon him insistently, saying:

Utsutaarita: 'Mondoyū, ihoemito, porohana, irujī irujī'

As he entered the small Mōkohau with Pairinku, the master said:

Utsutaarita: 'Today, I have invited about twenty Ramasō to offer them a feast, so I would like you, the Porohana of Naranuorosu, to eat first.'

He then urged him to eat dumplings made of American flour with mutton filling, boiled over a fire of Giufun on a stove. Hideo was troubled by being offered dumplings with a strange odor, but he could not refuse the kindness of the Mōko people, so he gratefully stuffed two or three into his mouth. The wife of the house kept pouring tea and bringing more dumplings to offer him. Hideo said:

Hideo: 'I cannot eat any more, as my stomach is full.'

He declined politely and kept putting tea and tobacco into his dry mouth. The daughter of this house, Utsutagura, was fourteen years old that year and was a rare beauty, being tall for her age.

The name Utsutagura means the most beautiful woman in the Orient. She possessed a dignity that seemed to come from nowhere, with fair skin and cool eyes, appearing just like Kanzeon Bosatsu. Hideo said to this young girl:

Hideo: 'Chinni sēna honmon (Thou, beautiful woman).'

Upon hearing this praise, her father immediately said to Hideo:

Utsutaarita: 'Pīshiya, muttorute, chinnin, utsutagura, shyarutogeya.'

The meaning of this was:

Utsutaarita: 'You are a distinguished person. I shall give my daughter Utsutagura to you.'

Hideo did not answer anything and returned home laughing. Then, from the next day, the girl changed from her tattered clothes into fine garments and came to Hideo's side, serving him by pouring tea and wringing out handkerchiefs in hot water, attending to him without regard for herself. When he inquired carefully, he was told that her parents thought, 'Since he is a living Buddha from Japan, he surely has no wife or children. If we give him this daughter, he will surely marry her off to a suitable place as his own child.' Mōko people have a habit of saying, 'I will give you my child,' when they see Japanese people. The Japanese party was also feeling troubled by the kindness of being told, 'Shall I give you a child?' here and there.

One day, about thirty Ramas gathered at the house next to Utsutanasuto, and from around six in the morning until evening, they were chanting sutras incessantly, 'Dada-butsu, dada-butsu.' Hideo, feeling suspicious, entered the house to peek inside and saw that the Ramas were praying with all their might, with a seriously ill person placed in the center. The patient was only suffering more and more and showed no signs of recovery. The Ramas said:

Ramas: 'We are praying for his end to come quickly so that he may be saved in heaven and relieved of the suffering of his illness by changing his country as soon as possible.'

Thereupon, Hideo turned to the master of the house and said:

Hideo: 'Shall I heal this illness immediately?'

The master bowed his head low and begged for his prayers. Hideo immediately bowed to the many Ramas, placed his hand lightly on the patient's forehead, and shouted, 'Evil spirit, begone!' Instantly, the high fever broke, and the patient sat up vigorously on the spot and began to laugh loudly in joy.

The Ramasō were astonished at this miraculous sign and came to respect Hideo all the more as a great living Buddha. Because Moritaka and Natahiko were boasting of their jujutsu from morning till night without pause, Okazaki and Ōgenki were becoming angry. When Natahiko grabbed Okazaki's hand and said boastfully, 'This is what jujutsu is,' Okazaki flared up in anger and threw a metal washbasin containing urine at Natahiko's face. Natahiko was extremely mortified, but fearing Okazaki's vehemence and being calmed by Hideo, he suppressed his anger while grinding his teeth. Thereafter, because Pairinku would listen to various requests from Hideo and Masumiwake but would not listen to others, the Japanese side came to hate Pairinku greatly, and Toshio Ino and others struck Pairinku's face hard with a piece of wood. Instantly, his face swelled up and blood began to ooze out. Pairinku held his face, crouched down, and shed tears while enduring the pain. Although Pairinku's father was nearby in the same Konefu, Pairinku did not try to tell his father about this violent treatment by the Japanese and sat crying without moving a step. Hideo, unable to watch any longer, sprayed shōchū on Pairinku's face and performed chinkon; within about thirty minutes, the swelling subsided and his face returned to its original state. Hideo took Pairinku to the mountain behind the house under the pretext of a walk and, in his uncertain Mōkogo, said:

'It is admirable that you did not go to inform your parents even after being treated so terribly by Inokun.'

Upon being praised thus, Haku replied with joy:

'Since I have become a Kerai of Daisensei, I can no longer rely on my father. And as I intend to become a Deshi of Sensei and become a Rama, I will obey the orders of Masumiwake-dono, who is the Dairi of Sensei, but I have no Gimu to obey the orders of other Nitsuponjin. Even if a Nitsuponjin were to get angry and kill me, I would not accept the orders of a person who is not on the right path. To do such a thing would be a disgrace to a Mōkodanshi.'

Hideo was impressed and praised Haku, and then he told him about the Fūzoku and Shifukuwan of Nitsupon:

'What you say may be reasonable as a Mōkojin, but such Rikutsu does not pass with Nitsuponjin, so when there is time, I would like you to listen to what other Nitsuponjin say and let them provide you with Sewa.'

Haku agreed, and thereafter he came to accept what Tarekare said without Kubetsu.

Haku's father, Pairichiu, came and requested politely:

'As this boy is my Hitorimusuko, I do not wish to send him too far away. And since it has been decided that Pairinku will take a bride from the neighboring village, could you somehow manage to let him be free?'

Hearing the father's words, Hideo felt pity and said to Pairinku:

'You are a Hitorimusuko, and your father is also elderly, so accompanying the army to Daikurin might become an act of unfilial piety toward your parents. And even if you were to go on an expedition leaving your parents and wife behind, you would not be at ease, so follow your father's words.'

When he said this, Haku shook his head from side to side and said:

'No, no. Once a Mōkodanshi has pledged his word, it is as firm as gold and iron. It is fine to leave my father and wife to the gods. I will accompany Daisensei wherever he goes, no matter how far.'

As he would not listen, his father seemed to have resigned himself, and laughing loudly, 'Ahahahaha,' he said:

'Please take good care of my son.'

He greeted him and went home, and until the morning Hideo departed from Kōyafu, his father did not come to visit again. Seeing this, one can understand the masculine temperament of the Mōkojin.

Perhaps because he possessed such a disposition, Haku received the protection of the gods; even at the time of the disaster at Hakuontara on the twenty-first of June, he was captured by Shina soldiers and, at the very moment he was made to stand at the place of execution, the Chief of Staff appeared and said:

'There is no point in killing a child like this.'

Saying this, he let Haku escape. From there, Haku endured various hardships and sufferings, and was able to return safely to Kōyafu with nothing.

と云つて聞かないので、父も観念したと見え、『アハヽヽヽヽ』と大きく笑つて、

と挨拶して帰つたきり、日出雄が公爺府を出立する朝迄、その父は訪ねて来なかつた。之を見ても蒙古人の男性的気性が知れるのである。

彼白はかう云ふ心掛を有つて居たから神の保護を受けたものか、六月二十一日の白音太拉の遭難の時も支那兵に捕へられ、銃殺の場に立たされた一刹那、参謀長が出て来て、

『こんな子供を殺した所が仕方が無い』

と云つて白を逃がしてやつた。それより白は色々と艱難辛苦して、無一物で公爺府へ無事帰る事を得たのである。
