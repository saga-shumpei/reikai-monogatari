Chapter 29: The Day of the Tango Festival (Tango no Hi) [29]

端午の日〔29〕

§ 1

The march continued toward the Seihoku, crossing mountains, traversing valleys, and cutting across the Kaugen. Suddenly, a magnificent Kauzan appeared lying ahead. It is a characteristic of the Tairiku that one often reaches the summit before realizing it, due to the Kuwankōbai that makes one wonder if the mountain could even be crossed on horseback.

When it became the Rokuguwatsu Itsuka, for some reason, the Shinro was suddenly shifted toward the Nanpau. Voices of Fushin arose from the Nitsuponjingaha, asking, 'Is the Haukau not wrong? Where are we going? Are we not going to the Seichi of Kōanrei?' However, as the Senpō had already advanced far ahead, it was difficult for those with Chirifuannai to engage in Jiiukaudō.

The scenery of the Shii had changed before they knew it; as far as the eye could see, it appeared to be the site of a volcanic eruption, and passing through the solidified lava or volcanic ash was a spectacle that words could not fully describe. While on horseback, Okazaki called out to Hideo:

Okazaki: 'Sensei, is this not a massive volcano?'

Hideo: 'Indeed, it is truly magnificent.'

Masumi: 'Sensei, the volcano of Aso is also large-scale, and it is truly a spectacle as geologists call it the world's greatest volcano, but is this place not even larger in scale? It seems to be a place with some sort of history.'

Hideo: 'Indeed, this is a part of the Tenpōzan found in the first volume of the Reikai Monogatari; perhaps it has not yet been introduced to the world because the footsteps of geologists have not reached it.'

Masumi: 'I had been counting in my heart that this Mongolian expedition contains much of the realization of the Reikai Monogatari, and thanks to this, I have understood one more thing.'

Conversing in such a manner, they headed toward the Tairiku Jiiu Hotel with grass for a mattress and stars for a quilt.

Around noon on the Rokuguwatsu Muika, the fifth day of the fifth month by the lunar calendar, Hideo and his party were, needless to say, indulging in casual conversation while eating various provisions such as canned goods and cakes made of American flour amidst a vast grassy field surrounded by a screen of mountains.

As this day was the auspicious day of the fifth of the fifth month, the executive members took a commemorative photograph, and each corps chose an appropriate position, gathering under their unit flags to fulfill their duties of protection from afar.

Okazaki: 'In such a wide field as this, with a river flowing through the center, and judging by the state of the grass, the soil seems good; I think it would be possible to create excellent paddy fields.'

Moritaka: 'Even judging from my experience in engaging in land reclamation while I was in Hokkaido, excellent paddy fields can be made. Among the parts of Mongolia I have traveled to until today, the vast fertile plains west of the Kōyōfu are lying idle. Even judging from the feeling of the climate and natural features, anything that can be grown in Hokkaido can be grown here; it is a pity.'

Sakamoto: 'If I could be given this much, I would be satisfied. I would make half into paddy fields and vegetable gardens, and half into a pasture; since a Mongolian tent made of sheepskin and my favorite rice would be enough, I would like to live here together.'

Masumi: 'Sakamoto-san, assuming the rice is sent later, why don't you be the first to remain here alone and start the preparations? Hahaha.'

Sakamoto: 'Hahahaha, well, as long as you acknowledge my priority rights, that is fine.'

Masumi: 'It is truly a pity that people are snarling at each other in cramped places when such a great heaven and earth, with no restrictions or interference, lies so abundantly waiting for the arrival of humanity. By the way, Mandakan-san, is there no good mine in this vicinity? I want to have Zhao Che-san earn one hundred million yen quickly, ahaha.'

Mandakan: 'Well, I do not know well, but there are occasionally crystal caves in the deserts of Outer Mongolia. Also, I have heard that there are ruby rocks in the places where the trees are dense at the water source of the central volcanic range, but I have not yet been there.'

Sakamoto: 'I have heard that there is a solid gold Buddha statue about the size of a twelve or thirteen-year-old child in the Lama temple of Outer Mongolia, so I would like to have at least that one.'

§ 2

Inōhe: 'Sakamoto-san, it is troublesome to transport such heavy things even if you receive them now. Rather than that, try going to Shinkiyan; there are large grains of gold dust rolling about everywhere.'

Rosenkuwai: 'I believe Shinkiyan is the treasure house of the world. When you break and lift the hard, ice-like snow in the mountain valleys, it is not rare to find places where gold dust of about eighteen-karat quality is stuck to the underside of the snow.'

Hideo: 'From what I see in the Reikai, in the areas from the Arutai region to the Chibetsutozakahi border of Shinkiyan, there are many things hidden that should be called gold rocks rather than gold dust. It is not just minerals; Shinkiyan is a place essential to the Keirin of Kami, and it is generally a land rich in Tenkei.'

Masumi: 'Sensei, it is just as shown in the Goshin-yu. It is quite troublesome because there are many people who do not believe in Kami until they see the actual place.'

Hideo: 'That is why it is hard work for Kami.'

Rosenkuwai: 'However, unless you have someone who knows the way guide you when entering Shinkiyan, there are strange deserts, and if you carelessly step into them, you will sink deep into them along with your horses and men.'

Masumi: 'Sensei, is the place that Rosenkuwai-san just mentioned not the place explained in the chapter on the Arutai region in Volume Ten of the Reikai Monogatari?'

Hideo: 'That seems to be the case.'

While the conversation was being exchanged one after another, Chief Medical Officer Ino came running while clutching a large block of ice in his hands.

Ino: 'Sensei, I have found this ice. It is sufficiently thawed up to about three shaku underground, but at the six or seven shaku mark, it is still like this. I entered a crack in the ground at the edge of the river and went to great pains to break it off.'

The group quenched their thirst with the fragments of ice provided by the thoughtful efforts of Medical Officer Ino and continued their march once again. From the edge where the Aono-ga-hara ended, they advanced through a field of flowers that stretched as far as the eye could see. Yellow flowers resembling Fukujusō, flowers resembling purple Chinese milk vetch, and purple flowers resembling irises were each vying for beauty in red, yellow, white, and purple.

Masumiwake: 'As the moonlight emerges into the world, The kingdom of the spiritual realm, Shall open in the eastern land. The sun of truth, brilliantly, Shining across, eternally, The pure water of life that never ends, Overflowing at the roots of the lower rocks, The rain of compassion pours down. The incomparable, solemn light, To illuminate the souls of the people of the world, Has appeared; O people, Awaken as soon as you can. Hear from the countries in all directions, Hear the voice of the true Kami. You who thirst for the water of the spirit, Be moistened by the divine spirit of Mizu.'

While singing this, Masumiwake was the one advancing grandly on horseback, as if the wide flower field belonged to him, trailing more than ten chō behind the main unit. Sakamoto, following behind, let out a voice of admiration, saying, 'That is truly so,' and began to sing with an interesting melody, saying it was a song currently popular in the interior.

Sakamoto: 'I am going, so you go too, I am tired of living in narrow Japan. Beyond the waves lies China, In China, four hundred million people wait. Yesterday in the east, today in the west, My body is like floating grass. Alone in the endless field, Looking up at the moon, using grass as a pillow. This body with skin like a jewel, Now bears spear wounds and sword cuts. This is what a true man is, With a needle-like beard on a smiling face. I have no father or mother, And no home in my birthplace. Though there are mountains I have known for years, There is no one to mourn my parting. Only my pitiful lover, And my friends from childhood, Where do they live? Now, only, In dreams do I cherish their figures. In the morning wind of the Kōan-rei, Raising my sword and looking down, The great plains of North Manchuria, Are still too narrow for my dwelling. More than ten years since I left my country, Now I am a great horse bandit of Mongolia. From between the high peaks of Asia, Five thousand subordinates emerge. Letting the hooves of the horses be silent, The moon slips out from between the clouds, Tomorrow we shall raid the Fengtian Prefecture, Illuminating the Gobi Desert.'

By the time the flower field ended and they arrived at a valley between mountains on three sides, the sky began to cloud over gloomily, and finally, the clouds broke and turned into rain. The sun had already begun to set, and ahead, mountain upon mountain appeared, layered one after another. However, with this rain, dreaming of camping was out of the question. At this very moment, Hideo climbed onto a small, high rock and began to recite the Kamigoto; the entire sky cleared up instantly, and the five-day-old moon began to emit a brilliant, divine light in the western sky. Here, Hideo's party felt at ease and began preparations for sleep, but whatever military council had been concluded among the staff officers, a report came to start a night march immediately, so the party began to march again while grumbling, and before long, the sky became one of drizzling rain again. Hideo said, 'It is no longer our responsibility,' and Masumiwake did not try to pray separately; in the end, they were struck by a heavy downpour, and the entire army had no choice but to spend the night in the shadows of the rocks in the mountain valley. At this time, Chō Hikozō sighed and lamented greatly, saying, 'Even though Sensei went to the trouble of stopping the rain, the commander changed the encampment without permission, so we received divine punishment.'

花野も尽きて三方山の谷間に着いた頃、空はどんよりと曇り始め、遂に雲は綻びて雨となつた。日は既に傾きかけた上、前途は山又山が重畳と折重なつて見えてゐる。併し此雨では野営の夢を結ぶなどは思ひも寄らぬ事である。此時こそはと、日出雄は小高き岩上に登り立ち神言を奏上し初むるや、一天ガラリと晴れ渡り、五日の月西天に玲瓏たる慈光を放ち初めた。茲に日出雄一行も心を安じ、就寝の準備にかかると、如何なる軍議が参謀の間に纏りしか、引続き夜間行軍開始の報告が来たので、一行は呟き乍ら又行進し始めると、間もなく再び小雨そぼ降る空となつた。日出雄は『最早吾々の責任でない』と云ひ、真澄別も別に祈願しようとしない、終に豪雨に見舞はれて、全軍山間の岩影に夜を明すの已むを得ざる事となつた。此時張彦三は『先生が折角雨を止めて下さつたのに司令が無断で宿営地を変更したから神罰を受けたのだ』と吐息を漏らして大に歎じた。
