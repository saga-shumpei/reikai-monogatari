Chapter 36: The Heavenly Rock Cave (Ame no Iwato) [36]

天の岩戸〔36〕

§ 1

Having finished a journey of Ikiyōbanri, and bearing the rather unwelcome Akumei of Sekifutorikeshi for Fukei and Shinbunshihafuihan, I finally came into contact with the Fūkuō of Nitsupon once more. Being met from Harubaru by Jiyunshin Yakuin and Shinto, I was sent to the Mojisuijiyōkeisatsushiyo and received a Shitsumon of mere Keishiki. As I sat in an Isu by the north window on the Sankai of the Mojishiyo where the Kazeata was good, puffing on a Tabako and looking down at the Kaidau below, a multitude of Shinto in Haorihakama or Yōfukusugata formed a line on the Daidau and were looking up at Jibunra. Among them, some were even in Kiyoki out of the excess of their joy. Hideo thought within his heart,

Hideo: 'Ah, although the Shinto are welcoming me like that, I am not even given time to speak leisurely of the tales of Mongolia; I am sent from police station to police station, and within two or three days, I must become one imprisoned in pre-trial detention at the Wakamatsu Prison of Naniwae—be it for good or ill—rather than the auspicious Wakamatsu-sama. That I planned an unprecedented and unrepeatable great undertaking and was unfortunately forced to return home midway is an inevitable matter as the Divine Providence of the Spirit World. Also, I have no particular great worry. However, what must be the feelings within the hearts of my wife and children, my brothers, and the Yakuin and believers?'

As he thought such things, ten thousand emotions filled his breast, and before he knew it, tears welled up in both eyes. Regaining his composure by his own hand,

Hideo: 'Eh, how ridiculous! With such a weak spirit, how can I serve the Divine Work of saving the world? Relatively speaking... though human beings seem strong, they are weak things, aren't they?'

While thus rebuking and mocking his own heart and waiting for time to pass, the Mojishiyo—perhaps thinking it would be a grave matter if he escaped again—drove the many believers toward the station, and in a hurried manner, placed Hideo, Masumiwake, and others onto a small steamboat and delivered them to the Shimonoseki Station. Upon arriving at the Shimonoseki Station, not a single person who appeared to be a believer had come. After a short rest, when he became a passenger in an automobile escorted by three Shimonoseki officers, Naohi was waiting at the gate of the police station.

The watermelons, muskmelons, bananas, apples, and such at the greengrocer's, seen for the first time since returning to the mainland, gave off a delicious and fragrant scent that greatly stimulated the nose that had wandered the wilds of Mongolia. Although he thought, 'I would like to eat one watermelon after such a long time,' he restrained himself, thinking it would be bad to express such selfishness.

When he arrived at the station, Shibata Kenjirō-san came running in a hurry, all alone, while dripping with sweat. When he boarded the third-class carriage with the escorting policemen, it was packed with Naohi, Chairman Inoue, Higashio, Yuasa, and other Yakuin and Shinto. Subsequently, staying overnight at the lockups of police stations such as Ōtake and Kamigōri, they headed toward Ōsaka. At the Ōtake Police Station, he had water boiled and performed a bath in a washtub. Seeing that his body weight had suddenly decreased to fifteen kan and five hundred me, his shoulder bones were sharp, and his ribs were prominently visible, Katō Akiko, who had come to wash his back—oh, what a shameful sight—could not help but be a bit taken aback by her crying. In Kamigōri, he spent the night in a new lockup together with Masumiwake, and upon arriving at Kōbe Station, many people came to meet him and offered greetings one by one. Nidai and Uchimaru had been together with him since Kamigōri Station. After passing through the Aioibashi Station, as soon as he got off at Ōsaka Station, the spectators were like a mountain of ants, and while the motion picture crew of the Mainichi Shinbun and various newspaper companies waited with their lenses pointed, he pushed through the wave of people, rode a rickshaw, and was sent to the Sonezaki Station; then, after being thrown into the lockup of the Tenma Station for about thirty minutes, just as he was about to go on foot from the back gate of the same station to the Wakamatsu Branch, the Ōmoto Yakuin, Shinto, and spectators reached the height of congestion like a mountain. At the entrance of the Branch, again many photography squads from newspaper companies were waiting, making the sound of shutters frequently. As soon as he passed through this gate, he had to part from the beloved Yakuin and Shinto. A feeling as if crawling into an almost dark hole drifted about. He was immediately led to the Branch Manager's room, and after first having his height, body weight, and physical characteristics examined, he changed into a light Yukata that had been sent in advance; in the Branch Manager's informal talk, which was unlike that of an official, he told the stories of the fierce battles and struggles in Mongolia interestingly, making the Manager exclaim in surprise, and was immediately confined in Room 98 on the second floor of the same place. This room had a window open to the north, and since no buildings stood there, the wind was extremely good, and if one looked out the window, one could see as far as the vicinity of Umeda Station; it was the finest first-class room within the Branch. Every single solitary cell was a narrow Western-style prison cell of four shaku in width and a little over seven shaku in length, which was almost less than one tsubo. Within that, one had to spread the Futon, use washwater, place luggage, and perform urination and defecation. Even if one always says with a face of enlightenment, 'Half a tatami mat while awake, one tatami mat while sleeping,' in reality, being thrust into such a place was quite painful. When he thought of how he had slept soundly on the fields of Mongolia that stretched boundlessly to the heavens, with the unobstructed sky as a roof and the limitless earth as a bedding, he suddenly felt as if an elephant had been transformed into a microbe. This year the heat was especially intense, almost to an unbearable degree, and sweat like oil oozed from his entire body; although there was a window, it was open six shaku above, so it was not very cool. 'Well, well, it is Ketsukō; if I remain here, my Inochi is Daijiyōbu. Right now, I am tucked inside a cocoon like a silkworm pupa in such a narrow place, but there is no need to worry about being recklessly thrown into Nieyu and killed; soon I shall break this shell, fukuwa as a butterfly, give birth to Takusan of children, and meet the spring of Saisei once again.'

Making this his Yuiitsu pleasure, he spent Futsuka and Mitsuka. Until now, no Sashiire had been permitted in the Keimushiyo except for Shiyoseki concerning Hafuritsu and Shiyoseki concerning Shiukyō, but from one month Izen, Sashiire of lighthearted Kaudan magazines, Omoshirokurabu, and other things concerning Jiji were permitted, which became very convenient for comforting his Bureu. Also, as the Sashiire of 'Kami no Kuni' and 'Reikaimonogatari' was permitted, he was able to know the Jiyōkiyō of Ōmoto while staying there. However, if there is a good thing, on the Hanmen, there are bad things. He felt extremely frustrated to learn that the Ōmoto officers were turning pale over the debt problem and that new creditors had begun making severe demands; nevertheless, being a captive, he could not help but feel a bit perplexed as he lacked freedom. He was fairly busy in the mornings with meetings with officers, followers, and lawyers; he wrote postcards every other day, had his hair cut once a week, entered the bath about every fourth day, and received medical examinations; meanwhile, he held his brush and inkstone to write poetry and read interesting novels; as the days passed, the number of letters increased, and he began to feel the busyness. For that reason, he did not feel the ninety-eight days of imprisonment to be very long. Along with unconvicted prisoners of murder, rioting, fraud, and theft, he was escorted by a guard every day except Sundays, crossing the long corridor and waiting for his turn at the meeting place. During that time, he heard various interesting stories and was able to know their psychological states thoroughly. Lawyer Adachi came and said that since he had obtained the understanding of the authorities, the permission for bail would likely be granted soon, so he should be at ease. Just as he himself was thinking he wished to leave the prison even one day sooner to return and quickly speak of the Mongolian situation to the officers and followers to set them at ease, a very inconvenient and lengthy document from Mr. ○hara ○rei flew in addressed to Hideo; as a result, the bail finally came to nothing, and he fell into a plight where he had to stay in prison for ninety-eight days. Nevertheless, during that time he practiced mental cultivation, read Hideo's articles such as the Prophecies of Nichiren and the Dream of the Mongolian Kingdom, and looked at the famous-place postcards sent by Takusan of believers; he found it extremely interesting and joyful, forgetting his status as a prisoner, and passed the summer and autumn before he knew it. During his imprisonment, he saw Takusan of interesting dreams. I shall record one or two of them here.

On the night of the fifteenth day of the old seventh month, a goddess of seventeen or eighteen years appeared suddenly, handed me one packet of Asahi cigarettes... and vanished with a smile. After I awoke, I felt that the Iwato would soon open because I had received the Asahi cigarettes. However, it likely meant I should have a quiet smoke and wait for the season, and that I would by no means be able to leave prison within the next five or ten days.

Four or five days after that, I saw a dream where I went on a mission to Hokkaidō and found a large pumpkin field; the owner of the pumpkins plucked the largest among them and gave me two. When I returned to Ayabe after leaving prison, a believer from Hokkaidō brought two pumpkins exactly like those Hideo had seen in the dream four or five days later; I could not help but marvel at the fulfillment of the dream.

In the middle of the new tenth month, there was a hill like Hongū-yama, and while I was walking at the foot of that mountain with my mother, her figure suddenly vanished. Thinking my mother might have hidden inside the mountain, I climbed up from the southern foot of the small hill, pushing through the lush kaya grass, and saw one of the Ōmoto believers working hard to cut down miscellaneous trees for about one chō and leveling the earth to open a path of about three ken. As I walked up there, a great road passed through in a cross shape. In short, the part that had been blocked was only about fifty or sixty ken. I felt that this surely meant bail would be granted and I would be able to leave in the near future.

Next, I found myself having climbed atop an exceedingly high and jagged rocky mountain before I knew it, yet there was no path to descend anywhere; while I was wondering what to do, two white horses appeared, and clenching iron chains in their mouths, they hooked them onto the rock where I stood with a metallic clatter, then returned across the mountain's flank with the speed of a rushing torrent. After I awoke, I felt that there were believers performing activities with the fury of a courageous lion in order to open a path for me.

Next, I saw a dream in which Nakano Ihata-san was desperately leveling the sloping land at the eastern foot of Hongūyama and was orderly planting small pines.

One day, Masumiwake came to visit and spoke.

Masumiwake: 'Sensei, I was shown the number eleven through my spiritual eye; what could this mean?'

Hideo replied to this inquiry.

Hideo: 'Humph, it likely means one in ten; it might mean that nine out of ten chances for bail will not be granted. Then again, if one thinks about it, it might mean that even eleven out of ten will be found innocent.'

So he spoke, laughed, and they parted. However, the date Hideo's bail was decided was the first day of the tenth month of the old calendar, and the time he departed the Wakamatsu Branch Office was at eleven minutes past eleven o'clock in the morning on the first day of the eleventh month of the new calendar. I, who had reduced to a body weight of fifteen kan and five hundred me, had increased in body weight to seventeen kan and six hundred me as a result of ninety-eight days of prison cell life. When I stepped outside the gate, seen off by the Branch Chief and all the officials within the office, hundreds of Ōmoto officers and believers, as well as newspaper reporters and spectators from Osaka City, were lined up along the road like a black mountain. Amidst the drizzling rain falling softly, I entered the Sanukiya Inn, shared a dining table with many believers here, and it was decided that I would return to Ayabe without incident.

Prior to this, the Autumn Grand Festival was held at the Holy Grounds, and the meeting of branch and sub-branch heads began, where quite a heated argument had started regarding the issue of financial reorganization. Just then, a telegram regarding the granting of bail arrived from the lawyer in Osaka, so the disputes up until then vanished like bubbles on water; everyone recited the Kamigoto in unison, and thereafter the whole group gathered together and came to Osaka to receive me.

With this, the great play of the Entry into Mongolia has, for a moment, seen the black curtain fall.

と云つて笑つて別れた。然るに日出雄の保釈が決定したのは旧十月一日であり、若松支所を出たのは新暦十一月一日の午前十一時十一分であつた。十五貫五百目の体量に減じてゐた自分は、九十八日間の監房生活の結果十七貫六百目に体量が増してゐた。支所長始め所内の役人全部に見送られて門内を出ると、大本役員信者数百名、其他新聞記者及び大阪市内の見物人が黒山の如くに沿道に堵列してゐる。小雨がシヨボシヨボと降つてゐる中を、さぬきや旅館に這入り、ここにて数多の信者と食卓を共にし、無事帰綾する事となつた。

これより先、聖地では秋季大祭があり、分所支部長会議が始まり財政整理問題について、かなりの激論が始まつてゐた。そこへ保釈許可の電報が大阪の弁護士からついたので、今迄の争論は水の泡の如く消え、何れも一斉に神言を奏上し、それより一同打揃ふて大阪に迎へに来たのであつた。

これで蒙古入の大芝居も一寸黒幕が下りたやうなものである。
