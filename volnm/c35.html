Chapter 35: Return from the Underworld (Yomigaeri) [35]

黄泉帰〔35〕

§ 1

When Hideo was captured in his sleep at the Kōhinriyokuwan in Paintara on the night of Rokuguwatsu Nijifuichinichi, a certain Nitsuponjin who happened to be staying there looked out into the garden at dawn and found that a single Shiyakushi, used for the Kiguwan of Biyōshiya as a Shinki of Kuōdauōmoto, had been abandoned.

Upon that Shiyakushi, it was inscribed on the front:

This Shiyakushi which saveth the Mitama of Ametsuchi According to my heart's desire I shall save the Yobito

And on the back it was inscribed:

This Shiyakushi doth truly resemble the form of the moon On the Jifuniya when I was born

Oni: '...'

Having discovered this with the Su-boin O-pressed upon it, the person was startled to learn of the Saunan of Hideo's party, and taking the Ichiban Kishiya from Paintara, reported the matter to the Nitsuponriyōjikuwan in Teikaton. At the Riyōjikuwan, they were astonished and decided to dispatch Secretary Tsuchiya in haste. Now, Hideo had all his possessions confiscated under the pretext of being kept at the military barracks, and it is said that Masumiwake and all the others had their money, hats, shoes, belts, and other personal belongings plundered by the Chinese patrolmen. Since those patrolmen had heard that all the Nitsuponjin were to be executed by firing squad that night, they stripped them stark naked with the thought that whatever they took was theirs to keep. Meanwhile, Secretary Tsuchiya arrived in Paintara toward the evening of the Nijifunichi, went to the Tsūryō public office, met with the governor, and negotiated the handover of Hideo's party. Then, early the next morning, Mr. Tsuchiya came to visit the prison where Hideo was confined, kindly comforted him, and departed saying that since the Riyōjikuwan had intervened, his life was safe and he should be at ease.

The night of the Nijifunichi when the secretary arrived in Paintara was somehow restless; the voices of hundreds or thousands of dogs could be heard from all directions, and it seemed as though something was about to break out. As was heard later, before the secretary met with Hideo, preparations were being made to shoot them as Mongolians. However, once it became known to the Nitsuponriyōjikuwan, fearing international repercussions, they did not proceed with the execution that night. Only after the secretary met with Hideo and the others were their ropes finally untied, and each was placed in leg irons; Hideo and Moritaka, Inoue and Hagiwara, Masumiwake and Sakamoto were linked in three pairs by handcuffs, and even when going to the latrine, soldiers with loaded rifles and fixed bayonets stood guard, and patrolmen with loaded guns followed them for both major and minor needs.

The following day, two gentlemen, Tsutomu Ōta, the chairman of the resident Nitsuponjin association, and Shūji Shiga of the Mantetsu office, came to visit, and as a result of various negotiations with the Chinese authorities, finally only the handcuffs were removed. When four or five days had passed, Yoshikuni Hirose arrived from Japan with a certain Ono of the Mizunaya Company, visited the public office to meet Hideo, and entrusted money to both Mr. Ōta and Mr. Shiga to request that all necessary supplies be provided to Hideo's party. Until then, Hideo's party had been leading a constrained prison life, eating Chinese meals of kaoliang rice accompanied by unpalatable miso. When various supplies began to arrive, the Chinese patrol chief would open each tiered box and the like, saying whether it looked delicious or unpalatable, taking a bite or two, and the patrolmen would scramble like hungry ghosts to eat the leftovers of the lunch boxes. Furthermore, when empty bottles of beer, cider, or wine were produced, they would privately entreat Hideo's party to give them the bottles and take them home.

Then, the governor, the prison warden, and others openly demanded bribes, saying, 'We wish to receive an appropriate reward; it was through the mediation of the governor and the prison warden that your lives were saved when you were about to be killed.' Compared to Japanese police officers, their meanness, the baseness of their character, and their gluttony are beyond what people in the home islands could possibly imagine. A certain patrol chief named Wang kindly washed Hideo's grime-covered feet with hot water, massaged his shoulders, and provided kind nursing care.

日出雄が六月二十一日の夜、白音太拉の鴻賓旅館で寝込を捕縛された時、折よく其処に宿つて居た日本人某が、朝になつてふと庭を見ると、皇道大本の神器として病者の祈願に用ゆる杓子が一本遺棄されてあつた。

其杓子には、

天地の身魂を救ふ此杓子心のままに世人救はむ

と表に誌し其裏には、

この杓子我生れたる十二夜の月の姿にさも似たるかな

王仁

と誌し、スの拇印が押捺してあつたのを見つけ出し、驚いて日出雄一行の遭難を知り、白音太拉から一番汽車に乗り、鄭家屯の日本領事館に届けて出た。領事館では驚いて土屋書記生を急行せしむる事となつた。扨て日出雄は総ての所持品を兵営に預ると云ふ名の下に没収され、真澄別外一同は所持金から帽子、靴、帯革其他所有携帯品を支那巡警から掠奪されて仕舞つたさうである。彼巡警等は今晩日本人全部銃殺の刑に処せらるると云ふ事を聞いて居たから、取つたら取得だと云ふ考で、真裸体として了つたのである。一方土屋書記生は二十二日の夕頃に白音太拉に着き、通遼公署に到り、知事に面会し、日出雄一行の引渡しを交渉した。さうして翌早朝土屋氏は日出雄の繋がれて居る監獄へ見舞に来て、親切に慰め、もう領事館から出て来た上は、生命は大丈夫です安心なさいと云ふて帰つて往つた。

書記生が白音太拉に着いた二十二日の夜は、何となく騒がしく、四方八方から数百千とも知れぬ犬の声が聞え、何事か勃発しさうな形勢であつた。後から聞いて見れば、書記生が日出雄に面会する前、蒙古人として銃殺する準備をして居たと云ふ事である。併し乍ら最早日本領事館に判つた以上、国際上後難を怖れ、其夜は決行に至らなかつた。書記生が日出雄等に面会してより漸くにして縄を解き、各々足枷を入れられ、日出雄と守高、井上と萩原、真澄別と坂本と三組、二人づつ手枷で連がれ、便所へ行くにも弾丸をこめた兵が銃剣を擬して警戒し、巡警は弾込め銃を持つて、大小便ともついて来た。

其翌日、居留日本人会長太田勤及び満鉄公所の志賀秀二の二氏が面会に来て、種々支那官憲と交渉した結果、漸くにして手枷のみを解かれる事となつた。四五日経つた時、日本より広瀬義邦が水也商会の小野某と共に公署を訪ひ日出雄に面会し、かつ太田、志賀の両氏に金を預けて日出雄等一行の凡ての差入れを依頼した。それ迄は日出雄一行は支那食の高粱飯に不味い味噌をそへて食ひ、不自由な獄舎生活を送つて居たのである。種々の差入ものが出て来ると、支那の巡警部長は一々折箱などを開けて見ては、之は美味だらうとか、不味だらうとか云つては一口食ひ、二口食ひ、弁当の余剰つたのは餓鬼の如く争ひて食て了ふ。又ビールやサイダー、葡萄酒などの空瓶が出来ると私に呉れ呉れと、日出雄一行に頼み込んで持つて帰るのであつた。

さうして知事や監獄長、其他から、吾々にも相当の謝礼を貰ひ度い、貴方方が殺される所であつたのを、知事や監獄長の斡旋で生命が助かつたのだ、と公然賄賂を要求する。日本の警察官に比ぶれば其卑しい事、品格の下劣なること、口卑しい事など、到底内地人の想像せられぬ程である。巡警部長の王某は親切に日出雄の垢の付いた足を湯で洗ひ、肩を揉みなどして親切に介抱をし、

Ōbuchiyō: 'When Taijin returns to Nitsupon, please take me with you. I wish to quit being a Jiyunkei and become a Shinjiya of Ōmoto, and be allowed to perform even the Nihasauji.'

He pleaded thus. Hideo: 'Once I arrive in Hōten, I will send a Denpau depending on the Moyō. When the Denpau reaches you, come out to Hōten. I shall take you with me to Nitsupon.'

When he said this, Ōbuchiyō was Hijiyō happy. According to Kokusaihafu, when a Riyōjikuwan demands a Hikiwata, the person should be handed over within Nijifuyojikannai; however, the fact that they were kept tied in Paintara for Tōkakan from the night of the Nijifuichinichi until the Sanjifunichi was because the Kausefu between the Nitsuponriyōjikuwan and the Shinakuwanken was Yohodo difficult.

After Shigonichi had passed, the Nitsuponjin Ichidō were brought out to the Hafutei of the Tsūreukenchiji and underwent an investigation through a Japanese interpreter. The main point of this investigation was: 'On Hideo-san's name card, it is written "Susun Khan," but "Khan" is the title of the foremost king of Mongolia. Furthermore, the place of birth is listed as the country of Mongolia, but since Mongolia is within the territory of China, there should be no such country name as the Country of Mongolia. You must have instigated Lu Zhankui to plot Mongolian independence under the guise of religion.' Hideo, remaining calm:

Hideo: 'I am a missionary of the new Chinese religion, Daoyuan. I possess the special privilege of propagating religion in the land of Mongolia. Furthermore, my calling it the Country of Mongolia has no particularly deep meaning. In our country of Nitsupon, there are over eighty country names, and we call small districts by country names such as the Country of Tanba, the Country of Tango, and the Country of Yamashiro. That is why I wrote Mongolia, which is a region of China, as the Country of Mongolia. I intend to establish a religious kingdom not only in Mongolia, but also in Xinjiang, India, Tibet, and China, not to mention through Russia and Siberia to the very heavens and earth of Europe; therefore, I am not obsessed with a small place like Mongolia. Moreover, religion transcends national borders.'

When he stated this, the Governor nodded two or three times and returned Hideo to the prison house. Next, the five members from Masumiwake downward were pulled out one by one and underwent investigation. Masumiwake, becoming indignant, faced the Governor and other interrogating officials:

Masumiwake: 'It is outrageous to call us bandits. You call Lu Zhankui a bandit, but I have accompanied him in action for a long time and observed him, and there was not the slightest bandit-like behavior in him. Rather, did not the Chinese government soldiers plunder our possessed money and steal all our other belongings? Do you call those who commit robbery government soldiers, and those who do not commit robbery bandits? Even Zhang Zuolin and other famous military governors were originally bandits, were they not? If you wish to call me a bandit, so be it. First, I would like you to let me hear the definition of a bandit.'

Hagiwara also said the same things as Masumiwake and caused trouble for the Governor and others.

Next, toward the three—Moritaka, Inoue, and Sakamoto—they strictly interrogated them, asking if they had been carrying weapons. All of them escaped the questioning by saying they were disciples of Hideo-sensei and were religionists, and at last the investigation by the public office was finished. Then, the party of six, while still fitted with shackles, were lined up at the gate and had a single photograph taken; on the following day, June 30th, they were placed on three carts, escorted by six soldiers with fixed bayonets, boarded a train, and were escorted to the Tao-yin of Zhengjiatun. Subsequently, they were placed separately in the detention center and police rooms of the local police training school while still fitted with shackles. There, Yokoo Takayoshi and Iguchi Tōgorō came to visit and comforted the Hideo party. There, they again underwent investigation by the Tao-yin, but the questions asked and the answers given were the same as at Bayantala.

At the dusk of July 5th, it was decided that they would be handed over to the Nitsuponriyōjikuwan in Zhengjiatun. Upon the occasion of parting, more than ten people, including high-ranking officials of the Tao-yin, prepared ink and brushes and begged for Hideo's calligraphy. Hideo pleasantly responded to their Eukiu and, wielding his arm, wrote and gave them dozens of characters, large and small. Around twelve o'clock at night, they passed into the hands of the Nitsuponriyōjikuwan and underwent a preliminary investigation; at the Riyōjikuwan, they used a bath for the first time in a long while to wash off the dirt, spent the night in a detention cell, and on the following day, the 6th, escorted by five people including the police chief of the Riyōjikuwan in ordinary clothes, they were accommodated at the Hōten Consulate General. At that time, Natahiko, Yamada, and Kobayashi had already been accommodated, and after a short while, Ōkura entered, making a total of ten people. As a result of the investigation, the matter was settled with a three-year expulsion order from China.

Prior to this, two people from Nitsupon, Nakano Iwata and Takamitsuhiko, had been dispatched as representatives of the officials and Shinjiya, and along with Nishijima of the Hōten branch, they busied themselves regarding sent-in goods and other matters.

On July 21st, escorted by Prosecutor Miura and three other police officers, they arrived at the water police station in Dalian; from this place, escorted again by two police officers, they arrived at Moji on the Maru Harubin. During the voyage, Hideo responded to the requests of the captain and passengers by giving lectures concerning the Ōmoto doctrine and performing much calligraphy. Welcomed by many Shinjiya, he arrived safely at the gateway of Nitsupon on the morning of July 25th. The scene was just as if welcoming a triumphant general.

原文（近代文語）

『大人が日本に帰らるる時は私も連れて帰つて下さい、巡警を止めて大本の信者となり、お庭掃除でもさして頂き度い』

と頼み込むのであつた。日出雄は、

『奉天に着いた上、模様に依つて電報を打つから、電報が届いたら奉天迄出て来い、日本へ同道する』

と云つてやつたら、王部長は非常に喜んだ。国際法に依つて領事館から引渡しを要求した時は、二十四時間内に引渡すべきものなるに、二十一日の夜から三十日迄十日間パインタラに繋いで置いたのは、余程日本領事館と支那官憲との交渉が六ケ敷かつた為であつたとの事である。

四五日経つてから日本人一同は通遼県知事の法廷に引出され、日本語の通訳官を介して取調べを受けた。此取調の要点は、日出雄の名刺に素尊汗と書いてあるが、汗と云へば蒙古第一の王の名称である。又出生地が蒙古の国としてあるが、蒙古は支那の版図であつて、蒙古国と云ふ国名は無い筈だ。汝は盧占魁を使嗾して宗教を表に蒙古独立を企てたのであらう、と云ふのである。日出雄は平然として、

『自分は支那の新宗教道院の宣伝使だ。蒙古の地に宗教を宣伝するの特権を持つてゐる。さうして蒙古の国と云つたのは別に深い意味があるのでは無い。我々日本の国には八十余個の国名があり、丹波の国、丹後の国、山城の国などと小区劃に国名を呼んで居る。さうだから支那の一区域たる蒙古を蒙古の国と書いたのだ。自分は蒙古のみならず、新彊、印度、西蔵、支那は云ふに及ばず、露西亜、西比利亜を経て欧羅巴の天地に迄宗教的王国を建設するのだから、小さい蒙古などに執着して居るのではない。さうして宗教は国境を超越して居るのだ』

と述べた所、知事は二三回うなづいて、日出雄を獄舎に帰した。次には真澄別以下の五名は一人づつ引き出されて取調べを受けたが、真澄別は憤然として知事其他の訊問官に向ひ、

『吾々を馬賊とは怪しからぬ、貴官は盧占魁を馬賊と云はれるが、彼と久しく行動を共にして見て居たが、彼には少しも馬賊的行為は無かつた。それよりも支那官兵は自分等の所持金を掠奪し、其他一切の所持品を盗み取つたではないか。泥棒する者を官兵と云ひ、泥棒せない者を馬賊と云ふのか、張作霖だつて、其他有名の督軍だつて元は馬賊ぢやないか、自分を馬賊と云ふのならそれでよい。まづ馬賊の定義から聴かして貰ひ度い』

と云ふ。萩原も亦真澄別と同じ事を云ふて知事其他を手こずらす。

次に守高、井上、坂本の三人に対し、武器を携帯して居ただらうと厳しく訊問した。何れも日出雄先生のお弟子であつて宗教家だと云ひ抜け、やつと公署の調べも済んだ。それから一行六人は足枷を篏められた儘、門口に並べられ一葉の写真を取られた上、其翌日六月三十日荷車三台に載せられ、銃剣をつけた兵士六名に送られて汽車に乗り、鄭家屯道尹に護送され、次で同地の警官教習所の拘留所や警官室に足枷を篏められた儘分置せられた。其処へ横尾敬義、井口藤五郎の両人が見舞に来て日出雄一行を慰めた。其処で再び道尹の取調べを受けたが、尋ねる事も、答へる事も、白音太拉と同様であつた。

七月五日の夕暮、鄭家屯の日本領事館に引き渡さるる事になつた。別れに臨んで道尹の高等官以下十数名は、墨と筆とを用意して日出雄の揮毫を乞ふた。日出雄は快く彼等の請求に応じ、腕を揮ふて大小数十の文字を書き与へた。夜の十二時頃日本領事館の手に渡り、一応の取調べを受け、領事館にて久し振りで湯を使ひ垢を落し、監房に一夜を明し、翌六日通常服の領事館の警察署長以下五人に送られ奉天総領事館に収容された。其時既に名田彦、山田、小林の三人は収容されて居り、暫くして大倉が這入つて来て都合十人になつた。取調べの結果三ケ年の退支処分で一件落着した。

是より先、日本から中野岩太、隆光彦の二人が役員信者代表として出張し、差入れ物其他について奉天支部の西島と共に奔走した。

七月二十一日三浦検事外三名の警察官に送られ大連の水上署に着き、此地より又二名の警官に送られ、ハルピン丸にて門司に着いた。航海中日出雄は船長以下乗客の依頼に応じて大本教義に関する演説を為し、沢山の揮毫をし、数多の信者に迎へられ、日本の玄関口に安着したのは七月二十五日の午前であつた。其光景は恰も凱旋将軍を迎ふるが如き有様であつた。
