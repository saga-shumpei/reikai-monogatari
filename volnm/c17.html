Chapter 17: Light and Darkness in Alternation (Meian Tomo-ni) [17]

明暗交々〔17〕

§ 1

Three days after Hideo took lodging in the bag of the elder Rauinkun, a manager of Konefu, the house of a man named Utanasuto in the neighborhood was opened and assigned as a lodging for the party.

The simple-hearted Mōkojin from far and near came yearning for them, saying, 'Naranuorosun, ihoemitoporohana, irujiē irujiē irujiē,' and the number of those who came increased day by day.

Translating the aforementioned Mōkogo, it means that the Great Daikuwatsushin of Hiizurukuni has arrived.

Each person, filled with joy, begged for Chinkon and received treatment for the eye diseases, skin diseases, Ibiyō, Baidoku, toothaches, and Naubiyō that are most prevalent in this land; having fully recovered, they expressed gratitude for the divine virtue and revered them as the Great Daikuwatsushin. Many of the eye diseases, skin diseases, and Baidoku cases stem from uncleanliness, and they do not realize in the slightest that venereal diseases are contagious. They merely attribute it to the result of horseback riding and leave themselves to the spread of the virus. In particular, there are an extraordinary number of Lama monks suffering from Baidoku. The women of Mōko adorn their heads with many jewels and large pearls, all of which are gathered from within the black mussels, some over one shaku in diameter, that have propagated in the Liao River and the Heilongjiang. As the Mōkojin do not use shellfish as food, these black mussels have inhabited the waters for many hundreds and thousands of years. As for the food of the Mōkojin, in the eastern Mōko regions near the Chinese interior, they consume food similar to the Chinese, such as kaoliang, soybeans, millet, and pork, but in the pure Mōko regions of the interior around Konefu, they make milk and roasted rice their staple diet. Sometimes they add meat to this to eat as a rice porridge, but mixing in meat is considered the highest class of meal. In ordinary households, luxury items like meat are rarely eaten. Making udon with American flour and mixing in sheep meat is the finest feast. Since animal husbandry has been their sole occupation since the time of their ancestors, milk from cows and sheep is abundant, and many households provide it for their daily food. They do not boil milk to drink like the Japanese; they drink it cold and prepare it into various dishes. First, they take butter from the fat that floats on the top of the milk jar, and for the substance that settles at the bottom, they put it into a cloth bag to let the liquid drip into the jar; they then solidify the dregs collected inside the bag to make milk cakes. They call this naidoufu. Because it is convenient for preservation, it becomes a staple food in some regions, serves as a substitute for sweets, and at times acts as a substitute for currency and a unit for barter, making it truly valuable. Furthermore, sour milk is obtained from the remainder after taking the butter and naidoufu, which becomes the only seasoning to pour over roasted rice to eat. Besides the above, they evaporate milk to make milk liquor. Even while eating nothing but milk and roasted rice all year round, the Mōkojin have remarkably magnificent physiques. Also, the Mōkojin do not eat any vegetables like the Chinese do; one reason for this may be that vegetables do not grow well because there is so much livestock. Yet, the reason they do not suffer from scurvy is the result of making the milk of cows, which constantly feed on grass, their staple food. Thus, the cow is the mother of life for the Mōkojin, and the horse is their sole means of transportation. The saying 'boats in the south, horses in the north' is a phrase that is only truly understood upon coming to Mōko in the northern continent.

One day, Hideo went rabbit hunting to the mountain behind Konefu together with Mōkojin such as Utanasuto and Ōtokushō, who were soldiers of Konefu, and the adjutant Onchōkō. The tool for rabbit hunting is a stick about one shaku five sun long with a curved tip, to which a string of about three sun is tied, with a lump of iron weight attached to the end of the string. Waiting for a rabbit to jump out, they skillfully throw this stick. When they do so, the iron piece hanging from the tip of the stick spins around, and when it hits the rabbit, the mechanism is such that the string wraps around it. The Mōkojin always carry this stick as one of their seven essential tools, along with a pipe and flint. However, when they surround a rabbit with four or five Mōko dogs, they can catch it easily.

一日蒙古人の丑他那寸止や王得勝など云ふ公爺府の兵士や、副官の温長興と倶に公爺府の裏山へ兎狩に出掛けた。兎狩の道具は一尺五寸許りの先の曲つた棒で、その尖端に三寸許りの紐を結び着け紐の尖に一塊の鉄の重りが付いて居る。兎の飛び出すのを待つて此の棒を巧妙に投げ付ける。さうすると棒の尖にブラ下つて居る鉄片が、グルグル舞ひ乍ら兎に当ると紐が捲きつく仕組である。蒙古人は煙管や火打石と共に七ツ道具の一として常に此棒を携帯して居るのである。併し蒙古犬の四五匹を以て兎を囲む時は容易に捕へる事が出来るのである。

§ 2

When Hideo attempted to climb Kunimiyama together with Onchiyōkō and Okazaki Tetsushiyu, they were surrounded by fierce dogs peculiar to Mōko called 'shīgō' and were about to be bitten, so his adjutants, Onchiyōkō and Okazaki Tetsushiyu, strove to defend them by brandishing their sutetsuki and picking up stones to strike at them. Hearing the barking of the fierce dogs, dozens more gathered from far and near in an instant, surrounding the three men in ten and twenty layers, and though the three were faced with the terrifying momentum of the dogs baring their fangs and leaping at them, the two men fought with all their might. When Hideo also put his heart into chanting Norito in a loud voice, perhaps daunted by the great voice of a Nitsuponjin, the fierce dogs, despite their ferocity, lowered their tails and scattered in all directions. From this time on, the dogs of this region would lower their tails, shrink, and run away whenever they saw the three men.

Moritaka had gone out to a house somewhat far away to perform a soul-pacifying ritual for the illness of an official of the Kōfu. There, a senior official came from the Prince of Chinkoku and requested that the great living Buddha of the land of Hideo be invited to the mansion. Hideo, together with his interpreter, immediately went out to the Kōfu. The Prince of Chinkoku was greatly pleased and, through the interpreter, attempted various conversations and inquired about the circumstances of this entry into Mōko. Ō Genki spoke earnestly about raising a volunteer army to rescue both Inner and Outer Mōko. The Prince was extremely pleased and deeply grateful for his goodwill. He then treated Hideo with the best hospitality he could offer. That the King of a Kōfu, who governs a territory two hundred ri east to west and eight hundred ri north to south, would go out of his way to visit Hideo's residence and welcome him again with such courtesy as a great religious leader and a person of high rank from Nitsupon, struck Hideo with a strange emotion. Compared to the tendency in his own country where even local officials would often throw their weight around and make a fool of Hideo, he was filled with deep emotion. Although Mōko is said to be an undeveloped country, the people, both high and low, are well-harmonized, reminiscent of the vestiges of the age of the gods; their appearance also closely resembles that of Nitsuponjin, and they have a tendency to rely on Nitsuponjin as their only strength. They firmly believe that Nitsuponjin share the same ancestors as the Mōko people, and that the descendants of Minamoto no Yoshitsune have all become the one hundred and one kings of Mōko.

Okazaki, speaking to the Mōko people in the Chinese language, said, 'We intend to save Mōko from its ruinous fate, chase the Red Army stationed in Daikurin like chasing rabbits, construct a new Mōko Kingdom in Daikurin, sweep through China, Manshū, and Shiberia, and announce the true value of Nitsupon and Mōko men to the world. Why, even the big-nosed ones and the Chinese will immediately collapse if the people of Nitsupon and Mōko unite, ahahaha...' He was lost in his own pleasure, spouting such grandiloquent words. A senior official of the Kōfu heard this from the side with a worried look, but immediately summoned Rōinkun and rushed hurriedly to the mansion of the Prince of Chinkoku. After two or three hours, Rōinkun returned with a troubled look on his face. Then, turning to Okazaki, he said,

Rōinkun: 'Do you gentlemen from Nitsupon possess travel permits for the Three Eastern Provinces? Those without permits cannot be allowed to stay here even for a day. In particular, I cannot take care of people who plot the independence of Mōko, for if I do, the heads of the King and even this white-haired old man will be sent flying... If you are going to Mōko, please return to Hōten once and obtain your permits.'

He had changed his attitude completely. Upon hearing this, Onchiyōkō was greatly angered and said,

Onchiyōkō: 'What an outrageous thing for an old man to say. We received a large sum of money from Commander Ro to take care of our party, and yet to hear such things from the mouth of an old man now is completely unacceptable. Furthermore, what is the meaning of stuffing us into such a hovel and letting us be tormented by bedbugs? In any case, I will return to Hōten for now and negotiate with the Commander.'

§ 3

He is fuming with rage. The sudden change in Rauinkun's attitude was caused by Okazaki's Daigensaugo. This was because Chinkokukō and the Jiuyakuren were afraid of the Shinaseifu and Chiyōsakurin. Okazaki began to leak his Fuhei again.

Okazaki: 'That Gaki called Rosenkuwai, pushing me into such a remote place, and colluding with Sasaki and Ōkura to swindle Sensei and us Nitsuponjin—very well, I have my own ideas. I will return to Hōten and expose every single one of Rosenkuwai's Himitsu. Furthermore, those scoundrels Sasaki and Ōkura blustered that since Bazoku roam west of Taunan, I must not carry any Nimotsu or Kinsen, not even a single Itsusen, and that since money had been handed over to Manda-kan and Rauinkun, everything would be taken care of without any inconvenience. But look at this state of affairs! There is not even a single blanket, and how can one sleep on a single mat spread in a den of bedbugs? They also blustered that I should not carry a single mon of money, but thanks to Sensei, who secretly slipped some into my pocket and brought it along, I was able to buy eggs and cover my travel expenses. If I had done as those fellows said, we would have had no choice but to starve to death in the depths of Mongolia.'

He grumbles and begins to get angry. However, it is no wonder that Okazaki is angry. Even though the Ondoru was heated, there was not a single blanket, and it was a miserable situation where they had to sleep at night covered in their overcoats. Hideo had carried twenty thousand yen in travel expenses in his pocket at Taunan-fu, intending to use it for the expenses of the Nitsuponjin party, but he was persuaded by Sasaki, Ōkura, and Tōkokuwake in various ways, and in the end, he had handed it over to them. In any case, they decided to have Onchōkō ride a horse to meet the Masumiwake party in Taunan-fu, report the current situation of the party at the Kōya-fu, and have him depart with a letter stating that they could do nothing unless they sent all their luggage as soon as possible.

Ōgenki would go to a Mongolian house from morning till night to indulge in a gambling game called Mahjong, and would not perform his duties as an interpreter at all; even if one said a word, he would immediately get angry.

Ōgenki: 'Since I am not suited for this, please let me go home.'

He would throw a tantrum, sensing their weakness, which greatly troubled Hideo, who had to use writing to communicate through Hakuryōkaku to handle all negotiations with the Mongolians. Moritaka was teaching his specialty, Jujutsu, to the Mongolians outdoors where a cold wind was blowing. Because he had a martial arts face that looked like he had some quirks at first glance, the Mongolians felt uneasy, but out of curiosity, they tried practicing Jujutsu once or twice. Moritaka, thinking that he must show his strength to people of such a barbaric country or else he would be despised, grabbed the vital points of the Mongolians' wrists with all his might, causing the Mongolians to turn pale and collapse. The Mongolians, not knowing that this was a Jujutsu technique and because they could not understand the language, greatly misunderstood Moritaka as a villain. The Mongolian men in the village gathered at Ōtokushō's house and decided to consult on shooting Moritaka to death under the cover of a dark night. Hakuryōkaku, being a Mongolian himself, was worried about the result of their consultation and secretly informed Hideo. Thereupon, Hideo gave Ōtokushō a wristwatch and some money, and through Hakuryōkaku, explained the gist of Judo and that Moritaka was a good-natured person, so the Mongolians finally understood, their ill feelings toward Moritaka subsided slightly, and fortunately, he was able to remain safe. Hideo's anxiety at this time was extraordinary.

Rauinkun, unable to withstand the persistent pursuit of Onchōkō, suddenly decided to move the Hideo party to a new house he owned. It was located about five chō to the southwest from Rauinkun's house. As the walls were not yet sufficiently dry, they burned dead willow branches vigorously to try to dry the interior. Although one cartload of firewood for the oxcart cost fifty sen, they ended up burning about two cartloads to dry the walls. Finally, on April 4th (the 2nd day of the third month by the old calendar), they moved into the new house and were finally relieved. They had Onchōkō carry a letter to report the difficult circumstances at the Kōya-fu to Rosenkuwai and the Nitsuponjin side, and to convey that they were waiting for the Masumiwake party to arrive at the government office as soon as possible. Onchōkō whipped his horse and dashed off toward Taunan with great vigor. However, around 6:00 p.m. that day, when Hideo was looking at the wilderness in the direction of Taunan with binoculars, he noticed four carriages racing straight toward them; looking closely, he saw that it was the Masumiwake party, fully loaded with plenty of luggage and food. The joy of Hideo and Okazaki at this time was extraordinary.

王元祺は朝から晩まで蒙古人の家に行つて麻雀と云ふ賭博に耽り、少しも通訳の用をしない、一言いつても直ぐに腹を立て、

『私はお間に合ひませぬから帰らして貰ひます』

など、足許を見込んで駄々をこねるので、日出雄も大に困り、筆談を以て白凌閣を介し、蒙古人との一切の交渉に当らしめてゐた。守高は得意の柔術を蒙古人に寒い風の吹く戸外で教へてゐた。一見しても一癖あり相な武術面をしてゐるので、蒙古人は薄気味悪く感じてゐたけれ共、物珍らしさに一二回の柔術の稽古をやつて見た。守高は……こんな野蛮国の人間には自分の力を見せておかねば軽蔑されると云ふ考へから、蒙古人の手首の急所を力一杯掴み締めたので、蒙古人は青くなつてヘタバつた。それを蒙古人は柔術の手といふ事は知らず、且つ言葉の通じない所より非常に守高を悪党と誤解し、村中の蒙古男子が王得勝の家に集まつて、暗夜に乗じ守高を鉄砲で討ち殺さうといふ相談を定めた。白凌閣は蒙古人の事でもあり、其相談の結果を心配して日出雄に密告した。そこで日出雄は王得勝に腕時計や若干の金子を与へ、白凌閣を介して柔道の大略や守高の好人物たる事を云ひ聞かせたので、蒙古人も漸く了解して、守高に対する悪感は稍薄らぎ、幸に無事なるを得たのである。此時の日出雄の心配は一通りではなかつたのである。

老印君は温長興のきびしき追撃に堪へかねて、俄に自分所有の新宅に日出雄一行を移転させる事とした。老印君の宅から西南に当り殆ど五丁許りの距離がある。まだ壁も十分乾いてゐないので、盛んに楊の枯枝を燃やして室内の乾燥を図つた。そして牛車一台の薪は五十銭であつたが、壁を乾かすのに二台許りの薪をくすべて了つた。漸くにして四月四日（旧三月二日）新宅に移転し、ヤツト一安心した。そして温長興に手紙を持たせて、盧占魁や日本人側へ公爺府に於ける困難の事情を報告せしめ、且真澄別一行の一日も早く着府するのを待つてゐる事を伝へしめた。温長興は駒に鞭ち勢ひよく洮南を指して駆け出した。ところが其日の午後六時頃望遠鏡を以て洮南方面の原野を眺めてゐると、四台の轎車がまつしぐらに馳けて来るのが目についた、よくよく見れば真澄別の一行が沢山な荷物や食料を満載して来るのであつた。此時の日出雄、岡崎の喜びは一通りではなかつた。

§ 4

Ichidō felt as if they had met a Buddha in Jigoku and rejoiced, their eyes clouded with tears of joy for one another. Seeing that a takusan amount of nimotsu and shiyokureu had arrived, Rauinkun appeared surprised, and his taido toward the Nitsuponjin suddenly changed completely. When one considers it, it is no muri that Rauinkun had doubted the Nitsuponjin itsukau. They were all Hotsukokukaminari, possessing not a single nanihito item of note, and their appearance was such that one could not tell from where these ochiudo had emerged. The itsukau could not help but feel suspicious, wondering if Rauinkun was being kaukuwatsu and feigning ignorance, or if the Nitsupongaha sanningumi had deceived the Hideo itsukau and sent them into the interior, and they could not fathom the truth. And so, the bad kanjiyō toward Rauinkun gradually began to fade. The Nitsuponjin who had come following Masumiwake were the riyōnin, Nadahiko and Ino Toshio.

Prior to this, three men with dangerous-looking, acorn-like eyes, claiming to be leaders of a band of mounted bandits in the Kokuriukō region, came to visit Okazaki and negotiated to be allowed to join the autonomous army. Okazaki immediately wrote a letter and sent them to Lu Zhankui and Sasaki to consult with them.

原文（近代文語）

一同は地獄で仏に会ふたやうな心持になつて打喜び、互に嬉し涙に眼を曇らした。沢山の荷物や食料が来たので老印君は驚いたと見え俄かに日本人に対する態度がガラリと変つて来た。考へて見れば老印君の日本人一行を疑つたのも無理はない。何れも北国雷で何一つ目ぼしい携帯品もなく、どこの落人が出て来たか分らぬ様な体裁だつたからである。老印君が狡猾で知らぬ顔をしたのか、但しは日本側の三人組が日出雄一行をだまして奥へやつたのか合点が行かぬと、一行は怪しみに堪へなかつた。そして老印君に対する悪い感情も次第に剥げて来た。真澄別に従つてやつて来た日本人は名田彦、猪野敏夫の両人であつた。

之より先き黒竜江方面の馬賊団の頭目と称する団栗眼の物騒な面した男が三人岡崎の許へ尋ねて来て、自治軍に参加させてくれないかと掛合つた。岡崎は早速手紙を認めて盧占魁と佐々木へ宛て相談に向はしめた。
