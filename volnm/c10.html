Chapter 10: Departure from Mukden (Hōten Shuppatsu) [10]

奉天出発〔10〕

§ 1

On the first day of the third month (the twenty-sixth day of the first month by the Chiugokureki), Hideo, Rosenkuwai, Masumiwake, Okazaki, Yōkiyohau, Sasaki, Ōkura, Karakuniwake, Moritaka, and others gathered at the Rokōkuwan and blossomed into a discussion on the management of Mongolia.

Ōkura: 'Sensei, the internal order for the Commander-in-Chief of the Seihokujichigun has finally been issued from Chiyōsakurin to Rosen-san. According to Chiyōsakurin's opinion, as per your plan, Sensei, the instruction is to first recruit soldiers at Sōronzan, establish a headquarters, and commence activities. Regarding this, Kishishōshiyō has also gone to great lengths for us. With this, we can be at ease for the time being. Sasaki-san has also been very worried, but since we have finally seen the dawn of the fulfillment of our great vow, please be at ease.'

Hideo: 'That was a great deal of trouble. I rejoice that, through divine assistance, the matter was settled unexpectedly quickly. However, Sasaki-san, this negotiation must have been quite difficult, must it not?'

Sasaki: 'Yes, no matter what one says, China is a place that moves by money, so I have placed a golden bit in the mouths of those serving close to Chiyōsakurin to ensure they do not oppose us. From here on, I shall set to work on military preparations. And as for transportation, it has become very easy from Dairen to Tōnan. Since Chiyōsakurin has agreed to take charge of and send the transport of weapons, please be at ease.'

Hideo: 'If that is the case, I intend to enter Mongolia one step ahead. I would like you all to complete all preparations and come after me. Since it is said that about three hundred lamas will come to welcome us from Ōyabyō, we cannot afford to dally.'

Sasaki: 'Please wait for two or three days; if you do so, Yōsuitei-san, who went to order lama robes, should return from Beijing, and we will also be able to obtain Chiyōsakurin's passport. It would be safer to act after that.'

Okazaki: 'What, what use is a passport? I am a high-ranking official of the Tōsanshō; if I accompany Sensei, what need is there for any passport or nonsense? Our purpose is to do everything in accordance with the divine command. Sensei's words are the words of God. I have inspected the route from here to Tōnanfu, and since I have made arrangements with Japanese people living at each destination to take care of various matters, you all should remain behind and make preparations. I intend to hire an automobile within two or three days and dash to Teikaton.'

Sasaki: 'Even though there is a train running from Hōten, is there any need to pay a high price to ride in an automobile? I will attach about thirty guards, so why not take the trouble of going from Hōten station, Sensei? I think that would be safe and better.'

Okazaki: 'That may be so, but what need does God have for guards? If I accompany you, it is as solid as a rock. Since no one has ever driven an automobile from Hōten to Teikaton before, an automobile journey should also be interesting for the sake of knowing the geography. Japanese people always speak proudly of having inspected Manchuria and Mongolia, but just riding a train and inspecting the roadside is not enough; it would surely be more interesting to go by automobile.'

Sasaki: 'On such bad roads, there is a great danger of having to break through mountains and fields and cross the Ryōga. Where is the need to pass through such a dangerous road? I will not say anything bad, so I really want you to choose the train journey. We are also worried, you see.'

Hideo: 'I also wish to investigate the circumstances of Manchuria, and since it is not a journey that requires haste, I would like to try an automobile trip. It would also be interesting to pass through places where people do not usually go.'

Okazaki: 'Sasaki and Ōkura are cowards, which is why they say such things, but what is there to worry about? Shall we not decide on an automobile trip?'

Sasaki: 'If that is your opinion, Sensei, there is no help for it. I shall now inquire with the automobile dealer.'

佐々木『此の道の悪いのに、山や畑を突破し、遼河を渡らねばならぬ大危険がある。そんな危険の道を通る必要がどこにあるか。悪い事は云はないから、是非汽車旅行にして欲しいものだ。吾々も心配でならないからなあ』

日出雄『満州の事情も調べたいから、別に急ぐ旅でもなし、自動車旅行を試みたいものだ。人の通らない所を通つて見るのも面白いだらう』

岡崎『佐々木や大倉は臆病だから、そんな事を云ふが、何、心配要るものか、自動車旅行と決定しようぢやないか』

佐々木『先生が其御意見なら仕方が無い、之から自動車屋に照会して見よう』

§ 2

Yōkiyohau: 'I shall speak to a man named Ōjiyudau, an acquaintance of mine who is under the patronage of Mr. Lu and formerly served as a company commander in the Chiyokureigun, for he operates an automobile company within the city walls. He is a man of great courage and strong sense of chivalry, so he is not the type to be daunted by the appearance of a few bandits.'

Hideo: 'That is convenient; let us settle on that. We shall depart from this place on the third day of the third month.'

Lu: 'I am determined to follow your will in all things, Sensei, so I shall order the automobiles as you desire.'

The consultation having concluded in this manner, it was decided that Hideo would drive an automobile from Hōten toward Teikaton. Furthermore, it was agreed that the three men, Masumiwake, Ōkura, and Nadahiko, would depart from Hōten by train to arrive first at Tōnan-fu in Mongolia, where they would make all necessary preparations at the Tōnan Hotel and await the others.

At four o'clock in the afternoon on the third day of the third month (the twenty-eighth day of the first month by the lunar calendar), Hideo, Okazaki, Moritaka, and Ōgenki were to depart from Hōten, split between two automobiles belonging to Ōjiyudau. The automobile shop owner, Ōjiyudau, concerned about the treacherous state of the roads, decided to serve as the driver himself and follow along with three of his subordinates. The roads were extremely treacherous, and the driving did not go as intended; they would drive for about an hour and then stop for another hour to repair the vehicles, forcing their way through rivers, fields, and small hills regardless of day or night. The cold was biting, and they had almost lost all sensation in their hands and feet. As the prevalence of bandits was particularly severe between Hōten and Teikaton, they decided to extinguish the headlights while the automobiles were stopped and use flashlights to begin repairs. When the water ran out, they would break the ice in the vicinity and pack it into the engine, enduring various hardships as they pressed on toward the north, guided by the light of the stars.

Fortunately, they broke through the nets of government soldiers, police, and bandits, and advanced to the banks of the Ryōga River at the foot of Ryūshuzan, located in front of Tairei. As the automobile descended the steep slope of the riverbank and was about to cross the Ryōga River, the driver let out a cry of surprise: 'Ah!' At that instant, the automobile stopped as if by a miracle. Upon closer inspection, they saw that only one or two shaku ahead, the ice, which had appeared so thick, had melted, and the bluish water was flowing; it was truly a dangerous situation. The interpreter Ōgenki and Moritaka were riding in this automobile, and both gave thanks for the divine grace, attributing their safety to the protection of the gods. At this location, Ryūshuzan towered high to the northwest, and because the river embankment was extremely high, it blocked the cold northwest wind, preventing the water from freezing. Having no other choice, they returned two or three shiri, knocked on the doors of houses to wake the inhabitants, hired a guide, and drove precariously over small hills and through fields until they reached the main road. Trains running across the vast plains of Manchuria emitted long, thin rays of light in the far distance. They arrived within the city walls of Kaigen at seven o'clock on the morning of the fourth. They entered an eatery to consume greasy Chinese food and spent about an hour there repairing the vehicle bodies. As automobiles had never passed through this region, the news that a strange vehicle had arrived spread from one person to another, and men and women of all ages gathered in a dense, noisy crowd. Meanwhile, carts pulled by four or five oxen or donkeys passed by. The confusion was beyond description in words. Once the repairs were finally completed, the two automobiles sounded their horns, pushed through the crowd, and sped off, arriving safely just before Shōzufu around one o'clock in the afternoon. One of the automobiles had suffered major damage and became inoperable, so they had no choice but to stay at the wooden-fare hotel, Shop No. 3, in Shōzufu, and send a man back to Hōten to procure parts. To have undertaken such an adventurous journey across a two-hundred-and-forty-shiri plateau, which no one had ever attempted before, was a magnificent feat since the dawn of time. Okazaki was in high spirits, declaring it a greater achievement than even the retreat of Minamoto no Yoshitsune from the capital.

かくの如く相談が纏つたので、日出雄は愈々奉天から鄭家屯に向つて自動車を駆る事となつた。さうして真澄別、大倉、名田彦の三名は奉天より汽車に乗つて蒙古の洮南府に先着し、洮南ホテルに於て諸般の準備を調へて待つ事に相談がまとまつた。

愈々三月三日（旧正月廿八日）午後四時から王樹棠の自動車二台に日出雄、岡崎、守高、王元祺の四名は分乗して奉天を出発する事となつた。自動車屋の主人王樹棠は道路の険悪なるを気づかひ自ら運転手となり三人の部下と共に随ふ事となつた。道路は極めて険悪にして運転意の如くならず、時々機関に損障を来し、一時間許り走ると又一時間許り停車して車の修繕を為し、昼夜の区別なく川の中や畑の中、小山などを無理やりに進む事となつた。寒気凛烈にして、手も足も殆んど知覚を失つて居る。奉天から鄭家屯迄の間は馬賊の横行が特に甚だしいので、自動車の停車中はヘツドライトを消し、懐中電燈を持つて修繕に着手する事とした。水が切れると其処辺の氷を割つて機関に詰め込むなど、いろいろの難苦ををかし、北へ北へと星の光を力に進んで行く。

幸に官兵や巡警、馬賊の網を突破して、鉄嶺の前方竜首山の麓なる遼河畔に進んだ。河辺の急坂を下つて自動車は将に遼河を横ぎらうとした時、運転手は『アツ』と驚きの声を発した。其刹那に不思議にも自動車は止つた。よくよく見れば、一二尺前方には、さしもに厚い氷が解けて蒼味だつた水が漂うて居る、実に危険な場合であつた。此の自動車には通訳の王元祺と守高が乗つて居たが、何れも神明の御加護として神恩を謝した。此地点は西北に竜首山が高く聳え、且つ河の堤が非常に高かつた為め、西北の寒風を遮り、氷結して居なかつたためである。是非なく二三支里後へ戻つて人家を叩き起し、案内者を雇うて、小山の上や畑の中を危くも馳駆して大道に出た。満州広野を走る列車は、遥の遠方に細長く薄い光を放つて走つて居た。翌四日午前七時開原の城内に着いた。飲食店に入つて油濃い支那食を喫し、此処で一時間許りも車体の修繕をした。此地方では自動車の通行した事が無いので、妙な車が来たと云ふので次から次へ言ひ伝へ、老若男女が真黒に蝟集してワイワイと騒ぎ立てて居る。其間牛や驢馬などが四頭曳き、五頭曳きで大車を引いて通る。其混雑は到底筆紙に尽し難い。漸く修繕が済んだので、二台の自動車は、ブウブウと警笛を鳴らし、群集を押しわけ、大速力でかけ出し、午後一時頃には昌図府の手前迄安着した。一台の自動車は大破損を来たし運転不能となつて仕舞つたので、是非なく昌図府の木賃ホテル三号店に宿泊し、奉天へ人を派して機械を取り寄せる事にした。二百四十支里の高原を未だ何人も試みし事なき冒険的旅行をやつたのは、開闢以来の壮挙だ。源の義経の都落にも比して偉大なる放れ業だと、岡崎の気焔当るべからざるものがあつた。
