Chapter 6: Parting Words on Marching to the Front (Shussei no Ji) [6]

出征の辞〔6〕

§ 1

On the twelfth day of the second month of the tenth year of Taishiyō, which was the fifth day of the first month of the lunar calendar, the moon in its first quarter and the planet Venus shone with a strange brilliance in the broad daylight of the clear, cloudless sky over Naniha, signaling a transformation of heaven and earth. On this day, Hideo was at Umeda Station, the gateway to Ōsakashi, presiding over the business of the company as the president of the Taishiyōnichinichishinbunshiyachiyō.

This day was the very day the Ōmotojiken broke out, and Hideo was summoned from the newspaper company to the Kiyōtofu Keisatsubu, subsequently subjected to formal interrogation by the preliminary judge of the Kiyōto District Court, and cast into the Kiyōto prison—it was a truly memorable day for Ōmoto. This strange phenomenon in the heavens did not end there; later, on the twelfth day of the second month of the thirteenth year of Taishiyō, the same oval-shaped moon and the planet Venus began to shine with a strange brilliance in the broad daylight of the same day. Hideo could not help but look up at the sky and recall the twelfth day of the second month of the departing tenth year of Taishiyō. Moreover, that such a similar anomaly should appear in the sky in broad daylight on the same day exactly three years later could by no means be a mere coincidence. He became convinced that he was being urged by Heaven to carry out his mission for the salvation of all people and the realization of love for humanity, in accordance with the divine command. From this moment, he suddenly resolved to cross into China and informed the several officials who were close to him that he would depart that very night. The attendants were somewhat flustered by his overly abrupt declaration. However, he believed in the divine command, and once he had resolved to depart that night, he knew that it was impossible to turn him from his purpose, given his usual way of doing things.

The clear stream of the Wachigawa River reflects the pine trees along its banks in reverse, and fish leap among the branches of the pine trees. The sound of the soughing pine wind causes waves like fish scales to drift upon the surface of the water. Shōunkaku, standing forlornly beside it, was the birthplace of the Reikai Monogatari, where he had lain his sickly body to dictate one hundred thousand pages of manuscript.

On this day, he suddenly resolved to travel and visited the owner of Shōunkaku, Nakano Iwata-dono, along with a few followers to bid him farewell. Satō Rokugōo, Yonekura Kahee, and Yonekura Noriharu, who had come from Tōkyō, along with more than ten other enthusiastic believers, gathered unexpectedly. At this time, he revealed his resolution to enter Mongolia to take a step toward the execution of the divine plan, and also attempted to deliver a speech. His speech:

Hideo: 'By divine connection, I am here to serve a part of the divine plan, and as I am now determined to carry out my crossing into China and Mongolia, beginning this very evening, I firmly believe that you all, who have gathered without being summoned, have been drawn here by the deep threads of the divine plan of the spirit world. Our Ōmoto is not a religion like the established religions that merely regard the present world as a land of defilement to be shunned, while yearning for a future heaven or the Pure Land. As subjects of the Imperial Nation born as people of the divine land of Japan, in accordance with the infinite benevolence of the divine decree of the Kuso-no-Kami, we must serve the divine work that will bring about peace and happiness not only for our fellow countrymen, but also for the countries of East Asia and the world, and it is the Ōmoto believers who bear the responsibility for this, as well as for the peace and happiness of the Emperor above. As you all know, the divine revelation states, "When the Setsubun of the tenth year of Taishiyō is over, God will take the soul of the Henseijoshi to a place where people do not go." That divine revelation was fulfilled without the slightest error, for on the twelfth day of the second month, I was cast into the aforementioned Kiyōto prison. And now, I cannot help but think that a divine mission has descended upon me to go to a place where people do not go, on the twelfth day, just as the Setsubun festival has ended this time as well. I firmly believe that I must clarify the great spirit of Japan's founding to the world, demonstrate widely to the world that the dignity of the unbroken Imperial Line is incomparable, and that the spirit of Japan's founding lies not in conquest, nor in aggression, but in pacifying the people of all nations toward the Great Way of God through the Kototama of beautiful words and virtuous speech. Governing the people of the world can never be achieved through military or intellectual power. I believe that in the end, there is no other way than to rely on the power of a new religion that is not bound by any old customs, which is the element of spiritual unity.'

大正十年二月十二日、陰暦正月五日晴天白日の空に上弦の月と、太白星は白昼燦然として浪花の空に異様の光輝を放ち、天地の変動を示してゐる。此日日出雄は大阪市の玄関口梅田駅頭に、大正日々新聞社長として社務を総理してゐた。

此日は例の大本事件の勃発した日であつて、日出雄は同新聞社より京都府警察部へ招致され、次いで京都地方裁判所予審判事の形式的取調を受けて京都監獄に投ぜられた──大本にとつて実に印象深い日であつた。此天空に於ける異様の現象は之に止まらず、下つて大正十三年二月十二日、而も同日の天空に楕円形の月と太白星が白昼燦然と輝き出した。日出雄は天空を仰いで去る大正十年の二月十二日を追懐せずには居られなかつた。而も満三ケ年を経た同月同日の白昼に、天空に同様の異変あるは決して只事ではあるまい。愈々自分が神命を奉じ万民救済の為、人類愛実行の為、天より我にその実行を促すものと考へたのである。これより彼は俄に渡支の決心を定め、今夜の中に出発せむことを数名の側近く侍する役員に告げた。あまり急激な彼の宣言に侍者は稍狼狽の気味であつた。けれ共彼は神命を信じ、是非今夜出発せむと決心した上は、彼の平素のやり方に対し到底その考へをひるがへすことは出来ない事を知つてゐた。

和知川の清流、並木の松を逆に映し、魚は松樹の枝に躍つてゐる。颯々たる松風の音、水面に魚鱗の波をただよはしてゐる。その傍に悄然として建てる祥雲閣は、彼が病躯を横へて十万枚の原稿を口述したる霊界物語の発祥地であつた。

此日彼は俄に旅行の決心を定め、祥雲閣の主人中野岩太氏に別れを告ぐる為、二三の従者と共に訪ねて来た。東京より来合せてゐた佐藤六合雄、米倉嘉兵衛、米倉範治を初め、十数人の熱心なる信者が期せずして集つて来た。此時彼は神示の経綸実行の一歩を進むべく蒙古入の決心を打明け、且つ一場の演説を試みた。彼の演説、

『神縁に依つて私が茲に神の経綸の一端に奉仕し、今晩を期して愈々渡支渡蒙を決行せむとするに当り、招かずしてお集りになつた諸氏は必ずや神界の深き経綸の糸に引かれて、お出になつた方々と固く信じます。我大本は既成宗教の如く、現界を厭離穢土となし未来の天国や極楽浄土を希求するのみの宗教ではありませぬ。国祖の神の仁慈無限なる神勅に依り、日本神州の民と生れたる我々皇国の臣民は、此の尊き大神様の御神示を拝し、上は御一人に対し奉り、下は同胞の平和と幸福の為めのみならず、東亜諸国並に世界の平和と幸福を来すべき神業に奉仕せなくてはならない責任を持つてゐるのは大本信者でありませう。御神示にある通り「大正十年の節分が済みたら、変性女子の身魂を神が人の行かない処に連れ行くぞよ」とお示しになつて居ることは、皆さま御承知のことと思ひます。その神示は毫末の間違ひもなく、二月十二日私は御承知の京都監獄に投ぜられたのでありました。そして又本回も節分祭のすみた十二日に、人のよう行かない処へ行かねばならぬ神の使命が下つて来たやうに考へられてなりませぬ。私は日本建国の大精神を天下に明にし、万世一系の皇室の尊厳無比なる事を洽く天下に示し、且つ日本の建国の精神は征伐に非ず、侵略に非ず、善言美詞の言霊を以て万国の民を神の大道に言向和すにある事を固く信じます。凡て世界の人民を治むるは武力や智力では到底駄目です。結局は精神的結合の要素たる、凡ての旧慣に囚はれざる新宗教の力に依るより外はないと信じます。

§ 2

When I contemplate the current state of our nation, statisticians tell us that the population of Wagakuni is increasing by seven hundred thousand every year. If it proceeds at this rate, it will reach seventy-seven million compatriots by Taishiyō 31, and by the 51st year, it will reach over one hundred million, according to calculations. In any case, the increase in the population of Wagakuni is a matter proven by the facts of each year, and since it is also a fact that the food supply, namely rice and wheat, is already reporting a shortage of seven hundred to eight hundred thousand koku annually, this imbalance between population and food must become a problem for the survival of Wagakuni. There is a theory that if we thoroughly carry out the reclamation of uncultivated land within the country and the consolidation of farmland, we could obtain about two million chōbu of paddy and dry fields, and increase the harvest of rice and wheat by twenty million koku; however, when will such reclamation and consolidation be completed? Even if, as a result of the sincere efforts of our government and people, it were to be completed after several decades, the population would surely already be over one hundred million by that time. Will this balance between population and food still be maintained? If we are anxious about the future of the state, we must aim for a long-term plan of a hundred or a thousand years. Temporary stopgap measures will never be able to guarantee the eternal survival of the state. Nevertheless, the colonial policy of Wagakuni seems to have started from such a basis. In particular, regarding the future survival and development of our state, it will not suffice merely to obtain rice and wheat satisfactorily. In the future of a world advancing day by day, it is a clear subject even today that there is an infinite demand for many materials, primarily steel, cotton, wool, and leather, yet in Wagakuni, is there a secure policy established to fulfill this in the future? Truly, when I think about it, it is a disheartening situation. If, in the event of an emergency, the supply from overseas were cut off, by what method could Wagakuni satisfy that demand? When I think of this, I cannot help but feel shuddered. Those who are in charge of the administration of Wagakuni, as a result of worrying about the future of the state, have established a so-called colonial policy, intending to move the excess population elsewhere and ensure the stability of the lives of those emigrants.

First of all, besides territories full of hatred for other races like the United States, they seem to be targeting Mexico, South America, and the South Sea Islands, but from the perspective of a long-term plan for the state for ten thousand years, it will not end with just sending agricultural immigrants to these remote regions. In our neighboring vicinity, there exist states such as China and Russia, and whether our mutual relations are good or bad, they are ultimately inseparable. Furthermore, within our territory, there is Chōsen, and regarding its future, those who are called the so-called intellectuals seem to be constantly racking their brains. If our Imperial Nation is to make its eternal survival secure and enjoy the benefits of coexistence and co-prosperity with the related countries, we must by all means establish a great national policy to accompany this. The so-called Manchurian and Mongolian policy of the Empire is established from this objective spirit. Indeed, the land of Manchuria and Mongolia is wedged between China proper and the Russian territory of Siberia, and not only is it connected to our Chōsen across the waters of the Yalu River, but it is also endowed with all kinds of industrial resources, and the future of its development truly gives a sense of boundless spring breeze; moreover, historical relations in modern times have inevitably made it so that our Imperial Nation must bear the task of its development. Therefore, if our nation now unites from top to bottom to thoroughly implement the established development plan, by placing the basis of our entire policy toward China on Manchuria and Mongolia, we will be able to mutually guide the current state of the deadlocked Japan-China relations, and at the same time, establish a plan for eternal harmony in the future. We will also be able to make it a relay point for negotiations with Russia. We will be able to provide stability of life to many Chōsen people and make them a powerful auxiliary for the governance of Chōsen. It will also be able to achieve truly great effects in the adjustment of population and food. We will also be able to make it a supply base for our important materials. We will also be able to make it the front-line key position for the national defense of our Imperial Nation. However, there is a great discrepancy between the discussion and the actual practice of managing Manchuria and Mongolia. No matter how thorough the policy established by any intellectual may be, if one does not obtain the right person for the essential task, it will never be completed. The vast desert of Manchuria and Mongolia, stretching to the heavens, has had its infinite wealth abandoned there since the beginning of heaven and earth. This great plain of Mongolia must be the only gift that Heaven has bestowed upon Wagakuni.

§ 3

If the Iseishiya of Wagakuni merely lay down the Mantetsu as a Kaihatsusaku for Manmō, and only touch upon a small portion of Higashimōko such as Teikaton, Taunanfu, or Pain-tara, this Kaihatsusaku will surely come to naught. I firmly believe that for the sake of the Sonritsu of our Kuōkoku, for the safety of Tōa, and for the peace of the Sekai, we are in a position where Wagakuni must take the lead in pioneering the Tenyo Daimōko. And to achieve that purpose, I consider the propagation of a new Shinshiukyō, unfettered by Kiukuwan, to be the primary Shiyudan and Hauhafu. If one looks at the current state of Kiseishiukyō in Wagakuni, is it not in a state of struggling merely to maintain the status quo, let alone developing religion? How could the Kiseishiukyō of Wagakuni, which is gasping for breath and on the verge of death, have the capacity to undertake such a great enterprise? Furthermore, it goes without saying that it is foolish to rely on the dying religion of Wagakuni, which shows no sign of producing even a single heroic religious leader. Therefore, fearing the instability of life that arises with the increase of the Japanese population, worrying about the safety of our compatriots in Chōsen, and fearing the occurrence of turmoil in Tōa, I am finally setting out on a long journey with a few comrades, empty-handed, in obedience to a divine decree.

As you are aware, I know absolutely no Shina language or Mōko language. Moreover, Mōko has an area nearly sixteen times that of Wagakuni, and its people are fierce and are the ferocious people whom the Shina populace have feared since ancient times. In addition, the rampant activity of horse bandits is severe, robbing travelers and taking their lives, and it is said that not a single person who enters the hinterlands of Mōko, whether Japanese or Shina, returns alive. As I set out with a plan in my heart that is almost like a dream, whether one calls it bold or reckless, I am struck by emotions that are truly beyond description. However, I believe in the God who created heaven and earth. It is my true desire to bear the cross for the sake of all people under heaven, to taste every hardship, and to enter and exit the boundary between life and death. I cannot help but worry that if we do not start the execution of the Manmō Kaihatsusaku at this time, the future of our Kuōkoku, which is as perfect as a golden bowl, will become very precarious. We were born in the land of God, eat the grain of the land of God, serve the Great Sovereign of the land of God, and as a people chosen by God, we cannot bear to sit and watch the current state of the world today. I hope that you, the gentlemen with deep divine connections who have gathered here today, will understand my departure for this expedition. And so on.'

Having finished speaking, he wrote the following, which was neither quite prose nor poetry, on the sliding door of Shōunkaku for the sake of commemoration:

Pushing aside the wisdom and courage of the whole body. Pioneering the vast wilderness of ten thousand miles. Although the divine dragon hides in the abyss, it is not a creature of the pond. The heavenly fortune circulates and comes here. Replacing heaven and earth to establish a great enterprise. Alas, the fairyland of northern Mōko. Mountains, rivers, grasses, and trees are in magnificent attire. Cheering and waiting for the arrival of our divine army. Is the heart of a hero not also magnificent?

Furthermore, until the moment of the train's departure, he was cheerfully discussing the management of Tōa while busily moving his pen and paper. To the eyes of those watching from the side, his attitude did not look like that of a person who was about to depart for an uncivilized land ten thousand miles overseas in a moment's time. There are many poems he composed at the time of his departure. I shall introduce one of them here:

Tired of eating the dumplings of the sun, earth, moon, and stars, I now swallow the heavenly sea of the universe.

At this point, one cannot help but be astonished at the increasingly abnormal state of his psychology. Is he a god, a demon, a human, a megalomaniac, a person with a dual personality, or perhaps a madman who has reached the extreme of abnormal psychology? There are almost no words to describe him.

我国の為政者が満蒙開発策として満鉄を敷設し、鄭家屯や、洮南府や、パインタラの東蒙古の一部に少し計り手をつけてゐる位では、到底此開発策は物にはならないであらう。どうしても我皇国存立の為、東亜安全の為、世界平和の為に、我国が率先して天与の大蒙古を開拓せなくてはならない位置にある事を私は固く信じます。そしてその目的を達するには、旧慣に囚はれざる新宗教の宣伝を以て第一の手段方法と考へるのであります。我国に於ける既成宗教の現状を見れば、宗教の発展どころか、現状維持に汲々たる有様ではありませぬ乎。気息奄々として瀕死の境にある我国の既成宗教が、如何にして此大事業に着手するの余裕がありませう。又一人の英雄的宗教家の輩出せむとする気配もなき、我国の瀕死的宗教に頼るの愚なる事は言をまたないでありませう。故に私は日本人口の増加に伴ひ発生する生活の不安定を憂慮し、朝鮮に於ける同胞の安危を憂ひ、次いで東亜の動乱の発生せむ事を恐るるのあまり、愈々神勅を奉じて徒手空拳二三の同志と共に長途の旅に上らむとするのであります。私は御承知の通り支那語も蒙古語も皆目知りませぬ。さうして蒙古は我国の面積に比べて殆ど十六倍の面積があり、その民は慓悍にして支那民衆の古来恐怖する獰猛の民である。加ふるに馬賊の横行甚しく、旅人を掠め生命を奪ひ、日支人の奥地に入るものは一人の生還者もないと伝へられてゐる蒙古の地に、大胆と云はふか、無謀と云はふか、殆ど夢に等しい経綸を胸に描いて出て行く私としては、実に名状すべからざる感慨に打たれるのであります。然し乍ら私は天地創造の神を信じます。天下万民の為に十字架を負ひあらゆる艱難を嘗め、生死の境に出入することを寧ろ本懐とするものであります。今の時に於て満蒙開発の実行に着手せなくては、金甌無欠の我皇国も前途甚だ心細い事になるであらうと憂慮に堪へないのであります。吾々は神の国に生れ、神の国の粟を喰み、神の国の大君に仕へ、神に選まれたる民として、今日の世界の現状を坐視するに忍びないのであります。どうか今此の席にお集りになつた神縁深き諸氏は、今回の私の遠征の首途に対し御諒解あらむことを希望致します。云々』

と述べ終り、記念の為とて祥雲閣の襖に左の如き文章ともつかず、詩ともつかないやうな文字を書いた。

推倒全身之智勇。開拓万里之荒原。神竜雖潜淵。曷池中物。天運茲循環来而。代天地樹立鴻業。嗚呼北蒙之仙境。山河草木凝盛装。歓呼而待望我神軍到矣。英雄之心事亦々非壮快哉。

又彼は発車の間際まで、嘻々として快活に東亜の経綸を談じつつ頻りに筆紙を動かして居た。傍より伺ふ者の目には、寸時の後に海外万里の未開国に向つて出発する人の態度とは見えなかつた。彼が出発の際に詠んだ沢山な歌がある。その中の一首を左に紹介しやう。

日地月星の団子も食ひ飽きて今や宇宙の天海を呑む

ここに至つて彼の心理状態の益々異状なるに驚かざるを得ない。神か、魔か、人か、誇大妄想狂か、二重人格者か、将又変態心理の極地に達せる狂人か、殆ど評するの言葉も出ない。
