Chapter 11: The Barrier of Ataka (Ataka no Seki) [11]

安宅の関〔11〕

§ 1

Due to the damage to the Jidōshiya, the party of four decided to wait at the third branch of the establishment in Shiyōtofu until the Daiyō Kikai arrived from Hōten. From one o'clock in the Gogo, they were given a humble Itsushitsu, where they lit the Kang and lay down, indulging in Kuōmiyōdan regarding their Zento.

After six o'clock in the Gogo, two Shina Jiyunkei came out to conduct a Shiyutsuchiyō for the Shiyukuhakunin. Okazaki was a Nitsuponjin, and the other three were presented as Shinajin, all lying down while wearing Shinafuku.

The Jiyunkei said to Okazaki,

Jiyunkei: 'I have heard that you are a Nitsuponjin, but a passport is required to travel in the interior of Shina; if you would be so kind, may I see your passport?'

Okazaki: 'I have come out on an inspection under the orders of Zhang Zuolin. If you do not know whether a Shina official requires a passport to travel within Shina, I shall show you the proof.'

With that, he became haughty and, looking triumphant, brandished from his bag a letter of appointment stating, 'Appointed as Chief Engineer of the Three Eastern Provinces Yutong Printing Office, monthly salary 360 yuan,' and furthermore, flourished a large business card reading 'Former Military Advisor to the Henan Governor, Okazaki Tetsushu,' dismissing the Shina Jiyunkei by declaring that there was no need for him to undergo an investigation.

The Jiyunkei, looking as if he were taken aback, glanced at Hideo, Moritaka, and Wang Yuanqi, then turned to Okazaki and said,

Jiyunkei: 'For what purpose are these three people traveling by Jidōshiya, despite the availability of trains?'

He asked this in a somewhat interrogative manner. Okazaki, remaining calm, laughed innocently, 'Aha, ha, ha, ha!'

Okazaki: 'What is the point of asking such things? These gentlemen are wealthy merchants from the Southern Shina region. They asked me if I would guide them as they wished to travel through Manchuria for once, so I decided to attempt a Jidōshiya journey while conducting my own inspection.'

The Jiyunkei, while scrutinizing the three, seemed relieved to see them wearing fine Shinafuku, and said,

Jiyunkei: 'Ah, I apologize for the intrusion.'

He bowed politely and left. After he had gone, Okazaki began to boast again as usual.

Okazaki: 'Aha, ha, ha, ha! Sensei, I am a great man, am I not? No matter how grandly Sasaki or Ōkura may boast, they could never pull off such a daring feat. In times like these, this business card acts as a substitute for a passport. It is quite impressive that I did not allow the Shina Jiyunkei to say a single word to you, Sensei, no matter how much he tried to investigate. Ehe, he, he!'

Hideo: 'You are the only one for a trip through Manchuria and Mongolia. Thanks to you, we are able to pass through the Barrier of Ataka safely. I am grateful to you.'

Okazaki: 'No matter what anyone says, I have been traveling throughout Shina since the Russo-Japanese War and am well-versed in the affairs of Shina and Manchuria... so, Sensei, you have nothing to worry about.'

He was exposing his triumphant face incessantly. Time passed gradually, and it became around nine o'clock in the Gogo. The sound of footsteps of four or five people and the clattering of sabers were heard at the entrance of the third branch. At that very moment, the person who suddenly opened the dirty wooden door and entered Hideo's room was a Shina military officer.

A sergeant, followed by four soldiers, had learned from a police report that a Nitsuponjin was staying there and had come specifically to investigate. Hideo and Moritaka remained lying down, pretending not to notice while wearing their Shinafuku, and listened to the exchange between Okazaki and the sergeant.

Sergeant: 'I apologize for disturbing you late at night, but I have heard that you are a high-ranking official of the Three Eastern Provinces. A passport is required to travel in the interior of Shina; do you have one with you?'

Okazaki took out the aforementioned business card and letter of appointment from his bag to show him, and,

Okazaki: 'Atsuha, ha, ha, ha!'

He shook his body nonchalantly and laughed, 'There, you see? This time, a wealthy friend of mine from the Southern Shina region asked me to guide him as he wanted to try a Jidōshiya trip through Manchuria for once. Thinking I would do something eccentric to surprise the Shina officials and people, I hired a Jidōshiya and came here. Since the roads in Shina are generally in poor condition, the sturdy Jidōshiya was wrecked and became inoperable, so I am staying in this dirty, cheap hotel until the machinery arrives from Hōten. Aha, ha, ha, ha! If you continue to interfere unnecessarily, I shall report you to Zhang Zuolin.'

岡崎は例の名刺や辞令を鞄から取り出して見せ、

と無造作に体をゆすつて笑ひ、

§ 2

He suppresses them from the very beginning. The Gunsau, with extreme Ingin and gentle Kotoba, says to Okazaki:

Gunsau: 'It is clear from this Jireishiyo that Kika is a high-ranking official of the Tōsanshiyō. However, since travel in Manshiu is extremely Kiken due to the Ōkau of the Bazoku, and should various Shiyōgai arise along the Tochiu, it would not be settled well with the Nitsupon Seifu; therefore, let us attach Goeihei to accompany you to your destination.'

Okazaki: 'Atsuhahaha, no, thank you very much. However, we are Nitsupondanshi. Even if you were to send twenty or thirty of those Kojiki-like Shina Yatohihei, they would be of no use. I am grateful for your Goshinsetsu, but I must decline. If there is a Hitsueu, I shall request it from the local authorities...'

The Gunsau asked various questions of Wang Yuanqi. As Wang Yuanqi was a Shina person by nature, he responded in his specialty Shina language with a certain pitter-patter. The Gunsau, casting suspicious glances at both Hideo and Moritaka, said:

Gunsau: 'I apologize for disturbing you in the middle of the night.'

He leaves with an Ingin greeting and departs. Okazaki becomes increasingly triumphant, raising his spirits high, scattering talk of the Chōkokukai and Sensei Inukai recklessly, and prattling on and on about the weak-kneed nature of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs:

Okazaki: 'My eyes gaze upon the gate of Kasumigaseki, thinking to see the future of the nation.'

Okazaki: 'Ahahaha, this is a poem I composed. Whenever we try to do something in Shina that would benefit Nitsupon, the weak-kneed Nitsupon diplomats immediately suppress us. That is why the development of Shina and the management of Manmō always end up as a mere painted cake at ninety-nine percent. This time, I am determined to execute the Manmō policy with full resolve. Since Sensei is a missionary of the Shina Dōin and I am a high-ranking official of the Tōsanshiyō, the Nitsupon Seifu should have no right to interfere clumsily. Ahahaha, interesting, interesting, the future is promising.'

He keeps his facial muscles active and his tongue running like a wheel. Then, the sound of boots and sabers came again.

Voice: 'Excuse me.'

The one who entered was a member of the Nitsupon Consulate in Changtu, accompanied by two policemen, who had come to investigate their identities.

Japanese Policeman: 'Are you the person called Okazaki Tetsushu?'

He asked this while casting a suspicious glance at Okazaki, who was in military uniform. Okazaki showed off his usual business card and Jireishiyo, and while laughing with his usual wide mouth, 'Ahahaha,' he said:

Okazaki: 'Is it not already past midnight? It is truly a nuisance to come at such an hour. What is your business?'

Japanese Policeman: 'Eh, a report came from the Shina police just now that Nitsupon people were staying here, so I came out to inquire about it for the time being.'

Okazaki: 'Ya, that was a lot of trouble for you. Do not worry about it. Although I am a Nitsupon person, I have come here to inspect the interior of Shina under the orders of Zhang Zuolin of the Tōsanshiyō, so I will never cause any worry for the Nitsupon Consulate.'

Japanese Policeman: 'Where are these three people from? They do not seem to be Shina people, do they?'

Okazaki pointed to Hideo and said:

Okazaki: 'This person is the master of the Fengtian Ping'an Tong Shuiye Shōkai. He has come for the purpose of commercial inspection.'

Japanese Policeman: 'Ah, is that so? Then he is a Nitsupon person. When will you be leaving?'

Okazaki: 'Yes, the automobile is broken, so it cannot move. I have sent someone to Fengtian to get parts, so I intend to depart after the messenger returns and repairs are made. First, around two o'clock tomorrow afternoon. Until then, I intend to stay here in this cheap hotel. Ahahaha.'

Japanese Policeman: 'Do you have a passport?'

Okazaki: 'What would I need a passport for? An official of the Tōsanshiyō is traveling within the Tōsanshiyō, so ahahaha.'

He masked it with a laugh. The Nitsupon Jiyunsa replied,

Jiyunsa: 'Ya, I have caused you an Ojiyama.'

He went back, and Hideo, with a somewhat worried look, said,

Hideo: 'Okazaki-san, you told the Shina Jiyunkei and Gunsau that you were a Gaushiyō from the Nanshinhaumen region, but then you told the Nitsupon Kuwanken that you were a Nitsuponjin. This will surely arouse suspicion at the Riyōjikuwan, and they might come to conduct a Chōsa again tomorrow morning. We must think of something.'

Okazaki, scratching his head, replied,

Okazaki: 'I talked too much and ended up saying something Heta. Naani, what does it matter? If they come from the Riyōjikuwan tomorrow, I will just blow them away with my Sanzun Zetsupō. Sensei, please leave it to Okazaki; I will not cause you any Gomeiwaku. Aha-ha-ha!'

He laughed softly.

Hideo: 'In any case, it will be a Mendau if the Riyōjikuwanin come, so shall we decide to have one vehicle depart ahead early tomorrow morning?'

Okazaki: 'In that case, Sensei and I shall depart first to a place called Daishikashi, about twenty Shiri to the north. Let us decide that Moritaka-sama and Ō-kun will follow us as soon as the repairs are finished.'

Okazaki: 'That would be best.'

Having said this, he lay down and immediately fell asleep, snoring like thunder. Okazaki and the other two were also so exhausted from the journey that they lost consciousness and slept soundly until the night began to brighten.
