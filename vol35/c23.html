Chapter 23: Pure 交 [987]

清交〔987〕

§ 1

Near the gate of the Fire Kuniyakata, singing a Sendenka while entering, was one Sendenshi, Tamaharuwakenomikoto.

Kami appearing in the outer world, Dividing the zenshin and jiyashin, Kurohime, lost in love, At the Seijiyō of Onokorojima, Morn and eve with all her heart, Serving and fulfilling her husband, Takayamahiko no Kamuzukasa, Having crossed to the island of Tsukushi, Fixing it firmly in a single heart, Enduring a hundred sorrows, The Senden of Ananai-kyō, Continuing to chant as we go over the waves, Treading across Tsukushi-ga-dake, Shunning the roots of rocks and trees, A valley path where human footprints are rare, Mukafutauge and Yakatamura, Looking back behind at Araidake, Accompanied by two attendants, Climbing up and descending down the steep slope of the first province of Fire, Proceeding onward. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara, If Kurohime Tsukasa should appear here now, How surprised she surely would be! Takayamahikogami, whom I thought of with all my heart as my true husband, Was not my true husband, But the husband of a person I never expected, Entrusted to the hand-scrolls of the hands of the Great Deity who shines in the heavens, In the divine essence of the Five Hundred Misumaru jewels, He was none other than the living Hayatuhikone no Kamuzukasa, Known as Takakuniwake; If she hears this, even the stout-hearted Kurohime, How astonished she surely will be! Thinking of it, thinking of it, how touching she is! Even a single day sooner, even a single moment, To clear away and help Kurohime-sama's delusion, On the wondrous island where the Kami gather, Leading her back to the high Plain of High Heaven, Together with Takayamahiko, Rejoicing and growing close, serving the true path of Smeragi no Kami, Let her serve purely in the divine work of the Mani no Hoju! Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara, Reverence be to the words of Kami Susanuo no Ōkami, In the land of Fusa, Erecting thick great shrine pillars at the summit of Mount Ubusuna, Stand towering the Saienkan, Leaving the sacred ground far behind, Crossing the Eden River in a boat, Crossing the ocean of Fusa, Landing at the port of Tatehi in Kumaso-koku on the island of Tsukushi, Wishing to help Kurohime-sama, I have advanced thus far! Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara, May your august spirits be blessed!

So singing, he appeared near the gate.

Karu-kimi, the gatekeeper, was emboldened by this Sendenka, straightened his formal posture, opened the gate, and said, 'Hearing that Tamaharuwake no Kami no Mikoto has come forth, How my heart is emboldened! I hear that Kurohime Tsukasa is coming forth, Following behind Takayamahiko no Kami no Mikoto. Two persons among Kurohime's attendants, Powerless and weeping, have returned. Tamakuniwake no Kami no Mikoto no Kamuzukasa Has come forth to Katsura Waterfall. Tamaharuwake no Kami no Mikoto, swiftly Pass through the iron gate and enter the inner chambers. How deep is the grace of Kamunagara no Kami, That today I meet a venerable Kami! How blessed it is at the coming forth of Tamaharuwake, That the august sky has also cleared up so purely! How beautiful is the august spirit of Tamaharuwake, Resembling even the stars of the great sky!'

He praised to the utmost and welcomed him.

Tamaharuwake: 'Karu-kimi, who guards the iron gate of the beautiful capital of the Land of Fire, you are indeed a brave boy! I am none other than Tamaharuwake no Kamuzukasa, And I wish to meet the lord of the mansion. I hear that Takakuniwake no Kami no Mikoto, though brave indeed, has come forth to Katsura Waterfall. Nevertheless, the beloved princess must be within; I wish to speak on her behalf.'

Karu-kimi, the gatekeeper: 'The lord of the divine mansion is not present now, Yet I shall cause you to meet the beloved princess. Tamaharuwake no Kami no Mikoto, I now Shall guide you for your august sake.'

Tamaharuwake: 'Now indeed has human feeling been known, In the words of the person guarding the iron gate. Kurohime will presently come to this place; Pass her through easily, you who guard the iron gate.'

Karu-kimi: 'Though I have no means to pass Kurohime through easily, There is no recourse against your words. To whom other than you should I open this iron gate, Without the permission of the lord of the mansion?'

Tamaharuwake: 'Come now, let us proceed to the wondrous mansion, With heart and feet thanks to the grace of Karu-kimi.'

Thus responding, before he knew it, he arrived at the entrance. Karu-kimi turned toward Tama-kimi at the reception and chanted a poem:

Karu-kimi: 'Tama-kimi, from now on guard the outer iron gate; I shall henceforth become the receptionist.'

Tama-kimi: 'Come now, standing at the exterior in a majestic manner, I shall guard the iron gate according to your august command.'

With that, he stepped briskly out to the front to resume his role as gatekeeper.

§ 2

This Karukō is a Sendenshi whom Tsugarunomikoto, the master of the mansion, relies upon as his right arm. Tsugarunomikoto turns toward Tamaharuwakenomikoto and chants thus:

Tsugarunomikoto: 'Come quickly now and pass into the inner chamber; Aikohime awaits you.'

Tamaharuwakenomikoto: 'Though the master Kami of the Kamuyakata is not present, What shall I say to the lord of my heart?'

Guided by Tsugarunomikoto, he proceeds forward toward the inner chamber. Hearing of the arrival of Tamaharuwakenomikoto, Aikohime hurriedly adjusts her garments, smooths her hair, and walks serenely toward this place. As she steps into the long corridor, she encounters Tamaharuwake by chance,

Aikohime: 'Aikohime who guides and saves the people of the world, has come hither to welcome you. Hearing that your Mikoto comes to this mansion, I have awaited you day after day with wearying longing. O lovely Kami-tsukasa Tamaharuwake, Your lofty Mina truly echoes through the world. Ah, to behold and look up at the pure form of the Kami before my very eyes, Today is a day of joy.'

Tamaharuwake replies to this,

Tamaharuwake: 'Kami-tsukasa of the high capital of fire of lofty name, Are you indeed the lady of my heart, how wondrous. The beloved daughter of the Kami Susanō no Mikoto, I have come yearning for the Mikoto, the Hime. Your lovely Mikokoro is made manifest, And even your Misugata shines brilliantly. Like the moon in the bright Amatsumisora, Now I gaze upon your pure Misugata.'

Chanting thus, Aikohime, with Tsugarunomikoto guarding her before and behind, proceeds leisurely into the interior of the chamber. Within the inner room, the three sit in a tripod formation, opening their hearts to each other and conversing happily.

Aikohime: 'Tamaharuwake-sendenshi-sama, how very far you have come to visit us. Unfortunately, my husband Takakuniwake set out this morning to the Katsura Waterfall for purification, offering a hundred-day vow, and so he is away; while it is truly inconvenient, please rest at your leisure and tell us wondrous tales of various lands.'

Tamaharuwake: 'Yes, thank you very much.'

As Aikohime relates the whereabouts of her younger sister one by one and details her active deeds, Aikohime, with an expression of indescribable delight, speaks:

Aikohime: 'Tamaharuwake-sama, you too have endured considerable hardships. For the sake of Kami and for the sake of the people of the world, I pray you will serve in your divine duties in good health.'

Tamaharuwake: 'Yes, thank you very much. I too am of a fortune-crossed station, abandoned by my parents, and am truly a solitary person, but ever since I converted to Izumokyou, I took the younger sister of Kuniyoriwake-sama as my wife, and now husband and wife unite our strength and devotedly attend to the service of Kami. Ah, even if one speaks of hardships, since it is hardship walking the path together with Kami, no matter what land I visit, it is truly unbearable in its pleasantness.'

Aikohime: 'Ah, is that so. I too wish somehow to tour the countries of the world like my sister, but no matter what I say, being a person with a husband, it does not go as I might wish. It seems one can do no other service than that which accords with the karma of one's spirit and soul.'

Tamaharuwake: 'Indeed, that is likely so. At times, Kurohime-sama of Izumokyou has a master named Takayamahiko, but in the sacred land of Aya, they quarreled over a trifling matter, and Takayamahiko-sama, declaring he would go to the island of Tsukushi, disappeared somewhere. Therefore, Kurohime-sama, believing firmly that Takayamahiko-sama must be in Tsukushi Island, has crossed oceans and mountains far to come to this land. Since the other Mina of Takakuniwake-sama is called Takayamahiko, Kurohime-sama is convinced he is none other than her own husband, and before long she will likely come hither; therefore, I have a favor to ask of you. Please let her pass into the inner rooms and listen to her story for a moment. This is the request of Tamaharuwake.'

Aikohime: 'That indeed is a strange matter. The Mina of Kurohime-sama's master was also Takayamahiko, was it not? Is the whereabouts of that Takayamahiko-sama known to you?'

Takayamahiko-sama: 'Thinking that Takayamahiko-sama had crossed over to Africa, I instead found him appearing as ever in the Seichi, serving the Kami day and night. Whenever I see that, my heart aches with pity for Kurohime-sama's okokorone. I thought of once going to the residence in the Iso where the Lord God Kamususanōnōokami dwells, crossing over here anew, meeting Kurohime-sama, and letting her know. Combining this with senden, I have come alongside to welcome her.'

Goshinsetsu: 'That is truly a goshinsetsu matter. If Kurohime-sama were to hear your goshintei, she would surely be overjoyed.'

Takayamahiko-sama: 'Yes, arigata. According to what I have heard, I think we will be able to have a gomenkuwai with Kurohime-sama within this residence before too long.'

Goshinsetsu: 'I too understand it to be sayō. I do hope she comes over quickly.'

Just as they were speaking thus, the monban Tamakō hurriedly entered, and said,

Tamakō: 'An old hag called Kurohime of Ananai-kyō has appeared at the omotemon, bringing five araotoko, and is saying to open this gate... What shall we do?'

Tsugarunomikoto said genka,

Tsugarunomikoto: 'Tamakō, thank you for your trouble, but quickly open the omotemon and guide Kurohime-sama's party here.'

Tamakō wore a suspicious expression and said,

Tamakō: 'Hé, is it all right to let through even an old hag who brings along so many bad fellows? She was saying something like she is Takayamahiko-sama's wife, you know. If you were to drag in such an old hag, it would be terrible. Above all, Yoshikohime-sama would surely be troubled.'

Tsugarunomikoto: 'It does not matter, so go quickly and welcome them.'

Tamakō: 'Hai.'

Replying thus, the monban ran off toward the front.
