夢の誡〔974〕

§ 1

Sankō of the village of Yakata, and the men nicknamed the Otokodate, like a kauriu climbing to the ten, men of the rough-hewn araotoko of the village of Takeno, boasting far and wide. Torakō, together with Lady Oai, accompanied by Magokō, to pacify through the Kototama of the teachings of Kami the magakami lurking in the waters of the kosui of the soft-shelled turtle, and to avert the hundreds of misfortunes of the people of the world. Two men of kefukaku spirit, their feet eager upon the yamamichi, winding to the right and to the left, receiving the blazing summer sun upon their heads, press forward. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara, may your mitama be blessed and prosper.

The party of four finally approached the foothills of Shirayamatauge. It is a considerably large mountain pass, said to be three ri of ascent and three ri of descent. Already the summer sun has sunk into the western sky, and a sultry breeze caresses the faces of the party, gently passing by while shaking the treetops on all sides.

Magokō: 'My compass seems quite exhausted. Fortunately the sun has set, so shall we ask for a night's lodging around here?'

Torakō: 'No matter what, it's such a steep slope, so traveling by night is dangerous. Why don't we camp out for the night right at the foot of this pass, hey Sankō...'

Sankō gave a single word, 'Fine,' and nodded happily. The party stuffed themselves with plenty of plucked tree leaves to spread out, and upon the improvised mat of green leaves, they fell fast asleep, oblivious to the weariness of the long journey.

Magokō, hearing the thunderous snoring of the three men, found it impossible to sleep, so he sat cross-legged in the grass about four or five ken away, looked up at the sky to gaze at the Orion constellation, and prayed for something under his breath.

Toward there rustled the cogon grass, and a single white shadow appeared. Magokō gazed intently at this shadow without blinking, wondering if it was the emergence of some mysterious being. The mysterious shadow appeared timidly before Magokō, and in a gentle woman's voice,

Yoshikohime: 'Excuse me, traveler-sama, I have a request.'

Magokō: 'Yaa, aren't you a woman? You have a bold spirit to come all alone to such a grassy plain. What sort of thing is it you want to ask of me, out with it. Depending on the matter, it's not as though I cannot lend you my aid...'

Yoshikohime: 'Yes, thank you very much. I will tell you the details later. Please do follow me.'

Magokō: 'If it's a nearby place that's fine, but I must decline anything too far. Look, our three traveling companions are sleeping over there, so I cannot possibly leave them. I am standing night watch for the three of them right here, you see.'

Yoshikohime: 'If that's so, you-sama, are you a lackey to those three gentlemen? What a dim-witted person you are, aren't you. As the saying goes, would you rather be the tail of an ox though it be large, or the head of a chicken though it be small? Besides, with such a large physique as yours, to follow behind such a kefukaku or a high-karas woman—you really are a man of no backbone, aren't you.'

Magokō: 'Hey you woman, don't make fun of me. I am by no means a lackey to those three fellows. A man among men who cannot be pushed around or shaken, Magokō the propagandist of Sankyō—that is who I am. You had better not mistake me so greatly. And you, to look upon a man like me as spineless and make a request—what is the meaning of this? You must be a pretty spineless woman yourself.'

Yoshikohime: 'Ho ho ho ho, thinking I wouldn't know, pretending to be a propagandist of Sankyō and the like, how well you were able to tell such a lie. Aren't you Magokō of Jitendōshima who came accompanying a propagandist named Kurohime? It is still too early for you to become a propagandist, since your qualifications are not yet complete, so please try not to blow such tall tales. In this land of Kumaso there are bad people, but no matter how bad they are, they do not tell lies. The people of Jitendōshima are good at lying, and that is why people of other countries call them a dangerous race and instigate anti-Japanese thought everywhere, so you should take this to heart a little.'

'Ah, do not say such inauspicious things. As if I could endure having a dying day like a wandering ghost or something.'

'Ho ho ho ho, do you still think you are alive? Your soul died long ago in the distant past, and only your trunk remains. So to speak, you are a living corpse of this mundane world. It is only natural that you should have a dying day.'

誠の教を諾ひて 夢をば醒せ目を醒せ 唯今其処に現はれし 怪しの女は何者ぞ 汝が心に潜みたる 横恋慕と名のついた 八十の曲津の化身ぞや 一日も早く宣り直せ 汝が姿も見直して 虎公三公両侠の 清き御魂に神習ひ お愛の方を思ひ切り 心にさやる白山の 峠を早く踏みしめて 誠一つに進み行け 朝日は照るとも曇るとも 月は盈つとも虧くるとも 仮令大地は沈むとも 大蛇の曲は荒ぶとも 三五教の神の道

誠一つは世を救ふ あゝ惟神々々 神の御言を諾ひて 玉治別の神司 神に代つて説き諭す 進めよ進めいざ進め 誠の道に逸早く 進めや進め湖の傍 大蛇の御魂の亡ぶまで 心の曲津の失する迄 あゝ惟神々々 御霊幸はひましませよ』

と聞ゆるかと見れば、以前の女の影はいつの間にか煙の如く消えて仕舞ひ、夜嵐冷やかに孫公の顔を撫でて通る。ふと気がつけば孫公は三人の寝て居る四五間許りの傍の芝生に横たはつて居た。

『アヽ何だ夢だつたか、しようもない。併しながら夢だとて油断は出来ない。神様が玉治別の名をかりて御注意して下さつたのだらう。俺もこれで大分に悟る事を得た。あゝ惟神々々御霊幸倍ましませよ』

と云ひながら、拍手を打ち、声高らかに天津祝詞を奏上する。
