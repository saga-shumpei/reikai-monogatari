§ 1

Kurohime, gazing at the scenery in all directions,

Kurohime: 'Looking out, all around is hazy, a sea of mist, Where might my dear lord be sitting? In the sea of mist the waves are calm, and in the Land of Fire Cobalt-colored mountains are floating. Coming to Arai-ga-take with a heartfelt mind, Joyful it is today to look out over the four directions. As I gaze, the mountains of the Land of Fire and Mukafuyama, How clear is the figure of Hanamigatake. Of the Land of Fire where lovely mist drifts, The plains are pure and leave no dust. Fields and mountains grow hidden, wrapped in mist, Going astray from my dear lord, perhaps. Though the mountains sink in mist and cannot be seen, The head of Takayamahiko remains visible. Accompanied by Tokukō and Kiukō, the two, A truly fascinating journey we make. Mistaking travelers for thieves, Tokukō and the two felt their hearts throb. Seeing the figures of Toku and Kyu, the travelers Mistook them for thieves and trembled.'

Kurohime: 'A little while longer, whipping the horse of my mind, Let us proceed onward to the capital of the Land of Fire. Following the teachings of the Kami, Kamunagara, It is joyful to travel around the island of Tsukushi. Since I am one born in the Land of the Kami, Day and night I will advance upon the Way of the Kami. The light of the heavenly sun is also wrapped in mist, At the ridge of Arai-ga-take where the wind is calm.'

Tokukō mounts a bandwagon and chants,

Tokukō: 'As I follow along after Kurohime, even the mountain winds Brought out thieves upon Arai-ga-take. Kiukō, who mistook us for thieves, Had his spirit taken and lost his hips. To have become travel companions with a weak and spineless man Is the misfortune and loss of Tokukō. Puffing and blowing up the steep slope with a squeak, Kiukō's wretch of a fellow goes climbing up. The Kami, Kamunagara, appears in the open And turns the fire-kami's land into a sea of mist. Pulling tight the sash of mist, The mountains await the figure of my journey. Climbing up to the summit of the mountain of arrogance, How pitiful is Kiukō, at his wit's end.'

Kiukō, refusing to be outdone, chants again,

Kiukō: 'Upon the bald mountain top, looking like Toku-head has fallen ill, Tokukō's rascal is trembling. With a mouth born ten years too early, A man speaks sophistries with fluent ease. Shall I extract the fat to my heart's content, For the sake of the immoral Tokukō? Fields and mountains are wrapped in haze and mist, Wandering between spring and autumn. If I look to see if it be spring or autumn, it is neither spring nor autumn, Perhaps the strength of a summer's early dawn. Morning mists begin to trail across, and the mountains Can be seen as if they were floating. Those who float such a great mountain Must be the mist, the haze, or the sky of white clouds. Even though it appears to be floating, Hanamiyama, Its roots are hidden in the mists of the Land of Fire. Though this mountain is called Arai-ga-take, A quiet wind blows across it. Where is my leader, Torakō, now? He must be crossing the lonely Shirayama Pass.'

Kiukō: 'Along with Lady Ai, our leader Must be trembling in his heart at the steep mountain pass. More than Sankō's leader, Torakō A man of might with tireless feet is he. By now, leader Sankō must be farting with fear And blowing a weak tune calling for Toku, Toku. Hey Toku, hurry and leave this place behind, You must go search for your weak master. If it comes to this, to serve Kurohime-tsukasa Kiukō alone should be quite enough. Being the travel companion of such a troublesome fellow, she is surely out of patience, rubbing her belly in vexation, there is no mistake about it. Targeted by a man like this, even Kurohimetsukasa cannot endure it. If a garbage dump were anywhere in sight, I would promptly and without a shred of reluctance toss him away and be on my way, I think to myself; yet the mountains are enveloped in haze, and everywhere is the same sea of fog. With not even a place to cast him aside, he is a hopeless nuisance, the very height of extreme vexation. 'Ottodotsukoi,' have you forgotten the teachings of the fine words and beautiful phrases of the Kototama, or wait a moment? Kurohime, Tokukō, what I uttered just now was not me; the assistant guardian spirit who protects your physical body possessed me,

and uttered irreverent Kototama, there is no doubt about it. Do not grow angry, Kamunohohi; O Great Direct Sun, whose heart is vast, serenely look straight and listen straight. Man is the child of God, the palace of God, and there is no enemy that opposes God. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, may your Mitama be blessed and prosper! Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if the capital of the Land of Fire is engulfed and sinks tenfold and twentyfold in the fog, willy-nilly we must accompany Kurohimetsukasa, and we cannot fail to escort her. When I consider the obligation entrusted to us by our boss, there is no way we can back down here and now.

Applying another twist to my arm, splashing oil on my legs, like a Hizakurige crossing mountains and fields, whipping the steed of my heart, shall you and I go on affectionately, pulling each other's hands? Ah, how interesting, how interesting, the eastern sky has cleared up, and the wind blowing now is the eastern wind. My Kototama will surely resonate clearly in the capital of the Land of Fire. Even Takayamatsukasa by now—listen intently to the Kototama proclaimed by Kyūkō, whose divine virtue is peerless. When the living God appears, sake and appetizers will undoubtedly be prepared and waiting; this too is thanks to the divine ministry of Kurohime. Come, let us go, come, let us go!

With this excessive rest, white roots seem to grow out of my buttocks; it is a road we must travel anyway. Advance, press on, come, advance! Tokukō you rascal, go on ahead; Kurohimetsukasa is the rear guard. I, Kyūkō, stand in the middle, acting as the role of the middle-taking minister, and shall we descend such a steep slope without difficulty? Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, may your Mitama be blessed and prosper!

So singing, he stands up. Kurohime puts Tokukō in the lead, and descending step by step with force concentrated in her fingers down the precipitous slope that looks like a built wall.

Tokukō, applying strength step by step, descends singing down the western slope of the famed steep slope Araigamine.

Summer wind crossing the green leaves, whether fog or haze I know not, but one ken ahead cannot be seen. Gradually sinking into the sea of fog, 'Untokodotsukoi,' Kurohime, please be careful of your footing. Unlike other passes, it is the steepest slope in the Land of Fire; one side is a sheer precipice, and the other side is the bottom of a deep valley. If you happen to miss your footing then, this irreplaceable life will be completely turned into jam. Kyūkō you rascal too, be careful, and descend with your heart focused on your toes step by step. If even one person gets seriously injured halfway through, 'Dotsukoishyo,' 'Untokodotsukoi,' to our boss, how on earth could an excuse ever stand?

Accompanying Kurohimetsukasa, to throw away one's life on such a deep slope is not worth it. Hey, hey, this is Ichinoseki; grasp the rocks with both hands, close your eyes and feel with your feet, and slip down stealthily. Ah, ah, what a tough, difficult place this is! How on earth did the woman ahead climb up here so easily, 'Dotsukoishyo'? It becomes so mysterious I cannot bear it. Kurohimetsukasa is elderly; calm your heart and slowly, while putting strength into your legs, holding a walking stick in your left hand and grasping the rock roots with your right hand, please descend quietly. Ah, ah, it is dangerous, if you descend just a little more there is a gentle, safe and secure road. Until we reach that place, 'Dotsukoishyo,' our hearts cannot be at ease no matter what. If we commit any impropriety, to the God Takahimahiko who dwells in the capital of the Land of Fire,

to the extent that we have no face to show, ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, may your Mitama be blessed and prosper, and may you safely and without mishap let us descend this steep slope! God is with us, and we are the children of God, the palace of God. Having said that, 'Dotsukoishyo,' as we still bear human bodies, we cannot act like play-acting gods. Distributing our hearts and taking care, sliding over this difficult barrier, and as quickly as possible to the flowery capital of the Land of Fire, 'Untokoshiyo'.
