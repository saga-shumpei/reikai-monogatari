Chapter 17: Mist の Sea [981]

霧の海〔981〕

§ 1

The green leaves are fragrant, and upon the peak of Araikadake where the mists wander, three men and women sit resting their hips, looking out over the four quarters, and indulging in idle chat.

Though there is scarcely any wind, the morning air feels somehow cool.

Here and there, hazes that are neither quite smoke nor quite mist envelop the entire earth, and from amidst them, cobalt-colored mountain ranges emerge as if floating.

Kurohime: 'Moshimoshi Kurohime-sama, what a magnificent view it is! In midday it is terribly distressing, but being thus enveloped in morning air, feeling the cool breeze, and looking down upon the four quarters, does not a grand feeling drift over you, like that of a monarch who has grasped everything on earth?'

Kurohiko: 'It is truly a magnificent scenery. Around which parts does the capital of Hinokuni lie?'

Kurohime: 'From here straight to the west, there should be a mountain looking as if it floats with a faint blackish hue. That is said to be Hanamikadake soaring to the west of the capital of Hinokuni, and it is the foremost celebrated mountain of Hinokuni. That which is visible to the east is Hinokunikadake; the one a little to the north whose peak appears slightly floating is Mukafuyama; and then straight to the north, peering faintly from within the mist, is Shirayamatauge. All the other mountains are submerged in the sea of mist, so they cannot be seen. If this mist were to clear away smoothly, that would truly be a magnificent spectacle of the realm. Hanachodake, Tengukadake, Koshinoyama, Haruyamatauge, Shiganoyama, and such, splendid green mountains are dotted here and there. The Hinokunigawa river weaving between them flows through the wilderness of Hinokuni windingly like Amanotabatahime spreading out cloth, an indescribable sight. And toward the southwest, there is a quite large body of water called the Dragon Lake, but that too is unfortunately enveloped by the haze. Then, the five-storied pagoda, the famous feature of the capital of Hinokuni, faintly catches the eye when there is no mist. Every time I see it, I am overcome by an indescribable feeling and grow sleepy.'

Yotahiko: 'Tokukō-sama, you are someone very well-versed in geography. If we have such a person to guide us, we will be all right, won't we?'

Tokukō: 'However, this Araitōge is in truth called Miyokadake, but because bandits always come out and do rough things, it came to be nicknamed Araitōge by no one knows whom. Another name for it is Kubikiri-tōge, and it is a dangerous zone where severed heads have never ceased upon this pass. Since the boss of the Sannin knows that this Tokukō is well-versed in geography and moreover a man of great valor, he selected him and commanded him to stand as guide, so no matter what happens, as long as Tokukō is here you are all right, so please be at ease. Even if a thousand or ten thousand thieves should gather in swarms and press forward, I will display a power double that of the enemy, cut and slash, beat them about, drive them away, chase the enemy a thousand ri, and protect the safety of your august persons.'

Yoshikohime: 'Oh-ho-ho-ho, you are quite the braggart of the mouth!'

Tokukō: 'Now, that is just so! Since it is the Land of the Gods where the blessings of Kototama assist and sustain life, being able to be bold when one is bold, and being able to regret when one is regretful is the truth of heaven and earth and the fundamental meaning of the science of Kototama; thus, speaking strongly with all one's might and subduing the malevolent deities of Araikadake is Tokukō's one-rin mechanism—truly a most valiant state of affairs, ha-ha-ha-ha.'

Yoshikohime: 'Oh-ho-ho-ho, what an energetic Tokukō-sama!'

Hisakō: 'Oi Toku, morning is already breaking. What sleep-talking are you uttering? Go perform your ablutions somewhere.'

Tokukō: 'Oi Hisaku, it is you who are talking in your sleep, surely. Otherwise how could you utter such foolish things? Think it well over. In such a high mountain peak, even if you say to perform ablutions, is there any water? That is why I say you are talking in your sleep.'

Hisakō: 'I am not saying to use water having physical form. I said to use ablutions with formless clear water. Since it is a land where the blessings of Kototama assist and sustain life, the moment I said this, clear water might suddenly gush forth bubbling from this summit. Do not interject too much petty chaff.'

『あゝそれで落着きました……オイお前達、モウ心配するには及ばぬ、気を確に持て。こんな弱い事で荒井峠が越されると思ふか。仮令泥棒の千匹万匹押寄せ来るとも、此鉄公が鉄拳を揮つて、泥棒の群に縦横無尽に飛込んだが最後、さしも暴悪無道の泥棒の群も風に木の葉の散る如く先を争ひ、ムラムラパツと逃げ散つたり。逃げる奴には目はかけず、寄せくる奴は片つぱしからブンなぐり、素首ひきぬき、股をさき手をむしり、子供の人形箱のやうに致してくれむは案の内、ヤア面白し面白し。実に名にし負ふ荒井ケ岳の勇将と、名を万世に轟かす、比ひ稀なる豪傑なり………と云ふ様なものだ。マアマア木ツぱ共、否臆病者共、此鉄公さまに従ひ来れ、オツホーン』

『アハヽヽヽ又久公の二代目が出来ましたなア』

『久公の副守護神が憑依したのですよ、アハヽヽヽ』

旅の男は五人の男女を差し招き、法螺を吹き、空威張りし乍ら、ヤツパリどこかに薄気味が悪いと見え、下り坂になつたを幸ひ、転けつまろびつ、立板に砂利をブチまけたやうに、バラバラと命カラガラ逃げて行く。
