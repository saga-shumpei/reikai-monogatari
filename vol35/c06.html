Chapter 6: Song の Virtue [970]

歌の徳〔970〕

§ 1

Kurohime sings cheerfully to celebrate the reconciliation of both sides. Her song goes:

Flowers of the plum in the three-thousandfold world, When they open all at once, the time is now! On Tenkyōzan and Chikyōzan, And flourishing upon Ōgonzan, The sacred teachings of the Great Deity of Ananai-kyō, Shining brightly without a single dark corner Over all the lands of the four directions, Resound all the way to the depths of the mountains In Tsukushi's Kumasokuni. Truly, how venerable is the Way of God! Starting with Torakō-sama, And those two figures resembling the dragon and the tiger, Famed as the Three Lords of the Great Serpent, Having opened their hearts from the very bottom, Obeyed the venerable divine teachings, O, the awe-inspiring wonder of it! The common people under the heavens, Without any distinction of high or low, Without any aversion for the old or young, men or women, Are the children created by Kuniharutachinōokami

And Toyokuninushinōomikami, So, cherishing and drawing close to one another each other, Living and surviving in the divine age created by God, Nourished by the dewdrops of divine grace, Making the flowers of joy open and bear fruit, Mutually drawing close with the same heart Until the end of a thousand and myriads of generations, To flourish is indeed the duty of human beings Born into this world. Torakō-sama, your most beloved Dear wife and younger sister, Oume, Was ruthlessly persecuted by the Three Lords Under their direct orders; Yet, holding back tears of bitter regret, Reflecting upon the heart of God, O, the reverence of beholding all grudges Turned into pure goodness by Kami-naobi, With a heart as vast as Oonaobi!

God protects us all; Man is the child of God, the palace of God. God and God are goodness and goodness; There is no enemy who opposes goodness. Ah, A-Nagara-Kami-Kami, May your august spirits be blessed and manifest, And continue the joy of today's birth-day Unchanged for a thousand and eight thousand generations, O Heavenly Deities, Earthly Deities, and myriad deities, Before the august presence of the deity of the national soul, Kurohime of Ananai-kyō, Reverently, respectfully, and joyfully, Offers these words of gratitude. Come, from now on, all people, Cast away the barriers of your hearts, Draw close like parents and children, Polish the jewel of your hearts, And obey the Great Way of God! Whether the morning sun shines or is cloudy,

Whether the moon is full or wanes, Even if the great earth should sink, Sincerity's power saves the world; Sincerity is the treasure of this world. Like the adamantine indestructible wish-granting jewel, How brilliantly the Mani jewel shines! No matter how venerable it may be, Can it ever surpass the sincere soul? Polish your souls, O people! How joyful is the body that reveres The achievements of the soul eternally! Ah, A-Nagara-Kami-Kami, May your august spirits be blessed and manifest!

Tokukō stood up sharply and began to sing:

Ah, A-Nagara-Kami-Kami, Receiving the grace of God, Tokukō here now respectfully sings This song of Kototama. That person called the Three Lords of the Great Serpent Is truly a person with a bad stomach. Listening to it through the recent sacred song, Having been made to hear the principle of heaven and earth By the incarnation of Konohanahime in the middle of last night, Blowing away the clouds of the heart, And becoming a righteous soul, He puts on an ignorant face until this very moment, Urging on Tokukō-sama, Saying that Oai-sama, who was buried At the mountain foot of Mukōtōge, Should be rescued as quickly as possible, Taken away to some mountain, And coaxed successfully by you, So that she will eventually utter, 'Even with a bamboo pillar and a reed roof,

Even with a hanging iron pan, I do not mind,' So do break her down and win her over successfully! Urging him on with such a plausible air, And urging the wine-intoxicated Toku-sama Without any rhyme or reason, He is truly a person with a bad stomach. Unlike other matters, this is a different affair; There is a limit even to joking. I am so astonished that words fail me; I have truly been made a fool of. If I had known it would come to this, I would not have worried so much. When I think of it, how foolish it is! Even the fellow Yosankō was deceived; Truly, Tokukō, if we do not hurry, Master will roll his big eyeballs, He rattled off threats, Making steam rise from his octopus-like bald head, When I think of the things he urged upon me,

It has come to the point where I can no longer tell Whether it is a dream, reality, or an illusion. Today, too, an unexpected banquet of sake; The whole world is extremely peaceful. When that fellow Kanekō staggered Back here to this place, My heart was crushed, wondering if the ghosts Had come to take our heads; The agony of having my lower back go limp! Nevertheless, if one gazes intently Upon the faces of everyone, The color of every face is one of delight; Conjecturing that this fellow must have no serious trouble, I put on a bold front and asked Torakō To please massage my lower back, And when I thrust out my lower back—which had not even slipped— For the sake of testing the waters, Torakō, having forgotten his grudge, Said, 'You are a troubled fellow,' while...

Revolving behind me with a tsu, the kindness of rubbing my back; seeing this, this Tokukō also stroked his thumping chest with relief, and thought, 'Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara! Please drink plenty of the sacred omiki of Kami. Since it is not I who am offering it, it is the sweet wine that the oyabun of the Orochi, Sankō, had kept hidden—a wine of deep lineage, thrown out without regret as if leaping over Jigoku, cast away for the sake of shiyakuwai. You must never feel enriyo. Drink until you are dead drunk like a zuburoku, until your tongue cannot roll, your eyes cannot see, and your legs cannot even stand, without any enriyoeshiyaku.'

'Drink it down gulping and guping, for it is the wine of the oyabun! My futokoro will not ache. Truly ketsukō, ketsukō, a medeta thing has come to pass. "Dokukoidokuiko dokukoishiyo." Wine to the sakaya, good tea to the chiyaya, and the betsupin is here. Lady Aoi and Lady Ume, please break the ice from now on and pour me a cup of amazake, won't you? Kurohime is a little up in years, but she is still undeniably a woman. Rather than receiving it from a man's hand, a woman's hand makes it taste better. Come, come, everyone, let us dance, drinking, eating, leaping about, singing, and passing the night.'

'Even the spirit of the Yamata no Orochi slipped away through the body of Sankō-sama, making clouds of mists, and now it is the venerable shrine of Kami. There is no kami to whom the omiki is not offered. Sankō Oyabun, the guardian kami, must be oh-so-very satisfied. Lady Aoi and Torakō, and everyone present are also satisfied. Along the way, I am also satisfied. With thousands of guests coming forever, day and night without distinction, getting drunk on wine, I want to live in amusing joy. This is the virtue of a lifetime. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara! I do not care whether it is warmed or cold, pour me a cup quickly! Pour me another one while you are at it. Truly, it is a sweet wine! While holding such good wine,'

'-possessed by the spirit of the Orochi, hiding it from us, Sankō-sama must have been drinking it! The amazake brought out yesterday was like spoiled wine. Now from here on, Oyabun, since you too have reformed, you should drink the bad wine and make your kobun drink plenty of the good wine. As proof that the atrocious and lawless Sankō has been illuminated by the light of Kami and has reformed, so that there may be no mistake, please swallow down well what this Tokukō says. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara! The warmed wine is even sweeter,'

singing whatever came into his mouth while dancing wildly.
