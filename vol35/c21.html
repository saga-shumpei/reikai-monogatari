§ 1

Fusakō and Yoshikō, both of them,

Leaving Takehi's mansion behind,

Chasing after Kurohime-sama's footsteps,

Up the steep mountain paths, tonton,

With a twisted headband and tucked-up skirts,

Making steam rise from their Yakuwanatama heads,

Pursuing and coming to see,

The climbing entrance of the Hinokuni Pass,

Kurohime-sama's figure,

Is it cloud or mist, a demon or a kami?

Vanishing with a doron, leaving not even a shadow,

"Untokodotsukoi," climbing such

A fierce slope with an "Untokoshiyo,"

How could Kurohime-sama's elderly self

Have climbed on up?

With neither shadow nor shape to be seen,

The two quickened their paces,

Along the slope where old trees flourished,

"Enyarayā Enyarayā,"

Puffing and panting, heaving and blowing,

Dragging their feet and slipping,

Like planks set upright, "Dotsukoishyo,"

Up such a steep slope,

Crawling along like a rabbit,

The master herself, Kurohime,

Mistaking her path and crossing

A log bridge to the middle of the forest,

Not knowing that she is saving

Aio and the other men,

How pitiful as she presses onward.

By the time the sun had completely set, the two finally reached the summit of the Hinokuni Pass. There stood seven or eight interesting-branched yamamomo trees, presumptuously covering the sky.

Yoshikō: 'Oi Yoshikō, though we have run with all our might like this, we cannot catch up with Kurohime-sama. Surely our path must have been the wrong one, I wonder.'

Yoshikō: 'That is true. It is somehow suspicious. There was indeed a narrow path leading to the right at the foot of the slope, and perhaps she wandered off into that direction. No matter how you think about it, is there any other road besides that? Well, at any rate, let us borrow a lodging under this tree for tonight. Since it is too late to hurry along the night road when we can just ask if someone happens to pass by, I am quite exhausted anyway.'

Fusakō: 'If that is so, there is no help for it. Yoshikō, shall we stay the night?'

So saying, the two spread their straw capes and lay down with a guren.

There came an old man with white hair, climbing up from the west while tapping the small stones of the path with the tip of his staff, clinking-clanking. The old man drew near to the place where the two were resting, and with the tip of his staff, he repeatedly poked them one by one right around their foreheads, guigui. Saying "Aita!", the two sprang up with a gabatsu, and peering through the dim light,

Fusakō: 'Who, who on earth are you? The fellow who poked my head with a staff—I will not let you get away with such a playful trick!'

Old Man: 'Ahaaa, because it was far too dark... When I drew near upon hearing something snoring, I saw one shining thing in the gloom, and right beside it, another black thing lying down. Thinking that this might be a tanuki's testicles, I just poked it lightly with the tip of my staff. No matter what you say, it was dark, and as an old man my eyes are dim, so please bear with it even if one or two heads got cracked. No matter how angry you get, it is the rule that the elderly must be treated with care...'

Fusakō: 'I know not what old man you are, but even if the deed done in ignorance cannot be helped, to offer not a single word of apology and instead roll out a discourse on respecting the elderly—you are a bold fellow. No doubt you are a spirit of Kēshū. Come, reveal your true form!'

Old Man: 'Ohoooo, no doubt I am a spirit of Kēshū anyway, but since there was a shining Yakuwanatama head in the kind of darkness that would make even me lose my nerve, I just poked it with the tip of a mud-stained staff, thinking it might be a withered tanuki's testicles. So please understand my intention. They say that an unknown kami brings no curse, so do not vent your venom on an old man like that.'

Fusakō: 'If you please, grandfather, what was done in ignorance cannot be helped. Since we two travelers did not put up a notice stating that we were staying under this tree, we cannot say anything even if we were mistaken. However, have you perhaps happened to meet an old woman of about fifty called Kurohime?'

'I somehow keep bumping into black things one after another, but since the other party just passed by in silence, I cannot tell at all whether it is Kurohime, Kurogitsune, a bear, or a crow!'

'Grandfather, in this darkness, wherever on earth do you intend to go?'

'I have no choice, as I have two good-for-nothing sons who should be climbing up this slope right now, and I have come to meet them.'

'Hey, and what kind of people are those two sons of yours?'

'Well now, one of them is a short man with a head shining like a kettle even on a dark evening, whose waist is slightly bent. And the other is a thirty-year-old man with a somewhat large build, but he has a bad habit and is a weak sumo wrestler—a troublesome son who loses and loses and goes on losing, and was even nicknamed a potlid by people. While accompanying a sendenshi named Kurohime, he got drunk on unsavory treat-wine, lost sight of his essential master, came to a place like this, and is sleeping leisurely... he is a good-for-nothing son who defies description...'

Roaring with a voice like thunder, the two were startled by this voice and leaped up, rushing blindly about three or four shaku in the darkness, and Fusakō and Yoshikō collided head-on with rapid speed, shooting sparks out of their two eyes, and clutching their eyes as they cried,

'Ouch, ouch, ouch, ouch!'

and crouched down facing each other.

'Hahaha, Fusanomaru and Yoshinomaru have collided, haven't they. I wonder if there was any major bankruptcy. It looked as though an engine shed had exploded, a quite magnificent flash indeed, wahahaha!'

'Hey, you transformed old man, where is there someone so immoral and unfeeling as to laugh as if it were amusing upon seeing another person's distress, you are a dung-grandfather just like a demon!'

'Just as you say, I am a dung-grandfather with not a sight to be seen. I have eye-dung, tooth-dung, ear-dung, and nose-dung—I do not wear hip-dung like you, but I am a thoroughly filthy dung-grandfather.'

'Hey, dung-grandfather, don't say such impertinent things as that I wear hip-dung. Having vomited that your eyes were hard to see in this darkness, how on earth do you even know about hip-dung? Dung is so astounded that the outhouse would dance.'

'What a terribly noisy man you are, my word. I am called the Sage of the Land of Fire, and I am the cow's hip that knows, knows, knows through everything no matter what it is. Because I even know down to how many hairs are on your hips...'

'Hey you transformed old man, if so, try guessing how many hairs are on my hips!'

'Ohohoho, as I look here, aren't there not even a single one? It seems you doted on O-Taki's Sometame, and got your hip hairs plucked out to the very spot where not a single one remains. It is a black hip entirely like the cut end of a burdock root or the cut end of charcoal.'

'What on earth are you vomiting out. Won't you draw back already, you hole of a hip!'

'Even if you tell me to draw back, it is a prolapse I have been suffering from for about ten years, so it won't easily draw back, you see. And this too is because I have good-for-nothing sons called Fusakō and Yoshikō, and because that became a worry such an illness arose. To think there were such unfilial sons. It would be good if such fellows soon met with divine punishment and were swallowed by the great serpent of the Fire Country Pass, so that the nuisance that blocks this mundane world would be gone... Since the gods have appeared in the open to reconstruct good and evil, anyway the lifespans of the two good-for-nothing sons cannot be long. Ah, what a pitiful yet gratifying thing it is, ohohoho!'

With neither reserve nor mercy, he suddenly revealed his figure in the gloom and jeered. Fusakō, bleeding from the nose in the previous head-on collision, could not even utter a word from the pain, and lay prostrate on the ground weeping. The old man began to chant in a withered voice,

Escorting Grandmother Kurohime, Both persons whose hearts are also dark, Climb up the dark mountain pass, Amidst the dark clouds seeing neither back nor front, Taking off the rudders of their hearts, Face and face collided, The kettle-head struck its nose, With bright red nose-bleed trickling down, How touching to crouch down shedding it, Enticed by Preceptor Kurohime, The hardships of a companion who followed from afar, Forsaking them to stride along resolutely, Climbing all the way up Mount Takayama in a dash, An unfeeling deed, Not something that can be done with a calm face, In the hearts of you two, There dwells a black-faced demon Even worse than Kurohime, Driving away those demons,

As the song ended, the figure of the mysterious old man vanished like smoke, and afterwards, the sound of the pine wind blowing across the mountain ridge could be heard rustling away.

Oi Fusakō, how is it, has your nose bridge gotten a little better? Since you always walk around with such a high nose, the Kami who just appeared tried to twist your nose off to make you repent. You are always so full of arrogance and keep your nose in the air, which is why you met with such an experience. That business about the Tengu along the way refers to you.

Whatever you say is fine. I am already so terrified I cannot care about anything else. That must have undoubtedly been the great Tengu of this mountain. Didn't he know everything about us?

Stop talking about Tengu already. Hearing about Tengu makes the back of my neck feel all chilled and spooky. Ah, Kannagara Tamashii Sakihau Mase Yo.

Staying here long is frightening. Let us go. Kurohime-sama is probably waiting for us in the Land of Fire.

Even if you say let us go, with such a steep slope and darkness upon us, there is nothing we can do. Well, let us recite the Amatsu Norito here, pray to Kami, and wait for the night to pass.
