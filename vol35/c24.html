Chapter 24: Joy の涙 [988]

歓喜の涙〔988〕

§ 1

Aikohime, hearing of Kurohime's visit, comes out to the entranceway together with Tsugarunomikoto, somewhat apprehensively. Tamaharuwake remains behind, arms folded alone, lost in some contemplation. Kurohime stands at the entranceway,

Kurohime: 'Following after the august life of Takayamahiko, I, the Kurohime Tsukasa, have come hither. How is it that the august life of Takayamahiko does not come forth to welcome me?'

Aikohime: 'Ah, how awe-inspiring! That the Kurohime Tsukasa has come from afar fills my heart with joy. Now, hasten and ascend into the inner chambers of the mansion, for you are she whom I have been awaiting. The august life of the god Tamaharuwake has also come forth and waits for your arrival.'

Kurohime: 'Now then, if you have no objection, I shall advance to the inner room and speak my questions to my husband. Oh, the heartlessness of the august life of Takayamahiko, to abandon me and reach to such a land! Without a thought for my aged body, what is the meaning of taking a wife like young grass? Ah, what a lamentable appearance is your august life, when I think that it is the very image of my lord and husband!'

Aikohime: 'Hear me, O minister of the deity Kurohime! My lord and husband is the deity Takakuniwake. Though he is called by the august name of the deity Takayamahiko, he is indeed none other than the deity Ikutsuhikone. Be that as it may, enter the inner chambers, O Kurohime, lord and minister of the deity of Ananahi.'

Kurohime: 'If that is so, I shall rush further inward from here, and without listening to yes or no, I will investigate and see! Not knowing that this world is full of deception, how sorrowful is the heart that has wandered hither!'

Aikohime: 'Let the clouds of doubt be clearly cleared away by looking upon the painted portrait of my lord and husband.'

Kurohime: 'Yet still, your words are beyond my understanding; are you not a fox from Araigatake?'

Tsugarunomikoto: 'This is quite something, but your tongue is foul beyond measure, O Kurohime-sama, upon what evidence do you say this?'

Kurohime: 'Leaving Onokorojima behind, the august life of Takayamahiko, who concealed his form, my husband, saying he would cross over to the island of Tsukushi, since he left, abandoning the sacred ground, I, yearning after him, have crossed afar the distant sea routes, have trodden and crossed rugged mountains, exposed to the rain and the fierce wind, combing my hair, trudging along, followed by three attendants, and have advanced to reach this place! Ah, Kannagara, O Kami, O Kami! If the true Kami truly dwell here, Aikohime is cold and heartless, and deep within wraps and conceals the august life of my lord and husband, whitewashing and deceiving me— reveal, O Kami, according to your divine will, before the august presence of the Great Ancestral Kami! As minister of the deity of the teachings of Ananahi, I, Kurohime, reverently pray.'

Aikohime: 'Heaven and earth, and the Kami too, behold the truth! How could Aikohime, whose heart is stained with impurity, in vain tempt another's man and make him her own household's husband? The lord and husband of Kurohime-sama, since you call him Takayamahiko, must be one who, mistaking my husband— who shares the same name but is a different person— for her true husband, has fixated upon him and has grown bewildered. Hear me, O Kurohime Tsukasa! Since I too am one who guards the great Dao of the Kami, how could I ever employ deceptive words? Hasten and advance to the inner chambers; and the doubts of your august life shall vanish like dew in the morning sun. Ah, Kannagara, O Kami, O Kami, may your august spirits be blessed and peaceful!'

So she finishes chanting, and stands downcast, filled with tears. At Kurohime's harsh words, Aikohime feels as though she has been bitterly insulted, and the sadness of a woman's heart wells up within her.

Kurohime: 'They told me that Takayamahiko-sama had gone to some Katsura Waterfall or other for ascetic training and was therefore absent; but with such an enemy before me, this Kurohime is not a woman to back down. A woman's single-minded devotion will pierce even rock, and I will not be satisfied until I have investigated this to the very end! Doubtless he is hiding deep within. Foreseeing through some oracle of Inari or the like that this Kurohime was coming here, no doubt they have all put their heads together, instructed even the gatekeepers, and hidden him away. No matter what one says, what is hidden must come to light, and in the end your tails will show! Heh, excuse me—now then, head clerk, please guide me to the inner chambers. This Kurohime will not budge an inch until the whereabouts of her husband are known; well then, I shall trouble you for a while, ohohoho!'

Aikohime takes the lead and guides her to the inner room. Here, Tamaharuwake sits with his arms folded, lost in some contemplation.

§ 2

Kore sore Oai-sama, thou too seemest quite a hardened rogue, trying thus to bamboozle this old fellow who has passed through a thousand wars and ten thousand horses... Hey, right there sits a man who looks somehow familiar to me. Well now, what on earth is the meaning of this? I figured it must be something like this all along. As expected, Takayamahiko-sama went off to Katsura Waterfall, didn't he? To bring in such a man during his absence—oh, this is simply beyond words, ohohoho!

Moshimoshi Kurohime-sama, do not say such cruel things to a wife who has a husband. It is bad for my reputation.

Whose reputation is bad, pray tell? In place of Takayamahiko's husband, I shall carry out the punishment of the adulterer. Now, Oai-dono, however regrettable it may be, get out at once. Ahh, when Takayamahiko is away, everything is in a complete mess. Unless one performs the Great Purification Rite of the demons once, even the Kami will not deign to settle in this mansion... Hey Oai, why are you lingering and weeping? Why on earth did you do things that require you to weep, ohohoho! Oh, what a truly pitiful thing. Not even a tear of sympathy wells up within me, ufuha-ha-ha, and look at those pathetic, sniffling sobs!

Tamaharuwake raised his face abruptly and said,

Hey, you are Kurohime-sama, I have been waiting since a long time ago. Now please come this way.

What, aren't you Tama? If there is a Tama at the gate, there is also a Tama inside. You must be Oai's paramour, aren't you? What a slip of a man. Chasing after Takayamahiko's coattails to come all the way to a place like this, and having Oai pet you so coquettishly, ohohoho. It is a common custom when one is young. Truly, you are a man of prompt action. You had better boldly put the principle of flesh-first and spirit-second into practice. Youth does not come twice, you see. However, think carefully about it—aren't you already past the slope of thirty? You are not nineteen or twenty, so you ought to know better. But I am not here to criticize your love affair. Now hurry up and elope with Oai right now. When Takayamahiko returns, it will be a huge commotion, so make haste and clear out quickly. I am telling you this out of kindness because I feel sorry for you.

Ah-ah, things have come to such a pathetic pass. Kurohime-sama, I arrived at this mansion just a moment ago. In truth, Takayamahiko-sama parted ways with you, using the parting words that he would cross over to the island of Tsukushi. Thinking that was indeed the case, lo and behold, Takayamahiko-sama had supposedly hidden himself in the inner parlor of Iseya for a while; but from around the time when Kurohime-sama finally set out from Jitentōshima, he popped right out, and every single day he has been going to work at Nishiki no Miya. Therefore, when Yotoribe no Mikoto-sama was about to set out from the sacred ground... saying that Kurohime-sama would be pitiful, he gave the command: 'Though it will be a trouble for you, go as an envoy for propaganda to the island of Tsukushi, go and welcome Kurohime-sama back, and arrange things so that you may be united as husband and wife and serve in the divine work'—and so I have followed your footsteps all the way here. Since such a thing as Akokohime-sama is not even in my dreams, please understand.

Seeing that expression on his face where sincerity was revealed and grief was beyond remedy, Kurohime softened her mind a little and said,

What, Takayamahiko-sama is at the sacred ground? Is that really true?

Why would I tell a lie? Would anyone cross the waves of ten thousandri and come all the way to a place like this to tell a lie? Kurohime-sama, look closely. Since this portrait is the living likeness of our household's master, please examine it well. The thirty-five-year-old stalwart and flourishing Ikutsuhikone Kami-sama of this year is calling himself Takakuniwake and is openly called Takayamahiko, so he is a person of completely the same name but a different person from your master.

Kurohime gazed intently at her portrait, and

Kurohime: 'It is indeed different. ... Oh, Aikohime-sama, I have uttered a most discourteous thing. Please do not think of me as an unrefined woman, and with Kamunahohi, look again and listen again properly, I pray you.'

Aikohime: 'Yes, thank you. Once understanding is reached, there can be no greater joy than this. Please stay comfortably for the night and let us hear the stories of the Kamisama.'

Aikohime: 'Aikohime-sama, Kurohime-sama did not speak with any evil intent. She came here all this way, having so single-mindedly fixed her thoughts upon the master of this house as her own husband, and since it is only natural that she should go half out of her mind, please do not think ill of her.'

Aikohime: 'Yes, thank you very much.'

So she spoke, and in the joy of her doubts being cleared, even the sound of Aikohime's weeping was audible.

Kurohime: 'Ah, is there anyone in the world as burdened by inguwa as I am? When I came searching all this way along the footsteps of my husband, it was a case of mistaken identity; then, thinking it might be the abandoned child I forsook, when I went all the way to the mansion of Takehi, that too proved to be a case of mistaken identity. Why do all my actions and deeds cross and clash with one another to this degree? This must surely be the retribution of sins from zenshiyō; no, the heavenly punishment for abandoning the son granted to me by the Kamisama in the rashness of wakage has come to reward me... Ah, Kamisama, please forgive me. And now that I know the whereabouts of my husband, though it is presumptuous of me, please let me know the whereabouts of my son. I cannot even die without meeting my son once. Ah, Kannagara Tamashii Sakiwaese.'

Thus she prayed facing the kamidana, joining her hands, amidst her tears. Tamajiwake, tilting his head, said,

Tamajiwake: 'Excuse me, Kurohime-sama, I have heard it for the first time just now, but did you have a child? And when did you abandon that child? Actually, I too am an abandoned child, but to this day I do not know my parents, so day and night I pray to the Kamisama, wishing to meet my parents even for a single glance, and even just now I was sinking in sorrow.'

Kurohime: 'What, Tamajiwake-sama, you are also an abandoned child? That is the first I have heard of it. If my child were alive today, he ought to be thirty-five years old. How old might you be, I wonder?'

Tamajiwake: 'Yes, this year I have turned thirty-five.'

Kurohime: 'What, thirty-five years old! Such a mysterious thing can truly happen. However, on the back of the child I abandoned, there should have been a white birthmark in the shape of Mount Fuji right down the center of the spine. Saying that this was a child connected by fate to Konohanahime-sama, I had given him the name Fujisaki, but because the world made too much of a fuss, I wrote the name Fujisaki on an amulet bag and abandoned him at a four-way crossroads. The more I think of it, the more pitiful a thing I did.'

And she sank into weeping.

Tamajiwake: 'What are you saying? Did you say that abandoned child was called Fujisaki? And that there is a white birthmark in the shape of Mount Fuji on the back—these are words that fail to make sense. If I may be excused for a moment, Kurohime-sama, would you look at my back? When I was small, I was called Fujisaki. And according to what people say, some kind of mountain-like birthmark has formed there.'

Kurohime: 'That is news worth hearing indeed! Show it to me for a moment!'

Answering 'Yes,' Tamajiwake bared his skin and thrust forth his back. Kurohime looked closely and scrutinized it, then exclaimed,

Kurohime: 'Ah, it is unquestionably the birthmark of Mount Fuji, and hearing that your childhood name was Fujisaki, you were indeed my son! Ah, I did not know, I did not know! Kamisama, thank you very much. The Ōkami said that wondrous things would happen through the gathering of those bound by karma, and this is truly a gathering of those bound by karma.'

And she was overcome with tears of joy.

Tamajiwake: 'Then were you my mother? Not knowing this, I have behaved discourteously at every turn. Please forgive me, Mother. Ah, Kannagara Tamashii Sakiwaese.'

So saying, he joined both hands and was overcome with tears of joy.

From this time forth, Kurohime expressed deep gratitude to Aikohime, apologized for her discourtesy, thanked Tokukou and Kyūkou as well for their trouble, and bade them farewell. Lighthearted and cheerful, together with Tamajiwake, Sonkou, Hōkou, and Hōkou, she once again rowed out in a boat from the port of Takehi, stopped by at the mansion of Akiyamahiko in the port of Yura, participated in the divine work of Manahakuju, and returned auspiciously to the sacred land—all of which is clearly told in the thirty-third volume. Kannagara Tamashii Sakiwaese.

When this was finished being narrated, the time was precisely six o'clock in the afternoon. When I stepped outside and looked up at the heavens, across the heavily clouded sky, dividing the north and the south, there lay something visible as a blue cloud two or three ken wide, stretching from the edge of the eastern mountains far across the western sky, with precise outlines like a sash or the Milky Way.

と神前に向ひ手を合せ、涙乍らに祈願する。玉治別は首を傾け乍ら、

『モシ黒姫さま、今始めて承はりましたが、貴女にはお子さまがあつたのですか。そして其子はいつお捨てになりましたか。実は私も捨子で御座いますが、未だに両親が分りませぬので、日夜神さまに祈り、一目なりとも両親に会ひたいと、今も今とて憂ひに沈んで居つた所で御座います』

『何、玉治別さま、お前も捨子ですか、そりや初耳だ。丁度私の子が今生きて居つたならば三十五歳になつてる筈だ。お前の年は幾つだつたかなア』

『ハイ、当年三十五歳になりました』

『何三十五歳！　そりや又不思議な事もあるものだ。併し私の捨てた子には、背中の正中に富士の山の形が、白い痣で出て居つた筈だ。これは全く木花咲耶姫さまの因縁のある子供だからといつて富士咲といふ名をつけておいたのだが、余り世間が喧ましいので、守り袋に富士咲と名を書きしるし四辻にすてました。思へば思へば可哀相なことをしました』

と泣き沈む。

『何と仰有います。其捨子は富士咲と申しましたか、そして背中に富士の山の形の白い痣があるとは合点のゆかぬ御言葉、一寸失礼ですが、黒姫さま、私の背中を見て下さいませぬか。私の小さい時は富士咲と申しました。そして人の話によると、何だか山のやうな痣が出来て居るさうです』

『それは又耳よりの話だ。一寸見せて御覧！』

「ハイ」と答へて玉治別は肌をぬぎ背をつき出す、黒姫は念入りにすかして見て、

『ヤアてつきり富士の山の痣、そしてお前の幼名が富士咲と聞く上は、全く私の伜だつたか。アヽ知らなんだ知らなんだ、神さま、有難う御座います。因縁者の寄合で珍らしい事が出来るぞよと大神さまが仰有つたが、いかにも因縁者の寄合だなア』

と嬉し涙にかきくれる。

『そんなら貴女私の母上で御座いましたか。存ぜぬ事とて、何時とても御無礼を致しました。どうぞお母さま御赦し下さいませ。あゝ惟神霊幸倍坐世』

と両手を合せ、嬉し涙にかきくれる。

これより黒姫は愛子姫に厚く礼を述べ、無礼を謝し且つ徳公、久公にも其労を謝し別れを告げ、いそいそとして玉治別、孫公、房公、芳公と共に再び建日の港より船を漕ぎ出し、由良の港の秋山彦が館に立寄り、麻邇宝珠の神業に参加し、目出度く聖地に帰る事となりたるは、三十三巻の物語に明かな所であります。惟神霊幸倍坐世。

○

かく述べ終られた時しも正に午後六時、表に出て天空を見れば、ドンヨリと曇つた大空を南北に区劃した青雲巾二三間と見ゆるもの、東の山の端より西の空遠く、輪廓正しく帯の如く銀河の如く横たはりつつありました。
