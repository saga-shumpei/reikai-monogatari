山颪〔977〕

§ 1

At the peak of Shirayamatauge, as the result of a general election, a Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, calling himself Magokōwake, stood up with great spirit and valor, squaring his shoulders and singing a Sendenka. Just as he was singing and taking heart, a voice of Kami came from nowhere: 'I am Tamaharuwaketsukasa. Being a bonbu whose steps a mere inch ahead cannot be seen, what is the meaning of you acting as a Sendenshi and holding an election? Quickly straighten your heart,' it was told. Magokōwake, crestfallen, with hunched shoulders and marching briskly down the steep slope of Shirayamatauge toward Tōhoku, keeping the beat with his feet, while shouting: 'Untokodotskoi, dotskoishyo! Do not stumble, do not stumble, be careful!'

He goes down shouting out. Magokōwake, with unsteady footing and putting strength into his toes, began to sing as he descended the slope:

At the peak of Shirayamatauge, 'Untokodotskoi, it is dangerous!' To obtain the supreme commander of the departure for the Kototamasen, Tora, San, and Aoi, the three of them, held a council with heads together, and as a result, 'Untokodotskoi,' he slips again. They made me a noble Sendenshi, a messenger of Kami. Does not the proverb of the world also say, 'Untokodotskoi,' names are not guests but the true masters! Sincerely obeying the words of Mizu-no-Mitama, aligning our hearts with one another, the place we have chosen is 'Untokoshyo.' 'Dotskoi,' from the top of my head, while saying that he was Tamaharuwake, with a voice like thunder, 'Dotskoi,' he scolded me. What kind of matter is this again? The giant serpent's rogue must have transformed itself to break our spirits and performed such a farce. Tora, San, and Aoi, you three, do not worry by any means. Deceived by Magatsuhi-no-Kami, how could our divine service ever be fulfilled? Things called evil spirits pay attention from corner to corner, make airtight arrangements, and wait with open arms. Considering that point, 'Untokodotskoi,' you must not rashly advance. Keeping your footing carefully against each other, so as not to ride the smooth-talking words of the serpent, nor the stone cart, you must be cautious.

Be that as it may, I too, for some reason, feeling unwell, 'Untokodotskoi,' my enthusiasm completely vanished. 'Dotskoidotskoi,' wait a while. Did Kami take such a measure precisely to test our hearts? What on earth is it for a bonbu; the situation is entirely incomprehensible. Truly, in human life, all matters are left to the heart of Kami. Once completely entrusted, what kind of matter would fail to be accomplished? Fierce though the serpent may be, we protect Ananai-kyō. There is nothing that can oppose the might of the infinite, merciful Sumeru-no-Kami. Since I have conviction, you all must hold your hearts firm. 'Dotskoishyo, dotskoishyo!'

A steeper slope than expected, 'Garagaragaragarai tatatsu!' For talking too much, as divine punishment, 'Dotskoidotskoi, dotskoishyo!' Slipping and tumbling down, I hit my spine hard. This is unbearable, 'Dotskoishyo.' Be that as it may, 'Untokose.' From now on, if I lose heart, how will anything ever be fulfilled? The divine power of my constant and unceasing faith, 'Untokoshyo,' now is the time for it to manifest. When Kami appears in the open and distinguishes good from evil, right from wrong, if those words are true, do not worry at all, 'Dotskoishyo.' Rejoice, you all, there is no need. Ah, Kannagara-kamigami!

Before the presence of Kunitama-no-Kami no Jun'yohime and Tsukihirohiko, Magokō of Ananai-kyō appeared as Magokōwake, and 'Untokodotskoi,' chosen, now a noble Sendenshi. With heart calm and peaceful, granting permission, grant us the divine power to pacify and harmonize all the giant serpents of the lake of the turtle. Praying with sincere heart before the presence of Kami, 'Untokodotskoi, dotskoishyo!'

A record-breaking wind blows, 'Untokodotskoi,' do not be scattered. If you are careless, your hat will fly off. Not just the hat, but even your body feels as though it will scatter like tree leaves. 'Untokodotskoi,' muscle power, all over the body, 'Untokoshyo.'

Aioata begins to sing in a quiet voice.

Famed even in the land of Kumaso, higher than the peak of Shirayamatauge, serving in the path of the highly praised Ananai, as the attendant of Kurohime-sama, Magokō appears. Yet, the divine will of the Sumeōkami he fails to comprehend atop the mountain. Reaching the summit, enveloped in the clouds of conceit, calling himself a Sendenshi whose name is fairer than his own self, taking the name of Magokōwake, he strides forth in high spirits and rejoices. At that very moment, suddenly in the heavenly skies, the terrifying voice of Kami is heard. Magokō-sama, caught in the desire for fame and profit, remains unaware of it. Taking the Sendenshi as a shield, he descends Shirayamatauge.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, Magokō of Ananai-kyō! Pray, correct your heart, wipe away your attachments, and as the original Magokō once more, go forth to subdue the Orochi! Rather than harboring such ambitions, reform in the present moment, return to your original heart, crouching down in the heavenly skies, and prostrating upon the great earth, walk the path of Ananai with humility. This indeed is Aioata's true kindness toward Magokō; do not take it amiss.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, may divine blessings prevail, and may our party of four grandly establish our achievements before Kami, so that we may return to our hometown as quickly as a single day, O heavenly deities, kunitsukami, the hundred kami, before the presence of the Kunitakamigami, with a heart filled with sincerity we pray to You.'

Sankō descends the slope and begins to sing.

"Yatsutokodotsukoi untokoshiyo!" The lake seen in the distance—our father and mother had their lives taken by what people fear as a crooked deity, a remnant of the Yamata no Orochi, "Dotsukoishiyo!" A lake of deep grievances to remember. Having heard the teachings of Ananai-kyō, reforming my heart, keeping my mind peaceful and calm, to lead and pacify the enemy with words—once I had resolved in my heart, how could this be, "Untokoshiyo!" Can I let go without avenging my grievance? Sankō of Yakata village, whose name was sold in the land of Kumaso, a matter affecting even his face. Seeing the enemies of his parents before him, how could the teaching of non-resistance be put into practice?

Thinking and thinking, anger rises. Year in and year out, I was determined to destroy that Orochi wretch, constantly uttering "Orochi, Orochi" and chanting "Untokodotsukoi," while world's wretches said, "Dotsukoishiyo!" They called me the Orochi's Sankō. Everyone, watch your step! Centipedes and salamanders are crawling about around here. Magokō-sama, the Sendenshi, no matter how much divine power he has, somehow his shadow looks faint. If it's going to be like this, it would have been good if I had forcibly asked Kurohime to come, "Dotsukoishiyo!" Realizing it later, "Dotsukoishiyo!" A foolish idea like an epileptic's, but it's a post-festival and nothing can be done.

From now on, we have no other way than to pray single-heartedly to the Kami of Heaven and Earth, and protected by the divine guardianship, "Untokodotsukoi," to destroy them rather than anything else.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, before the presence of Yoshikohime, with a heart filled, we pray to You. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, whether the Orochi of the lake rages, whether earthquakes, thunder, and rain of fire attack all at once—with the single breath of sincerity's Kototama, leading and pacifying the Orochi with words, we must return in triumph, "Dotsukoishiyo!" Otherwise, Sankō's manhood will not stand before the many disciples, "Dotsukoishiyo!" The retribution for the sins committed up to now instantly manifests, and he is abandoned by Kurohime-sama...
