§ 1

Roaring tigers, wolves, and Shishi and Orochi, In the high plains of the land of Kumaso, Building a mansion far and near, Men who wielded violence, The Sankō of Orochi by name, All night long in the detached room, Exchanging cups of sake, shadows of men Indulging in talk about matters far and wide, Projected on the shōji screen at five or six, The night deepens far and wide, The voice of the night wind crossing the arano, Subsiding before one knows it, Faintly audible, the valley stream's Biting the rocks and gushing forth, Only the sound of water echoes far and wide.

Excited in their nerves somehow by the mood of the sake and unable to fall asleep, the three men, Arata, Hisu, and Toku, wandering about the precincts at leisure, gazed at the shadows cast upon the shōji screen and began to shout with rolled tongues.

Arata: 'Hey Shinkō, look at the shadow on that shōji! Isn't that the nice shadow of the girl who always spills drinks for you when you become the boss's absentee, projected with a shantokosei and untokosei? She's truly an amazing fellow. I used to think Lady Oai wasn't just an ordinary fox either, but she said she's a daughter of Yashimawake or something. Long ago at the council of Tokoyo, a fox named Yashika came out and made Tokoyohime say "Ahun", so she might be a descendant of that fellow.'

Toku: 'Hey Toku, if you spout off in such a loud voice, won't the Sankō hear you? Since you blurted out that you wouldn't let it leak, this Shinkō kindly opened the key of mystery and let you hear it, didn't he? Since the night is this far advanced and things around here are as quiet as can be, even a small whisper can be heard, so why don't you speak in a low voice? It will be terrible if it reaches Lady Oai.'

Arata: 'Your voice is much louder, isn't it? Hey Shinkō, that fellow called Oume is said to be the boss's younger sister, but did she elope bringing even her younger sister along? Truly a thorough fellow.'

Toku: 'Speaking of a younger sister, well, she's a younger sister. As for whether she's a real one, she's a foundling too. Our Tora-kō says outwardly she's a younger sister, but in reality, she's a woman born in the land of Fusa, and her older sister is a fine nice named Omatsu.'

Arata: 'How do you know that Omatsu?'

Hey Toku-kō, shall I arrange Lady Oume as a wife for you to commemorate your reform?'

Toku: 'How could one become husband and wife with such a young piece of goods? It's too shameful before the world.'

Arata: 'You, if you have a conscience that cares about public opinion, why did you become the henchman of such a scoundrel Sankō? That is much worse for public opinion. My boss is none other than the general commander of Toyohinowakemiko-sama of the land of Toyo, Tora-wakahiko-mikoto-sama. In his youth he fell into imprudent love and came down to the land of Kumaso, but no matter what anyone says, since seed is seed, he is great. A fellow called the Sankō of Orochi is a base lowlife whose origin is unknown—whether he's ox-bone or horse-bone—so he knows neither human feeling nor understands true reason, and like a cowardly and spineless fellow, he forced his way into the absent home of the boss and tried to force Lady Oai. Is there any decent fellow among those who carry lanterns for such a guy, hey Hisu-kō?'

Hisu: 'Hey hey, if you raise your voice like that, won't the boss hear you? If he hears, things will be troublesome again.'

Arata: '(Jōruri) That will not be heard, Hisu-sama... dai, to say words are unreasonable... chin-chin... but though I think not ooooo... I am too anxious, not even having time to rejoice in having such a splendid boss, if I am driven out, where and how shall this Arata live on, pressing my thundering chest in anxious suspense... ke... wailing and lamenting with that facial expression...'

Saying thus, the sannin vanished into the darkness and went off to sober up amidst the rice fields.

Left behind were the usual Torakō, Kurohime, Magokō, Kanakō, Ai, Ume, and the seven sannin on the master's side, placing a desk between them and continuing to whisper. Captivated since early evening by the discourse on the sacred administration of the Lord God, without even realizing the night was growing late, and without feeling drowsiness due to the excessive delight, they interspersed various personal histories in lowered voices and recounted the pathways to their initiation. Kurohime spoke,

'Listening to the talk of the three men outside the window just now, are the personal stories of Ai-sama, Torakō-sama, and Ume-sama truly as they were told?'

'Since younger fellows are speaking while drunk, they are not quite to be relied upon.'

'It is said that drunken speech does not alter one's true nature, so it is probably not something entirely without trace or shadow. When people are drunk, they are relatively honest, you see, Torakō-sama.'

'There are parts that hit the mark and parts that miss, so anyway they are talking about things they misheard, which is truly troublesome.'

'It was said that Ume-sama is a younger sister of Omatsu-sama, wasn't it? Where might that Omatsu-sama be living now? If it is no inconvenience, please tell us.'

'Yes, I had an older sister. My middle sister, Otake-sama, went to the Caucasus Mountains and has been missing ever since, and my eldest sister, Omatsu-sama, crossed the sea from the land of Fusa and went to some distant country, or so the story goes. In any case, we parted when I was small, so I know no detailed particulars.'

'What do they say about your parents?'

'If I am to trust rumors among people, my parents were led away by a general called Onimunahiko of Brahmanism, and I heard that they had their lives taken. As for myself, I was abducted by some wicked person, and just as I arrived at the summit of Mount Tsukushi, my older brother appeared, scattered the wicked persons, rescued me, brought me back, and has taken care of me until now. Through my older brother's arrangement, since people in the world would look down on me if I said I was a child without parents or siblings, he told me it would be best to say I was a younger sister who had come to visit from his home province, and he said he would think of me as his true younger sister and cherish me.'

Shedding tears, they wept aloud. Torakō, Oai, and Kurohime also clasped their hands and lowered their heads, breathing heavily.

Sankō: 'I have just learned of the matter concerning Umesama for the first time. Oh, Torakō-sama, you are truly a kind person. Ah, I am now completely filled with admiration.'

Saying this, he too contains his tears.

Magokō: 'Ah, now Magokō as well understands the whole secret of the three of you. In that regard, leader of the three, whose child might you be? If you would be so good as to tell us while we are at it, how would that be? Well, since things have come to this, we are just like close brothers, so there should be no need for any distinctions between us.'

Torakō: 'My father lived in a town in Egypt, and was called Harukō, while my mother was called Otsune. Once, taking advantage of having become a missionary of Ananai-kyō, and saying he wanted to perform a feat of arms in his first battle and present it to the Kamisama, he left me behind at home. Saying that he and my wife—ah, my wife? No, saying that husband and wife were going to make the great serpent inhabiting the Lake of Suppon at the headwaters of Shirasegaha submit to reason, the two of them went. As it turned out, it would seem that my parents' divine power was still insufficient, and they were swallowed without difficulty by the great serpent of the lake. I was left keeping house all alone with a manservant, but hearing of this affair by the wind's report, and unable to hold back, I hastened to the residence of Natsuyamahiko-sama, the chieftain of Egypt and a missionary of Ananai-kyō, whom my father had made his close confidant. Having him consult the divine oracle, the reply was: "Your parents have hitherto accumulated far too much wealth, and merely kept saying they would help needy people without ever giving even a single handful of rice. The heavy attachments of a great multitude gathered together and became a great serpent, which has destroyed your parents, so it is no longer any use. At the very least, pray for the peace of your parents' souls, and pray that they may be reborn into this world anew as fine human beings"—thus it was spoken. However, even considering that my parents would be reborn somewhere, since I had already been robbed of my parents, I could not remain at ease. Determined to hack and slaughter the great serpent of the Lake of Suppon one and all and avenge my parents, I refused to listen even to Natsuyamahiko and his wife who kindly tried to stop me. Taking advantage of the night, I bolted from my home, and upon coming to the shores of the lake, saw it was a boundless, wide lake. Figuring that this fellow could by no means be managed by the strength of one or two people, I gave up on that. From then on, far away in the province of Kumaso, at Yakatamura in the shade of the oak forest, I built a hermitage, became a chivalrous knight, and supported numerous underlings. When things had reached the point where I could say I was all right, resolved to exterminate the great serpent in a single swoop, though not evil from the bottom of my heart, I pretended to be evil and gathered together a gang of wicked men, making preparations to subdue the great serpent. No matter how many believers adhering to a doctrine of non-resistance like Ananai-kyō there might be, they would by no means perform such a murderous deed as conquering a great serpent. Therefore, as they say that like attracts like, bad people gather where bad people are. To gather a great many of those bad fellows and wipe out the bad great serpent is the method known as using poison to control poison, so up until today I have maintained that resolve. Furthermore, the reason I acted discourteously toward Oaisama was actually that, cloaking it in the guise of romantic affection, I drew Oaisama close to my hand. Since she possesses the spiritual lineage of Takehinakawake no Mikoto, I thought she might prove useful for an emergency, and thus devised various miscellaneous and clumsy schemes. It was truly an infantile thought, and now it makes me feel ashamed.'

Telling this tale while shedding tears like rain, he droops his head crestfallen.

Magokō: 'Ah, so now Torakō's situation is thoroughly understood as well. The unmasking of the four of us has successfully concluded, which is a most happy occasion. Hearing such a story, one cannot simply sit still. Now from here on, Sankō-sama, let us go together toward the Lake of Suppon to open a Kototama battle. However, since the doctrine of Ananai is to bring joy and exact vengeance, we are not going to take the great serpent's life; rather, through the living Kototama of the Amatsunorito, we shall release the great serpent's spirit, rescue it up into heaven, and ensure that henceforth it never brings disaster upon the people. It is regrettable about your parents who were once killed, but since this too is the retribution of sins they created themselves which caused them to be swallowed by the great serpent, we as human beings can do nothing about it. Moreover, even you, Sankō-sama, cannot harbor resentment against the great serpent as your parents' enemy. In short, the demons created by Harukō and Otsune themselves are what attacked them; so to speak, it is reaping what one sows, and there is nothing else to do but leave everything to the divine judgment of the Kamisama. Now everyone, shall we go to the Kototama battle?'

『私の父はエヂプトの町に住んで居りまして、春公と申し、母はお常といひました。或時、三五教の宣伝使となつたのを幸ひ、初陣の功名をして神様に御目にかけたいとか云つて、私を家に残し、白瀬川の水上、スツポンの湖に棲む大蛇を言向和すとか云つて、夫婦が参りました。さうした所が、私の両親はまだ神力が足らなかつたと見えまして、湖の大蛇に苦もなく呑まれて了つたので御座います。私はただ一人下男と留守をして居りましたが、此事を風の便りに聞き、矢も楯もたまらず、三五教の宣伝使で埃及の酋長なる夏山彦様の御館へ、父が入魂にして頂いて居つたのを幸ひ馳せ参じ、神勅を伺つて貰つた所「お前の両親は今迄余り沢山に財産を拵へ、難儀な者を助ける助けると云つた計りで、米一掴み与へた事もなし、大勢の者の執着心が重なつて大蛇となり、お前の両親を亡ぼして了つたのだから、モウ駄目だ。せめては両親の冥福を祈り、再び此世へ立派な人間として生れて来るやうに祈つてやれ」……と仰有いました。併し乍ら両親はどこかへ生れ変るにした所で、私としては最早親を取られたのだから、安閑としては居られない、スツポンの湖の大蛇を片つ端から切り屠り、親の仇を討つてやらむと、夏山彦御夫婦が親切におとめ下さるのも聞かず、夜に紛れて吾家を飛出し、湖の畔に来て見れば、際限もなき広い湖、此奴ア到底一人や二人の力では可かないと断念し、それから遥々と熊襲の国の屋方村、樫の森の木かげに庵を結び、侠客となつて数多の乾児を養ひ、サア是で大丈夫と云ふやうになつた所で、一挙にして大蛇を殲滅せむと、心の底より悪ではないが、悪を装うて悪人原をかりあつめ、大蛇退治の用意をして居つたのです。三五教の様な無抵抗主義の教を奉ずる信者が幾らあつても、到底大蛇征伐の様な殺生な事は致しますまいから、類を以て集まるとか云つて、悪い者の所へは悪い者が集まります。其悪い奴を沢山集めて悪い大蛇を平げるのは所謂毒を以て毒を制すると云ふ筆法ですから、今日迄其覚悟を持つてゐたので御座います。さうしてお愛様に対し失礼な事を致しましたのも、実の所は恋慕に事寄せ、お愛様を私の手許に引寄せ、建日向別命様の霊系であるから、まさかの時の用意にと思つて、いろいろ雑多と拙劣な計劃をめぐらしてゐたので御座います。実に幼稚な考へで、只今となつては恥しう御座います』

と物語りつつ、涙を雨の如くに流し乍ら、悄然として俯むく。

『ヤアそれで虎公もスツカリと様子が分つた。いよいよ四人の種明かしも無事に終了してお目出度い、其話を聞く以上は、ジツとしちやゐられない。サア是から三公さま、吾々と共にスツポンの湖に向つて言霊戦を開きに参りませう。併し乍ら、三五の教は喜ばれて仇を討つといふ教だから、大蛇の命を取りに行くのではない、大蛇の霊を、天津祝詞の生言霊に依つて解脱させ、天国に救ひ上げ、今後は決して国民に災をなさないやうにするのだ。一旦殺された御両親は気の毒だが、是も自分から作つた罪が酬うて大蛇に呑まれたのだから、吾々人間として如何ともする事は出来ない。又三公さまだとて、大蛇を親の仇だと恨む訳には参りますまい。要するに春公、お常御両人の自ら造つた悪魔が、自らを攻めたのだから、言はば自業自得、何事も神様の御裁断に任すより仕方がない。サア皆さま、言霊戦に参らうぢやありませぬか』

§ 4

Torakō: 'That is indeed ketsukō. We shall arrange for this yankō of a Sankō to bring his kobun without delay and come along.'

Aihime: 'No, no, there is no hitsueu to take any kobun along. Since the Kami of Yahoyorozu protect us, we do not need many ninzu; three sannin will be daijiyōbu.'

Torakō: 'If that is sayō, then Torakō-sama, Aihime-sama, and Sankō-sama, you shall have the gokurau. Kurohime will now take Magokō and proceed to the kunimiyako of Fire.'

Sankō: 'Ah, that is gokurau for you. If that is sayō, pray go forth in good kigen. Shall we have one or two kobun act as your goannai?'

Kurohime: 'Hai, arigata gozaimasu. Whoever it may be, if you would lend us hitori who knows the katsute of the roads, I should be arigata.'

Sankō: 'If so, we shall have Toku attend you as an escort, so we kindly ask you to accept him. Guwanrai, he is a man of little wit, so he might conversely prove to be an ashitemato, but…'

Kurohime: 'Hai, thank you for your goshinsetsu. If so, Toku-sama shall have the gokurau.'

Magokō: 'Kurohime-sama, since I cannot let you go hitori—you who saved the life of my wife—I shall have Hisakō act as your goannai hitori. If so, you will have three travelling companions, which will be daijiyōbu.'

Kurohime: 'Hai, arigata gozaimasu. Wherever we go, travelling with the Kami, hitori is ketsukō enough, but once we arrive yonder, negotiations might be inconvenient hitori; therefore, let us accept the services of you futari in return, we shall have Magokō accompany your party… Look here, Magokō-sama, serve your two leaders well, pacify the great serpent with words, and then come to visit us at the kunimiyako of Fire.'

Magokō: 'Hai, this is more than I could have prayed for, arigata gozaimasu. If so, I shall now depart. I sincerely pray that you will remain entirely safe and well.'

Here, upon the orders of Torakō, Ume, together with Shin and Hachi, prepared to return to the empty house in Takenomura. The four—Torakō, Aihime, Sankō, and Magokō—departed from the village of Yakata without a moment's delay, and in high spirits prepared their travelling attire to proceed and pacify the great serpent of the lake of Suppon with words.

Kanekō, Yosakō, and Takakō, along with numerous kobun, remained behind to perform the duties of guarding the empty post.
