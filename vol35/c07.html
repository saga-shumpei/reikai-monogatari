Chapter 7: Turmoil Dance [971]

乱舞〔971〕

§ 1

Hachikō, drawn in by Tokukō's song, began to chant rather uncertainly,

The crystal ball that Takeno's villager Tamakō had inherited from his parents' generation suddenly clouded over black. Wondering what could be the matter, I listened in secret and learned that Kurohime had come to Tsukushi Island to spew mud. Convinced without a doubt that this was so, based on an utterly off-target judgment, we begged Oyakata-sama for help and set off up Takayamatauge, pretending to pick figs. Catching the Kurohime-tsukasa and forcing her to undergo kaishin—with this mission entrusted to him by oyabun-sama, the three of us, namely Shin, Kyū, and Hachi, climbed the steep mountain, sat in a circle at the very peak, and began the shirokuroishiura. Just as we did so, Kurohime arrived right here. We, boiling with rage, upon seeing the Kurohime-tsukasa, suddenly underwent a complete shinkiitsuten. Filled with a profound awe for her divine virtue that seemed to arise from nowhere, we resolved to go before Takekuniwake, the kamuzukasa of the Takehi shrine, so that she might meet with her parent, and we pressed on across mountains and valleys. However, Kurohime proved to be quite different from our expectations—she was neither parent nor child. Viewed from a physical standpoint, she turned out to be a complete stranger. With no other choice, we left this place and descended the ninety-nine winding mountain paths, following right behind oyabun-sama. When we came down to the mountain pass of the Hinokuni Highway, Hrokō was waiting there with a large band of followers, dressed in fierce fighting attire and with his head bound in a hachimaki. Thinking that even the formidable Torukō must drive off all those wretches and hurry off at a dead run to rescue Lady Oai from her peril, I rushed ahead.

Having crossed the log bridge over Fukayadani, I encountered Oai-sama and Oume-sama whom I had been worrying about, and hardly had time to feel a sense of relief when, stirred with zeal to save the three villagers of Ykata onto the Great Way of Misoji, I came descending the mountain path of Mukaitouge, keeping time with the rhythm, 'Untokodokkoi, dokkoi,' and before I knew it, 'Dokkoishiyo!'

The crystal ball has vanished? No, no, it is not like that at all. The owner of the crystal ball, that scoundrel Tamakō, has vanished like a cloud. As for the fellow who knows nothing, just let him go! Making the earth rumble with a thumping sound, stepping across fearful paths where giant snakes and centipedes lie outstretched, when I came to Ykata village and looked, the unexpected three villagers—the ogres had instantly turned into gods, and the great serpents had fled, becoming divine shrines and noble persons. Ah, Kamunagara! The blessings of the gods are right before our eyes. If those three villagers who were once great serpents had remained with yesterday's mindset, by now they surely would have been locked in a fierce, struggling wrestling match with Oyakata. Swords flashing, thunder roaring, and a rain of bloody mist must have certainly fallen! If I had dawdled even a little, even I would have had my hat-rim torn off and made a wretched death-journey to the dark Hades, left scolding by the old hag at the crossing of the Sanzu River! Yet, calling it an old hag all the same, in the presence of the kamuzukasa of Misoji, the Kurohime-tsukasa, drinking exquisite, exquisite sake, all gathered together in affectionate harmony—a great drunken banquet of reconciliation through a brawl. Could there ever be such a joyful thing? Today's outcome is so contrary to expectations that I leap for joy in my delight, my hands beginning to dance and my feet to caper. Somehow, quite of itself, my whole body felt as though it were floating up into the air. It is not only gourds that float when they rise!

Hachikō's body, too, has now floated up. Come, float, float away, drinking sake! Cross over this floating world, everyone. To live sunk in the sorrows of the floating world—that indeed is the work of demons. Since it is called a world of dreams and floating bubbles, people are meant to do nothing if not float! Even the rafts on the Hinokuni River float from morning till night. The grebes in the pond of Yosami, and even the mandarin ducks swimming in pairs, live together in harmony and float. Float, receive the blessing of floating, all you wretches! Like a ship on the vast ocean, as the saying goes, 'Untokodokkoi,' is not the world of men three parts a floating world? From morning till night, stirring up strife and enduring all manner of floating grief—I cannot fathom the minds of fools who do such things. If one only drinks sake, the heart constantly floats, and even the pressing demands of creditors bloom like plum blossoms, sounding just like a warbling bush warbler's voice at just the right pitch.

Man's mind is the primary thing. With just the way one holds one's mind, living afloat is one lifetime, and living sunk in despair is also one lifetime. 'Untokodokkoi,' floating and sinking—to say that there are seven falls in human life, I do not know what wretch uttered such words, but whoever spoke them is a fool! Seven times, eight times, nine times, a hundred times, a thousand times, ten thousand times—to live afloat is this floating world! We are not mere stones or tiles; possessing the divine spirit bestowed upon us, how could we meekly endure living sunk in gloom as human beings? Ah, Kamunagara!

§ 2

Warm sake is better than cold sake

'Kankankaraketsu kaan kaan'

'Kankaraberabō dokukoishiyō'

Tying a Buddhist priest's headband tightly,

Even if one tries to act big, there is no backbone,

Nevertheless, when one drinks sake,

No matter what, once one becomes a hopeless drunk,

The end result is a boiled octopus,

Face, hands, and feet turn bright red,

Bones soften, turning all mushy and limp,

Like a block of konniyaku, completely helpless.

Both body and mind soften,

For the first time, all under heaven is at peace.

Even in my own household, a matriarchy reigns,

If only you are allowed to drink sake,

For a while, you can indulge in dreams

Of peaceful tranquility.

If it is free sake, I do not mind,

Everyone, please drink to your heart's content,

Without thinking of us as immature fools.

Not cold sake, but heated Nahohi,

The variety of feasts is also Ōnahohi,

Please look anew and listen anew,

And forgive our rudeness.

Just as the worldly proverb says,

Touching upon all the likes of the host,

As one mentions them to incoming guests,

While making up clever excuses,

How delicious the taste of the sacred sake received,

Flying over long mountain slopes,

To the place where the heart is relieved,

Beating the drum of the tongue in delight,

The belly instantly becomes Hotei-sama,

The merry play of the Seven Lucky Gods,

Bensaiten's beloved-sama,

With a face like Daikoku,

Izunome's Kurohime-sama,

Yosahō-don's Jūrōjin visage,

Head like Bishamon and Fukurokuju.

The seven Otafuku gather together,

And drink sake with delightful amusement.

Ah, ever true to the gods, ever true to the gods,

May our spirits be blessed and prosper!

Ah, ever true to the gods, ever true to the gods,

May our spirits be blessed and prosper!'

So he sings and dances wildly, improvising from his mouth.

Takahiko begins to sing once more.

'Listen well, Hachikō, listen well, Hachikō,

As you well know, I am

A good-for-nothing son born of Mokuhei

In the village of Takeno,

Who stole my grandfather's treasure,

Drank sake from morning till night,

Turned a deaf ear to everyone's advice,

Was driven out by father and mother,

Like a forsaken small boat on the shores of wandering,

With nowhere to cling to, aimlessly wandering

Through the Pass of the Land of Fire,

Passing through with tears in my eyes,

When I was picked up at last

By the great bandit boss Sankō,

Whose fierce name shook men,

Catching my breath, morning and evening I took up sandals,

And diligently cleaned the privy,

When the boss said,

You are a surprisingly fine fellow,

I will make you a sworn brother.

Grateful for this extraordinary promotion,

Becoming a man who wields great influence,

Cutting through the wind with my shoulders, near and far,

The joy of walking about challenging others.

Even though I say I have reformed now,

He who touches vermilion becomes red,

Becoming the subordinate of a boss

Who was bad from the beginning, how could I

Ever attain a true heart?

Doing bad things morning and evening,

Acting high and mighty.

At such a time, this recent

Great turmoil at the Mukō Pass,

Beloved-sama, whom I thought dead,

Even Kanehiko came along,

Frightening us with eerie ghostly sounds,

And stealing my bold spirit.

If I had not been drinking sake,

I would have thought, what impudent ghost fellows!

And clenching my fists,

Starting with Kanehiko's rascal,

Beating down every single ghost,

It was a place where I should have punished them.

Just because I was drunk on sake,

In the sorrow of not being able to stand on my feet,

Swallowing my resentment as I watched,

They were genuine, true human beings,

Calling them ghosts was just a false skin.

When I think of this, because Takahiko

Was drunk on sake,

The great turmoil did not even begin,

And it safely proclaimed a resolution.

When I think of this, drinking sake

And losing one's footing is also in accordance with the gods.

Whatever kind of contrivance this may be,

Since it is not something easily understood,

Everyone drink plenty of sake,

And losing your footing may be the best thing.

Drink sake when it is time to drink sake,

Work when it is time to work,

It is good to share both bitterness and joy.

To say that there is joy within bitterness and

There is bitterness within joy is precisely this matter.

Ah, ever true to the gods, ever true to the gods,

Warmed sake tastes even better.

Excuse me, excuse me, Kurohime-sama,

Please do partake in a cup.

I will pour for you.

If you sit there glaring at me

With such a stern face, you are Enma-sama,

Who has come from the realm of the dead

And will exact a heavy fine!

Ah, ever true to the gods, ever true to the gods,

May our spirits be blessed and prosper!'

Overwhelmed with joy, he chants a praise song to sake full of digressions, and it ends up in an Ural-cult style. However, since it is a joyous banquet where their hearts have melted toward one another, Kurohime does not particularly blame him; Torakiko, Sankō, and Lady Aoi, declaring that today is an extraterritorial zone, harden their resolve and leave the wild, free dancing of their henchmen to take its course.
