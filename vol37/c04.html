Chapter 4: 素破抜 [1016]

素破抜〔1016〕

§ 1

'In wide Kameoka's Jifusanmachi, No geisha looks back as she goes,'

As the popular song goes, in the town of Kameoka, right down to the geishas and nakais, everyone was a rustic arrival from Kiyōto, a cheap-goods purchaser from the bargain-bins; playing the samisen meant little more than tying up a sash-cord and swirling it around the tatami room—scarcely better than a clumsy amateur. Pushing between their thighs for money, the kuginukionna wandered around the restaurants here and there, numbering around a score.

At the Shiyōguwatsuya in Gofukumachi, which served as Kankō's lodging, there were two or three retained geishas who were unusually peeled of their astringent skins for the countryside, slightly chic, and capable of playing the samisen.

The purpose of all of them was to sell spring.

Among them, a woman named Oi, about twenty years of age, repeatedly frequented Kahachiya, and had made a promise that if she successfully fulfilled her term in good cheer, they would even become man and wife.

Kankō, having gathered all five of his henchmen, grandly rode into Shiyōguwatsuya, and in the old, decayed six-tatami back room, with pillars shining pitch-black from grime, he indulged in boasting tales.

Oi came pouring tea there,

Oi: 'Brother-san, nay, oyakata-san, what ever became of that Jiromatsu affair? That fellow called Kimama or Kiraku was said to be rather thick-skulled last time too, but has it been settled neatly somehow?'

Kankō: 'Well, frightened by my might, even Kiraku has finally given in to tears, and today he is coming to apologize. Around seven o'clock this evening, Kiraku, Jiromatsu, and Nagakichi—the three of them—are supposed to come here to make amends.'

Oi: 'That is a most pleasant thing indeed. I'd love to see Kiraku apologize to the oyakata for once.'

Kankō: 'Ha ha ha ha, chivalrous gamblers have etiquette appropriate to them. It is no place for women and the like to come and watch. Go prepare a seat on the second floor and make sure no one comes near. Once that ceremony is done, I'll give a hand-clap, so come up then and pour the drinks.'

Oi: 'Shall I pour sake for Kimama-san or Kiraku-san first?'

Whether such things were spoken or not, Kiraku was at that very time only just setting out from Anō, so no matter how great his heavenly ear-penetration might be, he could not possibly have heard it.

Around seven in the afternoon, accompanied by Jiromatsu-san, whose lower back was somewhat out of sorts and who walked feebly, and Nagakichi the small man, he slipped through the gateway of Shiyōguwatsuya.

That same Oi wore a sneer on her face, and when our side said 'Excuse us,' she unpleasantly replied 'Hmph' and turned her back.

Kiraku felt like saying... This slut of a wretch, taking a vital customer and making a fool of him, does she expect her business to prosper with an attitude like that...?

Yet, somehow or other, considering that the other party was dealing with ruffians, a faint cloud of unease traversed his heart, so he purposely put on a smile,

Kiraku: 'Is Kahachiya-san in?'

he asked, and Oi,

Oi: 'Hmph, the oyabun? He has been here since around noon playing with his henchmen. Are you Kiraku-san? In the end, you were beaten by Kahachiya-han, weren't you?'

laughed coldly. Her attitude made his stomach turn once more.

Kiraku: 'Hey Nagakichi, Jiromatsu-san, isn't it pointless just standing in a place like this? Shall we have the maid guide us and pass into the inner rooms?'

he shouted in a somewhat strained voice.

Coming down the stairs of the eight-tatami second-floor room overlooking the garden with a clatter was Yosakō.

Yosaku: 'Oh Kiraku-san, please come up quickly. The oyakata is waiting. He says the appointed time is late, and he is terribly angry. Come, come, quickly.'

He took the lead and climbed the stairs. The three followed closely behind and went up to the second floor.

Properly set out on legless dining trays were five or six confection bowls and plates, and even the slop basin reflected the lamp's shadow; at the sound of the three climbing up, the sea within the slop basin made the lamp's moon tremble.

長吉『ナアニそんな事あるものか。河内屋奴が五人の乾児を伴れて、あんな痳病やみの次郎松サンとこへ押よせて来たもんだから、今まで何回も喜楽サンが掛合つて居つたのだけれど、今度はたまりかねて応援に往つたのだ。河内屋も抜いた刀が鞘に納まりかねて困つて居つた所、わしの兄の勝ちやんが仲裁に這入つて、ソレから又喜楽が談判をして、次郎松から十五円、河内屋から十五円、勝負なしに、仲直りといふ相談が出来たのだ。一方は侠客の親分、一方は安閑坊の喜楽、そんな者と喧嘩をして、五分々々の別れと云ふのだから、つまり河内屋が負なのだ。そこを喜楽が折角売り出した河内屋の顔を潰しては可哀相だと思うて、ズツと譲歩して五分々々と云ふ所で体能うキリをつけたのだよ。今夜の御馳走は三十円の御馳走だのに、なぜ又これ程高いのだ。吉川の桑酒屋へ行つて五円出しや、これ位の御馳走はしてくれるが、お前とこもチト勉強せぬと、商売が流行らぬやうになるかも知れぬぞ』

Oai: 'Is that really true?'

Oai changed her expression and went up the stairs with a thudding sound,

Oai: 'Excuse me, master of Kahachiya, just a moment...'

and gave a significant look. Saying 'Un,' Kahachiya followed behind Oai, descended the stairs, and remained hidden from view for about ten minutes.

With drunken, hazy eyes, Chiyōkichi crawled up the stairs on all fours to the second floor. There Kankō came up with a changed expression,

Kankō: 'Hey Chiyōkichi, this latest incident is as good as caused by you. While the likes of us, Kiraku-san, and Matsu-san are still sitting right here, how on earth could you leave your seat of your own accord? You have broken the ritual of reconciliation and smeared mud on the faces of chivalrous men. Hey Yosa, Toku, drag Chiyōkichi off, take him down to the wellside, and douse him with about a hundred buckets of water right over his head!'

Cursing him foully, the rough men began to use all their strength to stamp on and kick the head and lower back of Chiyōkichi, who was dead drunk.

Kiraku: 'Hey Kahachiya, now that the cups of reconciliation have been exchanged, isn't it fine wherever Chiyōkichi goes? If Chiyōkichi did something wrong, it would be better to deal with it later. Since I have taken personal custody of him until tomorrow morning from his parents and brothers, I cannot let anyone lay so much as a single finger on him.'

Kankō: 'He is an unforgivable rascal, but out of consideration for Kiraku-san and Jirōmatsu-san, I will let it go for tonight. Once day breaks tomorrow, make sure you come straight to my place.'

Chiyōkichi: 'I am sorry, please forgive me. It is not as though I was speaking ill of you. The maid downstairs said that tonight's feast was a five-yen affair made to look like a ten-yen feast, so I said there could be no such thing and that it was thirty-yen, so please bear with it without taking it amiss.'

Kankō: 'Shut up, you noisy fool. If you start spouting nonsense after a reconciliation is finished, it will give cause for yet another grievance. Come on, hurry up and go home... Kiraku-san, Matsu-san, please calm your spirits and drink until daybreak. From now on we are bringing up the women, set up so that the first session is ten yen and the second party is twenty yen, yet without even listening to the reasons Chiyōkichi had to go and say such a thing—truly a hopeless wretch... Hey Oai, you had better stop flapping your jaws too, we are just about to place the order for the second party. Because you say such useless things, Kiraku-san has been given cause to suspect innocent motives, and you have crushed a man's honor.'

As he spoke, he slapped the cheek of his dearest Oai with a sharp, stinging sound. Oai let out a shriek of 'Kya,' tumbled down the stairs, and lay sprawled flat on her back in the garden with her white buttocks exposed. The two maids hurriedly picked her up and seem to have taken her away to the separate building in the rear.

Kiraku: 'My friend, since there is a place I must go to early tomorrow morning, I would love to join the second party, but please excuse me. Won't you two drink double portions in my place and make a thorough uproar? I will be taking both Matsu-san and Chiyōkichi back with me...'

Kankō: 'If you have official business, it cannot be helped. In that case, I will spend another twenty yen in your place. Hey Yosa, Toku, Ken, go downstairs and place the order.'

Not knowing Kankō's inner intentions, the three jumped down in a flurry, caught the mistress of the house, and are currently placing the order for the second party. Kiraku and the other two depart from this place and head back along the night road toward Anō. Feeling a nagging sense of uncertainty as to when Kankō's underlings might make a detour and what they might do, they try to hurry home, but because the leprous, wobbly-legged man is drunk on alcohol and Chiyōkichi is also dead drunk, they keep walking in circles like crabs, so that they make absolutely no headway on the road; having left the Shōgatsuya around ten o'clock, they spent about two hours covering a mere twelve or thirteen chō up to the pine trees.
