Chapter 8: 梟の Twilight 企 [1020]

梟の宵企〔1020〕

§ 1

Returning home after a long absence, I felt relaxed and had been sleeping since the early evening hours. Suddenly, someone shook my body awake, crying out, 'Oi! Oi!' Startled, I opened my eyes and looked around, but no one was there. Only my raubo, mother, and younger sister were still awake despite the early hour, not yet sleeping, sitting near the ando—some looking at shōsetsubon, others spreading out and looking at picture scrolls. Suddenly, my body rose automatically like a kikai and began to walk naturally. A physical body possessed by a spirit cannot be made to do anything by one's own ishi.

When I left my body to move as it would, before I knew it, I was guided to a small hill called Tonoyama, near the Ubusuna shrine. At the very moment when a round mass rolled with a sound from around my navel and came dancing up to the vicinity of my throat,

Ōshimotengu...

it bellowed out. I,

Kiraku: 'Moshi Ōshimo-sama, it is fine for you to possess me, but to be made to suffer so is more than I can bear. Please possess me more comfortably.'

Ōshimotengu: 'To possess you comfortably is what the Kami desires as well. Even the Kami suffers. Because your doubts have not yet been removed, you must suffer for it. You must quickly reform and make yourself useful for the Kamisama's goyō. Do you remember the matter of the training at Takakumayama?'

Kiraku: 'It has been so painful that for now I have forgotten it entirely. Somehow my head has become all hazy, and it has come to feel as though I do not understand. I suppose I will remember it bit by bit.'

Ōshimotengu: 'Since I will now let you know the whereabouts of the gold spoken of to Motoichi, prepare pickaxes and spades, take a fuku of baskets, and go to Okuyama. When the time comes, I will let you know again...'

Kiraku: 'Going alone to a place like Okuyama is troublesome. Shall I bring even Uichi along?'

Kiraku: 'I have already entered the path of the Kamisama, so I have no desire for gold. Even when I hear about gold, I shudder.'

Kiraku: 'That is also true. However, to say that I will carry back many heavy things—on a dark mountain path, won't that be troublesome?'

Ōshimotengu: 'I will reveal my true form as a tengu, and if it is heavy, I will carry it back on my shoulders.'

Thus spoke my mouth, and thus it answered; it was a matter of asking and answering oneself for a short while.

Hearing this, I came to feel as though gold were indeed necessary, thinking I would dig it out before Uichi came, and carrying a small dirt-carrying basket, holding a muku staff, a pickaxe, a spade, and feeling as though I were carrying a basket full of gold coins on my shoulder as I returned, I slipped quietly out of the humble hut within the shrine wall, and proceeded step by step from Maedani along the foot of Mount Atago, Uba-no-fukuro, Toraike, Shinike, Shibagahara, and Sunadome, gazing to the right at the training ground of Takakumayama, climbing steadily up Inokuma Pass, ascending the slope called Uchi-koshi which was fraught with extreme danger, crossing Soroban-iwa, and passing once more through the perils of Umanose, advancing into the valley in Okuyama known as Tamagohara.

Quietly I lowered the empty basket, sat down on the mountain to rest my breath, and began to chant the Amatsu Norito. For some reason, the whole area around there had grown pitch black. I had a feeling as though something like grey clouds was pressing in toward this place bit by bit from the lower part of the valley, and before I knew it, both my hands and feet were trembling. I was seized by a lonely and pitiful feeling that defies description, and even if there were one hundred million yen in gold, I would have no desire to dig it out, nor would I want it. Stronger than that, a weak feeling came over me, urging that I wanted to return quickly to my own jitaku. Fortunately, from the eastern sky, a hazy spring moon rose with a thin face. Driven by the state of my mind, there came to be heard all around that place a sad, unpleasant voice—lonely beyond description, and impossible to tell whether it belonged to a person, an insect, or a beast. Once more, three masses began to move with a rumbling sound from below my navel, dancing up to my throat. Just as I thought to myself that this was spirit possession once again,

Ōshimo: 'I am Ōshimo. Look, koban are buried right here. Dig this spot, take the tsurubashi!'

He shouted thus. As Kiraku gripped the handle of the tsurubashi at the command, both of his hands clung to the handle as if biting into it and would not let go, and mechanically the tsurubashi began to move with a clinking sound. Even though he thought to himself that his body was so wretchedly tired and he wanted to take a short rest, the handle of the tsurubashi adhered to his hands and would not leave them, and of its own accord the tsurubashi began to peck away at the hard earth with a clicking sound. For nearly two hours he was made to peck at the soil and have it scraped away with a jiyoren, then again poke at the earth with the tsurubashi and have it scraped away with the jiyoren, and once more dig the soil with the tsurubashi, until he was made to dig an area four shaku square to a depth of about two shaku.

From deep within his belly,

Ōshimo: 'You too must be quite exhausted. Since the Kami is also quite tired, I shall take a brief rest.'

Along with these words, the jiyoren that had been pulled along released itself from his hands. Resting his hips for nearly ten minutes and gazing at the hole he had dug,

Kiraku: 'What on earth is ever going to come out no matter how long one digs a place like this?'

He could not help feeling thus in his heart. From deep within his belly,

Ōshimo: 'Hey, Kiraku, you are still doubting. Do you think there is no gold here? If I say there is a Kami, most certainly there is. It is a trial of just one more breath. If so, your doubts will surely be resolved. Oban and koban, shining brilliantly with a blinding radiance, will appear in inexhaustible abundance. You have seen silver and copper coins thus far, but you surely have not seen gold. So that you are not startled, I warn you beforehand. You must tightly fasten your haraobi to brace yourself. If you are reluctant, the Kami will punish you. Shall I choke your throat again?'

Kiraku: 'I am not saying anything objectionable or otherwise. Am I not simply doing just as the Kami-sama says?'

Ōshimo: 'You must be quite amused. No matter how much you wag your glib tongue saying you do not need gold, once you think that gold is hidden, your heart will still flutter with excitement. If only this gold exists, you can journey through this world comfortably without needing to toil. You are a truly fortunate fellow. Come, quickly take the tsurubashi!'

Scarcely had these words been spoken when his own body mechanically stood up with a hop, instantly grasped the tsurubashi, and began to peck at the great earth with a clinking sound. Dig and dig as he might, only natural rock lined the earth about two shaku below. He was made to dig for about another hour, but this time not a single inch could be dug. The tip of the tsurubashi became blunt and utterly ceased to be of any use.

Kiraku: 'Pecking away at nothing but such rocks as this no matter how long one tries will be to no avail. If someone had buried something here, a rock lid should have appeared. This fellow is unquestionably a natural rock. Is the location not a bit mistaken? For either greed or virtue, I cannot possibly work any further above this.'

Ōshimo: 'Hahaha, what a coward. How can you accomplish the divine service of the Kami-sama with such feeble determination? Unless you have the resolve to pierce through to the center of the earth, you will never reach the place of the koban by digging three or five ken.'

Kiraku: 'Tengu-san, you-san deceived me, didn't you? Is this not altogether cruel? Rather than making Motoichi, who actually desires the gold, do the digging, you bring me here—me, who have been saying I do not need any gold—and deceive me; this is simply too much. Come, you shall no longer remain in my physical body. Depart quickly.'

Ōshimo: 'No matter how much you tell me to come out, it is not possible as long as your life remains. The truth is it was a lie; I was testing your heart. As if there could possibly be gold in a place like this. Hahaha.'

Kiraku: 'Hey, you great badger! Creeping into a person's body and making a complete fool of me has its limits. No matter what you say now, I will not let you stay in my body any longer.'

Ōshimo: 'Do you still desire gold?'

Kiraku: 'I do not desire it in the least. Since the Tengu fellow is the one who wants it, he used me, and his expectations simply missed the mark. Do not make such a fool of me, come, begone with you.'

He struck his chest and belly with his clenched fist with all his might. With that, the lump inside his belly stopped rising up, and he stopped uttering a word about Buddhism or the Law. Once it came to this, he suddenly felt overwhelmingly lonely and miserable. Thinking that it would look bad to have come all this way and return carrying an empty wicker basket, he snapped off the purple and red azaleas shining with dew in the moonlight, prepared a load of firewood-like flowers, hid his pickaxe and jiyoren hoe among them, and while grumbling about the hazy moon and starting nervously at every shadow, he finally made his way back to Sunadome.

There, though not clearly discernible, two dark shadows were sitting and puffing furiously on their kiserutabako pipes. Kiraku thought to himself within his heart,

'I wonder why there are men smoking in a place like that at this time of night. They might just be dorobau thieves. If they are thieves, they might steal all the koban gold coins I went to all the trouble of digging up, and even take my life. Well, it is a good thing I have no money. If the thieves tell me to hand something over, I bet they will be terribly startled if I thrust these flowers out at them.'

Thinking thus, and approaching the dark shadows timidly along the single-file path,

'Oh, Daisensei, congratulations! From here on, I will carry it for you. The truth is, because Ōshimo was freezing so hard, I did not accompany you, but when you were digging away with all your might, I kept watch from about a hundred yards away. You must have dug up quite a large amount. Come, I will carry it for you from here. After all, gold is heavy for its bulk...' he babbled on gleefully and noisily.

Kiraku: 'No, it is not heavy at all. It is just like an empty basket, so I will carry it back just like this. It was a total failure.'

Motoichi: 'A failure, you say? That is only to be expected. Let us give up on this place, and how about you return straight to my house?'

Kiraku: 'Motoichi-san, it is all just an empty basket filled with azalea flowers.'

Motoichi: 'Even if the top is azaleas, that is fine, isn't it? Here, I will carry it.'

He forcibly snatched the carrying pole and hoisted it onto his shoulder,

Motoichi: 'Ah, it is relatively light. Even so, this must be worth around ten thousand yen. For an empty basket, it is awfully heavy...'

Kiraku: 'The weight is just the heft of the pickaxe.'

Motoichi: 'Well, splendid, splendid. As long as we have capital, even a little will do. Come, from here we shall make eighty thousand yen and set about building the Tengu-sama park.'

And so, gleefully rejoicing, they returned to their home.

From then on, he lost the trust of Motoichi and his son, and was even denied access to the training grounds. Having no other choice, he returned to his own home to practice self-cultivation. Tada Kotoko had returned to Nakamura, immersing herself in the waters of Okuyamakawa to perform misogi purification, and was zealously practicing chinkon and kishin spiritual training alongside four or five others.
