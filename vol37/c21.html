Chapter 21: 参綾 [1033]

参綾〔1033〕

§ 1

It was in the hottest height of old Rokuguwatsu. Having reluctantly bade farewell to Rausobo and the shiugefushiya, he left Anao all alone and proceeded further and further to the north. When he had come to a place about a niri along the michinori, there stood the boundary marker of Minamikuhada and Funaigun. There was what is known as the famous Toratenine, through which flowed the seiriu of the Ōigaha. Beside Toratenine, a small chiyamise was built, overlooking the rows of trees with their fine edabu. Kiraku nanigezaku stopped at that chiyamise and was taking a kiusoku.

A plump and fleshy saikun of about sanjifu appeared and ladled out some shibuchiya for him. Then, gazing at Kiraku's iyō appearance, she spoke.

Woman: 'Anata, are you not a person who performs the sacred service of the Kamisama?'

So she said. Kiraku immediately replied,

Kiraku: 'I am one who performs saniwa for the Kamisama. I have thoroughly investigated the kiyōkai around there, but they were nothing more than platforms for foxes and tanuki. Aha-ha-ha-ha.'

And he laughed effortlessly. This woman, pressing on as if folding cloth one over another,

Woman: 'Moshi Sensei, there is one thing I wish to ask of you. My mother is living in Ayabe now; originally she believed in Konko-sama, but suddenly the Ushitora no Konjin-sama possessed her, and many people received divine virtues, and a sensei of the Konko kiyōkai is taking care of her. However, the Kamisama who possessed my mother said that the one who will share my fate will come from the east. The mother said that once that person appeared, Degami Nao's fate would become clear. Therefore, my husband and I purposely opened a chiyamise here by the roadside to have travelers rest, and have been searching for the person my mother spoke of. I cannot help thinking it must be you, after all. Won't you please investigate my mother's fate just once? This is the ohudesaki that my mother's Kamisama wrote.'

What she brought out and presented was a scattered, single-sheet ohudesaki.

Seeing this ohudesaki, Kiraku was astonished to find that it coincided with a certain part of what he had seen and heard with his spiritual eye during his austerities on Mount Takakuma; accepting the woman's request, he promised to go up to Ayabe shortly, settled down at an inn in Sonobe called Hirodaya, visited acquaintances here and there, and engaged in the propagation of spiritual science.

On the twenty-third day of old Rokugwatsu, he visited the founder's house in the back streets of Ayabe for the first time, and stayed there for two or three days. However, Adachi Masanobu, the missionary-in-charge of the Konko kiyōkai, along with caretakers Nakamura Takezō, Murakami Seijirō, Nishimura Bunsakuemon, and others, offered intense opposition, and judging that the time was not yet ripe, he bade farewell to the founder, left the land of Ayabe, borrowed the tatami room of Nishida Unosuke in Aza Kuroda, Sonobe village, and continued to propagate the Way of God. Although he witnessed and encountered many interesting stories concerning various possessions even in this region, he omits them here for fear of straying onto wrong paths. There was a zealous believer in Kamihonmachi, Sonobe, named Okumura Tokujirō. Because so many believers came to visit Kiraku, the influential figures of Sonobe town, regardless of faith, viewed it as a measure for the prosperity of the land and sought to construct a splendid propagation hall within the Sonobe park, having Kiraku reside there permanently. While the influential people of the land were running about in all directions and the matter was mostly settled, Kataoka Heizō-san arrived all the way from Ayabe as an emissary of the founder Deguchi.

At that time, Kiraku was fishing downstream from the great bridge of the Sonobe River. Heizō-san came there, and from the riverbank said,

Heizō: 'Moshi-moshi, is Ueda-sama about this neighborhood? When I went to Kuroda's house just now, I was told that you had gone to the Sonobe riverbed to catch fish, so I followed the edge of this river and came all the way here.'

So he spoke. From amidst the river, Kiraku answered,

Kiraku: 'Hi, Ueda is me. Are you the Kataoka-san from Ayabe who sent me a letter recently? I have had quite enough of Ayabe, so I am going to stop going there. Since things are interesting right now, I will catch a few more fish and then return; please come when the sun goes down.'

And so he spoke.

Hearing this, Shikata-shi from above the embankment,

Heizau: 'If so, it cannot be helped. As I am staying at the Afugiya in Sonobe this evening, I shall visit you again.'

Saying thus, Shikata-shi crossed the Õhashi and walked off toward the Afugiya. Kiraku finished his fishing, returned to Kuroda's home, changed his clothes, and went to visit Shikata-shi at the Afugiya in Sonobe. Thus, it became known that this time, strictly confidentially to Adachi, Nakamura, and other Yakuin, the Kyōso and he himself had consulted together and come to welcome Kiraku. When it came to this, it was like plunging oneself into the midst of the enemy. Thinking that he must make up his mind with firm resolve, he promptly consented to go to Ayabe, returned to Anao through the eight-ri round-trip night road, told his rausobo and mother that he was definitely going to Ayabe, offered prayers to the Ubusuna no Ōkami, and before daybreak finally returned to the Afugiya in Sonobe. However, Shikata-shi had not the slightest inkling that Kiraku had returned to Anao.

From then on, he returned with Shikata-shi to Kuroda's home, made various preparations, set out from Kuroda around five o'clock, and finally arrived at Hiyama. The day had completely darkened. Shikata-shi, whose eyes were somewhat weak, was no longer able to walk. With no other choice, they put up at an inn called Taruya. Suddenly a heavy rain began to fall, even the thunder rumbled, and it became truly a terrifying universe. Given the back detached room of Taruya, Kiraku and Shikata-shi indulged in talks about all sorts of things, and around one o'clock in the night finally went to sleep. Around four o'clock in the morning, Shikata-shi woke up and was already chanting the Amatsu Norito. Kiraku woke up at the sound, hurriedly got up and looked, and it was still pouring rain like pouring water through a carriage wheel as before. Shikata-shi,

Shikata: 'Sensei, are you awake? I am sorry for waking you up by speaking noisily from early on. Last night, my spirits felt somehow exhilarated and I could not sleep a wink. It appears the Gods are exceptionally spirited. However, it has been a terrible heavy rain continuing since last night. I wonder if this will clear up.'

He asks with a worried look. Kiraku closed his eyes for a moment, perceived, and,

Kiraku: 'When it comes to nine o'clock, it will clear up completely. Until then, well, let us leisurely listen to your talk. You said you came from Ayabe, but I think your home is quite a mountain village, is it not? At the back of the house, there is a reservoir where clear water wells up, and in front, there is an interesting pine tree with branches spreading about a foot thick. And a little to the front right, along the highway, there is something like a hut, where a shop selling things like sweets is set out, and an old woman of about sixty can be seen, is that correct?'

He asked. Shikata-shi was startled,

Shikata: 'Yes, that is just how it is! Can you see even such things so well? Are you, in that case, making use of even Inari?'

He peered into his face with a puzzled look. Kiraku shook his head,

Kiraku: 'No, no, there is no such thing. I saw it through Tengantsū, a part of Reigaku. If anyone only attains a sincere heart, even Tengantsū will straightway become clear to them.'

Shikata: 'Ah, with that I am relieved. I was an old believer of Konkōkyō, but the teacher of Konkōkyō said that things through which places five or six ri away from such a place can be seen are foxes or badgers. If Sensei goes to Ayabe and does something like that, he will be bluntly told that he is simply a fox-user and sent back, so when you go to Ayabe, please refrain from doing that magic for a while. Because receiving suspicion would be a trouble to you, you see.'

Kiraku: 'If it is a place where only such ignorant fellows reside, I will decline with thanks and go back right from here!'

Shikata: 'Without showing such short temper, since you came after all by the inner command of the Kyōso-sama, please think of seeing Ayabe once. Since many sweetfish of the Wachi River are caught these days, please think of going to eat sweetfish, and well, come along once anyway. If you were to abandon me and return right here now, I would have no excuse to offer to the Kyōso-sama.'

Kiraku: 'Since I must have you understand Reigaku first, I shall show you here in practice whether I use a fox or not. If you are satisfied with that, we shall proceed. There is no need to go as far as Ayukui to eat, for Sonobe is quite sufficient...'

Shikata: 'Even for an amateur like me, would such Tengantsū be possible?'

Kiraku: 'Well, sit there, close your eyes, and clasp your hands together.'

Shikata: 'In that case, I will rely on you.'

Shikata-shi sat straight in the middle of the Zashiki, clasped his hands, and closed his eyes as he was told. Kiraku said,

Kiraku: 'Now, Shikata-san, I shall bestow Tengantsū upon you. The moment I say... "Look there!"... some image will appear, so try telling me what it is...'

Shikata: 'Yes...'

Saying this, he closed his eyes with all his might, already becoming a Reikanshiya, breathing a little heavily, and his body trembling with a tingling sensation. Kiraku gave a great shout,

'Look there!'

Shikata: 'Yes, I can see it. There is one small, old Warabuki house, and another dirty house to the side in front, and there is a pond with beautiful water welling up there. And in the back, I can see a Kaya tree and a large Muku tree. A narrow, beautiful river is flowing under the road.'

Kiraku: 'Ah, with that, your Tengantsū has finally opened. That is the house where I was born. That is enough now.'

When he said this, Shikata-shi released his clasped hands and opened his eyes.

Shikata: 'My, what a magnificent Kami. Oh, I am truly impressed. As expected, the Kyōso-sama is indeed great. It was worth the effort of hiding from the many officials and believers to go and welcome you as I was told. If one can understand such things, the work of the Kami who says that the three thousand worlds can be seen through at once will surely be fulfilled.'

He was rejoicing excessively. After that, he asked various questions about illnesses and tested the Tengantsū, and having had Shikata-shi first realize the preciousness of Reigaku, they ate breakfast and were just about to set out when the heavy rain stopped completely, and the sun began to shine brightly in the clear Japanese sky. Shikata-shi said,

Shikata: 'Hey, just as you said, it cleared up completely when it reached nine o'clock. Truly, Reigaku is a wonderful thing. When I return to Ayabe, it will be troublesome if the teachers and officials of the Konkōkyō make a fuss, so let us go quietly to the Kyōso-sama's house in Uramachi.'

Saying this, he crossed the mountain paths for six ri and, around four o'clock that afternoon, finally arrived safely at the Kyōso's house in Uramachi.

Whether someone had told them, seven or eight believers, including Shikata Yohei, Kuroda Kiyoko, Shikata Sumiko, and Shiomi Junko, had already gathered, and they said,

'Heizō-san, you have received such magnificent divine virtue. You have brought us a good teacher.'

They were overjoyed and went around to notify the old believers of Konkōkyō. Adachi Masanobu-shi was surprised by this momentum and sent Nakamura Takezō to Uramachi to stir up trouble and interfere in various ways. However, the momentum of the Kyōso Deguchi and Shikata Heizō-shi was so intense that eventually, Nakamura Takezō also yielded and submitted to the command of the Kyōso.

The caretakers, such as Shikata Gennosuke, Nishioka Yakichi, Nishimura Bun'emon, Murakami Seijirō, Nishimura Shōtarō, and Shikata Izaemon, gathered at the house in Uramachi, and through the explanations of Heizō-shi and the Kyōso, they resonated deeply. They took the character 'Kin' (gold) from Ushitora no Konjin-sama, combined the 'Tsuki' (moon) and 'Hi' (sun) from the Hi no Ōkami-sama and Tsuki no Ōkami-sama, and organized a group called the Kinmeikai. Believers gathered from far and near day and night, and since it became impossible to move in the narrow warehouse in Uramachi, they moved the Kinmeikai to Nakamura Takezō's main house in Honmachi. Shikata-shi flourished by using his Tengantsū, performing divine divination, and praying for the healing of illnesses, gaining immense popularity. Because Shikata Heizō-san had received such divine virtue after being taught Reigaku only once, there were soon more than twenty people who wished to undergo Yūsai training, thinking that anyone could receive divine virtue if they only practiced. Kiraku rented a detached house behind the believer Nishimura Shōbei's house across the way, slept and stayed there, and told the caretakers various stories about the Kami. Adachi Masanobu-shi of the Konkōkyō church had no more strategies to employ, and although he visited the houses of Murakami Seijirō, Nakamura Takezō, Shikata Sumi, Shiomi Jun, and Kuroda Kiyo to attempt various opposition movements, they could no longer produce any effect.

と云ひ乍ら六里の山坂を越えて其日の午後四時頃、漸くにして裏町の教祖の宅へ安着した。

誰が喋つたのか早くも信者の四方与平、黒田清子、四方すみ子、塩見じゆん子を始め七八人の信者が集まつて来て、

『平蔵サン、結構な御神徳を頂きなさつた。よい先生を迎へて来て下さいました』

などと喜び勇み、金光教の旧信者へ通知に各自廻つて了つた。此勢に足立正信氏は吃驚して中村竹造を裏町へ遣はし、色々と水をさし妨害を加へた。されど出口教祖を始め、四方平蔵氏の勢があんまり猛烈なので、到頭中村竹造も我を折り教祖の命に服従して了つた。

四方源之助、西岡弥吉、西村文右衛門、村上清次郎、西村庄太郎、四方伊左衛門等と云ふ世話係は裏町の宅へ集まり来たり、平蔵氏と教祖の説明によつて非常に共鳴し、艮金神様の金の字をとり、日の大神様、月の大神様の月日を合せて金明会と云ふ団体を組織し、信者は日に夜に遠近より集まり来り、裏町の狭い倉の中では身動きもならぬやうになつたので、本町の中村竹造の本宅へ金明会を移して了つた。四方氏は得意の天眼通を振りまはし神占をしたり、病気平癒を祈つたりして非常な人気である。只の一回位、霊学を教へて貰ふて、四方平蔵サンはあれ丈け御神徳を貰ふたのだから、修行さへしたら誰でも神徳が頂けるだらうと、幽斎修行の希望者が瞬く内に二十人あまりも出来て来た。喜楽は向側の西村庄兵衛と云ふ信者の裏の離家を借つて其処に寝泊りをしたり、世話方に色々と神の話を聞かして居た。金光教会の足立正信氏は最早策の施すべき所なく、村上清次郎、中村竹造、四方すみ、塩見じゆん、黒田清などの宅を訪問して、いろいろの反対運動を試みたけれども、到底効を奏する事が出来なくなつて来た。

§ 4

Kyōso felt deep pity for Adachi's kiyōgū, and sent believers alternately to present him with kozukaisen, rice, and other provisions, urging him to datsutai from Konkuōkyō and follow the oshihe of Kyōso. However, Adachi remained guwanko and refused to yield, raising the kisei of hantai both overtly and covertly.

Adachi: 'A kitsunetsuka named Uheda has been dragged to a place like this, and he has started working as a yamako, so be careful not to be deceived.'

With such words, he dispatched Nakamura Takezau, Murakami Fusanosuke, and others to the homes of believers near and far, beginning various acts of hinan and kōgeki. Although Nakamura had lent his own home to Kinmeikuwai, he followed Adachi's meirei and carried out the undō for hantai without distinction between chiuya. Yet, it was impossible to resist the momentum of the times; not even a hitori of the believers would go, and Adachi fell into a plight where he could move neither hands nor feet.

Therefore, having no other choice, Adachi broke his stubborn pride and came to beg:

Adachi: 'I will reform sincerely, so please let me use the Kinmeikuwai.'

The executive members of Kinmeikuwai promptly opened a consultative meeting, and as a result, the following conclusion was reached:

'Adachi Masanobu-shi has poor support among believers, has engaged in scandalous relations with XX and YY, and is defiling the name of the Kami. Therefore, it is best to absolutely reject his entry into Kinmeikuwai at this juncture.'

The reason Adachi came wagging his tail was not because he truly submitted in his heart to Kinmeikuwai. Since his aged mother and children were suddenly facing starvation, he feigned outward submission as a temporary expedient of desperation, intending to overthrow Kinmeikuwai when the opportunity arose and cast out Kiraku-sensei. Such a plot was evident from Adachi's past actions, and even those who had previously received enthusiastic guidance and nurturing from him began to insist on his utmost exclusion, considering it the better judgment to sever all ties taking advantage of this favorable opportunity.

Kiraku pitied Adachi's kiyōgū, and furthermore, he was utterly astounded at the fickle cruelty in the human feelings of the executives and believers who had hitherto believed in Konkuōkyō, thinking within himself:

'There is a saying that today it is another's misfortune, and tomorrow it is one's own. To stay with such unfeeling people will ultimately lead to nothing. If even I leave this place, the difficulties of Adachi and his child will surely be removed. The divine intermediary who ought to save people from difficulties must not afflict them.'

Thinking thus, he turned to the leading executives, starting with Yotsuda-shi, and declared:

Uheda: 'Because I came here, it has resulted in Adachi and his child facing difficulties. I feel deeply apologetic toward him, and so I shall return starting today. Please become good friends with Adachi-shi and perform the divine service.'

At this, the numerous executives were greatly flustered, and as a result of their huddle and deliberation, they pleaded with one voice:

'Regarding the treatment of Adachi-shi, we leave the matter entirely in the hands of Uheda-sensei, so please by all means remain by the side of Kyōso-sama and exert your utmost efforts for the propagation of Ōmoto.'

Thus, Kiraku appointed Adachi-shi as the vice-chairman of Kinmeikuwai, and they came to serve the divine tasks harmoniously under the name of Kinmeikuwai. Deguchi Kyōso also felt extreme anxiety in secret regarding Adachi's situation, but upon Kiraku's compassionate disposition, expressed immense relief and gave thanks. Adachi was exceedingly overjoyed, and the executives and believers, moved by Kiraku's profound sincerity, immediately altered their previous attitude, and following Kyōso, began to revere Kiraku as their master. The confrontation between Konkuōkyō and Kinmeikuwai, which at one time bore the appearance of an impending major conflict, thus reached an amicable resolution, and both sides were able to devote themselves to the service of the Kami in harmony, with light hearts and high spirits.

原文（近代文語）

教祖は足立氏の境遇を気の毒に思ひ、小遣銭や米等を贈つて金光教を脱退し、教祖の教に従へと信者を交る交る遣はして勧められた。けれども足立氏は頑固として応ぜず、陰に陽に反対の気勢を挙げ、

足立『上田と云ふ狐使ひをこんな処へ引張つて来て、山子を始め出したから騙されない様にせよ』

と中村竹造や村上房之助等を遠近の信者の宅に遣はし、色々と非難攻撃を始めた。中村は自分の家を金明会へ貸しておき乍ら、足立の命令に従つて反対運動を昼夜の区別なくやつて居た。併し乍ら時の勢には抗すべくもあらず、一人も信者が行かなくなり、手も足もまはらぬ破目に足立氏は陥つて了つた。そこで止むを得ず足立氏は我を折つて、

足立『何卒改心するから金明会へ使つて下さい』

と頼みに来た。金明会の役員連は速に協議会を開いた結果、

『足立正信氏は信者の受けも悪し、○○や○○と醜関係をつけ、神の名を汚して居るから、此際絶対に金明会へ這入る事は謝絶するがよい』

と云ふ事に協議が纏まつて了つた。足立氏が尾を振つて来たのは、心の裡から金明会へ心従して居るのではない。老母や子供が忽ち糊口に窮する処から、一時の窮策として表面心従したと見せかけ時機を見て金明会を転覆させ、喜楽先生を放り出す計略なる事は、今迄の足立氏の行動に徴して明白だから、今度の好い機会を幸ひに一切の関係を絶つ方が上分別だと、今まで同氏の熱心な教養をうけたものさへ、極力排斥を主張する様になつて来た。喜楽は足立氏の境遇を憐れみ、且又今迄金光教を信じて居た役員や信者の人情の浮薄冷酷なるに呆れ果て、

『今日は人の身の上、明日は吾が身の上と云ふ事がある。こんな薄情な人間の処へ居つては到底駄目だ。自分さへ此処を立ち去つたならば足立氏親子の困難は除かれるだらう。世人の困難を救ふべき神の取次が人を困らせてはならない』

と思つたから、四方氏を始め重なる役員に向ひ、

上田『私が此処へ来たために、足立氏親子が困難を来すべき結果を生じたのだから、私は同氏に対して済まないから今日から帰ります。何卒足立氏と仲良うして神様の御用をして下さい』

と申し込んだ。そこで数多の役員は大に狼狽し、鳩首謀議の結果、

『足立氏の処置に就いては上田先生に一任しますから、是非とも教祖様の側に居て、大本の宣伝に力を尽して下され』

と異口同音に頼み込む。

そこで喜楽は足立氏を金明会の副会長に任じ、金明会の名の許に仲良く神務に奉仕する事となつた。出口教祖も足立氏の身の上につき心密かに非常な心痛をして居られたが、喜楽の同情ある処置に対し、非常に安心をしたと云つて感謝せられた。足立氏は大変に喜び、役員信者も喜楽の赤誠に感じ、直に今迄の態度を改め、教祖に次いで喜楽を師匠と尊敬し出した。一時は大争乱が勃発しさうの模様のあつた金光教対金明会も、茲に円満な解決が出来て、双方とも心持克く勇んで和合の裡に神様の御用に尽す事を得たのである。
