Chapter 24: Kami 助 [1036]

神助〔1036〕

§ 1

Tsuchida Katsuhiro, who was serving as the branch head of the Yagi branch of the Konkuōkyō Church, heard that Adachi Masanobu of Konkuōkyō had surrendered to Kinmeikuwai, and in panic and confusion, after conferring with Sugita Masajirō, who was an upper-level church official, followed by the highly zealous Konkuōkyō member Fukushima Toranosuke of Yagi, rushed to Ayabe to have a direct meeting with Adachi Masanobu.

Adachi-san, 'As I am thinking of asking for reinforcements from the Konkuōkyō Honbu and making every effort myself to assist, withdraw from the worthless Kinmeikuwai, and no matter how difficult it may be, since it is only for a while, continue your solitary struggle. Surrendering to incomprehensible reigaku is not the true essence of a Konkuōkyō teacher. After going to all this trouble in Konkuōkyō up until now, to yield your armor to the enemy so easily is regrettable.'

So he encouraged Adachi-san with hot tears streaming down his face. However, since Adachi-san was already utterly dismayed at the callous and heartless nature of the Konkuōkyō Church toward its subordinates, and at a time when he had finally been moved by the warmth of the Founder and Kiraku, he brushed aside his zealous friend's warning with a single word, and moreover exhausted every word to preach upon the profundity and marvelous spiritual nature of Ōmoto's teachings, and in the end even Tsuchida Katsuhiro became a missionary of Kinmeikuwai.

Then, while Kiraku was explaining stories of the gods and methods of yūsai with Adachi, Tsuchida, and Fukushima-san in the room next to the shrine, a person who had been suffering from a gastrointestinal disease for over ten years came carried in a palanquin from Ōhara, accompanied by two or three relatives, to pray for healing, so Kiraku offered a brief prayer to the gods,

Kiraku: 'Evil spirits, begone!'

And when he discharged just a single word of Kototama, miraculously his illness of many years was cured on the spot, and rejoicing with courage, on his way back he walked by himself, and came to return humming songs and the like. Also, from a place called Taitō, a fifteen-year-old boy named Katayama Unosuke fell ill with an inability to stand on his feet, and he too came riding in a palanquin, but right in front of Adachi, Tsuchida, and Fukushima-san, his legs immediately became able to stand.

The three who witnessed this scene were extremely astonished at the efficacious manifestation of reijutsu. Suddenly Fukushima Toranosuke began to become possessed,

Fukushima Toranosuke: 'Un, un.'

He began to groan, and next Tsuchida Katsuhiro also became a spiritual medium, and came to master a part of tennantsū. Since Adachi Masanobu had been a Konkuōkyō missionary who until then did not know the actual state of the spiritual world, seeing a spirit-possession state was the very first time since his birth, so he felt extremely strange, and thinking that this must surely be the work of an evil spirit or whether Kiraku was a magician, he began to slowly harbor doubts, but after his friend Tsuchida actually obtained the divine assistance of spiritual inspiration,

Tsuchida Katsuhiro: 'The Konkuōkyō Church up until now is something worthless. It is an edited teaching patched together by human beings, so it is not the teaching of the true God.'

He began to chant, and this time in reverse persuaded Adachi-san,

Tsuchida Katsuhiro: 'The doctrines of Ōmoto originate from the true heart of God.'

He strongly asserted. However, Adachi-san still seemed to be struggling with the judgment of right and wrong, true and false.

Due to the earnest requests of the Founder and the officers, Kiraku came to train yūsai practitioners here, and at the residence of Nakamura Takezō-san in Honmachi, for several days alongside propagation he executed yūsai training, seekers of the Way gradually increased, the Honmachi residence of Nakamura-san also became narrow, and he transferred the training hall to the eastern four-way intersection of Hongū, the former open grounds of Konkuōkyō. Fukushima Toranosuke's spirit possession was quite a violent one, being a spirit that could even be called the leader of the wicked spirit world, and it caused a great deal of trouble for the saniwa and officers. On the east side at that time there was the Ayabe police station, and every day the practice of fencing interfered with yūsai, and moreover because of the numerous worshippers he could not practice as he wished, and so after inquiring to the divine world, by the divine decree of Sarutahiko no Ōkami it was decided to transfer the training hall to Takanozu in Yamaga Village. The poem at that time:

To the Takanozu of Mount Takachiho of great omike, The guiding god is Sarutahiko no Ōkami.

He directly transferred the training hall to the residence of Shikata Heizō-san in Takanozu, and after two or three days transferred again to the residence of the local believer Shikata Yūsuke-san.

The practitioners were all disciples of the Yakuin and Shinjiya, and more than twenty of them, including Toranosuke Fukushima, Heizau Shikata, Iusuke Shikata, Kumazau Shikata, Haruzau Shikata, Jinnojiyō Shikata, Sumiko Shikata, Tō Ōtsuki, Seiko Shihomi, Kikuko Nakamura, Tsuyako Tanaka, Hisako Shikata, Tokusaburau Nozaki, Makiko Nishimura, Komatsu Nishimura, Fusanosuke Murakami, Kiyoko Kuroda, Gitarau Uhenaka, Yasuzau Shikata, Tōtarau Shikata, and Takezau Nakamura, gathered and engaged in the practice of Yūsai from morning until night with a rhythmic thumping.

As more than twenty people were in a state of spiritual activation at once, the floor joists began to warp, and Iusuke-san's son, Yūichi-san, was greatly troubled and secretly went to the Ayabe Police Station to petition them, saying, 'Kiraku and Adachi are teaching useless things and causing trouble, so please drive them away.'

He could have simply refused them by exercising his rights as the head of the household, but out of his own cowardice, he chose to take such measures.

Kiraku, having foreseen this through the deity of Komatsubayashi, moved that very night with the practitioners to the residence of Izaemon Shikata-san in Uetani, and taking advantage of the fact that there was a fairly large waterfall in the valley ahead where Fudōson was enshrined, he designated Uetani as the training ground and became absorbed in Yūsai.

As expected, the police came to drive them away.

However, as they were all enthusiastic practitioners who were devoting their sincere hearts to the way of the Kami, they were not in the least bit daunted or concerned, and they continued their practice steadily.

Sarutahiko no Kami once again became possessed, and gave the following divine poem:

The upper valley of the clouds shines bright, Illuminating the unchanging reign of the Lord.

There were hundreds of other divine poems given at that time, but I do not clearly remember them now.

The results of the Yūsai practice were extremely favorable; among the many practitioners, only two or three irregular failures emerged, while all others reached the state of union between Kami and man, and among them were those who used brush and paper to prophesy world turmoil, those who spoke divine oracles regarding the Boxer Rebellion, and those who were possessed by spirits that foretold the Russo-Japanese War or predicted world wars.

Some also became proficient in divine arts such as the heavenly eye, the heavenly ear, the knowledge of past lives, and spiritual communion.

There were not a few believers who greatly realized the incomparable dignity of the way of the Kami.

Most mysterious among them was an eighteen-year-old woman named Makiko Nishimura, who was what is commonly called an idiot, but as soon as she became possessed, her usual speech and behavior changed completely, and like Ukanomitama no Hime in the age of the Kami, she performed the miracle of producing countless grains of millet from her ears, adzuki beans from her nose, and wheat seeds from her private parts.

Seeing this, one can realize that the divine scriptures of our country convey extraordinary truths.

As the Yūsai practice gradually developed, I entrusted the house during my absence to Tōtarau Shikata, and decided to accompany Heizau Shikata-san to visit the Yamanashi Shrine, where Master Yūtate Nagasawa of Fujimi Village, Shizuoka Prefecture, was serving.

While on the road, as we were discussing the boundless divine virtues of the Great Kami, and had crossed the Suchiyama Pass and traversed the Oohara and Kareki Passes, when we reached the mountain village of Totsugawa, Heizau Shikata-san suddenly began to show signs of spiritual activation, his body shaking violently, so we had no choice but to rest at the summit of Kareki Pass, where I performed Chin-kon on him while standing.

The divine messenger Matsuoka temporarily possessed Heizau Shikata-san, and his heavenly eye became even clearer.

As I had become a disciple of the spiritual studies of the elderly Yūtate Nagasawa as previously mentioned, I visited the Yamanashi Shrine, which enshrines Uzume no Mikoto, partly to report to him.

At last, we arrived safely at the residence of Master Nagasawa in Shimoshimizu, Fujimi Village.

Then, Heizau Shikata-san explained, citing examples one by one, that there is a world of difference in both quality and method between spiritual possession and the Inari-sage that is practiced among the common people, and having finally come to understand the essence of spiritual studies, after staying for two days and nights, we bade a reluctant farewell and set out on the road back to Ayabe.

§ 3

Walking about twenty chō of road from Shimoshimizu to Ejiri, just as they were about to board the 1:00 AM express train, the stop was a mere two minutes short. Especially since the train was of the bogie type, and unlike country steam trains, there were not numerous entrances. Furthermore, Shikata unfortunately had poor eyesight, and at night he could hardly see even with the lights. In addition, he had a large amount of luggage slung over his shoulder. Kiraku also boarded quickly with hands full of luggage. When Shikata looked at the boarding platform to see how it was, the train had just begun to move the moment he put one hand on it. The station employee was shouting at the top of his lungs, repeatedly calling out, 'Dangerous! Dangerous!'

Shikata had been dragged for seven or eight ken in the meantime. Kiraku exerted his vajra-like strength and hauled him up to the boarding platform along with the luggage. Thinking of this incident even now makes one shudder. Giving thanks within his heart that he had been saved from a dangerous injury entirely through the divine protection, they safely arrived at Kyoto Station around 1:00 PM the following day. The two entered a certain restaurant in front of Higashi Honganji to finish lunch, and from there headed west along Shichijō Street to reach Nishi Shichijō. From this place, they bought tickets for a shared carriage bound for Kameoka and were waiting for the departure time. Suddenly, Shikata began to suffer from octopus poisoning that he had bought and eaten at a teahouse in front of Honganji, vomiting and having diarrhea, reaching over ten times. His complexion turned pale blue, and he collapsed on the spot, becoming almost like a dead person. The owner of the carriage company was startled and said:

'Customer-san, you have cholera. Come on, please leave this place as quickly as possible. If the police find out, everything will be burned up, and business will become impossible. If something like that happens, the house will be in great turmoil. Come on, please go home quickly.'

With that, he returned the money he had once accepted and took back the ticket. Kiraku took out the protective talisman bestowed by the Kyōshu from his bosom, placed it over Shikata's shoulders, and also made him hold two monetary offerings received from the Kyōshu-sama in his mouth, performing chinokon. The divine virtue manifested immediately, and for the first time Shikata's speech became clear, and his energy gradually recovered. Kiraku took Shikata by the hand and went out to the gate. Spotting two pull-cart drivers who happened to be pulling empty carts, recognizing it as a gift from heaven, they immediately boarded them. With an innocent face, they had them run for about a ri and a half, and when they approached the Katsura Great Bridge, Shikata had completely regained his spirits as before, and from the top of the cart, in a clear voice, he began to chat about various topics and sing songs. From there, dashing through Ōe-zaka, Ōji, and Shinomura, when they returned to just a little before the Shinomura Hachiman Hall, the iron ring of the rickshaw that Shikata was riding suddenly came off with a rattle, and it shattered completely limp. Shikata was thrown headfirst onto the street, but fortunately he was unhurt without a single scratch and safe.

Shikata appeared to be a person of considerable strong fortune, and it is truly wondrous that within a single day he was saved from the perils of a train, a carriage, and a rickshaw as many as three times. There is no other judgment to make than to consider this as well to be the divine virtue of the Lord God. Within the life of a human being, once or twice, good fortune will certainly come facing them. Similarly, once or twice, great disasters will also come. By the power of Shikata's faith and the grace of the Great Kami, being saved from such a dangerous place by ninety-nine percent was entirely thanks to serving the Kami with a single heart.

四方氏は余程運の強い人と見え、一日の間に三度まで汽車、馬車、人力車の危難に救はれるといふ事は、実に不思議である。これも神様の御神徳と考へるより外に判断はつかぬ。人間には一生の中には必ず一度や二度は幸運が向ふて来る。それと同様に又一度や二度は大難が来るものである。四方氏の信仰の力と大神様のお蔭で、斯る危ない所を九分九厘で助けられたのは、全く神様に一心に仕へて居たお蔭である。
