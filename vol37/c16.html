§ 1

The planting of rice having also been completed, intending to thresh barley, they spread the barley all over the courtyard and dried it in the sunlight.

Around eleven o'clock that day, three men came asking,

Kiraku-san: 'Is Kiraku-san at home?'

Lately, because of the barley threshing and rice planting, there were hardly any pilgrims, so intending to help with the barley threshing, he had assumed the appearance of a farmer and had been carrying barley from the paddy fields and spreading it out since early morning.

The three men who came thither were Iwata Yatarō, Iba Kyūsuke, and Irie Koutarō from a place called Inji in Asahimura.

Their business was that the wife of Iwata Yatarō, called Ofuji, had become a spiritual medium since two or three months ago, cooked five or six shō of rice a day and consumed it without a care, drank three shō of liquor without fail, and while helping with sericulture, she was a troublesome person who pinched and ate the silkworms that had grown with so much effort, one after another from the edge.

However,

Ofuji: 'This place is Shiraki Daimyōjin.'

saying thus, she glared with strange eyes and gave various directions.

Various sick people were completely cured by everyone through this Ofuji's prayers and the medicines she directed, and that became a rumor, so from morning till evening, pilgrims filled the small house.

In the neighboring village, there was a church enshrining an Inari called Kurokami Daimyōjin, and the attendant priestess of that place was an old woman of about fifty.

That old woman reported to the police box, saying that Iwata Ofuji had begun acting as a mountain shaman and was deceiving people, so a policeman came and said,

Policeman: 'It is not allowed to gather people at your house without permission or to give directions for medicine.'

speaking noisily.

Nevertheless, Iwata Ofuji, being a spiritual medium, would not listen no matter what anyone said, and boldly gave divine pronouncements, performed prayers, and gave directions for medicines.

Then, as usual, she drank heavily every day, ate huge amounts of rice, and even seized and ate the silkworms that were on the verge of making cocoons from the edge one after another, so the three men consulted on how they wanted someone to calm her down somehow, and taking advantage of the holiday after the rice planting was finished, they came to ask Kiraku for help.

Kiraku directly commanded Komatsu, the kannushi, to perform a clairvoyant vision of Inji's Iwata Ofuji's house and appearance.

As a result, he completely clairvoyantly saw that her house was thatched with tiles, how many tatami mats there were, how many rows of silkworm shelves were lined up, that there were pockmarks on Ofuji's face, that a little to the side of the house there was a tutelary deity's shrine, and that toward the back there was a river five or six ken wide without water, with bamboo groves on both of its banks.

When he told the three men about this, they looked at each other's faces in mutual astonishment and exclaimed,

The three men: 'Indeed, it is just as you say. The gods are truly awe-inspiring things, are they not?'

admiring it.

Therefore, Kiraku accepted the request of the three men, and accompanied by Komatsu the kannushi in a party of five, after finishing their midday meal and bathing in the scorching summer sun, they crossed the O-hashi bridges of Yoshida, Kobayashi, Ogawa, Kawaseki, and Yagi, and finally arrived at Iwata Ofuji's residence around three o'clock that day.

When they went and looked, dozens of pilgrims were sitting in the tatami room.

Iwata Ofuji came out to the doorway to greet them, turned toward her husband Yatarō, and asked in a low voice,

Ofuji: 'Yatayarn, has Kiraku-san arrived?'

Yatarō had been instructed by Kiraku,

Kiraku: 'Say he is out, and then since it is necessary to investigate whether or not the divine possession knows that I am Kiraku, it will be fine to say that people from Kobayashi came along the way to ask for prayers.'

so Yatarō intentionally feigned ignorance,

Yatarō: 'Although I went with much effort, Kiraku-sensei was out. Eh, it cannot be helped.'

he showed, drooping his head dejectedly.

Ofuji, perhaps taking a look at Kiraku's mustache shaped like the kanji for eight and mistaking him for a policeman, asked anxiously in a low voice,

Ofuji: 'Yatayarn, isn't that person a policeman?'

Yatarō replied,

Yatarō: 'No, that person is a rug merchant staying at Izutsuya in Kobayashi, and when I told him about your divine possession, he strongly agreed and said he also wanted to have a matter regarding his trade and his bodily illness divined, so he followed along. And that thick-eyed woman is a maid from Izutsuya.'

Thus she is deceitfully smoothing things over. Ofuda suspects nothing in particular,

Ofuda: 'Oh, is that so. Then I shall now pray to the Kamisama.'

So saying, she turned toward the Goshinden, clapped her hands in hakushiyu, and began to chant the Kamigoto with a katakotomaji. Kiraku,

Kiraku: 'What a woman with muddy kototama. A tanuki is most likely possessing her, no doubt.'

Thinking thus, while blending into the crowd, he had Kosue clearly see-through the guardian deity of the other, and holding back with an innocent expression, he waited. When the norito ended, Ofuda gnashed her teeth loudly, 'kiri-kiri-kiri,' bared her eyes like acorns, and from time to time sticking her tongue out fluttering this way and that, she abruptly turned her back to the shindan and faced the direction of the sankeinin. No matter who looked, it did not look like the face of a ningen; it had become the very physiognomy of a tanuki. Seeing her own appearance, Ofuda suddenly changed her attitude, placed both hands on the floor,

Ofuda: 'Was this, was this Kiraku-sensei? I am Shiraki-daimyōjin, who guards the first peak of Inariyama. Today, while you were busy with the thought of hulling barley, Yatarō and both other persons intruded and partook of a grand feast. You have gracefully come over. I followed behind Yatarō and came as far as your honorable home. Therefore, when resting at the Izutsuya in Kobayashi, I have properly heard all that you said to Yatarō in order to test me. By all means, please grant me a divine name, and so that I may perform the service of the spirit world, I beg you to intercede with the Lord God.'

She gnashed her teeth furiously once again with a 'kiri-kiri-kiri.' Dozens of worshippers concentrated their gazes upon Ofuda and Kiraku, holding their breath and staring intently.

Kosue, leaping up with a single shout,

Kosue: 'You!'

That night, having been allowed to lodge at the house of Shiba Kusuke, giving shusai to Iwata Fuji, when the two of them walked together and returned to Anō, it was a situation where the patrolman had come and was about to drag several shugyōsha off to the kōbansha.

Kiraku engaged in various reigaku-based discussions with the patrolman and Sugiyama, and finally the patrolman was convinced and returned safely.

This man called Sugiyama was originally an outcast of Kinki who had served as the village chochō, but during the drought of the 16th year of Meiji, a gyōja of Mitakekyō named Yamashita came to Yobe in Kameoka to pray for rain, performing a two-week religious fast inside the riverbed to make rain fall.

However, although the rain did not fall enough to be fully satisfying, it is a fact that toward the end of the two weeks, it came down in a scattering drizzle.

At that time, a young woman named Takashima Ofumi, the wife of a certain family in Yobe, was moved by the divine virtue of Yamashita and went to the river every night to perform suigyō, when before she knew it, her body began to shake,

'This is Inari Daimyōjin!'

and she began to chatter.

From then on, called the Inari-san of Yobe, people coming to worship began to emerge from all directions.

The man who came to worship at that time was Sugiyama Tōgorō.

'You darn fox, take your things and get out of here,'

Thinking that somehow or other he wanted to crush him, he had persuaded the residential patrolman and come here.

其夜は射場久助の家に泊めて貰ひ、岩田藤に修斎を与へ、二人連れ立つて穴太へ帰つて見ると、四五人の修業者を巡査がやつて来て交番所へ引張つて去のうとしてる処であつた。さうして御嶽教の太元教会の杉山某と云ふ男が其処に立つて居る。喜楽は巡査と杉山に向つて種々と霊学上の議論を闘はし、漸く巡査は納得して無事に帰つて行つた。

此杉山と云ふ男は元は金岐の隠亡であつて、村の戸長を勤めて居た男であつたが、明治十六年の旱魃で、御嶽教の山下と云ふ行者が、亀岡の余部へやつて来て雨乞をして、雨を降らすと二週間の断食を河原の中でやつた。けれども雨はあまり足りになる程降らなかつたが、二週間の上りにバラバラと降つてきたのは事実である。其時に、余部の或家の女房で、高島おふみと云ふ若い女が、山下某の神徳に感じ、毎晩川へ行つて水行をやつて居ると何時の間にやら体が震ひ出し、

『此方は稲荷大明神だ！』

と喋り出した。それから余部のお稲荷サンと云つて、四方八方から参詣する者が出来て来た。其時に参拝したのが杉山藤五郎と云ふ男であつた。此男は相当に学問もあり財産もあり、人格もあまり悪くない。何時も高島ふみの教会へ通ふて受付等をやつて居たが、知らぬ間に妙な仲となつたのを、ふみの夫が嗅つけ、

『ド狐もつて出て行きさらせ』

と一言の許に放り出され、二人はそれより天下晴れての夫婦気取りで、同じ余部の町へ宏大な家を借り、そこへ稲荷サンを祀つて附近の信者を集めて居た。然るに今度喜楽が、あまり遠からぬ穴太で伺ひや祈祷をやり出したので、此教会へうつつを抜かして通ふて居た信者が残らず穴太へ集まるので、何とかして叩き潰してやらうと考へ、駐在所の巡査を説きつけてやつて来たものである。

§ 4

Kiraku had also been visiting Fumino Takashima's church frequently since three or four years prior because his father was ill, in order to have prayers performed or to receive guidance, and had become quite intimate with Sugiyama.

However, Sugiyama had been incited by Fumino Takashima and was plotting to send Kiraku of Anao away somewhere.

At the Taigenkyōkuwai in Amarube, a man named Hattori was working as the receptionist.

It was in the spring of the 29th year of Meiji.

Hearing that the spring grand festival was to be held, Kiraku made an early pilgrimage to see, and found only Hattori alone, waiting at the entrance while sipping sake little by little.

At first glance, he was an old man who looked to be about fifty-seven or eight, or sixty-two or three.

Kiraku: 'Good morning, Hattori-san. I heard today is the festival, so I came to worship.'

Saying this, Hattori flickered his eyes and spoke.

Hattori: 'Ah, Kiraku-san, go ahead and have a cup. The festival here is supposed to be at one o'clock in the afternoon, but since everyone is coming from distant places, no matter what it won't start until around four o'clock. Sugiyama-san and Ofumi Takashima-san went running off to the managers' houses since early this morning and are out, but even Inari-san is just so-so. ...Kiraku-san, how about you also wake up properly? You tie a fox's tail to your buttocks and poke it out from the lining of your haori now and then, deceiving people as if the real Inari-san has possessed you, so I can't bring myself to worship in earnest. It's ridiculous, being worked to the bone from morning till night for bowls of rice is unbearable. They bought and left a shō of sake calling it sacred wine, so I put water in the sacred sake flask and am drinking the sake myself.'

He was blurting out secrets from start to finish.

Kiraku: '...Surely there's no such thing. That fellow Hattori must be uttering such slander and defamation out of excessive discontent...' Thinking thus, he took out his bento to eat and waited for the time of the grand ceremony to arrive.

原文（近代文語）

喜楽も三四年以前から父が病気なので、祈祷して貰つたり伺つて貰ふ為めに高島ふみの教会へチヨコチヨコ通ひ、杉山とは余程懇意になつて居たのである。けれども杉山は高島ふみに唆されて、穴太の喜楽を何処かへやつてやらうと企んで来たのであつた。余部の太元教会には服部某と云ふ男が受付をして居た。明治二十九年の春の事であつた。春季大祭が行はれると云ふので早うから参拝して見ると、服部某が只一人、玄関口に酒をチヨビリチヨビリ飲み乍ら控へて居た。一見して五十七八か六十二三位に見える爺サンである。喜楽は、

喜楽『服部サン、お早う。今日はお祭りぢやさうで参拝しました』

と云ふと、服部は目をちらつかせ乍ら、

服部『あゝ喜楽サンか、ま一杯やりなさい。此処の祭は午後一時と云ふのだが、皆遠い所から参るので如何しても四時頃にならぬと始まらぬ。杉山サンも高島おふみサンも朝早うから世話方の家へ奔走に行つたので留守だが、稲荷サンも宜い加減なものだぜ。……喜楽サン、お前サンも宜い加減に目を醒ましたら如何だ。尻に狐の尻尾を結びつけて羽織の裏からチヨイチヨイ出し、本当の稲荷サンが憑つて来られた様に誤魔化すので、本気に拝む気になりはせないわ。阿呆らしい、朝から晩まで椀給でこき使はれて堪つたものぢやない。お神酒だといつて一升買ふて置きやがつたから、お神酒徳利に水を入れて、酒は此通り俺が飲んで居るのだ』

と何から何まで秘密を素破抜いて居る。喜楽は、

……マサカそんな事はあるまい。服部奴が不平のあまりこんな中傷讒誣をするのだらう……と思ひ乍ら、弁当を出して食ひ祭典の時の至るのを待つて居た。
