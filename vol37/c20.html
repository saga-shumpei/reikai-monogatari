The story returns a little to the beginning.

On the day of the Jinmutenōsai festival on the third day of the fourth month in the thirty-first year of Meiji, Kiraku had been purifying the Shinden since early morning and conducting the Saiten together with the practitioners.

A man of over fifty came wandering up unexpectedly.

The man strode unceremoniously across the threshold and entered abruptly,

Man: 'I am a person from Kishiu and my name is Mitsuya Kiuemon.

I am the Fukuiken Headquarters Director of the Inari Kau, and the distribution officer of the Inari Kaushiya Sōhonbu, affiliated with the Yamanashi Inari Jinjiya in Abegun, Shizuokaken.

While on a circuit of Kishiu, I heard rumors about this place, and upon immediately going up to the Sōhonbu and asking Sōri Nagasawa-dono, I was told that since it is a person with a karmic connection, I should investigate anyway.

Because it is the words of Nagasawa-san, the great master of spiritual science who sees through the past, present, and future in a single glance, you should gladly accept this, and I am certainly not distributing paper talismans as a mere formality.'

Before Kiraku could say even a single word in reply, he was acting as overbearing as a fox borrowing the tiger's majesty.

Kiraku felt somewhat troubled regarding the name Inari Kaushiya.

This was because in the Kuwatanba area, mentioning the Inari Kaushiya immediately brought to mind Inari-oroshi, and it would be misunderstood as worshiping foxes and raccoon dogs.

However, upon hearing that the great master of spiritual science who penetrates the past, present, and future was Nagasawa-san, it came about that this Mitsuya could not be dismissed empty-handed.

This was because after returning from the ascetic training on Mount Takakuma since February, he had been engrossed in spiritual inspiration problems, and day and night, he had concentrated his spirit on the resolution of spiritual science.

Furthermore, even his relatives, brothers, and villagers indulged in jeering and abusive language, calling him a mountain child, an izuna-user, a fox, a raccoon dog, a field tengu, or half-mad, so he had been worrying about how somehow to make this light shine and open people's eyes.

Since Mitsuya had come to a place where he was thus concerned, he felt as though he had recognized a single ray of radiant light, rejoiced eagerly, immediately had Mitsuya stay at his own home, and put various questions on spiritual science to him to listen to, but as he seemed to be merely a man of talisman distribution, the talk on spiritual science was full of digressions, and no matter what he asked, there was not a single thing to be gained.

Then, managing travel expenses and guided by Mitsuya, on the thirteenth of the very same month, he finally departed from Anō and walked all the way to Kyōto, and getting on a third-class train for the first time since his birth, he was able to arrive safely at the home of Nagasawa-san in Shizuoka.

Nagasawa-san was at that time still forty years old and in the very prime of his vigor.

He spoke continuously for three or four hours about various matters of spiritual science and the personal history of Okina Honda Chikatomo.

Even when Kiraku thought to say a single word, there was not the slightest opening.

Without even listening to the purpose of their visit, he chattered away for about four hours, walked straight off to the toilet, sat down again in his original place, rolled his large eyes with white showing on three sides, and because his eyes were somewhat close together, he bent his back, peered at us, and continued his own talk once more.

Beneath the desk, newspapers from two or three months were scattered unceremoniously.

Many letters received, both opened and unopened, were mixed up with the newspapers and scattered all around the wide desk.

Nagasawa-san pulled off the edge of a torn paper on the shōji with a quick motion, blew his nose sharply, folded the paper again as the nasal mucus threatened to flow drop by drop, and finally, when the mucus overflowed and threatened to drop onto his knees, this time he hastily tore off the edge of a newspaper, wrapped the mucus paper in it, threw it unceremoniously under the desk, and with an unconcerned face continued chattering for about another five hours.

His body was exhausted from the long train journey.

Even when he thought he would like to stretch his legs somewhere for a moment, the master did not move, so there was nothing he could do, and at last that day ended with him merely stating his address and name, and with nothing but Nagasawa-san's talk.

The master's maternal mother was a person named Toyoko, who had attained a considerable degree of spiritual inspiration.

From around nine o'clock in the morning, Nagasaha Sensei summoned him again. Hastening directly into the presence of Sensei, and determined this time to speak on his own and not let Sensei utter a single word, the moment they met he breathlessly poured forth every single detail of his becoming kamigakari for about three hours. Sensei merely gave occasional responses of 'Hai, hai,' and listened with rapt attention to Kiraku's three-hour long tale. As a result, it was decided that an attempt at an osonae evaluation would be made, with Kiraku sitting in the seat of the kannushi and Sensei acting as the osonae official, and the yūsai ritual began. As a result of this, it became clear beyond a shadow of a doubt that it was the divine possession of Komatsubayashi no Mikoto, and he was even presented with a certificate attesting to the higher-grade completion of the two courses of chinkon kishin. Kiraku had hitherto been reviled by numerous people as a madman, a mountain savage, or one possessed by a fox, with not a single person recognizing him; but now, as a result of such divine judgment, he was conclusively determined to be experiencing a high-grade kamigakari. Overjoyed with the thought that this very Sensei was unequaled in the world and would become a mighty source of support for him, he immediately begged to be initiated as a disciple. In short, he became a disciple of Nagasaha Sensei not so much to study reigaku as because he was overjoyed to have his own spiritual inspiration acknowledged.

Following Sensei thereafter, he crossed over to Miho no Matsubara, paid homage at Miho Shrine, gazed upon the Robe of Feathers Pine, and viewed ancient textiles claimed to be torn fragments of the heavenly being's robe housed within a glass bottle. Being cared for for about a week, he finally managed to return to his home in Anō on the night of the twenty-second. Mitsuya Kiuemon also followed him back to Anō, traveled back and forth to the residence of Shimoji Kumakichi in Sonobe, and ended up even acting as the go-between for the match between Saitō Shizuko and Kumakichi. Completely altering his previous attitude, he incited Shimoji Kuma and various anti-movements began to be directed against Kiraku.

Shimoji Kuma had as his wife Saitō Shizuko, who possessed a rather poor kamigakari, and he himself also became possessed by a kami and began practicing market divination. Among Shimoji's confidants was a man named Fujita Taihei. This man made his living by taking in bolts of fabric from people, tailoring kimono and haori, and receiving wages. At the request of Shimoji Kuma, he had pawned various bolts of fabric entrusted to him from all over, borrowed money, and had all of it spent by Shimoji Kuma; and now, facing urgent demands from his clients, he was suffering in extreme agony. Since lingering would likely cause a criminal matter to arise, Taihei himself came all the way to Anō, and while weeping, recounted to Shimoji Kuma how he had fallen into an inescapable plight, asking if there might not be some way to help him out. Then Saitō Uichi came forth together with Shimoji Kuma, who had become his aunt's husband, and Saitō said: 'Is there no way to devise a plan to help Fujita? そこへ巡回の巡査がやつて来て、下司熊を労はり八木まで送り届けてくれた。喜楽も宇一も巡査の後に従いて八木の橋詰まで帰つて来た。来て見れば橋の西側に劇場があつて、芝居が始まつてゐる。勧進元は下司熊の父親の下司市といふ可なり名の売れた顔役である。下司熊は喜楽や宇一を楽屋の中まで引張て行き裸になつて見せて、

下司『あゝ喜楽サン、折角に世話になつた牛の金が最前の喧嘩でおとしたとみえて、これ此通り一文も無い』

としらばくれてゐる。後から事情を探つて見れば、実際は五十円に牛を売り、ワザとに八百長喧嘩を仕組み、一文も残らず引つたくつてやるといふ計略に乗せられたのであつた。又藤田の来たのも、下司や宇一との計略に依つて喜楽の牛を売らし其金をせしめようといふ計略であつた事が判明したのである。それが分つたので喜楽は神懸になり、腹の中の憑霊に向つて、

喜楽『なぜ喜楽の肉体がこんな目に会うてるのに知らさなんだか、言はいでもよいこと許り喋る癖に、なぜかう云ふ時に知らしてくれぬのだ。モウこれからお前らの言ふことは聞かぬ。サア私の体からトツトと帰つてくれ』

と腹立紛れに呶鳴り立てた。さうすると暫く静まつて居つた玉ゴロが、又もや喉元へこみ上げて来て、小さい声で、

『アツハヽヽヽ、馬鹿だのう』

といつたぎり、何と云つても、キウともスウとも答へてくれぬ。とうとう泣き寝入りになつて了うた。

§ 4

Geshikuma later conspired with the village Shinkuwan at a place called Ihashimizu in Suchi, burying a wooden statue of Kuwannon deep in the earth, and went to request the Kuchiyō's residence, saying, 'Now, I have heard through a divine revelation that the statue of Kuwannon is buried at a certain point in Ihashimizu, and I would like to have it investigated once.'

Having obtained permission from the Kuchiyō, he went out to dig it up together with the Kamunushi of the shrine, whereupon the wooden statue of Kuwannon emerged, and making that the Goshintai, he constructed an altar at the house of a certain Funakoshi, enshrining wooden statues of various Shinbutsu like the shop of a Furudauguya, erecting twenty or thirty banners here and there, boiling water under the pretext of its being the miraculous medicinal bath of Daishi, causing kuwanjiya to bathe in it, and thus gathering a great many Gufugufu.

And because it was said that the wooden statue of Kuwannon had appeared by divine revelation, it became immensely popular, and for a time it was exceedingly prosperous, but at length he was fined by the police and subjected to detention, from which time believers gradually ceased to come, and having no choice, he left Ihashimizu and danced his way back to Sonobe again, and as the Kami business no longer flourished at all, he entered the ranks of gamblers once more, and finally contracted orchitis and died prematurely. Taihei also passed away from a sudden illness after two or three years.

Hearing it said that his cow had been taken as a servant, Yoshimatsu flared up in anger once again, and Yoshimatsu: 'This Kami is a blind Kami, so I just silently watched my fool of an older brother being deceived; it's a coward Kami. I will no longer keep it in my house,' saying thus, he overturned the Saidan again and rampaged about, so Kiraku could not remain at ease either, wondering what would be best to do from now on, and thinking he would try asking Kami, he went to worship at the local Ujigami shrine and received a divine oracle. At that time, Komatsubayashi no Mikoto possessed Kiraku and declared in a mighty voice, 'Set forth toward the northwest as early as even a single day, for the workings of the spiritual realm are prepared. There are people waiting for your coming. Without being attached to anything, depart quickly from here and head toward Sonobe!'

From that time, Kiraku resolved to leave his hometown.

原文（近代文語）

下司熊は其後須知の岩清水といふ所で、村の神官と諜し合せ、観音の木像を土の中へ深く埋けておいて、「サテ自分は神さまのお告に依り岩清水の或地点に観音さまが埋まつて御座ることを聞きましたが、一ぺん調べさして頂きたい」と区長の宅へ頼みに行つた。それから区長の許しを受けて、宮の神主と共に掘り出しに行つた所、観音の木像が出たので、それを御神体とし、船越某の家で祭壇を作り、所在神仏の木像を古道具屋の店のやうに祀りこみ、二三十本の幟をあちら此方に立て、お大師さまの御夢想の湯だと云つて、湯をわかし、患者を入浴せしめなどして、沢山の愚夫愚婦を集めて居た。そして観音の木像が神さまのお告げで現はれたといふので大変な人気となり、一時は非常に繁昌してゐたが遂に警察から科料を取られ、拘留に処せられ、それより段々信者が来なくなつて了ひ、やむを得ず、岩清水を立つて、再び園部へ舞ひもどり、神さま商売もテンと流行らなくなつたので、再び博徒の群に入り、とうとう睾丸炎を起して夭死して了つた。泰平も亦二三年を経て急病でなくなつて了つた。牛を下司に取られたといふので、又々由松が怒り出し、

由松『此神は盲神だから、兄貴の馬鹿がだまされて居るのを、黙つて見てやがつた、腰抜神だ。モウ俺の内にはおいてやらぬ』

といつて再斎壇を引つくり返し、暴れまはるので、喜楽も安閑として居る訳にも行かず、此上如何したらよからうか、一つ神さまに伺つて見ようと、産土の社に参拝して神勅を受けた。其時小松林命喜楽に神懸りして、

『一日も早く西北の方をさして行け、神界の仕組がしてある。お前の来るのを待つてゐる人がある。何事にも頓着なく速にここを立つて園部の方へ向つて行け！』

と大きな声できめつけられた。それより喜楽は故郷を離れる事を決意したのである。
