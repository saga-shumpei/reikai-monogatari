Chapter 18: 奥 Field 操 [1030]

奥野操〔1030〕

§ 1

Once the training ground had been ordered to retreat from the tatami room of Saitō Uichi, the old man's mood improved once again, and the secondary training ground was granted a two-bay adjoining inner parlor.

At that time, Tada Koto, Ishida Kosue, Ueda Kaukichi, and others were experiencing the most fascinating divine possessions, displaying various wondrous divine arts.

Tada Koto clasped both hands to become the saniha, Ishida Kosue served as the kamunushi, and when she said,

Tada Koto: 'Now, an earthquake!'

suddenly the house began to rattle and shake, and a roaring sound could be heard.

However, the earthquake was confined to this house alone; upon stepping out the doorway, there was no longer anything amiss, and the great crowd of people were flabbergasted and left speechless by that unfathomable divine power.

When Tada Koto commanded,

Tada: 'I am Tomoegozen! Hey, retainers, pour tea for everyone!'

a clattering sound came from the cupboard, and tea bowls soared several feet through the void, appearing before the two of them.

When Tada gave the strict command,

Tada: 'Distribute them one by one before everyone,'

though no one was there, the tea bowls passed five to six inches above the tatami mats and neatly settled right before each person's knees, and the wonder was that upon settling, every single tea bowl spun around sharply two or three times.

When Tada Koto commanded,

Tada: 'Now, pour the tea!'

Ishida Kosue held a clay teapot in her hand and made the motion of pouring tea. Thereupon, the teapot that had been resting on the brazier of its own accord swung through the void, and as if someone were holding it to pour tea, it went around filling each cup to about eighty percent, one after another.

In the beginning, they praised Kiraku's spiritual science as a magnificent thing, but gradually they began to revile it to the utmost as mere sorcery and izuna-tsukai.

Unconcerned by this, they manifested spirits to move the brazier, raised the desk two or three feet into the void, and revolved the teapot, immersing themselves day and night in research.

At no other time was anything so amusing and exhilarating.

The spirit possessing Tada Koto, which called itself Tomoegozen, spoke to Kiraku, saying:

Tada: 'Since the gods are guarding and granting us various spiritual arts in this manner, why don't we form a troupe from now on, rush out to Kyoto and Osaka, become magicians, rake in a pile of money, and use that capital to build a grand divine hall and establish a headquarters?'

Thus she urged him.

Thinking that to do such a foolish thing in the Way of the Gods would instead defile the divine path, Kiraku hesitated, whereupon Ishida Kosue's possessing spirit stirred once again and urged:

Kosue: 'Now, from here on I shall walk through the void! Anything and everything is as you desire. With such splendid divine arts, it is a pity to keep them hidden deep in the mountains of Tamba. If we go out to Kyoto and Osaka and splendidly display these divine arts, surely the divine virtue of the gods will be understood at once, and the Path will open up. Now, make up your mind quickly!'

Thus the two possessed individuals, Tada and Kosue, pressed in from both sides.

The mountain-loving father and child, Motokichi and company, swallowed their saliva in delight and said,

'Now, this is the divine virtue of the gods! From the celestial eye, celestial ear, and celestial speech, down to such divine arts—if we show these, no man, no matter how strong his logic, will be able to avoid yielding. By making money, the Way of the Gods will be spread all the more! Hehehe, could there ever be such splendid things in this world?'

becoming quite enthusiastic.

Even Iwamori Toku and Saitō Takako mastered various wondrous divine arts and came to agree.

In Kiraku's heart: ...To go out to such a place and perform like an entertainer feels somehow too embarrassing to bear; yet, if the Way of the Gods is thereby opened, perhaps there is no compelling reason to stop it either. Leaving everything to divine possession, shall I make up my mind and go to Kyoto and Osaka for a single theatrical run? ...So resolving, he went to worship at the ujigami shrine and inquired.

When he did so, two or three lumps once again welled up from his own belly, dancing up to the vicinity of his throat.

Dona! Dona! Donatsu!

Tada: 'I was a person called Okuno Misao, fencing instructor to Lord Koide, the daimyo of Sonobe; but due to the jealousy of a colleague, I was slandered, driven out of Sonobe, came to Kameyama, became fencing instructor to Lord Matsudaira, and while serving there, I was infatuated by the eighteen-year-old younger sister of the lord, and at last was slandered once again by my colleague, swallowing tears of vexation, exactly eighty years ago tonight, I am a bushi who committed seppuku and met my end. he rebuked. The possessed spirit shook its head from side to side,

"Though you are a hero of the realm, a fencing instructor, such rude and insolent words and intentions I will not put up with!"

saying thus, with a leap she stood up,

"Yaa! Yaa!"

calling out, flying around back and forth and left and right above Kiraku's head, occasionally kicking his head and raising a commotion; no matter how much chinkon was performed, it only grew wilder and did not calm down in the least. Kiraku, almost at his wits' end, slipped away from this place and hurried back to the Ubusuna Shrine, offering up prayers. Running up thither, Ishida Kosue came,

Kosue: 'Moshi Sensei, it has calmed down. A bushi named Misao has one favor to ask of Sensei, so he is asking you to return quickly. Please go back.'

he said, bowing his head politely and pleading. Kiraku,

Kiraku: 'Oh, it has calmed down already? That is indeed grateful, it is thanks to Ubusunasama.'

saying thus, giving thanks before the shrine, he immediately returned to Motoichi's residence.

Upon returning, Tada Koto was seated in stern solemnity,

Tada: 'Aiya Ueda-shi, as I stated earlier, I am a person named Okuno Misao, fencing instructor to Lord Kameyama. Having neither wife nor child, nor any kin, there is none to perform memorial rites for me, and I am wandering in the void. Since this is a person named Nishio, please investigate thoroughly; the kaimyō is inscribed as such-and-such and he is enshrined at Nishio's residence and a certain temple in Nishimachi. First of all, after investigating the truth and falsehood of this matter, if you will enshrine this spirit of mine, I shall return to the seat of the kami, and you will protect the kamiwaza; not to mention throughout the land of Japan, reaching to the corners of the world, within ten years I shall make your name resound. If you are still doubtful, go to the graveyard in Kameyoka Koseura and investigate. The third stone pagoda from the right as you enter is my stone pagoda. As a sign of my spiritual presence, if Ueda-shi stands before the stone pagoda it will move of its own accord, so please use that as proof and enshrine me. A bushi bows his head to the son of a commoner and makes this request.'

He stood pompously, bracing himself. From this, Kiraku took Uichi and two or three other trainees to investigate a certain temple in Nishimachi, and went to the paper-scrap dealer Nishio's house to ask about it. Although the name Misao was not clearly known, the fact called Bōbōinden Bōbō Koji was precisely correct. Believing without a doubt that it was indeed Misao's spirit, he boldly went to look at the cemetery in Koshōra. However, the condition of the entire cemetery was, just as the wandering spirit had said, without a single inch of error; moss had accumulated on the third gravestone on the right, and though it was not entirely clear, characters that looked similar were appearing. Waiting for nearly an hour, wondering whether the stone pagoda would move now or move now, yet it remained completely motionless, not budging an inch. Thinking it strange—making a fuss over what was not even strange—he closed his eyes and investigated with his spiritual vision, whereupon he noticed a large old tanuki glaring with its eyes wide open at the back of the stone pagoda.

"You damned old tanuki, making a fool of humans! I'll go back now and make the sahō of the Tada zither rigorous!"

Thinking thus, he strode briskly back to the training hall in Anō. By that time, the sun had already set. In the training hall, a dim lamp was lit, and on the other side, Tada and Ishida sat stiffly and gravely in wait. The moment they saw his figure,

"Hey, Ueda-dono, a great, great task! Well done indeed on your investigation. What this side says cannot possibly be mistaken, so quickly enshrine the spirit of so-and-so. Hey, Motoichi-dono, and the rest of you, you've gone through great trouble, ah-ha-ha-ha!"

He let out a heroic laugh, shaking his shoulders in unison.

Kiraku: 'Hey, you old tanuki possessing Tada, you petty follower of tanuki dabbling in trivialities, you've made a fool of this sahō master, haven't you! Now, I won't let you off. From here on, I will cast spiritual bindings and tie you down, so prepare yourself.'

The possessing spirit of Tada shook its shoulders even more broadly, opened its mouth wide,

"Ah-ha-ha-ha, hi-hi-hi-hi!"

And laughing thus, it collapsed sideways with a thud. Kiraku crossed his hands and wholeheartedly applied spiritual bindings with murmurs of concentration. Along with Ishida's cry of exertion, he collapsed with a thud. In exchange, Tada's physical body rose sharply, and raising and lowering his twenty-kan body by about two shaku like pounding mochi with the rhythmic sounds of *don-don-don*, he flew around the tatami room. Kiraku became desperate,

Kiraku: 'I humbly beg you to calm down.'

He lowered his head and approached gently. The possessing spirit opened its mouth wide,

"Ah-ha-ha-ha! I was asked by Komatsubayashi-sama to make you fellows undergo sahō training. The truth of the matter is Matsuoka. You were so easily fooled! When you saw the tanuki at the back of the stone pagoda, you had quite a strange look on your face! Hotsu-ho-ho-ho, ah-ha-ha-ha!"

And holding its stomach, it laughed convulsively again.

Abruptly, the indoor air began to smell like a dead body. ...Ah, it stinks, it stinks, everyone pinched their noses. Just as the chanting of a Buddhist priest could be heard from nowhere, pilgrim songs were heard in an annoying voice, and the funeral procession noises of *chindon* and *jaburin* entered their ears. Thinking this fellow was suspicious, they crossed their hands, closed their eyes, and illuminated their spiritual vision. Countless phantoms, each holding a sedge hat and a staff, following behind a beggar priest, caught their eye as they crawled in through the narrow holes of the shōji screen. And the face of the phantom appearing right at the very front was the exact spitting image of the face of the neighbor girl named Omon. Before he could even realize it,

Kiraku: 'Hey, Omon-san!'

He shouted. Yet, with no answer at all, phantoms came piling up heavily into the room, until numbering in the hundreds they piled upon one another, turning and glaring this way. The two spirit-possessed women, Saitō Takako and Iwamori Tokuko, shouting that it was terrifying, clung to Kiraku's body and trembled as they wept.

An indescribable, unpleasant odor filled the room, and the light of the lamp naturally grew faint until the whole surrounding area became dim. Then, he tried lighting four or five candles, but every single one of them had its flame dwindle and go out. Having no other choice, he purified his body with the clear water of the narrow ditch on the east side, steadied his core, and desperately began to recite the Amatsu Norito. The shadow of the suspicious phantoms decreased by one, then by two, and finally fled away completely through the original narrow, torn hole of the shōji screen. Just as this was happening, an old woman named Oshi, the mother of Omon, arrived in a great flurry...

と叫んだ。されど何の答もなしに座敷へドカドカと亡者が重なり来り、遂には何百とも知れず重なり合うて、こちらを向いて睨んでゐる。斎藤高子、岩森徳子の二人の神憑はコワイコワイと叫び乍ら、喜楽の体に喰ひついて震ひ泣いてゐる。

原文（近代文語）

お初『モシモシ喜楽サン、最前から俄にお紋が病気になつて囈言許りいつてゐます、そして喜楽サンどうぞこらへて下さいと、幾度となく繰返して居りますから、どうぞ、どんな悪い事をしたか知らぬが、まだ年の行かぬ子供の事だから、カニーしてやつて下され。大変な熱で、臭うて側へもよりつけませぬ』

と言ひ乍ら、泣いて居る。喜楽はこれを聞くより隣のお紋サンの家に行き見れば、お初婆アサンの云つた通り、熱臭く不快な臭が漂ひ娘はウンウンと唸つて居る。丁度元市の修業場で嗅いだ臭とソツクリであつた。そこで又もや天津祝詞を声高らかに奏上し、鎮魂を施せば、お紋サンは夢中になつて、

『のきます　のきます』

と云ひ乍ら、寝所から立上り、二足三足門口の方へ歩き出し、バタリと其場に倒れて了つた。それと同時に病気はスツカリ治つて了つたのである。
