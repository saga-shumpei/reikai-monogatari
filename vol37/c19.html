Chapter 19: 逆襲 [1031]

逆襲〔1031〕

§ 1

Futo, when I looked at the advertisement column of the Hinodeshinbun that had been delivered, a fine advertisement saying they were recruiting saushi actors appeared. As I was gazing intently at that advertisement, Tada Koto leaped up with a bound and became possessed by a spirit,

Tada: 'I am Komatsubayashinomikoto, a kenzoku of Otokoyama. Now, exactly as in that advertisement, a troupe of saushi theatricals called Seigidan is being recruited through the shigumi of Shinkai. From now on, you shall apply the reijiyutsu you have labored to learn until now and become a theatrical yakushiya. Since Kami will shiyugo you and bring about any miraculous thing whatsoever, I shall make you a famous actor superior to Kawakami Otojirō. How is it, do you consent to what Kami says, or do you say nay, give me your immediate reply.'

Saying this, he smiled cheerfully and questioned me with compulsion. Kiraku, having been at the point when he had been brooding and thinking,

"I too would like to try becoming a saushi actor for once,"

since it was right at that time, he rejoiced without a moment's hesitation,

Kiraku: 'Yes, if Kami-sama even will grant permission, I will become a saushi actor.'

He answered promptly. Thereupon, the possessed spirit calling himself Komatsubayashi made a cheerful face and with a voice softened even in its tone,

"As expected of a saniwa who sees far ahead and understands the future. Come, if you dilly-dally and the applicants fill up, it will be no good, so set out this very night right away. And go with about fifteen or sixteen yen estimated and prepared."

He commanded thus.

Gathering together money that was hardly there, somehow managing to raise fifteen yen, I rode a boat from the beach of Hozu, descended the valley to arrive at Saga, and then sought out an inn at Takeyamachō Tominokōji. A man calling himself the leader of Seigidan, whose name I have forgotten, guided me right away to the second floor and demanded ten yen as an admission fee. Immediately throwing down the ten yen and finishing various procedures, I then searched for a cheap inn, and day by day practiced the forms of jiujitsu and memorized lines, with twelve or thirteen men doing the same. If one dilly-dallies, the five-yen money runs out completely. Furthermore, a large boil formed on my buttocks so that I could not budge an inch, aching and unbearable.

"With things like this, it is not a matter for joking about theater. I want to somehow return to my own home, but I cannot walk away, and there is no jinrikisha fare; might as well go to my younger brother Seiichi's place where he is serving near Kikyōtei, and have him transport me there by jinrikisha and rely on his care, ah."

As I was brooding thus, from deep within my belly, once again a spirit-ball surged up to my throat. And then,

"Aha-ha-ha-ha,"

he laughed as if it were amusing.

"Though the swelling on my foot hurts and nothing is right anywhere, what sort of magatsuhi is the fellow who laughs so amusingly from deep within his belly,"

I shouted back. From deep within his belly, with a voice that sounded exceedingly amused and gleeful,

"Ihi-hi-hi-hi,"

he continued laughing continuously for about ten minutes. And then,

"I am Matsuoka; since you looked at the newspaper advertisement and seemed to want to become an actor, I teased you just now for the sake of reform. Truly, you are the greatest fool in Japan, oho-ha-ha-ha,"

he laughed aloud. Kiraku, having his advance and retreat both blocked, could do nothing at all; he paid three yen and sixty sen for three days' worth of inn rent, and attempting to return to Tamba, set out stepping out from the front door of the inn. Before he knew it, the large boil on his buttocks had healed as if it had told a lie. From that very moment on, the desire to become a saushi actor completely vanished, and it became a mindset of devoting oneself single-mindedly and undistractedly to the divine business of Shinkai.

A certain son of a weaver named Saitō of the same Anō is suffering from pulmonary tuberculosis and is troubled with the medicines having no effect, so if you go there and help him, how would it be... an old woman named Oiyo appeared and urged him persistently, so Kiraku also,

"If I heal the illness of that person's son, Keisan, the villagers too will probably come to their senses a bit. They will probably come to have faith,"

thinking thus, from early in the morning he visited that house dressed in haori and hakama,

Kiraku: 'This is the one place for patience. If I do not clear up the misunderstandings of such a noisy woman, it will not be good for the future.'

Thinking thus, he various ways expounded and preached the path of the gods, but she stopped her ears entirely and refused to listen. Oetsu-san let out a half-crying voice,

Oetsu: 'Eh, eh, how annoying. No matter how sweetly the rakugo storyteller Kiraku-san speaks, facts are better than argument, and presently my own flesh-and-blood Jiroamatsu-san is the witness... Oh, how filthy, please leave quickly. Hey, O-Tome, bring some salt...'

Calling even the maidservant by name, she tried to drive him away by sprinkling salt on him. Having no other choice, he trudged back toward his own home.

When he came back as far as the foot of Obatabashi Bridge, he met Jiroamatsu-san coming out with a pale face. Jiroamatsu wore an unusual gentleness on his face,

Jiroamatsu: 'I say, Ueda-sensei, please do me a favor for a moment. From two or three days ago, a fox has possessed my Aguri (the name of my only daughter) and she talks deliriously, goes to the privy and scoops out what comes from her behind with her hands, makes round dumplings to offer to the family Buddhist altar, dances with strange gestures, and after leaping about, she sleeps completely wrapped up in her futon. Now I won\'t oppose you anymore, so please forgive me. Because I opposed you too much, you got angry and... well... you caused something to possess her, didn\'t you? From now on I will definitely do as you say, so please take the-what-you-call-it away. Now, Kiraku-sensei, I beg of you.'

Thinking it would look bad if people heard him, he looked around here and there restlessly, but Matsu-san chattered on without a care about his daughter\'s illness. Having no other choice, Kiraku said,

Kiraku: 'At any rate, let us go and see.'

次郎松『宅の娘に喜楽がド狐を憑けて苦しめよつた。到頭化けが現はれて俺に責め付けられ、仕様事なしに骨折つて憑けた狐をおひ出して連れて去によつた。俺は親類で居つて云ふのだから嘘ぢやない。みな用心しなされや』

と其処等中を触れ歩いた。喜楽こそ宜い面の皮である。
