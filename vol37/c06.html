手料理〔1018〕

§ 1

As Kiraku's figure had not been seen since the night of February 9th from the Kirakutei in front of the village jinjiya, his mother and kiyōdai thought nothing of it, supposing that he had gone off somewhere to amuse himself with a woman to dispel his gloom, or perhaps had gone around to Kameoka to indulge in sanzai. When two days and then three days passed and he did not return, it gradually became a great commotion among the usual Jiromatsu, his western neighbor the goke Omasa, and the neighbors within the kabūchi.

A man named Chiyōkichi made a pilgrimage to the place beneath the Inari shrine at Gokenmachi in Kameoka, kept by Nakai Denkyō of the Himorogikyōin, and asked for an oracle from the Inari Daimiyōjin. Sensei Denkyō wore white robes (biyakui), white hakama (shirobakama), and an eboshi, and solemnly chanted the Tenshin-hōroku, the Rokkon Shōjō no Harae, the Heart Sutra, and others in a syncretic Shinto-Buddhist manner. After a short while, the divine spirit descended abruptly,

Beware of the waters; if you do not search for him soon, his life will be in danger. This man shows signs of madness.

Having obtained this divine oracle, he returned in a panic and searched around ponds, wells, rivers, and the like, but found not the slightest clue.

The goke Omasa-san, worrying because it was a matter concerning the kabūchi, went to the Miyagawa Myōrei Church in Miyamae village and asked for a divine pronouncement. According to the divine pronouncement of a kyōdōshoku named Nishida Seiki,

He has eloped far off toward the east with a woman with whom he made promises. However, a postcard will arrive within a week, so be at ease.

There was also such a comical divine pronouncement. The goke Omasa-san then had her fortune told by an old woman called the Jizō of Tatsue, who enshrined the Kōbō Daishi of Shinochaya Shinden,

This man has met with a divine abduction. Since he has been bewitched by an evil tengu, there is no danger to his life, but like an outrageous arch-fool or having gone mad, he will surely return after a week, so be at ease.

It is said that this was the oracle.

Jiromatsu-san went to a fortune-teller in Kameoka to have a judgment made,

He has gone out carrying about one hundred yen of the proceeds from the stock farm, but this fellow intends to go abroad. Harboring unexpected ambitions, he intends to cross from Korea to Manchuria and join a band of mounted bandits, so apply for protection as quickly as possible and prevent him from crossing abroad.

It is said that this was an absurd judgment.

People's rumors were varied: some were saying that since it was just before the seasonal settlement, he had done a midnight flight because he was troubled by payments, or that his spending of money had been far too extravagant...

He eloped with a certain woman. No, he was bewitched by a tengu, he went mad, he was deceived by foxes and badgers and taken deep into the mountains. Kawachiya's Kankichi or Wakanishi heartlessly abandoned his parents and ran off somewhere; he is a thoroughly unfilial fellow, an arch-fool, an incomprehensible fellow, a coward... and so forth, as the blossoms of discussion bloomed in every direction.

On a sheet of rolled paper left on Kiraku's desk, the following song was recorded:

I am a bird soaring through the sky,

I am a bird soaring through the sky,

Riding upon clouds far and high,

While gazing down at the people of this lower world,

Imprisoned in various joys and sorrows,

Making gestures and moving their feet,

I watch them while forgetting myself.

Truly, how fascinating is this human world!

Yet, would that I might not ride too far on the excitement,

And fall down upon the earth.

O Divine God, be ever with me.

It was written in brush and ink. No one knew what it meant.

Before noon on the seventh day, the fifteenth of Niguwatsu, the man in question, Kiraku, returned to the blind alley of Miyagakiuchi. The joy of his family goes without saying, and the people of the kabūchi and the neighborhood, hearing that he had returned, came crowding in one after another. Treating him as a rarity, as if a dead person had returned from the underworld,

Hey, Kiraku-san, where on earth have you been, and what have you been doing? The worry during your absence was beyond ordinary.

They released arrows of questions to the point of annoyance. To respond to them one by one would be endless. Feeling somewhat ashamed himself,

Then, one after another, four or five shinsetsuya came and offered various chiukoku and iken, saying this and that. Valuing the shinchiyoku, I simply listened in silence. Moreover, no matter how much I tried to make excuses or explained that I had gone on the business of Kami, it was useless. Suddenly, the worms in my belly protested the emptiness. I took out a tray myself and wolfed down two bowls of cold barley rice in an instant. It felt truly superior to the delicacies of land and sea.

Sleepiness attacked with the force of a breached embankment... just as the folk song lyric says—when sleepy, even fifty packloads of gold to a horse are worthless—letting go of all attachment to everything, I stretched out flat right in the middle of the torn tatami of the dark room, and for a while afterward, deep in a dreamless sleep once more, I wandered through heaven. The joy during that time was a state unlike any before or since.

Around two o'clock in the afternoon of the sixteenth, my eyes finally began to open. By the pillow, four or five men and women had come to visit as before, chatting about various rumors while tending to me. When I opened my eyes, I felt extremely awkward. Suddenly, I became frantic and made a pilgrimage to the Obata Shrine of my birth deity, and with those very feet, walking along the mountain paths, I went to pull up a young pine by the roots and offer it at my father's grave.

Following behind me, half-hidden and half-seen, was a thirty-year-old man named Shigekichi Hatta from the southern neighbor. Around the time the sun had completely sunk into the western mountains, dragging heavy feet and wearing a look of anxiety, I returned to the humble house. Jiromatsu-san and the widow Omasa crowded around annoyingly, trying to cross-examine me about various things, but I shook my head left and right and answered nothing at all.

From the early morning of the seventeenth, my body began to feel more and more strange. As if placed under hypnosis, stiffness began from my limbs, and then my mouth and tongue also stiffened and stopped moving. I could no longer utter a single word nor move my body even an inch, becoming like a living corpse. However, only my ears could hear the voices of the people very well. My ears had become so sharp that I could even hear the ticking of the hands of a pocket watch. Family members and relatives gathered around, patting and rubbing me in various ways, and burning moxibustion on me.

'Three days since today, he's slept soundly like a shark, it looks like he was quite exhausted. It's best to let him sleep until he wakes up naturally...'

That the consultation of the gathering had concluded was clearly understood by my ears. Even after four days passed, my body wouldn't budge an inch, and I did not wake from sleep. The family members and relatives were suddenly enveloped in clouds of suspicion and grew flustered all at once. ...'It's no good anymore, it's time for worship, we must make preparations...'

The words that Matsu-san had spoken spread in the blink of an eye, building a mountain of visiting guests. Whose requested visitor was it—the voice of the doctor could be heard. 二十三日の早朝、京都の誓願寺の祈願僧が尋ねて来た。溺るる者はわらしべ一本にもたよらうとする諺の如く、何でもかでも助けてやらうと云ふ者さへあらば、無暗矢鱈に引張込んで来る。此祈祷僧は皺枯れた声で『南無妙法蓮華経』と幾回もくり返し次に心経を二三回許り唱へ乍ら、一人で拍子木を叩く、太鼓をうつ、まだ其間に鐘を叩く、汗みどろになつて勤行する、其熱心さ実に感謝に価すると思うた。併し自分の耳がつんぼになり相であつた。これ程喧しう騒がねば聞えぬとは、余程耳の遠い仏さまだなア……と心の中で可笑しくて堪らなかつた。

拍子木打ち太鼓鐘叩き経を読む 法華坊主の芸の多さよ

此坊サン次第々々に声がかすれ出し、御幣を手に持ち、又もや「高天原」に「六根清浄」の祓を上げる。俄に彼の身体はドスーンドスーンと上下に震動し、稲荷下げのやうな事を始め出した。そして狭い部屋中をグルリグルリところげまはり『ウンウン』と言ひ乍ら座に直り大声を張り上げて、

'I am the Shiyōichii Tenko Tsunetomiinaridaimiyōjin who guards the Shintaki of Meukenyama; if you have any matter of inquiry, draw near and make your petition.'

Such was the divine oracle. All those assembled bowed low to the ground, holding their breath in awe. Jiromatsu-san changed his countenance, placed both hands upon the floor, and said,

'We humbly inquire of the gracious Tsunetomiinaidaimiyōjin, whose doing is this thing, pray let us know.'

Tsunetomi: 'Thirty years ago from now, there was a man called Yosaburō within this house's lineage. A tanuki possessed that man. The people of this house and others from the neighborhood came to this household, saying to that Yosaburō that botamochi had been made, and told him to eat, drawing him here. Yosaburo said, "To be invited to eat botamochi is a blessed thing..." while stretching out his hand with a jerk. Old Mother Mutsuba in the neighborhood showed Yosaburō that since an old tanuki was attached to him, she would not give him botamochi until after driving this fellow out, and while glaring at Yosaburō, she said it was tanuki subjugation, mixed red pepper into green pine needles, smoked him through the nose, and destroyed even Yosaburō's physical body. In order to reap revenge for that, the vengeful spirit of Yosaburo has used the tanuki that was attached to itself as an advance scout, bewildering the son of this place, building a nest within his belly, and tormenting him. However, by the divine power of this Tsunetomiidaimiyōjin, the hateful enemy shall be made to disperse instantly, therefore be deeply grateful and keep your faith. After a short while, both the dead spirit of Yosaburō and the old tanuki shall surrender completely. Un, un.'

So saying, he seemed to have returned to his senses. How laughable it was to me who had been listening to this. The assembly believed this oracle as a lifeline, was overcome with tears of gratitude, and even the sound of sniffing noses could be heard. However, even after one hour passed, and even after half a day passed, the dead spirit did not disperse, nor did the old tanuki appear to leave, and Kiraku's physical state continued as before in a state of rigidity. The prayer priest seemed to feel his backside itch, and with a face as if going to the privy, before anyone knew it, he had disappeared, leaving the gift offerings just as they were. Toward the hour of dusk, the usual Jiromatsu came again.

'Ah, as expected, that shit priest was also a tailless tanuki. At last he fled before his tail could be seen. The prayer of the priest who had been spouting grandly, and the divine oracle of Tsunetomi Inari, both brayed countless lies that could not be relied upon. Rather than that, home cooking is the best. Above first of all, that this fellow went to the grave is suspicious. It is certain that a mud-tanuki is attached to him, the same fellow that possessed Yosaburo of old. Even if you smoke him with mere green pine needles, since this fellow has experienced much and is one whose hair has become four-pronged, he will not easily meet his demise. If you mix shichimi, red pepper, and sanshō, and smoke him with green pine needles, he will likely meet his demise. Kiraku himself died two or three days ago. His body is merely warm from the tanuki's breath. ... Hey you tanuki-san, it is no longer any use, are you prepared, go away in an appropriate manner.'

So saying, he lifted an extremely disrespectful foot and kicked my head or twisted my nose. It appeared that preparations of green pine needles and red pepper had been made, and Jiromatsu, becoming triumphant, said,

'Hey tanuki-san, from now on it is a treat of shichimi, red pepper, sanshō grains, and pine needle smoking. Come, ascend in floods without reserve.'

Thus superstitious people had gathered and were performing preparatory acts for murder. Possessing sharp hearing, I thought that now things had come to this, there was nowhere else to go, and I tried to rise by throwing the strength of my whole body into it, but it would not budge an inch, nor could I speak. Jiromatsu lit a fire in a brazier with a chipped rim, burned red pepper and green pine needles, and at the very instant he tried to fan them toward the tip of my nose with a round fan, my mother,

'Matsu-san... just a moment.'

lamented something above my head. And at that very instant when the tears that fell from my mother's eyes moistened my face, from somewhere, a single golden rope descended from above. The moment I thought I had grasped it quickly, wondrously, my body became able to move freely at will. Everyone was stricken with tears of joy. I too was filled with a joy as though I had been resurrected.

『われこそは妙見山の新滝に守護いたす、正一位天狐恒富稲荷大明神なり、伺ひの筋あらば近うよつて願へツ』

との御託宣であつた。一座の者は低頭平身、息をこらして畏まつてゐる。次郎松サンは容を改め両手をついて、

『有難き恒富大明神さまに御伺ひ致しますが、一体これは何者の仕業で御座いますか、どうぞ御知らせを願ひます』

恒富『これは今より三十年前、此家の株内に与三郎といふ男があつたであらう。其男に狸が憑いた。此家の者、其外近所の者が当家によつて来て、其与三郎に牡丹餅が出来たから食てくれと言つて、ここへ引よせた。与三は牡丹餅をよんでやらうとは有難い……といひ乍ら手をニユツとつき出した。近所のお睦婆アが、与三には古狸がついて居るから、此奴を追出した後でなくては牡丹餅はやらぬと、与三に見せつけておき乍ら、狸退治だと云つて、青松葉に唐芥子をまぜて、鼻からくすべ、与三の肉体まで亡くして了つた。其恨をはらすが為に、与三の怨霊が、自分についてゐた狸をお先に使つて、ここの息子をたぶらかし、腹の中に巣をくんで悩めて居るのだ。併し乍ら此恒富大明神の神力に依つて、怨敵忽ち退散さす程に有難く思つて信心致せ。一時の後には与三の死霊も古狸もサツパリ降服するぞよ。ウンウン』

と言つて正気に返つて了つた様子である。これを聞いて居つた自分の可笑しさ。一座は此託宣を命の綱と信じ、有難涙にかきくれて、鼻を啜る声さへ聞えて居る。併し乍ら、一時間たつても、半日経つても、死霊も退散せねば、古狸も去なぬと見えて、喜楽の体は依然として強直状態を続けてゐる。祈祷坊主は尻こそばゆくなつたと見え、雪隠へ行くやうな顔して、何時の間にか、礼物を貰つた儘姿をかくしたやうな按配であつた。黄昏時になつて、又例の次郎松がやつて来た。

『あゝヤツパリあの糞坊主も、尾のない狸だつた。とうとう尻尾をみられぬ先に逃げよつたなア。偉相に吐して居つた坊主の御祈祷も、恒富稲荷の御託宣も、当にならぬ嘘八百をコキ並べよつた。それよりも手料理に限る。第一此奴が墓へ参りよつたのがウサンぢや。キツとドブ狸がついてゐるにきまつて居る、昔の与三に憑いて居つた奴だろ。青松葉位でくすべた所で、此奴は余程劫を経て、毛が四つ股になつてる奴ぢやから、中々往生は致すまい。七味や唐辛や山椒をまぜて、青松葉でくすべたら往生するだろ。本人の喜楽は二三日前に死んで了うてゐるのだ。只狸の息で体がぬくい丈だ。……オイコラ狸サン、モウ駄目だぞ、覚悟はよいか、いいかげんに去にさらせ』

といひ乍ら、失敬千万な足をあげて、自分の頭を蹴つたり、鼻を捻ぢたりしてゐる。青松葉や唐辛の用意が出来たと見え、次郎松は得意になつて、

『オイたぬサン、これから七味や唐辛山椒粒に松葉くすべの御馳走だ。サ、ドツトと遠慮なしに上つてくれ』

と迷信家が寄つて殺人の準備行為をやつて居る。耳のよく聞える自分は、モウ斯うなつては何所でない、全身の力をこめて起上らうとしたが、ビクともしない、口も利かない。次郎松はふちの欠けた火鉢に火をおこし、唐辛と青松葉をくべて、団扇で鼻の先へ扇ぎこまうとしてる一刹那、母が、

『松サン……一寸』

と何か頭の先で歎いてゐられる。そして母の目からおちた涙が、自分の顔をうるほはした其一刹那、どこともなく、上の方から一筋の金色の綱が下つて来た。それを手早く握りしめたと思ふ途端に、不思議にも自分の体は自由自在に活動することが出来るやうになつた。一同は歓喜の涙にうたれてゐる。自分も復活したやうな喜びに充たされて居た。
