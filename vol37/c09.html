Chapter 9: Ox の糞 [1021]

牛の糞〔1021〕

§ 1

Saitō Motoichi grew disgusted with the way Ōshimotengu's takusen completely missed the mark, refused to lend his training ground any longer, and from then on not only ceased to look toward him, but also began to revile Kiraku, whom he had for a while revered as 'Daisensei', calling him a 'Dobudanuki, dobudanuki, notengu, dokichigahi'. Furthermore, he pulled his wife's younger sister Chinko's Shizuko away from the Nakamura training ground and married her off to a man named Kumakichi, a gambling bakuchiuchi who practiced inarisa in Sonobe. As for thirteen-year-old Takako, because her kamugaka was amusing, she was undergoing training within Tada Kame's household in Nakamura. Uichi, sneaking past his grandfather's watchful eyes, gradually began frequenting Kiraku's residence. And he was somewhat unsteadily studying the way of the gods.

It was on the night of the fifth day since returning in failure from the deep mountains. Once again, Ōshimotengu-san broke five days of silence, churned upward from deep within the belly, reached the throat, and began to roar. When Kiraku was troubled, thinking, 'Oh, not again,' in a voice as loud as thunder,

Ōshimotengu: 'I am Ōshimotengu, a kenzoku of Sumiyoshi. By the command of Komatsubara, a kenzoku of Otokoyama, I have appeared here once more, and since I have something to declare to you, you had better listen well. It would be just as well for Uichi to withdraw from this seat for a while.'

Uichi assumed the air of a saniwa, and

Uichi: 'Hey, Ōshimotengu-san, do not make such a fool of people too much. To say that gold is buried in the deep mountains—how could you possibly utter such wild nonsense? From now on, I will not listen to a single word of what you say anymore... Hey Kiraku, you must pull yourself together properly or you will be finished. Is it not from your own mouth that you speak? If you do not watch yourself thoroughly, you will end up a kichigahi... Hey Ōshimotengu, do you still say there are no two words to what a god says? Spouting such a ridiculous boast of eight hundred thousand yen, you made fools of us father and son.'

Ōshimotengu: 'Whether eight hundred thousand yen or eight million yen, I will grant it according to your heart. Because your reformation is not yet complete, I cannot tell you the truth. Once your greed for gold departs, I will grant you as much gold as you wish.'

Uichi: 'It is because there is a need for money that one comes to desire it. No matter who it is, there is no one who desires things they do not need; if it is money I do not want, I do not need it. Since it is equivalent to stone and tile, where on earth is there a malicious god who says he will give money to a fellow who does not desire money, and will not give it to a fellow who desires it? Think about it a little. The saniwa is watching out.'

Ōshimotengu: 'In that case, from now on the gods will also reform and grant it precisely to the fellows who desire it.'

Uichi: 'Yes, I personally have no particular need for it, but since our grandfather completely lost our ancestral property on the stock market, he is attacked in various ways by our relatives, and he laments, thinking it regrettable that people say of him that that adopted son is no good at all and is a wicked fellow. Since we do not ask for anything overly greedy, please grant us money up to the point of returning to our former financial standing. If you do that, Grandfather will also gladly practice faith. Lately he says that Ōshimotengu-san cheated Kiraku and took him in, and he is furious. That is why I too, secretly from Grandfather, am studying hard so that I may be allowed to perform the divine business of the gods like this.'

Ōshimotengu: 'You are a praiseworthy fellow unbefitting your parents; if you have that much resolve, that is splendid. In that case, I will truly let you know the whereabouts of the gold, so do not doubt it in the least. If you raise such doubts as thinking that since you were deceived before, this time too it must be a lie, who knows, perhaps a purse filled with gold and silver will turn into bull dung once again. Is that understood!'

Uichi: 'It is not that I am doubting the words of the god from the very beginning, but when I am deceived by the gods like the other day, my heart naturally becomes twisted, wondering if I am going to be deceived once again, and doubts rise up within me just a little bit.'

Nodding with a 'Yes, yes,' Uichi hastily departed from his thatched home. Kiraku followed behind him, tracing the footpath through the paddies, seeing Amekawa Village to the right, crossing Deiyama, and reaching the grove of the Mitama Shrine in Kamisaeki. They sat down on the stump of a cedar tree in the grove, bathing their bodies in the hazy spring breeze of the night, and stubbornly enduring their sleepiness as they waited for the time to arrive.

At last, the clock at the shrine office struck eleven.

'Ah, it's already eleven, let's go quickly,'

Uichi said, taking the lead. Kiraku trotted right after him, climbing the steep Ashinonoyama Pass for about seven or eight cho. There was a pass teahouse called Yamada-ya. Since it was still early, thinking they would pause for just a moment and go on, he peeked inside through the gap in the door, and the young men of the village, hailing from no further than five or six houses away, were still hanging around playing. ...This is rather inconvenient instead... Saying this, they proceeded into the pine grove on the right side of the pass, and while waiting for a while for the time to arrive, both of them fell fast asleep.

The one whose eyes snapped open first was Uichi.

Uichi: 'Hey Kiraku, won't you wake up quickly? When I just peeked toward the road a moment ago, exactly as the god said, a single black man passed by carrying something like a purse. Then two men went along about a cho behind him. What the god says truly never fails. Right now is just the exact moment his purse is being snatched. If we go too quickly, we'll be mistaken for thieves and scolded by the tengu, which would be terrible, so let's take our time and go.'

He whispered in a small voice. Deep inside Kiraku's heart, he could not believe it even up to eighty percent; no matter what, it felt like a lie. Yet, to the extent of about twenty percent, he somehow felt as though he were tied to a thread of hope.

So the two descended from the grove to the highway, and when they walked down the pass for about two cho, there was a sharp bend. Looking closely with sharp eyes to ensure there was no mistake, something resembling a purse lay blackly on the ground. When the two reached out and touched that black object counting one, two, three, what they thought was a purse turned out to be a mound of cow dung.

The two felt it was utterly foolish, and without saying anything to each other, thinking for sure that there must still be something else fallen outside, they rubbed their soiled hands onto the grass nearby to wipe them clean, and searched about the grass with a rustling, crunching sound. This was a usual resting place for ox-carts, and because oxen were tethered to the roadside meadow, there was cow dung everywhere. ...Come to think of it, this isn't it... Descending about another cho, they searched the whole area, but not a single thing had fallen.
