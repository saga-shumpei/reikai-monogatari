Chapter 2: 葱節 [1014]

葱節〔1014〕

§ 1

To the west is Hangoku, to the east is Atago, to the south is Meuken, and to the north is Taishiyaku; drawing a folding screen of mountains around, in the midst of Anao, cattle are kept.

The Nodokubiguchi of the Sanindau, which has Aogakiyama surrounding it on all four sides, is the birthplace of Zuigetsu Oni, located near Kameoka in Tanba. Nearly twenty-seven years had passed like a dream since he first cried out in the Shizugafuseya, and on the eighth day of Kisaragi in the thirty-first year of Meiji, when he reached his twenty-eighth year, the half-moon shone brightly in the Tenkū, and on the Chijiyō, the fragrance of plum blossoms, carried by the cold wind, was elegant, and people's hearts were also floating in a mood of welcoming spring in a somewhat brilliant manner.

At that time, Zuigetsu made it his only pleasure to chant Jōruri during his leisure time from business. He had been taught the melodies by a blind master named Azumadayū who had come down from the land of Naniwa, and although he had no shamisen, he was taught the rhythm by tapping.

This evening, seven or eight of his Jōruri practice friends, intending to hold a recital, hired a female Gidayū at the house of an acquaintance named Oishi, and while making her play the shamisen, they were boastfully shouting the Negibushi. As it is said that one is fond of what one is bad at, Zuigetsu served as the first to clear the way, and after finishing the act of Kagamiyama Matasuke-yakata while drenched in sweat, and when the Jōruri of two or three other amateur enthusiasts, with voices like blowing through bamboo pipes and lacking rhythm, ceased, once again, he was dressed in something stiff and angular like a March diamond-shaped rice cake cut in two, and was chanting while beating a broken fan. At that time, it was the climax of the tenth act of the Taikōki, where Mitsuhide says, 'Appearing from this side of the evening glory trellis...' It was a great success, with young and old, men and women, not only in the garden but even standing at the gate to listen, as the small farmhouse was full.

At that time, a man named Wakanishiki, who had been performing shrine sumo, led a group of five, including the chivalrous men Koushi, Tomekō, Yosakō, and Moichi, who suddenly climbed onto the stage and, without allowing any argument, carried Zuigetsu away to a nearby mulberry field and began a great chaotic brawl of hitting, kicking, and punching.

A fellow Jōruri friend and neighbor named Usokatsu, who was also a chivalrous man, led two or three of his subordinates, each holding a piece of firewood about two shaku long, and jumped into the midst of the five to begin a fight. The quarrel moved to another place before anyone knew it, and they fled toward the southeast while shouting and scattering. Usokatsu's group chased after them.

After that, two or three friends came, helped Zuigetsu, and took him back to his own establishment called the Seinyūkan, a dairy farm. As a result of being hit on the head five or six times with firewood, his head felt heavy for no reason, began to throb, and his ears began to hear a sound like a ringing alarm bell. Sometimes, wondering if it was a fire alarm, he would lift his injured body, open the door, and look outside.

The business of Seinyūkan was to milk cows and sell the milk to nearby villages. The milk deliverymen came from before dawn, waiting for the milk to be measured and handed over. Zuigetsu, with his headache and dizziness, was in no state to deal with milking. The twenty-odd pasture cows began to groan in unison, complaining of hunger or making sad sounds because their udders were full. When that sound echoed in his head, he felt as if his head were splitting even more. Even so, he did not think to pray to the gods, nor did he think to call a doctor or apply or drink medicine. All that was going back and forth in his mind was that he had completely forgotten the business he had valued until now, and he held onto only one thread of hope: that he must recover quickly and pay back the group of Wakanishiki with a great fight. The doors at the front and the back were locked. Therefore, the deliverymen could not enter, and having no choice, they ran to his mother's house within the shrine grounds,

Deliveryman: 'For some reason, the front door is locked; please come for a moment.'

其時宮相撲をとつて居た若錦と云ふ男を先頭に、侠客の小牛、留公、与三公、茂一の五人連れ、矢庭に演壇に上り、有無を云はせず瑞月を担いで附近の桑畑の中へ連れ行き、打つ、蹴る、殴るの大乱痴気騒ぎを始めた。

浄瑠璃友達で隣家の嘘勝と云ふデモ侠客が二三人の手下を引き連れ、二尺許りの割木を各自に持つて五人の仲に飛び込み格闘を始めた。喧嘩は何時の間にか一方へ転宅して了ひ、バラバラバラと喚きつつ東南の方へ逃げて行く。嘘勝の一隊は後を追つかける。

其後へ二三の友人がやつて来て、瑞月を助けて牧畜場の精乳館と云ふ自分の館へ連れて帰つて呉れた。ひどく頭部を五つ六つ割木で殴られた結果、何とはなしに頭が重たくなり、うづき出し、耳はジヤンジヤンと早鐘をつく様に聞えて来た。時々火事の警鐘ではないかと、負傷した身体を擡げて戸を開き外を眺めた事もあつた。

精乳館は牛乳を搾り附近の村落に販売するのが営業であつた。牛乳配達人は未明からやつて来て搾乳の量り渡しを待つて居る。瑞月は頭痛み目晦めき、搾乳どころの騒ぎではない。二十数頭の牧牛は空腹を訴へたり、乳の張り切る為め悲し相な声を出して一斉に呻り出した。其声が頭に響くと一層頭が割れる様な気分がする。それでも神様を祈らうとも思はねば、医者を呼び、薬を付け様とも飲まうとも思はない。只自分の心裡に往復して居るのは、今迄大切に思ふて居た営業はスツカリ忘れて了ひ、若錦一派の奴に対し、早く本復して仕返しの大喧嘩をやつてやらねばならぬと、そればかりを一縷の望みの綱として居た。門口の戸も裏口の戸も錠が卸してある。それ故配達人は這入る事も出来ぬ、已むを得ず宮垣内の母の宅へ走り、

『何故か門口が締つて居る、一寸来て下さい』

§ 2

So saying, he went to call his mother. Since Murakami so-and-so of the opposing party was about to come around before long, he thought it would not look very good to be seen injured from his fight the previous night; giving in to a reluctance to admit defeat, he tied his head up with a tenuguhi, kept his eyes covered, and—knowing the familiar road well—hid himself in a small, humble hut before the local shrine called Kirakutei, which he had once borrowed, pulling the yagu over his head and lying down while holding his breath.

After a while, calling his name from the kadoguchi, his mother came hurrying inside. Zuigetsu,

Zuigetsu: 'Oh this is terrible, she must have found out about last night's fight, so that she won't see the wound on my hitahiguchi...'

pulling the yagu thoroughly over himself and curling up so that everything from his knees down protruded, he feigned sleep. Without a speck of enriyoeshiyaku, his mother rolled back the yagu,

Mother: 'You've gone and gotten into another fight, haven't you. Until kiyonen, oyaji-san was here so no one laid as much as a yubiitsupon on you, but taking advantage of me becoming a goke, they've put my son through such a cruel ordeal. This makes exactly the ninth time since last winter; there is no one as fukau as one who is parted from her husband midway, nor is there anything as kaaisau as a child without a parent. Your younger brother Yoshimatsu went rushing off to Wakanishiki's place claiming he was going to avenge his older brother, but got his head thumped instead, and came back bleeding to lie groaning at home. And now here you are, the older brother, just like this—are there truly no gods or Buddhas in this world?'

failing to think that her own son was in the wrong, she laid the blame on the aggressor. Hearing this, he felt an unbearable pity for her, and wherever the pain of his wounds had been, it vanished and fled away.

To tell the truth, up until now his father had been suffering from a lingering illness for two or three years, which had weighed heavily on his mind; not wanting to speak of things he longed to say or cause his father any worry, he had tried as much as possible to avoid getting into fights with people. Consequently, he had never been hated in the least by the village people or the young crowd; rather, he was valued and treated with affection, addressed fondly as Kiraku-san, Kiraku-san. Such being the case, in the summer of the 30th year of Meiji, his father's illness defied all medicines and he finally passed away, so that he no longer had to worry a sickly father. Every time he saw or heard of broken swashbucklers lording it over the countryside and tormenting good citizens, it irritated him to distraction. Unasked, he would plunge into the midst of fights to mediate them, and eventually, getting carried away, he came to regard squaring up against villains and fighting as if it were some remarkable feat of prowess. Having successfully mediated a couple of fights and tasted success,

Zuigetsu: 'For mediating fights, there is no one like Kiraku-san'

being flattered by the villagers, he grew more and more puffed up with pride,

Zuigetsu: 'Will no one pick an interesting fight for me, so I can go and mediate it once more and make a name for myself?'

driven by such petty ambition, even when passing gates where people spoke in high-pitched voices, he had come to prick up his ears and listen.

Around that time, in a place called Hozue in Kameoka, there was a swashbuckler named Ekitokichi, and acting as his sworn brother and lording it over others was an innkeeper's son named Kankichi, a man of large physical build and great height, immensely strong, who wrestled in shrine sumo and was renowned far and wide. Furthermore, his father was named Santetsu, a well-known swashbuckler in the vicinity. His son Kankichi was once again rising rapidly to prominence, and the villagers were in great distress. First of all, he opened a gambling den, took a cut every single day and night, and kept four or five henchmen. His own younger brother frequented Kankichi's gambling den day and night, selling his own watch and clothes, and finally dragging out a milk cow purchased for hundreds of yen in the middle of the night, selling it off dirt cheap for fifty or sixty yen around Kameoka, and using that as capital for gambling. When he offered advice, his brother threw Kankichi's authority in his face and proved utterly unmanageable. The sons of the entire village, like rats pulling rice cakes, were drawn into Kankichi's gambling den day after day, one today and two tomorrow, and the parents lamented it deeply. Yet, fearing what might be done to them if it ever reached Kankichi's ears, each of them merely muttered under their breath.

Hearing this, Jibun could not contain his anger, so he shouldered a tobiguchi used for fires, dashed alone into the gambling den of Kahachiya no Kankichi around eight o'clock at night, hooked the tobiguchi onto the obi of his younger brother who was sitting in a kurumaza playing chiyōhan, and dragged him out for two or three ken.

Upon doing this, the oyabun Kankichi became makijita and shouted:

Kankichi: 'Did you come to wreck the gambling den of Kankichi who sold his manhood? I cannot stay silent and let a shirōto like you do this to me... Hey Yosakō, Tomekō, stretch out Kiraku.'

Jibun, thinking that running away was his best trump card, stuck his backside out and patted it with a smack two or three times, then fled at ichimokusan back to the bokujiyō.

Then he fastened the door bar tightly, and thinking that if they dared to smash the door and hai, he would beat them down, he held a Muku stick and listened to the footsteps outside.

That night, nothing happened at all.

Thinking Kankichi was a fellow of no great account and feeling anshin, Jibun was sleeping at the bokujiyō when, around ten o'clock at night, Kankichi came to the doorway bringing two or three kobun.

And he shouted:

Kankichi: 'Hey Kiraku, I have a little business so come outside.'

Perhaps thinking that even they could not afford to carelessly hai and be done in by a tobiguchi, Kankichi was standing at the doorway trying to lure him out.

Jibun intentionally faked a loud snore and pretended to be asleep, keeping an oak stick beside his bedding.

After a while, a woman's voice was heard saying:

Woman: 'You han, aren't you a splendid kyōkaku-san? To come pick a fight with just one such weak Kiraku-san lowers your manhood, come now you han, let's go out for a cup of drink to the kuwazaya.'

And the sound of her slapping Kankichi's cheeks pishipishi could be heard.

This woman was the daughter of an old kyōkaku from Nakamura named Tada Kame, and her name was Tada Koto.

Due to a certain occasion, they had formed a strange relationship.

That Koto had come all the way from Nakamura and happened to encounter Kahachiya at the doorway.

Even the mighty kyōkaku, struck on the cheek with a gentle voice, turned to jelly and went off to drink sake at the kuwazaya in Yoshikawa Village, five or six chō down.

From then on, Jibun began studying kyōkaku learning under Tada Koto's father, Tada Kame.

What Tada Kame said was:

Tada Kame: 'If you want to become a kyōkaku and make a name for yourself, you cannot become the real thing unless your head is split open or an arm is practically taken. If this side makes up its mind to throw away its life, hundreds of enemies will run away. Anyway, presence of mind is primary.'

Knowing full well that Jibun was his own daughter's lover, he earnestly taught him such worthless things.

And Tada Kame said:

Tada Kame: 'Though I have quite a lot of kobun, there is no one to inherit the line. Since I am going to train you from now on, it would be a waste to let these kobun go, so how would it be if you became the young oyabun? I plan to consult O-kome-san (Mizuki's mother) and have you adopted into this family as a son-in-law. I also have my reasons for leaving my only daughter at your free disposal. Surely you wouldn't have defiled my only daughter in a momentary whim, would you?'

With that, he drove in a nail that allowed no retreat.

While his father was still alive, Jibun had been asking to let his younger brother inherit the Ueda family line.

His parents had a fortune-teller in Kameoka cast divination sticks, and the fortune-teller said:

Fortune-teller: 'Though this child is born as the eldest, if he stays in his parents' residence he will die young, so it is better to send him out as an adopted son.'

Hearing this, his parents had already consented to his going out for adoption.

However, they had never thought of sending him to be adopted by a kyōkaku.

Born into a poor family since childhood, Jibun felt an extreme sense of displeasure at the tyranny of the strong against the weak.

If a human being raises their head even a little and saves some money, no matter how foolish they are, they are looked upon as wise and respected, but if they drop even a little below the horizon, even children gather around and try to trample on them.

In the countryside where the ideology of serving the great is thriving, it is even more intense.

Unless one rises above the crowd in everything, one cannot hold up one's head and life has no value—so Jibun had brooded since childhood.

Without education, he could not become a government official, nor could he become a military man; he thought up such foolish things that becoming a kyōkaku who helps the weak and crushes the strong would probably bring the most fame, and hearing the chiyongare of Bandōin Chōbei, there were frequent times when he even thought, I shall become the Bandōin Chōbei of the Meiji era.

That habitual thought and the resentment of being oppressed by the strong merged into one, and his sympathy for the victims of society erupted upon his father's return to the unseen realm, prompting him to plot the life-risking crushing of kyōkaku; because they were glared at by society as cheeky fellows, within less than a year he was made to suffer terrible experiences nine times.

Reflecting that if he had not been doing the service of the Lord God, he might have been beaten to death by the age of thirty-four or thirty-five, the grace of the Lord God came to feel deeply and gratefully precious to him.

Just as his mother's words stated, Jibun was never made to suffer by the kyōkaku because his father had passed away; at that time he was already able to realize that it was a disaster he had brought upon himself.

と呶鳴つて居る。流石に先方も、迂闊に這入つて鳶口でやられては堪らぬと思ふたか、門口に立つて誘ひ出してゐる。自分は故意とに作り鼾をして寝たふりをして居た。そして樫の棒を寝床の横に置いてあつた。暫らくすると女の声で、

『あんたハン、立派な侠客サンぢやおまへんか、たつた一人の、あんな弱々しい喜楽サンに喧嘩に来るなんて、男が下りまつせ、さアあんたハン、一杯桑酒屋へ飲みに行きまほ』

と勘吉の頬辺をピシヤピシヤたたいて居る音が聞えて来た。此女は中村の多田亀と云ふ老侠客の娘で、多田琴と云ふ女である。或機会から妙な仲となつて居つた。其琴が中村から遥々とやつて来て、門口で河内屋に出会ふたのである。流石の侠客も、横面をやさしい声で殴られてグニヤグニヤになり、五六丁下の吉川村の桑酒屋へ酒を飲みに行つて了つた。

それから自分は多田琴の父親の多田亀に就いて侠客学問を研究し始めた。多田亀の云ふのには、

『侠客になつて名を挙げ様と思へば、頭を割られたり、腕の一本位とられなくては本物にならぬ。此方が生命を捨てる気になれば、何百人の敵も逃げるものだ。兎に角気転が第一だ』

と自分の娘の情夫と知り乍ら、碌でもない事を一生懸命に教へて呉れた。さうして多田亀の云ふのには、

『俺の乾児も大分沢山あるのだが、跡を継がす者がない。これからお前に仕込んでやるから、此乾児を捨てるのは惜いから、若親分になつたら如何だ。お米サン（瑞月の母）に相談して、お前サンを此方の養子に貰ふ積だ。此方も一人の娘をお前サンの自由にさして、黙つて居るのについては考へがあるのだ。よもや一時のテンゴに、俺の一人娘をなぶり者にしたのぢやあるまいなア』

と退引させぬ釘をさされた。

父の居る中から、上田の跡は弟に継がして貰ひ度いと云つて頼んで居つた。両親は亀岡の或易者に卦を立てて貰ひ、

『此子は総領に生まれて居るけれども、親の屋敷に居つては若死をするから養子にやつたが良い』

といつたとかで、両親は已に自分の養子に行くのを承認して了つた。然し侠客の養子に遣らうとは思うて居なかつたのである。

自分は幼時から貧家に生れ、弱者に対する強者の横暴を非常に不快に感じて居た。人間は少しく頭をあげて金でも貯めれば、如何な馬鹿でも賢う見られ、敬はれるが、少しく地平線下に落ちると、子供迄が寄つて集つて踏みつけ様とする。事大思想の盛んな田舎では尚更はげしいのである。何でも一つ衆に擢んでなければ頭があがらない、生存の価値がないと、幼時から思ひつめて居た。学問が無ければ官吏になる事も出来ず、軍人に成り度うても成れず、弱い者を助け、強い者を凹ます侠客になつた方が、一番名が挙がるだらうと下らぬ事を考へ、幡随院長兵衛のちよんがれを聞いて、明治の幡随院長兵衛は俺がなつてやらうかと迄思ふ事が屡々あつた。其平素の思ひと強者に虐げられた無念とが一つになつて、社会の弱者に対する同情心が、父の帰幽と共に突発し、生命懸けの侠客凹ませを企て、猪口才な奴と彼等が社会から睨まれて居たから、一年経たぬ中に九回迄も酷い目に会はされたのである。若しも神様の御用をせなかつたらば、自分は三十四五迄に叩き殺されて居るかも知れないと思ひ浮べて、神様の御恩がシミジミと有難くなつて来たのである。

自分は母の言葉の如く、決して父が逝くなつた為めに侠客に苦しめられたのではない、つまり自分から招いた災である事を其時已に自覚し得たのである。
