Chapter 17: Fox の尾 [1029]

狐の尾〔1029〕

§ 1

After a while, Takashima Fumiko, called Odai-san, came back together with Sugiyama-san, who was both Sōmukaku and her otsuto. The old man named Hattori was astonished, and suddenly hid his tokuri between his thighs. Seeing Kiraku's appearance, Sugiyama smiled cheerfully, made a smiling expression, and said,

Sugiyama: 'Ah, Kiraku-san, thank you very much for coming. Today we must ask you for itsu-seki of kauen. As you know, our believers have increased greatly, and we cannot go on forever renting such an inconvenient house. We think we would like to build a new one somewhere, and for that purpose, about three thousand yen is necessary, so we have now prepared a subscription list, divided the work among ourselves, and the sewakata have just come to an agreement to walk around collecting donations. In this connection, since it would somehow not be very interesting for me to talk about building a church, won't you stand up on the podium in my place?'

At that time Kiraku was still twenty-six years old, and since he had been practicing faith because he wanted his father's illness cured, he had come to feel that he would grudge nothing when it came to matters of the gods. Therefore, he immediately signified his consent, was shown the specifications and designs of the church, and secretly formed a plan in his mind to try giving a speech once the festival was over. Both Takashima Fumiko and Sugiyama rejoiced immensely, ordered polite dishes, and entertained him in the inner room. With a crimson face, breathing heavily and painfully, Hattori came before Takashima and said,

Hattori: 'Now the sewakata have arrived. Before long the believers will gradually gather, so shall we have the sewakata drink sake until then?'

In a low voice, Takashima said,

Takashima: 'No matter what you call sewakata, they always just come out and drink sake, and nothing ever comes of it.'

Catching this in her speech, and with his tongue rolling, Hattori said,

Hattori: 'Nha-ha! What is this! The Odai-san of Inari, the ki-ki fox-san, is of no use. Since you are worked to the bone from morning till night for a meager allowance, even if you drink about a go of sake by chance, don't go complaining about every little thing.'

Takashima: 'Come now, Hattori-san, whatever are you talking about? Quickly bring out some sake or the like for the sewakata and treat them politely.'

Hattori: 'Today is the once-a-year spring grand festival of the god, so surely I won't be grumbled at just for having received the sacred wine of the god, would I? To tell the truth, there is someone who says he wants to use me as a school custodian, but a friend told me not to do such a thing because you would get angry and might even set a fox on me or something, which would be troublesome, so I am enduring it; but today Kiraku-san has already come, so please stop just the fox's tail.'

So saying, he ran in a wobbling, staggering manner toward the entrance.

When it finally passed three o'clock in the afternoon, worshippers gradually gathered, and plenty of offerings were made such as rice cakes, confectioneries, rice, wrapped bundles, red beans, and ordinary beans; and hitting the exact hour of four o'clock in the afternoon, the norito began, and they started burning goma in front of the sanctuary. On small pieces of wood cut and split to about five sun in length, they wrote down names and ages one by one, piled them high, and set fire to them to burn inside a large iron pot, above which paper gohei hung down, cut out. Sugiyama, Hattori, and all the other numerous sewakata chanted the norito with all their might from around the brazier made of the pot. The fire burned with a roaring sound. Just as the fire was about to catch on the gohei suspended above, they poked in a sakaki branch dripping with water to prevent the fire; and just as it was about to flare up again, they poked in sakaki leaves. Even so, the fire gradually grew more and more violent. Takashima Fumiko went into her customary divine possession, and while showing a red fox's tail flickering from the gap of her haori, she shook the gohei, thrust it fiercely into the raging fire, and while preventing the flames from spreading to the gohei hanging from above, she waved that gohei once more to the left and right over the heads of the believers. From corner to corner, she went around flogging the heads of one hundred and forty or fifty people one by one with the gohei. The goma fire grew higher and higher, and when it approached the danger of catching fire and spreading to the fluttering, tufted gohei suspended above, four or five sewakata raised their voices a notch louder, chanting norito as if hiccuping; and taking that as a signal, Takashima Fumiko frantically thrust the gohei tied to the green leaves of the sakaki right between the fire and the hanging gohei, thus preventing it from catching fire—such was her dexterity. When Kiraku firmly grasped the fox's tail flickering from Takashima Fumiko's buttocks, Fumiko was startled, and while uttering 'Shyu-shyu,' with an attitude like toying with a caterpillar, she gave her hips a neat swing to one side, waved the gohei in a fluttering manner, and swept it away toward the other direction. Thus the festival was brought to a safe conclusion. He realized that the fox's tail mentioned by old man Hattori was not entirely a lie. It was partly laughable and partly foolish; his faith suddenly cooled, and from then until February of the thirty-first year, when he reached twenty-eight years of age, he came to hate joining his hands in prayer to the gods, and ended up becoming an extreme atheist.

原文（近代文語）

暫くあつて御台サンと称する高島ふみ子は、総務格兼良人なる杉山氏と共に帰つて来た。服部と云ふ爺は驚いて、俄に徳利を股にかくす。杉山は喜楽の姿を見て、嬉し相に笑ひ、笑顔を作り、

杉山『あゝ喜楽サン、能う来て下さいました。今日はあなたに一席の講演を願はねばなりませぬ。御存じの通り信者が沢山殖えまして、何時までもこんな不便な家を借つて居る訳にも行きませぬ。どつかに新築をしたいと思ひますがそれに就いては一寸三千円許り必要なので、今寄附帳を拵へて、各自に手分けをなし、世話方が寄附金募集に歩かうと相談の纏まつた所で御座います。就いては私が教会建築の話をするのも何だか面白くありませぬから、一つあなたが私に代つて演壇に立つて下さいますまいかなア』

と云ふ。喜楽は其時はまだ二十六歳、父の病気が治して欲しさに信仰をして居つたのだから、神様の事なら何でもいとはぬと云ふ気になつて居た。そこで直に承諾の旨を告げ、教会の仕様書や設計などを見せて貰ひ、祭典がすめば一場の演説を試みようと心ひそかに腹案を作つてゐた。高島ふみ子、杉山の両人は非常に喜び、丁重な料理を取よせて、奥の間で饗応してくれた。服部は真赤な顔をし、フーフーと苦しさうな息をし乍ら、高島の前にやつて来て、

服部『今世話方衆が見えました。やがて信者も追々集まりませうから、世話方に夫れまで酒を呑んで貰ひませうか』

高島は小声で、

高島『世話方なんと云つた所で、いつも出て来て酒をのむ許りで何にもなりやしない』

と云つたのを、服部は聞きかじつて、巻舌になり乍ら、

服部『ナヽ何がイヽ稲荷のお台サン、キヽ狐サン、役に立たぬのだ。朝から晩までおめし給銀でこき使はれて居るのだから、たまたま一升位酒を飲んだつて、ゴテゴテ言ひなさるな』

高島『コレ服部サン、ソリヤ何を言つてゐなさるのだ。早う御世話方にお酒でも出して叮嚀にあしらうて下さい』

服部『今日は神様の一年に一度の春季大祭だから、私が神さまの御神酒を頂いた位で、ゴテゴテ言ひはしませぬだらうな。実の所は学校の小使に使うてやらうと云ふ人があるのだけれど、お前サンが怒つて、狐でも使ふてあたんでもすると困るから、そんな事アおけと友達が言ふてくれるので、辛抱して居るのだが、今日はモウ喜楽サンが来て居るのだから、狐の尾丈はやめなさいや』

と言ひ乍ら、ヒヨロリ　ヒヨロリと玄関口の方へ走つて行く。

いよいよ午後の三時過になると、ボツボツと参詣人が集つて来て、牡丹餅や菓子、米、包み物、小豆、豆など沢山に供へられ、いよいよ午後四時を期して祝詞が始まり、神殿の前で護摩をたき始めた。五寸許りに切つて割つた木切れに、一々姓名や年齢を書き記し、それを高くつんで、大きな鍋の中で、火をつけてもやす、其上には幣が切つてぶら下つてゐる。杉山を始め服部其他沢山の世話方は、お鍋で作つた火鉢のぐるりから、祝詞を一生懸命に称へる。火はポーポーと音を立ててもえる。アワヤ上に吊つた御幣に火が燃えつかうとすると、水の垂るやうな榊をポツとくべて火を防ぐ、又燃上らうとすると榊の葉をくべる、それでも火はだんだんと烈しくなつてくる。高島ふみ子は例の神憑りになり、羽織のあひからチヨロチヨロと赤い色の狐の尻尾を見せながら、御幣をふつて、烈しく燃え上がる火の中へ突き出し、上から吊つた御幣に延焼せむとするのを防ぎつつ、其幣を又もや信者の頭の上に左右左とふる。すみからすみまで、百四五十人の頭の上を一つ一つ御幣でしばいてまはる、護摩の火はだんだん高くなり、アワヤ吊り下たフサフサとした御幣に燃え移らうとする危険に迫ると、四五人の世話方が一割大きな声で、シヤクル様に祝詞を上げる、それを合図に高島ふみ子は榊の青葉に括りつけた御幣を、あわてて火と吊幣との間にグツとつき出し延焼を防ぐ其巧妙さ。喜楽は高島ふみ子の尻からチヨロチヨロ見えて居る狐の尻尾をグツと握ると、ふみ子は驚いて『シユーシユー』と云ひ乍ら芋虫を弄ふた様な体裁でプリンと尻を一方へふり、御幣をプイプイと振り廻し又向ふの方へ払ひもつて行く。斯の如くして祭典は無事に終了を告げた。服部爺サンの言つた狐の尾も万更ウソでない事を悟つた。可笑しいやら馬鹿らしいやら、俄に信仰がさめて了ひ、それから三十一年の二月、廿八歳になるまで、神様に手を合すのがいやになり、極端な無神論者になつて了つたのである。

§ 2

The saitō was safely concluded. Having been asked by a certain Sugiyama to prepare and deliver a speech, after seeing this tail I somehow lost enthusiasm, and the draft I had painstakingly prepared vanished somewhere; in a perfunctory manner, I managed a disjointed, incoherent speech lasting about ten minutes. Yet, strangely enough, starting with Sugiyama, the attendant believers clapped their hands and seemed deeply impressed. Facing a superstitious crowd of old hags who understood nothing, I spoke of things I myself did not understand, and the fact that I met with almost no opposition and was instead greeted with applause was quite incomprehensible. Regarding human beings who understand nothing, speaking to them of things they cannot understand is something that truly enters their ears well, I could not help but admire to myself.

The saitō safely concluded, at the bidding of the o-dai-san, Ofumi-san, a believer named Nakagawa from Umajimura said it was an expression of gratitude for the safe birth of a child, and I went around distributing two of the many rice cakes offered to a hundred or fifty or sixty believers. And Ofumi-san's way of speaking was amusing.

Everyone, Nakagawa-san's wife, twelve months have passed since she conceived, yet the child has not come forth; so when she came to worship at this Great Deity and asked for guidance, she was told that because this person stepped over a cow's rope after becoming pregnant, a cow's calf took lodging there as punishment, and for twelve months it remained in her womb. According to the deity's words, since a cow's calf will be born if left as it is, she was told to practice faith. Then, Nakagawa-san and his wife took turns making pilgrimage over a distance of two ri, and finally a splendid human baby boy was born; today, taking advantage of the festival, they have offered rice cakes. Everyone, please receive the blessing by association. If one's faith is only strong, the deity will grant any request whatsoever. Please, everyone, without doubting, practice faith, and you will surely receive vast divine benefits.

With a knowing look and still wearing her religious vestments, she stood at the upper seat, chattered away, and entered the next room. The multitude of believers received them in their hands, took a single bite, and with strange expressions, pulled paper from their bosoms, wrapped them up, and put them in their sleeves. Everyone without exception wore a displeased expression. I too received two, but thinking them to have a strange aroma, when I broke them open, cow dung was wrapped inside.

From among the crowd,

Hey, o-dai-san, look here, cow dung is mixed in!

someone shouted. Upon that, from over there and from over here,

Ah, it smells foul! Eh, eh!

people using paper began to appear. Takashimafumiko-san was startled, took off her outer robe, and leaving the fox's tail bound with her narrow sash, forgetting to take it off, came running to this place,

Takashimafumiko: 'Everyone, do not say such sacrilegious things. There is no way such a thing would be offered to the deity.'

Scarcely had she spoken when a man named Nakagawa, thirty-two or three years old, with a slightly dark complexion and a narrow, elongated face, stood before the altar,

Nakagawa: 'I am a person called Nakagawa of Umajimura. Since my domestic partner became pregnant, even when the months were full she did not give birth, so when I came here to seek guidance, the sly fox here blurted out that because she had stepped over a cow's rope, a cow's calf had lodged within, and that if we only practiced faith it would be born as a human child—thinking there is a limit to making fools of human beings, I had thought so, but I believed I could not know until it came to pass, and I came every day in place of my wife. When I did so, despite its being blurted out that a girl would be born, a splendid boy was born. An Inari that can know even such inner matters of the belly—if one were to offer cow dung inside rice cakes and worship it, surely it would know; if it were a matter of not knowing, thinking it a mountain-child hag's ditch-tanuki, as expected, it was the absurdity of offering cow dung before the deity and worshiping it. I did not bring the rice cakes saying please eat them, all of you, so do not be angry. The old hag here is bad, ha ha ha, mountain-child hag beneath the Inari, go eat shit too! From now on, I will go shouting this whole story all around.'

So saying, he bolted away in a single dash and went home. From this time, the church, which had been at the height of its prosperity like the rising sun, gradually grew desolate, until finally maintenance became impossible, and transferring to the precincts of the Tenjin-sama in Kyōmachi, it barely preserved its life span and continued until around the forty-fifth year of Meiji.

原文（近代文語）

祭典は無事に済んだ。杉山某から御馳走迄拵へて頼まれた演説も此尻尾を見てから何となく気乗がせず、折角拵へておいた腹案もどつかへ消えて了ひ、申し訳的に十分間程取とめもない、支離滅裂な演説をやつてのけた。それでも不思議な事には、杉山を始め世話方信者は手を叩いて、非常に感服してゐる様子であつた。何も訳の分らぬ婆嬶の迷信連に向つて、自分でさへも訳の分らぬ事を云つたのに、余り反対も受けず、却て拍手を以て迎へられたのは合点のいかぬ事であつた。訳の分らぬ人間に対しては、ヤツパリ分らぬ事を云ふて聞かすのが、よく耳に這入るものだなアと、自ら感心せざるを得なかつた。

祭典は無事に済み、お台サンのおふみサンの言ひ付けで馬路村の或る中川と云ふ信者から、子が無事に生れたお礼だと云つて、御供へした沢山の牡丹餅を百四五十人の信者に二つづつ配つて廻つた。そしておふみサンの言草が面白い。

『皆サン中川サンの奥サンは、御妊娠をなさつてから、十二ケ月になるのに、子が出ませぬので、此大神様にお参りになり、お伺ひ遊ばした所、此人は懐妊になつてから、牛の綱を跨げたから、其罰で牛の子が宿つたので、十二ケ月も腹に居らはつたのです。神様の御言葉では、此儘放つといたら牛の子が生れるに依つて、信仰をせよと仰有りました。それから中川サン御夫婦は二里もある所を代る代る御参拝になつて、とうとう立派な人間の男の子がお生れになつたので、今日はお祭を幸ひに、牡丹餅をお供へになつたので御座います。皆サンあやかつて下さいませ。神様は信心さへ強うすればどんな事でも聞いて下はります。どうぞ皆サンも疑はずに信心をして下さりませ、キツと広大な御利益が頂けますぞえ』

としたり顔に教服をつけたまま、上座に立つて喋り立て、次の間に這入つた。大勢の信者は手に頂いて、一口かぶつては妙な顔をし乍ら懐から紙を出して包み袂に入れる。誰もかれも厭相な顔をしてゐる。自分も二つ貰うたが、妙な香だと思うて割つて見ると牛糞が包んであつた。

大勢の中から、

『オイお台サン、コリヤ牛糞が交ぜつとりますぜ』

と叫ぶ者がある。さうするとあちらからも此方からも、

『あゝ臭かつた、エーエー』

と紙を使ふものも出来て来た。高島ふみ子サンは驚いて、上装束をぬぎ、狐の尾を細帯で括つたまま、取るのを忘れて、此場へ走り来り、

高島『皆サン勿体ない事を仰有るな。そんな物を神様にお供へしそうな事がありませぬ』

と云ふや否や、中川と云ふ男、三十二三歳の少し色の黒い、細長い顔をして、神壇の前に立ち、

中川『私は馬路村の中川某と云ふ者です。私の家内が妊娠をしてから月が満ちても出産せぬので、ここへ伺ひに来た所、ここの奴狐がぬかすには、牛の綱をまたげたから、牛の子が宿つて居るのだ、信心さへすれば人間の子に生れさしてやると、バカな事をぬかしやがる、人間さまを馬鹿にしやがるも程があると思うて居つたが、それでも出来て見ねば分らぬと思ひ、女房の代りに毎日参つて居りました。そした所、女子が出来るとぬかしたに拘らず、立派な男の子が生れました。そんな事の腹の中の事まで分る様な稲荷なら、牡丹餅の中へ牛糞を入れて供へたらキツト知つてるだらう、モシ知らぬやうな事なら山子婆の溝狸だと思うてをつたら、案の条、牛糞を神様の前に供へて拝んでをる可笑しさ。私は皆さまに食て下さいと云ふて牡丹餅を持つて来たのだないから、皆さま怒つて下さるな。ここの婆アが悪いのだ、アハヽヽ稲荷下の山子バヽ、尻でもくらへ、これから俺がそこら中、此次第をふれ歩いてやる』

と言ひ乍ら、一目散に飛出し帰つて行つた。これより旭日昇天の勢ひであつた、此教会も次第々々にさびれて、遂には維持が出来なくなり、京町の天神さまの境内へ移転して、僅かに命脈を保つて、明治四十五年頃まで継続して居たのであつた。
