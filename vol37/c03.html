Chapter 3: 破軍 Stars [1015]

破軍星〔1015〕

§ 1

From Ōsaka down to the countryside came a dance teacher, an aging woman of about forty named Otama. The young men of the village went every night to take lessons in Hauta, dancing, and choreography. Before long, this Otama had become the common-law wife of the chivalrous outlaw Kankichi. Then Kankichi would attach the village youths to Otama, corner them there, bring charges against them, and extort money. This woman also knew a little about Jiyōruri, and would teach the young men playfully using cards.

A man named Jiromatsu, despite having crossed the threshold of fifty and suffering from the loneliness of widowhood, went every night without fail to observe the young men's lessons in dancing, choreography, and Jiyōruri. In the end, he fell into the trap of Otama and Kankichi's blackmail scheme, was caught in the act of sleeping, was heavily beaten on the head and back, turned pale, fled back to his own home, and trembled violently. Thereupon, a twenty-five-year-old man from Jiromatsu's neighborhood named Uhedachiyōkichi came along and spoke thus:

'Since I have stepped in between Kankichi and Otama and managed the matter skillfully, produce two hundred yen. Then Kankichi will surely not be angry.'

Jiromatsu, a miser by birth, managed the money with a painful effort, feeling as though he were jumping from the stage of Kiyomizu Temple, and handing fifty yen into Chiyōkichi's hands. Chiyōkichi said to Otama,

'Since Jiromatsu-san has forked out twenty-five yen, be satisfied with this. This twenty-five yen is my own money, but I give it to you.'

Thus he coaxed her smoothly, and once again established a peculiar relation with Otama.

Kankichi, knowing nothing of such matters, took five or six henchmen with him, marched over to Jiromatsu's house in the dark of night, and began a fierce dispute. 'Jiromatsu, who has always been reviled by the whole village as a faultfinder of sorrows and joys, is going to be done in by Kawachiya tonight—what a fine punishment, how amusing, shall we go and see?'—so they muttered to one another from one to the next, and the gate was packed with a crowd of people. Jiromatsu's aged mother bolted out from the back door, came to our house, and cried, trembling:

'Now then, Kiraku-san, a terrible thing has happened! As you are a relative, if our Matsu does not put up two hundred ryo of money right now, he is going to be stuffed into a bale and thrown into Jigoku River, so please hurry over and negotiate with Kankichi…'

Though I said, 'Ah, I am coming!'... the voices of a great crowd could be heard murmuring in the neighborhood, and Kankichi's shouting rang in my ears as if close at hand. A fraction of me whispered at the bottom of my belly, 'This is perilous; I cannot afford to go carelessly.' And my eighty-four-year-old grandmother and mother seemed to be waiting with anxious expressions to see what my answer would be.

Granny Oko, acting with all her might because it was a momentous affair concerning her own child, wept aloud:

'Kiraku-san, please come quickly, Matsu is going to be ruined…'

'Then let us go,'

I attempted to stand up. My grandmother signaled with her eyes not to go. Granny Oko said:

'Now then, Kiraku-san, if you are a relative and will not come to help at a time like this, return the twenty yen I lent you. I must use it as a contribution for Kawachiya, and if you do not come at a time like this, I will never listen to whatever you ask of me henceforth!'

She tried to forcefully extract a small debt by laying on a sense of obligation. I was slightly provoked... yet I thought that the people of the world would not suppose I failed to go because of such circumstances, but would instead be cowed by Kankichi and slander me, saying that even Kiraku failed to come after all. To be called a corpse just when I was about to become a fledgling chivalrous outlaw—if that happened, all my past efforts would vanish into bubbles. Never mind! Just as Tada Kame taught, as long as I go with my life at stake, it will be all right. I will cast aside all cowardice for once; now is the time to make a name for myself!—suddenly growing bold, pretending not to see the anxious looks on the faces of my old mother and grandmother, I bolted out of my house, slipped through the rear bamboo-grove fence twice while getting tangled in spiderwebs, entered the inner room of Jiromatsu's house from the back exit, appeared suddenly from the inner part with an innocent face right beside the brazier, threw on a padded dotera featuring an Izumo-pattern loosely, and sat down with majestic composure. And I shall turn the sword tip of the Hagun Star toward the enemy; since I have taken my seat upon the hilt of the sword blade, I am certain to win decisively—while falling slightly under the spell of superstition,

原文（近代文語）

大阪から田舎下しの舞の師匠に、お玉といふ四十位の年増があつた。村の若者は端唄や舞や踊を毎晩稽古に往つて居つた。何時の間にかは此お玉は侠客の勘吉の内縁の妻となつてゐた。そして勘吉は其お玉に村の若い者をくつつけ、そこを押へては物言ひを付け、金銭を絞り取つて居たのである。此女は少し浄瑠璃も知つてゐて、若者にチヨコチヨコ札で教へて居た。

次郎松といふ男、五十の坂を越えて居乍ら鰥の淋しさに、若い者の舞や踊や浄瑠璃の稽古を毎夜欠かさず見聞に行き、遂にはお玉と勘吉の美人局に陥り寝込みを押へられ、頭や背中をしたたか殴られ、真青になつて吾家に逃げ帰り、ブルブル慄へて居た。そこへ上田長吉といふ、次郎松の近所の二十五歳の男がやつて来て、いふやう、

『わしが勘吉とお玉との中へ這入つて話をうまくつけて来たから、二百円出しなさい。そしたら、勘吉も怒りはすまい』

と言つた。次郎松は生れついての吝嗇坊、惜相に工面して、清水の舞台から飛んだやうな心持で、五十円の金を拵へ、長吉の手に渡した。長吉はお玉に向つて、

『次郎松サンが二十五円出して呉れたから、これで勘弁しなさい。此廿五円はわしの金ぢやが、お前に上げる』

と甘くチヨロまかして、又お玉に妙な関係をつけて了つた。

肝腎の勘吉はそんなこととは知らず、五六人の乾児を伴れ、暗夜に次郎松の家に押掛け行き、強談判を始め出した。平素から憂ひ喜びの悪口言ひと、村中から憎まれてゐた次郎松が、今夜は河内屋にやられるのだ、よい罰だ、面白い、見て来うか……と次から次へ言ひ合はし、門には一杯の人だかりになつてゐる。次郎松の老母は裏口から飛び出し、吾家に来り、

『コレコレ喜楽サン、大変なことが起つて来た。お前も親類のことであり、内の松が今二百両の金を出さねば、地獄川へ俵につめて放り込まれるとこだから、早う来て勘吉に談判しておくれ……』

と慄ひ慄ひ泣いてゐる。自分は『ヨシ来た！』……と言つたものの、近所にワアワアと大勢の声が聞えてゐる、勘吉の呶鳴り声も手に取る如く耳にひびく。幾分か、コリヤ険呑だ、ウツカリ行く訳には行こまい……と、稍卑怯心の虫が腹の底の方で囁き出した。そして八十四歳になつた老祖母や母が、不安な顔色をして、自分の返事を如何いふかと待つてゐるやうである。

おこの婆アサンは吾子の一大事だと、一生懸命に、

『喜楽サン、早う来ておくれ、松がやられて了ふ……』

と泣き立てる。

『そんなら行きませう』

と自分は立上らうとする。老祖母は行くなと目で知らす。おこの婆アサンは、

『コレ喜楽サン、親類で居つて、こんな時に助けに来てくれんのなら、お前の所へ二十円貸した金を未返しておくれ。河内屋にやる足しにせんならんから、そしてこんな時に来てくれな、モウこれから何を頼まれても聞きませんぞえ』

と少しの借金を恩にきせて無理に引出さうとする。自分は一寸むかついたが、……併し世間の者は、そんな事情で怒つて行かなんだとは思はずに、勘吉に辟易して、とうとう喜楽も能う来なんだと誹るであらう。折角侠客の玉子になりかけた所を、なきがらだと言はれては、今までの事が水泡に帰する、ナアニ多田亀の教へた通り、命を的にかけて行きさへすれば大丈夫だ、一つ度胸を放り出してやらう、名を売るのは今ぢや……と俄に強くなつて、老母や母の不安な顔色を見ぬ振りして、吾家を飛び出し、裏の藪の垣を蜘蛛の巣に引つかかり乍ら、二つもくぐりぬけて、背戸口から次郎松の奥の間へ入りこみ、何くはぬ顔して、奥からヌツと火鉢の側へ現はれて、井筒型の模様のあるドテラをフワリと羽織り、鷹揚に坐り込んだ。そして破軍星の剣先を敵に向けてやらう、自分は剣先の柄に座を占めたれば、キツと勝つに違ひないと、稍迷信に囚はれ乍ら、

Hey Kahachiya, what is the meaning of a crowd of five or six mighty, flourishing kefukaku bringing their kobun and coming to attack such a feeble, wobbly old man? Do you even know what the character for kefukaku means? Isn't the true honbun of a kefukaku to go to an iukuwaku and sell your manhood? In such a tiny inaka, by tormenting a hebo old man, far from making a name for yourself, your reputation will plummet instead!

Thus he snapped at him right from the start. Wondering what he was thinking, Kahachiya went out to the kadoguchi without a word, brought the five kobun inside, and issued the gaurei:

Beat Kiraku to a pulp! Drag Jiromatsu out!

Granny Okono had come to her own house and, out of fear, was trembling and refusing to return. Remaining seated before the nagahibachi with a matsusao face, Jiromatsu asked in an off-chōshi voice:

Which way is the Hagunsei pointing, hey Kiraku-san...

Stirred up by the oyabun, Tomekō and Yosakō, who were reputed to be the strongest riyōude among the kobun, were trembling all over as they cautiously thrust out their hands, shaking by half:

Come on, Kiraku-san, please come out. Jiromatsu-san, the oyabun is saying such things, so please come out.

Kahachiya, unlike the howling of a dog, merely barked his gaurei sternly from the kadoguchi. With his hands tucked inside his futokorode, he sat heavily with a thud, tilted his chin upward, and deliberately put on a bold, karaiba front like a gauketsu. Nevertheless, around his armpits and waist, unlike the cold of yosamu, sweat was soaking his clothes thoroughly. Young villagers and women came crowding one after another to the kadoguchi, making a noisy fuss. Because he was universally resented from of old, not a single person attempted to step in as a mediator.

After a short while, a man called Usukatsu brought along his younger brother, Nagayoshi. Since this man was constantly being looked after by Jiromatsu, and because it was a neighborhood matter as well as something concerning his own younger brother, he had brought Nagayoshi along and entered through the back door.

Turning toward Nagayoshi, I raised my voice on purpose and shouted:

What happened to the fifty yen in money that I asked you to give to Otama-san the other day?

Trembling, Nagayoshi replied:

I definitely handed that fifty yen over to Otama-san.

Upon that, so that everyone could hear, Kiraku shouted:

If I hear correctly, isn't that woman called Otama Kahachiya's mistress? Even if it is a kefukaku's common-law affair, for a woman who is to become a wife to take fifty yen in money from a man is an outrageous wretch. This is surely not Kahachiya's instruction. Having such a woman not only stains the name of a kefukaku, but it is a disgrace to this village. Kahachiya, a rising star of the present day who fancies himself a man of consequence, would never do such a cowardly thing as to use a woman as a pawn to extort money. You must have pocketed it yourself, chiyotsuto.

With a peculiar expression on his face, Usukatsu shouted:

At any rate, since my younger brother Nagayoshi is in the wrong, I want you to leave this matter to me. Even Kahachiya will not keep silent when mud has been smeared on a man's face. A man who is a kefukaku is hardly likely to do such a thing as use a woman as a pawn to take money. If a thing like this were even to reach the ears of the oyabun of Kantera, that truly would be a terrible disaster.

Kahachiya seemed to have felt a little ashamed upon realizing that he had been pestering them, claiming that Jiromatsu had violated Otama, smeared mud on the kefukaku's face, and that he would not let it pass unless they forked over two hundred yen or he would throw him into Jigokugawa; he came all the way from the kadoguchi to the front of the firebox at the entrance, and

Kankichi: 'If I, this Kankichi, am told that I used a woman as a pawn to extort money, I cannot hold my head up as a man. There must be some mistake... Hey Yosakō, Tomekō, did you fellows say such stupid things to Jiromatsu-san? You are troublesome rascals.'

と頭から咬みつけて見た。河内屋は何と思ふたか、物も言はず門口へ出て、乾児の五人を中へ入れ、

『オイ喜楽を叩きのばせ！　次郎松を引ずり出せ！』

と号令をかけてゐる。おこの婆アサンは自分の宅へ来たなり、怖がつて震うて帰つて来ない。次郎松は長火鉢の前に坐つたまま、真青な顔して、

『破軍星はどつちを向いてる、なア喜楽サン……』

などと調子外れな声で尋ねてゐる。乾児の中の両腕と聞えたる、留公、与三公は親分にケシを掛けられ、震ひ震ひ、

『コレ喜楽サン、一寸出て下され。次郎松サン、親分があない言うてますから出て下さい』

などと怖々ニユツと手をつき出して、半分ふるうてゐる。河内屋は犬の遠吠に似ず、門口から号令をきびしくかける計りである。自分は懐手をした儘、ドスンとすわり、揚げ面をしてワザと豪傑らしく空威張りをしてゐた。併し乍ら脇の下や腰のあたりは秋の夜寒にも似ず、汗がビツシヨリと着物をぬらしてゐた。門口には村の若い者や女が先ぐり先ぐりやつて来て、ワイワイとぞめいてゐる。不断から憎まれてゐるので、誰一人仲裁に入らうとする者がない。

暫くすると嘘勝と言ふ男が弟の長吉を引張つて来た。此男は次郎松から常に世話になつて居る所から、近所の事でもあり、且つ自分の弟に関した事でもあるので、裏口から長吉を伴れて這入つて来たのである。自分は長吉に向ひ、ワザと大きな声で、

『此間松サンからお玉サンに渡してくれといつて、ことづけた五十円の金は如何したのか？』

と呶なりつけて見た。長吉は震ひ乍ら、

『其五十円は確にお玉サンに渡しました』

と云ふ。そこで喜楽は皆に聞える様に、

『お玉といふ女は聞けば、河内屋の囲女ぢやないか。侠客の内縁にもせよ、女房になる女が、男から金の五十円も取るとは怪しからん奴だ。これは要するに河内屋が差図ではあるまい。こんな女を持つて居ると、侠客の名が汚れるのみならず、此村の恥だ。男達を以て任ずる当時売出しの河内屋が、女を玉に使うて金を取るといふ、卑怯なことは決してする筈がない。大方貴様がチヨロまかしたのだろ』

と呶鳴つて見せた。嘘勝は妙な顔をして、

『とも角、弟の長吉が悪いのだから、此事は私に任して貰ひたい。河内屋だつて、男の顔に泥をぬられて黙つておろまい。侠客といふ者は、女を玉に使つて金を取るといふやうなことはしそうな筈がない。こんな事がカンテラの親分にでも聞えたら、それこそ大変だぞ』

と呶鳴りかけた。河内屋はお玉を次郎松が犯し、侠客の顔に泥を塗つたから、承知しない、二百円の金を出さねば地獄川へ放り込むとねだつて居たのが、少し恥しくなつたと見え、門口から再び上り口の火鉢の前迄やつて来て、

勘吉『此勘吉は、女を玉に金をねだつたなどと言はれちや、男が立ちません。何かの間違だらう……コラ与三公、留公、貴様、そんな馬鹿なことを次郎松サンに言うたのか、不都合な奴だ』

he roared.

Yosakō and Tomekō looked as if they wanted to say that it was an order from their Oyabun, but realizing they could not quite put it that way, they scratched their heads vigorously and said,

'Hē, we don't remember saying anything like that at all.'

Before they knew it, their twisted tongues had slipped back into their native country dialect.

Kahachiya softened his expression and said,

'Yaa Kiraku-san, I am sorry for causing you worry.

As the saying goes, disasters come from below, and because one of my subordinates blurted out something I knew nothing about, it turned into such a commotion.

However, as you know, I am an up-and-coming kyōkaku.

If people say I was put in a tight spot by an amateur like Kiraku-san, a man cannot show his face.

Let us make up over this once and settle things cleanly and neatly.'

He approached in a conciliatory manner.

Kiraku asked,

'If you understand things that way, that is fine.

In that case, Jiromatsu will put up fifteen yen, so you put up fifteen yen as well, and let us hold a banquet or something to make peace.'

Kahachiya looked as if a load had been taken off his shoulders, and finding himself at a loss where to sheath his drawn sword, he fortunately accepted with an immediate double answer and said,

'I will leave everything to Kiraku-san.

In that case, let us make peace tomorrow evening at Shōgatsuya in Gofukumachi, Kameoka.

From six o'clock in the afternoon...'

Jiromatsu seemed vastly relieved; since two hundred yen had been reduced to fifteen yen, he also agreed without objection to disburse the funds.

And as for Usokatsu, when Kahachiya invited him to go to the banquet together the following evening, he politely declined on the pretext that sudden urgent business had come up with relatives in Osaka tomorrow.

With this, the disturbance of that evening was temporarily brought to a conclusion, and the following day, the three of them—Zuigetsu, Jiromatsu, and Chōkichi—were to go to a small two-story restaurant called Shōgatsuya in Gofukumachi, Kameoka.
