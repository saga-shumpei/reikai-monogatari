Chapter 12: 邪 Kami 憑 [1024]

邪神憑〔1024〕

§ 1

Kiraku, having gone to perform his ascetic practices at the Yada Waterfall, took the opportunity on his way back to briefly visit his aunt's house in Kameoka early in the morning.

His aunt was a devout follower of Inari, and at that time she was also somewhat infatuated with Tenrikyō.

It was precisely when she had heard that Kiraku had become possessed by a spirit and was thinking of going to see him once.

Accompanied by Saitō Uichi, when he visited his aunt that day, it had always been her custom to disparage him mercilessly, calling him 'Kirakubau Kirakubau' unceremoniously and saying, 'You are a good-for-nothing, a man without capability; my son has a fine physical constitution and is extremely clever.'

On this occasion, however, she changed completely; the moment he stepped into the entrance, his aunt came running over and said,

Oba: 'Oh my, Myōdaisama, you have been so good to come; please walk right into the sitting room and make yourself comfortable. I will prepare whatever you order for a feast. Would fried tofu be to your liking, or shall I boil some red bean rice? When the divine medium of Hyōtan-sama graces us with her presence, she fancies lightly salted amberjack, so she eats it raw, and she even consumes rapeseed oil. According to the gods, she can manage about five go of kerosene as well. What is your preference? Please order whatever you like; there is nothing more delightful than having the gods partake of food with zest.'

Thus she treated him completely like a medium who descended with Inari. Kiraku shook his head from side to side and said,

Kiraku: 'Aunt-san, even if a god possesses me, such vulgar things are not what they partake of; it is because the Tengu-sama of Mount Fuji has possessed me...'

Oba: 'If it is a Tengu-sama, all the more reason for him to partake of things! The Demon King of Mount Kurama always possesses Myōdaisama, and he partakes of anything and everything, and in the end even Hyōtan-sama possesses her, and when she sings and dances in a fine voice, it feels indescribably pleasant. If that is the case, your god must be a dead god, since he eats nothing...'

His aunt, who was always summoning badgers and foxes, seemed to think that spiritual possession meant eating anything and everything. In the vicinity of Kameoka, superstitious people always gathered to invite Inari-lowering mediums and perform what was called cold-season austerities. The women who became mediums on those occasions chanted syncretic Buddhist-Shinto sutras, shook gohei, and... saying 'I am the Inari of such-and-such a place,' or the Demon King, or Gorōsuke, or Tarōhachi, even badgers would come, and they would stuff everything into one person's mouth with all their might. Swallowing about three sho of red bean rice all at once without a care, eating fifty pieces of fried tofu without difficulty, ten pieces of raw bonito, twenty pieces of kamaboko, one sho of rapeseed oil, five go of soy sauce, along with rice and sake, they consumed quantities almost beyond imagination, and then emitting fine voices, they danced and frotted nimbly. At last, when the spirit-summoning concluded, they returned to their original flesh. Then those Inari mediums—most of whom were women—would say,

'Ah, I am terribly hungry. Shall I be served a meal?'

prompting it themselves and eating more than a single portion. Unless it was this kind of spiritual possession, it would not be prized in the Kameoka region. Since his aunt was always seeing this tradition, when he told her that the god possessing him ate nothing, she said,

Oba: 'There, that is your nerves. After all, it is not a god.'

saying such things, her attitude changed completely once more, and

Oba: 'Hey Kisō, wake up to your senses, come home quickly and go back to running your milk shop or farming. Who knows how worried your mother will be if you remain so sickly and frail. Shall I take you to the Tenri ō-sama in Hatagomachō right now and have them pray for you?'

she said with an air of kindness. Uichi had been listening to the two of them questioning each other with a blank expression, but thinking something or other, he silently and abruptly left the house. Kiraku, taking Uichi's departure as a stroke of good fortune, slipped away from his aunt's house, caught up with Uichi in front of Arazuka Village on his way back to Anō, and from there, on that very foot, they decided to go to the place of an Inari medium named Kichikichi in Teramura to investigate.

§ 2

At last they arrived at the house of Jiukichi Kotani in Teramura.

A man named Umakaha Namikaha served as the head of the caretakers.

Uichi was on familiar terms with Umakō, due to their kinship relations.

The moment Umakaha caught sight of Uichi,

Umakaha: 'Ah, is that you, Uichi-san? You have come most opportunely. I was just thinking of going to Anao to consult about something. Since last night, the divine possession has been extremely violent, and I simply do not know what to do; I was thinking of having the master of Anao calm it down. What do you think? Do you suppose he would come?'

he asked.

Uichi:

Uichi: 'This person is Kiraku-san; it would be well to ask him.'

Umakaha:

Umakaha: 'That is beyond my hopes! Oh, excuse me. Were you Kiraku-san? I humbly beg your favor in all things. Come, please step right through to the inner rooms.'

For a thatched rural dwelling, it was a fairly large house.

Led by Umakaha's guidance, the two passed through to the inner rooms, where an indescribably strange groaning voice could be heard coming from the adjacent room.

Umakaha pointed briefly in the direction of the voice,

Umakaha: 'Oh, Master Kiraku-sama, just as you hear, it has been groaning continuously for two or three days. Up to now, he has saved twenty or thirty sick people, so everyone believed in him, saying he was a mighty kami; but it seems he got a bit carried away, went mad, and is chattering on and on like this about disjointed, incomprehensible things. Is there not some way to cure him, I wonder?'

he asked with a worried look.

Kiraku hung his head, crossed his arms, and was lost in thought.

Uichi:

Uichi: 'Has he started groaning since two or three days ago? Ah, that is likely the work of a great tengu opening its mouth! Why, even when I was undergoing austerities at my place for three weeks, the house would roar and shake—ah, such tremendous things happened then. It was such a great commotion that my grandfather got angry and yelled at Kiraku-san to take his training ground elsewhere. At that time my stomach turned quite a bit too, but now that I have experience with divine possessions, a groaning voice like that is nothing at all. Even if it is not Kiraku-san, shall I go in and calm him down? Hey, Kiraku-san, what should we do?'

Kiraku:

Kiraku: 'Well, try doing it; if by any chance it does not go well, I shall step out.'

Uichi:

Uichi: 'Done! Kiraku-san, wait here... Hey, Uma-san, you come along too; I will act as a saiwain to open the tengu's mouth, and if it is an evil kami, I will place a reibaku upon it.'

Acting as if filled with conviction and in high spirits, he crossed a short corridor and proceeded toward the two-story annex, pointing toward the direction from which the groaning voice came.

After a while, a terribly loud and thick voice could be heard... I wonder if a wild tengu has appeared and is opening its mouth... thinking thus, he scooped some tea for himself and waited for the two to return, but no matter how much time passed, they did not come back. The groaning voice shouting 'Un, un' gradually grew more violent. The people of this house were terrified and had fled to relatives in the same village, leaving the place vacant.

As dusk approached, the corners of the tatami room gradually grew dim.

Crossing the narrow corridor and going to the living room where the voice was coming from, he saw that both of them had been caught in a reibaku the reverse way and lay completely stretched out flat. On top of that, Jiukichi remained possessed by the kami with his eyes rounded and glowing, looking just like Myōken-san raising the Namikirimaru treasure sword, with his bare arms full of bulging muscles shaped like the katakana character 'fu' right above his head, holding his left hand in a fist and pressing down ruthlessly on Umakaha's head.

Jiukichi:

Jiukichi: 'Hey, you evildoers, will you repent or not? You have been doing such and such with my wife, haven't you? Confess! Hey, Uichi, your nature is not very good either! The Great Sōjō of Mount Kurama has thoroughly investigated all your wicked deeds and is going to punish you. Now, what do you say?'
