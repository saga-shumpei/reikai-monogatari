Chapter 3: The Mikasa-maru [396]

第3章　三笠丸〔396〕

§ 1

With a sincere heart devoted to one's master, a pure red heart surpassing the color of autumn leaves, Teruhiko awaits the day he meets his father, as does Matsuyohime; as long as the bamboo field of the heart endures, the hardships piled high as mountains, and together with the precious Umegakahime no Mikoto, where the lonely crying sound of the bush warbler is heard as the valley door opens, crossing the river of Eden, while the world is at ninety-nine percent, crossing over foot-trailing mountains and traversing the plains, the company of four with encouraged hearts arrived at the port of Yoru in Africa, having exhausted all devotion.

Now the ship, catching the wind in its sails, was about to head toward the land of Chiri. The sailors shouted at the top of their voices that the time of departure was drawing near. Dozens of passengers scrambled to board ahead of one another. As Matsuyohime's party also finally boarded the ship, the Mikasamaru parted from the shore dripping with green leaves, and glided quietly across the surface of the sea, heading ever westward while plucking the koto of the waves.

The sun gradually slanted, and the ocean, so exceedingly vast, became a brocade mat of golden and silver waves, a truly extraordinary sight. The boatman raised his voice,

"Leaving the land of Tsukushi behind, Hurrying toward the capital of Uzu; The distance is three thousand and three hundred ri, A far journey across the waves,"

so he sang in a carefree manner. Just as the party looked back, gazing at the drifting clouds and overcome with longing for home, the sun finally sank below the horizon, the curtain of night fell, and upon the waves where black and white could no longer be distinguished, only the faint sound of the ship was heard across the gliding sea surface. At this moment, from a corner of the ship,

"Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, Whether the moon waxes or wanes, Though the sea of Tsukushi be deep, Though the heavenly sky be high, They cannot compare to the blessing of the Kami; Born from the breath of the Kami, Where is my mother now? My mother has departed to Yomi, Without any tidings upon the waves; Remaining behind, to search for The whereabouts of my father, Momogamihiko no Mikoto, Beneath the traveling sky; The Kami appears openly To distinguish good from evil; Kaminaobi who created this world, My father's sins and crimes are deep, Cleansing his pollution here on the rough shore, A noble dream, the Mikasamaru Departs from the capital of Uzu, Crossing the swirling ocean.

Parent islands and child islands here and there, In a world where many float, Parted in life from my only parent, Advancing through clouds and mists, Pitiable indeed are the three spirits of this body. Ah, Sumegami, O Sumegami, Broad of heart, through Ōnaobi, Look upon us favorably, and even for a moment, Most swiftly, permit me to meet My father, O Wadatsukami. Across the sea where the wind has calmed, Though the waves have subsided in tranquility, Like a fledgeling longing for its parent, The waves of my heart are turbulent; The waves of my heart are turbulent. Though I have heard it as the Kami's teaching That in all matters of the human world One should look again and listen again with Naobi, And recite away the afflictions of the body, Higher than the mountains and deeper than the sea,

"Offspring of the Kami of deep grace, to Momogamihiko, my father, hear peacefully and most tranquilly the heart that sings high with the voice of the plovers that pass to the paired-growth Awaji Island."

Thus a gentle woman's voice was heard. The vast sky of the deepening spring night was vaguely gloomy, and the stars were sparse, merely peeking through the opened curtain of clouds—one there, and three, five, ten here.

The passengers gradually drifted into deep slumber and went to sleep. The prevailing east wind ceased completely, the filled sail shrank and withered, and it was exceedingly silent. Four or five passengers, without sleeping, were beginning to chat.

Person A: "Hey, what was that gentle voice just now? It seemed to say something like, 'Whether the moon shines or is clouded,' but right now it is pitch-black. How could it shine or be clouded? What a person, talking nonsense."

Person B: "You are an uncomprehending fellow. That is a missionary hymn. It is not talking about tonight. That is a divine song sung by a missionary of the Ananai teachings. Listen carefully; it has quite a flavor."

Person A: "Even so, you, it said 'upon the sea.' It is speaking of right now. Are you defending it just because you think she is a female missionary? What a truly crafty fellow. Boarding a ship while attempting to board yet another ship—even if one harbors such an ambition, when ninety-nine percent is reached, it will be overturned completely. The other day when I was aboard the Asahimaru, a missionary of the Ananai teachings said that unless one reforms, one never knows when the ship will capsize."

Person C: "Do not say such unlucky things as capsizing or not capsizing inside a ship."

Person A: "No matter what ship it may be, it does not just go away forever. Eventually, it returns once. How could it not return? This morning, too, as I was leaving, the Otohime at my place said, 'Once your business is done, please return as soon as possible; go to the country of Chiri aboard the Mikasamaru, and make sure not to board a strange ship and contract syphilis, and please return quickly,' spouting such things and tapping this person's shoulder tap-tap. And then, 'Return quickly to my ship,' she said."

Person C: "Do not make a fool of people. What do you mean by Otohime? Treating a wife who looks like a dried fat-faced squash as precious—Otohime herself would be dumbfounded to hear it. You will probably return wearing a hat from the country of Chiri, do something strange, and make your wife's nose into a fallen princess; that must be the punchline."

Otsu: "Hey, hey, aren't there three beauties on board? If you talk such nonsense, they will lose all liking for you."

Hei: "What do I care if they lose liking for me? They are not ours to do with as we please anyway. You certainly still have an eye for women, don't you?"

Otsu: "Of course I do! How could there be anyone in this world without amorous desire, conceit, and greed? You have no greed; a no-good fellow is what they call an evil man."

"What nonsense are you spouting?"

Aiming for the voice, he struck him smack on the head.

"Ouch! Hey, are you picking a fight? These arms of mine have been exposed to the sea breezes of the Mediterranean. Come on, then!"

So clenching his fist in the darkness and taking aim, he swung a punch, whereupon,

"Eeeek!"

came a woman's voice.

"Well now, my apologies. That badger fellow used a stand-in, didn't he? Why don't you speak up? Isn't that cowardly?"

From the darkness,

Hei: "Having fallen into such cowardice myself, I cannot hold my head up either. It is not that I cannot even utter a mouse's squeak."

Tei: "He is just like a mouse, isn't he?"

Thus they were whispering. Suddenly from far out at sea, the sound of raging waves came rushing near, and in the pitch darkness where one could not discern what was right before one's eyes, the waves shook their manes and snapped at the gunwales; at this moment, once again in a man's voice, a refreshing propagation song was heard from a corner of the ship.
