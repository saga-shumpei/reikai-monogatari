第7章　地獄の沙汰〔400〕

§ 1

The ship at last safely arrived within the harbor of Teru at dusk. The lights of the houses, reflected in the water, hung down into the sea like pillars, just as if stakes of fire-pillars had been driven in; their shimmering and flaring state, swayed by the ripples, was the spectacle of fire-dragons ascending toward the water's surface from the bottom of the sea by the tens and hundreds.

Crossing the vast ocean desiring to see Teru, Is it the roar of the sea, or the sound of the waves?

So the boatman was singing in a calm voice.

Princess Matsuyo gently rose, parted her flower-like lips, and raising her slender hand as she looked out joyfully upon the harbor of Teru,

'Is this the famed harbor of Teru, Where days pass as in Takasago? Though there is no moon in the sky, The messenger of Princess Konohana, The flower blooming higher than heaven, The living spirit of Tsukiteruhiko, Guarded by Princess Tatsuyo, The land of Teru, also named Takasago. Joyful it is that here, traveling beneath heaven, We three companions of Moon, Snow, and Flower, Together with the brave warriors, The servants of Teruhiko who share the heart's color, Before our beloved father, Shall tell of the bygone days of old, Inexhaustible to recount, of our homeland, The tale of this visible world and the hidden realm, Fickle as clouds drifting across the sky. Father and child at last to be united, Awaiting the time like the pine breeze, Princess Matsuyo, Without a shadow in the bamboo field of her heart,

Conversing at every rising and lying down, The fragrance of plums whose buds also open, Plum tidings as a souvenir of our home. The cool moon, too, like Teruhiko, When the flower buds open, When the color of the heart wafts fragrance. Ah, how reassuring, how reassuring! This island where my father dwells Is a most dear island of the gods. However steep the mountain paths, However wild the sand plains where rough winds rage, What can deter a maiden? A sincere heart that pierces even rock, The gods surely shall approve. Like a cuckoo with thoughts of spitting blood, Crossing the ocean folds eight thousand and eight, At last, here, the hearts of the sisters Have cleared like the skies of May. Deliver but a single cry of greeting To my father Momogamihiko,

Who resides in the capital of Uzu; Report to his side, and convey The full measure of the heart of his noble child, Like the dripping green leaves of the summer mountains, Convey the full measure of my heart!'

Thus she gracefully sang. The Mikasamaru, having finally arrived at dusk, dropped anchor within the harbor and passed the night together with all the passengers. Once again within the ship, flowers of idle talk began to bloom:

Party A: 'Hey, this is the harbor of Teru; our lives are no longer in danger. Even if we brag and blow our horns to our heart's content, it will be quite alright.'

Party B: 'Do not talk so big. When that great wind was blowing, and the ship ran upon a reef, broke with a rattle and a crack right down the middle, and was about to sink, how were you then? Did you not seize my neck and tremble and shiver? Yes, indeed, when I told you to let go because clinging to me troubled me as well, you whined in weakness, saying, "I do not know how to swim, so if I sink, I will cling to your back and have you save me," and the shameful spectacle of you blubbering was simply not something fit to be seen.'

"There is no use talking about what has already happened; be quiet, be quiet, once we reach this island we will be fine. There is no danger to our lives, you see."

"No, when it comes to life, one cannot rest easy whether on land or at sea. Just recently, a splendid missionary named Momogamihiko from the land of Uzu, exceedingly bold, crossed Orochi Pass and Uzuyama Pass—where terrifying giant serpents appear—entirely alone; and while he was singing a propagation hymn of the Sankō Religion below the castle of Takatoribetsu in the capital of Haru, a camel caravan of hundreds appeared, and with sharp spears they repeatedly stabbed and tortured the missionary to death, as if skewering small taro corms or grilled tofu, and then buried him in the desert—this was the tale I reliably heard from a man who came out of the land of Haru when I boarded this ship. Because there are many wicked people, unless one is very cautious, there will be great danger."

Princess Matsuyo and her party listened intently to their conversation, their faces changing color; but Teruhiko swiftly rose from a corner of the boat, sat before these three men, and said,

"Pardon me, but listening just now, I heard that Lord Momogamihiko-no-Mikoto of the land of Uzu was killed in the land of Haru; is that true? We have come from afar for certain reasons, wishing to meet that Lord Momogamihiko-no-Mikoto. Would you kindly tell us what you have heard in as much detail as possible?"

Hei: "What, are you asking me to tell the tale of Momogamihiko? Well, it is not that I will not tell it, but as they say, 'Even the verdicts of hell depend on something-or-other,' and 'If the fish has the heart, the water has the mind; if the water has the mind, the fish has the heart,' after all. Heh-heh-heh."

"What you say makes no sense at all; please speak more clearly."

"Yes, by being vague, I intend to make my point. You know, 'Even the verdicts of hell depend on something-or-other.'"

"Ah, I understand; you are asking for drink money. Ah, very well, I shall give it to you. Please tell the story in detail."

"Yes, Mo-mo, Ga-ga, much obliged. Mi-mi, a large amount of money, all neatly counted out; hi-hi, it is just like picking it up from the ground. Hee-hee, declining politely would be my true intention, but ko-ko, this too must be some twist of fate—to meet such a person in such a place, aboard such a boat, to receive so much money; I hope such wonderful things happen many times in the future. Thanks to the tale of Mo-mo, Ga-ga, Mi-mi, Hi-hi, Ko-ko, I have come into such wonderful money."

"Hey, hey, that does not matter at all, so please tell us the news of Momogamihiko no Mikoto quickly."

"Even if you say let go, once I have received it, it is mine. Why, why should I let go, how could I bear to let it go, I will never let go until I die!"

"Hey, that is a little different; it is not 'let go', but 'speak' that I am saying."

"No, whatever you say, telling me to let go or speak of such a wonderful thing, that is cruel greed. Once I have grasped it, Mo-ka-mi, even if I bite into stone, Hi-ko, even if I am dragged along, I will not let go, I will not let go, how could I let go of this money that is more precious than life..."

"Ah, what a troublesome man; is Momogamihiko no Mikoto alive, or is he dead, which is it? I am telling you to let us hear it."

So he inquired, putting strength into his voice.

"That is one of two things. Those who are dead are not alive, and those who are alive are not dead. Momogamihiko is not dead."

"Does saying he is not dead mean that he is alive?"

"He is not dead."

"Is Momogamihiko no Mikoto not dead and living? Or do you mean he has died and is no longer in this world? Answer clearly."

So he inquired, sharpening his voice somewhat impatiently.

"As for that, sir, I only speak as much as... Since as much is as much, even judgment in hell depends on, well, whatever."

"Ugh, what an annoying fellow! I suppose you mean as much as the money; you must be saying that judgment in hell depends on money. How you crave money! There is no helping it, since you have seen my weakness. Here, I will give you this much, so tell me clearly."

The short summer night had fully dawned before anyone knew, and the ship was brought alongside the harbor.

"Momogamihiko was buried in the desert and died. Even if I told you what became of a dead fellow, it would amount to nothing. He has gone to a far, far away land hundreds of billions of realms distant."

Throwing out these words, he hitched up his garments, ran like the wind, and disappeared while still clutching the money.

Ah, what of the hearts of the three maidens? How was the inside of Teruhiko's heart?

『エヽ五月蝿い奴だ。金だけの事を云うてやらう、地獄の沙汰も金次第と云つて居るのであらう。よう金を欲しがる奴だなあ。足許を見られて居るから仕方がないワ。サア、これだけ貴様に遣るから判然と云へ』

短き夏の夜は、何時しか明け放れて、船は港に横づけとなる。

『桃上彦は沙漠の中へ埋められて死んで了つたよ。死んだ奴のあとまで云うたところで何にもなりはしない。十万億土の遠い遠い国へ行つて了つたのだよ』

と云ひ棄てて尻を捲つて韋駄天走りに、金子を握つたまま姿を隠しける。

アヽ三人の娘の心は？　照彦の胸の中は如何ぞ。
