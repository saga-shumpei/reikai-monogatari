Chapter 2: The Crossing at Eden [395]

第2章　エデンの渡〔395〕

§ 1

The three beauties—Matsu, Take, and Ume—at last reached the ferry landing of Eden. Matsuyohime faced the five men, her full cheeks flushed with a tide of crimson, and while smoothing the disarray of her glossy black hair before her moist, cool eyes, said:

"Excuse me, are you people of this village? Would you please be so kind as to ferry me to the opposite shore?"

Man A: "Oh, are you Tanabatame-sama, descended down the River of Heaven? Yes, yes, I shall gladly accompany you. It is not deep like the River of Heaven, nor is it high, so you are unlikely ever to fall into heaven. Come, come, Heavenly Tanabatame-sama and your companions, please come to my home."

"No, I have not come from heaven. Seeking my one father, I am on my way from the Holy Ground of Jerusalem to the Land of Uzu."

Man B: "Ah, so you are indeed a child of mortals. Because you are so beautiful, just now the five of us were wondering if you were a celestial maiden descended from heaven, or perhaps Tanabatame-sama. Ah, at a glance your age must be sixteen or eighteen, or nineteen or twenty in full bloom; it is truly a shame. And just now you said you are seeking your one father, but who might that father of yours be?"

"Yes, my father is Momogamihiko no Mikoto, who was formerly the Archangel of the Holy Ground of Jerusalem."

Man C: "What, the daughter of the utterly villainous Momogamihiko no Mikoto? Did a crow give birth to a crane, or a kite give birth to a hawk? What a strange world it is. Our younger sister was also kidnapped by a wretch who was a retainer of Momogamihiko no Mikoto, and to this day her whereabouts remain unknown; wondering what became of her, day after day we have kept our sister's location in mind, never for a moment forgetting her. When you think about it, she is part of the enemy. Ah, today I have come to feel quite strange; no matter how beautiful a woman she may be, hearing that she is the daughter of our enemy, bah, it is not amusing in the least."

Thrusting out his dark arm and extending his clenched fist before the three maidens, he said:

"Hey you! Thinking you were a deity, we were a bit taken aback. Furthermore, since you yourself volunteered the name of Momogamihiko's daughter, there can hardly be any doubt about it. Well, now that it has come to this, it is five rough men against three frail women. Floundering will do you no good now. Will you gracefully become our wives? If you dare shake that white neck of yours and say no, this iron fist will strike you square on the head. Well, what is your answer?"

Otsu: "Hey, hey, the more I look, the more beautiful you are; what a waste. In any case, you too must take a husband at least once, and whatever man you marry is a matter of karmic affinity. Don't you feel like becoming our wives? Hey, what, do you say no? Nod your heads obediently and say yes. Princesses, though we may look this frightening, we are still a man and women. When it comes to women, I am easily moved to tears. As they say, first black, second red, third white—a dark fellow has good flavor. Well, how about it? Let us hear your reply."

"Ohohoho, gentlemen, do you tease such an awkward woman? Please keep your joking within bounds. I do not know if my father was an evil person as you say, but I have no sin or guilt whatsoever. Fortunately we are three sisters in female form; 'in travel a companion, in life compassion,' and I have heard that there are no demons in the world. Please do not say such things to me, and ferry us across this river."

Hei: "'Please do not say such things to me, and ferry us across'—well now, what are you babbling? Saying 'Please ferry us across this crossing'—when we act as hard as stone, you act as soft as cotton; no indeed, putting on a gentle face, you are someone who cannot be handled with vinegar or konjac. Hey, everyone, it is bothersome to negotiate. Let us put these three wretches onto this boat, take them to the middle of the river, and hold a negotiation from which they cannot retreat. In any case, once we put them on the boat, they are ours. It is best to stop the boat in the middle of the river and negotiate at leisure. They say a woman's single-minded will pierces even rock, but a man's single-minded will pierces without saying a single word or half a phrase. If we take them into the middle of the river, easily changeable is a woman's heart; it is right before our eyes that you will say, 'It is a boat already boarded; ah, it cannot be helped, then I shall do as you say. Like this River of Eden, love me deeply, deeply.' The future of mankind changes like pools and shallows, like the Asuka River of yesterday and today, life unknown even tomorrow, an inch ahead is a world of darkness. If we can unite with such beautiful women even for the span of a single breath, it is the honor of a lifetime. Well, you three ladies, please board the boat, we shall take you aboard. In exchange, we too must be taken aboard by you, understood? Doing kindness, helping and being helped, the world goes around. Poling a boat through the floating world and crossing the violent river rapids must also be some karmic affinity. This is not the Sanzu River, but Ken'on-kyō, where flowers are lovely and fruit is abundant; indeed, you too shall become flowers of Ken'on-kyō and live harmoniously. If that happens, vengeance for the younger sister and everything else will be washed away into this River of Eden like an entirely settled account. Becoming completely open, as if having washed your backside in a flowing river, enjoy the blessings of Ken'on-kyō, pure and clear, without a drop leaking. A word spoken, a word returned; if the fish has heart for the water, the water has heart for the fish; despite how I look, I am a truly gentle man. Try living with a person, try straddling a horse, try riding a boat, as they say. Come, quickly, all aboard, all aboard!"

Takenohime, hesitating, said,

"Elder sister, younger sister, what shall we do? I am afraid."

"Elder sister, shall we give it up, and shall we go home already? Never since I was born have I met with such a dreadful experience. Ah, is there no one who might come to our rescue?"

Thus Umegakahime, showing sorrow, wiped her tears with her sleeve.

A: "Come, get on quickly, what are you dawdling for? Did you not ask us to take you aboard? We held various consultations, and finally decided to take you on board. Do you mean to say you will not board, setting aside a person's kindness? To talk of boarding or not boarding at this late stage—how could such nonsense be allowed? If you will not get on, so be it, but a man's resolve penetrates even rock; even if we must bind you tightly, we will take you on board."

Saying this, the five men were now about to resort to violence toward the three beauties. At this moment, a man wearing a light-blue coat with crossed cords tying up his sleeves appeared on the scene, out of breath,

"Hold on, hold on! When I say wait, you had better wait!"

A: "Wh-wh-what, why do you interfere, you blockhead? Coming here when we were ninety-nine percent done—waiting or not waiting is out of the question! If you spread your interference, your life will be forfeit!"

The one man laughed heartily,

"I am indeed the Great Tengu who dwells on the mountain of earthly teachings! If you utter foolish drivel, shall I wrench off your arms, tear your thighs apart, and fling you into the River of Eden?"

All of them said,

"What? Save your big talk for later!"

Thus each clenched their fists and struck from all sides, but the one man ran freely among the five, seized one by the nape of his hair and hurled him with a splash into the flow of Eden, and grabbing the next fellow who came by the neck, threw him in with a splash as before—a swift feat. The remaining three, wearing twisted headbands, attacked him furiously once again,

"Confound it, what a nuisance!"

and as he raised his foot and kicked him sharply, the fellow staggered and fell flat on the ground with limbs outstretched. The remaining two fled like the wind, running with extreme swiftness. The three—Matsu, Take, and Ume—felt as if they had met a Buddha in hell, appeared before the one man, and placed both hands on the ground,

Matsuyohime: "I do not know who you are, but you have saved us from grave peril..."

甲『さあ早く乗らぬかい、何を愚図々々してるのだ。乗せて呉れえと頼んだぢやないか。吾々は色々と評議をして、到頭お前たちを乗せてやることになつたのだ。人の親切を無にして乗らぬと云ふのか。この場になつて乗るの乗らぬのと、そんな馬鹿なことがあつたものかい、乗らぬなら乗らぬで宜い、男の一心いはいでも徹す、フン縛つてでも乗せてやるのだ』

と云ひながら五人の男は、今や三人の美人に向つて乱暴に及ばむとする。此時浅黄の被布に襷を綾取つた男、息せききつて此場に現はれ、

『ヤアヤア、待つた待つた、待てと申さば待つが宜からうぞ』

甲『ナヽヽ、ナヽヽ何邪魔をするのだ、唐変木奴が。九分九厘と云ふ処へやつて来よつて、待つも待たぬもあつたものかい、邪魔をひろぐと生命がないぞ』

一人の男はカラカラと打笑ひ、

『吾こそは地教の山に鎮まる大天狗だ。愚図々々吐すと、腕をむしり股を引裂き、エデンの河に投込んでやらうか』

一同は、

『何、その広言は後にせよ』

と、各自に拳骨を固めて四方より打つてかかるを、一人の男は縦横無尽に五人の間を駆廻り、襟髪とつてドツとばかりエデンの流れに向つて投げつけ、また来る奴を首筋掴んで、以前の如くドツとばかりに投り込む早業。残る三人は捻鉢巻をしながら又もや武者振りつくを、

『エイ面倒』

と足をあげてポンと蹴る途端に、ヨロヨロヨロとよろめき大地に大の字に倒れ伏す。残る二人は雲を霞と韋駄天走り……。松、竹、梅の三人は地獄で仏に会うたる心地して、一人の男の前に現はれ両手をつき、

松代姫『何処の方かは知りませぬが、危き処をお助け下さいまして……』

と云はむとすれば、男は大地に平伏して、

§ 4

"Oh, this is too gracious, Princess; I am Teruhiko. It was a perilous matter by just a single step. Surely the Kami has saved us at the ninety-nine percent mark. On this day alone, unexpected strength came forth from me. This is entirely due to the divine virtue of Kuniharutachi-no-Ōkami; let us all assemble here to offer our gratitude to the Kami."

"Ah, you are Teruhiko; how well you have come at such a good moment. We sisters are sorry to have departed without telling you, but thinking it pitiful to make you suffer the hardships of travel, the three of us sisters conferred together, and though we managed to come this far, crossing over the mountain ridges where tigers, wolves, lions, and giant serpents rage fiercely, at this lonely time, scarcely having caught our breath at this ferry, faced once again with an unreasonable demand from a rough man, at that very moment of being driven to extremity, meeting you is entirely the arrangement of the Kami; will you not please escort us to our father's country?"

Teruhiko,

"Yes,"

answered and prostrated himself. The two younger sisters, looking pleased,

"Ah, Teruhiko, you have done well to come. Come, let us all chant the norito."

Hereupon, the four masters and retainers sat upright on the grass by the roadside, clapped their hands, chanted the Amatsu Norito, and gave thanks for the divine favor.
