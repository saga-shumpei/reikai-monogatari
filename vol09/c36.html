Chapter 36: False Divine Possession [429]

第36章　偽神懸〔429〕

§ 1

Having abandoned his horse, Teruhiko, the deity Toyamatsumi-no-kami, who appeared leisurely in this place together with Haruyamahiko, was greatly surprised upon seeing the faces of everyone, and said,

"Oh, what a surprise! Sisters Matsu, Take, and Ume, I have met you in an unexpected place. Sisters, or rather, my mistresses, under the traveler's sky that concerns my heart day and night, wondering under which sky you might dwell, I looked up at the sky day and night, gazing at the course of the clouds, and was troubling my heart,"

and wiped with his sleeve the falling tears.

The three, Matsu, Take, and Ume, said,

"You are Teruhiko... no, Lord Toyamatsumi-no-kami, how wonderful that you have remained safe. All of this is due to the blessing of the deities of the Anana teachings who protect us,"

and were weeping tears of joy.

"Well now, Teruhiko—ah, not that, Lord Toyamatsumi-no-kami—this summer in the deep mountains of Chiri, you were of immense help to us. Indeed, how bitter was that time! Komayamahiko once held resentment, thinking you were not worth having as a friend, but upon reflection, everything is the divine arrangement of the deities. Even so, please be careful not to fall into divine possession anymore."

Teruhiko deliberately imitated divine possession, crying,

"A-a-a!"

"Oh, it has started again! If our shameful secrets are exposed before these seven beautiful goddesses, it will be utterly unbearable. Ah, please calm down today for the sake of everyone present!"

"A-a-a! A missionary of the Anana teachings, wandering in the wilderness all alone, what pathetic helplessness to choke in tears! I-i-i! Like a shriveled newt clinging and crawling, as if diving to the bottom of a well, terrified of Magatsu, barely escaping with his life to come all the way here, that certain missionary! U-u-u! Abandoned by Mezurashiyamahiko, unable to move at the bottom of the valley, how gloomy, how sad, like a cripple whose body will not obey for a hundred days and a hundred nights, does the cuckoo spend its days weeping? E-e-e! In high spirits and frolicking, advancing without looking front or back, blind to what lies ahead, that certain missionary! O-o-o! How laughable it was, how amusing it was! With a terrified face, left behind in the dark ravine, that foolish, certain missionary!"

"Kotodama again? You cannot match this Komayamahiko, who has mastered the subtleties of Kotodama. Very well, I too am prepared! Groaning and foaming at the mouth, with a fool's face, that certain missionary! Having lost sight of the three princesses, with his open mouth unable to close, glib-tongued, that certain missionary—what a pitiful state! I-i-i! Quibbling with needless logic, utterly loathed like briars by the people around him, that malicious, bogus missionary! U-u-u! Flustered and pacing, frantically raising an uproar over the princesses' trail, babbling delirium even in dreams, whether he told lies or not who knows, struck by frost, striking his head, whether dream, reality, or Santarō, put upon a horse and barely escaping with his life to come all the way here, that certain missionary! E-e-e! Subjected to harsh torment, his legs giving out, yet boasting pompously in front of others, o-o-o, that supreme fool and coward! Enough, enough, if you carry on with such foolish divine possession, nobody will listen to you anymore!"

Teruhiko,

"Ka, ka, ka!"

...thus began to utter.

"Oh, has the divine possession begun again? When this fellow falls into divine possession, it is persistent beyond words; like rotten sardines stuck to a net, it does not easily come off, causing great distress."

"Ka, ka, ka! Deceiving others by calling a crow a heron, walking about in disgrace—that missionary from somewhere! He says one thing and scratches his head; he is refuted and put to shame. A worthless missionary who wastes his one irreplaceable head. Stubborn through and through, a pest infesting dried bonito. Ki, ki, ki! He advanced ever northward, but was blown about by a fierce storm, had his life spared at a critical juncture, felt as if he would perish, and suffered a severe rebuke—that missionary from somewhere! Ku, ku, ku! With a dark face, smoldering, having forty-eight bad habits enumerated, weak-spirited and collapsed at the bottom of the ravine. Ke, ke, ke! Peculiar, monstrous, stingy, ko, ko, ko, jelly-spined! Deserted secretly by two men, utterly perplexed—the missionary Komayamahiko!"

"That fellow Teruhiko, does he mock me to the end? To pile up abusive words and violent speech before so great a crowd! He who does not keep the divine teaching of good words and fair speech is a vessel of Magatsu. Ka, ka, ka! I will not forgive you; are you prepared? Tit for tat! If you spout still more self-serving blather on top of this, I too have plenty to say. Ki, ki, ki! If you promptly and swiftly consent to what this person says, well and good; if you will not listen, I shall make you hear it whether you will or no! With an odd face like yours, speaking like a madman, hurting people, performing bizarre and preposterous Magatsu possession, and chattering only what displeases others—do you think that feels good? Displaying eerie gestures, what did you spew at the bottom of the ravine of Mount Chiri? Ku, ku, ku! Did I not tell you that your hardships were insufficient, so you must undergo more hardships? Possessing two legs, what is the idea of riding a horse just because hardship is painful? Ke, ke, ke! Holding the status of a retainer, yet abandoning your master, ko, ko, ko, smartly and pettily quibbling—that missionary from somewhere! Speak if you will; no matter how much you say, it will not affect me in the least; I am quite different from the Komayamahiko of the past!"

"Sa, sa, sa! Do not clamor, do not chatter! I have no ear to listen to contrary quibbles, as though you were drunk with sake; what a noisy fellow indeed! Shi, shi, shi! Pursued by ugly hags and Sagunome, su, su, su, gasping for breath, briskly fleeing—that missionary around here! As if having eaten a sweet bun in the privy, so, so, so, wearing an innocent face, stinking, stinking missionary!"

If you are too forward, shall I show you my t-tail, you stubborn fellow? Even if you think to a-attack Komayamahiko, who knows s-sour and sweet, th-that will not work. I am an autumn rat that does not fall for that trick."

"D-Do not strike, do not strike, do not strike your jaw. Shall I twist off your high nose and make you reform? A worthless fellow who, possessing n-neither power nor divine virtue, s-spouts one excuse after another, wearing a crestfallen face as if having had a cauldron stolen on a moonlit night, st-straddling heaven and earth, losing his way and bustling in confusion, a foolish-looking missionary from God knows where. Teruhiko: "Do n-not speak such lazy words, you missionary who was ch-chased all around by the minions of Uraruhiko, r-ran around escaping, put on an un-unabashed face, f-feigned innocence, and strutted about like a field rat."

"I am Komayamahiko; well now, wh-what are you babbling?"

So saying, he changed color and attempted to stand up. Miraculously, before he knew it, his body had stiffened, and from the neck down, it would not budge at all again.

"Hey, missionary of Komayamahiko—no, Hayamatsumi—how about standing up and leaping about?"

"Hey, you have cast the spirit bind on me again. I surrender, I surrender; no matter what, this missionary must yield to you. Once more, Lord Toyamatsumi-no-kami, please humbly forgive my rudeness until now. Ah, how eerie this is. Being forced into surrender is more than I can bear."

Teruhiko uttered a single "Hm." Hayamatsumi stood up, and said,

"Ah, with this, it is like giving an iron club to an ogre, and wings to boot. Now, shall I go from here to the Land of Tokoyo, pluck the feathers of Takatoribake, leap and jump and bound about, and become Hayamatsumi?"

The whole company raised their voices and involuntarily,

"Wahahaha, ohohoho,"

collapsed in laughter.

Haruyamahiko: "What a strange thing divine possession is! Lord Toyamatsumi, what kind of feeling do you experience during divine possession?"
