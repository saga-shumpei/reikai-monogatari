Chapter 6: Faith of an Instant [399]

第6章　刹那信心〔399〕

§ 1

The storm, roaring like hundreds and thousands of tigers and wolves howling and groaning all at once, sounded its ferocious voice and blew violently without the least restraint. The sight of the rough waves rising and approaching without any mercy was indeed a dreadful scene.

The Mikasamaru made strange noises, rattling and cracking, and was now about to sink to the ocean floor.

Many passengers, turning pale, stood up and sat down, running about madly within the confines of the ship.

The four—the maidens Matsu, Take, and Ume, together with Teruhiko—remained firm as a rock without the slightest agitation, and joining their hands toward heaven, earnestly prayed something.

Graceful as a flower, with eyebrows clearly drawn like the crescent moon, endowed with natural, beautiful features and looking like a peach blossom, Takenohime stood up resolutely, faced the blowing wind, and began to sing in a gentle voice:

"The heavenly wind that clears the defilement Of the dark world where black and white cannot be discerned, The seasonal wind that blows even now— Blow, blow, O wind of Shinato, blow! Blow away the darkness of the eternal world And purify the dust of this world. Rise, O waves, rise, Until the pines on the crest of Takasago are hidden; Take me to the side of my father Who resides hidden in Takasago. The recurring waves of the eternal world— Is the sound of them surging the pine wind, Or the mountain storm, or the voice Of my beloved father whom I yearn for? Like the white plum blossoms that open at once With all the fullness of the heart, With a face like a flower and eyebrows like the moon, The figure of Otomehime Enshrined in the Dragon Palace; The countenance of Konohanahime

In this sudden storm, O Ōtarashihiko No Kami no Mikoto, Tarashimahiko! Though it fall, let it rise; though it sink, Let it surface immediately, according to the divine will. This ship of divine salvation Is the Mikasamaru of deep blessings. When I look up into the sky, It is like Mount Kasuga, Mount Mikasa; Let this longing for the moonlight rising over the peak to clear Continue just a little longer. Cease for a while, O seasonal wind! Having heard by the wind that my father Dwells in the capital of Uzu, Understand the dew of the heart Of this maiden deity who has come seeking him.

Even if the ship should capsize And become the scum of the sea, This body received from the deities— How could it possibly die? O Shinatohiko, O Shinatohime, gently Subside with all haste! Ah, how precious, the Mikasamaru! Relying on the favor of divine grace, Taking the pure teachings as a staff, Carried aboard the ship of Miroku, We advance toward Takasago Island. So she sang, opening her flower-like lips.

At these kotodama, the rain, the wind, and the waves all stopped abruptly, becoming once again a tranquil ocean, and the glaring summer sun shone across the surface of the sea.

The Mikasamaru, which had run aground on a reef and almost sunk into the sea, was, wondrously, without any damage, quietly gliding across the calm surface of the sea and proceeding westward and westward.

Inside the ship, the whispering voices of four or five people were heard in a corner.

First Person: "That was a terrible thing. Thanks to the presence of the three Benzaiten aboard this ship, our lives were saved. Well, well, thanks to them we were saved from going to hell. An inch below is a water-hell; while atop a single plank like this is an easy paradise, yet if it is an inch off, it is hell, is it not? Considering this, we must think deeply. The deeds we perform daily are just like this ship. Since a single misstep leads to hell, one cannot carelessly navigate this world; no matter that one is on land, one cannot say one will not sink. Even if on land, if one commits evil deeds, one's heart sinks, one's body sinks, one's entire family and relatives all sink, and there remains no chance of rising again. One cannot live carelessly."

Second Person: "Indeed so; at all events, it was something to be grateful for. Those three goddesses are surely not humans like us. Since our hubris is so strong, if this world can be traversed by human strength, just try and traverse it. Even if powerless, dim-witted, foolish humans who cannot see an inch ahead boast arrogantly of conquering nature, opening the door of mystery, or seizing the wondrous workings of creation, spouting worthless sophistry—when confronted with waves like today's, even the boatmen whose daily trade is to steer ships can do nothing about it. Apart from the deities, there is not a single thing humans can accomplish in this world. The very fact that we are speaking words like this is because the precious water and fire of the deities dwell within us. Like the propagandists of the Ural faith, spouting arbitrary raving such as 'Drink, make merry, for an inch ahead is darkness, and after the darkness the moon will rise,' no matter how much they put on airs, as long as one is human it is futile; by all means, one must rely on the broad and profound grace of the deities. Ah, Kannagara tamachi haemase, kannagara tamachi haemase."

Hei said, "Hey, you have quite a good attitude. From whom and where did you hear such a fine thing?"

"Ever since I heard the teachings of the Ananai Religion, which Lord Haniyasuhiko no Mikoto who appeared on Mount Ōgon began, I have naturally become unable to drink the liquor I liked so much, and nowadays even if I take a little sacred sake, I quickly become pleasantly tipsy. Until now, the more I drank, the more I wanted to hop from drink to drink, and my temper would rise. I would get irritated over trivial things, strike my wife, throw sake bottles, break cups, and make bowls dance about, being a nuisance whom the neighbors called a nasty drunk and a madman; but somehow, when a missionary of Ryūgū Island named Iiyorihiko of the Ananai Religion came to the land of Kumaso, gathered everyone, and administered the baptism of soul-calming, my spirits cleared completely, I came to dislike alcohol, and somehow the world became joyful. It is truly a good teaching. Why do you not also convert to the Ananai Religion? In its greatness it is a teaching to govern the nation and bring peace to the world, and in its smallness it is a splendid path of teaching that serves as the foundation for cultivating oneself and regulating the family."

Hei, Tei, and Bo said, "Indeed, that is splendid. When we were safe and sound, we boasted, 'What, how could gods exist in this world? Humans are gods, humans are the lords of all creation, and the masters who govern heaven and earth'; but having encountered an ordeal like today's, even an atheist like me feels that somehow the evil lump inside the belly is about to leap out from the throat, and truly in times of helplessness one turns to the gods, feeling like putting one's hands together and clinging to them. Ah, human beings are such weak creatures."

Carrying the murmuring voices of the people, the divine-protected Mikasamaru crossed the waves of myriad miles and, at dusk of that day, approached the port of Chiri.

丙・丁・戊「なるほど結構だなあ。我々も無事安全な時には、何、神がこの世にいるものか、人間は神だ、人は万物の長だ、天地経綸の司宰者だと威張っていたが、今日のような目に遭っては、我輩のような無神論者でも、何だか腹の中の悪いコロコロが喉から飛び出しそうになって、本当に叶わぬ時の神頼みで、手を合わせて縋る気になってくるよ。ああ、人間というものは弱いものだなあ」

諸人のささやき声を満載した神の守護の三笠丸は、万里の波を渡って、その日の黄昏時、智利の港に近づいた。
