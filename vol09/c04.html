Chapter 4: Ōtarashihiko [397]

第4章　大足彦〔397〕

§ 1

The sea wind, which had blown violently in lordly fashion over the broad ocean, fearing neither heaven nor earth, suddenly ceased in the twinkling of an eye, and once again the passengers began to chatter noisily.

Passenger A: "Well, well, it gave us a reckless fright. That rascal spring wind swept across the flat surface of the broad sea, blowing up even the hem of Princess Otohime, and it seemed as though this precious vessel were the Mikasa-maru."

Passenger B: "There you go frolicking again. When the wind blows, you cling to the bottom of the ship and tremble, but as soon as the wind dies down, you jump right up like a gnat or a mosquito. If you do not keep quiet, waves like the ones just now will rise again."

"How could they not rise? When one sees a ship, it is only natural for the oar to stand up. Standing, standing, standing persistently and furiously. Kankan karatsuku, kan-kan. Furious rogue, the bonbora monk's ottokodokkoi, with a twisted headband on a shaved head, rowing and rowing away."

"What is that?"

"It is the sound of a ship, the sound of an oar rowing a ship. Do not press me so closely with your questions; it would be troublesome if we were overturned at the ninety-nine percent mark."

"What does 'hekaru' mean?"

"When I say 'hekaru,' it should be mostly obvious. Since it is unlucky, I reversed the word to say it."

"Overturning is even worse, is it not? A procession of frogs, you reckless drowned corpse!"

"A drowned corpse... This person tells me not to say 'kui-kui,' but the world consists of eating, drinking, and ***."

Passenger C: "Ouch, ouch, w-who is that, pinching my nose? It is exceedingly and utterly out of line!"

Passenger D: "Even without that, aboard a ship on the dark sea, to have your nose pinched and find it boring?"

Passenger C: "What? As if this were the Corridor of the Pines, do not make a fool of me! Since it would be utterly dull for me just to take it without giving back, shall I celebrate your nose as well?"

Stretching out a long arm, taking aim in the darkness, and saying that a voice came from around here, he pinches the bridge of the nose with merciless force and twists it hard.

Passenger D: "Ouch, it hurts! Let go, will you not let go?"

Passenger C: "As if I would let go! This nose, I shall not release even unto myriad kalpas to the end of time, unto the reign of Miroku. Hey there, my dear nose, is the relationship between you and me a matter of only yesterday or today's burial shroud?"

Passenger A: "Confound it, how ill-omened! What nonsense is a shroud for today or a shroud for tomorrow? Aboard a ship, one ought to celebrate good omens; what on earth do you mean by a Kyoto robe?"

Hei: "The Asuka River of yesterday and today, the crow that caws 'kawai, kawai,' the black crow takes a bridegroom, the oars taken row along with black faces, in pitch darkness where one cannot distinguish black from white, is this fellow's face charcoal or a charcoal ball? Pitch black, pitch black, pitch black.... Pinching my nose—do not make such a fool of me! My face is upon a lotus pedestal. Standing sharply and proudly high, Konohana-hime rests here. What do you take Lady Konohana for? In spite of how I look, I am not a te-te-tengu; I am a living god of Mount Tenkyō. 'One cannot know what sort of noble person has fallen here,' as the Ananaikyō surely says, does it not?"

Tei: "The high-nosed one along the way, whose nose alone is high and blocks his eyes, so that he cannot see above, still less can he see ahead, and beneath his feet is pitch black; only when he falls into a deep mire, gets covered in mud, and groans 'ahun' will his eyes be opened."

"From whom did you catch half-hearing such a thing? What a foolish fellow! That refers to you. Since only your ears go to paradise and you chatter like a shrike, surely when you die, your jew's-ear and herring roe will go to Takamanohara to become a cripple, and falling down to the Root Land will be your end!"

"Even if the jew's-ear and herring roe went to heaven, what of it?"

"What a fool! Your ears are 'listening ears' (kikurage) that only hear good things. They will dry up bone-dry and turn into jew's-ear mushrooms. And because without putting things into practice you only wag your tongue, your tongue will turn into dried food and become herring roe. When you go to the Root Land, you will be the kind of creature who cries and dries his weeping face, vomiting out: 'Ah, I was only listening to the splendid teachings of the Ananaikyō, but because I merely listened and did not put them into practice, did I come to such a place? Ah, ah, I have done—done—an irreversible thing!'"

From someone unknown, the faint voice of a song came heard.

B: 'Hey now, whoever heard of saying "a stray little boat"?'

'Ah, leave it alone, let it be! This is my parting shot. By a hair's breadth, I almost threw away my very life in those rough waves. Truly, if that wind had kept blowing until now, all of your lives would have gone completely to naught. Not even the sun deity could hear us. Bubbling and sputtering foam, by now we would have faced execution in the land of Ne and the land of Soko!'

The night faintly breaks into dawn. Across the immensely vast ocean, seagulls and albatrosses fly about here and there.

A: 'Hey! Plenty of your friends are right here—the albatrosses!'

At this moment, from ahead, dozens of large and small vessels with raised white sails came advancing toward them, the sounds of their sterns brave as they caught the wind.

『ヤアヤア捨てとけ、ほつとけだ。これも俺の捨台詞だ。スツテの事であの荒波に生命までも捨てるとこだつた。本当にあの風が今までつづきよつたら、貴様等の生命はさつぱりステテコテンノテンだ。テントウさまも聞えませぬ。ブクブクブクと泡を吹きよつて、今頃にや根の国、底の国の御成敗だよ』

夜はほのぼのと明け渡る。さしもに広き海原を、あちらこちらと鴎や信天翁が飛びまはりゐる。

甲『オーイ、貴様らのお友達が沢山においでだぞ、あはうどりが』

この時前方より、白帆をあげた大船小船、幾十隻となく此方に向つて、艫の音勇ましく風を孕み進み来るあり。
