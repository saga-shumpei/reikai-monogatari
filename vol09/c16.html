Chapter 16: The Octopus Snared [409]

第16章　蛸釣られ〔409〕

§ 1

Left behind in this ravine, where the blessings of the Kami are ever deeper, Komayamahiko gazed at the figure of Odoyamatsumi walking away,

"Hey! Hey!"

he called to stop him, but Odoyamatsumi turned a deaf ear to his voice, plugging his ears and walking down leisurely while singing a propagation song. The moon gradually emerged from the edge of the mountain and shone brilliantly across the scene, making the faces of the two clear. Looking closely, Teruhiko's countenance had suddenly altered, having transformed into the form of Mezurayamahiko.

Komayamahiko: "Hey, here I was thinking you were Teruhiko, but what is this, Mezurayamahiko of Kakatora? Do not take me for a fool; there is a limit even to jokes."

Teruhiko: "Su-su-completely unnerved, Komayamahiko the propagator. In such a cowardly state, how can the Way spread? How can the great Way advance? Even if you are clever only with your mouth like the master of sparrows and swallows..."

Komayamahiko: "Su-su-so what are you saying? Instead of playing such bad pranks endlessly, how about performing chinkon upon me and making my legs stand?"

"Su-su-unless it comes to crying 'Lo, an emergency!', your hips will not stand. What did you take Kakatora for, who knows all the sour and the sweet alike? A propagator lacking hardship, who ate ready-served meals as if told, 'We have grown the rice plants, pounded the rice, cooked the meal, so come and eat.' Completely deceived by Magatsu, your soul is full of openings; fools of a soul like yours must be few. To intend to go directly to Tokoyo-no-kuni, where Sukunahikona-no-Kami resides, is a bit too much presumption. Amassing the merits of ascetic practices deep in the mountains of this Hosotani River, receiving the help of the Kami to wash and purify your soul, polishing it into a heart like the sun and moon without the slightest blemish, becoming the valiant rebirth of Susanoo-no-Mikoto, paying care from the top of your head to the bottom of your body, and striding swiftly along the mountain path is your calling. With an upright heart, serve the Kami for all time. Taking the face of tonight's moon, clear from corner to corner, as the mirror of your heart, serve the Sumera-ōkami. Stand up with nimble legs and proceed smoothly upon the path of sincerity."

"Se-se-one after another, oh my, having received such strict admonitions, I am fed up. Although the world is vast and my heart is impatient, the more I hurry, the less my legs and hips will rise; if people of the world were to see me in such a state, they would be disgusted, abandon me, and my honor as a propagator would be utterly ruined."

§ 2

"So, so, so, indeed, indeed, what is that restless state of yours? You say there is no one about, but do you not see so many deities before your eyes? If you care so much about public repute, pull yourself together and purge that conceit from the bottom of your heart. Do not the clouds passing through the sky run freely? What a disgraceful state your legs are in; do you still not realize it? Why not slowly break your stubborn pride?"

"Well, you might as well let my legs stand by now."

"No, no, no, I will not let them stand, I will not let them stand. What an absurd matter, you preaching missionary who talks so much! Shall I chastise you until your breath is faint—nay, until you collapse on this very spot—break off your haughty nose, boil you, and eat you up? Will you become prey to the fierce beasts of the hills and fields, or shall I make mincemeat of a fool like an octopus with neither bones nor anything, or if you dislike that, will you repent at once? If you can repent, your legs will stand. Do not lose your temper; if you get angry, your legs will certainly not stand. To make a comparison, it is like the flowers on a lofty peak or the moon in the vast sky; unless you repent deeply, you cannot grasp the depths of divine sincerity. No matter how much is said, what can be accomplished with the two-edged sword of lead of a blunt, dull spirit that cannot understand reason and is pitiable? A lifetime spent weeping is one life, and a lifetime spent laughing is one life. One cannot shake sleeves that do not exist; one cannot wring wisdom that does not exist. Has your courage wilted? Pray anew to the spirit of the Naohi. The divine path is quite difficult; if you take it lightly, there will often be times when you weep again and again before your tearful face dries. If there is neither sin nor defilement, and no cloud upon the jewel of the heart, anything can be accomplished. That which is firmly rooted in sincerity will prosper for a long time. Though you have crossed over to this divine island of Takasago, whose very name is auspicious, the ascetic practice you have performed does not even equal the tears of a gnat among mosquitoes and tigers; in whatever you do, endurance is essential. A demon cannot be slain with a blunt sword. Unless you quietly calm the waves of your heart, taste hardships, and become a soul that does not fear even the bottom of the abyss, nothing will be accomplished. Things are accomplished not on the day they come to fruition, but on the days when they do not. Quickly attain the divine mind, Komayamahiko! Follow the path of the gods and traverse this world. Do not rely on worldly intellect or learning. Do not be misled by immediate greed. Do not seek direct grasp. Ponder deeply and advance. Correct what has deviated from the path. Do not let knowledge get to your small mind, put on a haughty face, and be laughed at. Ponder things a little, save the fallen human beings, and make the teachings of the mighty gods shine upon the world. Do not scatter your mind upon all things; blow away the dust of the heart, and put true wisdom to work. The moon is about to hide behind the edge of the mountains. Well, that rascal Teruhiko must also depart from this place. Komayamahiko, you had better train thoroughly from now on. Farewell."

§ 3

So saying, Teruhiko stood up swiftly and calmly prepared to leave this place.

Komayamahiko said, "W-w-wait, please. Just when I thought the moon had finally appeared in this narrow ravine, it hides again, leaving everything in pitch darkness. Being left behind all alone in such a place is more than I can bear. Hey, Teruhiko, it seems your divine possession has somehow calmed down. Won't you take me back with you?"

Teruhiko said, "There are no second words in the speech of the deities. Farewell."

And, once again singing a propagation song, he vanished into the darkness and departed toward an unknown destination.

Komayamahiko said, "Ah, what a wretched ordeal they have put me through. Putting me through an experience just like being tricked by a fox, for those two fellows to go off and leave me behind—ah, humans are truly unreliable. In times of emergency, the only thing that becomes one's staff, one's strength, and follows one everywhere is merely one's own shadow. And even that shadow does not follow one on a dark night. When it comes to this, humans too are wretched beings. Though called a master of the divine teachings, could there be such a miserable, bitter thing in this world? My lifeline is utterly severed; cast down to the bottom of a ravine where I have never been and where not even the moon can be seen, forced to sit upon the bare ground, whether it is for expiation of sins or whatever, the agony of having octopuses fished from me. Thinking it over deeply, is this the retribution for sins accumulated day and night? Prolonging this dew-like life, without staff, without strength, without connections, the pitifulness of weeping, suffering, and writhing. Told to maintain faith constantly, taught day after day as if being pressed, though I managed at last to become a missionary, it is truly an unfeeling transient world. Even if one speaks bold words, when it is this painful, it is utterly unendurable. Ah, becoming a missionary haphazardly, was it my afflicted month, the end of my luck? When I reflect upon it carefully, clouds to the moon and wind to the blossoms, in this bottom of the ravine filled with endless thoughts, am I to become prey to tigers and wolves? Ah, ah."

He babbled on, muttering this monologue.

At this moment, from within the darkness, a loud voice once again came resounding from nowhere.

Komayamahiko was made to kneel in this ravine for a hundred days and a hundred nights, accumulated the ascetic practice of fasting, received divine instruction day and night, and finally became a splendid missionary, changed his name to Hayamatsumi no Kami, and participated in the divine work at Yomotsu Hirasaka. And that Teruhiko was a divided spirit of a certain exalted deity, and later became Toyamatsumi no Kami.

駒山彦『アヽ、つまらぬ目に遇はしよつた。まるで狐に抓まれたやうな目に遇はしよつて、二人の奴、俺を置去りにして行くとは、アヽ人間も当にならぬものだ。まさかの時に自分の杖となり力となり、何処までも随いて来るものは自分の影法師ばつかりだ。その影法師さへも、闇の夜には随いて来てくれぬ。斯うなつて来ると人間も詰まらぬものだ。神の教の司と言ひながら、こんな拙ない、辛い事が世にあらうか。頼みの綱も断れ果てて、終に会うた事もない、月さへ見えぬ谷底に突き落され、地の上に坐らされて、罪滅しか何か知らぬが、蛸を釣られて居る苦しさ。ツクヅク思ひ廻らせば、日に夜に積んだ罪の酬いか。露の命を存らへて、杖も、力も、伝手も、泣く泣く苦しみ悶える浅間しさ。信心は常にせよと、毎日日日、詰めかけるやうに教へられて、漸く宣伝使になるは成つたものの、実につれない浮世だナア。強いことを言つて居つても、斯う辛うては到底忍耐れたものぢやない。アヽ、行きつきばつたりに宣伝使になつたのが、吾身の病み月で運の月かい。つくづく思案をして見れば、月に村雲花に風、尽きぬ思ひの此谷底で、虎狼の餌食になるのであらうか、アーアー』

と独言を言つてほざいてゐる。

この時闇中よりまたもや大声が何処ともなく響き来る。

駒山彦は、この谷間に百日百夜、跪坐らされ、断食の行を積み、日夜神の教訓を受け、いよいよ立派な宣伝使となつて、名を羽山津見神と改め、黄泉比良坂の神業に参加したり。而して彼照彦は、或る尊き神の分霊にして、後には戸山津見神となりたり。
