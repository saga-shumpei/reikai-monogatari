Chapter 28: Encounter in the Cave [421]

第28章　窟の邂逅〔421〕

§ 1

The cold moon shone brightly in the heavens, the wintry blast pierced the skin, and along the narrow path of the chilly night penetrating even to the bone, accompanied by a man, Umegakahime, a bud of a flower not yet sixteen or eighteen years of age, her weary feet tottering, was led to a certain neat solitary house in the shadow of a mountain. The man said,

"Until now, regarding it as a matter on the road, I have not even told you my name, but I am one called Haruyamahiko. Though it is truly a shabby hovel, please rest yourself at ease, and let me hear your noble teachings."

While greeting her thus, he quietly opened the gate door and welcomed Umegakahime inside. Haruyamahiko served a simple evening meal, and taking up chopsticks together with Umegakahime, finished the meal then and there with chazuke. Seeming to be Haruyamahiko's daughters, three maidens who would put flowers to shame cleared the table while bowing courteously. Haruyamahiko's wife, Natsuhime, appeared at this place, and offering a polite greeting, said:

"O missionary, that you should travel through the world for the sake of the Way, unsuited to your tender years, truly fills me with admiration. As you see, I too have three daughters, yet each and every one of them has been raised delicately; even in stepping outside the gate, they complain of the wind blowing, of catching cold, or of feeling shy, doing nothing but indulging in their parents' care; nevertheless, I am truly impressed by your valiant resolution. I, too, became a believer in the Ananai teachings around the beginning of this summer, enshrining the Kami and practicing the faith; yet at any rate, this place is the throat where one crosses from the island of Takasago to the land of Tokoyo, and the authority of Takatoribake, the Grand Minister of Tokoyo Shin'o, is powerful. A strict decree was circulated ordering that if missionaries of the Ananai teachings pass through here, every single one without exception must be bound and brought to Tokoyo Castle; hence, one and all, the people of this land are blinded by greed to receive the reward, searching day and night for the passing of missionaries. My husband Haruyamahiko is a man of strong faith; when he returned here from the land of Chiri at the beginning of summer, he heard the songs of three beautiful missionary sisters aboard the vessel Ataru-maru, completely abandoned the Ural faith he had observed until then, and converted to the Ananai teachings. However, if one openly professes faith in the Ananai teachings, it will incur the displeasure of Tokoyo Shin'o, and there is no telling what tortures one might be subjected to; therefore, we secretly built a rock chamber in the mountain behind us and enshrine the Kami within a stone cell. Since it is a fairly spacious room, would you not kindly chant the propagation hymns and divine words before the Kami at least once?"

"Thank you very much. Having been rescued from a perilous place, I have no way to return your kindness. If that is so, let me present divine words to the deity."

Natsuhime said,

"I shall guide you,"

and taking the lead, she crossed the back garden and accompanied her to the side of a wide cavern.

Inside the stone chamber, a song of propagation could be heard in a graceful woman's voice. Turning to Umegakahime, Natsuhime said,

"Come, if you will kindly push this door inward, there is a fairly large room where the great deity is enshrined. Please present your divine words and take a restful ease. Having a little business out front, I shall take my leave here,"

and concealed herself toward the main residence.

Umegakahime stood at the doorway, listening yearningly to the propagation song within the room.

"In the beautiful land / Protected by the deities of heaven and earth, / The human beings born therein / Flourish and prosper here and there; / Among them, praised as flowers of this world / In the precious palace of Takamanohara, / Were the three sisters, Matsu, Take, and Ume; / Day and night, longing and yearning / For the traces of their father, Momogamihiko, / The head of the deities, / Suffering in their hearts / Through rainy evenings and windy mornings, / Seeking to dispel the darkness of their minds, / On the third day of the flowering spring, / They departed from the city of flowers, / Crossing mountains and rivers, parting the waves / Of the vast ocean, and at last / Arrived at the port of Chiri; / From the port where the morning sun shines, / Placed aboard the ship of the great serpent,

A cuckoo crying across the sky, / The three sorrowful sisters, / Together with the accompanying deity, / Pure-hearted Teruhiko, / Beneath the May sky where irises give forth fragrance, / On the joyful evening of the fifth day, / Found the bond between father and daughters to be deep, / And aided by divine grace, / Met with their father at the Mansion of Uzu; / Joyful in meeting, the celebration banquet of longevity / Had scarcely been rejoiced over / When, in accord with the divine will, / Seeking to open the divine teachings, / The four men and three women, messengers of propagation, / Left their father's mansion behind, / Stood at the summit of the Chiriyama pass, / Looked back toward the capital, / Lingering in parting from their father and mother, / And set sail from the port of Haru; / Passing the border between Peru and Karu,

Leaving the port of Ataru behind, / Crossing the sacred mountain / Radiant with divine majesty, / Proceeding with light steps into the land of Karu; / Here the three sisters / Parted ways, each on her own, / Singing the propagation songs of the Anai teachings / As they went on, / Coming to the borderland; / At the time toward the end of autumn, / Blown by the wintry blast on the threshold of winter, / Suffering under the sky of their journey, / Oppressed by the inspectors under Takatoribeko, / When the breath of life was about to expire, / Rescued by the divine Haruyamahiko, / Possessing both flower and fruit; / Matsuyohime, enduring this world, / And Takeno no Hime are now here, / Looking into each other's beautiful faces—"

"Though I proclaim the words of the deity, what weighs upon my heart is Umegakahime. My younger sister traveling alone, at what distant end is she wandering, paining her feet upon her journey? Ah, my beloved sister! Ah, beautiful Umegakahime-no-Mikoto! Does the heart of your elder sisters, Matsu and Take, also drift upon the ocean of white waves, or wander at the edge of the wilderness? Is she oppressed by the evil deities, the wicked minions of the arrogant Takatoriwake, suffering through myriad torments? What I long to hear is word from my sister; how pitiful it is! Yearning to meet her, longing to see her, and feeling such dear affection, my heart clouds over. Kaminaobi, who created this world,

And Ōnaobi of vast heart—if only people would view anew and hear anew everything in the human world through Naobi! The more I ponder the teachings of Anaism, the more I recall my sister's whereabouts, without even time for my tears to dry. Ah, these tears of mine—may these tears rise to heaven and become rain, become snow, and wash the jewel of the hearts of worldly people! Where now is the servant deity of Teruhiko, upon whom I rely? Has he crossed over to the underworld island, the land of Yomi where evil deities rage, or is he wandering in the land of Tokoyo? Though I wish at least for word carried by the wild geese traversing the sky, in my present state of hiding from this world, I am utterly helpless.

O true deity! O Sovereign Deity! Please let me meet my sister and Teruhiko even a day sooner! I would fain meet them a day sooner! Ah, Umegaka, my sister! Ah, my sister, Teruhiko!"

Thus she was singing. Hearing this voice, Umegakahime was both astonished and overjoyed; she quietly opened the door and tumbled into the room.

"Ah, my beloved elder sisters!"

Having said only this, she was choked with joy, unable to speak, and did nothing but weep.

Matsuyohime and Takenohime, faced with this unexpected reunion of sisters, could not dam their tears of wild joy, and the three of them could only choke with tears of gladness in utter silence.

Just at that moment, a tumultuous noise was heard coming from the front. Ah, what is to become of the fate of these three maidens? It is an uneasy situation indeed.

心も広き大直日 唯何事も人の世は 直日に見直し聞き直し 三五教の御教を 思ひ廻せば廻す程 妹の行方偲ばれて 涙のかわく暇もなし あゝわが涙この涙 天に昇りて雨となり 雪ともなりて世の人の 心の玉を洗へかし 力に思ふ照彦の 下僕の神は今何処 曲神の猛ぶ黄泉島 黄泉の国に渡れるか 常世の国にさまよふか せめては空行く雁の 便りもがもと思へども この世を忍ぶ今の身の 何と詮方なくばかり

誠の神よ皇神よ わが妹や照彦に 一日も早く会はしませ 一日も早く会はせまし あゝ梅ケ香よ妹よ あゝ妹よ照彦よ』

と歌つてゐる。梅ケ香姫はこの声を聞いて、かつ驚きかつ悦び、静かに戸を開けて一室の内にまろび込み、

『あゝ恋しき姉上様』

と言つたきり、嬉しさに言葉詰つて泣くばかりなり。

松代姫、竹野姫は思はぬ姉妹の対面に、狂喜の涙堰きあへず、三人は無言のまま嬉し涙に咽ぶのみなり。

折しも表に当つて騒々しき物音聞え来る。アヽこの三人の娘の運命は如何になるべきか、心許なき次第なり。
