Chapter 13: Song of Farewell [406]

第13章　訣別の歌〔406〕

§ 1

The propagation emissary Odo-yamazumi, whipping the Komayama of his heart, and Uzu-yamahiko-no-Kami who advances, in the divine land shining in the morning sun, together with the three deities of Moon, Snow, and Flower, the Hime-no-Mikoto, and accompanied by the official of the retinue whose heart's color is Teruhiko, enduring the already hot summer day with straw raincoats and woven hats, their attire of straw sandals and gaiters appeared truly brisk and diligent; crossing over Mount Heguri, at the summit of Teruyama Pass the party finally arrived.

Odo-yamazumi: "I have sweated heavily. Shall we catch our breath for a while in the shade of the trees at this mountain summit?"

Uzu-yamahiko: "Of course; once we climb to a mountain summit, it is decided that we rest. This is an unwritten law that we propagation emissaries must keep. Matsushiro-hime-no-Mikoto and the other two ladies, once we descend this pass, the country of Uzu where your father resides will no longer be visible. It would be well for you to take a thorough look."

"Letting the eyes wander, mountains stretch and peaks overlap; the distant green brows seem to speak, and the near delicate charms seem to smile. Amid the mist and distant haze, looking down and gazing over the mountains and rivers, the surging mountain stream is clear as if exposing a white sash, and broadly, boundlessly it pours into the sea."

Komayamahiko: "Hey, Uzu-yamahiko, what is that? That is a strange poem."

"My poetry is strange poetry, funny poetry, amusing poetry, and also bitter poetry, cool poetry amidst sweltering poetry—a rare and peculiar poetry; it troubles me if you do not savor and appreciate my poetry. Although I look like this, I am a great poet. Praise be to the Great Merciful and Great Compassionate Kanzeon Bosatsu! Ahahaha."

Odo-yamazumi: "What is visible over there is the port of Hara; since in the country of Chiri the propagation emissary Sayorihiko is stationed, and in the country of Hirō the propagation emissary Momijihiko is stationed, how would it be if we directly arrived at the capital of Ataru from the port of Hara, and went swiftly straight to the country of Karu?"

Uzu-yamahiko: "That is so; although we have many places to go, going to the countries of Chiri and Hirō to propagate would be like wearing a woven hat on top of another woven hat. In any case, they are probably conducting propagation full of digressions, but even so, regarding the places through which new water has already run, let that be well enough, and let us travel smoothly by boat over the sea to the new country of Karu."

Komayamahiko: "Well, three goddesses, how are your legs? Well now, having the frail bodies of delicate maidens, you have come along with us very well. Truly admirable, admirable!"

Takeno-hime: "I am a youthful, delicate maiden, just as you say. I was worried whether I could accompany you or not, fearing that I might cause you trouble along the way; but thanks to the Kami, my legs unexpectedly carried me forward and forward, and I was not tired at all."

§ 2

"Elder sisters," said Umegakahime, "since this is the place where we part from the country of our father, shall we sing something once for our farewell?"

Matsuyohime said, "Let the three of us sisters sing a song and bid farewell to the country of Uzu."

Thus, the three began to sing together in one voice.

"Leaving behind the capital of Uzu, In the country of Uzu where our father and mother dwell forever in peace, Becoming the deities Ōkamuzumi, The three companions who have climbed up Onto the slope of this Mount Chiri. Is the wind blowing now an east wind? Bearing the deepest yearnings of our hearts, As the three of us yearn for our father and mother, Go, go forth to the country of Uzu! Fragrant is the wind in the sky of the deep mountains, Auspicious in color are the divine Momogamihiko no Mikoto And Satsuki-hime. Though destined to proceed toward Tokoyo, We hesitate to advance under the sky of Uzu. Clouds moving through the sky, if you have a heart, Take our thoughts and bear them To our father's side, as far as the blue clouds trail, To the very end where the white clouds settle and lie low.

Propagators of Pine, Bamboo, and Plum, Going forth to convey the divine teachings, Protected by the pure attendant deities of Teruhiko, Whose heart is radiant; Setting sail from the harbor of Hara, Crossing the ocean of rough wind and waves, Heading for the harbor of Ataru. Even our attire prepared at the border of Karu, Looking up at the sky of the land of flowers of the heart and eyes, To the country of Tokoyo, Treading over the Rocky Mountains, Crossing over the ocean once again; Subduing with divine words the forces of Yomi On the Isle of Yomi where Magatsu rages fiercely; Together with the deity Odochiyamatsumi, With the courageous Komayamahiko, And with Uzuyamahiko, Leaving great achievements to future generations, Higher even than Mount Fuyō, Far deeper than the Sea of the Dragon,

To manifest the divine light and blessings Ever more eternally! Ah, father, mother! Our sorrow of parting is endless atop the mountain. Unable to repay even one Of the blessings higher than the mountains, Forgive me as I depart, Pardon us as we go forward! In the human world, whatsoever happens, Revising with Naohi in seeing and hearing, Declaring afresh the transgressions of one's person: O divine teachings of the Ananai religion! Most peacefully and tranquilly, Declare afresh and purify my sins! Whether the morning sun shines or is clouded, Whether the moon waxes or wanes, Even should the great earth sink, The maternal affection of our mother— In what age could it ever be forgotten? In what age could it ever be forgotten?

The three companions of Pine, Bamboo, and Plum, With devout hearts, from atop Mount Chiri, Reverently worship from afar, Reverently worship from afar."

Having finished singing, they gracefully sat down upon the grass to rest.

Uzuyamahiko said, "Ah, truly you are women. That was a gentle song suited to women. That is quite fine, quite fine. Now, now, let us all proceed."

So saying, he took the lead and went down the Teruyama Pass.
