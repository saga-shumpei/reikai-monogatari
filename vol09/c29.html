Chapter 29: The Nine Maidens [422]

第29章　九人娘〔422〕

§ 1

The sixteenth-night moon of early winter is shining brightly in the sky.

Before the gate of Haruyamahiko, the two figures of Teruyamahiko and Takeyamahiko, leading numerous retainers and carrying thrusting-poles, man-catchers, jitte, and bows and arrows, broke through the gate, advanced, and shouted in a loud voice.

"Is Haruyamahiko at home?"

As they called out thus, Haruyamahiko quietly pushed open the gate door, saying,

"Well, well, I wondered who it might be, but it is Teruyamahiko and Takeyamahiko, both of you lords, bringing numerous attendants and arriving in this dead of night; you are most welcome indeed."

"Oh, we have not come as welcome guests today. Teruyamahiko has come with ill intent toward you. Pitiful though it is, as for today's duty, there are particulars to announce; guide us to the interior."

Haruyamahiko led the two into a room. Takeyamahiko turned to his numerous subordinates, saying,

"You all, surround this mansion. By no means let your guard down, any of you."

Having left these words, he adjusted himself into a formal seated posture, whereupon Haruyamahiko said,

"You are retainers of the god Takatoriwake; on what business have you come in this dead of night? I would be grateful if you would kindly tell me the nature of your business."

Teruyamahiko composed his manner, glared fiercely at Haruyamahiko, and said,

"Our coming today is for no other matter. We hear that while serving as the inspector of this Hazama country, you have defied the lord's command and secretly harbored the three missionaries of Ananaikyō: Matsu, Take, and Ume. Since we hold solid proof through the tip-off of the villagers, you cannot say yes or no. Struggling will avail you nothing now. Even if you offer millions of words in excuse, this Teruyamahiko will let them pass like the wind blowing through the empty sky."

Takeyamahiko, with a solemn and imposing demeanor, said,

"Now that it has come to this, your time is up; it will be well if you drag out the three women to this place as quickly as possible and hand them over; if you resort to hesitating over this and that, we will tie you up together with them, take you back to the land of Tokoyo, and make you confess even by torture. Come, Haruyamahiko, what is your answer?"

"Well, well, this command of Takatoriwake comes as a bolt from the blue; now that it has come to this, there is no help for it. I shall hand over to you here my three lovely daughters, irreplaceable in heaven and earth... no, the three missionaries whom I cherish like daughters. Since there are various preparations for that as well, I beg of you a delay of about an hour."

Teruyamahiko: "No, I will not fall for that trick. It is your scheme to delay things endlessly and flee like the wind from this house; since several hundred subordinates have been stationed around the mansion, there is not even room for a flea to leap out. It would be for your own good to hand them over immediately."

§ 2

"Nay, Lord Teruyamahiko, as you say, since there is no longer any fear of their flight, let us grant a respite of about half a double-hour and rest here to wait; I, Takeyamahiko, give my assurance."

"Then I grant a respite of half a double-hour. In the meantime, bring out the three propagators and line them up here."

Haruyamahiko felt as though a clamp were being driven into his chest.

"I have understood,"

he said, and while concealing his falling tears, he calmly departed from the place and proceeded into the annexed hall. Just as his wife, Natsuhime, was anxiously wondering how matters were, she saw the unusual expression on Haruyamahiko's face and said,

"What is the situation regarding the unexpected midnight messenger?"

Haruyamahiko, while heaving a sigh, said,

"Wife, I have a lifelong request to ask of you. Will you not grant it to me?"

"These are formal words indeed. As your wife, how could I disobey my husband's words? Command me anything, if it is something that can be fulfilled."

"Oh, Natsuhime, you have spoken well. Give me the lives of the three—Akizukihime, Miyukihime, and Tachibanahime—whom we husband and wife have raised with tenderest care over long years."

"What!"

"Does your silence mean refusal? Even the ferocious beasts of the fields and mountains, could there be any that do not care for their young? A pheasant in a burning field, a crane calling in the night—having raised them morning and evening with tenderest care, my three daughters of moon, snow, and flowers whose budding blossoms have just begun to open, it is my sole wish as your husband to offer them as substitutes for the propagators; please, I beg you, consent to this. In contrast to our three daughters, raised as sheltered maidens unable to work for the world, the daughters of the deity Masaki Yamatsumi no Kami, who dwell in the Capital of Uzu, have become propagators throughout the realm to work for the salvation of all living beings; considering their pure resolve, how could we possibly hand them over to Takatoriwake? Has the kindness we extended until now turned into our undoing? Ah, how can we escape the agony of this situation? Come, Natsuhime, let me hear your answer."

Natsuhime merely hung her head without making any reply, wiping her tears with her sleeve.

At this time, opening the door of a room, the three daughters appeared; having donned the garments of the propagators without anyone noticing, they said,

"Father, Mother, we three sisters will serve the purpose of the propagators and be taken away to the Tokoyo no Kuni. Uncertainty of age is the way of the world; please live in good health,"

and concealing their tears with their sleeves, they placed their hands upon the tatami mats and pleaded earnestly.

§ 3

The moment they laid eyes upon them, Haruyamahiko and his wife were deeply moved by the determination of their three children and were about to burst into tears all at once, but thinking, "Wait a moment, it would be a disaster if we were heard," it was painful for them to conceal their tears.

At this juncture, the missionaries Shōchikubai appeared and came forth, saying:

"We have heard the entire state of affairs without omission. Having received kindness vast as the sea and mountains, and over and above that, how unworthy we feel—how could we ever forget, in any world to come, your gracious intention to save us by putting forward your three lovely daughters, irreplaceable in heaven and earth, as our substitutes? Ah, we receive that intention a thousandfold, a myriadfold. Young ladies, how kind of you to have thought of us to such an extent. However, our role as missionaries is to save others; how could we bear cowardly and regretfully to deceive the enemy, use scapegoats, and hand over the three young ladies to the enemy? We thank you for your kindness, but it only pains our hearts. Having made your precious daughters stand as our substitutes, how could the three of us shamelessly go on living in this world? Please, we beg you, give up this idea alone. We shall go forth and proudly announce our names."

So saying, the three of Shōchikubai took the lead and were about to go to Teruyamahiko's living room, when the parents and children, five in all, clung to the missionaries, and Haruyamahiko said in a fluster:

"Oh, please wait. It is the cherished aspiration of my daughters; you have the mission of having to save this world for the sake of the Kami. For my daughters to stand as your substitutes is indeed the height of honor, and they will gladly be allowed to stand as substitutes. Please, grant my daughters' aspiration."

Thus he pleaded.

Teruyamahiko shouted in a loud voice:

"Hey, Haruyamahiko, the time has drawn near. Bring the missionaries out to this place at once. What are you dawdling about?"

So sounded his roaring voice.

"Yes, yes, please wait a moment. We are coming right away."

Takeyamahiko: "What a sluggish and unsettled business. Bring the three out here at once."

Haruyamahiko, having no other choice, appeared before the two of them and said:

"I shall bring them here right now. Please inspect them closely."

Takeyamahiko: "Oh, bring them out quickly. I have heard that there are three daughters in this house: Akizukihime, Miyukihime, and Tachibanahime. I, Takeyamahiko, know those daughters' faces very well, so do not miscalculate by trying to put forward substitutes and then cry about it later."

『マア待つて下さいませ。折角の娘が志、あなたは神様の為にこの世を救はねばならぬお役。その身代りに立つた娘は、まことに光栄の至り、喜んで身代りに立たしていただきます。どうか娘の志を叶へさして下さいませ』

と頼み入る。

照山彦は大音声、

『アイヤ春山彦、時が迫つた。早く宣伝使をこの場へ連れ出せ。何をぐづぐづ致して居るか』

と呶鳴り声。

『ハイハイ、暫くお待ち下さいませ。今直に参ります』

竹山彦『何をぐづぐづ埒の明かぬこと。早く三人をこれへ出せ』

春山彦は是非もなく、二人の前に立現はれ、

『只今これへ連れ参ります。よく御実検下さいませ』

竹山彦『オー、早く出せ。ここの家には秋月姫、深雪姫、橘姫の三人の娘があると云ふ事は聞いてゐる。その娘の顔をよく見知つたる竹山彦、身代りを出さうなどと量見違ひいたして、あとで吠面をかわくな』

§ 4

Haruyamahiko was driven to his wit's end, and wondering what to do, in his heart,

"Nodachihiko-no-Mikoto, Nodachihime-no-Mikoto, Konohanahime-no-Mikoto, pray protect us,"

he prayed with all his might. The missionaries of pine, bamboo, and plum appeared before him, and said,

"O emissaries of Teruyamahiko and Takeyamahiko, we are missionaries of Ananaikyō. Formerly born in Jerusalem as daughters of the flourishing Archangel Momogamihiko-no-Mikoto—Matsushirohime, Takenohime, and Umegakahime—we are now missionaries of the realm. Examine our faces well, take us back as quickly as possible, and claim your credit before the Divine King of Tokoyo. Well, Haruyamahiko, in what lifetime could we ever forget your devotion? Though we three are now captured and taken to the Land of Tokoyo, by the grace of the precious deities, we may meet you again. May your family and married life be harmonious and healthy."

Haruyamahiko, wiping away his tears, said,

"These are gracious words indeed from you missionaries; please return safely."

Teruyamahiko said, "Hey, what are you dawdling and whimpering about? Leave this place at once! Lord Takeyamahiko, examine them carefully."

Takeyamahiko looked closely at the faces of the three, and said,

"Oh, these are neither Akizukihime, nor Miyukihime, nor Tachibanahime. Unmistakably, they are the missionaries of pine, bamboo, and plum. Here now, Haruyamahiko, I pardon your crime of hiding these three missionaries until today. From now on, be careful and do not commit such an improper act ever again."

Saying this, he forced the three missionaries into palanquins, had his numerous retainers carry them, and departed while singing songs of triumph.

Haruyamahiko and Natsuhime burst into loud weeping and fell to the ground. Startled by this voice, the three daughters—Tsuki, Yuki, and Hana—and the missionaries of pine, bamboo, and plum rushed hurriedly to this spot, and called out,

"Oh, Father! Mother!"

"Lord Haruyamahiko, Lady Natsuhime!"

Addressed thus, the couple raised their heads and were twice startled; unable to comprehend whether it was a dream, reality, or an illusion, the husband and wife looked at each other's faces and were lost in thought.

Ah, of what deity were the missionaries of pine, bamboo, and plum who were just led away an incarnation? It was mysterious.
