Chapter 5: The Divine Manifestation on the Sea [398]

第5章　海上の神姿〔398〕

§ 1

Dozens of large and small boats, with the wind filling their sails, advanced majestically in formation. The Mikasa-maru, while going against the wind, proceeded forward with the loud sound of oars. On the large boat across from them, a noble goddess appeared standing at the bow; her cool eyes seemed as if dripping with moisture, her countenance was graceful and her figure delicate, and, filled from somewhere with majesty, she seemed to be praying something as she bowed toward the eastern sky. Beside her stood a great divine man of striking brows and a straight-bridged nose, whose skin was exceedingly fair, his sinews sturdy, and whose glittering eyes pierced people. Without prior agreement, the people aboard the ship all fixed their eyes upon this single deity.

"Upon the boundless waves, Leading forth the ships of salvation, To rescue and lift up the hundred souls That had fallen into the land of Yomi, Carrying even to the bottom of the blue ocean The divine teachings of the deity Konohana-hime-no-kami-no-mikoto, Who dwells on the towering mountain of Tenkyo, I am a missionary who proclaims and spreads them abroad, Serving at the side of the Great Deity Kami-Izanami-no-Okami, I am the Sun-Rise Divine Minister. Subduing with words the host of Yomi On the Isle of Yomi where the foul gather, The world now rests upon the waves of peace; Following the Sovereign Great Deity, Manifesting as a deity of salvation, Distinguishing good from evil, Kaminaobi, who made this world, And Onaobi, broad of heart;

Taking the soul of Naobi as a shield, The stern spirit and the auspicious spirit, The Aramitama that advances side by side, Fearing not the waves of the rough sea, The Nigimitama before which even the wind subsides, The Sakimitama that rescues the people of the world, The Kushimitama that illuminates the dark world-- Gathered here together, the divine work Of the deity Idunome-no-kami-no-mikoto Is higher than the mountains, A design deeper than the eight-thousand-fathom sea, A design deeper than the sea."

The singing voice, too, was obstructed by the wind, until finally only the sound of the waves was heard. Teruhiko listened intently to this song, tilted his head, and said,

"Pray, Lady Matsushiro-hime, the two deities standing at the bow of the ship that just passed by must be none other than the august Great Deity Izanami and the world-renowned Hinode-no-kami. Judging from the meaning of the faintly heard song, this can indeed be affirmed. Izanami-no-mikoto had departed for Ne-no-kuni and Soko-no-kuni, and we had prepared ourselves to believe that Her form could no longer be beheld in this world. Yet, to meet the Deity Izanami upon this unexpected ocean expanse--what a truly blessed thing it is! Ah, indeed, on this ship where you have set out in search of your father, when one considers that a deity who once departed for the other world has appeared again in this world aboard a ship and set forth pointing toward the east--wherever that may be--meeting your father is by no means hopeless. Nay, not only your father, but your mother too may be safe. What an auspicious day this is!"

と歌う声も風に遮られて、ついには波の音だけが聞こえた。照彦はこの歌に耳を澄まし、頭を傾け、

『もし松代姫様、今すれ違いました船の船首に立っておられる二柱の神様は、恐れ多くも伊邪那美の大神様と、天下に名高い日の出神様でありましょう。かすかに聞こえる歌の意味によって、確かに頷けます。伊邪那美の命様は、根の国、底の国へおいでになり、もはやこの世でお姿を拝することはできないものと、私どもは覚悟いたしておりました。それなのに思いもかけないこの海原で、伊邪那美の神様にお目にかかるというのは、何と有難いことでございましょうか。ああ実に、あなたが父上を訪ねて乗っておられる船の上で、あの世へ一度行かれた神様が、再びこの世へ船に乗って現れ、どこかは知りませんが東を指してお出ましになったことを思えば、父上にお会いになることは決して絶望ではございません。いや父上のみならず、母上もご無事でいらっしゃるかもしれません。何と今日はめでたい日でございましょう』

数十艘の大船小船は真帆に風を孕んで、堂々と陣容を整へ進み来る。三笠丸は風に逆らひながら、櫂の音高く進み行く。向ふの大船には、気高き女神舷頭に立ちあらはれ、涼しき瞳滴るが如く、楚々たる容貌、窈窕たる姿、いづこともなく威厳に満ち東天を拝して何事か祈るものの如くなり。傍に眉秀で鼻筋通り、色飽くまで白く、筋骨たくましく、眼光炯々として人を射る大神人立ちゐたり。船中の人々は期せずして此の一神に眼を注ぐ。

直日のみたまを楯となし 厳のみたまや瑞みたま 並んで進む荒海の 波をも怖ぢぬ荒魂 風も鎮まる和魂 世人を救ふ幸魂 暗世を照す奇魂 茲に揃うて伊都能売の 神の命の神業は 山より高く八千尋の 海より深き仕組なり 海より深き仕組なり』

と歌ふ声も風にさへぎられて、終には波の音のみ聞えけり。照彦はこの歌に耳をすませ、頭を傾け、

『モシ松代姫様、今往きちがひました船の舷頭に立てる二人の神様は、恐れ多くも伊邪那美の大神様と、天下に名高き日の出神様でありませう。幽かに聞ゆる歌の心によつて、慥に頷かれます。伊邪那美の命様は、根の国、底の国へお出で遊ばし、最早や此の世に御姿を拝することの出来ないものと、私共は覚悟致してをりました。然るに思ひもかけぬ此の海原で、伊邪那美の神様にお目にかかるといふは、何とした有難い事でございませうか。あゝ実に、貴女様はお父上を探ねてお出で遊ばす船の上で、あの世へ一旦行つた神様が、再び此の世へ船に乗つて現はれ、何処かは知らぬが東を指してお出ましになつた事を思へば、お父上に御面会遊ばすのは決して絶望ではありませぬ。否お父上のみならず、母上も御無事でゐらつしやるかも分りませぬ。何と今日は目出度い事でございませう』

§ 2

"Ah, when I beheld that noble figure, I felt an indescribably sublime feeling. And when I beheld the figure of that deity called Hinode-no-Kami, I felt somewhat drawn to him, and could not help thinking he was someone who knew my father's whereabouts. Could it be that my father is aboard that ship? Ah, what a strangely dear ship it is."

...she spoke, slightly clouding her expression. Takenohime said,

"Dear sister, in addition to meeting our father, could it be that we might also meet our mother?"

Umekagohime said quietly,

"In my usual dreams, I can always meet our father, but even when I meet our mother, she is enveloped in a strange mist and is not clear. By the grace of the deities, we may be able to meet our father, but meeting our mother will likely be uncertain."

The four, master and servants, were whispering such talk in a corner of the ship. Unable to bear the boredom aboard the ship, there were three young men pouring sake from gourds at their hips and exchanging cups with one another. Gradually drunkenness set in, and finally their tongues began to roll:

A: "Wh-wh-wh-wh-who is that? In the midst of this rough sea, going on about wanting to meet a dead mother, or being able to meet her—how inauspicious! Where do you think this is? It is the middle of the Pacific Ocean! It is neither the Sanzu River nor the Blood Pond Hell. If you want to meet a dead person so much, why not take a boat and cross over to the Blood Pond? It is not amusing in the least. The sweet sake I went to the trouble of enjoying has been spoiled!"

B: "You are the kind of fellow who, when you drink sake, always babbles and complains. Picking a quarrel over anything and everything, interrupting other people's conversation—why do you go muttering and looking for a fight? That lady, unlike a low-born palanquin bearer like you, has a countenance of an entirely different rank from the very start. What a useless scoundrel of a bearer you are, spouting nonsense; like a widow taking an open-air bath, she might well be laughing in her heart, thinking, 'He is just talking to himself.' That is precisely why I say I refuse to travel with you. What a nasty drunk you are. Like driving an emaciated horse over an African pass, when you are not drinking you blurt out 'Yes, yes, yes, yes,' doing nothing but breaking wind—truly a completely helpless, cowardly, worthless person; but once you drink sake, you act as though you rule the world. What are you babbling, you fellow who does not know your place? Just who do you think that lady is? She is the sheltered, precious daughter of the splendid deity who graciously served as the Archangel in the Palace of Jerusalem!"

「ああ、あの気高いお姿をわたくしが拝んだ時、何とも言えない崇高な感じがしました。また日の出の神様とやらのお姿を拝した時は、何となく心惹かれる感じがして、わが父上の居所をご存じの方のように思われてなりません。もしや父上は、あのお船にお乗りになっていらっしゃるのではありますまいか。ああ、何となく恋しい船だ。」

と少し顔色を曇らせながら語る。竹野姫は、

「お姉さま、お父さまにお会いできた上に、またお母さまにお会いできるようなことがございましょうか。」

梅ケ香姫は静かに、

「わたくしのいつもの夢で、お父さまにはいつも会えますが、お母さまに会っても、何だか妙な霞に包まれてはっきりいたしません。神様のおかげで父上にはめぐり会うことはできましょうが、お母さまに会うということは覚束ないでしょう。」

主従四人はこのような話を船の片隅でひそひそとしている。船中の退屈に堪えかねて、腰の瓢箪から酒を注いで、互いに杯を交わす三人の若者がいる。だんだんと酔いが回り、ついには巻き舌となり、

甲「だ、だ、だ、だ、誰だい。この荒い海の中で、死んだお母さまに会いたいの、会えるのと言いおって、縁起でもない。ここをどこだと思っているのかい、太平洋の真ん中だぞ。三途の川でも血の池地獄でもないわ。死んだ者にそれほど会いたければ、血の池へでも舟に乗って渡らないかい。まったく面白くもない。せっかくの甘い酒がまずくなってしまうわ。」

乙「貴様、酒を飲むと、よくぶつぶつ管を巻く奴だな。何でも彼でも言いがかりをつけて他人の話を横取りしおって、何をぶつぶつ喧嘩を買いおるのだ。あのお方はな、貴様のような素性の卑しい雲助のような奴とは、初めからお顔の格が違うのだ。なんだ、仕様もない雲助野郎が、わけの分からぬことを言って、寡婦の行水ではないが、『一人言っているわい』と心の中で笑っていらっしゃるのかもしれぬぞ。それだから貴様と一緒に旅をするのはごめんだと言うのだ。酒癖の悪い奴だな。アフリカ峠で痩せ馬を追うように、酒を飲まない時には『はい、はい、はい、はい』と吐かしおって、屁ばかり垂れて、本当に進退きわまる腰抜けのつまらぬ人間だが、酒を飲むと天下でも取ったような気になって、何をほざくのだ、身の程知らず奴が。一体あのお方をどなたと思っているのか。恐れ多くもエルサレムの宮で天使長をお勤めあそばされた結構な神様の箱入り娘さまだぞ。」

甲『タヽヽヽヽ誰だい。この荒い海の中で、死んだお母さまに会ひたいの、会はれるのと言ひよつて、縁起の悪い。ここは何処だと思つてるのかい、太平洋の真中だぞ。三途の川でも血の池地獄でもないワ。死んだ者に、それ程会ひたきや、血の池へでも舟に乗つて渡らぬかい。クソ面白くもない。折角甘い酒がマヅくなつて了ふワ』

§ 3

"Wh-wh-wh-what, a sheltered maiden, does a sheltered maiden speak? Do not talk such foolishness. If it is sheltered maidens, I have plenty of them at my place. Not only maidens, but sheltered sons, sheltered grandfathers, sheltered grandmothers, and even sheltered cows, I have properly kept them all."

"You have mistaken it; you must mean a doll box."

"That is right, it was dolls. As if dolls could speak!"

"Oh, that doll reminded me: they say in this sea there is something called a mermaid, with the head of a human and the body of a fish. It is said that if you catch it, cook it, and eat it, you will not age even after a thousand years or ten thousand years. I would certainly like to have one."

Hei: "Do not speak so loudly. It will spoil my precious drunkenness. Standing right in front of people's eyes—at least do not block my view. For a while now, I have been repeatedly beholding the faces of those three princesses with crescent-moon eyebrows and flower-like beautiful forms, quietly enjoying them as an accompaniment to my drinks. If you stand in such a place and babble noisily, you get in the way of my eyes."

Just as they were speaking thus, a strange noise was heard at the bottom of the ship. All together,

"Oh, what is it?"

they stood up all at once in astonishment. The boatman, in a powerless voice, said,

"Guests, everyone, prepare yourselves. It is no use now."

Kō: "N-n-n-no use, wh-wh-wh-what is no use? Are the b-b-b-beauties saying n-n-n-no?"

The boatman shouted in a loud voice,

"The ship has struck a rock! Strip naked and jump in; it is sinking, it is sinking!"

Everyone inside the ship instantly turned into cries of agony and lamentation. Ah, what will be the fate of the party of graceful maidens, the Pine, Bamboo, and Plum?
