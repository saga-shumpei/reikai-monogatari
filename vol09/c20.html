Chapter 20: The Mirror of the Soul [413]

第20章　心の鏡〔413〕

§ 1

Illuminated by the bright moonlight of the thirteenth night of the sixth month, the three maidens of Pine, Bamboo, and Plum—with their smooth facial skin, moist corners of the eyes, willow brows, crimson cheeks, and snowy skin remarkably striking and beautiful—possessed an unspeakably sublime countenance, like the full moon of the fifteenth night, or accumulated snow reflecting the moon of a winter night, or peach, cherry, or white plum blossoms, or red-crowned cranes descending to disport in a rubbish heap. The three goddesses stood at the bow and, clapping their hands toward the surface of the sea, sang gracefully in gentle voices:

"The deities appear in the open To distinguish between good and evil. Kaminaobi, who created this world, And Ōnaobi of vast heart: In all matters of the human world, Re-examine and rehear uprightly, And correct through declaration the errors of oneself. Born of the stern breath Of the Sumegami who created this world, The green human grass is the living shrine Where the deities dwell in serenity. The souls of humans from the very beginning Were never clouded or defiled. Receiving the breath Of pure, noble heaven and earth, Because they were born as children of the deities, In Thy exceedingly vast divine heart, Correct every transgression through declaration, And bestow salvation, O Amatsukami, Kunitsumikami, and the myriad deities.

O Kotohirawake no Ōkami, Forgive their transgressions deeper than the sea, Place them aboard the divine ship, And pray save them Upon the island of Takasago where flowers bloom and fragrance spreads. The waves of his heart having subsided, The sky of the heart of Toragimi, who repented of his sin, Is like a clear mirror; Re-examining it in the divine heart Of Tsukiteruhiko of pure radiance, Save their souls, and restore them To the pure souls of Teruhiko; Save the two children, Kuma and Tora, who fell into the sea; Save the two children. The Sango Teachings are the divine Way That purifies the defilements of the present world And saves the sorrows of humankind. Not to mention the birds and beasts, Even unto the realm of insects,

Moistened by the dew of the teachings, The divine heart purifies and clarifies The vast seas in all four quarters of heaven and earth. By the blessing of the great divine heart, Grant salvation, O noble child of god. Blowing away the defilements of the bodies And the dust of the hearts Of the people sinking in the rapids of grief, In the land of Teru where the morning sun is pure, In the realm of daytime where there is no night, The light of the sun and moon leaving no dark corner, Save them unto the harbor of Ataru; Save them unto the harbor of Ataru."

Having thus sung and recited the divine words, they returned once more to their original seats. Whispered talk began again inside the vessel.

A: "What do you think of the spirit of that divine possession and song just now? It was truly fearful, yet grateful, and magnificent indeed. Listening to those words of divine possession, every single one struck home to me, and I felt as though I myself were being rebuked."

B: "Indeed, it is the same with us. It is not only an evil person like Toragimi. When I place my hand on my chest and reflect, there is an evil fellow inside our bellies too, and I cannot help feeling that we are being dragged in secret toward sin, ever toward sin."

§ 2

Hei: "Ah, once the lid is opened, anyone is much the same. It is only a matter of whether one commits evil openly like Torakō, or does not. Among those with gentle faces who look like good people who would not kill a single flea, there are rather truly wicked fellows. Among those who are despised by people as evil persons, there are rather good persons; and among those who pretend to be saints and gentlemen and are acclaimed by the world as wise men and good people, there are rather evil persons. What is called a devil uses the body of a good person as a vessel, disguising itself to do evil deeds. Those who are praised by the people of a world in which evil has prospered to ninety-nine point nine percent are surely evil. Among those who are attacked by the world as wicked fellows, there are true good people. That fellow named Torakō was a considerably notorious villain, but it seems his true inborn nature was that of a good person, for being unable to bear the pangs of remorse, did he not cast away his precious life? Humans are indeed children of God. As the saying goes, 'When a bird is about to die, its cry is sorrowful; when a man is about to die, his words are good.' We too would like to clear away the clouds of our hearts as quickly as possible, acquire hearts as beautiful as tonight's moon, and make our way through the world."

Tei: "However, haven't strange things been happening these days? Upstream of the waterfall of Arina there is a large cavern, and a mysterious pond called the Mirror Pond is said to have formed there. It is said that a strange-faced man named Sayorihiko, a missionary of Sango-kyō, came there and has been administering baptism to numerous people; and all who received baptism there became splendid people, doing no evil, having no quarrels, suffering no burglaries, and becoming able to sleep peacefully at night without even locking their doors. We too wish to receive baptism at least once. Under such circumstances, it is now said that another great missionary has appeared at the Tamagawa waterfall in the land of Hiru. Beside that waterfall, too, there is a strange cave, and God speaks and lets people hear various matters, so it is said. I have come thinking to visit there once, but if you like, why not go together with me?"

Kō: "Is that so? Such a thing is happening? In fact, we received baptism from that missionary named Sayorihiko. Until now, we drank heavily, fought, and often made people weep; but when a strange voice of God was heard from within that Mirror Pond and all that we had done up to now was exposed, the terror of that moment makes our hair stand on end even when recalling it. That we subsequently received the missionary's baptism, lived harmoniously as an entire family, and have now come to be valued by the villagers is entirely thanks to God."

§ 3

Otsu said, "That is well and good, but I wonder what has become of Torakō. Since the three beautiful missionaries have just prayed, God, being of supreme benevolence and love, will surely save him, but he is truly pitiable."

Tei said, "There is no need to worry about that; God will surely save those who have reformed. By the time this ship reaches the port of Ataru, he will surely be waiting on the pier, properly saved by the Dragon God, in a manner as if saying, 'Everyone, excuse me for going ahead of you.'"

Hei said, "I wonder if such a convenient thing could really happen. If the two of them should be saved, our whole village will follow the teachings of those missionaries."

Kō, Otsu, Hei, and Tei were whispering and talking about divine virtue. Chin'yamahiko remained silent, listening smilingly to the conversation of the four.

The Ataru-maru at last arrived safely at the port of Ataru at the hour of itsutsu on the following day. On the pier stood Torakō and Kumakō, waiting to welcome the ship.
