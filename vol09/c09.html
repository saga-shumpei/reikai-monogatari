Chapter 9: The Mandarin Duck Coverlet [402]

第9章　鴛鴦の衾〔402〕

§ 1

Both the distant heavenly skies and the earthly soil, in a world that turns left and right, where parents and children have reunited, the fifth-month darkness in the sky of their hearts clearing into a joyous summer day; stroking her black hair dripping with green, entering with news of her father, Matsuyo-hime; in the joints of the bamboo of her heart accumulating her feelings, the delicate child, opening her lips like a flower bud, Umegaka-hime, the noble daughter; on the third day of the third month, slipping out of the mansion in Jerusalem, a company of three; Teruhiko of the moon, snow, and flowers, master and servant leaving the capital with Tatsuyo-hime; at last, here where the divine soul of Tsukiteruhiko is enshrined, the presiding deity of the capital of Uzu, administered by Momogamihiko,

They arrived at the mansion of Uzu. The shade beneath the trees of the fifth-month sky, evergreen trees that will clear on the fifth day, this evening of the fifth day of the fifth month; the reunion of father and children, and of husband and wife, the cups going round and round, her face like the moon, Satsuki-hime; pine, bamboo, and plum for a thousand ages and eight thousand ages, becoming the foundation of prosperity, echoing afar. The voices of the banquet resound here and there; in the capital of Uzu and across the land, the liveliness is such that it seems to shake.

Masakayamatsumi no Kami had concluded his wedding ceremony with Satsuki-hime, and in the midst of the banquet alongside the three deities Okitsuyamatsumi, Komayamahiko, and Uzuyamahiko, upon hearing that his most beloved daughters from his homeland—for whom his heart had ached morning and evening—Matsuyo, Takeno, and Umegaka, had come to visit, just as he was choked with tears of joy, the party appeared in this place guided by Kunihiko. The chests of the three daughters tightened with joy, and faster than looking upon their father's face, the three bowed their heads all at once; had there been no people beside them, they would have leapt to embrace him and weep together, and the parent's heart held the very same feeling; Momogamihiko also was choked for a time with tears of joy, unable to utter even a single word, and the banquet seats that had been lively until now took on, somehow, the damp atmosphere of a fifth-month rain. Uzuyamahiko said:

"Well, well, auspicious events truly accumulate when they do! Today is the fifth day of the fifth month, the Festival of Irises. Just as Satsuki-hime—a missionary who passes through the dark world where black and white cannot be distinguished, illuminating it, and who may be likened unto a heavenly maiden—has bound the vows of lifelong fidelity with Momogamihiko no Mikoto, who is comparable to the peach blossoms of the third day of the third month, the company of three of the Mizu no Mitama—the noble daughter Matsuyo-hime who celebrates the age of the Pine of Miroku, Takeno-hime of upright heart, and Umegaka-hime, like a plum blossom just opening as the teachings of Anaimikyō unfold across the universe all at once—have had their reunion of father and children; how auspicious a matter this is! And what is still more auspicious, that Teruhiko, who bears the name of the deity Tsukiteruhiko, accompanies them—what a wondrous combination this is! Ah, with this, the joy of the plum of Ōshukubai, the flower-haze of the peach grove, and the fifth-month darkness will completely clear, and the divine age of Miroku, where the sun and moon shine across the skies, will draw near. Satsuki-hime, whom he first met on that night when the full moon of the fifteenth shone—even if her father's name was Yamiyamatsumi, now that things have come to this, he is Teruyamatsumi. Everyone, in order to celebrate this joyous occasion of today, shall we sing of the auspiciousness of parents, children, and husband and wife intimately united?"

§ 2

Odoyamazumi said,

"That is truly splendid. Please, as the initiator, do sing first."

Upon being requested thus, Uzuyamahiko said,

"Then shall I act as the herald and begin?"

and, unlike his strange shrill voice up to now, raised a beautiful voice with lingering resonance and began to sing.

"The morning sun shines bright, the moon waxes full; The deities of heaven and earth are roused. The true deity appears, Peach blossoms on the third day of the third month. The flowers are crimson, the leaves are green; Upon the pine-clad mountain dripping with green, The cuckoo comes to sing amidst the green leaves, Crying out its eight thousand eight calls. The joyful clearing of the fifth-month sky; With joy in their hearts, in the spring of three thousand years When flowers bloom, Momogamihiko-no-mikoto, Brother and sister, wife and husband, The eternal joy of mother and offspring, The reunion of the parents and children, five in all. On this evening of the fifth day of the fifth month, Since the distant age of the gods, The world that shunned the twilight as ill-omened Has transformed, and now at twilight The joy filling heaven and earth Is peerless, like upon a waterfall. It is as if pure water were endlessly gushing From the Mirror Pond. Matsushirohime celebrating the divine age, Umegakahime blossoming all at once, Noble deities, and Takenohime Growing swiftly and vigorously. Leaving behind the noble capital, The moon shines upon the capital of Uzue, The land of Uzue under lofty Takasago skies. The tree blossoms of Uzuyamahiko Reach ever higher and commune With the divine deity Takateruhime. Placed aboard the serpent ship, Here the four divine persons, Masters and servants, parents and children gather; Uniting their hearts for all time, Making the stern spirit the warp, Making the gracious spirit the weft, Causing the teachings of the full moon of the fifteenth day To shine through the four quarters of heaven and earth; Shine through the four quarters of heaven and earth."

When he finished singing, Odoyamazumi-no-kami opened his mouth once more and sang a congratulatory song.

"Peach blossoms of the third day of the third month, Nurtured and cultivated since three thousand years ago, The peaches of the garden. The peach fruits offered to you, The hearts are in spring at this banquet. The irises of the fifth day of the fifth month, Satsukihime of delightful fragrance. The great sky clears, and beneath the shade of the pines Upon the ridge of Takasago, As an old man and an old woman, Cross through this world purely and forever. Heads covered in white hair like deep snow, Beloved daughters of Pine, Bamboo, and Plum. Even though the world is pitch-dark, The sun and moon are in the sky, Teruhiko, Dazzling in radiant light, a handsome couple. The crane dances its thousand years, The turtle dances and sings for ten thousand generations. In the land of Uzue of Akitsushima, Five sons and three daughters, and Satsukihime. Ruled for a thousand generations by Kunihiko, Awaiting prosperity is indeed auspicious; Awaiting prosperity is indeed auspicious."

Uzuyamahiko said,

"Oh, auspicious, truly auspicious! Listen here, Satsukihime, from now on you are the splendid mistress of this house, and these three young maidens are your true children. Without having endured the pain of childbirth, how joyful you must be to have as children such splendid heavenly goddesses whom one cannot tell from the moon, snow, or flowers! Destiny is a wondrous thing: it is the ordinary match of the world for a beautiful woman to be wed to an ugly husband, or for a foolish man to marry a beautiful woman. Yet you have as your husband Masakayamazumi-no-kami, a missionary of the Ananai teachings who combines wisdom, benevolence, and valor, lacking not a single defect. With the warm dream of the mandarin duck pledge between a handsome man and a beautiful woman, for husband, wife, parents, and children to lead a pure life like flowers, like the moon, and like snow—there can surely be no greater happiness in all the world than this. It is said that in love there is no distinction between right and wrong, beauty and ugliness, wisdom and folly, but your love is complete. The eternal joy that has both the flowers and the fruits of the peach and the iris is so great that words pledging love for countless generations fail on the lips. The newly shared pillow beneath the mandarin duck quilt is truly auspicious, most auspicious."

Satsukihime said,

"Thank you,"

and with just this single word, flushed and looked down slightly. Uzuyamahiko said,

"Excuse me, Satsukihime, you are the blossom of this evening. Shall we have you sing splendidly?"

Satsukihime stood up bashfully, gracefully moved her long sleeves, and began to sing and dance.

五月姫は耻かしげに立ち上り、長袖淑やかに歌ひ舞ひ始めたり。
