Chapter 32: The One-Stringed Zither [425]

第32章　一絃琴〔425〕

§ 1

The reverberation of the rock-boat that had thundered in the sky had faded away before anyone knew, and though it had become the beginning of winter when the sound of the cold blast blowing through the winter trees was mournfully heard, inside the mansion of Haruyamahiko-no-Kami, which was bright with spring-like warmth, the clear tone of the plectrum on the pure string of the one-stringed zither, whose sacred words blessed the deities and reached between heaven and earth, cleansed the five viscera and six bowels. Just at that time, a man stood before the gate, holding one hand to his ear; wondering whether it was the storm upon the peak in early winter when the cold wind rages, or the pine wind, or the sound of the zither of someone paying a visit, Komayamahiko-no-Kami reined in his horse of mind and listened, which was itself the beginning of a bond, to the sound of the zither that resonated with his heart.

Matsushirohime of the evergreen pine and Takenohime of deep thought were prominent, and how delightful was the melody echoing outside! It was the pastime of the noble Umegakahime, blossoming all at once, and guided by that voice of indescribable grace, he spoke to himself.

Komayamahiko: "Incomprehensible is the condition of this mansion; the timbre of the one-stringed zither that Umegakahime plays daily; this mansion of Haruyamahiko which seems to hold some hidden circumstance. Though the steed of my heart runs eagerly forward to advance inside and discover the truth of the matter, I am separated by the hedge of public gaze, and helpless on this winter day, what shall I do if I am yelled at and driven away by a short-tempered gatekeeper? Whether a foreboding informs me, somehow I can hardly depart from this gateway. If I knock with the true teachings of the deities, the innermost chest shall open; shall I try knocking, or wait a while? This place is the dwelling of the chieftain of the village of Haruyama, a wicked deity who follows the teachings of Uraruhiko. Although it is easy to pacify and harmonize with words, this is a petty detail before a great mission; if I consume time on trivial matters and fail in my crucial duty, what excuse could I offer before the deities of heaven and earth? Ah, how spiteful is Uraruhiko! Shall I open it and enter, or shall I remain without opening? It is like a precious treasure box that brings regret once opened."

Pounding firm the sacred pillar of his soul and whipping the steed of his heart, he raised a resolute, loud voice.

"The missionary of the Ananai-kyō, Komayamahiko, is none other than myself. Haruyamahiko, who aligns with the evil spirit Takatoriwake of heinous villainy, open this gate!"

Thus clenching his right fist, he struck and pounded upon the leaf of the gate as if it would break. Startled by the sound, Matsushirohime, hearing that voice at the gate which sounded somehow familiar, said:

"Takenohime, Umegakahime, though it is a trouble to you, go forth to the gateway and discover what sort of person it may be."

"Yes."

Answering thus, the two slowly stood up and proceeded to the gateway.

"Who are you that knock upon the portal? Your voice is somehow endearing and familiar to our ears; pray declare your name."

When they called out thus, Komayamahiko responded from outside the gate:

"Ah, the voices I hear are those of Lady Takenohime and Lady Umegakahime; I am indeed the missionary Komayamahiko, who parted from you in the land of Teru. Being missionaries of Ananaikyō, why do you two ladies conceal yourselves in the mansion of a crooked deity who follows the Ural faith, Haruyamahiko, a vassal of the utterly wicked and unprincipled Takatoriwake? There must be a deep reason for this; please explain everything in detail without concealment."

"As there are profound circumstances behind this, pray enter first of all,"

they said, removing the bar of the gate, opening it to the left and right, and appearing; taking Komayamahiko by the hand, they proceeded further and further inside. The two maidens supported his hands and said,

"Ah, welcome indeed, Missionary Komayamahiko; in this wild plain where evil spirits rage, you must surely have been in great distress. Pray enter first of all,"

saying so, and pushed the gate open with a rattle. Komayamahiko said,

"Pardon me, then,"

and as he proceeded straight into the parlor, unexpectedly Lady Matsudayohime and the members of Haruyamahiko's household were in the very midst of presenting offerings from the mountains, plains, rivers, and seas before the Great Imperial Deity, comforting the god; with an expression of suspicion, Komayamahiko said,

"Unexpected Lady Matsudayohime, and Master Haruyamahiko, the master of this house: although I heard that you were the village head of Haruyama, following the Ural faith and flattering Takatoriwake, the sight of this place where you reverently worship before the altar the Great Imperial Deity venerated by Ananaikyō and comfort the divine will is beyond my comprehension; please recount the whole matter in detail without concealing anything,"

as he pressed him, whereat Lady Matsudayohime said,

"You are Komayamahiko; having had no news of you since our parting, in bitter seasons of rain, wind, and frost, I have been anxious about your safety, and how wonderful it is that you are safe. Truly shamefully, we three sisters were caught in the plot of Takatoriwake, and just when our lives were in peril, we were rescued by the deeply compassionate divine couple of Haruyamahiko; that we have met you at the critical moment when we are now about to leave this place is an inscrutable providence of the deities; please convey deep gratitude to the couple on my behalf."

"Both our meeting after so long and Haruyamahiko's submission are an unexpected divine arrangement. Ah, worthy couple, how very kindly you have cared for them; I am deeply grateful,"

he said, and even the stout-hearted Komayamahiko wrung his sleeves wet with tears of joy.

Haruyamahiko opened his mouth for the first time and said,

"This country created by the deities is a paradise of blooming blessings; under every sky, we firmly erect divine pillars and guard it; how great is the joy of the parents and child, the family of Haruyamahiko, who have been chosen as those pillars. We are rather overwhelmed by your thanks; please protect our hearts eternally and unchangingly, four missionaries."

§ 3

Beginning with his wife Natsuhime, the three of Tsuki, Yuki, and Hana pressed their hands together like maple leaves, wept tears of joy, and prostrated themselves before the noble figure of Komayamahiko in admirable devotion. Wishing to clear the sky of their hearts clouded by tears of joy, Komayamahiko sprang to his feet and said:

"Aiming to strike down The Kototamawake director Of the divine army attacking The snow-gleaming Kōhaku Mountain, At the command of Tokoyohiko And Tokoyohime, I led a multitude of divine troops; Just as we attacked, Shattered by the fierce cannonballs Hurled down from the Rock-Ships And Bird-Ships roaring through the great sky, How could I endure it? Just as the thread of my life was about to extinguish, Scooped up in the net of compassion Of the Sovereign God who saves this world, I prolonged my precious life; Within the ship Mikasa-no-maru, Rescued by Hinode-no-Kami, The shining morning sun, I became Hayamatsumi-no-Kami, A missionary who saves this world.

Abandoned in the depths of the deep mountains, Enduring the suffering Of a hundred days and hundred nights At the bottom of the heart-darkened valley, Here my heart crossed the land of Chile, Trusting my feet to the land of Peru, Repeating night journeys, The thousandfold sins made light as the sky, Advancing ever northward, Crossing over the Hazama forest, Worrying over how the missionaries Of Pine, Bamboo, and Plum fared, Guided by the threads of fate, Though it was winter, standing at the mansion Of Haruyama, peering at the state Inside the gate, To my ears came the familiar Ichigenkin, Its kotodama ringing clear, The timbre of the koto echoing purely, With a clinging heart at the doorway.

As I lingered, behold the noble figures Of Takenohime and Umegakahime; Unimagined on this day, This meeting granted by God's permission! O Haruyamahiko, O Natsuhime, O three daughters, Tsuki, Yuki, and Hana! May the long-prospering age of the pine endure, Like the pine, bamboo, and plum forever, Unfolding the teachings before God. How joyous, how joyous, how delightful! To the deeply benevolent Nodatihiko, To Nodatihime and Konohanahime, For the supreme grace of the divine spirits, I offer my deep gratitude from afar."

Thus he related the tale from beginning to end; the face of Komayamahiko, filled with surging valor, appeared courageous even to the eyes of onlookers.

妻夏姫を始めとし月、雪、花の三人は紅葉の如き手を合はせ、嬉し涙にかきくれて駒山彦の英姿をば伏拝むこそ殊勝なれ。嬉し涙に掻曇る、心の空を霽さむと、駒山彦は衝立ち上り、

『雪に輝く高白の 山に攻め来る御軍の 言霊別の司をば 撃つて捨てむと常世彦 常世の姫の仰せにて 数多の神軍引率し 攻むる折しも大空を 轟き来る磐船や 鳥船よりは投げ下す 激しき弾に砕かれて 何かは堪らむ玉の緒の 生命消えなむ折柄に この世を救ふ皇神の 情の網に掬はれて 惜き生命をながらへつ 三笠の丸の船中に 光り輝く朝日子の 日の出神に助けられ この世を救ふ宣伝使 羽山津見の神となり

深山の奥に捨てられて 心も闇き谷底の 百日百夜の苦しみを 凌ぎて此処に村肝の 心も智利の国を越え 足に任せて秘露の国 夜の旅路を重ねつつ 千座の罪もカルの空 北へ北へと進み来る ハザマの森を乗り越えて 松竹梅の宣伝使 如何ならむと煩ひつ 縁の糸に操られ 冬とは言へど春山の 館に立ちて門内の 様子窺ふ折柄に 耳に馴染の一絃琴 その言霊も澄み渡り 琴の音色も清々と 縋る思ひの門の口

佇む折柄竹野姫 梅ケ香姫の御姿 思ひもかけぬ今日の日の 神の許しのこの対面 春山彦よ夏姫よ 月雪花の三娘よ 栄え久しき松の代を 松竹梅の永久に 教も開く神の前 嬉しし嬉し喜ばし 御恵み深き野立彦 野立の姫や木の花姫の 神の命の御高恩 遥に感謝し奉る』

と始め終りの物語、勇みに勇む駒山彦のその顔、他所の見る目も勇ましき。
