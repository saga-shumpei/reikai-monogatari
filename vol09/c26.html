Chapter 26: The Dance of the Whirling Crest [419]

第26章　巴の舞〔419〕

§ 1

Presently, the sound of the storm blowing down from Mount Takateru and the great roar of the stone door gradually subsided, until afterward only the sound of the thousand-jō Kotodaki Falls cascading down was heard murmuring. From within the cavern, once again a voice like the blowing of a bamboo pipe sounded:

"Shikakō, listen closely to what this Orochihiko says. A sudden, eleventh-hour conversion will not serve. Like twisting a rope only after catching a thief, it will not avail you in time of need. Take what this kami says thoroughly into your heart, be reborn as a true human being, listen well to the teachings of the kami, and devote your strength to the world. Evil schemes are barren blossoms. Their color will not last forever. Such flowers do not flourish, nor do they bear fruit. Having been born into this brief world, do not lose the eternal life of your spirit. Taking after the evergreen trees whose branches and leaves flourish, regain the ever-green pine-tree mind, the wise mind; become gentle-hearted, draw close to the kami, and associate with people. There are no barren blossoms in the teachings of the kami. Incline your ears, calm your mind; the more you listen, the more divine virtue you will acquire. It is fine indeed to endure hardships for the sake of the world and for the sake of sincerity. Never, never display a mind of the body ruling the spirit as you have done until now. Sweep clean from the bottom of your heart, be aided by the grace of the deities Kaminaohi and Ōnaohi, and become a model for the long-flourishing pine-tree world. Whether in life or death, the human body is embraced within the bosom of the kami; simply entrust yourself to the kami. Pray with all your sincere heart. Meekly repent and perform refreshing deeds. Clear away and purge that bad name, having been called an oni or a demon by the people of the world. In the true wind of the teachings of the kami, at the bottom of the valley of Mount Takateru, have your fat drawn out with all your might, your bowels washed, and your gall tempered, and only then become a strong human being who destroys the demons of this world. Understand the deep mind of the kami of heaven and earth, and without distinction of far and near, without division of dark and light, perform pure and righteous deeds such as will catch the eye of the kami, who surveys the world at a glance. In all matters, taking the heart of the kami as your own, devote sincere heart to the world, help the weak, bring forth the majesty of the kami into the world, and like this Kotodaki Falls, make a pure name resound in all four directions."

Shikakō: "Ah, indeed, your thorough, careful, and gracious words have pierced me to the bone. Astray in boundless greed, clouding my mind day after day, I have done nothing but insolent and improper deeds. I pray that through the vast and deep mind of the kami You may graciously re-pronounce and re-hear, and forgive our deep sins. Ah, now with this I shall take my leave."

鹿公『いやもう、何から何まで抜目のない、入念でありがたい仰せ、骨身にしみました。果てしない欲心に迷い、日ごとに心を曇らせ、不埒で不都合なことばかりして参りました。どうぞ神様の広くて厚い御心によって宣り直し聞き直して、私どもの深い罪をお許しくださいませ。ああ、もうこれでお暇を頂戴いたします』

折から高照山より吹き下す嵐の音も、岩戸の大音響も、次第々々に鎮まりて、後には千丈の琴滝の落つる音、淙々と聞ゆるのみ。巌窟の中より又もや竹筒を吹いた様な声がして、

『鹿公よ、この大蛇彦の申す事を確り聴けよ。俄の改心は間に合はぬ。盗人捕へて縄を綯ふやうな事ではまさかの時の間に合はぬぞ。この神の申すこととつくりと腹に容れて、誠の人間に生れ変り、神の教をよく聴いて、世の中の為に力をつくせ。悪の企みは仇花だ。何時までも色は保たぬ。花は栄えぬ、実は結ばぬぞ。短い此世に生れ来て、永い霊魂の命を失ふな。枝葉も茂る常磐木の、何時も青々松心、賢しき心を取直し、穏かな心になつて神に親しみ、人に交はれ。神の教に仇花はない。耳を傾けて心を落付け、聴けば聴くほど神徳がつく。世界の為に誠の為に、苦労を致すは結構だ。決して決して今までのやうな体主霊従の心を出すな。心の底から掃除して、神直日、大直日の神の恵に助けられ、栄え久しき松の世の鑑となれよ。死んでも生きても神の懐に抱かれた人間の身、只神に任せよ。誠心を籠めて祈れよ。素直に改心いたして涼やかな行ひを致せ。世間の人に、鬼よ悪魔よといはれたるその悪名を雪げよ、祓へよ。神の教の誠の風に、高照山の谷の底で、力一杯膏を抜かれ、腸を洗はれ、胆を練られて、始めてこの世の中の悪魔を滅ぼす強い人間となれ。天地の神の深き御心を悟り、遠き近きの隔てなく、暗き明きの分ちなく、世界一目に見渡す神の眼に止まる様の、清き正しき行ひをして呉れ。何事によらず、神の心を心として、世界の為に誠心をつくし、弱き者を助け、神の威勢を世に出して、この琴滝のやうに清き名を四方に轟かせ』

鹿公『いやもう、何から何まで抜目のない、御念の入つた有難き仰せ、骨身にこたへました。果しなき欲心に迷ひ、日々に心を曇らせ、不埒な不都合な事ばかり致して来ました。どうぞ神様の広き厚き御心に宣り直し聞き直して、吾々の深い罪をお宥し下さいませ。アヽもう是でお暇を頂戴いたします』

§ 2

From within the cave,

"Not yet, not yet; you must not return."

Kumakō: "Hey, Shikakō, be patient a little longer."

Shikakō: "Well, well, well, they say 'Not yet, not yet, ah, not yet.' Well, what should we do? It would be good if they would bring down the curtain at an appropriate time. Because there is a man like Kumakō who rolled up his tail, rolled his tongue, and rolled his loincloth before the inescapable words of the Kami, even I, Shikakō, have been dragged into this. Come, from now on I must put on a twisted headband and dedicate myself to the world."

"Rather than a deer dragged into it, a deer with a twisted headband and a rolling tongue is hardly very noble, is it?"

From within the cave,

"Consider your place, act in a manner befitting your station, and do not let people call you immature. Do not do ugly things and be looked down upon, nor be despised. Do not say troublesome things. Do not make unreasonable demands of the people of the world as you have done until now, and do not take money recklessly. The retribution for evil is dreadful. Do not accumulate sinful karma. Do not act blindly and recklessly, heedless of consequences, doing wasteful things. Return to the Yamato spirit, and with the steadfast heart of a pine tree that never wavers, be purer than snow, more lovely than flowers, become the light of the world, become the salt of the world. Abandon your deeds until now—which lacked no form of violence and outrage—and your selfishness, cease your base means, reflect upon yourself, and with a heart like the beautiful plum blossom that never changes, observe well the Way of the Kami. Never forget the lovely teachings of Anai even in your dreams, and offer pure prayers day and night without neglect. I, Orochihiko-no-kami, warn you. O, o."

Thus from within the cave a great sound was heard once again.

Kumakō and Shikakō stood before the cave and sang.

"The majestic power of the Kami also shines from Takateru, From the mountain falls the Kotodama, The sound of the waterfall is murmuring, Resounding far and near. In the rock cave, firm and everlasting, Orochihiko, the messenger of the Kami, The divided spirit of Konohana-hime, Has appeared here, And the teachings that cease neither day nor night To the Kami and people of heaven, earth, and the four directions He fully proclaims, While washing away the heart in the waterfall Of Kotodama whose stream is pure, Appearing as the Spirit of Mizu; Even unto a single blade of grass, That the world may be peaceful and tranquil, Ceasing contentious words, and rule the age of the Kami Firm and everlasting, Breaking his heart into a thousand pieces, Pouring forth tears like a torrent,

He saves us. Ah, Sumegami, Sumegami! Man is the precious child of the Kami, The living shrine of the precious Kami. The world is dark as the pitch-black berry, And the souls defiled by dust and rubbish In this Kototaki waterfall Are purified through misogi, growing straight and tall, Becoming the jewel of Naohi-no-kami; The dark valley, by the meritorious deed Of Idunome-no-kami, higher than Mount Takateru, Shall surely illuminate. Of the Sumegami who appears In the eastern and western caves, The heart is indeed the form of the spirit of water Of the pure Kototaki waterfall. Ah, would that as this water Is pure, you wash and cleanse The souls of the people of the world. We are children of man and children of the Kami,

The Kami and man harmonize with one another, Heaven and earth are in good order, Unchanging for a thousand ages, for ten thousand ages, An unshakeable reign where pines flourish, May the prosperity of the pine of Takasago, Pure also in the divine age, endure long, May the prosperity of the pine endure long."

Thus with an intriguing melody they sang in harmony. Drawn out by the intrigue of this song, Shigeyamatsumi came forth from within the cave waving his hands and dancing with his feet, blowing a bamboo pipe, and appeared before the cave; forgetting themselves, the three danced frantically in a threefold tomoe.

Thereupon the three—Shigeyamatsumi, Kumakō, and Shikakō—performed misogi day and night in the waters of the Kototaki waterfall, divided the tasks among themselves, and came to spread the teachings of the Anai religion in all four directions. Kumakō performed the activities of Iwasaku-no-kami, and Shikakō performed the activities of Nesaku-no-kami, participating in the divine work at Yomotsuhirasaka and achieving great merit.

と節を面白く調子を合わせて歌う。この歌の面白さに、志芸山津見は釣り出されて、巌窟の中から、手を振り足を躍らせ、竹筒を吹きながら巌窟の前に現れてきて、我を忘れて三人三つ巴となって踊り狂う。

ここに志芸山津見、熊公、鹿公の三人は琴滝の水で日夜禊を行い、それぞれ手分けをして、三五教の教えを四方に伝えることとなった。熊公は石拆の神の働きをし、鹿公は根拆の神の働きをして、黄泉比良坂の神業に参加し大きな功績を立てたのである。
