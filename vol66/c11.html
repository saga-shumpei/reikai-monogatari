亜魔の河〔1693〕

§ 1

Sugako had been taken into the cave of Mount Ora by Genshinbau and made to wait for a whole week, under the pretext of examining her divine family register in Tengoku. To comfort her loneliness during that time, she was singing a song of longing for her homeland.

Sugako: 'Though the peaks of Ora be high, Though this Tanigaha be deep, Compared to the grace of my sincere parents Who nurtured and raised this body of mine, Not even a single hair of nine oxen Would match it. Though night after night the footsteps of the wind pass by, Visiting the amado of my dear father's ima, And murmuring, what can I do? The wind has no spirit, no words, Having no means to convey My Kototama in detail, While my father, worrying and grieving Over the future of my body, Sinks into sorrow, and morning and evening, Even failing to touch his tabemono, Must be letting out sighs. Ah, how I yearn for my father's onkanbase, Parted from his wife, all alone, Tossed by the waves of Ukiyo, Treating me as his staff and strength, Without even taking a nochizoe, He cherished and nurtured me. Enticed by the storm of the night, My only daughter vanished into the clouds, With no way to search, save for the voice Sweeping across the arakaze field, Seeing his grieving arasima Now vividly before my eyes, If this body of mine had wings, I would escape from this Ihayado And return home, yet Fascinated and frozen by Genshinbau's suspicious gaze, Unable to advance and unable to retreat, Such a painful and wretched helplessness! Though the man called Genshinbau Claims to be an incarnation of the heavenly Kami himself, how doubtful it is! Without anything else changing, Morning and evening he draws close to my side, Pouring upon me disgusting glances, And the ends of his hateful kotoba somehow Seem terribly base. Possessed by the monstrous Magu-no-magatami, Plotting to disturb the world, Could he be playing such mischievous tricks? Ah, almighty Kami, Bonten, Taishaku, Jizaiten, Great Okumikoto no Ōkami, Quickly, even in a single day, clear away The clouds in my breast! Though the sun shines, though it clouds over, Though the moon fills, though it wanes, Even if the great earth sinks, The divine grace and the father's benevolence, Could I forget them for a single day or a brief moment? Created in the form of Kami, I am a child of Kami, the shrine of Kami, Yet even upon this body equal to Kami, Do clouds and mist of crookedness gather? Truly, what a dubious state of the world! Have mercy, before the true Kami Of heaven and earth, I pray with reverence and awe.' Did I think he was a true Kami? To think the guardian of Kami is he who lusts after my body. Though I thought him a most sacred pillar of Kami, What a man of many suspicious deeds he is! If I let the months and days pass in vain like this, I too shall become prey to the crooked. Though the moon and days pass in vain, I too shall become the prey of the crooked."

As she was thus singing, Genshinbau unlocked the bolt with a key, slipped quietly inside,

Genshinbau: 'Lady Sugako, it has been terribly busy. Today there were exceptionally many worshippers, and this side too was extremely busy. However, it is now past seven, and people have finally returned to their homes, so first I thought to look upon your beautiful onkanbase and rest from the fatigue of the day. My, what a beautiful face!'

With a repulsive smile and thrusting forward his teeth like jagged stakes in a riverbed, he tries to kiss Sugako's cheek. Sugako, startled, saying 'Oh my!', with white, beautiful hands like ivory carving, pushed hard against Genshinbau's forehead.

Genshinbau: 'Ha ha ha ha ha, are you frightened, is it funny, are you embarrassed? What an innocent person you are, oh! Hey, Sugako, today for the first time a messenger came from heaven and examined your divine register, and oh, rejoice! You were a heavenly being of the First Spirit Realm, and moreover, the spirit of my wife. Therefore, by the reason that befits it, in heaven you are the birds that share wings, on earth you are the branches that share twin trunks; no matter what, by the truth of heaven and earth corresponding, you and I must become husband and wife, a karma is bound that cannot be helped! Ever since the time I saved you from danger, I thought you were a graceful woman, but upon examining thoroughly, as you see above, how is it, princess, are you happy?'

Sugako: 'Ah, how regrettable, I have been deceived by you. What should I do now? O Bonten, Taishiyaku, and Jizaiten-sama, please save me from this critical plight, Kamunagara Chihahe Mase.'

Genshin: 'Aha ha ha ha, as expected of an otome, you are truly embarrassed, aren't you. Even if it starts like a Sanbasō, in the end it gets deeper. Ah ya ya ya, oh ha, katsutakata, finally, it gets settled with katsutakata, after all danshi have won and won in love, aha ha ha ha.'

With Sugako making an irritated face and frowning around her eyebrows,

Sugako: 'Moshi Genshinsama, please forgive me. In exchange, for any other matter, I will do whatever goyō you wish.'

Genshin: 'Yaa, since your spirit is that of a tennin of the Daiichireigoku, humble suiji and sauji are unbefitting to your spirit. If you only perform the goyō of my shingefu, namely kamiu and hitō, there is no need even to raise a fallen broom. Oh, what a kaūn birth you have.'

Thus Genshinbō, having his inner thoughts seen through by Sugako and being loathed like a centipede, was blinded by eyes darkened by love, losing all sense of direction, wrapped in the dark clouds of love, becoming arrogant, and persistently importuning her. Thinking she would cause him some trouble, while wearing a calm expression and letting float a smile,

Sugako: 'My most revered master and lord, you have saved my precious life, a noble grace impossible to replace with heaven or earth, and even unto the end of myriad kalpas, down to the age of Miroku, I shall never forget it. Furthermore, that you would even investigate my divine registry—how immeasurably awe-inspiring it is. You spoke of me as the spirit of a tennin of the Daiichireigoku, but perhaps I am none other than the spirit of Tanabatahime-no-Mikoto?'

Genshin: 'Yaa, as expected you are a magnificent one. Just as you say, your spirit is entirely that of Tanabatahime-no-Mikoto. Speaking in terms of stars, you are the princess star on the right side, separated by the Amanogawa. And I am Hikoboshi.'

Sugako: 'Ah, I understand now. It is said that Tanabatahime-sama meets but once a year, but is that a fact?'

Genshin: 'That is so; by the heavenly law that cannot be moved since the creation of the universe, it is decided for myriad kalpas, a truly harmonious couple. Since the gods gaze upon each other's faces across the Amanogawa all year round are ourselves spirits, you see. Therefore, you and I have the karmic bond to gaze upon each other's faces from morning till night and live together in harmony.'

Sugako: 'Indeed, so it is. If that be the case, between myself and my master and lord, we are husband and wife in spirit, and as for the once-a-year tryst of which you speak, your instruction is most reasonable. Moreover, by the principle of the correspondence of heaven and earth, I cannot become a husband-and-wife relationship with my master and lord until the night of the seventh day of the seventh month. Much less are you the incarnation of Tentei, so surely there can be no such thing as Tentei Himself disturbing the divine laws. Please, regard this valley stream as the Amanogawa, and if you will place me on the opposite side of the stream, is that not precisely the union of heaven and earth and a matching mirror?'

Thus she cleverly talked her way out of it. Genshinbō... having carelessly let himself be reeled in by the thread of her words, and realizing it had become a matter of no return... was startled for a moment, but being an old scoundrel, opening his mouth wide with an 'aha ha ha ha' and laughing unconcernedly,

Genshin: 'Oi, Sugako-hime, truly you are not Tanabatahime-sama, but a far, far, far deeper and more magnificent god.'

Sugako: 'Hee, and what kind of god might that be?'

Genshin: 'Well now, your spirit is the Great Ōkami of Konohanahime, and my spirit is Iwanagahime-no-Mikoto. Therefore, no matter what, we simply must become husband and wife. This Genshinbō is a shugenja endowed with majesty and firm as a rock. Until pebbles turn into boulders and are covered with moss in the age of eternal firmness, I am just like the great Miroku-sama of the bedrock at the bottom of the earth who protects this world. And since your spirit is Konohanasakuya-hime-no-Mikoto, you are a radiant beauty like a flower, nay, a beauty surpassing even flowers. It is said that gentleness overcomes strength; you are the gentle, and I am the strong. Yet there is also the saying that strength overcomes gentleness. There is gentleness within strength, and strength within gentleness; there is an inseparable, intimately solid relationship, so no matter what anyone says, we are a husband-and-wife relationship bound together by heaven and earth.'

玄真『コリヤ姫、何と云ふ、姫御前の優しい面にも似ず、無茶なことをいふのだ。お前は私のいふことを誤解してゐるな。よく考へてみよ。此玄真坊は神様に仕へる時は即ち天帝の化身であり、神様の御用を休んだ時は一介の修験者だ。神の籍に於ては神の活動をなし、人の籍に於ては人の活動をなし、変現出没自由自在、或時は天に蟠まる竜となり、或時は古池になく蛙となり、時ありては蠑螈蚯蚓になる。之が即ち神の神たる所以だ。ここの道理をトツクリと考へて、いさぎよい返事をしてくれ、なあスガコ』

スガコ『あゝ左様で厶いますか、貴方は神となつたり、人となつたり、甚だしきは蛙となつたり、蠑螈蚯蚓となつたり、何とマア器用な御方ですこと、併し乍ら玄真坊様、私は益々貴方が怖いらしくなつて参りましたよ。そして夫婦になれと仰有る様ですが、私も月に七日の障ある人間の肉体、神様と添ふ訳には行かず、又修験者は女に接する時は死後仏罰に仍つて七万有尋の大蛇となり、且つ修験者に犯された女は八万地獄に墜ちるといふバラモンの教、かう考へてみますれば、修験者としての貴方の妻になる事は絶対に厭で厶います。況んや人間と生れ乍ら、蛙、蠑螈、蚯蚓などと夫婦約束は到底出来ませぬ。どうか悪からず此理由を御賢察下されまして、忌まはしい夫婦関係などには言及なさらないよう、御願申します。其代り妾はどこ迄も、貴方様を師の君様と尊崇し、敬愛し、誠を尽しますから、貴方も妾を愛して下さいませ。そして結構な経文を教て下さい。お願申します』

玄真『マアマア今日はこれ位にしておかう、お前も神経昂奮してゐるから、何を云つても耳に入るまい。女といふ者は一日に七度も心が変るといふから、水の出ばなに何をいつても駄目だ。又風向のよい時にゆつくり話さう程に。左様ならスガコ殿、ゆつくりお休みなさい』

といひ乍ら、やや悄気気味になつて、岩の戸をあけ、吾居間なる次の岩窟に帰り行くのであつた。後にスガコ姫はハツと吐息をつき乍ら、独言。

スガコ『あゝ情ないことになつたものだなア。かやうな所へ拐かされ、悪人輩の恋の犠牲に供せられむとするのか。一度は拒んでみても、かれ玄真坊の燃ゆるが如き恋の炎は容易に消すことは出来まい。何とか彼とか言つて一日送りに日を送り、助け人の来る迄時節を待つより仕方がない。あゝ父上は嘸、妾の行方について御心配して厶るだらう。何とマア不運な父娘だらう。悩み禍の浮世とは云ひ乍らジャンクの家には、これ程迄に禍の見舞ふものか。テもさても残酷な世の中だなア。あゝ惟神霊幸倍坐世。天地の大神あはれみ玉へ救ひ玉へ』
