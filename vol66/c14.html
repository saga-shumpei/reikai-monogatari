Chapter 14: 相生 Pine Wind [1696]

相生松風〔1696〕

§ 1

Sandā and Sugako Riyōnin made various excuses regarding Genshinbau's eukiu, and because they did not give a clear shiyōdaku, Genshinbau also became somewhatake gimi, and thinking to subject them to starvation, cause them an ōjiyō, and make them obey his ishi, he put on a genjiu jiyōmahe and shut both futari up.

Since the futari were in an ahidagara of saushi, even though they were shut up in such a gankutsu without being given shiyokumotsu, they did not think it excessively painful, and taking as their yuiitsu delight that they had been able to meet the person they longed for, they exchanged various talks.

Sugako: 'Sandā-sama, anata, why on earth have you been captured and come to such a place?'

Sandā: 'Because your yukuhe became unknown, that became a shinpai, and for itsutoki I fell into an iūtsushiyō, and I suffered to the extent that I thought even myself that this time I surely had no inochi left. The ishiya said it was utterly dame, and my piyōshin were worried; while resigning myself that you and I were utterly without fate, wrapped in the darkness of an uncontainable love, I continued my worries and afflictions day and night. However, saying he was a shugenja of Mount Ōra, an old man with white hair and a strange appearance passed by my front door and boasted of various miraculous efficacies; for a moment I even felt suspicion, but being in the weak state of mind of having my heart stolen by love, wanting to know your whereabouts through divine virtue, and hiding it from my piyōshin, I came along the night road. He appeared to be one of the petty thieves employed by Genshinbau, cleverly deceiving me and bringing me to this gankutsu. However, today, meeting you whom I long for, it is an indescribable joy. Even if I die here now, I am satisfied.'

So saying, she wipes away her tears with her sleeve.

Sugako: 'Even a person like me—you have thought of me to that extent. Thank you very much. You are the finest bishōnen in the nation of Tolman; although it was said we were pledged by our piyōshin, I thought that someone like me would certainly not be taken as a partner in an emergency, and from ordinary times I had given up. Not knowing even in a dream that you had such deep grace towards me, I lived on sometimes harboring resentments against you—please forgive my sin.'

Sandā: 'At any rate, for a man and woman of saushi to meet in such a place is truly a wondrous bond. What Genshinbau said was that unless both of us obey what he says, he will make us suffer starvation; even if we meet with starvation, now that I have met you whom I long for, let us take each other's hands and pray to God for a journey to the heavenly country where the true God resides. For there are no longer any paths for us to open up, you see.'

Sugako: 'You are quite right. Whether it be the Sanzu River, the Mountain of Death, or the Heavenly Crossroads, if I go along with you, Anata, there will be no pain at all. How I wish to leave this world even an ahidatsu sooner! Ah, the sin of filial impiety in leaving a single father behind and going before him—please forgive me.'

Sugako, having resolved herself to die, let a soft tear fall upon her knees.

Sandā: 'Since things have come to this, let us make our resolve mutually. Facing this parting of this world, let us compose a poem or two and serenely await our death.'

Sugako: 'Yes, as a keepsake of this fleeting world, I too will have a poem composed; first, please let me hear yours, Anata.'

Sandā: 'The heavens are vast and high, The earth is boundless and stretches without limit. Ah, I, As a child of Heaven and Earth, Where light and darkness cross and mingle, Was born as a person of this transient world. Whether by fortune or misfortune, I became a child of the village house, Young in years and still unversed in the ways of the world, Knowing not the taste of hardship. Welcoming at last the spring of my eighteenth year, Drifting upon the waves of the sea of love, Swallowed by raging waves and towering billows, My boat is about to capsize. Ah, what shall I do? My beloved Jeweled boat, Toyed with by the swirling waves, Where in the sky do I wander now? Morning and evening, hoping to track it down—'

Crying to heaven and weeping upon earth, / My heart is distracted into a thousand pieces, / And my karatama grows thinner day and night. / Ah, the agony of a heart tormented by love! / However harsh the world of kanku may be, / Would it ever surpass the intensity / Of the love's torment / That has fallen upon my own body? / Ah, what shall I do? / Wondering about the yukuhe of your inochi, / I cast my heart upon the clouds drifting through the empty sky, / Or else, amid the sound of evening insects, / I bring forth your omokage, / Until neither night is night nor day is day, / Wandering as if lost in the darkness of tokoyo. / Rather, casting my fate to heaven, / Leaving this ajiki ukiyo, / Ascending to tengoku, / And preserving tokoshihe no inochi— / Quietly stepping out of the gate,

Just as I was gazing at the keshiki of the nobe, / There appears / A shiyugenjiya with hakuhatsu and an iyō appearance, / Who speaks thus toward me: / That in the seijiyō of Mount Ora, / A kiuseishiyu with musau shinriki / Is now manifesting. / Prompted by my oshi, / And out of the excess of my longing for you, / Firing a single thread of hope, / Supporting even my body, wasted by sickness, / With the power of love, / At last I climbed up, and while I was deep in shian in the ihaya, / Genshinbau, with an impure heart, / Ended up becoming the captive of love, / Finding himself in a predicament where he could not make a miugo, / Entrusting his words to right and left, / Day after day until this very day, / The pitifulness of having sent the time drifting on! / Ah, Omiakamiakami,

Bonten, Taishaku, Jizaiten, / Have pity upon our plight, / And swiftly, I pray, / Save both our bodies into the light, / Thus do I earnestly pray.

Leaving the ukiyo behind, into the distant / Imperial Land of Heaven shall I advance. / Receiving the permission of Ōkami, I shall now / Go together with Sugako to tengoku. / Two hearts matched like the liver within the villagers, / Will the Imperial Deities surely grant it? / Escaping the demonic hands of the ugly deity, / How joyful must be the body ascending to the Heavenly Land. / If I go, surely the ugly deity will be startled, / Stamping his feet within the ihaya. / The thread of the jeweled-string inochi, moment by moment, / Is about to snap, awaiting the Divine Land.

Sugako sings in a low voice.

Sugako: 'From the heavenly sky, far higher / Than the grace of God and the mercy of a father, / Deeper than the sea is the mercy of a mother, / Nurtured by both father and mother, / Until this very day of the spring of twice-eight, / Raised amidst flowers and the moon, / To the extent that nothing is lacking, / Receiving the happiness of this world upon my body, / With an august figure purer than the moon, / Making Lord Sandā my spouse, / Becoming the flower of the village of Tarai, / And becoming even the moon to the people of the world, / While hoping to pass the joyful august reign of tengoku / While being envied by others—all turned out to be mere bubbles. / Blown by the storm of ugliness, / My body is within the rock-cave of eternal darkness, / Imprisoned by evil deities, / Morning and evening, the disgusting / Love-whip of the official of ugliness,

Every time I receive it, both my body / And my soul suffer an agony / As if being shattered into ten thousand pieces. / What kind of sins and karma from past lives / Have come around to visit my own body? / Though I look up and cry out to heaven, heaven does not answer; falling prostrate upon the earth, / Though I lament, upon the earth there is no voice either. / The mountain wind of Mount Ora, / Though it blows unceasingly, for my soul, / The path of communication to my father's side / Has been utterly severed—such is the torment. / Even if it be only in my father's dreams, / Praying that he let my soul's suffering / Be made known, though I pray, / Alas, what use is prayer? / Repeating morning and evening how many times, / Though I wait, though the days pass, there is no news; / The only crying sounds are monkeys, / And the voices of birds echoing against the wooden spirits.

Ah, what can be done, what can be done? / Once and for all, taking leave of this world, / Becoming a spirit, O my dear father, / By the side of you, my longing one, / I think I shall pass through—at the very moment I thought so, / Wondrously, it is the voice of you, my beloved! / Pressing my ear to the door of the rock-cave, / Just as I was listening to every detail without missing anything, / Is it a dream, is it reality, is it an illusion? / The voice of my beloved rings out.

Though I worship heaven, though I worship earth, / Without even time to rejoice, the rock door / Is opened, and in comes Genshinbau. / Taking the hand of my beloved person, / Throwing us into this ihaya, / Glaring at us with hatred, / The fact that he departed—how detestable it is! / Ah, nevertheless, nevertheless, / The noble figure of my spouse, whom I have yearned for and longed after, / Right before my eyes.

The joy of having worshipped him! / Even if I should die of starvation, / If we ascend to Takamanohara, / The provisions of the spirit shall be abundantly / Stored in the august storehouses / Of the Land of the Gods, and shall be given to you and me, / By the strict God, / The Great Deity of the August Spirit of Mura. / Feeling as though I am now, before my eyes, distinctly / Worshipping his countenance filled with compassion. / Lord Sandā, my spouse, / Even if whatever may happen, / Binding our hearts together, / Fastening our subsequent lives / To the entwining thread of the jeweled-string destiny, / To whatever sky we may reach, / Two evergreen trees that never part from one another, / Flourishing eternally like green pine needles, / Even if we fall and wither, we are a pair.'

Thus both recited their reflections and death poems, forgetting the hunger and fatigue of their bodies, and as their spirits soared to the paradise of Heaven, with a click the padlock was removed, and leisurely entering was Genshimbō, whom the two men and women loathed like a centipede. Seeing the emaciated figures of the two, Genshimbō smiled broadly and cheerfully,

Genshin: 'Aha-ha-ha-ha, hey, how is it, you two? Have you prepared yourselves by now? Your lives are grasped right in the palm of this Genshimbō's hand. While there is life there is hope; without stubbornly talking nonsense forever, surrender with a yes, and tell me your personal advantage and the key to your lives—give me your answer.'

Sandā and Sugako had already resolved themselves to die, yet somewhere within them an attachment to life still remained. ...If they could somehow deceive him and preserve their lives for even a single day, the two might safely escape this cave, emerge openly into the world, and fulfill their love in this life. Whatever may happen, let us deceive this bewildered, fraudulent monk with the tip of our tongue... thus it spontaneously arose in the breasts of both.

Sandā: 'Genshimbō-sama, I too have made up my mind. Thanks to you, I have come to understand the gains and losses, so I shall alter my hitherto stubborn and uncompromising attitude and follow your august will as much as possible. Since I am suffering from hunger, please give us some bread.'

At these words, Genshimbō leaped with joy, yet purposely assumed a cold, feigned ignorance and reluctant expression,

Genshin: 'Humph, if you have come to your senses, it is your own good fortune. Fasting is the essential secret of spiritual training to calm the mind and attain enlightenment. It is never because this Genshimbō intended to starve you both to death that I did not give you food. I arranged it this way out of great mercy and compassion, desiring to make you undergo the practice of fasting and realize the true ultimate truth. How is it? Do you understand my merciful heart?'

Sandā: 'Yes, I have understood it clearly. I have been allowed to undergo a wonderful practice. I have indeed come to realize that you are the savior chosen by Heaven.'

Genshin: 'Aha-ha-ha-ha, it must not be otherwise! Now, now, Sugako, how about you? Have you understood the truth a little? Have you realized the sincere heart of this Genshimbō?'

Sugako: 'Yes, I have been made to realize it.'

Genshin: 'What have you realized?'

Sugako: 'Yes, I have thoroughly realized the demonic heart of my master and lord.'

Genshin: 'Aha-ha-ha-ha! If your enlightenment has opened, you will obey absolutely what I say, won't you? Surely you two will not disobey, will you?'

Sandā: 'Please give us bread. I no longer have the courage to make any reply.'

Genshin: 'Indeed, that is only natural. Wait, wait; I, the humble monk, shall prepare the food and drink myself, and feed you, my dear ones.'

So saying with a broad grin, he slammed the door shut, lowered the padlock once more, and walked away.

サンダー『天津国昇り得ずして八衢に よし彷徨も二人楽しき。 根の国によしおつる共吾と汝と 二人なりせば楽しかるらむ。 八衢の関所を無事に通りぬけ 恋の花さく神国に至らむ。 月花に譬ふべらなる吾と汝は 天八衢の栄えなるらむ』

スガコ『八衢によし迷ふ共何かあらむ 恋しき汝を光と思へば。 常暗の根底の国に落つる共 月の光の汝としあらば。 月影を仰ぎみる度思ふ哉 いつも清けき君の姿を』

サンダー『春夏の野に咲く花を眺めては 君の御姿思ひうかべつ。 山百合の花に微風の当るさま 汝がスタイルによくも似しかな。 世の中のすべての事を打忘れ 只君のみに心注ぎぬ』

スガコ『たらちねの親と親との許嫁 なりとし聞けどいとも恥かし。 仰ぎ見る高根の花か大空の 月にひとしき君にありせば。 手折られて君が館の床のべに かをらむものと思ひける哉』

斯く両人は述懐や辞世をよんで、身の餓ゑ疲れを忘れ、霊を天国の楽園に馳せ居る折しも、ガチンと錠前を外して、悠々と入来りしは二人の男女が蚰蜒の如く嫌つて居た玄真坊であつた。玄真坊は両人の痩こけた姿を見て、さも愉快げに打笑み乍ら、

玄真『アハヽヽヽ、オイ何うだ両人、最早覚悟はついたか、其方等の生命は、此玄真坊が手の内に握つてゐるのだ。生命あつての物種、いつ迄も頑固な事を申さずに、ウンと靡いたが、其方の身の得、命の鍵、返答を聞かして呉れ』

サンダー、スガコの両人は既に死を決してゐたものの、まだ何処やらに生の執着心が残つてゐた。……何とかゴマかして一日でも生命を保ち居らば、両人が此岩窟を無事に逃出し、天下晴れて、此世で添ひ遂げる事が出来るであらう。何は兎もあれ、迷ひきつた此売僧、口の先にてゴマかしやらむ……と期せずして両人の胸に泛んだ。

サンダー『玄真坊様、妾も決心を致しました。おかげに依りまして、利害得失を悟り得ましたから、今迄の頑固一点の態度を更め可成的御意に従ひませう。どうか空腹に悩んで居りますから、パンをお与へ下さいませ』

玄真坊は此言葉に飛立つ許り打喜び乍ら、ワザと素知らぬ渋り切つた顔して、

玄真『ウン、気が付いたならば、そち共の幸福だ。断食といふものは精神が落付いて、悟りを開く第一の修行の要訣だ。決して此玄真坊は汝等両人を干し殺さうと思つて食を与へないのではない。断食の修行をさせて、誠の真理を悟らしてやりたいといふ、大慈大悲の心より斯く取計らつたのだ。どうだ、吾慈悲心が分つたか』

サンダー『ハイ確に分りまして厶います。結構な修行をさして頂きました。いかにも貴方は天の選みし救世主だと悟らして頂きました』

玄真『アハヽヽヽ、さうなくては叶はぬ事、これこれスガコ、そちは何うだ。少し真理が分つたか、此玄真坊が真心を悟つたか』

スガコ『ハイ、悟らして頂きました』

玄真『何う悟つたのだ』

スガコ『ハイ、師の君様の魔心をスツカリ悟らして貰ひました』

玄真『アハヽヽヽ、悟りが開けたならば、此方の申す事は絶対服従するだらうな。ヨモヤ両人、違背は致すまいのう』

サンダー『どうかパンをお与へ下さい。最早お答へする勇気が出て参りませぬ』

玄真『成程無理もない。まてまて拙僧が自ら飲食を調理し、可愛い其方等に食はしてやらう』

とニコニコし乍ら、戸をピシヤリと締め、再び錠をおろして立去つた。
