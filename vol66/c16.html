Chapter 16: 恋の Dream 路 [1698]

恋の夢路〔1698〕

§ 1

Umekō: 'The plum blossoms of the three-thousandfold world Open all at once by the divine teaching; Opening, scattering, and bearing fruit, Know the grace of Tsukihi and the great earth! The living gods who purify this world Gather in Takaamahara as gods; When the gods appear in the manifest world, Sweeping away with the stern breath of Ibuki And purifying with the sacred water of Shimizu The heaven and earth enveloped by black clouds, Constructing the heavenly pure land eternally Upon this very ground, Saving the people of the myriad lands Into the everlasting joy of Shinraku, Far and near, the sendenshi of the divine will Distribute it generously, how venerable! Following Terukuniwake, Leaving the reijiyō of Ubusunayama behind And viewing Kajikayama in the distance, Enduring the toils of mokūshitsupū, day and night succeeding, through the plateau region Where even the winds of dekatan blow fiercely, If you come to the land of Tolman, Encrusted upon the capital of Haruna, By the evil spirits of the Yamata no Orochi And the magatsuhami manipulated by them, The military forces of Daikokunushi burn down people's dwellings, Plunder gold, silver, and supplies, Without distinction of wives and daughters, Extend their demonic hands to snatch and capture them, And steal away deep into the mountains. Trembling at this, the people of the land Can sleep soundly neither night nor day; Whenever I witness the suffering of the depths of misery, though my heart's steed chafes and urges me How somehow to save them, the grief of not obtaining the permission Of my master, O Lord, While suppressing the steed of my heart, Following the army of righteousness and valor, I advance toward the castle of Barugan; The morale rises suddenly, And its momentum pierces the heavens. O master, O Lord, or Januku-sama, If you command this army And cause it to advance, no matter how many Brahman warriors there may be, Like the wind passing over a wilderness, Their subjugation is right before our eyes. Whether I, Umekō, am alone in the army or not, What amount of influence should this battle of the whole situation have? Though rebelling against the master's will Is a matter of my own resolve as well, The overflowing soul Is Hanakohime of the village of Tarai, Sugako's princess, and Sandā-sama, To rescue the pitiful circumstances of those three Into the joy where heavenly flowers bloom and fragrance spreads, Unfailingly saving them, I shouted with martial vigor; Escaping the gaze of my master, O Lord, Blending into the night shadows, hoarsely, Scourging my steed, now in this place, Where no bounds are known, upon the wild plain, Having come without even a destination. Looking out over the peak of Ōra, the strange light glowing night after night Must be the assembly of crooked spirits Making ugly plots And afflicting the people of the world. Whether it is due to my own heart I know not, Yet the mountain of Ōra weighs upon my mind, And sleeping or waking I cannot forget it; Making my life a wager, a single rider, Until the hoofs of my steed continue, No matter how steep the mountain path may be, How could my Yamato spirit falter? The arrow of iron-hard resolve Whose thought is fixed has already Let go of the true bow's string; My spirit is one that no longer turns back. Grant that those three graceful persons Be successfully and safely saved! Protecting the Misoshiru, I humbly pray and beseech before the august presence Of the Great Deity Kuninotokotachi And the Great Deity Kami Susanoo.'

Umekō, when staying at Januku's house, heard that Sugako and Sandā had been snatched away by someone, and somehow received a hint from someone that they seemed to be imprisoned in Mount Ōra for the sake of villains. And when he visited the old woman's house and heard that Sanyo's daughter Hanaki had been captured by the Brahman army, he felt that she too must be hidden somewhere deep in the mountains. Not out of love or lust, but driven somehow by a sympathetic heart, he was filled with chivalrous spirit wishing to help this pitiful woman. However, he was a follower of Terukuniwake and could not leave the ranks at his own whim and fancy. Wondering what to do, pursuing one thought and catching up with another, he followed Terukuniwake and Januku's righteous army, whipped his horse, and dashed out into the boundless vast plain; but as dusk fell, no matter how much he whipped or restrained his mount, it would not take a single step forward. Meanwhile, the numerous righteous army left Umekō behind in the midst of the vast plain, kicking up dust with their horses' hoofs, and advanced toward Barugan Castle with the force of a gale. Umekō folded both hands upon his saddle and lost himself in thought for a while... Why on earth does my horse alone not advance? There must be some profound divine will at work here... As he tentatively pulled the right rein just a little, the steed straightened its head toward the west and dashed into the starry wilderness with the momentum of a thousand leagues in a single flash. Umekō thought... Ah, I am sorry for my master, O Lord; yet the very fact that the essential steed will not advance must mean some special mission has been sent down from the divine realm. Now that it has come to this, I shall simply act in all things according to the will of the gods... and thus firmed the resolve of his navel. The riding horse, as if it had gradually come to understand Umekō's heart, swinging its thick, long, fluffy tail from side to side, bravely and loudly neighing, began to walk quietly with a clopping sound.

§ 2

Against the pale blue sky, the stars of Kinginshiyoku twinkled sharply as though pressing gold leaf upon the firmament, seeming to watch over the actions of Umekō. The high heavens were calm and silent, and the earth was an endless, boundless wilderness; neither the voices of fierce beasts nor the sound of a softly blowing wind could be heard, only the breathing of the horse's nostrils and the sound of its hooves galloping across the grassy sandy plain, softly and rhythmically pattering. Looking toward the left at a densely wooded small hill, fire burned and flickered amidst the trees. Thinking to himself that there must be dwellings at the foot of that mountain and that, come what may, he would stop by and ascertain the situation, Umekō turned his horse's head to the left, whereupon the steed eagerly and swiftly pressed forward. With the fire as his mark, Umekō reined in the horse's footsteps and approached the shade of the trees to survey the scene, where he beheld four or five rough-looking men, appearing to be runaway soldiers of Brahman, binding a single beautiful woman with her hands behind her back. Crying out roughly about some matter, they were mercilessly beating the woman's body with whips without regard for place. At each such blow, the woman raised shrill, agonizing cries, tossing her disheveled hair, biting her lips in bitter vexation, and maintaining a ferocious visage despite her beauty, until even Umekō was left practically stunned. Umekō thought: 'I must somehow manage to rescue her. Is she now about to become the prey of blood-hungry jackals and wolves? Ah, how pitiable. Yet, the enemies are several fierce warriors, while I am alone. Nevertheless, relying upon a horse of a thousand li, should anything go wrong, I need only flee at full speed. Let me try to frighten them by way of experiment.' Approaching his horse within the shadow of the dense trees and raising a mighty voice from atop his saddle,

Umekō: 'Ha ha ha ha! I am a great tengu named Amakurabō who dwells upon Mount Ōra. You unmitigated scoundrels, abominable villains who toy with a delicate woman, plot lawless desires, and attempt to achieve illicit ambitions! Now, upon the reports of my mountain's attendants, I have come here to chastise all you evil men! Insolent wretches, do not move from that spot! Ooo-ooo!'

As he roared these words, the Brahmans, taken utterly by surprise as if by a bolt from the blue, instantly lost their wits, abandoned the woman, and shouting,

'It's a tengu! It's a tengu!'

while flinging their military uniforms, caps, boots, and swords right there on the spot, scattered and fled in every direction.

Umekō: 'Ha ha ha ha! What unexpected cowards! How amusing that they fled so weakly, terrified by my Kototama. Truly, this is a most delightful affair!'

Muttering to himself thus, Umekō tightened the horse's reins, drew closer and closer to the woman's side, leapt nimbly down from the saddle, untied the woman's ropes, made her take water from his water flask into her mouth, and patted her back two or three times, whereupon the woman gasped and revived her breath. Seeing Umekō's face through the starlight,

Woman: 'Please, I beg you, save me. No matter what you may say, I have a fiancé, so I cannot defile my body. I ask you to forgive me for this alone. No matter how much you torment me, even in exchange for my life, I must protect my chastity.'

she pressed her palms together.

Umekō: 'Now, now, my good woman, I am by no means a Brahman soldier. I am a divine messenger of the Samandakyō named Umekō. Unable to bear seeing you tormented by a multitude of men, I put my life at stake and chased the enemies away. Do not fear. I shall never speak of anything that would violate your chastity, so rest assured. If we linger in such a place as this, the enemy may well return. We shall hear the detailed story along the way. Mount my horse.'

So speaking, he lifted the woman, mounted the horse's back together with her, and once again proceeded westward across the original plateau. While still upon the horse, Umekō asked the woman about the particulars of her ordeal.

Umekō: 'Now, my good woman, how on earth were you lured and brought to a place like this? There must be some profound reason. As far as it causes no inconvenience, I would have you tell me every single detail, ah.'

Hanaka: 'Night after night, I meet a wondrous shinjin in my dreams. That okata always straddles a horse, guides me through sanya, and gives me various kyōkun, but now, upon beholding your face, I cannot contain my joy, thinking that for the very first time I have met the kohibito from my dreams. I have formed a deep vow with the kohibito of my dreams. The one who appeared in that dream is the spitting image of you. From your face and your voice to the way you ride a horse, even this horse is the spitting image. How could anyone doubt this?'

Umekō let out a sigh, and

Umekō: 'Hmm, could this be a dream? There are things that simply do not make gatsuten. While accompanying a sendenshi, it is hardly possible to walk around bringing one's niyōbau, so a troubling deki has arisen.'

Hanaka: 'Anata, are you not by chance called Umekō? Always in my dreams, calling you Umekōsama, Umekōsama, I have been tsukiaing with you.'

Umekō: 'Naruhodo, my name is Umekō. And your name is Hanaka. It is a place that sounds like the fragrance of plum blossoms blooming all at once across sanzensekai, ha ha ha ha. Somehow I am beginning to lose track of what this means.'

Hanaka: 'Anata... do you not feel that the bond between the futari is a true fūfu ordained by kami...?'

Umekō: 'Well, please let me take my time in shian. It cannot be understood while up in the bajiyō. Let us dismount somewhere and think it over at leisure. Oh, fortunately right here there is a good bashiyo. Hanaka-sama, anata, please remain mounted on the horse just as you are. I shall perform chinkon for a moment and listen to the shinchiyoku.'

So saying, he nimbly leaped down from the horse.

Hanaka: 'Umekō-sama, what sort of riddle might it be for a woman to be riding a horse? Please think about it for a moment.'

Umekō: 'A woman on a horse—humph, I see, it is a pun on the character for mother. Surely no one since kaibyaku has ever experienced such wondrous things.'

So saying, he folded his hands, closed his eyes, and entered into the practice of chinkon. Hanaka likewise nimbly leaped down from the horse, sat beside him, and assumed the posture of chinkon. The innocent horse closed its eyes and grazed on the grass of the four sides. After a while, neither Umekō nor Hanaka spoke a word to each other, and once again riding together on the horse, they proceeded toward the peak of Mount Aura across the boundless wilderness, while being blown by the wild wind.
