Chapter 1: Dawn の空 [1683]

暁の空〔1683〕

§ 1

Savior of Sanzensekai, washing clean Utsushiyo stained with mud, the glorious Mizumitama of the Lord God Kamususanō, resting firmly and eternally in the great sacred ground of Mount Ubusuna, at the sacred pillar of the hall in the shrine of Iso, receiving the command of the Lord of Yashima, to direct the fate of this world and the moon, the dark Magatsu lurking in the capital of Haruna, into the true path of Ananahi, as a Sendenshi to bring peace and happiness to the world, the separate Mikoto of Terukuni, illuminating even the darkness of Tokoyo, accompanied by Kunikō, Umekō, and Terukō, ascending and descending the fierce-wind-swept Kajikayama with its frost-shivering winter trees, in awe of the divine mission,

Climbing up and down steadily, finally breaking through the perilous spot where numerous enemies were stationed, even the moonlight on the marsh of Aoi clarifying down to the water's bed, meeting with the instructors Yoshikohime and Kōgohime, parting sleeves here while chanting the divine messenger's send-off song with cool voices, heading towards the southeast, passing days and nights until at last they arrived at the storefront of the teahouse at the tip of the pole in the village of Tarai on the plateau of the Dekatan country.

Umekō: 'Sensei, the marsh of Aoi is quite vast indeed. Ever since we parted from Yoshikohime-sama and Kōgohime-sama, with the momentum of a thousand-ri flight, riding a man-made taxi, I intended that we dashed along quite a bit, but has the dawn not finally broken? Judging from the speed and considering the time, we must have surely broken through twenty ri to begin with. Meanwhile, on one side is the moonlight on the lake surface, and on the other is a boundless wilderness with no human dwellings, startled from time to time by the sounds of monsters fleeing, and now finally finding houses here, if one tries to rest, are not every single house keeping its doors closed and remaining completely silent?'

Terukuni: 'Hmm, with the morning sun shining so brightly, it looks like a carefree place, doesn't it? Since it is a boundless wilderness with a sparse population, such odious commotions as class struggle or the difficulty of living do not arise, and these people of the country, engrossed in a peaceful dream, are truly to be envied, aren't they?'

Umekō: 'However, Sensei, according to my view, the fact that every house keeps its doors closed and remains dead silent cannot be the peaceful dream of which you speak. Look here, blood is flowing right here. This blood can by no means be the blood of dogs or pigs. Might this not be a memorial of Ōtasuwake's subordinates slaughtering the people or committing various atrocious deeds? My view is that the people are frightened, keeping their doors closed, and holding their breath.'

Terukuni: 'Since General Ōtasuwake apparently passed through here, he may have engaged in all sorts of evil deeds such as plunder, robbery, rape, and slaughter. Truly, those Brahman soldiers are a violent lot; anyway, let's bang on this house and ask about the situation, shall we?'

Umekō: 'Somehow, the monster called wrath within my body has reared its head, and my chest has become somewhat unsettled. By any chance, might a ringleader of the Brahmans be lurking inside this house? The villagers have already abandoned their homes and fled, and those Brahman scoundrels may have boldly moved in to plot a surprise attack against us.'

Terukuni: 'Anyway, let's knock on the door, wake them up, and investigate the secrets inside.'

Umekō: 'Hey, Teru-sama, what are you feeling so gloomy about? What is the use of being discouraged in a place like this? From here on is the decisive battle field of a thousand-rider clash. What is that pale face of a brave warrior of the divine army?'

Terukō: 'I am not particularly feeling gloomy.'

Umekō: 'Then what is it? You must have bolted the door of your heart and be trembling in the corner of the house like the master of this place. Where on earth have you lost that transient mind you are always speaking of so grandly?'

Terukō: 'Just open this house and see; there will be no one inside. A house where humans live looks lively even when looking at the ridge of the roof. This is definitely an empty house.'

Umekō: 'Don't talk nonsense; no matter how depressed the economy is or how high the rent gets, it stands to reason that five or six houses wouldn't stand vacant in a row. Besides, if they were vacant, there ought to be diagonal paper strips pasted across them. If you look at the places where no diagonal paper is pasted, you can be sure there are people inside, you know.'

Terukō: 'What do you mean by diagonal paper strips?'

Umekō: 'Ha-ha-ha-ha, what a sheltered young master you are, not knowing the state of the world! A diagonal paper strip means writing "house for rent" on white paper, shutting the door tight, and pasting it diagonally. The places where that hasn't been done definitely have people inside. Anyway, shall I go and knock on the door? The Savior of the Sanzensekai, the envoy of that Savior is standing right at the gate, yet the blind of heart and the deaf of heart do not know to open the gate, welcome Him in, and taste Heiwa and Kaufuku.'

So saying, clenching his fists thump-thump-thump, he hammered recklessly and wildly enough to break the door. No matter how much he struck and pounded, not even the sound of a mouse's voice came out.

Umekō: 'This is somehow strange, you know. If you force your way into an empty house while someone is away, you will be misunderstood as a burglar, and furthermore, even if there is someone inside, unless they say come on in, it constitutes the crime of house trespassing. Come, let's give up and go to some other house to take a rest. Sensei, how would it be if we wished for that?'

Terukō: 'Un, what you say is plausible enough for once, but shouldn't we make up our minds and knock the door down to get in?'

Umekō: 'If it is Sensei's command, I will smash it down.'

Forcibly pulling the door open, the three advanced into the interior. When they looked, in the corner of a not-very-spacious tatami room, an old woman lay bound hand and foot, bleeding from her forehead, reduced to her last gasp.

Umekō: 'Sensei, it is indeed not a vacant house after all, you know. Poor thing, isn't there such an old elderly woman, moreover bearing a wound on her brow and tightly bound up? Shall we save this obāsama and hear the circumstances?'

Terukō: 'Ah, anyway let's pray to the Kamisama. Come, you all also shall chant the kamigoto together with me.'

Umekō and Teru: 'Hai, certainly,' and with resonant voices they chanted the kamigoto, repeating the heavenly counting song several times.

As if she had come to her senses for the first time, raising a thin voice like an autumn insect tormented by frost,

Old woman: 'I know not which honorable person you may be, but oh how well you have saved me. Please, out of mercy, untie these ropes for me.'

Umekō: 'Even if you didn't ask, I came to save you.'

So saying, taking out a short sword, he cut the rope strands with a snap,

Umekō: 'Now, now, obāsama, rest easy. Now that we have appeared, even tigers, wolves, and lions are not to be feared. The Sensei who is here is called Terukowake, a warrior of peerless divine power. There must be some deep reason for this. Won't you tell us once?'

Old woman: 'Hai, thank you very much. My throat is parched, so I humbly wonder if I might be granted a cup of water?'

Umekō: 'Yoshi-yoshi, water is free. As for the life-or-death situation where you were drawing your last breath, this is water in a canteen purchased for free at Aoi Swamp; come, come, have no hesitation, drink as much as you like. If one cup isn't enough, I am a young man's legs, I will draw and bring you as much as you want.'

And he held the canteen to the old woman's mouth. Like a starving and thirsty gaku, she made a gulping sound in her throat and emptied the canteen in the blink of an eye.

Umekō: 'This is what is called the living water of life, the grace of the divine spirit of Mizu. Obāsama, pull yourself together.'

Old woman: 'Hai, thank you very much. This pure water you gave me has soaked into my five viscera and six bowels, and my whole body has come to feel lively. And it has suddenly grown warm. Thank you very much.'

Hearing the kind words of the villagers, she forgot all shame and appearances, and fell weeping.

He sighed.

Takuson: 'Oh, being in such a flurry, I forgot to greet the three gentlemen as well. ...Well now, three gentlemen, I have been truly rude. And are you masters propagandists of Brahman?'

Umekō: 'No, we are not Brahmans. Obeying the divine decree of the Great Deity of the Mizu-no-Mitama of the Mansion of Saien, we are propagandists of Izukyō traveling around to save the world.'
