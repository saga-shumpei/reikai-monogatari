§ 1

Umekō and Hanaka secretly stole away to the foot of the giant cedar, and upon examining it closely, a wire ladder dangled loosely down from the top of the tree.

Umekō: 'Ha-ha, those rascally Magakami must have climbed this ladder to light fires atop the tree, thereby deceiving the world into believing that stars were falling from the heavens. Oh, this is amusing; I shall first extinguish this light upon which the Magakami rely as their sole power.'

So saying, along with Hanaka, he traced the ladder and climbed up to the crown of the giant cedar, blowing out every single one of the sixteen lanterns without leaving even one behind. Upon the roots of the cedar, which had deceived the day, a curtain of darkness suddenly descended. Genshinbau and Shigō, stirred to suspicion by the sudden extinguishing of the fires, stood lingering beneath the tree,

Shigō: 'Hey Genshinbau, isn't this a strange thing to happen? It is not yet time for the oil to run out, and it is truly odd that all sixteen of them should go out together. The people of the vicinity look upon this giant lantern of the great cedar, believing it to be a star of heaven, and hold it in reverent adoration and longing; if the light were to be extinguished even for a brief moment, the foundation of their faith is bound to wobble. Should that happen, our objective cannot possibly be achieved. It is not as though a particularly strong wind has blown either—what on earth could have happened?'

Genshinbau: 'It is exceedingly strange indeed. There is no reason why such a thing should occur. I checked thoroughly and made sure the oil was poured in, so could this perhaps be the deed of a tengu dwelling in this mountain? In any case, it is a mysterious affair.'

As the two of them worriedly conferred in earnest, a voice like a crashing wave resounded from the top of the tree,

'Ooh, ooh, ooh, ooh, ooh!'

A continuous roaring voice was heard. Its terror soaked straight into their viscera and five internal organs, and an internal earthquake suddenly erupted within the bodies of the pair. Once more from above the tree,

'You Shigō and Genshinbau, who barricade yourselves upon Mount Aura and commit outrages of extreme wickedness, listen well. I am the true Genshinbau Great Tengu, who has made the entire Aura Mountain range my home since ancient times. You, with outrageous audacity, have stolen my name to call yourselves Genshinbau, have lit lamps on the branches of the giant cedar, and have pretended that stars are descending from heaven to listen to your sermons, thereby deceiving all the people under heaven. Not to mention money and goods, you are scoundrels who abduct men's wives and daughters and plunder gold and grain. Since your heavenly fate has now reached its end, repent quickly and disband yourselves. If you continue your evil deeds indefinitely, I, the Genshinbau Great Tengu, shall wrench off your very necks. What say you to this? Ooh, ooh, ooh!'

Upon hearing this, the pair changed color, trembling violently as they spoke,

Shigō: 'Hey, hey, Genshin, hasn't this turned into a serious matter? The tengu is saying such things. It is regrettable to halt midway the theatrical farce we have gone to such lengths to rig up, but can we not trade it for our lives?'

Genshinbau, while trembling,

Genshinbau: 'S-s-so saying, we cannot simply stop it all of a sudden right now, and since we have many henchmen as well, shall we not somehow make a humble petition to the Lord Tengu and ask for a brief respite?'

Shigō: 'Mm, that is true too. It matters not what becomes of the likes of us, but if we were to suddenly disband now, our numerous subordinates would instantly be cast adrift upon the streets. Come, let us try making a petition to him.'

Genshinbau: 'Pardon me, Great Tengu-sama up in the tree, though it is a truly inexcusable matter, if we were to dissolve our group abruptly now, our three thousand subordinates would instantly be cast out onto the streets, scattering in all four directions, and they might become all the more outrageous in their evil. Should that happen, the sufferings of the populace may become still more severe. Since we shall reform ourselves, I will gather my subordinates, thoroughly impart your teachings to them, and lead them back to their home provinces, so I beg for your forbearance until then. This is not merely the hardship of myself alone; it is the hardship of three thousand subordinates.'

Yoriko: 'With so much oil applied and no wind blowing, there is no reason for a fire to go out; and furthermore, for a tengu to perch in a tree and deliver an oracle is also absurd. Yoriko: 'Ho-ho-ho-ho, what a spineless coward he is. What is there to be afraid of in a tengu? It is surely the mischief of someone or other. Go and investigate thoroughly.'

Shīgo: 'Hai, there is already no investigating any further than this. Yoriko: 'My word, you say strange things, do you not? Well then, I shall go myself and investigate.'

With a completely composed air, she leisurely straightened her garments and, calmly following behind Shōgo, arrived beneath the giant cedar.

Yoriko: 'Is that not Genshinbau over there? What is the matter, are you trembling over a mere tengu?'

Genshin: 'Hai, Empress-sama, please save me. A terrible thing has come to pass. This is already my time to pay the tax of years. My body has grown rigid and I can no longer move.'
