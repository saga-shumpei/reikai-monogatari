Chapter 6: Kami 軍義兵 [1688]

神軍義兵〔1688〕

§ 1

Standing and blocking the Genkuwanguchi, shouting out, 'Tamanou, tamanou,' the old man of hakuhatsu and iyō appearance turned towards the gejiyo of this tauke, acting most ōhei.

Raujin: 'Oi, gejiyo-dono, how is the shiyujin of the tauke faring? I am the shiyugenjiya of Mount Ora, a person called Shīgō. Since I have matters to state to the shiyujin of the tauke, mendau though it may be, kindly annai me.'

Gejiyo: 'Hai, donata you might be, but due to the sudden goshiyutsujin of the dannasama, it is an ōsaudō from top to bottom, so please denaho once again. Nanibun you see that he is gotabau.'

Raujin: 'Ahahahahaha. In order to save the dainan involving the tauke, I am a shiyugenjiya who has appeared and come from afar as the messenger of the kiuseishiyu from Mount Ora. Be that as it may, I desire to menkuwai the shiyujin.'

Just as this was being said, the shiyujin Zhank, wearing busau, appeared and came, and turning towards the rauō,

Zhank: 'Donata you may be, I know not, but now is an ichidaiji of the kokuka, and the mansion must be left to the bantō, and we must shiyutsujin from this konsōchō. Goyō it may be, I know not, but this is no longer the case of dealing with the affairs of one's own body and household. Please return home.'

Shīgō: 'Ahahahahaha. That I, zessō, set out from Mount Ora, and while getting drenched in the dew on the yato overgrown with grass, searched while the night had not even broken, was by the command of the Ōkami; whether to save the kokuka or to save the household, it is a matter that cannot be accomplished unless by Kami. If you too are shiyutsujining in the army of giyū in order to save the kokuka, listen to these few words of mine. Even if you lend your ear just a single time, it will hardly result in much loss.'

Zhank: 'If there is a method to save the kokuka, nothing could be more splendid; although it is the brink of shiyutsujin, we cannot afford to let this pass unheard. This place is the Genkuwanguchi, come, come, please proceed within. I shall humbly listen to your tale in detail.'

Shīgō: 'If so, excuse me, I shall pass right through.'

So saying, striking his shakujō, with an ōhei face he was accompanied to the detached betsu-den, and placing a round desk in the middle, host and guest faced each other and conversation began.

Zhank: 'I have heard that you are the messenger of Kami and a shiyugenjiya, and that you will show me the essential path to save the kokuka; for this I am truly filled with gratitude. I humbly beg for your guidance.'

Shīgō: 'Zessō is the ikigami of Mount Ora, a person called Shīgōbō, a senior disciple of Genshinbō. On this occasion, as for Genshinbō my master, desiring to save the crisis of the country of Torman, to deliver the people from suffering, and in the small scale to deliver the peril of a single household and individual, he practices spiritual discipline day and night, and saves all sufferings and hardships. The conspicuousness of that reikō is unprecedented from ancient times to the present. The shinmei of heaven and earth also admire the high virtue of my master and desire to receive his teachings, and every night, upon the great cedar tree of Mount Ora, he causes the starry body of Tanabatahime to descend from heaven, letting them hear deep and subtle doctrines—such is his majestic power. Even if the Brahman army acts in bakkotoryō, by the prayers of Genshinbō my master, that it will melt away like smoke and vanish is clearer than seeing fire. In connection with this, it is said that the daughter of your house's whereabouts are unknown; she must undoubtedly have been captured by the spies of the Brahman army. Do you not have the spirit to request Genshinbō my master to save your most beloved daughter?'

Zhank, while looking suspicious, lowered his head, and for a short while was lost in thought.

Zhank: 'Shīgō-sama or whatever you are called, thank you very much for your kindness in coming from a distant road. Nevertheless, in today's situation, this is no time to let our hearts be troubled by matters such as a daughter. If you have the path to save the ichidaiji of the kokuka, I would like to be shown it.'

Shīgō: 'Ahahahahaha, you too are one of the hypocrites, are you not? No matter how much you speak of the sake of the kokuka, you must surely possess fully the spirit that you desire the peril of your daughter, who has no substitute either in heaven or on earth, to be saved. A parent who cannot love his own child cannot possibly possess the qualifications of a ruler of men or of a village head.'

Junk: 'Ah, I am much obliged by your astute discernment. Where beneath the heavens is there a parent who does not think of their child? Nevertheless, in today's situation, it is fitting to cast aside lesser love and pour forth a greater love toward that which is called the state and the sovereign. Since both the state and the person of His Majesty are already facing a crisis akin to burning at the stake, even if I must make my most beloved daughter a sacrifice, my thought is to devote my full mental and physical strength for the sake of that greater love.'

Seigō: 'I truly admire your resolve to save the state even by sacrificing your precious daughter. If the state, the sovereign, and your beloved child are saved, you surely harbor no attachment to your family's property, do you?'

Junk: 'Of course not. My daughter's whereabouts are unknown, and her life or death is uncertain; furthermore, leading forth my aged body to advance to the battlefield where sword blades flash, I am prepared for death from the very beginning. After my death, what need will there be for my myriad riches?'

Seigō: 'I quite understand and deeply sympathize with your noble spirit. If so, I would like to offer a consultation: to save the sovereign, the state, and your daughter, would you not be inclined to present this wealth to my master, the Lord Genshinbō of Mount Aura? If you bestow it upon that peerless living god, whose virtue causes even mountains, rivers, plants, and trees, and the stars of heaven to yearn and draw near, I consider it the finest act of merit, first for the sake of your ancestors, and then for the state.'

At that moment, the receptionist Seiru entered in a hurried manner,

Seiru: 'Master, the preparations throughout the village are complete. The villagers have come to beg us to set out to the battle lines as soon as possible, armed and ready. The horses are also prepared, so...'

Junk: 'Ah, is that so? Tell them I am coming at once.'

Seiru: 'Very well, understood.'

With that, he hurriedly departed.

Seigō: 'How is it? Your decision is...'

Junk: 'For the sake of the state, for the sake of His Majesty, I shall hand over all my property to Genshinbō-sama.'

Seigō: 'Ah, as expected of the perceptive Village Chief Satosho, splendid indeed! Then, for the sake of the future, please write out a certificate stating that you, with your own hand, are giving away all your property.'

Junk: 'A man's word is as firm as iron and stone; there is no need for troublesome certificates or the like. If this were a transaction between ordinary people, that would be one thing, but since I am giving it to a living god of holy wisdom, would it not rather be disrespectful to present a certificate?'

Seigō: 'Indeed, that is a reasonable argument. In truth, I myself am a living witness. Let Brahma, Shakra, and the Lord of Free Heaven also bear witness; you will surely never speak double words, will you?'

Junk: 'What, you are persistent. Even if I am merely the commander-in-chief of the volunteer army, a warrior's word has no second tongue.'

Seigō: 'If so, from this very day and hour, this property has reverted to the possession of my master Genshinbō. You are aware of this, are you not?'

Junk: 'Please make good use of my property, and for the sake of the state, for the sake of His Majesty, and for the sake of my daughter, enshrine the deities with utmost reverence and enfold our prayers within it. As the hour is pressing, I take my leave of you from here.'

Seigō: 'Ah, this monk too shall report this joyful news to my master, and promptly set about our prayers.'

So saying, he went away in high spirits. Uaikō had been secretly eavesdropping while wondering about the two men's exchange.

Junk entered the living room of Terukuniwake,

Junk: 'Ah, apologies for keeping you waiting. Come, let us set forth to battle from here.'

Uaikō hurriedly entered from the adjacent room,

Uaikō: 'Pardon me, Junk-sama, please be on your guard. That old man called a shugensha is a scoundrel. The sign of sword-calamity floats upon his physiognomy, and an evil countenance fills his face. You seem to have provided all your property to this so-called Genshinbō, but you would do well to cancel it right now; I offer you a brief warning.'
