Chapter 3: 酒浮気 [1685]

酒浮気〔1685〕

§ 1

At the outer gate of Junk's house in the village of Tarai, two gatekeepers, Kō and Otsu, sat cross-legged and indulged in idle chat.

Kō: 'Hey, Banko, isn't there something quite bizarre about the weather these days? Just when you think the great sun-maru is blazing fiercely from early morning, black clouds immediately come over from the west and swallow up the sun; then just as you think the moon-maru is shining, it gets swallowed by the clouds, and in this hot land a piercing cold wind blows, giving one the eerie feeling of almost being attacked by demons—isn't it just unbearable without having a drink of sake?'

Banko: 'Hey, Anko, what are you talking about? Far from talking about sake, that all-important only daughter, Princess Hime, has her whereabouts unknown, and Master Junk is gasping for breath day after day, offering fire-abstinence to the great deity Daijizaiten through bitter trials and tribulations—how could we, the servants of that household, commit such a blunder as to calmly drink sake? We are waiting to receive celebratory sake for three or five days as soon as Princess Hime returns. Even though the master is abstaining from fire, aren't we receiving fine stipends and quietly allowed to serve as gatekeepers? It is an outrage, enough to exhaust one's divine favor.'

Anko: 'Even so, human beings shouldn't just be so pessimistic. Just look into the principles of the universe: black clouds swallow the sun and moon, giant serpents swallow men, snakes swallow frogs, big fish swallow small fish, tyrants swallow men's nations, heroes swallow the realm, wives swallow their husbands, a carpenter's treasures are the mallet and chisel, humans swallow sake, swallow tea, swallow water, geisha swallow people's storehouses, and to top it all off, the creatures that crawl up from under the floorboards to bite us without a shred of reserve and suck our blood are fleas. That being the case, if we spend our days only expending our mental energy solely on being gatekeepers and being constantly cautious, how on earth could human life be preserved? Aren't human beings alive in order to live?'

Banko: 'Stop talking nonsense, is there any such thing as living for humans nowadays? That which is called living is what is referred to as a person of life who makes the finest activities in society with full vitality, dwells in the virtue of love and goodness for the sake of the world and for the sake of people as a child of God, bathes in the light of true faith, and sends forth a beautiful and good career. Everyone, each and every one of them, vomits out like scholars about how livelihood difficulties are this and life is that, but where is life? Anyone and everyone, wriggling and swarming around like mechanical dolls, falling into the dark realm, and while in a half-dead state with the breath of an insect, talking about a new life or whatever—it's laughable. You must be talking about the difficulty of mere survival, not the difficulty of livelihood.'

Anko: 'We are active precisely to overcome the difficulty of survival and break away from our half-dead state, and that is why it is called living. Look at the various doctrinaires of today—isn't that fellow…………… leaning to the left? Leaning to the left is nothing like sake. That's why it's called the left-leaning (sake) party. Ah, sake thou art! Sake is the chief of all medicines. It is the god of universal love and protection, the dawn of peace.'

Banko: 'Hey, hey, Anko, it won't do for you to derail like that, aren't you confusing sake with the left-leaning party?'

Anko: 'That's a settled matter. The virtue of sake is to rant and rave after getting drunk on sake, and in the very end, to slap down the side of the face of an unpleasing hag, drive her out of the house, and bring in a goddess rich in the virtue of love and goodness and filled with the light of true faith to start afresh. This is called the world-reform sake-abstinence (means) of universal love. The left-leaning party is much the same. Since they intend to rebuild the old society and set up a new peaceful society, it is bound to bring about the dark realm of falling blossoms, lawlessness, and utter ruinous chaos—just like getting drunk on sake—at one time or another. It is not destruction for the sake of destruction, but destruction for the sake of construction. Now, because of that, no matter how the master may be, I as for myself place my trust in sake.'

タライの村の里庄ジャンクの家の表門には、甲乙二人の門番が胡坐をかいて雑談に耽つて居た。

甲『オイ、バンコ、どうも此頃位怪体な時候はないぢやないか。朝も早くから日の丸様がカンカンとお照りなさるかと思へば、直様西の方から黒雲がやつて来て日の丸を呑んで了ひ、お月様が照るかと思へば、雲に呑まれ、この暑い国も底冷たい風が吹き何とはなしに悪魔に襲はれさうな気分で、酒でも飲まなければやりきれぬぢやないか』

バンコ『オイ、アンコ、それは何を云ふのだ。酒どころかい、大事な大事な一人娘のお姫様はお行衛が分らず、ジャンクの旦那様が日々青息吐息で、大自在天様に火物断をし艱難苦労をして御座るのに、其家の子の吾々が安閑と酒を呑むやうな不始末が出来ようか。姫様がお帰りになつたら三日なりと五日なりとお祝の酒を頂かうとままだ。旦那様が火物断をして厶るのに、吾々は結構なお扶持を頂き安閑と門番をさして頂いて居るのぢやないか。勿体ない、冥加に尽きるぞよ』

アンコ『夫れだつて人間はさう悲観斗りするものぢやない。天地の道理を考へて見よ、黒雲は日月を呑み、大蛇は人を呑み、蛇は蛙を呑み、大魚は小魚を呑み、暴君は人の国家を呑み、英雄は天下を呑み、女房は夫を呑み、大工の宝は槌と鑿、人間は酒を呑み、茶を呑み、水を呑み、芸者は人の家倉を呑み、おまけに床の下から這い上つて来て吾々を遠慮会釈もなく噛んで血を啜る奴は蚤だ。それだから門番のみに心力を費し謹慎々々と謹慎のみに日を送つて居るやうな事で、どうして人間様の生命が保たれようか。人間は生活する為に生て居るのぢやないか』

バンコ『馬鹿云ふな、今の人間に生活なんてあるものか。生活と云ふ奴は生々として社会に最善的活動をなし、神の御子として世の為、人の為に愛善の徳に住し、信真の光に浴して、善美なる生涯を送るものを称して生活者と云ふのだ。どいつも、こいつも生活難がどうだの、生命がどうだのと学者振つて吐いて居るが、生命がどこにあるか。何奴も此奴も、機械人形のやうに、ウヨウヨと蠢動して暗黒界に堕落し、虫の息で半死半生の境遇にあり乍ら、新生命だとか何とか云つて居るのが、をかしいわい。生活難ではなくて生存難の事を云ふのだらう』

アンコ『生存難に打ち勝ち半死半生の境涯を脱却しようとして吾々は活動をして居るのだ、それだから生活といふのだ。今日の種々の主義者を見よ彼奴だつて……………、左に傾いているのぢやないか。左に傾くのは酒に限る。それだから左傾（酒）党と云ふのぢや、あゝ酒なるかな、酒は百薬の長だ。万民愛護の神様だ、平和の曙光だ』

バンコ『オイオイ　アンコ、さう脱線しては困るぢやないか、酒と左傾党とを混同してるぢやないか』

アンコ『定つた事だ。酒に酔うた上に管を巻き、終ひの果には気に入らない嬶の横面をはり倒し、家から追出して新規播直しに愛善の徳に富み、信真の光に満ちた女神様を入れるのが酒の徳だよ。之を称して万民愛の世界改革酒断（手段）と云ふのだ。左傾党だつて同じやうなものだ。古い社会を立替へて新しい平和の社会を立て直さうとするのだから、いづれ一度は酒の酔と同様に、落花狼藉乱離骨敗、矛盾混沌の暗黒界を来さないとも限らない。破壊の為の破壊でなく、建設の為の破壊だよ。さあさうだから僕は仮令主人がどうあらうとも、僕は僕として酒に信頼するのだ』

Banko: 'That may be so, but as far as possible I would like to avoid sake.'

Anko: 'Though the mountains dry up and the seas run dry, in this world never would my heart part from sake. There is such an old poem, is there not? That is why I cannot give up sake. Anyone and everyone goes about with pitiful faces, swinging loyal facades, drifting on the currents of the world, blown about by the winds of the times, and trembling with fear, so it is quite a predicament. The opposite of sake is uke, or getting intoxicated. Does it not say, "Drink sake, drink sake, and once you have drunk, get intoxicated"? No matter how much you are told to float and drift through the floating world, you are not a gourd, so how could you be intoxicated without even drinking sake? The blockheads who do not know this contemporary truth that left-leaning is right-leaning (sake is intoxication) cannot possibly manage to get by in the modern world.'

Banko: 'Hey, you should not go around shouting about left-leaning and so forth in such a loud voice. Lately, Brahmin spies are coming and going without distinguishing between day and night. You would do well to avoid any slightly dangerous language. Just how do you regard the laws promulgated by Ōkuronushi?'

Anko: 'Things called laws are merely instruments that trample upon justice and injustice, good and evil, and glorify the dictatorship of tyrants. They are demonic institutions where the strong grow ever stronger, the weak grow ever weaker, and vice runs rampant. Do today's legal scholars and corrupt officials not all wear the masks of goodness while relentlessly carrying out evil? That is precisely why it is called a world of pitch-dark ignorance. To save the present predicament, one drinks sake to nourish the vast spirit, becoming enraptured so that one's soul wanders in the paradise of heaven. Is there any savior in the world that surpasses this? Ah, sake it is, sake it is! I am firmly of the sake party. The sake party is none other than the intoxication party. How is that, have you been able to receive my doctrine?'

Banko: 'I have received it right securely, ahahahaha! However, do you not stink?'

Anko: 'Do not talk nonsense, I am twenty-eight years old, at the flowery peak of my prime. I have truly reached the pinnacle of beauty as a man. Even Yoshikohime-sama was just a little infatuated with me, you see. It was precisely because I looked forward to gazing upon the face of Yoshikohime-sama every now and then that I became such a lowly gatekeeper. Even though a horned dragon lurks in a pool, how long could it remain merely a thing of the pool? Once it obtains wind and clouds, it is a monstrous hero possessing the divine power to coil across heaven and earth and swallow and spit out the sun and moon.'

Banko: 'Hahahahaha, how grandly you boast, like a decadent. If you expect to be loved by Yoshikohime-sama, being a gatekeeper shows a lack of wits. Why do you not coil across heaven and earth and whip up wind and clouds just as you say, and be hailed as a man among men? Just try becoming the most popular man under heaven. Beauties surpassing Yoshikohime will gather around you in troublesome numbers. While remaining a mere gatekeeper like this, to let out talk of love and romance toward Yoshikohime-sama is laughable enough to burst one's sides, ahahahaha! Not knowing your master's mind, hiding a sake vessel in your bosom from morning till night, and sneaking quick sips behind people's backs—with such a petty disposition, you will never be able to hold your head high, hahahahaha!'

Anko: 'Now see here, Banko, what is wrong with my drinking with my own money? You are my senior and you always swagger about as if you are my superior, but can people be employed with such attitudes? Making such a fuss over petty trifles—how is the world to be governed with such nagging ways? The more we are oppressed, the more we lift our heads. Resistance, protest, and revolution are our very life! Damn it, if you do not open your eyes while there is still time, an uprising of Anko-ists might break out, overthrowing your position and leaving Banko unable to ever float to the surface again, you know. Eeen! Now that this sake flask I was drinking from in my bosom has been seen through by you, what further hesitation could possibly be needed? To hell with it, it is sake out of spite, and words are no longer of any use. It is direct action.'

So saying, he hardened the demon's bracken and struck Banko over the head, crying, 'Let even thy neck fly off!'

Banko uttered a sharp cry and collapsed upon the spot.

To this place came running in haste Seru, the shimobe of Junku's household,

Seru: 'Hey, what is this, what is this? Did I not hear a strange himei?'

Anko: 'Ahahahahaha, Anko-sama has set forth to execute his daily shiyugi (drinking doctrines and arguments) for the sake of the kokuka's anko and to establish the basis of Banko's heiwa. Yet how fragile a thing it is indeed, hihihihi!'

Seru: 'Hey, Anko, instead of uttering such kiraku things, is not Banko falling into a state of jinjifusei?'

Anko: 'Ahahahahaha! That he falls into jinjifusei is also the work of Ten's making. Since this koitsu thinks only of jibun and does not look back upon jinji, he has fallen into jinjifusei. Despite that, he climbs upon the heads of others and does naught but fusei things, and because he attempts to oppress from above the wine—which is yuiitsu wareware's goraku—asking this and that of it, the spirits of wine allied together and this became a chiyokusetsu kaudō, ahahaha!'

Seru: 'Chiyetsu! Sakenomi things are truly beyond shikata. Come now, I must give him kaihau.'

And Seru scooped up pure yumizu, and without sparing the head and face, sprinkled it with the mists of Ibuki, and... 'O Supreme Brahma, save him, help him'..., offering prayers all the while with sweat pouring like rain (ryōkan rinri).

When about half an hour had passed, Banko breathed anew, and guided by the hand of Seru, staggering and tottering, he was led away toward his own living quarters.

Seeing him off, Anko wore a strange smile,

Anko: 'Chiyotsu, e! Showing a face like a Luohanz statue made from twisted scraps of waste paper, and what is this about wine being this or that? Moreover, Seru is also Seru. Uttering a voice like a frog crushed to death in the dark, he spat out didactic opinions at me. Hen, old-headed are one and all. Yet perhaps I too may be somewhat in the wrong, for chiyokusetsu kaudō was far too hasty. Nevertheless, the master of our house is also to be pitied for such matters. His only daughter was carried off by villains in the night and her whereabouts remain unknown to this day, and upon that scene rushed Ōashiwake-no-ko, the great general of the Brahma army, leading a horde of subordinates, who carried off gold, silver, pearls, and jewels and returned; thus it is no wonder the master feels anxiety. Ah, when I think of it, he is truly to be pitied.'

So murmuring to himself, he once again produced a wine flask from his bosom, and while smacking his forehead repeatedly with his right hand, carried the mouth of the flask to his own mouth with his left hand.

There was a man who came hurriedly threading his way through the gate. Anko, seeing this with sharp eyes,

Anko: 'Hey, hey, hey, where art thou going? There is a gatekeeper here, thou seeest. To cross this barrier, thou must receive permission from me. Pray, what is thy name?'

Man: 'Hai, I am a peasant from the neighboring village, and my name is Inka. I have come in haste to report to Junku-dono. I beg of thee, let me pass.'

Anko: 'Hahahaha! Thy name is called Inka? If so, I might not withhold permission to pass the gate, but art thou of the Rightist Party or the Leftist Party, which? If thou art of the Leftist Party, swallow this wine and proceed.'

Inka: 'Nay, I thank thee, but whenever I hear tales of wine, I forget even the most important business. I pray thee, let me drink upon my return.'

Anko: 'Nay, nay, is there such a thing as rendering another's goodwill vain? First, gulp down a cup, and then report thy mission to Junku's master. One should not live life so cramped and small, crumpling it up like paper. Come, take a sip, now.'
