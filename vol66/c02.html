Chapter 2: 祖先の Blessing [1684]

祖先の恵〔1684〕

§ 1

The great gale of the Dekatan Plateau sweeps away rocks and twists down trees, and even this house with its low roof makes the beams creak with a rattling sound. Nevertheless, Sanyo the old woman, accustomed to the fierce wind blowing as if it were her daily routine, cared not the least, and remained completely calm inside the house which swayed back and forth and from side to side.

Terukuniwake, Terukō, Umekō, Takuson, and Eruson sat around in a circle and had begun questions and answers regarding the actual state of the traces left ruined by the Brahman army.

Umekō: 'My, what a wonderfully extreme and intense wind is blowing! Grandmother, you remain calm even with such a wind blowing, is there nothing terrifying about it?'

Sanyo: 'This wind is a specialty of the Dekatan Plateau, you see. Once or twice a year, a fierce wind blows that might even lift a house up into the air. Therefore, every house is built to dig a basement and take refuge. Such a wind is still merely the beginning of the evening. More terrifying than that is the wind of the Brahmans.'

Umekō: 'Hum, what an extraordinarily rough country for wind. Is this what they call the so-called Fūkokukiyōhei, ah-ha-ha-ha!'

Terukō: 'What kind of wind is it that they call the Brahman wind?'

Sanyo: 'It is a wind worse than diphtheria, worse than influenza. It is a demon wind that burns houses, plunders wealth, enters people's houses, and without aversion to wives or daughters, snatches them all away somewhere. Above all else, there is no wind as terrifying as this.'

Umekō: 'Indeed, it is a riddle calling it the wind-god who preys upon weakness. Now now, Takuson-sama, Eruson-sama, won't you let us hear the actual state of the aftermath of the Brahman storm? For we too have countermeasures, you see.'

Takuson: 'Yes, even without you asking, I was thinking of telling you the circumstances in detail and begging for your help. My wife is named Mīru, and she is still in the full bloom of twenty years; a few days ago, the Brahman army garrisoned this village, bound old and young alike, plundered all provisions and money, and as for my wife, seeing any pretty daughter in the village, they carried them all off forcibly. Not only that, but several miles away from here, along the mountain range of Mount Aura, in a place called the Shinowa Valley, a band of several thousand mounted bandits are entrenched, and from time to time they enter the villages near and far, requisition money and goods, and without aversion to wives and daughters, they carry them all off forcibly, so we people cannot sleep with our pillows high even for a single day. Just when I thought they might suppress the bandits of the Shinowa Valley, Ōashiwake of the Brahman army is more atrociously lawless than the bandits, and we Brahman believers have already exhausted our means of livelihood, taste the bitterness of the depths of misery, and weep over our misfortune. Please, by the divine virtue of Izunome, grant that we be spared this hardship.'

Umekō: 'Teacher, the more I hear, the more pathetic it is, isn't it? As propaganda emissaries who ought to save the three worlds of the visible, the invisible, and the divine, can we overlook this? Ah, my blood surges and my arms leap. Isn't this just like the opening of my own stage of activity?'

Terukuniwake: 'Indeed, what you say is true. We have truly been given a stage where we ourselves must act in earnest. However, Umekō, if we handle matters too rashly we will fail, so here we should leave all things entirely to the Lord God, and leisurely advance divine strategies as the most foolproof plan, wouldn't you say.'

Takuson: 'Yes, thank you very much. However, it is not because my wife was taken that I am shedding tears from a man's eyes. I am shedding tears for the sake of all people under heaven.'

Umekō: 'Ha ha ha ha ha, well, you speak out boldly. Without such spirit a man is no good. I take my hat off to you, I like you even better now. Since I will make you a disciple of Umekōwake, you must faithfully obey what I say.'

Takuson: 'No, thank you very much. However, I want to make myself a retainer of Sensei. You have Sensei, don't you?'

Umekō: 'Sensei possesses divine virtues far too high, and the degree of his wisdom and enlightenment is at such a great distance from yours, that it would be presumptuous for you to be his direct disciple. Divine punishment would strike you. Rather than that, according to the principle of matching mitama, I will make you my disciple. Kami operate by order, you see. Leaving aside order, there is neither Kami nor the Way. Now, Sensei, would it be all right to make him my direct disciple for a while?'

Terukuni: 'Faith is free. Anyway, it would be best to leave it to Takuson-san's will, according to the principle of appropriateness, you know.'

Terukō: 'Ha ha ha ha, hey Umesama, what do you think? From Sensei's eyes, there seems to be no distinction in grade between you and Takuson-kun. It seems that through Brahmanism his soul has been polished to a considerable degree, and a light is shining on his countenance from nowhere in particular. As expected, Sensei is truly great.'

Umekō: 'In that case, Takuson-kun, I leave it to your will. Hey, Eruson-kun, according to the principle of appropriateness, I will make you my disciple. You must be satisfied, right?'

Eruson: 'Yes, thank you very much for your kindness. However, young though I am, I have studied the Way of Kami a little. Even in Brahmanism, there can hardly be two truths. Since Takuson and I are childhood friends, my thought is to share the same action. By all means, please become my brother in the path of teachings.'

Umekō: 'Ha ha ha ha ha, what a mighty driving force.'

Terukō: 'Did Umekō get rebuffed again? That is why the divine revelation states, "If you think there is none like yourself and speak boastfully, you will take a blunder." Even if you plan things as cleverly as plum blossoms, wholesale dealers do not sell things at such clever prices. Well, this time it would be best for you two to become sworn brothers and joyfully serve in the divine work. We do not want any adopted disciples at all. We want sworn brothers.'

Umekō: 'Boss, if it were the relationship of adopted disciples, orders would be carried out in times of emergency, order would be maintained, and matters would be settled neatly, but things called brothers are distant. As they say, "Brothers are the beginning of strangers."'

Terukō: 'Don't talk nonsense. People say, "Strangers are the beginning of brothers." Strangers gather together and make a promise of brotherhood. That is precisely why they are called righteous brothers. Universal brotherhood also begins right here. By joining forces as brothers, even a weak and small organization will eventually become strong and great. Ah, how great it is, how great it is! Brothers (Kyōdai) are the so-called stand of a mirror (kyōdai). Encouraging one another, supplying each other's shortcomings and taking each other's strengths, casting aside evil and embracing good, and serving in the divine work—this is the duty of universal brotherhood and compatriots under heaven.'

Umekō: 'Ah, your repeated sermons are like a white lotus blooming and scattering its fragrance, and it feels as though a new world has opened in my breast. If so, from now on the four of us shall become close friends and sworn brothers, and shall we not become a mirror that clearly judges the good and evil, right and wrong of the world? The lord of the country returned to Armenia out of the joy of meeting his parent, and while the three brothers had become two, we were feeling a slightly desolate mood. Here, heaven has granted us two supplementary brothers, and with all four spirits complete, shall we set out side by side on horseback to advance to the capital of Haruna?'

The old woman, knowing her way around the kitchen, boiled hot water in a black-soot-smoked earthen kettle, poured the hot water to the brim into bamboo tea bowls made by simply cutting natural bamboo, treated the party with hospitality, and plucked and brought jujube fruits that were bearing heavily with branches bending at the rear threshold.
