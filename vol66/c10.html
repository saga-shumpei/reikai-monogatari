Chapter 10: 八百 Long 劇 [1692]

八百長劇〔1692〕

§ 1

Yoshikohime no Daitōmoku, Shīgō, and Genshinbau, the three of them, were exchanging cups of sake and indulging in various malicious consultations.

Presently, a subordinate hurriedly came running up,

Subordinate: 'I report to you, Oyabun.'

Yoshikohime: 'Such a hurried manner of yours, what on earth has happened? You are Kori, aren't you?'

Kori: 'Yes, just so. By the command of Oyabun Shīgō, leading more than ten men, we made a night raid into the Rishiyō house in the village of Tarai, and at last captured the peerless beauty Sugakohime and brought her back. At the entrance to Shinowadani, our comrades are staging a play, so please, as the savior, appear all by yourself.'

Yoshikohime: 'Well done indeed. If she is a girl from that place, she must be quite a beauty. And we will probably be able to demand a lot of gold, too. Ha, interesting, interesting. Hey, Kori, who is the hero responsible for abducting that girl?'

Kori: 'Well, in any case, since I led them there, whoever actually abducted her, it is naturally the result of all my good planning, so first of all, I think the laurel wreath should belong to this Kori.'

Yoshikohime: 'Hearing that she is a girl from the village where I was born, somehow I feel a bit ashamed. And it would be terrible if my face were seen, so I will move my residence to the underground room under the floor of the Tennō Shrine. Genshinbau and Kori, you two together had better handle this well.'

The two bowed their heads, saying 'Yes,' and remained smiling wryly, murmuring that a good snipe had been caught...

Yoshikohime: 'Hey, Shīgō-dono, follow behind me into the living room. Because I have another business for you.'

So saying, she accompanied Shīgō and returned to the underground room. Genshinbau put on stern clerical vestments, made his shakujo staff rattle and clang, and blowing a conch shell loudly, descended the mountain path of Nanatsudagari.

At the entrance to Shinowadani, fifteen or sixteen subordinates placed a single beauty in their midst,

A: 'Hey, woman, up until now you have lived in glorious luxury without a single lack as a daughter of Rishiyō, but by now your destiny is also in the realm of exhaustion. Come, settle your resolve from now on, and will you become the wife of this person, or if you spit out refusal, you will become the rust of this person's sword? You had better make up your mind and give a solid answer.'

Woman: 'You must be petty thieves entrenched in this mountain. Born as human beings just the same, why on earth do you engage in such a cowardly trade? Are you not afraid of the divine punishment of Jizaiteiten-sama?'

A: 'Atsuha, ha, ha, ha! Is Great Jizaiteiten so frightening that such a trade cannot be done? Do not say stupid things. How about it, reform yourself, become the wife of a thief, and do you not have the desire to show your influence as the chieftain of female bandits?'

Woman: 'Eh, how filthy! As people of the status of petty thieves, what are you saying to a noble lady? Step back!'

A: 'Atsuha, ha, ha, ha! She talks back a little bit. As expected of a daughter of Rishiyō, somewhere or other she has spirit. Ha, admirable, admirable. Admiring that spirit, I have fallen in love with you. You call me a petty thief, but I am by no means such a person. I am a fine man called Shōru-sama born in the castle town of Bargan in the country of Toruman. I am an elder brother called a man among men. With the way my chin swings, I am a man who has the free and arbitrary power to decide whether your life will be saved or not. How about it, think it over once and would you like to become my wife?'

B: 'Shōru-sama, eh, what a bother, if you keep dealing with such a nun-like creature, the day will end. Come, wrap it up, wrap it up. Who knows what kind of scolding we will get when Oyabun Kori comes back. If we struggle with even one or two such women, a man's pride won't stand. Hey, all you fellows, beat them down.'

'Yōshi gatsuten da,' and together they take up their weapons and rush to attack a single woman.

The woman, too, is a shiremono without a migamae, her riubi standing on end,

Woman: 'What are a mere ten or twenty Kodorobō? Is there anything to fear? Now is the time to display the meugi of the jiujiyutsu I have learned day by day. Come on, approach!'

so she says, spreading her ōde and waiting.

'How chiyokosai!'

with that, all together, with a mushiyaburi, they take her by hand and foot and instantly nejifu her to the chijiyō.

Shōru: 'Hey woman, even if you thrash about it is dame now. Come, do you submit to my heart or what? Depending on your ichigon, your seishi will be decided.'

Woman: 'Bah, how filthy, I am Sugakohime, the daughter of Rishiyō of the village of Tarai. I am not a woman to yield to a petty thief with a soiled heart like yours. If you want to kill me, kill me. To scatter while being yearned for is the neuchi of a flower. Come, kill me, kill me!'

thus she cries out.

Shōru also... won't Genshinbau come quickly... waiting with a somewhat temochi busata feeling, there is a man coming down the steep slope with a clatter and a clang, blowing his hora with a boo-boo sound.

He is Genshinbau, who, upon Kori's report, has come dressed in the vestments of a shugenja in order to settle this theatrical affair.

Genshinbau, with a loud voice that echoes all around,

Genshin: 'I am none other than the Great Savior, Genshinbau, who received the command of Heaven and descended to Mount Ōra. Looking at this, it seems to be the scheme of a mere whippersnapper, abducting a helpless woman and committing violence and outrage. Wait, I shall presently inflict divine retribution, do not move from there!'

at this booming voice, Shōru and his subordinates, feeling it is a rare stroke of luck, scatter like spiderlings, fleeing in all directions and hiding their figures far away.

Sugakohime clenches her white teeth in excessive mortification and regret, weeping and sobbing.

Genshinbau approaches from close by, gently strokes the hime's head and back, and with an especially gentle and tender voice,

Genshin: 'I know not from what village maiden you are, but it was a terrible disaster indeed. Now that I, the mendicant monk, have appeared, please rest assured. It was indeed a perilous place, to be sure.'

Sugako: 'I know not what personage you may be, but I thank you from my heart for saving me at the brink of life and death. Whose divine deity might you be, I pray, and might I humbly ask your hallowed name?'

Genshin: 'I make Heaven my father, Earth my mother, become the master of the universe, and for the salvation of the Tolman country, I stay my sacred traces upon this Ōra Mountain, transforming into a shugenja thus to guide and save all sentient beings; now that you have come before my eyes, no matter what kind of warped deity it may be, they cannot lay even a single finger upon your body. Please rest assured.'

Sugako: 'Thank you so very much. Are you indeed the famed Genshinbau-sama of Mount Ōra of high repute? This too is surely a divine arrangement and must be some karmic bond. I am Sugako hime, the daughter of Rishiyō of the village of Tarai, a pitiful person with only a father and no mother, but last night a band of thieves broke in, gagged me, dragged me around the wide wilderness, and now here the thief leader made an unreasonable demand for love, and because I would not follow his will, he tried to take my life; just at the very moment when many men rushed upon me and pinned me to the ground, no sooner did I think I heard the sound of a precious horagai than the savior-sama made his august arrival, and thanks to you I was saved from a perilous pass, for which I express my gratitude a hundredfold over.'

Genshin: 'Nay, I am troubled if you thank me. All the myriads of people under heaven are all my children, my disciples. It is only natural for a parent to save a child in crisis, and there is no need whatsoever to offer thanks. Come, let us go together from here to the sacred precinct of Mount Ōra and rest.'

Sugako: 'Yes, thank you very much for your kindness, but at my home, not to mention my father, my household servants must be searching for me in frantic worry, turning things upside down from top to bottom, so might you not be able to escort me all the way to my father's house? And would you not deign to stay there for ten days, twenty days, or a hundred days, and bestow splendid blessings upon the villagers?'

Genshin: 'Though I would gladly grant your wish, this Genshinbau must consult with the gods of Tenchi upon descending sevenfold, and the stars descend from Tenchi to halt at the treetops of the great cedar to come and hear my Kyōsetsu... such being the case, I cannot go off to the Satogata for a little while yet. That being said, if I were to send you back alone just like this, it would be hard to guarantee that you might not fall into the misfortune of thieves once again. Therefore, it would be well for you to accompany me for a little while longer to the Seijiyō of Mount Aura, offer up the sincerity of thanksgiving and Kiguwan at the shrine of Tenō, and tarry there for a Shibaraku. As for your father, I shall dispatch someone from here to send a report, so he will doubtless come to welcome you presently. There are no two words in the Kotoba of the Kiuseishiyu. Come, come, follow along with me.'

Sugako: 'If you speak with such Goshinsetsu, I have no reason to refuse. If it is so, this clumsy concubine shall humbly place herself in your care for a Shibaraku.'

Genshin: 'Mm, good, good, as expected of a lady of clear discernment, nay, a wise woman.'

So praising her, he took Sugako by the hand, climbed the steep slope with a clattering step, and returned to his own dwelling. Already the sun had sunk below the summit of the mountain, the surroundings were growing dim, and at the treetops of the great cedar the brilliant light of a bewitching star was shining. Pointing to the light above his head and letting a smile brim over his entire face, Genshinbau spoke:

Genshin: 'Look, look, my good woman, behold that treetop; just as you see, the moment day ends, the little stars descend from TenTen and wait eagerly to hear the Kyōsetsu of the Kiuseishiyu.'

Sugako looked up overhead, and seeing the dazzling light scattered among the treetops, feeling both astonished and suspicious, her face grew pale and she trembled. Seeing this demeanor, Genshinbau burst into loud laughter,

Genshin: 'Aha-ha-ha-ha, as expected of a young lady raised in the inner chambers. It seems you are afraid of the little stars that have descended from TenTen. That pale face of yours...'

Sugako: 'Genshinbau-sama, due to the extreme height of your divine virtue, my breath was taken away. How wondrous a thing this is to be! Since the opening of Tenchi, that stars should descend and listen to the teachings is written in neither the words left behind nor the writings left behind. When I think of it, when I think of it, you must be the incarnation of a living god equipped with the absolute and infinite divine power; it is so profoundly precious that I have no words to express my gratitude.'

Sugako is a truly remarkable and talented woman, and you have well seen through the very nature of this one's soul up to this point. You are no ordinary human being. You are the incarnation of some noble goddess.'

Sugako: 'Ho-ho-ho-ho, how undeserving, to speak to a lowly woman like myself of being the incarnation of a goddess—Genshinbau-sama, your jokes go quite far enough! You are indeed a wicked man. If you tease me so, I will unconsciously grow filled with arrogance, you know.'

Genshin: 'No, this is by no means teasing; there should be no falsehood or deceit in the Kotoba of the Kiuseishiyu. This monk's Kotoba is the Kotoba of the gods. Sugako-dono, be at ease.'

Sugako: 'Yes, thank you very much; if that is so, for a Shibaraku, until the nature of my soul is understood, I should like to be kept by your side.'

Genshin: 'Good, good, that shall be well. You are the descent of a celestial being from the First Spirit Realm. I shall tell the eight million gods to investigate your heavenly origins. Within this single week, your true nature will doubtless become clearly known.'

Sugako: 'Yes, thank you very much, I beg your kindest favor in all things.'

Sugako possessed a considerable degree of education, and she also had wisdom superior to ordinary people, and she was a beauty by nature. However, no matter how wise a woman may be, when she encounters an unexpected calamity and faces a situation of nine deaths and one life, being rescued by an unexpected person and told by that person... 'You are a splendid goddess,' or 'You are an incarnation'... any woman of clear intellect cannot help but be led astray. Sugako finally fell into the trap laid and awaited by those villains, believing Genshinbau to be the true Kiuseishiyu, and without knowing that she was entering the den of tigers and wolves, she cheerfully followed behind the wicked priest, and was finally led beneath the great cedar into Genshinbau's living room. Ah, what will become of Sugako's future lot in life?

玄真『イヤ決して揶揄のではない、救世主の言葉には嘘詐りはない筈だ。拙僧の言葉は神の言葉だ。スガコ殿、安心をなされよ』

スガコ『ハイ有難う厶います、そんなら少時、魂の素性が分る迄、貴方のお側において頂きたいもので厶いますなア』

玄真『ヨシヨシ、それが可からう、お前は第一霊国の天人の天降りだ。八百万の神に云ひつけて、天国の身許調べをしてやらう。ここ一週間の中には、お前の素性が判然と分るだらう』

スガコ『ハイ、有難う御座います、何分宜しく御願ひ致します』

スガコは相当の教育もあり、凡人にすぐれた智慧も有つてゐた、そして天性の美人であつた。併し乍ら何程賢明な婦人でも思はぬ厄難に会ひ、九死一生の場合、思はぬ人に助けられ、其人から……お前は立派な女神さまだの、化身だの……と言はれては、如何なる明智の女でも迷はざるを得ないのである。スガコは到頭彼等悪人輩の待ち受けた穽に陥り、玄真坊を真の救世主と信じ、虎狼の窟に入るとは知らず、欣然として妖僧の後に従ひ、漸く大杉の下、玄真坊が居間に導かるる事となつた。あゝスガコの今後の身の上は何うなるであらうか。
