Janku of Rishiyō in the village of Tarai, despondent over his recurring hiun, performed the himonoda in his inner chamber; with a pale ganshiyoku, he played the nigenkin while so emaciated that he hardly appeared to be a person of this world, thereby soothing the hanmon and kunau of his heart.

Just at this moment, guided by the gatekeeper, the chivalrous Inka from the neighboring village pushed open the partition screen and, while bowing respectfully,

Inka: 'Eh, Danna-sama, if you please.'

At this voice, Janku stopped his hands on the koto and, while hiding his tears,

Janku: 'Yo, ah, you are the renowned chivalrous leader Inka from the neighboring village, are you not? Yo, well, thank you for coming. And what might be the nature of your business? I would like to hear it quickly.'

Inka: 'Hai, I have searched for the whereabouts of your daughter, wishing to bring joy to you, Danna-sama, as soon as possible; I have distributed fifty of my subordinates far and near in all directions and conducted a great search, but as of now, her location remains unknown, and I have no excuse to offer. I, this Inka, truly sympathize with your feelings.'

Janku: 'Ah, I am truly sorry to have troubled your heart. Through your prestige and kindness, my daughter Sugako will surely return before long. I beg you, I beg you, please continue to help me.'

Inka: 'Hai, I understand. I have no excuse, but thin or gaunt, I am a chivalrous man who has sold his name far and near; I shall act with all my might and strive to meet your wishes. However, in the midst of such lamentation, it is very painful for me to report another matter of concern, but your daughter's fiancé, Sanda-sama, has been gloomy day and night, finding no joy, and eventually his whereabouts have become unknown; I was requested by his father, Maruku-sama, to search for him, and I have been working hard to find him for the past two or three days, but I cannot find his whereabouts, and I have lost my face as well.'

Janku: 'What, the son of Marku-sama is missing? If such a thing has come to pass, why did you not come to inform me immediately, given our close relationship? This is truly a fushigi matter.'

Inka: 'In truth, Marku-dannasama intended to notify you sugusama, but he thought that if he spoke of this while you were in the saichiu of worrying about your daughter, you would surely be deeply disheartened. Thus, he began a sōsaku secretly, keeping it hidden until now, thinking that if only his son, Sanda-sama, returned, there would be no need to cause you goshinpai. However, as his yukuhe remains unknown, and since Sanda-sama is the designated gōyōshi of your tauke, he felt he could not remain without reporting it to you. That is why he asked me today, "Could you go on an errand for me?" and that is how I have come to be here.'

Janku: 'Alas, sorrows only pile upon sorrows. First the funshitsu of my daughter, and now the yukuhefumei of my daughter's ihinazuke, the yōshi Sanda. Ah, is the tendau just or unjust? Can it be that grief must pile up to such a degree? I know not what karmic sins of a past life this may be, but this is too cruel, ah.'

He sighed, hung his head, and let hot tears fall drop by drop.

Inka: 'Your grief is understandable, but grieving will not resolve the matter. First, please calm your heart. Surely the gods will grant their aid.'

Janku: 'Ah, thank you. It is the foolishness of an old man to lament over what cannot be returned, and so my tears fall once more. Aha, aha, aha. Tell me, Inka-sama, could Sanda have been captured by the Brahman army?'

Inka: 'As we are not gods, I cannot say for certain, but since Sanda-sama is always dressed in women's clothing, perhaps the troops of Ōashiwake mistook him for a woman and took him away. I, too, have thought of risking my life to rush into the Brahman camp to investigate the truth, but no matter what one says, they are an overwhelming army, a pack of wolves and tigers starving for blood. Thinking that it would be better than dying a futile death... I do not know if it is cowardice, but I am in the saichiu of agonizing over how to save him safely. Though a chivalrous man does not begrudge his life when he sees justice, it is not the spirit of a man to die in vain without achieving his purpose. I am holding back my surging heart and waiting for the right moment. However, evil shall not prevail over good. Surely both your daughter and Sanda-sama will return safely. Please wait with a stout heart.'

Janku: 'Yes. In this dark world of the strong preying upon the weak, I, a helpless man of Satosho with no one to rely upon, am keeping my thin thread of life alive by looking to you alone as my savior. I humbly ask for your favor in all matters.'

As they were speaking, the old retainer Takuson entered in a flurry, and while pressing his head to the tatami, said:

Takuson: 'Dannasama, I offer my deepest sympathies for your sorrow. Is there still no news regarding the yukuhe of your daughter?'

Janku: 'Ah, is that you, Takuson? You have done well to come. I was just consulting with Inka-oyabun regarding the yukuhe of my daughter. You, too, must be worried, having had your own wife taken.'

Takuson: 'Dannasama, such words are too kind. My wife is of no account, but the heart of a dannasama who has had his precious, only daughter taken must be beyond endurance. I should have come to offer my condolences immediately and sought out the whereabouts of your daughter, but because of the rampant behavior of the Brahman army, my own wife was snatched away, and all the wives of the village have fallen into their poisonous hands, creating a hell of wailing and gnashing of teeth. Knowing it is inexcusable to you, I have been held back by these circumstances, trying to devise various measures, and have thus been remiss in my duties.'

Junk: 'What, do not worry about such a thing. Hearing that you too are exerting your utmost efforts for the villagers, I too was secretly choking with tears of gratitude. My daughter does not matter at all. If only the disaster of the village is saved, there is no joy that surpasses this.'

Takuson: 'If the villagers were to hear those words of deep compassion from Dannasama, how glad they would surely be. Your words soak into my heart like the compassionate words of Kami, and in my gratitude and awe, my tears flow naturally. Ah, now, Oyabun Inka-sama, thank you for your kindness. I ask you, in place of Dannasama, to please lend a hand of assistance for the young lady.'

Inka also, while tearing up, thought that if his betrothed Sanda had been snatched away yet again, it was a reason to pile grief upon grief. Thinking that it would be better not to let this Takuson know, he did not mention the matter of Sanda,

Inka: 'Please do not worry about the young lady. Surely Kamisama will save her. And I too shall exert my utmost efforts through the power of Kamisama.'

Takuson: 'Yes, thank you very much. Now that things have come to this, there is no other way than to entrust everything to Kamisama. Now, Dannasama, Inka-sama, please rejoice. A missionary of Ananai-kyō dispatched from the mansion of Iso, a living Kami called Terukuniwake, has come to this village, stopped by the house of Sanyo, and saved the grandmother from her critical danger. Furthermore, since he said he would pacify and harmonize the evil Kami of Brahman, although the sect we hold is different, I thought that there is no two ways about the help of Kami, and I made a request, and just now I accompanied him as far as the entrance of this house. Thinking that if you asked the missionary, the whereabouts of the young lady would surely become clear, without consulting Dannasama, abruptly, and with apologies, I have guided him here.'

Junk: 'What, do you mean you have guided the missionary-sama of Ananai-kyō here? Ah, whatever the case may be, it must be the matchmaking of the Lord Great Freedom. Come, come, it will not do to keep him waiting at the entrance. Quickly have him pass into the inner chambers... Is Seru not there, Seru, Seru.'

When called, answering 'Yes,' Seru appeared at this place,

Seru: 'Dannasama, what is your business?'

Junk: 'Do you not know that a guest has appeared at the entrance? Go quickly and welcome him. Without even tending to the entrance duty, where have you been going?'

Seru: 'Yes, I have been truly improper. In truth, as I was stationed at the front gate, a noisy voice was making a commotion, so taking whatever was at hand I went to look, and Anko and Banko were in a great brawl. Banko took an iron fist and fainted, so I attended to him in various ways and finally revived him, but it seemed he was about to enter a state of unconsciousness once more, so I could not take my eyes off him and was tending to him. Thinking it would not do to mention even such a trivial gatekeeper's quarrel on top of Dannasama's grief, I was holding back.'

Junk: 'That is an outrageous fellow. Anko is a man who cannot hold his liquor, you see. However, come what may, go and guide the missionary here.'

Answering 'Yes,' Seru stood revealed at the entrance and guided the whole company. Terukuniwake, beginning with Baikō, Terukō, and Eruson, the four of them came to Junk's living room, offered a bow, and took their seats.

Junk: 'This is indeed the missionary-sama of Ananai-kyō; you have been so good as to visit my humble dwelling. By all means, I ask for your kind favor. I am one called Junk who serves as the satoshō of this village.'

Terukuniwake: 'We meet for the first time. At the entrance of this village I met Takuson-sama, and when I heard from him that there were pressing matters at your mansion, hearing that, we missionaries could not simply let it pass unheeded, so we intruded to inquire after your health. In any case, being practitioners deficient in divine power, nothing we do will be adequate, but through the power of Kamisama, we wish to exert the most optimal methods.'
