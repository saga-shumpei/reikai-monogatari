Chapter 12: 恋の Darkness 路 [1694]

恋の暗路〔1694〕

§ 1

Sandā, the second son of Rishiyō in the village of Koma, became deeply gloomy and joyless ever since his ihinazuke, the girl Sugako, went missing. As a result of day and night agony and torment, he developed a nervous disorder, shut himself away in a single room, and came to hate meeting people. His parents looked upon Sandā's state of sinking into melancholy and tormented their hearts in various ways, making a fuss about doctors, medicines, and shugenjas. Yet Sandā rejected all medicines, disliked the prayers of shugenjas, lurked in his room, and from time to time plucked out his own hair, scratched his ears, clawed all over his body with his own nails, and became like one half-crazed. Then, from time to time, he expressed his musings in strange voices.

Sandā: 'Born in the house of Junk, Famous even in the land of Toruman, Revered as a beauty named Lady Sugako, Led together by the gods of binding, When shall we meet, younger sister and back to back, Tying our red fate like kelp, Couples like suffering tangle kelp, Without the reward of looking forward to the joy, This world is tasteless and of impermanence, Now even the whereabouts, like white clouds, At the end of what place might you be dwelling? Willow eyebrows and cool eye areas, Chubby and gathered nose appearance, Crimson lips, flower-like mouth, Seven-treasure ornaments hung on the ears, Nocturnal luminous stones shining from time to time, Hair the wet-feather color of a crow, Movements and demeanor graceful, Like the heavenly descent of the Weaver Princess from the great skies, Born as a person in this world, Becoming my beloved wife, Fleetingly even in the near sky, Scattering and ending like autumn tree leaves, In what place and where are you abiding? Or could you have become the prey Of fierce beasts? Thinking and thinking, it is tasteless, Left behind in this sorrowful world, until when Shall be the shame of prolonging life? Since I too am at the end of being a male, Having my beloved wife snatched away, How could I stop weeping just like this? As long as the thread of my spirit's jewel remains, Even parting the clouds I shall search her out, The face of the princess with beautiful features, Meeting again, the depths of our liver-like hearts Talking and pouring out to one another, Taking and intertwining hands mutually, Making this wide world pleasant, Rather than thinking to pass the days, My head aches and my chest is choked, Sun and moon shine in the sky, With the feeling of being exceedingly dark, It becomes like crossing a dark night. Ah, what shall I do, the grievances of a thousand autumns Will never end, the sky of clouds Clogging the land of Toruman. If you, wild geese crossing the sky, have a heart, Seek out the whereabouts Of the beloved princess and convey My loving heart to her! While saying the world goes not as one wishes, Forsaken by gods and buddhas, Must I taste such sorrowful eyes? Who is it that calls this the parents' crime? True gods have no reason To heavily add even to the soul Of a child born to them The grave sins of the parents. Ah, almighty gods, If there are gods between heaven and earth, Promptly make the wind of Kotobe Sweep away the suffering of the front of my chest! Morning and evening, with a true heart, I offer and pray that the body of the princess May be exceedingly peaceful, I offer and pray.'

Having finished chanting thus, Sandā came out to the outskirts as if for a feeble distraction and gazed at the passersby in the traffic. Sandā was the finest handsome man in the land, a fair-faced youth, who always preferred women's clothing and possessed such beauty that no one who met him recognized him as a man. He stood at the doorway, staring blankly at the clouds crossing the sky, when a white-haired, strange shugenja appeared there making a clanking sound with his shakujō. Seeing his beauty, the shugenja stopped his shakujō before him, softening his complexion:

Shugenja: 'Now, now, young lady, your complexion looks poor, but might there be some worry troubling you? Is it not the disease called love, so common among young women? If so, pay homage to the savior who appears upon Mount Ōra and offer up your prayers. There will surely be miraculous efficacy. This humble monk is none other than Shīgōbō, a senior disciple of the living god Genshinbō-sama of Mount Ōra, a shugenja who wanders various regions for the sake of helping people. Any anguish or torment whatsoever, if inquired of Genshinbō-sama of Mount Ōra, will instantly scatter like smoke into nothingness, and the sun of peace and happiness will surely shine in the sky of your heart. Return in wonder, pay homage, perform Shinto spells, respectfully presented with double bows.'

§ 2

He chants the jiyumon solemnly. Sandā had hitherto been urged by both his parents and by those who frequented his house to seek the prayers of a shiyugenjiya, but his heart had not inclined to it in the least. Yet now, meeting a shiyugenjiya whose majestic demeanour was sternly impressive, drawn in by his somehow reliable kotoba, his heart began to move a little.

Sandā: 'If you please, Shiyugenjiya-sama, I am sunk in an abyss of anguish and agony too heavy to bear. I have grown weary of this life, and was just now brooding over whether to set out on the journey to Tengoku, but if I pray to an ikigamisama named Genshinbau, will he grant me relief?'

Shīgō: 'Have you not heard yet? The remarkable nature of his goreiken is like the jitsugetsu; zennan and zenniyo flocking toward Mount Ōra are like ants gathering to sweetness. From the hour of the rabbit until the seventh hour of gogo, the gunshifu never ceases, and meimei return having received his shintoku. Among them, those who had lost wives or daughters and lived in constant shinpai have had their arika found through the tenganriki of Genshinbau, and there are many who are bathing in tears of joy. Pray, make your sanpai even once.'

Sandā: 'I have heard repeatedly that a miraculous god has appeared on Mount Ōra, but since this is an age when false saviors and false Christs appear like bamboo shoots after the rain, I assumed he was of that same sort and, ignoring the advice of those around me, had despised him until now; yet upon hearing your words, my heart has somehow begun to stir. If that is so, in the near future, considering a propitious day, I shall come to worship.'

Shīgō: 'Ah, that is splendid; you will certainly receive divine blessings. Pray, come to worship as early as possible.'

So saying, with an innocent air and making his shakujō rattle once more, he proceeds far into the distance.

Sandā felt as though he had obtained a ray of light in the pitch-dark night, and stealing quietly out of his home from the twilight of that day, with the starlight on the giant cedar of Mount Ōra as his guide, he proceeded on his way, trudging along as he recited a poem while blown by the night wind.

Sandā: 'The heavenly skies are clear and wide, Out from the grass the Sun-Goddess came, And into the grass She has entered once more, Leaving behind the shining full moon. The light of the stars is sparse and faint, Yet the air is clear over the great moor. In the blowing wind, the rustling sound Of a hundred grasses is noisy and loud. The barking of dogs is clamorous too, From the villages here and there, What is heard approaching is terrifying. Determined to search out the arika of the Princess Sugako, Concealing it from the eyes of my mother and father, A solitary journey hidden by the night. Why must even this lonely field path be so? Because there is my beloved Sugako, For whom I would trade my very life. Princess Sugako, Lady Sugako, In what sky are you now? If there is such a thing as a wordless kototama, Send me a single word of tidings Saying that you are here. Night after night, my soul seeks your arika, Yet though I wander confusedly in the chūin, The immense breadth of this land is boundless, With no clue whatsoever; Wrinkling my sleeves with tears, Even the sounds of insects passing through my pillow On a lonely night of sleeping alone Are mistakes for the whispers of your life, How wretched and forlorn it is. If there is a god in this world, Our mutual bond, yearning and longing so, is brought together by the god of binding; Though I comfort myself with the thought That he will surely make us meet, Understand this dark heart of mine, Oh Princess Sugako! Though I call and cry out, in the sadness of the howling wind blocking the way, How regrettable that my true feelings do not reach your ears directly. Yearning to meet even if only in a dream, When I lie down to sleep, it enters my dream; Thinking it is the voice of my beloved Calling upon me, I find it is the sound of rain, The voice of the wind crossing the moor. You are the pine-moth, the bell-cricket, Truly the ephemeral bagworm Fading day by day beneath the frost. Pray, grasp this truly lonely heart of mine, Oh Princess Sugako! Yearning to meet you, morning and evening, My longing along this path of love has grown, Until now my body has become entirely Too ill to manage. Nevertheless, my thoughts of you— Ah, the wondrous power of love!'

Raising a sickly body, Through wild paths and mountain tracks, Discerning and parting the white dews as I go. Of the living kami of Mount Aura, Receiving the august miizu, That I may meet thee, my younger sister and spouse, As a joy, While bathing in the cold night wind, Treading upon the dewy shibagusa, Taking the brightness of the sky of my heart As a staff and strength, I joyfully climb up, I joyfully climb up.

Sandā plodded on toward Mount Aura across the plateau where night dews dripped, but along the way, day broke completely. His shintai felt as heavy as cotton and utterly exhausted; he sat down on a square rock by the roadside to rest his breath, and finally fell asleep. A biseinen famous by repute, his complexion was infinitely white, with skin like a jewel that seemed to shine through his garments; his eyebrows were cool and his hanasuji straight, his teeth as white as zaugezaiku and lustrous, and his eyes, with large black irises, were tinged with dew. To anyone's eyes, he looked entirely like a woman. He was a bijin who seemed to have stepped out of a painting created by the concentrated seikon of a daiguwahaku, filled with the sensation that a sweet haukau perfumed the four sides and ongaku was heard. The sight of such a bidanshi sleeping with his cheek resting on his hand (hohozue) while sitting on the roadside rock in women's attire (jiyosau) was like a begonia drooping in the rain, or plum blossoms fragrant in the morning sun; it was a spectacle that at first glance left one entranced and sent one's spirit flying into the air.

To this place came Shōru, a subordinate of Shigō, followed by some seventeen or eighteen followers, with dew glistening on their beards and their tucked-up straw sandals rustling as they appeared. Seeing Sandā's sleeping figure, they wondered if it were a dream, reality, or perhaps the descent of a heavenly maiden. Bewildered as to whether it was a demon or a woman, they stood lingering for a while.

Shōru shook his head repeatedly, looked back at his subordinates to the left and right, and said,

Shōru: 'My, what a pretty thing. Hey, if you gaze straight at it, don't your eyes get all dazzled? I wonder if that really is a human. If it's a human, why don't we pull off a little trick like the other day and drag them into Genshimbō-sama's cave? If we do that, he'll surely reward us and treat us to sweet sake again, you know. Right, Kori, what do you think?'

Kori: 'Mm, absolutely.'

Shōru: 'What do you mean, "absolutely"? Doesn't "absolutely" leave the meaning unclear?'

Kori: 'Mm-mm, anyway, it's absolute. I sincerely beg off. That fellow must surely be a transformed creature. There's no way such a bijin could exist in this world, so don't have anything to do with him. Even the bijin from the other day—no matter how much Genshimbō-sama, of lofty shintoku, tried to coax and win her over, she wouldn't yield at all. Petty thieves like us are better off not dealing with her at all. Let sleeping dogs lie. You never know when he'll show his true colors, so come on, run away! Run away!'

They clamored noisily. Awakened suddenly by this sound, Sandā looked around and saw mountain crows cawing purely far and wide, while small birds shook the eardrums with their chirping and twittering. Turning toward the band of Shōru and Kori, Sandā slowly opened his mouth and said,

Sandā: 'Excuse me, you people staying there, I would like to ask you something: where is the residence of Genshimbō, the shugenja of Mount Aura? I came from afar using the big cedar tree as a landmark, but after reaching the foothills I lost sight of my goal and am troubled about my path. Would you be so kind as to guide me?'

Shōru was finally reassured by this voice, and

Shōru: 'It really is a human after all,'

whispered in an undertone, and continued,

Shōru: 'Well, I am a thief hanging around this neighborhood, and you ask me to guide you to Genshimbō-sama's place, but a wicked person like me can never even approach his side. Just the other day we suffered a rain of iron fists from Genshimbō, and all of us are feeling thoroughly chastened (korikori shite iru). Right, Kori, that hurt, didn't it? If Genshimbō-sama were to find us hanging around a place like this once again, he'd say, "Hey, you guys, even though I've warned you so much, are you Shōru and Kori still playing petty thief without learning your lesson?" and give us such a terrifying scolding that our testicles would shrink right up. If you go straight ahead along this path for about thirty chō, that is Genshimbō-sama's mansion. If you don't understand, please wait a while. In another half-hour or so, lots of worshippers will pass through here. If you go up together with them, you won't even need a guide, will you?'

コリ『ウンウン何しろ全くだ。全く御免を蒙り度いわい。彼奴ア、キツト化衆だ。此世の中にあんな美人があらう筈がない、相手になるな。此間の美人だつて神徳高き玄真坊様が何程口説いてもたらしても、お挺にあはないのだもの。俺達のやうな小盗児連は、まづ相手にならぬ方がましだよ。いらはぬ神に祟なしだ。何時、尻尾を出すか知れない、サア逃げろ　逃げろ』

とがやがや立ち騒ぐ。此声にサンダーはフツト目を覚まし、四辺を見れば森の烏はカアカアと清く鳴き亘り、小鳥はチユンチユン　ジヤンジヤンと鼓膜を揺がせる。サンダーはショール、コリの一隊に向ひ徐に口を開いて、

サンダー『もし、そこに居らるる方々、物をお尋ね致しますがオーラ山の修験者、玄真坊のお住居は何処で厶いますか。遠方から大杉を見当に参りましたが、麓にかかつてより目標を見失なひ、行手に悩んで居ります。どうか御案内下さいますまいか』

ショールは此の声にやつと安心し、

ショール『ヤツパリ人間だ』

と小声に囁き乍ら、

ショール『ハイ、私は此辺をうろついてゐる泥棒で厶いますが、玄真坊様の処へ案内せよと仰有いますけど、私のやうな悪人は到底お側へも寄れませぬ。此間も玄真坊に鉄拳の雨にあひ、吾々一同はコリコリ致して居ります。のうコリ、痛かつたな。又もや、こんな処にうろついて居る所を玄真坊様に見付けられたならば、「これ貴様はあれほど戒めて居るのに、ショールコリもせず小盗児をやつてるか」と、いかいお目玉を頂いては睾丸が縮み上ります。此道をスツト一直線に三十町許りお上りになれば、そこが玄真坊様のお館です。分らな、暫く待つて下さい。もう半時もすれば沢山な参詣者がここを通りますよ。さうすれば一緒にお上りになれば御案内もいりますまい』

§ 4

Sandā: 'It is a first for me to hear a dorobau call himself a dorobau. Can you make your tosei as a dorobau with such shiyōjiki ways?'

Shōru: 'Hai, it is only toward anata that I have said such shiyōjiki things. Even if I hid it, your gankō would see right through it at once. Ōkata, ojōsama must have heard from Shigō's shiyugenjiya and come to worship, I suppose.'

Sandā: 'Ah, that is right. A shiyugenjiya passed by my monzen and told me there is a miraculous Kamisama, so I should go worship; leaving everything behind without a moment to lose, I hurried along the night road and have come here, you see.'

Shōru: 'There is kotoba usage like a man, and there is kotoba usage like a woman; as for me, your shiyōtai is unclear, but you are saying such things while investigating our kaudō, are you not? You must be the ōoyabun, Yorikohime Niyoteisama, are you not?'

Sandā was... in jiyosau, so those fellows evidently took her for a woman, and calling her Yorikohime Niyotei just now might be the name of some sanzoku's oyabun. Such kotsupanusuto are the sort who cannot even see the face of their oyabun. Surely he is an ōoyabun holding a dantai of sūsen. Shall I put this fellow into a keiriyaku, and to help my tired legs in walking, have them carry me up... thus she suddenly raised a bold heart, and making a deliberate otyobo mouth,

Sandā: 'Ohohohoho, you appear to be my bunshi, it is indeed Yorikohime Niyotei just as you guessed. Guide me to the side of Genshinbō-sama. Treating my body with care, rough men must gather and carry me up this slope, you see. Come, I shall give you this as a reward.'

So saying she took out koban from her bosom, and scattered them one after another onto the earth; the petty thieves scrambled to pick them up and thrust them into their bosoms, taking Sandā for the ōoyabun, and placing her upon a palanquin, they carried her away, sweating profusely up the tight, slippery red-clay slope, sending her off to the place where Genshinbō resided beneath the giant cedar.
