Chapter 15: 喰ひ違ひ [1697]

喰ひ違ひ〔1697〕

§ 1

Genshinbau, completely infatuated with the two bijin, Thunder and Sugako, and desiring to play the irōtoko who held flowers in both hands, took advantage of the departure of amata shinjiya as the afternoon waned to take the hauchiyō into his own hands and set about cooking chinmi and kakau that the riyōnin would likely enjoy, eagerly busying himself in the itaba with a nejihachimaki and tasukigake. Just then, after finishing the senden of the fukin sonraku, the tōmoku Seigōbau returned with a heavy tread, making his shiyakujiyō rattle.

Seigō: 'Well, well, Genshinbau-dono, despite having takusan buka, to perform suiji with your own hands is fushigi senban; they say that if a person of weight is not grave, he will neither inspire awe nor be respected, and if you act so karugaru, the kuwanme of the jiuchin who governs his subordinates will vanish to nothing. Come now, quickly order your subordinates to do the cooking, and you, kisō, attend before the goniyotei, Yoshikohime. I shall henceforth report the state of the senden to her Majesty.'

Genshinbau, thinking,

Genshinbau: 'Seigō has returned to a bad place.'

was somewhat taken aback, but like the seasoned rogue he was, with an unconcerned air,

Genshinbau: 'Ahahaha, well, well, Seigō-dono, thank you for your long labor in the senden. Due to your day-and-night unremitting efforts, the gathering of foolish men and women has grown layer upon layer, daily building a mountain of people, and since I too am quite exhausted, I thought today to cook by myself, enjoy a pleasant cup of wine, and nourish my kōzen no ki. Please hurry to the side of her Majesty and rest. I shall surely come to pay my respects afterwards.'

Seigō: 'I too am quite exhausted. It will also be splendid to appreciate your cooking. I certainly hope you will prepare a feast with all your heart. By the way, Genshinobau-dono, a flower-like bijin who tells lies, named Thunder, the daughter of the village head of Koma, ought to have arrived at this mountain; you must know of her, surely? Having something in mind, wishing to console the loneliness of living in the mountains, I wielded my flowing eloquence and lured her as far as this mountain. If you know of her, I want you to let me meet her just for a moment, ahahaha.'

Genshinbau felt as though cold water had been dashed over his head from above at these words, but since there was no longer any way to hide it, he made up his mind and said,

Genshinbau: 'Indeed, a bijin with her outer skin slightly peeled off came the other day.'

Seigō: 'Where is that bijin now? I certainly want to meet her for a moment. Once my body is as fatigued as a rope, even if I drink a cup or two of wine, my vigor will not be restored by any means. Gazing upon the flower-like face of an incomparable bijin and hearing with my ears the words quietly issuing from her cinnabar lips is my sole source of strength. Since a bijin's smile becomes the so-called fountain of life, I actually directed her to this mountain with the eloquence of Varuna so that I might use her to wash my life clean; since you are a tactful kisō, you must have surely kept her and treated her with great care. Your cooking must be intended to be offered to such bijin as her. Oh dear, I am truly grateful. Although you are not a person who would ordinarily cook yourself, once you are caught in the snare called romance, you are utterly useless. You become a slave of love and appear willingly to play the role of a lowly servant. Strong though anything in the world may be, there is nothing as powerful as that fellow called love. A bearded warrior who can deceive demons, yet when this white-haired old man gazes upon a tender form whose flower bud is about to open and inhales its fragrant scent, it feels as if he has returned to the days of old, ahahaha.'

Genshinbau had kept the two bijin for a long time under a food blockade to torment them, and today, at last, as the two uttered fragrant words, thinking his romantic desire was fulfilled, he strove with all his heart and effort, squeezing out sweat, to prepare a feast, giving them food of the best taste and most favorable condition so as to gain their favor layer upon layer; just as he had finished making the delicacies, Seigō returned and made him hear various things hard to the ear, and with a face like an owl that missed its midnight meal or a paddy frog chased by a snake that failed to cry out properly, grumbling complaints deep in his throat, hastily preparing the meal trays to make Seigō quickly leave the place, throwing out various riddles; but the ill-natured Seigō planted himself firmly in this spot and refused to go and pay his respects at the side of her Majesty.

Shīgō: 'Ha-ha-ha-ha, this Zenbu is for three people, isn't it? Indeed, it is for the Empress, myself, and Kiden, is it not? Oh, it is not right to let you alone go to so much trouble. I shall carry the Zenbu.'

Genshin: 'Nay, please do not trouble yourself. Tetsutōtetsubi, I shall take charge of it. Come, quickly go and pay your respects to the Empress's gokigen. For the Empress has also been awaiting your return since senjitsu, you see.'

Shīgō: 'If so, let us now go and offer goaisatsu to the Empress. Fortunately I am also hungry, the jibun is good, and the Empress must also be feeling empty in her stomach. It will surely be yukuwai for the three of us to partake together of the hiyakumi onjiki that you have crafted with your own hands through tansei. Oh, it is a joyous thing.'

Genshinbau had thought to make the two bijin happy, and while enjoying the drinks together with a shitatuzumi and looking upon their happy faces, to have itsupai, but just at kubukurin, Shīgō had returned,

'In any case, the gochisō has now come to be carried off to an unexpected place. Furthermore, Sandā, whom I brought to yield to my will through kushin, somehow seems to be the object of Shīgō's affection. Judging from Shīgō's words just now, there is no doubt that he made her come here intending to make her his own wife. Yes, he certainly had a purpose and used his eloquence to send Sandā here. Ah, what a troubling matter this is. Since the gochisō for three people has been discovered by Shīgō, there is nothing to be done but offer it to the Empress. Even if I were to leave my own share and send enough for two people, it would be strange to give two women only a single person's Zenbu, and besides, if the Empress says... "Let us all three gather and have itsupai"... and so forth, even a single person's Zenbu will not be saved. Ah, if I do that, there is all the more reason for this Genshinbau to lose credit with the two bijin. ..."What a cruel fellow who deceived people"... if they come to resent me all the more, the objective of love will not be achieved. Tchy, what a grand dilemma I have fallen into! Ah-ah, if you set up this side, the other side does not stand; if you set up the other side, this one does not stand. If both were to stand, my own body would not stand... this applies precisely to myself. Ah-ah.'

He sighed. Just then, Panku rushed over as the messenger of the Empress,

Panku: 'Moshiboshi, Genshinbau-sama, I have come as a messenger from the Empress. Now that Shīgō-sama has finished his sendeden after a long absence and returned, the Empress is also extremely delighted. The Empress, Samori, and Amori, all three of you, are to drink celebratory sake and partake of the gochisō, and the Empress commanded me, "Prepare the food." When I said this, Shīgō-sama replied: "Nay, Empress, do not worry. Now Genshinbau-sama used his tennantsū to foreknow my return and has prepared a gochisō for three people, so if that is simply brought here, it will be fine." Even the Empress herself was astonished and praised him, saying, "What a great fellow Genshinbau-sama is!" Come, everyone is waiting for you, so please come to the Empress's living quarters. I will have the Zenbushoku carried for you.'

Genshinbau, with no other choice and with a dissatisfied face like a raccoon dog that has had boiling tea poured over its head, left lingering attachments in his heart toward the two women and proceeded toward the underground room built beneath the floor of the shrine of Tennō—the living quarters of the Empress.

The Empress occupied a seat upon a dais of one step higher, while Shīgō and Genshinbau sat slightly lower in teiza, and while ladling and exchanging cups of sake, they let the flowers of their tales of exploits bloom. Panku faithfully performed the duties of serving sake and dishing out rice. Since whatever was said was done by a bandit chief, there was no overly fastidious etiquette. While drinking sake and while eating, they continued their conversation earnestly:

Yoriko: 'Shīgō-dono, your long propaganda work has been truly commendable. That effect has not been in vain, and Kamisama is enjoying tremendous prosperity. You must have broken your bones quite a bit. As an effect of that, Genshindono also seems to have been extremely busy to the extreme. Once you returned, I was thinking of holding an Iraukuwai for you. Come, while drinking and eating, let me hear about your kuwatsudō performance.'

Shīgō: 'Hai, first of all, speaking of my prominent work, starting with Sugako, the beauty of the village of Tarai, and then bringing Lady Sandā, the beauty of the village of Koma, successfully here to this side; while continuing my propaganda further, subordinates of Ōdaruwake, a brave general of the Brahmin army heading from the capital of Haruna toward Chikyōzan, set up camp in nearby sonraku, plundering kinsen and butsupin, violating fujiyo, and worst of all, carrying away beauties and burning houses, leaving no violence and lawlessness undone. They disturbed our territory to an extreme degree, human hearts trembled in anxiety, and it was the opportune moment seeking the salvation of heaven—thinking I must not miss this chance, with the momentum of a lion breaking away from a boulder, I conducted propaganda day and night without distinguishing day from night. Among them, the most delightful thing was that Janku, the village headman of Tarai, raised a volunteer army and decided to head toward the castle of Barugan. His beloved daughter's whereabouts were unknown, and since he himself was now heading to the battlefield, he naturally never thought of returning alive, falling into the predicament of having no one to inherit his immense wealth, which was a pity. Thinking this was a gift from heaven and must not be left unpicked, taking advantage of his disguise as a shugenja, I visited him right at the verge of his departure. Putting on the busyness of having almost no leisure for reception, I bargained with him, saying, "Offer all your wealth to Genshimbō, the heavenly savior who descends upon Mount Ōra!" Upon Janku replying, "In today's case where even the fate of a single daughter is unknown, if I too head to the battlefield, I am prepared to expose my corpse to mountain and field, so wealth is no longer necessary; since I offer it to Kamisama, use it well for the sake of all the people under heaven," oh dear, what a great success it is, ahahahaha!'

Yoriko: 'Your work is not just starting today; truly brilliant, brilliant. It will surely be usable as military funds for a rainy day. As expected of Shīgō-dono, a dog that has eaten wild boar is indeed a dog that has eaten wild boar. Only for that reason are you a leader who need not feel ashamed as a leader of three thousand people.'

Thus she praises him unceasingly. Shīgō, with triumph covering his whole face, opens his mouth wide and laughs while shaking his shoulders, turns toward Genshimbō, and says,

Shīgō: 'Genshindono, my skill is roughly like this. You too should take example by this for a moment. Having received the words of praise from the female emperor, I feel as if I have already grasped all under heaven, ahahahaha!'

He drowns it in drunkenness and laughs haughtily. Genshimbō—having had the trays of food, which he had put his heart and soul into preparing with the thought of pleasing the two nice people for once, rolled up and taken away, exposing a blank expression like an eccentric country grandfather who went to a tofu shop three ri away, bought fried tofu, and had it snatched away by a kite on his way back—with the appearance of half crying,

Genshin: 'Even if it is I, the priest, there is considerable hardship. The opponent changes but the master changes not; from morning till night, toward the crowds of old and young, men and women gathering like clouds and mist, one by one I must deceive them with something or other. Among them, there are gritty fellows who take up difficult philosophies as shields, arguing "There is no such reason" or "The logic does not fit," and how many times a day I feel a chill down my spine and my heart go pit-a-pat, who knows. Each time that happens, cold sweat pours out, my heart leaps, and my belly turns upside down. No matter how much my urination is pressed, as a living Kamisama I cannot go to the lavatory in the middle, and defecation is all the more out of the question—the agony of sitting on the high seat with a pale green face! Unlike Shīgō-dono, who roams freely and strides across wide wildernesses, the hardship of it may be manifold times greater. My kuwatsudō is plain, but it is most painful and its achievements are many. Shīgō-dono's kuwatsudō is, so to speak, external, flashy, and pleasant, and in addition, his meritorious services are visible one by one, so at a glance it may seem that his military merits are far higher than mine, but why, oh why, compared to my painstaking efforts, it is not even like a single hair from nine oxen.'

と頻りに褒めそやかす。シーゴーは満面の得意に大口を開けて笑ひ乍ら肩を揺すつて玄真坊に向ひ、

シーゴー『玄真殿、拙者の腕前はザツトこんなもので厶る。貴殿も一寸、おあやかりなさい。女帝様のお褒めの言葉を頂いて、もはや天下を握つたやうな気分が致すで厶る、アハヽヽヽ』

と酔に紛らし威丈高に笑ふ。玄真坊は折角二人のナイスを喜ばせようと思ひ丹精凝らして料理した膳部は捲き上げられ、田舎の爺が三里もある豆腐屋に行つて、油揚を買つて帰りに鳶に攫はれたやうな気のぬけた面をさらし、半泣きの態にて、

玄真『拙僧だとて、仲々の苦労が厶る。相手変れど主変らず、朝から晩まで雲霞の如く集まり来る老若男女に対して一々何とか、かんとか、ごまかさねばならず、中には骨のある奴があつて「そんな筈がない」とか「理が合はぬ」とか、難かしい哲学を楯に取つて理窟をこねる事もあり、日に幾度、ヒヤヒヤ、アブアブ、する事があるか知れないのですよ。その度毎に冷汗は出る、心臓は躍る、腹はデングり返る。何程小便が張りきつて居つても、活神様が中途に便所に行く訳にも行かず、大便は尚更のこと、真青な顔して、高座に上つてゐる時の苦しさ。シーゴー殿のやうに自由自在に広い原野を横行濶歩するのと違い、その苦しさは幾層倍かも知れませぬぞ。拙者の活動は地味ではあるが、最も苦しく且功績も多い。シーゴー殿の活動は云はば外的で花々しくて、愉快で、加ふるに功労は一々目に見えるのだから、拙者よりも余程勲功が高いやうに、一寸は見えるが、どうしてどうして拙僧の苦心に比ぶれば九牛の一毛にも如かないだらう』

§ 4

Shīgo: 'Heng, no matter how painful you say it is, when you go down seven levels you strike Agemata and while wooing a beautiful woman you can rest, so it is an easy thing. As for Setsushiya, without distinction between chiuya, blown about by arakaze, occasionally chased by police, driven around by the Brahman army, barked at by dogs, threatened by beasts of prey, furthermore bathing my body in the bullets of dew and the swords of frost, the hardship of galloping jiuōmujin through the midst of kuōriyō genya—it is not something that Yamori-sama, residing in chitsukiyo within an ihaya, can possibly imagine. Eh, what has become of the two beauties, Sugako and Sandā, whom Setsushiya, wielding my benzetsu and exhausting senpenbankuwa hijiyutsu, dispatched to tauzan? In a shiyuen of only men, there is nekura no thrill to begin with. Genshinbau-dono, pray won't you lead the futari forth here and make them sing songs or the like as drinking companions?'

Genshin: 'Indeed, though it is reasonable, since they are now in the midst of fasting discipline, even if you call them forth, it will scarcely suit the moment. Their complexions haggard like earth, figures emaciated as though they were hardly people of this world—rather, it is a flower not to see them.'

Shīgo: 'If so, after the discipline is finished, Setsushiya too will leisurely meet the goddess. If the Empress wills, as my reward I would like to be given the woman called Sandā.'

Yoriko: 'Whether Sandā or Sugako, both are the results of your arduous toils, drawn hither, so it is fine to have them as you please. Since I am a woman and have no need for a beauty, in order to bind your courage, it is fine for you to have them freely. Because Genshinbau is an avatar of the heavenly emperor, for a little while here he should not set his heart upon women, but pass himself off thoroughly as a holy man, and until the achievement of our purpose I would have him endure it. Isn't that so, Genshinbau, you too must have about that much of a consideration.'

Genshinbau, scratching his head,

Genshinbau: 'Hai, eh, how shall I put it. Eh, this monk too, passing himself off thoroughly as a holy man, wishing to preserve his trust as a living god, wrings his heart chiuya, but no matter what anyone says, the two beauties, harboring romantic love for me, day in and day out crying Genshin, Genshin, have become dream and reality, pining with love so that now they are a sight too wretched to look at; if we leave these riyōnin just as they are, there is no longer any path other than for their lives to perish. Thinking that these very riyōnin are the daughters of the famously wealthy, we must make them preserve their lives to the utmost as hostages, fleece their parents' wealth, and aim at the enrichment of our military funds, and so, while finding their burning romantic love a bother, letting it flow past like the wind through willows, and crossing the wet banks in a wiggling manner, down to this very day I have consoled their hearts and made them hold hopes for the future, but when Setsushiya's face is not seen they die of vexation. Therefore, this monk, thinking to go and visit them in their ihaya henceforth, teaching and enlightening them with truth as they become sacrifices, maintaining a stance of inseparable non-detachment, and comforting them occasionally until their vigor recovers, I beg of you not to view it with suspicion even should I visit them from now on. Until the vigor of the two of them recovers and they return to their original bodies, I ask the Empress to issue strict orders that even Shizen-dono [Shīgo-dono] not enter or leave their chambers...'

Thus speaking with a self-serving precautionary line.

Shīgo shook his shoulders and gave a loud laugh,

Shīgo: 'Aha-ha-ha-ha, Genshin-dono's precautionary line, nay, barbed wire! Well now, Shīgo is deeply moved. Without that much skill, one would hardly have the qualifications to make remarks concerning beauties. Genshinbau is young in years and has a good face. As you can see, Setsushiya bears frost and snow upon his head, and is in no way of an aspect that young women favor. In this point I must yield a step to Genshinbau-dono. However, Genshinbau-dono, this feast ought not to be something entering the mouths of the Empress and us. The menu directed toward someone practicing austerities, who is said to be fasting, was doubtless prepared as a shadow-meal. Setsushiya, receiving your favor, ate his fill of the feast, but the two fasting ones must be waiting impatiently. Come, Genshinbau-dono, though it is a trouble, go to the kitchen and prepare food for two, nay, three portions. Truly, what a dexterous person you are. Aha-ha-ha-ha.'

Jeered at thus, Genshinbau wears a face like a botched Imato-ware Hotei and stiffens up formally.

Yoriko: 'How amusing is Genshinbau's countenance, A lohan face that failed to cry.'

Genshinbau: 'How strange is this, a lohan face—how suspicious, It seems not to be the words of the Empress.'

Shīgo: 'For the enchanting person shut up in the stone door, Does your trembling wring your heart?'

Genshinbau: 'Slander however you will, I simply Proceed according to the will of the gods.'

Yoriko: 'Let everything flow away into the water—within the sake cup Even the clear moonlight of hope.'
