Chapter 17: 縁 Horse の別 [1699]

縁馬の別〔1699〕

§ 1

Umekō and Hanaka ride together on a shared horse, proceeding steadily with a clicking sound across the wind-swept plateau, aiming for Mount Ora.

Hanaka: 'Moshi Umekō-sama, when you performed Chinkon, you must have received a divine revelation from Ōkamisama. What I have said surely accords with Goshini, does it not?'

Umekō: 'To be pursued with such impatience is quite unbearable, why. Tenki must not be easily disclosed. First of all, let us keep it for future enjoyment and jot it down in Kiokuchiyō.'

Hanaka: 'Will you not let me have a brief glimpse of that Kiokuchiyō? I too know the outline through Goshinchiyoku. And I have also recorded various Shinpi in my Kiokuchiyō. If you would leak just a little of your Kiokuchiyō, I will show you mine as well, na.'

Umekō: 'Ha ha ha ha, you too are quite thorough, Anata. Instead of a Kiokuchiyō, I feel a sudden timidity and can say nothing at all, why, ha ha hi hi hi.'

So saying, while tightening the reins, he looks back over his shoulder,

Umekō: 'Hanaka-sama, please hold on tightly. From here on I am going to make it fly a bit.'

Hanaka: 'Fly however much you please. From behind I will clasp Anata's waist to my heart's content. Come now, make it fly.'

So she clasped him so tightly that his breath was cut short.

Umekō: 'Ah, it hurts, it hurts, I ask you to be a little gentler. My breath will run out. If I cannot use the reins as I wish, I might fall off the horse together with Anata, you see.'

Hanaka: 'Ho ho ho, you need have no such worries. If it looks like we are about to fall off, I will hold you and jump down from the horse's back. Even if we were to fall, strike our heads, and meet Zetsumei, I would be satisfied as long as I am with Anata. To cross the Sanzu River riding this horse would also be quite a diversion.'

Umekō: 'Now, now, Hanaka-sama, you must not say such inauspicious things. Far from the Sanzu River, I am going single-mindedly because I wish to save two beauties.'

Hanaka: 'When you say you wish to save two beauties, whose are they, pray tell?'

Umekō: 'Whosever they may be, it is fine. It will become clear later on. At any rate, following the mission of the Kami, I was given an indication as though the people of purpose were captured on Mount Ora. How would it be if Anata also climbed Mount Ora together with me? If you are reluctant, I intend to escort Anata back to your home and head towards the rescue party alone as a single horseman...''

Hanaka: 'I will accompany Anata to the ends of the earth. Even if it is the lair of demons or the bottommost depths of hell, please let me accompany you. A wife is an appendage to her husband, you see.'

Umekō: 'This is astonishing, Hanaka-sama, once again talking of husbands and wives, is it not unseemly?'

Hanaka: 'Yes, I am an unseemly woman. Doubtless I shall not suit Anata's taste. After all, you speak of going to the lair of demons to save beauties. Who knows what illustrious person is waiting for Anata. Yet, since Anata is the husband permitted to me by the Kami in a dream, I hold the right of first refusal to Anata. Saying such things sounds as though I am feeling jealous, but I am never jealous at all. Just as I was happy to have my perilous crisis saved by Anata, the person in Anata's heart will surely be all the more happy to have her perilous crisis saved by Anata. And since I would love to see the faces even once of the women who love Anata, I earnestly desire to accompany you.'

Umekō: 'Somehow the arrow of Kototama has turned toward a strange place. In order to clear away suspicions, I might as well speak out and explain. Actually, Hanaka-sama, while you were away from home, I met Takuson-sama of the village of Tarai, and when I went to the mansion of Junk-sama of Satoshō, I heard that his young lady, Sugakohime-sama, had been snatched away by villains and her whereabouts are unknown, causing extreme anxiety. Furthermore, even his betrothed, Thunder-sama, has left home, causing grave concern to both families; as propaganda emissaries, we can hardly overlook this. Just at that opportune moment, an imperial messenger of King Tolka arrived, and Junk-sama organized a volunteer army, led a troop, and went out to conquer the Brahman army and protect the royal castle. I too followed that army partway, but no matter what, the horse would not move, so I intuitively perceived this as a divine indication to... save the two people... and turning the horse around, I rushed off as the horse willed, and by good fortune was able to save Anata from your peril.'
