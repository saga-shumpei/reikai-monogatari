Chapter 21: 汀の歎き [2002]

汀の歎き〔2002〕

§ 1

Kakitsubata, exhausted by the agony of her first labor and holding her Miko, had fallen into a deep sleep, forgetting all Zengo. Yet, feeling intense shame that her Saiai husband Adeyaka had discovered her ugly original form, she realized that she could no longer maintain his love. Troubled by the shame of her own body, she resolved to cast herself into the Ōigaha, transform completely back into her Riutai, and sink forever into this abyss, finding solace in listening to the voice of Adeyaka in secret. However, once Kakitsubata had returned to her Riutai, she could no longer utter Jingo, and thus she sang the fullness of her thoughts within her heart.

‘The dew of my husband’s affection shines, And my beloved Miko is now born. Yet, in the agony of the birth pangs, I slept, forgetting Atosaki. Since my husband saw my original form While I slept, there is no remedy. My form, even if I lament, must be beyond reach, Now that it has touched your eyes. Even if I return to my original Karada, I shall never forget your affection for Toha. Our intimate pillows night after night were but a dream; Now Geniu separate our places. As the waters of the Ōigaha are clear, I think it fitting For this to be the Sumika of my body. It goes without saying that my husband, As the child grows, will resent me. Though I became the wife of a Kunitsukami, my original body Was but that of a lowly beast. Thinking of the happiness of my husband, I now Intend to cast away my precious Inochi.’

‘May the Inochi of my soul sink deep Into this abyss forever to protect you. Be it so, whatever the Ukina, I shall endure the shame Of the dragon’s body and sink into the abyss. My Onchichi will surely be startled, And my Onhaha will surely lament my body of Kefu. My husband will surely sink into sorrow night after night, Thinking he had such a terrifying wife. It is sad to think of the heart of my husband, When he is slandered for having embraced a beast.’

Having finished her Jiyutsukuwai in such secret, her Reishin maintained its former Riutai deep at the bottom of the Ōi river.

Here, the gods Yamagamihiko and Kawagamihime, hearing of the midnight event, were enveloped in surprise and lamentation. Ashamed that their faces might be seen by others, they shut their front doors tightly and remained secluded for seven days and seven nights.

Adeyaka, upon this sudden event, was startled, astonished, and filled with lamentation. Yet, being a true husband, he pitied the sad heart of his wife, and every morning and evening he stood by the shore of the Ōi river, singing a song of remembrance.

‘Alas, alas, where is my beloved wife now? Every morning and evening, Though I stand and look at the shore, There is no shadow, and my tears fall like rain. Even the blowing wind seems lonely, Caressing the tears upon my cheeks. By what bond of Shukuse did this happen? Captivated by the gentle form of Kakitsubata, Who possessed the body of a beast, I swore our bond as husband and wife would last for eight thousand generations, But now, it has already vanished Like the foam on the water of the abyss. Ah, my love, my sorrow. If your soul lives in this world, Give me but one word of reply, Speak it fully, Kakitsubata. Even if you have become a dragon’s form, I shall not loathe you. You and I, Were bound by the dew of affection.’

‘The daily growth of Ryūhiko, Born of our noble Miko, and his skin like a jewel, I wish to show to you, but Now it is in vain, for you have become A god of the Yūkai. Nevertheless, my heart Cannot forget your affection, And I wander to the riverside morning and evening. Repeating these useless, rambling words, I spend these sorrowful years As a comfort to my heart. Ah, Kakitsubata, Kakitsubata, Not the flower that blooms Purely and purple upon the shore, But you, who bear the same name, If you have now become so frail, Thinking of it, the sadness is unbearable. Shall I too follow after you And enter this abyss? I wander, evening after evening.’

‘The agony of being lost in thought, Do you not know, do you not hear? Ah, the agony, the sorrow. Though I think to end my Inochi, I have my aged parents, And I have my child who cannot yet walk. At the very least, I must see My child’s growth to the end, and then follow you, Yearning to sink into the abyss. Please wait for me, my wife.’

§ 2

As there is no one around, he laments, bearing a burden of Senmanmuriyō thoughts.

The Tsukasara of the Shitenō, startled by the excessive Ihen, are speechless and gather at the Kariden, sighing in an Aoikitoiki state, lamenting how things will turn out from here on. Thinking that they cannot remain in such a state, they visit the Ima to consult with Adeyaka-no-Kimi on how to handle the future, but finding it empty, they are startled. Fearing that perhaps the Wakagimi has followed the Hime, Iwakagane accompanies the Shitenō—Masago, Shirasago, Minaoto, and Seoto—and rushes to the Fuchise of the Ōigaha. Upon arriving, they see Adeyaka with his Riyōgan swollen, wringing his sleeves of tears, and lamenting over Nanigoto.

Iwakagane approaches Adeyaka with Sashiashinukiashi, grabs him firmly from the Haigo, and says:

Iwakagane: 'Is the Wakagimi here? Do not let yourself hold a weak heart. The Himegimi has become the Mokuzu of the waters, and I know the suffering of your heart. Yet, you are one who has Chichihaha. Do not let your Yumetamashihi be clouded. Until you see the growth of your lovely Chigo, restrain your Yatakegokoro. Since you are a lord who has parted with his wife, it is natural that your heart is disturbed. Even if you call to one who has departed, they will not return. Reconsider for the sake of the country.'

Adeyaka replies to this:

Adeyaka: 'Though I understand the deep heart of Iwakagane, for me now, there is nothing but death. I have set my heart to follow the Hime and head toward the same path. Yet, when I think deeply, the lamentation of my Chichihaha is deeper than my own.'

Masago says:

Masago: 'Since she is a Hime who came from the island of the Ryūjin, I had thought beforehand that this might happen. Yet, if a successor is born, the mansion on the water will prosper forever. Thinking of the prosperity of this country, Wakagimi, please prolong your life for a while. If there is a mistake on the part of the Wakagimi, I, too, have no face to serve the mansion. Though I do not know the heart of the Ōigaha depths, our lamentation is deep. Though I think the Wakagimi's lament is natural, please endure it for the sake of the gods.'

Adeyaka replies to this:

Adeyaka: 'Though I hear your words as reasonable, my soul trembles and shudders, thinking of death. By causing your hearts to suffer, my soul must have rushed into a group of demons. Thinking that Ryūhiko is the orphan of our two hearts, I am at ease even if my life ends. Please take my place and raise Ryūhiko until he inherits the reign.'

Masago replies:

Masago: 'The Ōigafuchi, where even the Shirasago on the riverbed are visible, is muddy today. Clouds overlap in the Amatsusora, and a light rain falls, drawing ripples on the surface of the abyss. The gods of heaven and earth must also be lamenting. The Amatsuhikari is not visible, and the Mizukami-yama is wrapped in clouds of lamentation; the mansion of the noble is falling under a light rain. On this day when the spring rain falls softly, is it your tears or my tears? In this morning when the blowing wind is cold and lonely, I am wringing my sleeves by the riverside.'

Minaoto sings:

Minaoto: 'The Iwakagane standing at the Kamitsuse of the Ōigaha is struck by the Minaoto, which sounds faint. Does the Ōigaha, calming its sound, lament this day as the fine rain falls from the sky? I, Minaoto, never thought I would see such lamentation in such an age. In any case, we have come here searching for you to soothe the heart of the Wakagimi. Pitying the sad hearts of the gods, please return to the mansion for now. Please look closely at the face of your noble child and soothe today's lamentation.'

Adeyaka replies to this:

Adeyaka: 'When I see the flowing river water, my heart longs to die, for the sadness is too great. Yet, know that I am not a rock or tree that does not know the feelings of a human child.'

Seoto raises a sad voice and says:

Seoto: 'The sound of the Takitsuse also sounds lonely, ever since the day Tsubamehime passed away. The purple of the irises blooming by the shore also looks withered in today's lamentation. In any case, death is easy; do not throw away your precious life. It feels as if the heaven and earth have now opened. If you open the Iwato of your heart, we will be at ease again and serve the mansion long for the sake of the country. Please clear away the clouds of lamentation and illuminate this mansion forever.'

Here, Iwakagane and the other four accompany Adeyaka, and they return to the noble mansion with light hearts.
