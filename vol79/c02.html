Chapter 2: Love の追跡 [1983]

愛の追跡〔1983〕

§ 1

Ever since about the age of forty-five, Uraraka, who had never been separated from him even like a sake flask, suddenly vanished from sight; whereupon her older brother Adeyaka was seized with sudden impatience and a sense of desolation, and wishing to search for his younger sister's whereabouts, he strolled through the moonlit fields, raised his sorrowful voice, and gently sang.

The moon shines brightly in the distant sky, Tonight is truly like the Heavenly Land. Does the jewel-dew resting on the grass leaves also reflect the moon's light and shine like stars? Swept by the softly blowing midnight wind, may not the dewdrops on the grass scatter like jewels? The midnight wind that passes, stroking my face, its sound is indeed like my sister's voice. Even the sound of the night wind blowing through the reed fields is mistaken for the voice of my sister. Where can my sister be tonight, her form unseen, utterly desolate? Though cactus flowers bloom brightly by the wayside, Uraraka, the form of the flower, is not seen. Though the light of the February sun shines brightly, the iris flowers hide their colors. Oh, what a wondrous world where the ominaheshi blooms yellow, yet to the eye of night appears pure white! Though the ominaheshi blooms in dark clusters by the roadside, to my eyes she is lacking—my lord Uraraka.

O Uraraka, ah, my sister, where dwellest thou now? Raise thy voice in a declaration! Even on such a moonlit night, if thou art not here, my soul is equal to the darkness. Clear away the darkness of my murakimo heart, like the moon that has emerged from the clouds. O dear and beloved sister, Uraraka, where art thou? I am here. If thou hast life, meet with me, Uraraka. In the fields where I walk, a hundred flowers are fragrant; yet though a hundred thousand flowers vie in beauty and fragrance, there is no flower's scent that excels thine. The land of the reed fields is vast, but if thou art not here, I feel I would rather die in sorrow. Why hast thou concealed thy form, while I am quite unable to fathom my heart? Hast thou not sworn until the very end of myriad generations, art thou not the very life of my body? Though the forgiveness of our parents is not yet granted, I shall never forget the things sworn with thee.

While waiting and waiting, without ever seeing thy form, I have come searching in sorrow. The moon in the great sky too hides among the clouds; I think it is a world filled with precedents, yet I feel desolate. If thou art not here, even my own life will not be dear, considering this body of mine that lives to no purpose. Since the brief moment when thy shadow ceased to be seen, I have grown so suddenly desolate. I have realized for the first time the desolation of thy absence; with thy gentle voice suddenly cut off, remembering now thy smile and the gentleness of thy words, I am doubly sorrowful. Hast thou forgotten the words we swore, that if thou wert not here, I too should not be in this world? Without uttering a single declaration, thou now hast departed alone and in desolation. When I look up, the stars whisper to me, but to my ears, far away, they are unheeded and sorrowful. However pure the stream upon the waters may be, it cannot compare to thine adornment, methinks.

The voices of insects sound sorrowful tonight, translating the color of my longing heart. Why dost thou turn away from my eyes? Filled with uncertainty, I have surely lost my way. My father, a father in truth, and my mother, the mother of my birth, will surely lament. The night fields where the curved god rampantly rages— my heart is drawn to thee who art wandering there. Morning and evening we grew familiar, mingled, and rejoiced; thou art not seen, what a sorrowful midnight this is. The wind blowing through the rustic path pierces my body all the more, this night when my heart is desolate. Ah, where dost thou dwell? Even the notes of the insects sorrowfully search for thee. Like the endlessly winding thread of the Odamaki spool, repeating my plaints, I weep. Because of thee, who never leavest me for a single moment, tonight is sorrowful indeed. Thy voice still lingering in my ears, even the insect sounds are mistaken for thee.

Just as he thus sings, from somewhere amidst the luxuriant growth of daylilies, in the voice of Uraraka:

O my dear lord Adeyaka, my lord of endless longing, thou hast truly come well! Unable to bear the feelings of my lord Adeyaka any longer, I sorrowfully hide in the grassy field. The brilliance of the moon's light in the great sky is just like thine adornment; the maiden of the dianthus flower is bright, moistened by the dew of the moon's light.

かく歌ふ折しも、萱草の生ひ茂れる中と思しきあたりより、麗子の声として、

『なつかしの艶男の君よ恋しさの 果てなき君よよくも来ませる 艶男の君の情に堪へ兼ねて われは悲しく草野にひそむも 大空の月の光のあざやかさ 君のよそほひ其の儘にして 撫子の花の乙女はあざやかなる 月の光の露にうるほふ』

§ 2

Adeyaka felt as if he had come back to life,

Adeyaka: 'That voice is truly my younger sister Uraraka / Come forth quickly from your bed of grass / Should the wind blow, scattering like sleeves in the daylily's / Dew upon the leaves tangled like jewels / Seeking and yearning for your footsteps now here / I have come, out of the depth of sorrow / In this boundless and endless great sky / Shining is only the light of the moon / I too, gazing upon your figure as the moon / In the great sky, have come searching for you.'

From amidst the daylilies came Uraraka's voice.

Uraraka: 'Precious and lamentable are your words / Though I hide in the thicket and hear them / If this be an age when the Magakami rage fiercely / Do not yield your heart, Lord Adeyaka / Because I think of you as the only lord in heaven and earth / I have left my soul in the thicket / My physical body is not here now, but my soul / Having congealed, awaits you in the shade of the grass leaves / Since I now have no physical body in the world / There was no way to meet you in life / Deceived by the foul schemes of the Tatsugami / I must have perished all too helplessly / My soul plays peacefully in the flower garden of the dragon's capital / Not forgetting you / Though I wish to meet you for just a single glance / It is a body that now has no means to do so / The regret of having been deceived, I now / Share the geniu realm apart from you / Though served by the Tatsugami in the capital at the bottom of the lake / I now dwell in glory.'

Adeyaka, lamenting that Uraraka had been seduced by the dragon's capital, and that they could never meet again in the mortal world,

Adeyaka: 'You are now leaving lonely me behind / And I hear you are going forth to the dragon's capital / Though I lament, though I call, you are a lord who answers not / When I think of it, I become still more sorrowful / There is no help for what to do beyond this / I shall die and go to your side once more.'

From the shadow of the grass leaves,

Uraraka: 'Precious are the words of a brother filled with affection / Though I die, you must not lament / The sincere heart that comes searching for my footsteps / I think of with joy, but wait for a while / Leaving behind an aging father and mother / Is it not a sin against heaven and earth? / Thinking of my tarachine father with longing and my mother too / As dear, my bewilderment does not clear away / In order to console me even a little / Please remain in this world, you.'

Adeyaka, responding to this,

Adeyaka: 'Though your words sound truly beautiful to me / There is no way to preserve my life / Sinking in the waters of Tamayako, I now / Think to advance to your mimoto.'

Uraraka:

Uraraka: 'Now then, I shall from here return / To the capital of the Tatsugami, dwell in peace.'

So she finished singing, became a single kuwadan, soared up, pointing toward the sky of Lake Tamayako, trailing a long kuwakuō like a sash, and hid her figure in the mid-heaven.

Adeyaka, unable to forget Uraraka, thinking that even if she had entered the yūkai, he too would cast away his life and pursue her footsteps, pointed toward the waters of Lake Tamayako and advanced while parting the grassy fields.

Adeyaka: 'Ah, lonely / Ah, sorrowful indeed / The jade-cord-like / Inochi of you, my lord, have passed away / The lord of inochi is now no more in the world / While preserving a dewy inochi / Rather than weeping over love and romance / I too am a man, resolutely / The bonds of this world / Cutting them off, shaking them off, in a single stream / I will go forth searching for my younger sister's footsteps / No matter how high the waves and wind may blow / No matter how the darkness may assail my body / Even amidst the fierce beasts / My younger sister's dear yearning / I shall press onward from now on / I will forget my father too, and my mother / Abandoning them, I will press onward to the other world / Even if I touch the wrath of the gods of heaven and earth / I shall not hesitate / Because it is for love, to the ends of the earth / Because it is for my younger sister, even the bottom of the lake / I shall not hesitate, in a single path / Heading all the way to the dragon's capital / Enduring a hundred hardships / To go will be the good fortune of my body / The waters of Lake Tamayako lift up hands / Of small waves and beckon me / The sound of insects singing faintly / Sends off my journey / The moon shines in the august sky / As if guarding the road of death / Thus I think in a hundred and thousand ways / Is the crumbling heart the light of the stars? / When I think of it, it is yearning and sorrowful.'

Singing thus, hurrying toward the direction of Lake Tamayako, he finally reached the lake shore, clapped his hands facing the moon and again clapped his hands facing the lake water,

Adeyaka: 'How magnificent, how magnificent, I am going / To my younger sister's side in the dragon's capital at the water's bottom.'

So singing to the end, with a splash he cast his body into the middle of the lake, and disappeared leaving behind white waves.

『その声は正しく妹の麗子よ 早や出でませよ草の褥を 風吹かば袖に散るらむ萱草の 葉末の露は玉と乱れて 君のあと慕ひ慕ひて今此処に われは来つるも悲しさのあまり 限りなく果てなきこれの大空に 光るは月の光ばかりなり われも亦汝の姿を大空の 月と仰ぎてたづね来にけり』

萱草の中より麗子の声。

艶男は麗子の事を忘れかね、仮令幽界に入りたりとも、自分も生命を捨てて彼女があとを追はむものと、玉耶湖水を指して草野を分けながら進みゆく。

かく歌ひながら、玉耶湖の方面指して急ぎつつ、漸く湖畔にたどりつき、月に向つて合掌し湖水に向つて再び手を拍ち、

『天晴れ天晴れわれはゆくなり水底の 竜の都の妹が側に』

と歌ひ終り、ザンブとばかり湖中に身を投じ、あと白波と消え失せにける。
