Chapter 3: 離れ Island [1984]

離れ島〔1984〕

§ 1

Uraraka was snatched away by a monster of human face and dragon body in the lake, and while being carried away through the dark clouds for a while to somewhere with the speed of a shooting star, she was let down before a certain gate of purple walls and blue tiles, and when she suddenly looked around, it was right before the front gate of the capital of the dragon gods floating flush with the lake water.

Countless dragon gods of human face and dragon body, crying 'Oo, oo', draped their bodies in garments of algae, leaving only their faces exposed, sat in rows by the hundreds and thousands on both sides of the gate to welcome her.

The king of the dragon gods sings in a clear voice: 'Ah, how pitiful, how pitiful, Waited upon for a hundred, a thousand years, The beloved daughter of the ancestral deities of the land, Urarakahime is welcomed here now, From this day forth, becoming the ruler of the capital of dragons, All of our clans Shall have this appearance of human face and dragon body Reborn into human forms, And make all the dragon gods Revive into the figures of the earthly deities, So that the blessings of heaven and earth May shine even into the depths of the lake, O Uraraka no Hime! Oo, oo. Ah, how bright and joyful, The iron gates of the capital of dragons now Have begun to open as clearly and brightly As if they opened simultaneously in heaven and earth, Ah, how reverend! Ah, how bright and joyful, Ah, how wonderful is this coming forth!'

With this, each played various kinds of music to express their welcome, and the dragon gods stood up all at once, appearing and disappearing on land and in the lake, dancing wildly to voices that seemed enough to shatter heaven and earth.

Uraraka, overwhelmed by the utterly unfamiliar spectacle, could not dispel her sense of doubt for a little while, unable to forget tsuyao, her beloved elder brother, and remained silently looking down while worrying about the safety of her parents.

The king of the dragon gods smiled gently, took Uraraka's hand softly, and said: 'This island, which you may find mysterious, Is the capital where the dragon gods dwell. Without you, the dragon gods forever Would maintain this wretched form— Only the head is born human, while the body Is this wretched shape of a dragon.'

Hereupon Uraraka made up her mind that there was no help for it anymore, and resolved to wed this wretched tribe of human face and dragon body, and to bear human descendants, and bravely tried to sever her romantic feelings toward Tsuyao, but somehow a shred of attachment remained at the bottom of her heart.

'Could I have imagined it would not be Tsuyao no Kimi, But the lord of the capital of dragons? If it be thus, what is there to lament? From this day forth, I shall serve the lord of the capital of dragons. My nursing father and mother, surely, surely, Must be lamenting over my absence. And he who is the very life within my heart, Surely he too must be lamenting. Yet comforting myself with the thought that this is a divine work To save the multitude of the dragon gods, I never realized until this very moment That such a capital existed beneath the water. If this be a dream, let me awaken quickly; I have been lured Into an unfamiliar land. Welcomed by ten thousand dragon gods, I feel as though I am treading a dream road. O king of the dragon gods and all you clansmen, I am from this day the ruler of the land. As one who has abandoned parents and abandoned love, How could I falter in this island country? If I had no power to assent to these words, I would set off on a journey to Yomi. Though lowly, I am the child of the ancestral deities of the land, How could I possibly fear you dragon gods?'

The king of the dragon gods knelt before Uraraka where she stood straight and said: 'How grateful are your sacred words, O Uraraka no Kimi, Which shall never waver though ages pass. The savior god of the dragon gods, Who bore such wretched forms, has descended from heaven. With the fulfillment of a hundred-year prayer, I now Have met the savior god at last. Until now, reviled by the lake demons and the earthly deities, We lived without affliction. From this day forth, renewing our true hearts, We shall humbly serve the divine works of heaven and earth. The island root of Ryūgū, connected to Mount Ibuki, Shall be our eternal dwelling place. Your lordship shall appear from today as the younger princess of Ryūgū, To protect us and watch over us, And as long as the younger princess of Ryūgū is here, This island root shall remain peaceful.'

Uraraka sings: 'The great sky is high and boundless, the lake bottom Is deep and vast; by the divine majesty, Let all the tribes of fish, without exception, submit To my virtue, and I shall guard them in peace. Born as the child of the earthly deities, to become the ruler Of this island—is this the will of the gods? Is the moonlight seen on this island not a light Equal to that seen on Mount Mizukami? Let both moon and sun shine without a shadow; the lord of the island Roots of Ryūgū is here. When night falls, the moon shines brightly in the sky, To peacefully illuminate this island root. When morning comes, the heavenly sun shines brilliantly, To illuminate even the depths of the lake. Let the moon shine clear, let the stars twinkle, let the sands Of the lake bottom gleam, let the fish live! After a hundred years, would that I could turn The unsightly bodies of the dragon gods into human forms. Wedded now as the queen of the dragon gods, I think to bear perfect children.'

Since I descended to this isle, even the trees and grasses have suddenly increased their light.'

To such a place, from far in the distance, the dragon gods came carrying a mikoshi adorned with gold, silver, menau, ruri, shiyako, sango, suishiyō, and the like, lowering it with a heavy thud right in front of where Otohime stood and prostrating themselves.

The king of the dragon gods, pointing to this palanquin, turned toward Otohime and sang:

'Come, ride the august okago; having passed a momotose and a chitose, this is a palanquin crafted for you, my lord.

A mikoshi that has not been used even hitotabi; I will offer it to you as my magokoro.

Using our hearts through a momotose and a chitose, we crafted the palanquin with arayuru treasures.

Other than you, my lord, we think there is no kami upon the earth who should ride this palanquin.'

Uraraka, with eyes wide in a kiyōi beyond all iguwai, said:

'Did I ever think that in such a Shimane there would be a palanquin of such uraraka treasures?

Since it is a palanquin imbued with the hearts of the dragon gods, I too shall not refuse; shall I board it and proceed?'

The king of the dragon gods greatly rejoiced and sang:

'How arigata are the mikotonori of Otohimegami;

to hear them is the inochi of wagami.

This mikoshi, crafted in exchange even for our inochi;

pray, board it quickly, O Otohime no Kami.

The momogami must surely rejoice, now that our devotion through momochitose has been rewarded.'

Here Urarakahime, feeling as though in a yumemi within a dream, opened the iron doors of the mikoshi, stepped swiftly inside, and assumed a posture of standing firm and motionless; yet there was no anxiety of her head touching the ceiling, for it was a mikoshi of the utmost height and breadth, and the dragon god tribe carrying the long poles were a multitude countless by hundreds.

The king of the dragon gods stood before the mikoshi, waving heihoku to lead the vanguard, and proceeded toward a palace far in the distance.

The dragon gods raised a unanimous cry of 'Oooh, oooh,' and with a vigor as if to shake heaven and earth, advanced solemnly and in orderly procession.

Going about twenty chō or so, there stood majestically a large tower gate adorned with seven treasures. Dragon gods clad in white garments instantly swung open the iron doors to the left and right. The mikoshi solemnly passed into the gate. The voices of kalavinka drifted from the treetops here and there, and its solemnity was beyond the power of language to express; the mikoshi was reverently lowered at the entrance of the Great Dragon Hall.

The king of the dragon gods sang:

'This indeed is the mansion where I dwell, an eternal realm;

come, advance as far as the inner hall.

The great hall, fashioned by the artisans of a momotose,

has been awaiting your august appearance.

A great hall that has at last been built;

first, do you, O inochi, dwell within and govern it.'

Uraraka opened the door of the mikoshi, and in a pure and clear figure, calmly stepped out into the pure courtyard, following behind the king of the dragon gods to advance deep into the inner hall.

From this time forth, Uraraka was hailed as Otohime of the Dragon Palace, gave the name of Ōryūmihiko no Mikoto to the king of the dragon gods, and came to shine brightly as the ruler of this isle.

Uraraka, unawares, was invited by the dragon gods and became the ruler of the isle.

Leaving her traces upon this isle, she was revered as Otohimegami of the Dragon Palace.

Without the meritorious deeds of this kami, the fish tribes within the sea would never flourish through eternity.
