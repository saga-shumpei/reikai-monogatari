Chapter 19: Great 井の淵 [2000]

大井の淵〔2000〕

§ 1

Since the return of Adeyaka, the Kamuyakata of Minakamiyama suddenly transformed into a Kuwanrakukiyō, where the Kunitsukamira of this region, partaking of abundant fruits and Yasai, knew no suffering of Seikuwatsu, bathed in the blessings of Tenchi, and Ōka the Miyo of Shichitaihei.

Adeyaka cherished and loved Kakitsubata as something beyond compare, and would go out to the Teien morning and evening, strolling together hand in hand; yet for some reason, Kakitsubata favored the Suihen, and it was her custom to gaze intently at the Seiriu of the Ōigaha without floating away.

Adeyaka, wondering at the Yōsu of Kakitsubata, sought to probe the heart of Hime through song, and sang with a clear voice.

Adeyaka: 'Standing by this river of clear water, / The mystery of you, who seem lost in Monomo. / When I look closely at your face, in your eyes, / You let red tears well up. / Is it because you long for your Furusato, / That your face, as you stand by the Migihabe, is so lonely? / Even the Migihabe where Kakitsubata bloom and scent, / Are things one should never tire of seeing, no matter how Ikutabi. / I have grown weary of playing by the river, / Yet you seem filled with longing.'

Kakitsubata sang in an Obotsuka voice.

Kakitsubata: 'Your Kotoba, my Lord, are awe-inspiring; I only / Long for the flowing water. / If I do not gaze at the flow of the river water once a day, / My body feels as if it will burn. / Do I retain the nature of a Ryūjin in my body, / That I find the flowing water so dear? / If I had your permission, my Lord, I would cast off my robes, / And immerse myself in this river.'

Adeyaka sang in reply.

Adeyaka: 'Your wish is easy to grant, my dear; / Immerse yourself as your heart desires. / The water of the Ōigaha, flowing through moon and sun, / Is a spring where the scent of flowers drifts. / The Masago on the riverbed also shines, as the water flows, / Permeating the skin as the sun flows through. / When evening falls, the moon flows down the Ōigaha; / As I sit and watch, my own body feels purified.'

Kakitsubata sang.

Kakitsubata: 'It is embarrassing to become stark naked; I pray, / I wish to enter the river alone. / I know not if I scatter the flowers of your heart, / But I wish to bathe alone in the evening river.'

Adeyaka sang, looking slightly displeased.

Adeyaka: 'You speak of heartless things; are we not a pair / Bound by a vow like that of mandarin ducks? / The autumn wind blows against the surface of the river, / And the Hagi of the shore seems about to scatter. / Even if your heart should drift away, / I shall never forget your noble appearance.'

Kakitsubata sang.

Kakitsubata: 'To think that I would disobey the sincerity / Of you, my Lord, who think of me so deeply, is sorrowful. / From time to time, I return to my original form, / And wish to purify my body. / It is because of the pain of being seen by you in that form, / That I spoke such heartless words. / My dear Lord, look upon me again with a broad heart, / And simply grant the wish of your servant.'

Adeyaka sang.

Adeyaka: 'It is not that I do not know of your suffering, / But the pain of being apart from you, even for a moment, is great. / Why is it that above this river, / Smoke rises from the Fujigaoka?'

Kakitsubata sang.

Kakitsubata: 'The burning heart that longs for you is ignited, / And perhaps the water and fire of the Ryūjin are burning. / I pray you, dam this river water, / And let the water stagnate.'

Adeyaka sang.

Adeyaka: 'I grant your wish, and from tomorrow, / I shall have the Kunitsukamira dam it. / Since the depth of your heart has become a deep pool, / I shall dam this flowing water and see.'

Kakitsubata sang.

Kakitsubata: 'I am grateful for your pure sincerity; / Thinking of your depth, I am moved to tears.'

Thus, while speaking to each other through songs, as the moon tilted in the evening, they returned toward the mansion.

Adeyaka called together many Kunitsukamira and had them gather large and small rocks scattered in the river to build a weir to dam the water of the Ōigaha. The river gradually deepened, becoming a deep pool of several tens of Jō.

This weir was named the Ōi no Seki. The river water overflowing the Ōi no Seki became a waterfall and descended downstream. Its grandeur was as if the Milky Way were falling straight toward the earth.

Iwagane, Masago, Shirasuna, Mizuoto, and Seoto, who served in the positions of the Four Heavenly Kings, wishing to see the grandeur of this Takitsuse, attempted a half-day of pure leisure, each singing of the extent of their hearts.

§ 2

The song of Iwagane.

Iwagane: 'How majestic and pure is the Ōigaha, / With the sound of its rushing waters falling from high. / The high waterfall hanging at the foot of Minakamiyama, / I have never heard of such a thing since the Kamiyo. / May the Kahaseki, built firmly against the Iwagane, / Remain unshaken for a thousand, nay, eight thousand generations. / The river water scatters like jewels, and as it falls, / Shining over the Takaseki, how majestic and pure it is. / Even the Kotodaki of Riugūjima, famed for its sound, / Cannot compare to this Takitsuse. / The sound of the Takitsuse falling in torrents is pure, / And at night it roars with even greater intensity. / Since the Takaseki was built, the water has deepened, / And hundreds of Urokuzu have gathered here. / If one were to take a boat and play in these deep pools, / How pure and refreshed one's heart would become. / The water has become so deep that the Kahasoko cannot be seen, / And the flow has surely become gentle. / Since this weir was built, the river water pours, / Into the noble Misono of Minakamiyama.

On the surface of the garden, the Ōna and Sana are lively, / Playing and celebrating a spring of a thousand years.'

Masago sings.

Masago: 'The water is deep, the sound of the weir is profound, / And the Masago of the Kahasoko can no longer be seen. / The Ōiha are piled high to build this, / And this Takaseki is clear to behold. / I am troubled, wondering what will happen, / If it rains heavily for days on end. / The sound of the Takitsuse echoes day and night, / And the Misono of Minakamiyama is bustling. / The river water has suddenly turned into a deep pool, / And its depths can no longer be seen. / This pool, where the Tatsugami dwells, / Is so fearsome that even a single glance makes one tremble. / Do you know or do you not know, / The deep Himitsu of this pool that holds the blue water? / It is charming to think that this pool and its currents, / Became so at the request of Kakitsubahime-no-Mikoto.'

Shirosuna sings.

Shirosuna: 'The water above the Ōiseki forms a pool, / Yet the Shirosuna at the bottom can be clearly seen. / How deep must this pool and its currents be, / To hold the water until the color of the Shirosuna turns blue? / The sincerity of Kakitsubahime-no-Mikoto, / Must be deeper than this pool and its currents. / There is nothing that she wishes for that does not come to pass, / The design of Tsuyoo-no-Kimi is truly brilliant.'

Mizuoto sings.

Mizuoto: 'The Ōiseki hangs high and the water falls and churns, / The Mizuoto echoes with sudden strength. / The sound of the water echoes high, and the Ōigaha, / And the reeds on the shore sway violently. / The Fujinami and the Yamabuki flowers, / Can be seen hanging like blinds beneath the water's surface. / The sound of the Kajika singing on the shore, / Drowns out the sound of the water and grows louder. / The Kajika have gathered on the shore of this pool, / And are singing that this is a pure place. / Even the sound of the water biting the rocks has subsided, / And the Ōi-no-Fuchi has become deep with water. / The view of the Ōi-no-Fuchi, where both sun and moon float quietly, / Is beautiful, and the flowers are reflected too.'

Seoto sings.

Seoto: 'The water of the Ōigaha roars, / And the Seoto that echoed morning and evening has vanished. / The Ōigaha, which once flowed shallow and clear, / Has deepened due to the height of the weir. / Where has the Seoto gone? Now, only, / The flapping of ducks' wings can be heard. / The water birds have gathered from here and there, / Floating and diving as if to eat the Urokuzu. / In any case, it is a wonderful thing, / That this weir was built on this river, which did not exist even in the Kamiyo.'

Each of the presiding deities and others praised the waterfall water, and as dusk fell, they headed home pointing toward their houses.

When the light of the evening moon cast its silver glow and rose from the ridge of Takamitsuyama, Kakitsuba thought it was the perfect time to play in the currents of the Ōi River, and after bowing silently to Tsuyoo, she left, leaving behind words that he must by no means follow her.

Tsuyoo, finding Kakitsuba's words impossible to understand, pretended to be indifferent and nodded once, but as time passed and his wife did not return, he became anxious, secretly followed the garden path under the moon, hid himself among the reeds by the river, and watched his wife's movements.

Kakitsuba stripped naked and jumped into the water, exposing only her head, and as she returned to her original sword-skin form and washed away the insects that had grown densely between her scales, the pool was so deep that her figure was invisible to Tsuyoo's eyes, and only her head shone under the moon.

While bathing in the pool water for the first time in a long while, Kakitsuba was struck by its refreshing nature and sang unconsciously.

'Ah, how joyful! / In this pool where the moon floats, / As I immerse my Karada, / The insects of the skin that troubled me daily / Flee in fear of the Mamizu. / Today, for the first time, I feel as if I am truly alive. / How wretched / Is the appearance of my own body! / If it were to meet the eyes of my lord, / The vow of Imose would instantly / Be broken, and until this day / I have endured such suffering. / The Onnasake of my lord / Has granted me this Kiyofuchi, / And to play here evening after evening / Is a comfort; my soul / Is reviving and rejoicing. / Were it not for this Kahafuchi, / My Inochi could not be sustained, / Troubled by the Sannetsu of the day. / For me, even a single Suiyoku / Is like the Tengokujiyōdo. / Ah, how reliable, how pure! / May this Takaseki / Remain unbroken for Toha. / May the water of the Ōi river be pure, / Flowing and flowing, and may it preserve / My dew-like Inochi for Toha. / The Toshitsuki I have spent / Worrying that the thread of fate might break, / As my lord gazes upon my form / And fears what he sees, / Are now flowing away peacefully in the Kahamizu; / Its merit is truly magnificent. / Ah, Kamunagara, Lord God, / Living by Thy grace, may my form / Never touch the Onme of my lord / For Ikuchiyo to come, / And may our bond flourish for Toha. / Amatsuonkami, Kunitsukami, / Suijin who guards the Kahasoko, / Before Thy Mimahe I humbly pray, / Before Thy Mimahe I humbly pray.'

She sang this with a Yukuwai air. Adeyaka, seeing the figure of the princess looking so Yukuwai, rejoiced greatly; though the moon was clear, it was night, and since only her gentle face above the water was visible, he did not notice in the slightest that she had returned to her dragon form. He parted the reeds, stood at the water's edge, and sang:

'Though I wait long for your shadow, you do not return; / I have come to meet you, finding the night lonely. / Your face floats in the clear waves, / Like the moon that appears so white. / The moon floating on the surface of the water is like your face, / Resembling it, shining purely like a jewel. / Do you not remember the Ryūgū, / As you bathe in this new pool?'

Kakitsubata, with her white face still floating on the surface of the water, kept her Karada hidden and sang a song in response:

'I have grown weary of the island of Ryūgū, / For you, my flower-like lord, are not there. / By performing Misogi night after night in this clear pool, / My soul is renewed, and I am joyful. / When I look at the sky, the moon is clear; when I look at the bottom of the water, / The white sand is shining. / By performing Misogi night after night in this clear pool, / I am one who enjoys the Inochi of life. / My lord, please return soon; for a little while longer / I shall finish my Misogi and return. / Because I am ashamed to have my naked body seen, / Please forgive my heartless words.'

Adeyaka, without a shadow of doubt at his wife's words, said:

'Very well then, return soon; I shall go ahead of you / And return to the mansion.'

Saying this, he walked quickly, following the path of flowers fragrant under the moon. After seeing him off, Kakitsubata, as if she had become somewhat relieved, suddenly dashed about the bottom of the water, front and back, left and right, rampaging wildly. While slapping the surface of the water with her sword-like tail, she tried to shake off every single maggot living between her scales, sending up high sprays of water. Eventually, she crawled up to the water's edge, recited a spell reverently, transformed into a perfect human form, and draped herself in white robes. Wearing a long scarlet hakama on her lower body, she walked slowly and calmly toward her husband's mansion, as if nothing had happened. The moon shone brightly, illuminating the surface of the Ōi river, and the waves were still, with only the sound of the Takitsuse echoing loudly.

『君のかげ気長く待てど帰りまさぬ 夜を淋しみ迎へ来つるも 汝が面清き波間に浮び出でて 真白く見えぬ月の如くに 水の面に浮べる月は汝が面に まがひて清し玉と映えつつ 新しきこれの淵瀬に浴み給ふ 君は竜宮を思ひ出さずや』

燕子花は水の面に白き顔を浮かせたるまま、身体をかくして応への歌をうたふ。

『竜宮の島根はもはや飽きにけり 花なる君のおはしまさねば 此清き淵に夜な夜な禊して あらたまりゆくわが魂嬉しも 空見れば月は冴えたり水底見れば 真砂は白く輝きにけり 此清き淵瀬に夜な夜な禊して 生きの生命を楽しむわれなり 背の君は早や帰りませよ今暫し 妾は禊すまして帰らむ 裸身を見らるる事の恥かしさに 情なき言葉を許させ給へ』

艶男は妻の言葉に、何の疑ふ色もなく、

『いざさらば早や帰りませわれは今 汝に先立ち館に帰らむ』

と言ひつつ足ばやに、月下に匂ふ花の径を辿りて帰り行く。後見送りて燕子花は、稍安心したものの如く、忽ち水底を前後左右に駈け巡り、暴れ狂ひ、太刀膚の尻尾を以て水面をはたきながら、鱗の間に棲息せる蛆を一匹も残らず払はむとして、高き水煙を立てゐたりけるが、漸くにして汀辺に這ひ上り、恭しく呪文を唱へ、全き人体と化して白衣を身に纒ひ、下半身は緋の長袴を穿ち、何喰はぬ顔にてしづしづと、夫の館をさして帰り行きぬ。月は皎々として大井の淵の水面を照し、波も静かに滝津瀬の音の高く響くばかりなりける。
