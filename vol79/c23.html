Chapter 23: 二名の Island [2004]

二名の島〔2004〕

§ 1

In the region of Minakamiyama, natural disasters and strange phenomena continued for several days; thunder roared, lightning flashed, and violent storms arrived incessantly. The torrential rain fell like a waterfall, and ground tremors and vibrations occurred continuously. The Ōi-ga-seki weir saw muddy waters flowing down in torrents, and the roaring water force, harmonizing with the thunder, became a great commotion that seemed to split the ears.

In the abyss of Ōi, four dragon gods glared at each other, continuing their struggle without interruption to seize one handsome man. The blood flowing from their dragon bodies mingled with the muddy water, turning it crimson, and even the vast Lake Tamaya transformed into a lake of red. As the water level rose day by day, the Kunitsukamitachi living in the lowlands had their homes washed away. Many who were deprived of their lives had been climbing nearby mountains to avoid the disaster, but they were shaken off by the storms and earthquakes, and the number of those who sank into the water and lost their lives was unknown.

Yamagamihiko and Kawagamihime, together with Iwakane, Seoto, and Mizuoto, held their young infant and secluded themselves in the shrine at the summit, praying for the heavenly disasters and strange phenomena to subside as quickly as possible. However, there was no way to deal with the situation, and the tragic state only increased in intensity.

At such a place, parting the black clouds of the great sky and accompanied by four attendant deities, a god descended toward the summit of Minakamiyama amidst resonant music; it was the deity Asagirihime-no-kami, the Mihishiro-kami. The attendant deities were Ōmiteru-no-kami, Asasorao-no-kami, Kunio-no-kami, and Kokorohime-no-kami.

Asagirihime-no-kami, unperturbed by the heavenly disasters and strange phenomena, declared with solemnity:

Asagirihime-no-kami: 'I have descended as the Mihishiro-kami, bearing the word of the Lord God. The land of Ashihara has been defiled by beasts, inviting the wrath of heaven and earth. Because of the sin of defiling the maiden of Ryūgashima, the Kunimitama-kami has been angered. As I have descended to govern the land of Ashihara, I see a miserable state. It is a sin to act as if this land, which the Amatsukami caused to be born and which you are to consume, is your own. That Yamagamihiko and Kawagamihime encounter such lamentation this day is also the will of the gods. From this day forth, cast aside your arrogant hearts and serve the gods rightly and purely. This land is not a land for you to govern; it is a land governed by the Mihishiro-kami. Ryūgashima, floating within Lake Tamaya, is a land not yet fully prepared. The goddess who lacks a human face is, for the most part, a beast in body.

To take as a wife a princess who possesses the soul of a child of God but acts as a beast is a sin, know this. The handsome man has perished due to the retribution for rebelling against the laws of God. From this day forth, let every god purify and reform their hearts, deep within their souls.

One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten. A hundred, a thousand, eighty million. Let the wind calm quickly, let the rain fall no more. Clouds, disperse; earthquakes, cease. This land of the gods is the pure place where the Mihishiro-kami, whom the Lord God has sent, shall dwell eternally. Let the rain clear, let the land clear, let the clouds clear. From this day forth, the roots of the reed island shall be opened, re-declared as a land to consume where golden flowers bloom. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase. May there be power in my Kototama. May there be light in the living Kototama.'

As she declared this, the thunder that had been so fierce subsided, the lightning hid its shadow, the storm vanished without a trace like clouds above a tail, and the earthquake stopped abruptly, returning to the tranquility of the past; it was truly awe-inspiring.

Yamagamihiko, watching the muddy current gradually decrease, sang in fear and reverence:

Yamagamihiko: 'How awe-inspiring is the light of the Mihishiro-kami. Since a hundred sorrows have vanished, I, who have come inheriting the footprints of the Ōmiya-no-kami, realize now, though I was an enemy of the imperial land, that this is a land which the god who ought to govern it is governing. Please forgive, O Mihishiro-kami, the sins and transgressions that have accumulated since my ancestors. Has my son vanishing like foam on the water come about as the cycle of my ancestors' sins? How awe-inspiring; the noble Kototama has brought happiness, and the sorrows of the land have vanished. From this day forth, I shall purify my heart and submit to the teachings of the Mihishiro-kami.'

Kawagamihime prostrated herself before the presence with fear and trembling, and sang of her reflections:

川神姫は恐る恐る御前にひれ伏して、述懐を歌ふ。

§ 2

'Hashikeyashi, the stern Onkami has descended from heaven, and has saved us from our suffering. I was one who committed sins unknowingly; O Kami who descends from heaven, I pray Thee, forgive me. My eyes are dazzled as I look upon Thy divine countenance. My clouded vision is overwhelmed by the brilliance of Thy divine appearance; how noble it is. Now, I shall not begrudge the life of my child; I shall simply entrust it to the Kami, in Kamunagara. Even if our lives are taken, since our sins are washed away, I have no regrets. It is shameful that I, who have dwelt upon this hill since ancient times and protected the land, have realized that even good deeds are sins if done without the permission of the Lord God.'

Mihishirogami Asagirihime-no-kami nods and says, 'Thy words are clear; it is well, it is well. As thou hast been the governor of the land, I shall here reform and forgive thy sins. Dwell forever upon the mountain of Minakami.'

Yamagamihiko, wiping his tears with his sleeves, says, 'I feel as though I am reborn, while feeling gratitude for the merciful words of Mihishirogami. The Kami of Ametsuchi were angered, and the Kunihara became a street of Shiyura. I am one who trembles before the Kami who descended while illuminating the world of Tokoyami.'

Iwagene sings with trepidation, 'Ashamed am I, who have lived in peace until this day while serving the Futahashira Kami. As I prostrate myself before Mihishirogami, my body has become frozen with awe. I am sorrowful, for I have piled up sins by serving without the permission of the Lord God. Alas, the homes where the Kunitsukami dwelt as far as the eye could see have vanished without a trace. I am a sinner without propriety, who has cultivated the seeds of such lamentation. Even if my life should end, I would not loathe it; I pray Thee, forgive the suffering Kami. I have grown old serving in this mansion since ancient times, yet I have left no trace of distinguished merit.'

Minaoto sings, 'I trembled before the Kami who descended after parting the Hisakata Kumoi. How noble is the Kami who descended to illuminate the ugly Kunihara of Tokoyami. The sound of the water in the Takitsuse has suddenly calmed, and the surging water has subsided. The drifting water flows on one after another, and the mist rises over this Kunihara. It is our sincere heart that thinks of how we might repay Thy noble grace.'

Seoto sings with reverence, 'My soul, which wept in the lamentation of Tokoyami, has been revived by the light of the Kami. I shall never forget, even to the end of a thousand generations, that the grace of the Kami is ever-effective. Alas, alas, the sacred place of Minakami-yama is reviving, and the morning sun shines upon it. It has become a world where both grass and trees rejoice, for the Kami of Light has descended.'

Omiteru-no-kami recites a divine poem, 'Since I am the Kami of the Great Divine Illumination, the sun of heaven, I have descended accompanying Mihishirogami. From this day forth, I shall blow away the clouds and mist of the sky, and I intend to bring life to the land of Ashihara. If I am present, the world where light and darkness alternate will be bright in the Kunihara even as the evening approaches. Be mindful of the maidens of the island of the Dragon Kami, for they are not perfect Kami. When Mihishirogami has crossed over, the root of the Dragon Island shall become a living land. The Magatsumi gathering on the ridge of Mount Ibuki are blooming and fragrant as if they were hundreds or thousands of flowers.'

Iwagene, pressing his head to the ground, says, 'How grateful I am for the divine revelation of the Heavenly Onkami. I shall engrave it upon my heart and never forget it. While I have spent days of lamentation, today I have encountered a good day of joy.'

Asasoraotoko-no-kami recites a divine poem, 'I have descended, feeling pity for the state of Minakami-yama, which was in such distress. I, Asasoraotoko-no-kami, have descended following in the footsteps of Mihishirogami. The morning sun shall rise in the sky, and the evening moon shall hang over the ridge to illuminate the land. I shall govern the land that the Lord God graciously partakes of as a beautiful land, and I shall solidify the foundation of Toha. Yamagamihiko, Iwagene, Seoto, and Minaoto, join your strength together and raise the divine child. Make the grown divine child the governor of this land, and govern the nearby regions in peace.'

Yamagamihiko, overcome with joy, prostrates himself on the ground while shedding tears and sings,

'Deeply sinful are we, yet thou dost bless our grandchildren, and the thought fills me with sorrow. With all the Magokoro I possess, I shall serve Mihishirogami for Toha.'

Kahakamihime likewise prostrates herself and sings:

'The world of Tokoyami has cleared away without a shadow, thanks to the light of Mihishirogami. Together with my husband, I shall humbly govern the nearby regions of this Kuni.'

Kuniumio no Kami sings a Miuta:

'The Kuni of Yoshihara is vast and boundless, so I shall exhaust all my strength. Descending to this endless Kunihara, I feel emboldened at the thought of building a capital.'

Mihishiro no Kami sings again:

'Morning mists rise in Yomo, and evening Yufukasumi begins to Tanabi over this Kunihara. Minakamiyama, this Sugado, shall let the aged Futari rest and entrust them to Iwagane. When this Miko grows up, Iwagane, return the Matsuri of the Kuni to the Miko. Since this Miko was born from the womb of a Tatsugami, the Kunitsukami shall not be able to raise him. I shall have Kogokorohime no Kami protect the Mizuko, and I intend to raise him peacefully and Ō.'

Iwagane prostrates himself on the ground and sings:

'I am grateful to receive the command of my aged Lord, and I shall serve at the mountain of Minakami. When the Uzumiko grows up, I shall directly return this Mikuni to him. I entrust the upbringing of the precious child to Mihishiro no Kami and Kogokorohime no Kami.'

Kogokorohime no Kami sings a Miuta:

'Humbly receiving the command of Mihishiro no Kami, I shall bless and raise him morning and evening.'

Having sung thus, she takes the Miko Tatsuhiko into her arms and sings:

'Precious child, beloved Miko, thou art the pillar of the Kuni; grow up tall and strong. The blessings of Kami are ever-widening. I shall pray morning and evening for the longevity of thy life and nurture thee. Yamagamihiko, Kahakamihime, be at peace. Iwagane, too, look forward with joy to the growth of this child. Kannagara. I shall now depart, pointing toward the mountain of Takamitsu, following Mihishiro no Kami. Ah, Kannagara, Kami, Kami. May the divine favor be for Toha. May the divine favor be for Toha.'

As she sings this, she calmly raises clouds, and Mihishiro no Kami and the other four pillars depart, pointing toward the direction of Mount Takamitsu.

Incidentally, with Mount Takamitsu as the boundary, the noble residence of Mihishirogami was built to the east, the palace of Toa was constructed, and it was renamed the Kuni of Toa; the region west of Mount Takamitsu was named the Kuni of Yosan, and the Kuni of Yoshihara was collectively referred to as the noble Futana-shima; how awe-inspiring.

と歌はせ給ひつつ、悠然として雲を起し、御樋代の神の他四柱は、高光山の方面指して出で給ひける。

因に言ふ、高光山を境として、東に御樋代神の貴の御舎は建てられ、土阿の宮殿を造り、改めて土阿の国と名付け給ひ、高光山以西を予讃の国と名付け給ひ、葭原の国土を総称して貴の二名島と称へ給ひけるぞ畏けれ。
