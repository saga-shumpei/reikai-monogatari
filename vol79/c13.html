Chapter 13: 鰐の背 [1994]

鰐の背〔1994〕

§ 1

Adeyaka had conspired with Kakitsubata, and having determined to leave Riugūjima under the cover of yain, he had steeled his resolve to return to the land where his chichihaha resided.

Fearing that he might be stopped by the command of Ōtatsumihiko, and without even informing the Otohimegami of Uraraka, he feigned a sanpo in the kauguwai; in the middle of the mayonakagoro, with the moon shining brightly, he hurried through the dairōmon toward the daiichimon.

The momogusachigusa by the roadside were adorned with the getsukuō shining upon the yotsuyu beads, creating a fuzei that defied description. Gazing upon this shizen, Adeyaka sang:

Adeyaka: 'Even upon the flowers of the momokusa blooming by the wayside, the mitama of the moon has deigned to dwell. The sound of the insects singing of love upon the grasses is also clear and sharp in the moon tonight. While listening to the namioto lapping at the nagisabe, we walk the yomichi of the moon together. This tabidachi to the Mototsumikuni, stolen away in secret, is startled even by the voices of the insects. As the moon illuminates the sky where the futari walk, it seems as though Tsukuyomi is deigning to protect me. Deign to return in peace to the Mototsumikuni, O God of Tsukuyomi, shining in the firmament.'

Kakitsubata sang:

Kakitsubata: 'I, Kakitsubata, who grew up on the island of the Dragon God, shall flourish on the mountain above the waters. When I look at the moon, I wonder if it is the dragon's jewel. I walk the yomichi with you, my lord. The moon shines upon the white dew resting on the leaves of grass, and it seems our sleeves have become wet. I feel both fearful and joyful, for I am a person being led away by you. When I think that today is the parting of lingering memories on this island, why is it so sad? Because you are more precious than a jewel that glows in the night, I follow you with joy.'

Thus they arrived at the first gate, opened the iron gate with ease from within, and emerged onto the nagisabe; gazing at the crests of the waves leaping in frenzy, Adeyaka sang:

Adeyaka: 'What shall I do, a body without wings? There is no way to leave the island roots of the dragon amidst the white waves. I gaze upon the moon shining on the white sails of the waves, and thinking of returning, I revere it as a jewel. Yet, I have neither wings nor fins; how sad it is to be able to become neither bird nor fish. The moon in the great sky must surely have pity upon the figures of the futari standing on the shore. Unexpectedly bathed in the dew of your affection, I am once again wet with the dew of the shore. O Gods of Heaven and Earth, have pity upon us and let us cross to the mountain above the waters. Even if we cross, we have no boat; what shall we do with this island root floating upon the waves?'

Just as he sang thus, an eight-fathom crocodile appeared, parting the waves. It revealed its back on the surface of the water, as if to say, 'Please ride upon this.' When the futari jumped onto the back of the crocodile as if it were a gift from heaven, the crocodile, as if to say it understood everything, swam through the lake of rough waves and raced toward the south, ever toward the south. The futari riding on the back of the crocodile sang in turn.

Adeyaka: 'Saved by the back of the crocodile, I feel joy thinking of returning to the foot of the mountain above the waters. The great sky and the depths of the lake are both illuminated by the moon, and the body traveling through that central plain is pure. The stars twinkling in the depths of the lake, reflecting the firmament, are surely like grains of sand. Caressed on the face by the softly blowing wind, I return with you, my beloved. I must return quickly with the help of this crocodile, for this lake plain is endless and the crocodile is swift. This crocodile is a gift bestowed by the Gods; how could I think of it lightly? O crocodile, O crocodile, help me and let me return quickly to the Mototsumikuni, while parting the waves.'

Kakitsubata sang:

Kakitsubata: 'Saved by the crocodile, the messenger of the Gods, I am going to your country, my lord. If I also possessed my original body, I would send you off just like the crocodile. If it has come to this, I have lost the strength to cross the lake plain in my human body. Is this the grace of the Kannagara Gods? The moon in the great sky is shining with extra clarity. Following the rising of the white waves, Tsukuyomi deigns to cast his shadow and shine. How clear the moon is tonight, that even the fish tribes at the bottom of the water can be seen. I need not fear the pursuit of the Dragon God, for I rely on the crocodile with its swift legs. The sound of the plovers flying across the lake plain is also sharp, and the moonlit lake plain is pure of heart. The joy of today, running across the waves, is an example that reaches to Heaven and Earth.'

Just as they sang thus, the war cries of ten million people were heard from behind, sounding all at once. When Adeyaka and Kakitsubata looked back and raised their hands to see through the moonlight, what could it be? Their escape by the power of the moonlit night had been discovered, and Ōtatsumihiko no Mikoto had issued orders to the many Dragon Gods, strictly commanding them to pursue the pair. The Dragon Gods, each vying to be the first, pursued them with the force of a thousand leagues in a single leap, floating and sinking between the waves, chasing them relentlessly.

原文（近代文語）

艶男は燕子花と諜し合せ、夜陰にまぎれて竜宮島を立出で、父母のいます国に帰らむと、決心を固めてゐた。

大竜身彦の命に止められむ事をおそれ、麗子の弟姫神にも告げず、郊外の散歩にことよせ、月照り渡る真夜中頃、大楼門をくぐり第一門の方へと急ぎゆく。道の側の百草千草は夜露の玉に月光輝き、得も言はれぬ風情である。艶男はその自然をながめて、

『道の辺に咲く百草の花にさへ 月の御霊は宿らせ給へり 草の上に恋を歌へる虫の音も 今宵は清く月に冴えたり 渚辺に寄する波音聞きながら 月の夜路を二人ゆくかも しのびゆく元津御国の旅立は 虫の声にも驚かされける 二人ゆく空を照して月読は 吾を守らせ給ふがに見ゆ 心安く元津御国に還らせ給へ 御空に輝く月読の神』

燕子花は歌ふ。

『竜神の島に生ひ立ちし燕子花 吾は水上の山に栄えむ 月見れば竜の玉かと疑はる 吾は夜路を君と行くなり 草の葉に置く白露に月照りて 二人の袖はぬれにけらしな 恐ろしく又楽しくも思ふかな 君に引かれて離りゆく身は この島に今日を名残の別れぞと おもへば何か悲しかりけり 夜光る玉にもまさる君故に 吾は嬉しく従ひゆくも』

斯くして第一の門に着き、鉄門を中より易々と開き、渚辺に出で、躍り狂ふ波の秀をながめて艶男は、

『翼なき身は如何にせむ白波の 竜の島根を去る由もなし 打寄する波の白帆に照る月を 玉と仰ぎて帰らまく思ふ さりながら翼もなければ鰭もなし 鳥にも魚にもなれぬかなしさ 大空の月も憐れみ給ふらむ 汀に立てる二人の姿を ゆくりなく君が情の露あびて 又も汀の露にぬれぬる 天地の神よ吾等を憐れみて 水上の山へ渡らせ給へ 渡るにも御舟なければ如何にせむ 波の上に浮くこの島ケ根を』

斯く歌ふ折しも、波を分けてぬつと現はれ来る八尋鰐あり。水面に背を現し、之に乗らせ給へと言ひたげなり。二人は天の与へと鰐の背に飛び乗れば、鰐は何事も万事承知の上とばかり、荒波の湖を游ぎながら、南へ南へと走りゆく。鰐の背に乗れる二人は交々歌ふ。

艶男『鰐の背に救はれ水上の山もとに 帰る思へば楽しかりけり 大空も湖の底ひも月照りて その中原を行く身は清し 湖底に御空うつしてまたたける 星は真砂にさも似たるかな そよと吹く風に面をなでられて こひしき君と吾帰り行くも 果てしなきこの湖原も足早き 鰐の助けにとく帰るべし この鰐は神のたまひし賜ぞ 吾おろそかに如何で思はむ 鰐よ鰐吾を助けて元津国に とく帰らせよ波を分けつつ』

燕子花は歌ふ。

『有難き神の使ひの鰐の子に 救はれ君が御国に行くかも 吾も亦元の身体を持ち居らば 鰐の如くに送らむものを 斯くならば吾は人の身湖原を 渡らむ力失せにけらしな 惟神神の恵みか大空の 月は一入冴え渡りける 白波の立ちのまにまに月読は かげをおとして輝き給ふ 水底の魚族までも見えわたる 今宵の月のさやかなるかも 竜神の追及き来るも恐るまじ みあしの早き鰐に頼れば 湖原を飛び交ふ千鳥の音も冴えて 心清しき月の湖原 天地にかかる例はあらなみの 上分け走る今日の嬉しさ』

斯く歌ふ折しも、後方に当りて千万人の鬨の声、どつとばかりに聞え来るにぞ、艶男、燕子花の二人は後振り返り手を差上げ月光に透し見れば、こはそもいかに、両人が月夜を力に逃げ去りし事発覚し、大竜身彦の命は数多の竜神等に下知を降し、二人の後を追つかけよと厳命なしければ、竜神は吾も吾もと先を争ひ、波間を浮きつ沈みつ、一瀉千里の勢にて追ひしき追ひまくるにぞありけり。

§ 2

Kakitsubata cried out that this was an Ichidaiji, and taking advantage of having assumed a Jinshin form, she fervently offered up the Kazūta of Ama; this voice resounded through Ametsuchi, and the lake seemed as if it would split asunder.

‘One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, hundred, thousand, May the Chiyorozu of Kami protect us, May the Kitsusaki of the Tatsugami be stayed, O Wani, O Wani, run with all your might, run, Minakamiyama floats dimly in the depths of the mist, Run, run, with all your Inochi.’

As they sang thus, the war cries ceased abruptly, and the pace of the Wani became Hitoshiho faster.

‘Arigata, for the Kototama of mine, the Miisao of the Wadatsumi, Hath manifested itself, Escaping the Yasa of the Moromoro Tatsugamitachi that come to attack, We reach the peaceful Unabara, Keeping the Miisao of Ōtatsumihiko, We dwell in the peaceful Unabara of the night.’

Adeyaka sang.

‘The Ichirin of the moon shines in the Misora, I ride upon a single Wani, Since the legs of the Yahirowani are swift, there is no longer any fear of the Tatsugami catching up, My aged mother, she must be lamenting day and night, searching for my whereabouts, Thinking that I would see her face as soon as possible, My heart grows impatient, My Inochi, saved by the Kami of water, fire, and earth, This is the Unabara where I have been revived, Minakamiyama has begun to appear faintly, Dimly in the moonlit sky, To travel this Unabara where the plovers cry with a maiden, Is as if I were tracing a path in a dream, While seeing a dream in a world of dreams, to cross the Unabara riding on the back of a Wani with a maiden, Though the island of the Ryūgū is good, Minakamiyama, My hometown, is what I yearn for, Parting from Reikohime of Itokoya, Now I return accompanied by Kakitsubata, O Kakitsubata, since you are a Omokachikami, Pray, bring the Kunitsugamitachi to submission.’

Kakitsubata sang.

‘Accepting the words of my noble lord, I shall bring the Kunitsugamitachi to submission, Though I am a maiden of a remote island, if I am with you, I shall fear no one, The lake breeze has grown strong, and the crests of the waves, Have begun to rage ever higher, Even if the wind is strong and the waves rise, what of it? For I am a body embraced by the grace of Kami, Embraced by the Kami of Ametsuchi, and embraced by the husband I love, I am in the peaceful Unabara, I shall fear neither the wind nor the waves of the Unabara that I cross easily on the back of the Wani, Alas, my sleeves have become damp, They have become wet with the spray of the white waves, Even as I weep while we embrace in the evening, My sleeves grow ever wetter.’

Just as they sang thus, a boat appeared suddenly from the depths of the waves and came rowing toward them. When they looked closer, who could have expected it, it was the Kami of water, fire, and earth.

The Kami of water, fire, and earth, smiling broadly, said:

‘I have brought my boat to welcome you, my lord, who are returning after a long time, From here, the currents of the lake are strong; return the Wani boat, And transfer to this one, Your father and mother are lamenting your whereabouts and have grown frail, Though Minakamiyama is visible only faintly, The wave path is long, and I shall escort you.’

Adeyaka replied to this:

‘Arigata, the heart of the Kami of water, fire, and earth, Even if my Inochi should perish, I shall never forget it, This is Kakitsubata, the flower bud I sought after crossing to the island of the Ryūgū, I wish to plant this flower, which I have transplanted from the roots of the Ryūgū island, On Minakamiyama, O Kami of water, fire, and earth, together with my wife, Pray, escort us to Minakamiyama.’

Saying this, he moved from the back of the Wani to the boat of the Kami of water, fire, and earth, and while pressing his palms together toward the Wani, said:

‘I shall offer my gratitude to you, who have safely escorted us across the great Unabara where the waves rage, Perhaps you are a messenger of the Amatsukami; I shall never forget the Miisao of this day, O Yahirowani, Now then, I shall bid you farewell, O Yahirowani, You are the Inochi of my own body, When I return to Minakamiyama, my hometown, I shall enshrine you as a guardian Kami.’

As he sang thus, the Yahirowani quietly submerged its entire body into the water and became a white wake behind them. From this point on, the Kami of water, fire, and earth rowed toward the south, parting the crests of the waves, across the moonlit Unabara.

原文（近代文語）

こは一大事と燕子花は声を張り上げ、人身となりしを幸ひ、天の数歌を頻に奏上する、この声天地に響き湖も割るるばかりなり。

『一二三四五六七八九十百千万 千万の神守り給へ 竜神の切先を止めさせ給へ 鰐よ鰐よ力限りに走れよ走れよ 水上山は霞の奥にぼんやりと浮く 走れよ走れよ生命限りに』

斯く歌ふや、鬨の声はぴたりと止みて、鰐の足は一入速くなりける。

『有難し吾言霊に海津見の 神の功は現れにけり 諸々の竜神等の襲ひ来る 矢先き逃れて安けき湖原 大竜身彦の命の御功を とどめて安き夜の湖原』

艶男は歌ふ。

『一輪の月は御空に輝けり 吾は一つの鰐に乗り行く 八尋鰐足はやければ竜神は 最早や追付くおそれだもなし 老いませる吾垂乳根は吾行方 探ねて日夜を歎かせ給はむ 一時も早く御顔拝まむと 思へば心いらだちにけり 水火土の神に救はれ吾生命 蘇りたる湖原はこれ ほのぼのと水上山は見え初めぬ 月夜の空にぼんやりとして 千鳥啼くこの湖原を細女と 行くは夢路を辿るが如し 夢の世に夢を見ながら細女と 鰐の背に乗り湖原渡るも 竜宮の島もよけれど水上山 吾故郷は恋しかりけり いとこやの麗子姫に立ち別れ 今燕子花を伴ひ帰るも

燕子花よ汝は面勝神なれば 言向けやはせ国津神等を』

燕子花は歌ふ。

『尊かる君の言葉をうべなひて 言向けやはさむ国津神等を 離れ島の乙女なれども君と在れば 吾は恐れじ如何なる人にも 湖風は強くなりけり波の秀は いや高々と狂ひ出しぬ 風強み波おこるとも何かあらむ 神の恵に抱かるる身は 天地の神に抱かれ吾恋ふる 夫に抱かれ安き湖原 鰐の背に易々渡る湖原の 風も恐れじ波も恐れじ 白波のしぶきにあはれ吾袖は うるほひにけり湿らひにけり 抱き合ひて泣ける夕の涙にも いやまさりつつ濡るる吾袖』

斯く歌ふ折しも、波の奥より忽然として現れし一艘の舟、此方に向つて漕ぎ来る。近より見れば豈計らむや、水火土の神にましける。

水火土の神はにこにこしながら、

『久々に帰らす君を迎へむと 吾は御舟を持ちて来れり これよりは湖の瀬強し鰐の舟 返してこれに乗りうつらせよ 垂乳根の父と母とは汝が行方 歎かせ給ひ衰へ給へり 水上の山はほんのり見ゆれども 波路は遠し吾は送らむ』

艶男は之に答へて、

『ありがたし水火土の神の御心 生命死すとも忘れざらまし 竜宮の島に渡りて求めたる 花の蕾の燕子花はこれ 竜宮の島根ゆ移すこの花を 水上の山に植ゑたく思ふ 水火土の神よ吾妻諸共に 送らせ給へ水上の山へ』

と言ひながら、鰐の背より水火土の神の御舟に移り、鰐に向つて合掌しながら、

『浪猛る大湖原を安々と 送りし君に感謝を捧げむ 天津神の御使ならめや八尋鰐の 今日の功は永久に忘れじ いざさらば別れを告げむ八尋鰐よ 汝は吾身の生命なるぞや 水上山吾故郷に帰りなば 汝を守護の神と斎かむ』

斯く歌ふや、八尋鰐は静々その全身を水に没し、後白波となりにける。これより水火土の神は波の秀を分けながら、月照る湖原を南へ南へと漕ぎ行く。
