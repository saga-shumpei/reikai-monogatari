Chapter 22: 天変地 Phantom [2003]

天変地妖〔2003〕

§ 1

Adeyaka, having been persuaded to abandon his thoughts of death by the exhaustive remonstrances of Iwakene and the Shitenōra, nonetheless found himself unable to overcome his longing for the Ooigafuchi abyss, and thus, without distinction of morning or evening, he made it his sole consolation to float his boat upon the waters and wander there.

Iwakene, fretting with anxiety as to whether Adeyaka might truly be beyond danger, caused Masago and Shirasago to follow him as both guards and attendants. Shortly before the evening when a light rain was falling, Adeyaka, as was his custom, took Masago and Shirasago with him and attempted a brief excursion by boat upon the Ooigafuchi. Just as the curtain of twilight was beginning to draw near, strange to say, a faint voice echoed from nowhere; and when he listened with careful attention, he realized it was the sorrowful voice of Shirahagi, who had once harbored deep feelings for Adeyaka at the Shimane of the Dragon.

The voice of Shirahagi.

The white bush clover, waiting long for the autumn wind, / Has come to seek thee in thy distant thoughts. / The one I thought would share my very life is but a shadow, / And I scatter like the petals of the white bush clover. / In the sorrow of the dream where I hoped we might be joined, / My own body withers like the white bush clover. / How resentful I am of thee, for thou hast plucked / Only the irises, leaving not even a drop of dew for me. / In what age shall this longing be cleared away? / It is a bitter flower of the bush clover that loves in vain. / Thinking that my life is of no worth in this Utsushiyo, / I suffer, abandoned by thee. / Amidst the multitude of a hundred flowers, that thou, my lord, / Shouldst love only the iris is a cause for my resentment. / On the branches of the bush clover, heavy with dew each morning, / Thou hast never cast thy heart to look. / The white bush clover of my home, the Dragon's Capital, / Was moved to the vicinity of Fujigaoka to bloom. / Abandoned by the dew of affection, the white bush clover / Maintains its thin life upon the water's edge.

Thy appearance, fragrant as the mandarin orange, / Was the very seed that made me think of thee. / Parting the rough waves, I crossed over to this abyss, / Drawing near to thee, burning with love. / Longing to hear thy voice morning and evening, / I, the white bush clover, hide within the abyss. / Fearing the blade of thy sword, thou hast left Shimane, / And I wonder if thou hast forgotten me. / Though I relied upon thee as my very life, / I resent thy heartless, cold indifference. / Though I am of the body of a Dragon God, / Can there be any change in a heart that loves a human? / Resentment and sorrow are congealed here, / And I weep in the depths of the abyss of love.

Adeyaka, hearing this voice faintly and feeling his eyes moisten at the pathos of Shirahagi's heart, replied:

Though I am not a being of wood or stone, / Being but one person, how shall I make recompense? / I, too, am sinking in an abyss of lamentation, / And wander here to clear my heavy breast. / Hearing the voice of thy lamentation in the twilight, / I have become sad and lonely. / For the sake of love, I would not spare my life, / Yet I linger only for the sake of my child.

Just as he said this, another sorrowful voice was heard from the upper reaches.

I am the white chrysanthemum that longed for thee, / A bud moistened by the dew. / I thought we were a single flower of the mandarin orange, / Yet thou hast plucked a hundred and a thousand flowers. / Unable to bear the life of resentment and sorrow, / I have crossed the lake to come here. / Because my longing is so deep, I have hidden / In the bottom of this abyss and become a dragon. / That thou shouldst wither the white chrysanthemum, / Which would not wither even at the end of ten thousand generations, is a cause for my resentment. / If thou wert human, I would ask thee / Of the things of the divine age, O flower of the white chrysanthemum. / Think kindly of the white chrysanthemum, / Which blooms while the sky of the long month is clear in thy reign. / Why is it that the water beneath the chrysanthemums on the mountain peak / Flows down to sink into the abyss? / At what time wilt thou pluck the white chrysanthemum / That I wait for with prayer in the long month? / The face of the moon emerging from the mountain's edge / Is thy face, O flower of the white chrysanthemum.

Because my longing is so deep, I have crossed the lake / Of eight thousand fathoms to come seeking thee. / Having fallen in love with thy peerless appearance, / I, the white chrysanthemum, am on the verge of losing my way. / I would not spare my life, the thread of jewels, / If I could but dwell near thee, even at the bottom of the abyss.

Adeyaka, hearing this voice, felt his heart burning as if in madness, and replied:

Is today another day to hear such sorrowful voices? / Now, all around me is but white waves. / I have remained unchanged in my heart, / Thinking to pluck the white chrysanthemum when autumn comes. / The fragrance of the white chrysanthemum is wonderful, / And it lingers on my sleeves, never to be forgotten. / Though my life is not one to be spared, for a little while longer, / I pray thee, wait for my child to grow.

艶男は、岩ケ根他四天王等の言葉を尽しての諫めに、死を思ひとどまりたれども、何故か大井ケ淵の恋しくて堪らず、朝な夕なの区別なく、淵に舟を浮べて遊ぶを唯一の慰みとなしゐたりける。

白萩の声。

艶男は微にこの声を聞きて、白萩の心の憐れさに両眼をうるほしながら、

『われとても木石ならぬ身なれども 一つの身なり如何に報いむ われも亦歎きの淵に沈みつつ 胸晴らさむと此処に遊べる 黄昏を君の歎きの声聞きて 悲しく淋しくなりにけらしな 恋故に生命惜しまじわれはただ 垂乳根の為めながらふのみなり』

かくいふ折しも、又もや上手の方より悲しき声聞え来る。

『われこそは汝を慕ひし白菊の 露に霑ふ蕾の花よ 一本の花橘と思ひしを 君は百花千花を手折れり 恨めしく悲しく生命堪へがてに われは湖路を渡り来しはや わが思ひいや深ければ此の淵の 底に潜みて竜となりぬる 万代の末にも枯れぬ白菊を 枯らし給へる君ぞ恨めし 千早振る神代の事も人ならば 問はましものを白菊の花 君ケ代をいと長月の空清く 咲かむ白菊あはれと思へ 水上山菊の下水如何なれば 流れて淵に沈むなるらむ 祈りつつ待つ長月の菊の花を 何れの時か君の手折るや 山の端を出でゐる月の顔は 君の面と白菊の花

艶男はこの声を聞き、狂気の如く胸を燃やしながら、

『今日は又悲しき声を聞く日かな 今は前後も白波の上 秋されば白菊の花手折らむと われは心を替へずありける 白菊の匂ひめでたくわが袖に 香りて時じく忘らへなくに 惜しまるる生命ならねど今しばし わが子の生ひたち待たせ給はれ』

§ 2

As the voice of Shiragiku:

If thy Onkotoba be not false, I too shall clear away my sorrows and await the time.

At this very moment, from a point slightly downstream, the thin voice of Ominaheshi came echoing. Listening with ears strained to the utmost, it said:

I am the Ominaheshi, a frail flower raised on the Shimane of the dragon.

Longing for you, my body has withered, and how many times have I thought to end my Inochi, this thread of life?

Crossing the Yashihoji, I have come here to the Ōigaha, and that I sink into the Fuchise is because I think of you.

The rain falling in a thin drizzle is but the tears of sorrowful me, thinking of you.

Wondering how I might clear away this sad longing, I parted the Namiji and came here to Koko.

Do you not deign to look upon the gentle appearance of the Ominaheshi blooming on the Nazohe of Minakamiyama?

Longing for you, how many Ikuhi have I spent here?

Thinking that I might be drenched in the rain of dew-like affection, I find it sad, for I think of the dragon, a Shikogami whose form I cannot bear to look upon even now.

Remaining sunk in this Fuchise for Ikuchiyo, I think to protect your Misachi.

Shirahagi, the lord Shiragiku, and I as well, await you at Fujigaokabe.

Until your Inochi, the thread of life, comes to an end, I think to wait in sorrow and suffering.

Pondering the sad heart of the Tatsugami, deign to remember me even in the pillow of your dreams.

As it echoed thus, the water of the Fuchise suddenly drew great Hamon, and something rose up together with the Mizukemuri. Looking closely, it was a Ninmenriushin Kakitsubata, and Adeyaka, Masago, and Shirasago were all startled at once, and stood in a daze, gazing at the Mizukemuri that stood and swirled here and there. The Hamon became increasingly violent, and at last, it became such a rough sea that the boat was about to capsize, so Adeyaka made up his mind and stood up:

Is this the raging of the Kakitsubata-hime whom I love? I have not even the strength to stop it.

If it has come to this, what shall I refuse? I too shall become the scum of the water and vanish.

Masago and Shirasago were startled, and grasping the left and right hands of Adeyaka, with tears in their eyes, they said:

Young Lord, do not do such a rash thing.

Do you not have your parents and your child?

To end your Inochi, the thread of life, is very easy.

What is heavy is your duty to the land.

Did we ever think that such sorrow would press upon us during our boat excursion on the Ōigaha?

The voices heard in the Kamitsuse, the Nakatsuse, and the Shimotsuse must be those of demons.

Young Lord, do not let yourself be caught by the sweet words of demons and throw away your Inochi.

We, Masago and Shirasago, shall take charge of your Inochi with all our might.

Shirasago said in a panic:

The wind has grown rough and the waves have risen; come, let us bring the boat to the shore; Masago, row the boat.

Masago replied:

The stern and the oars have been swept away by the waves; how shall we reach the shore in this rough river?

As they said this, the whole sky suddenly clouded over, a gale blew fiercely, the earth shook, and with the fury of the rough waves, the figures of the three were hidden deep underwater along with the boat.

From this time on, the tremors of day and night did not cease, thunder roared, lightning flashed, a gale blew, the rain fell as if a tub had been overturned, the rumbling of the earth and the tremors were without interruption, and the sacred ground of Minakamiyama, which had been so peaceful, turned into a scene of agony and wailing. Iwagane, carrying Tatsuhiko on his back as a matter of great importance, climbed to the high hall and escaped the disaster.

The state of the Kunitsukami running about in confusion and crying out was a tragedy too painful to behold.

To such a place, a goddess of beautiful appearance descended from heaven, accompanied by four attendant deities, and deigned to descend to this place.

これより日夜の震動止まず、雷轟き、稲妻閃き、暴風吹き荒み、雨は盆を覆せし如く、地鳴震動間断なく、さしもに平穏なりし水上山の聖場は、阿鼻叫喚の巷と化し去り、岩ケ根は竜彦を一大事と背に負ひ、高殿に上りて、難を免れゐたりける。

国津神たちの右往左往に泣き叫ぶ状、目も当てられぬ惨状なりける。

かかるところへ、天より降り来ませる容姿端麗なる女神、四柱の侍神を伴ひ、此場に降らせ給ふ。
