Chapter 7: The Episode of 相聞 (Part I) [1988]

相聞（一）〔1988〕

§ 1

Floating upon the vast expanse of the ocean, in the true Mahoraba of the Kuni of Yoshihara, right at the center of Lake Tamayakosui, soaring up to pierce the mid-heaven, comparable to the very nose of the Daichi, the rear of Mount Ibuki is a famous mountain connecting continuously to Takamitsuyama.

The scenic island crag, protruding at the southern tip of this mountain, rich in myriad trees and grasses, and condensed with coral reefs, is called Riugūjima.

This island crag is still new, and numerous dragons with human faces and dragon bodies reside here, giving the impression of a Shinsenkiyō. Although the heads and both arms of the slightly evolved dragons are gradually resembling the appearance of Kunitsukami, the rest are not yet completely human bodies; from the shoulders downward, they are bizarre beast-like creatures whose entire bodies are wrapped in scales.

Reikohime, who had been captured and brought to such a new island crag, was exquisite in appearance like a heavenly being. The dragon race immediately revered her as a deity, and even the great dragon-bodied Oritatsuhiko, while making Reikohime his wife, revered her as a queen of deities, serving her with the utmost sincerity day and night.

When Tsuyoo, the elder brother of Reikohime, arrived at such a place, the young women of this island crag, as if receiving a sudden shower in a time of drought, rejoiced and yearned, flocking to compete in being the first to behold the face of the heavenly being, even if only for a single glance. Among the dragons, the maidens with lovely eyebrows and features served morning and evening in the divine hall of Oritatsuhiko, pulling sleeves and catching eyes as they gazed upon the exquisite appearance of Tsuyoo, while harboring smiles.

The prominent deities among the attending goddesses bore splendid names such as Kikikyō, Yamabuki, Ominaeshi, Hagi, Nadeshiko, Fujibakama, Shiragiku, Yamagiku, Yuri no Hana, Tsubaki, Sakura, Kakitsubata, Ayame, and Shichiku. Although all these goddesses were of dragon form, their countenances were exquisite and appeared easily inviolable.

Yamabuki, apprehensively and close to Tsuyoo, approached attired in a uchikake, and sang the depths of her heart:

Tsuyoo answered to this:

§ 2

Yamabuki answered to this,

Yamabuki: 'The depth of my feelings I revealed to Kimigari, oh, how embarrassing it is! Be that as it may, let me break off the flower blooming in the garden of Riugū.'

Adeyaka replied,

Adeyaka: 'Be that as it may, wait for my reply just for a little while.'

So saying, with a calm air, treading upon the white sand of the garden, he proceeds toward the shade of the trees by Magatamaike.

Here, the attendant goddess Shiragiku stands looking troubled. Her graceful figure has an air like a begonia drooping under the rain.

Adeyaka sees this and chants,

Adeyaka: 'The flowers blooming at the edge of the pond of Magatama, which flower is it that I long to ask about? The divine figure clearly reflected at the bottom of the pond is indeed a rare adornment in this world.'

Shiragiku chants,

Shiragiku: 'Where is my heart going, as this morning I am looking into the water mirror like a white chrysanthemum! You are a person of the Amatsukunibito, looking up ever since the day you descended to this island. When I look up, the face of the moon, the color of the flowers—you are a rare and charming man in this Shimane! Thinking of the charming man every morning and evening, I pray in the pond of Magatama. Though the water of Magatama is clear to the very bottom, my heart is clouded at the bottom of the waters. Revealing the bottom of my deeply profound thoughts, there is one thing I want to show you. Will you not draw, will you not cup in your hands the jade water where the shadow of Shiragiku is reflected? Though I have a lowly body, how should there be a barrier in a pure heart that loves a person? The dew of the white chrysanthemum flowers blooming in Shimane—let me cup it in my hands, granting the pillow of a single night.'

Adeyaka chants,

Adeyaka: 'Having unexpectedly crossed over to the island of Shimane, I meet with the rain of the dew of affection. Verily, I am Hikoji, inheriting the house of the God, the Kunitsumioya of Minakamiyama. Since this is not an island where one should dwell forever, I know not even the method to break off a branch of the white chrysanthemum flower.'

Shiragiku chants,

Shiragiku: 'How embarrassing my body is, rejected by your sleeves, the flowers are about to scatter! Where did my heart go, oh, what an embarrassing morning for a white chrysanthemum flower! The kototama I rashly released hit the rock and was bounced back. The sadness of a maiden's heart morning and evening—how heartless you are, refusing to draw and let me drink! Come now, let us part for a while, and in the shade of the embarrassing woods I will rest.'

Chanting thus, covering her face with her sleeves, the noble maiden droopingly and softly slipped through the dim shadows beneath the trees, and went away to parts unknown.

Adeyaka, letting out a deep sigh,

Adeyaka: 'Ah, I have strayed! Yearning for the footsteps of Reiko, am I suffering here? Now I keenly understand the heart of Princess Reiko, and it moves me to tears. Even though she is the queen of the dragon god, to be embraced in the body of a dragon must be a lonely thing. I who came seeking the footsteps of Reiko have been afflicted by the rain of affection. Is love such a painful thing as this? I think of the life of my soul dying. Though I think of dying, because my doting parents are in my hometown, my heart is not my own. Saved by the gods of water, fire, and earth, now I am tormented by the same anguish. If not for the eye of the dragon god, I would quietly adorn my boat and return home. The sight of a dragon god's maiden, unsuited to my heartfelt feelings, is also unsightly. Yet, when I gaze upon her expression, she is merely a maiden to be pitied.

Bathed in the dew of a maiden's pure affection, my heart has surely grown sorrowful. In this island where countless goddesses dwell, how can a single boy endure it? If I break off a single branch, it will become a situation where I must break off all the hundred and thousand flowers indifferently. Maintaining this single life of mine now, how am I to sustain a hundred lives?'

Just as he is chanting thus, from the forest ahead, seven maidens, wearing white inner robes and scarlet divided skirts, each holding a crystal vase, come in gently and quietly as if to draw water from Magatamaike.

Adeyaka, thinking it would be terrible to be seen again, suddenly turns back, takes aim at the tree shade by the mirror lake on the mid-slope of Mount Ibuki, and flees hurriedly.

Adeyaka chants while resting in the shade of a tree,

Adeyaka: 'At last I have fled and come here. Let me rest in the shade of the Katsuragi trees. In this island where countless goddesses dwell, I am troubled with a doubtful heart. Though for a little while I keep my heart at peace, the storm of love will soon attack. If I had wings, I am one who suffers, wishing to flee back to the mountain of Minakami in the dead of night. To the island where your beloved Reiko dwells, I am a featherless bird with clipped wings. My thoughts are like the white clouds wrapping the ridge of Mount Ibuki, with never a clearing sky.'

斯く歌ふ折もあれ、前方の森林より七人の乙女、白衣の直垂に緋の長袴を穿ち、各自に水晶の壺を抱きながら、曲玉池の水を汲まむとや、しとしとと入り来る。

艶男はまた見付かつては大変と、忽ち踵をかへし、伊吹の山の中腹なる鏡湖のかたへの樹蔭を目がけ、急ぎ逃げて行く。

艶男は樹蔭に憩ひながら歌ふ。

『漸くにわれは遁れて来りけり いざ休らはむ桂木のかげ 女神のみ数多住まへる此島に 心いぶかしくわれは悩むも 今暫し心安らけく保てども やがて襲はむ恋の嵐は 翼あらば水上の山に夜の間に 逃げ帰らむと悩めるわれかも 麗子の恋しき君のいます島に 翼はがれし裸鳥われは 伊吹山尾根を包める白雲の 晴るるひまなきわが思ひかな』
