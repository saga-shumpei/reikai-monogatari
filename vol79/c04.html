Chapter 4: 救ひの Ship [1985]

救ひの船〔1985〕

§ 1

Adeyaka, realizing by the roots of the grass under the moonlight that Uraraka had already become a spirit body and was no longer a person of this world, and weeping and mourning with excessive longing, sought to follow after Uraraka, and reached the shores of Tamayako,

'Come now, fearlessly to the depths of the lake, I shall go to my beloved's side; Today, when I am parted from Uraraka, A body that lives in the world in vain.'

So saying, he flung himself boldly toward the moonlit surface of the lake, and vanished, leaving behind only white waves. To this place, an old man with white hair and of strange appearance was rowing a single small boat while dangling a fishing rod, but looking back at the sound of Adeyaka's plunge, and gazing through the moonbeams upon the water's surface,

'How wondrous, the sound of water heard just now, Could it be the sound of a person rather than fish tribes? Though I know not who it may be, I must rescue and save them, To the extent that the strength of my body may avail; In this lake where the heavens are pure and the water is likewise pure, What manner of person might he be who leaps in?'

So saying, he remained for a while lost in thought. Far in the distance, a dark shadow floated up with a plop. The old man deftly rowed his small boat close, reached out his hand to the dark shadow, and rescued it up into the boat. When he looked closely, it was Adeyaka, the child of the Kunitsukami. The old man made him expel water, applied artificial respiration, and at last caused him to be revived.

'Thou art indeed the scion of the country's ancestors, Art thou not the lord Adeyaka? Though I am myself of a lowly fisherman's body, I am one who has served thy parents; Though I know not what matters may have befallen thee, Is it not pitiful to cast away thy life? Does thou not know, or dost thou fail to realize, The blessing of preserving life in heaven and earth? In the world there is nothing more precious than life, Do not hold it lightly; Death is easy, living is hard, of the jewelled string, Do not cast away thy life so lightly.'

Having thus chanted and called him back to life, Adeyaka, slowly returning to his senses,

'How strange, when I thought my life had died, Behold, I am alive within the boat; Though I am myself one whom thy lordly self hath saved, Forgive me for casting away my life. Parted from the person I love, in this world I would not think of a life that must be lived; Not regretting the life cast away for the sake of love, Did I leap into the lake; Thou must be a boatman, why then Didst thou save me? I feel it with resentment.'

The old man, with stern solemnity,

'I am none other than the old man of the lake, appearing as the god Of Water, Fire, and Earth, guarding this lake; Serving Yoshikohime and Kawakamihime, I am the god who guards the waters, fires, earth, and lake; What would it avail to throw away the jewelled string of life? Live once more and guard the country.'

Adeyaka, with a look of uncertainty,

'Could I have thought that thou art the god of Water, Fire, Earth, and the lake? I am ashamed and have no desire to live; Nevertheless, rescued by thee and now here, Should preserving my life be a blessing?'

The god of Water, Fire, and Earth, smilingly,

'The folly of casting away the jewelled string of life, Knowest thou not, it is the life of the gods; The jewelled string of life that has become the water and fire of the Lord God, To cast away life is a heavy sin; Repent of thy sin, change thy heart, and let thy loving heart Be washed in this lake; If there is a bond, there should be a time to meet again, How impatient thy heart is.'

Adeyaka, suddenly turning about in spirit,

'How wondrously grateful is the divine teaching of the god of Water, Fire, and Earth, My soul has been revived; Following after the tracks of Uraraka, I now Threw away my life in this lake; Oh, my foolish heart, now once more At thy teachings, a heart of repentance wells up; When I look up, the moon in the august sky smilingly Bows and admonishes me; Even the moon reflected at the bottom of the water seems to Shine upon my face and admonish me; Even the blowing wind so softly across my face Caresses and passes through, consoling my heart; Who is there that would not cherish the jewelled string of life, Even to the fish tribes at the bottom of the lake; When I think that the life I threw away was for the sake of love, Even to see thy face makes my heart feel ashamed; In the moonlight illuminating my foolish body, Heaven and earth are narrow, and shame wells up; Yet Uraraka must have been snatched away By the wicked gods and lost her life.'

『あら不思議今聞えたる水音は 魚族ならで人の音かも 何人か知らねど救ひ助くべし わが身の力のあらむかぎりは 天清く水また清きこの湖に 飛び込む者は何人なるらむ』

と言ひながら暫し考へ込んでゐる。遥か先方に黒い影がぽかりと浮いた。老人は手早く小舟を漕ぎ寄せ、黒き影に手を差し延べ船中に救ひあげた。よくよく見れば国津神の子艶男である。老人は水を吐かせ、人工呼吸を施し、漸くにして蘇生せしめた。

『君こそは国の御祖の御伜 艶男の君におはしまさずや 吾こそはいやしき漁師の身なれども 汝が両親に仕へたるもの 何事のおはしますかは知らねども 生命捨つるは浅ましからずや 天地に生命を保つ幸を 君は知らずや悟らざるにや 生命より尊きものは世の中に あらざるものを軽んじ給ふな 死はやすし生くるはかたし玉の緒の 生命かろがろ捨てさせ給ふな』

§ 2

Shihotsuchigami smiled broadly,

Urarakahime: 'Thy life hath been saved, And thou becomest the ruler of the Dragon's Capital. From now on, guiding thy life, I shall escort and present thee to the Dragon's Capital. The Dragon Palace, where numerous Tatsugami dwell, Is distant, so haste not.'

Adeyaka chants:

The moon shines brilliantly crossing the sacred sky, I, Adeyaka, cross the vast lake. Seeing the starlight twinkle in the sky, Like gold and silver sand strewn about, It closely resembles the sacred garden of Kami. Gazing down upon the depths of the lake, Gold and silver sparkle brightly, While gleaming in the moonlight, Illuminating the scene of Tengokujiyōdo Right before my eyes now. Oh, how reassuring, how reassuring, Escorted by Shihotsuchigami, Drifting in the midst of the boundless lake, The Dragon Palace Island, Where Urarakahime is enshrined, Thinking whether we are advancing to the palace of the dragon, How joyful, how wondrous! Is not the moon also an image of Ruten? What is there to lament of the past, Arousing from a dream, I now Reviving and advancing across the lake With the speed of Itsushiyasenri. The sacred sky is pure, the moon is pure, The starlight glitters, Safely guiding the boat of my journey To the opposite shore. Oh Kamunagara, Kamunagara, My father and mother must surely, oh surely, Searching for the whereabouts of us two, Breaking their hearts a thousandfold, How they must be lamenting! Oh Kamunagara, Kamunagara, May the grace of Kami's progress be pure, And forever peacefully Protect my beloved parents! Hearing that Urarakahime, Whom we thought was no longer in this world, Is the ruler of the island of the Dragon Palace, My courage increases manifold beyond usual, My heart filled with hope. Oh, how brave, how brave! At last the universe is revived, And the sunken heaven and earth pop up Right before my eyes, Singing of eternal life. Could I have imagined that Urarakahime did not die, But is in the palace of the dragon, so I hear? I too have once had my life saved, Crossing purely over these lake waters. Had Shihotsuchigami not been here, my life Would surely have ceased to exist in this world. Embraced by the blessings of the heaven and earth Kami, I have preserved my life without casting it away. Oars dipped into this lake with pure winds, How joyful is the journey to the Dragon Palace. Crossing over to the wondrous Dragon Palace Island, My heart has also grown youthful. Embracing boundless thoughts, Am I not the one crossing the boundless lake?'

Shihotsuchigami, while rowing the stern,

The night grows deeper, The blowing wind also rustles softly, Crossing from north to south. Though the island of the Dragon Palace is distant, Hoisting the sail to this north wind, If we advance, it cannot be too far. The moon in the sacred sky grows ever brighter, Penetrating even to the bottom of the lake, Even the manner of the aquatic tribes dancing Is vividly reflected in my eyes. Proceeding over the pure fine sand, This jade boat is the sacred progress Created by the Kami of the Dragon Palace. Oh Kamunagara, Kamunagara, Being the one who guards the lake, On this auspicious day and auspicious hour, Protecting Lord Adeyaka, How joyful it is to cross! The merit of Shihotsuchigami Shall shine until the end of ten thousand generations.

Chanting thus, they row out with all their might. At length they arrive at the first gate of the Dragon Palace. Ōtatsumihiko no Mikoto, having precognition of Adeyaka's coming to seek them, sent numerous retainers to the first gate, awaiting them and expressing welcome by chanting 'urō urō'.

Protected by Shihotsuchigami, Adeyaka has crossed over to this island. Adeyaka, who cast his body down into the water's depths, Has been rescued by Shihotsuchigami. Protected by the merits of Shihotsuchigami, He shall meet Uraraka, his younger sister, once again. Though calling each other brother and sister, they proclaimed themselves thus, Yet they never performed the act of marriage. Yearning with deep love, having entrusted their lives to one another, How brilliant must be the tryst of those two.
