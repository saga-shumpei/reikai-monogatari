Chapter 5: Lake 畔の遊び [1986]

湖畔の遊び〔1986〕

§ 1

Where flowers bloom and fragrance fills the Riugū through all four seasons, Is it perhaps the very image of Amatsumikuni? Both grass and trees, bathing in the light of spring, Flourish eternally in this island realm. Even the tree fruits of the everlasting island of Tokonatsu Are bountiful indeed, wherever one may go. Mountains and rivers alike flow purely, purely, and the heart of the Tatsugami Is gentle, not as it may appear to the eye. All the Urokuzutachi gather together upon this island, And their Inochi flourishes. Reflecting down to the depth of Yachihiro, the shadow of the Dairiuden In the Riugū is pure indeed. There were four pillars of gods who served Ōtatsumihiko since ancient times: One was called Harukihiko, and another Came to be praised as Natsukahahiko; Yet another was Akimizuhiko, and Fuyukazehiko; These four persons were the Mitomogami.

Ōtatsumihiko-no-Mikoto, accompanied by Otohimegami and followed by four pillar ministers, wishing to behold the full bloom of flowers on Ibukiyama, prepared a bento, had numerous Tatsugami guard the front, rear, left, and right, and attempted a day's excursion.

On the mid-slope of Ibukiyama, there is a somewhat large lake. Reflecting the sun, moon, stars, and constellations, and shining eternally like a mirror, this lake is called the Mirror Lake.

Ōtatsumihiko-no-Mikoto spread a narrow mat by the edge of the lake, and while ladling and exchanging wine of fruit and the like,

Otohimegami: 'The valley water tumbling down from the summit of Ibukiyama Flows purely and has become the Mirror Lake. Is this lake the heart of Otohimegami, Pure and clear, never muddy for all eternity? Gathering mountain waters here to make a lake, We have caused many yamame to live and thrive. If I clap my hands, the mountain fish gather and draw near— I would show their gentle forms to you.'

Otohimegami, smiling:

Otohimegami: 'As I have crossed over to this land, every single thing That meets my eyes is wondrous indeed. The gentleness of the fish that draw near when I clap my hands I behold as the very form of my Riugū. Reflecting the shadows of the two of us upon the lake water, Let us pledge forever our eternal bond. I once thought myself lost to this world— To wander upon such a blissful mountain! The patrinia flowers, swayed by the wind, bloom in rich fragrance— How gentle is the form of those blossoms! The fragrance of Chinese bellflowers glowing in purple, Frail for a thousand ages—may I not weary of it even to the end! Wisteria flowers bloom and fragrance to the point of crowding out all else— How pure is the shadow at the edge of this lake. Floating like blooming flowers upon the water's bed, The wisteria waves are especially marvelous.'

Ōtatsumihiko-no-Mikoto sings:

Ōtatsumihiko-no-Mikoto: 'From the ancient days of the distant age of gods, Taught to be the divine nose of the Lord Ōkami, Ibukiyama as it is handed down; Every single grass and tree that grows there Is heard to be naught but medicine Healing the illnesses of men. From over its ridges, falling and rushing down, The spring waters of the valley are gathered Down to the very bottom of the Mirror Lake; Even a single drop thereof is said to be medicine! Scooping up this medicinal water in the palms of our hands, Through a thousand and eight thousand generations, like the cord of jewels, Preserving our Inochi in ageless immortality, Let us enjoy the spring of Tokoyo. This water turns to wine, And becoming medicine, it illuminates and consoles True indeed, echoing as a noble sacred ground of the Riugū renowned by name, the heart of gods and men. Lowering the sacred seed of the Kunitsumikami,'

Ōtatsumihiko-no-Mikoto: 'The deep grace of the Lord God who bestowed it Could the bottom of the Yachihiro Mirror Lake ever be cloudy? High grace—would it not reach Even beyond the cloud-ridden heights of the great sky? Ah, how marvelous, ah, how clear! The opening threshold of the Heavenly Rock Cave, From today onward, the island of the Riugū Is the Earthly Paradise itself, And human Inochi too shall be prolonged. Rejoice, rejoice, ye hundred gods, Dance, dance with spirit, Until the bottom of the great earth gives way.'

At this song, the guard Tatsugami surrounded the Mirror Lake, playing various kinds of music and dancing wildly back and forth. The state of it was like a hundred thousand butterflies going mad all at once, or like scattering cherry blossoms in full bloom blown about by the spring wind.

Harukihiko stood at the water's edge, gazed out over the lake's surface, and sang in a loud voice:

Harukihiko: 'How wondrous that Otohimegami has appeared, And will guard our Inochi! Bathing in the light of the spring sun, the Tatsugami Shall live eternally in an imperial reign where flowers bloom. The leaves of the trees of Ibukiyama in spring have sprouted, As a single fragment of divine grace. The mountain cherry blossoms, blooming in full and riotous profusion, Revive my eyes anew! Blooming and rich in fragrance without knowing the boundaries of spring and summer, The fragrance of a hundred flowers is marvelous. That spring flowers and autumn flowers alike bloom together at once To celebrate the imperial reign is truly marvelous.'

伊吹山の中腹には稍大いなる湖水あり。日月星辰をうつして永久に鏡の如く光りゐる、この湖水を鏡の湖と称ふ。

大竜身彦の命は歌ふ。

『遠き神代の昔より 主の大神の御鼻と つたはり来る伊吹山 生ふる草木はことごとに 人の病をいやすてふ 薬ばかりと聞くからは これの尾の上ゆ落ちたぎつ 谷の清水を集めたる 鏡の湖の水底まで その一滴も薬とや 薬の水を掌に掬び 千代も八千代も玉の緒の 生命保ちて不老不死 常世の春を楽しまむ この水変じて酒となり 薬となりて神人の 心を照しなぐさめつ げにも名におふ竜宮の 貴の聖地とひびくらむ 国津御神の御種を

この歌に警護の竜神等は鏡の湖を取り巻きて、種々の楽を奏でつつ右往左往に舞ひ狂ふ。その状態は、百千万の胡蝶の一時に狂ふが如く、爛漫たる桜花の春風に吹き散る如き有様なりける。

春木彦は汀辺に立ち、湖面を眺めて声高らかに歌ふ。

『有難し弟姫神の現れまして 吾等が生命守り給はむ 春の陽の光りをあびて竜神は 永久に花咲く御代に生きなむ 伊吹山春の木の葉は芽ぐみたり 神の恵の一片として 爛漫と咲き乱れたる山桜 吾眼新しくよみがへらすも 春夏のけぢめも知らに咲き匂ふ 百の花香の匂ひめでたし 春の花秋の花香も一時に 御代を祝ひて咲くはめでたき』

§ 2

Natsukahahiko sings:

'I too stand by the shore and speak words, Celebrating the blessings of Riugūjima, The form of the Himegami descending from the Kuni of Yoshihara Is truly peerless in this world. Matching the Kuhashime, I pray that the fortune Of my Lord may be forever, Urarakahime is the Otohime of Riugūjiyō, And she graciously blesses us anew. If the Otohime no Kami had not descended, Our shison would never float eternally. O Otohime no Kami, marvelous to the eyes Of the Tatsugami possessing a pitiable form, How joyous it is that Natsukahasen too flows without ceasing Into the mirror-like Kagamiko. Could the pure misugata of Kagamiko Be the very form of Otohimegami? In Kagamiko where both moon and sun float amidst namima, The chiuya view is rare indeed in this world, Urokuzu rejoice at the coming forth of our Lord, Their hiyakusen shadows leaping upon the minomo.'

Akimizuhiko sings:

'The small waves of the dashing Kagamiko Have cleansed my dragon form purely. Today, the shadows of the water-worms dwelling among my scaly body Hide away in delightful comfort. From today, all of our lineage without exception Shall escape the suffering of the three cold and three hot realms. Enduring three thousand years of torment to become human, It is sacred to think we live in this world. Shrinking three thousand years of days and months to preserve human bodies, We enjoy the blessings of Otohime. With time passing, my dragon form grows smooth, Oh, what a joyous and easy age to feel human at last! As the Kototama of the norito proclaimed by Otohimegami resounds, My physical body is steadily renewed. Even the unstained autumn waters saw urokuzu Afflicted by numerous worms, While the shadows of the maggots lurking between my scaly skin Vanished and disappeared one after another. Did the Kuni of Yoshihara, floating amidst the myriad miles of ocean, Also harbor such afflictions? Deep within the vast expanse of Tamayako, the island of Riugū Has truly become a beloved isle. Must they have called the gathering of sinful and turbid jewels The foundation of the dragon's isle? Why are the people living on this island's root With their human faces and dragon bodies so sorrowful? The time of Heaven has now arrived, and we meet The blessings of Urarakahime of Mount Minase.'

Fuyufuhiko sings:

'In the cold wind sweeping down from the peaks of Mount Ibuki, When winter approaches, it freezes the lake. When winter comes, all the urokuzu of the lake Hide in the depths of the waters and lament the age. The grace of the Lord Kami has manifested more than ever, And Otohimegami has descended from heaven. The Kototama and blessings of Otohime Will surely prevent the lake from freezing even in winter. It is my sorrowful thought That grasses and trees wither as winter draws near. From today, this island's root shall know no winter, And flourish as an island of eternal spring. Serving in the retinue of my Lord, the banquet mats By the shores of Kagamiko are filled with joy. Myriad kami rejoice at the coming forth of your Lordship, Dancing and plunging wildly to and fro. Oh how clear, oh how delightful, the foundation of the dragon's isle Is buried deep in flowers. Mountains are green, valley waters clear, and the surface of the lake Floats the sun and moon, drifting in heaven.'

Great Tatsumihiko no Mikoto sings:

'Looking up, white clouds upon the ridge of Ibuki Play peacefully, swayed by the wind. White clouds play upon the mountaintop, while I play Near the lake of Nakahara. Peering through the gaps of the white clouds, the Hinogami Shines His pure light upon us. Looking closely at the white clouds, in their very center Purple clouds are seen hidden. Opening the purple clouds, the Amatsuhi Shines upon the surface of this lake. Even Mount Ibuki, tradition says is inhabited by Magatsuhika at its peak, Is pure today. Feeling as though heaven and earth have opened at once, I play by the shores of Otohime's lake.'

Otohime of Uraraka sings with a clear voice:

'From the distant age of the gods when the majestic deities ruled, Since times unknown in the dragon capital Of Riugū, I crossed over and beheld the shadow of a strange man. Just as my heart was filled with wonder, Adorned with gold, silver, pearls, and jewels, Upon a high palanquin with shining eyes, I was hoisted and carried onward step by step. Passing through an iron gate and then another, Passing through a towering iron gate, Escorted to a lofty palace, Together with Great Tatsumihiko, To guard the dragon's island root, Guarding the morning and the evening, As I proclaimed the divine words of the kami's possession With a clear and lofty voice, How wondrous it is, the grasses and trees growing upon the island root Deepen in their colors, A hundred flowers and a thousand blossoms all at once Compete in their brilliance and bloom in wild profusion.'

Perfumes burning in all four directions, It goes without saying that the Kariyōbinga speak, Even the singing voices of Momotorichidori Resound more and more delightfully, The blowing wind is also gentle, From the ridges of the mountains to the hems of the garments, Moistened by the dew of blessing, Oh, the wondrousness of being revived! I, for three years upon this island, Living and flourishing, to the island people Eternal life and divine prosperity, Swearing to God, shall bestow, Be courageous, be courageous, oh Momogami! Rejoice, dance, sing, and whirl, The rock door of heaven has opened, The iron gate of darkness is flung wide, The sun and moon of the august sky are bright, Illuminating this Kunihara, To the extent that there is no distinction between day and night, The sun and moon together shine alternately.

It goes without saying that the Tatsugamitachi speak, From the fish of the lake, the mountains, and the fields, Down to the tips of the grasses and trees, The dew of blessing shines, May the world flourish eternally! Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, The living Kototama of divine possession, Proclaim morning and evening, oh Tatsugamitachi!
