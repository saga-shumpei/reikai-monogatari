Chapter 16: 共鳴の庭 [1997]

共鳴の庭〔1997〕

§ 1

The Himegamitachi of the dragon's Shimane, which unfolds at the Sanroku of Ibuki, searched for the Adeyaka who had vanished like clouds in a single Ichiya, and the uproar turned the world upside down, until the voices of Hitan filled the dragon's Shimane. In the midst of this, the absence of Kakitsubatahime was considered the height of mystery, and the voices of the Megami, who whispered, grew angry, lamented, or cursed, were indistinguishable from the insects chirping in the autumn Chigusa or the Momodori singing in the Kigi, for they were beyond Keiyō.

Ōtatsumihiko-no-Mikoto, in his deep Shitsubaurakutan at the sight of the Adeyaka’s shadow being nowhere to be found, entered the Okuden, bolted the door, and refused to show his face; Urarakahime-no-Himegami, as if it were all Imasara, was flustered and panicked, ordering her Jijiyo to search every corner of the Dennai, yet without finding a single clue.

‘Where is the Adeyaka of Itokoya now? / I had relied on him as the strength of my Inochi. / Upon the waves where there is no way to return to my Furusato, / what has become of you, my Adeyaka-no-Kimi? / Are you returning home, resenting me / for having become the wife of Ōtatsumihiko-no-Mikoto? / In any case, your form is dear to me, / though I had calmed myself with my own strength. / Without you, my heart to dwell in this land / fades away, and my sorrow only grows. / Where is Ōtatsumihiko-no-Mikoto now? / I cannot see his Onkage, and my heart is restless. / That I am with my husband of Tachihada / is only because I think my brother is here. / The thought that I would easily return / to the land of my parents has become a dream. / After living on this island for one or two years, / I thought I would leave, relying on you. / Now, my fierce heart has faded away, / and I feel that living is of no worth.

Though the flowers and Chigusa bloom in the mountains and fields, / they are but sad tidings to me. / There is no way to illuminate my heart. / Abandoned by my brother of light, / I am lonely for the form of you, / whom I relied upon day and night as the power of severity. / The clouds I look up to and the lake plains / remain silent to my lamentations. / With nothing to rely on, / I have no desire but to die. / On this island where many dragon gods dwell, / it is lonely for a human to live alone. / I have endured a hundred sorrows / while staying morning and evening with my husband of Tachihada.

Though the mountain of Ibuki is high, / though the lake of Tamaya is deep, / though the heavenly sky is clear, / for one who has no place to be, / I think my Inochi will end. / But having no weapon in my hand today, / I am helpless and my tears / never cease to dry on my sleeves, / as I sink into heavy sorrow. / If the Lord God dwells in this world, / then on this island floating above the waves, / save this suffering goddess / and return her to her original land. / I try this and that, / but the more I do, the more miserable I feel. / There is no one who resembles me, / only dragon gods of Tachihada. / Even our Kototama do not pass / between us properly, such is the suffering of today. / As I think it over and over,

The retribution for having lightly heard / the words of my father and mother / is that I was snatched by a dragon messenger / to this island I did not know even in dreams. / It is for my sins that I have come here. / Please forgive me, O Amatsukami, / and all you Kunitsukami of the eight million, / I pray before your noble presence. / Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, / whether the moon waxes or wanes, / even if the dragon's Shimane sinks, / could I ever have two hearts / toward the Adeyaka-no-Kimi whom I love morning and evening? / O my brother, / please understand the purity of my heart. / I swear it by the Kannagara. / Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara, / please grant me your divine grace.’

Just as she was reciting this song of lamentation, a Jijiyo-Megami suddenly appeared. When one looked closely, it was Shirahagi, who had been yearning for the Adeyaka even to the point of desiring his Inochi. Shirahagi crept up before the younger Himegami and sang with tears in her hand: / ‘The Kimi I love has vanished from sight. / The Kimi of Inochi whom I yearned for morning and evening, / the form of Kakitsubatahime is also nowhere to be seen. / Perhaps the two of them have stepped upon the crest of the waves. / The path of the waves is far, and they return to the mountains above the water. / Since there is no longer any connection, / how does the younger Hime-no-Kimi feel? / The tears of sorrow for the dwelling of the Adeyaka-no-Kimi / fall like rain, / and how heavy are the sleeves of my hitatare. / Whether it is the past or the present, I cannot tell, / my soul seems to have gone mad. / The light of the Adeyaka-no-Kimi, / which is like a dream, a reality, or an illusion, is fading into darkness.’

斯く歎きの歌を宣らす折しも、忽然としてここに現はれたる侍女神あり。よくよく見れば、艶男に生命までもと焦れたる白萩なりけり。白萩は弟姫神の前ににじり寄り、涙片手に歌ふ。

『わが恋ふる君は見えなくなりましぬ 朝夕慕ふ生命の君は 燕子花姫の姿も見えずなりぬ 二人は波の秀踏みましにけむ 波路はろか水上の山にかへります よすがは絶えてあらざるものを 弟姫の御君如何に思召すや 艶男の君の御舎を 悲しさの涙は雨と降りしきり わが直垂の袖の重さよ その昔か今は現かわかぬまで わが魂は狂ひけらしな 夢現まぼろしなるよ艶男の 君の光は闇と消えつつ』

§ 2

Otohimegami Uraraka, replying to this, said:

'The sorrow of my thoughts is the same as yours, My heart is in turmoil, a dream within a dream. I am now filled with agitation, For a while, I shall rest in the inner chamber.'

Having finished speaking, she entered deep into the Okuden. Shirahagi, left all alone, descended from the Ōtono in a dejected state, wandering listlessly through the Teien, until she reached the Migihabe of the Kiyoike, where she strove to clear the melancholy of her heart while listening to the sound of the Kotodaki cascading down.

'O Takimizu falling from the Kagamiko, clearly and purely, Wash away this painful skin of mine. It is of no avail to regret or weep now, Though my sad tears fall like the waterfall. Following the Adeyaka-no-kimi, the Kakitsubata, Must have hidden their bodies together. How shall I seek the Kakurega of the Lord? It is not I alone who thinks this. All the Megami who serve in this palace, Must be sunk in grief just as I am. Rather than living in such a world and meeting with grief, I would prefer to end my life as the Mokuzu of the lake.'

Just as she was singing this, a Megami approached stealthily on tiptoe; it was Shiragiku. Shiragiku sang faintly:

'Is the Himegami weeping while gazing at the Takitsuse, Shiragihagi-no-kimi? As for the whereabouts of the Adeyaka-no-kimi, I, Shiragiku, Am a sad Otome. Even hearing the sound of the Takitsuse, for some reason, I feel so lonely today. I, too, am a Shiragiku of the same sentiment, A sad Otome weeping tears of blood.'

At this voice, Shirahagi was startled, looked back, and while blinking her eyes wet with tears, said:

'Was it you, Shiragiku-no-kimi, at the Takitsuse? I have been wringing out my tears. The shadow of the Hikari-no-kimi whom I love has vanished, And it is a lonely morning. Though I think to search even beyond the clouds of the Ōzora, It is of no avail, for I am a body without wings.'

Shiragiku sang:

'I, too, am searching for the whereabouts of the Lord, and I, Shiragiku, Am lamenting in a way that puts the flowers to shame. Even if there comes a world where the moon falls and the lake dries up, This love of mine shall never be lost. Feeling as if the jewel in my palm has been snatched away, I spend my mornings and evenings in lamentation.'

弟姫神の麗子、これに答へて、

『かなしさの思ひは同じ吾とても 心乱れて夢現なる 吾は今心騒ぎてありにけり 今しばらくを奥にてやすまむ』

と宣り終り、奥殿深く入らせ給ふ。白萩は只一人悄然と大殿を降りて、庭園を力もなげに逍遥しながら、清池の汀辺に着き、琴滝の漲り落つる音を聞きつつ、心の憂さを晴らさむと努めてゐる。

『鏡湖ゆ落つる滝水さやさやに 苦しき膚を洗ひ流せよ 今となり悔むも泣くも詮なけれ かなしき涙は滝とおつれど 艶男の君に従ひ燕子花は ともにその身を忍ばせにけむ 如何にして君の隠家探ねむと 思ふは一人吾のみならず この宮に仕ふる女神のことごとは われと等しく歎きに沈まむ かかる世に生きて歎きにあふよりも 湖の藻屑となりて果てばや』

斯く歌ふ折しも、差し足抜き足、忍び寄る女神は白菊であつた。白菊はかすかに歌ふ。

『滝津瀬を眺めて泣ける姫神は 君の行方をしら萩の君か 艶男の君の行方もしら菊の 吾はかなしき乙女なりけり 滝津瀬の音聞くさへも何かしら 今日はさみしく思はるるかな われもまた同じ思ひの白菊の 血に泣くかなしき乙女なりけり』

此の声に白萩は驚き、後振り返り、涙にしめる目をしばたたきながら、
