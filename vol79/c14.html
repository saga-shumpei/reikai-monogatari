Chapter 14: 再生の歓び [1995]

再生の歓び〔1995〕

§ 1

Yamagamihiko and Kahakamihime, the aged Kami couple who had reigned here since the days of their ancestors as the Obito of the Kunitsukami, possessing a land of about twenty ri square centered on Mount Minakamiyama, were in a state of wretched decline, their bodies wasting away day by day as they wept without distinction between night and day, ever since their two children, an Otodoi pair unparalleled in heaven and earth, had unexpectedly hidden their forms.

The many Kunitsukami who served Mount Minakamiyama ran about in all directions searching for the whereabouts of the brother and sister, but today, after about a month had passed, there was no news, only the sound of the Aranami beating against the shore.

The two pillars, Yamagamihiko and Kahakamihime, wandered along the shore of Lake Tamaya and sang in thin, faint voices.

Yamagamihiko: 'How sorrowful it is / That I should meet with such grief / I never imagined / Where is the destination / Of my beloved child / Though I have searched for thirty days and thirty nights / Until this very day / There is no news, only the sound of the waves / Beating against the shore / My myriad tears / Have already dried up / Even my voice has withered / My body, day and night / Wastes away, devoid of strength / There is no meaning in living in this world / Even in a dream, I wish to know / The whereabouts of the brother and sister / I wish to see their faces / But thinking of it is in vain, and I, today / Have no recourse but to weep.

If there be Kami in this world / I pray you let me know / The whereabouts of my beloved child / Though the sun and moon shine in the sky / Though the stars twinkle without end / Though the grass grows thick upon the earth / Though the waves of the lake roar / There is no word of my beloved child / Only the silence of the waves / And the voices of the plovers flying to and fro / Ah, how sad, how bitter / My life is not worth living / It would be easy to cast it away / But if I hear that my beloved child / Is alive upon the earth / Surely, oh surely / My heart would revive / Have mercy, O Itsunomitama / Before the Mizunomitama / I offer this aged, withered body / And pray with all my heart.

Where is my child, I long to hear / Where is my daughter, morning and evening / My search is in vain, I only hear / The sound of the wild wind blowing through the fields.'

Yamagamihiko: 'When I look at the sky, my heart is sad; when I look at the lake / I am utterly lonely, for I am one without children / Though this is a land filled with the blessings of heaven and earth / I wish to die, for I have no child / Searching for the whereabouts of my beloved child, my soul / Has withered like a thread / In the misery of a body that is but bones / I am a lonely one, my strength failing.'

Kahakamihime: 'Born into the world of men, I have grown old / The life of my husband has also waned / My beloved child, whom I relied upon for strength / By what evil influence / Has he vanished like the foam / Upon the white waves that rise and roar / How wretched it is / Did he wander and cross / To the demon island where the Ryujin dwells / In Lake Tamaya / There is no news, only the loneliness of the sound / Of the waves beating against the shore / We long for our child morning and evening / We weep tears longing for our princess / They have long since dried up, and thus / We have wasted away / Even my words / Are not as I wish, such is my suffering / The hundred Kami have searched for the two children / Roaming the mountains and rivers, but even now.

The news of the wind is but weeping tears / My sleeves are never dry / Have mercy, have pity / O Itsunomitama and Mizunomitama / I pray before the presence of the Kami / Listening to the sound of insects chirping in the grass / And the hundred birds singing in the treetops / I wonder, could it be / The voice of my child / How wretched is the body that yearns for its child / In my old age, lost and wandering / Today, on this sad shore / Holding onto a hopeless wish / As I stand, the evening sun / Is wrapped in clouds and disappears / Over the ridge of the distant mountains, leaving no trace.

Separated from our beloved children, we have nothing to rely on but the Kami of heaven and earth / O Kami, O Kami, have mercy upon us / And return our two children to us / Today, without the shadow of my beloved child / Whose features were so clear and beautiful, I am utterly sad / When I approach, thinking it is my child, and look / It is but the voices of insects chirping in the thicket at evening / Though the sun shines and the moon is clear, my heart / Is clouded and I cannot distinguish anything / The freshness of the irises blooming on the shore / Has no charm for me / I gaze out at the endless expanse of the great lake / And think of my children as birds sleeping on the water / Though the birds sing, the flowers bloom, and the insects cry / It is a lonely spring for me.'

Kahakamihime: '...'

葭原の国の一部、水上山を中心として約二十里四方の土地を領有ぎ、国津神の頭人となりて父祖の代よりここに君臨したる御祖の神、山神彦、川神姫の翁と姥は、天にも地にもかけがへなき二人の兄妹が、ゆくりなくも其姿を隠せしより、夜昼の区別なく慟哭して身体は日に日に衰へ、見るかげもなき憐れな姿となりゐたりける。

水上山に仕ふる数多の国津神等は、四方八方に馳け廻り、兄妹の所在をさがし求むれども、約一ケ月を経たる今日、何の便りも荒波の磯に打寄すばかりなりける。

山神彦、川神姫の二柱は、玉耶湖の汀辺をさまよひながら、声を細々と歌ふ。

『あな悲しや かかる歎きにあはむとは 思はざりしよ いとし子の 行方は何処 今日が日まで 三十日三十夜を探ぬれど 何の便りも波の音 磯打ち寄するばかりなり 万斛の 涙はすでに涸れ果てぬ わが声さへもしをれけり わが身体は日に夜に 痩せ衰へて力なく この世に生きてたよりなし 夢になりとも兄妹の 所在知りたや 顔見たや 思へど詮なき今日のわれは 泣くより外に術もなし

川神姫は歌ふ。

『人の世に生れてわれは年老いぬ 夫の命も衰へぬ 力と頼むいとし子は 如何なる曲の荒びにや 行方しら波立ち騒ぐ 水泡と消えしか浅ましや 玉耶の湖に浮びたる 竜神棲める魔の島に 若しも迷ひて渡りしか 何の便りもあら波の 磯打つ音の淋しさよ われらは朝夕子を慕ひ 姫を慕ひてなく涙 早や涸れ果てて斯くの如 痩せ衰へぬ 言葉さへ 思ふにまかせぬ苦しさよ 百神たちは二人の子の 行方探ぬと山川を 騒ぎ廻れど今にして

風の便りも泣く涙 乾く暇なき袖袂 恵ませ給へ憐れみ給へ 厳の御霊や瑞御霊 神の御前に願ぎまつる 草葉にすだく虫の音や 梢に囀る百鳥の 声を聞きつつ若しや若し わが子の声にあらぬかと 子を恋ふる身の浅ましや まよひにまよふ老の身の 今日は悲しき汀辺に あらぬ望みを抱へつつ 佇み居れば夕津陽は 雲に包まれ遠山の 尾の上に消えてあともなし。

いとし子に離れしわれらは天地の 神を頼むの外なかるらむ 神よ神吾等を憐れみ給ひまして 二人の御子を返させ給へ 眉目形勝れて清きいとし子の かげなき今日はひたに悲しも わが子かと近より見れば叢に 夕べを鳴ける虫の声々 陽は照れど月は冴ゆれど村肝の 心曇りてあやめも分かず 汀辺に匂ふ菖蒲の清しさも われには何の趣もなし 果てしなき大湖原を打見やり 御子と思へば浮寝鳥なる 鳥うたひ百花匂ひ虫鳴けど われには淋しき春なりにけり』

§ 2

Yamagamihiko sings again.

I shall not tire of parting the grass and diving into the earth, until I have tracked the whereabouts of my child. In this world of dreams, while seeing dreams, like a dream, I have searched for my aimless child. Has my Miko become the seaweed of the lake? Is that not my beloved child? Since there is even one who claims to be my Miko, neither bird nor beast shall I shun. Has my Miko become a bird, or has he become a fish of the Unabara? How uncertain it is. While the Inochi of the Tama-no-o lasts, even if only Tadaichido, I wish to see the face of my beloved child.

As the Fūfu wandered the Kohen, singing songs of lamentation, Masago, the Kunitsukami who serves the mansion, followed their trail and came after them.

My Lord is here, my Lord is standing here, how joyful it is. Searching for your whereabouts here and there, I have met you on the Masago-no-Isobe. How grateful I am, protected by the grace of Kami, the Futahashira are alive. Where is the Adeyaka Lord? Is the whereabouts of the Uraraka Hime still unknown?

Yamagamihiko replies to this:

Though I have searched everywhere for the place where Hitogusa should go, there is not even a shadow. Beyond this, I shall leave it to Kami and return, for my child is not of this world.

Masago sings:

My Lord, do not speak of such lonely things; you shall surely return alive. If I inquire into the dream I saw Yufube, the Adeyaka Lord shall return. The whereabouts of the Uraraka Lord is that she has crossed to the island of Riugū and become a queen. Yet, it is difficult to declare it with certainty, as it was only in an Oboroge dream.

Kahakamihime, with a Chikaraegaho and a Bishō, says:

Even if your Kotoba is not the truth, my heart flutters at the story of a dream that says he lives. In any case, I shall return to the mansion and wait for the time, for the Tasoga has fallen and I cannot distinguish Ayame.

Yamagamihiko and Kahakamihime, protected on the night road by the attendant Kami Masago, returned for the time being to the mansion, and that night, repeating only things that were not, they fell into a lonely sleep.

Awakened by the voice of the rooster announcing the dawn and the voice of the magpie, the two rose from their bed, were led again by Masago, and went out to the beach of Sakae-i.

Looking out into the far distance, a boat was advancing, rowing its stern toward this side.

Yamagamihiko, gazing at this scene, watched the surface of the lake without looking aside, wondering if it might be his child.

Yamagamihiko: 'Could it be, could it be my child, the speed of the boat rowing near the beach? If I knew it was the boat of my child, I would gather the hundred Kami and go out to welcome him.'

Masago: 'It appears to be the boat of the brother and sister, for the Kami of Water, Fire, and Earth are steering the stern.'

Just as he sang this, the boat gradually approached the beach. Looking closely, the ones steering the boat were the Kami of Water, Fire, and Earth, and the one standing at the bow appeared to be certainly Adeyaka, but what was suspicious was that it was a female Kami, and they watched without looking aside.

Kahakamihime: 'The one standing at the bow is certainly Adeyaka. But the figure of the woman is strange. It seems Adeyaka is returning in high spirits, carrying a woman I do not know. Protected by the thick grace of the Kami, my child was surely alive.'

Just as she sang this, the boat steered by the Kami of Water, Fire, and Earth finally arrived safely at the Kohen where the three were standing.

The aged parents, not knowing where to dance with their hands or step with their feet, immediately offered prayers of gratitude to heaven, and went back toward the mansion of Mizukami-yama with high spirits.

『草を別け土を潜るもいとはまじ わが子の行方突きとむるまで 夢の世に夢を見ながら夢の如 あてなきわが子を探ねぬるかな わが御子は湖の藻屑となりしかと わがいとし子は彼れならずやと わが御子と名告るものさへあるなれば 鳥も獣もいとはざるべし わが御子は鳥となりしか湖原の 魚となりしか心許なや 玉の緒の生命ある間只一度 見まく思ふもいとし子の面』

かく夫婦は湖辺をさまよひ、歎きの歌をうたふ折しも、館に仕ふる国津神真砂は、あとを探ねて追ひ来り、

暁告ぐる鶏の声、鵲の声に呼び覚されて、二人は寝間を起き出で、再び真砂に導かれて栄居の浜辺に出でてゆく。

遥か前方を見渡せば、一艘の舟、此方に向つて艫を漕ぎながら進み来る。

かく歌ふ折しも、漸くにして水火土の神のあやつる御舟は、三人の立てる湖辺に安々着きにける。

老いたる両親は、手の舞ひ足の踏む所を知らず、忽ち天に向つて感謝言を奏上し、勇み進んで水上山の館をさして帰りゆく。
