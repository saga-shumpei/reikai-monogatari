Chapter 11: 瀑下の乙女 [1992]

瀑下の乙女〔1992〕

§ 1

Ōtatsumihiko Mikoto, in his desire to treat Adeyaka, the Miko of the Mioya Kami who had come from afar by crossing the waves of the sea from the Seijiyō of Minakamiyama, with special favor, and as a result of much shōriyo, decided to have him reside in a palace built at the lower part of Kagamiko, the most scenic spot on Riugūjima, incorporating Kotodaki into the garden, and constructed with timber from Ōgahiogahi, featuring hekiguwa and sekiheki. Kotodaki gathered the waters of Kagamiko, and like a senjiyō hironuno hanging there, it caused the sound of a koto to resonate without kandan day and night, and its saugon and grandeur were beyond words.

Adeyaka would offer up Amatsu Norito and living Kototama in this shinden every morning and evening, praying for the development of the foundation of the island of the dragon, while many goddesses, yearning for his harmonious and clear voice and enamored with his graceful appearance, gathered in the spacious garden of this shinden, and while diligently practicing Kototama on the side, they immersed themselves in the joys of heaven. The clear spring where this waterfall falls is called the Pond of the Sword.

By the power of the Kototama recited morning and evening, the giant rocks scattered on the surface of the earth instantly transformed into agate, and the sunlight reflected on the surface touched by the spray of the waterfall, emitting an indescribable luster.

Adeyaka stands on the railing and sings:

'The pure water of the Mirror Lake / Is reflected here and falls in torrents / The vigor of this Kotodaki / Is it the falling of the Heavenly River / I think only of such a scene / I, morning and evening, this waterfall's / Precious sound of water listening / Wash my heart clean / The Kototama bestowed by Kami / Purifies water and fire and rings out / The freshness of this dwelling / In heaven and on earth, like this / A pure and joyful pure place / Thinking there is no other, night after night / Without sleeping, I stay awake to watch and listen / Rejoicing in the spring of Tokoyo / When I gaze at the surface of the spring / Large fish and small fish gather / At night they play among the waves / Looking up at the blessings of heaven and earth / Ah, is it heaven or paradise / The sound of the water gushing and falling / From the head of the waterfall on the island of Riugū / Is it the voice of the Kami of heaven and earth / Ah, how interesting, how promising / Living for a hundred or a thousand years / In this pure place, forever / I shall enjoy the life of existence / One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, hundred, thousand, ten thousand / O ten thousand Kami, please protect us.'

Just at such a moment, the cock crowed to announce the dawn, and magpies with white wings gathered on the shore of the Pond of the Sword, crying out 'caw-caw' on the roof of the Seiden.

The swans of the lake had gathered at the Pond of the Sword before anyone knew it, appearing as if they wished to listen to the Kototama of Adeyaka.

When one looks closely at the surface of the garden that has dawned brightly, among the many maidens serving in the Riugū-jō, seven maidens of superior beauty are seen facing the surface of the pond, pressing their hands together in prayer, praying fervently for something.

Adeyaka stands on the railing and, while gazing at this scene, sings:

'The pitch-black night has dawned, in my garden / On the surface of the pond, pure maidens stand / Looking closely, the graceful figures of seven maidens / I wish to hear what they are praying for.'

These seven maidens were the attendant goddesses named Shirahagi, Shiragiku, Ominaeshi, Kakitsubata, Ayame, Nadeshiko, and Fujibakama.

Shirahagi sings:

'Drawn by your beautiful voice / Without thinking, without knowing, I came here / As I look at your figure, my soul is emboldened / And the life of existence flourishes / The Kototama that does not mingle with the sound of the waterfall's rapids / How great is your power / Night after night, in dreams and in reality, your face / Floats before my eyes, and I cannot sleep / To clear this feeling of longing, I am now / Scooping the clear water of the Pond of the Sword / This water is the clear water of life, the true clear water / The water of life that has touched your eyes / Coming here, I have realized what is called love / Since I am also among the number of goddesses / The Kototama recited morning and evening resonates / And my heart is beating high.'

Adeyaka sings:

'I am one troubled by suffering; I am ashamed / That I have no way to repay your sincerity.'

Shirahagi sings:

'Even if you are in a body of suffering, what does it matter / My heart is filled by your Kototama.'

Shiragiku sings:

‘Though I am but a withered thing, O Shiragiku, In my longing, I have come at dawn. Like the clear water of the Tsurugiike pond, I think of you while my heart remains pure. Led by the cord of yearning, in the early dawn, I have come near to where you dwell. Whenever I hear your Mikoe, courageously, My soul is revived. Clad in white Shirotahe robes, you, my Lord, Your Onyosoho is truly magnificent. More than the Kotodaki that releases Kototama at all times, You are even more pure. Even if my wishes are not fulfilled, if I am near you, And can hear your Mikoe, I shall be happy. As Shiragiku blooms in the Yamano, there is no chance For an Adebito to pluck it with his Mite. Even the Shiragiku flower blooming in the field, when it meets The dew of Mimegumi, it hangs its head. Wishing to bathe in the compassion of a single dewdrop, I have come to the foot of the waterfall. Think of the Shiragiku flower as gentle, For it is a body revived by the dew of compassion.’

Adeyaka sings:

‘The figures of the maidens are so graceful, I gaze upon them and my heart flutters. The Onsugata of the seven gentle Otome, How clear they are, how beautiful they are. Since crossing to this island, I see things Rare and strange, with clear eyes. Among the rare things, above all, How lovely are the seven Otome. As I am like the moon in the Suichiu, I can only gaze upon your gentle shadows.’

Ominaheshi sings:

‘Like the Masago at the bottom of the Tsurugiike, You appear so clear, my Lord. Suppressing my longing heart, night after night, I have wetted my sleeves with tears. Bathed in the spray of the Takitsuse, my sleeves Have become wet along with my tears. Grant me the compassion of a single night’s dew, O Ominaheshi blooming at the foot of Ibuki. Because the sound of the Takitsuse biting the rocks is loud, My words of Kototama are about to vanish. The sound of the Kotodaki falls and roars, Effacing the heart that sings of sadness. How shall I speak of the depth of my longing? It is in vain, for the sound of the waterfall is high. Standing on the Obashima, separated by the spring of Tsurugiike, You are there, my Lord, what shall I do? Do you not understand the deep heart of the pond water? Are you not a man, not wood or stone? My heart that longs for you is tangled, Like a spool of thread that cannot be undone.’

Adeyaka sings:

‘Though I understand the sad hearts of the maidens, I pray you, O Kunitsukami, forgive me. Swearing by the Chihayaburu gods, I now Declare that I hold you dear. Nevertheless, grant me the promise of the night, For I am Adeyaka who serves the gods.’

Kakitsubata sings:

‘Even if you proclaim a thousand living Kototama, I shall not yield, I shall not sleep. Even if this life, like a thread of jewels, should perish, I shall not stop without sharing a pillow for one night. Ignoring the sad hearts of the seven Otome, Do you cast away our sincere hearts? Because you are a boy who knows not the compassion of the world, You are a demon, a monster, a boy without a soul. Since this sincerity does not reach you anywhere, I shall become a demon and torment you. Since you are the one who gave me this tormenting heart, I think of you as the true form of a demon. My Lord, my Lord, no matter how angry you may be, I shall not fear, I shall hate you to the end. Even if I hate you in despair and cast away my jeweled life, My soul shall not leave you for even a moment. Without you, I am tormented morning and evening, And I am about to lose my jeweled life. Because of this torment and suffering, in the early dawn, I have wandered here to hear your Mikoe. Even if I am struck by your hand and dismissed, I shall not hate you, I shall not lament. I do not accept your words that I am a withered thing, I am not a weak woman. I have loved you with my life, like a thread of jewels, Your life is now my own. Rather than longing and perishing in vain, Shall I take your life and smile? If it comes to this, I shall no longer fear the eyes of men, Nor the anger of the gods, for they are of no account. Becoming a demon, a thunderbolt, and a devil, I shall take your life.’

Adeyaka, feeling slightly overwhelmed by the intense love of Kakitsubata, sings despondently:

‘Did I ever think that on this island root, I would hear the fierce roar of such a strong maiden? Having crossed to an unknown island, I am thought of By one I did not expect, and I feel it is painful. No matter how many words of compassion you proclaim, I think I shall not change the root of my heart. Even if my life is taken, I shall not hate you, For I think of it as a blade filled with compassion.’

§ 3

While singing thus, Adeyaka, already having no courage to resist the intense renai of Kakitsubata and having resolved to follow her will, feigned ignorance and sang:

Kakitsubata: 'After I have made my vow to the Kami of Ametsuchi, I shall answer you; wait for a while.'

Kakitsubata sings:

Kakitsubata: 'If there be no machiga in your mikotoba, I too shall set my heart at ease and await the time.'

Ayame sings:

Ayame: 'The red hearts of the otome have gathered, and the masago has been dyed red. Because of you, my lord, whose akadama shines so brightly, the carp in the miike have also been dyed red. The color of the urokuzu, turning red one after another, appears as our magokoro. The purple of the ayame blooming by the pond, I shall offer to you; please accept it. The hitomoto of shirosango that grew at the minasoko has also been dyed red, one after another. Upon the branches of the rare red sango, flowers of kogane, shirogane, and shinjiyu bloom. The sangojiyu, stretching its branches over the surface of the water, grows toward the sky as we watch. Is it the manifestation of the heart of Adeyaka-dono, or the hearts of the otome, that all have turned red? The agate rocks on the migihabe also change color one after another, reflecting my face. Such a thing has never been seen since ancient times; how rare is this garden where the waterfall falls.

Though I think of it as a love that cannot be reached, my heart has become unable to distinguish even the ayame. Having no strength to speak to you alone, I have invited seven otome to come. Because of my shame and my love, I have remained silent without even uttering a kototama. Since it has come to this, I shall not fear; I only wish to hear your voice, which you proclaim to be kind. Even as I look upon your figure, the pounding of my chest does not cease; it is a painful morning.'

Nadeshiko sings:

Nadeshiko: 'The nadeshiko that blooms and scents the ridge and the foot of Mount Ibuki, today scents the migihabe. When I look upon your figure standing on the railing of the Yahiro-dono, it is as pure as the white hagi. Do you cast aside the gentle heart of the otome who loved you even at the cost of her life? Even if I should wither away pining for you, I shall not regret it, nor shall I hold a grudge. The sadness that the nadeshiko, trembling weakly and lonely, does not catch your eye. Do you not know the sad hearts of the seven otome gathered here to speak of love? Is the masurao who knows not the feelings of the world an incarnation of an oni or an incarnation of an akuma? Hear the sincere hearts of the otome, and do not let your anger rise; this is the voice of magokoro.'

Fujibakama sings:

Fujibakama: 'Though the seven otome express their magokoro one after another, are you, my lord, deaf as the wood-ear mushroom? The fujibakama, a grass flower with no beauty to behold, feels ashamed to meet you. Though the scent of the flower is not very visible, please draw out the heart from the depths of the fujibakama. Morning and evening, wrapped in the mountain mist, I weep while bowing my head. Even though I repeat the extent of my magokoro again and again, you, my lord, remain silent. Perhaps the otome who weeps in blood while suddenly releasing the arrow of love is a hototogisu.'

Adeyaka sings:

Adeyaka: 'The kototama that the seven otome gather to proclaim morning and evening is truly sad. Since I was born as a man under the heavens, I pray to the Kami of Ametsuchi to forgive one. Having heard the magokoro of the otome, I have no choice but to weep.'

The golden sand and silver sand of the Sword Pond, through the kototama of Adeyaka, changed into true gold and silver, and the hitomoto of shirosango that grew at the minasoko, having been dyed by the red hearts of the otome, increased in color little by little and transformed into red sango; the spray of the waterfall lingered on the branches of the sango, blooming and scenting with shinjiyu, agate, kogane, and shirogane, the aura of the auspiciousness of Ametsuchi filled the four sides, and the voices of peacocks, phoenixes, and kalavinka singing of great peace echoed from all around.

Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haemaese.

『伊吹山尾根に麓に咲き匂ふ 撫子今日は汀辺に匂ふ 八尋殿の欄干に立たす御姿 見れば清しも白萩に似て 生命までかけて恋せし乙女子の 優しき心を君は捨つるや よしやよし君に焦れて罷るとも われは悔まじ恨まじと思ふ 力なく淋しくふるふ撫子の 君が御目にとまらぬ悲しさ 七乙女ここに揃ひて恋語る 悲しき心を君は知らずや 世の中に情を知らぬ益良男は 鬼の化身か悪魔の化身か 乙女らのいやなき心聞し召して 怒らせ給ふな真心の声よ』

藤袴は歌ふ。

『七乙女いやつぎつぎに真心を 述ぶれど君は木耳の耳か 見るかげもなき草花の藤袴 君にまみえむことの恥かしも 花の香はあまり見えねど藤袴 底の心を汲ませ給はれ 朝夕を峯の狭霧に包まれて うなかぶしつつわれは泣くなり かくまでも真心の丈を繰り返し 繰り返せども音なしの君 うちつけに恋の征矢をば放ちつつ 血に泣く乙女はほととぎすかも』

艶男は歌ふ。

『七乙女朝な夕なに集ひ来て 宣る言霊はかなしかりけり 天の下に男の子と生れしわれなれば ひとりは許せ天地の神 乙女らの真心聞きてわれは唯 泣くより他に術なかりけり』

剣の池の金砂銀砂は、艶男の言霊によりて真誠の金銀と変化し、水底に一本生ひし白珊瑚は乙女の赤き心に染まりしか、次第々々に色を増して赤珊瑚と変じ、滝のしぶきは珊瑚の梢にとどまりて、真珠、瑪瑙、黄金、白銀と咲き匂ひ、天地瑞祥の気は四辺に充満し、孔雀、鳳凰、迦陵頻伽の泰平をうたふ声四辺より響き来れる。

ああ惟神霊幸倍坐世。
