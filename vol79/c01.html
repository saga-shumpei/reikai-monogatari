Chapter 1: Lake 中の Monster [1982]

湖中の怪〔1982〕

§ 1

Since Amanominehio no Kami was born in Takaamahara of the great universe, though several thousand seisau had passed, Ten was not yet established, and Chi was still tender; among the floating kurage-like shimajima, there was one island of Tenkei, exceptionally beautiful and firmly solidified. This island is called the island of Yoshihara, and also the Kuni of Ashihara. This shimaguni possesses a kuōbau about ten times that of the kuni of Ashihara, and is an ikushima floating amidst the ocean of Made.

The kauzan standing precipitously in the center of this island is called Mount Ibuki, and the hyakuri of kosui encircling its base is called Lake Tamayako. The water of this kosui is clear, as if one could see through the shiyareki on the kotei like a mirror, and the spirit of seirei drifts forth naturally.

On Mount Ibuki, kikiyō, ominaheshi, nadeshiko, hagi, yamabuki, tsubaki, and other flower trees grow profusely, blooming in brilliant competition without distinction of spring, summer, autumn, or winter; their sweet fragrance perfumes the breeze, as though manifesting a heavenly paradise on earth. A multitude of tribes called dragon gods (ryūjin) appear and disappear on the surface of the kosui centered around this mountain, or climb the mountain and enjoy a life of ease. Nevertheless, since the ryūjin tribe all possessed human faces and dragon bodies, and had not yet attained the form of human beings, the king of the ryūjin tribe was anxious day and night, desiring by all means to attain a human body like the Kunitsukami.

Corresponding to the upper reaches of this kosui, there was a large bun-shaped hill called Mount Mizukami, and the Kunitsukami were spending a life of ease centered around this hill. The chieftain of this village was called the ancestor of the Kunitsukami, his name was designated as Yamahiko, and his wife's name was praised as Kawahime.

Between the husband and wife Yamahiko and Kawahime, there were two deities, brother and sister, whose countenances were preeminently manly yet gentle. The elder brother was called Adeyaka, and the younger sister was called Uraraka. The two siblings cherished and became close to each other, following one another like shadow to body, and no matter which land they reached, they were never separated.

There was not a single cloud in the vast sky, the moon rose brightly from the grass of the eastern field, the stars caused the light of gold and silver to sparkle across the entire sky, presenting a view impossible to describe in words.

Uraraka yearned for the scenery of this great nature, and going down the banks of the Mizukami River alone, forgetting herself—the moonlight reflecting on the clear water surface, the twinkling of the stars, and the state of the river water flowing while glittering in silver color—yearning at how lovely and auspicious it was, she stood by the riverbank under the moon and sang:

'Ah, how serene and clear! Even the Amanomiharato has opened wide, And the clouds and mists of the midair have no shadows. When I look at the eastern sky, the moon of the fourteenth night Has risen, illuminating the grasses and trees of the kuni of Yoshihara; When I look at the dew on the shining fields, It is precisely like the light of the stars. When I gaze at the Mizukami River, The water is thoroughly clear, clear to its depth, Flowing peacefully in the moonlight, Like glittering sand, the light of the stars. Ah, is it heaven? Is it paradise? For me alone to behold this spectacle Is a pity; O my father, my father, Come hither too! O my mother, my mother, Come hither too, and behold this view To your heart's content. And my elder brother, Adeyaka, Do come following after my footsteps! Ah, this clear water of the river, Is it the radiance of the life of existence? O sweet flag fragrant at the water's edge, Illuminated by the moonlight, Your deep purple, gentle figure Is so brilliant—just like the adornment Of my elder brother, my lord! When I gaze at my feet, There is the gentle, pure nadeshiko, The ominaheshi shining in yellow, And the cactus flowers blooming brightly red, Fragrant by the riverbank where I stand. Ah, my elder brother, Adeyaka!'

Just as she was thus singing, illuminating the riverbed as if it were broad daylight, a man appeared with his head and upper body thrust above the water surface, and sang. Upon looking closely, it was none other than the person in Uraraka's song, her elder brother Adeyaka.

Uraraka, with excessive wonder and joy, was about to leap into the river with her garment's hem tucked up, but the moment she put down one foot, startled by the coldness that felt as if it would cut her shins, she dashed back up to the original riverbank and stood gazing blankly at the man's face. This was in truth not Uraraka's brother, but the king of the ryūjin tribe lurking at the bottom of this kosui.

天之峯火夫の神、大宇宙の高天原に生れましてより、幾千年の星霜を経たれども、天未だ備はらず、地又稚くして、水母なす漂へる島々の中にも、別けて美しく地固まりし天恵の島あり。この島を葭の島と言ひ又葭原の国土とも言ふ。此島国は葦原の国土に比して約十倍の広袤を有し、万里の海の中に漂ふ生島なり。

この島の中央に屹立せる高山を伊吹の山と称し、その麓をめぐる幾百里の湖水を玉耶湖と言ふ。この湖水は水清くして湖底の砂礫鏡を透して見る如く、清麗の気自ら漂ふ。

伊吹の山には、桔梗、女郎花、撫子、萩、山吹、椿などの花樹繁茂し、春夏秋冬の分ちなく艶を競ひて咲き乱れ、芳香風に薫じ、さながら地上の天国を現出せるものの如し。数多の竜神と称する種族、この山を中心として湖面に出没し、或は山に登りて安逸なる生活を楽しみつつありき。さりながら、竜神族はいづれも人面竜身にして、未だ人間としての形体そなはらざりければ竜神族の王は、如何にもして国津神のごとく人体をそなへたきものと、日夜焦慮しつつありける。

この湖水の上流に当りて、水上山といふ饅頭形の大丘陵ありて、国津神はこの丘陵を中心に安逸なる生活を送りつつありき。この里の酋長を国津神の祖と称し、その名を山神彦と言ひ、妻の名を川神姫と称へられける。

山神彦、川神姫の夫婦が中に、眉目形すぐれて雄々しくやさしき、男女二柱の御子ありき。兄をあでやか（艶男）と言ひ、妹をうららか（麗子）と言ふ。二人の兄妹は互に睦び親しみて、影の身体に従ふが如く、いづれの土地に到るも離れたることなかりける。

大空に一点の雲もなく、月は皎々として東の野辺の草より昇らせ給ひ、星は黄金白銀の光りを御空一面にまたたかせ、えも言はれぬ眺めなりける。

麗子はこの大自然の風光に憧れ、只一人吾を忘れて水上川の岸辺を下り行けば、清しき水面にうつる月の光り、星のまたたき、川水を銀色に輝かせつつ流るる状態のいとも床しくいとも目出度きに憧れながら、月下の川辺に立ちて歌ふ。

『あなさやけおけ 天之岩戸も開け放れ 御空の雲霧かげもなし 東の御空を見れば十四夜の 月は昇れり葭原の 国土の草木をいてらして 輝く野辺の露見れば さながら星の光かも 水上川を眺むれば 水は澄みきり澄みきらひ 静に流るる月の光 真砂にまがふ星の光 ああ天国か楽園か この光景を吾一人 見るは惜しけれ父のみの 父も来ませよ母そはの 母も来ましてこの眺め 心ゆくまでみそなはせ 兄の君なる艶男も 吾後追ひて来りませ

ああこの清水川水よ 生きの生命の輝きか 汀に香るあやめ草 月の光に照らされて 濃き紫のやさ姿 あざやかなるかも吾兄の 君の粧ひそのままよ 吾足下を眺むれば やさしき清き撫子や 黄色に照らふ女郎花 仙人掌の花あかあかと 吾立つ川辺に香るなり ああ兄の君よ艶男よ』

斯く歌ふ折しも、川底を真昼の如く輝かしながら、ぬつと首から上を水面に現し、歌ふ男がある。よくよく見れば麗子が歌中の人物、艶男の兄であつた。

麗子は余りの不思議さと嬉しさとに、川中に裳裾からげて飛び入らむとしたが、片足をさし入れた刹那、脛もきれるばかりの冷たさに驚き、元の岸辺に馳せ上り、茫然として男の顔を眺めゐたりき。これはその実麗子が兄ではなく、此湖底に潜む竜神族の王であつた。

§ 2

The king of the dragons was pondering how to capture a Kunitsukami, wed with him, escape his human-faced dragon body, and bear a child just like a Kunitsukami. However, since the dragon body in the water was wrapped with bottom algae, it appeared to Uraraka's eyes just as though it were wearing blue Kimono.

Believing him to be the charming man whom she yearned for and loved day and night, Uraraka strains her voice with joy and fondness and sings:

Aha, aha! The Misora is pure, the moon is clear, The starlight is bright. Such an awe-inspiring Amazora, Reflecting and floating just as it is, How pure is the lake of Tamaya! In the very Mannaka of that purity, Mount Ibuki towers high; The mist of Ibuki has cleared away already, Ten is vivid, and the earth is serene. The elder brother whom I yearn for and adore, By what time did he come to play In this lake, I wonder—how mysterious! However pure and clear it may be, At the Minasoko of this lake, To such a coldness that makes me think Even my feet might be cut off, I am trembling and shuddering. How is it, and until when will thou Immerse thyself in this water, I wonder.

Ascend quickly onto the land! However pure the Minomo may be, Compared to the view of the land where flowers bloom in full, How could it ever match this serene earth? Ascend quickly onto the dry land, My beloved lord, my cherished lord, come quickly! A loving Otome is here, A loving Otome is here. Ascend quickly, my elder brother!

Singing with all her might, she urges the charming man to land. The man in the water answers this:

My dear Uraraka, my lord, my younger sister, You and I, eternally, In this land of Yoshihara, Prolonging our lives like the thread of jewels, For a hundred years, a thousand years, eight thousand years, Living and prospering endlessly, Becoming the master of Mount Ibuki, With the sincere heart of wishing to build Heaven upon this earth, Preceding you, right here, I have come, oh! This water Changes according to one's heart! If you possess a cold heart, This river water will grow cold; If you possess a warm heart, This lake water will grow warm. Neither too warm nor too cold, Pleasantly now, I shall welcome you To the dragon's capital; Cheer your heart in the water,

And leap in, my dear Uraraka!

Singing thus, he urges Uraraka to enter the water. Uraraka's heart dances at the invitation of her beloved elder brother expressed in full words, and she thought of leaping into the water at once, but wait a moment—there is something that does not sit well with her. That her elder brother, who always detests being in the water, should immerse his whole body in such cold water, and raising only his face, speak to her with such ease, cannot be an ordinary matter. Thinking he might perhaps be the incarnation of a dragon god, she hangs her head in deep thought. The man in the water, with the exact voice of the charming man, says:

Aha, aha! The Misora is clear and the moon is bright; The lake water of Tamaya, down to its depths, Growing thoroughly clear, Reflects the moon of the great sky, And sets countless stars flowing! In thine eyes, reflecting the solemnity Of the heavenly august land, The water of the pure Tamaya Lake Is gentle and fragrant. What is it that thou art hesitating for? The pledge of husband and wife, right here, Shall I bind with thee, O stern spirit, With the mediation of the auspicious spirit, A pure and undefiled marital bond— Come hither now, pray!

Uraraka stands on the shore and sings:

Though I deem my elder brother's command to be good, I am one who lacks the permission Of my father left behind at home; The mind of my mother is also yet unknown. How shall we of today Perform the way of marriage? Even if you say how beloved a lord you are, Without the permission of father and mother— Ay, even if there were such permission, In this wide land of Yoshihara, Should I wed upon the narrow water's surface? Come what may, of our doting parents Returning home, we must decide. Pray, ascend upon the dry land!

Thus, both of them—the brother and sister—are calling out to each other from both water and land: leap into the water, no, land upon the shore.

Just as this was happening, the sky suddenly clouded over, masses of darkness fell on all sides, vision became dim within a foot, the waves raged wildly, and the heart of the gentle maiden was almost driven to madness. Just as a mass of fire appeared from the midst of the darkness, the man who had seemed a charming man transformed into a human-faced dragon body, snatched up the maiden's body, held her high above the water, and fled away toward the direction of Mount Ibuki floating in the midst of the lake.

The maiden was captured by the incarnation of the dragon god, And her whereabouts have become completely unknown.

斯く互に、水中に飛び込めよ、上陸せよ、と兄妹が水陸両方面からかけ合つてゐる。

斯かる所へ一天にはかにかき曇り、闇の塊は四辺に落下し、咫尺暗澹、波狂ひ立ちて優しき乙女の心は、狂はむばかりなりにける。忽ち暗中より一塊の火光現るよと見る間に、艶男と見えし男は人面竜身と変じ、乙女の体をひつ抱へ、水上高く捧げながら湖中に浮ぶ伊吹山の方面さして逃げ去りにける。
