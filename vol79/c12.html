Chapter 12: 樹下の Dream [1993]

樹下の夢〔1993〕

§ 1

Adeyaka, wishing to soothe his tsurezure, stepped out of the Misono and, while strolling here and there through the shadows of the forest where the Yutsukatsuragi grew thick along the nihazuta, recalled the sorrowful voices of the Nanaotome and sang secretly.

‘Does God say to lament, even while my heart dances in a love that cannot be fulfilled? The Nanaotome, with all their might, have revealed the depths of their murakimo hearts. Where has my heart gone, while listening to the sad Kotoba of the Otomego? Now, I have come to feel sorrow, thinking of the red Magokoro of the Otome. Even the Kotori chirping on the branches of the Katsuragi tell of their own loves. When I listen with a calmed heart to the sound of insects, they all seem to be voices of affection. In this world, that which is called love is a demon that steals the Inochi of the thread of jewels. Being loved by the seven Otome, I feel such sadness and pain, and I have no way to return it. I am a lonely soul wandering in the forest of Katsuragi, harboring a heart that knows no way out. As I ponder, lost in thought at twilight, the voice of an owl sounds as if it is laughing.

The sound of the wind blowing over the Shimane also echoes in my ears, and I feel lonely thinking it is sorrowful. Suffering thus morning and evening, what kind of heart is this joyful self of mine? Unable to resolve my own Kokorone, I go back and forth along the Yamaji of love. How many times have I thought that if there were no one here, I would weep until my heart was satisfied? When I see the Otome, I feel love and sorrow; when I see the Misono, how pure it is, this island of the Tatsugami. I wander the forest of Shigeki all alone, wetting my sleeves with longing for the Otome. Thanks to the Mimegu of Ōtatsumihiko Mikoto, I sleep alone in the Yasudo. In the middle of the night, the Nanaotome appear in my dreams, one after another, telling of their sorrowful affairs. To think of returning to Mizukamiyama is futile for me today. Reiko has become a king, and I am left alone, only lamenting the coldness of the night.

Though the Nanaotome are beautiful, I think they cannot compare to the flower-like figure of Reiko. Because they are not of human form, even the lovely Otome of this island made me hesitate. Born as a child of the Kunitsukami, I feel it is a pity to think that I am meeting with the Otome of the Tatsugami. Though they are beautiful Otome, it is lonely that there is something of the scent of the shore about them. I think to myself that I shall pass the time here, gazing at them as flowers to be viewed, but the Otome do not seem to consent. How mysterious, ah, how mysterious; those who live on this island have human faces but the bodies of dragons. Their scales appear all over like hakama, yet from the shoulders up, they are of human form.

While slightly waving their left and right hands, and wearing long hakama, they go back and forth in all directions. Whenever I see this state of affairs, I, who possess both hands and feet, feel even more terrified and disgusted; even their way of speaking is strange. Far removed from the human world, the Otomego who have grown up eternally on the Shimane of the dragon—their pure and gentle divine forms. When they approach, the scent of seaweed and the scent of scales irritate my nose, and it is truly unbearable. I have sworn to open up the land, living for a thousand or eight thousand generations on the Shimane of the Ryūgū. How sorrowful are these words of Kototama. Now, I only have the heart to return to my hometown.

Gathering morning and evening, I have become filled with sorrow. Ah, what shall I do? When will the resentment of a thousand autumns be cleared? Divine spirits of rigor and divine spirits of purity, hear my prayer. Save me, even if only for a moment, as soon as possible. Surrounded by these rare Otome on this island floating upon the waves, have pity on the suffering of one who cannot move, O divine spirit of rigor. Before the presence of the divine spirit of purity, I offer my Inochi and pray.

Save my soul, which is sinking in endless suffering, and return me to the original land of the gods. I shall wait for a good day, entrusting myself to the hearts of the divine spirit of rigor and the divine spirit of purity. Though there are beautiful Otome, their bodies are so ugly and foul-smelling that I cannot bear them. If I were to embrace them and touch their skin, I would be terrified, and my soul would tremble. Though they are beautiful flowers, they are Otome like thistles with thorns by the roadside. How shall I escape from this Shimane? The suffering of worrying morning and evening. If I were not here, Reiko-hime would surely be lamenting all alone on this Shimane. Thinking of the sad plight of Reiko, I remain on this Shimane, though I am alone.’

§ 2

At this moment, Kakitsubata, who was known as the most Imuka Kami among the Nanatariotome, appeared with stealthy steps, and while supporting her body against the Yutsukatsuragi, she spoke:

Kakitsubata: 'Seeking the whereabouts of you, Adeyaka-no-Kimi, I have come to the forest of Katsuragi. Why do you wander in the shade of the trees? My heart is restless and I am filled with sorrow. Did you flee, O heartless one, angered by the red Kotoba I declared? Since you are the one to whom I have pledged my Inochi, the thread of jewels, I must search for you even if I must part the grasses. Searching even to the corners of this forest, I have at last met you here. As my heart burns with love, the Tsukihi of the Ōzora seem to have grown dark. Is this forest, wrapped in low-hanging clouds and Ogura, a reflection of my heart that longs for you? Even if you should dive to the Misoko, I shall pursue you with my Inochi at stake. If it must be so, there is no help for it now; let my thoughts be released, and pray, rest for Ichiya.'

Adeyaka was startled, but with an indifferent face, he replied:

Adeyaka: 'I knew not that you were here, O Itokoya-no-Kimi. Under the shade of this Katsuragi forest, you have searched well and come to me. Seeing you, whom I love, I feel as if I am revived. Holding an endless longing, I was wandering in the shade of the trees, unaware of all else.'

Kakitsubata sings:

Kakitsubata: 'Though I think you are one who speaks Soragoto, I am filled with joy when I see your Onomo. Even if you were to seclude yourself in the Ibuki mountains of the Dragon Island, I am not one who would think of sleeping. The boat that travels upon the Sazanami of the Wada plains relies on the power of the wind. When I stand in the shade of the Katsuragi, longing for you, even the tips of the Obana do not beckon to me. If this is a world where even a false name is cast upon me, O Adeyaka-no-Kimi, do not fear, but forgive my love. Do you not draw from the heart of the Kagamiko, where the Sazanami rise quietly? While keeping my distance, I have followed only your footsteps, and I have come here as my burning heart dictates. Praying to the Kami of Tenchi, I have met you so that the color of my love might not fade. To fulfill one's desires is truly the love of the heart of the Kami of Tenchi. For Ikuyo, while listening to the sound of the Takitsuse, I could not sleep, wondering how you were. If I were to harbor a treacherous heart, leaving you behind, the depths of the Kagamiko would surely dry up. Though you claim to be a body of illness, your body and your spirit shine like a mirror. How hateful are the words that claim you are ill, while possessing a body without clouding. Do you not have compassion for the maiden who has offered even her Inochi to you? Are you not thinking of returning to your own land, while you now tread upon the distant waves? Even if you must leave this island root, you must surely take me with you. Though I possess the lowly body of a Ryujin, it is perhaps shameful that I love you. I cast aside my shameful thoughts and follow you because of the intensity of my love and my sorrow. The forest where the Yutsukatsuragi grows thick is hidden from human eyes. Pray, rest upon the bed of grass. If you are one who stands upon a journey with a grass pillow, you should not despise a pillow of dew. Let us play in the heavenly realm, using the roots of the trees in this forest as our pillow and the soft grasses as our bed.'

Adeyaka, conquered by the intense romantic passion of Kakitsubata and her forceful demeanor, finally came to tie the dream of a grass pillow. From this time on, Kakitsubata, fearing not the eyes of the other seven maidens, openly rose and slept in the palace of Adeyaka morning and evening, serving him with all her heart and body, as diligently as she could, to purchase the favor of her husband. Adeyaka, standing out in the morning garden and rinsing his face in the pond of swords, recalled the events of yesterday and sang a song of reflection:

Adeyaka: 'Ah, how shameful, how heartless. My spirit, as a man of Yamato, which had hardened like a rock of a thousand pulls, has been shattered, and I have left myself to the mercy of the growing soft grasses. The strong spirit of a woman is a mulberry bow that pierces even the rock. Kakitsubata, who pierces through until her intense heart reaches its mark. Before the Imuka of the Hime-no-Mikoto, I have been broken so easily. May the Kami of Tenchi forgive me. Though they are called Ryujin maidens, I have married an island maiden who wanders in the form of a beast; the sin of my own body is terrifying. Ah, it is of no use, it is of no use. Though I blame myself, wondering if my heart is truly this weak, I am already the target of a broken bow.'

The way to pierce is upon the waves, in this world of men sinking and floating, shall I cast it away here, how wretched. Thinking of this, Urarakahime-sama must surely be suffering as well. Though called the King of the country, having a husband who is half-beast, how must she be passing this sorrowful world. I am a child of God and a child of man, the shame of laying my pillow beside an Otomego who is close to a beast. Have mercy, Izumitama, before the presence of Mizunomitama, I speak with a heart full of shame. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. May there be power in my Kototama. May there be light in my Kototama.

By the blessing of the Ikukototama that I shall proclaim, make this Otome a perfect human. The elegant figure of the Otome who lies beside my skin, become a child of God, become a child of man. Kakitsubata, my wife who blooms in purple, manifest as a true human being.

One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten. Momochiyorozu of Kami, have mercy, save us.

Thus, through the Kototama that Adeyaka proclaimed without Kandan for Nanukananayo, a Fushigi occurred; the entire form of Kakitsubata instantly transformed into a Jinshin, and with no shadow of her Araara Tachihada remaining, her Zenshin became as soft as mochi, her skin fine, and she was reborn completely as a Jinshin.

Adeyaka sings:

How grateful I am, by the blessing of the Ikukototama, the Otome has become of skin like a jewel. Like the white folds of Takuzunu or Ahayuki, her skin has become youthful.

Kakitsubata sings:

Purified by the dew of my Lord's grace, my Tachihada has surely vanished. If it be so, there is no longer anything to be ashamed of. Perhaps I shall serve my Lord and bear a Miko. How grateful I am, my defiled Karatama has become human through the grace of God.
