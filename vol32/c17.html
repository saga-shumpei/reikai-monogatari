悔悟の歌〔908〕

§ 1

Matsuwakahiko opened his silver fan, and singing and dancing himself, expressed his congratulations. His song:

'The Kamutsukasa of the capital of Chin, / Momogamihiko who reigns in glory, / My father who performed the rituals / Of the divine Mikoto, / And offered the Anana, / Following in his footsteps, / While I was guarding the Kamuyakata, / Purifying my heart and cleansing my body, / The Suekohime descended from Ama, / Parting the Yahekumo of Ama, / Together with the Suteko Hime, / Came to this Seichi, / And opened the Oshihe of Kami / To the Kunibito far and near, / Bestowing the dew of grace / Without omission, how noble it is. / Matsuwakahiko, the noble Miko / Of the divine Susanō Ōkami, / While serving the Sueko Hime / Morning and evening, / And attending to the Ananahi Oshihe,

The Kamutsukasa Kotoyoriwake, / Descended from Takamanohara / To the Chiushinchi of Onokorojima, / The Oshihe of Kami is flourishing more and more, / Like the flowers of the trees / Blooming all at once. / Such an example has never been heard of / Even in dreams since ancient times, / The light of this auspicious sign / Is pure and vies with the sun and moon / In its divine majesty, / And the pure figure of the divine Susanō Ōkami / Who has descended again, / How joyful it is to worship Him with awe. / Matsuwakahiko, not to mention / The hundred officials, / And the Kunibito of the four quarters, / Rejoicing and yearning for His divine virtue, / While celebrating the age of Kami / Of drumming bellies and digging fields,

Welcoming the party of Kotoyoriwake / The Zui no Mikoto, / Who has returned after converting / The crooked spirits of the Amazon River / To the Oshihe of Kami, / It is as if flowers bloom on a withered tree, / Or as if purple flowers / Bloom on roasted beans. / This joy of a thousand generations, this pleasure of eternal spring, / As long as Takasagojima exists, / I shall never forget. / Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara, / Blessed by the divine water and fire of Kami, / The divine nation of Argentina / Guarded by the Sueko Hime, / Is free from the sorrows of the three great disasters, / And the evils of the three small disasters, / And forever in the age of pine, / By the divine grace of the Miroku Kami,

May You be pleased, Kannagara. / Before the presence of Kami, for a thousand years, / Matsuwakahiko, with reverence, / Rebuilds the depths of his heart, / Praying with all his might, / Praying with single-minded devotion. / Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara, / Grant us the blessings of Your divine spirit.'

Having finished singing, he bowed to everyone in a flurry, and hid his figure in the inner sanctuary once again.

Takayohime opened her silver fan, and singing and dancing herself, expressed her congratulations. Her song:

'In the Toyoashihara no Nakatsukuni, / In the land of Mesopotamia, / At the head temple of the Brahman religion, / Enshrining Ōkunihiko, / Following Onigumohiko / Who founded the Brahman religion, / While spreading the Oshihe to the four quarters, / We crossed over to Onokorojima, / And established the Alps religion again / In the rocky cave of Mount Takaharu, / Calling together the Teristan and the Karinsu, / And the hundred officials, / While reverently enshrining / The purple jewel,

At the time when the Kamutsukasa of the Ananahi Oshihe, / Takahime and Kurohime, / Came out, / Making the Ama no Torifune roar / High in the sky, / Taking that moment as a rare opportunity, / We devised a plan and, / While they were shut inside the rocky cave,

Tamawake and Mokusuke, / With Kuniyoriwake and Ryukokuwake / At the head, came out, / And by the Kototama of the young and innocent / Hatsuwakahime, / They were severely struck, / Abandoned the Alps religion, / And submitted to the Oshihe of Kami of Ananahi. / The joy of realizing / That Ryukokuwake was my own child! / Sent to the Seichi of Aya, / Serving with reverence / In the Nishiki no Miya morning and evening, / But Kurohime, who was entrusted / With the safekeeping of the golden jewel, / Unexpectedly lost the jewel, / And in the midst of the confusion, / Takahime Kamutsukasa appeared, / And gave an unexpected and difficult challenge.

Starting with Kurohime-sama, / Takayohime, accompanied by / The Oshihe-tsukasa of Ryukokuwake, / Left the noble Seichi, / The five spirits of Teristan and Karinsu / Determined to search / For the whereabouts of the golden jewel / To the very end, / Crossing the vast ocean, / Wandering and searching / Even to the ends of Ryugujima, Tokoyonokuni, / And Takasagojima, but / With no way to find it, / Appearing at the waterfall of Arina, / Calling together various / Large and small countless treasures / From the countries of the four quarters / By means of strategy, / While waiting for the time, / The noble jewel of gold / Was settled from the village of Tena,

Having landed at the port of Zemu, crossing over Tenshiyōzan, and ascending the river currents of the Amazon and the port of Chin by boat, they arrived safely at the sacred ground of Chin, the capital of the Rabbit King, located on the south side of the forest of Shigure, where they paused their journey at the holy land surrounded by clear lake waters dedicated to the Great Ōkami of the Moon.

While they were there, they brought the many fierce beasts to submission and harmony through the Kototama of the divine Miiki, and having become the administrators of the Amazon rabbits, they were in the midst of bestowing the dew of grace upon all without exception, when Takahime, who had come searching by crossing the Amazon River, celebrated a miraculous reunion together with her party of eight.

From beyond Bōshigatake, infinite Reikuō was emitted, illuminating the spirits. From this moment, the group stood up with courage, went out to the Amazon River, conquered the Magami of ugliness, and brought them to submission through the grace of Kami.

The party stood up in joy and courage, and the twelve children of Kami, while searching for the miraculous Reikuō, climbed up Bōshigatake, and together with Kotoyoriwake-no-Mizumitama and Kuniyoriwake, they celebrated their safe triumphant return, chanting the Futo-norito to the Kami of Heaven and Earth.

Having finished the chanting, the party crossed mountains and fields, climbed slopes, traversed valleys of clear streams, and arrived safely at the capital of Uzu in Argentina, where deep grace abounds.

Oh, the joy of the triumphant return! When they arrived at the mansion of Uzu, they found the noble state of Lord Susanoo-Ōkami, the noble children of the eight maidens, the youngest princess born to them, and Matsuwakahiko, all together, widely spreading the noble divine teachings to the people of the world.

The clouds hanging over their hearts cleared away as if wiped clean, and the sun and moon of the absolute truth shone in the heavens, and today, in the happiness of the moment, the group, having realized the sincerity of the three-five, prostrated themselves before the Imperial Great Ōkami.

Ah, Kannagara, may the spirits be blessed, and may the great work of the pine age, which Kunitokotachi-Ōkami and Toyokunihime-Ōkami had structured, be served as the divine work of the brocade loom, and may you fulfill your pure duties as the noble children of Heaven and Earth forever, Kannagara.

Praying before the Kami, with the joy of this day at peace in their hearts, they celebrated before the Kami: 'Ah, Kannagara, may the spirits be blessed.'

Takahime: 'I am the divine administrator Takahime. In the hidden village of the northern mountains in the land of Fusa, I built a divine mansion and established the divine teachings of divination, but I doubted and suspected the heart of the Great Ōkami of the Mizumitama, and being agitated by various miscellaneous thoughts, I made the teachings of the warp of Kunitokotachi-Ōkami the master, and completely damaged and destroyed the teachings of the woof, thinking that I would bring about the five-six-seven divine age of the pine era, but that too became bubbles on the water.'

Takahime: 'In my foolishness of misunderstanding the true heart of the Mizumitama, I repented of my past mistakes and returned to the divine teachings of the three-five, and while I was serving the noble divine work by weaving the teachings of the transformed male and the teachings of the transformed female into warp and woof...'

Takahime: '...the diamond-indestructible wish-fulfilling jewel and the purple treasure gem were lost, and my heart was overturned; I searched through all the islands, and finally appeared as far as the land of Jion on the single island of Ryūgū, and though I searched, breaking my heart and body, even the shadow was not to be found, and crossing over the waves, I returned again to the island of self-delusion.'

Takahime: 'While I was searching for the whereabouts of the jewel again with deep obsession, the treasure of Tamayorihime, which appeared from Ryūgū Island, and the Mani jewel of blue, red, white, yellow, and purple, which is connected to heaven, fire, water, and earth...'

Takahime: '...caused me to raise suspicions again during the inspection of the Mani jewel, and when I followed after Kotoyoriwake and came to Takasagoshima, I suspected that the golden jewel, not to mention the wish-fulfilling jewel, the purple jewel, and the Mani jewel, were hidden in the Kakyō Palace on the shore of the Mirror Pond, and I quarreled over various miscellaneous things.'

Takahime: 'Accompanied by Tsunehiko and Haruhiko, I crossed over the mountains of Arina on the border of Teru and Uzu, and when I came to the wilderness, the golden jewel was shining, dangling on a poplar tree.'

Takahime: 'Just as I was rejoicing and leaping for joy, thinking it was a gift from heaven, the incarnation of Konohanahime, Hinodewake, appeared.'

With joy for the detailed teachings of the Dauri of Tenchi, I awoke from the dream of delusion, cast off Shifuchiyakushin, crossed the Arano and rivers, circled the Kosui, and at last arrived safely at the port of Aru.

Orikara, as I boarded a Hansen to cross the Unabara, I chanced upon a Mahito named Yobu in the Senchiu, and having formed a Shitei bond, we landed at the port of Zemu.

Crossing Tenshiyōzan, the Chin port, and the Amazon River, I wandered in search of the Arika of Takayorihime in the Kitanohara of the Shigure forest, when the fearsome Morubando attacked with great ferocity.

Barely escaping with our lives, we took refuge in the branches of a Tokihagi, and as we recited the Amatsunorito and proclaimed the Kototama of Itsunomitama, Akiyamawake, Morisu, Yasuhiko, and Munehiko approached, singing the Sendenka of Ananai-kyō with clear voices.

Just then, the Morubando were startled by the spiritual light of Ryū and Kyū that shone through the clouds from the northwest, and they fled in dismay; thus, the eight of us celebrated our safe encounter, parted the forest on the banks of the Amazon River, crossed the bridge of crocodiles, and appeared in the southern forest.

Facing the rare dwelling of Takayorihime and Ryūkokubake, the party celebrated their reunion and recited Norito before the presence of the Ōkami, established the law of Itsunomitama for the tigers, wolves, lions, bears, and many other beasts, and after ensuring they kept it strictly, we stood upon the shore once more, and with the twelve of us aiming for the spiritual light of Ryū and Kyū, we subdued and reconciled the Morubando and Erubando, those fierce Magakami, and returned with brave hearts, welcomed by the noble Mikoto of Kotoyoriwake and Kuniyoriwake, our hearts filled with gratitude and prayers as we wept tears of joy.

The eighteen divine officials of the party stood up with courage, traveled day and night toward the land of Uzu, and upon arriving at the palace and seeing the hall of eight fathoms, they beheld the nobility of the Lord God Susanoo Ōkami, the savior of the Miroku age, and his noble child Suetsuhime, along with many other divine officials, all seated like the stars in the heavenly firmament.

Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara; having received the grace of God, Takahime, whose heart was clouded, was illuminated by the sun and moon of Shinnyo, and her Mitama became as clear as a polished mirror, shining brightly.

Even if the morning sun should shine or cloud over, even if the moon should wax or wane, even if the Tenchi should overturn, as long as the sun and moon shine in the heavenly firmament, I shall never forget the deep grace of the Ōkami.

I shall recite the noble teachings of God morning and evening, repent for the sins I have committed until now, and in the presence of the noble God, I shall perform meritorious deeds forever, even unto the end of the pine-tree Miroku age.

Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara; may your divine spirit be blessed.

Takayorihime: '...' (She finishes singing and sits down with a gentle smile.)
