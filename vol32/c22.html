Chapter 22: Bridge 架 [913]

橋架〔913〕

§ 1

Kuniyoriwake, Takahime, Takayorihime, Tatsukuniwake, and other Sendenshi were each given a Kiusokushitsu, where they slept at night and rested their Kinkotsu.

Early the Yokuasa, Matsuwakahiko visited the room of Kuniyoriwake.

Matsuwakahiko: 'Kuniyoriwake-sama, Ohaya. I have come to you early and apologize for the Ojiyama, as I have a matter of Gosaudan that I must discuss with you in earnest.'

He says this with a smile that seems to hold some hidden meaning. Kuniyoriwake replies:

Kuniyoriwake: 'This is a rather formal Okotoba. What kind of Gosaudan could you possibly have for me? Since Akechi no Kotoyoriwake-dono is present, would it not be better to consult with Mikotosama?'

Matsuwakahiko: 'The truth is, following the Ome of Kamususanōnōokami-sama last night, the three of us—Kotoyoriwake-dono, myself, and the others—held a Gosaudan, and as a result, I have been chosen as the Tokumei Zenken Koushi. If by any chance this mission ends in failure, I, this Matsuwakahiko, having been granted a travel voucher for overseas, must cut my stomach.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'That sounds like a very grave mission, but please, tell me quickly. If it is within my power, I shall accept any task, for this body is dedicated to the Gods.'

Matsuwakahiko: 'In truth, when my father Kunihiko went to the front at Yomotsuhirasaka with Masakayamatsumi-no-kami-sama and Suetsuhime-sama, he was given a strict command: not only to look after the people of Uzu-no-kuni, but to take charge of this Kamudate, and when the time came, the noble child of the Mizu-no-mitama of Kamususanōnōokami-sama would descend to this land. Until then, you must protect my Mikoto and keep this land and Kamudate. My father, for better or worse, has already departed to the Yūkai, but I, remaining behind, have succeeded him and have been guarding this hall. Then, just as Masakayamatsumi-no-kami-sama had foretold, the daughter of the Mizu-no-mitama no Ōkami-sama, Suetsuhime-sama, arrived. I immediately handed the hall over to the Hime-sama, surrendered this land, and as you know, have served as the Sōshi. However, this time, her father, Kamususanōnō-no-mikoto, descended from heaven, was greatly pleased, and asked, "Suetsuhime is now of a good age, and I wish to find her a suitable husband..." When we, led by Kotoyoriwake-no-mikoto, suggested, "How would it be if you made Kuniyoriwake-sama her husband?" the Ōkami-sama was greatly pleased and said, "In truth, I have come all this way for that very reason. Please, you must arrange for Kuniyoriwake, who possesses strong divine virtue, to become the husband of Suetsuhime..." With that command, I have come to you without delay, thinking that you would have no objection... Heh heh heh, I have received the role of Zenken Koushi and have come to inquire. Since it is best to act quickly, I ask for a favorable reply soon.'

Kuniyoriwake, with a look of unexpected surprise, tilts his head, folds his hands, and while heaving a deep sigh, remains silent, with tears even dripping from both eyes.

Matsuwakahiko: 'Moshi, Kuniyoriwake-sama, why are you so troubled? It seems you are shedding tears. Is it that you cannot accept it at all?'

Kuniyoriwake: 'No, no, how could it be that I do not like it? It is just too much, and I feel unworthy; I have no words to express it. Since my youth, I have exhausted every indulgence, and I am a mass of sins, having caused the ruin of many women and the collapse of many families. Today, as a Sendenshi of the San-go-kyō, I have resolved to sever all relations with women and live a life of celibacy for the rest of my days. No matter how much it may be the will of the Ōkami-sama, I am a broken man, a remnant of a reckless life. No matter how much my soul may have been polished, it is like ink soaking into white cloth; no matter how much you wash it, it will never return to the original white fabric. In short, I am a scarred soul. For such a scarred man to become the husband of Suetsuhime-sama, who is a maiden of crystal Mitama, is something my conscience cannot allow. I have become fearful of the divine retribution. Therefore, as this is the situation, please, without taking offense, explain my true nature to the Ōkami-sama and kindly decline on my behalf.'

It was a very hard fish. Just like that, my desire for carp has now fossilized, and as I am a cold-hearted Ningen like an Ishijizau, this marriage proposal will surely never warm up. Since a creature called a carp dwells constantly in the water, its body is quite cold, you see. Hahaha!'
