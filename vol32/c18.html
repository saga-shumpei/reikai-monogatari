Chapter 18: Dragon 国別 [909]

竜国別〔909〕

§ 1

Tatsukuniwake opens his silver fan, dancing and singing of his own accord. His song:

I am Tatsukuniwaketsukasa, / Respecting and fearing the teachings / Of Takahime of the Uranai-kyō, / I set my heart's steed aright, / And as a gatekeeper of Matsuhimeyakata / At the foot of Takashiroyama, / I serve from morning till night. / While guarding the Kanado, / Seeing the two, Miko of the Ananai-kyō, / Named Horse and Deer, come to visit, / The gatekeepers rose in fury, / And though they inflicted various rough torments, / The two remained patient, / Leaving all to the heart of Kami. / Moved by their sincerity, / Ashamed of our own arrogance and rudeness, / We suddenly mimicked the dragon, / And crawled deep into the Okuden.

Shinbatsu came upon us instantly, / And all of us were transformed / Into the bodies of beasts. / To all people born in the land of Kami, / 'Purify your Kototama and your deeds, / And maintain a pure character,' / Thus the divine teaching shows us. / The clouds of delusion cleared away, / And before the eyes of Matsuhime and Osetsu, / The divine messenger, the Kamibito, / Explained the truth of heaven, earth, and nature. / Converted to the teachings of Ananai, / My old name of Tatsuwakatsukasa was changed, / And I was bestowed the name Tatsukuniwake. / Here, together with the noble missionaries / Tamajiwake and Kuniyoriwake, the masters of the teaching,

We stood to rescue Takahime and Kurohime, / Who were captured at Takaharuyama. / With the support of Mokusuke and Ohatsu, / We directed the Kamitsukasa of the Alps-kyō, / Takayorihime, to the task. / Receiving the purple jewel / And the two, Takahime and Kurohime, / I looked closely at the Kamitsukasa, Takayorihime, / And realized she was the birth mother / I had never expected to see. / Surprised and joyful, thanking the divine grace, / Parent and child, together with the people of Ananai-kyō, / Joined hands like brocade.

At the sacred site of Takamagahara, / We set the great palace pillars firmly, / And while offering our red hearts / To the divine bed established there,

As parent and child served / With all their hearts and bodies / From morning till night, / The golden jewel kept by Kurohime-sama / Disappeared somewhere, no one knew where. / Suspected by Kurohime-sama / And driven out by Takahime-sama, / The parent and child set out on a sad, wandering journey, / Searching for the whereabouts of the jewel. / To clear Takahime's doubts and purify her, / They entrusted themselves to a boat without a shelf, / Rowing over the waves of the current place, / And after much hardship and ascetic practice / Across the vast ocean of a thousand ri, / They arrived at the land of Teru on Takasagojima, / In Teristan and Karlins. / The party of four landed safely.

Though they searched for the whereabouts of the jewel, / How could they ever find it / In a vast land that knows no end, / Even if they searched for a hundred years? / The parent and child tilted their heads in thought. / As a result of a thousand considerations, / They saw a prospect above the Arina Falls, / Appearing in the Mirror Pond / That illuminates the hearts of parent and child. / At the former site of Saruyo-no-hiko, / They built a hermitage beside a rocky cave, / Worshipping Kami while keeping / Takayorihime's mother hidden deep within the cave. / Troubled and pained by the evil deeds of intrigue, / They devised a plan: / I would take the role of the Kannagi, / And my mother would become Tsukiteruhiko, / In Teristan and Karlins.

They spread the word that the two were Kami, / And proclaimed throughout Takasagojima / That if anyone offered the jewel / To the Great Kami Tsukiteruhiko, / All their wishes would be granted. / The effect was not in vain, / And oh, the absurdity of those / Who came to worship, bringing various jewels / From near and far lands! / Though in the bottom of my heart I felt / Somehow ashamed, / This was a means to search / For the whereabouts of the golden jewel. / May the Kami of heaven and earth / Witness my sincerity. / Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, / Proclaim anew with a broad heart, / Re-examine and re-listen to the good, / And pray that the golden jewel / May be retrieved as soon as possible. / Just as they prayed, the chieftain of the village of Tena / In the land of Hiru offered a golden jewel, / And while clicking their tongues at it, / They crossed over to Mount Arina under the cover of night. / While staying overnight in the dew of the wilderness, / They were admonished and converted / By the incarnation of the divine command of Konohana. / Crossing the wilderness and the sea, / Ascending the Amazon River, / They appeared at the sacred site of Aogakiyama / In the southern forest of Shigure-no-mori, / Where the rabbit clan dwelt, / And while teaching the sincerity of Ananai to the birds and beasts / And demonstrating the power of Kototama,

§ 2

From the vicinity of Mount Bōshigadake, in the Reikuō that shines forth, we all stood up with courage, and at the moment we offered our gratitude and prayers before the Mimahe of Tsukiōkami, Takahime of Ananai-kyō, leading a multitude of officials, came advancing while proclaiming the Izukototama, aided by the tribe of crocodiles across the raging, surging torrents; how joyful it is.

Here we danced for joy, and having received the Oshihe of Kami, we, the party of twelve, while establishing the Itsutei Fuhen Ritsupofu, subdued and harmonized the Kuwaijiu lurking by the riverbanks of the Amazon, bestowing upon them the light of the grace of Tengoku.

Taking the Reikuō of Ryū and Kyū as our Meate, we climbed up to Mount Bōshigadake, a great high mountain near the clouds in the Seihoku, and met with the commands of the Kami, Kotomowake and Kuniowake; while spending our time in tears of joy, we congratulated each other on our safety and rejoiced in the bright future of our path.

Here, the eighteen souls of the party traveled across mountains and fields, and after many days, finally arrived at the palace of Uzu; when we saw it, truly, the noble Suekohime and the princess Sutegohime had crossed the distant waves together, and having descended here, how noble is their governance.

The meritorious service of Matsuwakahiko, the noble child of Kunihiko-tsukasa, who toils with loyalty, shines like the sun and moon; without even the shadow of strange clouds, the people of the land rejoice and live in harmony, and without shaming the name of Takasagoshima, the name of the capital of Uzu also flourishes purely, and in the midst of this, the great leader of the faith, Kotomowake, descended from afar to this place, and the divine virtue shone ever more brilliantly.

Kotomowake-kami himself, fearing the pure and noble divine possession of Lord Susanoo-ōkami, who commanded that our party, which had been lost in the Amazon River, be saved, climbed Mount Bōshigadake; and as for our party, not to mention the Amazon River or the forests, he bestowed infinite benevolence upon the Magatsu lurking there, and his divine virtue shone ever higher, glittering at the summit of Mount Bōshigadake. At this very time, Lord Susanoo-ōkami, who has appeared as the Kami of salvation for this world, has descended from heaven to subdue the ugly spirits dwelling in Takasagoshima and to save them in peace; how noble it is. When we think and think of it, the joy of us, parent and child, who have unexpectedly met the noble Kami and worshiped his divine image, is such that even if heaven and earth were to change, we shall not forget it for a thousand, nay, eight thousand generations. Ah, Kannagara, may your divine spirit be blessed, and may you bestow upon the joy of this parent and child today, without change for thousands of generations, Kannagara; we pray before the Mimahe of Kami, we pray before the Mimahe of Kami.' So saying, he finished singing and took his seat.
