Chapter 19: 軽 Stone 車 [910]

軽石車〔910〕

§ 1

Ishikuma, who had hitherto been the Kyōshiyu of the Brahman Oshihe, built a Kuōdai mansion at the Sanroku of Takateruyama, and expanded his Seiriyoku to the Yomo, was, while performing misogi at the Inui waterfall, Mii by an Orochi, his Shintai became Kiyōchiyoku, and now at the moment of Kikiitsupatsu, he was saved by the Kototama of the party of Suekohime, and here at last he Kijiyun to the Ananahi Oshihe, crossed over the Yamatauge, and in attempting to Kotomu and pacify the Magami of the Tatsumi pond, he performed a Daishitsupai, lost his footing, and for an Ichiji lost the Mama of his Riyōashi and was in Konkiu, but was saved by the Kichi of Karl, and chasing after Karl, and yearning for the go-ato of Suekohime, he arrived and paid homage here, and led all the believers of the Brahman Oshihe to the Ananahi Oshihe, making the sacred ground of Takateruyama a branch office of Suekohime of the Uzu mansion, and had Sutegohime and Karl guard this sacred mansion, but on this occasion, when Kotoyoriwake-no-Mikoto descended to the Uzu mansion, Ishikuma invited Karl, accompanied by Haruhiko, followed the Kyōshiyu Kotoyoriwake, headed toward the Amazon River, and at last returned in triumph to the capital of Uzu with the party of eighteen, and seeing and hearing of the unexpected descent of the Lord God Susanoo-no-Ōkami, he could not contain his joy, and here he began to sing a celebratory song and dance.

The song:

'The Jizaiten of the Tokoyo-no-kuni, the Oshihe of the Brahman which the God-no-Mikoto, Ōkunihiko and Ōkuniwake, established, I have made the supreme path of salvation, and serving before the Lord God with all my heart and all my body, accumulating the labor of bathing in rain and combing in wind, and opening the Oshihe to the Yomo, I mistakenly thought of the Ananahi Oshihe as an enemy before my eyes, and hiding the Oshihe-no-miko in this mansion of the Uzu-no-kuni, I have spun various miscellaneous schemes, oh what foolishness.

On the middle of the Yamatauge, there is a large waterfall hanging high, and while purifying my Shintai in the water coming down from the Inui pond, I prayed for the granting of the divine power of Ten-gan-tsū, when my Shintai became Kiyōchiyoku, and in the mystery of being unable to move my body, I looked up and gazed at the waterfall, and a truly terrifying Orochi-yatsu was glaring with its eyes and opening its mouth, preparing to swallow and eat me, and in that terror my body trembled and my chest roared in distress, when suddenly I heard the propaganda song of the true path of Ananahi, and in the joy of its approaching, I regained my former heart and was thanking it privately, when the savior of the three thousand worlds, the Lord God Susanoo-no-Ōkami, with Suekohime of the eight maidens, accompanied by Sutego-hime, and with the Tsukasa Karl who served near my side at the head, advanced leisurely.

While they saved me easily from the danger of nine deaths and one life, and poured the dew-jewel of the deep grace of Kototama toward the Orochi, the Magami, which had been ferocious, shed tears and rejoiced, and having released its attachment, while being pulled by the rope of grace, it raised clouds and ascended to the heavens, rejoicing and brave. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-kamu, the preciousness of the grace of the Lord God. Following Suekohime, climbing and descending the steep slopes of the Yamatauge, and sheltering from the rain for one night in the shade of a camphor tree, I advanced with the party of Suekohime, with my heart brave to Kotomu and pacify the Orochi that was tormenting and eating the people of the world.

The Kototama that Ishikuma, lacking in divine virtue, chanted, instantly provoked the anger of the Orochi, the wind blew wildly and the waves raged, the rain came down like the spokes of a wheel, and in such helplessness, I took off my helmet and apologized for the sins of my heart before the Tsukasa of the Ananahi Lord God, when Suekohime instantly chanted the strict Kototama, blew away the clouds and fog to the Yomo, calmed the waves and stopped the rain, oh the preciousness of the divine power of Reii shown before my eyes. Instantly the Orochi was liberated, and splitting the waves of the Tatsumi pond, it revealed its form here, and while giving thanks, it raised clouds high in the sky and ascended.

The party of Suekohime finished the divine work, and looking back at the edge of the pond, they departed. How could it be, Ishikuma lost the Mama of his Riyōashi, and since he could not even walk freely, he called and stopped the Tsukasa Karl, and though he relied on that divine work of Chinkon to seek salvation, Karl, wondering what he was thinking, ridiculed him to the limit, tucked up his skirt, and ran away. In my frustration at that, I became angry, and thinking, 'Tsukasa Karl, know this, I will not leave you until I strike down my enemy,' at the moment I roared, my Riyōashi moved as I wished.'

Following in the footsteps of Karl, when I arrived at the Uzu Mansion, I found Suekohime and Sutekohime, who were overwhelmed with tears of joy at the treatment bestowed upon them by the sincere heart of Matsuwakahiko, and in gratitude for such profound grace, they immediately led the believers of the Brahman religion to the Ananai-kyō, expressing their sincerity of submission, and just as they were serving before the Kami morning and evening, the Kamuzukasa of the Ananahi-Kotoyoriwake appeared here and proclaimed the noble teachings; ah, Kamunagara, how grateful is the blessing of the Kami, and just as they were offering their sincere hearts with the feeling that there is no shame, the Mizumitama, Kamususanōnōokami, borrowing the mouth of Sutekohime, proclaimed that they should quickly head toward the support of Takahime and Takayorihime, who were facing the forests of the Amazon River.

Revering that divine decree, the Great Kyōshu of Kotoyoriwake, starting with Masazumihiko, accompanied by Karl, Ishikuma, and Harukō, left the mansion, crossed the vast wilderness of a thousand ri, and headed toward Mount Bōshi, where they were met by the Kamuzukasa of Kuniyoriwake, and through the light of the jewels of Ryū and Kyū, they subdued and pacified the ferocity of the demons of the Amazon River and the wild beasts of the forest, and how grateful it is that they were illuminated by the divine light.

The joy of Takayorihime and Takahime meeting together at Mount Bōshi with the Kamuzukasa, and following the Lord Kyōshu, returning in triumph to the Uzu Mansion with brave hearts; ah, Kamunagara, the blessing of the Kami is not shallow, and the Savior of the Mizumitama, Kamususanōnōokami, whom we yearn for morning and evening, left the mansion of the Saien and descended here in person, and how grateful it is that He has graced us with His presence to purify our defiled bodies and souls; ah, Kamunagara, having received the blessing of the Kami, I pray that the heavy sins of Ishikuma, whose heart is impure, may be forgiven.

Without forgetting the nobility of the divine grace for thousands and eight thousand generations, and transmitting it to our descendants, we shall serve firmly in Kamunagara, and before the Great Kami of the Mizumitama and the Kunitamakami, we offer our divine congratulations and prayers, bowing and bowing in reverence; ah, Kamunagara, may the spirit be blessed.

Thus, he spoke of his own repentance and history, and expressed gratitude for the descent of the Great Kami of the Mizumitama, and returned to his seat.

Karl stood up again, clapped his hands, shook his hips, and sang while dancing amusingly.

The sin is also Karl's Kamuzukasa, the mouth is also Karl's Kamuzukasa, the hands and feet are also Karl's Kamuzukasa, and as all the Karl-girls recite the noble songs, dancing and performing, listing tens of millions of words of history, and finally hearing that the Savior, Kamususanōnōokami, descended to the Uzu Mansion, the joy of not knowing where to place one's hands or feet, driving his specialty Kototama, he states it elegantly and nobly; ah, Kamunagara, thinking that I, Karl, must also add my tail to the horse and sing something, I stood up straight in this place and showed off my specialty hand dance.

Let us push the Kototama cart; the Kamuzukasa of the Brahman religion, the stubborn-headed Ishikuma, has built a mansion of teachings at the foot of Mount Takateru, and with a deep heart of suspicion, thinking of the Ananai-kyō as a hindrance morning and evening, he sends a spy to the Uzu Mansion, disguised as a believer, and by devising every means, he intends to seize the giant profit of a fisherman; wishing to overthrow that hateful behavior, I appeared before Matsuwakahiko and opened up the depth of my heart, and when I prayed, the Kami Matsuwakahiko tilted his head for a while, let out a sigh, and proclaimed: ah, it cannot be helped, it cannot be helped.

It is fine for Karl to do as he pleases; it is well to sneak into the Ishikuma mansion and scout, but do not become a captive; your heart is fluttering, a floating weed whose faith is not decided; blooming on this shore yesterday and blooming on the other shore today, it is not reliable; when he said this, Karl shook his head from side to side and said, I am also a man; no matter how Ishikuma uses his eloquence to try to entice me, how could I ever be moved; please do not worry at all; the craftsmanship is excellent, so please watch the finish; and so, disguised as a Brahmanist, I became a favorite of Ishikuma-sama and became an important official at his side.

The agony of being trusted. It was just at that time that the noble Miko of the god Susanō, Suekohime and Sutekohime, crossed the distant Namiji and set out for the Uzu mansion. Though it is unknown if it was by Tenganriki, Ishikuma-sama had foreknowledge and, having our group of five cross the pass of Takateruyama, lay in wait for the two women at the port of Haru. With a most imposing Gomeirei to bind them without letting them speak a word and return to Takateruyama, the Goninzu, whose hearts were not in it, were resting their breath on the grass past Tasogare. At that moment, a strange, thin Kuwaisei heard from the shade of a tree on one side made the accompanying Yatsura lose their nerve.

They fled, scattering in all directions. Karl remained alone behind. As he stood in the shade of the tree and listened, he knew for certain that this was Suekohime and Sutekohime. While serving as a guide for the Yamatauge pass, at the moment they were climbing, the sound of a waterfall was loud. Upon the command of Suekohime, when he went to look at the waterfall of Kanoto, who could have guessed that the Kami-no-tsukasa, Ishikuma, was huddled under the waterfall like a fossil, his eyes rolling and his misery showing? By the Kototama of Suekohime, the spirit of the great serpent was liberated, and having brought Ishikuma-sama into submission, they descended the steep slope of the Yamatauge pass while singing in a lively manner.

Descending to the forest of camphor trees, they spent the short summer night and arrived at the edge of the Tatsumi pond. Together with the people who came to meet them, the group of seven stood face to face. By the Kototama of Ishikuma-sama, the serpent god rose in anger, and as the situation became unstable, Suekohime solemnly proclaimed the noble Kototama, and while pacifying the serpent through Kototama, she had it saved by Heaven. Ishikuma-sama lost his footing and cried out, 'Aitattatuta aitattata! What has happened to my legs? They will not listen to me at all. Please help me!'

He was a man who said strange things. Even if his ears were far away, he should have been able to hear, but his legs were another matter. 'Are they a substitute for your ears? Stand up quickly, stand up!' though he pushed the Kototama-guruma, Ishikuma, who had become a cripple, was in a pitiful state where he could not move his body. Thinking he must heal him, when he listed various things with a sense of exasperation, Ishikuma raised his eyes in anger and said, 'You fool, Karl! Do not make a fool of me. I will make you realize it soon,' and forgetting the pain in his legs, he made the earth resound with a thudding sound and followed after Karl.

Ishikuma, whose legs were now Karl's, understood Karl's sincerity from the bottom of his heart for the first time. Shedding tears of joy, he offered his thanks. 'Kore kore moushi, Ishikuma-sama. Please do not offer thanks. It is not that I am the one who healed you; it must be the noble blessing of the Kami and the fact that your own heart was uplifted that allowed your legs to stand. Rather than thanking me, it would be better to offer a Futonorito of gratitude and prayer to the Kami of San-Ikkyō.' When he taught him this briefly, Ishikuma-sama clapped his hands and said, 'Karl-sama, just as you say, I am truly impressed.' The face with which he smiled broadly still flickers before my eyes even now, and I can never forget it.

Karl and Ishikuma, both of them, while increasingly aligning their hearts, served well as the overseers of the teachings of Suekohime and Matsuwakahiko. While they were striving for sincerity, the great religious leader Kotoyoriwake suddenly descended here. He proclaimed the noble teachings of the Kami morning and evening, and as if the heavenly rock cave had been opened, their hearts were encouraged. 'With this, the night has finally dawned. There are no clouds hanging over my heart.' Rejoicing with 'Happy, happy,' there were times when they served with diligence.

There were also times when they rejoiced with courage, hearing that the true Kami Susanō-Ōkami, who possesses a physical body, was appearing here. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara. Even we lowly ones, moistened by the dew of the blessings of Heaven and Earth, are serving close to the Savior of the Mizu-no-mitama, Susanō-Ōkami. Since the beginning of the opening of Heaven and Earth, there is no joy greater than this. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara. May the happy dream never awaken, Uzu mansion. May the scent of the tree flowers that bloom all at once be auspicious, and may they never scatter forever, Kannagara. Offering prayers before the Kami,

Before the Kunitsu-kami, I respectfully and reverently pray. I respectfully and reverently pray.' So he finished singing, and made the solemn banquet burst into laughter.
