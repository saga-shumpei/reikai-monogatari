Chapter 5: 琉球の Light [896]

琉球の光〔896〕

§ 1

Kārinsu heard the song of the three Sendenshi, and thinking that he too must sing and show his power to the Ichizoku of rabbits and crocodiles, he stood up at once, and began to sing while dancing with strange gestures.

'This place is the famous land of Haru, standing along the Amazon River, which people call the Shigure forest of the Daishinrin. Morubando and Erubando, and a hundred other beasts, have made this their Chiyo Sumika, a Kenjiyōtetsupeki fortress. Without fearing the Tenjitsu, without hesitating before the earth, and without fearing the light of the moon, in this Jiiujizai, Hōkaudogau, and demonic cavernous plain, where Iushiyōretsupai and Jiyakunikukiyōshiyoku Shiyura prevail, this forest was given to your Ichizoku as a secret land of the Sekai since Sūhiyakumannen ago. The dream that the master of the forest was the king of rabbits has vanished before one knew it, and now it has ended up as the Sumika of Akuma.'

'In the Utsushiyo where things change, the fate of the rabbits is no exception to the rule, and the Seikuwatsu of the crocodile Ichizoku is lonely. Choosing a point of ten Jifuri in all Yomo within the Kuōbōsenri forest, the sacred ground where they lived, relying on it as an Eugai fortress, has now become dangerous. The Yamata no Orochi, the ugly foxes, and the descendants of the spirits of the crooked demons, lurking in the deep river rapids where the violent waves and torrents rage, are targeting to exterminate your entire Ichizoku, making them the prey of the thread of life. Morubando and Erubando, amidst such enemies, with tigers, wolves, lions, bears, giant snakes, and flocks of monkeys, are aiming at your Ichizoku.'

'There are times when they keep a watchful eye, but the fact that the Sendenshi of the three-five gods protected by Kuninotokotachi no Ōkami, starting with Takayorihime, and the Sendenshi of Ryūkokubetsu, Tērisutan and Kārinsu, the four noble children of the gods, have appeared as gods of salvation and descended to this place, must be like flowers blooming on a withered tree, or like receiving rain in a great drought. Rejoice and be glad, rabbits and crocodiles. We shall from here on, with the divine water and fire, pacify the forest of the Amazon River in the land of Haru, and proceed further to descend to the land of Chin, the land of Teru of the morning sun, the land of Hiru, and the land of Karu, to pacify the Ichizoku of Yamata no Orochi, and to spread the awe-inspiring and pure government of the age of the gods.'

'Even if the Akuma of Amazon lurk deep in the riverbed, and even if the flock of fierce beasts attacks your group, as long as we have come here, believe firmly in the divine power that will not let you be shaken, and morning and evening, rejoice and be brave before the gods. The presence of the Moon God, who has appeared as the Mizu no Mitama, goes without saying, and you must worship and revere the princess of the dragon world who appears as the god of the Kunitama. I am a child of God, and you are also the same child of God, so what separation should there be? God bestows equal love upon all things in the universe.'

'Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara. Appearing in the capital of the rabbits, in the sacred ground where the flock of crocodiles with firm hearts gather and protect, the reliability of building the foundation of the land of God of the San-go-kyō, limitlessly firm and eternal. Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara. Rejoicing in the blessings of God, you flocks of rabbits and crocodiles, rejoice and be brave before the gods. Until the echo of the forest resounds, sing and pray for the grace of God. Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara. May the Mitama be blessed.'

From this time on, Takayorihime and the other three incited many rabbits, became the kings of the rabbit capital, and spent almost one year in this separate world happily and interestingly.

One night, just as the moon was casting its bright light upon the surface of the lake, from the hills in Yomo, the voices of Hōkaudogau and shouting came all at once, 'Woo, woo,' and it sounded as if it would tear the ears apart. The king of the rabbits was surprised and ran before Takayorihime,

Rabbit King: 'I report to Takayorihime-sama. Just now, surrounding the mountains in Yomo, they have gathered many Ichizoku of tigers, wolves, lions, giant snakes, and bear kings, and like clouds and mist, they have occupied this sacred ground and are attacking fiercely to capture our subordinates. The head of the crocodiles has gathered many kenzoku and is fighting with all his might, but no matter what, it is a great army that is unbearable to look at, and since it is impossible to repel them easily, I beg you, on behalf of the Ichizoku, to pacify the approaching demonic army with your divine power.'

原文（近代文語）

カーリンスは三人の宣伝使の歌を聞き、吾も亦歌をうたひ、兎、鰐の一族に対し、巾を利かさねばなるまいと思つたか、直に立上り、妙な手附をして踊りながら、歌ひ始むる。

『此処は名に負ふハルの国 アマゾン河に沿ひて樹てる 大森林の時雨の森と人も言ふ モールバンドやエルバンド 其外百の獣たち 堅城鉄壁千代の棲処と 天日に怖ぢず地に憚らず 月の光に恐れず 自由自在に 咆哮怒号の魔窟ケ原 優勝劣敗 弱肉強食の修羅の巷を 数百万年の昔より 世界の秘密国として 汝が一族に 与へられたる此森よ 森の主は兎の王と 誇りし夢も何時しか消えて 今は悪魔の 棲処と成り果てぬ

眼を配る時もあれ 国治立大神の 守り給へる三五の神の司 鷹依姫を始めとし 竜国別の宣伝使 テーリスタンやカーリンス 四人の貴の神の子が 救ひの神と現はれて 此処まで降り来りしは 枯木に花の咲きしが如く 大旱に雨を得たるが如くなるべし 勇み喜べ兎よ鰐よ 吾等は是よりハルの国 アマゾン河の森林を 神の御水火に言向け和し 尚も進んで珍の国 旭のテルやヒルの国 カルの国まで天降り 八岐大蛇の一族を 言向け和し神の世の

是より鷹依姫外三人は数多の兎を使嗾し、兎の都の王となつて、殆ど一年の日子を此別世界に楽しく面白く送りたり。

或夜、月皎々と光りを湖面に投ぐる折しも、四方の丘の上より、一斉に『ウーウー』と咆哮怒号突喊の声、耳も引裂くるばかり聞え来りぬ。兎の王は驚きて鷹依姫の前に走り来り、

『鷹依姫様に申上げます。只今四方の山々を取囲み、虎、狼、獅子、大蛇、熊王、数多の一族を呼び集め、雲霞のごとく此霊地を占領し、吾等が部下を捉へむと勢猛く攻め寄せました。鰐の頭は数多の眷族を呼びあつめ、死力を尽して闘つて居るでは御座いませうが、何を云つても目に余る大軍、容易に撃退することは不可能なれば、何とか御神力を以て彼等寄せ来る魔軍を言向け和し給はむ事を、偏に一族に代り御願ひ申上げます』

§ 2

Breathlessly and in haste, they plead for help. Takayorihime had been dozing, but she suddenly rose to her feet and climbed to the highest chiten where the Moon Ōkami was enshrined. As she gazed sharply at the shihen, the light of taimatsu shone brilliantly here and there beyond the Aogakiyama that surrounded the four directions. The sounds of hōkōdogō were like the simultaneous roar of banrai, and the monosugo grew more gekiretsu with each kokukoku.

Takayorihime immediately clapped her hands, recited the Amatsunorito, and proclaimed the Kototama toward the tenchi.

Amatsukamitachi Yahoyorozu, Kunitsukamitachi Yahoyorozu, beginning with the Kunitamagami, and especially the Kuniharutachinōomikami, Toyokunihimenōomikami, Amate Ōmikami, and Kami Susanoo Ōkami, who created this world; before your noble presence, Takayorihime, appointed as a divine minister of the three-five, offers her prayers with all her heart. Ah, Ōkami, Ōkami! In the land of Haru on the island of Takasago, and on both banks of the Amazon River, there stands a great forest where thousands of years of time have accumulated. Within it lies this sacred land, a dwelling place for a thousand generations, where the spirits are pure and beautiful.

May the flocks of rabbits and the swarms of crocodiles dwell here forever, rejoicing in the blessings of the gods. May you look with compassion upon their noble hearts, as they bow morning and evening to the Moon God who traverses the heavens, giving thanks for the blessings of the tenchi. Blow away the approaching majin with the divine fire and water of the Ōkami, and make this a safe zone. Ah, Kannagara-kamukami! May this sacred land, the capital of the rabbits, wrapped in the blessings of the gods, remain forever and ever, for a thousand and eight thousand generations, without even a single finger of the Magatsunokami touching it, and may it remain unharmed. May the spring of Tokoyo bloom with joy and courage forevermore, and using this sacred land as the origin, may it extend to those who dwell in the forest of shigure.

May you save even the fierce beasts and the great serpents, leaving none out of the divine blessings; I pray before the gods, Kannagara. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if the enemy's forces are fierce, may you cause the Kototama we received from the gods—the divine power of us four—to shine like the sun in the heavenly sky, and may you establish our achievements for a thousand and eight thousand generations forever, Kannagara. Prostrating myself before the gods, I praise the Kototama.

Just as she sang this, from the direction of Bōshigatake, the highest point of the Byōbu mountain range in the southwest corner, two beams of light shone like searchlights. The demon army surrounding the four directions was struck by the light, stifled their voices, hid their claws, retracted their fangs, closed their eyes, and fell flat upon the earth, trembling in fear.

Ryūkokuwake stood up, bowed twice toward the light, and sang while clapping his hands.

The rabbit children who have long dwelt in this sacred land surrounded by Aogakiyama serve with all their heart, worshipping the Moon God morning and evening to escape the calamities of the majin. It is awe-inspiring that the eight-fathom crocodiles, sympathizing with that sincerity, have gathered at the edge of the lake and are guarding the sacred ground, knowing no distinction between night and day. At this very moment, we four, the divine ministers who transmit the path of the three-five, having left Jitentōtō, have arrived here, drawn by the warp of the divine plan, and behold, the capital of the rabbits is forever.

Protected by the eight-fathom crocodiles, it flourished as if one were seeing the heavenly pure land before one's eyes. Ah, Kannagara-kamukami! Rejoicing in the blessings of the gods, we have become kings here, believing in the heart of the Ōkami who bestows blessings even upon the smallest of birds, beasts, insects, and fish. As we were distributing the blessings of the gods to the clans of rabbits and crocodiles, the tigers, wolves, lions, bears, and the spirits of the great serpents came over the Aogaki mountains, trampling this sacred land and pressing in to annihilate the clans of rabbits and crocodiles. To escape that disaster, we recited the Kototama of the divine norito morning and evening, and at last, we arrived safely.

However, once again, the Magatsunokami came attacking like clouds and mist over the peaks of the mountains in all four directions, and the intensity with which they came, breathing hard to seize this sacred ground, was terrible. As we four were exhausting the depths of our hearts, praying in silence for a while, the spiritual light of Ryū and Kyū shone like electric fire from the summit of Bōshigatake, the Byōbu mountain that separates the northwest sky, and the roar of the majin ceased instantly without a trace.

Ah, Kannagara-kamukami! What divine salvation is this? What human rescue is this?

Truly, this is a grateful evening. Ryūkokuwake, with reverence, purifies his heart and body before the presence of the Kōtaijin, and offers his thanks from afar. Ah, Kannagara-kamukami! May your spirits be blessed.

Having proclaimed the Kototama of gratitude in this way, he finished clapping his hands toward the presence of the Moon Ōkami once more and announced to the King of the Rabbits that they should be at ease for the time being. The King of the Rabbits rejoiced and conveyed this to his subordinates.

The leader of the crocodiles appeared here, greatly encouraged, and said, 'As long as this divine protection appears, we shall take up positions at the lakeside. Even if millions of tigers, wolves, lions, bears, and great serpents come to attack, I will not let them cross this lake water by even a single step. Be at ease, King of the Rabbits.'

Encouraged, they thanked the spiritual light shining from Bōshigatake, and the whole group raised cheers to celebrate the divine favor, and thus they passed the night in safety.

斯く感謝の言霊を宣り上げ、再び月の大神の神前に向つて拍手を終り、兎の王に一先づ安堵すべき事を宣示した。兎の王は喜び勇んで此旨を部下に伝達せり。

鰐の頭此処に現はれ来り、大に勇みて、

『斯く天祐の現はれ来る限りは、吾等は湖辺に陣を取り、虎、狼、獅子、熊、大蛇の群、仮令幾百万襲ひ来るとも、これの湖水は一歩も渡らせじ、御安心あれ兎の王よ』

と勇み立ち、帽子ケ岳より輝き来る霊光に向つて感謝し、一同は歓声を挙げて天祐を祝し、其夜は無事に明かす事とはなりぬ。
