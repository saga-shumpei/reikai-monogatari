Chapter 16: 回顧の Song [907]

回顧の歌〔907〕

§ 1

In the Yahirodono of the Kamuyakata of Uzu, a grand welcoming banquet was held at the initiative of Suekohime, and Kotoyoriwakenomikoto stood up to sing a simple Shiyukuka.

The Ōkami of Kuniharutachi, who created and solidified this world, and who exists as the Mitama of severity, resolved to save the Shinjin of the Momoyasokuni without exception, eternally, in the Miyo of Miroku. Establishing the Ritsupofu of Ametsuchi, the Ōkami set forth the pure Oshihe. Toyokunihime Ōkami, appearing as the Mitama of Mizu, caused the loom of brocade to be woven, and settling firmly in the sacred mountains of Tenkyō and Chikyō, together with the noble Seichi, opened the Oshihe.

At that very time, the Magatsukami, born from the spirits of Adaruhiko and Ebahime, appeared as the Yamata-no-Orochi, ugly foxes, and wicked demons. They rampaged throughout the Toyoashihara-no-Mizuho-kuni, and by entangling the eight kings and eight-headed gods who were the agents of Kami, they gradually planted their influence. Making Shionagahiko their mastermind, they drove the Ōkami of Kuniharutachi to the point of being forced into retirement from this world, and the world fell into the disorder of cut reeds.

Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara. The Ōkami of Kuniharutachi, the Lord Kami and Mitama of severity, leaped from the crater of Mount Tenkyō and fell once into the Ne-no-kuni. Yet, the sincerity of heart that yearns for this world solidified and descended again. Appearing as Nodatehiko, and together with Nodatehime, who is the incarnation of Toyokunihime, the Ōkami appeared on the two mountains of Tenkyō and Chikyō to open the Oshihe of Sango.

Dividing the Mitama of severity once more, the Mitama of severity of Haniyasu-hiko and the Princess Mikoto, awaiting the season, appeared beneath the golden mountain of Jerusalem to preach the path of salvation.

Revering that divine will, the Ōkami of Kuniōtachi, who exists as the four-souled Kami, and the divine authority of the Ōkami of Ōyashimahiko and the divine authority of Ōtaruhiko, appeared as the Mitama of Mizu of Kamikuniwake and Kototamawake. How noble it was that they opened the pure Oshihe of Sango once again to the four corners of the land.

The Ōkami of Kuniōtachi appeared as the Ōkami of Kami-Susanoo, and taking upon his own body the sins and defilements of so many, he took upon himself the crimes of the hundred Kami of Ametsuchi. How noble is his diligence in seeking to pacify and save the Yamata-no-Orochi and the ugly Kami who dwell in the land of Yashima, by leading them to the great path of Tianjin-Makoto through Kototama.

Appearing for a while at the Saienkan of Mount Ubusuna, the Imperial Kami left Hinodewake behind and disguised himself in various forms. Crossing the great ocean to the eighty islands of the sea, he emerged at Jitentōtō and ascended to the Aya-no-Seichi, which is known as a noble sacred site. He hid in the mountain of Shio.

Together with the Ōkami of Kunitakehiko, the incarnation of Kuniharutachi, he aligned his heart, and while arranging the threads of warp and weft to save the world, he wove the loom of brocade. Attended by the noble Mikoto of Tamateruhiko and Tamateruhime, the divine officials of the Brocade Palace, he opened the Oshihe of Sango in the Yahirodono. Entrusted with the role of the leader of the Oshihe, he was opening the Oshihe.

The Mitama of severity and the Mitama of Mizu, revering the words of the Ōkami of warp and weft, looked back at the Seichi and crossed the sea of Wada. When he arrived here, he saw Suekohime, who was born as the precious child of the unexpected Mitama of Mizu, the Kami-Susanoo. Appearing in the hall of the Oshihe where Momogamihiko was settled, she used the Oshihe of Kami as a shield, and while bestowing the dew of grace upon the heads of the common people, she revealed the state of the Miyo of Miroku before our very eyes.

What joy it is to have come to such a noble sacred site. At that very time, the noble Kami-Susanoo came far out to this hall, and descending upon the abandoned princess, he commanded her to pacify and save the Magatsukami of the Amazon River. Revering those words of Kototama, she stood facing Mount Bōshi, which faces Mount Byōbu, and met with Kuniyoriwake. By pacifying the many evil Kami with the spiritual light of Ryu and Kyu, she triumphantly played the song of victory.

When the eighteen divine children returned safely to the hall of Uzu, how grateful it was. The Great Ōkami of Kami-Susanoo came far out to this place, and what joy it is to see him resting.

Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara. May the Mitama be blessed. Not to mention Takasagoshima, may the Toyoashihara-no-Mizuho-kuni, even to the ends of the hundred and eighty islands, be moistened by the dew of grace. May the world bloom with the flowers of peace, and may the Miyo of Miroku, with the scent of plum blossoms and the integrity of the pine, flourish eternally without changing its color. Kannagara. I pray before the presence of Kami.

Even if the morning sun shines or clouds over.

Though the moon may wax and wane,

Though the earth may sink away,

The power of Truth shall save the world.

The God of Truth is here and now,

And since He has revealed Himself,

Together with heaven and earth, forevermore,

The Word of God shall never perish.

The Izu no Mitama and the Mizumitama,

The Great God Kinkatsukane,

The Hinodewake and the Sakuya Hime of the flowers,

Their divine power,

Deep in the bottom of the Riugūkai,

High in the sky of Tenkyōzan,

Unwavering for a thousand and eight thousand generations,

Shall shine across the heavens and earth,

Becoming the light and shining forth.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara,

Reverently and gratefully receiving,

The noble Mimegumi of God,

Beginning with the ministers of God,

All the Tamigusa of the four directions,

Rejoicing in the grace of God,

As long as the Tokiha pine,

Unchanging, like the Takasago,

With the crane nesting in the branches,

Of the green pine growing on the island root,

The names themselves are auspicious, the old man and the old woman,

For as long as the turtle lives,

The waves of the great ocean are clear,

Even the blowing wind is serene,

We pray for peace, Kamunagara, Kamunagara,

May the Mitama be blessed.'

Having finished singing, he follows Suekohime and proceeds toward the inner hall where the Great God Kami Susanoo is resting.

Kuniyoriwake stands up, opens his golden fan, and begins to sing and dance a celebratory song. The song is as follows:
