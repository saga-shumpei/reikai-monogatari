Chapter 6: Lion 粉塵 [897]

獅子粉塵〔897〕

§ 1

The Ōusagi, who is in the position of Samori serving near the King of Rabbits, stands up to express his gratitude and begins to sing once again.

'The jewel of the dragon that illuminates the dark night, the jewel of the sphere that saves us; the two jewels shine like the sun and moon in the great sky. Our clouded faces, which had withered in blue, instantly shine with joy and prosperity in the divine radiance of the two jewels. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. Is this the salvation of Kami, or the manifestation of the light of the soul of the Shinjin who saves us with a sincere heart? From the summit of Mount Bōshigadake, which rises above the clouds on the Yamanami of Byōbugamine standing in the south and north, the Kamutsukasa of the divine spirit of Mizu, the Daikyōshiyu of Kotoyoriwake, and the Masapito of Kuniyoriwake have exerted the divine power of the Ryū and the Tama. By saving us in detail from this suffering of ours, it was shown at the moment we prostrated ourselves before Tsukiōkami. We shall henceforth observe the teachings of the Kami of heaven and earth, not violate one another, and live in harmony and intimacy, fulfilling the great mercy and compassion of the Sumerakami. Following the command of the Sumerōkami, enduring a hundred hardships, opening the Iwato of the heart, and coming all the way to this place, you have become the Kami of our salvation. You have blown away the hundred hardships everywhere like the wind of Shinatobe, and the gratitude for having washed them away in the rapid currents of the Amazon River.

The Kamutsukasa of the precious life of the highly virtuous Takayorihime, starting with Ryūkokubake, and the Masapito of Tērisutan whose heart is clear and bright, and the Kamutsukasa of Kārinsu who deals with the weeds growing in the forest; how noble it is that you have caused the four pillars to stand here, descended to the sacred ground of ten square ri in the center of the Shigure forest, and opened the divine teachings of the clear spring of the lake. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. Having received the divine virtue of the Kami, we too shall quickly cast off the garments of attachment, go to the Amatsukuni to worship at the side of the Kami, and be born as people of fortune; not to mention the Aohitogusa.

Even to the end of the birds, beasts, insects, and fish, we shall save them all without omission or failure through the teachings of the San-iku-kyō, establish merit, and serve as children of the Kami with purity, Kamunagara. We pray before the Kami. Kaminao-hi who created this world, Ōnao-hi whose heart is vast; in this manifest world, just see straight, hear straight, and proclaim straight, O Amatsukami. Before the Kunitamakami, representing all the rabbits, I pray that you let the hand of salvation descend as quickly as possible. Please save the spirits that have fallen to the level of beasts, Kamunagara. I pray to the Kami. Even if the morning sun shines or clouds over, even if heaven and earth should change.

Even if the moon waxes or wanes, as long as my spirit exists, I shall serve the great path of San-iku by purifying my heart for the sake of the Sumerōkami and the world. Please save us, Amatsukami. Please grant us grace, Kunitsukami. Kunitamakami and Ryūseihime, I pray before you. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. May your spirits be blessed.'

To such a place, a group of rabbits returns, carried on the backs of crocodiles, breathing their last. Looking at them, four or five rabbits have had their hands bitten, are wounded, their bellies gouged, and have fallen into a state of near-death.

Upon seeing this, Ryūkokubake immediately draws near, and while singing the heavenly number song in a loud voice, extends his right hand to stroke and rub each of their wounds, tenderly caring for them, and reciting the Amatsu-norito, continuing his silent prayer that the pain of the wounds might cease as quickly as possible; and wonder of wonders, the divine virtue instantly manifests, the pain of the four or five rabbits subsides all at once, and the wounds heal before one's eyes. The rabbits kneel, express their gratitude to Ryūkokubake, and prostrate themselves while shedding tears.

The King of Rabbits turns to the group and urges them to report the details of the disaster as quickly as possible, whereupon the largest rabbit among them turns to the Rabbit King, kneels on his front legs, and tells the war situation in detail.

Rabbit: 'Although it is a matter not worth mentioning, we, together with fifty brothers of the party, crossed the peaks of Tārisu and intended to advance to the forest of Arasu, near the habitat of the Lion King.

Rabbit: 'Just as we were climbing the steep slope, we suddenly heard the roar of a beast troop. Thinking it a great emergency, we hid our bodies in the shade of the trees on all sides, and while we were observing the situation, a pack of bears appeared. They attacked our troop with great force, and relying on their strength, they either pulled off our heads, tore off our limbs, or took us prisoner and dragged us beyond the mountains. We, of course, against the powerful pack of bears...'

斯かる所へ兎の一群、鰐の背に乗せられ、気息奄々として帰り来るあり。見れば四五の兎は手を咬まれ、傷つけられ、腹をえぐられ、殆ど瀕死の状態に陥れり。

之を見るより竜国別は忽ち側に寄り添ひ、天の数歌を声高く歌ひ上げながら、右の手を伸べて各自其傷所を撫でさすり、懇に労はり且つ天津祝詞を奏上し、一刻も早く傷所の痛みを止めさせ給へと、暗祈黙祷を続けたるに、不思議なるかな、神徳忽ち現はれて四五の兎は一斉に痛み鎮まり、疵口は見る間に癒えにける。兎は跪いて、竜国別に感謝の意を表し涙を流して俯伏せり。

兎の王は一同に向ひ、一刻も早く遭難の顛末を言上せよと迫れば、其中にて最も大なる兎は、兎王に向ひ、前膝をつき、詳さに戦況を物語る。

Without the strength to resist, and while suffering from wounds, we were fleeing. Just as we were about to perish, caught in the claws and fangs of the bears, a dazzling spiritual light shone down from the direction of Mount Bōshi, illuminating us like a mirror. The bear troop, though peerless in their mighty strength, were instantly blinded and fell prostrate on the spot. Seizing this opportunity, our troop barely managed to escape to the shore of the lake, where we were saved by the crocodiles guarding the lakeside and returned here.

We reported this war situation to the King in complete detail, and with hearts as tense as a drawn bow, we returned, wishing that he would launch a punitive war against the enemy, and make preparations to ensure that the evil gods would repent and never again launch reckless wars.

However, how noble and grateful it is! We were saved from our precarious lives by the Kamutsukasa of the Ananai-kyō, the god Tatsukuniwake, whom we have long revered and served. Our pain was instantly healed; could there be such a noble and joyful thing anywhere else in the world?

Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara. Having received the grace of the gods before our very eyes, we shall from now on offer prayers of gratitude to the Great God of the Moon, and protect the sacred land of the Capital of Rabbits, making it firm and everlasting.

Takaorihime-sama, Tatsukuniwake-sama, and the other two Kamutsukasa, please remain in this village for a thousand, nay, eight thousand generations, and protect our spirits. We offer our gratitude for your great kindness, deeper than the sea, and pray here for your protection in the future.

Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara. May your spirits be blessed.

Speaker: 'As they shed tears of joy, they thanked the gods from the bottom of their hearts, rejoiced in the kindness of Tatsukuniwake, and yearned for him as if he were their own parent.'

The troop of fierce beasts, which had surged forward like clouds and mist, were illuminated by the spiritual light of Ryū and Kyū, and returned to the camp of the Lion King in the Forest of Arasu, stumbling and falling, barely escaping with their lives.

The Lion King had been waiting with his neck stretched out, wondering about the victory of this attack on the Capital of Rabbits. To such a place, the swift-flying Bald Eagle returned, spreading its wings of about three jō, soaring through the air and flying back before the Lion King like an arrow. The Lion King recognized him at once and asked impatiently, as if he could wait no longer,

Speaker: 'Are you not the King of the Bald Eagles? Tell me in detail the victory or defeat of our side in today's battle!'

The Bald Eagle, while flapping his wings, spoke of the battle's progress to the Lion King with a look of deep resentment.

Speaker: 'The Wolf King advanced from the south of Mount Aogaki, the Bear King from the north, the Tiger King charged from the west, the Jackal King from the valley in the east, the Great Serpent from the southeast and northwest, and the Eagle troop took positions in the northeast and southwest. Just as they surrounded the Capital of Rabbits ten and twenty times over and advanced while raising a war cry all at once, the tens of thousands of crocodiles lurking by the lakeside suddenly stood up, making the waves dance and blowing water. Although their momentum seemed difficult to approach, we exerted the strength of a lion, and attacking with our lives as the stake, we finally captured forty-five of the Rabbit troop.'

Speaker: 'After catching our breath, we advanced further, crossed the lake in a single sweep, and headed toward the sacred land of the Moon. Just as we were raging, intending to capture the King of the Rabbits and finally achieve our goal, a mysterious spiritual light appeared from the summit of Mount Bōshi. When it shone upon our army, our eyes were blinded, our hands went numb, and our legs trembled, making it impossible to advance. Barely escaping with our lives, we climbed Mount Aogaki again, and swallowing our resentment, we retreated in all directions. Our allies are scattered in the four quarters, and the whereabouts of the Bear King, the Tiger King, the Wolf King, and the King of the Great Serpent remain unknown even now.'

Speaker: 'Regrettably, I am the only one who has flown through the air and finally returned to report.'

As he spoke, the Lion King crossed his arms and was overcome with tears of grief and anger. But from somewhere, a song of propaganda began to echo on the wind. The moment he heard that voice, the Lion King clutched his head, covered his eyes, and suddenly fell prostrate on the spot, writhing in agony. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara. May your spirits be blessed.
