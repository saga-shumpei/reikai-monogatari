Chapter 21: 奉答 Song [912]

奉答歌〔912〕

§ 1

Suekohime and all the Kamutsukasa, unable to contain their tears of gratitude at this Senji of the Ōkami, could only bow their heads and rejoice in the Kuōdaimuhen of the Shinon.

Sutekohime, representing them all, sang a Tafureika to the Ōkami in a tone of Kingen. The song is as follows:

The precious Miko born of the Mihana of Kamuizanaginōokami, like the plum blossoms opening all at once, the fruit they bear is abundant, and its flavor is the Kamumitama of Susanō, which He clarifies. Appearing as a God of salvation, He takes upon His own body the hundred Tsumitoga of Ametsuchi, and to pacify and save the Yamataorochi, Shikogitsune, Sagume, Shikome, and Magaoni that coil within the land of Yashima, by the Kototama of the path of truth, He spares not His own noble body, crossing over snow-laden mountains, climbing over fields where tigers crouch, and traveling far across the vast ocean, clearing away the disasters and illnesses of the people.

Pacifying and harmonizing the Kami, He travels high in the sky in the bird-ship of heaven, looking down upon the mountain pass of the land of Teru beyond the clouds and mist, and appearing in the divine hall of Argentina is the precious Miko, the Suekohime, the eighth maiden, who, while saving the people of the world with the teachings of spirit and flesh, spends the days and months in the divine hall, descending from heaven without aversion, and for the Kamutsukasa of the Ananai-kyō, and for the Miko of the teachings, not to mention even the descendants of the common people, to bestow the dew of grace, how noble is His appearance.

Suekohime, Sutekohime, the Kamutsukasa of Kototawake, Kuniyoriwake, Takahime, Takayori-hime, and the Kyōtsukasa of Ryūkokubetsu, marveling at the ever-increasing Shinon, find even their words of gratitude faltering in their mouths.

In this truly joyous sacred place, may You never weary of staying, and while relaxing Your Kamumitama, please bestow the moisture of the divine spirit upon the people of the land of Takasago-shima, Kannagara.

Representing the Kamutsukasa, Sutekohime, with reverence, prays before Your presence: even if the morning sun shines or clouds over, even if the moon waxes or wanes, even if the earth itself should sink, we shall uphold the one truth.

Respecting the great heart of the Kami Susanō Ōkami, who protects and blesses this world, we shall never forget the teachings of Ananai even in our dreams, and we shall expand them to the four quarters, and to repay even a ten-thousandth of Your great grace, we pledge our pure hearts.

May You deign to leave Your footprints forever in the capital of Uzu. Ah, Kannagara Kamunagara, may Your spirit be blessed.

Thus she finished her simple greeting.

Susanō Ōkami, smiling with satisfaction, followed by Suekohime and Kototawake-no-Mikoto, proceeded once again deep into the inner sanctuary.

Although there were celebratory songs from Yasuhiko, Munehiko, Akiyama-wake, Morris, Masazumihiko, Tsunehiko, Haruhiko, Job, Thelistan, Karlins, Harukimi, and others, they were deemed too tedious and were omitted.

The assembly visited the shrine and recited the Amatsu-Norito with clear voices, and upon finishing, they sang the heavenly number songs, recited the Kamugoto, thanked the Shinon more deeply and profoundly than ever before, and each took their assigned seats, binding the dream of a warm night.

Here, Susanō Ōkami, in the inner sanctuary, consulted with Kototawake-no-Mikoto and the official Matsuwakahiko, and deliberated on the great issue concerning the personal affairs of Suekohime. What kind of agreement was reached, I wonder? Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Hae Mase.

Today is the twenty-fourth day of the eighth month of the eleventh year of Taishō, the second day of the seventh month by the old calendar. Since last night's heavy rain, the Kano River has flooded with muddy water, and fearing that the oral dictation in the riverside mansion would be difficult to hear due to the roaring sound of the water, it was decided to compile the thirty-second volume of the Reikai Monogatari in the back room of the newly built temporary Kyōshu-den, together with the scribe Mr. Matsumura. From the sacred ground of Aya, both Ms. Fukushima and Ms. Hoshida have arrived at the Yumoto-kan, and they are busy writing something, recording the usual Fudesaki. Suddenly, a telegram arrives. The Second Kyōshu is coming to consult urgently with Mizuki, arriving on the express train at eight last night. Mizuki, lying down in the rain-drenched mansion, speaks with a face expectant of a visitor. Kannagara Tamachi Hae Mase.

原文（近代文語）

末子姫を始め、一同の神司は大神の此宣示に感謝の涙堪へ難く、只俯むいて神恩の広大無辺なるに驚喜するばかりなりき。

捨子姫は一同を代表し、大神に対し、答礼歌を謹厳なる口調にて歌ひ上げ奉る。其歌、

『神伊弉諾大神の 御鼻に生れます貴の御子 一度に開く梅の花 結ぶみのりも豊やかに 其味はひも素盞嗚の 澄み切り給ふ神身魂 救ひの神と現れまして 天地百の罪科を 御身一つに負はせつつ 八洲の国に蟠まる 八岐大蛇や醜狐 探女醜女や曲鬼を 誠の道の言霊に 言向け和し救はむと 尊き御身も厭はずに 雪積む山を打渡り 虎伏す野辺を乗越えて 大海原をはるばると 巡り給ひて人草の 災払ひ病気の

神を言向け和しつつ 天の鳥船空高く 雲霧分けてテルの国 テル山峠を下に見て アルゼンチンの神館 八人乙女の貴の御子 末子の姫の現れまして 霊と肉との教もて 世人を救ひ給ひつつ 月日を送る神館 厭ひ給はず天降りまし 三五教の神司 教の御子は云ふも更 青人草の末までも 恵の露を与へむと 出でさせ給ひし尊さよ 末子の姫や捨子姫 言依別の神司 国依別や高姫や 鷹依姫や竜国別の

教司は神恩の いやちこなるに感歎し 感謝の詞さへ口籠る 実にも嬉しき此聖場 厭はせ給はず何時迄も 身魂を寛ぎ給ひつつ 高砂島の国人に 瑞の御霊のうるほひを 恵ませ給へ惟神 神の司を代表し 捨子の姫が畏みて 御前に願ひ奉る 朝日は照るとも曇るとも 月は盈つとも虧くるとも 仮令大地は沈むとも 誠一つを立て通し 此世を守り恵みます 神素盞嗚大神の 大御心を慎みて 夢寐にも忘れず三五の

教を四方に拡充し 大御恵の万一に 酬い奉らむ吾々が 清き心を諾なひて ウヅの都に末永く 御跡を垂れさせ給へかし あゝ惟神々々 御霊幸はひましませよ』

と簡単に挨拶を述べ終る。

神素盞嗚大神は、満足げに微笑み給ひながら、末子姫、言依別命を従へ、再び奥殿深く進み入り給ふ。

安彦、宗彦、秋山別、モリス、正純彦、常彦、春彦、ヨブ、テーリスタン、カーリンス、春公などの祝歌あれども、余りくだくだしければ、省略する事となしぬ。

一同は神殿に詣で天津祝詞を声も涼しく奏上し、終つて天の数歌をうたひ上げ、神言を奏上し、神恩を今更の如く深く厚く感謝し、各々与へられたる席に着き、暖かなる一夜の夢を結ぶ事となりぬ。

茲に神素盞嗚大神は、奥殿に於て言依別命、松若彦の司と謀り、末子の姫の一身上に関する大問題につき協議を凝らし給うた。果して如何なる協議が纏まつたであらうか。あゝ惟神霊幸倍坐世。

○

今日は大正十一年八月二十四日旧七月二日、昨夜来の豪雨に狩野川は濁水氾濫し、水声轟々として、川辺の館に於ける口述は声低き物語、聞取り難きを慮り、新築されし臨時教主殿の奥の間に於て、筆者松村氏と共に第三十二巻の霊界物語を編むこととなつた。湯本館の二階には綾の聖地より、福島、星田両女史出張し、何事か筆を走らせ例の筆先を認めて居られる。忽ち飛電あり。二代教主、瑞月に急々相談あり、昨夜八時の急行にて来ると。瑞月は雨の館に身を横へながら人待顔に述べ立つる。惟神霊幸倍坐世。
