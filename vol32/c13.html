Chapter 13: 平等 Love [904]

平等愛〔904〕

§ 1

Takahime and the seven others crossed the Bridge of Crocodiles, were welcomed by a multitude of rabbits in the southern forest, and at last arrived at the sacred site where the Great God of the Moon is enshrined, the capital of the forest surrounding Aogakiyama. Takayorihime wears a crown of white hair, has tamed all fierce beasts as if they were her own children, and is devoting her full strength to the Saido of the spirits of all beasts.

Takahime met with Takayorihime for the first time in a long while, grasped her hands firmly without saying a word, and tears streamed down both her cheeks from joy and nostalgia. Here, the twelve Mitama—the eight of Takahime's party and the four of Takayorihime's party—offered up uninterrupted Kamigoto to Tenchi for Nanukananayo, Kotomu and pacified all the fierce beasts, and gave them the promise that after leaving their Nikutai, they would surely reach Tengoku, be reborn on this earth as Shinjin, and participate in Shingefu, causing the fierce beasts present to be intoxicated with tears of joy.

No matter how ferocious a lion, tiger, wolf, bear, great serpent, jackal, or leopard may be, when their hunger is satisfied, they never commit such cruelty as to harm other animal species. It is only when pressed by hunger that they, for the sake of preserving their Nikutai, unavoidably take and eat the lives of other animals.

However, humans, the spiritual leaders of all creation, even when their storehouses are full, still indulge their greed, strike down others, merely fatten their own purses, buy fertile fields for their descendants, and occupy the majority of those who have no intention of pitying or helping others. Nevertheless, in the age of the gods, the social organization was most simple, the system of barter was naturally practiced, and even for money, rare seashells or the seeds of coconut palms were fashioned into various vessels and used as substitutes for current currency, or gold dust was gathered and used as a substitute for currency. Furthermore, no fixed price was established. Therefore, the people of the age of the gods were most free from greed, and even those called villains only fought for the sake of passion. Although Ogetsuhime-no-Kami appeared and the luxury of food, clothing, and housing began, and the distinction between rich and poor gradually appeared, such a great disparity as in the present age never occurred.

Although there were those who divided and occupied land and mountains and fields, such as Ōyamatsumi and the god of Nozuchi, and viewed them as private property, these were not as petty as those of the present day, and it was truly a peaceful time.

Takahime, Takayorihime, and Ryūkokuwake here received permission from the gods regarding the fierce beasts, established laws, and made them strictly observe them. The gist of those laws is as follows:

One: Bears shall be bears, tigers shall be tigers, wolves shall be wolves, lions shall be lions, snakes shall be snakes, and rabbits shall be rabbits, limiting themselves to certain locations, creating settlements there, and not invading the dwellings of other beasts.

One: Each beast tribe shall abolish all meat-eating, make tree nuts, grass leaves, or tree buds their regular food, and yet their bodies shall not become thin or weak, their natures shall become gentle, and they shall not engage in the strife of devouring one another.

One: From time to time, each beast group shall send representatives to the capital of the rabbits to hold the best deliberations on life.

One: The crocodiles shall be made to prepare for the invasion of the Moleband and Elband, and also serve the purpose of traffic on the Amazon River.

One: The crocodiles shall be respected as being in the rank next to the Lion King, and every year, each beast shall gather before the shrine of the Great God of the Moon to hold a social gathering, making the crocodiles the guests of honor, and rewarding them for their labor throughout the year.

One: Those who violate the above laws shall, by the order of the Lion King, have their Nikutai devoured, and their descendants shall be given the divine punishment of receiving the bodies of beasts forever and living on the earth.

Having established these several articles of law, they had the Lion King and the kings of each beast proclaim them to their tribes in general.

From this time on, those who observed the laws and visited the palace of the Great God of the Moon to offer their sincere hearts, upon passing away after a certain period of their Nikutai, their spirits would immediately ascend to Tengoku and be reborn on earth as humans.

Also, each beast that violated these laws, down to its descendants, remained as they were, retaining the forms of their ancestors, and even now continues a painful life in deep mountains, secluded valleys, and forests where human footprints are rare. Ah, how noble is the benevolence of the Great God of the Moon.

高姫は久し振りに鷹依姫に面会し、固く手を握りものをも言はず、嬉しさと懐しさに涙を両頬より垂らしてゐる。ここに愈高姫一行八人と、鷹依姫の一行四人を加へ十二の身魂は、天地に向つて七日七夜の間断なき神言を奏上し、すべての猛獣を悉く言向け和し、肉体を離れたる後は必ず天国に到り、神人となつて再び此土に生れ来り、神業に参加すべき約束を与へ、所在猛獣をして歓喜の涙に酔はしめたり。

如何に猛悪なる獅子、虎、狼、熊、大蛇、豺、豹と雖も、口腹充つる時は、決して他の獣類を犯す如き暴虐はなさないものである。只飢に迫り、其肉体の保存上、止むを得ずして他の動物の生命を奪り食ふのみである。

然るに万物の霊長たる人間は、倉廩満ちても猶欲を逞しうし、他人を倒し、只単に自己の財嚢を肥し、吾子孫の為に美田を買ひ、決して他を憐み助くるの意思なき者、大多数を占めてゐる。併し乍ら、神代は社会上の組織、最も簡単にして、物々交換の制度自然に行はれ、金銭と雖も珍しき貝殻、或は椰子の実の種をいろいろの器になし、之を現今の金に代用し、又は砂金などを拾ひて通貨の代用にしてゐたのである。さうして一定の価格も定まつてゐなかつた。それ故神代の人は最も寡欲にして、如何に悪人と称せらるる者と雖も、只々情欲の為に争ふ位のものであつた。時には大宜津姫神現はれて、衣食住の贅沢始まり、貧富の区別漸く現はれたりと雖も、現代の如き大懸隔は到底起らなかつたのである。

大山祇、野槌の神などの土地山野を区劃して占領し、私有物視したる者も出で来りたれども、これ亦現代の如くせせこましき者にあらず、実に安泰なものであつた。

高姫、鷹依姫、竜国別は、茲に猛獣に対し、神に許しを受けて、律法を定め、彼等をして固く守らしめた。其律法の大要は、

一、熊は熊、虎は虎、狼は狼、獅子は獅子、蛇は蛇、兎は兎として或地点を限り、其処に部落を作り、互に他獣の住所を侵さざる事

一、各獣族は一切の肉食を廃し、木の実又は草の葉、木の芽などを常食とし、而も身体少しも痩衰へず、性質温良になり、互に呑噬の争ひをなさざる事

一、時々各獣団体より代表者を兎の都に派遣し、最善の生活上の評議をなす事

一、鰐をして、モールバンド、エルバンドの襲来に備へ、且つアマゾン河の往来の用に任ずる事

一、鰐を獅子王の次の位と尊敬し、年々、各獣、月の大神の社前に集まりて、懇談会を開き鰐を主賓となし、年中の労苦を犒ふ事

一、右の律法に違反したるものは、獅子王の命により、其肉体は取り喰はれ、其子孫永遠に獣類の身体を受得して、地上に棲息するの神罰を与へらるる事

等の数ケ条の律法を定め、獅子王を始め各獣の王をして、之を其種族一般に布告せしめた。

これより其律法を遵守し、月の大神の宮に詣でて赤誠を捧げたるものは、一定の肉体の期間を経て帰幽するや、直に其霊は天国に上り、再び人間として地上に生れ来ることとなりぬ。

又此律法に違反したる各獣は、其子孫に至る迄、依然として祖先の形体を保ち、今に尚人跡稀なる深山幽谷森林などに、苦しき生活を続けてゐるのである。あゝ尊き哉、月の大神の御仁慈よ。

§ 2

Kuniharutachinōokami has bestowed light upon the spirits of all Shinjin, down to the Kinjiuchiugiyo, and has not allowed them to continue in the wretched bodies of beasts forever; rather, He has created a path of salvation, caused them to observe the Ritsupofu, and caused their spirits to Kōjiyō. Therefore, the Nikutai of the Kinjiuchiugiyo that have undergone Kiiu are never Iki on the Chijiyō, and the fact that they can ascend to Ten just as they are, through the Hauhofu of Shike, is a Tokuken common to all animals other than Ningen. This is true even for Maujiu, to say nothing of others. Crows, kites, sparrows, swallows, and other Yachō that fly in the Kūchiu never leave their corpses Iki on the Chijiyō and never come to the sight of man; this is all because, through the grace of Kami, they have accumulated various Shiyugefu over a certain Kikan and ascend to Tenjiyō to Kōjiyō their spirits. The only cases where they die and leave their Taiku behind are those of animals that have met an unnatural death, such as being shot by a Tetsupau, killed by a bow, or caught and killed by larger birds, and thus fallen to the Chijiyō. That all other Kinjiuchiugiyo, who maintain their natural lifespan and undergo Kiiu, can ascend to Tenjiyō through the Hauhofu of Shike without exception is proof that the infinite benevolence of the Kami of heaven and earth is bestowed equally and without partiality, even down to the Kinjiuchiugiyo. If we consider that maintaining a lowly Nikutai as a Kinjiuchiugiyo and existing in this world is a stage of progress toward becoming a Ningen, we must realize that we cannot treat even the smallest animal with disrespect. Unless one awakens to that spirit, one can never become a true soul of the Divine Kingdom, nor can one attain the heart of Kami. Furthermore, one would lack the qualifications of a Ningen.

If one speaks thus, some may say: 'Without fishermen to catch fish, we cannot maintain our precious lives; without hunters to catch beasts, we would feel inconvenienced in the necessities of daily life; even if we do not commit useless slaughter, useful slaughter must be unavoidable.' There will surely appear those who raise counterarguments, saying that if one were to take such a path and truly observe it, the inconveniences to society would indeed be severe. However, each has their own heaven-ordained vocation: the cat catches the mouse, the mouse catches and eats the mites that cause harm to humanity, the fish eats the mosquito larvae, the frog catches the rice insects, the mountain hunter catches the lion and bear, and the river fisherman and sea fisherman catch fish; that they observe their respective occupations is all due to the causality of past lives and is something specially permitted by heaven. Therefore, the Kinjiuchiugiyo that fall into the hands of mountain hunters are only those whose time has come to receive the divine punishment of Kami for having already broken the laws of heaven, and thus they are destined to perish at the hands of the hunter. This is the same for both sea fish and river fish. However, in the modern age, to call it 'sport hunting,' for workers to use their holidays to fish, or for officials and other bureaucrats to be granted hunting licenses and hunt in the mountains and fields, is truly a great sin against the laws of heaven. To take the precious life of a Kinjiuchiugiyo for the sake of momentarily comforting one's own heart must be called a cruel, demonic heart that surpasses even that of a beast.

Each person has a profession determined by heaven. To devote oneself single-mindedly to this, and for the samurai, farmers, artisans, and merchants all to participate in the divine work, is the true duty of human life. Even the thinking of Ningen who, when the plague is prevalent, plot to exterminate all mice by spreading poison, is theoretically sound but ultimately impossible to carry out. Furthermore, when there are no mice in a house, the evil spirits generated from the breathing of sleeping humans condense on the ceiling and generate small mites, and because of these insects, one ends up shortening one's precious life. Kami caused the mouse to exist according to the needs of humans to remove this harm. The mouse is most fond of mites. Therefore, its cry is always 'chiu-chiu.' The Kototama reversal of 'chiu' becomes 'tsu.' However, when the breeding of mice is excessive, because there are few mites to eat, they are forced to gnaw on rice chests and cause various harms. Therefore, Kami created the cat to regulate the breeding of mice. What the cat likes to eat is the mouse. The Kototama reversal of the mouse becomes 'ni.' The cat's cry is 'nyan'; 'ya' is to retreat, and 'n' is a sound that resonates at the end of speech as a natural trait of beasts. Therefore, when one hears the sound 'nyan,' the 'ni' of the mouse is frightened and hides; this is an immutable truth in the study of Kototama. If a person tries, when mice are running wild, to make the sound of a cat 'nyan' once or twice with an inhaled breath, the rampaging mice will instantly quiet down and flee far away. The Kototama reversal of 'niya' becomes 'na.' Therefore, among cats, those with pure Kototama cry 'nan.'

§ 3

All Kinjiuchiugiyo produce sound by inhaling, while Mikunibito produce resonant Seion from their Seikatanden by exhaling; one must not forget that there are also races that produce language intermediate between inhaling and exhaling.

Among birds, there are also those that occasionally produce sounds intermediate between exhaling and inhaling for one or two notes. Since horses are Yōsei animals, they produce sounds like 'ha-hi-fu-he-ho,' and since cows are Insei animals, they produce the sounds of 'ma-mi-mu-me-mo.' As stated at the beginning, all other animals possess their own unique voices and express their intentions completely.

Since horses are Yōsei beasts, if a person straddles their back and calls out 'hai,' they immediately move forward unconsciously. 'Ha' is the Kototama of opening and advancing, and 'i' is the breath of left and right. In other words, it becomes a command to open the left and right legs and advance. Since cows are Insei beasts, if a person stands behind them and says 'shii,' they move forward. 'Shi' is the meaning of water, and also of flowing and moving while bowing down. 'I' is as stated before. Horses raise their heads and advance by showing the breath of Yang, while cows lower their heads and advance by showing the water and fire of Yin. Yōsei horses stop when one says 'dō,' and Insei cows stop when one says 'ou.' 'Do' is the meaning of Yōtekifudō, and 'ou' is the meaning of the Kototama of Intekifudō.

Viewing it from this perspective, it is a clear fact that all Kinjiuchiugiyo move, stop, advance, and retreat according to Kototama in a Kamunagarateki manner. All other Kinjiuchiugiyo are the same.

Although it is written in some ancient books that when Michael stands and cries out, the mountains, rivers, grasses, trees, and all things in heaven and earth respond to it, this shows the marvelous utility of Kototama, where a true person who has realized the true meaning and application of Kototama appears in the end times, shakes heaven and earth, scolds or drives the wind, rain, thunder, and lightning, calms the mountains, rivers, grasses, and trees, and gives them rest. Ah, how great is the marvelous utility of Kototama!

From this point, Takahime, Takayohime, Ryūkokuwake, and nine others finished bowing respectfully before the Great God of the Moon, had the King of Rabbits serve them generously, went to the banks of the Amazon River, and toward the tribes of the Morband and the Elband, gave the Kototama of good words and beautiful phrases, causing them to be pleased and submit. Eventually, the Morband and Elband were moved by the marvelous utility of Kototama, raised clouds, suddenly became dragon-bodied and ascended to heaven, raised winds, called for rain, gave rain and dew to all things on earth, gave fresh winds everywhere, and became messengers of God who make the gods, humans, and all things dwell in peace.

However, some of those monsters and fierce beasts who have not yet repented still pass down their wretched physical bodies to their descendants, and some are still spending unpleasant time lurking in forests, in deep valleys, or hiding on the seabed or riverbeds.

Compared to today, the strange beasts of ancient times were most numerous in their count and in their species. However, through the benevolence of the God of San-iku-kyo and the marvelous utility of Kototama, they have gradually been purified and have come to be born as human bodies. Therefore, even today, there has come to be a distinction of high and low, superiority and inferiority in the origin and nature of the spirit. However, since the root of all of them originated from the Yin and Yang water and fire of the three creation gods, Amenominakanushi-no-Ōkami, Takamimusubi-no-Kami, and Kamumusubi-no-Kami, all the myriad things in the universe have the same root and are all like brothers.

The fact that there are distinctions of righteousness and evil, wisdom and foolishness, while possessing the same human form, receiving the same education, living in the same country, and eating the same food, is, in short, the result of the origin and nature of the spirit.

Among some theorists, there are those who argue that since all humans are divided spirits of the same Heavenly Emperor, there is no reason for distinctions such as the origin, nature, lineage, direct line, or collateral line of the spirit. Such arguments fall into mere logic and are people who do not know the profound and subtle history of the soul. Just as there are length, shortness, fatness, thinness, beauty, and ugliness in the human body, it is a natural principle that the soul also follows this. In short, since the human body is like a suit for the soul, one must realize that each human's form is the form of the soul itself. That the soul, when leaving the body and wandering in the spirit world, possesses the same form as the body it has shed, is something that Western spiritual scholars are finally beginning to acknowledge.

It must be said that it is a tragic situation where heaven and earth are inverted and host and guest are reversed, in that the study of material civilization was pioneered by Westerners, and our country, the home of soul studies, is also having soul studies pioneered by Westerners. For several decades, we have been studying soul studies, wagging our tongues, and shouting until our voices were hoarse. However, the Japanese have the bad habit of not believing in even the most profound truths unless they come from the mouths or pens of Westerners. Therefore, we are those who greatly lament that we are a blind race that cannot believe in any high theory or excellent opinion unless it is once exported to Western countries and then imported again with the help of Westerners. We anticipate that this story, too, will not be believed by the majority of Japanese people until it has passed through the ears and eyes of philosophers in Western countries and has been translated and imported again, and we deeply lament this situation. Kannagara Tamachi Haema Se.

すべて禽獣虫魚は引く息を以て音声を発し、神国人は吹く息を以て臍下丹田より嚠喨たる声音を発し、又引く息、吹く息の中間的言語を発する人種もあることを忘れてはならぬ。

又鳥の中にも、吹く息、引く息の中間的の声音を一二声発するものが、たまにはあるものである。馬は陽性の動物なれば、『ハヒフヘホ』と声音を発し、牛は陰性の動物なれば、『マミムメモ』の声音を発す。其他一切の動物、各特有の音声を有し、完全に其意思を表示することは発端に述べた通りである。

馬は陽性の獣類なれば、人其背に跨がり『ハイ』と声をかくれば、忽ち無意識に前進す。『ハ』は開き進むの言霊であり『イ』は左右の息である。即ち左右の脚を開きて進めと云ふ命令詞となる。牛は陰性の獣類なれば、人あり、後より『シイ』と言へば前進す。『シ』は水にして且つ俯むき流れ動くの意である。『イ』は前に述べた通りである。馬は頭をあげて、陽の息を示して進み、牛は頭を下げて陰の水火を示して進む。陽性の馬は『ドー』と言へば止まり、陰性の牛は『オウ』と言へば止まる。『ド』は陽的不動の意味であり、『オー』は陰的不動の言霊の意味である。

之を以て之を見れば、禽獣虫魚一切、惟神的に言霊によりて動止進退することは明白なる事実である。其他の禽獣皆然りである。

或古書にミカエル立ちて叫び給へば、山川草木、天地一切これに応ずとあるも、言霊の真意活用を悟りたる真人の末世に現はれて、天地を震撼し、風雨雷霆を叱咤し又は駆使し、山川草木を鎮定せしめ、安息を与ふる言霊の妙用を示されたものである。あゝ偉大なる哉、言霊の妙用！

○

是より高姫、鷹依姫、竜国別、外九人は月の大神の御前に恭しく拝礼を了り、兎の王をして厚く仕へしめ、アマゾン河の畔に出でて、モールバンドを始めエルバンドの一族に向ひ、善言美詞の言霊を与へて、彼等を悦服せしめ、遂にモールバンド、エルバンドは言霊の妙用に感じ、雲を起し、忽ち竜体となつて天に昇り、風を起し、雨を呼び、地上の一切に雨露を与へ、清鮮の風を万遍なく与へて、神人万有を安住せしむる神の使となりたり。

併し乍ら、まだ悔い改めざる彼等怪獣及猛獣の一部は、今尚浅ましき肉体を子孫に伝へて、或は森林に或は幽谷に潜み、海底、河底に潜伏などして、面白からぬ光陰を送つてゐるものもあるのである。

古の怪しき獣は、今日に比ぶれば、其数に於て其種類に於て最も夥しかつた。併しながら三五教の神の仁慈と言霊の妙用によつて、追々に浄化し、人体となつて生れ来ることとなつた。故に霊の因縁性来等に於て、今日と雖も、高下勝劣の差別を来たすこととなつたのである。併しながら何れも其根本は天御中主大神、高皇産霊神、神皇産霊神の造化三神の陰陽の水火より発生したるものなれば、宇宙一切の森羅万象は皆同根にして、何れも兄弟同様である。

同じ人間の形体を備へ、同じ教育をうけ、同じ国に住み、同じ食物を食しながら、正邪賢愚の区別あるは、要するに霊の因縁性来のしからしむる所以である。

或理窟屋の中には、総ての人間は同じ天帝の分霊なれば、霊の因縁性来、系統、直系、傍系などの区別ある理由なしと論ずる人がある。斯の如き論説は、只一片の道理に堕して、幽玄微妙なる霊魂の経緯を知らざる人である。人の肉体に長短肥瘠、美醜ある如く、霊魂も亦これに倣ふは自然の道理である。要するに人間の肉体は霊魂のサツクのやうなものであるから、人間各自の形体は霊魂そのものの形体であることを悟らねばならぬ。霊魂肉体を離れ、霊界に遊ぶ時は、其脱却したる肉体と同様の形体を備へ居る事は、欧米霊学者の漸く認むる所である。

物質文明の学は泰西人に先鞭をつけられ、霊魂学の本場たる我国は亦泰西人に霊魂学迄先鞭をつけられつつあるは、天地顛倒、主客相反する惨状と云はねばならぬ。我々は数十年来霊魂学の研究につき、舌をただらし、声をからして叫んで来た。されど邦人は如何に深遠なる真理と雖も、泰西人の口より筆より出でざれば、之を信ぜざるの悪癖がある。故に如何なる高論卓説と雖も、一旦泰西諸国に輸出し、再び泰西人の手を借りて、輸入し来らざれば、信ずること能はざる盲目人種たることを、我々は大に歎く者である。此物語も亦一度泰西諸国の哲人の耳目に通じ、再び訳されて輸入し来る迄は、邦人の多数は之を信じないだらうと予想し、且つ深く歎く次第であります。惟神霊幸倍坐世。
