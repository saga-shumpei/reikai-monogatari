Kunidono: 'Ah, a troublesome matter has arisen. What should I do, Kunidono?'

Kunidono: 'No matter how troubled you are, there is nothing to be done. As for this marriage proposal, no matter what Kotoyoriwake may say, even if heaven and earth were to turn upside down, I must interfere and make a mess of it at all costs; otherwise, the Uzu-no-kuni that the Ōkamisama has built with such hardship and toil will collapse entirely. If only you could have a change of heart, everyone in the country would rejoice; so, for a man to bravely give up one woman to save many people—you cannot imagine how noble that is. You cannot even fathom how joyful it would be.'

Kunidono: 'Ah, I have become sick of the Iukai and the Shinkai. Takahime-sama, can you truly not understand the bottom of my heart, the Shinkai?'

Takahime: 'What are you saying... I cannot understand the bottom of your heart at all, can I?'

Kunidono: 'Ah, it cannot be helped... I have a sudden urgent matter, so I must go there. Please, come and visit again in four or five days to talk at leisure.'

Takahime: 'This marriage is already pressing upon us tomorrow, and you ask me to come back in four or five days... Hmph, you say such naive things. No matter what, I must make you decide your intentions tonight, and then I must go and offer my opinion to Suekohime-sama, so do not try to run away; listen to me carefully.'

Kunidono: 'Even if you tell me to listen, I will not listen even if it is a crisis... Excuse me, Takahime-sama, I am in a hurry with preparations for the wedding; I must comb my hair, shave my beard, apply a little powder, put on some rouge, and touch up my lipstick, and I must also take a look in the mirror. If I stay with an old woman with such a dark face, I will only miss Suekohime-sama all the more. Farewell...'

He tries to rush out in a panic. Takahime grabs him from behind and holds him tight.

Takahime: 'Hey, hey, Kunidono, where are you going? Wait a moment, sit down properly, and listen to the principles of heaven and earth. I will never tell you anything bad.'

Kunidono: 'Please let me go. It hurts so much when you hold me with such hard hands. I must go to Suekohime-sama's side as soon as possible. The difference between being held by a rock and being held by silk is unbearable... Takahime-sama, please, for the sake of my future, let me go.'

Takahime: 'No, I will not let you go.'

Kunidono: 'Takahime-sama, you just said, "I will not let you go," did you not? Does that mean you are of the opinion that you will not separate the two of us after all?'

Takahime: 'That is a different story. The talk is that I will make you separate. Now is the time for you to decide your fate; come, make a noble declaration right here that you have completely given up.'

Kunidono: 'In that case... I have given it up completely.'

Takahime: 'Oh, how happy I am; you are truly admirable.'

Kunidono: 'What I have given up completely is my association with the wrinkled old hag Takahime-sama, ha ha ha ha!'

Takahime: 'Hey, Kunidono, you just wait and see. Tomorrow night, I will make you regret it; a woman's single-mindedness can pierce even a rock, how could this not prevail!'

She leaves the place in a hurry, with her eyes narrowed in anger.
