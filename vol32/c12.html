Chapter 12: 鰐の Bridge [903]

鰐の橋〔903〕

§ 1

Haruhiko, together with Yobu, rushed into the dense forest while shouting in a loud voice to save Takahime from her peril; yet, call and shout as he might, only the echoes of his own voice returned from the forest's Kodama, and with no sense of direction, he had no choice but to begin wandering through the forest here and there while singing in a loud voice.

Haruhiko: 'Takahime-sama, whom we have relied upon as our master, has once again had her soul drawn out by the Kongaufue Niyoihotsushiyu and other jewels. How shameful it is that here in the center of the forest, she has had the depths of her heart examined by the Miyōjin of the sun and moon, and the rising sun; she has been squeezed dry of her spirit, and though her body is covered in mud, she still clings to her stubbornness. While uttering nothing but impudent words, she abandoned the two of us and returned to the path from which she came, setting out to search for the Arika of Takayorihime. We two have no choice but to head toward the northwest, and as we proceed, we find a stone Jizō on the side of the road, its ears gone, its hands severed, its head missing, standing frozen with a black face by the roadside. While we rested our breath for a moment before it, we spoke only of Takahime-sama, when suddenly the stone Jizō stood up, sat down before us with legs crossed, and strangely began to chatter away—what a comical sight! According to the transformed Jizō, Takahime-sama and Tsunehiko were surrounded by Morubando, climbed up into the branches of a great tree, and while they were in the midst of taking refuge, a swarm of savage beasts arrived and attacked them relentlessly. The Morubando are waiting beneath the tree, glaring with angry eyes. With their retreat cut off, even the brave Takahime-sama must be embracing Tsunehiko, sighing and wondering what on earth they should do. Haruhiko and Yobu, the two of us, having heard such a tale, how could we possibly leave them to their fate? Even if it be the lair of a great serpent or the habitat of the Morubando, we shall advance without fear or hesitation, for we cannot leave them without rescuing them. Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Hae Mase. By the light of the Ōkami, illuminate the Arika of Takahime-sama and show it to us. From now on, Takahime-sama will also reform from the depths of her heart, and even if she is called a pillar of the San-Go-Kyō or a messenger of God, she will become a soul not to be ashamed of, and surely she will perform great deeds. Open the door of her heart as quickly as possible, reveal her Arika, and let the two of us, Haruhiko and Yobu, know, O Amatsukami, Kunitsukami, and all the spirits of the land. Before the princess of the dragon world, I pray with a purified heart. Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Hae Mase. May your spirits be blessed.'

Singing thus, he inspected the roots of the great trees one by one, and while gazing up at the sky, he searched deeper and deeper into the forest.

Haruhiko and Yobu finally reached the vicinity of the great tree where Takahime had taken refuge, and just as the transformed stone Jizō had said, a Morubando with a body like a small mountain had chosen a spot where the forest trees were sparse, glaring at Takahime with angry eyes, and its ferocity was such that it was panting, trying to find a way to strike her dead with a single blow. Haruhiko turned pale and began to tremble, and his 'Kannagara Tamachi Hae Mase' and the heavenly counting songs became fragmented, making it impossible for him to emit any beautiful Kototama.

The Morubando seemed to have noticed that Haruhiko and Yobu had come near, for it slowly turned its mountain-like body toward the two, coiled its long, massive tail, and showed its intention to strike them down with a single blow. The two of them, fearing for their lives, scrambled toward a nearby large tree and finally climbed to the top, and for the moment, they clung to the top of the evergreen tree as a temporary refuge, hoping that someone might come to their rescue.

While on the tree, Haruhiko said with a trembling voice, 'Look here, Yobu-sama, is this not a terrible situation? I am not so afraid of lions, tigers, bears, wolves, or even great serpents, but no matter how much courage I muster, I cannot do anything about that Morubando. A natural trembling has come from the depths of my belly, and I have lost track of where my own body is. How about you, sir?'

Singing thus, with Yasuhiko in the lead, the four, Munehiko, Akiyamawake, and Morris, whether by the divine guidance of Kami, unexpectedly advanced near the tree shade where Takahime, Haruhiko, and others were taking refuge.

The two groups of refugees, Haruhiko and Takahime, heard this Sendenka from the trees; although it was a voice they had never heard even once, they thought, 'Surely these must be those who have been moved by the teachings of Kotoyoriwake and Kuniyoriwake and have entered the faith; ah, how grateful, how unashamed, the divine hand of salvation...' and suddenly recovering their spirits, they finished clapping, and from the trees, they began to recite the Amatsunorito in loud voices. At the sound of Haruhiko's Norito, Takahime and Tsunehiko, who were taking refuge in a tree far away, learned for the first time of Haruhiko's whereabouts, felt greatly encouraged, and with even more vigor, recited the Amatsunorito with all their might, as if to shake the very heavens and earth.

Yasuhiko and Munehiko's party, hearing the voices of the Norito from the trees coming from both sides, realized for the first time that Takahime's party was there, and their joy was boundless; they summoned even more energy and began to recite the Amatsunorito. The Maulband did not yield in the slightest, but glared with angry eyes, lashed its tail, and charged toward the four in every direction. The four immediately managed to take refuge in a large tree nearby, and while reciting the Norito incessantly from the trees, they prayed, 'May this monster quickly flee and save our party.'

Suddenly, a spherical great firelight, glowing as if to scorch the northwestern sky, appeared; the surroundings instantly turned red like fire. This was the spiritual light emitted by the Kami-tsukasa, Kotoyoriwake and Kuniyoriwake, from Mount Bōshi for the sake of rescue. The Maulband, startled, tucked its tail, hunched its neck, and slunk out into the forest, slipping through tens of ri of woods, and fled away to the Amazon River.

Yasuhiko's party of four breathed a sigh of relief, climbed down from the large tree with good branches, and while calling out, Yasuhiko: 'Takahime-sama, Takahime-sama, Takahime-sama,' they circled under the trees, looking up at the sky to search for Takahime's whereabouts.

Takahime, together with Tsunehiko, came down while feeling relieved. Haruhiko and Job also finally descended here from the hollow of a large tree, looked at each other's faces, and wept while embracing in tears of joy.

From this point, the eight held each other's hands, celebrated their safety, and while Takahime's party expressed deep words of gratitude to Yasuhiko's party, they turned back toward the banks of the Amazon River; since Yasuhiko had conveyed from Kuniyoriwake that Takayorihime's party was living in the forest on the south bank as the king of wild beasts, they proceeded toward the riverbank without delay. At the sound of the Amatsunorito which the party recited with all their hearts, the wild beasts of the northern forest vied to gather here, and following behind the eight, they followed in a line, numbering in the hundreds and thousands, all the way to the shore of the Amazon River.

From here, Takahime gazed at the rapid currents of the Amazon River and prayed with all her might, 'Please let us cross safely to the south bank...' Instantly, eight-fathom crocodiles appeared from the riverbed, countless in number, and overlapping one another, they immediately built a crocodile bridge. Takahime thanked the divine virtue of the Great Kami and the goodwill of the crocodiles one by one, and together with the seven, she managed to cross to the south bank. The width of the river was such that one could not tell how many ri it spanned.

The wild beasts that lived in the northern forest had escorted them this far, and though they showed a moment of hesitation while gazing at this torrent, they gained strength when the crocodile bridge was built, and without a single one remaining, they crossed over to the southern forest, escorted Takahime's party, and proceeded toward the capital of the rabbits.

高姫は常彦と共にヤツと安心し乍ら下つて来た。春彦もヨブも亦一つの大木の空より此処に漸く下り来り、互に顔を見合せ、嬉し泣きに抱き合ひて泣く。

是より八人は互に手を取り、無事を祝し、且つ高姫一行は安彦一行に向ひ厚く感謝の詞を述べながら、アマゾン河の沿岸に向つて引返し、南岸の森林中に鷹依姫一行が猛獣の王として住ひ居ることを、安彦を以て国依別より伝達したれば、取る物も取り敢ず、河端に向つて進む。一行が一心をこめて奏上したる天津祝詞の声に、北の森林の猛獣共は争つて此処に集まり来り、八人の後に従ひ、これ亦アマゾン河の岸迄、幾百千とも知れず、列を作つて従ひ来る。

これより高姫は、アマゾン河の急流を眺め、一生懸命に、南岸に無事渡らせ給へ……と祈念した。忽ち川底より八尋鰐、幾千万とも限りなく現はれ重なり合うて、忽ち鰐橋を架けたり。高姫は大神の神徳と鰐の好意を一々感謝し、七人と共に南岸に辛うじて渡ることを得たり。此間何里とも分らぬ位、広き河幅なりけり。

北の森林に棲める猛獣は此処まで送り来り、此激流を眺めて稍躊躇の色ありしが、忽ち鰐橋の架りたるに力を得、一匹も残らず、南森林に打渡り、高姫一行を送りて兎の都に向ひ進み行く。
