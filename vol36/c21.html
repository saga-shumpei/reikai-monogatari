Chapter 21: Fire の洗礼 [1009]

火の洗礼〔1009〕

§ 1

Dedicated with absolute devotion to King Sagaren, the incomparable kamutsukasa of peerless seichiu, starting with Anan and Seiru, together with Shirureng, the tsukasa of Yūzu, and amata dōshi, summoned an army of giyū, and riding upon the yain, swiftly as a bamboo-splitting force they infiltrated the fortress of the sacred kauji hidden by Riūn, unaware that there existed an otoshiana of Riūn's deep schemes, advancing rashly and falling into unexpected kikuwa, bound hand and foot, cruelly the four were thrown into a hitoya to spend their days morning and evening worrying and grieving over the fate of King Sagaren, whispering all manner of rumors.

At that very time, the magatsukami's raubangashira, the fellow Beru, strictly guarding the orders of the magatsu no kami, morning and evening monitored the personal surroundings of the four tsukasa, which was terrifying indeed. How suspicious it was—Riūn suddenly underwent a change of heart, and ordered that the four tsukasa be released from the hitoya as quickly as a single moment and rescued. The ugly Beru muttered complaints, swelling his face like a pumpkin, and came stomping right before the prison, engaging in all sorts of miscellaneous back-and-forth arguments. The four, all aligning their voices, declared: 'Under the direction of an evil god like Riūn, how could we possibly move from this place?'

Starting with you, you Riūn possessed by the magatsu no kami, unless you repent as quickly as a single moment, express the true way, and come before us with your tail lowered, we shall reject prison release no matter how long it takes—how resolute was their retort! Beru no tsukasa was astonished, pulled open the sturdy iron door, entered inside unawares, and before even a moment had passed of releasing the flying arrows of Kototama in a word or two, cruelly Beru was beaten by the fists like rain and hail from the comrades of Anan and others, struck regardless of the place, and just as he raised a piercing scream and begged for rescue, it resounded sharply in the ears of the gokusotsu who had come to inspect the prison.

The yatoto came running with high footsteps, and opening the door to rescue Beru, stepping in a pace or two, just as he looked around, Anan, Seiru, Shirureng, and the four of the Yūzu bound the two hand and foot, and leisurely marched out of the hitoya. Gritting their teeth and peeling their eyes wide with pointing mouths, shouting to the limit of their voices, 'The intruders have now escaped! You amata gokusotsu, bind those four swiftly!'—yet while they roared without any echo and made wry faces, the whole group glanced sideways, sneered, and said, 'The retribution of evil is just like this; show your repentance swiftly!'

Leaving these words behind, they hid themselves hither and thither within the castle grounds according to their own thoughts, to nowhere in particular. Suddenly a mountain blast came blowing, knocking down large trees, splitting branches, and ruthlessly blowing down buildings into a complete mess—how terrifying it was! From within the architectural structures inside the castle by the power of the wind, blazing and murky smoke gushed forth, and from within the smoke, flickered out crimson tongues, swallowing the mansion like a house of cards in the twinkling of an eye. Innumerable men and women hurried right and left, crying and shouting; it was the hellish path of abikukyōkan, a scene painful to even look upon.

Keerusu-hime and Riūn were wrapped in the tongues of flame, their breath blocked, hurrying in dark clouds, unsettled and clamoring, just as the roots of their breath were about to be cut now, suddenly a propaganda song was heard. Gougou, garagara, batabata—the sound of the mansion's ridge falling, the roaring of the mighty wind, the voices of the old and young to the limit of their breath calling for rescue, pressing upon the cries of kyōkan and coming to be heard. The valorous nature of that Kototama made them feel as though they were revived, and within their chests they looked up with joy, considering it the august rescue of the kami. Up to a place where they could not even distinguish an inch, enveloped in smoke and suffering, Keerusu-hime, unexpectedly, suddenly settled her heart,

and gazed fixedly at the ridge of the mansion burning cracklingly, having determined her resolve; contrary to this, however, Riūn was flustered and panicked, running around right and left, before and behind, hitting his head against the pillar, shouting: 'Aita-ta-ta-tsuta! Oh, it is painful! My breath is blocked, blood is oozing! Won't some loyal person come and rescue me?'—how pitiful his struggling and suffering.

§ 2

A Sendenka is heard close at hand.

When tuning its Kototama,

Beginning with King Sagaren,

Kimikohime of the Mizu no Mitama,

Master Tālchin and Kiyokohime,

Starting with Princess Kings,

Ames, Thelis, Zem, and Loose,

Yol, Letto, and Talen,

Their cool voices can be heard.

Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara,

Kami appears in the manifest world,

Establishing and separating right and wrong, good and evil.

The Kamunahohi who created this world,

And Ōnahohi of expansive heart.

Now, in human society, whatever happens,

Look upon it straight and listen straight again.

The fault of Master Riūntsukasa,

Right here and now is recast by fire.

Sendenshi serving the teachings

Of Ananai-kyō and Brahmanism,

I am the Kamutsukasa of Kimiko,

I am the Kami of Kuniwakehiko,

Receiving the power of Kami in our bodies,

We hole up within the castle of Kauji.

Recasting the spirits of Magatsu,

Administering the baptism of fire,

Burning and purifying all defilements,

Shōnetsujigoku instantly

Transforms completely into Takaamahara.

Desiring to transmit the teachings

Of the almighty and infinite Ōkami,

To promulgate benevolent government,

And to save and succor the populace in peace,

We have headed here.

Twisted Riūn,

Beginning with Princess Kels,

Teil, Halma, and all others,

Every single one of the hideous fiends,

Drive out the eighty Magatsu

Secreted within your breasts,

And return to the path of sincerity.

Since Kami dwells in supreme love,

No matter how twisted your hearts may be,

You who are such, certainly shall not

Take your lives in vain.

Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara,

Revering the grace of Kami,

Quickly, even within a single day, correct your inner hearts,

Purify your bodies,

And become your original mitama.

From today, the castle of Kauji

Has become a wondrous sacred ground

Ruled over by King Sagaren.

Anan, Seil, and Shirleng,

Bound within the prison,

Where are the masters of Yūzū now?

Quickly appear in this place,

Plunge into the smoke

To rescue those friends of theirs—

The Magatsu fleeing in bewilderment,

Suffering and agonizing—

And save them out, leaving not a single one behind.

Kami protects us,

And surely will generously protect

You, who are noble and pure.

Advance, advance, come advance!

What of the roaring flames spitting forth tongues of crimson?

We, who are fulfilling the will of Kami,

Are mighty warriors who shall never be burned by fire

Nor ever drown in water.

Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara,

May Mitama bless and prosper us.

King Sagaren stands amidst the black flames, chanting the Sendenka, commanding his numerous subordinates, and striving to rescue the men and women, young and old, inside the castle.

Kimikohime and Kiyokohime advance deep into the inner sanctum amidst the raging fires, resolved to die, hardening their resolve, and carrying the unperturbed Princess Kels tucked under their arms, they rescue her out to the castle's riding ground. Kiyokohime valiantly pulls and holds up Riūn, the mastermind of the demons who has lost his mind and is raving, and following Kimikohime, rescues him out to the castle's open square. Furthermore, the valiant warriors of supreme sincerity led by King Sagaren running through the smoke to rescue every single person without leaving one behind—this was truly known to be the divine assistance of Kami. From nowhere, the Sendenka is heard once more, entering the ears of both friend and foe alike with a deeply moving grace.

The true Kami who created and solidified this world has appeared,

Establishing and separating good and evil, right and wrong.

I am the Hokkō Kamutsukasa,

Reverently holding the divine words

Of Kami Susanoo no Ōkami,

Looking back from the Saienkang, far in the distance,

Traversing the plain of Fusa,

Journeying south through the land of Tsuki,

Crossing the sea to the island of Shiro,

Wishing to save the lives

Of King Sagaren, Kimikohime,

Master Riūntsukasa, and Princess Kels,

I have parted the cloud-paths and descended—

The one-eyed Kami of heaven am I.

The baptism of fire has been faultlessly concluded

According to the will of Kami.

The Yamata-no-Orochi, hideous foxes,

And the kindred of wicked fiends locked within this castle

Were struck with awe and fear by the baptism of fire,

Raised black clouds, summoned rain,

And scattered in flight as the wind blew.

Now the sacred ground of Kauji

Has been wiped clean of defilements and twists,

And has grown as pure as crystal.

Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara,

Receiving the divine water and fire of Kami,

And born forth into this world,

The human body, praised as the lord of all creation,

Being a thing equal unto Kami,

How could there be any twist within it?

Tormented in their pure hearts by the base desires of fame, rank, wealth, and fortune,

At last, incited by the demonic winds of matter-over-mind,

They become spirits of pure selfishness,

And their pure mitama are instantly

Injured, shattered, and utterly defiled.
