§ 1

Itsukau, Retto, Bitto, Ranchi, and Rūzu's party drank themselves into a stupor with the sake in the gourds, lost their footing, and collapsed right there on the spot, blowing out idle, drunken nonsense step by step from their sluggish, unmoving tongues. Drink sake and your legs give out, you make a fuss over nothing; don't drink and you commit evil acts, gamble, chase after women—they are nothing but hopeless goods no matter how you look at it.

Retto: 'Oi, kiyōdai, don't you feel things getting into a fine mood? The tongue is tangling just moderately, and the feet have floated up onto the ground as if riding in a boat. Since it has come to this, isn't there no longer any need to sweat it out ascending this steep slope and keep doing the continuing enterprise of tramping along? ...Ranzatsukotsupahi, rakukuwamijin, enjin rolling up the sky and scattering in the wind... I am a fellow who wouldn't budge an inch even if a great commotion like that were to break out. When the divine protection of the god of sake blesses us so neatly, I somehow found myself wanting to shed a tear of a single cup's sympathy upon the state of King Sagaren's person from the other night. It is quite enough to make a man of deep regrets and great talent arouse feelings of nostalgia. Why, the hangar of the stomach is fully packed, and the sake barrel within the belly is just like the phrasing of a norito... The jars and bottles are filled high, the bellies of the jars are lined up, partake of the ears of red millet, with awe and reverence we speak... it is just that sort of style. Hey, General Yoru, once it gets to be such a good mood as this, there is no longer any such thing as Yoru or Hīru. Shall we decide to take a nap right here, resting a kettle on a tree root? Because the character for pillow is written with the radical for wood and yuto. Eh, eputsu, garagaragaragara...'

Bitto: 'Ah, it stinks, it stinks, don't you understand a bit, turning upwind... It is not an August gale, but it is a nuisance like buckwheat. Somehow the foundation of my heart has begun to wobble, and I have come to feel so pitiful for the heart of King Sagaren-sama. No matter how much you say you will let us rise in the world, with the master of Ryūun changing his mind as quick as a cat's eyes every time you ask for this and that, isn't it a bit disheartening?'

Yoru: 'Kora kora, don't you understand in moderation? That is why I tell you not to drink too much sake. What a troublesome fellow you are. Carrying important business and yet drinking yourself into a stupor at the very critical moment, why didn't you drink after consulting with the inside of your belly? What an ignorant wretch!'

Bitto: 'Eh, don't talk so noisily. My legs are going to give out anyway. Whether my legs give out from fear of King Sagaren's majestic power, or whether my legs give out from being drunk on sake, either way I am fully equipped with the potential for my legs to give out. One who stands at the head of others should not catch hold of such trifling matters and fuss over them. How would it be if you made your belly a bit broader, made your liver bolder, and made your heart bigger? As the saying goes, if the head doesn't turn, the tail won't turn. Altogether, General Yoru, you talk big, but can your legs actually stand?'

Yoru: 'There are times when I stand with Yoru, and there are times when I do not stand. At any rate, one who is a general does not need to work by himself, but must ably entrust tasks to others, face the grand strategy without fretting or being bogged down by minor details, deduce the cleverness or stupidity, goodness or badness of his subordinates, and make each exert his own ability. What a person who is to be a general must work at is the magnanimity of a general. Even if I am an alcoholic and my legs cannot stand, I have the authority to command you fellows, so do not harbor such worries. You fellows simply need to follow the orders of this Yoru and perform the labor of dogs and horses. Eh eh, what are your faces? Just when I thought they were becoming square, they turn triangular, sticking seven, eight, or even ten eyeballs onto your faces, and even for an extraordinary hero and general like Lord Yoru, an encirclement attack of ugly faces does not feel all that pleasant. Won't you subordinates pull yourselves together a bit? What, when it comes to a situation of a thousand riders against one, crying out that your legs have given out, that King Sagaren is terrifying, and uttering such defeatist mournful sounds, bearing such a despairing sorrow—to think that we have dragged out such cowardly and pusillanimous subordinates, how could we not sink into the abyss of despair... Geh, utsu, pu, garagaragara, ah, it's painful, even the villain sake has started flowing backward up the great belly river!'

Retto: 'Hey, Commander Yōl, it is almost time for us to appear slowly. What kind of plan do you have to deal with it if rebels like Ames and Theris come out now? Just as they were talking thus, someone from behind the great rock was singing with a cool voice. Yōl: 'Look, how is this? They say Heaven favors justice, so taking pity on our loyal devotion, has not a Myōon Bosatsu appeared from one side of heaven, parting the eightfold clouds of heaven from behind this Thousand-Cho Rock, releasing a beautiful sound like that of a bell cricket or pine cricket to add to the excitement of the drink and bestow a new life upon our exhausted spirits? Yōl: 'Do not be so noisy.
