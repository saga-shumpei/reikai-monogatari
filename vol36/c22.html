Chapter 22: Spring の Snow [1010]

春の雪〔1010〕

§ 1

The castle of Kauji, having been miraculously baptized by fire, saw all the Akuma within disperse without exception, every structure reduced to uiu, the heavens clear and the breeze refreshing, the earth burning away all its jinkai to its very core, thus manifesting the Shiyabasokujiyakukuōdo of tenshiyōjiyō, chishiyōjiyō, jinshiyōjiyō, and rokukonshiyōjiyō.

Beginning with the righteous hitobito led by King Sagaren, the myriad hitobito who had remained within the castle, succumbing to the ishi of Riūn and unconsciously falling into jiyadau, all awoke from their stupor and gathered upon the spacious castle banba; shedding tears of gratitude for the divine virtue whereby not even a bishiyō was sustained upon their bodies, and beneath the guidance of the Mahito of heaven, to enshrine Kuniharutachinōokami, Shionagahiko no Ōkami, and Ōkunihiko no Ōkami, they offered up the Amatsu Norito before a hastily constructed altar, praised the shintoku, focused their prayers of repentance, devoid of any barriers between foe and friend, forgetting the differences of religion, and solely giving thanks for the divine grace, whereby the heavenly kingdom upon earth was forthwith constructed.

Although the castle of Kauji, through the baptism of fire, had been reduced to uiu without leaving a single thing remaining upon the earth, the Yahirodono on the opposite bank of the Kawamori River, which King Sagaren had newly constructed in readiness for an emergency, remained completely intact without a single person having yet moved into it; therefore, King Sagaren led all the hitobito and entered the new Yahirodono, arranged various foods and drinks offered by the hitobito of the capital rushing in advance as fire-disaster sympathy gifts to entertain everyone, and furthermore, with the Mahito of heaven, Yoshikohime, and Kiyokohime as the guests of honor, it came to pass that a banquet of gratitude and consolation was held.

Both Kerusuhime and Riūn also sat dejectedly, shrinking back in shame within a corner. Verily, is it not true that human nature is fundamentally good? Kerusuhime, having been temporarily led astray by the ki of ayakashi and having fallen into the trap of the schemes of evil-hearted Riūn, deeply repented from the bottom of her heart for that wicked deed of presumptuously ignoring and expelling Kunibrakuhiko no Kami, who was her own lord and master, and now sat crouching, holding her breath, and cowering in a modest corner, overwhelmed by her conscience in a state of utter wretchedness. Riūn, too, deeply regretted having been entangled by temporary greed, having become a captive of akki and jashin, and having repeated heinous and lawless atrocities; burdened by the terrifying pangs of remorse for the sins he had committed up to now, he had nowhere to hide his face, lacked the courage to look upon the hitobito, and sat cowering in a corner with lowered head. Hitherto, Riūn had been of large stature and corpulent build, appearing at first glance like a gentle and virtuous kami-man; however, along with his remorse for his own evil deeds, his being—from which the magakami that had soaked deeply into his mitama had finished escaping—suddenly shrank and withered, turning into the sorrowful sight of a changed, emaciated monk unpleasant to behold.

Pondering this, one is made to realize that all hitobito elevate their mitama or lower them depending upon the nature of tsukimono, unconsciously conducting themselves in various mixed and miscellaneous ways of good and evil, righteousness and wickedness. As the shinyu also indicates,

'If a kami of good guards and protects you, you perform only deeds of good; if an evil spirit guards and protects your physical body, you perform deeds of evil,'

it must be said this is indeed so. Moreover, an akuma never manifests with an evil aspect; it always dons the mask of goodness to dazzle people's eyes and attempts to brazenly carry out evil. The fact that even those hitobito who at first glance appear to be men of utmost rectitude and straightforwardness, or saints of gentleness and compassion, as well as males who appear as docile and modest as women, perform heinous and lawless deeds is fundamentally because evil kami possess them and use their mitama freely at will. Furthermore, among those hitobito who at first glance appear terrifying like ogres or akuma, there are also a vast number in whom the mitama of the true Kami actively operates, performing good deeds and virtuous acts. Therefore, with the weak visual power of human beings, one can by no means distinguish the good and evil, righteousness and wickedness of others. Judging people is ultimately a matter beyond the power of man to accomplish; the One who possesses the authority to judge this is Kami alone. § 2

'Kami appears on the surface and separates good from evil,'

It is thus declared. To rashly judge the good and evil, right and wrong of human beings is what is called usurping the authority of Kami, and from the righteous eyes of Kami, it is a grave and heinous crime. Moreover, there are times when a person whose shinkon is pure and whose conduct is upright, at a single glance, reveals the state of their heart and appears as a person of good who is supreme in goodness, beauty, and directness. Again, there are times when the physical body of a person whose heart is warped and defiled, committing wicked deeds, appears wicked and vile at a single glance. Since the yōbau of all people is a mirror of the heart, as the proverb says,

'If a thought is within, the color will surely appear without,'

There are also many who do not escape this shingen. However, fierce and ferocious jiyashin do not easily reveal their ugly form in the yōbau of the person they possess; on the contrary, they present a mien like that of a seijinkunshi, commit evil, afflict yobito, and take delight in this by themselves, and there are many such. Therefore, one must consider that it is utterly impossible to vainly look at the goodness, evil, beauty, or ugliness of a person's yōbau and appraise that person's goodness, evil, or character.

Changing infinitely, manifesting and disappearing without limit, and boldly carrying out evil deeds in broad daylight is what the devil excels in. The devil detests clarity and brightness, rejoices in darkness, and hiding in the shadows, delights in committing all manner of crimes and evils. However, this is the work to be done by ordinary devils. When it comes to great devils, it is not so; on the contrary, they openly run rampant in the clear and bright heaven and earth, delude the myriad folk, boldly carry out their evil deeds in the light of the sun, and rather, by people of dark hearts, are given the honorable titles of seijinkunshi, heroes, and men of valor, and proudly boast of it, and thinking yobito are easily dealt with, they turn to the shadows and slyly stick out their tongues—it is a world where one cannot say such do not exist in great numbers.

Tatsugumo, who once became a vessel of the devil, wielded tyrannical might in all directions, and faced multitudes of subordinates with the momentum of the rising sun in the morning sky, terrified by the divine might of the ara-mitama, vanished into the clouds and mist; thus he, who until now had been majestic in his wind and power, has now become completely like a different person, suffering mutations in various parts of his body, and has turned into a naturally inferior person of feeble and base character. Nevertheless, if this Tatsugumo should once again tread the path of justice and public righteousness, pile up faith, and bathe in the grace of Kami, it is natural that he should be bestowed with the shinkon of a seijinkunshi superior to before, and be transformed into a gentleman of warm, generous, and faithful character. Princess Kéers, stepping a step ahead of Tatsugumo, wiped away the baleful clouds of her heart, made the sun and moon of shinnyo shine in her shinkon, and came to repent of her past wrongs; thus, even finding herself in this place, she did not allow her shinkon to be shaken comparatively, and remained calm and composed while entrusting her whole self to Kami.

Tatsugumo stood up with a look of shame, chanted a song of repentance toward the assembled company, and fulfilled his sincerity of apology to the divine spirits of heaven and earth.
