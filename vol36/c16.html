Chapter 16: Gate 雀 [1004]

門雀〔1004〕

§ 1

At the outer gate of the castle of Kauji, the two gatekeepers, Shīru and Besu, in order to forget their excessive boredom and the heat, are sitting cross-legged, drinking sake sip by sip, and indulging in idle chatter.

Besu: 'Hey, Besu—no, Shīru—why on earth did Princess Kērusu rush back here with a changed expression, her sleeves corded in a crisscross fashion, her rear headband tightly bound, her skirts bare, clasping a large halberd under her arm, and with an idle look, scurrying right back to her original mansion? Doesn't she look just like a female warrior on a battlefield? Since I am dutifully keeping watch here at the gate along with the sake, why don't you step inside the mansion for a moment and go find out what is going on? I've started to feel a somewhat unsettling mood, you see.'

Shīru: 'That's true. Such an incomprehensible state of the Princess must have some reason behind it. Even if I don't know whether I'll get scolded, I am going a little way deep inside. As gatekeepers, our station does not permit us to ascend into the hall, but something extraordinary must undoubtedly have broken out in the inner chambers. If by any remote chance a major crisis should befall Lord Ryūun's person, we too cannot afford to remain leisurely and unconcerned. Even if I get scolded I don't care; I'm stepping right into the depths. The situation will probably become clear in detail afterwards.'

Having said thus much, Shīru—no, Besu tied a white headband, crisscrossed his sleeves with cords, tucked up his skirts, and unceremoniously advanced toward the inner palace. Catching a glimpse of Keriya's injury, and the scenes of astonishment of Ryūun, Princess Kērusu, and Tēru, while peeking at the general situation, he muttered that at this rate there shouldn't be anything too serious, just a dream-like commotion, and as he was about to briskly turn on his heels, Princess Kērusu, sharp-eyed, caught sight of Besu's figure and raised her voice shrilly,

Princess Kērusu: 'Are you not the gatekeeper Besu? Breaking the taboos of the mansion, what have you come here to such deep inner chambers for?'

Berating him thus, Besu trembled while scratching his head,

Besu: 'Ha-ha-ha, tr-tr-truly I have no excuse to offer. In truth, beholding the extraordinary state of Princess Kērusu, I thought some rare and unusual event must have suddenly broken out in this inner palace. However humble a gatekeeper I may be, I cannot simply refrain from saving my lord from peril. Though I am fully and thoroughly aware of the strict prohibition against entering the hall, in such an emergency I cannot possibly observe such rules. Without fail, I had to help the two of you, so I consulted with Shīru and came all the way here disregarding any impropriety. I beg for your gracious pardon.'

Princess Kērusu: 'Such conduct of yours is hard to forgive. Ordinarily, this should not be passed over as it is, but in consideration of your loyal devotion in thinking of us, I shall overlook it for this once only. Guard the gate carefully and strictly. Furthermore, you must not speak a word of this state of affairs in the inner palace to anyone. I strictly place a gag on your tongue.'

Besu: 'Yes, thank you very much. Assuredly, assuredly, even if my head should fly off I will never speak of it, so please be at ease.'

Ryūun raised her voice shrilly,

Ryūun: 'Besu, quickly retreat from this place!'

'Yes,' replied Besu, and left the spot. Keriya, with his foot slashed by the Princess, emitted cries of anguish and groaned, 'Un, un.' Tēru, still having lost the use of his hips, kept rotating his eyeballs with extreme speed like a gyrocompass, alternating between white and black.

The defiled-hearted Ryūun Became a pet favorite, morning and evening, And the young Tēru who served closely Entered a chamber to reverently read the divine writings of the Ural religion, Unawares falling into a dream; The crooked demon lurking within the heart, Roamed about freely at will, And there are times when it traces the dream road. At the outer gate of the castle of Kauji, The sounds of men and horses are terribly fierce, It is the war cry of attacking enemies; Startled by the wonder of it, leaping up, Climbed the railing of the mansion, And if one looks fixedly across the view below, The Sagaren King whom hitherto he had relied upon as lord, Is bravely riding upon a coin-patterned dappled horse, Putting on a saddle with golden rings.

§ 2

With horses neighing gallantly, waving their saihai, they close in. Following them, upon aokage and kurokage mounts, who are these valiant tsuhamono? Fixing his gaze to observe, they are the two yūshi Tērisu and Eimusu. Brandishing their golden saihai, with stern gechi, the tsuhamono surge forth like the tide. Ah, crying that it is an ichidaiji, he dashes down, leading sujifu of yūshi, and charges into the enemy. Relying on their numbers, the enemy presses on, but even they are hekieki; like the waves of the shore rolling and returning, the hatairo appears unfavorable.

Yet, echoing beneath their knees, the battle cries resound. A taigun like unka, deploying in tanteijin, despising their shōsū mikata, charges like Ashiyuraō. The mikata are heukan ketsushi warriors; gauketsu who even break kishin—how must it have been? Clatter, clatter, pierced by sharp blades, they fall effortlessly on the spot. Tērusu, astonished, all alone, secretly enters deep within the gates, and locking the gates, swift as can be, makes his way straight toward the living quarters of Kērusu Hime and Riūn. The enemy has drawn close; quickly flee from this place and make preparations for another day, he speaks with plausible earnestness. Taking a daydream for reality, Riūn changes the very color of his face in a flash, instantly losing the use of his legs on the spot, trembling and quaking—how amusing it is!

Strongest in a time of crisis is a woman's single-minded heart. Kērusu Hime stands up, snatches the naginata from the nageshi, runs out toward the front, and without leaving behind even a single horseman of the oncoming enemy, mows them down, displaying a woman's bravery. Thinking that now is the very time to rush forth and step out, Keriya of Samori enters. To this peaceful and safe castle, why on earth, Hime-sama, are you in such a grand and exaggerated guise? Stop yourself, he says. Thereupon, Hime angrily and instantly mows down Keriya's legs. Crying out with a single 'Ugh!', she falls on the spot—how pitiful! Kērusu Hime, possessed by desperation,

tightening her rear hachimaki resolutely, crossing her tasuki in twain, declares, 'Formidable foes are indeed come! I shall teach you a lesson right now!' and rushes out like a fierce tiger. Now how is this? Before the gate, there is not even the sound of a softly blowing wind; heaven and earth are as calm as if asleep, and an atmosphere of quietude drifts. Hime, unable to bear her suspicion, returns once more to the inner chambers of the mansion, feeling at a loss. When she investigates the situation closely, it was all an empty commotion born of Tērusu's dream. Her tightly strung soul goes slack, and Kērusu Hime turns upon Tērusu in his office, blaming him for his impropriety. The panicker who dreamt a dream within a dream, Tērusu, scratching his head,

feeling as if he had barely been pardoned and escaped the jaws of death, smoothing down his chest—how comical it is! Though there are no ogres in this world, tormented by the ogre of the murakimogokoro created by one's own self, is only Tērusu suffering and afflicted? Kērusu Hime and Riūn alike, hurting their hearts just the same, lose their composure and mannerly deportment, and tremble in fear—how terrifying.

Fortunately, the wound from Keriya's naginata was shallower than expected; after applying clay to the cut and wrapping it with a bandage, after a few days he recovered to the point where he could barely manage to walk about the palace.

From this time onward, incomprehensible events occurred continuously within the castle, and Riūn, beginning with all the officials high and low, spent their days in trembling anxiety without a moment of peace, living in loneliness.
