Chapter 6: 意外な答 [994]

意外な答〔994〕

§ 1

Anan said farewell to the dōshi of Sāru, Sēru, and Uinchi, met with Tērisu, and determined to plan an internal and external cooperation so as to capture Riūn at one stroke, and appeared near Kaujijiyō in a leisurely manner.

Mēru and Yōru, both of them, suddenly captured Anan and cast him into prison.

Anan had been confident that Tērisu should remain the warden of the prison, but who could have expected that the villain Bēru had somehow replaced him.

Riūn also had firmly trusted Tērisu, relying upon him as his right arm and hand, and had ordered him to be the warden of the precious prison; yet, thinking that he too was a villain who might perchance communicate with the righteous faction and bring about some unforeseeable deed, Riūn, skilled in cunning, had caused Bēru to take his place.

Once things had come to this, Anan was just like a moth flying into the fire.

He could not but intensely fear that all his plans would hereby be utterly confounded and mismatched.

Anan secretly chanted within the prison, and that song goes:

'He who made heaven and earth, in the age where the true Kami abides, what manner of thing is there to fear? The light of the stars sparkles in the heaven, the earth grows lush and green, the mountains and rivers are pure and beautiful, lotus flowers bloom and send forth their fragrance far and near in the Land of Kami. We are the children of Kami, the shrines of Kami; how could we ever commit crookedness? Come what torments may encounter us, entrusting both heart and body to the Sumera-mikami, I am the one who fulfills the sincerity of faith, and Kami will surely deign to survey it. Though once vexed by enemies and encountering a hundred tortures, before long the gate of Kami opens. This Shiro Island is the land where the sovereign, King Sagaren, dwelling eternally, shall rule the subjects with utmost peace and tranquility, the Land of Kami. Though Riūn, the kami-governor of the road of Urali, wields whatever violence, how can a crooked kami ever prevail against righteousness? Even a bird reared in a cage, before long breaking forth, shall have a time when it leisurely sings of spring in the vast august gardens of heaven and earth. Ah, Kannagara, Kami, Kami! May the spirit of Kami bring bliss, and beginning with King Sagaren, may you peacefully guard the souls of those thick in loyalty such as Tārutin, Princess Kingusu, Zemu, Ēru, Ēmusu, Yūzu, and Shirureng, people who are precious and righteous.

Even though Anan is captured by the enemy and spends bitter months and days, if it is for the sake of the sovereign, for the sake of the country, and for the sake of the people, how could I ever begrudge my life? Whichever enemy may appear—water-torture and fire-torture are not yet enough—even if I encounter the tortures of the sword, this very life is dedicated to the sovereign. Imprisoned in the gaol of Kauji on the broad island of Shiro, my body is a caged bird beyond my control; yet my heart is pure and clear, roaming freely and at will through the realm of heaven and earth, and the august light of the Sumerahikami shines upon my body, and joy wells up and fills my breast. Whether the morning sun shines or is clouded, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even though the great earth should sink,

Having made the ugly governors possessed by the Yamata-no-orochi turn their words hither, my heart shall forever guard this Land of Kami. In life I shall become the shield of the imperial land, in death I shall become the guardian demon of the country, and the joy of piercingly maintaining sincerity alone throughout heaven and earth! Though the eyes of Bēru shine, how can the body of a man, which cannot see at night, ever probe and find out our innermost hearts? Kannagara. Turning your backs upon the teachings of Kami, yearning for temporary glory, fawning and flattering upon the demon kami, aiming only at the prosperity and advancement of your own single body—how despicable! Thinking and thinking upon it, Bēru indeed is truly a pitiable person! Ah, Kannagara, Kami, Kami,

May your august spirits deign to bestow bliss!'

Ēmusu, moreover, being within the prison, is privately expressing his reflections, and that song goes:

'Kunitokotachi no Ōmikami, who created and solidified heaven and earth,

Kami Susanō no Ōmikami, who appeared as the power of the great earth,

Mikatsuhiko no Ōmikami, who appeared as the spirit of the great earth—

The Mizuho Province which they deign to guard,

And preeminently the precious island of Shiro.

This Land of Kami is a wondrous land

Flourishing and advancing through the exceptionally profound august grace of heaven and earth.

King Sagaren, who rules this Land of Kami,

Dwells as the master of the common folk, dwells as their teacher,

And dwells as their parent, an austere kami.

His lofty august majesty—even the mountains of the Himalayas are nothing, and passing clouds hesitate in their journey—is the wondrous king.

Riūn of the road of Urali, having been led astray by his ungrounded schemes—'

Though you now suffer for a while, How could Kami ever forgive Kērusu-hime and Riūn's Cruel and lawless deeds, Crimes so monstrous Tenchi cannot contain? Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! Kami shall appear in the open, Distinguish between good and evil, right and wrong, Destroying the Magami and saving the Zenshin Before our very eyes. Though we are thrown into a hitoya And meet with mugen sufferings, Our hearts are like the wide plain of Ama, Like birds flying through the empty sky. No matter how lawless Riūn may be, The magawaza of binding and tormenting Our pure souls, No matter how much he exhausts his heart, Could tautei never be performed. With the brilliance of jitsugetsu Shining across the sky of the heart, Men of seigi, How could we ever fear a hitoya? Kami sits together with us. Before the presence of the Jizaiten of Brahmanism And Ōkunihiko, Purifying our hearts and cleansing our bodies, That as soon as possible our King's Heart may be set at ease and his suffering Be relieved, Kamunagara! Close beside the Sagaren King, Born of the miiki of Kami, Serving as a right guardian deity, Erusu, the master of the Way, Secretly, secretly prays. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! May your mitama bless and prosper us,'

Chanting thus, just at that moment, Bēru appears on the scene with heavy footsteps, and from outside the hitoya says,

Ēmusu-sama, just now from Riūn-dono, since the suspicion against you has been entirely cleared, the word is to forgive you. Because you are a distinguished person who saved Riūn-sama's peril, please leave this place quickly.'

So he says in a softened voice and with an attitude that has changed to extreme politeness, whereupon Ēmusu shakes his head from side to side and says,

That is truly, indeed, much obliged. However, I no longer think of going out into society to work. Therefore, since this villa is unusually comfortable for me, please ask Riūn-dono to keep me in the hitoya for a long time. Because it is a place I have grown so used to staying in, I have come to hate to leave it. Aha-ha-ha-ha.'

This is astonishing, Ēmusu-sama. To say that the inside of a hitoya is splendid, do you really mean that from the bottom of your heart?'

I hope to spend my whole life right here. Even if I were tautei forgiven by Riūn-sama, or perhaps employed for some official duty, an unlucky person remains unlucky no matter where he goes, and again, even if I were able to do something like helping a person and being thrown into a prison, it would amount to nothing; so if I stay right here like this, without making sins or troubling my heart, it is splendid. Please release me and grant me not to suffer any more hardships above this. Somehow, by my nature, I cannot do bad things. Human beings who can calmly and skillfully carry out bad things will steadily advance in the world through Riūn-sama's patronage, while I, who saved a life and did a good deed, am cast into a hitoya—such is a topsy-turvy world, so tautei there is no place for me to go out into society and be active. The reason I say I want to be kept here forever is just for that very reason, so please convey this matter to Riūn-dono without taking it amiss.'

This is also a strange opinion; Bēru cannot comprehend it at all. Anyway, I shall go and ask Riūn-sama once.'

So saying, he strides briskly away from the scene.
