Chapter 12: 無住居士 [1000]

無住居士〔1000〕

§ 1

In the valley of Matsūra, the village of Koito is a place of extremely perilous terrain, where on one side lies a deep valley of a thousand jō, flowing north and south, and a natural, massive cavern is pierced into the slope of the rocky mountain. To reach this cavern, one must leap back and forth across narrow rock paths, barely managing to cross it; it is a naturally strategic stronghold of such a nature that if a single soldier defends it, ten thousand soldiers cannot pass through it. The place where Tomohiko of Brahminism once built a hermitage with Koitohime, established his teachings, and resided was an extremely flat location four or five chō before this cavern, past which the Hosotanigaha flows.

King Sagaren built an improvised mansion on this flat ground, had Thēris, Aimsu, and others guard it, and deep within the cavern himself, he was plotting a heaven-turning strategy. Starting with the reform faction of Ryūun's subordinates—Yōru, Bitto, Retto, and Rūzu—who had been waiting with bared limbs to capture the King's entourage near the great boulders of the valley path, Sāru, Winchi, Zemu, Eru, Tārchin, and Princess Kingsu frequented the space between this mansion and the cavern, stopping there for a while, and troubled in heart and body for the King's sake.

Both Thēris and Aimsu gathered numerous subordinates at the mansion on the flat ground, trained in martial arts, and set about preparing to subjugate Ryūun. Thither an old man of strange appearance with white hair, step by step making the sound of his staff, climbed up the rocky path, stood before the mansion, and earnestly chanted the sacred texts of Brahminism. From the very first glance, Aimsu courteously invited the old man into the mansion, provided him with hot water and food, and as they stayed up late into the night talking about various matters, it eventually came to passing that he inquired into the old man's background.

Aimsu: 'Moshi, for an elder like you to climb up this mountain path and moreover make a journey without a traveling companion must mean there is some deep circumstance. If it is no trouble, I should like you to give a general account.'

'I am called Mujū Kyōshi; I know not the place where I was born, I know no parents, I have no children—in other words, I am a vagrant of the world. Hearing on the way that at this mansion distinguished people of Brahminism have gathered and are practicing martial arts, although I have grown as old as this, martial arts are my great favorite, and I should like to gaze upon your practice ground.'

Aimsu: 'Mujū-sama, you have come all the way hither surely not merely to see the practice of martial arts.'

'To say I want to see the martial arts practice is a mere polite greeting toward you all. In truth, hearing of the grave crisis of King Sagaren and that you are hiding in this mansion, I have sought him out from afar.'

'With what intention do you seek that King? That is what I wish to hear!'

With these words, growing slightly agitated, he unknowingly raised his voice and questioned him.

'Aha-ha-ha-ha, to be trampled by an evil god like Ryūun, abandon a stronghold that might be called impregnable, effeminately flee with two subordinates to such a place out of thrift for life, hide your body within a cavern like a rock bee or something, and make preparations for a comeback—is this not an utterly foolish and absurd way of doing things? If preparations are completed on this side, corresponding preparations ought to be made on the opposing side as well. If you intend to achieve your objective, first of all, with a swiftness that allows not a hair's breadth of time, you must make a sudden attack, and ultimately there is no prospect of victory. Now is the time when Ryūun is riding on victory, arrogant, and has almost lost his common sense; if you do not raise an affair at this very juncture, while you are gathering powerless allies over a long and distant time, the other side will also gradually awaken, establish even stricter security, accumulate defensive power, and if things go awry, it is hard to tell whether they might come attacking in a single stroke with a massive army like clouds and mist. No matter how steep and well-guarded the stronghold may be, if you are besieged by the enemy for a long time, the water supply will be cut off, provisions will starve, and you will have to surrender while sitting idly. Without even this much thought, how can you ever hope to subjugate Ryūun, who is skilled in crafty wisdom? Moreover, in a world where there are enemies even among allies, you would do well to be thoroughly careful.'

A certain person: 'Indeed, what you say is very reasonable; however, since our comrades are all loyal warriors without peer toward the King, to say nothing of outsiders, such vile creatures should surely not be mixed among us. Does it look that way to your eyes?'

Another person: 'Hahahaha! You are young, so young! In truth, a spy is mingling right here. If you do not even notice such a thing, no matter what purpose you set up, you will be overthrown by ninety-nine percent.'

The first person: 'Whom do you mean by that spy?'

Another person: 'I will not mention that here now. Without enough discernment to see through such a spy, it is totally hopeless. Take a good look at how the people coming and going in this hall use their eyes, how they walk their feet, and how they move their bodies! You will be able to tell right and wrong at a single glance.'

Amesu shows a look of sighing, clasps his hands together, bows his head, and is lost in thought.

Terisu opens his mouth for the first time and says,

Terisu: 'Muju-sama, will this plan of ours succeed completely? If you have any divine strategy, I would like to ask for your instruction.'

Muju: 'Hahahaha! Do you ask to be told whether it will succeed or not? With such uncertain and flimsy things, how can a great enterprise be accomplished? First of all, the placing of your minds is wrong. To use your body and mind for the sake of loyalty to King Sagaren is truly the height of feeling as a subject. But, however, do you know that there is someone more noble than King Sagaren? If you do not understand that, this time's purpose will, I am sorry to say, end entirely in a painted mochi. No, it is bound to become the cause of inviting a great disaster. Rather than that, it is a shorter cut to take off your armor while there is still time, bear a thorny whip under the knees of Ryūun, and offer your surrender. Hahahaha!'

So saying, he shakes his shoulders and laughs loudly. Terisu is not convinced by Muju's words in the slightest, and presses him hard, asking breathlessly,

Terisu: 'We understand that in this land of Shiro, there is no one more noble than King Sagaren. Who is the person more noble than the King? Please grant us your instruction.'

Muju: 'You must be a priest of Brahmanism and at the same time a subject of the King. While submissive to Anagama, out of a weak-kneed thought such as telling one to follow the current times, you must have converted to Brahmanism patronized by King Sagaren in order to plot your own worldly success, did you not? Well then, is there any mistake in what this Muju says?'

Terisu: 'Yes, exactly as you command. However, I have never, ever abandoned Anagama from the bottom of my heart. Since the path of any god is originally one root, I understand that regarding our actions there is not the slightest contradiction toward the gods, but...'

Muju: 'Entering any path makes no difference to the path of truth. There is probably no particular blame in that matter. Nevertheless, if you are going to devote your true heart to that extent and strive for the King, why do you not devote your true heart for the sake of the supreme and highest god? Have you forgotten the true essence of the teaching that god is first? With such a state of mind, no matter how much thousand-fold thought and myriad hardships you endure, it is utterly hopeless. If your mind is to lean upon the divine power of god and assist King Sagaren, a scoundrel like that Ryūun would hardly be worth counting. If only the true divine power is possessed, it is the natural consequence that someone like Ryūun will self-destruct by natural force, just like exposing ice to the sun. What are you suffering for, gathering numerous comrades and practicing martial arts of slaughter? No matter how skilled martial arts become, they can only amount to the work of one person against one person. By virtue of the infinite and absolute power of god, if true divine power is possessed upon your soul, you should have nothing to fear even against the spirit of one country or against the spirits of hundreds of millions of innumerable people; and furthermore, if divine power is completely possessed, you will be able to oppose the power of hundreds of millions of innumerable people with the power of you alone, and oppose the bodies of hundreds of millions of innumerable people with the body of you alone, and thus well be able to carry through your purpose.'

Thēris: 'Reverberating teachings, deeply soaked into my heart, I thank you with profound gratitude. Following this, in the inner grotto dwells King Sagaren, so I shall guide you. I earnestly beg for an audience once.'

'I recognize no necessity whatsoever to grant an audience to a king. If the king desires an audience with me, I shall spare a moment of time.'

Ēmusu: 'Though I know not which noble personage you may be, do not speak so rigidly, and please do come forth before the King. The King will surely rejoice greatly...'

'Ahahahaha, that is precisely where the contradiction lies. I am the Sendenshi of Tenka, straddling Godaishiu, the messenger of Kami who grants Eienmukiu life to the Fukiufumetsu souls of Banmin. To visit, of my own accord, such a King Sagaren who can scarcely govern a mere Shōkoku—that is the very height of Tenchi tentau. King Sagaren merely plays the role of protecting and supervising the brief Seimei of the human nikutai in this Shimaguni. His ruling authority over spiritual souls is utterly nonexistent. While such an unremoved spirit of matter-over-spirit remains, no matter what divine host you raise, subduing the demonic dragon-clouds is quite unthinkable. Already I have no business in this place, farewell...'

Before these words were fully uttered, as he hastily sought to depart, both Thēris and Ēmusu in a panic pulled at his sleeves and stayed him,

'Prithee, Muju Koji-sama, pray wait a moment. As we have just heard, you stated you are the Sendenshi of Tenka; if you are a Sendenshi, I beseech you to pity our loyal sincerity and teach us the best method. And of what religion are you the Sendenshi? That much we would fain hear in a single word.'

'As for subduing demons like dragon-clouds, there is neither argument nor nonsense to be had. It is utterly sufficient simply to cast off the attachments, pride, and self-conceit lurking in your hearts, and return purely to the right path of Kannagara. Not a single plan or anything else is needed. Ahahahaha.'

So saying and casting the words aside, he again shook off his sleeves and made to depart. Thēris, on the verge of tears, knelt down, and firmly grasping Muju's staff, cried out,

'Ēmusu, quickly go and welcome the King hither. If Muju Koji is allowed to return now, it will be as though we lost the rudder and oars of a boat navigating through the dark night. Come, quickly, quickly...'

As she urged him on, Ēmusu nodded, and hurriedly traversed the perilous rock wall, hastening toward the grotto.

Muju: 'To waste precious time, even for the duration of a single breath, is truly inexcusable before the Kami of Tenchi. Already there should be no further need for Muju; return well to your true hearts, see and hear purely and directly, and consult with your own hearts.'

Thēris: 'Yes, these repeated teachings, we truly thank you for them. Following this, I beg you to wait for a little while, until the King comes hither...'

『Does King Sagaren think an audience with me can be attained with such a spirit? There is a limit to such misapprehension. Can you perceive my true identity?』

Thēris: 'Alas, I cannot distinguish whether you are a Kami or a Sendenshi. I beseech you, I earnestly beg you for a short delay.'

So she pleaded, joining her palms, while hot tears streamed down her cheeks.

§ 4

Atone for sins and take upon thy body chiyorozu of sufferings, conform to the ōji of Kami, and manifest thy magokoro; Kami is with thee, yet though it be said so, how could Kami protect thy heart? In the mitama protected by Kami there is neither dust nor cloudiness; the sky of the heart is brightly illuminated by the light of jitsugetsu, the wind of heiwa blows toha, flowers fragrance, birds sing, construct the kensetsu of Kami's country rich in harvest within the world of the heart, place thy body outside of uchiu, heigei shinrabanshiyō, and if thou returnest to the origin of the heart, thou art mohaya the shrine of Kami, and shalt become the mitama of Kami.

Ah, kamunagara, kamunagara, reverence the ōji of Kami, advance, advance, you kamuzukasa who uphold the oshihe of Brahmin, and you hitobito who follow it; I leave this hitokoto of this rauō facing the parting; ah, kamunagara, kamunagara, settle the murakimo of our form and well comprehend it; although I think I should like to wait until the coming of King Sagaren, there is no way to return the power of time to the origin forever; come, come, farewell, come, farewell, you futari of sincere kamuzukasa, we must part sleeves here.'

When it seemed as if he spoke thus, like a hichō, making his rōku no trouble of things, quickening his steps, he had already vanished into the nōmu. I wonder of what nanigami this raujin is indeed the transformation?
