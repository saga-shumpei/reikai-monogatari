Chapter 13: Blessing の Flower [1001]

恵の花〔1001〕

§ 1

After the white-haired old man who called himself Mujiyūkoji departed in great haste, Theris folded his arms, bowed his head, and stood lost in thought. Even to the ears of Theris—who until this very moment had been filled with a valiant, vigorous spirit of martial valor capable of swallowing the Kogaikokatsu—the sound of bamboo swords clashing with the cries of 'Ya, E, Tō' began to echo with a strange melancholy. Compared to the infinite and boundless power of Kami, the art of fencing between man and man somehow felt devoid of power, and he could not help but feel a lingering sense of contempt floating within himself. Theris looked around the four quarters, saw that no one was around, and spoke to himself in a soliloquy.

Ah, what Kami was the incarnation of that white-haired old man who suddenly appeared here as if drifting on the wind and called himself a sendenshi? Or of what teaching was he a powerful sendenshi? Truly, his instruction felt as though it were the direct manifestation of Ōkami... Come to think of it, what a terrible misunderstanding I have been harboring until now. While hearing the path of the non-resistant Izumishima-no-michi since my childhood days, I forgot the great august mind of Kami, attempted to meet violence with violence, and sought to subjugate Ryūun, possessed by a demon—how foolish of my heart was that, nay, how ruthless. Soldiers are what are called instruments of killing. A short while ago, I tried to subjugate that Ryūun in a single sweep, had numerous subordinates equipped with heavy armor, burst in through the front gate of Shinjijō Castle, and intended to afflict the enemy, yet instead I ended up wounding and killing my own allies; this is repeatedly the height of folly, the ultimate in ineptitude, a slaughter committed for which even regret is of no avail. Having thus damaged the lives of my subordinates and killed the beloved children of the Great Deity of Heaven and Earth, how could I possibly ever subjugate that Ryūun? No matter how lawless Ryūun might be, he threw Taruchin, Princess Kings, and others into prison and made them suffer, yet he provided them with adequate food and drink and did not inflict physical harm upon their bodies—that was truly a remarkable way of doing things. My heart is fiercer than his, deeper than that of a jackal and wolf; out of an obsession with the King and hatred for him, I sought to exterminate the evildoers following Ryūun root and branch to cut off the nation's calamity, yet in the end I was unable to injure even a single enemy, while fully thirty percent of my allies suffered death or injury—this must surely be an admonition from Heaven. That Kami should appear outwardly and make a clear distinction between good and evil must be this very thing. Ryūun is also a great criminal whom heaven and earth cannot contain, but I am likewise a great criminal inferior to none. Yet, self-styling myself as a loyal retainer and righteous warrior, how shallow was my heart in planning this subjugation! The command within the old man's words—to cast off self-conceit!—was undoubtedly taught with such force for this very reason. Kami harbors not a particle of partiality. Loving all human beings in the world and the myriad things of creation equally, I failed to realize such a merciful and great august mind; using my self-licensed sincerity as a shield, I attempted to commit crimes even worse than Ryūun's, and how shameful it is that I proudly nurtured soldiers and practiced the martial arts. Starting with King Sagaren, if we truly comprehend the great august mind of Kami and exhaust our sincerity in a manner pleasing to Kami, how could Kami of infinite and absolute power possibly fail to aid us? Ah, I have erred, I have erred... Kuninotokotachi-no-Ōkami, Toyokunihime-no-Ōkami, Takehaya-Susanoo-no-Ōkami, grant me your forgiveness! Omikami nuru sawayamase...

With these words, lost in a flood of tears, he spent his time in prayer and supplication.

To such a place, Eames traversed the extremely perilous rock walls, followed King Sagaren, and briskly entered before this hall. Looking anxiously around the four quarters and feeling suspicion upon failing to see the figure of the former old man, he turned to Theris and spoke.

Oh, Lord Theris, we have come to welcome His Majesty. Where might that old man be?

Up until now, a myriad of emotions had surged within Theris's breast, plunging him into tears of remorse and making him almost forget that his own body was even in this place. Yet at this voice from Eames, he started as if snapping back to awareness, looked around the four quarters, gazed upon King Sagaren, bowed respectfully to lower his head, and finished making a single salutation.

§ 2

Sagaren-ōsama, oh, how wonderfully you have graced us with your presence! An extraordinary elder appeared here in a detached manner, bestowed various precious teachings, and even I, Taelis, have repented of my past foolishness as if for the very first time... Since His Majesty the King is about to grace us with his presence right now, please wait a moment... though I used every possible courtesy to implore him, the elder who called himself Mujiyūkoji said: 'I am a Sendenshi of the tenka, so I cannot afford to waste a single moment of time'... and no matter how much I tried to stop him, he would not listen, brushed aside his sleeve, and departed like lightning. Having gone out of your way to come all the way here, this is truly an inconvenience for which I have no words to express, but I humbly beg your utmost forgiveness."

Sagaren-ō: 'Didst thou glean aught from the elder's words? Wilt thou not recount them in detail to me for reference?'

Taelis: 'Words cannot express how gladly I shall do so.'

And as he meticulously expounded the former elder's teachings step by step, omitting not a single word, before the King, the King tilted his head and folded his arms, lost in thought for a while, but at length nodded his head up and down repeatedly,

"'Indeed! Exactly so!'"

And saying thus, tears flowed in torrents down his cheeks.

Ames: 'Since we have listened to the elder's teachings and repented from the bottom of our hearts, there is no longer any need for grandiose martial practice. Simply conforming to the Great Way of Tenchi-Kaminagara, imitating in our divine devotion the infinite mercy and great august heart of the supreme deities, and making love and sincerity our primary weapons, we shall fight. Lord Taelis, what are your thoughts on this?'

"'If Your Majesty so consents, as of this very moment we shall abolish martial arts practice, and as our very first endeavor, we shall set about polishing our souls.'"

So he spoke with a bewildered expression. Sagaren-ō, smiling gently, accompanied Ames and returned once more to the interior of the original cavern.

Later, Taelis appeared at the martial arts training ground, stood upright in a slightly elevated place, faced everyone, and declared:

"'From this very day and hour, martial arts practice must be totally abolished! You must obey the King's command, and from this very day and hour, purify your hearts, purify your bodies, receive with reverence the great august heart of the infinitely merciful Great Deity, and perform the discipline of sincerity alone!'"

When he sternly issued this command, a man named Chiru, the most proficient in swordsmanship among them all, came forth before Taelis and pressed him closely, breathing heavily and with a slight flush of anger:

"'This is, this is quite unexpected, even from our master's lips! While facing a formidable enemy right before us, why on earth do you abolish the martial arts, which are of the utmost and vital importance? Martial arts exist to protect the country, and a nation's actual strength relies upon martial arts above all else. Yet, what madness possesses you to issue such a command?'"

Taelis replied with utter detachment:

"'When one calmly reflects, under the heavens there are neither enemies nor allies. All enemies arise entirely from within our own hearts, and gradually growing, they finally lead to the destruction of our very selves. When the sun and moon of compassion shine brightly in the heart, heaven and earth are clear and serene, free from even a speck of dark cloud or turbidity. Thus, what we call enemies and what we call allies all spring from the delusions of the heart.'"

As he casually tossed out these words, Chiru retorted:

"'As you say, personal enemies may naturally vanish depending solely on one's state of mind. Even so, what of that great villain Ryūun, who—reverence be fitting—has plotted rebellion against Sagaren-ō, the divine administrator of the capital of the sacred land? Is he not the King's enemy? As loyal subjects of the King, how can we possibly overlook this? I earnestly beg you to reconsider.'"

"'Indeed, as thou sayest, Ryūun is truly a wicked and lawless scoundrel, a mortal enemy to our sovereign. For subjects in our position to overlook this is to turn our backs upon the so-called Way of the Subject. Yet, however violent and lawless Ryūun may be, if we confront him from our side with great mercy and supreme sincerity, will he not surely be smitten by that whip of boundless compassion, submit to the King from the bottom of his heart, express remorse for his past sins, and serve as a loyal subject? That is by no means a difficult task. If we are unable to lead such a wicked person as Ryūun to reconciliation and repentance, it is entirely due to a lack of our own sincerity. No matter what demon it may be, when we revere the august heart of the Great Deity of great mercy and compassion, making utmost sincerity and truth our guiding principle, and confront it, it will surely rejoice with courage and follow us with gratitude—that is clearer than seeing fire. First of all, abandon your thoughts of martial arts, and polish your souls as quickly as possible!'"

と謡ひ終り、テーリスに向つてわが改心の次第を述べ立てる。
