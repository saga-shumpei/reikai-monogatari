Chapter 18: 心の天国 [1006]

心の天国〔1006〕

§ 1

Anan, Seiru, Yūzu, and Shirurengu, the four of them, raised troops and attempted a night raid, advancing rashly into the inner chambers of Kaujijiyō, only to fall into a temporarily constructed trap; being captured, the four were thrown together into a prison, and whispering in low tones about the state of King Sagaren, they worried and murmured over it. Anan, lowering his voice, spoke:

Anan: 'At last, the wicked deity of the dragon cloud has fallen deep into the trap, and it has come to this plight. Well, once it has come to this, who knows when we might be released, and since the power of life and death is held by the dragon cloud, are not the lives of all of us together like cherry blossoms before a tempest? Does not even the great deity of Brahman hear us far too little? For wicked men to walk and swagger freely in broad daylight, while we men of utmost sincerity and reality must suffer such cruel insults of the fetters and groan day and night beneath iron windows—what manner of thing is this!'

As he let drop a somewhat weak lament, Seiru smiled wryly and retorted:

Seiru: 'Unlike Anan, who is usually so bold, what kind of weak lament is this you are uttering? You are the most senior among us four. Furthermore, are you not regarded in our midst as a general endowed with both wisdom and courage? Why on earth are you uttering such lonely sentiments today?'

Anan: 'As for me, I have already begun to feel the impermanence of this world. No matter how much a mortal may fret, misfortunes and fortunes are things that cannot possibly be swayed by us at all. Rather than resigning oneself to the thought that everything is fate, there is no longer any other path left, you see.'

Yūzu: 'Nonsense, you should not utter such weak-spirited things. You fellows simply lack faith. As for this Yūzu, though my physical body is thus bound, my soul—which is the vital and essential true deity—is roaming freely and cheerfully throughout the universe. Even if the physical body can be slain, they do not possess the power to kill our soul as well. The only one who has the power to slay both soul and physical body alike is the Lord God. When viewed from the eyes of the free men among the vulgar mob, they surely think we have fallen into truly cramped and pitiful circumstances, but if Yūzu were to speak, the hearts and minds of Keriya, Haruma, Bēru, and the rest, who fawn and flatter a demon like the dragon cloud, actually become the ones to be pitied. Even the dragon cloud is precisely the same thing. Those fellows are chained in the prison of heaven. Their souls have been stripped away by petty avarice such as fame, position, and property, and their free souls are bound by the hellish demon of attachment, causing them to groan as pitifully extreme figures. Even Bēru always fancies himself grandly serving as the head jail warden, displaying an arrogant countenance and swaggering past this window outside two or three times a day, but when one reflects upon it, he is truly a pitiful object. From morning till night he declares his duties are paramount, unable to go even where he wishes to go, and at night he cannot sleep properly by the side of his hag of a wife, nor can he properly gaze upon even his own children, a truly pitiful object. Setting out from his home early in the morning while it is still dark, and returning to his home late at night, his children are invariably in deep slumber. Because of that, Bēru's children must surely think he is a fatherless child. When one thinks and thinks on it, is he not an extremely pitiful object indeed?

Though the sorts of sorrowful things change in this world, there is no one who dwells with a peaceful heart,

just as the ancient poem says, not a single person—not even half a person—lives peacefully and serenely in such a dark and benighted present world. When one reflects upon that matter, we are truly fortunate beings. They will even guarantee our clothing, food, and shelter, and even should we fall victim to poisoned blades, it is merely a worn-out garment of flesh after all. Since the true deity cannot be dealt with in any way, it is a sentiment akin to saying that the boiling cauldron is as sweet as syrup. There is nothing at all to worry about; if one simply leaves everything in the hands of the great august will of the Lord God, one feels utmost peace. To regret the past is already futile. Even if one worries about the future and gives way to over-anxious fretting, it will not amount to a straw. This very instant, this very moment, is the precious time granted to us with the authority to exercise and enjoy our free will. If we simply continue to enjoy it moment by moment, the moments accumulate to become an hour, times accumulate to become a day, a month, a year, and ten years, a hundred years, a thousand years, ten thousand years... moistened by the dew of the great deity's grace, rejoicing today, and yesterday and tomorrow alike filled with rejoicing... with merely such a single attitude of mind, a splendid world is suddenly established anew. Ah, how wondrous, how wondrous, could there possibly be another such place of peace and ease in all the world!'

§ 2

Saying this, he was looking cheerful. Shyleng had been listening to the conversation of the three, and with arms folded in a corner, was lost in some deep thought,

Shirurengu: 'Ah, I am in complete agreement with Yūzu's opinion. My spirits too have suddenly grown clear and bright. Come what may, I shall dwell on nothing further.'

At Shyleng's cheerful words, the one who had come prowling stealthily around the outside of the window was Bēru, the evil head jailer. Bēru:

Bēru: 'Hey, Anan, and the rest of you, will you keep quiet? Where do you think this place is?'

He roared with haughty arrogance.

Yūzu: 'What, are you not Bēru, who until just the other day was employed as a subordinate under this Yūzu? To ask where we think this place is—is that not a completely senseless question? You are indeed quite a complete fool. Since you do not understand, I shall tell you and make you hear. This place is the heavenly realm of the divine spirit world, vast and clear as the open sky, where there are neither clouds nor defilements, neither afflictions nor torments. You are merely a witless fool whose hands and feet are bound by clinging attachments, whose soul is entangled, and who moans and groans within the prison known as this fleeting world. That such a fool cannot see this heavenly pure land is perhaps not entirely unreasonable. Since it would be pitiful to find fault, I shall forgive you, but henceforth keep it firmly in mind and speak no more such foolish things as you just did.'

Bēru raised his voice to a sharp pitch,

Bēru: 'You, being a criminal by status, utter such outrageous speech toward me—this is something I cannot let pass unheard. Just wait, I shall punish you shortly. When that time comes, make sure you do not weep or wail. Water torture and fire torment are still trifling; I will flay and kill you with the tips of our spears, so wait and enjoy yourselves with that thought in mind. How truly pitiful creatures you are, hi-hi-hi-hi!'

Yūzu: 'What nonsense are you spewing? The inversion of master and guest is truly extreme indeed. You yourself are a rebellious, lawless criminal whom neither heaven nor earth can tolerate. We are men of utmost sincerity and loyalty. We are men of noble ambition who harbor not a single point of shame before heaven and earth. For you, a mere criminal, to utter outrageous speech against us, who are of utmost sincerity and faithfulness, is something I cannot let pass unheard. Just you remember this. With earthquakes, thunder, fire, and the old man of fire, I shall make it rain fire and punish you severely. How very pathetic a creature you are, hi-hi-hi-hi!'

Bēru: 'Hey there, Yūzu, have you gone mad? Though captured and brought into a prison, not even recognizing your own circumstances, you boldly spit out spiteful insults like a great fool—do you not know that this will work to your disadvantage?'

Yūzu: 'Ha-ha-ha-ha, it appears you are still fond of this world after all. To attempt to fathom the noble state of mind of us heroes with your small and defiled heart is indeed the height of presumption. You yourself are a madman cast out by society. Question your own conscience a little. Which of us is mad, and even a stubborn fool like you should be able to understand. In a world where evil runs rampant, if you look upon us through the evil eyes of the likes of you, we would indeed appear mad. To be sure, there is no doubt we are mad; however, the manner of our madness is different. Even though we are called mad, we are certainly not raving madmen like you, so cleanse your ears and listen carefully lest you get confused. If one tries to suit the minds of the people in the present degenerate world, one cannot attain the true divine will of the supreme and pure sincerity. If one tries to suit the minds of the great madmen of the vulgar and evil world, one cannot attain the will of the Great Kami. Human life is a mere hundred years; compared to long ages, it is like a fragment of a dream. The life bestowed upon us by the Kami is one of eternal and endless existence. We will not do such foolish things as to seek pleasure in a mere hundred-year body at the cost of leaving sin for an infinite eternity. That is the great point where we and you differ in our madness. You too should repent to a reasonable degree, do what you think is best in the present age, and crawl into a heavenly realm like this just as we have done. Out of old friendship, I give you this warning. Ahem.'

You are such a fellow who talks about things that make even less sense. The truth of the matter is that today an order was issued from Riūndono to release four fellows, but since a fellow who talks back like that shows no sign whatsoever of repentance, he shall be detained here indefinitely. Now, Anan, Sēru, and Shirurengu, since permission has been granted, you had better escape from here quickly. No one would ever want to stay in such a cramped place forever. Now, I will open this door quickly, so you had better come out.

Unlocking the bolt with a loud clatter, he flings open the iron door at the entrance.

Anan: 'If it is Riūn's pardon, I will certainly not go out. Where is there any qualification for such a fellow to grant a pardon? Against His Awe-inspiring Majesty the Sagaren, to dare to rebel as a supreme criminal—to pardon loyal subjects like us in the capacity of criminals is beyond all reason. If you speak such absurd nonsense, convey it to that fellow Riūn that Anan refuses to accept it. Aha-ha-ha-ha!'

Ah-ah, it seems that one and all have completely gone mad. That is no wonder either. Thrust into such a dark place, there is no way anyone's mind wouldn't snap. Ah-ah, what a pitiful object. Well then, Sēru, you must be glad to go out. You had better get out quickly.

Sēru: 'Hey, Bēru, you are quite a capable Saru-bēru indeed. With a tongue like a snake's tip, flattering and fawning on Riūn with your "Enyara-yatsu," unseemly and unmanly, selected as the prison warden and thinking it your utmost glory, you are a grinning object, so you will never be able to taste the true pleasure within our hearts. Truly a pitiful object. I have never entered a prison. You are the one who is wretched in the extreme, caught and suffering in the prison of the Avīci hell. I have been thinking of somehow setting you free, but no matter what I say, your sins are so heavy that I cannot easily bring myself to grant your release. Truly the height of pathos.'

Bēru tilts his head with a look of suspicion,

Ah-ah, this place is no good either. So many madmen have swarmed and been born here. Since they think of such a dark, painful prison as if it were a supreme paradise, there is nothing that can be done. After all, unless this fellow's soul is completely refired from scratch, he will never return to his original self. Hey, Shirurengu, you alone must have a tiny bit of sanity left. Now, get out of here quickly, get out!

Hey, Bēru, shall we drop it? Even if I go out, they will find some fault or other again, and if the wind blows the wrong way, I will just be captured again and slammed back in, which accomplishes nothing at all. Since it is troublesome, my intention is to enshrine myself here for all eternity. Your kindness is appreciated, but it would rather become a bother for us. You had better give up on such a thing. Rather than worrying about another person's hernia as if it were your own headache, it is far better for your own afterlife to cherish every single moment and strive rigorously not to let innocent criminals escape. Since everything in the modern world is completely upside down, it is an age where stones float and tree leaves sink, fathers buy prostitutes, and children enter into the recitation of the Nenbutsu. You too should exhaust every limit of matter-master-spirit-servant and contradictory absurdity, and wait cheerfully to be cast into the heavenly prison, ufu-fu-fu-...

Though the body may lurk within the prison, The heart sports in the divine garden of the Gods. Caught by a hundred demon gods called desires, How pitiful is the suffering Bēru! Within the clouded heart of the crooked Bēru, He has begun to think of the prison as hell. Within the heart where the light of sun and moon shines brilliantly, There are neither demons nor prisons. Though one may bind the palace of flesh six feet tall, The heart dances high in the vast open sky. Hey, Bēru, quickly rectify your heart, Come to the prison and let me teach you sincerity. Your suffering must be far greater than mine, Trudging about without sleep day and night.

§ 4

Yūzu also sang from within,

Yūzu is truly the sincere shrine of Kami, Since neither ogres nor demons can assail her, How could Kami not feel pity rather than hatred For the crooked deeds of Riūn and Princess Kērusu? Rather than hate, Kami surely looks upon them with compassion, Falling of their own accord into Hell and the realm of hungry ghosts, The life of the King of Sagaren is one with Kamunagara, Safely shall he abide in the protection of Kami. Though for a brief while the storm winds blow, yet soon again The winds will calm across the expanse and abundance shall be met, Though strong be the might of the tornado's clouds, They shall be scattered, blown by the wind of Shinado.

Sēru also sang,

How comical are those wretches who bore right down to the floor of the Ōoku And live trembling in fear! If only there were sincerity, surely nothing in Heaven and Earth Would be a thing to fear, Forsaking the Sovereign, forsaking sincerity, Riūn Shall presently cast away the life of his own body.

Anan also sang,

Leaving Anan cast away within the zashiki While digging a pitfall to drop people into the depths of the earth, Into the trap set by the life of Princess Kērusu, Unawares has Riūn fallen. Wait a while, when Kami appears at the front He shall sever and fling away the head of Magatsu. Even though Riūn and Bēru lament and implore, How could they ever issue forth from this raugoku? Pleasantly smiling within the raugoku, I shall gaze upon the gestures of Magatsu and pass the days.

Bēru was at his wits' end, and he too was drawn in before he knew it by the verses of the four, and before he realized it he had crept inside the raugoku, and the very moment the door was slammed shut from within, the lock dropped with a heavy clack. The vital key lay silently resting outside the window.

Yūzu: 'Oi, Bēru, you have finally reformed after all, haven't you? Come now, since things have come to this, the power of life and death is held by Yūzu and the other three. How is it, have you surrendered to my Kototama?'

Bēru realized it for the first time,

Bēru: 'Ya! This fellow has made a blunder! A thing has been done that cannot be undone, oh dear!'

Anan: 'Ara, there is no need for you to be in such a hurry. Having gone out of your way to visit us from afar, all of us are truly filled with gratitude. It is our greatest wish to offer you tea, but as you know, our household is poor, so we have no decent tea to speak of. Here we have Anan's thermos. Since this should be a little warm, please partake of it without reserve.'

So saying, she attempted to roll up her sleeves. Bēru frantically shook his head, pressing his palms together,

Bēru: 'No, please, do not trouble yourself. Actually, I do not drink tea. Because I made a vow to abstain from tea before Banko Shinō-sama...'

Yūzu: 'Whatever else may be, since one member has been added to Yūzu's household, it is a happy occasion. I beg that you will be on good terms with us. Being split spirits of the Kami of the same Heaven and Earth, and brothers within the four seas, let us cherish and love one another, and enjoy this spring of the heavenly realm.'

Sēru: 'Since Bēru-sama has taken the trouble to come here, we would like to offer something to please him. Oh, fortunately, here is a turban shell roasted in its shell, inherited from Sēru's parents. Shall I present this to you?'

So saying, she exchanged a glance. The four at once clenched their fists tightly,

Four: 'Bēru, who allies yourself with the perverse and lawless Riūn, prepare to know your fate!'

And raining down blows from all four directions, Bēru let out a shriek with all his might, calling for salvation. Hearing this voice, a jailer rushed over in a flurry.
