Chapter 9: 濃 Mist の途 [997]

濃霧の途〔997〕

§ 1

Jizaiten of the land of Tokoyo, the rare child of Ōkuniwake, King Sagaren, who was born into the world, leaving Kenonkiyō behind, crossing the land of Persia, wandering far and near through the land of India, at last arrived safely at the island of Shiro, preached the teachings of Brahmanism morning and evening, and at length finding his time here, built and lined up his mansions at the pinnacle point where the three sides of the Aogakiyama cleanly encircle the bungalow of the capital of the divine land, and being hailed as the sovereign of the entire land of Shiro, he took Princess Kērusu to wife and governed the imperial reign, but gradually the way in which evil spirits watched for a chance was just like insects breeding in delicious tree fruits.

The vile spirits of Yamataorochi, changing their bodies in various ways, appeared as dragon-clouds using sorcery, invaded the bungalow and the divine mansion of the capital of the land of gods, and working every possible means, advanced close to the side of Princess Kērusu with such terrifying force that, failing not to fulfill the metaphor of the ant-hole eventually collapsing the embankment, they occupied without difficulty the stout divine mansion and the residential castle of King Sagaren right here, and displayed their tyrannical might with such exasperating hatred that, aided by loyal retainers and righteous warriors, barely escaping the grave danger, though standing upon the august position of ninety-five, King Sagaren, who escaped with his very body, hurried away to the vast field.

Even while hurting his soul against the wind blowing across, tracing along the riverside of Kawamori with quick steps, he secretly came along with the two persons, Tērisu and Eimusu, to the village of Semu. Deep valleys were filled with mists, and at the very moment when he advanced to the riverside where only the sound of water resounded echoingly, the purity-tainting dragon-clouds had sent out a few young men as lookouts, who hid their bodies in the misty single-mouthed valley path and waited with bated breath. That such a plot existed was dangerous indeed for the master and servants, who, knowing it not even in dreams, walked down quietly while singing propagation songs.

Ah, Kamu-nagara Kami-gami-no, may your august spirits bestow happiness upon us.

Tērisu sings along the way:

Parting the clouds of the heavenly august sky, appearing as the divine ancestor of the origin of the world who descended, Jizaiten of the land of Tokoyo, that august descendant of Ōkunihiko, the divine minister Kuniwakehiko, my lord King Sagaren, appearing in the rare mansion of the bungalow in the capital of the divine land, while graciously protecting and watching over the common people under the heavens, just at the time when he was opening an administration of infinite benevolence and compassion, Kurin-no-kami, who disturbs this world, and the vile great serpent appeared, deceived Princess Kērusu, and further advanced to occupy the throne, shattering their very core hearts, morning and evening.

Preaching and admonishing those bent on fame and profit, the wicked people, unknowingly, firmly united the solid alliance of evil parties seeking their own kind, and while casting undeserved criminal charges upon all the men of loyalty, throwing every single one of them into prison, the dragon-cloud plotted to achieve his own unrighteous ambitions with such terrifying dread. I first guessed that the dragon-cloud was a suspicious person when he came to the capital of the divine land, and wishing to probe his mind, I marshaled unbefitting flattery every time I met him, and finally being perceived by him, with what joy was I able to probe out every single plan one by one, here and there, without letting it seem so.

However, if one were to expose them in vain, one would fall into the trap contrived by the evil deities. Though my mind wandered in a thousand directions, holding down my breast firmly, I advanced still further, and when I struck the belly of the dragon-cloud, as expected, Rēru, Kingusu, and Bettosuru—his machinations were visible as clearly as if taking them in the palm of one's hand. Ah, Kamu-nagara Kami-gami-no, Great Deity Ōkunihiko, pray overturn his strategy from the very roots, cause the bent deities to reform from the bottom of their hearts, surrender to the true path of Brahmanism, and make them offer a pure, righteous, and sincere heart before the august presence of King Sagaren, and turn them into subjects of absolute loyalty.

Even the things prayed for became bubbles in the water; the evil mind grew ever more arrogant, and using Princess Kērusu as a stepping stone, various maneuvers progressed day by day and month by month, until here they staged the great tragedy of a coup d'état. Yet, as long as righteous deities exist in heaven and earth, that they help the good and chastise the evil deities is before our very eyes. For now, there is nothing to be done for this body; hiding my form for a while deep in the mountains, waiting for the time while the wind blows over the treetops, and calmly concocting the divine strategy to drive away the dragon-cloud, we shall make a triumphant return to the bungalow, make it the king's capital once again, and display the loyalty of the two of us to the august heart of our lord...

Consoling thee now for a while, / Pray endure it, King Sagaren, / For both Theris and Ames, / To the utmost of their hearts and limits of their bodies, / With the great secret art of a myriad transformations, / Exhausting it to begin with thy august person, / And this august land, like Taizan, / Shall save thee in peace. / Ah, Kamunagara, / May thy mitama be ever prosperous and blessed.'

And following the valley path of the Kaumorigaha where the mists thickened, crossing the village of Semu, pointing toward the mansion of Koito in the village of Matsura, they hastened their way, intending to steal forth.

Ames sings again:

'On the boundless ocean of a thousand Ri, / Floating is the pure island of Shiro, / The ruler of the kami and the sovereign of the land, / Combining both to govern, / King Sagaren, the Kuniwakehiko, / His benevolence reaches under heaven, / Down to the trees and grasses of the four quarters, / Dropping the dew of grace, / Is the ruler's fame illuminating the great void? / Is it the Amatsuhi shining across? / Shining like the moon, / Which is made to be the guardian of the night, / Shining like a glittering star, / Among the numerous kami who come to submit, / The black clouds suddenly rolled up in the mid-air, / Appearing as cloud-fiends, / The tortuous and changing dragon-clouds / Concealed the sun and moon and wrapped the light of all the stars, / And this land

For a while became the darkness of eternal night, / The heavenly rock-door was closed, / The Yamata no Orochi and hideous foxes, / Wicked demons, Sagume, and ugly hags, / Swarmed like May-flies, / Until it became a world where black and white could not be distinguished. / Starting with Keriya and Haruma, / Evil kami siding with wickedness, / Down to Veel, Mêl, and Yōru, / Blinded by the desire for fame and profit, / Not only abandoning our deeply indebted lord, / But even adding perils to it, / And attempting to establish their factional desires, / Oh, what foolishness! / Even if the sky is wrapped in clouds / And an age comes where not even stars are visible, / How could the divine breath of the kami's wind / Fail to blow until when?

Since we hear the teaching of the kami / That heaven and earth are living things from the beginning, / Once again the timely wind shall blow, / Just as the whole sky pours ink, / The enfolding black clouds of the ugly ones / Shall also clear away as if wiped clean, / To see the light of the sun and moon, / Which brilliantly and brightly illuminates all things, / Right before our eyes, / O ruler of the kami, O great monarch, / Do not trouble your heart, / Nor let your body grow weak. / The sincere hearts of Theris, Ames, to begin with, / Sāru, Uinchi, Shirurengu, / Yūzu, Anan, Zemu, Eeru, / And the rulers of Sēru / Shall surely pierce through to heaven, / And the flowers of sincerity shall bloom, / That the harvest of our lord's reign once again / Should bear fruit is Kamunagara, / Dwelling in the heart of the kami. / From now on, we shall guide the great monarch

To the mansion of Koito of the Brahman in the village of Matsura, / Here to reverently receive the divine oracle, / Waiting for the time when the dragon-clouds / Will utterly overthrow the desires of the ugly ones from the roots—wait a while! / Until the wind of Shinato, / Manifested in the divine water and fire, / Blows high and fierce across the sky, / Until the sun and moon / Appear once more in this world / To illuminate the head of the demon, / Ah, Kamunagara, / According to the will of the kami, washing the heart of our liver-marrow / And purifying our bodies, / Even if we cast away our lives for the sake of King Sagaren, / How could we fail to carry out to the end the sincerity / Of warriors steadfast in loyalty? / How could the red heart / Fail to shine brightly forever and ever?

Ah, Kamunagara, / Before the august presence of the great Okuninushi no Ōkami / We humbly pray, / Before the august presence we respectfully pray.'

Praying with quiet voices, they advanced through the fog along the riverside, guided by the sound of the roaring torrent.

In this valley river, dense fog always lingered, and it had naturally become a suitable habitat for numerous great serpents and fierce beasts. The paths where mountain bandits and the like appeared in broad daylight were mostly along this route. The King's party, fearing the pursuit of the dragon-cloud's captors, unwillingly chose this difficult path.

と声も静かに祈りつつ、轟々たる激流の音を便りに川辺伝ひに霧の中を進み行く。

此谷川には常に濃霧立ちこめ、数多の大蛇、猛獣などの好適の棲息所と自然になつてゐた。山賊などの白昼出没するも、大部分此道筋である。王の一行は竜雲の捕手の追及を恐れて、心ならずも此難路を選まれたのである。
