Chapter 14: 歎願 [1002]

歎願〔1002〕

§ 1

King Sagaren left the mansion, returned once more to the original Gankutsu accompanied by Amesu, sat in Tanza in the inner room, chanted the Amatsunorito, and further recited a Shinka; that song is as follows.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! / Compared to the love of the Kami / Of the boundless Tenchi, / I was but a small being. / Appearing as a minister of the Kami / On the floating island of Ceylon, / I was transmitting the teachings of Brahminism / To the yobito, / But inevitably becoming a king, / Serving morning and evening / In the matsurigoto of Keniuitsuchi, / Facing the Kami of Tenchi, / Thinking that I had served / The noble and pure kamuwaza / From the bottom of my heart— / Oh, the foolishness of it! / The boundless Daiuchiu, / Fifty-six billion, seven hundred million— / I hear that there are so many numbers of the uchiu, / Yet just a single shōuchiu / Is illuminated by the Amatsuhi!

As far as the moonlight shines, / Aohitogusa, birds and beasts, / The Ashihara where grasses and trees grow— / In a corner of the land of Mizuho, / Drifting on the great ocean, / This divine country was in the great ocean / A thing smaller than a single grain of millet / Thrown away. / Crouching in such a Tenchi, / Squabbling that this is good or bad, / Surrounded by infinite desires, / Without even comprehending / The great august spirit of the Great Kami of infinite benevolence and compassion, / I who came hither am a fool. / Surely the Great Kami of Tenchi / Must be laughing at our small hearts! / Even if there were an age / When I alone should rule the uchiu, / Compared to the current state / Of the boundless Daiuchiu,

It is something beyond comparison. / Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! / The Kami rules the uchiu; / Reflecting upon that divine virtue, / From now on, changing the heart / Of our very core, / Obeying the true path of faith, / As long as this body lasts, / Offering the sincerity of the asa-bashira / Reaching unto my body, / I shall reverently reward / One ten-thousandth part / Of the achievements of the Great Kami. / O Tārchin and Kingus, who served as my Hayato-no-kami, / O Princess Amesu, / And all other hundreds of ministers— / Do you also from now on / Purify and rightly re-proclaim your spirits, / Sweep away the attachments of the small floating world / In the wind of Shinato, / And serve, Kamunagara! / Vowing to the Kami, / The minister of the Kami, King Sagaren, proclaims and transmits: / Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! / May your august spirits be blessed and prosper.

Hearing the song of King Sagaren, Tārchin stood up joyfully, and with a delighted heart recited and danced.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! / The grace of the Kami being deep, / The darkness of mumyō has cleared away! / When the hour of the ox, / When even water sleeps, / Has passed and the hour of the tiger, / And then the exact hour of the rabbit has come, / Illuminating the eastern sky, / The august shadow of the sun rising in Toyosaka— / Revealing His noble and pure august form, / He illuminates the divine country of Ashihara, / Not to mention Aohitogusa, / Birds and beasts, and tribes of insects, / Up to the single leaves of grasses and trees, / The country of pearls protected by Him. / In a corner of that ocean, / Floating eternally and everlastingly, / The island of Shiro strung with a sacred rope— / Appearing in that Mahuraba,

Born as the lord and prince of Ōkunihiko / In the age of the Kami, / With power also divided, the Jizaiten, / Awe-struck by the august words of the Kami, / The divine minister of Brahminism / Established for the sake of the yobito, / Born as a king of human children, / Who has ruled over us— / His achievements are higher / Than Mount Tenkyō. / Even the clouds traversing the sky hesitate, / And the majesty of the King conceals even their shadows; / Wrapping it up, right here now, / Proclaiming the precious kototama, / How noble is His humility! / Though men of old proclaimed / That man is a child of the Kami and a shrine of the Kami, / The Yamata-no-Orochi has spread widely, / And the mi-tama of Aohitogusa / Have been determined as the dwelling of a thousand ages; / The yobito of the present age are all

Shrines of the Kami pure in name only, / In truth they are vessels of crookedness. / Entangled in petty desires, / They hate, quarrel, weep, and cry out; / The burning hell and the water hell, / The alley of Shura is not yet the worst— / Sinking into the country of the foundation, / The groaning voices of Avīci and Saṃjīva / Unheard, triumphantly, / Unwittingly and unknowingly / The pitifulness of the yobito following the path of the demon! / Although we too are persons / Who have served the Kami morning and evening, / The darkness of our heart does not clear away, / Sinners whose defilement of the mi-tama / Can never be washed away! / Yet the Kami, viewing His august spirit / As broader than heaven, from Kaminadoki / To Ōnadoki, / How grateful that He has forgiven us!

With the body of a humble person / Who is bogged down in sins and defilements, / How could we ever / Meet the august heart of the Kami? There is no way. / Simply, whatever happens, we / Shall take the heart of the Kami as our heart, / Proclaim the path of the Kami of great mercy and great compassion / Widely to the yobito, / And serve with our heart / Even unto the ends of Tsukushi, / Kamunagara. / Reverently before the presence of the Kami, / Awe-struck and awe-struck, we pray: / Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! / May your august spirits be blessed and prosper.

タールチンはサガレン王の歌を聞き嬉しく立ち上り、心欣々として謡ひ舞ふ。

『あゝ惟神々々 神の恵みの深くして 無明の闇は開けにけり 水も眠れる丑満の 時刻はたちて寅の刻 卯の正刻となりぬれば 東の空を輝かし 豊栄昇る日の御影 尊き清き御姿を 現し給ひて葦原の 神の御国を照らしまし 青人草は云ふも更 鳥獣や虫族や 草木の片葉に至る迄 守らせ給ふ珍の国 其海原の片隅に 弥永久に浮かみたる 七五三を張りたるシロの島 其真秀良場に現れまして 神の御代に大国彦の

§ 2

Kingshime opens her white fan and stands up, dancing and chanting gracefully in her long sleeves.

Toyoashihara's precious isle / Called the land of the Kami / Surrounded by the sea on all four sides / A pure and noble isle of Shiro / Appearing in the capital of the divine land / Upon the four corners of the land with dew of grace / Without a shadow upon the Aohitogusa / The Ōgimi who poured forth his blessings / By what manner of twisted violence / With his noble Kiugo body / Pitifully, he descended / To the village of Koito in Matsūra / In a rock cave where even the Amatsuhikage / Does not reach in its fullness / Hiding his noble person / For the sake of the country and for the sake of the people / Devoting his heart / Is truly the height of gratitude / From what sky did it come / The heart-stained Riūn / Deceiving the Himekimi / Whose name is worthy to be heard / With ugly manners / Oh, the sadness of being thrown into chaos / Even if this body becomes / The seaweed of the Unabara and decays / The Kusemono who opposes the King / With the Kototama of the true Kami / I shall Kotomu and harmonize, and the black clouds / That rage across the world / Shall I not sweep away and purify / Ah, Kannagara Kami-Kami / May the spirit of the Kami bring happiness / May the sincerity of us, husband and wife / Be heard peacefully by the Ōmikokoro / And may you protect the isle of Shiro / As a divine land without incident, peace, and wind / And without knowing clouds / Kannagara / I pray before the Kami / Even if the morning sun shines or clouds over / Even if the moon waxes or wanes / No matter how strong the demons may be / As long as the true Kami exists / Forever and ever, the reign of the King / Shall be governed peacefully and calmly / The hundred clans of people shall gather / Rejoicing and cheering for the reign of the King / Like small sparrows flocking / We shall praise it for a thousand generations / It shall be a reign where the high and low harmonize / And Kami and man love and befriend one another / The prosperity of the country, the world of Miroku / With the Kototama of rigor and auspiciousness / I pray that you save us / Ah, Kannagara Kami-Kami / May your spirit be blessed.'

Eru stands and chants.

Man is the child of the Kami, the palace of the Kami / Standing firm in sincerity alone / If we strive morning and evening / In deeds that accord with the Way / Under heaven, there shall be no / Enemies or Magatsu to fear / Yet, the Kamitsukasa who serve the King / Of the Brahmins / Anan, Sēru, and Shirurengu / And the friends of Yūzu have been captured / By the twisted schemes of Riūn / And have fallen into a bottomless pit / So we have heard / Hearing such news, shall we / Remain with folded arms and watch / The suffering of our friends / Morning and evening / I know not if it violates the law of heaven / But for the sake of our dear friends / We shall stake our lives and stand against them / The heart-stained Riūn who spreads / In the capital of the divine land / And the twisted ones, shall we not / Strike and judge them / And quickly save our four friends / As soon as possible, in a single moment / Achieving this purpose / And the sincerity of being believers / Who serve the Great Way of the Kami / We shall devote ourselves, Kannagara / King Sagaren, the Kamitsukasa / Please hear our request with a peaceful heart / And grant our wish / The Kami is with us / Kotomu the evil, and save the good / And let them be moistened / By the grace of the Kami, Kamitsukasa / Before the presence of our Ōgimi / I pray with reverence and caution / Ah, Kannagara Kami-Kami / May your spirit be blessed.'

Thus, he pleads to the King to be sent to the capital of the divine land to save the four Kamitsukasa. King Sagaren, as well as Tāruchin, Kingshime, Tērisu, and the superiors of Ēmusu, inclined their heads to the Kototama of Eru, clasped their hands, and remained for a while in tears of regret.

At this moment, a beautiful woman's propaganda song is heard, sounding like music echoing the spirits of the trees in the valley. Ah, indeed, whose propaganda song could it be?

と王に向つて四人の神司を救ふべく、神地の都に遣はされむ事を懇願する。サガレン王を始めとし、タールチンやキングス姫、テーリス、エームスの上司は、エールの言霊に頭を傾け、両手を組んで、稍暫し無念の涙に暮れて居た。

かかる処へ美はしき女の宣伝歌、谷に木霊を響かせつ音楽の如く聞え来る。あゝ果たして如何なる人の宣伝歌であらうか。
