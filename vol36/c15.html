Ryūun: 'Since whatever we say makes us badgers dwelling in the same hole, this responsibility must be shared by the two of us. Yet in the present world, those who possess enough talent to carry out treachery, sycophancy, outrageous wickedness, and lawlessness are praised and feted as heroes, gallants, gentlemen, merchants, and the elite of the nation. Having experienced the essence of contemporary thought to the extreme, we two are truly the champions and conquerors of the present age. Things like good and evil change according to time, place, and status. As long as human beings possess physical bodies, no matter what is said, if we do not desire the perfection of food, clothing, and shelter, life is a lie. Individuals who are caught up in worthless old moral concepts and superstitions that performing half-human acts is the path of supreme good no longer have any value of survival or discernment in this world; they are the height of fools. Therefore, this Ryūun all the more detests religions such as Aizendō, which champions the principle of non-resistance, and Brahmanism, which nonchalantly commits contradictions such as dividing human classes into three stages and giving different social treatments to the upper, middle, and lower streams. All human races in the world, regardless of colored or uncolored, should have all things equally receive the grace of God... or in other words, natural divine blessings should be equally distributed without partiality. We must smash the habits of society today, which are full of contradictions and unreasonableness, make the wise exert their wisdom, make the brave activate their bravery, and make free competition the principle of society. If we do so, we will be able to make great activities with all our might, and the saints shouting in the fields will lift up their heads and come to exhibit their marvelous wisdom and plans which they usually harbor; this is what is called absolute equality, conforming to the partiality-free, natural divine will. That being the case, you also must completely cast aside old customs up to now, and since today is characterized by rapid daily progress, you must follow my teaching, become the newest woman in the first place of the world, and possess the resolve to make your brave name shine in the world together with Ryūun. Such a husband has such a wife; there is even a saying in the proverb about an ogre husband and a snake wife. To deal with a world where ogres, serpents, and demons swarm to this extent, nothing short of a pressure greater than that will ever work at all. Ryūn shrugged his shoulders and brushed it off with a hearty laugh, saying, 'Just as there are obstructions of wind and rain in the ten, afflictions of illness in men, and clouds that gather in the heart, even though the jitsugetsu shine brightly and illuminate the gekai, when dark clouds close in across the chiukū just as they do konnichi, the light of the jitsugetsu cannot penetrate down to chijiyō. Since this ryūn is, after all, a miniature of the uchiu and a mere danpen of the tenchi, it is only natural that the jitsugetsu of the shinten should occasionally be obscured by kokūn. Yet once the clouds of doubt clear away, the shinten instantly becomes tōtetsu and clear, and the jitsugetsu of shinniyo comes to shed its radiance. That is merely a temporary obstruction by the clouds. Lord Bankoshinō has never made an oyakusoku that a clouded sky will never clear away for eien. How could anyone manage and sustain this itsukoku if one were to fret over such shōmondai? Even with all her supposed wisdom, this is truly a woman's foolish complaint; ah, I am simply too dumbfounded to speak, ha ha ha ha!'

His cunning demeanor was such that even the great Bankoshinō Himself would surely be astonished and keep His distance by three stages.

Princess Kērusu sat in silence with her head bowed down, struggling for a while with the judgment of good and evil, right and wrong. Witnessing the constant transformations of her own heart—at times becoming a demon, at times a god, a beast, or a giant serpent—she plunged ever deeper into a labyrinth, falling into a psychological state as dark and stifling as being sealed within a small pot. On the other side, Ryūn had also put on a bold front and spoken his piece just as before, yet somehow the whisperings of his conscience seemed to rise up with fierce momentum, striving to refute his every argument. The two of them remained silent, keeping up a curtain of speechlessness for a while.

Into such a place came rushing hurriedly, with a changed countenance, a young man named Teiru, whom Ryūn constantly cherished as his trusted right-hand man. He was an ardent worshipper of Ryūn who had shut himself up in his private quarters, studying the scriptures of the Ural religion with single-minded devotion.

'O two-sama, a momentous and extraordinary emergency has suddenly broken out. This is surely no time for us to remain leisurely and calm! Come, cast down a rope ladder this very instant, descend these caverns, cross the valley river, and conceal your honorable persons for a while; danger approaches by the very second!'

As he spoke these words with an altered complexion and a restless demeanor, Ryūn and Princess Kērusu were struck with amazement at Teiru's speech. Raising himself up on his knees, Ryūn demanded in a hurried tone,

'What manner of grave matter is this?'

Instantly changing his posture and pressing the question with urgency, Teiru gulped down two or three mouthfuls of water from a jar placed before him, stroked his chest to calm himself, and replied, 'Indeed so; while I, Teiru, was shut up in my study researching the sacred books, the flocks of crows in the garden suddenly grew noisy, the sounds of men and horses became clamorous, and the wind brought the assaulting war drums. With the fierce, encroaching echoes of gongs and drums drawing ever closer, the enemy has pressed in near at hand. Wondering whose rebellion this might be, when I looked around, I wasted no time in calling the balustrade of the corridor my fortunate chance. Climbing up swiftly like a monkey and casting a sharp gaze out over the village roofs below, I saw the unexpected banner of the three-leaf hollyhock. Even as I thought this incomprehensible, leading the vanguard was King Sagaren, followed by Tērisu, Ēmusu, and other brave generals, who had equipped their armor and led a vast army like drifting clouds. Adopting a single-ladder formation from three directions, they attacked with piercing, successive thrusts—what a solemn and imposing spectacle! Deeming this a grave crisis, I, Teiru, gathered together the defending soldiers of our side. Several dozen brave and desperate young men, aligning their hand-spears, charged in a straight line toward the oncoming enemy. We thrust, slashed, and hacked in all directions, pressing forward with the indomitable force of a raging tiger plunging into a river. Just as I thought the enemy, startled by this, had retreated in an avalanche for about three chō while leaving behind numerous casualties and dead, suddenly from the forests on both sides emerged tens of thousands of enemy troops. Deeming this a grave matter, and thinking that to advance too deeply would only be to fall into the enemy's trap, the several dozen of our men who turned back toward the front gate to make our planned retreat were either struck down or fled in rout. Alas, the only one remaining was Teiru, so frail and helpless had we become. Keriya and Haruma were captured by the enemy before our very eyes, and the rest of the subordinates shamefully removed their helmets, hoisted white flags, and surrendered to the enemy—such was their utter lack of spine. No matter how much Teiru alone might gnash his teeth and stamp his feet in vexation, just as a great mansion is about to collapse and a single piece of wood cannot prop it up, with bitter regret I have cut and slashed my way through the swarming enemy all alone, just to report this critical emergency to you.
