§ 1

In the inner chamber of Karuchin, Haruman, Samarihime, and the Shiyujin sit in a Kanaheza, whispering together, lost in conversation and unaware that the night is turning into dawn.

Haruman: 'Dannasama, you left the mansion quietly before Hirumahe, and since you had not returned by nightfall, I was worried that you might have been drinking in the Jiyōnai, causing trouble for everyone as you usually do. I quietly peeked into the Jiyōnai, but the Monban said they had not seen Umori-sama today, and when I asked the Jiyochiu, they said you had not arrived. As I was searching everywhere, I heard a strange sound on the surface of the Irunagaha. Thinking it was Fushigi, I Tachido to consider the Yōsu, when a large fox walked slowly across the bridge toward the north. Thinking this Koitsu was strange, I looked at the Suimen and saw a black shadow Ukiaga suddenly. I jumped in to save your Inochi, and when I looked closely, I saw it was you, Dannasama. You were saying things that made no sense, and I, Haruman, was truly worried about what had happened. If something had happened while Okusama was away, I, Haruman, would have no excuse. Well, I am glad you are safe.'

Samarihime: 'Otōsama, since Okā-sama is away these days, please do not go anywhere and stay inside. I am worried. If you must go to the castle, please take two or three servants with you as you always do. Even though you hold the office of Umori, is it not too reckless to walk alone?'

Umori: 'Naani, there is a secret behind walking alone that cannot be told. My divine strategy and ghostly schemes are not for a young girl to understand. Well, you had better leave things to me.'

Haruman: 'That might be fine during the day, but it would be a disaster if something like tonight happened again, so I beg you to return before the sun sets from now on. Please understand that it is not right for a young man to walk around secretly at night like someone chasing a lover. Hime-sama is also very worried about you...'

Umori: 'No, the truth is, as you know, my wife has gone to the front, so first of all, I was offering prayers to the Lord Daijizaiten and visiting the graves of my ancestors, praying for my wife to display her martial prowess. Then, from behind the stone pagoda of my ancestors, a monster with the very face of Tenahime appeared, spitting out, "The cold rice of resentment..." and making strange gestures. At the end of it all, it peeled its eyes like balls. Then another monster came along and peeled its eyes like umbrellas, so even I was startled for a moment. On my way running back toward my house, I accidentally fell into the Irunagaha. You happened to pass by and saved me, so I must thank you. However, eh... I wonder what happened to that one's life.'

Haruman: 'What do you mean by this one's life or that one's life? You were saying "Yasudara, Yasudara" so much; do you have something in mind regarding Yasudarahime?'

Umori: 'What, I do not have any particular thoughts, but I am just a little concerned about whether that one is dead or alive.'

Samarihime: 'I am also concerned about Yasudarahime-sama. I heard a rumor that she has returned from the Teruman country. If she were to become a married couple with the King, I do not know what I would do; that is all I am worried about.'

Umori: 'Here, daughter, you do not need to worry about that at all. You are the consort of King Seran through and through. I will guarantee it and make sure you are together, so be at ease. However, if my wife were to lose her life in this battle, what would you think?'

Haruman felt relieved that he had escaped, and thinking that it would be a Taihen matter if he were thrown out anyway, he intentionally put on a happy face.

Haruman: 'Hai, Arigata-u gozaimashita. From now on, I shall never say anything. I swear Zetsutai-fukujiyū, so please, Suenaga-ku, treat me with Kaai and use me.'

Yūfutesu: 'Un, yoshi-yoshi, as long as you are prudent about that, it is not that I want to give you your leave, Kisama.'

Haruman: 'By the way, Yūfutesu-sama, is it Jitsusai that such a Fushigi is occurring within the Jiyōnai? Does it not seem a bit hard to Gaten?'

Yūfutesu: 'Un, it is truly Fukashigisenban.'

Samarī: 'If that is the case, Kāruchin-dono, I shall remain in Chinmoku for a while and wait for the time to pass; farewell.'

Saying this, she left, and while dragging her hem with a trailing sound, she proceeded toward the inner room. Haruman and Yūfutesu also followed, and it was decided that they would Hitoma-tachikahe toward their own Iheji.

Kāruchin: 'Yare-yare, the Jiyamamono has been cleared away.'

Uchiyoroko-ing, she entered the Keshiyōshitsu, finished the Seiri of her face in various ways, draped beautiful Ifuku upon her body, and from the Uramon, smiling, she proceeded once again toward the Jiyōnai.
