Chapter 11: River 底の Monster [1136]

河底の怪〔1136〕

§ 1

Having wandered into a Hakaba, and having his Aragimo stolen by a Kuwaibutsu, he was startled Nido, and gasping for breath, he dashed out onto the road in the Yohiyami, intent on meeting Yasudarahime-sama of Iruna Castle. The Jifugoya Mangetsu began to cast its light upon the Chijiyō. Karuchin, finally finding Anshin in the moonlight, came to a Tachido, joined his Riyōte, and while worshipping the Getsushin, he spoke in Hitorigo:

Karchin: 'Ah, it seems the darkness of love is brightening up. They say the god of marriage is the Getsukahiyōjin, so the Tsukisama that illuminates the darkness of my Kohiji is like a savior to me. Ah, the moon, the moon. From here on, I shall arrive at the Ima of Yasudarahime-sama, engage in various Zatsuta and Ichiya, cling to her, act like a Mushiya, and in the end, I might even grow the horns of Rinki and reach the Dandori of biting and snapping at her. Ehehehe.'

While drooling, he arrived at the Hashizume of the Irunagaha. It was a Fushigi; the riverbed, which was over an Ichijiyō deep, was as transparent as Suishiyō, and despite the moonlit night, even the swimming of small fish could be seen clearly. Karuchin spoke in Hitorigo while peering into it:

Karuchin: 'What a Fushigi thing. Even in the daytime, this river is murky and the bottom has never been visible, so what could be the reason for this today? The transparent Suishiyō water is flowing. This must be because the Great Ōkami of the moon is blessing the future of our love.'

As he spoke to himself and peered in, Tena-hime came flowing by, struggling on the riverbed.

Karuchin: 'Hey, that wretch Tena must have fallen from her horse upstream and been washed down to this point. It seems she is still moving on the riverbed. Hey, it looks like she is finally going to settle there.'

A voice came from nowhere:

Voice: 'Is Tena-hime not your wife? Why do you not risk your life and jump into the river to save her? You are truly a cold-hearted man.'

When he looked back, it was Yūfutesu.

Karuchin: 'Hey, Yūfutesu, where did you come from? Whether I save her or not, she is my wife. It is not within the scope of you people to interfere. Stay silent.'

Tena-hime sat on the riverbed, raising her hands as if calling for rescue. Her voice turned entirely into bubbles of water, and like bubbles of flatulence in a bath, they lined up and floated to the surface.

Yūfutesu: 'Danna-sama, you have suddenly become very cold-hearted. No matter how much she may be an obstacle to your love, is it not a bit immoral to let your wife die?'

Karuchin: 'It may be immoral, but if it is the course of events, then there is nothing to be done, is there?'

As they were talking, the figure of a woman came rolling and flowing along the riverbed, visible as if one could touch her. The two opened their eyes wide, and when they looked closely, they saw the beautiful young Yasudarahime-sama flowing by with her hair hanging down, still dressed in her beautiful garments. And, as luck would have it, she settled right where Tena-hime was submerged.

Karuchin: 'Hey, this is a disaster; it seems the essential object of my desire has committed suicide. This will not do; I must save her.'

Just as he was about to strip down, Yūfutesu gripped his hand tightly.

Yūfutesu: 'Moshi-moshi Danna-sama, it is dangerous, it is dangerous; if you jump into a place like this, it will be like committing a double suicide with Tena-hime-sama. Please stop.'

Karuchin: 'Nani, I am going to commit suicide with Yasudarahime-sama. Do not interfere with me.'

As he stripped down and tried to jump in, Yūfutesu grabbed him by the collar.

Yūfutesu: 'If I ask you to wait, then please, first of all, wait.'

Karuchin: 'Eee, do not get in my way; if you dawdle, will Yasudarahime-sama not breathe her last?'

On the riverbed, the two women began to grapple with all their might, clutching and being clutched. Karuchin...

Tenahime: 'Hey, Tenahime, what are you doing? I shall not forgive this!'

No sooner had he spoken than he plunged in, clothes and all, and the moment he splashed in, he sank like a stone thrown into the water. At the foot of the bridge, the cold wind whistled as it passed. The man who had appeared to be Euphrates suddenly transformed into a gigantic white fox, and with a slow, lumbering gait, he crossed the bridge and headed toward Iruna Castle.

After the departure of Tenahime for the front, Halman, a giant of a man who had been left behind by the Haruna reinforcements and entrusted with the defense of the mansion, had become suspicious of the unusual behavior of Kalchin of late. Worried that his master had not returned even after nightfall, he had come as far as the bridge approach. The surface of the river was shimmering in the moonlight. Suddenly, a black shadow emerged from the riverbed. Halman peered closely and said,

Halman: 'Oh, someone has fallen into the river and is dying. I must save them!'

He cast off his clothes, leaped into the air, and plunged into the water with a great splash. He immediately grabbed the black shadow, swam with powerful strokes, parting the water with one hand, reached the bank, pulled the person up, and after much care, revived them. When he looked closely, it was Kalchin, the Umorinokami. Halman was doubly surprised, and his words came in a rush,

Halman: 'Oh, are you not my master? That was a dangerous situation. Please, pull yourself together.'

Kalchin finally regained consciousness and said,

Kalchin: 'Ah, are you Yasudahime? That was a dangerous affair. I jumped in thinking I would save your life with all my might. Well, that is good. Now, let us go into the castle; it would be terrible if we were seen loitering in a place like this.'

Halman: 'Excuse me, Master, please pull yourself together. Do you know where you are?'

Kalchin: 'Is this not the bank of the Iruna River? Come, let us go quickly. You are not Tanumura Kihei again, rolling your eyes and scaring me with strange gestures, are you? Ehn!'

Halman: 'Excuse me, Master, I am not Yasudahime. I am your servant, Halman. Please, pull yourself together.'

Kalchin: 'Was Yasudahime's life saved? How is it? Will you not tell me the situation quickly?'

Halman: 'I do not know what became of such a person. Is it not enough that my duty is fulfilled if I save you? Why should you concern yourself with a woman like Yasudahime, who seems to have fled from the Teruman country? You should just leave that wretch to live or die as she pleases. Were you not the one who hated Yasudahime so much? Samarihime-sama says that your mind has changed lately and that you seem intent on making Yasudahime the King's wife, and she is so furious that she does nothing but cry. I find it too pitiful to watch, which is why I came searching for you like this. Why on earth did you fall into this river, when you are not even blind?'

Kalchin: 'What time is it now? I have become completely unable to understand the reason for anything.'

Halman: 'It is the hour of the five, and as you can see, the moon is rising in the eastern sky. Come, I shall accompany you home. Your clothes are soaked through, and it would be terrible if you caught a cold from the wind.'

So saying, Halman, a man of peerless strength, forcibly lifted Kalchin and headed toward the mansion of the Umorinokami, his footsteps thundering against the ground.
