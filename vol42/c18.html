Chapter 18: 酊苑 [1143]

酊苑〔1143〕

§ 1

Taking advantage of the fact that the wicket of the Omotemon was left open, the Shitsurentau members Karlchin, Yūfutesu, and Manmosu, along with over a dozen subordinates, kicked down the door of the Genkuwan, drew their Daitō in a row, and broke into the Ima of King Seran.

Advancing near the King's Ima, intending to cut down everyone indiscriminately—starting with the King, Ōgonhime, Kiyoteruhime, Serisu-hime, and other Kinji, with the exception of Yasudara-hime—Karlchin shouted in a Daionjiyō:

Karlchin: 'I am Karlchin, the Umori no Tsukasa. To achieve my long-held Mokuteki, I have taken advantage of the Yain to approach you to claim the Onkubi of the Umori no Tsukasa. Since there is no escape now, will you perform Katsupuku in a Jinjiyō manner, or shall I, Karlchin, strike you down? I await your Hentafu... You, the root cause of the Akugami who use the Magami of Ōgonhime and Kiyoteruhime to cause Kakuran in Iruna Castle and lead people astray, your hundredth year has come, so prepare yourself.'

Startled by this voice, Ōgonhime, Kiyoteruhime, Serisu-hime, and the other Kinji each drew their spears and Naginata, and before they could even say, 'You insolent rebels, suffer this divine punishment,' they lunged forward.

Karlchin and Yūfutesu, shouting 'How insolent,' fought with the ferocity of a lion, swinging their torches and slashing away.

The intensity of the sparks flying as they clashed, high and low, was immense.

Eventually, the King, Ōgonhime, Kiyoteruhime, Yasudara-hime, Serisu-hime, and all the others were struck down without a single survivor.

The blades held by Karlchin's faction were either broken or notched like the teeth of a saw.

Karlchin ordered his subordinates to carry the corpses out of the gate one by one, and with a splash, they committed them to a water burial in the raging current of the Iruna River.

Intending to hold a victory banquet, they advanced into the inner palace again, drinking and exchanging cups, passing the time with boasts and stories of their success, and their loud laughter echoed even outside the gate.

In the seat of honor sat Karlchin, leaning against an armrest like a king, having them bring out the finest sake from the storehouse, while the band of fifteen or sixteen men sat cross-legged, indulging in unceremonious chatter.

Yūfutesu: 'Umori-sama, no, no, Kshatriya-sama, you have performed truly miraculous and mysterious deeds. First of all, we can rest easy now. Please, since today is a joyous day, enjoy yourself to the fullest. Even I, Yūfutesu, feel my spirits rising.'

Karlchin: 'No matter what anyone says, my strategy and planning were flawless, so I knew today's success was certain from the start. Ha-ha-ha-ha! As for Yasudara-hime, who charged at me like a Yasha, I cannot tell you how much I agonized over wanting to save her, but since my own life was in danger if I hesitated, the moment my skilled spear tip struck and I let out a cry, her head danced into the air; the joy and regret of that moment is a sorrow that will last a thousand years, eh.'

Yūfutesu: 'Well, if one is to carry out such a great ambition, some sacrifices must be made. However, even for me, Yūfutesu, the regret and pleasure of cutting down Serisu-hime—I cannot even describe it; when I think of it, tears of love fall, ah.'

Manmosu, his tongue already slurring, said: 'He-he-he, I sympathize with you both for cruelly crushing the jewels in your palms. Thanks to good fortune, Samoa-hime did not appear here, so her life is spared. Thinking of that, there is no one as happy as this Manmosu.'

Yūfutesu: 'Ufu-fu-fu, what are you saying? Are you still clinging to Samoa-hime, who gave you the cold shoulder? You are a troublesome fellow. There is no one as unfortunate as you. Karlchin-sama, or rather Kshatriya-sama, and I, Yū-sama, have cut down the women who were our rivals in love and who gave us the cold shoulder, so we have already shed our attachments; there is no happiness like this. Since Samoa still remains in this world for you, you must be quite anxious. He-he-he, I hate to say it, but Samoa-hime is definitely smitten with me. We tacitly agreed on that when we met the other day. The gaze she cast toward me, Yū-sama, was truly filled with sincerity. She sent out radio waves so strong they dazzled my eyes. Now that it has come to this, Manmosu, you should give up... no, you would be better off resigning yourself.'

§ 2

At this moment, Samarihime, Samoahime, and Haruman arrived in a flurry, and upon seeing this state of affairs,

Samarihime: 'Tōsama, surely King Seran must be in Gokenzen?'

Karchin: 'Hmm, well, he is likely in Gokenzen in some country or other.'

Samarihime: 'You surely have not plotted any Fuki, have you? If by Manmanichi you have done such a thing, I am the consort of King Seran, and I must avenge the King. When the time comes, for parent and child, master and servant to cross blades and contend is the way of the Mononofu; you must have sufficient Kakugo for that, must you not?'

Karchin: 'No matter if it is for the sake of your husband, where is there a child who would Hamuka against a parent? You Fukaumono, get down!'

He spat this out as if scolding her from a heart of triumph.

At this time, from within a haze that could not be distinguished as smoke or fog, appearing suddenly in Shiroshiyōzoku was the Iurei of the King. The King said in a faint voice,

King's Iurei: 'I was subjected to a Yashifu by Karchin and my Inochi was taken... You, Samarihime, if you hold your husband as Daiji, take the Inochi of Karchin.'

Samarihime: 'Ya, so it was you, Karchin, who killed the Ōsama. I shall no longer show any Reuken. Prepare yourself to be the rust on the blade of this Samarihime.'

No sooner had she taken the Naginata that had fallen Soko than she swung it like a Mizuguruma and raged wildly. Karchin, too, in a Shinimonoguru, drew his Daitau with a flourish and struck at Samarihime; cruelly, Samarihime was slashed seven or eight sun across the shoulder, staggered back seven or eight paces, collapsed, gnashed her teeth in frustration, and breathed her last on that spot.

Karchin: 'Aha! That girl dared to meddle in a place of Daiji and hindered my great ambition; divine punishment has arrived instantly, and this miserable state—though she is my own child, I have lost all affection for her. Aha!'

Though he masked it with a boisterous laugh, it was a voice somehow laden with sorrow. Samoahime once again tucked her Naginata under her arm,

Samoahime: 'Know the consequence for the enemy of Hime-sama!'

Before she had even finished speaking, she struck at Karchin. Karchin nimbly dodged, leaping to the front, back, left, and right,

Karchin: 'Wait, wait for a moment!'

He cried out at the top of his lungs to restrain her while fleeing about. Samoahime paid no heed and struck at Karchin. Even the formidable Karchin lost his footing and fell splash into a well.

Yufutesu and Manmosu said,

Yufutesu and Manmosu: 'You ruffian, we shall show no mercy!'

They struck at Samoahime from the left and right. The sharpness of Samoahime's blade caused the two to lose their nerve; they threw their swords onto the ground and joined their hands in a pathetic plea for salvation. Haruman finally managed to pull Karchin up from the bottom of the well and, speaking fiercely to Samoahime,

Haruman: 'Wait for a while!'

He shouted with a roar. At this time, Samarihime, who had been slashed to death by Karchin, had returned to her original form before anyone knew it, and once again swung her Naginata like a Mizuguruma, striking at Karchin. Karchin said,

Karchin: 'There is nothing left but to fight to the bitter end now!'

As he spoke, he brandished his Naginata again, and they clashed blades with a metallic ring, fighting as sparks flew. Twelve or thirteen retainers were slashed down by the Naginata of Samarihime and Samoahime in the blink of an eye. At this point, the voice of a Sendenka was heard from nowhere.

The God appears in the world of light, To distinguish between good and evil. The God who created this world, Kamunao-bi, With a heart as vast as Ōnao-bi. In all things in the world of men, Look straight and listen straight with Naobi. Correct your own transgressions through proclamation, Such is the teaching of the true God. The temple of the Brahman religion, The Kshatriya of the castle of Iruna, Attacked under the cover of night, Destroying them and planning thereafter, To plunder the spoils, Karchin of the defiled heart. His fate, too, is in the land of the moon, In the garden of the castle of Iruna, Where blood washes blood between parent and child, This cruel and extreme living drama, Is all born from the delusions of the heart.

At this voice, Karlchin, Yufutesu, and Manmosu suddenly came to their senses, and when they looked, Samari-hime and Samoa-hime were nowhere to be seen, nor was there any sign of Haruman. They realized that they were all sitting heavily upon the soil in the garden within the castle, where the moon was shining brightly. That they had been shown such hallucinations was entirely due to the divine plan of Kami, and the result of the activities of Asahi, Tsukihi, and Takakura-myōjin. However, the fact that they had drunk sake was indeed true, and all of them were so drunk that their eyes were flickering and they could not even stand on their feet. While they were in this state, the sky turned red in the east, the night broke completely, and the brilliant winter sun shone obliquely upon the lower world.

Miru and Boruchi, the gatekeepers, woke up and were astonished to find Umori-no-tsukasa and the others dead drunk on the soil of the garden, and they ran into the inner palace.
