Chapter 19: Field 襲 [1144]

野襲〔1144〕

§ 1

In one room deep within Irunajiyō Castle, the four, Ōgonhime, Kiyoteruhime, Yasudarahime, and Sērisuhime, sat surrounding a hibachi, lost in myths, and their conversation shifted to the circumstances of Kāruchin.

Ōgonhime: 'Umoritsukasa also thought to attain the rank of Setsuteiri, and he racked his brains over and over, but in the end, he was beguiled by the beauty of Kiyoteruhime-sama. He tried to walk the two paths of greed and love, but he gained neither, and in the end, it seems he gave up... He sent soldiers to the land of Haruna, saying, "Alas, the widowed bird whose wings were stolen..." and other such complaints before the Ōsama, and then he returned. However, I do not believe he has truly repented. Surely, around this evening, he may gather the Shitsurengumi and come back to stir up trouble again, so everyone, you must never let your guard down.'

Sērisuhime: 'There should be no need for such worry. ...He said, "If it comes to this, I shall offer my life before the Lord and the King, the Kamuzukasa of Umori," so it is unlikely he could do such a thing. His three hundred horsemen have already been dispatched to the land of Haruna, and his military power has already been cut from the roots, so no matter how reckless Umori may be, he would not do such a foolish thing. ...He even said, "Come now, take my life, King Sēran, for I am one lost in greed and love," and offered his very life, after all.'

Kiyoteruhime: 'You cannot be so optimistic. The spite of love is a terrifying thing, you know. I impersonated Yasudarahime-sama and toyed with him with all my might, so how could a man lower his face and just let it go with a silent cry? As Mother says, surely around this evening, the Shitsurengumi will organize an assassination squad and come for us... He was spilling his grievances like a lingering attachment, saying, "I think sadly of the fact that I was loved and longed for by one I did not expect," so he will surely not let it go with a silent cry like this.'

Sērisuhime: 'And yet, did Umoritsukasa not declare before the Ōsama, "From now on, I shall be reborn as a babe and offer my sincerity to the God and the King"? At that moment, I felt those were words that came from the very bottom of Umoritsukasa's heart.'

Yasudarahime: 'Why must there be such endless dark strife in this Irunajiyō Castle? I have heard since long ago that the Samori and Umori are like dogs and cats. Is it not true that the proverb for those who do not get along is not "dog and monkey," but has even become the term "the Samori and Umori of Irunajiyō Castle"? I wish they would protect us so that such things do not happen, somehow.'

Ōgonhime: 'Ah, this may also be the path through which the way of sincerity opens. Nay, this is rather the noble protection of the Gods. It is said that if a King has five ministers who contend, he shall not lose his position; if a feudal lord has three ministers who contend, he shall not lose his country; and if a high official has two ministers who contend, he shall not lose his house. Thus, strife is something that can never be eradicated. Moreover, when strife is eradicated, that is the time when a nation perishes, so this must be the divine providence of Kannagara, where there is stillness in motion and motion in stillness. Because there was the conspiracy of Umoritsukasa, the prestige of King Sēran shines throughout the world. If everything were always peaceful and without incident, the hearts of the people, starting with the King, would slacken, the nation would decline more and more, politics would be neglected, and in the end, it would fall into the tragic fate of national self-destruction. Thinking of this, Umoritsukasa is also a pillar of the Irunajiyō nation; although his inner schemes are to be hated, because he plotted rebellion, the position of the King became all the more firm, the loosened hoops of Irunajiyō Castle were tightened, and it is as if the foundation for a hundred years of the nation was built. Therefore, now that Umoritsukasa has repented, we must forgive him to the end. Tell me, Yasudarahime-sama, what do you think?'

Speaker: 'Since it is the Kamisama who judges all matters of Zenakuseijiya, we cannot possibly judge the sins of Umoritsukasa. Furthermore, if we look within ourselves, how could we, who are ourselves stained with sin, judge others? There is no other way but to leave it entirely to Kamunagara.'

Speaker: 'That is true. Umoritsukasa is also a Bunrei of the same Kamisama, and is not an Akunin by nature. Since he has been possessed by an Akugami and attempted to carry out a Yashin that his Riyōshin would not permit, we must pity that Akugami and pity his Nikutai, and lead him back to the Zendau; otherwise, we cannot fulfill our duties as Sendenshi. As we are all Ujiko of the same Kamisama, we cannot face the Shinkai if even one person is cast into the abyss. If we discuss Umoritsukasa using the method of the Spring and Autumn Annals, we might see and hear that he is exerting loyal service to Inuna Castle, which protects the throne of Umoritsukasa. In a sense, he is a god of salvation for Inuna Castle. The bird called the hawk is a bird of prey that lives only by eating live bait, but when winter comes, its claws become cold and it cannot bear it; it is said that it catches a small bird, holds it gently with its claws to warm them, and then lets it go. And does it not carefully note the direction in which the small bird flees, and refrain from catching small birds in that direction for three days? Since even birds have such consideration, one might say the King is the hawk and Umoritsukasa is like the warming bird. Surely the wise King will forgive the sins of Umoritsukasa. In any case, they will likely come for a night attack this evening, but as long as the clans of Oeyama, Asahi, Tsukihime, and Takakura-myōjin-sama are protecting us, they will surely fail to achieve their goal and will come to repentance.'

As they were speaking, King Seran appeared, accompanied by Ryūun and other loyal retainers, and turning to Koganehime, said,

Speaker: 'Thanks to your various concerns, the dawn of peace has finally been sighted for Inuna Castle. It is entirely the result of the protection of you and your son, Koganehime-sama. I am grateful beyond words.'

He expressed his gratitude, and Koganehime replied with a poem:

Speaker: 'The moon and the sun appear in the castle of Inuna, Illuminating the teachings of the three-five moon. Revere the teachings of the three-five God, And protect this great path, my lord. Without distinguishing between good and evil in this world, The divine power of the God protects us all.'

Speaker: 'Now that I have realized the preciousness of the divine teachings, How foolish I have been. Upon my foolish heart, the light of wisdom Has been shone by the three-five God.'

Speaker: 'For the sake of the Great King and the sake of the country, I worried and forgot the God. Without the God, how can the country be governed? From now on, I shall follow the God with a single heart. Revering the God and the King, I shall teach and lead the people With sincerity.'

Speaker: 'If one walks the great path of the three-five, Can the evil Magatsu-kami truly hinder one? Even I, Ryūun, with my twisted heart, Have been illuminated by the God and become a true human. To know the God and to know the teachings Must be the first duty of a human being.'

Speaker: 'The divine power of the Imperial God is in the sky, and in Kiyoteruhime, The office of the God also shines in the heart. Now, the clouds and mists that shrouded Inuna Castle Feel as though they have been cleared away.'

Speaker: 'Even if the ugly clouds and mists shroud us for a little while longer, They shall be blown away by the breath of the God. Hear the command of King Seran, For tonight, Umori will surely be restless.'

Speaker: 'Even if Umoritsukasa rages, My body is protected by the divine shield. I leave it to the teachings of the God, Kamunagara, And have no clouds of worry in my heart. Both sorrow and trembling have vanished, For I am illuminated by the light of the God.'

Speaker: 'My King, do not let your guard down, For we live in an age where time passes as swiftly as a galloping horse.'

Speaker: 'I now follow the office of the God, And feel as if I am living in Takamagahara. Yet, I do not let my guard down, knowing that even Takamagahara Is a world where suffering mingles.'

As they sang thus, suddenly, due to the commotion at the Genkuwanguchi, Rebu received permission from the Ichidō and ran out to the front to perform a Shisatsu. When Rebu held his breath and peered at the Yōsu outside, the Umoritsukasa, along with Euphrates, Mammoth, and over a dozen others, were sitting heavily on the ground without even spreading a Shikimono, and while drinking sake from the gourds they carried, they were clapping their hands and singing incessantly. Just when one thought that, they would draw their Daitau, cut the air, run about to the right and left, and then fall down with a thud only to Okiaga, performing a strange and bizarre state of madness. Rebu, his suspicion not cleared, immediately returned to the inner palace and reported to the King.

'I humbly report: hearing the noise in the garden, I peered to see what was the matter as commanded, and to my surprise, the Umoritsukasa was sitting heavily at the gate with over a dozen subordinates, drinking sake and singing; and when I looked, they were drawing their long swords and slashing about in all directions. Judging from the situation, they must have been deceived by the White Fox-sama and are holding a sake banquet, feeling good on the moonlit ground. The Umoritsukasa was reciting poetry in a very fine voice... Whether raising a strategy under the moon of the long shore, or rowing a boat on the waves of the curved bay, when the gibbon cries once and the boat is moored near the bright moon gorge, the barbarian horse suddenly neighs and loses its way behind the yellow sand dunes... he was performing a sword dance with such high spirits. Judging from that poem, it seems he is dreaming of banishing the King and seizing the world for himself. It is truly a scene of drunken madness that is quite a sight to behold.'

'He will wake up soon enough, so it would be best to leave him be until tomorrow morning. Since he is enjoying himself dreaming that he has finally taken the world, it would be pitiful to wake him halfway. In this world, there are those who wish to take the world even if it is only in a dream, so it would be best to let him enjoy it for as long as possible without waking him. Aha-ha-ha-ha.'

Ōgonhime: 'Oho-ho-ho-ho. The King has also developed a great deal of benevolence. Without such a heart, one cannot become the head of others. Now, everyone, let us sleep soundly until tomorrow morning. We shall be able to see another interesting play tomorrow.'

'Mother-sama, I have a request, but would it not be possible for me alone to go out to that place for a moment? I shall not change my heart and send amorous glances to the Umoritsukasa-sama, so...'

'Oho-ho-ho-ho. No matter what you say, it is too dangerous and unbearable, so Kiyosama-san, you must not leave my side for even a moment; it is like leaving a bonito flake with a cat. Oho-ho.'

'So that Mother-sama will not worry, Seris-hime-sama, shall we two go together? If we do that, Mother-sama will surely not be worried.'
