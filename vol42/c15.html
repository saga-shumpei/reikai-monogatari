Chapter 15: 帰 Castle [1140]

帰城〔1140〕

§ 1

The Kamuzukasa of the eye of Ame, leaving behind the Gankutsu of Takateruyama where Takeno-hime-sama dwells, walked briskly away. Leading Ōgonhime-sama and many Tomobito including Thames, Rebu, Karu, and Riida, as well as the Kami no Inochi and Riūn of the scenery of Yomo and Kiyoteruhime-sama, the Hime no Inochi of King Seran and Yasudara rode proudly on their steeds across the Aranohara, exposed to the blowing wind of the mountain path where wolves howl. Crossing the Teruyamatauge, they returned with bridles aligned, their Misugata truly Ō.

At the moment they returned to the entrance of the capital of Iruna, the Kamuzukasa of Samori, leading his Rautau, came out to greet them with great Ingin. Rejoicing and emboldened, he guarded the return of King Seran with his soldiers, and they finally returned to the castle with drums and flags in grand procession.

Ōgonhime-sama locked herself in a room in the back, and the Kamuzukasa of Migimori, using the voice of King Seran, manipulated everything at will. Kiyoteruhime-sama, assuming the name of the Hime no Inochi of Yasudara, brandished the Kototama sword and tormented the Migimori who had gone mad with love.

At that moment, the Kamuzukasa Miru, whose back was bent and who served with humility and awe at the reception, appeared before the Kamuzukasa of Migimori.

Upon reporting that the party of King Seran, along with their many Tomobito, had just returned to the castle, he was struck with terror. Looking around restlessly, he sat down with a thud, clasping his hands together, finding the mysterious turn of events deeply troubling.

The party of King Seran advanced into the inner chamber, and seeing Kiyoteruhime-sama and Karuchin, both sat silently with their heads bowed. King Seran immediately asked with suspicion in a thirty-one-syllable poem:

King Seran: 'Did I ever think that you, Karuchin, the Kamuzukasa of Migimori, would be in my dwelling that prospers for a thousand generations? I know not what has occurred, but you are a fool who violates this pure dwelling. Quickly, Kamuzukasa of Migimori, purify my dwelling and depart, according to the will of the Kami. It is strange, for the voice of a demon spirit echoes, wrapping around the inside and outside of the castle of Iruna.'

Yasudara: 'Are you not Karuchin, the Kamuzukasa of Migimori, who oppressed my body? Karuchin, purify your heart, which is the root of your soul, as quickly as possible, and return to sincerity. I did not think there were two Himegami of Yasudara, for the Himegami of Yasudara is but one.'

Kiyoteruhime: 'Since the soul of the Himegami of Yasudara shines as Kiyoteruhime-sama, I have borrowed your noble name for a while, only because I wished to save the ugly one.'

Yasudara: 'Where is the Hime no Inochi of Ōgonhime now? I long to hear news of her.'

Kiyoteruhime: 'The Hime no Inochi of Ōgonhime is in the back room, using the voice of King Seran. The mother and daughter are racking their hearts to wash the soul of the ugly Karuchin.'

Karuchin: 'I never thought I would end up a captive of love and suffer such shame. I sent soldiers to the land of Haruna, and like a widowed bird whose wings have been clipped, I am pitiful. If it has come to this, I, the Kamuzukasa of Migimori, shall offer my life before you. Come then, take my life, King Seran, for I am lost in greed and love.'

Seran: 'No matter how much sin or impurity there may be, the Kami of Naohi will surely forgive you. Manipulated by the demon of love in many ways, your eyes as a Kamuzukasa must have awakened.'

Ōgonhime-sama pushed open the sliding door from the back room and came out smiling. Facing King Seran and Hime-sama Yasudara, she bowed and recited a thirty-one-syllable poem:

Ōgonhime: 'How grateful and how awe-inspiring it is, to see the safe return of my noble lord to the castle. It is as if the parents and children who guard the rear of the Great Lord have seen a mysterious dream. The way Karuchin, Yufutesu, and others wander in the paths of love is truly amusing. I have endured the laughter that would break my bowels, waiting for this very day.'

§ 2

Karuchin: 'Having cast aside the wife to whom I pledged until the second life, I turned my heart toward an unexpected direction. I feel sorrowful thinking of how I was loved and longed for by one I did not love. Now, the darkness of my heart has cleared away, and I behold the light of the moon of Shinniyo.'

Riūn: 'I, too, once served in the base work similar to the office of Umori. Yet, the Ōkami of deep Mimegumi did not blame me, but let me live. O official of the god Karuchin, listen well; repentance is the treasure of man.'

Karuchin: 'How awe-inspiring, the Mikotoba of Riūntsukasa resounds like the voice of a saving Kami. From now on, I shall be reborn as an Akago and offer my sincerity to the Kami and the King.'

Tēmusu: 'The Murakumo that enveloped the inside and outside of Iruna Castle has cleared, and what a joyful sky it is today.'

Rīdā: 'Having departed from the Teruman country and traveled Harubaru, I have seen a joyful dream today.'

Rēbu: 'I, too, was not a blessed being from the beginning, but a Magatsukami who rebelled against my lord. Yet, illuminated by the precious Mihikari of the Kami, I have now become a Mabito.'

Karu: 'Following the army of Ōkuronushi, I stepped off the path and fell into the Tanima. How joyful it is that Terukuniwake appeared in this world to save me.'

Kiyoteru: 'With gratitude and humility, recite the Futonorito before the Ōmahe, morning and evening. The Mikoto of the lord of Sēran is the original Setsuteiri, whose honor is high in Iruna Castle. Please look upon Umori, who plotted various evils, with Minaho and save him. Do not anger the Mikoto of Kiyoteruhime-kami, O official Umori.'

Sērisuhime-sama, hearing of the King's return to the castle, came running in haste, 'How rare, I shall celebrate the return to the castle of King Sēran, my elder sister, and the hundred officials. Ever since I heard that my elder sister Yasudara was returning, I have been looking up at the sky of Mount Takateru.'
