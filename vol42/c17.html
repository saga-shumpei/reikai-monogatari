Chapter 17: 酒 Moon [1142]

酒月〔1142〕

§ 1

It was the beginning of winter, and the sound of the Kogarashi wind passing through the Kigi was as fierce as the roaring of a Maujiu in the Yufumagure. Miru and Boruchi, who were guarding the Omotemon of Iruna Castle, were, as is the custom of Monban, whiling away their Mureu by playing Shiyōgi and Go from morning till night. After Nichibotsugo, as there was no longer any need for the Kaihei of the gate, they were clicking their tongues in appreciation of strong sake, feeling like Shiyosei discussing Tenka on the second floor of their Geshiyuku, and passing the time in a Giron that did not suit their nature.

Miru: 'Oi, Boruchi, since Ōsama Seran returned today after a long time with many Gokerai, Oretachi have also somehow come to feel a warm Kibun all over. When the Kanjin Shiyujin is Fuzai, it feels cold and lonely everywhere. Let us celebrate today by getting drunk on Torotsupiki, shall we?'

Boruchi: 'Somehow, due to the sake, my cold body has also started to warm up. However, you, you must have heard, but they say he is going to abdicate the throne to that Karuchin, is that really true? Why on earth would he do something so strange, abandoning the throne and yielding it to such an unpopular Karuchin? Perhaps the world is so chaotic that he has grown tired of politics, and intends to abandon us subjects and the people, and lead a refined life in the mountains and forests, making friends with the clear breeze and the bright moon.'

Miru: 'No matter what one says, one cannot defy the power of the times, even if one is a god, so it cannot be helped. They say that power is transferred through abdication or conquest, and it is not the case that the world will remain in one's hands forever. Perhaps it is good that the head of the world changes from time to time, as it refreshes the popularity of the regime. It must be that the time has come for Karuchin-sama to become a Kshatriya, according to the arrangements of the Shinkai.'

Boruchi: 'Is it not a bit out of order to yield the throne to the Right-Guardian, who is one rank lower, while passing over the Left-Guardian-sama?'

Miru: 'In the Han dynasty, there was an Emperor called Teigyō. After seventy years on the throne, being already old, he asked his ministers to whom he should yield the Tenka. The ministers all flattered him, saying that since the Crown Prince was present, he should yield it to Tanshu. Then, the Emperor Teigyō said that the Tenka is not the Tenka of one man, and why should he yield the throne to one who lacks virtue and does not know the true meaning of government just because he is his own son, and thereby cause the people of the four seas to suffer? He did not yield it to Tanshu, the Crown Prince, but sought out where a wise man might be in the fields, even among those living in seclusion. Finally, in a place called Bizan, there was a wise man named Kyoyū who had abandoned the world and hidden his light, hanging a single gourd on a rock where the moss was deep and the pines were thin, awakening from the dreams of human delusion with the sound of the wind. When Emperor Teigyō heard of this, he sent an imperial messenger to command him to take the throne, but Kyoyū would not answer the imperial decree. Moreover, he said that his ears, which had finally become refreshed by listening to the clear sound of the pine wind and the mountain stream, had been dirtied by hearing of such base things as wealth and honor, and he washed his ears in the water of the Eigawa. A wise man named Sōho, who had also abandoned his body and lived in seclusion in the same mountains, came leading an ox, and seeing Kyoyū washing his ears incessantly, asked him wonderingly why he was washing his ears. Then Kyoyū replied that he felt as if his ears had been dirtied by hearing Emperor Teigyō say he would yield the Tenka to him, and that was why he was washing them. Sōho also shook his head and said that indeed, that was why the water had looked muddier than usual, and that if he let his ox drink water used to wash such dirty ears, his precious ox would be dirtied, and he led his ox away and returned home. Then, Emperor Teigyō, wondering to whom he should yield this Tenka, searched everywhere, and as a result, there was a lowly man named Goshun in Kishū. His father was a blind man, his mother was a woman whose spirit was twisted and who did not follow words of loyalty and faith, nor did she conform to the path of virtue, and his younger brother was a rogue named Shō, who was arrogant and rebellious. Only Goshun was deeply filial, and when he went to Rekizan to plow to support his parents, the other people were moved by his virtue and yielded the ridges to him; when he went down to Raisaku to fish, the people of the shore yielded their dwellings to him; when he made pottery at the riverbank, his vessels were all without flaws; and wherever Shun went and stayed, a village was formed in two years, and a capital was formed in three years, and he was a splendid man to whom all people were drawn by his virtue. When he was twenty, his reputation for filial piety was known throughout Tenka, so Emperor Teigyō thought to yield the Tenka to this man, and wishing to observe his conduct, he gave his two daughters, Princesses Kōō and Ei, to Shun as wives. And it was in the China of three hundred thousand years in the future that he had his nine children serve Shun as subjects, following him cautiously at his side. Since Karuchin-sama is also having the throne yielded to him by Ōsama through divine virtue, it is quite a thing, ah, Gēutsu-pū-garagara-gara, the sake again, because the weather is bad, it has come flowing back up. Aahn.'

Speaker: 'You are taking the conversation to a strange place, are you not? However, that King called Teigeu of the Kanchō dynasty is a strange one. He had not a single point of shirishiyoku, not a particle of that wareyoshi which would lead one to buy biden for the sake of one's shison, and his goseishin of wishing to abdicate the world to seijin and kenjin to bring peace to the sekai and banmin is something that should be a mohan for any jinshiyu. It seems that our King Sēran is also a meikun like King Teigeu. However, in my opinion, I cannot think of Umorinokami Karuchin as being such a seijin or kenjin. The only point that sticks is that he is the father of Hime-sama Samari, and perhaps that is why he will be granted the setsuteiri. If that happens, we and the rest of the kokumin will have to suffer a taihen meiwaku.'

Speaker: 'How would we monban know such things? Unless one is a Kami, one cannot understand zenakuseijiya. As a lowly shinka, even if we were to criticize such matters, it would not be worth a fart. Rather than that, why don't you happily drink some hannyatō?'

Speaker: 'That fellow called Kyoyu, I do not know if he is a kenjin, but from my perspective, he is quite a fool. They say that under the heavens and upon the earth, there is nothing that is not the land and subjects of the King. Even regarding the mountains and forests that the King protects, where is the fool who, while living in the land, does not receive the imperial edict of the King, and becomes a hermit of wareyoshi who forgets the state, claiming to enjoy the cool breeze and the bright moon? I am utterly disgusted because there are many people nowadays who praise such stubborn fools as seijin and kenjin. Sōho is also a fool who would break a stick if he tried to carry it. He looks quite poverty-stricken. To say that if a cow drinks the water from a river where one has washed one's ears, the cow will be defiled—is he not a fool beyond measure? When it comes to that, Goshun is great. He received two of the King's himegimi as his wives and eventually took the whole world for himself, so from our way of thinking, he is a quite ideal figure. That he has been praised even by people of later generations as the age of Gyōshun is because Goshun exercised the work of his mitama in a free and flexible manner. Compared to Shun, Kyoyu and Sōho are not even worth talking about, are they?'

Speaker: 'That is true. Even if I were Kyoyu, instead of washing my ears, I would have accepted it with a double reply, and like a cat pouncing on a bonito, I would have latched onto it without any hesitation. However, Karuchin-sama is also of the Goshun school. Since he accepts the setsuteiri without a second thought, he is indeed great. Holding over three hundred cavalrymen, he has successfully jumped into the throat of Daikokunushi and is gripping his testicles, so his luck has turned today. Unlike the Sadaijin-sama who says that weapons are instruments of evil and that soldiers are not needed to rule the people with the path of sincerity, he does not keep anything resembling a soldier, not even for himself or the King, so perhaps he is being forced to abdicate with the feeling that one cannot resist the momentum of the times. Thinking of this, after all, power is what matters for a human. To be skilled in the art of social intercourse, to wag one's tail at the powerful, to bow one's head, and to move agilely—that is the winner in society.'

Speaker: 'Hey, Miru, even if Karuchin-sama becomes the setsuteiri, do you still intend to serve as a monban faithfully? Surely you would not do such a foolish thing. After all, there is the saying that a loyal subject does not serve two masters.'

Speaker: 'That is not something to be announced right now. I have my own perspective as a person... The night seems to have grown quite late. Well, shall we go to sleep now?'

§ 3

Hardly had he said this when he tumbled over in a drunken stupor, rowing the Shirakahayobune and instantly departing for the land of Kuwashiyo.

Unable to sleep due to Miru's Takaibiki, Boruchī, in a state of Horoyohi, slid open the wicket gate with a rattle, and seeking to sober up by exposing his flushed face to the Yokaze, began to wander aimlessly in front of the Monzen.

Orikara, the moonlight, having pushed aside the clouds, shone mercilessly upon Boruchī's head.

Boruchī: 'Ah, what a fine Kigen. The autumn moon is pleasant, but the moon hanging in the winter night sky has a Sugomi from somewhere, and this too is a kind of Fūriu. That Gaki called the moon is never unpleasant to look at, no matter when. Yet, compared to Nitsutensama, it is immensely lacking in wit. It lets itself be made an Omochiya by the Ningen-sama, with their Mamemeigetsu, Imomeigetsu, and Tsukimi banquets, and remains in the sky in a state of Heiki and Heiza. Gae, garagara-gara, ah, it is painful, it is painful; I have spoken too many Kogoto against the moon, and I feel as if I might Hedo. I am Hedo-tsuki, Mago-tsuki, and kyorotsuki, and because I could not sleep well, I have been pushed out to a place like this. The personage called Nitsutensama has Shini that is Kuwakukuwaku and inviolable; if one were to prepare sake and food in the middle of the day like one does for the moon-sama, and try to worship His face by saying one wishes to gaze at the sun, one's eyes would instantly go blind, one's head would throb, and the divine punishment would strike immediately. Daijizaitensama is the sun. They say that San-iku-kyō is the moon-heaven-sama, but since the divine power of Daijizaiten and Miroku-sama differs to this extent, our Brahmanism is indeed the finest teaching under heaven.'

Thus he was rambling and chirping to himself.

It was then that the leader of the broken-hearted group, Kāruchin, followed by Yūfutesu, Manmosu, and their men, arrived in a flurry.

Boruchī bumped squarely into Manmosu, who was leading the group, and while staggering back five or six steps, he sat down with a thud upon the withered grass by the roadside.

Upon the grass, the swords of frost were flashing, illuminated by the moon.

Boruchī: 'Ouch, ouch, who are you, bumping into people? Why don't you say you are sorry, huh? There is a limit to not knowing etiquette. Who on earth are you? Who do you think I am? I am Boruchī-sama, the gatekeeper of Iruna Castle, you know.'

Manmosu: 'Ya, we have met at a good time. You, you shall lead us from here and open the front gate. Now, stand up.'

Boruchī: 'I am a little bit drunk, so I will gaze at the moon here for a while to sober up; you go on ahead. I have left the wicket gate open... Ya, what a lot of black hoods there are.'

Manmosu: 'The future Kshatriya, Kāruchin-sama, is passing through.'

With shoulders squared and elbows flared, he advanced into the castle with the party.
