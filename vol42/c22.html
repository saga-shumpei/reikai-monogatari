Chapter 22: 別離の Song [1147]

別離の歌〔1147〕

§ 1

Ōgonhime sings a Sendenka as she faces her departure.

Ōgonhime: 'Though the morning sun may shine or cloud over, / Though the moon may wax or wane, / Even if the earth should sink, / The one truth of the Ananahi / Oshihe path shall save the world. / Since leaving the Iso mansion, / Protected by the grace of Kami, / Through the Aranohara where tigers, wolves, lions, and bears / Fiercely rage, / Gradually crossing over, guided to the / Precious Ihaya of Takateru, / While receiving the Kuntau of the Kami / Of the Kitateru with infinite Shintoku, / Parent and child bravely entered Irunajiyō. / The Kurokumo blocking the Yomo / Were blown away by the power of Kami, / And the Kinan of King Seran / Was saved and aided; now, / Tenchiseimei and Jitsugetsu / Shine in the Amatsumisora. / Though it is called winter, the flowers are fragrant, / Feeling like the spring of Tokoyo. / The turbulence of Magatsu has subsided, / And I, who am departing, am joyful. / King Seran, hear me. / No matter what may come to pass, / The Samarihime, the consort / Given by the Kami of heaven and earth, / You must never abandon. / The path of husband and wife, likened to heaven and earth, / Must be guarded and advanced forever. / Husband and wife are the foundation of the path. / Yasudarahime, the Kami-tsukasa, / Shall never cause your life's heart / To be troubled, / And blowing away the clouds of attachment / With the wind of Shinato, / Establish the sincerity of heaven and earth / For a thousand generations and forever, Kannagara. / Praying, I vow to the Kami. / From here on, aided by the grace / Of the Kami of heaven and earth, / Advancing through the Aranohara where the Majin rage, / By day and by night, / To the Haruna castle where the flowers of joy bloom, / To speak to the Magatsukami nesting / In the physical body of Ōkuninushi, / And to fulfill the heavenly duty of the Kami-tsukasa, / Kannagara. / The Kami is with us. / Kitateru-kami, King Seran, / Pray with hearts calmed at the Kamidoko / That the path I tread may be protected / Without mishap. / Heaven and earth, together with the life / Of Kiyoteruhime of Kiyoteru, / The era of peace is also the Kami-tsukasa / Of the noble life of Yasudarahime, / The Senden-shi of the Ananahi-oshihe. / With Haruman of Komahiko-tsukasa, / Here the four of us travel together, / Looking back with lingering affection / At the pure and noble sacred castle. / From here I depart. / Ah, Kannagara, Kami, Kami, / May your spirits be blessed.'

Kiyoteruhime sings again.

Kiyoteruhime: 'The Kami appears on the surface / To stand and distinguish between good and evil Kami. / The Senden-shi of the Ananahi-oshihe, / Kiyoteruhime, the Kami-tsukasa. / With the heart's horse set straight / Upon the pure and noble teachings, / Whipping on the Hizamurige, / With light heart, wearing straw raincoat and hat, / Body braced with straw sandals and leggings, / In the Aranohara where the fierce tiger rages, / Striking out with the Kototama of good words and beautiful phrases, / How brave it is to advance majestically. / Even if some Magatsukami should block / The path ahead, / What is there to fear? The Yamato-gokoro / Of Shikishima shines clearly. / Shooting arrows from the horse of benevolence, / There shall be no ugly Magatsu-rei. / Ah, how brave, how brave! / The Kurokumo of the Iruna castle / Have cleared away, pure and cool, / And the sky today reveals / The state of the heavenly pure land. / There is no lingering in my heart. / Now, from here, parent and child / Shall join hearts and solidify their strength / Into one, and toward the land of the moon, / Speaking to all the obstructing Magatsu / With the spirit of Mizu, / We shall advance, Kannagara. / To the noble life of Kitateru-kami and King Seran, / I joyfully and respectfully bid farewell. / Ah, Kannagara, Kami, Kami, / May your spirits be blessed.'

Yasudarahime sings again.

Yasudarahime: 'The Kshatriya of the land of Iruna, / Born into a noble lineage, / Kurinsu of the Left-Mamori-tsukasa, / As the noble child of the Kami-tsukasa, / I, Yasudarahime, born to meet him, / By the command of the Kami, now here, / Leaving behind the land where my beloved father / And the Great King reside, / Truly following the noble Ananahi, / The Kami-tsukasa of Ōgonhime, / And Kiyoteruhime, / I ascend the path of the conquest of demons. / Ah, Kannagara, Kami, Kami, / May your spirits be blessed, / And upon the soul of Yasudarahime, / Pour down the infinite and eternal power of the Kami. / Before the Great Imperial Kami of heaven and earth, / I respectfully and reverently pray. / Though the morning sun may shine or cloud over, / Though the moon may wax or wane, / Even if the earth should sink, / The power of sincerity shall save the world. / Guarding the one truth of the Ananahi / Oshihe forever, / May you protect the land of Iruna in peace. / Before the Great King, respectfully, Yasudarahime, / Respectfully and in awe, here and now, / Facing departure, I pray. / The flowers of the heavenly pure land bloom, / And in the Miroku age of the Kami, the Kitateru, / The one-eyed Kami-tsukasa of heaven, / When you return to Takateru-yama, / To your wife-kami, Takenohime, / Before her, Yasudarahime, / Please convey in detail the joy / Of having received various graces / From the Michi-no-tsukasa, and that I am grateful, Kannagara.'

§ 2

I swear to Kami and pray: From this moment, following the command of Ōgonhime-no-Mikoto, I shall traverse the wilderness, combing my hair in the storms, my body soaked by sleet and snow, and clad in the humble minokasa, I shall advance with light steps. O King Seran, O Hime-sama Samari, after I have departed, I pray that you live in harmony, helping one another in the Shingefu of the Kami of Ametsuchi. I pray as I depart, O Kamuzukasa Yasudarahime-sama, at this parting. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, may your Mitama be blessed.

Haruman sings again.

I am the Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, the Kamuzukasa Komahiko. By the Mikoto of Kotoyoriwake, I disguised myself and stole into the land of Iruna three years ago. I took refuge in the mansion of the Umoritsukasa, who flourishes in the Shiyōka, and descended to become a lowly shimobe. In order to lead all the Magabitotachi, who are lost in the Brahman religion, back to the true path and report to the Mimahe of Kami, I prayed to Kami Asayufu, and my efforts were not in vain. The Tokitsukaze is blowing ever stronger, and my Shiyokuseki is now finally fulfilled. I shall now advance to conquer the Yamata-no-Orochi that dwells in the capital of Haruna, the land of the moon where the Magatsu-no-kami rages, together with the pillar of Ananai-kyō, Ōgonhime.

It is valiant to advance to the conquest. Three years have passed since I left the sacred grounds of Mount Caucasus, and though I lamented in my heart that I had spent my days in vain, the flower of sincerity of the Ananai-kyō, which heralds the return of the sun, has bloomed. With a heart as spirited as a spring horse, I have changed my name to Haruman and advance in a dignified manner. Ah, how wonderful, how wonderful. O Hokkō-no-kami, O Ōkimi, after I have departed, I pray that you illuminate the Oshihe of the Kami of Ametsuchi Asayufu, and forever protect the people in peace and tranquility. Carrying the Mitama of the noble Ōkami upon my back, I leave behind the capital of Iruna, which I have come to know as my second home, and bid it farewell here. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, may your Mitama be blessed.

Now then, Komahiko, with a spirited heart, I shall set out and head toward the capital of Haruna. O Ōkimi, may your heart be at peace, for the Kami dwells within your soul. I speak to the Mikoto of the Hokkō-no-kami: please protect the path where I am going.

Hokkō: 'Are you truly the Kamuzukasa Komahiko, the Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō? How very rare. The Keirin of the Kami of Ametsuchi reaches everywhere; what a noble age it is. How foolish I was not to realize that the Kamuzukasa Haruman was Komahiko.'

Seishō: 'Were the Kamuzukasa of Seishōhime and Komahiko all of the same kind as monsters? I am but a small monster, and I find Komahiko to be a great monster, which I fear.'

Komahiko: 'I shall ask Seishōhime-no-Mikoto: is your heart not that of a monster? Your Mikoto is even more terrifying than mine, for you toyed with the Umoritsukasa. I was used by the Umoritsukasa and endured the shame of being a shimobe. When I look back, compared to you, I am truly but a small monster.'

Ōgon: 'At the end of the world, great monsters will appear and devour the wise and the scholars. Most of the people who flourish in the Genkai are vessels of great monsters. The path of Ananai is what leads and reconciles all the great monsters of the world.'

Yasudara: 'How interesting, a monster of Kami appearing in an age plagued by great monsters. Karlchin, whose soul was washed after being deceived, I too wish to be deceived.'

Karlchin: 'Manipulated by Seishōhime-no-Mikoto, I was crushed and became a Mahito. I now realize the truth that in this world, good and evil are not two, and right and wrong are one. I know that what we call good and what we call evil are but the tamagaki that divide the world of men.'
