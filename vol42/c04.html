Chapter 4: 下り坂 [1129]

下り坂〔1129〕

§ 1

Rebu: 'Tsukihi are shining in the sky, descending the steep slope of Teruyama pass. Following King Seran, high high high high, dough dough dough. If I do not descend this slope, I cannot go to the capital of Iruna. Karuchin, the official of Umori, has been possessed by the Akuma of greed. He has come this far with his Akugiyakubudau schemes, but there is no way such schemes will ever be accomplished. High high high high, you Chikushiyō, pull yourself together and be careful. If you stumble on a sharp stone and fall into the Chihiro valley, you will die instantly. Ha ha high high, what a steep, steep Sakamichi. Kamutsukasa of Yasudahime-hime.'

Kamutsukasa of Yasudahime-hime: 'From now on, just a little more Goshinbau, and soon the capital will be visible. Even if that fellow Karuchin plots various evil things, as long as the Kami of Tenchi exist, there is no precedent for evil to prosper. It will vanish instantly like spring snow. High high high high, Haibō is as if seen in a mirror. Dotukkoi, Chikushiyō, be careful. You are just passing Mamebe, truly, truly, ha ha ha. Ōbakamono, you Chikushiyō, why is my horse so Tonma? Gara gara gara gara, aitatsuta. Hin hin hin hin, you Chikushiyō. Is it funny that I fell? You are a four-legged creature, while Rebu-sama is a two-legged creature by birth.'

Rebu: 'How can a two-legged creature and a four-legged creature compete? Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. Baramon Tenō, dotukkoishiyo. I should not have said that. I pray solely for the protection of Kuniharutachinōokami, the Mototsu-kami who created Tenchi. What shall I do if I encounter the Teshita of the Umori official at such a Nanshiyo? Rebu does not care at all, but what weighs on my heart is the King and the fate of Yasudahime-hime. Ryūun-sama, be firm. Your horse is also suspicious. When I look from behind, the state of your horse with its tail tucked in is something I cannot bear to look at with my own eyes. Ha ha ha ha, high high high.'

Ryūun: 'You Chikushiyō, be careful. Why are you biting my head? It was a close call, and my hat stand was about to be bitten off. Even if you seem clever, you are a Chikushiyō. This fellow has a large frame, yet he lets out whinnies and passes gas, is handled by a small man, is ridden on his back and whipped, and yet he follows along so obediently, ha ha ha. You foolish fellow. Hey, hey, excuse me, Ryūun-sama, why do you go ahead and leave me behind? My compass is capable, but this essential horse of mine will not walk as I wish. Try being in my heart, leading a horse that passes gas. Truly, truly, it is frustrating.'

Ryūun: 'Nevertheless, high high high. I have heard that even a slug, if it walks a hundred thousand ri, will eventually reach its destination. Teruyama pass is a Nanshiyo worthy of its name. When I have safely led the horse down this place, I will set the horse straight again, summon my courage, and with the momentum of a waterfall falling a thousand ri, proceed majestically to the holy castle of Iruna. Without any suffering, under the moonlit sky, ha ha ha ha, high high high. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, whether Teruyama pass is steep or not, I will cross this journey before I know it. The future is quite promising, and the capital of victory is drawing near.'

Ryūun: 'The Kamutsukasa of the golden world are stretching their necks and waiting. To worship the stern faces of Yoshikohime-hime and Kiyoteruhime-hime is gradually drawing closer. Following the teachings of the Kami of Hokkō, I shall turn all the Kyokutsukasa who dwell in the castle of Iruna toward the path of Makoto. I shall make the divine power of San-go-kyō shine throughout the world. As a highly praised missionary, I, Rebu, will make my name known in the land of the moon, high high high high, the land of Fusa. From Rome to Greece, to Asia Minor, and to the ends of the island of Tsukushi, leaving my footprints, I shall serve. Please grant this, my wish, O Amatsukami and Kunitsukami, the eight million Kami.'

§ 2

'Offering my Magokoro together with the horse, I pray to Thee. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, may Thy Mitama be blessed and prosperous.'

As he poured out his random fervor like a Kokedotsukuri, he followed at the end of the party, descending with unsteady footing. Thames began to sing while leading the horse.

'The steep slope of Teruyama, down which we descend, guarding the Miosaki of King Seran and the Hime-Mikoto of Yasudara, is hi-hi-hi. It is a Nanshiyo that will surely not be encountered again. The slope gradually grows steeper, and my footing has become uncertain. Like rain, it pours out; like leaves scattered by the wind, it drives them away, bara-bara-bat. Hi-hi-hi-hai, do-do-do. You Chikushiyō, be careful, it is Abunai. What is this, foaming at the mouth? Ha-ha-hat-hai, is a slope like this so painful? Though I say so, even for me, it has become painful. In times of suffering, one relies on Kami. O Kamisama of Ananai-kyō, I pray to Thee, Nanitozo Yoroshiku.'

'May Thou let us descend this Sakamichi together with the Jinba without incident, hi-hi-hi. Kamunagara. This Thames, who advances along the path of truth, is also a Miko of Kami. Since there is the protection of Kami, even if Kutsu-no-tsu attacks and surrounds us from front, back, left, and right, and the momentum of the fierce tiger is terrible, even if the tips of their spears, hi-hi-hi, come advancing in a row, how could I fear? We Masurao have bones in our arms. We are the pure essence of the Yamato-damashii, the chosen Kami-tsukasa. No matter how wide the world may be, is there anyone who can be our enemy? The fine horse, which is useful when climbing a slope, is troubled by the downhill path. Knowing it is a hindrance,'

'I must lead it, hi-hi-hi, or we cannot cross Onogahara. The subordinates of the Migimori-no-tsukasa are Kamunagara-dotsukoi-sho, hiding under the leaves, waiting with bated breath, hi-hi-hi, to make our party suffer miserably. Even if an enemy army of tens of thousands were to press upon us, the Kototama of the Zen-gon-bi-shi that I learned in Ananai-kyō, hi-hi-hi-hi, bu-bu-bu. Ee, you stinking Chikushiyō! I cannot stand it when you do that while the wind is blowing from behind. You should know some manners. We are the retinue of King Seran. You are a most rude Chikushiyō. Ha-ha-ha-hai, aitatsuta.'

'I stumbled on a high rock. Everyone, please be careful. If you ride on a stone cart that is rolling around there, you will instantly fall into a thousand-fathom valley together with the horse, and you will never be able to see the light of this world again. Ha-ha-ha-hai, do-do-do. Thanks to that, we have crossed the difficult pass. Rejoice, you horse Chikushiyō. From here on, the walking is easy. I have also begun to find it interesting. The life of a person is just like crossing this mountain, hi-hi-hi-hi. When you take your eyes off for a moment, hi-hi, you instantly lose your footing, end up as a wanderer of the world, and are despised by the people of the world,'

'ha-ha-hai-hai, until you end up being trampled by cattle and horses. Nevertheless, no matter how much a human may rush, there is a limit to that power. Rather than anything else, it is best to leave it to the providence of the noble and pure Kamisama, who possesses infinite divine power, hi-hi-hi-hai-do-do-do. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. May Thy Mitama be blessed and prosperous, and I pray that Thou wilt protect us who follow Kami, hi-hi-hi, and let us advance to the capital of Iruna without incident. Dotsukoi-dotsukoi, dotsukoi-sho. The road is gradually becoming wider, and the pebbles have become fewer. The suffering of falling seven times and rising eight times,'

'has finally been overcome on this downhill slope. By directing the Naohi-no-mitama toward the ugly spirits of the Migimori-no-tsukasa, the victory shout is right before our eyes. Ah, how brave, how brave!'

Singing thus, the party finally descended to the foot of the mountain pass.

They quenched their thirst with the clear water flowing in the valley, and after resting for a while, they became people on horseback once again. With all of them lining up their bridles, the sound of the bells ringing bravely, they galloped toward the capital with the momentum of a fierce tiger crossing a river, through the Onohara where the winter wind raged.
