Sērisuhime: 'Ah, you are Umorinokami-sama, thank you for coming. I have been waiting for you very much.'

Umorinokami: 'I heard that Yasudahime-sama was feeling a little unwell; how is her progress since then?'

Sērisuhime: 'Yes, thank you. As you can see, she has become energetic just like this. Believing without doubt that you would come, I told my sister, and strangely enough, she has a face as if she has completely forgotten her serious illness, and though I do not know what she is happy about, she is smiling and is in high spirits.'

Karlchin: 'Indeed, hearing that, it is most reasonable. If so, I shall firmly promise to make you my principal consort.'

Yasudarahime: 'Thank you very much; then from this day forth, I am truly your wife, am I not?'

So she spoke in a seductive voice, leaning against him with a face brimming with smiles. At her graceful charm, Karlchin seemed to lose both his bone and soul, becoming completely limp and weak; breaking the composure of his countenance, he lowered his usually drooping eyes all the more, and, even altering the tone of his voice, spoke:

Karlchin: 'Yes, yes, very well; if it is what you say, I would even give my life. You are truly a lovely creature, ehehehe.'

Yasudarahime: 'If that is so, I have one request. "As expected of my daughter. You have noticed a fine point; if you act thus, the secret struggle with Umoriji will surely be completely dissolved by this..." he said, parting from me with great joy and courage, so please do not worry about my father at all.'

Karlchin: 'Yasudarahime-dono, I am reluctant to part from you, but let us meet again tomorrow.'

So saying, quietly wiping his dripping saliva, he returned joyfully to his own residence. Afterwards, Kiyoteruhime stretched her neck and watched his departing figure, but before one knew it, her head moved up and down three or four times, and even her red tongue was sticking out.
