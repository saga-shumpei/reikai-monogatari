Chapter 1: 北 Light 照 Darkness [1126]

北光照暗〔1126〕

§ 1

The divine Miizu of Kami, within the Kakihatokiha Gankutsu of Takateruyama, where Kitateruhiko has Amo, the Ten-no-Mahito-Kamutsukasa, saves even the fierce Kemono-zoku with the Jigan of Izu, and how noble it is that he opens the Mioshihe of Mizu to the Ochikochi.

Takenohime, who appears as his Tsumagami, with a heart that is upright, loves all the Kemono-zoku from morning until night, and it is awe-inspiring how she embodies the Mioshihe of the Mitama of Mizu.

While being pulled by the Mitsuna of Kami, Yasudarahime-no-Mikoto, at a time when she could no longer endure the Baujiyō of the husband of Shiyāru in the house of Bishiya in the country of Teruman, was helped by a Chiusei-no-Muhi-Shimōtoko.

Following the day into the night to reach Irunakoku, when they arrived at the riverbank of the lotus, the master and servant were surrounded by several dozen henchmen released by the Tsukasa of Migimori, and their progress was brought to a standstill.

At that very instant, as they thought they heard a voice singing a Sendenka with a cool tone, Ryūun, who was on a pilgrimage through the provinces, appeared there, saved the master and servant from their crisis, and drove the pursuers away to the fields beyond.

While Yasudarahime and the leader were thanking him for the grace of saving them from danger, and while they were being protected by Ryūun-no-Kamutsukasa and advancing to the foot of Teruyama-tōge, they encountered the Senden-shi of the Anana-kyō, Koganehime and Kiyoteruhime, and their party.

Hearing the message of Kitateru-no-Kami, they were filled with joy and courage, and as they parted ways, the three of them pointed toward the deep mountains of Takateru where the wolves make their dens, whipped their chestnut horses, crossed many valleys, and finally arrived at the Gankutsu-kan where Kitateru-no-Kami dwells; the Ten-no-Mahito-Kamutsukasa and Takenohime-sama also rejoiced, treated these precious guests with hospitality, and at the time of teaching the Senden of the one truth of Anana, King Sēran, who had flourished as the lord of the castle of Iruna, along with Karu, Rēbu, and other followers, whipped their horses and came out, and once again, it was a mysterious meeting, a conversation between a man and a woman of mutual love whom they had longed for, which truly appeared inseparable; Kitateru-no-Kami sincerely and with all his heart preached and transmitted the Keirin of the Kami of heaven and earth, and just as withered summer grasses are revived by a white rain, the lonely Gankutsu bloomed with the flowers of the Tenkoku-Jōdo; Ah, Kannagara-kamu-kamu, may the Mitama prosper, and I humbly and respectfully pray before the great presence of the awe-inspiring Kami who protects the world, that the story of the forty-second volume may advance as smoothly as the wheels of a cart.

Kitateru-no-Kami, the Ten-no-Mahito-no-Kami, while stroking his white beard, was giving a detailed interpretation regarding the Keirin of the Kamikai and the Shinji to King Sēran, Yasudarahime, Ryūun, and the others.

Sēran: 'Since yesterday, I have heard about the end of the world, and I would like to request a detailed explanation once more. In the Shinji of the Mitama of Mizu, it is written: "When the end of the world comes, after the tribulation of those days, the sun shall be darkened, and the moon shall not give her light, and the stars shall fall from heaven, and the powers of the heavens shall be shaken. And then shall appear the sign of the Son of man in heaven. And then shall all the tribes of the earth mourn, and they shall see the Son of man coming in the clouds of heaven with power and great glory. And he shall send his angels with a great sound of a trumpet, and they shall gather together his elect from the four winds, from one end of heaven to the other." How should I interpret this if I take it exactly as the words say? If it is to be taken literally, I must see these terrifying events appearing at the time of the end of the world, which is called the final judgment of Kami.'

Kitateru-no-Kami: 'It seems there are quite a few people who interpret this prophecy literally according to the text of the teaching. These people not only think that the sun and moon will lose their light, the stars will fall from the sky, the sign of the Lord Kami will appear in heaven, and the angels with trumpets will descend from heaven realistically with the Messiah of the Mitama of Mizu from within the clouds, but they also jump to the conclusion that the world as far as one can see will be completely destroyed, and only then will the appearance of a new heaven and earth be possible. Among the Senden-shi of the Anana-kyō, there seem to be some who believe in this way. Those who believe in this manner do not know that there is a secret meaning even in the minute parts of the Shinju. Within the Shinju, it is not a natural or worldly matter to be interpreted as the letters suggest, but rather, spiritual and Kamikai secrets are contained therein. Even within a single sentence or phrase, it is all indicated according to the principle of correspondence so that an inner meaning is included in each one. Therefore, the Shinju cannot be truly understood by ordinary knowledge or the power of scholarship. This is the very reason why it is a sacred Shinju.'

北光の神なる天の目一つの神は白髯を撫でながら、セーラン王や耶須陀羅姫、竜雲その他を集めて、神界の御経綸や神示に就て綿密なる解釈を与へつつあつた。

セーラン『昨日より承はりました世界の終焉に就て、今一応詳細なる説明を御願ひ申上げ度きもので御座ります。瑞の御霊の御神示の中に、世の終りの来る時は其日の患難の後、直ちに日は暗く月は光を失ひ、星は空より墜ち、天の勢ひ震ふべし。其時、人の子の徴天にあらはる。又地上にある諸族は哭き哀しみ、且つ人の子の権威と大なる栄光とを以て天の雲に乗り来るを見む。又その使等を遣はし、ラツパの大なる声を出さしめて、天の彼の極みより此の極みまで、四方より其選ばれし者を集むべし……とあるのは、其言葉の通りに解すれば如何なもので御座りませうか、文字通りに解すべきものとすれば、最後の神の審判と云はれてある世界終焉の時に、是等の恐るべき事件が出現すると見なくてはなりませぬなあ』

『この予言を以て教示の文字通りに解するものは可成沢山あるさうです。是等の人は日月光を失ひ、星は空より墜ち、主なる神の徴天に現はれ、又雲の中よりラツパを持つた天使は、瑞の御霊の救世主と共に、現実的に天より降り給ふものと思考して居るのみならず、見る限りの世界は悉く滅びて、茲に始めて新しき天地の出現を見得らるるものと早合点して居るのである。三五教の宣伝使の中に於ても、此の如く信じて居る人があるやうです。斯の如く信じて居る人は、神諭の微細なる所に至るまで密意の存在しある事を知らないのである。神諭の裡には文字の如く解すべき自然的世間的の事では無くして、心霊的、神界的の秘事を包含されて居る。一文一句のうちにも、一々内義を含ましめむために、悉く相応の理に由りて示諭されてある。故に神諭は、普通の知識や学問の力では、到底真解さるるものでは無い。是即ち神聖なる神諭たる所以である。

§ 2

The Shinji stating that the Lord God shall come riding upon the clouds of the Ōzora must also be interpreted according to this Naigi.

That is to say, the day that shall become dark represents the Kiuseishiyu's Iwa-no-Mitama seen from the aspect of love,

the moon represents the Kiuseishiyu's Mizu-no-Mitama seen from the aspect of faith,

the stars represent the knowledge of good and faith, or the knowledge of love and faith,

the sign of the Son of Man in the Ten represents the Kenji of Shinshin,

the Moromoronomono who shall weep and mourn upon the Chijiyō represent all things that come from truth and good, or from faith and love,

the statement that the Lord, or the Kiuseishiyu, shall come riding upon the clouds of the Ten with Keni and Eikuō represents the Genzon of the Kiuseishiyu within the Shinyu,

and it also represents its Mokuji in advance,

the clouds represent that which is manifested in the Moji of the Shinyu,

the Eikuō represents the Igi hidden within the Shinyu,

and the statement that the Tennin shall hold a trumpet and emit a great voice represents the Tenjiyōkai from which Shinshin shall come.

Therefore, if one asks what is the Igi of the aforementioned words spoken by the Kiuseishiyu, it means that at the Shiuki of the Seijiyō of the Oshihe, when faith and love both perish, the Kiuseishiyu shall enlighten the inner meaning of the Shinyu and reveal the secret mysteries of the divine realm.

It is no exaggeration to say that almost none of the current Brahmanists, Uralists, or Ananai-kyō followers know this.

In fact, even those who are in the position of propagandists are only those who would deny my interpretation of the Shinyu.

And many of them say, 'Who is it that can explore the divine realm and speak of such things?'

I see that those who advocate such theories are particularly numerous among those who are clever in worldly wisdom.

Lamenting that the poison of this might reach sincere and pure people and eventually lead to the destruction of their faith, I have always purified my soul to commune with the Tennin and speak with them.

Being permitted by the divine realm, just as in the human world, to exchange language with the Tennin, being allowed to see the various events occurring in the heavenly realm and the state of hell, and striving to convey, demonstrate, and preach the truth of the divine realm to all people under heaven is for the sake of illuminating the world of ignorance and removing the calamity of disbelief.

Even if the Shinyu indicates that the heaven and earth shall be overturned, that it shall become a sea of mud, that humanity shall be divided into three, or that a great confusion shall come, it must never be interpreted literally by the Moji.

Everything must be interpreted in an inner, divine, and spiritual sense.

I believe one must be aware that if it is not so, it will conversely spread great poison throughout the world and trouble the divine will.

However, this is the private opinion of the Hokkō family.

It may be a digression, haha-ha-ha-ha!'

Speaker: 'Having received your profound instruction, we have finally awakened from our delusive dreams. Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haese.'

They choked with tears of gratitude.

Yasudarahime, Ryūun, and all the others were listening to the instruction of Hokkō-kami without even catching their breath, and were lost in tears of gratitude.

Speaker: 'Come, come, King Seran, Yasudarahime, Rebu, Lord Karu, from here, enter the castle of Inna and march out without delay to bring the evil spirits to harmony through Kototama. It will be a disaster if you are late. Princess Kogane and Kiyoteruhime are also waiting.'

He urged them on, unlike the calm god he usually was.

King Seran stood up at these words,

Speaker: 'I have received your repeated kindness. Then, as we set out to guide the wicked to the path of good using the Kototama of Ananai-kyō, I shall offer a song of propagation to the Kami.'

He opened his silver fan and began to sing in a clear voice.

The song:

To distinguish between the good spirits and the evil spirits.

The Kami who created heaven and earth is Naohi,

The Mitama is also the vast Ōnaohi.

In the world of men, whatever happens,

Ever discern between right and wrong?

Though I think in my heart it is the manifestation of Yamata-no-Orochi,

To save the weak Mitama,

He manifests as an evil spirit,

And one cannot fathom that He is testing us.

Without thinking others are evil,

If one enjoys a life of gratitude,

§ 3

If it be that the state of my own Tsumi is reflected therein, ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara. There shall be no enemy to oppose the Mioshihe of Muteikaushiyugi. All Magatsu, all Shikogami, and even the Orochi are none other than things outside. Shifuchiyakushin, deep as clouds, possesses the impure Mitama, and the Tsumi of my own body torments and attacks my own body of its own accord. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara. Together with Yasudarahime, I shall Tatenaho the horse of my heart. As for the Jiyashin and the despised Karlchin, it goes without saying for the Hime of Tena, and pitying Samaryhime, I shall Nahohi, Minaho, and hear again the Burei given to us, and Norinao with a broad heart.

I shall save the Tamigusa of the land of Iruna, peacefully and joyfully, in the joy of the spring where the flowers of Shinkoku bloom. I shall guide and admonish them in the Tayori and Fukuin of Amatsukamikuni, and in the Mioshihe of the one sincerity of Ananahi. I shall have them learn, and let Shiyōka live in harmony, enjoying the spring of Tokoyo. They shall serve as the pillars of God, the heaven that has descended to earth. O Hokkōshin, O Takenohime, come, come, farewell, come, farewell. From here I bid you farewell, and shall return to the capital of Iruna, riding proudly with bridles aligned. I shall pacify and save the hearts of the people of the land, and transmit the true Mioshihe of the Lord Ōkami. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara.

May your Mitama be blessed.'

Having finished singing, they leaped nimbly onto the horses they had prepared. The party of seven bade farewell to Hokkōshin and his wife, gathered their reins, and were escorted by a pack of wolves along the mountain path. Urging their horses on with cries of 'Hai, hai, hai,' they descended Mount Takateru and proceeded toward the capital of Iruna.
