Chapter 25: 惜別 Song [1150]

惜別歌〔1150〕

§ 1

Thames sings again:

'In the capital of Haruna, the Kamutsukasa of Ōkuronushi, who dwells there, consulted with the Umori-no-tsukasa. They plotted to occupy the castle of Iruna and banish King Seran, for the Umori-no-tsukasa, being a Setsuteiri, sought to attain the height of wealth and glory. In the gravity of this situation, Kurinsu, the Samori-no-tsukasa, grieved for the state and bowed before the Daibontenō, praying in the Ōmahe. At that moment, the Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, the Kamubashira of the Ōgon Sekai, the two pillars of Hime-no-mikoto, whose divine Miizu is as clear and bright as the Momijiba that colors the Akino, crossed the land of Iruna and appeared in the capital of Haruna, welcoming the two on their way.'

'The Gosenji was most sacred, commanding to remove the Kinan of the capital of Iruna. The Samori-no-tsukasa, having been called near by the Lord, was told to take four or five attendants and welcome the Kamutsukasa of peerless divine power. Leaving the castle of Iruna behind, crossing the fields where the autumn wind blows, and waiting for the Lord at the barrier of the underworld before the forest of Iruna, the Kamutsukasa came singing a Senden-ka with a clear voice. Ah, how grateful and sacred, I prostrated myself in my heart, but before I could rejoice, the two pillars vanished, hiding their divine forms, to my great sorrow. After a while, the two pillars appeared in the capital of Iruna.'

'They rescued King Seran, who had been tormented by demons, and guided him to the rocky cave of Mount Takateru, where they preached the teachings of the Kami in their thousands. Through the grace of the Hokkō-no-kami, the castle of Iruna, which had been leaning toward ruin, was restored to its original state. Now, having swept away the evil clouds in an instant and recognized the auspicious signs of supreme peace and prosperity, I am filled with joy. King Seran and Kurinsu, following the teachings of the Kamutsukasa, and Thames, the Sendenshi whose very name is sacred, followed Tatsuyamawake. Thus, I became one who goes forth to organize the Kototama Sentai in the wilderness where the Majin rage.'

'When I think of it, when I think of it, the grace of the Kami of heaven and earth is not shallow. The clouds of the heart have cleared away, and the pure and sacred Sendenshi strike the Kototama in all directions. The dew of grace shall reach even to the ends of the evergreen trees, and I shall continue to assist in the way of Kannagara. Before the sacred presence of the Kami, our party humbly and reverently prays for success. Even if the morning sun shines or clouds over, even if the moon waxes or wanes, even if the earth itself should sink, how could the Sendenshi who serves the Kami change his heart? Even if I become prey to the ugly wolves, lions, or bears, my soul, given by the Kami, shall ascend to the heavenly pure land.'

'Having revealed the tidings of the Kamireikai and descended once more to the earth with joy, how could I leave the teachings of Ananai unopened, even if I must be reborn seven or eight times? Today is a joyous departure for the conquest of demons. Tatsuyamawake, Rebu, Karu, take courage, take courage, take good courage. Pray, pray, pray well. Cast aside useless logic, and proceed along the single path of benevolence. The Sendenshi advances, saving the people of the world and opening the countries in all directions in detail, truly sacred beyond measure. King Seran, Kamutsukasa of Hokkō, farewell, I must take my leave from here.'

'I shall travel through the moon-countries of over seven thousand lands, preaching the sacred way of the Ōkami. Advancing further to the land of Tsukushi, and even to the islands of Ryūgū, Takasago, and Tokoyo, to the very ends of Onogoroshima, illuminated by the light of the Kami, I shall fulfill my divine mission with courage. I humbly pray before the presence of Kuninotokotachi-no-Ōmikami, Toyokumunu-no-Ōmikami, and Kamususanō-no-Ōmikami.'

'The Miizu of Kuninotokotachi-no-mikoto, who created this world, is sacred. I leave the capital of Iruna now, filled with the joy of advancing on the path of truth. As I advance, I shall illuminate the ugly souls that obstruct the great field of Hara. How joyous it is for me to follow behind the head of Tatsuyamawake-no-mikoto. Even in the dark of night, I believe that the path I walk together with Tatsuyamawake shall not be dark.'
