Chapter 24: 出陣 Song [1149]

出陣歌〔1149〕

§ 1

Rebu sings:

'Crossing the Lion River, over the steep slopes of Tamayamatauge, following the retinue of Ōgonhime while leading my horse, I finally crossed the Sekishiyo of Thames-tauge safely. Encountering the army of Ōkuronushi at the foot of the slope, falling into the Chihiro valley, and crossing the Sanzu River together with the Tsukasa of Karu, I explored the threshold of the Tengoku Jiyōdo. Awakened by the retinue of Terukuniwake, I was revived. Near the swamp of Aoi, surrounded by the enemy once again, I was saved from a perilous life. Coming to the other side of the swamp, I felt the revival of the spirit upon seeing the Gokiusoku of the noble life of Ōgonhime and Kiyoteruhime. Rebu and Karu, the two, accompanied Ōgonhime and came to the forest of Iruna. Encountering Teru, Teku, and Aruma, who were released by the Tsukasa of Migimori, we finally scattered the enemy. Together with the Tsukasa of Thames, we advanced into the castle of Iruna. While resting there for a while, summoned by Hokkōshin, King Seran departed toward the high-shining deep mountains, pushing aside the nine-layered clouds. Dark is the path of darkness. Thames, Rebu, and Karu, the three, rode side by side, their horses' bits clattering, following the King as they advanced toward the rocky cave of Mount Takateru.

Ah, Kannagara-kamu-kamu. Deep is the grace of the God. Encountering the life of the Princess of Yasudara, which I had not expected, I spent days there for a while. Urged by Hokkōshin, I mounted my horse and rode majestically. Returning to the castle of Iruna under the cover of night, I lamented to see that Karuchin, in his awe-inspiring arrogance, was plotting to depose my great Lord of ten thousand chariots and fulfill his own desires. How long will the Gods of Heaven and Earth continue to overlook the rampages of the evil Gods so easily? Suddenly, the conspiracy was exposed, and the Yamata-no-Orochi and the ugly Gods lurking in the Tsukasa of Migimori vanished as if they had been wiped away.

Ah, how grateful, how grateful. The divine majesty of the God instantly shines across the countries of the four directions. The heavens are clear, the earth is abundant, and it has become an era of auspicious omens. By the divine decree of King Seran, following Tatsuyamawake, I shall teach and transmit the teachings of Ananaikyo to the countries and islands of the four directions without exception. As a missionary whose very name is auspicious, with all my soul and all my body, I shall open the path of God even to the ends of Tsukushi. Hokkōshin, farewell. King Seran, Samari-hime, and the Tsukasa of Hidarimori and Migimori. After I depart, while offering sincerity to the Gods of Heaven and Earth, I, the divine minister, pray earnestly that you shall make the light of Ananaikyo shine upon the country of Iruna, not to mention the country of Teruman and the other hundred countries, swearing to the Gods of Heaven and Earth.

Even if the morning sun shines or clouds over, even if the moon waxes or wanes, even if the earth itself sinks, my body is entrusted to God. As long as my life continues, shall I not manifest the state of supreme joy and supreme happiness of Tengoku by purifying the country of Toyoashihara, which is filled with clouds and filth? Ah, Kannagara-kamu-kamu. Before the God of 567, of the Ananaikyo of sincerity alone, I pray with reverence and awe.

Blessed by the grace of the Chihayaburu God, I am happy to be in a body that saves the people of the world. Abandoning evil, I enter the path of sincerity and leave the castle of Iruna behind as I advance. O Tsukasa of the God of Hokkō, protect my soul forevermore. The Ananaikyo, protected by the life of the God Susanoo, is the path to save the world. It is a joy to hear that all people are the precious dwelling places of the Great Gods of Heaven and Earth. From now on, I shall straighten the horse of my heart and divide the wilderness where the evil Gods rampage. In a world where there is joy even amidst suffering, I shall not fear any Magatsuhi that may come.'

Karu also sings:

'Leaving the castle of Iruna behind, together with Rebu and Thames, joining our hearts and taking each other's hands, we go up to conquer the demons. I am a noble divine minister. From today, the Tsukasa of Tatsukumo shall change his name to Tatsuyamawake. Please advance while singing the missionary songs, easily traversing the mountains and rivers where the evil Gods rampage.

Even if the morning sun shines or clouds over, even if the moon waxes or wanes, even if the stars in the sky fall, even if there comes a world where the seas dry up, we four missionaries who have once entrusted ourselves to God must never change our hearts. Knowing that the moment you forget God is the moment you will feel suffering in your body and soul, do not change.'

『入那の城を後にして レーブ、テームス両人と 心を協せ手をとりて 悪魔の征討に上り行く 吾は尊き神司 竜雲司も今日よりは 竜山別と名を変へて 魔神の荒ぶ山川を いと易々と宣伝歌 歌ひて進み出でませよ 朝日は照るとも曇るとも 月は盈つとも虧くるとも 御空の星は落つるとも 海はあせなむ世ありとも 一旦神に任したる 吾等四人の宣伝使 決して変心する勿れ 神を忘れし其時は 身魂に忽ち苦みを 覚ゆる時と知る上は

§ 2

No matter what Nayami one may encounter, with Kami as one's strength, with the sincerity of Ananahi as one's staff, one shall briskly advance along the Ōji of the path, fundamentally Sautau the Saukutsu where Magami gather, illuminate the Ōji of our Ananahi to the Sekai, expand the Miizu of Ōkami to the Yomo, stand between Kami and man, transcend Zenakuseijiya, and with a heart pure as the Kamunahohi entrusted by Ōkami, as a Sendenshi, one shall proceed to Minaho and perform the work of rectification. Ah, Omoshiro, Omoshiro! Kami is with Warera, and Warera are the children of Kami and the shrines of Kami. No matter what Magami may come, how could they ever prevail against Kami?

Ah, courageous, courageous! Leaving the castle of Iruna behind, the Yotarizu align their Ashinami, and with Kiko Daudau and without incident, they face the capital of Shiyōri, and the raising of the victory cry before the presence of Kami is right before our eyes. Come, come now, come now! Before the presence of Hokkō-kami and the other hundred Tsukasa, we here bid our farewells. Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara! May your spirits be blessed.

Yotarizu: 'How joyful it is to be one who opens the Kannagara Ōji of Kami to the countries of the Yomo. Though one may say how vast the heaven and earth are, there is no land that Kami does not protect. Embraced by the blessings of the Kami of heaven and earth, we go forth to open the land of Kami. How sorrowful I am to be parting now from the Tsukasa of Hokkō-kami and the Ōkimi. Yet, as it is said that all who live must die and all who meet must part, I think that we shall meet again after we have parted.'
