Chapter 23: ブール酒 [865]

ブール酒〔865〕

§ 1

In the inner chamber of the Gankutsuyakata, the Reijiyō of Mount Higura, the Kyōshiyu Būru sat, while Anan and Yūzu waited in a dejected state.

Būru bowed before the deity within the Gankutsunai, offering prayers that the subordinates dispatched to the City of Hiru might achieve a great victory and return in triumph as soon as possible.

Upon finishing his prayers, he returned quietly to his Ima, but to his surprise, the two brave generals who had marched out in such a grand manner the previous night were waiting in his room, looking crestfallen.

Seeing the two figures, who appeared strangely lacking in energy and dim of presence, he was overcome with a sense of unease.

Būru: 'Hey, you two, Anan and Yūzu, your complexions look quite pale, do they not? You must have failed in your mission somewhere along the way, and have now returned in such a cowardly and shameful manner.'

At these words, realizing they were in the presence of the Kyōshiyu, the two suddenly feigned vigor.

Anan: 'Oh, was that you, Kyōshiyusama? Well, actually, it was, how should I put it... a tremendous something, and truly unpleasant... wait, no, it was a magnificent affair. Hey, Yūzu, you too, use your wits and report the war situation properly, without any omissions.'

Yūzu: 'I shall report. Here, the Great Deity Izanami gave birth to the child Kagutsuchi-no-Kami and passed away. The Great Deity Izanagi was greatly enraged, and when he cut off the head of his child Kagutsuchi-no-Kami, the names of the deities born from the splashing Yutsu-Iwamura were Satsuparikon, the Deity of Rock-Splitting, and the Deity of Root-Splitting.'

Būru: 'Hey, hey, what are you talking about? I am asking how the war situation is. Can you not report in detail?'

Yūzu: 'Yes, a report, a report indeed. With red-hearted loyalty, I did my best for your sake, and while I worked my many subordinates to the point of black death, no, I mean overused them, I started the election campaign; however, regrettably, let alone winning a single seat, I became the runner-up. Well, they say war is a matter of fortune, so after we thoroughly intimidated the enemy and were about to attack the City of Hiru, we were easily routed.'

Būru: 'Which side was routed?'

Yūzu: 'Well, let us see. In every battle, unless one side loses, peace cannot be restored. We met with a most disgraceful and miserable fate.'

Būru: 'I cannot understand a word you are saying... Hey, General Anan, Yūzu seems to have lost his mind. You report the whole story from beginning to end in detail.'

Anan: 'There is no need for you to ask. We led our brave warriors, rode side by side, and marched grandly along both banks of the Higura River, forming a long snake-like formation while advancing with flags and drums. Just then, there were countless Jūyō divine crests, horse-insignia, and high-hanging lanterns, and the subordinates of Kaedewake-no-Mikoto of the City of Hiru, nearly three thousand in total, came attacking toward us, creating a human wall on both banks of the Higura River in such a menacing manner. To oppose the many with the few is a feat only heroes and great men can accomplish; we two, with only a few hundred soldiers, faced them. First, we divided our bamboo-spear unit, aimed at the enemy's surprise, and charged from the flank with spear tips aligned and war cries raised. The enemy panicked in their defeat, and we pursued them further, driving every last one of them into the rushing current of the Higura River. The many enemies who were caught by surprise immediately ran up the north bank, scrambling to escape, and as they climbed the riverbank, Yūzu's drawn-sword unit, which had been lying in wait, shouted that these were worthy opponents and waved their batons. When they gave the order to the mere three hundred allies, our brave warriors aligned their sword tips and cut and mowed them down one after another. In an instant, it was a great victory of blood-rivers and corpse-mountains; the Higura River was suddenly flooded with a deluge of blood, and the corpses of the many enemies drifted in the blood-stream, leaving not one behind, as they flowed toward the Pacific Ocean. The magnificence of it is truly beyond words.'

Būru: 'Is this not a report of a rather Shinshōbōdaiteki nature? That the Higurashi River should overflow with a turbulent Dakuriu of blood, and that three thousand corpses should have been washed away without a single one remaining, all due to the blood of a mere three thousand enemies—this report of yours does not Gaten. Unless the blood of humans were several hundred times that of their physical Nikutai, such a thing could not possibly occur. This must be some kind of mistake.'

Anan: 'Yes, mis... not a mistake, but a mix-up of words... er, what was it, yes, it has become a bit scattered, and in my excessive joy and great Daishiyōri, my Maoshia is all topsy-turvy and Shiri-metsuretsu, but in truth, it is such a great victory and such an unexpected Kauketsukuwa that I know not where to dance with my hands or tread with my feet, and I have just come down from a state of joyful Giyakujiyō. I have only just returned to report on the Senkiyō, and my head is not yet settled. Please grant me a moment of Chinshimokukau. In return, my hundreds of subordinates have suffered not a single injury and are currently resting in the great hall, singing songs of triumph. I beg you to let them drink plenty of wine as a reward and for the celebration of our return. One who stands as a leader must love his subordinates. We are generals only because we have subordinates. You, too, are a Kyōshu only because you have us, so even if there are minor flaws, to look upon us with correction and listen with correction, and to face us with compassion, is the divine duty of a Kyōshu of infinite benevolence. To look upon all things in the Sanzenkyō with Naohi, to listen with Naohi, and to correct the errors of the body is, I believe, a splendid thing for the Ural religion. I believe it is a most convenient doctrine for us as well.'

Būru: 'Be that as it may, it seems to have been a fierce battle. To have slaughtered over three thousand enemies without a single casualty among your own men—your achievement must have been quite taxing. It is no wonder you are Giyakujiyō. Come, come, open the storehouse, bring out the wine, and let everyone drink their fill. I shall now go to worship before the Ōkami to offer my thanks for this triumphant return, so you should drink your wine at leisure and let everyone take their rest.'

Having said this, he turned and once again proceeded eagerly toward the shrine.

Seeing him off, the two looked at each other, sighed with relief, stuck out their tongues, and lowered their voices.

Anan: 'Ah, at last we have escaped the tiger's mouth. Now, I shall distribute plenty of wine to all the fellows as hush money. This, too, is the result of my knowledge. It seems the Kyōshu Būru has become a bit dull-witted of late. Since he was terrified in the valley of Mt. Okura and startled twice in the Forest of Arashi, and is now at a point where his Bikkuri-mushi has grown and he is beginning to suffer from spleen ailments, it seems he was so happy to hear the single word that we won that he was easily deceived by our disjointed and Shiri-metsuretsu explanation, as if patching bamboo onto wood. Now, wine, wine!'

So saying, Anan took the lead and proceeded to the large room where everyone was gathered, and standing in a slightly elevated place, he spoke.

Anan: 'Because the general Būru has permitted all subordinates to drink wine to their hearts' content today in celebration of our triumphant return, you should all rejoice. Now that the vice-general Yūzu has taken the key to the wine cellar, all of you should go before the storehouse, carry the wine here, and drink your fill. For those who do not drink, there is grape water without alcohol, so you should drink that. Since this arrangement also comes from the sincere heart of Anan who loves his subordinates, even if the Kyōshu should appear here and ask you anything, you must not say a single word. In all matters, tell him to ask the generals Anan and Yūzu; that is how you must smooth things over. In short, you must report in detail that due to the self-sacrificial great activity of all the subordinates, Kaedewake of the capital of Hiru collided with our troops at the temporary enemy position and the log bridge, and that we cut them all down, stabbed them to death, threw them into the river, and their corpses flowed into the Pacific Ocean; you must all hold to that account. Now, go quickly to the storehouse and bring the wine. This wine is a miraculous medicine to lock the mouths of all of you, so please ensure that your mouths do not open in any reckless manner.'

Strict orders for silence have been issued.

After a short while, mountain-like bottles of wine were brought in.

The two, Anan and Yūzu, in order to prevent the Kyōshiyu from appearing in this place, visited the Shinden, and upon finishing their kiguwan, cleverly talked their way into the Kyōshiyushitsu, flattering the Kyōshiyu's kigen with various detaramebanashi.

As the alcohol gradually went to their heads, tales of the previous yūbe's shitsupaidan continued to be told everywhere, regardless of surroundings.

The first person, letting his tongue slur, spoke:

Kō: 'Hey, all of you, how was it last yūbe? Didn't you have a zuibun sanzan time of it? Facing only one or two enemies, a great ōzei surrounded them, only to be grabbed and thrown, grabbed and thrown, having a sanzan time in the river, scraping our hizagashira, twisting the tefutsugahi of our hips, it was zuibun painful, hah. Yet what a kichinichi kefu is. I was worried that if I returned defeated in battle, my head might even be taken. And then, to be told to stuff ourselves to the limit with gafuhara-inducing, haujiyun wine until we were vomiting, making us unable to kentau anything at all. If things are going to be like this, we should follow wars at jiyōji and always come back defeated, hah.'

Otsu: 'That's settled. Isn't there such a thing as winning by losing? Thanks to losing, we can get this sweet wine to our hearts' content. Whether losing to lacquer or losing to sumo, there's never been a boro deal like this. Whatever happens, we just act as the gods' maké will have it, ah-ha-ha-ha.'

Hei: 'Hey, Kō and Otsu, don't go chirping about things like that. To cover up yūbe's defeat, the generals Anan and Yūzu, so that it wouldn't affect you and us, exercised their yūzu, sweetly deceived the Kyōshiyu, and brought in the wine by buru-ing it, so won't you show a little consideration for the general's feelings and be quiet?'

Otsu: 'Whether they buru-ed it or boro-ed it, I don't know, but they ought to have gonzered it a bit sweeter.'

Hei: 'The two generals put forth their best with all their might, and biyakuru-ed as much as they could biyaku-ed, so if you complain so much, punishment will befall you.'

Kō: 'Don't talk so noisily. From here, let's go to buru-sama's place, and dancing to our heart's content for the celebration, won't we? Once you drink alcohol, getting drunk is natural, and once you get drunk, dancing is a matter of course. There is crying sake, laughing sake, and angry sake. Those who want to cry, cry; those who want to get angry, get angry; those who want to laugh, laugh with all your might and enjoy yourselves, hah.'

Tei: 'Speaking of laughing, I actually want to laugh so much I can't help it. Won't you let me laugh enough?'

Kō: 'Laugh, laugh, so that everyone can plainly tell with a do-tsu, stand erect on that high platform, and laugh so much that the magazine market quotations go crazy. Fortune comes to homes that laugh, come on, laugh and laugh!'

Tei suddenly climbed onto the high platform, holding a bottle of wine in one hand, and while drinking straight from the bottle, spoke:

'Ah-ha-ha-ha, how foolish. Defeated in battle, thrown into the riverbed, hitting my shin, it hurt, but it was sort of funny, and I couldn't stop laughing.

Ih-hi-hi-hi, it's like having a weasel's final fart blown upon you, making stinky, stinky excuses, wrapping the fart-sounding buru Kyōshiyu sweetly in a smoke of farts, and treating us to fart-sounding budōshiyu, the defeated generals and soldiers getting full of themselves and gulping down alcohol—the comicality of that style.

Uf-u-ha-ha-ha, striking and changing, those attitudes of Anan and Yūzu, the two maistros (generals) who lost their footing at the Log Bridge battle before the lord Kyōshiyu, lost their nerve, uf-u-ha-ha-ha, flustered, made a fuss, and fled back, smoothly pulling out good wine from their bōkura, making us dead drunk, and trying to hush it up—the comicality of that. The general class, who always drink wine by themselves and act all pleased with themselves, this time seem to have gotten quite flustered and thoroughly closed their mouths, don't their attitudes look changed?'

Ehehehehe, ah, what a cursedly bad thing. To forcefully make us drink such vile, horrible, delicious wine, and get us drunk on alcohol just to kill us—why, that's far too greedy of a notion. A loach might be killed by liquor, I know not, but we human beings won't go down that easily, now.

Ohohohoho, the tail is white, the tail is white, the head is white too, the tail-white tanuki's belly drum; let's drink until our bellies are fit to burst, beat our bellies, pop farts from our bottoms going bur-bur, strain our utmost, and dance away, shall we?

Hahahaahahaha, my belly gets angry, my navel gets twisted, and I grow so sad. Alcohol, they call it, what a strange fellow it is. Hey, you fellows, won't you laugh a little? The sake will sink right down.

Hihehehehehe, when I remember last night's terrible ordeal at the Higurashi River, it makes me angry; dragged out in the middle of the night, and thrown into a river full of gravel just like a frog being pelted—when I think of that time, I felt so sad. Yet, as soon as I think that, having been made to drink a flood of such sweet sake, no matter how much I intend to be angry, it becomes interesting, comical, and joyful; how could anyone stay without laughing at this?

Fuhehehehehe, it's a windfall dropped from the skies. Wielding the eloquence of Furuna, the deep plots of Anan and Yūzu in today's work—no matter what anyone says, as long as we drink sake, we are in the most exquisite spirits.

Hehehehehehe, passing gas underwater—those unreliable, witless cowards, displaying ugly labels like the Gohiyakurakan, what on earth is that state of theirs when they guzzle sake? Shall we call it Hiyakukichiukau, or shall we call it Senkiyakau? It's just like giving water to hungry gaki; what a pathetic state! Every last one of them is a foul-mouthed wretch, and well, my, how they've all gathered together like this. Even Lord Būru, who keeps such creatures, must be at his wits' end.

Hohohohoho, truly, really, if we could lose and receive such sweet sake, I'd want to go out to battle every single night and taste defeat. No matter what, since a huge crowd is facing one or two enemies, there's no anxiety about winning. Just you try winning—it's not just a matter of a sake-house. Anan and Yūzu's commanders alone get to eat the sake while we are made to endure by drinking water—that's about the extent of it.

Wahahaahahaha, if there are bad things, there are bound to be good things afterwards. That's why we're drunk on sake, drunk on smoke, and intoxicated with faith. Drunk, drunk, getting dead drunk, lining up Ryorisuku with all our might, we are going to carry out today's victory celebration to perfection. This wine is truly full of good things. Drunk, drunk, sweet and drunk, piling up in the belly and drunk, gaining strength and drunk, even the mood is good, they say—so it's only natural that the intoxication spreads, ufuhehehehe, ehehehehehe, ohohohoho, it's the end. Someone, an angry drunk or a crying drunk, take my place. As for me, this is already the curtain call.'

So saying, staggering unsteadily, he scrambled down the high seat and chattered away haphazardly. Here, Anan came alone, and

Anan: 'Hey you fellows, good work. It must have been quite back-breaking, I suppose.'

Sū: 'It was such sweet sake, and it broke our backs just to drink it. As expected of Anan-sama, standing above the waters of mankind, you are truly great. Your skillful feat of tricking Commander Būru with sweet words, making a defeated army look like victors, and opening even the grape storehouses is truly fitting for our commander. Hey Kōshū, it would be good if at least something like this could last for ten days, wouldn't it?'

Anan: 'Hey, hey, won't you be quiet? It would be a disaster if it reached the Kōshu's ears. You won't be able to drink with such a big face.'

Sū: 'No matter when the commander or the Kōshu comes and tells us not to drink, who cares? We've already drunk as much as we could drink, so even if you told us to drink more on top of this, who would drink such rotted-looking sake? that's it, ahahaha.'

At this time, upon the announcement of 'The Kōshu's Grand Entrance,' the company suddenly pulled themselves together, re-seated themselves, straightened their collars, and broke into a great uproar.

Būru appeared with a grand air, faced the company, and said,

Būru: 'Just now, at the gate of the cavern, two strong warriors of Mitakekyō have come attacking; let all of you be on your guard.'

The company merely uttered, 'Hai,' and instantly sobering up from their drunkenness, their faces turned pale green as they trembled. In accordance with the Kōshu's command, the company scattered and dashed toward the entrance.
