Chapter 5: 下坂の Song [847]

下坂の歌〔847〕

§ 1

The party of four, exposed to the blazing sun of early summer, while fluttering the sleeves of their garments in the blowing cool breeze, descended the steep slope of rugged rocks, putting strength into their toes step by step in an Abt system manner.

They had come to a mountain path with a slightly gentler gradient. Ishikuma, who had newly surrendered, had cherished a deep hope in his heart to attach himself to the coattails of Suekohime and Sutekohime upon the summit of Mount Teru and sing a jiyutsukuwaika poem; however, unexpectedly, since Suekohime cut short her rest early and began descending the slope, he had no choice but to keep silence and was descending the steep slope. Taking the opportunity that they had just come upon a slightly gentler and safer mountain path, while walking and keeping time with his feet, Ishikuma began to chant the poem of his reflections.

Ishikuma: 'Ah, how interesting, how interesting! / Upon the peak of Mount Teru, / Two peerless nice ones— / Is it the descent of heavenly maidens? / Is it the appearance of Konohanahime? / Even Ishikuma, who is no tree or stone, / Firmly guarding the teachings of Brahmanism, / Until now, like a solid rock, / Using the teachings as a shield, / Was laughed at by the believers of the divine teachings of Brahmanism / As having turned into a fossil. / This Kenzō too, entering the faith of the divine teachings of Izutsu, / Was ashamed at his heart being moved / By the gentle figure and countenance. / Be that as it may, we / Are originally of humble station; / To yearn and pine howsoever / For the pine flowers blooming on the high peak,

Ishikuma: 'To catch the starlight of the heavenly august sky / At the tip of a fishing pole— / That must be an extremely difficult thing. / Ah, Kannagara kamigami, / Granting the reliance of the august spirits, / This Ishikuma, who has submitted to the Izutsu teachings, / Severely whipping and chastising my inner feelings, / Pray conquer quickly / The strange, strange darkness of love / And the lurking crooked demons! / The divine law of Brahmanism / Has nothing particularly changed; / Be that as it may, compared to / The divine teachings of Izutsu, / Somewhere it lacks something. / The teaching of Reishutaijū advocated by my Brahmanism / Is merely to damage and break / The physical body given by us by the Kami.

Ishikuma: 'It lacks the path of saving / The people of the world universally / Through the great, great divine virtues. / Ah, Kannagara kamigami, / The teachings of the Kami are all one; / They change only due to the difference / Of execution and non-execution. / Appearing as the noble august child / Of the living Kami who saves this world / Driven out from Takamanohara— / Suekohime whose heart is clear, / And Sutekohime who opens the teachings / By casting away her body for the sake of the people. / Though being the bodies of frail women, / They tread and break distant mountains and rivers, / Cross over long sea-routes, / And appearing far away here upon the cloud-piercing peak of Mount Teru, / How noble it is that they cause us to serve / For the sake of the Kami and for the sake of the world!

Ishikuma: 'I was born in the land of Tokoyo's eyes / And here entered the faith of the divine teachings of Brahmanism; / Guided specially by Onikuma, / Hearing and remembering the teachings of Brahmanism by halves and running, / Building a hall of teachings at the foot of Mount Takateru, / There can be no teaching in heaven and earth / Superior to this path!— / Thinking it indeed a noble teaching, / I abandoned both my heart and body, / With my body exhausted, morning and evening, / Accumulating the labor of moku-u shikkū, / And the teachings, day by day, like the morning sun / Rising in its abundant glory, / Flourished together with the moon and sun. / Be that as it may, the teachings of Brahmanism / Have one flaw: / Bringing out fresh blood and showing it to the august eyes of the Ōkami,

Ishikuma: 'Mistaking it for the sincerity of sacrifice, / The believers, like ice melting in the morning sun, / Gradually and steadily declined, / And as they were about to let the lamp of the law go out, / Devising a single plan here, / I heard of it as the dwelling place of a great serpent / Famous even on Mount Teru— / A frightful great waterfall / Which people fear and do not approach, / The northern waterfall, morning and evening, / Visiting it frequently, taking cold-water ablutions, / While purifying my body and soul, / Plying my ascetic practices for about two years— / Before I knew it, it became known throughout the four quarters, / Admiring my zeal, / Once again flowers bloomed on withered trees, / And the teachings shone brightly on high.

Ishikuma: 'The sacred place of Mount Takateru / Suddenly had its remaining branches blossom into flowers / And became very bustling. / Not letting this opportunity slip, wishing to transmit / The teachings of Brahmanism to the four quarters, / And when I tried to stretch the teaching network as far as the land of Chinu, / Through the zeal of Matsuwakahiko, / A kami-priest born as the august child of Kunihiko, / The faith of the Izutsu teachings / Resounded like thunder, / Standing in opposition to Brahmanism, / As a teaching enemy difficult to despise, / Now completely had it become. / Will the Izutsu teachings fall? / Will Brahmanism fall? / Crushing our livers and gallbladders at the boundary of life and death, / Twisting out a single plan here, the kami-priest / Of Izutsu in the capital of Uzu—'

Turning toward the hall of Oshihe, pretending to be mamehito of Ananai-kyō day and night, prying into the yōsu of the uchito, knowing it to be an unrighteous deed, they had exhausted all kenbōjiyutsusū hitherto.

The retinue of the youngest hime, who appeared as the rare onko of Kamususanōnōokami, set forth toward the land of Teru, crossed the Teru mountain pass, and as they advanced, from the fundamental sacred ground of the way of Brahman, a silent Kototama discourse was cast upon them—

Regarding this as an extraordinary dekigoto, the kamuzukasa of deep shinnin, beginning with Isaac and Karl, together with five others including Sina, Til, and Nero, were dispatched to the western foothills of the Teru mountain pass, to intercept the pair, lead them back to the mountain hall of Takateru, and accomplish their motive.

When he appeared at the waterfall of Inui and prayed with itsunifuran in the mizukōri, customary at all times, suddenly his body stiffened into rigidity, and at the agony of being unable to stir an inch, he looked up to the sky above the waterfall.

The very moment he looked up, a hideous, giant serpent opened its mouth, glaring with angry eyes.

His heart trembled, his body shrank, advance and retreat both blocked in a valley, and just as he had hardened his resolution of preparedness—

At that very time, the youngest hime, leading two companions, came forth and proclaimed the pure and sacred Kototama of Ananai, whereupon the magatsuhi vanished like cloud and mist.

Ah, Kannagara kamugami! If the youngest hime had not come, I would have been swallowed in the serpent's belly, and the Brahman teaching, built up with such pains even unto this place, would have collapsed before our very eyes like tree leaves scattering in a storm.
