Chapter 4: 懐旧の Song [846]

懐旧の歌〔846〕

§ 1

Suekohime newly permitted the submission of Ishikuma of Brahmanism, and together with Sutekohime and Karl's party of four, they at last reached the summit of Mount Teru's pass.

Ishikuma: 'Now, here is the famous summit of Mount Teru. Before the great battle of Yomotsuhirasaka, the divine daughters of the deity Masakayamazumi of the Capital of Chin, the Senden-shi of Shōchikubai, first made their initial deployment of Senden at this very place, guided by a Keshin of the deity Konohanahime of Mount Tenkyōzan named Kagatora, and as they passed through, the Senden-shi of Shōchikubai looked back from afar upon the Capital of Chin and sang their song of farewell to their parents. It is a spot of unspeakable breathtaking beauty, with mountain ranges rolling away in all directions and the waves of the Atlantic Ocean tiering like haze. Shall we pause here for a moment to catch our breath and begin our descent leisurely?'

Sueke: 'There is a breeze here that is beyond all words, refreshing. Though it is a shame, shall we rest here for a little while? After all, even if we walked for two or three days, we could not easily reach the Capital of Chin...'

Suteko: 'It looks as though it is right beneath our eyes, yet it seems there is a considerable distance to cover...'

Karl: 'I say, Suekohime-sama, since this is the historic site where the Senden-shi of Shōchikubai were driven by homesickness and chanted their song of farewell, how would it be if you, too, as you take your leave of the Land of Teru, chant with your own masterly Kototama?'

Sueke: 'O-ho-ho-ho, it is terribly embarrassing, but if I hear that it is a place of such venerable tradition where those splendid Senden-shi chanted their song, I could not possibly refrain from singing... Sutekohime-sama, how would it be if you also chanted one?'

Suteko: 'Please, do break the ice first. I shall merely tag along in your wake and add a superfluous tail...'

Suekohime, being caressed by the cool breeze atop the mountain, began to sing with a gentle modulation of her voice.

The divine capital of Jerusalem, where he appeared as the chief of angels, the great deity Momogamihiko, leaving behind the three pillars of the Senden-shi of Shōchikubai, in their tender, youthful girlhood, within the mansion of Chin, leaving the chaos of the holy land behind, boarding a ship fearful even to look upon, at the very moment of putting out with the help of his life into the wide expanse of the Wada plain, by the grace of the precious deity, once guarding the golden gates of Ryūgū, and helped by Otomihime, at the time of passing sad days and months, appearing upon Mount Tenkyōzan, appearing as the precious child of the deity Izanagi no Ōkami, helped by the deity Hinode, riding upon the turtle of Kotohirawake, together with Odotamamazumi, arriving safely here at Takasago, going forth into the Capital of Chin, spreading the teachings of Sangokyō, climbing over the pass of Mount Chin, even the sky of the heart becomes the Land of Haru, coming forth into the castle town of Haru guarded by Takatoriwake, surrounded by numerous enemies, stabbed regardless of where we were, buried amidst the desert, escaping for our lives from the Land of Haru, and while nurturing our illness at the hot spring welling up in the valley of Mount Chin, encountering by chance the divine officials Kagatora and Odotamamazumi, Komayahiko and Satsukiwako, the party of five crossing over the ridge of the mountain of heavenly clouds, placed aboard the ship of the great snake, gradually returning as far as the Capital of Udu, and Satsukiwako, by the matchmaking of Chinyamahiko, concluding the pledge of the quilt of mandarin ducks, how felicitous it was! On the evening twilight of the fifth day of the fifth month, leaving the holy land behind, the three beloved daughters of Shōchikubai came visiting, and caused to be held a joyous reunion of parent and child, and the Capital of Chin could be dimly seen beyond the white clouds. Here the three sisters, desiring to transmit the divine teachings, firmed up their bodies with straw sandals and puttees, announcing their departure to their two parents, the father's command and the mother's command, accompanied by the three officials, climbing up to this pass, yeaning over the parting from father and mother, when I think of the color of the heart in times past upon Mount Teru, it is filled with longing. I too, as a maiden of the eight maidens, in my feeble person, leaving far behind the knees of my father who dwells in tranquility within the Saikan-kan, proceeding far away to the Kenshōnkyō of Mesopotamia, and advancing from there to the land of Persia, at the time of opening the teachings, caught by the people of Brahmanism, the wide expanse of the Wada plain, the small boat of the letter's shore, the eight maidens scattered apart, sadness of rowing across the white waves according to the designs of the gods! Marching forward toward the east, at the foot of Mount Teru's pass, the Kamuzukasa of Ananai-kyō and the Shinjin of sin and Kāru, meeting unexpectedly, approached the waterfall of Kenu and, as they stood admiring its grandeur, right above the waterfall, looking terribly fierce with wide-open eyes and glaring, captivated by the hideous Orochi, upon one side of the rock the Kami's Tsukasa of Ishikuma stood erect, suffering pitiably; reforming, seeing straight, and listening straight before the divine Mimahe, dedicating the heart of Murakimo, he prayed. Our Kototama communed instantly with Tenchi, and the Miyai of the Kami of Ishikuma became Jiiujizai.

The Orochi was instantly Gedatsu, disappearing like clouds and mist. Pushing and measuring the red heart of Ishikuma, whose heart was also firm, wishing to transmit together the great Ōji of the Kami, singing the Sendenka, he climbed at last to the Sanchiyō, and when he looked back after ascending, the Sankasaumoku were glorious, the blessings of the Kami were full and complete, and the appearance of the Tengoku and Jiyōdo was manifested everywhere without a hitch. Ah, Kannagara-kamugami-kamugami! The blessings of the Kami are distinct, the flowers of the teaching are extremely pure, and we peacefully here, upon the clouds overlooking the four directions at the border of the lands of Chin and Teru, stand by the divine grace.

That being so, where now is the honorable Kami, my father? Starting with my sisters, Irogohime and Manahime, how do my five sisters manage to pass through this world? Gazing out over the waves whose destination is unknown, looking far away beyond the clouds, morning and evening, my heart worries and grieves over the news of my father and the lives of my sisters. Before long, may it clear up! The curtain of Kuragari is opened, the Amanozora, the moon and sun are pure, the wind is pure upon the ridge of Mount Teru, the heart is joyful in the age of pines; parents and sisters at one time, with joyful faces in the age of Miroku, hiding our bodies on this divine island of Takasago according to the will of the Kami, let us serve the teachings of the Kami.

Ah, Kannagara-kamugami-kamugami! May the Mitama bless us!'

Thus singing a song of retrospection, he entrusted his body upon a suitable resting rock and wiped his sweat. While Sutegohime had her face blown by the wind, looking out over the four directions with a most cheerful expression, she rotated her body in the four cardinal directions and began to sing:

'East, west, north, and south, when I gaze upon the shapes of the lands in the four directions, without shaming the name of Takasago Island floating in the vast ocean, Taiping pines, camphor trees, and sturdy oaks are verdant and lush; as far as the eye can see, upon the mountains, the beauty of their overlapping growth makes the Tengoku and Jiyōdo seem right before my eyes. Living while gazing upon them, I completely forget the troubles of travel, and looking up at the gentle-figured youngest princess who appeared as the precious child of Kami Susanoo-no-Ōkami as the master lord, from what village, gazing at the white clouds in the sky and upon the ocean, having gradually crossed over here, when the moon and sun are pure and I climb to the ridge of Mount Teru and gaze out,

the blowing wind is also fragrant, the treetops are in full bloom, delicious tree fruits are without limit, branches are heavy and full, without starving we enjoy ourselves, and we do not even feel thirsty, having the feeling of meeting the everlasting spring of Tokoyo. Appointed as the Tsukasa of the teachings of Ananai, opened by the great ancestor Ōkami who created Tenchi, wherever the end may be, of the long journey, to advance is joyful.

Ah, Kannagara-kamugami-kamugami! May the divine Mitama bless us, and for the sake of the people of the world, may the Suteko Kamuzukasa and the youngest princess's close attendant serve near the youngest princess forever, building thick achievements,

born as a divine child, please fulfill my heavenly vocation thoroughly, O Amatsukami, Kunitsukami, and Yaoyorozu no Kami, especially before the mimahe of the severely honorable Kuninotokotachi no Mikoto, Toyokuhime no Mikoto, and Mizu no Mikoto, with sincerity I pray.

Ah, Kannagara-kamugami-kamugami! May the divine Mitama bless us, and swiftly directing the Yamata no Orochi before the precious Mimahe of Amaterasu Ōsumikami who appeared upon Mount Amakyo, may Kami Susanoo-no-Ōkami make his achievements reach Takamanohara even a day earlier, and quickly cause the sword of Murakumo swallowed by the Orochi to be presented before the Mimahe of Ōkami.

May you thoroughly and purely employ the youngest princess who appeared as the youngest child of the eight maidens of the Mitama of Mizu no Kami, Sutegohime who attends her, the newly serving Kamuzukasa of Ishikuma, and even Kāru who walks the path of Ananai, protecting them deeply so that they may serve in the divine government of the age of Miroku.

Kami is with us; we are the children of Kami, the shrine of Kami. Building the chigi high above the cloud-wells, serving and erecting the miyashibira thickly, may you cause the pure norito before the Mimahe of Ōkami to be reported in detail.

Though the morning sun shines, though it clouds over, though the moon waxes, though it wanes, though Takasago Island sinks,

though Magatsuhi fiercely attacks, our soul that serves Kami, even until the end of the age of Miroku, shall not change before the Mimahe of Kami—this I cautiously vow.

That being so, where now is the honorable Kami Susanoo who was in the mountains of Caucasus? How do the divine mikoto of Hinodewake and Kotoyoriwake spend their days in the path?

Where now are Irogohime, Manahime, and Eigohime? We who have passed some time since parting have no way to receive news over the waves.

Purely floating Takasago, at the summit of Mount Teru's pass, looking back and looking back again, the feeling of the sorrow of parting and separation is deep.

Ah, imperial deities, imperial deities!

Blessing us with the fuyu of the Mitama, cause us to meet Ōkami even a day earlier.

With the single-mindedness of a woman's heart, I worship from afar.

Ah, Kannagara-kamugami-kamugami! May the Mitama bless us.'

So singing to the end, from here with Sutegohime in the lead, the party of four descended Mount Teru's pass toward the east.
