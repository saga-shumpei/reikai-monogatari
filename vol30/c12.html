§ 1

Here Suekohime, along with Sutekohime, Karl, Harukō, Ikukō, Takakō, and Ishikuma's party, successfully persuaded and harmonized the Great Serpent of the southeast pond, caused it to attain liberation, offered words of gratitude facing the pond, performed a deep chinkon so that henceforth no such ryūjiyashin would dwell in this pond and torment yobito, sealed away misfortune, and finally set forth toward the capital of Chin.

At this time, by some mystery, the uneven lengths of Karl's legs had somehow become matched, and his walking had become extremely easy. Yet Karl did not notice it in the least, remaining as if he still believed himself to be a lame person with a disability. Suekohime chanted the Amatsunorito, sang the heavenly songs of numbers, turned to the company, and:

Suekohime: 'Now everyone, thank you for your hard work. Let us now set out step by step.'

And she took the lead and walked forward.

Ishikuma: 'Hello everyone! Please wait. For some reason, my legs will not move at all. Please perform chinkon for me. I feel as though they are being bound tightly, and I cannot bear it.'

Karl: 'My, Suekohime-sama, General Ishikuma says his legs cannot stand. What a troublesome fellow he is.'

Suekohime: 'No, no, you need have no worry at all. When the proper time comes, they will surely become able to stand... Now, Karl-sama, do you know what is meant by actions speaking louder than words?'

Karl: 'Yes, I understand. Well then, Princess, please go on ahead. Sutekohime-sama, I ask you to go together as well... Hey Haru, Iku, Taka, you three lords, please accompany the two ladies and return. I am going to fix General Ishikuma's legs a little before I return...'

Haru: 'Hey, Karl, aren't you going to stop at a reasonable point. Divine punishment has fallen as a reward for the excessively wicked things you have done up until now. It is no use no matter how good your Kototama is or how splendid your prayers are. Unless the Gods grant permission, your legs can by no means possibly stand. Because your mitama is too defiled, the Gods have cast an immovable spell of binding so that you may not come to the sacred ground of Uzu. Why don't you just go home at a reasonable time?'

Karl: 'How could I do such a thing? To see someone in distress and return without saving them—how could I ever act as an intermediary for the Gods? Come what may, it is the Princess's command. Stop grumbling and hurry along to accompany the Princess back. I will catch up right away...'

Haru: 'Is that so, well that has truly become a commendable frame of mind.'

Saying this with a sneer, he followed behind the two and walked back toward the capital of Uzu.

Afterwards, the two continued silent prayer toward the water's surface for a short while.

Karl: 'Hey General Ishikuma! Until now I have become your disciple and received tremendous care. Today, in return for that favor and to expiate my sins, come what may, I must fix your legs. If by any chance your legs still cannot stand, I am prepared to carry you on my back and take you to the capital of Uzu, so set your mind at ease.'

Ishikuma: 'That is truly kind. I am sorry to put you to trouble.'

Karl: 'The world is a reciprocal affair. If you thank me even before your illness is cured, I do not know what to say and am at a loss for a reply. Well, you had better calm your mind leisurely. Now wait, I am going to chant the Kototama for a fresh start from here on, so it is guaranteed that your legs will become like the southeast pond. When that happens, hand in hand we shall gracefully chant the song of propaganda, head briskly straight for the northwest pond toward the capital of Uzu. We are a pair between whom not even water can leak. We must exhaust our kindness toward each other like brothers, opening our hearts without hiding anything within our banks. We must no longer alter our water-distant intercourse where water is too abundant like this pond. Now, here is one living Kototama from Karl-sama. Now stick your legs out straight over there. I shall ask for one chinkon to be performed for you. And then we shall chant the Kototama song. Do not laugh!'
