Chapter 14: Spirit とパン [856]

霊とパン〔856〕

§ 1

Having safely arrived at the port of Teru aboard the Takashimamaru, Kotoyoriwakenokami and Kuniyoriwake proceed further and further to the north. Here stands a high mountain called Mikurayama, and based on the faith of the Kunibito, a quite splendid shrine dedicated to Tatsuyohimenomikoto is built. This is called the Shrine of Mikura. It is the most surpassingly high peak of the great mountain range rising steeply on the border between Teru and Hiru. The sanctuary stood at the foot of Mikurayama. And a clear, wide mountain torrent sends up flying spray and roars aloud. Kotoyoriwakenokami, together with Kuniyoriwake, first of all made a pilgrimage to this place.

In this mountain torrent alone, there dwell in great abundance beautiful fish called Mikurauo, measuring five to six shaku in length, marked with various miscellaneous patterns. However, the Kunibito believed them strictly to be the divine messengers of the Mikura Shrine, and not a single person attempted to capture them. If anyone were to catch and eat these fish, their mouths would immediately become distorted, they would turn mute, and patterns of blue, red, white, yellow, purple, and yellowish-green would appear all over their faces; thus, not only did no one ever try to capture them, but whenever they saw these fish, they revered them like deities, and it was their custom to clap their hands and pray for their own desires.

Around this time, throughout the region of almost fifty square ri on the border between Hiru and Teru, no rain fell day after day, the trees and plants were almost like withered leaves, the fruits bore no seeds, and not a single grain of the five cereals could be harvested. Because of this, starving people filled the roadsides, and their tragic state was beyond looking upon. Countless Kunibito gathered at the foot of Mikurayama, and those who prayed to these Mikurauo to be saved from famine came in an endless stream, so that this mountain valley was almost buried by people.

Kotoyoriwakenokami, together with Kuniyoriwake, gazes upon this state of affairs, unable to bear the feeling of pity, and tilting his head in deep thought, ponders how by any means he might save them from hunger and enable them to preserve their precious lives. Looking down deep into the bottom of the valley, four or five Uralian missionaries dressed in white garments appear, earnestly preaching and instructing on the gospel of the heavenly kingdom. The people crowd before the missionaries, and holding empty stomachs, try asking various questions.

A, in a thin voice:

A: 'Missionary-san, why has a famine like this come to pass? For us, our lives are already on the verge of morning and evening, with no way left but to await death; my parents starved to death four or five days ago, and my wife, holding an infant, also starved to death in the same way, leaving me alone behind, but already the span of three days has become difficult for me to sustain. O God who saves this world, if You truly exist, why is it that even such innocent children are suffering the agony of starvation, yet You do nothing about it at all? We have come to this day and can no longer help doubting the existence of God. My village, having believed in the Uralian religion up to seven or eight tenths, has after all starved to death. The people who have come here like this are, as you know, wasted and withered away, and no matter how hale a person may be, they are all placed in a fate where every single one of them must starve to death within these ten days. Is there really no way that you can help us with this suffering?'

Missionary: 'O wandering ones! Hear the gospel of the God of the Uralian religion! When man is born into this present world, compared to the infinite heavens and earth of the dark and profound, his life is merely as short as the morning dew. The transient world is a temporary floating world, a house of suffering, a burning house. Why grieve and yearn for the transient world, or seek suffering? The time draws near, the time of judgment is... Repent before me, and confess your heavy sins until now before the great deity Pankō. Then, even though your life should starve to death, your soul shall be saved in the heavenly kingdom where eternal flowers bloom and bear fruit, receive infinite glorious bliss, and be able to enjoy the spring of Tokoyo. The time to repent is now. Ah, foolish ones, do you not know the preciousness of the kingdom of God, and being caught in material desires, burned by the unquenchable fire of the scorching hell, do you seek to afflict your bodies? The kingdom of heaven is at hand; repent and hear the gospel of salvation! God is with you, and you, as pure servants of God, praise and magnify God day and night!'

此頃ヒルとテルとの国境に於ける殆んど五十里四方の地域は連日雨降らず、草木は殆んど枯葉の如く、果物は実入らず、五穀も亦一粒も取れなかつた。夫れが為路傍に餓孚充ち、其惨状目も当てられない計りであつた。数多の国人は御倉山の山麓に集まり、此御倉魚に向つて、饑饉を免れむことを祈願する者引きも切らず、此谷間は殆ど人を以て埋もれてゐた。

Thus he preaches with patient earnestness.

Otu: 'Sendenshi-sama, your discourse is truly magnificent, but we five have now fallen into the realm of the dying and are actually suffering the torments of hell. Before our eyes now, our beloved father has fallen to hunger, our wives have likewise fallen, and right down to our brothers, sisters, and infants, this is the state in which they are perishing due to famine; though we may think it a blessing to be saved in heaven, unless you give us bread, we cannot live. Why is it that God does not save this tragic plight?'

Sendenshi: 'I am a holy office charged with transmitting the gospel of heaven to you. Not to cling to this defiled and utterly turbid present world, but to be drawn by the hand of God's summoning and leave the present world behind to reach heaven, is surely the supreme and utmost joy. If you comprehend the truth of the kingdom of God, wearying of the present world, directly seeing death shall fill you with the rapturous joy of sleeping and tasting sweet dew. O you of shallow faith! Come ye to God and praise God; God is with you, and to be drawn by the hand of God to reach the heavenly pure land is the fulfillment of life's greatest purpose.'

Otu: 'Might you not be able to give us a single block of food? Since you are a Sendenshi who transmits the gospel of God, would you not save us in the present, and cause us, while yet alive, to comprehend the gospel of heaven and participate in the work of God? Now, no matter how wondrous a gospel we are given, as for us, with bodies pressed by hunger, those of us who are not saved in the present world and exist in such a miserable circumstance, how can we possibly obtain the joy of the spring of heaven? ...Sendenshi-sama! As you can see, there are very many storehouse-fish dwelling in this valley, might we not be permitted to take them as food and stave off our hunger?'

Sendenshi: 'Kill not that which has life, lest you too be slain!'

Hota: 'Ah, ah, it is utterly no use anymore. No matter how wondrous things we are made to hear, I think there is nothing other than food to save us. While seeing so many fish swimming about here, we are not permitted to catch and eat them; if we eat them, we shall instantly incur divine punishment, our eyes will grow dim, our mouths will twist, blotches will break out upon our faces, and our very bodies will end up in a guise that our own figures would fear... Ah, what would be best to do, ah?'

Thus the many starving people heaved sighs, gazing upon the lively fish of the valley stream.

Kotonochinokotomikoto, together with Kuninochiwake, singing proselytizing songs while paying homage at the storehouse shrine where the deity of the national soul is enshrined, with clear voices intoned the heavenly congratulatory liturgy and finished chanting the heavenly counting songs, advancing while singing proselytizing songs before the place where the Sendenshi of the Ural religion was preaching and transmitting the gospel of heaven to the many believers.

Kotonochinokotomikoto: 'When God appears in the open To set apart the good and evil, The God who created this world is Naobi, Of heart broad and great, Oonaobi. In the world of men, whatever may be, Re-see and re-hear it directly through Naobi; If there be errors, they are corrected and retold. The hundred gods who guard the teachings Of the infinite benevolence of the three-five, Beholding perfectly and in minute detail The tragic plight of the people of this land, Pray, save the lives Of the common folk troubled by famine. However much their living spirits may receive The grace of heaven, Unless now, right before our eyes, relief is given To the suffering of their bodies, Their hearts will grow warped and their souls clouded, And the precious physical bodies that should ascend To the divine kingdom will abruptly Fall into the nethermost land. If we look across this valley stream, There are storehouse-fish swimming To the extent of crowding the place; Ah, truly beautiful storehouse-fish, I pray, bestow them upon these people. God is not only of the spiritual realm, He is the one who causes the people living in this world, Leaving not a single one behind, to be moistened By the dew of His grace and protects their eternal lives. The divine ministers of the Mitsugoshikyō, Kotonochinokotomikoto and Kuninochiwake, As teaching ministers, have now come here to worship At the shrine of the national soul deity, And before the eyes of the gods of heaven and earth, Unable to bear the sight of the starving people Gathering close in this valley, we humbly entreat: If they be fish of the divine messenger, In order to preserve the lives of mortals, O God! I pray, grant them unto us. I shall henceforth have the multitudes Catch alive the fish of the valley, And grant them aid to preserve their lives. Ah, Kanagara-kamukamusemi, May your august spirits flourish and bring peace.'

So singing, they advance. Hearing this Sendenka, the sendenshi of the Ural-kyō lifts his eyes in anger, steps briskly up before Kotoyoriwake, and

Sendenshi: 'I am called Boor, a sendenshi of the Ural-kyō. Now, upon hearing your Sendenka, it seems you are uttering truly outrageous things. God instructs us not to kill any seibutsu. Yet you speak words of catching the fish of the valley sent as messengers of God, feeding them to hitobito in amata, and saving their lives... This must be called a most excessive violation of tensokuihan. Therefore, the teachings of the Ananai-kyō are something that leads the world into ankoku forever. You had better promptly face toward the Kami and perform norinaoshi. We cannot hear such kotoba and let them pass as kikizute. Come, let us hear your hentafu.'

Kotoyoriwake: 'Indeed, if the Kami dwells in shijin and shiai, it is only natural reason that from such mikokoro He dislikes taking the life of seibutsu. However, which is tauzen, is the life of man precious, or is the life of fish precious? If you think it over well, you will understand.'

Boor: 'Since human beings are a mass of sin, it is tauzen to suffer the torments of hell while yet alive. Therefore, is it not the true essence of human life to praise the mide of Pangu, the creator of the world where infinite and absolute power resides, abandon this manifest world of suffering, grief, and many disasters, and ascend to heaven as quickly as even for a moment? To take and eat the fish of the messengers of Kami, who are without sin, and bury them in the bellies of tainted humans—is it not determined by the divine teachings that divine punishment will instantly arrive and we will suffer the torments of hell eternally? You do not truly love the people under heaven, but are performing a merciless act trying to make them go against the heart of Kami and suffer the torments of hell eternally. For the sake of all the people under heaven, we must fight against you and your principles to the very end. Proof is better than argument: when someone happens to take and eat the fish of this valley, falling under the wrath of Kami and having divine punishment arrive instantly is already thoroughly known by the people of the land. You neither know the customs of this land nor understand the ordinances of Kami, and to utter such reckless things must be called a spirit of utmost wickedness and lawlessness.'

Kotoyoriwake: 'Today is an emergency situation where the people are just about to starve to death; even if they are messengers of Kami, is there any reason why the Kami of infinite benevolence and compassion would not permit this? If there were such a Kami, without a doubt it would be the hideous deity of Yamata-no-Orochi, or the wicked fox with golden fur and nine tails, or the doing of crooked demons. We have come to feel that your very words are the wicked and lawless conduct of leading humans, who are the children of Kami, into the evil path and making them suffer. There is fundamentally no reason whatsoever that divine punishment should fall just because we take and eat this fish. I assert this firmly.'

Boor: 'Are not the teachings of the Ananai-kyō a merciless teaching of utmost lawlessness? Then, do you try, among so many fish like this, even if it is just a small fish, to catch and eat one before my eyes. That divine punishment will instantly arrive is a fact clearer than seeing fire. No matter how splendidly your logic stands, you cannot win against the fact itself... Come, do you have the courage to eat this as well?'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Mr. Boor of the Ural-kyō, what nonsense are you talking. Are the teachings of the Ural-kyō so narrow-minded? Then you cannot be called a true Kami. Contrary to this, if I take and eat this fish and there is no reaction whatsoever, what do you intend to do? First of all, we must make a promise about this right now. What is your reply?'

Bool: 'That would be interesting. Now, go ahead and catch and eat the divine messenger, the mikurauo, right before my eyes. If there is no reaction whatsoever, we will lead the entire Ural Church and surrender at your religious sect's gates.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Are you certain? There surely cannot be any mistake, can there?'

Bool: 'How could a missionary ever utter two conflicting words? Now, execute it quickly.'

I do not know what it eats, but my, how fat and plump it has grown! ...Hello, Kyōshiyusama, things have become interesting. Please keep the missionary Bool's words well in your belly. Ah, how interesting, how interesting!'

Kuniyoriwake went down into the tanigaha while speaking thus, plunged right into the myriad schools of fish where water and fish were almost equal in proportion, grabbed a single, vibrant mikurauo with beautiful markings measuring about three shiyaku, and appeared before Bool,

Kuniyoriwake: 'This fellow indeed looks like the supreme prize. Even among divine messengers, it is an item akin to a divisional commander class, so if divine punishment is incurred by killing and eating it anyway, it is more profitable to devour a large, sweet-looking fellow. No matter how much one speaks of spirit overriding body, as long as the flesh exists, the worm in the stomach demands food. It is not something one can endure by sitting still and holding back. ...Now, Bool-sama and the myriad people lined up here, I, the Sankyō missionary Kuniyoriwake, will strike dead and prepare this fish which the Kami is said to have forbidden, and right before everyone's eyes, I shall go musha musha musha and eat it. If I eat it and divine punishment is not incurred, then divine punishment will surely not be incurred by you all either. Unlike certain missionaries somewhere who puff themselves up saying that man shall not live by bread alone, I cannot afford such a carefree attitude. I find myself wanting to say the opposite: man shall not live by spirit alone. ...What kind of human is one without bread... indeed.'

Speaking thus, he took out a short sword, pierced the throat of this vibrant large fish with a stab, killed it in a single stroke, quickly took pieces of the flesh, and crammed them into his cheeks one after another,

Kuniyoriwake: 'Aha-ha-ha-ha, what an indescribably sweet taste it gives! As long as fish exist in such inexhaustible numbers here, feeding fifty or a million people is an easy thing. Ah, will my mouth not distort soon? Will I not become unable to speak? My eyes look as though they will go blind, and why do markings not form all over my face? They are bound not to form; the Kami gave this to humans intending to sustain their lives, and created this fish exclusively in this land. As a preparation for times when such a famine arrives, the Kami purposefully prohibited it during normal times and stored it away for us. ...Tell me, Bool-sama, how is this? I am sorry to say, but the Ural Church has no choice but to surrender to the gates of Sankyō... Hear me, everyone, you need not be astonished at all, absolutely not. I have shown you the example in this manner. What is this about starving and dying without even receiving this bait given to us by the Kami? Now, eat quickly.'

At this voice, the myriad people acted as if greatly relieved, instantly leaped into the river, scooped up appropriately sized fish, and were eating them right there with joyful smacks of their lips. Bool and four or five missionaries of the Ural Church had slipped away and hidden their forms from this place before anyone knew it.

From this time on, the people of this land grew utterly disgusted with the Ural Church, revered the shrine of the God of Kunitama day and night, and furthermore became steadfast believers of Sankyō.

Kotooriwakenomikoto addressed Kuniyoriwake,

'I shall henceforth cross the land of Teru and proceed directly to the capital of Uzu, so you shall remain here for a while, preach the teachings to the people of the land, then cross over to the capital of Hiru, make a round of the land of Haru, and make your way to Uzu. Upon that, we shall deliberate leisurely and anew.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Understood in every detail. If that is so, it cannot be helped. We part ways here. I pray that you serve the divine missions in good health.'

Kotooriwake: 'Yes, thank you.'

Resounding the missionary song into the echoes of the valley, he advanced aiming for the land of Uzu.

Kuniyoriwake stopped for a while at the shrine of Mikura, taught the doctrine to the people coming to worship, and administered baptism. From this time on, the teachings of Sankyō became a force rising like the morning sun.
