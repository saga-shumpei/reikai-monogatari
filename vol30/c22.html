Chapter 22: Great 蜈蚣 [864]

大蜈蚣〔864〕

§ 1

Machiko and Kijiko, both sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, followed Kuniyoriwake, tracing the night road little by little.

As the night broke into dawn along the dewy path, they surged forward with vigor, marching briskly while singing trivial songs.

Arriving at the foot of Mount Arashika, they rested here awhile, and once more setting forth upon their shank's mare, they bravely climbed the steep slope, at length reaching the summit.

Bathing in the refreshing breeze, ah, how delightful, how delightful, as they looked out over the fields in all directions: the mountains and fields were blue, the rivers clear, and to the southwest, standing steeply with a crown of clouds, soared the sacred ground at the foot of Mount Higurashi.

Pointing at it, Kijiko indicated the sacred ground where the kyōshiyu of the Boole-Ural teaching was holed up, pointing it out to the noble official Kuniyoriwake while beginning idle talk, and then pointing to the northeast once more.

In the vast plain extending a thousand ri, the town of Hill, built long ago, saw Kaede-no-wake settle down to open Ananai-kyō.

With the divine hall towering below their eyes, its gable ridges rising high, though they could not clearly discern it as such, the banner fluttering in the wind made them feel elated that the capital of victory was close beneath their feet.

Just as they were enjoying the cool breeze to their hearts' content, the noble official Kuniyoriwake took the lead and dashed off, saying they would go quickly.

The two of them, lingering over their reluctance to part, unavoidably followed behind, descending the steep slope.

On one side of the road, old camphor trees grew thickly, where they discovered the Shin-ō-shi enshrining the founder of the Ural teaching.

Drawing near to look, a young woman, her face haggard, her hair disheveled, joined her hands and bowed her head before the shrine, whispering and praying about something.

Seeing this strange state, Kijiko drew close to her side, stroked her back, and spoke gently to comfort her, asking about the state of affairs.

At last the woman raised her face, and said she was the child of Esu who served as a sendenshi of the Ural teaching at the foot of Mount Arashika.

Her father had called a stop to the divine official of Ananai-kyō and lodged him in their home, and for this crime, he had been dragged away to the sacred ground of Mount Higurashi, confined in a pitch-dark, cruel and merciless water dungeon, suffering and lamenting morning and night, surely bewailing this fleeting world.

Rather than hearing of her father's calamity, her mother had been startled, causing her chronic colic to relapse, reaching a critical condition where she could not even drink water, growing emaciated and frail as the thread of a jewel, on the verge of breathing her last.

How could she, the daughter, just sit idly by and watch this happen without doing anything?

Before the divine presence of the founder who opened the Ural teaching, praying single-heartedly with her true heart dedicated to escaping her father's peril and healing her mother's illness even in a single day, she asked him to consider her heart.

When she replied thus, Kijiko, drowning in tears, said: 'Do not worry, Lady Erina! Kijiko, who serves the gods, will now display her power and surely save your father, Esu.'

'Machiko, you follow this way, go to the house of Esu, and quickly save the mother suffering from illness through the divine work of Chinkon Kishin.'

'As for the Lord God Kuniyoriwake, he will go forth to the hall of Hill where Kaede-no-wake, the divine official, is settled, and while Kijiko makes her triumphant return, please wait leisurely.'

At Kijiko's resolute words, Machiko sidled up and said: 'Hey, hey, Kijiko, that is unreasonable! No matter how weak the enemy may be, you are alone and it is perilous.'

'I will follow the command of Kuniyoriwake and come along with you too.'

'O Lord God Kuniyoriwake, and next Lady Erina, after we have made a glorious triumphant return, we shall meet again in days to come.'

Sooner said than done, Machiko tucked up her skirts, climbed and descended the pass of Arashika, and dashed off at a single stroke.

Crying 'Hey, hey, wait, Machiko!', Kijiko also tucked up her skirts.

They advanced running like Idaten.

Ah, gods of manifestation, let your august spirits bless us.

Kijiko and Machiko finally reached the foot of the log bridge over the Higurashi River.

Kijiko: 'Hey, Machiko, the previous night's fierce battle by Kijiko has resulted in this ancient battlefield of great exploits. This neighborhood is strewn with countless corpses; if you dig one foot underground, white bones appear, and at night, ghosts wail mournfully, making the loneliness press upon one's body—such a place of deeply memorable impressions. Shall we perform a memorial service for the enemy's wandering ghosts? Ah-ha-ha-ha, Namu Anan, Yūzu Daiji, sudden birth in Bodhi! How about it, shall I dedicate a propaganda song to you?'

§ 2

Machi: 'What are you talking about? This is not an old battlefield at all; it is a brand-new battlefield. Since it is the sacred land where we won a grand victory, it must be a shinsen-jiyō where we offer shinsen to the Kamisama as a token of our gratitude. Ah, the fresh air flows in like water and wipes away our sweat. How brave! How magnificent! Come, let us take itsupuku.'

So saying before he even finished speaking, he stretched himself out in the shape of the character for 'large' on the shibafu.

Kiji: 'Hey, are you already celebrating the grand victory and lying down in the shape of 'large'? If you lie there with your haibu down like that, you are bound to meet with the misfortune of a daihai.'

Machi: 'What? I am celebrating the engi that we will make the enemy suffer a daihai. If you look downward, it becomes a taifuku meaning that we will utterly crush the enemy. Ahahahaha.'

While chattering away such harmless things, exhausted from having run with such intense momentum, both men fell fast asleep before they knew it.

Awakened by the pain when one of his ears was bitten by an ōmukade that had crawled up with a rustling sound from who knows where, Kiji-kō promptly spat toward the centipede. At the spittle, which is daikinmotsu to the centipede, the ōmukade suddenly stretched its body stiff, turned blue, and collapsed on the spot. Without minding the pain in his ear, Kiji-kō applied spittle to his finger and smeared it onto the wound. Miraculously, the pain stopped instantly, and the swelling in his ear shrank away in the twinkling of an eye. Machi-kō awoke to this commotion, and upon looking around, saw that the ōmukade, overcome by the poison of the spittle, was practically at its last gasp. Gazing at this, Machi spoke:

Machi: 'Oi, Kiji-kō, you mustn't do things like sesshō. You spat medicine that is fatal to centipedes, didn't you? To kill a living creature is a violation of natural law. Why don't you hurry and help the centipede?'

Kiji: 'It isn't as though I deliberately like to commit useless sesshō. Since it was a wicked fellow that pried upon me during my sound sleep and bit my ear, I spat upon it thinking it was precisely a pest that deserved to be spat upon. My spittle is something mighty, isn't it? The moment I spit just once, such a giant centipede immediately attains jakumetsu-jaraku and tonshō-bodai, you see. Ahahahaha, samurai are certainly strange creatures, aren't they?'

Machi: 'If you dawdle like this, Centipede-kō, won't it really perish? Quickly take it down to the river and let it drink some water. If you do that, it will recover completely in no time and become active, brisk and lively just as before, you know.'

Kiji: 'When I look at the way this centipede walks, it is the exact spitting image of the way that fellow Anan came along last night, leading a large force of bamboo spear troops and forming a single-column array. This too must be some sort of omen. Shall we just leave it as it is? If that fellow Bū, like the centipede reviving, gets too spirited, it will become quite perilous for us since we are such a small minority party.'

Machi: 'Ahahahaha, it seems you are harboring anxiety somewhere after all, doesn't it? Didn't you boast quite a bit in front of Yoribake-sama? If you are going to pipe such cowardly tunes in a place like this, you won't be able to do a single thing when you face the vital battlefield.'

Kiji: 'Not at all, we just need to work when it is time to work. Right now, we are acting weak like this to store up our strength and cultivate our latent power. Oh, how bothersome, I'll go ahead and help the centipede.'

So saying, aiming at the current in the middle of the river, he grabbed it with his hand and threw it in. As the centipede plunged into the water, the poison vanished; swimming barely through the water, it climbed onto the bank, put forth all its horse-power into its hundred legs with great vigor right at their feet, and came rushing forward with a great velocity. The two men felt somehow intimidated and ran away, thumping along. Miraculously, the centipede kept chasing them as if it would go on forever—how loathsome it was! It pursued them all the way to the front of the cave on Mount Higurashi, known as the sacred land of the Ural religion, and then suddenly vanished. This centipede was manifested by Kuniyoribake-no-Mikoto, using the spiritual power of the jewel of spheres, in order to encourage the two men on their departure for battle.

と云ひ乍ら、川の中の流れを目がけて、手に掴んで投げ込んだ。蜈蚣は水に陥ると共に、毒は消ゑ、水中を辛うじて泳ぎ乍ら、岸に登り、二人が足許に勢能く、百本の足に馬力をかけ、大速力で突進し来る。二人は何となく、怖気つき、トントンと逃げ出した。不思議や蜈蚣は何処までもと云ふ調子で追つかけ来る、厭らしさ、とうとうウラル教の霊地と聞ゑたる日暮シ山の岩窟の前迄追つかけ来り、忽然として姿を消して了つた。此蜈蚣は言依別命が球の玉の霊力を以て、二人の出陣を励ますべく顕現せしめたのであつた。
