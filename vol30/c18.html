Chapter 18: Day 暮シの River [860]

日暮シの河〔860〕

§ 1

Ananai-kyō's Sendenshi Kuniyoriwake, leaving the shrine of Mikurayama behind, crossed rivers and climbed over mountain paths, gradually arriving at the outskirts of Chiru village, and just as he reached Arashi forest to rest his breath, the faction of Uraru no Hiko—who had established a cave in Higurashiyama and opened their teaching, starting with the Boule leader, relying upon the two persons Yuzu and Anan as the pillars of their left and right, and leading numerous subordinates—came out into Arashi forest, surrounded Kuniyoriwake, and how comical it was that they swarmed in to slay him in a single blow, while Kuniyoriwake, with a serene and enriched heart, within his mouth

having finished proclaiming the Ikukototama, crossed over to Riukiutau island, received the jewel of the dragon's jaws, and while harboring the divine power of the orb within his own mitama, today's journey found him transformed into a peerless divine person, facing countless enemies, clenching his right fist, extending his index finger, and when he emitted spiritual light toward the surging enemy army, smitten by the power, the entire crowd fled in an avalanche, while Kuniyoriwake, all alone, while sending off with his eyes the shadows of the enemies fleeing so fragilely, laughed aloud and gave thanks to the divine majesty and virtue, at which very moment, step by step, the shadow of a person appeared

The two believers Machi and Kiji drew near before Kuniyoriwake, placed both hands on the ground, and spoke thus: 'We are people of Chiru country, the two persons named Machi and Kiji who revere the god of Uraru; suffering from the calamity of famine, as the jewel-like life approached its bitter end today or tomorrow, when we gazed upon the form of the divine messenger, the Mikurayama fish, in the valley river below the shrine where the Kunitamagami is enshrined, and folded our hands to pray for safety and peace, the Sendenshi of the way of Uraru came out leading many, and preached the teaching that makes light of life and death; yet nevertheless, as far as our fleshly lives have breath, bread is one thing and the way of God is another,'

'however supreme it may be, if there be no worldly benefit, how could we ever assent? What shall we do?'—while they tossed to and fro, troubled in the darkness of denial, truly the precious Sendenshi—proclaiming the holy teachings of the moon of Ananai clearly and purely even from the heavenly skies, from the age of gods—caught and ate the Mikurayama fish, which God had forbidden, thereby tying the thread of life, regarding this world as the heavenly pure land, prolonging precious lives so as to work long in this present world—at this unexpected gospel of a heavenly way of salvation, we all revived, and the rain of joy instantly became tears that in the valley river

flowed and poured down, oh how precious! Ah, Kannagara-kamugami! How could we ever let such precious divine persons pass by unheeded in this manner? Until the end of our lives, yearning for your sacred footprints, granted permission to spread the pure teachings of the heavenly kingdom to all four quarters of the land, down to every single blade of grass, wishing to grant the dew of blessings—we two consulted together, and leaving our hometown behind, step by step, came yearning after your sacred footprints; O divine priest Kuniyoriwake, pray mercifully grant our desire purely, and we shall recompense one ten-thousandth of your great grace; our steadfast hearts for a thousand and eight thousand

generations shall never change until the age of Izumo,'—thus the men of valor, having fixed their hearts, begged with tears that their sincerity be received, while Kuniyoriwake remained lost in thought for a little while, but then said, 'Ah, it cannot be helped, it cannot be helped; though the transmission of the divine teaching is a path destined by the Great Deity as a journey of a single person, there is no reason to flatly refuse your earnest desire; if so, follow me, and for the sake of the teachings of Ananai-kyō, serve before the august presence of the Imperial Great Deity, O Machi and Kiji! Swearing together to the Tianjin Ōkami, Kunitsukami, and Kunitamagami, I shall grant your desire; ah, Kannagara-kamugami,'

'May the august spirits be at peace and flourish,' when he proclaimed the Ikukototama, Machi and Kiji leaped for joy, their bodies entrusted to the gods, even to the ends of the fields, the depths of the mountains, or the bottom of a thousand-fathom sea, serving the gods, never turning their backs on the declaration of the divine priest Kuniyoriwake, engraving it deep within the marrow of their hearts to keep it; ah, Kannagara-kamugami! Partaking of the divine grace, today is a joyous journey in the skies, as we go to serve as the disciples of the living shrine of Kuniyoriwake; from the very beginning when heaven and earth opened, encountering the true God, moistened by the dew of blessings, and advancing while bowing low in worship to the moonlight dwelling in the evening dew—such is the path of our advance.

§ 2

Warera wa ikaga naru kuwahau zo to / yorokobi kuruu zo isamashiki.

Kiji and Machi, the new believers, followed Kuniyoriwake, and headed toward the Capital of Hiru, joyfully and bravely advancing along the cool night road. They arrived at the banks of a large river flowing from the ravines of Mount Higura. In the wide riverbed, a clear stream flowed gently. However, due to the prolonged drought, the river water had dwindled, and now only on one side of the river did water flow like a sash, as if determined by custom. Kuniyoriwake came to a halt,

Kuniyoriwake: 'Ya, this is the famous River Higura, isn't it? It is a remarkably wide river, but with the severe drought lately, the river water seems to have decreased considerably. There is nothing a mere Ningen can do about this alone, is there.'

Kiji: 'Sendenshi-sama, further up this river is the sacred site of Mount Higura, where a faction of the Ural religion dug out a large cavern and enshrined the spirit of Panku Shin'ō there, boasting tremendous influence. However, at the foot of Mount Takateru in the Land of Teru, a Sendenshi named Ishikuma of the Brahman religion appeared with immense power, encroaching upon the sphere of influence of the Ural religion and making numerous converts, so these days they appear to have declined considerably. The religious leader named Buur, who appeared in the valley stream of Mount Okura the other day and fled home abashed by your Kototama, is entrenched in this sacred site upstream.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Ah, is that so? It must be quite a difficult place.'

Kiji: 'It is an extremely difficult place, but usually this River Higura has deep, gentle, clean water, so sailboats go all the way to the foot of the mountain. Thus transportation was carried out smoothly, but with the water gone from the river like this, it is terribly inconvenient. I have also made pilgrimages there once or twice as a believer of the Ural religion; it is truly a splendid cavern, so vast that the eye can hardly reach its ends.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'I should love to go and explore it at least once.'

Machi: 'That would be vastly interesting. We could make you Momotarō, Kiji-kō can play the role of the pheasant, and I shall be a dog named Machi and act as your guide. However, since there is no one to play the role of the monkey, we are in a bit of a fix, aren't we.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Ahahaha, that is a good idea. But it must be quite far, I suppose.'

Machi: 'As for the distance, according to rumors, some say eight ri, some ten ri, and others thirteen ri, which are all conflicting rumors, so if we take the middle ground and set it at about nine ri or so, how would that be.'

Kiji: 'If we make such detours, we will be late in getting to the Capital of Hiru. How would it be if you first finished with Hiru and came back leisurely afterwards, since someone to play the role of the monkey might turn up in the Capital of Hiru as well...'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Ah, that would be fine too. Laden to the brim with all kinds of jewels such as gold, silver, pearls, coral trees, agates, and giant clams, a triumphal return like Momotarō's would also be quite interesting.'

Thus they indulged in jesting talk.

Just as they were doing so, from the direction upstream, gleaming in the moonlight, came a party of countless white flags with triple-hollyhock crests dyed red in the night wind, fluttering as countless shadows of people, forming a centipede formation along the river, with noisy footsteps and busy strides, advancing toward this direction. This was none of other than the group of Ural religion Sendenshi and believers led by Anan and Yūzu, on their way to attack the Capital of Hiru.
