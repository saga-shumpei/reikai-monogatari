Chapter 9: Ugly の Kotodama [851]

醜の言霊〔851〕

§ 1

From the valley of Momotanichitani in Kauzan, the southeastern pool flowing and gathering together is filled with azure waves, praised as a bottomless pool; sometimes even when there is no wind, circular waves are drawn here and there within the pool, and water spray like a whale spouting water soars fiercely into the sky, which can be known even by seeing this that it is no ordinary pool.

The party of Suekohime gradually, taking the road slightly to the right, arrived at the bank of the southeastern pool in question.

The Amatsuhi no Kami, who had been shining brilliantly until now, was enveloped in black clouds, the clouds gradually increasing in density, and descending by and by, the curtain of darkness descended upon the vicinity of the pool.

The curtain of fear and gloom, whose situation was not easy, descended.

The sound of the waves is roaring, like a hundred thunders roaring all at once, making one's flesh creep.

Suekohime quietly in words turned toward Ishikuma,

Suekohime: 'Ishikuma-sama, on the way coming here, there was Karl-sama's thorough caution. Nevertheless, once I have given permission from my lips, there is no reversing it. Moreover, it must be a matter of regret for you too to abandon your hard-won hope midway. Now please, you, take the vanguard.'

Ishikuma turned pale, trembling his lips violently, and while clattering his teeth,

Ishikuma: 'Yes, ah, I am very grateful. The truth of the matter is I thought so, but having heard Karl-sama's opinion on the way, for us whose spirits are truly not yet polished, to put aside the distinguished missionary-sama and try to win the merit of the vanguard, was an unthinkable impropriety. Please withdraw just this matter, I beg of you.'

So he declined, nearly crying.

Suekohime repressed her sense of humor, purposely straightening her posture, and with solemn words,

Suekohime: 'Ishikuma-sama! There is no revocation in the words of a missionary. In Arototo, there is no such thing as retreating upon seeing difficulty. Moreover, to carry through one's initial resolve is the true duty of a man.'

Being thus pinned down by Suekohime, who was still young and like a flower bud, with no words to return and scratching his head,

Ishikuma: 'Yes... If so, following your command, I shall... Ha, launch the Kototama.'

Karl: 'Ahahahaha, interesting, interesting! Ishikuma-sama's outstanding achievement. Come, let us place ourselves in the neutral zone and watch today's battle... Ishikuma-sama! I rely on you firmly. Set your waist and take it on; if you do not fasten your bellyband tightly, there is a risk of congestion after childbirth, so shall I go call a midwife or prepare a morphine injection? Ahahahaha!'

Suekohime: 'Hey, Karl-sama! Please restrain yourself for a while.'

Karl: 'Yes, I shall strictly behave myself.'

Building wide the hall of Kyō, Revered as the lord of Kyōshiyu, A Gauketsu peerless under heaven, Even the preceptors of Brahmanism, Possess this much courage indeed; He who fashioned and solidified this world, The Shiyusaishin of Ananai-kyō, Kuniharutachinōokami's, Rare disciple have I become, Appearing as the Mitama of Mizu, Kamususanōnōokami's, Rare child Suekohime, Excel greatly in Kototama, Becoming a disciple of that noble personage, Just now I have come forth here; Though yesterday I came forth strongly, Today I am even stronger, The strength-peerless Ishikuma of Shinriki, Swinging the heavenly Nuhoko, Savoring the proclaimed Kototama, You had better take off your helmet quickly; God appearing in the front, Distinguishing Zenshin and Jiyashin, Knowing that evil's schemes forever, Will not continue on, You too reform your heart, And quickly attain liberation; Ah, Kannagara kamugami-gami, May the Mitama prosper and bless us, Blowing away the eight-layered dark clouds, Wrapped in the serpent's sorcery, This rain like hailstones, Pray let it clear up swiftly; Ananai-kyō's missionary, With heart and body Ishikuma, Exerting his iron-stone heart, Of the great merciful and compassionate Ōkami, To bestow salvation through His grace, At last I have headed right here; You must be the female serpent of the great serpent, The male great serpent is Suekohime, To the Kototama proclaimed, Shedding joyful tears, Liberating his fellow beasts, Ascending merrily to heaven; By now, no doubt, up in heaven, He has gone before the divine presence, At ease in Takamagahara, Seated to the right of Sumeru Ōkami, Peering down at the nether world; Ah, Kannagara kamugami-gami, Receiving the power of God, Commencing the Kototama battle; If you do not understand my words, You may do as you please; With the disposition of a single heart, Whether to spend this world in suffering, Or spend it spiritedly and easily, Ascending to heaven to become a god, Crossing the world in peace, Until when will you dwell at the bottom of the pond, Receiving the afflictions of three fevers and three chills a day, Spending forever in affliction—that is entirely a matter of your own heart's breast; You had better repent quickly; God appearing in the front, Distinguishing good and evil; Kunisatsuchi no Ōkami who fashioned this world, Oohirume no Mikoto of wide heart, Seeing straight, looking again, listening again, To say nothing of Aonohito-gusa, Even the spirits of serpents and demons, I shall save and help unto peace; Rejoicing in the grace of God, Obey my Kototama; Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, Whether the moon waxes or wanes, Even if the great earth sinks, With the sole Kototama of sincerity, As long as life continues, I will not let you off without saving you; Scooping up my precious true heart deeply and quickly, Blowing away the eightfold clouds of heaven, Ceasing the falling of the strange rain, Making manifest once more the heavenly divine radiance, O ugly serpent! The true heart that saves you, Overflowing right here is the water of the pond, Scoop up the deep grace! Ah, Kannagara kamugami-gami, May the Mitama prosper and bless us!

Thus he finished chanting his poem. For some reason the clouds swirled down all the more, the water surface grew ever more high-waved, and the rain poured down like hailstones, pattering here and there in patches, marking ripples upon the pond's surface as if small stones had been thrown into it. Yet, wondrously, not a single drop of rain fell upon the bodies of the seven travelers.

Karl: 'Ahahaha, what a wonderfully effective Kototama it is! Every time you unleash the Kototama, the battle grows more and more fierce—such is the tune, the waves gradually grow higher, the clouds grow denser by and by, and the large drops of rain fall thicker by the hour; truly, depending on the user, even the Kototama can turn out any which way! To let a man like this unleash the Kototama is just like handing a torch to a madman and letting him run loose; it is extremely dangerous and I can't bear to watch. That is why I advised you along the way that it would be best to refrain, but you had to go meddling uninvited and made a fine fool of yourself. I stopped you because I somehow didn't want to let you fail, you see. In short, I merely wanted to let you perform the feat of an unsheathed sword. Why wouldn't you listen to this Karl's advice—Karl, who has never lost even once to Marishiten, the strongest sumo god in the whole wide world? Even if Yoshikohime-hime urged you on, isn't it the proper path to hold back?'

Ishikuma: 'Do not blame me so. The efficacy of the Kototama will surely manifest itself presently, so give me a little time and you had best observe my skill. Furthermore, just now you said that you have never even been defeated by Marishitensama, but where and when did you engage in Sumau?'

Kāru: 'Even the god of Sumau, the most powerful in the Sekaiichi, has never been known to lose if he does not lock arms, has he? That is why I thought that if you too remained silent and left things to Himesama, you would not have to suffer the shame of being told you were defeated by Orochi and expose yourself to the disgrace of the Matsudai, so I spoke to you out of Shinsetsu. There is a saying that good medicine is bitter to the mouth and remonstrance grates upon the ear, so my blunt Chiukoku is hardly likely to be welcomed by you. Nevertheless, I am not a person of Ayutennei, so I expressed my Shisei Seiseidaudau and gave you a Chiui. However, now that things have come to this, there is no taking it back. Ah, what a predicament... Look at that! Are not the waves rising higher and raging wildly all the more?'

Ishikuma: 'In Ōkata, struck by the power of my Kototama, that fellow the Orochi is writhing and raging in agony deep in the earth, I suppose. If that were not so, there would be no reason for the waves to rise and roar to that extent. The craftsmanship is flowing, so just watch the finishing touch, ha ha ha ha!'

While putting on a bold face thus, he was driven by a sense of anxiety in his heart.

Masshi: 'Ishikuma-sama, somehow your Kototama was a bit unsuccessful. Recite it once more, please.'

Ishikuma: 'Well, the materials for the Kototama are already exhausted, and there is nothing I can do about it. Will you please lend me a little capital?'

Masshi: 'Ho ho ho ho ho, saying such carefree things, if you do not do it quickly, a terrible thing will come to pass. As for the matter you started, you must bring it to a conclusion, and no one else can do anything about it. Come, now quickly chant the Amatsu Norito, sing the hymns of heaven to the end, purify your heart anew, and once more direct your words toward the Orochi, and dispatch the Kototama.'

Kāru: 'Look at that... Did I not tell you from the very beginning not to meddle? This is what they call being driven into a tight corner with nowhere to turn. Have you not fallen into a plight just like stepping one's foot into a birdlime tub? Come, as Himesama commands, firmly steady your torso, cram your breath deep into your seika tanten, and launch the Kototama of serene brightness. And toward the Orochi, you must not use overly contemptuous language. To make all things submit through the sacred divine words of good speech and beautiful terms is the military art of divine affairs. Come quickly, reform and rebuild your heart, press open your seika tanten, and launch the living Kototama.'

Ishikuma, having no other choice, once again began reciting the Kototama with all his might facing the surface of the pond. The waves of the pond mounted higher moment by moment, becoming like mountains. The grimness of that spectacle was utterly beyond the power of brush and tongue to exhaust.
