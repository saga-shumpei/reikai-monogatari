Chapter 6: 樹下の一宿 [848]

樹下の一宿〔848〕

§ 1

Yonin were Tanza upon the rock of a Tennen chair, and Shibaraku rested their breath.

Sutekohime said, 'Kāru-san, you have raised quite a grand metoru, haven't you. Thanks to that, I descended here so Omoshiro and down the Kifuhan before knowing it. I felt that poetry is truly an indispensable thing for a journey. Thanks to you singing it with such a pleasing rhythm, my heavy legs and body both Yakudō-ed, and it was Taihen Yukuwai, wa.'

Kāru said, 'Hai, I spoke Detarame with my Kāru mouth. By that Okage, your Miashi moved like a Kāru, I suppose, aha haha. However, since Ishikuma are rolling about, it must have been a Jiyama, and you must have used your eyes quite a bit.'

Ishikuma: 'I do not know why, but such things like Dangoishi or Kāru stones are rolling all about on this steep slope, and at each bounce my feet could not even move; with such a too-ridiculous song, even my Hizabauzu were laughing, you know. With a voice like dragging a Yakan across Ishihara, I felt Zuibun Arigatameiwaku. Aha haha.'

Kāru: 'To call it Arigatameiwaku, aren't your mouth a bit too foul? You seem to have an Ijikune... ottodokkoi, it seems Ishikuma is not going well, ah.'

Ishikuma: 'Now, everyone, shall we Tekurimasu again, little by little?'

Sueko: 'Hai, let us set off leisurely... Kāru-san, please pray for us again.'

Kāru: 'Oi Ishikuma-san, how is it, you were able to endure it well, weren't you. What about your own song, how pitiful, didn't you throw out a defamed verse of mournful tone, saying it was a single hint of fondness and a letter for two Naisu? Won't you pull yourself together a bit?'

Ishikuma: 'Do not speak so noisily, it is an attachment of unfaked emotion.'

Kāru: 'At any rate, with Ishikuma-san's repentance and allegiance, this Kāru Great Propaganda Envoy is also overjoyed from the bottom of his heart, atsuha haha. Now, I will drive the Kototama carriage once more... one, two, three, advance altogether!'

Ishikuma: 'Aha haha, what an incorrigible fellow... Now, Himesama, please set forth, I shall accompany you.'

Kāru: 'Aha haha, how this is a changing Kāru world. Until now, you were a heartless fellow like stone, making a countenance as if you had bitten into a bitter bug whenever you saw a woman, yet the master of Brahmanism suddenly turned his temple-bell mouth into a puckered mouth and made his chestnut eyes narrow... Now Himesama, please set forth, I shall accompany you... No matter what anyone says, it is the height of rolling with laughter. If you are too captivated only by Himesama's style, you will truly have to capstone-topple and fall on the slope road. What a troublesome blockhead has become my companion on the road. Still, a withered tree is a bustling of the mountain; isn't it better than nothing?'

Sueko: 'Oh my, how foul Kāru-san's mouth is.'

Ishikuma: 'No, this Kāru's mouth is too good, and he chatters away well. Since he is a fellow like a bird called a shrike, whichever way he goes to hell, he is an article that will be troubled by the nail-puller. Aha haha.'

Sutekohime: 'Oho haha, now let us set out at last. This time, I shall become Sarutahiko and go ahead.'

And standing first, she descended the steep slope. Riding on the rhythm, Kāru once more kept time step by step, and began to chant in a style as if a Yakkosusha were stamping out the six directions.

Kāru: 'Untokodokkoi, dokkoisho / Mountain path full of Ishikuma / Princess Sueko and Princess Sute / Accompanying the two honored ones / Walking down the rugged slope road / The Sun Lord is blazing hot / Teru country upon the head / The steep slope of Teru Mountain Pass / Ottodokkoi, it is dangerous / I stepped on an Ishikuma again / Is that roaring the valley river? / Do not vomit like jang-jang, cicadas / Dokkoishō, dokkoishō / Sweat and fat squeezed in a single wringing / My wife at home is dokkoisho / Without sleeping a single night, with kindness / The single-layered robe she sewed for me / Dokkoidokkoi, drenched through / It has become, dokkoisho / The two Naisu are just like this'

Walking boldly at the very front, The style of your descending step Is like the heavenly eight-fold clouds parting, As Tanabatahime descends from on high— Such is the pure and dashing grace. With nostrils flared and puffing loud, Oh, the wretched and exhausted Ishikuma, What a strange posture for your footfalls! With a loincloth measuring one fathom and two feet, Surely you must have some pride to uphold. Back in the ancient Age of Gods in the land of Uzu, The royal daughter of Momogamihiko, With the rhythmic chant of Matsutake-ume, dokkoishiyo! Oops, danger, I stumbled and tripped! A Sendenshi who puts flowers to shame, Alongside Odoyamazumi and Komayamahiko, With the rousing beats of Susanoo's playful strain, Marching at the front like an incarnation Of the god Kagatora, hand in hand with him, Down this steep slope, marching with bold strides,

Until you arrived at the port of Hara— When you think of the journey you have made so far, Even if you call it steep, it is a downward slope; What bitter hardship could possibly remain? Untoko dokkoi, dokkoishiyo! Stubborn and crooked-hearted Ishikuma Is tumbling down head over heels all around. Everyone, please take great care! Lest by mischance you fall flat on your back, And your precious ship of jewels upon the slope Goes dokkoi, dokkoi, dokkoishiyo! Oh, excuse me, what a blunder, a failure, a song— Carried away by the rhythm, treating the boat like a cart, Disconnecting the shafts and steering wheel, Unawares, with a dokkoishiyo, You displayed the very art of derailing! And speaking of that, I wonder what Nero Is doing now and where he might be? To swindle Sheena, Chill, and Isaac so sweetly,

It is truly pitiful that you followed along To help those two nice gentlemen! If they were to hear that Ishikuma-sama Surrendered to the divine path of Mitsugo, And became the follower of those two nice gentlemen Whom he once targeted as his deadly foes, Journeying all the way to the capital of Uzu with a dokkoishiyo, Just imagine how shocked Isaac, Sheena, and Chill Would surely be! Ah, how amusing, how amusing! Today's sky changes from yesterday's; Clear skies suddenly turn into rain, The blue sea transforms into earth. The weather alters in a flash, Surrendering to the enemy's camp, Flinging down spears and rolling up flags— Dokkoi, dokkoi, a skinny dog With a sweet-faced traveler, Wagging its tail with a dokkoishiyo,

Following along in such a style, If they were to hear that a Brahmin priest Had accompanied them on the way, Surely they would be astounded and dumbfounded, Their souls flying straight up to heaven! Dokkoi, dokkoi, their testicles Would instantly take a journey across the sea! Dokkoi, I slipped, ouch, ouch, ouch! Smooth and fluid without delay, A carriage wheel spinning sweetly astray. Along with it, another stone wheel rolls on— A treacherous downward slope, I earnestly beg everyone to be on guard. Untoko dokkoi, danger lies ahead! If we descend just a little further from here, We hear that the lingering spawn Of the eight-branched Yamata no Orochi Are lurking about, And the pond of Tatsumi shines deep and blue Like a polished mirror. If you listen to the rumors, dokkoishiyo,

They say that monster snake in the pond of Qian Is nothing less than its very counterpart. Now, O youngest princess of mine, Why not stop by as a further occasion, And conquer the spirit of the great serpent Through the almighty power of Kototama? Dokkoi, dokkoi, along the path of our journey, Perform a glorious feat of arms! I will gladly act as your guide. I wonder what has become of Ishikuma-sama, Seized as he is by a sudden chill of cowardice; As for me, I want to go and see no matter what. Your pure Kototama Will instantly cause evil gods and wicked demons To cry dokkoi, dokkoi, danger ahead! Ah, what a dangerous and grueling road it is! They will surely surrender without a doubt. An opportunity like this will never come again; I humbly and earnestly beg you. If you can lift the calamities

Afflicting the people of Argentina night and day, Then you are truly the sacred pillar of Uzu; The entire citizenry will be filled with awe and joy, And worshiping your boundless divine virtue, They will yield to the sacred grace of Mitsugo. Ah, oh Kami, oh Kami! Manifest the divine heart And I beg you to carry this through with resolve! This is the heartfelt prayer to which Karl Has devoted his entire lifetime. With reverence and awe, we two Humbly pray before your august presence. Ah, oh Kami, oh Kami, May your august spirits flourish in peace!'

§ 3

At last, descending the steep eight-ri slope around dusk, the party of four reached the foot of the mountain, sheltered from the night dew in a camphor grove, and resting their breath, once again indulged in conversation.

Sueko: 'Thanks to your grace, we have safely crossed the difficult pass. The virtue of Kototama is truly a wonderful thing, ah.'

Kāru: 'Most certainly. Especially since my Kototama achieves such harmony, the heavenly deities and divine spirits were moved, and even the scorching sun finally took pity on us and hid itself below the horizon, no doubt.'

Ishikuma: 'Ah-ha-ha-ha, what nonsense are you spitting out. When the time for dusk comes, even without your Kototama, the sun will enter on its own accord. Do not get too carried away and become conceited.'

Kāru: 'Look here, what a scenic view. You are a terribly inflexible and foolishly honest man, aren't you. Adjusting to the society by grasping both the long and the short is the most precious method of life. At this rate, even the teacher of Brahmanism is hopeless, ah.'

Ishikuma: 'A human being like you, whose one leg is made shorter, doesn't have any complementing of the short with the long, so I surely cannot imitate you.'

Kāru: 'Don't say stupid things. My leg is not short; one of my legs is longer than you fellows', ah-ha-ha-ha.'

Ishikuma: 'You are a man who hates to lose and argues with twisted logic. Although it is not Mount Caucasus, you descended the steep slope with the style of Bikkusu God stamping out the six directions, so it was quite comical to watch.'

Suteko: 'Ho-ho-ho-ho, it is immensely pleasant to travel with you people, ah.'

Kāru: 'That is naturally how it should be. Since I am a teacher who has seen through pleasure, the phenomenal world, and the divine realm all at a glance, it is the natural consequence, wah. Ah-ha-ha-ha.'

Ishikuma: 'Consequence, stumbling, there is nothing of the sort. You are a hero who tumbles and falls on a mere steep slope like this, after all.'

Kāru: 'A rump is a rump, but I am a hero of Jean-Jahier. As a person of the populace, do not go about saying rump-rump.'

Sueko: 'O-ho-ho-ho, ever since we departed from Ken'onkō, I have never had such a pleasant feeling as today. ...However, Kāru-sama, you mentioned earlier that a fragment of the great snake of the northwest pond is lurking in the southeast pond or somewhere, causing harm to the people, didn't you?'

Kāru: 'Yes, if we go about three ri south from here, there is a terribly deep and large pond. A demon god of a great snake has made its dwelling there since when I know not, and whenever it sees a passing young man, it immediately transforms into a wondrously beautiful woman, deceitfully lures him into the pond, and swallows him up. One cannot know how many hundreds of people have had their lives taken by the great snake. Therefore, I wish to make one request of you.'

Sueko: 'That sounds like it could be interesting. Tonight we shall take shelter from the rain here, drop by the southeast pond early tomorrow morning, and try beginning a Kototama battle. ...Suteko-hime-sama, what do you think?'

Suteko: 'I am exceedingly in agreement. Enjoying tomorrow's sun, let us decide to wait here this evening.'

Kāru: 'Thank you for your prompt consent. ...Hey Ishikuma-sama, it would be a pity if you were to fall into a tonic state again, so I shall decide to exempt you.'

Ishikuma: 'I say, you two esteemed ones... Won't you please let me accompany you for the sake of observation?'

Sueko: 'Please come along after us.'

Ishikuma: 'Thank you very much. Tomorrow I too must perform with the activity of a thousand horsemen and give Kāru-sama a good surprise, you see.'

Suteko: 'Please do so.'

Thus the four of them sheltered from the rain and dew in the dense forest where the camphor trees sealed the sky, and spent the night bringing about the dawn.

末子『それは面白う御座いませう。今晩はここで雨宿りを致しまして、明朝早々巽の池へ立寄り、言霊戦を始めて見ませう。……捨子姫さま、あなた如何思ひますか？』

捨子『至極賛成です。明日の日を楽しんで、今宵は此処で待つことに致しませう』

カール『早速の御承知、有難う御座います。……オイ石熊さま、お前は又強直状態になつて了うと、気の毒だから、免除する事にしてやらう』

石熊『モシ、お二方様……どうぞ私も見学の為、随行さして下さいませぬか』

末子『どうぞ跟いて来て下さい』

石熊『有難う御座います。私も明日は千騎一騎の活動を致しまして、カールさまに一つビツクリさしてやらねばなりませぬからなア』

捨子『さうなさいませ』

茲に四人は楠の空を封じたる密林に雨露を凌ぎ夜を明かすこととなりける。
