Chapter 1: 主従二人 [843]

主従二人〔843〕

§ 1

Appearing in the land of Tokoyo, Tokoyoshinō Jizaiten made Ōkunihiko the principal deity, and as the priestly administrator of Ōkuniwake who founded Brahmanism, crossing over ten thousand waves, he appeared in the land of Egypt, built up the training ground of the Brahman teachings in the capital of Iho, and for a time, like the rising sun, the light of the teachings shone across lands in all directions; yet the divine teachings of Brahmanism, unless they beheld the living blood of human beings, could not be pacified; their cruel and lawless severe ascetic practices caused believers who entered the faith, under the name of mind-matter subordination, to wear wooden clogs driven with iron nails, to go naked and leap into thorn chambers, tearing and scratching their bodies, or to enter raging fires, plunge to the bottoms of waters, and in manifold sufferings too harsh to endure; even believers who once approached drifted away day by day, sorrowing and lamenting, until before long they fled, and just as the lamp of the Dharma was about to be extinguished and tilted toward tragic fortune, Natsumayamahiko and Iwaihime, along with the priestly administrator Hirahikowake, taking the capital of Egypt as their base, widely proclaimed and transmitted the truth of mind-matter subordination to the people of the world; and the teachings of Izunomesa, protected by the strict divine spirit of the ancestor of the land of infinite benevolence, Kunisachiritsu Ōkami, flourished day by day alongside the sun and moon.

The great leaders of Brahmanism, starting with Onigumohiko, together with the two couples of Onikumowake, leaving the capital of Egypt behind, leading numerous believers, crossed the Eden River into Ken'ongō, a place of deep historical origin where the Southern King was secluded, and here once again built up and solidified the foundation of the Brahman Way, piling up boulders and stacking stones as a towering fortress of impregnable sheer cliffs, and relying upon this Ken'ongō; the crooked teachings, turning toward the momentum of rebirth, starting with the Central Land of Mesopotamia, from the land of Fusa to the land of India, fiercely proclaimed themselves; however, perhaps unable to endure the savage, extreme, and severe ascetic practices, voices of grief and complaint were heard echoing near and far, and unable to bear witnessing a pity akin to the buzzing of mosquitoes, the True August Spirit, Kami Susanoo no Ōkami, desired to have the numerous propagation envoys of the Brahman priestly administrator speak to and guide the people, earnestly preaching and explaining the profound heart of the Great Deity who created heaven and earth to the Brahman people, causing the entire world to bathe in the dew of the grace of the August Deities, and making them look up to the divine government of the age of Miroku; and so, dispatching eight maidens, he caused them to enter the teaching hall of Ken'ongō, outwardly adorned as believers of Brahmanism.

The eight maidens, embracing the deep heart of the Great Deity in their hempen pillars, without aversion for their own precious august bodies, boldly and fearlessly served morning and evening at the ugly fortress of the crooked deity with careful minds. Kami Susanoo no Ōkami sent Futo-tama no Kami, one of the five companion deities who opened the Heavenly Rock Cave, to guide the group of Onigumohiko into the true way, and crossed the Eden River alone, advancing deep into Mesopotamia to open the pure foundation of the divine country, while chasing behind him, the priestly administrators, lining up their horse heads, advanced without difficulty into the innermost chamber of Ken'ongō where the eight maidens were hiding; there Onigumohiko of Brahmanism came out to welcome them, and with cunning words smoothed things over, beginning a plot of poisoned wine; but the evil plot was suddenly and thoroughly exposed, and abandoning their own dwelling like clouds and mist, they easily and frailously concealed their figures in the land of Persia.

Hereupon the propagation envoy Futo-tama remained behind in Ken'ongō to transmit the divine teachings, while the eight maidens, each accompanied by female attendants, set out from the land of Persia to propagate their faith, with the purpose of guiding and reconciling the ugly spirits of the Yamata no Orochi coiled throughout the lands of the Eight Islands, and banishing a hundred disasters from beneath heaven; but as they advanced to begin their mission, they were seized by the remnants of Brahmanism, separated from one another, and placed into plankless boats to be cast adrift far out across the ocean. The youngest daughter born as the last child of the eight maidens, Suekohime, set afloat in a small abandoned boat risking her precious life upon the waves, comforting the lord of her master, dexterously took up the oars, and threading her way among the large and small islands scattered across the central Atlantic, drifted with the waves to land at the port of Hara in the country of Teru, crossed over the Utsuyama Pass, and advanced toward the capital, the historic site of Momogamahiko; ah, may the august spirits grant their blessing and favor.

§ 2

Suekohime, together with Sutekohime, at last safely arrived at the port of Hara, and hurrying her steps to proceed to the land of Chin, she approached the foot of Mount Teru Pass, the border between the lands of Teru and Uzu. The eighth-day moon showed its elliptical shape high in mid-heaven, peering down upon the heads of these two lovely beauties.

Suekohime: 'Although we met with terribly frightening experiences countless times, thanks to Kamisama we were at last sent as far as Takasagojima, and is this not a most joyous thing? This too was entirely through the agency of Brahmanism, whereby the two of us handmaids were sent forth under the deep divine Oshigumi of Kamisama. I have heard that this land was a place once ruled over by the Kamisama Masakayamazumi, but shall we not from here proceed to the capital of Chin, search out the present Yōsu of Ananai-kyō, and engage in Senden?'

Sutekohime: 'That is indeed so. Although you are still so young in years, you have come to this vast land, and your Yūki in thinking to perform Senden makes me truly feel Kokorozuyo. However, when we arrived at the port of Hara, people were talking about how the Seiriyoku of Brahmanism and Uralism also mingle here, and that there is a latent power not to be taken lightly, so we must never be off our guard. Nevertheless, with it now becoming such nightfall, traveling along the mountain paths feels somehow insecure, and furthermore our bodies are terribly grass-weary, so shall we, here upon this turf for the first time in a long while, let our bodies commune with the earth, wait for dawn, and decide to cross this pass?'

Suekohime: 'That would be most proper.'

So saying, the two fell asleep with their elbows as pillows.

Four or five men who had descended Mount Teru Pass quietly, without even knowing that two persons lay fast asleep and utterly grass-weary on the turf in the shade of the trees, appeared here,

Ko: 'Hey, our legs seem to have grown quite weary. How about taking a rest here for a bit?'

Otsu: 'Is this a suspension of our Hizakurige? All right, let us rest and go on.'

Hei: 'You cannot afford to say such carefree things. Since there is the command from Great General Ishikuma that a party of female Senden-shi of Ananai-kyō is sure to drift ashore around tonight, and that the moment we find them we are to capture them and bring them back to the mansion of Takateruyama, we cannot afford to rest too comfortably tonight. It was said they would surely arrive safely at the port of Hara, and notice was given to Great General Ishikuma-sama by wireless spirit-speech from the land of Fusa. Come, one more final push. It would be a disaster if they went off toward the waterfall of Alina or somewhere, so leaving rest for later, shall we dash out one step further in the cool of the night?'

Tei and Bo, speaking in unison:

''As the oil for my transportation has run out, it is no use whatever you might say. It cannot be bartered for either greed or gain. Come, do not be like that, how about joining us for a puff?'

Ko: 'What a weak fellow. If so, I will make a sudden concession and grant you a brief rest.'

Tei: 'Even officials who stamp blindly without reading, starting late in the morning and returning early in the afternoon, are given midsummer vacations these days, so for me, being made to work night and day, I simply cannot endure it, ha-ha-ha-ha!'

Ko: 'Even officials have had their midsummer vacations abolished recently.'

Hei: 'Even by abolishing midsummer vacations and trying to force dullards to move, it is of no use. It only lowers efficiency instead. As Chiiru and Nero say, let us be granted a sound vacation right here tonight. Right... General Isaku...'

Isaku: 'Do not call me Isaku, it cannot be helped, rest and go on. Sleep in silence.'

Chiiru: 'I am not like you, sleeping and talking at the same time, nor am I capable of such cleverness, so please rest assured. You are indeed a fellow who talks in his sleep remarkably well. When you talk in your sleep like that, it looks so unseemly that it puts me in a mood where I cannot remain quietly at ease beside you.'

Shīna: 'Did you hear my sleep-talking? Because my singular devotion to bringing peace and security to all under heaven is hardened and concentrated within me, whether sleeping or waking, I am filled with the sincere words of lamenting for the world. Since you do not have much learning, you probably don't understand my words.'

Chīru: 'Don't talk nonsense as if you're thinking about the affairs of the world. No matter when it is, your household is a petticoat government, so it seems you've become utterly henpecked; as soon as you fall asleep, you open your mouth wide saying "Ah, ah, ah, ah," snoring away, and you end up saying things like "It hurts, it hurts" or "It's heavy, it's heavy." ...Hey, Nero, this fellow's sleep-talking is always as if it were stamped with movable type, isn't it?'

Nero: 'This fellow here, without a doubt, must have grabbed a passing traveler in the valley of Takateruyama, and after doing such-and-such, done such-and-such to them. Doesn't he always say strange things in his sleep? ...Hey, Shīna, you are now putting on great airs as though you are a propagandist of Brahmanism, but it looks like you've done something bad.'

Shīna: 'I know nothing of the sort. Don't go saying such annoying things in the middle of the night; I've started feeling ill. An unknown god brings no curse.'

Chīru: 'Ha ha ha ha, what a man he is, getting terribly frightened of the talk about Takateruyama when night falls!'

Nero: 'That's a given. There's been quite an extraordinary matter. The only one holding the key to that secret is Lord Nero. Since a ghost called Nero is following right behind this fellow all the time, sticking to him with just the two of them, even though he talks proudly, when night comes he shakes with fear just stepping out one doorway. If we talk about things like this, that ghost will come along and go "Whoosh, muddy and swirling" again.'

Shīna: 'I beg you, please don't say any more.'

Nero: 'Since this is the end of the talk, I'll just say half of it and stop. In the valley of Takateruyama, you did such-and-such to two lovely young women, and finally did such-and-such, and did such-and-such to the bottom of the ravine, and that ghost always appears in your dreams and visions trying to pull off this fellow's fresh head. ...Hey everyone, if you walk along with such a Shīna, a clumsy and unreliable article like this, who knows, he might take on a strange Shīna-like appearance and make a grand appearance with a "whoosh"! Ah, somehow my surroundings have started creeping me out. Shall we stop this story already?'

Isaku: 'Hey, Shīna, do you have any memory of such a thing? Hearing Nero's story, I'm starting to feel sick too. Come on, let's stop this kind of talk and get going!'

Nero: 'I can't move no matter what anymore, so let's just make Chīru into Nero right here, zzz, zzz, zzz.'

Kāru: 'Ha ha ha ha, he was talking nonsense and teasing Shīna, but he's already fallen asleep. What an innocent fellow. Come on, let's rest even more.'

And Kāru, once again flopping over with his elbow as a pillow, lay down by the side of the road. Isaku and Chīru also drifted off to sleep. Shīna was left all alone behind, feeling a creepy sensation at the nape of his neck, unable to even sleep, and clinging to Isaku's waist while trembling with fear.
