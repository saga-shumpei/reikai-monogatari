Chapter 13: Capital 入 [855]

都入〔855〕

§ 1

By the Kototama of the divine Ibuki, Suekohime subdued the Magakami of the Tatsumi Pond, and accompanied by Suteko-hime, she traveled through the scorching Enten, carrying her delicate feet. Escorted by Haru, Iku, and Taka, she crossed the Kusano, traversed rivers, climbed over mountains once more, and followed the valleys. After resting her head on many a travel pillow, she finally approached the Seichi of Uzu, the Kamuyakata of the Ananai-kyō where Momogamihiko resided.

Matsuwakahiko, riding on horseback and leading many Kunibito, had two Mikoshi carried. To welcome Suekohime, the precious Miko of Kamususanōnōokami, and Suteko-hime, he straightened his Igi, dyed the red Toeu crest on his Shirahata, and let it flutter in the wind. While forming a Chiyōda formation in a dignified manner, he appeared in the Karina village on the outskirts of the Uzu capital. The party of Matsuwakahiko met the party of Suekohime in the Karina village, and he immediately leaped from his horse and approached before Suekohime.

Matsuwakahiko: 'As the Kamutsukasa born as the Miko of the Kunihiko of the precious capital, I, Matsuwakahiko, am here now. Having been informed by a divine oracle of the arrival of you two, the Miko of the Mizu no Mitama, Suekohime-sama and Suteko-hime-sama, I have newly crafted these Mikoshi to welcome you here. Although you possess noble bodies, you have traveled well to this particularly hot land of Uzu, for the sake of the Kami, for the sake of the Way, and for the sake of the people of the world. The Kunibito have been counting the days, waiting impatiently for the descent of the Mizu no Mitama, and are filled with joy and courage. O noble Hime-sama of the precious land, and Suteko-sama who follows her, please rest here.'

As he spoke with great courtesy, Suekohime also bowed in acknowledgment.

Suekohime: 'Are you Matsuwakahiko of the renowned Uzu-yakata? You have kindly sent the three, Haru, Iku, and Taka, all the way to the foot of the Teruyama Pass to welcome our party. Thanks to you, we have arrived here safely without any trouble on our journey. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara. Through the noble guidance of the Kami, we have reached this place. When we left Mesopotamia of the endless white clouds to open the teachings, our sisterhood and our followers were oppressed by the priests of the Brahmanism, like a small boat without a mooring, cast away into the terrifying rough seas. I, fortunately, received the blessings of the Kami of Heaven and Earth, and together with Suteko-hime, arrived safely at the port of Hara, crossed the Teruyama Pass, and subdued the Great Serpent Kami lurking in the Tatsumi Pond. With heart and body emboldened, we have made the Uzu capital, where Matsuwakahiko appears, our destination, and have finally arrived here. As a Kamutsukasa of Matsuwakahiko, I, still a weak and inexperienced maiden, humbly ask for your favor. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara. Please listen fully and in detail to this first greeting I offer before the Kyōshi who serves the teachings of the Kami.'

As she spoke these words quietly, Matsuwakahiko bowed his waist, rubbed his hands together in joy, took the hand of Suekohime, and gripped it with all his might.

Matsuwakahiko: 'Ah, Hime-sama, Hime-sama. From this moment on, in the land of Uzu, with the descent of your life, the world shall be governed peacefully and tranquilly, an age where no gates are closed. Demons and Great Serpents shall be blown away by the wild winds, leaving no shadow behind, and the divine authority of the Kami shall shine ever more brilliantly. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara. Before you, Suekohime, the descendant of the Kami and the Miko of the precious Mitama, I, Matsuwakahiko, offer my sincere heart and serve you with gratitude.'

Having finished their greetings to each other, they entered the shade of a beautiful forest and decided to rest for a while.

Matsuwakahiko, speaking with polite words and bowing his waist toward Suekohime and Suteko-hime, said:

Matsuwaka: 'O renowned Mizu no Mitama, Suekohime-sama, who has appeared as the precious Miko of Kamususanōnōokami-sama, and Suteko-hime-sama who accompanies her, you have traveled so far to descend to this hot land. It goes without saying for me, but I wonder how happy the many Kunibito, including the Kamutsukasa who serve in the shrine of the Uzu capital, must be. Truly, I was so overjoyed at the descent of the Savior that I could hardly rest at night for the past two or three days. This Mikoshi was prepared in such a hurry that it is quite humble, but please, I beg you to board it and enter the castle.'

§ 2

She entreats thus. Suekohime shakes her head from side to side,

Suekohime: 'Although it is truly regrettable to make nothing of your kind intention, and while it is indeed a precious gift to receive feet from the august deities, how could I possibly ride upon something like a palanquin? Pray forgive me for this matter alone, despite your kindness.'

Suteko: 'Since the Hime-sama speaks thus, I beg you to make this utterly unnecessary. Furthermore, as I am a maidservant serving closely by the Hime-sama's side morning and evening as her attendant, even if the Hime-sama were to ride in it, such an awe-inspiring thing is utterly beyond me, so please graciously accept my refusal.'

Matsuwaka: 'That may well be, but since it is a palanquin created by the people of the land rejoicing at your august descent, gathering together without distinction of day and night, I earnestly beg you on behalf of all, yielding to the sincere devotion of the people, to deign to ride in it.'

Suekohime: 'Though it may seem stubborn, how could an inexperienced young woman like myself, devoid of divine virtue, ever be permitted to ride in such a splendid palanquin used by the deities?'

Matsuwaka: 'You have deigned to come forth as the governor of the land of Udu in accordance with the divine plan of the deities. To you, such matters may not occupy your thoughts, but when the deity Masakayamatsumi set forth for the battle of Yomotsu Hirasaka, he spoke thus unto my father Kunihiko... "Since the divine child of the Great Deity Kami-Susanoo will deign to descend to this land and deign to govern the entire land of Utsu, until that time, thou, Kunihiko, guard my lodge well; when the precious divine child descends, revere this and make it the governor of the land, and do thou become the god of the left guard and the right guard, and serve in the divine work."... Such was the divine instruction. My father has already removed to another province, but this Matsuwaka-hiko, who has inherited his place, forgetting not his father's words even in slumber, has guarded it with all his might unto this day, until the descent of the precious divine child. You are indeed invested with the sacred office of becoming the queen of this land, governing the people, and moreover becoming a religious teacher to teach and guide the countryfolk into the august and venerable divine path.'

Suekohime: 'Inadequate though I am, that mission I too have received from my father the Great Deity. I earnestly beg for your continued guidance.'

Matsuwaka: 'Those awe-inspiring words... taken upon the person of Matsuwaka-hiko, I truly deem it the supreme honor. Though I am a foolish and inadequate fellow, please guide me well and employ me long. I earnestly entreat you.'

Suekohime: 'Let us treat each other kindly.'

Matsuwaka: 'If you know well that you are to become the queen of this land by the command of your father the Great Deity, all the more reason that you must deign to ride in this palanquin. By no means is riding in the palanquin done for the sake of luxury, nor is it done to make the journey easy. Since this world has been created through the august grace of Heaven and Earth, leaving aside Heaven, upon Earth the divine spirits of the ancestral land-deities lodge everywhere, so even to tread and walk upon the great earth is a matter of great awe for us mortals. That being said, if the general populace were to be afraid of treading upon the great earth, it would stand to reason that they could neither walk the roads nor perform a single act of tillage; therefore, it is the august vocation of the Queen, who appears as the ruler of the land, to bow before Heaven and scrape the earth on behalf of all the multitudes, to revere the deities, and, acting as the representative of the people, to deign not to tread upon the soil. Therefore, I pray you understand this reason of the place; since here onwards is the castle town, at least for the sake of the castle town alone, we desire you to spare us the trouble and take our place in not treading upon the soil. Moreover, since Suteko-hime-sama also holds the office of close attendant and is to deign to accompany the Hime-sama, she most assuredly must also ride in this palanquin. This request I earnestly and solely entreat of you.'

Sueko: 'If it is thus heard, there is no reason to refuse. If so, as you command, I shall be cared for. ...Sutekohime-dono, I permit you, nay, I command you, get into that palanquin and follow behind me.'

Thus at length the words of Suekohime somehow began to take on authority. Sutekohime, having no reason to refuse, obediently answered 'Hai', was made to ride in another palanquin from behind the palanquin of Suekohime, sent off by the shouts of acclamation of numerous kunibito, and it came to pass that they entered the castle with great fanfare.

At the entrance to the Capital of Uzu, an extraordinarily splendid gate is built. Suekohime is carried in her palanquin through this front gate, and the procession bravely advances toward the main castle. The passageway is paved with white sand, and on the left and right of the road, numerous kunibito kneel upon the ground, shedding tears at the advent of the Savior, lost in the genuine emotion of profound joy. Escorted by the sounds of various kinds of music, before dusk, they safely arrived at the inner palace.

Karu was overtaken by a stone bear on the way, and here the two of them, taking each other by the hand, breathless and panting, advance into the castle along the road paved with white sand.

末子『さう承はらば、否むに由なきことで御座います。左様ならば仰せの通り、御世話になりませう。……捨子姫殿、妾が許します、否命令します、あの輿に乗つて妾が後に従ひ来られよ』

と漸く末子姫の言葉は何処となく権威を帯びて来た。捨子姫は否むに由なく、素直に『ハイ』と答へて、末子姫の輿の後より外の輿に乗せられ、数多の国人の歓呼の声に送られ、賑々しく入城することとなつた。

ウヅの都の入口には非常な立派な門が建てられてある。末子姫は此表門より輿に舁つがれ、行列勇ましく本城さして進み入る。通路は白砂を布き詰め、道の左右には数多の国人、地上に跪き、救世主の降臨と涙を流し、感喜の真情に暮れてゐる。いろいろの音楽の音に送られ黄昏前、奥殿に安着した。

カールは途中に石熊に追つ付かれ、茲に両人は手に手を取つて、白砂の布きつめたる道を、息もせきせき城内指して進み入る。
