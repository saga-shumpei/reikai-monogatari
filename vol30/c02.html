Chapter 2: 乾の滝 [844]

乾の滝〔844〕

§ 1

Shīna was somehow driven by a sense of kiyōfushin, and trembled and shook. Suekohime and Sutekohime, who had been listening privately to the conversation of the gonin from the saizen while nenaga, realized that these were the subordinates of Ishikuma of Brahmanism, persons who had come here to capture jibunra; removing their uhagi, the riyōnin secretly consulted with one another and transformed into biyakui, disheveling their hair wildly and letting it hang down limply, extending their white hands forward simultaneously in the ichido,

"How hateful, in the valley of Takateruyama..."

They began in a thin, mournful voice. Shīna glanced at this figure, shook Isaku awake, and

Shīna: 'Oi, oi... Isaku, wake up... Everyone, this is a taihen emergency, they have appeared, they have appeared, they have really come out!'

He was trembling with fear. Chīru was startled by this voice, and when he looked around, from among the thicket of trees appeared an eerie-looking ghost clad in biyakui, floating vaguely.

Chīru: 'Hey, this is taihen! Run, run!'

Striving to go first, the sannin—Isaku, Shīna, and Chīru—tumbling and rolling, scattered and fled to izuko for their very inochi. Kāru and Nero, the riyōnin, were not disturbed in the least, and with taizen composure, watched the two mysterious figures for a while.

Kāru: 'Shitsurei nagara... Are not you two ladies Her Ladyship Suekohime, the youngest daughter of the Lord God Kami-Susanoo-no-Ōkami, and your attendant Her Ladyship Sutekohime, who left Kenonjiyō, were captured for the sake of Katahiko, and drifted ashore here? To tell the truth, I am a propagandist of Anago-kyō serving Lord Matsuwakahiko of Chin-no-Miyako; the single man is named Nero, and he too is a believer in the Way of Anago-kyō. Lately, in Takateruyama, a faction of Brahmanism led by a man named Ishikuma has appeared, causing spies to infiltrate Chin-no-Miyako and plotting its overthrow; therefore, we have obeyed the secret command of our leader Matsuwakahiko, and in order to investigate the state of Brahmanism, we have become believers of Brahmanism and have lived until today. However, according to the words of the general Ishikuma, there is no doubt that you ladies will cross the ocean within today and tomorrow, land at the port of Hara, and proceed to the country of Chin. If the futari should board and enter Chin-no-Miyako, it would be as if a formidable enemy arose against Brahmanism, so he commanded us to keep watch at Teruyama Pass, seize both of you, and drag you back to Takateruyama. Rejoicing and emboldened that this is the very time to render our usual service to the gods, we joined the party and have come this far.'

Nero: 'I too, quite unexpectedly by the light of the moon, perceived the fact that you ladies' party was resting here; therefore, taking advantage of my knowing Shīna's past misdeeds, I thought to frighten him into fleeing, and spoke various things loudly so as to be heard by you, Anata. Prompted by your immediate wit, the three fellows have fled and disappeared just as you see. We now have peace of mind. Come, from here, together with Kāru-sama, though you may be fatigued, even if only slightly, please ascend this pass while there is still time. As for myself, just in case the three men who have just fled should turn back, which would be inconvenient, I shall henceforth pursue their tracks and carry out containment maneuvers so that the two of you may arrive safely at Chin-no-Miyako.'

Sueko: 'Ah, are you a person of Anago-kyō? How very kind and thank you. Even if ten or twenty men should come rushing upon us, for my part I have no particular anxiety, but rather than engage in unnecessary slaughter, if we can reach our destination safely, there is nothing quite so splendid as this for both ourselves and others.'

Suteko: 'I am Sutekohime, the handmaid of the Princess. As I am utterly inexperienced, I humbly beg for your kind guidance.'

Nero: 'If that is the case, we shall part ways for a while now, and meet again formally another day.'

Before he had even finished speaking, he departed from this place.

Karl: 'You two, it is quite all right now. Come, I shall guide you.'

Nijiyo: 'Yes, thank you.'

With that, the three quickened their footsteps and climbed up the Teru mountain pass, where the night dew was dripping, leaving their steps to chance.

They climbed up to the very center of the Teru mountain pass, which required eightri of climbing and eightri of descending. The night seemed to have finally broken, and the voices of small birds were heard in abundance. Although the sun had already risen from below the horizon and climbed somewhat higher, because the peak of Mount Teru stood in the east, it was impossible to behold its perfectly circular divine figure. Due to the swirling wind, the sound of the waterfall echoing with a booming roar caused Sutekohime to tilt her ear, and she spoke.

Sutekohime: 'Karl-sama, can you not hear a sound that seems terribly refreshing? Might there be a waterfall hanging somewhere close by?'

Karl: 'Yes, if you draw about two or three cho to the north, there is a great pond in the center of the mountain called the Inui Waterfall, and a grand waterfall dropping from that cascade hangs down like bleached cloth.'

Sutekohime: 'Having been blown by the sea breeze for a good long while and having sweated terribly as well, how would it be if we made a slight detour and went to see the waterfall? What do you say, Sutekohime-san?'

Suteko: 'Ah, that is a matter I could only wish for. Shall we ask Karl-sama to guide us?'

Karl: 'Well... that is something to think about for a moment. In the Inui Pond above that Inui Waterfall, a terrible, great serpent lurks. Moreover, the children of the great serpent constantly wander about the vicinity of the waterfall, possessing people or biting them, so the believers of the Sango faith call that place the Waterfall of the Demon God, and no one has ever approached it. It is a great rumor that from time to time the religious leader of Brahmanism, the missionary Ishikuma, goes secretly alone and without companions to visit the waterfall. Brahmanism had temporarily fallen into a desolation as if its fire had gone out, but the rumor that the religious leader Ishikuma occasionally comes to the Inui Waterfall for ascetic training has become a major attraction; many people are moved by his divine virtue, and lately they have recovered considerable influence, advancing to set up mission halls near the capital of Jin, introducing many spies into the capital, causing Matsuwakahime-sama of the Jin mansion to pass away, and attempting various plans to bring her into the sphere of influence of their own religion. Therefore, if the general Ishikuma should happen to be there bathing in the waterfall, trouble would arise instead; so how would it be if, for now, you did not make a detour and instead proceeded straight to the capital of Jin? That would likely be the safest course of all.'

Sutekohime: 'That may well be so, but ever since that sound of the waterfall entered my ears, somehow I cannot help but want to see the waterfall just once. Even if it is just to look for a brief moment, shall we not stop by?'

Suteko: 'Karl-sama, no matter what anyone says, since she is Sutekohime-sama, the end of the bloodline of the deity Kami Susanoo-no-Mikoto who possesses peerless divine power, you surely have no cause for worry whatsoever. Will you not please guide us?'

Karl: 'If you insist so strongly, this too must be Kannagara... Very well, I shall guide you.'

Taking the lead, he advanced obliquely northwards and northwards through a forest overgrown with giant trees. When they looked, a grand waterfall like the Milky Way set vertically hung straight down the center of giant rocks whose height of tens of jo was unknown, and white foam floated thickly in the bluish waterfall basin, bringing a truly deep coolness that made the summer heat vanish nowhere, suddenly tightening their bodies and filling them with a refreshing sensation. When they looked closely, right beside the waterfall stood only a single big man with both hands clasped together, his face turned pale blue, his lips changed to purple, his eyes darting restlessly, maintaining a posture of upright immobility. Karl, quick-eyed as he was, saw this and was startled with a mere 'Ah!', but suppressing his throbbing chest himself, he brought his mouth close to Sutekohime's ear.
