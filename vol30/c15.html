§ 1

Depending on the understanding of the sendenshi of Kotoyoriwake and Kuniyoriwake, the residents of this entire region were about to be almost entirely annihilated; however, ever since they were permitted to eat mikurauo, their genki was suddenly recovered. Moreover, they came to respect Kotoyoriwake and the sendenshi of Kuniyoriwake as if they were Kami, and straightway proclaimed that among the teachings of kiuseisaimin, none could equal Ananai-kyō. Hundreds of thousands of people withdrew from the Ural teaching and all entered Ananai-kyō. However, since Kotoyoriwake had already left this land, Kuniyoriwake alone remained behind. Centered around the shrine of Mikura, the kyōsei suddenly flourished, to the extent that everyone, high and low, came to worship the belief that unless one belonged to Ananai-kyō, it was difficult to be saved, and one could not even be considered a proper ningen.

Kuniyoriwake patiently and persuasively taught the kyōri of Ananai-kyō, taught the Amatsunorito and kami words, and enshrined Kunichinotachinomikoto, Toyokunihikemikoto, and other various kami spirits at the shrine of Mikura, establishing them as the object of reverence and worship. Whether traversing the mountain peaks or plunging into the river, in such hours let the murakimo heart sing in peace! Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, may the divine spirits bring happiness! The god Kamunahohi who created this world, and the great Ōnahohi whose heart is vast. Truly, whatever happens in the world of men, behold it straightly with nahohi, listen to it straightly! If there are errors, correct the proclamation. Never, ever forget the Ananai-kyō! Leave it behind for the sake of the people of the world forever!'

Having composed this poem, [he] bestowed the name Taruhiko upon a man named Pāksu, the cleverest among the most zealous believers, added him to the ranks of sendenshi, and commanded him to protect the shrine of Mikura while preaching and instructing the teachings of Ananai-kyō to the kunibito. Kuniyoriwake once again set forth from this place, advancing toward the capital of the land of Hiru.

Just as they approached the storm-swept forest of the village of Chiru, four or five men appeared, blending into the gloom of twilight, spreading their arms wide before Kuniyoriwake,

Kō: 'You are a sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, who has dared to bring shame upon us! Since it is thus, even without stating your name, you should know that our names are already clear; well then, what say you? However skilled in eloquence you may be, you cannot match practical might. Will you peaceably turn your hand, or rather cast yourself into the ravine of Chiru under our surveillance and perish by your own hand? If even that is unacceptable, we all, much to our regret, shall make you the rust of our swords. However valiant you may think you are, if hundreds of allies surround you ten or twenty layers deep, surely, surely, there will be no room for you to escape your fate. It is needless to say that we are the sendenshi of the Uraru-kyō: the individuals Būru, Yūzu, and Anan! You are the hateful instigators of the violation of heavenly law who ate the sacred fish forbidden by the gods in the valley of Mikura, and moreover caused the kunibito to eat it without exception! You are the villains who turned centuries of effort by the Uraru-kyō into bubbles in a single morning; prepare yourselves now!'

At this shouting voice, dozens of human figures naturally gathered, coming with a noisy roar of 'Wāi-wāi!' Kuniyoriwake opened his mouth wide and broke into a loud laugh,

Kuniyoriwake: 'Ah-ha-ha-ha! Such cowardly and base behavior of yours! In the valleys of Mount Mikura, like mice chased by cats, you could not even raise a squeak, but slinked away in cowardice. With such a wretched state, how could you ever hope to edify the people who are the children of the gods? Unthinkable! You cowardly lot, relying on numbers to torment a single sendenshi, if you are truly going to be opponents, come and try it. By the divine power of my Kototama, I shall guide and harmonize every single one of you into the path of truth, and reform the Uraru-kyō you revere from its very roots. Ah, how amusing, how amusing!'

And once again he laughed heartily, cackling away. The leaders Būru, Yūzu, and Anan gave some signal to the dozens of allies who had gathered, and how ridiculous it was as they all rushed wildly toward Kuniyoriwake at once. Kuniyoriwake gathered his breath with a single grunt of 'Un!', and with the index finger of his right hand swung rapidly from left to right toward them like a rapid-fire gun; the divine light of Kuniyoriwake, who embodied the divine power of the orb, shone even more intensely, and some were blinded and fled in a sorry state, while others fell to the ground and writhed in agony, scattering away in utter confusion, just like petals scattered by a storm, utterly ridiculous to behold. Seeing the figure of this wretched enemy, Kuniyoriwake once again cried out in a loud voice,

Kuniyoriwake: 'Ah-ha-ha-ha, amusing, amusing!'
