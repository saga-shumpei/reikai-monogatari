Appendix: Travelogue Poems of Iwai Hot Springs

附録　岩井温泉紀行歌
Zuigetsu, Deguchi Onisaburo（瑞月　出口王仁三郎）
Translator's Introduction & Literary Note on the Orikomi-uta Tradition:
In January 1922 (Taisho 11), amidst heavy winter snows, Master Deguchi Onisaburo departed the Golden Pavilion of Ayabe and traveled via the San'in railway line unto Iwai Hot Springs in Inaba Province (modern-day Iwami, Tottori Prefecture). Staying at the venerable Komaya Inn, Onisaburo recuperated in the healing thermal waters while continuously dictating the profound chapters of Reikai Monogatari Volume 3 alongside his dedicated scribes Toyama Toyoji, Kato Akiko, and Inoue Tomegoro.

Similar to the celebrated Appendix of Volume 2 (Takakumayama Sanpai Kikōka), Onisaburo composed this extraordinary travelogue epic in classical 7-5 meter utilizing the ancient poetic technique of orikomi-uta (折込歌・詠み込み歌, acrostic name-incorporation poetry). Embedded directly into the flowing natural narrative—which vividly describes the winter locomotive journey, snow-covered valleys, the famous rhythmic folk tradition of "Yukaburi" ladle bathing, and the ancient Izumo mythos—are the personal names of 60 recording scribes, companions, and visiting devotees, notably including Aikido founder Morihei Ueshiba (植芝盛隆).

In the English translation below, the entire travelogue and acrostic poetry are rendered in rhythmic blank verse, with each devotee's woven name identified in brackets for bilingual study and historical appreciation.
Section 1 • The Snowy Journey from Ayabe to Iwai Hot Springs

Bound by deep destiny unto the Holy Spirit of Mizu (Mizu-no-Mitama), in the snowy silver world of January in the Year of the Dog (1922), Master Onisaburo departed the Golden Pavilion of Ayabe at the hour of the Snake (ten o'clock in the morning) on the eighth day, journeying unto Iwai Hot Springs in Inaba to cleanse the spiritual dust of body and soul. Seeing him off with heartfelt prayers were faithful devotees Yuasa, Shinohara, Ueshiba [Morihei Ueshiba], Takeshita of Oomoto, Fukushima, and Kondo. Shaken in a swift automobile through ancient townhouses along Shinmachi, the party reached Ayabe Station. With a resounding whistle the locomotive steamed forth, carrying scribes Toyama Toyoji, Kato Akiko, and Nishimura Tokuji with high spirits past Isa Station and Hase Bridge. Whirling through wind-swept Fukuchiyama and past Onigajō, plunging through blizzard snows past Kamikawaguchi, Shimoyakuno, and Kamiyakuno, crossing the roaring bridge at Yanase into Wadayama, Yabu, Yōka, and Ebara, they reached Toyooka amidst crowds of bustling passengers. Beholding the basalt pillars of Genbudō and Kinosaki's bathhouses on the right, steaming past Takeno and Satsu, they gazed upon the winter surges of the Sea of Japan under heavy snow clouds at Kasumi Station. Viewing terraced rice fields clad in snow at Yoroi and deep ravines choked with white drifts—the unmistakable crest of the cold San'in winter—they bought tea, sweet confections, and boxed meals to cheer their journey, resting in the corner of the carriage past Igumi until descending at Iwami Station at the hour of the Monkey (four in the afternoon). Beneath snowy skies, the company of six crowded into a dilapidated automobile with torn canvas hood, rattling along rural lanes like a sputtering jalopy, until arriving at the Komaya Inn of Iwai Hot Springs. After a hearty evening meal, they joined in the rhythmic chanting of the famous "Yukaburi" hot water ladle bath, singing "Yare, yare, yare" in merry unison. Watching the snowy garden through the long night, dawn broke on January 9 amidst a raging blizzard; huddled over a charcoal brazier (kotatsu) against the piercing cold, Master Zuigetsu stood dictating the wondrous chapters of the Age of the Gods, as scribes Toyama, Kato, and Inoue recorded every word with swift brushes.

壬戌（みずのえいぬ・大正十一年）一月、一面の白雪が降り積もり銀世界と化した朝、瑞の御魂・王仁三郎は、黄金閣（綾部の大本本苑）をあとにし、八日午前の巳の刻（午前十時頃）、心身の垢を洗い清めんとして、因幡の名湯・岩井温泉（鳥取県岩美町）を目指して出発した。

見送りに駆けつけた湯浅、篠原、植芝（合気道開祖・植芝盛隆）、大本の竹下氏、福島、近藤らの信仰厚き信徒たちに送られ、古い家並みの続く新町を快速の自動車に揺られて綾部駅へと到着した。

汽笛一声、汽車は雪煙を上げて走り出し、同乗する口述筆記者の外山豊二氏、加藤明子女史、西村徳治氏らを伴い、勇んで石原駅、初瀬橋を越え、科戸の風吹きすさぶ福知山、鬼ケ城を通過し、上川口、下夜久野、上夜久野の深い雪を突破して進む。梁瀬、和田山、養父、八鹿、江原を過ぎ、但馬の中心・豊岡では多くの乗降客を迎える。玄武洞、城崎温泉を車窓右手に眺め、竹野、佐津、そして荒海と雪雲の広がる日本海と香住駅を過ぎ、鎧の棚田や深い谷を埋める白雪に山陰の冬の気配を実感する。

車中では駅弁やお茶、煙草を買い求め空腹を満たし、居組を経て申の刻（午後四時頃）、ついに岩美駅へと降り立った。雪より白いお梅さん、雲の上の雪空のもと、幌の破れたオンボロ自動車に一行六人が乗り込み、ガタガタと揺られながら屁のような音を立てて田舎道を走り、古湯・岩井温泉の「駒屋旅館」へと到着した。

湯治の宿で一同夕餉を済ませ、満腹となって名物の「湯冠り（頭に手ぬぐいを乗せ柄杓で湯をかぶりながら歌う湯治法）」の「ヤレヤレヤレ」の拍子歌に合わせて賑やかに湯を浴び、雪の庭を眺めながら長夜を明かした。

翌一月九日の未明、激しい吹雪が吹き荒れる中、寒さに凍える瑞月（王仁三郎）は炬燵の中で暖を取りながら、横に立つ筆記者・外山、加藤、井上の三氏に神代の霊界物語の奇しき物語を口述筆記させたのである。

Section 2 • Visitor Acrostic Poem: Scribes and San'in Devotees (Names 1–31)

Manifested as the guardian deity of healing hot springs, the great spirit of Izumo, Ōnamuchi-no-Kami: Master Deguchi Onisaburo [Deguchi Onisaburo]. Though the hot spring source of Iwai is slender, it is hailed as the sovereign medicine, imbued with God's threefold soul of benevolence; parting the roots of illness, its fame is carried by Nishimura Tokuji [Nishimura Tokuji], seeking purification from the western village. Attending as recording scribe, Kato Akiko [Kato Akiko] arrives alongside devout ladies. At the Komaya Inn, amidst mountains of honored guests, Toyama Toyoji [Toyama Toyoji] plies his diligent brush. Basking in divine virtue like pure well-water upon the heights, Inoue Tomegoro [Inoue Tomegoro] reclines on the third floor, recording celestial tales of the spiritual realm. Holding faith firmer than stone, walking the fragrant path of Tamba, stands Ishiwata Kaoru [Ishiwata Kaoru]. Drawing from the deep well of sincere truth, firmer than eternal bedrock, descended from Heaven as a noble man of virtue: Iwabuchi Hisao [Iwabuchi Hisao]. Pledging to deliver mortals wandering upon the ridges of Sagane: Sagane Tamizo [Sagane Tamizo]. Born into the vast fields with profound destiny: Ono Tadajiro [Ono Tadajiro]. Seeking the sacred village through open fields, entreating the Almighty: Tamura Kentaro [Tamura Kentaro]. In the sacred garden where white plums bloom in profusion, keeping the storehouse pure: Sasaki Seizo [Sasaki Seizo]. Bearing an iron soul purer than white paper within Oomoto: Kamimoto Tetsuzo [Kamimoto Tetsuzo]. Supporting the Second Spiritual Leader upon Mount Hongū, receiving the blessings of the golden pillar deity: Kameyama Kintaro [Kameyama Kintaro]. Gazing upon landscapes incomparable even to Mount Fuji, blessed with venerable years: Fujita Taketoshi [Fujita Taketoshi]. Recalling ancient days when waves were stilled in peace: Furuta Tokiji [Furuta Tokiji]. Crossing by boat to Miho Bay to revere Ebisu as San'in's foremost holy ground: Funakoshi Eiichi [Funakoshi Eiichi]. Advancing across wave-tossed sea roads past Takashima: Takashima Yuka [Takashima Yuka]. Welcoming the divine spirit unto Ayabe, revering the holy words of Ushitora-no-Konjin: Endo Torakichi [Endo Torakichi]. Treading the grassy paths amidst rice plantings in full spiritual flower: Aikido founder Ueshiba Moritaka [Ueshiba Moritaka]. Bearing a walking staff beneath pine and plum, braving the tangled brushwood: Takeshita Shigeru [Takeshita Shigeru]. Admiring the dawn colors of eastern skies, attaining venerable wisdom: Higashi Ryoshun [Higashi Ryoshun]. Parting the paths of Kuwabara to joyously revere the Grand Shrine of Izumo: Kuwabara Dozen [Kuwabara Dozen]. Reflecting on the sacred governance over blessed pines and fields: Matsuda Masaji [Matsuda Masaji]. Before the evergreen shrine entwined with wisteria, heart open and serene: Fujimatsu Ryokan [Fujimatsu Ryokan]. Beholding Mount Misen beyond the divine groves: Kobayashi Hideo [Kobayashi Hideo]. Passing beneath the First Torii preserving divine dignity: Kobayashi Yasuichi [Kobayashi Yasuichi]. Revering the sanctuary of Suga where the sword of the clustered clouds shines in serene splendor: Inamura Sumi [Inamura Sumi]. Smiting the monstrous serpent of Yamada along the Hii River beneath dragon-shaped clouds: Yokoyama Tatsujiro [Yokoyama Tatsujiro]. Passing under towering trees where clear springs murmur: Kinoshita Senzo [Kinoshita Senzo]. In the fragrant vales of eightfold clouds where husband and wife dwell in sacred peace: Okumura Yoshio [Okumura Yoshio]. Whose eightfold glory flourisheth through long years: Kondo Shigetoshi [Kondo Shigetoshi].

Section 4 • Author's Postscript and Historical Context (附言)

At seven-forty on the morning following the grand inaugural assembly of the Showa Shinseikai branch held at the Public Hall of Miyazaki City in Hyūga Province—the sacred cradle of the Imperial Way—I set down this postscript from the second floor of the Kandabashi Inn, gazing upon the celebrated bridges and crystal currents of the Ōyodo River. Thus the proofreading of Volume 3 of Reikai Monogatari (Spirit Dominates Body: Tiger Volume) is brought to a close. (Early morning, January 19, 1935) [Appended Note] On May 15 (lunar calendar), 1901 (Meiji 34), Foundress Nao Deguchi received a divine command to undertake a sacred pilgrimage to the Grand Shrine of Izumo (Amenohisumi-no-Miya). Walking along the San'in Highway, her party of fifteen stayed overnight at the Komaya Inn of Iwai Hot Springs, and lodged there once again on their return journey; hence this hot spring has a profound spiritual connection with Oomoto. Master Zuigetsu had previously lodged here in the spring of 1914 (Taisho 3) together with Third Spiritual Leader Naohi and Mr. Nobuyuki Umeda. This present visit marked his third sojourn in these healing waters. While recuperating and simultaneously carrying out the daily dictation of Reikai Monogatari, overwhelmed with joy and gratitude for God's boundless grace, and wishing to honor the devoted recording scribes and the faithful believers who traveled from afar to assist in diverse ways, he composed this long chōka incorporating their personal names into the verse, appending it forever to the conclusion of Volume 3.

現代日本語訳
