Chapter 10: Indiscretion [110]

§ 1

By order of the Archangel Ōyashimahiko no Mikoto, Sugahiko set out toward Mount Kijōzan as the second messenger, taking no companions, mounted alone upon a dragon-horse with the loud sound of hoofbeats. Sugahiko was a handsome man of fair countenance and clean, refined features, exceedingly fair-skinned and smooth of flesh, so that one might almost suspect him to be the reincarnation of a heavenly maiden.

Without the slightest sign of hesitation, Sugahiko whipped his dragon-horse through the castle gates of Mount Kijōzan and penetrated deep within; abandoning his horse at the entrance, he advanced into the inner palace and spoke in a loud voice:

"I have come forth as the direct envoy of Kuniharitoko no Mikoto, who governs the Takamanohara of the Earth, and of the Archangel Ōyashimahiko no Mikoto; I have particulars to proclaim. Is Miyamahiko not here? Where is Kuniteruhime? Come forth before my eyes at once, and receive the command of the direct envoy!"

Thus he cried out. The power of his kotodama truly roared like thunder, possessing a gentle grace even amid its somehow terrifying force. Overwhelmed by Sugahiko's kotodama, Miyamahiko was seized with a degree of fear, showing signs of hesitation and indecision.

At this moment, Kuniteruhime ran out from a room; utterly disheartened by Sugahiko's graceful appearance and his bearing that somehow brimmed with authority, she merely bowed her head and looked on in silence without a word, but another woman quietly entered. No sooner had Sugahiko caught sight of her than he glared sharply at her, and—

"Traitorous and disloyal Kuchikohime, it is filthy even to look upon you; depart from this place as quickly as possible."

Glared at in this manner, Kuchikohime looked up resentfully at Sugahiko's face, and while wiping with her sleeve the tears that dripped, she cast her eyes around, pressed her breast, and seemed to hint that a secret was contained within, though she uttered nothing.

Among the daughters of Miyamahiko, there was a peerlessly beautiful young woman named Kozakurahime. This Kozakurahime had been peeking from behind the door at Sugahiko's handsome countenance from earlier on, causing her heart to beat violently, but at last, unable to bear it, she pushed open the door while blushing, quietly and bashfully placed both hands on the floor before where Sugahiko stood, and was so tenderly graceful as she stammered even in her polite greeting.

The instant Kozakurahime resolutely raised her face, her gaze met with that of Sugahiko. Neither yielding to flowers nor autumn leaves, two beauties competing in charm and grace, the eyes of both appeared to tell something. At this time, Miyamahiko, Kuniteruhime, and Kuchikohime appeared on the spot, brought out delicacies of mountain and sea, and politely entertained Sugahiko, whereupon the five divine officials opened their hearts to one another and exchanged conversation.

Sugahiko calmly conveyed the purport of the envoy and prompted Miyamahiko for a reply, whereupon Miyamahiko said:

"I have certainly received and understood the intent of the messenger. However, until I gather the various shrine officials in the castle and conclude our consultation, would you not grant me a respite of a few days?"

Thus turning her face slightly to the left, she looked up at Sugahiko's face to see what his answer might be. Sugahiko granted that request, remained in the castle for several days, and waited for a reply. Kuniteruhime ordered Kozakurahime to attend upon Sugahiko's person and facilitate his affairs.

Seemingly far yet near, and appearing difficult yet easy, such is said to be the way of man and woman. Hereupon, Sugahiko and Kozakurahime broke through the barrier of public scrutiny and ended up bound in an inseparable relationship. Kuniteruhime and Kuchikohime, having perceived this situation, rejoiced greatly, and thinking to keep Sugahiko and make him their son-in-law, they gave attentive care in various ways.

From then on, Sugahiko and Kozakurahime took on the airs of husband and wife with the tacit approval of her parents, and it was lamentable that he came to forget the vital mission of the Okami. Sugahiko had his soul stolen away by Kozakurahime, indulged in wanton drinking day and night with the princess as his companion, and when a cherry-blossom viewing banquet was held together with the numerous shrine officials in the castle, the various shrine officials became dead drunk, and beneath the cherry trees blooming and fragrant at their peak in the front garden, some sang while others danced, dancing madly to the beat of bells and drums, and taking Sugahiko's hand, saying, "You too must sing, you too must dance," they took both his hands and invited him beneath a great cherry tree, and while being showered by a blizzard of blossoms scattering in the spring breeze, Sugahiko, appearing cheerful, borrowed the power of liquor and began to sing. If the outline of its meaning is presented here in the imayo style, it was a song containing the following meaning:

"A complexion like a flower, brows like the moon, A brow like Mount Fuji, skin like snow, The reincarnation of a heavenly maiden? Is she Ono no Komachi, or Terute-hime? Is she Nelson Batee, or Manryu? Is she Madame Xinxin, or Yang Guifei? Is she Princess Baosi? Unable to judge good or ill, Tomitaya Yachiyo would flee barefoot; Her age sixteen, or not yet eighteen, The smile of Kozakurahime Would topple even the castles of the realm. Even Enma of the Nether Court, Feared by fierce deities, would drool. Her graceful form is like a crabapple flower Moistened by the rain. Could such a beauty exist once more In this world, in the three thousand realms? Round as the dawn moon, Her height neither tall nor short, Is she like red plum blossoms opening all at once, Bursting into bloom in the dew? The light of teeth shown from her mouth, Is she the rebirth of Komyohime or Sotoorihime, The lovely Mikoto? Is that refreshing voice a bell cricket? Or is it a bush warbler of the third month? Rather than encountering the divine age of pines, Together with Kozakurahime, Deep in the mountains of Yoshino, With bamboo pillars and a thatched roof, Even were it the dwelling of tigers and wolves, Why should I shun it, weeping cherry? Even if scattered by the midnight storm, The love-garments of the two will not scatter. In love there is no separation of high and low; Having no separation is the way of love, Having no separation is the way of love. The heart is Sugahiko, clear and fresh, And the sugagaki strumming of the Yakumo koto: Shatchin, shatchin, shatchinchin, Shatchin, shatchin, shatchinchin."

Thus, playing the two-stringed koto, heedless of his surroundings, he danced madly as though bursting a dike.

In this manner, Sugahiko finally became a captive of love, did not report back to the earthly Takamanohara, and passing himself off as an adopted son-in-law of the enemy castle, became a disloyal subject. The delightful pledge of mandarin ducks lasted but a brief moment; from an unknown quarter a white-feathered arrow came flying, and piercing Sugahiko's chest, he pitifully met an agonizing death. Ah, truly, what ought to be restrained is the way of man and woman.

と顔をやや左方に傾け、須賀彦の返答はいかにとその顔を見上げた。須賀彦はその請求を許し、数日城内に滞在して返事を待っていた。国照姫は小桜姫に命じ、須賀彦の身辺に侍らせ、用務の便宜を図らせた。
