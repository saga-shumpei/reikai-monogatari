§ 1

The Seireikai is the intermediate boundary between Tenkai and Jigoku where Zenrei and Akurei gather, and it has already been stated that this is called the Yachimata of Ame. Now, this Yachimata is like the crossroads of the Rokudau spoken of by the Butsukyōshiya. Furthermore, when people come to the barrier that is the Chiushin of this Yachimata after death, they follow various paths. Each Seirei gathers at this barrier, using it as the Chiukan, from the eight directions of Tauzainanboku and Inuihitsujisarutatsumiushitora. Those coming from the east are for the most part the better portion among the Seirei, and the Shōzu river flows there. They must pass through this barrier no matter what. Those coming from the west are those whose souls have become somewhat clouded, and they are those who cross the so-called mountain of swords, which looks as if needles have been set upright. Crossing this place, there is only a narrow path that barely allows one to place one's feet, with only footprints left here and there; if one is careless for even a moment, one will immediately tear one's feet, and should one stumble and fall, one will be pierced by needles and suffer over one's entire body. Those coming from the north cross over a bridge of cold ice. If one is slightly careless, one will fall into a deep, muddy stream of unknown depth, and beneath that bridge, an indescribable, loathsome monster waits with a mouth like a crocodile, waiting to swallow those who fall. Moreover, a wind blows that cuts to the bone, and one is filled with such painful thoughts that one's hands and feet freeze, and one can barely distinguish between life and death. The Seirei coming from the south must pass through flames and smoke, where fire is burning over the entire mountain. If one is slightly careless here as well, one will be enveloped in smoke, one's clothing will be burned, and one must suffer from severe burns. However, if one is sufficiently careful, one can avoid the hardship of burns and reach the Chiushin of the Yachimata. Those coming from the northeast cross the Kanpyōdō, where the snow is so cold that it buries the body; while being blown by the gale, they stumble and fall, becoming desperate, and after crossing a long road of several dozen ri, they finally reach the Chiushin of the Yachimata. The Seirei coming from the southeast walks all alone, trudging through a desolate, withered field. And through muddy rice fields, shikusiku plains, and places where strange insects dwell, they barely make their way toward the Chiushin. The Seirei coming from the southwest jumps here and there over rugged mountain slopes and rocks, and while being attacked from time to time by various monsters, injuring their hands and feet, and jumping and tumbling, they finally emerge at the Chiushin of the Yachimata. The Seirei coming from the northwest goes barefoot, and while hurting their feet on a road of sharp pebbles, they finally reach the Yachimata with their lives barely intact. However, the Seirei who gather here from each direction after undergoing such suffering are all Seirei of the dark fukushugoshin who are destined to go to Jigoku. And the reason why each direction differs and the degree of pain differs depends on the degree of evil and falsehood of that Seirei. On the other hand, the Zenrei, that is, the Seirei of the seishugoshin, do not suffer much, no matter which direction they come from, as if they were traveling comfortably through the fields in spring or autumn. This is because, through the virtue of love and goodness and the virtue of true faith cultivated during their lifetime, they are able to traverse the Seireikai with ease. When the Seirei of good heads toward the Yachimata, it is almost as if they are walking through the fields of the present world with beautiful scenery, or crossing beautiful rivers, following the seashore, or crossing mountains where beautiful flowers bloom, or easily crossing large rivers by boat, or climbing mountain paths with beautiful scenery, and they finally arrive at the Yachimata. This Yachimata highway, which the seishugoshin pass through, is almost similar to the state of the lowest level of heaven. And the barrier of the Yachimata is a place where all things, both seishugoshin and fukushugoshin, gather; here, good and evil, truth and falsehood are examined, and they are made to undergo training, and those who have no prospect of improving their evil are, after a certain period of time, cast into the Jigoku-kai, while the Zenrei can ascend to each level of heaven according to the degree of their virtue.

Katahiko: 'Hey, what is this strange place? What is the meaning of binding me and bringing me to such a place?'

Gariya: 'Excuse me, General-sama, there is a strange thing. General Katahiko, whom we threw into the Tanisoko from the Monomiyagura and killed, is lying inside that gate. I wonder when General Katahiko escaped to such a place.'
