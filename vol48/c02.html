武乱泥〔1256〕

§ 1

In the staff officer room at the encampment of the Hall of Ukiki, the familiar two, Āku and Tāaru, sat with a hibachi between them, indulging in idle talk over cups of tea. Tāaru, incessantly pouring tea from the dobin, spoke:

Tāaru: 'Hey, Āku, having worked the Ama-no-nuhoko so hard since saizen, my throat has gone parched, and the foam at the corners of my mouth has acquired an extreme nenchiyakusei, hasn't it? Come, have a cup of tea. Tea dissipates gloom, nourishes the shinki, and moreover causes the shinkon to be at ease.'

Āku: 'Tea possesses color. Color is precisely that which readily fades... "The color of the flowers has faded in vain, while in long rains my life passed through this world"... so Naisu of the mirai is said to have uttered. I dislike tea; rather than that, I prefer clear water like suishiyō, devoid of the slightest color. Its tōtetsuburi truly represents the tankuwai and daukau of a kunshi who is self-sufficient and seeks nothing from others. As long as even a single cup of water is imbued with the grace of the Kami, even the fine wine of port wine cannot ultimately equal the calm, endless flavor of the way that remains unexhausted even when scooped up. It is said that the benevolent man delights in mountains, and the wise man delights in water; for us, the water of suishiyō is spirit-appropriate. And this Āku-sama, who delights in both mountain and water alike, is the so-called model of the wise and benevolent man.'

Tāaru: 'A daylight bandit seeking to steal the name of a holy kunshi endowed with both wisdom and benevolence, a twisted fellow seeking to cover and possess the world with a single word... "Move not from there!"... so I ought to draw a sword and cleave you down, yet I shall forget it in celebration of having ascended to an exalted position today, entering the ranks of General Ranchi's staff.'

Āku: 'Ahaha! What a blockhead! The spirit of a person who loves tea is after all a complete mess. A cheeky boyish character. Entering the ranks of General Ranchi's staff with such a mindset is utterly absurd—no, it is useless.'

Tāaru: 'Even without purposely entering the mountains to delight in mountains, or approaching the water to delight in water, we can objectively view all of human life and face it coolly; hence, amidst the ten-thousand-fathom red dust, within the domain of heavy bonds of affection, one can still delight in the quietude of the mountains and the flavor of the way found in clear water.'

Āku: 'You have become quite adept at fine-spun logic, haven't you?'

Tāaru: 'It is a settled matter. It is due to the instruction of Chikokuwake-sama; after all, there is a difference as great as heaven and earth compared to the teachings of General Ranchi, the brave warrior endowed with both madness and dementia hitherto.'

Āku: 'Hey, if you speak in such a loud voice, it will enter his ears; won't you exercise a little discretion?'

Tāaru: 'Nonsense! No matter how loud a voice I use, what risk is there that the holy words of a realized one who has received the inner flow of divinity will pass into his ears? The words that pass into General Ranchi's ears are merely falsehood, intrigue, evil desires, and female charms. Such hellish words can be heard immediately even if whispered in the softest voice. In short, his inner part is blocked and only his outer part is open. There is no reason why our holy words should be heard by the leader of the Brahman army, who is filled with worldly sins; rest assured first of all. Rather than that, look at that Imoriwake; he is truly making a fool of us, isn't he? No matter how much it is said that social intercourse in the world lacks depth unless gold is abundant, is it not truly astounding? Today's social intercourse is copper-smelling rather than watery. Merely having donated five thousand ryō of military funds, he was conveyed in a palanquin by that fellow Ekisu, accompanied a rotten-like woman to grandly request an interview with General Ranchi, and the General also broke the composure of his facial features, exchanging a firm handshake—whether there was an embracing kiss or not—did he not? I have truly come to detest it completely.'

Āku: 'That is truly so, isn't it? "A world where gold is omnipotent" is a well-said phrase. However, Chikokuwake-sama's face cannot be seen at all, can it? What could have happened to him?'

How absurd it would be—how could one throw away one's precious life for the sake of a spiritual enemy?'

tāru: 'Indeed, I agree. But Tāru, when a crisis comes, one cannot die for the sake of parents, or a master, or a Shishiyō. āku: 'Ohohoho, what nonsense are you spouting? You are making a fool of people. Won't you become a little serious? Eh!'

tāru: 'In a world where such monsters run rampant, how could one remain serious and steadfast? For a serious, honest, and truly righteous human being is instead regarded as an evil person in the present age. It is a world where everyone utters sweet slogans about being for the sake of the realm, for the sake of society, or for the sake of others, desiring only to attain their own selfish desires. We hate such counterfeits. We love unclothed words and deeds, like a pure and immaculate child.'

At this moment, a soldier appeared,

Soldier: 'Excuse me, by the order of Shiyōgun Ranchi-sama just now, rare guests have arrived, and he desires you to come and entertain them. Please proceed to the drawing room at once.'

Āku: 'All right, tell him we will come immediately. But these rare guests, from where did they come?'

Soldier: 'Yes, I do not know where they are from, but two people, looking just like remarkably beautiful goddesses, walked straight into the inner quarters. It is likely about them.'

Āku: 'Hmm, all right, tell him we shall come right away.'

Soldier: 'Yes.'

Answering thus, the soldier left the place. Left behind, the two looked at each other's faces,

Āku: 'Hey, Tāru, what do you think? Hasn't this encampment become somewhat strange? Just when we thought Imoriwake was bringing Otami along, two beauties have arrived; doesn't it make it all the more incomprehensible?'

Tāru: 'Hmm, indeed, they are probably monsters. That two such beauties should boldly and fearlessly invade such a desolate camp is utterly incomprehensible. However, it is an order from the Shiyōgun; we cannot disobey it. What if we go?'

Āku: 'Of course I intend to go, but unless we consider the general situation beforehand, we might commit an irreversible blunder.'
