§ 1

Harukuniwake and Tamayoriwake left the highest Reigoku behind, traversed the Chiukanreigoku on their return journey, and descended to the lowest level of Tengoku. On their way up, because their Shiyōkaku was not as it is now, both of them felt it to be exceedingly dazzling; however, having received Shintoku at the Wakamiya of the Sun, the two felt no pain whatsoever in their journey through the Daisantengoku. Nevertheless, as they descended through the Daiichi, Daini, and Daisan levels, it was truly strange that they felt as if their own Shinriki were diminishing, and that their clear Saunen was also becoming severely inferior.

At last, the two arrived at the Yachimata barrier. Ibukido-no-Kami, leading a multitude of guards, welcomed the two. The two were guided to the inner chamber of the mansion, received the hospitality of tea and sweets, and exchanged various conversations regarding the spiritual world.

Ibuki: 'Harukuniwake-sama, congratulations on successfully reaching the deepest Tengoku and the Reigoku. You must have many interesting stories to tell.'

Harukuniwake: 'Because my virtue is insufficient, the burden was too heavy in every Tengoku, and I was extremely humbled. However, through the guidance of various angels, I was barely led to the deepest Tengoku, toured a part of its organization, and have finally returned to this place. Yet, the bitterness of ascending from the lower Tengoku to the deepest Tengoku in order is indescribable. It was just like making a three-year-old child carry a heavy golden rod; it was so magnificent that, lacking the corresponding Shinriki, I ended up feeling ashamed everywhere I went.'

Ibuki: 'It must have been easy on your way down, I suppose.'

Harukuniwake: 'Yes, the return journey was difficult in its own way. It felt as if my Shintoku were gradually slipping away.'

Ibuki: 'The entire spiritual world is a world of Saunen; therefore, because one dwells in a Shiyōkaku corresponding to the land due to shifts in emotion, you have simply followed the order. The rules of Takamagahara are very strict, and it is arranged so that one cannot violate the boundaries of others. The highest Tengoku, the middle Tengoku, the lower Tengoku, and the three layers of Reigoku are established with solemn distinctions, and the various celestial beings of each heavenly realm cannot even visit one another. It is the rule that a celestial being of a lower Tengoku cannot ascend to a middle Tengoku, nor can those of an upper Tengoku descend to a lower Tengoku. If there were a celestial being ascending from a lower Tengoku to an upper Tengoku, it would surely cause them great agony of heart, make them suffer and writhe, and their eyes would be so dazzled that they could not even see the things around them. Much less could they ever exchange words with the celestial beings of the upper Tengoku. Furthermore, a celestial being descending from an upper Tengoku to a lower Tengoku instantly loses their Shiyōkaku, so if they attempt to exchange words, their speech becomes sluggish and heavy, and their spirit is completely discouraged. Therefore, even if a celestial being of a lower Tengoku reaches a middle Tengoku, or a celestial being of a middle Tengoku reaches the deepest Tengoku, they can never taste happiness for their own body. Celestial beings above the Tengoku where one resides shine with such brilliance that one is struck by their power, and one's eyes are dazzled, making it impossible to see even a single celestial being. In short, this is because their inner nature is not as open as that of the celestial beings of the upper Tengoku. Therefore, their visual perception is not clear, they feel extreme pain in their hearts, and they encounter such suffering that they do not even know if their own life exists or not. However, because you received the special permission of the Lord God and were accompanied by intermediary celestial beings, namely the messengers of the Reigoku, the path of communication was opened for each stage and each organization, and thus your tour was successfully completed. And so, the Lord God accomplishes the connection between the upper heaven and the lower heaven through two types of inner flow. These two types of inner flow are direct inner flow and indirect inner flow.'

Tamayori: 'Yes, I am truly moved by the Gojinji of Ōkamisama.'

Harukuniwake: 'Ah, truly, I cannot contain my gratitude for the Mimegumi of Ōkamisama. Even in the Shinyu of the Izu no Mitama, it is shown that... "I have assigned a humble Goyō to my children. For I cannot allow harm to come to the children of men..." Truly, the Mikokoro of Ōkamisama is an Arigata thing that cannot be measured.'

Having said this, he let out a Toiki and remained Sashiutsumu.

Harukuniwake: 'It is not only I; Tsukiteruhikonokami-sama, Hiroyasuhikonokami-sama, Sukunahikonanokami-sama, Sumiyohime-sama, Masumihime-sama, Tatsuyohime-sama, and other Ketsukō Kamigamisama are all direct Miko of the Ōkami of Izu no Mitama and Mizu no Mitama, yet they have all gone to the Chitei no Kuni, which other Kamigami abhor, and are performing painful Goshiyugo. When I think of this, you are truly Ketsukō. The Shinyu of the Izu no Mitama also indicates... "There is no one as Ketsukō as the people..." does it not?'

Harukuniwake: 'Indeed, the extent of the Mikokoro of Ōkamisama is truly not something we humans can measure. Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Hae Mase, Miroku no Ōkamisama...'

He shed tears like a waterfall, moved by the profoundness of the divine grace, and together with Ryūkō, he pressed his palms together and prostrated himself on the spot. Ibukidonushinokami, blinking his eyes, said:

Ibukidonushinokami: 'Both of you, with that heart, I pray you will work for the sake of the Kami, for the sake of the Way, and for the sake of the people in the Genkai. If so, let us part here.'

No sooner had he spoken than he busily hid his figure in the inner room. The two watched his retreating figure, bowed respectfully, left the mansion, passed through the red gate, expressed deep gratitude to the red and white guards, and began to walk along the Yachimata highway, leaving their path to the direction of their thoughts. Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Hae Mase.
