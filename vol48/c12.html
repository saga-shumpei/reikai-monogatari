Chapter 12: 西王母 [1266]

西王母〔1266〕

§ 1

The Sōtōshin of Takaamahara, that is to say, the Daishiyusaishin, is Ōkunitokotachinomikoto. His other Mina is called Amenominakanushinōokami, and the state in which His Reitoku is perfectly Hatsuki is called Amaterasusumeōkami. And this Ōkamisama is called Izunomitama. The meaning of Izu is that which is Shigen, Shiki, and Shison, and it is the meaning of the Kami who penetrates the Kuwako, Genzai, and Mirai, and who exists in Mugen, Zetsutai, Mushi, and Mushiu. Furthermore, He is the Shisei and Shikau Goshinkaku who appears as the Gensen of love and faith. And at times, He appears as Mizunomitama, appearing and disappearing in the three realms of the manifest world, the spirit world, and the divine world, bestowing eternal life upon all things and granting joy and pleasure. The meaning of Mizu is that which is fresh, and it signifies that which is Shizen, Shibi, Shiai, and Shishin, and is the great light of perfect completeness. Also, because He protects in connection with the three great elements of spirit, power, and body, He is called the Three Souls, and because He protects the three realms of the manifest world, the spirit world (hell), and the divine world, He is also called the Three Souls. In short, although there is only one Kami in the universe, He manifests as ten thousand Kami according to the emotions of His Goshinkaku. And Izunomitama is called the spirit of the warp and becomes the substance of the divine character, while Mizunomitama exists in practical activity and appears to fulfill the purpose, or use, of the Goshinkaku. Therefore, in the study of Kototama, He is called Toyokuninushinomikoto and is also called Kami Susanoonomikoto. And Izunomitama appears as the sun of Takaamahara, and Mizunomitama appears as the moon of Takaamahara. Therefore, the Miroku Ōkami is called the Great Kami of the Moon. The meaning of Miroku is that of supreme benevolence and supreme love. And because He directly undertakes the reconstruction of the universe through that benevolence, love, and true faith, one can know the nature of the divine work of this Kami by seeing that when written with the kanji for Miroku, it signifies the power to change things more and more. Even things like the non-duality of good and evil and the oneness of right and wrong cannot be understood in their truth if based on the laws of the natural world. The words 'non-duality of good and evil' and 'oneness of right and wrong' are not for humans of the natural world to speak; they are words spoken only when viewed from the eyes of the great mercy and compassion of the Kami, and they mean that the Kami does not distinguish between the thickness or thinness of His great love based on the distinctions of good and evil, right and wrong. However, even in the things of the natural world, good and evil are mixed, and beauty and ugliness intermingle, so that all things grow and all order is established. Therefore, even if a person places themselves in the natural world by saying 'spirit first, body second,' they must stand in the world with everything putting the Kami first, and with the goodness of love and the wisdom of faith as the master. However, modern people, who have become unable to see what spiritual things are, cannot thoroughly believe in the invisible spirit world no matter what, and even those who have some spiritual ideas cannot escape the realm of groping in the dark. Therefore, people inevitably value the body, slight the spirit, are easily driven by material desires, and easily fall into hell. In order to compensate for such deficiencies and shortcomings of the manifest world, the Ōkami descended to the earth Himself, filled the spirits according to His Goshinkaku, and came to preach the gospel of the heavenly world to the living beings on earth through prophets. The reason all humans were born into the reality of the world is, so to speak, like the placenta of a heavenly being. And it becomes a training vessel for heavenly beings, a seedbed, and a warm nest for spiritual children, and while it can become a farmer who raises the seedlings of heavenly beings, humans are heavenly beings themselves, and heavenly beings in heaven are humans whose virtues have developed. And heavenly beings can maintain eternal life through love, goodness, and true faith. Therefore, humans are created according to the purpose of the Ōkami so that they may end their life in the manifest world, be resurrected in the heavenly world, lead a life similar to that of a person in the manifest world forever, and act to make the perfection of the heavenly world even more perfect. Therefore, there is not a single heavenly being in the heavenly world or spirit world in Takaamahara who did not come from a human. Except for the Ōkamisama, not a single heavenly being was born in the heavenly world. The inflow of the divine character must come to the human body, which is the terminal point, stay here to develop its spirituality, and then be resurrected to the heavenly world, where for the first time it comes to constitute the various groups of the heavenly world. Therefore, it is said that man is the administrator of the management of heaven and earth, the flower of heaven and earth, and the living palace of the Kami. Ignorant religious figures and preachers of the past and present generally do not understand this principle; they think that when one speaks of heavenly beings, they were born in the heavenly world from the beginning by the special grace of the Kami, and they think that the evil spirits in hell were born in hell from the beginning, and they only think that the evil spirits of hell control or torment the fallen souls of humans. This is a great misunderstanding, and it must be said that the confusion it causes to many people is truly great. Here, the time has come for the Kami to consider the timing, to send Miroku down to the world, to have Him contain the entirety of the whole heavenly world within His womb, to teach this to all the people on earth, and to show the gospel of the heavenly world completely and in detail. However, because the Ōkami enters the thoughts of the prophets and conveys them based on their memories, when He descends into the bodies of Japanese people, He manifests using the Japanese language. Modern scholars or clever people who are fascinated by scientific brains try to criticize, saying, 'The Kami should be omniscient and omnipotent. Why then does He not show Himself using the languages of all places on earth so that the people of each country can easily understand?' and they curse the words of the prophets sent by the Kami as strange, chaotic, and insane, or they are blind people who like to complain, calling them the words of the uneducated or incomplete discourses, which is something that also causes great trouble for the prophets of the Kami.

ケチをつけたがる盲が多いのは、神の予言者も大に迷惑を感ずる所である。

§ 2

Takaamahara and Tenkai are an individual possessing a supreme form. Furthermore, the various organizations of Tengoku constituted in Takaamahara are like an individual possessing a great form next to this. And the Tennin are again its smallest individual. It has been stated repeatedly that Ningen is also a model of Tenkai and a Shōtenchi. Because Kami becomes the Zunau of this individual Takaamahara and dwells within it, governing Baniuitsusai, it is a natural Dauri that He also governs Jigokukai. Ningen also possesses within its Keitaichiu various Shiyokuwannō that are small organizations of Tengoku, stores various Kikuwan, and thus contains both Tengoku and Jigoku.

Now, in something that possesses a form as extremely Enman and Gusoku as Takaamahara, each part has the reflection of the whole, and the whole has the reflection of each part. The reason is that Takaamahara is like a single association, sharing all its possessions with the multitude, and the multitude in turn receives all its possessions from the association to live their lives. In this way, because the Tennin of Tenkai are the recipients of all heavenly things, he also becomes an extremely small part of Tenkai. Even if it is Ningen of the Genkai, as long as he receives the goodness of Takaamahara within his body, he becomes a recipient like a Tennin, becomes a single Tenkai, and also becomes a single Tennin.

Chikokuwake and Ryūkō finally arrived at a certain place in the First Tengoku through the guidance of Kototama-wake-no-Mikoto. In this place stands a palace that has reached an indescribable solemnity. This is the Tengoku Shibikyū of the Tosotsu-ten where the Sun Ōkami dwells eternally, and is what Shintōists call the Sun's Wakamiya. Numerous Tennin, filled with wisdom, enlightenment, the goodness of love, and the truth of faith, formed lines in the courtyard before this palace, welcoming the arrival of the party of Kototama-wake with feelings of joy. Kototama-wake-no-Mikoto pointed to this scene from far away and said,

Kototama-wake: 'Chikokuwake-sama, that which is shining brilliantly in gold before us is Jerusalem. And please look at how it is built and solidified with such huge stone walls, and surrounded by city walls of several hundred cubits.'

Chikokuwake: 'Yes, thank you. For some reason, because we lack the degree of perfection, I cannot tell where Jerusalem is, where the stone walls are, or the height of the city walls. All that is reflected in my eyes is the dazzling light and the faint appearance of the Tennin. And you mentioned Jerusalem, but is that not the holy land in Turkey in Asia Minor?'

Kototama-wake: 'The palace of Jerusalem means a holy place that conveys the teachings of the Ōkami. It also means a high place, which is to say, it refers to the center of the highest Tenkai. What I call stone walls are the very truth of Kami that prevents the invasion of falsehood and sin. What I call the degree is the very nature of the essence, and as the holy words say, a person is one who possesses all truth and virtue. That is to say, one who possesses Tenkai within the inner part of Ningen is called a person, and one who does not possess Tenkai is called a human-beast. Here, there are Tennin who are truly people, but there are no human-beasts like in the Genkai. However, when I said human-beast just now, I spoke from a spiritual perspective. Any holy place where Kami dwells and any holy place that conveys His teachings is referred to as the capital of the noble, or called the palace of Jerusalem.'

Chikokuwake: 'If that is the case, are the Saienkan of Mount Ubusuna in the Genkai, and the holy lands of Kogane-yama, Ryōju-sen, Aya, Tenkyō-zan, Chikyō-zan, Manju-san, and others all Tengoku and called the palace of Jerusalem?'

Kototama-wake: 'That is correct. Indeed, anything that has an Enman organization created by the divinity is also called Jerusalem, and on earth, it is what should be called the Takaamahara of the earth. Since the world on earth is, in short, a copy of Takaamahara, if one does not register with the organization of Takaamahara while on earth, and if one goes against the Dauri and order of spiritual correspondence, one cannot live the life of Tengoku after death. Now, from here, I shall guide you to the center of the innermost Tengoku, the residence of the Ōkami.'

§ 3

He walked ahead and started to step forward. The two felt a sensation as if the soles of their feet were ticklish, and they followed with a very bashful air. The many Tennin lined up along the roadside clapped their hands and shouted 'Woo, woo,' expressing their feelings of Aizen.

The two followed behind Kototamawakenomikoto, and while wearing their Himenpu, their hearts became calm, and they quietly slipped through the front gate of the Wakamiya of the Sun.

Kototamawakenomikoto left the two waiting inside the gate and proceeded deep within with a leisurely air. The two stood within the gate, gazing at the beauty of the fruit trees flourishing in the garden, clicking their tongues, tilting their heads, and letting out sighs as they were struck with amazement, saying 'Ah.'

After a while, a young and beautiful Tenniyo entered leisurely from one side of the garden, emitting a light so brilliant it was dazzling to the eyes. The two, without thinking, were startled and knelt on the ground, expressing their reverence. This Megami was called Seiōbo, and she was a manifestation of Izanaminomikoto, the Kon no Konjin. Even calling her Seiōbo is but a different name for the same divine body. As the Megami took the two by the hands, she led them to the peach orchard, wishing to have them tour the garden while waiting for Kototamawakenomikoto to return from the inner palace. The two, while being led by the hand with trepidation, were guided into the garden of fragrant peach trees. Here, three thousand peach trees were flourishing in orderly rows.

They were divided into the front garden, the middle garden, and the back garden; the front garden had one thousand peach trees, where beautiful flowers bloomed, and it was not rare for them to bear beautiful fruit as well. The peaches in this front garden have small flowers and their fruit is also small. They bloom and ripen once every three thousand years, and those who eat them are added to the ranks of the Tennin of the highest heaven. Furthermore, this peach fruit is not given unless one is very much in accordance with the heart of God. Seiōbo explained the details of these peach fruits to the two one by one, and then stepped into the middle garden.

Here too, there were one thousand peach trees, where beautiful double-petaled flowers were in full bloom and delicious-looking fruit was growing. This is a beautiful fruit that blooms and bears fruit once every six thousand years, and those who eat it live as long as heaven and earth, continuing a life of eternal youth and immortality in any situation. Seiōbo again explained the connection of the peach orchard in detail, and upon finishing, she stepped into the back garden. Here too, one thousand peach trees stood in orderly rows, with large flowers blooming and fragrant, and the fruit was so large that the branches seemed about to break under their weight. This peach tree blooms and bears fruit once every nine thousand years, and it is a most precious divine fruit, said to grant those who eat it a life equal to that of heaven, earth, the sun, and the moon. Seiōbo explained this connection in the greatest detail to Chikokuwake-dono.

However, the secret meaning of these peaches is not easily permitted to be revealed; nevertheless, it is shown in this story that the peach blooms on the third day of the third month on earth and ripens completely on the fifth day of the fifth month. From this, one may be able to roughly infer what kind of divine plan exists for this peach. The fact that there is a time when Seiōbo will descend to earth once, wearing robes of yellow brocade, and offer this to the divine authorities of the earth along with many angels, may also be roughly inferred from the Noh songs currently in vogue.

Changing the subject, Seiōbo finished the tour of the peach orchard, and while leading the two by the hands, she brought them back to the original gate, and said, 'Chikokuwake-dono, Ryūkō-dono, I bid you farewell here,' and no sooner had she spoken than she hid her divine form within the peach orchard. The two bowed down to her retreating figure, and having understood the deep and subtle secret meaning of the divine world's plan through the explanation of the Queen Mother, they were choked with tears of gratitude while unconsciously clasping their hands together.

To such a place, a beautiful Tenniyo appeared, opening the golden middle gate of the Shibi Palace; the solemnity of her appearance as she emerged wearing a feather robe filled with five-colored radiance was such that, although all the Tennin of the heavenly kingdom possess beautiful and perfect features, it was the first time since their tour of the heavenly kingdom that the two had seen a divine being endowed with such perfect and wonderful features.

Incidentally, the feather robe is by no means an imaginary thing that flies through the air as in Noh songs. It is a divine garment where the most beautiful sleeves are several feet longer than the hands, and the hem hangs one or two jō behind.

Now, the twelve Tenniyo, without a word, beckoned the two, and with six in front and six behind, they guarded the two and led them through the golden middle gate. At this time, subtle music, such as they had never heard before, arose from all four directions, and the fragrance was rich and aromatic; the two were filled with such joy that they forgot where they were, and while reciting the Amatsu Norito in a low voice, they proceeded inside with great delight.

原文（近代文語）

と先に立つて歩み出した。二人は足の裏がこそばゆい様な気分に打たれ、いと恥かし気に従ひ行く。路傍に堵列せる数多の天人は『ウオーウオー』と手を拍つて叫び、愛善の意の表示をなしてゐる。二人は言霊別命の背後に従ひ、被面布を冠りながら心落ち着き、静々と日の若宮の表門を潜り入る。

言霊別命は二人を門内に待たせ置き悠々として奥深く入り給うた。二人は門内に佇み園内に繁茂せる果樹の美しきを眺めやり、舌うちしながら頭を傾け『アーア』と驚きに打たれ吐息を洩らしてゐる。暫くすると庭園の一方より目も眩むばかりの光を放ち悠々と入り来り給ふ妙齢の天女があつた。二人は思はずハツと大地に踞み敬礼を表した。此女神は西王母と云つて伊邪那美尊の御分身、坤の金神であつた。西王母と云ふも同身異名である。女神は二人の手をとりながら言霊別命の奥殿より帰り来る間、庭園を巡覧させむと桃畑に導き給うた。二人は恐る恐る手を曳かれながら芳しき桃樹の園に導かれ行く。此処には三千株の桃の樹が行儀よく繁茂してゐる。さうして前園、中園、後園と区劃され、前園には一千株の桃樹があつて美はしき花が咲き且一方には美はしき実を結んだのも尠なくはない。此前園の桃園は花も小さく又其実も小さい。さうして三千年に一度花咲き熟して、之を食ふものは最高天国の天人の列に加へらるるものである。さうして此桃の実は余程神の御心に叶つたものでなければ与へられないものである。西王母は二人に一々此桃の実の説明をしながら中園に足を踏み入れた。ここにも亦一千株の桃の樹があり、美はしき八重の花が咲き充ち又甘さうな実がなつて居る。之は六千年に一度花咲き実り、之を食ふものは天地と共に長生し、如何なる場合にも不老不死の生命を続けると云ふ美はしき果物である。西王母は又もや詳細に桃畑の因縁を説き諭し、終つて後園に足を入れ給うた。此処にも亦一千株の桃樹が行儀よく立ち並び、大いなる花が咲き匂ひ、実も非常に大きなのが枝も折れむばかりに実つてゐる。此桃の樹は九千年に一度花咲き実り、之を食ふものは天地日月と共に生命を等しうすると云ふ重宝至極な神果である。西王母は此因縁を最も詳細に治国別に諭し給うた。然し此桃の密意については容易に発表を許されない、然しながら桃は三月三日に地上に於ては花咲き、五月五日に完全に熟するものなる事は、此物語に於て示されたる所である。之によつて此桃に如何なる御経綸のあるかは略推知し得らるるであらう。西王母は一度地上に降臨して黄錦の御衣を着し、数多のエンゼルと共に之を地上の神権者に献げ給ふ時機ある事は、現在流行する謡曲によつても略推知さるるであらう。

却説、西王母は桃園の案内を終り、二人の手を引きながら元の門口に送り来り、

『治国別殿、竜公殿、之にてお別れ申す』

と云ふより早く桃園内に神姿を隠し給うた。二人は其後姿を伏し拝み、王母が説明によつて神界経綸の深遠微妙なる密意を悟り、思はず合掌して感涙に咽びつつあつた。

かかる所へ紫微宮の黄金の中門を開いて現はれ来る美はしき天女、五色の光輝に充ちた羽衣を着しながら出で来る其荘厳さ、天国の天人は何れも美はしく円満の相を具備すれども、未だ嘗て斯の如き円満なる妙相を備へたる神人を見るのは二人とも天国巡覧以来初めてであつた。

因に羽衣とは決して謡曲にある如き中空を翔ける空想的のものでない。最も美はしき袖は手より数尺長く、裾は一二丈も後に垂れてゐる神衣の事である。

扨て十二人の天女は、無言の儘二人を指し招き、前に六人、後に六人、二人を守りながら黄金の中門を潜つて迎へ入れるのであつた。此時今まで聞いた事もない様な微妙な音楽四方より起り、芳香馥郁として薫じ、二人は吾身の何処にあるやも忘るるばかり歓喜に充されながら、微の声にて天津祝詞を奏上しつつ欣々として進み入る。
