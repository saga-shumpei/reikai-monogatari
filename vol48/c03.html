Chapter 3: 観 Sound 経 [1257]

観音経〔1257〕

§ 1

When Kami appears in the world, He distinguishes between good and evil. The party of Harukuniwake, the Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, passed through the mysterious forest and arrived at the forest of Ukiki. At the entrance, they encountered the Bansotsu guarding the camp of General Katahiko of Ranchi, and as they engaged in various questions and answers, they were cast into a trap plotted by the enemy army. Though their lives appeared in peril, the Kamuzukasa protected by Kami, while falling into the dangerous trap, suffered not even a scratch as slight as the dew on a rabbit's hair. Harukuniwake and Tatsukō, with the quick wit of the Bansotsu, persuaded Ark and Taaru, escaped the danger, and climbed back up.

As they explained the precious Mioshihe of Kami in great detail, the two Bansotsu, who had originally received the Mitama of Kami, suddenly underwent a Shinkiitsuten. With the flowers of Kuwaigo in full bloom, they surrendered from the bottom of their hearts and, accompanying Harukuniwake, headed toward the camp of Ranchi. General Katahiko of Ranchi, observing that the party of Harukuniwake had arrived here unexpectedly, entered into a state of smug satisfaction. While using soft words to decorate the surface and speaking of the sake of reconciliation, he invited the two to a secret place in the inner room. It was truly hateful that he cast them into a deep, thousand-fathom dark cavern with a Tsubamegaeshi strategy.

Harukuniwake and Tatsukō instantly lost their senses, their souls flew into the void, and they wandered into the Seireikai. Guided by a single guard, they climbed up a narrow valley path and finally arrived before the barrier of Yatsumado. While watching with bated breath the scene where the spirits of good and evil gathered and received judgment at Yatsumado, they gained an obscure realization of the true essence of the two worlds of Gen and Yū. While resting for a while at the mansion of Ibukido-nushi and gazing at the scenery outside, a great fireball suddenly illuminated the mid-heaven and fell before the two.

Sparks scattered in all directions, and for a while, they were enveloped in clouds, making the surroundings invisible. The two became increasingly suspicious and stared intently with fixed eyes. Suddenly, a Kami-bito with radiant features and garments appeared, turned toward Harukuniwake, and said, 'I am the Kami of Kototama-wake. We have met in a mysterious place. By the command of the Sumer-Ōkami, I have now been entrusted with the heavy mission of being a mediator between heaven and earth. Come, I shall guide you as you tour the heavens from here. As for Tatsukō, though your body has not yet opened to the enlightenment of Shōgaku, I shall specially permit you to accompany us. Wear this,' he said, and while speaking, he searched his bosom, took out a face-covering cloth, and placed it upon Tatsukō.

Here, the two stood up with courage and toured a part of the lowest heaven. After finishing the tour, they ascended toward the middle heaven. Konohanahime appeared, and while admonishing the hearts of both, she led them to the mansion of Uzuhiko. To show the outline of the precious Keirin of Kami, the Kami of Kototama-wake appeared here as the disciple of Harukuniwake, the one called Gosan-kō-sama, and bestowed upon them yet another precious lesson; how noble it was.

From here, the two, led by the guidance of Gosan-kō, toured each organization of heaven, visited the highest heaven and even the Reikoku, and received pure teachings from the Moon-no-Ōkami, the Sun-no-Ōkami, and a hundred other angels. Having received wisdom and Shōgaku, they returned to their original physical bodies and told the story of how they brought all the ugly officials of the Brahmins to submission through Kototama. As they spoke, it became more interesting, and they entered deeper and deeper into the truth. I pray before Kami, 'May You protect us so that we may reach its profound mysteries. Imi-na-kami.'

Imoriwake, Otami, Ark, and Taaru, having sobered up from their intoxication during the day, thrust their faces forward before General Ranchi with faces that betrayed nothing. General Ranchi showed an unusually broad, smiling face and said,

Ranchi: 'Ah, you four gentlemen, I am happy to see you in such good health. Do you have some business with me?'

He was displaying his eccentric nature. It appears that Ranchi had been treated with considerable sympathy by his unexpected guests, which had caused a crack in a part of his mind. Imoriwake is also a man who has always had a slight mental deficiency, but now, looking at General Ranchi's face, he laughed with a grin.

『アハヽヽヽヽ、何とえらい勢で厶るな。見ると聞くとは大違ひ、今迄ならば其嚇しは利くだらうが、もはや今日となつては内兜を見透した此方、そんな嚇し文句は、いつかな　いつかな喰ひませぬぞや。何なつと業力を出してランチ将軍の息の根をとめて御覧、それが出来れば拙者の役目をお譲り申す約束を今からしてもよろしい。マサカ其神力は厶るまい。現在お民に秋風を吹かされて居る様な今の体裁、これお民殿、今其方は蠑螈別と乞食する位なら片彦将軍のお妾になると云つたな。ウツフヽヽ出来した出来した天晴天晴、女丈夫の亀鑑、貞女の鑑、名を末代に伝ふであらう』

と脱線だらけの業託を吐いてゐる。

蠑螈別は躍気となり、

『然らば此方の法力によつて此陣中をたたき破り、先づ第一に気の毒ながらランチ将軍の息の根をとめてくれむ。後で後悔召さるな』

と云ひ放ち、次の間へ行つて大自在天の前に数珠をもみながらキチンと端坐し、先づバラモン大自在天を念じ、次に惟神霊幸倍坐世を奏上し、大広木正宗殿、義理天上日の出神と称へ終り、ソロソロ得意の観音経を誦じ初めた。

§ 4

'Shinkuwanshiyōjiyōkuwan Kuōdaichiekuwan Hikuwangiujiyōkuwan Jiyōguwanjiyōsengō Mukushiyōjiyōkuō Enichihashōan Nōfukusaifūkuwa Fumiyōshōseken Hitaikairaishin Jiimeudaiun Jiyukanrohofū Metsujiyobonnauen Zeushiyōkiyōkuwanshiyo Fuigunjinjiu Nenpikuwannonriki Shiuonshitsutaisan Meuonkuwanzeon Bononkaichōon Shiyōhisekenon Zekoshiyujiyōnen Nennenmotsushiyōgi Kuwanzeonjiyōshiyō Okunaushiyaku Nōisaeko'

He is chanting to Kuwannon with such itsushiyōkenmei, sweat dripping down in streams. Yet, there seems to be no sign of a response from Kuwannon, and as the Buddha does not say whether the law is to be followed or to tell Kuwannon to ketsukura, Imoriwake becomes impatient and mutters to himself:

Imoriwake: 'Hey, you fellows called Buddhas, you just line up fine-sounding descriptions, but when the moment of truth comes, you are of no use at all. From now on, I will not worship a fellow like you. From here on, it is "Ketsukura Kuwannon" from my side.'

The absurdity of him grumbling thus. He has a dissatisfied look on his face, like a cat that has had its hard-earned dried bonito snatched away by a dog, and he is sniffing his nose.

原文（近代文語）

『真観清浄観 広大智慧観 悲観及慈観 常願常瞻仰 無垢清浄光 慧日破諸闇 能伏災風火 普明照世間 悲体戒雷震 慈意妙大雲 樹甘露法雨 滅除煩悩炎 諍証経官処 怖畏軍陣中 念彼観音力 衆怨悉退散 妙音観世音 梵音海潮音 勝彼世間音 是故須常念

念々勿生疑 観世音浄聖 於苦悩死厄 能為作依怙』

かく一生懸命に汗をタラタラ流しながら観音を念じてゐる。されど観音の感応はありさうもなく、仏が法とも尻喰へ観音とも仏が云はぬので、蠑螈別は業を煮やし、

『エー、仏と云ふ奴は、立派な能書きばかり並べよつて、マサカの時はチツとも間に合はぬものぢやな。もう之から貴様の様な奴は拝んでやらぬわい。之からはこつちから尻喰へ観音ぢや』

とブツブツ呟いて居る其可笑しさ。猫が折角くはへた松魚節を犬にふんだくられた時の様な不足さうな面をして洟をすすつてゐる。
