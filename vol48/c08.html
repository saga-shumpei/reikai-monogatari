Chapter 8: 亡者苦雑 [1262]

亡者苦雑〔1262〕

§ 1

When a Seirei departs from its Nikutai and arrives at the Sekishiyo of the Seireikai, its Yōbau for the first interval possesses the same Menbau as when it still resided in the Genkai, and its Onsei, Dōsa, and the Chiyōtan of its stature differ not in the slightest. At this time, it still remains in a state of Guwaibun, for its Naibun has not yet had the leisure to open. After a short while, its Menbau, Gengo, and the like gradually undergo Tenkuwa, until finally they differ entirely from their Izen form. If one asks why such a Henkuwa occurs, it is because when that Seirei was in the Genkai, its Menbau would undergo Tenkuwa and correspond to the Jiyōdō, depending on the nature of the love, or Jiyōdō, that had become the primary master within its Naibun. This is because when that Seirei was still within its Nikutai, it held this love, or Jiyōdō, as its Yuiitsu Seimei. Furthermore, the Menbau of a Ningen's Seirei is by no means identical to the Menbau of its Nikutai. Although the Menbau of the Nikutai is inherited from one's parents and thus bears some resemblance to the Menbau and vocal tone of one's parents, the Menbau of the Seirei is determined by the nature of its Jiyōdō; therefore, one may say that its Menbau is the evidence and image of its Jiyōdō.

After a Seirei has departed from its Nikutai—that is, when it has crossed the barrier that the people of the Genkai call death—the Menbau that the Seirei manifests is itself the evidence and image of its Jiyōdō. At this time, the Guwaibun has already been cast away, and only the Naibun emerges. However, for a Seirei that has died and not yet passed much time, one can recognize at a glance whether it was an acquaintance, a brother, a parent, or a relative by its Menbau and Onsei; yet, as time passes, they change to the extent that they can no longer recognize one another.

Those who possess the Jiyōdō of Aizen have a beautiful Menbau and an indefinable air of refinement, shining with brilliance, whereas the Menbau of those who dwell in evil Jiyōdō is truly ugly and filthy, to the point that one might suspect at first glance that they are monsters. Viewed from the perspective of their own nature, all Ningen Seirei are nothing other than Jiyōdō itself. And this Menbau is that which is called Jiyōdō manifested outwardly. Because the Menbau undergoes Tenkuwa in this manner, one cannot feign or disguise an evil Jiyōdō that is not one's own while in the Reikai. Consequently, one cannot assume a Menbau that contradicts the love one possesses. Since all Seirei in the Reikai manifest exactly according to their thoughts, reveal their intentions just as they are upon their faces, and exist in a Jiyōtai that appears in every part of their bodies, the Menbau of every Seirei is, in short, the form and the evidence and image of its Jiyōdō. Therefore, those who knew each other in the Genkai can recognize one another in the Seireikai. However, in Takamagahara and the Land of Roots, such things no longer occur. Thus, it is extremely difficult for them to know for themselves whether one was an acquaintance, a brother, or a parent and child; indeed, one might say it is virtually impossible.

Although a Seirei undergoes a gradual change in its Menbau and Onsei after death, the Menbau of a hypocrite's Seirei changes more slowly than others. This is because their Naibun, or heart, is accustomed to constantly imitating a good Jiyōdō. Therefore, these Seirei do not expose their inherent ugliness for a long time. Yet, because the gilding of their falsehood is gradually removed and peels away of its own accord, the Naibun they have formed must inevitably undergo transformation according to the original form of their Jiyōdō. When this happens, the hypocrite has their true value exposed, reaching the depths of ugliness, and is truly miserable. Furthermore, even while in the Genkai, a hypocrite may pose as a god, a celestial being, a wise man, or a righteous person, and may speak of matters of the Reikai in great detail; yet, in their Naibun, they only acknowledge the natural world, do not actually recognize divinity, and consequently deny the state of Takamagahara or the teachings of God. Therefore, they are treated as hypocrites in the Reikai. Conversely, if the Jiyōdō becomes increasingly internal and conforms more greatly to Takamagahara, the Menbau becomes truly supreme in beauty. This is because they take the very love of the celestial world as their own heart and their own Yōbau. Moreover, those whose Jiyōdō is external, who do not realize the truth, who do not love God, and who do not believe in the Holy Word, act against the Jiyōtai of the so-called Takamagahara; thus, their Menbau becomes dark and ugly, becoming even more inferior, base, and hideous than when they were in the Genkai. The Oomoto Shin-yu states: 'When the Age of the Gods arrives, those with a beautiful heart will appear more splendid in the eyes of God than those with a beautiful face. No matter how beautiful a face one may have, or how grandly one may act, once one comes before God, one's appearance will instantly change. You have been granted a Nikutai that corresponds to your Mi-Tama,' and so on. This is a warning to the Seirei and should be called an accurate instruction regarding the Jiyōtai of the Seirei in the Reikai. Therefore, those in the Reikai become perfect and beautiful according to the degree of their Naibun, and as they turn toward the Guwaibun, they become increasingly deficient. Thus, no matter what master painter may exist, even if they exhaust their technique and wield a spiritual brush, they cannot depict even one ten-thousandth of the beauty, brilliance, and vibrant vitality of the Menbau and form of the celestial beings in the highest, primary Heaven and Reikoku. Nevertheless, for those in the lowest level of Heaven and Reikoku, only when the most skilled and famous painter exerts their utmost effort, and their technique enters the realm of the divine and reaches the marvelous, can they perhaps depict their Menbau to some extent and reveal a portion of their true state.

General Shiyōgun, General Katahiko, and the two adjutants Gariya and Kēsu had their identities checked one by one by the Akazura guard at the gate of the checkpoint. The first to be examined, in order of arrival, was General Katahiko. Since his lifetime, General Katahiko had been most stubborn and hypocritical; while serving as a propagandist for Brahmanism, he acknowledged no Kami in his inner heart, and merely used Kami as a means for his own selfishness, much like the religious figures of the Gendai era. Furthermore, he preached the paths of Tenkai and Jigoku to many Ningen, causing the gumai to be either delighted or terrified, and led them to believe that he was a Zentokushiya and a sage, and indeed a representative of Kami. On the other hand, he used military force to kill or torment those who did not believe his words, thereby maintaining his authority and forcibly preserving order. For this reason, even though he had arrived in the Seireikai where one's appearance should change immediately after death, he did not easily undergo such a transformation.

Guard: 'On such and such a year, month, and day, at such and such a place, you must have committed adultery with such and such a maiden, would you not say? And again, on such and such a year, month, and day, at such and such an hour and minute, at such and such a place, you secretly defiled another man's wife, deceived the woman, and extorted a great deal of money from her, would you not say?'

As he was pointed out as if his palm were being read, even the formidable Katahiko was at a loss for an answer.

Having said only that, he hung his head.

Katahiko: 'Hai, I would never say you are lying, but it is truly painful for me to have you read them. I beg you to omit them.'

Guard: 'Do not talk like a fool. You committed these selfish acts yourself, and now that you are to be exposed before this great crowd, you find it painful and ask for omissions? Katahiko: 'Hai, there is no help for it. I beg you to proceed according to the rules.'

Akazura read aloud one by one, in a loud voice, without missing a single word up to the nine hundred and ninety-fifth case. The details were truly astonishing; he read out clearly many things that Katahiko had forgotten, including the location, time, the age of the other party, what Katahiko had said to the woman, what the woman had replied, and even the movements of their hands and feet. Having his dark shame exposed to the light, Katahiko held his head, cold sweat streaming down, and trembled with a bright red face.

Guard: 'Is there no mistake in this? If there is no mistake, place your thumbprint here.'

Katahiko: 'Hai.'

So saying, he fearfully wrote on the ledger, 'There is not a hair's breadth of difference in this record of my conduct during my lifetime, General Katahiko,' and pressed his thumbprint upon it.

Guard: 'Un, this is sufficient. Now then, you must have committed fraud during your lifetime. You must have also committed theft from time to time. You must have committed robbery as well. You took bribes, did you not? Furthermore, not to mention murder and wounding others, you slandered the path of Kami, slandered people, obstructed the businesses of others, and exhausted the full extent of the body-master-spirit-servant state. Now, confess them one by one.'

Katahiko: 'Hai, please, I beg you to spare me. It is too much.'

Guard: 'Do not talk like a fool. The Yatsumado checkpoint is where we investigate so that there is not a single fraction of a mistake. No matter how much time it takes, how could we do such a simple thing as to omit them?'

『アイタヽヽヽヽ、許せ許せ息が切れるわい、あゝ死ぬ死ぬ死ぬ』

『アハヽヽ、痛いか、苦しいか、之も自業自得だ、三途川の鬼婆だぞ』

お民『コリヤ　コリヤ、其方はお寅の副守護神でないか、逐一其方の罪状を読み上げるから、聞いたがよからう』

『ナーニ、此お寅はこんな所へ来て、罪を数へられるやうな悪い事はせぬワ、現に此処に居るエキスが、私の金を取りやがつたのだ。なぜ之を査べぬのか』

『蠑螈別が其方の金を取つたのでないか』

『蠑螈別は取つたにした所で、私の最愛の男だ、お寅の物は蠑螈別の物、蠑螈別の物は即ちお寅の物だ、それをうまくチヨロまかして、まき上げた此エキスこそ大悪人だ』

と云ひながら、フと顔を上ぐればお民であつた。お寅はお民を見るよりクワツと怒り、

『コリヤお民、貴様こそ大悪人だ、蠑螈別をくはへて金を盗ませ、こんな所まで連れて来よつたぢやないか。サ、いい所で会うた、生首を引抜いてやらう、サア覚悟致せ』

と狼のやうな声ふりあげてお民に武者ぶりつく。お民は一生懸命にお寅と組んず組まれつ、もみ合うてゐる。蠑螈別、エキスはヒヨロヒヨロしながら立ち上り、

『マアマア待つた、待たんせ、コリヤどうぢや』

と二人の中に割つて入り、顔を引つ掻かれたり、抓られたり、蠑螈別も焼糞になつて撲る、蹴る、誰彼なしに金切声をふり上げ、犬の咬み合のやうに喚き立ててゐる。此声を聞きつけて赤、白の守衛は宙を飛んで此場に現はれ来り、赤は大きな鉄棒を振上げ、

『コラツ』

と一喝した。此声に驚いて、お民も、お寅も、蠑螈別、エキスも命カラガラ散り散りバラバラに何処ともなく逃去つて了つた。

白『本当に面白いものですなア、あゝして娑婆へ追ひ返せば、何れ改心をして、又来るでせう』
