Chapter 18: 冥 Song [1272]

冥歌〔1272〕

§ 1

In the camp of the Ukiki forest, the hosts and guests relaxed, and a banquet was held to celebrate the safe completion of the Iumei journey. It goes without saying that it also served as a feast for the reconciliation of friends and foes alike. Starting with the generals Ranchi and Katahiko, Harukuniwake took his seat in the Shiyōza, and the members including Āku, Tāru, Ekisu, Imoriwake, O-tami, O-tora, Tatsukō, Mankō, Matsuhiko, Aku, Taku, Teku, Gariya, and Kēsu formed a circle in the fairly spacious living room, gathered delicacies from the mountains and seas, and sang and danced until they broke the banks. Of course, it should be noted that prior to this, they had already enshrined the Ōkami of Ananai-kyō and offered prayers of gratitude and supplication.

Harukuniwake sings with a gentle tone:

Where is the Takamagahara? The land of the pure and noble God, Where the flowers of prosperity forever Bloom in full, fragrant in the land of God.

Tatsukō: 'Where is the Takamagahara? Parting the eight-fold layers of clouds, Ascending to the Tokiwa-Kaniwa Of the pure and noble divine beings, Struck by infinite joy, It is a land where one plays in delight and bliss.'

Harukuniwake: 'The divine land of Takamagahara, Filled to the brim with the virtue of love, All the heavenly beings who dwell there, Are wrapped in the grace of God. Diligent in their daily tasks, For the sake of the divine realm of God, Uniting their hearts as one, In a group of perfect harmony, Ever more pure and beautiful, It is a land where heavenly beings dwell, Opening the way forward, rejoicing and brave.'

Tatsukō: 'The spiritual land of Takamagahara, Where the Moon-no-Ōkami forever Resides in peace, the land of abundance. Mountains and rivers are pure, the fields are lush, A land of wonders filled with the serenity Of spring and summer scenery. The hundred fruits ripen well, And the hundred birds, whose names are unknown, Celebrate the eternal spring of Tokoyo, Playing in delight and bliss. With faces shining clear, With gentle countenances, smiling and bright, It is the land of God that knows no sorrow. We who were born as humans, Living in this world, for the sake of the way of the Ōkami, And for the sake of the world, Polishing our hearts and exhausting our bodies, After the separation of spirit and flesh, Without crossing the eight-forked barrier of heaven, Directly to the heavenly realm, To ascend as spirits, we must advance, So polish your hearts from now on. God is with us, Humans are the children of God, the palaces of God, How could we not return to God? Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, May your spirits be blessed.'

Ranchi: 'The land of the roots is the land of ugliness, A land where eighty kinds of crooked spirits and ugly souls Swarm and dwell. Defiled by dust and filth, A land so foul it stings the nose. Before and behind the darkness of Tokoyo, A land where one cannot even see one's own feet. Descending into the dark tunnel, Striking one's head against the rocks, Blood flows like a waterfall, An ugly land of pain and suffering. A narrow, dangerous bridge, Suspended over the deep valley river, Where cold winds that cut the body Blow forever and ever. A cold and chilly land of ugliness. Here and there, the skeletal ghosts, Like insects in an autumn evening, Their sorrow and lamentations are heard, A dark land that stifles the chest. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, We, illuminated by the divine light of God, Saved here and becoming humans of this world again, Have become beings who can worship The light of the sun and moon in detail. When I think, when I think of the grace of the God Of heaven and earth, is there ever a time When we, as frail human beings, Can repay it? Exhausting our hearts and bodies, No matter what suffering we encounter, When compared to the noble grace of God, The sincerity we offer Does not even reach the level of a single hair Of nine oxen. Please forgive us, Kannagara, We apologize before the presence of God.'

Katahiko: 'Having wandered unknowingly Into the eight-forked road of the Chūūkai, From the time I was born into this world until today, All the evil deeds of body-master and spirit-servant That I have committed, When they were exposed by the gatekeeper Of Ibukido, I felt as if I were neither in body nor in the world, And I felt as if I wanted to disappear As quickly as possible if there were a hole. In the indescribable suffering, Cast down into the land of the roots, In the dark rock cave filled with the stench of filth, Bending my waist, hurting my feet, and hitting my head, When I finally reached the end of the bridge, I saw many Meikan standing in a row, Looking at me with scary faces, And the terror of being scolded and driven away. Even with that, from all four directions, Skeletal ghosts, emaciated to bone and skin, Appeared as if a beehive had been poked, And the unpleasantness of them biting into our bodies And clinging to our hands and feet. The wind that blew was foul, And the stench that naturally twisted one's nose Blew all around. That even those with such deep sins Would be dropped into such a place, I wondered many times in my heart, If even the God Ōkunihiko-no-Kami Would not hear me. Though I called, screamed, and prayed, There was no proof, only bitter tears, And in the moments of suffering and agony, I heard the faint sound of the Sendenka. The moment I heard it, the skeletal ghosts Vanished like smoke.'

The Meikuwan, with their demon-like faces, hid their forms, and gradually the Atari became somewhat brighter. At the moment I thought, 'Oh, how joyful,' a goddess descended leisurely, riding upon a cloud of forgiveness. The goddess, accompanied by two Jijiyo, appeared before us and spoke in a most gentle Onkoe: 'Are you General Ranchi? Are you General Katahiko? By the Mikoto of the Sumeōkami who has appeared in the Hinodewake of the Saiau of Takaamahara, I have come down to assist in your Kunen, as an angel of the Murasakihime no Mikoto.' How joyful it was to hear her proclaim this; is this what they mean by 'a Buddha in Jigoku'?

Feeling as if I had been revived, at the moment I lifted my head in surprise, Murasakihime spoke the words of Kami with grace. Wishing to serve the Mikokoro of Kinkatsukanenōokami, she took her Jijiyo, rode upon a cloud, and crossed the Hotsupau sky in an Iuiu manner, hiding her form. How joyful it was when, shortly thereafter, I was saved by the Sendenshi, Harukuniwake, Tatsukō, Matsuhikotsukasa, and other Mahito, and was able to behold the light of this world once again. To repay this great benevolence, from this moment on, I shall offer my sincerity for the sake of Kami, for the sake of the Way, and for the sake of the people of the world.

In order to illuminate the dark Genkai of the Tokoyo with the precious light of the Ōkami as quickly as possible, I shall follow Harukuniwake. Even if the Moon's Mikuni is still foolish, I shall serve for the sake of the Way even to the ends of the one hundred and eighty countries, braving the rain and dew. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase, I offer my sincerity before the presence of Kami and pray.

Indignant at that miserable sight, I hid myself in the valley of Natsutani together with Tatsukō, and while discussing a strategy for the aftermath, I descended the Tobotobo slope with the horses that had been left behind. Looking closely, O-Tami-sama had fallen limp like a frog thrown upon the earth, her life force severed. 'Oh, this is a crisis!' cried Mankō, and the group of Aku, Teku, and Taku performed artificial respiration and recited the Amatsunorito. As they prayed, a miracle occurred; the Shintoku manifested instantly, and the joy of her returning breath was immense. They realized this was entirely the work of the Kamisama, who had planned this to guide them, and they bowed in reverence. Now, having achieved a safe reunion, they cast aside their former grudges and felt the ease of entrusting everything to the Kamisama. From here on, we too shall tighten our spiritual Haraobi, cross the Aranohara where the Magami rage, and serve with all our might for the sake of the Kami. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! O Great Imperial Kami, who created the Tenchi, I pray that you look with pity upon the humble heart of O-Tora and grant that this great work be brought to completion.

Mankō: 'Well, everyone, the Yūmei-gumi has recovered its vigor, and the driving of the Kototama-sha was quite spirited indeed. Shall we have O-Tami-Nyora-sama sing us something interesting? Come now, O-Tami-sama, you are quite the master when it comes to the path of the occult, are you not?'

O-Tami-sama: 'Ho, ho, ho, ho. I was too arrogant and climbed to such heights that I incurred divine punishment, tumbled down the stairs, and went on a complete journey through the Yūmei. It seems I struck the back of my head quite hard, and my mind has become strange; I cannot possibly sing anything. I beg you to excuse me.'

Mankō: 'Come, come, O-Tami-sama. We are the saviors of your life. I do not mean to hold it over you, but surely singing a song or two would not bring you much divine punishment. You should not act so coy, even before Imoriwake-sama. Sing, just sing!'

O-Tami-sama: 'Well, if you insist, I have no choice. Since I am one who has died and returned, I cannot sing songs of the living. Is anything acceptable?'

Mankō: 'Anything is fine. I am satisfied just to hear your voice. It does not matter if a ghostly mood is mixed in. I have heard plenty of songs from the Genkai, but I have never heard a song from the Yūkai. So please, sing what you learned in the Yūkai, even if it is just a little.'

O-Tami-sama: 'Very well, it is embarrassing, but I shall sing for you.'

Saying this, she thrust both hands out, turned her palms down with a flop, and danced while flicking her tongue in and out. The sight was so comical that the group burst into laughter, suddenly adding to the excitement.

O-Tami-sama: 'Ah, how resentful, how resentful. I was deceived by the wicked man, Imoriwake-sama, and followed him against my will all the way to the Forest of Ukiki. Hiyū-hiyū, doro-doro, hiyū, doro-doro. How resentful... How resentful it is that rice sticks to the soles of my feet, how hateful.
