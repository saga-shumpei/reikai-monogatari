Chapter 10: 天国の富 [1264]

天国の富〔1264〕

§ 1

Between the myriad things of the Genkai, that is to say the Shizenkai, and the myriad things of the Reikai, there exists a correspondence according to the order of Kamunagara. Furthermore, there exists an immutable Rihafu between all things of Ningen and the myriad things of the Tenkai, and by their Renketsu, there exists a correspondence. And Ningen possesses all that the Tennin possess, while at the same time possessing that which they do not. Ningen, viewed from their Naibun, dwell in the Reikai, but at the same time, viewed from their Guwaibun, Ningen dwell in the Shizenkai. That which belongs to the natural, or Guwaiteki, Kioku of a person is termed Guwaibun, and it refers to all Jibutsu related to Saunen and the Sauzau arising therefrom. To speak briefly, all Etsuraku and Kuwaikan derived from the knowledge, Gakumon, and the like of Ningen, which bear a Sekenteki Shiyumi, as well as the various Kuwaikan, sensations, language, and actions belonging to the senses of the physical body, are collectively termed the Guwaibun of Ningen. These Guwaibun are all Jibutsu at the ultimate point where the divine, or spiritual, inflow coming from the Ōkami ceases. This is because the divine inflow never ceases midway, but inevitably proceeds to its ultimate destination. The place where this divine order culminates is Ningen, who are the leaders of the myriad things, the living palaces of God, the masters of the governance of heaven and earth, and the training grounds for Tennin. Therefore, Ningen should know that they are the root and foundation of all things of the Kami-sama. Moreover, the intermediate space through which the inflow of divinity passes is Takamagahara, and its ultimate destination is Ningen. Therefore, nothing that does not enter into this Renketsu can exist. Thus, when the Tenkai and humanity harmonize and connect, it can be clarified that both rely upon each other to continue to exist. Therefore, Ningen separated from the Tenkai are like a chain without a lock, and the Tenkai separated from humanity are like a house without a foundation; the two must harmonize with each other.

Such noble Ningen, by turning their Naibun away from the Kami and severing their connection with Takamagahara, instead directing it toward the Shizenkai and themselves, loving themselves, loving the world, and facing only their Guwaibun, consequently caused Ningen to retreat and become unable to again serve as the root and foundation of Takamagahara. Because of this, the Ōkami, having no other choice, established here a mediator Tennin as a prophet and sent him down to the earth, making that Kami-hito the root and foundation of the Tenkai, and by this, caused the Tenkai and Ningen to harmonize, and performed a most profound governance to make the earth a land like the kingdom of heaven. The dawn when this governance is completed is called the Age of Matsu, the World of Miroku, or the World of the Kingdom of Heaven. And through the hands and mouths of the prophets of the warp and woof of the Izu-no-Mitama and Mizu-no-Mitama, the Ōkami transmitted the sacred words and sought to complete a land of perfect union between heaven and earth. In the Shin-yu of the founder of Ōmoto, it is shown simply and clearly: 'Rolling heaven and earth into one, I shall make it the world of the Kingdom of God. The plum blossoms of the three thousand worlds open at once; I, the Ushitora no Konjin, sit upon Mount Sumeru and guard it. This Ōmoto is the great bridge of heaven and earth; unless the people of the world cross this bridge, they shall not know the true grace.' Yet, modern Ningen do not lend their ears to such simple and clear sacred words, but instead rummage through incomprehensible and difficult books, and it is truly pathetic that they attempt to boast of their knowledge by misunderstanding these in a half-baked manner.

Chikokuwake, together with Ryūkō, was guided by Gosankō, who is the manifestation of Kototama-wake-no-Mikoto, and after leaving the mansion of Uzuhiko, they were to visit various groups in the intermediate Tenkai. Gosankō walked briskly along a flat path that looked as if it were paved with gold and silver sand, where indescribably beautiful trees were dotted at regular intervals. On both sides of the road, the Sada, Nagata, Takada, and Kubota of the Tenkai unfolded, and the sound of rice leaves rustling in an indescribably cool breeze echoed pleasantly in their ears. The male and female Tennin, while possessing indescribably beautiful countenances, revealed shins as transparent as agate, and while entering the paddy fields to weed, they sang weeding songs in brave voices. Although the sun was shining overhead without being too high, there was no scorching heat like in the Shizenkai, and it was a climate as comfortable as entering a truly pleasant hot spring. Furthermore, there is never winter in this Tenkai; the plants and trees are forever lush, and not even a glimpse of deciduous trees can be found. The surface of the fields is filled with gold and silver colored water, and every time the rice leaves turn in the blue wind, they strike gold and silver waves; it is a scene that defies description by brush or tongue. Harukuniwake-sama, Goran nasaimase, in Tengoku as well, the businesses of Nōkōshiyō are being conducted. And as you see, each person forms an Ichidan and enjoys their work, living a life of Kuwanki. Is it not a truly Yukuwai Kuōkei to behold?

Naruhodo, indeed, the sight of each person forgetting the self and enjoying their work in unison is a state of affairs that one could not even dream of in Genkai. And do those Tennin also own land individually?

Ie-ie, the land is entirely the Kōiu of the Dantai. There are no such detestable systems as the Ōjinushi, Jisakunō, or tenant farmers as in the world on earth; everyone, equal in all things, rejoices that everything is a divine work, sweats their brows, and is active for the sake of the Lord God. And because they find interest in their work, there is not one person who utters a complaint; the heart of one is the heart of another, and the heart of another is the heart of one; each person unites their hearts, believing that everything is a divine work, and they are working away as Yukuwai as you see.

If that is the case, is there no distinction between rich and poor in Tengoku, and is a so-called socialist system being practiced?

There is also a distinction between rich and poor in Tengoku. Even within the same Dantai, there are both the wealthy and the poor. However, wealth and business are separate things.

If one does not receive a reward according to one's labor, there should be no way for a distinction between rich and poor to arise, should there? How could a distinction between rich and poor arise among Tennin who work in the same way and continue their lives by distributing the same things?

In Genkai, everything has become a law of body-dominant and spirit-subordinate. Therefore, it is the way of the natural world that those who are superior and those who work well receive more rewards. In Tengoku, everything belongs to the Lord God, and every Tennin holds the thought that all business is done for the Lord God. Therefore, even if there is a distinction between rich and poor in Tengoku, the poor never resent the rich. Because they live their days in gratitude, thinking that everyone is allowed to work by the grace of the Lord God and is allowed to live by the divine character of the Lord God, the Tennin do not even keep wealth in mind. And wealth is entirely what the Lord God bestows, not something that the Tennin have acquired through their own individual efforts. Depending on the degree of virtuous deeds performed while in the real world, wealth is bestowed upon them in Tengoku in a Kannagara manner. The poor look at the rich, take them as a model, and think only of accumulating virtuous deeds. The wealth in Tengoku is without even a hair's breadth of error, and there is no unfairness. It is bestowed by the Lord God in accordance with that virtue.

What kind of things did the wealthy of Tengoku do while in Genkai?

The wealthiest of the Tengoku Dantai are those who, while in Genkai, understood the Lord God well, performed love for the sake of love, performed good for the sake of good, performed truth for the sake of truth, cast aside self-interest, and devoted their hearts and bodies for the construction of the Kingdom of God, becoming faithful servants of the Lord God; and furthermore, those virtuous people who were fully active in Genkai, offered their surplus wealth to the church, and devoted their hearts and bodies only to the glory of the Lord God and the expansion of the Way are the so-called wealthy of Tengoku. In short, they are the people who accumulated treasures in the storehouse of Tengoku while in Genkai. And the moderately wealthy are those who saved their acquaintances and friends from danger, or spent their wealth for the relief of the public, and accumulated hidden virtues; they have come to Tengoku and have been bestowed with appropriate property by the Great Lord God and are continuing their lives in comfort. And since all the property received in Tengoku is bestowed by the Lord God, one cannot freely bestow it upon other Tennin as in the world on earth. They only use that property for the expenses of the festivals of the Lord God, or invite the Dantai of Tennin to their homes on public holidays, and spend their own funds to prepare feasts and enjoy themselves with many people; therefore, the wealthy of Tengoku are the objects of respect for all.

『成程、此楽園には被面布を透して眺めますれば、種々雑多の樹木、花卉の吾々地上に嘗て見ざるものが沢山ございますな』

§ 4

These beautiful Jiyumoku bloom and bear fruit according to the virtue of love possessed by those who dwell in the correct knowledge of Kami. Does not the Shinyu of the Izu no Mitama also say, 'The plum blossoms that open all at once, opening and scattering to bear fruit, nourish the seeds of Su'? The opening plum blossoms refer to the state corresponding to Chie and Shiyōkaku. When this comes to pass, the Jiyumoku and Kuwaki that bear fruit in the Entei and Rakuen of Tengoku manifest according to the virtue of each Tennin, as offerings to Kami and as seeds of joy for each individual. I have heard that such a Rakuen exists in Takaamahara, but those who truly know this are limited to those who dwell in the virtue of Aizen bestowed by Kami, and those who have not destroyed the light of Tenkai within their own Kiyōchiu through the light of Shizenkai and its falsehoods. Therefore, those whose eyes have not yet seen and whose ears have not yet heard regarding Takaamahara, even if they are currently approaching that place, cannot see this Kuōkei nor hear the sounds of such beautiful Ongaku.
