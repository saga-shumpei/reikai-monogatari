§ 1

The Reigoku and Tengoku of Takaamahara are worlds of Kuōmiyō. That Kuōmiyō is Shinshin in Jitsusei, that is to say, it is Reitekishintekishiyōkaku. This Shinshin Kuōmiyō simultaneously illuminates the Naishi and Guwaishi of the Shiyotennin. Now, Naishi refers to that which is within the heart of the Tennin themselves, and Guwaishi refers to that which is in their eyes. Furthermore, the Shiyotennin are enveloped in the heat of the love of Takaamahara. That is to say, this heat is Shinzen, or Shinai, in Jitsusei, and the Jiyōdau and Guwanmau that we possess, which lead us to enter ever more deeply into Shiyōkaku, come from this heat. In short, the Reigoku and Tengoku of Takaamahara are the gathering place of Manzen. The degree of Shiyōkaku of the Tennin is not something that the Kōzetsu of Genkaijin can fully exhaust. Even that which Ningen cannot exhaust though they expend itsusengen, the Tennin are able to explain with sūgen. Moreover, the fact that immeasurable and infinite secret meanings are contained within even a single word or phrase of the Tennin cannot be expressed by the characters belonging to the language of Ningen. What the Tennin cannot sufficiently express with the so-called words used in their language, they supplement with yūgen and subtle tones. And by those tones, they express Jiyōdau, and the various concepts within the thoughts derived from Jiyōdau are expressed by them through words. The Shin-yu of the Ōmoto Kaiso also contains secret meanings that are by no means easily understood by the wisdom and Shiyōkaku of modern people. However, when a Tennin who possesses wisdom and Shiyōkaku reads them, they can immediately perceive that immeasurable and infinite secret meanings are contained therein. And regarding these words, as the situation is shown in the place called 'First Tengoku' in the second series, third volume (fifteenth volume) of the Reikai Monogatari, please refer to it. Therefore, Ningen must realize that unless they temper their spirits into Zen and Shin, and while still living, hold a place in the organization of Takaamahara, the Shin-yu of Ōmoto cannot be easily interpreted. Since the Shin-yu of Ōmoto is something in which the Kokuso Ōkunitokotachi-no-Mikoto manifested as Genrei, filled the spirits of Wakahimegimi-no-Mikoto, Kunitakehiko-no-Mikoto, and others with their divinity, and came to the physical body of the Deguchi Kaiso, who is a prophet holding a place in the organization of the Tennin, to transmit the direct teachings of the Ōkami, it is impossible to understand it unless one possesses a high degree of Zentoku and wisdom and Shiyōkaku. However, because the Lord God is of supreme benevolence and supreme love, in order to make the secret meanings of this Shin-yu easily understood by the external Ningen of the natural world, the Lord God played out the divine plan of filling the spirit with the divinity of Zuirei, causing it to come to the physical body of a Henseijoshi, and through those hands and through that mouth, to make the truth of the Reikai understood.

The reason why each word and phrase of the Ōmoto Shin-yu contains matters that should lead a person to internal Shiyōkaku is that the language of the Tennin filled with divinity, that is, the Motoshugojin, corresponds to Jiyōdau, and each word corresponds to a concept. Also, the words of the Tennin change infinitely according to the direct state of the things contained within their thoughts. Furthermore, the Tennin of the inner realm can perceive and thoroughly know the entire life of a person through the voice of the speaker and the few words that they speak. This is because the Tennin observe the state in which the voice changes in various ways according to the various concepts contained within those words, and by this, they know what the love, faith, and wisdom and Shiyōkaku that the person holds as primary are. Even among Ningen of the Genkai, those who have a little wisdom, Shiyōkaku, and are fair and selfless hold their place in the organization of the Tennin while still living, so they can discern the destiny of a person's life through the meaning contained in the words and phrases of the other party. The thoughts and Jiyōdau of Ningen appear in their voice and appear on their skin, and in no way can they be hidden before a spiritual wise man or sage. 'This word contains love,' or 'This phrase is sincere,' or 'That phrase is courage,' or 'This phrase is wisdom'—everything appears and disappears even in a single word or phrase, and no matter what saint or sage it may be, one cannot hide the thoughts in one's heart before one who possesses wisdom and Shiyōkaku; this is the reason why divine authority cannot be covered up in any way. Those who have no evil or greed in their hearts and are filled with the virtue of Zen have their wisdom developed accordingly, and because the changes in their Jiyōdau are extremely active, it is easy to see through to the very bottom of the other party's heart and know it thoroughly; but if there is greed in the heart, if one harbors evil and has selfishness, then their Jiyōdau becomes dull, their wisdom declines, their will becomes deranged, and it is not easy to see through the heart of the other party. Therefore, those who are deceived by others are all so because their hearts are filled with evil, greed, and selfishness. A saint or a person of virtue who is filled with the virtue of Aizen and filled with the light of Shinsin is never deceived by evil people who are lost in the physical desires of the natural world. In short, it is because greedy Ningen who are self-centered are deceived by greedy evil people according to the law of correspondence, and invite failures that cannot be undone.

Now, the two propaganda generals, the hypocrites Ranch and Katahiko, were to be driven into hell by the arrangement of the god Ibukido-nushi-no-kami. Gariya and Kesu also followed after them. They were forced by the guards through a gap in a large rock and fell into a pitch-black hole. A tunnel sloped downward with severe twists and turns. The four of them descended step by step, feeling as if they were being pushed from behind, through a dark path where they did not know when they might strike their heads against the rock walls if they did not feel their way with both hands, or when they might stumble if they did not pay attention to their feet. If they tried to straighten their backs even a little, they were blocked by the rock wall above their heads. They fell deeper and deeper, endlessly feeling their way along a path that smelled of an indescribable stench, hunched over like shrimp. The four of them finally arrived at a place that was slightly brighter. There, about six holes, just large enough for a human to crawl through, were lined up like peepholes. Guards with blue and red faces stood by each one, and without saying a word, they forcibly shoved the four of them into the same hole. The area was filled with a foul odor that induced vomiting, a total unpleasantness, but the four of them actually felt relieved; they twitched their noses at the smell of rotting flesh and the scent of manure, and as they sniffed it with pleasure, they sighed as if they were finally at ease, and once again they went down, thumping along a tunnel that was slightly brighter than before, turning to the right and left. The four of them finally arrived at a slightly wider place. Looking, they saw a large river lying there. And a long, narrow bridge was built across it. About twenty underworld officials with stern faces were waiting there, armed. One of the underworld officials stepped forward and approached them.

とせき立て睨みつける。四人は四肢五体の力何時しかスツカリ抜けて了ひ、手足がブルブルと震ひ戦き、満足に歩く事も出来なくなつてゐた。さうして此橋には罪人橋と橋詰に立札が立つてゐる。其長さは目の届かぬばかり殆ど数百町に及んでゐる。さうして橋の幅が僅かに一尺ばかり、一寸体の平均を失つたが最後、真逆様に百尺以上の川に落ち込まねばならぬ。さうして其水の深さは地球の中心に達して居ると伝へられ、幾千万丈の深さとも分らない。此橋には欄もなく、加ふるにヒヨヒヨとして上下左右に揺り動く、実に危険な橋である。さうして橋の下には激流が飛沫をとばし赤黒い汚穢の水が流れてゐる。さうして何とも形容の出来ぬ怪物が沢山に棲み、橋の上を通行するものが過つて落ちて来るのを、大口を開けて待つて居るその恐ろしさ。一目見ても身慄ひする様である。さうして橋の上には膚を劈く如き寒風吹き、何とも云へぬ厭らしき声、八方より聞えて来るのであつた。

ランチは余りの恐ろしさに身体すくみ、ビリビリ慄うて居ると冥官の一人は、

『サア、ランチ将軍、其方は現界に於ては立派なるバラモンの宣伝将軍ではなかつたか。沢山の敵味方の命をとつたる英雄豪傑でありながら、何故これしきの橋が恐ろしいのか。サア早く向ふへ渡れ』

と厳命した。ランチは震ひ声を出して、

『ならぬならぬ、決して霊界に於ては汝等に斯かる責苦を与へ、之を以て快楽としてゐるのではない。大神様を初め、すべてのエンゼルも冥官も、一人なりとも天国へ上り得る身魂の来り得る事を待つてゐるのだ。否唯一の歓喜としてゐるのだ。汝自らの罪業によつて汝自ら此罪人橋を渡るべく準備致し、又汝永久の住家を向ふの地獄に作りおいた以上は、汝の身魂は、其処まで行かねばならぬ。可愛さうなれど吾々は救ふ事が出来ぬ。汝は神の愛を信じて自ら天国を開くべき処を、自然界の欲に精霊を汚し、斯くも浅猿しき身の上となつたのだから、自縄自縛と諦めて行つてくれ』

と流石の冥官も憐愍の情に堪へかねてか、両眼より涙をポロポロと流してゐる。

Yotahiko: 'Must we truly go to Jigoku?'

Guard: 'Looking at the Reii of you four, there still remains a somewhat Oboroge Ensau. Your Meisū is not yet due to come to Meido, but because there has been a Gomeirei from Ibukidonushi-no-Kamisama, I must let you pass here regardless. It is Fushigisenban that four Shiyabakusa Maujiya should come here, all four of you.'

He tilts his head and wears a Shiangaho.

At this place, a troop of Garigarimaujiya, reduced to mere bone and skin, appeared in numbers beyond counting, like fish bones scattered about, swarming and writhing. They faced the two generals, Ranch and Katahiko, and with voices indescribably loathsome, they pressed upon them from Zengosaiu with a terrifying Mushiyabu. Gariya and Kesu were also surrounded by equally loathsome Kuwaibutsu, screaming and crying out in agony.

At this moment, the song of the numbers of heaven was heard faintly from nowhere. In the blink of an eye, the Garigarimaujiya vanished like smoke. Then, the figures of the many stern Meikan began to decrease one by one, then two by two, gradually diminishing in number. It seemed the voice of the Sendenka was drawing nearer, for it could be heard growing louder and louder. The surroundings, which had been extremely dim, gradually brightened as if a thin layer of paper had been peeled away.
