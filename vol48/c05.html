Chapter 5: 鞘当 [1259]

鞘当〔1259〕

§ 1

When Shiyōgun Ranchi returned in a hurry to his inner Ima, he found Kiyoteruhime, Hatsuwakahime, and Shiyōgun Katahiko sitting in a circle like a three-fold tomoegata around a Hibachi, whispering and laughing together. Seeing this, Shiyōgun Ranchi could not contain his jealousy; he instantly drew his sword, intending to strike Shiyōgun Katahiko down with a Nashiwari cut, but thinking that the two women would surely find him an unrefined man, he suppressed his Haradachi with a great effort and stepped into the room, pretending to know nothing. Yet, he felt a violent Shindō in his lips, his hands, and even to the tips of his feet. When one reaches the Chiyōjiyō of anger, the entire Zenshin is bound to tremble violently. Seeing Shiyōgun Ranchi enter, Katahiko lowered his eyes and said:

Katahiko: 'Ah, Shiyōgundono, where have you been? I have been led by the hands of these two nice ladies, overwhelmed by Umazake, and have become quite Meitei. Please forgive my Goburei.'

Ranchi: 'I do not mean to reproach you, but as a Shiyōgun who holds the noble Shiyokuken to command the Sangun, you should observe proper Sahafu. To be dallying with women while I am away, what kind of behavior is this? Please show some restraint.'

Katahiko: 'Ah, your words are quite Gomotsuto. I, too, stand in an important position where I must set an example for my subordinates, and I am not so weak as to have my heart melted by women. However, as these two have poured such passionate love upon me, I felt it would not be the way of a man to cast them aside, so I came here led by them, much to my own trouble. Alas, even the firm Katahiko cannot resist the magical power of women; my bones and joints have loosened, and I have become completely Gatahiko. Until a moment ago, these nice ladies seemed to be offering their passionate love to you, but now, as the cold snow falls outside, it seems their affection for you has also grown cold. I would like to have one of them serve your needs, but for some reason, both of them shake their heads and say they will neither serve Shiyōgun Ranchiki nor be served by him. Alas, this Katahiko is troubled by it all. Aha, ha, ha!'

Kiyoteruhime: 'My, Shiyōgun Ranchi-sama, where have you been? You cannot imagine how much I have been looking for you.'

Ranchi felt as if he had been brought back to life by this voice; his complexion became valiant, and he suddenly regained his spirits.

Ranchi: 'Ah, Kiyoteruhime-dono, I am truly sorry. In fact, there was a matter regarding military affairs that I had to investigate, so I stepped away for a while.'

Kiyoteruhime: 'Shiyōgun-sama, that must be a lie. You must have hidden somewhere because you were tired of me. I am so disappointed, an-an-an, on-on-on.'

Ranchi: 'Ehe, he, he, hey Katahiko-dono, what do you think? She is quite adorable, is she not?'

Katahiko: 'Now, now, Kiyoteruhime-dono, have you changed your mind again?'

Kiyoteruhime: 'Since Shiyōgun Ranchi-sama opened those large eyes of his and sent me electric waves—no, telegrams—do I not have an obligation to send a reply (change my heart)? Ho, ho, ho, ho.'

Katahiko: 'Ah, there is nothing to be done. In any case, it is no use for Katahiko to keep two beauties by his side and monopolize the nice ladies all to himself. Perhaps Kiyoteruhime changing her mind is also a dispensation of Heaven. Well, Kiyoteruhime, I shall exercise my magnanimous and valiant heart and leave you to Shiyōgun Ranchi. From this moment on, you should put Katahiko out of your mind and devote your fidelity to Shiyōgun Ranchi.'

Kiyoteruhime: 'Oho, ho, ho, how self-serving that Katahiko-sama is; he should have some limit to his vanity. To tell me to forget someone I never even thought of—my, what a vain man he is. How detestable. Men are truly creatures of such great vanity.'

Katahiko: 'Ranchi-dono, thou art surely Gomanzoku. Een, een, Setsushiya shall make a great Jiyōho and be Manzoku with the younger Hatsuwakahime. I pray thee, acknowledge Setsushiya's Gariyō.'

Ranchi: 'Otsushiya whatever thou wishest Katsute; both women are Setsushiya's. Hen, Bakabaka! Thinking Setsushiya would remain Dama, thou hast become puffed up; there is a limit to being Zuzu.'

Katahiko: 'Otsushiyare not so, Ranchi-dono! Setsushiya did nothing at all; the woman sent Shiuha herself and requested the Yakusoku. Since Setsushiya offereth to entrust her to Anata, Wareware ought to receive Kanshiya instead. I have no wish to hear such a Goaisatsu as if thou hadst rubbed thy nose against an enoki tree. Come, Hatsuwakahime-dono, rather than staying with such an unreasonable Shiyōgun, let us repair to my Ima; I shall love Anata for Eikiu.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Eee, how disagreeable! When did I ever love Anata? I love the person whom my elder sister loveth. Gomen-kudasaimase!'

Katahiko: 'Thinking the Yōki outside had changed, even Hatsuwakahime-sama's Hanaiki hath changed! Ha-ha, um, I understand—she is ashamed! She is putting on airs in Hitomahe. Um, um, all right, all right, what a Kaai thing she is!'

Thus he muttered in the back of his mouth. Hatsuwakahime, hearing this voice with her Eibin ears, said:

Hatsuwakahime: 'Moshi, Katahiko-sama, pray do not say such things as "what a Kaai thing she is." I feel sick at heart.'

Ranchi: 'Ahahaha, Katahiko-dono, how is that? Dost thou know the saying that a mature woman's charm pricketh sharp like a thorn? Ahahaha!'

Katahiko: 'Chotsu, ee-ei!'

Ranchi: 'Katahiko-dono, is not "Chotsu, ee-ei" a Goburei? It is the command of thy superior officer! Retreat from this place!'

Katahiko, unable to resist this single decisive command from above, uttered:

Katahiko: 'Heee!'

While making a reluctant reply, he left his heart behind with the two women, sprang out into the next room, tightened his upper and lower teeth behind the sliding door, popped his lips open, and cried:

Katahiko: 'Iin!'

Striking the empty air two or three times with his fist, he muttered:

Katahiko: 'Chotsu! Calling it the command of a superior officer—chotsu, he maketh a fool of me! But it cannot be helped. I shall also go have a glass of warm sake, making Otami my companion.'

So saying, he returned dejectedly.

Ranchi pushed Katahiko out of the room, sat cross-legged on a folding stool between the two beauties like a coxcomb, narrowed his eyes, and said:

Ranchi: 'Now then, Kiyoteruhime-dono, avoiding the eyes of this Ranchi, didst thou not engage in what they call Ishindenshin with Katahiko before anyone knew it?'

Kiyoteruhime: 'Hai, there was no such thing in particular. However, while I also like Anata, I took a fancy to the thoughts held by Katahiko-sama; therefore, though Katahiko-sama matters little to me, I thought it a new idea and fell in love with those thoughts. If Anata would hold the same thoughts as my thoughts, there could be no greater joy. In truth, toward Anata I love physical beauty, while toward Katahiko-sama I love his thoughts.'

Ranchi: 'What kind of thoughts are Katahiko's new thoughts? As for thoughts, even I rummage through advanced new books, so I do not intend to lose to Katahiko. What in the world is he saying?'

Kiyoteruhime: 'Hai, wondering what Katahiko-sama's thoughts were, I probed into them, and found them to be truly captivating thoughts. To put it in a nutshell, he is an advocate of the unnecessary armaments theory, an advocate of military abolition, and wisheth to lead a peaceful, aesthetic life—truly new thoughts.'

Ranchi: 'Did Katahiko say such things while being registered in the military? That is extremely improper... no, it is a thought that entirely gaineth my assent. Mm, and what did he say regarding martial arts?'

Shiyōgunsama: 'Is there any Goyō for me?'

Shiyōgunsama: 'Yes, today is a rare Ōyuki. The Shihau is an Ichimen Ginsekai; I shall hold a Yukimi banquet, so you Mahetachi must accompany me. And tell them to bring five or six Kago.'

The two Fukukuwan said, 'Hai,' and immediately left the place and departed.

Ranchi ordered his Jiyūsotsu to search for the Arika of Āku, Tāru, Ekisu, Imoriwake, and others, intending to take them along for the Yukimi, but because they were buried in the Orikara Sekisetsu, he was unable to Hatsuken them. At this time, Otami was invited to the Ima of Katahiko Shiyōgun, and while fidgeting with her knees, she was spending time looking pained at the various meaningful talks of Katahiko. Ranchi Shiyōgun, seeing that the Yonin were not to be seen, thought that they must have gone to the Kauguwai with the intention of doing some Yukimi, and accompanied by two Fukukuwan and two Bijin, he proceeded toward the watchtower while being rocked in a Kago with a Goninzu. With the Sekisetsu about two shaku deep on the ground, the footsteps of the Kago-bearers made a sound like a groom cutting horse feed with a straw cutter, going 'zaku, zaku, zaku,' and while imprinting many Subaru stars on the beautiful snowy path, they finally arrived safely at the watchtower. Here, they prepared charcoal fire, warmed sake, and held a Yukimi banquet.

On the other hand, Katahiko, having heard through the secret report of a Jiyūsotsu that Ranchi Shiyōgun was holding a Yukimi banquet at the watchtower accompanied by two women, and thinking that Imoriwake and others were likely accompanying him as well, ordered two Kago and, together with Otami, flew through the air and proceeded toward the watchtower.
