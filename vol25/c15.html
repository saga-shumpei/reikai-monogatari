Chapter 15: 改心の実 [761]

改心の実〔761〕

§ 1

Ōriyōhime, Umekohime, Tomohiko, Teresuhime, and Mukadehime, the five of them together, decided to leave Chionjiyō hundreds of ri behind, cross the mountain paths, and proceed to the Dragon Palace Castle of Lake Suha in Tamanohara.

Behind them, they left the core staff including Samori, the Smartborō couple, Umori Tsurukō, Kuwanshiu, Takekō, Māru, and Miyūzu to serve as the resident division commanders; equipped with light disguises of grass raincloaks and bamboo hats, and striking with taro tree branches, they ascended and descended the rugged, serpentining mountain paths of rocks and undulations, leaped across valleys and traversed ravines, and at last arrived at the village where Tomohiko of Jiyanna held sway.

Led by a fierce man like a demon, one whose face was adorned all over with blue tattooing upon a copper-colored countenance, the young and old, men and women, with serious faces, welcomed the party of Ōriyōhime while raising war cries of 'Oooh, oooh!'.

This village, shrouded in a forest dark even by day, consisted at first glance of a race resembling demons, yet they were extremely rustic, honest, and rich in faith.

The villagers, who revered and respected the crooked-nosed, red Tomohiko as a heaven-sent savior, gazed upon the jade-defying, pure figures of Ōriyōhime and Umekohime like heavenly maidens, firmly believing that they had descended from the Plain of High Heaven to Mount Nelson riding a bird-boat, and that they had been welcomed and brought back by Tomohiko and his wife, the saviors of the village of Jiyanna.

Simultaneously spreading out large, black hands that looked like coal shovels from a sugar shop,

Indigenous Villagers: 'Utsu-potsupo utsu-potsupo, ōrensu, sāchiraisu, tārensu, chītā chītā'

They shouted thus and expressed their welcome.

The meaning of this was as follows: 'Are you gods, heavenly messengers, or perhaps the god of light who saves us? Truly a magnificent great savior has descended to this village.

We shall no doubt never again encounter affliction, and will be able to taste the joys of the Heavenly Pure Land unto eternity.

That tree nuts will bear fruit abundantly and we shall bathe in the grace of beating our bellies and singing in joy is clearer than seeing fire.

How grateful, how awe-inspiring, how precious, how joyous.

We villagers shall offer the utmost of our strength and the depths of our hearts to this living god.'

Tomohiko, revered as the savior of the village of Jiyanna, turned to the villagers and shouted,

'Tārisuto, teretāin, hāruēsu, ōrensu, sāchiraisu, kāteru, raido'

At this voice, everyone prostrated themselves on the earth, shed tears of joy, and rejoiced.

Tomohiko once again raised his hands and shouted,

'Hāru hāru'

At this, the multitude of natives placed the party onto handcarts, and with a demeanor akin to carrying portable shrines, talking about incomprehensible matters, they sent them deep into a somewhat spacious hall where the thirty-five gods were enshrined.

The party of Ōriyōhime was invited deep into Tomohiko's hall, treated to various rare fruits, and while smacking their lips at the taste of bananas, they sojourned here for a day or two; after bestowing baptism upon the villagers by Ōriyōhime and Umekohime and teaching them propaganda songs, they were seen off by dozens of villagers, and the party of five safely arrived at last at the open ground of Tamanegahara.

Eating tree nuts along the way, drinking valley water, and using banana leaves as sleeping mats, while bestowing Kototama upon flocks of ferocious beasts and giant snakes to make them submit and obey, they finally reached this wilderness where the sands of gold and silver glittered.

The party sat properly before a small shrine built on the shore of Lake Suha, recited the heavenly congratulatory prayers, and spent the night in the nearby coconut tree forest.

The great sun, appearing from behind the rear of Mount Estan, reflected upon the fish-scale waves of the waters of Lake Suha like spreading out mats of gold and silver; its beauty was incomparable to anything, and the five members of the party purified their bodies in the lake water, practiced ritual purification here for seven days and seven nights, and gave thanks for divine grace.

The setting sun was already sloping, and unspeakably beautiful bird calls echoed as they sought their roosts and returned to their respective dense forests.

Large birds with pure white wings pierced the darkness and appeared low from twilight onwards, soaring across the lake surface in all directions.

Their numbers were countless, running into many tens of millions, presenting a sight bright enough even on a moonless midnight.

These were the ancestors of the albatross, large birds called anborī.

So saying with tears of joy floating in his eyes, Tomohiko, overflowing with such a burden of gladness that it was more than his body could bear, prostrated himself upon the earth unable even to raise his face, and choked with tears of joy and repentance.

Mukadehime placed her hands before her in reverence before Umekohime,

Mukadehime: 'Umekohime-sama, please, I beg of you, forgive all my rudeness until now. As you well know, I was an evil person like an old demon-hag, who exhausted every limit of ruthless and lawless wickedness. When I went to Chionjiyō and witnessed the advancement of my daughter, a haughty mind unconsciously arose within me, and furthermore, driven by feelings of attachment, I relegated the essential Ōkami to second place, and moreover, I am a blind-hearted one who always faced you with eyes of contempt. At the time when the banquet was held for the sake of Tomohiko in Chionjiyō, you stood upon the purple Rengeiha and sang of my true nature, yet within my heart I harbored extreme dissatisfaction. Now when I think of it, within those words of yours at that time was the great merciful and compassionate saving heart of the Ōkami... why could I not realize it at that moment? Being completely color-blind toward all creation, I have committed only blunders and have no words to offer in apology to the Ōkami, nor to you, venerable Umekohime. Please, have pity upon us mother and child, and I earnestly beg you to intercede so that we may apologize for our deep sins of having opposed the Ōkami until now.'

As she burst out into a loud voice and wept prostrate, Umekohime smiled gently,

Umekohime: 'Ah, Mukadehime-sama, you have today and this very moment become for the first time a true pillar of God; that is splendid. From now on, I sincerely hope that you will devote your heart and soul with me to the service of the 35 Ōkami. Whatever sins, defilements, or transgressions there may be, Umekohime will take upon herself the thousand seats of purification, so please rest at ease.'

Mukadehime could only say, 'Thank you,' and pressing her head against the earth, she sank into sobs of grateful tears. Teles-hime once again placed both hands before Umekohime,

Teles-hime: 'No matter how many unwitting rudenesses or unpleasantries someone as sinful as myself may have committed, I beg you to pray to the divine realm so that you will somehow forgive me.'

So she implored, pressing her palms together in prayer.

Umekohime: 'You are a child of God whose spirit and soul are the purest, even among all here. Today you have committed no particular improprieties against the divine realm. I hope that from now on, just as before, without error, you will continue to serve in the work of God.'

When she thus replied, Teles-hime could only squeeze out grateful tears at Umekohime's words of loving compassion.

Umekohime turned toward the lake's surface, pressed her palms together, and silently offered a dark prayer for a short while; suddenly, subtle music was heard from nowhere, sealing the northwestern sky, and flying toward this place with the momentum of a thousand ri at a single dash, those previous Ambories, numbering in the hundreds, flew back side by side, aiming for the lake's surface—a beauty so exquisite it could not be painted even in a picture.
