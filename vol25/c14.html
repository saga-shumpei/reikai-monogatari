園遊会〔760〕

§ 1

Though one island of Australia at present is almost entirely summery, in this era the distinction of spring, summer, autumn, and winter was slightly preserved.

The daytime was almost of the same heat throughout the year, but the climate of morning, evening, and night naturally manifested the distinctions of the four seasons.

To speak in terms of our country, it was almost about the height of late summer, and a grand garden party was held for the sake of Tomohiko and his wife in a horse-riding ground where ancient trees, enclosing the sky outside the castle gates, grew luxuriantly far and near.

Chionkiyō, surrounded on all sides by mountains, was, as a flatland, the highest plateau in the entire island.

Mountain streams flowed north and south, and with rugged rocks standing like walls in every valley, it was a scenic land of peerless and supreme views.

Among the scenic spots on the single island, one counts first the lake of Suwa, and secondly the vicinity of the waterfall basin of Kushi in the village of Hiru.

However, the gorge of Hiru was extremely narrow in area with almost no flatland, and compared to Chionkiyō, it was incomparable in terms of its size, breadth, and narrowness.

The points that the land was high and the breeze pure, furthermore its area was broad and the great trees were verdant, were acclaimed as the foremost in the entire island.

In the forest of the plaza outside the gates, rocks of interesting shapes in red, white, black, blue, and crimson here and there raised their heads from the ground, and at a glance, one would think great flowers had bloomed forth from the ground.

The majority of the rocks formed a natural shape resembling blossoming lotus flowers, and the country people simply called them the Lotus Rocks, or also named them the Lotus Riding Ground.

Kōryūhime and several hundred people moved hither and thither, engaging in various chosen games, singing, dancing, playing stone flutes, striking stones, the sounds of the single-stringed, two-stringed, and three-stringed kotos resounding clearly, while the sounds of transverse flutes, vertical flutes, and percussion gongs were most bustling, involuntarily intoxicating their bodies into heaven, struck with a sensation as if the wonderful-voiced bodhisattvas had come to play music.

Umekohime climbed upon the highest purple lotus rock in the center and sang an interesting song to add to the merriment.

Takekō, seeing this mirage for the first time, cried out 'Ah!' in astonishment, and wishing to report it to Ōriyōhime, stumbling and rolling over, staggering this way and that, walking in a group of eight, concealed his figure deep within, while all gazed up at the heavens, struck with wonder at the magnificent mirage, and amused themselves with the thought that it was a diversion for the garden party.
