Chapter 13: 握手の涙 [759]

握手の涙〔759〕

§ 1

Tenon is rich with divine grace, and Chionjiyō shines like eternal spring, autumn, and winter under a perpetual summer scenery.

The leaves of the trees are radiant and lush, while fruits ripen abundantly, allowing all to struggle for the path of clothing and food without suffering any hardships or lacking in this single island.

Leaving Kenonkiyō behind, Tomohiko barricaded himself in Seirontau, leaving behind his beloved wife in her chamber, and crossed the vast ocean.

With eyes like cormorants and hawks, he watched and targeted the destination of Koitohime, wandering from island to island in the prime of his manhood.

Having opened the path of Brahman in his quest for her whereabouts, Tomohiko, aided by the power of time, encountered Mukadehime amidst the perilous sea straits, where waves float eternally like firm and unchanging bedrock.

In the rocky cave of Kunishiroyama on Shōdo Island, renowned even by name, the joy and fear of meeting were overwhelming, as the chief of the Sumoto estate was bound by the hands of the arresting officers.

With no excuses left to offer on Awajishima, through the compassion of the couple Tosuke, the sins they had committed vanished like ephemeral water bubbles into a small fishing boat.

Kiyotake, Tsuru, and the three of them crossed the waves of the Bakan Strait, dashing their boat against the Senbiki Rock at a time when their lives were in peril.

Rescued from their precious lives by the party of Tamawakehihiko, the divine officiant of Azuma (Mikkyō/Izuruhome), Mukadehime and Takahime drifted ashore at Ambon.

When they landed at the supreme dragon palace of the South Seas, what awaited them there?

Reuniting with Mukadehime, the birth mother of Koitohime, what excuses could there be as they rode over rough wave after rough wave, finally landing by the skin of their teeth with the party of Mukadehime on the floating island of Australia?

When they arrived and looked upon the residence of Koitohime in Chionjiyō, they were mercilessly beaten and driven out by the heartless country folk.

Lurking in the nearby woods while experiencing the joy of reunion with the husband of Kōryūhime's dwelling, the dream abruptly shattered before they could even fully rejoice.

When they looked around, they found themselves upon a rock, with even their hip bones shattered, and in the misery of being unable to move their bodies, they barely rested their breath and offered the divine words of Azuma with utmost sincerity.

As they proclaimed it, the grace of Gods instantly manifested; their bodies grew upright, and making their way over the windy ridge, they reached the summit of Nelson's Peak.

When they looked back and gazed behind them, beyond a thousand ri of clouds, wondering where Chionjiyō could possibly be, they stared wide-eyed and yearned.

At that very moment, caught in a violent gale, Tomohiko was swept away by the wind, tossed about like a wingless falcon or kite—what could a body without wings possibly do?

He plummeted into the village of Janna, and while he was unconscious, the villagers living in this place strangely drew close to his side, and upon looking closely, recognized him as the savior they had long and anxiously awaited.

Despite being somewhat crooked, his red nose gleamed as he rejoiced with courage and leapt like a sparrow, carried back to the headquarters of the Jannah religion in a spectacle of sheer amusement.

Cherished by Terushihiko (Terrus), the divine officiant of the Jannah religion, here they shared a new bridal pillow, and as months and years accumulated, the transforming power of Azuma spread throughout the village of Janna.

The divine teachings of the Thirty-Five (Izuru) Moon rose with the momentum of the morning sun, expanding and flourishing in all four directions.

Tomohiko and his wife resolved in their hearts to go before the presence of Kōryūhime, who caused the flowers of divine virtue to bloom in Chionjiyō, to apologize for the deep sins that had once troubled the mother and daughter, Mukadehime and Koitohimé, in times past.

Recounting the past circumstances in detail to Terushihiko and finally gaining the understanding of his wife, the couple set out together, bidding a brief farewell to the people of Janna, marching valiantly without even a single companion.

Ah, how truly divine! Receiving the sacred imperial progress, Tomohiko, who repented of his past wrongs, knelt before the mother and daughter, clearing away the clouds of his heart, and served as a foundational pillar of Azuma in that pure and noble tale of ancient times.

From the divine water and fire of the Gods, the Mizu-no-Mitama was assembled; like a phonograph of crude mass production operated by a clumsy hand turning the crank, the out-of-tune record began to rotate once more.

Ah, how truly divine! May the spirits multiply and prosper in abundance! O heavenly deities, earthly deities, and great deities of Ōyahishima, before the presence of the divine life, we revere and humbly pray, smoothly and clearly spinning the threads of the tangled romance between Kōryūhime and Tomohiko.

Tomohiko and his wife, invited by Koitohimé, proceeded deep into the inner sanctuary. Hearing of Tomohiko's visit, Mukadehime, whose heart danced with joy, along with Smartball and the rest of the company, greeted them with an expression that seemed a mix of curiosity and aversion, standing in a semi-crouched posture. Kōryūhime firmly grasped Tomohiko's hand and shook it two or three times.
