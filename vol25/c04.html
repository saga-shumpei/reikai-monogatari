Chapter 4: 望の縁 [750]

望の縁〔750〕

§ 1

Born as a precious child of Ame and Chi, Uzuhime, the queen of the Castle of Chion and the kamutsukasa of Ananai-kyō, bound by the kototama of Ōriyōhime, made her murakimo chest leap, wept secretly, pressed by the shimegi of giri and onai, and without even a moment to spare, she shivered in her body and fell prostrate.

Whether by fortune or misfortune, Tsurukō, the beloved man who served as the umorinokami of Ananahi, hearing her voice, came running up, and with tender nursing, he stroked down his chest with a sigh of relief, feeling as though he had been yomigahe-ed, and even before he had time to rejoice, at the footsteps heard in the rauha outside the room,

Recovering their wits, both of them altered their appearance, straightened their collars, feigned an attitude of ignorance, and looked into each other's faces, letting wild waves beat against their chests.

Kiyokō of Samori, pushing open the partition door violently and entering, upon seeing the figures of the two, instantly changed his complexion, hooked his eyes upward, blew strong breath through his nose, made the tatami mat rough, and with a heavy thump took a seat in the middle of the two,

Kiyokō: 'Aha-ha-ha-ha, my dear two, right in the midst of your cozy enjoyment, an intruder has popped out and I have truly done a rude thing. They say clouds on the moon and a storm on the flowers—I, Samori no Kami, a tasteless man, that such an intimate romance is being fully displayed, have no means of knowing as a being not a god, and with utmost awe... oh my... I humbly suppose. To the right Imoyama and to the left Seyama, flowing by splitting the bond of Imose, the time of all times being the Aganogawa, likened even to the flowers of a thousand sheets at a glance, precious figure of Yoshikohime-sama, I have truly been rude. Please cozily take each other by the hand in hand, and by all means enjoy yourselves. Now... now, what a hateful fellow has dropped by, wah-ha-ha-ha.'

Opening his mouth wide, shaking his shoulders, and causing a small earthquake on the tatami, Tsurukō turned stern,

Tsurukō: 'This is strange, an unexpected suspicion from Samori no Kami; the space between the two of us is as pure as crystal, and I beg for your most impartial judgment.'

Kiyokō: 'Aha-ha-ha-ha, say it, say it; with clever official phrasing, to shamelessly bury away this inescapable state of affairs and cover up your own blunder, the clever eloquent speech of Umori-dono—oh, it is no longer something we can reach. Brandishing such torrent-like eloquence, with skillful words and flattering expressions, making Uzuhime, who had been so firm in her dōshin, into a jewel in the palm of her hand, and Tsurukō-sama, who takes pride in being the greatest romantic in the realm—at his skill, I step back three paces and feel a mix of admiration and indignation.'

Deliberately bringing his head down to the tatami and putting both hands on the ground, he prostrated himself maliciously. Tsurukō, biting his teeth in chagrin, trembled in body, made his breath heave, stretched his elbows, moved his lips violently, tensed his facial muscles, and sat stiffly and squarely.

Kiyokō: 'Aha-ha-ha-ha, one who is firm before people is most soft before a woman where no one is present; truly, one who is weak before gods is most strong before people, and one who is strong before people goes before gods and is most weak—so they say, and oh, I have listened to and observed practical education in person. Such a resourceful... oh, scheming Umorinokami, the Castle of Chion where I wait in the second seat is banzai, oh my, how joyous, how joyous. ...This is strange, Uzuhime-dono, meeting the man you love day and year, and moreover enjoying yourself to your heart's content, what are you lacking that you weep? Oh no, was it tears from an excess of joy? For a blockhead, bloodless and ruthless person like me, the qualifications to speak of romance are totally nonexistent. Being shown a grateful sight, Samori is truly satisfied. From here, I shall have an audience with the Queen, the two of us shall act as go-betweens, and Uzuhime shall be the wife of Tsurukō-sama's lodge, bound together in the vow of sharing the same grave for eternity to come. Oh, what a thing it is that human beings want to be born as handsome men; truly, it is the height of envying the mountain camellia—the flowers bloom but the fruit does not form, and that which does not fruit is a vain flower, and the custom that flowers scatter from the treetop while half-opened is something that often happens in this world; by all means, you two, being on your guard is of the utmost importance. This is the unfeigned and sincere warning of Kiyokō toward the two of you. Oh my, it is a joyous matter, not a bad feeling at all. Waha-ha-ha.'

§ 2

Shrugging his shoulders wildly and intentionally kicking the hibachi about, attempting to bolt from this place in a single bound, he heard a multitude of footsteps outside the room,

It is the entry of the Jiyōusama. Uzuhime, go out to welcome her.

At this booming voice, Kiyokō straightened his collar and sat down politely in the lower seat. Tsurukō returned the hibachi that Kiyokō had kicked away to its original place, scooped up the scattered ash in his hand, took out a broom in a flurry, and was sweeping the ash to one side. No sooner had this moment arrived than that moment came; bursting through the door, Ōriyōhime entered leisurely, her face brimming with smiles, seated herself quietly in the space of the high seat, and looked down upon the faces of the three.

Uzuhime: 'My, oh my, Jiyōusama, how very good of you to honor us with your presence. Just as I was hoping to offer you our reply promptly, an unexpected visitor arrived, causing me to become somewhat late and troubling you once again with your footsteps—please forgive this impropriety of mine.'

She greeted her with utmost courtesy. Hearing these words, Ōriyōhime gently shook her head.

Kiyokō: 'My, oh my, Ōriyōhime-sama, how graciously you have entered. As you have just witnessed, with a faultlessness so extreme as to forget the duties of the Migimori, pushing aside the welcoming of Jiyōusama's arrival, and as you see, sweeping things out with a broom to inflict infinite contempt—such an unpardonable act, yet you pass over it without blame and grant us your pardon without fault; this is entirely due to the inadequacy and discourtesy of us who stand above...'

He spoke fluently, setting forth to entrap his romantic rival. Ōriyōhime, with graceful composure,

Ōriyōhime: 'Ah, pray do not trouble your heart over it at all. Since I came without notice, it caused you anxiety, and Tsurukō-sama must have been paying respect to me by tidying up the tatami room. It would have been fine had you kept me waiting a little longer. In any case, because I hurried in, I caused you a great deal of worry and troubled your hearts—that was my mistake...'

At Ōriyōhime's words, interpreted in the best of intentions, Tsurukō was startled and choked with tears of gratitude, bowing down in his heart with the thought, 'As expected of the Jiyōusama of Jionjō.' Uzuhime, touched by the warm words imbued with the Queen's tender feelings, let float tears of joy, her breast choked with gratitude as she cast her eyes downward. Kiyokō,

Kiyokō: 'As expected of a Queen of such profound benevolence. To speak the truth, we have always slighted the sentiment of respect and looked down upon our precious Jiyōusama. That crime has now manifested itself, and Tsurukō, who serves in my secondary position' (saying this in a loud voice)... 'despite having inflicted such unmannered insults, the way you look back upon us and reconsider with Kami Naobi and Ō Naobi—how truly grateful we are, and we have no words to offer our thanks. Even a bamboo whose inner nodes are hollow still has joints. If we let any sort of impropriety pass by just as it is, there is a fear that the discipline within the castle will be entirely broken and the beginning of the collapse of public morals will be opened; therefore, bearing this responsibility upon my own person alone, I hereby resign from my office from this very moment. If you will be so very good as to grant a hearing to this matter, I shall be most deeply grateful.'

He deliberately framed his words, harboring the foulness within his heart to bring about the downfall of the Migamori no Kami. Ōriyōhime, perceiving this with keen swiftness,

Ōriyōhime: 'Since your utmost sincerity has reached the heavens, I hereby relieve you of your priestly office as Hidarimori from this very day and hour, just as you desire. You shall descend from here to the lower mansion and await my command.'

Thus she pronounced it effortlessly. Kiyokō, the Hidarimori no Kami, contrary to his expectations, hung a face like that of an owl that had missed its supper, and with great reluctance,

Kiyokō: 'Ha… I am most grate… ful… for your prompt compliance…'

He accepted it in a trembling voice. Tsurukō wiped away his tears, placed both hands on the floor,

Tsurukō: 'I beg to address Jiyōusama. Today's discourtesy is entirely a matter unknown to Hidarimori-sama. It is entirely our impropriety; I most earnestly beg that you release me from my office and restore your trust in Hidarimori-sama just as before.'

Ōriyōhime: 'The utmost goodness, beauty, truth, and reality of your heart—I am deeply touched by it. Nevertheless, if you are persons who entrust everything to the heart of the Great Deity, you ought not to sway this sacred office by means of your own personal will. From this time forth, do you obey my command, become Hidarimori, and serve in the divine work. Moreover, Uzuhime, let this Ōriyōhime act as go-between, and you shall become the wife of Tsurukō, and together you shall eternally assist in our divine work.'

Thus he sternly declared.

Kiyokō, unable to simply leave the place and crawling like a dog, said:

Tsurukōdono, you must be immensely satisfied. As the former Samori Kiyokō, I offer my heartfelt congratulations to Uzuhime-sama.

Uzuhime was caught in the tightening vise of love and duty, writhing in her body without a single word to utter.

Tsurukō: 'I appreciate the Queen's words, but this matter alone, I beg of you to grant me a pardon.'

Ōriyōhime: 'Why is that so? Will you not hearken to my command?'

Tsurukō: 'However much it may be the master's command, for this one matter, I humbly and earnestly beseech your forgiveness.'

Ōriyōhime: 'Are you minded to disobey my command? Now then, Uzuhime, you shall henceforth become the wife of Tsurukō, and standing alongside Buranjī and Kuronbā, serve in the divine work. This is no private utterance of Ōriyōhime; it is the sacred will of the Sumeōkami of Ananai-kyō.'

With a stern and unyielding countenance, Uzuhime could endure no more, and said:

Everyone, this is my farewell to this world.

Pushing open the door, she bolted outside like an idaten runner, and vanished completely amidst the trailing white clouds.

Ōriyōhime, in severe words, declared:

You two, search out and track down the whereabouts of Uzuhime, and bring her back before my presence without a single moment's delay.

So she commanded, and once again leisurely strode into the inner chambers.

Both Kiyokō and Tsurukō, exclaiming:

This is a terrible crisis! We cannot just sit here.

ran with all their might like idaten runners, pursuing Uzuhime's tracks out of the castle gate, and striking the earth with a thud-thud-thud, rushed desperately across the mountain slopes enveloped in white clouds.

(By the way, this place Enjō is a highland sacred land surrounded on all sides by high mountains.)

Just as the sky suddenly darkened and even the land of Enjō-gō was locked in a gloomy atmosphere, Sumātobōru (Smartball), Chiyankī (Chianky), and Monkī (Monkey) caught a fleeting glimpse of Uzuhime's figure—her hair dishevelled, rushing out of the castle gate and running like a shot—and shouting out 'O-oi! O-oi!', they gave chase with all their might, fearing that losing sight of her would be a catastrophe.

Uzuhime, attempting to leap and throw herself from the thousand-chō cliff down into the blue abyss of the ravine, chanted:

Aimakanagara amamurakisamase.

and plunged right in with a splash.

Sumātobōru, Chiyankī, and Monkī, having pursued her this far, came to a dead end and stood somewhat bewildered.

At this moment, the two pillars Samori and Samori (Rigi), running and parting the white clouds while shouting 'O-oi! O-oi!', came advancing and crying out:

Seize Uzuhime!

Sumātobōru, resolved that it was now or never with a desperate determination, leaped with a splash straight toward the blue abyss of the ravine a thousand chō deep.

Chiyankī, Monkī, and the two pillars Samori and Samori, crying out 'Oh dear! Oh dear!', stamped their feet upon the rocks.

Sumātobōru, who had plunged to the bottom of the valley, scooped Uzuhime under his arm and rescued her up, made her vomit water, applied artificial respiration, and after various kinds of nursing, gradually brought her back to life.

Sumātobōru: 'Mosimosi Uzuhime-sama, why on earth did you do such a thing? There must be some deep circumstances behind this. However, whatever circumstances there may be, you must never lose your patience.'

Uzuhime, catching her breath, said:

Thinking who it might be, you are Smartball-san. ...There is a reason why I must die. Please overlook me.

Sumātobōru: 'Whatever may happen, you must endure and persevere, fulfill your heavenly lifespan, and serve in the divine work as much as possible. Suicide is the greatest of all sins. Your physical body is by no means your own thing. Both body and soul are precious borrowed treasures from the Gods. If you do such a selfish thing, that sin will never be forgiven for generations to come. Whatever may happen, Smartball will help you with all his might, so please give up on suicide at least.'
