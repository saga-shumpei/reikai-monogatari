Chapter 11: Wind Voice Crane 唳 [757]

風声鶴唳〔757〕

§ 1

Not a single cloud dimmed the vast blue sky; the starlight was sparse, and the mid-autumn moon hung high in the expanse of midair, illuminating the lower world with a countenance as serene as a mirror.

The golden crest of the Ten Luminaries, inlaid upon the ridge of Chionjiyō Castle, gleamed so dazzlingly in the moonlight that it hurt the eyes.

Ōriyōhime, Queen of Chionjiyō Castle, accompanied by Umekohime, Mukadehime, Samori's Smartball, Uzuhime, and further attended by Umori's Tsurukō, Kuwanshiu, Takekō, and others, ascended the high pavilion and held a moon-viewing banquet.

The wine of fruits emitted a fragrant aroma, while citrus, bananas, peaches, and other nuts were piled high in exquisite vessels; just as everyone was exhausting their joy and looking up at the moonlight, Ōriyōhime's complexion suddenly turned pale, her body began to tremble incessantly, and she fell into a state as though enveloped in clouds of inner anxiety.

To the eyes of Umekohime and the others, the mid-autumn moon shone brilliantly, the azure sky grew ever higher, the breeze was cool, and they were enveloped in an indescribable mood.

Reflected solely in the eyes of Ōriyōhime—led by Tomohiko, who had become the husband of Princess Therese of the land of Janna, propagated the way of 3-5 to the west of Mt. Nelson, and was acclaimed to possess the momentum of the rising sun—were countless demon-like natives riding upon strange dark clouds, carrying sharp spears without limit from the midair to survey the ground, raining steel halberds like a cloudburst toward the head of Ōriyōhime, dragging carts of fire, clad in tiger-skin loincloths with horns like cattle, advancing to attack with such terror that her body suddenly convulsed, causing her finally to plummet from the high pavilion and fall unconscious.

Mukadehime was startled and speechless, diligently descending the stairs despite her aged body.

Yet to Umekohime, Smartball, and the rest of the company, the figures of both Ōriyōhime and Mukadehime appeared as before, remaining in the high pavilion to enjoy the moon.

Therefore, they knew not even in a dream that Mukadehime had flurriedly descended the stairs, nor that Ōriyōhime had fallen from the high pavilion.

In short, the true guardian deities of Ōriyōhime and Mukadehime manifested their exact forms as before in this high pavilion, enjoying the moon with joyous abandon.

Due to the demons of attachment remaining within their physical bodies, they produced such hallucinations, and furthermore, their physical bodies, formed from the coagulation of their sins, were hurled down into the thousand-fathom ravine beneath the high pavilion as vessels for their sub-guardian deities.

The figure of Ōriyōhime left behind afterward became semi-transparent like tortoiseshell, adding a further layer of beauty, and her words abruptly grew cool and laden with solemnity.

Umekohime: 'Queen Ōriyōhime-sama, how wondrous a thing this is.

Compared to your appearance until now, it has completely transformed; your complexion is exceptionally splendid, and even the posture of your body seems somehow imbued with added dignity.

Even if it changes, for you to elevate yourself with such swiftness is nothing short of wondrous.'

Ōriyōhime: 'Ah, unworthily have I become a divine officiant of the 3-5 Teaching and even risen to the rank of Queen of Chionjiyō Castle; floating slightly upon hues of triumph and struck by a feeling of relief, unworthily I began treating the Great Deity of the Moon as a plaything or the like, bringing out food and drink and treating it as a moon-viewing banquet—beginning such a presumptuous thing like cherry-blossom viewing or snow viewing without a single thought.

Yet, suddenly illuminated by the majestic power of the Bodhisattva of Moonlight in the vast heavens, alas, what an unpardonable thing I have done.

Right now, living securely here with a power that could even pluck flying birds from the sky, a feeling came over me as though my old scars were completely reflected in the mirror of the moon.

Far from moon-viewing, I felt as though I wanted to crawl into any hole should one appear, and just as I was suffering with remorse, the all-encompassing stars were enveloped in dark clouds, the moonlight hid its radiance, and before I thought of how the four directions had become pitch dark and within arm's reach nothing could be seen, Tomohiko—who transmits the way of 3-5 in the land of Janna—accompanied numerous demons to avenge the insults I had formerly inflicted upon him.

Descending from the heavens, they rained steel halberds down like rain and approached to welcome my physical body with carts of fire—how terrifying it was.

The garment of my sin-encrusted physical body was stripped away by the grace of the deities, and moreover, my mother's garment of attachment, drowning in maternal love, fell into this valley, turned into white smoke, and vanished.

Ah, now that things have come to this, there is no longer a single cloud or mist upon my physical body, and it seems I have been reborn as a spirit just like the moon of this mid-autumn.

Therefore, everyone, starting right now we shall abolish the moon-viewing banquet and offer our apologies to the deities.'

§ 2

Hearing this Monogata, Umekohime was deeply moved, and becoming a Daushi herself, she sat in Tanza upon the Takadono, offered up the Kamigoto of thanksgiving and prayer toward the Getsukuō, threw down every single vessel used for the Tsukimi from this Takadono toward the Tanisoko below without leaving even a single Hitoshina, pledged to the Shinmei that she would nevermore hold the banquet of the Tsukimi, and looking Shōzen, hid her figure in the Okuden of Chionjiyō, whereupon all gathered together once more to Sōjiyō the Amatsunorito.

Beginning with Ōriyōhime of Chionjiyō, the key Kanbu entered the Okuden, and while they were receiving the Miki after completing the Sōjiyō of the Norito, both Kuwanshiu and Takekō came Kakei hurriedly toward the inner chamber, breathless, with their rear headbands tightly bound, and each grasping a whip of thorns as they reported,

Kuwanshiu and Takekō: 'We humbly report. Ta-ta-ta-ta, this is a grave emergency. Please be prepared.'

Smartball: 'You two, Kuwanshiu and Takekō, what is this about an emergency? There is no need to suffer; come closer and tell the details clearly.'

Kuwanshiu, striking his chest with both hands and leaning slightly forward, said:

Kuwanshiu: 'The two of us, by the order of Smartball, supervising numerous subordinates at the gates of Chionjiyō and guarding them with strict vigilance—at that very moment, a war cry was faintly heard. Wondering what it could be, we ran at full speed up to the Takadono, and looking intently ahead under the moonlight, the banner bearing the crest of the ten suns and the gourd-shaped horse-markers appeared without end among the trees, in and out they emerged and vanished. With red headbands and red sashes, straddling numerous horses, they raised a war cry and rushed to attack with terrifying momentum. Wondering who the enemy might be, we sent out scouts, and looking closely, who could have ever expected it—they were the former Brahman followers Tomohiko and the fierce warriors of Janna who had been cast out of the castle earlier, leading them on, shouting that they would have a stern discussion with Ōriyōhime, and attacking with the momentum of ferocious tigers. While our side wears thin robes of figured brocade, the tens of thousands of enemy soldiers are clad in armor, wearing gauntlets and greaves, carrying sharp weapons, and attacking in magnificent array with banners and drums. We two drove together all the men of our side, commenced the Kototama battle against the advancing enemy, chanted the hymns of heaven, joined our hands, emitted five-colored divine light from our fingertips, and fought to defend against the demon army of the enemy; yet they too are mighty warriors, retaliating with the Kototama, and furthermore carrying sharp weapons, approaching closer hour by hour with perilous intent. Although Chionjiyō follows the principle of non-resistance on ordinary days, when things have come to this, there is no help for it; replacing weapons with stones gathered from the place, we pelted them like rain and hail against the advancing enemy army, whereupon the enemy collapsed like an avalanche and withdrew in a flash for a cho or two once, but raising a war cry yet again, with the terrifying momentum of a ferocious tiger crossing a river, breathing flames from their mouths, blue, red, and black kishin stood at the vanguard, unleashing monstrous voices like thunder as they attacked. Our side numbered barely over three hundred, and though we fought desperately, the enemy was a renowned, massive army, cut down smoothly in the blink of an eye, and with profound regret, the two of us cut our way through the enemy and finally returned safely to this place; therefore, to remain in this spot is perilous to your sacred person. Quickly, escape at once through the back gate, following the mountain trails toward Mount Zion. Now, hurry, hurry!'

Trembling in body, shaking their left and right hands back and forth as they made their urgent report with such eeriness, Smartball could not comprehend it at all and said:

Smartball: 'We who have been holding the Tsukimi banquet up to this moment in the Takadono—if there were signs of the enemy advancing, surely there ought to have been some revelation from the Shinkai; yet this is truly incomprehensible. ...Excuse me, Yoshikohime-sama, Umekohime-sama, what are your thoughts on this incomprehensible report brought by these two?'

He looked back and forth into their faces, his heart racing with a slightly uneasy expression. Ōriyōhime remained unruffled and majestic, giving the strict command:

Ōriyōhime: 'Hey there, Smartball, ...bring both Kuwanshiu and Takekō before me.'

At this strict command, Smartball pulled the hands of the two and guided them to the knees of Ōriyōhime. The two hung their heads, shrinking like mice chased by a cat, trembling all over.

Ōriyōhime: 'Hey, Kuwanshiu, you two Takekō, is the matter you just reported a past event, or is it a future event, or is it present? Answer me clearly.'

Takekō: 'Yes, if it were a past event or a future occurrence, we would never worry like this. Since the people inside the castle have already almost perished, and both of us have suffered such wounds on our faces, it is a matter of the very present, right now... Look, look, right over there, the voices that have grown so close, the sound of men and horses! Presently Tomohiko, brandishing his spear of steel, will attack and come rushing into this place, so please hurry and escape safely through the back gate even a moment sooner.'

Ōriyōhime: 'Hey, Smartball, you go to the front gate and investigate the truth or falsity of it.'

Smartball replies 'Yes' and tries to stand up. Kuwanshiu catches the hem of his garment,

'Moshimoshi Sasamori-sama, please wait. The many cannot oppose the few, like an insect flying into the fire. I will never tell you anything bad... Ōriyōhime-sama, please make your preparations quickly, quickly.'

Umekohime: 'Just now you spoke of the approaching war cries and the sound of men and horses in accordance with Takekō's words, but isn't heaven and earth extremely quiet, without a single sound?'

Takekō: 'Th-th-th-that, what are you saying? Do you not understand those voices?'

Changing his expression and looking unsettled, he answers while trembling.

Smartball shakes off Kuwanshiu's hand, appears at the front gate in a single dash, and when he looks, the moon shines brilliantly and not a single cat can be seen anywhere around. Thinking 'How strange,' he climbs up to the high hall and gazes sharply in all directions; the mountains are tinged a cobalt blue without a single speck of white cloud, and the silhouettes on the mountain ridges stand out all the more clearly, filling the loneliness with an indescribable elegance. Smartball leisurely returns to the inner palace, appears standing before the two men, and suddenly strikes Kuwanshiu and Takekō across their cheeks two or three times with a smack, whereupon the two expose faces as if completely dumbfounded for the first time,

Riyōnin: 'Ya... This is, this is an extreme rudeness, somehow invading the Queen's palace before we knew it! It is truly a matter for which we are sorry. Please forgive us.'

They prostrate themselves.

Mukademehime: 'Really, really, you nearly snatched away my courage! What on earth are you talking about? You must have been having a dream for the most part.'

Riyōnin: 'Yes, now that we think about it, it was a continuous sequence of dreams. Because we were so worried that Tomohiko kept appearing and appearing, we must have had a terrible dream... Ah, Kannagara Tamashii Sakiwaesei.'

They put their hands together,

Riyōnin: 'Well, well, it was good that it was a dream... Everyone, congratulations, I wish you joy.'

Ōriyōhime, Umekohime, and Uzuhime all burst out laughing at once,

'Pufufu..., ohahaha...'

Outside the mansion, the brilliant bright moon shines, and the voices of pine crickets, bell crickets, grasshoppers, and katydids are heard bustling.

蜈蚣姫『ほんにほんに、妾の荒肝を取りかけよつた。お前は一体何と言ふ事を言つて来るのだ。大方夢を見て居つたのだらう』

両人『ハイ、今考へて見ますれば、夢の連続的行為で御座いました。あまり友彦が出て来る出て来ると心配して居つたものですから、ドエライ夢を見たので御座いませう……あゝ惟神霊幸倍坐世』

と両手を合せ、

両人『マアマア夢で結構で御座いました。……皆さま、お目出度う、お祝ひ申します』

黄竜姫、梅子姫、宇豆姫一度に吹き出し、

『プツフヽヽヽ、オホヽヽヽ』

館の外には皎々たる明月輝き、松虫、鈴虫、蟋蟀、螽斯の声賑しく聞えて居る。
