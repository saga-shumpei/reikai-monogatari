§ 1

A tale that comprehensively surveyed the three realms of Geniushin, transcending time and space into the past, future, and present.

In the twenty-fifth year of Meiji, a felicitous omen when the Izukoan Kyōso received the divine words of the northeastern Kami and appeared.

The number of the volume corresponds thereto: sitting upon Shiyumisenzan and protecting Sanzensekai, just as the Kami do.

Even as we hold it in awe, the great universe of Takaamahara where the divine Kami have manifested, placing oneself on the outside thereof.

Here Mizumitama surveyed the six directions without a single shadow, and Ōyashimahiko grasped the divine heart of Kami Susanoo.

Manifesting as the divine soul of Tsukiteru, the command of the Kami concealed itself within the sacred land of Aya.

Borrowing the mouth of an infant who had crossed the fifty-steps slope twice, opening the teachings of the thirty-five Kami in the village of Chion, where golden sand is spread, in the island of Oceania, the auspicious Dragon Palace drifting upon the vast ocean.

Yoshikohime and Umekohime, born as the rare children of Onikumawake, are the tales of the ancient age of the gods, where they understood the infinite mercy of the primal deity of heaven and earth and devoted themselves to the Way.

Leaving the Ryūgūkan of Matsutakebune, the flow too is pure: Kogumogawa.

The old pines in the row are the Kami of good fortune, summoned by the wind and blowing forth, though it be neither conch nor trumpet—is it a dream, an illusion, truth, or a lie? It can hardly be a lie, nor yet quite the truth; a tale truly hard to comprehend.
