Three men looking like Shinto priests, dressed in white garments, were waving ōnawa frantically back and forth, chanting a propaganda song mixed with broken words, and the natives, following their voices, were singing along while shedding drops of sweat. When he looked very closely, there was a great serpent, which he thought might be a full jō in circumference, revealing its red belly like a striped curtain, raising its head two or three ken high, and ceaselessly blowing out poisonous vapor.

In ancient times, pure sweet sake used to gush forth naturally from this waterfall, and the mountain dragon god would occasionally come and drink of it, but from the time when Hinodewake first crossed over to this island, the gushing volume of sake gradually decreased, until at last not a single drop came out. The neighboring waterfall flows down murmuring just as it did in the old days. The great serpent inhabiting this water, having come to drink sake and finding not a single drop, grew impatient and came to swallow human children up night after night.

To prevent this harm, the villagers of the neighborhood made Imoriwake-sama, a descendant of Iiroyohiko no Mikoto, ask for a divine oracle from Iimoriwake. At this time, the spirit of the great serpent possessed someone and spoke...

Serpent: 'If you think it sad to have the lives of your beloved children taken, gather fruit everywhere, brew sake, fill yonder waterfall basin, and offer it to me. If you do so, I shall stop swallowing your beloved children.'

So saying. From then on, the villagers gathered numerous fruits to make sake, climbed up this mountain slope, and made filling this waterfall basin with sake like their daily work. It had become customary for the great serpent to appear once a month and drink the sake in the waterfall basin down to the very last drop.

If it were only about once a year, there would be some way to deal with it, but having to fill this waterfall basin, which held several koku, with fruit sake every single month was, for the not-so-numerous villagers, an unbearable, supreme agony. When they neglected it even for a single month, the great serpent would suddenly come and swallow the children of the village, and when that was not enough, it would swallow young, marriageable women one after another. Wanting somehow to escape this tragic state, the villagers conferred together and mixed fennel powder into the sake, making it a fragrant sake, filled this waterfall basin with it, and waited while hiding in the shade of the trees, holding their breath, for the great serpent to come and drink.

The great serpent had appeared here from two or three days before, leisurely drinking up several koku of sake, and had already finished drinking about half of it in three days, and while the poison circulated, it was writhing in agony. Thither the divine priests Iimoriwake, Kukiwake, and Kukuwake, together with numerous villagers, surrounded the great serpent on all four sides in the center, and were offering prayers hoping that the poison would circulate through the great serpent's whole body as quickly as possible and that it would perish. The great serpent opened its mouth wide, measuring about a jō, and while convulsing its neck like one mad with death, it took aim at the villagers and tried to bite them. Iimoriwake and the other two priests were troubling the great serpent by waving and brandishing the ōnawa, called the hire of the great serpent, on every such occasion.

一年に一回位ならば如何なつと仕様もあるが、数十石を満す此滝壺に、毎月果物の酒を充さねばならぬと云ふ事は、余り多からぬ郷人に取つては、此上なき大苦痛であつた。一ケ月にても之を怠りし時は、大蛇忽ち来りて郷の児を呑み喰ひ、足らざる時は若き妙齢の女を片ツ端から呑んで了ふ。此惨状を如何にもして逃れむと郷人諜し合せ酒に茴香の粉末を混じ、香よき酒となし、此滝壺に充し置き、大蛇の来り呑むを、木蔭に潜んで息をこらして待つて居た。

大蛇は二三日前より此処に現はれ、数十石の酒を悠々として呑み干し、最早半分許り三日間に呑み終り、毒が廻つて悶え苦みつつあつた。そこへ飯依別、久木別、久々別の神司は、数多の郷人と共に、大蛇を中に四方を取囲み、一時も早く毒が大蛇の全身に廻り、亡びます様と祈願をこめつつあつたのである。大蛇は一丈許りの大口をパツと開いて、首をシヤクリ乍ら死物狂になつて、郷人を目蒐けて噛みつかむとする。飯依別外二人は、其度毎に大蛇の比礼と称する、大麻を打振り打振り大蛇を悩ませて居た。

§ 4

However, with a dose of fennel poison fit for a monstrous serpent body, it was merely like a human being getting heavily drunk, lacking the potency to be fatal to its life. Therefore, once the intoxication wore off, there was no knowing what manner of revenge this great serpent might come to perpetrate anew; beside themselves with anxiety, the numerous villagers had gathered together and were intensely offering up prayers for the extermination of the great serpent. Hack at it or thrust at it as they might, its scales were as hard as steel, and it was utterly impossible for human beings to do anything about it whatsoever. There was a strong man of this village's natives named Tariya, who, carrying a large scythe, purposely allowed himself to be swallowed by the great serpent, attempting to slash apart its viscera with the scythe from within its belly and slay the great serpent, and though he had purposely let himself be swallowed two or three days before, as yet what had become of that man—the great serpent merely changed the color of its eyes slightly and thrashed about in agony. Yet it did not look as though it were going to die so very suddenly either, and it appeared no different from a human being slightly intoxicated by alcoholic liquor and shaking his head, so the anxiety of Ihiyoriwake and everyone else was by no means small. There was simply no other path left to pray upon than to have the great serpent exterminated through the power of the gods.

Kiyokimi lurked in the shade of the trees, gazing intently upon this state of affairs while his chest throbbed slightly. Before he knew it, he noticed that he felt a numbness in various parts of his body, and had become completely unable to move a single limb freely. This time, the great serpent sluggishly broke through the crowd of people, crawled out widely, wagged its tail incessantly, thrashed about wildly, and whipped down the trees in the surrounding area. Among the villagers, those who were killed by being struck by the tip of its tail increased by the moment. The great serpent thrashed about with ever-increasing madness. Due to the fennel poison having circulated throughout its entire body, and the torment of the iron-made scythe—which was most toxic to a serpent's body—even the formidable great serpent was driven to frantic desperation and began to suffer. Kiyokimi was struck by its tail, swept up into mid-air for about two or three chō, and dropped with a thud near the rocks where Chiyankī and Monkī were waiting with paralyzed legs. Truly, all three cripples had now been gathered together in this place. Ah, what would be the fates of these three individuals, beginning with the body of the great serpent?

されど巨大なる蛇体に茴香の毒位にては、人間が悪酔した時の様なもので、生命に関はる丈の効果はない。そこで酔が醒めたる上は、此大蛇再び来りて如何なる復讐をなすかも知れずと、心も心ならず、数多の郷人は寄つて集つて、大蛇退治の祈願を凝らしつつあつたのである。斬れども突けども鱗堅くして鋼鉄の如く、到底人間としては如何ともする事が出来なかつた。此村の土人にしてタリヤと云ふ力強の男、大鎌を携へ大蛇にワザと呑まれ、腹中にて鎌を以て臓腑を斬り破り、大蛇を殪さむと試み、二三日以前にワザと呑まれて了つたのだが、今に其男は如何なつたか、唯大蛇は少しく目の色を変へて苦しげに狂ふ計りである。されどさう急に死にさうにもなし、人間が酒の酔に少々酩酊して首を振つてる位な態度とよりか見えないので、飯依別始め一同の心配は容易でなかつた。唯此上は神の力に依りて大蛇を退治し貰はむと祈るより外に道がなかつたのである。

清公は木蔭に潜み、稍胸を躍らせ乍ら此態を熟視して居た。何時の間にか身体の各部に痳痺を感じ、一寸も自由の利かぬ様になつて居る事に気が付いた。大蛇は今度はノソリノソリと人垣を破つて広く這ひ出し、尾を頻りに振つて、四辺の樹木をしばき倒し暴れ狂ふ。郷人は尾の先に叩かれて死する者刻々に殖えて来る。大蛇は益々暴れ狂ふ。茴香の毒が全身に廻つたのと、蛇体に最も有毒なる鉄製鎌の悩みとに依つて、流石の大蛇も死物狂ひになり、苦しみ出したのである。清公は其尾に振られて二三丁許り中空に捲上げられ、チヤンキー、モンキーの腰を抜かして待つて居る岩石の傍にドサリと降つて来た。愈此処に三人の躄が揃つた訳だ。嗚呼、大蛇の身体を始め此三人の運命は如何なるであらうか。
