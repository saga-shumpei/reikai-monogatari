Chapter 7: Great Serpent 解脱 [753]

大蛇解脱〔753〕

§ 1

Kiyokō, Chiyankī, and Monkī, the three of them, advanced toward the waterfall of sake while singing the Sendenka, and when they looked, even the great Orochi, having become drunk on the poisonous sake, had writhed in the agony of death, and appeared to have grown immensely fatigued, dropping its head limply and biting down on its jaws, becoming almost half-dead in appearance.

Ihiyoriwake, a descendant of Ihiyorihiko, together with the three kamuzukasa of Kukuwake and Kukiwake, had gathered together the amata satobito who had scattered and fled, and holding takeyari in meimei, were in the very matsusaichiu of trying to kill the body of the Orochi by thrusting at it with all their chikarakagi from between the scales.

Ihiyoriwake faced the body of the Orochi and declared its execution:

Ihiyoriwake: 'Thou hast ravaged this village night and day, squeezed the kauketsu of the jinmin, and furthermore swallowed and eaten up our saishi, and art the root and source of the akugami that hath troubled the satobito; how long will Heaven never forgive such an akugami as thee? Unworthily, by the divine virtue of Masumihime-no-Mikoto, our strategy is to weaken and chastise thee—how well thou hast perished, ah. Come, from this moment we shall take the physical life of thee and remove the disastrous calamity of this village. Thou too surely, as a living creature between heaven and earth, receivest the divine spirit of the Gods. Without fail, if thou dost harbor resentment against our doings today, wander in the shadow of the reikon, and do a thing like adding harm once again to this village, by the divine power of our prayer we shall torment that reikon and chase it away to the nethermost land... Come, Orochi, in both spirit and flesh mayest thou attain sabumetsuraku, and thou must never leave thy heart behind in this world.'

Thus making the declaration of execution toward the Orochi, under the command of both Kukuwake and Kukiwake, the amata satobito matched their voices to strain the takeyari, inserted the tips of the spears between the scales, raised the battle cry of 'One, Two, Three,' and at the very moment when they prepared to thrust all at once, the Sendenmakishi led by Kiyokō who had appeared along with the voice of the Sendenka drew near to this place with brisk steps,

Kiyokō: 'We are those called Kiyokō, Chiyankī, and Monkī, subordinates of Yoshikohime-sama, the Queen of Ryūgūjima who appeareth in Chionkyō; if we listen in hiding in the shade of the trees now, the plan of Ihiyoriwake and the other two pillars to remove the harm of the satobito by means of the poisonous sake strategy and execute this Orochi—though that intention of yours is truly reasonable, even if it be an Orochi, since it is a bunshin buntai of Tentei, you must not kill it lightly. First of all, you must face it with the Kototama, and when it will not surrender by any means, then with respective weapons you should commence direct action. Pray, wait for the present.'

Thus restraining them, he leaped lightly upon the head of the Orochi and began to sing the Sendenka with great vigor.

Kiyokō: 'Gods appear in the outer world, To set apart and establish good and evil. The God who created this world is Naobi, With a heart as vast as ōnaobi. Simply, whatever may happen in the world of men, Look anew, listen anew with naobi, Errors of the body are confessed anew, The divine path of Ananaikyō. Even though the Orochi should die, The reikon of the Orochi eternally Remains in this world and various Evil crooked acts are repeated, To vent resentment, disturb the world, Growing wild and fierce is before our very eyes. Even the perverse crooked deities of wickedness and lawlessness Cannot contend against the path of good. Every month and every day to this village Appearing and coming, all people Troubled and suffering, this Orochi Is not from the beginning an evil god. In the distant age of the gods, in that antiquity, Hinode-no-Kami appeared, Firmly building the pillar of the great palace For the national spirit of the princess of Masumi, Deigning to enshrine and worship Him, And appointing Ihiyorihiko-no-Mikoto As the kamuzukasa, Down to descendants generation after generation, That the great path of the unity of rites and government Must not be forgotten even in a dream, The words that were taught and left behind— All the satobito forgot them utterly, Sinking their bodies in the abyss of avarice, Yearning for the mountain of victory, The action of body-master and spirit-subordinate Continuing ever more vigorously, Touching the great wrath of the gods of heaven and earth, Makatsuhi of their own accord Produced by the people of the village, This Orochi that hath now appeared, Is the reflection of the souls of all the satobito And the colors of their hearts, A thing that hath manifested itself. With just a single holding of the heart, Appearing as a god, becoming a demon, Even becoming an Orochi is due to the discord Of the words, hearts, and deeds of each and every one, A thing that hath been brought forth. Rather than cruelly killing this Orochi With the weapons of takeyari, Pacifying the Orochi that lurks In the true bottom of thy heart, The light of Ananai of sincerity alone, the mirror of Masumi, Behold it by illuminating—even the ugly flesh of the crooked spirit Suddenly disappearing without a trace or shadow, With neither demon nor Orochi left behind, O satobito who shall bow and submit To the divine grace of the path of sincerity!'

と制止し大蛇の頭部にヒラリと飛び上り、盛んに宣伝歌を歌ひ始めた。

Such manifestation of the Great Serpent, / If we trace its very source, / Is due solely to the faults / In the hearts of the unmistakable villagers. / Oh Great Serpent, oh Great Serpent, oh Magakami, / If you possess a true soul, / Listen respectfully to my Kototama, / Grasp it thoroughly and minutely, / And this strange and lowly form of yours, / Like white clouds vanishing / Without a trace into the Amatsusora, / Will surely meet the joyous age / That is pure, righteous, and clear. / Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, / May your Mitama be prosperous and blessed, / Repeating day by day, every single day, / The teachings of Ananai-kyō / Handed down by the gods of old, / Remembering them, and dedicating yourselves / To the service of Shinkai, oh villagers!

How could the world governed by Kami / Ever be overrun by Akuma? / Akuma lurks within the human heart. / Promptly, even in a single day, make the stern Kototama of the Ōkami your warp, / And let the conduct of your life be your weft, / Establishing with sheer sincerity alone / The achievement of having been born as a human / In the midst between Ame and Chi, / Transforming yourselves into righteous kami, / And build the Miroku no Miyo! / The Sairin of Christ the Messiah, / The dawn of Miroku's birth, / The auspicious sign of Kanrodai, / The sacred music upon the Lotus pedestal, / The very spirit of the Divine Land itself— / All of these are one and the same unchanging thing. / Cast aside your narrow minds, / And practice the way of the kami of Ananai-kyō, / Bearing it ever in your thoughts.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, / May your Mitama be ever prosperous and blessed!'

As he finished proclaiming this, the Great Serpent, which had hitherto displayed a long and gigantic form, gradually shrank and diminished, finally becoming a small snake that crawled happily around Kiyokimi's feet. Kiyokimi still chanted the Amatsu Norito, performed Chinkon directed toward this snake, and

Do not appear in this village ever again, nor repeat such atrocious and wicked deeds. Furthermore, let the villagers radically reform their hearts up to now, believe in Ananai-kyō as taught by Hinode no Kami, and offer thanks to the divine spirit of Masumihikonomikoto; then the oni and the great serpent lurking within your hearts will suddenly hide their forms and vanish away like smoke.'

As he finished speaking, Iimoriwake, Kugiwake, Kuguwake, and all the village priests, together with all the villagers, prostrated themselves before Kiyokimi, repented their sins of disbelief up to that time, and prayed to become once again the rare children of the Ōkami.

In Ji'ongō, Umejikohime and Kōryūhime have appeared, and their divine virtues shine in all directions; therefore, to express gratitude for having washed away and purified the evils of this village, you must all assemble and pay a visit.'

When he commanded thus, Iimoriwake and everyone else yielded to this view without a second thought, and it was decided that they would visit Ji'ongō with Monkey as their guide.

Floating dozens of boats upon the sea surface, they rowed out of the port of Hiru. While the boats were abuzz with talk of divine virtue, they also followed the boat in which Kiyokimi and his party had arrived, landing at the port of Taka to pay their respects. At this time, by divine command, the boat in which Kiyokimi had arrived was returned to its original place, and seeking its owner, they bestowed various rare things from the village of Hiru upon him, expressing their deep gratitude. Ah, Kamunagara Mitama Chihahe Mase.

と宣り終るや、今迄長大なる姿を現はしたる大蛇も、次第々々に縮小し、遂には小さき蛇となりて、清公が足許を嬉し気に這ひ廻る。清公は尚も天津祝詞を奏上し此蛇に向つて鎮魂を修し、

『汝再び此郷に現はれ、かかる悪逆無道を繰返す勿れ。又郷人も今迄の心を根底より立直し、日の出神の教へ置かれし三五教を信じ、真澄姫命の神霊に拝謝せば汝等が心中に潜める鬼、大蛇は忽ち姿を隠して、煙となりて消え失せむ』

と宣べ終るや、飯依別、久木別、久々別の神司を始め郷人一同は、清公が前に平伏し、今迄の不信仰の罪を悔い、再び大神の珍の御子とならむことを祈る。清公は、

『地恩郷に三五教の梅子姫、黄竜姫の現はれ給ひて神徳四方に輝きあれば、此郷の害悪を洗ひ清められし謝恩の為めに、打揃ひ参拝す可し』

と命ずれば、飯依別を始め一同は、一も二もなく此説に服し、モンキーを案内者として地恩郷に参拝することとなつた。

幾十の船を海面に浮べてヒルの港を漕ぎ出した。船中は神徳の話で持切りながら、清公一同が乗り来たりし船をも従へて、タカの港へ上陸し、参詣する事となつた。此時神命に依つて清公の乗り来りし船は、元の所に帰され、其持主を求めて色々とヒルの郷の珍しき物を与へ、厚く謝辞を述べた。あゝ惟神霊幸倍坐世。
