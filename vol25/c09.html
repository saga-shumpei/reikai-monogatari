§ 1

The great pillars of Ananai-kyō, the lineage of Henjiyōnanshi, making the sole reliance and the tateyoko their guide, Takahime with her Tengu-like nose behaving with wadamama and kimama, appeared as the left guardian deity of Kiyokō, who served as her subordinate, rising like smoke up a pole, and plotted to make Uzuhime of the river of love his wife; but the mechanism collapsed completely, and from the summit of the castle of Chion, she plunged headlong down to the earth; wishing to restore her lost meiyo, burning her chest with the embers of shifuchiyakushin, Kiyokō, accompanied by a pair of chiankiyomonki, set out from the port of Taka, entrusted his body to a roofless boat, and while raising rough waves in his heart,

Lost in the gloom, Wada no Hara, the leech port of sincere Akashi, the misapprehension of true magoto, appearing long ago in the deep mountains where the spring of sake gushed forth, manifesting in that waterfall basin, gazing upon the wild rage of the giant serpent, making the flowers of sudden enlightenment bloom, reviving as newborn babes, designating even the two villagers of Hiru as companions on the journey, crossing over the deep valleys of the continuous Mount Sēran, advancing while chanting propaganda songs through Makutsugahara where wolves, ogres, giant serpents, baboons, and orangutans gather, crossing many a stone pillow for a roof of clouds and a mattress of dew,

With a heart shining like Tamanohara, the vast and boundless vault of heaven, having arrived at the village of Suwa where gold and silver sands are spread thickly, her heart and guts being washed by the jewel of the heart of the giant baboon, the incarnation of Konohanahime, the party of five, realizing the blessings of gods of heaven and earth, with soaring hearts and light bodies, reached the shores of the Dragon Palace settled deep at the bottom of the five sacred jewels of Lake Suwa, where Toyotamahime eternally hid herself and filled with deep blue waves; Kiyokō, who had washed his heart, starting with chiankiyomonki, the five pillars of Airu and Tēna, arrived before the shrine, and just at that very moment in the heavens,

Black clouds suddenly manifested, expanding before one's eyes in all four directions, until the whole sky became like flowing ink, making it impossible to distinguish even black from white; ah, o Kami, o Kami, let your august spirits grant peace, blow away the clouds and fog of the heart, and return both the heavenly august skies and the land to a clear, cool, and lucid state where the heavenly sun shines brightly, they chanted the norito with five mouths, and when they recited it with clear voices, ah, how wondrous, a single mass of fire light emerging from amidst the black clouds fell with a booming sound like thunder, and at the very moment it seemed as though the blossoms of Konoha opened all at once and scattered in four directions,

Rokugō, which had been wrapped in such deep darkness, instantly became a world illuminated brilliantly by the morning sun; when the five came to their senses and looked out over the surface of Lake Suwa, oh, the sublimity of the deep blue waves sparkling and flashing in the sunlight like fish scales; from far off toward the shadow of an island, countless pure white true sails and partial sails appeared and advanced toward this side, a scene resembling butterflies dancing and fluttering madly among the flowers in the green plains of a spring field; Kamunaohi who created this world, the vast-hearted Ōnaohi, truly whatever the human world may be, look at it straight and listen to it straight,

Correct your own faults and chant them anew; the divine path of Ananai-kyō, where ogres, giant serpents, and Magatsunomiko turn their own mitama outside, are filled with the feeling that even the shadows and shapes vanish like white smoke into the delightful world created by gods, feeling as though heaven and earth and all things themselves had transformed into a world of gods that is supreme goodness and supreme beauty; the five stripped off their garments and leaped into the lake water all at once with a splash, and their figures vanished instantly to the bottom of the water without a trace, like foam—was it a dream, a reality, or an illusion? An august divine plan that a non-divine body has no way of knowing how to fathom, one ten-thousandth of the august heart of the gods high up in the distant heavens,

The story unfolded by opening the jewel box of deep designs at the very bottom of the Dragon Palace Sea, where Zuigetsu in the innermost room of the Master of the Dragon Palace Hall lets his heart shine high like the sun in heaven, floating the moon of Shinnyo of the three-five upon the sea of the heart, while holding the sincere heart thinking of the nation above the clouds, Togorō illuminates the great divine way of the gods and records with a sharpened pencil and leaning against a mulberry desk, while Matsumura, desiring not to leak a single word, writes it down with all his heart, keeping Mujuni Bosatsu by his side as an attendant and standing forth to speak his sincerity.

Now here the Scroll of Gogo, written to about the halfway mark. Opening the divine egress's Innen upon the eternal pine winds, body and soul alike clear and pure, to chant on is indeed auspicious.

Kiyokō and the other four sat in seza before a small shrine on the shore of Lake Suha, recited the Amatsunorito, chanted the Kazūta to its end, and afterwards stood upon the shore of Lake Suha where waves of konjiyō drifted, gazing out at the boundless, broad surface of the lake, struck by a sublime spirit. Suddenly undergoing a shinkiitsuten, they were struck by impressions purer and more beautiful than Tengoku, and the ichidō, unbidden, stripped off their irui and leaped casually into the lake with a splash. Thinking it to be a bottomless, deep aqueous bed, the water puddle was shallower than they had thought, and the bodies of seven to eight shaku were no deeper than the navel at the shallowest and the neck at the deepest; yet, while once more reciting divine words in astonishment, they pushed the waves apart and advanced further and further to the north.

Slipping their feet on the lakebed shaped like a mortar, the five of them simultaneously plunged deep into the water's bottom, and for a moment met with the misfortune of losing consciousness. Being rescued and lifted up by a divine ship crafted with inlays of gold and silver that happened to be floating up, they were transported further and further north.

Rescued and lifted up to a corner of a husband-and-wife island floating in the lake, the bodies of the five were simply left unattended until they naturally regained consciousness. Though the blazing sun shone brilliantly as if scorching, this floating island, where ancient trees grew thick and blocked the heavens, had cool winds blowing gently and leisurely, and they did not feel the summer heat in the slightest. On this island, countless large and small gold and silver snakes played and frolicked without an inch of vacant space. The five were rescued and lifted up right onto those gold and silver snakes covering the ground like the eyes of a tatami mat, and for a while, oblivious to everything, they indulged freely in sleep.

Kiyokō was crawled into through the gap where his mouth was left agape by a somewhat thick golden snake; awakening to a tightness in his chest and startled by the sound of him crying 'Kyiisss kyiisss', Chiankyī, Monkyī, Airu, and Tēna realized it for the first time, and looking around, they saw golden and silver snakes spread like somen noodles, covering the trees, beneath the trees, and even over the tree trunks in an unbroken blanket. In Kiyokō's mouth, a thick golden snake had crawled in up to seven or eight parts out of ten, leaving only a mere seven or eight sun of the tip of its tail, and the tail wagged briskly back and forth and left and right, scrubbing the ear holes, nose holes, eyes, and so on recklessly with the tip of its tail. Chiankyī grabbed that tail to pull out the snake and tried to save Kiyokō, but the moment he stretched out his monkey arm and grabbed the tail, he was flicked away with a snap and hurled across to the neighboring island.

On the one island, gold and silver centipedes without number, spread like woven mats with their numerous legs neatly aligned, covered the ground. Even upon the trees and tree trunks, centipedes of gold and silver colors coiled without an empty space. Before their very eyes, centipedes two to three shaku long crawled sluggishly up Chiankyī's body and covered his body without an empty space. Yet, wondrously, he felt not the slightest pain or bitterness, only beginning to feel a tiny bit ticklish, and as they began to lick his body with their centipede legs and tongues, the ticklishness increased that degree more and more, until finally he could not stop laughing and came to writhe in agony rolling over and over on the centipede plain holding his stomach. This island is called the Female Island. The other island, where Kiyokō swallowed the golden snake, is called the Male Island.

Kiyokō's body suddenly transformed into a golden color, emitting a light like diamond from both eyes, and while moving his mouth up and down as if trying to say something, the tail that had been protruding seven or eight sun from his mouth settled deep into his abdomen before he knew it. Kiyokō's complexion glowed, adding yet another layer of solemnity, and his height extended by about one shaku while the entirety of his body increased in thickness. Without saying a word, Kiyokō grabbed Airu's scruff firmly and tossed him effortlessly toward the Female Island as if throwing a kitten. He fell with a soft thud onto the back of Chiankyī, who was bowing his head and laughing, just like riding a horse. The centipedes instantly enveloped Airu's whole body. Airu, too, suddenly began to laugh boundlessly. In the twinkling of an eye, the centipedes became as if scorched across his entire body, and the bodies of both men were completely covered in the mottled patterns of centipedes. Monkī on the Island of Men:

'Hey there, Kiyokimi Daimyōjin, you have ended up becoming the avatar of the golden dragon, and even the three fellows are all wearing garments of gold and silver centipedes, swaggering about as goddesses of peace or whatever, but what are you going to do with just this Monkī here? At first, seeing the snakes and centipedes made my hair stand on end, and thinking that because a guy with heavy sins came to such a place, he was suffering by being tortured by snakes and centipedes, I felt rather triumphant, thinking: Ah, I am the ringleader of an honest man who went to return the stolen boat, so neither snakes nor centipedes can do anything to me. For some reason, within a radius of about two feet around Monkī, the gold and silver snakes did not approach. When things came to this, Monkī could not make out at all whether he was detested by the gods or liked by them. Having no other choice, and feeling somewhat desperate, he began to walk around the island. The wondrous thing was that the snakes vied with one another, hastily clearing the path so as not to be stepped on by Monkī.

With this, he clapped his hands, finished chanting the norito at the top of his voice, and once more sat squarely upon the rock, folded his arms, and fell back into deep thought.

Just as Monkey was crossing both hands upon the rock, lowering his head, and concentrating his body and mind on the resolution of good and evil, startled to hear exquisite music echoing below his eyes, and opening his eyes to gaze, he saw: wrapped in gold, silver, jewels, and precious stones, upon a solemn lacquer-painted boat, an indescribably sublime goddess steering the helm, while Kiyokō, Chiankyō, Airu, and Tēna—four men who ought to have been stark naked—were clad in indescribably lovely thin garments, their bodies purified and transparent like crystal, each playing transverse flutes, shō, and hichiriki, wearing upon their heads crowns attached with beautiful yōraku, proceeding in high spirits over the surface of the waves—this spectacle appeared vividly through the gaps of the pine thicket. Monkey involuntarily cried out, 'Ah!' The four spread their golden fans, beckoning toward Monkey with 'Come quickly!', and amidst the sounds of exquisite music, they gracefully concealed their forms in the shadows of the distant island over the dark-blue waves.

As Monkey let out a heavy breath and looked back upon his own body, from his rough, black-red skin resembling copper dross, where hair grew in thick tufts, an indescribably sweat-odorous, foul stench was emitted, striking his very own nose.

When he opened his eyes and looked, his own body, together with the turtle, had come ashore at the rocky edge of Oneshima.
