不意の客〔758〕

§ 1

Tormented by the oni of her own heart, In days of yore, Ōriyōhime, At the tender age of sixteen in full bloom, Coldly and heartlessly Deep in the mountains of Seirontō, To Tomohiko—a rough man devoid of duty or affection, In a lonely bedchamber, Plied with wine through a trick of escape, Subjected to the sorrowful ordeal—that old scar, Even to this day, having become a queen, Lingered deep within her heart, Suffering and agonizing she had remained; but Illuminated by the moonlight Of a cloudless midautumn night, Pondering the inner feelings of the man she abandoned, Unable to bear it even for a second, Surrounded by the clouds of suffering and terrified, While gazing up at the sky, The heavenly vault suddenly clouded over,

Leading numerous oni, Pouring a rain of steel spears, The oni under Tomohiko's command Trembled at the shadows and into a thousand-fathom Ravine suddenly tumbled down, Following them, her mother Mukadehime, Wishing to save Ōriyōhime, Descending the stairs missed her footing, Likewise tumbled down into the ravine, At the bottom of the valley paying for her sins, Oh, how terrifying, how terrifying, The workings of past sins coming around, Had she fallen into the realm of the nether roots? O gods of heaven and earth, O Tomohiko, Pardon my sins— Just as she prayed, blown by the autumn wind, A single withered leaf falling, A red safflower fluttering into my eyes, As she thought this, her dream awoke, And raising her eyes, she was in the original lofty hall,

Bathed in the moonlight Along with Umekohime and the others; In this true dream, Koitorihime, Now here before the presence of the Mikumari God, putting her hands together, From the bottom of her heart to the moon-light god While apologizing for the discourtesy of moon-viewing, Proceeding deep into the inner sanctuary, Reciting the Futo-notori Before the presence of the Kami—just at that moment Kanshū, Breathlessly rushed over, And recounted the events from within the dream With an air of utter truth, Whereupon Ōriyōhime, feeling suspicious, Sent Smartball outside the gate To have him scout the situation, There was not even a shadow of attacking enemies, And the moonlight shone brilliantly, Illuminating every corner without exception; Beginning with Ōriyōhime,

Within the breasts of Kanshū and Bukō, In the black clouds not yet cleared away, Spawning doubt and suspicion in a haunted mind, Troubled by their own hands, A tale of awakening from delusion— While lying sideways, Mizuki, Counting the rafters of the ceiling, Stuffing her cheeks full of a round-shaped Anpan bread, Relates the tale of Ōriyōhime As it progresses, how wondrous it is! Ah, Kannagara Kamigami, May your divine spirits prosper and bless us.

In the shade of the trees in the open square inside the castle of Jionjō, generals Kanshū and Bukō alongside the gatekeeper and all the others, stretch out their bodies upon the lawn or sit about, indulging in idle chatter.

The gatekeeper Māru (Manzō) turns toward Kanshū,

Māru: 'Mosimosi, Ball-sama (meaning the leader), it seems the forces of Tomohiko came this way recently, how did it turn out, I wonder?'

Kanshū: 'It is none of your concern, Māru. It was a story akin to a mirage, as far as Māru is concerned.'

Māru: 'Your report to Ōriyōhime-sama was said to be like a dream, but even for a dream, it is quite an exaggeration.'

Kanshū: 'It is not just me. Even Bukō saw the exact same dream, so we are completely of one mind on this. Since I truly could not believe it to be a lie, I went as far as to make a report. However, we are loyal vassals deeply concerned for the divine person of Ōriyōhime-sama, so the dreams you see differ somewhat in caliber.'

Māru: 'Because they differ in caliber, you saw a dream of war and reported the battle situation in terror, did you not? ...Bukō-sama, did you really see such a dream too?'

Bukō: 'I cannot exactly say I saw a dream as such, but just as I was constantly worrying whether such a thing might happen, Kanshū spoke of it in such a flustered manner that, whether it was a dream or reality I know not, I was simply swept up in it and went along to report. However, since it was the manifestation of sincere devotion born of credulous reporting (loyal service to the gods), Ōriyōhime-sama did not particularly blame us, and it ended up as if everything was settled safely.'

Māru: 'In that case, shall I henceforth bestow upon the two of you the honorable title of Mirage Ball? ...Isn't that right, Myūzu (Myōshū)?'

§ 4

"It is surely, surely never for any such matter that you have come. I have something in my mind that touches upon this. At any rate, in order to pay respect to the great pillar of Ananai-kyō in Australia, I, Ōriyōhime, shall go out to the outer gate to welcome you."

Tsurukō: 'Speaking as Umori, for one of your status as the queen of Chionjiyō to go out lightly would affect your dignity. I earnestly beg that you cancel this matter alone. Since I will go out to welcome him, I beg you to wait in the inner hall.'

Ōriyōhime shook her head from side to side,

"Nay, we are all differentiated spirits of the same heaven and earth, and there is no distinction of upper and lower among humans. Moreover, that Tomohiko-sama has now become a splendid personage governing the people of the village of Janna, and that I have become the queen of Chionjiyō as well, must be ties of no shallow karma. Even if we were husband and wife for only a single year, I must go out to welcome him."

Taking advantage of the absence of Mukaderihime and Umekohime from this place, she briskly appeared at the front gate by herself, drew close to the side where Tomohiko sat in proper posture, and said,

"I am Ōriyōhime of Chionjiyō, formerly Koitohime. Tomohiko-sama, alongside Terusuhime-sama, pray come forward with me to the inner hall."

When Tomohiko looked up suddenly at this voice, she was indeed Koitohime, with traces of her former visage vividly lingering somewhere on her face... Tomohiko gave a start, lowered his head, respectfully placed both hands on the ground, and spoke.

Tomohiko: 'Well, well, Your Majesty, to think you have deigned to come to such a place as this... This here is she whom I call my wife, Terusuhime. Now, as a devoted believer of Ananai-kyō, she assists me in my divine duties to the utmost of her strength. There are mountains of things I wish to tell you, but if so, let us speak leisurely in the inner hall.'

Terusuhime: 'Your Majesty, I have the honor of meeting you for the first time. I beg for your kind favor in all things.'

Led by Tomohiko and following behind Ōriyōhime, they hid their forms deep within the gate. Tsurukō the Umori reluctantly followed behind the three of them. Kānsuu, Takou, and all the others looked at each other's faces in utter amazement, wondering if it were a dream, reality, or an illusion, and finding themselves once more without words, gazing blankly at the clouds drifting through the sky.
