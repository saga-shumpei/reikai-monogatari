Chapter 10: 開悟の Flower [756]

開悟の花〔756〕

§ 1

The color of the heart as well, Kiyokō, beginning with Chiyankī (Chōkichi) and Monkī (Mokichi), along with Airu and Tēna, the five of them, crossing over Tamanegahara, the center of Riugūjima in the Wadanaka where golden flowers bloom, while receiving the light of Kokushiyo, sitting in straight posture before the shrine in Suha where Suzukaze is fragrant, chanting Amatsunorito, casting off the garments of Ukiyo, the pure nakedness of a newborn Akago, and the Mitama before and behind-the five Mitama together leaping and plunging their bodies, with Kiyokō, who is deeper than the Chihiro depths even more so than the coagulation of sins, set at the head, each and every one stepping forward to sink their bodies into the deep Misoko of the Suribachi,

Though once their breath had ceased, being rescued by a Shinjin coming to pole the Shinsen of Menashikatama inlaid with Kinginshiyugiyoku, and saved upon the shores of Ojima where the evergreen Tokihagi stand sealing the Ten-now this island is where the myriad deities serving the gods of Riugū transform into golden and silver snakes, or transform into centipedes, enduring limitless suffering through the three cold and three hot seasons morning and evening to save the world in order to build the Age of Gods by saving and helping the Mappō world of the degenerate age, acting as a training ground for all benevolent dragon gods, the propagation envoys of Izumicō turning into newborn Akago, and Kiyokō whose heart's color as well,

What manner of entity was the golden snake that crept in aiming for the throat? It was the split Mitama of Tamaorihime, the incarnation of Tamanagahime, who, wishing to purify Riugūsu, borrowed the body of Kiyokō by name, casting Airu, Tēna, and Chiyankī away upon the Island of Centipedes, imposing the painful training which is the boundary between the phenomenal and spiritual worlds, polishing them into crystal Jimon, and carried upon a brocade boat light of garment with sins and impurities removed, proceeding onward to the palace of the dragon. The single man Monkī, left behind upon the shores of Ojima, seeing off the four figures, just as he was wavering in his judgment of good and evil, right and wrong, a golden and silver wave parting as it floats hither,

A blue-green-furred great turtle suddenly crawls up to Monkī's feet sluggishly, and when it crawls out aiming for the mountain peak, Monkī here, not to be outdone, chasing after the turtle while running up the branches of a great tree, suddenly tumbling down to the ground from a high place together with the turtle, striking his precious head, and blowing the divine breath of life upon the palms of both hands to stroke the wound smoothly and gently, the wound was healed instantly. The green-furred turtle swiftly dashes out like clouds and mist. Thinking not to be outdone, just as Monkī advances to the water's edge, the green-furred turtle suddenly leaps its body into the water,

Plunging in with a splash and nothing more. When Monkī leaps into the water once again not wishing to be left behind by the turtle, his limbs grow weary, his body weakens, and just as his breath is about to cease, the green-furred turtle, for some reason, floating its form upon the lake surface, resting its limbs and looking back, was waiting for Monkī to come. At last clinging to the turtle, holding its shell with both hands, he follows it at the risk of his life. The turtle immediately dives through the water into the deep seabed, resting its breath once again, then floating up to the lake surface once more, and suddenly transforming into a boat, Monkī, whose life is at its limit, as though revived for the first time, with a courageous heart and courageous spirit,

Entrusting his body to the boat of salvation, illuminating the darkness of his heart in the judgment of good and evil, right and wrong, following the waves at the whim of the boat, heading toward the tall towers of Riugū with its vermilion railings and azure tiles, and proceeding forward according to the divine will.

The boat advanced and came to the shallow shore of the lake. The lakebed was as clear as crystal, and the light of gold and silver sands shone in the sun with a beauty beyond all comparison. Small blue, red, and purple fish, shaking tails like goldfish, were swimming about vigorously in all directions. Like shining stars of heaven, at places where the depth of the water was from five sun to about one shaku, a light like diamond emitted a blindingly strong light at intervals of five to six shaku or one to two shaku. Gliding lightly even over water barely one sun deep, the boat finally arrived at the shore of gold sands.

Monkī jumped down from the boat and began to walk upon the sandy plain. Every time he took a step, a voice like that of a Japanese bush warbler came forth from beneath his feet. When he looked back and saw the footprints marked in the sand, they shone golden as if paved with large oval gold coins. Monkī stopped short for a moment, and when he looked at the boat he had ridden in and the lake surface, the beauty of blue, crimson, purple, white, yellow, olive, and other indescribable jewels floating to and fro, back and forth in front and behind like butterflies frolicking among flowers at a place about three shaku above the lake, the jewels frequently collided with one another, casting a light like fireworks upon the lake surface. It was a view resembling the scattering of powdered gemstone. While Monkī doubted his own body wondering if it was a dream and gazed intently at the lake surface, someone unexpectedly grasped both his shoulders firmly from behind. When he turned his eyes toward his chest to see what manner of entity it might be, they were the hands of a tiger bearing alternating bars of gold and black. Monkī, while firmly grasping those hands with both his own, felt a sensation as though being pulled by someone, and before he knew it, his form was guided into a forest where many beautiful purple leaves grew thick, and he proceeded onward unconsciously into a cave pierced beneath the roots of rocks resembling lapis lazuli light.

遠浅の湖岸に船は進んで来た。湖底は水晶の如く明かに、金砂銀砂の光太陽に映じて何物にも譬方なき麗しさ。小さき青、赤、紫の魚は金魚のやうな尾を掉つて縦横に溌溂として游いで居る。天の星の輝くやうに水の深さ五寸乃至一尺許りの所になりて、金剛石のやうな光、五六尺或は一二尺を隔てて目も眩む許り強き光を放つて居る。船は一寸許りの水の上さへも軽々しく辷りつつ、遂に金砂の磯端に着いた。

モンキーは船を飛び下り、砂原を歩みかけた。一歩々々運ぶ毎に足の下から鶯のやうな声が出て来る。振り返つて砂に印した足跡を見れば、大なる小判を敷いたやうに金色に光つて居る。モンキーはふと佇み、乗り来し船や湖面を見れば、青、紅、紫、白、黄、橄欖色、其他得も言はれぬ宝玉、湖上三尺許りの所を蝶の花に戯る如くに前後左右に浮動して居る麗しさ、玉と玉とは時々衝突して煙火の如き光を湖面に投げて居る。恰も宝玉の粉末を撒き散らした様な眺めである。モンキーは夢では無いかと我と我身を疑ひつつ尚も湖面を熟視して居ると、後の方より思はず両方の肩をグツと抱へた者がある。何者ならむと吾胸の辺に目を転ずれば、金色、黒色のダンダラ条のある虎の両手であつた。モンキーは其手を我両手に固く握り乍ら、何者にか引かるる如き心地し、自分の姿は何時の間にやら、紫の麗しき木の葉の数多茂れる林の中に導かれ、瑠璃光の如き岩石の根下に穿たれたる岩窟の中へ、不知不識に進み入りける。

§ 2

Monky was resolved to firmly set the navel of his determination and surrender entirely to Kamunagara, abandoning reason and abandoning whatever might happen; thus, discarding all calculation, he simply left himself to be drawn by the nature of his own body.

While Monky's body naturally spun round and round inside a cave glowing brilliantly with gold and silver light, guided by the strains of music heard from parts unknown, it now and then danced upward, sometimes lay horizontal, and sometimes walked upside down using its hands; completely entrusting his very body, mind, and soul to Kami, before he knew it, his body was sitting dignified and upright upon a throne adorned with beautiful gemstones. From far across the interior of the pit, the light of five jewels—blue, red, purple, white, and yellow—began radiating outward toward this side with a luminous power thousands of times greater than a searchlight, dazzlingly bright to the eyes. Involuntarily closing his eyes and gazing toward the shining jewels, to his utter astonishment, the faces of Hatsuwakahime were distinctly reflected within the purple jewel, Tamanohime within the red jewel, Tamaharuwake within the blue jewel, Kiusuke within the white jewel, and Omin within the yellow jewel. Upon seeing this, Monky's spirit suddenly felt as though it were floating into the void, and he collapsed right there on the spot without knowing what was before or behind him.

原文（近代文語）

モンキーは、如何なること有りとも、理智を捨てて唯惟神に任すべく、決心の臍を固めて居た際であるから、一切の考慮を捨て、唯吾身の自然に引かるるままに任して居るのみ。

モンキーの体は、金銀色の光輝く洞穴の中に自然にキリキリ舞ひ乍ら、何処ともなく聞ゆる音楽の声に従れて時々舞ひ上り、或は横になり、或は逆様に手を以て歩むなど、全く一身心魂を神に任せて、何時の間にか其身体は美はしき宝玉をもつて飾られたる宝座の上に端坐して居た。坑内の遥向ふより青、赤、紫、白、黄の五つの玉の光、サーチライトの数千倍の光力をもつて、目も眩む許り此方に向つて光を放射し出した。モンキーは思はず目を塞ぎ、玉の光る方を眺むれば豈図らむや、紫の玉の中には初稚姫、赤き玉には玉能姫、青き玉には玉治別、白き玉には久助、黄色の玉にはお民の顔がありありと映つて居た。モンキーは是を見るより忽ち精神宙に浮き上る如く前後も知らず其場に倒れて仕舞つた。
