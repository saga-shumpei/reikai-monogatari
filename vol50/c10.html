§ 4

'Well, the face of Hatsuwakahime-sama flickers before my eyes; even after the sun sets, whether I am sleeping or waking, or even in the Setsuchin, she appears before me. How strange it is that Hatsuwakahime-sama has fallen for me so. Wherever I go, she follows. Thinking that a man who does not eat a Suezen served before him is no man at all, I suppressed my thumping chest, summoned my Yūki, and the moment I firmly grasped her gentle hand, she said, "Woo, woof-woof," and bit down on my earlobe; look, this is how she caused this wound.'

Teru: 'What a terrifying woman, so unlike her face.'

'What, I thought she was a Hime-sama, but because I grasped the hand of a Mauken with all my might, the beast was Bitsukuri and bit me, ah-hahahaha.'

Saru: 'What is this. I thought that was about the extent of it. Because the activity of your Ichireishikon is Fukuwanzen, you keep doing such Tonma things. Why don't you do a little Kenkiu of Reigaku; Danshiyakusama will be careful, you know.'

'Hen, Danshiyaku, do not make a Baka of me. Are you not born of Shiyuda?'

'A man who pours his own drink is a Danshiyaku. I, who pour my own drink as I please, am a Shishiyaku. If you say such Koshiyaku things, I will not Shiyōchi it.'

'I drank sake and vomited it out along with Hedo, so I am a Hakushakusama (a vomiting-pourer). As a Hakushakusama, I am a man of one jō and seven shaku, so I have room to spare.'

'Hen, one jō and seven shaku—do not speak so grandly, you small man who does not even reach seven shaku.'

'Eight shaku plus nine shaku makes eight-nine shaku. Where is the abacus wrong in saying one jō and seven shaku? What a crude brain you possess. Do not speak so grandly about how the Ichireishikon is this or that. If you are going to speak so grandly, try interpreting it for once.'

'It is a pity to let such ears as yours hear it, but it would be laughable if I were thought to be unlearned. Because of your provocation, not providing an explanation as a man would damage the prestige of a scholar, so I shall exert myself and instruct you. Ehem, in the first place, what is called Ichireishikon refers to Naohi, Aramitama, Kushimitama, Sakimitama, and Nigimitama. And Aramitama is courage, Sakimitama is love, Kushimitama is wisdom, and Nigimitama is parental affection; do you understand? I am quite well-versed in academic principles, am I not?'

'What is courage? Will you not explain courage?'

'A man who is a man is called a Yūsha (brave person). How is that, do you understand? Next is the lecture on parental affection. Parental affection is the character for parent. Showing someone a bitter experience eight times is called parental affection. Next is love. Any heart that purrs and accepts something if it is advantageous, this is called love. Hen, and a guy like you who makes mistakes with his mouth every day is called wisdom. Seeing things as ten eyes see one is called the Nao of Naohi. No matter what anyone says, I am Saru-sama, am I not? Because there is no one in the world who can touch (saru) my knowledge, I am called Saru-sama, ehem.'

Teru: 'Indeed, marvelous, it has been thoroughly explained. Characters are things that contain admirable meanings, aren't they?'

Just as they were talking, Kaede called out from beyond the forest with her usual voice,

'Iru-sama, everyone, please return quickly.'

The five of them, without taking time to gather their belongings, went hurrying back toward the office.
