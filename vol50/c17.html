§ 1

The situation at Takahime began to present an unexpectedly ominous scene, a bleak atmosphere where it was hard to tell when a low-pressure system might strike. Iru and the other three became desperate and began to drink sake again, gulping it down. Then Iru shouted in a loud voice,

Iru: 'I am the living shrine of the Giritenjiyō Hinodenokami. Since I am to write the splendid, the most splendid Fudesaki at the living shrine, make preparations of brush, ink, and paper.'

Sāru immediately ground the ink, bound the paper, washed the brush, and respectfully handed them to Iru. Iru snatched the brush while intentionally putting on an arrogant face, placed a wooden desk in front of him, and began to write with all his might while shaking his head at something. Iru, mimicking Takahime's voice, said,

Iru: 'Listen, all of you, or rather, you Yachimataninsoku, now that the Giritenjiyō Hinodenokami has written this Fudesaki, you should read it with gratitude. You will never see such a splendid Fudesaki again.
