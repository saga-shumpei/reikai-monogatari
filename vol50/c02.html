Chapter 2: 照 Demon 灯 [1296]

照魔灯〔1296〕

§ 1

The Tennin of the Reigoku and Tengoku in the Saiau of Takaamahara are all endowed with the Zentoku of love, have achieved the Shinzen of faith, and are filled with Chieshiyōkaku; therefore, unlike the Tennin of the Chiukantengoku and below, they never preach faith, nor do they even know what faith is. Furthermore, they do not engage in discourse regarding the truth of Kami. The reason is that such Reiteki and Tenteki Saikautennin are filled with the Shinkaku of the Ōkami, and Aizenshinshin is the Hontai of the Tennin themselves. Thus, unlike the Tennin of the Takai, they do not argue about truth, saying whether this is truly good or evil. The only ones who argue are those of low degree in the Naibun of the Tennin of the Chiukan and Kasōtenkai. Moreover, the Tennin of the Saiau do not rely on Shikaku, but always on their Chiyōkaku—that is, by the resonance of the five great father-sounds of Aouei that permeate the universe—they make their Chieshiyōkaku increasingly perfect. The Ōmoto Shin-yu reveals, 'Unless you become like a newborn babe, you shall not understand the truth of Kami...' but since the heart of every babe is pure and innocent like crystal, even if their Chieshiyōkaku is inferior, that purity and innocence can immediately harmonize with the Saiau Tenkai. Also, those elders who have cast off social bonds, abandoned all worldly desires, enjoy their later years in leisure, keep far from sin and evil, and enjoy their heaven-ordained fate, manifest that they are beings who possess Chieshiyōkaku and are innocent. When the Kaiso of Ōmoto finished her worldly life, saw off her husband, and entered a life of innocence, only then did Kami, as a prophet, cause a spirit filled with Shinkaku to descend upon her, and she spread the gospel of Tengoku throughout the earth; this was truly because she had been resurrected into a pure and innocent body and soul, and the spirit had been placed in the register of Tengoku. Therefore, one like the Kaiso had already undergone a Reiteki resurrection while still alive. This resurrection is called the rebirth of the Reiteki personality. The Ōkami causes goodness and truth to flow into a human being as their age advances. First, He leads the human into the knowledge of goodness and truth, from there advances them into the wisdom of the immovable and immortal, and finally, from that wisdom, leads them into what Buddhists call the Alaya-vijnana (the eighth consciousness), that is, Chieshiyōkaku. In Buddhism, this is called Anuttara-samyak-sambodhi (supreme enlightenment). However, as modern humans age, they become increasingly adept at cunning; even if they appear on the surface like a retired person enjoying life, a recluse, or a saintly gentleman, the majority are in reality advancing into the realm of the corrupt elderly. Standing in a modern age that regards the survival of the fittest and the law of the jungle as the truth of society, those who lead large factions to run rampant in the political or business worlds, who increasingly indulge in schemes and stratagems to barely maintain their positions, and who regard the grasping of worldly power as supreme glory—such figures, when viewed from the Reikai, are truly pitiable blind men. Rather than such power-holders in the Genkai, there are many humans who, though ignorant, toil at their daily labor, are overwhelmed by the unjust power of modern people, follow them blindly with diligence, and spend their lives in unfortunate circumstances, yet upon reaching the Reikai, they bathe in the grace of Kami, their souls emit light corresponding to their wisdom, and they are wrapped in the virtues of goodness and truth, having inverted their positions from their previous lives. Therefore, from a Reiteki observation, rather than those who are powerful, wealthy, or called wise scholars, it is those who are poor, lowly, weak, and who were trodden underfoot by the world as the smallest in the Genkai, who instead dwell in the virtue of Aizen, shine with the light of Shinzen, and lead a perfect life in the organization of Tengoku. Therefore, believe that there is not a shred of partiality or bias in Kami; if you only love Kami, follow Kami, and faithfully follow the teachings of a righteous prophet, even if you cannot obtain material satisfaction while alive, the joy and pleasure you receive in your Naibun is something that the wealthy, powerful, or wise scholars of the Genkai can never fathom. Hatsuwakahime, the protagonist of this story, once again received the command of Heaven, descended to earth to become the Kaiso of Ōmoto, completed her worldly duties, and in order to give birth to eight children and have them each serve in the internal affairs of the Kamikai, she fulfilled her mission unknowingly, descended to the lowly, and by exerting overflowing benevolence and penetrating wisdom of faith, she served in the divine work of illuminating and breaking through the dark and lawless Jigokukai; having finished her mission, she entrusted the future to a prophet filled with Mizurei, and here she happily ascended and was resurrected. Therefore, from before her death, the Kaiso's appearance was like that of a young girl, her voice was elegant and beautiful, also like a young girl, and she possessed a countenance like a clear, round jewel; those who approached the Kaiso were, before they knew it, influenced by her perfect Reiyō, illuminated by Reikō, and while good people followed and respected her, evil people disliked and feared her. Hatsuwakahime, the previous incarnation of the Kaiso, was also a person of Shinkaku in the age of the gods and was a great prophet. She radiated golden light from her appearance and her whole body, making it impossible for demons to approach her easily. However, Hatsuwakahime deeply hid her Reitoku and Reikō, and in order to teach all people and save them for Tengoku through an attitude of 'harmonizing with the light and mingling with the dust,' she intentionally hid her Kamiso and continued her Reiteki and natural activities. The Kaiso often said... 'If Deguchi Nao were to reveal her true form, the people would be blinded and would never be able to approach her; therefore, I intentionally let myself fall into worldly ruin, and until now, I have been transforming myself for the salvation of all living beings...' It was frequent that she told this story. The officers who were close to her at the time respected the Kaiso's ordinary personality, but because her words were so high-toned, there were many who only thought that the Kaiso had become arrogant. Because Kami always maintains order and manifests the virtue of harmony according to the principle of correspondence, He appears in a way that is not too distant from the person He is facing. Therefore, it is unavoidable that there is an extraordinary difference in the eyes with which people see Kami or the Kaiso, depending on the virtue and wisdom of the person facing them. Kami called Mizugetsu and had him proclaimed through the prophet as the 'Great Monster.' Hearing the name 'Great Monster,' there are not a few modern people who recognize it as a synonym for a great villain, or another name for a schemer, or an evil person who uses honeyed words and flattering looks to decorate themselves with goodness on the surface while practicing falsehoods to deceive the world. However, when one who is filled with Shinkaku is viewed through the eyes of a human registered in the stubborn and spiritless Jigokukai, one is immediately blinded, suffers a headache, becomes breathless, and instantly turns into a madman or a fool, driven by fear, and is unable to perceive the truth. Therefore, when standing in the position of such a human and looking up at a prophet, one can only see them as a Great Monster. Alas, to cause such stubborn followers to bathe in the light of Kami, to dwell in the virtue of Aizen, and to make them live the eternal and infinite life of Tengoku while still alive is truly the greatest difficulty. It was in the fifth year of Taisho. While the narrator was in the office of the officers and opening the Shin-yu, the one who rushed in was Takashima Hisako, the daughter of the Kaiso. Occupied in body and mind by the physical evil spirit as described in the previous section, she ran into my living room, bowed with reverence, and said, 'Mizugetsu-sama, a great event has occurred. I shall inform you of the secret of the one-rin.' Before she could finish, she brought her mouth close to my ear, and while spraying foul breath and saliva from her toothless mouth onto my face, she chattered on about trivial and irrational things. Then Mizugetsu said sternly, 'There is no reason for there to be secrets in the path of truth; the secret of the secret is that it must always be shown. It is not something that should ever be concealed. Whispering in one's ear is not what a Kami-human should do. This is an act dared by a human-beast who is body-master and spirit-servant.' No sooner had he said this than the spirit of Takashima Hisako became greatly enraged, pulled my earlobe with her left hand, slapped my cheek with her right hand, and screamed, 'If you do not listen to the secret advice of the Giritenjō Hinodewake, a great disturbance will arise in the Takaamahara of the earth. No matter what happens, Hinodewake will not know of it,' and she ran about in a frenzy. Then Mizugetsu tried to earnestly persuade her that she was possessed by an evil spirit, but for her, whose spirit and flesh were completely occupied by the evil spirit, it had no effect; moreover, she became greatly enraged, jumped at my throat, and tried to bite me. Then Mizugetsu, having no other choice, pointed his right index finger forward, prayed to Kami with a single 'Un,' and as soon as he illuminated her face with Reikō, she immediately collapsed with a thud. Then Mizugetsu immediately apologized to Kami on her behalf and asked for forgiveness. She stood up with a start, and while screaming at the top of her lungs, 'You dung-slave of the hensei-joshi, you dung-teacher, you devil of the Komatsubayashi,' she ran with the speed of Idaten down the long corridor and invaded the Kaiso's living room.

The Kiyōrei that had possessed Hisako, upon beholding the countenance of the Kaiso, fell backward with a cry of 'Atsu!', and while shrieking 'Kyaa, kyaa!', rolled like a ball along the Nagarauka, fleeing back to my Ima. It then began to act out a Kiyōtai, shouting: 'You Dokaiso, you Kusokaiso! From now on, I am the true Ushitora no Konjin. Both Henjiyōnanshi and Niyoshi, get out of here! From now on, the Takaamahara of the earth shall appear as Takashima Hisako, the Ushitora no Konjin Henjiyōnanshi. I shall bring the Hinodenokami to the earth, use the spirit of Ōhirokimasamunedono for my Goyō, and serve in the Shingefu for the Jiyōjiyu of the Shinsei. Therefore, any of you who do not listen to what I say, leave, every last one of you! The Mitama of Kinkatsukanenokami is strong within me. The Ikimiya of Konohanasakuyahime is also beyond your understanding. From now on, I shall borrow the Karada of this Takashima Hisako and make the truth known.' It shook its body violently, revealed a fierce and evil aspect, and stood glaring in the center of the room like a Nio statue. When the Kaiso came there, leaning on his plum-wood staff, and opened the shoji to peek in for a moment, it shrieked 'Kyaatsu!' once again, collapsed on the spot, and rolled like a ball as it rushed outside. Later, when asked, Takashima Hisako said: 'As soon as the shoji of the Kaiso's Ima was opened, the entire body of the Kaiso was filled with a golden, brilliant light. I could not gaze upon that figure, and I was suddenly struck with such terror that my guardian spirit rushed out with all its might.' She also clearly recorded this in the Fudesaki written by her own hand. After that, Hisako went around to the front and intruded into the Kinryū-den. There, many officials and practitioners were in the midst of Yūsai. Hisako suddenly began to rampage and said: 'You blind officials, Yūsai and such are out of the question! Listen to what this Ikimiya says!' While shouting this, she grabbed a young man named Kawai Yoshio who had come for training, mounted him inside the hall, and began to urinate on the young man's head, saying 'Jiyō, jiyō, jiyō... someone like you is perfect for this!' She continued to act out this Kiyōtai. This, too, is recorded boastfully in the Fudesaki written by the spirit of Takashima Hisako. All evil spirits of the Kiyōtō-kai do not understand order, nor can they distinguish between good and evil, beauty and ugliness; thus, they are proud of urinating on a person's head within the sacred Kinryū-den. Furthermore, she says: 'My guardian spirit is truly great. If an outsider were to urinate in such a holy palace, both the guardian spirit and the physical body would immediately be struck by divine punishment. But no matter what I do, because my divine rank is high, I was not punished in the slightest.' To boast of such things is to be a truly difficult case to save. She is a demented fool who does not know the principle that is the same as when cats or rats build nests within the sacred doors where the Ōkami resides, or even defecate and urinate there; the God overlooks it as mere animals and does not punish them. People who are strong in self-love and who think that only worldly love is the sole good, conversely take such eccentric behavior as a mysterious act, and rejoicing in it with longing, they say: 'That conduct is absolutely not human. With a human heart, how could one mount a person's head and urinate within the hall? It is truly proof of the God.' Thus, they are impressed. As they say, it is certainly not human. Postscript: Among the readers of the Reikai Monogatari, it seems there are some who criticize that because there are no humble words such as 'it is so...' or 'I think so...' and it is dictated in a definitive and high-handed manner such as 'it is...' or 'it is that...', the so-called narrator is conceited and uses such insolent language. However, if one says 'arimasu', one must use four characters of type. If one says 'aru', the matter is settled with two characters. Therefore, in such a voluminous story, it has come from a faithful intention to omit even one redundant word and to let the reader know as many meanings as possible. And since the narrator himself is merely entrusting his spirit and body to the Seirei filled with divinity, even if the narrator tries to change this, it is unavoidable if the Seirei who is in charge of the essential part does not listen. 其後かれ悪霊は久子の肉体に対し、いろいろと幻覚を示し、益々誑惑の淵に陥れ、或は一ケ月間の断食を与へ、地獄の有様を眼前に髣髴せしめ……汝わが言を用ゐざる時は、斯の如き無間地獄に陥落すべし。又わが言を信従し、わが頤使に従つて活動する時は、汝をして将来斯の如き結構なる位地につかしむべし……と、或はたらし或は威し、漸くにして開祖の身内たる肉体を、わが自由に駆使する事を得たのである。彼等が悪業を遂行せむとすれば、現界人の浅薄なる識見より見て、開祖の血統と生れし人間なれば、大丈夫、決して悪神の憑依すべきものでないと信用させ得るの便宜があるからである。かれ兇霊は無智なる久子の霊肉を完全に占領した上、地の高天原の霊光にゐたたまらず、二三の迷へる信者を引連れ、一目散に八木へさして逃げ帰り、ここに久子の記憶と信仰を基礎として、其想念中に深く入り込み、兇党界の団体をして益々大ならしめ、大神の神業を極力妨害せむと企みつつあるのである。さりながら久子其人は元来開祖を思ふ事最も深く且無智にして比較的其心も清ければ、遉の兇霊も開祖の神諭を非難することを得ず、且又厳の御霊、瑞の御霊を極力排斥し、誹謗しては其目的を完成し得ざるを知るが故に、表面に厳瑞二霊を尊敬し信従する如く装ひ、先づ久子を誑惑し其口と手を以て世人を魔道に引入れむと企みつつあるのである。之は決して瑞月が卑しき心より述ぶるのではない。大神の御子たる可憐なる精霊や人間をして、一人なりとも邪道に陥らざらしめむが為の慈愛心に外ならぬのである。かれ精霊は久子の肉体を綾部の停車場に仰向けに倒し、陰部を曝して大呼して云ふ……われは地の高天原の変性男子出口直の肉体をかりて生れた日出神の生宮であるぞよ。皆の者、これを見て、大本の教を悟れよ……と呶鳴り立てた。精霊が久子に斯かる衆人環視の前にて狂態を演ぜしめた其底意は、要するに神の名を冒涜し、世人をして大本を信用せしめざらむが為の悪計であつた。されど暗愚なる信者は、そんな所に少しも注意せずしていふ……ああ吾々が改心が足らぬ故に神様が変性男子の系統の肉宮をかつて、お戒め下さつたのであらう、お前達の心は此通り醜いのだ、お前達が神界より罰せられ、地獄の釜のドン底へ落されるのだが、高島久子に千座の置戸を負はして助けてやつて居るぞよ、との深き思召であらう……などと妙な所へ曲解して益々随喜渇仰し、精霊の誑惑に乗せられて、遠近の神社を調査するといつて、或は其費用を献じ、或は随伴してゐる者も沢山現はれて来たのは、実に神界の為悲しむべきことである。されど神は決して斯の如き兇霊に汚され給ふものでもなく、又如何に妨害せむとするも聊かの痛痒も感じ給はないのである。只々可憐なる神の御子が彼等兇霊に心身を誑惑され邪神界に引入れられ、無間地獄に陥落しゆくを悲しみ給ふのみである。かかる仁慈無限の大神の御心も知らず、男子が何うだとか、女子の言行がなんだとか云つて、その光明に反き、醜穢極まる地獄に転移するは、実に仁慈の目より見て忍び難き所である。かれ精霊は久子を又もや八木の停車場に連れ行き、大声叱呼して云ふ……此女は元を糺せば、丹波国何鹿の郡綾部町、本宮新宮坪の内、変性男子の身魂出口直の体をかり、出口政五郎といふ父を持ち、若い時から男女と呼ばれたる、ヤンチヤ娘の出口久子、今は神の因縁に依つて、八木の高島寅之助が妻となり、あの山の、山のほでらのあばらや住居、今はおちぶれて居れども、結構な身魂が世におとしてあるぞよ。侮りて居りたものは、アフンと致してあいた口がすぼまらぬぞよ。今に天地がかへるぞよ。欲を致して沢山の金をためて居りても、其宝は持切には出来ぬ宝であるぞよ。此神の申した事には一分一厘間違ひはないぞよ。先をみてゐて下されよ、と前をまくつて大音声……と自ら呼ばはり、停車場に集まる人々を驚かせ、之を鎮定せむと入り来りし長左といふ男の腕にかぶりつき、狂態を演じ、大本の教を破壊せむと企んだ事もあつたのである。兇霊は此筆法を以て、或時は変性男子を極力賞讃し、また対者の心の中に男子女子を否むと認むる時は、声を秘そめて切りに誹謗し、吾薬籠中のものとなさむと企むものである。

追伸霊界物語の読者の中には凡て、斯様であります……とか、斯う考へます……とかいふ謙譲の言葉がなく、かうである……どうである……などと断定的に、且高圧的に口述してあるのは、所謂口述者が慢心した結果、かかる不遜の言辞を弄するのだと非難する人が間々あるさうです。併しながら『あります』と云へば活字を四字用ひなくてはなりませぬ。『ある』といへば二字で事がすみます。それ故にかかる洪瀚な物語には一字なりとも冗言を省き、可成数多の意味を読者に知らさむが為の忠実なる意思より出でたのであります。而して口述者自身は只神格にみたされたる聖霊に霊と体を任せきつてゐるのでありますから、口述者が之を改めようと致しましても、肝腎の局に当る聖霊が聞かなければ是非ない事であります。一寸茲に一言断つておく次第であります。
