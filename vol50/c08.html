Chapter 8: 常世 Gloom [1302]

常世闇〔1302〕

§ 1

Most Ningen do not have their Naibun fully opened toward Takaamahara. Therefore, it is common for the Ōkami to govern Ningen through Seirei. This is because Ningen are born amidst the various Zaiaku of the Jigokukai, which are produced from Shizenai and Jigokuai, and thus exist in a Jiyōtai that is in Haihan to Kamunagara, or the Shintekijiyunjiyo. However, once one is born as a Ningen, it is necessary for them to Fukukuwatsu and Kisei within the Jiyunjiyo of Kamunagara by any means. Yet, this path of Fukukuwatsu and Kisei is difficult to achieve without passing through Seirei indirectly. Nevertheless, if it is a Shinjin like Hatsuwakahime, who is the protagonist of this Monogatari, she was born from the beginning amidst the various virtues based on the Shintekijiyunjiyo of Takaamahara, and therefore, there is never a need to Fukukuwatsu and Kisei through Seirei. When a Ningen reaches the mysterious state of Shinjin-wago, they are not governed by the Ōkami through the medium of Seirei, but are governed by the direct inflow of the Ōkami according to the Jiyunjiyo, that is, the providence of Kamunagara.

The direct inflow coming from the Ōkami originates from the divine personality of God and enters into the will of Ningen, from there it enters their intellect, and thus it enters their goodness and then through that goodness enters into truth. To enter into truth is, in short, to enter into love. After passing through this love, one enters into holy faith. Therefore, this inflow never enters into faith without love, nor into truth without goodness, nor into intellect that does not originate from the will. Thus, since one like Hatsuwakahime can be called a person of a pure and immaculate divine personality, there is nothing in what she thinks, says, or does that is not in unity with the great divine heart of God. Such a Shinjin is called a true living god.

The reason why Tenjin and Seirei are so closely united with Ningen, and why they consider everything belonging to Ningen as if it were their own, is because Ningen are the organs of harmony between the spiritual world and the phenomenal world, existing in such close proximity that the two can almost be regarded as one. However, because modern Ningen have naturally closed their Naibun due to greed for Takaamahara and have come to be far removed from Takaamahara where the Ōkami resides, the Ōkami has performed a certain Keirin here, causing Tenjin and Seirei to dwell with each individual Ningen, and has adopted the method of governing Ningen through Tenjin, that is, the main guardian spirits, and Seirei, the righteous guardian spirits.

The Seirei that has invaded the body of Takahime, especially that most evil spirit among them, is constantly exchanging language with Takahime, yet in reality, it does not actually believe that Takahime is a Ningen. It firmly believes that the body of Takahime is its own physical body. Therefore, although Takahime negotiates with the Seirei in various ways, in truth, the Seirei thinks that although it is invisible to others, there is a Seirei called Takahime, and it is exchanging conversation with itself from the outside. Furthermore, on the part of the Seirei, it believes that the physical body of Takahime knows nothing at all, and that what is known is only due to the knowledge of the Seirei itself; consequently, it believes that all things known by Takahime are entirely its own doing. However, because Takahime frequently tells the Seirei, 'You are nesting in my body,' the Seirei, that is, the evil spirit possessing her, has begun to feel, albeit vaguely, that perhaps there is a kind of strange animal body called Takahime that has entered into it besides itself. Takahime also thinks that what the Seirei says and knows is what she herself says and knows, and she believes that because her physical body was specially created as an organ with a connection to the Keirin of the divine world, the main guardian spirits and deputy guardian spirits have come to borrow lodging. And interestingly, the Seirei dwelling within Takahime's body makes various incoherent prophecies and writes Fudesaki based on Takahime's memories and thoughts, and without being aware that it is unreasonable and full of falsehoods, it believes everything to be good and firmly believes it to be truth; therefore, even while saying that it is an evil god or planning evil, it is not true evil, and it is strange because it feels as if it is teasing something other than itself. Takahime herself also tries to think, 'Might this be a slightly evil way of acting?' or at other times, 'No, no, there is not a speck of evil in what I think and do. It must look evil because people of the phenomenal world who do not understand the reason are observing our words and deeds, which are filled with divinity. The true God will surely praise me for acting as one in a thousand for the sake of God and the Way. I am likely being sent as a divine pillar as one who fulfills God's will. Even if the people of the phenomenal world who do not understand the reason call me a devil, I cannot be bothered by such things; only God knows the work I do.' She maintains such a calm attitude, believing that there is no true teaching or truth to be preached or practiced by anyone other than herself, and because she must show the model of supreme goodness and supreme love to the world and bear the thousand-seat Oki-do to save the Zaiaku of all people, she believes she is a sacrifice and savior sent by God, and thus she is beyond help. It goes without saying that not only Takahime, but also the self-proclaimed prophets and self-proclaimed saviors who are springing up like bamboo shoots after rain in the world are all similar to Takahime.

高姫の身体に侵入したる精霊、中にも最も兇悪なる彼兇霊は、常に高姫と言語を交換してゐるものの、その実高姫が人間なる事を実際に信じてゐないのである。高姫の身体は即ち自分の肉体と固く信じてゐるのである。故に高姫が精霊に対して色々と談判をすると雖も、其実精霊の意思では他に目には見えないけれども、高姫なる精霊があつて、外部より自分に向つて談話の交換をしてゐる様に思つて居るのである。又精霊の方に於ては、高姫の肉体は決して何も知つて居ない、知つてゐるのは只精霊自身の知識によるものと思ひ、従つて高姫が知つてゐる所の一切の事物は、皆自分の所為と信じ居るものである。併しながら高姫が余りに……俺の肉体にお前は巣喰つて居るのだ……と、精霊に向つて屡告ぐるによつて、彼に憑依せる精霊即ち兇霊は、うすうすながら自分以外に高姫といふ一種異様の動物の肉体に這入つて居るのではあるまいか……位に感じだしたのである。高姫は又精霊の言ふ所、知る所を、自分の言ふ所、知る所と思惟し、而して精霊が、自分の肉体は神界経綸の因縁のある機関として特別に造られたのだから、正守護神や副守護神が宿を借りに来て居るものと信じて居るのである。而して面白い事には、高姫の体内に居る精霊は、高姫の記憶と想念を基としていろいろと支離滅裂な予言をしたり、筆先を書いたりしながら、其不合理にして虚偽に充てる事を自覚せず、凡てを善と信じ、真理と固く信じてゐるのだから、自分が悪神だと云つたり、或は悪を企まうなどと言つてゐながらも、決して真の悪ではない、実は自分が或自己以外の何物かと揶揄つて居るやうな気でゐるのだから不思議である。又高姫自身も、少し許り悪の行り方ではあるまいかと思うて見たり、或時は……イヤイヤ決して自分の思ふ事、行ふ所は微塵も悪がない、只訳の分らぬ人間の目から、神格に充されたる吾々の言行を観察するのだから悪に見えるだらう。真の神は必ず自分が神の為道の為に千騎一騎の活動してゐる事をキツトお褒め遊ばすだらう。神に叶へるものとして、神柱とお使ひ遊ばしてゐられるのであらう。訳の分らぬ現界の人間が、仮令悪魔と言はうとも、そんな事は構つてゐられない、吾がなす業は神のみぞ知り給ふ……といふ様な冷静な態度を構へ、如何なる真の教示も、真理も、自己以外に説くものはない、又行ふ真の人間もないのだから、至善至愛の標本を天下に示し、千座の置戸を負うて万民の罪悪を救うてやらねばならぬ。自分は神の遣はし給ふ犠牲者、救世主だと信じて居るのだから始末に了へぬのである。高姫のみならず、世の中に雨後の筍の如く、ムクムクと簇生する自称予言者、自称救世主なども、すべては高姫に類したものなることは言ふ迄もない事である。

§ 2

Furthermore, Dōbutsu are governed by the general Nairiu from the Seireikai; indeed, because the Shiyōgai of these Dōbutsu resides within the Honrai Jiyunjiyochiu of the Uchiu, all Dōbutsu possess no Risei. Because they lack Risei, they cannot Hairei against the Shintekijiyunjiyo, nor can they Hakuwai it. This is where Ningen and Dōbutsu differ. However, an Eibin Reijiu such as Smart, whose Seirei is almost like that of a Ningen and who possesses both the Honrai Jiyunboku Seishin and the same Risei as a Ningen, was able to Dōsatsu the Ishi of a Shinjin and serve like a Chiuboku. All Dōbutsu are capable of acting by receiving the Nairiu of the Seirei possessed by Ningen. However, ordinary Dōbutsu lack Risei in their Reikon, and thus cannot receive the Nairiu of a Tenjin of the earth such as Hatsuwakahime. Yet, although this Smart is a Dōbutsu in body, it appeared in a way necessary to protect the surroundings of Hatsuwakahime by a special method from the Lord God, namely, by the method of Kaso. Hatsuwakahime also perceives this message well, so she never treats it as an ordinary dog. Only because she knows that the Lord God has manifested a part of His Shinkaku through Kaso, can she live in harmony and affection with it, sometimes as a sister, sometimes as a servant, and at other times as a friend. When an ordinary Ningen harmonizes with a Dōbutsu, it is a case of having fallen completely into the Chikushōdō. Furthermore, when a Ningen is in the Jigokukai and Seireikai after the separation of spirit and flesh, if their Nenryo to inflict harm upon an enemy in the present world is strong, they harmonize with the Seirei of a Dōbutsu to clear their resentment. Therefore, a Dōbutsu spirit always accompanies a Ningen possessed by an Ikiryō or a Shiryō. This is why, when performing Chinkon on a Ningen suffering from a certain serious illness, or when investigating it by reciting Shingen, the name of the Ningen's Ikiryō or Shiryō is always given. And when a skilled Saniwa strictly questions it, it finally confesses that the Ningen spirit and the Dōbutsu spirit have harmonized and become the vanguard of that Ningen spirit. When the spirits of foxes, tanuki, snakes, toads, dogs, cats, and other Dōbutsu come to a Ningen, they enter the memory and thoughts of the Ningen, use the mouth and tongue of their body, or take on the thoughts of the Ningen spirit with which they are driven and united, and come to utter language like a Ningen. Scholars who are ignorant of the messages of the Reikai mock this, saying that it is impossible for foxes and other Dōbutsu to possess a Ningen and use human language, that those who believe such things are primitive savages of ancient times, and that to believe in the absurdity of Dōbutsu possessing a Ningen and speaking human language in the present day, when human culture has developed so much, is the height of insanity and dementia; such is the discourse of modern half-baked scholars. How could they know that they themselves are the truly pitiable, stubborn, and foolish ones, and indeed the insane ones, when viewed from the Reikai? How could a Ningen, who cannot see their own eyes with their own eyes, nor see their own head, neck, or back, possibly understand the profound and subtle truth and reality of the Reikai? Ningen must kneel before the Lord God and apologize for their stupidity, ignorance, and arrogance.

Dōbutsu, for example, dogs, cats, deer, cows, and horses, follow Kannagara, that is, the Shintekijiyunjiyo, so their mating periods are fixed, and they never engage in self-indulgent acts like Ningen, such as going into heat at any time. Also, plants and the like conform to the order of the Reikai and the natural world, blooming and bearing fruit, sprouting tender buds, and shedding leaves at times determined in a Kannagara manner, and their order is so orderly that we Ningen can never even reach their feet. Thus, dogs are dogs, cats are cats, and horses are horses, each exerting its own innate characteristics and fully manifesting the nature adapted to its circumstances. Furthermore, plants and the like each possess their own characteristics, manifesting their own inherent instincts of sweetness, spiciness, sourness, bitterness, etc., and have not changed their taste since ten thousand years ago. In short, a taro cannot replace the taste of an eggplant, and a chili pepper will never become the taste of a mandarin orange. Also, even while planted in the same field and absorbing the same soil, a watermelon still has the taste of a watermelon, a chili pepper the taste of a chili pepper, a chestnut the taste of a chestnut, and a persimmon the taste of a persimmon, each having its own unique form and taste. And this uniqueness all comes from the spiritual, and the degree of its growth and prosperity depends on the light, heat, and fertility of the soil in the natural world. However, because Ningen possess what is called Risei, when the soil changes a little or when they move to a land with a drastic change in climate, they suddenly change their Ishi, and those who have been abroad for ten years end up with thoughts exactly like those of foreigners. This is the point where Ningen differ from Dōbutsu or plants. Thus, while Ningen have the convenience of being able to change their thoughts and even the color of their bodies to some extent through Risei, they are also prone to move toward evil and fall easily. Therefore, the Lord God never has the trouble of preaching to Dōbutsu and plants and is extremely at ease, but because Ningen possess a nature of evil that does not follow the Shintekijiyunjiyo like Dōbutsu and plants, He especially sent down prophets to teach them to follow the heavenly order. However, because the worlds of both good and evil are open to Ningen, they can also, from another perspective, become the organs of the Lord God. I wish that we Ningen would love the Lord God, believe in the Lord God, and being loved by the Lord God, stand as living shrines of the Lord God to serve the purpose of the Lord God's creation of heaven and earth.

Sate, Takahime, intending to gather tamatake, climbed stealthily onto the branch of an Ōsugi, but was pecked in both eyes by an owl with its sharp beak, crying out 'Atsu!' and falling to the ground like a mekurazaru, where she groaned in unbearable pain from striking her koshibone; she was then cared for by Iru, Iku, Saru, and others, and finally carried to her ima. However, as Takahime was by nature a woman of fortitude, she was not the type to be troubled by a mere dislocation of the koshibone. Furthermore, due to her nature, she could absolutely never speak of being in pain or suffering. Yet, having been pecked in both eyes, her ganken immediately became jiuketsu, swelled shut, and she was unable to see the kuōmiyō, which caused even the indomitable Takahime a great deal of meiwaku.

Iru: 'Saa, Takahime-sama, this is your ima. Maa, please rest here until you are fully honpuku. Since there are kutsukiyō men like Iru, Iku, Saru, Haru, and Teru here, we will take care of you in every way, so please feel at ease and rely on us.'

Takahime: 'Are you Iru? Oh, thank you for your kindness. Now that it has come to this, my eyes are swollen shut and I cannot see a thing, so I have no choice but to rely on your sewa. I suppose my eyes will be able to see once this swelling goes down, so I am sorry to trouble you, but please care for me for two or three days. Ah, what a miserable thing this is. Just at this moment, Mokusuke-sama stumbled and fell, breaking his brow and suffering, and in my desire to heal that pain, I climbed up to gather tamatake, only to be pecked in both eyes by that tengu living in the Ōsugi and meet with such a miserable fate like a monkey fallen from a tree... Ah, what could the gods be thinking? Even the Lord God is being too harsh...'
