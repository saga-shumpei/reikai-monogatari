Chapter 12: 敵愾心 [1306]

敵愾心〔1306〕

§ 1

At the sound of Kaede's cry, the Renchiu of Zubu-roku, without even taking time to gather their belongings, came rushing to this place in a scattered manner. Takahime glared at the five drunkards, and raising her voice, she spoke:

Takahime: 'Hey, you Mōroku wretches, why have you come here in such a noisy, bustling crowd? Because you are saying Futsugafu things, Giritenjiyō Hinodenokami is administering Setsukan. If you interfere unnecessarily and meddle in this, I shall not have Reuken for it.'

Iru: 'Hey, Iru-sama, Iku-sama, please release Tenjiyō-sama quickly.'

Iru: 'Yes, very well, I have come to save you.'

He climbed up onto the Zashiki. Takahime said:

Takahime: 'Eeei, you Koshiyaku Mōroku wretches!'

She struck him hard in the chest. Iru staggered, reeled, and fell backward from the Engaha. Seeing this, the four, Iku, Saru, Haru, and Teru, while swaying unsteadily, were nevertheless high-spirited and attempted to rush at Takahime with a Mushiyabu. Takahime released Kaede, spread her Riyōte, and using her strength, pushed the approaching fellows with a grunt. Since they were all dead drunk, they could not withstand it; they were unable to resist even the Hiriki of Takahime and were thrown out in a Shiyōgidafu. Then, the five seemed to have their vision blurred, and they were suffering while foaming at the mouth. Takahime stood in the Mannaka of the Zashiki in a large Jigata, and in a Daionjiyō, she declared:

Takahime: 'The Shinriki of the Ikimiya of Giritenjiyō Hinodenokami is just like this. Tatohe there be Hiyakunin, Sennin, or Ichimanbiki, come if you will; now is the time for the Goshintoku to manifest.'

She boasted, riding high on her own conceit. She had become so preoccupied with what was in front of her that she had completely forgotten that Kaede was behind her. Kaede quietly swept Takahime's legs from behind. With a cry of 'Ah!', she fell over with a thud, and with the excess momentum, she rolled over again and collapsed with a heavy thud on top of the five. Meanwhile, Kaede, in order to report the emergency to her parents, ran barefoot toward the shrine from the back entrance. Takahime struck her knees and shins against the floorboards, grimaced, and without even raising her voice, she groaned with deep breaths, 'Uun, uun.' The five drunkards finally managed to get up, and seeing Takahime fallen there, Iru said:

Iru: 'Hey, Takahime, do you know who you are dealing with? It is Iru-sama. Did you not suffer a divine punishment, have your eyes pecked by a bird of prey, fall down from a tree with a crash, and suffer great hardship? Do you remember that it was I, Iru-sama, who nursed you back to health at that time? When you are too evil, this is what happens. Ee, you! Why did you beat Kaede-sama, who is the beloved woman of my heart? Sa, sa, you, since you are lying there, I shall take revenge for my beloved. Ee, hey, Kaede-sama, I am going to take revenge for you now, so watch closely.'

Iku: 'Hey, Iru, has Kaede-sama not run away?'

Iru: 'Uun, if the principal person is not here, it feels somehow lacking. If it is not a feat in front of the horse, being a supporter behind the scenes is no fun.'

Saru: 'This old woman is again, and again, a person who does nothing but violent things. After she fell from the tree and suffered greatly, causing us trouble, the moment she thought her illness was cured, she started acting up again and beating such a cute girl; she is an outrageous wretch. I would like to take Kaede-sama's side and beat this old woman until she says she has had enough. Hey, Takahime, you, if you keep spinning up and down and using magic like that, even I find it hard to catch you. Won't you be quiet for a moment? Hey, look, Iku, Haru, Iru, and even Teru are spinning around, one on top of the other, and the house has even started to spin. Hey, it is a great earthquake. We must stop this small quarrel and go out as an emergency squad; hey, everyone, this is not the time to be spinning around in such a place.'

§ 2

Takahime, finding that the severe pain from her wounds had subsided considerably, rose up with a start.

Takahime: 'Hey, you five senile fools, what do you take this Takahime's Giritenjiyōsama to be? To get drunk on sake without permission, and on top of that, to raise your hands against Ikimiyasama—what is the meaning of this? Your lack of common sense has reached its limit.'

Teru: 'W-w-what are you spewing, you mastermind of evil? You must have gone insane because you lost track of the old man. Hey, you lot, don't you dare engage with such a lunatic.'

Takahime: 'Hey, I will not forgive you anymore; this is for the sake of making an example of you. Taste the iron fist of Hinodenokami.'

She clenched her fists and began to strike out from the small entrance. At this moment, from behind, came the one known as Smart, growling, 'Utsu, utsu.'

Smart, ensuring Takahime would not be injured, caught her hem in his teeth and, using his immense strength, dragged her backward, thudding along the ground.

Takahime, with her lower body exposed, cried out, 'Hey, Iru, Iku, Saru, will you not come to my aid? Hey, hey!' as she was dragged into the forest with a series of thuds.

The five, stunned by this terrifying display of Smart's power, felt their drunkenness fade; they stood with their mouths agape, stretching their necks to watch her go as she was dragged away into the forest while still screaming.

Kaede ran back in a flurry.

Kaede: 'Ah, everyone, thank you so much for saving me. Thanks to you, I have escaped the poisonous fangs of Takahime. Who was it that rushed here first?'

Iru: 'Heh, it was I. Just as we five were sobering up at the reception desk, (in a theatrical tone) suddenly I heard a strange voice, and the cry of a beautiful young woman at that... Hearing it, I went mad with frenzy, thinking I must save her... I tightened my headband, crossed my sash, pulled up the legs of my hakama, and pointing this way, I ran at full speed, dash, dash, dash. When I arrived, I saw the fool of heaven, the Ikimiyasama of Hinodenokami, who is no better than a tiger or wolf, daring to strike my beloved Kaedehime-sama—how outrageous! Just as I rushed in to cut her down, the enemy's strength must have been superior; I was struck hard in the chest, and staggering, wobbling, I fell backward from the veranda, crashing down with a thud, shattering my skull and bruising my hip bones. But thinking that it was a sacrifice for my beloved Kaedehime-sama, I was prepared to die... Truly a brave warrior, am I not?'

Kaede: 'Ho ho ho, how very energetic you are. That is why I dislike it when you men get drunk on sake.'

Saru: 'Ah ha ha ha, hey Iru, where is that headband you are wearing? Where are your crossed sash and your pulled-up hakama? Do not make such a fool of yourself. If you say such things, Kaede-sama will laugh at you.'

Iru: 'What, I was just acting a little bit. Ah ha ha ha ha.'

Kaede: 'By the way, everyone, where has Takahime-sama gone?'

Iru: 'What, do not worry. By now, she is surely being devoured by that fierce dog. Since that dog could not possibly eat such a large body entirely, if even one arm remains, we can just bury that. Well, it is a good thing that your formidable enemy has perished, so please set your mind at ease. As long as such a man of matchless valor as I remains barricaded in this mansion, no matter what enemy comes rushing, why should I ever flinch? I shall trample them into dust, kick them down, mow them down, and in an instant, I shall display my glorious achievements and raise a shout of victory. Princess, please, by all means, do not worry yourself.'

He continued in his theatrical manner.

While carving the ground with a military gait, he ran off in search of the thicket where Takahime had been dragged away. Watching from behind, Hatsuwakahime pressed her hands together and prayed to the Shinkai for the safety of Takahime.

Kaede: 'Hime-sama, that old woman who calls herself Giritenjiyō is truly a reckless person. Whenever I recall the excessive Gofutaku she speaks, I cannot help but feel furious. If you had not helped us, my father and mother would surely be dead by now. As a child, how could I possibly remain silent about this? If I do not punish her a little, there is no telling when she might poison my father or mother. For the past week, I have hardly been able to sleep at night. I am so worried about the fate of my parents that I cannot help myself.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Ah, that is indeed reasonable. However, leave everything to Kamisama. As the Ananai-kyō Sendenka says... Kami shall appear in the world and distinguish between good and evil... so surely, Kamisama will take vengeance upon the Akunin. It is safer for humans to leave everything to Kamisama, you see.'

Kaede: 'Yes, thank you very much.'

Uzuhiko: 'Hime-sama, you have spoken to her well. We, as a couple, are finally at ease because of this. In truth, we have not been able to sleep a wink since the other day. We did not know when this Kaede might take up a dagger and, calling them the enemies of her parents, strike at Takahime-sama; so for the past seven or eight days, we have not slept a single moment at night. We prayed to the Ōkami that there would be no impropriety. Thanks to that, we have managed to live safely until today. We are truly grateful. Even while Takahime-sama was suffering from her great illness, we should have visited, but as our daughter would not listen, we were unintentionally very rude. Furthermore, as for our daughter, she asked us to send her to visit Takahime-sama, but she would not let go of her dagger, and it was too dangerous, so we did not send her to visit, nor did we go ourselves; it is no wonder that Takahime-sama feels dissatisfied. No matter what, she is a daughter who says she will take vengeance on her parents' enemies and performs the hour-of-the-ox pilgrimage to that unpleasant Yamaguchi forest. She is truly a difficult person, a girl with such a strong sense of hostility that there is nothing one can do. Please, with your power, speak to her thoroughly and make her understand.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Kaede-sama, you must be angry, but please come and visit my room. Let us sing some interesting songs and play. Come, let us go.'

Saying this, she gently took Kaede by the hand, and the two seventeen-year-old girls walked toward the room of Hatsuwakahime.
