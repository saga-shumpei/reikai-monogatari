Chapter 6: Jewel 茸 [1300]

玉茸〔1300〕

§ 1

Takahime searched the forest of the shrine thoroughly. At last, she caught sight of Mokusuke sleeping in the grass while groaning in pain.

Takahime: 'Hey, Mokusuke-sama, come on, let us return. It would be a disaster if you caught a cold lying in a place like this. Come, will you not return home with me?'

Mokusuke: 'Do not go on so noisily. My head aches just a little, so I shall rest here for a while, enjoying the natural earth, and then return; you had better go over there and rest.'

Takahime: 'Hey, Mokusuke-sama, what a distant thing to say. Are we not a couple who pledged our lives to each other for two generations? A husband's hardship is a wife's hardship, and a wife's joy is a husband's joy; are we not a couple who pledged to share both joy and sorrow to the end? There is no need for such reserve at all. Come, please, let me nurse you.'

Mokusuke: 'No, please do not bother, I beg you, do not touch my body, I ask you...'

Takahime: 'Hey, you person, you have come to dislike me, have you not? Where is the logic in a wife not touching her husband's body? Can I, as a wife, leave my husband's illness alone and pretend not to know? Once I, Takahime, have made a marriage vow with you, even if this body were to turn to dust, I must remain faithful to the end. You should not say such distant things.'

Mokusuke: 'Hey, do not take offense just because I said this. It is not that I dislike you at all. Come, please return to the mansion quickly and take care of everything.'

Takahime: 'Even if you say that, can I, however strong-willed I may be, leave you behind and return? Shall I go and find some medicine for you?'

Mokusuke: 'No, I need nothing. If I were to receive such precious things, I might even incur divine punishment. That I have been injured like this is entirely a warning from the gods. If the gods permit it, they will surely heal me. No matter how much a human struggles, there is nothing to be done.'

Takahime: 'In that case, shall I recite the Amatsu Norito for you?'

Mokusuke: 'No, that is unnecessary. That things have come to this is also the will of the gods. To recite the Norito would be, on the contrary, too fearful. I beg you, please return to the mansion and attend to your duties. That is the greatest merit of all. If you do that, my injury will surely heal soon.'

Takahime: 'In that case, shall I perform Chinkon for you? ...One, two, three, four...'

As she began to say this, Mokusuke stopped her in a panic, letting out a pained voice.

Mokusuke: 'Takahime, that is unnecessary. Please do not say such wasteful things. It only makes me suffer more.'

Takahime: 'In that case, shall I go and call Hatsuwakahime-sama? If I let her nurse you, perhaps it will be to your liking. After all, with the nursing of an old woman like me, your illness will only grow worse.'

She began to sharpen her words, feeling a bit jealous.

This Mokusuke was, in truth, a fierce spirit possessing a body known as the arm of the Yamata no Orochi, which dwelt on Mount Daigun in the capital of Haruna; he was a monster of both lion and tiger nature, a great creature called Yogenbo. Yogenbo had been bitten in the brow by Smart and was continuing to suffer great agony here. However, fearing that Takahime might see through his true form, he wished for nothing more than for Takahime to leave this place as soon as possible. Yet Takahime, convinced that she must nurse her precious husband no matter what, would not move from that spot at all. For Yogenbo-Mokusuke, maintaining a human form while wounded was an immense pain. As he was on the verge of revealing his true form through carelessness, he raised his voice and spoke in a slightly sharp tone,

Mokusuke: 'Hey, Takahime, why will you not listen to what your husband says? You call yourself Takahime, but are you not quite a strong-willed person? Are you going to put the essential work of the Kamisama second, distracted by your husband's illness? If you are such a thoughtless person, then there is no help for it. I will sever our ties, so please go your own way.'

Takahime: 'Mokusuke-sama, you must have hit your head and are suffering from a bit of delirium. If that were not the case, you would never say such cold things. Oh, what a pitiful thing. Oh, Kamunagara Tamachihahe Mase.'

Mokusuke: 'Look here, Takahime, as I said just before, I am under the discipline of the Kamisama, so did I not tell you not to say such things? Why do you not heed your husband's words?'

Takahime: 'Oh, please do not say such things. When I spent a thousand words persuading Smart, whom Hatsuwakahime cherished so much, saying it was by my father's command, Hatsuwaka finally followed my instructions and struck Smart with five large stones, cracked its skull, and it bled terribly; so Smart, crying out in a shrill, piercing voice, ran away. By now, it must surely have collapsed and died somewhere near the valley of Kajitōge.'

Mokusuke: 'What, you say Hatsuwakahime struck Smart with stones and sent it away? Ya, that is excellent. Truly, children are something to be treasured.'

Takahime: 'Well, I suppose so. She is a daughter who would not be painful even if she entered your eyes. You say that wives are not to be treasured, which is a cold-hearted sentiment, is it not?'

Mokusuke: 'Do not nag me about such things while my head is already aching. Once the pain stops, I will listen to your complaints or whatever else you have. More than that, hurry home, call Hatsuwakahime, and bring her here. And could you, without anyone knowing, climb the great cedar tree in front of the reception desk and pick the mushroom called Tamatake? If I only have that, my illness will be cured at once.'

Takahime: 'In that case, shall I have Haru or Iru set up a ladder to pick it?'

Mokusuke: 'No, no, this must be done in secret for the efficacy to manifest. Even if my daughter Hatsuwakahime finds out, it will be ineffective. In any case, rather than calling Hatsuwakahime, can you not quickly and quietly go and pick that Tamatake? Your husband is joining his hands and begging his wife...'

With tears streaming from both eyes, he bowed down to Takahime. Takahime, flustered and with tears welling in her eyes, said,

Takahime: 'Hey, Mokusuke-sama, in what world does a husband join his hands and beg his wife? In that case, I will go and get the Tamatake now. Please wait here for a while. I know it must be painful, though.'

Mokusuke: 'That is a great trouble. Please be careful not to get hurt, and pick it so that no one sees you. Until then, you must not tell Hatsuwakahime or anyone else.'

Takahime: 'Yes, I have understood everything. In that case, I will go and get it for you now. For a while, stay here... Ne, Mokusuke-sama, you are also the spirit of Marishiten-sama, so you should not easily die from a wound of this degree.'

Mokusuke: 'Yes, that is right, I am fine. This is your first act of fidelity toward your husband, so please be careful not to get hurt while picking the Tamatake.'

Takahime: 'Yes, I understand.'

With that, she wiggled her large hips and, looking back at Mokusuke's fallen figure again and again, she trotted over and quietly hid herself in the shadow of the great cedar tree in front of the reception desk. Yōgenbō, who had transformed into Mokusuke, felt a sense of relief, reverted to his original monster form, and thinking that if he did not leave this place at once, the pain would be unbearable if he were discovered by people again, he followed the flow of the valley, rustling as he crossed over the mountain ridge, lay down in a hollow, sunny spot on the other side, and, fortunate that there was no one around, groaned in agony.

§ 3

Meanwhile, Takahime of Itsupau hid herself in the shade of the trees, pondering the state of the Uketsuke. Iru and Haru were huddled around a Hibachi in the Mannaka, laughing about something—'Atsu-hatsu-hahaha-atsu-hatsu-hahaha'—with an attitude as if they had taken on the Ōshigoto of repairing Setsutanaho, showing no sign of moving at any Itsu.

Takahime could not help but feel anxious, and she stamped her feet in the Morikage, thinking, 'I wish those two would go somewhere soon. They are such dull-witted fellows. I must get the Tamatake and take it to Mokusuke-sama quickly; at that Anbai, his wounds are so deep that he might not reach Honpuku. But if I am seen by Aitsura, the medicine will not work, and oh, how frustrating this is.'

Haru and Iru, unaware of this, continued to laugh and amuse themselves. Takahime had no choice but to strain her ears and listen to their conversation. Hiding her figure behind a large Ōsugi so as not to be seen, she listened as Iru spoke.

Iru: 'Is it not all very suspicious? Even Mokusuke-sama has ears that twitch, and Hatsuwakahime-sama, who came this time, does not seem to be an ordinary person. I had thought Kahedehime-sama was a stunning beauty, but is this not a Nice that is many times more so?'

Haru: 'Yes, that is true. Since we were born as men, we would like to be with such a Nice, even if only for one night. However, since both Hatsuwakahime-sama and Kahedehime-sama must eventually take a husband, there is no reason why our goal should be missed. When it comes to such people, they actually do not prefer mere good looks. They prefer a man who is manly, with a firm mouth and a swarthy complexion. Since you and I have received military training, we have a lingering military spirit that makes us look dignified, and even in the way we move—the about-face, the one-two-three style—we are truly the kind of men women seem to like.'

Iru: 'Yes, that is right. You are not exactly a man of discarded looks, and I am not a man thrown away on a rubbish heap either; we are most suitable as candidates for a husband. Furthermore, if one marries someone of a different religion, the faith differs, and for that reason, it is said that domestic harmony is inevitably lost; therefore, believers of San-Go-Kyo are to marry believers of San-Go-Kyo. If one wishes to go to the same heaven in the same group after death, one must hold the same thoughts and faith, or it will not work. People like them are well aware of such things.'

Haru: 'Etsu-hehehehe, the future is quite promising. This is why they say San-Go-Kyo is excellent. No matter what, Lord Susanoo of the Saion-no-Yakata is the god of Idumu, the god of matchmaking. And there is a very flexible and stylish god called Kan-Katsu-Yo-no-Kami, who says, "If it is a bond you wish to join, I shall join it; if it is a bond you wish to sever, I shall sever it for you." He is a god with whom one can truly talk, so he is perfect for us.'

Iru: 'That aside, is there any chance that Iku, Saru, Teru, and the others might stand in the competitive arena and chase the deer of the Central Plains?'

Haru: 'That is safe. How could those dull-witted fools with such shifty faces ever be favored by the two Nices? That is absolutely impossible. You may rest assured.'

Iru: 'I cannot be as optimistic and conceited as you are.'

Haru: 'What are you saying? Such worry is unnecessary. Our destiny is already decided. When Hatsuwakahime-sama passed by with that dignified dog, she glanced at my face and smiled at me. At that moment, she was overflowing with deep affection. And the beauty of the electric waves sent from those cool eyes—it was just like an angel. Judging from that look, it is an undeniable fact that she surely has feelings for me. Ah, but it is still a worrying matter.'

§ 4

Yotahiko: 'Hey, Kisama, do you think such a sweet thing could happen? I will have no wife other than Hatsuwakahime-sama. Kahedehime-sama is the one who suits you and is a match for you. Kahedehime-sama has always said of you, "What a manly person he is." You should make up your mind on that.'

Yajihiko: 'Do not talk nonsense. The right of priority belongs to me. Do not say such self-serving things.'

Yotahiko: 'Do not make a fool of me. I will surely make Hatsuwakahime-sama yield to me and show you.'

Yajihiko: 'What, I will make her yield and show you, not the other way around.'

They continued to indulge in such fantasies endlessly. Takahime was so irritated that she was nearly driven to kiyōran, yet no matter how long she waited, there was no sign of them moving; the conversation between the two men entered its kakiyō, and even as the sun set and the night wore on, they showed no sign of moving easily. Takahime had no choice but to go around to the back entrance, pull out a ladder used for fushin, lean it against a side invisible from the uketsuke of the Ōsugi, and while bending her thick body like a bow as the ladder creaked, she finally managed to reach the first branch; while getting caught in kumo webs, she searched around for the tamatake that Mokusuke had spoken of. However, nothing resembling a mushroom could be found at all. Since Mokusuke-sama had said for certain that it was this cedar, she thought it must be somewhere, and as she climbed from branch to branch as quietly as possible, an owl, which could not see well in the daylight, was perched there with its round eyes wide open. Takahime was so flustered that it seemed her vision had become distorted, and seeing the owl's two eyes, she whispered in a small voice, "Truly, it is a mushroom as round as a jewel. Indeed, it is well called a tamatake," and she stroked the owl's eyes for a moment. The owl was startled and, regarding Takahime's shining eyes as an enemy, pecked at them with its sharp beak, which was unbearable; Takahime went sliding down and fell with a thud, crying out in a small voice, "Kyaa, ouch, ouch!" However, the two, Iru and Haru, were so absorbed in their own selfish talk, sparks flying over their discussion of choosing a wife, that they did not notice the body of Takahime, who was suffering after falling to the base of the great cedar tree. The flock of crows in the forest of the shrine was suddenly startled by something, and they were soaring through the air, cawing with voices that sounded ominous.

原文（近代文語）

『こりや、貴様、そんな甘い事があるかい。俺は初稚姫さまぢやなけりや、どんな女房だつて持たないのだ。貴様は楓姫さまが合うたり叶うたりだ。楓姫さまは何時もお前の事を「何と男らしい方だね」と云つて厶つたぞ。それに決めておけ』

『馬鹿を云ふない。先取権は俺にあるのだ。そんな虫のいい事は云つてくれなよ』

『馬鹿にするない。屹度俺が初稚姫さまを此方へ靡かして見せようぞ』

『何、俺が見ん事、靡かしてお目にかけよう』

などと何時迄も限りなしに、こんな空想談に耽つてゐる。高姫は気も狂乱せむばかり苛だてども、何時迄待つても動く気遣ひはなし、二人の話は益々佳境に入り、日が暮れても夜通ししても容易に動かぬ様子である。高姫は是非なく裏口にまはり、普請に使つた梯子を引張り出し来り、大杉の受付から見えない方面に立てかけ、太い体をシワシワと梯子を弓の如くしわませながら漸く一の枝へとりつき、蜘蛛の巣にひつかかりながら、杢助の云つた玉茸は何処にあるかと探しまはつた。されど茸らしいものは少しも見当らない。杢助さまが確に此杉だと云つたのだから、何処かにあるだらうと可成く音のせぬやう、枝から枝へ伝ひ上つて行くと、昼目の見えない梟鳥が丸い目を剥いてとまつてゐた。高姫は余り慌ててゐるので、視覚に変調を来して居たと見え、梟の両眼を見て、

『ほんに玉の様に丸い茸だ。成程玉茸とはよく云つたものだ』

と小さい声で囁き乍ら梟の目を一寸撫でた。梟は驚いて無性矢鱈に高姫の光つた目を敵と見做し、尖つた嘴でこついたから堪らない、高姫はズズズズドスン、「キヤツ、イイイイ痛い」と小声に叫んだ。されどイル、ハルの両人は勝手な話に現を抜かし、女房の選択談に火花を散らしてゐたから、杉の大木の根元に落ちて苦しんでゐる高姫の体が目につかなかつたのである。祠の森の群烏は俄に何物に驚いたか、ガアガアと縁起の悪さうな声をして中空を鳴きながら翺翔してゐる。
