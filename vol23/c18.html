Chapter 18: Waves 濤万里 [730]

波濤万里〔730〕

§ 1

Famed as the Rakudo of Sekai, within the Mizuhokuni of Mesopotamia, manifested in Kenonkiyō, the supreme Tōriyōshin of Brahmanism, Onikumohiko, relying on his futokoro blade as his Fukutōriyō, Onikumawake and Mukadehime. Born in their midst, a single bud-like flower, Koitohime, aged around sixteen or seventeen, yearning for her bewitching form, Tomohiko of the path of Oshihe, clinging with all his might, exhausting his words to persuade her, crossed the barriers of their two parents and human eyes, traversed the wild plains of Fusa, climbed over countless Kauzan, hiding his body in Seirontau, the Tsukinokuni where Shiyaka was born,

Living together in marital harmony, stirred by the demonic wind of love, Koitohime, relying solely on her strength, left behind a single letter of farewell and crossed the ocean waves. To what remote end of white-capped waves her form vanished on a ship, leaving behind Tomohiko, like a nocturnal owl out of season, exposing a wretched face, deeply obsessed as if beguiled by a serpent, his mind deranged, burning his breast, he advanced toward the self-overturning Kamishima. With no means to inquire, stung on the shins by the insects of a summer night, crossing mountain streams, lightly equipped with straw raincoat and hat, here and there wandering about, planting his palanquin in the village of Utsuyama,

Just as he was preaching and spreading a disjointed sermon, the missionary of Sansokyō, the divine being of Ama-no-Mina, terrified by his divine power, once more bolted from this place, flowing endlessly to Awaji Island, wandering through every nook and cranny to the northern extremity. The chieftain of the village of Sumoto, amid a blunder within his mansion, escaping a cage through the privy hole, dyeing his garments yellow, drifting upon the waves of the strait of this world, washed ashore on Awazujima, which floats unchanging and steadfast, with a bewildered expression as if bewitched by foxes and badgers, arriving purposelessly at the rock cave of Kokujizan, and looking within, the palpitation of his breast was Takahime,

Lowering her gaze and speaking to herself, when she asked who it was upon hearing, Takahime of the eloquence of Furuna, born tongue-first, began to chatter to her utmost. With insufferable sophistry, she instantly assumed a bold and brazen front. Knowing full well she was a stinking old hag, yet pinching her nose and coming along into the rock cave to look, lo and behold, an unexpected Mukadehime, with a face like a hannya, a protruding-toothed man with a red nose, seeing the Debagame-style Tomohiko sooner than expected, with a sour face, blowing bubbles like a crab, repeating a myriad of grievances. Such was the terror of that dread aspect that even Tomohiko lost his nerve,

Drawing out with trembling hands the crumpled farewell letter left behind by Koitohime, he handed it to the hag's hand. The hag, smoothing it out simultaneously with the wrinkles of her own face, making her moist eyes shine, peered at it to see what this could be—written in Spartan script, the handwriting of her beloved daughter, listing the various cold rebuffs. In her amusement at seeing them marshaled, even the old hag burst out laughing. Famed in story throughout Oceania, renowned in Ryūgūjima, Huanglonghime is none other than my child, Mukadehime realized for the first time. Amidst a mixture of tears and laughter, of joy and sorrow, just as the gathering fell flat, a man pushed open the mouth of the rock cave and came barging out,

Ah, truly the gods! The foolish Tomohiko, whose spirit and loins were utterly drained, his personal plight indeed pitiful.

The ritual in the inner chamber seemed to have concluded, as numerous men and women, young and old, appeared barging into this place, reporting to Mukadehime that the ritual had safely ended. A man whose crown was bald like a manju, upon seeing Tomohiko's face,

Man: 'Hey, you must be that fellow named Tomohiko who sneaked into my house six months ago, took advantage of my absence to violate my wife, inflicted a wound on her forehead, and fled. Your face is the very spitting image of the fellow who came flying out of my house brandishing a bloody sword at the very moment I returned to my doorway. You are the scoundrel who used honeyed words to slip into an empty house, claimed to be a missionary of Tomohiko, seized the chance of my wife's inattention to commit an outrageous deed, and made off. Now your heavenly mandate is spent; prepare yourself.'

At this man's words, Mukadehime and all the rest were struck dumb, staring at the faces of the two as if to bore holes through them.

Tomohiko: 'You would cause me distress by speaking of such preposterous things. It is said that misfortunes of mistaken identity happen; in Sekai, there are surely persons of the same style, even two or three. This must be your mistake. Please examine the matter thoroughly.'

Man: 'I am a person called Kiusuke of the village of Akashi, and though your face is not well recognized in the gathering dusk, the red feature on the tip of my nose is the greatest proof. To confirm it further, my wife is secluded in the next room, so I will call her out and have her examine you. ...Hey there, Ominami, come here for a moment.'

Tomohiko: 'No, no, that is absurd. I am sorry for causing both husband and wife such trouble. To let bygones be bygones would rather suit your convenience. Past troubles are strictly forbidden on the path of the gods.'

Kiusuke: 'Your convenience may be fine, but as for Kiusuke, it is truly inconvenient to be thought by all the people as having made unreasonable demands.'

As he was speaking, his wife Ominami came out softly, and the moment she saw Tomohiko's face,

Ominami: 'Ah, a thief!'

She simply uttered those words, lost her footing with a start, and collapsed.

Kiusuke, startled, said,

Kiusuke: 'Now, now, wife, since there are so many people here as this, a single thief is nothing to fear. Well, pull yourself together.'

He roughly patted her on the back. Kuwanshiu crossed both hands and sent spiritual energy, saying 'Un.' Ominami gradually regained her composure,

Ominami: 'You are the scoundrel who came into my home this spring, subjected me to insolence, and fled after scooping up our money; the scar on my forehead is the trace of the sin you left behind. Do you still have anything to say for yourself?'

She clenched her teeth, opened her eyes wide, and glared at him.

Tomohiko:

Tomohiko: 'Yes, indeed...'

Having said barely that much, he pressed his head to the floor and was performing the divine possession of the god of earthquakes.

Three men opened the ihato and entered,

Man: 'Hey, pardon us, we are the chieftains of Sumoto in Awaji Island, now Kiyoshi, Takeo, and Tsuruo, three men who have become retainers of Lord Tōsuke. There are those who complain that a great thief, calling himself a missionary of the lawless Brahmanism, slipped out of the crossing hole of Reiin, stole the chieftain's private boat moored at the beach, manipulated the oars, and fled out to the open sea. Ordering us directly as chieftains, we prepared a boat and came in pursuit of Tomohiko, when we encountered a violent storm, our oars broke, we lost our freedom of movement, and at last drifted to this island. Looking, it is a fishing boat with the brand of Tōsuke in which the thief came riding; thinking surely he must be hiding in this island, we three came chasing after him, and by the power of this dog with its sharp sense of smell, we have searched and arrived all the way to this place as pursuing officials. Now that it has come to this, it is no use even if you hide. As long as the stench of dung continues all the way into this cavern, the thief must surely be inside this cavern; come, hand him over swiftly.'

He stood with his shoulders and arms puffed up, acting like a Niō statue, and strained himself with intense force.

Mukadehime: 'Everyone, thank you for your trouble. There is a dog here. Please hurry and tie him up before you return. He is a stray dog called Tomohiko with whom nothing can be done. Ohohoho.'

She smiled as if it spilled from her toothless mouth. Takahime looked sharply at the faces of the three,

Takahime: 'If I wonder who it might be, it is none other than the three of Kiyoshi, Takeo, and Tsuruo. In conspiracy with the boatman Tōsuke, you have made a fool of Takahime here. Ah, you have vanished into a good place. I will not overlook this anymore. ...Now, Mukadehime-sama, please lend me about ten of your subordinates. Such an incorrigible rebel must be tied up for punishment, and we must extract some grease from him just a bit.'

The three: 'Ahahaha, hey Taka... old hag, what are you vomiting out? We are the retainers of Lord Tōsuke, the director of human children whose thunderous name is hidden nowhere in Sumoto of Awaji Island, however unthinkable it may be. Until now, we have been ordered around at the tip of your chin by you as Kiyoshi, Takeo, and Tsuruo, but today we are of a slightly different market price, so keep that resolve in mind and make a careful reply. If you do something rude, shall we capture you and take you back together?'

Takahime remained calmly and composedly unmoved,

Takahime: 'Oh-ho-ho-ho, what is that leaden Enma-like attitude of yours? How disgraceful. Even if you try to frighten me with a toy nine-sun-gobu dagger, this Takahime is never, ever such a weak woman who would flinch, you see. Even this woman is a dog that has eaten wild boar. If you can brilliantly capture me, then try and capture me.'

Pondering the gap amid that struggling, Tomohiko went down on all fours, and creeping heavily with a black loincloth hanging down like a tail, he attempted to escape toward the front entrance.

The foreign dog brought by the three barked threateningly and bit into Tomohiko's bottom.

Mukadehime, bewildered by this struggle in which two or three groups were all mixed up, lost her nerve and with all her might,

Mukadehime: 'Namu Brahman Kyōshiyu Ōkuniwakenomikoto, protect us and bless us.'

She prayed wholeheartedly while shedding sweat.

Kiyoshi, Takeshi, and Tsuru bound Tomohiko tightly by his hands and arms, dragged him out of the cave, took up thorn moss,

and dragging him along said,

'Now, thief, walk smartly!'

From this time, Mukadehime, accompanied by Takahime, Kanshū, and Kyūsuke, concurrently searching for the whereabouts of the treasure, and intending to seek an audience with Kōryūhime, the Queen of Australia, entrusted her body to a single boat loaded with numerous fruits, left Shōdoshima behind, rode across the Seto Inland Sea, crossed the Bakan Strait, and proceeded west and ever west.
