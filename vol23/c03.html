Chapter 3: Pine 上の苦悶 [715]

松上の苦悶〔715〕

§ 1

The three men invited into the inner chamber of Wakahikoyakata, which commanded a distant view of the vast plain, were Mokusuke, Tamaharuwake, and Kuniyoriwake.

Wakahikoyakata: 'Well, you three, it is a great pleasure that you have all come together to visit me. Even now, we were just talking about you. They say that speaking of someone is like calling them, and true enough it is.'

Mokusuke: 'By the command of the Kyōshiyu of Kotoyoriwake, we have made a hurried business trip to the province of Kii.'

Mokusuke: 'Is the Kyōshiyu of Kotoyoriwake still continuing to work diligently as before?'

Mokusuke: 'What a strange question this is; how could the Kyōshiyu ever change?'

Wakahikoyakata: 'What has become of Takahime?'

Mokusuke: 'Takahime is working at the seichi just as before.'

Wakahikoyakata is lost in thought, muttering, 'Indeed?'

Wakahikoyakata: 'How has Tamanohime been?'

Mokusuke: 'Tamanohime and Hatsuwakahime, the two of them, are serving in the betsuden of the Palace of Brocade. But you are asking strange things. Has someone come to our tauyakata?'

Wakahikoyakata: 'Yes, a short while ago both Magahiko and Takehiko arrived.'

Upon hearing this, Kuniyoriwake suddenly raised his eyebrows and allowed an ominous look to float across his face.

Kuniyoriwake: 'Where has that Magahiko gone?'

Wakahikoyakata: 'They are resting in the detached guest room.'

Mokusuke: 'Ahahaha, this is strange. Evil deeds cannot be hidden, it seems.'

Wakahikoyakata: 'What has Magahiko done?'

Mokusuke: 'No, there is nothing so frightening as the human heart.'

Wakahikoyakata: 'For some reason, both of them have been acting very restless, so thinking there must be something deep behind this, I had five burly men keep watch over them so they could not escape anywhere. Exactly what kind of thing have they done?'

Mokusuke whispered in a low voice that Magahiko and Takehiko had pushed Tamaharuwake and Kuniyoriwake down into the valley from the summit of Aoyama Pass, plotting their murder. Wakahikoyakata was shocked to the verge of collapsing,

Wakahikoyakata: 'The crafty schemes of Takahime's faction are tenacious to the end. It seems that the guardian deities of the golden fox, giant snake, and evil demons will simply not withdraw. What shall we do? We must adopt one of two courses: either chase them away as they are, or make them submit.'

Mokusuke: 'Well, leave it to me.'

Crossing his arms, he sinks into a slight reverie. After a while, Mokusuke draws close to Wakahikoyakata's ear. Wakahikoyakata nods, leaves this place, proceeds into the detached guest room, and turns to the five men,

Wakahikoyakata: 'Ah, good work, everyone. Return to your respective rooms and rest. ... Magahiko-sama, Takehiko-sama, thank you for waiting so long. You must be quite bored.'

Magahiko: 'Please do not worry about us. How are your guests?'

Wakahikoyakata: 'Yes, merely some farmers from close by came by. They all finished their business and left. Please make yourselves completely at home. However, there is one favor I would like to ask of you.'

Magahiko: 'What is this favor you speak of?'

Wakahikoyakata: 'Actually, a zealous believer has fallen ill and is confined in this yakata, but it is a most suspicious illness, so I would like to ask you to perform your chinokon for them just once.'

Magahiko: 'With someone as filled with divine virtue as you are present, how could someone like me be of any use?'

Wakahikoyakata: 'No matter what, that sick person cannot be cured without receiving your chinokon. They say that all things are bound by karmic affinity; no matter how great one says a deity's divine virtue is, if spirits do not match, it will ultimately have no effect. Since you are in no hurry, please perform the chinokon after you have rested.'

Magahiko: 'Understood. I shall ask the kami and see.'

Wakahikoyakata: 'I appreciate your prompt consent, and the person himself will surely be pleased. However, it appears that some mononoke is possessing him, though it is calm during the day. I must ask you to do it after nightfall.'

§ 2

Magahiko, with an arrogant air,

Magahiko: 'Very well.'

shaking his head openly. Outside, Mokusuke, Tamaharuwake, and Kuniyoriwake, the three of them lowering their voices, are absorbed in talking about something. Wakahiko turns toward the two,

Wakahiko: 'I have a little business outside, so please excuse me. Do please rest at your ease today. I shall not fail to be indebted to you this evening.'

and leaving these words behind, he walks away. Afterwards, the two lower their voices,

Magahiko: 'Isn't this somehow suspicious? It feels for all the world as though Mokusuke has come. If we make the slightest mistake, might not the conspiracy of Aoyamatauge have been exposed?'

Takehiko: 'I too somehow feel unwell. Is there no device by which we might escape from this place?'

Magahiko: 'By some chance, those two fellows might have been resuscitated at the bottom of the ravine. If so, it is a serious matter. Come, do you try performing a divine possession for me.'

Takehiko folds his hands instantly and closes his eyes. Suddenly his body trembles,

Takehiko: 'Ugh, ugh! We are the familiars of the eight-forked serpent of Yamata. Now Mokusuke, Tamaharuwake, and Kuniyoriwake have appeared outside, and are carrying out a scheme to wait for tonight and exact their revenge!'

Magahiko: 'That is terrible! Is there not some device by which we may be saved?'

Takehiko: 'Ugh, ugh! Once it has come to this, the mansion is surrounded by fierce men who are keeping watch. Mokusuke of great strength is hiding outside. Already like rats in a bag, there seems to be no prospect of the two persons escaping.'

Magahiko: 'Oh, this is a troublesome matter. What had we better do?'

changing his countenance and becoming flustered.

Takehiko: 'Ugh, ugh! There is no need to be flustered. First, calm your spirit. It is precisely at such a time that the single-moment mind is necessary. In any case, he who curses another digs two graves; it is like spitting toward heaven. It is retribution for one's own acts; you had better resign yourselves and surrender your lives to the three men.'

Magahiko, becoming still more flustered,

Magahiko: 'Out of attachment to our lives we practice faith and also serve as propagation emissaries. How could such a thing be endured? It is the gods who save us in such places. I beg you for some good instructions.'

Takehiko: 'Mm, mm. It is retribution for one's own acts. There is no help for it; right now outside, the three men are sharpening the swords of shakubuku to the utmost of their strength. With those fine blades, if you are cleanly dispatched, the bodies of the two of you will splendidly be split like pears! Heh, heh, heh!'

Magahiko: 'I beg of you, save the two of us from this place. We shall reform from this very moment on...'

Takehiko: 'Mm, mm, mm. First, without flustering, you had no sooner better wait until the sun sets. No matter how much you may apologize, because so many strong fellows are surrounding the place, nothing can be done. Rather than foolishly hiding in flight, having your life taken by a nameless fellow, and exposing your shame, you had better pierce each other and die with the short swords you possess. That is the best method.'

Magahiko: 'How can this state of anxiety wait until tonight? Moreover, one precious life cannot be so lightly thrown away.'

Takehiko: 'Ugh, ugh! This flesh too is a pitiable thing, and you also are pitiable. However, because it was Magahiko who plotted to lightly take the lives of Tamaharuwake and Kuniyoriwake, there is no help for it; make up your mind.'

Magahiko: 'How can I make up my mind to this?'

Takehiko: 'Ugh, ugh! Is your life precious? Pinch your own body and know the pain of others. That your life is precious and that the lives of both Tam and Kuni are precious are one and the same thing. However, both Tam and Kuni are wont to say at all times that life is precious, so they must surely hate to die. In contrast to that, you, together with Takahime, say day in and day out like crying crows that you have no need for life, and that if it is for the sake of the Way, no matter what happens, you do not regret it. The disappearance of your life is the realization of your daily desire; there could scarcely be another auspicious event like this. Ha, ha, ha!'

Magahiko: 'I know not what god you may be, but you utter things somewhat displeasing to my taste. I must ask you to take your leave.'

Takehiko: 'Ugh, indeed, it is displeasing, no doubt. Most naturally so; though with your lips you profess to have no need for your life, when it comes to the critical and vital moment, for a human being to have remnants of lingering attachment to this transient world is the ordinary and natural grasping of a mortal. Unless you strip away that attachment, the true divine work cannot be accomplished.'

Magahiko: 'If it comes to the same thing, I would fain perform our divine service while still retaining my physical body. Ah, what a blunder I have made. What on earth had I better do? The day is drawing to a close step by step. If I dally and hesitate, I know not what sort of dreadful treatment I shall be made to suffer; if I only had wings, I would fly straight back home.'

Takehiko: 'Ugh-ugh-ugh, ah-ha-ha-ha, if you prize your life to that degree, will you do as this person tells you?'

Magahiko: 'If it means saving my life, I will do anything whatsoever. I beg of you, speak quickly.'

Takehiko: 'Un-un-un, you two must climb to the very summit of this pine tree in the courtyard, and earnestly chant the Amatsunorito. If you do so, from the heavens a purple cloud shall come to take up your bodies, and I shall send you away to a zone of safety. How now, are you rejoiced?'

Magahiko: 'Yes, if it saves me, that is splendid. In that case, from what hour shall we climb?'

Takehiko: 'If time is delayed it is a matter of supreme gravity; not even a half-hour's grace is permitted. Set your sights upon the pine tree and climb upward. Takehiko's physical body shall climb along with you, too. Un-un-un.'

So speaking, the spirit returned to its original state. Magahiko looked anxiously about in all directions, and taking advantage of the absence of others, he scrambled up the great tree in the courtyard like a monkey, staking his life upon it. Takehiko likewise followed and reached the summit. The two of them earnestly chanted the Amatsunorito at the very top of their voices. Startled by these voices, Wakahiko to begin with, along with Mokusuke, Tama, Kuni, and all the rest, beheld the two figures atop the pine and burst into boisterous laughter, murmuring amidst the din, 'Ah-ha-ha-ha.' The two of them, soaked in sweat and straining every nerve, were chanting Kannagara tamachihau masaye. Mokusuke, intentionally raising a mighty voice, cried out,

Mokusuke: 'Come now, from here on we shall deal with both fellows, Magahiko and Taketoriwake, and have a cup of wine with them as a relish!'

He roared this forth like thunder. Hearing this, Magahiko shuddered with terror, his voice trembling more and more until finally no breath would come forth at all. Takehiko, uttering an 'Ugh,' once more began divine possession upon the pine top.

Magahiko: 'Just as you commanded, I have fled up to this place, but as you see, Mokusuke and his crew are surrounding the base of the tree. I beg you, come quickly with a cloud to welcome us.'

Takehiko: 'Un-un-un, do your eyes not perceive how a thick purple cloud like this is enveloping your body? Open your living eyes and gaze intently all around.'

Magahiko: 'No matter how we look, we cannot see it with our eyes.'

Takehiko: 'Un-un-un, even if you cannot see it, a cloud is still a cloud. Clasp hands with Takehiko's physical body and leap forth toward the heavens. If you do so, I shall pluck you up, cause you to escape from this mansion, and save you in a zone of safety. For a man, the power of decision is paramount. Come now, quickly, quickly!'

Urged on thus, Magahiko, beside himself in a trance, took Takehiko's hand, and the very moment they chanted together in unison 'One, two, three!' and leaped a mere foot or two into the air, from the top of the pine they plummeted headlong straight down onto the courtyard paved with rough gravel, shivering and quivering in their hands and feet like a crushed frog, and fell completely unconscious. Wakahiko, Mokusuke, Tama, Kuni, and all the rest were astonished at this spectacle, instantly forming a mountain of people beneath the tree, hurrying frantically hither and thither crying, 'Water, water!' Omitsu came running hastily, carrying a bucket. Mokusuke immediately took water into his mouth and blew the mist of his breath upon the faces of the two men; at long last, groaning, the two recovered their vitality, looked anxiously about in all directions, beheld the figures of Tamajiwake and Kuniyoriwake, shrieked 'Eep!', and once more plunged into a state of unconsciousness. Tamajiwake hoisted up Magahiko, while Kuniyoriwake hoisted up Takehiko, carried them deep into the inner chamber, spread bedding for them, laid them down courteously, chanted the Amatsunorito facing the altar, and administered pacification of the spirit anew. At last, the two breathed life back into their bodies.

と促され、魔我彦は無我夢中になつて竹彦の手をとり、一イ二ウ三ツと声を揃へて一二尺飛び上つた途端に、松上より眼下の荒砂を敷きつめた庭に真逆様に墜落し、蛙をぶつつけたやうにビリビリと手足を慄はせ、人事不省に陥つた。若彦、杢助、玉、国其他の者は此光景に驚き、忽ち樹下に人山を築き、水よ水よと右往左往に慌て廻る。お光は手桶を提げ慌しく走り来る。杢助は直ちに水を含み、両人の面部に息吹の狭霧を吹きかけ、漸くにして二人は唸りながら生気に復し、四辺をキヨロキヨロ見廻し、玉治別、国依別の姿を見て『キヤツ』と叫び、又もや人事不省に陥つて仕舞つた。玉治別は魔我彦を、国依別は竹彦をひつ抱へ、奥の間深く運び入れ、夜具を敷いて鄭重に寝させ、神前に向つて天津祝詞を奏上し、更めて鎮魂を施した。漸くにして二人は息を吹きかへす。

Tamaharuwake: 'Magahiko-sama, how was it? You must have worried greatly.'

Magahiko: 'Yes, I have truly done an inexcusable thing. I humbly beg you to spare only my life.'

Tamaharuwake: 'Why would a Sendenshi who saves people want your life? Thanks to you, I was given a severe spiritual training. However, I would like you to stop such dangerous things after this. Magahiko turned red in the face and cast his eyes down.

Takehiko: 'Yes, I have come to my senses. Evil deeds cannot succeed, alas. Mokusuke: 'You are a fellow who talks back quite impudently. But by now you must understand that you cannot do evil either.'

Magahiko: 'It was not my physical body that did it either. Since the kindred of the Yamata no Orochi possessed me, I beg you to look upon me with Kaminaohi and Ōnaohi and reconsider and rehear my case.'

Mokusuke: 'Generally speaking, since you fellows are infatuated with Takahime's derailed enthusiasm, you calmly do such unrighteous things and think you can perform splendid divine work.'

Magahiko: 'Thinking that everything was as Hinode Kami-sama commanded, just as I was slightly trying to fathom Takahime-sama's will, my guardian deity came and threw both of us down to the bottom of the valley, trying to kill us. However, Magahiko's physical body knows nothing of it.'

Mokusuke: 'Tamaharuwake, the Sendenshi Kuniyoriwake were thrown down from the summit of Aoyama Pass into that deep ravine, and even if they were subjected to an ordeal that nearly crushed their five bodies to pieces, the fact that they do not harbor even a dewdrop of cormorant feather's worth of grudge against both of you is truly the height of admiration. Why don't you too take to heart the feelings of these two Sendenshi and reform yourselves a bit? And to prove your reformation, you had better confess here and now all the schemes of Takahime's faction up to this point.'

Magahiko: 'That alone I cannot confess; Takahime-sama strictly ordered me not to speak of it even if I should die, and I firmly vowed that even through my eternal existences, should my tongue be pulled out, I would not tell.'

Mokusuke: 'Even if it be for good or for evil, it seems there is still a gleam in your conscience, and your resolve to keep a promise is admirable. We will pursue this no further than this. Tamaharuwake-sama, Kuniyoriwake-sama, you will forgive these two, won't you?'

Tamaharuwake: 'There is no question of forgiving or not forgiving. Since everything is the divine providence, and they gave us the practical education that carelessness is a great enemy, toward both of you who were used for that duty, far from feeling even the slightest deficiency or grudge, we feel nothing but gratitude and call your work hard labor.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'I feel the same as Tamaharuwake. Magahiko-sama, Takehiko-sama, please be at ease. There is a saying to hit and be smashed. Shall we henceforth become all the more intimate and serve the divine affairs of the divine realm?'

Mokusuke stood up and sang a poem, seeking to restore the awkward atmosphere of this place.

Treading across Yamato and Kawachi, Gradually here in Kuni of Ki, In the ravine of Aoyama Pass, With the divine words of Kototoriwake, If I come forward with spirited courage, Famed by renown, the rushing pools and splashing waters Of the valley of Totsukawa, Taking off our clothes stark naked, Plunging right in with a splash, Just as we were performing the misogi purification, The green leaves of the trees one after the other Growing dark, and the light of the heavenly sun Gradually hiding itself, Just as it was dyeing the darkness, Falling and grazing our heads, Two shadows instantly Plummeted toward the blue abyss, Raising up the two of them near the waterfall Where they became unconscious, Look closely, what is this?
