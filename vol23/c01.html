Chapter 1: Jewel の露 [713]

玉の露〔713〕

§ 1

Weaving the loom of horizontal and vertical threads, the divine administration of the Brocade Palace, Tamateruhiko and Tamateruhime, proclaiming the divine decrees of Kami's will as sendenshi conveying them to the four quarters, Kuniyoriwake and Tamaharuwake, the divine commands and divine shintoku continuing along the peaks of Odaigahara, flowing down from Hinodewake, along the banks of Fukatanigaha, amidst the foliage where green leaves drip with dew, gazing at the breathtaking view of the valley of a thousand fathoms where the spray flies, just as they are resting their feet, a sendenka gradually draws near.

Looking back and gazing behind them, in the attire of waraji and kiyahan, with kongauzue and manjiugasa, two shadows sway and advance toward this place.

Kuniyoriwake: 'Tamaharuwake-sama, you too have been performing the silent ascetic practice for quite a long time, haven't you? The sendenshi Wakahiko was performing rigorous austerities at the waterfall of Kumano, but how is it, I wonder, is he still continuing them?'

Tamaharuwake: 'I actually wish to meet Wakahiko-sama as well, and while on my way, I unexpectedly happened to meet you, and was just thinking to inquire about the situation.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Since Tamanohime-sama performed that level of divine work, Wakahiko's sendenshi will surely be overjoyed upon hearing of it. Regarding that, since we cannot have him stay in the province of Kii indefinitely, I have actually come to welcome him, obeying the inner command of Kotoyoriwake no Mikoto-sama.'

Tamaharuwake: 'I too must maintain strict secrecy and cannot speak outside about Tamanohime's divine work, but since it is just between the two of us, there should be no harm in telling you. However, as evil spirits are lurking about our persons, we must not speak carelessly... By the way, that sendenka of theirs looks rather like Izurunoha. Shall we wait here, gazing at this magnificent scenery, until whoever it is draws near?'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Ah, they have already drawn near enough for their faces to be clearly distinguished. At any rate, let us decide to wait.'

Just as this was said, the sendenka suddenly ceased, and two straw hats gradually divided the foliage of the trees and drew near. When they looked, they saw both Magahiko and Takehiko, and with voices as if surprised at the two sitting properly in solemn posture,

Magahiko: 'Ah, were you gentlemen Tamaharuwake and Kuniyoriwake, the sendenshi? With what intention have you come here for propagation?'

Kuniyoriwake: 'If I wonder who it might be, it is Magahiko-sama and Takehiko-sama. Where on earth are you going, and for what business do you come? Were you ordered by the kyōshu of Kotoyoriwake? This region of Kii Province is fixed as the propagation area of Wakahiko. For you to come there does not make much sense.'

Being thus asked, Magahiko hesitates for a moment, making a fidgety expression,

Magahiko: 'Well... I have not come for propagation. I have come to the waterfall of Kumano on rigorous austerities to wash away sins and defilements.'

Tamaharuwake: 'Even without coming all the way to such a place for austere practices, are there not the magnificent falls of Nachi falling right there in the sacred ground?'

Magahiko, while fidgeting restlessly,

Magahiko: 'My word, it is a magnificent view of the world! One might say the treetops dripping with green and the sound of the water in this valley river are truly valiant, are they not?'

He strives to turn the conversation elsewhere as much as possible. Sensing this intent, the two, Tama and Kuni, act purposely as if they have forgotten,

Tamaharuwake: 'As might be expected of one whose source is Odaigahara, it is a remarkably magnificent stream. To speak of the sight where the water bites into the boulders and strange rocks of that mountain stream, raising water smoke and sending forth white-silver jewels, it is truly the supreme scenic wonder of the world. If one were to play in such a place for at least three days, I feel as though one's life would be prolonged.'

Magahiko looks down into the bottom of the valley with a terrified expression, and as if startled,

Magahiko: 'Ah, how terrible, how terrible!'

and scrambles with his feet. At that start, the two, Tama and Kuni, thinking some momentous crisis must have broken out, hastily peer down into the bottom of the valley. Exchanging a glance with Takehiko, Magahiko gathered all the strength of his body and gave a sudden, heavy shove from behind the two men. How could it be endured? The two plunged headlong, cutting through the wind into the thousand-fathom valley.

The green leaves of the trees gradually darken, the sun hides its figure behind the summit of the high mountains, and the twilight air presses upon all sides.

Craftily getting into the good graces of both the jewel and the country, as well as the master of Kotoyoriwake, making Takahime-sama of the Henjiyōnanshi lineage swallow Agetsubo, and making Wakahiko's wife—formerly Osetsu or Mokusuke's maid Tsuchiyo—serve us, were all the schemes of these fellows. Ah, it cannot be helped even if one regrets it... Such must be your psychological state, is it not? Do not worry. Since I am likewise a co-conspirator, I will rarely say clumsy things. However, when the wandering spirits of Kuniyoriwake and Tamayoriwake possess you or me and speak, this is an exception so it cannot be helped, aha-ha-ha-ha.'

Laughing with careless indifference, he rolled about. Magahiko, folding his arms and turning pale, breathed heavily through his shoulders while sinking into deep reflection. The curtain of night grew denser and denser, until at last even each other's figures were swallowed up by the darkness. The sound of the gale tearing through the tops of the trees was somehow tumultuous, the gloom pressed heavily upon the body, and they felt such a fit of eerie despair and desolation as though wandering alone in the Nether-world at the roots of the earth. The two men put on a show of bravado, somehow suppressing the terror deep within their hearts, and spoke of stout things in an attempt to distract themselves from this loneliness and anxiety. The wind grew ever more violent, and the night gradually wore on. The voices of small monkeys, sounding as though they were tormenting a woman, could be heard echoing here and there like shrill shrieks; and just as one thought of that, the howling of wolves, enough to shake the mountains, grew louder moment by moment. Pale bluish flames appeared with a pop from out of the darkness, burning dully and vanishing, burning and vanishing, surrounding the pair on all sides, and finally raging and roaring above their heads. The two closed their eyes, stopped their ears, clutched their heads, and pressed their faces flat against the ground. Someone, no one knew who, caressed the vicinity of the nape with a hand like ice. From the tips of their heads down to their testicles, they felt a chill like ice. Takehiko uttered a trembling voice,

Takehiko: 'Oh, how lingeringly resentful! How now, Magahiko, a cowardly and mean villain to strike by deception. Now that things have come to this, I shall pluck out your very neck-head and cast you down into the Root-country and the Bottom-country. Prepare yourself!'

And in the gloom, he began to act as if possessed by a spirit. Magahiko said,

Magahiko: 'Hey, Takehiko, you should not do such disgusting things. Why don't you calm down a bit? That is just your nerves, you know. What good is going mad from now on? Why don't you pluck up your courage a little?'

Takehiko: 'No matter what you say, how could I let this resentment go unavenged... The demon in your heart, which plotted to make victims of Tamajitewake and Kuniyoriwake, those undisputed propaganda emissaries, has now appeared here, and borrowing Takehiko's flesh, it will avenge the wrong. After you too became a subordinate of Takahime and tormented the hensei nyoshi, still unsatisfied, to thrust both of us down to the bottom of a ravine and kill us—you are a heinous and lawless idiot. Having just received orders from the Judgment Hall of the Spirit World, I have come to meet and receive you. Come now, there is no longer any escape. Resign yourself honorably. Flowers are the triple Yoshino, and men are samurai. As a parting token, it would be best for you to scatter cleanly.'

With cold hands, he stroked around the neck. The blue flames burned and vanished, burned and vanished, roaring with a buzzing sound as they flew about Magahiko's surroundings. The voices of monkeys and wolves grew fiercer moment by moment. Magahiko, out of sheer terror, lost his soul and fainted right on the spot. Takehiko also collapsed heavily in that same place, and afterward there was only the sound of the wind. Presently, the waning moon revealed a shape like a sharpened grass-clearing sickle, floating up from the Kumano Sea, and peered suspiciously at the figures of the two. The night gradually broke into dawn. A troop of small monkeys flew out from nowhere before the two, scratching the soles of their feet and scratching their faces. Aroused by the pain, the two started up together all at once.

Magahiko: 'Ah, last night I met with a terribly frightening experience. Thanks to it, the new sun-deity has come forth, and I have grown somewhat more stout-hearted. This is entirely due to the assistance of the Hinode-no-Kami. Things like the moon's spirit are things that appear or disappear, grow large or grow small, just like a hensei nyoshi, and are not reliable in the least. The decree stated that a mirror would be brought forth from heaven and shown, but truly it is a god that exhausts one's patience. The only one who always shines brilliantly without changing form is the Hinode-no-Kami. That is why I say that unless it is the living shrine of the Hinode-no-Kami, the night will not break. As for the moon's spirit, aren't things whose mental state is unsettled plain to see just by looking at the sky? In addition to that, there is the Konjin of Kun. That fellow called Mii-no-Saru is no good fellow at all. He is a fellow who gobbles up paper and scratches people about.'

Takehiko: 'Indeed, that's so. The monkey rascal played pranks and scratched all over the place. As soon as I was startled and woke up, it bolted away in a flash, didn't it? This is also proof that the Konjin of the northeast lacks power. Aha-ha-ha-ha!'

Saying this, he goes ahead briskly, putting on his straw raincoat and sedge hat as before, striking his iron-shod staff, and goes descending the rocky path while making a clinking sound.

At the bottom of the ravine, a single man, stark naked, was performing water purification austerities. Into the dim twilight there, two shadows came tumbling right down toward the blue pool. The man, startled, promptly rescued the two, applied artificial respiration in various ways, bent their fingers, and brought them back to life.

Man: 'Moshi-moshi, judging by your attire—though in the dead of night I cannot see clearly—you appear to be propagation messengers, but who on earth might you be?'

Tamajiwake: 'I too am actually a propagation messenger. Even though so many rocks are lined up like this, to have fallen into this blue pool so successfully without a single injury is thanks to the gods, and also thanks to your help. This deep debt of gratitude I will never forget.'

Asked thus, the two replied,

Tama, Kuni: 'Yes, that is so. And which person are you...?'

Before they could even hear the full reply, the man spoke,

Just as I had begun my water purification, you two happened to fall down. If I had arrived even a little later, it would have been a terrible matter. I am Mokusuke, you see.'

Hearing this, the two could not contain their feelings of relief and joy, clung to Mokusuke's body, and wept tears of gladness.

Mokusuke: 'It is a remarkably dark night, but from your two voices there is not the slightest room for doubt. It is no fun staying in a place like this for long. Since my mission this time is likely finished with this, let us go somewhere flat and I will listen to the details in full. No matter what is said, with the sound of the water in this mountain stream, a proper conversation cannot be had.'

So saying, he feels his way down the winding path that shone white in the darkness. When the path becomes hidden by the shade of trees and is no longer visible, the shadow of a white fox walks along leisurely one or two ken ahead. Mokusuke descends about seven or eight cho using that trace as a guide, sits down upon a flat rock, and speaks,

Mokusuke: 'Now, now, you two honorable ones, shall we leisurely await the break of day right here?'

The two say 'Yes' while removing their wet garments, wringing them out with all their might, and spreading them out wide upon the rocks to dry. Judging by how the rocks had been baked by the heat of the day, there is an extraordinary warmth. The garments dried just as before within a short space of time.

Kuniyoriwake: 'They well say that Heaven does not kill a man. Everywhere else has grown chilly with the night dew, but this rock alone is completely like a stove. Even though the sun does not rise, for clothes to dry is a rare thing. This too must be the grace of the gods. Now everyone, let us chant the divine words.'
