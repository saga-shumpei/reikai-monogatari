Chapter 11: 難破 Ship [723]

難破船〔723〕

§ 1

Even the sky of the heart, as Takahime accompanied by four attendants crossed the dark, pitch-black ocean of the boundless sea—never ceasing to search out the whereabouts of the three treasures—advancing toward Ejima as the horse of her heart ran wild, her purpose being to seek the whereabouts of the jewel aboard the Tamanomaru, gliding onward over the crests of the white waves to find the track, the four men dripping sweat like waterfalls, drifting at the mercy of the waves at last losing their course, and just as their hearts reached toward the island shadows of Ahaji, the prow of the boat collided with a submerged reef, plunging instantly into the waves, while witnessing the mournful sight of nine deaths and a single life,

Yet whatever her heart might see, Takahime, deep in obsession, weaves this tale of treasure-hunting; lying stretched out upon the boat of her bedding, she utters this Reikaimonogatari of the auspicious moon as the sun and moon float upon the sea of the heart, pressing forward by parting and pushing aside the violent waves of the bottomless Magatsukami and the divine scheme—ah, Kamunagara, may your august spirits bless and preserve us.

Takahime successfully scattered the three boatmen without a hitch, and the party of five, in high spirits, rowed out into the dark expanse of the ocean. The faint lights on the shore gradually grew distant until they vanished entirely from sight. Wriggling her nose, Takahime spoke,

Takahime: 'Ah, everyone, thank you for your hard work. Now that things have come to this, we are safe; there is hardly any likelihood of anyone coming in pursuit from behind. Even if someone should come, once we have found the whereabouts of the treasures, we can simply load them secretly onto this boat, take a detour, and return safely. Furthermore, if I just swallow that jewel down inside this belly of Takahime's, there is no fear of anyone taking it no matter who comes. Ho ho ho ho, they say if you wait, the winds of the sea route will blow; one must simply await the right time.'

Kanshu: 'Now, now, Takahime-sama, you speak as if the treasure were already in your hands—might this not be counting the tanuki's skin before the animal is caught?'

Takahime: 'Oh, it will be fine! No matter what anyone says, once the living shrine of the Sun God casts its gaze upon it, there can be no mistake. The Prelate of Kototama, Mokusuke, Tamanohimako and her crew will no doubt be utterly dumbfounded. Imagining their faces at that very moment makes me feel a strange, pitiful delight. As proof of the Sun God's august protection, just for tonight not even the moon appears, leaving only a sky full of stars. Perhaps it is because I have always desired that jewel so deeply day and night, but tonight's starlight feels truly exceptional. No matter what anyone says, since those are the supreme treasures of the realm that the Sun God watches over so abundantly, calling them stars, getting our hands on them will be a magnificent feat. No matter what anyone says, once I swallow the jewel I shall return to the sacred ground, organize the Takahime Cabinet, and appoint you all as my staff officers, so pray row the boat with high courage.'

Kanshu: 'You pushed us much too hard, so my steering mechanism has lost its conscious mind and can no longer function at all.'

Takahime: 'What, talking of such faint-hearted things at a time like this! Like rabbit droppings, things that cannot last long are utterly useless in times of emergency. This is a moment of one thousand riders against one, a dividing line between success and failure, so put some twist into your arms and row away!'

Tsurukō: 'Hey Kanshu, are you the same? It seems as though the oil in my engine has run dry. My arms feel as if they are about to be ripped right off.'

Kiyokō: 'I am the same.'

Takahime: 'When this is still a long sea route ahead, it will never do for you to collapse right here before the Akashi Strait! Unless you pull yourselves together with firm resolve and make one more effort, you will never be able to fulfill the vital and essential divine task of the three thousand worlds. If you desire advancement, you must exert yourselves now, or later regrets will be too late. If you wish to manifest fame unparalleled in the three thousand worlds, you must do what people cannot normally do and go where people cannot normally go to receive true divine virtue. Come, everyone, for the joy ahead, let us make one final push!'

Kuwanshiu: 'When my arms grow as limp as if they are falling off, can I care about profit or loss? I cannot trade them for greed or profit, this misery tonight; perhaps this simply does not please Kamisama, I suppose. Somehow, from the bottom of my heart, I grow afraid, and I cannot shake the feeling that I am committing a daizai. If we were to run aground on even a single reef, we might instantly become jiyakumetsuiraku, change places with a dozaemon, and be forced to make the riugūyu. How about it, leaving it to the wind and the waves, if everyone is allowed to rest, perhaps we can regain our genki and work again.'

Takahime: 'Since there is not even a moment of iuyo, resting is zetsutai out of the question.'

Kuwanshiu: 'Ah, no matter how much I think I can work without resting, the kanjin handle will not obey our meirei, so there is nothing to be done. Takahime-sama, why don't you try rowing for once, and then perhaps our painful ordeal can be tasted. If you intend to use men, unless you try being used by men, you cannot understand the kutsū of your buka, you see.'

Takahime: 'What did you come along for? Do you mean to tell the Ikigamisama to row the boat; does not that show a slight difference in your riyōken? If Takahime could use the boat, who would bring the likes of you along for such himitsu goshingefu? Since I brought you along to make you row the boat, do not feel so disheartened and please put your heart and soul into your mitama and brace yourselves. In return, if this matter is successfully accomplished, I shall properly express my thanks to you.'

Tsurukō: 'We need neither thanks nor anything else. We do not possess such small hearts as wanting to make a tegara, wanting to leave a name, or wanting to boast to people. Always, as if it were your habit, you say that if this matter is successfully accomplished you will let us rise in the world, or you will express thanks, or you will leave our names to the end of kalpas, or you will enshrine us as kami; but first of all, those words of yours do not sit well with me. How can one perform the goyo of Kamisama, driven by such honor and desire? It is insulting to the stomach to be thought of as following you because we want to make a tegara. I cannot help feeling as though my own conscience has been insulted. Please do your best not to say such child-deceiving things from now on. While endings like "if" or "however" are attached, it is no good. Such interrogative words from root to leaf will never be approved by the worms in my belly.'

Takahime: 'You say such splendid things with your mouth, but the bottom of your heart is probably not like that. There is no way that a fellow without a sense of honor exists in this wide world. If we bare our chests and tap the bottoms of our hearts, there is no one who does not harbor the desire to make a tegara. Even the Ikimiya of Hinodegami likewise wants honor and wants to make a tegara; this is the unvarnished confession of net price. All of you, there can be no mistake about that, ohohoho.'

The group: 'Ehehe, is that so?'

They give a lukewarm, helpless response as if tasting cunning miso that neither commits nor avoids. The whole sky suddenly clouded over as if ink had been poured across it, and even the starlight could no longer be seen. A sudden gale blowing up caused waves like mountains to rage and madden, and they began tossing the Tamanomaru about like a ball. Before long, the boat seemed to have advanced close to the northern shore of Awaji Island, and the lights of the island began to flicker faintly. Takahime sternly commands them to row the boat taking that firelight as their mark. The four men now found both arms so withered that they could no longer maneuver the oars and sculls. The boat seemed to have suddenly struck an underwater reef, and with a great roar of baribari, pachipachi, and mekimeki, the hull was shattered to smithereens, and Takahime and the others were swallowed up by the rough waves.

Tamanohime busily tied up her sleeves in a cross sash and skillfully manipulated the oars, while Takahime followed closely behind. At that very moment, caught in a sudden gale and taking the flickering firelight of Ahajishima as her mark, she barely managed to make a safe landing on the rocky shore, and decided to wait for daybreak. The wind ceased and the clouds dispersed; suddenly, in the azure sky, the sun with a golden rim peered over the mountain edge, casting a fresh and vivid light upon the sea surface, while flocks of seagulls joyfully soared back and forth over the waves. Appearing like a group of fishermen, four or five small sail-driven boats floated in the distant horizon, now visible and now hidden, illumined by the sun.

Just as Tamanohime turned toward the eastern sky to offer a norito, a groan was heard from nearby. Wondering and looking closely, she saw the bodies of five people washed ashore, in a state of unconsciousness, uttering only faint groans of 'Un, un.' Approaching to take a closer look, she was struck with amazement to find it was Takahime's party; she administered various forms of nursing care, performed chinkon, chanted the spirit-summoning divine words, and at length restored the five to life. Gently rubbing Takahime's back, Tamanohime spoke.

Tamanohime: 'Pull yourself together, please. Have you regained consciousness?'

She consoled her with a tender voice. As if coming to her senses for the first time, Takahime spoke.

Takahime: 'Ah, I know not who you may be, but thank you so much for saving me from such a perilous place. This must undoubtedly be because Hinodewake-sama descended upon you and helped me. Ah, what has become of the others?'

Tamanohime: 'Takahime-sama, please do not worry; I have cared for all of you, and you have gradually regained consciousness. You seem terribly exhausted, so they are lying down right here.'

Takahime: 'What person are you who knows my name?'

Looking intently, she saw that it was none other than Tamanohime.

Takahime: 'My! Are you Tamanohime, oh my, you have indeed come. To manage a boat all by yourself amidst the raging waves of the night before and cross this vast ocean—what a daring woman you are. And so, you saved me, likely intending to hold sway over Takahime, but that will never do, mind you.'

Tamanohime: 'Oh no, how absurd; why would I harbor such an ambitious thought? No matter where we go, Tamanohime is Tamanohime, and Takahime is Takahime.'

Takahime: 'What are you saying? Didn't you just say Tamanohime is Tamanohime? In short, your arrogant and audacious mindset—you are yourself and I am myself—and that tone of yours as if seizing equal rights by taking advantage of my weakness; however I look at it, Hinodewake-sama does not sit well with it. Thinking that I was saved by you makes for a fine story, yet I feel somehow less than pleased. Ah, that's it, that's it! Hinodewake-sama used Tamanohime's physical body as a temporary vessel. ...Ah, Hinodewake-sama, thank you so much for saving me. To descend upon a clouded soul-body like Tamanohime's could not have been an ordinary task. I sympathize with your trouble. ...Now, now, Tamanohime, from this very moment Hinodewake-sama has transferred his possession to this living shrine, so never, ever hereafter try to imitate me and pretend to be the living shrine of Hinodewake-sama, and harbor no such ambitions. Exercise a little modesty.'

Tamanohime: 'Be that as it may, it is truly auspicious that thanks to Hinodewake-sama, your precious lives have been saved.'

Takahime: 'As for that, my dear, it is a lie, isn't it? Even though it would be better if she died, an old hag persists forever and interferes with the bond of husband and wife; to call it auspicious is merely on the surface, and deep in your true heart it must be far from auspicious, ho, ho, ho, ho!'

Tamanohime: 'What on earth are you saying? Isn't that the height of abusive language?'

Takahime: 'It is a settled matter. Aligning yourself with the leader of Kototomegasane, Mokusuke, and others, making this living shrine swallow an octopus-trap, causing me to lose face, and plunging Takahime into a desperate predicament where retreat and advance are both blocked—where is the reason to call it auspicious that I was rescued? Flattering, sycophantic, and full of honeyed words with no limits, you folks certainly earned Takahime's heartfelt admiration. Without that degree of wicked cunning, you could never accomplish such a grandiose ambition.'
