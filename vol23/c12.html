Chapter 12: 家 Island 探 [724]

家島探〔724〕

§ 1

Fierce of spirit was Takahime,

When a fisherman's boat came rowing by at that moment;

Taking this as good fortune she leaped aboard,

Driven by an attachment even deeper than the ocean floor,

Resolved that right to the ends of Morokoshiyama,

She would search out and find the whereabouts of the three treasures,

Or else never let the matter rest.

Though she should become a snake or turn into a demon,

Though her corpse be exposed upon the open moor,

Though she should become mere seaweed of the sea,

If she failed to clear away this single thought,

How could the Yamatodamashihi ever stand firm?

Boasting of herself as the living palace of Hinodewake,

She felt she had now tracked down the reality,

And like a yasha with disheveled hair caught in the sea breeze,

Combing her body through the rough waves to display the divine power of Hinodewake,

Rowing through and cutting across the billows, at last she arrived off the shore of Takasago.

Sendō: 'Excuse me, customer, what you see on the right is the famous forest of Takasago, followed by the Stone Hōden and the scenic pine trees of Sone; how about it, would you like to drop by there for a moment?'

Takahime: 'Oh, I am in no mood for such things. I must reach Ieshima as quickly as possible, even for a single moment. Sendō, thank you for your trouble, but please do your best and row as fast as you can. Look, look, those five villains from before are chasing after us right behind. It will be terrible if we are caught, you know.'

Sendō: 'I am rowing with all my might too, but no matter what, the pursuers are five men, and since they take turns resting their bodies, it is a clever arrangement. However, in this neighborhood I have made a name for myself with the oar, and if you speak of Tōsuke the sendō, I am a well-known man. In return, you must pay me three times the normal fare.'

Takahime: 'Whether it is three times, five times, or ten times, what does it matter? As long as we arrive even a single step earlier, I will pay you. However, if there is even a single step of delay, I will pay nothing beyond the usual three persons' share.'

Tōsuke: 'Thank you, then I will make one big push from now on. No matter how skilled anyone else may be, they cannot match this Tōsuke.'

With that, tying up his twisted headband, baring his dark skin, facing the cold autumn wind and letting sweat flow, he rowed out his oar with even greater fury.

Takahime: 'Hey, hey, Sendō-sama, an island looking like a steamed bun is floating on the left; what island is that called?'

Sendō: 'That one? That is called the solitary island of the Seto Sea, also known as the island of the gods; it is a truly terrifying island. From ancient times even a sendō like myself has never drawn near it.'

Takahime: 'Surely there was some woman who rowed her boat to that island on the fifth day of May last year. You must have heard of it, right?'

Sendō: 'Do not speak of such absurd things. Even on ordinary days boats do not reach that island. Much less on the fifth day of May, since that is the day when the god of the island of the gods makes his crossing to the forest of Takasago, the sendō take a complete holiday. In this whole region, boats have never been put out on the fifth day since ancient times. If by any chance someone endured it and tried to send out a boat, the boat would instantly capsize and their life would be taken away, so what kind of fool would ever put out a boat on such a day or cross to a terrifying island like that?'

Takahime: 'Have you not heard any rumors as if some treasure were hidden on that island?'

Sendō: 'I associate with many sendō, but I have never heard even the pride of such a story. Even if we talk of the gods, they say the sea becomes rough, so please let this be the end of this talk.'

Takahime: 'Is that so? Well, if it is so, it must undoubtedly be Ieshima. Come, hurry please.'

Sendō: 'Understood.'

Struggling with all his might against the headwind, he finally reached the shore of Ieshima.

Takahime: 'Ah, that was hard work. As expected of Tōsuke-sama, you brought me here so swiftly. I will reward you handsomely, so do not say that I climbed up this mountain even if the gang from behind comes. If they ask, tell them that Takahime went up to the island of the gods, absolutely.'

Sendō: 'Yes, I understand.'

§ 2

Takahime fluttered busily and concealed her form within the dense forest of old trees. Tōsuke sat alone upon the gunwale, puffing on his pine-leaf tobacco.

After about half an hour, the small boat carrying Tamanohime's party drew close to this spot as if shooting an arrow,

Tamanohime: 'Ah, you are the boatman who brought Takahime. Oh, thank you very much for your trouble. Did Takahime climb this mountain?'

Tōsuke: 'Well... as for that... what... indeed it is.'

And he was scratching his head vigorously. Meanwhile, the boat was tied to the rocky edge and the five people landed.

Tamanohime: 'Did the female passenger you brought climb this mountain?'

Tōsuke: 'Yes, whether she climbed or did not climb, I was just... taking a nap, so I haven't the slightest idea. If you people happened to come here and inquire, I suppose I sent her off to Kamishima.'

Tsurukō: 'Ha-ha-ha-ha-ha, how indecisive your tongue is, giving an answer that commits to neither side. Tell me, boatman, did you stop at Kamishima?'

Tōsuke: 'Far from it, who would ever put in at such a place?'

Tsurukō: 'If that's so, how is it that Takahime-sama stopped at Kamishima? The truth of the matter is that she arrived right here at Ejima, but you were probably asked to hand her a fool's errand by saying she went to Kamishima. There is no doubt about it. You are a man with a black heart, quite unlike a mere boatman.'

Tōsuke: 'No matter what one says, it is a world where money does the talking. Even for a boatman, making money is still important, you see.'

Tsurukō: 'Ha-ha-ha-ha-ha, I understand, I understand. It is definitely this island. Tamanohime-sama, come, let us climb quickly. It would be a terrible disaster if your precious treasure were dug up and swallowed down.'

Tamanohime: 'That may be so, but there is no need to be in such a great hurry. Even if you say we will search, with an island this large, it is not so easily found. Come, let us have a pipe of tobacco.'

Kanshu: 'Isn't it as Tamanohime-sama says, no need to be in such a hurry? Takahime will search as she pleases; even if we walked for a day or two, we could never search it all through. Come, Tamanohime-sama, first let us take our ease. We are quite exhausted, you see.'

Tamanohime: 'Ah, please do so. As for me, the truth is I do not know where the treasure is located. Once I merely touched it, and afterwards the Dragon God hid it somewhere... so it must be hidden on some island in this vast world. The reason I pursued her here is out of concern for Takahime's well-being, wondering if her mind might have deranged, and thinking to assist her in an emergency as a propaganda envoy, so let us stop climbing such a dangerous mountain. Come, come, let us enter the shade of the trees and warm ourselves in the sun at a place sheltered from the wind.'

She led the way, climbing the mountain for two or three chō, and rested in a sunny spot. When she looked, a pond with an exceptionally large expanse of clear water unfolded before them.

Tsurukō: 'What a fine scenery it is. It is a wonder that there is such a large pond upon so high a mountain.'

Tamanohime: 'This place may well be the Dragon Palace of the land.'

Tōsuke: 'On this island, it is not just such a small pond. At both the summit and the middle of the mountain, there are very large, deep ponds, and they say they are bottomless. It is truly a mysterious island. Another wonder is that from ancient times not a single person has ever lived on this vast island; the rumor is that a great serpent always comes out, and whenever it smells human scent it swallows them all up, so no matter who it is, there is no one who dwells here.'

Kanshu: 'Is that so, it appears to be a dreadfully fearful place. If one were left behind all alone in such a place, one could hardly bear it.'

Kiyokō: 'That is so indeed. No one could possibly endure it.'

Indulging in various idle chats, they wasted kuōin for about a hitotoki.

Kuwanshiu: 'Come now, Tamanohime-sama and Takahime-sama have surely climbed straight toward the chiyōjiyō of this mountain without a doubt. It will be taihen if they dig out the treasure first and swallow it up, so let us set out at a leisurely pace.'

Tamanohime: 'I have hurt my foot a little, so I will rest here and wait. Dōzo, though it is gokurau for you, please go along as a goninzu.'

Kuwanshiu: 'Nay, that will not do. Now that things have come to this, I will speak my honne: we are absolutist sūhaishiya of Takahime. Look here, Oseto, since it has come to this, thrashing about will do you no good. Confess cleanly and swiftly the arika of the jewel. If you hem and haw to the saigo, I will tie a rock to this pond, have four araotoko throw you in, and make an end of it; how is that?'

Tamanohime: 'Even without venting such a bluff now, ever since we set sail from Awajishima, you must have been playing a prearranged sham fight with Takahime and making eye signals to each other. Do you take me for a Tamanohime who would not understand such a thing? I am just a bit different from a foolish woman who would carelessly fall for such a sequence of intimidating threats. Despite my delicate and weak appearance, this Tamanohime is the wife of Yoshihiko and a shrewd woman who has been honored with the trust of Kyōshu Kototamawakemikoto-sama. What good will it do for five or ten of you to twist your headbands and put on airs? The moment I let out a single shout and cast a reibaku, I am sorry to say, you will become stone jizō unable to move for mangoku-maida. If you still understand this, try doing whatever you like by way of test.'

Kuwanshiu: 'Ah, what a hopeless woman.'

Tsurukō: 'Moshimoshi Tamanohime-sama, it is a lie. Kuwanshiu always has the habit of playing such kyōgen and putting on a big bluff. Aha-ha-ha-ha.'

Tōsuke: 'What, thinking you fellows might be mountain bandits, I find you playing plays here leisurely in the midst of this mountain; what a remarkably clumsy play it is.'

Tamanohime: 'Anything is fine. Shall I meet Takahime from now on and let you know the arika of the jewel?'

Tamanohime: 'It must have been the field tengu of unified spirit and flesh who spoke, ho-ho-ho-ho. If that be the case, lest you worry, I shall walk right in the middle. Exercise sufficient supervision so that Tamanohime does not escape.'

Kuwanshiu: 'There is no particular need to supervise you, and I would be troubled if you let your thoughts wander to bad places.'

Tsurukō: 'What kind of thing are you saying? To die is your own business. However, it is troublesome for us, so after you confess the whereabouts of the treasure, you may die or live as you please. Until then, no matter how much you struggle, as long as these five rough men are attached to you, it is no use. Tamanohime: 'With that, the bottom of your bellies has become entirely clear to me. I too have a resolution.'

Before she could even finish speaking, she effortlessly knocked down the three men following right behind her, and aiming down the steep slope, she ran like Idaten back down the road she originally came from.

The five men, tightening their twisted headbands,

The Five Men: 'Ohey, Tamanohime, wait, do you think we'll let you escape? Hey, hey, everyone, we must grab her before she gets on the boat. Come on, hurry, hurry!'

They chased after her with all their might. Tamanohime, disheveling her hair like Ashiyuraō, changed her expression and came running down with all her strength. An old woman who appeared standing right in the middle of the road with her arms spread wide was Takahime.

Takahime: 'Ohohoho, you've finally fallen into Takahime's scheme and been dragged all the way to this island. What a fool you are. Come on, now that it has come to this, no matter how much you struggle, it's no use. Where did you hide the treasure? It would be best for you to confess obediently. If you dawdle and speak like that at this stage, it is not as though I might not take even your life. I want you to try putting yourself in this Takahime's position as well. Being of a good age, to be made a fool of by a young woman like you or a rice-bug like Hatsuwakahime, how could I possibly walk about in the world? Even with your wise appearance, as is expected of your youth, the essential wisdom is missing from you. Come on, how is it, Tamanohime, there surely can be no yes or no anymore.'

Tamanohime: 'Ohohoho, you are a person whose reasons are completely incomprehensible and who is suspicious to the end. No matter what you say of things I do not know, I do not know them. Even if my head were to be torn off, since I have said I will not speak, I will not speak, so make your resolve with that frame of mind.'

As they thus argued, the five men, making the ground rumble, came bustling to this spot. Tamanohime's surroundings closed in to a hair's breadth of crisis. Even Tamanohime, cornered with nowhere to turn and wondering what she should do, put all her heart into her prayer with all her might, 'Konohanasakuyahime no Mikoto, help me.' Suddenly, the surroundings were enveloped in a thick fog and one could not tell an inch ahead, and it became completely invisible as if a white folding screen had been set up—taking advantage of this, Tamanohime slightly shifted her path to the side, and when she looked back, the thick fog was strictly limited to the vicinity of Takahime's faction, while outside it was clear weather all around. Tamanohime, thanking the divine grace, finally managed to reach the water's edge.

Worrying and troubling over what had become of Tamanohime's whereabouts, both Abu and Hachi were operating a single boat and had arrived precisely at this spot.

Tamanohime: 'Ah, you are Abu and Hachi, you have come just in time. Let us chat leisurely about the story later.'

Abukō: 'Please get into this boat.'

Tamanohime: 'No, no, I have the boat I came on. I will operate it by myself and return, so you just follow along behind me and return.'

Before she could even say this, she leaped directly into the boat, untied the rope of the boat in which Takahime had come, left it drifting at the mercy of the waves, and the two boats headed toward the foot of Futatabi Mountain like flying arrows as they returned.
