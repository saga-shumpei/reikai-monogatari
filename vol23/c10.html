家宅侵入〔722〕

§ 1

As Akifuyu, clearing and clouding by turns, / Soratakahime lets the moon of true thusness be clouded, / And in the ocean of her mind, rough waves rise up in tumult, / Only to grow clouded once more. / Pride and repentance alternate; / The color of her heart knows no constancy. / Her clinging attachments surge and swell anew, / Lifting up their heads once more, / Down to the deepest depths of Konrinnaraku, wherever it may be, / Impatiently burning with the thought that she must search out / The whereabouts of the three sacred treasures and not rest until she does. / Accompanied by four attendants, / Crossing the mountain ridges by and by, / To the southern limit in Kii Province, / The Kumano of the paths without mischance, / With her chest smitten by the aimless waterfall of Nachi, sorrowfully / She makes her way toward Wakahikoyakata, / While the flame in her chest burns fiercely.

Flinging unreasonable demands, / Tormenting Wakahiko, his wife, and the other believers, / She was thrown outside the mansion by the physical strength of Tsunekusuji-sama, / And clenching her teeth in bitter mortification, / Looking back and looking back again, / Scowling at the mansion with sullen steps, / Together with her four men, / Climbing and descending the treacherous mountain paths, / Whether good or ill like the white-bleached hempen robes of Nanihae, / She clothes herself and turns back. / At the foot of Mount Futatabi, / The newly built divine pavilion, / Which First-Wakahime had guarded, / She returns to the forest of Ikuta.

Takahime: 'Ah, this is the original mansion of that ill-natured Mokusuke. Tama-nohime has heretofore flourished in the sacred ground, gleaming and shining just a little bit like a firefly, but in the end, because her pride was strong and her greed deep, she was cleverly taken in by Mokusuke's stratagem, and flew out of the splendid sacred ground... Since you inevitably have a karmic bond with the forest of Ikuta, it is a hardship, but please guard the divine pavilion at the foot of Mount Futatabi... Taken in by such a clever official appointment, she is a simple-minded creature who rejoices at being relegated to a place like this. That the deceiving fox was deceived—this is precisely that. Truly, a pitiful thing indeed. Wicked people seem to attract wicked people, and those faces of the fellows who gathered at Wakahikoyakata were nothing but creatures who looked capable of even thievery. From now on, you too must be fully on your guard; no matter what anyone may say, you cannot oppose the command of the God of Sunrise. Kanshū, Takushū, bear up firmly.'

Kanshū: 'I have understood every detail. However, from now on, is the God of Sunrise deigning to return to the sacred ground, or will you set out for some other direction? Please do inquire for a moment.'

Kanshū: 'Yet, Madam, do you not always proclaim yourself to be the living shrine of the God of Sunrise? A shrine is something that neither speaks nor moves, but your shrine moves quite well wherever it goes.'

Takahime: 'Eh, what a dense fellow you are! Because it goes wherever it wishes, it is called a living shrine. Since you do not understand this water-and-fire union of spirit and flesh, it cannot be helped. I took you all the way to Kii Province, judging you to be a man of considerable greatness, yet when this God of Sunrise was kicked out of Wakahikoyakata, bruised her lower back, and was suffering, you could not offer a single word of response, nor could you retort, but trembled and shook like a konjac monster, making a weeping sound... "Pardon me, Takahime-sama, whatever can be the matter?"—and the like. You are so utterly lacking in backbone, it was so foolish that I lost all patience. Ah, whoever they may be, when the critical moment comes, they are weak. The four companions who have come here used to say so readily, "If it is for the sake of Takahime-sama, we will die at any time, we will surrender our very lives." If you had that much courage, why did you not make your lives a target and beat down Wakahiko and the other ruffians? All bark and no bite—that is precisely you! From now on, if you do not tighten your bellyband and reform your hearts, you will not be able to serve in the crucial divine work. From now on, will you reform your hearts and listen to whatever the God of Sunrise says? Come, let me hear your revised answer. Yorigurashi like the ones until now will no longer be accepted, so it is no use.'

Takahime: '...' (Note: Paragraph 7 is part of the continuous dialogue of Takahime within the text block, or if it constitutes the end of the quote, it flows directly.)

Kuwanshiu: 'From this very day I reform my heart, and whatever you say, Anata, I will obey absolutely.'

Takahime: 'Even if I were to tell you to die, would you die, I wonder?'

Kuwanshiu: 'Yes, once the promise is made, I am a man who has girded himself with a loincloth one jo and two shaku long. I shall certainly make no mistake, my friend.'

Takahime: 'Ah, now at last I feel at ease. Now then, Takekō, Kiyokō, Tsurukō, how do you all stand?'

Takekō: 'Yes, I am of much the same opinion. Depending on the matter and the thing, I would even give my life.'

Tsurukō: 'What is this talk about "much the same opinion"? It seems you are still infected with the spirit of Kotoyoriwakenomikoto, characterized by indecision and the heunenshiyugi philosophy. Since such Tsutsuishiki fellows will be excommunicated from this day forth, pack up and be off at once.'

Takekō: 'To say that, when a life is but a single one and cannot be replaced, can one just hand it over to you so recklessly? The life I have given to the Great Gods cannot be disposed of at your whim, Anata. Even if we call it absolute obedience, after all, it is a conditionally absolute obedience... eh, Kuwanshiu, your absolute obedience probably stops right about here, doesn't it?'

Kuwanshiu: '......'

Tsurukō: 'Hey, Kuwanshiu, you obey Takahime-sama absolutely, and though it is not the Genpei War, you intend to go all the way to Dannoura in Nagato, or so you may think, but when the three of us are excommunicated by Takahime-sama, what are you planning to do? You surely have not forgotten that the four of us swore to act together wherever we might go, have you?'

Kuwanshiu: 'That I certainly have not forgotten. We made a promise that we must never forget each other, but the details have not yet been settled, so you must leave that to my free will. In exchange, the promise not to forget will be kept no matter where, and I shall never forget, so rest assured.'

Tsurukō: 'What kind of nonsense are you spitting out? Execution is what matters. Forgetting or not forgetting is ultimately a secondary problem. The three of us have just now... received our leave from Takahime-sama, and therefore we sever relations with you. In return, it is not only on moonlit nights, you know. On dark nights, please be on your guard.'

Kuwanshiu: 'To spin such dumpling-logic and threaten me like that is quite more than I can bear, is it not? I would be in a fix if you could adopt a bit of a detached spirit and interpret my words in a benevolent light.'

Takekō: 'No matter what you say, we were trained by Takahime-sama, who has strong attachments, so even if you tell us to become detached, how could we possibly do so? It is like ordering a kite to caw "caw-caw" or a crane to sing "cock-a-doodle-doo".'

Takahime: 'Now, now, while you are facing a most momentous divine work, why do you catch at every single word I said and make such a fuss? I said harsh things only to spur your hearts on. Even this Takahime has... wait... hold on, I have countless shapeless allies, you see. In this situation, even a single person is precious. Who on earth would ever want to excommunicate anyone? You ought to have the intuition to understand that much, shouldn't you? Isn't that so, Tsurukō, Kiyokō, everyone?'

Thus she speaks coaxingly.

Tsurukō: 'If you unbend so graciously, Anata, we have no desire to wrinkle our brows in anger and stiffen up as if we were made of glued paper either. We will listen to whatever you have to say, so please do command us as you wish.'

Takahime: 'Ah, then at last the internal strife has been safely quelled. Yet once again you say things that incite a revolutionary mood, which is why you are such a trial. Now, listen well to what the living shrine of the Hinodewake god has to say.'

Tsurukō: 'Yes, yes, we shall obey. Whatever the matter may be, if you will command us without hesitation, we shall be deeply grateful.'

So saying, he sits formally upon the lawn, places both hands on the ground, and deliberately bows with elaborate politeness.

Takahime: 'If that is the case, this mansion is locked up tight right now, but even if you have to snap the locks off, get inside and investigate. That wretch of a Tamanouehime—there is no knowing what she might be up to. Inspect every single drawer of the chests, and if you find any secret documents, bring them out without fail.'

Tsurukō: 'Understood. We shall break in right away and ransack the place thoroughly.'

Takahime: 'Be careful not to let her spot you, and make sure you do a clean job of it. Now, go at once!'
