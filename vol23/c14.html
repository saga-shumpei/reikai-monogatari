Chapter 14: 籠抜 [726]

籠抜〔726〕

§ 1

Before the absent master's gate of Tōsuke, the director of Hitogo whose name was renowned in the village of Sumoto, stood a single Sendenshi, quietly chanting a Sendenka.

Hashitame, the maid-servant, heard this voice from the kitchen, and when she opened the courtyard door and looked out, it was a Sendenshi of about forty years in the prime of his manhood, attired in a rain-hat, straw sandals, and leggings.

The Sendenshi turned toward Hashitame,

Sendenshi: 'From my everyday experience, whenever I pass by the front of this mansion, I somehow feel as though a sudden and unexpected henji has broken out within this house. Is there nothing amiss in your household?'

he asked with gentle words. Hashitame tilted her head slightly,

Hashitame: 'Please wait just a moment. I will go into the back and ask the Okusama...'

leaving these words behind, she straightway concealed her presence.

In the back chamber, the wife, Yuri, was leaning against the hibachi, seemingly deep in thought about something. Hashitame softly slid open the fusuma,

Hashitame: 'Okusama, Okusama!'

she called out. Yuri seemed caught up by something, and Hashitame's voice did not reach her ears. Hashitame fearfully crept close before Yuri,

Hashitame: 'Moushi Okusama, a mysterious Sendenshi is standing at the gate. What on earth shall we do?'

at these words, Yuri raised her face,

Yuri: 'What, a Sendenshi is standing at the gate? That is a fortunate thing. There is something I would like to ask him to divine... pray, have him pass this way.'

Hashitame: 'Yes, I understand.'

she swiftly emerged to the front,

Hashitame: 'Moshimoshi Sendenshi-sama, the Okusama apparently has something she wishes to request of you, so please do pass through to the back.'

The Sendenshi nodded in agreement, followed behind Hashitame, took off his straw sandals, washed his feet, and was ushered into Yuri's living room. Yuri stepped down from her seat, invited the Sendenshi to the upper seat, and bowed respectfully,

Yuri: 'Sendenshi-sama, you are most welcome to have stopped by. First, please rest at your leisure.'

Sendenshi: 'I am a Sendenshi named Tomohiko of the Brahman religion. When I was about to pass the front gate of your house, I could not help feeling a sense of unease, and thinking that some unimaginable incident must have suddenly broken out in your household, I have paused to make inquiry.'

Yuri: 'That is truly, truly kind and I am deeply grateful. The truth of the matter is that my husband, a person named Tōsuke, has no one knows where gone since two or three days ago, and his whereabouts are entirely unknown. Thinking that perhaps in the typhoon of the other day, being a husband who prided himself on his boat, he steered his vessel and was swallowed by the rough waves, it has been utter chaos with everyone turning upside down. The villagers have divided themselves into search parties, and not to mention the mountain forests and wildernesses, they have searched every nook and cranny of the nearby seas to no avail, and his whereabouts remain completely unknown. Whether he is alive or dead, even that we do not know. Might I beg of you to consult the gods for us once?'

Tomohiko had observed the commotion of the nearby people, had previously heard from afar that Tōsuke was missing, and had purposely stopped by. Nevertheless, pretending to know nothing at all,

Tomohiko: 'That must indeed be a grave worry for you. Let me try to consult the oracle for you.'

he washed his hands, rinsed his mouth, and seeing that there was no one about, let out a cunning grin and stuck out his tongue,

Tomohiko: 'According to the villagers' talk, since they searched that thoroughly, there is no longer any fear that he is alive. If I skillfully hoodwink them, as the wealthiest family in Awaji, I shall become the master, and if I expand the Brahman religion throughout the whole of Awaji with this wealth-power, it is a matter of nothing. Ah, what a favorable wind has begun to blow. However, it would be a terrible disaster if the master were to return alive by some remote chance, but such a thing will rarely happen. Let me summon my courage and give it a try.'

he muttered in a low voice. Right at that moment, the wife Yuri returned holding a fresh hand towel,

Yuri: 'Sendenshi-sama, pray wipe your hands with this.'

§ 2

He thrusts out his chin. Looking at that profile,

Tomohiko: 'Ah, what a pretty woman she is! ...However, I hope my current soliloquy wasn't overheard.'

Feeling driven by a slight sense of unease, he peered askance at Oyuri's face, but Oyuri showed no such awareness. With a sigh of relief, Tomohiko stroked his chest in reassurance, sat up calmly and properly on the raised tatami platform (tanza), finished chanting the Brahmin scriptures, and becoming falsely possessed by a spirit (nisekamugaka),

Tomohiko: 'Mm, mm, mm, I am Daijizaiten Ōkuniwakenomikoto.'

He thundered out in a roaring voice like rolling thunder. Startled, Oyuri prostrated herself flat on the ground,

Oyuri: 'Yes, thank you very much.'

She spoke in a tearful voice. Tomohiko opened his mouth once more,

Tomohiko: 'Tōsuke, the master of this house, though leading a life free from all cares, had the bad habit of loving sea-fishing or engaging in adventurous enterprises. That is why he threw away his life. Pitiful though it may be, it is now beyond help. If only this propagandist had come to this house three days ago, I would have let you know, but alas, what a regrettable thing it was. Now, beyond this, nothing can be done. Praying for the peaceful repose of his soul, performing solemn memorial rites so that the master may be saved in heaven, and taking a husband like a powerful god to succeed Tōsuke—if you do not do this, this house will surely never endure eternally. Furthermore, since Tōsuke suffered from a testicular disease and could not have children, you must give over the wealth he took such pains to accumulate to strangers. You should take the words of the god deeply to heart, and it is the best judgment for you to do everything according to the command of Ōkuniwakenomikoto.'

Oyuri: 'Yes, yes, thank you very much. ...If it is the command of the god, I will not oppose it no matter what.'

Tomohiko: 'What a remarkably fine creature you are. Being just the wife of Tōsuke after all, your spirit has been well polished. The god is also filled with admiration.'

Oyuri: 'No matter what I may say, as an ignorant woman of the world, there is not a single thing about me worthy of being praised by a god.'

Tomohiko: 'According to what is reported among the common folk, you are indeed a woman of steadfast virtue. Even without hearing the rumors of the world, the god sees clearly right down to the bottom of your heart. The master who once died will no longer answer when called, nor return when cried out to; resigning yourself to the fact that it cannot be helped, guarding your future household with care, giving birth to and multiplying descendants, and protecting the ancestors' home—is this at least your wifely fidelity to Tōsuke? Do you understand?'

Oyuri: 'Yes, yes, I humbly understand. However, for a person like me, how could there ever be someone willing to come as a second husband? Somehow it feels as though my mind cannot be at ease regarding my husband's spirit. And as for having such a husband, might I be permitted to ask that it be done at least after finishing the three-year memorial service?'

Tomohiko: 'Listen well to what Ōkuniwakenomikoto says. Human sophistry is not worth discussing. Delays are dangerous, so you would do well to welcome a husband as early as even a single day. As for that husband, since the god will bestow him upon you... if you do so, your descendants will multiply like the stars in heaven, your house will be unperishing for ten thousand generations, and I will make you the happiest person in the world.'

Oyuri: 'Yes, yes, thank you very much. Please treat me favorably, I earnestly beg you. And as for this husband you speak of, from where would it be best to receive him? I should like to ask you about this as well.'

Tomohiko: 'There is no need to go searching anywhere else. It is pitch dark right under the lighthouse; right now, before your very eyes, a peerless bridegroom of the three realms has come. Since the god intends to act as a matchmaker and has brought him all this way, you would do well to rejoice and obey the command.'

Oyuri: 'My Lord God, strictly speaking, I see no one around here at all.'

Tomohiko: 'How lacking in perception you are. I am the living shrine's propagandist of Ōkuniwakenomikoto, who is now conveying the divine oracle right before your eyes.'

Oyuri started with a sudden shock, gazed intently at Tomohiko's face,

Oyuri: 'You are someone whom I seem to have met once upon a time in the village of Naniwa, are you not?'

お百合『オホヽヽヽ、何と悪魔と云ふものは、どこまでも抜目のないものだ。的きり此奴は姉さんの宅で三百両騙り取つた奴に間違ない。まだ主人の生死さへも分らない内から其処ら近所で噂を聞いて来よつて、良い加減な事を言ひ、若後家を誑らかさうと思うてやつて来よつたのだなア。どうやら目を眩かして居るらしい。今の間に細帯で手足を括り、庭先へ引摺り出し、水でもかけて気を付けてやりませう。……アーアそれにしても東助さまは如何なつたのかいな。村の衆は、未だに誰も報告に来て下さらず、イヨイヨ妾も未亡人になれば、今迄とは層一層腹帯を締めねばなるまい。あゝ困つた事が出来て来た』

§ 4

Just as she was thus saying, Ossatsu hurried up to this place,

Ossatsu: 'Okusama, please rejoice. Dannasama has just returned in good spirits.'

Oyuri was overjoyed as if ready to take flight,

Oyuri: 'What, Dannasama has returned? Ah, I cannot remain like this. Come, come, I must go out to welcome him.'

As she was straightening her collar, Tōsuke had already appeared, leading three men and making the edge-boards of the corridor resound sternly,

Tōsuke: 'Ah, Oyuri, you must have worried since my return was rather late. It seems you have also caused a great deal of trouble to the villagers. Not that I was blown away by the wind after all, but I went as well as to Ejima. Well, please be at ease.'

Oyuri: 'That is of all things the happiest news. In connection with this, while you were away, a four-legged creature crawled in, so I have kept it caught alive just now. Please take a thorough look at it.'

She pointed at Tomohiko.

Tōsuke: 'What, is this not a human being? Is he not bound tightly?'

Oyuri: 'Yes, I bound him for a moment. This fellow is the thief who defrauded three hundred ryo in my elder sister's household last winter. Hearing the rumor that your whereabouts were unknown, he skillfully coaxed me and appeared with the intention of usurping this house.'

Tōsuke: 'That is an outrageous fellow. However, we cannot leave him bound like this. Since I must save him... Hey, hey, Tsurukou, Kiyokou, Takekou, it is troublesome for you men, but untie his bonds, pour water upon him, and tend to him carefully.'

The three untied the bonds as ordered and poured water over him. At long last Tomohiko recovered his senses, sat up, saw the figures of Tōsuke and the others, was greatly astonished, pressed his head to the tatami, and apologized with tears. Turning toward Tomohiko, Tōsuke spoke,

Tōsuke: 'You assume the appearance of a splendid propagative messenger, but judging from what I hear now, you seem to be a terrible scoundrel. In this world, evil can never prevail anywhere.'

Tomohiko: 'Yes, indeed I was in the wrong. I have no face left to show. Please spare my life alone. From this very moment, I will abandon being a propagative messenger.'

Tōsuke: 'I do not say that you should quit the role of a splendid propagative messenger. Polish your soul all the more and become a fine propagative messenger. And your true calling is to guide the people of the world into the path of goodness. As for things like turning deities into dried bonito to cajole women, or seeking out houses with sick people and taking advantage of their vulnerability to commit fraud as you have done up to now, you would do well to put an end to them from this very day.'

Tomohiko: 'Yes, thank you very much. Please save me. From this very time on, I will reform my evil ways.'

At such a juncture, the voices of a multitude arose at the gate,

Voices: 'Tōsuke-sama has returned alive. He has come back safely, hurrah, hurrah!'

The voices of joyous acclamation echoed as if even the mountains were shaking.

Tōsuke: 'You, if you truly aspire to reform, a great many villagers have come to the gate just now, so make a confession speech or something for them. Without hiding a single detail from beginning to end, lay bare your past misdeeds and show the state of your repentance. If you cannot do that, as a great thief, I, Tōsuke, shall exercise the authority of a chieftain to punish you.'

Tomohiko said in a small voice,

Tomohiko: 'Yes, I will do it.'

Tōsuke: 'Come, go out to the gate quickly and begin your confession speech.'

Tomohiko:

Tomohiko: 'Yes, I will go at once. Suddenly I feel the urge to relieve myself. Please grant me a moment's grace to go to the outhouse.'

Tōsuke: 'If it is the outhouse, it is right there. Come, you had better go quickly.'

Tomohiko:

Tomohiko: 'Yes, thank you very much.'

He immediately entered the privy, crept out through the straddling hole, and taking advantage of the dusk which had just begun to fall, headed straight for the dense forest behind the mountain and hid himself with all his might.

The three men, Tsurukou, Kiyokou, and Takekou, stayed for a while at Tōsuke's house, and finally being influenced by Tōsuke, repented of their past errors and came to faithfully observe the teachings of Kototamawakenomikoto from the bottom of their hearts.
