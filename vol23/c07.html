Chapter 7: 知らぬが仏 [719]

知らぬが仏〔719〕

§ 1

Akihiko and Komahiko, the Sendenshi, together with the elderly couple Tsunekusu and Ohisa, departed from the village of Kiyama and gradually arrived at the banks of the Kurisugaha and the forest of Kurisu. As they were aged people, they felt hirau, and here Komahiko's father, Tsunekusu, suddenly felt an intense kiyōfukubu, developed a severe hatsunetsu, and became unable to make even the slightest miugo. Ohisa, along with Akihiko and Komahiko, both riyōnin, earnestly desiring to bring about the kuwaifuku of Tsunekusu's biyōki, stopped by the half-ruined nagatoko of the Kurisu Shrine and tried various ways to administer kaihō, but the illness only grew heavier ohihisa, and his tanseki approached closely.

The two Sendenshi heard that there was a meuyaku in the upper reaches of the Kurisugaha, and making tewake, they proceeded deep into the mountains to seek medicinal herbs. Afterward, Ohisa was unremitting in nursing her husband, exerting her mental and physical strength, forgetting even the labors of her old age, and devoted herself to nursing him. This lonely forest of the Kurisu Shrine, far from human habitation, had no human voices, and only the calls of crows and the murmurs of shrikes could be heard from time to time, while the winter wind blew in distinct gusts. Just as one would expect, even the winter in the warm region felt particularly severe on this very day, and goosebumps broke out all over the body.

The night deepened profoundly, and the moon shone brightly in the mid-heaven, as if looking down with pity upon the circumstances of the pitiful elderly couple, while pale clouds drifted past the face of the moon from time to time. Each time, it brightened suddenly, then grew dim again suddenly, and light brown clouds and white clouds mixed together, wandering swiftly right and left in the sky.

At this time, two large men with covered faces, whispering something to each other in low voices, came advancing toward this forest, and without knowing that the elderly couple was inside the nagatoko, they sat down on the veranda, indulged in whispering, but finally, caught up in the excitement, began to chatter in loud voices.

A: 'Hey Abukō, lately the thief business is completely withered by winter, and the pocket is cold too. Some good bird is bound to come flying by. The gold coins we took by lodging at the old man and old woman's house in the village of Kiyama have mostly been used up, and now we can't go forward or backward. If we don't do a big job here, we won't even be able to feed the foster children we can't afford, and even the foster children's wives and children will end up completely dried up. Can't you come up with some good idea, hey?'

Abukō: 'Hey Hachikō, when you get your hands on gold coins, you scatter them away right away like scattering ashes in a great wind, so it's really troublesome. You have few foster children and live a single life, don't you? Someone like me has as many as ten foster children I can't even afford, and with Mokubei in the neighborhood holding a family of seven or eight people, we simply can't get by with small work. The gold coins we took in the village of Kiyama were also about a hundred ryō, but since you took fifty ryō as an equal split and went home, your spending must have a lot of leeway. Our responsibilities are terribly different from yours...'

Hachikō: 'No matter what you say, fifty ryō is fifty ryō. There is no reason why fifty ryō can be used as a hundred ryō just because the household is small, and even if the household and foster children are many, fifty ryō remains fifty ryō. There is hardly any fear of it becoming twenty ryō. Don't say such stingy things.'

Abukō: 'That habit of yours—poor thing, you did that to that girl and ripped her apart right in front of her parents, didn't you? How on earth could you do such a demonic thing?'

Hachikō: 'Henh, if I'm a demon, you're a demon too.'

Abukō: 'Even among demons there are good and bad, and yours is a specially made horned demon, a so-called male. Mine is a female, so she's a demon without horns, you see.'

Hachikō: 'That's settled. If we are demons, we must fulfill our duty as demons thoroughly to the very end; with someone like you, the moment you get a little gold coin, you try to reverse toward the path of the Buddha or the path of money, how could you ever accomplish a big job like that?'

Thus they were talking. A voice of agony from inside the nagatoko pierced the ears of the two men.

Abukō: 'Hey, isn't that a strange voice?'

Hachikō: 'Indeed, a queer voice has come to be heard. It sounds entirely like the growling of a wolf. I wonder what on earth it is. How about investigating it once?'

Abukō: 'No, no, a man of virtue does not approach danger. It might be a ghost, you know.'

Hachikō: 'It makes me sick to hear you talk of a man of virtue. Is there any blind person anywhere who would take a scoundrel like you for a man of virtue? It might be that Okō's ghost thinks you fellows are coming here and is waiting for you. Somehow, the nape of my neck has begun to tingle. Outside a cold wind is blowing, and inside a creepy voice is heard; hasn't it become unbearable?'

Abukō: 'If you keep talking such timid nonsense, it will turn out—not to mention our names—just like falling between two stools, won't it? Who knows, maybe travelers with a lot of money are sleeping there, and that yellow-gold stuff is groaning "un, un." Let's muster our courage, step right in, and find out the true identity of that "un," shall we? Who knows, it might be the opening of our luck.'

Hachikō: 'Make sure that if you carelessly bungle it, the luck doesn't come down and turn into "un" coming out of your rear end. Since you are always a panicky fellow, you've never had your rear end under control. You are a fellow who reaches out his hand all year round and shits himself, haha.'

He laughs. Hearing this laughter, Okō jumps to the premature conclusion that the two sendenshi whom she had been waiting and waiting for have returned, opens the door from the inside, and says,

Okō: 'Ah, I've kept you waiting. You two gentlemen, please come in quickly. You must have been terribly cold.'

Both at once:

'Hey, if it isn't someone else, are you the mistress of this house? Anyway, let us have a puff of smoke then.'

And they enter the house. The faces of Abaku and Hachi are reflected in the faint light. Okō gazes at them and says,

Okō: 'Ah, aren't you the thieves who stayed at my house the other day, took my daughter's life, scooped up all my money, and ran away? Now then, since things have come to this, you are my child's sworn enemies, and I will no longer forgive you. Prepare yourselves!'

Holding her short sword in a reverse grip with a ferocious look, she assumed an upper-rank posture.

Abaku and Hachi open their mouths wide and burst into loud laughter: 'Aha ha ha ha.'

Okō: 'For thieves to be audacious is just like you! Prepare yourselves for this old woman's desperate struggle; my life as an old person is no longer something to regret.'

She rushes in to slash at them.

The two smoothly draw their long swords,

'Now, come,'

they plant their hips, and wait, poised to cut if anyone approaches.

Okō, too, finds no opening to exploit in the battle-readiness of the two fierce warriors, and without even blinking, aims to slash if the slightest opening appears.

The two draw closer step by step, with their drawn swords in both hands, gripping the hilts near their bellies.

Tsunehisa is running a high fever, out of his mind, and groaning 'Un, un.'

At this juncture, both Akihiko and Komahiko return with quickened steps; when Komahiko enters the interior first and sees this state of affairs,

Komahiko: 'Hey, I am called Komahiko, a sendenshi of Misoshiru. You look like a thief, but coming to a house of only old people, even if you tried to steal something, there wouldn't be anything to steal. Rather than doing unnecessary things, you had better leave this place quickly. If you dawdle, it will not be to your advantage.'

Okō notices this voice for the first time, and brandishing her short sword, says,

Okō: 'Is that you, my son Komahiko? My, thank goodness you've returned to such a dangerous place... Akihiko-sama, please give us your support. This fellow is the villain who killed my daughter and stole our money.'

Hearing this, the two instantly clasp their hands together, imbue their whole bodies with spiritual power, and with a single shout of 'Un,' apply spiritual binding.

Remaining in their battle stances, the two become stiff and turn like wooden statues, rolling only their eyes around.
