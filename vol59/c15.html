Chapter 15: 和 Song 意 [1515]

和歌意〔1515〕

§ 1

Michihiko, Itahiko, and Debisuhime— The three of them ran out into the open courtyard of the mansion, Taking advantage of the dark. At that very moment, into a cavern deep beneath the ground, They slipped and fell headlong with a sudden crash. Passing the night in this place, To console their boredom, Commander Itahiko came out to the entryway, And while sitting cross-legged on a folding stool, Was lost in deep thought. Just then, slipping down smoothly and rapidly, He saw a single man falling. Surprised, he nevertheless composed his mind, And with the amusing quick-wittedness Of a newly opened innkeeper, Commander Hēru, who had been thrown into the pitfall While competing in love, Was utterly astonished at first sight. 'Where are you from, and who might you be? How suspicious,' he inquired.

Scratching his head, Itahiko: 'I am the clerk of the Itaya Inn. Please, do stay at our humble home for a night. We have first-class, second-class, and third-class rooms, Divided accordingly; Yet examining your character, You are stark naked without any money. Therefore, you are restricted to our first-class inn. As we have just opened, Nothing is properly prepared. Whether it be ogre-ferns, iron fists, Or plenty of twisted rice-cakes, Please partake of them freely. As for the tea, it is neither hot nor lukewarm; Naturally from a vacuum flask, Though it smells a little, I shall pour as much as you wish. Come now, quickly, Wash your feet and proceed to the inner room. Pass right through, come now, quickly.'

'Today is our auspicious opening day, How wonderful that you have stayed with us.' As he mocked him with this and that, Even the great Hēru was completely dumbfounded: 'Well then, I shall accept your hospitality; I kindly ask for your favor in every way.' Promising an lodging fee of ten thousand en per day, He passed through into the inner room, Where the maid, Debisuhime-sama, And Michihiko, disguised as the cook, Sat formally and politely, Separated by a mere single room. Hēru called out to the two of them: 'A first-class guest has arrived! Make haste and draw some tea. For a cook and a maid to be sitting idly In the inner room in the dead of afternoon, Engaging in secret chatter— How could such an improper thing be permitted? A-ha-ha-ha-ha-ha, a-ha-ha-ha-ha!'

The three of them raised their voices together, And just as they were enjoying their laugh, There came entering yet another rough man. Looking closely, it was none other than the master of this house, Captain Chiruteru, Glaring intently with fierce eyes— How terrifying was his countenance!

Hēru: 'Oh, Chiruteru-sama! You have done well to pursue me and come here. If only this were a woman, it would be exceedingly convenient, ehe-he-he-he!'

Chiruteru: 'Is that woman over there not Debisu? Bound so tightly and tied up inside the storehouse as she was, how on earth did she come out to a place like this?'

Debisuhime: 'O-ho-ho-ho-ho! Are you not Captain-sama, the barrier keeper? You were of great service to me the other day. Coming all the way to my bedchamber, lifting me up like a portable shrine or something with a "Heave-ho, heave-ho!", and politely putting me inside the storehouse—thank you very much. However, Lieutenant Kanna-sama came along and pressed me, saying, "I wish to make this storehouse my residence, so please vacate it quickly!" Having been evicted by the landlord, I had no choice but to relocate to this Cavern Hotel, where we have only just opened for business.'

Chiruteru: 'What? Swapped places with Kanna? Hmph, you speak of incomprehensible things. Hey, Michihiko is also right there, isn't he? Snuck into my residence in the middle of the night, plotted some evil deed, and by divine retribution must have slipped down into this pitfall.'

Michihiko: 'A-ha-ha-ha! Hey, Chiruteru-sama, my wife was under your great care. Thank you very much. But how did you come to be in this place?'

Chiruteru: 'This area is under my jurisdiction, so I merely came for a brief inspection. What of it?'

Hēru: 'Ha-ha-ha-ha! You say such sweet things! Look here, Michihiko-sama. The truth is, enchanted by the charms of Hatsuwakahime-sama, the two of us lengthened our upper lips and courted her, whereupon Hatsuwakahime-sama said she would become the wife of whoever won a sumo match. That being the case, by bad luck I misstepped and plunged down headfirst. After that, this general here was supposed to match against Teku, but apparently he lost as well and came tumbling down into this cavern. Once a barrier keeper of Kiyo falls into this cavern even a single time, he is dismissed from office; therefore, today's Chiruteru is no longer a Captain. Throwing away a rank like Junker would be fine enough, but to throw away the title of Captain, which he labored so hard to knead together, is a bit pitiful indeed, ufu-fu-fu! Look here, Michihiko-sama, now that things have come to this, we are no longer Brahman soldiers or anything of the sort; please show us affection with the spirit of universal brotherhood under the four seas. The ones who carried away your wife were not us. It was this Chiruteru who brought his soldiers and stole her away! And then he did all that to make Hatsuwakahime-sama his own wife, and to bestow Debisuhime-sama upon Lieutenant Kanna-sama to keep his mouth shut. I sympathize with your trouble!'

§ 2

Debisuhime: 'The person who carried me off, now I sit face to face with him in harmony. Since I have realized that all things are the Shigumi of Kamisama, I hold no grudge at all.'

Chiruteru: 'Blinded by the ugly Magami called love, I have piled up sins I never intended. From here on, I shall reveal the sins of my heart in Matsubusa before you and apologize.'

Debisuhime: 'You, whose Magokoro has not yet been lost, are entering the Kado of Kami's salvation.'

Chiruteru: 'Arigata, your Kotoba makes me tear up, feeling the deep compassion of the Sumekami. You are a Kami, for you do not hate an Adabito but treat me with such kindness.'

Hēru: 'Do not be taken in by the glib tongue of the captain, for this is but a lament born of suffering. How hateful is this man who has once again brought out his Jifuhachiban trump card. Chiruteru drove away his beloved wife and sought to take a treacherous woman as his bride. I am still a celibate who has not taken a wife; have pity on my Muku body.'

Chiruteru: 'How amusing it was when you lowered your eyes to the wife of the Yokochiyō Tokoya and were rejected. How funny you are, Hēru, crying and sniveling after being given the Hijitetsubō by the Tafuku woman.'

Hēru: 'Exposing the faults of others before an Ōzei, you must be a vessel of a Magakami.'

Chiruteru: 'Whether I am a Magakami or a true Kami, I do not know, but I am speaking the truth as it was.'

Michihiko: 'Omoshiro, the emotions of people are the same in Kara and Yamato, no matter how far the ends of the earth may be. I wonder what has become of Itahiko; he has not shown his figure yet, and I am anxious.'

Hēru: 'He has settled in as the head clerk of Itaya, with a ledger in one hand and an abacus in the other. What an interesting man he is; he fell into a rocky cave and is now acting like an innkeeper.'

Michihiko: 'From here on, let us remove the fences of our hearts and, helping one another, walk the path of Kami.'

Chiruteru: 'Arigata, your heart is without discrimination as you preach the path of truth.'

Hēru: 'If this were the office of the Brahmin, it would never go this way.'

Debisuhime: 'Bound by the thread of fate from ancient times, how delightful it is to be in harmony today.'

Chiruteru: 'From here on, we must purify our hearts and advance toward the great path of the Kami of three and five.'

Michihiko: 'Without abandoning the teachings of the Brahmin's god, protect our path of three and five.'

Thus the four became reconciled, exchanged their opinions, and came to live in harmony like brothers. Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haema se.
