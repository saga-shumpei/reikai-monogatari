Chapter 3: 蜜議 [1503]

蜜議〔1503〕

§ 1

The Ikigami of Ananai-kyō and the renowned Sendenshi, the Kamutsukasa Hatsuwakahime, and the party of Tamakuniwake, in order to save and protect them from danger, led the Mauken Smart, crossed the lake of Kiyo, and stopped at the Chirtel mansion of the Brahman army's Sekimori. In a most beautiful detached house, while playing the Ichigenkin, they devoted their hearts and exhausted their bodies to the Kamuwaza of the Kami's guidance, serving with reverence.

The Chirtel official who was in charge of this Sekishiyo had his soul stolen by the noble Yōshi of Hatsuwakahime, and disregarding his own status as a married man, he kept her within his Yashiki, waiting for the time to serve his own desires.

Waiting for the time when this Hime would yearn for him, and intending to achieve his Yabau of love, he gave her an Ichigenkin and hid her quietly in a Hitoma.

Hatsuwakahime, the Sendenshi, as the Chirtel official requested, confined herself in a Hitoma of the detached house, and while secretly chanting the Kamigoto, she played the chords to soothe her sorrow, waiting for the time to arrive.

Chiruna-hime, unable to fathom the depths of her husband's heart, and burning with the flames of Rinki, secretly called the two men, Kanna and Heeru, near to her, revealed the extent of her heart, and plotted to grant the two men a Hakarigoto to Hauchiku the Hime from the mansion.

How pitiful it is to see them so preoccupied. Kanna and Heeru, having become slaves to love, advanced near the side of the Hime's mansion, sang strange songs, danced and whirled, and fluttered their Kohiginu in the wind of the Aoba, raging as if this were their Sendo.

When Hatsuwakahime opened the window sash and looked out at the garden, she found it suspicious. The two men in their Churick attire were singing broken songs of love-madness, looking in with flushed faces.

Hatsuwakahime called out to them, invited the two men in, served them tea and sweets with her own hands, and treated them with great kindness, which was contrary to the expectations of the two men.

With their eyes drooping and drooling, thinking the time for the fulfillment of their desires had come, how comical it was to see their chests pounding. As the heart-jewel of the Kamutsukasa Hatsuwakahime shone brilliantly, the two men, being rough fellows who knew not how to approach her, trembled in their bodies, and with a manner as if they were some kind of pug or cat borrowed from another's house, they remained reserved and flushed with red faces.

Hatsuwakahime: 'Listening to your Churick, it sounds as if you are a Lieutenant and a Yunker, but what dignified and manly figures you have. Men should always be soldiers. A warrior who has both flower and fruit is also well-versed in literature, but from what I hear now, you are masters of both literary and military arts; I am truly impressed.'

Kanna: 'Heh, not at all. I am honored by such praise. I am but a mere warrior; I have no literary taste whatsoever. It is a dry and tasteless thing, you see.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Oh, why do you say that? To compose such a song on the spot is a feat that cannot be done without considerable literary cultivation. You are a soldier now, but I suppose you must have graduated from a liberal arts university with honors, must you not?'

Kanna: 'Oh, you flatter me. Actually, I am a graduate of the Red Gate, but thanks to that, I did not receive a silver watch... Aha-ha-ha.'

Heeru: 'I, for one, am a true-blue graduate of a liberal arts university. My career has been full of ups and downs, truly rich in a tragic history. Kanna-kun could never even come close to me.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'I would very much like to hear about your interesting history and your future policies.'

Heeru, thinking this was the moment, stepped forward a pace or two. Since he had told this Nice that he was a graduate of a liberal arts university, he felt that now was the time to demonstrate his literary flair, to describe his history through fluent poetry, to move the Hime's heart, and to make her admire his literary talent as the primary superior method. Rolling his eyes, he began to describe his history through a song.

§ 2

Heru: 'Since the days of Tenchikaibiyaku, the Taiyō has grazed the Tōten and risen, entering the Seiten every day. When the sun sinks into the Seiten, the darkness of Ankoku suddenly arrives. The moon suddenly reveals its form in the Seiten, shining Shōshō upon the Ten. The moon sets, the crow cries, and again the Taiyō appears in the Tōten. The starlight of the Manten hides its shadow in an Ichiji, and the Ginga appears in the Tōzai or flows in the Nanboku. The Ten is Sausau and without Saigen, and the Chi is Kōkō and has no Kiukiyoku. I, having received the Seiki of the Tenchi, have been granted life in this world of Manmokushiyōshiyō. Aa, man is the Reichiyō of all things, the flower of the Tenchi. Suddenly growing to become a man, I carried my books and climbed to the capital of Haruna, breathing the air of Bunmeikaikuwa, passing in and out of the gates of the Bunkuwadaigaku, and bearing the honor of excellence, I returned home in glory. At such a time, the commander-in-chief of the Baramon army, by the divine will of the Lord Ōkuninushi-no-Kami, became the most excellent and noble soldier of human life. In the morning I tread upon the moon, and in the evening I crown myself with stars, devoting myself to military duties. Sometimes I cross rivers and seas, cultivating the Kōzen-no-ki, and follow the divine army. Traveling a thousand ri to the land of Izumi, I finally reached the port of Kiyo. Do not speak of me as a mere low-ranking officer; my future is vast and limitless, and there is hope for success. I now pause my steps here to protect the living spirits. A beautiful maiden, Yōchōsenken, has descended from the Ten and is in this mansion. Who would have known that the person of my heart would appear before my eyes? Human affairs are like the horse of the old man at the frontier; how could I, a minor official, treat this lightly? I pray that you will perceive the grand design I hold within my heart, and that you will allow us to bind the vows of mandarin ducks, kneeling and praying before the Baramon deities.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Ohohoho. As one would expect from a graduate of the Bunkuwadaigaku, it is a poem with a lingering charm that is everywhere. I, too, am very fond of literature. It truly makes the spirit of spring sunshine drift about.'

Heru: 'Ehehehe. Oh, I am quite embarrassed. Now, Kanna-kun, Lieutenant-dono, why don't you also tap the depths of your brain and trouble the Princess-sama with your listening here?'

Kanna: 'Princess-sama, I shall now recite a poem for a short while. I humbly ask you to be the judge.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Yes, if that is the case, I shall serve as the temporary chief judge and listen. I am looking forward to it from now on, as I am sure I will hear an excellent poem.'

Kanna: 'Then, with your permission, I shall recite a verse. Hey, Heru-sama, please listen carefully.'

Kanna: 'The sun shines, clouds gather, and rain falls. The moon waxes, shines, wanes, and glows. The shadow of the sun crossing the vast sky, and the figure of the moon, are now here. Compared to the Princess who appears, they feel like they do not even compare. The complexion of this Princess-sama is the appearance of the God of Hinode. The depths of her heart are the Mizu-no-Mitama, shining across like the moon of the fifteenth night. When I look at her head, the jewels sparkle like stars, with a brilliance that shines across. Though I have heard that man is the spiritual being of the Tenchi and a miniature of the universe, until now I have never seen a miniature where the name and reality truly meet. The appearance of Hatsuwakahime, is she a celestial being from the Heavenly Kingdom, or perhaps the Princess of the Ryūgū?'

Kanna: 'Her whole body is glistening, and without distinction between inside and outside, she shines like a crystal ball. With a head adorned with the colors of gold, silver, agate, glass, and coral, and wearing hairpins of giant clam, she has appeared in the land of Izumi and descended to this mansion. With the vow of saving all sentient beings, possessing the thirty-three manifestations, the Kannon, Seishi, and Myōon Bodhisattvas, I now bow before you in person, and the darkness of my heart clears up, how noble it is. Heru-sama, lost on the path of love, has squeezed out his specialty literature, recited a poem of seven difficulties, and though he is impatient to make her say "Ah" and move the heart of the Princess to fulfill his desires,'

Kanna: 'How could the Chibiki-iwa move? Though we push and pull, our weak strength cannot reach it. Aa, Kannagara-kamu-nagara. If the blessing of the divine spirit exists, and if there is a connection, let us bind the vows of mandarin ducks with this nice person forever. For the sake of the Gods and for the sake of the world, may you open the true teachings to the countries of the four directions, O Jizaiten. Before the presence of Ōkunihiko, I, Kanna, who serves as a Lieutenant, purify my heart and pray. Aa, Kannagara-kamu-nagara. May the divine spirit bless us. This place is the Kiyo-no-Sekimori-Kamitsukasa of the famous Baramon; may it remain peaceful forever.'

Ananai-kyō and Ural-kyō, and all other hundred Shikomichi, spreading through the world and clouding the hearts of Hitobito, the Magakami are captured and punished at the Daiseijiyō; as the deputy to Captain Chiruteru, who was appointed as the official in charge of that place, I, Kanna, pray that you may grant me the fulfillment of my desire as soon as possible.

Asahiko: 'The figure of the Hime, whose smile flourishes, surpasses even the Amatsuotome. I wonder how I might speak the full measure of my heart, yet I find myself alone and faltering in my speech.'

Heru: 'Everything must proceed according to the will of the Kami, for in this path alone, there is no other way to act.'

Hatsuwaka: 'I speak to the compassionate Mononofu, my own self is truly joyful. I wish to serve, uniting my heart for the sake of the Kami and for the sake of the world.'

Kanna: 'I feel somehow that something is still lacking, for I cannot fathom the Mikokoro of the Hime.'

Heru: 'If you wish to wear the Kohigoromo, wash away the filth of the Utsusomi and become pure. If you polish your Murakimo heart for the Kami, how could even the Amatsuotome dislike you?'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Pushing through the Yomogi-ga-hara of Michinoku, please pluck the flower that does not wither, my lord.'

Kanna: 'Please understand the agony of my heart that wishes to pluck it, oh precious Yokihito.'

Thus, while probing each other's hearts through songs, they spent the long summer day without even realizing it had ended. Chiruna-hime, worrying and fretting over the success of the two, stood outside the window with hushed footsteps, holding her breath and listening.
