歌垣〔1502〕

§ 1

In the Rusutaku of Captain Chiruteru, the Sōtorishimari of the Sekishiyo at the port of Kiyo, his wife Chirunahime-sama was whispering about something with the lieutenant Kanna and the junker Heru, the three of them putting their heads together in secret.

Chiruna: 'Look here, Kanna-sama, Heru-sama, do you not think that the Yōsu of the Dannasama of late is a bit strange?'

Kanna: 'That is so, and though it is difficult to Maoshia as you are the Okusama, it seems that the Dannasama has become quite suspicious of late. Is that not so, Heru?'

Heru: 'Hmm, that is true. However, we lowly ones have no Kenri to say anything regarding the Kaudō of our superior officer.'

Chiruna: 'Look here, Heru-sama, setting aside matters of official duty, we are speaking openly today regarding private matters, so if you think something is not right about the Dannasama, it is your duty to give me your Chiukoku. If you cannot say it yourself, I shall speak to him when he is in a better Kigen, so if you have noticed anything, do not feel any Enriyo and tell me straight away. No matter what kind of Eiyū or hero a man may be, he cannot succeed if his wife is not firm.'

Kanna: 'Indeed, as you say, Okusama, no matter how difficult the problem, if one sneaks in through the back door and manages to get into the good graces of the Okusama, it will be solved immediately. Those who enter through the front door do not know the art of swimming in the sea of officialdom. If one quietly brings in some fine cloth or jewels that the Okusama might like through the back door, one is sure to succeed. No matter what, one cannot succeed in this world without a woman behind them. Hahaha!'

Chiruna: 'Look here, that is enough of that. What do you two think of that woman who plays the ichigenkin in the back room from morning till night?'

Kanna: 'That is so. First of all, I find that truly strange. That beautiful woman who keeps the room shut from morning till night and only plays the koto has not even greeted us once, and she is always whispering and giggling with the Dannasama. And since she does not even greet you, the Okusama, I think it is a suspicious matter.'

Heru: 'Hmm, that one? When I asked the Dannasama about her, he said, "That person is Abarōkitē-Shuwara-sama who has descended from the heavens. She is a celestial being who has descended to protect the Brahman religion." Okusama, please do not worry; if I may be so bold, surely you would not be so base as to feel jealousy. Jealousy is the most fearful demon that defiles feminine virtue. Since they say she is the savior of the Trailō-Kyabokurā, it would be a disaster if those with defiled bodies were to approach her carelessly.'

Chiruna: 'I know not how much of a Kanjizaiten she may be, but when I hear her speaking in such sweet, honeyed tones with my own husband, using her beautiful female body, it does not make me feel very good.'

Heru: 'Indeed, from your position, Okusama, it is no wonder you feel that way. However, that is where patience comes in. Well now, please observe the Yōsu for a while. There is no way that such a virtuous Dannasama would bring in another woman when he has such a wonderful Okusama.'

Kanna: 'Hey, Heru, you cannot be so optimistic. When it comes to women, a man is blind and deaf. Much less when she is a peerless beauty of the world, young in age, with skin the color of purple-polished gold, full of charm, and a woman Bodhisattva who possesses all thirty-two marks from every angle—no matter how strong-willed a man is, once he is caught by a single glance, his bones and everything else will vanish like an octopus. With that appearance, adorned with the seven treasures such as Suvarana, Rūbuya, Burawāza, Bazumarāka, Maniratsuna, Musaragaruwa, and Asumagaruta, from head to toe, how could any human who is not made of wood or stone not have their heart moved? Truly, Okusama, if you are not careful, it will be a dangerous situation. If you are not careful, who knows when a low-pressure system will strike, or when a great uproar of earthquakes, thunder, and rain of fire will break out, with him saying, "Chirunahime has no love for her husband, so I shall dismiss her as of today."'

Chiruna: 'Dō, Kanna-sama's Gokuwansatsu must be correct. I wonder if any good ideas will come to you two. Actually, ever since that woman came to this mansion, my Shinkei has been in such a state of Kōfun that I cannot sleep even for a Hitome.'

Kanna: 'Naruhodo, your eyes, Okusama, are Chibashi. If you do not exercise Yōjin, you will become hysterical.'

Chiruna: 'That is true, she is a Bijin who might become the seed of my own Fukau at any Nandoki, so there is no way I can feel Anshin, is there? That person is by no means Kuwanjizaiten or Monjiyushiribosatsu. She is, after all, a normal Ningen. Dannasama is just saying such clever things to Gomakuwa you all. Kanna: 'Heh, that is a Gomeirei I could not wish for more, and I would like to accept it immediately, but if I were to do such a thing and offend Dannasama's Gokigen, would that not be like the Tabiya sign being Ashiaga, and I would instantly become a wanderer?'

Chiruna: 'Ho ho ho ho, my, your soul is behind the times. Are you that afraid of being stripped of your rank of Lieutenant? Think about it well; if you were to make such a nice woman your wife, you would become famous throughout the world and be respected even more than a General. You could live in comfort for a generation just by turning one of the jewels she wears into money, could you not? If you let such a Bijin slip through your fingers, you had better retire from being a man. For a man, decisiveness is the most important thing.'

Kanna: 'Naruhodo, when I hear that, my appetite for it is greatly stirred. However, I had thought to endure for another ten years or so, rise to at least the position of Colonel, return home in glory, become a candidate for the Diet, win the election successfully, start a Kototama battle brilliantly on the floor of the Diet, and be praised as a splendid politician, but this is a point where I must deliberate.'

Chiruna: 'Rather than being showered with cold criticism by the public as a Diet-cluttering assemblyman, a contradictory assemblyman, a coal-wearing assemblyman, an accident-prone assemblyman, a puppet assemblyman, or a tombstone assemblyman, and being called a dung-frog of the Shibire-ga-hara, it is a much shorter path to success to have such a nice woman as your wife, have her slip in and out through the back door of the Prime Minister's office to brush the dust off your beard, and become a minister of the table. Even if you become a puppet, you will never be able to become a governor. First of all, if you want to succeed, you must have a Bijin of that caliber as your wife.'

Heeru: 'Excuse me, Okusama, is this Heeru outside the budget? If Kanna is to have that Bijin as his wife, I would like to have her too. Two men for one woman, does that not seem unbalanced?'

Chiruna: 'As for that, you two should just have an election race and win it successfully. If you lose, it just means your campaign was insufficient, so you have no choice but to give up. You can just wait for the next general election and try again.'

Heeru: 'Excuse me, how should I go about that campaign method? No matter what, the other party is a Bijin unparalleled in the world, and she possesses as much treasure as she likes, so I cannot win her favor with gold and silver, and if I try to rely on my looks, I am a zero, and my eloquence is not good, so I cannot get close to her at all, can I?'

Chiruna: 'Ah, that is why they say elections are unpredictable. They say that fate is a strange and curious thing, and women fall for strange things, so rather than trying to be clever and having your inner thoughts seen through, it is the shortest path to act as comically as you can to make the woman loosen her chin and think, "My, what a cheerful person, he is quite a clown; it would surely be fun to be with a man like this. It is not fun for me to be playing the koto all by myself in a place like this. For the first time in a while, the strings of my chin have loosened. What a nice, silly fellow."'

§ 4

Tanabata has its meeting once a year, yet why is the bridge of love impassable to me?

Kanna: 'Following the will of the Kamunagara Kami, I sing a song. How happy I am to see the face of the Hime as it opens. Open the window and look out at the garden; there are two sinful men standing there. Chiruna-hime, you grow horns and grumble, but there is a reason that cannot be spoken. Open the Tsumado that I am knocking on, for I am not a man who has been cast aside.'

Hearing the voices of the two singing, the Bijin stopped playing the Ichigenkin, tilted her head, and pondered the situation for a while.

Kanna: 'The Neiro of the Ichigenkin has stopped abruptly; she is lost in thought within the window.'

Heru: 'Now, open the Shiyōji that was shut tight, and the Kuwannonbosatsu peeks out. At that moment, we must tidy our faces and compare ourselves as men. Ah, yoitosa, yoitosa!'

When the Bijin quickly opened the Shiyōji of the Renjimado and looked out at the garden, the two men in Churikku attire were exposing their buttocks and performing a Kotsukeiodori.

Bijin: 'Looking at the garden, I see the shadows of strange people; my heart leaps and the people dance. I know not who they are, but the sight of them dancing wildly before my window is amusing. They sing an interesting song, and these strange-faced men clap their hands and dance in a frenzy.'

Kanna: 'With all the Murakimo of my heart, I have exhausted my Magokoro to come and yearn for you, my lady.'

Heru: 'I am surprised anew by your face, which is like the moon and the form of a flower.'

Bijin: 'What shall I do? Since I am a maiden of the heavens, I have no way to accept the love of a mortal man.'

Kanna: 'How strange. While possessing a human body, are you exempt as a maiden of the heavens? I, too, am a Masurao who has received the soul of a celestial being from Takamagahara.'

Heru: 'This man is a fellow who shaves the heads of men, and that is why he is called Kanna.'

Kanna: 'This man drinks only sake, and his fortune melts away in the sun like ice; he is a fool named Heru.'

Bijin: 'In any case, you rare Masurao, I invite you to advance into my room.'

Kanna: 'Following the words of the Kamunagara Hime, shall we advance into your presence?'

Heru: 'Now, brandishing the belief that love is best, I shall easily pass through the gate of success.'

Bijin: 'In any case, you gentle Masurao, come before me with a peaceful heart.'

Kanna: 'How awe-inspiring is the Hime who has opened the door of the gate so easily, just as I had hoped.'

While chanting this, they opened the front gate with a rattle, and while moving their limbs in a restless manner, they sat down before the Bijin with an air of shyness.
