Chapter 21: 客々 Boat [1521]

客々舟〔1521〕

§ 1

Rather than witnessing the destruction of the Shiyōjiyōō's physical body, the Saagarariuō performed a Kōdori dance, rushed up onto Shiyōjiyōjima, and, intending to swallow the entire flock of Kozaru, grew thick and long in an Enen manner, coiling three or four times around the rocks that formed the island's defenses, and while opening its massive mouth, waited, intending to swallow them all whole without leaving a single one behind. The many Shiyōjiyō were startled, and in their Urota and clamor, they raised Himei, crying out 'Kya-kya!', and approached Yakkosu, Haaru, and Sabooru to beg for salvation, but the three, valuing their own lives more than the Shiyōjiyō, even if they were to be eaten by the Akuriu, attempted to resist as much as they could, and hoping for a Geukau to save their own lives, grabbed stones from the Isobata and threw them at the Akuriu, aiming at its Mega.

The three hundred and some Shiyōjiyō, imitating the humans, each took stones in their hands and threw them. Even the formidable Akuriu was Menkura, and for a moment it hesitated, when Itahiko-shi appeared, recited the Amatsu Norito, and the rigorous Kototama of Hifumiyoi-munaya-koto-momo-chi-yorozu, with a voice of a hundred, a thousand, and ten thousand, struck out the Kototama stone-balls. As one watched, from the body of the dragon, black smoke rose up in thick clouds, and from between its hard scales, it spat out a Guren tongue.

Unable to endure that extreme heat, the Saagarariuō descended from the rocky mountain and, with a splash, hid its form in the sea; what joy it was. Yakkosu, Haaru, and Sabooru, with a start, stroked their chests in relief, and turning toward the twenty ships led by Itahiko-shi, they joined their hands and choked with tears of gratitude; how pitiful they were. The many Shiyōjiyō joined their palms, turned toward Itahiko-shi, and raised a war cry of 'Kya-kya-kya!', weeping and shouting in tears of joy.

Suddenly, the lake water surged and boiled, turning into hot water, and the countless large and small scaly tribes all floated on the surface, bellies up. Itahiko-shi, feeling pity for them, suddenly turned toward the Ōkami of Heaven and Earth and recited the Norito: Hifumiyoi-mutsu-nanaya-kokonotsu-tari-momo-chi-yorozu. With that voice, the scaly tribes floating in the sea instantly recovered their vitality, and full of vigor, they splashed about, and all together, lining up their heads, expressed their gratitude. Itahiko, the messenger of God, saved the three children of God who had remained on the island, rescued the lives of the hundreds of Shiyōjiyō who had served as the kin of the Tenō-no-mori, and while saving even the lives of the scaly tribes floating in the sea, demonstrated the divine work of Truth, Goodness, Beauty, and Love to the utmost extreme.

Upon the waves where the heart also feels courageous, offering prayers of gratitude to the Amatsukami and Kunitsukami, the hundred angel messengers who protect the Sangokyō, and the other gods of the sea, they were sent off by the Shinato-no-kaze and returned with light hearts. Such an example is truly noble; from the beginning when Heaven and Earth opened, it is a divine ship crossing over the rough waves of the world once more; it is indeed a most auspicious occasion. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, may the spirits be blessed, and as Zuigetsu, who is floating on the waves of the tatami in a rectangular ark, prays for this story to be told in detail, without a single drop leaking, and growing steadily.

While lying down and taking the helm, blowing smoke that does not twist from a Shikishima cigarette mast, on April 3rd, at Kitamura, Kitamura Takamitsu, while bathing in the back in the light of the morning sun that shines brilliantly, continues to tell the tale. In the province of Hōki, at Yonago Station, in the second-floor room of the Hamaya Inn in the village of Kaike, a name that is itself auspicious, he tells this story, a living reproduction of the Age of the Gods, a work written by a maiden, telling it here in detail. The Kami-naohi who created this world, and the Ō-naohi whose heart is vast, say only this: in the world of men, whatever happens, look at it again and listen to it again with Naohi. Correct your own mistakes by reciting them again. The Ōmikami of the Sangokyō, in this world at the end of three hundred thousand years.

The story that Zuigetsu, born into this world, tells and transmits while parting the clouds and mist. Even if there are many digressions and errors, please correct them with a broad heart and forgive them, Kannagara. He continues to tell it as the gods will. Originally, because five people of the party came to this village with Zuigetsu, the villagers whisper that it is an unprecedented catch of fish. Hearing it without intending to hear, it is charming that the villagers are saying one after another that they have received this divine virtue since the God of Ōmoto-kyō came to this land. The waves on the beach of Yomi, connected to the Sea of Japan, are clear, and it is the pivot of the land of Japan.

§ 2

Deep within the Kaitei, the Daisen of Hinokahakami rises, its pure form draped in robes of snow against the Tenkū, gazing down at the Mizukagami of the sea. With both Ten and earth purified, the pine forests and the Kaisui, and the Rokukon of human Mitama all cleansed, the Monogatari of Shiyōjiyōjima is proclaimed with a courageous heart.

The waves beat the drums of Taihei, and the lake of purification is free of dust. The Otohime of Riugūkai sends forth a multitude of Giyozoku, and Shiyōjiyōbune, not mere Hakobune, are pushed forward by countless Daishōmusū of Urokuzu, numbering in the Ikuhiyakuman, leaping and splashing as they propel the Mifune.

Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara, may the Mitama be blessed.

Itahiko sings with a clear voice. The Shiyōjiyō keep time in unison.

Itahiko: 'Reflecting the shadows of Amatsumisora, the lake of Kiyo holds no dust. Lining up twenty ships, heading toward the island of Shiyōjiyō, we rescue and return with three hundred and thirty-three of the kin of the forest of the Ten-ō, along with the three of one-transformation-nine. Kyatsu-kyatsu-kyatsu-kyatsu, kyatsu-kyatsu-kyatsu! Dondoko, dondoko, dokodokodon! With the waves calmed, this ship gliding straight across the surface is a rescue boat of the Amatsumikuni. With the Shiyōjiyō troop on the maiden voyage, filling the twenty ships, we uncork the mirror of sake and drink as we please, gazing now upon the scenery of the Tenkoku Jōdo.'

Itahiko: 'With man, beast, and monster all rejoicing in today's sky, it is truly a joyous occasion. Kyatsu-kyatsu-kyatsu-kyatsu, kyatsu-kyatsu-kyatsu! Dondoko, dondoko, dokodokodon! Try once to accompany a person; try once to ride a horse. The brush-tips of the Sangokyō clearly reveal that one never knows what kind of person is hidden where. Thinking of whom this might be, it was this Itahiko, who even I believed to be but a shoe-bearer in the group. No matter how great Masumihiko or Mitsuhiko may be called, they have never wielded the power of leading a group of over three hundred.'

Itahiko: 'How did my Mitama become so pure? Kyatsu-kyatsu-kyatsu-kyatsu, kyatsu-kyatsu-kyatsu! Dondoko, dondoko, dokodokodon! No, wait a moment, wait a moment. Even if I have raised my head as a leader of three hundred, there is no reason to act so haughty, leading humans with such faces. If it is a creature with a human face, I care not if they are a beggar or a thief; it is interesting to be their leader. Though I say this is a bit daunting, since the soul is superior to others and we are called Shiyōjiyō-sama, perhaps I may boast a little. Thinking even this far, my strength suddenly wanes.'

Itahiko: 'I cannot go on boasting with a big face. If the master teacher Tamakuniwake-dono were to say but one word—that he sent for me because it was fitting for my Mitama—that would turn everything into foam. I would surely be mocked by Masumihiko, Mitsuhiko, and especially Debisu-hime. When I think of it, it is foolish. In the sadness of being called a fool, I will at least strip the mast bare, roll down the sail of the fool, and row back with the strength of my arms. My courage is only this much. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara, I leave it to the will of the Kami. Kyatsu-kyatsu-kyatsu-kyatsu, kyatsu-kyatsu-kyatsu! Dondoko, dondoko, dokodokodon.'

Itahiko: 'In the lake of Kiyo where the waves calm, the Mizukagami of the Misu-no-Mitama. When I peek into the Mizukagami, oh, how strange; even my face is wrinkled. That is to be expected, for this ship rowing upon the waves is but boards joined together. Waves strike this form that has not aged, yet my strength surpasses that of others.'

Singing thus, he gulps down sake with a long-handled ladle, and escorted by tens of thousands of Giyozoku, he heads south and returns.
