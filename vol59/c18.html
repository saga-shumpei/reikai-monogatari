Chapter 18: 手苦番 [1518]

手苦番〔1518〕

§ 1

Tamakuniwake and his party, having been saved from a life-or-death crisis by the unexpected Kamubashira Hatsuwakahime, escaped from the rocky cave, parted from Hatsuwakahime, and headed back toward the mansion of Bachiru in the village of Suma, where the roofs stand high along the southern foot of Mount Azumosu.

Teku, a great drinker who had been serving as a spy for the Brahman religion, walked ahead of the party, and while putting strength into his legs, he flapped his wings and sang as he walked: 'The mountain wind of Mount Terumon, gently brushing the surface of Lake Kiyo, passes by; its tip hits the mansion of Bachiru at the foot of Mount Azumosu.'

The air today is noisy and boisterous, with the young and old, men and women, all excited and making a great clamor of drinking and singing. Teku suddenly disguised himself as a servant of the house of Bachiru, and while offering sake to the villagers as part of the celebration, Chiluteru, who had long served as a gatekeeper of the Brahman religion, came riding and whipping his horse, approaching the front gate with the intention of capturing every single one of the Kamutsukasa of the Ananai-kyō.

Seeing that it would not go well, Teku spread out the secret documents of Songo, and no sooner had he scooped up sweet sake with a long-handled ladle than he puffed it toward their noses; as if they had absorbed an anesthetic, their initial momentum vanished, and they nimbly jumped off their horses and entered among the young and old, men and women, drinking and eating like whales and horses.

No matter how arrogant a captain may be, he is fragile when it comes to sake; as the intoxication gradually set in, Teku deceived them while under the influence of alcohol, saying that if they lured the Nice of Hatsuwakahime, who was hidden in the detached room of the mansion, he would make them lieutenants. Knowing from the start that it was a lie, they took it as a diversion and stood up with courage, appearing before Hatsuwakahime and repeatedly saying 'Arimasu' with a soldier-like air, which was comical; but the one they thought was Hatsuwakahime was not a true person.

In the end, she showed her tail and turned into a white fox like a small calf, and the horror of it crawling out was such that strange events appeared one after another without end, and finally, everyone was knocked down into a cellar and gasped for breath. Just then, the true Princess, leading her fierce dog Smart, saved my master and his party, including Chiluteru and the others; how grateful I am. Ah, Kannagara-kamunagara; the grace of God shone instantly like the morning sun, and even the road in the night that had not yet dawned felt pleasant for some reason as we headed toward the Bachiru mansion.

The village of Suma, not dreaming that such a commotion had occurred, saw the young and old, men and women, gathering and sitting firmly for a seven-day and seven-night banquet, drinking and eating like whales and horses, crying, laughing, and getting angry, and surely they were making their throats ring. Now, from here on, this Teku-sama will also drink his fill of Hannyatō for the first time in a while, gulping it down to set his spirits right. Ah, Kannagara-kamunagara; the missionaries of Tamakuniwake and the other hundred officials, though I cannot see clearly in the night, the shadow of the forest visible in the distance is surely the divine mansion of Bachiru. Let us whip our legs and return home with one more effort; whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, or whether the moon waxes or wanes, the taste of sake will never change for ten thousand eons. Is there anything in the three thousand worlds with a better smile than sake? It is interesting to laugh, to cry, and to be enraged. People of the world will never blame what is done under the influence of sake. What a good excuse I have found. Ah, warm sake, warm sake; cold sake and sea bream soup are not sweet.'

Singing such trivial songs, he walked on with spirit and slipped through the front gate, where many young and old, men and women, were lying about naked day and night, exposing their black bodies, their bellies swollen like mackerel sushi, snoring loudly, with the sounds of sleep-talking and farts.

Watching the scene of great chaos that looked like a toy box had been spilled, Teku burst out laughing, 'Aha-ha-ha-ha, aha-ha-ha-ha,' and finding it amusing, he stepped over the main entrance and advanced into the inner room with a heavy thud, thud, thud.

§ 2

Akisu and Karl, the two, encountered Teku face-to-face in the tatami corridor while the party was attempting to proceed to the inner room. Upon seeing them, Teku said,

Teku: 'Well now, the head clerk has returned. Quickly, bring about one to of sake. Why are you dawdling? You should come to the inner room as well and listen intently to the splendid, meritorious, and heroic tale of the new head clerk.'

Akisu: 'Hmph, do not make a fool of me. You have arbitrarily decided that I am a head clerk, and you, Banta the watchdog, if you and your lot were to become the head clerks of this house, that would indeed be a terrible disruption.'

Teku: 'Do not talk nonsense; I have been duly entrusted by the master. The entire household management of the Birch family is under the governance of this Teku-sama.'

Tamakuni: 'Hey, hey, is it not pointless to quarrel in a place like this?'

Teku: 'Yes, I understand. Akisu, that fellow, was saying such disrespectful things, calling me Banta and a watchdog, so it slightly offended my pride. Well, in any case, let us report this latest meritorious achievement to the master.'

With that, he swung his arms exaggeratedly and, while stomping his feet with a heavy thud-thud, proceeded into the master's living room.

Birch and Princess Sabel were in the midst of praying with all their might before the gods for the safety and security of the party of Tamakuniwake. The party of Tamakuniwake also finished clapping their hands and thanking the gods for their safe triumphant return, and then took their seats. The couple finished their prayers, looked at the safe faces of the party of Tamakuniwake, and were filled with joy; while shedding tears, they said,

Birch: 'Ah, Sensei, you have returned safely. We were so worried about you.'

Sabel: 'Ah, Princess Debisu, Michihiko-sama, Itahiko-sama, how have you been? My husband and I were terribly worried, clinging to the gods and crying as we prayed. Oh, congratulations.'

Tamakuni: 'Yes, thank you. We encountered various trials from the gods. Oh, I am so sorry for causing you such great worry. Well, as you can see, we have returned safely without a single injury, so please rejoice.'

The couple bowed their heads at once, overcome with joy and tears of happiness.

Teku: '(Nori) Pray, listen, Master, I beg you to hear me out. To save the official Michihiko, Following the divine official Tamakuniwake, And the noble Masumi no Hiko, We had the former clerk Anchi, Guide us as we walked briskly, Pointing the way through the back path. In the sky, the sun shone fiercely, Radiating its brilliance, And in the agony of the scorching heat, We stopped in the shade of the forest, Letting Tamakuniwake, Masumi no Hiko, And Anchi, those three, rest. This fellow Teku, all alone, Proceeded to the back gate of the Chiruteru mansion, And seeing it, the door was locked tight, So, losing my nerve, I went around to the front gate, And seeing it, there was no one there.

I looked to see how the office was, But not even a kitten was to be found, Only mice in the ceiling, squeaking, Fighting over love with frantic voices. This, I thought, was strange, so I went to the back entrance, And looking out, I saw the parlor of the detached house, Where a nice woman called Hatsuwakahime, With a white face, was smiling, Placing two naked figures before her, And issuing some kind of order. This is interesting, very interesting, I thought, and as I rushed over to see the details, To my surprise, the two, Chiruteru and Heeru, Were wrestling, And the ghost was saying sweet things, That she would become the wife of the one who won, And I, this fellow Teku, was taken in by the lie, And began stomping my feet with a thud-thud, While wringing out my sweat drop by drop.

Suddenly turning into a wrestler, I threw the two of them with ease, And cast them into the cavern of the earth. Then Hatsuwakahime, with a gentle face, Approached me, asking if I would shake her hand, Thinking, 'This is interesting, this is interesting,' Since I was born a man, I reached out my hand, thinking I would try To shake her hand and give her a kiss, But when I grabbed it, what do you think? The slender arm was covered in hair, And instantly she transformed into a white fox, Glaring at me with angry eyes. Terrified by that fierce demeanor, Without even looking back, I ran off, saying, 'Etsa-tsa, Etsa-tsa,' And 'Stakora-yoiyasa,' Into the darkness of the forest, Where Tamakuniwake was waiting.'

Ichidō: 'Utsufuhahaha.'

Bāchiru: 'Hey Teku, you have reported various things in a theatrical manner, but the truth is hard to understand. However, since everyone has returned safely, there is nothing happier than this. Now, go over there and drink plenty of sake. And you must never tease Akisu, Kāru, and the others.'

Teku: 'Yes, I understand. Then, Goshiyujin-fūfu-sama and Tamakuniwake-itsutō-sama, please excuse me for a short while. Farewell.'

No sooner had he said this than he ran off toward the place where the sake was.
