暗傷〔1508〕

§ 1

The flowers that bloom and scent within a marriage, until when shall they fall, Chiruna-hime? Wishing to bear the fruit of domestic peace, and to share the joy of growing old together for a thousand, nay, eight thousand generations, she prayed to Kami to pass this life in tranquility; yet, Chiruna-hime could not set her heart at ease. By the blowing of an unexpected wind, she heard that her husband, Chiruteru-tsukasa, with whom she had pledged their lives in two worlds, had had his pure spirit clouded by a Shikogami, and having become a man of lust, was dallying with women here and there. Chiruna-hime was startled, and suddenly grew the horns of jealousy; she smashed the household tools, tore the shiyōji and fusuma, and fell into a state of half-madness.

Chiruteru and Kanna were startled, thinking that the takitsukegusuri they had plotted had worked too well and caused an unexpected shitsutai, so they left the room of Hatsuwakahime. They jumped into this place immediately, and Chiruteru grabbed the topknot of Chiruna-hime with his left hand, clenched his sazae-like fist, and struck her three or four times with a loud thud. Chiruna-hime, in a fit of rage, clung to him, grabbed the tea-bag hanging between the legs of Chiruteru-tsukasa with all her might, and pulled it with all her strength. He could not bear it, and with a single cry of 'Ah!', he fell into a swoon, foaming at the mouth, and collapsed in the shape of a large character.

The sight of him trembling was ridiculous; even Chiruna was surprised, and in her anxiety for water and medicine, she scolded Kanna-tsukasa and wept in agitation. Mitsuhiko and Itahiko, who had been following the trail of Debisu-hime and had come to inquire, saw this state of affairs from afar and flew over. They crawled into the room immediately, joined their hands, and chanted the counting song: 'One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, a hundred, a thousand.' As they chanted, he let out a groan, raised his voice in pain, sat up with a grimace, looked around in all directions, and remained dumbfounded with an 'Afun.' Chiruna-hime, upon seeing the two, stood up in anger.

She turned the spearhead of her jealous rage toward the two tsukasa who had saved her husband from his crisis, crying, 'Who are you fellows who intrude into the middle of a marital quarrel without permission and act so presumptuously? Leave this place at once, for I cannot tolerate you even for a moment!' Chiruna-hime, her heart disturbed, saw everyone she looked at as her enemy, which was truly inevitable. Chiruteru and Kanna, upon seeing the figures of Mitsuhiko and his companion, joined their hands and said, 'Oh, how well you have helped us in this time of crisis! Please, first of all, take a rest in the back room.' While saying this, the foul-hearted Chiruteru signaled to Kanna with his eyes, indicating with a look that they should capture these two and lock them in the storehouse.

Mitsuhiko and Itahiko perceived their hearts, but thinking that they must find the whereabouts of Debisu-hime, they feigned ignorance and followed behind Kanna, walking slowly into the dark garden, watching for an opening. Mitsuhiko stopped suddenly, grabbed Kanna's arm firmly, and said, 'You are a servant of this house, a lieutenant of the Brahman religion, a fellow called Kanna, are you not? I have already realized that the hiding of Debisu-hime was entirely by the instruction of the master of this house, Chiruteru. Tell me the whereabouts in detail, and very quickly.'

'If you fail to comply, I shall take your life, which is as fragile as a jewel-string. What is your answer?' he pressed. Even Kanna was at a loss, trembling all over, and with tears in his eyes, he joined his hands and said, 'Yes, yes, I will confess! Please, just spare my life.' He led the two to the front of the first storehouse, pushed open the door, and while untying the tight ropes of Debisu-hime who was inside, he said politely to the two, 'Excuse me, excuse me, messengers of the propaganda. The lady who is here must be the Debisu-hime you were searching for. I was away from the house, and for that reason, I do not know the circumstances. Please, first of all, examine her.'

When he said this, Mitsuhiko and Itahiko kept watch in all directions, and leaving Itahiko to wait outside, Mitsuhiko entered the storehouse alone. When he lit a light and looked, she was gagged with a bit in her mouth, her hands and feet were bound, and she was lying on the dirt floor in a most miserable state. Mitsuhiko was enraged at the sight, and while untying the princess's bonds, he immediately bound Kanna, threw him down on the spot, and while tending to Debisu-hime, he finally rescued her from the storehouse. Here, Mitsuhiko slammed the door of the storehouse shut, locked it, and while comforting and consoling the princess, they slipped away into the darkness.

He leaves this place behind.

When Heiru finishes his night Jiyunshi and returns to the mansion, Chiruna-hime is sitting with her hair disheveled and her Ketsusau changed. Chiruteru is sitting with a Matsusao face, holding his Kintama and groaning in pain. Heiru approaches them briskly.

Heiru: 'Oh, Captain Dannasama, Okusama, what is this unusual Goyōsu? Who has committed a Shifurai?'

Chiruna: 'You, Heiru, you have come at a good time. Chiruteru-sama is truly a person of Dokushiyō. He casts me aside, brings in the Amatsuchiyo who resides in the back Hanare and many other women, and intends to lead a life of Katsute Kimama. If such things were to reach the capital of Haruna, it would be the end of Onmi. I have already made up my mind. Instead of being Ohida from this house, I shall return to the capital of Haruna, tell the Ichibushijifu, and awaken the eyes of my Shiyujin. Heiru-dono, I leave the rest to you. I shall take my leave now.'

Heiru: 'Moshi-moshi, please wait a Chiyotsuto. It is because you are too close that such a Kenkuwa begins. Dannasama always praises you, saying you are a great wife, a beautiful person, and a kind person.'

Chiruna: 'Eh, you are in league with Dannasama and intend to cast me out, are you not? I have heard everything from Kanna. I am not a Chiruna who would eat such temporary flattery. Farewell, Dannasama, enjoy yourself with your harlots.'

She says this and tries to fly out with a changed Ketsusau. Chiruteru, who had wanted her to fly out, decides that it would be a disaster if such things were reported to the capital of Haruna, and that it would be best to lock her in the warehouse forever. Enduring the pain, he says:

Chiruteru: 'Hey, Heiru, my wife has gone mad and tried to murder me by grabbing my Kintama. Come, tie her up quickly and throw her into the number one warehouse. This is not a request from Chiruteru, it is an order from the Captain.'

Heiru: 'Ha.'

He straightens his posture, moves behind Chiruna-hime in an instant, and says:

Heiru: 'As a criminal of attempted murder, I arrest you by the order of the guard of the Brahman army and the Captain. Submit yourself to the rope.'

He says this, twists Chiruna-hime's slender arms behind her back with a jerk, and binds her with a rope.

Heiru: 'Walk quickly.'

He says this while dragging her toward the first warehouse.

Heiru: 'Haha, this is the warehouse where a person called Devisu-hime is thrown in. If I throw two women together, the flowers of jealousy will surely bloom. Yes, an interesting Kenkuwa will surely begin.'

He mutters this, opens the door with a rattle, forces her inside, slams the door shut, and locks it. If one throws a small stone into the small hole of this lock, it will not open from the inside no matter how much one struggles. It is designed to open freely from the outside. In short, although it is called a warehouse, it is a confinement room.

The moment Chiruna-hime is pushed in by Heiru, she staggers and falls flat on top of Kanna, who is tied up and lying on the floor. It is pitch black. She cannot tell who it is. However, Heiru just muttered about a Devisu-hime. It must be that woman; this one is also a part of the enemy. Chiruteru the old man must be hiding such a woman and having secret meetings with her. Oh, how regrettable. I want to punish her somehow, but with my hands tied like this, I cannot do anything... she mutters in a low voice and bites into Kanna's thigh. Kanna is startled and cries out:

'Aita, it hurts, it hurts!'

Chiruna: 'Eh, you wicked woman, it hurts a little, shall I bite you more?'

チルナ『ここにデビス姫とかが縛つて投げ込むであるといふことだから、一つ探つてみて仇を討つから、お前さまそれなつと見て、気を慰めなされ。あゝ腹立たしい、何こにすつ込んでゐるのだな。オイ、デビス、声を立てぬか。何程黙つて居つても昼になればチツと明くなるから、所在を見付け、成敗を致すぞや。今素直に此処に居りますと申せば腕の一本位で堪へてやる』

カンナ『モシ、奥さま、其デビスは私と入替に旦那様のお使が出て来て連れ出して了ひました。大方今頃は其女を看護婦代用にしてゐられるでせうよ。アイタヽヽ、あゝ痛い痛い、本当にエライことかぶられて、太腿が三所も四所も赤い口をあけて欠伸をしてゐるやうだ。本当に酷い目に会はしましたなア』

チルナ『ホツホヽヽヽ、常平生から旦那さまを咬かし、初稚などと云ふ女を連れて来たのも、元を糺せばお前ぢやないか。つまり云へば自業自得だよ。マア天罰が当つたと思うて辛抱しなさい。お前は太腿の三片や四片取られたとて夫れ程苦しいのか。妾は大事の大事の夫の身体を全体取られたでないか、あゝ残念やなア、ウンウンウンウン』

倉の隅から猫の様な劫経た大きい鼠が、

『クウクウクウクウ、チウチウチウチウ、ガタガタガタガタ、ゴトゴトゴトゴトゴト』

と厭らしい音を立ててゐる。
