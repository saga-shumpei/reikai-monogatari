Chapter 4: 陰 Messenger [1504]

陰使〔1504〕

§ 1

From the distant age of the gods, in the sacred Seijiyō of Mount Adsumo, the Shinrei of Ōkunihiko, along with Bonten, Taishiyaku, and Jizaiten, were enshrined and served with reverence and awe morning and night in the forest of the Tennō. In the village of Suma in the land of Idzumi, the master of the mansion, Bāchiru, who had been chosen for the duties of the village head, set out with Anchī to fish in the Unabara. When the villagers, wondering what had become of them, gazed upon the surface of the Shiranami, they were overcome with tears of grief and performed the funeral rites, resigning themselves to the loss. On a summer evening, three years later, without a trace of sorrow, they saw the shadows of the master and his servant returning with smiles upon their faces.

As if flowers had bloomed on a withered tree, the Rauniyakunanniyo gathered around, crying, 'Medeta, medeta, omedetai! The Ama no Ihato has opened!' They opened the storehouses of the Nijitsutomahe, brought out the Hanniyatō that had been stored away, and carried it out to their gardens. Like Gaki parched with thirst soaking in a river, they drank gulp after gulp, losing their senses and becoming mad with intoxication. To this place, Chiruteru, the barrier guard of Kiyo of the Baramon, arrived with a troop of soldiers, having heard that the propagandists of the San-iku-kyō were hiding deep within this house. Whipping his horse to investigate the truth of the matter, he arrived with great dignity, slipped through the front gate, and reached the vast garden that stretched beyond sight.

When he cast his eyes about, he saw the Satobito filling the space, moving about happily, exchanging cups of sake, and singing songs. Seeing them in such a state of madness, the worm in his throat could not remain silent. At the scent of the ladle thrust toward him, he lost his composure, leaped nimbly from his horse into the garden, and together with his many followers, began to click his tongue and devour the drink. It was truly a wretched sight of base gluttony.

Chiruteru was a troublesome fellow who had no sense of shame when it came to sake and women. Having had the Hanniyatō thrust toward his nose by the long-handled ladles of Teku, Akisu, and Āru, he instantly forgot his mission as if it were nothing, and began to drink gulp after gulp, mingling with the crowd with foul language. Teku, lost in the excitement, began to loll his tongue and stagger about here and there.

Teku: 'Well, well, Captain Chiruteru, how kind of you to come. Today, our new master Bāchiru has returned home after a long absence, and we are holding a celebration. It would be proper to send an invitation to the office first, but as you are a man of integrity and purity, your status is different, and you are not like a spy such as this Teku; thus, I hesitated to offer an invitation. But that you, Captain, have come yourself—what an honor this is! Come, the fountain of sake gushes forth endlessly. Please, drink to your heart's content and join us in our unrestrained revelry. This floor is in the open air, but as we have pounded it firm from the very depths of the abyss, there is no need to worry about the floor collapsing. Please, enjoy yourself without reserve. On behalf of my master Bāchiru, I, the new head clerk Teku, offer you my humble greetings.'

So saying, he carelessly pushed his nose to the left with his right palm and wiped the sweat from his face with his elbow.

Chiruteru: 'Are you not Teku? How impatient you are; how can you become a head clerk on your own authority without obtaining permission to resign? Do you not fear the power of the Baramon? You are a scoundrel.'

Teku: 'Oh, Captain, if you make such a difficult face at a sake party, the delicious sake will turn bitter. Well, I shall hear your reprimands another time. Who could look at this sake and not laugh? Come, let me pour you a cup. If it were the sake poured by that Naitsu whom we have kept in the back room, the sake would surely be even sweeter, but that is unfortunately not possible, heh heh heh.'

Chiruteru: 'Could you somehow, without Chiruna knowing, secretly lure that woman here for me? I will give you any reward you desire.'

Teku: 'Heh, I understand. In return for the reward, I do not need money, and since I can have plenty of sake here as I please, I would like to receive something other than that.'

Chiruteru: 'What is it that you wish to receive?'

Teku: 'Heh, I wish to receive a kan.'

Chiruteru: 'If it is a kan, can I not provide that immediately? It is not amusing to be drinking only cold hiyazake all the time, after all.'

Teku: 'Oh my, your kan is poor; when I say kan, I mean kan. It is different from the kan for sake. I mean the kan, the official who catches the jinmin and throws his weight around.'

Chiruteru: 'Naruhodo, if that is the case, upon your seikō, I shall make you a metsukegashira.'

Teku: 'Metsusau, I would like to be excused from such a lowly shiyokushiyō. I would like you to promote me to lieutenant at the very least.'

Chiruteru: 'If you can skillfully drag that naisu here to this place without Chiruna knowing, I shall make you a lieutenant. Do try to exert yourself.'

Teku: 'Metsusau, these days nothing is done without sakigin or tetsuke, so let us agree that if I fail, I will return it; in any case, please appoint me as lieutenant. If you do not, that fellow Kanna will throw his weight around calling himself a lieutenant, and the moment I sneak in through the uraguchi, if I am given an itsukatsu by that fellow Kanna, I will be finished. If I am a lieutenant, we will be dōyaku, you see.'

Chiruteru: 'Ah, it cannot be helped; I shall make you a military police lieutenant. However, if you speak too much like a kenpei, I will strip you of your rank, so keep that in mind.'

Teku: 'Ya, thank you. From now on, with Teku's technique, I shall drag her here skillfully; please wait for a while. And if you see Teku's skill, it will be an honor. ...Ah, how busy I am. Having suddenly risen in status to the head clerk of the Bachiru house and a lieutenant, my duties have suddenly become complicated. Perhaps it would be better to be a commoner without office after all.'

Chiruteru: 'If you prefer to be a commoner without office, I will retract my words just now.'

Teku: 'Metsusau, once a word has left the mouth of a warrior, it is improper to retract it; that is not permitted. Is not the word of a superior heavier than gold or stone? If you were to say things that change like morning orders and evening revisions, your credibility would instantly fall to the ground.'

Chiruteru: 'Eh, it cannot be helped. In that case, as a Brahmin lieutenant, be very careful, and without letting my wife Chiruna-hime know, take advantage of the sunset to skillfully use your eloquence to bring the naisu here. It would be delightful to have her pour drinks and make all those fellows say "Ah," and show off my skills to all of them to my heart's content. Now, go quickly, go.'

Teku: 'Ehe-he-he, I am not a Teku who is careless about such things. Now, I shall go in a dash. Please enjoy your sake for a while... Ya, kore waisa no, shitekoi na.'

With such strange gestures, he pulls his hood tight and slips through the crowd, staggering as he makes his way toward the separate room of Chiruteru's garrison.

The sky is wrapped in black clouds, and from two or three rifts in the clouds, faint stars are twinkling. In the darkness of May, he sneaks along, using the light from the shōji as a guide, and when he peers in, three silhouettes are reflected. One is a woman, and the other two appear to be men. Teku mutters to himself, 'Haha, thinking from those silhouettes, it appears to be Kanna and Heeru. They are cunning fellows. They are taking advantage of the captain-sama's absence and are hatching a plot to seize her, attacking her. But those fellows are not very clever. Where is there a man who takes companions along when he goes to woo a single woman? However, the trouble is that if two such fellows are clinging to her side, I cannot fulfill my essential mission. I shall cast aside my fear here and threaten them as a military police lieutenant. Then, with a single great shout of "I have found the unrighteous..." I will make them scatter and flee in pieces, ehe-he-he. When it comes to this, being a lieutenant is a grateful thing,' and while muttering to himself, he deliberately brushes against the outside of the window with loud footsteps.

初稚『何卒お二人様、御悠なさいませ。何も御心配は要りませぬ。……コレコレ　テク様とやら、そこでは蚊がたべます。何卒お這入りなさいませ』

テク『イヤア、お出たな、矢張り、リュウチナントといふ声を聞いて、幾分か心が動いたとみえるワイ、イヒヽヽヽ』

と幽かに笑ひ乍ら、『オホン』と一つ咳払、表戸をソツとあけ、故意とすました面をして、直立不動の態度を示し、

テク『これはこれは、古今無双のナイス様、私は新中尉で厶います。一寸キャプテン様の命令に仍つて、貴方に折入つての御願がありますので、使者に罷り越しました。何卒此等両人を少時遠ざけて戴きたう厶います』

初稚『お二人様、何だか御用があるさうで厶いますから、失礼で厶いますが、一寸少時席をお外し下さいませぬか』

カンナ『ヘー、長いことですか、……長ければ永いで、此方にも事務上の都合が厶いますから、実の所は忙がしい中を繰合せて、奥様の御命令……ウン否々、奥様の目を忍んで一寸御機嫌伺ひに参つたので厶いますからなア』

初稚『奥様は御機嫌が宜しう厶いますかな、何うしたものか、私が御面会を申込んでもお忙しいと見えて、まだ会つて頂けませぬ』

ヘール『そらさうでせう。何と云つても、悋けて仕方がないのですからなア、ヘツヘヽヽヽ。オイ、カンナ、姫様の請求に仍つて、暫時離席することにせうかい』

とスゴスゴと立出で暗に忍んで二人の話を聞いてゐる。
