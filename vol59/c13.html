Chapter 13: 案知 [1513]

案知〔1513〕

§ 1

To explore the mansion of the Sekishiyo of Kiyo, Teku walked along, taking leave of Tamakuniwake, and feeling like a member of the Sekikōtai, he went to the Chiruteru mansion; however, when he arrived, the Kanjinkaname Jimushitsu was devoid of even a single cat, and only the sound of mice in the Tenjiyō squeaking and chirping was heard quite noisily.

Without Enriyo or Eshiyaku, the Araotoko snuck into the Uraniha, and as he headed toward the Ima with Hatsuwakahime as his Meate, he saw the Riyōnin, Chiruteru and Hēru, having cast off their clothes and standing Matsupadaka, flexing their muscles and straining themselves, in the very Matsusaichiu of a Kohakugekijiyō like the Niō guarding the Sanmon; thinking, 'Oh, how Omoshiro, how Omoshiro,' and though he had no Uchiwa, he thought he would act as the Giyōji now, so he stepped forward with courage.

When he approached, Hatsuwakahime called out, 'Anata is Teku-sama, the Lieutenant; I shall act as the Giyōji. Anata, too, take off your clothes here, and in this Chikarakura, to the one who gains the final Shiyōri, I shall pour my love. I intend to take a man of great strength as my husband,' and moving her lips like plum blossoms most adorably, she declared these words coolly; upon hearing this, Teku danced with joy, forgetting that he had come as a scout for Tamakuniwake, and becoming lost in a trance, he took off his clothes, waiting and saying, 'Come on, let us have a match,' but just then, Chiruteru threw Hēru down brilliantly, and the moment he saw him thrown into the terrifying pit, he clapped his hands saying, 'All right, let us have a match,' and with the Shishi-funjin spirit of a wild boar, he lunged and bit into him.

Their sweat was slick, and they were like an eel and a catfish grappling and struggling; they joined and separated, and separated and joined again, moving with a thudding sound, denting the small pebbles of the garden, and as they fought as if this were their last stand, Hatsuwakahime raised her hands, laughing 'Ohohohoho,' and while bathing their heads with a gaze filled with charm, she waited to see how the match would turn out; for the two, Chiruteru and Teku, it was a glorious match before the Empress.

Thinking to make the world's number one handsome man and the world's number one 'nice' person her husband, and to break the hearts of all the men of the world and boast of her success, they challenged each other, staking their lives; the newcomer Teku finally grabbed his opponent's loincloth, and with a 'thud,' he threw him down onto the ground like a falling rock, and with the momentum, he rolled over two or three times, and his sweat-soaked body instantly became a pillar of sand, and having turned into a sand-covered pole, he fell into the deep pit in the blink of an eye; even the redoubtable Teku relaxed, thinking he had won the laurel wreath, and with a heart full of joy, he sat his black buttocks down on the ground with a thud.

While his heart was pounding, he looked around and, to his surprise, Wax, Ex, Hellman, and El—the four of them—were standing there smiling; Hatsuwakahime, looking happy, called out to Teku, 'Lieutenant Teku-sama, Anata is truly a strong man. Come, come, now I shall become your wife as promised,' and while saying, 'Let us shake hands,' she reached out her gentle white hand, and thinking, 'Forget a handshake, I do not care if it is a kiss or anything else,' he stretched out his monkey-like arms, narrowed his eyes, and just as he was about to grasp her white arm,

Hatsuwakahime's peerless 'nice' person, faster than he could take Teku's hand, revealed white fur, and Teku, startled and dumbfounded, looked up at her face, and at that moment, oh, how strange; her eyes turned upward and her mouth split to her ears, and seeing that figure, he was surprised and fell backward with a thud, and as he rolled his eyes, Hatsuwakahime instantly reverted to the form of a white fox, and waving her tail like a broom, she began to walk away slowly; Wax and the other three were surprised and panicked, and all four of them fell into the pit with a thud, and their figures were no longer visible on the ground; Teku was astonished again, and while finally standing up, his legs wobbling and stumbling, he snuck out through the kitchen gate, tucked his tail, and ran off at full speed toward the Tadasu forest where Tamakuniwake was hiding; the sun had gradually set, and although the moon shone in the sky, it was blocked by the thicket of treetops, and he could not see even an inch ahead; the curtain of darkness had fallen, and Masumino-hiko was walking along, wandering through the dark forest; at this time, a man running from the front suddenly hit his chest against Masumino-hiko's chest with a thud, and he fell down with a cry, groaning and screaming; Masumino-hiko was puzzled.

Tamakuniwake beckoned him quickly, took out a flint, and struck a light. When he looked closely, what could this be? The scout leader, that fellow Teku, was lying there with his mouth wide open, looking up at the sky. His posture was like that of an Ikefuna intoxicated by mud. He kept his mouth open, crying 'Appa, appa,' and stared with rolling eyes.

Masumihiko provided various kinds of care, lifted Teku up, and while patting and stroking his back, said:

Masumihiko: 'Hey, Teku, pull yourself together. What is the situation with the enemy? Have you found the whereabouts of Michihiko? Come, report quickly.'

Teku rose with difficulty, placed both hands on the ground, and said:

Teku: 'Heh, it is truly terrible. A grand sumo match, the greatest in the world since the dawn of time, had begun. I also participated in that sumo match and achieved a great victory. And then, I received a wriggling female fox as a reward.'

Masumihiko: 'Hey, Teku, pull yourself together. What about the reconnaissance? Have we not been waiting for this day for so long? What have you been doing? Come, report in detail.'

Teku: 'Oh, even the great Teku was stumped by the exploration of the Bakemonoyashiki. Sumo wrestlers fell into pits, foxes appeared, and a Brahmin wax figure came out to watch; it was a huge sensation, indeed. I had struggled for my life to win a great victory, thinking I had taken the peerless beauty Hatsuwakahime as my wife, but she turned into a fox and crawled away sluggishly. Oh, it was so terrifying, words cannot describe it. My drunkenness and everything else vanished in an instant. Ah, ah, it is so painful. I have never experienced anything so terrifying and painful since I was born. Please, Tamakuniwake-sensei, if you go to such a place, you will surely be cast into a pit. And you will be deceived if you think the fellows there are human. They all turn into foxes. Please do not go to such a dangerous place. Your person is precious, after all...'

Masumihiko: 'Hey Teku, you are not making any sense at all. Can you not report more clearly?'

Teku: 'Heh, heh, it is natural that I do not make sense. For I myself have lost my sense of it. ...Truly, truly, I have encountered a strange and bizarre experience, unique and unparalleled in history.'

Masumihiko: 'Have you found the whereabouts of Michihiko?'

Teku: 'I was so terrified that my eyes were dazzled, and I know nothing of Michihiko or Kawahiko. When it is this dark, I cannot even tell when I bump into a person, so how could I possibly know where Michihiko is? Ah, ah, I have truly, truly had a miserable experience.'

Masumihiko: 'What a hopeless fellow. What was the purpose of your mission? Can you not be a little more reliable?'

Tamakuniwake: 'Hey, Teku, calm yourself a little and tell me slowly. I cannot understand the point at all from your words.'

After resting for a while, Teku advanced from the front of the mansion and reported in detail that when he looked into the backyard, a sumo match was beginning, that he himself had wrestled and won, and that the woman he thought was Hatsuwakahime was actually a large white fox. He then replied with a worried look that he did not know the whereabouts of Michihiko and the other two, but that they had likely fallen into a pit. Tamakuniwake tilted his head for a while, lost in thought.

Masumihiko: 'Please, sensei, what shall we do? We cannot just leave Michihiko and the other two to their fate, but if we carelessly go in this darkness, it will be terrible if we are also thrown into a pit.'

Anchi: 'Please, sensei-sama, you need not worry about such things. I will scout the way to repay your kindness, so please follow in my footsteps. If I happen to fall in, you can simply avoid passing through that spot, and you should be safe. Come, let us go. If we dally, we do not know what will become of Michihiko-sama and his wife, or Itahiko-sama. It is said that there are many soldiers in that mansion, after all.'
