Chapter 25: 歓呼 [1525]

歓呼〔1525〕

§ 1

As the Taiyō sank into the western Namima, the Seikuō of gold and silver began to twinkle in the Taikū. The Amata Kozaru, appearing to feel a sense of Fuan as they gazed out upon the Kaijiyō at night, began to cry out in unison from their three hundred and more mouths with a 'kyatsu-kyatsu-kyatsu' sound. Overwhelmed by this noise, the sound of the Funauta, the Hanashigoe, and the sound of the waves beating like drums against the Funazoko were all engulfed and finished along with the darkness.

Itahiko, showing his spirit and wishing to calm the hearts of the Kozaru, stood at the Gentō and, while waving his hands to the Saiu, danced and sang in an Omoshiro and Oka manner. The Shiyōjiyō, whose eyes shone in the night, saw this figure and, feeling somewhat Anshin, suddenly became Yōkida; they all waved their hands and began to dance joyfully with a 'kyatsu-kyatsu-kyatsu-kyatsu'. The Sendō, in order to control the Dōeu of the ship, kept a firm grip on the oar to maintain its Heikau while hoisting the sail against the Kitakaze, sliding across the Kaimen.

Itahiko: 'The curtain of night has been lowered, and at last the Atari has grown silent. When I gaze upon the heavenly Amatsu-Mikara, the countless large and small star-shadows—gold, silver, agate, lapis lazuli, giant clam, and even diamonds—are not merely there, but each one exerts its utmost to shine and glitter. When I gaze upon the bottom of the waves, countless large and small scaly tribes pass to and fro, before and behind, to the left and to the right. Each time they do, they hide and block the brightly shining star-shadows; how charming it is. Within the ship, the Shiyōjiyō-sama raise voices like those of infants, singing "kyatsu-kyatsu-kyatsu" as they proceed, knowingly or unknowingly, toward the presence of their beloved mother. I am Itahiko, the messenger of propagation.'

Itahiko: 'To save the princess of Davis, I accompanied Sankohiko-sama and, aiming for the mansion of the barrier guard of the port of Kiyo, I slipped into the night, hiding my footsteps. I rescued the princess of Davis, but the moment we fled, we were caught in the trap of the Magatsukami's plotting and fell into the bottom of the abyss. Resigned to my fate and bracing my courage, I entrusted my body to the Kami, seeking to annihilate the agony within my heart. While playing the role of a hotel clerk and lamenting my tragic fate, how strange it was that the Yunker of the Brahmin Haeru-shi and the Captain of the Chiruteru-shi came falling in, stark naked with only loincloths on.'

Itahiko: 'Displaying wit and humor, I guided them to a room in the back, and just as I sat back down at the counter to wait for guests, a great crowd came falling in like an avalanche. While I was distracting myself from my agony by joking about this "thousand guests, ten thousand arrivals" great prosperity, how unexpected it was that the messenger of propagation of the Tamakuniwake of the three-fives, along with Mashumi-no-hiko, Anchi, and the Teku-shi, came sliding in with a "dos-dos-dos". This encounter was beyond my imagination; enemies and allies alike gathered in one place, bowing their heads to hear the story of the noble teachings of the Kamikai, and as we opened our hearts to one another, we worshiped the divine grace of the Sumeru-Ōkami with tears.'

Itahiko: 'As I wondered what the sound of dogs echoing in my eardrums could be, suspecting it was strange, a rescue boat appeared beyond my expectations. The Kami-tsukasa of Hatsuwakahime-hime, leading the fierce dog Smart, opened the iron door at the entrance of the cave of the Shikuno-iwaya and came to us. You saved all of us safely to the noble earth, and then, how mysterious it was that you suddenly hid your noble figure. Returning to the Bachiru mansion with the party of Tamakuniwake, while receiving various pure treatments of sea, river, mountain, and field, we were worshiping the divine grace with joy and pleasure when the princess of the Shiyōjiyō appeared, possessed by the spirit of the princess of the Saber-no-mikoto.'

Itahiko: 'She begged sorrowfully, looking pitiful, that we should welcome the kin left on the island to this mansion as quickly as possible and save them. Having obtained the permission of Tamakuniwake, we gathered twenty ships, and with preparations fully complete, we arrived safely at Shiyōjiyō-jima, following the command of the Mikoto, and having fully accomplished our mission, we have finally returned here. It is already only fourteen or fifteen ri to the lake route, and when the Toyosaka of the morning sun rises, we shall arrive at Suma at sunrise. When I think of it, when I think of it, how brave it is. Before the presence of the hundred great Ōkami of heaven and earth and my master, I humbly offer my gratitude. Even if the morning sun shines or clouds over, even if the great earth sinks, I shall save the Yakkosu of the Brahmin religion, and the three, Hāru and Sabōru, without resentment or hatred, to the very end, according to the will of the Kami. Be at peace, all three of you. How could the divine path of truth, goodness, beauty, and love harm anyone? Take courage, take courage, all of you take courage. Even the Shiyōjiyō are dancing with joy just like that. If we, who possess human bodies, do not celebrate this auspicious sign, it would be inexcusable before the Kami. Take courage, take courage, all together. I, Itahiko-tsukasa, with all my heart, swear to the Kami and preach that I shall save you. Ah, Kannagara, Kamigami, may your spirits be blessed.'

太陽は漸く西の浪間に沈むと共に、大空には金銀の星光瞬き初めた。数多の小猿は、夜の海上を眺めて稍不安の念を起したと見え、三百有余匹の口からキヤツキヤツキヤツと一斉に叫び出した。此声に圧せられて、欵乃の声も話声も船底を打つ浪の鼓の音も、闇と共に包まれて仕舞つた。伊太彦は勢を見せ、小猿等の心を安むぜむと舷頭に立ち、手を左右にふり乍ら面白可笑しく、歌ひ踊つて見せた。夜目の光る猩々は此姿を見て稍安心しながら、俄に陽気立ち、いづれも手を振り、嬉しげにキヤツキヤツキヤツキヤツと踊り出した。船頭は船の動揺を制すべく、頻りに櫓を握つて其平衡を保ちつつ北風に帆を揚げて海面を辷り行く。

§ 2

No sooner had they finished singing than the eastern sky was tinged with madder, and from within the waves, the Taiyō of Kinpukurin peered forth, casting forth its Senrei Kuōki. Looking toward the Zenpau, one could see hundreds and thousands of Rauniyakunanniyo on the beach of Suma, sounding gongs and Taiko, raising shouts of victory, recognizing the Funakage, and stirring in a great clamor. Upon seeing this Kuōkei, the Riyōnin, Yakkosu and Sabōru, were suddenly seized with Ojike, and leaping up, they plunged into the Kaichiu and hid their forms. The Gunshiu standing on the Isobe, seeing the Jiyusui of the Futari, waved their hands crying 'Are yo, are yo,' and began to clamor at the top of their voices. Just then, as if shot from a bow, an Itsusō of a Kobune appeared, and headed straight for the Hajiyō where the two had plunged, keeping them in Mega. This was the boat steered by Masumihiko and Michihiko.

Receiving the blessings of Jitsugetsu, this Monogatari is explained in Matsubusa. The Dekigoto of the ancient Kamiyo of Isonokami are now explained anew.

原文（近代文語）

かく歌ひ終るや否や、東の空は茜さし、浪の中より金覆輪の太陽は、鮮麗なる光輝を放つて覗き玉ふた。前方を見ればスマの浜に数百千の老若男女が鉦や太鼓を鳴らし、鬨の声を造りて、船影を認め、どよめき渡つて居る。此光景を見るよりヤッコス、サボールの両人は俄に怖気づき、身を躍らして海中に飛び込み姿を隠した。磯辺に立つた群衆は二人の入水を見て、アレヨ　アレヨと手を振り、声を限りにぞよめき出した。かかる所へ矢を射る如く、一艘の小舟現はれ来り、二人の飛び込むだ波上を目蒐け進み行く。これは真純彦、三千彦の操る船であつた。
