§ 1

Across the spacious garden, Hatsuwakahime sat with a calm heart, holding an ichigenkin and singing in a thin, beautiful voice, while maintaining an attitude of complete indifference toward the debauchery of the Kiyo barrier guard, Captain Chiruteru, and his wife, Princess Chiruna.

To such a place, Heeru appeared with a brisk step, standing in a flustered manner, swaying his body back and forth and side to side, and stamping his feet.

Heeru: 'Excuse me, Princess, are you really so cheerful? Can you not hear that debauchery going on?'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Yes, has some trouble occurred? I was captivated by the ichigenkin and indulging in dreams of peace, so I know nothing. It seems as though the husband and wife at the mansion have been doing a bit of dancing over their wine. Have they gone to rest yet?'

Heeru: 'Eh, Princess, how can you be so carefree? A terrible thing has happened. An acute Chiruteru-hernia has broken out, and it is a great uproar with doctors and medicine.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Ah, is that so? If you gave them some mankintan, perhaps they would feel better.'

Heeru: 'The essential mankintan was snatched away by the great general Chiruteru, Princess Chiruna, so he immediately rolled his eyes, and with a piri-piri-piri, kyaa, uun, dotan-batan, gacha-gacha-gacha, gara-gara, an artificial earthquake broke out. Everyone here is a coward; they all seem to have evacuated to a safe zone, and I, Heeru alone, am serving as the nursing staff, running about in all directions. I would like you to help nurse the patient. Rather than being nursed by the rough hands of a man, it might be much more comforting for the patient to be nursed by the gentle, soft hands of a woman. Come, I beg of you. Let us provide care quickly. Since you have come to a house of strangers and are being treated so courteously, you should at least understand a little bit of duty and human feeling.'
