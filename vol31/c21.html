Chapter 21: 白毫の Light [887]

白毫の光〔887〕

§ 1

The two carriages of fire stopped in front of the old woman's hut, and from within them, blue-and-red drivers and technicians, with cockscomb-like, affected jaws wagging, thrusting out gold-and-silver horns abruptly, stepped down from the carriages and strode forward to stand right in front of the two men,

Akiyamawake: 'You must be Akiyamawake and Morris, both of you. Come, this is a welcome from Meifu. If you dally, time will run out. Get into this carriage of fire quickly.'

roaring with wide-opened giant eyes and glaring fiercely. Akiyamawake turned desperate,

Akiyamawake: 'Oh, I am a man too. What is there to fear about a carriage of fire? In Shiyaba, we are the heroes of a burning house who have driven carriages of fire day after day, with fires descending upon our houses due to daily financial distress and fires attached to our bottoms. I have not the slightest reluctance to board, but come now, listen well. What kind of face is yours? Sporting an insolent mug like a copper-thatched roof by the seashore, riding in such a red carriage, what are you trembling and turning pale for? Why don't you pull yourself together firmly? Hey, you single fellow, what kind of face is your mug? You have a bright red face like Pindola, wandering out in front of a Buddhist image, smeared with the eye-mucus and nasal mucus of old hags, with the bridge of your nose and everything scraped off. What is the meaning of this? You must have been drinking sake in broad daylight and getting drunk. Can official duties be discharged with such ways? Seeing my face, trembling violently and turning pale, or drivers and conductors boarding with faces turned bright red from gorging on sake like Benkei—riding in a carriage of fire with such fellows is far too dangerous to undertake. Come, go back and start over. I will leisurely board tomorrow, wah.'

Morris, looking timid,

Morris: 'Hey, Aki-kō, don't say such outrageous things. Since it has come to this, there is no help for it. It is to your advantage to behave yourself. ... Hello, Blue-sama, Red-sama, please forgive Akiyamawake's impoliteness just now.'

Akiyamawake: 'Well, it's not that I won't forgive it, but this is the eighth block of Hell. You must have been saying this ever since you were in Shiyaba. That is, the saying that even the affairs of Hell depend on...'

Akiyamawake: 'Ha ha ha ha, are you spitting out that it ultimately depends on money, after all? That must be so. When this Aki-sama was in Shiyaba and boarded a train, if you paid twice as much as an ordinary person, they would let you board a very convenient second-class compartment. If you paid three times as much, they would let you board an even better first-class compartment holding a white ticket. Even the tickets had three distinct grades: blue, red, and white. Your face is a blue ticket, isn't it? Very well, are you a red ticket? If so, unless I start by determining things from the red ticket side, there is no standard to give to Blue-sama, wah. How much per mile is this carriage of fire?'

Akiyamawake: 'The fare for the carriage of fire is not something to be charged. This is a special, high-class carriage of fire dispatched from Meifu. And while we receive a considerable allowance, as for that, it is just as I said a moment before.'

Akiyamawake: 'Yosh, understood. I am not an Aki-sama who lacks wit for such matters. I do not know what it is called here, but in Shiyaba it is called a bribe under the sleeve, isn't it? With Western clothes like yours, there are no sleeves either; where on earth am I supposed to put it? Don't you see I have no idea?'

Akiyamawake: 'Even if there are no sleeves, pockets are prepared all over Western clothes, wah. Depending on whether those pockets are heavy or light, whether I am led to the bottom of the scorching hell or sent to the entrance of the much more comfortable Shinkai, well, that remains to be seen. Because it depends on...'

Akiyamawake: 'If that is the case, since I will do mine together with Mori-kō's, make sure both of us are saved to the same place. In connection with that, since there are four of us for two carriages of fire, even if we give a hundred ryō each, that makes four hundred ryō. We must surely hand over hush money to this old Ba-asama. That would make five hundred ryō, which pinches the pocketbook a little bit, but—ah, there's no help for it. Resolving ourselves—ah, five hundred ryō for the two of us, you had better examine it well and accept it.'

'Koriya, koriya, you are a fellow who does outrageous things! An audacious fellow trying to bribe me with wairo. It is easy to accept this, but I too would be questioned on the charge of shiuwai, and as for you, your crime would grow heavier and heavier as zōwaizai, so you had better keep it in mind from now on. However, I will forget it for today only...'

'Even if you do not tell me to be cautious from now on, once you have brought out this much, I am muitsubutsu. If that is the case, it is best to forget it completely; we are mutually ketsukō in the backside.'

'Yoshiyoshi, I shall forget it. Come, get on quickly. It is a little hot. In exchange, I will leave the windows wide open for you.'

Aki and Mori turned toward Datsupibaba,

'Obāsa-sama, thank you very much for your care. Thanks to you, we are traveling to Tengoku. Sayōnara...'

saying thus, they handed over a hiyakuriyō each to the five of them, boarded two fire-chariots split apart, made a booming sound while releasing smelly farts, stirred up sunakeburi in mōmō clouds, and galloped away straight toward the north.

Before they knew it, the fire-chariots sped away furiously, and pulled up right in front of the gate of the red scorching hell enclosed high by iron walls.

'Come, this is the scorching hell. Hey, Ao, Aka, and Shiro, quickly drag these two down and throw them inside the gate. It is my command.'

'Moshimoshi Ao-sama, is that not different from our promise? Have you forgotten the saying that even hell's judgments depend on money?'

'It is a settled matter. Did you not say just a moment ago that you wanted me to forget it for today only? Since I have forgotten everything, it is fine if I just pack you in according to the rules.'

'That must be wrong. If you say such things, when we meet Enma-sama, I will tell him everything from beginning to end. If I do so, your head will instantly fly off, and you will be dropped into the scorching hell just like us.'

'Ha-ha-ha-ha-ha, what a foolishly honest fellow you are. Since ogres have their own cliques, how could anyone touch you with a single finger from the outside? Do not speak of foolish things. Mountain hunters catch bears and deer, sea fishermen catch fish, cats catch rats, monkeys catch fleas, and our trade is catching wandering spirits. Whether it be good or bad, we have no such concerns. No matter what, if we drag in even one extra person, our income gets better, so you had better give up moderately without complaining. If you are going to tell Enma-sama, go ahead and try; he is a fox of the same hole as us. It is certain that you fellows will receive a severe scolding.'

'My, what a sacred place that praises good and punishes evil, Morisu had thought it was, but if things are like this at all, the world is in darkness. Heaven and earth are dark and dim, unable to distinguish an inch before one's eyes, is it not a world of the Heavenly Rock Cave's concealment?'

'It is a settled matter. That is why this place is called the netherworld. Because you make a fuss about some helpless Misoshigi and the like, light up around there, talk about sincerity, and make old morals strut about, infringing upon the domain of us officials... evil ogres, we were just waiting with bated breath to drag you in the moment there was the slightest hook. How wonderfully you have walked right into the trap. What a fool you are. Come, quickly stand up straight and stiff!'

With that, the blue, red, and white ogres seized the two and tried to force them into the iron gate. Thinking it would be a major crisis if they were pushed inside, right at the moment they desperately chanted 'Ameno-Mizuho-No-Kagurau-Mae', suddenly a group of firelight illuminated the surroundings from ahead, and like an arrow shot forth, they appeared at this place and exploded with a loud roar. Where on earth the fire-chariots and the four ogres had vanished, not a shadow nor a shape remained.

Akiyamawake: 'Hey, are you Akiyamawake? Are you Morris? It is still too early for you to come here. You have a remaining lifespan in which you must work in the Genkai. Pull yourself together!'

With these words, he clenched his fist and struck them on the back two or three times with such force that they groaned. The two were startled, returned to their senses, and as they looked around, to their surprise, they found themselves rescued and being cared for by three men on the banks of the Season River. These three were the party of Kuniyoriwakenomikoto, Yasuhiko, and Munehiko.

Akiyamawake: 'Oh, this is, this is the Sendenshi-sama of Kuniyoriwake. To think that you would not abandon a wicked man like me, but instead save me. I am truly grateful. I was on the verge of being cast into the Shōnetsu Jigoku.'

Morris: 'I have truly committed nothing but Goburei. To think that you would save a wicked man like me.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'There is no need for you to offer Orei. The Ōkamisama has commanded me to perform this Goyō. Please offer your deepest thanks to the Ōkamisama.'

The two said, 'Yes, thank you,' and sat properly on the riverbank, clapped their hands, recited the Amatsu Norito, and gave thanks for the divine grace.

From this time on, the two, Akiyamawake and Morris, repented from the bottom of their hearts, revered Kuniyoriwake as if he were a god, and became his disciples anew. They proceeded with high spirits, singing Sendenshi songs, to conquer the evil spirits in the great forests on both banks of the Amazon River, crossing the great plains of Haru no Kuni and traversing high mountains.
