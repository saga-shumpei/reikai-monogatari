Kanekari: 'In that case, won't you take this Kanekari along with you as well? If it is something to be had for free, I do not mind even a lizard or a frog, and if you will guide me for nothing, I wouldn't care even if it were a mountain of needles or a pool of blood hell. Anyway, I am of a disposition that loves to receive things. I am a ghost who hates sticking out his tongue or giving anything out when it comes to parting with things. Come, let us go quickly.'

Morisu: 'This is a lie. Why on earth would I make a companion of someone like you? Kurenaihime has plunged into the Season River and is waiting for me on the road to Meido, so if I were to take along someone like you, that truly would lower Morisu's handsome face.'

Kanekari: 'You are using a double tongue even now that you have come to Meido. You are a thoroughgoing arch-villain. Very well, this Kanekari-sama will now pull out that double tongue of yours.'

No sooner had he spoken than he flipped over a well-sized stone at the riverbank, and from beneath it, countless nail-pullers appeared in a jangling heap. The Kanekari ghost seized them immediately and rushed upon Morisu with such fierce momentum that even the stalwart Morisu could not withstand it; instantly he plunged with a splash into the raging current,

Morisu: 'Akiyamawake, come quickly!'

so crying, he thrust out his arms with a powerful stroke and barely made his way across the torrent to the opposite shore, where he stripped off his drenched kimono and began to wring it out with all his might. Akiyamawake also managed to swim ashore and likewise wrung out his garments; then the two of them jutted out their chins and clenched their fists at the two ghosts on the far side of the river, punching at the empty air and mocking them to the utmost, feeling as though they were being pulled by someone with all their might, and ran on toward the north, ever northward.

An unspeakably unpleasant, blood-smelling wind came blowing. They began to feel a heat as though being boiled in oil. The two became utterly exhausted, and looking around with rolling eyes to rest if there should be any shady tree, they saw a single tree of indescribable form standing forlornly with withered leaves. When they approached it hoping at least for this shade, nasty caterpillars of various kinds were crawling all over it in swarms. Overcoming their horror, the two set off once again upon the scorching-hot ground.

A short distance ahead, a small house thatched with pampas grass stood all alone, a rare sight indeed. Thinking this a stroke of luck, they drew near and peered cautiously through a gap in the door woven of grass to look inside. There sat a single old person, indistinguishable as either grandfather or grandmother, letting a watery snot dangle down with a drip-drip while making straw sandals incessantly.

Akiyamawake: 'Excuse me, are you honorable grandfather or honorable grandmother, whichever it may be I know not, but we are travelers. It is so terribly hot that we can no longer bear it. Please let us rest awhile at your cool dwelling.'

From inside the hut came a withered voice:

Dappinbaba: 'This is the eighth ward of the Burning Hell. How well you have gone astray and wandered in. I received an order from the Great King Emma stating that the two of you were coming here, and telling me to lie in wait here, so I have been waiting since two or three days ago. You have come to the right place. Come, enter at your leisure and take your rest. Before long, the red ogres and black ogres will come bearing a chariot of fire to welcome you two, so it is best that you wait in cheerful anticipation. It must be quite amusing to ride upon a chariot of fire for once.'

Akiyamawake: 'Excuse me, isn't that just a bit troublesome? Why on earth must we do something so bad that we deserve to ride such a chariot of fire? Surely that must be some kind of mistaken identity, isn't it?'

Dappinbaba: 'I am one called Dappinbaba of the Scorched Plain. At the Sanzu River there is one called Datsueba who strips off people's garments, but those who pass through there are folks of light sins. Those who travel through this Burning Hell are the ones among whom the wickedest sinners emerge. Therefore, I shall strip off your flesh and skin entirely, stuff them as specimens, and display them in the museum of the Land of Yomi, while the physical body stripped of its skin will be placed upon a chariot of fire and sent off to Emma's court, where the ogres will gladly salt-roast it and eat it all up, so there is no need for you to worry. It is no use worrying now that it has come to this. It is a destiny settled clear and plain...'

§ 3

Grandma, is that really true? It doesn't quite make sense to me, though.

There is good reason it doesn't make sense. Since you've been doing nothing but things that don't make sense, it's no wonder, but now you have to come to terms with the fact that it is a matter of fate. The wheel of fire coming to fetch you is a strange derail-and-overturn sort of vehicle called the pride-wheel, which is dangerous, but an oni-woman with a red face like Kurenai and glaring eyes, along with another slightly older woman-oni named Erina, have two wheels of fire and the arrangements are all properly made to come and welcome you, so while there is still time, you should at least sing some song comfortably. Once the wheel of fire arrives, your body will be like Fudō-sama, with the passionate fires of love blazing up, and you'll have to suffer a hot time, you see. Ah, when I think of it, when I think of it, how pitiless it is.

The wheel of fire is not in hell particularly, Self-made, oneself rides and goes therein.

So it is said, and since it is a complete and flawless wheel of fire created by yourself, you need no hesitation toward anyone. Go right ahead and board it. In this world, everything is retribution for one's own acts. Good causes bring good fruits, evil causes bring evil fruits, as they say seeds not sown do not sprout, and the seeds you yourself sowed grow, flowers bloom and fruit forms, and you must harvest them yourself—such is the law of heaven, earth, and nature, you see.

Hey, Akiyamawake did feel romantic attachment toward women, but ever since being born, I have never violated a single woman. Why on earth am I to be punished with such a heavy crime? If such minor offenses as this were made into such a noisy dispute and punished, the prisons of hell would be unbearable.

Light sins are all overlooked, making them strip their garments at the Sanzu River, and then like newborn infants, stark naked, sending them back to the spiritual homeland, but a sinner like you cannot be sent back no matter what. And no matter how splendid the judging oni may be, some among them are blind, so even though I hear your crime and think of it as merely light, the verdict has been rendered to put you on the wheel of fire and drop you into the scorching hell, so there is nothing this old woman's power can do about it. Even Enma-sama doesn't examine things directly, so there must be omissions, and pitiful humans arriving on false charges also seem to happen frequently. No matter how brilliantly the rules of the underworld are completed, if the judging oni who operate them are blind, it's no use. Well, there's nothing for it but to resign yourself. Starting from the general at the top, they are nothing but blind ghosts, which is troublesome. This old woman also expresses my utmost sympathy for you, but since I am pressed from above, I can do nothing about it. If I were to make even a single excuse for you, that would be truly terrible. Being a low-ranking official, they would say, 'What are you babbling on about regarding the judgment of a superior?' and have you dismissed on the spot. If that happens, you would end up skin and bones like the selfish ghosts you saw in the river of greed you just crossed. Ah, how miserable is a world of darkness!

Thus saying, Obā-sama sniffled and began to cry softly.

Just as this was happening, with a rattling and uproarious sound, red oni and blue oni with stern faces, carrying iron clubs shaped like long confetti, dragged two wheels of fire and came dashing fiercely toward this place. The two cried out in shock and fell prostrate on the spot.

『お婆アさま、そりや本当ですかい。チツとモリスには合点が往きませぬがなア』

『合点が往かぬ筈だよ。合点の往かぬ事計りやつて来たのだから、無理はなけね共、もういい加減に因縁づくぢやと合点をせなきやならなくなつて来たよ。お前を迎へに来る火の車は自惚車といふ妙な脱線し転覆する車で危ないものだが、紅井の様な赤い顔をして、目を剥いた女の鬼が一人、又少し年増のエリナと云ふ女鬼が一人、火の車を二つ持つて、お前を迎へに来る段取がチヤンと出来てゐるのだから、今の間なりと気楽に歌でも唄つておかつしやい。火の車が来たが最後、お前の体は不動さまのように、恋の情火が燃え立つて、熱い目に会はねばならぬのだからな。あゝ思へば思へば不愍なものだワイ。

火の車別に地獄にやなけれ共 己が作つて己が乗り行く

とか云つて、お前が作つた完全無欠な火の車だから、誰に遠慮も要らぬ。ドンドンと乗つて行かつしやれや。何事も世の中は自業自得だ。善因善果、悪因悪果、蒔かぬ種は生えぬとやら、自分が蒔いた種が成長して、花が咲き実がのり、又自分が収穫をせなくちやならぬ天地自然の法則だからなア』

『エー、秋山別は別に女に対し、恋慕は致しましたが、まだ生れてから、女一人犯したことは御座りませぬ。何が為にそれ程重い罪を科せられるのでせうか。是れ位な微罪を、さう喧かましく詮議立てをし、処罰をして居つたならば、地獄の牢屋もやり切れますまい』

『軽い罪は皆見のがして、三途の川で衣を脱がし、それから生れ赤子の赤裸にして、霊の故郷へ帰してやるのだが、お前の様な罪人は何うしても帰す事が出来ないよ。又何程立派な審判の鬼だとて、中には盲もあるから、お前の罪は俺が聞いても、ホンの軽い様に思ふが、火の車に乗せられて、焦熱地獄へ落してやらうと判決されたのだから、此婆アの力ぢや如何する事も出来ない。閻魔さまだつて直接に調べるのぢやないから、疎漏もあるだらうし、無実の罪で来て居る憐れな人間もチヨイチヨイあるやうだ。何程冥途の規則が立派に出来上つて居つても、それを運用する審判の鬼が盲だつたら駄目だからな。マア諦めるより仕方があるまいぞよ。上の大将からして、盲の幽霊計りだから困つたものだよ。此婆アもお前には満腔の同情を表してゐるけれど、上から押へられるのだから、どうする事も出来やしない。お前の言訳を一つでもせうものなら、それこそ大変だ。下の役の癖に上役の裁いた事を、何ゴテゴテ言ふかと云つて、一遍に免職さされて了うのだ。さうすればお前が今渡つて来た欲の川に居つた我利々々亡者の様に骨と皮とになつて了はねばならぬ。アーア暗がりの世の中と云ふものは情ないものだわい』

と婆アさまは鼻をすすり、そろそろと泣き出した。

斯かる所へガラガラガラとけたたましき音を立て、いかめしき面した赤鬼、青鬼、金平糖を長うした様な金棒を携へ、二台の火の車を引つれて、此場に向つて勢よく駆けつけ来る。二人は『アツ』と驚き其場に倒れ伏しける。
