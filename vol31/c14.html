Chapter 14: 樹下の宿 [880]

樹下の宿〔880〕

§ 1

Floating upon the waves, Takasago, Hiru and Haru's Kuniyoriwake, Climbing up the rugged mountains, With Yasuhiko and Munesuke, both men, Following as he ascends, crossing over the narrow valleys of Brazil, Journeying day and night, far and wide, Treading across the plains of Haru, Wandering unknowingly and unwittingly Into the forests of the Amazon River, Chance encounters with the party of Takayorihime Or meeting up with Takahime's party, Taming and pacifying the monsters of Morubando, Removing and purifying the fearsomely tragic disasters of the myriad people— Such is the tale we now set forth to tell here.

Kuniyoriwake gave the name Yasuhiko to Kiji, and bestowed the name Munesuke upon Machi, and while preaching and instructing them along the way in the teachings of Ananahi, they rested their breath upon the summit of the Brazilian Pass—where of old Odoyamazuminokami had passed by together with Kagatora, who was the incarnation of Hanasakuyahime—and from thence they were to set forth out into Daigenya.

The party of Kuniyoriwake spent the day at the foot of the Brazilian Pass, sheltering from the night dew at the root of a massive tree and passing the single night.

Kuniyoriwake and Yasuhiko, without a care in the world from the fatigue of travel, were sleeping soundly. Munesuke felt a nameless foreboding in his breast, unable to close his eyes even for a moment, and lay sandwiched between the two. Suddenly hearing the roar of a wild beast, his heart leaping and his soul nearly vanishing with terror, Munesuke could not endure the shuddering, clung tightly to Yasuhiko's body, and prayed for the break of day to come even a moment sooner.

Appearing as though dawn were drawing near, the cries of the wild beasts faded away imperceptibly. At that moment, two men sat upon the stump of a giant tree, engrossed in whispered conversation. Even though it was a single giant tree whose trunk measured some fifty jo around, the fact that three men were sleeping soundly on one side gave no way of catching the eyes of the two sitting upon the other.

From somewhere or other, whispered words drifted into his ears, so Munesuke, peering stealthily into the sky, drew closer to the direction of the voices, cocked his ear, and listened so as not to let slip a single word.

Aki, hey... I've searched all the way here, but it's no use anymore. Up at Mount Hiwashi, we were chased around by Haru and Nairu, and when I asked about how things stood, they said Kuniyoriwake set out this very morning. When I asked how many people he had in tow, he held out three fingers. His plan is to wander the realm under the pretext of propagation, taking along Mato-kiri, the Ku-in, and the E-in. Out of a man's pride, even if it costs me my life, I'm going to stalk his heels, watch for an opening in Kuniyoriwake, thrust him down to the bottom of a ravine, and unless we make those two nice girls our own, it's so foolish I won't even be able to show my face to the world, isn't it? I can't just cry myself to sleep and let it go at that when his whereabouts are no longer known, so don't you have some scheme or other, Lord Aki?

Mori-ko, you certainly have a deep attachment, don't you. Sticking your heels in and marching all the way out to a place like this—the woman targeted by such fellows is just like a toad charmed by a snake. Truly, the more I think about it, the more pitiful those two women have become. I too was drawn here by the madness of a restless mind and a wild heart, but before I knew it, both the monkey of my heart and the horse of my desires seemed to have given up in disgust, yelled in despair, and gone on back home. There's nothing more to be done, why don't we turn back from here?

Do as you please, I am going to carry this through to the very end. How could a man return to his homeland with such a shameless face?

Yet, no matter how infatuated you are with the two women, as long as Kuniyoriwake is there, it's no use, isn't it? If we could only do something about that fellow...

So speaking, they whispered something to each other in low voices right by their ears for a short while. Munesuke could not hear it no matter what, because their voices were far too low.

...If you go about a ri ahead from here, there is a log bridge. It is a dangerously deep mountain stream, and if you were to fall in there, even the sturdiest of men would have his five limbs smashed to pieces, so let's get ahead while there's still time, remove the props of that bridge, and rig it so that the moment Kuniyoriwake takes a single stride across, down he will plunge with a crash. We'll tie one end of the bridge with wisteria vines or something, hide at the bottom of the ravine the instant Kuniyoriwake steps across, and pull that rope. Then it'll be, whoosh, slam, ugh, kyaa... and that's the end of it, a truly pitiful state of affairs indeed. Isn't that how we should do it?

'Even if you break your bones like that, since it is after Kuniyoriwake has passed, won't it just turn out to be all bone-breaking and fatigue for nothing?'

'Nonsense, it is daijiyōbu. Even if they set out half a day ahead, the other side is leading ashiyowaonna, and our side consists entirely of kenkiyakuka, so surely I will win and get ahead. Aitsu is no doubt still lingering two or three ri behind, dallying with the woman. Come, if we do not hurry, it will be taihen once we are caught up. If they come while the sagefu is unfinished, it will amount to nothing at all. If the woman attempts to cross, we will let her cross in silence. The very moment Kuniyoriwake takes two or three steps, we pull the rope—one, two, three. How is that, Akisama, a brilliant plan?'

'What if one woman goes first, Kuniyoriwake stands in the middle, and another woman is at the back, making a tanjiujin as they cross all at once? Won't that turn out to be kutabiremau for nothing, catching neither horsefly nor bee?'

'Well, the wind will blow as it pleases then, won't it? Tatohe we ochiko into the ravine, and even if we get a slight kega, there is no fear of all sannin dying together. At that point, Kuniyoriwake will be dumbfounded, so we just let him go with an innocent face and shimahi. We will make the twain women drink water, care for them... saying, 'Now now, travelers' ojiyochiu...' and rescue them. If we do that, there is no reason why Benaihime might not call us her saviors of life and cast amorous glances our way. In fact, the very reason Kuniyoriwake was rabādo was because he rescued her during the great earthquake, you see.'

'That is true as well. Come, let us go quickly and set about the preparations. If we dawdle, it will be like chrysanthemums after the ninth day—a festival after it is all over—and amount to nothing at all. O-ne, two, three!'

Thus, they advanced along the narrow valley path, peering suspiciously about.

Sōsuke, after the futari had gone, muttered:

'Just as I thought I could not sleep tonight of all nights, Akiyamawake and Morisu are plotting such wicked things. What is more, they chattered away so loudly that it reached our ears as if by tenbatsu. The gods must have placed a spirit upon me and kept me awake precisely because those two are hatching such a scheme. How thorough and far-reaching the benevolence of the gods is from beginning to end! Whenever anyone thinks nobody knows and plots evil, things always turn out just like this. Heaven knows, earth knows, and I myself know—even Sōsuke knows, they say, which makes it quite frightening. Let me see, shall I catch this secret and report it as a feat to Kuniyoriwake-sama?... No, no, wait, wait, if I speak too fast it will lose its value. When we reach the edge of the bridge, just as Kuniyoriwake-sama is about to step onto it... I will stop him, crying, 'Pardon me, wait, if I look with my tennantsū, this bridge is a floating bridge, so it is perilous. Aki and Mori are pulling the ropes...' By doing so, Kuniyoriwake-sama might be overjoyed and restore my name, which he changed, back to my old name of Sōsukehiko-sama. Oh yes, silence is golden.'

Carried away by his own mood, before he knew it, he was chirping in a high voice. Yasuhiko woke up and said:

'Oi Sōsuke, you are thinking foolish things, aren't you?'

Sōsuke lowered his voice and said:

'Oi, did you hear? It is a secret from the commander, so please keep it in mind.'

Yasuhiko raised his voice purposely and said:

'What about the single-plank bridge?'

'Won't you rest without shouting so loudly? Aki and Mori are in the midst of the sagefu right now to drop the log bridge. Isn't it interesting?'

'Tensensei-sama, do you know about this?'

'I have heard the whole thing from the beginning. Shall I have your name changed to Sōsukehiko?'

'Oh, thank you very much indeed. I humbly beg your kind favor.'

'In that case, I shall raise your rank by one degree and grant you the name Munehiko from this very moment.'

'Ah, I have no words to express my gratitude... Hey, Yasuhiko, how is this? From right now, you and I are colleagues. I cannot have you treating me like a younger brother with such an arrogant air.'

'I received the name Hiko three days ago. Didn't you just receive yours now? Even if twins are born, a distinction is made between brothers. Since there is actually a difference of three days, you are naturally the younger brother.'

'Ah, there is no help for it then. Well, I will make a concession and become the younger brother in appearance only. In exchange, the older brother must possess the capability befitting an older brother, and he has the responsibility to cherish and care for the younger brother. If the younger brother is feeling weak, I must take his hand and look after him, or even carry him on my back, otherwise my value as an older brother is nil, you see.'

'Hey, hey, won't you stop chattering and rest? There is still a little time before daybreak. We will rest leisurely here, and proceed step by step after daybreak. No doubt those two fellows are hiding in the tree thickets at the bottom of the valley, so you two must go using the voices of women. And then all three of you will successfully cross over.'

'For three people to use women's voices for two women, isn't that a bit strange?'

'As for that, it will become two. Kuniyoriwake will use the voice of Beniihime, and Yasuhiko will carry his younger brother Munehiko on his back, and thus taking on the voice of Erina, you only need to cross over and you will be safe. After you have crossed, each of you will burst into a loud laugh with a man's voice, and startle them.'

'Kuniyoriwake-san, unlike a serious missionary, you certainly do love mischief. Will even I be able to cross a single-log bridge carrying such a man on my back?'

'For a fellow who plots such evil things, isn't it fine if we teasingly play a trick on him just this once? After all, if it goes wrong, it's a place where we might lose our lives... And you will strap Munehiko to your back, so even if you mistakenly fall, for Kuniyoriwake it will be neither painful nor itchy, ha-ha-ha-ha-ha.'

Yasuhiko tilted his head, gazing intently at Kuniyoriwake's face,

'Heng, what an indifferent person you are, my goodness!'

'Well, since we are crossing the mountain stream and falling into the valley water, it is bound to be a bit indifferent, isn't it? At the bottom of the valley, the Spirit of Water is waiting, and the moment you plunge in, it will receive you with open hands and save you, so you have nothing to worry about. You shouldn't indulge in such needless worries. Ah, how I long for the time. Missionary-san, wouldn't it be about time to set out?'

'Didn't the two of them just go a moment ago? Preparing to pull out the bridge piles of that deep mountain stream or attaching vines to pull them is not something that can be done with a respite of one or two hours. Isn't it more interesting to take it easy here and make fools of them? Even if we are late by about half a day, it's not like we'll have a fine for tardiness deducted; come on, let's enjoy the road ahead and rest at ease.'

So saying, he used the root of a large tree as a pillow and fell fast asleep.

'Isn't a person called a missionary remarkably bold, hey, Munehiko? Even though the enemy is presently carrying out preparations to drop the bridge, he calmly lies down just like that and goes to sleep with a loud snore as soon as he touches the ground. Since we still cannot shake off our worldly attachments and cherish our lives, whenever we think the enemy is making preparations for murder right before us, our mental spirits become agitated here and there and we cannot sleep at all.'

'Since you try to compare Kuniyoriwake-san with a cheap fellow like Yasuhiko, such doubts arise, you see. A living deity and a manure-producing vessel cannot be treated in the same way, why.'

'If I am a manure-producing vessel, aren't you a manure-producing vessel too?'

'You are a manure-producing vessel, but this Munehiko is a feces-manufacturing vessel. It sounds like the same meaning, but there is a slight difference in the nuance there. Ah-ha-ha-ha.'

Laughing thus, while circling round and round the great tree, they decided to wait for the break of dawn. Gradually, the eastern sky began to whiten, and the voices of a hundred birds resounded noisily from the surrounding forests. The morning breeze sweeping over the green leaves blew upon the three of them an unspeakably refreshing coolness, begrudging nothing. At last, the party of three made their footgear preparations, traced the narrow path of the valley, and proceeded forward toward the log bridge.

『今二人が行つたばかしぢやないか。あの深い谷川の橋杭を取つたり、蔓をつけて引つ張る用意するのは、一時や二時の猶予で出来るものぢやない。茲でゆつくりしてアフンとさしてやる方が面白いぢやないか。別に半日位遅れたつて、遅刻の罰金を取られるのでもなし、マア先を楽ンで、ゆつくり休ンで行かう』

と言ひ乍ら、大木の根を枕に寝て了つた。

『何と宣伝使と云ふものは大胆な者ぢやないか、ナア宗彦。現在敵が落橋準備をやつてゐるのに、平気であの通り、横になるが早いか高鼾をかいて寝て了はれた。俺達はまだ執着心が離れぬので、命が惜しくて、敵が前に殺人準備をやつて居ると思へば、どこともなしに心気興奮して寝られないがなア』

『国依別さまと安物の安彦と比べやうとするから、そンな疑問が起るのだよ。活神さまと製糞器とは同じ様にはいかぬワイ』

『俺が製糞器なら、お前も製糞器ぢやないか』

『お前は製糞器だよ、この宗彦は糞造器だ。同じ意味の様だが、そこに一寸違う訳があるのだ。アハヽヽヽ』

と笑ひ乍ら、大木の周囲をクルクルと繞りつつ夜明を待つ事にした。漸くにして、東は白み出し、百鳥の声、あたりの森林より、かしましく聞え来たる。青葉を渡る旦の風は、得も言はれぬ涼味を惜しげもなく、三人に向つて吹きつける。いよいよ一行三人は足仕度をなし、谷の細路を伝ひ、丸木橋に向ひ進み行く事とはなりにける。
