Chapter 4: 不知恋 [870]

不知恋〔870〕

§ 1

At the earnest entreaty of Kaedewakenomikoto, Kuniyoriwake came to stay here for a while. However, feeling anxious and troubled over the messages and whereabouts of the pheasant and Machi whom he had dispatched to the grotto of Mount Higura, as well as the whereabouts of Esu, he desperately wished to leave this mansion somehow, search for their whereabouts as quickly as possible, and rescue them. Yet, multitudes of people gathered around, revering him as if he were a Kami, and because of the great earthquake of this time, day after day without fail numbering in the hundreds, people who had sustained injuries—some carried on palanquins and others placed upon door-boards—came seeking salvation. Thus, Kuniyoriwake could not simply abandon this harrowing scene and depart, and so he performed Chinkon for the afflicted people, saved them, and unconsciously let days and times pass by.

Now then, Kurenaihime, who had been saved from the crisis of nine deaths and a life, from this time forth felt a strange and unusual sentiment of love and adoration toward Kuniyoriwake arise within her moment by moment like clouds. Already scorched by the fire of passion, her chest ached, her heart beat with the waves of a hand-drum, and while panting hot breaths, she was yet unable to utter her first love from her lips, breathing heavily through her shoulders, and at last, pressed by her thoughts, her body grew emaciated and withered, her complexion turned pale, and she came to moan upon her sickbed.

Observing Kurenaihime's illness, Kaedewakenomikoto felt great anxiety and, earnestly desiring to make her recover completely by any means possible, offered prayers before the divine altar morning and evening. Although the maidservants Ārī and Sāru never left Kurenaihime's side for a single moment, pouring their hearts into nursing her day and night, the princess's illness only grew heavier day by day, and there were no remedies to apply.

Hearing of the princess's serious illness, Kuniyoriwake visited her sickbed purposely to save her from the affliction through Chinkon. When the princess heard of Kuniyoriwake's visit, she raised her heavy head, blushed slightly, lowered her gaze a little, looked at Kuniyoriwake's face as if stealing a glance, let out a smile, and joyfully pressed her hands together to express her gratitude.

Kuniyoriwake sat squarely at Kurenaihime's pillow, chanted the heavenly Norito, intoned the heavenly number-songs, and while bestowing words of consolation upon the princess, he quietly departed from this place, returned to his assigned guest room, and once again offered prayers to Kami—how admirable indeed.

Akiyamawake, who served Kaedewakenomikoto with the deepest trust and gathered the admiration of numerous believers, turned his heart toward Kurenaihime's exquisite beauty and charm, his chest burning with a love and yearning that grew day by day, and he exercised his mind to buy the princess's favor at every opportunity.

Moreover, Morisu, the director of inner affairs, as opportunities to have audience with Kurenaihime increased, unknowingly let his heart dissolve into the princess's beauty, deeply expecting in his heart that the one who would concentrate Kurenaihime's love upon himself in the future would surely be none other than him. Therefore, in regard to the princess's serious illness this time, resolving to exhaust the utmost of his sincerity and buy her favor, Morisu neglected his duties in inner affairs, and whenever he had leisure, visiting the princess's bedchamber under the pretext of visiting the sick and nursing her was his sole divine stratagem.

On the other hand, Akiyamawake, finding the fire of his yearning and passionate love equally hard to quench, came visiting the bedchamber of the shabby and emaciated Kurenaihime morning and evening without fixed times, exhausting the utmost of his sincerity, and deep within his heart he was eagerly anticipating that as soon as the princess made a full recovery, the ceremony of union should be held at the earliest possible day.

Nevertheless, ever since Kuniyoriwake came to this mansion, Kurenaihime's attitude toward Akiyamawake and also toward Morisu seemed to have become somehow cold, causing the two men to sink into the abyss of agony; how laughable indeed that, driven by the tricksters within their hearts, they exhausted every limit of honeyed words, clever speech, and fawning flattery to somehow recover the princess's trust.

From the very beginning, Kurenaihime merely interacted gently with Akiyamawake and Morisu as officials of the ordinary path of teachings or as supervisors performing duties of inner affairs, and in her dreams she held no feelings of romantic love toward these two men. Yet the two men, every time they heard Kurenaihime's gentle words, deluded themselves into thinking that she loved them, and facing their mirrors of self-conceit with the belief that there was no suitable candidate for the groom of the three countries other than Akiyamawake, they twitched their noses and passed their days relying upon groundless matters. Likewise, Morisu the director of inner affairs also determined within his heart that the future husband of Kurenaihime would surely be Morisu, and while fixing it in his mind, he was anticipating day by day that the auspicious day and fortunate hour would arrive as soon as possible.

Akiyamawake was pained by the somewhat suspicious attitude of Morris toward Hime lately, and was beginning to grow the horns of Hofukairinki, while Morris, in turn, was irritated by the conspicuously kind attitude of Akiyamawake toward Hime, and as a rival in love, was keeping a vigilant watch over the actions of Akiyamawake. Thus, Akiyamawake took the maid Allie into his confidence and made her a person in his pocket, while Morris took the maid Sarah into his confidence and made her a person in his pocket, and it was amusing to see them competing for their victory and defeat, secretly continuing their competition of love.

At such a time, Kuniyoriwakenomikoto, who was respected as a Shinjin and Ikigami of the world, had saved Kurenaihime from a life-or-death crisis, and since the trust of Hime in him grew day by day, the hearts of the two were constantly unable to bear the feelings of distress. They were beginning to prepare for a campaign of impeachment in secret, like a manji-tomoe, while burning with the flames of love like a futatsu-domoe, intending to find the Ketsuten of Kuniyoriwake and expose them before the Kyōshu, Kaedewakenomikoto, and Kurenaihime, so as to damage and break that trust. However, Kuniyoriwake was by nature not at all attached to women, and he did not have even a hair's breadth of suspicious intent toward Kurenaihime; therefore, Kuniyoriwake, without any hesitation, was simply worried from the bottom of his heart to save Hime from her serious illness, and visited Hime's bedroom day and night to pray to God and frequently inquire about the progress of her illness.

However, this action of Kuniyoriwake was felt as extreme pain in the eyes of the love-trapped, gaunt-dog Akiyamawake and Morris, and they eventually came to regard him as a Kiuteki.

Just then, a woman visited the entrance. Morris immediately invited her into his living room, and when he asked her the purpose of her visit, the woman, while somewhat shy, spoke in a gentle tone:

Speaker: 'Is the propagator of San-go-kyo, Kuniyoriwake-sama, at the mansion? If he is here, I would like you to convey that a woman named Erina from the foot of Arashika Pass has come to visit.'

Morris thought to himself:

Speaker: 'Ha! This woman must be Kuniyoriwake's mistress. She has come at a good time. Now that this secret is known, no matter how devoted Kurenaihime-sama is to Kuniyoriwake, if she hears that he has a woman, even a thousand-year love will surely awaken at once. I will sweeten her up, get her to talk, and probe the bottom of her heart...'

He made up his mind and said kindly:

Speaker: 'That was very kind of you to come and visit. It was a terrible earthquake, but I hope your home is not too badly off?'

Speaker: 'Yes, thank you. In that great earthquake, my small house was destroyed and burned, and my mother passed away due to the earthquake and fire. I am truly an unfortunate woman.'

She said, already containing tears.

Speaker: 'That is a pitiful thing. I sympathize with you. However, old people are destined to die first anyway. If one were to speak of the most auspicious things, they say: old man dies, old woman dies, grandfather dies, grandmother dies, child dies, grandchild dies; there is nothing more auspicious than this. It is natural for those who should die first to die first; just imagine if the old people were left behind and the young people died first. Even if you were to face a cruel situation where you become old and cannot stand on your legs and loins, and are incontinent of urine and feces, if the young people were to pass away first, there would likely be no one to take care of you with personal concern. Besides, you and Kuniyoriwake-sama are a young couple left behind, so there is nothing more auspicious than this. Everything depends on how one thinks, you see. Instead of your parents, you should go ahead and pound the New Year's mochi, and make plenty of child-mochi, as many as the stars in the heavens. That is the best service to God, ha-ha-ha!'

Speaker: 'I am by no means the wife of Kuniyoriwake-sama or anything of the sort. It is just that my mother was in trouble with a sudden illness, so I was visiting the shrine of Tokoyonokami-ōkami, and there the propagator Kuniyoriwake appeared with two retainers, and he kindly came to my house... and said, "I shall pray for the recovery of your mother's illness"... and that is all, he stayed for five or six days.'
