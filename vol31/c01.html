From the distant age of the gods, when Chihayaburu times prevailed, to the wondrous capital of Jerusalem, by the divine decree of Amatsumikami, Kuniharutachinōokami, desiring to save all people universally, appeared as Nodachinohiko. The divine spirit of Toyokunihime appeared as Nodachinohiko's counterpart, Nodachihime, holding both Heaven and Earth in sway, and with her heart distributed in a thousand ways to save and help all human beings, spirits, grasses, and trees in peace. Haniyasuhiko and Haniyasuhime appeared as noble mikoto, establishing the divine teachings of Ananahi beneath Mount Ōgonsanka, and proclaiming the way of salvation. The wondrous child of Hinodewake assists in the divine works of the august spirit of Kamususanōnōokami, spreading them widely throughout the world—how venerable this is! In the land of Toyoashihara no Mizuho-kuni, known by the name of Nebetsu no Kuni, at the center of the self-revolving island, within the spiritual domain of the lofty plain of Aya, Kuniharutachinōokami temporarily hid himself and, changing his name to Kokutehikohiko at Mount Yobuse, desired to bring forth the sacred reign of Fifty-Six-Seven. Here the three men steadily walked the night road, climbed the rugged pass of Arasika, and descended heading toward the northeast. The two men parted ways with Koto-yoribetsu and marched forward spiritedly, wishing to rescue Esu, who was suffering in captivity in the cave of Mount Hinemurashi, even a day earlier.

Kuniyoriwake, the minister of the gods, following the daughter of Esu, approached the mansion of Esu located at the foot of Mount Arashika, stayed here for a while, proclaimed and transmitted the teachings of the gods, stopped by the capital of Hiru, met with Kaedenowake, and wishing to aid the two, Kiji and Machi, proceeded to the cave of Mount Higura. Then the minister of the gods of Būru pacified and subdued the spirits, and his majestic fame, not to mention in Takasagojima, thundered throughout the land of Toyoashihara, a tale now recounted. Gazing upon the mountain stream of Kano, bathing in the early autumn breeze, reclining in an easy chair, puffing away on Shikishima tobacco, facing the Jiyōshiyabosatsu,

Now at last, the Reikaimonogatari, the thirty-first volume begins, and the Kototamaguruma rolls forth. Ah, Kamunagara, O Gods, may your august spirits flourish and bless us.

Kuniyoriwake, guided by Erina, finally arrived at the house of Esu. Erina's mother was suffering from an unexpected, grave illness, to the extent of being almost unconscious. Without a moment to lose, Kuniyoriwake hastily kicked off his straw sandals, rushed up onto the tatami room, immediately chanted the Heavenly Congratulatory Liturgy, raised high the numbers song of heaven, performed the pacification of the spirit, and putting forth various zealous efforts, sought a way for her recovery. Yet, how could it be? The sick woman showed not the slightest sign of improvement, growing drastically weaker day by day, and had already advanced into the realm of despair. Erina, devoting herself with all her heart, performed ritual ablutions with cold water...

Lord Kuniharu Tachinomoto-sama of the Izumo teachings, King Tokoyogami-sama, I beseech you, I implore you, make my father escape his critical peril, save my mother from her incurable illness once again, so that husband and wife, parent and child, even if only for a single day, may happily and joyfully look upon each other's faces, and be moistened by the dew of grace...

Thus she prayed earnestly, forgetting herself. Yet Erina's inner mind could not yet attain the spirit of undivided concentration without straying, and doubts remained. The reason was this: Is Kuniharu Tachinomoto truly a benevolent god? Or is King Tokoyogami rather the benevolent god? If she prayed to Kuniharu Tachinoshimigoto, would she incur the divine wrath of King Tokoyogami, causing her mother's illness to become ever heavier and her father's grave peril to grow even deeper? Such doubts were passing back and forth within her mind.

Kuniyoriwake discerned the state of Erina's heart, and having stayed here for four or five days, he preached and admonished her in various ways concerning the truth that good and evil are not two, and that the manifest and the hidden are of one essence. However, being the affair of a young maiden driven to panic and confusion by the calamities of her parents, Kuniyoriwake's teachings, exhausting a thousand words and ten thousand phrases, did not easily enter her head. Her thoughts were solely on saving her father from mortal peril and healing her mother's serious illness as early as a single day, and while becoming single-mindedly frantic, she was profoundly bewildered and suffering in regard to her faith. Therefore, neither the pacification of the spirit nor the Kototama performed by Kuniyoriwake, whose divine virtue was fully accomplished, reached the point of manifesting miraculous efficacy.

In general, true faith requires one to cast aside bewilderment, sweep away idle thoughts, refrain from running to mere intellect, and entrust everything unconditionally to the divine will, becoming a mind of undivided concentration; otherwise, no matter how venerable the prayer of a divine person, and no matter how authoritative the Kototama, it is only natural that its effect does not easily manifest. In short, Erina's faith was divided in two, or to put it unfavorably, had fallen into a straddling, opportunistic faith. From her early childhood, she had been deeply instilled from the bottom of her heart with the belief that there was no god in the universe more venerable than King Tokoyogami, and no authority superior to him; and that should she ever incur the taboo of King Tokoyogami, not to mention in the phenomenal world, she would receive infinite suffering even in the spiritual realm and be subjected to severe punishment. Therefore, even while listening with joy to the teachings of the true gods, she remained enveloped in clouds of anxiety, continuing her vexation and anguish.

Kuniyoriwake realized that Erina's faith would not easily be moved, and further comprehended that her mother's illness could by no means be saved, whereupon he finally made the resolution to depart from this place and head toward the capital of Hiru.
