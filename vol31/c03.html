Chapter 3: 救世 Kami [869]

救世神〔869〕

§ 1

The weather was calm and clear, with not a single speck of cloud in the azure sky. The four classes of society each enjoyed their respective occupations, celebrating the spring of Tokoyo. Some married, others danced and sang; the mountains and rivers were pure and clear, the trees performed a natural dance, the streams played a natural music, birds sang in the treetops, butterflies danced in the fields, playing among the flowers, and frolicking with joyous delight. This phenomenon of heaven and earth in peace and safety was just like the age of the gods. In the twinkling of an eye, the whole sky suddenly clouded over, and the entire heavens became as if pouring ink. The myriad birds soaring through the vast and azure sky suddenly plunged toward the ground as if shooting arrows. The great earth suddenly shuddered, and the heavenly realm and Pure Land instantly

transformed into the hell of hunger, the realm of beasts, and the alley of Shura. Moment by moment, the violent tremor of the great earth added to its severity. Mountains collapsed, wilderness split, dwellings fell, bridges instantly collapsed, and here and there, blazing furiously, scorching the sky as they burned, occurred an unprecedented, peerless conflagration. Its terror made one's flesh creep and hair stand on end. Even in the heavenly realm of divine grace, the divine beings beneath the heavens—their perverted and crooked souls mingling with the overlapping kototama of ugliness—caused the atmospheric vapours of malevolent demons to accumulate, darkening the great august mind of the Lord God of Heaven and Earth. Thus arose sudden heavenly changes and earthly monsters. What must be guarded against by the people of the world is

the very state of their heart—its ears, nose, mouth, and innermost feelings. Ah, Kami-nagara! As I witness this tragic state, the people of the island of Takasago profoundly and meticulously comprehended the precious august mind of the gods, and at last here they bowed in awe to the authority of the gods of heaven and earth, corrected their hearts, reformed their conduct, and served the gods. Such is the precious tale of ancient times, which we state and uphold according to the will of the gods. Ah, Kami-nagara! May the august spirits of the gods bless us. The people of the island of Takasago who dwelt in the distant age of the gods—need it even be said? Beneath the heavens, in the august realms of the four quarters, like the number of glittering stars in the great sky,

the people who were born to meet this time should not think of it as a thing of the past. Purify your heart, purify your body, and as a bookmark for turning from delusion to enlightenment, carve it upon your heart: Kami-nagara! Born as the divine children of the gods, let us fulfill our heavenly mission. Now then, the gods are divine spirits that pervade all things. Human beings are the children of the gods born from the divine water and fire of heaven and earth. As those who are born to be rulers who, displaying the divine power of the supreme great deities, open up heaven and earth, let the bodies of humans who have come into being realize their own heavenly mission and submit to the true god! Whether the sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon fills or wanes, even though the great earth shakes,

even though mountains split and seas dry up, fulfill your sincerity before the face of the god who created this world, and fulfill your duty as human beings—Kami-nagara! The gods are with you. The great rectifying deity (Naobi) who created this world, and the great rectifying deity of boundless breadth—even for the bodies of humans who have grown utterly defiled, the heavenly deities, the earthly deities, and the eight million gods, beginning with the deities of the land's soul, will re-examine sins, listen anew, and rectify their pronouncements. Reverently worship the local tutelary deities who watch over us intimately, and pray that in this beautiful heaven and earth, free from gales, floods, great fires, famines, wars, and diseases, keeping the divine will of universal brotherhood in mind morning and evening,

you may fulfill the heavenly mission of being the vessels of the gods! Ah, Kami-nagara! May the august spirits bless and comfort you.

In the capital of Hiru, there was a peerless beauty named Kōaihime, the younger sister of Kaedewake-no-mikoto, who had reached the age of nineteen that spring. The water of the river Nigurushi greatly decreased, and numerous lively fresh fish all gathered in large numbers near the capital of Hiru in the lower reaches. Thus the sport of cormorant fishing was enthusiastically practiced day and night without distinction. Ali and Sāru, who served as maids to Kōaihime, together with Moris, who managed the domestic affairs of the mansion, desired to try this cormorant fishing just once. Yet, no matter what, being bound by their duties and saddened by their lack of freedom, they came before Kōaihime on every occasion and spoke of how exhilarating the cormorant fishing on the river Nigurushi was. Kōaihime, slightly inclined to listen to her maids' entertaining talk, asked her brother Kaedewake-no-mikoto of her own accord to grant her permission for a day of pure excursion. Since Kaedewake-no-mikoto knew that Kōaihime always remained solely within the deep boudoir and rarely went out, he had no reason to refuse her rare request, and cheerfully granted it. He permitted her the sport of cormorant fishing on the river Nigurushi flowing outside the castle, accompanied by Moris, the manager of domestic affairs, along with the maids Ali and Sāru, and four or five other attendants.

§ 2

At this moment, Kurenaihime, feeling as if she were ascending even to heaven, followed by Morris and others, finished her cormorant fishing, captured a multitude of fresh fish, and was returning to the castle town with a triumphant expression on her face, when suddenly the heaven and earth quaked, the great earth began to sway up and down and left and right, the houses collapsed flat like falling dominoes, and Kurenaihime's entourage was likewise crushed by the houses within the city, becoming invisible as they were enveloped in smoke.

Fires broke out in various places, and instantly the capital of Hiru transformed into an alley of lamentation amidst agony and suffering. Into such a place, a single missionary advancing leisurely while singing a missionary song was the missionary Kuniyoriwake.

The houses on both sides fell ruinously and utterly into the street, and the fire encroached progressively from front, back, left, and right with such terrifying intensity. Instantly enveloped in smoke, they reached a state where they could not discern even an inch ahead. Hearing the screams of a woman close at hand at her very feet, the missionary Kuniyoriwake groped his way guided by that voice, removed a pinned-down pillar relying on his great strength, and without knowing who she might be, hoisted her instantly onto his back,

Amatsukami, Kunitsukami, Kunitama no Kami, Ubusuna no Kami, quickly save this tragic situation at once...

Praying thus while chanting a missionary song, parting the smoke, he ran before he knew it to the natural stronghold where rocks of Shimotsuiwane were laid out in the center of the capital of Hiru, the divine lodge of Kaedewake no Mikoto.

Kuniyoriwake heaved a sigh of relief with a gasp, took the woman down from his back to that spot, and caught his breath. The woman, in a faint voice,

I do not know from whence you are, but thank you very much for saving me from such a perilous place. Indeed, you are truly a living god who has saved my life.

Wringing out tears of gratitude, she pressed her palms together and bowed down prostrate. Kuniyoriwake, with quiet words,

I am a missionary of Izumo Shinpo. On my way, seeking afar to pay a single visit to Kaedewake no Mikoto who dwells in this divine lodge, in the midst of the great earthquake disaster just now, saving you unexpectedly must surely be the divine guidance of the gods. I rather feel troubled to be thanked.

I am called Kurenaihime, the younger sister of Kaedewake no Mikoto. On my way back from cormorant fishing at Himurashi River, I encountered the calamity of an unexpected great earthquake, and I am one whose life was saved by you. Come, come, please pass through quickly. My brother will surely be overjoyed.

Ah, is that so? What a wondrous bond of fate we share. However... Please look at that. The mountains on all sides have begun to erupt vigorously, black clouds seal the heavens, the earth splits, turbid water spews from various places, and already the lowlands have become a great flood, while the cries of people weeping and shouting have grown louder moment by moment. From now on, I must use the divine power of Tamatama to calm this heavenly upheaval and earthly specter, and save all the people. Won't you please hurry and enter the lodge? I shall come shortly...

If that is so, please excuse me ahead. I shall be waiting for you eagerly, so please be sure to enter.

Saying this, she hurriedly ran into the interior of the lodge. Kuniyoriwake with all his heart,

Kunimioya Kunisaritachi no Mikoto, Toyokunihime no Mikoto, Kunitama no Kami, quickly calm this earthly anomaly and heavenly upheaval at once, to say nothing of the common people, please save all living things...

Praying thus, infusing the spiritual power of his whole body, he chanted the heavenly numbers loudly, and when he emitted the kototama of 'U-U', wondrously the tremor of the great earth ceased instantly, the eruptions of various mountains stopped accordingly, the gushing floods halted with a snap, and before his eyes they began to subside. The power of the breath of kototama was remarkably manifest, the black clouds wrapping the entire sky cleared away as if wiped clean, the radiant sun shone brilliantly in the mid-heaven, and he graciously illuminated the tragic scene of the nether world with pity.

Kaedewake no Mikoto ascended the high hall, desiring to rescue this tragic state, with single-minded devotion forgetting completely from his mind matters such as Kurenaihime's personal situation, and offered up prayers solely and exclusively for all the people under heaven.

§ 3

At this juncture, the Sendenshi of Kuniyoriwake appeared, and as he recited the Amatsunorito and the Ikukototama in a manner both perfect and clear, all terrestrial and celestial disturbances, through the Shinriki of the Kototama, returned once more to tranquility, leading the people to look up to the light of the Tenjitsu, and the voices of joy from the Banmin filled the Tenchi. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, the Shinriki of the Ikukototama is truly beyond measure in its nobility.

Upon the Haukoku of Kurenaihime, Kaedewakenomikoto was filled with Kiyōki, and in haste he came before where Kuniyoriwake stood to express his Kanshiyō for his Shinriki, and furthermore,

Kaedewakenomikoto: 'The gratitude I feel for your having saved even the life of Kurenaihime is such that my Orei cannot be exhausted by mere Kotoba...'

While choking back tears of emotion, he accompanied Kuniyoriwake and proceeded toward the Okuden.

First, accepting the words of Kuniyoriwake, and for the sake of the emergency, he opened the storehouses of the five grains that had been kept in reserve; Kaedewakenomikoto, Kuniyoriwake, and Kurenaihime, followed by Akiyamawake and Shinayamawake, mounted swift horses and rode throughout the castle town, crying out in loud voices,

Kaedewakenomikoto, Akiyamawake, Shinayamawake: 'People of the city, gather as quickly as possible at the Kamudono of the castle of Hiru. We shall save you all!'

As they rode through every corner of the city and along the muddy roads, the tragic state of those who had been crushed by pillars and were half-dead, those who had drowned in the water and already perished, and those who had been burned by fire was such that one could hardly bear to look upon it.

Kaedewakenomikoto commanded Akiyamawake and Shinayamawake to have the men and women within the mansion immediately prepare food, and they labored day and night for relief; they healed the wounded through Chinkon, bestowed benevolence upon a great number of people, and the Shinriki of Kuniyoriwake and the sincere Jin-ai of Kaedewakenomikoto came to be widely proclaimed throughout the country. From this time on, Kuniyoriwake, as Kaedewakenomikoto urged, stayed for a while at the Kamudono and imparted the Shinkyo anew to many of the countrymen, and those who had hitherto believed in the Ural religion or the Brahman religion rejoiced from the bottom of their hearts that they had been saved by the Sangokyo through these terrestrial and celestial disturbances, and the entire nation became true believers of the Sango.
