Chapter 18: シーズンの流 [884]

シーズンの流〔884〕

§ 1

Elina cast a mocking side-glance at Morris as he engaged in his silent, earnest prayer, and turning toward Akiyamawake,

Elina: 'Hey, Akiyamakun, what have you done to Kurenaikun? Surely she is not such a foolish woman as to throw herself into the Season River, as you say. If I were you, doing something like throwing away one's life for a lizard like you would be too ridiculous to even consider; besides, aren't you the one to blame? Since Kurenaikun, who was such a sweet heart to you, has sunk into the waters, you cannot simply stand by and watch it as if it were a fire on the opposite bank, can you? Men are truly heartless and ruthless creatures, and that is precisely why we awakened women have come to find ourselves driven to chant about women's liberation, loudly advocate for the expansion of women's rights, and engage in a frantic movement to enact laws of female supremacy and male subordination. If you truly feel sympathy for Kurenaikun, why do you not risk your own body, dive into the water, and pull her up? You must have grown terrified upon gazing at this torrent. Truly, you are a cowardly man. Kurenaikun must be troubled indeed to have been targeted by such a man. Even I, if I had to spend so much as a single day with a man like this—not that I am Kurenaikun, mind you—would feel like plunging myself into the abyss river once and for all and kissing the hand of the god of death. Considering how much of a foolish dandy you are, you really are a senseless person, aren't you?'

Akiyamawake: 'Hey, you have started babbling strange things again. They say there are countless evil spirits around these parts, so poor thing, you must have been possessed. Hey, hey, Morris-kun, why don't you worry about her a little bit too? No matter how much you pray, there is no likelihood that this state of possession will easily have its current cut off. Shall we two join forces to persuade Elina-kun and help her recover her original state of mind?'

Tokagekun: 'Hey, Akiyamawake, it seems you have been infected as well. Do not use such words as you and I, just like Elina-sama. Why don't you use words like ore, or vashi, or omae, or kisama, as you have until now? When you use such words, all of a sudden something like a world called the twentieth century comes to mind.'

Akiyamawake: 'Ah, that is right. I was careless and almost met the misfortune of a collateral fire. Fortunately, since you had your steam pump, we escaped the harm of the spreading fire, which is truly a relief. However, this state of possession of Elina-sama is quite troubling, isn't it?'

Elina: 'Everyone, please do not worry. Thanks to you all, my mind has become cheerful and lively. How on earth did I become such a high-gapped tomboy as I am right now? I have even come to feel ashamed to look upon the faces of you two. Ho ho ho ho.'

So saying with a red face, she concealed it with her sleeve, possessing a peerless and indescribable charm. The two men gazed at Elina-hime in a trance.

Morris turned toward Akiyamawake, bowed slightly at the waist, and with exceedingly polite language,

Morris: 'Akiyamasam, today such an unexpected thing has come to pass; you must be feeling deeply sorrowful, and I offer you my heartfelt sympathies. Just when you had finally rowed your way up to this point and were on the verge of holding the marriage ceremony between husband and wife, Kurenaikun-hime was invited by the ruthless wind and set off on a journey to a distant land; you must feel so lonely. It touches my heart so deeply that I cannot hold back tears of sympathy.'

Akiyamawake: 'Yes, thank you very much. The flower of Kurenaikun has scattered while still half-open. Blown by the merciless storm, she was struck down helplessly, which is truly regrettable.'

So saying, he sniffled.

Morris: 'Say, Akiyamasam, did you truly have the intention of making Kurenaikun-hime your wife?'

Akiyamawake: 'Yes. Sleeping or waking, Kurenaikun-hime fluttered before my eyes, and I never forgot her for even a single moment; what a truly regrettable thing has happened. If Kaedewake-sama of the capital of Hiru were to hear of this, how deeply grieved he would surely be.'

So saying, he sniffled once again.

Morris: 'Moshimoshi, Elina-sama, please wait a moment.'

Morris: 'For what purpose do you suppose I have come all this way here so far?'

Kurenaihime: 'Hai, I know it very well.'

Kurenaihime: 'Were not you two lords consumed by the bonds of love, competing day and night to make Kurenaihime-sama your wife, and burning with Shura?'

Kurenaihime: 'I am merely a low woman who was regarded as nothing more than a byproduct by your eyes.'

Kurenaihime: 'Now that Kurenaihime-sama, your true object of desire, has turned out this way, your obsessive attachment to women must have faded as well.'

Kurenaihime: 'Since I am a person having no relation whatsoever to you, I shall take my leave first, begging your pardon though it is regrettable.'

Kurenaihime: 'If I walk about accompanied by a man, the world will start gossiping this and that once again, and it is more than I can bear, so before a false rumor is spread and an undeserved stigma is pinned upon me, it is for the benefit of both sides that I depart from here.'

Morris: 'Etsu, please wait a moment.'

Morris: 'Since Akiyamawake too has come all the way here like this, thinking to follow after you two, bind the promise of husband and wife, create a harmonious home, and perform divine work, it is truly against my true intention to part ways here.'

Morris: 'Come now, Elina-sama, from now on you are the wife of Akiyamawake's lodging, that should not be too bad, na a.'

Kurenaihime: 'Ho ho ho ho ho, stop it please.'

Kurenaihime: 'You must have devoted yourself heart and soul to Kurenaihime-sama, saying things like if she dies we shall die together, and even leading each other by the hand across the Mountain of Death and the Sanzu River, as you courted her.'

Kurenaihime: 'Now that the very same princess upon whom you pinned such complete devotion has thrown herself into this ravine and died, you have neither the kindness to pull her up nor the sincerity to search out her remains and bury them properly, and right in the midst of her having just died, what kind of things are you saying to me?'

Kurenaihime: 'That is why men are hopeless creatures... and thus you come to be viewed as persons of caution by women.'

Kurenaihime: 'Heng, how absurd, even if you thought to treat me as a temporary flower and make me a plaything, there is not likely to be even a half-person of such a foolish woman in the wide world.'

Kurenaihime: 'Do not speak of such foolish things.'

Kurenaihime: 'It is also deeply pitiful toward Kurenaihime-sama wa.'

Morris: 'Never, never, we harbor no such cold-hearted feelings, but no matter how much one laments or how much one is impatient, a person who has died once stands to reason not to return.'

Morris: 'If I were to let fall tears and weep, precisely because of that, Kurenaihime's soul would wander through the cosmos, unable to go where it ought to go, and it would pain her to suffer, which would be pitiable.'

Morris: 'Therefore, it is out of kindness, driven to despair, that I thought it best to completely put her out of my mind so that no attachment of the princess remains and she may quickly attain Buddhahood; while shedding tears within my belly, on the surface I am speaking in this neatly cheerful manner, you see.'

Morris: 'Please, comprehend my heart which has parted from the woman I love.'

§ 3

Wiping her tears,

Anonymous: 'A woman who has a man of such profound shintei as her husband must be so, so kaufuku. Erina-sama, if you understand my feelings, please shed a single drop of itsuteki dōjiyō. And to comfort this sorrow of mine, please say in one hitokuchi that you will remain an unchanging fūfu for nise and sanse. If you do that, needless to say, I do not know how much Kurenaihime-sama will rejoice. Ah, it has become sad. Oh, what shall I do?'

She sheds tears on purpose.

Elina smiles coldly,

Elina: 'That is why koshinukeotoko are troublesome. What is that attitude over a mere woman? I have truly grown sick of it. Morris-sama, if you keep walking in itsushiyo everywhere with such a koshinukeotoko, you will be made a baka. Make up your mind to a reasonable kagen and perform great daikuwatsudō. What is this, losing your wits over Kurenaihime-sama, two men fighting for love, and in the end, being smoothly sweet-talked and wrapped around the finger by Akiyamawake—ah, well then, since it is impossible to divide the body of one woman between two men, will you make even Elina your niyōbau as a tauza hanafusa, and endure for a while, and such a selfish and presumptuous calculation—how utterly foolish to have come all the way to tokomade like this, yatsupari. No matter how much of a baka woman Elina may be, do you think she will be used as such a convenient stopgap? Do not make too much of a baka of me. Elina yatsupari has her shiyōnen, too. How much and in what way does she differ from Kurenaihime-sama? Is it not merely that there are highs and lows in the classes of aristocracy and commoners decided by human beings? Wake up to a reasonable kagen and stop such baka things. You do not yet possess the qualifications on your body to talk about women. As I face our parting, Elina will admonish you for the sake of your future.'

Morris panics,

Morris: 'Moshi moshi, did your mind change so suddenly, you? It was not supposed to be like that, yatsupari.'

Elina: 'The Elina in the dream created at your own pleasure was your niyōbau, it seems.'

Morris: 'No, why, far from a dream, I am in deadly earnest. Please do not take it so badly. Please become my niyōbau.'

Elina: 'I utterly detest a koshinukeotoko who begs a woman to become his niyōbau.'

Akiyamawake: 'Hey, hey, Erina-dono, since you are a woman who caught our fancy, Akiyamawake will promote you and make you the wife of my dwelling. Once you are made my niyōbau, this Akiyamawake possesses the magnanimity to look over minor flaws and failures, and to look again, listen again, and proclaim again in accordance with Naorahi Ōnaorahi; truly, Erina-hime-dono must also be happy, ya.'

Elina: 'Oh, stop it. Only hyottoko-faced koshinukeoyaji have wandered into such a place, two of them—how unsightly, have a little sense of shame, you baka. I thought you were a fellow with a bit more wits, but with such extreme koshinukeoyaji, even I have lost my affection. It would be for the sake of society if you threw yourselves into the river and died.'

Akiyamawake and Morris both become angry and resentful,

Akiyamawake and Morris: 'If we let you talk endlessly, hurling insults and abusive language at penniless men of might, we will no longer tolerate this. Two men and one woman. Now, prepare yourselves.'

Clenching their iron fists, they strike from left and right, but Elina parries to the right, avoids to the left, and finally seizing Akiyamawake by the nape of the neck, she plunges him with a splash into the raging current of the Shizun River.

Morris: 'What, insolent!'

Striking with all his might, Elina says, 'Ah, troublesome,' seizes him by the nape of the neck once more, plunges him with a splash toward the raging current, and turning into smoke, she herself disappears from that spot.

The two men are swallowed by the raging current, and even their figures cannot be seen, only the sound of the raging current being heard.

『えゝおきなさいよ。ヒヨツトコ男の腰抜野郎計りが、二人も斯ンな処に迷ひ込ンで来て、アタ態の悪い、いい加減に恥を知りなさい、馬鹿だなア。君もモチと気の利いた男だと思つてゐたのに、余りの腰抜野郎で、僕も愛想がつきた。川の中へなと、身を投げて死ンだ方が、社会の為だらうよ』

秋山別、モリス両人はムツと腹を立て、

『言はしておけば、際限もなく、裸一貫の丈夫に向つて、罵詈雑言、モウ此上は了見致さぬ。二人の男に一人の女だ。サア覚悟せよ』

と鉄拳を固めて左右より打つてかかるを、エリナは右にすかし、左に避け、遂には秋山別の首筋を掴むでシーズン河の激流へザンブと計り投げ込みにけり。

『何、猪口才な』

とモリスは力限りに打つてかかるを、エリナは、『エヽ面倒なり』と又もや首筋を引掴み、激流目がけて、ザンブと計り投げ込み、煙となつて、自分も其場に消え失せにけり。

二人は激流に呑まれ、其姿さへ見えず只激流の音のみ聞へける。
