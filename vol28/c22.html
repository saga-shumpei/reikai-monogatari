Takahime was sitting alone among the crowd of passengers with her cheeks puffed out, but feeling quite unwell, she once again showed her figure on the fine-view deck.

There, both Tsunehiko and Haruhiko were holding hands tightly, singing songs and dancing wildly.

Takahime raised her eyes in anger and said in a loud voice,

Aha-ha-ha-ha.'

Haruhiko: 'Takahime-sama, without making such a stiff face, it is a long journey over the sea. Be a little more carefree. Living in suffering, living in joy, crying, and getting angry are all the same one day. Yay, interesting, interesting, yay Tsunehiko, come on, dance and dance.'

And once again, making strange gestures in a reckless manner, he began to dance the suteko dance. Takahime glared with her eyes and stamped her feet,

Takahime: 'Hey Tsunekō, Harukō, who said they would disown you? Takahime certainly did not say so. That was just the sub-guardian deity possessing you, borrowing my mouth to say such a thing. Carried away by the rhythm and dancing wildly, Haruhiko missed his footing on the deck and fell right down with a splash into the churning waves. Tsunehiko ran back and forth on the deck with a pale face, spinning wildly in a frenzy.

Takahime: 'Hey Tsunehiko, no matter how much you spin around like a top, once you've fallen into these rough waves, there is simply no saving your life. Give up already. That is why, if you are too arrogant, you will writhe and squirm in agony afterwards, and even if you spin around like a top and make a fuss, it will be a post-festival regret; the Divine Writing says that no matter how much you pray to the gods after that, the gods will no longer know you, you see. If it doesn't please you, you'll be thrown away to the gods again like Haruhiko.'

Without paying any attention, his mind frantic with desperation, Tsunehiko cried out at the top of his lungs,

Tsunehiko: 'Please save him—!'

One of the passengers tied a piece of board to a long rope and hurled it forcefully toward the place where Haruhiko was drifting in the waves. Haruhiko quickly bit onto the board. The passenger pulled the rope with all his might and finally rescued Haruhiko onto the ship. Tsunehiko was overjoyed, immediately ran down to the deck, hurried to the side of the passenger who had saved Haruhiko, and shed tears of gratitude from the bottom of his heart. Looking closely, that passenger was Kuniyowake.

Tsunehiko: 'Yay, you are Kuniyowake-sama, how wonderfully you have saved him.'

Takahime runs on with all her might, pointing toward the capital of Teru. The two men, Tsunehiko and Haruhiko, chase after Takahime, now visible and now hidden.

The captain, Tarchil, gives his ship to his son and vice-captain, Terchil, making him captain, and together with Kotonoiwake no Mikoto and Kuniyoriwake, chanting sendenka, they advance into somewhere unknown on Takasago Island. Iwanaki Tamukeba Komasu.
