Chapter 11: Tree 茄子 [811]

木茄子〔811〕

§ 1

Hitate, Tsukihoko, and Yurikohime, three of them, avoiding human eyes and following the mountain ridges, crossed from Mt. Arisu through the Shiyuan mountain ranges, covering themselves with the night garments of blue clouds on stone pillows, and while spending many nights, finally arrived at the valley of Teruna.

At that moment, the sun was hiding behind the mountains, and dusk was drawing near.

Since their journey in the mountains for this past day or two had followed only the tops of the ridge lines, there were no desirable tree nuts, and they were in distress for food, suffering from hunger, unable to walk freely, with their throats parched and no water, being utterly exhausted.

At this time, far off in the valley, weaving through the darkness of dusk, a single mass of firelight was flickering.

The three gained strength from that firelight, and dragging their tired feet, proceeded onward.

Centered around the firelight, many disgusting-looking hairy warrior men, numbering in the tens and completely naked, were laughing and clamoring loudly about something.

The three laid their bodies down in the shade of a tree, moistened their throats with the valley water for a while, and as they were resting their breaths, there was something that brushed against the heads of the three, shaken by the valley wind blowing swiftly. Tsukihoko unexpectedly raised a hand above the head, and the moment it touched, what came into hand was a tree eggplant dripping with water.

The three rejoiced with courage as if given by heaven, plucked the tree eggplant on the spot, and filled their bellies.

With the fatigue of the long journey, their bellies were filled, drowsiness was suddenly induced, and all three fell into a sound sleep innocently right on that spot.

Today was the grand festival day of Brahmanism in the village of Teruna, and it was the midst of preparations where many villagers gathered and were discussing various plans to hold a night festival.

To offer it to today's festival, the only tree eggplant of its kind in this village had been carefully preserved without a single one being plucked until now.

That was entirely plucked and eaten up by the three persons.

When the time of the festival finally arrived, four or five villagers clad in white garments, waving hemp wands, and chanting the sacred songs of Brahmanism, came to pluck the precious tree eggplant to make it an offering, but when they looked, the precious tree eggplant had been stolen by someone, and not a single one remained on the branches.

While astonished, when the whole group peered around, three men and women were sleeping nearby, snoring like thunder.

The villagers suddenly raised a commotion, brought lit torches, and when they looked closely, one man was holding a tree eggplant in one hand, having bitten about half of it, and was sleeping soundly.

Kō: 'Hey, hey all you fellows, a terrible fellow has come out, has taken and eaten all the tree eggplant for the divine offerings, swollen his belly, and is sleeping soundly without a care, isn't he? What an outrageous fellow. ...Hey someone, report this matter to the chieftain of Teruna as soon as possible. If you don't do that, we will be suspected of having taken and eaten it, and there is no telling what kind of fierce scolding we will get from the chieftain.'

One of them replied 'Hey,' and ran off like Idaten towards the sacrificial ground where the crowd gathered in order to report to the chieftain.

Kō, tapping the heads of the three repeatedly with the tip of his staff,

Kō: 'Hey there, you audacious arch-thief, will you not wake up quickly?'

he roared.

When the three woke up in astonishment and looked around, four or five men holding torches, together with a man dressed in white garments, were glaring at them with demon-like faces, peeling back round chestnut eyes, and trembling their lips violently.

Hitate: 'What persons are you to... strike the heads of us three with the tip of a staff, with such discourtesy beyond measure... what is the meaning of this?'

Before he could even finish speaking, Kō stretched out his hairy arm and grasped Hitate's nape with a tight grip.

Hitate's complexion turned pale from the pain as if his neck were being torn off, and he was suffering with his lips turned purple.

The big man raised his voice,

Big Man: 'You fellows are likely propagandists of Aisankyo. Today is the grand festival day of the Great Deity of Brahmanism; the villagers had treasured it to make it the offering for today's festival, but you have taken and eaten every last tree eggplant, and slumbered in unceremoniousness beyond measure in this sacred ground—such bold and fearless behavior of yours, just wait, the chieftain is coming to this place presently, and there will be some judgment or other for you.'

Having said this, he released the hand that had been gripping the neck of Hitate. Hitate, due to the excessive pain, could not even speak, and just clung to the earth, enduring the agony.

Tsukihoko bowed respectfully to them all, placing both hands on the ground.

Tsukihoko: 'We three are by no means missionaries of the Ananai-kyō. We are pilgrims visiting the sacred sites of the Brahman religion. Having lost our way in the deep mountains, and while suffering from hunger, we spotted a single point of light in the valley. Relying on that, we came out as far as this place. While we were resting, the Kinasubi happened to touch our heads. Not knowing it was such a precious object, and having no intention of stealing it, we took it. It is truly an inexcusable matter. Please, we ask you to intercede with the chief on our behalf.'

Kō: 'Today is the great festival of the Brahman religion, and it is a vital day on which we must offer human sacrifice. However, the chief, thinking it truly cruel to take human life, prayed to the Lord God, saying that he would offer this Kinasubi, of which there is only one in this valley, as a substitute for a human, and begged that the human sacrifice be spared. Since then, this fruit has been treated as the property of the God; the villagers have not even pointed a finger at it, and have kept it guarded with night watches. Now that you three have foolishly eaten it, there is no help for it. There must still be some remaining in your stomachs. You must immediately cut open your bellies, scoop out the Kinasubi, offer your bodies as a sacrifice to the God, and appease the God's wrath. All of you, you had better prepare yourselves.'

Tsukihoko: 'That is a terrible thing indeed. However, it is the heart of a God to save people; a God who takes human life or rejoices in having sacrifices offered to it cannot be a true God. Though we are unworthy, we shall try to give that God a word of admonition.'

Kō: 'What, are you saying something foolish? For a human to give an admonition to a God is an act of extreme insolence. If you say such things, you will incur the wrath of the God, and this village of Teruna will suffer from the failure of crops and from typhoons and great floods. You are truly an incorrigible fool.'

Saying this, he swung his staff with all his might and struck the three of them until their spines were nearly broken.

After a while, James, the chief of the village of Teruna, appeared on the scene with four or five attendants carrying sharp blue dragon swords. Seeing the three of them, he said in a harsh voice.

James: 'You are great criminals who have eaten a precious fruit; I cannot forgive you as things stand. You three must now become a sacrifice to the God to appease the God's wrath. Come, prepare yourselves.'

He gave the order. Yurikohime clasped her hands together, inched toward James, raised her sorrowful face, and shed tears.

Yurikohime: 'I beg you, as it was done in ignorance, please overlook this matter just this once.'

The moment James saw Yurikohime's face, his expression softened at once.

James: 'Although you are unforgivable criminals, if you agree to become my wife, I will spare your life alone. How is it... are you grateful?'

He peered into Yurikohime's face with a somewhat relaxed expression.

Yurikohime: 'Please, not only me, but please spare the lives of the two men as well. If you will grant that, I will respond to any of your demands.'

Chief: 'No, that cannot be. In any case, I must take the life of one person and make them a sacrifice. Your husband must be among them. I will spare your husband for your sake.'

Hitate: 'James, or whatever your name is, even if we ate the fruit of the tree in ignorance, where is the reason for you to take our lives? If you want to take our lives, try taking them if you can.'

James opened his mouth wide,

"Aha, ha, ha, ha,"

and laughing loudly while doing so,

James: 'Thou, howsoever great thy divine power may be, surrounded as thou art by this vast multitude even with a mere two or three, canst do nothing at all. Prepare thy resolve properly in a wondrous manner and become a sacrifice.'

Yurikohime: 'Pardon me, Chief-sama, that is my husband. And the other one is my husband's younger brother. I will humbly accept whatever may happen. In exchange, I beg you to spare the lives of the two.'

James: 'Ah, it cannot be helped. What thou, so kaai, sayest cannot be refused flatly either. If so, I shall spare at least their lives. Today is the grand festival of Brahmanism; let both of you cross through raging fire, pass over the bridge of swords, put on nail-studded ashida, and become matsupadaka to creep through the thicket of thorns. This is the path of apology to the first kami of Brahmanism. Considering that your lives would be taken, this is an easy thing. We make such practices an annual event and do not consider them particularly bitter at all. If you too are pilgrims, you should be able to endure this much of training.'

Both of them at once,

"We have understood. If only our lives are spared, whatever training... we will gladly perform."

James: 'Already the time of the festival is approaching. Come quickly hither. Then, naked and barefoot, cross through the raging fire and dispel the anger of the kami.'

Thus having a vast multitude guard the three before and behind, he led them to the sacrificial ground.

When they arrived at the sacrificial ground and looked, numerous fruits were decorated before the kami like small mountains, and in the open courtyard ahead, withered firewood was piled like a mountain; when fire was set to this, it blazed furiously in such a terrifying manner. The two were thrown in by the crowd and leaped inevitably into the midst of the fire. Singing the heavenly number songs with all their might, without suffering even the slightest burn amidst the fierce flames, they circled round and round countless times and returned to their original place. The whole assembly was dumbfounded by their divine power and gazed at the faces of the two.

Yurikohime was given gorgeous garments as the sudden bride of the chief and stood side by side with the chief on the sacrificial altar. Suddenly, from nowhere, a mass of firelight came flying and exploded right here, and James's body was swept up into midair and vanished. The whole assembly was dumbfounded by this, running about in utter confusion to and fro, or losing the strength in their hips, while some had even their facial color turn purplish and were groaning in a state between life and death.

Yurikohime cast off her gorgeous garments on the spot and immediately threw them into the fire, and together with Hinodewake and Tsukihoko, the three stood before the altar, chanted the norito of gratitude and prayer, and began to sing the propaganda song.

Yurikohime: 'Though the morning sun shines or clouds over, Though the moon waxes or wanes, Even if the great earth should sink, The power of sincerity saves the world. The kami appear in the front And separate good and evil. The kami who created this world is Oinaobi, With a heart vast as Oinaobi. Simply, whatever happens in the world of men, Look straight and see anew, listen straight and hear anew, Confess and rectify the errors of your body. The people under the heaven Are all precious children of the kami. Human bodies, weak in strength, How could they fail to make mistakes without erring? The Brahmans, ignorant of sincerity, James, the official of the kami, And those who follow him, How pitiful their dark hearts! To take the lives of precious people And offer them as sacrifices Before the presence of the kami, What kind of matter is this? If indeed the kami Desire sacrifices, then the Brahman Ōkugahiko is Magatsuhahi. Before the presence of Tatsuyohime, May the official of the Sanju teachings, My husband who bore the names Of the sun and moon shining in heaven, The deity of Hinodewake and the deity of Tsukihoko, Make all the people of the land, without leaving a single one, Obey the precious teachings, And transform the dark and lawless world Into a heavenly pure land! Ah, wondrous kami, kami, Grant us the blessings of the divine spirits!'

ユリコ姫は美々しき衣裳を矢庭に脱ぎ捨て直ちに火中に投じ、日楯、月鉾と共に三人祭壇の前に立ち、感謝祈願の祝詞を奏上し、宣伝歌を歌ひ始めたり。

§ 4

Thus she finished her prayer. Before they knew it, James the chieftain, who had been whirled up into the mid-heaven, was now arrayed in ceremonial attire, and together with four or five attendants, he brought forth rare fruits, presented them respectfully before the three of them,

James: 'I am James, the chieftain of this village whom you saw a moment ago. I have uttered impertinent words toward you precious ones. For the crime of rudeness in proposing such difficult challenges—such as asking the living goddess to become the wife of one as lowly as we, or to perform the fire-crossing, though it was most presumptuous—please forgive me. All this was spoken entirely in strict obedience to the precepts of Brahmanism. The only thing for which I cannot forgive myself, even from the standpoint of Brahmanism, is the grave sin of having asked the precious goddess to become my wife. Because of that, I incurred the wrath of Lord Daijizaiten, and a great fire-bullet of warning was hurled down from the heavens. At that very moment, I was whirled up into the air, and I had resigned myself to the belief that my life was already at an end. However, thanks to the profound divine protection of one known as the Kunitama-shin-Ryūsehime-sama, my precious life was saved, and moreover, I received various admonitions. Therefore, leaving behind everything, I have come in haste to offer my apologies to you. Please, with this staff, beat me anywhere upon my body until your stomach is full, and I believe a part of my sins will thus be atoned for. I humbly beg of you.'

shedding hot tears, he pleaded from the depths of his sincerity. The numerous villagers who had fled scattered about gradually gathered again, and while feeling suspicious as to how not a single one of them had suffered any injury, seeing this attitude of the chieftain, the entire assembly pressed their palms together toward the three, expressing feelings of reverence as though toward gods, and prostrated themselves in worship.

The three patiently explained and expounded the doctrines of Izanagi-no-Mikoto's teaching, and escorted by the chieftain and dozens of others, after several days, they finally arrived at the port of Kiel.
