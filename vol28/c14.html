Chapter 14: 二男三女 [814]

二男三女〔814〕

§ 1

Shining across Amatsumisora, Hitate and Tsukihoko, the two, Teruhikōu and Terukohime, parting from amata people, leaving the castle of Sawara behind, Yurikohime of the flower-blooming field of green, crimson, white, and kiiro, Yachiyohime of the endless miyo, Teruyohime of the divine miizu, the fivemitama of the ninansanniyo, taking the secret letter of Teruhikōu as their strength, advance toward the high peak of Kōyō.

The ninansanniyo proceed silently, heading toward Kōyōzan. In the secret letter given by Teruhikōu:

'Until the bank of the Ōtanigaha flowing at the foot of Kōyōzan, you must by no means utter gengo. As you cross that river, hatsugen shall be jiiu. Tsunekususennin permanently resides on Kōyōzan and shall grant to all of you the sword of setsuju and the sword of shakubuku. Receiving these, you must head for Taian Castle as quickly as a day, subdue the demonic army with kototama, and save Princess Yachiyoin, Madōhiko, King Castle, and all the others.'

Such was the purport shown. Going almost five or six ri, feeling somewhat tired from walking on foot, it seemed utterly difficult to reach the mountain foot within the day.

On the boundless wild field of kaya, bush clovers, bellflowers, and lilies bloom and fragrance nicely. Parting the grass, the ninansanniyo at last drew near to a point where even the treetops at the foot of Kōyōzan could be discerned with the naked eye. Suddenly, right in front, a large marsh lies across. The water bottom is extremely deep, the surrounding trees cast their reflections upside down at the bottom of the marsh, and the pale clouds of the open sky are reflected at the marsh bottom. Mysteriously, the figures of the five are reflected upside down in the marsh water, making an indescribably beautiful sight. The whole company bumped straight into it and were lost in thought as to what to do, but because they were forbidden to utter gengo, they could not consult with one another, and are talking over what they should do by means of gestures, eye movements, and the like.

The sun gradually sank its figure upon the ridge of the mountain, the evening breeze began to blow softly, and the shadows swaying at the tips of the tree leaves, reflected upside down on the marsh bottom, came to look as though numerous small fish were leaping. The five, whose advance and retreat were equally blocked, thoroughly resolved themselves, and set out bustlingly toward the bottomless marsh. Mysteriously, notwithstanding this deep marsh, the bodies of the five did not even sink as far as their knees, and easily they crossed over the surface of the so vast marsh to the further shore. When they looked back and gazed, there was not even the shadow of a marsh, and various wild flowers were blooming full across the wide wilderness. This was because Tsunekususennin employed sennjutsu and projected a mirror of the earth in order to test the faith power of the five.

The curtain of night was gently lowered, the moon, separated by the surrounding high mountains, showed no figure, and the light of the stars flickered somehow low and beautiful like a sky of rainy weather. The ninansanniyo made signals with their customary gestures, and it came to pass that they would spend a night here.

The roaring voices of fierce beasts resound higher and more violently moment by moment from front and back, left and right. Within their hearts, the company praise the heavenly sunka and continue dark prayer and silent devotion. Thither an unusual kind of great monster, wearing upon its head things about one jo in branch-antlers like a deer, and a great animal like a great elephant, comes marching crushingly, making its giant eyes like mirrors shine before the five, opening its wide mouth, and swinging its long wide tongue like a laundry stretching board from side to side, and is attempting to enwrap the party, aiming at them with its tongue. Hitate and Tsukihoko, remaining silent, clasp both hands and draw near before the monster. As they do so, the monster, just as an elephant rolls up a child with its trunk, licked and wrapped them up one by one with its tongue, and swallowed both of them together into its throat at once.

The three women firmly consolidated their resolve and left everything to the doing of the monster. As before, the monster wrapped them up one by one with its tongue and lifted them upon its back, and with all three of them riding upon its great back—which seemed countless times larger than a great elephant—it advances toward Kōyōzan, making the earth rumble with heavy thuds. Both Hitate and Tsukihoko, while being swallowed into the belly of the monster, felt no particular pain, but felt as if they had entered a warm bath, and left their fate to Munakata.

三人の女は愈決心を固め怪物のなすが儘に任した。怪物は以前の如く、舌にて一人一人捲いては吾背に乗せあげ、三人共大象の幾倍とも知れぬ様な大背中に乗せた儘、向陽山を目蒐けてドシンドシンと地響きさせ乍ら進んで行く。日楯、月鉾の両人は怪物の腹に呑まれ乍ら、別に痛苦も感ぜず、暖かき湯に入りたる如き心地して、運命を惟神に任せて居た。

§ 2

Suddenly, a roaring sound of water entered their ears, and even as they thought so, the surroundings suddenly grew vividly bright. Looking around, he found that his body had crossed the torrential rapids of the Ōtanigaha at the foot of Kōyōsanroku, and was standing upon its bank. The three women were gazing at the raging torrent from the other side of the bank, looking with envy upon the two who had successfully crossed over to the mountain base.

Hitate opened his mouth first,

Kamunagara tama saki hahe mase

saying thus,

Hitate: 'Tsukihoko-san, even the practice of silence has been quite a bitter ordeal, hasn't it ah. Moreover, the shock to my heart when we encountered the swamp of Chikiyō, and before we could even feel at ease for a moment, we next encountered a great monster, were swallowed into its belly wrapped by its tongue, and I had been worrying about what would become of us; yet before we knew it, the shadow of the monster was gone, and the two of us had safely crossed this impassable raging torrent—no matter how I think of it, does it not fail to make sense? Come now, we cannot afford to be heedless. No matter what is said, this is the sacred ground where Tsunetsune Sennin is in seclusion, so we must be reverent beyond being reverent, for we cannot bear to be subjected to such trials many times over, ah.'

Tsukihoko: 'That is so. Ever since we crossed over to this ball island, truly, there have been nothing but wondrous things; it is a mystical island, ah. Be that as it may, Prince Teruhiko and Princess Teruko have shown us in their imperial instruction to go and meet the sennin to obtain the Sword of Shōju and the Sword of Shakubuku, but I wonder if they will indeed be granted to us; that alone worries me incessantly, wa.'

Hitate: 'Prince Teruhiko, in order to make us perform this service, previously climbed some high mountain with his consort, underwent extraordinary hardships, and returned after receiving a divine oracle, so there is scarcely any likelihood of a mistake. To doubt will only become the reason for further impairing the divine will. At any rate, believing firmly in the very words of the instruction, no matter what hardships and sufferings we encounter henceforth, without yielding and without breaking, strengthening our patience, and achieving our purpose, we would have lived in vain to have come all this way in the distant journey. First, let us offer the Amatsu Norito here.'

Tsukihoko also nodded to these words, and the two sat properly on the riverbank and offered the Amatsu Norito.

Wondrously, the three women came calmly crossing over the raging torrent to this side. The two clapped their hands in joy, and once again offered a prayer of thanksgiving for the deep divine protection. The three barely crossed the torrent, came before the two, and while letting smiles brim over with delight, beckoning the two with their hands, they climbed up the steep slope just as a monkey traverses tree branches. In the twinkling of an eye, the figures of the three women were enveloped in the mountain mists and vanished from sight.

The sun and moon [Hitate and Tsukihoko] looked into each other's faces,

Hitate: 'How is it, Tsukihoko-san, in the divine realm of immortals, mystical occurrences continue to break out one after another, ah.'

Tsukihoko: 'Truly, there are nothing but wondrous things, wa. Still, the speed of those three women's feet cannot be thought of as human work. For the most part, perhaps the souls of sennin have even possessed them, ah.'

Hitate: 'Be that as it may, we must finish climbing this high mountain without a single moment's delay. Come, let us hurry.'

Hitate took the lead, singing a propaganda song as he climbed.

Mount Kōyō is a sacred mountain that soars majestically amidst overlapping mountain peaks. Until reaching the summit, one must cross mountains whose exact numbers are unknown, traverse valleys, or cross broad lakes within the mountains; it is an impregnable and supreme scenic spot that cannot possibly be attained otherwise.

The two finally arrived at the bank of a somewhat broad lake in the mountains.

Suddenly, a woman's scream echoed through the surrounding forest. When they abruptly gazed in the direction of the voice, a giant serpent of unknown length had swallowed the body of Princess Yuriko up to around her waist, raising its sickle-like neck as it coiled. Hitate and Tsukihoko were greatly startled, and for a short while, as they wondered what should be done, they tilted their heads in hesitation. Princess Yuriko cried out at the top of her voice,

Princess Yuriko: 'Lord Hitate, please, save me.'

Folding his hands, he was crying out at the top of his voice. Once again came the weeping of a woman; when he hurriedly turned his eyes to the right, Teruyohime and Yachiyohime were likewise being swallowed halfway by two great serpents, the color of their faces having turned pale, scarcely able to raise their voices, pressing their hands together toward the two persons, and seeking salvation.

Hitate was torn in his course, wondering whether he should save his own wife, what he should do with Yachiyohime and Teruyohime, whether he should save Yachiyohime and Teruyohime, and what he should do with the life of his wife. Tsukihoko let out a single cry of 'Uhn' along with the voice of the final agony, and fell down on the spot, falling into a state of unconsciousness. Hitate thought that while his younger brother in the present was thus and his wife's life was also approaching in an instant, and while he was most eager to save them, to first save Yachiyohime and Teruyohime must surely be the path that a human being ought to follow; and sooner than breaking a thick square stake fallen and scattered about the vicinity, he struck with all his might around the back of the neck toward the great serpents that were swallowing Yachiyohime and Teruyohime. When he looked, there was neither the shadow of the great serpents nor the figure of the women, and only Tsukihoko had fallen at his feet.

Hitate: 'How strange!'

When he looked around, an old man of strange white hair, leaning upon an arrowhead-root staff, was approaching at a slow, shuffling pace while parting the thickets of trees. Hitate immediately took the water of the lake into his mouth and poured it over the face of Tsukihoko. Tsukihoko gradually recovered his sanity and looked around restlessly. Tsukihoko had collapsed because he had been startled by seeing the figures of Yurikohime and two others being swallowed by the great serpents.

The old man of strange white hair turned toward the two persons, and while floating a smile, beckoning to them, and unlike his aged body, he climbed swiftly aiming for the summit of Mount Kōyō as if soaring through the clouds. The two followed behind the old man, panting for breath, and hurried to climb as far as their legs could carry them.

The figure of the old man had already vanished into the white clouds enveloping Mount Kōyō. When the two climbed with all their might for about a ri, there was a great rock face they abruptly bumped into. When they looked closely, this rock shone brilliantly in the sunlight like a mirror, and the figures of the three women were clearly reflected deep within. The three daughters were looking at the figures of the two and beckoning for them to come quickly. The distance was scarcely about two hundred ken. Even though the two brothers hurried to go to the side of the three, this rock transparent like a mirror had no discernible entrance, and they were extremely worried.

Yurikohime and the other two eagerly beckoned, saying, 'Come quickly...' The brothers became impatient in their hearts and tried to advance and enter, but they bumped against the glass-like rock, and no matter how they struggled, the entrance could not be found. At this time, a voice was heard from high above their heads saying, 'Amatsu Norito'.... The two were startled to awareness, immediately clapped their hands, and began solemnly reciting the Amatsu Norito with a fresh voice.

As if sucked in by some entity, the bodies of the two naturally advanced and entered into the transparent cavern. Just as a great roar loud enough to shatter the mountains was heard coming, more than a dozen black great serpents, each about a jō in circumference, their belly scales festering with blood, advanced fiercely toward this cavern, and though they opened their wide mouths and fretted to swallow the brothers, because the entrance could not be found, the serpents lined up their heads outside with an air of deep regret and gazed at the figures of the two men and three women.

While deeply thanking the divine grace in their hearts, the two men and three women advanced further and further into the depths. Thinking with a slight sense of terror whether the serpents might find the entrance to the endless cavern and pursue them from behind, before they knew it their feet carried them unusually fast, and at last they reached the terminus of the cavern. Here, the old man of strange white hair who had appeared previously stood revealed in stern dignity, and speaking solemnly to all, he said,

Old Man: 'I am Tsunakusu Sennin, who, centering upon this mountain, protects the twin islands of Ryūkyū. It is truly the height of admirable conduct that you have come all this way in order to save the trials of your father and further rescue the hardships of the king and numerous people. From this moment onward, leave this island as quickly as possible, board a ship from the port of Eru, and together with Teruyohime and Yachiyohime, return toward the port of Kīru. Moreover, as for the divine treasures that should be bestowed upon you, since they are at the entrance of this cavern, take possession of one of them according to your spirit-body and return. Farewell.'

ユリコ姫外二人は頻りに早く来れ……と差招く。兄弟は心をいらち、進み入らむとすれ共、硝子の如き岩に突当つて、入口がどう藻掻いても分らない。此時頭の上の方から『天津祝詞』……と云ふ声が聞えて来た。二人はハツと気がつき、直に拍手し、天津祝詞を声もすがすがしく奏上し始めた。

二人の身体は何者にか吸ひ込まるる様に、透明なる岩窟の中に自然に進み入つた。忽ち山嶽も崩るる計りの大音響聞え来ると見る間に、周囲一丈計りの黒色の大蛇、腹の鱗は血にただれ乍ら、十数匹、此岩窟に向つて勢猛く進み来り、兄弟を呑まむと、大口を開けて焦れども、入口の分らざる為、大蛇は外にて残念相に頭を並べて二男三女の姿を眺めて居る。

二男三女は心中に深く神徳を感謝し乍ら、尚も奥へ奥へと進み入る。際限もなき岩窟をもしや蛇の入口を探り、後より追ひ来らざるやと、稍恐ろしさに、知らず知らずに足は意外に早く運びて、終に岩窟の終点に着いた。茲には以前現はれし白髪異様の老人が厳然として立現はれ、一同に向ひ言葉おごそかに、

老人『われは当山を中心として琉、球の夫婦島を守護致す常楠仙人であるぞ。其方は父の難を救ひ且つ国王を始め、数多の人々の苦難を救はむ為に遥々此処に来ること、実に殊勝の至りである。汝等は之より一刻も早く此島を離れ、エルの港より船に乗り、照代姫、八千代姫と諸共に、キールの港に向つて立帰れよ。又汝に与ふべき神宝は、此岩窟の入口にあれば、身魂相応に其一個を所持して帰れよ。さらば』

§ 4

Having said thus, the figure of the old man turned into smoke and vanished completely. The five persons here once again chanted the Amatsunorito, and wondering anxiously whether the Orochi had returned to the entrance of the cave from which they had originally set out, they at last looked out of the entrance, where two jewels and three mirrors were placed. These were the treasures possessed by the Orochi that had attacked them earlier, but having felt the sentiment of renbo upon beholding the forms of Ninansanniyo, it had finally escaped from its tainai unknowingly and returned after coming back.

Hitate took the red jewel associated with the color of the sun, Tsukihoko picked up the white jewel, and Yurikohime, Yachiyohime, and Teruyohime each picked up one of the three radiant mirrors of daichiushō sizes, held them up high, and descended Kōyōzan while chanting the heavenly kazūta along the way.

At last they arrived at the bank of the Ōtanigaha. Such a rapid torrent did not look as though it could be crossed easily. Yurikohime took out the large mirror from her bosom and shone it upon the surface of the river. Miraculously, the water of the Ōtanigaha parted sideways as if dammed up by a board, and a path without a single drop of water appeared. When Ninansanniyo hurried across the middle of the river to the other side and looked back, the Ōtanigaha had once again become a rushing torrent of fierce pools and splashing foam. From this time on, the company hastened their steps, arrived at the port of El after three days and three nights, and entrusted their bodies to a moored boat. Hitate and Tsukihoko worked the oars, easily rowed across the raging ocean, and safely arrived at the port of Kiel around midnight.

From this time, Ninansanniyo temporarily returned to the sacred site of Tamamozan, enshrined the jewels and mirrors, and prayed day and night, waiting for the divine decree of Tatsuyohime-no-Mikoto to descend so that they might commence great activities; thus they devoted themselves to prayer day and night.

Ninansanniyo obtained the divine treasures of the regalia mirrors, and full of high spirits, without stopping by the sacred sites of Amamine and Tamine, they returned straight to the sacred site of Tamamozan, which was the central sacred ground. Welcomed by their remaining sincere believers, they deposited the treasures of the regalia deep within the palace and held a banquet of joyous triumphal return.

Mariyasuhime, accompanied by Terinhime, joyfully joined this gathering, praised the achievements of Ninansanniyo to the utmost, and in order to express her festive sentiments before the gods, she began to sing and dance by herself. Her song:

Mariyasuhime: 'The heavenly fortune has now revolved hither, Even upon withered trees flowers bloom, It has truly become a noble reign. In the days of the age of the gods, Kuninotokotachi-no-Ōkami, In the Land of Luxuriant Reed Plains and Abundant Rice Ears, Reputed as the central point, In the capital of honor called Jerusalem, Creating all things in the universe With infinite, absolute, without beginning and without end, Amaterasu Ōmikami, Whose other name is the great divine decree of Kuninotokotachi-no-Ōkami, Receiving the august divine decree, In the Land of Luxuriant Reed Plains and Abundant Rice Ears, Desiring to promulgate the divine government Of the divine country of the heavenly expanse, While breaking her heart into a thousand pieces, Endeavoring extensively for the bodies and souls Of heaven, earth, gods, and men, Even those divine works, like a running horse fleeting by, Were obstructed by the ferocity of crookedness. Though possessing an august divine body, Abandoning this world towards the northeast, Hideshuma Country of the rotating inverted island, Changing his name to Kunimukehiko, With the brilliant brocade of the lower foundations, Wishing to establish a bright age of the gods Where the color of autumn leaves is also crimson, Declaring the Kototama of Hifumi, Forever within the mountains of Shio, Concealing the five august spirits, Waiting for the age of the gods to come in a miraculous manner, How venerable it is. The jade camellia of the spring of eight thousand ages, Blooming forth with nine flowers, Building up the age of the gods of ten, Bestowing the dew of grace Upon the myriad common people, In the age of pines of the noble reign, The heart of the gods is eternal, Where the princess of Tatsuyo peacefully resides. Making Hanamorihiko-no-Mikoto descend Upon this divine island, and manifesting actual events, Entrusting the mysterious affairs to Michihiko-no-Mikoto, The divine work is such that The boulder of a thousand paces remains unmoved, The color of the everlasting pine never fades, Having appeared here now, The calculations of the age of pines By Kuninotokotachi-no-Ōkami Have now become the age that opens. Michihiko-no-Mikoto was cast Into the prison within the dark cave Where even black and white cannot be distinguished, And though he tasted hardships day and night, The rock door of heaven, when its time comes, Is the custom of the age that opens suddenly. The sincere hearts of both Hitate and Tsukihoko, Who have appeared in the Tamagawa, Have manifested, And their divine majesty is also guided here by the divine teachings of Teruhiko-no-Ōkami, Climbing up Kōyōzan, According to the meticulous arrangements Of Tsunekusennin who comprehended The miraculous divine arts of divine transformations, The jewels and mirrors of the dragon's jaws Were bestowed upon Ninansanniyo safely According to their respective bodies and souls. That they return and come back To the sacred site of Tamamozan, Floating in the midst of Tamagawa Where divine virtue shines brightly, Is indeed the opening of the flower of the teachings of Sankyō, And Mariyasuhime, even today, Rising up in high spirits from the bottom of her heart, Praising the sincere heart With which the divine men of the five august spirits Have exerted themselves for the sake of the nation, And exalting them—how joyous it is! Ah, Kannagara kamugami kamugami, May your august spirits prosper and bless us!'

Having finished singing and dancing, she returned to her original seat.
