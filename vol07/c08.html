Chapter 8: The Pine of the Feathered Robe [308]

第8章　羽衣の松〔308〕

§ 1

The large ship carrying Hinode-no-Kami rowed out from the bay of Kumano and, raising its true sails to the favorable wind that happened to blow, advanced toward the east.

So high was the mountain of the Nine Mountains and Eight Seas of Tenkyō, casting its reflection upon the waves of the Tōkai like a white folding fan hung upside down, in peaceful tranquility. Continuing day and night, the wave-path advanced far into Tōtōmi. Suddenly the waves rose up toward heaven, and the ship drifted like a leaf in peril. All the passengers, praying to the gods when all else failed, clapped their hands individually toward the God of the Vast Ocean, offering earnest prayers in Suruga Bay. Before anyone knew, Mount Tenkyō had become enveloped in clouds.

Seeking to escape this adverse wind, aiming toward the pine grove of Miho and the ancient pine tree of the heavenly feathered robe in the distance, the ship at last reached the shore. The faces of the party were pale as if they were dead, and they had lost even the courage to stand.

Hinode-no-Kami landed first, and the people followed, barely escaping with their lives to reach this island of white sands and green pines; though they breathed a sigh of relief, the wind grew ever fiercer and the waves raged so furiously that the Pine of the Feathered Robe was on the verge of being submerged in water.

Hinode-no-Kami and the many people who had been rescued onto this island ran up to a slightly elevated place on the island, and just as they were waiting for the sea to calm, exquisite music was heard from the heavens, making various fragrant flowers fall, truly as if a carpet of flowers had been spread all over.

After a short while, two deities, male and female, descended to this place riding upon a cloud, and nodding to Hinode-no-Kami, raised their fluent voices and began to dance to the song of the Heavenly Maiden's Dance.

"Ah, this Mount Fuji of Suruga, the pride of this world! Even if in this world wickedness Should pile up higher than Mount Ashitaka, Let it pile up; by the august light Of the divine Konohana-hime Who dwells on Mount Tenkyō, The world shines bright like a thin robe; Wearing the heavenly robe of feathers, The land of fresh rice ears shall dance to an end, Praising it as an auspicious land for a thousand, nay, eight thousand generations, A divine land governed and pure. Even though dense clouds block all four directions, With a red, sincere heart, Uphold the true path straight and firm, Fulfill the truth, O sincere Propagator of the Sunrise of God. Though wild winds rage and roar, Though waves rise steep and rough, Our Land of the Rising Sun is a divine land, So long as Konohana-hime abides therein.

The pine grove of Miho of the divine age, Where the world is eternally at ease; The pines too add their thousand-year hue, Mihotsuhiko adding green, And Mihotsuhime now here Appear and come forth to protect Thy future with purity. With the protection Of the divine Awanagi And the divine Awanami, Cross peacefully over the vast ocean Unto the Land of Tokoyo. The evil deity that appeared on the Ural Mountains Has now departed from Hinodedake In the Land of Tokoyo, And comes again to assault the august land. Now is the very midst of the divine plan, Now is the very midst of the divine plan. Under the divine protection of Awanagihiko and Awanami, Flourishing vigorously, Set forth swiftly, O Propagator!

Set forth swiftly, O Propagator!"

Thus they sang with refreshing voices, and while dancing in midair, concealed their forms toward Mount Tenkyō.

These two deities, Awanagi and Awanami, are the divided spirits of Mihotsuhiko and Mihotsuhime who had just appeared. From this time, Hinode-no-Kami endured various hardships and at last crossed over again to the Land of Tokoyo.
