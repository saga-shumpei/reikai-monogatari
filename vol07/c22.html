Chapter 22: Treasures of the Dragon Palace [322]

第22章　竜宮の宝〔322〕

§ 1

Hinode-no-kami commanded Ihiyorihiko to thickly plant the shrine pillars and enshrine in peace the mitama of Masumihime, the kunitama of Ryūgū-jō; and here, accompanied by Hahurihime, Tsuranagi, Ama-no-kuhizamochi, and Kuni-no-kuhizamochi, hoisting sails to a favorable wind, what bravery as they advanced toward the southwest. Beginning with Ihiyorihiko, Kukunokami and Kukinoshi stood on the wharf and reluctantly saw this ship off. Opening a white folding fan, Ihiyorihiko saw this party off while singing.

"Setting up the shrine pillars in Takamanohara, With crossbeams soaring high, eternally Resides Izanagi-no-kami-no-mikoto, And Konohanahime-no-miko-no-mikoto's teachings Having made and consolidated, at the foot Of the golden mountain having appeared, The Great God of the Anai teachings' Doctrines to the four directions, of Shikishima The propagators pure of heart. Even should the world turn to eternal darkness, Shining with divine authority in the vast sky, Hinode-no-kami and Hahurihime, And the other three divine persons Depart on their journey for the Everlasting Land. My heart seeing them off, O wind, be calm, be calm; and rain, Do not fall, and waves be smooth, Go forth in utmost peace, Illumined by the waves of divine virtue.

The darkness of the heart completely cleared away, Bearing even the auspicious name Of Ihiyorihiko-no-mikoto, the noble name, The island guard of the everlasting and firm Ryūgū, Entrusted by you, Serving the shrine of the maiden deity Whose heart is pure, spreading the Anai's Pure teachings far and near, Devoting heart and body, Permit me to serve, O Hinode-no-kami. The regrets of parting are endless upon the waves; Go forth in utmost peace, Cross over in tranquil peace."

Thus he sang instead of a greeting. When he was called Tayorihiko until now, his soul had been vulgar, and even in singing a single poem, it bore a rustic nature; but now, having received the divine name of Ihiyorihiko, and being moved by the divine virtue of Masumihime-no-kami, the mitama who was the guardian deity of this island, that he was able to sing such an elegant and poised poem must be proof that his soul had progressed.

Kukunokami again sang a poem.

"Time is something that one must await; The time has come, has come, for Tokihiko To stop even the sake he loves. Thanks to time, Tokihiko too Caught the eye of the living deities Who descended from heaven, and as Kukunokami, Following Ihiyorihiko, Will serve Princess Masumi well, And surely protect this island. Afterward, my heart with the ship in the offing, Gazing upon the speeding mast, I shall worship until it disappears. Please be safe and in good health, And cross over this sea. There will also be a time when we meet again; When we meet, that will be the decisive moment; Between the two of us, Yoshihiko And I who cannot be parted, like a clamping nail. Never worry, O noble Hinode-no-kami!

§ 2

"And other noble messengers, With this we bid you farewell,"

so singing and dancing with open fans, they saw this ship off. Kukinokami said,

"I cannot sing; I shall bid you farewell by dancing,"

and murmuring something in a low voice deep in his mouth, he stomped the ground with a rumble, sweating profusely, and danced wildly with amusing motions of hands and feet. Both the people aboard the ship and the many divine beings seeing them off burst into loud laughter all at once. Without delay, the ship untied its moorings and, sounding the creaking of its oars, gradually moved away from the shore. The boatman stood at the bow and sang:

"Here is the great ocean of the Dragon Palace; alas, the treasures lie at the bottom of the sea,"

raising his voice accustomed to the sea breeze, singing it over and over. Hinode-no-kami said,

"Hey, boatman, the song you just sang said that alas, the treasures lie at the bottom of the sea; won't you tell me the reason for that?"

"Yes, that is so. In a spring sea like this time, it cannot be discerned, but when autumn arrives, the bottom of the sea can be seen clearly. Truly beautiful gold and silver lie piled like mountains all over the sea floor. Directly beneath here is the place where they are most abundant."

"Why do you not take those beautiful treasures?"

"Oh, heaven forbid! At the bottom of this sea there are many fine treasures, but there are also many frightening things. A tremendously thick Dragon God glitters with golden and silver scales, guarding them so that no one may take them. The creatures living at the bottom of this sea—be they sea bream, shrimp, or octopus—all have the color of gold or silver. If even a single shrimp were caught, the Dragon Palace deity would become enraged and the sea would become terribly rough. Therefore, no one catches even a single small fish around here. Even speaking in a loud voice will provoke the Dragon Palace deity's anger here. What the Dragon Palace deity likes is only songs. If it is a song, it does not matter how loudly one sings it, but if one speaks of strange matters or talks of greedy things, one will be subjected to a truly terrible ordeal."

"Is that so? That fellow called the Dragon God seems to be a fellow quite fond of songs."

"Excuse me, sir! If you say such a disrespectful thing, the Dragon God will be angry. Please correct yourself and say that the Dragon God is a very carefree deity,"

saying this as his complexion changed, trembling violently.

§ 3

"There is no need to worry at all. I shall now negotiate with the Ryūjin and have him show us those treasures. Hello, Lord Ryūjin, attendants of Otohime, I am Hinode-no-Kami; show us the treasures just once."

Even before he had finished speaking, the sea surface suddenly began to swirl into perfectly round whirlpools in all directions. The boatman was astonished and trembled all the more. With a bubbling sound, a large lump of gold floated up onto the waves, and subsequently, to the right and left, before and behind, countless splendid treasures and jewels—such as gold, silver, pearls, agates, lapis lazuli, and giant clam shells—floated up to the water's surface, creating a truly indescribable spectacle. Hinode-no-Kami said,

"That is quite enough; please give my regards to Lady Otohime."

As soon as his words ended, the various treasures that had surfaced once again sank without exception into the seabed, making bubbling sounds. All the passengers clapped their hands and praised that magnificent sight. The ship proceeded leisurely toward the southwest.
