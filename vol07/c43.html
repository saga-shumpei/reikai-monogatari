Chapter 43: The Land of the Gods [343]

第43章　神の国〔343〕

§ 1

The sun tilted toward the western mountains, flocks of crows were returning to their roosts, and evening bells echoed in the distance, ringing out "gong, gong"; in the outskirts of the capital of Tsukushi, enveloped in an air of desolation and dark on all sides, a clamorous noise of several dozen people shouting at once was heard ahead. Wondering what was the matter, the four missionaries quickened their pace and approached toward the sound of the voices, lurking in the darkness and intently observing the scene. Looking, several dozen people were burning bonfires vigorously, placing a gourd-faced man with a shining head in their midst,

A: "Hey, you bald-headed fellow who prates about being a crane fly, a flea-catcher, or Katori, hanging down a gourd-like face, dangling numerous gourds just like your own head limply from your waist, singing the propaganda song of Uraruhiko—what nonsense are you spouting with, 'Drink, make merry, an inch ahead is a dark night; after the darkness, the moon will rise'? Look at this, look at this sky! Is it not pitch-dark, a true dark night? If the moon is going to rise, let us see it rise! On such a dark night where nothing can be made out unless bonfires are lit, for you to say the moon will rise—humph, probably a liar's moon will come out! Look there, your own moon, though you might not know it since it is attached to your head, has bonfires shining on it! Your head is glowing like the moon; saying that the moon rises after darkness must be a boast about your own bald head!"

B: "What do you think this place is? It is the capital where a magnificent personage named Shirahitowake resides! It is the divine land that upholds the teachings of the deities of Ananai-kyo, who graciously manifested at the foot of Mount Kogane! Declare it anew, say it over again!"

Katoriwake: "Yes, yes, I shall declare it anew. Please, forgive me."

B: "If you declare it anew, I shall forgive you; come, will you not quickly declare it over again?"

Katoriwake uttered a trembling voice, raising a peculiar shrill cry:

"Do not drink, do not make merry, one foot ahead is a dark night; in the dark night, the moon does not rise."

B: "Fool, what are you spouting? That is even worse; declare it over again more properly!"

Katoriwake, uttering a sorrowful voice:

"I was born a fool, so even if told to declare it anew all of a sudden, I cannot declare anything decent. In any case, there is nothing that will please you, but if you tell me to declare it, it cannot be helped, for while there is life, there is hope. Instead of dying, I shall break the resolve I held to sing this song throughout my entire life, and I will try declaring it anew once more: 'Get out of the way, do not obstruct, all of you people; disperse, and after dispersing, drink sake.' Yes, yes."

Otsu said, "This fellow is ever more a fool. I shall teach you; listen carefully, and speak thus: 'Extend, prosper; though blooming but a little, it is the age of the gods, and the moon shines upon the country of the gods.' Do you understand? Come, say it, say it!"

Katoriwake, while scratching his head,

"Yes, yes, please say it once more; I have forgotten it. Yes."

"How can you say you forgot? A warrior's word is never broken; remember it and recite it again."

"No... er... no... er... yes, yes."

Otsu said, "Say it quickly!"

"Yes, yes, Ex... tend... ye... sake barrel, heh, heh."

"Wait, that is wrong! 'Sake barrel'—you speak of sake at once. 'Extend, prosper,' this is how you must say it!"

"P-P-Pardon me; instead, I shall make my confession. I cannot memorize such difficult words."

"Now, that should be amusing. Tell us of your personal history. Surely, nothing good will come of it anyway."

"Yes, yes, I was in the wrong. It is not that I am bad; the sake I drank is entirely to blame. Sake, sake, what a troublesome situation this has become."

The crowd placed Katoriwake in their midst and was ridiculing him in amusement as if he were a spectacle. From within the darkness,

"Extend and flourish, though blooming but an inch, it is the divine age; The moon shines upon the land of God.
