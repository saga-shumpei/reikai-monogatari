Chapter 29: Vistas from the Mountain Summit [329]

第29章　山上の眺〔329〕

§ 1

Going and going, with no known end along the foot-wearying mountain path, the missionaries traced their way, leaping right and left across mountain streams of violent pools and flying waterfalls, advancing day and night. Here, the three missionaries sat down on such a high mountain ridge, and gazing out over the scenery of the surrounding mountains, indulged in idle talk. Hinodegami said:

"How thoroughly indeed the Magatsukami have laid their schemes from beginning to end! Sending their retainers deep into the mountains of such an uncivilized island of Tsukushi, their relentless design to sweep across the whole realm truly strikes us with wonder. In a world where evil is eighty percent and good is twenty percent, we cannot remain careless. Look, isn't there strange smoke rising over there in the north?"

Iwaihime said, "Indeed, a strange smoke is rising, of an indescribably lovely purple hue. Surely some noble deity must be dwelling there. Looking out over the four directions from atop this high mountain makes one feel magnificent, as if beholding the whole world at a single glance, and it is truly exhilarating."

Hinodegami said, "Indeed, it is exhilarating; for human beings, there is nothing like climbing a mountain. The pleasantness of looking down upon the surrounding mountains and seas spread out beneath our eyes is just like looking down on Jitentō Island from Mount Tenkyō. Look! Behold that smoke; has it not taken on an even more lovely display of five colors?"

Omonagi said, "Might that place be the province of Hi?"

Hinodegami said, "Likely so. By all accounts, bordering along the mountain range of Mount Kumaso lies the province of Hi, and Takehinamukebake (? Tatehinamukebake ?) should be guarding it. However, having fallen to the poisonous fangs of Tokoyo Jin'ō, the divine people of that province may have deteriorated once again. Perhaps I shall go and attempt to preach there."

Omonagi said, "That is well and good, but if we do not return in good time, won't the ship for the land of Tokoyo depart without us? Being left behind on such an island would be unbearable."

Hinodegami said, "What, why should we mind? Everything is kannagara. That is not the only ship; another ship will come. Since we intended to go to the land of Tokoyo by the deity's design, but were washed ashore in such a place, there must be some deep reason within the divine realm. We need not worry about tomorrow. In this very moment called the present, we need only think good, speak good, and do good. We have only to serve purely and correctly from moment to moment. Neither worrying in advance nor fretting over the past avails anything. A breath later, this world is already the past and does not belong to us. And a breath ahead, that is the future. For mere mortals, worrying in advance or fretting over the past amounts to nothing. Well, in all things, it is best to entrust ourselves to the gods."

「曲津神というものは、どこからどこまで、よくも企みを巡らせたものだな。このような未開の筑紫の島の山奥まで眷族を遣わし、どこまでも天下を席巻しようとする執念深い仕組みには、我々は実に感服の至りだ。悪が八分で善が二分の世の中、我々もうかうかとしては居られない。やや、あの北の方に怪しい煙が立ち上っているではないか」

祝姫「いかにも妙な煙が立ちますね、紫の麗しい何とも言えない煙の色です。あそこにはきっと尊い神様がいらっしゃるのでしょう。こうして高い山の上から四方を見晴らせば、実に世界を一目に見るような雄大な心地がいたしまして、実に壮快ですね」

日出神「いかにも壮快だ、人間は山へ登るに限る。こうして展開された四方の山や海を眼下に見下ろす心地よさは、ちょうど天教山から自転倒島を見下ろすようだな。やや、あの煙を見たまえ、ますます麗しい五色の彩りになったではないか」

面那芸「あそこは肥の国でしょうか」

日出神「そうだろう、聞くところではこの熊襲山の山脈を境に肥の国があって、そこには武日向別（？建日向別？）が守っているはずだ。しかしながら常世神王の毒牙にかかって、かの国の神人はまたしても悪化しているかもしれない。一つ行って宣伝をやってみようかな」

面那芸「それも結構ですが、適当なところで帰りませんと、常世の国への船は出てしまいはしませんでしょうか。こんな島に取り残されてはたまりませんよ」

日出神「何、構うことがあるものか、何事も神のまにまに（惟神）だ。船はあれだけではない、また次の船が来るよ。せっかく神のお計らいで常世の国へ行くつもりが、このような所へ押し流されたのだから、何か深い神界のご都合があるのだろう。我々は翌日の事は心配しなくてもよい。今というこの瞬間に善を思い、善を言い、善を行えばよいのだ。我々はその刹那刹那を清く正しく勤めていけばよい。取り越し苦労も過ぎ越し苦労も、何にもならない。一息後のこの世は、もはや過去となって我々のものではない。また一息先といえども、それは未来だ。人間の分際で取り越し苦労をしたり、過ぎ越し苦労をしたところで何にもならない。まあ何事も神にお任せするのがよかろう」

§ 2

Hafurihime said, "Just as you say, my lord, let us leave everything to the will of the gods."

Tsuranagi said, "Indeed that is so; well then, let us gradually make our way."

The three missionaries, singing mission songs once again, advanced their tired feet toward the mountain where five-colored clouds rose, descending the steep mountain.

Just as they were advancing, following the ridges, descending into valleys, and climbing mountains and descending into valleys again, human voices were heard from nowhere in particular, so the three stopped, thinking human habitation was near, and listened intently to the voices.

In the valley, several tens of people with black faces like before were meddling with the strange stones and bizarre rocks of the valley while whispering something.

Person A said, "Hey, isn't this miserable? Made to carry such heavy stones every single day, our bellies become empty, our clothes get torn, we get scrapes—scrapes are still fine, but to be crushed by a rock like Donkō, having one's body flattened like paper and dying, is more than one can bear. If everyone does not watch out, who knows when you might be crushed by a stone and meet the same fate as Donkō. Be careful."

Person B said, "Watching out is all well and good, but do you fellows know God? If only you have faith in God, you won't get injured at all. That bastard Donkō, even though I had listened to the teachings of the Ananai-kyō missionaries and warned him, saying, 'If you do not believe in God too, you will suffer a terrible injury today; a suspicious shadow has appeared upon your face,' that rascal Donkō spewed out nonsense, saying, 'What, God? Where could such a thing exist? If there is a God, let me meet Him; if you show me God's figure even for a glance, I'll believe it's real. Even a fart, though invisible, makes a sound, doesn't it? Yet He makes no sound, has no voice, and His figure cannot be seen; how could anyone believe in such an unreliable, nonexistent God? In my house, there is a magnificent, real deity enshrined called the Great Goddess Wife, who speaks words and lends a helping hand. What is this, then, telling me to believe in a God not even worth a fart, spouting such elusive nonsense like grasping at clouds? Don't make a fool of me; my eyes are sharp, they aren't knotholes!' Humming a tune, he went off to carry stones. Then, no sooner did that big boulder roll over toward Donkō than with a single shriek he parted from this world, setting out on the journey of death to the wretched netherworld—a pitiful turn of events. If you too do not have a bit of faith in God, you will follow right in Donkō's footsteps."

Just at the moment they were whispering thus, the three missionaries came descending toward the valley while singing mission songs.

谷間には、数十人の以前のような黒い顔の人間が、何事かを囁きながら谷間の奇石怪岩をいじっている。

甲「おい、くだらないじゃないか。毎日毎日こんな重たい石を担がされて、腹は減るし、着物は破れるし、かすり傷はできるし、かすり傷はまだいいが、鈍公のように岩に押し潰されて、体が紙のようになって死んでしまっては、たまらないぜ。皆気を付けないと、いつ石に押し潰されて、また鈍公のような目に遭うかもしれないぞ。気を付けろよ」

乙「気を付けるのもよいが、お前たちは神様を知っているのか。神様さえ信ずれば、怪我なんかしないよ。あの鈍公の野郎はな、俺が三五教の宣伝使の教えを聞いて、『お前も神様を信仰しないと、今日はえらい怪我をするぞ、お前の顔には怪しい曇りが現れている』と注意してやったのに、鈍公の野郎は『何が神様だ、そんなものがどこにあるか。神様があるなら俺に会わせてみろ、一目でも神の姿を見せてくれたら本当と信じる。屁でさえも、姿が見えなくても音ぐらいするだろう。それなのに音もしなければ声もなく、姿も見えず、そんな頼りないありもしない神が信じられるか。俺のところには、立派な、物も言われる、手伝ってもくださる結構な嬶大明神という現実の神様が鎮座ましますのだ。それに何だ、屁でもない神様を信じろなどと、雲を掴むようなことを言いやがって、人を馬鹿にするな、俺の目は光っているぞ、節穴じゃないぞ』と出鱈目を吐き散らして、鼻歌を歌って、石運搬に行きやがった。そうするとあの大きな岩めが、鈍公の方にごろりと転がったと思うや否や、ぎゃっと一声この世の別れ、嫌な冥土への死出の旅、気の毒な次第となったのだ。お前も、少しは神様を信じないと、また鈍公の二の舞だぞ」

このように囁き合っている折しも、三柱の宣伝使は宣伝歌を歌いながら谷間に向かって下りてきた。
