Chapter 17: Upon the Tortoise's Back [317]

第17章　亀の背〔317〕

§ 1

The night was gradually breaking into full day, and the light of the morning sun rising, cleaving the waves of the eastern sea, instantly tinted the exceedingly vast ocean into golden waves; the silhouette of the island visible across was an island of New Zealand, Kutsujima, pierced and hollowed out by Kuniharutachi-no-Ōkami, where Ohounabarahiko is enshrined and wherein the pearl of Masumi is placed. The sight of it floating and sinking at the mercy of the waves stirred a feeling of solemnity pressing upon the body.

Straddling a strange vessel, the figures of a man and a woman drifting upon the waves drew near, heading toward the ship. Looking closely, remarkably enough, they were the young woman of Shirayukisato and the long-haired man with graying hair who had cast themselves into the raging waves. When Hinode-no-Kami gestured and called out, 'Come, come,' the strange figure carrying the man and woman gradually drew near the ship, and looking upon it, it was a gigantic turtle. The two immediately leapt onto the ship. The giant turtle faced Hinode-no-Kami, and while exchanging intent gazes, it plunged its form deep into the sea once again. Ah, of what deity might this turtle be an incarnation?

Here, with a feeling of rebirth, the two became guests aboard the ship once more, and facing Hinode-no-Kami, with tears welling in their eyes, they recounted their respective personal histories, and moreover,

'Just as I was casting myself into the sea, a ray of bright light appeared from nowhere. Setting that light as my mark, I drifted upon the waves, rising and sinking. From behind me, a man also came chasing, and while this was taking place, the light vanished into pitch darkness, and our bodies were both placed atop something. And through the sound of the raging wind and the roar of the waves, the propagation song of Hinode-no-Kami was heard as clearly as if taken by hand. Accompanying that song, I joined in chorus with it. Wondrously, the heavenly skies cleared completely, the wind calmed, the waves grew peaceful, and upon the serene spring waves, having been saved upon the back of an incomparable giant turtle and rescued onto this ship—in what lifetime could I ever forget this joy? Truly it is the profound grace of the deeply merciful Ōkami.'

—thus, overwhelmed with tears of joy, the two gave thanks in turn.

The name of the one was Kunihiko, and this woman was called Kushihime. Looking sideways at the face of the man who sat silently beside him with downcast head, Kunihiko said,

'Ah, are you Takahiko?'

When he cried this out, the young man said,

'Ah, are you my father? It was the excess of youth; forgetting the precious favor of my parent, I caused you terrible hardship. Please forgive me. I only pray that in all things my sins thus far be looked upon anew, heard anew, and proclaimed anew.'

夜はしだいにすっかり明け離れ、東海の波を割って昇る朝日の光は、あれほどに広い海原をたちまち金色の波に彩り、向こうに見える島影は、ニュージーランドの一つの島、大海原彦（オホウナバラヒコ）が鎮まっておられる、真澄の玉が納められた、国治立大神（クニハルタチノオオカミ）が穿ち開かれた沓島である。波のまにまに浮き沈みする様子は、荘厳さが身に迫る思いがする。

怪しい船にまたがって、波に漂う男女の影が、船を目がけて近寄ってくる。よくよく見ればなんと意外なことに、猛り狂う波間に身を投げ捨てた白雪郷の若い女と、白髪混じりの長髪の男の二人であった。日の出神が手招きし「来なさい、来なさい」と呼びかけると、男女を乗せた怪しい影は、次第に船に近寄って来たのを見ると巨大な亀であった。二人は直ちに船に飛び乗った。巨大な亀は日の出神に向かい合い、じっくりと顔を見合わせながら、再び姿を海の深くへ沈めた。ああ、この亀はいかなる神の化身であろうか。

二人はここで再生の思いをなして再び船中の客となり、日の出神に向かい合い、涙をたたえながら各自の経歴を語り、かつ、

「海中に身を投げる折しも、どこからともなく一筋の光明が現れてきました。わたくしはその光を目当てに波に漂い、浮き沈みして参りました。私の後ろから一人の男がまたも追いかけて来て、そうこうするうちに、光は消えて真っ暗になり、わたくしたちは何者かの上に二人とも乗せられていました。そうして日の出神様の宣伝歌は、猛り狂う風の音、波の響きを透して手に取るように聞こえました。私はその歌に合わせて共に合唱いたしました。不思議なことに天津御空は晴れ渡り、風は凪ぎ波は静まり、のどかな春の波の上に比類ない大亀の背に救われ、御船に助けられた嬉しさを、いつの世にか忘れましょうか。実にありがたい大神の深い恵みでございます」

と、嬉し涙に暮れて二人は交互に感謝する。

一人の名は国彦といい、この女は奇姫という。国彦は傍らに黙然としてうつむいている男の顔を横目で見て、

「やあ、お前は高彦か」

と叫ぶと、若き男は、

「ああ、父上様ですか、若気の至り、尊い親の恩を忘れ、大変な苦労をかけました。お赦しくださいませ。ただ何事も今までの罪は見直し聞き直し、宣り直しをお願いいたします」

『ヤアお前は高彦か』

と叫べば、若き男は、

§ 2

..., he spoke with tears. Kunihiko, tears welling in both his eyes,

"Ah, Takahiko, in heaven and earth we are but an only parent and an only child; how could I ever hate you? My remaining life is short, and having been separated in life from my only child, there was no meaning in living on in this world; even if your whereabouts were never to be known for my entire life, I resolved that I wished at least to become the soil of the Eternal Land to which you had crossed over, and this was the heart of a parent who has come this far—how could I possibly hate you? Do not worry. After you broke the rules of the village and were banished from Shirayukisato, this aged body left behind did nothing but wring sleeves of tears day and night; yet by what divine arrangement has it happened that there are no demons in this passing world? Moreover, what is also joyful is that the maiden whom you longed for is now right here. I will act as matchmaker from now on, and let us splendidly form the bonds of parent and child, and husband and wife. Hey, Takahiko, speak gentle words to this lovely maiden, saying she is welcome. In this vast world, becoming a parent, becoming a child, and becoming a wife are all deep affinities tied by the gods since the ancient Divine Age, sharing the same fate in the same boat."

..., he said, his face clouded with tears of joy. Here they formed the bonds of parent and child, and husband and wife, and how admirable it was that the three took each other by the hand, sang propagation songs, and offered thanksgiving to the gods! On one side of the ship,

Man A: "Hey, do not make fools of us! Showing off your romance like that! Even though I am like this now, once I return to my home province, I have wrinkled parents, and I also have an exceedingly beautiful wife. And so the fellows in my neighborhood made up a song about me and sing, 'Having a good wife is the blessing of a lifetime; the neighbors rejoice, the old man rejoices, the old woman rejoices, first of all Master Kuma rejoices, and far from just Master Kuma, the son rejoices too'—this is the kind of song they sing!"

Man B: "Hey, wipe your drool, will you? It is unsightly."

Kuma: "Do not be so jealous; if you burn too much with jealousy, your skin will turn black."

Man B: "Do you still think that wife of yours is a beauty? Don't make me laugh! Her nose is like a lion-dance mask, her eyes are like a cat's, she is covered with pockmarks, and on top of that she limps, so when it comes to bad features she is absolute perfection! Ah, speaking of butts, that reminds me: your wife waddles around swinging that enormous float of a backside, bigger than any in the village; her manner of walking is beyond terrible! Even so, you probably think that even pockmarks are dimples, that a pug nose is rather gentle, and that her walking figure is elegant. You really are an incurably naive fellow!"

Kuma said, "Hey, won't you speak in a low voice? We are in front of others. The fellows here do not know my wife; that is why I am talking as if she were a beauty, yet you blurt it out in a loud voice—aren't you tactless? If you do not show some consideration, I am in trouble."

Otsu said, "Even if you say you are in trouble, a fact is a fact, isn't it? Your wife is a field pumpkin and an utter moon-faced fright, and moreover pockmarked, severely lame, shaking her big rump as she walks—such a figure that one cannot bear to look upon it. And on top of that, she is the famous specialty of my village."

As he thus intentionally shouted in a loud voice, Master Kuma restrained him with a low voice, "Hush!"

Otsu said, "You say 'Hush!'—do you treat me as if you were driving cattle? If I am an ox, you are a bear. A black bear, or a red bear and badger lord who does nothing but worship the Great Goddess Wife!"

Thus he rattled on, becoming somewhat desperate. While they were talking in this manner, the boat safely arrived at the port of Kutsujima.

Takahiko was the former incarnation of Ame-no-kuhizamochi-no-tsukasa, and Kushihime was the former incarnation of Kuni-no-kuhizamochi-no-tsukasa.

と自暴自棄気味になってしゃべり立てている。こう話すうちに船は沓島の港に無事に着いた。
