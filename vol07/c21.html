Chapter 21: The Undrinkable Wine [321]

第21章　飲めぬ酒〔321〕

§ 1

Once again, the sea raged fiercely with rough waves, making it unavoidable to postpone the sailing, and waiting for favorable winds lasted for nearly a month.

This island was a deeply fateful island where the Jewels of Ebb and Flow were secretly hidden and had been protected by Toyotamahime-no-kami and Tamayorihime-no-kami; however, prior to the great world deluge, the jewels were seized by the army led by Uralhiko, and the two goddesses fled far to the east to conceal themselves on Kanmuri-jima and Kutsu-jima of the Heavenly Well of Manai.

Thereafter, a subordinate of Uralhiko named Arakumawake occupied this island, gathered numerous subordinates, filled a spring of sake, and indulged to the utmost in the state of the body ruling over the spirit. However, Kamu-Izanagi-no-kami, who was enshrined on Mount Tenkyō, dispatched Masumihime-no-mikoto as the guardian deity of this island. Thereupon, fearing the divine majesty, Arakumawake took advantage of the darkness of night and fled back to the Land of Tokoyo. As a remnant of that time, the spring of sake was still gushing forth copiously to this day.

To enshrine the divine spirit of Masumihime-no-mikoto, Hinode-no-kami commanded Kukukami and Kukikami to fell trees in the great and small ravines and build a beautiful shrine. This is called the Dragon Palace of Ryūgū Island. And He appointed Tayorihiko as the guardian deity of this island, having him change his name to Iiyorihiko.

Kukukami and Kukikami gathered the people of this island and worked faithfully as master carpenters for the construction of the palace. In the valleys of the island, the sounds of felling trees, the sounds of shaving wood, and the shouts of people were loudly heard.

Hinode-no-kami ascended a high pavilion by the seaside with a fine view, waiting for the sea to become calm.

Deep in the mountains, the sounds of axes and broadaxes rang out clearly here and there, and trees were being felled vigorously.

"Hey, why don't we all take a break? Just now Kukukami went over there, and as the saying goes, even when an aunt dies, there is time to eat. While that demon-like commander is away over there, let us do the wash while the demon is away. Hey, hey, rest, rest!"

"Hey everyone, shall we rest together?"

"Still, if we rest, the sound will stop, so we will be yelled at again."

"We can just rest and keep tapping on the trees around here!"

"In the first place, talking about building a shrine or something, they work us dreadfully without letting us sleep even at night, as if someone were coughing up blood. While that fine sake is springing forth over there, they tell us it cannot be drunk, which is simply unbearable. In between work, since it is merely gushing naturally, they might as well let us drink at least some sake. What kind of shrine on earth is this, anyway?"

"Because they do not let you drink sake, you get angry; to keep you from becoming angry, they have you worship a kami. And so, it is said to be deities called Haragatatsuyo-hime, Masumi-hime, or Masunomi-hime."

"What strange kami. It is not just tempers that rise. Fits of passion rise, and birds take flight; how about worshipping Tatsukan-hime-no-Kami or Tatsutori-hime-no-Kami as well?"

"Do not talk nonsense. Look, there comes a painful fellow over there again. Look, look, it is Kugi-no-Kami."

"It is not a nail. He is called Kuki-no-Kami. If you loaf about, he will turn into a head-lopping kami."

To such a place Kuki-no-Kami came around, and said:

"Oh, well done, everyone. You have faces that look as though you want to drink sake, but it is better not to drink much sake. I, too, loved sake until now, but at last I have come to dislike it. Even if one is told to forcibly stop what one likes, it is not a thing that stops. If you drink sake to your heart's content and are satisfied, in the end your tongues may tangle, your mouths go numb, your eyeballs pop out, and you may become unable to drink sake. If you endure while thinking 'I want to drink, I want to drink,' your nature will warp, which is not good. Sake is the leader of all medicines, and there is no kami who does not partake of sacred sake; so if you also wish to become kami, there is no need for hesitation at all. Since it is sake that wells up naturally, go and drink without holding back."

Tossing off these words, he left this place. Seeing him off from behind, they said:

"Hey, hey, Kuku-no-Kami noisily spat out that we should not drink sake, but Kuki-no-Kami, who just came, is indeed a man of worldly wisdom. He said to go and drink as much as we want because it will not do for our nature to become warped. Permission has been granted. It is officially sanctioned. Shall we go and drink?"

"Splendid, splendid,"

And so, the crowd vied with one another to be first, running toward the plunge basin where the sake welled up.

When they arrived and looked, the plunge basin of the spring of sake was completely enclosed by immense boulders, and holes about the size of a human mouth were open here and there on the upper surface.

"Hey, hey, everyone, is not Kuki-no-Kami ill-natured as well? Having tightly covered it with such gigantic rocks before we knew it, and telling us to go drink if we want to drink—is he not making fools of us?"

"That is true, but are there not holes open there? How about thrusting our mouths into those holes?"

"Oh, that is true too. Everyone, let us drink from here, let us drink!"

All of them clung to the top of the rock lid and stuck out their tongues through the hole to see, but—

"Hey, there is plenty of delicious-looking sake, but my tongue cannot reach it. It is just about one bu away."

"Your tongue is short; here, let me try drinking it."

"You are always a fellow with a long tongue. Since you spout words with a long tongue, it is handy at a time like this. Give it a try."

"Ahem!"

Clearing his throat thus, he thrust his tongue into the hole of the rock, but this too did not reach. They tried by turns, but by all means there was a distance to where the sake was, and they could not lick it. However, because an indescribably fragrant smell of sake was coming from that hole, each placed his mouth against it and inhaled the scent. Their throats individually began to growl and rumble, and the heated stones inside their bellies, without exception, fell down into the sake jar while hissing and sizzling.

From then on, the people of this village came to hate even smelling the scent of sake, strictly kept the teachings of the deity, followed the commands of Iiroyohiko no Kami, and led a joyful life on Ryūgūjima, worthy of its name.
