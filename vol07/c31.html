§ 1

Soaring into the heavenly skies of hisakata, In the lofty Tenkyō Mountain that one looks up to, Enshrined is Konohana-hime-no-Kami; Saving the world through Her divine word, Spreading the pure teachings to the nations of the four directions, Across the eighty, the hundred and eighty lands, The missionary Yashimawake Dwells firmly and eternally In Tokiwa Castle of the divine province of Hi; Just then arises the battle cry; Yashimawake, wondering at it, Pushes open the door and gazes out; East and west and north and south, Without room for even an ant to crawl forth, The multitude of people pressing close, A wave of people fierce in force, The rough-hearted provincials' Hideous, wild rampage is indeed dreadful.

From among the multitude of tens of thousands, an exceptionally tall man of dark reddish complexion, with large eyeballs and a wide mouth, leading four or five ugly men, clenching his fists and swinging his arms broadly, appeared before the gate of Yashimawake,

"Hey! Hey! Open this gate!"

he was bellowing like thunder. The gatekeeper said,

"Whoever you may be, not fearing the august authority of Lord Yashimawake who has graciously descended from Mount Tenkyō, to demand opening this gate is insolence in the extreme; I have no ears to listen to what ruffians like you say. Clear off at once."

The big man, clenching fists like wooden mallets, said,

"Are you too scared to open it, you burrowing insect, toad-worm, or if not, you mole, you earthworm lurking in the dust; without carping, open it quickly!"

Gatekeeper: "Cawing like crows at dawn, oh how noisy! If it is opening you want, open I may, but it will be an open-and-be-astonished treasure box; so that your soul does not switch lodging, you had better hold your senses firmly in place."

When the gatekeeper reluctantly opened the gate doors quickly to the left and right while making a rattling noise, the party of five, who were pitch-black all over their bodies like roof-thatchers' helpers, whether bear-monsters or masters of crows, whose heads and faces could hardly be distinguished at a glance, spoke in unison with eyes slanted upward:

"I am Torakorobimawake, chosen by the tens of thousands of people of the province of Hi, who have come for negotiations. Guide me before Yashimawake without a moment's delay."

Gatekeeper: "What is this, darkies? If you do not hold dear your only life, I will let you meet him; do not be startled. Come, follow me,"

and he proceeded forward toward the inner palace.

Gatekeeper: "Excuse me, person at the reception desk, an outrageous fellow has arrived. A pitch-black fellow calling himself Kumakorobi or Toraneko or some such bizarre thing says he wants to be allowed to meet Yashimawake; please make an announcement."

"Hey, gatekeeper, do not say unnecessary things; it is enough to let Lord Tora meet Yashimawake."

"Oh, Toratenwake-sama, welcome. Please proceed inside at once."

Saying this, she grinned, took both of his hands, and guided him inside.

Having his hands, rough as crocodile scales, held by a peerless beauty, Toratenwake went limp as an octopus and drooled as he was led deep inside. Because an aroma of unidentified sake was drifting about, Toratenwake stopped, twitched his nose like a dog, and turned his gaze toward the sake barrels piled high as a mountain.

The woman turned to Toratenwake and said,

"Excuse me, Toraten-sama, there is plenty of sake placed here, as you see. Yashimawake-sama, through his divine power, knew in advance of your arrival, and instructed that the rare guest be given plenty of sake to drink his fill. Come, come, I shall pour for you. Please partake without hesitation."

As she said this, casting an alluring glance at Toratenwake, Toratenwake purred like a cat, his previous vigor vanishing completely, and said,

"Yashimawake-sama is quite reasonable; he is truly thoughtful. Yet, though I feel bad, since he took the trouble to prepare all this, it would be cruel to flatly decline. It feels bad to accept, but it also feels bad to refuse. If it is all the same either way, I shall drink heartily."

The woman replied, "That would be best."

Toratenwake immediately beamed, sat down cross-legged flat on the spot, and his four attendants formed a crane-wing formation centered on him, lining up to his left and right. The names of the three beauties were Haruhime, Natsuhime, and Akihime. Haruhime spread open a white folding fan, waved her long sleeves, and began to dance; Natsuhime struck a sounding stone to keep time; and Akihime filled large cups to the brim, offering sake in turn to Toratenwake and the four attendants. In exceedingly high spirits, Toratenwake completely forgot that he was inside Yashimawake's mansion, raised his gong-like voice, swayed his head from side to side, and began to sing:

"Though people call this the divine land of Tsukushi, It is the land of Tokoyo, The domain of Toratenwake, The envoy of the Divine King of Tokoyo. Knowing not the power of this one, Who seizes and devours demons, serpents, and even Enma, What an insolent posture they take! The missionary of Yashimawake, Descending from Mount Tenkyō, Built a castle in this province of Hi, Under the name of Tate no Hinatawake. Babbling senseless nonsense, He misleads the people of the world. I cannot stomach that. And so I, gathering many of the country's folk, Made them carry boulders from the valleys to build a castle, Deceiving them that it was a castle Where Yashimawake would dwell eternally, And of its foundations, mostly

Begun to be laid is my plot; Once it finally succeeds, Toratenwake, inside the castle, Will make ready bows and arrows, A plot to finish off the missionary Yashimawake With but a single blow. A fine plot indeed, ah, how delicious! If anything is sweet, it is this sake; There is nothing sweeter than sake. Plying Toraten with sake To bring about his ruin is amusing! As long as you give me sake to drink, I care not, for the night is still young; When I get drunk and reel, my tail is white, An old fox with a white head too, Toraten transformed, the master of deceit, A kinsman of the golden-furred, nine-tailed fox! Ah, how amusing, how amusing!"

Thus, completely dead drunk, he confessed all of his plots without holding back, which was truly amusing.
