Chapter 6: Vain Bravado [306]

第6章　空威張〔306〕

§ 1

The sun was about to sink into the western sea, and waves of gold and silver drifted upon the surface of the waters.

Looking down at the full sails and half-sails of hundreds and thousands of ships gathering toward the harbor, four or five travelers sat upon the slope road, absorbed in conversation.

Tayorihiko: "Ah, will we receive today as well?"

Yoshihiko: "Receive, what do you mean? There is no one who gives anything to us. If you speak of giving, it is only that Ōyabiko gets angry at whatever we do, spouts off about this and that, and gives us a fist now and then."

Tayorihiko: "When I say 'kureru', I mean the sun setting (kureru). Therefore, I asked if we should take it if it is given."

Tokihiko: "Fool, you always say things as pointless as farting under water, entertaining notions like a monkey trying to grasp the moon in a pond, thinking of nothing but receiving; if you want to receive so much, you had better become a beggar. Hearing the name Tayorihiko, one would think you might be dependable (tayori), yet you are always saying unreliable things. It is just like: 'A deserted boat on the tideway shore, with no island to cling to'—ihihihihi!"

Tayorihiko: "What are you babbling? Even so, I was originally a distinguished personage of the Dragon Palace Castle!"

Yoshihiko: "You are merely the fellow who had his jewel stolen."

Tayorihiko: "And what about you? At the banquet of the Yahiro Palace, you fell into Takekuma's trap, showed your stubborn pride, and were the very ringleader who had your jewel snatched away!"

Tokihiko: "Shall we drop the talk about jewels now?"

Tayorihiko: "Tokihiko, you were bewitched by an old badger on the plain of Armenia, were you not?"

Tokihiko: "Say no more; this is the end of the jewel talk. We have run out of jewels (tamagire)!"

Yoshihiko: "It must be unbearable (tamaranu) for you."

Tokihiko: "Hmph, it is utterly insufferable. Even while having possessed it by chance (tamatama)..."

Tayorihiko: "This is no joke. More importantly, an official proclamation from Ōgotooshio-sama went around recently; have you heard it?"

Tokihiko: "Did some grave matter (ōgoto) occur?"

Tayorihiko: "No, it is the name of a deity. On the plain of Armenia where Tokihiko was deceived, a deity whom Tayorihiko likes—whether called Kureruhiko or Kurenuhiko—appeared, saying such sensible things as, 'Drink and make merry, for an inch ahead is pure darkness.'"

Yoshihiko: "Indeed, he says things very clearly. Even Tokihiko, who braced himself declaring, 'Throughout all eternity, I will never surrender this jewel even in exchange for my life, until the dawn of the Miroku Divine Rule, even into fire or the depths of water,' had his jewel snatched away, so truly an inch ahead is complete darkness. Speaking of darkness, is that not the deity of Ōdaigahara these days? Every single day, he snatches people from over there and humans from over here; there are only rumors of someone being eaten, someone being swallowed, being devoured, swallowed up, or dragged away. If we dawdle, it will not be just a matter of jewels; our very lives will be taken. Thus, it is an unprecedented grave matter, and life is precious; that is why he is called Ōgotooshio. They say he is a terribly evil deity."

§ 2

Tamahiko said, "Do not talk nonsense. As for Lord Ōkotooshio, it means that whatever great upheaval occurs, or whatever great hardships arise, he endures well with calm self-possession like the mountain of Ōdaigahara, and devotes himself for the sake of the world. Your idea of enduring is merely that when someone stronger than yourselves appears, you endure out of fear, which in reality is surrendering. We are different from such endurance. Taking the august heart of Lord Ōkotooshio as our own heart, we endure well through any hardships and privations, without uttering a single complaint."

Tokihiko said, "Aha, so being doted upon foolishly by your wife and having your jewels taken away is not yielding, but enduring well; how admirable. However, recently a great missionary called Hinode-no-Kami has been traveling around this area, has he not? They say that declaring he would destroy Lord Ōkotooshio of Ōdaigahara, he climbed the mountain all by himself. At any rate, he is merely a summer moth flying into the fire. In any case, obey the prevailing power of the times. One cannot resist the times. No matter how strong a deity may be, one cannot prevail against a multitude. Moreover, when fighting against the demonic deities established inside the huge cavern, the outcome is completely obvious. Well now, shall we quickly head home?"

Yoshihiko said, "Hey, Tayorihiko, your brother-in-law Mametora was dragged off into the cavern at last, was he not? Surely by now he must have been eaten up. Nearly ten years have passed since he was dragged away, yet there has not been the slightest word from him, has there?"

Tayorihiko said, "Whether sending a letter or sending a message, inside the cavern nothing can be done. Rather than that, it would be better to return home quickly and humor Princess Kusakahime. Look, it has already grown dark around here; let us return, let us return."

Just as they were about to stand up, Mametora appeared, singing a propagation song together with the three deities Hinode-no-Kami, Ōtodhibiwake, and Amebifukio.

Mametora, vigorously squaring his shoulders, sang:

"Though the sun has turned to twilight, With a bald head shining dazzlingly, Lord Mametora has appeared, And the joy of this day, Having become a retainer of Hinode-no-Kami, Cannot be forgotten whether I die or live. Even if a thousand years or ten thousand years pass, Could this joy ever be forgotten? Escaping from inside the cavern, Though I have at last returned here, I want to see the face of Kusaka just once. After ten long years, surely, surely, My wife Kusaka will rejoice, At once leaping at me and clinging tight: 'Were you safe and sound, Lord Mametora?' Weeping, 'I am so glad, so glad!' And in addition to that, the one Who never sent word is Tayorihiko, Tamahiko stripped of his jewels, And Yoshihiko, who might as well die,

And Tokihiko whose heart throbs— Making them envious by flirting, Let me give them a surprise. Ah, delightful, delightful, The delightful belly-drumming of the tanuki. Whether the mountain splits or collapses, Show them the courage of Mametora, Who will not flinch in the least. Oh my, joyful, joyful!"

Thus, waving his hands and dancing with his feet in a strange manner, he came down this sloping path.

The four men, Tayorihiko and the others resting by the roadside, aimed at Mametora's head shining in the dark, picked up nearby tree branches, and using the gleam as a target, threw them with a sharp smack; whereupon Mametora cried out "Ack!" and collapsed on the spot,

"I-i-it appeared, it appeared, a m-m-monster! H-h-hinode-no-Kami, h-h-help, help!"

And it was truly comical how he wept and screamed at the top of his voice.
