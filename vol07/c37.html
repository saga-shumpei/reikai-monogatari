Chapter 37: Oriruyu [237]

第37章　老利留油〔237〕

§ 1

Radiating the light of God, These four missionaries— Beginning with Hinode-no-Kami, The missionary Toyohiwake, Whose heart is richly tranquil, Shiko-no-Magatsu, Hafurihime, Together with Tsuranagihiko— Parting from Yashimawake-no-Mikoto of the eighty lands of the country, At last from here, Takehimuka, They advance, separating on their respective ways. In the abundant land of Toyoashihara, Carrying the mild spring sun on their backs, Entrusting their steps to their legs across mountains and slopes, Treading through roots of rocks and roots of trees, Crossing over deep valleys, Shirahiwake, who bears the name Of the bountiful, rich land of the gods, Intending to cross over into the land of Tsukushi, Goes forth boldly; how valiant it is.

Upon the ridge of Mount Kirishima, where mist rises, the four deities sat down, resting their buttocks firmly upon the grass, and while wiping away their flowing sweat, gazed at the scenery in all directions and indulged in innocent conversation.

Toyohiwake said, "Ah, truly, the scenery viewed from a high mountain is magnificent. Rather than playing the bat in a birdless village in the land of Hi—surrounded on four sides by mountains and occasionally assaulted on one side by rough waves—arguing over narrow-minded matters like rights, duties, gain, and loss, or fretting day and night over incomprehensible humans, worrying while being called the devil or ogre of Torakorobawake, being reborn with our original hearts together with you like this and freely traversing mountains and fields is truly an indescribable heavenly blessing. Regarding that, I must become the Toyohiwake of the land of Toyo and offer protection, but in which direction does the land of Toyo lie?"

Looking around in all directions and pointing toward the great desert stretching below, Missionary Tsuranagi said,

"The land of Toyo is that reddish-white place visible to the southwest of here."

Toyohiwake said, "Oh, what is this? Not a single blade of grass or tree is growing there. Is that not a desert?"

Tsuranagi said, "It is a great desert. Planting trees and grasses there, causing the five grains to ripen, and turning it into the bountiful land of Toyo is your role."

Toyohiwake said, "Far from a heavenly blessing, it is an extraordinary heavenly punishment. How on earth could trees and grasses flourish, human beings multiply, and it become a splendid land?"

Hinode-no-Kami said, "If hair grows on Lord Toyohiwake's bald head, trees and grasses will probably grow in that desert as well. If you wish to grow it, you must undergo a terribly painful experience."

"Will hair grow on this bald head?"

Hinode-no-Kami said, "It will grow if you experience pain. Shall I make it grow for you?"

Toyohiwake said, "Even if I experience a little pain, as long as it benefits the realm, I do not mind."

面那芸宣伝使は四方を見回しながら、眼下に広がっている大砂漠を指さして、

「豊の国はこの南西に当たる、赤白く見える所ですよ」

豊日別「おや、何だ、草も木も一本も生えていないではありませんか。あれは砂漠ではありませんか」

面那芸「大砂漠ですよ。そこに草木を植え付け、五穀を実らせ、豊かな豊の国とするのが貴方の役目ですよ」

豊日別「天恵どころか、非常な天刑です。どうしたら草木が繁茂し、人間が繁殖して立派な国土になるでしょうかね」

日出神「豊日別さまの頭の禿げに毛が生えたら、あの砂漠にも草木が生えるだろう。それを生やそうと思えば、大変につらい目に遭わなくてはならない」

「この禿げた頭に毛が生えますか」

日出神「痛い目に遭えば生える。生やしてあげようか」

豊日別「少々痛い目に遭ったって、天下のためになることなら構いません」

§ 2

Hinode-no-kami quickly stood up and hid within the nearby trees, but after a short while he returned holding green tree branches, piled the tree branches upon a nearby rock, and began striking them with a suitable stone as if Osan were beating a fulling block; as they were struck more and more, both branches and leaves reduced in volume, and moisture began to drip forth. Hinode-no-kami wrapped them tightly in a black face cloth and squeezed with all his might, and the juice that came out filled a slight hollow in the rock, turning into oil.

Hinode-no-kami said, "Now, I will make hair grow for you. It will hurt a little, but can you bear it?"

So saying, he grabbed rough sand from nearby and vigorously rubbed Toyohiwake's head, whereupon Toyohiwake cried:

"Eeek!"

Hinode-no-kami said, "A propagator must not whine that it hurts; that is what makes a man, so brace yourself."

"Eeek! It feels good!"

Hinode-no-kami rubbed more and more harshly. The thin skin peeled, and blood oozed out.

"Eeek! It feels exceedingly good!"

Hinode-no-kami said, "All right, now I will make you feel even better."

So saying, he scooped up the oil just squeezed onto the rock and smeared it wetly all over. Toyohiwake grimaced and once again cried:

"Eeek! Eeek!"

and his voice turned into weeping.

Hinode-no-kami said, "Do not whine again."

Toyohiwake said, "Eeek! Eeek! It is quite enough. If possible, please stop already, hooo, I want nothing more."

Thus he braced himself, tears falling profusely.

Hinode-no-kami said, "Now, this is done,"

and sat down again upon the lawn. Toyohiwake held his head, closed his eyes, held his breath, and crouched down. After a short while the pain ceased, and Toyohiwake finally felt relieved and relaxed his face.

Hinode-no-kami said, "How was it? It must feel good. A human being must cross the great mountain pass at least once. Crossing the great mountain pass is quite painful."

Toyohiwake said, "Well, I climbed up to this great pass, and I did not think it was that painful, but this was far beyond any great pass! It hurt considerably, yet indeed, I have met with a most splendid experience."

Hinode-no-kami said, "Put your hand to your head and see."

Toyohiwake stroked his head and said:

"Oh, it has grown, it has grown! It has completely grown! Thank you!"

and he suddenly leapt up and rejoiced. This was the oil of a tree called Orīru.

"Come, come, let us go,"

said Hinode-no-kami, standing at the head. Toyohiwake greatly rejoiced that hair had grown upon his bald head, and said:

"Now, with this I have become young,"

and squaring his shoulders, he steadily descended the mountain pass. The propagation songs sung by the four echoed across the valleys.
