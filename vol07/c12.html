Chapter 12: Deep Contemplation [312]

第12章　熟々尽〔312〕

§ 1

Hachi: "Truly, he finally lost his nerve and collapsed. However, thanks to my collapsing, you fellows were saved and can remain here in peace like this. Stop harping on hips and fright, you cowardly rascal!"

Shika: "However, where did that missionary named Taketora, who glared with such terrifying, wide eyes, flee to? The chieftain is not here, is he?"

Kō: "Just now, deities such as the Great Sun Deity or the Sunset Deity suddenly appeared from heaven and sang the missionary songs of Anaikyō. Thereupon, that fellow Taketorahiko, the henchman of Uralhiko, completely closed those large eyes of his, held his giant head, shrank down, and in the end fled deep into the mountains together with the small rats like a field rat. Then that Sunset Deity or whatever granted the names Uranagi and Uranami to the chieftain and his wife, and rejoicing, the chieftain and his wife made their way steadily into this mountain."

Shika: "What! Uranagi? Is it called Uranagi because they suffered terrible hardship on account of Uralhiko?"

Kō: "I know nothing of it."

Otsu: "If you do not know, shall I tell you? It is not Uranagi, it is Tsuranagi. The origin of that name is roughly as I shall explain. Ahem, when I ponder it carefully, in this painful world we suffer bitter hardships, having our octopuses hooked and being forced to hear hard-to-hear missionary songs—what a fine cheek! It was so painful that I thought I would even have to hang myself by the neck. Without even thinking of it as painful, remaining still and enduring it, the chieftain bore the bitter hardship, letting the tears that would fall outward spill inward, putting on an unconcerned face and refusing to drink the sake he longed to drink; even when sharp blades were thrust before his eyes and his face was exposed, he maintained a countenance as if to say, 'What could be painful about this?' That is why they are the deities Tsuranagi-no-kami and Tsuranami-no-kami. Do you understand?"

Shika: "Heh, putting on such a brazen, great face, what endless pretexts are you stringing together? Having to listen to such things is also quite an imposition. Hey, might Uralhiko's missionaries not have left some sake behind around here?"

All looked around in all directions,

"Oh, there are many sake bottles left both there and here. If it is free sake, shall we drink it up?"

§ 2

Kō said, "Hey, hey, stop, stop! If we were going to drink that, we would not have suffered like this."

Otsu said, "That is obvious. In front of that fellow or in front of the chieftain, we put on a brave face, but now that Taketorahiko is gone, no one will know no matter how much we drink, will they? It was simply vexing to drink before the missionary."

Hei said, "Even so, the gods are watching. Stop, stop."

As they were thus talking among themselves, there appeared Menanami-no-Kami, the chieftain's wife. Menanami-no-Kami faced the gathered crowd and said:

"You have kept the teachings of the gods and endured well. From now on, I will become the chieftain and protect you. Starting today, my husband becomes a missionary of the Anaikyō and will travel about to save the people of the world. Until now, he protected only this small Shirayuki Village, but it is no longer the time for such a thing. I alone am more than enough for a village such as this, and since he is departing this village this very day, you too should come deep into these mountains to bid him farewell. You cannot constantly drink sake like the missionary Uraruhiko, but today is a celebration of departure, so you may drink plenty of sake."

Otsu said, "See that? Did I not say just now that we could drink today? There is sake around here left by the missionary Uraruhiko. Shall we drink it all?"

As they scrambled to pick up the bottles and began to drink, Menanami-no-Kami looked upon this scene, grimaced, and said:

"What gluttonous creatures humans are! Ah, looking after things in my husband's absence will certainly be no easy task. At any rate, there is no choice but to entrust everything to the gods."

Muttering thus to herself, she began singing a missionary hymn in a low voice and turned back along the road she had come. With bottles clutched in one hand, the men and women of all ages followed behind the goddess deep into the mountains, where in a slightly flat place, Hinode-no-Kami sat upright together with the chieftain before a beautiful woman. This woman was the missionary Iwaihime, who had been mentioned before. Iwaihime had been surrounded by Taketorahiko's many disciples and tormented with sake and swords just as the chieftain had been, but without fear in the least, she had earnestly expounded and taught the doctrines of the Anaikyō; just as the crowd grew furious and was about to strike her dead, Hinode-no-Kami appeared and sang a missionary hymn in a loud voice. It was the moment when, cowering at that voice, they had all slunk away and hidden in all directions.

Here, the three deities—Hinode-no-Kami, Menagi-no-Kami, and Iwaihime—descended Mount Shirayuki, stayed one night at the chieftain's house in Shirayuki Village, and, filled with joy, departed on their mission.
