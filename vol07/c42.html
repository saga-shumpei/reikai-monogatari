Chapter 42: The Great Divide [342]

第42章　分水嶺〔342〕

§ 1

The three missionaries, who had been listening intently to Takateruhiko's reminiscence steeped in grief, gazed at the clouds passing through the sky of mutable change with deep and boundless emotion, and Tsuranagi no Kami turned toward Hinode no Kami, saying:

"Just now, having heard the words of Takateruhiko-sama, I was truly impressed. Thinking upon this, we had lived in this world as in a dream, serving as chieftains in the small, narrow Shirayukisato, yet compared to the hardships of Takateruhiko-sama, ours is a hardship that hardly amounts to a single hair among nine oxen. Fortunately, we were persuaded by the missionaries of Hinode no Kami of the splendid teachings of Anaai-kyō, and completely repenting, though we have managed to come this far, our hardships are still far from enough. To confess the truth, day in and day out, I wonder how my wife, Tsuranami-hime, whom I left behind in Shirayukisato, is doing, and how, as chieftain with a woman's weak, slender arms, she can govern villagers who are like incomprehensible tigers and bears. The more I think of it, the more pitiable it seems. Becoming husband and wife is a deep karmic tie, yet though it is for the sake of the gods, crossing seas and mountains the two of us suffered a painful, living parting; thinking that the sorrow of the four birds and the grief of the hooked fish must describe our situation, when I reflect on how day and night I secretly shed tears of attachment, it is truly pathetic. What cowardice! Ah, what lingering attachment I possess! All living beings must perish; those who meet must part. If one is driven by thoughts of the pain of parting from loved ones, one can never become a pure missionary who saves this world. Ah, I was wrong. Ah, ah, Kami-sama, please bestow the strong power of the noble deities upon my weak heart."

Thus he pressed his hands together in prayer toward heaven, shedding tears.

Hinode no Kami nodded and said:

"Ah, that is well. Without that mindset, one cannot possibly be a missionary. To tell you the truth, regarding your spiritual resolve, I too felt that something was somehow slightly lacking. I was gripped by a slight unease that you might fall away from divine virtue midway, or stumble. Ah, hearing that, I am truly relieved. In this world of mutable change, there is no other way than to leave everything to the will of the gods. This moment called the present is the watershed of good and evil, right and wrong. Without regretting the past and without fearing the future, let us all unite our hearts and go forward according to the divine command."

「ただいま高照彦様のお話を聞きまして、実に感心いたしました。これを思えば、我々はわずかな狭い白雪郷で酋長となって、夢のようにこの世を暮らしてきましたが、高照彦様のご苦労のことを思えば、ほとんど九牛の一毛にも及ばない苦労です。幸いにも日の出神の宣伝使に三五教の結構な教えを説き諭され、翻然として悔い改め、ここまで来ることには来ましたが、まだまだ我々は苦労が足りません。実際のことを白状いたしますが、明けても暮れても、白雪郷に残してきた我が妻の面那美姫はどうしているだろうか、わけのわからない虎や熊のような里人を、女の弱い細腕で酋長としてどのように治めていくだろうか。思えば思うほど不憫なことです。夫婦となるのも深い因縁であるのに、神のためとは言いながら、海や山を越えて二人は悲しい生木の別れ、四鳥の悲しみ釣魚の嘆きとは我々の境遇であろうかと、明け暮れ愛着の涙を人知れず絞ったことを思えば、実に情けないことです。何たる卑怯でしょうか。ああ、何たる未練な私でしょうか。生者必滅、会者定離です。愛別離苦の念に駆られるようでは、到底この世を救う清き宣伝使となることはできません。ああ、悪かった。ああ、ああ神様、どうぞ私の弱き心に、貴神の強き力を与えてくださいませ」

と天に向かって合掌し、涙を流した。

日の出神はうなずき、

「ああ、それでよろしい。その心掛けでなくては、とても宣伝使にはなれない。私も実のところを言えば、あなたの精神的な覚悟の点において、もう少しいささか物足りない気持ちがしていた。途中で神徳を失いはしないか、挫折しはしないかと、やや不安の念に駆られていたのだ。ああ、私もそれを聞いて本当に安心した。有為転変の世の中は、何事も神の御心のままに任せるよりほかない。今というこの瞬間は、善悪正邪の分水嶺だ。過去を悔やまず未来を恐れず、神命のままに皆で心を合わせて進みましょう」

§ 2

So saying decisively, he went ahead, and once again raised his clear, refreshing voice:

"The valiant man giving his all of heart, Receiving the decree of the Kami, Advances toward the land of Tsukushi In Africa that drifts upon the waves. Though the spirit rushes forth like an arrow from a bamboo bow, Exhausted are these legs that carry him; The feet are torn by straw sandals, Yet relying on a sincere heart Red like autumn leaves stained with blood, How delightful is this journey Treading through the Reed-Plain Land of Fresh Rice Ears! Indeed, hearing that this island has but one body With four faces, The one remaining is now only one face; Propagators sharing the very same purpose, Purifying the sacred Kotodama they proclaim, Blowing away with their breath the dense mist That enveloped the great ocean, Unto the capital where Shirahitowake, The ruler of the land, resides in peace: Advance, advance, come, advance! Advance, advance, come, advance!"

And with that, he dashed forward vigorously.

Just at that moment, no sooner was a deafening roar heard than black smoke rose billowing toward the northeastern sky, and great boulders turned into fiery projectiles, falling down upon the earth. At this explosion, the party instinctively halted their steps, stood still on the path for a while, and gazed up from afar at that dreadful spectacle.

Omonagi: "Excuse me, which mountain has erupted over there? Could it be celebrating our future path, or might an evil deity be cursing us?"

Iwaihime: "No, we are those who travel throughout the realm for the service of the Kami; the Great Kami of Heaven and Earth must have set off fireworks to celebrate the departure of our party. Did not the Hinode Deity say just a moment ago that this is a world of constant change and flux?"

Omonagi: "Could it really be so? Even so, it was far too loud a noise. Shameful as it is to admit, my courage was nearly overturned."

Hinode-no-Kami: "Hahaha, Lord Omonagi, you must brace your courage a bit more, or you will not do; even the very earth we are now walking upon might explode. What do you intend to do at that time?"

Omonagi: "Well, it is the spirit of each fleeting moment, is it not? A watershed dividing good and evil, right and wrong; one cannot know what lies even an inch ahead."

Hinode-no-Kami: "Indeed, indeed. Yet everything in this world is as the Kami wills. What erupted just now is the volcano of Etna. Ever since it exploded at the time of the festival on Mount Tacoma, it had remained calm until today, but its sudden eruption once again is likely a warning from Heaven to us. It is said that at the very instant the volcano of Etna erupted, Kotodamawake-no-Kami of the Dragon Palace Castle was poisoned by an evil deity and suffered terribly. We too must be careful, for upon arriving at the capital of Tsukushi, we know not what trap of an evil deity's scheme we might be ensnared in, so we must remain vigilant."

§ 3

Takateruhiko said, "At such times, what should we do?"

Hinodenokami said, "There is nothing particular to do or not do. If you have the time to utter idle chatter, you need only chant the divine words that freely govern heaven and earth and command the wind, rain, thunder, and lightning, and all calamities will be driven away."

Hafurihime said, "How would it be if we all gathered together here now to chant the divine words? My feet have also grown very weary; shall we chant the divine words while taking a rest?"

Hinodenokami said, "What do you mean by 'while taking a rest'? You cannot offer the divine words merely in passing. Resting is resting, and chanting the divine words is chanting."

Hafurihime said, "Oh, thank you; I carelessly made a mistake."

Takateruhiko said, "That is why they say that female missionaries are unreliable."

Hinodenokami said, "Do not mind other people. Having only just become a missionary, is it any place for you to talk about others? It is you whom I consider unreliable."

While continuing various conversations along the road, the four missionaries finally emerged onto a great plain, and looking toward the south, the towering mansion where Shirahikowake-no-tsukasa was enshrined came into view.
