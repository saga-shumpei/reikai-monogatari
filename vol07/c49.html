Chapter 49: Embark Anew [349]

第49章　乗り直せ〔349〕

§ 1

Letting the midsummer night wind that blew at that moment strike his face, the boatman stood at the bow,

Wishing to see the Dragon Palace, as I cross the Seto Sea, What is visible over there is a single island,

and thus he began to sing.

The boat quietly glided over the rippling waves. Scattered stars shone across the entire sky, and the moon, with a face as if dripping with water in midair, was looking down upon the sea surface. At the bottom of the sea sank the moon's reflection like a coiling dragon. At this time, rowing an oar and steering a helm from ahead, making creaking and groaning sounds, was a single boat advancing toward this side. At the bow of that boat stood a man wearing a straw raincoat, a sedge hat, and a face-cloth. Hokkō-shin raised his voice toward that boat,

'Exerting heart and exerting body, Unto the gods of heaven and earth, like a hempen pillar, The missionary who firmly establishes the Way; Having toured the lands of the four directions, At last arrived here, of Hokkō I am Mehitotsu-no-kami; Declare your name, O missionary Standing at the bow of that boat, O divine person standing at the bow!'

When he sang thus, in response to that voice, from the boat over there:

'The Heavenly Gods and Earthly Gods, The teachings of Konohana-hime Unto the lands and islands of the four directions; The missionary of Hiromichiwake, Where are you dwelling? From here, I am crossing over to The country of Kumaso in Africa; Though the country of Kumaso be fierce, Through the kotodama bestowed by the gods, All the evil spirits of the four directions I shall pacify with words, until, before the divine command Of Konohana-hime who dwells on the mountain of Tenkyō, And before Haniyasu-no-kami, Who appeared at the foot of the Golden Mountain, I establish wondrous merit, The missionary crossing the path of waves, Whose divine majesty is broad—the messenger of God Of Hiromichiwake is I! The messenger of God is I!'

The singing voice also gradually grew faint. The two stood up toward each other, waved their face-cloths, and congratulated each other on their safety. The people inside the boat once again began to chat.

Person A: 'The Dragon Palace Castle is already in sight right there. If we do not stay quiet, and the Dragon Palace deity gets angry and raises rough waves, we will have to suffer by setting up a greengrocer's shop again like yesterday, so shall we speak in low voices?'

Person B: 'Do you fellows know the history of this Dragon Palace? Only now can we comfortably go from the Seto Sea to the Dragon Palace Castle aboard a boat like this, but in ancient times, right in the middle between the Dragon Palace and the Seto Sea, there was an exceedingly high mountain called Mount Zion, and it is said that an extraordinary jewel came forth from it. When the great flood occurred, an earthquake shook, that mountain sank to the bottom of the earth, and the Dragon Palace and the Seto Sea became one, so the story goes.'

§ 2

Hei: "Is that all? That is something anyone has heard since our grandfathers' generation. Is there not any more novel tale?"

Otsu: "In Ryūgū Castle there was an exceedingly beautiful deity named Wakazakurahime, and there magnificent deities called Ōyashimahiko no Mikoto and Ōdaruhiko guarded Ryūgū Castle and the palace of Jerusalem. Then, a great woman called Tokoyohime of the land of Tokoyo intended to occupy Ryūgū, and it is said that time and time again battles between great deities began. When one compares the present Ryūgū and palace of Jerusalem with the tales of old, they are truly miserable things. There was a great river called the Jordan River, but that river was buried at the time of the flood, and now it has become a small, narrow stream where dirty water flows. How the world changes indeed. Considering this, no matter how great a deity may be, they are of no avail. Even the deity called Wakazakurahime, with power great enough to bring down flying birds, and the deity called Ōyashimahiko were utterly brought to ruin by the likes of Tokoyohime, and it is said that they fled to that place called Fuji, where we cannot go."

Hei: "Hmm, is that so? If that is the case, even if we speak boldly now, there is no knowing how things will change."

Otsu: "That is obvious; since even deities cannot contend with the times and seasons, we are all the more helpless. However, it is said that a magnificent missionary is aboard this ship; how would it be if we had him tell us a tale once?"

Kō: "No, the missionary's songs are also utterly irresponsible. Talking about a deity appearing openly to separate good from evil is like speaking of steam rising from cold rice, and it is too absurd to listen to. Such songs are matters from before the great flood. At the time of the great flood, good deities were saved at places like Mount Tenkyō and Amanohashi, while the wicked drifted in the floodwaters or were cast onto the Mountain of Centipedes and the Mountain of Ants, and it is said that there was a clear separation between good and evil. Since it is a tale from our grandfathers' generation, I do not know the details, but anyone who sings of such things nowadays is like counting the age of a dead child. Isn't it absurd? How could a world that has become this enlightened be so easily overturned and reconstructed? They speak of separating good from evil or of things changing, but wicked men like Hisakō in my village steadily prosper, whereas good men like Shinkō grew ever poorer, until finally he sold his household goods, husband and wife were separated from their children, and they went out as beggars, did they not? When I think of that, I have no mind to rely upon deities. Missionaries' songs are so antiquated, like irises on the sixth day or chrysanthemums on the tenth. However, while it is fine to listen to them for distraction, if you take them to be the truth, you are mistaken. A missionary is someone who uses expedient means to keep us from doing evil, but in a dark world like today where evil prospers and good declines, no matter how loudly a missionary bellows, it is not worth a pinch of snuff."

§ 3

At this time, from one side of the boat,

"The kami appears out in the open And separates good from evil."

Kō said, "There it has started; my head hurts. This is unbearable. Strange fellows are riding on this boat."

"Kamunaohi who created this world, And Ōnaohi of vast heart, Simply in all matters in the human world, Look again and listen again through Naohi, And confess anew the errors of the self."

Otsu said, "Hey you, you need not worry. The missionary was saying that he would transfer to another boat right now. He will transfer to the boat that is coming there now. If so, you will not have to listen to the unpleasant song."

Kō said, "Won't he hurry up and transfer boats?"

"The kami appears out in the open..."

Kō said, "There, he has started it again! Ouch, ouch, ouch, ouch!"

"And separates good from evil..."

Kō said, "Ah, ouch, unbearable, unbearable!"

Saying this, Kō stood up and chanted,

"Hey, hey, hey, missionary, There it comes, there it comes, a boat is coming; A fellow who chatters such painful things As you do should transfer to that boat As quickly as possible, Hurry, board, board, transfer boats, Look, it has come, look, it has come, the boat has come, Won't you board, won't you board, missionary, Thanks to you I have a headache."

Thus he was shouting. From behind, once again, the second missionary stood up,

"The kami appears out in the open And separates good from evil."

Kō said, "It has come out again. What on earth is this boat? I have had quite enough of boat journeys."

The boatman shouted, "Passengers, the boat has arrived. Go ashore quickly!"

Kō held his head, dashed out of the boat first of all, and disappeared to somewhere unknown. What manner of person, indeed, could this man be?
