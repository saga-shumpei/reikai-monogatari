Chapter 46: Shirahibiwake [346]

第46章　白日別〔346〕

§ 1

The night faintly begins to dawn. To the voice of the rooster crowing "Kokkakōkō," the Hinode-sendenshi, breathing the fresh morning air as the eastern sky reddens and the sun opens the clouds and rises, pushing through the dewy path, advances toward the mansion of Shirahiwake no Kami as the day grows brightly white.

Katoriwake, making the numerous gourds at his waist rattle, dragging his lame leg while bobbing his head up and down in the air, breathlessly follows behind the four people. The comicality of his appearance was just like an ocellated octopus standing up and fleeing from a potato field.

The party of Hinode no Kami drew near Shirahiwake's mansion, and though they knocked and pounded on the gate, there was no answer, so Hinode no Kami said toward Katoriwake,

"Cross over this wall, and open the gate from inside,"

he commanded, whereupon Katoriwake said,

"How can a lame person like me cross over such a high wall?"

Hinode no Kami: "You can indeed cross it; there is a way to cross it. Do it like this,"

saying this, he pulled the stopper from the gourd at his waist,

Hinode no Kami: "Since you have come to dislike sake, you need no more sake; I will throw it away for you. Have you no lingering regret?"

Katoriwake: "Yes, I have neither mirin nor shōchū; there is only ordinary sake. Even if you discard it, I do not think it regrettable in the least. However, when I think that this cuts my ties with sake for a lifetime, I feel somewhat reluctant to part with it. I will let it be discarded, but may I take just a lick of it?"

Hinode no Kami: "You base fellow, what an indecisive man you are,"

saying this, he pulled the stoppers from the gourds and threw it all away onto the ground. And as Hinode no Kami blew his breath with all his might into the mouth of each of the many gourds, the gourds expanded before their eyes, becoming as thin as balloons, and Katoriwake naturally soared up into the air.

Katoriwake: "Hello, hello, I am rising, I am rising, what should I do?"

Hinode no Kami: "Cast off those gourds one by one. Since they have become thin, tear them with your fingernails."

Katoriwake tore them with the tips of his fingernails with popping sounds. All at once the gourds burst, and he fell headlong into the premises. Inside the gate, a thud and a voice crying "Ouch!" were heard.

Hinode no Kami: "Hey, will you not open the gate quickly?"

Katoriwake: "It will not open, it will not open, it will not open at all. It is dislocated, it is dislocated."

§ 2

Tsuranagi: "What gave way?"

Katoriwake: "My hips, my hips!"

Tsuranagi: "What a useless fellow. Going and having your hips give out!"

Saying this, he lightly dodged, turned a somersault, and leaped into the gate; instantly, the gate swiftly opened to the left and right.

Tsuranagi: "Everyone, I have kept you waiting; come, please enter."

The four missionaries marched steadily toward the interior.

Katoriwake: "Ah, excuse me, what will you do with me? With me, with me!"

Thus he was shouting. Iwaihime felt pity, turned back, and took Katoriwake's hand, attempting to lift him up, but Katoriwake would not raise his hips by any means.

Iwaihime: "Why do you not get up?"

Katoriwake: "Yes, more than my wife and more than my child, I have come to completely hate the sake that I loved. When it comes to this, what I remember is the wife I left behind in my homeland. Ah, ah, by now whether she is dead or alive, at any rate across the Pacific over long years and months, no matter how much of a wife I had, she is of no help in an emergency; as far as I can surmise, you seem to be single; please marry into my house. If you do, my hips will rise."

Iwaihime: "Ohohoho, are you still drunk on sake? However much of a drought of men the world may be in, pardon me for saying so, but who would become the wife of a black person like you? In time, a crow might take you as a bridegroom. Even if I were to surrender myself to a crow, I flatly refuse a gourd-faced man like you. How foolish; come, come, stand up quickly. If the God of Hinode glares at you, you will not be able to bear it."

Katoriwake: "Ah, ah, what comes to pass is distasteful, what I wish for does not come to pass; the person I like is also liked by others. Ah, ah, what an anxious thing it is!"

Iwaihime: "I do not know."

Thus acting cold, she quickened her steps and tried to go quickly into the interior. Katoriwake grasped her straw raincoat and said,

"Excuse me, one should not be so cold. Companions on a journey, compassion in life."

Iwaihime: "Eh, how miserable!"

So saying, she slapped his bald head with a sharp smack and ran away as fast as she could. Katoriwake, with his hips still having given way,

"Oi, oi!"

was shouting.

The party of the God of Hinode searched thoroughly inside the mansion, but not even a single cat was there. Thinking it strange, when they opened everywhere in that vicinity, the divine soul of Sumiyohime, the Kunitama no Kami, was reverently enshrined in the inner sanctuary. Moreover, all utensils were neatly arranged in good order. The entire company chanted the Amatsu Norito toward the shrine, but there was not a single offering in the shrine. Something was written upon a single slip of paper. The God of Hinode advanced respectfully to the shrine, received it in his hands with reverence, and read it; contrary to the expectation that it would be a liturgy chanted to the deity, the following was recorded:

"Item: I am Shirahiwake, the Grand Chieftain of the land of Tsukushi. In a dream the night before last, there was a dream revelation saying, 'Because the precious august child of Kuniharutachi-no-Mikoto and the precious august child of Kamu-Izanagi-no-Mikoto will descend upon this island of Tsukushi, you and your clan must surrender this land and mansion, depart for the island of Takasago as quickly as possible, and become the protectors of that island. If Takateruhiko-no-Kami subsequently settles here, the land of Tsukushi and the Land of Reed Plains and Abundant Rice Ears shall be peacefully governed.' Therefore, leading my clan during the night, I vacated this island. I leave the rest in your care.

To Hinode-no-Kami,

To Takateruhiko-sama,

And to all the others,"

so it was written. In accordance with this testament, Hinode-no-Kami appointed Takateruhiko as the protector of the land of Tsukushi, caused his name to be changed to Shirahiwake, and resolved to await the arrival of divine destiny.

From here, Hinode-no-Kami was to proceed toward the land of Tokoyo, Omonagitsukasa toward Mount Tenkyō, and Iwaihime toward Mount Kogane; thus the three deities parted with regret at this place.
