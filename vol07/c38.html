Chapter 38: Unten Pottery [338]

第38章　雲天焼〔338〕

§ 1

The springtime mountainside exposed its green face to heaven and smiled, fragrant flowers of yellow, purple, red, and white bloomed profusely in various places to weave through the trees, and hundreds of birds raised their tranquil voices to extol this world. Parting the mist of the spring mountain and descending, the pure-hearted missionary, lightly clad in straw raincoat and hat, wearing armor and helmet, with straw sandals, leggings, gauntlets, and shin guards, came advancing vigorously. Sitting beside a clear valley stream that flowed murmuringly, there were four or five woodcutters engrossed in idle talk.

A: "Hey, did you fellows hear? Lately, in the direction of the northeast, five-colored clouds rose up, didn't they? What on earth do you think that was?"

B: "What is a beggar's cloud? That must be your kin. Beggars and palanquin bearers rose up—even if it's not the northeast, on this mountain path, aren't palanquin bearers carrying litters and beggars wandering about every day?"

A: "Fool, you are utterly deaf. I am telling you that five-colored clouds rose up."

...he roared in a loud voice, putting his mouth close to B's ear.

"Even if you do not make such a loud voice, I can hear you well. What do you say happened to the beggars and palanquin bearers?"

A: "Hahaha, amusing, how amusing, you are as obstinate as an iron bar; if I talk to such a deaf man, the sun will set. Hey, Hachikō, tell me the reason for the five-colored clouds."

Hachi assumed a domineering attitude and said:

"They say that in the Land of Hi there are evil gods with strange names like Torakorobawake and Untenyaki. They set fire to the mansion of a missionary called Yakeshimawake, and that smoke soared into heaven, burning the clouds in the sky, which is why he is called Untenyaki. And so the splendid mansion of the missionary was completely burned down, hence 'Yaketeshimatta-wake.' In any case, having no place to stay, that Yaketeshimatta-wake might cross this pass and come out here. And if such a fellow flees to the Land of Toyo and sings stiff, tedious songs, since the Land of Toyo is not full of cowards, a second Untenyaki is bound to appear; what a dangerous matter it is."

B: "I wonder where in this vast Land of Toyo that fellow called Untenyaki might be."

Hachikō: "How could he not be here? How could it be otherwise? For I myself will become that Untenyaki."

B: "What will you do becoming Untenyaki?"

Hachikō: "If such a fellow comes, I will become desperate and burn him to ashes."

§ 2

Otsu: "Look at you, if you act in desperation (yakekuso), it is not Kumotenyake. It is Kusotenyake."

Thus he was talking nonsense. There, faintly heard, came the sound of a propagation song.

Hachikō: "Ah, how terrifying! A voice that makes my shoulders stiff has begun to be heard. It is dangerous to linger; run, run!"

Otsu: "Serve you right! You did nothing but boast loudly, and without even knowing whether it is the voice of a propagation song, the sound of water, or the roar of the wind, just like a fellow who lost the use of his legs mistaking the plume of miscanthus grass for a ghost at twilight, are you not disgraceful?"

Hachikō: "Do not speak so loudly! My head aches; run, run!"

Otsu tries to run away at top speed.

Hachikō: "Wait a moment! Will you not take me along with you to escape?"

Otsu: "You have legs, do you not? Why do you not walk on your own?"

Hachikō: "Somehow it seems my belly has settled firm, and I cannot move an inch."

Otsu: "You coward, you have collapsed in fear, have you not?"

The propagation song came to be heard closer and closer.

"The deities appear in public And divide good from evil. The three deities of Hinode-no-Kami Now descend to Toyo no Kuni. The trees and grasses in all directions Will bend low in the divine wind; The vile weeds Will be completely scattered by the divine breath. After they are scattered, they will bear fruit; Toyo no Kuni of the divine teachings, Manifesting as Toyohinowake, A propagation song spreading to the four directions."

Echoing throughout the surroundings, the song drew nearer voice by voice. Four or five woodcutters held their heads, held their breath, lay on the valley path, and were trembling violently. Among them, one extraordinarily large man remained completely calm, tilted his head, and listened to the propagation song with apparent delight.

As the voice drew closer moment by moment, it began to be heard ever louder, and the large man stood up and began to sing toward the direction of the voice:

"This place is Africa, Toyo no Kuni, A succession of vast deserts, A barren land, a wilderness plain. O missionary of the land of God, However much power you may possess, Blown by the rough wind of the wilderness plain, Your body is covered with sand, The hair of your head gleams brightly, A bald head with strong glare. Whatever deity you are, appear and be gone! The divine land of Toyohinowake, The divine land of Toyohinowake Has raging deities and Magatsukami, And fellows with twisted hearts Like Hachikō and Torakō."

Hachikō: "Hey, what are you saying? Do you think only you will be saved because the missionary is coming, going so far as to sing ill of me? You outrageous fellow! Remember this!"

The large man: "Hachikō, Kumakō, come this far! Heave-ho, heave-ho!"

§ 3

So saying, sticking out his tongue and waving his hands, he mocked Hachikō and ran toward the direction from which the voices of the missionaries came.

Hinode-no-kami: "Are you a person of Toyo no Kuni?"

Giant Man: "Yes, I am the fellow called Kuma of Toyo no Kuni. You have done well to come. However, this land is about seventy percent desert, and the wind blows day after day. It is a dreadful cloud of sand, so that one cannot keep one's eyes or nose open. Therefore, we choose mountains here and there where trees and grass are overgrown, eat tree nuts, and capture hares and boars alive to live; it is a miserable land. There are many camels, but they are foolish creatures; having huge bodies, they are of no use at all, and from time to time, chased by tigers and wolves, they come pressing upon the places where we humans dwell while making strange cries, and at such times they cause us to suffer such that we do not even have room to walk. You have taken the trouble to come to this land, but it would be best for you to return now. The world is wide, so it is of no use for you to come to such a bad land. It would be pitiable if another villain called Torakoroshiwake, like Yashimawake of Hi no Kuni, should appear and make you suffer cruel treatment; therefore, please turn back from this mountain once and for all and go to Kumasu no Kuni or somewhere. I certainly say nothing bad. The propagation song that you sing is truly splendid, but it is far too noble to enter the ears of the humans of this land."

Thus he babbled on, not knowing that Toyohiwake, formerly Torakoroshiwake, was right before his eyes.

Toyohiwake: "I am that villain Torakoroshiwake. Now, by the arrangement of Hinode-no-kami, I have become the governor of this Toyo no Kuni. Guide all of us to the capital of Toyo no Kuni."

The giant man stared intently at Toyohiwake's face and said,

Giant Man: "No, no; that fellow Torakoroshiwake of Hi no Kuni is said to be a bald-headed villain, yet you have hair growing on your head; you must be a fine missionary. And what is this about being the intensely evil Torakoroshiwake, hated by people like a demon? There is a limit even to jokes; please tell me your true name."

Toyohiwake: "That is the truth. In any case, guide us to the capital of Toyo no Kuni."

The giant man, though reluctant, took the lead and guided the four missionaries toward the capital of Toyo no Kuni. While Hachikō and four or five other woodcutters remained paralyzed by the roadside,

Hachikō: "Hey, hey, Kumakō, where are you going? If you guide such a fellow to the capital of Toyo, this land will be in grave danger. Even if I am alone, I will become Untenyaki!"

so he shouted.

Kumakō: "You coward Hachikō, do not be noisy; whether it is good or evil is not yet known. 'Try riding a horse, try associating with a person.' Heal your hips quickly and come seek me later."

So saying, he hurried rapidly toward the capital.

The four missionaries proceeded forward, once again singing their propagation song.

大男『ハイ私は豊の国の熊といふ野郎です。能う来て下さいました。しかし、この国は七分どほり沙漠で毎日日日風が吹きます。それはそれはえらい砂烟で目も鼻も開けて居る事は出来ませぬ。それで彼方此方の木草の繁つた山を撰ンで、木の実を食つたり兎や猪を生捕て生活をして居る惨目な国であります。駱駝は沢山に居りますが、彼奴馬鹿な奴で大きな図体をしよつて何も役に立たず、時々虎や狼に追はれて吾々人間の居る処へ妙な声を出して押寄せて来るなり、その時には吾々の歩く場も無いやうな目に会せます。貴下はこの国に折角御出で下さつたが、もう御帰りになつたが宜しからう。世界は広いのにこンな悪い国に御出でになつたつて仕方が有りませぬ。肥の国の八島別のやうに又虎転別とか云ふ悪者が出てきて惨い目に会はされては御気の毒ですから、もうこれ限りこの山を引き返して熊襲の国にでも御出でなさいませ。私は決して悪いことは申しませぬ。貴下の歌はつしやる宣伝歌は誠に結構ですが、この国の人間の耳には余り立派過ぎて這入りませぬ』

と虎転別の豊日別が現在眼の前に居るのも知らずに喋り立てゐる。

豊日別『俺はその悪者の虎転別だよ。今は日の出神の御取計らひに依つて此の豊の国の守護職と成つたのだ。お前らは豊の国の都へ吾々一同を案内いたせ』

大の男熟々と豊日別の顔を見て、

大男『イヤー、肥の国の虎転別といふ奴は頭の禿げた悪者だといふ事だのに、それにお前さまは毛が生えて居るでは無いか、結構な宣伝使様だらう。それに何ぞや鬼のやうな人の嫌ふ悪の強い虎転別じやなンて戯言にも程がある、本当の名を仰言つて下さい』

豊日別『そりや実際だ。何は兎もあれ、豊の国の都へ案内してくれ』

大の男は、不承無精ながら先に立つて、豊の国の都へ、四人の宣伝使を導き行く。八公その他四五の杣人は路傍に腰を抜かしたるまま、

八公『オイオイ熊公貴様どこへ行く。豊の都にでもそンな奴を案内したら、この国は大変だぞ。俺一人でも雲天焼に成つてやるぞ』

と叫ぶ。

熊公『八公の腰抜け、喧しう云ふない、善か悪か未だ知れやしない。馬には乗つて見い、人には添うて見いだ。貴様も早く腰を癒して後から俺の処を探ねて来い』

と云ひながら都を指してドンドンと急ぎ行く。

四人の宣伝使は又もや宣伝歌を歌ひつつ進み行く。
