Chapter 4: Iwashitsuhiko [304]

第4章　石土毘古〔304〕

§ 1

Hinodenokami faced the White Dragon, saying,

"Why hast thou now revealed thy true form before me and expressed an intention to submit? Thou must have a most profound plot. Dost thou pretend to submit once, causing the gods to relax their vigilance, and take advantage of that opening to destroy us? We will not fall for such a trick; confess the truth."

As they pressed closely from three directions, the White Dragon filled both eyes with tears and pressed its head against the earth, expressing absolute submission; whereupon Matetsuhiko brandished his long sword and, swift as lightning, slashed at the White Dragon's head, and no sooner was a single streak of bloody mist seen rising toward the sky than white clouds billowed densely, until one could not discern what was right before one's eyes. After a short while, the former woman appeared vacantly from within the dense white clouds and spoke in a faint voice:

"I am indeed the child of Ame-no-Mihashira-no-Kami, named Iwatsuhime. My husband is called Iwatsuchibiko. Intending us to be the foundation of an everlasting world, firm as rock and pine, my father, the great deity, created a cavern in this sacred mountain and made our married pair dwell here. Nevertheless, the Yamata-no-Orochi that possesses Uralhiko of Armenia, having somehow discovered this enchanted realm, led numerous evil spirits to attack this mountain to destroy us, husband and wife, and usurp the mastery of this mountain for itself; but I feigned to be its ally and was awaiting the arrival of you deities of salvation, and now heavenly fortune has turned, and I meet with this joy."

Thus was the secret of the cavern revealed for the first time, and the three deities said in unison:

"Art thou indeed Iwatsuhime, of whom we have heard by rumor? An unexpected meeting in an unforeseen place; this is truly the arrangement of Nodatehiko-no-Kami who dwells in the unseen realm; how joyful, how grateful we are."

Thus the four deities together clapped their hands and offered divine words. At this moment, a man came running hurriedly from ahead, and while placing both hands on the ground facing Iwatsuhime, said:

"A grave event has occurred. Iwatsuchibiko is now about to be slaughtered by Yamata-no-Orochi. Unable to bear seeing that tragic spectacle, I have come to report to you. Please come at once."

No sooner had he said this than he deftly dodged aside and ran at full speed back along the path by which he had come.

This was the deepest chamber of the cavern, where a great waterfall of unknown hundreds of fathoms fell. Beside the waterfall, countless natural stone figures formed of stalactites, large and small, stood without number; and on the other hand, to the left side of the waterfall, mother rocks of quartz stood arrayed like needles, just as though blades of ice had been set upright. On a high seat nearby, an unusual, white-haired giant—the transformation of the great serpent—commanded numerous subordinates, bound Iwatsuchibiko securely, and had his attendants violently dash Iwatsuchibiko's body upon the blades of ice, lift him above their heads again, dash him down once more, and finally cast him into the great waterfall; and upon seeing him float up, they thrust him back into the waterfall basin with two-pronged spears from front, rear, left, and right, exhausting every form of torment, and having dragged him before the great serpent again to make him kneel, they began a severe interrogation. と言うより早く、ひらりと身をかわし、一目散に元来た道を走って行く。

と云ふより早く、ひらりと体を躱し一目散にもと来し道を走り行く。

ここは巌窟の最も奥深き一室にして、幾百丈とも知れぬ大瀑布が落ちゐたり。瀑布の傍には大小無数の鐘乳石よりなれる自然の石像、数限りなく停立し、かつ一方瀑布の左側には、水晶の母岩針のごとく立ち並び、あたかも氷の刃を立てたる如くなりき。傍の高座には白髪異様の大男、大蛇の変化は、数多の部下を従へ石土毘古を高手小手に縛め、従者共をして石土毘古の身体を氷の刃の上に、どつとばかりに投げつけ、またもや之を頭上に差し上げ、再び投げつけ、終つて大瀑布に投じ、浮み来るを見るや再び刺股をもつて前後左右より滝壺に押し込み、虐待の限りをつくし、再び大蛇の前に引き据ゑきたつて厳酷なる訊問を始めたり。その中の大男の一人は、

"Are you not Iwatsuchibiko? Until now, feigning to be the dragon deity of Ōdaigahara, you have entrapped us; you are a treacherous, atrocious enemy seeking to destroy our long-held ambition. Making it appear on the surface as if you had submitted, together with your wife Iwasuhime, you served near me, searched out the secrets of the cavern, and surely reported them secretly to Hinode-no-kami of the sacred ground. Confess at once. If you utter even one more word of deceit, I shall make you the blood-offering for our departure, massacre your wife likewise, next destroy Hinode-no-kami, and immediately leap forth into the world to trample the Land of Fresh Rice Ears according to my will. However brave you may be, fallen into the midst of the enemy, no matter how much you fret, the few cannot match the many; rather than making a futile show of loyalty, will you surrender to me from now on and obey me from the bottom of your heart? Depending on your reply, the lives of you two, husband and wife, are a flickering light before the wind. What is your intention?"

When thus severely questioned and accused, Iwatsuchibiko showed not the slightest fear, and said:

"Though indeed the few cannot match the many, we husband and wife are the offspring of Kami-Izanagi-no-Mikoto and the masters of this mountain. How could we ever surrender to a base-born evil deity such as Yamata-no-Orochi, the ringleader of evil spirits? Will you not from this moment repent of evil, turn to good, follow us, and participate in the divine work? God loves all divine beings and humans. To slaughter evil deities in vain is not what I desire. Today already, the three brave generals—Hinode-no-kami, Yasuyohiko, and Matetsuhiko—have advanced deep into the cavern. I am truly like an ogre with an iron club. The destiny of you evil deities is already exhausted. Resisting my rock-hard Iwatsuchibiko with a chicken's egg is merely bringing ruin upon yourselves. Repent quickly!"

When he thus reasoned with and admonished them while his limbs were bound, Yamata-no-Orochi flew into a great rage and said:

"What babbling on the verge of death! You minions, pierce him, strike him, slash him!"

When he thus severely commanded, they answered,

"Aye,"

and numerous subordinates, each bearing a poled weapon, attacked and surrounded him from all quarters. One attendant minion, thinking who knows what, fled headlong from this place and vanished without a trace.
