Chapter 16: The Angler's Lament [316]

第16章　釣魚の悲〔316〕

§ 1

At the stormy sight once again, the people aboard the ship were gripped by anxiety yet again, lowering their heads and holding their breath like mice chased by a cat, trembling and prostrating so that they could not even utter a squeak.

This vicinity was an exceedingly perilous area, with countless rocks large and small undulating upon the water's surface. It was a point where a single misstep would instantly bring upon the ship the disaster of destruction and capsizing, a single leap into the cauldron of hell; the lives of the people were as precarious as fish in a rut or eggs piled upon a stone, as danger approached moment by moment. At this moment, a voice arose from one side of the ship:

"In this human world fraught with disasters, Targeting even the brief respite of a meal, The merciless storm rages violently; Without any clemency, amid rough waves, Sunken into the abyss of tears, To save the people of the world By the grace of the Heavenly Sun Deity, Having appeared upon Mount Kogane, The missionary of the Ananai Teachings, Appearing as Hinode-no-Kami, Crossing the rough sea where wind and waves rage, To repair and solidify a peaceful Age of the Gods here, Proceeding toward the Eternal Land; O vast ocean expanse, Deeper and deeper is the divine grace; The august majesty Towers into the distant heavenly sky; Mount Tenkyō is no ordinary sight; Of high divine virtue, Terutae-no-

Awaken from slumber, open your eyes! I am Hinode-no-Kami; My kotodama journeys through the Eternal Land, Illuminating the darkness and purging The hundred crooked matters of the world; The calm surface of the sea Is calmed by the stern breath of Menagi."

§ 2

"Truly precious is the grace of God; Truly precious is the virtue of God."

As the song ended, once again the wind died down upon the surface of the sea, the waves subsided, the moon began to shine brightly in the midst of heaven, and even the stout-hearted people aboard the ship involuntarily clapped their hands, giving thanks for the vast virtue of the Gods of Heaven and Earth.

From among the passengers, a man of dark complexion, firm-set mouth, and medium build and height appeared before Hinode-no-Kami, and respectfully placing his hands on the floor,

"Not once, but twice have you saved us from this disaster and bestowed upon us your pure and beautiful teachings, for which I offer my heartfelt gratitude."

Wiping away his tears as he spoke, he said,

"Now, there is something I wish to ask of the missionary. Will you kindly hear my inquiry?"

he said bashfully. Hinode-no-Kami

"I am a missionary who guides the world; ask whatever you will,"

graciously replied, whereupon the man spoke:

"I am in truth a native of Shirayukigō. Through an unforeseen circumstance, I fell in love with a woman of the village, was driven out of Shirayukigō, and attempted to flee to the Land of Tokoyo. However, by mere difference of a single step, the ship had set sail for the Land of Tokoyo, and having waited for the next ship, I am now attempting to cross over to the Land of Tokoyo. Yet the woman I love pursued after me, and through whatever karmic tie, happened to board this very same ship. But she did not know in her wildest dreams that I was aboard this ship. Nor did I notice in the slightest that she was aboard the ship. Just then, there was a woman who began to sing a song in one part of the ship, and looking closely, she was none other than the one I had long loved and longed for. Thinking, 'Ah, she has kept the word once pledged and come seeking me across the far-off waves! Oh, my beloved!' I wished to call out my name, embrace her, and weep together. But when I looked to my side, to my utter surprise, I noticed my father sternly present within the ship. Sharing the same fate together, while I hesitated back and forth and let out sighs, what could she have thought—the woman finally cast herself into the deep, thousand-fathom sea, vanishing as foam in utter pitifulness. And how agonizing for me to see my father pursue after her, likewise turning into wreckage of the sea. At that moment, I too resolved to pursue her and my father and cast myself into this ocean; yet from the bottom of my heart, a voice repeatedly whispered, 'Hold on, hold on. Who will perform the memorial rites for your beloved woman and deeply revered father? You, who in all heaven and earth have only each other as parent and child, desist from throwing yourself into the sea.' It was through my own carelessness that I caused that woman's death and turned the life of my deeply indebted father into water foam; when I think of this sin of mine, deeper than the thousand-fathom sea, I can neither stand nor sit. Please, I beseech you, illuminate the confusion of my heart, O missionary of Hinode-no-Kami."

As he thus spoke with tears, Hinode-no-Kami smiled gently and said nonchalantly:

"In this world, the young and the old face an uncertain lifespan, and those who meet must part. Everything, all the destinies of human beings, is held in the hand of God. Living is the divine will of God, and dying is also the divine will of God. In all things of the human world, simply look again and listen again with Naohi, and confess anew the errors of oneself. In the coming spring, God will surely grant a reunion of parents, children, and spouses, as auspicious as the twin pines. From now on, return to your true heart, uphold the teachings of Anaikyō, and praise the Kami of heaven and earth from your true heart."

When the deity thus taught him, while brimming with warm tears, he gave thanks to Hinode-no-Kami and immediately began to sing the propagation song in a loud voice. Hinode-no-Kami clapped his hands, turned to the man, and said:

"Look over there."

Thus the deity pointed. Beyond the waves, bobbing up and down, the forms of a man and a woman carried upon something were seen. Who, indeed, might this man and woman be?
