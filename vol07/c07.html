Chapter 7: The Mountain Conflagration [307]

第7章　山火事〔307〕

§ 1

At this time, there was a voice in the darkness:

"The Kami appears out in the open, Separating good and evil. Polish your spirit, undergo renewal, Rectify your conduct. Kamunaohi, who created this world, Ōnaohi of broad heart; In all things of the human world, View them anew, hear them anew in rectitude, And speak anew to rectify your errors."

Singing thus, they advanced rapidly without minding Mametora. Mametora,

"Hello, Hinode-no-Kami-sama, Ōtohibiwake-sama, Amebuki-o-sama, please wait a while. My legs have given way, and my head has been cracked open. Help me, help me,"

he was shouting.

The voices of the propagators only grew steadily more distant.

"That fellow Mametora left his house, And Princess Kusaka rejoiced. His wife came out to the front, Setting out a meal for Tamahiko; Peel your eyes and undergo renewal, For conduct has completely Turned from good to evil. The vital fierce deity who created this world, With glowing eyes, Smacked Mametora sharply on the head, Which shines even at night; Hinode-no-Kami swiftly Runs away like white waves left behind. No matter how brightly his head shines, His heart is a dark midget badger; Charmed by foxes and badgers, Dragged into the rocky cave, The deity of sparks flying from the eyes appeared, And the coward collapsed into the dark."

There was someone who began to sing thus. Mametora had some vague recollection of that voice, so

"Hey! Who is the fellow that smacked my head in the dark, made sparks fly from my eyes, frightened me, and made my legs give way?"

he called out, and from the darkness,

"Isn't that Mametora who lost the use of his legs?"

a person shouted. Mametora, while sprawling on the ground, said:

"It sounds like a familiar voice, but you said my wife set out a meal for that fellow Tamahiko, didn't you?"

"Strike while the iron is hot; good causes bring good results, evil turns into good, and good turns into evil. In either case, it is Lord Tamahiko of the mutual polishing of jewels!"

Tokihiko: "Fool!"

Tamahiko: "What do you mean, fool? You jewel-robbed fellow!"

Tokihiko: "The one whose jewel was taken is you, you wife-stealing fellow! Shall I secretly inform your wife?"

Tamahiko: "Wait, fox from the same den, I will inform on you too!"

Tayorihiko took out a flint and tinder from his tinder bag, began striking it with a clicking sound, and as he gathered fallen leaves and dead branches nearby in the darkness and lit them, the fire flared up blazingly, and everyone's faces became bright for the first time. Fanned by the fierce wind blowing at that moment, the fire visibly spread in all directions, and roaring loudly, the surroundings instantly became as bright as day. As the fire gradually spread and gained momentum to almost burn down the entire mountain, Hinode-no-Kami and his party were startled by the sudden brightening of their surroundings, and when they looked back, nearly the entire mountain had turned into a mountain of fire. The three deities, concerned about the fate of Ihatsuchibiko and Ihasuhime, turned back headlong and came climbing up the mountain in haste.

§ 2

At this time, there was a missionary climbing toward the mountain summit. This was Umegakahiko, the third son of Kunihiko, who was propagating the Ananai teachings of Mount Ōgonzan throughout the realm. Just as the fire, having burned up almost the entire mountain, was already about to spread to the foot of Tateiwa, Umegakahiko put forth all the strength in his body, concentrated his prayer to Ibukidonushi-no-Kami, and breathed upon the spreading flames; whereupon the wind suddenly changed direction, a tempest came blowing from the mountain summit, and in the twinkling of an eye, the fire vanished completely.

Just at that time, the night at last broke completely, and everything below the eighth station of the mountain had turned into a mountain of ash. Because the dwellings of the divine people at the foot of the mountain were all burned down, the residents at the foot made arrangements in all directions, wondering whose deed it was, and went around searching every corner of the mountain valleys. Mametora, Tayorihiko, Tokihiko, Yoshihiko, and Tamahiko, terrified and paralyzed by the immense fire, had gathered their heads in one place and were trembling with fear. Many people who had lost their dwellings appeared at this place and said in chorus:

"The ones who burned this mountain are surely you fellows. If you rebuild and restore things as they were, well and good; otherwise, we shall bind you, take you back, and sentence you to execution by burning at the stake before the chieftain."

Thus they yelled furiously with terrible countenances of wrath, whereat Mametora panicked and said:

"I-I-I-I, I... a-a-a-a li-li-li-li-ttle..."

From among the multitude:

"You gourd!"

Saying this, someone tapped him with a piece of stick in hand, whereupon Mametora raised his voice and began to weep, rubbing both eyes with his right upper arm:

"What an unlucky day this is! Having been saved at great pains by Hinode-no-Kami, no sooner did I think of returning quickly to cling to my beloved wife Kusakahime and embrace each other and weep, than I am now to weep and die here—how wretched! Saved by Hinode-no-Kami, am I now to be killed by the sunrise of fire? Kusakahime, at my dying moment, show me your gentle face just once. I do not begrudge this dying body of mine, but Kusakahime left behind will surely weep upon hearing this. When I think of it, how sad and pitiful it is!"

From among the crowd:

"Confound it! Blubbering with such a tearful face, we have no desire to hear such things. Who started the fire? Answer clearly!"

The four men merely hung their heads in silence. Mametora said:

"C-C-C-Certainly Tayorihiko did it."

As soon as he was about to say this, Tayorihiko shouted:

"Hey, you fool!"

§ 3

So saying, he struck Mametora's head once again with a broken stick with a thud. At this moment, Hinode-no-kami came down from the mountain top, and seeing this state of affairs, said:

"Ah, is that you, Mametora? What happened to your head? Why are you weeping?"

Mametora felt as though he had met a Buddha in hell, and said:

"Oh, oh, how wonderful of you to have come!"

So saying, he stood up, and cried:

"Hey, you, Tayorihiko, Tokihiko, Yoshihiko, Tamahiko, and all the rest of you fellows, listen well! This person is the augustly revered first retainer of Hinode-no-kami, the retainer of that retainer's retainer, and the retainer a little further below that—Lord Mametorahiko! The crime of committing such insolence shall not be forgiven!"

Assuming a posture like a leaping octopus, he squared his shoulders and elbows, suddenly regaining his vigor. Overawed by this threatening attitude, some were terrified, while others burst out laughing; yet, without a word, as if by mutual agreement, they prostrated themselves on the ground. This was because they were somehow struck with awe by the majesty of Hinode-no-kami, as well as Umegakahiko, Ōtodihawake, and Amenofukio. Mametora, believing they had surrendered to him, puffed his nose even more fiercely, and said:

"Hey, Tayorihiko, what did you say just now? You blurted out that Tamahiko made off with my wife while I was away, didn't you? Confess whether it is the truth! Seeing that you are my wife's brother, I have a mountain of desire to forgive you, yet in the way of the deities, there is no distinction between parents, children, siblings, or strangers. Hey, Tamahiko, what is your answer?"

Thus he began to bluster. Hinode-no-kami, singing a propagation hymn once again, was about to abandon this place and depart. Mametora said:

"Hello, where are you going, leaving your retainer behind? That is far too cruel!"

So saying, he clung to the deity's sleeve and wept.

Hinode-no-kami bestowed the name Kazakitsuwake-no-oshio upon Umegakahiko to reward his services, and placed Mametora and the other three in the custody of Ōyabiko, the chieftain at the foot of the mountain, ordering them to newly build houses for the people whose dwellings had burned down. Mametora was here given the name Kukunochi. Hinode-no-kami went down the mountain and crossed the sea; here the four deities parted company and advanced in all directions—east, west, south, and north—aimlessly, as messengers of propagation.

『まあまあ、よう来て下さいました』

と立上り、

『やいこら田依彦、時彦、芳彦、玉彦、その外みなの奴らよつく聞け。この方は勿体なくも日の出神の一の御家来、そのまた家来のその家来、もうちつと下のその家来、豆寅彦さまだぞ、無礼をひろいだその罪容赦はならぬ』

と章魚の跳る様な姿になつて肩肱怒らしにはかに元気づく。衆人はこの見幕に或は恐れ或は噴き出し、無言のまま言ひ合した様に大地に平伏したり。これは日の出神をはじめ梅ケ香彦、大戸日別、天吹男の威厳に何ンとなく打たれたる故なりき。豆寅は自分に降伏したものと思ひ、ますます鼻息荒く、

『やい田依彦、貴様は最前何と云うた。玉彦が俺の留守中に、俺の嬶をちよろまかしたと吐かしただらう、本当か白状いたせ。貴様は嬶の兄弟ぢやと思うて、許してやりたいは山々なれど、神の道には親子兄弟他人の区別はない。やい玉彦返答はどうだ』

と威張りだす。日の出神は又もや宣伝歌を歌ひながらこの場を見捨てて行かむとす。豆寅は、

『もしもし家来を捨てて何処に御越し遊ばす。夫れはあんまり胴欲ぢや』

と袖に縋つて泣き付く。

日の出神は梅ケ香彦に、風木津別之忍男と名を与へてその功労を賞し、豆寅以下の四人を山麓の酋長なる大屋毘古の身許に預けて、焼け失せたる人々の住家を新に造らしめたり。豆寅はここに久々能智といふ名を与へられける。日の出神は山を下り海を渡り四柱ここに袖を分ちて、東西南北に何処ともなく、宣伝使として進み行きける。
