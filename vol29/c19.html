Chapter 19: 生 Spirit の頼 [841]

生霊の頼〔841〕

§ 1

Before the famed Daibakufu, the party of six pushed forward braving the drizzle, and Takahime brought down the stone image from her back, fashioned a broom out of leaves and bamboo grass near the waterfall, swept and purified the place, scooped up with their own hands the gravel exposed by the Takitsubo, laid out cloth in its place, enshrined the stone image, plucked nuts from the surrounding trees and offered them, and further using oak branches as Tamagushi, offered them one by one, chanted the Amatsunorito, leaped into the Takitsubo, and performed misogi. The task of misogi having finally ended, they sat in tanza once more before the stone image, and proceeded to the iusai shiugefu.

By the request of Maru, she made him a kamunushi, had him sit upon the beautiful fine sand, and Takahime herself served in the role of saniha.

In the country of Brazil, renowned even in name, Rising high to press against the clouds, From Tenshiyōzan falling down, At the white waterfall of ikusenjiyō, The six of Takahime's party, Purifying their hearts and cleansing their bodies, When they performed misogi and harai, the air of seiriyō Filled the surroundings, With the feeling of the plum blossoms of Tokoyo's spring Fragrant on all four sides, They began their iusai shiugefu; With Takahime serving as saniha, Having Maru sit in tanza upon the sandy court, They reverently engaged in the rite Of divine-human communion. Maru's body shook violently, With his hands still clasped, Swinging them to the right and to the left, Raising and lowering his hands all the while, He began to groan, 'Un, un, un!'

Is it a lion-wolf or a field tengu? Is it a golden-haired nine-tailed fox or a crooked demon? Or is it merely a field fox or a raccoon dog? Possessed by a god with a bad posture, This fellow is terribly suspicious— Glaring with rounded eyes, Opening a mouth with bad teeth, While Takahime performs saniha, Maru's body rises high into the midheaven And falls back down. Seeing this state, Tsunehiko thought: This is surely the possession Of a deceptive, wayward god. Wishing to guide him with words and pacify him, And to teach him the true path of the divine realm, He stood by the side of Takahime the officiant, And while adopting the posture of chinkon With clasped hands, He directed his spirit toward the body of the kamunushi. Slowly, slowly, Maru grew calm,

While letting sweat flow in drops, Clenching his mouth into the shape of the character for 'he', Impatient to utter words, Yet with the kototama not easily coming forth, 'Waa, waa, waa, I am Ah, ah, ah, the missionary Of the divine teachings of Misoso, Ta, ta, ta, ta, the imperial messenger of Takayori, Or Tatsukuniwake, te, te, te, Or Telistan, ka, ka, ka, As part of the entourage of Karlins, Ku, ku, ku, Kurohime Has lost the golden jewel, Ta, ta, ta, ta, with eyes glaring at Takahime, Su, su, su, leaving behind the sacred land of Aya Where she was accustomed to living, Crossing the great ocean, To search for the whereabouts of the jewel, Ku, ku, ku, Kurohime and Takayamahiko As a married couple Went, wa, wa, wa, to Ryūgūjima in Wadachū. Takayorihime, together with three divine officiants, Crossed over, ta, ta, ta, to the secret island of Takasago, To search for the golden jewel, Advancing south along the road of Teru, Ah, ah, ah, hurting her feet, And finally arriving safely here at Takotori village; Following the god Hinode From times of old, times of old, Opening the teachings of Misosofu, Sa, sa, sa, the former site of Sayorihiko, Having her body struck by the waters of the waterfall of Arina, And beside the mirror pond, Building a hermitage, ta, ta, ta, Takayorihime stealthily entered the cave While carrying, ta, ta, ta, a bamboo tube, Deceiving Mitsuteruhiko, Ta, ta, ta, holding the bamboo tube In her toothless mouth, And blowing through it, "Fu, fu, fu," making a disguised voice: "Mitsuteruhiko Ōkami Has appeared here once again; People of Takasagojima, If you desire good fortune, virtue, and longevity, You must come to offer jewels. I will surely grant you boundless divine virtue," Sending forth the proclaiming gods of Te and Ka, The land of Asahi, Teru, and Hiru, The land of Haru where flowers bloom and scatter their fragrance, And feet marching forth into the land of Karu, Wandering as far as the land of Utsu, If you offer the precious jewel, da, da, da, Beside the mirror pond, There is surely divine virtue!" Thus she proclaimed here and there, And her efforts not being in vain, Numerous jewels gathered together, Shining her eyes and searching around one by one, Yet the essential golden jewel did not appear. Ko, ko, ko, no matter how long Such a thing is kept up, The essential and vital golden Rare divine treasure, de, de, de, Does not come out!" Te and Ka Began to grumble and murmur: "Divine officiant of Tatsukuniwake, Comfort the two and wait a while; So many and various jewels Are at last gathering near."

The most vital treasure of all, To the very rear guard, With the patience of 'Wait a while longer', While soothing and coaxing as she waits, Upon the white flag fluttering among the trees, Ah, bearing the inscription 'The Offering of the Golden Jewel', Escorted by a great multitude of people, Carrying a sacred palanquin, they climb up. At that very moment, The jeweled box, Right at the feet inside the cavern Where Takayorihime was hiding, Having flown so opportunely, That she had been masquerading as Tsukiterahiko Kamisama, she completely forgot in her confusion. Takayorihime, all by herself, Administers to the nursing of the three men, Until at last they slowly catch their breath. Binding together a grass hermitage, Enduring the loathsome shadows Of centipedes and snakes enfolding the poplar trunks, Until those shadows vanish, a trial of endurance— 'Shall we wait here Until the jewel naturally falls?' she says, as she steps Into the grass hermitage, Just at the moment of passing the night, Kera-kera-kera! rings a burst of laughter. Terrified, thinking it a specter or goblin, The three men blow foam from their mouths, Trembling in fear—how ludicrous indeed! Takayorihime steps forth To confront the mysterious voice, And when she negotiates, 'What is the meaning of this?- A venerable Kami has appeared, And commands you to cast away Your attachments utterly!' Takayorihime, too, breaks her stubborn pride, Transforming into a newborn babe, To expiate her sins across the lands of the four quarters, To open up the path of Truth, Parting the grass of the moor of Kunijihara, Moistening her throat with strawberries, Taking shelter from the rain beneath palm trees Thriving by the shores of the jeweled lake, 'This body entrusted unto the Kami— Who can foretell when or what kind of calamity May draw near?' Thinking to carve the figures of the party As a memento to leave behind, The stone statue that Tatsukuniwake With all his heart fashioned, Leaving it behind, they arrive at the port of Aru; The four of them board a ship here, And as they proceed further and further north, 'I, through my own error, fell Into the depths of the sea, And sank deep down to the waterbed,'— Just as Tatsukuniwake, to rescue And save his mother from mortal peril, Leaps forth with a bounding spring, Followed likewise by both Te and Kā.
