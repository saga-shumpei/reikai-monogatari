Chapter 13: Love 流 River [835]

愛流川〔835〕

§ 1

Takahime proceeds eastward and ever eastward through the vast wilderness and oak groves of Argentina, accompanied by Tsunehiko and Haruhiko. As far as the port of Aru, the distance is nearly three hundred and seventy or eighty ri. No matter how much they hasten, unless they spend a full month's time, they cannot reach the port of Aru. Threading their way among the pampas grass stocks to the right and left across a grassy plain where numerous lizards sluggishly crawl, eating sweet-tasting strawberries that ripen red over the fields as if by divine grace, and piling up five or six grass pillows, they finally emerged at a spot where trees grew somewhat thickly.

Here a moderately wide river flowed purely. Along the riverbank, giant pines and evergreen oaks grew neatly. Along the waterside, flowers such as bellflowers and patrinia bloomed in profusion out of season. It was just like an autumn grass field in the mainland. The three descended to the edge of the river, quenched their throats with the clear spring, gazed at the surrounding scenery, and recalled how troublesome the lizards, horseflies, gadflies, and blowflies had been as they came along, how delicious the taste of the strawberries was, and how the yellow, red, blue, white, purple, and various other beautiful wild flowers blooming to the very edges had comforted their travel-weary hearts, while giving thanks for the depth of divine grace.

Toward that place, an old woman of about seventy approached leisurely, wearing a grass raincoat, putting on a woven sedge hat, and leaning on a bamboo staff. The three gazed into the old woman's face with a suspicious look, thinking... Well, do people live in such a place?... The old woman beckoned the three with her hand and concealed herself in a small thatched house one or two cho upstream.

Tsunehiko: 'I say Takahime-sama, what could that old woman be? She went and crept into that small house at the root of that pine tree, but didn't she beckon us with a joyful look? Perhaps that old woman's spouse has fallen ill or something, and she has come to ask for our help. Whatever the case may be, why don't we drop by for a moment?'

Takahime: 'Ah well, since parting from Tamakō and Ryūkō, for six days until this very day, I have not seen the figure of a person, but today I have been able to meet a human being, which is a rare thing. At any rate, let's go as far as that old woman's hermitage. However, since we do not know how God may test us, you must not get angry under any circumstances.'

Tsunehiko: 'Yes, understood. I definitely will not get angry at all. Please rest assured. ...Now Haruhiko, you are the same, aren't you?'

Haruhiko: 'Yeah, I am just the same. Takahime-sama, come, let us go.'

The three finally arrived at the old woman's hermitage. The old woman welcomed the three with joy,

Old Woman: 'This is indeed, this is indeed, Izunome's propagation emissaries, how good of you to visit such an ugly thatched hut. Accordingly, there is something I earnestly wish to ask of you. Would there be any way you might listen, thinking of it as saving a single person?'

Takahime: 'Yes, if it is something within our power, please speak of whatever sort of matter it may be.'

Old Woman: 'That is a prompt consent, thank you very much. The truth of the matter is that my master at home has already crossed seven of the slope of eighty, and thinking to celebrate his square-measure celebration next year, his grandchildren and children were rejoicing, but finally from around the spring of this year, he was seized by a disease that people detest; saying that that master has a heavenly punishment disease and cannot be kept in the village, a makeshift house was built on the lonely riverbank of such a single-dwelling place, and an aging old woman is nursing him while enduring the rain and dew. I gathered various herbs of a hundred kinds, prepared medicine, and made him swallow it or applied it, but the illness only grew heavier day by day, his body became listless, a foul odor that cannot be described emanated, numerous flies alighted, and even for me, his wife, to look upon makes my hair stand on end. However, from four or five days ago, I have been seeing strange dreams continuously. That dream, you see, is that an Izunome propagation emissary would appear on the banks of that Aru River, so the goddess's dream oracle told me to rely on that person to heal him; and since I saw that exact same dream every single night, five nights in a row until last night, I have been waiting from this thatched hut, gazing at nothing but the river like jade. However, just as God said, you venerable propagation emissaries arrived in a party of three, and the joy when I beheld your figures resting by the river made hot tears spill forth unawares. Accordingly, the god's oracle said that even for this listless disease, if an Izunome propagation emissary came and licked clean the body's fluids and pus, he would recover completely on the spot. Truly, it is a fearful thing to request such a matter, but since it is a dream oracle from God, I thought it might offend you, yet I have ventured to mention it.'

Takahime looked down for a while, folded her arms, and pondered.

Takahime: 'Ah, very well, very well. I shall suck out any pus or fluid just as you request. There is a precedent in the island of Riugū where Hatsuwakahime, Tamanohime, and their party saved a leper by sucking out the bloody pus. Come, please guide me to the Zashiki of the old man.'

Old Woman: 'Yes, thank you. I shall guide you.'

She stood up and proceeded to the inner room. Though it was called an inner room, it was merely a small house of two rooms partitioned by walls of Kayakusa. Tsunehiko and Haruhiko followed Takahime into the inner room. Looking, they saw golden flies swarming in a solid black mass. The old man was lying on his back, reduced to skin and bone, his body covered in pus, and he was suffering in a state of half-death, left as he was to be sucked by the flies. As soon as Takahime recited the Amatsunorito, the many golden flies, without a single one remaining, buzzed loudly and flew out of the window. Takahime placed her mouth on the old man's body and began to suck the bloody pus from around his chest. Tsunehiko from the feet and Haruhiko from the head, without showing any sign of disgust, and with their hearts full of the desire to save this old man, forgot themselves and calmly sucked the foul-smelling pus.

The old man said, 'Un,' and sprang up. Looking, it was a wonder; he had become a beautiful woman of fair age, revealing skin of purple-gold.

Beautiful Woman: 'Ya, Takahime, I have seen the depths of your heart. I am the incarnation of Konohanahime-no-Mikoto who dwells on Mount Tenkyō. From now on, you shall surely be able to serve in the divine work as a magnificent pillar of God. You will encounter the trials of God many more times. If you break through them, your true physical body will become the living shrine of the Hinodewake and serve Him; this shall not be a difficult thing. You must never become arrogant. Also, Tsunehiko and Haruhiko, it is best that you make the practice of non-action the first priority so that you do not mistake the teachings of the San-go-kyō. Since Konohanahime has arranged this for the three of you, you must never be off your guard in the future.'

Takahime and the other two replied, 'Hai,' and prostrated themselves.

From somewhere, a fragrant scent wafted in, the sound of music rang out clearly and brilliantly, and a cool breeze passed through the window, brushing the faces of the three.

When they suddenly raised their heads, how could this be? There was no thatched hut, no figure of the old man or woman, nor the figure of the goddess; they were still dozing and nodding off in a boat on the riverbank during the day.

Takahime sighed and said,

Takahime: 'Ah, was that just a dream? I received a most magnificent divine virtue in my dream. It must be because it was a dream that I was able to do such a thing. No, no, I must truly become that heart in reality. Ah, how grateful, how grateful.'

She kept murmuring to herself.

Tsunehiko: 'Takahime-sama, I also had a dream. It was a very self-serving dream. The three of us, including Haruhiko, were made to care for a filthy sick person and were made to suck the bloody pus, but it tasted like nectar, something beyond description. As I was absorbed in sucking it, I thought it was a filthy old man, but it was Konohanasakuya-hime-sama of Mount Tenkyō; she showed us everything from the extreme of ugliness to the extreme of beauty. ...Takahime-sama, was your dream the same? ...Haruhiko, how was your dream?'

Haruhiko: 'No, there is not the slightest difference. It seems the three of us all had the same dream. What a strange thing. That filthy sick person might be a reflection of our own hearts. There is no doubt that Konohanasakuya-hime-Ōkami-sama is sucking away the filth of our spirits, just as we sucked the pus of the sick person, for our own spirits are as filthy and wretched as that. Ah, it is truly awe-inspiring. This is surely not about someone else; it must be that we were shown our own souls. Isn't that so, Takahime-sama?'

Takahime: 'That is certainly true. If viewed from the perspective of the Kamisama, my Mitama must be rotting with filth and wallowing in sloth. Ah, Kamunagara Tamachihahe Mase. May You sweep away and purify all my various sins and defilements.'

She suddenly clasps her hands together in earnest prayer.

Tsunehiko: 'Takahime-sama, no matter what you say, you are of the lineage of the Henjiyōnanshi, so even if you say you are defiled, it is only a trifling matter. That state of defilement is surely nothing but a projection of our own Mitama.'

Takahime: 'Please do not speak of the lineage of the Henjiyōnanshi anymore. If you were to call someone like me by that lineage, it would truly injure the Goshintoku of the Henjiyōnanshisama. From now on, I shall never speak of the lineage of the Henjiyōnanshi again, so please, you too, keep that in mind.'

Haruhiko: 'Even so, facts are facts, so there is nothing that can be done about it.'

Takahime: 'If I am to be of that lineage, it would be shameful if I could not perform deeds befitting it. Until I have achieved a splendid Kaishin, until my sincerity reaches the Ten, and until the Ōkami-sama says to me, "Because you are of that lineage, your deeds are different from all others; you are a true mirror of sincerity," I am not worthy of that lineage. Since I am one who would injure the virtue of the Henjiyōnanshisama, please, for the time being, stop calling me by that lineage.'

Haruhiko: 'Things certainly change when they change. You used to repeat the word "lineage" day in and day out, but Kaishin is a terrifying thing indeed. In that case, I too shall change my attitude toward you from now on.'

Takahime: 'Yes, I shall change as well, so please treat me as a younger sister in the path of the teachings, without regard for status. I shall never again treat you as a disciple or a servant as I have until now.'

Tsunehiko: 'I shall also interact with you with that intention. However, what have you done regarding the matter of the living shrine of the Hinodewake?'

Takahime: 'Please, I beg you, do not speak of such things. It was not the Hinodewake-sama, but the Golden-furred Nine-tailed Fox that possessed my body and made me say such things and filled me with arrogance. Beneath the white poplar tree in Ichigahara, it revealed its true form and departed from my body completely. Therefore, the me of today is neither a living shrine of a true Kamisama nor a den of an evil god. From here on, I must have my main guardian deity firmly in place and serve the purpose of the splendid Kamisama.'

Tsunehiko: 'Ah, that is excellent. When I was looking toward the east from the summit of Mount Arina, I saw the Golden-furred Nine-tailed evil fox riding on a black cloud, heading toward the land of Tokoyo, fleeing with great force. It must have departed from your body at that time.'

Takahime: 'Ah, was that so? It is a terrifying thing. When it left my body, it showed its form for a fleeting moment; it stood before me suddenly, wearing a long, white-faced robe that seemed to fill an eight-mat room. When I glared at it, saying, "You wretched Golden-furred Nine-tailed evil fox," it instantly transformed into the Golden-furred Nine-tailed fox, and with many glowing objects like peacock tail feathers attached to the tips of its tails, it soared into the sky and fled toward the northern horizon. You must have seen that very moment. Ah, how terrifying; it gave me the chills. Kamunagara Tamachihahe Mase, Kamunagara Tamachihahe Mase.'

Tsunehiko: 'By the way, Takahime-sama, how shall we cross this great river? There is no bridge, and there is nothing we can do, is there? If we had wings, we could fly, but this is a wide and deep river, and a rapid current at that, so we cannot swim across; it is a predicament. What shall we do?'

Takahime: 'There is no way but to pray to the Kamisama. This too is surely a trial from the Kamisama. In any case, let us cross, taking the Kamisama as our strength and sincerity as our staff. Kamunagara Tamachihahe Mase, Kamunagara Tamachihahe Mase.'
