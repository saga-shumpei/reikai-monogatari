Chapter 18: 天祥 Mountain [840]

天祥山〔840〕

§ 1

Floating amidst the vast ocean, on the island of Kārin, there lived notorious and lawless villains named Māru and Bōru. Hated by the men and women of the island, and finding themselves with no other recourse, they took advantage of the dark night to steal secretly into the house of Yobu, pillaging his treasures and escaping under the cover of darkness. Although the heavenly net is vast and wide, with meshes sparse, yet in accordance with the rule that nothing ever slips through, their fleeing figure was caught at the doorway by Yobu, the master of that house. Being called out to and asked, 'Are you Māru and Bōru?', they shrank back in terror, realizing that if they loitered there, they would surely be subjected to the island's laws and face execution by hanging on the morrow. Concluding that nothing surpassed flight, the two of them stole a small boat at the water's edge,

and rowing with all their might across the wind-tossed and churning open sea, they headed ever northward. Tossed by a sudden violent gale like falling leaves in autumn, and barely managing to cling to their precious threads of life, they drifted ashore at the port of Zemu. When they looked back and reflected upon it, the crime they had committed was merely the result of a momentary impulse, and the terror of it weighed heavily upon them. Plunged into a cloud of anxiety as if pursuers were pressing upon them from behind, they resolved to make atonement for their sins before the gods of heaven and earth. Thus, they went to the great waterfall on Mount Tenshō—the Hando Waterfall—where they subjected their bodies to its cascading waters and steadfastly performed ascetic practices for seven days and seven nights. Just as they were engaged in this, terrifyingly enough, a monstrous beast of Mōrubando

unexpectedly came shuffling out right before them, brandishing the sharp blade at the tip of its tail with violent swings and charging directly at the two men. With their retreat completely cut off and their backs to the wall, Māru and Bōru steadied their hearts, bowed to heaven and earth, and with all their might chanted the great norito, the heavenly numbersong: 'Hifumi, yitsu mutsu nanatsu yatsu, kokonotsu to momo yorozu yoro zuma', praying with all their devoted hearts. As the Mōrubando, without showing any mercy, gathered all its strength into its tail to strike down the two men, a propaganda song was suddenly heard. As it drew closer and closer by degrees, even the ferocious beast gradually shrank and shrivelled up,

withdrawing its weapon, curling in its tail, lowering its head, and slinking clumsily away to hide its body in the surrounding woods before finally turning into white clouds and vanishing. Māru and Bōru, finding themselves in this situation of surviving by a miracle, drew close, joined their hands, knelt upon the ground, and asked with tears: 'What god are you, living deity, who have graciously saved us?' Beginning with the divine officials Takayohime and Ryūkokubetsu of the Misrokai, along with four miraculous divine officials including Tērisutan and Kārinsu, the two prostrated themselves, expressed their words of thanks and gratitude for the saving of their lives, and becoming disciples of Takayohime, they immersed their bodies in the waters of this waterfall, morning and evening praying to the great kami

and saving the many people who came on pilgrimage here, thus spending joyous months and days. However, today marked the very first anniversary of that miraculous day when they had snatched their lives back from the jaws of death. Resolved to repay even a fraction of their deep debt of gratitude to their benefactor of life, and determined to manifest their utmost sincerity before the divine presence of the gods of heaven and earth by punishing Takahime—she who had oppressed their benefactor and teacher of the teachings, Takayohime—and awaiting her arrival here, they had been lying in wait. Just at that very moment, Takahime, accompanied by Tsunehiko, Haruhiko, and leading Yobu, came advancing leisurely, pointing toward Mount Tenshō—a party of four coming forward. Māru and Bōru thought, 'This is indeed the target, Takahime!'

and brandishing their nine-sun-five-bu daggers, thrust them to the right and to the left. Takahime, as might be expected, shifted her body and leapt backward like a flying bird. Thereupon Yobu-sama of Kārin Island broke into the space between the two and called out, 'Wait just a moment, you two! Are you not the two men, Māru and Bōru? How is it that you have come here? Let me hear how things stand.' Hearing the name of Yobu, the two men were startled and flustered, their hands trembling; rebuked by the ghosts within their hearts, yet admirable in their readiness to repent, they cried out: 'Ah, Kannagara kamugamishi, may the divine spirits be blessed and prosper! Through this gathering of those bound by karma, upon this pathway the Way of the Gods is clearly and exhaustively preached, and the inner hearts of Takayohime and Takahime, purer than snow,

are shining brightly—how venerable it is! Ah, Kannagara kamugamishi, may the divine spirits be blessed and prosper!'

Guided by the admonitions of Takahime and Yobu, Māru and Bōru were enlightened with complete clarity, recognizing the errors of their own thoughts, and it was thus decided that they would proceed as guides to the Hando Waterfall on Mount Tenshō.

§ 2

Here, the party of six bravely sang Sendenka songs along the way, threatening giant lizards and horseflies by the roadside, and soon reached the foot of Tenshiyōzan. To the Handu Falls, there was still a distance of fourteen or fifteen chō. Even so, being a famous waterfall second only to the waterfalls of Niagara, the rushing sound of the waterfall could be heard as if taking it in hand. The voices of oil cicadas and niino-semi crying were loud enough to deafen the ears. As expected of this region of Brazil in the tropical country, the cool breeze blowing down from Tenshiyōzan for a distance of two or three ri made it just like autumn in Naichi; as they approached the waterfall, it felt chilly to the skin, and even their teeth began to chatter.

Tsunehiko: 'It is a remarkably cool place, ah. Ever since crossing over to Takasagojima, it is the first time I have experienced such a cool sensation. I heard from a passenger on the ship that all sorts of terrifying ferocious beasts dwell in this mountain, is it not truly a terrifying sight?'

Māru: 'In this mountain, ferocious beasts such as lions, wild dogs, tigers, and bears appear and disappear, showing themselves in the villages night after night and causing harm to the elderly and children, so we enshrine the deities of Izumooeki beside this waterfall and offer up prayers morning and evening for the safety of the people. Thanks to that divine virtue, these days the shadows of ferocious beasts have also decreased considerably. Compared to two years ago, there are no longer even one-twentieth of them. And there is yet another terrifying monster called Mōrubando that comes at times. That beast has a body like gathering ten elephants, with four webbed feet having long claws, and at the tip of a tail like an alligator's is attached a sharp sword, and it is a terrifying animal that whips and strikes dead all ferocious beasts with its tail and eats them. These days, because ferocious beasts have become few, Mōrubando rarely comes either, but if by any chance one falls into its sight, whether human or beast, without mercy it beats them to death one after another with its tail and eats them all up, so you must be quite careful. Sometimes when it gets hot, it lies down at the Handu Falls and bathes in the waterfall water, sleeping while snoring loudly. I too have spotted it four or five times since last year. At such times, it is the better strategy to steal softly on tiptoe and not approach. If one has divine virtue like the propagandists of Takayohime, one can scatter them using Kototama, but for those of us utterly lacking in divine virtue, not approaching is the best thing. However, today is the anniversary of having my life saved by Takayohime-sama at that waterfall, so from now on we must hold a festival beneath that waterfall and offer our thanks to the Great Deities of Heaven and Earth. Fortunately, you all are serving as propagandists, so will you not please lend a spirited hand to this festival?'

Takahime: 'That is the most convenient thing of all.'

So saying, they had already arrived close to the side of the waterfall.

The spray of the grand waterfall scattered about and, like a mist, thickly enveloped the trees around. A deep mist hung thick to the extent that even each other's figures could not be clearly seen.
