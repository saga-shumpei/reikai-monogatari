Chapter 2: 懸 Bridge 御殿 [824]

懸橋御殿〔824〕

§ 1

Tatsukuniwake, Telistan, and Karlins, catching a fleeting glimpse through the trees of the banner proclaiming the presentation of the golden jewel, felt their hearts leap and their nerves suddenly tense. Adopting a solemn and dignified air, as if wholly absorbed in prayer, they prayed with all their might with feigned innocence. Meanwhile, having his palanquin carried and accompanied by numerous villagers, Ahr, the chief of Tena in the land of Hiru, arrayed in full festive attire, made his way with difficulty along the narrow path. Glancing sideways at the waterfall of Arina, he arrived before this cave, was deeply moved upon seeing the three figures at prayer, and quietly had his palanquin set down upon a beautiful rock nearby. Becoming utterly engrossed, he turned toward the Mirror Pond, kneeling and clasping his hands in reverence. The entire company, maintaining absolute silence, lined up in ranks behind the chief of Tena, knelt, and worshipped the interior of the cave across the Mirror Pond.

From within the cave, in a strange, unearthly voice stripped of its normal resonance,

Tsukiteruhikonomikoto is here. Hand over immediately the golden jewel to the shinshi who is there, O you soul of exquisite purity who have brought it hither from afar. Your name is bestowed by the god Tsukiteruhiko as Kunitamayoriwakemikoto.

Chief Ahr prostrated himself with a start,

Ahr: 'With all due reverence, Lord Tsukiteruhiko Ōkami, I am Ahr, a humble servant of the gods, and chief of the village of Tena in the land of Hiru. Having heard that the gods are gathering various jewels in accordance with the divine administration, I have consulted with my wife, Princess Aruna, and brought here this very day the golden jewel that our family has treasured and carefully guarded. I humbly pray that You will examine and accept it, and both my wife Aruna and I will be filled with supreme awe and gratitude.'

From within the rock cave,

Kunitamayoriwakemikoto, the time has arrived for the gods to voice their words before mortals, but for Me to manifest My physical form is not permitted by the rules of the divine realm. Therefore, let Tatsukuniwake the sanjakusha stationed there receive it as the proxy of the god Tsukiteruhiko; you would do well to understand it thus.

The couple, Ahr and Aruna, shed tears of gratitude in sheer astonishment,

Ahr and Aruna: 'Truly, these are wondrous divine words, a joy beyond compare. ... O shinshi of Tatsukuniwake, I humbly beseech you to examine this precious jewel well and receive it.'

Tatsukuniwake opened his mouth with labored reluctance,

Tatsukuniwake: 'Were you not the chief of the village of Tena, renowned in rumor? Since you are a soul deeply tied to the divine realm by karma, you have been able to perform such a magnificent sacred service. Be deeply grateful. ... Ho there, you two, Tei and Kā! Examine quickly the golden precious jewel presented by Chief Aruna and bring it forth before Me.'

Tei and Kā muttered in undertones,

Tei and Kā: 'What is this? How utterly ridiculous. That fellow Tatsukuniwake suddenly uses pompous kototama and treats us like mere servants. What an outrageous fellow. It would be splendid if this truly turns out to be the golden jewel we are searching for, but if by any chance it should be a mere gilded trinket, the two of us will only be compounding our foolishness.'

So whispering, they stepped forward to the front of the palanquin. Aruna reverently opened the door of the palanquin, took out the jewel box, and presented it before the two men, Tei and Kā. Taking the jewel box into their hands from left and right, the two men held it high above their heads and, moving sideways like crabs, began to step gingerly with their four feet. Their minds being too captivated by the jewel box overhead, they paid no heed to their footing, and the moment they crashed and stumbled against a rocky corner, the two of them plunged headlong into the Mirror Pond along with the jewel box, making a loud plop. Tatsukuniwake, the chief and his wife, and all the rest rose to their feet as one, cried out 'Ah!', and gazed upon the surface of the pond. Fortunately, struck by the momentum of the two men's fall, the jewel box conveniently bounded right into the interior of the cave.

Princess Takayori inside the cave, captivated in her heart by the jewel box that had flown so vigorously right to her feet, forgot even the presence of the chief and the other people outside. Clutching the jewel box to her breast, she hurried out of the cave with brisk steps and brought it before Tatsukuniwake,

From here, for the sake of taking leave at this Mirror Pond, they recited the Tianjin Norito, prayed well for what comes after,

§ 3

Praying with itsushiyōkenmei, the party of four, lost in the darkness, crossed mountains as if clouds were mist, and continuing through the night, finally arrived at the plain called Kunugi-ga-hara in the land of Uzu.

The chief and his wife of Aruna, convinced of the shinji of Tsukiteruhiko, finished their itsuchiuya of misogi, and when they came respectfully before the Mirror Pond, the hermitage was empty like a cicada shell, and neither the voice of the Kamisama nor the figures of the three men were to be seen. 'How fushigi,' they thought, and as they searched the hermitage thoroughly, they found a pile of beautiful stones, and upon them, the Tamabako that the jibun had brought was placed neatly. Looking closely, the same monku as before was written upon it. The chief and his wife placed the mikoshi that had been sent on the opposite side of the Mirror Pond, at the entrance of the gankutsu, enshrined the Tamabako within it once more, and firmly believing in the written monku, the husband became Kunitamayoriwakenomikoto and the wife became Tamatatsuhimenomikoto. They offered kiguwan asayufu before the mikoshi, and at night they entered the hermitage, making it their dwelling, and for the sake of the people who came from all directions, they offered kiguwan for fukutoku-enman, jumyō-chōkyū, and byōki-heiyu.

The propaganda of the two, Te and Ka, seemed to have been highly successful, and day by day, in continuous succession, people came carrying stone blocks in the shape of jewels or bringing various nuts to offer before the deity. The number of those who requested kiguwan from the couple Kunitamayoriwake and returned after receiving appropriate goshintoku increased month by month. Eventually, due to the excessive number of pilgrims, there was no room to contain them, and the area around the Mirror Pond, which was not very wide, became so crowded that one could not move. Unavoidably, after consultation among the many believers, they built bridges from valley to valley, constructed a grand Yahiro-dono, and there re-enshrined the Tamabako. The couple served before the deity as kannushi, with people such as Kuni, Tama, Yori, Ryū, and Wake serving as executives, and they came to propagate the teachings of the 3-5 God day and night. Because this Yahiro-dono was built blocking the valley like a bridge, it was called the Kakehashi-no-Goten.

The couple Kunitamayoriwake, feeling suspicious because strange noises were heard in the sanctuary every night at the hour of the ox, decided on a night when the fifteen-day moon was shining brightly, without informing the executives, to sit upright before the deity, just the two of them, and wait to investigate the true nature of the strange noises. From beneath the floor of the sanctuary, strange smoke rose up with a puff, and a grotesque deity with a bald head like an octopus, appearing hazy, emerged from the smoke with its waist bent in the shape of a 'ku'. It circled around the palanquin many times, spinning round and round; its strangeness and repulsiveness were profound. Tamatatsuhime lost her nerve, let out a scream of 'Kya!', and collapsed on the spot. Kunitamayoriwake, being a man of stout heart, faced the strange shadow and spoke in a stern voice:

Kunitamayoriwake: 'I am the kannushi who, obeying the command of the god Tsukiteruhiko, has enshrined the goshinrei in the Tamabako and serves here morning and evening, having built this Yahiro-dono. Who are you, appearing night after night in this sacred sanctuary without our permission, a suspicious person lurking around the goshinpō? State your name. Depending on your answer, I shall show no mercy.'

He stood his ground, bracing himself. The strange shadow increased in density, approached the collapsed Tamatatsuhime without a sound, and when it stretched out a thin hand and stroked her chest two or three times, the princess immediately regained consciousness. She clasped her hands together toward the monster and, without fear, expressed her gratitude.

Kunitamayoriwake, not understanding, watched the figure of the monster in silence. The monster spoke in a cool, thin voice:

Monster: 'I am the reitai of the god Sayohiko, who was propagating the 3-5 teachings at the gankutsu of the Mirror Pond, upstream of the Arina waterfall. After I returned to the spirit world, there was no one to worship my soul, and not a single offering was made to me. However, one year ago, the propaganda envoy of the 3-5 teachings, Takayorihime and Ryūkokubetsu, brought two attendants, came to the gankutsu of the Mirror Pond, gathered jewels, and finally stole the golden jewel, the treasure of your house, and returned with it. I appear night after night to make this known to you. They replaced the contents of that Tamabako with agate beads and made off with the golden jewel; the four of them are now out at Kunugi-ga-hara in the land of Uzu, wandering here and there on the plain. Do you not have the intention to take back that jewel?'

国玉依別夫婦は、毎夜丑満の頃に、神殿に怪しき物音の聞ゆるに不審を起し、十五の月の照り輝く夜、幹部にも知らさず、夫婦只二人、神前に端坐して、怪しき物音の正体を調べむと待構へてゐた。神殿の床下より怪しき煙ポーツと立昇り、朦朧として蛸の様な禿頭の不細工な一柱の神、腰をくの字に曲げて、煙の中より現はれ、輿のぐるりを幾回となく、クルクルと廻つてゐる、其怪しさ、厭らしさ。玉竜姫は肝を潰し『キヤツ』と悲鳴をあげて其場に倒れた。国玉依別は流石気丈の男子とて、怪しき影に向ひ、言葉厳かに、

『吾こそは月照彦神の命を奉じ、御神霊を玉筥に納め、朝夕此八尋殿を作りて奉仕する神司なるぞ。汝何者なるか、吾々の許しもなく、神聖なる神殿に夜な夜な現はれ来り、御神宝の辺りに附纏ふ曲者、名を名乗れ、返答次第に依つては容赦は致さぬぞ』

とキツとなつて身構へした。怪しき影は追々濃厚の度を増し、そこに倒れたる玉竜姫の傍に足音も立てず寄添ひ、細き手を伸べて、二三回胸のあたりを撫でさすれば、姫は忽ち正気づき、両手を合せて怪物に向ひ、恐れ気もなく感謝の意を表し居たりける。

国玉依別は合点行かず、黙然として怪物の姿を眺めて居た。怪物は涼しき、細き声にて、

怪物『吾れこそはアリナの滝の水上、鏡の池の岩窟に三五の教を宣伝し居たる狭依彦の神の霊体である。吾れ帰幽後は、一人として吾霊魂を祭る者なく、御供一つ供へ呉るる者もなし。然るに一年以前、三五教の宣伝使鷹依姫、竜国別は二人の供人を引連れ、鏡の池の岩窟に来り、玉集めをなし、遂に汝が家の重宝黄金の玉を奪ひ取り帰りたり。吾れは此事を汝に知らさむが為に夜な夜な出現するものなり。其玉筥の中は瑪瑙の玉と摺替へおき、黄金の玉を引さらへて、四人の者は今や宇都の国の櫟が原に出で、草原をあちらこちらとさまよひつつあり。汝は其玉を取返す所存なきか』

§ 4

Kunitamayori: 'Thinking it was some Kami, I find it was none other than Sayorihiko-sama, who is famed as the Kami of the Mirror Pond. I thank you for your kindness, but as I have resigned myself to everything being a matter of karmic fate, even if a golden ball were to turn into an agate ball, it would not trouble me in the least. As long as it is the august spirit of Tsukiteruhiko that is made to hang upon it, even if it were a dumpling-stone ball, to me I know not how much more precious it would be. I hold not the slightest attachment to golden balls or the like.'

Sayorihiko: 'Truly, deeply touched am I by your mindset; precisely for that reason will the teachings of the Kami of Ananai-kyō spread throughout Tenka, and be able to save the banmin. Sayorihiko, too, is touched by the noble and pure sincerity of you husband and wife, and from this time forth, serving hidden before this shinzen, I shall assist your kamiwaza. By no means doubt it.'

And once more, amidst a sudden rising smoke, he made his wondrous figure disappear. From this time forth, to enshrine the spirit of Sayorihiko, the husband and wife built a shrine beside the Mirror Pond, morning and evening presented various offerings, and their reverence knew no slackening. By virtue of the spiritual power of Tsukiteruhiko no Kami, the protection of Sayorihiko, and the majestic virtue of Kunitamachin, the divine altar solemnly enshrined deep within the inner chambers of the Kakehashi no Goten shone with divine virtue day by day and month by month, and at length the great fame of the married couple Kunitamayoriwake spread far and wide throughout the entirety of Takasagojima.

As the saying goes, even the head of a sardine comes from faith; when one sees that the golden ball was switched, and even the agate ball that bore no resemblance whatsoever came to emit an infinite and supreme brilliance through the power of the divine spirit of the Kami and the sincerity of faith, one should be able to realize that treasures of a physical form are things not overly worthy of respect. Ah, Kannagara Tamashii Sakiwaimase.

By the way, it is said that the party of Tatsukowake crossed the waves of ten thousand leagues of distant ocean to seek the whereabouts of the ball, precisely because they blindly believed in the return of the spirit into Takayahime. Of those who fall into superstition regarding the return of spirits, there can be none more pitiful. Nevertheless, on the other hand, the fact that they were thereby able to carry out the propagation and proclamation of the teachings overseas must be called the divine intent.

国玉依『何れの神様かと思へば、昔鏡の池の神として有名なる狭依彦様で御座いましたか。御親切は有難う御座いまするが、何事も因縁づくと諦めて居りますれば、仮令黄金の玉が瑪瑙の玉に変ればとて、少しも苦しうは御座いませぬ。月照彦の御神霊の懸らせ玉ふ以上は、仮令団子石の玉にても、私に取つては、それの方が何程重宝だか知れませぬ。黄金の玉などには少しも執着はかけませぬ』

狭依彦『実に感じ入つたる其方の心掛け、それでこそ三五教の神の教は天下に拡まり、万民を救う事が出来るであらう。狭依彦も汝夫婦が尊き、清き真心に感じ、これより此神前に幽仕して、汝が神業を助けむ。ゆめゆめ疑ふ事勿れ』

と又もやパツと立上る煙の中に、怪しき姿を消しにける。これより夫婦は狭依彦の霊を祀るべく鏡の池の傍に宮を造り、朝夕に種々の供物を献じ、崇敬怠らざりき。月照彦神の霊力と狭依彦の守護と、国魂神の威徳に依つて、懸橋の御殿の奥深く斎き奉れる神壇は日に月に神徳輝き、遂には高砂島全部に国玉依別夫婦の盛名は隈なく喧伝さるるに至りける。

鰯の頭も信心からとやら、黄金の玉は掏り替られ、似ても似つかぬ瑪瑙の玉も神の神霊の力と、信仰の誠に依つて無限絶大なる光輝を放つに至りしを見れば、形体上の宝の、余り尊重すべき物にあらざるを悟り得らるるなるべし。あゝ惟神霊幸倍坐世。
