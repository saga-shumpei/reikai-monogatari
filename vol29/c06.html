Chapter 6: Jewel の行衛 [828]

玉の行衛〔828〕

§ 1

Takahime softened her tone,

Takahime: 'Hey there, Tsunehiko-sama. I brought you along because I thought you were the most quick-witted and splendid person among all the Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō, and... well, my eyes as Takahime have not deceived me. You were sharp enough to notice that Kotoyoriwake and Kuniyoriwake were on the ship.'

Tsunehiko: 'Well, a snake knows the way of a snake. The scent of Kotoyoriwake and Kuniyoriwake has been bothering my nose ever since they boarded the ship, so I had been thinking about it for a while, and I finally realized that this fellow was suspicious and tracked it down.'

Haruhiko: 'You are a man with a nose as sharp as a dog's. It is not the way of a snake, but the way of a dog for a boar, is it not? And did Kotoyoriwake-sama and Kuniyoriwake really have the splendid jewel with them?'

Tsunehiko: 'The passenger who threw you a rope when you danced into the sea and fell in was Kuniyoriwake. In short, you had your life saved by Kuniyoriwake-sama.'

Haruhiko: 'Ah, is that so? That is grateful. I was going to offer my thanks, but since you did not tell me, I ended up being rude. As expected, Kuniyoriwake-sama is kind. Secondly, there is an Ikigami who says that if you do not properly cast off those who become a burden, such a fool will become a nuisance; the world is full of various things. And did you see the splendid jewel?'

Tsunehiko: 'The heavenly secret must not be leaked. Do not speak in such a loud voice. Is Takahime-sama not listening right there? When Takahime-sama is not around, I will let only you know the one-rin secret. Oops, I finished it, I accidentally spoke too loudly... Say, Takahime-sama, did you hear what I just said? You probably did not hear it too clearly, did you? It would be terrible if you did. Ah, Kuwabara, Kuwabara, one must be cautious with one's Kototama, ha ha ha.'

Takahime: 'Hey, Tsunehiko-sama, you should not be teasing me like that. Speak plainly at once. If you do, I, Takahime, will become the leader of the Nishiki-no-Miya, not to mention making you the general manager and using you for splendid divine work. Just try to see the world of Miroku emerge. It is a divine arrangement where such a person becomes this kind of person, and that is why they say you cannot fathom the gods.'

Tsunehiko: 'Ha ha, if so, then my turning into Anna and Kanan was not entirely in vain. I am Anna, Haruhiko is Kanan, I am a person who was this and became that, and Haruhiko becomes a splendid human, and even Takahime-sama or anyone else will eventually become a splendid human called Kanan—is that the omen? Ha ha ha ha. This is all because Kuniyoriwake-sama kindly warned me strictly never to tell anyone about the whereabouts of the jewel, which is truly grateful. He understood my soul well. They say a warrior dies for the one who knows him, and there is nothing more grateful than a person who sees through one's true heart. Since I was trusted as a man and was shown the whereabouts of the secret jewel of great importance, and even allowed to see the real thing, I will not speak of it until Kuniyoriwake-sama says I may, even if this head were to be torn off. Ah, if I do not speak, I do not understand, and if I speak, I must not; it is a difficult arrangement... the gods say in the Ofudesaki, and this is likely what it means. The phrases of the Ofudesaki come to me one after another, and it feels as if they are soaking into my body.'

Takahime: 'You do not tell the people you should not, and you try to tell the people you should not, which is why Kuniyoriwake-sama strictly forbade you to speak. Try to think about it carefully. The fact that he revealed the secret to you, who have come as my attendant, is a riddle meaning that you should let Takahime know. If he had told you to convey this matter in detail to Takahime, you might have felt at ease and forgotten it, so by telling you not to speak, he made you think it was a matter of great importance, and he made you keep it in your mind, thinking that you would secretly tell me, and he spoke to you while anticipating the future. Kuniyoriwake is quite clever. He is a man who talks a lot of logic, but I thought he was a man with a certain fragrant quality. ...Hey, hey, Tsunehiko, tell me, I will surely take care of the rest...'

It would be like smearing mud on the face of the spirit body of the Hensei-danshi, and I would have nothing to say to the Hinodegami-sama. That is why you can be at ease. To speak before or to speak now is the same thing. Without being so reluctant to share, since it is not a matter of your own stomach aching, would it not be good if you told me clearly with your mouth that it is thus? Come, Tsunehiko, you truly are a good-natured person. Do not sulk so stiffly, and please tell me quickly.'

Takahime: 'Hey Tsunehiko, what kind of thing are you saying? Is this not the height of heaven and earth turning upside down? Who on earth is going to become your disciple? I am, if you please, the living shrine of the Hinodegami. Do not make too much of a fool of me.'

Tsunehiko: 'Even a cat, having caught a rat it loves so much, would hardly gobble it down just like that. Taking it in its mouth and tossing it up, taking it in its mouth and tossing it up, chasing it and holding it down, teasing it over and over and over again, and in the end teasing it to death to enjoy eating it—if this story too is told so directly, it is a big incident and will lose its value. Come, follow me while enjoying it. In exchange, I will let you know when a certain time comes, so we must make a promise. Takahime-sama, the one who is taught things is the disciple, and the one who teaches is the teacher, is that not so?'

Takahime: 'That is a settled matter. The one who teaches is the teacher. That is why you people have become my disciples, is it not?'

Tsunehiko: 'Ah, I understand from that. If that is your thought, by and by I shall teach you the whereabouts of the jewel. In exchange, from today I am the teacher and you are the disciple. Come, take the luggage and follow along.'

Tsunehiko: 'This is, this is a most impudent thing to say. In that case, please teach me everything. Since I have no qualification to teach, I shall say nothing more from this very moment. If I say I will teach you, I will receive a slap in the face, so that way is better. From this very moment, just as you said last night, take the people of this country as your disciples, and leave us—the temple-bell thief and the lizard-faced one—to act on our own. ...Say, Haruhiko, having the temple-bell thief and the lizard-faced one following along would be a hindrance to Takahime-sama, so shall we part ways with this?'

Haruhiko: 'I have no earthly idea what is going on, and it is getting to the point where I cannot make heads or tails of it at all. Hey Tsunehiko, instead of saying such mean things, why don't you act like a man and tell Takahime-sama straightforwardly?'

Takahime: 'Ugh, what a sickeningly stubborn fellow you are. Very well, very well, before long I shall report this to Shinkai and put a paralysis spell on you so that you cannot move a muscle or utter a single word; how would you like that?'

Tsunehiko: 'Please, cast it upon me quickly. It is so utterly agonizing to be constantly hounded by you demanding that I tell you the whereabouts of the jewel, so if you will just render me speechless, that will lift all responsibility from my shoulders. Please, do cast it upon me at once—a binding paralysis of absolute stillness...'

So saying, he slips the tip of his tongue between his upper and lower lips, juts out his chin before Takahime, and thrusts it forward to show her.

Takahime: 'Ugh, you are truly a beast of a fellow with whom nothing can be done, neither raised nor lowered.'

Tsunehiko: 'Hey Haruhiko, double-time, double-time. We're going to shake off Takahime-sama.'

Tents tucked up high, making the ground rumble with all his might, he bolted down the descending slope. From behind, Takahime frantically chased after them, desperate not to lose sight of the two figures.

Takahime trips over a high stone and collapses flat onto the earth, striking her forehead heavily, bleeding steadily, and bumping her knees as well; she strokes her head, rubs her legs, and writhes about in distress. Believing that Takahime is bound to be pursuing them, the two run on with all their might, heading south and ever south.

Four or five men happening to pass this way feel pity upon seeing Takahime's injuries, dissolving some unadulterated soil from the roadside in water and smearing it onto her forehead and legs.

Takahime: 'Whoever you may be, thank you so much for saving me. This is entirely due to the divine virtue of Hinode no Kamisama. You gentlemen have also received most wondrous divine virtue. The person called Takahime is indeed a most wondrous mitama, and anyone who offers even a single cup of water or a single drop of tea to this mitama shall, through the great joy of the Okami, bring forth auspicious signs of generations of wealth and prosperity, long-lasting descendants, abundant five grains, healing of illnesses, and throngs of visitors. You gentlemen have performed a most wondrous sacred service. Now, from here on, offer your gratitude to the Kami of Aizankyo. I shall join you in offering thanks as well.'

A: 'What an odd old hag she is. Having people take care of her, and in return telling them to show gratitude and offering to thank them for them—doesn't make any sense at all. This is most likely some madwoman. It would be terrible if we carelessly got involved with her. Let's just leave her be and get going.'

Takahime: 'Now, now, young men, I am mad indeed! I am the living kami of the lineage of the Great Fool, the Great Monster, and the Hensei Danshi of the Three Thousand Worlds.'

Hei: 'Since there are so many pilgrims, I suppose there must be all sorts of jewels gathered there as well.'

Otsu: 'Well, you see, I have been there once myself, and there were such magnificent jewels piled up like mountains before the Kami. They were calling them Kongaufue Niyoihotsushiyu, golden jewels, and the Manihotsushiyu of the Riugū; purple, blue, white, red, and yellow—magnificent jewels were offered up, so dazzling to the eyes.'

Takahime: 'Hey, you there, where are those jewels offered? Could you tell me for a moment?'

Otsu: 'The arika of those jewels, you ask? Well, unless you do something for me, I cannot tell you where such and such is kept in such and such a place.'

Takahime: 'In that case, I shall give you money, so please tell me.'

Otsu: 'Actually, I am also suffering from binbafu. If it is something that will lead to kanemau, perhaps I will tell you. Although there are gonin here, I am the only one who knows the bashiyo of the jewels, so I can profit haudai. How much money will you offer for a hitokuchi?'

Takahime: 'I will give you one ichiriyō for a hitokuchi. Please tell me in as much detail as you can in about two futakuchi.'

Otsu: 'I will not tell you in just one or two futakuchi; let us exchange one ichiriyō for each hitokuchi. And it must be sakisen, mind you.'

Takahime: 'Here, one ichiriyō.'

She thrusts it out.

Otsu: 'A...'

Takahime: 'Will you not say the rest?'

Otsu: 'Just one more ichiriyō; I only said one hitokuchi, did I not? Give me one more ichiriyō. I shall tell you the next part then.'

Takahime: 'Ah, there is no shikata, here is one ichiriyō.'

She thrusts it out again.

Otsu: 'Ri...'

While saying this, he thrusts out his hand again, demanding another ichiriyō.

Takahime: 'What an expensive annaireu this is. Could you not say a little more at once?'

Otsu: 'It is the original agreement; if I say "A...", that costs one hitokuchi. If I say "Ri...", that is another hitokuchi, is it not?'

Takahime: 'Oh, what a greedy man. Here is one ichiriyō, this time tell me a bit longer.'

Otsu stuffs another ichiriyō into his pocket.

Otsu: 'This time I will speak at length. ...Naaa...'

In this manner, she was taught that 'a great many precious jewels are offered before the Mirror Pond at the headwaters of the Arina Waterfall,' and Takahime, fired with enthusiasm, points toward the Arina Waterfall in the land of Teru and runs off with all her might.

Tsunehiko and Haruhiko, who were resting in the shade of a tree by the roadside, watched the figure of Takahime as she went with a changed expression.

Tsunehiko: 'Hey, hey, Takahime-sama, please wait a moment.'

They called out. Takahime turned back for a moment, pressed her upper and lower teeth together, bared them with a grimace, jerked her chin two or three times, and said:

Takahime: 'No, no, thank you very much. I have been informed of the arika of the jewels by Hinode-Kami-sama. You must by no means follow me.'

She runs off with all her might. Tsunehiko said:

Tsunehiko: 'I wonder if the jewels are really hidden in this country. If we follow after Takahime-sama, we must pick up at least one white jewel or yellow jewel, otherwise there is no point in having come all this way. ...Hey Haruhiko, hurry.'

Tucking up his skirts and taking long strides, he runs off at full speed in the wake of Takahime, hair disheveled and kicking up clouds of dust.
