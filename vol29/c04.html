Chapter 4: Field 辺の訓戒 [826]

野辺の訓戒〔826〕

§ 1

Karinsu, who had approached the foot of the hakuyōjiyu tree, no sooner laid his hands upon the trunk than he cried out 'Ah!' and collapsed on the spot. Terisutan had severely bruised his waist, making it impossible for him to walk even a little, and lay stretched out in his original place. Tatsukuniwake, alarmed, drew near the base of the tree, and once more uttering a single cry of 'Ah!', fell down facing south with his head pillowed alongside Karinsu. Behind them remained Takayorihime alone; being a woman of naturally stout heart, she did not panic in the least, but with absolute composure chanted the Amatsunorito, sang the Ama no kazūta, and prayed for the recovery of the two men.

Both Tatsukuniwake and Karinsu continued to groan in response to one another, emitting unpleasant sounds like the roar of a tiger, 'Un-un'. Hearing these sounds, Takayorihime thought... Ah, there is no danger to their lives; well, they will be all right. Once dawn breaks, some plan or other will surely present herself... and so forth, while she persistently chanted the norito, having gathered the golden jewels through a strategy, apologizing to Ōkami for her sin of having cunningly deceived others to come this far, and waited for the break of night.

Dyeing the eastern sky crimson, at last the Amatsuhinokami revealed His round form above the horizon. Terisutan's back pain finally healed, making him somewhat more spirited, and together with Takayorihime, he plucked wild strawberries from all around, had the two men take them into their mouths, and devotedly administered chinkon. The two men gradually regained their senses, sat up, and

Tatsukuniwake: 'Ah, what a terribly frightening thing it was. We were finally dragged all the way to Enma's court, subjected to the tortures of large lizards and poisonous snakes, burdened on our backs with countless golden jewels until our bones were near to breaking; and by that weight and agony, our bodies gradually sank down into the earth, suffering torments beyond description. Ah, there is nothing so frightening as attachment. ...Pray, mother-sama, should we not abandon the matter of the jewels?'

Kā: 'Tatsukuniwake-san, was it the same for you too? I was subjected to a similar sort of experience. And since I heard a sound moaning in pain with "un-un" off to the side, when I quietly peeked to see, oh how terrifying, how terrifying, Takahime-sama and Kurohime-sama were surrounded and pressed upon by Nyoi hōju and purple jewels, reduced to bodies as thin as paper, their eyes drifted away to one side like flounders, making quite ghastly faces, vomiting black blood from their mouths, and it was a sight not fit to be seen at all. It appears that our spirits have fallen into hell while yet alive. ...Ah, Lord God, please forgive me. I will surely reform my heart from this very day.'

so saying, she pressed her palms together and let flow tears like a waterfall.

Ter: 'Hey Kā, your eyes were dazzled and you were just seeing such a dream. Since before nightfall, because my waist hurt, I remained lying down and watched you fall while staying perfectly still, yet there was no sign of your going to hell; you were merely groaning "un-un" in dialogue with Tatsukuniwake-san right here under this tree. Do not say such weak-minded things. That is surely the delusion of your heart. Demons and snakes, hell and paradise, all turn however they will depending solely on the rudder of one's own heart's ship. Without uttering such superstitious things, pull yourself together a little.'

Tatsukuniwake: 'Nay, even so, it cannot be thought a dream. Nor is it by any means a delusion of our hearts. If we were merely seeing what we ordinarily think about, it might be judged as an illusion or a dream, but we do not think we have done anything so wicked. With the thought that we were making the utmost effort for the sake of the world and for the sake of the Gods, we even regarded what we did rather as a source of pride, so it is by no means a fantasy or delusion. At any rate, there must have been unreasonable elements in the course we have pursued thus far. Since the Gods demonstrate through the tip of the writing brush that if one makes a single mistake, chastisement immediately follows to make one take heed... I cannot afford to carelessly doubt it.'

Kā: 'What Tatsukuniwake-sama says is true. I am beginning to grow terrified of the future, wow!'

Takayori: 'Are you fellows not full-fledged, imposing men wearing a loincloth of one jo and two shaku? No matter what kind of things may happen, to carry through one's initial resolve is the proper duty of a man. I am a person of an aged woman's body just like this. Yet I do not possess even a bit of such a weak heart. Even if I were cast down to the bottom of Hell and made to suffer whatever kind of retribution, if it is for the sake of the world and for the sake of the O Michi, resolutely I cannot bend my initial resolve. With things like dreams being so fearful, how on earth could we manage to live in this dream-like floating world? The Ōkamisama is playing with our minds in various ways in order to test your hearts. ...Bah, how utterly squeamish, there is no help for it anymore. Even if I step astray from the top of this tree and plummet, smash my skull, and make a transfer of domains, unless I go and take that jewel, I will not let it be. Since I will let down that bag from above, you fellows stay down below, open your hands quietly, and receive it properly, won't you?'

So saying, she placed her hand upon the root of a white poplar tree about one armful around. Upon the trunk of the white poplar, large centipedes measuring three to four shaku in length were coiled all over. And thick snakes about one shaku in size, with tortoise-shell patterns, sticking out their red tongues and glaring with angry eyes, were hovering around the tree in numbers that could not be counted, not even by the tens. From the root to the treetop, centipedes and snakes tangled around the trunk and branches without an empty space, how loathsome it was. Even the usually unflinching Takayoriwakehime flinched at this, and while stepping back two or three paces,

Takayori: 'Hey, hey, Tatsukuniwake, Tei, Kā, indeed it is impossible to climb this easily. Fortunately, strawberries are growing in abundance just like this. Since there is no inconvenience no matter how long one stays for food, let us wage a war of attrition here until that jewel happens to fall naturally because of the wind or something, or until the centipedes and snakes give up and run away, whichever settles the matter. ...Come, come, everyone, since it will be troublesome if it rains, let us gather miscanthus grass from around here in the meantime, build a grass hermitage, and have a battle of endurance with those snakes and centipedes.'

The three followed Takayoriwakehime's words, suddenly cut and gathered trees and grass to build a hermitage, and set about preparations for a siege. At last, a makeshift grass hermitage for shelter from the rain was completed. While enduring the night dew, the three offered up prayers inside the hermitage, praying day and night without a single moment of interruption for the jewel to return to their hands favorably and for the evil insects of snakes and centipedes to disperse.

From the outside, a loathsome laughing voice was heard saying 'Kera kera kera.' The three of Tatsukuniwake, Tei, and Kā, no sooner did this voice enter their ears, felt as if hundreds of koku of freezing water had been poured over their heads from above, began to tremble violently, and were clattering their teeth. Takayoriwakehime, with an unconcerned face,

Takayori: 'Hey, hey, you fellows, why are you shrinking back with such pale faces? What on earth is scary? Most likely, the laughing voice just now must have been scary. Oho ho ho, what cowards you are. Well, well, I will step outside for a bit, and whoever they might be, I shall pacify and converse with them.'

So saying, she stood up sluggishly, pushed open the mat-door made of miscanthus, and tried to go out. Tatsukuniwake, startled, firmly embraced Takayoriwakehime's waist to hold her back,

Tatsukuniwake: 'Now, now, Okaa-sama, even if you do not do such a dangerous thing, there are three young men waiting here. Please wait, I beg of you.'

At this time once again, a loathsome voice laughing 'Kera kera kera' was heard in succession. The three men began to feel a creepy sensation down the nape of their necks, and once again their teeth began to chatter.

Takayori: 'Ho ho ho, those monsters, laughing kera kera kera, what kind of mischief are they playing? If they have business, instead of playing the opponent from afar like a dog barking in the distance, why do they not enter here? What good-for-nothing cowards they are.'
