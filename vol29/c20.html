Chapter 20: Way すがら [842]

道すがら〔842〕

§ 1

In the age of the great and righteous sacred, at the end of the summer of Jifuichinen, on the third day of Hachiguwatsu, the three—Oni Deguchi, Matsumura-san, and Isao Saga—were weaving the threads of the strict and the gentle. Together with the four in the party, including Sugiyama-san of the Izu spirit, they bathed in the setting sun from the train window, gazing left and right at Hongūyama and the Kokumogaha as they ascended.

Passing through Yamaga Station and Wachi Station, while explaining the Shigumi of Kami as fine sesame, they contemplated this Shigumi where the flowers of the trees bloom all at once. Passing through the flower-filled Sonobe and Komugiyama, and before they knew it, crossing the barrier of Yagi, they were seen off by the officers of the Zuishōkai of Kameoka, who celebrate the Yorozuyo, and entered the valley.

Passing through seven or eight tunnels that pierce the rocks, they arrived at Kyoto Station, passing Arashiyama, Hanazono, Nijō, and Tanbaguchi. Entrusting themselves to the express train, and crossing the barrier road of Ōsaka by the grace of Kami within the night, they traveled through the summer night of Ōtsu, gazing at the waters of Lake Biwa, passing Hikone Castle, which is deep in history where the spirit of Izu appeared, and passing Maibara Station.

The train, crowded with passengers getting on and off at Ōgaki and Gifu, stopped at the seaside stations of Nagoya, Toyohashi, and Hamamatsu. Welcomed by many believers in Shizuoka, where the sound of the wheels echoed, they arrived at Numazu Station.

Welcomed by people of the spirit of Izu who brought automobiles, they traveled in a line to the village of peaches, which bears the name of the Peach Blossom Spring. They visited the shrine where the Imperial Kami is enshrined at Tennōyama in the village of Kuchino, near the clear view of Eno-ura, and after reciting the Amatsu Norito, they pulled the weeds in front of the shrine.

While admiring the clear view of Mount Fuji, which was hazy in the distance, and seeing Wani-no-shima before them, they became passengers in the car again. In the blink of an eye, passing Hōjō and Nanjō, they arrived safely at the residence of Sugihara in Tagyō-mura, which felt like shooting an arrow, and at the sake brewery of Yoshida.

Leaving the car there, while taking a pleasant rest for half a day to wipe away their sweat, they became passengers in the car again. The number of those welcoming them grew at Ōhito, and they saw the black shadows at Yokose and Tateno, and the people fishing for sweetfish at Ōdaira.

Passing the village of the Deguchi of Kami, where Matsugase is, and where the hook catches, they crossed the village of Deguchi, and beyond the sky of Tsukigase, where there are no plum blossoms but the eleventh-night moon shines, they crossed Kadonogahara and arrived at the long-awaited Andō Inn hot spring resort in Yugashima.

The torrent of the Kano River was loud, the valley mouth was clear, and the cool breeze was full; it was enough to make one forget the summer. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-mae, may the spirits be blessed, and after purifying themselves in the hot spring, Isao, when the day dawned on the twelfth of the old calendar, lay down on the armchair sent by Sugihara-san.

As the strange story of the age of the gods, the twenty-eighth volume, finally begins to move the Kototama-car, Matsumura-san, holding a fountain pen and pulling his sleeves, writes down each thing here with a clear and pure heart. Matsumura-san and Sugiyama-san of the age of the gods, and Fukui-san of the rare divine wind who is the only one in the three-thousand worlds, reflect the shadows of the forest of brushes with my own spirit.

The sea of the inkstone is calm, and the great deity of the strict spirit who appeared in Aokigahara, the teachings of Kuninotokotachi-no-Ōkami, are sought morning and evening, and the Hinodewake and the flowers of the trees, the rare English fragrance, and the Kototama that are proclaimed gradually soothe Andō's chest, and Tadao also begins to speak white: the story of the island of Taiwan.
