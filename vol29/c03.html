Chapter 3: 白楊樹 [825]

白楊樹〔825〕

§ 1

Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, starting with Takayorihime, alongside Tatsukuniwake, Tē, and Kā—a party of four—advanced deep into the mountain recesses of Takotorimura, and proceeding upstream to the Arina Waterfall, where in the cave of the Mirror Pond—manifested by the divine will of Tsukiteruhiko in the ancient days of Kamiyo—they concealed themselves, wishing to search out the whereabouts of the Kogane jewel; and hiding Takayorihime within the cave to act as a kami, Tatsukuniwake built a hut on the edge of the pond, morning and evening chanting Amatsunorito while fulfilling the duties of a saniha, while the two men Tēristan and Kārinsu were sent away to the countries of west, east, north, and south.

Proclaiming the oracle and disguising themselves as kami, Tsukiteruhiko appeared upstream at the Arina Waterfall, spreading the word that if any person whatsoever possessed anything named a jewel, and coming forth offered it to the kami, then the wind, water, and fire of Daisansai, along with the famine, sickness, and warfare of Shōsansai, would be forgiven to that person, granting them mugen and muriyō longevity, fulfilling gokoku and fruits, and bestowing boundless virtue; thus they went about spreading this list of all good things, and the villagers, whose eyes were blinded by greed, brought out round stones closely resembling jewels, agate jewels, giant clam and jade, and coral beads, crossing distant mountains and fields to leave them as offerings beside the Mirror Pond and returning—how laughable it was.

Tatsukuniwake looked over them one by one with glowing eyes, but not a single one was any good; whenever he happened to see a yellowish jewel, its surface was adorned with gold plating, and junk jewels piled up like mountains in great numbers, and though days passed, within the brocade palace of San-go there was not even a shadow of the Kogane jewel; and the two men Tē and Kā, one after another, began grumbling a hundred complaints, saying, 'Shall we leave the Mirror Pond behind, change our lodging to the ends of where, and put on yet another play?' When they thus raised their voices, Takayorihime and Tatsukuniwake cajoled the two, saying, 'Wait a while; just one more breath of patience—endurance and forbearance will become the mother of happiness,' and while waiting upon them thus,

The chieftain of the village of Tēna brought forth a Kogane jewel, and riding upon a jewel palanquin while leading a multitude of people, advanced with banners fluttering in the wind, wishing to offer it before the divine presence at the Arina Waterfall and the Mirror Pond—how valiant he was. Though the truth or falsehood was unknown, smacking their lips over a matchless Kogane jewel, they gave the chieftain of the village of Tēna the name Kunitamayakewake, temporarily commanding him to perform the rites of purification on the borders of the Arina Waterfall, and substituting it with an agate jewel, they ascended the valley river hidden by the gloom of night, traversing the peaks of Mount Arina, and arrived at last through Utsu no Kuni and Kunugigahara as a party of four into the great wild field where kaya reeds grew thick.

Tatsukuniwake, Tēristan, and Kārinsu, taking turns placing the Kogane jewel into a brocade bag and hoisting it upon their shoulders, climbed up and down the steep slopes of Mount Arina, sweating profusely and arriving here at last; and somehow or other, this jewel increased in weight step by step, feeling as though someone were pulling from behind, and unless they bent their heads forward considerably, they felt as though they would be pulled back by the weight of the jewel and fall backward. Barely managing to reach this place with their lives, they decided to stretch out their legs in the shade of the poplar trees and take a brief rest.

Big lizards about five shaku in length ran hither and thither across the kaya field in the hundreds; sparrow-like bear-bees and horseflies gathered, smelling the sweat-stench, and golden blowflies resembling oil-cicadas swarmed to such a degree that they could not distinguish even an inch, buzzing and making a din—how annoying it was. The four bundled up the ears of kaya to use as prayer wands, and while using their right hands to brush away horseflies, bees, and golden blowflies, since evening had fallen they had no choice, and holding the jewel, the four lay down upon the grass, exhausted to the point of sleeping soundly even if they were carried or thrown.

In the howling night storm that blew up just then, the kaya grass rustled noisily, and the poplar trees caught the wind and, like bows, stroked the earth, while the horseflies, bees, and flies were blown away somewhere and not a single one remained. The moment the poplar trees, blown by the wind like bows, stroked the ground, they assailed the bodies of the four.

§ 2

Telisutan, still half asleep, tied the jewel bag around his neck while half-dreaming and hugged the branch of the hakuyōjiyu with all his might. The fierce baufū stopped in an instant, and the hakuyōjiyu reaching up to the ten stood straight upright as before. Looking closely, Telisutan had somehow climbed up into the treetop of the hakuyōjiyu. The moment he gasped in astonishment, he missed his footing, making a roaring sound, and fell headlong close beside the three who were sleeping, hitting the ground with a groan. Concealing the first kurotama, he rolled his eyes showing only the shirotama, lay sprawling in the shape of the kanji character for big, and shook his teashi violently. The brocade bag wrapping the jewels hung and swung in the sky of the hakuyōjiyu.

The three were startled, hurried over to the side of Telisutan, and crying out, 'Water, water,' looked around, but there were no mizutama anywhere. The moon parted the tanun and at last cast its light upon the gekai. Looking around, the strawberries were ripening all over the place, dyeing the ground red. Tatsukuniwake quickly plucked two or three, clenched his teeth, forcibly pried open the mouth of Telisutan who had collapsed, crushed the strawberries, and forced the juice into kōchiyū. Telisutan finally recovered his breath, grimaced, and frantically rubbed around his waist.

Kā: 'Oi Tē, what on earth has happened to you? Do not pull such a stunt and make us feel helpless in a place like this. Hey, and where on earth did you put the jewel bag that was hanging around your neck?'

Tē: 'Where I put it, I do not remember at all. I had a dream that I was carried off by a tengu and lifted up to a high place, but it was just the same place as before. The tengu in the dream might have taken it and gone back, for all I know. No matter what is said, it is a magnificent golden jewel, so it seems even the tengu would want it. Hugging a little jewel brings guilt—that is truly this case. Ah, my waist hurts, is this the commotion of a jewel place? If you do not do something about it, my breath is about to choke. Ouch, ouch, ouch, ouch.'

He was grimacing as he said this. Takayohime was startled and changed the color of her face,

Takayohime: 'Hey, Tē-sama, what have you done to the golden jewel that we obtained with such hardship and suffering until now? Come on, return it quickly. If you should lose that jewel, I will not forgive you.'

Tē: 'Even if you say such a thing, you cannot draw water from an empty well, can you? It is probably somewhere at the Arina Waterfall. Because we spoke sweet words and did this and that with the chieftain's family treasure, divine punishment is immediate, and who knows if this and that came and did this and that. You yourself probably do not have the qualification to grumble in too loud a voice. Even if it was stolen by a thief, is it not the same as from the beginning? It is a settled matter if you think the thief's skimming was taken off. Ah, with this, reform yourself and abandon the political and tactical path as of today. If only the jewel of the heart shines, even if three or four golden jewels are lost, it is not even worth counting. It appears that the grudge jewel of the chieftain fellow is clinging and haunting, and even when crossing the Arina Mountains, it felt quite like being pulled from behind, so heavy and painful that I could not bear it. If we were to be made to walk around this wide Takasagojima carrying such a heavy thing, we would simply run out of breath, Wā. Even if we lost the golden jewel, it is nothing to be so pessimistic about. In other words, if you think the Great Gods have changed a great calamity into a small one and taken away our sins, there is nothing as magnificent as this. Come, come, everyone, please offer words of praise and prayer of gratitude to the Great Gods.'

Takayohime: 'Hey Tē-sama, what arbitrary things are you uttering? How about you bear even a little sense of responsibility? To have lost so carelessly the divine treasures of the three thousand worlds that we crossed mountains and seas with great effort, suffering, and hardship, and finally managed to get—well, how could you say such arbitrary things. No matter what, until that jewel is discovered again, Tē-sama, you must not move from here, even for ten years or a hundred years.'

Te: 'Ah, what a bother. No matter how much the moon shines, I wonder how Tsukiteruhikonokami-sama is doing. He should be able to clearly inform me where that jewel is hidden or who has stolen it. Ah, I shall be scolded by Takayorihime-sama, and my hip bones are twisted, aching and bitter. To be subjected to such a plight where I cannot even stand on my own feet in this Tokagebara—what is the meaning of this? The gods do not seem to hear me either, an-an-an.'

And he began to cry softly.

Takayori: 'Now Te, no matter how much you cry, the jewel will not return. Pull yourself together a little, place your hand on your chest, and think. You must not have truly awakened yet.'

Te: 'Well, do not be so impatient; please grant me a little time. Unless I contemplate deeply where the whereabouts of the jewel might be, even if you demand it of me as suddenly as if I were suffering from a hemorrhage, my mouth that has opened in surprise will not be able to close, you know.'

Takayori: 'It is not you, but rather from my side—the situation is so absurd and ridiculous that my mouth, which has opened in surprise, truly cannot close, you know.'

Karinsu looked up at the sky,

Karin: 'Hey, I cannot tell clearly because of the moonlight, but I see something shining and dangling from the tip of that poplar tree. That looks exactly like the brocade bag containing the jewel.'

Tatsukuniwake looked up at the sky of the white poplar tree,

Tatsukuni: 'Hey, indeed, that is the brocade bag. Hey Te, it was fine that you took the trouble to tie the bag to the tip of such a high tree to prevent it from being stolen, but you must have missed your footing, fallen headlong, struck your hip, and been left dazed, haven't you? Ahahaha. Now, it is a bit of a chore, but shall we have Te-sama climb up and bring it down? Since you are the one who went all the way up there to tie it, it seems you are quite skilled at tree climbing. Now, hurry and bring it down.'

Te looked up at the sky,

Te: 'Hey, how truly mysterious. Before I knew it, who could have carried it up to such a place? I am a poor tree climber by nature, so there is no way I could ever climb to such a place. It is likely that a tengu rascal played a prank. It is impossible for anyone but a tengu to climb to such a place... hey Tatsukuniwake-sama, please perform chinkon to summon the tengu and have them bring that bag here.'

Tatsukuni: 'Mother, what do you think? Is this not something that does not add up? As you know, Te is a heavy-bodied man, and it is unlikely he could climb to such a place. This is surely a prank by a tengu, without a doubt.'

Takayori: 'There are many wild tengu in this area, so we cannot afford to be careless. Unless we somehow pray to the gods to bring it down for us, we will never be able to leave this place no matter how much time passes. ...Hey, Karin, you seem a bit light-bodied. Since it is a treasure for the sake of the gods and the world, there should be no great impropriety if you climb up to retrieve it. I will now pray with all my might to the Ōkami, so I am sorry to trouble you, but would you mind climbing up for a moment?'

Karin: 'That is true, well, let me try to see if I can climb it for a moment.'

And so, he approached the base of the white poplar tree, which was as thick as a man could embrace, placed his hand on the trunk for a moment, let out a scream of 'Kya!', and Karinsu collapsed on the spot, falling into a state of unconsciousness.

鷹依『此辺には野天狗が沢山に居るから、油断をする事は出来ませぬ。これは何とかして、神様に御願申し下ろして頂かねば吾々は何時になつても此処を離れる事は出来ませぬ。……コレ、カー、お前はチツとばかり身が軽さうだ。神様の為、世界の為の御宝だから、取りにあがつても滅多に無調法はありますまい。私がこれから大神様に一生懸命願をこめるから、お前御苦労だが、一寸登つて来て呉れまいかなア』

カー『さうですな、マア一寸試に登れるか登れぬか、調べて見ませう』

と、一抱もある白楊樹の根元に立寄り、木の幹に一寸手をかけ『キヤツ』と悲鳴をあげて、其場にカーリンスは打倒れ人事不省に陥りにける。
