Chapter 5: 引懸戻し [827]

引懸戻し〔827〕

§ 1

The Great Leader of Ananai-kyō, following in the divine footsteps of the deities Kotoyoriwake and Kuniyoriwake, with her heart swelling in arrogance, Takahime—beginning with the wish-granting jewel, the purple divine treasures, golden spheres, and mani jewels—was obsessed day and night with the thought that Kotoyoriwake had taken them in his possession and crossed over to Takasagojima; she coaxed Tsunehiko and Haruhiko to become her attendants, crossed the myriad paths of the tides, was saved by the Takashimamaru, and at last arrived safely at the port where the morning sun shone; pushing aside the throngs of passengers, Takahime, in the vanguard, vanished like clouds and mist, her body slanted as she pressed eagerly forward along the mountain path.

Tsunehiko and Haruhiko, following behind the leader Takahime, while telling each other not to dawdle and lose sight of Takahime, threaded their way through and ducked beneath the mountain path thick with old trees, crossed the valley stream many times, and at length caught up at the mountain pass of Kuramayama.

Takahime lay down in the pine grove thick with weeds at the mountain pass of Kuramayama, and said: 'Now then, if we have come this far, we are safe. Surely Tsunehiko and Haruhiko cannot be able to come chasing after us. No matter how much they search, in this broad Takasagojima, there is little fear of our crossing paths by chance. Ah, now I am at ease with this. Since the seas required the steering of a ship, I had absolute need of the two fellows, but if two such clumsy men tag along, my true colors will become too glaringly transparent to the people of the land, and I shall not be able to perform my vital divine mission to perfection. Come, from here on, manifesting the divine power of Hinode Kami, employing the miraculous and mysterious arts of the gods, even though it be Magatsuhi, I shall place myself under the care of the Golden-Haired Nine-Tailed Fox, manifest one miracle, prepare a multitude of new disciples, and if I simply prompt the people of the land who know the ways of the world to gladly seek the jewels far and near, then without exerting myself too much, the jewels will surely gather together. Moreover, once I find the whereabouts of Kotoyoriwake and instantly report it, rewards will be granted according to one's desires—ah, I shall stage a grand theatrical play! Concerning that, it is inconvenient to have trailing after me such absent-minded, dull-witted, half-witted-bell-thief Tsunehiko and lizard-faced, pathetic-looking Haruhiko; I managed them sweetly. Ah, the living shrine of Hinode Kami possesses truly extraordinary wisdom. Since such excessive wisdom wells up, even this Takahime is filled with wonder at her own hands. That is precisely why, until my desires are fulfilled, unlike Kurohime-sama, I shall not panic and take a husband. If any person intends to become my husband, unless he is a man of brilliance in the three thousand worlds, he will not suit my fancy in the least.'

Speaking to herself in triumph and getting carried away, her voice gradually grew louder. Tsunehiko and Haruhiko, having run up quietly from behind, concealed their bodies in the thicket of shrubs, heard every single word of Takahime's soliloquy, looked at each other with wide-open eyes, stuck out their tongues, and grinned. Takahime, not noticing it in the least, said:

'Now then, from here on is the decisive scene. However, having come to this land of Teru, without a single familiar face, how shall I manage to sweetly hook the people of the land? The human being who is hooked first is of the utmost importance. To make a splendid person whom the people of the land praise, saying "If it's that person...", into a disciple, versus making a botch of a human being like Tsuna or Haru into a disciple, exerts an extraordinary influence upon the faith of the people of the land. Please, O Kami, grant me one or two such splendid persons in the land of Teru as my disciples. I beg of you.'

So saying, she clapped her hands and began to recite the Amatsu Norito. The sun gradually sank into the peak of Kuramayama, and the surroundings grew dark by degrees.

Takahime said: 'Ah, the sun has set already. It cannot be helped. Shall I spend the night here and ponder my plans anew for tomorrow? Ah, that too shall be well.'

Thus questioning and answering to herself, she threw herself down. Yet somehow her heart remained unsettled and she could not sleep sweetly, so she indulged in various meditations.

Tsunehiko and Haruhiko suddenly groaned with a 'Utsu,' and making a rustling sound, they hurriedly hid themselves toward the other side of the forest.

Takahime: 'What is it, a four-legged beast? It is something one cannot be careless about; perhaps it has been listening to my Hitorigoto since a while ago. Even if it is a four-legged beast, its spirit is still a Bunrei of the Kamisama, so it is not a very pleasant feeling to have such things overheard. Ah, what I must guard is my mouth. Well, I shall seal my mouth from now on and perform the practice of silence.'

She then lay down again with a thud. After a short while, there were two Tabibito who passed by this place while talking loudly.

A: 'To where are you heading from here?'

B: 'Yes, I am a man called Kanan from the capital of Teru. I heard that a jewel had appeared at Kuramayama, so I went there, but it had already been dug up by someone else.'

A: 'If you are Kanan-sama from the capital of Teru, are you not the Kanan-sama who is the Sobatsuki to the Kokuōsama?'

B: 'Yes, that is so.'

A: 'This is, this is... I have been longing to meet you once, and here I have met you in such a good place. This must be entirely due to the Ohikiaha of the Ananahi no Kami. I am a man called Anna, who serves as a Kinji to the Kokuō of the capital of Hiru.'

B: 'Ah, are you that famous Anna-sama? What a strange coincidence this is.'

They were standing and talking. Hearing this conversation, Takahime thought:

Takahime: 'Oh, good fellows have come along. Between Anna and a famous man called Kanan, even if they are to be taken as companions, there is a great difference. It is a windfall that concerns one person and ten thousand. It is easy to obtain ten thousand soldiers, but difficult to obtain one general; how sweetly the Kamisama arranges things for me. Thank you.'

While thanking them in the depths of her heart, she let out a cool, young voice from the darkness.

Takahime: 'Hey, hey, you two, Anna and Kanan, wait a moment. I am the Ikumiya of the Hinodewake no Kami, who appeared on Mount Amagiyo, of the lineage of the Hensei-danshi, the Kamitsukasa of Takahime. By the divine decree of the Kuninotokotachi no Ōkami, I foresaw that you two would pass through here. I, this single divine pillar, crossed the sea and mountains to this place, and have been waiting at the entrance of Kuramayama. From now on, you two shall become my subordinates and I shall bestow upon you the office of the Sen-densi. Be grateful.'

A: 'Yes, I am truly not grateful at all.'

B: 'I am so grateful that my navel is boiling tea.'

Takahime: 'Hey, hey, you called Anna and Kanan, how do you understand what the Ikumiya of the Hinodewake no Kami says?'

A: 'I am already tired of hearing about the Ikumiya of the Hinodewake no Kami.'

Takahime: 'Ah, I suppose so. The name of the Ikumiya must be echoing throughout this Takashimajima to the extent that you are tired of hearing it.'

B: 'The divine plan of the Hinodewake no Kamisama is always full of failures; you even lost the jewel you had swallowed, and even the servants of Tsunehiko and Haruhiko met me on the way just a while ago and were saying that Anna is ridiculous, Kanan. Ufu-fu-fu.'

Takahime: 'Hey, hey, your true colors are gradually appearing. Are you not the two, Tsunehiko and Haruhiko? Do you think you can deceive this Hinodewake no Kami in the darkness? ...Hmph, do you think I will be deceived? I was pretending to be foolish on purpose, and you got carried away, saying this about Anna and that about Kanan. It is truly hateful. Do you not have enough shame to stop, just as a wicked man chases after a woman's skirt?'

Tsunehiko: 'In truth, we are Tsunehiko and Haruhiko. Since you were speaking such distant Hitorigoto from a while ago, I also made you worry a little in retaliation. I am truly sorry. Since you were so distant, I have one interesting secret, but if there is fish-heart, there is water-heart. I will not say it anymore. Hey, Haruhiko, look, we met Yoritsukewake-dono and Kuniyoriwake-dono on the ship Takashimamaru, and they told us the location of the jewel, so it is surely hidden on this island. Shall we go there, dig it up as soon as possible, and take it somewhere to make a name for ourselves? Takahime-sama speaks so distantly and treats us like nuisances, so it is actually better for us. I followed you, hiding on purpose, wanting to hear those words. Now that the two of us have heard them, no matter what excuses you make, it is no use. Farewell...'
