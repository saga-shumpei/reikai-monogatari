Chapter 12: 悔悟の幕 [834]

悔悟の幕〔834〕

§ 1

Tsunehiko and Haruhiko, together with the two, Tama and Ryū, who had followed them from behind, managed to climb the high mountain of Arina, and realizing they could by no means catch up with Takahime, they gave up the pursuit, rested their breath at the summit of the high mountain, and spent the night there.

At what seemed to be the hour of the dead of night, when they gazed casually into the eastern sky, suddenly black clouds arose, the stars of the full sky concealed their forms without a single one remaining, and gradually the wind blew fiercely, swirling over the ridges of the peaks; the four could not even sleep, and while gazing with wonder at those clouds, they looked on. Suddenly, from amidst the black clouds in the eastern sky, a great monster with white hair and clear outlines appeared, and its form was seen descending toward the ground. The four pointed, crying 'Look there, look there!' and watched it. After a while, just as a goddess of peerless beauty appeared once more in the air, a most terrifying golden-furred, nine-tailed, white-faced evil fox, illuminating the darkness, ran toward the sky of Tokoyonokuni in the north. Struck by a strange feeling, the four watched the eastern sky without taking their eyes off it. Strange to say, they saw a brocade bag in the air, illuminating its surroundings, fluttering down once more onto the oak grove.

At last, the eastern sky was colored with five-colored clouds, and the Sun of Heaven arose leisurely. The four immediately finished reciting the Amatsu Norito, plucked and ate the fruits of the trees in place of breakfast, and singing the propaganda song, they went down at their feet's will to search for Takahime's whereabouts.

At length, around the time the sun was setting, they reached the base of the white poplar tree in the oak grove where Takahime was sitting properly. Takahime's heart and soul had opened, the sun and moon of Shinnyo shone in the sky of her heart, and having attained the power of heavenly eye penetration, she knew that the four were coming in pursuit of her; thus she sat properly there, reciting the norito, and was waiting for the party.

As the divine spirit of the Mirror Pond, And Tsukiyohiko who appeared, By the admonition of the Deity's command, Takahime of the long tengu nose, Had her high nose crushed down, Suddenly her body shook and trembled, Falling into unconsciousness, Carried away into the bridge-pillar palace, Starting with Kunitamayawake, And the multitude of divine officials, Saying 'Water! Medicine! Norito!', With warm-hearted nursing she breathed again, Thinking she would give thanks, Once more her usual twisted logic— The teacher couple also astonished, Lost patience and to the annex Hastily concealed their forms. Afterwards, Takahime arrogantly Served close to the palace— The propaganda emissaries of Kuni, Tama, and Ryū, Turning to the other side, spouted endless talk: 'Though the iroha flowers scatter, With forty-eight characters all things In this world, Takahime Will solve,' she blustered. 'The vajra-indestructible wish-granting jewel, The treasure of Mani—Kuniyowake's Teaching officials and Tamajirawake, And the teaching lords of Kotoyowake Have built a wide palace, And without a doubt they are concealing The divine treasures of the three thousand worlds. Since Takahime has come here, It is already the hundredth year from which she cannot escape; Hand them over neat and clean,' She blew forth impossible demands. Tsunehiko, Haruhiko, and the others as well, Officials serving in the palace, Faced each other in perplexity at Takahime, Whose suspicions ran deep,

And urged her to depart from this place Even a moment sooner. Takahime flared her eyes in anger, And burst forth with abusive words, Until finally she rushed up like the Asura King Into the sacred shrine, And at the moment she placed both hands on the door, Sayingarayohiko, the guardian deity of the Mirror Pond, Appeared, And seizing Takahime the official by the nape of the neck, Hardly hurled her down straight to the bottom Of the stone steps. As befitting Takahime who was dizzy, With a half-dead and half-living appearance, 'Let her be left as a warning for the future,' Said Tsunehiko and Haruhiko, both of them, Putting aside for a while the kindness Of the divine officials such as Kuni, Tama, Irayawake, and Ryū, Arrived at the annex And partook of the sacred sake,

While indulging in idle talk, A strange cry arose from the palace. Wondering what matter it could be, the family, Running closer to look—ah, how is this? A big man took Takahime in his hands and tormented her, And straightway concealed his form. Takahime, astonished, stood up, Stepped out to the entrance, Quickly put straw sandals on her feet, And fled down the steep slope of Mount Arina Like an arrow shot from a bow. Takahime gradually reached the great plain, The thicket of pampas grass and white poplars, Reaching the base of a large tree In the oak grove where they stood in a row, Exhausted completely, she sat down on her waist, And fell asleep without knowing front or back. Takahime awoke in the middle of the night, And when she strained her ears, oh, how is this?

Countless lions, wolves, tigers, and bears, in numbers beyond hundreds or thousands, roared in unison, a horror that made one's hair stand on end. Black clouds blocked the four directions, and even the ground beneath one's feet could not be seen. Pushing aside the curtain of darkness, a white-haired giant appeared, tall enough to touch the clouds. With a gaping mouth torn to the ears, he advanced, blood oozing from his lips, and laughed loudly, 'Aha, aha, aha!'

'The time must be awaited. I have been starving until now, wanting to eat the flesh of living humans. Is this a blessing from Heaven? How grateful I am. This Takahime has aged, and though her flesh may be a little tough, for my empty stomach, it will surely not taste bad. Ah, how grateful, how grateful. I shall partake of it now.'

He approached Takahime-tsukasa, gripped her hair tightly, and the hideousness of his laughter was such that, as if a field mouse had been caught by a great cat, she trembled violently. Just as she quaked, the music of the Amatsumisora resounded with a clear, ringing tone, and a single beam of light shone upon the Daichi, causing the terrifying monster to vanish like smoke.

A beautiful goddess appeared, filled with smiles, and as she taught Takahime the true principles of heaven and earth in all their variety, the stubborn Takahime-tsukasa repented from the bottom of her heart. The attachment and delusion she held toward the jewel were cast away here into the clear flow of the Hayakawa.

Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase. May the spirits be blessed.

Tsunehiko, Haruhiko, and the other two, seeing Takahime—who had changed from her former arrogant face to an extremely gentle expression, sitting upright on the grass with refined and modest manners, and chanting prayers in a low voice—were surprised, contrary to their expectations.

Tsunehiko: 'Excuse me, Takahime-sama, how have you been? Fearing it would be terrible to lose sight of you, we followed and ran after you with all our might, but no matter what we said, the high mountains of Arina... in the end, we could not keep up with your swift feet. We finally gave up on the mountain ridge, spent the night, and have at last arrived here. Thank you for waiting for us. Ah, it feels as if a heavy burden has been lifted from my shoulders.'

Takahime: 'Tsunehiko, Haruhiko, and the officials of Kagami-no-ike, you have come all this way over such steep paths. Thanks to you, I have awakened from a long dream. The Takahime of this moment is not the arrogant Takahime of yesterday, with her strong ego and deep suspicion. I have received the teachings of the Kami and have repented, so please be at ease. And this golden jewel, which is in this brocade bag here, is a sacred treasure that the Kyōshu of Kunitama-yoriwake brought all the way from the village of Tēna. Please, Tamakō and Ryūkō, though it is a trouble for you, I ask you to accompany this treasure back to the Kakehashi-goden of Kagami-no-ike and have Kunitama-yoriwake-sama enshrine it in the sacred hall. This treasure was hanging on the tip of a white poplar branch until last night, but thanks to the divine power of the Hinodewake-hime-kami of Tenkyōzan, it has safely descended before me; it is a precious treasure of the Shinkai. Please, take a look. It is shining with a light so brilliant that it dazzles the eyes.'

Saying this, she smiled, untied the string of the brocade bag, and revealed the jewel before the four. The four were stunned by the rare divine treasure, and simply stood in a daze, biting their tongues and staring with wide eyes, continuing to marvel in silence for a while. After a moment, Tsunehiko pursed his lips and said, 'Takahime-sama, until now, whether sleeping or waking, you have been like a madman for jewels, but now that you have obtained such a magnificent jewel, I would have thought you would be satisfied. Your noble heart in offering to hand it over to Tamakō and Ryūkō is something I admire, but is your change of heart not a bit too sudden?'

Takahime: 'This is not early at all. My reform has been delayed by exactly twelve years. Because of that, I have caused various troubles to those in the sacred land, hindered the divine work, and committed inexcusable rudeness even toward the great gods. When I think that the gods, having long patience with a troublemaker of the spirit realm with clouded soul like me, thought to reform me and make use of me, and have endured this far, I am overwhelmed with awe, and I know neither how to offer an apology nor how to express my gratitude. From now on, my plan is to cross this Kunakigahara to the east, endure various hardships for the sake of the gods, have my deep sins of the past washed away, go up the Amazon River, meet with Takayorihime-sama, Tatsukuniwake-sama, and if possible, even Kotoyoriwake-sama, and after offering apologies for my deep sins up to now, return to Onokorojima and serve a part of the divine work... Look here, Tsunehiko, Haruhiko, you too, follow me and faithfully fulfill your divine service from here on.'

Tsunehiko, shedding tears,

Tsunehiko: 'Yes, thank you, that you have come to that state. Confessing now, to tell you the truth, the two of us came in your company at the secret request of Mokusuke-sama, having received a mission to make you thoroughly reform. Ah, hearing that, tears of joy somehow flow from me too... Right, Haruhiko, isn't it wonderful?'

Haruhiko: 'Yeah, it is wonderful.'

Having said that much, hiding their tears, they hung their heads.

From this, the two beings, Tama and Ryū, received the golden jewel housed in a brocade bag from the hand of Takahime, bade farewell to the three, crossed Mount Arina, returned to the Palace of the Hanging Bridge, placed it before the Kyōshu couple and the officials and believers, performed purification, with the Kyōshu himself acting as the ritual chief, and it was decided to enshrine it in the sanctuary. From this, the divine virtue of the Palace of the Hanging Bridge shone ever more brightly in the four directions, and eventually swept across the western half of Takasagojima.

Also Takahime, together with Tsunehiko and Haruhiko, crossed the wilderness swarming with giant lizards, centipedes, snakes, bees, and horseflies, advanced aiming for the port of Aru, and from there, traveling along the coast, boarded a convenient ship, landed at the port of Zemu, and again from the port of Chin emerged at the mouth of the Amazon River, made the boat go upstream, and proceeded into the forest of Tama. It is the intention to describe the account of the journey during this time in a separate section.
