Chapter 7: Ox 童丸 [829]

牛童丸〔829〕

§ 1

Takahime pushed through the long journey with desperate resolve; her throat parched, her body exhausted, she had no choice but to lay her body down in the shade of a roadside tree, quench her thirst in the Hosotanigaha, pluck and eat wild Tsuruichigo, and resolved to spend the night right here, whispering the Amatsunorito in a soft voice as she offered it up.

Tsunehiko and Haruhiko lagged behind by about ten steps, running desperately with all their might, zigzagging southwards down the narrow Telu highway. A village boy was leading his cow into the river at dusk, straddling its back, and returning home while playing a horizontal flute. In his desperate haste, Tsunehiko failed to notice the cow right before him, and delivered a direct headbutt right into the cow's rear. The cow jumped up in surprise, and the boy riding on its back was instantly hurled down to the ground, stood up groggily, and tightly gripping the cow's tether, cried out:

Dōji: 'Hey, I don't know whose fellow you are, but aren't you going to be careful? Are your eyeballs made of knot-holes in a board?'

Thus, contrasting with his small stature, he dared to shout defiantly at a grown man.

Tsunehiko: 'This is truly, truly an affair of the gathering dusk, and I was in such a frantic hurry that I ran headlong into the cow's backside. Please, have mercy and forgive me.'

Dōji: 'Hey, do you think apologizing is the end of the matter? Making a person go through an experience like treating him like a mere cow, and trying to escape this place with just a single word of excuse won't get you past the cow, you know. Hey, flatten yourself out right here for a moment!'

Tsunehiko: 'Yes, if that's the case, I will flatten myself. Please be content with this and pardon me.'

Dōji: 'Just you won't do. That other fellow with a face like a lizard, he must sit down too!'

Haruhiko: 'My, oh my, what a tiny little posture you have, yet you're a fellow who prattles impertinences to an adult. I didn't crash into you on purpose. Why should even I have to apologize?'

Dōji: 'You're of the same kind, too. If you keep grumbling and dilly-dallying, shall I set the cow on fire and trample you to death? Even though my body is small, the cow belonging to my retainer is quite large, you see.'

Tsunehiko: 'Now now, Dōji-sama, please judge things with a bit more moderation, won't you.'

Dōji: 'Who do you think my true identity is? If you guess that correctly, I'll forgive you.'

Tsunehiko: 'Yes, surely you must be Ushidōmaru-sama, are you not? I have heard that on Takasago Island, a god called Ushidōmaru invariably appears, riding upon a cow and playing a horizontal flute.'

Dōji: 'Do you know of which god Ushidōmaru is the divine incarnation?'

Tsunehiko: 'Yes, I know. Is he—or is he not—the self-proclaimed Ushitora no Konjin-sama, a farmer of Ontoshi Village...?'

Dōji: 'I am a god of farmers. I am the incarnation of Ōtoshi no Kami.'

Haruhiko: 'Ha! So that's why Tsunehiko bumped into your cow, and you're Dōji coming from the back, is that Lord Ōtoshi no Kami, ha ha ha ha ha! But what a tiny little posture you have, yet you take a big human being for Lord Ōtoshi no Kami!'

Dōji: 'You're the fellow named Haruhiko, aren't you? Come over here for a moment. I have something I want to give you.'

Haruhiko: 'Yes, thank you. If it's a matter of putting things out, I hate sticking out my tongue or holding out my hands; but if it's a matter of receiving something, I will accept even a dog's funeral or a cow's bones.'

Thinking him to be mere a child, and half in mockery, Haruhiko crept close before Dōji. Holding his horizontal flute reverse-grip, Dōji aimed straight at Haruhiko's cheek from atop the cow's back, and

Ushidō: 'Lord Ōtoshi no Kami strikes your cheek with all his might using a horizontal flute, Haruhiko!'

he struck him so hard that his neck was twisted awry, and

Ushidō: 'Shall I give you one more?'

he laughed unconcernedly.

Haruhiko: 'No, no, that is quite enough, thank you. Even though you are so small, you certainly have mighty strength, my! With this much arm-strength, it's no wonder you catch grown men and mock them. That's why they say, no matter how small a person may be, one must never despise them, for you never know what magnificent personage might be in disguise... is what they taught us. ...Hey Tsunehiko, let's endure this as much as is enough now, and let's be on our way.'

§ 2

Tsunehiko: 'Well then... Mo, mo, mo, Ushidōmarusama, if that is so, we shall now take our leave of you.'

Ushidō: 'Wait, wait! I want to give you two one more large thing.'

Haruhiko: 'No, we are quite fine as we are. That is already more than enough for us. If we receive anything more on top of this, the very platform of our sedge hats will fly right off.'

Ushidō: 'Now, do not worry. I will give you this ox, so ride it all the way to the Arina Waterfall. Your legs look terribly tired... And as for Takahime, if you go about ten chō south from here, there is a small stream. Take that small stream to the left and go up it for about ten ken, and Takahime is resting right there, so ride this ox and go splashing up the river. Farewell...'

Just as one would think to say this, the child's figure could no longer be seen.

Tsunehiko: 'Hey Haruhiko, what do you think? Didn't I just happen to bump into him and receive such a splendid vehicle? Come, let's both straddle the back of this ox and set off now, shall we?'

Haruhiko: 'You may be fine with it, but I was hit across the head with a transverse flute, and it hurts so much I don't know what to do, wah!'

Tsunehiko: 'Nonsense, it is a whip of divine grace. Since you were struck by Ushidōmarusama, you should feel quite honored. If you had been beaten by Takahime like this, then indeed you would be so angry you couldn't stand it, but anyway, the Kami said, 'Haruhiko, are you parting already? It is so sad to let you go,' and lovingly struck you. Come, let's ride quickly. 'The world we once saw as an ox is now so dear to us'—saying that, right now might be the most splendid thing of all. Not to eat a set meal is unworthy of a real man. Come, get on quickly, get on quickly.'

Haruhiko: 'I wonder if it will be like the dreams of Yaji and Yota at Koshika Pass, riding on an ox again and having to listen to grumbling from the ox fellow.'

Tsunehiko: 'Do not worry.'

So saying, he leaped nimbly onto the back. Haruhiko, pulling the ox's lead rope, advanced south and still further south, finally taking the Hosoya River taught by the child to the left, tracing the river upstream, and advanced to within two or three ken of where Takahime was resting, and checking the ox with 'O-o-o,' he leaped nimbly down.

Haruhiko: 'Hello there, Ousam, you have had a most difficult time. Now please return home.'

Ox: 'Un, un, un, uoo!'

It bellowed out with a voice as if even the mountains were splitting. Takahime had been dozing along in her dreamworld, but startled by this voice, she opened her eyes and saw Tsunehiko and Haruhiko, the two of them, standing on either side of the giant ox.

Takahime: 'Aren't you the two, Tsune and Haru? What is it, dragging such a large thing here... You must have done something or other to the peasants' treasures on the road again. It seems your thief nature can never be corrected anywhere. That is why I said that if I, this Takahime, walked around taking fellows like you along, my divine virtue would be lowered. Ugh, how filthy, hurry up and go away! Tsu, tsu, tsu!'

So saying, she spat out saliva and made a motion of spraying it at the two.

Takahime-sama, if a change of heart can go that far, you are certainly thoroughgoing. Now I shall not say anything more to you, Ousam. I had wanted to let you know the whereabouts of the jewel after seeing right through your mind, but since a person who changes like a cat's eyes—crying 'give it, give it, give it'—is too perilous, I shall tell you no secrets from here on, so please be prepared for that.'

Takahime: 'Hey Tsune, come now, what are you saying? Facing the audacious living shrine of the Hinode no Kami, is there any such thing as telling or not telling? If I look on with an ignorant face to draw you out, you put on grand airs and make me feel obligated, lining up only prefaces and generalities while telling me graspablehothings like letting out a fart in the water for the vital core, I suppose. I heard about the whereabouts of the jewel clearly right from Hinodewake no Kami. I have no more use for you, and I shall never rely on you for the rest of my life. Go away to a place where I won't have to lay eyes on you!'

Tsunehiko: 'Takahime-sama, you should not cut such a bold and sharp retort. Even a rotten rope has its own use, as they say; just now, something will come up that requires you to rely even on me, so it would be better not to speak in such an arrogant manner.'

Takahime: 'Ugh, how annoying.'

Tsunehiko: 'If that is how it is, I shall take my leave ahead of you, riding upon this ox, to Arina, which is worth a silver coin for a single word. On the way, I was taught everything in detail by Ushidōmaru-sama, and he even let me know that you are here, and I have even been granted a splendid four-footed vehicle, so I will not feel even a tiny bit of travel fatigue. It would normally take about ten days to reach Arina, but if I ride on this, I can get there in about three days. ...Well then, ahead of you, Takahime-sama... Farewell.'

He tries to straddle the ox once again. Takahime, thinking this is a terrible crisis, hurriedly gets up, grabs Tsunehiko firmly by the waist,

Takahime: 'Wait, wait, Tsunehiko, I was in the wrong. Please do not be so angry. I just wanted to test your spirits, thinking I did not know what you would say.'

Tsunehiko: 'Is this your usual trick again? That trick will not work on me. ...Now Haruhiko, you get on too. ...Takahime-sama, I am taking the wish-granting jewel and the other sacred treasures and returning ahead of you. Ariyōsu.'

Takahime: 'Hey Tsunekō, Harukō, if I tell you to wait, how can you just not wait? You should not dislike Takahime so much.'

Making her round eyes intentionally narrow and forming a small pouting mouth, she tries to coax him. Although it is completely obscured by the moonlight, from her way of speaking and her posture, it was clearly acknowledged.

Takahime: 'How would it be if you had the ox go back now? It might otherwise not serve as training at all.'

Tsunehiko: 'Ah, that is right. Well then, Mr. Ox, please go back now.'

At Tsunehiko's single word, the ox vanished from that spot like foam. Seeing this, Takahime stroked her chest in temporary relief and slowly began to speak strongly again.

Takahime: 'No matter how fast your legs are, you probably cannot keep up with me. That is why I am constantly teaching you not to be arrogant.'

Tsunehiko: 'And once again Takahime-sama is taking advantage of my weakness to say such things. Ah, if it were like this, I should not have had the ox go back. ...Hello, Mr. Ox, please come back here once more. And just as Ushidōmaru told you, please take me all the way to the waterfall of Arina.'

He shouted out of nowhere. Call as he might, there was neither an echo nor any sign of a response.

Tsunehiko: 'Ah, thinking I was greatly helped by Mr. Ox, must I tomorrow trudge along the dusty road once more, applying grease to my parent-inherited means of transportation? ...The sentiment of the poem—The world once seen as an ox is now longed for... has now become a reality.'

Takahime: 'Otsuho-ho-ho, look there, the Heike who pride themselves do not last long... and so, loaches do not stay under the willow tree forever. Taking someone like you along is a hindrance to my hands and feet, but it cannot be helped. In that case, I will make a bold investment and permit you to accompany me. Now, rest leisurely here.'

Haruhiko: 'When you say such things, while we are sleeping soundly, won't Takahime-sama quietly slip away, go on ahead, and take all the jewels for herself?'

Tsunehiko: 'Um, surely, you wouldn't do something like that, would you? Anyway, what I have heard lies elsewhere, so we don't need to worry that much.'

Takahime: 'That is why I say you people are no good. Doubting one's heart is a grave sin in the divine realm. Clear your doubts and reform yourselves completely; if you cannot reform, I will not allow you to accompany me.'

Tsunehiko: 'Yes, I will reform.'

Takahime: 'Haruhiko, that must be so, too.'

Haruhiko: 'Most certainly so, indeed.'

As they were thus conversing, from the treetop of the grand cedar branch, though it was unknown what manner of creature it might be,

Takahime Takahime, Tsunehiko kottsuko, Haruhiko kottsukoko

Thus it cried out in a voice like that of an owl.

Takahime, feeling an eerie chill, glumly rose from her seat and fled back along the way she had originally come.

The two also, feeling the eerie chill, followed behind Takahime, emerged onto the Teru highway, and the three of them, desperately rubbing their sleepy eyes awake all of a sudden, trudged onward toward the south and ever southward.

Using the grass as bedding, tree stumps as pillows, and tearing off banana leaves to serve as substitutes for night-garments, after about seven days they finally came out upon the coastline of Takotorimura, where Saruyohiko had left his miracles.

At this time the sun had already sunk into the western mountains, and the crescent moon of the second day appeared to float close above the waves in the western quarter.

The three of them clasped their hands in reverence toward the moon, recited the Amatsunorito, and chanted the heavenly counting songs as they pressed onward trudgingly through the midnight without yielding, aiming for the waterfall of Arina.
