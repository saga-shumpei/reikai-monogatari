Chapter 14: カーリン丸 [836]

カーリン丸〔836〕

§ 1

The sannin spent the night in a forest of yashijiyu near the kosui.

That night the wind was relatively strong, and the sound of the waves on the kosui echoed from time to time like thunder with a rumbling roar.

The name of this kosui is called Lake Tama.

It was a daikosui measuring about fifty ri from tōzai and thirty-five ri from nanpoku.

And the shape of this kosui is like a heutan split vertically with one half turned facing afumu upwards.

The shinku taiyō, newly born forth from above the chiheisenjiyō, smilingly dances and frenziedly whirls while momokoku ascending shiyōten.

The ichidō washed their faces in the kosui, rinsed their mouths, purified their hands, recited words of hakushiyu and kanshiya, plucked a itsupai of tsuruichigo into the palms of their hands, and used them in place of asameshi.

さて高姫は石像を背に負ひ、エチエチし乍ら草野を分けて湖畔を東へ東へと二人の同行と共に進み行く。

Tsunehiko: 'Indeed, Tenkuwasuichimusubi and Aoakamurasakishiroki have their vertical lines running in good order. This must be what is called the vertical fish. Ah, look, over here too, another fish of the same sort with five-colored mottling is swimming horizontally. Which one is the male, I wonder, and which one is the female?'

Haruhiko: 'That is a settled matter. The one with the vertical lines is the male, and the one with the horizontal lines is the female. Since it is said that the warp and the weft come together as husband and wife to weave a brocade loom, they are called brocade fish. Look at these fins, aren't they quite magnificent fins?'

Tsunehiko: 'But doesn't this fish lack eyes? Isn't this fellow rather strange?'

Haruhiko: 'This blind fish with vertical lines is called one-name Takahimeuo, and the one with horizontal lines is called Haruhikōo. Since both are blind, they are equally matched. See how they neither run nor do anything like that, just like this. Yet when you take them in your hand, they still bounce and jump. Ah, a true brocade fish has come swimming over there. This fellow has vertical and horizontal intersecting lines, wonderfully and boundlessly clear patterns, and is shining brilliantly. It also has large eyes wide open. ... Well, Takahime-sama, even by looking at this, is it not the divine teaching that unless the warp and weft are aligned, neither the lineage of the hen-turned-rooster alone can be seen, nor the whereabouts of the woman from beginning to end?'

Takahime repeatedly shakes her head,

Takahime: 'Oof, how truly awe-inspiring the divine plan of the Lord God is! I must repent upon seeing this. If we keep weaving blind stripes with blind halves of warp and weft, like the Izumo Shinshu until now, the brocade loom will never be finished weaving. As for that, I was the most at fault. The warp threads should have just stayed still, but because I struggled more than the weft threads, it turned into a total mess. Ah, no matter what I look at, it is nothing but divine teachings; why on earth could I not understand such an easy-to-see reason until now? It was simply because I was blinded by the golden-haired nine-tailed fox.'

She sighs deeply and at length. From this time on, the party hurries night and day, and at last arrives at the coast of Aru. Fortunately, it was just the moment when a ship was about to set sail for the port of Zemu. Takahime hurriedly called out, 'Hey, hey!' The boatman untied the mooring rope, pulled back the ship which had already drifted a little away from the port, let the three board, filled the sails with a timely southern wind, and glided gently over the waves toward the port of Zemu.

To while away the tedium of the long voyage, casual chats began here and there among the passengers. Takahime and her party shrank into a small corner of the ship and kept quiet.

Passenger A: 'Was it last year? In a conversation among passengers when crossing to the port of Zemu on this ship, it was said that a tremendous jewel-taking deity appeared at Arina Waterfall in the land of Teru, and greedy fellows without stop made pilgrimages from both far and near to offer various kinds of jewels and have their various wishes granted. Then someone important from a country called Hiru or Yoru offered a jewel of gold. The jewel-taking deity seemed to take a liking to that golden jewel, hoisted the jewel away in the dead of night, escaped to somewhere, and at a place like Kunugigahara in the land of Uzu, the jewel that had been taken out with so much trouble was seized by a tengu. In a wretched plight, he traversed the vast plain of the land of Uzu (Argentina), took a ship from the port of Aru, and went all the way up to the upper reaches of the Amazon River. But among deities there are all sorts, and there really are greedy deities too. As for the commander of that jewel-taking deity, there is some noisy goddess with a name like a hawk, kite, or crow from the Jitentō Island, who got angry because the jewel-taking deity lost the treasure she had carefully guarded and drove him out, telling him not to come back until he got his hands on that jewel... To say such unreasonable things and torment him when, in this wide world, finding even a single jewel somehow seems impossible no matter how much one searches—I heard such a story, and there really are terribly bad deities too. Surely that fellow must be possessed by the Great Serpent of Yamato or the golden-haired nine-tailed fox, making him say and do such unreasonable things. Even if they are truly deities, one cannot blindly place faith in them. After all, it is a world where there are stingy deities called Takatobihime or Tamatorihime.'

He makes his eyes stark red, gnashes his teeth, clenches his fists, and pants with a ferocious aspect.

Kō: 'Ha ha ha ha ha, what they call grieving over another person's senki as your own zutsū is just like you. Leave off such a thing at Kagen. No matter how much you strain yourself, it's no use because the principal person himself is at Onokorojima across ten thousand ri of ocean.'

Hei: 'Suddenly my body has begun to tremble. Surely isn't that fellow called Takahime riding on this ship? Hey, would you mind asking the names of the female passengers one by one for me?'

Kō: 'Don't talk nonsense; even if I don't ask, if you ask the captain-dono, they are properly entered in the account book.'

Hei: 'That's so too, then shall I try asking?'

He attempts to stand up. Takahime holds back Hei's sleeve,

Takahime: 'Moshimoshi, I do not know where you are from, but for the sake of the party of Takayorihime and Tatsukuniwake, you think so earnestly from the bottom of your heart. Without doubt, those four persons must also be rejoicing from Meido. As I have heard from the very beginning, you say you saw the four fall into the sea, but was there anything left behind afterward? I am Takahime who came from Onokorojima, whom you think hateful. I am a person of deep sin; come, please do as you please with your you. If you do so, those four persons surely will be able to float as well. Upon the stone which I am now bearing upon my back, the figures of the aforesaid four are carved. As if an insect informed me that such a thing would happen, it appears I properly made and left a stone monument of my own accord. Ah, what is called karma is a fearful thing. The net of Heaven is vast and wide; though sparse, it lets nothing slip. Had I known it would come to this, I would not have uttered such cruel things.'

So saying, she sets the stone image from her back to the front, clasps her hands,

Takahime: 'Kore kore, four august persons, please bear with it. Though mine is a precious body that must participate in the August Divine Work of the Three Thousand Worlds, I shall now have my raw head pulled off by this person, change my country, go to the side of you-sama, and apologize anew. Ah, Kannagara Tamashii Sakihamau Koso. Takayorihime, Tatsukuniwake, Tērisutan to Kārinsu, Tonshō Bodai, ah, Kannagara Tamashii Sakihamau Koso.'

She is praying with all her might. As for Hei, by those words and behavior of Takahime repenting from her true heart, the momentum that had been so tightly strung up till now vanished somewhere, and now, on the contrary, before he knew it in his heart, he had unconsciously become an admirer of Takahime.
