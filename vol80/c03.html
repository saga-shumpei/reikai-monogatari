Chapter 3: 復活 [2007]

復活〔2007〕

§ 1

Watching the dead body of Fuyuo, who had finally lost his Inochi after falling into the Keiriyaku of the laughing hag, the hag laughed aloud, sticking out her long tongue like that of an ox.

Ahahahaha-tsuha, ihihihihi, ufuhuhuhu-tsufu, ehehehehe, ohohohoho-tsuho, how Omoshiro, how Kokochi, she said, while beating and tormenting the Mitama and Karatama of Fuyuo with a whip of thorns.

As the exhausted Fuyuo cried out in Himei, begging for help, a Baufūu suddenly arose.

Thunder roared and lightning flashed across the Tenchi, and from the darkness appeared an Araotoko who deceived the demons.

The Futari stood out here, grabbed the hag by her neck, and threw her down onto the Daichi with a thud.

Thrown down, the Warai-baba shook her chin three or four times and said, 'Ahahahaha-tsuha, how insolent. Are you not the Gaki of Kumakō and Torakō, who also lost their bodies after falling into my Keiriyaku? Are you wandering in the Shinrin of Shimizugaoka as Sudama? How truly, truly Kokochi it is. What is this Arisama? Your Kimono is torn, your obi is cut, is your head a bird's Sugomo? Are your Teashi like the Arakaha of a pine tree? What a dismal sight to behold. Do not Temuka to this Baba-sama and Kōkuwai later, you Gaki who know not the value of Inochi.'

As she cursed them recklessly, the spirits of Kumakō and Torakō flared up like a raging fire, and clenching their demon-like fists, they struck the fallen hag from both sides with all their might.

Thinking the hag would flinch, they were surprised as she laughed aloud again, sticking out her long tongue and wagging her chin with such spite.

Looking closely, the fists of the two were festering and bleeding, a miserable sight to behold, and the hag laughed again, saying, 'You blind fools of Kumakō and Torakō, my body is here. You are striking the sharp rock face recklessly and hurting your own fists, you reckless ones. From now on, I will not let you be if you do such foolish things.'

She cursed them with foul language, saying, 'Do you think you can stay in the Yūmeikai after opposing the Warai-baba-san, whose name is famous in Oshigaoaka?' Torakō and Kumakō stood in anger, and when they pulled the hag's hands from both sides with all their might, even the hag was overwhelmed.

Grimacing and saying, 'This is unbearable,' she turned into a ball of fire and fled, running through the distant sky back to Oshigaoka. Fuyuo finally stood up, his heart finally calmed, and looking around, he saw a wonder: Kumakō and Torakō, his vassals who served the mountain of Minakami, were kneeling before his face with smiles, saying, 'Young Lord, you are safe,' while shedding tears and bowing.

§ 2

Kumakō sings:

If it be so, shall we three, master and servants, unite our hearts and destroy the hag? She is a hag who cannot be dealt with by one or two simple tricks, for she knows any method beforehand. Yet, in this very moment, while she suffers from her two injured arms, I shall destroy her.

Fuyuo sings:

How amusing, how brave it is to destroy the enemy who steals the Inochi of the Kunitsukami. Should I leave the laughing hag, who boasts of being the head of the Suihonki, without taking her spirit's Inochi? I think to take the Inochi of the laughing hag and ward off a hundred calamities from the Kunitsukami.

Torakō: 'I agree with the Mikotoba of Wakagimi; I too shall add my strength to the Kita of the hag. If the three Mihashira of Masurao unite their strength, destroying the hag should be easy. Come then, let us depart from Shimizugaoka and advance straight to the mansion of the hag.'

With this, the three reached an Itsuketsu, and once again they proceeded along the Noji where the Suihonsau grew so thick as to be Tokorose, pointing toward Shinobugaoka, the Sumika of the hag.

With Kumakō in the Sentō, Fuyuo in the middle, and Torakō serving as the rear guard, they advanced along the Noji where the Yoshigusa and Suihonsau were overgrown to the point of being Tokorose, being swayed by the blowing wind, and as is the custom of Seirei, they advanced while singing a song of conquest in a staggering manner.

The song of Kumakō:

Ah, how brave, how brave! In the center of Ōnogahara, On the summit of Shinobugaoka, Which is wide and opens low, Lives the laughing hag of old. We go forth to Kita and drive away The Magatsumi who takes pleasure In taking the Inochi of the Kunitsukami. Today my heart is also brave. Though the blowing wind smells of raw flesh, Though many Ijichi dwell there, Though poisonous insects swarm, Why should we fear? We are rare Masurao in the world. When we were in the manifest world, We were warriors known to the world As peerless heroes of all time. Even though we are now Seirei, The power of our souls shall not wane. If the hag is also a spirit of the same Mitama, why should we fear? With the fists of our Yamototake-gokoro, We shall strike and beat her head, Ward off the calamities of both the manifest and hidden worlds, And become gods of the Yūmei, To pass down our names for long. Ah, how amusing, how amusing! The time to clear our daily grudges Has finally come around. Though Shinobugaoka is wide, Though the Suihonsau is thick, Though many enemies may come, We shall not fear; we shall advance Bathed in the blessings of the gods Who dwell in the mountains of the water's edge. Ah, how amusing, how brave! Now is the time to avenge our enemy, Now is the time to destroy the hag. Fortunately, as the darkness is deep, Even the hag, as she is, may be sleeping Without knowing of our approach. Her left and right arms are torn off By the strong power of the two, And we shall aim for the gap While she suffers in agony. Advance, advance, come, advance, Until the laughing hag is destroyed.

Fuyuo sings:

The hag who deceived and killed all the travelers crossing Ōnohara is hateful. I too was lured by the poisonous hand of the hag And had my jewel-like Inochi stolen. Though the Inochi of a Seirei remains, The Inochi of my manifest body Can no longer return to my hometown. If it be so, the three of us shall unite our hearts And destroy the laughing hag. Advance with the care of Murakimo, For the hag's underlings wait on the road. Unexpectedly, I was deceived by the demon hag of Shinobugaoka Who went around offering tea. Three noble maidens must also have fallen Into the poisonous hands of the demon hag and perished. I shall save the Inochi of the three maidens And illuminate the darkness of Shinobugaoka.

Torakō, while serving as the rear guard, sings:

The time has come To open the rock-door of heaven and earth. Now is the time To clear the grudges of a hundred thousand. The time has come To dispel all sufferings. Serving the King, the Lord, at the divine mansion of the mountain of the water's edge, Born as the child of the firm rock-root, To serve the Wakagimi, Is this not also the blessing of the gods of heaven and earth? Having spent years at Shimizugaoka Turning into a demon of grudge And watching for the Inochi of the hag, It was worth it, and now I am Being pushed by a strong power And advancing; how brave it is. Ah, I am A fallen warrior who traveled like the Wakagimi, Was deceived by the laughing hag, And had my Inochi taken. Together with Kumakō, I spent years Living at Shimizugaoka. The time has finally become full, It has become a good day To Kita the hag; how brave. Even though I am in the body of a Seirei, I know not why, but I feel a very strong Power added to me As I advance through Ōnogahara. Fortunately, there is no moon in the sky, And it is a dark night without even the light of stars; Today's departure is amusing. Thinking that I will clear my grudge Before the eyes of the laughing hag Whom I have resented for years, I am happy.

§ 3

Singing thus, around the middle of the night, the three Seirei arrived at the front of the house of the laughing old woman of Shinobugaoka.

The three stood quietly outside the broken door, and when they peered at the Yōsu within, they saw the old woman suffering and writhing as if her Tamashihi were about to break, due to the pain of being pulled by two people until her Riyōte were nearly torn off, and she was raising a voice of Umeki, groaning 'un-un'. When they looked closely at the three Otome, they wondered how this could be, thinking they were perhaps nursing her, but they were like the Nidaime of the old woman.

Three Otome: 'Ahahahaha-tsuha, ihihihihi, ufuhuhuhu-tsufu, ehehehehe, ohohohoho-tsuho, how Omoshiro! The laughing old woman-san, her Gashifu is too strong, and having taken Kazukagi of Jinmei, she created the village of Shinobu and remained as its ruler, but has her Tenun already run out? She was cleverly escaped by the Masurao called Fuyuo from the mountain of Minakami where she stayed Sakuya, and in her Munen, she stomped her Jidanda, chased after him, reached Shimizugaoka, made a Fukaku, and fled back, only to have her Saiu Teashi torn off, and now she is in this Arisama, unable to Miugo and suffering; what is the meaning of this? We three Otome were also deceived by the laughing old woman-san, and finally became ghosts of the Yūmei, and even to the houses of our beloved parents, there is no way to return, how miserable! Hoping for something aimless, thinking, "When will this grudge be cleared?" we waited, and the days and months were worth it; today, the death of the Omoshiro ghost-hag is already at hand. Ah, how Omoshiro, how reliable! There will surely be no precedent for the laughing old woman-san to be resurrected anymore. Even if the heaven falls and the earth splits, there shall be no precedent for the old woman-san to be resurrected again; in this moment, we shall press down on the old woman-san's sleeping neck, and though we are but frail Otome, we shall clear our daily grudges. Ah, how Omoshiro, how reliable! Ahahahaha-tsuha, ihihihihi, ufuhuhuhu-tsufu, ehehehehe, ohohohoho-tsuho, how Omoshiro!'

Singing and laughing thus, the three stood at the bedside. Although the laughing old woman reached the peak of fury, in this situation where she could no longer move even a bit, knowing that she was in the hands of the three Otome, whether they boiled and ate her or grilled and ate her, the cunning old woman pretended not to hear, enduring the pain and masking it with laughter.
