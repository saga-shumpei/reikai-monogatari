Chapter 6: Autumn Field の Journey [2010]

秋野の旅〔2010〕

§ 1

Although winter had passed, spring and summer had elapsed, and the beginning of autumn had arrived since Fuyuo was dispatched to inspect the topography of the land to the west of Takamitsuyama, no word had been received from him; thus, the atmosphere around Iwaganeyori was filled with a certain anxiety, whereupon he summoned his chief vassals Minaoto and Seoto, and together with the three brothers Haruo, Natsuo, and Akio, they assembled at the Shitsuseishiyo and held a close council.

Iwaganeyori spake in song:

Iwaganeyori: 'Fuyuo, who departed to investigate the topography of the land to the west of Takamitsuyama, hath not returned even now. Could it be that Fuyuo hath perished, poisoned by the Suihonsau? Night after night I have seen strange dreams, and my heart is troubled regarding the state of Fuyuo. If this be so, I think to send men once again to search for the Arika of Fuyuo.'

Minaoto replied:

Minaoto: 'The words declared by the Shitsusei have also, methinks, weighed upon my heart morning and evening. I have heard by rumor that a Suihonki at Shinobigaoka harmeth travelers on their journey. It is said that the laughing hag of the Suihonki standeth by the roadside, enticing and slaying travelers.'

Seoto sang:

Seoto: 'In Yoshihara, I hear that the harm of the Ijichi lurking at the roots of the Suihonsau is fearful. Considering the matter, Lord Fuyuo must have been harmed by the Ijichi or other Dokumushi. At all events, nothing can compare to dispatching men to Takamitsuyama to ascertain the Anpi of Fuyuo.'

Hereupon, by the consultation of the three persons, it was decided that Iwaganeyori should appoint his third son Akio as the leader, accompanied by four attendants, and set forth from the mansion at Mizukamiyama to search the great wilderness reaching to Takamitsuyama.

Akio, accompanied by his four attendants Matsu, Take, Ume, and Sakura, departed from Mizukamiyama, firmly determined to avoid the path taken by his younger brother Fuyuo, to head toward the south, and to advance in the direction of Takamitsuyama; he offered prayers for his departure at the shrine, and turning toward his father Iwaganeyori, quietly declared in song:

Akio: 'Receiving the august words of my father, I shall advance toward Takamitsuyama. Whatever suffering there may be, it is for the sake of the country, and I shall not fear even if I should die. I shall search for the Arika of my younger brother and utterly set the hearts of my parents at ease. I shall cut down and cast away the Yoshi grass and Suihonsau that obstruct the realm of Yoshihara. An evil deity lurketh in the fields of Suihonsau, said to bring calamity to those who pass by. Nevertheless, I possess the divine protection, and I shall advance without fearing any Magatsu. Father and Mother, set your hearts at ease, for I am not on a solitary journey.'

Iwaganeyori, shaking off his tears, sang with an appearance of vigor:

Iwaganeyori: 'Until the whereabouts of thy younger brother Fuyuo are known, return thou not, but survey the land. When the Shōsoku of thy brother is known, inform me swiftly by commanding Sakura.'

Akio sang again:

Akio: 'Reverently obeying the stern words of my father, I shall exert my strength to the utmost.'

Minaoto sang:

Minaoto: 'The heart of Minaoto, who speedeth the departure of the brave Lord Akio, is full of sadness. Even the sound of the water and the roar of the wind make me anxious, praying that your journey may be peaceful. As you set forth crossing wild paths and traversing rivers, I shall speed your manly figure.'

Seoto sang:

Seoto: 'I also shall obey the father's august words and protect the divine land, so depart in peace. Without leaving your heart behind in the mansion, depart swiftly for the sake of the divine land, O Lord. Though this mansion will be lonely when you go, it cannot be helped when we think of the divine land. Into the great wilderness where the Magatsugami rage fiercely, advance, Lord, by the power of the deity.'

Akio sang:

Akio: 'Gracious is your sincerity; I shall never forget it, but advance making it my strength. Now then, I shall advance, uniting my strength with my four companions. As autumn passes, many herbs bloom and scent the fields, welcoming and speeding me on my journey. Wrapped in the fragrance of flowers, my journey through the autumn fields shall be pure. As soon as I discover the Arika of my younger brother, I shall send Sakura back to inform you. Grant me strength as I set forth to open up a new realm. When I think of leaving the sacred grounds of Mizukamiyama where I have lived so long, tears well in my eyes. My tears are not tears of grief, but tears of joy for setting forth on my journey. Accompanied by Matsu, Take, Ume, and Sakura, I go through the fields where Ominaeshi, Kikyo, and Karukaya emit their fragrance.'

原文（近代文語）

高光山以西の国形を視察すべく遣はしたる冬男は、冬去り春夏も過ぎ秋の初めとなりけれども、何の消息もなきままに、巌ケ根は稍不安の空気に満され、重臣の水音、瀬音を招き、且つ春男、夏男、秋男の三人と共に、執政所に集り鳩首謀議を凝らした。

巌ケ根は歌もて語る。

『高光山以西の国形調査ぶべく 出でにし冬男は今に帰らず。 もしやもし水奔草の中毒に 冬男は身亡せたるにあらずや。 夜な夜なにあやしき夢を吾見たり 心にかかる冬男の身の上。 斯くならば再び人を遣はして 冬男の所在を探させむと思ふ』

水音は答へて、

『執政の宣り言宜よ吾も亦 朝夕心にかかりけらしな。 音に聞く忍ケ丘に水奔鬼 旅行く人を損ふと聞く。 水奔鬼笑ひ婆アは道の辺に 立ちて旅人を誘ひ殺すと』

瀬音は歌ふ。

『葭原や水奔草の根にひそむ 湿虫の害は恐ろしと聞く。 案ずるに湿虫その他の毒虫に 冬男の君は損はれけむ。 兎も角も高光山に人を派し 冬男の安否を探るにしかず』

ここに三人の協議により、巌ケ根は第三男の秋男を首領とし、四人の従者を従へて、高光山に至る大原野を探させしむべく、水上山の館を出立せしむる事となりぬ。

秋男は松、竹、梅、桜の四人の従者を従へ、水上山を立出で、弟冬男のとりし道を避け、南方に向ひ、高光山の方面に進まむと決心の臍を固め、神殿に出立の祈願をこめ、父の巌ケ根に向つて言葉静に歌もて宣る。

『ちちのみの父の御言を被りて 吾は高光山に進まむ。 いかならむ悩みありとも国の為と 吾は恐れじたとへ死すとも。 弟の所在を探ねあくまでも 父母の心を安んじ奉らむ。 葭原の国土を閉せる葭草や 水奔草を刈りて放らむ。 悪神は水奔草の野辺に潜み 行手の人に災すといふ。 さりながら吾には神の守りあり 如何なる曲津も恐れず進まむ。 父上も母も心を安んじませ 吾は一人の旅にあらねば』

巌ケ根は涙をふるひながら、表面は元気さうに歌ふ。

『弟の冬男の行方わかるまで 汝は帰らず国見して来よ。 弟の消息判ればすみやかに 知らせ来れよ桜に仰せて』

秋男は又歌ふ。

『父上の厳の御言葉謹みて 吾はあくまで力つくさむ』

水音は歌ふ。

『勇ましき秋男の君の出で立ちを 送る水音の心はかなしも。 水の音風の響も気遣はる 君の旅路の安くあれよと。 野路を越え川を渡りて出でてゆく 君の雄々しき姿を送らむ』

瀬音は歌ふ。

『吾も亦父の御言に従ひて 神国守れば安く出でませ。 御館に心残さずとくとくと 神国の為に出でませ君よ。 君行かばこの御館は淋しけれど 神国の為と思へば詮なし。 曲津神の伊猛り狂ふ大野原 進ませ給へ神の力に』

秋男は歌ふ。

『ありがたし君の誠はどこまでも 忘れず力と進み行くべし。 いざさらば吾は進まむ四柱の 友と力を組合せつつ。 秋さりて野辺に百草咲き匂ひ 旅ゆく吾を迎へ送りす。 花の香に包まれてゆく秋の野の 吾旅立ちは清しかるべし。 弟の所在探れば直様に 桜を帰して知らせ奉らむ。 新しき国土拓かむと出でてゆく 吾に力を添へさせ給へ。 住みなれし水上山の聖場を 去らむと思へば涙ぐまるる。 吾涙歎きの涙にあらずして 旅ゆく嬉し涙なるぞや。 女郎花桔梗刈萱匂ふ野を 松、竹、梅、桜伴ひてゆく』

§ 2

Having finished singing thus, Akio bravely set out from the mansion of his father at Iwagane, together with his four attendants.

Akio: 'Departing from Minakamiyama, I advance to see the Kunigata. How valiant is my journey! Crossing over the Ōkaha and the Ogaha, as I advance, the Momogusa and Kikiyō bloom in competition, their beauty fragrant and charming, enlivening the path we tread. Where is my younger brother Fuyuo now? Is it not possible that he has lost his Inochi in the disaster of the Suihonsau, which grows so thick as to leave no space? For some reason, it weighs upon my mind. During the Yakuichinen that has passed, not a single message has come, like the summer wind. Autumn has finally arrived, and the Chibana in the fields are releasing their Haukau all around. I have heard faintly that there is a laughing old woman at Shinobugaoka, but I shall set my path to the south. Circling to the eastern side of Shinobugaoka, I shall investigate the Shōsoku of my brother, and upon that, I shall determine my Haushin. Advance, advance, let us advance! Even if the Akuriu and Dokujiya are thick, with the Kototama bestowed by Kami, I shall subdue and pacify them. Even the Kumade of the road shall be passed without incident, and we shall advance most Yasuyasu. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, this journey of Kami's guidance, there shall be nothing in the world to hinder it. Matsu, Take, Ume, and you, Sakura, my attendants, do not fear at all. We are the children of Kami, the palace of Kami; since we have entrusted ourselves to Kami, why should we fear any Magatsu? Advance, advance, let us advance!'

The attendant deity Matsu sings:

Matsu: 'Ah, how valiant, how valiant! At the warrior-like bearing of Akio-kimi, our hearts also rise with courage. As if walking through an uninhabited field, we shall cut down and mow down all the Magatsu that would hinder us, and press straight ahead. To the sacred ground of Takamitsuyama, receiving the power of Kami, it is joyful to advance. Following Akio-kimi, we traverse the mountain edges and fields where hundreds and thousands of flowers bloom and fragrance wafts. Today's departure is valiant. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, may there be blessings upon our journey.'

The attendant deity Take sings:

Take: 'Departing from Minakamiyama, following Akio-kimi through the autumn fields where flowers bloom and fragrance wafts, it is joyful to advance. We too are the children of Kami, the palace of Kami. Even if any Magatsu should hinder us, why should we fear? Our firm hearts, like the Iwagane, shall keep the commands of our lord and ascend the path of the demons. Even if the Suihonsau are thick, even if the power of the demons is strong, we shall display our brave hearts and advance without bending or fearing. May there be happiness for our party. The mountain of flames is faintly visible. Let us advance to the great plains. As we cross the mountain edges where the Momogusa are fragrant, the autumn wind blows against our faces.'

Take: 'Adding coolness to the evening dusk, when I look up and gaze at the sky, the pale midday moon shows its face, smiling faintly. Is it letting us know that there is happiness for our party? How grateful I am. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, may there be power in the living Kototama. May there be happiness for our party.'

The attendant deity Ume sings:

Ume: 'Ah, how joyful, how joyful! Following Akio-kimi, it is a joy to advance across the great plains. The mountain of flames draws near, and why does my heart feel so brave? The insects chirping in the shade of the grass, and the voices of the hundred birds twittering in the treetops have become clear, but the sun has already darkened in the west, and the path ahead can no longer be seen. Nevertheless, the command of the lord is noble and heavy, and cannot be disobeyed. Whether this way or that, we must advance to the foot of the mountain of flames. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, may there be happiness for our party.'

The attendant deity Sakura sings:

Sakura: 'In the autumn sky where the sound of insects is clear, the sun leans toward the western mountains, and the light of the moon becomes ever more brilliant. The stars fill the heavens and sparkle, and my feet have become bright. Even if evil spirits and poisonous beasts attack, I shall not fear. Holding high the sword of Kototama, with the Takamitsuyama, where the Mihishiro-kami has appeared, as our goal, we shall advance with a sincere heart. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, may you bestow your divine grace.'

As they sang thus and advanced for about an hour, they suddenly came upon a small hill. The five members of the party climbed up this hill, and while gazing at the moon through the trees, they rested for a while from the fatigue of their journey.

斯く歌ひ終り、秋男は勇ましく四人の供人と共に、巌ケ根の父の館を立ち出でにける。

『水上山を立ち出でて 国形見むと進み行く 吾旅立ちのいさましさ 大川小川乗り越えて 進めば床し百草桔梗 艶を競ひて咲き匂ふ 吾等が行手を賑はせり 弟冬男は今いづこ 処せきまで茂りたる 水奔草の災に 生命捨てしにあらざるか 何とはなしに気にかかる 約一年のその間 何の便りも夏の風 漸く秋も来向ひて 野辺の千花はプンプンと あたりに芳香放つなり 忍ケ丘に笑ひ婆 ありとほのかに聞きつれど 吾は道をば南して

従神の桜は歌ふ。

『虫の音清しき秋の空 陽は西山にかたむきて いよいよ冴ゆる月の光 星満天にきらめきて わが足下は明くなりぬ 悪鬼毒獣せめるとも 吾は恐れじ言霊の 剣を高くかざしつつ 御樋代神の現れませる 高光山を目あてとし 真心もちて進むべし ああ惟神々々 恩頼を賜へかし』

斯く歌ひつつ前進する事一時ばかり、ふと突き当りたる小さき丘あり。一行五人はこの丘に攀ぢ登り、木の間の月を眺めながら、しばらく旅の疲労を休め居る。
