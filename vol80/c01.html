Chapter 1: 独り Journey [2005]

独り旅〔2005〕

§ 1

The Mamono that brings joy to men, makes them weep, makes them angry, causes them to die, lays waste to Sanya, overturns Taikakaurō and transforms the world into a street of Shiyura, is not the Daisansai of Fūsuikuwa, nor is it the disaster of the Shōsansai of Kibiyōsen; when viewed from the Heumen, it is Renai itself, which seems so beautiful and tastes so sweet, causing the spirit of Shiyunyō to drift about the Shihen. The Uhebito, the Nōkōshiyō, the vagabonds, and the Kinjiuchiugiyo all stake their Seimei to fight for the sake of love.

Here, the Adeyaka who resided as the administrator of the Kuni at the mansion of Minakamiyama was unexpectedly rescued by the Shimane of the dragon, surrounded by the storms of love, the rains of affection, and the gravel of passion, and Shirazushirazu, he suffered a Rakujiyō and was imprisoned by the love of the Ninmenriushin Kakitsubata; however, becoming the target of the Senbau of Amata Jiyoriushintachi, he could no longer remain at the Shimane of the dragon, and conspiring with Kakitsubata, he took advantage of the Tsukiyo and secretly slipped out of the Omotemon of the dragon's capital, was aided by the boat of the Yahiro-wani and the gods of water, fire, and earth, returned to the mansion of his parents at Minakamiyama, took Kakitsubata as his wife, and spent happy days in harmony like the zither and the flute; yet, as the proverb goes that good things are often beset by demons, the three goddesses of the Shimane of the dragon, Shirahagi, Shiragiku, and Ominaeshi, transformed back into their original dragon bodies, crossed the waves of Lake Tamaya, and hid themselves at Fujigaoka on the opposite bank of the Ōi River, which flows at the foot of Minakamiyama where the Adeyaka lived, enjoying themselves by listening to the voice of the Adeyaka day and night; but here, when Kakitsubata, in the pain of giving birth to a child, returned to her original dragon body and was sleeping while holding the precious child, she was glimpsed, and the Adeyaka, terrified by her fearsome appearance, fled into his mansion and remained trembling.

Now, Kakitsubata, ashamed that her husband had discovered her wretched dragon body, left the child in the birth hut, threw herself into the deep abyss of the Ōi River and became a demon of the abyss; yet, unable to forget his wife's affection, and though fearful, out of longing and sadness, when he attempted a pleasure trip by floating a boat on the abyss of Ōi, the spirits of the dragon gods Shirahagi, Shiragiku, and Ominaeshi competed for love with the dragon god Kakitsubata, grinding their teeth against each other and staining their mouths red; the Adeyaka, unable to bear it, suddenly threw himself into the abyss, whereupon the heavens and earth shook, a violent wind blew up, heavy rain fell incessantly, and it suddenly transformed into a world of darkness, and the sacred ground of Minakamiyama became a street of Shiyura. When the Kunitsukami lamented and grieved, and the hell of Avici was manifested, the god Mihishirogami, Asagirihime-no-kami, parted the clouds of the sky and descended majestically toward the summit of Minakamiyama, accompanied by four attendant gods, and by proclaiming the Kototama of rigor, she calmed the tragic scene of the natural disasters, caused the heavenly pure land to be built, entrusted the infant Tatsuhiko, born between the Adeyaka and Kakitsubata, to the god Kokorohime-no-kami until he reached adulthood, caused the old couple who had lost their heir to retire, and had the chief retainer Iwagane take charge of state affairs until Tatsuhiko reached adulthood; here, Asagirihime-no-kami, together with her attendant gods, raised clouds of living Kototama, established her palace on the eastern face of Mount Takamitsu, which towers in the distant sky, and set out to reign over this Kuni eternally. Iwagane, who remained behind, revered the divine words of Mihishirogami, served his aged lord, made the Kuni of Yosan a world of supreme governance and peace, comforted the hearts of the old couple, and seeking to repay the divine grace of Mihishirogami, prayed to the gods morning and evening, cherished the Kunitsukami, and thus had no other thoughts but state affairs.

The area about ten ri or more to the east of Minakamiyama was reclaimed through the power of Yamagamihiko, and the Kunitsukami and others were engaged in the work of cultivation with peace of mind, but the distance to the foot of Mount Takamitsu was about three hundred ri. Furthermore, to the west of Mount Takamitsu, according to the command of Mihishirogami, Iwagane, the administrator of the state mansion of Minakamiyama, was anxious day and night, wondering how he might reclaim it. This Iwagane was unexpectedly blessed with many children, having four sons: Haruo, Natsuo, Akio, and Fuyuo, all of whom were adults capable of standing in the world on their own.

人を喜ばせ、人を泣かせ、人を怒らせ、人を死なしめ、山野を滅尽し、大廈高楼を覆へして修羅の巷と世を化す魔物は、大三災の風水火ならずとも、小三災の飢病戦の災ならずとも、表面より見れば、さも美しく味ひよく、春陽の気を四辺に漂はしむる恋愛そのものである。雲の上人も、農工商も、ルンペンも、禽獣虫魚も、恋のためには、生命を賭して戦ふものである。

茲に水上山の館に、国土の司として鎮まれる艶男は、竜の島根にゆくりなくも救ひ上げられ、恋の嵐、愛の雨、情の礫に取りまかれ、不知不識に落城して、人面竜身の燕子花の恋に囚はれたるが、数多の女竜神等の羨望の的となり、竜の島ケ根に居たたまらず、燕子花と諜し合はせ、月夜を幸ひ、窃かに竜の都の表門を忍び出で、八尋鰐や水火土の神の舟に助けられ、水上山の父母の館に帰り、燕子花を妻となし、琴瑟相和して楽しき月日を送りけるが、好事魔多しとの譬に洩れず、竜の島根の女神、白萩、白菊、女郎花の三人は元の竜体と変じ、玉耶湖の波を渡りて、艶男の住まへる水上山の麓を流るる大井川の対岸、藤ケ丘に身を潜め、艶男の日夜の声を聞きて楽しみ居たりけるが、茲に艶男の妻燕子花の子を生める苦しさに、元の竜体となりて玉の御子を抱き寝れるを垣間見され、その恐ろしき姿に仰天して、艶男は吾館に逃げ入りて震へ居たりける。

さて燕子花は、その浅ましき竜体を夫に見付けられしを恥ぢ、御子を産屋に残し置き、大井川の深淵に身を投じて淵の鬼となりけるを、妻の愛情忘れ難く、恐は怖はながら、恋しさ、悲しさ、大井の淵に舟を浮べて清遊を試むる折、白萩、白菊、女郎花の竜神の霊、燕子花の竜神と恋を争ひ、互に鎬を削りて、口を紅に染めけるが、艶男も堪りかね、忽ち淵に身を投じたるより、天地震動し、暴風吹き起り、大雨頻りに臻り、忽ち暗黒界と化し、水上山の聖場は修羅の巷となりにける。国津神は嘆き悲しみ、阿鼻叫喚の地獄を現出したる折もあれ、御空の雲を分けて悠然と水上山の頂上さして、御樋代神なる朝霧比女の神は、四柱の侍神を従へ降りまし、厳の言霊を宣り上げて、天変地異の惨状をしづめたまひて、天国浄土を築かせたまひ、艶男と燕子花との中に生れたる嬰児の竜彦が成人するまで、子心比女の神に托し給ひ、世継の御子を失ひし老夫婦を退隠せしめ、重臣の巌ケ根をして、竜彦の成人まで国務を預らしめ置きて、茲に朝霧比女の神は従神と共に生言霊の雲を起し、遥かの空に聳ゆる高光山の東面に御宮居を定め、永遠に此国土に君臨せむと出でさせ給ひける。跡に残りし巌ケ根は、御樋代神の神言畏み、老いたる君に仕へつつ、予讃の国をして至治泰平の世となし、老身夫婦の心を慰め、御樋代神の恩命に報いむとして、朝な夕な神を祈り、国津神を愛み、以て国務に余念なかりける。

水上山より以東約十余里の地点は、山神彦の力によりて開拓され、国津神等も心を安んじて耕作の業に従事し居たれども、高光山の山麓までは約三百里までの遠距離あり。而して高光山以西は御樋代神の御命令に依り、水上山の国館の執政巌ケ根は、如何にもして開拓せむと日夜焦慮しつつありける。この巌ケ根は意外の子福者にして、春男、夏男、秋男、冬男の四柱の男子を持ち、何れも一人世に立つまでの成年者なりける。

§ 2

The Kuni of Yoshihara was, as its name suggests, covered entirely with Yoshigusa, and among them grew a plant called Suihonsau, which bloomed with yellow flowers; though they appeared truly beautiful from the outside, both the flowers, leaves, and stems contained a potent poison, and anyone who touched them would be struck by the toxin and instantly lose their Seimei, so that neither the Kunitsukamitachi nor the Kinjiuchiugiyo dared to dwell in the vast Genya for fear of that peril.

The only creatures capable of preserving their Seimei were a Hachiuzoku called Ijichi, which resembled a mixture of a crocodile with a Kafura, a snake, and a Mukade, and as they inflicted harm upon all Jinchiku, it was not a place for the Kunitsukami to reside, and thus from the beginning of the Hira, the land was left to the overgrowth of Yoshigusa and Suihonsau, the Monosugo nature of which defied Gengo.

Iwakane, intending to Hira this Arechi, first caused his Daiyonan, Fuyuo, to undertake a Bōkenteki Tabiyu and sent him to the Chiyōjiyō of Takamitsuyama.

Fuyuo, obeying his father's command alone, with his heart swelling with courage and a desire to Hira the boundless Genya, first set out to inspect the shape of the Kuni as his initial step, and having clad his entire body in leather garments, he forged ahead through the thickets of Suihonsau until he came upon a wide, low hill.

Here, many Kunitsukami had dug holes here and there to dwell, forming a somewhat large village, but they were none of them living humans; they were a group of ghosts called Suihonki who had lost their Seimei after being struck by the poison of the Suihonsau.

Fuyuo, knowing nothing of this, reached this hill, approached a small house, and stood at the gate to ask for a night's lodging.

Fuyuo: 'Crossing the wilderness where the ugly weeds grow thick, I have come; pray, grant me a night's lodging like the dew. I am Fuyuo, born as the fourth son of Iwakane, the administrator of the time at the sacred palace of the mountain of Minakami. To Hira this Kuni-hara, I have obeyed my father's command, and having crossed the wilderness where the poisonous weeds grow, the twilight has fallen upon the path I must take to the sacred site of Takamitsuyama. Grant me a night's lodging.'

As he sang, an old woman with white hair and a strange appearance emerged from within, limping as she stood at the gate.

Old Woman: 'I am a crone called Warai, one of the Kunitsukami. If you do not mind, please stay here. Ahahaha, I have a strange habit; I laugh whenever I see a person's face. Ihihihi, Ufufufu, Ehehehe, Ohohoho. How amusing, how funny, how interesting. This crone has lived here for a long time and knows everything about the age of the gods in detail. A night is too short; stay for three days, five days, ten days, or twenty days without worry. My house is dirty, but it is spacious in the back. I have three daughters, beautiful as jewels—Yama, Kawa, and Umi—and we live very peacefully. First, please proceed to the back. Ahahaha, Ihihihi, Ufufufu, Ehehehe, Ohohoho. Oh, how interesting, how funny.'

Saying this, the old woman held her stomach and rolled about on the ground, as if she could not contain her amusement.

Fuyuo, following the old woman's suggestion, proceeded deep inside, where three maidens of sixteen, beautiful in appearance, came out to welcome him with smiles and kindness.

Yama: 'Are you a god who has come from the sacred site of the mountain of Minakami? What a valiant figure you are.'

Kawa: 'How beautiful and manly are your eyes; your face resembles the moon. The handsome man I saw in my dreams night after night closely resembles your figure. Living on this hill morning and evening, I have longed for your arrival. You are destined to be my husband and the ruler of this village.'

Umi: 'We sisters have waited impatiently, and now we have met you, our beautiful lord. Since you have come to this village, do not think of returning to the Mototsu-kuni; please resign yourself to staying.'

Fuyuo, with a puzzled look, sang.

Fuyuo: 'I hear strange things; I am currently advancing to Hira the Kuni. I asked for a night's lodging, and tomorrow I must travel early to Takamitsuyama. Being detained by three beautiful maidens, my soul has begun to tremble.'

§ 3

The mountain sings:

Mountain: 'Strange it is indeed, to be surrounded by the demon of love, as you are. You who have crossed the path of Shikogusa, shall never be able to return to the Mikuni again. No matter how much you struggle, how could we three allow your departure tomorrow?'

Fuyuo sings:

Fuyuo: 'Revering the Mikoto of the Mihishiro Kami, I have come to behold the Kunigata. Should I, a Kunitsukami, be detained by the words of three bewitching women?'

The river sings:

River: 'What power could you possibly possess to defy the words spoken by my sisters? A Masurao who lacks the heart of love is but a fool, akin to wood and stone. This village is a village of sleep; how could the Murabito let you return in peace?'

Fuyuo sings:

Fuyuo: 'Having come after passing through the fearsome Shikogusahara, am I to be troubled by women once again?'

The sea sings:

Sea: 'Do you not know, you who are afflicted by the curse of the Suihonsau by the roadside?'

Fuyuo sings:

Fuyuo: 'As I have come wearing my Kahagoromo, how could it ever affect my Inochi?'

Just as he sings this, the old woman from before comes limping into the depths, saying:

Old Woman: 'If the power of the three maidens is not enough, I shall never permit it, no matter where you go. Hahahaha! Ihihihi! What a pitiable man you are.'

Saying this, she goes out again, heading toward the front. Fuyuo, exhausted from his day-long journey as if his body were bound by ropes, collapses on the spot, while the three Otome, at his head, at his feet, and at his waist, as if biting into him, were whispering something like a spell. The night deepens profoundly, and the strange voices of the birds, beasts, insects, and fish living around this hill tear through the darkness of the surroundings and gradually grow louder.
