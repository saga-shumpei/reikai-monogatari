Chapter 15: 憤死 [2019]

憤死〔2019〕

§ 1

Akio stands in the shade of the trees as before, and taking this place as his final stand, he proclaims the Ikukototama.

Akio: 'In Takaamahara appearing, By the Mikoto of the Lord Ōkami, Born of the Kototama of the sound A, The Kamubashira of the Mizu no Mitama, Akitsuo’s divine Kuniguni, Having traveled through, the Kototama’s Iki he condensed to create Kami, For the Isaoshi of creating the Kunitsuchi, Both the Ōunabara and the Kunitsuchi, Were created in detail and with grace. Among them, the vast and boundless sea, Floating in the very center, Among the many Shimajima, Especially the vast Yoshihara, Yoshiharaguni is that which was born, From the precious Oniki of the Lord Kami. Yamakahakusaki and Hitogusa, Even to the ends of birds and beasts, All were the Mitamamono born, From the Oniki of the Lord Kami. In this Keshikuni, peacefully, Living the life granted in this world, Not only humans but also the Mitama of the Yoyomogi, There shall be none who do not receive the blessing. By the Mikoto of Asagirihime, Sent by the Lord Ōkami, Our father’s Iwakane, At the sacred site of Mizukamiyama, Has built a precious mansion. He is the ruler who shall govern, All of the Kunihara of Yosan. I am the third child of Iwakane, A Kunitsukami named Akio. The lions and bears lurking on the peaks, The wolves and venomous snakes, And even the Warai-baba, Are all under the command of my father’s Mikoto. If, by my Kototama, there should be, Any who come to oppose me, In this world, let alone the Yoyomogi, To the ends of anywhere, I shall never, Absolutely permit a place to dwell. You, Magatsumi and Magatsubi, To the Kototama I strike forth, Incline your ears and open your eyes, Sweep away the clouds and mists of your hearts, Return to the heart of sincerity, And serve in obedience to Kami. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-kamu, Taking the Great Heart of the Mitama of the Ōkami, As one's own heart, Here Akio reverently, Proclaims and transmits for your sake: One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, Hundred, thousand, ten thousand, eighty million Kami, Protect us and bless us. May there be power in my Kototama, May there be light in my Kototama.'

Thus he sings with a clear voice. Perhaps the Magatsukami were also softened in heart by this Kototama, for a fragrant breeze blew comfortably, shaking the ominaeshi on the mountainside, the voice of the kalavinka chirping on the branches became exceptionally clear, and the sound of insects gathering in the small grasses sang most beautifully.

The pine sings:

Pine: 'How grateful I am, for in the Kototama of Lord Akio, I feel as if heaven and earth are opening. Even to speak of it is awe-inspiring, for in the strict Kototama, My own Mitama stands up in vigor. Since the Kototama of my glorious Lord is pure, Even the Magatsu shall surely come to submit.'

The bamboo sings:

Bamboo: 'The black clouds that wrapped the great sky have scattered and vanished, And the sun and moon are clearing across the heavens. Because the Kototama proclaimed by my Lord brings fortune, The wind blowing over Yoshihara has calmed. My heart has become clear, I know not why, And I think of the fortune of my journey ahead. Though the mountain of flames heard of in legend is steep, If I proclaim the Kototama, I shall climb it with ease. I have heard that fierce beasts gather at the summit, And guard the seeds of fire. Even the mischief of Warai-baba and the mocking hag, Have become but a wind blowing over the fields. The hag who raged at Tsukimigaoka the previous night, Alas, has hidden her shadow.'

The plum sings:

Plum: 'In the strict Kototama proclaimed aloud, The clouds and mists of the four directions of heaven and earth are clearing. The Kototama of the divine breath of the Chihayaburu Kami, Is the power that washes this world. I think there can be nothing more precious, Than the Ikukototama in this world. A wind of peace rises in the mountains and fields, And the blooming and fruiting of flowers is the fortune of the Kototama. I did not know my Lord was so precious, Oh, the living Kototama of the Kami Akio.'

The cherry sings:

Cherry: 'Having encountered various hardships, we, Have realized the power of the Ikukototama. Even if tens of thousands of enemies appear, I shall not fear, For my Lord’s Kototama rings out clearly. I hear that this heaven and earth were born, From the Isaoshi of the five great father-sounds of A-O-U-E-I. Now, have I realized the preciousness of the power of Kami, Foolish as I am. Repeating my travels with a grass pillow, at the foot of the mountain, In the shade of the dense trees, I realized the Way. What does it matter how Mizuhashiri-oni schemes, When I carry the sword of Kototama? Though the sword of Kototama I carry may rust, My Lord possesses a sharp power.'

Here, Akio resolves himself, gaining strength from the preciousness of the merit of the Ikukototama, and standing at the head, he climbs upward while singing, tracing the wall-like mountain surface to the right and left.

茲に秋男は意を決し、生言霊の功の尊さに力を得、自ら先頭に立ちて、壁立つ山肌を右に左に伝ひながら歌ひつつ登り行く。

§ 2

'Though the mountain of Kuwaen be steep, / Through the Momokusachigusa I shall go. / Though they block and hinder my path, / What of this Yamamichi shall I fear? / With the sword of Ikukototama, / I shall slash and sweep to the left and right, / And climb, purifying the path ahead. / At the Kuwakō of this summit, / It is said that Shishiō, Kumaō, Toraō, / Wolves, and Orochi gather, / Guarding it through the Chiuya. / No matter how fierce the beasts may be, / By the sword of the Kototama / Bestowed by Kami, I shall make them Matsuro. / Shall I not seize and bring back / The fire-seed of the Shigumi of Kami? / Though this Yamamichi is steep / And difficult to traverse, with all my Magokoro, / Exhausting my body and soul, / For the sake of Kami and for the sake of the world, / To us who advance, / What Magatsu can possibly hinder us? / Pine, Bamboo, Plum, and Cherry, / Take heart and follow me. / Though once I was caught off guard, / My Mitama, having fully realized / The power of the Ikukototama, / No longer fears anything. / Ah, how brave, how Omoshiro! / In the Saukutsu where the Magami gather, / With the Kototamatsurugi drawn, / I shall advance steadily.'

Climbing while treading upon the Ihane and Kine, the mountain of Kuwaen is indeed pure. Looking down, the white clouds at the foot of the mountain play abundantly and sway in the wind. Having climbed this mountain that towers into the sky of white clouds, I shall look upon the shape of the lands in the four directions. Is that the figure of Mount Takamitsu, hazily shimmering in the distant spring sky? Mount Takamitsu is venerable, for it is the sacred ground where the Kami of the Mihishiro resides. How solemn is the figure of the mountain of Takamitsu, where Asagirihime-sama dwells eternally. If we advance a little longer, we shall reach the summit; let us rest our breath here for a while.

So singing, they spread out the daylilies by the roadside, sat down heavily, and while Pine, Bamboo, Plum, and Cherry all looked out over the four directions below, each sang in turn.

Pine sings:

'At the foot, a hundred trees grow thick, and in this vicinity, / Only daylilies have grown. / When I climb and look from this high mountain that pierces the clouds, / Even my own breath is painful. / The land of Ashihara is entirely wrapped in white clouds, / As if it were the surface of the sea. / When I look at Mount Takamitsu, / Which stands hazily in the sky yonder, it is venerable. / Is it a mountain from which venerability naturally springs, / As the residence of the Mihishiro Kami?'

Bamboo sings:

'The blowing wind also pierces my body with a chill, / And I feel as if my body has become light. / When I follow behind the young lord and climb, / Even the sound of insects has ceased to be heard. / This place on Mount Kuwaen is finally the seventh station, / Yet there is no sound of birds or insects. / Because there are no other plants or trees / Swaying in the wind on the field of pampas grass, there is no scent of flowers either.'

Plum sings:

'It is a sacred place with a fine view, / To the extent that it does not look like a mountain where the Magatsu-kami gather. / The Magatsu-kami must be dwelling in groups at the foot, / With the line of white clouds as their boundary. / As far as the eye can see, it is a wilderness where reeds grow thick, / And the mountain of Mizukami floats above the clouds. / I yearn for the Iwanekane-tsukasa / That dwells on the summit of the far-off Mount Mizukami. / Although I have encountered various hardships of Magatsu, / I have finally climbed to this place. / The mountain wind blows from beneath my feet; / When I think of it, it is indeed a high mountain. / This mountain, where lions, bears, tigers, wolves, and even Orochi dwell, / Spews Kuwaen. / It is terrifying to hear that when night falls, the light of the flames / Illuminates the fields for over a hundred ri. / I wish to follow the young lord, take the fire-seed for the sake of the country, / And descend the mountain.'

Cherry sings:

'Though you have the sword of Kototama, be careful, / For the preparations of the Magatsu are strict. / The Magatsu-mi must be waiting, / Constructing their final defenses to trap us. / You must climb with all your strength in your soul, / For the dwelling of the Magatsu is near.'

Just as they were singing thus, suddenly a rain of large rocks fell from the summit, making a sound like a hundred thunderbolts falling, and the danger as they descended upon the five was beyond compare. The five, taking this as their final stand, dodged the rain of rocks and barely reached the summit, whereupon the fierce beasts and poisonous snakes, thinking them to be formidable enemies, glared with angry eyes, sharpened their fangs, and roared and bellowed with their mouths wide open as they bit at the five. The five heroes, thinking, 'How insolent, no matter what Magatsu hinders us, shall we not take the fire-seed?', approached the burning fire, and the group of fierce beasts and poisonous snakes, having watched them, attacked with all their might, grabbed the five heroes in their mouths, swung them around, and immediately threw them into the Kuwakō, and the sound of their triumphant songs and roars was like a hundred thunderbolts becoming one and rumbling. Thus, the poor five heroes were burned by the intense fire, turned into white bones, were tossed about by the breath of the Kuwaen, danced high into the sky, and fell back to the ground.
