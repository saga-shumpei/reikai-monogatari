Chapter 13: 樹下の囁き [2017]

樹下の囁き〔2017〕

§ 1

The five who had been revived, a party of five, rested under the shade of a tree at the foot of Kuwaenzan, and while gazing at the surrounding scenery, Akio sang in the morning sky.

Akio: 'Having traveled through the days of autumn, now here / I see the morning dew shining. Harassed by various Onibaba, My soul's valor ceases not. In the beautiful morning view, my soul Is reviving and showing its valor. What are the ugly schemes of the Onibaba to me, When they vanish in the rising morning sun? Magatsumi fears the light and relies on the dark night, How pathetic it is to act with Itake. In the light of the Asatsuhi, Who shines brilliantly, the host of Suihonki perishes. While bathing in the dew, the tips of the Kigi Maintain the Rurikuō in the shadow of the morning sun. Where is the shadow of the Nonoshiri-baba, Who raised black clouds and afflicted me? With a loathsomeness that makes one's hair stand on end, The ugly Onibaba utters her jeering voice. While bathing in the pleasant mountain breeze of an autumn morning, How enjoyable is the Sumika of the Maga. Softly, the Chibanamomobana blow and pass, The sound of the wind is clear, and my soul prospers. Even where the Shirahagi bloom in abundance, It is a mystery that demons dwell at the foot of this mountain. Is it the color of the clear sky, or have the colors of the Kusaki On the Yamahada turned a shade of blue? I shall subdue and harmonize all the attacking ugly Maga With the Ikukototama. How pathetic is the end of the Nonoshiri-baba, Who raised black clouds in the Ōzora and raged. Though it be high, I shall finish climbing the mountain, Take the fire seed, and establish the land. I saw a dream of standing by a river of blood, And realized the depth of the demons' schemes. Both moon and sun shine clearly in the sky, How pure is the rest of this morning. While bathing in the light of the shining Amatsuhi, I shall advance toward the summit. As the voices of the birds become clear, the mountain sleeves, The autumn wind blowing is cool.'

The pine tree sings.

Pine: 'When I see the Shirahagi flowers blooming in the fields and mountains, I wonder if the demons' hearts are disturbed by the wind. Though they have a gentle appearance, it is sad To see the disorder of the Shirahagi flowers. Is the Nonoshiri-baba like the Shirahagi, Which scatters so easily in the blowing wind? The Kusaki of the autumn mountains are all turning autumn colors, And trembling in the north wind. Perhaps the Onibaba is like the Ochiha, Which scatters in the wind, so frail. What does the Ho-susuki beckon with its weak strength, As it is knocked down by the autumn wind?'

The bamboo tree sings.

Bamboo: 'I wish to hear the whereabouts of the five maidens Whom I met at the Onibaba's mansion. Though I am a spirit, I shall not forget The promise to protect my journey. Although the flames of Kuwaenzan pierce the heavens, The autumn wind blows at the foot of this mountain. It is a mountain of fire that occasionally roars And rains burning stones in all directions. I think we five shall not leave this mountain Until the fire seed is in our hands. The summit of this mountain is wrapped in clouds, Are the evil beasts and poisonous snakes gathering there?'

The plum tree sings.

Plum: 'When I see the Ominaeshi swaying in the wind, I think of the attire of the noble maidens. At the foot of the mountain where Hagi and Kikyo bloom in purple, How pleasant it is to rest, bathed in the morning sun. Now that I have come, the summit of the mountain of fire Is ever more distant and ever more high. As you go through this mountain where various Magatsukami gather, Be mindful, while proclaiming the Kototama.'

The cherry tree sings.

Cherry: 'In this mountain, which is said to be the territory of the Onibaba, Strange things happen one after another. Even so, if we advance with a sincere heart, We shall climb over any hardship with ease. If we possess one fire seed from Kuwaenzan, This land should be at peace. Having come from far away, leaving the mountains above the water, Today we face the army of the Majin. I saw a dream of the Warai-baba and the Nonoshiri-baba, And furthermore, the Okori-baba. Even hearing the name "baba" is detestable, And I have come to think of it as filthy.'

Akio sings.

秋男は歌ふ。

§ 2

'With the Masurao's Yatakegokoro stirred up, Let us ascend the Sanchiyō of Kuwaen.'

Singing thus, with Matsu and Take in the Sentō, and Ume and Sakura in the rear, they climb the Kabeta Yōchiyō Sakamichi, carving out their path Hitoashi by Hitoashi.

Akio sings:

'Untokodotsukoi, dotsukoishiyo. Though the mountain of Kuwaen be steep, Though the fury of the Akuma be strong, How could a Masurao fear? Exerting a firm heart, I climb this Kifuhan. Obana sways, and Momobana, To the right and left of my walking feet, Fragrant and pure, the sound of insects, Is heard so clearly. Ah, how brave, how brave! Since the beginning when Ametsuchi opened, An unprecedented mountain climb. Though Mauriyōdokujiya may lurk, With the sword of Ikukototama, I shall cut through and clear the Yoshihara, To solidify the foundation of the Mikuni. Up to the Kuwakō on the mountain ridge, With Yamatodama that will not cease to advance, Advance, advance, come, advance! Amatsumisora is ever high, Looking down upon the Chijiyō, The Kogane Nobe, bathed in the light Of the Amatsubi, sparkles, Shining as far as the eye can see. Though the path I walk is long, Someday I shall climb the Kuwaen-yama. And upon its summit, holding A single spark of fire, I shall save the people of the world universally. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, May fortune be with our party. Amatsukami and Kunitsukami, All the hundred gods, hear me. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, Advancing according to the will of the gods, Untokodotsukoi, dotsukoishiyo.'

Singing thus, Akio, making nothing of the Kifuhan, climbs on valiantly.

Matsu sings:

'As I climb, the summit appears ever more distant, Oh, the Kuwaen-yama that makes the heart uneasy. Is it a mysterious mountain? Though I walk and walk, It is a high peak that knows no end. Has the Akugami caused an obstruction? My feet Have grown heavy and I cannot move them forward. In any case, let us rest our breath here, Shall we climb on, Akio-wakagimi?'

Saying this, he sits heavily upon a rock that protrudes about a shaku from the ground, gasping for breath. The party follows suit, sitting heavily upon the kayagusa, trying to calm their rough breathing.

Akio sings:

'Though I walk and walk, this mountain that knows no end, How mysterious it is, receding further and further. I know not if it be a mountain of demons or a mountain of hell, But it is a suspicious mountain that gradually recedes. Have I fallen into the trap of the Magatsukami once again? This mountain climb that makes the heart uneasy.'

Take sings:

'Your words are correct, Wakagimi. If this mountain Be the dwelling of the Warai-baba. Strange is this mountain climb; forever We go and return to the same place. My eyes seem to be dazzled, and though my heart Burns with impatience, the path makes no progress.'

Ume sings:

'When I rub my eyes and look well, The position of my body has not changed in the least. Perhaps we have only been moving our feet While standing in the shade of the Komori-gi.'

Sakura sings:

'Though I think of how I might climb, The fury of the Magatsu is a strong obstruction. I wish to climb while singing The Kazauta that the Amatsukami bestowed upon me. Oh, Warai-baba who fled when struck by the Kazauta, There can be no weapon that surpasses this.'

Akio sings:

'That may be so. From here on, I shall Sing the celestial Kazauta of Kototama and climb. Hitotsu, Futatsu, Mittsu, Yottsu, Itsutsu, Muttsu, Nanatsu, Yattsu, Kokonotsu, Tō, Momo, Chi, Yorozu, May the eight million gods protect me. Helped by the severe power of Kototama, I shall finish climbing to the Sanchiyō of the mountain.'

Just as he sings this, from the direction of the treetops of the Komori-gi,

'Atsuhahahaha, Itsuhihihihi, Utsufufufu, Foolish ones, have you realized it? I am the master of the Mizuhashiri-oni who has lived for years at Shinobugaoka, the Warai-baba. How dare you wander into my dwelling! Now that it has come to this, you are mine. Oh, how pathetic you are. Itsuhihihihi, Assuming a stern appearance and acting grand by conquering demons, how ridiculous, how stupid. Will you surrender completely because you cannot succeed, or will you hang yourselves and die? What is your answer?'

§ 3

Utsufufufufu.

Fuyuo: 'How foolish are the ones who have fallen into the trap of the Takumi I have laid in advance. Truly, what a pitiful creature this Gaki is; he shall vanish like dew upon the mountain of Kuwaen. I shall shatter both the Inochi of the Tama-no-o and the Inochi of the Mitama, causing them to suffer and agonize. There shall be no day as pleasant as this Kefu, now that I have avenged the grudge of the Gaki of Fuyuo.'

Akio sings:

'I am a Masurao who shall not cease until I have conquered the Magatsumi that opposes me to the end. Since I am a Miko of the Kami who governs the rocky foundations, I shall not retreat even a single Hitoashi. Thinking of the shame of my Oyaoya, I shall not retreat a single Hitoashi from the Sumika of the Maga. I shall conquer the laughing crone, the one called the governor of the Magakami, and the mocking crone.'

A strange voice was heard again from the Jiyujiyō:

Gyahahahaha.

Magaonisama: 'This is Tsukimi-ga-oka. I am the Magaonisama, the mocking crone, who is the younger sister of the laughing crone, the governor of the Suihonki who has tormented you, and the one who mocks all Yogoto and Magakoto. Cleanse your ears well and listen. Since it has come to this, I shall not let you escape; prepare yourselves and surrender to the Gunmon of this crone. Since this life of the world cannot be maintained for long, offer it cleanly to me, become my subordinates, and faithfully perform evil. If you disobey, I have no choice but to twist and crush your bodies and Mitama, trample them to pieces, and show you infinite misery. Watsuhahahaha, Watsuhahahaha.'

The unpleasantness of the crone's voice, as if it were shattering, was such that goosebumps rose all over their bodies.

Akio, his sense of suspicion not clearing, suddenly looked up at the vast sky, and the brilliant light of the Amatsu-hi that had been there until now had vanished without a trace; the entire sky was blocked by black clouds, a gloomy atmosphere locked the four directions, and strange noises grew louder one after another.

The party of five, thinking this was their moment of life-or-death, were chanting the Ama-no-kazauta with all their might.

Having fallen once again into the trap of the Magakami, the five are pitifully enveloped in darkness.

The Takumi of the Akugami is deep, sevenfold and eightfold, as the curtain of black clouds wraps around them and attacks.

Feeling as if they were climbing a steep slope, they were wandering in the same place in the shadow of the trees.
