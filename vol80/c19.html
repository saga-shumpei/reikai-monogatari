Chapter 19: 笑譏 Wrath 泣 [2023]

笑譏怒泣〔2023〕

§ 1

Leaving the thicket forest, Haruo, Natsuo, and others, including Minaoto and Seoto, followed by a great number of Tomobito, advanced toward the Genya in the southeast.

Due to the Kanraku of the Kuwaen mountain, the Kuōkei of the surroundings underwent a Gekihen, and though it could not be clearly distinguished, a thick mist rose and covered the Daichi, perhaps a lake.

The shadows of Maujiu and Dokujiya were numerous, obstructing the path ahead, and none among them were without Fushiyō.

Fortunately for the party of Haruo, the Magakami who had suffered Jiyūshiyō were powerless, and so they advanced quite briskly.

The wind that blew felt somehow oppressive to the chest, and they walked through the Nobe where frost lay in the morning.

The cold was bitter, and the Inutsu Kūki filled the Tenchi.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara.

Whether the Yukusaki of Haruo's party would be Fukau or not, it shall become Meireu as the Monogatari is read on.

Haruo sings:

Leaving behind the mountain of water, crossing the Kayano, and climbing the hills, twilight fell upon the hill of thickets, and the party spent the night here. While listening to the cries of Maujiu and Dokujiya, I looked, and the Kuwaen mountain was gone without a trace, black smoke filling the sky, darkening the shadows of the sun and moon. Looking out far over the earth, thick mist stands to the right and left, and though it is daytime, the path ahead is uncertain in today's mystery. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. I wish to hear what has become of my two younger brothers. If the gods dwell in the Tenchi, I beseech you to teach us the fate of the two, clearly and in detail. I pray for this alone.

Natsuo sings:

Revering the father, the Mikoto, I have traveled through the wilderness where ugly weeds grow, and upon arriving here, white smoke fills the earth, and the path ahead has become invisible. I wish to see the shape of the land, but the surroundings are wrapped in haze, and the wind that blows is unpleasant; it is a winter journey where the cold pierces the body. The voices of the hundred birds chirping in the trees sound sad. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. I pray that you illuminate the path I walk.

Minaoto sings:

Has the Kuwaen mountain I looked up at collapsed? Only a lake is visible, and there is no sign of a mountain. I did not know there was a lake in such a field. Has this land changed? The sun and moon of heaven are hidden, and I, walking the path of the field in eternal darkness, feel lonely.

Seoto sings:

A lonely voice is heard from somewhere. Is it the cry of the Magatsu, or the sound of birds? Or is it the sound of winter insects? It is a journey of darkness where the path ahead is unknown. It is strange, even though it is still daytime. The low mountain visible ahead must be the hill of moon-viewing; we must hasten our steps and advance.

Thus, the party finally arrived at Tsukimi-ga-oka. Although the sun could not be seen, it appeared to be twilight, and the darkness deepened further. This was the place where the party of Akio had borrowed lodging for the night and attempted a Kototama battle with the mocking hag.

Haruo sings:

Upon finally arriving at Tsukimi-ga-oka, the curtain of twilight has fallen. What a lonely hill it is, where even the grass and trees have reddened from the frost. The tips of the bare trees, mixed among the evergreens, are stroking the dark sky. This hill feels all the more strange; is it where my younger brother stayed?

Natsuo sings:

Since the darkness of twilight is deep, it cannot be helped; I shall spend the night upon this hill. It is a hill of darkness where even the moon of the vast sky is hidden, and not a single star shines. The wind that blows pierces the skin, and it seems that strange voices can be heard from somewhere.

Minaoto sings:

Crossing the field where Maga-oni and great serpents swarm, I have arrived here in peace. Yet, I cannot set my heart at ease in this darkness, for I cannot fathom when the Maga might attack. If I sleep, will the Maga attack? I shall steel my heart and wait for the dawn.

Seoto sings:

A strange voice is echoing from somewhere. Do you not hear the voice of lamentation? Whether it be Maga-oni, a great serpent, or an Ijichi, I know not, but it is the voice of my soul trembling.

Just as they were singing thus, a discordant, off-key music began to resound, and suddenly the surroundings became as bright as day. However, while the area of about ten ken square was as if lamps had been lit indoors, the rest remained as if a screen of darkness had been erected.

『ちちのみの父のみことを畏みて 醜草茂る荒野原 辿りてここに来て見れば 白煙もうもう地に満ちて 行く手もわかずなりにけり 国形見むと思へども あたりは靄につつまれて 吹き来る風もいやらしく 寒さ身にしむ冬の旅 樹々に囀る百鳥の 声もかなしく聞ゆなり ああ惟神々々 わが行く道をあきらかに 照らさせ給へと願ぎ奉る』

『うち仰ぐ火炎の山はくづれしか 湖のみ見えて山かげもなし。 かかる野に湖ありとは知らざりき この地の上は変りたるにや。 天津日の月もかくれて常闇の 野路ゆく吾はさびしかりけり』

斯くして一行は漸く月見ケ丘に着きぬ。太陽は見えねども、最早黄昏時と見えて闇は益々深くなりぬ。ここは秋男一行が一夜の宿を借りて、譏り婆と言霊戦を試みたる跡なりき。

瀬音は歌ふ。

斯く歌ふ折しも、リズムの合はぬ、トンチンカンなる音楽響き来り、忽ちあたりは昼の如く明るくなりける。然しながら約十間四方は室内に灯をとぼしたる如くなれども、其他は依然として闇の襖を立てたるが如し。

§ 2

From the darkness, four beauties appeared in an iuiu manner. Each was a meurei of seventeen or eighteen years, with a yōshiyoku that was tanrei and a monogoshi that was graceful; rubbing their hands, which were like zaugezaiku, they approached with coquettish gestures. The first woman bowed respectfully toward the itsukau, and with a bishō, she sang:

Satsuki: 'I am the Kunitsukami of the village of Yoshii, the daughter of Yoshii, Satsuki. Since the Kuwaenzan collapsed, my home has sunk to the bottom of the lake. At last, saved by the grace of the Kami, I have been avoiding disaster here on Tsukimigaoka. The three otome here are my otodoi. Please have mercy upon us, O Kunitsukami.'

Haruo, while feeling suspicious, sang:

Haruo: 'It is a fushigi thing you proclaim, that you are hiding here on this hill to avoid disaster. Though your mimekatachi is beautiful, somehow, your expression does not sit well with me.'

Satsuki sang:

Satsuki: 'Do not doubt me, for I am a Kunitsukami, and there is no mistake that I am the daughter of Yoshii. Hearing beforehand that you were coming, I have been waiting here, illuminating the Tokoyami.'

Haruo, still feeling suspicious, sang:

Haruo: 'No matter how beautifully you proclaim the Kototama, your appearance is suspicious.'

Sayuri sang:

Sayuri: 'I am the younger sister who names myself Sayuri. Please show us favor, traveler. When I think of the heart of the traveler who doubts my sister, I become sad. I am the beloved daughter who names myself Sayuri, once called the flower of the village of Yoshii. In any case, because of my longing, I now bow down before your figure. We have no home and no parents; please save our pitiful otodoi.'

Mizune sang:

Mizune: 'Young Lord, do not lend your ears to the Kototama of the song; his ears are moving. This woman is an incarnation of the Azakeri-baba; be careful, for she is a flower of darkness. It is suspicious that this area alone shines brightly in a dark night without even a lamp. Since the schemes of the Magatsukami are shallow, their tails will be exposed in an instant. I do not know if my eyes are deceived, but I see a thick tail on Satsuki. The otome who names herself the younger sister Sayuri also has a thin tail appearing.'

As they sang this, the surroundings, which had been as bright as day, suddenly changed into Tokoyami, and unpleasant voices were heard incessantly.

Azakeri-baba: 'Gyahahahaha! Indeed, this one is the end result of the Azakeri-baba. It was my plan to torment and kill your younger brother, the greenhorn named Akio, throw him into the crater of Kuwaenzan, and take his life. Now that it has come to this, I shall tell you everything. The tail seen from the buttocks is the hair of your younger brother Akio; the tail tucked into the buttocks of the woman who names herself Sayuri is the hair of your brother. Ihihihihi! How pleasant it feels, ah! The tails of the two women are done, but since two more tails are needed, we appeared here now to take the lives of you two brothers, make them the tails of the two otome, and use the myōjutsu of freedom at will—that is our plan. How adorable it is! Ihihihihi! The Warai-baba, the Azakeri-baba, the Gyaku-baba, and the Naki-baba—the four of us disguised ourselves to make us appear as beautiful otome to your eyes. How pitiful! Your two eyes are no longer of this world; you have departed for the Yūmeikai. Though they look like human eyes from the outside, they are now no better than knot-holes that serve no purpose. How miserable! You are great fools who did not notice that you were caught in my scheme. Last night, in the shade of the forest of Shigeki, we gouged out the eyes of you four and replaced them with tree knot-holes—our divine mystery of change. Are you surprised? Have you met your end? Ihihihihi! Kiyahahahaha! Kiki-ki, how pitiful, how delightful!'

Haruo, Natsuo, and the others, Mizune and Sene, were startled and each reached for their own eyes to examine them, but they were not knot-holes at all; they were entirely their own eyes. As if relieved, the four of them, unexpectedly and together with their followers, proclaimed the Heavenly Number Song in a great voice that shook heaven and earth. The Magatsukami, who had transformed into the four otome, seemed to be overwhelmed by this Kototama and fled in all directions while letting out strange screams.

It was a fushigi; the curtain of darkness over Tsukimigaoka was suddenly opened, and the moon in the vast sky shone brightly. As the itsukau stood on the hill and looked toward the southeast, a newly born lake of fire spread without limit, the waves were calm, a cool breeze blew, and it was silent, reflecting the shadows of the moon and stars.

春男、夏男はじめ水音、瀬音は驚き、各自両眼に手をやりて調べ見れど、別に節穴にもあらず、全く自分の眼なるに、やつと安心せしものの如く、四人は期せずして天の数歌を従者と共に、天地も轟くばかり大音声に宣り上げたり。四人の乙女と変じたる曲鬼は、この言霊に辟易しけむ、怪しき悲鳴をあげながら、いづれともなく逃げ去りける。

不思議や、月見ケ丘は、闇の幕俄に開かれ、大空の月は皎々とかがやき渡りけるにぞ、一行は丘の上に立ちて、東南方を眺むれば、新たに生れたる火の湖、際限もなく展開し、波静かに涼風いたり、月星の影を浮べて寂然たりけり。
