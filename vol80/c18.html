Chapter 18: Great 挙出発 [2022]

大挙出発〔2022〕

§ 1

Iwagakene, who served as the Shitsusei of the Kamuyakata on Minakamiyama, had sent his fourth son, Fuyuo, alone to inspect the Kunigata west of Takamitsuyama; however, as many months passed without any Shōsoku, he began to feel a sense of Fuan. Together with the Jiyūshin, Minaoto and Seoto, he held a Kiushiyubōgi, and as a Ketsukuwa, he ordered his third son, Akio, to depart, accompanied by four attendants—Matsu, Take, Ume, and Sakura—to both ascertain the Arika of Fuyuo and inspect the Kunigata. Yet, like an arrow released from a Yuzuru, he too departed and sent no Shōsoku. Once again, the Kamuyakata of Minakamiyama sank into sorrow, and just as they were in the midst of a Kotohaka to send a Miko for the third time to conduct a Chōsa, the Kaenzan, which illuminated the earth for a hundred ri at night with its flames, erupted with a thunderous roar. The ground beneath the Kamuyakata of Minakamiyama shook violently, and the hearts of the people were filled with dread.

Here, Iwagakene and the Jiyūshin, worried for the safety of the two Miko, decided to set out in a great force to search for their Shōsoku. They determined to head out for the third Chōsa, leading a large number of attendants including Haruo, Natsuo, Minaoto, Seoto, and others.

Facing their departure, Iwagakene became the Saishu, and the others followed behind him. They prostrated themselves before the shrine where the Amatsukami were enshrined, offered various delicious foods, and recited the Futo-norito-goto. The Norito said:

Iwagakene: 'Hanging the words in awe, before the three great presences of the Lord God who dwells in the Takahi no Miya, the God of Takahoko, and the God of Kamihoko, I, Iwagakene, Shitsusei of the Kamuyakata of Minakamiyama, here humbly and respectfully, with fear and trembling, offer this petition. Verily, this divine land of Ashihara is a divine land governed by the eternal presence of the Mikoshi no Kami, Amagiri-hime no Kami. We, though lowly, have taken Takamitsuyama as our boundary, and under the divine command of the Mikoshi no Kami to govern the Kunihara of Yosan, we have devoted our hearts and bodies, day and night, to serve in the administration of the country, praying for its peace. The land of Yosan is still young, and as various things are not yet in order and it remains like floating fat, I sent my fourth Miko, Fuyuo, to inspect the Kunigata. Although many months have passed, he has not yet returned to report. Fearing that perhaps he did not fulfill the will of the Lord God, or that there exists some Magatsu-kami along the way that has harmed him, I have consulted with the various officials, and after praying before the gods, I sent my third son, Akio, with the four attendants Matsu, Take, Ume, and Sakura, to once again inspect the Kunigata and clarify the Arika of Fuyuo. They departed from this hall on a past day, but even now, no report has been made, and the officials are troubled. Furthermore, the Kaenzan, which stands in the Mahoyuraba of the land of Yosan, has shaken heaven and earth, and it seems to have shattered from its peak to its base; the shadow we gazed upon morning and evening is no longer visible, and its light has vanished. Believing this to be no ordinary event, we have held a Kotohaka here for the third time. To search for the Arika of the two Miko and to inspect the Kunigata, we are sending Haruo and Natsuo, accompanied by the officials Minaoto and Seoto, leading a hundred gods, to travel for the sake of the divine land on this auspicious day and at this auspicious hour. I beseech thee, Lord God, to listen with compassion and in detail. May this departure be free from obstacles along the way, free from loss, and free from trouble, and may they return to report with all speed. We offer these various offerings of sea, river, mountain, and field, piled high like a mountain on a hundred tables. Please listen to this with peace and tranquility, as we bow our heads to the ground and prostrate ourselves, offering this petition with fear and trembling. Himukami-reikō-sai-base, Himukami-reikō-sai-base.'

Having finished the Sōjō, they bowed respectfully, descended from the shrine, and gathered once again at the Shitsusei-den to celebrate the departure and hold a Kotohaka.

Iwagakene: 'The two Miko have not returned. / Did they fail to fulfill the divine will? / Thinking of the whereabouts of the two children / Upon whom I relied as the strength of my aged body, my heart is troubled. / I hear that the Mizuhashirakusa / Grows along the path and harms people. / It is ominous to hear that the Mizuhashira-oni, / Who perished by striking the Mizuhashirakusa, causes calamity. / Though a year has passed, Fuyuo has not returned, / And my heart is filled with anxiety. / In the past autumn, I entrusted Akio once again / And sent him on a journey to inspect the Kunigata. / Winter draws near and the withered leaves blow, but my Miko / Sends no word, which is a source of sorrow. / Though accompanied by the four, Matsu, Take, Ume, and Sakura, / It is suspicious that there is no word even now. / Has the Kaenzan erupted, for heaven and earth / Have rumbled and the shadow of the mountain has vanished? / Seeing the changes in the Kaenzan, / My soul is troubled all the more.'

How could I disobey the Mikotoba of Ihane-dono given last night, and not hasten on this journey? This mansion is unsettling, for there has been no Kaherigoto from the messengers sent before. I shall go forth, revering the Mikoto of my Chichiuhe, accompanied by a hundred officials. The Kuwaenzan has vanished without a trace, and the Kunigata of Yosa must be in turmoil. In any case, the mansion of Minakami holds the duty of governing the Kunigata of Yosa. To repay the divine words of the Mihishiro-no-Kami, I shall overcome any suffering and advance. Has not my younger brother had his Inochi stolen by the Suihonki or the Magatsu-kami? Even if my brother's Inochi is no more, I shall advance to the mountain of Takamitsu. Natsuo sings:

It is a journey of this Kefu, searching for the whereabouts of my brother while observing the Kunigata of the Yoshihara. The Obana on the peaks above the mountain of Minakami seem to lament my departure as they sway. The mountain bush clover and the bellflowers have scattered, and the Obana sway in the wind, looking lonely. The sound of insects has grown faint, and the wind blowing along the field path has gradually turned cold. Come then, protected by the grace of Kami, let us depart from this mansion.

Ihane sings with tears in his eyes:

How valiant is the departure of Haruo and Natsuo; I am joyful, thinking it to be the strengthening of the land. Minaoto and Seoto, you officials, protect the two, Haruo and Natsuo, and go in peace. You are heroic, advancing through the winter sky where the cold wind of the withered trees blows. Do not let your hearts dwell on the mansion of Minakami; advance, you officials.

Minaoto, with a clouded voice:

Come then, I shall part from you for the sake of the land, heading toward the wilderness where the Magatsu-kami rage.

Seoto sings:

Do not let your heart be troubled, for we are brave, and our path shall be fortunate. As we go forth to repay the divine words of the Mihishiro-no-Kami, how could the Magatsu-kami stand in our way?

Having finished singing, the four travelers, together with many attendants, took their course to the southeast along the field path where the winter wind blew fiercely, and went forth advancing with courage.

Haruo, while parting the Yoshihara where the winter wind blew fiercely to the right and left, occasionally looked back at the mansion of the mountain of Minakami and sang a marching song:

Autumn has gradually come to an end, and it has become the beginning of winter. The mountain of Minakami stands firm, shining behind where I go. I know not how my beloved father fares, worrying and fretting over our path. Does he pray before the great Amatsu-Mikami? How noble he is. Autumn has finally ended, and I welcome the cold winter day with the wind of withered trees; how lonely it is to advance. Thinking of the fate of Akio and Fuyuo, I feel even more lonely. The mountain of fire that towered and shone in the heavens has no shadow, and the great firelight that illuminated the midnight has now completely vanished. I, walking the field path where Yoshigusa and Shikogusa grow thick, do not even loathe the dew.

I go forth with a feeling of resignation. Ah, Kannagara-kami-kami, may there be fortune in my departure, and may there be light on the path I tread. No matter how the demons may rage, or how the poisonous insects may swarm, I shall make the light of Kami my strength and advance without slackening or yielding. Revering the Mikotoba of my father, I go forth to observe the Kunigata. Though the mountain of Takamitsu be steep and the land of Yoshihara be wide, if I advance day by day, it shall be very peaceful, Kannagara. I shall advance according to the will of Kami. We are the children of Kami, the palace of Kami. How should we fear? With the single-mindedness of a brave heart, I shall fulfill my original intention and report to the great presence at the end of this journey.

Natsuo sings:

Akio, Fuyuo, where are you now? Today, after more than a year has passed, without a single word of news, the path I seek is dim and vague. Oh, the loneliness of having no place to settle. Minaoto sings:

When winter comes, even the Minaoto of the Yamakaha hush their voices. The surroundings are lonely, leaves are falling, and bare trees tremble in many places. The moon in the sky is white, and on this winter night that seems to freeze, I walk through the frost. The withered field is eerie. The mountain of flames has vanished, and in this night path where I cannot distinguish one thing from another, I can no longer advance even a single step. Fortunately, in the shade of this forest, I shall take shelter for the dew of one night. With the light of the Amatsubi that rises in glory as my strength, I shall advance. This wilderness, where wild beasts and poisonous snakes abound, is especially dangerous. Oh, you people, how is your heart? I pray that you will declare your answer.

Seoto sings:

Leaving the mountains at the head of the water, I have reached the twilight in the forest of dense trees in the winter field. Since the light of the mountain of flames is gone, I have no choice but to spend the night in the shade of this forest. It is an evening that feels restless for some reason. What kind of Magatsuhi is coming to attack? I am searching for the two divine children as I advance. Today has been a sad journey. When I hear the sound of the winter wind blowing across, the heart of winter feels lonely. The moon boat trembles in the sky, and the sound of insects chirps at the roots of the grass in the winter twilight. Lonely is the travel garment of a winter evening; it is the rain of tears falling upon my sleeves.

Here, the party stopped in the shade of the thicket, gathered themselves together while being blown by the lonely winter wind, and while worrying in their hearts about the departure for tomorrow, they finally went to sleep.

As this area was a place where many creatures wounded by the collapse of the mountain of flames had gathered, throughout the night, unpleasant groans and a strange, disagreeable air drifted about.

瀬音は歌ふ。

『水上の山を立ち出で冬の野の 繁樹の森にたそがれにけり。 火炎山光りなければ止むを得じ 此森かげに一夜を明さむ。 何となくうら騒がしき夕なり 如何なる曲津の襲ひ来るにや。 二柱御子を探ねて進み行く 今日は悲しき旅なりにけり。 木枯の吹き渡り行く音聞けば 冬の心の淋しかりけり。 月舟は御空にふるひ虫の音は 草の根に鳴く冬の夕暮。 淋しきは冬の夕の旅衣 袖に降り来る涙の雨なり』

茲に一行は茂みの森蔭に立ち寄り、淋しき木枯に吹かれながら身を一所に集め、明日の旅立ちの事など心の中に思ひ悩みながら、漸く寝に就きける。
