Chapter 11: Fire 炎 Mountain [2015]

火炎山〔2015〕

§ 1

Akio and his party, parting the fields where poisonous grasses grew thick to the right and left, pressed forward day and night, and at twilight on the third day, they finally reached the foot of Kuwaenzan.

Kuwaenzan is a great volcano famous by reputation; at night, its great firelight extends for a hundred ri, and at times it rains down burning stones, causing great harm to humans and beasts alike.

The Kunitsukamitachi of Yoshihara fear it, calling it by the name Jigokuyama.

This mountain is the habitat of all manner of fierce beasts and poisonous snakes, and it is the main base of the hag Keshiribaba.

Warai-baba, who had suffered an unexpected defeat at Shinobugaoka, had also fled here and, entrusting herself to the mansion of Keshiribaba, was healing the wounds of her Reishin.

Akio, thinking to take the fire from the volcano and, riding the wind, burn away the Yoshigusa and Suihonsau that grew thick in the land, and thereby burn up all the fierce beasts and poisonous snakes, had come forward to this place for the time being.

Though it was twilight, the firelight erupting from the Sanchiyō made it as bright as day, and even the shadows of insects lurking at the roots of the grass could be seen clearly.

Akio, seeing the solemn state of the eruption, sat down upon the grass and sang:

Akio: 'By the light of the flames spewed forth by Kuwaenzan, there is no night in this vicinity. Stitching through the space where black smoke rises in thick clouds, the tongue of crimson lotus flames strikes toward the heavens. I shall take the fire-seed of this mountain and burn away the ugly weeds of the land of Yoshihara. How shall I climb this mountain, where so many fierce beasts and poisonous snakes dwell, up to the crater? Oh, flames of fire that strike the heavens until the light of the moon in the great sky fades. Thinking that this is the habitat of Keshiribaba, my liver turns cold, yet I shall harden my heart and climb the mountain. If I possess but one seed of fire, it will be easy to cultivate the land of Yoshihara. Tired from the journey of many days, my legs have become unable to move; I shall rest for a while.'

Matsu sang:

Matsu: 'The volcanic smoke of the mountain of fire, heard of by reputation, I think it reaches the heavenly sky. Seeing the crimson lotus tongue within the black smoke, how terrifying is Kuwaenzan. No matter how many subordinates Keshiribaba may have, I shall burn them to extinction with a single seed of fire. Thinking to avenge the grudge of Fuyuotokogimi by burning the habitat of the demon hag, I feel courageous. The voices of insects in the autumn fields come clearly, even to this mountain of hell. Though the moon of the eternal sky cannot be seen, this land is brighter than in the day. Thinking that Keshiribaba dwells in the rocky caves of this high mountain, she is a terrifying creature. If there is anything that is terrifying, foul-hearted, and persistent, there can be nothing that surpasses that hag. Even hearing the name hag, somehow a loathsome feeling has welled up within me. Especially, even hearing the twisted words of Keshiribaba makes my chest feel ill.'

Take sang:

Take: 'When I gaze upon the flames spewed forth by Kuwaenzan, I feel courageous and my heart flutters. If I climb up to the summit and take the seed of fire and return, the land shall be settled. How could I return without reaching the summit of Kuwaenzan? Even if tens of thousands of Suihonki come in a swarm, I shall drive them away with the living Kototama. My brave warrior heart burns, just like the flames of Kuwaenzan. The ugly master of this mountain, Keshiribaba, must be groaning in pain from her wounds. The Keshirigoto of the demon hag, whose Kototama is deeply muddied, has defiled my ears. Having crossed the endless Ōnogahara, today I rest at Kuwaenzan. The blowing wind is lukewarm and feels unpleasant; are the swarms of Suihonki watching us? Even if the Suihonki gather like aphids, I, a man of valor, will not move a single bit. With the point of my brave warrior heart, I shall try to pierce and strike down the demons that approach. My spirit is more valiant than even the flames that courageously strike the heavens. How interesting, ah, how courageous; from now on, I shall strike at the habitat of Keshiribaba.'

Ume sang:

Ume: 'Having departed from the mountain above the water and traveled day after day, I have finally come to Kuwaenzan. When I see the volcanic smoke of Kuwaenzan, my spirit flourishes as if ascending to the heavens. The scenery of the fields, bright as day, is a gift of the firelight of Kuwaenzan. Yet when night falls, by the lamps of Kuwaenzan, darkness shall not come; it is good for battle. The red tongue of fire from within the black smoke is leaning toward the wind blowing from the north.'

Sakura sings:

'In this mountain, winter shall not be, for even the wind at the foot is warm. The foot of Mount Kuwaen is fearsome, overgrown to the point where trees and grasses are indistinguishable. I think I shall pick up a spark from the Itadaki and burn these Susono. It is an interesting evening; the earth rumbles, the Kigusa resound, and the mountain spits fire. As far as the Meji reaches, the Hirono of Suihonsau and Yoshigusa appear to be bright throughout.'

Just as she sings this, a strange voice comes from nowhere:

'Ahahahaha, Ihihihihi, Ufufufu, Ehehehe, Ohohoho, how interesting. Ohohoho, how interesting.

Is this Akio, the elder brother of Fuyuo, who perished by my Keiriyaku? I am the Suihonki, the laughing hag who has long dwelt in Shinobugaoka. How well you have wandered here to your doom. Now is the time to strike; I shall avenge the grudge of Fuyuo upon his brother Akio. Hey, hey, my Teshita, bind these Gonin of Gaki one by one and throw them into the Kuwakō of Mount Kuwaen. Ahahahaha, what a pleasant Kokochi.'

So resounds the high voice, sounding so Nikuniku. Akio, forgetting the fatigue of his feet at this voice, stands up straight:

'Now I understand the Shōsoku of my younger brother. Was he deceived by the laughing hag and died? I am a Mononofu of Chin; how could I not avenge my brother's enemy? I knew of the Sumika of the laughing hag, the Suihonki, but I did not think I would meet her here. I shall ask the laughing hag once more: where is the Sumika of the mocking hag?'

'Ahahahaha, Ihihihihi.

Do not say such Baka things. The mocking hag is this one's younger sister; can you not see that she is standing here right now, you blind fools? Now that it has come to this, you are Dōzen to fish caught in a net. We shall call together our Teshita and torture you to death as we please; how truly pitiful you are.

Ihihihihi, Ohohohoho.'

Matsu reaches the height of anger, and with a rough voice says:

'Interesting, the laughing hag and the mocking hag. I shall destroy you both in a single strike. I hear my enemies are gathered here; tonight's battle will require no extra time or effort.'

Take sings:

'No matter how the Suihonki may swarm and attack, I shall pierce and destroy them with a fierce heart.'

Just as they say this, strangely, the eruption of Mount Kuwaen stops abruptly. The surroundings become pitch black, and Akio's party, finding themselves in a dilemma, sit heavily on the ground, fold their hands, and remain lost in thought for a while.

From within the darkness, the outline of the laughing hag's face appears clearly, and while sticking out her long tongue, she approaches close to the front of Akio:

'Ahahahaha, Ihihihihi.

Now that it has come to this, you are in my hands. Prepare yourselves and drink this poisoned tea; come, eat with joy.'

Saying this, she pours the poisoned tea from a large jar onto Akio's face.

Akio, unable to bear it, covers his face with both hands and in his heart says:

'One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten.

Hundred, thousand, ten thousand, ten million gods,

Protect me, save me.'

As he prays, the face of the laughing hag vanishes instantly, and only a disgusting laughter can be heard from far away.

From behind comes the voice of the mocking hag:

'Gyahahahaha, Gyufufufu.

Akio and his party of spineless cowards, have you learned your lesson? This is the skill of the mocking hag. Now that it has come to this, no matter how much you struggle or cry, you shall not escape. How truly satisfying this is. Gyufufufu, this hag is the most powerful among the Suihonki. And what is this? To think that you, in the body of small humans, would attempt to conquer the mocking hag is laughable. Now that it has come to this, I am free to do as I please; what truly pathetic spineless cowards you are.'

Akio, filled with unbearable regret, clenches his fists and leaps toward the direction of the voice, risking his life, but at that moment, he falls headlong into a pit in the darkness.

Kikiri-baba: 'Gyahahahaha, how delightful, how truly delightful! That rascal Akio has fallen into my trap and lost his life so easily. The mountain of Kuwaen once again spewed forth Funen, and the Kuwakuō soared into the Ten, creating a truly Monosugo Kuōkei.

Akio and his Itsukau fell into the dark pit and Haka lost their lives of Utsusomi, but their Seirei, being Furaufushi, were resurrected there, and the five of them bowed their heads together, resenting having been caught in the treacherous scheme of the old woman.

『思はざる不覚を取りて主従は あたら生命を失ひにけり。 さりながら吾精霊はかくの如 生きてありせば恐るるに足らず。 どこ迄も婆アの生命取らざれば 大丈夫吾等の意気地は立たず。 八千尋の深き穴底に落されて 生命亡せしと思へば恨めし。 この恨みやがてはらさむ笑ひ婆 譏り婆アの首引きぬきて』

斯く主従五人は、今更の如くあたら生命を奪はれたるを怒り且つ歎き、仇を報ずべきを語り合ひつつ、千尋の深き穴の底に佇んで居る。

時こそあれ、いづくともなく、いやらしき笑ひ婆の笑ひ声、譏り婆の破鐘の声、物凄く響き来る。
