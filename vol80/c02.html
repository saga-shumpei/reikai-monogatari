Chapter 2: 行倒 [2006]

行倒〔2006〕

§ 1

Over the Noji path where suihonsau grew in thick profusion, Fuyuo wandered far and wide; striking against a broad okabe, he climbed it intending to rest his breath. Just at that time the sky turned to tasoga, and a vaguely, softly warm wind disgustingly licked about his shihen as it blew past. As he happened to stop by a certain waraya upon this hill, a shiraga old woman emerged from within, brimming with a smile, and stood near the door of the gate. Fuyuo raised a tired voice and said, 'I am a traveler on a journey. Please grant me a single night's lodging from the dew.' When he so spoke, the old woman smiled broadly and said, 'I am an old woman called "Warahi." Come, come, please stay the night. Ahahaha-ha-ha, ihihihi-hi.'

'Ufufufu-fu-fu, ehehehe-he, ohohoho-ho-ho, how omoshiro, how charming!' she cried, tumbling down and lying prostrate. Without even casting an eye upon the strange old woman, Fuyuo was praying to Kami; at that moment, the old woman abruptly sat up and said, 'Mitariotome await in the back; please proceed quickly!' No sooner had she spoken than Fuyuo dragged his fatigued feet into the back room; as he advanced, fushigi indeed, mitari maidens, deceiving even gems by their beauty, gazed upon Fuyuo's face, arraying sweet kotoba before him, and argued among themselves, 'Now that you have come here, we shall not let you return to Mototsumikuni!' Thus each shot her soya of love. Fuyuo's shintai was utterly exhausted, and he fell asleep knowing neither zengo.

The miotome of Yama, Kawa, and Umi stroked and rubbed Fuyuo's zenshin, spending the night in tefutefunannan, until the single night's dream awoke. Fuyuo, rubbing his eyes, arose from his fushido and looked around; the mitari otomera were smiling cheerfully. Fuyuo, while marvelling at this spectacle, asked:

Fuyuo: 'Exhausted by my journey, I requested a single night's lodging, yet the surrounding air somehow bears an uninviting presence that does not sit well with my heart. The old woman who seemed to be the mistress of this place laughed incessantly. In contrast to her, three alluring otomera repeat sweet words at my side and press themselves upon me—what is the meaning of this? Having received the divine word at the sacred sanctuary of Mount Takamitsu, I am about to advance; I must leave this place as quickly as possible, even for a single moment, and proceed according to my mission.'

When he spoke thus, the mitari otomera shook their heads from side to side and said:

Miotome: 'Today, when you, for whom we have longed day after day, have finally arrived, how could we easily let you return? First of all, please drink some tea. The tea we offer is a wondrous medicine of eternal youth and immortality. If you drink this hot water, instantly your heart will become refreshed, and your fatigue will be cleanly cured.'

Saying thus, the elder daughter Yama drew hot water into a wooden bowl and presented it before Fuyuo; Fuyuo, whose throat was parched from the day's fatigue, had no time to think of anything, and gulped it down as if greedily devouring it. Its taste was somehow fragrant, yet it possessed a foul odor. Startled for a moment, thinking, 'Could this perchance be tea made from the leaves of suihonsau?', he nevertheless assumed a nonchalant air, worshipped the heavens and worshipped the earth, and with the utmost strength of his voice, chanted the vivid Kototama:

'Hito, futa, mi, yo, itsu, muyu, nana, ya, kokonotsu, tari, momo, chi, yoru. May the myriads of Kami save me!'

The moment he proclaimed it, instantly the house, the laughing old woman, and the mitari daughters vanished without a trace into smoke, leaving only a hill where white birch trees grew sparsely amidst sprouting weeds.

Fuyuo was astonished anew, and while singing the Heavenly Number Song of the vivid Kototama, he once again pressed forward through the damp plain where reeds and suihonsau grew densely to the point of leaving no space, relying on his leather garments. Fuyuo sang along the way: 'Ah, how suspicious, how suspicious! As the day reached tasoga, I finally Reached a single hill, And standing before the front Of a mere semblance of a dilapidated house, I inquired; From within, a shiraga laughing old woman Appeared and granted me A single night's lodging. Seeking to rest from the fatigue of travel, I advanced toward the inner room, Where three strange otomera Wooed me in various ways; Unknowingly I fell asleep, Yet at the unpleasantness of the surrounding air, Just as I was feeling suspicious, the night dawned, And the voices of birds were heard nearby. When I drank the tea offered by the maidens, My condition became increasingly ill; Fearing it might perchance be poisonous water, I worshipped the sky and worshipped the earth,'

§ 2

When the Kazūta of Ama was chanted, the old woman, the daughter, and the house itself turned into smoke and vanished. Gazing intently at the aftermath, I was startled to realize it was a forest of Shirakaba where Zatsusau grew. Fleeing down from Shinobigaoka, I headed east once more. On the long journey, my throat grew parched, and though I wished to drink, the pond water and river water, tainted with the Dokuki of Suihonsau, offered no way for us to partake. Lamenting, I proceeded along the long Noji; there was no Semusube to be found. My head ached, my legs grew weary, and my Seiriyoku faded suddenly; it is sorrowful to see the light in my eyes dim one by one. Was the woman of Yazen truly not a Suihonki? The more I think, the more suspicious it becomes.

As he sang this while moving forward, he saw another small hill lying across his path. Fuyuo, one way or another, reached that hill, hoping that if there were water, he might moisten his throat and rest his breath. Summoning his Yūki into his exhausted Karatama, he arrived at the edge of the small hill around the time of Tasogare that day.

Fuyuo sang in a faint voice:

Crossing the field where Shikogusa grows, gasping and panting, I have finally reached this hill. If there were Mashimizu on this hill, I would moisten my parched throat and be revived. Even if Mashimizu were to spring forth, there is no way to search for it on the Noji without a path in the Tasogare. Having been made to drink Dokuchiya by a mysterious woman, this evening is more painful than death for me. Kokukoku, my Karatama swells up, and now I have reached the point where I cannot Miugo. Alone in the dark Arano leading to Tokoyo, I am writhing in sorrow. Thinking of my Furusato, I feel longing; thinking of my Chichihaha, I feel the sorrow of a journey at dusk. The loneliness of me, traveling alone, is like the feeling of living in the depths of the mountains. If the life of my Tama no o should end, what shall I do? There is no way for my Furusato to know. Saved by the power of the majesty of Kototama, I have barely escaped with my life. Coming here, I feel lonely, thinking that my dew-like life might vanish. Having slept one night on the hill where Suihonki gather, I have shortened my life of Tama.

Just as he was singing this,

Ahahahaha! Ihihihihi! Ufufufufu! Ehehehehe!

I am the laughing hag of Shinobigaoka. To think you have managed to flee all the way here. I am the laughing hag of the land of Yomi, who laughs at the suffering of men. Your state, with both body and soul exhausted, is truly amusing to behold. I am the laughing hag, who envies the sincerity of the people of the world morning and night. You must have seen the three daughters; those are the women who perished by the Dokuchiya. At this final moment, when the life of your Tama no o is about to end, promise to become my husband. Though I may look foul, my soul shines like a jewel. If you consent to my words, from now on, I shall let your life of Tama live in peace.

She appeared suddenly in the darkness while raising a repulsive voice. Fuyuo, who was on the verge of death, gritted his teeth in frustration at having fallen into the hag's trap, and sang with his dying breath:

Even if my life should end, would I ever submit to a foul hag like you? Even if my Karatama should perish, my soul shall live on to torment you. Born in the noble mansion above the water, I am a true Kunitsukami. You are the ghost hag of the Suihonki, known by reputation; leave this place at once.

The hag opened her pale blue mouth, which was split to her ears, and while sticking out a tongue like that of an ox, she said:

Gahahahatsuha, gihihihihi, gufufufufutsuhu, gehehehehe, giyohohohohotsuho, what pathetic cowards you are. You are mere Utsusori who fell for my schemes and sold off your precious, precious life of Tama cheaply. If you dislike it, then dislike it; I shall not ask again. Giyahahahaha! I will drag you back to Shinobigaoka, call together my entire clan, and after you are gone, I will take your soul's life once more and satisfy my grudge. Giyahahahaha! How delightful this is.

Fuyuo, gritting his teeth in deep regret, sang:

Even if my life should end, I am not one to succumb to a demon like you. I, too, shall be reborn as a demon and take your lives to torment you. I am the son of the King of Mount Mizukami, which excels and surpasses the land of Ashihara. I am not the end of a lowly demon like you; I possess the power of majesty.

Singing thus, he gasped his last and collapsed on the spot, passing away.
