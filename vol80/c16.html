Chapter 16: Fire の Lake [2020]

火の湖〔2020〕

§ 1

Akio and the group of five, including Matsu, Take, Ume, and Sakura, were seized by the masters of the wild beasts and thrown into the great volcanic crater of Kuwaenzan. Their bodies turned to Hakukotsu, ascended high into the Chiukū, and fell back upon the Sanjiyō. After about Hantoki had passed, Kuwaenzan suddenly began a Daimeidō, shaking in Zengo, Saiu, and Jiyōge, and finally, it erupted in a Daibakuhatsu. As one watched, the once-high Yamakage vanished without an Atokata, transforming into a Daikosui. The majority of the wild beasts, Dokujiya, and Suihonki met the calamity of Zenmetsu, leaving only a small Kojima in the Chiuau. The Seirei saved on this small island were Akio and his group, Asagiri, Yūgiri, Akikaze, Nowaki, Akisame, and a small portion of the remaining Suihonki, wild beasts, and Dokujiya.

Here, Akio became the master of the Seirei world on this island, but he struggled in every way, enduring hardships from all directions, to annihilate the remaining wild beasts and Dokujiya that still possessed physical bodies, wishing to create a safe zone for the Seirei. However, as Akio was now a Seirei, he lacked the power to oppose the wild beasts and Dokujiya that held physical bodies, and he had no choice but to pray to the heavens and the gods, awaiting the descent of a savior god.

Now, Asagirihime-no-kami, Ōmiteru-no-kami, Asasorao-no-kami, Kunio-no-kami, and Kokorohime-no-kami, who had descended upon Takamitsuyama, gathered on the throne of the rocky cave at the summit. Gazing out toward the far west, they heard a great explosion and saw that the flames of Kuwaenzan, which had once pierced the heavens, had vanished without an Atokata, leaving only black clouds spreading across the sky. Grieving that a natural disaster had occurred in a part of the Ashihara-no-nakatsukuni, they held a council. Asagirihime-no-kami recited a poem:

Asagirihime-no-kami: 'Looking out, the mountain of fire has vanished, shaking the heavens and earth. The tongue of fire that licked the vast sky is now completely invisible. The sacred fire of grace that turned the Magatsukami of the Ashihara-no-nakatsukuni toward the truth has vanished. How shall we govern this Ashihara now that the divine treasure of fire has been extinguished? In any case, the mountain of fire has disappeared; it has become a lake, and my heart is filled with unease.'

Ōmiteru-no-kami: 'I, too, feel my heart clouded when I think of the eruption of the mountain of fire. The fire has been extinguished amidst the process of burning away the reeds and the water-running grasses. Since the night has fallen upon the islands of Ashihara, it was bright before, but now there is nothing to be done. Thick black clouds cover the heavens, and the mountain of fire is no longer visible. Because it was a mountain where many Magatsukami dwelt, I hesitated in the task of taking the sacred fire. While I hesitated, the sacred fire was extinguished; how shall I govern this land? From today, though the sacred fire is extinguished, govern the world by illuminating the water and fire with the Kototama.'

Ohishiro-no-kami: 'Since you are indeed Ōmiteru-no-kami, illuminate the darkness with the living Kototama. Although the greater part of the Magatsumi perished in the upheaval of heaven and earth, it is a pity that the fire is gone.'

Ōmiteru-no-kami: 'Revering the words of my Lord, from today I shall descend to the valley and perform Misogi. I shall exert my utmost efforts until I satisfy the heart of the Misogi-no-kami.'

Ohishiro-no-kami: 'Hearing your poem, my soul is revived, and I feel as if the heavens and earth have opened.'

Asasorao-no-kami: 'From this high mountain in the land of Ashihara, when I look at the shape of the country, the mountain of fire is steep. The steep mountain of fire has suddenly become a lake, and its form is no longer visible. This mountain, where many Magatsukami dwelt, has vanished, perhaps by the divine Keirin. Be that as it may, the land of Yosan must be in turmoil; if you permit me, I shall go forth. Adding strength to the god of the rocky roots, I shall revive the land of Yosan.'

The two Kami, singing as they traversed the Kūchiu, descended quietly through the Yahekumo over the Kuni of Yosa, and the Torifune landed Yasuyasu upon the Heichi of Oshino-ga-oka, where the laughing hag had once dwelt.

Although the land for about Hiyakuyori around the Kuwaenzan had been transformed into a Daikosui, Oshino-ga-oka was fortunately placed outside its Kenguwai, and the Kosui had spread to within nearly one Ichiri. The two Kami descended upon this hill and, facing the Shinrei of Tenchi, offered up the Kototama of Kanshiya and sang the Kazūta.

Asazorao-no-Kami sings:

Hisakata no, the morning sky, Ō-ly I have crossed, riding upon the Torifune. Parting the clouds, I look down upon Oshino-ga-oka, an unexpected Yasudo it was. Since the new Kuwaen-no-Kosui is Majika, it is visible clearly from this hill.

Kunioo-no-Kami sings:

The darkness of my troubled heart has cleared away, and I have descended safely upon the hill. Looking out, the Kuwaen-no-Kosui is vast, and I see the mist rising faintly. From today, I shall build a house upon this hill and think to bring life to the Kuni of Yosa.

Just as they were singing thus, Fuyuo, the youngest child of the Kunitsukami, a spirit whose life had been stolen by the laughing hag, appeared timidly together with Kumakō, Torakō, and the spirits of the three daughters of the mountain, river, and sea, and with a faint voice, sang the sincerity of their Kanshiya toward the two Kami.

Fuyuo: 'From the Hisakata Amatsu-mikara, you have descended, two pillars of Kami-son. The Mikuni of Yosa in the Ashihara was noisy, with Magatsukami raging wildly. Due to the eruption of the Kuwaenzan, most of those Magatsukami have perished and vanished. I am Fuyuo, the youngest child of Iwagane, though now I am no longer a being of this world. I am one whose life in the manifest world was stolen, deceived by the laughing hag of the Mizuhonki. These companions who prostrate themselves before you have all become spirits. From this day, when you two pillars have descended, the spirit world shall also be at peace.'

Kuma sings:

I too am a servant who served the lord of Iwagane, though I have no manifest body. Torakō and these three maidens are all spirits; please have mercy upon us.

Asazorao-no-Kami sings:

The village where the Mizuhonki dwells, of which I have heard before, tell me in detail where it lies.

Fuyuo sings:

The hill where the laughing hag, the leader of the Mizuhonki, dwelt is this very place. Fearing our power, the laughing hag fled toward the Kuwaenzan. Since the Kuwaenzan has become a Kosui, the laughing hag must also have perished.

Kunioo-no-Kami:

I have heard a rare tale, that you have driven away the laughing hag. Though you are spirits, I am amazed at the strong power of Fuyuo's soul.

Yama sings:

I am the spirit of Fuyuo's wife; please protect us, two pillars of Kami. It is a joy to meet such noble Kami who have descended according to the Kamigoto of the Kami of the Mihishiro. If it be so, the Kuni of the Ashihara shall be at peace, in the manifest world, the spirit world, and the hidden world alike.

Kawa sings:

I am the river that became a spirit due to the schemes of the Mizuhonki laughing hag. Becoming the wife of the spirit Torakō, I have spent years on Oshino-ga-oka. With the appearance of you two pillars of noble Kami, I, a spirit, have been revived.

Umi sings:

I am the sea, the wife of Torakō's spirit, standing before the noble Kami. From today, while having mercy upon us, please subdue the Magatsukami and their kind. The messenger of the Mihishiro-Kami, whom we have awaited and awaited, has descended upon Oshino-ga-oka.

Asazorao-no-Kami sings:

Since we two Kami have descended here, be at peace in your hearts; we shall protect you forever.

Thus, singing to one another, they rested their breath that night at the mansion of Fuyuo on Oshino-ga-oka.

熊は歌ふ。
