Chapter 9: 露の路 [2013]

露の路〔2013〕

§ 1

Here, Akio and his party left Tsukimigaoka behind, and while bathing their heads in the light of the Amatsu-hi rising in Toyosaka, they walked briskly across the dew-laden fields, choosing the higher ground, winding and turning to the right and left, and escorted by the Ominaheshi, Hagi, and Kikiyō of autumn's glory, they proceeded with a most joyful air. Akio raised his voice and sang of the depths of his heart, and his progress was truly valiant.

Mats: 'Having departed from Minakamiyama, we traversed the Ōnohara, a land we had never trodden before. Avoiding Dokumushi and Dokujiya, and paying no heed to the poisonous harm of the Suihonsau, we came to the end of our path as twilight fell, and sought a lodging of dew for one night at Tsukimigaoka. Upon the hill, Momobana and Chibana bloomed and scented the air until there was no space left, and the sound of insects in the autumn evening could be heard, clear and bright. When I looked out over the vast Ōzora, the green sky was Iyataka, and the depths of the clouds were Iyafuka; in the Mannaka of this expanse, the Mushin moon shone Kaukau, illuminating all things upon the earth. We, the party, were on this hill, and while admiring the moon, listening to the insects, and praising the Iroka of the flowers, we were singing and playing when, suddenly, the whole sky clouded over. Black clouds wrapped around the four sides, swallowing the moon's light, which had been like white silver, and even the stars, until a mass of darkness fell upon the earth. As the darkness was profound, we spoke to one another, putting strength into our voices. At that very moment, a suspicious and mocking hag appeared and laughed with all her might. We then steeled our hearts, bowed down before the gods of heaven and earth, and proclaimed the living Kototama; the fierce hag, as expected, immediately furled her banners and hid herself, no one knew where. Once again, the moon shone Kaukau, washing away the clouds and appearing. As the grass and trees of Tsukimigaoka, the Momobana, the Chibana, and the shadows of the insects could be seen as if they were in our hands, we rejoiced, calling it a blessing of heaven, and sang a song of the autumn Obana, and thus that night finally dawned. We, a party of five, have pushed aside the dew of the Obana and have finally come this far, intending to advance to the mountain of flames where the mocking hag is said to dwell. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, protected by the light of the gods, to advance on the path of conquering evil—there is no greater happiness than this. Advance, advance, now advance! O Pine, Bamboo, and Plum, and you too, Cherry, be valiant in this departure.'

Mats stands at the head and sings.

Mats: 'The evil dragon rages and the damp insects dance. In the middle path where the daylily grows, we harden our bodies in garments of leather. We, a party of five, are advancing to Takamitsuyama. Even if the path ahead is far, and even if the demons try to hinder us, we shall not fear, for we are men of courage. Revealing the true power of the brave, we shall serve in the divine work entrusted by the gods, advancing with valor. The hag in the darkness who has obstructed our path has been defeated by the living Kototama. Now, from here on, while purifying the water and fire of the Kototama more and more, making the gods our true strength and the true teachings our staff, we shall advance without wavering or stopping, despite any demon's capture. Until we find the whereabouts of Fuyuotio-kimi, we shall not turn back; our high hearts are like the mulberry bow, and our Yamato-damashii will not allow us to be stopped. May the Amatsu-kami protect us. Before the presence of the Kunitsu-mikami, we purify our hearts and pray. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, may there be happiness in today's departure, and may there be light in my Kototama.'

Take sings.

Take: 'We advance to the foot of Takamitsuyama to see the shape of the land. Following Akio-kimi, we have come through the autumn fields where flowers bloom and scent the air, escorted by the sound of insects. As we advanced, the light of the daytime moon appeared white in the sky, and autumn came in its floating figure. The autumn day was short and twilight fell, and we spent one night at the sacred ground of Tsukimigaoka, which lay on our path, and saw and heard various good and evil things. The shelter under the trees was soon left behind, and today we are in our joyful travel attire, advancing on the path to conquer the demons. We shall search out the whereabouts of Fuyuotio-kimi, even by parting the grass, and report his safety to you. Even if he has been destroyed by the Magatsu and has become a person of the other world, though there is no remedy, we shall surely strike down the enemy and clear away the grudge of the lord. If the words of the mocking hag are true, that Fuyuotio-kimi was deceived by the hag and lost his life, can we remain silent? As long as we have strength, as long as the living Kototama continues, we shall strike, defeat, and cut them down. It is my heart's desire to make this land of the reed plains peaceful and tranquil, and to open it up. May the gods of heaven and earth grant this, and before their presence, I pray.'

§ 2

Ume sings:

'The scent of Obana is pure and clear, As I follow the path where it drifts, Avoiding the Dokuriu Idichi, I go to serve in your retinue. How brave is this journey of today, As autumn gradually deepens, Momokusachigusa flowers bloom, And their fragrance perfumes the four directions. Even the wings of birds traversing the sky, Bathed in Akihi, shine brilliantly, Glittering like jewels in the Misora. Ah, how brave, how brave, In the autumn fields where the wind blows clear, I, who travel this path, feel the Murakimo, My heart's steed prancing with courage, Forgetting even the weariness of my body. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, May there be happiness in this journey through the Akino, May there be power in my Ikukototama.'

Sakura sings:

'The blowing wind is clear and the sky is high, The steed is brave and neighs aloud. Setting out from Minakamiyama, Serving in your retinue, With the dew of Ichiya as my Kusamakura, Gradually the dawn begins to break here, Aiming for the mountain of Kuwaen, We ascend the path of the Akuma. Struck by the Dokuefu of the Suihonsau, The Suihonki perish, We advance through the strange Nobe, Where they lie in wait Anatakonata. We are the children of Kami, the shrines of Kami, No matter what crookedness may hinder us, The sound of insects gathering on the grass, The sound of birds chirping in the forest, The murmuring of the stream flowing in the valley, All echo as if to add strength to us, Saying advance, advance, Ah, how interesting, how joyful. Following you, the Lord Akio, To Takamitsuyama where the Mihishiro-Kami resides, We dance and ascend, Looking out over the shape of the lands in the four directions, To open this great field, We advance, the Amatsukami, We pray that you protect us, So that even the corners of the path may be without harm. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, May there be happiness in the journey I take, May there be power in my Kototama.'

As they sang thus, the party of five advanced bravely, when suddenly their thirst became unbearable; however, in the shade of a dense, lush evergreen tree, there was something like a small teahouse, where four or five young maidens were shading their eyes with their hands, beckoning the party to come.

The party of five, having been beckoned, approached the teahouse.
