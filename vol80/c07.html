Chapter 7: Moon 見ケ丘 [2011]

月見ケ丘〔2011〕

§ 1

Akio and his party of five, having finally reached the weed-grown Tsukimigaoka at dusk, rest here while gazing at the moon through the pines and singing.

Akio: 'Having crossed the Ōnohara, at last I behold the moon hanging in the pines of Tsukimigaoka.'

The Pine sings:

'The moonlight on Tsukimigaoka where the pines grow, sways in the autumn evening breeze. As the moon is clear, the sound of insects gathering in the grass leaves grows ever more vibrant as the night deepens. On this hill where the autumn moon shines quietly, it is an evening to rest from the weariness of travel. The moonbeams dwelling in the tips of the Komorigi are shattered into a thousand pieces and sway in the wind. Though I know not the heart of the moon in the Ōzora, it is a joyful radiance to me. Resting on this hill where the moon is clear, I hear the voice of autumn in the sound of the wind through the pines. It is like the form of Tengoku; how joyful it is to see the clear moonlight on the pine branches.'

The Bamboo sings:

'Coming here, I behold the moon emerging from the mountain ridge of my Furusato, which I think of as dear. As I travel the path toward the mountain of Takamitsu, I gaze upon the moon of Tsukimigaoka. Closing and opening the curtain of white clouds, the Katsurao of the moon peers down at us. The Suzumushi also raise their clear voices to praise and sing of this evening's moon. The light of the moon shining on the hill where bush clovers and Kikiyō are fragrant is especially clear. Following the noble Akio, I gaze upon the clear moon this evening. Coming here and gazing at the moon traversing the sky, I cannot think of this as a wild path where Akuma dwell. When I see the moon, even the Masurao spirit of Yotahiko begins to soften, like the flowers of the maiden flower. When I look closely at the maiden flower blooming on a moonlit night, it is leaning while bathed in dew. The great sky of this evening is so clear that even the path the moon travels is visible. Because the light of the moon floating in the Ōzora is clear, it casts the shadows of the pines upon the ground.'

The Plum sings:

'The clouds in the clear moonlit sky have one by one grown thin and vanished. Surrounded by the moon and the dew of the flowers, I now compose a song at Tsukimigaoka. If not for the blessing of the dew of Tsukiyomi, the hundred grasses and trees would not be able to grow. The clear moon of this evening must be shining brightly over the deep pools of the Ōigawa. Gazing at the moon floating in the pool, the dragon god will likely float up to the surface of the water to play. The moonlight crossing the peaks of the fiery mountains is red, and the dew on the thousand grasses scatters in the wind. Having finished a day's journey, I now find intimacy with the moon of Tsukimigaoka. The joyful things of the autumn field are the hundred thousand flowers and the sound of insects playing to the moon. Coming here, I heard the clear chirping of the pine crickets, the Suzumushi, and the crickets. My soul was revived by the sound of insects, which I do not even hear on the mountains of the upper waters. At the start of the journey to advance toward the mountain of Takamitsu, I beheld the clear moon. Receiving the dew, the undergrowth beneath the moon crossing the sky each glitters like a jewel. My sleeves are dampened by the dew, and I have even let the light of the moon traversing the sky dwell within them. I am on a grass pillow this evening, bathed in moonlight that seems to rival the midday sun. When the moon is clear, the clouds in the sky fade away, and the sound of the wind blowing through the pines is also clear. Although there is never a night when I tire of the moon, it is joyful to see it on an evening of travel. When I look far into the heavens, the green is deep; autumn is truly the light of the moon. The moon between the pine trees on the mountain of the upper waters is clear; did it see me on this hill as it sent me off? The moon of this evening is such that one can even see the number of wild geese flying with their wings beating between the clouds.'

The Cherry sings:

'Following the noble Akio, I look upon the face of the clear moon. There is nothing that does not adorn autumn: the scent of flowers, the sound of insects, and the moonlight. Nothing can surpass the joy of an autumn journey, with the hundred flowers fragrant and the moon clear. As I walk the lower path where the moon is clear, the voices of insects tremble in the dew, and the flowers are fragrant. The voices of the Suzumushi ring out, shaking the clear air of the moonlit night. I, who am traveling, wish to possess the heart of the moonlight that illuminates the heavens and earth without exception. The sound of the murmuring stream can also be heard, and it is a moonlit night when the sound of insects is clear on the hillside. The moon, as bright as the day, makes the maiden flower, the Kikiyō, and the Karukaya appear to fade in color.'

原文（近代文語）

秋男の一行五人は、漸くにして雑草生ひ茂る月見ケ丘の夕を、ここに息を休めながら松間の月を眺めて歌ふ。

秋男『大野原渉りて漸く月見ケ丘の 松に懸れる月を見しかな』

松は歌ふ。

『松生ふる月見ケ丘の月光は 秋の夕の風にゆれつつ。 月清み草葉にすだく虫の音も いやさえざえに小夜更けにけり。 秋月の静かに照れる丘の上に 旅の疲れを休らふ宵かな。 籠木の梢に宿る月影は 千々に砕けて風にさゆれつ。 大空の月の心は知らねども われには楽しきかげにぞありける。 月澄めるこれの丘辺に休らひて 松風聞けば秋の声あり。 天国の姿なるかな松ケ枝に 澄む月影を見れば楽しき』

竹は歌ふ。

『此処に来て親しと思ふ故郷の 山の端出づる月を見しかな。 高光の山に進まむ道すがら 月見ケ丘の月を見るかな。 白雲の幕を閉ぢつつ開きつつ 月の桂男われ等をのぞけり。 鈴虫も清しき声を張りあげて 今宵の月を称へうたふも。 萩、桔梗匂へる丘に照る月の かげは一入清しかりけり。 空高き秋男の君に従ひて 今宵は清しき月を見るかな。 此処に来て空ゆく月を眺むれば 悪魔のすまふ野路とは思へず。 大丈夫の弥猛心も月見れば 柔らぎ初めぬ女郎花の花。 月の夜に咲く女郎花よく見れば 露を浴みつつ傾きにけり。 月の行く道も確に見ゆるまで 澄み渡りたり今宵の大空。

大空に浮べる月の光清み 地上に松の影を描けり』

梅は歌ふ。

『月澄める御空の雲は次々に 薄らぎにつつ消え失せにけり。 月と花の露にかこまれわれは今 月見ケ丘に歌を詠むなり。 月読の露の恵みの無かりせば 百の草木も育たざるべし。 澄み渡る今宵の月は大井川 竜の淵瀬に冴え渡るらむ。 淵に浮く月を眺めて竜神は 水の面に浮び出でて遊ばむ。 火炎山峰越す月の光赤み 千草の露も風に散るなり。 一日の旅を終りてわれは今 月見ケ丘の月に親しむ。 秋の野の楽しきものは百千花 月に奏づる虫の音なりけり。 此処に来て松虫鈴虫きりぎりす 清けき虫の鳴く音聞きしよ。 水上の山にも聞かぬ虫の音に わが魂は蘇りける。

高光山進まむ道の首途に われは冴えたる月を見しかな。 空渡る月の下草露うけて おのもおのもに玉とかがよふ。 わが袖は露にしめりて御空ゆく 月の光さへ宿らせにけり。 真昼間にまがふべらなる月光を 浴びて今宵の草枕かな。 月澄めば御空の雲も消えゆきて 松吹く風の音もさやけき。 何時とても月を倦く夜はなけれども 旅の夕に見るは楽しき。 天の原ふりさけ見れば緑深し 秋こそ月の光なるかな。 水上山松の木の間の月冴えて われを送るかこの丘に見つ。 雲の間に翼を搏ちて飛ぶ雁の 数さへ見ゆる今宵の月はも』

桜は歌ふ。

『澄み渡る秋男の君に従ひて 冴えたる月の顔を見る。 花の香も虫の鳴く音も月光も 秋を飾らぬものなかりけり。 楽しきは秋の旅路に如かざらめ 百花匂ひ月の冴ゆれば。 月冴ゆる下道ゆけば虫の声 露にふるひて花香るなり。 月の夜の冴ゆる空気をゆるがせて 透きとほるなり鈴虫の声。 天地を隈なく照らす月光の 心持ちたし旅ゆくわれは。 せせらぎの音も聞えて丘の辺に 虫の音冴ゆる月の夜頃よ。 昼の如明るき月も女郎花 桔梗刈萱色褪せて見ゆ』

§ 2

While the party was resting and praising the clear moon of the autumn night, a mass of black clouds appeared in the southeastern sky, and while they watched, it spread in all directions, and the bright moonlight was suddenly enveloped by the black clouds, and it became dark at a short distance, and a lump of darkness fell heavily upon the forest of Shigeki in Tsukimigaoka. The figures of the party became invisible in the darkness, and there was no other means but to wait for the sky to clear, relying only on their voices.

Akio sings:

Even the sky that was clear as day has suddenly / Become impossible to distinguish. / The clouds that have wrapped around the tens of thousands of stars / Blow cold and black. / The sound of insects has stopped completely, and the wind blowing through the treetops / Has begun to howl in a sinister way. / Pine, Bamboo, Plum, and Cherry, take heed / For this must be the prologue to the emergence of the Akuma. / Lowering the curtain of darkness to deceive our eyes / The swarm of Akuma seems to be plotting. / Fearing our power, the Akugami / Must have cast darkness upon the earth. / Kamunagara, may the Mitama be blessed / And clear away this darkness. / From somewhere, a strange voice is heard / Is it the laughing hag or the mocking hag? / No matter how the Maga may attack / I possess the Kototama of rigor.

Pine sings:

The hundred trees of Pine in Tsukimigaoka are hidden / By black clouds, and the sound of insects is faint. / Even if tens of thousands of Suihonki come / I shall strike them with the Ikukototama and release them. / Be that as it may, I wish to clear the darkness / That wraps around Tsukimigaoka and proceed.

Bamboo sings:

Should a man of great courage fear the darkness / For the sake of the country? / The scent of flowers blooming in full splendor has faded away / And the darkness is deepening more and more. / As the sound of insects gathering on this hill grows faint / It is a midnight when a strange wind blows. / Though I speak out bravely, my heart / Has become lonely.

Plum sings:

Are you not a man of great courage, that you fear / This night of eternal darkness so much? / Even if the curtain of darkness wraps around me in many layers / I shall advance by the light of the sincerity of my heart. / While the Maga tries to obstruct us / By wrapping around the clear moonlight of the vast sky. / Even if the night of eternal darkness is deep / I shall not fear the Akuma, for how could I be daunted?

Cherry sings:

I have climbed to Tsukimigaoka, a rare event / And have comforted my heart. / It is hateful that the ugly black clouds / Should wrap around the moment when the soul is revived. / Though the black clouds wrap around, the moon / Must be shining brightly in the sky. / No matter how thick the clouds in the mid-air may be / They will soon clear, for the moon is there.

Just as they were singing this, a sinister voice was heard from nowhere.

Gia-hahahaha, giyohohohō. / Cowardly, weak men have gathered / And are singing weak songs in Tsukimigaoka. / How ridiculous it is for cowards to sing / Worthless songs, relying on the moon crossing the sky. / You are squeezing out sad voices / Like insects chirping while wrapped in a curtain of darkness. / Stop your journey to Takamitsuyama, you cowards / With your weak spirit, you will never reach it. / The Fuyuo you seek has already / Given up and become a weak demon. / You are crying and weeping in tears / And living out your days in tears at Shinobugaoka. / You too, like Fuyuo, give up / And become a demon of Tsukimigaoka. / I have been listening to your songs along the way / Do you not know who I am, you fools? / I am the hag who mocks and enjoys / Everything in the world. / If you are angry at being mocked, bite your eyes / And die, and that will settle it. / You cowards who are wrapped in the darkness of my plan / And are weeping in tears. / Giyahahahaha, you are being choked by the throat / You are the ones who will soon meet a sad end. / Now that it has come to this, you are mine / You had better bite your tongues and die. / There is no one as great as me in the world / Is there any Suijōkan or God? / Hearing that the brother of the cowardly Fuyuo was coming / I have been waiting for you in Tsukimigaoka. / Now that it has come to this, you are mine to do as I please / Whether I boil you and eat you, or roast you and eat you. / Ahahaha, how pitiful these brats are / I will have my companions torture them to death. / Do you petty, weak-willed brats / Intend to disturb my territory? / If I let you disturb my territory so easily / Would the face of the mocking hag stand? / I am the younger sister of the laughing hag, the mocking hag / I will mock you until you foam at the mouth.

原文（近代文語）

かく一行は、秋の夜の澄みきる月を称へ、休らひ居たる折もあれ、東南の天に当りて一塊の黒雲現はるるよと見る間に、次第々々に四方に拡がり、さしもに明るき月光も、忽ち黒雲に包まれ、咫尺黯澹として、どつかりと闇の塊は月見ケ丘の茂樹の森に落ち来りぬ。一行の姿は互に見えぬまで暗黒と化し、只声のみを頼りに空の晴るるを待つより外に手段なかりける。

秋男は歌ふ。

『昼の如晴れたる空も忽ちに あやめも分かずなりにけらしな。 幾万の星を残らず包みたる 雲黒々と吹く風寒し。 虫の音もひたと止まりて梢吹く 風いやらしくうなり初めたり。 松、竹よ梅よ桜よ心せよ 悪魔の出づる序幕なるらむ。 闇の幕下してわれ等が目をかすめ 事謀るらし悪魔の群は。 われわれの力に怖ぢて悪神は 地上に闇を落せしならむ。 惟神御霊幸はひましまして この暗闇を晴らさせ給へ。 何処よりか怪しき声の聞ゆなり 笑ひ婆アか譏り婆アか。 如何ならむ曲津の襲ひ来るとも われには厳の言霊ありけり』

松は歌ふ。

『月見ケ丘松の百木も黒雲に かくれて見えず虫の音細し。 水奔鬼たとへ幾万来るとも 生言霊に打ちて放らむ。 兎にもあれ月見ケ丘を包みたる 闇を晴らして進みゆかばや』

竹は歌ふ。

『大丈夫の弥猛心は暗闇に 恐るべしやは国の御為。 爛漫と匂へる花の香消え失せて 闇はますます深みけるかな。 この丘にすだく虫の音細りつつ 怪しき風の吹き来る夜半なり。 雄々しくは言挙すれど村肝の 心淋しくなりにけらしな』

梅は歌ふ。

『大丈夫に君は非ずや常闇の 今宵をさまで恐れ給ふか。 闇の幕幾重にわれを包むとも 心の誠の光りに進まむ。 大空の清き月光包みつつ 曲津はわれ等にさやらむとすも。 よしやよし常闇の夜は深くとも 如何でひるまむ悪魔も恐れじ』

桜は歌ふ。

『珍しく月見ケ丘に登り来て われは心を慰めしはや。 魂の蘇りたるたまゆらを 包むも憎し醜の黒雲。 黒雲は包めど月は皎々と 御空に輝き給ふなるらむ。 中空の雲は如何程厚くとも やがては晴れむ月のいませば』

斯く歌へる折しも、何処ともなく聞え来るいやらしき声。

『ギアハハハハハー、ギヨホホホホー

腰抜けのヒヨロヒヨロ男が集りて 弱音吹くかな月見ケ丘に。 空渡る月を力に腰抜けが くだらぬ歌をうたふ可笑しさ。 闇の幕に包まれ虫の鳴く如き 悲しき声をしぼり居るかな。 高光山の旅をとどまれ貴様等の 弱腰にてはとても及ばじ。 貴様等の求むる冬男は早や既に へこたれよつて弱鬼となりしよ。 メソメソと吠面かわき赤恥を 忍ケ丘に泣き暮し居る。 其方も冬男の如くへこたれて 月見ケ丘の鬼となれなれ。 汝等の歌を道々聞いてゐた 俺の姿を知らぬか馬鹿者。 此方は世界の奴を悉く 譏り楽しむ婆アなるぞや。 譏られて腹が立つなら目を噛んで 死んでしまへば埒があくぞや。

此方の経綸の闇に包まれて 吠面かわく腰抜野郎よ。 ギヤハハハハーぎゆうぎゆう喉をしめられて 今に悲しき最後をする奴。 かうなればもう俺のもの貴様等は 舌など噛んで死んだがよいぞや。 世の中に俺程えらい者はない 水上館も神もあるかい。 腰抜の冬男の兄が来ると聞きて 待つて居たぞよ月見ケ丘に。 かくなればもうこちのもの煮て喰はうと 焼いて喰はうとしたい放題。 アハハハハあはれなるかな此餓鬼は 俺等の仲間で嬲り殺しよ。 ちよこざいな腰弱男の餓鬼どもが 俺の縄張荒らそとするか。 縄張をむざむざ貴様に荒らされて 譏り婆さんの顔がたつかい。 笑ひ婆の妹の俺は譏り婆よ 譏り散らして泡を吹かさむ。

§ 3

To rest upon this hill, having fallen into our trap, is the end of your luck.

That you gazed at the moon in the Ōzora is the very end of your fortune.

How pitiful are you five men, wandering, stumbling, and looking about in the exhaustion of your luck.

Gyahahahahā, gyohohohohō, gyufufufuhū.

Such loathsome voices continued in the darkness. Akio, no longer able to endure it, worshipped the heavens and the earth, and while clapping his hands, said:

One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten.

Hundred, thousand, eight million Kami.

Clear away the Amatsumisora.

He repeated the Kazūta of heaven.

From within the darkness, a voice like a Waregane said:

Watsuhahahahā, ufufufuhū, how truly adorable you are. I am the Binbafugami, the mocking hag who ridicules and teases the wretches of the Sekai one after another, the younger sister of the laughing hag, the Suihonki who dwells on Shinobugaoka; are you terrified? Do not spout such insolence as the Kazūta of heaven. I am not an Onibaba who will flinch at such things. Gyahahahahā, gyufufufuhū, the end.

As soon as she said this, the mysterious voices stopped abruptly.

原文（近代文語）

此方の罠にかかつてこの丘に 休むといふは運の尽きぞや。 大空の月見したのが其の方の いよいよ運の尽きとなりける。 運のつきまごつきうろつききよろつきの 五人男の憐れなるかな。

ギヤハハハハー、ギヨホホホホー、ギユフフフフー』

といやらしき声が闇の中に連続してゐる。秋男は最早や堪り兼ね、天を拝し地を拝し拍手しながら、

『一二三四五六七八九十

百千万八千万の神

天津御空を晴らさせ給へ』

と天の数歌を繰り返してゐる。

闇の中より破鐘の様な声、

『ワツハハハハー、ウフフフフー、てもさてもいぢらしいものだのう。此方は忍ケ丘に棲む水奔鬼の笑ひ婆アさんの妹、世界の奴ども片つぱしから譏り散らして茶々を入れる譏り婆さんの貧乏神ぞや、恐れ入つたか。天の数歌なんぞと減らず口を叩くな。そんな事でビクとも致す鬼婆ではないぞや。ギヤハハハハー、ギユフフフフー、終り』

と言つた限り、ピタリと怪しき声は止つた。
