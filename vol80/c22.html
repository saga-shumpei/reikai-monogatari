Chapter 22: 迎への Bird Ship [2026]

迎への鳥船〔2026〕

§ 1

The retinue of Asakahime-no-Kami crossed over to the Island of Lamentation, bestowed flint upon the Kunitsukami, caused the desolate lands to be cultivated throughout, and renamed it the Island of Joy. Then, together with their horses, they boarded the imperial ship again, and while rowing to the right and left across the distant island roots, they arrived at last around midday at the port of Matsūra on the eastern coast of the land of Yoshihara, which is famed as the first in vastness within the great ocean. They laid the ship along the shore and landed together with the four pillars of Ubugamihiko-no-Kami, Okitatsuhiko-no-Kami, Tatsuyohime-no-Kami, and Ameharehime-no-Kami, and while gazing with a pleasant feeling upon the wave-beaten shore where old pines grew thick, they chanted a divine song.

'To meet Akitsuo-no-Kami, I have crossed the mountains, rivers, and the ocean to come to this place. How pure is the sight of the white waves washing upon the shore, shining in the sun. I have crossed safely over the waves, whose depths are unknown even to eight thousand fathoms, and have arrived here. As I rest in the shade of the evergreen trees, how refreshing is the sound of the wind blowing over the sea. Thinking of the work of bearing divine children, I set out on this painful journey morning and evening. This island is overgrown with reeds, and it seems that the Magatsukami are lurking here and there. How joyful I feel, thinking that this island is a sacred place governed by Asagirihime-no-Kami. The sun of heaven hangs over the pine treetops, bestowing a cool shade upon me.'

Ubugamihiko-no-Kami chants:

'Serving in the retinue of my Lord, I have looked up to your various noble achievements. The sound of the waves and the resonance of the pines seem to praise the achievements of my Lord purely. My Lord, you who have crossed the mountains, rivers, and seas where the Magatsumi rage to the right and left. The noble achievements of Asakahime will shine until the very end of ten thousand generations. Hearing the sound of the hundreds of birds, it appears as if they are waiting for my Lord, holding back their joy.'

Okitatsuhiko-no-Kami chants:

'I have come following the achievements of the Kami of the imperial substitute who descended from the high-shining mountains. Akitsuo-no-Kami must also be rejoicing at the pure heart of the Kami of the imperial substitute. O my Lord, who serves the divine work of the Kami, having crossed the endless mountains, rivers, and oceans. Encouraged by the bravery of the Kami of the imperial substitute, I, the Kami of Okitatsu, have overcome my weak heart. This is the time when the achievements of the Kami of Okitatsu appear in the land of Yoshihara, where the land is still young. When I look far out over the high-shining mountains, purple clouds are trailing. Is the area of purple clouds the Aokigahara where Asagirihime-no-Kami dwells eternally? I, too, wish to visit the pure garden of Aokigahara and see the form of the land in the four directions.'

Tatsuyohime-no-Kami chants:

'Following Asakahime of the imperial substitute, I travel through the waves of the ocean, which are precious. The bravery of the Kami who pacified the various evil Kami and cultivated the land. Seeing the achievements of the Kami who descended from heaven, I, Tatsuyohime, watch over them and tears fall. In the purity of the sound of the wind blowing through the pines, the fatigue of the journey is forgotten. It is a day when the wings of the gulls shine as they fly to the right and left over the waves. Having crossed the ocean where light and darkness intersect, we have now arrived at the port of Matsūra. The view of the port of Matsūra is pure; as far as the eye can see, there is white sand and white waves.'

Ameharehime-no-Kami chants:

'How joyful it is to rest in the port on this midday when heaven and earth are clear. When I look up at the western sky, dark shadows are flapping their wings and advancing. One after another, they appear larger in form, and the roar of their wings can be heard. How wonderful, how wonderful, they are not birds, but perhaps the Iwakusu ships on which the Kami are riding.'

Asakahime-no-Kami raised her right hand high to shield her eyes from the sunlight and looked out over the sky; the heavenly bird ships were skimming the western sky, causing the air to rumble as they descended toward the port of Matsūra. Seeing their brave appearance, she chanted a divine song:

'How wonderful, how wonderful, the heavenly bird ships have descended; they must be the messengers coming to welcome me. It seems they are the imperial ships sent by the divine word of Asagirihime-no-Kami to welcome us.'

Just as she was chanting thus, the bird ships descended onto the fine sand of the port of Matsūra, silencing their sound quietly.

Ubugamihiko-no-Kami chants in astonishment:

立世比女の神は歌ふ。

天晴比女の神は歌ふ。

『天地の晴れ渡りたる此の真昼を 嬉しきかもよ港に休らふ。 西空をふりさけ見れば黒き影 翼を摶ちて進み来るかも。 次ぎ次ぎに形大きく見えにつつ 翼の音の轟き聞ゆる。 天晴れ天晴れ鳥にはあらぬ神々の 乗らせ給へる磐樟船かも』

朝香比女の神は、右手を高くさし上げ日光を遮り、御空を見渡し給へば、天の鳥船は西空をかすめて空気をどよもしながら、松浦の港をさして降り来るあり。その勇ましき姿を見て、御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『天晴れ天晴れ天の鳥船降り来ぬ われを迎ふる使なるらむ。 朝霧比女神の神言の遣はせし 吾等を迎への御船なるらし』

かく歌ひ給ふ折もあれ、松浦の港の真砂の上に、鳥船は静かに鳴りを鎮めて降り来る。

初頭比古の神は驚きながら歌ふ。

§ 2

'How rare a divine ship this is, that there should exist a ship of the Kami that traverses above the clouds. I know of ships that cross the waves, but I knew not of a ship that travels the sky. Through the blessing of the noble Kototama of the Lord God, this ship that travels the sky has been built.'

Okitatsuhiko-no-Kami sings:

'How rare a divine ship this is, a Torifune of the heavens such as even the Chihayabu Kamiyo has not heard of. Though I have heard that there are Kami who ride upon the wings of the Ōtori, I have not heard of a ship of the sky.'

Tatsuyohime-no-Kami sings:

'Having come to serve in the retinue of the Mihishiro-no-Kami, today I have seen a rare divine ship.'

Ameharehime-no-Kami sings:

'Making the Hisakata clear sky resound, the Aha Torifune has descended.'

At the moment of this singing, the three pillars, led by Ōmiterashi-no-Kami, Asazorao-no-Kami, and Kuniumio-no-Kami, descended from the Torifune with a smile, advanced quietly before Asakahime-no-Kami, performed a Saikeirei, then exchanged a Mokurei with the four pillars of the retinue, and while pointing to the Torifune, said:

'We have descended from Takamitsuyama to welcome the Mihishiro Asakahimegami. Revering the divine words of Asagirihime-no-Kami, we have come parting the cloud-paths to welcome you, O Princess. We pray that you board this divine ship, and we shall escort you to Takamitsuyama.'

Asazorao-no-Kami sings:

'The appearance of the Mihishiro-no-Kami who has descended from the Hisakata Takamitsuyama is awe-inspiring. Asagirihime-no-Kami has been awaiting the departure of the eight pillars of the Mihishiro-no-Kami. I have come having built this Torifune; come, board this sturdy divine ship.'

Kuniumio-no-Kami sings:

'I am Kuniumio-no-Kami, a presiding Kami who serves the mountain of Takamitsu. Since the divine words of Asakahimegami are in the divine light, may you illuminate our land. This ship that crosses the Hisakata cloud-wells is more peaceful than a ship that travels the sea.'

Asakahime-no-Kami sings:

'Accepting the deep heart of the Mihishiro-no-Kami, I shall ascend to the sacred place by the power of the divine ship. Gazing upon the rare Torifune of the heavens, I contemplate the merits of the Lord God. I shall praise the brave heart of the Kami who has come parting the Hisakata cloud-paths.'

Sentōhiko-no-Kami sings:

'The joy of coming here to serve in the retinue of my Princess and boarding a ship of the sky. Though I have heard that the land is still young, I am surprised that the Ashihara has been opened up like this. Since the merits of the Torifune that travels the sky are great, we shall ascend Takamitsuyama in peace.'

Okitatsuhiko-no-Kami sings:

'It is a joy to entrust my body to the first ship of the sky and travel while looking down upon the lower world.'

Tatsuyohime-no-Kami sings:

'Though I am a Himegami, I am happy to think that I am advancing while riding upon the Torifune.'

Ameharehime-no-Kami sings:

'On this day when the heavens and earth are clear, the ship that travels the sky must be pure.'

Ōmiterashi-no-Kami sings:

'Come then, starting with Asakahimegami, let the four pillars of the Kami also board.'

Here, the eight pillars of the host and guests entrusted their bodies to the Torifune with strong and wide wings, soared high from the port of Matsuura into the mid-air, and while looking down upon the mountains, rivers, and seas of the lower world, continued their journey through the cloud-wells, aiming for the summit of Takamitsuyama.

Asakahime-no-Kami, looking down upon the shape of the land below, sings:

'Am I not a bird that travels the sky? I see all the mountains and rivers beneath my eyes. The mountains, the fields, and the great ocean shine clearly beneath my eyes. The long journey of the grass-pillow has become a cloud-pillow this day. If there be fire-seeds in this land, I shall burn the reeds and the water-running grasses to open it up. Fortunately, I possess the flint; I shall offer it as a gift to the Mihishiro-no-Kami. We who advance parting the cloud-paths are joyful; our wings are swift and the sound of our feathers is pure.'

Ōmiterashi-no-Kami sings:

'Asakahime-no-Kami, be at peace. Though there are many air pockets, how brave is the sound of the wings of the Torifune as it advances against the blowing wind. The high mountain visible in the Hisakata sky is the sacred place where the Lord God is enshrined.'

Asazorao-no-Kami sings:

'The Torifune that welcomed the Mihishiro-no-Kami, Asakahime, pushes aside the distant cloud-wells, advancing toward the sky, taking a course to the sacred ground of Takamitsuyama. Even if the blowing wind is strong, even if black clouds wrap the peaks, how could I fear? Revering the noble divine words of the Lord God, there is no crookedness in the path we shall advance. Even if there are many air pockets, I have confidence in my two arms. Praying to the Kami, I advance to overcome any difficult place and achieve the intended purpose. The lake visible below is famous in the land of Yosan, a water mirror newly born from the collapse of the Kaenzan.'

§ 3

Tsukihi floats and shines. Looking out toward the far Anata, the Seijiyō of Minakamiyama is wrapped in thick Shiun, and the beauty of the land's excellence is visible. Yoshigusa and Suihonsau grow thick, swarming until there is no room left. Below one's eyes, the Aranogahara, where Oni and Orochi lurk, spreads out widely. There is a feeling of looking up and waiting for the Kaurin of Mihishirogami. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. The summit of Takamitsuyama draws near shidaishidai, and even the roof tiles of the Miyakata built in the Seijiyō of Aokigahara can be seen quite clearly. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. How noble is the protection of Kami.

Mihishirogami: 'After half a hantoki, you shall arrive safely at the noble Seijiyō.'

Parting the Yahe Kumoji, around the evening of that day, the Torifune steered by the Kami of Ōmiterashi arrived safely and quietly at the Seijiyō of Aokigahara, accompanying Mihishirogami.

The Tsukasatachi of Takamitsuyama raised both hands high, and expressing their welcome, they raised a war cry, 'Wō wō.'

八重の雲路を分けながら、其の日の夕ごろ、大御照の神の操れる鳥船は、青木ケ原の聖場に御樋代神を伴ひまつり、安く静かに着きにけり。

高光山の司等は両手を高くさし上げ、「ウオーウオー」と鬨をつくりて歓迎の意を表しける。
