Chapter 22: 天祐 [1385]

天祐〔1385〕

§ 1

Haruna was entrusted with the supreme command of the entire army by Setsuteiri, gathered eight hundred soldiers within the castle, caused them to guard each gate firmly, and waited for the enemy's shifurai with their bubi fully prepared. Under the supreme command of Berutsu, Shieru led one unit, and while fluttering the gensuiki in the shiyoka wind, they raised war cries and attacked straight from the tōzainanboku of the castle. However, this time, unlike the Brahman army, they did not set fire to the minka. The one thousand kishi and the two thousand hastily assembled nōhei, each carrying their emono, were deceived by the nengon of Berutsu that now was the time to destroy the gajiyō of the akuma and save the kokumin from the totan no ku, and their momentum as they approached, neglecting their agriculture, was like splitting bamboo. Berutsu first headed toward the front gate on horseback and called out in a loud voice:

Berutsu: 'General Berutsu, commander-in-chief of the people's army, has arrived leading fifty thousand soldiers. No matter how much you rely on the authority of Setsuteiri, you surely cannot oppose this. Open the gate immediately and surrender, or else, as this castle is surrounded by our friendly forces from all directions in ten and twenty layers, it is certain that it will be pulverized in an instant. I shall await your reply.'

He called out thus. However, the guards within the castle were silent, and not a single person answered; they were waiting with bated breath, holding their weapons ready to strike should the enemy approach. Even the formidable Berutsu could not easily advance as the castle gate was firm, and he showed a hint of hesitation, unable to fathom what kind of strategy might be hidden in the excessive silence of the enemy. He thought that if he were to surround the castle and engage in a war of attrition, the castle would soon run out of provisions and raise a white flag to surrender. Thinking it would be a convenient way to achieve a great victory without losing a single soldier of his own, he decided to adopt a war of attrition, occasionally raising shouts of 'Wai, Wai' to intimidate the guards within the castle. Meanwhile, Shieru, who had headed toward the back gate, was so happy to have become a general that, as if to say that now was the time to show his power, he tried to strike the back gate and enter, but he was daunted by the arrows raining down like rain and fled far away, circling the castle at a distance of about one chō. At night, the light of the bonfires burned brightly, and it was an indescribable beauty when viewed from inside the castle. Haruna, the supreme commander, ran around the castle giving commands, strictly ordering that no one should fight until the drums on the watchtowers sounded, and the eight hundred fierce soldiers remained calm, holding their breath. The three thousand enemy troops surrounding the four sides kept a distance of about one chō and did not attempt to push forward, and the standoff lasted for almost a month. There is nothing more painful than watching the castle from morning till night without any purpose, waiting for orders to come down. Among them, some began to fight to console their boredom, others got drunk and swung their iron fists; discipline in the camp gradually fell into disarray, and some even began to sneak away under the cover of night. The three thousand soldiers who had gathered at the front had reduced to about fifteen hundred during the month-long standoff. Inside the castle, Setsuteiri, the Left Guard, the Right Guard, Taruman, and others climbed to the high tower, looked down at the enemy's formation, prayed to the gods, or shared sake, waiting for reinforcements to arrive. Changing the subject, Hiruna-hime and Karuna-hime, who had been dragged along by the two generals Oniharawake and Kumehiko and had left the capital of Victoria far behind on horseback, arrived at the foot of Shimeji Pass with the generals. The distance between these places reached almost fifty ri. This Shimeji Pass is a continuation of the peaks of Mount Inokura and is the most difficult passage. It is impossible to pass through on horseback. The two generals were the first to escape to this point, spread out a folding stool on the green grass, and while making Hiruna-hime and Karuna-hime, the two beauties, wait before them, they drank the sake from the gourd they carried, licking their lips as if to savor every drop, and discussed gathering the entire friendly army that was escaping one after another to reorganize their ranks and barricade themselves in the rock caves of Mount Inokura again. From the beginning, they had no courage to attack Mount Kogane. However, fearing that they would dampen the morale of the troops, they were claiming to be conquering Mount Kogane. Their plan was to lead the entire army if they found a suitable place, annex a small country, and build a fortress on Mount Inokura to construct a great kingdom. Having escaped with their lives, the two generals were extremely hungry. Having suddenly gulped down the sake, they began to get very intoxicated in proportion to the amount of sake they had consumed.

While saying this, he lifted Hirunahime and placed her lightly upon a horse. Kumehiko likewise placed Karunahime upon a horse. The two Hime had thrown a tantrum, complaining of aching feet and aching buttocks, solely for the purpose of escaping on horseback.

No sooner had the two mounted than they turned their horses' heads sharply to the east, applied a single lash, and dashed away at full speed like a flash of lightning. The two Shiyōgun shouted until their voices were hoarse:

Shiyōgun: 'Hey, hey, you subordinates! Pursue them and seize them!'

As they were on this steep slope, all the Kishi had dismounted and finished preparing for the ascent by tying their spurred boots to the saddles; thus, they could not suddenly mount their horses, and while they were fumbling to untie and put on their boots, the two had already fled beyond the reach of their sight. Suddenly, Marishiten, disguised as Mokusuke of Ananai-kyō and leading hundreds of Shishi, straddled a Kiyodai Shishi and let out a roar, 'Uuu!', that echoed through the surrounding Sangaku, galloping freely through the Guntai. The Shiyōgun and the entire army were terrified by the unexpected attack of the Shishi; some were paralyzed with fear, some fled barefoot, some wailed and screamed, and others fled in various directions as they pleased, leaving behind only the cowards who had lost the use of their legs. The horses, startled by the voices of the Shishi, scattered and fled in all directions. The pack of Shishi gathered in one place and, raising their voices in unison, let out a roar like the rumbling of a hundred thunders, 'Uuu!', to intimidate them; then, under the command of Marishiten, they pursued Hiruna and Karuna, vanishing like clouds and mist.

Belts, after more than a month of encampment, saw the morale of his troops gradually crumbling, with desertions occurring one after another under the cover of night; he resolved that he must inspire morale through a battle. Ciel from the back gate and Belts from the front gate, with the momentum of a charging Shishi and with reckless warriors in the lead, broke through the supposedly solid great gates and stormed into the castle. Haruna commanded eight hundred soldiers, dividing them in eight directions to defend and fight. However, the enemy forces, surging like a tide, increased their numbers by the moment; even the common soldiers who had once scattered returned, shouting 'Wai, wai!', and once again the three thousand soldiers entered the castle without exception, rampaging at will. Haruna was suddenly taken prisoner, and just as the surroundings of Kshatriya, Sashū-no-tsukasa, and Taruman appeared to be in peril, the sound of a Propagational Song was heard coming from the front gate. This was Kunisawake, leading the subordinates of Matsuhiko, Ryūkō, and Mankō, arriving to the rescue.

The other soldiers shuddered with fear at the groaning of the Shishi, their bodies shrinking as they bit the earth and trembled. Hiruna quickly spotted the fallen figure of Belts and tied him onto the back of a horse; finding a horse abandoned by the enemy, she leaped onto it lightly and, together with Karuna, galloped vigorously back through the back gate with a clattering of hooves, for the King's great emergency.

と下知する。此急坂にかかつたので何れの騎士も全部馬を下り、鞍には拍車のついた靴を括りつけ登坂の用意をして了つた際とて、俄に馬に乗る訳にも行かず靴を解き足に穿ち、グヅグヅしてゐる間に、二人は早くも目の届かぬ所まで逃げてゐる。忽ち幾百とも知れぬ獅子を引連れた三五教の杢助に扮した摩利支天は、巨大なる獅子に跨り『ウー』と四辺の山岳を響かせ、軍隊の中を縦横無尽に駆け廻つた。将軍初め全軍は思はぬ獅子の襲来に肝を潰し、腰をぬかす者、真裸足で逃げるもの、泣き叫ぶ者、其外種々雑多に思ひ思ひに逃走し、残るものは腰を抜かした弱虫ばかりであつた。馬は獅子の声に驚いて思ひ思ひに逃げ散つて了つた。獅子の群は一所に集まり、一斉に声を揃へて『ウー』と百雷の轟く如く唸り立て威喝を試みた上、ヒルナ、カルナの後を追うて、摩利支天指揮のもとに雲を霞と追うて行く。

ベルツは一ケ月余の滞陣に、士気漸く乱れ、夜陰に乗じて脱隊するもの相次いて踵を接するため一戦して士気を鼓舞せねばならぬと覚悟をきめ、シエールは裏門よりベルツは表門より獅子奮迅の勢にて、猪武者を先頭に、さしも堅固の大門を打破り城内に乱れ入つた。ハルナは八百の手兵を指揮し、兵を八方に分つて防ぎ戦うた。されど潮の如く押寄せた敵軍は、刻々に其数を増し、一旦逃げ散りし雑兵迄帰り来つて『ワーイワーイ』と喚き立ち乍ら、又もとの如く三千の兵士は城内に残らず進入し、手当り次第に暴れ出した。忽ちハルナは捕虜となり刹帝利、左守司、タルマンの身辺も今や危しと見る間に、表門に当つて宣伝歌の声が聞えて来た。之は治国別が松彦、竜公、万公の部下を率ゐて救援に向うたのである。

『神が表に現はれて 善と悪とを立別ける 音に名高きビクの国 東にライオン川を負ひ 西にビクトル山控へ 要害堅固の鉄城を ここに築きて永久に 百の国民治めます ビクトリア王の御居城 八岐大蛇や醜神に 誑惑されし右守の司 ベルツの司は軍隊を 率ゐて不羈を図らむと 攻め寄せ来る浅ましさ 天地を造り玉ひたる 誠の神は善を褒め 悪を懲して地の上に 天国浄土を建設し 上は王者を初めとし 下国民の端迄も

守らせ玉ふ尊さよ 三五教の宣伝使 治国別の一行が 現はれ来る上からは 幾十万の強敵が 一度に襲ひ攻め来とも 如何でか恐れむビクの国 刹帝利王よ心安く 思召されよ天地の 神の賜ひし言霊を 完全に委曲に打出し 救ひまつらむ惟神 神に誓ひて宣りまつる ああ惟神々々 御霊幸はひましませよ 朝日は照るとも曇るとも 月は盈つとも虧くるとも 仮令大地は沈むとも 誠一つは世を救ふ 誠に刃向ふ仇はなし

勇めよ勇め刹帝利 従ひ玉ふ諸々の 誠の司よ悪神の 此襲撃を恐れずに 神に心を任せつつ 祈らせ玉へ惟神 神に代りて宣り伝ふ』

此言霊を聞くよりベルツは俄に慄ひ出し、駒に跨り裏門より驀地に駆け出す。此時シエールは庭石に躓き倒れた途端に、足を折り悲鳴を挙げて救ひを求めてゐる。怖気ついたる軍勢は、現在目の前に倒れた大将を見向きもやらず、土足のまま踏み越え踏み越え、シエールの身体一面泥まぶれにし乍ら、先を争うてバラバラバラと逃げ出す可笑しさ。ベルツの後に従つて大多数の軍隊は西へ西へと駆けり行く。此時向ふの方より駒に跨り驀地に馳帰つたのはヒルナ、カルナの両女であつた。続いて杢助に扮した摩利支天は、巨大な獅子に跨り数百の獅子を引連れ、ベルツが逃げ路を扼し、声を揃へて『ウーウー』と百雷の轟く如く唸り出した。ベルツは此声に驚いて馬上より真逆様に転落し、路傍にふんのびてゐる。其他の軍卒は獅子の呻り声に戦き恐れ、身体竦み大地に噛ぶりついて慄ひ戦いてゐた。ヒルナはベルツの倒れた姿を目敏くも見つけて馬の背に引括り、敵の乗り棄てた馬を見つけて、又もやヒラリと飛び乗り、カルナと共に王の一大事と驀地に戛々と裏門より勢よく帰り来たりぬ。
