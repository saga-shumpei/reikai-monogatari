Chapter 2: 蜉蝣 [1365]

蜉蝣〔1365〕

§ 1

Centering on Mount Victor in the lower reaches of the Lion River, this place was where King Victoria, who believed in the Ural Kyō, protected the people of the neighboring lands as a Setsuteiri. This kingdom was not a very large country, measuring ten ri from east to west and fifteen ri (thirty-six chō to one ri) from north to south. The name of the country was Biku. King Victoria is an old man, now nearly over seventy years of age. However, unfortunately, he had no heir. Queen Hirunahime was originally a maid to Princess Victoria, but before anyone knew it, she had caught the King's eye, gradually gained power, was praised as the flower of the castle, and was managing everything. Her age was exactly twenty-three, in the prime of her womanhood. Seeking to gain the favor of Hirunahime, many officials flattered her, the national administration became disordered day by day, the voices of the people's resentment filled the four directions, and things like peasant uprisings broke out here and there, reaching a point where they could not be brought under control, and the House of King Victoria had reached the point of imminent collapse.

The Left Guardian God, Cupid, was an extremely loyal old minister who, having suffered much for the sake of the King, was trying to protect the nation. Contrary to this, the Right Guardian God, Belts, was a treacherous and cunning villain who, having ingratiated himself with Hirunahime and offering various schemes, exerted such tyranny that he did not even hold the Setsuteiri or the hundred officials in his eyes. The opinion of Hirunahime was the opinion of Belts, and all of Belts's plots were conveyed entirely through the mouth of Hirunahime. And the Left Guardian God, Cupid, had a loyal steward named Ex, while the Right Guardian God, Belts, had a treacherous and evil steward named Ciel, and together with their master, the Right Guardian, they were racking their brains day and night, hoping to occupy the Biku Kingdom. Belts invited Ciel into his private room, sealed the chamber, and was engaged in a whispered consultation.

Belts: 'Hey, Ciel, what do you think? Hirunahime has become almost entirely a person in my pocket, but the stubborn King Victoria somehow shows signs of disliking me, and by keeping Cupid close, he tries to take a rebellious attitude toward my suggestions one by one, which must be called a major obstacle to our goal. Since it is said that he who would shoot the general must first shoot the horse, if we do not oust that Cupid or do away with him, our conspiracy, which has succeeded up to ninety-nine percent, will not only come to nothing, but it is hard to calculate what kind of severe punishment we might be subjected to instead. Is there not some good plan?'

Ciel: 'Right Guardian-dono, there is no need for such worry. King Victoria is already an old man who has crossed the slope of seventy; even if you do not rush, he cannot have much of his life left. Rather than stirring up trouble and losing the trust of the people, only to be called a gang of wicked, lawless, disloyal, and unrighteous men, I believe it is the better judgment to wait for a while, as it is only a matter of patience. If you look at the state of the people, they are suffering from the difficulty of living, the voices of resentment against heavy taxes fill the four directions, and it is impossible to tell when a riot might break out; as the momentum for revolution is growing day by day, if you do something unreasonable, it will only increase the chaos in the world. Fortunately, King Victoria has no heir, and since Hirunahime-sama is a maid who rose in status, after the King's passing, it will surely be as you wish. If you devise sufficient schemes while there is still time, and take advantage of the fact that Hirunahime-sama trusts you, by cultivating your hidden power, when the time comes, your desires will be fulfilled of their own accord. I believe that is the better judgment.'

Belts: 'That is true, but since King Victoria is physically very healthy, he will probably be fine for another twenty years or so. No matter how much you say to wait for the right time, we are filled with spirit, our arms are itching, and our hearts are pounding; we cannot endure waiting for another twenty years from now. Is there not a method or means to achieve our goal more quickly and efficiently?'

Shieru: 'I, who have earned the nickname of the Treasury of Knowledge, possess all manner of brilliant plans and ingenious strategies, yet the circumstances of today do not permit immediate action. For today, the internal chaos of the nation has reached its peak, and it is only by applying extreme restraint that we are barely maintaining the current state of affairs; therefore, I consider it the height of wisdom to wait for the right moment without acting in haste. In these days when the King's power wanes daily and there is no real strength to control the four seas, it is no easy task for any man, no matter how brilliant his schemes, to pacify this situation. Therefore, I humbly suggest that it would be the wisest course of action for us to utilize the current state of society, stir up the people by further extending our influence, leave King Victoria with no means to act, and force him to abdicate voluntarily.'

Berutsu: 'Indeed, that is a brilliant plan. In that case, Shieru, you must have a concrete proposal, do you not?'

Shieru: 'It is not that I do not have one, but as they say, one should settle future quarrels beforehand; if you become the Kshatriya, will you promise to appoint me as the Minister of the Left? Unless that is decided, I have no motivation to work.'

Berutsu: 'Hahahaha, you are a shrewd man. Once the objective is achieved, I shall certainly employ you in a high position. Please exert yourself with that to look forward to.'

Shieru: 'To be told merely that I will be employed in a high position leaves me in a state of uncertainty. I wish to receive your word of honor that you will definitely appoint me as the Minister of the Left.'

Berutsu: 'One who is to be the Kshatriya of the Biku nation must select wise ministers to entrust with the governance of the state. No matter how clever you may claim to be, Shieru, you do not yet possess the skills to manage the affairs of state. Do not indulge in such groundless worries; it is your master's command. Why do you not set about executing it?'

Shieru: 'Hahahaha, my master, you are quite a cunning person yourself. A disadvantageous situation like the hunting dog being boiled after the hunt is finished is something that a wise man like me could never allow. In short, you are saying that I lack the qualifications for the Minister of the Left, are you not? Very well, if that is the case, rather than attempting such a dangerous act of rebellion, shall I expose your conspiracy before the King? How does that sound?'

The cat, which had been slowly hiding its claws, began to show the tips of its hooked talons. Startled, Berutsu replied:

Berutsu: 'Ah, um, we cannot talk if you get so angry. In truth, I had already decided in my heart to appoint you as the Minister of the Left. However, I was merely teasing you a little to test your zeal. Hahahaha.'

Shieru: 'My master, this is no time for teasing; we are at a critical juncture where one in a thousand might survive.'

Berutsu: 'A hero has his moments of leisure; even in the midst of a campaign, he composes poems, plays the shakuhachi, and remains calm and composed, neither fearing nor shrinking, neither agitated nor impatient, resolving all things in the midst of conversation and laughter. Such is the heroic spirit. How different is the heart of a brave general who possesses both wisdom and courage. Ot-ho-ho-ho.'

Although Shieru is a villain, he is a relatively simple-minded fellow. Being skillfully licked by Berutsu's tongue, he is now flexing his arms and roaring with ambition, intending to carry out an outrageous evil deed without knowing his own limitations.

Shieru: 'Indeed, I have understood everything. Not knowing that you were such a great lord, I beg your forgiveness for my rude remarks.'

Berutsu: 'In terms of righteousness, we are master and servant, but in terms of affection, it is the relationship between parent and child. So to speak, I am the parent, and you are the child. It is the natural law of heaven and earth for a parent to love a child. And to temper the heart and gall of that child, to enlighten his knowledge, and to mold him into a man of talent by uttering harsh words and applying the whip is what one might call a wise parent's hood. From now on, regardless of the matter, I would like you to act with total self-sacrifice according to my will for a while.'

Berutsu: 'Ah, how valiant, how valiant! Your Otake, your bright future, the day you reach the Higan of your Mokuteki cannot be far off. Oh, how it gladdens my Kokochi!'

He stretched out both his Riyōte, clenched his fists, and while moving his Saiu knees in Komogomo Kifuku, he let out a roar as if to shake the floor.

Hearing the excessively loud voice, Berutsu's younger sister, Karunahime, ran to the next room, overheard the entire Danwa of the two, and while knitting her brows, with an indifferent air,

Karunahime: 'Brother, Gomen nasaimase.'

She entered. Shieru placed his Riyōte on the floor, and with great reverence,

Shieru: 'This is, Karunahime-sama, to see your Gosauken face, I, as your Karei, feel Kiyōetsu Shigoku.'

Karunahime: 'Are you not Shieru? You were talking with my brother so merrily since Saizen. Separated by the sliding door, I could not understand exactly what was said, but it seems to be a matter of no small consequence. Please, tell me about it.'

Shieru: 'Heh, no, it is nothing at all. My master and I were drunk on sake and were just telling stories of heroes from the old days. Hehehehe, it was a very interesting story, indeed.'

Karunahime: 'In those old stories, were there people such as King Bikutoria, Hime Hiruna, and the Samori of Kiyūbitsuto?'

She said this while making her gentle eyes shine, raising her tone slightly, and glaring at him. Berutsu, the Samori, felt a wave hit his heart: 'If my sister hears of this conspiracy, it will be a disaster. That sister of mine is casting amorous glances at Haruna, the son of the Samori-gami, so might she not even commit treason? When heated by love, one forgets even their own parents and siblings. How troublesome this is.' Yet, with a feigned look of ignorance,

Berutsu: 'Hahahaha, it is a... bloody old story, not something a woman should hear. You had better go to the back room quickly and read the Ramūru you like so much.'

Karunahime: 'Somehow, when I hear your talk, my heart is troubled, and I have no desire to read Historia Amoris or the like. Truly, your plan is bleak; the signs of doom are appearing on your forehead without fail.'

Berutsu: 'A woman should not come into a man's living room. Did the sage of China not say so? That men and women of seven years should not sit in the same seat? Now, go away quickly.'

Karunahime: 'My, my, your mouth is quite convenient, is it not? In truth, I have heard the whole situation from the beginning. No matter how much you try to hide it, it is already too late.'

Berutsu: 'Tch, what a troublesome sister. Oi, Karuna, do you have no intention of helping your brother?'

Berutsu: 'Umm, let me see, I shall think about it.'

Karunahime: 'It must be very troublesome for you to give your sister to Haruna-sama, the son of Samori-sama, whom you target as a thorn in your side and an obstacle to your Mokuteki. However, yours can never be recognized as legitimate... but the conjugal love I claim is the right of a legitimate wife, so you cannot simply refuse this problem without reason. Tell me, Shieru, is that not so?'

She turned her words toward the steward.

Shieru: 'Indeed, Hime-sama's words contain not a shred of contradiction. Nay, I sympathize with them greatly. In that regard, there is something you must consider, Hime-sama. Just as you love Haruna-sama passionately, do you also love Samorinokami?'

Karunahime: 'He is the father of my beloved husband, so rather than love, I pay him respect.'

Shieru: 'Compared to the degree to which you respect your elder brother, there must be a vast difference, must there not?'

Karunahime: 'That is certainly so; is it not said that siblings are the beginning of strangers? Once I become a married couple with Haruna-sama and a child is born, a truly intimate parent-child relationship will be formed, so it is only natural that I respect Samorinokami-sama more than my brother.'

Shieru: 'Nay, this is outrageous. Pray, Hime-sama, think back to the origin. Is your elder brother not your true sibling? Haruna-sama is a complete stranger. You have only become a married couple and taken the name of parent and child through the formality of marriage. If you do not think carefully about that, a tragedy where blood washes blood might break out against your own brother. I would like you to place your hand on your heart and think it over well.'

Karunahime: 'Yes, I shall give you an answer after careful consideration.'

Berutsu: 'Do not forget that you are siblings born from the same womb, a bond that cannot be severed. Ah, what a troublesome sister. This is why it is troublesome to provide higher education to women. My parents were fond of new things, so they ended up making you into such a harlot.'

Karunahime: 'Ho ho ho ho, it is not only me; they even provided higher education to you, elder brother, and made you into such a villain.'

Berutsu: 'Hey, you, Karuna, you had better think carefully and consider the pros and cons. You must not do anything that will surely not benefit us siblings.'

Karunahime: 'Yes, I understand. Please, for the sake of us siblings, do not think of obstructing the love between siblings. Ho ho ho ho, if that is the case, you two, please think it over thoroughly. And I would like you to reform any behavior that shames your conscience as soon as possible. I ask you to reconsider once more, so that you do not act like a hyena in bedclothes and leave behind regrets that you cannot even lick your own navel.'

She admonished the two and, with composure, returned to her own room. Left behind, the two heaved sighs in bewilderment, and for a while, a curtain of silence unfolded.
