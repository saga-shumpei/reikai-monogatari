Chapter 23: 純潔 [1386]

純潔〔1386〕

§ 1

Harukuniwake first rescued Haruna, the sōshireikuwan within the jiyōnai, who had been taken prisoner by the enemy and bound to a nihaki; then, guided by Haruna, he was conducted deep into the denchiu to the ima of the King. Here, the King himself, along with Samori, Umori, and Taruman, were praying with itsushiyōkenmei before the shinzen.

Haruna: 'Setsuteirisama, thanks to the Kamisama, at the critical moment of a kikiitsupatsu, we were saved by the party of Harukuniwake-sama, the Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō. This is Harukuniwake-sama.'

He introduced him thus. The King first invited Harukuniwake to the jiyōza, urging him to come this way. Harukuniwake declined, saying that this spot was quite sufficient, and did not sit in the jiyōseki. The King, while urging him to take the jiyōza, saying 'Well, well,' spoke:

Setsuteiri: 'In this time of kikifusonbau, I am truly grateful. You are a living god who has saved the land of Biku. We shall never forget this great favor, no matter how much time passes. Now, please relax and rest at your leisure.'

Harukuniwake bowed his head politely, and said:

Harukuniwake: 'I am meeting you for the first time. I am overwhelmed by such polite greetings from your noble self to us Sendenshi. By no means do we possess the power to save your country. It was by the divine power of Izunomitama and Mizunomitama that the evil enemies were easily routed, so please offer your thanks to the Kamisama.'

Setsuteiri: 'Yes, I have no words to express my thanks. Izunomitama-sama, Mizunomitama-sama, Bankoshin'ō-sama, I thank you.'

Thus he joined his hands in prayer, shedding tears of joy.

Samori: 'I am Kyūbitto, the Samori serving the King. You have truly helped us together with the Kamisama in this great calamity. Furthermore, you even saved the life of my imperiled son, for which my gratitude is boundess. Ah, kannagara tamachi haimase.'

And he was overwhelmed with tears.

Harukuniwake: 'While I was undergoing divine training at the Tēmusu Pass, Kamu-Susanoo-no-Mikoto appeared and commanded me, "Thou must turn thy way as quickly as possible and go to the capital of Biku to save Setsuteiri-dono from his danger." Without even taking time to gather our belongings, I rushed over with my three disciples, and seeing this situation of kikifusonbau, we were able to perform a splendid service.'

Samori: 'The divine will of the Ōkami-sama and the kindness of your entire party cannot be thanked enough with words.'

And he was once again overwhelmed with tears of joy.

Umori: 'I am Ekusu, serving as the Umori. My gratitude cannot be expressed in words. I humbly request your continued guidance and kindness without forsaking us in the future.'

Harukuniwake: 'Let us best wish well to each other.'

Taruman: 'I am a Sendenshi of the Ural religion, and having been honored with the trust of Setsuteirisama, I also serve as the minister of internal affairs; however, in this time of emergency, due to my lack of divine virtue, I was helpless and at my wit's end. I am truly grateful that you have saved us. If you would kindly add me as your disciple, I would be exceedingly grateful.'

Harukuniwake: 'Is that so? You are a Sendenshi of the Ural religion; thank you for your hard work. In this regard, not only you, but how would it be if Setsuteirisama were also to hear the teachings of Ananai-kyō? In the shrine grove of Ananai-kyō, Kunitokotachi-no-Ōkami-sama, Bankoshin'ō-sama, and Daijizaiten-sama are enshrined; though the name of the teaching changes, there is not the slightest difference in the Kamisama.'

Taruman: 'I humbly request your favor in every way. Now, Setsuteirisama, what do you think?'

Setsuteiri: 'Needless to say, let us place ourselves under the care of Harukuniwake-sama. Yes, Harukuniwake-sama, please treat us favorably in every way. Consequently, everyone in the jiyōnai, beginning with Samori and Umori, will gather and submit to your religion, so please intercede for us with the Ōkami-sama.'

Harukuniwake: 'Yes, I have understood.'

Katsuhiko: 'Master, how about presenting a song of celebration?'

Setsuteiri: 'Saved by the majestic power of the Sumekami, Now my heart has become clear and serene. Nevertheless, where is Hirunahime now? I wish to search for her as she wanders. Karnahime must surely be weeping now, Yearning for her husband in the fields and in the mountains.'

Haruna: 'Even if my wife exposes her corpse in the fields, She would not mind it for the sake of you, my Lord. The army of the Magatsuhi, Beltsu, has fled and scattered, And it is a very peaceful state within the castle.'

While they were thus exchanging songs, the sound of horses' hooves returned gallantly to the front gate; it was Hirunahime and Karnahime, whom Setsuteiri and Haruna could not forget for even a moment. The two of them unloaded the body of Beltsu inside the gate, left the guards to watch over him, leaped off their horses, and entered the King's living room with light and cheerful steps. The King was startled and overjoyed to see the two of them.

Setsuteiri: 'Oh, you are Hirunahime! You have returned safely. Well, well, wonderful, wonderful. You have truly gone through much hardship. Oh, and you are Karnahime! You have endured until now and served for the sake of the Royal House and the country. I have nothing to say but this.'

Thus, he pressed his palms together to express his gratitude. The two shed tears of joy, which fell pitter-patter, and prostrated themselves weeping on the spot. Samori-no-tsukasa and Haruna were so overjoyed they seemed almost driven mad; they stood up, sat down, and ran around the room to the left and right carrying a brazier. When one reaches the height of joy, even a wise man loses his composure and becomes flustered.

Taruman: 'Samori-dono, Haruna-dono, please calm yourselves.'

Being thus cautioned, Haruna gently set down the brazier he was carrying.

Haruna: 'You are Hirunahime-sama, and you were Karna? How did you return?'

He was thus submerged in tears of joy. Karna, in her excess of joy, shed tears that fell rapidly and remained looking downward.

Setsuteiri: 'How did you return? You must have surely faced great difficulties.'

Hirunahime: 'Yes, thank you. When we reached the foot of Shimeji Pass, Marishiten-sama appeared and became several hundred lions, throwing the Brahman army into confusion; because of that, we were able to escape and return successfully.'

Setsuteiri: 'Indeed, it is the help of the Kami. Ah, how grateful, how grateful. Kannagara Tamachihaemase. Karna, you too must have suffered much along with Hiruna. Both I and Haruna know the hearts of you two very well. You are truly the paragons of virtuous and chaste women.'

§ 4

When examining that countenance, it is the unexpected Ikusagimi of Beltsu. At any rate, placing him upon a horse, she determined her heart to return. When she returned and looked upon the Onyakata, the traces of a fierce battle upon the Kogane castle and the iron walls appeared most vividly. Thinking this was no Tadagoto, she dismounted from her horse, cast the Majin of Beltsu upon the ground, left him to be guarded by the Eihei, and together with Karuna, she hurried back. When she returned and looked, her lord was sitting in great health. Furthermore, the hundred Tsukasara also, healthy as always without change, surrounded the lord, sitting there looking so joyful. Ah, how Arigata, how Arigata, rejoicing in the Kami's grace.

In the very moment of offering Kanshiya in her heart, she heard that the Kamuzukasa Harukuniwake had appeared and saved the army of her lord; oh, the joy of that time! Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara. Before the precious Kami, Hirunahime, defiled by sin, in awe and reverence before the presence, rejoices and offers Kanshiya for the preciousness of the Ōmimegumi. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara. May the Mitama of the Kami grant Sachiha, and may this mansion eternally, and the Kokishi of Biku forever, live long and prosper. May the hundred Kunibito in peace and great tranquility prosper; protect them, O Ōmikami, I pray with all my Magokoro.'

Thus she finished singing.

From this time, Harukuniwake, as well as Mankō, Matsuhiko, and Tatsuhiko, by the Konjiyō of Setsuteiri, guided the executives and others within the castle in the doctrines and rituals of Anai, newly established a divine shrine and a teaching hall, and in the middle of summer, the entire group set out toward Jerusalem, following after Kishunwake and the others.
