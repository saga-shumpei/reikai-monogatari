Chapter 11: 艶兵 [1374]

艶兵〔1374〕

§ 1

Shayam, whom Oniharuwake relies upon as his Kokō, attacked the Jiyōnai with Matsushigura, cutting through and Nagitafu the Guntai led by Haruna one after another. The Mikata within the castle fell into Shiushiyōraubai, threw away their Buki, and in their Hikefu, scattered in Ichimokusan to the Shihau and Hatsupau. Haruna, brandishing a spear, entered the enemy's Jinchiu and fought with Jiuōmujin, but the enemy was an overwhelming Taigun; finally, his strength exhausted, he suffered Jifusūsau, and while gritting his teeth in Munen, he collapsed with a thud. Shayam ordered his Buka to bind him with Takate and Kote to make him a prisoner, and threw him into the storehouse within the castle to keep him bound. From there, he advanced into the King's Denchiu with the force of an Ashiyuraō, captured the Umori, Belts, without difficulty, and had him bound in the same storehouse where Haruna had been thrown. King Victoria and Cupid notched arrows to their bows and struck down seven or eight of the approaching enemy. The King's bowstring snapped with a pop; thinking his fate had reached its end, at the very moment he drew his short sword to commit suicide, the Left Guard stopped his hand from the bow, clung to the King's hand, and with tears, pleaded with him to abandon the thought of suicide.

Left Guard: 'Moshi, my Lord, do not be so short-tempered. As long as we have the protection of the Gods, this battle can surely be recovered. If you were to pass away, how could the three armies be commanded? Please, for the sake of the nation, refrain from death.'

As he tried to stop him with all his might, the King showed a look of determination.

Kshatriya: 'At this juncture, to attempt to preserve my life in such a Hikefu and cowardly manner, and instead offer my head to nameless common soldiers, would be a disgrace to the royal house; release your hand.'

Left Guard: 'No, I will not release it.'

As they argued, Shayam advanced, and without allowing any room for debate, captured both of them along with several dozen common soldiers, gagged them, bound them in the same storehouse, and led the Brahman army in three cheers of Banzai.

Princess Karuna, seeing that it was impossible to contend with the enemy's power, suddenly discarded her armor, adorned her beautiful body, donned a straw cloak and hat, disguised herself as a traveler, and lay down on the roadside where the army was advancing, letting out groans on purpose.

Emishi, the adjutant to General Kumehiko, leading over a hundred troops, saw someone lying on the roadside where they were advancing and ordered his Buka, Maruta, to investigate.

Maruta: 'Hey you, lying on the roadside of our advance, this is highly improper; who are you?'

Saying this, he roughly tore off the straw cloak and hat, and saw a young, beautiful woman groaning in pain, 'Un, un.'

Maruta: 'Moshi, Emishi-sama, she is a beauty of incomparable splendor. She appears to be a traveler, but perhaps she was startled by the force of such a large army and, due to a woman's timid heart, has fainted; what a beautiful person she is.'

Emishi: 'Indeed, she is a magnificent woman. In any case, what if we take her before Kumehiko-sama and offer her to the General for his comfort?'

Maruta: 'Indeed, the General will surely be satisfied. In that case, I shall deliver her from here.'

Emishi: 'Maruta, you must never claim this as your own achievement... tell him that Emishi said he was delivering her to the General-sama.'

Maruta: 'Hehehe, there shall be no lack of tact, please rest assured.'

Having three or four Buka carry her, Maruta followed behind and sent her to the General's temporary camp. Emishi, pointing toward the Jiyōnai, set fire to the houses in the vicinity and advanced with the force of a fierce tiger, riding the momentum of victory.

Meanwhile, Princess Hiruna, seeing that there was no hope of victory, likewise disguised herself as a traveler and lay on the road where the army was advancing. Following the precedent of the Great God who used the red-skirted troop of peach fruits in the battle of Yomotsuhirasaka, she risked danger out of pure loyalty, hoping to persuade the enemy's commander-in-chief with her beauty and eloquence. The one who came marching majestically to this place, having organized his ranks, was Spahr, whom Oniharuwake relied upon as his Kokō. Spahr, sharp-eyed, saw Princess Hiruna, was stunned by her beauty, stopped the advance of the army, and approached before Princess Hiruna.

Supāru: 'What are you doing on the path of the Shingun? Will you not Tachisa at once?'

He scolded in a loud voice on purpose.

Hirunahime: 'Yes, I am a woman on a journey. I have come from far away to perform Sanpai at the shrine of Bankoshinnōsama on Mount Bikutoru, but I was so overwhelmed by the sight of so many Bukesama that I lost my nerve, my legs gave way, and I could not take a single Hitoashi further. Dōzo, I beg you to help me. As I am a woman who carries no Suntetsu on her person, I shall offer no Temuka.'

She said this while Namidagu. Supāru Jiyukushi the Bibau of Hirunahime, and while tilting his head and clicking his tongue, he stared at her, entranced.

After a while, Supāru softened his Kahoiro and said,

Supāru: 'Nay, Jiyochiu of the journey, do not have any Goshinpai. Setsushiya shall protect your person in Anzen. ...Jiyūsotsu, go and deliver this woman to the Onmahe of General Oniharuwake, and tell him that Supāru has entrusted her to his care.'

'Yes,' they replied, and the two Heisotsu from the Zenretsu, together with two Jiyūsotsu, carried Hirunahime with great care and delivered her to the camp of General Oniharuwake.

The camps of the two generals, Oniharuwake and Kumehiko, were set up temporarily on the young grass, surrounded by tents. The two generals were separated by only a single tent, with a distance of about two ken between them. General Kumehiko was listening to the battle reports of his allies while spreading out the maps of the inside and outside of Bikutoria Castle to examine the deployment of the enemy and his own forces. Just then, Maruta entered with the beautiful woman being carried by four Heisotsu, and said,

Maruta: 'Eh, I report to you, General. It appears that the enemy within the castle has been almost entirely annihilated, so please be at ease. Furthermore, as I found a woman who, not knowing where she was from, had collapsed by the roadside, terrified by the might of our army, and had lost her senses, I thought it would not be the way of a warrior to simply ignore her. When I approached, I found such a beautiful woman of young age, and after much effort to nurse her back to health, she finally breathed again. However, your adjutant, Emishi-dono, narrowed his eyes the moment he saw her and began to drool... saying such selfish things as, "What a pity, I would like to make this woman my wife to comfort me in the boredom of the camp." But it was I, Maruta, who found her, nursed her, and picked her up. In a manner of speaking, she is like a war trophy, and I shall not let Emishi have his way with her. I wish to present her to you, General, to serve as a comfort in the camp, and so I have gone to the trouble of sending her here. I would be grateful if you would inspect her and accept her.'

He spoke, piling on the flattery. Kumehiko was enchanted the moment he saw her, narrowing his eyes, unaware that he was drooling. However, as his superior, Oniharuwake, was stationed in the adjacent tent, he raised his voice sharply on purpose and said,

Kumehiko: 'This is most improper! To bring a woman into this camp is an act unbecoming of a warrior. It is filthy! Take her away at once!'

Maruta: 'Heh, does this beauty not please you, contrary to your usual nature? If that is the case, then it cannot be helped. Until this war is over, I shall hide her somewhere, make her my wife, retire from the army, and spend a happy life with this nice woman. Even if one is a soldier, abandoning a woman is not the path a warrior should take. If she does not please you, I shall take her elsewhere.'

He signaled to the four men to take her back. Kumehiko, in a panic, waved his hands repeatedly and said,

Kumehiko: 'Ah, no, no! When I said it was filthy, that was only for show. Quietly leave that woman here, and you had best head to the front lines as quickly as possible.'

Maruta: 'Heh heh heh, so you did like her after all. Like a cat with a bonito, or a man with a woman—no matter how much of a soldier one is, there is no man who dislikes women. However, there are many men who are drooling and wanting her, so I will not force someone upon you if you do not desire her. To offer such a beauty to you, only to be met with a face like you are biting into a bitter bug—there is no point in my efforts or my trouble at all.'

Kumehiko: 'A soldier is only good for making war. Stop your grumbling, leave at once, and head to the front lines. What an outrageous thing you are.'

He shouted this deliberately so that it could be heard in the next room. Maruta puffed up his face, and while muttering complaints, he slunk away and entered the camp once more.

Kumehiko assigned various tasks to the staff officers in the vicinity to keep them at a distance, then drew close to the woman, stroked her back, and spoke in a wheedling, cat-like voice.

Kumehiko: 'Where are you from? You must have been terribly frightened to encounter such a bloodthirsty army. I am General Kumehiko, the commander of the entire force. Now that you have come under my protection, you are safe; set your mind at ease.'

Woman: 'Yes, I am called Karuna, and I am a native of this land. While I was on my way to pay my respects to Bankoshinnōsama, whom I worship daily, I encountered many warriors along with my companion. I was startled, my eyes grew dim, and I collapsed by the roadside. But then, I was saved by a kind-hearted warrior, and I have never known such joy. I wonder if I might return home without further worry; if possible, could I receive a token from you, sir, so that I might pass through the army and be allowed to return to my country?'

Kumehiko, fearing that he would lose this beauty he had finally obtained, immediately devised a plan.

Kumehiko: 'A warrior considers recognizing compassion as his primary duty. However, as various unsavory soldiers are currently mixed in here, it is truly perilous. Why not stay in my camp until this battle is settled? I believe that is the safest course for your own sake. For now, just consider yourself under the care of a parent, calm your spirit, and take your ease.'

Karuna-hime: 'Yes, thank you very much. If that is the case, I shall accept your kind offer and trouble you for your care.'

Kumehiko: 'Good, good. With that, I too am finally at ease.'

Karuna-hime: 'What a pleasant atmosphere it has become. To see the General, who commands the three armies, with his tent pitched upon this green grass and his camp established—your heroic figure truly stirs a feeling that is beyond words. As a woman, once born, I truly wish to become the wife of a soldier, ho ho ho.'

Kumehiko: 'It seems you are still unmarried, then.'

Karuna-hime: 'Yes. Since the men of today all talk of the sanctity of love, democracy, or aesthetic living, while in truth they are all just men as rotten as women, I cannot find a man to settle upon as my husband, so I remain in single life.'

Kumehiko: 'So, the husband you idealize is a soldier, then? There is nothing as simple, pure, and brave as a soldier. If you are to have a husband, it must be a soldier, ha ha ha ha.'

Karuna-hime: 'No matter how much someone like me might wish to have a soldier for a husband, it is no use. There are many kinds of soldiers, and from the General above to the common private below, they are all soldiers, but I would absolutely refuse a soldier who polishes boots or cleans horses. I am hoping to have a husband who is at least an officer.'

Kumehiko held his nose and said,

Kumehiko: 'Am I not to your liking?'

Karuna-hime: 'Oh my, what are you saying? That is too much of an honor. I am satisfied with an officer-class man. Are you not of the general-class, General? It would be a sin to even dream of such a thing, ho ho ho ho ho. Please do not tease me so. Won't you, General?'

Kumehiko abandoned his strategic plans and maps, forgot that Oni-haruwake was waiting in the next tent, and with a soft, foolish, and doting voice, he drew Karuna toward his knees and began to stroke her shoulders.
