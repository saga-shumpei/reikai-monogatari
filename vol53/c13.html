醜嵐〔1376〕

§ 1

Through the mediation of Supāru and Emishi, the sword fight between the two generals, Oniharuwake and Kumehiko, finally subsided. The two generals sat on chairs, wiping away sweat while their hearts were still pounding.

Supāru turned to Oniharuwake and said respectfully,

Supāru: 'Shiyōgunsama, why are you fighting? You possess a noble status, expected to command the three armies and set a model for your subordinates; what is the meaning of this state of affairs? I believe there must be some reason for this, and if you would disclose it to this adjutant without concealment, I intend to devise the best possible solution as your subordinate.'

Oniharuwake blushed and spoke as if it were difficult to say,

Oniharuwake: 'No, it is nothing of great importance. Having too much idle time, I was merely practicing Gekiken with Kumehiko-dono. Ahahahaha.'

Supāru: 'If it were Gekiken practice, why did you not carry Shinai? To draw Shinken against each other and engage in Ōchiahi is Kennonsenban; had my arrival been even a little later, there is no telling what fate might have befallen both generals. Should the army lose its pillars in such a manner, military discipline would instantly collapse, and the soldiers would fall into disarray. Please, I beg you to cease such reckless play.'

Oniharuwake: 'Ahahahaha, how terrible... I have caused you worry. It was just that the provocation turned into a serious matter, and it was a pointless affair.'

Emishi turned to Kumehiko and said,

Emishi: 'Shiyōgunsama, did you truly engage in Gekiken as General Oniharuwake just stated?'

Kumehiko spoke as if it were difficult to say,

Kumehiko: 'Hmm, if you call it Gekiken, it is Gekiken, but in truth, because General Oniharuwake intended to disrupt military law, I attempted to cut him down with a single stroke. Just when I was a moment away, you people arrived and caused such a nuisance. Ahahahaha.'

Emishi: 'I cannot believe these are the words of a general. As your adjutant, I have served you faithfully until now, but even if General Oniharuwake has committed some impropriety, is it right to act so violently as to turn a blade against him? From this moment, I shall leave your service and sympathize with General Oniharuwake. What is that look upon your face? Your entire face is distorted, your eyes are upturned, your complexion has faded, and your lips have turned purple. In contrast, General Oniharuwake has not changed his expression in the slightest, remaining composed and maintaining the attitude of a hero. ...Oniharuwake-sama, I do not mind even if it is as the lowest of soldiers. I wish for you to employ me under your direct command.'

Oniharuwake: 'Later, I shall consult thoroughly with Kumehiko-dono, and after hearing his opinions, if Kumehiko-dono has no objections, I shall make you my subordinate.'

Supāru: 'According to my humble opinion, I believe this conflict is a struggle for the possession of Hiruna-hime and Karuna-hime, who are here. Since I rescued Hiruna-hime-sama on the way and presented her to Oniharuwake-sama, there should be no dispute regarding her. Furthermore, as Emishi rescued Karuna-hime and presented her to General Kumehiko, it was a settled matter from the beginning. I do not know what conflict of opinion you two generals have, but since this is a gift from Heaven, why not handle it in such a way?'

Oniharuwake smiled and said,

Oniharuwake: 'Indeed, as Supāru says, if we do that, there is no problem. Kumehiko-dono, what do you think? You should have no objection to this, right?'

Kumehiko: 'Yes, there is no need to argue. In that case, I shall be content with Karuna. Just as a temporary stopgap.'

Hearing this, Karuna-hime deliberately arched her willow-leaf eyebrows and said,

Karuna-hime: 'Look here, General Kumehiko, I am a woman of standing; under such conditions as being a temporary stopgap or even just Karuna-hime, I cannot entrust my body to you. It appears you are a person who does not know that love is best. Have you not fully exposed the nature of a wicked man who has many hearts and treats women as playthings? You are a philanderer, like duckweed. I flatly refuse you, and I intend to ask General Oniharuwake, who understands the true essence of love, to employ me, even if it be as a maid. Please, let us end our connection here. Rather than entrust my body to such a heartless person, I would be much happier living a life of celibacy. Your love is what is called a false love.'

Being rebuffed so severely, Kumehiko Shiyōgun once again placed his hand upon his hilt and, in a fit of rage, attempted to strike Karunahime down with a single stroke. Seeing this, Emishi gripped Kumehiko's hand firmly.

Emishi: 'Shiyōgundono, your opponent is a woman. Pray, compose yourself.'

Kumehiko gave a reluctant 'Hmph' and sat down in his chair.

Karunahime: 'Ho ho ho ho ho, how unmanly, how utterly contemptible you are, Kumehiko Shiyōgun-sama. To draw your blade against a single, frail woman—such cowardice, such lingering attachment, I have grown truly sick of it. Ho ho ho ho ho. If I said to Oniharuwake Shiyōgun-sama that I wished to serve as a maid, that was merely for appearances; I beseech you, please love me eternally as your wife.'

Oniharuwake assumed the air of a lady-killer.

Oniharuwake: 'Ah ha ha ha ha, how very adorable. However, as I have already reserved the treasure that is Hirunahime, I have no choice but to decline your request, despite your earnestness. If Hirunahime were to permit it, I might not object to taking you as a second wife, though.'

So saying, he cast a glance at Hirunahime. Hirunahime deliberately arched her willow-leaf brows and sharpened her voice.

Hirunahime: 'Now, Shiyōgunsama, what a heartless person you are. Everything you said to me was a lie, was it not? Your nature is that of an Amanjaku, so you must be laying your hands on every woman you see, whether it be this one or that one. True love is a matter of one against one. Though I may look this way, I am certainly no harlot, so I will not tolerate such cannibalistic misconduct. You have dealt a painful insult to a woman, have you not?'

Oniharuwake: 'Ah, no, no, I cannot bear it if you get so angry. That was merely a joke. Do you think I could say such a thing in your presence? Think it over well. You are truly a woman, after all.'

Hirunahime: 'Is it possible for one who commands three armies to speak in jest? If you utter such nonsense, you will not be able to maintain the countenance of your army. How could you possibly control your subordinates? Think carefully. Since I have declared my intention to become your wife, Shiyōgunsama, I possess the authority to offer you sufficient caution.'

Oniharuwake: 'Ah ha ha ha ha, thanks to the remonstrance of the wise Hirunahime, I, Oniharuwake, seem to have awakened. What a clever woman you are.'

Hirunahime: 'Do you still intend to make use of Karuna, no matter what?'

Oniharuwake: 'That is so. Since I have been asked, I cannot simply refuse her outright. I think I shall let her serve as a maid. It would be inconvenient for you, too, if you had no lady-in-waiting.'

Hirunahime: 'Shiyōgunsama, I have no need for a lady-in-waiting. I shall perform all the duties of a maid myself. If you keep even a female cat by your side, this Hiruna will not stand for it.'

Oniharuwake: 'Ah ha ha ha ha, what a jealous woman you are. They say that jealousy causes a woman to ruin great things. You would do well to understand that a little. There is nothing that damages a woman's virtue as much as jealousy.'

Hirunahime: 'A marital relationship without jealousy is not true love. A woman who does not feel jealousy surely has something else going on. It is the behavior of a faithless person living a triangular life. Jealousy is what manifests the sanctity of love.'

Oniharuwake: 'Ah ha ha ha ha, having been struck so severely with a men, kote, dō, and tsuki, even a hero has no choice but to retreat. How troublesome it is to be born a handsome man. Ehehehehe.'

Karunahime: 'Oniharuwake Shiyōgun-sama, no matter what you say, I shall follow after you. I beseech you, please make use of me, even if it be only as your mistress.'

Hirunahime: 'Now, Karuna-sama, if you are truly that much in love with Oniharuwake-sama, it would be laughable for me to be told that I, the mistress of the master, have snatched away your love; so, please, do become the legal wife of Oniharuwake-sama. I, for my part, shall have Kumehiko Shiyōgunsama take me as his legal wife.'

The skill with which both women aligned their intentions to manipulate the two generals was extraordinary. The two generals, completely ensnared by love, had bloodshot eyes and were holding their breath in the midst of a fierce struggle for the prize. Kumehiko Shiyōgun had been rebuffed by the maid Karuna-hime, and feeling dejected and losing his sense of manhood, he felt a strength as if he had gained a million reinforcements upon hearing Hirunahime say that she would have him take her as his legal wife; he immediately let his face brim with pride.

Kumehiko: 'Heh-heh-heh-heh, Hirunahime-dono, I too am a Shiyōgun, and if it is your desire, I shall grant your request. There is a proverb that says one should try living with a person and try riding a horse; perhaps you might find yourself much happier with me than by entrusting your body to a broom-tree like Shiyōgun Oniharuwake.'

Hirunahime: 'Yes, thank you very much. If you would wish for that, I would be truly happy. The true essence of Marido-love cannot be called complete unless there is a mutual understanding of spirits between the two, you see.'

Oniharuwake, sensing that the situation with Hirunahime had turned somewhat strange, began to knit his brows once again. Karuna-hime, feigning anger, spoke.

Karuna-hime: 'Now, Hiruna-sama, even if you say you are the mistress, you cannot usurp my love. I did not mean what I said to Kumehiko Shiyōgunsama from the bottom of my heart. I was merely acting out of jealousy and being difficult. Now, Shiyōgunsama, since you and I have a prior engagement, please do not let yourself be taken in by someone like Hiruna-sama.'

Kumehiko, blinded by love, did not notice in the slightest that he was being toyed with by the two women, and in his pride, he said:

Kumehiko: 'Heh-heh-heh-heh, ah, this is a dilemma. ...If I favor this one, the other is displeased; if I favor the other, this one is displeased; if I favor both, I myself shall not stand. ...It is a difficult thing to be a handsome man. Now, Oniharuwake-dono, though it may be humble, I shall fulfill our promise once; I shall take Karuna as my wife, and then I shall present the used goods to your Excellency, so please accept them with joy as befitting your spiritual status. Heh-heh-heh, this is entirely my kindness toward my superior; surely you cannot have any complaints.'

Oniharuwake, with a face like that of Enma having swallowed tobacco resin, widened his giant eyes, shuddered, placed his hand on the hilt of his sword, and with his face dyed deep red, let his killing intent swell.

Hirunahime: 'Kumehiko-sama, have some limit to your conceit. You should be content with the maid Karuna; I only said such things to test the degree of Shiyōgun Oniharuwake-sama's love. Never from the bottom of my heart would I send amorous glances to a person like you. I am sorry, but please consult with your own face. Tell me, Oniharuwake-sama, if one compares you and Kumehiko-sama, it is like the moon and a turtle, or clouds and mud; your characters are entirely different, are they not?'

Oniharuwake immediately relaxed his expression and turned into a smiling face. The countenances of the two generals were tossed about freely by the two women, changing like the autumn sky—now clearing, now drizzling—and the speed of their transformation was as if one were watching a revolving lantern.

Oniharuwake: 'Hey, Hirunahime, you are quite a wicked person, aren't you? I had heard that women who have received a modern education are not to be dealt with easily, but this is truly impressive.'

Hirunahime: 'Ho-ho-ho-ho, for a woman of today, this is merely the beginning of the evening. I am a lady who was praised as the most virtuous at the Takatake-ji Girls' School, you know. If you think I am lying, please go to the school and check my memorial. In the conduct record, it is clearly written: ...Virtuous and graceful, obedient and loving to friends, intimate with others, wisdom shining like the sun and moon, eyes like exquisite jewels, emitting a light from the pupils that overwhelms others, skin white to the utmost, ears normal, nose positioned correctly in the center of the face, crimson lips, agate-like teeth, height neither too tall nor too short, skin soft, and the beauty of the curves of her body is unparalleled in the world... Ho-ho-ho-ho.'
