Chapter 8: 連理 [1371]

連理〔1371〕

§ 1

Shinrau, the bridegroom, stood up, held a fan in one hand, and began to sing and dance.

Haruna: 'In Takaamahara appearing / The Onmegumi of Sumeōkami / Shihonagahiko having appeared / The Keiji of Kefu without incident / How joyful that You have bound us together / For until now, the two of us / Our fathers were Tekidōshi / In various ways and for various reasons / The wretchedness of having clashed / To settle this Sanjiyō / From a young Jibun / I had been worried and troubled / But how fortunate, Karuna-hime / Falling into a love of mutual affection with me / The Inguwa of a body that could not be abandoned / By what kind of Suguse and Innen / Our fathers were Tekidōshi / That the Kohiji would never be fulfilled / Even if we cannot be together in this world / Dying, in the Mirai, affectionately / Dragging our hands to the bottom of Jigoku / Thinking we would fall together / I spent my days in tears of love / But through the Rikairiyoku of father and brother / By good fortune, here and now / We have tied the Oshi bond / How joyful is the outcome of this Koyoi / In the heavens becoming birds of Hiyoku / On earth becoming branches of Renri / Husband and wife living in harmony / Not to mention father and brother / To the awe-inspiring Sovereign, with a sincere heart / Devotedly and purely serve / Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara / May Your spirits be blessed / Protect the bond of these two forever / Without ever letting it fray / Circling the Heavenly Pillar / Surrounding the Pillar of the Land / In the Keirin of heaven and earth / Serving, and bearing many children / Pray for the prosperity of the house of Samori / Forever and ever / Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara / Before the great presence of Shihonagahiko / With awe and reverence, I pray.'

He finished singing. Ekkusu, the steward of the house of Samori, danced with joy and sang a celebratory song following his lead.

Ekkusu: 'Even if the morning sun shines or clouds over / Even if the moon waxes or wanes / Even if the stars fall from the sky / Even if earthquakes, thunder, and fire-carts / Should all come at once joyful that today they have happily reconciled / And are sitting together at this banquet / Kamunao-bi who created this world / And Ōnao-bi with a broad heart / In all things in the world of men / See straight, hear straight / Correct the errors of the world / Though this is a song of the teachings / Of the San-go / Applying it to such an occasion / I celebrate today's banquet / May you both live long / For the sake of the country above / And to protect the peace of the house below / Live together in harmony, Kannagara / To never forget / The joy of this day, this Ekkusu / Will dance, leap, and sing / Drunk on sake, not knowing / Front from back, I will dance until my life's end / Ah, how grateful, how grateful / Karuna-hime and Haruna-sama / You must be so happy / That your daily thoughts have reached each other / That you, a couple in mutual love, are living in harmony / And enjoying your new life / It is entirely the protection of the gods / If you ever forget / The divine grace even in a dream / This wonderful good match / Might break halfway / So that there is no such sorrow / From today, renew your hearts / Revere Sumeōkami / Keep the pure teachings well / Loyalty to the Sovereign, filial piety to parents / Compassion even to neighbors / The duty of a human being / Who was created in the image of God / Please fulfill it, the steward of the Samori house / Ekkusu, with a sincere heart / Offers this advice / Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara / May your spirits be blessed.'

Shieru sang again.

Shieru: 'Appearing as the official of Uwamori / The steward of the office of Berutsu / Shieru, with a sincere heart here / Offers this, and celebrates today's wedding / With joy / Holding the power of military horses / The man of power in the land of Biku / The authority of Lord Berutsu / Shining like the morning sun / Enough to bring down even flying birds / Receiving Karuna-hime / The younger sister of the official of Berutsu / As his wife, Haruna-sama / You must be, indeed, indeed, very satisfied / The steward Shieru, seeing the two of you / With such happy faces / Has finally felt at ease / And somehow, it makes me feel / As if I am becoming envious.'

§ 2

Is this not, after all, what people call Hafukairinki?

A handsome man and a beautiful woman, famous in the Sekai,

Is there such a match anywhere else in the Sanzensekai?

Karunahime, who has been made to resemble the face of Konohanahime,

My eyes are dazzled even looking at her figure,

I feel as if a Gokuō is shining upon her.

If I were a little younger,

And thought I could receive such a beautiful Niyōbau,

I feel somehow weary of this Ukiyo.

Like a Tokage that has let out an Akubi,

A pockmarked mountain Kami,

Forced to take on four or five,

Gaki rolling about, created one after another,

Chased by Seikuwatsunan,

In a state of Aoikitoiki, such a Teitaraku,

Truly, the Unmei of Ningen,

Is there such a Kenkaku as this?

Even though we have gone to the trouble of being born as humans,

And boast of being the flowers of Tenchi and the Reichiyō of Banbutsu,

Though we spend the same Tsukihi,

That there is such a division between happiness and unhappiness,

Is it the retribution of the sins of a previous life?

In truth, my body is a worthless Shieru,

Compared to the Tsukasa of Haruna,

There is a difference between heaven and earth.

However, even if I complain of such things,

It is of no use.

Resigning myself that it is a matter of In-en,

For today's splendid wedding,

With a sincere heart, I pray for your happiness forever.

Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara,

May your spirits be blessed.'

He finished singing and sat down.

Samori: 'The dust that has piled up like a mountain since ancient times, I am happy that it has scattered today.'

Ummori: 'Everything is just Kannagara-kamu-nagara, I leave it only to the heart of Kami.'

Taruman: 'The intimacy of a pair of mandarin ducks that never separate, how enviable it is to behold.'

Haruna: 'Bound by the thread of Kannagara-en, I am happy to cross this world.'

Karunahime: 'When I gaze at the shadow of the moon crossing the heavens, it smiles, looking at my face.'

Ekusu: 'Haruna-kimi, who has taken Hime-sama as his bride, shall flourish like the flowers blooming in spring.'

Shieru: 'The intimacy between the peerless pine and pine, today we celebrate a prosperity that shall last for a thousand generations.'

Having finished singing in this manner, the auspicious wedding ceremony concluded; although the families of Samori and Ummori appeared somewhat reconciled on the surface, the heart of the Tsukasa of Ummori did not easily soften, and he continued to treat the Tsukasa of Samori as an obstacle.
