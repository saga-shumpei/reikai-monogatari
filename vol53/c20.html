Chapter 20: 背進 [1383]

背進〔1383〕

§ 1

It was only for a brief while that the two generals, Oniharuwake and Kumehiko, had the soldiers of the capital of Biku under their command and as their subordinates as a result of their consecutive victories. Through the mediation of Hirunahime and Karunahime, who exhausted their arts of Senpenbankuwa, a portion of the army of the land of Biku was finally subjugated under the rule of the Setsuteiri; Samori was temporarily entrusted by the Setsuteiri with the authority over the military, and came to maintain order within the castle; furthermore, Taruman continued to serve as the propaganda official and concurrently as the Naijitsukasa, while Ekusu was promoted to Umori. Thus, the camp at the foot of the castle was for the time being largely lent to the Brahman army, and here a clear demarcation was established between the Victoria family and the Brahman army.

The two generals, Oniharuwake and Kumehiko, did not wish to advance to the mansion of the Saien, yet they could not return to Haruna either; whether they should head to the Golden Mountain, or face certain defeat once again. At any rate, they built a temporary camp centered on Mount Victor, where they devoted their full strength to training their troops, oppressing the small neighboring countries, and constructing a barracks with the intention of building a kingdom, holding hope for the future. Hirunahime and Karunahime continued to serve the generals as before. However, the two beauties set up various excuses and did not surrender their bodies to the two generals. They always deceived them with eloquence, expressions, and sake, and the two women helped each other, intermingling to ensure there was almost no time for them to sleep together. Both King Victoria and Haruna understood the hearts of the two women well, and did not harbor the slightest doubt regarding their conduct. They were merely grateful for the pains the two women took, sacrificing their own bodies to protect the peace of their nation.

The barracks, which had been built in a great hurry, were mostly completed. Oniharuwake built his main camp at the most strategically important point at the foot of Mount Victor, and devoted his efforts solely to training his troops side by side with General Kumehiko; on the other hand, he relied on his beloved Naisu as his sole strength, expecting that in the future they would openly be a perfect married couple.

Into such a place came three foot soldiers of the river guards, A, B, and C, in a flurry. As Shiyamu was attending to business at the reception desk, the three said, gasping for breath,

Shiyamu: 'Report. Just now, it appears that a large number of knights are crossing the Lion River and rushing toward us. At any rate, I report this to you.'

Shiyamu: 'What, a large number of knights are crossing the river? That is incomprehensible. Who could they be?'

He tilted his head. A said,

A: 'Well, judging by the banners, since every one of them is dyed with the Mitsuba-aoi crest, I believe they are surely the army of the Brahman religion.'

Shiyamu: 'That cannot be. It is unlikely that the Brahman army would cross over here in such large numbers. If General Ranchi is holding his ground in the Ukigi Forest, it is impossible for the followers of the Sangokyo to be feigning and raising the Mitsuba-aoi banners to attack us. This is truly incomprehensible. In any case, I shall report this to the General. You return to the riverbank at once, investigate whether they are enemies or allies, and report back.'

Saying this, he proceeded to the living room of General Oniharuwake. Kumehiko happened to be there. Suparu and Emishi were also attending at his side, talking about something happily. Shiyamu, who appeared there, turned to Oniharuwake and, while bowing slightly, said,

Shiyamu: 'I report to the General. According to the report from the river guards just now, it appears that hundreds of knights are crossing the Lion River, waving the Mitsuba-aoi banners. How shall we handle this?'

Oniharuwake: 'What, a banner I do not understand. It cannot be that General Ranchi has fled here, can it? Kumehiko-dono, what is your opinion?'

Kumehiko: 'Judging by the situation, General Ranchi of the Ukigi Forest must have been defeated by the Kototama battle of Chikunowake and has fled, opening a bloody path. If they were the Sangokyo, they would not be accompanied by such a large force. This is a troublesome matter.'

Oniharuwake: 'In any case, Suparu, Shayam, you must mount your horses, observe whether they are friend or foe at once, and report back to me.'

Upon receiving this command, the two replied, 'Yes,' and immediately prepared their horses; with the brave sound of hooves, they rode off at full speed toward the riverbank.

The two generals folded their arms, bowed their heads, and were lost in thought for a while. Hirunahime and Karunahime, while smiling, feigned an innocent demeanor and toyed with their koto.

Oniharuwake: 'Hirunahime, stop playing the koto for a moment; a grave matter has occurred. Karunahime-dono, the same applies to you; this is no time for such trivialities.'

Hirunahime: 'Yes, has some worrying matter suddenly occurred? That is indeed a matter of concern.'

Oniharuwake: 'Well, it is not exactly something to worry about, but I have received a suspicious report. Depending on the circumstances, we may need to prepare our defenses.'

Hirunahime: 'Ho ho ho ho, there is no need for such defenses. If you would grant me leave, I shall mount my horse and negotiate with the approaching army.'

Oniharuwake: 'No, no, I am concerned if I send you to such a place. Besides, I would be troubled if you were to cast amorous glances at the general.'

Hirunahime: 'O ho ho ho, General-sama, you speak as if I were such a weak woman. Tell me, Karuna-sama, shall we two mount our horses, lead the Red Skirt Troop, and make the swarming enemy cry out in surprise?'

Karunahime: 'That is truly so. If I only had the permission of the General-sama, I would also like to do my part.'

The two women skillfully mounted their horses and left the scene... If it were the Brahmin army, they would have no choice but to bring the leader back and have him meet Oniharuwake, but if by any chance it were the propagandists or the army of the 135-Kyo, they would take the opportunity to escape from this place and hide themselves for a while... this thought flashed through both of their minds unexpectedly. However, the two generals were so worried that they might cause injury to the two lovely women that they did not easily permit them to go to the front.

Just then, the sound of a conch shell was heard blowing. Oniharuwake stood up abruptly and looked down; seeing hundreds of soldiers running toward them in disarray, he realized that they did not look like an enemy army, but rather like defeated soldiers fleeing. Oniharuwake breathed a sigh of relief and said,

Oniharuwake: 'Ah ha ha ha, General Kumehiko, look at that. Hundreds of soldiers are rushing toward us, but since we make benevolence and righteousness our principle, we must never move even a single soldier. We have only the method of making the enemy submit through the virtue of our love.'

Kumehiko looked down from the high railing again, and as if relieved, said,

Kumehiko: 'Ah ha ha ha, there is no need to tell me. No matter how many millions of enemies may press upon us, I am determined to face them with the virtue of love and never resort to slaughter. To make the enemy submit without fighting is what a brave general does. How is that, Karunahime? My non-resistance principle and philanthropy are truly thorough, are they not?'

He said this with a look of self-satisfaction.

Karunahime: 'That is so; there is no enemy for an army of benevolence and righteousness. I pray that you will maintain your non-resistance principle to the very end. To meet violence with violence is what the base people do; Karuna is increasingly impressed by the noble aspirations of such a wonderful General-sama.'

Although she said this on the surface... 'If the enemy should press in and do something to these two generals, that would be convenient. If that happens, the land of Biku will be fundamentally governed in peace,' she thought. Hirunahime also held the same thought as Karuna.

To such a place, guided by Supāru and Emishi, Terunsu, who served under Shiyōgun Ranchi, arrived running breathlessly. Leading several hundred knights, Terunsu had fled to this spot.

Kumehiko: 'Ah, are you not Ranchi-sama's subordinate Terunsu? I surmise there must be some peculiar circumstance. How fares Ranchi-dono?'

Terunsu: 'Oh, well met! First of all, I offer my hearty congratulations to both Shiyōgun-sama on your good health. Though it be useless to tell you now, Shiyōgun Ranchi and Shiyōgun Katahiko have completely surrendered to Harukuniwake, the Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, and having now disbanded their army, they themselves have received the magic of Ananai-kyō and turned into Sendenshi. Since such prominent leaders as Ranchi, Katahiko, Gariya, and Kēsu have come to this pass, before long they will surely march upon Mount Bikotoru with Harukuniwake at their head. Even Shiyōgun Ranchi, who led an army of three thousand men, surrendered without the slightest resistance. He is indeed a terrifyingly formidable enemy.'

Hearing this, Kumehiko's heart leapt into his mouth, his face turned pale, and suddenly trembling with voice,

Kumehiko: 'Kiharuwake-dono, th-this is terrible. We cannot remain like this. Shall we not devise some plan?'

Kiharuwake was also startled at this report, but since he could not show such a cowardly and unsightly appearance in front of Hirunahime, he feigned composure on purpose,

Kiharuwake: 'Ahahaha! What utmost insolence! Even if millions of Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō were to march upon us, if I master the wondrous law bestowed upon me by Daijizaiten-sama, blowing them away in a single breath is right before our eyes. Do not worry! Ahahaha!'

Saying so, he purposely shook his body, and the comicality of it lay in his trying to cover up the deep-seated tremor rising from the bottom of his stomach.

The two women, Hiruna and Karuna, quickly discerned that both Shiyōgun were seized with fear, yet they feigned ignorance and concealed their outward expressions.

Kumehiko: 'Nay, Shiyōgun-dono, we cannot afford such optimism. Let us organize the army as quickly as possible and march upon Mount Ōgon. Our mission is originally not to be besieged in such a place. If Harukuniwake is marching upon us, let us forestall him by capturing Mount Ōgon, relying on the fortress to defend against Harukuniwake's attacking force and annihilate them.'

Thus he spoke grandly with his mouth, but in his innermost heart, marching upon Mount Ōgon was what both Shiyōgun feared the most. Nevertheless, if they dawdled here, there was no telling when Harukuniwake might march upon them. If they suffered a disgraceful defeat, had their inner helmets seen through by Hiruna and Karuna, were thought to be cowardly men, and were disgusted by them, it would be a disaster; his mind was fretting over that alone.

Kiharuwake: 'Indeed... so to speak, the camp on Mount Bikotoru is merely a resting place. Since respectable Kshatriyas are present here, there should be no need for us to interfere. Sympathizing with your opinion, then let us lead the whole army and set about preparing for the march.'

Though he spoke with complete composure, he was on pins and needles, wondering if Harukuniwake had not already crossed the Raion River and come over to this side. However, as the readers know, Harukuniwake, leading his subordinates, was leisurely holding lecture meetings and educating the youth in the Forest of Kurusu and the Tēmusu Pass. Both Shiyōgun were possessed by the exact same psychological state as the weak warriors of the Taira clan who were frightened by the sound of waterfowl beating their wings and fled fragilely. For that reason, an indescribably restless air was visible.

Hirunahime, behaving with utmost composure,

Hirunahime: 'My Lord Shiyōgun, after all the trouble of building a barracks up to this point, are not preparations complete enough to defend against any enemy? In a place with such beautiful scenery, I wish to live with you for the rest of my life. How about giving up on marching forth?'

と落ち着き払つて言つてゐるものの、已に治国別はライオン河を渡つて、此方へ来て居るのではあるまいかと気が気でなかつた。併し治国別は部下を引つれ、クルスの森やテームス峠で悠々閑々と講演会を開き子弟を教育してゐたのは読者の知る所である。水鳥の羽音に驚いて、脆くも遁走した平家の弱武者其儘の心理状態に、両将軍は襲はれてゐた。それ故に何となく落つかない風が見える。

ヒルナ姫は落着き払つて、

ヒルナ姫『モシ将軍様、折角此処まで兵営を築き上げ、如何なる敵も防ぐ丈の準備が整つてるぢやありませぬか。かやうな風景の佳い所で、貴方と一生暮したう厶いますワ。進軍なんかおやめになつたら何うですか』

§ 4

Oniharuwake, in a faltering Kuchō, said,

Oniharuwake: 'Mm, yes, that is so, but to adapt to circumstances and respond to changes is the path that one who leads the Sangun must follow. Do not be so Shinpai; I shall take you with me wherever I go, so even if I say we have advanced, we are the Shiyōgun. I shall never place you in a position where Yadama might come... Now, you too must have Kakugo and follow me. You certainly do not need to feel Shinpai.'

Hirunahime: 'Even so, I feel as if I am being assailed by a spirit of Satsubatsu; tell me, Karunahime-sama, what do you think?'

Karunahime: 'I too would wish to remain in this Jinei forever, but... excuse me, Kumehiko-sama, would you not be so kind as to do that for us?'

Kumehiko: 'How can you say such Kiraku things? The enemy is pressing in Majika; if we are late, it will be the Hiun of our Mikata. Come, we must withdraw from here and Shingun as quickly as Itsukoku.'

Karunahime: 'Excuse me, Kumehiko-Shiyōgunsama, is this Shingun not a Matsuka deception, a planned Taikiyaku?'

Kumehiko: 'Do not talk Baka. The enemy is at Mount Kogane, and since it has come to this, the necessity has arisen to devise divine strategies and demonic schemes as quickly as possible and occupy Mount Kogane; we must prepare for Shingun as quickly as possible.'

He is fidgeting.

Karunahime: 'Your Shingun is surely a retreat, is it not? You can always find a justification for anything, can you not?'

Kumehiko: 'What a sharp-tongued woman you are.'

Oniharuwake: 'Now, prepare the horses quickly, and for the Hime, prepare mares. Supāru, Emishi, Terunsu, command the entire army and follow from behind! Come, Kumehiko-dono, let us take the vanguard.'

With this convenient excuse, he is already beginning to prepare for flight.

Hirunahime: 'Excuse me, Shiyōgunsama, is the vanguard not the role of scouts? You are the commander-in-chief; would it not be safer for you to proceed slowly at the end?'

Oniharuwake: 'That is true, but there is a saying that he who strikes first controls others; this is the secret of military strategy. It is said that if the head does not turn, the tail cannot turn. Since we are going in a long snake formation, the head goes first and the tail follows, just as a snake moves; that is why it is called a long snake formation.'

He is making a show of himself before the Hime, striving only to hide his own cowardice.

Oniharuwake and Kumehiko ride side by side with the two beauties, Hiruna and Karuna, and gallop away toward the west as fast as they can. Supāru and Emishi, who were left behind, were so flustered and panicked that they had no time to organize the troops; shouting 'Retreat! Retreat!', they hoisted their sails, mounted their horses, wore their military caps backward, put on only one shoe, and struck the horses' rumps recklessly, fleeing in a state resembling a defeated army. As the saying goes, if one horse goes mad, a thousand horses go mad; driven by the fear that a powerful enemy was attacking from behind, the three thousand soldiers knocked people down and trampled over them, each trying to be the first to flee toward the west, until not a single one remained.

Dozens of owls perched in the thicket of the forest on Mount Bikutoru, and began to cry in unison,

'Utsufū-utsufū-otsohoho, ahō-ahō-ahō.'
