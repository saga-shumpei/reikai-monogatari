Chapter 19: 刺客 [1382]

刺客〔1382〕

§ 1

King Victoria had become a prisoner of the enemy, and finding himself in a predicament where his very Inochi was uncertain, he was troubled in his heart; yet, through unexpected aid, he returned once more to his original mansion, and upon seeing the safety of Hirunahime-sama, he felt a sense of relief, and at that moment, he had the authority of the Heiba, his hope of Nenrai, returned by Umori, and at the same time, feeling assured that Oniharuwake and General Kumehiko were managing the Riyōjiyo, he felt his tension ease and, exhausted, he went to sleep. Haruna, concerned by the unusual Yōsu of Umorinokami, received permission from his father to serve Tonoi in the room adjacent to the King's this Konban.

Haruna thought of Karunahime-sama... Ah, she is truly a Ritsupa Jiyosei. Had it not been for Hirunahime-sama and her, the country of Biku, not to mention the Ōke and the Samorike, would have instantly fallen into the Hiun of Hametsu. Thinking of it now, perhaps my loving Karunahime-sama was not a matter of human affairs, but entirely the divine providence of the Lord God. Ah, how grateful, how grateful... he prayed silently in his heart. Just then, someone approached the King's living room, hiding their footsteps. As Haruna strained his ears to consider the Yōsu, a black shadow of a man appeared by the dim lamp; while looking at the blade of a long sword he had drawn smoothly in the light of the lamp, he was smiling with a grin. On the bed, King Victoria was sleeping peacefully, unaware that a crisis was closing in upon his own person. Haruna moved his feet stealthily, and using a rope, he hooked it around the man's neck from behind, threw the end of the rope over his shoulder, and ran down the corridor with a rhythmic sound. The man, with his chin hooked and still holding his drawn sword, was dragged down the corridor without uttering a single sound.

The King awoke at this noise, and looking closely, he saw the scabbard of a sword lying on the floor. Thinking that it would be a serious matter to make a sound, and that it must surely be an assassin, he looked into the corridor and saw a black shadow; the King immediately grabbed a spear from the nageshi and went into the corridor, where he saw Haruna dragging a man around by the neck, and the man appeared to be unconscious. The King lowered his voice and spoke.

Kshatriya: 'Are you not Haruna? What is the matter?'

Haruna: 'Yes, a suspicious person came and was spying on your bedchamber, my Lord, so I crept up from behind, put a rope around his neck, and dragged him here.'

Kshatriya: 'Oh, well done, well done. Quickly, examine this fellow's face and see who he is.'

Haruna replied, 'Yes,' and keeping the neck tightly constricted, he lit a hand-candle and looked at the assassin's face, finding it to be Shieru, the steward of Umori. Both the King and Haruna were startled, and for a short while, the master and servant looked at each other's faces.

Haruna: 'Kshatriya-sama, this fellow is the steward of Umori. Judging from this, it appears that Umori resents today's return of military authority and is plotting some kind of rebellion. If we make a commotion about this, we might fall into the enemy's trap. Let us quietly bind his hands and feet so that he cannot move even if he regains consciousness, and hide him away.'

Kshatriya: 'Hmm, ah, that would be best. Truly, that fellow called Umori is a wicked and lawless villain.'

Haruna: 'As you command, my Lord. Please, King-sama, be sufficiently on your guard.'

Saying this, he bound Shieru with his hands behind his back, shoved him into a closet, and with an indifferent face, Haruna stayed by the side of Kshatriya in his living room, keeping a strict watch without sleeping a wink.

Hirunahime-sama and Karunahime-sama had thought once of leaving their seats because the guests—Oniharuwake, Kumehiko, Supāru, Emishi, Shiamu, and Maruta—were helplessly drunk, but being the cautious pair that they were... 'No, no, wait, wait, this is a moment of great importance. I wish to meet Kshatriya-sama and explain the circumstances in detail, but if one or two among the six are feigning deep sleep and are perhaps watching the Yōsu, it would be a disaster. Ah, how I wish to see him...' though their hearts were burning with impatience, they prioritized the great matter, and while letting General Oniharuwake use their laps as pillows, they sat still even after the sun had set with faces that betrayed nothing. Karunahime-sama also wished to report the outcome of events to her beloved husband Haruna as soon as possible, and though she wished to be praised by him with a single word, she too, fearing that one or two among the six might be watching the Yōsu, prioritized the great matter and, like Hirunahime-sama, let Kumehiko use her lap as a pillow, occasionally communicating with Hirunahime-sama through her eyes. However, all six of these men were truly dead drunk and had lost all sense of their surroundings.

§ 2

The night storm blows outside the mansion, making a loud sound. Buffeted by the wind, the amado are rattling and trembling. Nijiyo had drifted into the path of dreams. A large man wearing a fukumenzukin drew his daitau and entered, hiding his ashioto, and first attempted to strike down General Kumehiko with a single blow. At this moment, Karunahime woke with a start and instantly struck the vital point of the kusemono's arm. The kusemono dropped the daitau with a clatter; the Hime quickly moved behind him, took a hosohimo from her bosom, bound him up, and pressed his head down firmly so he could not move.

Karunahime: 'Shiyōgunsama, Hirunahime-sama, Minasama, please wake up, an assassin has come.'

At this calling voice, everyone woke up.

'What is it? What is it?'

They approached Karuna's side. Karuna said,

Karunahime: 'Moshi Shiyōgunsama, a kusemono has come. He came with the intention of stabbing you all, so I have just now bound him up.'

Kumehiko: 'Ya, that is a great feat, a great feat. I too was in a dangerous spot. And who might this kusemono be?'

Karunahime: 'I do not know who he is because he is wearing a kurozukin, so please, do bring an akari here.'

Hirunahime brought an andō and approached, and when she removed the kurozukin, anihaka, it was Belts, the Umori. Belts had previously been stabbed in the arm by Karuna with a short sword, and because of that, his hand could not move as he wished, and he was easily bound by Karuna. Hiruna and Karuna were both startled, but with indifferent faces, they said,

'Oh my.'

They were feigning ignorance. In her heart, Karuna thought... Of all people, that I must capture my own brother, what a karmic fate this is. However, for the sake of the country and for the sake of the royal family, even if he is my brother, I cannot let him go... and she immediately composed her heart.

Oniharawake: 'He is likely a spy for the Kshatriya. To have his life spared and then, under the pretext of a banquet, make us let down our guard and attempt an assassination—this is beyond outrageous and inexcusable. Yōshitsu, from now on, I shall sentence not only the Kshatriya but every single one of these wretches to the punishment of the roasting pan. Ya, Spār, Emishi, bring a hundred soldiers here at once.'

Hirunahime stopped him in a hurry,

Hirunahime: 'Moshi Shiyōgunsama, please wait a moment. This is by no means a plot by the Kshatriya. This man is a wicked and lawless kusemono called the Umori-tsukasa who serves the Kshatriya. It seems he envied your authority and plotted to seize the power of the military for himself, and thus snuck in during the night. Please, wait a little while before bringing in the troops.'

Kumehiko: 'Oniharawake-dono, this is a grave incident. As you said, it would be better for our mutual safety to bring at least a hundred soldiers to this place.'

Karunahime: 'My husband, Kumehiko-sama, please wait first. For a general of such renowned heroic fame to use soldiers against a single spineless man like this would be a stain on your dignity as a general. If you truly love me, please do not do such a thing, and deal with it here.'

Kumehiko, stopped by his beloved Karuna and having had his life saved by her, did not have the courage to refuse.

Kumehiko: 'Un, yoshi yoshi, then I leave it to you. Oniharawake-dono, what cowards they are. A fly-like insect that was easily captured by the slender arms of my wife, Karunahime—please set your mind at ease now.'

Karunahime: 'Moshi, both Shiyōgunsama, what shall we do with this man?'

Oniharawake: 'Uun, I leave him to the care of Kumehiko's wife. However, you must never cast amorous glances at him.'

Karunahime: 'Hohohohoho, what a joke you are making. Sasa, kusemono, come here... Hirunahime-sama, let us throw this fellow into the storehouse, you and I.'

§ 4

Karuna felt a sense of pride upon hearing that Haruna had achieved a feat of merit.

Oniharuwake: 'Who is that Kusemono?'

Setsuteiri: 'Yes, it is truly embarrassing to say, but it is the Akunin called Shieru, the Karei of Umori.'

Oniharuwake: 'Naruhodo, just now, the fellow called Berutsu of Umori snuck into my Ima, intending to commit Ansatsu, but he was Toriosa by the arm of this Karuna, bound with Takate and Kote, and now, having tasted the blade of resentment from these two Naitsu, he has reached Jiyakumetsu. Ahahahaha.'

Setsuteiri: 'What, did Umori do such a thing? He is truly a Burei fellow. However, since the Akunin have perished for the sake of Anatagata, there is nothing more joyful than this. Come, let us hold a Nijikuwai once more for an Akumabarahi.'

Oniharuwake: 'Ya, that is most kind. Ah, however, since we have escaped such Kiken, I shall accept without Enriyo. And if we take that Kusemono called Shieru as a side dish and have Itsupai, it will be even more marvelous. Ahahahaha.'

Thus, they moved to a second Shiyuen, spent the night, and continued until the seventh hour of the following day, singing songs, dancing wildly, and exhausting every ounce of joy.
