Chapter 4: 蜜語 [1367]

蜜語〔1367〕

§ 1

In one room of Victoria Castle, Hirunahime is all alone, playing the koto and singing of her inner thoughts.

Hirunahime: 'The Bankoshinnō Shihonagahikonomikoto, who has appeared as the ruler of this world, is troubled in his heart over how to save the Kamigami of the four directions and the Tamigusa in the Shinkoku of the Tokoyo spring; how grateful I am for his concern. As a young woman, I serve Victoriahime, and while I spend my days happily, pouring my Magokoro and sincerity into my service morning and evening, I wonder what became of the Ōhi-no-kimi. Tempted by the wind of Mujiyō, she became a person of the other world. Though I lament and pray for her Meifuku to the Ōkami morning and evening, the departed does not return. On this lonely autumn evening, the voices of insects gather in the Nobe.'

Hirunahime: 'As the sky clears, I sink into worries that make life not worth living. The moon shining on the dew of the Kusaba loses its light, I know not where, and even the shadows of the stars appear hazy. Just as I look back, I hear a voice like a jewel calling, "Hiruna, Hiruna." My heart as a maiden thunders. When I look back, there is the unexpected Victoria-ō-no-kimi, beckoning me to come this way. He leads me to the Ima, and with most gentle words, he tries to fathom the heart of Himegimisama. Though I refused at first, I was bound by the deep heart of my lord who would not permit it, and I climbed to the position of the Empress, stepping over the Nyōgo and Kōi.'

Hirunahime: 'Yet, somehow, my heart is not at peace. Within the passing of three or four years, my lord, who cherished me as the most precious of beings, is now cold; how sad it is that the autumn wind blows so fiercely. If only there were someone to comfort my heart, to talk through the long night and clear away the sorrow of my body. Just as I think this, the Migimori-no-tsukasa, Beltsu-tsukasa, whose face and appearance are most beautiful, faithfully protects my person; what joy it is. Bound by his heart, which is filled with sincerity, we became inseparable, but it is a love that must hide from the eyes of others.'

Hirunahime: 'How many times have I suffered, breaking my heart morning and evening, fearing that the Nyōgo, Kōi, or the maids and servants might discover our relationship? The abyss of love deepens day by day, and we are trapped in it. I break my heart a thousand times, fearing that it might reach the ears of my lord. As I spend these sad days, what can I do but weep? Oh, Shihonagahiko-no-Ōkami, who protects the teachings of the Ural religion, please protect this endless bond forever. Although my lord has already passed his seventieth year, he is most stubborn and will not let me absorb new ideas, only the ways of the ancient sages.'

Hirunahime: 'He uses them as a shield morning and evening. The world is gradually falling into ruin, and the state is becoming precarious, but the Hidarimori-no-tsukasa, Kyūbitto, because of his stubbornness and lack of spirit, goes against the ideas of the times and makes the world increasingly chaotic. Truly, it is a miserable world. The Migimori-no-tsukasa, Beltsu-tsukasa, who has awakened to the times, quickly understood my heart and tries to exhaust his Magokoro to protect the ancient and noble Biku-no-kuni forever. Yet, my lord and the Hidarimori-no-kimi reject the words of the Migimori one by one, and though he presents the principles of perfect peace and governance, they refuse him without fail at every turn.'

Hirunahime: 'Ah, what shall I do for Biku-no-kuni? Is there no one who thinks to become a pillar and a staff to support it for a thousand, for eight thousand generations? The Migimori-no-tsukasa holds the power of the military, yet he argues that to move troops recklessly would only worsen the hearts of the people. He insists on opinions of peace, and without even defending, he waits forever. What kind of secret plan does he have? It is painful that he does not move. Yet, I am not a woman who should abandon my lord and walk a path that is not the way, but when I observe the course of the world, there is no choice but to rely on the Migimori.'

Hirunahime: 'Ah, Kannagara-kamigami-kamigami. May the spirit of the God be blessed, and may you clear away the stubbornness of my lord and the Hidarimori-no-tsukasa as soon as possible, and protect Biku-no-kuni forever. Before the great presence of Hirunahime, while playing the small jewel koto, I pray to comfort the divine will.'

§ 2

At this moment, adjusting his attire respectfully and guided by two or three officials of the castle, Umorinokami Belts arrived. Belts, acting as if he were the master, entered in a haughty manner and addressed the three officials:

Belts: 'Ya, thank you for your hard work. As I have something to discuss with Hirunahime-sama regarding state affairs for a short while, it would be best for you all to return to your original posts.'

'Hai,' the three replied and left the scene. Then, the three quietly hid themselves in the next room, pricking up their ears so as not to miss a single word of the two's conversation. These were the castle officials named Ramu, Ritsuto, and Beru. Since the relationship between Hirunahime and Umorinokami had been somewhat strange lately, they had been keeping a watchful eye on them, following the secret intentions of Samorinokami.

Hirunahime and Belts were not in the slightest aware that the three, Ramu, Ritsuto, and Beru, were listening in the next room. Belts sat cross-legged haughtily in front of the princess, and while smoking, said:

Belts: 'Hime-sama, as a matter I wish to consult you about has suddenly arisen, I have come for a moment.'

Hirunahime: 'You always say this or that and only think about distancing yourself from me, do you not? This consultation of yours today is, again, that usual flirtation, is it not? In these times when the nation is beset by many affairs, I cannot lend an ear to such things. It would be better for you to go to Tena-sama and consult with her; is this not a bit off-course?'

Belts: 'Well, I am overwhelmed to have the arrows of attack pointed at me so suddenly. Even a man of eight shaku cannot possibly stand against it. What I have come for today is not such a cheerful matter. At the least, it is a grave matter for the Umori house, and at the most, a grave matter for the entire land of Biku. Therefore, I have come out to consult with you thoroughly and decide upon a course of action after hearing your opinion. Please, listen to me seriously. You treat this Umori as a person in your pocket, using me as conveniently as you like, and when you have no more use for me, you intend to treat me with the cruelty of discarding worn-out sandals; somehow, I cannot help but feel that there is something about you against which I cannot let my guard down.'

Hirunahime: 'Hohohoho, how can you say such things, and where do you get that idea? Is there not an unbreakable bond between you and me? You promised that we would protect this nation by uniting eternally, did you not? And regarding that, did I not accept your demands and go even to paths where a woman should not tread? To say such things is truly cruel; please, try to fathom my heart a little. Even if we two were to fall to the bottom of the inferno, did we not swear to serve the nation together?'

Belts: 'No, I am humbled. There is no mistake in that. However, what I have come to consult you about today is serious. My younger sister, Karunahime, of all people, has become enamored with that fool of a son of Samorinokami, Haruna, and before I knew it, they have been exchanging love letters, and now it has reached a point where they will not budge for anything. Therefore, as you know, giving my sister to such a weakling as the stubborn and foolish Haruna, the son of Samori, is something that my steward Shieru and I are agonizing over, fearing it might cause a great disruption in all our plans. What is your opinion on this, Hime-sama?'

Hirunahime looked down and thought for a short while, but soon, while letting out a smile, said:

Hirunahime: 'Come now, Umori-sama, is that not a most welcome development? If this marriage goes well, Karunahime-sama will be your sister, and everything will be convenient in many ways. It is as if you have planted a spy in the enemy's camp; there could be no greater convenience than this.'

Beltsu: 'However, I simply cannot think of that fellow Karuna as a woman who possesses such Yūzū. She says only that love is best, inclines her heart solely toward Renai, and has a Keikau of trying to admonish my Kaudō; therefore, if I were to send her to the house of Samorinokami, I am worried only that she might become Wakudeki in Dautokuron and end up drawing a bow against her Aniki. I have come to consult you, hoping that you might use your influence with Setsuteirisama to pour cold water on this Endan so that it does not Seikō. If it is the Kotoba of Setsuteirisama, even Karuna, my sister who has become a captive of such Netsuretsu love, would have no reason to defy it. I Dōzo beg for your Ōneori. I have deliberated on various Shiyujiyu with the Karei, Shieru, and I have concluded that there is no other Hauhafu. I had once thought it might be convenient for the purpose of my goal to send my sister to the Samorike, and had almost decided upon it, but upon further reflection, I realized there is nothing so dangerous. Since I, as her brother, cannot possibly break this Endan, there is no other way but to borrow your power.'

Hirunahime: 'Do not worry yourself; there is no better opportunity than this to harmonize the secret strife between the Migimorinokami and Samorinokami houses. No matter how ancient and powerful the Setsuteiri house of Biku may be, when the dragon and tiger who are the essential Samorinokami and Migimorinokami fight each other, neither side will be able to maintain its strength, and in the end, the collapse of the nation from within is plain to see. Truly, for the sake of the nation and for the sake of the Setsuteiri house, there could be no more splendid Endan than this. On this matter alone, I, Hirunahime, insist that you must think it over thoroughly.'

Beltsu: 'Indeed, that may even be better. In that case, I shall leave everything to you, Hime-sama. I leave it all in your capable hands.'

Hirunahime: 'If that is the case, I shall summon Samorinokami from here and instruct him thoroughly myself. Now, walls have ears and the heavens have mouths; please return quickly so that this secret does not leak out until the proper time.'

Beltsu: 'It seems a cold wind has blown, but please do not act as if you are sweeping me away with a broom. I do not think it would be much of a sin if you told me to have a smoke and return. I think it would not be much of a hindrance if I were to take a breath or two and have a cigarette here.'

Hirunahime: 'Are you going to trouble me by saying such things again? If that is the case, please go ahead and smoke here for a hundred or a thousand years.'

Beltsu: 'Hahahaha, oh, I am truly humbled. If that is the case, this nuisance shall leave your presence at once. If you find a broom turned upside down with a headscarf draped over it somewhere, please do set it back to its original state, ehehehehe.'

Leaving behind a repulsive laugh, he walks away toward his own mansion, his footsteps echoing in the corridor. Ramu, Ritto, and Beru look at each other and smile bitterly, then stealthily hurry toward the mansion of Samorinokami.
