§ 1

Itakō: 'Who would have thought, kya, kya, kya, crying like a monkey, That we should be met with such a sight as a "kya-tsu!"?'

So saying, dangling his canteen, he heads down toward the ravine.

Itakō: 'Yaa, clear water is flowing right here. If I draw this and wash it for him, it will surely heal. Mauled by mountain monkeys, everything Seeks the salvation of the water's mitama. Since this water is the dew of Kami's grace, I would fain say that today he shall see. Having fallen into the ravine and come to draw water, Grant me to draw forth a deep heart. This water is the dew of grace that saves and heals Not only the eyes, but life itself. Let the light of Kamunagara Kami appear And illuminate the eyes of Tamakuniwake. How regretful it is to have been mauled By unknown mountain monkeys of Futokorodani. Yet perhaps this is the reward of the sin Of making light of the mission of Kami. The command of Tokiokashi Kami has appeared, And the eyes of the heart have opened. Wait a while, this is no time to dawdle, We must quickly make the eyes clear. Itakō's eyes are all right, yet I feel so painfully for the eyes of my master, the lord.'

Humming thus, he fills his canteen with the clear autumn valley water, and wishing to save Tamakuniwake even a moment sooner, he pushes his way through dwarf bamboos and briars, climbing up with out-of-breath gasps. Tamakuniwake, while dripping blood from both eyes, wipes away the blood-clot with a cloth, presses the palms of his hands to both eyes, enduring the pain as he hangs his head. Michikō and Junkō,

'Saa, this is terrible, terrible!'

flustered and running about in utter dismay, are not helpful in the least.

Tamakuniwake: 'Michikō, is the water not ready yet? Has Itakō not returned yet?'

Michikō: 'Hai, he went dashing down the mountain path, and to this very day, his figure is nowhere to be seen. While the Sensei is suffering so much from his wounds... Ee, what a dull-witted fellow he is. Oi Junkō, what on earth are you loitering around for? Hurry up and urge Itakō along for the water, Itakō-Itakō!'

Junkō: 'Ee, this is no time for jokes. We are in dire straits and have lost our way. Just an inch ahead is pitch darkness. Where on earth is there room for such carefree talk, iyai?'

Just as this was happening, Itakō came climbing up with heavy, blowing breaths,

Itakō: 'Aa, I am terribly sorry for being so late. Thinking I wanted to return even a single moment sooner, the more I hurried my heart, the steeper the slope and the more my feet slipped, and I have finally arrived up to here.'

Michikō: 'Oi, why don't you hurry up and take out the canteen? You don't have it on you at all, do you?'

Fumbling around his waist, Itakō let out a startled cry of 'Atsu!',

Itakō: 'Yaa, this is terrible. In my excessive haste, I left it right where I drew the water at the bottom of the ravine. Oi Michikō, won't you hurry and go fetch it? Since the Sensei's pain is so pitiful, if we don't cool his eyes quickly, they will swell up more and more and it will be terrible.'

Michikō: 'Ee, what a scatterbrain you are. Where on earth did you leave it? Why don't you tell me clearly?'

Itakō: 'When I say the ravine, it is the river flowing through the mountain valley. I drew that water and left it right there on the sand. Saa, won't you go quickly? We must help ease the Sensei's pain even for a minute.'

Junkō: 'Oi Itakō, since you were the one who left it there, shouldn't you go? There is no time to be dawdling and searching around like this, is there? You really are a troubling fellow. You are just like a pheasant's messenger. Even if you went to draw water, what good is it if you don't bring it back?'

Itakō and Sumikō both mutter complaints while parting the brushwood and going to search for the place where the canteen was dropped. At long last, about one chō down, the canteen had fallen. However, because it had fallen with its opening facing down and its bottom facing up, it had spewed out every single drop without a single one remaining, and as an empty canteen, it lay resting with an all's-right-with-the-world-and-peace-in-the-realm mood.

Itakō: 'Heh, what an unhelpful canteen. If it was going to fall, why didn't it fall facing upward? The water that I took the trouble to let you drink has all spewed out... My, what a poorly timed moment, truly a poorly timed thing, ah. Hey, Sumikō, it cannot be helped. Shall we run all the way down to the bottom of the valley one more time?'

Sumikō: 'That's so, ah. Now that the canteen has been found, it would be fine with just you alone, but since by nature you are a scatterbrain, if you drop it on the road again, it will come to nothing. I will follow along as a watchdog.'

Sumikō thrust the canteen into his bosom, parted the brushwood on the steep slope, sliding on the grass, and together with Itakō descended deep to the bottom of the valley, making the clear spring bubble round bubbles with a sloshing sound, and filled it to the brim.

Sumikō: 'This valley water, cleared and pure, into the canteen, letting it drink, the body returning is indeed joyful. The valley water that Itakō took the trouble to draw has turned into water bubbles and vanished away.'

Itakō: 'Even if I say it was not my intention to drop it, perhaps I dropped my unseen wisdom. Picking up the dropped jar, to wash the eyes of Otohiko, I am filled with awe.'

Sumikō: 'Come, hurry and follow along, you wretch of an Itakō, for Eye-Itakō is waiting.'

So saying, once again he climbed up the steep slope, at last arrived beside Tamakowake, scooped the water of the canteen in his hands, and carefully washed Tamakowake's both eyes.

Tamakowake: 'Ah, how thankful I am; with this, the pain in my eyes seems to have completely stopped.'

Itakō: 'Sensei, has the pain stopped? That is a matter of greater joy than anything. However, can you see the light?'

Tamakowake: 'Nay, the pain seems to have been greatly alleviated, but I cannot see a single bit, wow.'

Michikō: 'Heh, what are you saying, can your eyes not be seen? This is terrible. It is like the engine of a steamship bursting right in the middle of the Atlantic Ocean; what on earth should we do from now on, ah?'

Tamakowake: 'Do not worry. I cannot make out the shapes of things, but it seems that all around can be seen dimly bright. In any case, when the fever goes down, it will probably return to how it was before. That this has come about is the retribution for the crime that I made my own safety the primary concern, feared the fierce gale, and forgot to pray to the vital deities or to use the kototama to drive away the wind deity. Truly, I have received a good lesson. If only you would open even one eye like the Hokkō Kami, it would be splendid, ah.'

Michikō gazed intently at Tamakowake's both eyes, and 'Yo, this is a greater wound than I thought. Moshimoshi Sensei, the right eye has been completely crushed and ruined. The one that still holds promise is the left eye.'

Tamakowake: 'The left eye is the Sun Great Deity, and the right eye is the Moon Great Deity. That my right eye, which should be matched to the Moon Great Deity, was taken by a monkey on the way out on our expedition to conquer the Moon Land is without a doubt entirely divine punishment. No doubt the Kun no Ōkami has taken away my eyes. Ah, Kamunagara Emiwamasu.'

Michikō: 'Hey Itakō, Sumikō, come on, we cannot just sit around like this; from now on, shall the three of us go down to the bottom of the valley, perform Mizugori with all our might, and pray for Sensei\'s eyes?'

Just as they were speaking thus, along the valley path below, a party was ascending towards the northeast while singing the Sendenka. This was the party of Kērisu, Tākusu, and Pōro, carrying a letter from Terukuniwake, heading for ascetic practice at Isoyakata.

Michikō: 'Hey, that voice is the Sendenka of Ananai-kyō, isn\'t it? Oh Sensei, surely that must be our allies. Shall we chase after them and have them perform Chinkon for your eye affliction?'

Tamakuniwake: 'As one who embodies the status of a Sendenshi, to have had my eyes clawed out by a yamazaru, how could such a thing be done? Ashamed as I am, how could I ask for that? Since there is no path other than leaving everything in the hands of Kamisama, your Goshinsetsu is appreciated, but please, stop at least that much.'

Michikō: 'Even if you say that, in a case of emergency and peril, can you afford to be saying such things? By now, neither shame nor reputation matters at all. No matter how high in divine virtue a Sendenshi may be, injuries are something that go around, so there should be no such thing as it becoming a shame. Hey Itakō, Sumikō, what are you dawdling for? Is there any fellow who weeps in this thousand-risks-and-one-chance situation? Will you not quickly seek out the voice of the Sendenka and ask them?'

Itakō: 'That is also true. Hey Sumikō, it is troublesome for you too, but follow along with me.'

Sumikō: 'All right, understood. We must make haste; haste makes waste. Calm your spirits and let us go leisurely yet swiftly.'

Michikō: 'Please do so. Come, come, quickly, quickly, joining our hands, today I will ask them.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Hey you three, will you not listen to what I say no matter what, do you intend to bring shame upon me?'

Scratching his head, Michikō:

Michikō: 'Well anata, how can we remain unconcerned like this?'

Tamakuniwake: 'In the teachings of Kamisama, there is the saying: Do not lean on people as a staff, do not rely on your relatives as strength... Since I will pray to Kamisama myself regarding my eyes and have Him do something about it, please stop at least that much, I beg of you.'

Michikō: 'Hey Itai, Sumi, is there really no help for this?'

Itakō: 'She is our Sensei, so it is fine if the three of us pray to Kamisama to have her healed. It would also not do to expose Sensei\'s shame to other Sendenshi, you see.'

Tamakuniwake joined her hands towards the heavens, chanted the Amatsu Norito, praised the divine name of Ōkunitachi no Ōkami, and confessed her sins. Her words:

'O Great Ōkunitachi no Ōkami, who art the Master of Takamanohara, the Great Primal Spirit of One Spirit, Four Souls, Three Origins, and Eight Powers, I have been endowed with the precious spiritual energy and body of Your August Deity, and having been born into this world, from my tender youth I have committed only various and sundry evil things, defiled the world, impaired the Way, tormented people, made parents weep, caused trouble to others, and at the very end, became a Sendenshi of the Ural Teaching, and was saved by Hinodewake no Ōkami, and was made into a full-fledged Sendenshi. That You did not cast us aside even such sinful ones, did not turn Your face away, but did look upon us again, listen to us again, and proclaim to us anew with Your wide and thick Great August Heart, and used us as precious Sendenshi—for us sinful ones, this is supreme glory. Bathing in such vast and boundless divine grace, yet before I knew it, giving rise to arrogance and assuming the air of a Sendenshi of the world, thinking to heal and save the blind, deaf, mute, and lame of the world, I came bravely and eagerly all the way here, of which I feel truly ashamed. I gratefully appreciate the divine virtue whereby today and now, by borrowing the hands of a yamazaru, You have gouged out both our eyes, purified our defiled hearts, opened the eyes of our clouded hearts, and saved our body and spirit to brightness. Since it is said that the human body is the living palace of Kamisama, we must protect this physical body with utmost care to the very end, but through the foolishness of my own heart, I have damaged and broken this precious fleshly palace, and I have no words of apology to offer to Your August Deity who gave us our spirit and flesh. I have committed a truly inexcusable impropriety. Even if Tamakuniwake should lose the sight of both eyes, if You will but let the eyes of the heart be illuminated at least, I intend to fulfill the mission bestowed upon me by Lord Susanoo no Ōkami to the very end, and return to report to the hall of Isogakata. Beyond this, I shall never make unreasonable petitions. I respectfully pray that, in accordance with the divine providence of Kannagara, You will dispose of us entirely according to Your august will.'

So she finished her prayer, and tears were flowing from both eyes like rain. Seeing this state as well, the three of them were choked with sudden tears, pressed their faces to the earth, and concentrated their prayers of gratitude. Tamakuniwake was still making confession and repentance with all her might toward the Great Deities of Heaven and Earth. Wonder of wonders, her left eye suddenly grew bright, and the conditions of all around came to be seen as if in the palm of her hand. Choking with tears of joy, Tamakuniwake clapped her hands and bowed twice again to thank the divine grace.

Tamakuniwake: 'Oh Michikō, Itakō, Sumikō, rejoice. Somehow one of my eyes has begun to see. Kamisama has helped sinful Tamakuniwake. Ah, how thankful, how thankful!'

And she joined her hands again. The three of them were startled and rejoiced at these words,

'Ah, how thankful, how unworthy!'

joining their hands all together, they enthusiastically began to chant the Amatsu Norito once more. Ah, Kannagara Tamachihachimase.

玉国別『コリヤ三人、どうしても俺のいふ事を聞かぬのか、俺に恥をかかす積りか』

道公は頭を掻き乍ら、

道公『ダツて貴方、これが如何して安閑として居られませうか』

玉国別『神様の教に、人を杖につくな、身内を力にするな……といふ事がある。俺の目は俺が神様に祈つて何とかして貰ふから、どうぞそれ丈はやめてくれ、頼みだから』

道公『オイ伊太、純、どうも仕方がないぢやないか』

伊太公『俺達の先生だもの、俺達三人が神様に祈つて直して貰へばいいのだ。外の宣伝使に先生の恥を曝すのも済まないからなア』

玉国別は天に向つて合掌し、天津祝詞を奏上し、……国治立大神の神名を称へて、罪を謝した。其詞、

『高天原の主宰にして、一霊四魂三元八力の大元霊にまします大国治立大神様、私は貴神の尊き霊力体を賦与せられ、此地上に生れ来て、幼少の頃よりいろいろ雑多の善からぬ事のみ致しまして、世を汚し、道を損ひ、人を苦め、親を泣かせ、他人に迷惑をかけ、しまひの果にはウラル教の宣伝使となり、日の出別神様に救はれて一人前の宣伝使として頂きました。かかる罪深き吾々をも捨て玉はず、きため玉はず、広き厚き大御心に見直し聞直し詔直し下さいまして、尊き宣伝使にお使ひ下さいました事は、罪深き吾々に取つては、無上の光栄で厶います。かかる広大無辺なる御恩寵に浴し乍ら、知らず知らずの間に慢心を致し天下の宣伝使気分になつて、世の中の盲聾唖躄などを癒やし助けむと、勇み進んで此処迄参りました事を、誠に恥かしく存じます。今日只今山猿の手を借つて、吾々の両眼を刔出し、汚れたる心を清め、曇りたる心の眼を開かせ、身霊を明きに救ひ玉ひし其御恩徳を有難く感謝致します。人間の体は神様の生宮とある以上は何処迄も大切に此肉体を守らねばならないので厶いますが、自分の心の愚昧より大切なる肉の宮を損ひ破り、吾々の霊肉を与へ下さいました貴神様に対してお詫の申上げやうも厶いませぬ。誠にすまない無調法を致しました。仮令玉国別両眼の明を失する共、せめては心の眼を照らさせ下さいますれば神素盞嗚大神様より依さし玉ひし吾使命を飽迄も果たし、斎苑の館に復命をさして頂く考へで厶います。此上は御無理な願は決して致しませぬ。何卒々々惟神の御摂理に依りて、御心の儘にお取成し下さいます様に謹んで御願を申上げます』

と願ひ終り、両眼より雨の如く涙を流してゐる。三人も此有様を見て、思はず落涙にむせび、大地にかぶり付いて感謝の祈願を凝らしてゐる。玉国別は尚も一生懸命に、天地の大神に対し、懴悔の告白をなしつつあつた。不思議や左の目は俄に明くなり、四辺の状況は手に取る如く見えて来た。玉国別は嬉し涙に咽び乍ら、又もや拍手再拝して神恩を感謝する。

玉国別『イヤ道公、伊太公、純公、喜んでくれ。どうやら片眼が見え出したやうだ。神様は罪深き玉国別を助けて下さつた。あゝ有難し有難し』

と又もや合掌。三人は此言葉に驚喜し、

『あゝ有難し勿体なし』

と一斉に合掌し、勢込んで再び天津祝詞を奏上し始めた。あゝ惟神霊幸倍坐世。
