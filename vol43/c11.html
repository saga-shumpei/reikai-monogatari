§ 1

Harukuniwake and his party are looking down with amusement as the troops of Katahiko and Kumehiko, mounted on horseback, climb up in a most distressed state.

Mankō: 'Yo, they've come, they've come, the formidable enemy has made their arrival. The spectacle of them marching up this narrow slope in a winding fashion like a long serpent looks just like looking at a picture scroll. Frosted arrowroot coming, coming, lean horses farting as they go, it is a bizarre and grotesque massive force. Well then, let us wait here leisurely. Even supposing there are hundreds of men, since it is impossible to advance from anything more than a single file, if we deal with them one by one in a domino effect, it requires neither trouble nor time; truly, they are fellows with wretched luck. To have a single soldier defend it means that ten thousand soldiers cannot advance—such an impregnable strategic point is this very place. Aha-ha-ha-ha, Harukuniwake-sama, how delightful it is!'

Harukuniwake: 'Seeing the foes advancing to attack, as expected, they take the shape of the eight-forked giant serpent.'

Mankō: 'How interesting, while forming a centipede's battle array, the enemy approaching will surely end up like a dragon's head and a snake's tail.'

Harukuniwake: 'Setting out far away from the capital of Haruna, they are now frothing at the mouth at Kawajika Pass.'

Gosankō: 'Blowing bubbles on this mountain path where the wind blows, seeing this, Gosankō blows his own horn.'

Harukuniwake: 'Be mindful, you three officials of Kamagara, never dare to oppose the gods by leaving things to yourselves in dreams.'

Mankō: 'Is there any reason we should overlook and send back the perverse spirits who defile the mansion of the Lord God?'

Harukuniwake: 'Since all human beings are children of Heaven and Earth, how could we hate them even if they are enemies?'

Gosankō: 'Though the horse's footing climbing up this single steep slope appears precarious,'

Harukuniwake sits down leisurely by the side of the slope, looking down at the massive advancing force of the enemy,

'Hey, you three fellows, until this Harukuniwake begins negotiations with the enemy's general, you must not become flustered from your side; I warn you strictly.'

Mankō: 'If you say that and just sit there with an indifferent face, will they not become full of themselves and march right into Saienkan? Now that it has come to this, if you say such a thing, somehow the enthusiasm we had built up completely drains away, does it not?'

Harukuniwake: 'Against this mere sliver of an enemy army, while taking up a position at such an advantageous point, we do not need any particular effort or anything. Is it not fine to catch them one by one, bathe them in the kototama of good words and beautiful phrases, and make them submit to the True Way? However, if you fellows panic, there might be cases where our hard-earned operational plan comes to naught, which would be like falling short of a mountain's summit by a single basket of earth. First of all, it would be best for you to calm down.'

Mankō: 'Even if you say so, somehow my spirit is eager and will not be contained. I would be most grateful if you would assign the vanguard of this kototama warfare to me.'

Harukuniwake: 'Un, do it well; from here I will observe how you launch your kototama. I will lend you Harukō and Gosankō, so the three of you unite your hearts and unleash bullets of compassion toward the enemy. Is that all right? Do you understand?'

Gosankō: 'Eh, you just told me to unleash self-financed bullets, but I possess neither self-financed bullets nor officially funded bullets, so what should I do?'

Mankō: 'Eh, don't babble about things you don't even understand. Compassion means grace. To put it another way, it means affection. He is telling you to face the enemy with a heart of sympathy. It seems your worldly-minded head can never be shed, and you immediately interpret everything statistically, rushing to bring it to such a place like an accountant or something. It appears the grime of material desires cannot be washed off you at all, alas. You truly are a troublesome fellow. Look here, Teacher, if you let such a fellow participate in kototama warfare, it will rather be to the disadvantage of our allies. Since I respectfully return Gosankō alone to you, please wrap him up in some toilet paper and stuff him away into your sleeve by twelve o'clock tomorrow.'

Isokō: 'What are you blabbering about? Aren't you treating me like a mere insect? Can you call yourself a Kamutsukasa who uses the Kototama of Zengenbishi with such an attitude? You truly are a wretched fellow of bad Kototama.'

The enemy army had climbed the steep slope with a huff and a puff, barely reaching four or five ken ahead. Looking straight down the bottom of the valley, a cavalry unit extended for some twenty or thirty chō. Mankō strode right up in front of the knight standing at the vanguard, spread his arms wide, and said,

Mankō: 'You soldiers of Brahmanism, wait a moment.'

Kishi: 'Are you attempting to obstruct our advance? What an outrageous fellow. Step aside; if you dawdle, the tip of this spear shall pay you a visit.'

Mankō: 'Aha-ha-ha-ha, blabbering and more blabbering, you spineless warrior, who do you think you are dealing with? Unthinkingly and undeservedly, I am an apprentice missionary of the Izusan Cult, protected by Ōkuniharutachinomikoto, who manifests as the ancestral deity of Heaven and Earth. Hearing that your troop today would cross Kajika Pass and launch an attack upon the mansion of Saien, I lay in wait right here. Now, if you can advance even a single step beyond this point, go ahead and try.'

With these words, he spread his arms wide, raised and lowered his eyebrows, rolled his eyes frantically, and striking a theatrical pose, he delivered his display.

Kishi: 'Aha-ha-ha-ha, you pesky little blowflies unworthy of even being our attendants, I shall listen to your big talk later. Now, you shall be the rust of my spear; prepare your mind.'

Mankō: 'Hey, if you keep blabbering sluggishly, it will trigger the discharge of the Kototama.'

Kishi: 'Aha-ha-ha-ha-ha, what a grotesque fellow has appeared.'

As one of the men in the vanguard stopped, the cavalry units that had been following along gradually came to a halt on the steep slope, falling into a state of a complete deadlock. From the rear, General Kumehiko was brandishing his baton and issuing stern orders of 'Advance, advance!' In the rear guard, yet another general was brandishing his baton and chiding, 'Advance, advance!' Kishi: 'Hey there, you little brats, open the road.'

Kuniwake: 'Hey there, Mankō, I have never commanded you to engage in strife. Why do you not use the Kototama of Zengenbishi?'

Kuniwake: 'If that is so, Harukō, you take his place and discharge the Kototama.'

Harukō: 'Yes, I have understood clearly.'

Dismount from your horses quickly, and with your hearts calmed, listen to the teachings that save the people of the world, of the Izusan Cult of Zengenbishi.
