Michikō: 'Surrender thyself to the heart of the Kamunagara Kami, soon clearing away is the autumn's great sky.'

Mankō: 'Becoming parent, becoming child, and once again as brothers, to be born too must be the workings of Kami. Nevertheless, the living must surely die, and those who meet must part; what shall we make of this floating world's appearance?'

Harukō: 'Pondering deeply upon the master's profound heart, hazy indeed has become my own clear heart.'

Isokō: 'Master, think on it with a tranquil heart, shall not the flowers of the body that trusts bloom forth? Itakō, who lurked in the mountains of Kiyoharu, will be accompanied back by the two, Matsu and Tatsu. The two friends, Matsu and Tatsu, will presently hither return, brimming with smiles.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Hearing Matsu's words a moment ago, it appears that upon this mountain path the military forces of Oniharuwake are lying in wait in great numbers; as things have come to this, there is no particular need for us to hurry. Shall we decide to wait for a while until the enemy forces pass through this mountain path?'

Harukuniwake: 'That too may be one divine strategy. Even though there should be tens of thousands of enemy forces, having entrusted ourselves to Kami we are not startled in the least, yet simply chasing and scattering the enemy in all directions would be like chasing flies above food; to attack and come again to the Saien Hall will inevitably be certain. By no means is there any better plan than either making them surrender from the bottom of their hearts, or else holding this difficult pass and blocking and stopping their advance.'

Tamakuniwake: 'I shall pray before the shrine for a while until the easing of my eye pain. Please, do you return to your original place, nurture your vigor, and prepare for the enemy who returns to repeat the attack.'

Harukuniwake: 'If that is so, I will be excused for a while. Come, come Mankō, Harukō, let us go.'

Thus he goes first. Behind remain both Tamakuniwake and Michikō. Isokō and Junko also followed Harukuniwake and plunged their bodies into the shade of the woods. The late autumn wind blew violently once more. The half-ruined shrine made creaking, strange sounds and began to wail. The treetops of the trees blew whistles like fuu-fuu. Dry leaves fell scatteringly, patter-patter-patter. Even cold rain mixed in, and roughly beat upon the head of Tamakuniwake, whose eyes were bad. The two quickly put on straw raincoats, tied their sedge hats firmly, and managed to avoid the rain and wind behind the shrine.

The sun gradually inclined toward the western mountains, and the voices of birds deciding their roosts could be heard noisily from the valleys here and there. The wind chilling the skin through thin garments, the village showers falling from time to time—as the day drew to a close, desolation pressed ever more closely upon their bodies. Michikō tightly embraced Tamakuniwake's body from behind to keep his body warm, forgetting his own coldness and caring for him. The paroxysmal pain of the head, the intermittent pain of the eyes—it was an unspeakable suffering. Tamakuniwake secretly prayed to Kami and moreover apologized for the sins of his body. At this time, crushing the evening valley, the voice of a propaganda song could be heard.

'Of the Almighty God of supreme benevolence and supreme love, having become the august heart, the august teachings of the Anagama, to open and illuminate completely without leaving a place in the fruitful reed-plain land of Midzuho, and wishing to go forth revering the divine words of the Divine God Susanoo, you, the sound-lord of my dear husband, where are you now dwelling? Praying that within the sacred hall of the hall of Saien, you may bow down and make my husband accomplish his meritorious deeds, even as I pray, oh how wondrous, he sight reflected in my eyes appeared in the valley of Kaitani, famed even in the Kajika Pass, surrounded ten-fold and twenty-fold by troops of young monkeys, and at the very end of it, losing his two eyes, the state in which he is suffering, my heart that gazed upon it, to what shall I compare it, there is no way either; settling my heart and hardening my liver, bowing down before the august presence of Kami, and asking for the august imperial decree, among the august words of the Great August Goddess, Isokohime of the hall of Saien, to save her husband's distress, now making the child princess follow her, to go forth even a moment sooner, with the gratefulness of her august command, feeling as though climbing even to heaven, with a light and eager heart to Kajika Mountain, crossing over and coming as far as this place. Ah, Kamunagara Kamigami, may your august spirits prosper and bless, and saving the distress of my dear husband, may you accomplish the divine work of the divine transfer of Anagama completely and in full detail.'

Following after the footsteps of my master,

Pacifying by word, and sweeping away

The ogres, giant serpents, and hideous kami ministers

That block the four quarters of the Land of the Moon,

Treading down and tearing through root and rock,

She advances, yielding her feet to the path—

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara,

Let us advance before the mimahe of my master,

Advancing with courage, whithersoever

O Great Deity who art the August Parent of Heaven and Earth,

Great Deity Kami Susanoo,

Right before the august presence of the deity of Hinodewake

And the August Kami Konohanahime,

Piercing the very nape of the neck do I entreat Thee!'

Thus singing and making her way down toward the front of the grove of the shrine was Imako no hime. Isokohime and Imako no hime knew not even in their dreams that Tamakuniwake was sheltering from wind and rain behind this shrine,

Isokohime: 'Ah, Imako-san, we have safely reached the grove of the shrine at last. Thanks to this, the wind has calmed and the rain has stopped, so let us offer our prayers here and rest our feet for a while. If night falls so completely soaked as this, the mountain path will be perilous, you see.'

Imako no hime: 'Yes, let us do so. Somehow it is a venerable and solemn grove. I cannot shake the feeling that Tamakuniwake-sama must surely be dwelling within this very grove.'

Isokohime: 'Do you feel so too? I also feel, somehow or other, that it is a strangely nostalgic grove. Come, let us pause here for a moment.'

The two of them clapped their hands and bowed deeply, offering up their earnest prayers toward the half-ruined shrine.

At this time, Tamakuniwake, held in the arms of Michinokimi, was sleeping peacefully and comfortably, but suddenly opened his eyes,

'Ah, Michinokimi, you have had a hard time. You have been holding and supporting me all this while, haven't you. Thanks to you, I was able to sleep warmly. My headache has healed. I feel as though I have even forgotten the pain in my eyes. Ah, how thankful I am; this joy I shall never, ever forget.'

Michinokimi: 'Sensei, what are you saying? Is there ever a case of a disciple thanking his master? Even for me, thanks to being allowed to hold you, I slept truly warmly and soundly without even realizing it. This too is due to the august radiance of Sensei. You must not thank me; it is I who should be offering my thanks to you.'

Tamakuniwake: 'As Kamunagara, your garment of affection

Has shielded me from the chilling wind.'

Michinokimi: 'Receiving the warmth of my master's august body,

My very soul has been revived.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Setting forth on a journey, I have learned the warmth of human kindness;

A body that walks ever serving the Kami and the Way.'

Michikō: 'Though it is a ruined ancient shrine, new dews of grace have been bestowed upon us.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Harukuniwake no Mikoto, the ritual officiant, must surely be suffering terribly in the night wind. The wife I left behind in my lodging must indeed be anxiously searching for the whereabouts of my person.'

Isokohime and Imakohime, being quick-witted, heard the song faintly leaking from behind the shrine, and leaping up in great delight, pressing down their throbbing chests, thought, "Is my husband dwelling right here?"

Isokohime: 'Hearing the voice of my dear beloved lord, the moonlit shadow in my heart has grown so clear.'

Imakohime: 'My dear lord and master dwells peacefully, embraced by the shadow of Sumekami.'

Michikō spoke in a low voice,

'Moshi Sensei, did you hear that song? It certainly sounds like Isokohime-sama.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Un, it is definitely Isokohime. The other one can be none other than Imakohime.'

Michikō: 'Instead of saying such things, why don't you go and meet them quickly? Isokohime-sama has come all the way here, longing for and following after you.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Un, I yearn to meet them more than anything, but I cannot meet them now. Pitiful though it is, I must send them back to Isoyakata.'

Isokohime: 'Moshi, is that not my husband Tamakuniwake-sama residing there? Hearing from the Kamisama that you had sustained a terrible injury, and being beside ourselves with worry, Imako-san and I came following after you. How is your condition? Please let us know.'

Tamakuniwake: 'The blind eyes of my heart have opened, while my right eye was taken by a monkey.'

Isokohime: 'How cruel! The monkey that gouged out the eyes of my dear lord is hateful indeed.'

Imakohime: 'At any rate, please come forth, my lord and master, out of pity for the heart of Isokohime.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Though I greatly desire to meet my wife, the divine mission cannot be taken lightly. People would say that the official Tamakuniwake was rescued by his divine wife. How could I ever let her see these shameful eyes of mine, O wife of mine, like fresh wakaba?'

Michikō: 'Moshi Sensei, you don't need to be so overly scrupulous about such things, do you? Since your wife has come, who could possibly say anything? That is precisely the affection of a husband and wife, so come, let us go right up to the front of the shrine.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Well then, perhaps I shall go out and see them after all.'

Led by the hand of Michikō and relying on his staff for support, he came out before the shrine. Gazing upon her husband's face in the light of the seventeenth-night moon as it gradually rose over the edge of the mountain, Isokohime cried out,

Isokohime: 'Yaa, the scratches are far worse than I imagined! Oh, what ever shall we do, Imakohime-sama?'

Imakohime: 'How heartbreaking it is; I know not what to say, and words fail me. Yet do not worry, surely the Kamisama will heal you.'

Isokohime: 'Moshi, my dear husband, does it hurt you very much?'

Tamakuniwake: 'Un, it hurts just a little.'
