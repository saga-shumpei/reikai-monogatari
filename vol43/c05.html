Chapter 5: 感謝 Song [1156]

感謝歌〔1156〕

§ 1

Tamakuniwake: 'The grace of Ōkami has opened with such detail, / I, the official of the clear eye, Tamakuniwake. / How grateful I am, illuminated by the grace of Kami, / The darkness of Tokoyo has cleared away. / Perhaps it is to make me the official of the Kitateru Kami, / That one eye has been taken from me. / From now on, with my heart as one eye and my body as one eye, / I shall illuminate the light of Kami. / In a world where people are blind and in such a state, / Am I the happy Mahitotsu Kami? / The office of Tamakuniwake Kami, from now on, / I shall polish my Mitama and devote myself to the path. / Offering my soul to Kami and the Mikuni, / I shall advance along the path through the wilderness. / The darkness of Tokoyo has cleared, and my eye, / Has begun to shine with a Hitoshiho of clarity. / My eye, which was clawed by a Yamazaru, / Has saved the soul of Tamakuniwake. / I regret the enmity of my own body, / Which has accomplished evil things since long ago.'

Tamakuniwake: 'My soul has been revived in the Mikuni of Kami, / And I have seen the light of the Fukachisekai. / From the moment I lost my physical eye, / I was able to attain enlightenment regarding the Sekai of Kami. / The Katametsukasa shall illuminate, / The countries of the Yomo under heaven without a corner left behind. / Kami must have determined it as the eternal Katame, / The Mihashira of the country that stands in my heart. / Having now lost one eye, / I have hardened the navel of my Shiyozon. / I shall advance quickly along the Ōmichi of Kami, / Directing the Magagoto that obstruct my Yukute. / The Oshihe of the Ananahi Kami is indeed Arigata, / A path to save the blindness of the heart.'

Michikō: 'The command of Tamakuniwake Kami must be hardening our weak hearts. / Having crossed the Kajika mountain, the troop of monkeys, / Has awakened the eyes of us three.'

Itakō: 'It is painful, and as I look at your eyes, / Tears spill into my own eyes. / The command of Tokiokishi Kami, / Came riding upon a lion and saved me.'

Tamakuniwake: 'In my body, which had lost the light of its eyes, / I had not realized the appearance of Kami. / How venerable, are you manifesting to save the four of us, / Tokiokishi Kami? / The Kami of heaven and earth have protected us, / How venerable is the fact that you have helped us.'

Junkō: 'Even the autumn day when the vast sky is clear, / Sometimes has moments of being wrapped in clouds. / The water bottle was dropped on the path, and the way Itakō, / Flustered and made a fuss, was amusing. / In the rock water scooped down in the mountain stream, / The spirit of the water has washed away my troubles. / The divine virtue of the Mizumitama Kami Susanoo, / Perhaps flows even to the bottom of the valley. / Mizumitama brings happiness and is the light of the world, / The salvation of the Ananahi Kami is venerable.'

Michikō: 'Every time I meet a person on the path, I wish to preach and transmit, / The Oshihe of the Ananahi Kami. / Now, I have realized the divine authority of Kami, / Since the eyes of my teacher have opened. / I have realized anew the Oshihe of Kami, / That a time will come when neither eyes nor nose will open. / In a body that follows Kannagara, / What is there to fear on the path of the world?'

Tamakuniwake: 'By worshipping the light of the Kami of heaven and earth, / Even my Mitama has become bright. / Where is the command of Hinodewake Kami now? / May he dwell in peace under the great grace. / I wish to hear news of Yoshikohime and Kiyoteruhime, / As I am on a journey. / I shall revere the heart of the Ōkami who forgave my sins, / And teach of the divine reign. / That I am a body of deep sin, / I did not realize until this very moment when my eye was broken. / Reflecting upon it, that my body had become a vessel of evil, / Is truly shameful.'

Michikō: 'The Chihayaburu heavenly rock door has opened, / The eyes of my teacher are clear. / While entrusting my heart to Kami, / I must advance through the wilderness. / I shall guide all those who have lost their way on the Ōmichi, / To the Mikuni of Kami.'

Itakō: 'Unexpectedly, through the distress of my teacher, / I, Itakō, have now awakened. / Although my two eyes fortunately shine, / I grieve for the darkness of my spiritual eye.'

Junkō: 'When I look at the moon crossing the clear vast sky, / My heart becomes ashamed. / Although the moon and the sun illuminate the lower world, / Sometimes black clouds obstruct them, which is hateful.'

Tamakuniwake rejoices in having gained the light of his left eye, follows the three, descends the mountain to a slope, and goes down while singing a propaganda song.

'The plum blossoms of the three thousand worlds, / The tree flowers that bloom all at once, / The protection of Sakuya-hime, / Appearing as Mokusuke-tsukasa, / Riding on the back of a lion, / The troop of monkeys that were raging violently, / Were chased and scattered beyond the peaks, / The gratitude of having saved, / Our blind party, / Although the right eye is lost, / Our destiny is not yet exhausted, / Chosen as an official of Kami, / A propaganda envoy to Kotomu and harmonize, / The raging evil Kami, / The love of Kami that allows this, / With humility, from now on, / Even a hundred hardships I shall not loathe, / For the sake of the Kami of heaven and earth, / With sincerity as the one, the Ananahi, / Using the Oshihe as a shield, the countries of the Yomo.'

§ 2

Marching bravely and opening the path wide,

Ah, how grateful, how grateful!

God is with us always,

Born as the children of God,

The common people need not even be mentioned,

Down to every single leaf of grass and trees,

Bestowing the dews of grace.

Crossing the Thames Pass and the fierce currents

Of the lion's roar, overcoming even the steep slopes

Of Mount Tamayama,

To serve in the divine work entrusted by God,

We are a band of four.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara!

May the divine spirit bless us,

To accomplish the divine work completely and in full detail,

Before the presence of the noble God

Of heaven and earth,

Tamakuniwake offers his sincere heart

And prays devotedly.

Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over,

Whether the moon waxes or wanes,

Even if the great earth should sink,

How could we ever forget

The depth of God's grace?

The soul of a Yamato man of Shikishima

Is a mulberry bow that pierces even rocks,

A diamond heart that draws and never returns.

Tamakuniwake, whose light is as pure

As the sun and moon shining across the sky,

Casteth aside all things in the world,

For the sake of God and for the sake of the world,

Bearing truth even to the ends of Tsukushi,

Marching bravely forth!

Michikimi, Itaikimi, Junkimi yoin,

You too are children of God and palaces of God,

Follow me to any place whatsoever,

Learning the divine will

Of the Great Deity of supreme benevolence and supreme love,

And fulfilling your roles as pure ministers.

Illuminating and saving every corner

Of lands and islands near and far,

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara!

Descending the steep slope of the Kajika Pass

Guarded by God,

Tamakuniwake's profound sincerity

Is openly expressed with reverence as he prays.

Michikimi began to sing while keeping time with his feet.

Following Tamakuniwake down the steep slope of the Kajika Pass,

To the foot of the valley of Kaikoku,

Just as we had managed to arrive,

How pathetic it was to be blown about

By an unprecedented gale!

Starting with our master, our revered lord,

We three timid cowards

Clung tightly to the roots of trees,

Fanned by the cold wind,

Trembling in our misery.

The night grew deeper and more profound,

Kyakkyakkyaku went the voices of monkeys,

In the twinkling of an eye, thousands of them

Surrounded us from all four directions and eight quarters.

Their intimidation touched our impudence

And made us angry,

As we stood glaring at them.

The monkey rascals grew even more arrogant,

Drawing closer and closer to attack.

Itaikimi-sama with nose held high

Laid out a long speech,

And began to sing out of tune and without rhythm,

Recklessly and wildly chanting a propaganda song.

Even the monkey rascals were dumbfounded,

And murmuring noisily aszawaszawa,

They closed in on us with a pricking chikichiki.

Itaikimi, that rascal, recklessly

Seized one of the monkeys

And hurled it down with all his might.

Ah, that is when the real trouble began!

Despising us as a small party,

Relying on their numbers from all sides,

They pressed upon us with sharpened claws.

Among them, a superior large monkey

Came up from behind Tamakuniwake,

Without letting out a single cry like kyakku,

Scratched out both of his eyes,

And fled away raising a victory cry.

The numerous other small monkey rascals

Each picked up stones,

And hurled them upon us like rain and hail.

Danger approached every moment,

And just as we wondered what on earth to do,

A faint propaganda song was heard,

Followed shortly by the roar of a lion.

The monkeys, who had relied on their numbers,

Let out a single cry, turned their backs,

And how delightful it was to see them fleeing away

Like clouds and mist along the mountain ridges!

When we looked around us, what should this be?

Tamakuniwake, the divine minister,

Holding his eyes while crimson blood

Dripped down drop by drop,

Crouching upon the grass to endure the pain,

How sorrowful it truly was!

We three were thrown into panic,

And first sent Itaikimi off to the valley stream

With a canteen to draw water.

Being a scatterbrain, he lost the canteen on the way,

And returned with nothing to do.

Muttering that he was of no use at this most vital time,

We added Junkimi to the party

And sent them back down to the bottom of the valley to draw water.

With this and that, it was a great uproar.

Our master, the revered lord, joined his hands upon the earth

And offered apologies to the gods of heaven and earth.

Divine virtue manifested itself immediately,

The pain in the eyes was alleviated,

Gradually one eye was saved,

And how joyful it was to worship

The light of this world once again!

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara!

The grace of God is right before our eyes.

From that very moment, I too realized

The profound grace of the August Deities,

And my state of mind was transformed instantly.

How precious it is to have been changed

Into a believer firm in faith,

Who will not budge an inch no matter what!

The divine teachings of the noble path,

Where God appears in the open

To distinguish clearly between good and evil,

Are now understood more keenly than ever.

No matter how deep one's sins and crimes may be,

If one prays with a sincere heart,

God's love is proclaimed anew with a vast heart,

Reviewed, and swiftly forgiven.

Itaikimi and Junkimi, both of you,

This place is a steep slope worthy of its name,

Be careful of your toe tips,

And pray do not suffer any injuries.

I too will be careful from here on,

And descend quite leisurely down the slope

Which is steep as a board set on end.

Knowing that even this is entirely

The noble grace of the August Deities,

Never forget God even for a single moment!

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara!

May the divine spirits bless us and thrive!
