Chapter 10: Night の昼 [1161]

夜の昼〔1161〕

§ 1

Appearing at the mansion of Iso, As the Savior of the Mizu no Mitama, By the divine decree Of Kamususanōnōokami, Kamehiko, reverently receiving it, Renamed himself Harukuniwake, And accompanied by three attendants, Mankō, Harukō, and Isokō, Parting from his wife Kikukohime, The divine teaching and her command, Through the autumn fields where the wild withered winds blow, Letting his feet wander step by step, At the moment he advanced and arrived At the mountain foot of Kajikatauge, So that even the thousand-layered boulders might scatter, In the Shinado wind that blew Almost to the point of disbelief, Exposing his face, at last to the summit, Breathing heavily he climbed up, Sat upon the solemnity of the surroundings, And gazing far out over the wilderness in the four directions,

To look back and ponder upon My past steps and my future path is delightful.

Mankō: 'Senseisama, what a fine landscape this is, is it not? The sight of gazing out over the four directions from the summit of Kajikatauge is always wonderful, but please look at that. Far across the vast wilderness, draped in a robe of white clouds, that high mountain sticking its head out so neatly—is it not an indescribably proper figure? Below the eighth station it is wrapped in cotton robes, the top of its head is densely overgrown with evergreens, and around its waist it draws tight a belt of white clouds—to call this a sight is indescribably delightful; no, what a view it is. Standing upon a mountain top thus gazing out over the four directions, somehow a mood drifts over one as if one had climbed up even to the First Heaven. I wish that forever we might gaze upon such a sublime scenery, and here for a thousand or ten thousand years stick and remain, do I not.'

Harukuniwake: 'That's right, just as you say, what a grand scenery it is. That Sahohime should weave brocade of this extent all at once across the boundless wilderness is a task that must demand a great deal of pains. Thinking upon this, natural power—nay, the power of God—is magnificent. Compared to the active workings of the marvelous mechanism of creation, verily our own activities feel as though they do not amount to even one hair of nine cows, and verily it feels as though we should be ashamed before Kami. Ah, what a happy thing it is for us, the children of God, to be allowed to lead our lives in such a beautiful earthly heaven, at ease and untroubled. The mountains, rivers, and wilderness created by God do not need to make noisy complaints from morning till night like us; at the time when flowers bloom they cause flowers to bloom for all alike equally, and at the time when fruits form they form fruits in a unified manner. Verily the power of God is immense.'

Harukō: 'Verily it is a bright and clear scenery, is it not. Is it heaven or is it earth, is it earth or is it heaven—is it not a scenery of Paradise where one can scarcely make any distinction? To receive life in this infinite and utmost universe, to enjoy the natural divine blessings to the fullest, and while being endowed with the authority to manipulate all things freely and at will, caught in small desires they dispute the boundaries of their estates, or wrangle over the ridges of rice fields—how I cannot understand the hearts of men. Coming to this present time and as I look upon this scenery, I have been astonished at the greatness of Kami's power. After all, when human beings hustle and bustle in low places and live without seeing the world, naturally their spirits become small, they are caught in small profits and petty desires, and they come to sow the seeds of suffering with their own hands. Ah, Kamunagara Tamashii Sakihai Mase.'

Harukuniwake: 'However, according to what we have heard from Ōkami, the army of Daikokunushi of Brahmanism will cross this pass and attack and come to the mansion of Iso. That you sent off our missionaries to the Land of the Moon in groups of four or five as well must be with a deep divine intention. For the Capital of Haruna and the like, the train of Yoshikohime-sama's party should be quite sufficient. In short, we have been dispatched to commence a Kototama warfare against the army of Daikokunushi. Were it not so, no matter how infinitely powerful Daikokunushi might be, such a grand-scale thing as almost all the missionaries of the mansion of Iso turning out together is a thing Ōkami would never do. You too must remain with that thought in mind. The Land of the Moon is the great country true to its name, called the Five Indias and broadly divided into five provinces, where the Kshatriya clans of over seven thousand countries become kings and fiercely compete with one another, manifesting the Asura path in this beautiful earthly heaven; therefore, reverently upholding the heart of Ōkami of infinite benevolence and compassion, we, the company, must by all means strive for activities of breaking bones and crushing bodies for the sake of the emergence of the Izumo-shinsei (the Five-Six-Seven Divine Government), for verily we have been given a momentous mission. We must give ample thanks to the Ōkami of Heaven and Earth. Ah, wondrous wondrous, Kamunagara Tamashii Sakihai Mase.'

He joins his palms in Gatsushiyō, closing his eyes and bowing his head.

Isokō: 'Moshibē Senseisama, if what you say is true, the army of Ōkuronushi will surely encounter us on the way, won't they?'

Harukuniwake: 'Mm, it cannot be long now. Each of us must securely tighten our haraobi.'

Isokō: 'Yes, ever since we set out from the Isoyakata, I have tightened it with a hosōbi so much that my belly has become like a heutan. It leaves red stripes and is almost painful, so I am quite all right. Still, as I am a little hungry, shall I have some bread or something here? If I don't, I must retighten it once more, or it feels as if it's going to slip off.'

Harukuniwake: 'Aha-ha-ha-ha.'

Mankō: 'Hey Iso, what an insensible fellow you are. That is not the kind of haraobi I mean. Tighten the haraobi of your heart... that is what he is saying.'

Isokō: 'The haraobi of the heart—what sort of thing is that? How on earth do you tighten an intangible haraobi? If you talk such Kuōtaumukei nonsense, you will be hauled off to the Brahmin police station on the charge of agitating the public mind.'

Mankō: 'Atsuha-ha-ha-ha! You are a thoroughly Wakarazuya, aren't you? One might say this is a fortunate disposition of Heaven's providence. Thinking that if such supreme sages and worthies were all gathered together, the journey would be tedious and utterly devoid of interest, I find it quite amusing that a clodhopper like Isokō is mixed in among us. To put it badly, it is a prank of Heaven; to put it well, it is Heaven's providence. Having you around, if only a little, may perhaps serve as medicine for our humors. Aha-ha-ha-ha!'

Isokō: 'Look here, isn't your mouth running a bit too much? What, taking a magnificent living shrine of the gods and treating me like some sort of quack bamboo doctor or the like—calling it Heaven's providence—aren't you making too much fun of me?'

Mankō: 'Heh, heh, heh.'

Isokō: 'Hey, just like when a tanuki is smoked with green pine needles, what are you snickering and coughing up? Listen well and digest what I have to say too, for it will serve as a plaster. You good-for-nothing coolie fellow, will something like that ever be of any use in an emergency, hey!'

Mankō: 'Never mind about such things. Quickly have some bread, fill your stomach solidly, and prepare for the enemy's onslaught. We cannot afford to dawdle.'

Isokō: 'Is there enough bread to offer to the enemy? I have no more than what I offer to the throat-gods and Buddha-gods enshrined in my own living shrine, so is there any need to bother taking care of an unnecessary enemy? To give provisions to the enemy is the height of foolishness.'

Mankō: 'From the way of the gods, there should never be such a thing as either enemy or ally. It is said that three hundred thousand years in the future, when great lords named Kenshin and Shingen fought a war in Jitentōjima, there was a noble deed where salt was sent to the enemy on one side. Therefore, to bring the enemy to submission through benevolence and mercy, virtue and awe must be wielded together, otherwise it will ultimately come to nothing. By your logic, it is entirely the Ural religious style—an egoistic spiritual principle that says as long as one is well oneself, others do not matter. With such an attitude, we cannot accompany Harukuniwake-sensei, who bears the great responsibility upon his double shoulders. Aah!'

Harukuniwake: 'Hey Mankō, Isokō, you had better stop this unnecessary sibling quarrel. Now, from here on is your stage of action.'

Mankō: 'Without seeing even a shadow of the enemy, if we put on twisted headbands and brace ourselves from now on, won't we grow weary of waiting and lose all our strength when the crucial moment actually arrives?'

Harukuniwake: 'No, no, in about half an hour it is certain that we will encounter the enemy army. Tamakuniwake and we have everything arranged to launch Kototama from the upper and lower parts of the slope and lead them to surrender to the true path. You must calm your minds well and make sure not to panic. This is a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity; if we miss this, there will be no other time to manifest military merit before the great presence of the gods.'

Mankō: 'Are the enemy closing in upon us that closely? Hearing this, we too cannot afford to be idle. Still, I suppose Yoshikohime-sama and Terukuniwake-sama's party must have had a major clash, n'est-ce pas?'
