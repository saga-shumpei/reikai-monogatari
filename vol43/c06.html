Chapter 6: 祠前 [1157]

祠前〔1157〕

§ 1

Leaving behind Futokorodani, where the hordes of wild monkeys had gathered, the party of Tamakuniwake, the kamutsukasa whose one eye had been taken, stepping with might and main foot by foot upon the steep slope where the gneiss and rocks were jagged, descended heading toward the south. In the fierce gale that blew yet again, while having their sedge hats torn off and their gown-skirts rolled up, with eyes fixed and in Abt style, thud, thud, thud, on their hizakurige, with the air of knowing masters they descended.

Itakō, who served as their mitomo, raising his shiwagaregoe, taking the rhythm step by step, while shedding sweat in a torrent, without caring a whit for the chilling wind,

Itakō: '"Untokodotsu-koidotsu-koishiyo!" Following Tamakuniwake, we set out from the mansion of the Iso, and in high spirits upon our hizakurige, we advanced climbing up and down. A fellow far worse than the wind blowing now came rushing with a sudden burst, and because the rude blast tried to scatter our bodies into chiuten without a grain of enriyoeshiyaku, our master, the deep-thinking sendenshi, being considerate of us wareware, firmly clung to the roots of a tree by the michibata, and said: "Untokodotsu-koi, it is dangerous!" Another wind began to blow. If you are careless, you will be scattered. Come, come, you two attendants, cling firmly to the roots of the tree, and wait for the wind to pass. Thus he spake with a dottsukoi, did he. As a godsend, the three of us, while stroking our pounding chests, trembling and quaking, passed the night. In the raging wind that blew once again, valuing our lives above all, in a single dash we ran back to Futokorodani to seek refuge, at which moment: "Aita-ta-ta-tatsu-ta, you damned beast!" I tripped upon a high stone. The monkey fellow came running up, and like a beast as he was, made fun of us: "Untokoshiyo!" Every time I see that: "Untokoshiyo!" It touches my raw nerve and I cannot bear it. A fellow who knows not a whit of enriyoeshiyaku at last came right up to me. Lord Itakō, putting strength into his arms, knocked down one monkey. While howling kyatsu, kyatsu, kyatsu, kyatsu, with untrammeled freedom: "Untokoshiyo!" The terror of their swarming upon us! The big monkey fellow came flying, and from behind my master's shoulders, without uttering a single cry of kyatsu or anything, clawed at his both eyes, and dropped him into a doterai plight! I went to draw water in the valley river, at which place: "Dotsukoishiyo!" To think of the foolishness when I dropped the vital canteen right into the brushwood: "Yattsukoishiyo!" Receiving nothing but grumbling scoldings from Lord Michikō, is there any place left for me to stand? Ah, how foolish: "Dotsukoishiyo!" Pulling the hand of Lord Junkō, wishing to inquire after the whereabouts of the canteen, as I went down, upon the grass, with the utmost nonchalance the canteen fellow was lying there with an innocent face! Giving his bottom a slap with "Why don't you be more firm?", Lord Junkō grabbed him tight, and plunging down into the deep valley, thrust the canteen in: bubbling, bubbling, bubbling! While blowing foam like a fart, he drank his fill! Thinking that I must not drop it this time, gripping his very neck tight, I brought him back into the august presence of my master, and when I washed his both eyes: "Untokodotsukoishiyo!" Suddenly the pain in his eyes ceased! The august divine virtue of the Mizu-no-Mitamah! Ah, how wondrous, how revered! At the moment I joined my hands in worship, the senden-ka is heard at our feet. This is surely none other than the kamutsukasa of the three and five! Chasing after the rear from this point on, if I prayed for the calming of spirits with "Untokodotsu-koi" and begged him to pray for the complete recovery of the eye disease, the sendenshi, strict to the core, would by no means nod his head. "I am the sendenshi of the realm. Caught unawares by laxity of mind, having my eyes taken by the beast-field, what countenance have I to show? Do not ask me," with an "Untokoshiyo," "Yattsukodotsukoi, it is dangerous!"—he simply would not forgive us. I too set my stubborn loins, and now beyond this there is no path other than to beseech the Gods. Sitting down with a thump upon the withered grass, joining my hands in reverence to the Mitamah: "Mitamah sakihae mashimase!" Even the reward of praying was truly manifest,'

Ah, Kamunagara-Kamunagara

The blessing of Kami is right before the eyes

'Untokodotskoi dotskoishiyo'

From here on out, step by step

The slope seems to grow steep and fierce

Sumikō, watch out, Michikō-dono

Your footing is dangerous

My own knees too, somehow

Are vomiting with a kiyokuri kiyokuri

Truly, what a troublesome slope

Aita ta ta ta, I tripped

The stone one trips on is also the edge of destiny

Even drawing water from the stream of Itchika

Or taking shelter under the shade of Ichijiyu's single tree

Hearing that these are deep bonds

'Untokodotskoi, I tripped'

'Aitatatsuta, I fell over again.'

Sumikō sings again.

The leaves of the trees in the gale blowing

Across the mountain ridges of Kajikatauge

Have fluttered down helplessly, and the bare withered wood

Has become just like them

That adorned the autumn mountains and fields

And the trees of Kiyoteruhime in the setting Yufuhi

Now completely 'Dotskoishiyo'

Have become pitiful and vanished away

Having their eyeballs plucked out by those rascally Kozaru

'Untokodotskoi'

My master, how deeply regretful and lamentable it must be

'Yatsutokodotskoi dotskoishiyo'

You had better mind your footing

Lofty stones are lined up in a row

Those insolent monkey scoundrels with their small faces

The architects of heaven and earth

Human beings born as the supreme lords

Of all creation

Being made fools of, 'Dotskoishiyo'

How could this ever become a tale to tell the people of the world?

How shameful

Though there are three attendants

To subject our master, of all people, the vital essence,

To such cruel treatment with an 'Untokodotskoi'

What excuse can we possibly offer

Before the august presence of Kami Susanō-no-Mikoto?

Thinking and thinking on it, how pathetic

We are attendants not worth speaking of

From now on, all of us must be careful

And protect the personal safety of our master

Michikō, you must pull yourself together

Itako-san, you too had better stay firm

Aita ta ta tata, I tripped

What an extremely troublesome mountain path

If we grow careless, even the jewel-strung thread

Of our lives will be snatched away

Collapsing in a place like this

What excuse could we possibly give to the Kami of heaven and earth?

'Untokodotskoi korewaina'

Once again a fierce wind blows

From the tips of our heads to the tips of our nails

Mind your waist and surroundings

Put your strength into every single step

Shall we descend this slope with an 'Untokodotskoi'

And an enyarayatsu?

How did the noble administrators Ōgonhime and Kiyoteruhime

Descend this very slope?

I can only imagine their august hardships

The propaganda envoy Hinodewake

Along with the three attendants

Must surely have passed right through this place, 'Dotskoshiyo'

Without a doubt, they must have achieved

Great military exploits and honorable deeds

Throughout the more than seven thousand lands

No matter how great the general may be

If the attendants who accompany him are bad

He cannot move with all his might

If what Sumikō says

Gives offense to your ears

Look again straightly and listen again

Never, ever will I say anything bad

'Untokodotskoi.'

Singing thus, they descended before a shrine that was about eight parts ruined.

Tamakuniwake: 'Thanks to this, the pain in my eye has abated considerably, but somehow my head is throbbing and cracking a bit. Fortunately there is an open space here, and a ruined shrine stands before us. I know not what Kami is enshrined here, but let us rest a breath here, shall we?'

Michikō: 'Yes, that would be most appropriate. You are in a state of illness, sir, you must not overdo it. There is not only today; tomorrow and the day after exist as well, so please rest leisurely. Michikō too is terribly tired, so I shall gladly keep you company.'

Tamakuniwake nodded, spread his straw raincoat broadly upon the earth, sat down heavily upon it, brought his hands together before the ancient shrine, and began to chant the Amatsu Norito. The three others likewise chanted the Norito.

Tamakuniwake: 'Though I know not what god is enshrined in this desolate shrine, how grievously has it fallen into decay. Even though the palace be ruined, the truth of the deity shall remain eternally settled in steadfast firmness. The Mikoto of Kuniharutachinokami also, missing the time, has hidden away in the nethermost realm of the country. Ah, blessed with the grace of the Kami, how bravely I stand upon this land. A wretched wretch of an ugly Magatsumi is he who, born in the land of the Kamisama, reveres not the Kamisama. Whenever I look upon this shrine, I cannot but ponder what the palace of Hisu in Ama may now be. The ancient form of Jerusalem has vanished entirely, and now blows a lonely winter wind. Praying that the Kami may manifest in Their true form and govern the world is a time devoutly to be awaited. If it be in accordance with the heart of my desire, I shall build a palace with soaring Chigi-thatch. If the strength of the true path to uphold is mighty, a wondrous palace shall soon be built.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Weeping before the shrine I bow in worship, I yearn after the whereabouts of my Ōkami. Oh, how venerable is the Mikoto of Susanō, who pities the world and saves Hitogusa. How longing is the Mikoto of Kunitakehiko, who dwells upon the island of Onokoro. What will become of the noble Mikoto of Tamateruhiko and Tamateruhime, I wonder as I gaze up at the sky.'

Together with Mizumitama and Izu-no-mitama, they have constructed the Izago teachings. Since I am one who serves the path of the Izago, how could the ugly Magatsumi assail me? Aside from the Kami in Their divine-will, there is nothing at all, if I am a person who has neither parent nor child.'

Itaikō: 'I now follow the divine overseer and walk the slope of the great divine highway. What manner of Kami dwells in this shrine? Even without knowing, I have come to yearn to pray. Though the divine names are variously called, it must be the descendant of Kuniharutachi. O Sumeramikami, protect our future safely, especially the honorable body of my master. Even if some manner of evil obstruction should bar the way ahead, pray blow it away with the divine breath.'

Junko: 'If the moon of the heart within the breast is pure, surely no manner of evil obstruction will bar the way. Guarding the teachings of the moonlit moon, I advance toward the moonlit land. How shall we save the Yamata-no-Orochi coiled within the capital of Haruna in the land of the moon? If even evil deities are all children of Sumeramikami, how could we possibly hate them?'

Tamakuniwake: 'From this day forth, the divine office of Tamakuniwake shall advance according to the will of the Kami. Receiving the divine words at the Saienkan, we four persons are traversing the Great Highway. Though the autumn gale over the peaks of Mount Kajika be fierce, how could the divine soldiers ever fear? Advancing while being buffeted by the blowing winds, how valiant is the divine office! Even if the hosts of Brahmans come attacking ahead, never face their blades. Yet when the time of a thousand riders against a single horse arrives, we shall move, receiving the permission of the Kami. Driving firmly the immovable pillar of jade, we shall go forth to transmit the path of Aizen.'

Michikō: 'How joyful is the fortune of the person who guides with words the evil deities that obstructed the path of Sumeramikami. Let us question the thousand-year-old pine of this grove concerning the antiquity of Kuniharutachi. If the thousand-year-old pine of this grove could speak, it would surely tell us of the age of the gods. Following the dreams of a bygone past, we continue to live on in this dream-like floating world. The ancestral deity of the land governs and protects both this present world, the unseen world, and the world of the gods. Awe-inspiring is the divine pillar who established the path of Izago, the divine Susanō. If we accord with the wondrous mitama of Susanō, surely no manner of evil will turn away. I am now protected by the grace of the Kami, and following my master, I go upon a journey of dissemination. Itaikō: 'Though for a while the storm blows, I rejoice that we shall presently meet again the spring in which flowers bloom. 道公『皇神の道にさやりし曲神を 言向け進む身の幸楽しも。 此森の千歳の松に言とはむ 国治立の神の昔を。 此森の千歳の松の物言はば 神代の昔聞かましものを。 過ぎ去りし昔の夢を辿りつつ 夢の浮世に長らへてゆく。 現世も又幽世も神の世も すべ守ります国の祖神。 三五の道を立てたる神柱 神素盞嗚の尊畏き。 素盞嗚の瑞の御魂にかなひなば 如何なる曲も背かざるらむ。 吾は今神の恵に守られて 師の君に従ひ宣伝の旅行く。 ゆく先は空照り渡る月の国 ハルナの都と聞くは勇まし』

伊太公『暫くは嵐吹けどもやがて又 花咲く春に遇はむと楽しむ。 山々の諸木の末に至るまで 冬ごもりして時をまつなり。 田鶴巣ふ此の松ケ枝は夏冬の わかちも知らに栄えけるかも。 鶯の谷の戸あけて出る春を まつも嬉しき宣伝の旅。 杜鵑八千八声を鳴き涸らし 黄金花咲く秋をまちぬる。 冬来ぬと目にはさやかに見えねども 空吹く風にそれと偲ばる。 此森に常磐堅磐に在す神 吾等を永久に守らせ給へ』

§ 4

Sumikō: 'Gazing out at the clear autumn sky, Sudden ugly black clouds arise. After passing showers clear away, the early winter Moon shines brilliantly in the sacred sky. The sun has already sunk to the edge of Nishiyama, It has indeed become lonely and desolate. Nevertheless, if we return to the heart of Kami, Day and night alike, we shall feel joyous. Come then, O Master, my teacher, rise up, Before long we shall emerge onto the broad path.'

Tamakuniwake: 'How wondrous, the pain in my eyes is now already Forgotten through the grace of Kami. Come then, you three attendants, before the presence of Kami, Let us offer norito and set forth on our way. Wonderful it is, before the august presence of Kami, We rejoice in the grace of having rested our breath. It is a midnight of the moon, where the dew of divine grace Glitters across even the tips of the tree leaves. The full moon of the night of hope has appeared in the sacred sky, Thinking that the mountain shadows had grown bright.'

Having thus finished singing, they bade farewell to the kami of the shrine, and just as they were about to set out, the sound of horse hooves was heard noisily from ahead, and hundreds of tsuhamono advanced toward this direction moment by moment. Tamakuniwake and the other three strained their ears at this monōto, folded their morote, and remained lost in shian as to who it might be. The present monōto was the vanguard unit of General Oniharuwake, a subordinate of Ōkuronushi, marching toward the mansion of the sacred garden. Ah, how was the destiny of Tamakuniwake and his party about to unfold?
