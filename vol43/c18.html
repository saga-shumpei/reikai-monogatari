Chapter 18: Stone 室 [1169]

石室〔1169〕

§ 1

About halfway down the mountain path, an Ihamuro capable of holding seven or eight people had been hollowed out. A Yamaoroshi blew suddenly, mixed even with Ōtsubu rain. Matsukō said,

Matsukō: 'Hey, Itakō-sama, and all the rest of you, with the Amekaze striking all at once like this, we cannot continue our descent. Fortunately, shall we not wait for the Amekaze to pass in this Ihamuro?'

Tatsukō: 'That is Ketsukō, is it not? Everyone, shall we have an Itsupuku?'

Itakō: 'I am quite anxious, but I shall follow your suggestion and wait for the rain. Our bodies are not made of paper, so a little rain does not matter, but since I feel for you all, I shall rest and keep you company.'

They entered the Ihamuro, which had no door at the Iriguchi, and each sat upon a Tennen Ihaisu, resting their feet for a while. Suddenly, Tatsukō’s Kahoiro turned pale, he broke into a Hiyaase, and began to tremble violently. The others were startled and gathered around from the Gururi, saying, 'Hey, what is it, what is it? Tatsukō, pull yourself together!' and rubbed his back. While sweating, Tatsukō made his teeth chatter and opened his Dongurimanako wide.

Matsukō: 'Hey, this is troublesome. He has been struck by malaria at last. Excuse me, Itakō-sama, what should we do?'

Itakō: 'This has become a troublesome matter. This is surely malaria. Being in the middle of the journey, there is nothing to be done. To cure malaria, one would place a mortar on the patient's head, put a handful of moxa on top, and burn it; that would cure it immediately, but we have no mortar and no moxa. This is troublesome.'

Matsukō: 'I wonder, what god’s work is this malaria, anyway?'

Itakō: 'Malaria is entirely the work of Shiryō. It is the possession by the vengeful spirits of those who have fallen into mountain streams, ponds, or swamps. If there were a sulfur hot spring, we could plunge him in and it would retreat immediately, but it is a troublesome place to have caught malaria.'

Matsukō: 'If we put him in a hot spring, will the malaria be cured? Hey, that is the first I have heard of that. Fortunately, if we go down this mountain path to the right for about one chō, I hear a sulfur hot spring has been bubbling up since long ago. How would it be if we took him there to bathe? You are in a hurry, but in any case, there is no concern that Tamakuniwake-sama or Harukuniwake-sama will leave the forest of the shrine, so could you not make a small detour there?'

Itakō: 'That is an easy matter. I cannot leave someone who is suffering behind.'

Matsukō: 'That is grateful. In that case, I shall trouble you.'

Tatsukō, while grinding his teeth, glared with his eyes and said,

Tatsukō: 'Oh! I am by no means a Shiryō. Nor is this malaria. I am a fragment of the Yamata no Orochi who serves the Ōkuninushi. You five insolent wretches, you who plot to destroy our companions, you underlings of Susanoo no Mikoto, you who have cast off your helmets and turned against us like Tamakuniwake and Harukuniwake, I shall never forgive you. I shall take the life of this Tatsukō, then I shall take the life of Matsukō, and it goes without saying for the three wretches Iru, Iku, and Saru, and I shall even kill Itakō, so you had best prepare yourselves.'

Matsukō, pouting his lips, said,

Matsukō: 'Itakō, he is saying things like that. At this rate, the hot spring will be of no use either. Is there not some trick or other?'

Itakō: 'If it is understood that it is not malaria, there is another method. Now, let us use the Chinkon unique to the San-Go-Kyō and drive away the evil spirit brilliantly.'

Matsukō: 'Please, I beg of you. Hey, you three, you pray as well.'

Thereupon, Itakō and the other four joined their hands with all their might and chanted 'Kannagara Tamachi Hae Mase' about ten times. After that, Itakō clapped his hands 'pon-pon', finished reciting the Amatsu Norito, and chanted the Ama no Kazauta two or three times. The possessing spirit of the great serpent, terrified and frightened by the Ama no Kazauta, knocked Tatsukō down on the spot and fled away. 谷の下り道、半分許りの所に七八人這入れる石室が穿たれてあつた。俄に吹き来る山颪、大粒の雨さへ混つてゐる。松公は、

『オイ、伊太公さま、其外一同の者、かう雨風が一度に襲来しては下りる事も出来ない。幸ひ此石室で雨風の過ぐるを待つ事にしようではないか』

竜公『そりや結構だなア、皆さま、一服しませうかい』

伊太公『大変に気もせきますが、仰せに随つて雨をまつ事に致しませう、別に吾々の体は紙で拵へたのではないから、少々の雨位構ひませぬが、皆様がお気の毒だからおつきあひに憩ませて貰ひませう』

入口の戸もない石室に侵入し、天然の岩椅子に各自腰をかけ、暫く足をやすめて居た。竜公は俄に顔色蒼め、冷汗をかき、ブルブルと慄ひ出した。一同は驚いて『ヤア何だ何だ竜公確りせぬか』と周囲からよつて集つて撫でさする。竜公は汗を滲ませながら歯をガチガチ云はせ、団栗眼をむき出した。

松公『ヤアこいつは困つた、とうとう瘧に襲はれやがつたなア、モシモシ伊太公さま、どうしたらよろしからう』

伊太公『困つた事になつたものだ、こりや瘧に違ひない。途中の事と云ひ、どうも仕方がない。瘧をおとすには病人の頭へ擂鉢をかぶせ、艾を一つかみ其上にのせて灸を据ゑると直落ちるのだけれど、擂鉢もなし、艾もなし困つたものだ』

松公『一体瘧と云ふのは何神の仕業でせうかなア』

伊太公『瘧は皆死霊の業だ。谷川へ陥つたり、池や沼に落ち込んだ奴の亡霊が憑依するのだ。硫黄温泉でもあれば、そこへ突込んでやれば直退散するのだけれど、困つたところで瘧をふるつたものだわい』

松公『温泉へ入れたら瘧が落ちますか、ヤアそりや聞き初めだ。幸ひこの谷道を一丁ばかり右へ下りると、昔から硫黄温泉が湧いて居るとの事です、そこへ浴れてやつたら何うでせうなア。貴方もお急きでせうが、どうせ玉国別さまも治国別さまも祠の森をお離れなさる気遣ひはないから、一寸そこ迄廻つて貰へますまいかなア』

伊太公『そりやお易い事です、人の苦しんで居るのを見捨てて行く訳にも行きませぬから』

松公『そりや有難い、そんなら御苦労になりませうかなア』

竜公は歯をキリキリと云はせながら目を怒らせ、

『オヽ俺は決して死霊ではないぞ、瘧でもないぞ、大黒主に仕へ奉る八岐大蛇の片割だ。汝等五人の不届者奴、俺達の仲間を滅さむと計る、素盞嗚尊の手下、玉国別や治国別に甲を脱ぎ吾々に背くやつ、決して許しは致さぬぞ。此竜公が命を取り、次には松公が命をとり、イル、イク、サール三人の奴は申すに及ばず、伊太公迄もとり殺してやるのだから、其覚悟を致したらよからう』

松公は口を尖らし乍ら、

『伊太公、あんな事を云ひますわ、これでは温泉も駄目でせう、何とか工夫はありますまいかな』

伊太公『瘧でないと分れば、又方法もあります。サアこれから三五教独特の鎮魂を以て悪魔を見事退散さして見ませう』

松公『どうぞ宜しう願ひます。オイ三人のもの貴様も一つ祈つて呉れい』

茲に伊太公、外四人は一生懸命に両手を合せ、惟神霊幸倍坐世を十回許り唱へた後、伊太公はポンポンと手を拍ち天津祝詞を奏上し終つて天の数歌を二三回唱ひ上げた。大蛇の憑霊は、天の数歌に怯ぢ恐れ、竜公を其場に倒して逃げ去つて了つた。竜公はけろりとして汗をふきながら、

': 'Ah, how painful that was. Well now, Itakō-sama saved us; how wonderfully effective the sūtra of Ananai-kyō is, isn't it?'

So saying, he takes the lead, beats time with his feet once more, and sings:

Down the descending path of Kiyoharuyama, A perilous pass rare in Tenka, Just as we were descending, Tatsukō Leaped right into the stone chamber, Quaking and trembling in fright. From now on, I too shall utterly abandon The ugly teachings of the Brahmans, And following the divine teachings of Ananai, Whose divine virtue is high, Engaging in various and sundry ascetic practices, Even the name being auspicious, as a Kamutsukasa, Calling myself Matsukōwake, To save the afflictions of all people far and wide, Without fail, Kamunagara. As the Propagating Envoy who is the younger brother To the life of the elder brother, Born from the same womb, "Untokodotsukoi," as for my body, There can be no logic that says I cannot imitate my elder brother. Ah, how interesting, how interesting, The brightness of the future has come into view. To oppose the true deity Of Susanoo, whose divine virtue is high, is the act of one who knows not life.

From now on, I shall radically reform My state of heart, And thoroughly fulfill that heavenly vocation Born as a child of God Before the presence of God. Tatsukō, you too have been saved from a painful place By the Lord God, And that this is precious, "Dotsukoishyo," You must have finally understood. No matter how great a person may be, Even the lifespan of a single fly, Possessing a body unable to prolong Even by a single second, How could one oppose God? When I think and think, human beings, Compared to the power of God, Are like dust or motes. From now on, entrusting both body and soul To the Lord God, With single-minded devotion upon the good path,

Advancing for the sake of the way of the deities, Shall I exhaust my utmost strength? Ah, how valiant, how valiant, Even down a long slope, as we step on and on, If we descend step by step, Just as one finally reaches the foot of the mountain, No matter how small the faith, If piled up, it finally becomes a mountain. Higher than the mountains and deeper than the seas Is the precious grace of God. Should I leave it without returning gratitude? Not just in this world, but if we change our domain Even to the Shinkai, the Lord God Will still look after us. The true parent is the Lord God. Though I have lamented until now In death-parting from the yearning parent, That was the parent of this world. The parent who saves my body, Unchanging through myriad kalpas, Is none other than the Lord God. When I think and think, how wondrous and grateful! Though the morning sun shine or be clouded, Though the moon wax or wane, Though even the great earth should sink, Once we have entrusted ourselves to God, What manner of thing is there to fear? Should earthquakes, thunder, and cars of fire, And great floods come, Once we have made our ultimate resolve, The true divine path established by God, Ananai-kyō's sacred way, We shall never, never abandon.

"Unto-ko dotsukoi dotsukoishyo" We have descended a good deal of the slope. It is one more final push, everyone. You had better watch your footing. Here is the lair of demons. If you are careless, you never know When the great serpent fellow might possess you.

Tatsukō's fellow is a good example. Pay heed to your spirit and watch your footing, And descend with your mind attentive. Ah, Kamunagara kamugamigami, May your mitama flourish and prosper."

So singing with a fascinating rhythm, he hurries on toward the grove of the shrine where the Propagating Envoy of Tamakuniwake is resting.
