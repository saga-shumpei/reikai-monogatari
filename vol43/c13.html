軍談〔1164〕

§ 1

Toyed with by decades of wind and rain, with its roof scattered and pillars warped, before an old shrine that looked scarcely presentable, bathing in the moonlight that was covered by thin clouds and dimly revealed its contours indistinctly, seven men were engrossed in conversation. These were, of course, the party of Harukuniwake and Tamakuniwake.

Tamakuniwake: 'Harukuniwake-sama, you must have suffered terribly in yesterday's great storm. Besides, the forces of the Brahmanism have come, so it must have been an extra hardship for you.'

Tamakuniwake said... Wait, strict rule check: no added speech tags.

Harukuniwake: 'As I was descending the Kajikatauge this way, I encountered the forces of Katahiko and Kumehiko, so at any rate we took up a position at the ultimate difficult pass, and when we leisurely launched the Kototama, they scattered their ranks just as the leaves of the trees scatter in yesterday's violent wind, and fled away in a state of exhaustion. I was thinking that you would surely wait here in the shade of this forest for the rout of the enemy, launch the Kototama, and that a pincer attack of Kototama from both sides would be interesting. And thinking that there must be many surrenderers before the forest of the shrine, oh, I came with anticipation. Did the enemy not pass through this valley road?'

Tamakuniwake: 'Ah, I have done a regrettable thing. Suffering divine punishment from the Gods, getting seriously injured, and appearing disheartened, I missed all of the enemy fleeing like an avalanche, much to my regret.'

Harukuniwake: 'That cannot be helped in any way. Everything is probably according to the divine convenience of the spiritual realm. However, to think that you were severely injured...'

Tamakuniwake: 'Yes, my both eyes were scratched and rummaged by the monkey fellow, and once I went blind, but thanks to the gracious divine virtue I finally saved one eye, and resting in the shade of this forest, I was waiting for my headache and eye pain to heal.'

Tamakuniwake said... Wait, Harukuniwake is speaking here.

Harukuniwake: 'That is truly a thousand sorrows; although it is a moonlit night, it was too dim and I could not see your face, so come, let me see it for a moment.'

Saying thus, he peered into Tamakuniwake's face.

Harukuniwake: 'Ah, this is terrible. The area around your eyes is festering. It seems you scratched them quite fiercely.'

Tamakuniwake: 'We invited the calamity upon ourselves from our own mental carelessness. Indeed, as propaganda emissaries, we have no faces to show.'

Harukuniwake: 'It feels somewhat awkward here, so shall we not talk leisurely at some good place?'

Tamakuniwake: 'If we climb this forest for about one chō, there is a suitable resting place. The truth of the matter is that this evening too, convalescing in the shade of that forest, I was gazing at the enemy forces advancing.'

Harukuniwake: 'If so, I will accompany you to the resting place in the shade of that forest.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Since we do not know whether the remnants of the enemy will pass by again or come back to repeat the attack, shall we leave about two persons here as lookouts before going?'

Harukuniwake: 'Hey Gosankō, if it is not too much trouble for you, would you play the guard at the barrier for a while in front of this shrine?'

Gosankō: 'Yes, I understand. I would like to borrow one of Tamakuniwake-sama's subordinates. If possible, I will perform the barrier guard with Itaikō, with whom my chemistry is very good.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Regrettably, I cannot hand Itaikō over to you.'

Gosankō: 'Is it not the same no matter who it is? Since my teacher has thus ordered a single person to stay behind to mind the house, it seems only proper that you too should leave at least one person, like Itaikō, behind here.'

Michikō: 'The truth of the matter is that Itaikō, that fellow, has become a prisoner of the enemy. From now on, both of us are thinking of plunging into enemy territory to take Itaikō back, but somehow because the teacher is hurting his eyes and hurting his head, we cannot go either, and we are on pins and needles.'

Gosankō: 'Ah, is that so, that is terrible. Even I, if I had the teacher's permission, would want to go and seek out Itaikō's whereabouts.'

§ 2

Harukuniwake: 'Hark, Tamakuniwake-sama, has Itakō fallen into the enemy's hands as a captive?'

Tamakuniwake: 'Regrettably, yes...'

Harukuniwake: 'Oh, that is indeed a troublesome turn of events. Come, let us quietly confer in the shade of the forest. Now Isokō, I am sorry to trouble you, but be a good fellow and stand guard here by yourself.'

Isokō: 'Ha, it is not as though I refuse to do it, but somehow I feel as if I have been left all alone. Please, at least leave Harukō behind with me.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Very well, I shall leave Sumikō here. Ho, Sumikō, though it may trouble you, I ask you to act as temporary guard alongside Isokō-sama.'

Sumikō: 'Understood. It seems I am inevitably treated as a mere foot soldier, and am not permitted to participate in the officers' council. Now that we have scattered the enemy afar and attained a brief lull, there is nothing to be done, so Isokō-sama and I shall form yet another separate detachment and open our own officers' council. Well then, two teachers, and you others, Michikō, Harukō, and Mankō, go and take a good rest.'

Tamakuniwake: 'I rely on you completely. Should anything unusual happen, clap your hands to give a signal.'

Sumikō: 'I have grasped everything entirely.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Then I trust you well.'

With that, Tamakuniwake takes the lead and climbs up to the familiar forest shade.

The two propaganda envoys and the three others spread straw raincoats upon the heavily piled fallen leaves, and without concealing anything, they frankly open their hearts to one another and discuss the state of the Kototama warfare, the details of the calamity at Kaidani, and the like.

Meanwhile, before the ancient shrine, Sumikō and Isokō watch the moon, now hiding behind the nearby western mountains, depart.

Sumikō: 'Ah, the moon-sama has finally gone away and returned home. Has not the mountain shadow suddenly closed in upon us, turning this into a world of pitch darkness?'

Isokō: 'Even the moon-sama could hardly be expected to stay in the very same place. Before long, it will not be just night; dawn is also drawing near, so let us stretch out right here for a while and dream of subduing the Brahmans, shall we not?'

Sumikō: 'If you want to sleep, go ahead and sleep, but a guard cannot discharge his duties in such a fashion, so I shall keep my eyes open here and faithfully perform my duty. Ah, you must be thoroughly exhausted from the Kototama warfare as well; it is only natural, so I will even lend you my straw raincoat, come, sleep and sleep.'

Isokō: 'There must be yet another reason why you cannot sleep. You are worried about the whereabouts of Itakō, are you not?'

Sumikō: 'That is my primary concern. Even for a single second, could I ever forget that fellow's affairs if I tried? Though I am here peacefully performing my duty as a guard, when I think of Itakō being subjected to dreadful tortures, how could I possibly manage to sleep?'

Isokō: 'Ha, ha, ha, ha! Does it trouble you to that extent? Is it not fine even if something happens to one person? If you yourself are safe, it must be the greatest of joys. There are fellows who have been lively just until now, only to be blown away by the demon wind called death and set off on a journey to the nether world with a single groan. No matter how much you agitate your heart and fret, what on earth can you possibly do about it? It is best not to harbor such foolish thoughts as taking another's colic as your own headache. In the end, will you not end up ruining your own body?'

Sumikō: 'You are quite a cold-blooded fellow indeed. No matter how precious one's own self may be, how could one calmly look on and abandon a comrade in danger? That is the duty of a friend. Nay, rather than duty, is it not sheer affection?'

Isokō: 'Do not worry so. Itakō is not a man to be beaten to death or subjected to cruel treatment. Since that fellow is a clever man, he will skillfully flatter them there and display such sociable behavior that even the enemy will come to love him. He is bound to win the enemy's sympathy, surely!'
