In the wood where the moon shone brightly, there were two people talking in whispers. Needless to say, these two were Tamakuniwake and Sumikō. The full moon of perfect bliss, which had hitherto been covered in bronze-colored cloud robes, seemed to be peering down upon the dialogue of the two as if it had a mind of its own.

Sumikō: 'Sensei, after this desolate path of the demonic army, those shattered-sounding laughter echoing from nowhere—is this not a scene as though a paradise of pleasure has opened up in the wake of the Shura realm? It is extremes meeting extremes, indeed.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Mhm, when things reach an extreme they reverse; the height of sorrow is joy. The height of joy is sorrow once more. It seems some comedy is being enacted around the shrine as its center. Hearing that laughter, my headache too seems to have been wiped away and vanished completely. Whenever one suffers from illness, laughing is the best remedy. That very moment of opening wide one's mouth and laughing merrily without guile is the state of the supreme heaven. Ah, I too am feeling somehow cheerful. Ha ha ha ha!'

Sumikō: 'Ah, just when I thought I had beheld the face of the glorious moon Ōkami once more, I have beheld a smile brimming upon your crystal-jewel-like gentle countenance, Sensei. I too feel somehow gladdened and emboldened. Ha ha ha ha!'

Tamakuniwake: 'That loud laughter sounded very much like Michikō's voice, didn't it?'

Sumikō: 'It was a similar voice. This is the first time I have heard that man laugh with such a loud voice. However, why does he not clap his hands as per your command? I suspect those fellows of the Brahmanism are intoxicated with wine, swelling with the spirit of triumphant return, and bursting into loud laughter.'

Tamakuniwake: 'That can hardly be. Michikō must be casting himself into the vortex of laughter as well.'

Sumikō: 'With such a wind of peace blowing near the shrine, it does no good to remain here in peaceful retirement forever. There can be little time left before the army of Brahmanism, smitten by the Kototama of Hinodewake-sama, comes fleeing back. At any rate, let us make our way toward that shrine. Ah, hands have clapped. That is surely Michikō's signal. Come, let us go.'

Tamakuniwake: 'If so, let us go.'

So saying they stood up, the two hats shining white in the air and the two staves striking the earth while glistening in the moon, as they descended.

Before the shrine, exposing his smiling face to the moonlight, Michikō squatted all alone.

Sumikō: 'Hey, Michikō-san, were you all by yourself?'

Sumikō: 'Did you make the voices of two or three people all by yourself at once? You are a remarkably dexterous fellow. Sensei is here. Will you not offer a greeting?'

Michikō: 'Are we not just parted there a moment ago? Between master and disciple, even if separated by ten or twenty ken, is such a burdensome and overly polite greeting required? If we go about doing such meticulous and elaborate courtesies, nothing will be accomplished. Sensei, you know my heart, do you not?'

Tamakuniwake: 'Mhm, I know it well. Tell me, Michikō, was there no one else? Were there only two people?'

Michikō: 'Yes, descendants of monkeys, totaling just two of them, squatted like dogs before the shrine and were groaning together. By my divine power, I finally made them laugh themselves away. Ha ha ha ha!'

Tamakuniwake: 'Ah, while bathing in the moonlight, let us borrow the front of this shrine and wait for the enemy army to return.'

So saying, he seated himself on a suitable stone in front of the shrine.

Michikō: 'Hey, judging from what those two fellows were saying just now, Itakō seems to have somehow become a prisoner of the enemy. However, as things now stand, it is of no use to regret it. From now on, in order to save Itakō, shall we not fulfill our duty as friends?'
