Chapter 8: 噴飯 [1159]

噴飯〔1159〕

§ 1

Tamakuniwake pays no heed to Itakō's meirei, leaps out from the morikage, and rushes all alone toward the vanguard of the Brāhmanism. From afar, he watches the enemy forces falling into utter confusion before the shrine, running to and fro in astonishment and moving back and forth, yet remains unconcerned and holds back without the slightest intention of going to the rescue.

Sumikō grows impatient, involuntarily smacking his knees back and forth with both hands, and speaks in a hurried voice to Tamakuniwake,

'Moshi, Senseisama, what shall we do? With brave resolve, Itakō has plunged all alone into the midst of the enemy forces, paying no heed to the master's meirei. No matter how much courage Itakō has to crush kishin, he is like a moth flying into the fire. Since he is pitting the few against the many, he will surely be destroyed. Come, let Michikō and me join hearts and set out as enpei. Since your eyes are bad, pray hide yourself right here and behold our valorous deeds in battle. Come, Michikō, let us go.'

Tamakuniwake is calm and speaks slowly,

'Wait, Sumikō. To rush out now is far too reckless.'

Sumikō: 'Even so, do you intend to look on and let your subordinate be killed before your very eyes? How could I possibly watch the peril of a dear friend from a place of safety?'

Michikō: 'That fellow Itakō has finally let his aramitama run wild, hasn't he?'

Tamakuniwake: 'Itakō's is not an aramitama. It is a violent spirit. What is called an aramitama is a spirit of quiet endurance and self-restraint; his is merely the courage of a common fellow. All warriors are of two kinds.'

Sumikō: 'What sort of things are the two kinds?'

Tamakuniwake: 'That is to say, soldiers are of two kinds: warriors of fiery passion and warriors of benevolence and righteousness. Now, a warrior of fiery passion is one who, upon facing a battle, eagerly advances, stretches his arms, breaks the strong, and smashes the firm with the swiftness of demons and fierce gods. Yet when such soldiers are tempted with gain by the enemy and the day comes when their side loses power, if it is convenient to run away they will either surrender to the enemy and forget their shame, or turn their backs on the world in a way their hearts never intended. Such ones are indeed warriors of fiery passion. On the other hand, a warrior of benevolence and righteousness does not necessarily contend with others for the lead, nor when he sees the enemy does he show his courage with a loud voice and many words, nor does he flourish his power or stretch his arms. Yet once he makes a promise and is relied upon, he never harbors a double heart, nor does he change his mind. When faced with a grave crisis, he does not forfeit his resolve, and where things are falling to ruin, he makes light of his life. Such a one is indeed a warrior of benevolence and righteousness. In the present age, sages and wise men have long since departed, and since people are so often stained with villainy and evil, warriors of benevolence and righteousness are few indeed.'

Sumikō: 'Indeed, I understand well. However, in this critical hour of life and death, do we have the leisure to make such distinctions between warriors of fiery passion and warriors of benevolence and righteousness? Must not the two of us combine these, becoming both a warrior of fiery passion and a warrior of benevolence and righteousness to perform a grand deed and rescue Itakō at all costs?'

Michikō: 'That may be so, but since everything lies within the heart of the Gods, whatever happens is an arrangement. It is best to leave things as the Gods will. Since matters have come to this, we have no other path than to obey the Sensei's meirei just as it is.'

Sumikō: 'Why on earth is such a gentle breeze blowing tonight? To replace yesterday's strong wind, the overturning of heaven and earth is truly extreme. It seems as though weak and timid gods are nesting in this forest. No matter how much I try to settle down, how could I remain at ease and watch a friend's peril go unhelped?'

Tamakuniwake: 'Do not make such a fuss. It must be some arrangement on the part of the Gods. Everything is the divine providence of Kannagara.'

Sumikō: 'Sensei, it is no use if you are so intoxicated with Kannagara. Human effort must also be necessary here, must it not? Do they not say, "Do all that is in your human power and await the decree of Heaven"? To exhaust every limit of cowardice—avoiding danger, seeking comfort under the guise of looking on with folded arms—and then to make excuses by calling it the providence of Kannagara while sitting there with a composed face, what on earth is this? It will surely affect the dignity of the evangelists as well. Ah, there is no help for it. This Sumikō is also prepared to die a martyr's death for Itakō's sake. This is my farewell to this world. Senseisama, serve diligently and robustly in the divine work. Michikō, this may well be the last time we look upon each other's faces. Be strong, and since the Sensei has bad eyes, take good care of him. From now on the cold weather is approaching, so pray be mindful of your body and take care not to catch a cold.'

Wiping away her tears with a resolute look, she was already about to leave the spot and rush toward the throng of enemies, her momentum appearing exceedingly difficult to restrain.

Tamakuniwake sharpened his words slightly,

Tamakuniwake: 'Sumikō, do you ignore my command? My command is none other than the command of Kami. How do you intend to act in the midst of Tenchi by rebelling against Kami? Why not unleash your Aramitama for a moment? Anyone can endure what can be endured, but enduring what cannot be endured is true endurance. To endure the unendurable is the so-called activation of the Aramitama, the warrior of righteousness.'

Sumikō: 'Even so, those enemy scoundrels, not only did they call Itakō—and dread to think, the Kamubashira Kamususanōnōokami whom we serve—an evil kami and attempt to destroy him, but did they not sing a Shingunka just as you heard, Anata? How could this ever be overlooked? When the lord is shamed, the vassal dies—this is that very moment, and I shall die from this instant on. Please let go of my hand.'

Tamakuniwake: 'A warrior dies for one who knows him. Even as Tamakuniwake, how could I remain idle, handing over the peerless and loyal Itakō to the enemy without a fight? My countless and boundless feelings, brimming with a million floods of tears and floating with scorching blood in my breast—surely you might ponder them just a little.'

Sumikō: 'Heh-eh!'

Michikō: 'No matter what you say, wait for the proper time for a while just as the teacher says. No matter how clever you may think you are, surely you cannot surpass the wisdom of the teacher. Assuredly, even if Itakō is captured by the enemy, he is undoubtedly preserving his life safely. If those enemy scoundrels were to easily kill Itakō, they would undoubtedly keep him alive because they do not yet understand our side's situation. Whatever may come to pass is for the sake of the Michi, for the sake of the world. Do not worry too much.'

Tamakuniwake grew even more detached,

Tamakuniwake: 'In any case, because Itakō took independent action without consenting to our orders, viewed from the surface one might even call him a rebel, yet his feelings must also be understood. No doubt he will be captured by the enemy and suffer severe hardships, but Kami will by no means abandon him. We have no other path than to pray as much as possible for the mitigation of Itakō's suffering. Earlier, I confided our tactical plan quietly to Michikō, and the reason I did not tell Sumikō was because I had deep considerations. I thought it would be disastrous if a hasty person like Sumikō rushed out, got captured by the enemy, and carelessly blurted things out. Sumikō probably thought it cold and distant of me, but that too was unavoidable. Now that things have come to this, there is no need to hide anything. Let me tell you so that you may feel entirely at ease. The truth of the matter is that once we safely let the Brahman army pass, we will surely encounter the troop led by Chikuniwake-sama in the direction of Kaikoku. If we do so, the Kototama squads of Chikuniwake and Tamakuniwake will launch an attack from front and rear, with the intention of making the enemy surrender without harm and guiding them into the true Michi. Earlier, I briefly leaked this grand strategy to Michikō, and that is why Michikō halted his activation as well. Aha-ha-ha-ha!'

Sumikō: 'Ah, because of that I too have been set at ease. Before we knew it, has not the enemy army gone completely? Itakō has safely become a prisoner of the enemy, has he not?'

Tamakuniwake: 'Mm, he has certainly become a prisoner. Be that as it may, taking advantage of the moonlit night, shall we not head out as far as the front of the shrine? However, since it is not certain that no remnants of the enemy remain, let us make no sound, suppress our footsteps, and go see the front of the shrine.'

Michikō: 'Sensei, since your eyes are poor and you have a slight headache, please kindly rest here together with Sumikō. I shall briefly perform the duty of the scouting party. Then, if I think there is no one about, I shall clap my hands to give a signal; once the hands have sounded, please slowly come along.'

Tamakuniwake, holding his head with both hands,

Tamakuniwake: 'Sumikō, do not worry. For another hour at least, we are safe. When an hour has passed, the enemy forces will surely be struck by the Kototama of Harukuniwake and come fleeing back here without fail. Until then, you had better take ample rest and nourish your vital spirit.'

Tamakuniwake: 'There is no need for brute muscular strength. You must put tension into your mitama and make your soul like the great Mount Taizan, remaining calm and unshakable no matter who appears, without getting flustered, impatient, or acting with rash and reckless abandon over trivial matters. Such panicky and reckless behavior will never do for the sacred service of conquering demons.'

Sumikō: 'By the way, did Michikō not completely fail to clap his hands? Thinking of this, there must still be some remnants lingering in the vicinity of the shrine, I suppose.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Um, they are certainly bound to be there. About two guards must be keeping watch, I imagine.'

Tamakuniwake: 'That would be so. With the physical eye, they cannot be seen. I saw them with the eye of the heart. In front of the shrine are two men, talking about various strange things. Michikō is holding his breath from behind the shrine, listening so as not to miss a single word.'

Sumikō: 'Heh, can those voices be heard? Is it not that your head is acting abnormally and ringing with a bang, making that sound like human voices? Even with my robust head, it does not enter my ears, you see.'

Tamakuniwake: 'I, too, am listening with the ears of the heart. It is the power of heavenly clairaudience.'

Sumikō: 'Ah, I am truly impressed. As expected, my teacher has something different about him.'

Meanwhile, in front of the shrine, two Brahman inspectors, Ko and Otsu, sat heavily in front of the shrine, acting as temporary guards by the strict order of General Katahiko, wondering if the missionaries of Anisansankyō might come appearing from behind.

Ko: 'Hey, suddenly one of those fellows from Anisansankyō jumped out from behind the shrine and dealt us a terrible upset, did he not? General Katahiko-sama also took a painful blow from that fellow, saw stars from his eyes, and fell from his horse—it was a truly dangerous affair. Those fellows of Anisansankyō hold their lives as nothing, after all.'

No matter what, Anisansankyō is a formidable enemy in the world.'
