Chapter 17: 反 Song [1168]

反歌〔1168〕

§ 1

Led by Annai, Matsukō and Tatsukō went to the inner chamber of the cavern to find Itakō, Sāru, and Iku desperately grappling with each other.

Sāru and Iku were attempting to force Itakō into the prison, while Itakō resisted refusing to enter, right in the middle of a struggle.

Seeing this, Matsukō shouted out,

Matsukō: 'Hey, hey, wait!'

Startled by this voice, the two, Iku and Sāru, suddenly let go of their hands.

Matsukō: 'Hey you two, grabbing an important guest and what on earth are you beating him for?'

Iku: 'Well, no matter how much we told Iku-Iku-Iku to get in, this fellow is stubborn and won't go in, so we were just giving him a little iku-sāru.'

Sāru: 'He is quite a stubborn fellow, you see. Since we cannot make this Itakō learn his lesson unless we give him a rather painful time, both Iku and Sāru were facing Itakō and doing a temporary iku-sāru upon him. When we told him to go into the prison, he kept saying he would go or he wouldn't go, oh boy, we really had a terribly hard time.'

Matsukō: 'Aren't you two heavily intoxicated? What's with those feet of yours?'

Both of them scratching their heads all at once,

Both: 'High.'

Saying this, they squat down.

Changing his tone, Matsukō spoke,

Matsukō: 'You are Itakō-sama, the accompanying person of Tamakuniwake-sama, oh my, I was terribly rude last night. Today I have come to welcome you, so please follow me and return to the forest of the shrine.'

Itakō: 'Hey, you are the fellow who tied me up tightly last night, and you must be intending to put me through a terrible time once again. Now that I have come here, I don't feel like moving at all. Without making such a difficult face, how about having a drink? Itakō is the master of this cavern. You don't need to hold back, so drink, drink, drinking away like this, doing things in such a complicated way in this world comes to the same thing anyway. Rather than spending this world being hated by people, why don't you manifest the principle of universal brotherhood, cherish and be friendly with one another, and cross life holding hands? Little humans fighting or quarreling doesn't get you anywhere, does it?'

Matsukō: 'My, I am quite impressed. First of all, seeing your healthy face, this Matsukō has finally set his mind at ease. The one here is called Tatsukō and is my brother-in-law. Since he is my wife's brother, please treat him kindly in every way.'

Itakō: 'I don't know what is what, and I haven't the slightest idea what this is all about. Tell me, Matsukō or whatever your name is, where are you from?'

Matsukō: 'Well, my birthplace is Armenia.'

Itakō: 'What, Armenia you say? That's strange. There are many fine missionaries from Armenia in Hinodekyō. Even my teacher, Tamakuniwake-sama, was born in Armenia, and there are still many other people from Armenia outside as well.'

Matsukō: 'I am Harukuniwake, the missionary of Hinodekyō and his younger brother. I sincerely hope we can become closely acquainted from now on.'

Itakō: 'Indeed, hearing you say that, you are the very image of Harukuniwake. What a strange place to meet you in.'

Matsukō: 'That Harukuniwake is now resting in the forest of the shrine with Tamakuniwake-sama. However, because I was serving Brahmanism and joined the army that attacked the mansion of the sacred garden, my elder brother simply will not acknowledge me. He tells me, "If your sincerity is manifested," so I figured that unless I apologize for the crime of hiding Itakō-sama here and hand you over to Tamakuniwake-sama, he will not forgive me, and without a moment to lose, I have come to welcome you.'

Itakō: 'My, that is a marvelous coincidence. Tell me, are both missionaries Harukuniwake and Tamakuniwake in good spirits?'

Matsukō: 'Both are in good spirits. However, Tamakuniwake-sama seems to have injured himself slightly and looked somewhat unwell.'

§ 2

Itakō: 'Ah, that is a worrisome matter. In that case, I shall accompany you.'

Here, Matsukō, Tatsukō, and Itakō, along with three others, leave the grotto behind and descend the steep slopes of Kiyoharuyama. Itakō takes the lead and begins to chant:

Fastening tight the belt of clouds, Soaring high in the mid-air sky, When I come to Kiyoharuyama, The scenery unfolds in all four directions, And far beyond the vast expanse of a thousand ri, A great mountain range lies stretched out faint and dim, Like a phantom; As far as the eye can see, golden Fields of brocade have now appeared. Resting my breath in the grove of the shrine, Together with my master lord, Just as the time came to spend the night, The din of horses and men rang loud and harsh, Climbing up toward the mountain path. 'Lo, a great crisis! The wicked gods of the Brahmans!'—with ears alert, When I gaze through the moonlight, Before the shrine stands the shadow of a man, The neighing of steeds is tumultuous, Even the voices calling the roll of personnel

Heard high aloft, and somehow My arms quiver and my flesh dances; This Itakō, suddenly Forgetting his own body, waving his staff, Rushed headlong into the swarming army, And missing his footing toward the mountain stream, At the moment of falling, unmercifully, Bound by both hands and arms, To a cave whose very name is terrifying, How pitiful that I was led away! Thinking within my heart That ogres and rakshasas had gathered To mistreat me, When I came to face it, who would have expected That the three men would suddenly soften, Opening the sake cellar, setting up camp stools, Four of them facing one another in one place, Enjoying the long autumn night with cries of 'era-era', The joy and delight of it!

Itakō, contrary to expectation, Loosened the abdominal band of his heart, And while discussing the profound mysteries of the teachings of Ozuru and the Brahmans, Gradually spent the night. To such a place, at the entrance, Suddenly a human voice is heard; The Commander of the Iru is astonished, 'Matsukō the General has come, I beg you to enter the prison for a while,' Although he pleads, How could we, the messengers of God, Steal into a prison defiled to the utmost? Since both Iku and Sāru Are trying with all their might to throw Itakō in, Itakō inevitably resists, And while they are struggling and wrestling, Matsukō-sama enters, And all the circumstances become clear,

There is nothing so joyful as this! This too is, after all, The benevolent grace of the precious God of Ozuru. Untoko-dotsukoi, dotsukoishyo! Even the steep slope of Kajika Pass Is not as grueling as this. Why on earth would the Brahman teachings choose Such a perilous spot—dotsukoishyo— I cannot fathom what they have in mind. To build a fortress on a high mountain where even horses cannot tread, What is the purpose? Ah, kannagara kami-kami! With the divine majesty revealed, Wishing to meet and longing to see Matsukō, whom I thought an enemy, I have come to be led Before the august presence of Tamakawewake; Where my hands dance and my feet tread, I have come to the point of knowing nothing else. Ah, how valiant, how valiant!

That God surely dwells within Heaven and Earth—the fact that You can clearly understand even from this, Hey, hey, Matsukō, Tatsukō-sama, And you other three guards, From now on, renewing your hearts, Returning to the true path, And offering your sincerity before The august presence of the divine Lord Susanoo no Ōkami, Who has appeared as the saving God, Fulfilling your original duty as those born as the children of God, Yatsutoko dotsukoi dotsukoishyo, After leaving this world, To climb up toward the supreme heavenly paradise and pure land Where flowers bloom for a thousand and ten thousand generations, That preparation You must carry out! Though you have no time to speak, the shadow of death clings all around us,

While you have mouths, praise God, While your hands and feet can still work, Striving in deeds of sincerity, Become souls entrusted With the governance of Heaven and Earth! Ah, kannagara kami-kami! Before the august presence of God, Itakō Swears and proclaims to you: Though the morning sun shine or be clouded, Though the moon wax or wane, Untoko dotsukoi dotsukoishyo, Even though the Great Earth should sink, The living Gods who save this world Are the three deities: Kunitokotachi no Ōmikami, Toyokununushi no Ōmikami, And Kami Susanoo; Putting these Great Deities aside, There is no God to save us. Mount Tenkyōzan, Mount Chikyōzan, Caucasus Mountains, and the Saieenkan

Built upon Mount Ubusuna, Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa and Mount Shiboyama— Distributing divine pillars here and there to save the people of the world, Ozuru is the best under heaven, A most auspicious teaching in the world. Pray, oh pray, all of you pray, Morning and evening, with reverence, Do not neglect your faith! Ah, kannagara kami-kami, May your august spirits be blessed and peaceful!'

So chanting, facing the late autumn wind mixed with sleet, he descends the steep and forbidding Kiyoharuyama.
