§ 1

Itakō's propaganda songs, proudly proclaimed as top-grade and imported while he shed greasy sweat, proved entirely ineffective, and the crowds of long-tailed and short-tailed monkeys increased in number moment by moment, pressing close upon the party's side with prickly, stinging steps.

Michikō: 'Hey Itakō, temporary missionary, you spoke so grandly, but hasn't the Kototama proven completely ineffective? Because you're just acting like an ape and stirring things up, those darn monkeys show not the slightest sign of leaving, you crafty ape-brained fellow.'

Itakō: 'Give me a moment to have some time... As they say, "Surpassing what is heard of the monkey-sage's cunning is Mashibahisayoshi"... I am the Taikō himself! Let's see, I, this Michikō, will now teach the monkeys the Way and let them hear of a bloodied path. Since my method differs somewhat in its manufacturing process from your ways, you'd better listen well. Monkeys, oh monkeys, what troublesome creatures they are. Since this year is the Year of the Monkey, if we speak too harshly of them, the monkeys themselves won't stand for it, you know. Besides, we are receiving the divine protection of the monkey lords this year, aren't we? And since I was born in the Year of the Monkey, the Month of the Monkey, the Day of the Monkey, and the Hour of the Monkey, I am a Michikō equipped with fourfold monkey fortunes. They say that if one has a threefold alignment, one becomes a person of wealth throughout the land, but as for me, I was born with four monkeys aligned, saying "There is an ape-like person," so the monkeys are not complete strangers to me at all. They are bound to surrender and depart from this place. Now, Tamakuniwake-sama, even if you sing such useless songs, how could mere beasts possibly leave? Please, let me sing a specially designed propaganda song that has obtained utility model registration.'

Itakō: 'My, you have lined up so many points that they won't even fit in a woven basket. If we are told that, then from now on we have no choice but to become mutes. It really is a regrettable matter.'

Dōkō: 'Did you see that, Itakō? Even if you speak loftily, if a gag order is suddenly issued, you cannot even raise a squeak, can you? It is truly troublesome, isn't it?'

Itakō: 'Hold on, hold on, Dōkō! Didn't the missionary just say "Not to revile"? Look at that, the monkey fellow is becoming arrogant and approaching closer and closer precisely because the missionary said things like "monkey" and "basket".'

Dōkō: 'Well, monkeys have their own plan as monkeys, and they are pressing in. After all, because there is a fine man like Dōkō around. That monkey is not just male; since there are plenty of females too.'

Itakō: 'Ha ha ha ha, it makes a fine man's ears curl. You are telling visible lies, just like the saying, "A rakugo storyteller tells lies as if he had seen them." Because your face looks like a monkey, they think you are one of their companions and come flocking over.'

Dōkō: 'What, it is not that I look like a monkey, but that the monkeys take after me. Ha ha ha!'

Itakō: 'Whichever looks like the other, isn't it the same thing? There, they have come so close that we cannot even move. Hey, you wretched beast, won't you go away over there?'

With that, Itakō used all his might and shoved down a monkey that had come close right before his eyes. The group of monkeys suddenly began to shriek, and from the edge of the mountain yonder and the edge of the mountain hither, their numbers grew to countless tens of thousands, facing the four men, uttering shrill cries, lunging at them, and pouncing upon them, while the monkeys in the rear skillfully picked up stones and hurled them. The four men, outnumbered and facing overwhelming odds, were trying their hardest and putting all their energy into legitimate self-defense. Marching heavily from the rear, an even larger white-haired monkey unexpectedly clawed right at the eyes from behind Tamakuniwake. Tamakuniwake cried out and fell flat to the ground. Though the three men desperately swung their monk's staves at the monkeys to defend themselves and fight back, they grew exhausted as tens of thousands of monkeys rushed in by turns, replacing one another, and now things were becoming dangerous.

At that very moment, a roar of a lion echoed loudly enough to shatter the mountains, resounding as "Oo-oo." At this sound, the tens of thousands of monkeys let out sorrowful shrieks and fled away in a single rush.

From somewhere or other, the voice of the missionary song was heard clearly:

'The missionaries of Mikkyō, And Tamakuniwake's party, Forgot their noble mission, While in the wild wind crossing the mountain ridge, Their livers chilled and trembling with fear, Shaking and quaking into the valley's bosom, They hid for a while, How foolish to steal a moment of ease! Divine punishment swiftly came to report, A mob of monkeys, hardly human, Tormented and afflicted, Their both eyes torn to shreds—how shallow! I am Mokusuke, the missionary, Anxiously and fretfully worrying about Your party's departure, Straddling the back of a lion, When I followed behind and gazed upon the scene, A state of utmost misery, A disgrace to Mikkyō, How before the gods of heaven and earth—'

Apologizing for this blunder, The Sendenshi whose both eyes, compared unto tsukihi, Have been torn asunder, If the eyes of his heart grow dark, Like drifting upon the waves of fire, Where even the light of Kami is unknown, he becomes. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara, Returning unto the true heart, The reins of the steed of the heart, Tightening and tightening, by the august command of the Ōkami, Swiftly, as commanded, Making a fierce advance to the august land of Tsukihi, Quickly accomplish, even a day sooner, The Kamuwaza of being the yorishiro of Kami. Even though your eyes be torn, Since there is the august protection of Kami, Your spirit being opened, the path of salvation Of Tengoku peacefully Upon this earth establishing, You shall be able to serve as the master Of the keirin of Ten and Chi. Though the morning sun shine or grow cloudy, Though the moon wax or wane, Though the stars fall from heaven, Though the masago of the beach be exhausted, Never go against the words of the yorishiro of Kami. Kamunahohi who created this world, Ōnahohi of broad heart, May the mitama of Nahohi sachihahe, Opening the eyes of your clouded soul, He shall save you. Come, come now farewell, come farewell, Without fearing even the blowing winds, Nor the shifurai of wolves and tigers, Without flinching, without yielding, for the sake of the Way, Advance even a moment sooner. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara, May your august spirits sachihahe maseyo. So he finished chanting, and suddenly appearing before the four was the majestic figure of the deity Tokiokishi no Kami straddling a giant lion. Tamakuniwake had both his eyes crushed, and prostrating himself, had listened respectfully only to the voice of the prophetic song. Michigimi, Itagimi, and Sumigimi, the three of them, prostrated themselves in worship before the brave figure of Tokiokishi no Kami, letting torrents of tears drift across their cheeks. Tokiokishi no Kami, like a swift gale and flashing thunder, straddled the lion, stepped across the steep mountains, and concealed his form somewhere unknown.
