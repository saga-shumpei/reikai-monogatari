Chapter 9: 衡平運動 [1733]

衡平運動〔1733〕

§ 1

Though there were Daimiyō above, there was no Shōmiyō who understood the Jidai and served as a Hohitsu and Tōriyō capable of discerning the Eien Shinsaku of the Kokuka; everywhere, Kiyogi, Zaiaku, and Kenbōjiyutsusū were made the foundation of Shisei, heavy taxes were levied to squeeze Kōketsu, and those belonging to the bourgeois class standing above exhausted every conceivable luxury with Hibakeikiu, unconcerned with the Meiwaku of pedestrians, making automobiles fly with a buzzing roar, and going their way unconcernedly while bathing others in Shiuki funpun farts and Tsuchibokori. Even if the child of a hinmin was run over and crushed to death by an automobile, there was no way to bring a lawsuit concerning it; the kiyōshiya acted in a manner tantamount to gautō in broad daylight, striding about horizontally and vertically with kōkōzen, while the weak were trampled underfoot by the carriages and horses of the thoroughfares, raising piteous cries and groaning at the borders of the ninefold depths of death. Although the transportation facilities of civilization and its utilitarian instruments advanced and were completed to a considerable extent, the poor were unable to utilize them. Even though educational institutions were splendidly established, the poor were unable to enter them. Although yose theaters and the like were constructed on the four sides of the city, bringing forth a paradise on earth, the poor were once again unable to seek a single moment of solace in them. Although hospitals stood in rows with ridge-ends pointing to the sky in every place, the poor were unable to enter them to receive medical treatment. Even though delicious delicacies and fine dishes were lined up at the storefronts of restaurants, the poor were once again unable to bathe in this blessing. Although large draper's shops displaying brocades and damasks in their storefronts were densely crowded in the most prominent locations of the city, the poor were unable to purchase even a single piece of cloth. Day and night weeping from hunger, freezing from the cold, embracing hollow bellies, they only panted along the sides of the roads like half-patients, looking desolate. The rich united with the Dai-Shōmiyō to gorge themselves on exorbitant profits, prices soared day by day, the voices of hardship in survival grew clamorous day by day, those who cast themselves into abysses and rivers, those who committed disembowelment, those who performed the swing-deliverance, those who threw away their lives with railway tracks as pillows—day and night without limit, the curtain of pitch-black darkness was lowered day by day more thickly upon the lower-class society. The voices of indignation and bitter lamentation of the common people and the cries of loud weeping came to form a state resembling the Avīci hell. The voices calling for the eradication of the Dai-Shōmiyō arose in every part of the country, and citizen assemblies, popular assemblies, and other gatherings of the property-owning masses were opened in various places; the conflicts between the censors and the masses, and the blood-stained winds blew wildly on all sides without interruption, and even the Talahan country, which had hitherto been at ease, had now gradually become an alley of Asura. The reckless fringe groups, theatrical troupes, and various other organizations gathered without prior arrangement on the grand thoroughfares of the capital, and taking the fifth day of the fifth month—the anniversary of the founding of the Talahan country—as their term, they began setting fires simultaneously at locations within the castle town, and taking advantage of that emptiness, the masses starving for blood indulged their wicked deeds to the utmost, and for a time it became a state of almost no control, but gradually they were able to suppress the extraordinary disturbance by borrowing the strength of the samurai factions. Due to the outbreak of this commotion, those who caught the ricochet of the wealthy factions and had their meager properties burned down, those who lost parents, those who lost wives, those who parted from husbands, or those whose whole families were utterly destroyed were without number; the grand thoroughfares of the capital were transformed into alleys of spilled blood, and with corpses piled high, it became a tragic scene difficult to even look upon. Children wept from hunger upon their mothers' backs, old men lost the use of their loins and fell by the roadside, or half-dead and suffering from severe wounds, those who struggled were so numerous as to be beyond counting by the thousands. The various voluntary organizations ran about in the four directions of east, west, south, and north to rescue the disaster-stricken people, scrambling for rice, barley, vegetables, and the like in order to save the immediate emergency, but this was ultimately far from sufficient to satisfy even a part of their demands. Rumors and unfounded gossip arose flourishingly, the minds of the people were agitated and uneasy, and it was noised abroad by the mouths of people that the Talahan country would presently head toward the tragic fate of destruction. To such a place appeared a single woman of oversized, fat build who exceeded physical beauty, and while scattering bills upon the road and singing of something in a loud voice, she ran back and forth hither and thither through the checkerboard streets.

Woman: 'The gods appear outwardly, And strictly separate men and demons. In the heavens black clouds block the way, And the light of moon and sun does not shine upon the earth. The common people under the heavens, Layer days upon days of survival of the fittest; The strong climb high and reach the ultimate peak, While exhausting every extent of glory and splendor, They treat the distress of those below As wind blowing past deaf ears, Oppressing the impoverished folk, Squeezing their raw blood and fats, And sucking them up to the utmost of their strength, Until they waste away and wither like hungry demons, Reduced utterly to skin and bones. Since the gods exist in this world, How long will they forgive them? In this world the Lord God, So that all the myriad people equally, May pass their days joyously and happily'

To spread the divine rule of Tengoku and Jiyōdo is the divine will of the Kami. Yet how can one ever measure it? With Samori at the top above, and the Fuiuren and the wealthy elders behaving as they please and selfishly, the retribution for oppressing the lower Kunitami is now instantly before our eyes. Have you realized it yet, Samorikami, and you other hundred officials? If you do not reform your hearts right now, the Minshiu will raise a battle cry and press forward, shouting, 'Strike and punish them!' That ominous sign is vividly and clearly perceived from this moment on. Ah, Minshiu, O Minshiu, do not sorrow in the least. The Kami of supreme benevolence and supreme love will surely abandon your state of distress until when, pray tell?

Welcoming the spring of Ichiyōraifuku, eternally establishing the peaceful and joyful land of the Kami within this mortal world, He shall surely lift up you pure and righteous ones, who have suffered below until now, to the exalted heights. The heavens shall descend and become the earth, and the earth shall rise and become the heavens. In this world of Uitenpen, how long shall we permit the unbridled actions of Daimiyō and Shōmiyō? Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, the Kami is with you. We are the children of the Kami, the palaces of the Kami. When the season finally arrives, a hundred righteous Kami who until now have fallen and dwelled in this world shall lead countless divine armies, annihilating evil and flattening wickedness completely.

Be bold, be brave, O Minshiu! The time has come, the time is now; this is the dawn of Shinseifukuko. A sudden, unexpected great conflagration is not at all a feat achievable by human power. Every bit of it is the warning of the precious Kami against evil. No matter how the Daimiyō, Shōmiyō, and Fuiuren may wield their tyranny, the world in which they wield it has already reached its end. Be bold, be brave, let everyone be brave! Trample underfoot every single wicked person who oppresses the Minshiu, without shrinking, without cowardice, and with supreme dignity, administer the baptism of fire. Wash the world with bloodshed!

The tea-master of the Mukō no Mori had disguised himself as a second wife in the mansion, lurking in stealth for the past three years, and while frequenting the Fuiuren, had attended upon them and investigated their affairs. Now that the time was fully ripe, however, she commanded her numerous subordinates and carried out the baptism of fire— this woman of massive and sturdy physique. What is there to fear of the Fuiuren crew? Daimiyō and Shōmiyō can go eat dirt. How could one possibly live in this world in fear of directors and inspectors? Be bold, be brave, O Minshiu! Though she is a woman, my subordinates in the mountains and fields of the Tarahan country are hundreds of thousands of living spirits, flexing their arms and waiting. once the order is given at last, from every nook and cranny of the eight directions,

signal fires will rise one after another! Without letting slip the present opportune moment, believing your transformation of the world to be the lifeline of your existence, shake yourselves, stand up, and rise on your feet! That Balance, famed throughout the world as the chieftain of the Minshiu league, is none other than myself. Ah, how valiant, how valiant! Whenever I look upon this tragic scene, the collateral suffering of the lower people is truly a seed of tears, yet when I gaze upon the state of the Daimiyō and Shōmiyō in their panic, my pent-up spleen is somewhat eased. What good is the Kōseiin? That too is merely another trick of the Fuiuren and the like to squeeze the lifeblood from you general Minshiu. Do not waver, do not be deceived.

When I think upon it, when I think upon it, the core of my heart causes the Kami of my soul to leap in dance. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, may your august spirits be blessed and tranquil! May the eyeballs of those sly and crafty villains pop right out!'

Several hundred inspection officers lying in wait at the crossroads rushed forward without a word, seizing them by hand and foot, binding them tightly hand and foot with ropes, and hauled Balance off in a handcart to the inspection station at Yokoōji. The subordinates, disguised as common folk, surged forward like a tidal wave to reclaim Balance, staging a fierce struggle between the inspectors and league members. Just as the inspection squad appeared to be in grave danger, the sound of bugles blew valiantly as two thousand samurai charged to the scene, leveling their rifles to intimidate them. Possessing no complete weapons to begin with, the common folk could only gnash their teeth as they watched their grand leader snatched away before their very eyes, forced at last into retreat.

Balance was led out before the chief inspector and subjected to a rigorous interrogation. Without the slightest sign of intimidation, Balance launched fluently into a fiery retort against the chief inspector.

Metsukegashira: 'What is your name?'

Balance: 'My name is called Balance. I am the supreme leader of the Minshiukiugodan. Do you think you can serve as the Ōmetsukegashira when you are so dim-witted that you have not known the famous face of Balance until now? Since it is a Gendai of weak-eating and strong-eating that severely lacks Beudō, I was named Balance in order to restore balance.'

Metsukegashira: 'What do you mean by instigating the Minshiu, gathering Futei elements, setting fires in the Shigai, daring to commit Akugefu Arayuru, and not yet satisfied, further agitating the Minshiu? Such an extremely heavy criminal like you needs no trial; for the sake of Kokuka, although it is Fubin, you are sentenced to execution by Jiusatsu, so it would be better for you to chant some Nenbutsu as a parting memory of this world.'

Balance is a daring and fearless hero unbecoming a woman. Bound tightly to the point where he could not even move his body, without the slightest sign of fear, he opened his wide mouth and laughed aloud,

Balance: 'Aha-ha-ha-ha! Blind men who cannot see ahead! Jiusatsu, strangulation, or whatever, if you can do it, try it and see. The life of this Balance is a life of counterweight to the entire Tarahan country. If my tens of thousands of subordinates hear everywhere in the country that Balance has been killed, they will rise in rebellion all at once. Evil Metsuke like you should consider the future course of events well before laying hands on me. First of all, what is the conduct of those who should be models for the nation? Do you not smuggle a young woman into the thatched hut of the tea-master Taruchin who lives near the forest the other day, visit her night after night wearing the clothes of a laborer, and become a slave of love with a greasy face? With such behavior, how can you make your administration complete? You are afraid of fish that swallow boats and dare not approach them, while you fish for mere minnows and crucian carps like trash, and feeling mighty pleased with yourselves while you do not know the ruin of the country, you are fools!'

Metsukegashira: 'Balance, what a dreadful thing are you saying! As the status of the people, is there such a daring thing as criticizing their conduct?'

Balance: 'Ha-ha-ha-ha, does it bother you that much? If so, I shall leave this matter to your own conjecture. Call forth the tea-master and interrogate him well. Regarding that as well, what is unforgivable is that the son of Samori Gandhi, a fellow named Arina, has unpardonably won over the tea-master, not only acting as an intermediary with a country girl, but himself wearing brocade garments within the palace, impersonating a false grand general, and dazzling the eyes of the left and right guardians, is he not? This Balance is the representative of all the people of Tarahan; I never tell a lie, so you had better investigate immediately.'

At these words, both the Metsukegashira and the surrounding Metsuke lost their color, letting out heavy sighs and only looking at each other's faces. Once again, the voices of the Minshiu and the Metsuke officers fighting began to echo noisily near the garden.

バランス『ハツハヽヽ、それほどお邪魔に成りますかな。然らば此問題は御推量を願つて置きませう。能く茶坊主を呼出してお査べなさい。夫に付ても許し難きは左守ガンヂーが悴アリナと云ふ奴、不届至極にも茶坊主を取込み、山出し女との媒介を致して居るのみならず、自分は殿中に錦衣を着け、偽太将と成り代り、左守右守の目を眩まして居るでは無いか。大王殿下は御重病にて上下憂鬱に沈む折柄、悴たるものは女に狂ひ、又左守の悴は王位を奪はむとして居る大胆不敵の曲者、其他の大名共は之を見ても推して知るべしで有る。此バランスはタラハン国民衆全部の代表者だ、決して嘘は言はないぞ、早速調べて見るが宜からう』

此言葉に目付頭も並み居る目付等も色を失ひ、太き息を漏らして互に面を見合すのみで有つた。又もや民衆と目付役と闘ふ声、庭の近辺に喧しく響いて来た。
