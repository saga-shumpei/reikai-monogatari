Chapter 2: 恋盗詞 [1726]

恋盗詞〔1726〕

§ 1

Immersed in the study of political science and the pursuit of new ideologies, grieving deeply over the current state of the upper and lower classes of the nation of Tarahan to the point of severe physical and mental exhaustion, his once-sharp intellect now like viewing a mountain through a mist—hazy and lacking clarity—so much so that to outside observers he might be suspected of melancholia, Prince Sudarman, as a result of agonizing torment, shut himself deep within the palace.

Unaccustomed to women, and even more unaccustomed to romance, the Prince was captivated by the appearance of a pure and unsullied maiden—who might be seen as the embodiment of the goddess of beauty—enveloped deep within the mountains of Asakuradani, far removed from the human world.

Hanna: 'Hey buddy, when I remember that beauty called Princess Dariya brought here some time ago by that mountain-priest who claimed to be the incarnation of the Heavenly Emperor, for a romantic with intense views on love like me, it is truly deeply moving. Even you must want to take off your helmet of usual hypocritical rhetoric and express your agreement with my theory.'

Tanya: 'When I see splendid men of the world making a fuss over effeminate love and holiness, stoking the flames of public demoralization, and joyfully leaping into the fire themselves, I truly lose patience with the spinelessness of fellows in this world. Hmph, being of a thief's calling, to talk about love and holiness is the height of absurdity. Hey, Hanna, I don't want to hear such wishy-washy, spineless talk, so don't say it in front of me anymore, because it makes me feel sick.'

ハンナ『オイ君、此間天帝の化身とか云ふ山子坊主が連れて来たダリヤ姫とか云ふ美人のことを思ひ出すと、俺のやうな恋愛観念の濃厚な色男に取つては、実に感慨無量だ。君だつて平素の偽善的言辞も兜を脱いで俺の持論に賛意を表したく成るだらう』

タンヤ『堂々たる天下の男子が、女々しい恋愛だの、神聖だなぞと騒ぎ廻つて風俗壊乱の火の手を煽ふり、自分も又その火中へ喜んで飛び込んで行く悲惨の状態を見ると、実に世の中の奴の腰抜け加減に愛想が尽きて了ふわ。ヘン、泥坊稼ぎの身分でありながら、恋愛の、神聖のとは臍茶の至りだ。オイ、ハンナ、そんなハンナリせぬ腰抜論は聴きたくないから、俺の前ではもう言つて呉れな。気分が悪くなるからのう』

§ 2

Ha: 'Heng, there cannot be any reason why a thief cannot formulate a theory of love. First listen, to my brilliant and profound theory.'

Ta: 'Today is also the memorial day of my deceased wife, so for the sake of her memorial service, I intend to try a sufficient attack against your mistaken theory, but you are convinced of it, aren't you?'

Ha: 'Interesting, if there is any room for you to put your mouth into my theory of love, then try and see. It is as clear a fact as seeing fire that your stumbling parrying sword will break and you will surely surrender at the gates of my military camp. Ahem, in today's progressing and developing civilization, those who do not hold a hobby in the theory of love have already fallen themselves into the realm of the non-human. What I greatly feel these days is that discussions concerning things like sexual desire and love are becoming remarkably abstract. However, all discussions are ruminative, and it looks to me as if things once swallowed are purposely made abstract and brought out. Saying that love is sacred or partial, that the problem of sex should be thus and so, that to think of love as free in such a way is immoral, and that divorce is absolutely impossible—hearing that women's association circles have even put their heads together and reached a resolution, I feel a strange state of mind. Are things like love and sexual desire something that can be disposed of so simply and casually? As the so-called civilized people of today say, can it be disposed of simply, academically, and intellectually, as one if not two, two if not three? I somehow cannot hold such a thought.'

Ta: 'Heng, upon encountering the case of a national emergency, I cannot help but be amazed at the foolishness of a guy chanting something like the love problem. Is it not much more human-like to settle such a problem extremely simply? Ah, how stupid, viewed from the perspective of scholarship and morality, I think a guy who brings up love is the scum of humanity.'

Ha: 'Oi, Taniya, you seem to have a tasteless and dry psychology, but no matter how much you push the world through with logic, and no matter how much you attack with scholarship and knowledge, you cannot settle such a big problem concerning human life as love so easily. Rather, it is a fantasy, a fruitless labor. Since humans inevitably must have faith and love, this problem has a sufficient value to be studied extremely cautiously.'

Ta: 'Things called love are observed by people from the aspect of the spirit, or seen from the aspect of the flesh, seen from nature, or seen from mere matter, but in short, unless it is within the bounds of morality, one cannot discuss sacred love. If there were someone who chanted love that utterly ignores morality, that should be called a psychological state of animals other than humans.'

Ha: 'That is a one-sided view squeezed out from your tasteless and dry brain, but it cannot be said to have ultimately captured complete love or sex. Love is naturally of a nature that is unfathomable, just like nature. To put it extremely, love is so overly sacred that it is even to a degree where one cannot even discuss this and that. When love is discussed, one might even say that its true nature has already departed somewhere. In other words, love is only recognized in the place where spirit, flesh, nature, and matter are all beaten and made into a single ball, and everything is present simultaneously. Spirit and flesh unity is well said, but it is so fluid that it is not even sufficient. Therefore, in discussing love, like your theory, in ordinary ethical logic, you cannot say that because it is thus it is that, or because it is that it is thus. If it were an ordinary general fact, no matter what kind of thing it is, if you push forward from the result to think about it, the answer will come out fairly accurately, but limited only to love, it will not be settled so simply. When it becomes intellectual and ethical, it is already the dregs of things called love or sex, and no matter how much scholars and critics like you build up solemn discussions and opinions, self-conceiting that you have added a new, accurate, and just perspective upon the love theories of old, you are merely grasping at dregs and making a big fuss thinking it is a diamond, after the true substance of love has already slipped away and fled to a distance of ten thousand miles.'

ハ『面白い、僕の恋愛論に口を入れる余地があるならやつて見玉へ。しどろもどろの受太刀が折れて、屹度僕の軍門に降るは火を睹るよりも明かな事実だ。オホン、日進月歩文明の今日では、恋愛論に趣味を持たないものは、最早人外の境域に自ら堕落して居るものだ。この頃僕が大に感ずる事は、性欲とか恋愛と云ふ事に関する議論が、著しく抽象的に成つて居ることだ。然し凡ての議論が反芻的で一度呑み込んだものを、わざと抽象的にして出して居る様に僕には見えて成らぬ。ヤレ恋愛は神聖だとか偏的だとか、性の問題は斯くあるべきものだとか、そんな風に恋愛を自由なものに考へては不道徳だとか、離婚は絶対に不可いとか云つて、婦人会連中が首を鳩めて決議までやつたと聞いて、僕は不可思議な心持ちがするのだ。恋愛とか性欲とか云ふものは、そんなに簡単に無雑作に片付けらるるものだらうか。今の所謂文明人間の言ふが如く、一で無ければ二、二で無ければ三と云ふやうに、簡単に、学問的乃至知識的に片付けて了ふことの出来るものだらうか。僕は何うも左様な考へは持てないのだ』

タ『ヘン、国家危急の場合に当つた今日、恋愛問題なんか唱へる奴の野呂さ加減に呆れざるを得ないわ。そんな問題は極めて簡単に片付けて了ふ方が余程人間らしいぢや無いか。アタ阿呆らしい、学問上道徳上から見ても恋愛なんか口にする奴は、僕は人間の屑だと思つて居る』

ハ『オイ、タンヤ、君は無味乾燥な心理を持つて居る様だが、世の中は理窟で何程押し通したつて、学問や知識でいくら攻めて行つたつて、恋愛と云ふが如き人間生涯に関する大問題を、さう易々と片付ける訳には行かないよ。却てそれは空想だ、徒労だ。どうしても人間には信仰と恋愛が無くてはならないのだから、此問題は極めて慎重に研究すべき価値が充分にあるよ』

タ『恋愛と云ふものは人に由つて霊の方面から観察し、或は肉の方面から見たり、自然から見たり、又は単なる物質から見たりするのもあるが、要するに道徳の範囲内に於てでなければ、神聖な恋愛を論議する事は出来ぬ。万々一道徳を度外したる恋愛を唱ふるものありとすれば、夫れは人間以外の動物の心理状態と云ふべきものだ』

ハ『それは君の無味乾燥な頭脳から割り出した一面の見方であるが、到底完全なる恋愛、または性を捕捉したものとは言はれない。恋愛は元来自然と同様に端倪すべからざる性質のものだ。極端に言へば、恋愛なるものは余りに神聖過ぎて、彼此と論議する事さへも出来ない位のものだ。恋愛を論議された時には、モハヤ其本当のものは何処かに去つて了つて居ると言つても好い位だ。換言せば、恋愛は霊も肉も自然も物質も凡てを打つて一丸と為した処にのみ、恋愛の髣髴が認められるもので、何も彼もが凡て同時にあるのだ。霊肉一致とは好く言つたものだが、夫れでさへ充分で無い程流動的なものだ。だから恋愛を論ずるに当つては君の説の様に、普通の倫理学的論法で、斯うだから彼だとか、彼だから斯うだとか云ふ事は出来ない。普通一般的の事実なら、どんな事でも結果から押して考へて行けば、答へは可成正確に出て来るが、恋愛だけに限つて、さう簡単に片付かないよ。知識や倫理的に成つた時には、最早恋愛とか性とか言ふものの粕屑であつて、君の如き学者や、論客が何程鹿爪らしい議論や意見を立てて、自分こそは古来の恋愛論の上に新しい、そして的確な、正当な、一見地を加へたものと自惚れて居ても、徒に粕屑を握んで金剛石の様に思つて大騒ぎをして居るだけで、恋愛の本体は何時の間にやら千万里の遠方へ滑つて逃げて往つた後なのだ』

§ 3

T: 'Your theory is an evil thought as if to say that even if morality is entirely ignored, the order of society is thrown into disorder, and the family system is destroyed, if only love is carried out satisfactorily, peace will prevail under the heavens. Although freedom and love must be necessary things for human beings, to regard love as sacred even while disrupting the order of society and the family bears animality and is the assertion of an outsider path. I cannot agree to it. The frequent successive emergence of triangular problems and divorce problems in society is also because evil-thoughted persons like you push their way. Although love is indeed a sacred thing, I think it can be called sacred love for the first time by taking a little consideration of modesty, or the point of ethics. Your theory of love is a metamorphosis of the so-called theory of custom destruction.'

H: 'If you treat love too much as a moral problem like you do, its true entity will already lose both shadow and shape. Before one knows it, it has slipped away from between the fingers. Without even noticing that, you seem to catch only the dregs of love left behind and discuss this and that. We must think that we have to make it more of an active existent and be mindful to touch it deeply at every single moment.'

T: 'Love can become sacred for the first time by the strict observance of monogamy. And human beings must thoroughly guard the path of monogamy to the end, otherwise the dignity as a human being cannot be maintained. Therefore, thinking that love cannot be established unless it is ethical everywhere, I have been making efforts for many years alongside my trade of thievery.'

H: 'While being in the position of a thief whose motto is to rape the women of others and plunder properties, I greatly admire your spirit and courage of not sparing years of effort toward this problem with the theory of monogamy and a sense of morality, but as a secondary problem whether your firm assertion can be guarded or cannot be guarded when actually facing the occasion, anyway I love that readiness to try to make an effort. Indeed, even someone like myself, when standing in the painful position of a triangular state, keenly realizing that love is not something to be taken lightly, and acutely knowing that the soul of a human being is not something to be played with, there were times when I could not help soliloquizing, "After all, the system of monogamy is splendid. It is made in such a way, isn't it." Therefore, I too am one person who respects the affection-rich relationship of monogamy, the relationship of a harmonious and thick husband and wife who have no leisure to cast their eyes elsewhere mutually. However, that is not as a principle. I only want to stop at saying that it is a good thing. The reason is because nature is not something that can be finished being spoken of so simply. Also, no matter how ideal monogamy may be, if all human beings were able to do it without reason, or if this nature were made so that they could do it, life would become monotonous for that very reason, with neither subtle waves of beauty nor minute whirlpools of emotion, and it would become completely colorless and desolate. No, even if one could endure just that, after all this life would not hold together. It would become like something badly stereotyped, without a shred of leeway, and finally lead to a hundred breakdowns appearing everywhere. Moreover, even when viewed simply from the point of reproduction, it is well understood that life could not possibly be established with such a thing. Therefore, your theory of monogamy is not bad either, but it takes the form that it would be troublesome if all human beings became that way. Love must be more free and full of vitality, and it turns out that it must possess a strong power to quickly and easily break down from within whatever shapes such as hopes, ideals, morals, or conduct made by human intellect and consciousness, no matter how many are produced.'

§ 4

T: 'To a self-styled free-love advocate and sex-supremacist like you, even I am quite taken aback. My jaw has dropped open and won't close. Go ahead and chatter away about whatever you please.'

H: 'You mustn't misunderstand me. I am by no means a free-love advocate. Nor do I seek merely the satisfaction of sexual desire to throw the world into disorder. Once upon a time, as an exponent of naturalism, I was arbitrarily pigeonholed by society as someone who indulged in ruthless sexual desires akin to those of beasts, but I am certainly not a believer in the sect of sexual supremacy. I merely assert that what we call romance is precisely such a free and unbridled thing. Once it becomes mere knowledge, or as I said just a moment ago, once it is reduced to worthless and trivial arguments, as a vibrant and fluid existence, it is ultimately impossible to define it in such a rigid way—that is what I wish to say.'

T: 'Such unrestrained notions as your theory are unforgivable. If you view romance in that manner, does that not alone make you a sterling, certified free-love advocate?'

H: 'You might take it by interpreting it so superficially, but that point is truly difficult. There exists within it an extremely profound, intricate, and delicate psychological realm that is all too easily overlooked. It can be said to be a kind of understanding, yet it can also be called a kind of emotional stirring. Furthermore, to put it another way, one could say there is a profound sigh directed toward humanity, life at large, and vast nature. It is something close to the feeling that nothing can be done about it. Romance is something that can never be fully seen through. Because it is both simple and yet unfathomably deep, if you try to grasp it, it is right there before you, but once you try to pursue it to its end, it remains completely elusive. This very feeling is, in short, the purity of romance.'

T: 'Does not your entire theory sound precisely like the likes of Tayama Katai, a novelist who lived around the twentieth century?'

H: 'Naturally. In truth, I am infatuated with Tayama Katai's theory of romance, ha ha ha ha.'
