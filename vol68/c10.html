Chapter 10: 宗匠財 [1734]

宗匠財〔1734〕

§ 1

The conflict between the masses and the samurai centering around the Torishimarishiyo and in its vicinity became temporarily furious, but to recover the balance of the Daitōmoku who was revered as the savior of the masses and the Jibo of the hinmin, the general masses—old men and old women, those able to stand on their sunekoshi, and shiyakushi—gathered in crowds every successive kokukoku, and their momentum was fierce; neither the Metsuketai nor the samurai could do anything about it, and finally the Ōmetsukegashira also bent his own will, untied the rope of balance, planned a compromise with the masses, and moreover made it so as to present kangen. Summoning a chiyabauzu to investigate the jitsupi of the matter, he swore to punish Samori's son Alina with jiukei before the masses, and here at last the ōsaudō came to a chintei. The Metsuketai promptly ordered his subordinates to arrest the chiyabauzu. Seeing the chiyabauzu arrested, Princess Subaru was greatly surprised, and together with her lover who had come secretly in labor-garb, she concealed her figure far from the capital, mingling into the darkness. Alina too, realizing that the situation was not easy, fled secretly from inside the castle along with Shin. The chiyabauzu Tarutin, left tightly bound, was led out before the Ōmetsukegashira and received interrogation.

Ōmetsukegashira: 'What is your full name?'

Tarutin shook his long bald head two or three times, slightly bending his waist,

Tarutin: 'Hi, I am called Tarutin, a master of tea ceremony who lives near the forest of Mukō. What kind of special business do you have with me? I have no memory of officials suddenly coming and taking someone like me, who has done no wrong, to such a place as this. Is not this place a place where evildoers come? I am a person who, being pure and undefiled, transmits the tea ceremony respectfully to the honorable people and sends away my remaining years in lonely quietness. Besides, I have been deserted by my dearest, most precious wife whom I had lived with for three years, and in the midst of worry, to be brought to such a place as this—I cannot even receive my daily wages at all; truly, being a hinmin, my agobitsu will go up from tomorrow. Please, I want you to grant me reasonable daily wages. And since you have bound me, who have no fault, I would like to receive compensation. Today, when the law for compensating victims of wrongful accusations is about to be promulgated, please, as for that, I will not ask for anything overly high, so I would like you to supply it according to my value.'

Ōmetsukegashira: 'You are a fool. Even though you are in a position to go in and out of the households of the honorable people and teach the tea ceremony, is there such a thing as being led into a place like this and making such a demand, eh?'

Tarutin: 'No matter what kind of place I go to, I demand daily wages just the same. Even if I go to the residence of the office of Samori, or even if I go to the tea house inside Tarahan Castle with awe-inspiring reverence, I receive corresponding wages, so even if it is for half an hour, without receiving wages to that extent, I cannot make a living. To be looked at in the same way as a person like you, who uses many subordinates, comes to work from around nine in the morning, leans on a chair while looking at the newspaper with an amused expression, and while doing this and that twelve o'clock arrives—upon which you take a restaurant bento and heartily eat it, and then spend about an hour on post-meal exercise touring interesting places, and from there read the remaining newspapers, press seal-judgments two or three times pop-pop, briskly return home, receive the treatment of great and small lords, and take a huge monthly salary—it is a bit disadvantageous for me. It is impossible to speak on the same day as a person like me who puts high charcoal into the hearth, does nothing but bow saying "heēkora, haikora", and barely manages to keep body and soul together.'

Ōmetsukegashira: 'You said your wife ran away from you, but what is the name of that wife?'

Tarutin: 'Hi, my wife is an unexpected dotenba, but since she will not tell her name no matter what, I call her Bag. Running away with a thousand ryo of gold in that bag, I am plunged into the abyss of disappointment and despair, as if an owl missed its evening meal. If your office is for the protection of the people, please search for my wife. And I want you to make her return my wife and the gold. Actually, I thought of making a petition for protection, but because an honorable precious guest has come to visit, I could not take my eyes off, and on top of that comes that big fire, so I was a little late.'

§ 2

Ōmetsuke: 'You say you are living from day to day, but how did you come to possess that thousand ryō of gold?'

Taru: 'You say such a strange thing again. Money is something that humans should possess. Where is the mystery in a human possessing money?'

Ōmetsuke: 'I do not say that possessing it is bad; I am asking how you made it.'

Taru: 'This is a remark unbecoming even for an Ōmetsukegashira; do you think of me as a counterfeiter of paper money because you ask how I made it? I do not know if those banknotes are convertible or inconvertible, but are they not things printed by the printing bureau that you people manage? They are those bills that have the likes of Kewpie-sama and Fukusuke-sama attached to them. I am not a person skillful enough to make paper money. In the tea ceremony, I take the twelve temahe as the foundation, from which it branches into three hundred and sixteen temahe, and I also know the temahe from the first dan to the seventh dan as the kaumoku of the tea ceremony. If it is about the tea ceremony, I will answer as much as you like, but I do not know a bit about making money. This must be a mistake in your glasses, Oyakunin-sama, ahem.'

Ōmetsuke: 'Eh, what an uncomprehending fellow. I am asking how you earned that money.'

Taru: 'This is also a strange inquiry. I am a master of the tea ceremony by profession. No matter how I earn it, there is no reason for me to be scolded for what I have earned in business, nor do I have the obligation to explain that to you, and you have no right to say this or that about money others have earned; such things are unnecessary meddling. I also visit your relatives' homes from time to time for the tea ceremony, and when I hear the stories of your relatives, it is said that Ōmetsuke-sama takes many bribes to permit the operation of waiting houses in the middle of the town, and keeps mistresses there; this story will certainly not be wrong. No matter what you say, I heard it at your relative's, and what is more, at the home of your younger sister's marriage.'

Ōmetsuke: 'Hey, hey, what are you saying that is so bad for my reputation. Are there not many metsuke listening right there? Do you not understand the divine law? It is written that "regardless of the existence of the matter, those who slander others in public shall be punished by the penal code under Article 800 of the Chikeihō." Do not say reckless things.'

Taru: 'He-he-he, is it inconvenient for you? Since the tea ceremony temperature was a bit too high, I made you sweat hot. Ha-ha-ha.'

Ōmetsuke: 'It is said that a rare guest was at your house, eh, is that true?'

Taru: 'He-he, they were indeed there, and they are likely still there now. It is that the awe-inspiring Sudaruman-sama has asked me to hide the daughter of the former Samori, Subāru-hime, who is a beauty like a celestial maiden, and he visits night after night. She is truly a wonderful beauty. No matter what, I bowed and complied with the will of Sudaruman-sama, as it is fearful even to offer an opinion to his person. I am waiting with a long neck, thinking that I might even receive a reward from the vicinity of the police station. This Taruchin, who has comforted the heart of Myōhō-sama and given him supreme joy, surely has the value of the First Order of Merit. Despite that, I am being asked by Arina-no-kimi-sama, the only son of the famous and powerful Samori Gandī-sama of the Tarahan country, to teach the tea ceremony to the future queen, Subāru-jō-sama. No matter how grandly you speak, you would not be able to serve Myōhō-sama directly or teach the precious tea ceremony to the queen. Well, do not think with such a difficult face. If Myōhō-sama succeeds to the Great King's throne soon, I will become the teacher of the Great King-sama, and I will be promoted to the role of sniffing the ears of the Highness, deep within the palace. That is why, if you also wish to advance in your career, you should treat this Taruchin well while there is still time. It would be fine to even give me a bottle of wine, and please give me a kin of konpeitō from time to time. This will also be a supplement to my tea sweets, which is very convenient. Now is the time for you to curry favor with me, ahem.'

大目付『持つてゐるのが悪いとは云はぬ、どうして拵へたかと云ふのだ』

タル『之は又大目付頭にも似合はぬお言葉、どうして拵へたかとは私をも紙幣偽造犯人とお思ひですか。彼の紙幣は兌換だか不換だか知りませぬが、貴方がたが経営して御座る印刷局から刷り出された物ぢや御座りませぬか。キューピーさまや福助さまが付いて御座る彼のお札ですよ。私は紙幣を拵へるやうな器用なものでは厶いませぬ。茶の湯では十二手前を本とし、それから分れて三百十六手前となり、又茶の湯の綱目としては初段から七段迄の手前を存じて居ります。茶の湯の事なら、いくらでもお答へ致しますが、金を拵へる事はチツとも存じませぬ。之はお役人さまの、お眼鏡違ひで御座りませう、オツホン』

大目付『こりやこりや、外聞の悪い何を云ふのだ。沢山の目付が、其所に聞いて居るぢや無いか。其方は神法を心得ぬか、「事の有無に拘らず、人を公衆の前にて誹謗した者は知計法第八百条にて刑鉢に処す」と書いて有る。メツタの事を云ふものではないぞ』

タル『ヘツヘヽヽ、御都合が悪う御座りますかな。チツト茶の湯加減が過ぎましたので、熱い汗をかかせました。ハツハヽヽ』

大目付『お前の宅に、エー、珍客が居られたと云ふ事だが、本当か』

タル『ヘーヘー、居られましたとも、まだ現にゐられるでせう。畏れ多くもスダルマン様が、元の左守の娘子スバール姫と云ふ、夫は夫は天女のやうな美人をかくまつて呉れと云ふ事で、夜な夜なお通ひで御座ります。本当に素敵な美人ですよ。何と言つてもスダルマン様の御身、御意見申すも恐れ多いと謹んで御意に応じました。何分取締所あたりから御褒美でも頂け相なものと、首を長うして待つて居りますよ。妙法様のお心を慰め奉り、無上の歓喜をお与へ申した此タルチンは、正に勲一等功一級の価値は確にあるでせう。夫にも拘らず、タラハン国に於て雷名隠れなき最大権力者、左守のガンヂー様の一人息子アリナの君様に頼まれて、未来のお妃様のスバール嬢様に、お茶の手前を伝授申し上げて居るのです。何程偉相に申してもお前さまとしては、妙法様を直接にお世話したり、お妃さまに尊い茶道を伝授すると云ふ事は出来ますまい。マアそんな小難しい顔せずにお考へなさいませ。今に妙法様が、大王の跡を継がれましたならば、私は大王様のお師匠様と成つて、殿内深く、すまし込み、殿下の耳を嗅ぐ役に抜擢されますよ。夫だからお前さまも出世が仕度くば今の中、此タルチンを十分待遇して置きなさい。葡萄酒の一打位贈つても可し、金平糖の一斤位はチヨイチヨイ贈つて下さい。此方も茶菓子の足しにも成り、誠に好都合だ。お前さまも、私に取り入るのは今の中ですよ、オツホン』

§ 3

He sits composed, with an air of arrogant grandiosity.

Ōmetsuke: 'Oi, Haruya, untie Taruchin's bonds.'

'Hai, understood.'

Taruchin: 'Nay, nay, do not untie them; leave these bonds just as they are. I shall report in minute detail to Sudaruma-sama how you fellows gathered together and subjected me to such treatment. If I do so, he will surely favor Taruchin, and the lot of you shall be dismissed on the spot. It is a pity for you; if you dawdle, the sparks will fly even up to the head inspector, Ōmetsukegashira. If you wish these ropes untied, I shall let you untie them. How much secret service money are you willing to offer?'

Ōmetsuke: 'Atsuhahahahaha, this fellow is surely mad. One cannot arrest a lunatic and punish him by law. We shall recognize him as mentally deranged. Oi, Taruchin, from this very moment you are released; consider yourself fortunate.'

Taruchin: 'Henh, things will not go quite so smoothly as the wholesale merchant would have it. While binding fast Sudaruma-sama's auspiciously favored favorite retainer, there can be no talk of release or anything of the sort; is not your manner of doing things, you sir, rather misdirected? Do you suppose I would ever budge from this place? Before long, Sudaruma-sama will inquire after Taruchin's whereabouts and come to welcome me in a state-of-the-art automobile. Until then, no matter what anyone may say, I shall not move an inch from this spot!'

Ōmetsuke: 'Ah, what a troublesome creature we have dragged in. Oi, Haruya, at any rate, untie this madman's bonds and send him off to his mansion. And make sure to investigate thoroughly whether Sudaruma-sama is in residence or not. Mind you go with due care so that there are no hitches.'

So saying, the Ōmetsuke takes a pocket watch from his breast, and

'Ah, it is already time to leave.'

uttering this, as if fleeing, he opens the door and walks back toward his mistress's residence.

Taruchin raises his voice to a loud pitch,

Taruchin: 'Hoi there, Ōmetsukegashira's fellow, is there such a thing as running away? Wait, I have a moxu-cautery to administer to you. If you run off without listening to what I have to say, you will be dismissed starting tomorrow!'

thus he vociferously shouts.

At length, the numerous inspectors persuade the intractable Taruchin in various ways, ply him with wine, sweets, and the like to humor him, and it is arranged that he shall be escorted back to his house.

As Taruchin is being escorted back to his home by the corps of inspectors, the intoxication of the wine takes effect, and he begins to chant:

'Ah, how amusing, how amusing! What has come over this world, That it has grown so ridiculously delightful? Lord Sudaruma is in my home, Mistaking it for Mount Tarahan, Together with the princess, From morning till night they bill and coo, And drool as they sit there. Then comes the son of Gandy of the Left Guard, Alina by name, tripping along, And casting side glances as he approaches. While things are thus going on, The sound of the bell echoes through the town of Tarahan; When I push open the window and gaze out, Thud, thud, thud, throughout the town The people's houses burn, the people weep, In the twinkling of an eye, Half of the streets of Tarahan Have turned to black earth, what a pity! Those rich folk who until now Have indulged in a life of endless luxury, When one sees their plight of misery today, Ah, truly, what a delightful world! That fellow called the head inspector, Dragooned me on purpose, Sought out trivial matters to question me about, Was defeated by my logic, blew bubbles, Began to break wind and tremble all over Like a piece of konjac, Took out his pocket watch And declared it was already time to leave, Showered me with sweet excuses, And slunk away stealthily. A world where such trashy officials Strive mightily in the middle of the capital's grand avenue— How could we common people Sleep peacefully with our pillows high? Be that as it may, this too is entirely The grand design of the Great Deities. Even wretched and ruined old me, Chosen by Lord Sudaruma's discerning eye, Have become a guide taking the princess by the hand; Thanks to the tea ceremony, even this me Has had his kettle-head moistened. Ehehehe, etsuhe, ehehehehe! Yet truly, truly, in this world, All human affairs and everything else Are entirely the horse's dung of the old man of Saiō. Eat dung too, Ōmetsuke! Perhaps I will soon rise in the world And press down upon your head! Ehehehe, etsuhe, ehehehehe! The missus's pouch Forsook me and fled in all haste; This too was surely the divine will Assisting me. May you receive a missus with big hips, Who runs away of her own accord from the other side!'
