Chapter 17: 地の Rock Door [1741]

地の岩戸〔1741〕

§ 1

Umekōwake, a Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, riding upon a Hakuba, advanced ever eastward across the Daigenya of Dekatan Kaugen, which stretched Beubau up into Ten, aiming for the Nanroku of Mount Namiyama as his Meate, and reining in his steed before an old Suishiya-goya, offered this Hitorigoto:

Umekō: 'Well now, how strange! Just now, I surely heard a Hitogoe in this Atari. Spurs urged my steed to Chikayo, yet there stands only this single Abaraya where no man seems to dwell. Though a Suishiya is here, its Unten is in a state of Chiushi. Without a Saui, some Himitsu must lurk hidden within this Goya. Come, let me investigate.'

With that, he lightly leaped down from his steed, and while tying it to a post of the Suishiya-goya, he listened carefully to the Atari in various directions. From nowhere in particular, human voices could be heard. It seemed as if coming from the depths of the earth, yet also as if coming from above, so that the Dedokoro of the voices could not be known. Umekōwake spread a straw mat, sat in Tanza, closed his eyes in Meimoku, and offered up Kiguwan; as a Ketsukuwa of this, it became clear to him that "a noble person has been thrown into an underground cellar." Examining the Atari very carefully, he found floorboards polished smooth by straw sandals. Firmly placing his hand upon one and prying it up, he found a flight of stairs leading down into an underground cellar. As Umekōwake descended these stairs for about four or five ken while turning to the right and left, there he found two men huddling together and trembling in fear.

'Hey, who are you? By my judgment, this appears to be a den of evil where some evil Himitsu lies hidden. Tell me the plain truth!'

Sāman: 'Y-Yes, I-I-I am a h-h-human being named S-Sā-Sāman. I have absolutely no recollection of committing any bad misdeeds. By order of the Minister of the Right Guard, I am working here. Please, I beg you, let us off for today. Have mercy, have pity, I pray you! H-Hey, Kāku, won't you also offer a-a bit of an excuse and explanation?'

Kāku: 'Oh, Sendenshi-sama, I am named Kāku, an ordinary man of the world, neither particularly bad nor particularly good. The truth is, the Minister of the Right Guard plotted a grand rebellion, and secretly issued orders to capture Prince Sudarman, the Crown Prince of King Karapin, offering a reward of two thousand yen. Thus, twenty of us each made a tidy profit of a-a hundred yen. Please show us consideration. Since we have taken the money, we will return the Prince at any time. Right, Sāman? I-Isn't that so?'

Sāman: 'E-Even so, if we hand the Prince over to th-this person, won't our h-h-heads fly off?'

Umekō: 'What you two are saying makes no sense at all. In short, you are saying that at the request of the Right Guard, you hid the Crown Prince of Tarahan Castle somewhere, is that it?'

Sāman: 'Yes, that is exactly so. There is not the slightest difference. We have hidden him somewhere.'

Umekō: 'Saying "somewhere" explains nothing. Why don't you state his whereabouts clearly?'

Sāman: 'Yes, we hid him in a... place.'

Umekō: 'In what kind of place did you hide him?'

Sāman: 'Yes, it is a place that starts with Ch-Ch-Chi. Hey, Kāku, you say half of it too. If I reveal the Himitsu all by myself, I'll be held responsible. Let's say it one word at a time.'

Kāku: 'Yes, Sendenshi-sama, I shall speak without concealing anything. We hid him in "Kā".'

Sāman: 'We hid him in "Shī".'

Kāku: 'We hid him in "Tsū".'

Umekō: 'What? "Chī", "Kā", "Shī", and "Tsū"... Ah, Chikashitsu [underground cellar]? Speaking of the underground cellar, isn't it right here?'

Sāman: 'Sā, it is.'

Kāku: 'Yō, it is.'

Umekō: 'Hey, what a hassle! Couldn't you just say "Sayō de gozaimasu [That is so]"?'

Sāman: 'If we were to reveal such a Himitsu, we would be beheaded by the Minister of the Right Guard, so we deliberately split up the words so it would not be understood. Please understand us; if you think with your brilliant brain, you will surely comprehend.'

Subaruhime had been considering this state of affairs from saizen, but feeling as though she had met a Buddha in hell, she could not contain her joy, and with a most delighted air,

Caught and imprisoned in this puzzling hitoya, We two have spent our days weeping. The wondrous divine light of the Imperial Kami appearing, The joy of illuminating the darkness of eternal night.'

Umewakewake searched for the key to the prison, but no key-like thing was to be found anywhere, and when he turned to the two people and strictly interrogated them, the reply was that the key to the prison had been taken back by the Minister of the Right. While at his wits' end, Umewakewake chanted the heavenly numbers with all his might and began to pray. Miraculously, the stone door of the prison opened with a snap, and five-colored lights brilliantly illuminated the interior. Both the Taishi and Subaruhime rushed out of the prison as if tumbling, clung to Umewakewake's body from front and back, and, blinded by tears of joy, could not utter even a word for a short while.

Ume: 'If I hear correctly, the Crown Prince is the Taishi-sama of Tarahan Castle, and you, Madam, are Subaruhime-sama. Why, pray tell, were you shoved into such a place?'

Tai: 'Though it is shameful, the two of us fell in love, secretly slipped out of the castle interior, and were hiding in a ruined temple deep in the mountains, when that foul-hearted Minister of the Right, named Sakurensu, out of a plot to seize the royal family, regarded us as nuisances, ordered villains, gave them money to bind us tight, and brought us to such a place. The plot was to starve and freeze us two to death, and already the navel of my determination was fixed, but this unexpected help from you—such a joyful thing there could not possibly be.'

Su: 'Propaganda Envoy-sama, thank you very much. Thanks to you, my life has been saved. I shall not forget this obligation even unto the age of Miroku. Kamutsukasa-sama, parent of my life, I am deeply grateful.'

Ume: 'To save people is the role of the Propaganda Envoy; being thanked in that way is rather troublesome. Since Kami has saved you through my body, please express your gratitude to Kokusokunitokotachi-no-Ōkami and Toyokumuno-no-Ōkami. Come, raise your voices together with me and let us offer our thanks.'

'Hai, thank you.'

And together with Kamutsukasa Umewakewake, the two people offered up words of gratitude to the Ōkami from the very bottom of their hearts, dedicating their overflowing utmost sincerity, and after finishing, Umewakewake turned to the two people and said,

Ume: 'Come, everyone, staying long in such a place is something to be feared. From now on, I shall escort you back to Tarahan Castle. Correct your mistaken hearts of up until now, return to the castle interior, and set the mind of the Great King at ease.'

カ『アヽ、もしもし宣伝使様、決して私は此後に於て悪事は致しませぬから、どうぞ牢獄へ入れる事だけは許して下さいませ。その代りお馬の別当でも何でも致します』

梅『人を救けるは宣伝使の役だ。併し乍ら恐れ多くも太子殿下を苦しめ奉つた其方共なれば、一人だけ助けてやらう。一人は気の毒ながら此牢獄に打ち込んでおく積りだ。太子様どちらが比較的善人で厶いますか』

太『ハイ、私としては甲乙の区別がつきませぬ。揃ひも揃つて悪い奴で厶いますから』

サ『もし太子様私は何時も貴方に対し同情を持つて居たぢや厶いませぬか。このカークと云ふ奴、私が「太子様にお腹が空くだらうから、焼甘藷の蔕でも買つて来てソツと上げたらどうだらう」と云うた所、大悪党のカークの奴、「私はそんな宋襄の仁はやらない、断乎として水一杯も呑ます事は出来ない。右守の司にそんな事が聞えたら、俺の首が飛ぶ」と極端に自己愛を発揮した奴で厶いますから、どうか私をお助け下さいませ』

§ 4

Ume: 'Atsuhahahaha, oi, Kaku, did you say what Sāman just mentioned?'

Ka: 'Hai, there is no use in denying it. It is no use hiding anything before Kamisama. I said it just as he described. Truly, now that I think of it, I have done a thing for which there is no maoshiwake. Please, throw me into raugoku and return. Sāman has a niyōbau, but I am hitorimi; it does not matter what happens to me. I have no wife, no children, and even if I die at any itsu, there is no one to weep for me.'

Ume: 'Hahahaha, you are a surprisingly shiyōjiki fellow. It seems you are the more zennin of the two. Since you have confessed so aritei, your sin has vanished. I am afraid there is no shikata but to throw Sāman into the rauya. Taishisama, Denka, what are your thoughts on this?'

Tai: 'Ya, that would be omoshiro. It is fitting that an akunin who would reveal another's himitsu to save his own jibun should be kept in a cold raugoku forever as a punishment.'

Sa: 'Moshi Taishisama, Denkasama, please, I beg you to ōme the akuji I have committed until now. In return, if it is for the sake of Denka, I will die even if you otsushiya me to die now.'

Tai: 'Ya, how omoshiro. In that case, I shall forgive you from being thrown into raugoku. How is it, are you happy?'

Sa: 'Hai, I am very happy. Thank you so much for saving me. From kongo, if it is for the sake of Denka, I will offer my inochi at any time.'

Tai: 'Ya, what a dear fellow. In that case, become my migaha and die here and now. I intend to take your head, present it before the Migamorisukasa, claim it is the living head of Prince Sudaruman, and offer it as a gift in a head-bucket.'

Sa: 'Me-messō na, if my inochi is taken here and now, there is no kai in having been saved.'

Tai: 'Hahahaha, how could a living head like yours ever serve as my migaha? A roof tile cannot replace gold. Ah, all human hearts are likely just like this. The old retainers who serve closely by the side of my father, the King, used to say without shame, as if it were a habit, "If it is for the sake of the Great King, I will stake my inochi and work at any time." Yet, when the great disturbance broke out on the fifth day of the fifth month, the retainers, starting with the Hidarimorisukasa and Migamorisukasa, scattered in all directions, and not a single one came to the palace. If even the high-ranking retainers who were fed on large stipends are like that, it is no wonder that a commoner like you would begrudge your inochi. As a celebration for having been saved by the Senden-shi, I shall release both of you. You may go wherever you please.'

Upon hearing these words filled with the compassion of the Prince, the two men, who had been paralyzed with fear, rose up one after another. As if thinking they should flee before the royal will changed again, they exchanged a word of gratitude each, saying, 'A, ri, ga, tō, u, sa, ma,' and dashed up the stairs, disappearing into the clouds and mist as they raced toward their homes. Umekōwake caught two wild horses grazing in the distant field and offered them to the two men. Since Subāru-hime had no experience in riding, they placed her on the saddled horse that Umekōwake had ridden, while the two men mounted the wild horses, kicking up dust with the hooves of their steeds as they galloped toward the northeastern sky.

The captured Prince's child, too, by the grace of the three-five God, has been set free.

太子のこの情の籠もつた言葉を聞くより、今迄腰を抜かして居た両人はムクムクと起き上り、長居は恐れ又もや御意の変らぬ内にと云つたやうな調子で、「ア、リ、ガ、ト、ウ、サ、マ」と互に一言づつ謝辞を述べながら、一目散に階段を昇り雲を霞と吾家をさして馳帰り行く。梅公別は遥の原野に遊んで居る二頭の野馬を捉へ来つて両人に勧めた。スバール姫は騎馬の経験がないので、梅公別が乗り来つた鞍付の馬に乗せ、二人の男は荒馬に跨り乍ら駒の蹄に土埃を立て、東北の空を目当に駆けて行く。
