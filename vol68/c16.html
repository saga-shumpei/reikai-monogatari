戦伝歌〔1740〕

§ 1

In Takaamahara, the Miyabashira and Chigi stand high, and in the presence of the Izunome Kami no Mikoto who dwells there in Tokoshihe, I revere and cross over the Sanyakakai. Following Terukuniwake, I cross the Kajikatauge, traverse the Tanima of the bosom, and finally reach the forest of the shrine. Passing through the Yamaguchi Ukiki forest, crossing the Lion River, and purifying my heart in the moon that shines over the Aoi Marsh, I proceed. I subdue the Magakami who lurk in the mountains of Kotsuchi and Anata, tormenting the Kunibito, and with the Azusayumi, I draw the string and return not to the Lake of Haru. Entrusting my body to the Tama no Mifune, I save the Amata people.

Landing at the port of Suga, I transmit the Nori of the Kami. Once again crossing the Sanya, I advance day and night to join the Kami no gun of the master, Terukuniwake. Just as I hasten my steps, shouting valiantly that I must save the great Daikuwasai of the Tarahan Jiyōshi, while the Enen flames scorch the Ame, I am blocked by the Magakami. Spending five or six days in vain, I advance through the desolate wilderness of Hyōbyōsenri; how valiant it is to proceed. Though I worry my heart, wishing for the sun and moon to shine brightly in the Ame and illuminate the lower world, black clouds in the Nakatsu-misora gather in ten or twenty layers, hiding the light of the Tianjin sun.

Though it is said to be the beginning of summer, it feels like autumn, still chilly. The rice seedlings planted in the fields are not developing well, turning red, revealing the ill omen of famine. Ah, Kannagara Kami-kami, may your Mitama be blessed. Forgive the sins of the lower Manmin, and further, purify the hearts of the Magabito who stand above and dominate, take away their sins, and make them people of sincerity. So that there may be no enmity under the heaven, and no darkness or defilement, shine upon us, Kannagara. The Sendenshi of Ume-kōwake, combining the Izu no Mitama and the Mizu no Mitama, has been brought to pass.

I prostrate myself before the great Kami of Izunome-mitama and pray. Whether the morning sun shines or is clouded, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if the earth should crack, if one follows the Nori of the Kami of the Sango, which is but one sincerity, there is nothing in this world to fear. The Kami appears on the surface, distinguishing between Kami and Oni. The Kaminaohi who created this world, and the Ōnaohi whose heart is pure—in all things, the world of man is but Kannagara Kami-kami. With a wide heart, re-proclaim, re-examine and re-hear the sins, forgive them, and pass through the path of the Kami. Even if Maga appears in the path ahead and brings any enmity upon my body,

what could possibly harm a body of sincerity wrapped in the blessings of the Kami? Come, Magatsukami, challenge me, O Great Serpents. I have the preparation of Izu. With the weapon of the living Kototama, even the demon armies of billions can be easily and instantly subdued as I advance. The Ume flowers of the three thousand worlds open all at once by the Nori of the Kami. They open, scatter, and bear fruit; know the grace of the sun, moon, and earth. The Ikigami who saves this world has appeared in Takaamahara. Ah, how valiant, how valiant, the Sendenshi commissioned by the Kami. Descending to the land of the moon, and suffering various miscellaneous calamities and a hundred bitterness,

I go as if through an uninhabited realm, like the wind passing over the spring fields, opening the great path of the Kami. Ah, Kannagara Kami-kami, may your Mitama be blessed.

Kirk and Thurman, who were employed as guards at the watermill, heard the voice of the Sendenka and listened for a while.

Kirk: 'Oi, Thurman, it seems that voice is the Sendenka of the Sango-kyō. Doesn't it make you feel uneasy? If a fellow like that were to come here, he might instantly see through the distress of the Prince in the basement, cast some sort of spiritual binding on us, and perhaps snatch away the precious Tama and leave. It would be fortunate if he just passed by on the road outside, but it looks like he is riding a horse and coming this way. We must think of something for this fellow.'

Thurman: 'Yeah, indeed, my body has started to tremble. It feels like the monk of Kintama-ji is about to go up to the capital.'

Kirk: 'The other side is also a Sendenshi. To drive away a Sendenshi, we must also imitate a Sendenshi. For it is the military strategy of the Kami no gun to oppose spirit with spirit, body with body, and power with power.'
