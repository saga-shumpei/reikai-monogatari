Chapter 3: Mountain 出女 [1727]

山出女〔1727〕

§ 1

Nothing in this world is as fortunate and yet as strong as a fool who is neither used by anyone's partner, nor moved by anything, and remains completely indifferent. Therein lies the infinite charm of the fool. A fool is almost a title for one whom humanity has endowed with invincible power. Naturally, he is a hopeless case who cannot be governed by petty, bustling, ordinary and general rules and measures. Throughout all ages, past and present, East and West, no medical doctor whatsoever, neither Jiba nor Henshaku, can do aught save throw up their hands in despair, sighing, 'Ah, there is no medicine to cure a fool...' Toyotomi Taikofu likewise declared that nothing is as terrifying as a fool and a dark night, seized by a sense of dread. No matter under how strict a regulation he may be placed, he is absolved of all infinite responsibility by the single phrase, 'Because he is a fool.' When he finally reaches the stage of an advanced simpleton, he receives the titles of idiot and lunatic, immune to any sanctions of crime or law. Furthermore, when his foolishness multiplies and people laugh, saying, 'What a preposterous fellow!' all and sundry human faults are publicly forgiven, and instead he is petted and fussed over as a charming person. Moreover, when one hoists foolishness as a banner and pushes recklessly through the floating world, there is scarcely a shadow of an enemy before one, and not a hair's breadth of worry is needed. Those fellows in the world whom common people call clever wits and praise as smart are almost always flying about the commonplace, slippery, and indefensible alleys of censure and praise. Like hungry demons fighting for food, they grasp at mere immediate outcomes, gains, and losses, and many end their lives in continuous nocturnal vexation and agony. Yet, in leisurely ease, this wondrous hiding place of humanity knows not the name of the fool. Voluntarily hurrying themselves to distraction, carving away their very selves like whittled wood, crying out day and night in bitter agony and bewilderment, they are a pitiful world indeed. All people ordinarily accumulate wisdom so that it may serve in an emergency. Those of great talent and great wisdom do not casually dole out petty wisdom and wit on the installment plan in normal times; when they have no use for it, they all hide behind the two characters for 'fool,' remaining as indifferent and calm as a deaf horse or a water frog. 'Ah, this fellow is astonishing. That fellow cannot be taken lightly,'—making the worldly ones utter such a phrase is precisely the moment when a fellow who has long concealed his instincts under the name of the fool finally appears. Expressions such as 'A terribly strong fellow,' 'Truly awfully great,' 'He's really going at it lately,' 'Isn't he terribly spirited?' 'He is terribly calm,' 'It sells terribly well,' 'It's terribly delicious,' 'It's terribly pretty,' and 'He is not to be made light of' are all expressions of astonishment when an ordinarily smart fellow has had the hair plucked from his nose by a person of great talent. Sayings like 'He is so foolish that one cannot even make an opponent of him' represent the times when a person of great wisdom is most deeply lurking in the depths of foolishness.

Hanna and Tanya were likewise articles that did not miss being selected as fools. However, while these two chirp out philosophies with their mouths, spout theories of love, and occasionally babble on about politics, none of it springs from innate wisdom. Because it is borrowed knowledge—acquired by beating down the price of dusty old books exposed at night stalls during temple festivals to two or three mon and reading them ravenously—they are truly thoroughgoing fools. They do not know how to cleverly navigate the world hidden under the name of the fool, and from their own foolishness they flatter themselves: 'There is no one as wise as myself, no scholar like me, the new figure of modern times is I. Even if we are companions of thieves, my heart is never warped. And it has been stolen by no one. By birth I am a man of talent, a wise man. Even if it were any sort of person whatsoever, if I were to squeeze out my intellectual resources and confront them with all manner of varied stratagems, everything would be accomplished with ease.' Sometimes growing strong, sometimes growing weak, and seeing their advance and retreat to be irregular, they flatter themselves: 'I am a tactician, a military strategist well-versed in the stratagems of worldly life.' Even when they fail, they think: 'This is some turn of circumstances. By the way of Kami, the gods have made it thus. Surely after a bad turn comes a good one, and after a good turn comes a bad one. Failure is the mother of success. Wise men and sages play the underling to ordinary men, while fools conquer the world. That is why even I, a wise person, must pretend to be a fool. No matter how foolish a thing it may be, under the name of a fool it will be settled as cleanly as washing one's bottom in a running river...' They are fellows who put their own foolishness on the shelf and imagine they are cleverly navigating the world by pretending to be fools, which makes them quite impossible. Such a fellow is truly a hopeless, unplaceable, utter fool.

ハンナ、タンヤの両人は又馬鹿者の選に洩れない代物であつた。併し乍ら此二人は口には哲学を囀り、恋愛論をまくし立て、たまには政治論も喋々するが、何れも天性の智慧から出たのではなく、縁日の夜立店に埃まびれになつて、曝されてゐる古本を二銭か三銭で値切り倒して買つて来て読みあさつた付け知恵なのだから、真の徹底した馬鹿者である。馬鹿の名に隠れて、巧く世を渡ることは知らず、自分の馬鹿から、「自分程智者はない、学者はない、現代の新人物は俺だ、泥坊の、仮令仲間と雖も、決して自分の心は曲つてはゐない。そして誰にも盗まれてはゐない。生れつき、自分は才子だ、智者だ。仮令如何なる人物と雖も、自分の智嚢を絞り出して、千変万化の手術を尽し立向つたならば、一切万事易々として成就するものだ」と自惚れてゐる。時々強くなつてみたり、弱くなつてみたり、進退動作常ならざるを見て、「自分は処世上の兵法をよく心得た策士だ。軍師だ」と自惚れ、失敗をしても「之れは何かの都合だ。惟神的に神が斯うさせたのだ。キツと悪い後は善い。善い後は悪いものだ。失敗は成功の母だ。賢人智者は凡人の下ばたらきをなし、愚者は天下をとる者だ。さうだから自分は仮令賢者でも愚者を装つてをらねばならぬのだ。どんな愚者々々した事でも、馬鹿の名の下には、流れ川で尻を洗つた如く解決がつくものだ……」などと自分の馬鹿を棚へ上げ、自ら馬鹿を装うて世を巧く渡つてゐるやうな心持でゐる奴だからたまらない。此奴こそ本当に箸にも棒にもかからない、捨場所のない真馬鹿者である。

§ 2

Hanna and Tanya, the two of them, without even dreaming that Samori's son Arina was pursuing them, climbed up the mountain path lightly and swiftly like flying birds with accustomed feet, and at last, following along the mountain stream, they arrived at Shakanna's hideout in Asakuradani; Shakanna was eating a slightly late breakfast together with Princess Subaru.

Hanna: 'Hey, Oyakata, please excuse me. I haven't seen you for a long time. The truth is, when Princess Dariya, the wife of Genshinbau, fled under the cover of night, we, in accordance with your gomeirei, ran about the sanya searching for her arika, but in the end we caught neither one nor two; having no other choice, when we returned to the gankutsu of Mount Taniguku, how could such a thing be, anihaka, anihaka, the tatemono had been burned down, and the figures of Oyabunsama and Himesama were not to be seen; we thought to ourselves, what if they were even burned to death in a sudden kuwaji, and if such were the case, we must at least gather their bones and perform a teichiyō funeral rite for them, so we did itsushiyōkenmei rakings of the ashes, but nothing resembling bones was to be found, leaving only the bones of boars and badgers. Ah, thinking that Oyabunsama must have been startled by the kuwaji and somehow made his escape to a temporary refuge somewhere, we waited patiently as chikonan for some ten days without eating or drinking, but not even a piece of news came like a stone thrown into a river, and with no other alternative, we set out with the resolution to disband. However, when it came to the crucial moment, to abandon Oyabunsama in this deep mountain and walk away was something we, as foster children, could not endure in our hearts, and Tanya and I embraced each other and wept. Truly, the bond between an oyabun and his foster children is something quite exceptional, waah waah waah!'

Shakanna: 'Wah hah hah hah, without those fussy and troublesome old men around, you fellows must have really stretched out the wrinkles of your testicles. For me too, the troublesome fellows have been cleared away, and feeling light-bodied, I felt as if a boil that had throbbed continuously for a hundred days and nights had suddenly scattered without a trace. Now that I have become a hermit of the world in this manner, I no longer wish to engage in the business of a thief. You too, at a reasonable point, should wash your feet and take up an honest occupation.'

Hanna, scratching his head while speaking,

Hanna: 'Eh, those are words I would never expect from you, Oyabunsama; do you trust me that little? I love you, Oyabunsama, from the bottom of my heart. Isn't that right, Tanya? You always listen to my words, don't you? There hasn't been a day I haven't called out your name, Oyabunsama, dozens of times.'

Tanya: 'Un, that's right; just as you say, just as I hear it. No matter what anyone says, since my heart is honest, I never even think of telling a lie before Oyabunsama, nor could I ever tell one. Now, Oyabunsama, please believe Hanna's and my heart.'

Shakanna: 'Un, I know well whether the depths of your heart are empty or false, good or bad. You shouldn't have any business with me; you must have business with Princess Subaru, wouldn't you? Regarding that, this Shakanna must be a tremendous hindrance. I am deeply sorry for causing you trouble, atsuko hah hah hah!'

Hanna: 'Now, Oyabunsama, isn't that unreasonable of you? Himesama is still in the body of a young maiden; there is no such thing as romantic love nor carnal passion. Moreover, Himesama is someone whom we have raised since she was small—nay, someone whom we were permitted to take care of—so we harbor no particularly deep debt of gratitude toward her, but since we have been cared for by you, Oyabunsama, for a long time, we will never forget your kindness. The young lady has bestowed no grace upon us whatsoever. Much less do we harbor any feelings of romantic love in the least, so please set your mind at ease.'

Shakanna: 'You say with your mouth that you are indebted to your oyabun, but in the depths of your hearts, you are thinking: "We have taken care of the oyabun for a long time. The oyabun didn't even go outside, but only made his foster children work, licking the fat of the foster children while the oyabun ate." In short, you must be coming here with the mindset of: "I am the savior and protector of the oyabun, and it is only natural that the oyabun should express his gratitude to me."'

Subāru sits back with an intrepid attitude, stopping her meal and looking down at the two faces with a smile.

Shakan na: 'Iwashase okeba, kyūshujin ni mukatte zōgon burei, yōsha wa shinai, kono tekken o kue'

Just as he tried to strike Hanna's side-face as if even his neck might fly off, Hanna crouched down and swept Shakan na's legs. Shakan na fell with a heavy thud onto the narrow garden path, struck the back of his head against a garden stone, and lost consciousness. The two swiftly bound Shakan na's hands and feet with rough rope, carried him to the valley river, and attempted a water burial. Seeing this, Princess Subāru, frantic for her father's life, became desperate, and struck the two from behind with an axe, shouting fiercely. The two nimbly dodged, leaped forward, snatched away the axe, pinned Princess Subāru down hard against the earth, and tied her hands and feet so she could not move. Princess Subāru shrieked and cried out at the top of her lungs. Alina, who had advanced by slipping through the woods to about one chō away just then, heard the daughter's cries, forgot herself, ran over, and seeing this state... 'Yaa, this fellow is the ruffian I saw this morning, I shall give him a punishment'... without saying a word, grabbed them by the collar and hair, and threw them both straight into the mountain stream of Asakura Mountain with a splash, undoing the bonds of both persons. Princess Subāru pressed her gentle, maple-leaf-like hands together and expressed gratitude for the great grace of saving her life. Her father Shakan na was in a daze, almost unconscious. Alina and Princess Subāru prayed wholeheartedly to the gods, blew water onto his face, and at long last, Shakan na's mental state began to clear up.

Shakan na: 'Aa musume, sobata wa buji de atta ka. Maa ketsukō ketsukō, kore mo mattaku ten no tasuke da'

Subāru: 'Otōsama, watashi mo tsubarete imashita no. Abunai tokoro e, ato no Tsuki-no-Mikoto-sama no otomo o shite odeni natta Alina-sama ga arawarete, watashi ya anata o tasukete kudasatta no desu yo. Sā orei o mōshite kudasai'

Awakening to the voice of Princess Subāru and looking closely, Shakan na saw Alina crouching respectfully on the ground.

Shakan na: 'Aa sobata wa Alina-sama, yoku maa tasukete kudasaimashita. Anata wa wareware oyako ga saisei no onjin desu. Sā, dōzo uchi e ohairi kudasaimase'

スバールは食事の手を止め、二人の面を微笑を泛べ乍ら打眺め、大胆不敵な態度でおさまり返つてゐる。

シャ『云はしておけば、旧主人に向つて雑言無礼、容赦は致さぬ、此鉄拳を喰へ』

と首も飛べよと許り、ハンナの横面をなぐりつけむとする一刹那、ハンナは身をすくめてシャカンナの足を掬つた。シャカンナは狭い庭にドツと倒れ、庭の石に後頭部を打つけ気が遠くなつて了つた。二人は手早くシャカンナを荒縄を以て手足を縛り、谷川に持運んで水葬せむとする。之を見るよりスバール姫は父の大事と、死物狂になり、鉞を以て二人の背後よりウンと許り擲りつけた。二人は目早く体をかはし、跳りかかつて、鉞を奪ひとり、スバール姫を大地にグツと捻伏せ、手足を括つて動かせず。スバール姫は悲鳴を上げて、声を限りに泣き叫ぶ。此時一町許り手前迄、林を潜つて進んで来たアリナは、娘の悲鳴を聞き、吾身を忘れて、走り来り見れば此態である。……ヤア此奴は今朝見た曲者、懲らしめくれむ……と、物をもいはず、襟髪を掴んで浅倉山の溪流へ、二人共ザンブと許り投げ込んで了ひ、両人の縄目を解いた。スバール姫は紅葉のやうな優しき手を合はして、救命の大恩を感謝した。父のシャカンナは精神朦朧として殆ど人事不省の態である。アリナとスバール姫は一生懸命神に祈願を奉り、水を面部に吹きかけなどして、漸くの事で、シャカンナの精神状態は明瞭になつて来た。

シャ『あゝ娘、其方は無事であつたか。まあ結構々々、之も全く天のお助けだ』

ス『お父様、私も縛られてゐましたの。危い所へ、あとの月太子様のお伴をしてお出になつたアリナ様が現はれて、私や貴方を助けて下さつたのですよ。サアお礼を申して下さい』

シャカンナはスバール姫の声に目をさまして、よくよく見れば、アリナは恭しげに大地にしやがむでゐる。

シャ『あゝ其方はアリナさま、よくマア助けて下さいました。貴方は吾々父娘が再生の恩人です。サア、どうぞうちへお這入り下さいませ』

A: 'It was a perilous place, but above all, since you have recovered your wits, it is truly a chiyōjiyō. That being the case, allow us to rest.'

Saying this, he helped Shakanna to his feet, and together with Princess Subaru, they took the old man by the hand and proceeded into the dwelling.

Shakanna: 'Arina-sama, thank you truly. And are there no changes in Taishisama?'

A: 'Yes, thank you. He is quite in good gosauken. Accordingly, since I am one who has come as a messenger from Taishisama, please listen calmly and at your leisure to the purpose of the mission once your mind is at ease.'

Shakanna: 'Ah, my kibun has already become well. If it is a message from Taishisama, not even a hantoki of iuyo can be allowed; please convey that intention to me. If it is a matter within my power, we, father and daughter, will render our gohōkō to the utmost limits of our strength.'

A: 'Ah, thank you for your satsusoku goshiyōin. To summarize, Taishisama has met you, father and daughter, for the first time, and though you are aged, he admires the spirit of your kokoro, Shakanna-sama; next, he thinks of the peerless beauty Subaru-sama as one who would not bring shame even if taken in as an ōhi. Today's occasion may seem a little early in its jiki, but even so, Taishisama harbors an extraordinary gorenbo, a momentum so fierce that arrows and shields cannot stop it, and a desire to see Subaru-sama's face even a moment sooner—his ōboshimeshi. We, his retainers, perceiving his painful distress, reached a point where we could no longer just stand and watch, and so, hiding from the public eye, we have come to visit this mansion.'

Shakanna: 'When I wondered what manner of pronouncement it could be, it turns out to be a desire for Princess Subaru; if my daughter has no objection, I shall follow your command. Nevertheless, it is still not the jiki for me to go out to the capital. No matter how one puts it, with my outdated, old-fashioned head, should I handle the burdens of government, it would rather be something that causes Taishisama worry. Therefore, I wish to humbly decline that matter alone. Fortunately, hiding in this mountain recess and repeating misfortunes, yet not hanging my neck from a mountain tree, not casting my body to the bottom of a river, not committing a teppōbara, and having the experience of somehow living on safely to konnichi, I ask you not to let my matters burden your mind. In a place where a useless person like me goes up to the capital, it is nothing more than a nuisance to Taishisama, the same as keeping a stray beast and letting it die in captivity. How could wareware, who have had weak contact with today's society, stand up in a bustling world and conduct politics? My thatched hut, in mere form, is old, small, and foul, but for a solitary person after sending away a daughter, I do not feel it is overly narrow. I earnestly beg you to refuse this matter alone.'

A: 'Ah, the truth of the matter is that we still do not have the permission of the father king, and it is solely Taishisama's own thought. Therefore, if it is all the same, it would be more convenient for both sides if you, anata, were to stay here for another year or two and wait for the proper season. And since I will secretly escort your young lady and enclose her in the mansion of the tea master Tartin to protect the safety of her honorable person, please do not worry, and will you not entrust the young lady to me?'

Shakanna: 'Hey, Subaru, you must have heard the talk from the very beginning. Do you not have the desire to go up to the capital, accompanied by Arina-sama?'

Subaru: 'Yes, it would not do for me to go while leaving only my father behind in this mountain recess. If possible, I desire to accompany you together with my father.'

Subaru: 'Ha ha ha ha, did you not also say that filial piety toward a father and romantic love toward a husband are entirely separate problems? Princess Subaru, who is the very main object of the theory of the sacredness of romantic love, by no means do you need any reserve or hesitation toward your father. It would be best to go before Taishisama, whom you love so dearly, even a moment sooner. However, even when you meet Taishisama, you must not act selfishly, you see.'

Subaru: 'Yes, father, thank you. That being the case, I will go up to the capital. Please live your life in fine spirits. And I shall come to welcome you back even a moment sooner. And I am living looking forward to seeing your face early.'

Feeling both joyful and sorrowful, she was filled with a mind akin to one whose parent had died on the same day they acquired a new bridegroom. From the day following this, the figure of Subaru, the famous flower of Asakuradani, was no longer to be seen.
