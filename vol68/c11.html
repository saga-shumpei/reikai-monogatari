Chapter 11: Palace Mountain Storm [1735]

宮山嵐〔1735〕

§ 1

At a distance of some three thousand meters directly south from the Tarahan Castle, there stands an independent, slightly elevated mountain where a thousand-year-old rauboku grows thick and lush, creating a fine fūkei. The kunibito praise this as the Great Ōmiyayama of Tarahan. A deep pond circles the periphery of the mountain, brimming with a hekitan. On this mountain, Bankoshinō, the soshin of Ural Kyō, built the miyabashira futoshiki, and since the kaibiyaku of old, has been chinsai there. Regarded with the deepest sonkei by the ōke as their ujigami, the shimin have treated this place as their sole iuenchi, much like a kōen. The yōka moon displays an oval daenkei, shining high in the sky over Miyayama. The crying voices of countless hototogisu carry an indescribable refined elegance, and bunjin bokukyaku draw their canes there night after night in an endless stream. However, since the great fire of the city of Tarahan broke out, there were no leisurely persons inclined to go listen to hototogisu, and even if there were, none dared to step foot there, in dread of public eyes. Alina, the son of the left governor Gandī, avoiding the search party of the regulatory office, concealed himself within the old shrine dedicated to Bankoshinō, awaiting the arrival of the hour. Handa and Beltsu, renowned as members of the popular relief corps, avoiding the sharp vanguard of the inspectors, sat down halfway up the stairs of the shrine and whispered to each other.

Handa: 'Hey, Beltsu, didn't we do a regrettable thing? If the inspectors' mobilization had been just a little slower, we could have finished off the right governor's mansion too, didn't we do an irretrievable thing? Because we botched it at that critical moment, those inspector fellows have sent spies in all directions, and it is so dangerous that we cannot make a single move these days. Unless we somehow dispatch that fellow, we will surely never attain our objectives.'

Beltsu: 'Now that things have come to this, it does no good to grumble like counting the years of a dead child, does it? Well, well, just wait for the right time. However, what I regret is that we let the left governor Gandī escape. That night, that fellow was at the villa in Tamanohara, so he saved his life from being a botched death. It would have been good if we had checked his whereabouts a bit more before striking, though.'

Handa: 'Oh, such a senile old man—even if we leave him alone, he probably won't have three years left to live. It is better to destroy an enemy without lifting a hand; if left alone, he is a creature that will naturally die.'

Beltsu: 'Won't I too die when I grow old? In a world where this many nationals are suffering, if we keep such a fellow alive for even a single day, it is a loss to the nation for that single day; if he dies one day sooner, at least a thousand people will be saved. If he stays in this world for ten days, it is calculated that ten thousand people will starve to death. That is why I insisted that "not a single moment can be delayed," but generally speaking, because your way of doing things was so dilatory, you ended up doing something like poking a hornet's nest, and it has turned into a hopelessly inescapable situation, hasn't it? The supreme leader, Madame Balance, has been hauled off to the regulatory office—first of all, though our plan went awry and we barely retrieved the situation, since what followed is that seven or eight spies are tailing us, no matter how much of a heroic genius supreme leader Balance is, she has no way to make a move, doesn't she?'

Handa: 'Well, don't be in such a hurry. If the supreme leader is kept tailed like that, those fellows at the regulatory office are utterly dim-witted, so they are bound to gradually ease their watchful eyes with a sense of relief. At that time, replacing supreme leader Balance, you and I will incite the domestic corps and carry out a coup d'état under a foolproof plan. When spies are lurking and we are under pressure as we are today, no matter how peerless in strategic genius I may be, there is nothing I can get my hands on. Well, well, just wait for the season.'

Beru: 'It is a zannen that we let Samori and Umori slip away, but as for that fellow Arina, the son of Samori, although he is acting like that for now, in jitsusai he is an ally of warera. Even if we raise an affair next kondo, we surely must not fail to save that kiyatsu alone.'

Hē: 'Un, that may well be so. Lately, he has been hated by the ōmetsuke and doko managed to escape somewhere, so they say. There is a proverb that a kite gives birth to a hawk, but truly that fellow called Arina may be a tanomo jinbutsu for wareware. Since some time ago, I have been having four or five buka take turns snooping around the mansion of Sakurensu, but ever since that daikuwasai the security has become strict, and the gururi of the mansion is fortified with sūjifunin of metsuke. When he goes out, he has samurai carry tetsupau to tojiyō, which is such a genjiu mekubari display, so for a while now there is no shikata other than to leave that kiyatsu's inochi spared for the time being wa.'

Beru: 'They say that Samori's son Arina has escaped to doko, but if one hears the rumors, is it not also said that Myōhō-sama's whereabouts are unknown as well? That Crown Prince seems to possess quite new ideas. Considering that he used that Arina as his sole favorite retainer, he cannot be a mofunbunkAN like King Karapin. If only Myhō-sama would appear in the world and conduct splendid governance, we would gladly follow Him no matter doko. The only things that are hontau hateful are Umori, Samori, and the other chief retainers who defile the proximity of the throne. And the very thing I find most unpalatable are the great and lesser daimyos and the wealthy fellows. Since the voices of the minshū do not reach the deaf ears of those fellows, they are rather pitiful objects. They are objects sleeping serenely right on top of where landmines are concealed.'

Hē: 'Oi, Berutsu, does it not look like there is a shadow climbing up the stairs?'

Beru: 'Indeed, that lantern bears the insignia of the Samori house. That fellow Samori has come bringing a crowd of guard samurai. It looks like they have come to arrest us. Oi, negligence is the great enemy, run, run.'

So saying, the two men climbed the stairs, went around behind the main shrine, and frantically running through the forest where the undergrowth flourished thickly, tumbling and falling, getting caught in briars, scratching their faces and hands while creeping through the forest, they crossed the single-bridge at the southern foot of Miyayama and frantically fled toward the direction of Namiyama. Samori's Gandhi, leaning heavily on a thick staff, finally managed to climb the stairs, made twenty guards surround him on all four sides, sat down before the shrine, and quietly beginning to offer prayers with the sound of clapping hands.

Bangu Shinō Shiōnaga-hiko no Mikoto who eternally reigns by driving the sacred pillars deep into the upper bedrock of awe-inspiring Ōmiyayama, before Your great presence—I, Gandhi, the official of Samori who serves as a pillar-stone of the Tarahan Castle, respectfully, reverently, in awe and trembling, pray to You. Due to what malediction of a warped deity, King Karapin has fallen into an unexpected grave illness, and the extent of His life cannot be measured. Owing to Your divine grace, all the great and lesser daimyos sank into sorrow thinking that it was almost His end, but lately He has gradually come to be in a slightly better condition. Yet no matter what is said, He is of advanced age, and I fear that at this rate even His ordinary lifespan will be difficult. Now in the Tarahan country, riots are breaking out in various places and the emergency of the nation is approaching before our very eyes; if by any chance His Excellency King Karapin were to pass away, our grief as daimyos and as subjects beyond measure cannot be known. We humbly and earnestly pray that through the divine virtue of the Great Gods, His Majesty the Great King's illness may be completely healed as swiftly as even a single day. Next, concerning Crown Prince Myhō-sama, ever since the day of the fire the other day, He has concealed His traces and to this day His whereabouts remain unclear; the daimyos gather day and night within the castle to hold various consultations, and have dispatched metsuke-ren in all directions to diligently search for Him, but to this day there is no word whatsoever. We earnestly pray that swiftly, within even a single day, the whereabouts of the Crown Prince may be known so that we may welcome Him back into the castle. Should it happen by misfortune that His Majesty the Great King passes away, to not know the whereabouts of the Crown Prince—who must immediately inherit the imperial throne—makes it utterly impossible to govern this disturbed nation. We respectfully pray for the divine protection of the Great Gods so that the Crown Prince may remain safe and return to the castle at the earliest possible moment. Furthermore, as for the one called my son Arina, since the night of the fire on the fifth, he has been missing, so I humbly pray that he too may return safely; and since he seduced the Crown Prince and gave Him various undesirable wisdom, unless we capture him at the earliest possible moment and punish him severely before the eyes of the populace, this country will never be governed. Bangu Shinō-sama, we humbly pray that You will grant the prayers of this old retainer, dedicating our sincere hearts for the sake of the Royal House and for the sake of the nation.'

Alina hid herself within the shrine, listening completely to her father Gandhi's Kiguwan to the very end,

'Oh, this is a Taihen. Even the old man has joined the conspiracy to hunt me down and kill me before the Minshiu. Very well, if his Onigokoro is to kill his only child, then this side too is ready. "If a father is not a father, a child need not be a child" is the golden saying of the Seijiya; very well, I shall use a divine Kohairo to crush the old man's Kimodama,'

she muttered to herself, letting out a roaring voice as if the Shiyaden itself would burst, tightening her breath in her Saikatanden,

and began to roar, 'Oo, oo.' Gandhi, the Samori, and all the Morizamurahi were terrified by the roaring sound from within the Dennai, and trembling in their bodies, squatted down upon the Daichi.

Alina: 'I am Tenshinbau, the first Kenzoku of Bankoshinō Shihonagahikonōokami, who is consecrated and worshipped upon Ōmiyayama. You, called Gandhi, you are the principal culprit of monstrous wickedness who, with extreme Futodoki, instigated Princess Mondoru ten years ago to carry out Akugiyakubudau, drove even the Seishin of King Karapin mad, chased Shakanan, the peerless loyal retainer and Samori, out of the castle, took his place to become Samori, tormented the people, and disturbed the nation. The great commotion in the castle town on the fifth of this month, if you trace its origin, is also because of you. Why do you not realize your responsibility and commit suicide, and apologize for your crimes to the royal family and the people, you fool of extreme Futodoki? Is it that you grudge cutting open that wrinkled belly, or is it that you grudge your life? Are you hesitating to cut because you fear the pain? What a spineless coward you truly are, ah.'

Gandhi, letting out a trembling voice,

'Oh, I am already deeply struck with awe. My remaining life, short as it is, I would never grudge; but if I close my eyes at this juncture, the Tarahan country will perish in the twinkling of an eye, the royal family will fall, and finally the red flag will come to be planted upon the castle towers. Thinking of this, my precious life, for the sake of the nation, I cannot die.'

Alina: 'For you to exist in this world for even a single day is the nation's loss for a day. What hastens the nation's ruin is the fact that you are alive. If you truly have a sincere heart to save the nation and the people, you should commit suicide as soon as possible, or if you think that is too painful, return all your high offices to the royal family, dedicate your remaining unburned villa to the nation to serve as a recreation ground for the Minshiu, and as atonement for your sins, become a beggar and wander throughout the realm, newly investigating the living conditions of the lower multitudes of people. How is that, does that make sense to you?'

Gandhi: 'Ha, ha, yes, I understand it thus. However, even if I become a beggar, if it is for the sake of the nation I do not mind, but please save that boy of mine in my place. And I ask for your divine protection so that he may succeed to the house of the Samori, however humbly.'

Alina: 'Hear this, hear this, dotard, have you panicked, have you lost your mind? Did you not just say, "Unless we capture my son Alina as quickly as possible and subject him to severe punishment before the eyes of the Minshiu, we cannot govern the people's minds"? You have come before the god and are using hypocrisy with your mouth and your heart, a fellow of extreme Futodoki. Wait, presently the god will mete out punishment by his own hand, oo.'

With a loud voice that seemed to split the Shiyaden, she roared. The Morizamurahi abandoned Gandhi, rushed down the steps each striving to be first, threw away their weapons, and fled for their dear lives. Gandhi too, unable to endure the fear and loneliness, tumbled down the one hundred and twenty steps like a ball, sustained injuries in several places, and with a breathless body, returned toward the villa of Tamanohara, relying on his staff for strength. Alina, seeing her father Gandhi and the others fleeing back, at last stroked her chest in relief, descended Miyayama southward, crossed a perilous single-plank bridge, and regardless of mountain or river, whipping her knees like a traveler's horse on the moonlit night road, fearing even the ears of pampas grass, without a moment of peace in her heart, fled toward the southwestern sky aiming for her destination.

Hearing this dialogue between father and son, The divine deity will surely smile. Though knowing full well that the sincere heart Belongs to Alina, her son, the old man complains before the gods. At times hating his own child, at times Deeming it dear—such is a parent's heart. The crooked deity of Tarahan's castle, terrified Of the false deity of Ōmiya, has fled back. The Morizamurahi forgot their proper duty, Abandoning their master and fleeing in cowardice.

『ウーウー』

と唸り出した。左守のガンヂーを初め守侍共は殿内の唸り声に肝を潰し、体を慄はせ乍ら大地に蹲まつて仕舞つた。

アリナ『此方は大宮山に斎き祭れる盤古神王塩長彦大神の一の眷族天真坊で厶るぞよ。汝ガンヂーとやら、其方は不届至極にも十年の昔モンドル姫を唆かし悪逆無道を敢行せしめ、カラピン王の精神迄も狂はせ、無二の忠臣左守のシャカンナを城内より追ひ出し、己取つて代つて左守となり、国民を苦しめ、国家を乱せし悪逆無道の張本人だ。去る五日の城下の大騒動も元を糺せば汝がため。なぜ責任を悟つて自殺を遂げ、王家及び国民に其罪を謝さないのか、不届至極の痴漢奴。其皺腹を掻き切る位が惜しいのか、否命が惜しいのか。痛さに怯えてよう切らないのか。てもさてもいい腰抜野郎だなア』

ガンヂーは慄ひ声を出し乍ら、

『いやもう恐れ入つて厶います。老先短かき吾命、決して惜しみは致しませぬが、今此際私が目を眠りますればタラハン国は瞬く間に滅亡致し、王家は滅び、遂に赤色旗が城頭に立てらるる様に成るで御座りませう。是を思へば大切な私の命、国家の為を思へば死ぬ事は出来ませぬ』

ア『其方が此世にある事一日なれば一日国家の損害だ。国家の滅亡を早めるのは其方が生て居るからだ。真に国家国民を救はむとする赤心あらば、一時も早く自殺を致すか、それがつらいと思はば一切の重職を王家に返上し、焼け残つた別荘も国家に献じ民衆の娯楽場と為し、其方は罪亡ぼしの為め乞食となつて天下を流浪致し、下万民の生活状態を新しく調べて見るがよからう。どうだ合点が行つたか』

ガン『ハハ、ハイ、左様心得まして厶います。併し乍ら私は乞食になつても国家の為めなら厭いませぬが、あの悴奴を代りに助けて下さいませ。さうして細々乍らも左守の家を継ぎますやう、御守護を願ひ奉ります』

ア『これやこれや老耄、汝は狼狽たか、血迷うたか。「悴のアリナを一時も早く捕縛し、民衆の面前にて重き刑に処せなくては民心を治める事が出来ない」と唯今申したではないか。汝は神の前に来つて口と心の裏表を使ふ不届至極の奴だ。待て、今に神が手づから成敗を致してくれむ、ウー』

と社殿も割る許りの大音声にて唸り立てた。守侍はガンヂーを捨てて吾先にと階段を下り、武器を捨て命辛々逃げて行く。ガンヂーも亦、怖さ淋しさに居耐まらず百二十段の階段を毬の如く転げ乍ら落ち下り、数ケ所に打ち創を負ひ、はふはふの体にて玉の原の別荘さして杖を力に帰り行く。アリナは父ガンヂー其他の逃げ帰りしを見て、やつと胸を撫で下し、宮山を南に下り危げな一本橋を渡つて山と云はず河と云はず、膝栗毛に鞭ちて月照る夜の途を、薄の穂にも怖乍ら、もしや追手に出遇ひはせぬかと安き心もなく西南の空を目当てに逃て行く。

父と子が内と外との掛合を 聞きて御神は笑ませたまはむ。 赤心は確かアリナの悴とは 知りつつ爺御前に訴ふ。 或時は吾子を憎み或時は いとしと思ふ親心かな。 タラハンの城の曲神も大宮の 佯り神に恐れて帰りぬ。 守侍は吾職掌を打ち忘れ 主をすてて帰る卑怯さ。
