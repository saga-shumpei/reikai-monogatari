Chapter 21: Festival 政一致 [1745]

祭政一致〔1745〕

§ 1

Taishi Sudaruman was sent by the Sendenshi and, together with his Itsukau, returned safely to the interior of the castle of Tarahan. He met with his father, the Daiō, apologized for his past Futsugafu, and further swore that he would henceforth reform his heart, succeed his father, and Sōran the Matsurigoto of the Kokuka Banki.

No sooner did King Karapin see the figure of the Taishi than his spirit relaxed from the excess of joy. In that Setsuna, he suddenly fell into a state of Jinjifusei, and after Shigonichi, he reached the end of his Hachijifuitsusai Ichigo and bade farewell to this world. The Taishi succeeded to the position of his Chichiō and was titled King Karapin the Dainisei. He spread Jinsei throughout the Tenka, abolished the Kubetsu between the upper and lower Kunitami, broke down the Kiushifu, selected Kenshiya from among the Kunitami, and placed them in their respective government duties. The common people were pleased and submissive, and he brought about a golden age of prosperity. Arina and Balance were thrown into prison for a time in accordance with the dictates of the national law, but as soon as the Taishi ascended to the throne, he granted a general amnesty. The two escaped their prison life, which had been a mere formality of only one week. Arina became the illustrious Migimorishi, bore the expectations of the Kunitami, and served in the heavy responsibility of assistance. He then took Balance, the giant woman who had been the leader of the people's relief corps, as his wife, and the house of Arina prospered for generations. Furthermore, with the ascension of Taishi Sudaruman, Balance felt that the people's relief corps was no longer necessary and issued an order of dissolution to all her subordinates. Gandī, the Hidarimorishi, followed after King Karapin and likewise returned to the spirit world as if falling asleep. Shakanna, the former Hidarimorishi who had been hiding in the deep mountains of Mt. Asakura, was summoned by the new King, entered the castle, and took up the post of Hidarimorishi as before. He poured his full energy into the reform of the national government and gained the great trust of the Kunitami in general. Princess Subāru, whom the Taishi loved most, ascended to the position of Queen. She reformed the systems within the palace herself, completely abolished previous Kiushifu and emotional methods of appointment, and gathered wise women to strive for the reform of the palace. Also, Tarchin, a tea-master living near the forest of Mukōhi, came to serve Princess Subāru for the rest of his life. Princess Banna, the princess who had been thrown into the Indes River by the wicked woman Shinobu, was saved by the subordinates of Balance, was sent back to the palace in a happy state, and was requested by the Taishi of the country of Toruman to become his consort.

The shrine of the God-King Pangu on Mt. Ōmiya was reconstructed under the direction of the Sendenshi, Umekōwake, to be several times more magnificent and grand than before. The shrine was divided into three buildings. In the center, they enshrined Lord Kuninotokotachi and Lord Toyokumono. In the left palace, they enshrined Lord Susanoo and Lord Ōyashikohiko, and in the right palace, they enshrined the God-King Pangu and the God of the Kokudama. It was decided that as the tutelary deity of the Karapin royal family, the King himself would forever serve as the chief priest. The funeral rites for King Karapin and the Hidarimorishi Gandī were attended by all, both high and low, and were called the most magnificent ceremony since the creation of the world. Subsequently, due to the shrine transfer ceremony of Mt. Ōmiya, as well as the coronation and wedding ceremonies of the Taishi, people from all over the country gathered in the city of Tarahan in an endless stream to express their congratulations. The city of Tarahan, which had unexpectedly met with a great fire, recovered in less than a year and achieved a prosperity several times greater than before. It is said that this was all due to the grace of the new King, who accepted the will of the people and practiced a government of equality and philanthropy, such that even the children admired his virtue, and not a single person uttered a complaint. The details of the coronation ceremony are omitted here, and only the celebratory songs are introduced.

Taishi: 'I shall serve the heart of the heavenly gods, / And rule the land by erecting the sacred pillars. / That the daily lives of the Kunitami may be peaceful, / Morning and evening, I shall pray to the gods. / The land of the gods, opened by our ancestors, / I shall guard with reverence and awe. / While opening the government of the new land, / I shall seek out the sages in the fields far and wide. / Beneath the base of the great shrine, the rafters stand high, / How noble is the god who dwells in peace. / From now on, the teachings of the three-five gods, / I shall teach and spread to my Kunitami.' Queen: 'Obeying the imperial decree of my lord, / I shall serve as the mother of the nation. / While revering the heavenly and earthly gods, / I shall rule over the people of my divine land. / Leading the various officials of treasures, / I shall assist the path of my great lord. / Meeting the dew of the gods' precious blessings, / Today I shine within the nine-fold palace. / That the people of the world may be at peace forever, / To pray is my own heart's desire.'

原文（近代文語）

スダルマン太子は宣伝使に送られ、一行と共に無事タラハン城内に立帰り、父の大王に面会し、今迄の不都合を謝し、且つ今後は心を改めて、父の後を継ぎ、国家万機の政事を総攬せむ事を誓つた。カラピン王は太子の姿を見るより、喜びの余り気が緩み、ガツカリとした其刹那、忽ち人事不省に陥り、四五日を経て八十一才を一期となし、此世に暇を告げた。太子は父王の位＠を継承しカラピン王第二世と称し、天下に仁政を布き、国民上下の区別を撤回し、旧習を打破し、国民の中より賢者を選んで、夫れ夫れの政務に就かしめ、下民悦服して皷腹撃壤の聖代を現出した。アリナ及びバランスは国法の命ずる所に従ひ、一時牢獄に投ぜられたが、太子が王位に即くと共に大赦を行ひ、両人は僅に一週間の形式許りの牢獄住居を遁れ、アリナは天晴右守司となつて国民上下の輿望を担ひ、輔弼の重任を尽し奉つた。そして民衆救護団長たりし大女のバランスを妻に迎へ、アリナの家は子孫代々繁栄した。又バランスはスダルマン太子の即位と共に民衆救護団の必要なきを感じ、部下一般に対して、解散の命を下した。左守司のガンヂーはカラピン王の後を逐うて、之亦眠るが如く帰幽した。浅倉山の山奥に隠れてゐた前左守司シャカンナは新王に召されて、城中に入り元の如く左守の職に就き、国政の改革に全力を傾注し、国民一般の大に信任を得た。太子の最も寵愛せしスバール姫は王妃の位＠に上り、殿内の制度を自ら改革し、従前の因習や情実的採用法を全廃し、賢女を集めて殿内の革正に努めた。又向日の森の辺に住む茶坊主のタルチンはスバール姫に終身仕ふる事となつた。毒婦シノブの為にインデス河に投込まれた王女のバンナ姫はバランスの部下に救はれ芽出度宮中に送り帰され、トルマン国の太子に懇望されて其妃となつた。大宮山の盤古神王の社は梅公別の宣伝使が指揮に従ひ、以前よりも数倍宏大にして且つ立派なる社殿を造営し、社を三棟となし、中央には大国常立尊、豊雲野尊を祭り、左側の宮には神素盞嗚尊、大八洲彦命を鎮祭し、右側の宮には盤古神王及国魂の神を鎮祭し、カラピン王家の産土神として永遠に王自ら斎主となり奉仕する事となつた。

カラピン大王や左守ガンヂーの葬祭式には上下挙つて会葬し、開闢以来の盛儀と称せられた。次いで大宮山の遷宮式並に太子の即位式や結婚式等にて、タラハン城市に全国より祝意を表して集まり来る者引も切らず、期せずして大火災に会ひしタラハン市は一年ならずして復興し、以前に優る事数倍の繁栄を来たした。何れも新王が民意を容れ、平等博愛の政治を布き給ひし恩恵として子供の端に至る迄其徳を慕ひ、不平を洩らす者は只一人もなかつたといふ。即位式の状況に付いては茲に省略し、祝歌のみを紹介する。

新王『久方の天津御神の御心を 麻柱まつり国を治めむ。 国民の日々の暮しの安かれと 朝な夕なに神に祈らむ。 親々の開き給ひし神の国を 謹み畏み守りまつらむ。 新しき国の政を開きつつ 野に在る聖広く求めむ。 大宮の下つ岩根に千木高く 鎮まりゐます神ぞ尊き。 三五の神の教を今よりは あが国民に教へひろめむ』

妃『吾君の勅のままに服ひて 御国の母と仕へまつらむ。 天つ神国津御神を斎ひつつ 吾神国の御民を治めむ。 諸々の珍の司を率ゐつつ 吾大君の道を助けむ。 有難き神の恵の露に会ひて 今日九重にわれは輝く。 世の中の御民よ永遠に安かれと 祈るはおのが願なりけり』

§ 2

Arina: 'The Ōgimi ascends far to the Mikuni, and dwelling in Ame, shall rule the Miyo. My father, following our Ōgimi, must be ascending to the Kami no Mikuni. The beautiful Misugata of the Empress, praised as the Meiguwa of the Ashibiki mountains. I am now appointed as Umorinokami, and serve before the Mimahe of our Ōgimi. Even if Ame should split, the earth crumble, or the seas rise, I shall never forget the Mimegu of the sovereign. For the sake of the Ōkami and our Ōgimi, I shall offer both my heart and my body.'

Shakanna: 'Having served our Ōgimi who departed from the Kami, I have emerged into the world once again. By the Mimegu of our new Ōgimi, my beloved daughter has become a person. The plum blossoms that began to scent the Yamaoku are now bearing fruit in Takama. Though there is praise for parent and child, I follow the sovereign who dwells as the Empress of the Ōgimi. Returning to the capital of Chin after ten years, how joyful it is to serve the sovereign. From now on, I shall steady the horse of my heart and soothe the hearts of the Mitami.'

Balance: 'Though I am one raised in the countryside, I dwell today in the skies of the Kokonohe. How joyful it is to join hands with my husband and assist in the government. The bitterness of having endured for three years in the mansion of Taruchin, and returning it as an enemy. I had raged to strike down all the bourgeoisie and the Kaikifu. Even the evil act of setting fire to the streets of the capital was a heart to save the Miyo.'

Taruchin: 'My own small body, lacking strength, has been moistened by the dew of Mimegu and revived. How grateful I am to take the hand of the Empress, and how joyful it is to teach the Chiyadau.'

Umekōwake: 'The noble light of the Imperial Kami appears, and today the foundations of the world are opened.'

At the sacred grounds of Ōmiyama, / Setting the great pillars of the palace, / I pray with awe before the Mimahe / Of the Ōkami who was enshrined. / Now, this Kami-no-kuni, / From the ancient days of the distant age of Kami, / Has taken the hearts of the people as its own heart, / And the rulers of the land have taken / The hearts of the Kami of Ame and Earth as their own heart, / Removing the barriers between high and low, / Appearing as the Nakatori-omi, / Becoming the kings of the land, / And soothing the grass-like people of the four quarters / With great peace and gentleness. / Even in this awe-inspiring Miyo, in the middle ages, / By the crooked paths that came pressing in, / The Kami-no-kuni was all defiled, / And became a world where demons raged. / The officials serving above, / With hearts set on the greed of fame and profit,

Became darkened and turned into ogres, / Not caring for the suffering of the people, / Flowing solely toward egoism, / The world withered day by day and month by month, / And the voices of resentment in the fields and mountains, / And in every corner of the capital's great roads, / Roared with terror. / Just at that time, the incarnation of the Imperial Kami, / Sutaruman, / The Prince, was quick to act, / And being well-versed in the conditions of the people, / He promoted the lord Arina, / And loved him as his own right-hand minister, / Combining their hearts and their strength / Into one, to save and assist / The suffering of the people, / He broke his heart to do so, but / The clouds of the Kokonohe, which were completely clouded over, / Were deep, and / The darkness of the eternal night, which had no means to clear,

The rampant evil had no way / To be brought under control, and for a time / He left the mansion, and in the mountains and fields, / Wandered, and was made to suffer / A thousand million sorrows, but / The time of Yang returning and the cycle beginning again arrived, / And now he has ascended the throne, / Executing reforms of all government, / Opening the country anew, / Just as a loving mother treats her infant, / He treats the myriad people, / And it has become an awe-inspiring Miyo. / Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara, / Is this the harbinger of the Miyo of 567? / If one looks up, it is high, the distant heavens, / Is it the Mimegu of the noble Kami? / The divine majesty that cannot be praised enough, / Look up to it, all you people. / From the King above, / To the ends of every office,

Respect the Kami, and adore the Ōgimi, / And reform the evil / In your hearts, and Kannagara, / Perform the duties / That fulfill the heart of the Kami, Kannagara. / In place of the Kami, I, Umekōwake, / Shall state one word in parting. / Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara, / May your spirits be blessed. / Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, / Whether the moon waxes or wanes, / Even if the great earth should sink, / Sincerity alone saves the world. / The Kami appears on the surface, / And separates good from evil. / The Kami Naohi who created this world, / And the great Naohi with a wide heart, / In all things in the world of men, / Look again and listen again with Naohi, / And rectify the errors of the world.

Perform the Chin festival forever, / O Ōgimi. / The evangelist of the San-go-kyō, / Umekōwake, respectfully / Proclaims and transmits the will of the Kami.'

The evangelist Umekōwake preached and admonished the new King and the assembled high officials on the teachings of the Kami, and once again straddling his white horse, he looked back at Tarahan Castle, and to join the troop of Kunidewake, with the sound of hooves sounding brave, he returned as if shooting an arrow. Ah, Kannagara Reikō Haisazase.
