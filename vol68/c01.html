Chapter 1: 貞操論 [1725]

貞操論〔1725〕

§ 1

The green of the trees also features the tender buds of Mount Asakurayama, unfolding like rolled paper, leaf by leaf, protruding oval tongues, and continuing their silent whispers as they sway in the late spring breeze.

Although it is said to be the end of spring, deep in the mountains of the high-altitude zone, the growth of tree buds is about a month behind compared to the capital roads.

In this deep mountain where there is nothing else to be heard than the roaring of tigers, wolves, lions, and bears, the hundred-fold twittering of small birds, and the sound of the spring breeze rubbing against the treetops, Shakanna, who once served as the Minister of the Left of the Talahan Kingdom in ancient times, though aged, has not seen his courage wane from the old days. Should the time arrive one day, like a dormant dragon emerging from its abyss to leap into the heavens, he sought to try his powerful two arms—which had wielded power for the sake of the realm and the nation in former days—upon the affairs of state, gathering hundreds of subordinates in the deep mountains where human footprints are rare to pour his body and soul into divine strategies for turning the tide. Yet, by a sudden turn of fate, knowing the mandate of Heaven with the passing years, he set fire to his mountain stronghold, disbanded his many subordinates, and awaited the time of the return of spring (ichiyō-raifuku), making his beloved only daughter, Princess Subaru, who had reached her fifteenth spring this year, the strength of his old age.

Just as if it had dropped unexpectedly from the heavens, he met Crown Prince Sudarman of the Talahan Kingdom—his political enemy—who, together with Arina, the son of the Minister of the Left, knocked on the tattered door of a thatched hut barely capable of withstanding rain and dew. Breaking the dream of a temporary slumber, they achieved a master-servant reunion for the first time in ten years. With this fate of extraordinary strangeness, he felt just like leaves withering and suffering from a great drought that encounter the blessing of rain and dew, reviving fresh and vivid, and day and night he clenched his fists, gazed at the sky over Talahan Castle, and swelled with hopes of revitalization and active resurgence.

The voices of fierce beasts, which used to feel lonely day by day, and the twitterings of all birds filled with sorrow, now somehow felt as if they were full of vital energy, urging him out of his reclusive dwelling. Both the sound of the mountain stream's murmur and the sound of the wind crossing the treetops felt as though they were filled with the voice of hope.

Princess Subaru, a natural beauty untainted by the dust of the capital's main thoroughfares where vulgar odors run rampant and a dark society filled with wickedness and monsters with human faces and beastly hearts walk arrogantly in broad daylight, preserves her innocent life deep in the mountains of Mount Asakurayama, along the upper reaches of Tamagawa River, beside a bank where clear waters flow rapidly and send off splashes of deep rapids, in a river abyss demarcated by a thousand mats of rock. In a lonely, pit-built thatched hut together with her father Shakanna—who could crush even demons—her jade-like skin wrapped in the moss of a thousand years, she is treated like an empress or a princess by the dregs of humanity and petty thieves who have fallen behind society until now. Spending the spring of her life is at least a matter of pride in Princess Subaru's heart. If she were to remain as she is and not venture out into the world, her destiny would leave her no other path: she might become the wife of a beast-odored mountain man, or the wife of a thief, ending her life as the chieftain of a female band of thieves; or perhaps, as a flower meant to be seen, she would scatter and perish by the power of time without bearing fruit, becoming fertilizer for tree roots; or otherwise, she would have her short-lived beauty's lifetime embraced through to the end by pitch-black hairy shins, spending a vain and fleeting existence. Her visage was far too gorgeous for a human being; as the proverb says, "the thin-fated beauty," she is a precious thing to be buried in this deep mountain, hated by the gods of marital bonds. Nevertheless, blessed by her father's authority and her own tender youth, she has not become a victim with tree roots for a pillow against the violence of vicious men. Up until today in her fifteenth spring, not having had her body violated nor her spirit toyed with, she has passed the springs and autumns—at least as far as that maiden is concerned, this is her good fortune. Raised by her father's hands since the spring of her six years, with the clear stream of Asakuradani and the water mirror of the valley's abyss where the waters are checked by rocks, she simply twists and binds her long, black hair that touches the earth haphazardly into curls, knowing not even the echo of the floating world's winds. If someone were to push this beauty right into the middle of the capital's main thoroughfare, both her arms and shins, like extended jades that could not be seen even by spending ten thousand pieces of gold, would be exposed without regret, to be toyed with by the withered leaf debris scattered by the Taniguku gale. Laying her natural jade-like skin, unadorned by any artifice, deep in this mountain, whenever she had leisure, she would venture into the mountain forests—the resting place of lions and rabbits—carrying firewood on her back, and diligently assist her father's toil in all matters of cooking. Artificial cosmetic items such as red and white face powders, perfumes, and oils had never been seen nor worn since her birth; she simply grew up according to Kami's will (nakanagara). To expose the adornment of flowers blooming fragrantly in the wilderness unknown to people in these deep mountains is precisely the true beauty crafted by the skill of creation. Wherever a jade is rolled, it is a jade; no matter how humble, musk naturally possesses the fragrance of musk; everywhere she possessed an indescribable, captivating charm. Just as beans in the shade open flowers and bear fruit when the season arrives, Princess Subaru, who welcomed the spring of her fifteenth year, ever since she came into contact with the dignified, manly, and captivating appearance and voice of Crown Prince Sudarman—who seemed equal to an angel sent down from the Heavenly Pole's Tsuchimikado (Tenkyoku Shibi-gū)—scattered autumn leaves on an unseasonable face, feeling like a single plum blossom encountering the vernal sun and beginning to unfold, and even in her childish heart, she came to be captured by the uncanny monster of love called yearning. On rainy mornings and windy evenings, the maiden joined her palms toward the clear stream of Asakuradani, and upon seeing the raging and wild state of the turbulent rapids, she revered it as the manly august heart of Crown Prince Sudarman; gazing upon the pure mountain stream, she worshipped it as a clear mirror of the Prince's august heart. The voices of small birds and the sound of the wind crossing the treetops felt like the sweet words of the Prince, and gazing at the green, crimson, white, red, and yellow flowers blooming fragrantly in the forest, she recalled the Prince's face. Against the moonlight rising, suppressing the starlight of the full heavens, she yearned for that round moon's face as precisely the pure, gentle august figure of the Prince and the lifeline of her life. Touching things, encountering matters, and among all manifestations of the universe, there was not a single thing that was not the voice of the Prince nor not the figure of the Prince, sinking deeply with intense force like the plunging waters of a waterfall into the dark of love, down to the depths of a thousand fathoms.

恰も天から降つて湧いたる如く思ひがけなきタラハン国のスダルマン太子が吾政敵なる左守の悴、アリナと共に雨露を凌ぐに堪ゆべき茅屋の破れ戸を叩くに会ひ、仮寝の夢を破られ十年振りにて主従の対面をなしたる数奇極まる運命に、大旱になやんで萎れかかりし木の葉の雨露の恵みに遭ひて、生々と復活したるが如き心地し、日夜腕を扼してタラハン城の空を眺めて再生活躍の希望を漲らしつつあつた。

日頃淋しく感ずる猛獣の声も、悲哀に満ちた百鳥の囀りも此頃は何となく生気溌溂として己が出盧を促すものの如く、谷川のせせらぎの音にも梢を亘る風の音にも、希望の声が満ちて居るやうに感じられた。

俗臭紛々として罪悪に満ちたる暗黒の社会、人面獣心の化物共が白昼を横行濶歩する都大路の塵にも染まぬ天成の美人スバール姫が、浅倉山の山奥、玉の川の上流、清く流れて激潭飛沫をとばす岸の片辺、千畳の岩石を以て区劃された川の淵、淋しげに立てられた、掘り込み建ちの茅屋に鬼をも挫ぐ父のシャカンナと共に、無心の生命を保ち、千歳の苔に玉の肌を包まれて、今まで社会に落伍した人間の屑小盗人共に、女帝の如く、王女の如くもてはやされ、人生の春を過ごすこそ、せめてものスバール姫が心の誇り、もし此ままにして世に出でずば獣臭き山男の妻となるか、泥棒の妻となつて女盗賊の頭目として一生を終るか、但は見るべき花として時の力に散り失せ実を結ばず、木の根の肥料となるか、さもなくば可惜美人の生涯を真黒の毛脛に抱き通され、果敢なき一生を送るより外に道なき姫の運命、人間としては余りに艶麗に過ぎたる其容貌、諺に云ふ美人の薄命、結縁の神に憎まれて此山中に葬らるべき可惜もの。さりながら、父の権威と吾身の年若きに過ぎたるを幸ひ、悪性男の暴力に木の根を枕の犠牲にもならず、今日十五の春まで身を犯されず、霊を弄ばれず、春秋を送り迎へして来たのは、せめてもの彼の少女にとつては幸である。六才の春より父親の手に育てられ浅倉谷の清流、岩にせかるる谷の淵瀬の水鏡はあり乍ら、地を撫でる如き長き頭の黒髪を無雑作にクルクルとまきつけて結び、浮世の風の響さへ知らず、もし人あつて此美人を都大路の真中につき出さうものなら、万金を費しても見られぬ筈の玉を伸べたる如き腕も脛も露に惜気もなくタニグク颪に散り来る木の葉の屑に弄ばれ、一片の人工も施さず、天成そのままの玉の肌を此山奥に横たへ、暇ある時は、獅子、兎の安息所たる山林に別け入つて、薪を背負ひ、炊事万端まめまめしく父の労苦を助けて居た。紅白粉香油等の補助的粧飾品は生れて以来、見た事もなく、身につけた事もない、只惟神のままに生立つた。原野に咲き匂ふ花の粧を此山奥に人知れずさらすのは、宛然造化の技巧をこらして作り上げた天真の美貌、どこへ転がしても玉は玉、如何に粗末でも蘭麝は蘭麝の香を備ふる道理、どこともなく云ふに云はれぬ床し気がある。蔭裏の豆も時節が来れば花を開き果を結ぶ道理、今年十五の春を迎へたスバール姫も天極紫微宮より降らせ給ひしエンゼルにも等しきスダルマン太子の、どこともなく雄々しき男らしき床しき容貌と其謦咳に接してより、時ならぬ顔に紅葉を散らし、梅花一輪春陽に遭うて綻び初めし心地、子供心にも恋てふものの怪しき魔物に捕捉さるるに至つた。雨の朝、風の夕、少女は浅倉谷の清流に向つて両手を合せ、激湍飛沫の猛り狂ふ有様を見てはスダルマン太子の雄々しき御心と崇め、清き溪流を眺めては太子の御心の清き鏡と拝し、小鳥の声、梢を亘る風の音も太子の甘き言葉の如く思はれ、林に咲き匂ふ緑、紅、白、赤、黄色の花を眺めては太子の御顔を偲び、満天の星光を圧して昇る月影に対しては、あの円満なる月の顔は正しく太子の清き、やさしき御姿、吾生命の綱と憧がれ、物に接し、事に触れ、森羅万象悉く、一として太子の声ならざるはなく、太子の姿ならざるはなき、深くも恋の暗に滝津瀬のおちくる如く強度の勢を以て千尋の深き底に沈み行くのであつた。

§ 2

Shakanna was troubled in mind that Subaruhime's recent yōsu was altogether unconvincing, and as a parent to his daughter, he revolved every possible shisaku in his mind, and at tokidoki even breathed a tameiki.

Shakanna: 'Subaruhime, you too are now a maiden who has greeted the spring of jifugo, and as this parent looks upon your condition, atara meigiyoku is not something I wish to bury here in the yamaoku. I once itsutan withdrew from the shiyokushiyō of the Office of Samori, drove away the kanjiyaneijin coiling near the kunsoku, and maintained the annei and chitsujiyo of the jiyōka of the land of Tarahan, thinking to wait for jisetsu for the sake of the ōke on one hand and for the sake of kokuka and banmin on the other, to wield the power of itsupi, and thus I gathered together sanzoku in this yamaoku, waiting for the jiki of kendojiurai. Waiting for almost ten years, yet though numerous subordinates gathered together, not a single one opened up the bottom of his heart, and no one came forth to become the power of one arm for the execution of the great enterprise. For that reason the father thoroughly grew weary of the world, and as you also know, setting fire to the mountain fortress of Taniguku Valley, while my subordinates who were following in the footsteps of Genshinbō were absent, in this hiding place of Asakura Valley, I had made up my mind to lead a lonely dwelling of two people with you and to send my tasteless remaining life, but heroic and blazing, I could not endure the lament of hiyiniku, and rather than that, as a memory of this world, riding alone into the castle of Tarahan on a single horse, to destroy the villains coiling near the kunsoku, to remove the disaster of the state, and I wondering whether I might commit suicide on the spot to apologize for my crime—how many times did I ponder back and forth, yet whether in heaven or on earth, with only a single parent and a single daughter, to leave you behind and go before you would be pitiful toward you, and for you to be pointed at behind your back by the people of the world as the daughter of a great villain is also painful to my heart, and therefore, unable to achieve manly deeds, hesitating and wavering, effeminately even unto this day, I have vainly squandered atara kōin. Nevertheless, whether by the grace of heaven or the salvation of earth, unexpectedly about this time last month, Prince Sudarman, as though dreaming, strayed into my thatched hut, and with his august body of kinshigyokuyō, spent a single night in this thatched hut, which must surely be some divine guidance. Calmly reflecting upon it, it may be thought of as a hint from the gods that this Shakanna should leave the mountain as soon as possible, go up to the capital, and save the crisis of the state. Concerning that, you are fortunately a beauty rare in the world, and if by any chance, favored by fortune, you should catch the heart of the Prince-sama, that indeed would be nothing more convenient for the father's great ambition nor for the state. However, since all things among men are swayed by fate, the ultimate point will surely not go according to human strength. And again, the relation between man and woman is truly a wondrous thing, and no matter how much the Prince-sama may lavish his favor upon you, if there is no heart in your heart to yearn for the Prince, there is no reason to forcefully compel marriage by the authority of a parent, yet observed from the father's eyes, somehow the Prince-sama felt as though he had thoughts toward you. However, since marriage is established by love, no matter how much you are called a young girl, or called my own daughter, this alone does not become subject to the father's freedom. What do you think of your own thoughts? There is no need for reserve or hesitation. We are a parent-child relationship that cannot be cut even if cut. And it is the great event of a lifetime for you. Pray, let this father hear your heart beforehand. After hearing your heart, this father too has matters to plot.'

§ 3

Subaruhime was unbefitting a maiden, being reiri in seishitsu; though raised deep in the mountains, she was relatively versed in the kibi of ninjiyō. From around the age of jifunisansai, she felt a shiyumi in the matter called renai, inspected the menbau of sūhiyaku of buka one by one, paid attention to kahokatachi, seishitsu, tachii, furumahi, and the like, and possessed a sort of hihiyōgan toward danshi. Nevertheless, whichever man she looked at, they were all geretsu in shinsei and yōbau in yōbau, yamazaruteki ningen, and among them not a single ball worthy of being chosen as a husband to whom Subaru would entrust itsugiyō could be found. Having left Tarahan Castle at the age of six to be raised by her father's hand deep in these mountains, gazing solely upon grotesque menbau and dwarf-like companions of rough men, she...

Would all men in the world be such beast-like things? No matter which ningen one looked at, their left and right eyes were mismatched or placed one above the other, the bridge of the nose bent to the right or to the left; looking all the way from the shape of the mouth to the condition of teeth growth, tachii, and furumahi, ... truly, danshi were pitiful things. Why on earth did such monsters dwell in this world? Ah, what a pitiful floating world it was ... she had always sunk into the abyss of discouragement and disappointment, but by chance she had encountered the Crown Prince of Tarahan Castle, yearning for his lofty figure, and even the yōbau of Arina, the son of Samori, had points not easily dismissed. Thinking of this, ... it could not be just a pack of scrap men she had gazed upon for the past ten years; in the world, one in a hundred or two might even be men with ningen-like faces. When the Crown Prince was leaving, the joy she felt when he firmly grasped her hand and whispered by her ear, 'Now, Subaru, I will surely come to fetch you.' However, how could there be reason for a worldly-ignorant concubine raised in such deep mountains to be eternally favored? Both in terms of status and yōbau, as well as fame, being a rare young lord, there must be many splendid women along the capital's great thoroughfares. And relying upon the Crown Prince's authority and wealth, he would be able to draw any peerless beauty of the realm to himself. The Crown Prince was yearningly loved, no matter where. Sleeping or waking, she could not forget him. Somehow, these days even her own heart seemed to have grown hazy. Nevertheless, even if she went out to the capital's great thoroughfares and fortunately received the Crown Prince's imperial favor, it would be like morning glories in a dream; when the morning dew dried, they withered in the evening—rather than creating regretful sins, she would dedicate her loving heart to the Crown Prince, guard her lifelong chastity, and rot away in these mountains with her father ... thus she had bravely extinguished the flame of love by herself.

Thereupon, hearing her father Shakanna's meaningful words, Subaruhime felt a sense of a promising future well up within her without knowing why, and with a downward gaze, dyeing her face with crimson, she spoke bashfully.

Subaruhime: 'Ochichi-sama, since you told me to speak of what I am thinking without reserve, today is the one great affair of my life, and I will speak of everything I am thinking. Please do not scold me.'

Shakanna: 'What, scold you? Whatever it is you think, you may as well speak it before your father.'

Subaruhime: 'If so, I shall speak. Since things have come to this, it is no use hiding it either: when the Crown Prince was returning, he firmly grasped my hand and uttered, "Suburuhime, wait for a while. I will surely come to fetch you." I know not if it is mere self-conceit, but the Crown Prince ... is he in love with that concubine? And I too ...'

O-tōsama, kore de mo fudōri to kikoemasu kana.'

Sha: 'Ha-ha-ha-ha, maru de Taishi-sama o, taneuma to machigaete iru dewa nai ka. Fudugō senman na koto o iu yatsu da.'

Su: 'Sono taneuma ni o-nari-asobasu no ga, kuni no kimi taru kata no o-tenshoku deshou. Taishi-sama nomi naru zut, kuni no rippa na hito wa mina taneuma to shite shakai ni ko o takusan umi-otosanakute wa, shakai no konponteki kaizō wa dōshite mo dame desu.'

Sha: 'Sō suru to omae wa Taishi-sama ga takusan na onna o mochi ni natta toki wa dō suru tsumori da. Riron to jissai wa ōni chigau mono dakara, sono toki ni natte rinki no tsuno o hayashi, shitto no honō o moyashi, sono toki ni tsurai me o ajiwatte minakeba wakarumai. Ima koso riron de wa rippa na koto utteru ga jitchi ni nareba, sō wa ikanai yo. Kitsuto rinki suru ni sadamatte iru wa.'

Su: 'O-ho-ho-ho, sonna garyō no nai Subāru dewa arimasen sh. Mekake datte Taishi-sama ijō ni ai suru danshi ga dekita toki, Taishi-sama ga koshō o iwareru yō na koto ga atta toki wa, mekake no hō kara gomen o monuru dake no koto desu wa. Ippō no koi o appaku shi, dōshite enman ni ikemasu ka. Fūfu wa katei no jūyōhin desu. Katei to renai wa betsumono desu yo. Katei wa katei to shite enman ni iki, renai wa renai to shite jiyū ni okonau beki mono desu. Taishi-sama no uewanokata kara, konna tehon o dashite morawanakute wa, rinki to ka kantū to ka fudōtoku to ka no, imawashiki mondai ga zetsumetsu senai no desu. Ippū-tafu no Morumon-shū o goran nasai. Kesshite takusan na tsuma no naka ni, rinki ya shitto ya, ensa nado no koe wa arimasen yo. Tonikaku, kyūrai no rōshū o dahashinakute wa, katei mo kokuka mo osamarimasen. Mekake wa Sudaruman-taishi-sama koso wa renai ni taishite mo rikai o mochi, mata shakai dōtoku ni taishite mo kanzen ni kairyō suru shishitsu o motta hito to ukagaimashita. Sore de renai wa tokaku, kokuka shakai no tame hitsuyō no tame to kinbo no amari tsui ni renai ni tenka shita no desu wa, ho-ho-ho.'

To jūgosai no musume ni mo niazu, ohi-ohi kokoro no kiji o arawashi, chichi no Shakanna to emu ni maite shimatta.

Shakanna: 'A-a-a, tōnen tateba hitomukashi, kono yama no oku made mo shisaikai no akufū wa osōte kita no kana.'

原文（近代文語）

シャ『理窟はどうでもつくものだ。然し乍らタラハン国は一夫一婦が制度だ。之を破るものは道徳の破壊者だ。恋愛等末の末だ』

ス『今日の世の中に大人物の現はれないのは一夫一婦の制度が行はれてゐる弊害から来るのですよ。昔の神代の神様を御覧なさい。大国主の神様は打みる島の先々、垣見る磯のさきおちず賢女奇女を娶り、国魂の神を生み、大人物を沢山お造りなさつたぢやありませぬか。スダルマン太子のやうな賢明な君子的人格者は、妾のやうな賢女奇女を、沢山娶ひ遊ばし、そして大人物を四方に配り遊ばしたら、屹度世の中はよくなるでせう。あんな大人物こそ沢山な女があつても生殖の方から見て国家の宝を産み出す事になるでせう。之に反して愚夫愚婦と云へど矢張一夫一婦とすればガラクタ人間許り世に拡まり、益々世は堕落するのみでせう。要するに社会道徳の上から考へて、立派な人間は天の星の数程沢山な怜悧子を生み、野卑下劣な半獣的人間は、なるべく子を産まないやうにするのが、国家存立の上にも個人経済の上にも有利でせう。お父さま、之でも不道理と聞えますかな』

シャ『ハヽヽヽ、まるで太子様を、種馬と間違へてゐるぢやないか。不都合千万な事を云ふ奴ぢや』

ス『その種馬におなり遊ばすのが、国の君たる方の御天職でせう。太子様のみならず、国の立派な人は皆種馬として社会に子を沢山産み落さなくては、社会の根本的改造はどうしても駄目です』

シャ『さうするとお前は太子様が沢山な女をおもちになつた時はどうするつもりだ。理論と実際とは大に違ふものだから、その時になつて悋気の角を生やしたり、嫉妬の焔をもやしたり、その時に辛い目を味はつて見ねば解るまい。今こそ理論では立派な事云つてるが実地になれば、さうは行かないよ。屹度悋気するに定つてゐるわ』

ス『オホヽヽヽ、そんな雅量のないスバールぢや厶いませぬ。妾だつて太子様以上に愛する男子が出来た時、太子様が故障を云はれるやうな事があつた時は、妾の方から御免を蒙る丈けの事ですわ。一方の恋を圧迫し、どうして円満に行けますか。夫婦は家庭の重要品です。家庭と恋愛は別物ですよ。家庭は家庭として円満に行き、恋愛は恋愛として自由に行ふべきものです。太子様の上つ方から、こんな手本を出して貰はなくては、悋気とか姦通とか不道徳とかの、忌まわしい問題が絶滅せないのです。一夫多婦のモルモン宗を御覧なさい。決して沢山の妻の中に、悋気や嫉妬や、怨嗟等の声はありませぬよ。兎に角、旧来の陋習を打破せなくては、家庭も国家も治まりませぬ。妾はスダルマン太子様こそは恋愛に対しても理解を持ち、又社会道徳に対しても完全に改良する資質をもつた方と伺ひました。それで恋愛は兎も角、国家社会のため必要のためと欣慕のあまり遂に恋愛に転嫁したのですわ、ホヽヽヽ』

と十五才の娘にも似合はず、おひおひと心の生地を現はし、父のシャカンナを烟にまいて了つた。
