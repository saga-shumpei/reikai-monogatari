Chapter 18: 救の網 [1742]

救の網〔1742〕

§ 1

The lower reaches of the Tamanogaha River, which flow down from the Chio and Chidani valleys of the Asakura Sanmiyaku, are called the Indus River. This river flows through the center of the Tarahan country, and pours far from north to south into the cow's lake of the Karmaita country. Arina, the younger brother of Samori, lightly dressed in the guise of a Buddhist monk and wearing a deep sedge hat, traced the field path illuminated dimly by the twelfth-night moon and arrived at the bank of the Indus River.

The rushing torrent sends up spray with a surging sound of water, looking formidable as if dragons were leaping in the river rapids, and obstructed by rocks scattered all over the river, the water becomes like white jewels and scatters high into the air. Arina perceived a straw hut near the bridge and crept into it to rest his breath. This is a hut built for a bridge keeper to be dispatched and collect a bridge fee of three rin one-way for each passerby.

Arina, while looking up at the moonlight, opened the door of the straw hut and bathed in the cool breeze.

To such a place, two or three black shadows come crossing over the single-log bridge from the opposite bank while making it bend. One of the black shadows,

Kō: 'Ah you retainers, you must have grown very tired today. Indeed, search parties are also back-breaking things. Ah fortunately there is a bridge keeper's hut here. How is it, it is still a journey of forty or fifty chō to return home, so shall we take a rest here and slowly return?'

Otsu: 'Yes, let us rest and return. For the sun will not set on a night road.'

Kō: 'Even you, who are fearful of night roads, are together with your master today so you are all right.'

Otsu: 'Yes that is so. Today I am together with the master-sama so I am a thousand men strong. No matter how much the wolves of Mount Nami growl, I will not shake a bit.'

Hei: 'Atsuha-ha-ha-ha master-sama, this fellow is a notorious coward, and he is a fellow who has never walked a night road until now. He is a fellow who blabbers that night roads are scary even in daylight, and in return night meals are sweet even in daylight.'

Kō: 'Atsuha-ha-ha-ha'

Otsu: 'Don't say stupid things. Where on earth is a daytime night meal? You are also a fellow who says things that are quite out of wits.'

Kō: 'Ya somehow there seems to be a living creature in this bridge hut. Hey, hey, which one are you?'

Arina: 'This humble monk is an ascetic monk wandering through various provinces.'

Kō: 'Ha-ha-ah, a monk blowing conch shells around those parts, aren't you. Ya I understand, I understand. Eh, if you are a monk, you should be able to do prophecies and divine divination just a little bit. Would you please listen to one request of mine?'

Arina: 'Yes, I will accept whatever matter. This humble monk is a person called Tennenbō who shuts himself up in Mount Suga and practices the Buddha's Way, and most matters are a hundred hits and a hundred bullseyes, Tennenbō's star-hitting, matching is a mystery, not matching is also a mystery, owing to the changes of the sixty-one gua yarrow stalks, or turning into yang and turning into yin, Qian and Kun, Li and Dui, and so on, changing in various miscellaneous ways, so if you do not well savor my divine divination, you will make a mistake. You are a person of great power in the castle of Tarahan, and you must be a person who is fulfilling the official duty of Usamori-tsukasa, aren't you?'

Kō: 'Ya, this is humbled. Just as you guessed, I am Sakurensu of Usamori-tsukasa.'

Arina: 'Atsuha-ha-ha, Your Excellency seems to have some searched-for object, but with the name Sakurensu, the whereabouts of lost articles is thoroughly "sugurensu" [hard to find]. The person whom Your Excellency is seeking is neither an article, nor livestock, but must be a rice-eating insect, isn't it?'

Sakurensu: 'Yes that is exactly so. Why on earth do you know so well?'

Arina: 'Atsuha-ha-ha This humble monk is one who descended to this earth from the spirit of the moon for the salvation of all living beings, in order to punish evil people and save good people, entrusting an eight-foot body to a single sedge hat, a single straw raincoat, and a single staff, wandering through various provinces, but the Tarahan country looks like a terrible disturbance has broken out, doesn't it?'

Sakurensu: 'Yes I would like to ask one thing, will my hope be fulfilled?'

'Aye!'

Answering thus, both men make a rush at Alina from front and rear. Dodging like a flying bird, avoiding the sharp blades of the enemies to the right and left, Alina wards off and deflects the edges of the naked swords with his Kongō staff. However, Alina too is vastly outnumbered by a crowd; thinking it would be a terrible disaster to lose his life by lingering, just as he dashes full speed toward the western bank across a single-plank bridge, he trips over a crude bridge pile and plunges straight into the rushing torrent with a splash. Umori and the other retainers clap their hands, shout banzai three times, and with puffed-up shoulders and chests, march triumphantly back toward home.

Balance, the female leader of the People's Relief Corps, was struggling with the expenses of supporting numerous foster children, and at a place called Yogafuchi, an indifferently water-stagnant spot on the Indus River designated by Umori as a forbidden area, she had begun casting nets with her foster children, taking advantage of the moonlit night. This spot was overgrown with old trees on the banks, making it the most optimal location as a gathering place for fish, and moreover, a multitude of various fish and shellfish inhabited it. The strict rule dictated that should one cast a net in this place and be caught by officials, one would instantly be sentenced to the punishment of stoning. While having dozens of foster children keep watch, Balance cast her net again and again, capturing a copious amount of fish. When she had cast about ten times, something immensely heavy caught in the net. When Balance hoisted it up relying on her brute strength, it proved to be the corpse of a human being. Rich in chivalrous spirit, she thought,

'That it should be caught in my net must be some kind of karmic bond. Whoever's person they may be, if there is any means for his life to be saved, I must exhaust every possible measure to save him.'

Thus she commanded her foster children to gather a quantity of firewood, made a fire by the riverbank, and warmed him. Then she tried artificial respiration and various other measures, but he did not regain his breath. Just as she was sinking into the depths of near despair, the party of Crown Prince Sudarman, who had been captured in the watermill hut, appeared at this spot mounted on horses. Glancing at him through the moonlight from a single look, Balance recognized that he was Prince Sudarman. Turning desperate, thinking that if they uttered any nonsense she would rely on her mighty strength and cast them all together with their horses into the river, she braced herself. バラ『ハイ、思ひもよらぬ殿下の思召、何分鄙に育つた不作法者、到底廟堂に立つ事は出来ませぬ。まして今迄茶坊主の妻と迄成下つて居りました卑しき女で厶いまするから……』

Futoshi: 'Such words do not suit you either; if it is for the salvation and relief of the kokumin, surely you can willingly listen to my kotoba. I intend not to let such bad administration be practiced as heretofore, where the tokukenkaikifu throw their weight around, ignore the sufferings of the lower minshiu, and do things for their own selfish convenience. Will you not please grant my request?'

Barance: 'Yes, having been favored by such an unexpected omida of the Denka, it seems that the higoro guwanmau has also reached the time of jiyōjiyu. If that is so, according to the reishi, I shall accompany the Denka and enter the palace, nifudai.'

Futoshi: 'Ah, manzoku, manzoku! Without losing time, you must come with me into the jiyōnai.'

Barance: 'Understood. However, since I think Arina-sama would find it tautei difficult to walk in this yōsu, I shall become a horse, carry her on my back, and attend upon you as an escort.'

Ume: 'Ah, Barance-sama, atsupare, atsupare! Your yūki surpasses that of any danshi; you are indeed a person who might be called an incarnation of a deity.'

Barance: 'To praise me so makes me feel quite shy. Come, let us go.'

Carrying Arina on her back, Barance followed closely behind the swift-footed one. At this time, the moon in the vast sky pushed aside the pale clouds, smiling as it purely and brightly illuminated the nocturnal path of the party of five. The river waves of the Indus River bathed in the moonlight, sparkling glitteringly like golden scales.
