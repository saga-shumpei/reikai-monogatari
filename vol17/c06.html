瑞の宝座〔617〕

§ 1

In a realm of pure Kiyōiki, encompassed by mountains where Jiyumoku grow Utsusau and Ohishige, water clear as Suishiyō flows Sensen, and countless Seisen of a bluish hue lie scattered Tokorodokoro.

In the Chiukū, seven or eight Amatsuotome of beautiful Yōshiyoku dance in a frenzy, moving Uōsaō to the sound of Bimeu Ongaku, while Gariyōbinka, Hōō, and Kujiyaku, the Zuichiyō, Ahimaji and fly Zengosaiu; the spectacle is such that one might deem it the Daisaijitsu of the Tengoku Jiyōdo.

Upon a beautiful moss-covered rock sits a woman of Tanrei Yōshiyoku and Igi Genzen, holding a fan of the rising sun in both hands, and she sings:

"Jitentōtō is the land of pines, / Unwavering and eternal in its firmness, / The reign is peaceful and tranquil, / The land is governed in abundance, / With peace under heaven and the realm fulfilled, / The five grains ripen and the mountains are green, / The waters are clear, and truly, in the age ruled by the divine command of Toyokunihime, / Is it the divine land of the Amatsu-mikora? / In the eternal spring of Tokoyo, / The reign of the pine flourishes long, / The Amatsukami and the Kunitsukami, / Beginning with the myriad deities, / And gathering all the hundred common people, / The age of Miroku rejoices and thrives, / The Amatsuotome in the heavens, / Turn their brocade sleeves, / The birds sing and dance for ten thousand generations, / Between heaven and earth, a water mirror, / Floating the moon of Shinnyo."

"The Great Deity Kami-Susanoo, / Purifies and cleanses this world, / The Mizu-no-Mitama shines ever more brilliantly, / In the glorious reign that shines across all, / The praiseworthy and auspicious three-five, / The divine teachings reach far and near, / Resounding as Manaigahara, / The divine spirit of Toyokunihime, / The Mizutama of Kami-Susanoo, / Nodatehiko and Nodatehime, / Hinodewake who illuminates the dark night, / The divine figure of Sakuyahime, / Of the flowers that bloom all at once, / Mount Fuji of the high blue clouds, / The roaring sea of the Naruto Strait, / The divine dew of deep blessings, / How joyful is the world that is moistened, / How joyful is the world that is moistened, / Though it is called spring, it is still cold, / With snow on the trees of the mountains in Yomo."

"Singing of your reign as we gather, / The sovereign and the subjects live in harmony, / The Aohitogusa also submit, / And the world flourishes forever, / The age when Kuninotokotachi-no-Ōkami, / Appears openly to rule, / Like the pine, the bamboo, and the plum, eternal, / Like the long neck of the crane waiting, / Celebrating ten thousand generations, / With the lifespan of the green-haired tortoise without limit, / The divine teachings of the San-Go-Kyō, / Flourish for a thousand generations, forever, / Even unto the end of many billions of years, / Advance as an unmoving reign, / Open as an unchanging reign, / The path of the teaching opens wide, / As the fan of the reign spreads wide at the end, / In the world that sways in the divine wind, / Please come, Kannagara, / May the spirits be blessed and dwell in this world."

"Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara, / May the blessings of the spirits, / Be bestowed upon all people of the world, / O Great Deity, / The divine spirit of Toyokunihime, / I pray for a thousand and eight thousand generations."

It was Yoshikohime, the Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, who was chanting and dancing of her own accord. Otohiko stood up and sang:

Setting forth toward the sacred land, / He deliberated upon the Keirin of the fulfillment of divine government / In a divine council with the Ōkami Kunitakehiko. / Setting forth toward the East, / Eikohime, who remained behind, / And the Mikoto of the Kami, Kamehiko, who celebrates the myriad ages, / To quell the ferocity of the Maga at Ōeyama, / Accompanied by Yoshikohime, / At the bottom of the valley of Mount Kensenzan, / In the deep and historic sacred spring, / Purified their souls and served / The precious shrine of the Sumeragami.

Yoshikohime, accompanied by Aohiko, / Came again to Makutsugahara of Ōeyama, / And seeing the incoherent mutterings / Of the foul-hearted Kurohime, / Advanced, unable to subdue her with Kototama.

The path of snow, pure in heart, / With Amanohashidate left behind, / Whipping the horse, the journey continues, / And this Otohiko, together with them, / Following in the wake of Yoshikohime, / Came to Managahara, / And beheld a divine garden surpassing all tales, / Where, in the pure stream / Known as Ama no Manai, / They performed Misogi and were transformed into Mizu no Rei, / To subdue and harmonize the Magakami / Who wield power over the lands and islands of the four quarters, / Appearing as guardian Kami of the divine land, / Learning from the Kami in the spirit of Mizu no Rei, / To protect the imperial reign forevermore. / Ah, how courageous is my heart! / Ah, how beautiful is the divine garden! / Now is the time to fulfill the duty / Of the child of the Kami, born of the Kami.

Now is the time to shine and illuminate / The power of the Kami widely throughout the world. / Ah, Kannagara Kami-kami, / May the spirits be blessed. / Though the morning sun may shine or be clouded, / Though the moon may wax or wane, / Even if the great earth should sink, / The great path of the Kami shall not change. / Do not stray from the path of sincerity. / The form and countenance of Yoshikohime, / Aohiko in the sacred reign, / And Kamehiko who celebrates the myriad ages, / It is now that their Mitama shine. / Let us advance with courage and serve / The precious works of the Sumeragami. / Let us serve the precious works. / Ah, Kannagara Kami-kami, / May the spirits be blessed.

At this song of Otohiko, Yoshikohime and all those present were stirred to courage, and toward the central stone throne, where it is said Toyokunihime occasionally manifests her divine form, they recited the Amatsu Norito and finished chanting the song of propagation. Just then, Kamehiko came running, breathless.

"Ah, Yoshikohime, Otohiko, Aohiko, and all other Gorenchiusama, pray take Goyōjin. At this very moment, Kurohime, the chief of the Magami of the Uranai Kyō, has gathered a multitude of Kenzoku and is advancing toward this land. So he spoke, gasping for breath.

Otohiko replied, "Hahaha, that wretch Kurohime has exhausted all her schemes and has now become desperate; they say that a small person in distress will create disorder. Yaa, this is interesting, truly interesting; and because of this, you have suddenly become quite spirited, have you not?"

Kamehiko said, "I have heard that through the mediation of Takahime, a brave general named Takayamahiko has come from the land of Fusa, leading a great army. It is said that their plan is to hold a marriage ceremony with Kurohime, combine their forces to form a great organization, and in one stroke conquer Manai-ga-hara, the stronghold of Susanoo-no-Mikoto. Pray, by all means, do not be negligent."

Otohiko replied, "Hahaha, again and again, like an insect flying into a fire, what pitiful creatures they are. Aohiko, together with Kamehiko, use the Kototama to bring the approaching enemy to harmony; I, together with Yoshikohime, shall look up to the descent of Toyokunihime and seek the Shinchoku."

"We have understood every detail. As long as we two are here, even if a great army like clouds and mist should attack at once, we shall slaughter them with the rapid-fire cannon of Kototama. Ah, how interesting, how interesting!"

So they rejoiced with courage and fortitude. Yoshikohime called out to them,

"Yaa, Kamehiko-dono, Aohiko-dono, I have received the deep divine spirit of the Sumerōkami and have already mastered the divine work of Shinpen Jiyū, so there is nothing in the world that I fear. Do not let your hearts be drawn to me, but fight with all your might using the Kototama."

Kamehiko and Aohiko bowed their heads at once and placed both hands upon the ground,

"We have understood every detail; we humbly ask for your favor in all matters."

They rose with courage and prepared to depart from this place. At that very moment, just as Kamehiko’s urgent report had foretold, the army of Kurohime approached, with Takayamahiko at the lead, advancing toward this side with banners and drums in a most imposing and solemn manner.

Kamehiko and Aohiko, facing the approaching Gunzei of Takayamahiko, cried out, 'Ho, Takayamahiko, thou hast come to pay thy Gozan; ye fools who know not your own station, beware lest ye fall before the rapid-fire artillery of my Kototama.'

Takayamahiko, while astride his Bahai, replied, 'Ho, art thou that Kotsupamushiya of whom rumor speaks, called Kamehiko? Keep thy Kuōgen for later; ho, Monodomo, advance and attack Kamehiko and Aohiko!'

Tsunehiko, Kikuwaka, Natsuhiko, Tomihiko, and Ihataka, being of the Taishiyōkabu, under the Sashizu of Takayamahiko, each led Amata of their Teshita and surrounded the two, scattering them about.

'Come, Kamehiko, Aohiko, and the rest of you Yatsubara, it is of no avail now; rather than becoming the rust of our blades, will ye not repent your hearts this instant, cast aside the heresy of Susanōnomikoto, and submit to the true path of the Uranai-kyō? The Kami shall have mercy upon you; if ye humble yourselves and surrender, I shall forgive even the evil spirits who have opposed me. Come, what is your reply? Though ye be valiant, ye are few against many; your fortune is spent, what is your reply, what is your resolve?'

Thus, from all directions, they drew their swords in unison and pressed the attack; Kamehiko and Aohiko laughed aloud in one voice.

'Hahaha, ye fools who side with the black-hearted Kurohime, whose very color is pitch-black, even if tens of thousands of you come to attack, ye are but Yatsubara wielding the axe of a mantis against a dragon-chariot; behold the divine power of my Kototama!'

No sooner had they spoken than they clasped their hands together and, with all their might, emitted the injection of the divine spirit from their fingertips like a searchlight, swinging it to the right and to the left.

The Amata of the attackers were suddenly seized with headaches, dizziness, and paralyzed tongues; their bodies either stiffened or became numb, and they fell flat upon the ground, groaning in agony.

Seeing this state of affairs, Kurohime mounted the horse of Takayamahiko, and as the two embraced upon the horse, they fled into the clouds and mist, a sight most ridiculous.

Kamehiko laughed and said, 'Hahaha, Aohiko-dono, is this not truly a delightful affair? They came to attack with weapons, acting with such insolence toward us, the propagandists who transmit the teachings of the true Kami, only to be so easily struck down by the emission of my Kototama, their bodies paralyzed as they writhe upon the ground—how amusing! And what is even more interesting is the state of panic in which Kurohime and Takayamahiko, those two, have abandoned their allies and fled; is it not truly delightful?'

Aohiko replied, 'Hahaha, it is indeed delightful; the Sangokyō is truly different, is it not?'

Kamehiko said, 'Surely, you will not be turning back to the Uranai-kyō of Takahime now, will you?'

§ 5

Aohiko said, 'Even if the Daichi should be overturned, would it change?'

Kamehiko replied, 'Well, I cannot tell; there is still a slight turbidity in your Kototama, and that portion of turbidity is still an attachment to the Uranai-kyō.'

Aohiko said, 'Do not say such cruel things; that turbidity is likely the inertia of the faith in Uranai-kyō. If you wait a little longer, the true Kototama will surely emerge.'

Kamehiko said, 'Be that as it may, Yoshikohime-sama, Otohiko-sama must be waiting for us; come, let us hasten back to the Reijiyō.'

So saying, he went on ahead. At that moment, the Kami Toyokunihime-no-Kami was descending upon Yoshikohime under the Saniha of Otohiko.

Otohiko said, 'The Ōkami who now deigns to possess the fleshly shrine of Yoshikohime, which Kami are you? I beseech you to reveal your Mina. I am Otohiko, the Saniha and Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō. If you would deign to instruct us in detail regarding the Oboshimeshi of the Shinkai, we would be most grateful.'

The possessed one said, 'I am Toyokumuno-no-Mikoto, also known by the name Toyokunihime-no-Kami. Together with Kuninotokotachi-no-Ōkami, I once hid my form in the subterranean realm, and appearing again upon the mountains of the earth, I waited for the time to arrive while repairing and solidifying the lands drifting upon the great ocean. As the heavenly fortune has circulated, the Amatsukami have decreed this sacred ground to be my place of enshrinement. I shall fix my spirit in this place, and not to mention the self-revolving islands, I shall distribute my spirit throughout the lands and islands of Ōyashima to protect the world eternally. Since you are currently employing Onikumuhiko, and the remnant of the Yamata-no-Orochi is hiding its form in Onigajō-yama, watching for an opportunity and intending to trample upon this sacred ground, depart from this place as quickly as possible. Lead Kamehiko and Aohiko, and proceed toward Onigajō-yama along the ridges of this Hijiyama. I shall accompany your shadow-body and cause you to establish great merits eternally. Do not worry or fret, by any means. Even if tens of millions of Magakami should come to attack, do not yield, and do not fear. Rely upon Kami as your strength, take sincerity as your staff, and fight well. Take up the spear of sincerity and subdue the enemy with Kototama. Furthermore, as I shall protect this sacred ground with my spirit eternally, leave this place without a moment's delay, leaving no attachment of heart behind. Kamehiko and Aohiko, you too shall advance toward Onigajō together with Otohiko.'

Otohiko said, 'I have understood the details; come, from this moment, with Yoshikohime-sama at the lead, we shall all together, without losing a moment, stand to face the subjugation of the Yamata-no-Orochi. We humbly beseech your protection, again and again.'

§ 6

Toyokunihime spake, 'Entrust all things unto the Kamu, and exert your sincerity to the utmost of your strength.'

Having thus spoken and departed, she ascended unto the heavens; whereupon Yoshikohime returned for the first time to her senses, and cried, 'Ah, how grateful, how grateful! The Gokaurin of the Ōkami! Come, Lord Otohiko, and all you Goichidōsama, let us proceed without delay to Onigajō, in accordance with the Shinchiyoku.'

'We have understood the Isai,' they replied, 'if it be so, let us depart from here.'

Kamehiko said, 'Come, Heisuke, Onara, and Osetsu-dono, your efforts have been great; here we shall take our leave.'

Heisuke replied, 'After we part from you, what shall become of us? If a multitude of Gunzei should press upon us as they did just now, we shall be unable to defend ourselves or fight in any wise; we beseech you, will you not take us along with you?'

Otohiko answered, 'Nay, that may not be. Yet, have no Goshinpai regarding any manner of enemy. When you cannot prevail, recite the Norito of the Ananai-kyō and sing the Sendenka. Then, any formidable foe shall vanish like clouds and mist. Such is the power of the Kamu-uta. Farewell, Oyaji-dono, Oba-sama, and Musume-go; if there be a bond of fate, we shall meet again.'

Thus they parted to the left and right, and the four of the party proceeded along the ridge of Mount Hiji, singing the Sendenka as they journeyed toward the south. Heisuke and his family of three, lamenting the parting, walked slowly and dejectedly on their way back toward their home.
