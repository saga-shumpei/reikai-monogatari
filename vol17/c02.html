Chapter 2: Demon の Cavern [613]

魔の窟〔613〕

§ 1

Heisuke, together with his son and the three others, called out to the group consisting of Onihiko, Onitora, Iwa, Kan, and Kunugi, who, upon suddenly regaining their senses, found themselves in the midst of a field with a filthy privy at the center, their hands pressed together, fervently chanting the Norito.

Onihiko exclaimed, 'Ah, how ridiculous; it is a state in which one cannot walk in broad daylight with a proud face. This must surely be the retribution for having aided Onikumohiko of Ōeyama and having exhausted every manner of evil. If one considers it the repayment of the debts of the Mitama, it is well, yet before we knew it, all five of us have become as newborn Akago, stark Matsupadaka, and have ended up as Muichibutsu, possessing not even a single loincloth. A man is worth Hiyakukuwan when naked; come, now is the time to test our true strength as men, for if the spirit remains firm, what matters a little snow? Sumo wrestlers are stark naked even in the depths of winter; come, come, everyone, let us proceed.'

So saying, Onihiko took the lead.

Iwa-kō remarked, 'Good heavens, a procession of the naked is a shameful sight indeed. Furthermore, one moment Onihiko uses polite language, and the next he turns to rough speech; if you do not settle upon one or the other, we shall have no fixed policy in how to respond to you.'

Onihiko replied, 'The words of the Hon-shugojin, the Shō-shugojin, and the Fuku-shugojin emerge in a mixture, so it cannot be helped. Hey, Iwa-kō, pray be patient for a little while longer, for this Onihiko, too, lacks some composure of spirit.'

Saying this, he strode with long steps, crossing the snowy path and advancing deep into the mountains.

The sun of the twenty-eighth day of the first month shone brilliantly, the snow began to melt gradually, and the great masses of avalanche tumbling down from Manai-ga-take fell with a roaring sound, countless in number, in a perilous manner.

Suddenly, Osetsu was crushed by a falling mass of snow, leaving only her head exposed, and she raised a cry of distress,

'Help me, help me,'

she screamed and wept at the top of her voice.

Onihiko said, 'Hey, Onitora, last year we caused Osetsu-sama to suffer; shall we not remove that snow mass to save her life and offer our apologies?'

Onitora replied, 'That is so; now is the time to offer our apologies, for if we pass up this moment, when else shall such an opportunity arise? Excuse me, Osetsu-sama, I shall save you now, so please wait a moment. Ei, ei, what a hard mass of snow; what a cold fellow it is.'

Osetsu shook her head from side to side and said, 'No, no, even if I must die, I will not accept the assistance of Onihiko or Onitora; I pray that someone else will come forth and save me.'

§ 2

Onihiko exclaimed, 'Ah, how persistent and tenacious Osetsu-sama is; in this hour of crisis and life-or-death, this is no time for misanthropy. Come, Onitora, you and I, now is the time for atonement; come, one, two, three!'

So saying, he struck his naked body against the snow-mass. Though he pushed and shoved against the great mass, it would not budge in the least.

Heisuke and Onara strained their voices in weeping.

Heisuke cried, 'Ah, is there any in the Sanzensekai as ill-fated as we? Just when we saw you in good spirits and felt as though we had been resurrected, before a single day had passed, we are met with another unforeseen calamity; how can we continue to live? Hey, Onara, you and I shall now depart with our daughter on a journey to the Jifumanokudo. Come, make ready; are you prepared?'

Onara replied, 'Yes, yes, I too am but a woman; let the three of us, parent and child, end our lives here and depart for what they call the Shinkai. Now, Osetsu, as this old woman goes a step ahead, pray follow me at your leisure; the old man and I shall be waiting at the crossroads of the six realms, oh, oh, oh.'

Heisuke said, 'Now, now, Onara, all things are manipulated by the cords of fate. At this juncture, tears are forbidden; come, be resolute.'

Before he had finished speaking, he drew his dagger with a speed beyond sight and sought to thrust it deep into his own belly. Onihiko, startled, grasped Heisuke’s dominant arm firmly.

Onihiko said, 'Hold, Grandfather, wait, wait; it is too soon to die. Can flowers and fruit bloom if one is dead? How can you think to find peace in the next world without finding peace in this one? Now, now, wait, wait; impatience is a loss.'

Onara cried, 'Heisuke-dono, farewell!'

At the very instant she drew her short sword to thrust it into her throat, Onitora, forgetting himself, grasped Onara’s dominant arm firmly.

Onitora said, 'Grandmother, wait, wait.'

Onara exclaimed, 'Oh, if it is not Onitora, the foster child of Onikumohiko of Ōeyama. Ugh, how filthy; do not meddle with me, for I am casting away my own life. I am causing you not a hair’s breadth of harm; pray leave me be, for your meddling is uncalled for. Oh, how filthy; how could I live on brazenly in this world after being saved by a villain such as you? Ugh, leave me be!'

Onitora, his voice choked with tears, said, 'Onara-sama, will you not forgive my sins, no matter what?'

Onara replied, 'It is settled; even if I die, I shall not forgive you. Even if the world of Miroku should come, how could I ever forget my resentment toward you?'

§ 3

Onitora said, 'O-Nara-sama, if that be so, then by your own hand, I pray you torment and slay me to your heart's content; if you do so, perhaps your resentment may be somewhat appeased, and I beseech you to forget my sins.'

Heisuke, weeping with a loud voice, said, 'Hold, hold, O-Nara, will you not cease your complaining at long last? Onihiko and Onitora, both of whom have repented their former wrongs and returned to the soul of goodness, if you continue to pour out such grievances, it shall only become a deeper sin upon our part. Rather than that, shall we not gracefully embark upon the journey of the Shinkai together with our daughter? I pray you, leave not the slightest attachment to the Shiyaba behind. Ah, Onihiko and Onitora, your sincerity has pierced even the bones and marrow of the stubborn Heisuke. Never, never shall I harbor resentment against you henceforth; I beseech you, release your hands.'

Onihiko said, 'How could we, how could we ever abandon you to your deaths? Do not be short-tempered, I pray you, and reconsider once more. Oi, Onitora, do not release the arms of O-Nara-sama; hold fast to them, I entreat you. Ho, ho, Ihakō, Kan, Kunugi, why do you not hasten to rescue O-Setsu-sama? Why are you lingering and hesitating?'

Ihakō said, 'Do you not see that we three have been laboring from the very beginning to clear away these snow-masses? Come, come, O-Setsu-sama, you must feel much lighter now; pray, try to move a little.'

O-Setsu said, 'Yes, yes, I thank you most humbly. I was near to losing my breath, but thanks to your kindness, I have gradually begun to feel at ease. If you could but remove a little more, I shall surely be saved. Pray, Grandfather, Grandmother, I beseech you to hold fast; it seems that I, Setsu, shall be saved.'

Heisuke and O-Nara, at the same time, said, 'Ya, ya, O-Setsu, you shall be saved? That is better than anything. If you are to live in this world, how could your grandfather and grandmother depart from it? Now, everyone, be at peace; even if you tell us to die, we shall not die. You, Onihiko and Onitora, were indeed great evildoers, but it is well that you have attained such repentance. Come, come, let us offer our thanks to the Kami.'

Ihakō said, 'Pray, Grandfather, Grandmother, that is well and good, but these snow-masses cannot yet be easily removed. I pray you, recite the Norito. Ho, ho, Onihiko, Onitora, the Grandfather and Grandmother are now secure; come hither to assist, assist!'

§ 4

"Indeed," said Onihiko and Onitora, as they exerted their full strength to remove the mass of snow. At last, the snow was cleared away, and Osetsu rose up, slowly protruding her tongue some four or five sun, and with a loathsome laugh, she cried, "Kyaatsu, kyaatsu, kyaatsu, kyahahahaha!" Then, hitching up her skirts, she vanished into the deep mountains with a rhythmic patter.

The five men were struck with such terror that they nearly lost the use of their limbs, trembling from both the loathsomeness of the sight and the biting cold. Heisuke and Onara, raising their voices in hoarse cries, shouted, "Hey, Osetsu! O-oi, o-oi! Grandfather and Grandmother are here! Wait for us, wait for us!" Forgetting the danger, they climbed the mountain path where the avalanche had fallen, stumbling and rising with the aid of their staves.

The five naked men followed in their wake, shouting at the top of their lungs, "Grandfather, Grandmother, it is dangerous! Wait, wait! That which appeared to be Osetsu was a monster! Life is the seed of all things; it is dangerous, it is dangerous!" The old man and the old woman, in a state of desperate frenzy, pursued Osetsu with all their might.

Osetsu leaped across a certain mountain stream to and fro like a monkey, until she collapsed abruptly before a rock face, whereupon her form turned into white smoke, and the sound of melting snow dripped from the treetops like rain, plip-plop, plip-plop. Heisuke and his wife fell prostrate upon this spot, weeping inconsolably, oblivious to all around them. The five naked men appeared, and finding Heisuke and Onara in a swoon, they placed snow from the surroundings into their mouths and performed spiritual resuscitation with all their might, until the old couple finally gasped and returned to consciousness, crying out once more, "Osetsu, Osetsu!"

Onitora said, "Verily, this is the cavern of the demon. It was about this time last year that, by the command of Onikumohiko no Mikoto, I forced Osetsu into this cavern and sealed it so that none might enter or depart. Since then, the commander Onikumohiko was supposed to come here from time to time, but perhaps he had forgotten Osetsu, for there is no trace that this rock has been touched even once. As a large quantity of dried chestnuts was stored as a year's provision, she should not have starved to death, and as water springs forth naturally, she must still be alive if her life span has not yet reached its end. That which we thought was Osetsu just now must have been a specter or a transformation. Come, come, Grandfather and Grandmother, this Onihiko and Onitora shall, as proof of our repentance, bring you to meet the true Osetsu. We pray you, by this, cast aside your long-held resentment."

§ 5

Heisuke said, 'Will you allow me to meet my daughter Honto? If only my daughter is alive and safe, I shall forget all the grudges and everything of the past, shall I not, Onara?'

Onara wept bitterly, saying, 'I beseech you, save her quickly; I long to see my daughter Honto, oh, oh, oh.'

Onitora and Onihiko picked up suitable stones from the surroundings, and while signaling 'one, two, three,' they struck the rocky wall with all their might at once.

The stone door opened inward, revealing a pitch-black path leading into the interior.

Onihiko said, 'Now it has opened; it seems no one has entered, for there are truly formidable spider webs. Hey, Ihakō, go and break off some tree branches nearby, and you shall be the one to clear away the spider webs.'

Ihakō replied, 'What a strange cavern this is; very well, I am coming.'

He broke off a branch of a Tokihagi tree from the side, and while swinging it to the left, right, and left, he advanced, aiming toward the depths of the dark cavern.

Onitora looked back and said, 'Grandfather, Grandmother, the interior of the cavern is extremely dangerous; please wait here for a while. I shall bring Osetsu-sama back to you in a proper manner.'

Onara replied, 'Yes, yes, thank you; I beseech you to let us meet her as soon as possible.'

Heisuke said, 'I beseech you all, I rely upon you.'

Onitora replied, 'Understood.'

As they advanced step by step into the depths, Onitora turned to Onihiko and said in a low voice,

Onitora said, 'Hey Onihiko, it has been a year since we locked her in here, but it seems that the commander Onigumohiko has not come here even once since then. If by any chance Osetsu-sama has died, how should we make excuses to the old couple? It is certain that the couple will surely start a throat-stabbing commotion again. Is this not a worrisome matter?'

Onihiko replied, 'What, there is no need to worry. Surely the gods will be watching over her. Rather than having such unnecessary, premature anxieties, shall we not advance as quickly as possible and search for her safety? If by any chance Osetsu-sama has died, should we not also commit seppuku to atone for our sins?'

Ihakō said, 'Hey Kankō, Kunugikō, is today not a strange day? Everywhere you go, you only speak of ominous things like dying, committing seppuku, and changing countries; I feel quite anxious myself, and I have come to feel as if I want to crawl into a hole.'

Kankō replied, 'Are we not already inside a hole? How foolish.'

All of them exclaimed, 'Ahaha, we see a bright shadow over there. That must be the place, must it not, Onihiko? That is likely the place where Osetsu-sama is hidden.'

Onihiko said, 'Yes, that is right; it is already there, hurry, hurry.'

With Ihakō in the lead, the party of five advanced, aiming for the faint light within the cavern.

と泣きそそる。鬼虎、鬼彦は四辺の手ごろの石を拾ひ、一イ二ウ三つと合図しながら岩壁を一度に力限り撲つた。岩の戸は内に開いて中には真暗の道がついて居る。

鬼彦『サア開きました、誰も這入らないと見えて随分エライ蜘蛛の巣だ、オイ岩公、其辺の木の枝を折つて来い、さうして貴様蜘蛛の巣払ひだ』

岩公『妙な巌窟もあつたものだ、よし来た』

と傍の常磐木の枝を折り取り、左右左と振りながら暗き巌窟の奥を目蒐けて進み入る。鬼虎は後振り返り、

鬼虎『お爺イさま、お婆アさま、巌窟の中は大変に危険で御座います、暫く此処に待つて居て下さい、お節さまを立派にお連れ申て帰つて来ます』

お楢『ハイハイ有難う、何卒一時も早う会はして下され』

平助『何卒皆さま頼みます』

鬼虎『承知しました』

と段々と奥へ進みつつ鬼彦に向ひ小さい声で、

鬼虎『オイ鬼彦、此処へ押込めてからもう一年になるが、鬼雲彦の大将其後一度も此処に来て居ないやうだ、万々一お節さまが死んで了つて居つたら、老人夫婦にどうして云ひ訳をしたら好からうなア、屹度夫婦は又喉突き騒ぎをやるに極つて居る。ハテ心配な事ぢやないか』

鬼彦『ナニ心配するにや及ぶまい、屹度神様が守つて居て下さるだらう。ソンナ入らざる取越苦労をするよりも、一刻も早う前進して安否を探ることにしようぢやないか。もし万々一お節さまが死んで居たら、吾々も罪滅しに潔く割腹したらよいぢやないか』

岩公『オイ勘公、櫟公、何だか今日は怪体な日ぢやないか、彼方にも此方にも死ぬだの割腹だの国替だのと縁起の悪い事許り云ひよつて、俺達も何だか大変気にかかり、穴へでも這入り度いやうになつて仕舞つた』

勘公『既に吾々は穴へ這入つて居るぢやないか、穴阿呆らしい』

一同『アハヽヽヽ、向ふに明かるい影が見えるぞ。大方彼処の辺りだらう、ナア鬼彦、あの辺がお節さまの隠してある処でせう』

鬼彦『ウンさうだ、もう其処だ、急げ急げ』

と一行五人は岩彦を先頭に巌穴の幽かな光を目当てに進み行く。
