Chapter 11: 顕幽交通 [622]

顕幽交通〔622〕

§ 1

The sky, heavy and overcast, was shrouded in Hahiiro clouds, and across the Kayanogahara plain, where a Chinamagu wind raged, a gaunt woman journeyed alone, singing the Sendenka of Ananai-kyō in a faint voice as she advanced with a heavy heart.

Stopping by a Tsujidau where the winter wind blew fiercely, she looked on as the door behind the hall opened, and a naked man, tall enough to pierce the clouds, appeared, his teeth chattering as he spoke:

"Oh, Osetsu, so you have finally come forth. Do you remember how you took advantage of us when we were stripped Matsupadaka at Hijiyamatauge and treated us like Yotsuashiatsukahi? At that time, it vexed me... I thought to cast the old man, the old woman, and you, Kisama, together into the Tanisoko, but I reconsidered, for I feared the Kamisama, and I Kora. Before long, our Nikutai froze in the cold, our blood ceased to flow, and we were compelled to emerge into this wretched Meido. It was not for your sake that we died, but because you Oyako kept uttering such Gōtaku, it sickened me; that Mōnen remains even now, and thus, I intend to take the Inochi of you three, the parent and child. Five spirits are lying in wait at the Yotsutsuji, and we are waiting to cast you and your kin into the Jigoku. Come, your arrival here is the end of your fortune; I shall tear off your head and satisfy my grudge."

Osetsu replied, "Oh, oh, everyone, you must be filled with wrath. However, what the stubborn old man said is not true; I never mistreated you. I only carried you because Kunugi-san asked me to do so; I pray you, have mercy upon me."

Iwakō shouted, "If we were spirits who could grant such mercy, would we be loitering here in the eighth street of Jigoku? Do you know where this is? There is no standard of good or evil here, nor is there compassion or affection; it is the wilderness of resentment and jealousy. Do not babble any longer. Hey, hey, all of you, beat this wretch until she is stretched out, and tear off her limbs!"

Osetsu, finding herself in a desperate plight, cried out at the top of her voice:

"Will someone please come to me? I beseech you, save me, for I am but a frail woman. Kannagara Tamachi Haema Se, Kannagara Tamachi Haema Se."

She prayed with all her might. Suddenly, a pale-complexioned, refined man appeared upon the scene, and turning abruptly toward the five naked men, he brandished a large Ōnusa to the left, to the right, and to the left again, whereupon the naked men cried out, "Hah! An unexpected fellow has appeared. Hey, Kankō, Kunugikō, Iwakō, Onitora... follow Onihiko!"

§ 2

They sought to flee with Itsushiyōkenmei. As one man cast Reibaku upon the Gonin, crying 'Un!', the five stood firm with feet planted, becoming like Kuwaseki; they rolled their eyes, thrust their tongues forth, and trembled as tears flowed like a waterfall.

The man spoke: 'Ho, are you not Osetsu-sama of Tanbamura? How have you wandered into such a place? I am a Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō, known as Aohiko. By the command of Ōkamisama, I am in the midst of a Kototamasen against the Magami of Onigajō, but drawn by the voice with which you uttered "Kamunagara chihahe mase," my body was pulled as if by force, and I flew to this place. Come, it is perilous to remain in such a place. Return quickly to the Genkai.'

Osetsu replied: 'Are you indeed Aohiko-sama of the Ananai-kyō, of whom I have heard? When did you, too, come to the Iukai...?'

Aohiko answered: 'Nay, nay; my Nikutai is even now engaged in Daikuwatsudō together with Yoshikohime, Kamehiko, Otohiko, and others. It is my Nigimitama that has come hither during a brief respite of the Nikutai.'

Osetsu said: 'Ah, is that so? I thank you for saving me from such danger. Yet, I pray you, save those five naked ones as well.'

Aohiko replied: 'Ah, Osetsu-sama, how admirable; you ask that I save the very ghosts who were about to inflict such cruelty upon you? It is because of that heart that you shall be able to return to the Genkai once more.'

Osetsu asked: 'Is there no way to return those five to the Genkai as well?'

Aohiko answered: 'That is not possible. The primary guardian deities of those five men have already ascended as noble celestial beings, and clad in heavenly robes, they serve at the side of Toyokunihime-sama of Manai-ga-hara. Though they appear thus, they are the spirits of demons who serve as secondary guardian deities; they must endure further purification in the Iukai and extinguish their hearts of wrath before they can rise. Nevertheless, I shall release them from the Reibaku.'

Aohiko turned toward the five, and with a voice clear and cool, he intoned:

Aohiko chanted: 'Hifumiyo-itsumuyunaya-koto-momochiyorozu.'

As he repeated the counting song twice, the bodies of the five naked men returned to their former state, and they prostrated themselves before Aohiko, saying:

The Gonin spoke: 'This is indeed Aohiko-sama, you have graciously saved us. By letting us hear such splendid divine songs, our delusions of carnage have been washed away. Henceforth, we shall never, never bring harm upon the Nikutai of Osetsu-sama. We, too, shall henceforth become noble deities and find salvation in the divine realm.'

§ 3

As she wept, shedding tears, Aohiko said, 'Ah, it is well. You must recite the Norito together with me.'

The Oni replied, 'We are grateful. Hey, all you of the company, shall we recite the Norito, following the Sendenshi Aohiko?'

Thereupon, Aohiko began to recite the Kamigoto. Osetsu and the five Hadakaotoko joined their Riyōte together, and as soon as they finished reciting the Kamigoto with Aohiko, the figures of the five transformed into flowers like beautiful Botan, and being blown by a warm wind, they drifted lightly and hid their forms high in the Tenjiyō.

Aohiko said, 'Now, Osetsu-dono, you too must return. Let us meet again in the Genkai.'

Leaving these Kotoba behind, Aohiko became a beautiful Kuōgiyoku and hid his form in the Nanpau Ten. Osetsu suddenly felt her Shintai, which had been suffering until now, become Saukuwai, and while she perceived the sound of ineffable Ongaku, she regained her Shiyōki; when she looked at the Atari, she saw the old woman Onara sitting at her Makuramoto, gripping Osetsu's hand tightly, and weeping.

Osetsu said, 'Is it not you, Grandmother?'

Onara replied, 'Ah, Osetsu, have you regained consciousness? I am so happy, so happy. This is entirely due to the grace of the Kamisama; a woman called Kurohime of the Uranai-Kyō came, read and recited something called a Fudesaki, and sang something called a Sendenka, and in the end, your illness gradually worsened, until at last you ceased to breathe, and I was beside myself with anxiety; yet, I regained my composure and prayed with all my might to the Kamisama of Toyokuni-hime of Manai-ga-hara and the Kamisama Susanoo. Then, warmth returned to your body which had been growing cold, and as I watched to see if you would begin to breathe faintly, thanks to the grace of the gods, you became able to speak. Ah, how grateful I am, how grateful, to the Ōkami who has appeared in Manai-ga-hara...'

Thus the old woman wept with joy. Osetsu moved toward recovery day by day, and after four or five days had passed, she became able to work diligently, helping with all the household chores; and as they were in the midst of discussing that in another two or three days, she and her grandmother would go to pay a visit of thanks to the Hōza of Manai-ga-hara, someone pushed open the gate door and two or three figures peered inside; when they looked closely, they were the three, Kurohime, Natsuhiko, and Tsunehiko.

Kurohime said, 'Ah, Grandmother, why have you not come out to pay a visit of thanks now that your daughter has fully recovered?'

Onara replied, 'Are you not Kurohime? You, who boasted that you would heal Osetsu's illness, what has become of it? You pompously read something called a Fudesaki, which was long and incomprehensible, and on top of that, you made the young girl chirp things called Senbenka or Manbenka until my ears ached, and the girl's condition only worsened before my eyes, until she was at death's door, and seeing that she might die at any moment, you said something about having business in the Shinkai and snuck away, did you not? Do not speak so pompously. After all, the gods of the Uranai-Kyō are gods of junk, gods of poverty, gods of death, and cowardly gods. Even if I die, I will never believe in the Uranai-Kyō. ...Oh, how filthy! You sickness-gods, leave quickly, leave at once. I would be troubled if Osetsu, who has finally recovered, were to become ill again. Come, quickly, quickly, leave, leave!'

Kurohime: 'Hark, Grandmother, thou art in a grievous Torichigahi. Is it not by the grace of my Gokinen that she was saved? Even if it seemed ill at the time... to become ill is but a sign of becoming well. Even in crossing a mountain pass, one must endure hardship to reach the summit, and thereafter it is a descent. Since it is Kahaisau to keep Osetsu-don suffering forever like a Namagoroshi snake, I, this Kurohime, used my Shinriki to escort her to the pass; is it not by that grace that Osetsu-sama’s perilous Inochi was saved? To receive the saving of one’s Inochi and yet utter Kogoto will surely bring down a curse upon thee.'

Onara: 'Speak not so cunningly; I am not a grandmother to be deceived by such Gomakuwa. Do not hold me in such light regard. I am this Onara, who in my youth was called the Onimusume, and I know the Okusoko of the human heart at a Hitome, having tasted both the sour and the sweet.'

Kurohime: 'Grandmother, art thou not a little Nobo? Pray, calm thy spirit and listen to what I have to say, Hitotoho.'

Onara: 'Ah, Urusa! I will not listen, I will not listen. Begone at once. Osetsu, lend me the broom... I shall sweep that grandmother out. With a face that one cannot tell if it be black or white, and though thou hast no power, to think thou canst deceive me with thy Kuchisaki—I am not an Onara-baba to be deceived by such things.'

Kurohime: 'Onara-sama, pray listen well. If a Tokei is broken and one wishes to repair it from the Kotsupon, one must Itsutan dismantle the Kikai within entirely, and only then perform the Shiuzen, or it will not be Kuwanzen. It is Chiyōdo the same when one suffers a Taibiyō. The Kamisama has once pulled out the Kikai within Osetsu-sama’s body and has rebuilt it anew. An amateur who knows not the reason thinks that when a Tokei is dismantled it falls into pieces, and thus becomes angry, thinking the Tokei has become worse than before; yet, just as one cannot perform a perfect Shiuzen without first taking it all apart, when a Taibiyō occurs, the Kamisama performs a complete replacement of the Kikai. At such a time, it is natural for the condition to become slightly worse. That thou shouldst observe this and think it has become worse is the fundamental mistake. It is thanks to having become worse that she now possesses a body as vigorous as this. A curse... what Kogoto art thou uttering? Offer thy thanks together with Osetsu-sama to the Kamisama of the Uranai faith.'

Osetsu said, 'Lady Kurohime, though you speak with such kindness, no matter how I ponder it, I cannot bring myself to like the Uranai-kyō. Even hearing the sound of the letter U makes my head ache. Rather than that, I would desire to hear a sermon, if only for half a day, from the Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō named Aohiko.'

Kurohime replied, 'That fellow called Aohiko of Ananai-kyō is my Deshi. Though he is my right hand, I have recently sent him into Ananai-kyō as a Kanjiya. If Aohiko is great, then I, his Taishiyō, must surely possess even more Kage. Come, come, I shall let you worship me once.'

Onara and Osetsu spoke at once, 'No, no, please return home as quickly as you can.'
