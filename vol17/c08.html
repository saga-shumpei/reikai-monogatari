Chapter 8: 蚯蚓の囁 [619]

蚯蚓の囁〔619〕

§ 1

By the proposal of Kurohime and Takayamahiko, the plan to trample upon the Hōza of Mizu at Manai-ga-hara and, if possible, to occupy it, was determined. The Fūfu of Kurohime were extremely busy with the Atokatazuke of their Konrei.

The four generals of the Sangun, Natsuhiko, Tsunehiko, Ihataka, and Kikuwaka, sat cross-legged in the Iriguchi-no-ma, and prior to their departure, they were engaged in blooming conversation regarding Iroiro of Fuheidan.

Tsunehiko said, 'Are not Ningen selfish creatures? They say that like an Ishibekinkichikanakabuto, this Shinkau will not move even if pushed or struck, and that until the Jiyōjiyu of the Shinsei, they will not even let a man approach; they say they will use a Sanjifusanchi Taihau to drive away every man they see with a cold shoulder. They would preach strictly, saying that even if you are young, you must never cast your eyes upon women until the Jiyōjiyu of the Shinsei, and that if young men cast their eyes upon women, the Keikuwaku of the Shinkai will not be accomplished; they would tap their long pipes with a thud, their faces wrinkled in severity. Yet, the state of things last night was truly a sight to behold. Like a snow-daruma melting in the light of the sun, they let their countenances crumble, and with eyes narrowed like acorns, they uttered, "Oh, my husband Takayamahiko," or whatever it was they spat out; it was beyond understanding. I have become weary of it all.'

Ihataka replied, 'It is a settled matter; for a woman, a man is an essential accompaniment. Like a tea bowl and chopsticks, a chisel and a hammer, a pestle and a mortar, no matter what one says, in this world, things cannot be accomplished unless man and woman are paired. Just as a human can work freely and at will because they have two hands and two feet, is there not a saying that a thirty-year-old widow may stand, but a forty-year-old widow cannot?'

Tsunehiko retorted, 'If she were a forty-year-old widow, it could not be helped, but is she not a fifty-year-old widow? She would say, "Now, now, Tsune-sama, because you are a spirit of predestined connection, you must by no means take a wife until you are thirty," and she would claim that it is said a man stands at thirty. But in this season, a fellow who only stands at thirty is of no account. We have already been standing since we were sixteen or seventeen; now that I think of it, the only thing that stands is the belly.'

§ 2

Natsuhiko said, 'What trivialities are you all uttering? Even Takahime, though she cannot speak in too loud a voice, does this and that, and furthermore does such and such; she speaks with her mouth as if she were of firm Daushinkengo, yet there is not a single one in the Uranai Kyō whose words, heart, and deeds are in accord. I, too, believed at first that Magahiko, Hinodewake, and Takahime were exceptions, and that their Genkaushin were in Itsuchi, but of late, it has become somehow suspicious, and the spirit to truly exert myself has vanished. Until now, that fellow Kurohime would come to me, asking what Natsuhiko should do, what Tsunehiko should do, and consulting me on everything from Ihataka and Kikuwaka, from the first to the tenth, from the highest to the lowest. But since yesterday, the Tenkō has undergone a Gekihen, and they have completely erased Wareware from their Nentō; they smear white things upon their Shiwazura, bare their Tanishi-like teeth, and drink only sake—never even offering us a cup—while asking, "Oh, Takayamahiko-sama, oh, people here, what shall we do? Would it not be better to do thus?" As the Tenkō changes so drastically, one cannot know when thunder will roar above our heads or when a storm will strike; I have thoroughly understood the truth of the Uranai Kyō and my affection for it has reached its end. Even if I were to seek conversion to the Sangokyō now, I have followed the words of Takahime and Kurohime with all my might, spreading slander and abuse against Susanoo-no-Mikoto, so it must surely have reached the ears of those in the Sangokyō. If so, I cannot convert to the Sangokyō, and staying in the Uranai Kyō is not interesting, for they treat us as nuisances and drive us only toward suffering, so that my abacus truly does not balance. If we continue in this state forever, we shall be forced to undergo the Mametsu of our Mitama; let us firmly solidify the foundation of our Mitama while there is yet time. Would it not be intolerable to be used to the limit, only to be cast aside when the crucial moment arrives, under the arbitrary pretext that one cannot repent or that one's Mitama-no-innen is evil?'

Tsunehiko replied, 'That is indeed so. Merely because I heard that Takahime was of the lineage of the Hensei-danshi, I have followed her until now, thinking her to be the living shrine of Hinode-no-Kami, a propagator far more excellent than any Mitama of a Hensei-joshi. However, Hinode-no-Kami is also quite unreliable. Let us each prepare ourselves to withdraw from the Uranai Kyō.'

§ 3

Kikuwaka: 'Ho, speak in such a loud voice and it shall reach the inner chambers; will you not be silent?'

Natsuhiko: 'What, even if we speak with great volume today, shall our voices reach the ears of Kurohime? Only the voice of Takayamahiko enters those ears. If she possessed the kindness to pay such heed as to let our voices reach her, we would not have raised this problem in the first place. Like lice upon a beggar, she has skillfully slain us with the tips of her tongue, and until now, she has used us to her own advantage. It seems she is quite pleased with us; like a pair of withered pine needles, or perhaps like the eggs of lice, that fellow shall not depart from her even in death. Ha, ha, ha, ha!'

Ihataka: 'However, the hour for our departure to Manai-ga-take draws near; do you intend to set forth for the campaign?'

Natsuhiko: 'Even if I were to say no, what use would it be? Being within the Uranai-kyō, we must set forth for the campaign whether we will or no. Yet, from the root to the leaf, I have lost all inclination for it. Since there is no other way, let us set forth in mere formality, and shall we not intentionally suffer defeat at the hands of the Ananai-kyō and flee? If we do so, not to mention Kurohime, even Takahime will surely place her hand upon her breast and reflect a little. Even Takayamahiko may lose his affection and abandon Kurohime to depart. She thinks now that the bridegroom has arrived, and feigning elegance, she forces her large mouth into a tiny pout, appearing refined; but after a while, she will surely reveal her true nature and, woman though she be, will drink cider and beer straight from the bottle amidst the crowd. There is no reason why gilded metal should not eventually peel. We, too, have until now been coated in the gilding of being believers of the Uranai-kyō, but can we not endure it any longer, and has it not begun to peel away? Ha, ha, ha, ha!'

At this place, Torawaka and Tomihiko both appeared.

Tora, Tomi: 'Ho, commanders of the Four Heavenly Kings, it is the command of Takayamahiko and Lady Kurohime: make preparations for the campaign and head toward Manai-ga-hara without a moment's delay.'

So saying, they cast the words aside and departed from the place in haste.

Natsuhiko: 'What is this? They make fools of us. To use Torawaka and Tomihiko, who arrived only yesterday, to convey commands to us—do they not hold us in too much contempt? No matter that they are retainers brought by the favored Takayamahiko, to cast aside us veterans and issue commands to newcomers at their own discretion, and to lower us by one rank—this is why I say we must abandon them in due time.'

§ 4

Tsunehiko said, 'Ah, it cannot be helped; let us, at the very least, make a formal departure.'

Kurohime appeared suddenly upon the scene and asked, 'Now then, Natsuhiko, Tsunehiko, what was it that you were just saying?'

Tsunehiko replied, 'Yes, we were saying that we would make preparations to depart for Mount Manai.'

Kurohime said, 'That is a laborious task indeed, but pray, tell me what followed; what was it that you said next?'

Tsunehiko answered, 'Yes, yes, what followed was, indeed, what followed.'

Kurohime said, 'Do you not know the saying that the heavens have mouths and the walls have ears? From the very beginning, I have been hiding in the next room, listening to the conversation of you four from start to finish. You have certainly praised Takayama-sama and myself quite generously, have you not?'

The four men scratched their heads all at once and said, 'Nay, that is absolutely not the case; we were merely intoxicated by wine and our tongues slipped. We beseech you, by Kamunahohi and Ōnahohi, to look upon this and hear this with mercy and rectify it.'

'You say you were intoxicated, but when did you drink wine?'

Natsuhiko replied, 'Yes, we drank wine when you and Takayama-sama exchanged the nuptial cups; it is because of that the intoxication took hold, and we inadvertently digressed.'

Kurohime said, 'Do not speak such foolishness; where is there a man who becomes intoxicated without drinking wine and proceeds to ramble? You spoke with true sincerity, did you not? Come now, the Uranai-kyō shall be hindered by the presence of ignorant fellows such as yourselves. Come, depart this day for wherever you will. Begone, begone; it is loathsome even to look upon your faces.'

Natsuhiko said, 'That must be so; since the face you favor has begun to flicker before your eyes, you must find the sight of our faces unbearable.'

Kurohime said, 'Ah, do not speak such unnecessary things. Come, be off, be off. Dismissed from the Uranai-kyō, struck down by the Sangyō-kyō, wandering here and there like a stray dog, until at last you are struck upon the head with a cudgel, and must come yelping and wagging your tails back to the Uranai-kyō once more—the fact that this shall be your fate is plain to see. Crossing the great bridge of the origin of the Uranai-kyō, only to lose your way and return, for if you grow arrogant, it shall be just so; like unhulled rice mixed with white, it is pitiful to see you return to apologize and shrink into a corner. While there is yet time, repent and listen to what this Kurohime says. Though I, Kurohime, speak thus sternly with my lips, within my heart I am of a deep and compassionate nature, both flower and fruit, and single-minded in sincerity. I am the possessor of the Japanese spirit, the very essence of a pure crystal ball. Come, what say you? Give me a firm answer. What was Natsuhiko’s poem of last night? Thinking it a joyous time, I endured it, but you grew emboldened and spewed forth slander. Had it been any other person, I would have cast you out at that very moment, but I, Kurohime, who have realized the true depths of the divine path, have a broad heart and have let it pass like the wind blowing through the pines, forgiving you. And yet, you four, who are the leaders of the group, act like the master of the swallows, knowing not even half of what those who know nothing know—what is it that you are saying? Can you, who do not understand the great divine heart of sincerity, endure it? If you do not know, then remain silent.'

§ 5

Natsuhiko said, 'Yes, yes, I have truly no excuse; I pray you, for this time only, reconsider and overlook my fault.'

Kurohime replied, 'I shall forgive you for this occasion alone; but if hereafter you should utter even a single word or half a word concerning Takayama-sama or Kurohime, know that I shall strike you down for it. Come, come, Tsunehiko, Kikuwaka, Ihataka, prepare for departure; the Great General Takayamahiko has already completed the preparations for the campaign.'

The four of them answered in unison, 'We have certainly understood.'

Thereupon, Kurohime and Takayamahiko gathered their clan and retainers, and advanced toward Manai-ga-hara with banners flying and drums beating in grand array; yet, they were easily defeated by the Kototama of Kamehiko and Aohiko, and scattered in all directions like spiderlings dispersed.

The cabinet members—Natsuhiko, Ihataka, Kikuwaka, and Tsunehiko—who were the four heavenly kings relied upon by Kurohime, the holder of the keys to the Uranai-kyō, had lacked unity between high and low since the marriage of Kurohime, and their thoughts were naturally shifting silently toward the Sangokyō. Consequently, the long-planned attack on Manai-ga-hara was intentionally collapsed by the four heavenly kings of their own side, and the strategic plan, into which Kurohime had infused her divine power and demonic cunning, ended almost entirely as a mere drawing of a rice cake. Alas, it is sufficient to know that the difficulty of gathering the hearts of men cannot be swayed by false actions such as honeyed words, flattering looks, or Machiavellian schemes. In contrast, the Sangokyō possessed no hidden designs and no artifice, serving the divine work solely with supreme sincerity and truth, manifesting the spirit of Miroku in accordance with the way of the Kami. Thus, the hearts of the people gathered to the Sangokyō without expectation, increasing in number day and night, and before long, it became a great power in the world. The Uranai-kyō had not gathered a single believer directly throughout the vast land of Ōyashima; it merely enticed the immature believers who had submitted to the Sangokyō with honeyed words and flattering looks, and deceived the world by using Takahime, who descended from the lineage of the transformed male, as its sole signboard. Its foundation was as weak as a tower built upon sand, and its fragility was like gold leaf applied to a charcoal ball, or like bean curd; it possessed no substance, merely glossing over the surface with glib speech. What it preached was like the efficacy list of a medicine peddler—having a name but no reality—and it was even rumored that 'famous in name but empty in reality, harmful and useless luxury' was the very synonym for the so-called Uranai-kyō. Yet, the persistent Takahime and Kurohime did not yield in the slightest... 'A woman's single-mindedness can pierce even a rock; whether wrong be evil or evil be wrong, even if the sun were to rise in the western sky, the resolution once fixed within the heart shall never be broken, though one be reborn and die ten million times over, repeating the journey of life and death'—such was their heart of solid bedrock, a woman's stubbornness hardened to the core, a situation beyond all reason.

Kurohime, lamenting day and night that Aohiko, whom she relied upon as her strength, had submitted to the Sangokyō, and thinking to make him the mastermind of the Uranai-kyō once again, commanded Torawaka and Tomihiko, who had followed Takayamahiko from the land of Fusa, to persuade Osetsu, who was never absent from Aohiko's thoughts day or night, striving with all their might to cause Aohiko to lose his faith through her.

ウラナイ教の鍵鑰を握つて居た黒姫の部下四天王と頼みたる夏彦、岩高、菊若、常彦の閣僚は黒姫結婚以来上下の統一を欠ぎ、自然三五教に向つて其思想は暗遷黙移しつつありき。其の為め、折角の真名井ケ原の攻撃も味方の四天王より故意と崩解し、黒姫が神力を籠めたる神算鬼謀の作戦計画も殆ど画餅に帰し終りたるなりき。嗚呼人心を収攪せむとするの難き、到底巧言令色権謀術数等の虚偽行動をもつて左右すべからざるを知るに足る。之に反して三五教は一つの包蔵もなく手段もなく、唯々至誠至実をもつて神業に奉仕し、ミロクの精神を惟神的に発揮するのみ。されば人心は期せずして三五教に集まり、日に夜に其数を増加し、何時とはなしに天下の大勢力となりぬ。ウラナイ教は広い大八洲国に於て直接に信徒を集めたるもの唯一人もなく、唯々三五教に帰順したる未熟の信者に対し、巧言令色をもつて誘引し、且つ変性男子の系統より出でたる高姫を唯一の看板となし世を欺くのみにして、根底の弱き事、砂上に建てたる楼閣の如く、其剥脱し易き事炭団に着せたる金箔の如く、豆腐の如く、一つの要もなく唯弁に任し表面を糊塗するのみ、其説く所恰も売薬屋の効能書の如く、名のみあつて其実なく、有名無実、有害無益の贅物とは、所謂ウラナイ教の代名詞であらうと迄取沙汰されけり。されど執拗なる高姫、黒姫は少しも屈せず……女の一心岩でも突貫く、非が邪でも邪が非でも仮令太陽西天より昇る世ありとも、一旦思ひ詰めたる心の中の決心は、幾千万度生れ代り死代り生死往来の旅を重ぬるとも、いつかないつかな摧けてならうか……との大磐石心、固まりきつた女の片意地、張合もなき次第なり。

黒姫は力と頼む青彦の三五教に帰順せし事を日夜に惜み、如何にもして再びウラナイ教の謀主たらしめむと、千思万慮の結果、フサの国より高山彦に従ひ来れる虎若、富彦に命じ、青彦が日夜に念頭を離れざるお節を説きつけ、お節より青彦が信仰を落させむものと肝胆を砕きつつありける。
