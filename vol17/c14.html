Chapter 14: 空 Valley の足 Sound [625]

空谷の足音〔625〕

§ 1

It was the fifteenth day of Niguwatsu, when the moon, illuminating the eastern sky, rose above the mountain ridge; the Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō, the Kamutsukasa Aohiko, accompanied by Natsuhiko and Tsunehiko, withdrew into Onigajiyōzan, seeking to pacify the Ichirui of the Onikumawake, the Wakemitama of the Yamataorochi, and to save them with the dew of the grace of the Sumekami.

Leaving behind the Manai of Hinu, Yoshikohime, who had set forth across the snow of the Tanima, leaping over rivers and traversing the mountain ridges, appeared, followed by Otohiko and Kamehiko, the three of them running in pursuit.

As far as the eye could see, mountain upon mountain stretched forth, and as the day drew toward Tasogare, the Muragarasu sought their roosts.

At the very moment when the Kumataka, the kites, and the Momodori were returning to their respective dwellings, a fierce wind suddenly blew, and being buffeted by it, Aohiko, Natsuhiko, and Tsunehiko fell into a deep ravine, injuring their legs and striking their loins, and while they were writhing in agony, the song of the Sendenshi was heard from afar, echoing against the trees and brushing against the eardrums of the three.

Aohiko said, 'Ah, what a terrible thing has occurred. Blown asunder by a record-breaking gale, we fell into a chasm of a thousand fathoms and I bruised my loins somewhat; for a while I was dazed, but the voice of the song of the Sendenshi faintly echoed in my ears, and with this, I feel as though we are somehow on the right path. Natsuhiko, Tsunehiko, how are you? Have you sustained any injuries?'

Natsuhiko replied, 'The surroundings became pitch black, and the terror of that serpent, when it thrust its great tongue from the vast sky and hung suspended in the mid-heavens—after that, I remember nothing at all, but it seems I have sprained my leg. My heel has begun to ache with a throbbing pain. Where on earth might this place be?'

Aohiko said, 'This is, after all, the bottom of the valley of Mitakeyama. Hey, Tsunehiko, how are you?'

Tsunehiko replied, 'Alas, there is nothing to be said; whether I complain of pain or suffering, if I am to meet with such a cruel fate, I should have heeded the service of Kurohime after all. When we parted at Tanba-mura, that Kurohime glared with great eyes and departed with a loathsome smile; in that smile, there was clearly a silent expression, a look that said, "If you rebel against me, you shall fall to the bottom of the valley and suffer a terrible fate." Ah, ah, my knee joints feel as though they have been dislocated. O Great Deity of the Uranai-kyō, I have truly misunderstood. I beseech you, pray, forgive me.'

Aohiko laughed, 'Ha, ha, ha, you are a man whose spirit is easily shaken. Your faith is like a castle built upon sand, like a lamp before the wind.'

Natsuhiko said, 'This is not a flickering light before the wind, but a change of heart after the wind. Hahaha. I say, Aohiko-sama, is not the Sendenka of Ananai-kyō drawing ever nearer? How would it be if we were to raise a great voice from this place and give a signal?'

Aohiko replied, 'That is surely the party of Yoshikohime-sama. To be blown into such a bottomless valley and to be injured in this manner is unsightly. One must surely attend to one's own injuries oneself. To seek the salvation of others in a cowardly and lingering manner is a disgrace to a man. Rather than that, how would it be to call out and set forth from here?'

Tsunehiko said, 'Even if you say to set forth, my knees have given way, and the use of my compass has become impossible; how then could I walk?'

Natsuhiko said, 'Speak no such folly; I too have pain in my feet, and Aohiko-sama has also sprained the bones of his loins. Do you think one should endure by blowing weak sounds in such a place? In all things, one must conquer by Seishin. Is it possible for a man of seven shaku to be disheartened simply because one or two parts of his body are injured? Look at the frogs and the lizards; even when half their bodies are severed, do they not leap about with composure? In any case, for a human, Seishin is the first priority; come, let us go.'

Tsunehiko said, 'Even if you say such things, I cannot move, can I?'

Natsuhiko said, 'A middle-aged man with a bent waist like me has this much vigor even with an injured foot. Furthermore, what is this? Possessing a young and robust body, do not speak of being unable to move or unable to act.'

Tsunehiko said, 'Hahaha, I am a hero unparalleled under heaven; my faith is like a rock, and it does not move in the slightest. It is the genuine spirit of Japan. It is a thing that does not waver no matter what difficult situation I encounter.'

Natsuhiko said, 'Hmph, you speak well, as one trained by Kurohime. Your faith is a trembling and wavering one, and the only thing that does not move is your inherited means of locomotion. Since it is troublesome to hear you prattle, I shall leave you in the bottom of the valley. Come, Aohiko-sama, this fellow is indeed of the Kurohime party. Shall we abandon him and proceed?'

Aohiko said, 'Shall we pray for Chinkon so that Tsunehiko-sama's feet may rise?'

Natsuhiko said, 'No, that is enough; if I were to perform Chinkon on such a fellow and his feet were to rise, he would again make a swift change to a journey of reversed faith toward Kurohime. For the sake of chastisement, according to the Ofudesaki, if he does not repent, I shall cast him into the bottom of the valley. Let us cast him aside and proceed.'

Tsunehiko said, 'Ah, ha, ha, ha! It is a lie, a lie; there is not even a hair's breadth of injury, not even as much as a cormorant's feather could pierce. I am a Kuwanzentai of Kuwanzenmuketsu net price. I have caused you much Goshinpai in many ways. Come, come, let us proceed; you two, follow behind me and begone.'

Natsuhiko said, 'Hey, Tsunehiko, no matter how much filthy Kotoba you use toward me, it matters not as we are Tomodachi, but to speak in such a manner is a Goburei to Aohiko-sama. Will you not promptly retract your words?'

Tsunehiko said, 'The first part I addressed to Aohiko-sama with Goteinei. What I said about following and being gone, I said to you, Kisama, just as you requested. Ah, ha, ha, ha!'

Natsuhiko said, 'Thanks to you, I have not even a flea-bite's worth of Kega. I have caused you much Goshinpai.'

Aohiko said, 'Ah, I am also Daijiyōbu. Come, come, let us go.'

Tsunehiko said, 'Excuse me, Aohiko-sama, did you not say just Saizen that your loins had given way? Was that a lie? Is it permissible for a Sendenshi to utter a lie, even in jest?'

Aohiko said, 'When I said my loins were about to give way, I meant that the Shinkau of Tsunehiko-sama was about to give way. Had things gone awry, you were on the verge of a Giyakuten back to the Uranai religion, were you not?'

Tsunehiko said, 'Since my Hai into the Ananai-kyō, while I was walking along the exposed ridges of Mitakeyama, there arose a Taihen Ōkaze; my feet were unsteady, and on both sides lay chasms of a thousand fathoms. Thinking, "This is surely not the Ananai-kyō, but an Abunai-kyō," I walked in fear, when suddenly I was swept up by a gust of violent wind, tumbled through the air in Giyakuten many times, and finally landed safely here at the bottom of the valley. Having undergone this much training in Giyakuten, I have had quite enough of it. Please be at ease.'

Natsuhiko said, 'Tsunehiko's request for me to be at ease is also of a dubious sort. Do you not know that you are a fellow whose heart is never settled, and because you speak only lies like a fox, your colleagues call you Kitsunehiko?'

Tsunehiko said, 'Whether I am Kitsunehiko or Tanukihiko, pray do not concern yourself. Come, come, Kitsunehiko knows the ways of the mountains well. The horse shall follow behind the fox.'

Aohiko said, 'Ah, ha, ha, ha!'

The three, taking advantage of the moonlit night, went down on all fours and scrambled up the steep mountainside. Here, the party of Princess Etsuko, gazing at the bright full moon, sat upon the rocks atop the mountain and indulged in idle conversation.

Otohiko said, 'What of today's violent wind? From within the pitch-black clouds, that serpent performed a strange feat. The wind blew and blew and raged. Truly, it was like a scene of devastation, a journey through the Shura-dō. It was because Kamehiko spread such a grand tale about it being the two hundred and tenth day that such a thing occurred. In this world where all things are blessed by the Kototama, one must be cautious with the Kototama, must one not?'

Otohiko: 'What, how could such a thing be? As this is the Shinzan, he may have lost sight of our Itsukau and wandered astray. Depending on the circumstances, he might even be ahead of us. You should not pass such a Danteiteki Handan.'

Kamehiko: 'He is but a lumberjack of a Binbafunin. He is merely spinning mischief. Hahaha. Aohiko, that Aohyōtanyhiko, what in truth is he doing? I know not why, but my heart is troubled, and though it be not the Shōben of a monkey, it weighs upon my mind and I cannot help it.'

'Kamehiko-sama, I thank you for your concern.'
