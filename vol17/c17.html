Chapter 17: 有終の Beauty [628]

有終の美〔628〕

§ 1

Tsunehiko sang: The world hath become Tokoyami, where demons, Orochi, and Magatsukami roam beneath the Ame. The blowing wind is foul with the scent of blood, and the voices of men are filled with lamentation and sorrow. In the Ōeyama, said to be the dwelling of demons, and in the Ihaya of the Mitake, the Kataude of Onikumawake and the proud Onitaka, together with the merciless Arataka, sharpen their claws. They seize the Yobito as soon as they are found, and drag them back into the Ihaya. The Magatsukami torment the people, and beyond that, the Magamitama, leading the Amata Buka of Onigajō, behaves with Wagamama beneath the Ame. Beginning with Onikumawake, and Mukadehime who accompanies him,

Together with the Amata Magami, they use the Kyōri of the Brahman Kyō as a means to deceive the Yobito. Without distinction of man or woman, whenever they have leisure, they seize them and drag them back to their own Sumika, forcing them to become Buka against their will, and their numbers increase day by day. They gather a rabble and boast in vain, startling the world. Though the mountain fortress appears magnificent, its reality is in inverse proportion. When the wind blows, it rattles; the shōji dance and the doors cry out. The pillars begin to groan and creak. At the slightest breeze, the skin of the roof peels away, and the rain leaks in at once. I cannot fathom the minds of those who boast of such a mountain fortress as an impregnable iron castle.

What use is a fortification that looks as if it were patched with the filth of a nose? By the Kototama of Sangyō, the castle is instantly shattered into pieces, and the Magatsukami flee, scattering like spiderlings in all directions, and the absurdity of their crawling escape is pitiful to the eyes of others. Wait, hold, that is but the beginning. I perceive from afar the distress of Onitaka and Arataka, who are in the midst of defending with all their might. It is time to yield; surrender this castle, whose fortune hath run out, cleanly and completely. It is a wise method to surrender while it yet possesses both flower and fruit, for the Senden-shi who saves men,

Speaks not of clumsy things that would be of no benefit to either side. Come, how is it? Come, how is it? Let me hear your answer, let me hear it quickly. Whether the morning sun shines or is clouded, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if it should take a thousand years, as long as the Kototama of Sangyō continues, I shall press the attack. Water attacks and fire attacks are but foolishness; earthquakes, thunder, and the chariot of fire, and the great flood are but the beginning of the evening. More terrifying than those is my mouth. It must be vexing, but it is better to yield and surrender honestly. The owl that plotted in the evening hath missed its midnight meal, and it is pitiful to behold its face. The Kami-naohi who created this world, and the Ō-naohi of vast heart.

Only in all things, we of the Ananahi Kami's teachings, reflecting upon the deep grace of seeing and hearing anew through Nahohi, O ye retainers of Onikumawake, open the doors of your hearts and swiftly surrender the cavern of ugliness. In the passing world, there are no demons. To live in weeping is but one life; to live in anger is but one life. Live in laughter at the Demon's Castle; unto the gate of laughter, fortune shall come. The demon quickly changes into a Buddha; the Buddha is promoted unto a Kami. The Kami appears upon the surface and separates good from evil. Before the time of the reconstruction and separation of this world arrives, O ye retainers of Onikumawake, who were born as the children of the Kami, make haste.

Praying to the Kami only in times of helplessness; now that it has come to this, it is the hundredth year. Surrender the mountain fortress quickly. This Tsunehiko shall take heed. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, Mitama Sachihahe Mashimase.

Natsuhiko: 'What is this, Tsunehiko? Thou hast greatly mocked my Kototama, but what is thy Kototama? Is it not a boneless song, long and slippery like a great serpent, crawling and lingering?'

Tsunehiko: 'It is a settled matter. Since the opponent possesses the spirit of a great serpent, I have launched a long and slippery attack from this side as well.'

Kamehiko: 'It was a Kototama that could be understood fundamentally. Even the formidable Onikumawake shall surely be astounded by this and roll about laughing, clutching his belly. Ahahaha!'

Bakō stood upon the high railing of the cavern and began to chant while making strange gestures.

Departing from the flowery capital, a pair consisting of a horse and a deer, accompanying Princess Murasaki, intending to visit the Mana-i of Hinu, while coming from afar, the Demon-Hawk appeared as the child of the god of Brahmanism; by the two fellows of the fierce-hearted Wild-Hawk, being smoothly deceived by their glib tongues, the two persons, the horse and the deer, were made fools of and cast into the cavern of Mount Mitake. While living in weeping and anger, at that very time, the Princess Etsuko, accompanied by the two intermediaries, Lord Otohikō and Kamehiko, entered into the cavern. Fortuitously I was rescued, and suddenly changing into a believer of the Ananahi Kami's teachings, on this joyful day, joining the Kototama battle of the Demon's Castle where demons dwell, I shall stab and stab and keep on stabbing the belly of Onikumawake—oops, wait, that was wrong. Whatever the heart may hold, the outward appearance is still the Demon's Castle; Princess Etsuko commanded me to remain an ally of the demons. In this prearranged sham battle of Kototama, without distinction between enemy and ally, performing the mission of peaceful persuasion, the Ananahi Kami's teachings shall be spread throughout the Yamato islands of Shikishima, and even further, the Land of Fresh Rice Ears of the Abundant Reed Plains, the eighty countries and eighty islands of the land; the plum blossoms shall open all at once.
