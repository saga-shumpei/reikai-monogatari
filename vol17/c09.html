Chapter 9: Great 逆転 [620]

大逆転〔620〕

§ 1

Heisuke and his family of three, having made their pilgrimage to the auspicious Hōza of Toyokunihime, who manifested upon the Manai-ga-hara of Hinu, trod upon the sacred ground with hearts emboldened and returned with spirits soaring.

Heeding not the snow-laden path, they dragged their staves and at last arrived at the threshold of their Abaraya.

Heisuke said, 'Thanks to the divine grace, our Gekau has been accomplished without incident; come now, Onara, boil the water quickly, that we may wash our feet and rest at our ease.'

Osetsu replied, 'Nay, nay, Grandfather, I shall boil the water; Grandmother, I pray you, do take your rest.'

Onara said, 'Nay, nay, you have been confined for so long within such a dark cavern that I fear the smoke of the fire shall harm your eyes; I, this old woman, shall kindle it, so come now, Oyaji-dono, pray draw the water.'

Osetsu said, 'Grandfather, I shall draw the water; I pray you, do take your rest.'

Heisuke said, 'Nay, nay, your Karada is frail; I, this old man, shall draw it for you, so until the water boils, pray, do rest yourself in peace.'

The old man swung the Hanetsurube unsteadily, back and forth, time and again, and at last drew up the water, half-filling the bucket, drawing and transferring, drawing and transferring again.

'Come now, Onara, the water is drawn; boil it quickly; ah, when one grows old, one cannot even draw water properly.'

Onara said, 'It is said that in old age one should follow one's children; why do you not let Osetsu draw it? That is why they say you are always headstrong; if you were to break the bones of your waist, what would you do? It would not be your Nangi alone, but a Nangi for both this old woman and Osetsu as well.'

Heisuke said, 'Eh, do not be so Yakama; no matter how old I grow, I shall not let the water level be lowered.'

So saying, while carrying the bucket filled to the brim, he slipped upon the slick stone of the garden and fell backward with a thud.

'Ouch, ugh, ugh.'

With that groan, he lay prostrate in the center of the garden.

Onara and Osetsu were struck with terror, their minds in a state of frenzy, and they rushed to the head and feet of the old man.

Onara cried, 'Grandfather, oh, oh, pray, regain your senses.'

Osetsu also wept bitterly, crying, 'Grandfather, Grandfather.'

Onara said, 'Ah, this will not do at all; come now, Osetsu, since it has come to this, there is no choice but to beseech the Kami; I hear you know the songs of that which is called Sangokyō, so I pray you, sing quickly and restore the breath of Grandfather.'

§ 2

Osetsu said, 'Yes, yes, Shiyōchi. Grandmother, please attend to Grandfather quickly. One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, hundred, thousand, ten thousand.'

As she repeated this Sanshikuwai, Heisuke finally breathed again and said, 'Ah, what a terrible thing it was. The Ten was blue, and the mountains and fields were tinged with green. Various beautiful flowers bloomed and scented the air until there was no room left, and beautiful Ōtori and Kotori, whose beauty cannot be described, sang with cool voices, chanting of the spring of Tokoyo. As I proceeded along a Tanbomichi, which was a Hanamushiro spread across Sokora, so beautiful and Kirei that it defies description, I saw a Ritsupa Mihe tower in the distance. The tower was Koganezuku from top to bottom, and Nichirinsama shone upon it, filling me with a Saukuwai feeling that cannot be expressed. I felt as if I wished to go there as quickly as Ichiji, and as I Kakeda with all my might, leaning upon my staff, I heard the voice of Onara faintly from the distant, distant mountains behind, followed suddenly by the Kaai voice of Osetsu entering my ears. Ah, having come to such a beautiful place with such Setsukaku, why does this Bakamusume call me back? Ah, what a pitiful creature. However, I am not a Heisuke who would turn back just because my daughter, whom I have not seen for a long time, called me, even if she had called me persistently. But because the daughter called with such a Kaai and sorrowful voice, I stopped for a moment and listened, and someone, I know not whom, began to count my age, one, two, three, and finally called out a hundred, a thousand, and ten thousand. Ah, I have come to a splendid place I have never seen before, but could it be that I am traveling through the Shinkai? I am still only seventy, and one does not come to the Shinkai at such an age; I thought that one cannot come to such a splendid place without suffering in the Genkai for a hundred thousand years. The moment I thought this, I realized I had been lying fallen in a garden full of water. Ah, how pitiful, how pitiful. If it were like this, it would have been better not to have turned back; must I now suffer for another breath in this Shaba?'

Onara said, 'Hey, hey, Master Oyaji, what are you saying? We are a couple who swore to be together for two or three generations to come. Are you going to leave this old woman alone in the Shaba and go to Gokuraku by yourself?'

Heisuke said, 'Ah, that is true. In such a splendid place, you had slipped from my mind before I knew it. However, I had fainted and was seeing a dream. It is troublesome for you to take a dream story and get so serious and angry, is it not? Ah, is that Osetsu? Please, both of you, attend to my body, lay out my bedding, and let me rest, for the condition of my waist feels strange.'

§ 3

Onara and Osetsu, while shedding tears, prepared the Nedoko and, having performed the Sauji of his feet with haste, took Heisuke in their arms and laid him to rest upon the bed; from that time forth, Heisuke suffered from Hatsunetsu, and day after day he spoke Usagoto, until after the passing of about Hantsuki, he finally Kiiu.

Onara and Osetsu clung to the Shigai and Gaukifu, and having at last completed the Nobe no okuri, the two of them, as if it were a Nitsukuwa, would break off branches of Tokihagi three times, morning, noon, and night, and carry water to offer, seeking to console the spirit of the departed; the old woman Onara, having been predeceased by her husband, lived out her days of little Omoshiro, relying upon her daughter Osetsu for strength.

However, from the time of Heisuke's Kiiu, Osetsu's Shintai grew Masumasu Yaseotoro, and she became bedridden once more, even beginning to speak Usagoto; unable to endure this, the old woman began, with Itsushiyōkenmei, a Hadashimairi to Manai-ga-hara, and when she had climbed to the summit of the Hijiyamatauge, the aforementioned Kurohime, with her Shiwakuchiyagaho painted thick like Shirakabe, stood blocking her path in the company of two Meurei maidens, Teruko and Kiyoko, who put the very flowers to shame.

Kurohime spoke: 'Ho there, Grandmother, art thou not the same old woman who passed through here before with the parent and child, the three of you? Thou art again, and yet again, deluded by the teachings of the Akugami of Susanoo-no-Mikoto, and art making a pilgrimage to Mount Manai, art thou not?'

Onara replied: 'Aye, aye, it is even so; my husband, upon whom I relied for strength, had no sooner returned from his pilgrimage to the gods than he collapsed in the garden, and that being the cause, he suffered night and day, until at last he became a person of the other world; oh, oh, oh,' and she broke down in tears.

Kurohime smiled with a sneer and said: 'It is as I thought, it is as I thought; because thou didst make a pilgrimage to such a place, casting aside my kindness, thy precious life was taken by the Akugami of the Mizu no Mitama; pray, place thy hand upon thy breast and consider well; the purpose of a pilgrimage to the gods is surely not to collapse and die, but to live long for a thousand or ten thousand years, to bear children unto grandchildren, great-grandchildren, and great-great-grandchildren, and to pray that thou mayest live for a hundred or two hundred years; and what is this, that upon returning from a pilgrimage to the gods, he should collapse—if it were upon the tatami it might be another matter, but to stretch out in the middle of the garden like a squashed frog, and to die in such a state, even covered in water—what manner of faith is this? That is why I so kindly forbade thee; in the first place, thy husband, though his name be like a fart, was quite headstrong, so the retribution was immediate; if thou art sluggish, thy daughter too shall have her life taken, and in the end, thou shalt also die; no matter how much thou mayest weep and wail, regret after the fact is of no avail; well, as for the dead husband, it cannot be helped, so that thou alone mayest live long, repent clearly and completely, cast the god of the Sangōkyō into the river, and worship the true Ushitora no Konjin of the magnificent, magnificent Uranai-kyō; if thou wilt persevere, thou shalt achieve great things; thy daughter, too, was of a pale countenance, and by now, like a three-year-old child suffering from convulsions, she is likely crying out and shrieking from morning until night.'

Onara said, 'Yes, yes, as you say, Oji-sama, but I know not why, my precious grand-daughter’s Karada wastes away day by day; like ice melting in the Hinata, even the sound of her breath grows thin. I, too, having been preceded in death by Oyaji-dono, find that my grand-daughter, whom I deemed my strength, has fallen into a Taibiyō of which I know not when she shall die; furthermore, my ears have grown dull, my waist is bent, and my feet move not as they should. Ah, if it be thus, it were better had I died together with Oyaji-dono, and so, Yūbe, I went alone and secretly to the grave and prayed with all my Itsushiyōkenmei, saying, "Oyaji-dono, I beseech you, come quickly to call me; I have grown weary of this Ukiyo, and I wish to live with you in the world beyond." It seems that even in death one retains one’s Shiyōnen, for the figure of Oyaji-dono appeared suddenly from within the grave and said with a loathsome face, "Are you Onara, my Niyōbau? You have spoken well; if that be so, I shall now take you with me to the Meido." I, too, suddenly grew loath to die, and crying out in Bitsukuri, I stumbled and fell as I returned to my house, saying, "Not this time, nor the next, nor the next; after ten, twenty, thirty, or even a Hiyakunen has passed, then come to fetch me." Upon returning, I found my daughter Osetsu, I know not why, possessed by Aohiko, Aohiko, and uttering a loathsome voice. At the grave of Oyaji-dono, I was shown a pale face amidst a blue fire, and having escaped with my Inochi, I find my only daughter delirious with fever, groaning and possessed by Aohiko, Aohiko; how can I endure this? Ah, ah, un, un.' So saying, she collapsed in tears.

Kurohime said, 'What, does your daughter Osetsu call out for Aohiko, Aohiko? That is a grave matter. You must listen to what I say and become a follower of the Uranai Kyō; then, there may be one chance in a thousand that your daughter’s illness will be healed. Though you say you wish to die, now that the moment is here, you still fear death, do you not? Have faith in the God of the Uranai Kyō, who grants longevity for a thousand or ten thousand years. I shall now go to your house, and if the God of the Sangokyō is enshrined there, I shall cast it out and enshrine the Great God of the Uranai Kyō for you.'

Onara said, 'Nay, nay, the God of the Sangokyō is not yet enshrined; I am merely practicing faith because it is said that my daughter Osetsu was saved. It is most fortunate that you have come, and if you will enshrine the deity for me, my daughter’s illness will surely be healed, and I, too, shall attain longevity; therefore, I beseech you to do so without a moment’s delay. Un, un, un (sobbing).'

Kurohime said, 'Very well, I shall enshrine you, but as the Kamisama is most magnificent and not to be treated as one who carries luggage, I shall speak earnestly to Osetsu-dono and have her renounce the Ananai-kyō before I enshrine you; come now, go and worship at the throne of the waters of Manai in Hinu, and upon your return, lie down in the garden in the shape of the character for great... Ho ho ho.'

Onara replied, 'Nay, nay, how could I, how could I? After hearing your words, who would ever go to worship at Manai? At that time, I had listened to your words and was inclined to repent, but because the old-fashioned Oyaji-dono and my daughter Osetsu would not listen, I had no choice but to go; had I done as you said then, it would have been well, but Oyaji-dono has made an irreparable blunder, ah, ah, ah.'

Kurohime said, 'Come, old woman, if your Ketsushin is made, then there is no help for it; I shall make a Tokubetsu-taigū and go on a Shiyutsuchiyō for you. You are a person of a very fine type, and it is a rare thing indeed to have me come to you Chiyokusetsu.'

Onara replied, 'Yes, yes, it is too Motsutai, I am Arigata-gozaimasu; thanks to Kagesama, Osetsu's Biyōki shall surely reach Honpuku; Nanibun, I humbly beseech your favor.'

Kurohime said, 'Ah, to become a living Kamisama is a busy thing; even if it be but one daughter, there is no doubt that all are divided spirits of the gods of heaven and earth; I shall manifest a heart of impartial benevolence and supreme love to save her. Come now, Teru-sama, Kiyo-sama, you must follow along with me; now, Onara-sama, are you happy?'

Onara replied, 'Yes, yes, Arigatō-gozaimasu; in that case, I shall guide you from here.'

Kurohime said, 'Very well, I shall go for you; you are a very fortunate person, and now you have surely given up all thoughts of Mount Manai, have you not?'

Onara replied, 'Yes, yes, who would ever go to such a place as Mount Manai?'

So saying, she went on ahead. Kurohime, with a composed and triumphant smile, accompanied by the two daughters, proceeded in high spirits, pointing toward the humble hut of the old woman of Tanba Village.
