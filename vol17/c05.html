Chapter 5: 誘惑婆 [616]

誘惑婆〔616〕

§ 1

Heisuke, together with Onara and Osetsu, proceeds trudgingly and with a sense of desolation along the rugged mountain path.

Illuminated by the spring sun, the slope on the sunny side is melting its snow, revealing the surface of the mountain.

To such a place, an old woman clad in a straw raincoat and hat, accompanied by two young maidens, comes and blocks their path, and the old woman says, 'Well, well, grandfather, grandmother, where are you going, taking these pale and thin maidens with you?'

Heisuke replies, 'Yes, yes, I know not who you may be, but I am going to offer thanks to the magnificent Kami who has recently appeared at Manai-ga-hara; pray, step aside from there.'

The old woman says, 'You said you are going to Manai-ga-hara to offer thanks; what on earth are you going to do by going to such a place? That Kami is an Akugami.'

Heisuke says, 'Whether it be an Akugami or whatever, do not concern yourself with it; Shinkau is Jiiu, and it is by my own heart that I worship. I know not what old woman you are, but to think you would try to disparage the Shinkau of others and come out to such a mountain to obstruct us—what a strange Monozuki you are, indeed.'

The old woman says, 'Oh, what troubled people you are; it is truly Kawai. It is a world where good medicine is bitter to the mouth, and one is disliked for speaking of things that bring Rieki. You, if you intend to go further from here, you shall have no Inochi; will you perform Shinjin even if you cast away your Inochi?'

Heisuke says, 'What person would have the Monozuki to perform Shinjin by casting away their Inochi? It is for the desire of long life that I go on this pilgrimage. When one speaks of the Kami, Toyokuni-hime of Manai-ga-hara, she is a truly magnificent Kami of Inochi. Why do you not also go and worship to receive her blessings? It is a world where one is never satisfied no matter how long one lives. Come, come, let us go, let us go.'

The old woman says, 'Now, now, grandfather, take a rest; if you step a little to the side here, there is a small house, and that is my place of ascetic practice. I have suddenly prepared this place of practice, thinking to save the departed spirits who come forth, dazed by the Mizu-no-mitama like you. To dislike without tasting is not to be trusted; now, in any case, follow this old woman.'

Heisuke says, 'Hey, Onara, what shall we do? As this old woman says, my legs are quite tired. Shall I take a short rest and listen to what she has to say?'

Onara says, 'Grandfather, it is because you are like that that I say you are a problem; you are immediately taken in by the glib tongue of others, and can Shinjin be performed with such an attitude? Whenever I offer my opinion, you say it is the meddlesome heart of an old woman and refuse to listen, and secondly, you persist in your ways; but for once, listen to what this old woman says. You, too, have an old man's heart that prevails too much. I know not what old woman you are, but thinking you are a little younger than I, you are already becoming eager, but I dislike it, I dislike it; I will by no means go to such a place. Rather than that, we must quickly go on a pilgrimage to Manai-sama and offer our thanks. Come, come, Osetsu, let us go, let us go.'

§ 2

Heisuke said, 'Old woman, even if you say so, Osetsu, how about you? Do you not have a mind to draw near and see?'

Osetsu replied, 'Grandfather, let us proceed directly without dallying by the wayside.'

The old woman said, 'This is truly a matter of great impropriety, whether it be the old woman or the young maiden; is there such a thing as disobeying the words of one's husband or parents? In all likelihood, you must be a believer of Ananai-kyō.'

Osetsu replied, 'It is true that I am a believer of Ananai-kyō. Grandfather and Grandmother are non-believers. I was abducted by the general of the Brahmanism of Ōeyama, confined within a cavern, and suffered in agony. There, the merciful Kamisama of Ananai-kyō appeared in a dream and taught me a Sendenka. As I chanted that Sendenka, the evildoer soon underwent Kaishin and came to rescue me. Though one may speak of the nobility of any Kamisama in the world, there is no Kamisama as merciful as the Kamisama of Ananai-kyō. I am now on my way to offer thanks, for the Kamisama Toyokunihime, who protects Ananai-kyō, has this time made a Goshiyutsugen at Manai-ga-hara. I pray you, do not hinder me on my way to Kamimai; if you have words to speak, I shall listen to them at my leisure on my way back.'

The old woman said, 'Come, that is precisely what is unacceptable. Ananai-kyō is led by that fellow called Susanoo-no-Mikoto, who has been cast out of Takamagahara. Is it not because he is evil that he was driven out of such a splendid place? You must be the same. Who would drive out their own gentle siblings? Even if a wayward son who makes his parents weep, makes his siblings weep, exhausts every limit of mischief, and causes trouble not only to the neighborhood but to all around him, is beloved, one cannot keep him in the house for the sake of the world, can one? It is the same matter; it is to the extent that Izanagi-no-Ōkami and his elder sister Kamisama lost all affection for him and, saying it was unpardonable to the world, cast out one with whom the bond of siblings could not be severed. He is a creature who is neither vinegar nor konjac. To think that you would stray into Ananai-kyō, where that Susanoo-no-Mikoto wields his baton—what ignorant fools you are! If you wish to hear the truth of Ananai-kyō, I, this old woman, shall explain it to you thoroughly. Come, come, no matter what you say, I shall take you with me; come along, come along.'

Osetsu replied, 'Even if you, Grandfather and Grandmother, say you will go, I alone cannot go.'

§ 3

The old woman said, 'Ah, what an uncomprehending girl you are. Behold, these two Kirei maidens have Natsutoku in their hearts regarding what I say, and they serve me with Chiujitsu Asayufu. In this Jisetsu where new ways for women are Haya, why do you possess such an old Atama? That is to be expected, for having been Oshiko into a hole in the rocks for about an Ichinen without seeing the Seken, you surely do not understand the Yōsu of the world. The world has progressed Zuibun. Pull yourself together and listen to what this old woman says. Though I may appear thus, I am Kurohime, a Kuraunin who has accumulated Keiken in all manner of things since my youth. Without Kurau, the flower of sincerity will not bloom.'

Osetsu replied, 'Your words may be Gomotsuto, but I must decline your unnecessary Osetsukai, no matter what you command; I pray you, cease this unnecessary Osetsukai.'

Kurohime exclaimed, 'What a strong-willed woman you are. You have a face like an Aobeutan that has been made to inhale a fart, and you are a Komusume who chirps Rikutsu with such tiresome persistence. Nay, you are a Garakutamusume. Tell me, Oji-sama, I fear for your future if you, in your old age, continue to harbor such a mischievous girl. I shall educate you so that you may become a Majime and Honto Mitama, and so that you and your wife may find Anshin. Come, come, follow me to that place. As you can see, these two maidens are graceful; this is entirely because my education is excellent.'

Heisuke retorted, 'Be silent! Whether my daughter becomes a tomboy or whatever she may become, we have no need of your care. Come, Osetsu, if we remain involved with such a wretched old hag, the day will darken. Come, let us go, let us go.'

Onara said, 'Listen, Oji-sama, that is not the way to speak. Why do you not listen to what she has to say? It might even be for the sake of your afterlife.'

At this moment, two propagandists approached from the front, singing a song of propagation. Osetsu, gaining strength from this voice, joined in the song and, clapping her hands, began to dance:

'Though the morning sun may shine or be obscured, Though the moon may wax or wane, The power of sincerity shall save the world.'

Kurohime, caught between the songs of propagation from before and behind, cried out:

Kurohime said, 'Aie, aie! At this very critical moment, those Garakutamusume propagandists have come again, and my head aches. They utter such nation-destroying voices; ah, my chest is in pain. Come, Oji-sama and Oba-sama, it will be a disaster if we are discovered by such wretches. You, mischievous girl, follow quickly behind me.'

§ 4

Onara said, 'Come, come, Heisuke-sama, let us follow behind this person, for it is said that a fearsome being is about to appear.'

Heisuke replied, 'Having heard the words of Osetsu, I learned of the existence of something called Ananai-kyō; it is a voice that sounds like that of a Kamisama, and upon hearing it, I feel the Kamisama of Manai-ga-hara becoming all the more Arigata.'

Kurohime exclaimed, 'Ei, ei, what a hopeless, senile lot you are; when I think to save you through sincerity alone, you struggle with all your might to flee toward the path of Horobi. Aa, how well it is said that the sentient beings without affinity are difficult to Saido. Ee, how unpleasant; the Sendenka is drawing near, step by step. Come, come, Kiyosama, Terosama, quickly, quickly!'

So saying, she urged them on, and they hid themselves swiftly in the thicket of trees nearby.

The two Sendenshi who appeared upon this scene were Otohiko and Aohiko.

'Ya, you are the grandfather, grandmother, and daughter from Tanbamura, are you not? We hear that last night, Ihakō, Kankō, and Ichikō were greatly indebted to you for your Osewa. Yoshikohime-sama is waiting for you with great anticipation; come, come, let us go.'

Heisuke replied, 'No, no, how could that be? It is a truly humble house, and to be thanked is rather heart-rending; it is by the grace of the Kamisama that, for the first time in a year, I have been able to see the face of my precious, precious Osetsu. Come, come, Onara, Osetsu, these are the messengers of the Kamisama; come, come, will you not offer your thanks at once?'

Onara said, 'You are the messengers of the Kamisama; I have no words, but I am Arigata,' and she began to tear up.

Osetsu said, 'We were saved by the grace of the Kamisama; we humbly place ourselves in your care.'

Otohiko asked, 'Just now, a human shadow seemed to appear here, and I heard a rustling sound; where have they gone?'

Osetsu replied, 'Yes, an old woman called Kurohime appeared, and together with two beautiful young women, she told us, the parent and child, not to make a pilgrimage to Manai-ga-hara, but to come this way instead, blocking our path and causing us distress. Just then, we heard your Sendenka, and... at last, they hid themselves somewhere. Aa, you have come to a good place, and the three of us, parent and child, have been saved. This, too, must be the guidance of the Kamisama.'

Otohiko said, 'Ya, what are you saying? Kurohime has appeared? What a persistent wretch she is; I know not where she has gone.'

Osetsu said, 'Just now, she went slinking down through this grove.'

Otohiko said, 'What an incorrigible wretch she is. In any case, let us make our pilgrimage quickly; Yoshikohime-sama is waiting with great anticipation for your arrival, and we have come to welcome you. Come, let us go.'

With that, the five of them set out with vigor, treading their way toward the west, and further west.
