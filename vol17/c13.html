Chapter 13: 紫姫 [624]

紫姫〔624〕

§ 1

Bending low at the waist, they advanced some twenty or thirty ken through a narrow, dark hole, until they reached a point where a somewhat wider tunnel, illuminated by light, pierced through the path ahead.

Murasakihime spoke: 'Come, from here the ceiling is high; pray, straighten your backs and walk. This gankutsu is bored with tunnels in a cross-shape for a length of about three chō, and here and there are square chambers of rock. The places where Arataka and Onitaka dwell are built especially wide.'

Kamehiko replied: 'Ha, the demon fellow is doing things with such flair. It is a relief that he is away at Onigajō, but if we dally and he happens to return midway, he might seal that narrow entrance tight, or perhaps smoke us out with green pine needles; that would be a disaster. Once we have explored to a reasonable degree, it is best to retreat from such a dangerous zone, is it not, Otohikosama?'

Otohiko said: 'Indeed, if the single entrance is blocked, we shall be like rats in a bag.'

Murasakihime answered: 'Nay, nay, pray do not worry. There are two or three emergency exits open. I know them well, so please be at ease.'

Kamehiko said: 'Ah, is that so? Then I am relieved.'

So saying, he followed behind Murasakihime, swinging his arms and striding with wide steps.

Kamehiko exclaimed: 'Ha, many little demons are sleeping here! Hey, hey, you little demons, will you not wake up? It is an inspection by the party of Etsukohime, the propagandist of Sangōkyō. Come, come, the roll call and inspection begin now.'

Five or six men were startled and rose up, saying: 'We know not who you are, but unfortunately our commander is absent, and we cannot attend to you at all. Pray, rest yourselves at your leisure.'

Kamehiko declared: 'Even without your invitation, we shall rest. Yet, for what purpose are you keeping watch? Otohiko and Kamehiko, who surpass the demons in prowess, have crept deep into this place, and this gankutsu has reached a tragic state where there is no path left but surrender. To watch for the commander's absence, to drink and feast upon his treasured sake, and to sleep like sharks, unaware of what lies before or behind, like fools—this is most insolent. Now that you have caught the eye of this Kamehiko, I shall show no mercy. In place of Onitaka and Arataka, I dismiss you from this moment forth. Go wherever you please.'

The little demon cried: 'Hey, Tanshu, Kinshu, Genshu, Enshu, Banshu, this is a disaster! We are dismissed as of this moment!'

Tanshu replied: 'That is why I always said, do not drink sake; if you drink sake, you will shrink. I, too, followed the child of Kurohime of the Uranai-kyō for two or three years, and it was said that in Sangōkyō there are truly great heroes who use the arts of divine transformation and wonders, and who will appear anywhere without regard for their lives. Truly, Sangōkyō is not to be taken lightly. Since Sangōkyō has no holes of its own, they must be searching for and taking even such demon holes as this.'

Kamehiko: 'Hark, you Tanshiu, what is it you say? Will you not retreat in a manner befitting the divine, as I have commanded?'

Tanshiu: 'Art thou not a Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō? Is it not thy duty to save even the Akugami, the Akunin, the demons, the Gaki, and the Mushikera? Even if we have become the Kobun of demons, we are originally the magnificent Wakemitama of the Ōkamisama of Tenchi; if thou darest to touch us with even a single finger, try it if thou wilt, for thou shalt be cast headlong into the land of the roots and the bottom of the earth for the Daizai of Tensokuihan.'

Kamehiko: 'Ha, ha, ha, ha! This fellow, he speaks with much Rikutsu. Tell me, Tanshiu, what is thy place of Umare?'

Tanshiu: 'As for me? It is a settled matter, even without my speaking; I am of the land.'

Kamehiko: 'Though thou sayest the land, there are Takusan of them. Of which land art thou?'

Tanshiu: 'Eh, what a man of poor Atama thou art. Canst thou serve as a Sendenshi with such wit? If I am in the land of Tanba and say I am of the land, is it not settled that it is Tanba?'

Kamehiko: 'Ha, ha, ha, ha! And where in Tanba?'

Tanshiu: 'It is Mount Mitake in Tanba.'

Kamehiko: 'Mount Mitake is settled. From what village and from what person wert thou born?'

Tanshiu: 'If the village of my Umare were known, or my parents were identified, who would be here as a Kobun of demons? Thou, too, speakest of things that are quite beyond understanding.'

Otohiko: 'Come, come, Kamehiko, shall we not set forth on our exploration? If we dally, the leaders may return.'

Kamehiko: 'It is the return of the leaders that I await; I cannot go forth and boast before those who are but the underlings of a bandit chief, for it is a matter of no spirit, and I am troubled by it.'

Tanshiu: 'Pray, Kamehiko-san, do not trouble thyself. A moment ago, I, this Tanshiu, gave a signal toward the castle of the demons; even now, leading brave generals and fierce soldiers like clouds and mist, the great commander Onikumawake is marching toward us with banners flying and drums beating. Well, thy life is but for a moment; blow thy conch shell at thy leisure and enjoy the fleeting heart.'

Kamehiko: 'Ha, ha, ha, ha! What is it thou art spewing? Even if a thousand or ten thousand Akugami were to come, I would cause them to vanish in an instant by the unique spiritual emission of the Ananai-kyō. Will they not attack with all haste? Ah, how I long for them to arrive!'

Tanshiu: 'Ha, ha, ha, ha! I know not of thy strength, but thou blowest thy horn quite loudly.'

§ 3

Kamehiko said, 'It is decided; I am Kamehiko, who has earned the nickname Nihiyakutōka. If you dally, I shall blow your Kotsupaoni up into the Chiuten with a single breath of my nostrils.'

Tanshiu replied, 'In truth, I am one called Tamahiko, who appeared at Manai-ga-hara. Because the Akugami are so rampant, I received the Gomeirei of Toyokunihime, disguised myself as a Kōni, and slipped into this Ihaya to investigate the Jiyōsei of the Akuma. The two servants who accompanied Murasakihime have been carefully hidden in a certain place by my Hakarahi; I shall present them to you before long.'

Murasakihime said, 'Ho, what is it you say? You have hidden those servants? If that be so, why were the Kimono of the two stained Chimidoro? Pray, tell me the Wake for this.'

Tanshiu laughed, 'Ahaha, it is because, by my Hakarahi, I dyed their Kimono with the blood of a wild boar I had caught, and showed them to the commanders Arataka and Onitaka, saying, "I have tortured them to death in this manner." If you have any doubt, I shall show them to you now.'

Murasakihime said, 'I beseech you, let me see them as soon as possible.'

Tanshiu said, 'Hey, all you fellows, as I have always instructed you, you will obey me in all things, will you not?'

Kō, Otsu, Hei, and Tei bowed their heads all at once, saying, 'Yes, yes, we understand.'

Tanshiu said, 'Good, good; that is how my retainers should be. Now, Kinshū and Enshū, you two, bring those two here.'

'We obey,' they replied.

Kinshū and Enshū hurriedly hid themselves beyond the Ihaya, but after a short while, they returned accompanied by two men, saying, 'Now, Master, we have brought the two guests.'

Murasakihime said, 'Oh, you are Shika and Uma! How fortunate that you have remained safe.'

Shika and Uma both prostrated themselves at once, overcome with tears of joy.

Etsukohime said, 'Now, now, you who are called Tanshiu-sama, I thought from the moment I first saw you that you were a strange person, but is it true that you have come by the Gomeirei of the deity Toyokunihime?'

Tanshiu replied, 'Yes, yes, it is the truth, the absolute and genuine truth.'

Kamehiko said, 'Etsukohime-sama, Otohikosama, we came out with such vigor to explore this Ihaya, but it is like breaking wind in the darkness. Since there is no challenge left in such a place, how would it be to have someone show us a secret passage, so that we may traverse the rugged mountain paths and head toward Onigajō? As there is no such word as retreat in the Sangōkyō, we cannot turn back the way we came; we must escape through any secret hole we can find.'

Tanshiu said, 'In this Gankutsu there are three escape passages; one is the path leading to Onigajō, one is the path leading to Ōeyama, and one is the passage to go down to the valley to draw water. To which place shall I guide you?'

Kamehiko replied, 'If it is all the same, please guide us toward Onigajō.'

Tanshiu said, 'I understand.'

He stood in the lead and, the moment he pushed with one hand against the rock that blocked their way, it opened with a clatter. Looking out, it was the summit of Mitake, a place with a fine view in all directions, Shihau and Hatsupau.

Kamehiko said, 'Now, it is safe. Everyone, shall we gaze upon this Fūkei and take a Hitoyasu? How about you, Murasakihime? Let us accompany one another. There can be no joy in remaining in such a Gankutsu in Chitsukiyo forever. With Otohiko, whom you love, well...'

Murasakihime said, 'Ho, ho, ho, ho.'

She hid her face with her sleeve and Utsumu.

Ichidō sat down in a place with a beautiful view on the mountain and, gazing in all directions, indulged in idle talk. Just then, a mass of black clouds appeared in the southwestern sky, and as they watched, it expanded until it seemed as if ink had been poured across the whole sky, wrapping all directions in darkness until one could not distinguish even a foot ahead. A biting cold wind blew, fierce enough to send Ganseki flying and to fell trees.

The four, Kinshū, Genshū, Enshū, and Banshū, cried 'Ah!' and were swept away by the wind, hiding their figures in the dark bottom of the valley.

Otohiko said, 'Oh, this has become a terrible situation. A situation has emerged as if the Yamata no Orochi is attacking. Murasakihime, I shall hold your hand. If we dally, we shall be blown away. Hey, Kamehiko, you take charge of protecting Etsukohime. Tanshiu, it is a trouble, but please follow me alone.'

Tanshiu said, 'Yes, yes, I understand everything. However, would it not be better to rest here for a while longer? From here on, there are terrible precipices; it would be disastrous if you were to lose your footing in the darkness.'

Kamehiko said, 'What, what does it matter? Whatever happens is the will of the Kami. Can a Senden-shi fulfill his duty if he is daunted by a wind such as this?'

Tanshiu said, 'You have great strength of spirit, but can you see where you are stepping?'

Kamehiko said, 'That, I cannot see for a moment, for I have no eyes in my feet.'

The black clouds parted to the left and right, and a giant dragon body appeared abruptly; it revealed its half-body in the center, opened its great mouth, hung over the heads of the five, and thrust out a red tongue about one jō long, attempting to lick them.

Otohiko said, 'Aha, what a Yōki man you are. Shall we have you show us one more splendid piece of magic while you are at it?'

Kamehiko replied, 'Do not be so clamorous. The serpent is currently in the dressing room applying heavy makeup; pray wait a while. Next, I shall hang before your eyes a thing of most extraordinary nature.'

The wind ceased abruptly, the black clouds covering the heavens cleared away entirely, and the sunlight began to shine with brilliance.

Kamehiko cried, 'Come, come, it is the opening of the Heavenly Rock Cave. If it pleases you all, in place of applause, I pray you recite the Amatsu Norito.'

Thereupon, Princess Yoshikohime, Otohiko, Kamehiko, Murasakihime, Tanshu, Shikokō, and Umakō, all together, turned toward the western heavens, joined their hands, recited the Amatsu Norito, and finished singing the propaganda song; then the party proceeded gracefully, pointing toward the south, traveling along the mountains, and aiming for Onigajo which towered above the clouds.
