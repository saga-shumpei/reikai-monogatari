Chapter 7: 枯尾 Flower [618]

枯尾花〔618〕

§ 1

The number of the Mikata, within the Gankutsu pierced into the Makutsugahara of Ōeyama, saw Kurohime, while crossing the slope of Isoji, like the late-blooming flowers upon the lonely treetops of an autumn field where even the teeth have fallen. Upon this world, she applied Kurōrushi thickly to her hair, frosted with the autumn of age, like a Nihakazuku Yufugarasu. Raising her voice, withered and hoarse, she cried 'Kawai, kawai,' and with Karagenki, she transmitted the way of divination. Having determined Takayamahiko, with his Tengu-like nose, to be her husband of Sanze, she scattered Momiji upon her face with the bashfulness of a woman. With Oshiroi plastered thick upon a Kuroji, she wore a Magatsuzura that concealed her true Kiji. Like the clamorous swallows, chirping 'chū-chū' morning and evening,

As if unable to forget the Ondori even unto a hundred years, she yearned for a bridegroom, a Kataahite. Crowning the stars and treading upon the moon, she traversed the Yamasaka Mainichi, running to and fro. The number of men who came to visit was without limit; as the saying goes, even for the insect that eats knotweed, there are preferences. How miserable it is to see them emerge, infatuated with the Shiwazura of a terrible old hag. Though it may seem vast, the Seken is narrow. Kurohime, whose complexion was Matsukuro, chose as her husband Takayamahiko, who pleased her heart. Whether he be a hawk or a kite, she chose him as the bridegroom of a crow, and peering into the hole of Makutsugahara in Ōeyama, which bears a mysterious name, she advanced deeper and deeper. As the Sangokuichi Hanamuko approached, one league, two leagues,

Kurohime, having taken him, held a great banquet of celebration. With Kikuwaka, the stubborn Iwataka, the old man of the people Torawaka, the merciless Natsuhiko, Tomohiko, and Tsunehiko all together, they drank and made merry. The interior of the Iwaya became as if a beehive had been broken all at once.

Kurohime, upon her wrinkled, grime-blackened face, plastered white substance thick, a pure white heavy makeup like the top-coat of an iron storehouse. She dyed her white hair the color of a crow's wet wing, donned a gaudy garment scattered with plum blossoms, and awaiting the arrival of the Sangokuichi bridegroom, she stretched her thick, short neck long and thin, in the style of a lizard peering at a ceiling, and looked into the entrance of the Gankutsu. Adding color to her aged, hoarse voice, she intentionally produced a young voice accustomed to musical performance:

"Hey, Natsuhiko, Tsunehiko, have the guests not yet appeared? It is a trouble for you, but could you not go and welcome them just there? Since they are coming from the land of Fusa to the port of Yura, riding upon the heavenly bird-boat, if you hear a roaring sound, that is the retinue of Takayamahiko-sama. Please go, taking care of the sky and also of your footing."

Natsuhiko: 'Yes, yes, I understand. Though you say it is a distant place, it certainly requires time. Come, Tsunehiko, shall we go to welcome them?'

Tsunehiko: 'Kurohime, today is a day of celebration. Shall we then depart?'

Kurohime: 'What is this, Tsunehiko? Why this sudden formality, and what celebration is there to speak of? Do not mock an old woman by suggesting she takes a husband. Come, come, be off with you at once.'

Tsunehiko: 'If so, what manner of greeting should I offer? Is today not a day of celebration?'

Kurohime: 'If you call it a celebration, then it is one, but I have already passed the slope of fifty, and who would desire to take me as a husband? All this is done to spread the teachings of Kamisama, sacrificing this body of Kurohime to serve the Tenka and the Kokuka. Instead of saying congratulations, you should say Gokurausama.'

Tsunehiko: 'This is indeed the five-fold labor of four-fold suffering, Kurohime of the pitch-black, ten-fold labor to you.'

Kurohime: 'Hey, hey, are you mocking this Kurohime? There is no such thing as ten-fold labor. Do not be so insolent.'

Tsunehiko: 'Nay, heaven forbid. That you should sacrifice yourself for the sake of the Tenka is a labor of five. And that I, Tsunehiko, should go to meet the finest husband in the three realms, traveling along a narrow Yamaji as the sun sets, is also a labor of five. I have combined your five-fold labor and my five-fold labor to call it a ten-fold labor. Ha, ha, ha!'

Natsuhiko: 'Tsunehiko, shall we go?'

With that, he thrust his head into the Ihaana and said, 'Ah, I have divined it, I have divined it; there is no need to go now.'

Tsunehiko: 'What do you mean there is no need to go? Has Takayamahiko-sama appeared?'

Natsuhiko: 'It is certain. Pray, Kurohime, rejoice. He has appeared with a great force of retainers.'

Kurohime: 'That is indeed a laborious task. I pray you, go to the mouth of the cave to welcome them. As the entrance is quite small, it would be troublesome if they were to pass by.'

Natsuhiko thrust his head and shoulders out and waited for the approach of Takayamahiko's party.

Takayamahiko: 'Is this not Makutsugahara, known as the den of demons where Kurohime dwells? One would think someone would have come to welcome us by now; I wonder what they are doing.'

Torawaka: 'Ah, Great General, I have heard that this Makutsugahara is a vast place. They will surely come from the other side soon, but as we have arrived earlier than scheduled, they are likely preparing with due care, and perhaps they have simply been delayed. Even to prepare a feast in such an inconvenient land—as in the Sangokuichi, though not quite the same—one must look again, listen again, and compose one's spirits to advance but a little further.'

Takayamahiko said, 'That may be so, but tell me, Kurohime, even if you say there are no subordinates, it seems there should be twenty or thirty. Would it not be well to send three or five to meet us? It is said that a marriage proposal may grow cold even while the rice is cooking. Being so cold, might it not have chilled, eh, Torawaka?'

Torawaka replied, 'Tora, I do not know. In any case, as you see, snow has piled up here and there, so it must be bitterly cold. I, too, feel a chill in my body. When we left the land of Fusa, it was quite warm, but the cold during our flight through the Kūchiu, and the cold since arriving at this Onokorojima, pierces to the very bone.'

Takayamahiko, wearing a displeased face as if he had bitten into a bitter insect, advanced along the snow-covered path that ascended toward the peaks.

From the ground where snow had piled up over the entire surface, Natsuhiko thrust out only his head and said, 'Oh, oh, the arrival of Takayamahiko. Come, come, please enter. Kurohime is waiting for you with great anticipation. I thank you for coming from afar and sacrificing yourself for the sake of the Kokuka.'

Torawaka exclaimed, 'Hey, what is this? A head has fallen in such a place, and it is speaking... Ha, this fellow must be a monster of Ōeyama... Hey, monster, you say to enter, but you are not an earthworm; from where am I to enter? There is no entrance, is there? What has become of your body? It is impossible that you are living with only a head like a truffle or some such thing; you are a strange creature.'

Natsuhiko replied, 'Kurohime is so enamored with Lord Takayamahiko that she has fallen in head over heels, but as for this Natsuhiko, my head is outside, and only my body is buried. Come, come, it may seem like an inconvenient entrance, but the interior is a magnificent reception room; the entrance is intentionally made narrow for the sake of caution. Lord Takayamahiko, please, do enter. If you enter one by one, even a man of great stature can enter without getting caught.'

No sooner had he said this than Natsuhiko hid his form within the cavern.

Torawaka said, 'How strange. Contrary to what I saw, a large cave has opened. Oh, there are even stairs. Come, Lord Takayamahiko, I shall lead the way.'

With Torawaka in the lead, Takayamahiko, together with his many attendants, crept one after another into the rock cavern. At this time, Kurohime had already slipped into the inner chamber, and before a mirror, she was opening her mouth, rolling her eyes, pinching her nose, and was intent on arranging her face. "Hark, the Great General Takayamahiko has arrived. Pray, come hither with haste."

Kurohime replied, "Oh, how lacking in wit you are. Say something, serve him tea, and let him rest in the reception room. Before he enters, I must properly arrange my makeup and perform the wedding ceremony, if only in form. What are Kikuwaka and Ihataka doing? Is the preparation of the cuisine finished? Offer him tea and engage him in idle talk while you wait for him."

Natsuhiko said, "Today is a joyous wedding; would serving tea not result in a chaotic, tea-stained affair? How would it be to offer him water instead?"

Kurohime retorted, "Do not keep saying tea, tea. Tea is what is good. If one offers water, it may become 'mizukusa'—insipid and distant—so that will not do..."

Natsuhiko replied, "Ha, you intend to serve tea, and thus you say to let him drink tea... Very well, I understand."

Kurohime said, "Do not dally, but go over there and attend to Takayamahiko and his company. When the preparations here are complete, see that we may proceed to the nuptial cup. Ah, 'to employ others is to employ one's own suffering,' as the saying goes. To have only men and no woman's hand... how troublesome this is. There were two young women named Kiyosan and Terusan, but I left them at the hidden dwelling in Manai-ga-hara. It is inconvenient to be without women at such a time. Even in the pouring of sake, it is stiff and unpleasant with only men present. However, Kiyosan and Terusan are beautiful women, surpassing the common sort; fearing that my hard-won bridegroom might be snatched away, I did not bring them. Yet, while it is a relief, it is inconvenient. Now, now, I have become young again. Makeup is a marvelous thing. It is said from olden times that women are monsters... I have come to be enchanted by my own reflection. Even the handsome Takayamahiko, upon seeing this form, will surely leap toward me. For even I, a woman, am enchanted by my own appearance... It seems my soul is indeed good. Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haema Se, Kannagara Tamachi Haema Se. ...Here, here, Tsunehiko... Here, Natsuhiko, the preparations are complete. Natsuhiko, come hither for a moment."

Natsuhiko said, 'Eh, what is this? A strange voice reaches me. It seems that Kurohime-sama has, before I knew it, brought along young Teru-san and Kiyo-san. If she brings such beauties, my bridegroom position shall be snatched away.'

Kurohime said, 'Come, come, Natsuhiko-san, will you not come here quickly?'

Natsuhiko said, 'Unlike an old hag, the voice of a maiden is pleasant to the spirit, from wherever it may come. This evening, the old crone pair of Kurohime and Takayamahiko shall hold their wedding. Afterwards, it shall be the wedding of Teru-san and Natsuhiko-san. With so many men present, for her to call out "Natsuhiko-san" with that gentle voice, it seems she truly has some intention in mind. Well then, let me straighten my collar and present myself to her.'

He rubbed his eyes, picked his nose, licked his lips, adjusted the folds of his collar, examined everything from his sash to his hakama, and said, 'Yea, now I am a splendid irōtoko... Ahem.'

Changing his gait, he leaned back slightly, assumed the composed countenance of an irōtoko, slid open the shiyōji of one room with a rattle, and said, 'Was it you who called for me just now, Teru-san? What is your business?'

Kurohime said, 'Are you not Natsuhiko? What is with that composed face of yours... saying it is Teru-san... Day and night, you are obsessed with Teru-san... with that woman Teru. Do my words not enter your ears?'

Natsuhiko said, 'But I heard the voice of a young woman; and speaking of young women, at this moment, are there not only Teru-san and Kiyo-san?'

Kurohime said, 'Are Teru and Kiyo not left at the hideout on Mount Manai? Why are you feigning ignorance? It was I, Kurohime, who called you.'

Natsuhiko said, 'Heh, how is it that such a young voice comes forth?'

Kurohime said, 'It is a settled matter. Because I have practiced the Kototama, I can use the Kototama freely, whether it be the voice of an old man, the voice of an old woman, the voice of a woman of seventeen or eighteen, the voice of an akago, or that of a kite, a crow, a cat, or a mouse.'

Natsuhiko said, 'Ah, haha, is that so? In that case, this evening, I shall have you let me hear the cry of a mouse, shall I not? Ahahaha.'

Kurohime said, 'Aye, aye, you are noisy. Quickly attend to the guests, and then, that... make preparations for the rei.'

Natsuhiko said, 'Preparations for the rei... I do not understand what you mean.'

Welcoming the spring of Yachiyo, we shall proclaim the divine law of the Kyō of Uranai to the Kuniguni of the four quarters, serving in the divine work of the realization of the divine government, and in the beautiful and noble reign of the Miroku age. Even if the bell of the dawn of the three assemblies of Miroku should ring or break, the bond between the two shall never change. Ah, Kannagara Kami-Kami, may the spirits be blessed."

Having chanted this, he let his great bulk fall with a thud; at that moment, the sake cups and the decanter leaped a foot or two into the air, and suddenly began to perform a dance, adding an unexpected diversion. Natsuhiko, striking his waist, which was bent like the character 'ku', three or four times with his clenched fists, held an earthen cup in his right hand, and becoming unsteady with a 'choy-choy' gait, began to chant and dance of his own accord: "Ah, how auspicious, how auspicious, how truly auspicious! Though the years have grown old, the way of love, it seems, cannot be forgotten. Amidst daughters and grandchildren, like water in a country privy, the old hag floats and scatters her charms. With her face plastered thick with white powder, like the grandmother of the sparrow's inn, she lures the cock-bird from the high mountain with clever words. 'Do not tell, do not tell,' say we; oh, the auspiciousness of the tongue-cut sparrow! Night and day, 'chyon-chyon,' she weaves at the loom with wrinkled hands. The sight at the loom is unbearable. I have heard the saying that a sparrow never forgets its dance until it is a hundred. I, too, am a man, and I desire a partner, I desire one! To say one is lost on the path of love is..."

"...it is said that she cast rice upon the handsome man. The crab blows bubbles, the god of fortune; Ebisu and Daikoku, smiling, begin to dance, holding their bellies. Mimicking the goddess Benten, she smears her face thick with paint, and as the months pass, her Hotei-like belly swells, a sight to behold. Yet, I remain silent. Lord Jūrōjin, with his long head, having taken Takayamahiko as a son-in-law, strikes the round Bishamon, causing a great battle from top to bottom. Swept away by a great flood, a great commotion of celestial and terrestrial calamities ensues. In the dark night where black and white cannot be distinguished, an unexpected earthquake shall surely shake. Earthquakes, thunder, and wheels of fire; the world changes as it changes."

§ 10

He who hath children and grandchildren, taking a hawk as a son-in-law to a crow, with eyes gleaming, from this time forth, upon the Uzomuzo beneath the Ame, without any Yōshiya, like the Arataka, the mountain Kami of fierce power, the Kurau-laden Kurohime, her Yukusue is indeed Mede.

Ah, how I yearn for Natsuhiko; how does the Teru-sama, whom I forget not even in Mubi, fare in the Kakurega of Kurohime at Hijiyama, sleeping with his elbow as a pillow?

Ah, how I yearn, how I yearn; as I behold the Keiji of Takayamahiko and Kurohime this Kefu, my heart is troubled by the Hitorine of Teru-sama at the Hijiyamatauge; being shown such a place, I am envious, and tears fall drop by drop.

I have come to shed them; ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara; truly, it is a thing that cannot be fulfilled; in times of unfulfillment, I shall offer a prayer to the Kami of the Manai of Hinu.

Though ten Jifunen have passed since I entered the Oshihe of Uranai, the Mamehito of the Kami's Oshihe are told by Takahime-sama and Kurohime-sama, with their ever-strict Imashime, not to cast eyes upon women.

Furthermore, what is this today? Kurohime-sama has disguised her body, transforming herself like a celestial maiden; what is this talk of blooming again?

Was this what she meant when she said, as her habitual phrase, that the Oshihe has an obverse and a reverse, and that in the depths of the depths there is yet a deeper depth?

I have been foolish, doting too much upon the Kami; Natsuhiko, the fool-honest, shall henceforth reform his heart, and the path of love he has endured until now, he shall break the embankment and let it flow.

Come, Tsunehiko, Iwataka, and you young ones, Kikuwaka, who always listen to my tales, follow me and come forth; let us visit the house where Teru-sama and Kiyosama hide, prepared to face the fire of their guns; come, let us go.

Once the marriage of Takayamahiko and Kurohime is concluded this day, I shall take my leave; if I dally, I shall grow old; the prime of youth comes not twice.

Once I have become a wrinkled old man, no matter how I search for a wife, there will be none suitable; if the time is missed, it is a great calamity.

We, in the fullness of our flower, shall now reform our hearts, take wives, and live with purity, fulfilling the entirety of the principle of the body being the master and the spirit the servant; this is the gain of each in life; let us firmly resolve and go.

Come, shall we not go? Dokkoishō, dokkoishō; untoko dokkoi Kurohime-sama; yattoko dokkoi Takayamahiko's long-headed Gehō-sama; dokkoisho no dokkoisho.

Thus, falling into despair, he poured out his grievances with all his might and danced in a frenzy. Tsunehiko, Iwataka, and Kikuwaka also expressed their agreement with Natsuhiko's song, throwing their cups, breaking the sake bottles, and crushing the utensils, spending the night in a drunken, riotous revelry; yet Kurohime, as expected, being the night of her wedding celebration, did not murmur a single word, leaving Natsuhiko and the others to their wild behavior.

When the twenty-seventh day of the first month dawned, Kurohime invited Takayamahiko and the others to a room and proposed that they occupy the sacred throne of Mizu, where Toyokunihime of the Manai of Hinu was appearing. As a result of a unanimous decision, she led everyone who could stand, and Takayamahiko, mounting his steed, advanced straight toward Manaigahara. There, they commenced a great attack on the twenty-eighth day of the first month, but due to the Kototama of Aohiko and Kamehiko, they met with a miserable fate, scattered in all directions, and retreated once more to the rocky cavern of Makutsugahara to embark upon a second strategic plan. Alas, by what means would the faction of Kurohime seek to occupy the sacred ground of Manaigahara?
