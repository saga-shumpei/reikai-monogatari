Chapter 1: Golden の衣 [612]

黄金の衣〔612〕

§ 1

As far as the eye can see, both fields and mountains are a vast, level world of silver. Chilling the hearts of men, yet soothing the breast that yearns, the beloved daughter with skin like snow, ensnared by the deep Keiriyaku of the Magami, sheds a rain of tears within the Ihayado.

Before one knows it, Setsukohime, whose heart blossomed in the dew of the Sumekami's grace, awaits the spring of Ichiyōraifuku at the Manai-ga-hara of Hinu, while enduring the sorrowful Tsukihi of her captivity within this Ihayado, having passed through the twelve seasons of Harunatsuaki, until at last she feels as though the sky of Shinonome is clearing.

Setsukohime, having been escorted by Yoshikohime and returned to the home of her Sofubo, spends the night in tears of joy; and when the dawn breaks on the twenty-eighth day of Shiyōguwatsu, she proceeds with her Sofubo, Heisuke and Onara, traversing the snow-laden Noji, with a heart deep in pilgrimage, heading toward the foot of Manai-ga-take together with Ihakō, Kankō, and Ichikō.

Osetsu says, 'Honored guests, as you are aware, we are an aged Jii and Baa, and I, a frail woman; our footing does not progress as we might wish. You are those who serve the Kami, and Yoshikohime must surely be waiting for you with great anticipation. We shall meet before the Kami of Manai-ga-hara, so please, do not concern yourselves with us and proceed ahead.'

Ihakō replies, 'If that is the case, we shall go ahead to clear the path. We shall make all preparations and await your arrival, so please, proceed at your leisure. Honored Jii, honored Baa, and Osetsu-sama, if you will excuse us, we three shall take our leave and go ahead. Please, do come along slowly.'

Heisuke says, 'Ah, that is well. For us, an aged Jii and Baa, and a frail daughter, it is truly difficult to travel in the company of strong, young people such as yourselves. Furthermore, you must surely find us tedious. Rather than this, go quickly to the side of Yoshikohime and attend to all her needs. We three shall follow along at our own pace.'

Ihakō says, 'In that case, honored Jii-sama, honored Baa-sama, Osetsu-sama, we shall take our leave and go ahead. Now, Kankō, Ichikō, it is a forced march through the snow. Advance, advance, one, two, three!' With this cry, they dash off, scattering in their haste.

Onara says, 'My, how energetic those guests from last night were... Ah, one does not wish to grow old. To dash off headlong through a path with such deep snow, like wild boars or some such creatures, they have already vanished from sight. Come, come, let us proceed slowly.'

§ 2

The Sannin, leaning upon their staves, proceeded slowly, treading in the footprints left by Ihakō and his companions.

The narrative returns to the past.

Tormented by the demons of their own hearts and unable to find lodging, Onihiko and Onitora, the Riyōnin, trudged along the snow-laden Yomichi, facing the driving snow as they walked with arms linked, ever westward. Having lost their way, they fell headlong, still linked together, into a Kusotsubo by the roadside. From the crown of their heads to the Tsumasaki, they were instantly transformed into Ōgonbutsu. The two barely managed to crawl out with their Inochi, and amidst the Kanpū that blew with a piercing, whistling sound, they squeezed out trembling voices.

Onihiko said, 'The retribution is before our eyes. Had we known it would come to this, it would have been Mashi to have simply offered an Ayama to Jii Heisuke, even if it meant being struck once or twice upon the head, and to have been granted lodging. It is because you spoke of feeling embarrassed and displayed such Yasegaman that we have met with this fate. It is truly the height of Fungai.'

Onitora replied, 'What is the use of speaking now of things that are Sugiko? Have you forgotten that to dwell upon past Kurau is a Daikinmotsu? It is but a Setsunashin. Nevertheless, if we remain thus, our Kimono and our very bodies will suffer Hiyōketsu. To be walking in this freezing sky of twenty degrees below zero, clad in garments of excrement, is quite enough. Moreover, the Gods rejoice in that which is sacred... in purity and innocence. How could we possibly perform worship while wearing such Kimono? Perhaps the Gods cast us into the Kusotsubo, saying that because your evil has not yet been purged, you have no qualification for worship. Ah, it is cold, it is cold... The cold has passed, and my whole body has begun to ache. It is as if I am being pierced all over with awls. It is cold, it is painful. Ah, I cannot even weep. What shall we do?'

Onihiko said, 'Having been made to suffer the stench by Heisuke and Onara, and having met with such foul, embarrassing, and foolish experiences, we are now cast into a Kusotsubo. Ah, it is like the stench of old men and old women; it is as if Heisuke and Onara were here as well. Suffering such distress, we cannot even advance toward Manai-ga-hara. Oh, how hateful! If there were but a house somewhere here, I would not care what was said; I would force my way in, kindle a fire, warm my body, and boil water to purify myself, for what else can be done? If we dally, our bodies will become rigid, and we shall turn into stone Jizō. Come, let us go, let us go.'

§ 3

The Futari, with their Inochi barely preserved, advanced some Nisanchiyō, when they suddenly came upon yet another Abaraya.

"Yo, Tendau does not slay men. Here stands an Itsukenya. ...Well, this appears to be a Monōki Koya. ...Come, at any rate, let us Hai and attend to our bodies."

The Futari pushed open the door and, entering Kowagowa, beheld a red object in the darkness.

Onihiko said, "Haha, someone has been burning a fire here. It is likely the Shigoto of Otohiko and Kamehiko."

So saying, he dragged out the straw from the Atari, blew upon it to kindle a flame, and became Matsupadaka.

"Chiyotsuto, this is a relief. Yet, I must dry my Kimono. ...Even so, unless I perform a Sentaku once, even when dried, it will be so foul-smelling that there will be no Shikata for it, will there, Onitora?"

"Un, someone must have dwelt in this Abaraya originally. There ought to be an Ido. Let us search for one, and if there be an Ido, we shall thrust our Kimono into it, wash them vigorously, wring them out thoroughly to remove the moisture, and build a great fire to roast them dry. ...Oh, there it is, there it is! If we dally, we might fall in. It seems there is a puddle of water. ...Oi, your role is to tend the fire; I shall become a Sentaku-oni for a while. A human-oni. An oni doing laundry before the old hag arrives... Ahahaha!"

The two men, facing the puddle, swiftly stripped off their filth-covered Kimono and cast them in.

"Now, the preparations for the Sentaku are complete. However, we have nothing to wear. Is there not some straw matting or the like around here?"

The Futari rustled about, groping in the dark corners of the Koya, searching for old straw mats or old floor mats, and at last wrapped them about their bodies.

"Ah, with this, at least our Inochi is saved."

So saying, they built a great fire and the two warmed themselves.

"Oi, General Onitora, you are to begin the Sentaku. I shall serve in the role of drying them."

"For one to wash one's own Kimono and rely upon the care of another is a violation of the laws of heaven. ...Very well, I shall wash my own, and if it be your desire, I shall let you take the role of drying them. ...Ya, I thought it was an Ido, but it is another cesspool. Since they have built a privy inside the house, it is no wonder one might mistake it. But, well, it is fortunate that I did not fall in."

"Oi Onitora, mine appears to be the real thing; there is no excrement floating in it."
