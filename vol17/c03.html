Chapter 3: 生死不 Bright [614]

生死不明〔614〕

§ 1

Osetsu, the granddaughter of Heisuke of Tanbamura, confined within a dark Ihayado in the deep Yamaoku of Mount Manai, had grown pale and Yaseotoro, her Teashi like Harigane, her Iki growing faint, and with tears in one hand, she uttered Kudo, a sight pitiful to any Yoso who might behold her.

Deep within this Ihayado, far from Hitozato where even the light of Tsukihi could not penetrate, Osetsu, thus imprisoned, endured the frost of Shiyotō, and like the Danmatsuma of the insects in the Akino, she raised her voice in sorrow, entrusting her thoughts to song, seeking to console her Monmon heart.

"When I reflect upon the snowy sky of Kozo, / Though it is called spring, it is yet cold; / Snow lies deep upon the Yomo mountains, / And in the Yufuyami of the fierce Nowaki, / Two mysterious men came seeking my home. / Having caused many Magabito to lie in wait / At the Kadoguchi, they deceived my Toshioi / Grandfather and Grandmother, and even me, / With artful words. / Within a single room, Onihiko / And the fierce-hearted Onitora / Feigned sleep to plot against us. / At the hour of the ox, when even the waters sleep, / Rising at the signal of the bell, / Without mercy, like ruthless robbers / Beyond all expectation, / Pitifully, they bound my Grandfather and Grandmother, / Though they were innocent, most cruelly / To the pillars of the house."

"Drawing forth long blades, / They demanded, 'Surrender your treasures, bring forth your gold,' / In an inescapable, harsh confrontation. / Grandfather and Grandmother, struck with terror, / Begged for their lives, / Their voices piteous to hear. / Having plundered the treasures saved / Through toil since their youth, / The demons, still unsatisfied, / Without mercy or compassion, / Before the eyes of the two, / Demanded an unexpected, illicit love. / 'If you refuse,' they threatened, 'we shall make the old couple / The rust of our swords,' / And pressed Osetsu for her answer. / I, a frail maiden, / Could offer no response; / As demons and devils spread, / In this world where they rage with might, / Are there no gods to purify and save?"

"Is goodness withered and evil triumphant? / The world is in the true darkness of the raven's feather; / Caught by the Majin in the darkness, / I was dragged, roughly, / Before the face of the fearsome Oni-Kumohiko / Of the Oeyama. / Fainting with dread, / I was cast into a palanquin of wicker, / And swayed, dizzy and reeling, / To the north of Mount Hiji, / Where Mount Manai towers. / At the bottom of a valley in the snow-clad mountain, / They opened the rock and thrust me in, / And in this Ihayado of darkness where black and white cannot be distinguished, / I pass my days, confined all alone. / Alas, in all the world, / Is there any as ill-fated as I? / What karma of my past life / Has brought me to this?"

Within, she fretted in deep contemplation: If Kami truly dwell in this world, then I beseech the Amatsukami and the Kunitsukami, the Yahoyorozu, to have mercy upon the agonizing thoughts of parent and daughter, and to save us with haste, even if but for a single Hitohi or Katatoki.

By the Omamo of the myriad Kami, even as the Ihato of Ama is opened, may this Makutsu be opened straightway, and may you deliver me into the light; Kamunagara, may your Mitama Sachihahe.

Though the Asahi may shine or be obscured, though the moon may wax or wane, though the Daichi should sink, and though the Magatsu Kami should rage, the true Kami shall save the world.

Taking these Kamigoto as my strength, I have proclaimed them morning and evening.

The Kototama of Izunomitama shall open at once, like the plum blossoms that bloom even when spring has come but the flowers have yet to open; have mercy, O hundred Kami, upon this wretched Zumai in the Ihaya.

O Kamunahohi, who created this world, and O Ōnahohi of vast heart, in all things of the human world, look straight, listen straight, and proclaim straight the errors of the body.

Taught in Umara and Tsubara is the teaching of the Kami of Ananaikyo; ah, how grateful, how venerable.

The Ihato of the heart opens clearly, like the fragrance of the lotus flower, and the moon of Shinnyo shines beautifully in the sky, yet the sky of my heart clouds over once more.

My body is imprisoned in the Ihaya, and I know not the light of the sun and moon; the rain of tears that falls, how long shall it continue?

In this Ihaya of the Hinuma no Manai, when shall I escape the Izunomitama, and the hundred sufferings of Mizunomitama?

When shall the moon of Ananaikyo shine, and when shall parent and child, with thoughts deep, take each other by the hand, embrace, and weep?

Ah, Kamunagara, Kami, Kami, may your Mitama Sachihahe; ah, Kamunagara, Kami, Kami, may your Mitama Sachihahe.

Thus, a woman's voice was heard singing faintly.

Onihiko said, 'Ah, it is here, it is here; for the first time in a long while, I hear the voice of Osetsu-sama. Well, this means at least her life is saved, does it not, Onitora? We may be at ease for now.'

Onitora replied, 'Is that not a thin and unpleasant voice? Is it truly the voice of Osetsu-sama? This is the notorious Makutsu of demons; there is no telling what may be masquerading as her. We must examine the matter thoroughly first.'

Onihiko peered into the rocky cave and said, 'If I may speak.'

Osetsu, startled by this voice, looked toward the opening and saw the face of Onihiko, which could be mistaken for no other; upon seeing him, she cried, 'Ah, are you not Onihiko, the retainer of Onikumahiko of Mount Oe? You, who stole the treasures wrung from the sweat and toil of an aged couple, and furthermore abducted me, keeping me confined in this Ihaya for so long—you, a kinsman of the evil Kami! That you have come here is the guidance of the Kami I have prayed to daily; come, prepare yourself!'

Before the words had even left her lips, she seized a Ganseki that lay at hand, and with her Mete, she struck at the Menbu of Onihiko; yet the blow missed its mark and hit the Ganpeki, falling into the Gankutsunai with a clattering sound.

Osetsu picked it up once more, intending to strike at the Menbu of Onihiko again, but Onihiko, startled, drew back his peering face and spoke: 'Pray, Osetsu-sama, though your Harada is Gomotsuto, I am as different from the Onihiko of Kiyonen as Undei. I now repent my Zenpi and have come to assist you; Heisuke-sama and Onara-sama also wait at the Iriguchi of the Ihato. Come, come, I shall open it for you, so pray emerge.'

Osetsu replied: 'How glibly you chirp with your Neiben; do you think I shall be deceived by such a Shiyudan? Depart from this place at once.'

Onihiko said: 'Your Goritsupuku is Ichiō Gomotsuto, yet to remain here forever is a futile matter. In any case, I shall now open the Ihato, so Dōzo, I pray you, come forth. Ah, they say that once a man does evil, he is forever deemed to do evil; is this what is meant by the saying that the conduct of a Ningen is of Taisetsu importance? Come now, Onitora, are you to remain silent while I alone speak? Oh, how unmanly to be weeping so! How vexing; if you must weep, do so later at your leisure. This is a moment of one in a thousand; you too must disclose the circumstances until Osetsu-sama is satisfied. It is an easy matter to open this Ihato, but unless I obtain the consent of Osetsu-sama, I know not what fate might befall us the moment it is opened. Above all, negotiation is the essential thing.'

Onitora cried out and fell prostrate, weeping: 'Ah, there is no help for it. Even for a villain like me, who bears a name like a demon and a tiger, the evil deeds of the past rise from memory, and it is as if the demon of my heart is gouging out my entrails. Ah, I must open it, yet I must not; to open it is to regret the Tamatebako. How could I ever have the face to meet Osetsu-sama?'

Onihiko said: 'Hey, Iwakō, you are unscathed; will you not, in my stead, thrust your face from this hole and conduct the negotiations? You are a special minister plenipotentiary. If this goes well, I shall bestow upon you the First Order of Merit.'

Iwakō replied: 'I decline the First Order of Merit or anything else. Just try thrusting your own foolish head out and see if your neck is not pulled off; what a fine face that would be. A sage and a gentleman does not approach danger. Well, shall we desist?'

Onihiko exclaimed, 'Aah, both this one and that one are naught but Onimiso; they wear faces of strength, yet they are but cowardly Koshinu with timid Kimodama. Ho, Kankō, Ichikō, I appoint you as commissioners of Kausefu.'

Kankō replied, 'What are you spewing? This Danpan is an unavoidable Gimu for Onihiko and Onitora. Who would be such a fool as to take upon himself the Sekinin of others and risk danger in such a bitter Jisetsu? Is that not so, Ichikō?'

Ichikō answered, 'Oh, indeed. Well, well, the Hiyori is poor today, so let us cease for now.'

Onihiko said, 'Aah, there is no Shikata; I shall open it.'

As he pushed open the Ganpeki with a signal, Osetsu held in a reverse grip an Eiri Ishiyari fashioned from the rock wall she had trained with daily, and thrust it at Onihiko, aiming for his Mega. Onihiko escaped the Suidau from which he had come, his Inochi barely intact; Iwakō followed behind Osetsu, attempting to restrain her with his bare hands.

Iwakō cried, 'Pray, Osetsu-dono, wait a moment; there are deep circumstances behind this.'

Osetsu retorted, 'At this stage, what excuses remain? You too are one of the Akuma of Onikumohiko’s faction. Know the prowess of Osetsu of Tanba Village, who bears the divine power of the Ōkami!'

So saying, she turned and lunged. Iwakō, striking his head against the Suidau from which he had come and bruising his arms against the Ganpeki, fled toward the exit, stumbling and falling.

Osetsu, looking around in all directions, cried, 'Ha! Are you not that Onitora who cannot be mistaken? You have caused me much suffering. Now that it has come to this, it is the hundredth year; taste the blade of Osetsu’s resentment!'

She brandished her weapon overhead, her countenance terrifying as she prepared to leap upon him.

Onitora pleaded, 'Pray, Osetsu-sama, I was wrong, I was wrong. Please, I beg you to forgive me; there are deep circumstances behind this.'

Osetsu replied, 'I possess no ears to listen to such excuses.'

She thrust at him. Onitora dodged to the front, back, left, and right within the cavern, avoiding the sharp point.

Alas, Osetsu stumbled upon a rock, fell to the ground, and lay in a swoon.

Onitora lamented, 'Ah, I have failed; this is a disaster. Hey, Kankō, Ichikō, you must hasten to the exit and report this situation to Onihiko and Iwakō immediately. I shall attend to Osetsu-sama until she regains her senses, so please, go quickly. Hey, do not stumble and injure yourselves in the rugged Suidau; go with haste!'

'Very well, understood,' they replied.

With that, Kankō and Ichikō ran toward the exit with all their might. Onitora, in his desperation, turned to the gods, reciting the Amatsu-Norito and chanting the heavenly Kazauta in a loud, resonant voice.

The voice from the Itsupauguchi cave reached the ears of the Raufūfu, who stood before the cave that served as both entrance and exit, as well as the ears of Onihiko, Ihakō, and the others, as if it were piercing through them. Kankō and Ichikō appeared at this place from within the cave.

"Hark, Onihiko, General Ihakō, it is a great calamity! He is dead, dead, dead, dead!"

Heisuke replied, "What is this you say? Who has died? Will you not speak clearly?"

Kankō answered, "How could I speak clearly? He is dead, he is dead! Oh, oh, oh!"

Ihakō asked, "Has Onitora died?"

Kankō said, "He is dead, indeed he is dead, but I do not know the details. Go and see for yourselves, and you shall understand. I pray you, spare me from saying anything more."

Onihiko asked, "Is it not Osetsu who has died?"

Kankō replied, "I know not, I know not. In any case, one of the two is dead."

Heisuke exclaimed, "Hear me, you fellows! Are you mocking this old man? If Osetsu has indeed died, though I be aged, I still possess vigor enough to crush three or five of you; be prepared for that."

Kankō said, "Yet, I should be a Jiyogairei, should I not?"

Heisuke retorted, "What? I shall slay every one of you, this one and that one alike!"

Ichikō replied, "If it is a general slaughter, I shall accept it with pleasure."

Ihakō said, "Is that the state of things? Come, let us hasten! If we dally, the life of Osetsu, whom we saved with such effort, shall be in peril. Come, old man, whatever the case, let us go within and investigate. Come, follow me!"

So saying, he took the lead and ran forth. Heisuke and Onara followed the four, muttering to themselves. Onitora was reciting the divine words with all his might, performing the injection of the divine spirit. Osetsu gradually regained her breath, and as she lifted her face, she beheld Onitora, whom she had long held in deep resentment, praying with all his might with his hands clasped, stark naked.

Osetsu cried, "Ha! What strange conduct is this? Do you, a being of extreme evil and lawlessness, pretend to be virtuous and pray to the gods, seeking to deceive me? Know the weight of your deeds!"

With these words, she lunged at him once more.

Onitora pleaded, "Pray, wait, Osetsu-sama! There is a profound reason for this. Wait, I beseech you!"

Osetsu replied, "What? There is no such thing as waiting!"

As she lunged, Onitora dodged to the right and to the left, crying, "Calm yourself, calm yourself!" as he fled about.

Heisuke and Onara, who had come tumbling into this place, cried, "Ha! Is that not Osetsu?"

Osetsu asked, "Ha! Is it you, grandfather? Grandmother? How have you come to be here?"

"Ah, Osetsu, how we have longed to see you!"

"Ah, how fortunate! You have remained safe!"

The three embraced one another, forgetting all else, and were overcome with tears of joy. After a short while, through the testimony of the couple Heisuke and Onara, Osetsu was finally at ease. She then recited the Norito anew, and the party of eight departed from the cave, and together with the five naked men, they turned to the left and ascended toward the place of the appearance of Toyokuni-hime on Mount Manai.
