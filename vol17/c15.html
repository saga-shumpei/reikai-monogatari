Chapter 15: 敵味方 [626]

敵味方〔626〕

§ 1

Bathed in the moonlight of the fifteenth day of Niguwatsu, upon the level ground at the summit of Mitakeyama, they entered the land of Kuwashiyo, sharing a common fate, spreading their straw rain-capes, and using their elbows as pillows. Umakō and Shikakō, their dreams shattered by the sound of the storm blowing over the peaks, rose up at once with a start.

Shikakō: 'Ah, what a terrifying thing it was. Just as I thought we had escaped the peril of the rocky cavern through the compassion of Murasakihime, the two, Arataka and Onitaka, returned from Onigajō, bound us two, and thrust us back into the cavern; but it was a dream. Ah, how terrifying, how terrifying; even in a dream, such Akunin make one shudder.'

Umakō: 'Ha, did you also have a dream? I had a dream of the same kind. Somehow, the heart is ill at ease in this place. Since it is a moonlit night, shall we wander about here?'

Shikakō: 'That is so. If we have this many of our company, we should be safe in the event of an emergency. Even if we wander a chō or two away, there should be no cause for concern. Should Arataka or Onitaka appear by any chance, if we but say a single word, "O, help us," Kamehiko-sama will surely assist us by the firing of the Kototama, that is a certainty. Come, let us go, let us go. Everyone is sleeping so soundly. Those of us with deep sins are driven by fear and cannot even enjoy a proper sleep. If we are awake, we are subjected to terrifying sights; if we sleep, we have terrifying dreams; whether sleeping or waking, we are constantly tormented... Relying on the light of the magnificent moon, shall we not wander about here a little for the sake of our health?'

Umakō: 'That would be well.'

So saying, they rose abruptly, and the two, holding hands, wandered aimlessly about the summit of the mountain.

'Ah, what a beautiful scene. Although the wind is cold enough upon the mountain, dew has gathered on the leaves, and the moon dwells in each one; this spectacle is as if we were in a world of crystal. Ah, even unfortunate beings like us can behold such a delightful scene. How one wishes for a long life as a human being.'

So they sang in a humming voice, wandering here and there.

Kamehiko awoke in the midst of the night and said, 'Ah, everyone is gathered together, sleeping so soundly. The white face of Princess Etsuko, her peach-colored cheeks, the flower-like, beautiful figure of Murasakihime; on one side a flower-like countenance, on the other skin like snow, and in the sky the bright moon of the fifteenth night. It seems the moon itself is quite pleased, for it illuminates the two fair faces with special favor; how much more beautiful they appear. ...Ah, the face of Otohiko, what a posture, lacking in vigor. He sleeps with one arm bent in the shape of a kana character, his mouth open in an unrefined manner. At this hour, he is likely dreaming of Princess Isago. His beloved wife was snatched away by those of the Brahman religion and remains missing; when I think of it, I can well perceive his inner heart. Even so, he has been walking with us all this time, and seeing that he does not even utter the name of Princess Isago, he must be quite resolute... By looking at the sleeping face of a human, one can generally discern their spirit. Let me set out to inspect the head of Aohyōtanyahiko... Ha, this fellow is smiling happily. He must be dreaming of Osechi of Tanbamura or some such thing. Ha, he is smiling even more. As he is a fellow who might be called a phantom, there is something about his smile that is full of malice. I would like to show this to Osechi, ha ha ha. Ha, this fellow is from Tanshu, he has a rather fine face. He said it was by the command of the deity Princess Toyokuni, and indeed, there is a sense of dignity about him. Ha, a strange light is appearing in the center of his face. Is he an incarnation of Princess Konohana, or the return of the Myōon Bosatsu? This fellow is not one to be lightly despised. He appears to be a person of great character among our party. ...Ha, I have performed a good Saniwa. Tomorrow, I shall give Otohiko the leader a surprise. ...Hm, this fellow is the hunchbacked Natsuhiko, trained by Kurohime. What a pathetic, scowling face. Ha, this fellow is not worth criticizing. Here is one with a rather cunning face. This fellow is what they call a fox or a raccoon. Hm, yes, yes, Tomohiko, Tomohiko; while he is sleeping now, shall I tie his hair together?'

§ 2

Kamehiko seized the long hair of the two, and with the ends, bound them fast in a Jigokumusubi knot.

"Come, when this fellow awakens, it shall be truly Kotsukei. From here on, it is the turn of Otohiko-sama and Aohiko. Yet, the Kiyori is too great... it seems the hair will not reach. Wait, wait... ah, here is a rope. I shall bind them with this."

He bound them together with Tebaya, and said,

"Ha, ha, ha, ha, with this there is no fear of Funshitsu. Next shall be Yoshikohime-sama and Murasakihime-sama... Ah, this is a pity. To join Murasakihime and Tanshiu, and finally to join Yoshikohime and the Ōkami-sama, Kamehiko. With this, two times four is eight people, and two times eight is sixteen arms and legs. Ah, Omoshiro, Omoshiro."

He then reached out in the Tesagu and seized the hair of Murasakihime.

Murasakihime rose abruptly, seized the Udekubi of Kamehiko, and threw him with such force that Kamehiko fell with a thud toward the valley at his side.

"Aita, aita, aita, aita!" he cried out.

Murasakihime said, "Ah, everyone, please wake up. Once again, the Teshita Monodomo of Onikumawake have appeared. Come, Goyōi, Goyōi."

Startled by this voice, the Ichidō sprang up, and Tsunehiko cried,

"Aita, aita, aita, aita!"

Natsuhiko said, "Eh, eh, etaiwai, etaiwai, who is it, who is it, pulling at a person's hair... will you not let go?"

Tsunehiko said, "Oi, Natsu, it must be you."

Natsuhiko said, "Do not speak like a fool. It is you who are pulling my hair."

Aohiko said, "Ah, there is someone pulling my head... Ah, what is this? While we were sleeping, they have joined our hair together. The one who would play such a prank is likely Kame-kō. Oi, Kamehiko, where have you gone? Come out quickly and untie us."

Kamehiko said, "Oi, oi, I am in a terrible place, bound with my hands behind my back, and I am in distress. Someone come out and untie me."

Aohiko said, "Ah, so Kamehiko has been bound as well? This is why it is said that negligence is a great enemy. To let one's guard down while facing enemy territory and to fall asleep is our own lack of vigilance, yet it is fortunate that no one has been lost."

Kamehiko said, "Oi, Aohiko, everyone, do not worry yourselves. Mine is a case of self-binding and self-unbinding; I remain as I was before."

Aohiko said, "What is this, trying to frighten people... where were you bound?"

Kamehiko said, "Well, it matters not. What on earth are you all? You have attached long tails to your heads..."

Tanshiu: 'Kamehiko-sama, you have played a most mischievous prank. I have been watching you, feigning ignorance all the while. The sight of you being cast aside by Murasakihime-sama was truly amusing, ha ha ha ha!'

Kamehiko: 'Ah, a blunder. Everyone, I have performed a most rude act... I pray that Kamunahohi and Ōnahohi will look upon this and hear this with rectification.'

Otohiko: 'There is a limit even to jests. This is an act that harms the sanctity of a Sendenshi. As of this day, acting in the stead of Susanōnōokami, I hereby strip you of your office as Sendenshi. Be grateful for this.'

Kamehiko: 'This is a bit of a nuisance. I say, Otohiko-sama, pray grant me a stay of execution until the subjugation of Onigajo is complete.'

Otohiko: 'No, that shall not be.'

Kamehiko: 'I say, Yoshikohime-sama, pray intercede on my behalf.'

Yoshikohime: 'Now, Otohiko-sama, please admonish him well so that he shall not commit such mischief henceforth, and for this time, I pray you forgive him.'

Otohiko: 'Though you are one difficult to forgive, I shall follow the words of Yoshikohime-sama and overlook this matter for now.'

Kamehiko: 'Ha ha ha ha, what is it you are spouting? "Overlook"... the very word is laughable, ah ha ha ha, it is so funny that my stomach aches. What is with that solemn face you wear? ...Will you not clear the way? No matter how much you worry and fret over Isokohime, you will not meet your wife who is drifting on the solitary island of Ryūgū; will you not cast aside your fleeting heart and be a little more relaxed? It is no fun to travel when a fellow with such a difficult face is among us.'

Otohiko: 'What? Is Isokohime drifting on the solitary island of Ryūgū? When, and from whom, did you hear that?'

Kamehiko: 'How can one serve as a Sendenshi if one does not know such things? With the divine clairvoyance of Kamehiko-sama, it has been thoroughly investigated. She is there now on Ryūgū Island with Umekohime-sama, the lady-in-waiting Imakohime, and Uzuhime, the four of them together, in the midst of a fierce battle with the Brahman religion. However, do not worry, for the Gods are protecting them.'

I had been pondering in various ways, fearing that Isokohime might have been taken captive by the Uranai religion, and I had become a porter for Kurohime to observe the situation; but as it seemed she was not with the Uranai religion, I had thought perhaps she had been taken prisoner by Onigumohiko of Mt. Ōe. I had come this far with courage, thinking that if I went to Onigajo, I must rescue Isokohime if she were there. If that is the case, then Isokohime is not at Onigajo, is she?'
