Chapter 12: Flower と Flower [623]

花と花〔623〕

§ 1

Onikumohiko, who abandoned the Mizuhokuni of Mesopotamia—famed as the Rakudo of the Sekai—and fled to Onokorojima, exhausted his every effort to ravage the Kamikuni by constructing a Toride upon the Ōeyama of the hideous Magatsu.

Though he was driven away by Onitakehiko, who served the Ōkami of Kamususanō, and fled through the Chiukū to the vicinity of Mount Ibuki, scattering like clouds and mist, the Magatsukasa of peerless Gauriki upon whom Onikumohiko relied—the fearsome Onikumawake—was manipulated by the hideous spirit of the Yamataorochi.

Together with Arataka and Onitaka, he constructed a second Toride at Onigajō, along the peaks of the Ōe mountains.

Watching for the time and concentrating on a Hisaku for Kendojiurai, he strove in vain to occupy with all his might the Okefuseyama Renge-dai and the succeeding Shio-no-Kamiyama, which are the Keirin-chi for the achievement of the divine administration of the Ōkami of Kamususanō and the Ōkami of Kunitakehiko, by attacking the sacred ground in an Itsukiyo.

Etsuko-hime, a missionary of the San-kyō, accompanied by Oto-hiko and Kame-hiko, departed from Manai-ga-hara and approached Mount Sangoku. At that very moment, a beautiful woman was seen in the mountain stream, washing garments stained with blood; she gazed at the three figures with a look of bewilderment.

Oto-hiko said, 'Pray, Etsuko-hime, that a young woman of such beauty should be found in this deep mountain and secluded valley, washing blood-stained garments and gazing upon us with a look of contemplation—there must be some profound circumstance behind this. Shall I seize the woman and question her?'

Etsuko-hime replied, 'Whatever the reason, it is incomprehensible that she should be in this mountain stream, far from any human dwelling. I pray you, take the trouble to inquire of the woman gently.'

Oto-hiko said, 'I shall do so. Kame-hiko, stay here and wait with Etsuko-hime. I shall cross this mountain stream and put a question to the mysterious woman, for I shall likely come to understand the state of these mountains as well.'

No sooner had he spoken than he tucked up his skirts and crossed the mountain stream to the opposite bank.

Oto-hiko said, 'Hearken! For what reason does a young woman wash blood-stained garments in this deep mountain and secluded valley, far from human habitation? I suspect that there is a den of evil spirits in this vicinity that devours men. Come, tell me the particulars in full.' The woman, shedding tears, replied, 'Alas, I am a woman of the capital. While I was on my way to worship the deity of Manai-ga-hara, accompanied by two servants, the evil spirits of Onikumohiko’s faction of Ōeyama—Arataka and Onitaka—used clever words and stratagems to abduct my retinue and me. My two servants were ordered to perform various arduous labors, and as a result of physical exhaustion, they could no longer move their limbs. While they were suffering and writhing in agony, Arataka and Onitaka, devoid of mercy or compassion, commanded their subordinates to gather, beat the two servants to death, tear off their limbs, and bury them in the earth, leaving them stark naked. Since then, I have been oppressed by the heartless Arataka and Onitaka, and have spent sad days that are unbearable. Today, by good fortune, both were summoned by Onikumawake, the commander at Onigajō. Taking advantage of their absence, I made the remaining four or five servants drink sake until they were intoxicated, sought out the garments of the two servants, and came here to this mountain stream to wash and purify them in the sun. I intend to remake them into bedding, and at the very least, to wear them against my own skin as a memento of the servants, that I might console their spirits; thus, I came here to wash them, watching for an opportunity.'

§ 2

With that, she burst into a loud wail. Otohiko, folding his Riyōte and heaving a Futoiki, lamented, 'Aa, the Akumade Akugiyakubudau of Daijizaiten and the Zangiyaku of the Yatsubara of the Brahman Kyō.'

Otohiko, with tears streaming down his Shihen, remained in silent Mokunen, his head bowed.

The woman spoke: 'I know not who you may be, Anatasama, but at a glance, I perceive an Osugata of great strength. Where are you traveling with such a beautiful Gofujin? This Mitakeyama is a mountain of terrifying demons, standing at the Chiushinten between the Minetsuzu of Ōeyama and Onigajō. As many Magami dwell beyond this point, it is Kennon Senban to proceed further. I pray you, turn back from this place, for it would be a pity should you encounter the same Ukime as I.'

Otohiko, blinking his eyes, replied: 'Oh, Jiyochiu, you have spoken with such Goshinsetsu. Yet, we are the intermediaries of the Kami who save mankind, bearing a mission from the divine realm to bring even the most wicked demons to submission through the power of Kototama. Even if demons or great serpents should attack, we cannot retreat a single step. I pray you, guide us to the dwelling of the Arataka and Onitaka.'

The woman replied: 'That may be so, but you are a party of three, while the other side is a countless host of demons. No matter how much of a hero or a champion you may be, the outcome between the many and the few is clear without a battle. Come, come, I pray you, return quickly.'

Otohiko asked: 'I thank you for your Goshinsetsu. But, as I heard you say earlier that you are a woman of the capital, by what name are you called?'

The woman replied: 'Yes, it is shameful to be asked my name, but I am a humble woman called Murasakihime.'

From across the mountain stream, Kamehiko called out in a loud voice: 'Oi, oi, Otohiko, what are you dawdling about, having caught a beautiful woman? Are you concluding a secret pact while leaving us waiting? This is no time for such lightheartedness. Who would be flirting in the bottom of a valley like this? Finish your parley and report the enemy situation to the headquarters. Princess Etsuko is displeased and her countenance has changed. Ahahaha!'

Princess Etsuko, overhearing this, remarked: 'Now, now, Kamehiko-sama, what is this you say about Princess Etsuko being displeased?'

Kamehiko replied: 'Ah, I apologize. My words must have sounded profound; I pray you, reconsider and listen again. Caught up in the spirit of the moment, I was merely teasing Otohiko. On this side of the valley, we have Princess Etsuko, who puts flowers to shame, accompanied by Kamehiko; on the opposite bank, a young lady of fine age with Otohiko, the man of valor. Separated by the valley, this scene resembles a pair of young couples, does it not? It is a style worthy of a painting. Snow lingers among the trees, the murmuring mountain stream plays like a koto, and in this deep, secluded, and elegant valley, if a skilled painter were to sketch it, a truly magnificent work would be produced. Ahahaha!'

Yoshikohime said, 'Kamehiko-sama, this is the most dangerous kuiki; do you not feel a measure of kinchiyō?'

Kamehiko replied, 'Softness can indeed control hardness; there is softness within hardness, and hardness within softness. To face the tekichi with iuiu, kankan, and shiyakushiyaku, maintaining yoyū, is the shinji of a hero and gauketsu. Aha, ha, ha, ha!'

From across the kahamuka, Otohiko called out,

'Oi, oi, Kamehiko! Accompany Yoshikohime and cross over to this kotsuchi; I have tidings for your ears.'

Kamehiko said, 'Very well, very well. Yoshikohime-sama, come, let us proceed. This tanigaha is deep, and as you are a gofujin, you must be troubled. If you wish, please allow yourself to be obusa upon the back of Kamehiko.'

Yoshikohime replied, 'I am arigata for your goshinsetsu. However, Kamehiko-sama, must one not handle one's own jibun? The oshihe of the Kamisama states that one must never rely upon others as a staff.'

Kamehiko said, 'Your kotoba is reasonable, yet I cannot help but feel anxious. In that case, let me take your hand. Come, let us proceed.'

So saying, he tucked up his hems and descended into the tanigaha.

Yoshikohime said, 'Kamehiko-sama, let me take your hand and lead you, for this is a taihen dangerous gekiriu.'

Thus, they were mindful of each other's peril with admirable virtue.

Kamehiko said, 'Ah, how arigata, how arigata! Were it not for this tanigaha, I could not clasp hands or form an alliance with Yoshikohime-sama. Namu Tanigaha Daimyōjin!'

Yoshikohime said, 'Ho, ho, ho, ho! Pray, keep your jests within reason.'

Kamehiko said, 'From jests come leisure; from a gourd comes a horse; from Mount Sangoku appears a peerless hero; from Mount Ōe appears an oni; from Onigajō appears a great serpent; from my mouth flows saliva; I am so joyful that tears flow. Aha, ha, ha, ha!'

Yoshikohime said, 'Ho, ho, ho, ho! Now, now, Kamehiko-sama, will you not steady yourself? Would it not be taihen should you slip?'

Kamehiko said, 'What care I if I slip or tumble? If you and I were to rejoice in this tanigaha, rolling about, lying down, and slipping into a double suicide, would that not be exquisite? A romance of a handsome man and a beautiful woman—perhaps in some future age, a successor to Hieda no Are will appear, write of this romance in the Reikai Monogatari with great exaggeration, and leave our names to posterity as a tale for all time. Aha, ha, ha, ha!'

Yoshikohime said, 'Kamehiko-sama, thanks to your help, we have crossed over safely.'

Kamehiko said, 'Ah, it was a relatively narrow river. If I walk while clasping hands and forming an alliance with you, even if the river were three or five ri wide, I would not think it far in the slightest.'

§ 5

As he started to run ahead, Murasakihime raised her hand and said, 'Moshi-moshi, sir, I shall provide the Goannai. There are deep, deep pitfalls there; it is dangerous to venture forth so recklessly.'

Kamehiko, paying no heed, dashed headlong toward the cavern, but suddenly, with a thud, he fell into a pitfall.

Murasakihime cried, 'Ah, how terrible, how terrible! Minasama, I pray you, assist him.'

Otohiko said, 'What a hasty fellow; it cannot be helped.'

As he moved to run toward him, Murasakihime said, 'Moshi-moshi, do not be in such a hurry. There are many pitfalls; I shall provide the Goannai. Please follow behind me.'

Otohiko replied, 'Ah, is that so? You are indeed attentive to every detail. In that case, I shall rely on you to lead. Yoshikohime-sama, pray excuse me, I shall go ahead. Please step in my footprints, for it is dangerous.'

Yoshikohime replied, 'Yes, Arigata.'
