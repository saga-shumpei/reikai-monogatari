Chapter 4: 羽化登仙 [615]

羽化登仙〔615〕

§ 1

Having rescued Osetsu from the cavern of the demons, whose very name strikes terror, Onihiko and his party of five, remaining in their naked state, were buffeted by the Hijiyamaoroshi wind; trembling and shivering, they placed Heisuke and his child in the lead and ascended the mountain path amidst the melting snow.

Ihakō spoke: 'Ah, Heisuke-sama, Onara-sama, for those of your advanced years, to ascend this mountain path is surely no ordinary task. Osetsu-sama, too, having been confined within the rock for so long, must have grown weak in her limbs. I pray you, as we are young, will you not allow us to carry you upon our backs?'

Heisuke replied: 'Nay, nay, that would be a terrible impropriety; if we were to do such a thing, our pilgrimage would cease to be a pilgrimage. If we must rely upon the kindness of others to proceed, then the old woman and I would rather remain within our kotatsu and offer our prayers from there.'

Ihakō responded: 'This is a dilemma, Heisuke-sama; that may be true, yet I pray you, consider it a favor to us and allow yourselves to be carried. To speak the truth, being exposed to the wind in our naked state, even we who are of such vigor cannot endure it; if you would allow us to carry you, our bodies would grow warm, and you yourselves would be able to travel with ease. This is a case of itsukiyoriyōtoku—I shall rejoice, and you shall travel in comfort. Kamisama does not desire that we should seek hardship for its own sake. Since He rejoices when we can practice shinjin with even the slightest ease, I pray you, think of it as riding a lean horse and rest upon my back.'

Heisuke said: 'My back is not an inn, and... how could one perch upon it as if one were a bird? Do not make a fool of me so excessively.'

Ihakō replied: 'Ah, this is a grave error in my speech. I pray you, if you would but command us to serve as your horses, I should be most grateful.'

Heisuke said: 'Come now, Onara, Osetsu, it is a most arduous slope. Shall we think of it as riding a bare horse and accept the offer?'

Onara laughed: 'Ahaha, to ride a horse with two legs, grandfather, is that not a bit perilous?'

Heisuke said: 'Nay, this fellow has six legs. He may be more reliable than a true horse.'

Ihakō said: 'Grandfather, what do you mean by six legs? If you count the three of us together, I shall be somewhat troubled...'

Heisuke said: 'What, you? If three people gather, that makes eighteen legs. You alone have six. If you combine your two physical legs with the four legs of your auxiliary guardian spirits, does that not make six?'

Onihiko laughed: 'Ahahaha, he mocks us. He treats us as if we were beasts.'

Heisuke said, 'It is a settled matter. You are creatures who cannot be distinguished as foxes, badgers, or demons. To be called six-legged is still a mercy.'

Onihiko said, 'What, do not mock us by saying such useless things in this cold... Tell me, Onitora, is it not cold?'

Onitora said, 'Yes, I feel it quite keenly... Excuse me, Grandfather, Grandmother, please, thinking to save us, climb upon our backs... Ah, it is cold, it is cold, help us.'

Heisuke said, 'Ah, if that is the case, Onara, Osetsu, shall we ride? They seem quite cold. If we make them sweat a little, they might warm up. Thinking that this too is a virtuous act of pilgrimage to the Kami, I shall endure even if it is a little painful. In return, you lot, you must not drop us; if you drop us, it will be the end, and the divine punishment of the Kami will strike you, so you had best accompany us with great care.'

'Oh, for your immediate consent, Onihiko, Onitora, it is the height of joy and leaping for gladness for our bodies.'

Heisuke said, 'Hey, Hiko, Tora, who would ride such unruly horses as you? The one I shall ride is Ihauma. The old woman shall ride on the back of Kanuma, and Osetsu shall ride on the back of Ichiuma. Truly, I thank you for your great kindness.'

'Is there truly no way for us to receive your favor?'

Heisuke said, 'Eh, no matter how much money you give me, I will not endure riding on the likes of you. My body would be defiled. Even an inch-long insect has a soul of five bu. Do you think there are cowards who would clear their grudges just because they were given a ride after being subjected to terrible treatment? To the very end, I shall not be under your care.'

Onitora said, 'Ah, what a deep-seated old man. However, this is also rust born from one's own body... Eh, it cannot be helped, it is cold, it is cold, my body and everything seem as if they will freeze. I wonder if we shall successfully freeze to death at the Hijiyama Pass... Hey Onihiko, let us... since there is no other way, let us take advantage of being naked and wrestle to warm our bodies.'

'Oh, that is right. You have thought of a good thing.'

The two stood in a slightly wider place, and putting strength into it from both sides, they began to push against each other. Putting in too much strength, at the moment Onihiko staggered, the two, remaining stark naked, fell into the bottom of the valley, grazing against the steep slope where the miscellaneous trees grew thick. While being carried on their backs, Heisuke...

"Osetsu, Osetsu, what has happened, what has happened? Does your stomach ache? Why do you weep?"

Thus, from both sides, the Jii and the Baa, with faces contorted in a Shiga-like rage and complexions changed, questioned her. Wiping away her tears, Osetsu replied:

"Grandfather, Grandmother, I beseech you to undergo Kaishin. Those rough men, Iwa-sama, Kan-sama, and Kunugi-sama, have fulfilled the Mikokoro of Ōkamisama, have become those magnificent goddesses of Heiwa, and have stood to serve the Goyō of Kamisama. So saying, she fell into a fit of weeping once more, crying out with a loud 'Watsu'. At this moment, a voice came from Tenjiyō:

"Onihiko, Onitora, I grant you the Hagoromo which I now send down from Ten. The sincerity of your Kaishin has finally reached Ten."

Onihiko and Onitora, in their Uchiyoroko, were as if ready to take flight; both Riyōnin prostrated themselves upon the Daichi and said,

"Hahaa, Hatsu."

Having said this, they were overcome by tears of joy. As the two suddenly raised their faces, a Hagoromo of indescribable beauty was floating at a distance of one or two shaku from the Chijiyō. The moment they reached out to pick it up, it adhered perfectly to their bodies. Gradually the Hagoromo expanded, and their bodies rose naturally; as the two flew through the air, moving forward, backward, left, and right, they said,

"Heisuke-sama, Onara-sama, Osetsu-sama, farewell, we go ahead."

So saying, they soared leisurely through the air, heading toward the sacred ground of Manai-ga-take. The three left behind stood in astonishment, gazing at this spectacle without uttering a word.

Heisuke said, "Ah, human beings are creatures whose reasons are beyond understanding. A good man like me is exposed to the cold wind on this mountain, my daughter has grown thin and weak, and though we three, parent and child, have managed to come this far, what has become of the five kenzoku of Oeyama? To receive such magnificent garments from Ten, and to become beings of Uka-tōsen, free and at liberty. Kamisama is too much, truly too much. If such men can become Tennin, it would seem only right that we three, parent and child, should also be made into magnificent Tennin. Ah, is this, too, a matter of the fate and origin of our Mitama, that we can never attain success?"

Onara said, 'Father, nothing in this world proceeds otherwise than by the Oboshimeshi of Kamisama. There are Mitama that appear evil to the eyes of Ningen, yet are good; and when Ningen, in their own conceit, deem themselves righteous and grow full of Unubore, they fall into Jiyadau before they know it and suffer. The three of them proceeded, leaning upon their staves, along the snow-laden path toward the place where Toyokuni-hime-no-Mikoto of Manaigahara was appearing.

Incidentally, the reason Onihiko, Onitora, and the other three attained Uka-tōsen was that, in truth, they could not achieve thorough repentance in their physical bodies, and furthermore, lacking the qualifications to participate in the divine work, they were caused to undergo a change of land (freezing to death) by the mercy of the divine realm, and were saved in the heavenly realm to participate in the divine work.
