Chapter 16: Castle 攻 [627]

城攻〔627〕

§ 1

The cold wind blows upon Fukuchiyama, where the Onigajō castle towers into the Ōzora of the Ushitora; fortified with the Chibiki rock, it is the eternal Sumika of Onikumawake, a Kinjiyōtetsupeki which, if guarded by a single Itsusotsu, cannot be assailed by Bansotsu, serving as a zone of Zetsutaiteki Anzen against the Kōgeki of the enemy. Within the Yahirodono built inside the Gankutsu, the Tōriyō of the fortress, Onikumawake, together with his Inochi-no-niyōbau, the dark-complexioned Mukadehime, and a multitude of Ninsoku, stands guard day and night in Kengo.

Onikumawake summons his allies, including Arataka and Onitaka, whom he relies upon as his Futokorogatana, as well as Murasakihime and the soldiers of Tanshiu of beautiful Mimekatachi, to hold a grand council to deliberate on the defense against the advance of the Kototama-tai led by Princess Etsuko, the propagandist of the Sangōkyō; the multitude of subordinates surround the Gankutsu-do in ten and twenty layers, their martial appearance in preparation for the enemy's assault being a sight to behold.

Onikumawake addressed them all, saying: 'The wretches of the Sangōkyō have trampled upon the mansion of the Great General Onikumahiko residing at Ōeyama, and advancing further, have destroyed the Gankutsu of Mitake; now, reports reach us that they are pressing upon this main castle with but a small divine army. I possess a renowned great army and have constructed such a Kengo Kinjiyōtetsupeki that I need not yield to the assault of any hero, champion, or demon. Nevertheless, negligence is a great enemy; you shall henceforth lead your many subordinates, confront the enemy with all available weapons, and annihilate them in a single battle. If you have any opinions regarding the operational plan for this, present them before me.'

Upon this strict command, Arataka stepped forward and said: 'Great General, your command is reasonable, yet as you know, Onikumahiko of Ōeyama, despite possessing a multitude of allies, was defeated by a mere four propagandists and fled like mist into the clouds. It is truly unreliable to face the heaven-sent divine army with weapons alone. If there be any good method, pray, show it to us.'

Mukadehime replied: 'Even if you say so, in this Onigajō, we have no weapons against the enemy other than spears, halberds, and swords. What is your intention in this matter? If you have a good strategy, present it without reserve.'

Onitaka said: 'With reverence, I speak to Onikumawake and Mukadehime-sama. The enemy approaches with Kototama and possesses divine power that radiates the five-colored spiritual light, driving and tormenting their foes in every direction; thus, it is difficult to prevail as things stand. Fortunately, I have been transmitted by a strange white-haired divine being upon the summit of Mount Mitake a divine power that can shake heaven and earth with a single cry, and command wind, rain, thunder, and lightning with a single word. Now, all shall be well; even if there were tens of thousands of weapons, if the bodies of our subordinates are bound by spirit, there is nothing that can be done. I believe that the supreme strategy is to annihilate the enemy who approaches with Kototama by using Kototama ourselves.'

Onikumawake asked, 'Even if you alone know how to apply the Kototama, what do you intend to do regarding the other Bushiyō?'

Onitaka replied, 'With all due respect, Arataka, Tanshiu, and Murasakihime have all, at the same time as myself, received the transmission of the miraculous arts of the Kototama of divine transformation and mystery from the Shinjin; therefore, I pray you, have no Goshinpai. Even if the Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō should approach with their own miraculous spiritual arts, we shall use the power of our Kototama to harass and confound them in every possible way. You must not trouble your many Buka or send them forth with Kiyōki; we certainly have the Shiyōsan.'

As he spoke these words with an air of nonchalance, Onikumawake and Mukadehime filled their faces with smiles.

'If that be so, Onitaka, I command you to take command of the entire army; do not be negligent. I shall now ascend to the high tower to observe the state of your fierce battle. Mukadehime, come with me.'

So saying, they departed from that place and leisurely slipped into an inner chamber.

Suddenly, pricking up their ears at the sound of the Sendenka being chanted, Onitaka cried out, 'Hear me, all of you! The enemy has approached close at hand; prepare yourselves for defense.'

Arataka exclaimed, 'Even if the Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō—Etsukohime, Otohiko, Kamehiko, Aohyōtanhiko, the bent-waisted Natsuhiko, and Tsunehiko with eyes slanted like a fox—should come to attack, I shall wield the secret arts of Sun and Wu, nay, I shall manifest the miraculous arts of the Kototama of divine transformation and mystery, drive the enemy into a thousand changes and ten thousand transformations, and in an instant, we shall raise the shout of victory.'

His face was filled with courage and spirit, and his majestic air swept across the surroundings with such bravery.

All of them clapped their hands and raised a simultaneous shout of 'Uro-uro,' a scene as if the very mountains were crumbling.

Stirred by the sound of the Sendenka, Onitaka, Arataka, and the rest of the Kototama-tai appeared in the corridor and began to prepare their defense against the barrage of Kototama from the approaching divine army.

Kamehiko, a Bushiyō of the attacking force, began to chant the Sendenka with a clear voice:

Kami appears upon the surface, / To distinguish between the good and the evil. / The fortune of Onikumawake is exhausted, / The world that is perishing is like the second month. / The moon of Ananai shines in the vast sky, / Bright and clear, illuminating the heads of the demons. / I, Kamehiko, who celebrate the ten thousand generations, / Advance to attack the fortress of the demons. / The Kototama I chant are the hundred deities of heaven and earth, / And the eight million gods. / Yield, O Onigajo! / Subdue the gathering crooked ones, / And scatter them toward the western sea. / The Toyoashihara no Mizuho no Kuni, / Is illuminated everywhere by the Kototama. / Behold the water and fire of truth! / Even if you come to attack with spears, halberds, and swords, / With their points aligned, / The Sumeru-Ōkami shall protect us.'

The Magokoro of I, Kamehiko, is a Niyoihotsushiyu that fire cannot burn nor water drown; an Ikumitama that shall not decay for Yorozuyo. To restore the Mihashira of the jewel and to Kotomu and harmonize, I, Kamehiko, now advance. Thus having finished chanting, the many soldiers guarding the perimeter against the Senden-ka of Kamehiko shook their heads and for a while manifested a state of agony.

The young and fair-faced Tanshu, in order to respond to the Kototama of Kamehiko, unfurled his white fan and, waving it left and right, chanted:

Rising from the Ihayado, Shikakō once again brandished his fan, and facing the attacking force, commenced the Kototamasen with a Daionzeu. The song was as follows:

'To the Ōkami of Toyokunihime, who appeared upon the plains of Manai, the minister of the Kami, Murasakihime, departed from the capital. Accompanied by two attendants, a horse and a deer, she crossed mountains, valleys, and rivers, traversing the plains of Ōno and the villages. As they arrived before the plains of Manai, Arataka and Onitaka, who were entrenched in the Ihayado of Mitake, were deceived by their Teshita Magami. Believing the words that the plains of Manai were in this direction, they were lured into the dark Ihayado and cast into a deep pit; the horse and the deer thus saw a fool’s errand. Murasakihime, fortunately, by virtue of having shed her rough skin, was noticed by Onitaka and Arataka and saved. Revered as a queen, she took possession of the Ihayado and wielded authority with terrifying power. Soon there came the missionary of the teachings of the Kami of Asahashira, whose appearance and form were those of Etsukohime. Oto-hiko, the sound of the wind blowing through the pines, and the stiff-backed Kamehiko came lumbering to the mouth of the valley. Murasakihime, the minister of the Kami, was no old crone washing clothes with a splashing sound, but a figure of elegant grace. Kamehiko, struck with terror, came to the front of the Ihayado, stumbling and sliding down the slope overgrown with thorns, and fell with a thud into an unexpected pit, landing upon his backside.

Whether he landed or not, I know not. Thereafter, the three came creeping, lured by Murasakihime, and entered the Ihayado. Because she saved me, the two then entered into the faith of the teachings of the Kami of Asahashira. At the summit of Mount Mitake, they encountered a wind that broke all records, and unable to endure it, they crawled on all fours until they reached the thicket of trees. As they all rested there and slept as soundly as if they were on a voyage to Shirakawa, Onitaka and Arataka arrived. They dreamt that the deer and the horse were bound with their hands behind their backs and thrust once more into the Ihayado. Awakening in great astonishment, they wandered about the area while admiring the moonlight.

A black shadow appeared, and after much rubbing, kneading, and quarreling, they finally persuaded the two fellows, Onitaka and Arataka, and guided them to the Sangokyō. By the command of Princess Etsuko, they returned, or so it seemed on the surface. But wait, this is wrong. It was not I who said it; I was so happy that I made a mistake. Hey, Natsuhiko and Tsunehiko! Oops, I slipped in the ash shed, covered in ash and pitch black. Now that it has come to this, there is no help for it. Others are one thing, but I, from this moment on, shall let the general of Onikumawake bite my backside. Do not be angry, Mukade-hime. The skin of the lie has been carelessly peeled away; my Mitama is still dark.

The Kototamasen is all in vain. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase. The frog, before it knew it, exposed its own belly from its mouth. Since it is no longer possible, it flees; the missionary of the Sangokyō. Truly, I submit; I pray you, forgive me.'

With these words, he tucked up his skirts and fled at full speed, running toward the battle lines of Oto-hiko in a comical fashion. Princess Etsuko, Oto-hiko, and Kamehiko burst into laughter at the absurdity, rolling on the ground in mirth.
