Chapter 10: 四百種病 [621]

四百種病〔621〕

§ 1

Onara, who had been hastening with bated breath to make Sanpai at the precious Hōza of Manai-ga-hara, was unexpectedly persuaded by Kurohime, an Onnagauketsu who held the Kenyaku of the Uranai Kyō, and her Shinki underwent an Itsupen; turning her palms and the soles of her feet, she walked briskly, accompanying the Itsukau of Kurohime, and at last arrived at the Fuseya in Tanbamura.

Onara said, 'Excuse me, Taishiyōsama of the Uranai Kyō, this is my Abaraya. Pray, do come in. You must be suffering from much Kutabire.'

Kurohime replied, 'What, if I were to suffer Kutabire from a mere Yukimichi such as this, how could I ever accomplish the Goyō of the Shinkai of the Sanzensekai? There are no such Yowamushi in the Uranai Kyō, ho ho ho.'

Onara said, 'Please take care as you enter, for the Onioto of Ōeyama has been dug here; if you enter carelessly, a Taihen matter shall occur. Pray, follow exactly where I tread, for if you step even one Hitoashi astray, you shall fall into an Otoshiana.'

Kurohime said, 'What excellent Yōjin you have, ah, I am truly Kanshin, truly Kanshin. No matter what, we live in an age where evil gods of the Mizu-no-mitama appear at the Manai of Hinu, so one must surely exercise at least this much caution. Come, Teru-sama, Kiyo-sama, follow in my footsteps.'

Onara said, 'It is now safe. Pray, do come up.'

Kurohime said, 'Ha, so Heisuke-dono fell while drawing water from this well. Truly, it is a dangerous-looking well. Obā-sama, you are also quite aged, so pray take care.'

Onara said, 'I thank you. My daughter shall surely be pleased.'

Osetsu was in a trance, calling out, 'Aohiko-sama, Aohiko-sama.'

Kurohime said, 'Now, now, I shall offer a prayer to the gods for her. But regarding that, it is a matter to be decided after you have listened well to my words. Obā-sama, will you listen?'

Onara said, 'I pray you, let me hear the words of the merciful gods.'

Kurohime said, 'The illness of this girl is, in its entirety, a strange malady. There are many illnesses, known as the four hundred kinds of disease. Among them, one hundred kinds will heal even if left alone. Another one hundred kinds will recover with medicine and a physician. Yet another one hundred kinds can only be healed by the gods. And the final one hundred kinds cannot be healed by gods, physicians, or medicine. This is what is called the four hundred kinds of disease.'

Kurohime continued, 'This girl suffers from an illness that can never be healed unless it be through the faith in the gods, which I mentioned as the third kind. It is a strange and peculiar illness that even a physician or the waters of Arima could not cure. This Aohiko whom she calls for is likely a missionary of the Uranai Kyō whom I employ; he is a man who has recently become infatuated with the San-iku Kyō and was wandering about following a man named Oto-hiko.'

Kurohime concluded, 'When this girl recovers, I shall take Aohiko as an adopted son, and through the girl, I shall persuade Aohiko to return to the old Uranai Kyō; this is undoubtedly an illness orchestrated by the gods. Obā-sama, unless you understand this well, it cannot be healed.'
