Chapter 9: Gloom の River 畔 [1926]

闇の河畔〔1926〕

§ 1

Parted from her beloved spouse Hodohe, yearning for the deity Ōmotoakitsuo with a heart too restless like flying arrows and lifted shields, Asakahime Mikoto, completely alone, left Takachihoyama behind and whipped the back of her white horse. As cherry blossoms scattered in the evening wind, ignoring the remonstrances of the myriad deities even in the twilight of her journey, she advanced. Pushing aside all the eighty malignant deities obstructing the narrow mountain paths with living kototama, trampling them under her horse's hooves, and determined to accomplish her original resolve, she bravely advanced and set forth.

The curtain of darkness grew ever deeper, wrapping the vast earth in its entirety, and an atmosphere of tragic desolation drifted in all four directions.

Asakahime no Kami arrived at a certain riverbank, and while letting her horse drink from the dark currents, she sang in a hushed voice.

'O glorious, how glorious! Where now is my beloved spouse Kimi? Seeking and wandering forth on a summer night, the starlight in the moonlit sky is wrapped in the clouds of the murky firmament, a pitch-dark night where one cannot distinguish even an iris. The river's current shines whitely from north to south, and hearing the murmuring ripples whisper makes it so lonely. An endless, boundless great moor, my heart is not in darkness, but the longing for my spouse has piled up and piled up until today's journey has turned into eternal darkness. Illuminate this with divine grace and minute detail, O Asakahime no Kami, who has served for long years at the sacred ground of Takachihoyama. If the Lord Great Deity has a heart, cross this river easily for me. Though the steed of a thousand leagues neighs, there is no way to cross the deep and shallow white waves of this dark river. Protect me, O Imisumi, I beg for the grace of the Kami.'

Standing on the bank of the river of eternal darkness, she thinks within: The darkness of a heart clouded by love; stitching through a streak of darkness, the whitely flowing rapids resemble even me. Rushing through a dark field without even starlight, taking the sound of the horse's hooves as strength, there is surely no other way to advance than with the strength of the horse's neighing. If there were no darkness and night in the heavenly realm, my departure too would be peaceful. Though the night grows late and I try to sleep, I cannot sleep because the steed of my heart is racing forth. To trace across the vast and boundless wilderness is solely for the sake of yearning for my spouse. O darkness, O darkness, depart quickly, O morning sun, rise quickly and protect me. There is no way to extinguish the burning thoughts of fireflies smoothly stitching through the darkness. Do the fireflies too yearn for the spirit of water, faintly glowing upon the small grasses of the shore?

The early summer night has grown deep, and even the wind blowing upon my sleeves is dampened with tears. Tears of a myriad bowls flow on the night of darkness; standing at the river rapids, a burning firefly. How shall I cross this dark river, I wonder with endless thoughts in my sorrow.

Just as she was reciting these divine poems, the eighty malignant deities transformed into pale blue fireballs, drawing long tails from downriver and advancing while beating waves into the dark sky.

Asakahime no Kami thought that these must be the physical manifestations of the malignant deities, and locking her hands together in an impregnable posture that would not leak even water, she prayed:

'One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, a hundred, a thousand, ten thousand, a myriad deities of Magatsu, retreat from this strange fire!'

Though she prayed thus, the fireball showed no concern whatsoever, advanced close to Asakahime no Kami, and illuminating the surroundings as bright as midday, it suddenly transformed into a monster with one eye and eight mouths. Spewing out gigantic wasps from its mouth toward the Goddess, it attempted to bite her person. The horse was startled and leaped about back and forth, suddenly plunging deep into the middle of the river and sinking deep beneath the water. Asakahime no Kami, a steadfast Goddess, declared:

'I am she who has served as the Kami of the Mihishiro! Do not obstruct me, ye eighty malignant deities. In the heavenly realm born of the water and fire of the Lord Kami, what are you obstructing? Begone, Magatsu! I am none other than Asakahime no Kami, the victorious deity whose water and fire of the kototama are fully suffused!'

Though she recited these divine poems, the monster did not easily depart, but increasingly spewed out countless wasps, attempting to attack the Goddess's whole body. Thereupon, the Goddess devised a plan, taking out a flint and steel from her bosom, and striking them together toward the malignant deities with a sharp clack. Startled by the flash of true fire that suddenly burst forth, the monster's form vanished like smoke without a trace, leaving only the faint wind blowing across the field and the sound of the ripples audible.

§ 2

Seeing this Kuōkei, the Kami Asakahime felt her Yūki increase a hundredfold; tucking her flint into her bosom and bringing her Riyōte together, she turned toward Ten and chanted a song of Iyahi.

"The grace of the Lord God is awe-inspiring! Magatsuhi has vanished away in the power of Mahi. Driving away the fierce and ugly rage of Magakami, I give thanks for the Miizu of the Lord Ōkami. My steed has appeared from the depths of the river, ever since the ugly Magatsu vanished away. Diving through the Minasoko, how the noble Hayakoma escaped the affliction of the bees! The foolish work of Magatsuhi, turned into ikusenman bees, has perished in the true fire. The eastern sky gradually dawns; a new day is about to rise. Bathed in the new Hikage, I shall advance along the wild road where Magatsuhi rages madly. To be attacked by Magatsuhi in the pitch darkness of Tokoyami—this, too, is for the love of my Kimi. If there be a day when I meet my dear Kimi, I will fear no obstruction from ikuman Magatsuhi. For the sake of my dear Kimi, to whom I have dedicated my Inochi of the jewel-string, I will fear no affliction whatever. My heart, longing for Kimi, burns like a flame until the bottom of the river's flow is reached."

Just as she was thus chanting the Miuta, the voice of a magpie echoed faintly, and from the valley of Takateruyama, the Kami of the heavenly sun rose leisurely.

"Clear is the voice of the magpie announcing the dawn, echoing widely across the banks of the Sashi River. The curtain of darkness retreats to the depths of the great moor, and the light of the heavenly sun has risen. If it be thus, I shall fear nothing; even the deep blue river I shall easily cross."

Chanting thus, she sprang lightly upon the back of her steed, loosened the belly-band of the horse, grasped its mane firmly, and together with the steed crossed the deep, fierce current of the water, at length arriving at the opposite bank.

Generally, when crossing a deep current on horseback, one loosens the belly-band, makes the horse leap into the water, grasps the mane in one hand, and lets both horse and rider float together in the water to swim across; this is called the method of the water-horse.

The Kami Asakahime deeply comprehended the method of the water-horse, and by employing such a method, she was able to arrive safely at the farther shore.

"Both I and my steed have safely crossed the Sashi River, now that the art of the water-horse has appeared. Jade saddle, reins, and whip have all grown wet; perhaps I shall rest for a while and dry them in the sun. An innocent beast indeed, peacefully nibbling the grass at the riverbank! The food that the Hayakoma should eat is full to overflowing, since I am a body that sustains its life with grass. I too, by inhaling the water and fire of the Lord Ōkami, have preserved my long Inochi. The nourishment of the jewel-string Inochi is the blessing of the pure water and fire of Kototama. When the day breaks, there is not even a shadow of the eighty Magatsu, and the voices of insects ring clear and serene. The magpie sings of the morning, and the true crane celebrates the heavenly realm on the banks of the Sashi River. Flowers of various kinds bloom on nameless grasses, and the water of the Sashi River rapids is fragrant. Clouds well up above the ridge of the Takachihine Mountain; how the Kami of the shrine must be faring! In the shrine after my departure, the myriad Kami are gathering together and deliberating, no doubt. The land of the West is far; though I think of my steed as a horse of ten thousand leagues, it is lonely. To advance alone through the wilderness where the Magakami rage and run wild is because I yearn for my Kimi. The great sky is without a single cloud, clearing up to brighten my departure. Now that the saddle of my steed has dried completely, I will grasp the reins and advance once more."

Hereupon the Kami Asakahime mounted the back of the steed once more, and proceeded without a destination toward the southeast across the great moor where green grasses were sprouting.
