Chapter 11: 栄 Castle の Mountain 彦 [1928]

栄城の山彦〔1928〕

§ 1

Crossing the wilderness of Senri, as the Kami Asakahime at last arrived at the foot of Mount Sakaki in the twilight glowing with the new moon, the five Kami serving in the sanctuary of Mount Sakaki, foreknowing it through the wild goose message sent from the sanctuary of Mount Takachiho, welcomed the Himegami in bustling readiness.

The Kami Asakahime, turning toward the host of Kami, chanted a Miuta:

'Hearing it said to be the trace linked to the Kami Akitsuo, is this mountain of Sakaki a sacred ground? The light of the evening moon shines clearly upon the mountain's edge; in such an evening have I come hither.'

Here the Kami Hatatsukurio chanted a Miuta:

'To the Himegami of the eight pillars of the renowned sanctuary of Takachiho, how well you have come! I, who beheld the message of the wild goose letter, welcome your august coming forth, O Mount Sakaki, deep in the ties of the Mizumitama, where the moonlight is especially fair.'

The Kami Asakahime chanted a Miuta:

'Finding the light of the new moon refreshing, I have now come seeking Mount Sakaki. Deep is the color of the pines upon the ridge of Mount Sakaki, and the casting moonlight is ever more refreshing. Forsaking the station of the Mihishirogami of the eight pillars, I make a long journey to meet Kimi. Tonight I shall take lodging for a single night on Mount Sakaki; when the sun that traverses heaven sinks into the earth, even the fleet horse on the long journey of the fowl's tail hath grown utterly weary, so grant me lodging.'

The Kami Hatatsukurio chanted a Miuta in reply:

'Awe-inspiring it is to hear your august words, O Mihishiro Himegami of the eight pillars; may you dwell in peace. The bottom of the flowing Misogi River is pure, and the sickle-moon is made to float thereon. O Mizumitama born as the spirit of Tsukiyomi, well indeed have you come, O Mihishiro Himegami! This day when the Himegami hath come, the moonlight too is yet more keen and the wind blows clear and bright. Making this auspicious day of Mount Sakaki the limit, may this land of reeds flourish in peace.'

The Kami Sankahitorio chanted a Miuta:

'Spring having departed, the cherry blossoms of the peaks—the Kami Sankahitorio shall welcome the noble Himegami. Unto Mount Sakaki, where the scene of early summer drifts, hath the Himegami like a flower come forth. From the time I saw the message of the wild goose, morn and eve have I awaited with yearning your august coming forth. How glorious is the descent from the great sanctuary of the distant Takamanohara of Asakahimegami! The attire of the shining Asakahimegami must be such that even the moon and flowers cannot surpass. The breeze of the early summer evening, refreshing, shook the evergreens of Mount Sakaki. The evergreens, exulting and rejoicing, awaited the form of the Himegami like a flower. In the pure Misogi River murmuring along, floats the form of the Himegami like a flower. Even apart from that, above its purity, in the clear spring water how lovely is the reflecting august figure of Kimi.'

The Kami Nakawaritorio chanted a Miuta:

'Since I am the Kami who divides the midst of heaven and earth, I shall guard the path of your august journey. Dividing the midst of the Misogi River, the mountains, and the great plain, it gushed and flowed forth mightily and purely. Hearing the high neighing of the fleet horse and the rustling of the grasses as they grow, I look and behold, Kimi hath come. By all means rest yourself, laying down your body weary from the long journey.'

The Kami Asakahime chanted a Miuta:

'How precious is the sincere heart of the hundred Kami; however many ages pass, I shall never forget it. Heaping up pillow-grass journeys, at the words of the Kami filled with deep feeling, I have been moved to tears. Be that as it may, leave me aside, and grant a bedding and fodder unto the fleet horse.'

The Kami Sayofurenokami chanted a Miuta:

'Awe-inspiring even to name is the joy of this day welcoming the Kami of the Mihishiro. Even the blowing wind is fragrant out of season on Mount Sakaki; this sacred ground is the realm of eternal peace. I ponder the manliness of Kimi who came hither, crossing far and wide the endless wilderness. How manly must Kimi have been who came across the fiercely raging and wild wilderness of Magatsumi! Upon that shining august figure of yours, surely all the foul Magatsu hid their shadows.'

The Kami Shinakohitorio chanted a Miuta:

'Receiving the august word of the Kami Akitsuo, I have come to serve in the great sanctuary. The sanctuary standing purely upon the ridge of Mount Sakaki is where the divine spirit of the Lord Great Kami is enshrined. The mountain of Sakaki, for which I begged the divine decree of the Kami Akitsuo, was indeed a sacred ground. Upon the coming forth of the related Asakahime, the mountain of Sakaki hath smiled and flourished. Even the true crane nesting in the pines of the evergreen hath sung in celebration of the coming forth of Kimi.'

The Kami Shinakohitorio chanted a Miuta:

'Receiving the august word of the Kami Akitsuo, I have come to serve in the great sanctuary. The sanctuary standing purely upon the ridge of Mount Sakaki is where the divine spirit of the Lord Great Kami is enshrined. The mountain of Sakaki, for which I begged the divine decree of the Kami Akitsuo, was indeed a sacred ground. Upon the coming forth of the related Asakahime, the mountain of Sakaki hath smiled and flourished. Even the true crane nesting in the pines of the evergreen hath sung in celebration of the coming forth of Kimi.'

原文（近代文語）

千里の荒野を渉りて、朝香比女の神は栄城山の麓に、新月の輝く黄昏時漸く着き給へば、栄城山の宮居に仕ふる五柱の神々は、高地秀山の宮居より遣はし給ひたる雁の御文によりて前知し給ひ、賑々しく比女神を迎へ給ふ。

朝香比女の神は諸神に向ひ、御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『顕津男の神の由縁の御跡と聞く 栄城の山はこれの聖所なりや 夕月の光はさやかに山の端に かかる夕べを吾来つるかも』

茲に機造男の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『名に高き高地秀の宮居の八柱の 比女神にますかよくも来ませり 雁の文の便りを見しわれは 公の出でまし迎へまつるも 瑞御霊由縁の深き栄城山の 月の光はことさらによし』

朝香比女の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『新月の光を爽けみ吾はいま 栄城の山にたづね来にけり 栄城山尾の上の松の色深み さす月光はいよよ爽けし 八柱の御樋代神の位置を捨てて 岐美に会はまく長の旅すも 栄城山に今宵一夜の宿からむ 天渡る日も地に沈めば 鶏の尾の長き旅路に駿馬も 疲れ果てたり宿をたまはれ』

機造男の神は、答の御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『御言葉を聞くも畏し八柱の 御樋代比女神安くましませ 禊川流るる水の底清み 利鎌の月は浮ばせたまへり 月読の御霊と生れし瑞御霊の 御樋代比女神よくも来ませるよ 比女神の来ませる今日は月光も 一しほ冴えて風澄みきらふ 栄城山今日の吉日を限りとし この国原は安く栄えむ』

散花男の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『春去りて峰の桜も散花男の 神はみどりの公を迎へむ 初夏の景色ただよふ栄城山に 花なる公は出でましにけり 雁の便り見しより朝夕を 公の出でまし待ち佗びにけり 久方の高天原の大宮居ゆ 天降り給ひし朝香比女神天晴れ 輝ける朝香比女神の粧ひは 月さへ花さへ及ばざるべし 初夏の夕べの風はすずやかに 栄城の山の常磐樹ゆすりつ 常磐樹は勇み悦びさゆれつつ 花なる公のすがた待ち居り 潺々と流るるきよき禊川に 花なる公のすがた浮べる 只さへも清きが上に真清水に うつろふ公の御姿うるはし』

中割男の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『天地の中を割男の神なれば 公の行手を守りまつらむ 禊川山と大野の中割きて 雄々しく清しくたぎち流しつ 駿馬のいななき高く草の生ゆ 聞ゆと見れば公は来ませる 兎も角も休ませたまへ長旅の 疲れ給ひし身を横たへて』

朝香比女の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『ありがたし百神等の真心は 幾世経るとも吾は忘れじ 草枕旅を重ねて情ある 神の言葉に涙しにけり ともかくも吾はさて置き駿馬の 褥と餌を与へたまはれ』

小夜更の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『掛巻も綾に畏き御樋代の 神を迎ふる今日のうれしさ 吹く風も非時かをる栄城山の これの聖所は常安の国土よ 果しなき荒野をわたりはろばろと 来ませる公の雄々しさ思ふ 曲津見の伊猛り荒ぶ荒野原を わたり来し公の雄々しくもあるか 輝けるその御姿にもろもろの 醜の曲津は影かくしけむ』

親幸男の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『顕津男の神の神言を蒙りて われ大宮居に仕へ来にけり 栄城山尾の上に清しく立つ宮居は 主の大神の神霊祀れる 顕津男の神の神勅乞ひましし 栄城の山は聖所なりけり 由縁ある朝香の比女の出でましに 栄城の山は笑みさかえぬる 常磐樹の松に巣ぐへる真鶴も 公の出でまし寿ぎてうたへり』

§ 2

The deity Asakahime no Kami descended lightly from the back of her horse, performed misogi briefly in the Misogigaha River, and, guarded by the five deities, entered the resting place of the kamigami situated on the mid-slope of Mount Sakakiyama, where she rested from the fatigue of her long journey while having her reflective poems chanted.

'Selected as one of the eight mihishirogami, vainly have the years passed by for me. Crossing far and wide over the banri wilderness, I made pilgrimage to the miya of Amatsutakahi. Having made pilgrimage to the miya of Hisakata no Tsukushi, I have come to realize the path I must tread. Returning far and wide to the miya of Takachiho, my heart became more and more unsettled. Thinking this was not the miya I should serve eternally, I whipped my horse and departed. Will the seven mihishirogami pray for me before the presence of Kami? With my murakimo heart thus resolute, how could I ever change my initial resolve? Hoping to cross the sazui river of Tokoyami, I went forth and encountered the yaso magatsuhi. Proclaiming to the limit of the power of Kototama, when the true fire flashed forth, the magatsuhi vanished. Bathed in the light of Amatsubi, across the vast moor, straddling my horse, I have come even to this place. Deep is the bond with my spouse on the slopes of Mount Sakakiyama, and this evening my heart has grown pure. Coming here, I have forgotten the fatigue of travel. Amidst the warm hearts of the myriad kamigami, the neighing of steed is no longer heard. Easily shall I weave dreams of my journey. Though I know not the whereabouts of my spouse, within my true heart I yearn to meet him. Coming to this sanctuary of Mount Sakakiyama and seeing it, the message of my spouse is like the koochinashi flower. In the evening twilight where the koochinashi flower is fragrant, within this sanctuary, how my heart is filled with thought! I, the himegami of the mihishiro, serving in the divine work of child-bearing, feel my heart disturbed. Thinking to calm the steed of my heart that rages wildly, yet it is to no avail upon this burning road of love. The dew resting upon the leaf-tips of the trees of Mount Sakakiyama, glows as it harbors the spirit of the moon. Can there be an instance where the moonlight does not lodge within the dew of me, the himegami of the mihishiro? Chasing the moonlight that spreads ever wider day by day, gazing upon it, I set forth on a joyful journey. Because of my yearning, I forsook the lofty station of the eight mihishirogami to come hither. Though I descend as one of the eighty hime of the mihishiro, my heart is fulfilled since I have met my spouse.'

The deity Hatasukunoo no Kami chants an imperial poem:

'Allow me this evening, which lacks the means to console you, oh noble one who has come from afar, to be forgiven. Since the spouse whom you followed is not seated here, lonely indeed must you be, oh himegami of the mihishiro. With all the strength of my murakimo heart, with all my body, I think to devote myself to comforting you, oh himegami. Oh youthful land of Mount Sakasa, you lamented that there was nothing to comfort the himegami.'

The deity Asakahime no Kami chants a responsive poem:

'Oh awe-inspiring Kototama of the kamigami, knowing not even words of gratitude, coming here, my heart for the first time has grown calm. Amidst the green of the pines of Mount Sakakiyama, floating upon the pure current of the Misogigaha River, I have been revived by the light of the evening moon. Both the great sky and the riverbed are bright with clear moonlight, comforting my departure upon this journey. On the evening when I think of my spouse who emerged from the spirit of the moon in the great sky, even though my spouse be in distant lands many myriads of ri away, to the limit of my soul shall I wander forth to follow him.'

The deity Sankarano no Kami chants a poem:

'The white plum and cherry blossoms on the peak of Mount Sakakiyama have gradually scattered, and the peonies are fragrant. The dew resting upon the crimson peony flowers is surely the manifestation of a crimson heart. Yamahime has dyed the peony flowers crimson and has attired herself in summer robes. Into this mountain where the atmosphere of early summer drifts, we have welcomed you of the warm heart. The moon has already hidden behind the rear of Mount Sakakiyama, and darkness draws near. Clear is the light of the stars twinkling in the great sky, and the voice of the owl is heard in the forest. Though it be a turbid voice, when I hear the cry of the owl, indeed I find it yearning.'

The deity Nakawarano no Kami chants an imperial poem:

'Though I have served in the great miya for how many years have passed, such radiance as today's I have not yet seen. Sparkling and glowing brilliantly, the himegami presents a dazzling figure indeed. When dawn breaks, upon the summit of Mount Sakakiyama, I shall guide you to the sanctuary of the miya. If I look up, spanning from north to south, there are not even ripples upon the riverbed of heaven. Shining with golden sand and silver sand, the river of heaven presents such a lovely sight today. The wind blowing across the fields is fragrant, bearing the scent of the flowers that bloom among the myriad grasses. On Mount Sakakiyama, though the flowers scatter, azaleas bloom beneath the ever-verdant pines. When midday comes, purple azaleas and crimson azaleas are fragrant in the shade of the rhododendron trees. When dawn breaks, I think to pluck the myriad flowers beneath the pines and offer them to you, oh noble one.'

The deity Soyokeno no Kami chants a poem:

'Mount Sakakiyama has grown late into the night, and the crying voice of the owl has suddenly grown still. The true cranes have hushed their voices and rested. Himegami, if so, please enter your bedchamber.'

The deity Shinshukunoo no Kami chants a poem:

'Since this mansion is a small dwelling where we may rest, I shall guide you to a detached miya, had it been a palace newly built and prepared awaiting the coming forth of the himegami.'

Having thus chanted, the deity Shinshukunoo no Kami took the hands of the deity Asakahime no Kami and led her toward the new hall. Here the deity Asakahime no Kami, forgetting all else in the fatigue of her long journey, slept peacefully and quietly until daybreak, both in water and in fire.

原文（近代文語）

朝香比女の神は駒の背よりひらりと下り給ひ、禊川に暫し禊を修し給ひ、五柱の神に守られて、栄城山の中腹なる神々の御憩所に入らせ給ひ、長途の旅の疲れを休ませ給ひつつ、述懐歌をうたはせ給ふ。

『八柱の御樋代神と選まれて われは空しく年を経にける はろばろと万里の荒野を打ちわたり 天津高日の宮居に詣でし 久方の筑紫の宮居に詣でてゆ わが行く道を悟らひにけり はろばろと高地秀の宮居に帰り来て ますます心は落付かざりしよ 永久にわが仕ふべき宮居ならずと 駒に鞭うち離り来にけり 七柱御樋代神はわがために 神の御前に祈りたまはむ 村肝の心かためし吾にして 始めの心かへすべきやは 常闇の狭葦の河瀬を渡らむと 八十の曲津に出で会ひにける 言霊の力の限り宣りにつつ 真火打ち出づれば曲津は消えたる 天津日の光を浴びて大野原 駒に跨り此処に来つるも

背の岐美に由縁の深き栄城山の 夕べは心清しくなれり 此処に来て旅の疲れを忘れけり 百神等のあつき心に 駿馬の嘶き聞えずなりにけり やすやす旅の夢結ぶらむ 背の岐美の行方は何処か知らねども わが真心に逢はまくおもふ 栄城山これの聖所に来て見れど 岐美のおとづれくちなしの花 くちなしの花の香れる夕暮の これの聖所にもの思ふかな 御子生みの神業に仕ふる御樋代の 比女神われは心さわぐも 猛り狂ふ心の駒を鎮めむと 思へど詮なし燃ゆる恋路に 栄城山樹々の葉末に置く露も 月の御霊を宿してかがよふ 御樋代の比女神われに月読の 露の宿らぬためしあるべき

日を追ひて広ごりて行く月かげを 見つつ楽しき旅に立つかも 八柱の御樋代神の高き位置を 恋ゆゑ吾は捨てて来にけり 八十比女の御樋代神と下るとも 心足らへり岐美にし逢へば』

機造男の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『はろばろと来ませる公を犒はむ 術なき今宵を許したまはれ まきて来し背の岐美此処に坐さずして 淋しかるらむ御樋代比女神は 村肝の心のかぎり身のかぎり 尽して比女を犒はむとぞ思ふ 地稚き栄城の山よ比女神を 慰むるものなきが嘆てき』

朝香比女の神は答の御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『恐れおほき神々等の言霊よ 吾は感謝の言葉も知らずに 此処に来て始めて心落付きぬ 栄城の山の松のみどりに 禊川清き流れに浮びます 夕月の光によみがへりける 大空も水底も月の光冴えて わが旅立ちを慰むるかな 大空の月の御霊ゆ出でましし わが背の岐美を思ふ宵はも 幾万里の遠きに岐美はおはすとも 魂の限りはまぎて行かなむ』

散花男の神は歌詠ませ給ふ。

『栄城山峰の白梅桜花 漸く散りて牡丹は匂へり 紅の牡丹の花に置く露は 紅き心の現はれなるかも 山姫は牡丹の花を紅に 染めて夏衣纒ひたまへり 早夏の陽気ただよふこの山に あつき心の公をむかへつ 月ははや栄城の山の後手に 隠ろひまして闇はせまれり 大空にまたたく星の光清み 森に聞ゆる梟の声 濁りたる声にはあれど梟の 啼けるを聞けばゆかしくぞ思ふ』

中割男の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『大宮居に仕へて幾年経ぬれども 今日の輝き未だ見ざりき きらきらと光らせ給ふ比女神の 姿まぶしくおはしますかも 明けぬれば栄城の山の頂上の 宮居の聖所に導きまつらむ 仰ぎ見れば北より南に横はる 天の河原にさざなみもなし 金砂銀砂輝き渡る天の河の 今日の姿のうるはしきかな 野辺を吹く風は薫れり百草に 咲きつる花のかをり運びて 栄城山花は散れども常磐樹の 松のしたびにつつじ咲くなり 昼されば紫つつじ紅つつじ 石南花の花木蔭に匂へり 明けぬれば松の木下の百花を 手折りて公に参らせむと思ふ』

小夜更の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『栄城山小夜更けにけり梟の 啼く音も頓に静まりしはや 真鶴は声をひそめて休らひぬ 比女神さらば寝床に入りませ』

親幸男の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『この館は吾等が休む小家なれば 導きまつらむ離れの宮居に 新しく造り備へて比女神の 出でまし待ちし御殿なりせば』

斯く歌ひて、親幸男の神は朝香比女の神の御手を取らせつつ、新殿に導き給ひける。茲に朝香比女の神は長旅の疲れに前後も忘れて夜の明くるまで、御水火も静に安らかに御寝ましにける。
