Chapter 3: 行進 Song [1920]

行進歌〔1920〕

§ 1

The god Yasomagatsumi attempted to obstruct the path of the party of the deity Takanohime by transforming himself into a bottomless deep ravine and barring the way, but he was seen through by the sharp eyes of Takanohime no Kami, smitten by the living Kototama of Unarizu no Kami, and instantly reduced to a state of utter vaporisation, fleeing away high into the eastern sky at the whim of the wind; whereupon the ten deities of the party whipped their swift horses and, chanting a sacred Miuta, advanced toward the east across the plain of Ōnohara.

The sacred Miuta of Takanohime no Kami:

Dancing up into Takaamahara, Visiting Amatsutakamiya, Reverently receiving the divine edict Of the Lord Ōkami, all together Straighten the steeds of their hearts, And the previously afflicted, core-deep Tamashihi of ours grows clear at last; With Unarizu no Kami at the lead, Accompanied by Amatsumeo no Kami, Returning to the eastern Miya; The vast plain of Shibitenkai Is pure, clear, and serene to the utmost, And the voices of Momodori twittering in the woods Resound with pristine clarity, Momogusachigusa bloom and scatter their fragrance, While the sound of insects gathering at the roots Sings of the spring of the heavenly realm; The wind blowing forth also gently Caresses our faces; Ah, what a manifestation of Kami!

How joyous is today's journey! In contrast to this, Mizu-no-Mimisuchira, The god upon our backs, like Magatsumi, Advances day and night through the wild plain Where fierceness rages, Bathing in a hundred afflictions, That He may serve in the divine work Of the Lord Ōkami's entrustment; Oh, the supreme valor with which He proceeds! Were we ourselves goddesses in form, Serving in the tranquil sacred place Of Takayamahiko where the four-season flowers bloom, How could we pass the days in vain? Receiving once again the divine command Of the Lord Ōkami of Amatsutakamiya, The sky of our hearts, deep within, clears completely, And determined ever more to guard the sacred ground, Straddling the steed of ten thousand leagues, Ah, how wondrous is the road of our return!

Yasomagatsumi transformed his body Into a deep ravine a thousand fathoms deep, Barring our way and lying across it— How hateful it is! Here, arising from the Kototama of the U sound, The brave Kototama of Unarizu no Kami Was struck forth, whereupon the crooked god Transformed his body into strange clouds, Tossed about by the west-blowing wind, And vanished fleetingly into the eastern sky— How delightful! Ah, what a manifestation of Kami! Purifying our hearts and purifying our bodies In the sacred sanctuary of the eastern Miya, Performing misogi morning and evening, Praying for the imperial progress of Kimi, How joyous it is to serve Him!

Unarizu no Kami chants the Miuta:

With the divine edict of the Lord Ōkami, Reverently, respectfully, and with awe before the great sanctuary of Amatsutakamiya, Guarding the Mitoshiro-no-Kami, Crossing over the wild plains of ten thousand leagues, And coming here at last to look, Evil qi had gathered to become Magatsumi, Raising clouds and mist, Transforming his body into a deep ravine And obstructing our way; Takanohime no Kami magnificently Saw through the true nature of Magatsumi, And when she proclaimed the living Kototama, Truly, even I was startled, And to the extent that the Kototama of U existed, Exerting my golden-wheel power, When I proclaimed the Kototama of Taka, Magatsumi, as expected, was struck with fear and terror, And fled away, turning cloud into mist; In that very flash, the ravine

Vanished without a trace, With grass growing luxuriantly and thickly, A hundred flowers blooming in full array, And the sound of insects singing so softly, It had become the spring of the heavenly realm; Ah, what a manifestation of Kami! By the blessing of the living Kototama, The path we tread is smooth, Opened up in supreme peace, And the steeds we straddle are brave, Their hooves striking together as they neigh, Advancing across the endless fields; Whatever Magatsumi of the pathway May bar our way and bring disaster, By the blessing of the living Kototama Bestowed by the Lord Ōkami, We shall easily send you, the deities, Safely to the eastern Miya; Ah, what a manifestation of Kami!

Since the divine protection is strong, Do set your hearts at ease, O Mitoshiro-no-Kami; Unarizu no Kami, illuminating the true heart, Here proclaims his innermost convictions; Ah, what a manifestation of Kami! May the divine spirits flourish and prosper!

Asakohime no Kami chants abundantly upon her horse:

Though we were chosen by Kami And became Mitoshiro-no-Kami, Our hearts were clouded and our tamashihi twisted, And because the living Kototama grew muddy, How shameful it was that we resented The sneers of the myriad deities, Thinking ourselves unfit for the divine work of child-bearing! The deity Motomoto-Atsutsumu-no-Kami Did not possess a weak heart, Yet, thinking the sneers of the myriad deities To be an annoying nuisance, And hesitating for a while, Having perceived and known the state In which the tamashihi of the Mitoshiro-no-Kami Were heavily clouded, He set out swiftly from the eastern Miya, Met with the eighty Himegami distributed hither and thither, And in order to give birth to the Kunitokotachi-no-Kami, Whipped the steed of his heart...

§ 2

How awe-inspiring it is to have set forth! We, the Mihishirogami deities, while lamenting the ruthlessness of Kimi, passed the moon and days vainly in sorrow and resentment. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! The mind of the gods is like white clouds wandering in the sky. Illuminated by the divine radiance of the Lord Ōkami, from today onwards my soul shines like the moon and the sun, and I have intuitively perceived every nook and cranny of the lurking place of the crooked kami hidden within my heart. Now, what is called the essential divine work of Mikuni is nothing other than purifying the water and fire of Kototama to make them crystal clear, performing misogi morning and evening, guarding the path of love and faith, and serving Ōkami with a sincere heart rather than anything else.

Now that I have attained this realization, I shall not resent the Lord Ōkami, nor shall I murmur at the ruthlessness of Kimi. With my mind peaceful, calm, and clear like the Yata mirror, I have finally firmed up today my sincere resolve to eternally serve in this Mikuni. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! May the divine protection be profound, and may the blessings of Mitamanofuyu prevail.'

Amatsumeo no Kami, rich upon horseback, chants a miuta.

'Leaving the palace of Tsukushi behind, setting out on the journey of Shibitenkai, guarding and serving in the vanguard of Mihishirogami, and now advancing eagerly to see this place: the defilements of Kototama have solidified, eighty Magatsumi have been born, transforming their bodies into deep valleys and rivers, and with the grimness of blocking the path where we travel, the divine pillar born from the U-sound, Toshinaronude no Kami, springs forth with courage and proclaims the Kototama with all his might, shouting as if even heaven and earth were to shatter. Quailing before his mighty divine majesty, the eighty Magatsumi instantly turned into clouds and scattered high into the sky at the will of the wind. Ah, Kamunagara, how venerable is the power of water and fire in Kototama!

Since our minds have not yet cleared away the cloudy depths of our innermost souls, no matter how we proclaimed the Kototama, it had no effect other than making the treetops of the grass and trees tremble. Yet the Kototama chanted with such valor by Toshinaronude no Kami, whose water and fire of binding were purified, permeated even to the depths of our soul-lines, and leaving not a shadow of the crookedness lurking in heaven and earth, they fled away like clouds and mist. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! From today, reform your hearts, sharpen the living Kototama, serve morning and evening at the eastern palace, serve in the divine work of Mikuni, and pray for the daily blessings of the hundred deities. How joyous it is to advance while our horses are spiritedly shaking their manes to the front, back, left, and right, stepping shanyanshan across the flower-blooming fields!

Our destination is far ahead. Even if there were an instance of some crooked kami blocking our path ahead, if Toshinaronude no Kami, pure in Kototama, stands at the vanguard, how awe-inspiring it is for us to follow without any fear! Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! May there be blessings on the journey of Mikuni, may there be blessings on the journey of Mikuni.'

Here, Kaguhime no Kami chants a miuta.

'Looking out, clouds gather deep in the great field, and the moon in the august sky has become hidden. The dark clouds that wrapped the radiance of the daytime moon must be the congealed water and fire of Magatsumi. Though our horses are spiritedly advanced, the light of the moon and sun in the vast sky grows faint; the crooked kami must be blocking our way ahead, and heaven and earth have suddenly grown cloudy. Following in the rear of Takayamahime, am I now continuing the journey of the great field? Magatsumi, transforming into deep valleys and rivers, have blocked the path where we walk. The Kototama of Toshinaronude no Kami brought blessing, and turning into clouds, the Magatsuki have vanished. How hateful is the Magatsumi that blocked the way on our return journey, receiving the divine words of the Lord Kami.'

Umesakihime no Kami chants a miuta.

'How valiant is the sound of the hooves of the white horses ridden by the ten deities! The hoofprints of the horses journeying across the young and rugged wilderness remain deep. Pushing our horses side by side, we pray that our journey back to the eastern palace will be blessed. The Magatsumi that blocked the path we are to walk had become deep valleys and rivers; driven away by the living Kototama of Toshinaronude no Kami, alas, the Magatsumi have vanished away! Both mountains and fields have grown dark all at once. In the ugly dark clouds that enshrouded the moon and the sun, the crooked kami around here must be causing distress by stretching out webs of dark clouds. Be that as it may, let us continue the water and fire of Kototama and return to the sacred place.'

Suzukohime no Kami chants a sacred poem.

Though clouds of curtains wrap tenfold and twentyfold, What is there to fear for a body that dwells in Kami? On today's journey of a body that exists together with the Lord Kami, Whatever obstructs shall perish away. If the ten pillars of Kami setting forth intend to obstruct, The deities of Magatsumi who wait and pine in vain— Awe-struck by the divine word of Kamunagara, We are returning to the sacred place. Though they spread traps of various wiles, We shall break through and advance upon the water and fire of Kototama. Since Mikuni is a divine land of love and faith, Falsehood shall not be permitted, evil shall not be permitted. While the crooked deities fabricate falsehood as truth, Trying to obstruct the Kami of Makoto— How pitiful must their hearts be, that the wiles Of the deities of Magatsumi are suddenly shattered.

Uzukohime no Kami chants a sacred poem.

On the path where we are protected by the Kami of Unarizu, We deem there is no Magatsumi to obstruct us. Strong though the calamities of Magatsumi may be, We shall open our way and advance by the Kototama of U. Since I too am a goddess who has become the voice of U, How could I fear the wiles of Maga? The dark clouds that obscure only the sun and moon of the vast sky Were the figures of Yasomagatsumi. Sealing the vast sky, the eighty Magatsumi Are blocking even the light of the sun and moon. Wait a while, proclaiming the noble Kototama, We shall scatter the foul dark clouds in all directions. Along the borders of the path where I go forth, hundreds and thousands of flowers Blooming in splendor flourished in the spring. Riding side by side across the spring fields, I think proudly of our journey. How joyful it is to go forth side by side on horseback Through the spring fields where birds sing and butterflies dance. Even the deep valleys where the wiles of Magatsumi were schemed have vanished away into the living Kototama.

Should there be any Maga obstructing the path where I go forth, I shall trample them to pieces beneath my horses' hooves and advance.

Sabatakohime no Kami chants a sacred poem.

Continuing the distant journey through Tōno far away, The joy of drawing closer to the sacred place day by day. Even the blowing wind is exceedingly gentle, and the voices of a hundred birds Are clear and pure throughout the vernal spring. Even the foul dark clouds that enshrouded the vast sky, Restlessly swirling and gathering about— The blessings of the water and fire of the Kami of Shinatobe Shall blow and scatter the curtain of dark clouds. Though Magatsumi strive to obstruct the path ahead, Taking the form of mountains, valleys, and clouds— Joyful is the body that lives and works In Mikuni where there is not a single thing to fear. To be handed down unto the very end of ten thousand generations, The journey of Kototama that shall illuminate the divine majesty. Though the August Spirit sets forth on a journey of ten thousand leagues, When I look at the moon, it is not lonely at all. How hateful are the clouds that enshrouded The moonlight wherein the spirit of my spouse is hidden. By the Kototama of the divine word of Sabatakohime no Kami, We shall divide and scatter the clouds and mists of the august sky.

As she chanted thus, the Kami of Shina perhaps granted her prayer; the wind of Shinatobe suddenly blew forth, scattering the clouds and mists in all directions, and the formidable dark clouds vanished without a trace.

Gazing upon this state, Hanakohime no Kami chants a sacred poem.

How the clouds in the august sky have scattered and vanished By the Kototama of the divine word of Sabatakohime no Kami! As the dark clouds of the distant sky scatter away, The deities of the sun and moon are shining brightly. Even the hundred flowers blooming fully upon the earth Wear smiling faces, adoring the sun and moon. The voices of a hundred birds are also heard clearly Ever since the clouds of the august sky were blown away. The sound of the hooves of the white steeds continuing their journey of Mikuni Resounds with increasing clarity. On a spring day when even the wind blowing over the fields is fragrant, How joyful it is to advance with steeds abreast.

Sayakohime no Kami chants a sacred poem.

The dark clouds scattered by the wind of Shinatobe Turn into boulders to obstruct the way. The clouds that finished scattering gather once more In the distant vast sky, becoming mountain peaks; Billowing and surging, surging and billowing as peaks of clouds, Magatsumi obstruct the path ahead. Strong though the fury of Magatsumi may be, We shall ride over them with our horses' hooves and advance. Amidst the beautiful and pure Mikuni, How ominous indeed is the fury of Magatsumi! Through the merit of the Kami of Unarizu, the heart deep within Has grown strong and robust. My heart swells and expands, filling heaven and earth, How valiant it felt, brimming and overflowing! Now, no matter what kind of Maga obstruction may arise, What is there to fear for us who dwell in Kami?

§ 4

Amatsumeo no Kami chants a sacred poem as follows:

'Following the divine word of Takanohime,

pleasant is this journey of the heavenly land we tread.

Even upon this journey where Magakami watches for our weakness,

we shall advance in peace through the living kototama.

Through the merit of Unarizu no Kami, Magakami

has vanished like clouds and mist.

By the blessing of the divine word of Umesakuhime,

how fragrant is the wind that blows!

Through the enduring blessing of Kaguhime's mitama,

there is indeed the scent of the nuts of Kagu.

By the kototama of the divine word of Suzukohime,

this heaven and earth have grown clear and pure.

Asakahime is Omokatsukami,

and she is truly a deity who faces and strikes down the adversary.

The divine word of Uzukohime has a kototama

that is exceedingly beautiful and rings out clearly.

With the blessing of Sawakehime's kototama,

the clouds of the august sky have blown away and scattered.

By the blessing of Hanakohime's living kototama,

a hundred trees and grasses are filled with flowers.'

Sayokohime proclaims the majestic kototama,

whereby the midday moon has clearly appeared.

In this manner, the deities each chant their sacred poems, and gently swaying in their saddles, they advance day and night across the vast field of Ōnogahara, where the spring wind blows.
