Chapter 7: 外苑の逍遥 [1924]

外苑の逍遥〔1924〕

§ 1

The myriad deities, fatigued from their long journey, let themselves be wrapped in the dreams of a spring day; the wide courtyard of the palace in Takachiho grew as still as if water had been sprinkled over it, and only the voices of small birds exchanging their spring chirps were to be heard.

Miwakeo no Kami, seeking to let his steed rest from its weariness, was letting it roam and play freely in the pure garden of the vast and boundless outer enclosure, where the young grass was sprouting.

The spring breeze blew gently, carrying the fragrance of flowers across the four quarters, and mists gathered hazily on every side—truly a scene of serene late spring.

Asakahime no Kami, disdaining the fatigue of the long journey and thinking it a pity to sleep through such a serene spring day, stepped forth into the pure garden where flower buds were unclosing, and composed a sacred poem with a tranquil heart:

Azusayumi / The goddess of spring / has now passed on / to the green groves of the summer mountains. / The blowing wind is also serene; / the scenery of the late spring fields / is like boiling water. / The myriad grasses sprout forth / until the fields are crowded; / playing in the fields / is also a delightful feeling. / Stepping upon the young grass / drenched in dew, / how delightful indeed / the soles of my bare feet feel! / The purple flowers open wide, / and the irises of the garden blooming / in the pond water / are also lovely. / Soon now the iris flowers / will be dyed purple / and, growing fragrant day by day, / summer will deepen. / The large and small fish swimming / at the bottom of the pond— / how swift are the movements of their fins! / Thinking morning and evening / of where the lordly spouse of my back might be / at the end of the horizon, / I am troubled with longing. / In an age when the flowers / of the cherry trees that bloom / in full profusion / callously scatter away, / the white plums have already finished falling, / and upon the branches where young leaves sprout, / round nuts are surely peering out.

The heavenly sun, pale in the misty sky, / softened its light as it ascended. / Will summer come to meet us / in the endearing sight / of the spirited stallion's neighing / and eagerly plucking the grass? / Mountains and fields alike / are dressed in robes of green, / and the attire of summer / is grand and majestic. / I too am one pillar / of the Mihishiro deities; / should I simply wait for time / in idle vain? / All the myriad phenomena of heaven and earth / are shifting and changing; / should I remain silent / in this heavenly realm? / Though I vainly pine for my spouse / and let the years pass by, / now I regret my foolishness anew. / Though I know that a Mihishiro deity / has a mission beyond merely bearing children, / how ever could I endure keeping this hidden? / The heart's steed, yearning for my spouse, / leaps up impetuously, / and the goddess's breast / beats with a high and ceaseless throbbing. / Treading upon the dewy grass / with bare feet, / at the very edge of the garden / lies the hazy color of late spring. / Setting to rights / my weak heart of hesitation, / I shall advance with courage / to the place of my lordly spouse.

If I go, will the lordly spouse of my back / be enraged? / Illuminating with the Kototama, / shall I try to soothe him? / May the great palace, / whence Toshinakiwade no Kami issued forth, / flourish ever more abundantly / even if I am not here. / If I consult the myriad deities, / they will surely stop me with heavy hearts; / shall I therefore set out / on my journey in secret? / In the twilight as the heavenly sun / inclines to the west, / I shall mount my steed / and search the empty sky.

Asakahime no Kami walked leisurely upon the lawn while softly chanting her poem of personal reflection, but Miwakeo no Kami, with his sharp ears, overheard her sacred poem, and, startled, hastened back to the great palace. When he reported the particulars in detail to the seven pillar Mihishiro deities, beginning with Toshinakiwade no Kami and Amatsumeo no Kami, each of them was astonished and broke off their dreams. Seeking to stop the departure of Asakahime no Kami, they stepped out into the outer enclosure where the summer grasses were sprouting. At that very moment, Asakahime no Kami was causing a saddle to be placed upon the back of the steed she would ride, taking the reins in one of her august hands, and about to place her left foot halfway into the stirrup. Thereupon, Takanohime no Kami, startled, ran up, grasped the horse's bit firmly, and chanted a sacred poem:

Spring having passed, / into the pure garden / welcoming the coming of summer, / why, O you, do you cause the steed / to be saddled and ridden? / The travel attire for journeying forth / astride a spirited stallion / has surely already become / that of summer days. / To set out on a pillow-of-grass journey / should be during the time / of spring and autumn flowers / and crimson leaves.

Asakahime no Kami, holding the reins in her right hand, chanted her responding poem:

Thinking upon the lordly spouse / of my back, / my longing grows intense / and I can no longer endure it. / My heart, which began to scatter / in eighty tangled threads— / alas, there was no way / to tie it back together. / Even while knowing that I stray / from the great highway, / I would fain advance / to the place of my lordly spouse. / You would check my spirited steed's / yearning heart— / oh, how vexing / are your public words! / However much you would detain me, / my heart / has already determined / upon this journey. / Do not detain me in idle play; / my steed / paws the ground incessantly, / eager to set forth on the journey.

§ 2

Takanohime no Kami, with a solemn air, chants a sacred poem.

The Lord God's thou art the entrusted Mihishiro, Without permission, dost thou set forth on a journey? The celestial realm is the Mihishiro of the Lord Ōkami, How could I grant such self-willed thoughts?

Asakahime no Kami chants a poem in reply.

Knowing full well that I walk against the path of the Lord God, My yearning grows so intense that to die would be painful; My soul, suffering and struggling day and night, If it must fade, let it fade; if it must die, let it die. With a yearning so fierce that neither shield nor arrow can bar it, My heart throbs with unceasing agony; Now that things have come to this, to turn away my yearning heart, Forgive me, powerless that I am.

Unarizu no Kami, watching this state of affairs with astonishment, chants a sacred poem.

Thou hast been entrusted by the Lord God as the Mihishiro Kami, Is this not a public matter? Reflect upon it. For a lone Goddess to set forth on a journey amidst the kusamakura, Wait for a time when it would not be perilous. However much thou mayest urge thy heart forward, In this wakaguni there is no path to advance.

Asakahime no Kami chants resolutely.

Be that as it may, though I traverse the boundless arano, I shall fear nothing, possessing the Kototamatsurugi; Wielding the noble sword of Kototama, I shall smite and cast aside the obstructing Maga. The Miya of Takachiho is venerable, and my beloved Kimi, How could I yearn for him so deeply and remain in silence? Even if I alone am not the Miya, Unarizu no Kami, thou art stationed here. Thinking far and deep, I now Set forth upon a journey to bring forth divine children. Until I accomplish the divine work entrusted by the Lord God, I shall not return, so grant me thy forgiveness. O Himegamitachi of the Mihishiro, hear my desire Profoundly and in every detail, and grant it; My heart, heavier than the boulders of Chibiki, No power whatsoever shall be able to move.

Umesakihime no Kami gracefully chants a sacred poem.

My heart, which longs for my beloved Kimi, is not shallow, And I grieve at the true words of Asakahime. Even I, though I yearn for him day by day, Have no recourse since there is no divine permission. Until the divine permission is granted anew, Asakahime, wait for a while. To exhaust one's longing in vain, And grieve over it afterwards is a public sorrow. Serving as a Mihishiro Kami, even I Have waited long and painfully through the lingering months. Though I know full well what lies in thy heart, I fear the lack of divine permission. In any case, return to the Miya of Takachiho; Since thy heart is rushing onward, Calm thyself, converse on the future path of thy life, And act quietly, ever so quietly.

Asakahime no Kami replies with a poem.

How precious is the pronouncement of Umesakihime no Kami, I shall engrave it upon my heart and never forget it. Yet this Kimi, for whom I have yearned until my life almost fades, Is one my body finds hard to forsake. Though the myriad Kami may judge my body how they will, Without fear I shall go forth, lashing my horse with a whip. O Mihishiro Kami and officials of the Miya, Grant me detailed permission for my departure. Now then, I shall mount my steed and set forth, Farewell in good health, O Mihishiro Kami.

So saying, as she once more attempts to mount her steed, Suzukohime no Kami firmly grasps the horse's bit with her hands and chants a sacred poem.

Even I, longing for my beloved Kimi, Was a divine spirit mourning and spending mornings and evenings in sorrow. Yet without the permission of the Lord Ōkami, How could one depart from this sacred place? To the very depths of thy pure divine heart, I have understood thee, yet it pains me to hold thee back. Hiding tears that could not be checked, night and day, I have grown thus worn with lamentation. Yet since the divine trust is heavy, I have endured and waited through long months and days. This time, I pray thee, put aside thy yearning; Even the peony flowers, when they begin to bloom, Think of the world where the cherry blossoms, in full brilliance and fragrance, Are scattered by the night tempest. In the purple celestial realm of Aizen, forever more, Shall flowers no longer remain upon the twigs.

Utsukohime no Kami stands gracefully before the spirited steed, and seeking to halt the departure of Asakahime no Kami, chants a sacred poem.

Spring has passed, and summer has gradually drawn near, And young grasses sprout luxuriantly across the fields. Leaving behind the sacred place where summer grasses sprout, How ominous is your heart as you set forth on a journey. I pray thee, wait for a while; the day when the permission of the Lord God Shall soon be granted will surely come. If all things were to go according to one's own heart, I too would remain in silence and not hold thee back. Though I understand thy heart, so earnest, For the sake of the celestial realm I shall hold thee back. Do not disgrace thy task Of serving the great Miya morning and evening. Reverently serve for a while in the Miya of Takachiho As a Mihishiro Kami of Kototama. Weeping with the very same sorrow, I endure and continue to serve in the Miya.

§ 3

Sawakehime no Kami chants a sacred poem:

'The divine words and departure of Asakahime no Kami / It pains me so to try and stay them / The words with which I suppress this pain and would stay you / Grant them, I pray, O Asakahime no Kami / The cherry blossoms upon the peaks of Takachiho have fallen and faded / And the fields are dyed in the colors of everlasting summer / The cherry blossoms of early spring on Takachiho / Alas, have already scattered upon the body of Mihishirogami / How shall the trees and grasses, bereft of spring's blossoms / Meet in water and fire with Kimi, who is the flower? / Summer grasses have sprouted forth until they crowd the ground / You, O Kami, have already grown old / O Mihishirogami, grown old, become the foundation of Kototama and guard Mikuni / I too am one grown old, and as Mihishirogami of Kototama I shall serve / Thinking that the renowned flowers of the Miya of Takachiho would be scattered / I grieve for your departure, O Kimi.'

Hanakohime no Kami chants a sacred poem:

'Hanakohime, your floral form has faded / Would Asakahime no Kami also be thus distressed? / The deep and unstinting devotion of Asakahime / As I try to stay her, my tears overflow / To seek the sacred footprints of Akitsuo no Kami / Piteous is the heart of you, O Kimi, who so desires / Akitsuo no Kami, in the divine works of Kuniu and Kamiu / Is so busy that he will not turn his gaze back / Riding far and away across distant mountains and fields / I mourn for you, O Kimi, who makes others weep heartlessly / Gathering the reins of the steed of my heart / I pray you, stay here in the Miya of Takachiho.'

Sayakohime no Kami chants a sacred poem:

'Though my body is deep in the night, seeking the footprints of Akitsuo no Kami / Of radiant light, alas, I have no means to do so / Spring has departed, and on a summer evening you set forth on your journey / I weep, thinking with sorrow upon you, O Kimi.'

Asakahime no Kami, floating a look of resolute determination upon her countenance, chants a sacred poem:

'Though I understand the fervent hearts of the Kami / The stepping hooves of the steed of my heart cannot be stayed / Since you think of my divine soul with tender affection / Forgive, I pray, my departure this day / Though Magatsumichi may obstruct us, be it so / I shall yield to the living Kototama and press forward / Born as I am by the grace of Kototama / How could the water and fire of Kototama fail to shine? / Piercing the eager heart of my fleet steed / I shall press forward to the side of my Kimi.'

Amatsumesuo no Kami, looking dismayed, chants:

'Though I understand the strong heart of Asakahime / I beg you to wait just a little while longer / To suppress the bamboo-arrow heart of Himegami / The sincere hearts of the hundreds of Kami are piteous / Disregarding the gentle hearts of the hundreds of Kami / Cruel indeed is you, O Kimi, who would set forth on a journey.'

Asakahime no Kami, unable to contain herself any longer, with resolute determination grasps a whip tightly in her right hand and holds up the reins in her left, chanting a sacred poem:

'Come then, all ye hundreds of Kami, in the grand Miya / Morning and evening, continue your sacred service / Though you may think I act against the divine will of the hundreds of Kami / What can I do with this hardened heart of mine?'

Uttering this ritual declaration, she strikes her steed with a single whip and, rushing headlong as she tears apart the curtain of the evening darkness, dashes away in a single streak—alas, such is the way of things.

花子比女の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『花子比女花の姿はあせにけり 朝香の比女も斯くやましけむ あさからぬ朝香の比女の志 とどめむとして涙あふれつ 顕津男の神の御後を訪ねむと 思ほす公の心かなしも 顕津男の神は国土生み神生みの 神業忙しく顧みたまはじ 遥々と遠の山野をのり越えて 無情に泣かす公を悲しむ 村肝の心の駒を立て直し 止まり給へ高地秀の宮居に』

小夜子比女の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『小夜更けし身ながら光の顕津男の 神の御後を訪はす術なさ 春さりて夏の夕べを旅立たす 公を悲しとおもひて泣くも』

朝香比女の神は、決心の色を面に浮べて御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『神々のあつき心は悟れども 心の駒の足掻き止まずも わが神魂愛ぐしと思し給はれば 許させ給へ今日の旅立ちを よしやよし曲神道にさやるとも 生言霊になびけ進まむ 言霊の幸に生れしわれにして 言霊の水火輝かざらめや 駿馬のはやる心を貫ぬきて 吾は進まむ背の岐美許に』

天津女雄の神は憮然として歌ひ給ふ。

『朝香比女の強き心は悟れども 今暫くを待たせたまはれ 比女神の矢竹心をおさへむと 百神等の真心かなしも 百神のやさしき心をよそにして 旅立たむとする公ぞつれなき』

朝香比女の神は矢も楯もたまらず、決然として鞭を右手に手握り、左手に手綱をささげながら御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『いざさらば百神等よ大宮居に 朝な夕なを仕へましませ 百神等の御旨にそむくと思へども かたき心をわれ如何にせむ』

と言挙げしつつ一鞭あててまつしぐらに夕闇の幕分けつつ一目散に駆け出で給ふぞ是非なけれ。
