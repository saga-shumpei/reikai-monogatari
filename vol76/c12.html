Chapter 12: Mountain 上の Prayer [1929]

山上の祈り〔1929〕

§ 1

Asakahime no Kami rested her travel-weary limbs in an eight-fathom palace newly built by the hearts of the Kamigami on the mid-slope of Mount Sakakiyama, spending a night of the early summer. Awakening to the voice of a mountain crow announcing the dawn, she quietly sprang from her couch, adjusted her disheveled hair, and draped herself in a white, thin garment. When she pushed open the window of the living room, the morning breeze of Mount Sakakiyama blew in with a crisp, fragrant rustle, and the mist lingering over the unfolding vast fields gleamed in the sunlight, presenting a view beyond all description. A Kariyōbinga twittered among the trees, and a Manazuru sang of the dawn in the pine thicket.

As I look out, mist rises over the distant great fields, And the softly blowing breeze heralds the early summer. The sound of the wind blowing through the pines of Mount Sakakiyama is so crisp, How lovely the Manazuru singing in the treetops! Though there are no white plum blossoms, the voice of the nightingale Is so clear upon Mount Sakakiyama. Resting the fatigue of my long journey, now I Am gazing upon the land from the eight-fathom palace. How marvelous it is, The azalea flowers blooming and glowing beneath the pines of the evergreen trees! Rhododendron flowers have bloomed in peach color, And small birds have come to play. Yearning for the footprints of my dear Kimi, now I Am dwelling in peace on Mount Sakakiyama. Captivated by the warm hearts of the hundred deities, I slept a night on Mount Sakakiyama. How I long for the sacred mountain where of old My dear Kimi proclaimed the divine words! To the east, Mount Takahirayama is hazy, And to the west towers Mount Takachihirano.

Amidst Mount Takahirayama and Mount Takachihirano, How purely stands Mount Sakakiyama! If Kimi were present in this sanctuary of Mount Sakakiyama, I would pour out my soul in this heavenly realm. Ah, an uncertain floating world—my dear Kimi Has set out on a journey beyond the bounds of ten thousand miles. If I had wings, I would fly across Mount Takahirayama And go to the side of my radiant Kimi. No matter how swift the legs of a steed might be, The way of ten thousand miles is far indeed. Born as a deity of the Mihishiro, I never thought That I should be a soul so burdened with suffering. If my thoughts were faint and pure, Such anguish would surely never exist. With the cold heart of valley water, I wish to dwell in this heavenly realm. Yet what could I do with these thoughts of mine? They turned into flames and scorched my breast. Nothing can compare to the power of Mizu-no-Minochi To quench the flames within my breast.

Scorched by the warm heart that thinks of Kimi, Have I forgotten things that should bring me shame?

Just as she was having someone chant alone thus, Sayofuke no Kami, holding offerings of purple and crimson azaleas and rhododendron flowers, quietly entered the hall of Himegami, and chanted a poem as she sought to offer them to Himegami.

Glowing in the shade of the pines on Mount Sakakiyama, I humbly offer the flower of life to kimi. The deep red azaleas and peach-colored rhododendrons Promised to kimi as night deepened, please deign to look upon them.

Asakahime no Kami smiled gently as she received the azaleas and rhododendron flowers offered by Sayofuke no Kami, touched the base of the flowers to her lips, and chanted a poem.

Fragrant crimson flowers and purple ones, Peach-colored flowers, let me press my lips to you. These flowers are wondrously fragrant, as they glow According to the ebb and flow of water and fire of my dear Kimi! With the heart of the crimson azalea flower, When shall I ever appear before Kimi? The rhododendron flower has begun to blossom beautifully in peach color And to emit its fragrance, though not for my sake. Whenever I see the form of the peach-colored flower, I think of the cold heart of Kimi. The purple azalea flower that reproaches my dear Kimi— Ah, the heart of Sayofuke no Kami who plucked it!

When she asked thus, Sayofuke no Kami chanted a poem with awe.

By presenting the peach-colored rhododendron flower, Have I afflicted the heart of Hime? Thinking mindlessly that the rhododendron flower was beautiful, Please forgive my fault in offering it. The purple flower is a sign of auspiciousness; Thinking that I would soon meet Kimi, I wished to comfort your journey by offering The hearts of various flowers to Hime.

Asakahime no Kami smiled gently and chanted a poem.

Even as I regret that my ungrounded words Caused your divine heart to distress itself, Knowing deeply that they were simply flowers meant to comfort me, Ah, what a blunder I made! How I envy the butterflies that morning and evening Sip from the lips of the crimson flower! When I think of how someday I wish to sip From those crimson lips in full detail, my heart wanders far away.

§ 2

Just as she thus chanted, the Kami Hatatsukurio respectfully appeared at this place,

Asatsuhi has now arisen, come then, I shall guide you to the Miya upon the peak. Thinking that you must be wearied by the long journey, I have aroused the morning ima. Purple clouds trail across the eastern ōzora, And indeed the sun has risen. The hisakata misora has cleared without a cloud, And the itadaki of the Sakaki mountain is pure and fresh.

The Kami Asakahime chants a miuta.

Weary from the long journey, in Kimi's magokoro, I have found rest, sleeping for a single hitoyo. Guided to the splendid yahiro hall, I have melted into the morning keshiki. Blooming and fragrant beneath the pines of the tokihagi, My heart finds rest among the azalea flowers. Come then, guide me toward the sugado, Where my dear Kimi has prayed.

Here Hatatsukurio, together with shiyoshin, guarding Asakahime in the zengo and saiu, caused her to ascend toward the ōmahe of the Miya upon the itadaki.

Asakahime stood within the sugado of the Miya, and with an omomochi of kangaimuriyō, gazing upon the kunigata of yomo, she performed haiki before the ōmahe, and clearly and brightly proclaimed the divine words.

Though awe-inspiring and wondrously venerable, Upon the upper bedrock of Mount Sakaki, Erecting thick miya pillars, you dwell in tranquility, Before the ōmahe of the Lord Ōkami, Asakahime, reverently and respectfully, Offers this prayer: Truly, this great miya, Was opened by the Kami Akitsuo himself, By cutting the trees of the great and small valleys, And leading the myriad kami; Therefore, I feel this miya to be doubly venerable, And exceedingly dear. Receiving the deep blessings of the Lord Kami, Who dwells in tranquility within this miya, I pray that my dear Kimi May proceed safely and unharmed To the lands of the west whence he has set forth. Though the eighty Magatsuhikami rage, Though ugly hags obstruct the way, Though winds blow fiercely across the wilderness, Though giant serpents bar the path, Whipping the heavenly steed born of the divine fire and water, May he proceed safely and peacefully to the side of Kimi, And devoutly bring to completion His divine task of bearing divine children. May the pine branches of Mount Sakaki Deepen in their thousand-year green, May the voice of the true crane be ever purer, And the notes of the kalavinka ring clear, May the misora brighten ever more, Growing crystal clear even over the land, May clouds and mists blocking yomo Vanish without a trace, And may he unfalteringly serve In the divine task of the divine descent, Thus do I pray, with awe and trembling, Upon the summit of Mount Sakaki.

When I look out, Mount Sakaki towers above the clouds, standing tall while the tokihagi grow luxuriantly. As far as the eye can see, yomo is hazy; where are the mountains of Takayamahiko, veiled in black clouds? Beyond the clouds, deep in the sky, the sacred mountains of Takayamahiko must be soaring high. Standing upon the itadaki of Mount Sakaki and looking up, how deep is the azure of the misora! Having come here, I have come to realize all the more deeply the achievements of my dear Kimi. Receiving the profound blessings of the imperial kami, tomorrow once again I must set out on a journey.

The Kami Hatatsukurio chants a miuta.

This miya built by the Kami Akitsuo is ever enfolded in purple clouds. Today, upon the ascent of the Himegami, especially has the misora brightened and the clouds cleared away. Abide upon this sacred mountain for days on end, and morning and evening I shall serve you. When I look out, the great plains of the land are still youthful, and mists and vapors rise up swirling.

The Kami Sankanokami chants a miuta.

The hisakata misora has cleared, the mountains have cleared, how clear is this sacred mountain today. The Himegami of the Mihishiro has appeared here, and the clouds and mists of the sacred mountain have scattered far. Like a nightingale singing spring out of season, Mount Sakaki lives in its voice. Domestic roosters sing the hours on the face of the miya, and cranes cry out, blessing a thousand autumns. Upon the itadaki of this sacred mountain fragrant with wind, the light of the Hime standing there is radiant.

The Kami Nakawarikano-kami chants a miuta.

Though I have served in this miya for months and years, there has never been a propitious day as clear as this. On a sacred mountain cleared wide like today, I think of the joy of beholding the kunigata. May Mount Sakaki remain clear for eternity, from the day the Mihishiro-no-kami ascended. Even the Magatsumi dwelling in the ravines of Mount Sakaki shall raise no clouds from this day forth. At the echoing of the living kototama of the Himegami, all the eighty Magatsumi shall vanish without a trace. Ah, how splendid is the departure of the Mihishiro-no-kami, who creates the land and gives birth to the Kunidamakami. Thinking that I too shall serve in the retinue of the Himegami, I wonder what your august will might be.

§ 3

The sacred miuta of the Kami Asakahime.

Protected by the deep compassion of the Kamigami / I have climbed upon the ridge of Mount Sakaki / May Mount Sakaki prosper from this day onward / As the color of the tokihana pines deepens / The fields surrounding Mount Sakaki remain peaceful / Gathering ever more firmly so that the hime of the land is revealed / Here and there the dwellings of the Kunitamagami are seen / Inspiring thoughts of endless prosperity

The Kami Sayofuke recites a sacred miuta.

On this clear and auspicious day of kefu, to the ōmiya / Guarding the hime, I have made my pilgrimage / Mount Takachiho is hidden within the kumoma / While Mount Sakaki sees the kagerofu burning / Standing upon the ridge where the kagerofu blazes / It is a joy to behold the summer of the ōno / Both mountain and field adorn themselves in green robes / Welcoming the megami of summer / The summer wind blowing across the ridge of Mount Sakaki / Is it not both refreshing and cool?

The Kami Chikasachio recites a sacred miuta.

Guiding the Himegami who has come from afar / I have climbed to the cleared ridge of Sakaki / Standing in the sugado of the ōmiya, I have heard / The ikukototama of the Himegami / In the ametsuchi born from the iki of the kototama / How could one live without proclaiming the kototama? / Come then, let us descend the miyama and to the Yahirodono / Rest now, O Kami of the Mihishirohime.

The Kamigami, standing in the sugado of the ōmiya upon the ridge, each reciting their sacred miuta, trampling and parting the roots of rocks and trees, winding and turning to the right and to the left, descended the zigzagging slopes toward the Yahirodono, which was the resting place of the Himegami.
