Chapter 15: 御 Boat 巌 [1932]

御舟巌〔1932〕

§ 1

Yasomagatsumi, attempting to block the path of Asakahimeno-Kami, transformed itself into a vast swamp, and waiting in readiness to afflict the Goddess, it was solidified by the living Kototama of the Goddess, instantly turning into a true swamp, and came to lie forever in the exact middle of Ōnohara.

Moreover, the giant rock, which had been the true form of Yasomagatsumi, was turned by the blessing of the Kototama into an Ihakusubune floating upon the water, used perversely for the sacred service of ferrying the Himegami across to Higan, and when it was solidified once again at the water's edge as the immovable Mifuneiha of ten thousand ages, Yasomagatsumi could do nothing whatsoever about it, and the hundred Magatsukamitachi led by it were each transformed into shellfish at the bottom of the swamp, permitted only narrowly to preserve their lives.

Asakahimeno-Kami, beholding this state, deigns to compose and chant a Miuta:

How wondrous, Yasomagatsumi, that you have become an immovable swamp in the middle of Ōnohara!

Crossing over the surface of the water that had turned into the swamp of Magatsumi, riding in an Ihakusubune,

From today onward, remaining a swamp unto eternal ages, do not change your place until the end of the world.

All you Magatsukamitachi, while becoming shellfish at the bottom of the swamp, maintain your lives.

May all the shellfish assume the shape of the Kusu-boat in which I came riding, and hide themselves within the swamp.

The heavenly sun illuminates the surface of the lake of Maga, while dark mists and vapors rise up swirling.

The swamp of the foul fiend that had plotted against me has now in turn been plotted against by me.

The mountain shadow shimmering blue at the bottom of the water—is it the mountain of Sakaiseiro, fluttering in the waves?

This swamp, floating the light of Tsukuyomi, was it not thought to be the incarnation of Magatsu?

If one interprets everything with benevolent intent, surely all things will turn out for the best for me.

Even the Magatsukami are hard-pressed to defeat love and goodness, and have fallen prostrate upon the earth, overflowing as a swamp.

Producing daily fodder for the Kunitsukami, grow and nurture the fish and shellfish, O Lake of Maga.

The swamp water grows ever clearer and clearer, until it has surely become a deep lake.

The aquatic tribes gathering by the side of Mifuneiha shall maintain their lives unto eternal ages.

Since Mifuneiha is the deity that aided me, it shall surely not perish for thousands of generations.

May the aquatic tribes and all the shellfish dwelling at the roots of the rock live by the merit of having aided me.

Now then, I shall proceed, O lake; nourish the Kunitsukami unto eternal ages.

Chanting thus, she lightly straddles her horse and proceeds toward the fields of the southeast, whereupon in the exact middle of the nearby fields there is a low hill, and dozens of dwellings of the Kunitsukami stand built in a row, so Asakahimeno-Kami advances to visit the village where the Kunitsukami dwell.

Sannohiko, the chief of the Kunitsukami, kneeling before the presence of the Himegami and smiling broadly:

You who descend from Takamanohara, are you indeed a Goddess, with a glowing countenance?

Protecting the Kunitsukami in this township, I who dwell here am indeed Sannohiko.

I pray you, stay in this village and save the Kunitsukami.

The Kunitsukami suffer from daily fodder; they are starving and thirsty—grant them peace.

The Kunitsukami, who maintain their spirits as their only sustenance, are starving and thirsting without food.

Asakahimeno-Kami replies to Sannohiko:

I shall grant the daily provisions of the Kunitsukami; let them take the aquatic tribes of the lake of Maga.

Sannohiko replies:

Though I deem it awe-inspiring and most gracious, we are Kunitsukami who live upon tree fruits.

Since our lives are such that we must live by eating the aquatic tribes, we shall not be pressed by starvation.

Asakahimeno-Kami:

Though tree fruits are likewise good for life, if you eat the aquatic tribes your lives shall be all the longer.

I shall now grant the Kunitsukami the true fire with which to roast and eat the aquatic tribes.

Having deigned to declare thus, Asakahimeno-Kami causes them to fish for the aquatic tribes filling the brook of this village, strikes fire with a flint, causes it to catch upon wood and grass, hurls the aquatic tribes among them, and roasts them with goodly care, whereupon a fragrant scent fills all around.

The Kunitsukami, enticed by this fragrance, strive to be first and begin to eat as if devouring greedily. Sannohiko, rejoicing:

How awe-inspiring is the grace of the Himegami! You have taught us the good taste of the aquatic tribes.

From today onward, the Kunitsukami of this village shall have no fear of lamenting over starvation.

The true fire which the Himegami has cut forth—surely it is the divine spirit, shining and gleaming.

If you grant this true fire unto us, the Kunitsukami shall never perish unto eternal ages.

§ 2

Asakahime-no-Kami took out a reserve flint from her bosom and gave it to Sanuhiko; unable to contain his gratitude, Sanuhiko struck sparks, chink-chink, and singing a folk song in the excess of his joy, formed a circle of the moon with the Kunitsukamitachi, dancing and leaping about wildly.

Amatsuhimegami descended to this village, And granted eternal light; We, the Kunitsukami who hitherto lived Eating only tree nuts, Though summer and autumn are pleasant, When winter departs and spring approaches, Have suffered and ached with starvation. What a blessed day today is! In the lake waters, pond waters, and river bottoms, Abundant to overflowing, To eat the fish and preserve life eternally, She has taught us; She has given us a flint, And taught us to roast and eat them. How marvelous is this great divine grace! From today, we Kunitsukami Have gained the sustenance of life. Oh, how reassuring, how reassuring! The grace of the Himegami who descended from heaven, We shall never forget for a thousand generations. Oh, how marvelous, how marvelous! Rejoice, rejoice, O Kunitsukami! Dance, dance, O Kunitsukami! The skies are azure and the earth is vast, The sun and moon shine clearly, And the blowing wind is gentle; The divine land of the Amatsu is visibly Born forth, in accordance with the divine will. Before the august presence of Kami, the words of thanksgiving, How truly awe-inspiring are the words we speak! For a thousand and eight thousand generations, eternally, We shall never forget the grace of the Goddess. Rejoice, rejoice, dance, dance, Until the bottom of the great earth is knocked out, Until the cauldron of Ryūgū breaks.

Following the lead of Sanuhiko, the Kunitsukami gathered one after another, shaking heaven and earth as they danced and leaped wildly. Asakahime-no-Kami turned toward the various Kami and proclaimed in a sacred poem:

To drive away the base pranks of Magatsumi, I have given the flint unto you. From today, the fish inhabiting Lake Maga Shall be given as your sustenance. Do you never catch the fish dwelling around Funebune-no-Iwa; For Funebune-no-Iwa is the Kami who aided me, The nearby fish must be left unharmed. If you mistakenly eat the fish and shellfish living at the rock base, Straightway your lives shall be lost. Since it is a vast lake, everywhere The fish and shellfish you should eat are abundant. The fish must be roasted with fire and eaten, Never eat them while yet alive.

Sanuhiko joyfully replied in a sacred poem:

The divine words of the Himegami who descended from the distant heaven, Reverently heeding them, for a thousand generations we shall guard them. From today, the Kunitsukami in peace, Joyful and spirited, shall be immersed in grace. The fish are without number, and throughout the years, As they fill the lake, there is no suffering from hunger. We pray you, grant us water, This pure fresh water flowing in the small stream, Lest the Kunitsukami, drinking pure fresh water, suffer bellyaches And sorrow over losing their lives.

Hereupon Asakahime-no-Himegami kneaded authentic earth with water to make an earthen jar, dried it for a while in the light of the Amatsu sun, built a kiln with earth, kindled a fire to bake the jar, skillfully crafted it to perfection, and when she filled it with water, built a kiln, and caused a fire to bake it, instantly the water in the jar boiled and became lovely white hot water. When the Himegami gave this white hot water to the Kunitsukami and taught them to drink it, the Kunitsukami rejoiced valiantly and from then on made it their custom to drink white hot water; without suffering bellyaches as they did from raw water, each of them happily preserved their natural lifespan. From this time, the way of eating cooked food began.

Sanuhiko, in the excess of his joy, chanted a song of thanksgiving.

Moistened by the dew of the Himegami's grace, We have realized the taste of white hot water. The flavor of the fish roasted over fire is fragrant, And our lives are also revived. The divine work of kneading earth to make a jar, and baking that jar Once more in the fire, is truly awe-inspiring. Fill the baked jar to the brim with pure fresh water, And when firewood is burned, the white hot water boils. We, who have begun to realize the power of fire, Shall from today never weep from hunger. Preserving eternal life in this village, I shall prosper together with the Kunitsukami. If the attacks of Magatsumi come forth, with true fire We have felt the power to hurl them back and drive them away. Reverently heeding the divine words of the Himegami, the fish around Funebune-no-Iwa we shall never catch for eternity. The skies are clear, a summer wind blows over the earth, Pure is the heart as the Hime comes forth. The divine grace taught unto us by the Himegami, We shall share with the Kami of the four directions and rejoice.

§ 3

This Kuni has since ancient times been praised as Ashihara no Kuni, yet it was indeed Tokoyami; this Ashihara of Tokoyami, from Kefu henceforth, shall be revived by the power of Mahi; the grace of Himegami, who bestowed fire and water, shall never be forgotten for Toha; wishing never to forget the grace of Himegami for Toha, we shall build a Miyai and enshrine her.

Having thus proclaimed, Sanuhiko led numerous Kunitsukami to build a new and pure Miyai on the shores of Kohen, enshrined the Mitama of the Lord God to pray for the divine blessings of the Goddess Asakahime, and enshrine the Mitama of Asakahime together in the Ahidono, so that the Kunitsukami would serve in turns morning and evening.

Asakahime no Kami mounted her horse once more, and as she was about to depart from this Buraku, she chanted a Miuta.

Come now, let us bid farewell to the Kunitsukami who dwell in the Osato of Sanu; from this time forth, towards the young Kuni of the Nishikata, I shall press onward; be healthy and well.

Sanuhiko, grieving at the parting, proclaimed a poem.

The achievements of Himegami are profound; I beseech you, even for today, remain here; when I think that we are parting from the Goddess who has granted the sustenance of life to the Kunitsukami, I am filled with sorrow; all the Kunitsukami gather here, lamenting and grieving over your public departure; as the saving Kami of the village of Sanu, you appeared, and we wish to stay the journey of Himegami; eternally, together with the Lord Ōkami, we shall revere your Mitama.

Asakahime no Kami chanted a Miuta.

It is not that I do not understand the hearts of the Kunitsukami, but how could I remain here for the sake of bearing a child? You Kunitsukami, who are endowed with spirit, be free from suffering and remain diligent; I am indeed a Kami born from the sacred fire and water of the Lord Ōkami, and my body has no affliction; come now, beloved Kunitsukami, I shall press onward; flourish eternally.

Sanuhiko, grieving at the parting, chanted yet another poem.

If it be so, there is no means to stop you; I shall serve the Kunitsukami and the Mitama; I pray you, grant me permission to be your attendant; since the Maga forces on this path ahead are dense, even if I were to cast away the life of my string-of-jewels existence, it would not be a matter of regret for the sake of Hime; from Kefu onwards, serving as your attending Kami, I shall follow and go forth for the sake of Himegami.

Asakahime no Kami smiled and said,

Accepting the heartfelt devotion of the gentle Sanuhiko, from Kefu onwards I grant you permission to be my attendant.

Sanuhiko, unable to contain his joy, spoke:

There is the permission of Himegami; you Kunitsukami, serve purely in the Miyai of the Kami; come now, I shall render my service as an attendant to Asakahime no Kami; I pray you, strike the whip to your horse.

Hereupon Asakahime no Kami, having Sanuhiko follow her, bravely advanced side by side on horseback across the clear expanse of Ōnogahara.

『かくならば止めむ術もなかりけり 国津神等と神霊に仕へむ 願はくば御供を許し給へかし この行先の曲津しげければ 玉の緒の生きの生命をすつるとも 比女のためには惜しからざるべし 今日よりは御供の神と仕へつつ 比女神のために従ひ行かむ』

朝香比女の神は微笑みながら、

『やさしかる狭野比古の心うべなひて 今日よりわれの供を許さむ』

狭野比古は喜びに堪へず、

『比女神の許しありけり国津神よ 神の宮居に清く仕へませ いざさらば御供仕へむ朝香比女の 神よ御馬に鞭うたせませ』

朝香比女の神はここに狭野比古を従へ、晴れたる大野ケ原を、駒を並べて勇ましく進ませ給ひける。
