Chapter 10: 二本 Pine の蔭 [1927]

二本松の蔭〔1927〕

§ 1

As she crushed the morning dew of the summer-grass-growing great plain of Ōnohara beneath her swift horse's hooves, Asakahime no Kami proceeded splendidly on horseback, softly chanting a miuta as she advanced.

Leaving behind the sacred precinct of the miyai of Takachiho where the nibotan burns, straddling the back of the hayakoma, at the very moment when I advance into the endless ōnohara, as mists drift across the edge of the field, all alone and lonely, the Amatsu sunlight hides upon the earth, and the curtain of tasogare draws oppressively near. As the ashinami of the hayakoma briskly quickens and arrives at a certain kahabe, the darkness deepens ever more, and the color of the single stream's kawase lies white across the dark, while the sound of the seseragi echoes dimly. At such a lonely riverside, as I linger astride my horse, lo, the Magatsumi turns into a hidama and goes along the kahashimo, illuminating the darkness and pressing close. When I look closely, the ball of fire has a single eye and eight mouths; each mouth opens wide, spitting forth kiyodai bees to attack me and my horse. My horse wisely leaps and dives into the suichiu, thus escaping the peril of the bees. When I chant the Kototama, pray to Kami, and strike the hiuchiishi click-clack, instantly true fire flashes forth. By virtue of its merit illuminating the surroundings, as expected the Magatsumi is filled with fear, turning into smoke and vanishing away.

At that very moment, in the eastern sky the dawn breaks faintly, the calls of magpies are lively, the voices of insects are clear, and the morning breeze blows leisurely across the Ōnohara. As the sound of the seseragi resounds clearly and brightly, the Amatsuhi rising from the dawn sky shines with all the limitlessness of its light, illuminating the dewdrops on the grass-blades like jewels, and rises high into the mid-heaven. Ah, Ameno-mi-kuma-kuma! Having departed from the mountain of Takachiho, having chased away and driven off the misfortunes of the Magatsumi encountered for the very first time, across the limitless summer fields I advance all alone. Where now is my dear Kimi? Hoping to meet again as soon as possible, to open up my piled-up thoughts before Kimi's august presence, to uphold the sincerity of my daily love, and uniting water and fire with water and fire, how could I fail to serve in the divine work of Kami's yorisashi? Though I am the body of a goddess, chosen as the Mihishiro-gami, I am the precious vessel and treasure of the Lord Ōkami who gave birth to the land! Cranes dance and sport in the august sky, small birds sing the spring of the heavenly realm, and the buzzing voices of insects among the myriad grasses bless my departure. The neighing of my horse is brave, the sound of the wind blows sharp and clear, and the field where I walk is vast and wide, manifesting here the august majesty of the Lord Kami whose end is unknown. Ah, Ameno-mi-kuma-kuma! The mountain of Sakae-shiro has also drawn near. Resting the hooves of the hayakoma for a little while now, I too shall rest in the shade of these two evergreen trees, the twin pines.

So chanting, she leaps nimbly down from her horse, and rests for a while beneath the roots of the double pines.

The august sky of Hikutakata is high, the plain is wide; even as I walk all alone through its very center, riding my horse across the young land's great plain, to go is a joy, making Kimi my strength. In a wide plain where there is nothing to catch the eye, how wondrous are these twin pines! When I rest in the shade of the twin pines, a flock of white cranes dances low in the august sky. These pines are overhung by branches; this must be a sacred place where true cranes rest their wings. The hayakoma munches on the green grass, while I now inhale the water and fire of the living Kototama. Born in the heavenly realm, I am one who lives while inhaling the water and fire of the pure Kototama. The flowers of a hundred grasses bloom and fill the field in manifold colors, adorning the path I walk; in the Ōnohara where various flowers bloom and fragrance drifts, I shall rest for a while and chant Kimi's poem. The divine words and Mizu-no-mitama of Akitsuo no Kami—surely they will be unraveled within my passionate heart! Even though it be as hard as the ice of a winter's day, it is a Mizu-no-mitama that melts in the heat! Because you are Kimi who serves in the divine work of giving birth to children, I will not tire even if I walk on forever. Proclaiming myself magnanimously as a Mihishiro-gami, I shall serve before Kimi's august presence. Though I do not yet know where Kimi resides, how could the sincerity of an arrow-bamboo heart fail to commune? Departing from the precious miyai of Takachiho, traveling alone—is it not that I have come to meet Kimi, warding off the ugly rage of the Magatsumi? Even though Kimi is at the distant end of ten thousand leagues, let me go forth to seek with the horse of sincerity! My horse's steps are swift; for many ten thousand leagues, even the sky beyond will be crossed with ease. Standing in the shade of the twin ever-green pines, behold the forms of Kimi and myself! One is truly a male pine, and the other—might it be a female pine resembling my very own body?

と歌はせつつ駒をひらりと飛び下り、二本松の樹下に暫しを休らひ給ふ。

『久方の御空は高し野は広し その真中をわれ一人行くも 国土稚き大野を駒に跨りて 行くはたのしも岐美を力に 目にさはるもの一つなき広野原に 珍しきかも二本の松 二本の松の樹蔭に休らへば 御空に低う田鶴の舞ふなり この松は梢こもれり真鶴の 翼休むる聖所なるらむ 駿馬は青草むしりわれは今 生言霊の水火を吸ふなり 天界に生れて清き言霊の 水火を吸ひつつ生くる吾なり 百草の花はいろいろ咲き満ちて わが行く道を飾りたつるも 種々の花咲き匂ふ大野原に 暫しやすらひ岐美の歌詠まむ 顕津男の神の神言の瑞御霊 あつき心のわれには解けむ

§ 2

Though scattered are the pine needles, the twin leaves of the pine do not separate from one another. In the wide field, whose bounds are unknown, how rare are these twin pines that grow! Breaking the twigs of the male and female pine tree-tops, I shall adorn my hair and press forward across the distant great moor. Would that these pine twigs I adorn myself with might serve as a token of my vow offered unto Kimi! Do the true cranes dance, blessing my path, as they beat their wings high above my head? Though I am one who should dwell in peace, yet because of love I set forth upon a journey of ten thousand leagues. Through the vast Kamikuni, whose bounds are unknown, do I journey to meet Kimi alone? My love is as vast and distant as the misora, unlike the boat of Tsukiyomi. Is the steadfast heart of Himegami something that even the rock-bed cannot pierce without yielding? Come then, once more straddling my steed, I shall gallop forth across the wide plains of Banri.

Having thus chanted, she lightly vaulted onto her steed, and gazing up at the sunlight shining brilliantly in the misora, she chanted a miuta once more: 'The sun that emerged upon the ridge of the mountain of Takateru shines brightly above my kashirabe. By the time it sinks to rest upon the peak of Takachiho, I shall advance unto the mountain of Sakaki. O beloved mountain of Sakaki, when I think of it as the sugado where my Kimi once prayed! Worshipping at the miya upon the ridge of Sakakiyama, I shall inquire after the yukuhe of Kimi. Though I hear tidings that Kimi dwells within the kuni of the west, having toured the kuni of the south, in the kuni of the west there is no Mihishiro-no-Kami, and I shall advance to serve in the divine work. I hear that the great Magatsumi of Sūyatogoru becomes dark clouds and rages day and night. The ugly Magatsumi that torments my dear Kimi, I shall drive away with the power of the true fire. Against the power of the true fire taught by the divine words of the god of Hayaminade, there is no Magatsumi that can stand. As a weapon peerless when facing the Magatsumi, there is nothing that surpasses the true fire of the flint. The Magatsumi, fearing the yang-fire, torment the Kunitsukami with the yin-fire. Is the wind that blows gently also fragrant—is it the scent of a thousand flowers upon the mountain of Sakaki? Hurrying to the auspicious mountain of Sakaki, I shall yearn for the wake of Kimi. The mountain of Sakaki looms hazy in the depths of the distant moor; let me strike my steed with a single whip and hasten thither.'

Chanting thus, aiming for the mountain of Sakaki looming vaguely in the distant sky, Asakahime-no-Kami arrived with ease around the twilight of that day. The gods of Mount Sakaki, having learned through the tidings of wild geese that the Mihishiro-no-Kami was coming forth, came out to welcome her as far as the bank of the clear stream flowing at the foot of the mountain. The names of those gods were Hatazukuri-no-O-no-Kami, Sanbana-no-O-no-Kami, Nakawari-no-O-no-Kami, Sayofuke-no-Kami, and Chikasachi-no-O-no-Kami, being five pillars in all, and they were all gods born from the Kototama of the U-sound.

斯く歌ひ給ひ、ひらりと駒に跨り、御空に輝く日の御光を仰ぎながら、またもや御歌詠ませ給ふ。

斯く歌はせ給ひつつ、遥の空にぼんやりと霞む栄城の山を目当に、其の日の黄昏れる頃、朝香比女の神は安々と着かせ給ひける。栄城山の神々は御樋代神出でますと、雁の便りに聞き知りまして、山麓に横はる細溪川の岸辺まで出迎へ給ふ。其の神の御名は機造男の神、散花男の神、中割男の神、小夜更の神、親幸男の神の五柱にして、何れもウ声の言霊より生り出で給ひし神々におはせり。
