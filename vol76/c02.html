Chapter 2: Demon の渓流 [1919]

魔の渓流〔1919〕

§ 1

Herein, the eight Yahashira no Mihishirogami gathered far away in Amatsutakamiya, ascending to offer prayers; receiving the divine decree of the Lord Ōkami, they were bestowed with two pillars as the divine administrators of the Takachihomiya palace, namely Unarizu no Kami and Amatsumeo no Kami, and cheerfully mounting their white horses with the brave sound of hooves, while returning toward the Takachihomiya palace, Unarizu no Kami caused the march song to be chanted.

By the divine decree of the Lord Ōkami, Protecting the eight pillars of deities, In the true Mahoraba of the Shibitenkai, Towering stands the sacred Miyama, Intending to serve at the Takachihomiya At the foot of the divine mountain, Entrusting to the heart of the deity, How joyful it is to advance onward! The moonlight hanging in the bright Misora, And the Amatsuhikage are both pure, Clouds and mist clear away and Ametsuchi Sings of the eternal spring of Tokoyo. A hundred kinds of Kigusa emit Fragrant scents, and various flowers Competing in brilliance are in bloom, Truly a joyous land of Kunihara! Kotori sing and butterflies dance. In the true Mahoraba of the Shibitenkai, Receiving the divine decree of the deities, Together with the myriad deities,

On the path we advance, should there be Any hindering Magatsumi of ugliness? Ah, Omiya-no-Kami, Omiya-no-Kami, Crossing over the mountains and slopes of Wanri, We march forward in grand array. The horse I ride is a noble steed, Parting the blowing wind with its mane To the right and to the left, Neighing fiercely, it dashes forth. Ah, Omiya-no-Kami, Omiya-no-Kami, How inspiring is today's journey!

As they continued to chant thus and advance, deep in the mountain stream lying across their path, no divine horse could possibly cross it. Gazing out at the sheer precipices of the western bank, each dismounted from the back of their horse, stood by the riverbank to rest, and conversed on how to cross this mountain stream. When the eight pillar goddesses had visited Amatsutakamiya, there was no such deep mountain stream on their outward path; that such a perilous torrent should lie across the same route on their return must be a crafty scheme by Magatsumi no Kami intended to obstruct their divine work. Purifying their hearts and bodies, quietly chanting the living Kototama, and intending to drive this mountain stream away to the distant ocean beyond, first Takanohime no Kami recited a divine poem of the living Kototama.

Revering the divine decree of the Lord of Kami, I go forth; Are there any deities that would obstruct my path? In the clear realm of the Shibitenkai, Obstructing evils shall surely perish. The hidden, lurking, mischievous ugly deity, To my Kototama that reveals your spirit, Are you a great serpent or an ugly deity, O obstructing deity? Reveal your form before my eyes. Resounding like the roaring of rushing rapids, This mountain stream is the incarnation of a great serpent. Endlessly long and knowing no bounds, The flow of this mountain stream is heavily muddied indeed. Within this boundless heavenly realm, Should I fear the play of a petty evil? Even if the fierce rage of Magatsumi is strong, How could I fear, being a deity myself? However the eighty Magatsumi rage or riot, They cannot prevail against the water and fire of the living Kototama. If there is anything that would obstruct the path I advance, I shall hew it down with the sword of Shingon.

Open up my path, you who were entrusted As the Mihishiro as a wondrous divine vessel! In an age where trees and grasses bow to divine teachings, Why do the evil deities obstruct the way? Bearing the sword of Shikishima Yamato spirit, I shall cut down and hew the eighty Magatsumi. Being myself a divine vessel of the mighty gods, I shall cross easily, filling up the mountain stream. Right on the return path from visiting the western palace, Did the deities of evil obstruct the way? Serve at the Takachihomiya of high heaven, ancient and far; Come forth, administrators, revere and tremble in awe! Do you not yet know the eight pillars who are the Mihishiro and divine vessels, O Magatsumi? Flowing so intensely, this deep mountain stream, If I stop the water rapids and cross over, Do you not know the coming forth of Unarizu no Kami? The foolishness of the eighty Magatsumi deities,

The Magatsumi hiding within the dark clouds, Surely the time for them to perish has now come. The heavenly realm created by the Lord of Kami, There is no path for the deities of evil to dwell. While moon and sun shine brilliantly in the Misora, Protect our journey ahead. Clearing away the darkness of Yamatama, the solemn spirit, The shining spirit is brilliantly radiant. Bowing I see, looking up I see the wondrous form Of Ametsuchi, before which I am filled with awe. Swelling thickly, surging deep within the mountain stream, O eighty Magatsumi lurking in the clouds, Rising like steam, rising like smoke, How wondrous is the shape of the clouds! By the virtue of Unarizu no Kami, clearing away the obstruction Of the Magatsumi, I shall advance. I who was chosen to become a Mihishirogami, How could I fear the ugly clouds and mist? I feel pity for the spirits whose polluted water and fire Gather together to become Magatsumi.

§ 2

The sound of the murmuring stream is heard on high,

The flow of the ravine bites the boulders,

Clapping hands, proclaiming the divine words of the Amatsumakoto,

Let us try reciting them for the sake of the Magatsumi.

Though earnestly preached and admonished, the hearts of the Magatsumi

Merely become more and more clouded.

Pitiable are the Magatsumi who obstruct

The path of the peaceful and truly tranquil Kamikuni.

All things visible to the eye

Are the spiritual manifestations born from the Iki of the Lord God.

O Magatsumi who obstruct the Shibitenkai

Where we should spend our days smiling, flourishing, and rejoicing in courage.

Knowing neither distant nor near distinctions, He protects us—

Do you not know the light of Kami, O Magatsumi?

Are not all the demonic, monstrous, and hideous Magatsumi as well

Born from the Iki of Kami?

Washing the murakimo heart and walking the path,

Let the Mihishirogami pass, O Magatsumi.

Struggling along on the lofty banks of this rising ravine stream,

Even as we recite the divine words,

All the accompanying Mihishirogami

Are Gods of the Mihashira whose Kototama are pure.

Both fields and mountains are where purple clouds drift—

Withdraw, O Magatsumi, from the Shibitenkai.

Faintly rising, purple clouds ascend,

This Mikuni is the Misono where Kami dwells.

All the Magatsumi gather here

And lie across as the deep ravine stream.

Be that as it may, though high be the banks of this river,

Let us press forward, filling it with the Ikukototama.

Pitiable are the Magatsumi who attempt to disturb

The Mikuni that settles and flourishes day by day.'

§ 3

Asakahime no Kami chanted a divine poem.

Thinking to serve thee eternally, My Kimi hath gone upon a journey of myriad leagues. Crossing myriad leagues of mountains and fields, having made pilgrimage To the Western Palace, how my heart was renewed! That I was saved on this very day when I was about to fall Into the deep craft of Magatsumi, Had the merit of the Kami Unarizu not been present, Would this ravine-stream not have vanished? Alas, it was the form of the ugly Orochi of Magatsumi That blocked the path in the deep ravine-stream! Realizing that all things are according to the Mikokoro of Kami, Sometimes my clouding heart is put to shame. Halted my horse and rested my breath, as I gazed afar Upon the ravine-stream, the keshin of Magatsumi! Becoming mountains, becoming ravine-streams, becoming boulders, Yasomagatsumi blocked the true path; Yet from this time forth, polishing the Kototama and performing misogi, I shall press straight onward along the Great Way of Kami. Yearning for my Kimi, grieving for my Kimi, adoring my Kimi, The months and days that have passed have turned to clouds.

My thoughts surged forth as clouds and burned as mists, Enveloping the Amatsutsukihi; Struck by the pure light of the Lord Kami, I swept away the clouds of my heart. The clouds of my envious heart also clearing away, The light of the sun and moon shines brilliantly in my breast; Reflecting the sun and moon of the great vault of heaven, my breast Shone bright as if it were a mirror. In an age where grasses and trees alike revere the Amatsumikuni, Why should I lament over trivial matters? Having been defeated by the Kami of the Mihishiro, I regret That there was a darkening in my murakimo heart; Now I comprehend the true heart of my Kimi Who forsook us and set forth upon the journey. If my heart had its cloudiness, water and fire with water and fire Seemed to have no means to intermingle; Rather than resenting my Kimi, I ought first to have resented The cloudiness of my own heart.

Utsukohime no Kami chanted a divine poem.

Born even unto the fruits of Niitoki no Kagu no Ki, My soul-line was clouded; Holding this clouded heart, that I was proud as a Mihishiro— Even now I regret it all the more. Though flowers bloomed fragrantly on mountains and in fields, though all birds Sang, it felt nothing like spring. I am indeed one who defiled the palace dwelling Of Takahisemori, so reverend that even to speak its name is awe-inspiring. Ashamed even of the noble name of Utsuhime, Embracing a spirit whose divine soul was clouded and defiled, Thinking to wash the soul of my clouded heart, This ravine-stream must have been born into being. With Magatsumi dwelling within my murakimo heart, It must have blocked the path where I was bound. Not thinking of the deity of distortion as a foe, but born from the cloudiness Of my own soul, O deep ravine-stream! Startled by the living Kototama of Unarizu, Trembling and quaking, the distortion departed. At the Kototama proclaimed by the Kami Unarizu, Both body and soul-line trembled indeed.

From this day forth, returning to my primal heart, I shall serve in the sacred rituals of misogi.

Samewakehime no Kami chanted a divine poem.

For how many years serving in the palace dwelling of Takahisemori, Yet the clouding of my soul-line still did not depart; The longing in my heart for my Kimi piled up, And both my divine soul and water and fire were clouded! Purifying my murakimo heart, from this day forth With a sincere heart I shall serve Kami. Thinking that all things are the divine works of Kami's sa-izashi, There is no way I could resent my Kimi. Beholding the reverence of the form of Michitachi no Kami, All the more I think upon my Kimi. The divine pillars of the eternal heavenly Michitachi Smiled most solemnly. Thinking upon the loftiness of my Kimi, even surpassing the Kami of Michitachi, My heart can scarce endure it! Suppressing a heart so hard to endure, passing the months and years As one who serves—how painful it truly was! Though knowing it is a thing that will not return even with regret, At times it has come to pass that I am sorrowful. Though mountains and rivers are pure and clear, and the voices Of a hundred birds are clear, yet it was lonely.

The cloudiness of that lonely heart has cleared away; Even my breast is filled with the fresh life of today.

Hanakohime no Kami chanted a divine poem.

Though a hundred and a thousand flowers bloomed fragrantly in fields and mountains, Yet I thought them not beautiful. My heart, twisted and warped, single-mindedly Did nought but resent my Kimi!; My Kimi is upon a pillow of travel beyond a myriad leagues, Suffering to forge the heavenly realm. Serving peacefully in the palace dwelling of Takahisemori, How could I resent my Kimi who was in suffering? Kimi was indeed the divine pillar who gave birth to the divine land of Akitsuo no Kami and gave birth to the spirit of the land. Morning and evening, how shameful I am, Who resented the cold-heartedness of Kimi! Laying days together as I set forth on a journey, I think upon it: The journey of Kimi, whose end is even unknown. Born within the crystal-clear Shibi heavenly realm, What should I lament, I who am the Mihishiro Kami? Where good and evil, joy and sorrow intersect, How wondrous is the Shibi heavenly realm! Encountering calamity, it is a heavenly realm Where one thoroughly comprehends the joy of the true word.

『野に山に百花千花匂へども われ美しと思はざりけり 吾心ねぢり曲りてひたすらに 岐美の上のみ恨みてしはや わが岐美は万里の外の旅枕 天界造るとなやみたまはむ 安らかに高地秀の宮居に仕へつつ なやみの岐美をうらみけるはや 岐美こそは顕津男の神国土を生み 国魂生ます神柱なりし 朝夕に岐美のつれなき心根を 恨みまつりし吾はづかしき 日並べて旅に立ちつつ思ふかな 果てしも知らぬ岐美のみゆきを 澄みきりし紫微天界の中に生れ 何を歎かむ御樋代神われは 善と悪楽しみと苦しみ行き交ふ 紫微天界はありがたかりけり 災に遇ひて真言の喜びを つぶさに悟る天界なりけり

§ 4

When it becomes joy, even true joy / they did not think overly delightful / as I who have passed peaceful months and days / lamenting the age of the gods, even regretting it / joy and sorrow crossing over and back / the world must flourish eternally.'

The deity Sayokohime offers a sacred poem.

'Though there is not even leisure to layer the night-garment / you have set forth, Kimi, on a long journey / you who set forth on the journey of bearing the divine child, my Kimi / I weep, pondering in anguish / my tears rising to heaven to become clouds / falling as rain to pour down upon Kimi / the spring rain in the land where Kimi travels / is it perhaps my tears shed lamenting day and night? / enveloped in the clouds of a heart called love / wandering with the destiny of the soul unknown and bewildered / come now, all you Momogamitachi, aligning your steeds / advance to the palace of the East / through the merit of the deity Unarizu, the shadow of Magatsumi / fading away, let us pass in peace.'

The deity Amatsumeo offers a sacred poem.

'Guarding the Mihishirogami of the eight pillars / we encountered the ravine of demons / now anew, realizing the merit / of the deity Unarizu, with an awestruck heart / by the sharp discernment of the divine words of the deity Takanohime / Magatsu revealed its form / the swift horses have begun to neigh, come now / all you Momogami, mount into the saddles / though the wicked deities block the road ahead / how could we ever fear, with the weapons of Kototama?'

Hereupon the deities of the retinue nimbly straddled the heavenly swift horses, parting the blowing wind with the manes of their steeds, with the sound of bells sounding valiantly, with the deity Unarizu at the vanguard and the deity Amatsumeo as the rearguard, they proceed toward the sacred ground of the Takadoshi no Miya palace—how awe-inspiring it is.

ここに一行の神々は、天の駿馬にひらりと跨り、吹き来る風を駒のたてがみに切り分けつつ、鈴の音も勇ましく、鋭敏鳴出の神を先頭に、天津女雄の神を殿りとして、高地秀の宮の聖場さして進ませ給ふぞ畏けれ。
