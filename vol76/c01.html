Chapter 1: High Palace 参拝 [1918]

高宮参拝〔1918〕

§ 1

At the foot of Mount Takachiho, the primal ground for the establishment of divine government in Shibitenkai, where the shrine pillars were grandly erected and the cross-timber chigi stood high against Takaamahara, leaving behind the gleaming Takachiho Shrine—otherwise known as the Eastern Shrine—Ōmotoakitsuo no Kami, harboring a desire, left behind the eight Mihishirogami and departed without taking even a single attendant deity. Thereupon, the eight Mihishirogami proceeded to Amatsutakamiya, besought the divine will of the Lord Ōkami, and prayed that a deity fit to serve as the administrator of the shrine be sent down. The Lord Ōkami graciously granted their petition and descended two deities, Unarizu no Kami and Amatsumeo no Kami, entrusting them with the morning and evening service of the shrine—how awe-inspiring it was.

Takanohime no Kami offered a prayer before the great presence of Amatsutakamiya, her sacred words being as follows:

'Having come in pilgrimage to the distant Amatsutakamiya, / I have prayed, completing my purification rites. / Purifying myself, I have come from afar hither, / Where the eight Goddesses offer their sincere hearts in prayer. / Guard Ōmotoakitsuo no Kami, / The master of the distant Takachiho Shrine in the heavens. / Serving morning and evening in the dwelling of the Takachiho Shrine, / I lament even now that my true sincerity is still lacking. / As he departs to create the lands and beget the Kokutama no Kami, / Let no harm befall my Lord. / Because of my Lord, the single one amidst heaven and earth, / I yearn and ponder with a profound longing. / To what distant realm has my Lord gone forth? / With an uneasy heart I pray morning and evening. / How awe-inspiring is the grace of the Lord of Deities, / Who has sent down the two administrators for the shrine dwelling. / Unarizu no Kami is a reverend administrator; / From now on, the dwelling of the Takachiho Shrine shall prosper. / When I gaze upon the countenance of Amatsumeo no Kami, / He truly seems identical to the majestic Spirit of Auspice.'

Unarizu no Kami chanted his responsive sacred poem:

'Hearing the divine words and sacred poem of Takanohime no Kami, / I feel ashamed of my own inadequacy. / Though my strength is insufficient, / I shall devote my heart and soul to serve you. / Thinking that I shall now serve in the Eastern Shrine, / My heart rejoices and overflows with gladness. / Compared to the might of Ōmotoakitsuo no Kami, / There is a vast gulf like that between heaven and earth. / Though I am a deity devoid of power, / I intend to serve with all the sincerity that I possess. / Guarding the eight Mihishirogami, / I shall faithfully protect the Eastern Shrine. / Making the eight Mihishirogami my lords, / I shall serve them morning and evening. / As long as heaven and earth shall endure, / I revere them, knowing they are the Mihishiro of the Lord of Deities. / In the Shibitenkai, where the lands are yet tender, / The divine works to be accomplished are endlessly many. / Guarding the great shrine dwelling and serving, / In the divine work of the Lord of Deities, I find joy. / From this day forth, tend to me with care, / And employ me, O Goddesses of the Mihishiro.'

Amatsumeo no Kami chanted his sacred poem:

'How joyful I am today to have been chosen / As the auxiliary pillar to the administration of Unarizu no Kami! / Thinking that I shall serve at the shrine dwelling / Of Takachiho in the distant eastern lands, my spirit is emboldened. / Serving as an attendant to the deities of the Mihishiro, / Come, let us press forward to the Eastern Shrine. / The soul of my body, serving in the grand shrine dwelling / Of the tender lands in Shibitenkai, has grown radiant. / Filled with eternal prosperity and joy, / Let us advance toward the Takachiho Shrine. / How noble it is that the eight Mihishirogami have come from afar / To this sacred place! / Gentle yet valiant, the eight Mihishirogami / Are drawn by their divine spirits. / Ōmotoakitsuo no Kami appears as a radiant light, / Parting and brightening the clouds and mists of the four quarters. / Serving in the precious shrine dwelling where Ōmotoakitsuo no Kami served, / I feel a sense of humility. / Though I am a being devoid of radiance, / If it is the divine command of the Lord of Deities, I shall reverently serve.

'Assisting Unarizu no Kami, / I intend to serve the eight Mihishirogami. / From this day forth, O Goddesses of the eight Mihishiro, / Pray, supply what is lacking in me.'

Umesakihime no Kami chanted her sacred poem:

'In the heavenly realm where white plum blossoms bloom in radiant beauty, / I am a deity born as a Mihishiro. / Since I am Umesakihime no Kami, who blooms out of season, / I shall purify and serve the courtyard of the shrine. / How awe-inspiring is the grace of the Lord of Deities, / Who has granted administrators for the Eastern Shrine. / As you set forth on a journey across the boundless, tender lands of the realm, / Let no calamity befall my Lord. / Since my Lord is a deity of brilliant light, / Even the foul Magatsu no Kami should not be able to obstruct him. / Though eight years have passed since the day my Lord departed, / Not even a single message from the wild geese has reached us. / Where in the farthest reaches might you be residing, my Lord? / With an uneasy heart I ponder morning and evening. / In the pure courtyard of the distant Amatsutakamiya, / The proclaimed Kototama shines in pristine clarity. / Through the Kototama, all creation is born. / The only thing that remains unyielding to my will is the water and fire of my Lord. / Enduring the slander, mockery, and conjectures of ordinary deities, / Unfeelingly has my Lord departed.'

To give birth to the Kami of Kunitama, morning and night / we pray, yet in vain, as water and fire do not unite; / wondering if idly our youthful months and days have passed, / morning and night I weep; / now if it must be, to this Miya with Iyahigoto / declaring, I shall return to the Eastern Miya.'

Kaguhimesan no Kami chants this Miuta.

'The Yahashira no Mihishirogami in vain / have spent the years in the Eastern Miya; / mourning that I have no Isa / to report as Kaherigoto before the Mimahe of the Lord Kami, / rousing my Ogokoro and Yamatogokoro, / as the Miiki of Kimi, who thought to conceive, / became established high in Ama and wide in earth, / this Tenkai has begun to flourish; / born from the Tokijiku Kagu no Konomi, / I, a Mihishiro, live in the world and am lonely; / thinking to serve the Shinrei of Takachihoyama / to the utmost limits of water and fire, and illuminate Tenkai, / filling my Mitama with the Hikari of my Kimi, / I shall shine forth and make bright the Shibitenkai; / serving Kami and yearning for Kimi, morning and night, / I have passed the years upon the peak of Takachiho; / at the Tianjin Takamiya, which is awe-inspiring even to speak of, / parting, with subdued words I shall proclaim: / today, having gained the two Guji Kami, / surely makes it feel as though heaven and earth were opening!

Oh Mihishirogami, who shall return to the Eastern Miya, / cause sacred kagura to be played in this pure courtyard.'

Hereupon, Takanohihime no Kami greatly rejoices and consents to the proposal of Kaguhimesan no Kami's divine words, and bestows upon the two Guji Kami, Ebinaride no Kami and Tianjinmezuo no Kami, white and blue heis and two sets of fifty-sounding bells; when the two Kami stamp their feet and resound them in the sacred place of Tianjin Takamiya, and dance gracefully with white robes and long sleeves, beginning with the Yahashira no Mihishirohimesan, all the hundreds of Kami serving in Tianjin Takamiya likewise chant songs of celebration with one voice.

That Miuta.

'How splendid, how splendid, the very limits of auspicious words! / Ta-ta-tarari, tararira, agarirararito, chiriya, tarari, rarito.'

Hereupon, the two Guji Kami stamp the great earth, cause the fifty-sounding bells to ring gently, and while waving and dancing with white and blue heis in their right hands, all the hundreds of Kami feel as though heaven and earth opened at once, and rejoice and are gladdened with high courage. The hundred birds release wondrous voices, harmonizing with the rhythm of the o-kagura, and now more than ever manifest a realm of harmony between heaven and man. The Lord Ōkami pushes open the doors of Tianjin Takamiya from within, and how awe-inspiring it is to look upon this scene.

Hereupon, Suzukohihime no Kami chants this Miuta.

'Illuminated by the Hikari of the Lord Kami, / my eyes were blinded within this pure courtyard; / as my field of vision successively brightened, / ah, I beheld the kagura of today's celebration! / It has melted into the pure posture / of the kagura dance served by the two Guji Kami; / does the Lord Kami also consent, as He peers through / the doors opened just a crack? / From today, the Eastern Miya too will be bustling / and shining brightly, having gained the Guji; / forgetting even the loneliness of the Miya where my Kimi is not present, / did I behold the kagura in the sacred garden? / The eight Goddesses who were appointed as Mihishiro no Kami / have not yet performed their divine tasks, / yet morning and night in the pure courtyard of the Miya of Takachiho / I stood and gazed at the moon in the august sky; / gazing upon the mirror of the moon traversing heaven, / I worry and fret over the safety of my Kimi; / when I look upon the clouded moon, all the more / my thoughts dwell upon my Kimi.

On nights when I gaze upon the fresh mirror of the moon, / I think upon the august journey of my Kimi and rejoice; / though my Kimi has gone far away, my heart is comforted / by the figure of the moon which I look up and see.'

Utsukohihimesan no Kami chants this Miuta.

'Far away I set out from the Eastern Miya, / and made pilgrimage to the Miya where the Lord Kami dwells; / bright is the Miya where the Western and Eastern Sumeru Ōkami / eternally reign in peace; / the Yahashira no Mihishirohimesan have journeyed far / to make pilgrimage to the Western Miya today; / how solemn also is the divine appearance today / of the Kamitsukasa of the everlasting Path of Heaven; / and how solemn is the Tianjin Takamiya / where the Kamitachi serve eternally; / to pacify the young land where clouds and mists rose in all directions, / my Kimi has set forth; / I too wish to serve morning and night in the Miya of Takachiho / and encourage Kami; / since Tenkai is a divine nation of love and faith, / I consider true words and prayer to be essential; / the precious divine work of dwelling in Tenkai / consists of the Kototama of Isonokoto and misogi; / morning and night, performing misogi in the jewelled fountain, / I shall pray for the happiness of the hundreds of Kami.

The voices of a hundred birds resound clearly, / Within the courtyard of Amatsutakamiya among the hundred trees. / While the light of the daytime moon appears faintly, / There is not a cloud shadow obstructing the sky. / The light of the Amatsuhi, born from the sacred fire and water of the Lord God, / Continues to grow ever brighter and clearer. / The Amatsuhi shines with the utmost limit of its radiance, / Illuminating even our dark souls. / In the celestial realm where both moon and sun shine side by side, / Serving there, what should I lament? / In the vast sky where the moon sometimes waxes and sometimes wanes, / I have awakened to the state of this world. / By the light of the moon that grows old day by day, / Surely I have awakened to a world that never remains the same. / Come now, accompanied by the Futahashirakami, / Let us return together to the palace of Takachiho.

Sawakehime-no-Kami chants an imperial poem:

How wonderful, in the pure courtyard of Amatsutakamiya, / I have beheld the sacred kagura! / Feeling as though heaven and earth open at once, / I have beheld the kagura in this pure courtyard. / The sound of the bells echoes so purely and clearly, / And the Shibitenkai grows brighter and brighter. / The white plums bloom with tokijiku fragrance while the nightingales, / Freshly sing of the spring of yayohi. / Nesting in the branches of the tokihagi pines, / The cranes have laid twelve eggs. / With the clear, echoing voice of the manazuru singing of a thousand years, / How prosperous and bright is the celestial realm! / Though I do not yet know the whereabouts of my Kimi, / Gazing at the moon in the sky, I console myself. / On the clear night of the moon's mirror traversing the sky, / I think of Kimi's prosperity and rejoice. / Come now, worshipping the palace of Takahi, / Let us hurry back to the eastern palace. / The divine officials of the eternal heavenly path, / The stern teachings were indeed precious!

The divine teachings of the stern imperial spirit's auspicious soul, / Are perhaps the great origin that stamps and levels the world in a cross. / By the power of fire, water, and earth, the celestial realm, / Has now brilliantly and firmly solidified.

Hanakohime-no-Kami chants an imperial poem:

When I gaze upon the adornment of the white plum blossoms, / It feels just like the Kimi of the auspicious spirit. / The thousands of flowers in the divine garden fragrant with tokijiku, / I will pluck and offer before the presence of the God. / If I pluck a single branch of white plum blossoms and use it as a hairpin for my black hair, / I, Hanakohime, shall do so. / The fragrance of the flowers and the echo of the pines are pure, / In the courtyard of the palace where the Lord God dwells. / Accompanied by the official of the eastern palace, / This has truly become a day of joyful return. / Come now, with words of gratitude before the presence of the God, / Let us declare them in detail and return. / Although the peaks of Takachiho are distant and remote, / There is no hesitation in the hooves of our steeds.

Sayokohime-no-Kami chants an imperial poem:

Since I left the palace of Takachiho of the auspicious spirit, / I have spent the years lonely without the mihidoro. / Revering the Gods and longing for Kimi, like the tail of a pheasant, / Have I not spent these long days and months in twilight? / The pine winds of Mount Takachiho morning and evening, / Echo, yet there are no tidings from Kimi. / The moon seen above the ridges of the peaks of Takachiho, / When I think that it remains unchanged, how precious it was. / As the night grows deep, the light of the moon I gaze upon grows clear and bright, / Making me think of him even without words. / Thinking that the moon I see is the spirit of Kimi, / How I have consoled this long separation! / Having come at last to worship at this Takamiya after a long time, / My soul line is reviving. / The eight mihidoro Gods morning and evening, / Serving in the palace in peace and harmony. / Serving with a sincere heart free from resentment and jealousy, / There are neither clouds nor fog in the sanctuary of the palace. / Come now, offering prayers to the Lord Ōkami, / Let us return far away to the eastern palace.

Asakahime-no-Kami chants an imperial poem:

For the first time I have come to worship at the palace of the Lord God, / Feeling as though I were in the palace of Takachiho. / In the pure courtyard of the palace of Chikushi with a sincere heart, / I have beheld the kagura of the beginning of the land! / The western palace, the palace of Chikushi, of the Lord God, / The divine light has grown even more intense. / Chosen as a mihidoro God, I will not hesitate, / But follow right along Kimi's footprints. / Rather than waiting idly and letting days and months pass, / I will advance forward on the journey of bearing divine children. / Before the presence of the Lord God I shall swear and declare, / That I will advance and serve in the divine work. / Come now, leaving the palace of Chikushi behind, / Let us return together to the eastern palace.

As the respective deities chant their imperial poems, they solemnly present norito with clear voices before the great presence, bid farewell to the hundred deities and others serving in Amatsutakamiya, mount the backs of their respective heavenly spotted steeds, and hurry toward the palace of Takachiho, a truly awe-inspiring sight.
