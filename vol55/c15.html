Chapter 15: 公盗 [1423]

公盗〔1423〕

§ 1

Oniharuwake and the other three members of the Brahmin group, having been pardoned by Harukuniwake, became intermediate bikus between sendenshis and zokujins, had their long hair completely shaved off, and by Temes's thoughtfulness, were made to wear tailored kurogoromos, and while striking kongauzues, to perform ascetic practices as yamabushis in the valleys of Mount Terukuniyama, Mount Viktor, blowing boom-boom on conch shells that had been used for military purposes, they proceeded on their way singing senden-kas. They were given kaimiyōs: Oniharuwake was named Chidaukoji, Kumehiko was named Daukuwankoji, Supāru was named Sodaukoji, and Emishi was named Kiudaukoji. Chidaukoji, about to bid farewell to Harukuniwake, Temes, and all the others, composed a poem.

Chidate: 'The divine soul bestowed by the imperial deities, I shall govern as I walk the path of teachings. Now farewell, officials of the hundred ministries, O Temes, Rest in peace through a thousand and eight thousand reigns.'

Harukuniwake: 'Treading upon the dew of the great deity's grace, Go in peace to the waterfall of purification.'

Daukuwankoji: 'Piercing the sincerity of the path of the jade-spear, I shall serve as a divine shield for the gods. O divine officials, masters of this house, together with all, Protect the upper reaches of my body.'

Temes: 'Those who awaken to the path of sincerity of thirty-five, Shall surely receive the blessings of the gods.'

Sodaukoji: 'Returning to the original heart as a manifestation of the gods, I advance along the path of salvation. The crooked deities who secluded themselves in the mountains of Ikura, Are also going forth, illuminated by the divine light.'

Matsuhiko: 'You who are the precious child of the imperial deities, Go in peace, according to the will of the gods.'

Kiudaukoji: 'Seeking the sincere teachings morning and evening, Today I walk the joyous path of the gods. All people, rest in peace, even after my departure, Venerating the gods.'

Tatsuhiko: 'Noble are those who leave the mansion of Temes, Receiving the grace of the imperial deities.'

Mankō: 'Now farewell, four pillars of lords, with vigor, Guard your bodies and serve the gods.'

Haruharuwake: 'As I walk, dividing the righteous path of the gods, Even the hideous crooked tsu would scarcely touch me.'

Shīna: 'When you go, we who remain behind, Are like the cuckots crying in loneliness. Nevertheless, if Harukuniwake remains, Set forth in peace, leaving no regret in your hearts.'

Sumieru: 'Rebuilding the horse of the warrior's heart, How courageous is today, whipping it and pressing forward.'

Sugāru: 'The military lord who once flourished has also served, In the divine army of thirty-five.'

Azumosu: 'Today, bidding farewell to dear friends bound, By the thread of affinity with the imperial deities.'

Āshisu: 'My body, remaining in the mansion of Temes, Weeps while lamenting your destination.'

O Tami: 'To save the citizens in the heavenly imperial land, Your majestic figure as you set forth today.'

Chidaukoji: 'How grateful I am for the sincere hearts of the officials of the hundred ministries; I shall never forget them for thousands of years.'

And responding to one another with poems, the four-person party of shaved heads and black robes, blowing boom-boom on their conch shells, purifying the air of the mountains and fields, set forth, begrudging their parting.

Leading over three thousand horsemen, Stationed on Mount Ikura, The general who had wielded violent might Suddenly opened the flower of repentance, Rejoicing in the divine grace, Yielded to Harukuniwake, Sending the four men and women safely To the mansion of Temes, Carving the supremely mysterious and subtle teachings Deeply into his hearts, Changing into an ascetic of a different sort from yesterday, Transforming into the figure of a yamabushi, Using the kongauzué as his strength, Tracing along the narrow field paths, Worldly thoughts and self-love, Scattered like autumn tree leaves in the cold wind, A sincerity without a trace, Fiddling with the sleeves of his robe in the wind of Tokatabe, fluttering to and fro, While blowing the great conch shell,

Advancing with vigor to the northern forest, Stopping by in front of the shrine, For a while they rested their breath; The sun had completely set down, In true darkness where one could not distinguish an inch, Here the four intended to spend the night, Spreading their straw raincoats and dozing off, At that very moment near the back of the old shrine, Came a human voice, Chidaukoji, who caught it in his ears, Thinking it strange and peering closely, Heard a muddy human voice, Coming in three, four, five sounds.

Chidaukoji, realizing from their breathing that the other three were sleeping soundly without a care, could not sleep himself because of the strange voices of four or five people, and strained his ears to listen.

From behind the shrine, louder voices gradually began to be heard.

First speaker: 'Hey, things aren't settling down at all, eh? Think about it well. Just when I thought I had made it all the way to sergeant, our main general turned out to be a coward, ending up in that miserable state, completely throwing down our helmets to the Thirty-Five Teaching, and even if I get a tiny bit of separation money, there's no way I can go back home and support my wife and children, so I wonder how I should think about conducting myself from now on.'

Otsu: 'Since we have been raised in the militarism of slashing-and-taking robbery, even if we were to say we are taking up other occupations now, is there anything at all that we can do? To become a thief or to become a soldier of the Brahmin army are no different in name except for the scale of large and small. To commit highway robbery and strip people naked, or to lead countless armies to trample upon enemy countries and annex the lands of others, is still just being a thief. Fortunately, since we are carrying our military swords like this, why don't we organize a band of mounted bandits and make a grand development?'

Hei: 'Hey both of you, you must not think such foolish things. If we return by being thrifty with this money given to us by Shiyōgunsama, who can tell how much better it would be to go back to our home province and start some productive enterprise, or engage in honest commerce and make our parents, wives, and children rejoice? Even Shiyōgunsama has reformed, so shouldn't we also take this opportunity to return to a good heart?'

Otsu: 'Hum, don't talk nonsense. Industries based on fraudulence and commerce that tries to deceive people once you hold an abacus—what is so noble about that? They talk about enterprise-based nation-building and gentlemen and the like, arguing enthusiastically, but those fellows are just well-behaved thieves after all. Even big companies and big merchants are all well-behaved fellows of fraud and thievery. Rather, they are executors of covert malice. Since thieves perform grand overt wickedness, even when called the same crime, aren't they more shrewd? Is there any insidious and cowardly demon more than one who puts on a mask of goodness to deceive the world and plot private profit and selfish desires?'

Hei: 'If you put it that way, perhaps it really is so. Well then, I have heard of the three beneficial and harmful friends, and though I do not know if it is a loss or a gain, in our relations hitherto, shall I sympathize with you guys and become an executive of a robber company or something?'

Otsu: 'Don't talk nonsense, we are beneficial friends. The three beneficial friends. Hey, Tei, Bo, what about you two? Do you sympathize with this side's opinion? From this very day and hour is the opening of our thievery business. If you have any objections, I will strip away all your possessions as the first trade of thievery, so be grateful for it.'

Tei: 'S-s-such unreasonable things you must not say! Wishing to be a thief is not something done by one who has parents and children. Since we have parents and children, please somehow spare us just as we are. Hey Bo, you are the same, aren't you?'

Bo: 'Yeah, I also have an aged mother left behind, so it's a single parent and a single child. When I was leaving home, she clung to my sleeve and admonished me, saying, "Every single day pray to the Great Deity of Brahmin... that my child may become a fine human being, and that he may not develop a disposition of wanting other people's things... so please do not harbor evil thoughts." Therefore, I must humbly decline this much.'

Otsu: 'Ahahaha, what a coward. If so, as a grand opening celebration today, we will let you two off lightly. In exchange, hand over about half of the money you are carrying in your pockets to this side. Since we are turning a great disaster into a minor one...'

Kō: 'Hey Otsu, these five fellows have been like brothers until now, so forgive them cleanly. Besides, since many people pass by if we stay here, we can do as much business as we want.'

Otsu: 'Hey, Tei and Bo, both of you, I will let you off today. If I let you carry military swords, it would be like giving a blade to a madman. Simply because you are bound by morality and think you are eliminating evils for the sake of the realm, you might go crazy and slit our sleeping throats, so hand those military swords over here.'

Tei and Bo: 'We are taking these back as souvenirs to our hometown to be our household treasures, so there is certainly no intention of aiming at your necks. Please, just cleanly let us off today.'

Otsu: 'Eh, if that's how it is, you and I are severing diplomatic relations from today. Now, withdraw the legation at the earliest possible moment. Shoo, shoo, shoo.'

乙『コココラ、キキキサマは、ドドドドコの奴ぢやい。ササ最前から、観音を、拝んでゐよつたが、そんな事で、ビクつくやうな泥棒さまぢやないぞ。サア、持物一切を、綺麗、サツパリと、此処で脱いで……下さいませぬか……ウン、違ふ違ふ、脱いで、渡さぬかい。厭ぢやなんぞと吐すが最後、汝の素ツ首ひつつかまへ、笠の台をチヨン切つて炊いて食て了うてやるぞ。俺を何方と心得てる。バラモン教に於て驍名かくれなき鬼春別将軍の部下ベル、シャル、ヘル三人だ。サ、綺麗サツパリと脱いだり脱いだり。コラ、シャル、ヘル、汝もチツと加勢を致さぬかい……千騎一騎の場合だぞ。親方許りに働かすといふ事があるか』

ヘル『さうだから、こんな商売は止めといふのだよ』

ベル『乗りかけた舟だ。今となつて卑怯未練にやめられるかい。鬼春別様の顔に泥を塗るやうなものだ。鬼春別様は堂々と三千の軍隊を引率して、強盗強姦放火まで遊ばしたでないか。運がよければ人の国まで占領せうと云ふ大泥棒さまだ。それの乾児たる俺達が、そんな弱い事でどうならうかい』

ヘル『それでも、将軍様は神様の為、国家の危急を救ふ為に、敵を亡ぼすべくお出でになつたのだ。つまり云へば天下公共の為の泥棒だ。一身一家の利害の為になさるのぢやないから、一概には云へまいぞ。そんな事思うてると、却て将軍様の顔に泥を塗るやうなものだぞ』

ベル『エー、弱い奴だな。コリヤ修験者……か何か知らぬが、くたばつたとみえて、念彼観音力もほざかぬやうになつたでないか。サア、とつとと持物を渡したり渡したり』

治道『拙者は治道と申す修験者で厶る。併し乍ら衣類を渡す訳にはいかぬ。ここに金があるから、之を其方に遣はす。一時も早く国元へ帰り、泥棒を思ひ切つて、正業についたが宜からうぞ』

ベル『ヤア、此奴、中々気の利いた事を言ひやがるワイ、オイ百両や二百金の目腐れ金で、遠い道を歩いて国へ帰れば、後にや何にも残らない。一体幾ら渡すといふのだい』

治道『これつきり、泥棒をせないといふのならば、相当に金を渡してやらぬ事はない。幾らくれと云ふのだ』

ベル『ウーン、一寸待つてくれ。一つ計算をせぬと分らぬワイ。……これからハルナの近在まで帰る迄には、何程倹約致しても一人前百両の金が入る。それから母者人の土産に百両、女房の土産に二百両、子供の土産に百両、都合五百両だ。併しそれでは無一文で商売は出来ない。何程ちつぽけな八百屋店を出しても、八百屋だから八百両はいる。さうすると一人前千三百両、都合三千九百両だ。そこへ千円は着物代として此方へ綺麗サツパリと渡せばよし、四の五の吐すと命も共にバラして了ふぞ。バラすのはバラモンの特色だ』

ヘル『モシモシ旅のお方、余り厚かましう申しますけれど、此奴は云ひかけたら聞かぬ奴ですから、何卒半分でも宜しいから恵んで下さいますまいかな。のうシャル、皆貰ふのは余り厚かましいぢやないか』

ベル『エー、傍から茶々を入れやがつて、主人の商売を番頭が邪魔するといふ事があるか、気の利かない奴だなア』
