Chapter 6: 洗濯 Messenger [1414]

洗濯使〔1414〕

§ 1

To the living room of Harukuniwake, both Fueru and Azumosu carried the dining trays, and while making polite bows, Fueru was trembling.

Fueru had heard that Azumosu was a sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, so he had suddenly become frightened.

However, as he had never met Harukuniwake, Matsuhiko, and Tatsuhiko before, he merely thought of them as servants of this house.

Seeing the figures of Oniharuwake and three others, Fueru did not understand the situation, and with a bewildered look, bowed his head while glancing sideways to compare the faces of the four men.

Emishi, upon seeing Fueru's figure early on,

Emishi: 'Oh, aren't you Fueru? Why have you come here?'

Fueru: 'Were you actually Colonel-sama? Goodness, my... why have you come? By your orders, just as I was serving in the blockade unit of Ananai-kyō sendenshi at Shimeji Pass, I was bound in spirit by Sendenshi Michiharuwake, thrown into this household's storehouse, barely released this morning by the young master here, and have been assigned the duties of cooking and cleaning.'

Emishi: 'Are you alone?'

Fueru: 'No, there are two of us, including Betto. Betto, like myself, has been fiercely active since early morning.'

Emishi pointed at Oniharuwake and said,

'Hey, do you know this person?'

Fueru: 'Yes, isn't that General-sama? Why on earth have you come to such a place with the sendenshi of Ananai-kyō? And are you not caught in a spiritual binding as well?'

Onihariwake: 'Ahahaha, bound by the spiritual bonds of Lord Harukuniwake, I finally threw my generalship to the winds, and today I have become a mere soldier of Ananai-kyō.'

Fueru: 'Little did I dream that General-sama Would grace this house with his presence. This fleeting human world, where dreams Are seen within a world of dreams, Now I realize how transient it truly is.'

Oniharuwake: 'Illumined by the light of the God of Ananai, The darkness of the heart has cleared away.'

Harukuniwake: 'Meeting together at the precious mansion of Temusu, Today indeed we taste the grace of the gods.'

Azumosu: 'Honorable guests, pray listen peacefully To the taste of the menu prepared by Mankouwake.'

Tatsuhiko: 'Though I thought Mankou's figure was nowhere to be seen, He has ended up becoming the cook of the rice.'

Matsuhiko, noticing that the rice was slightly discolored,

Matsuhiko: 'Why does this rice somehow show a dark hue, Could it be something cooked by the ash-colored woman?'

Azumosu: 'Thinking of it as the menu of Mankouwake tinged with color, What wonder could there possibly be? However, looking anew and listening anew with honest eyes, Pray bear with it for this single morning.'

Matsuhiko: 'Somehow my teeth grind with a grating sound, The taste of the rice is like biting into sand.'

Tatsuhiko: 'Mankou must have acted like he owned the place And cooked this ash-colored rice.'

Harukuniwake said,

'Everyone, let us partake. Pardon me for starting first.'

As he spoke, he took a bite into his mouth, rolling his eyes in shock, yet unable to spit it out, and with tears streaming down his face, he gulped it down forcefully.

On top of the white rice, ashes were clumped black in places.

Harukuniwake: 'Thinking it to be rice sprinkled with black sesame, With eyes rolling white and black, I chew on barley and millet.'

Matsuhiko: 'In this ash-rice resembling the color of barley, Sorghum looks unpleasant as it blows away the millet.'

Tatsuhiko: 'Hey, head clerk, is there such a thing as serving this kind of rice to occasional guests? Isn't this far too terrible?'

Azumosu: 'Yes, it was cooked by the instruction of the young master of Mankouwake, so please be patient just for this morning. My, oh my, the kitchen was filled with terrible ash and dust. The rice itself, well, the mountain of a pot erupted and let ashes fall, turning the whole surrounding area into a mountain of ash, so before I knew it, ashes must have slipped past the thick pot lid and invaded. If you like, Oniharuwake-sama and Kumehiko-sama, please partake of it—you must be hungry, but please wait a little while. I will cook some delicious rice anew for you...'

Matsuhiko: 'Oh, Kumehiko-sama isn't even here, why are you calling him Kumehiko?'

Harukuni: 'Yeah, it is all the same whoever did it. Still, I would feel bad to let your kind intention go to waste, so I shall once wash it thoroughly in clear water, dry it in the sun, and partake of it as chiyazuke. Come, clear away the dining tray. No matter who it is, this rice alone is too precious to waste, yet it will not go down my throat...'

Azumosu: 'If that is so, let us first clear away the tray. Wait a while for a short space. Fueru-san, you too go along to the kitchen And quickly cook some fresh rice.'

Fueru: 'Depending on how it is cooked, the rice will grow, Fueru-sama. Now, here is where true endeavor begins. This rice, rumored to be the lifeline itself, Smudged in ashes, I am indeed haikara. Together with Mankōwake, my master and lord, I shall twist my sleeves and try doing it over.'

So saying, Azumosu and Fueru both quickly cleared away the food trays, and repeatedly apologizing, hurried back to the kitchen. When they looked, Mankōwake had caught hold of Omin and was giving her instructions with all his might.

Mankōwake: 'Hey Omin, is the lid of the ash-storage urn always kept properly in place? You must pay close attention so that the fire does not spread toward the bottom. Do not forget to throw away the ashes under the stove one by one. Where is it safe to use well water? Aside from simmering, cooking, and water boiled for drinking, you must never drink raw water. Since it is the rainy season lately, the water has saltiness to it and strange germs are growing, so boil it thoroughly. If you want to drink water, cool it and then drink it. Using raw water is good for nothing other than washing hands or wiping rags.'

Omin: 'What should I do when washing the sink, or washing tableware and cutting boards? Do I boil water for these as well?'

Mankōwake: 'Eh, do you not understand even that without being told? You must make do with cold water for those. And always keep water ready in the water jar, preparing for any emergency. Moreover, you must replace it with fresh water every day. If you leave ladles and such dipped in the water, the taste of the water will change, and the ladles will become waterlogged and damaged, so take them out one by one and leave them to dry. And after using the well, put the lid back on. When using a new bucket rope, if you do not use it carefully after stretching it well, the pulley will derail, so be careful.'

Omin: 'Fake master-dono, instead of just standing around giving orders, please lend a hand yourself as well! This is not a well, you know. We are using tap water.'

Mankōwake: 'Tap water or a well, it is all the same thing. Just like a leprosy patient letting urine drip, if you leave it running in a constant trickle all the time, it is not good for public morals, so firmly close the tap after you use it. And in the morning, use just one bucket of tap water for washing, and use the rest as rinse water for other purposes. If you treat water poorly, you will incur the divine punishment of the great Deity of the Moon.'

Omin: 'Yes, yes. My, what a fussy person you are. Could anyone serve as a head maid without knowing even such simple things?'

Mankōwake: 'What! Is there such a thing as talking back to your master? Why, did you not cook rice full of ashes, and on top of that, prepare a great heap of black, burnt rice caught with fire?'

Omin: 'Since you came out and made such a fuss poking your nose into it, my attention was distracted, and as I was looking at nothing but your face, it got scorched.'

Mankōwake: 'Heh heh heh heh, do you mean your attention was distracted and you were looking at nothing but my face? That is no good. You had better give up; there is no way I could make a maid into a wife, and besides, with those red cheeks of yours, no matter how good an eater Mankōwake might be, he would keep his distance by three stages at least.'

Omin: 'Hotsuhohoho, who considers you their master, or would fall in love with a face like yours? I simply thought it was an exceedingly strange face and was distracted by it. Now please be good enough to go away over there.'

万公『馬鹿云ふな、主人候補者の俺に向つて、チツと失礼ぢやないか。ここの背戸口で盥に水や湯を汲んでバサバサとやれば可いのだ。きめの粗い水だとみた時は、始めに曹達を能く溶かして使へばキツト美しうおちる、さうすると石鹸と時間とが経済になる。そして滌ぎ水は奇麗に濁らなくなる迄何遍も変へないといふと、生地が早くいたむぞ。そして水で洗うて可いものと、湯で洗うて可いものとある。それを第一心得ておかないと洗濯婆にはなれぬぞ。俺は世界の人民の霊を洗濯する三五教の洗濯使だ。併し乍ら今日は譲歩して、汝に衣類の洗濯方法を教てやるのだから、能く忘れぬやうに覚えておけ。洗方に注意せないと、折角の結構な衣類が台無しになつて了ふものだ。絹物や毛織物や色物は熱い湯につけて洗うと駄目だ。又毛織物は冷たい水に漬けても悪い。そして絹物、毛織物、麻織物は強く揉んでは駄目だぞ。曹達（灰汁）や悪い石鹸で、絹物はキツと洗つてはならない、色物は猶更だ。そして色物は皆陰干にせなくては、日向に出したら皆色が褪せて了ふ。干す時は竿か縄を通して、木から木へ掛けておくのだ。白い物を色物の竿にかけると、色がついて台なしになつて了ふぞ。あああ宣伝使も何から何迄知つてをらねば勤まらぬ、本当に難しい職掌だなア』

お民『ハハア、さうするとお前さまは若い時から洗濯屋の番頭をして居つたのだなア。男の癖にそんな事を知つて居るものは、首陀の内だつてありませぬワ。モウ余り喋りなさるな、お里が見えると、折角の縁談もフイになりますよ。ホツホホホホ』

万公『馬鹿云ふな、女子大学家政科の卒業生だ。それだから何もかも知つてるのだ』

お民『ホホホホ、女子大学へ男が行くのですか。さうするとお前さまは男の腐つた女の屑だな。道理でクヅクヅ云うと思つてゐた』

§ 4

Mankō: 'A woman's first duty is to keep her mouth shut. Where is there any such thing as arguing with a man? Do you know the saying "Act as you ought"?'

Otami: 'As for that sort of thing, I am Otami, who has known it since the distant past. It means that the master should act like a master, the servant like a servant, the maidservant like a maidservant, the sendenshi like a sendenshi, and the isafurafu like an isafurafu, doesn't it? If you too are a wakashiyujin, why do you not act like a wakashiyujin? When I think about it for a moment, you look like a baka, you look like a kere-mata, you look like an unuboreotoko, you look like a koshinuke, you look like a deresuke, you look like a hibari, you look like a kiukuwanchō, and if that is still not quite right, you look like an ōmu, ho-tsu-ho-ho-ho.'

As this was happening, Azumos and Fueru entered with gloomy faces,

Azumos: 'Hey, Otami, what kind of rice are you cooking? Feeding ash-rice to our precious guests, won't you cook it over again at once? Come on, hurry up, what are you dawdling for? Ah, you must have been losing your wits over this guest here, not even knowing the rice was burnt, nor noticing that ashes had gotten in.'

Otami: 'I say, Number Two Bantō-sama, please take this man away somewhere. He grumbles on and on about everything from turnips to radish seeds, so I cannot even put my heart into the cooking, oh dear.'

Azumos: 'Ah, you are last night's guest, please step right into the inner rooms. The master is waiting for you.'

Mankō: 'Un, you must be Azumos the bantō. With Suga-ru and Shīna laid up with illness, you must be busy too. However, bear with it for the next two or three days, and in return this wakashiyujin will lavishly bestow a reward upon you.'

Azumos: 'Hee, that is strange. When did you become the master of this place?'

Mankō: 'From the distant age of the gods, it has been decided by spiritual karma that I am the master here. I am the reincarnation of Temusu, the father of the current master.'

Azumos: 'Hee, from a brief glance, aren't you near forty years old? Only five or six years have passed since the master's father passed away.'

Mankō: 'That is yet another parent; when one says parent, all ancestors are called parents. That is why the Norito also speaks of the parents of successive distant divine ancestors. Humans may talk of grandfathers and great-grandfathers, but on the side of the gods, if you call them parents in a single word, that is sufficient. Without grumbling, since I have been teaching Otami everything from how to cook rice to the methods of laundry, listen well, prepare the meal quickly, and make sure to treat the distinguished guest properly.'

Azumos: 'I say, candidate for the young master, we will trouble ourselves to prepare the meal from now on, so please go to the sitting room of Kunikuniwake-sama and entertain the guest for a while, won't you?'

Mankō: 'Though there is no law that a master listens to the instructions of his bantō, since it is a special guest for a while, it would rather be impolite for the master not to show himself. In that case, pay good attention and do everything without a slip. ...Otami, you too, this time put your heart into cooking the rice.'

So saying and casting the words behind him, wandering and searching through the vast house, he finally found the building within the garden where Kunikuniwake's party was stationed and ran toward it.
