Chapter 10: Ogre 涙 [1418]

鬼涙〔1418〕

§ 1

In response to Harukuniwake's poem, Āshisu replied with a poem conveying his intention to consent to marriage between himself and Omin. That poem,

'The wind of Shinado too in the land of Fusa

At the foot of Mount Inokurayama

Gathering and standing, Tamakikiyō

Employed at the Thames Mansion

Morning and evening managing the household affairs

Āshisu, who has overseen them

Is not of the seed of the lowly Shiyuda

Of deep pedigree as well, the land of Biku

The Kiyūbitto of the office of Samori

Heard to be its fallen scion

A bachelor enduring this world

Sendenshi of Harukuniwake

Receiving the august word of Kami

Ruling over the land of Biku

Setsuteirisama or the Samorinokami

Having saved the peril of his father

Building the august shrine of Kami

Once more appearing right here

Lady Sumieru or Lady Sugāru

Michiharuwake or even Shīna

The gratitude of having had them saved

Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over

Whether the moon fills or wanes

Even if the stars fall from the sky

Having saved the Thames household

When shall this lofty grace

Ever be a thing to forget?

Living together with a lowly gejiyo

The lineage of Omin's side as well

As expected, born in the land of Biku

From the family lineage of Samorinokami

Surpassing and as a child of a lofty person

A rare august child born forth

Takusuke of the village of Chinu

Becoming a foster child and passing the days

Even in a fleeting body, the raging wind

Blustering forth, the parents

Already became people of the other world

A discarded small boat on a drifting shore

Wandering hither and thither

The end of hardship now here

To even become a gejiyo of the Thames Mansion

The pitifulness of having sunk down

Those who knew Omin's true nature

Except for Āshisu alone

Until now, there was no one

As a child of such a noble person

Omin-sama, who was born

By what kind of Kami's arrangement

The pledge of husband and wife for myself and her

Has come to be caused to be bound

At the Shiyuda mansion, the ceremony of union

To finally hold it

A relationship between the two one could never think of

Ah, Kaminagara-kamigami

The Kami of Idomu appeared

Possessed Harukuniwake

Our true nature in minute detail

The loftiness of having caused it to be revealed!

Even so, we are

Persons in the shade abandoned by the world

Though our two fathers sit

Without even a way to state our names

The misery of spending time lamenting

Has now gradually grown faint

The voice of the bird announcing the dawn

It feels as though the morning sun is near

Ah, Kaminagara-kamigami

Before the august presence of Sumerookami and

The waké official of Haruku

Awe-struck and trembling with awe, the true heart

We offer up and humbly give thanks.'

Omin sings.

Omin: 'Though there are a father and mother like the nourishing roots of Kami's grace,

Separated by the clouds of the floating world,

The karma of a body that cannot even state its name

A Setsuteiri high in the clouds

Though I was a rare child born to Queen Victoria,

The august wrath of the Queen's palace

Was exceedingly fierce,

So together with my mother Satsuki,

In the mountain and field village of the land of Fusa,

Takusuke of the village of Chinu,

Mother and child were entrusted to the mansion,

And spending a sad floating world,

Like the village clouds on the moon and storms on flowers,

Without leaking out of the precedents of a blustering world,

My foster father left this life early,

Just when my mother and I were spending dreary days

By the edge of the mountain,

A soldier of Brahmanism

Taking advantage of the gloom of night, entered in,

Kicking open the sliding doors and dancing in,

Mistaking me and my mother,

Raising a victory cry and dragging us back, but

Rather than knowing the aged mother,

The evil kami who knows no human feeling,

Regrettable and how I yearn for her, the mother,

Kicking her down into the well in the midst of the field,

The vexation of having stolen the life of a thread of jewels

I was left behind afterwards,

In that house for three or four days,

Wandering for five days at the longest,

Driven out of any house whatsoever,

Gradually here at the Thames,

Helped by the master lord,

Just as I was exerting myself in menial chores,

Parting the eightfold clouds of heaven,

The officials of Kami's teachings who descended,

The beloved daughters of the master's house,

Princess Sumieru and Princess Sugāru,

Running bravely up Mount Inokurayama where the blustering demon kami

Had gone forth to save them, after that,

I, folding both hands together,

Return raising a victory cry

Just as I am waiting for the auspicious day of birth,

Leading the lord of the army,

The joy of having returned

While saving four people!

Among the officials of Kami,

The kami official Mankohowake

Whose heart was scorched with love,

Whether it is Kami's contrivance or not,

Suddenly impersonating the master,

Taking care from top to bottom,

Under the hearth and the lid of the pot,

Wiping and cleaning hither and thither,

Even the work of burning fire, earnestly.'

お民は歌ふ。

お民『神の恵も足乳根の 父と母とはあり乍ら 浮き世の雲に隔てられ 名乗もならぬ身の因果 雲井に高き刹帝利 ビクトリア王の珍の子と 生れ出でたる吾身なれ共 后の宮の御憤り いと烈しくましましければ 母の皐月と諸共に フサの御国の山野里 チヌの村なる卓助が 館に母子預けられ 悲しき浮世を送りしも 月に村雲花には嵐 吹き荒ぶなる世の中の ためしに漏れず養ひの 父は此世を早く去り 母と妾は味気なき月日を 山の畔に送る折しも

§ 2

O, the gratitude of being taught by thee! Though there are no vexatious matters, yet, not knowing why, with a confused hubbub, every time it is said, my spirit rises in wrath, and at length results in unexpected ash-scattering. Everywhere is a muddy sea, so that there is not even a place to set one's foot; that it is defiled is truly inevitable. With pains and utmost exertions of heart and body, even the rice that at last has boiled down cannot pass through the throat, being covered over with ashes. With a start, blushing in the face, there are times when my bosom is pained. The Minister of Azumos appeared, and once more gave various teachings. Was his resting-place of the spirits evil? Colluding with the fellow Fueru, clenching fists like turban-shells, beating and thrashing me regardless of the place, and upbraiding me—there were times even thus.

The Minister of Ashis suddenly appeared and came to this place, seized and held down with effortless ease the two persons who had been raging wildly, and saved them—oh, the gratitude of it! The worldly proverb that 'kindness is not for the sake of others' is verified before my eyes. From that time onward, I revered and respected the Minister of Ashis, and thinking that if this were the world for it, I would serve him as my husband, there were times when my murakimo bosom was scorched. Today, the sacred and solemn imperial command of Harukuniwake, the Minister of the Three-Five Gods, that we should pledge the path of imose—the words of teaching he graciously gave, I received and obeyed with awe, reverence, and trembling; and I faithfully accept that I shall solemnly conclude the ritual of marriage here with my beloved Minister, Ashis! Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara! Beginning with the Great Deity Ōkuniharutachi, the divine pillars of binding ties, the Great Deity Kinkatsukane, and the Great Deity Kamususanō who appeared as the god Idomu, I revere your noble grace and offer words of thanksgiving! Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara! Grant us the blessing of your mitama!

Kishunwake became pleasantly intoxicated, and appearing to have grown somewhat bolder in heart than the restraint he had felt hitherto, raised his bronze voice and began to sing:

'I am the deity Ōkunihiko, serving Ōkuniwake, the great chief of the Brahman teachings, becoming a subordinate of Ōguronushi, and as the head-quarters of the Three-Five teachings renowned in the world, to slaughter them in a single battle, leading numerous troops, crossing mountains and fields, passing valley streams, and at last, in the village of floating trees where withered silver-grass grew thick and intertwined, building a spacious encampment, placing Generals Kumehiko and Katahiko as the vanguard, and while watching the battle situation—'

The divine minister Harukuniwake struck hard with his stern water and fire; smitten at heart by those Kototama, easily defeated, I fled back. Seeing that pitiful state—

'This battle being altogether hopeless, neither advancing nor retreating, becoming a distressing plight, following with over three thousand horsemen, leaving the encampment of floating trees, crossing across the Lion River, at the foot of Mount Victor in the old and venerable country of Biku, while once more setting up an encampment and advancing the troops—'

'Deceived by a demonic woman, fleeing far away to the great moorland, riding across Shimeji Pass, building a castle in the rock-cave of Ikurayama, making the countries far and near obey and submit, establishing the Brahman country, and thinking to raise a single banner—'

'Tempted by the crookedness of my heart, telling the people of my house to seize and bring back the two daughters of the Thames Mansion, and negotiating by turns with threats and coaxing, yet Kumehiko, while reeling in tongue at the resolute woman's spirit of never staining her chastity to the very end, being impatient to make her his own with deep-seated obsession, and having shut her up in a single room—'

'The divine minister Michiharuwake came forth, following Shiina, and when he pushed out the Kototama carriage, even those stout warriors were astounded, scattered in all directions, in utter dismay and panic, and at the end of it, throwing the four persons down into the bottom of a thousand-fathom rock-cave for the first time, and adding unspeakable rudeness—how shameful that was!'

'Attacked once more by the retinue of Harukuniwake, the dream of our clinging attachment also awoke, dispersing the soldiers of three thousand and odd men in the twinkling of an eye, sending off the four true persons, and coming at last to arrive here—who could have expected that the two persons, Fueru and Betto, would be shoved inside the august storehouse and suffering in distress? How wondrous! Even the tyrannical general, illuminated by the light of the gods, has now become a true person, and although being one who has borne malice toward this house, to rank in today's joyous banquet by the august shadow of Harukuniwake, enduring shame—that too must be because the thread of fate is tangled together to the very end!'

'Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara! Thames, look at me anew, hear me anew, and be pronounced anew! Lady Berishina, you two royal princesses, pray forgive the crimes of upbraiding inflicted upon you! Whether the morning sun shines brightly or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, Kishunwake, once awakened to the gods, shall to the very end spend his body for the sake of sincerity, return to the true heart of saving worldly people, and pray morning and evening for the happiness of the bodies of Thames and his wife! Forgive us, Kamunagara! I swear to the gods and offer my apologies!'

So saying, he finished singing, and bowing with deep reverence toward everyone, offered his thanks. Yet the suspicious Thames couple could not believe Kishunwake's remorse and apology from the bottom of his heart. Therefore, the couple did not utter even a single word in reply to this song. Although there are many songs by Kumehiko, Spar, Emishi, and others besides, since it is too long, the Kototama carriage is stopped here. Ah, Kamunagara Tamafuru-mawase.
