Chapter 11: 経愕 [1419]

経愕〔1419〕

§ 1

Temes could not trust from the bottom of his heart General Oniharuwake, who had repented, completely purified his spiritual soul, and reached the state of mind of a true Shinjin, together with Kumehiko, Spar, and Emishi, and therefore in the presence of Harukuniwake on the surface he treated them with due respect.

Yet in his innermost heart he detested them like centipedes and feared them all the more.

A sentiment of exclusion arose from nowhere.

Oniharuwake added patience upon patience and was well able to endure even ubiquitous insults.

Oniharuwake entered a separate room and chanted the Brahmanical scriptures.

His purpose was, on one hand, for repentance, and on the other hand, to extinguish the fierce fires of Temes's evil mind and self-love.

Temes's family had served as local landlords for generations and had long been hated by the people as saying that 'there is no law for the Jitou.'

It was generally rumored that his accumulated immense wealth had all been squeezed from the sweat and blood of the multitudes; though his status occupied a higher rank than the villagers, his public favor had almost fallen to the ground, and he was despised like a Chandala.

Wishing to bring this stubborn old man Temes to repentance, Oniharuwake intentionally began chanting the sutras in a loud voice.

The sutra text says:

I take refuge and bow my head in utmost reverence and worship.

Desiring to state this meaning once again, he delivered a gatha, saying:

Suppose a wealthy elder possesses a great mansion.

That mansion has long been old and moreover utterly dilapidated,

The halls and chambers are lofty and perilous, their pillar-bases are crushed and rotting, the ridge-poles and rafters are leaning and askew, the foundations and steps are collapsed and ruined,

The walls and fences are crumbling and split, the mud plaster is peeling and falling off, the thatch roof is disordered and tumbling down, the eaves and rafters are slipping and coming loose,

It is winding and crooked throughout, and filled and covered with miscellaneous filth, and five hundred persons dwell and stay within it.

Owls, eagle-owls, kites, eagles, crows, magpies, doves, pigeons, vipers, venomous snakes, scorpions, centipedes, millipedes, geckos, lizards, weasels, badgers, field mice, rats, and all manner of vile insects rush back and forth across one another,

In places where urine and excrement stink, impure flows overflow, and maggots, dung beetles, and various insects gather upon them.

Foxes, wolves, and jackals chew and trample, gnawing and biting at corpses, scattering bone and flesh, whereupon packs of dogs come running in competition to seize them, emaciated and frantic, seeking food from place to place,

Fighting and contending, tearing and snatching, snarling, snapping, and howling.

The terror and extraordinary state of that dwelling are thus.

In every place there are chimera and phantoms.

Yakshas and evil demons devour human flesh. (Chimera are the spirits of mountains, trees, and rocks. Phantoms are spirits dwelling in water.)

Tribes of poisonous insects and various wicked birds and beasts breed and give birth, each hiding and guarding its own.

Yakshas come running in competition, fighting to take and eat them.

When they are already satiated with eating them, their evil hearts turn yet more blazing,

And the sounds of combat are exceedingly to be feared.

Kumbhanda demons crouch upon earthen mounds.

At times they leave the ground by one or two feet, going and returning in sport, playing about with unrestrained abandon.

Seizing a dog by its two hind legs, they beat it so that it loses its voice, and placing a foot upon its neck, they frighten the dog and take pleasure in themselves.

Again, there are various demons whose bodies are tall and large, naked, black, and emaciated, who constantly dwell within.

Uttering great and evil cries, they shout aloud to seek food.

Again, there are various demons whose throats are like needles.

Again, there are various demons whose heads are like those of oxen.

Some eat human flesh, and others moreover devour dogs.

Their hair is disheveled and wild, their cruelty and malevolence extreme, and urged on by hunger and thirst, they shriek and run about.

Yakshas, hungry ghosts, and various wicked birds and beasts, pressed by hunger, turn in all directions and peer through the windows.

Such perils and terrors are immeasurable.

This decayed and old dwelling belongs to a single person.

Before that person has gone forth from it for long, a fire suddenly breaks out within the dwelling later,

And on all four sides at once, its flames blaze together.

The sound of ridge-poles, beams, and rafters cracking shudders and splits,

As they are crushed, broken, and fall down, and the walls collapse.

The various spirits and deities raise their voices and cry out loudly,

While eagles, vultures, and various birds, as well as Kumbhanda demons and the like, panic and fear, unable to escape by themselves.

Evil beasts and poisonous insects hide and steal away into holes, and Pisacha demons likewise dwell within.

Because their store of blessed virtue is meager, they are pressed by the fire, mutually harming one another, drinking blood and devouring flesh.

§ 2

The tribe of jackals, moreover, have already perished before. Various great fierce beasts come running up and devour them.

Stinking smoke billows thickly, filling the four directions. Centipedes, millipedes, and venomous snakes, scorched by the fire, run scrambling to issue from their holes. Kuhanda demons seize them and thereupon eat them.

Again, various hungry ghosts, with fire blazing upon their heads, suffer from the heat and thirst of starvation and run about in frantic confusion and agony.

Such is their dwelling, most terrifying indeed. Poisonous and harmful calamities by fire—the perils among the multitude are not limited to one.

At this time the master of the house, standing outside at the doorway, hears a certain person speaking,

Your various children, having formerly engaged in play, entered into this house, and being childish and ignorant, take delight and are greedily attached therein.

The elder, having heard this, is startled and enters the burning house,

intending properly to rescue them and cause there to be no burn injuries. He admonishes the children and explains the manifold distresses,

Evil demons and poisonous insects, and disastrous fires spread far and wide; the manifold sufferings follow one upon another without ceasing.

Venomous snakes, vipers, and pit vipers, alongside various yakshas, Kuhanda demons, jackals, foxes, and dogs, kites, vultures, owls, and horned owls, and the tribes of centipedes—the torments of hunger and thirst are pressing, and they are extremely terrifying.

This suffering itself is difficult to abide; how much the more so is the great fire!

Although the children are ignorant and hear their father's admonitions, still they remain greedily attached and do not cease their sports and play.

At this time the elder makes this thought, 'The children, being thus, augment my sorrow and affliction.

Now this homestead is not one thing that can be enjoyed. Yet the children, indulging and drowning themselves in play,

do not receive my teaching, and are about to be harmed by the fire.'

He forthwith reflects, establishes various expedients, and tells the children, 'I possess various precious and rare playthings, wondrous and fine carts,

sheep-carts, deer-carts, and great ox-carts.

Now they are outside the door; do you come forth,

for I have constructed these carts for your sakes. According to what your hearts desire, you may play with them.'

The children, hearing him speak of such various carts, instantly race in competition, run out at a run, arrive at the open space, and leave the various sufferings and perils.

The elder, seeing that his sons have been able to leave the burning house and are staying at the crossroads, sits upon a lion's seat,

and rejoicing of himself, says, 'I am now at ease and joyful. These various children—giving birth to them and raising them is exceedingly difficult.

Foolish, small, and ignorant, they nonetheless entered into the perilous house. The various poisonous insects were many, and the goblins were to be feared.

The great fire and fierce flames arose together in the four directions. Yet these children were greedily attached to their sports and play.

I have already rescued them and caused them to be able to escape the perils. For this reason, O all people, I am now at ease and joyful.'

At that time the children, knowing that their father is safely seated, all approach their father's place and, addressing their father, say,

'We pray you bestow upon us the three kinds of precious carts, just as you formerly promised, saying, "If the children come out, I shall surely use the three carts according to what you desire." Now is precisely the time; I pray you bestow them upon us.'

The elder is greatly wealthy, and his storehouses are very numerous. Gold, silver, lapis lazuli, giant clam shell, agate,

with manifold treasures he has constructed great carts. They are adorned and brightly decorated, surrounded with railings all around,

hung with bells on the four sides, and bound and entwined with golden cords. Nets of true pearls are spread and laid out over them,

and various tassels of golden flowers hang down in every place. Various colors are mixed and decorated, surrounding and enclosing them all around.

Soft, fine silks serve as mattresses and cushions, and the highest and finest delicate white cloth, valued at a hundred billion,

fresh, white, pure, and clean, is spread to cover over them. There are great white oxen, fat, strong, and of great power,

and their bodily forms are fine and fair; with these the precious carts are harnessed. Numerous retinues and attendants serve and guard them.

With these wondrous carts he equally bestows them upon the children. The children at this time, rejoicing and leaping for joy, ride upon these precious carts, playing in the four directions, sporting, happy, and joyful, as if they were free and unhindered.

I say unto Shariputra, I also am likewise this: the honored one among the multitudes of sages, the father of the world.

All sentient beings are every one my children. They are deeply attached to worldly pleasures and possess no discerning mind.

The three realms are not safe, being just like a burning house. They are filled with manifold sufferings, most terrifying indeed.

§ 3

There are constantly the worries of shiyōrau, biyōshi, and iukuwan. Fires such as these burn shinen and never cease.

The Niyorai has already departed from the burning house of the sangai, remaining jiyakunen, dwelling in kanko, and resting safely in the rinya.

Now this sangai is all this possession of Mine. The shiujiyō within it are all these children of Mine.

Yet now this place has many various kuwannan. I alone am the only one capable of providing kiugo.

Even though He instructs them, they still do not accept it, because their attachment to the various yokuzen is deep.

Therefore, employing hauben, He preaches the sanjiyō for their sake, making the various shiujiyō know the sufferings of the sangai,

and reveals and expounds the path of shiyutsuseken. If these various shiyoshira ketsujiyō their minds,

they will become gusoku with the sanmiyō and the rokujintsū, and there are those who will attain engaku and the non-retrogression bodhisattva.

You, Śāriputra, for the sake of My shiujiyō, preach the one Buddha-vehicle by means of this parable.

If you and others are able to accept and hold these words, all of you will definitely attain the accomplishment of the Buddha-path.

This vehicle is subtle and wondrous, and pure in the highest degree. Among all worlds, there is nothing superior to it for their sake.

It is that which is rejoiced in by the Buddha, and that which all shiujiyō ought to praise, make offerings to, and worship.

There are immeasurable hundreds of millions of various powers and liberations, meditative absorptions and wisdom, and other teachings of the Buddha.

Causing them to attain a vehicle such as this, He lets the various shiyoshira day and night throughout kalpas constantly enjoy themselves,

and together with the various bodhisattvas and the śrāvaka assembly, riding upon this treasure vehicle, He causes them to arrive directly at the bodhimanda.

Because of this karmic connection, when one seeks thoroughly in the ten directions, there is no further vehicle, excepting the Buddha's hauben.

He announces to Śāriputra, you and the other people are all these children of Mine, and I am certainly this father.

You and others have been burned by numerous sufferings throughout kalpas. I rescue and deliver you all, causing you to leave the sangai.

Even though I previously preached that you and others attain parinirvāṇa, it merely exhausts birth and death, yet in reality it is not extinguished. What ought to be practiced now is solely the wisdom of the Buddha.

If there are bodhisattvas, within this assembly, who are able with one mind to listen to the true teachings of the various Buddhas,

even though the various divine World-Honored Ones act by means of hauben, the shiujiyō to be transformed are all these bodhisattvas.

If people are of small wisdom and deeply attached to desires, for the sake of these ones, He preaches the suffering truth.

The hearts of the shiujiyō rejoice, obtaining what has never been before. The suffering truth preached by the holy ones is true and without difference.

If there are shiujiyō who do not know the root of suffering, deeply attached to the cause of suffering, and unable to abandon it even for a moment,

for the sake of these ones, He employs hauben and preaches the path. The root cause of all sufferings takes greed as its base.

If greed is destroyed, there is nothing to rely upon; the complete eradication of all sufferings is named the third truth.

For the sake of the truth of extinction, one practices the path. Leaving all the bonds of suffering is named attaining liberation.

In what does this person attain liberation? Merely leaving illusion is named making it liberation.

In truth, they have not yet attained any and all liberation. The holy ones preach that this person has not yet truly attained parinirvāṇa.

Because this person has not yet attained the supreme path, even in My mind, I do not desire to cause them to reach parinirvāṇa.

I am the Dharma King, free regarding the Dharma, and in order to cause the shiujiyō to be at peace and secure, I appear in the world.

You, Śāriputra, this Dharma seal of Mine is preached for the sake of desiring to benefit the world.

Wherever you may be, do not carelessly proclaim and spread it. If there are those who hear it, rejoice, and receive it upon their heads,

you should know that this person is an avivartika (non-retrogression). If there are those who accept and hold this sūtra and Dharma,

this person has already in the past seen Buddhas, respected and made offerings to them, and also heard this Dharma.

If a person is able to believe what you preach, then that is seeing Me, and also seeing you, along with the bhikṣu saṅgha and the various bodhisattvas.

This Dharma sūtra is preached for those of deep wisdom. Those of shallow understanding hear it, become bewildered, and do not understand.

汝等若し能く、是の語を信受せば、一切皆当に、仏道を成ずることを得べし。

是の乗は微妙にして、清浄第一なり。諸の世間に於て、為めに上有ること無し。

仏の悦可したまふ所、一切衆生の、応さに称讃し、供養し、礼拝すべき所なり。

無量億千の、諸力解脱、禅定智慧、及び仏の余の法あり。

是の如きの乗を得せしめて、諸子等をして、日夜劫数に、常に遊戯することを得、

諸々の菩薩、及び声聞衆と、此の宝乗に乗じて、直ちに道場に至らしむ。

是の因縁を以て、十方に諦に求むるに、更に余乗無し、仏の方便を除く。

舎利弗に告ぐ、汝諸人等は、皆な是れ吾が子なり、我は則ち是れ父なり。

汝等累劫に、衆苦に焼かる。我皆な済抜して、三界を出でしむ。

我先に、汝等滅度すと説くと雖も、但生死を尽くして、而も実には滅せず。今の応に作すべき所は、唯仏の智慧なり。

若し菩薩有らば、是の衆の中に於て、能く一心に、諸仏の実法を聴け。

諸神世尊は、方便を以てしたまふと雖も、所化の衆生は、皆な是れ菩薩なり。

若し人小智にして、深く愛欲に著せる、此れ等の為の故に、苦諦を説きたまふ。

衆生心喜びて、未曾有なることを得。聖者の説きたまふ苦諦は、真実にして異無し。

若し衆生有つて、苦の本を知らず。深く苦の因に著して、暫くも捨つること能はず、

是れ等の為の故に、方便して道を説きたまふ。諸苦の所因は、貪欲為れ本なり。

若し貪欲を滅すれば、依止する所無し、諸苦を滅尽するを、第三の諦と名づく。

滅諦の為の故に、道を修行す。諸の苦縛を離るるを、解脱を得と名づく。

是の人何に於てか、而も解脱を得る、但虚妄を離るるを、名づけて解脱と為す。

其れ実には未だ、一切の解脱を得ず。聖者是の人は、未だ実に滅度せずと説きたまふ。

斬の人未だ、無上道を得ざるが故に、我が意にも、滅度に至らしめたりと欲はず。

我は為れ法王、法に於て自在なり、衆生を安穏ならしめんが故に、世に現ず。

汝舎利弗、我が此の法印は、世間を利益せむと、欲するが為の故に説く。

所遊の方に在つて、妄りに宣伝すること勿れ。若し聞くこと有らむ者、随喜し頂受せば、

当に知るべし是の人は、阿惟越致なり（不退転）。若し此の経法を、信受すること有らむ者は、

是の人は已に曾て、過去の仏を見たてまつつて、恭敬供養し、亦是の法を聞けるなり。

若し人能く、汝が所説を信ずること有らむは、則ち為れ我を見、亦汝、及び比丘僧並びに諸の菩薩を見るなり。

斬の法経は、深智の為めに説く。浅識は之を聞いて、迷惑して解らず。

§ 4

All shiyōmon and biyakushibutsu are those who cannot attain their power within this sutra.

Thou, Shiyarihotsu, hast still been able to enter into this sutra through faith; how much more so the remaining shiyōmon!

The remaining shiyōmon also, because they believe the shiyōgo, follow and obey this sutra; it is not through their own chibun.

Again, Shiyarihotsu, do not preach this sutra to those who are of kyōmanketai and who calculate according to gaken.

Bonpu of senshiki, who are deeply attached to goyoku, cannot understand it even if they hear it, and therefore do not preach it to them either.

If a person does not believe and disparages this sutra, they shall cut off all the shōshiyu of the sekai.

Or else they will knit their brows and harbor giwaku; thou must now listen to the explanation of this person's zaihau.

Whether it be during the zaisei of the Buddha, or after his metsudo, there shall be those who slander kiyōten such as this.

Seeing those who dokujiyu and shiyochi the sutra, they will treat them with keisenzōshitsu and harbor ketsukon.

Thou shalt now hear again the zaihau of this person; that person, upon miyōjiu, shall enter into abigoku.

Fulfilling one ichigō, when the kalpa is exhausted they shall be born anew; in this manner, passing on successively, they shall reach innumerable kalpas.

If they emerge from hell, they shall surely fall into the realm of beasts; if they become dogs or jackals, their forms shall be emaciated,

Afflicted with ryōtoku-kairai, touched and vexed by men, and moreover despised and demeaned by men; constantly distressed by hunger and thirst, their flesh and bones shall be withered.

In life they shall suffer sodoku, and in death they shall be covered with tiles and stones; because they cut off the shōshiyu, they shall receive the zaihau of cutting and severing.

Or becoming camels, or born among donkeys, their bodies shall constantly bear heavy burdens, and they shall be subjected to various staves and cudgels.

Thinking only of water and grass, they shall know naught besides; because they blaspheme the supreme sutra, the retribution of sin which they acquire is thus.

Or making themselves jackals, if they come into and enter villages, their bodies shall be plagued with scabies and leprosy, and moreover they shall lack one eye.

Beaten and struck by various children, enduring various pains, at times they shall cause their own death.

Having died herein, they shall further receive the body of a python, whose form shall be long and large, measuring five hundred yojana.

Deaf, dumb, and footless, crawling and moving upon their bellies, devoured and bitten by various small insects,

Suffering pain day and night without a moment of rest; because they blaspheme the supreme sutra, the retribution of sin which they acquire is thus.

If they gain the state of becoming human, their faculties shall be dark and dull, stunted, contracted, and crippled, blind, deaf, and hunchbacked.

Whatever words they may speak, men will not believe and receive them; the breath of their mouth shall constantly be foul, and they shall be possessed by evil spirits.

Poverty-stricken and lowly, employed by others, suffering from multiple illnesses and emaciation, with no place to lean for support.

Though they attach themselves to men, they are not to men's minds; if they obtain anything, they shall forthwith lose it again.

If they practice the medical arts and cure illnesses according to prescriptions, they shall further increase other maladies or cause death.

If they themselves fall ill, there shall be none to rescue and heal them;

Even though they take fine medicines, their condition shall moreover become yet more severe.

If others rebel, plunder, and steal, such crimes and the like shall sideways fall upon them as a calamity.

Such sinners shall never behold the Buddha and the Kings of all Holy Sages preaching the Law and transforming beings.

Such sinners are constantly born in difficult places; mad, deaf, and with disturbed minds, they never hear the Law.

In the midst of innumerable kalpas like the sands of the Ganges, whenever they are born they are forthwith deaf and mute, and their faculties are incomplete.

Constantly dwelling in hell as if roaming in a garden; abiding in the other evil paths as if in their own home.

Camels, donkeys, pigs, and dogs—these shall be the places of their resort; because they slander this sutra, the retribution of sin which they acquire is thus.

If they obtain the state of becoming human, they shall be deaf, blind, and mute, poverty and various declines—with these they shall adorn themselves; dropsy and emaciation, scabies, leprosy, carbuncles and ulcers—diseases such as these, with these they shall make their garments.

Their bodies shall constantly be foul places, steeped in filth and impurity, deeply attached to gaken, increasing anger and resentment;

Lust shall be rampant, making no distinction among birds and beasts; because they slander this sutra, the retribution of sin which they acquire is thus.

一切の声聞、及び辟支仏は、此の経の中に於て、力及ばざる所なり。

§ 5

I say unto thee, Shiyarihotsu, if one should slander this sutra, and I were to declare his sin, even if an eon were exhausted, it would not come to an end.

By reason of this innen, I therefore speak to thee: 'Among ignorant people, do not preach this sutra.'

If there be any who are of keen faculties, clear in wisdom, possessed of extensive hearing and strong memory, and who seek the sacred path.

To such persons, then mayest thou preach it. If persons have formerly beheld hundreds of thousands of myriads of kakushiya, planted various zenpon, and their deep mind is firm, to such persons then mayest thou preach it.

If persons are shiyōjin, constantly cultivating jishin, and do not spare their shinmiyō, then mayest thou preach it.

If persons are kugiyō, without ishin, leaving all bongu, and dwell alone in sentaku.

To such persons then mayest thou preach it. Furthermore, Shiyarihotsu, if thou seest persons.

Abandoning akuchishiki and approaching zenu, to such persons then mayest thou preach it.

If thou seest shiyōshi, whose jikaishiyōketsu is like jiyōmiyōjiyu, seeking the daijiyōkiyō.

To such persons then mayest thou preach it. If persons are without anger, shichijikijiunan.

Always pitying itsusai and paying kugiyō to shiyoshiyō, to such persons then mayest thou preach it.

Again, if there be shiyōshi who, amidst daishiu, with a shiyōjiyō mind, by means of various innen.

And metaphors and words, expound the Law without hindrance, to such persons then mayest thou preach it.

If there be a bikku who, for the sake of omniscience, seeks the Law in the four quarters, joining his palms and receiving it upon his head.

And only delights in upholding the daijiyōkiyō-sutra, and even does not accept a single verse of other sutras.

To such persons then mayest thou preach it. Like one who with a sincere mind seeks sacred relics.

Thus seeks the sutra, and having obtained it, receives it upon his head, that person furthermore does not aspire to other sutras.

Nor has he ever meditated on the scriptures of heretics. To such persons then mayest thou preach it.'

I say unto thee, Shiyarihotsu, if I were to speak of such characteristics and of those who seek the sacred path, even if an eon were exhausted, I would not exhaust it.

Persons such as these will then be able to believe and understand.

Kimyō chōrei reihō kaji itsusai ku'yaku kaijo taisan. Kannagara. Tamashii sakiwaimasase. Kannagara. Tamashii sakiwaimasase.'
