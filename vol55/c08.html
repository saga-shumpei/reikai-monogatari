Chapter 8: 放棄 [1416]

放棄〔1416〕

§ 1

Azumosu returned with Fueru to the kitchen, caught hold of the maid Omin, and began to scold her little by little.

Azumosu: 'Hey, Omin, didn't you make a terrible disgrace because you would not be thorough? What on earth are you doing service in the kitchen for? For a woman, rice-cooking is the most vital thing. Weren't you about to make our precious distinguished guests eat rice covered in ash? If you do not exercise a bit of discretion, we cannot keep you in this house.'

Omin: 'Azumosu-bantō-sama, rice cannot be cooked precisely according to your orders, you know. Even Nitsutensama today shines and clouds alternately, does He not? ... Even the morning and evening rice Is hard or soft A world where things never go as one wishes... Is there not even a song like that? If you give me such a scold, shall I tuck up my skirts from this side and set out saying "Good-bye"? Nowadays, many factories have sprung up here and there, and women are in short supply. You rarely find anyone who will become a maid with such low wages as this. The reason I came to be a maid in this house is that I have come thinking it is a part of social service out of benevolence. Calling yourself Man'ōwake, and calling yourself osama, you are altogether a man like a rotted woman. It would be better for a milksop who fusses over women's matters to bite his eyes and die, you know. Even so, I am a smart graduate of a home economics school, so please do not fuss over me too much.'

Azumosu: 'You speak as if you were someone grand, but what on earth was the way you prepared this morning's cooking? Do you think anyone could eat such poorly adjusted stuff? Do not talk so grandly.'

Omin: 'If they cannot eat it, they do not have to eat it, do they? Since you people do not know the art of cooking, you talk in a muddled way, because you are vulgar fellows. Do you have the qualifications to talk grumblingly about cooking when you are of such a vulgar sort that you cannot distinguish whether it is bonito broth, kelp broth, small fish broth, or whether Ajinomoto was used?'

Azumosu: 'Do not talk big! What is that attitude? Making an appearance like a botched prostitute strutting in, do you think proper cooking can be done with ways like that? Now, in setting about cooking, one must put on a sash or wear an apron, tidy up one's attire so that even the hair does not scatter at all, and unless one washes hands that look as if moss has grown on them with soda or something to make them clean, won't germs be transmitted to the precious feast? And even in rinsing the rice, one must take care to remove sand; if you shake it carelessly, the sand settles at the bottom, so it is an easy matter. Rice mixed with ash and sand like this morning's—no one could eat it, could they? And in washing it as well, you must wash it so as not to break the grains of rice, right up to the point where the water becomes completely clear and there is no white water left.'

Omin: 'Oh, what a noisy bantō you are! Where on earth were you working as a boy, you petty stingy fellow who fusses so minutely even to the bottom of the pot!'

Azumosu: 'I have no particular desire to interfere, but since you understand so little, I am teaching you the art of cooking for the time being. Generally, when grilling small birds or fish on skewers, it is better to keep the fire far and make it strong, so that they will be deliciously grilled. Fish should be grilled from the flesh side, and small birds from the skin side. As it has been said since ancient times: fish flesh, bird skin. If you do not grill them sufficiently before turning them over, they will taste bad. And you should place the meat on the grill only after the nets and skewers have become hot. Then, when boiling, you must let the soy sauce and water boil thoroughly beforehand, and if you do not put them in after that, the sweet juices will escape. To boil vegetables to a vivid green, you boil them in water with a little salt added, without putting a lid on, and they retain their color as they are. And once they are boiled, you put them directly into cold water. Things with strong harshness, like burdock, butterbur, bamboo shoots, and lily bulbs, are parboiled once before you boil them down. And the utensils you use must always be neatly put away in their designated places; if you do not clean them and properly return them to their original spots, you will not be in time for an emergency. Consider whether dust is piling up on top of the shelf or not, and then put away nets, skewers, and thin pots. And surplus food must be put into a fly-proof container or covered with a dish towel, you know.'

Azumosu: 'Hey you, Omin! What do you mean, a laborer's cook? If I show leniency just because you're a woman, there's no telling what nonsense you'll spit out! How dare you commit such impropriety and talk back like that? Why, I happen to be the Prime Minister of this household!'

Omin: 'Ho ho ho ho! Prime Minister? It makes my rear end laugh! Isn't the Prime Minister of this house Shina-sama? You are merely the second head clerk, and the minister of cleaning for all the little corners around here. Without grumbling, grab a broom and go sweep the next room! Mount Mankōyama has erupted, and an awful lot of ash is falling. When you use a broom, make sure to hang it properly on a nail. If you leave it standing around, the tip of the broom will end up looking just like a naginata! And when you use the duster, lower your wrists and flap it from the ceiling down along the shoji lattice from top to bottom. For things that cannot possibly be moved, cover them up and sweep starting from the corners. And if you go against the grain of the tatami mats, all the dust will sink right into the tatami eyes! Sweep softly by lightly flicking the tip of the broom. Once that's done, do the cleaning of the engaha. Wring out the zafukin loosely yet firmly, and wipe along the wood grain, putting strength into it. At the corners, fold the zafukin into a sankaku shape and wipe, and it will become kirei. After that, varnish, lacquer, and cypress pillars must be wiped meticulously with a karabukin. You must never wipe them with a wet zafukin, mind you!'

Azumosu: 'Hey Omin, what is this? Being a mere maid, is there any such thing as giving orders to the head clerk?'

Omin: 'Henh! If I am a maid, then you are a manservant! Don't you dare speak to me with such airs. Now now, Fueru-sama! You are the ringleader of scattering ash. What are you dawdling for? Why won't you go clean together with Azumosu's manservant, aaah?'

Fueru: 'Don't shout at me so noisily. Even I was locked up inside the storehouse like a criminal until this morning, so unless I get a little rest, I simply can't bear it, can I?'

Omin:

'Eee, you cowardly, effeminate fellows!'

Without even waiting to say that, she scooped water with a ladylike dipper and dashed it over the two men. Azumosu and Fueru turned bright red, and

'Hey Omin, don't make a fool of us! Eat this!'

swinging iron fists from both sides, they beat down the lone woman. Beaten down by the two rough men, Omin shrieked and started screaming, 'Murder! Murder!' Startled by this voice, Āshisu came running, and all of a sudden hooked a hand towel from behind around Azumosu's neck and pulled him down with a jerk. Shocked by this fierce momentum, Fueru shrank away through the back door and fled.

Omin: 'Āshisu-sama, you have come just in time. This fellow was acting so full of himself and there was no helping it, so I poured water on him. Unmanly as they are, two rough men came and picked a fight, swinging their iron fists against a single woman!'

Āshisu: 'What an outrageous thing for men to do. Hey, Azumosu, what is the meaning of this? Seizing a maid and acting so utterly lacking in adult dignity!'

Azumosu: 'E-chotsu! You come flying in from the side and meddle like that, so my painstaking disciplinary punishment has gone to waste. Hey, Āshisu, what do you think you're doing choking my throat? Look at this, isn't there a mark left?'

Āshisu: 'Once a fight's conclusion is reached, isn't that good enough? Ah, how the whole tatami room is covered in ash! Why don't you just grab a broom and clean up the whole place around here? With all these guests keeping us so busy, is this any time to be playing around with a woman?'

Azumosu: 'This fellow too, seeing that he's been infected by Omin, is spitting out "grab a broom, grab a broom." Excuse my broom!'

アーシス『お上の方をお呼びするには御主人様を旦那様と云ふのだ。奥様はお部屋様とか奥様とか云つてよい。さうして御老人は御隠居様とか、大旦那様とか申上るのだよ。男のお子様なれば坊様、女のお子はお嬢様、或は坊ちやま、お嬢さまなど云つたらよい。二人以上の時は大きな坊ちやま、小さいお嬢様と云ふのだ。そして自分の事は私と云ひ、ウチだとか、アテだとか、ワタシなどは見つともないから云はぬがいい。さうして受け答へはヘエなんと云つてはいけない、ハイと云ふのだよ。朝起きたらお上へ御挨拶をするのに「お早う厶います」と云ひ、晩は「お寝み遊ばせ」、外出の時には「行つて参ります」、自分の用事で外出する時は「一寸やつて頂きます」と云ふのだ。帰宅の時は「唯今帰りました」、御飯の時は「頂きます」とか、「頂戴致します」とか云ふのだ。そして旦那様の外出の時は「行つていらつしやいませ」、お帰りになつた時には「お帰り遊ばしませ」と、かう叮嚀に云ふのだよ。総て言葉使はハツキリと叮嚀にさうして柔しみのあるやうに注意するのだ。使に往つて来たら、必ず直様復命しなくてはならない。後から序に申上ますと云ふやうな懶惰事をやつて居ると何時の間にか肝腎の用を忘れて仕舞ふからなア』

お民『何とまア此処の内の男衆は俄旦那様を初め、誰人も彼れも女みたやうな事を云ふ人が集つたものだ、オホホホホ、これで私も大分に勉強を致しました』

アーシス『お民さま、お前はどこともなしに下女に似合はぬ垢抜がして居るが、実際は何処から来たのだ。一寸聞かして貰ひ度いものだな』

お民『私はビクの城下に生れた者で厶いますが、一寸様子があつて親の名を名乗る事が出来ないのですよ』

アーシス『ウンさうすると父なし子だな』

お民『まアそんなものでせう。併し父親なしに出来る子は広い世界に一人もありますまいから何処かにあるでせう』

アーシス『お前の父親と云ふのは一体誰だ』

お民『私は血沼の村の卓助と云ふ人に育てられた者ですが、私のお父さまは立派な方だと云ふ事です。私の母が奉公に行つて居つて腹が膨れ、奥様が八釜しいので父が金をつけて卓助の家にやつたのださうですが、養家の両親も既に亡くなつて仕舞ひ、只の一人ぼつちで仕方がないので其処辺中を奉公し歩き、二三日前に此処に雇はれたのです』

§ 4

Āshisu: 'As for the truth, I was also born in the castle town of Victoria, but that is strange.'

Otami: 'Oh, you are from the castle town of Victoria as well? Then what is your father called?'

Āshisu: 'This is a secret so I cannot tell, but if you do not tell anyone, I will let you know. Actually, I was also secretly sent to this village as a foster child. The one they call my father is a gentleman named Qūbittu of the Office of Samori. Apparently, because I was born between him and a maidservant, I was sent away from the straw mat to the house of a Shiyuda in this village. Somehow I want to meet him once, but I cannot even reveal my identity and I am in a fix. And pray, whose child are you anyway?'

Otami: 'My mother was called Satsuki. When she had gone up to serve inside the castle of Victoria, she received the hand of a Setsuteirisama and her belly swelled, and because of that, she was quietly sent down to Takusuke's house, so I am told. If you go telling such things my life will be gone, so I beg you to keep it a secret.'

Āshisu: 'Indeed, no wonder you have a refined air about you from head to toe. Ah, I am quite impressed.'

Otami: 'Since the two of us have thus opened up to each other about everything, how would it be if you and I were to become husband and wife? If we do that, we can mutually keep our secrets, you see.'

Āshisu: 'That would be welcome, but somehow I cannot shake the feeling that it is too presumptuous; if times were different, you would be a splendid ōjiyosama. I am of a subject's status, after all.'

Otami: 'Is such hesitation necessary? Come, let us settle our discussion without further ado, shall we?'

While the two of them were talking thus, in the next room, nobody knew whose footsteps went sūsū and gradually faded away. This was Azumosu, who had been eavesdropping on the conversation between the two.
