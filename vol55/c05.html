Chapter 5: 飯の灰 [1413]

飯の灰〔1413〕

§ 1

The Thames fūfu, together with their shimobe Āshisu, were devoting all their zenriyoku to the nursing of the yonin.

As for the eight guests headed by Harukuniwake, they made Azumosu serve as their setsutaigakari, and hearing that Harukuniwake was hastily attempting to shiyutsutatsu from here, they were uchiodoro, and earnestly konguwan-ed that he would remain in their house at least until Michiharuwake's biyōki made a complete zenkuwai.

Harukuniwake, having no other choice, entered a considerably spacious betsutaku surrounded on all shihau by the nihasaki, and night and day preached and expounded the kyōri of ananahi to the renjiu of the Brahman group.

Mankō had arrived at this house and, after exchanging glances just once, turned toward the katsute direction, made a shiyujinkidori of employing the gejiyo Omin, and gave instructions regarding every detail of household affairs.

Mankō: 'Omin, since it is still not long since you came to my house either, you surely do not understand the katsute, and besides being fresh from the countryside, you smell of earth no matter where one looks from. From now on, the young master Mankōwake will teach you about all matters of household affairs, so with that intention, you are to obey with a cheerful yes and yes in everything.'

Omin: 'Mankōwake-dono, or whatever you are called, I have not heard talk of a marriage to begin with, and just whose bridegroom have you become anyway? It somehow makes me unable to feel that you are the master. Furthermore, for one who is the master of a large household to come to the cookhouse, catch the gejiyo, and give directions like this is such a despicable thing, that the shimobe and gejiyo will call you a stingy master and reject you.'

Mankō: 'Do not talk nonsense. If you do not pay attention from corner to corner, you lack the qualifications to be the master of a household. If we keep acting like masters as heretofore, in such a time when taxes are so heavy, will the budget not somehow fail to be maintained? That is why the superiors and inferiors are uniting, first of all arranging the organization of the household, and only after that setting to work on the outside.'

Omin: 'With the biyōki of the Ōhime-sama to begin with and the guests, the master cannot spare his hands, and the two Azumosu and Āshisu are engaged with the patients and guests; even if you make such a fuss, no matter how much you were a thousand-handed Kannon, how could things go smoothly like this within such a spacious interior with just a single woman? Do just try and think about it. From morning till evening, I am being made to suffer like a spinning top and am dancing a frantic whirl, so do not make a noisy fuss. You must be a sham master, must you not? Saying such things will get you nowhere.'

Mankō: 'Do not talk nonsense, since you are busy all by yourself, disregarding even my own person as master, I perceived your bitter trials and came to help you. Will you not clean the place around there? What is this state of messiness?'

Omin: 'Is there any time to do cleaning and the like? I have to throw rice balls to the Brahman guests staying in the storehouse, I have to carry water to them, and on top of that, there is a sudden crowd of guests, so please help out a little yourself too.'

Mankō: 'What, that Brahman guests are in the storehouse—this fellow is strange. What kind of fellows are they?'

Omin: 'They are men called something like Fueru or Betto.'

Mankō: 'Well, that fellow is interesting. Temporarily, I will use that fellow as a shimobe. If I do that, Betto and Fueru will likely be pleased too. Hey Omin, lend me the key to the storehouse.'

Omin: 'Truly, Mankō-sama, are you the young master? If there is no mistake that you are the master, I will hand over the key. Come, take this and go. It is the third storehouse from the east.'

And she took the key to the storehouse out of the drawer and handed it to Mankō. Mankō, eagerly carrying the key, opened the storehouse door, and when he looked inside for a moment with a fearful expression, Fueru and Betto were likewise trembling and huddled together, embracing each other in the corner of the storehouse.

Mankō: 'Hey, general of the Brahmans, I am the young master of this house. Today I will forgive you, so in place of a shimobe, you are to sweep and clean the household. Since guests have suddenly increased so much... you surely will not say that you are reluctant, will you?'

So saying, waving his arms broadly, he came to Ominu's kitchen.

Mankō: 'Oi Ominu, kagi o shimatsu te oite kure, saa kore da. Shinzansha no otokoshū ga futari deki ta kara nanji mo kokoroyōu shite yatte kure. Tada kokoroyōu sei to itte mo teido mondai da. Mashi ni anata no hoobeta wa akai kara, ikanu mono-zuki demo, tsumamigui suru yatsu wa aru mai kara, mā anshin da.'

Omin: 'Henh, just leave me alone, what a strange Dannasama you are, my goodness.'

Mankō: 'Hey Omin, you have four or five fires burning in the hearths all at once, what on earth are you cooking?'

So saying, he took off the lids of the pots one by one,

Mankō: 'Yaa, this fellow is rice... this fellow is okazu... Look here, look here, Omin, after the rice comes to a boil, you must turn the fire way down. And make sure not to spill the white bubbles outside, because the amami of the rice will all go back and vanish. When building a fire, if you think about the procedure of the work and use it continuously straight through, it becomes an economy of kuwariyoku, so if you try to warm up cold hearths all at once like you are doing, it is a terrible loss. If you pass the leftover fire along to the next one one after another, who knows how much of a profit it might be in terms of keizaijiyō. When building a fire, if you regulate it well and keep it to the extent that the tips of the flames touch the bottom of the pot, any fire licking out beyond that will only waste the firewood. Hey, Fuer, Bet, you too, listen well to what I say. Above all, because it will be a loss to the Thames household. As they say, with the servant spirit, when the master is not around, they only do uneconomical things, so this is quite different from until now. Because the new master is a keizaigakushiya, you see.'

Omin: 'Ho ho ho ho, once a master starts opening pot lids to inspect them, that house is already doomed. It seems the household finances are in quite a tight spot, my.'

Mankō: 'Don't say stupid things, don't you know what divine blessing is? Hey, Bet, Fuer, you will have many guests from now on, so please help Omin with her work. Above all, I beg you to value economy and not waste firewood or charcoal. If you use well-dried firewood and recklessly jam a lot of it under the cauldron, it will burn worse instead and end up smoldering. If it is split wood, pile up about four thick pieces. And unless you arrange them so the pieces of firewood do not overlap, it is hard to burn. And once things have boiled up, immediately put out the firewood you were using. By jamming it into a fire extinguisher or pouring water on it, you see...'

Fuer: 'Hi hi, understood. Hey, Omin-san, I also have some experience cooking rice in camp. Just let me show you a specimen.'

Omin: 'Ah, I was in trouble because it was so hot. Well, let me sit down here for a moment and admire your handiwork.'

Mankō: 'Hey, Omin, doesn't it smell burnt? Why don't you pull the firewood out quickly?'

Omin: 'It was because the sudden Dannasama made such a fuss and gave such orders that my attention was drawn outside and the rice got scorched; if it turns black, Fuer and Bet can just eat it, ho ho ho ho.'

Mankō: 'Hey you two, won't you do something? Isn't the pot making a slapping sound?'

At the moment when Fuer hastily dashed a bucket of water under the hearth, a 'boo' puff of ash rose up all over, and the kitchen became pitch black. And covered in ashes all over their bodies, pinching their noses, all four rushed out to the front and are breathing the air.

Asumos, because breakfast is late, gets hungry and comes to check on the situation in the kitchen, and ash smoke is rising all over the place. Asumos shouted in a loud voice... calling out, 'Omin, Omin.' Omin from outside...

Omin: 'Yes, is it the second head clerk-san? Do you have some business with me?'

Asumos: 'What are you staring around for? Why don't you bring the rice quickly, aren't all the guests hungry?'

Omin: 'Eh, though you are a man, you shouldn't make such a fuss. We were just cooking rice with the young master with all our might.'

Asumos: 'There is no way there is a young master in this house, what are you dazing about? Isn't this place covered in ashes all over? Why don't you clean up a bit?'

万公『オイ両人、何とかせぬかい、鍋がペチペチ云ふとるぢやないか』

フエルは手桶の水を慌てて竃の下へぶちやけた拍子に、ブーと灰が一面に立上り、炊事場は真黒になつて了つた。そして体中灰まぶれになり、鼻をつまんで、四人とも表へ駆け出し、空気を吸うてゐる。

アヅモスは朝飯が遅いので腹をへらし、炊事場の様子を考へに来ると、そこら一面灰煙が立つてゐる。アヅモスは大声で、……『お民お民』と呼んだ。お民は外から……

アヅモス『何をキヨロキヨロしてゐるのだ、早く御飯を持つて来んかい、皆お客さまがお腹がすいてるぢやないか』

お民『エ、お前は男の癖に、喧しう言ふものぢやありませぬ。今若旦那さまと一生懸命に、御飯をたいてゐた所ですよ』

アヅモス『当家に若旦那のある筈がない、何を呆けてゐるのだ。此処辺スツカリ灰まぶれぢやないか、チツと掃除をせぬかい』

§ 4

Mankō returned from the back door to the ash-covered kitchen,

Mankō: 'Ah, you are Azumosu, and I am Mankōwake, the young master. I was just now teaching Omin how to cook. I will have the meal trays carried right now to the newcomer Fueru and Betto, so be diligent in nursing the patient. And tell the parents-in-law as well—though it is a bit late, tell them I am bringing it just now... say that to them.'

Azumosu: 'Heh, how strange. When on earth did you become an adopted son?'

Mankō: 'Asking about such a thing is sheer yabo. Ask Sugāru, and if you still don't understand, just ask Tatsuhiko, our retainer who came out this time, and you'll understand. Hey, a man shouldn't be hanging around the kitchen; it's the young master's orders, so get over there quickly.'

Azumosu made a perplexed face, slunk away from the spot, and returned to the sickroom.

Mankō, Omin, and two others once again did a thorough cleaning with brooms, wiping rags, and dusters; using a ladle, they scraped off the ash on top of the rice that had entered through the gaps in the pot lid, dropped it into a water bucket, and washed it.

Mankō: 'In this house, guests suddenly increase, The rice-cooking man blows foam. Unaware that even the foamed rice is scorching, A party of four whose hearts are burning.'

Fueru: 'The young master directs all the cleaning, A flurry of ash dances about. Covered in ash, the body is smeared with mud, How funny to brush away the ash from the rice. Though you brush and brush, it keeps flying in, As the ash rises up into the four corners. It seems Omin-sama has a burning heart, A passion so hot she didn't even notice the rice burning. Young husband and wife, babbling on as husband and wife, Hearing their voices, they let the rice scorch. Burning hearts, scorching rice, covered in ash, Calling this feast the serving of meals.'

Thus exchanging foolish banter, they prepared the ash-covered meal trays and hurriedly carried the breakfast away to the guest room and the sickroom.
