Chapter 4: Truth 異 [1412]

真異〔1412〕

§ 1

With the general of the Brahmanism, Kumehiko who wielded his influence, and Oniharuwake at the head, the four-man party of Spar and Emishi sent four wounded men to the house of Rishiyō in the village of Tamaki.

With hearts aching and voices hushed, exhausted by tears of repentance and gasping for breath, they were in the midst of chanting the Sendenka of Ananai-kyō in whispers.

As they sat there in dejection, the floorboards of the corridor thundered, and Thames appeared.

Not knowing that the enemy general Oniharuwake had surrendered, and mistaking them for the party of Harukuniwake who had rescued his two daughters, he looked upon them with his aged eyes.

He knelt before the four men and began to offer his greetings with great politeness, which was a comical sight.

In the dim light of the lantern, the master of the house, Thames, not knowing they were the party of Oniharuwake, placed his hands on the floor and spoke politely.

Thames: 'Oh, oh, thank you so much for saving them. And may I ask who you might be?'

He peered stealthily at the face of Oniharuwake. Oniharuwake, at a loss for words, scratched his head and replied.

Oniharu: 'Yes, I am... Haruwake. Oh, I have caused you great concern.'

Thames: 'Not at all, it is I who have caused you such great trouble, and I have no words to apologize. I heard you were Harukuniwake-sama, but now I hear you say Haruwake. Oh, oh, please excuse me. My memory is poor due to my age, and I even forget people's names. Oh, I do not wish to grow any older. By the way, Haruwake-sama, it must have been a great struggle. I sympathize with your efforts.'

Oniharu: 'No, I am humbled by your kind words.'

Thames: 'No matter what, since they were captured by those devilish villains of the Brahmanism, Oniharuwake and Kumehiko, my daughters must have suffered terribly. And since men like that Okume and Kumehiko, who are like baboons, were with them, surely my daughters must have had their virtue defiled.'

Oniharu: 'No, you need not worry about that at all. They are quite strong, just as one would expect of your daughters. That, I guarantee.'

Thames: 'I had thought that even if their bodies were defiled, it would be fine as long as they returned alive, but as long as they have no serious injuries to their vital parts, there is nothing more joyful than this. Since they are daughters for whom I must find an adopted son-in-law, as a parent, I am very worried.'

Oniharu: 'Indeed, I understand your concern. Actually, I am that... well... I was indeed... Haruwake. In any case, since things have settled safely, it would be best to accept this as the will of the gods.'

Kume: 'It is a pleasure to meet you for the first time. Are you the master of this house? I have truly done something inexcusable. Thanks to Harukuniwake-sama, I have been saved by the way of the gods, and there is nothing more joyful than this. You must be very happy as well; congratulations.'

Thames: 'Yes, as you say, first through the efforts of Haruwake-sama, and next through your help, I am overjoyed to have them rescued from being abducted by those ill-natured thieves, Oniharuwake and Kumehiko. If I may ask, Haruwake-sama, I cannot thank you enough for saving my daughters, but will those thief generals not come back with many soldiers to take my daughters back after you have left? That is my primary concern.'

Kume: 'Please do not worry at all. Harukuniwake-sama, Matsuhiko-sama, Tatsuhiko-sama, and Mankō-sama have completely cleared out the rock cave, struck the two leaders with great iron hammers, and disbanded the three-thousand-man army, so they have returned here with no fear of future trouble. Please be at ease.'

Themms: 'Well, Haruwake-san, and other disciples, truly things like Goshintoku are magnificent. You said that Kami appears in the open and distinguishes good from evil, but indeed, if good goes to the very end, it always wins. And as for Oniharuwake and Kumehiko, those Dorobauninsoku, did they hang themselves and die, or perhaps they even performed Setsupuku? I should love to hear that as one of Miyagebanashi.'

Oniharuwake, scratching his head,

Oniharu: 'Hey, Oniharuwake as a general and Kumehiko have already vanished. Before long, we will be reborn and come to see you. At that time, please do not hate us as Akunin and speak to us.'

Themms: 'Since it is your words, I cannot refuse, but even if an Akutau like you covers Nanihodo the Tenchi and comes to apologize, rather than speaking a single word, I cannot even let you through the gate. Though you are Akutau whose worms do not settle even if I scraped your flesh, sipped your blood, and hammered your bones, I somehow want Kami's power to destroy you without a trace of Atokata.'

As they are talking thus, Harukuniwake, Matsuhiko, and Tatsuhiko appear, guided by a Gejiyo,

Harukuni: 'My, Themms-dono, I am Harukuniwake. I have rested leisurely. How is it, do all four of you seem a little better in spirit?'

Themms: 'Oh, you are Harukuniwake-sama, why, I was just thinking that the person here was you, Your Honor, and addressed you with greetings. Well, how very baffling it is.'

Harukuni: 'These four people are the famous generals Oniharuwake and Kumehiko, as well as the kernels-sama of Supāru and Emishi.'

Themms, astonished almost to fainting, turns pale in the face and trembling, raises his voice,

Themms: 'If you please, Harukuniwake-sama, make them go back somewhere quickly. Why did such a fellow enter without my knowing?'

Harukuni: 'These four people, as a result of complete reform, carried your young lady on their backs and sent her all the way here. They are no longer the generals of before; they are disciples of Harukuniwake, so please be at ease.'

Themms finally strokes his chest in relief,

Themms: 'Oh, with that I am somewhat relieved. Now, Haruwake-san, you are quite wicked at heart. Why did you not say you were Oniharuwake?'

Oniharu: 'I was too ashamed and had no face to show, so I said I was Haruwake. However, since the demon has already been removed, I am indeed Haruwake. When my evil deeds were laid out at length, it was so painful as to tear at my five viscera and six bowels. The ones here are General Kumehiko and the reformation party of Supāru and Emishi. By all means, please cast away the grievances of the past and associate with us as ordinary humans.'

Themms: 'While Harukuniwake-sama is here, I shall let you associate with us, but since I never know when you might flip back again, I would like you to go home if possible.'

Harukuni: 'Please do not worry at all. I can see straight to the bottom of these people's hearts, so it is already all right.'

Oniharuwake: 'Oniharuwake, possessed by the hideous deity, Through the grace of Kami, has become a human. O lord of the village of Themms, acting in accordance with Kami, In accord with Kami, please forgive us.'

Kumehiko: 'The grace of the Great Deity is deep, the precious spirit, Draw the water of the river rapids, unto me, Kumehiko. Draw and draw, the unexhausting grace of Kami, Flowing on, has washed away the spirit.'

Themms: 'How grateful, the hideous clouds have cleared away in all four directions, Today I see the joyous moon. Guiding ourselves by the teachings of the thirty-five, Forever and ever, serve Kami.'

Oniharuwake: 'Chasing and scattering the spirits of the crooked demons in every nook, This is the day I became Hinodewake. The crooked deities lurking in the fortress of Inokura Were blown away by the breath of Kami. Kumehiko after the departure of the crooked deities Is truly like the moon in a clear sky.'

Supāru: 'Following the lord of the Brahman host, and becoming a colonel, how shameful I am. Awakened by the trumpet of the salvation of the Ōkami, now I have awoken to the true path.'

Tēmusu: 'Can it be that the grateful grace of Kami saves even the Shikogusa without cutting them down?'

Emishi: 'I am indeed the chief of the Emishi, bathed in the light of the Ananahi, and I am the Emishi. How shameful I am, having caused the two sisters, the Miko, to suffer in various ways. O General, this is not the crime of the chief Supāru, but the wicked deed committed by Emishi alone.'

Oniharuwake: 'Hiding the crime of his comrade and bearing it all alone, the chief of the Emishi is indeed a true Mabito.'

Kumehiko: 'Smitten by the sin of my Murakimo heart, I have no words left to speak.'

Harukuniwake: 'The black clouds over the mountains of Inokura have cleared away, leaving only the river mist in the valley that cannot be reached.'

Matsuhiko: 'Awed be the grace of Konohanahime, who has appeared as the pioneer of the Matsu age. If there were not the Mikoto of the goddess Konohanahime, how could we save these sisters?'

Tatsuhiko: 'Just as the Ōkami decreed, the country of Biku is already Tatsuhiko's joy today. When I see the moon shining brightly across the Ōzora, I think of it as the Mitama of my master and lord.'

Oniharuwake: 'The chief Harukuniwake has minutely re-proclaimed the sins and defilements of Kokotaku. There are no enemies in the land under heaven in all four directions, since we are those who leave ourselves to the teachings of Kami.'

Tēmusu: 'Harukuniwake chief and all you hundred men, tonight make yourselves comfortable soon. My daughter must be waiting, looking for her father; grant me, I pray, to go forth.'

Harukuniwake: 'A valiant man who thinks of the affection between parent and child, how could he blame your words?'

Tēmusu: 'Grateful thanks, you hundred chiefs, now farewell; rest yourselves with an easy heart.'

Exchange of greetings finished, leaving his heart behind, he hurries away to the inner room. Omin brings in the dishes prepared by the maids and menservants, adds sake and sets them before the company,

Omin: 'Everyone, I am deeply grateful that you have shown such immense care to our young lady. Though I have only recently been hired and sent here, and still know little of the household ways and am full of shortcomings, it is by my master's strict command that you are asked to partake at your leisure. There is nothing special, but please eat your fill.'

Tatsuhiko: 'Amidst all this bustle, such a courteous feast—ah, it must have been a great trouble indeed. Then we shall gladly partake without reserve. Come, Sensei, Haruharuwake-sama, everyone, shall we partake?'

Harukuni: 'Thank you for your courtesy. As for us, we will partake as we please, so please do not trouble yourself, Omin-san or whoever you are.'

Omin: 'Yes, it is far better for you to make yourselves at home than to have an awkward clumsy person serving you.'

Exchanging brief greetings, she finishes arranging the feast and hurries off toward the kitchen. Mankō, alternating with Tēmusu, leaves the nursing of Michiharuwake and Shīna, and carrying his hunger, appears guided by voices,

Mankō: 'Ah, Sensei, such a grand feast has appeared. One, two, three, four, five, six, seven—ah, one tray is short! Where on earth is the chief Mankō's meal set?'

Tatsuhiko: 'Oi Mankō, you must have already partaken of the feast. Just looking at Sugāru-sama's face is a peerless feast. Surely you can endure going without rice for a day or two.'

Mankō: 'Henh, many thanks for your condescension; unless I were a wooden or golden Buddha, I still have a full man's appetite, so if you like, shall I partake of your portion?'

Tatsuhiko: 'Ya, you must have a separate seat prepared for you, since you are on the host's side, aren't you? We are guests; no matter what is said, we are the new adopted relatives of the Tēmusu household, so it would only be fitting if you served us a little.'

Mankō: 'Un, that was it. Do you think so too? Then my thoughts are not the least bit mistaken. In that case, I cannot sit here feeling like a carefree guest; I will tell the maid and treat you to a grand feast. ...... Although I wanted to feast you since everyone is busy, I cannot do it on a sudden. Tomorrow again, I will make you something sweet and pleasant somehow. Now guests, please enjoy yourselves at your leisure.'

Flustered, bumping into pillars and sliding paper doors, he hurries onward toward the kitchen.

万公『ヘン大きに憚り様、木仏金仏でない限り、矢張食欲が一人前は厶いますから、何ならお前の分を拙者が頂戴致しませうかい』

竜彦『ヤお前は別に席が拵へてあるのだらう、主人側だからなア。俺達はお客さまだ何と云つてもテームス家の新養子様だから、些と俺達にお給仕でもしたらよからう』

万公『ウンさうだつた。お前もさう思うて居るか、さうすると俺の考へもちつとも違はぬ、それでは気楽にお客さま面もして居れまい、下女に云ひつけ沢山御馳走をして上げる。………何分取込んで居るので御馳走をして上げたいと思ひましたが俄には出来ませぬ。又明日になつたら、何とか小甘いものでもして上げませう。まアお客様御悠くり召し上り下さいませ』

と慌て柱や襖にゆき当りながら炊事場さして進み行く。
