Chapter 16: 幽貝 [1424]

幽貝〔1424〕

§ 1

Oniharuwake no Chidaukoji, Daukuwan, Sodau, Kiudaukoji, these four pillars of shugenja, setting out from the northern forest, blowing the conch shell with sounds of bu-bu-bu-bu, making the tree spirits of the mountains and fields resound, stride along steadily with their staves as support and advance.

Chidaukoji set out from the northern forest and, together with the three others, arrived at the southern foothills of Shimeji Pass. From here onward, formidable and difficult spots exist here and there. Climbing panting and panting up the slope, where even human footsteps are absent and the day is dark as twilight amidst dense trees, they keep rhythm with their footsteps and sing as they go.

'The mountain ridge of Inokurayama, this place is the Shimeji Slope famed in name; even horses cannot pass this sloping road, being pulled by the reins of Kami, transforming our bodies into the guise of shramana, to govern the supreme and ultimate Way of Kami and save the people of the world, whipping the horse of our hearts, we four persons are climbing upward. Haa haa haa haa, what a steep slope; everyone, please be careful. If perchance you fall down, the austere training of Terukuniyama, granted by Kami with much pains, will become utterly useless. Ah, Kannagara kamigami! The sky of today is changing from yesterday. Haa haa haa haa, un un un. Truly the noisy voices of the cicadas, even the woodcutters of those higurashi cicadas,

do not easily cross this slope; climbing it seems a painful thing, but having our sins accumulated like mountains purified by the divine water and fire, thinking of it as the starting moment to ascend and go to the heavenly realm of everlasting glory, haa haa haa, no matter how steep the slope may be, how could we falter, Kannagara! If we advance, there is a wide level land; overcoming this difficulty, if we advance into the divine garden where flowers bloom in full brilliance, the sweat and oil squeezing out now will be wiped away here without toil, and like the angels of the divine land, healthy even in this world, serving for Kami and the Way, and for the sake of the people of the world, we shall be able to spend an interesting and precious remainder of life.

The perverse and lawless military men now have their hearts softened; receiving the dewdrops of the great mercy and compassion of Maitreya Kami, they head toward the divine land of the bhikkhus. Ah, Kannagara kamigami! May the grace of Kami be profound, and without bends in the road we walk, without disasters of evil beasts, may you make us advance! When we led the horses and descended this slope, haa haa haa, compared to our momentum then, now there is a difference as vast as heaven and earth. That evil demons and rakshasas instantly transformed into angels of infinite benevolence and mercy is also the gracious bestowal of the administration of the Saniso Kami.

If we look up, how lofty! Shining across the boundless heaven of the distant skies, the grace of the sun and moon is very pure, and the grasses and trees in all four directions are growing lushly and thriving—oh, the joy of reflecting the appearance of the heavenly realm! Ah, Kannagara kamigami! The body that advances according to the will of Kami, no matter to what sky it may arrive, how could it fear, as long as the Yamato spirit of Shikishima shines! Both heart and body, even to the ends of Chikushigata and Takasagojima, we shall advance and go along the Way of Kami; protect us, Kannagara! Untoko dotsukoi dotsukoisho. Before the Omikami who has manifested as the Lord of Heaven and Earth, with awe and reverence we humbly pray.'

Emishi no Kiudaukoji, letting sweat stream down in drops, stands before the party and climbs upward singing with high spirits.

'Spring flowers bloom and birds sing, even the tips of grasses and trees are lush and green; the summer days when they thrive and flourish also pass away unawares into the autumn wind, the winter sky where withered trees rage, the full heavens are suddenly wrapped in snow clouds, and though the sun and moon are hidden, when the time of the return of the yang principle comes again once more, the mountains and fields will again bloom in full brilliance, and how very beautiful flowers will bloom! If we contemplate the state of the world, are the human spirits born here on this earth ever things that do not shift and change?

The colonel of the Brahmin army and his numerous soldiers led and commanded, this Emishi who put on such airs—now completely haa haa haa, having awakened his eyes to the teachings of Kami, casting away all attachments, advancing and entering into the saving way of the unity of the manifest and hidden divine realms, oh, the joy of having comprehended the supreme and ultimate teachings! Whether the sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, whether strange phenomena of earth and heaven occur, this body entrusted to Kami, how could it—dotsukoisho—ever overturn its original resolve, Kannagara! This body entrusted to Kami, practicing austerities for a hundred days and a hundred nights in the valleys of Terukuniyama, receiving the divine virtues in our bodies, becoming bhikkhus who save the people of the world, the moon-lit land protected by the Omikami of Tsukiteruhiko shall open forever and ever. Ah, Kannagara kamigami!'

§ 2

Mitama sachihaimashimase yo

Singing thus, they reached the summit of Mount Shimeji. Six or seven weather-beaten, interestingly shaped pine trees stood in a row. The four of them sat down on the roots of the pines, wiping their sweat and resting for a short while.

Chidau: 'Miwataseba yomo no yamayama aoba shite kokoro mo kiyoku hare wataru nari'

Daukuwan: 'Raion no kawa no nagare wa iyatooku obi no gotoku ni mie ni keru kana'

Sodau: 'Miwataseba izuko mo onaji tengoku no sugata nararamu aoao to shite'

Kiudau: 'Ōzora mo daichi no ue mo aoao to aya o hagashite chiri mo todomezu. Toshi oishi matoki kage ni yasuraite Ase fukiharafu mine no natsukaze'

Chidau: 'Fuku kaze wa amatsumikuni no kamibito no miiki nararamu itomo suzushiki'

Sodau: 'Kurushimite yayaku koko ni nobori mireba suzushiki kaze no waga o machinuru'

Daukuwan: 'Iza saraba kono sakamichi o kudaru beshi tsudukase tamae kamutsukasa-tachi'

Kiudau: 'Kore yori wa iyaya kudari saka to naru saredo mitama wa kamikuni ni noboru'

Singing thus, the four of them descended the dangerous slope step by step with caution.

Daukuwan sings again.

Daukuwan: 'Baramonkyō no Kumehiko to yo ni utawareshi shōgun mo toki yo jisei no chikara nite kokoro no koma o tateru naoshi ono ga hentatsu hikurige Biku biku biku to biku no kuni biku no sugata ni mi o yatsushi kokoro no kagami mo terukuni no yama no tanima ni tachimukai tanima o ochiru iwashimizu tsuzumi no taki ni mi o utase kogare hatetaru akori o tori reiniku tomo ni seijō ni tachinaoshitaru sono ue ni Bikutoru yama no shinden ni sanpai nashite ima made no okaseshi tsumi o kotogotoko shabari wabite Bikutoria ō no mi-mae ni sankō shi sugishi haru no burei o ba haishe shimatsuri sango no makoto no michi no oshiego to shikamatsuramu waga kokoro mamorsetamae Kamagara Kunitokotachi no Ōkami no mi-mae ni tsutsushimi negi tatematsu asahi wa teru tomo kumoru tomo tsuki wa mitsu tomo kakuru tomo karisome daichi wa shizumu tomo makoto no chikara wa yo o sukuu ima made aku o tsukushitaru kokoro no kuraki Kumehiko mo chto-tachi hide no gugo to nari waga seirei wa tengoku ni oborite kami no eikō ni tsukuru mi to wa narinikeri Aa Kamagara kamukamu kami no megumi o tsutsushimite yorokobi kansha shi tatematsu'

Sodau, descending the slope and finally reaching flat ground to recover his energy, perhaps thinking he must sing like the others, raised a strange hoarse voice and began to sing, keeping time step by step.

Sodau: 'Sangokyō no sendenshi Kunikuniwake ni tasukerarete makoto no michi o oborite yu ima made tsudukeshi zaigyō ga ito osoroshiku nari kitari shigo no shōgai aru koto o omoeba mijakiki genze nite chiisaki yoki ni fumimayoi meiri no dorei to naru yori mo ikka banji chakushoku no koromo nugisutete biku to nari uma akago to narikawari kono yo o suteshi shugensha honrai kono yo wa muzai izuko ari nambushi kore uchū shikisoku zekū no yo no narai shūsoku zeshiki no shintai o yayaku oborite wareware wa ken o sutete kototama no kami no yosashi no mi-tsurugi ni mochinaoshitaru ureshisa yo Bū bū bū to hora no gai fukinarashi yuku ureshisa wa kono yo ni ikite jin’yoku ni toraware itaru hito no mi no tenmei kaigo no koe kiite me o samashitaru toki no koe somo hora gai to iu yatsu wa ikitaru toki wa koe mo nashi shinde shigai to narishi toki iki kototama no iki ni yori ōinaru koe o hariagete toki chikaki yamahiko o odokashi yuku kenseisa yo ware mo genze ni sumahite wa itomo chiisaki mono ni shite yobaharu kuni wa yomo no kuni todoroku subu mo nakeredomo kono yo o sarute reikai ni fukkatsushitaru sono toki wa kono hora gai ja nakeredomo sono kototama wa iyataku Takamagahara ni nariwatari chūkukai ya jigokukai samayohi kurushimu mitama o ba itomo totoki tengoku e dōbiki obosu zuishō to yorokobi isami fukitateru Po po po po po, putsu putsu putsu Aa Kamagara kamukamu Mitama sachihaimashimase yo'

Singing thus, the four of them straightened their ranks and, without stopping at the Land of Biku, advanced headlong straight through the bramble-grown mountain paths toward the valley of Mount Terukuni and the waterfall of purification.
