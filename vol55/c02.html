Chapter 2: Way 謡 [1410]

道謡〔1410〕

§ 1

Oniharuwake and the other three took off their zhenecks and holeš-caps, drifted into a coolie-case mood, and while simplification-fication-ing into their innate true hearts, feeling like newborn babies, they proceeded together with Harukuniwake along the spaces between the tumbling rocks, carrying heavy men on their backs, step by step.

Since they did not understand the Norito of Ananai-kyō, they chanted Brahmanic sutras instead, letting sweat stream down in drops as they descended.

Unlike his generalized life up to now, Oniharuwake volunteered for coolie-like service, resolved upon it as a redemption for his sins at the very least, and at the top of his lungs prayed with all his might,

Oniharuwake: 'In the East, Gōsanzemiyōō. In the South, Gundariyashiyamiyōō. In the West, Daiitokumiyōō. In the North, Kongauyashiyamiyōō. In the Center, Dainichidaishiyōfudōmiyōō. Onkorokoro, Sendarimatōki, On abiramunken sabowaka, Hūntaratōnkan ..., If anyone sees my body, let them awaken the mind for enlightenment; if anyone sees my body, let them awaken the mind for enlightenment; if anyone hears my name, let them cut off evil and practice good; if anyone listens to my teaching, let them attain great wisdom; if anyone knows my heart, let them instantly attain Buddhahood; if anyone knows my heart, let them instantly attain Buddhahood; Anuttara-samyak-sambodhi mind, refuge and prostration, practice and empowerment, Namo Brahman Great Honored Heaven.'

Kumehiko, not to be outdone, followed Oniharuwake's example and tried to recite the scriptural texts, but due to excessive suffering his words became choked, and casting aside his lifelong composure, he twisted out and sang a hymn of Ananai-kyō.

Miraculously, because it was a song of Ananai-kyō, it gushed forth like flowing water, in a kamagara manner, and before he knew it he forgot the pain in his body.

Kumehiko: 'When the Gods appear at the front, Dividing good deities from evil deities, How can those impudent ones, Those messengers of the Ananai Gods, With divine power infinite and the Lord of Freedom, Match themselves against Ōkunihiko? There is a limit to playing the fool; With a single breath we will press the attack, And give a blowing of bubbles To the divine pillars of Ananai-kyō, So setting out far away from Haruna, Day and night through the great plains, Climbing up rugged mountains, Crossing over many a swamp, Gathering in the forest of driftwood, I am Katahiko Shōgun, With the momentum of crossing rivers without boats and wrestling tigers, We climb up Kajika Pass. This army guarded by the gods, How could it ever be defeated by the enemy? Advancing, advancing, he gives his orders, At the eighth station of Kajika Pass, Just as we advance, the Senden-shi, Harukuniwake appears, Striking out living Kototama, Overwhelmed by that divine power, Our whole force collapses and flees. How pathetic is that spectacle! We, stricken with terror, Unable to advance and unable to retreat, A matter of grave importance Clinging to the honor of our divine army, For a while we put on a brave front here, Resolving to wait for our time, Bargaining with General Oniharuwake, A coming Victoria, Attacking his stronghold, At the very moment of raising shouts of victory, Are they heavenly maidens or heavenly beings, Or merely incarnations of the gods? Incomparable Yoshikohime, Those two nice princesses of Karna, Borne forth in palanquins, Bewitching the two of us in various ways With the demonic hands of love, And throwing our entire army into disorder. We, beings who are not gods, Thinking them women, let down our guard, Suffering the most cruel humiliation, Smearing mud upon the faces of men, In the frustration of regret, we shut ourselves up In the rock cave of the mountains of Ikura, Hearing that there is a beauty At the mansion of Thames in Tamaki village, We sent our subordinate soldiers And made them carry her back without difficulty. Though we exhaust every art of transformation And fight by challenging them, They will not budge an inch, Against the pure and sincere stubbornness Of the unexpected Princess Sugaru, Even we were nonplussed, And when we were at our wits' end, The Senden-shi Michiharuwake, Appearing together with Shiina, Rolled out the Kototama cart, Whereupon our allies were startled all at once, And as an expedient measure for the moment, Without looking before or behind, bound us tightly, And shoved us into a deep abyss a thousand fathoms down—how miserable! While we were searching for a second candidate To console the tedium of our army and scheming thus, Once again the Senden song is heard, Resounding like thunder. The crooked deities lurking in our bodies, Startled and flustered, Abandon our physical bodies and flee. From then on, our dreams too are broken—'

§ 2

As the sun and moon suddenly shone brightly upon the clouded soul, making it aware of the foolishness of my own body, ah, Kamunagara! How grateful it is to realize that the precious Kami do not abandon us yet! Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if the great earth should sink, once having followed the Kami, this Kumehiko will regret past errors to the end, devote his body for the teaching of Ananai, crush his mind, and single-mindedly serve the true duty. First of all, as our departure for submission to the Kami, carrying the director of Shina on our backs, and descending this steep slope,

Though it is not a path where one can walk normally, putting on a pot, advancing to the mansion of Thames, apologizing for the hundred sins up to now, and serving as believers of Ananai-kyō, Kamunagara. Please forgive us, great Ōmikami, before the great presence of Kuniharutachi, reverently and respectfully I pray.'

Thus he descends while chanting his reflections. Matsuhiko takes the lead, descending quietly while chanting in a low voice.

Matsuhiko: 'To the fortress of the mountain of Ikura, renowned in sound, four directors of the teaching of Ananai, Sumiel and Sugār, both of them, went forth to save them, the missionary of Michiharuwake, to save the director of Shina, Thames of the village of Tamaki having requested it, we climb up.

More than three thousand soldiers of the Brahman religion are locked in, with a momentum that shakes heaven and earth, without being frightened or feared, Kamunagara, going forth just as the Kami wills, as if walking through an uninhabited field, steps easily and spirits light, without incident facing the cave, by the pure light of the heart of the unexpected general,

Our chests too clearing up, blowing away the separation of enemy and ally into the wind of Shinato, the resentment also wet with the dews of the blessing of the Kami on the summer mountain road, the bravery of coming down. Ah, Kamunagara!

The valley water of Mount Ikura, forever gushing, scattering spray, holding up crystal jewels among the rock crevices, playing natural music together, as if celebrating the triumphant return of our party of us. The tips of the trees, green and lush, blown by the heavenly wind of Amatsu, singing together pure music, singing across this way and that,

The mountain cuckoos with clear voices, and various birds whose very names are unknown, raising voices of joy, as if presenting music before the august door of the Heavenly Rock Cave, the voices of excitement echoing near and far, lending an ear to hear,

The feeling of seeing the truth of the heavenly pure land right before one's eyes now, truly a delightful state of affairs. Ah, Kamunagara! Wrapped in the blessings of the Kami, saving the four from hardship, heading for the village of Tamaki, how joyful today's journey is!

Ah, Kamunagara! Before the august presence of the Kami, with awe and gratitude, I offer my success.'

Mankō, from the very end of the party, while descending the mountain road, begins to chant a song.

Mankō: 'The cave of Mount Ikura is a great demon cave famous in the world, that ghosts dwell there and people of the world fear and do not approach is no wonder. The great leader of the demon clouds, serving Daikokunushi,

Famed as a demon general, Oniharubetsu has taken up position, like a demon, a great serpent, or a Magatsumi, doing like the Yamata no Orochi, abducting people's daughters, as snacks for sake morning and evening, plotting to offer them. That strategy too exposed, the missionary of Ananai-kyō,

The party of Kuniwake without a guide inside the hole, the hole how interesting, how interesting, the snake and frog aiming at the cave, just when they are about to swallow now,

Sudden appearance Mankō-goshi, his twisted headband imposing, advancing thump, thump, thump, the nerve of the general Oniharubetsu, how daring! Ah, how brave, how brave!

From now on Mankō, the divine director, heading toward the village of Tamaki, laying open the tales of exploits, astonishing the Thames couple, boasting of that military merit,

As a substitute for the Golden Kite Medal, receiving Princess Sugār, taking each other's hands as husband and wife, resigning from the role of attendant, becoming the village headman of the village of Tamaki,

Thinking of the sideline Kami, hoping to send a peaceful life, my wish anticipated, at the dawn of final fulfillment,

Have you come toward me, how grateful! For the sake of the Kami, for the sake of the Way, cutting off that wretch Okiku, giving up diamonds once again,

Princess Sugār, three times the child of a snake, no matter how heartless my master, this time she will listen, won't she? A personality like Mankō,

A divine director is heavy of burden, thinking about what is called spiritual correspondence, O teacher of mine of Kuniwake, please reform,

And promote the mediation of this match, I beg you! Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if the great earth should sink,

If you would listen to this matter, performing work corresponding to the spirit, I shall show it to you!

Ah, Kamunagara! Firmly wishing and fulfilling, becoming the bridegroom of the house of the village headman, making our name shine for future generations, saving the villagers into the true way of Ananai,

Revealing the divine virtue of Ananai-kyō eternally and unshakeably. Please forgive me, O my master, the great Ōmikami of Kimikatsukan,

Before the august presence of the God of Idomu, I pray from now on.'

Thus chanting his own self-indulgent stray song, he descends the mountain path. Michiharuwake, Shina, Sumiel, and Sugār burst out laughing upon hearing Mankō's song, and while being carried on backs, forgetting the suffering of their wounds, they laughed out loud, saying, 'Ahahaha, ohōhōhō.' Nevertheless, lost in laughter at Mankō's various other interesting and continuous songs, the party amusingly and cheerfully slipped into laughter, and before they knew it, they were able to return safely to the front gate of Thames in the village of Tamaki.
