Chapter 13: 蘇 Song [1421]

蘇歌〔1421〕

§ 1

Aoba also rustling in the summer wind, clear and fine in one room, the master of the mansion, Themes, suspecting and also hating Oniharuwake and Kumehiko, though his heart hurried away in a thousand directions thinking that he would make them leave his house even a single moment sooner, yet holding down his breast with hesitation towards the entourage of Harukuniwake, the sendenshi of the Kami of Ananahi who had received great grace, he rushed into the interior of a room, made them put on Hohokamuri over Shiyurobauki and arranged four brooms; because they had not yet returned, praying to Kamisama to drive them out and trying to banish the misfortune of the house, tormented by the mind of Shini, he stole into the neighboring living room where the general of Oniharuwake was reading scriptures with Itsushinfuran, and waiting while chanting Darani with all his might in his belly, suddenly his Shintai vibrated, his head ached, his chest clogged, and he fell into Jinjifusei; that spirit quickly slipped out of the Shintainai, and with a stick for strength, he went forward step by step with tears into Karenohara where there is no knowing the limit, the hellish road where cold winds rage.

Finding a single house standing by the roadside and approaching to seek lodging for a night, behold, how is this: the pillars are rotting, the ridgepole is warped, evil beasts and vicious insects come and go, and how detestable it is as they gather targeting around his own person; if Themes runs away with all his might knowing this will not do, dreadful yokai henge appear on the left and right of the road, and while manifesting monstrous figures and strange bodies, beasts' flesh and men's flesh struggle, grudge, and eat one another; struck dumb with astonishment at that sight, though he tries to run away, his feet are heavy, and he is struggling, shedding sweat, in the very same place.

To such a place from the empty sky, thunder roars and a strange voice strikes his ears, and as he looks, terrible demons and rakshasa appear, and the dreadfulness of countless night-demons and evil fiends following and attacking; blown by the winds of extreme heat or the storms of extreme cold, his body scattered away into the empty sky to who knows where, and the strangeness of lingering upon a mountain far in the distance.

Suddenly he hears abisokyōkan, and when he looks well, thick fires are burning up in every valley at the foot of the mountain, and the dreadfulness of spirits of strange appearance and black snakes suffering and writhing in the fire, to the degree that makes one's flesh creep; once again the rakshasa appear, beginning with his wife Belisina, and cruelly maltreating both Sumiel and Sugal with all their might, tearing their bodies apart, squeezing out their blood, and crushing and eating them, and Themes, with eyes unable to be fixed upon it, while drowning in tears of repentance,

putting his palms together to pray for the salvation of the Great Kami, even the thing he had thought of became a bubble in water, the Kototama wheel stopped and not even a single word could he roll forth, and there was also a time when he was at his wits' end; a single great light scorching the heavens and coming down suddenly became a Kami-person of matchless countenance and peerless beauty; when he looked well, Mankō, who serves the Kami of Ananahi, wearing five-colored garments, turned toward old man Themes, preached and admonished various miscellaneous tales of cause and effect in the spiritual world, and while binding the bond of parent and child, carrying him upon his back, and dividing without difficulty the fireflame that was burning and spreading thickly,

the valorousness of rushing out straight towards the east into a great moor where green grasses flourish; while Mankōwake advances carrying Themes upon his back, he hits squarely in front of a great swamp with no limit, and resting his breath, putting his palms together, at the time when he prays to the Imperial Great Kami, the azure lake and marsh suddenly become a most fragrant blue tatami mat, and how wondrous it is that it has changed to the inner room of the Themes mansion.

Themes comes to his senses with a start, and when he looks around the four sides, starting with Harukuniwake of Ananahi, Matsuhiko, Tatsuhiko, Mankō, the military commanders of Oniharuwake and Kumehiko, even the children of the house, his wife Belisina, Sumiel, Sugal, Archis, and Adzmos, are holding their hands together at his pillow and praying for the help of the Kami of Ananahi; rather than seeing it as the very midst, Themes even of himself bends his ego, abandons his attachment, and decides to leave everything to the teachings of Ananahi; ah, how awe-inspiring are the Kami, may your august spirits multiply and prosper.

Temes: 'Ah, Berisina, you were here! Were Sumiel and Sugaru safe as well? Ah, truly wonderful, truly wonderful! I thought by now you had been torn apart and slain by the wicked demons and rakshasas, so this is indeed a marvel. Surely this is no dream.'

Berisina: 'Oyajidono, get a grip on yourself. You were just now blinded and nearly dying. Thanks to the devoted efforts of Harukuniwake-sama, Oniharuwake-sama, Mankō-sama, and all the rest, your sins have been forgiven, and you have been able to see the light of this world once again. Quickly, offer your thanks to Kamisama and everyone else.'

Temes: 'Ah, this is truly wondrous, everyone; how wonderfully you have saved me. Because of the karmic retributions stemming from my ancestors generation after generation, I fell into a hell of a kind that defies all description. There is nothing so terrifying as sin. Had you not come forth, not a single soul—from the ancestors of the Temes family down to our descendants—would have been spared from being cast into the Eightfold Great Hells to suffer boundless torments. Ever since I heard that my daughters Sumiel and Sugaru were imprisoned on Mount Inokura, cast into deep pits, and tormented, I grew resentful of Oniharuwake-sama, Kumihiko-sama, and the rest, and though on the surface I put on an indifferent face, deep within my heart my spirit raged like a demon, and I obstinately clung to my selfish desire to drive them from my house at the earliest moment. I have now come to deeply realize that the captivity of my daughters was entirely a divine arrangement to save our ancestors and descendants. Oniharuwake-sama, Kumihiko-sama, and all you other honored personages, please forgive my sins. I went to the spiritual world and have been rescued by your pure and noble spirits. And Mankō-sama as well... What a fickle, unsettled, and troublesome man he is; no matter how deeply my daughter Sugaru pines for him, I cannot possibly make such a man the adopted heir of the Temes family, yet while my daughter's infatuation has not cooled, there is nothing I can do. Should I perhaps even resort to poisoning him... Such wicked thoughts were unknowingly harbored in my heart. Mankō-sama, please forgive this sin of mine as well. It was your spirit that saved me when I was at an absolute impasse and in the depths of agony within the spiritual world. Come now, from this moment on, I entrust this household to Mankō-sama and the head clerk Shīna-sama, so please do as you see fit and aid the villagers. Temes no longer harbors even the slightest attachment to material wealth.'

Oniharuwake shed tears and spoke,

Oniharuwake: 'Temes-sama, how deeply you have come to understand. Through the guidance of Harukuniwake-sama, I too escaped hell while yet alive and was saved in heaven; it is precisely because of our karmic bond that I was thus permitted to stay here, if only for a single night. Furthermore, we must also bear the blame for having distressed your two maidens. As parents of daughters, it is entirely natural that you should feel resentment toward us. Even the prayers you offered, brandishing a broom and wishing for us to leave quickly, echoed faintly to my ears. Therefore, I deliberately chanted the sacred scriptures so that they would reach your ears, seeking to prompt your reflection, but struck by the power of the sutras, you lost consciousness. ...Ah, what a terrible thing I have done... As I was praying earnestly to the spiritual world, behold, you have been revived. Please, let this completely wash away any remaining bitterness.'

Temes: 'Oh, how unworthy I am! To hear such words would bring down divine retribution upon me. Thanks to you, the path has been opened for our entire clan to escape suffering the torments of the Avīci hell. Truly, there is nothing so terrifying as human grudges. I beg you, from this day forward, look kindly upon our inadequate clan and guide us.'

Floating warm tears in his eyes, he clasps his hands in prayer. Oniharuwake, blinded by tears of joy, cannot even speak and is sobbing convulsively. Both Emishi and Spār are grateful for the preciousness of divine grace, their voices choked and lost in tears.

Harukuni: 'Lord Thames, through this journey to the spirit realms, you must have come to understand the principle of karmic reincarnation.'

Thames: 'Yes, thanks to this, I have realized the fearful nature of the afterlife. I have deeply repented, realizing that human beings must follow the divine path of Kamunagara, transcend worldly love and natural love, live in the love of Kami, and accumulate virtuous deeds. Furthermore, I have deeply realized that Mankō-sama, whom I thought to be your disciple, is an honorable person who, through deep causation, has become the heir of the Thames family and will wipe away the sins and evils from our ancestors. By all means, although he is a companion of the splendid missionary, won't you bestow him upon the Thames family as an adopted son? I earnestly beg you to grant this.'

Harukuni: 'Provided Mankō-sama has no objections, I have not the slightest objection. Michiharuwake, Matsuhiko, Tatsuhiko, what are your opinions?'

Michiharuwake: 'The way of Kami clears the darkness and rises apart, / It must be the sacred will of the rising sun's Kami.'

Matsuhiko: 'To build a minor heavenly kingdom in the age of pines, / Kami must have sent down Mankō.'

Tatsuhiko: 'It must be the foundation opened by the teachings / Of the prince who cuts off a hundred sufferings of sin and defilement.'

Harukoniwake: 'Grateful I am for my three precious disciples, / To consent to this must also be the divine will. / Come now, Thames couple and Princess Spāru, / Make Mankō the pillar of your house.'

Thames: 'Grateful are we for the words of Harukuniwake, / Reverently shall we serve Kami.'

Berishina: 'Even the sin-laden house of Thames is purified / By the sacred breath of the three-five Kami.'

Spāru: 'From the very bottom of my liver's heart, I loved him, / How joyful it is to be wedded to Mankokuiwake.'

Shīna: 'Reverencing the sacred words of the Great Kami, / The flower of sincerity will surely bloom in the house of Thames.'

Sumieru: 'Since this is a bond tied by Kamunagara, / I too shall serve you, Lord Shīna.'

Āshisu: 'Venerable is the bond tied / Through the mouth of Harukuniwake, official of Kami.'

Omin: 'The three-five moon shines brightly in the sky, / And even my breast has cleared away completely.'

Oniharuwake: 'When I look at the shadow of the moon shining in the great sky, / The ridge of the Thames family has smiled in blessing.'

Kumehiko: 'The stern mitama and the auspicious mitama shine brightly / Upon the Tamaki mansion of Thames.'

Spāru: 'Tonight, with the moon clear and the great sky blue, / Indeed I feel as though the darkness has cleared away.'

Emishi: 'I too, who served as Colonel, commander of the Brahmin army, feel joyful in my heart.'

Harukuniwake: 'The glory of the house of Thames from today onward / Shall shine ever brighter like the sun and moon.'

Thames: 'Venerable is the Kami who has governed so purely / This sin-laden mansion of mine.'

Thus expressing their innermost feelings to one another, in a harmonious and genial atmosphere they bow low before the divine presence, each intoning norito, and Thames giving thanks for the divine grace of rebirth.

Outside the window, a cool summer breeze blows through like the hichiriki, passing gently by—ah, Kamunagara sakihaemase.
