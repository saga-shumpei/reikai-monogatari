Chapter 12: Spirit 婚 [1420]

霊婚〔1420〕

§ 1

Shihenantan surrounds them as the light of Jitsugetsuseishin is nowhere to be seen, and a piercing Kanpū blows as a blizzard from jiyōge and saiu. The cries of chimimauriyō echo unpleasantly from the tops of mountain ridges and the bottoms of rivers. With a stiffened body and a thin, withered frame, a single man walks down a rugged zuidau aimlessly, leaning on his staff. Seeing a faint glimmer of light, he finds a large house standing in the midst of a field; not knowing where he is at the end of such a lonely solitary journey, come what may, he decides to stop by and beg for a night's lodging, and when he goes inside by slipping through the gate, the pillars are worm-eaten, the walls are broken in places, even the tall hall's pillar-roots are crushed and rotting, the ridgepoles and beams are tilted and warped, the rafters and komai are fallen away, and an indescribable stench fills every corner. Beginning with bears, hawks, eagles, karasuhebi, pythons, vipers, mukade, gejigeji, osamushi, and badgers, hordes of nameless evil insects roam back and forth inside the building; the stench of urine and feces pierces his nose, and maggots and dung beetles gather at his feet with sickening dread. Just as Themesu, at his wits' end, thinks of leaving this house, packs of wild dogs numbering in the hundreds appear, surrounding Themesu from left and right, and starving and exhausted, they bark fiercely as if trying to bite and devour him. Themesu leaves the house at the risk of his life and calls for help, but how helpless it is; his voice falters and becomes so disordered that even he himself can hardly distinguish what he is saying. Driven by terror, however, as he flees stumbling and falling along the dim moorland path overgrown with daylilies, leaning on his staff, from ahead a yasha and a wicked ghost chase after two women. The women stumble and fall right before Themesu. Looking closely, they are his own children, Sumieru and Sugaru. The yasha and wicked ghost instantly catch up, and right before Themesu's eyes, they tear apart the limbs of his suffering daughters and devour them, with all its sickening dread. Seeing the peril of his own children right before his eyes, the agony of heart where he can bear neither body nor world, he desperately tries to clasp his hands in prayer to invoke the gods and at least save his own body, but how helpless it is as his body grows rigid and the utter misery of being unable to move freely takes hold. Thinking he cannot stay like this as he gazes at the heads of his daughters, who have been eaten away to eight or nine parts, telling himself this is his last parting glance in this life, he stumbles and falls, running ever northward, yet feeling as though someone is entangling his feet, and how sad it is that the more anxious he becomes, the less he is able to advance. From behind comes the deafening roar of yasha and wicked ghosts numbering in the countless millions.

Akki: 'Hey there, old man Themesu fleeing to that place, we shall capture you this very instant and offer you as our food.'

Startled by the voice shouting thus, he looks up into the sky and sees a six-faced, eight-armed yokai in midair, gripping the hair of his wife, Berishina, and dangling her in the air. Berishina lets out a piercing scream,

Berishina: 'Lord Themesu, save me.'

and the voice calling out permeates his liver and gall, leaving him with unbearable anguish and distress, his advance and retreat blocked here, and just as he stands hesitating and resigning himself to karma, the previous yokai appears before Themesu leading thousands of crooked ghosts, letting out a voice like thunder and speaking with sharp words,

Yokai: 'I am a Rakshasa serving Yogenbō, the great demon king of the wicked party. Your family has squeezed the fat and blood of the people from generation to generation, and while accumulating vast wealth, you knew not how to save the starving and freezing people; your greed and vice piled up day by day and month by month with no end to your crimes, and here your ancestors have suffered divine punishment, becoming such yasha and wicked ghosts, eating human waste, feeding on evil beasts and insects, and being tormented without end day after day by the agony of extreme heat and cold as you live out a tragic existence. Yet you, this time, through the help of Hinodewake, the propagation envoy of Misokyo protected by Okuninushi no Ōkami, the central pillar of Miroku's divine administration, had the peril of your most beloved daughter saved, and for a time you rejoiced and revered him as the parent of your life, even vowing to become a believer of Misokyo; are you not already harboring a secret aversion to Misokyo in your stubborn and foolish mind? Moreover, toward the true person who was saved and pardoned by the officials including Oniharuwake, the propagation general of Brahmanism, the crime of expressing infinite contempt in word and deed and troubling the spirits of the people is one that even ten thousand deaths cannot redeem. If you do not change your mind while there is still time, you shall be plunged headlong into the extreme cold hell and given infinite, eternal suffering. Come quickly!'

Taking him by the hand, he dragged him forcibly north and ever north.

The wretchedness of Temes being pursued from behind, with loathsome voices raised all at once by the many fiends and wicked demons said to be his ancestors, as they came rushing on. Amid this tragic state of the Jigokudau where flesh and blood devour one another, Temes lost even his human consciousness; weeping and crying as the Maō willed, barefoot upon the stony path of the endless withered moor, with his feet torn and staining the road with blood, he was dragged along in utter bewilderment.

Before he knew when it was, Temes had arrived at the foot of a gloomy and precipitous mountain. The shapes of the former wicked demons and Rasetsu vanished like smoke, and sorrowful cries were heard intermittently upon the mountains on all four sides as the wind willed. Just as he thought a fire-like wind was blowing to scorch his body, a freezing-cold wind suddenly blew back, and a rain of icicles and a rain of fire alternately converged and fell down upon his head. When he finally opened his eyes and looked around at the surroundings, tigers, wolves, bears, lions, and the like, looking as though famined for food, without limit to the hundreds, were glaringly poised to devour the flesh of a single Temes. Suddenly came the screech of a woman's scream; looking closely, he saw his wife Berisina having her legs bitten from both sides by a pack of lions and tigers, torn apart like green bamboo right before his eyes, and the beast pack quickly gathered together, making a crunching sound, and devoured the whole lot while snarling at one another.

Temes, driven to extremity, entrusted his fate to Heaven and closed his eyes in resignation. With the heat and cold, he had almost no human consciousness left. Suddenly thunder rumbled and lightning flashed across, Temes's body was swept up into the sky, and gently, softly looking down upon mountains and rivers knowing not how many hundreds of ri below, he fell upon the summit of a mountain where flames billowed up. Black smoke gave off an unusual stench and in an instant enveloped his body.

From somewhere or other, a powerful voice shouted out,

Open your eyes!

When he opened his eyes with fear and trembling, the specter Rasetsu that had descended from the sky earlier grasped the two legs of two women in its left and right hands right before him, holding them upside down, and pounded them like a mortar upon the rugged rocks, making a tapping, tapping sound as if striking with a staff. The two young daughters raised piercing shrieks and cried out bitterly in agony. Though Temes tried to utter a single word, his breath was blocked, his tongue became twisted, and not even a sound of 'ugh' could escape. The Rasetsu opened its giant eyes, raised its voice harshly, and spoke,

Rasetsu: 'Thou, by reason of thy karma from past lives, shalt suck the blood of thy present beloved children, eat their flesh, and grind their bones to dust to devour them. If thou dost not, thou too shalt be dealt with likewise.'

No sooner had it spoken than it smashed the heads of the two women against the rock with all its might, shattering them to a pulp.

Temes, having no other choice, nodded in assent. The Rasetsu scooped up fragments of the princess's head-flesh on the tip of a bamboo whisk and shoved them into Temes's mouth. Temes, having no alternative, could not help but eat them. His mouth grew numb, tasting pungent and bitter, and he felt a suffering as if swallowing poison. The Rasetsu opened its large mouth and burst into loud laughter,

Rasetsu: 'Ahahaha, thou hast now tasted the flesh of thy daughter and come to know its flavor. Thy ancestor, acting as the village headman of Tamaki, tormented the people and squeezed out the lifeblood of numerous paupers; and by thy generation it became ever more severe, piling up boundless wealth and building a towering residence in the village of Tamaki, fearing not Heaven and Earth, and exhausting the utmost limits of arrogance—an unrepentant wretch! We are the resentful spirits of the people united, appearing here as Rasetsu. Come, from this time forth we shall pound both thy spirit and thy flesh with stones, make of thee hundreds of lumps of flesh, and distribute them to the spirits tormented by thee during thy lifetime. Come quickly, lie down upon this rock here!'

So reviling him, it thrust him down to the spot.

To such a place, a spiritual light shone brilliantly from one quarter of Heaven and descended right before Temes. The Rasetsu, startled by this fireball, concealed its form none knew where. The fireball instantly transformed into a single divine person. Looking closely, General Kishunwakewake was clad in a sacred raiment endowed with perfection, standing with a gentle smile. Temes was struck with astonishment and for the first time opened his mouth to speak,

To such a place, riding upon a cloud, descended vigorously a single kamibito. When he looked closely, it was Mankō who had stayed at his own house.

Mankō stood before him with a gentle smile, lightly bowing while doing so,

万公『舅殿、此谷底を御覧なさいませ。沢山な妖怪や毒蛇が焼き亡ぼされてゐるでせう。これは皆テームス家の祖先が作つた罪業の化生した悪魔で厶いますよ。又此万公は貴方の祖先代々に苦しめられた憐れな人民の霊が凝結して万公となり、此世に生れ来たものです。私はそれ故どうしてもテームス家の後を継いで此テームス家の財産を人民に平等に分配し罪を亡ぼさねば、舅殿を始め祖先の罪は赦されますまい。お気がつきましたかな。万民の精霊が集まつて万公と名を負ひ現界に生れたのですよ』

テームス『いや、どうも有難う厶ります。因果応報の道理によつて先祖代々地獄の苦みを受けるのも已むを得ませぬが、三五教を奉じ玉ふ貴方が吾家の養子となり、祖先の罪を赦して下さるのなら、此位有難い事は厶いませぬ。併し乍ら、スミエル、スガールの両人は悪鬼羅刹の為に肉体を粉砕され、もはや現界には居りませぬ。どうして家を継ぐ事が出来ませうか。娘がなくても養子になつて下さるでせうか』

万公『御心配なさいますな。治国別の宣伝使がお守りあればスミエル、スガール両人は極めて安全に肉体を保つてゐられます。さアこんな処に何時迄居つても堪りませぬ。私と一緒に帰りませう』

テームス『伴れて帰つて下さるか。あ、それは有難い。然し罪多い吾々、どうして此火焔の山を下る事が出来ませう』

万公『いや宜しい。貴方も大変に足も疲れて居りまする。私が背に負うて帰りませう。僅か三千里ばかり走れば玉置村の宅へ帰れますから』

テームス『何、三千里、大変に遠い所迄何時の間に来たのだらうな』

万公『精霊の世界では三千里や五千里は現界の一丁を歩する暇もかかりませぬ。さア早く背をお抱へ下さい』

§ 4

And when he held out his hands, Thames became like a little child,

Thames: 'Ah, as the saying goes, in old age follow your child. Well then, Mukodono, I leave everything in your good hands.'

Mankō: 'There is no need for a parent to thank a child or make requests.'

So saying, he briskly carried him upon his back, and plunging straight through the fierce flames without suffering a single burn, descended like an arrow in flight.

As they descended the mountain borne upon Mankō's back, there lay a boundless plain overgrown with green grass. When they dashed out right into the middle of the genya with the momentum of an itsushiyasenri, they came upon a marsh filled with suishiyō waters. Even the stalwart Mankō was taken aback by this, rested his breath, and wishing to concentrate his shian, gently set Thames down upon the aoshiba, joined his morote, and

Mankō: 'The plum blossoms of the sanzensekai Open all at once, becoming The eternal age of the pine And the kami of the pine; To save the sankai Of keniushin, Appearing as the savior kami Of ananahi, Kuniharutachi no Ōmikami, Toyokunihime no Ōmikami, Serving the august mitama Of Kami Susanoo no Ōkami, I am Hinodewake's missionary of propagation, Matsuhiko and Tatsuhiko, divine ministers, Have pity on the sincere heart Of Mankōwake, And appearing here this very moment, Grant that we cross this marsh safely and successfully. Though the morning sun shine or grow cloudy, Though the moon wax or wane, Even should the great earth sink, The grace of the kami shall forever and ever Remain unchanging. Protect us, Kannagara. Thames of Tamaki no Sato, And Mankōwake who has become his heir, With a sincere heart before the august presence The kami, humbly and reverently we pray. Kaminaohi who created this world, And Ōnaohi of vast and broad heart, In human life, whatever may happen, Look upon all things straight, listen anew, And proclaim and rectify the errors of the world; Such is the deep grace of the divine decree. Looking up with reverence to the sky of this day, Save us, Kannagara. Before the presence of the precious kami, A parent and child together, with reverence, Pray for salvation. Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara, Grant us the blessing of the august mitama.'

Scarcely had he finished chanting thus when the boundless marsh suddenly transformed into blue tatami mats. When Thames opened his eyes and looked closely, he found himself lying dizzily fallen in the adjacent room where Oniharuwake had been chanting sutras. Kuniharutachi, Oniharuwake, Matsuhiko, Tatsuhiko, and all the others had gathered at the pillowside to tend to him with utmost solicitude, and were earnestly chanting the heavenly number-songs. Ah, Kannagara tamashii sakihamaucase.
