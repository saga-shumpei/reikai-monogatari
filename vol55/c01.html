心転〔1409〕

§ 1

High is Hisakata as we look up, / Appearing in Takaamahara, / The Creator of Tenchi, / Ōkunitokotachiōmikami, / Distributing the pristine Mitama, / As the Moon Kami in Reigoku, / And as the Sun Kami in Tengoku, / Appearing thus to bestow His Mikokoro / To aid all the myriad spirits of Tenchi / Into the peerless and majestic Tenkai, / Expanding the Sango teaching throughout Tenchi, / Selecting a hundred Kamigami, / Making them the Sendenshi and Daiheushin / Of the Sanzensekai, / Unifying a hundred administrators, / At Ōgonzan and Yotsuoyama, / The Caucasus mountains and the sacred grove / Of Ubusuna's mansion, erecting Kamubashira, / Standing firm as Kakiha and Tokiha, / Guiding humanity ever onward, / How awe-inspiring to build / The Tengoku Jiyōdo upon the earth! / The plum blossoms of the Sanzensekai / Opening all at once upon the evergreen trees, / The pine's green growing straight and tall / Toward the heavens, stretching ever upward. / The Izunomitama and Mizunomitama, / The Sango moon in the great firmament / Displaying its round form, / At midnight peering down upon the lower world, / Leaping across the ravine of Mount Ikura to the right / And crossing over to the left, / The crooked army of Brahmanism, / Three thousand horsemen encamped, / Ascending toward the rock cave. / Ah, Kannagara Kamigami, / Receiving the power of Kami in our bodies, / All the foul clouds and mist,

The party of Kunikuniwake, / Walking through the pine forest dark even by day, / Matsuhiko of pure heart, / Leaving the sacred site behind together with Tatsuhiko's / Administrator, striding onward step by step, / Tracing the night road as they climb. / The myriad administrator, expanding his shoulders and arms / While taking the lead, / Like the wind crossing over the mountain ridges, / Like the swift current of a valley stream, / The Sendenka learned by rote, / Overawing the echoes of the surrounding mountains, / Pressing forward with fierce vigor as they climb. / Onisenwake and Kumehiko, / Captured by the lord of the army, / The pitfall inside the rock cave, / Placed in a situation heartbreaking even to hear, / To rescue the four physical bodies at this very moment, / Trampling and pushing aside the roots of rocks and trees,

At last reaching the rock cave, / Together with Kunikuniwake, / Guarded by the power of Kami, / Rebuke the lord of the army, / Rescuing the four and striding downward, / Heading toward the mansion of Themes in the village of Tamaki, / As they make their return journey. / The story of the fifty-fifth volume, / The Kami of the heavenly sun shining in mid-air / Though the wind is cold, / Lying down within the Ryūgū mansion, / At the hour of the horse on the eleventh day, / Placing the square hibachi beside me, / Listening to the boiling sound of the kettle on the stove / As something exceedingly delightful, / Slipping inside the square kotatsu, / Piling up square zabuton / To serve as a pillow as I unfold / The emission of my Kototama,

Grasping the fountain pen in my hand, / Leaning against the square desk, / Determined not to let slip even a single word, / Pulling in his sleeves and stretching his legs, Mr. Matsumura, / The sky of his true, clear heart serene, / Writes on steadily and straight. / The story of the Sango Kami, / Is at last anchored down here. / Ah, Kannagara Kamigami, / Grant us the divine grace of the Mitama! / Flowers bloom and birds sing of the reign of our sovereign, / Spring passes with its songs of prosperity, / Followed by the early summer breeze with rustling green leaves, / The river currents foaming, / Sweetfish ascending the Lion River, / Even small crucian carp, eels, and catfishes, / Leaping about with vigorous splashes, / Swimming upstream a thousandri in a single rush. / The story of the pristine Mitama, / From now thirty-five hundred thousand years,

To tell of the ascetic practices and self-sacrifice / Of the Kami-humans of the Sango teaching, / Today is truly a joyful time. / Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, / Whether the moon waxes or wanes, / Even should the great earth sink into the abyss, / The congratulations of my Kototama / Shall never perish for tens of thousands of ages to come, / Standing firm as Kakiha and Tokiha. / The sacred Kototama of the living Kami who saves this world, / Flowing on unstoppably / Through a thousand and thousands of generations. / A single drop of that headwaters, / Upon the tip of the fountain pen, / The dew-like blessing that drips down, / A story that moistens and revitalizes / The souls parched to the very core. / Protect us, Kannagara, / O mighty Ooyashimahiko of supreme majesty, / Before the august presence of the Kami's decree,

With awe and reverence we humbly pray.

Harukuniwake turned to the two generals, Oniharuwake and Kumehiko,

Harukuniwake: 'I have long heard of the General's might and illustrious name, but today is the very first time I have the honor of meeting you in person. First of all, I am most delighted to see that both of you are in good health. That being the case, we are all brothers within the four seas; naturally, between myself and the General, there is neither any bitter grudge nor any troublesome background. As children of God born under the very same heaven and earth, I sincerely hope that from now on we may cultivate a close and amicable relationship with one another. To mobilize an army when there is no enemy, or for the sake of petty desires to become the servants of a single tyrant and engage in warfare resembling that of ravenous wolves, there can surely be no greater tragedy than this for human beings. Life lasts a mere three hundred years; during this short span of existence, if we do not make flawless preparations for our life in the second spiritual world, which is immutable and eternal, we will never be able to permanently uphold the true duty of having been born into this present world as human beings. Since human beings are divinely endowed by Kami with the sacred nature of turning from evil and reforming their ways in accordance with the divine will, I sincerely hope that you will now enter into a life of repentance at this very moment and fully manifest your true calling as the children of God.'

Oniharuwake: 'Yes, thank you very much. Coming to this day, we too have for the first time attained the feeling of the opening and enlightenment of heaven and earth. Illuminated by the precious and wondrous compassionate light of Kami, the tyrannical and lawless actions we have hitherto committed have suddenly filled us with dread, and we are writhing in agony with nowhere to shelter our bodies in the vast universe. We desire to repent as quickly as possible and return to the path of truth. We humbly ask for your thorough guidance in all matters.'

Kumehiko: 'Beginning with the divine minister Harukuniwake, I respectfully and earnestly implore all of you, just as General Oniharuwake has done, to guide my very self as well. Truly, my heart at this moment feels as though it has departed from darkness and turned toward the morning sun. And struck by the divine power of Kami, I have become so ashamed and distressed that I have nowhere to shelter my body. I humbly beseech you to instruct me in the precious teachings of the three and the five.'

Mankō: 'Hey, Teacher, you had better listen to this while putting spit on your eyebrows, for this might just be another Takahime... Repentance born of fear and anguish is worth nothing at all. Unless it is a repentance rooted deeply from the heart, you will immediately slip right back, so no matter how sweet things they say, you must not carelessly jump on them... Thus it appears in the divine revelations. Mankō offers a brief word of caution.'

Ryūkō: 'Hey Mankō, this is not something for you to understand. Keep quiet and stay back.'

Mankō: 'Hmph, do not talk so importantly. Thinking you explored the First Paradise in a dream, you should not act so arrogant.'

Harukuniwake: 'General, if that is the case, since four men and women are slightly injured from this point on, we must at any rate send them to the Thames Mansion in Tamaki Village; you too shall come along.'

Oniharuwake: 'Yes, I shall most certainly accompany you. As for having injured those four people, it is entirely our doing, so let us carry them on our backs across this mountain slope and send them off.'

Harukuniwake: 'Even if you do not go to such lengths, you surely have plenty of retainers.'

Oniharuwake: 'No, no, no matter how many retainers I have, it is not something for my retainers to know. Furthermore, now that I have made my repentance this very day, I shall not keep even a single retainer. As an atonement for my sins, please let me become the horse of Michiharewake-sama, carry them on my back, and escort them thither. This is at least my own atonement, so I beg you to grant me this favor against your better judgment.'

Harukuniwake: 'Then we shall leave it to your wishes.'

Kumehiko: 'I shall carry Shiina-sama on my back and accompany you.'

Mankō: 'I shall carry Sugaru-sama on my back and escort them.'

Kumehiko: 'My, absolutely not, let the Emishi carry Sugaru-sama.'

Oniharuwake summoned the colonels of Siam and Malta, and announced that he himself had thoroughly repented of his past faults, had converted to Ananai-kyō, and would become an ordinary believer to render considerable service for the sake of God and the world, and to hide himself in the world; he had them convey this fact to the army in general, and furthermore communicated that all should return to their homeland, follow the right path, and diligently pursue their respective family businesses. The three thousand troops were dumbfounded by the unexpected orders; some returned home with gladness, while others muttered complaints and did not return straight to their homeland at will, but pondered various undertakings and determined their paths in life, thus parting ways toward various and sundry directions. However, being by nature a mere rabble, not a single brave man emerged to gather the remnants, raise another battle, and exert himself on behalf of the Brahman leader Daikokunushi, which was a blessing for the realm.

Harukuniwake first of all emerged from the cave, and seeing the numerous soldiers, each receiving the command to disband, returning obediently without uttering a single word of complaint or offering resistance, he gave thanks entirely to the divine power of the Great Deity and the profound protection of the Great Deity who sat serenely upon the earth and guarded Ananai-kyō, beginning with the Brahman deities and Pangu Shenwang, and finally descended Mount Ikurayama with the party of twelve people.
