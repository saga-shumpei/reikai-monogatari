Chapter 3: 万民 [1411]

万民〔1411〕

§ 1

Feared by the people of the world as the Brahman general, Oniharuwake at last saw the flower of repentance open in his heart; regretting his past wrongs and realizing the precious grace of the Ōkami, the divine virtue of the Spirit of Mizu is like the Kumehiko whose well-springs are never exhausted, together with Supāru and Emishi, even the sky of the heart is surrendered to the divine edicts of the Kami Harukuniwake, and like the unwavering heart of Matsuhiko of the evergreen tree, or Tatsuhiko together with the administrator of the ugly cave, Kamutsukasa Michiharuwake briskly returning along the mountain path, bearing Shīna upon his back as an atonement for sins and defilements, crossing the valley where the stream flows like Sumieru, offering sincerity to the Ōkami, Princess Sugāru sent off by Mankōtsukasa like a sheep to the slaughter, downcast and with weary footsteps, at last arrived here in Tamakimura before the gates of Thames Mansion in the evening dusk of the moonlit sky, tilting his head, shedding sweat, panting for breath, and finally reaching home.

Mankōtsukasa stood before the front, raising his loud voice and crying: 'O Thames of the village of Tamaki, and ye servants who serve him, welcome the propaganda general of the triumphant return at the earliest moment and bathe in tears of joy; for indeed we are the propaganda envoys of the teachings of the Kami of Three-Five, the chief disciples of Harukuniwake, yea, the command of Mankōwake! We captured alive the general of the Brahman army barricaded in Mount Ikura and the high commander who wielded such great power, as easily as swatting a butterfly with a branch broom, and achieved a most happy triumphant return. The soul of Kumehiko, whose well-springs are never exhausted, with its many evil deeds, washed in the valley stream by the grace of the precious Kami, has now become a person of sincerity, a truly living spirit of its original state; the colonels of Supāru and Emishi, accompanying Mankōwake and sending off the two princesses, have come most admirably to offer an apology here, along with the propaganda envoys of Harukuniwake, Matsuhiko and Tatsuhiko together.'

'Though thou art indeed in the precious administration, if Mankōwake were not here, no matter how we tried or what we did, this battle could not have gone so well; rejoice, O Thames, grandmother of Princess Belishina, open this gate quickly! It is discourteous to keep the lord of our master standing before the gate where mosquitoes bite him for so long, and Mankōwake is also somewhat troubled. Open, open the front gate! The door of the rock cave where even the darkness of the everlasting night was swept away in an instant, prepare song, dance, and music, and quickly welcome the heroes of our company! Ah, Kannagara kamukamu, acting in place of the Kami, Mankōwake takes care of the master of the mansion.'

So singing, he struck the doors of the gate hard enough to split them. The gatekeepers Otsu and Hei, hearing the song of Manko, made their hearts pound, wondering anxiously whether the evil Kami of the Brahman religion had come once again to do such things, force open the gate, and rescue the two persons who had been tied up and kept in the storehouse, and rushed into the inner room to report to Thames that many people had gathered outside the gate. Thames thought that in such a perilous world one must not be careless, and making his preparations with a spear tucked under his arm, wishing at any rate to observe the situation, quietly appeared at the doorway; though separated by the doors of the gate so that the figures of the party could not be seen, he felt somehow as though his daughter had returned, and wondered whether Lord Harukuniwake had saved his daughter and brought her back, or whether she had been captured by the enemy and made to suffer a tragic fate, and whether the enemy, riding on their momentum, had not come pressing on to destroy his mansion; thus hesitating back and forth in deep thought, he stood lingering and pondering for a while.

Shīna, shaking his wound-covered head and speaking in a painful voice:

Shīna: 'Master, it is Shīna. By the help of Lord Harukuniwake, I have safely returned together with the young lady. Pray, open this gate.'

Thus he shouted out, but separated by the thick gate panels, his voice could not be heard within. Manko, growing impatient and in a loud voice:

Manko: 'I am indeed the peerless hero and champion serving as the right arm of Harukuniwake, the propaganda envoy of the Three-Five Teaching, Mankōwake Mikoto. O Lord Thames of the mansion, open the front gate at the earliest moment. The words I speak are without any error.'

と歌ひ乍ら門の戸が割れる程殴つて居る。門番の乙丙は万公の歌を聞いて胸を轟かせ、バラモン教の悪神が、又もや、あんな事を云つて門を開かせ倉につないで置いた両人を奪還しに来たのではあるまいかと案じ案じ奥の間に駆け込んで、テームスの前に……数多の人々が門外へ押寄せ来たれり……、と報告した。テームスは……物騒な世の中油断はならぬ……と身仕度をなし槍を小脇に抱へ乍ら、兎も角様子を窺はむと密かに門口に立現はれ、門の戸に隔てられて一行の姿は見えねども……何だか娘の帰つた様な気配がする、治国別様が娘を助けて帰つて下さつたのではあるまいか。但しは敵に捕らはれ玉ひ悲惨な憂目に会はせ玉うたのではなからうか。敵は勢に乗じて吾館を打滅さむと押寄せ来りしには非ずや……と、とつおいつ思案に暮れて暫し佇み考へてゐる。

シーナは傷だらけの頭をふり乍ら、苦しさうな声で、

シーナ『旦那様、シーナで厶ります。治国別様のお助けによつてお嬢さまと共に無事に皈りました。何卒此門開けて下さいませ』

と呶鳴つて見たが、どうしても厚い門扉に隔てられて中へは聞えなかつた。万公はもどかしがり大声にて、

万公『吾こそは、三五教の宣伝使治国別の片腕と仕へまつる、天下無双の英雄豪傑、万公別命で厶る。館の主テームス殿、一時も早く表門を開かれよ。某の申す言葉に間違は厶らぬ』

§ 2

He shouted out loudly.

Hearing Mankō's voice, Temesu felt relieved, and hastily opening the gate, although with mixed feelings, he looked closely; since it was a moonlit night, things were not entirely clear, but it seemed somehow that Shīna and the two other daughters were being carried back upon backs, whereupon, before he knew it, he ran out beyond the gate.

From off a back, Shīna spoke:

Shīna: 'Oh, Dannasama, thanks to you we have been saved. Please set your mind at ease.'

At these words, while filled with joyful astonishment, Temesu said:

Temesu: 'Oh everyone, you have gone through such hardships. Now please come right in here to the inner rooms. Well now, Harukuniwake-sama, thank you so much for carrying my daughter back all the way from that steep slope. You must be exceedingly tired.'

With tears of joy, he expressed his gratitude.

Harukuniwake led the way for everyone and entered within the gate.

The twelve people followed behind Temesu, guided by a servant with a light, and proceeded into a wide inner room.

Temesu and Berishina, husband and wife, in their overwhelming joy, ordered the maidservant and manservant, sweeping the tatami, bringing out cushions, arranging the tobacco tray, and rushing about in a flurry.

Mankō: 'Temesu-dono, please do not stand on ceremony for us at all. Now make yourself comfortable. We will tidy up around here on our own and take our rest. Since things have come to this, we are just like parents and children now...'

Already adapting to the mood of having become an adopted son, he spoke with easy familiarity.

Temesu: 'Yes, you are indeed our savior. I do not know how to express my thanks.'

Mankō: 'No, father-in-law, since the young ones are in attendance here and you are an elder, please rest at ease. However, since Michiharewake, Shīna, and Sumiru-sama, beginning with Sugāru, have been plunged into a deep pit and have hurt their bodies quite a bit, I want us to make up some beds for them somehow. We must make preparations for bedding and mosquito nets, but since I am a complete newcomer who just arrived here a moment ago, I do not know the ways of the house, so please teach me for a moment.'

Temesu: 'Yes, how unworthy I am. Could I ever let you do such a thing? Please make yourself completely at ease.'

Mankō: 'Father-in-law, what are you saying? If the young ones do not work, who is going to work? As for the messengers Harukuniwake, Matsuhiko, and Tatsuhiko, I as their representative will offer them my fullest thanks. Hey Haru-chan, Kume-chan, Sū-chan, Ē-chan, quickly take down the sick... no, the injured people. You folks too have had a hard time. In times like this, someone with a big build is truly a useful thing.'

Guided by the maidservant, the four took the injured people to a single room, laid out the bedding, gently laid them upon it, asked the maidservant to tend to them, and returned to the original living room.

Harukuniwake, along with Matsuhiko and Tatsuhiko, was guided by the maidservant to a detached room, rested their breath for the moment, offered a prayer of gratitude to the Ōkami, and afterwards drank tea and rested.

Kiharuwake, Kumehiko, Supāru, Emishi, and Mankō were in an inner room, utterly exhausted, stretching out their legs and busily massaging themselves.

Since the four of them had come carrying heavy human beings all the more, their legs were tired and their bodies weakened, and they lay sprawling like ropes with their arms as pillows.

The husband and wife Temesu and Berishina, in their joy at their daughters' return, forgot even to offer words of thanks to Harukuniwake, and while wandering aimlessly about the room, rushed into the living room where the four injured people lay; Berishina set about tending to the two daughters, while Temesu sat by the pillow-sides of Michiharewake and Shīna, rubbing their bodies and comforting them.

All of them had been lowered down into deep holes, had injured their bodies somewhere or other, and because they could not move as they wished, now that they were finally relieved, their spirits relaxed and they lay utterly limp, uttering not a word as they breathed in pain upon the beds.

The parents devoted themselves with the utmost possible kindness and had no mind for anything else, almost forgetting about Harukuniwake.

Mankō, seeing that the old couple had taken the two daughters, Michiharewake, and Shīna away somewhere and did not even show their faces, seemed to be slightly provoked and irritated, and catching sight of the maidservant wandering about the place, shouted at her in a loud voice, 'Draw some tea, massage my legs, what are you dawdling for?', already putting on the airs of a young master and ordering everyone around.

Mankō: 'Though you were saved by the very daughter and bantō who are so kanjin, old oyaji Thames, you do not even offer a word of thanks. Are you so flustered by the return of your beloved daughter that you have forgotten us, your guests? Do you not know that Mankō is the shiyujinkō of Princess Sugāru, a union permitted by kami? You servants, quickly go call your shiyujin and come back; won't you show some aisau to our masterly teacher? Living in such a grand house, naze do you make fools of us, oretachi?'

Gejiyo: 'The master of Thames, upon beholding the forms of the two sisters, was struck with awe and utterly flustered. Pray wait just shiba longer, kamuzukasa; when water first bursts from its spring, there is no sensube to stem it.'

Mankō: 'Even so, you should still know girininjiyō. Would you give the cold shoulder to the kami of salvation?'

Gejiyo: 'I am a hashitame named Otami. I know nothing of such difficult matters. As a hashitame who arrived here only sanyotsukamahe, how could I possibly understand the yōsu of this household? Without throwing such dada this konban, be docile and go off to sleep. How could a parent abandon the hime when she has suffered through senkiitsuki of agony? The master of Thames, his heart troubled, lies ailing for love of his beloved daughter. Though you possess a frame so kutsukiyō, why do you not bestir yourself and work with a bit more spirit? Even for me, with a household so ihenaka and vast, there is scarcely any managing every corner. Calmly settle down here tonight, and make your racket once day has dawned.'

Mankō: 'Why, you—though merely a woman, do you dare mock a brave fellow like a masurao? Are you making a fool of me, you foolish wretch?'

Otami: 'How comical it is that you, a young master, put on such airs the moment you arrive at this house. Even if people say she is a good-natured ojousama, there is no reason she would ever take a fancy to you. Naze, you ask? Because your appearance is that of an ahōzura, with even the strings of your face coming undone!'

Mankō: 'You babbling wretch of a hashitame, shall I dislocate your jaw with the power of kami?'

Thinking it pointless to continue matching wits with such a gejiyo, Mankō resolved that he must somehow drag out the master and make him pay his respects to his masterly teacher; thus, having Otami lead the way, he proceeded into the living room where the four lay sleeping. Looking in, he saw the husband and wife diligently tending to the four wounded persons in turn.

Mankō: 'Ah, master, I will take over the nursing. Pray come right away and offer your words of gratitude to the party of our masterly teacher. They are waiting over yonder.'

Thames: 'Yes, thank you very much indeed. Overwhelmed by joy and distracted by tending to my daughter, I completely forgot to offer even a single word of thanks to our very saviors.'

Berishina: 'It was truly inexcusable of us. In that case, Oyaji-dono, you—though it is terrible of me to ask—go and give our immediate thanks to the party of Chikuniwake-sama. I shall remain here to continue the nursing.'

Nodding in assent, Thames hurriedly crossed the corridor and made his way to the living room of Chikuniwake. Meanwhile, Mankō eagerly peered at Sugāru's profile while briskly assisting in the care of Michiharuwake and Shīna.
