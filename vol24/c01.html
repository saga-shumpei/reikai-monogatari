Chapter 1: 粉骨砕身 [731]

粉骨砕身〔731〕

§ 1

In the ancient Kamiyo of long ago, in the famously renowned capital of Ejiputokoku, appeared Onikumawake and his wife, a pair whom Onikumohiko, the supreme leader of Brahmanism, relied upon as his right arms. Following the fall of the Iho mansion, they concealed their tracks and fled to Mizuhokuni, where they re-established the shooting range of oshihe at Kenonjiyō in Mesopotamia. As the prosperity of the Brahmans surged like the rising sun of daybreak, oshihe shone brilliantly in the four quarters, adding splendor to the natural paradise of Ametsuchi—how truly joyous it was. Born between Onikumawake and Mukadehime was Koitohime, fifteen springs of age, their single grain of seed, their very first beloved daughter, raised with such tender care that even the winds through the gaps blew without purpose.

Spoiled beyond measure through excessive affection, a willful creature who made a mock of her father and mother's words, the young maiden engaged in wicked acts unbecoming her years. Yet her parents examined her with the straight eye, re-examined her with the listening ear, and proclaimed her anew, feeling that even if she were to fly directly into their eyes, it would not hurt at all. Though her parents were ruthless and lawless, drawn by the love for their own child, their eyes were blinded, their ears deafened, they had neither noses nor mouths; and as for the end of Koitohime's life—drowning in love and exhausting every sort of wildness—she was scorched by the flames of a first love she had known for the very first time. There, even if it were someone else, was a buck-toothed man, with round eyes and a crooked nose; and the red birthmark stamped upon the tip of his nose,

viewed through the eyes of the woman who adored him, appeared like a peony or cherry blossom. Gazing at his posture as he opened wide his large mouth and laughed, she thought him a man among men, and having fallen ill with this infatuation, she tied together the bond forbidden by her parents, concealing it from human eyes. Wet, soaked, and limp, she obsessed over Tomohiko in her sleeping quilt, considering him a being above all others, and hand in hand her parents slipped out of the mansion, crossing the Eden River, the barrier of human eyes, and the River of Tears. Flowing across and journeying to the land of Persia, uniting water and fire from far and near, she was dragged by the hand of a thirty-year-old man, her essential soul extracted by whispers sweeter than honey, and while entering a pot of smiles,

turning round and round, she crossed over to the land of India and the island of Ceylon. Binding together a small hut, they spoke thus: 'Our relationship, you and I, even if Ametsuchi were to overturn, even if the sun and moon were to rise from the west, for a thousand ages, eight thousand ages, eternally, and even unto the world of Miroku, shall never change. With bamboo pillars and a thatched roof, even if we carry a hand-pot, we do not regret it; we are a couple madly in love.' As they spent two or three months with expressions that monopolized the love of Ametsuchi in a single body, Koitohime gradually came to find the man's odor annoying, her passionate path of love fading day by day, blown by the cold autumn wind and transforming into a winter sky. Her jeweled skin, resembling even snow,

growing cold, no longer possessed any warmth; and given the slightest gap, she would leap out, surrender her body to her ideal husband, and be hailed as a flower of high society. Having shifted her mindset to make this her pride as a woman while prospering brilliantly, she took Tomohiko's annoyance as her good fortune, left behind a single letter of divorce, and let the future take care of itself—let the fields become fields, let the mountains become mountains. In a world where there was no drought of men, whatever might happen was like the Mama River; a floating boat cast away with a fickle heart, abandoning the spirit of the alphabet of love, she manipulated her oars and pushed out into the Indian Ocean upon the mercy of the waves. With the wild sea showing no mercy, her ship suddenly collided head-on with a reef, breaking apart with a creaking sound as souls flew forth into mid-heaven,

and among those who emerged was the missionary of Misogigyou, safely rescued by the divine ship of Isokohime, landing upon Ryugugu Island floating in the middle of the peaceful ocean. Applying her usual bold impudence to the rough-natured islanders, she was elevated like a rising eel as the queen of the island country, and submitting to the divine oshihe of Misogi, she transmitted the true path—this is the tale of Ouryuhime. Lazily resting her body upon the ship of bedding, while Mizuki illuminated the male waves and female waves of the peaceful ocean here, recounting the tale of ten thousand ri to the horizon, with her heart's color like the clear azure sky, peacefully lying down surrounded by the polar constellation,

surrounded and encircled by the stars of the Big Dipper, whetted with a leaden pen closely resembling the Seven Sword Stars, and meticulously set down with a myriad-year fountain pen handed down through a thousand ages, Matsumura-shi records it all, pulling back his calves so as not to omit a single word, cocking his ears, holding his breath, and noting it down just as the Gods will. Ah, Kannagara kamigami-gami, may your august spirits be blessed and prosper, and may this tale of twenty-four volumes be most swiftly completed, serving as a bookmark to save mankind into the great highway of the Gods. Thus we respectfully pray before the august presence of the Great Deities of Ametsuchi and the Four Quarters.

§ 2

The Seirontau floating within Wadanaka was formerly called the island of Shiro. A man called Kigisu, who served under Onikumawake of the Brahmin Kyō, being gifted in worldly talents, exhausted every resource of smooth-tongued flattery and successfully wormed his way into the good graces of Onikumawake and his wife. Having won their high favor, he was at last promoted to become a propagandist of the Brahmin Kyō, and his name was also changed to Tomohiko. It is common for small-minded men to lose their heads when puffed up with pride; before long, Tomohiko began to blow the shoots of wild ambition, and gradually revealing his true colors, he cast his eye upon Koitohime, the only daughter whom the couple loved and cherished as the jewel in their palm. Deep within Tomohiko's heart, his thoughts turned continually day and night, calculating that if even the couple's most beloved daughter fell into his hands, he could succeed to Onikumawake's position, become a splendid vice-leader of the Brahmin Kyō, and if fortune favored him, usurp Onikumawaka's status, thereby fulfilling his ambitions without a moment's intermission.

Onikumawake and his wife crossed the Eden River, climbed a slightly elevated hill on the opposite bank, and held a cherry blossom viewing banquet with numerous retainers. Intoxicated and dead drunk until their tongues could no longer articulate, walking with unsteady stagger, and dancing wildly with the feeling of monopolizing the spring of the realm, they proudly and triumphantly crossed the Eden River once more and returned toward this side. Onikumawake was extremely intoxicated, waving his arms, stamping his feet, and dancing wildly in the boat from bad liquor. Brandishing his iron fists recklessly and beating retainers indiscriminately, he was enjoying himself, but when one retainer tried to evade Onikumawake's iron fists, in his panicked scurry to run away, he collided with the body of Koitohime, who was fifteen years old this year and standing at the prow of the boat. The very moment they collided, Koitohime uttered a single cry of 'Ah!', fell into the swirling waves, and became a trailing white wake.

Starting with Onikumawake and his wife, the people in the boat for the first time shook off their intoxication and made a great clamor crying 'Oh dear! Oh dear!', but Koitohime's form was not to be seen, and they ran in a flurry back and forth across the narrow boat in utter panic. At this time, there were two men who leapt up and, stark naked, jumped into the river. One was Sansuke, who had collided with Koitohime, and the other was Tomohiko. Tomohiko was skilled in swimming, and floating and sinking, searching out the whereabouts of the princess with his feet, he swam on the surface while drifting downstream. When he finally caught sight of the princess's form, it was already more than a dozen chō downstream. Tomohiko clasped Koitohime under his arm, managed to climb up the riverbank, made her vomit water, exhausted every conceivable method with his hands, and at last caused her to revive. Triumphant and elated, he returned straight to Onikumawake's mansion.

Prior to this, Onikumawake and his wife had gathered a multitude of people, searched the riverbank where Koitohime had fallen with all their might, and concluding that it was utterly hopeless, they returned to their home. Just as the couple were lamenting and grieving together over their child's misfortune and drowning in tears, Tomohiko came walking in triumphantly, carrying Koitohime on his back, escorted by the gatekeeper, with an expression of self-satisfaction floating full across his face. Seeing this state of affairs, Onikumawake and his wife were overjoyed and astonished,

Oh, you have always been a clever man, so I singled you out and appointed you as a sendenshi, and my judging eye has not missed the mark by a hair. It is you alone. Even among all these numerous people, not a single fellow is of any use in an emergency. You have truly worked well. You are my foremost tegaramono.

Tomohiko, looking smug:

To receive such words of praise for so trivial a matter is truly the height of kangan for me. As I just heard you say, Anata, that even with so many kerai there is not a single fellow who is of any use in an emergency, it is simply because the kanbu yakuin of the Brahmanism do not understand. They are a renchiu who only treat the path of Kamisama as a kanban and think solely of their own shiyutsuse; if someone superior to you appears, they make all sorts of monoi and try to suppress your head, so no matter how splendid the Brahmanism may be, all the true kamubashira flee away. Although Kamisama says that the world shall not be left as a monopoly forever, the current kanbu have the cowardly desire to forever climb to high places and seize kenri, so they apply pressure and ostracize those of profound sincerity or figures who seem slightly useful; therefore, no matter how much time passes, unless the kanbu are reformed, or the kanbu ren become a little more god-hearted and carry out the rebuilding and reconstruction of their hearts, it will be no good. No matter how brightly the sun and moon shine in the heavens, their light does not reach the ground because of the dark clouds along the way. Even if a splendid and sincere person like myself remains hidden, there is neither an eye to recognize character, nor even if they recognize it, do they ostracize them instead to protect their own position, so all the splendid people end up hiding, and only the dregs come floating to the top. If you throw a handful of gravel mixed with dust into a puddle, the heavy, substantial stones instantly sink to the bottom and remain calm, while the dust floats right up to the top and drifts about at the whim of the wind. Just as the ears of rice bow their heads and look down as they ripen, substantial and sincere persons all maintain the virtue of humility, while empty ears of rice stand stiffly facing the sky; at this rate, Brahmanism will surely never develop. Nevertheless, Anata is a wise person, and recognizing the true ability of this Tomohiko, you heavily employed a pheasant who had been treated like a clod of earth by the kanbu and made him a sendenshi—that is truly splendid discernment, and Tomohiko too... Ah, what a splendid master I have come to have. There can be no other fortunate person like me in the world... so I think. It is said that a sage is in the fields, and a person who will truly become your strength and inherit the footsteps of the kyō... so to speak, a person who will become your adopted heir—even with all these numerous kanbu, and outwardly splendid sendenshi, there is rarely any such person. As for the selection of an adopted heir... you surely have no lack of tact... but I would like you to pay attention. No matter how bad the appearance may be, as long as the soul is splendid, one can succeed after Oni-kumawake-fuku-tōryō-sama.

Carried away by his own momentum and stringing together arbitrary theories, he is full of himself. Oni-kumawake, smiling broadly:

Hey Tomohiko... You are one who harbors lofty ideals that do not befit your face. For us it is the same thing; it is extremely difficult to obtain a tōryō's kerai. Seen in the light of the teachings of Daijaitensama, not a single person would pass. From among the assembled participants, jeers and insults were continuously hurled at him, such as 'Step down,' 'Retreat,' 'Spineless coward,' and 'Ring-leader of egoism'; amid the clamorous uproar bubbling like a boiling cauldron, Katahiko stood dead in his tracks upon the platform, unable to utter a single word, his lips trembling. Onikumumhiko and Onikumawake—both husband and wife—bowed lightly to all the believers and leisurely returned to the mansion. Tomohiko followed after them and exited as well.

Leaving Katahiko behind on the platform, everyone vied with each other to return to their respective homes. Left behind were a dozen prominent members of the executive staff, total of twelve people, who were engrossed in whispering about something.

Suddenly, the evening bell is heard, echoing forth that all things are transient; two crows perched upon the roof of the Yahiro-den open their wide mouths and caw, 'Aho, aho!'

原文（近代文語）

『私は只今の教主様の御演説につき感慨無量で御座います。皆様の大多数に置かせられましても、敬神、愛教、愛民の教主の御心中に、嘸嗚咽感激遊ばした事と確信致します。我々は大慈大悲の大神様は申すも更なり、教主様の仁慈無限の御精神に酬い奉り、副教主として、重任の地位に着かせ給ふ鬼熊別様の教を思ひ給ふ御熱情に対し、どこ迄も粉骨砕身、以て微力の有らむ限りを尽さねばならないではありませぬか。然るに今日の幹部は偸安姑息、大事に際して躊躇逡巡、なす所を知らずと云ふ為体では御座いますまいか』

聴衆の中より『賛成々々』と手を拍つ者四隅より現はれ来たりぬ。此声援に力を得て友彦は益々語気を強め、

『現にエデン河の小糸姫様の遭難に対し、幹部の御歴々は如何なる活動をなされましたか、幹部の総統たる片彦、釘彦の御両人は、我より以下の者共早く河中に飛び込み御救ひ致せ……と云ふ様な御態度でいらつしやる。次の幹部は又次へ、又次へ、我々幹部にあつては人を統率すれば良い、命令権を持つて居るのだから、直接活動すれば幹部の沽券を傷つけると云はぬばかり、次から次へ押せ押せに危ない事は塗りつけ合ひ、唯一人として身を挺しお救ひしようとする方々はなかつたぢやありませぬか。日頃のお言葉に似ず、実に冷淡極まる振舞、斯様な事でどうして神業が発展致しませうか。我々は実に遺憾に存じます』

と又もや卓を叩く。場内は『賛成々々』『尤も尤も』の声破るる許りに響き渡り、中には両手をあげて躍る者さへ現はれた。友彦は尚も語を継ぎ、

『皆さま、今日はバラモン教の立替の時機ではありますまいか。上流濁つて下流澄むと云ふ道理はありますまい。舎身的活動をなす至誠の士……例へて見れば九死一生の人の危難に際し、生命を的に助けに行く丈の真心を持つた真人をして、幹部の総統たらしめなば、名実相伴なふ所の立派なるバラモン教が築き上げられ、神政成就の大望も容易に運ぶでせう。抑も幹部たるものは重大なる責任が御座いますから、自己の安全のみに焦慮する如き人物は、この際信者多数の団結力を以て、根本改革を致さねば、迚も完全なる教は立ちますまい。何卒皆様の御熟考を煩はす次第で御座います』

と結んで降壇せむとするや、片彦は怒気を含み、拳を固め、壇上に現はれた。参集者の中より『下がれ』『退却』『無腸漢』『利己主義の張本人』などと、頻りに罵声を浴びせかけ、喧々囂々鼎の湧くが如く、片彦は壇上に立往生の儘一言も発し得ず、唇をビリつかせて居る。鬼雲彦、鬼熊別両夫婦は信者一同に軽く一礼し、館に悠々として立帰る。友彦も続いて退場する。

一同は片彦を壇場に残し、先を争うて各々住家に帰りゆく。あとには幹部の錚々たる者合せて十二人何事かヒソヒソと話に耽りゐる。

忽ち聞ゆる暮の鐘、諸行無常と鳴り響き、八尋殿の屋根に止まつた二羽の鴉、大口あけて『アホーアホー』と鳴きたてゐる。
