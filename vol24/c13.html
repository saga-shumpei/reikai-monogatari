Chapter 13: 治 Peace 内 Teaching [743]

治安内教〔743〕

§ 1

Landing upon a single island of the dragon palace where golden flowers bloom, drifting in the vast ocean, Takahime, Mukadehime, and Ōriyōhime passed through the castle gates of the capital of Iwa, and upon meeting Umeko-hime and Uzuhime, they were struck with wonder at their lofty achievements; and Takahime, whose nose of a tengu was high, conspired with Takayamahiko and Kurohime, pretending that the separate decrees of Tamanohime, Hatsuwakahime, and Tamaharu had crossed the ocean afar and returned to the island of Onokoro, while skillfully manning the boat, they set forth pointing toward the east, and the party of Tamaharuwake, Tamanohime, and Hatsuwakahime gazed upon the castle gates of the capital of Iwa behind them,

and following the ridges of Mount Tatsuoyama, stepping patter-patter, leaping across valleys and threading through rock crevices, streaming sweat profusely upon the summit of Mount Nelson, struggling against the scorching heat, they climbed up scarcely able to catch their breath; at which moment a refreshing breeze blowing forth brushed away their jeweled sweat, and looking back upon the capital of Iwa, they admired to their hearts' content the majestic and infinite scenery where mountain after mountain linked together, each respectfully offering up norito before the great presences of Amatsu-Ōkami, Kunitsu-Kami, and Kunitamatokami, and with brave voices of hand-clapping, praying that the propaganda of the dragon palace island might be safely accomplished, just as they prayed, from the mountainside

dense clouds suddenly surged forth, and the party of ten were instantly enveloped in darkness so that even their footsteps could scarce be seen; into such a place, parting the black clouds, came a swarm of giant serpents, which, spitting out tongues of flame, gathered and attacked the party; Smartball was startled and ran frantically through the darkness, and stepping off from the summit of Mount Nelson, suddenly plummeted down into a bottomless valley, and thereupon Kanzubukō, Kyūsuke, and Omin also each became unaware of their own whereabouts; Tamanohime, Kurohime, and Hatsuwakahime held hands, and in the midst of dark prayer and silent supplication, a fierce gale suddenly blew forth,

and the wild wind crossing over the ridge of the highest mountain on the main island was exceedingly strong, and the three of them, like leaves in the withered foliage, were swept up into mid-air, and sorrowfully, each of their whereabouts fell into a valley shrouded in white clouds; the deity appeared on the surface, separating right from wrong, Kami-Naobi who created this world, and Oonaobi of vast and generous heart, simply holding that in human life all things should be looked upon anew, listened to anew, and errors of the world be reproclaimed, the party who entrusted themselves to the teachings of the imperial deities of Aizankyo, praying deep within their hearts simply that in all things the divine will be manifest and the spirits be blessed and pacified,

plummeted down into the valley bottom whose depths are unknown, echoing the valley's spirits, and chanted the Amatsu Norito fluently—how brave indeed.

Carried out from the castle of Iwa, falling upon the rock in the valley, Tomohiko, who had bruised his waist, protected by the divine grace, suddenly regained his composure, and looking around, taking the absence of human shadows as good fortune, parting the grass of the forest bit by bit, he ate tree nuts, moistened his throat with valley water, and arrived at the mountain ridge of Nelson; once again blown by a fierce wind, Tomohiko's body was swept up, and flying across numerous high mountains, he fell headlong into the valley of Janna, losing consciousness.

Ah, Oujin-kamigami! Are the august spirits blessed and pacified? Tomohiko, who had escaped death by a hair's breadth, was carried by the believers of Jannaikyo and arrived at the hall of teachings of Teruhime.

This island was divided into east and west by the mountain range of Mount Nelson; the east was where Ōriyōhime propagated Aizankyo, and it was her sphere of influence. However, to the west of Mount Nelson, inhabitants were few, and countless hordes of fierce beasts, venomous snakes, and giant serpents inhabited it, and not a single person from the eastern half had ever crossed this mountain range to the west. Yet, even to the west of Mount Nelson, a considerable human population resided, making it akin to a secret land. It was a habitat of giants, being a race extremely ferocious and brave, where men were eight or nine shaku in height, and even women were not less than seven or eight shaku. Both men and women without exception practiced tattooing upon their faces, to the extent that at a glance it was difficult to distinguish the sexes. Weaving tree leaves to cover around their waists, everything else was completely naked, and they possessed skin like red bronze.

Because they had blue tattoos upon their black-colored faces, it was truly an indescribably terrifying appearance and nothing more. Such tattooing was done because of the warning that fierce beasts and giant serpents would fear and not draw near. Therefore, in this land, to speak of a handsome man meant the possessor of the most ferocious and hideous countenance, and one who was to become a chief looked like a demon at a single glance. Upon their heads they attached the horns of various beasts, in their hands they carried stone-made spears, and the custom was that when traveling, if there were not at least five or six companions, they would not step a single step outside. Their dwellings were mainly dug as caves in the mountainsides, spreading leaves as large as banana plants as bedding, and as for food, they subsisted regularly on tree nuts, mountain yams, pine nuts, and the like.

In the mountainous lands, fish were truly a rare delicacy, and if one could have the fortune to taste them once or twice in a lifetime, it could be said to be truly a luxurious life. Only occasionally did they capture a small fish about five sun long called miisu coming up into the mountain streams, and seeing the appearance of fish was exceedingly rare. Hares, mountain dogs, wildcats, and the like were captured and regarded as the finest delicacies.

However, as the doctrine of the Jiyannai teachings strictly prohibited the eating of animal flesh, those who broke this taboo held the belief that they would fall into the scorching hells, and thus easily avoided eating it. Anyone who once committed flesh-eating was expelled from their community and driven away to the banks of a mountain river. Those who had committed this flesh-eating and been driven out gathered and came to the wide valley on the western foot of Mount Nelson, revered the chieftain's daughter Teruhime as the leader to apologize for their sins to God, established the Jiyannai teachings, and were teaching the way of apology exclusively to the sinners of foul impurity. Those who did not eat flesh all built their dwellings above the middle slopes of the mountain, made the abundant tree nuts their regular food, and spent peaceful days and months. At the bottom of the valley, fierce beasts, giant serpents, and venomous snakes gathered in great numbers, making it truly a marshland of extreme danger.

At the bottom of this valley, the head temple of the Jiyannai teachings, with Teruhime as its leader, was built, and many believers offered up prayers morning and evening. The creed of the Jiyannai teachings was firmly believed as follows: Since we are sinners who have broken the taboo of the God of Āru, after death we shall certainly be those who receive the suffering of the nethermost land, therefore we must pray to God to apologize for our sins and escape the suffering of the world to come. And then... before long, a god with a red nose will descend to this land. This is our savior, who will pity our faith from heaven and descend from heaven... Thus, they firmly believed the teachings of Teruhime and awaited the arrival of the time. Moreover, holding the conviction that the red-nosed living god would rule the entire island of Australia and save us both in spirit and flesh, they awaited the descent of the savior as one looks for the clouds and rainbows in time of severe drought.

To such a place, blown about by the record-breaking fierce winds on top of Mount Nelson, and having crossed many peaks of the high mountains to now fall into this wide valley of Jiyanna, wondering what strange person could have come down, a dozen or so men and women who happened to come by placed Tomohiko's unconscious body upon a bed woven with wisteria vines and carried him into the head temple of the Jiyannai teachings. The many believers gathered round with curious interest, made him drink water, rubbed and stroked him, bent his hands, moved his feet, and revived him by various means.

At this time, Tomohiko had already fainted, his blood circulation had almost stopped, and his whole body had turned pale white. Chīchiyābōru, who was reputed to be Teruhime's chief disciple, was an exceptionally large man, the owner of a mouth split halfway to his cheeks, a large nose, and large eyes dominated by the whites. The leader Teruhime bore no tattoos at all, and was a relatively fair-skinned beauty with a slight reddish tinge. Those who served as leaders of the Jiyannai teachings made it the fundamental purpose of their teachings not to dye their natural flesh, and thus they received special treatment from the many believers and were made the object of respect.

Tomohiko gradually came to his senses, and when he looked around, he was startled to find tens and hundreds of a terrifying race whose men and women were indistinguishable and whose nature he could not fathom, surrounding his periphery ten and twenty deep, and tilting his head in wonder, he remained lost in thought as to what he should do. As his complexion gradually returned to normal and his whole body became full of color, the tip of his nose became redder than ever.

Chīchiyābōru turned toward the multitude and said,

'Ōrensu, sāchiraisu'

This meaning was the meaning of 'He is our savior.' The people all bent their hips, put their palms together, and while continuously shouting something or other toward Tomohiko in a chorus of voices, shed tears. Chīchiyābōru repeated and repeated,

'Ōrensu, sāchiraisu'

Although Tomohiko did not fully grasp the meaning, he thought that these natives were by no means those who would mistreat him, but rather those who mistakenly believed with curiosity that he was a heavenly descended race and were choked with tears of emotion, whereupon he gave full play to his habitual charlatan flair, clenched his right hand, raised his index finger, pointed to the sky,
