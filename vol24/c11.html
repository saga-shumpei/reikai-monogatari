Chapter 11: Dream の王者 [741]

夢の王者〔741〕

§ 1

In the inner chamber of Ōriyōhime, Mukadehime and Koitohime, the queen of the single island, sat formally in the Japanese style, and the tragicomic scene of a mother and daughter's reunion was being enacted.

Koitohime: 'Mother-sama, it has been a long time. Alas, in my childish heart, without distinguishing before and after, I was seduced by the lowly servant Tomohiko, abandoned my respected parents, and ran away from home. For this grave crime of filial impiety, I have no words of apology. Please do not think of the past events as the deeds of a hateful wretch, and forgive me.'

Thus she sat down in the lower seat and expressed her repentance. Drowning in tears of joy, Mukadehime spoke:

Mukadehime: 'Not at all, how could I scold you? For a time, both your parents were terribly worried, almost growing to loathe this world, and we sank into such a depth of pessimism that we even wondered if we, husband and wife, should throw ourselves into a deep river and die. However, thanks to the Lord God of great mercy and compassion, a ray of light began to shine even in our lonely and sorrowful hearts. Once again, your father and I collected ourselves, spreading the teachings of the Lord Daijaiten from the land of Persia to the land of India and Jitentōshima. We broke our lungs and labored with all our might, but no matter what we did, it seemed our actions failed to please the heart of Ōkami. Whatever we attempted crossed paths like the beak of a crossbill; your father, Oni-kumawake-sama, opened a meager garden of teachings at the foot of Mount Telmon on the border of Persia and India, while I darted about Jitentōshima. Hearing of your whereabouts through the wind, I crossed the waves of ten thousand miles and at last arrived at this island. If Oni-kumawake were to hear of this, how overjoyed he would surely be. My, how splendid you have grown. To become the queen of such a great island, you must have suffered a great deal of hardship.'

Koitohime: 'No, no, I have not suffered even a little hardship. Entrusting everything to the great divine heart of Ōkami, believing in the Three-Five teachings protected by Kunisokunitachireru no Mikoto, making the great heart of Mizu no Mitama my own heart, neither being impatient nor hurried, neither hating nor envying people, I faced all the people with heartfelt compassion and love. Through that virtue, this country has been governed in peace and safety, the fruits have ripened well, and the people beat their bellies and strike the earth in joy, just like the old days of Ken'ongō. I beg you, Mother-sama, you and your husband must replace and rebuild your past hearts, embody the great heart of the Lord God Kunisokunitachi no Ōkami, and become one in sincerity. There is nothing as strong or as wonderful as sincerity. To forgive any enemy whatsoever is the heart of the deities.'

Mukadehime: 'Truly a splendid attitude; without such a spirit, no wonderful path could ever be opened. And who is it that comes to assist you in your divine service?'

Koitohime: 'Well, the truth is, the four ladies who were at the head temple of Brahman in Ken'ongō—Isagohime-sama, Umekohime-sama, and the ladies attending them, Imakohime and Uzuhime—manifested boundless divine power, generously yielded all those achievements to me, and made me the queen of this island.'

Mukadehime: 'What! Did those Isagohime and Umekohime perform such a splendid deed? Are they not the evil deities who conspired with eight sisters, crept into Ken'ongō Castle, outwitted the Great Master, and smashed everything to pieces?'

Koitohime: 'Those ladies are the daughters of the deity Kami Susanoo no Mikoto. By the command of the deities, they descended to Ken'ongō as servants in order to save the people of the Brahman teachings onto the true path. You must never think ill of them. For the present, Isagohime-sama has set off to return to the sacred site of Jitentōshima together with Imakohime, and right now Umekohime-sama and Uzuhime assist and guide my divine government day and night; they are persons like wonderful deities. I beg you, Mother-sama, please offer your gratitude to them once with all due respect.'

Astonished by this tale and speechless, Mukadehime could only say 'Heh,' and for a short while no words came forth, passing the time with her head hung in deep thought.

After a short while, as she moistened her throat with the soybean tea brought by a maidservant, Mukadehime continued her words:

'You have upheld the path of pure virtue to such a degree, and have become a magnificent Queen, so you will surely forgive anyone who has shown you any impudence up to now, won't you?'

'Oh, to forgive or not to forgive... I possess no such presumptuous qualification as a human being. Since everything is entirely the divine arrangement of the Gods...'

'Even if it were a man like Tomohiko, if he came to apologize, would you forgive him?'

'Human nature is fundamentally good. If only he repents, he is originally a split-spirit of the Great Deity, and there can be neither sins nor defilements. My Hahauhesama, you may bear a grudge against Tomohiko, but Tomohiko is by no means in the wrong. It is I who have truly done something unforgivable toward Tomohiko. Receiving the divine virtues of the Gods and attaining such a magnificent station, no matter when I think of it, from morn till night it is of my Goriyōshinsama, and next, of Tomohiko. Sleeping or waking... Ah, to think that it was truly an unforgivable thing... makes me feel so ashamed that I can neither stand nor sit. I earnestly wish to see him once and offer my apologies. That is why I have guarded my chastity and continue to live a single life.'

Saying this, she wipes her tears with her sleeve. Mukadehime is utterly astonished, as if dumbfounded, and speaks,

'They say so well that in love there are no distinctions of high and low. As expected, do you still long for Tomohiko?'

Koitohime casts her eyes downward and, remaining silent, is overwhelmed in tears.

'If—by some remote chance—that Tomohiko were a swindler, a great thief, or a wicked scoundrel who steals other men's wives, what would you do, my dear?'

'I would not mind it in the least. Making Tomohiko into a bad man was entirely my own sin; if so, I must cherish him all the more.'

'Oh, is that so?'

So saying, she gazes searchingly and doubtfully into her daughter's face.

At this moment, a maidservant hurriedly appears upon the scene and speaks,

'I report to the Queen. Just now at the front entrance, a red-nosed, dark-complexioned, ugly man has suddenly appeared... roaring, "I am the husband of the Queen, let me meet Koitohime at once"... and though crowds of people try various ways to stop him, saying that such a madman must not be allowed near, this powerful red-nosed man is not easily calmed. He says rude things demanding to be announced to Koitohime, so what shall we do?'

Hearing this, Koitohime's complexion suddenly changes, and she says,

'What, a red-nosed man is saying he wants to meet Koitohime? Now then, I too shall go... Hahauhesama, please wait here for a little while.'

So saying and leaving her words behind, she follows behind the maidservant, proceeds along the long corridor, and heads toward the front entrance. Left behind, Mukadehime bites her tongue in amazement and exclaims,

'My, what intense infatuation! They say a sparrow never forgets its male bird until it is a hundred, and somehow even I have come to remember Onikumawake-san and feel a longing for him!'

Taking advantage of the absence of people all around, she weeps and cries out to her heart's content. Appearing at the entranceway, Koitohime looks at the figure of Tomohiko running wild while juggling numerous servants before and behind, to her left and right, and smilingly calls out, 'Tomohiko-dono, Tomohiko-dono.' At this voice, Tomohiko turns around, and while abruptly fixing his collar and adjusting his posture, he says,

'Oh, Koitohime-dono, it has been a long time since I last saw you. You had quite a formidable skill, didn't you?'

'It was truly an inexcusable matter. Now then, please do come inside the inner chambers. I shall take your hand.'

Everyone is completely dumbfounded by this behavior of Koitohime and stands gazing in blank astonishment. Tomohiko strikes away the hand that Koitohime has grasped and says,

'Absurd! You are a terrifying woman whose stratagems and schemes know no bounds; if I were to let even a careless hand be drawn by you, it would be as if I were struck by electricity, and I would inevitably fall into a fate of eternal tranquility and bliss. Ah, how terrifying, how terrifying! To possess the skill of becoming the queen of such a large island at the mere age of sixteen or seventeen, and to hold the arbitrary power of life and death—please, for the sake of our past friendship, spare at least my life. You have already given me quite a fright, so that should surely satisfy you by now. To torment me any further is sheer greed.'

Koitohime: 'Dear Tomohiko-sama, what a joke you are making. Day in and day out, thinking incessantly of you and of the dream-illusion, I have been waiting. Now, do not worry and go on back to the inner chambers; my mother Mukadehime is also resting within...'

Tomohiko, in a somewhat relieved manner, while trying to hide his red nose with his right hand, has his left hand grasped by the slender hand of Koitohime and follows along.

Koitohime: 'Tomohiko-sama, oh, how unsightly, putting your right hand against such a place, what are you doing?'

Tomohiko: 'Because my nose is much too red and quite unseemly...'

Koitohime: 'Ho ho ho ho, I happen to like that red nose of yours. Are there so many fine men with red tips to their noses anywhere in the world, ho ho ho ho.'

So laughing, she advances deep into the interior. Tomohiko finds joy, repulsiveness, and shame all combining within him, his legs trembling as he heads straight for the inner sanctuary.

In the inner chamber, Mukadehime is entirely alone, worrying over the fate of her husband and weeping with swollen eyes. Seeing Koitohime cheerfully enter, leading by the hand the hateful, hateful Tomohiko, she is struck with astonishment,

Mukadehime: 'Oh my, Koitohime-sama, if I thought it was just a joke, is it really true? To go dragging such a piece of dung like Kusohiko into the inner sanctum—it will surely touch upon your noble virtue. Let even your joking be within reason. ...Now, now Tomohiko, not knowing one's own station has its limits. This is no place for the likes of you, come, hurry on down to the servants' quarters or somewhere and take your rest. No matter how much Koitohime may fixate upon you, this very parent of hers will not allow it.'

Koitohime: 'Though my physical body is your daughter, I am now a queen holding the rights of a nation; thus, no matter how much they may be your words, mother, I cannot possibly receive them. ...Tomohiko-sama, please forgive your discourtesies up to now. ...Respected Mother, please forgive the sins of Tomohiko-sama until now. From today, I shall look upon Tomohiko-sama anew as my husband, and bestow upon him the name Ryūzuō. ...Now, Lord Ryūzuō, from this very day and hour I am your wife, and I humbly ask for your kindest favor.'

Tomohiko, wondering if this is a dream, pinches his cheeks and looks around, poking about the surroundings in a daze.

To such a scene comes Umekohime, arranging various delicacies in a beautiful vessel, entering slowly,

Umekohime: 'Well, well, Mukadehime-sama, most welcome indeed. It has been a long time since we last met. First of all, I am overjoyed beyond measure to find you in good health. I am Umekohime, daughter of the God Susanō-no-Mikoto, who has served at Gen'en Castle. This other one is called Uzuhime, formerly an attendant of Lord Onigumohiko; I humbly ask for your kind acquaintance.'

Mukadehime: 'Goodness me, how the years have piled up; indeed, you are without doubt Umekohime-sama. Even while wearing such a spiteful-looking face, to think you have helped my daughter this far and raised her to success. However, it is such a grandiose success that even this parent has become just a bit jealous. Having risen in the world far too much, she has come to pay no heed to what her mother says. Please, do give her a piece of your mind. Whether Koitohime becomes such a self-willed person or a gentle woman depends entirely upon the transforming power of her guide. If you will first teach her the way of parents and children, and husband and wife, and upon that foundation offer your opinions to my daughter, she will surely become the obedient, gentle young woman who listens to the words of a parent she has met by chance. And what is this, of all people, to revere such a red-nosed, ill-natured, clumsy man as Ryūzuō from today onward... Hmph... I am far too flabbergasted to find words, my word.'

So saying, she shrugs her shoulders heavily with a shudder, searching for a place to vent her dissatisfaction.

Ryūzuō: 'I am no longer the Tomohiko of until today. I am Ryūzuō, husband of Kōryūhime, Queen of Australia. Since the rights of this land are now grasped within my hands, it would be best for you to leave all matters to my direction, conduct yourself with modesty and reverence, and take all your actions accordingly.'

『これはこれは蜈蚣姫様、ようこそ御入来下さいました。久し振りでお目に懸ります。先づ御壮健にて、何より大慶至極に存じます。妾は顕恩城に仕へて居りました神素盞嗚尊の娘梅子姫で御座います。これなる一人は宇豆姫と申し、元は鬼雲彦様の侍女、何卒御眤懇に願ひ奉りまする』

『何とまア年が寄つた事、如何にもお前さまは梅子姫様に間違ひは無い。意地の悪さうな顔をして居乍ら、ようまア、娘をここ迄助けて出世をさして下さいました。然し乍ら、余り偉い出世で、此親もチツト位妬ましうなつた位で御座います。あまり出世をし過ぎて、母親の言ふ事も聞かぬ様になつて仕舞ひました。何卒貴女から一つ御意見遊ばして下さいませ。小糸姫が此様な我儘者になりますのも、温順な女になりますのも、指導者の感化力によりますのぢや。貴女から一つ淑やかになつて、親子夫婦の道をお悟り下さいまして、其上で娘に意見をして下さらば、偶会うた親の言ふ事を聞く従順な淑女になるでせう。それに何ぞや、人もあらうに、鼻赤の此様な不細工な性念の悪い男を、今日から竜頭王と仰ぎ奉るなぞと……ヘン……あまり呆れて物が言はれませぬワイなア』

と肩をプリンプリンと大きくシヤクツて、不平の持つて行き処を探し居たり。

『吾は今日より今迄の友彦に非ず。オーストラリヤの女王黄竜姫が夫、竜頭王で御座る。此国の権利は吾手に掌握致したれば、何事も此方の指図に任せ、慎み敬ひ凡ての行動を執つたがよからう』

§ 4

Thus for the first time displaying the majesty of a ruler, looking somewhat ashamed, she placed her hand to the tip of her nose and declared:

Ōriyōhime: 'Hmph, Tomohiko-sama, put that aside. No matter how much you say you have grasped the rights of Riugūjima, it will have no effect against me. I am the mother of Ōriyōhime. So to speak, I am your mother-in-law. No matter how much you have become the sovereign, you cannot go against what a parent says. Tomohiko-sama, shall I hear your reply?'

With jealousy and contempt jumbled together, breathing heavily, she unconsciously assumed a knee-up posture and glared straight into Tomohiko's face. Wanting to maintain the atmosphere of the entire gathering which had grown completely chilled, Uzuhime took Mukadehime by the hand and said:

Uzuhime: 'Now then, Mukadehime-sama, over here there is a splendid living room with a fine landscape. First, please take your ease and rest leisurely. I shall accompany you.'

Mukadehime: 'Yes, yes, thank you. For a wrinkled old hag to sit forever by the side of a young married couple would cause the gathering to grow chilly. Since we must not displease them either, if that is the case, I shall use my tact and withdraw from this place for the time being. Now, now, Tomohiko... Oops, heave-ho... Riuzū-ō-sama, Ōriyōhime-sama, as a married couple, please amicably discuss the governance of the world. Since it would not do to be in the way, using our tact in a place with a fine landscape, let us partake of a cup of tea or something.'

While hiccuping her words, shaking her body up and down and left and right roughly brushing the tatami mats, she advanced into another room.

Afterward, the two of them recounted to each other the course of events since their parting, poured out tales of their personal failures, and, like clearing skies after a sudden summer shower, once again became as inseparably close as of old.

As a violent gale blew from time to time, the high pavilion where Riuzū-ō was peacefully seated was blown down, smashed to tiny splinters with a crackling and rattling din, and his body fell from the high place along with fragments of timber onto the rocks above, and when he opened his eyes in sudden astonishment, ...who could have imagined it, at the edge of the woods outside the castle, Tomohiko, being carried out and abandoned by numerous servants, had fallen fast asleep from utter exhaustion, and the very moment he turned over in his sleep, he plummeted down onto the rocks two or three ken below, striking his lower back quite severely.
