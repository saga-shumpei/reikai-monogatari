Chapter 5: 蘇鉄の Forest [735]

蘇鉄の森〔735〕

§ 1

Depending on Inochi's cord as a lifeline, Takahime, raging like a yashiya, declared that no matter where the arika of the three Shinpau might be, she would search them out to the very end; crashing her boat against the island shadows of Akashigata and Ahaji where accumulated thoughts gathered, sinking, and rescued from the great dainan of kiushiitsushiyō by Tamano no Hime, one would think she might feel kanshiya, but she was obstructed by the akuma of jison lurking in the depths of her heart, various times cursing and mocking the parent of her Inochi, entrusting her body to the boat steered by Tōsuke, rushing her heart to arrive at Ejima, exhausting herself through miscellaneous ways and breaking her heart, at the ageku,

Sinking her body into the abyss of zetsubau, pondering ikaga to do and hesitating back and forth, just as she was plunged into shian, while entrusting her body to a newly built kobune tied to the hamabe, accompanied by Kanshi, rowing out with all her might across the sea surface of the Inochi no Seto, drifting ashore at Shōkodojima, and once again attempting to search out the arika of the jewel, running up toward the mountain of Kuniki, unexpectedly straying into the mansion of Mukadehime, the chief priestess of the Brahmin God inside the cavern, prompt and witty Takahime caught the feelings of Mukadehime, only putting on an appearance of friendship on the surface, gradually escaping the poisonous hands of the enemy,

Intending to use Mukadehime to search for the arika of the jewel, once again entrusting her body to the boat, a party of several people over the waves, passing across the Bakan Strait, proceeding west and south; Mukadehime, first of all seeking the arika of the jewel, and as two jewels for the purpose of searching for the arika of her beloved daughter, entangled by love and greed, followed by Smart Ball and others, Takahime pushed her boat with a pole and proceeded; enemy and ally whose hearts did not match, although the waves of the vast ocean were calm, the waves rising in the sea of the murakimo heart were a turbulent mood.

The party, bathed in scorching, sun-like sunlight, squeezed out sweat like waterfalls, and feeling thirst and desiring water, at last brought their boat alongside the rocky shore of a large island floating in the midst of the sea, and seeking fresh water hither and thither, parting the grasses and trees, calling out to each other with voices saying 'Oi-oi', and maintaining contact as they advanced deep into the interior of the island. From their parched throats, even hoarse voices could no longer be uttered.

Takahime at last arrived at the forest of sotos, and the soto trunks, measuring about a shaku in length, stood in rows as crowded as if a giant snake were standing upright and spreading an umbrella. Gazing at the soto's mara, she suddenly commanded Kanshi to pluck them off and began to cling to and gnaw at them, whereupon a sweet juice like inexplicable amrita oozed out as she chewed, and she at last felt the thought of resuscitation. …… Mukadehime and her party also at last appeared at this place, and at the very instant when they fixed their eyes on the mara plucked off by Takahime and, like ghouls, tried to bite into them to cure their thirst, the mara fruits suddenly elongated into a giant centipede about a shaku long, crawling out sluggishly, climbing right up the soto trunks, elongating like tapeworms one after another to wrap around the soto trunks without leaving a single one, and attempting to prevent even a single finger from being applied. The centipede increased its length and thickness from moment to moment, and within about the space of an hour, wrapped around this entire large island.

Takahime, Mukadehime, and the rest of the party were enveloped by the centipede along with the trees, and with their breath failing, they chanted the Amatsu Norito, recited the scriptures of Brahmanism, and had no other thought than simply praying for the safety of their bodies.

The centipede, formed out of the transformation of the mara, wrapped around the large island ten and twentyfold, making the four quarters dim and gloomy like darkness, and with an unspeakably unpleasant atmosphere, the function of their respiratory organs was almost halted. Takahime, having fallen into the plight of kiushiitsushiyō, thinking that this was the very end, detached herself from all clinging attachments, entrusted her destiny to Kami, and closing the eyes of contemplation, awaited death.

Instantly, along with feeling the agony of scorching one's head as if boiling water had been poured down from overhead, a gigantic god-man revealing a skin of refined gold suddenly appeared and came to this place,

Takahime, who art called the living palace of the Hinodewake God, if thou dost not repent now, thou shalt taste this present agony forever and be burned in the unquenchable fire of the foundational land.

So saying, the figure vanished. Meanwhile, Mukadehime felt an excruciating pain as if being stabbed from above by a blade of ice, and even as she writhed in agony, tossing about to and fro, a strange monster with a pitch-black, gigantic face like ink and glowing crimson eyeballs appeared, revealing its upper body and head against the darkness with an even blacker outline, sticking out its long tongue to lick Mukadehime's head and face; such was the terror that even the brave Mukadehime felt her hair stand on end with loathing, and she could only weep without any response; from the tip of the monster's tongue, countless tiny centipedes emerged like rain, enveloping Mukadehime's body without leaving an inch of open space, biting her everywhere with countless sharp, sword-like tongues, causing her to cry out 'Eek!' and collapse on the spot, rolling to the right and left. At this time, Takahime finally recovered her sanity and looked around to see the scorching sun shining brilliantly, numerous trees looking lush and green, dancing mindlessly to the blowing sea breeze. Takahime:

'Ah, was it a dream after all. Even so, this strange sōtetsu palm—it is terrifying to linger on an island where such eerie wonders keep occurring; I shall leave this island as early as possible and search for the whereabouts of the treasure. Kanshū, come!'

Looking around, she saw Kanshū sitting heavily, arms folded with closed eyes, frozen like a stone statue. Takahime chanted norito with all her might, pinched his cheeks, pinched his nose, and spent about half an hour trying various ways to revive him. However, Kanshū was like a stone statue without any circulation of blood, and no matter where she stroked him, not the slightest warmth remained. Takahime was seized by an inexplicable sense of loneliness, her voice mingling with tears:

'Hey Kanshū, if you die in a place like this now, what will become of me... Please pull yourself together.'

She pleaded while weeping. Kanshū finally opened his left eye with a blink. However, his pupil had hidden somewhere, leaving only the white of his eye exposed, with red blood surging through veins like tree roots, and his face appearing like the branches of a red coral tree.

Takahime concentrated her prayers with all her might. At this time, Mukadehime, who had hitherto been struck down flat on the earth, rose silently and stiffly, appearing abruptly before Takahime with a fierce expression of anger, clutched her fists, exposed a face like a flat-backed crab, and began to glare at her. Once again, Smartball rose stiffly, exposing only the whites of both eyes, pursing its lips, striking a pose like a mantis, and began hammering away mechanically at Takahime's head like a day laborer digging up the earth with a pickaxe. Her hands had become as hard as iron. Desperate to avoid this sharp edge, Takahime tried to dodge, but like a tree growing out of the ground, she could not move an inch, and had no choice but to be jabbed in the same spot over and over again by the pickaxe-like fists.

At this time, a majestic divine person parted the clouds in heaven, riding on a divine horse and descending toward this place. Accompanied by hundreds of mounted retainers, with the bells ringing jinglingly, step by step he descended through the air and spoke in a loud voice:

'Are you not Takahime? The guardian deity who calls himself the God of Sunrise is actually a silver-furred, eight-tailed evil fox originating from the Rocky Mountains of the Land of Eternal Night. I shall now untie your spiritual bonds. Repent as of today, and do not presume to call yourself the God of Sunrise even in jest. I myself am the true God of Sunrise. For the time being, in this place, through Kami-naobi and Ō-naobi, I shall look upon this anew and set it right, and forgive your sins. From now on, you shall cross to the South Seas together with Mukadehime's entourage, reach the solitary island of Ryūgū, and save Kurahime. Do not doubt this in the least.'

So saying, he turned his horse's head and, together with numerous attendant deities, rode side by side and ascended high into the heavens. At that moment, a great orb of light appeared from somewhere in the air, falling right in front of Takahime with a roaring sound, the fire exploded and scattered in all directions, and just as she thought the bodies of Takahime's entourage had been smashed to pieces, she opened her eyes to find herself collapsed under the great sōtetsu palm, clutching a melon. Mukadehime and the rest of the company lay at the very limit of their breath, being either stung or licked by giant horseflies and the like upon their heads on the grassy field under the blazing sun. § 4

Mukadehime burst into laughter,

"Ohohoho!"

and everyone joined in mutual laughter,

"Ahahaha!"

Kuwanshiu said,

"Ah, what on earth is going on? It has become utterly impossible to form any kentau."

Takahime jerked her chin up and said,

"It is a settled matter. Did not Henjiyōnanshi say that it is a shigumi beyond all kentau? Has it not been revealed in the Ofudesaki that there is not even one person in sekai who understands this matter... or so it is written. If you people were to understand the true shigumi, then interference would enter along the way, and things would not be accomplished. Ohohoho!"

Saying this, she shook her shoulders broadly and let out a war cry. Mukadehime gently touched her eyebrows with spittle and put on an innocent face...

"Tell me, Takahime-san, just when I think you say you are the living shrine of the gokenzoku of Daijizaiten, you also talk as if you are the living shrine of Denokami; which guardian deity is actually possessing you?"

"Being the guardian deity of Daijizaiten, who performs hengenshiyutsubotsu, senpenbankuwa, and jiyūjizai activity, sometimes I appear as Denokami, and also as the kenzoku of Ōkuninwakeshi... but in actual truth, the divine protection of Ōkuninushinomikoto is the principal one."

"Hinode and Ōkuro... That is a tremendous gap. To Mukadehime, the distinction between good and evil seems completely like the front and back of a thing."

"You are one to utter such a foolish question unsuited to you. Conjunction of the phenomenal and the noumenal, non-duality of good and evil; if there is a back, there is a front, and if there is a front, there is a back. To manifest the subtle and great activity of endless shifting between front and back is the true deity. Deities who act like carriage horses ultimately lack the qualification to direct people. Since we are the living shrines of Ōkuninushinomikoto, we are inherently endowed with the supreme prerogative to command all deities and men in place of Daijizaiten. We are the living shrines of the so-called opening of the rock cave of Denokami. Deities draw the heart in various ways, so please be careful not to get caught in their recoils."

"Before I knew it, your tongue has grown remarkably agile compared to when you were sneaking around disguised as a believer of Ken'onjō. Even Mukadehime is impressed."

"What on earth are you saying about sneaking around? I have believed in Daijizaiten with a true, sincere, and pure Yamato spirit. Whether one speaks of the Uranai teachings, the Izumo teachings, Brahmanism, or Jiannaiism, at the root they are all one stock, no different from the Great Deity who is the foundation of heaven and earth. However, for the present, I thought that the path of the Brahmanism you espouse is the most austere, relies on unsparing action, and performs severe ascetic practices, which would accord with the divine will; ever since we met at Kokujōyama, I have grown all the more fond of Brahmanism. Now, now, since things have come to this, we are like sisters, so let us set out at once to search for their whereabouts."

"I no longer have any attachment to jewels. More than that, I have realized that it is essential to polish the jewel of my heart."

"Hohohoho, what a convenient mouth you have. Because the whereabouts of the only Koitohime in heaven and earth has begun to become clear, this is no time to be talking about jewels. Your primary thought seems to be that you want to meet Koitohime as soon as possible. That is hardly unreasonable. No matter what, she is your only daughter who would not hurt even if she entered your eye, so naturally, your own daughter is more precious to you than the rise and fall of the nation—that is simply human emotion."

She spoke mockingly. Mukadehime felt irritated by Takahime's words, but thinking that no matter what she said, they were in the same boat, and that she could not reach her destination unless she kept Takahime in a good mood, she deliberately assumed a pleasant tone and said,

"Hohohoho, well, well, Takahime-sama's instruction, I am deeply moved. Drowning in the love of my own child and missing the great matter—that was my blunder; please do not laugh at me as a disgraceful woman. In that case, from now on, let us put the deities first and our own child second."

Takahime: 'And the jewel third, is that it? As you yourself have said, once your heart is polished to that extent, rather than a tangible jewel, you must be content just to meet Koitohime-sama. Do not harbor any more attachment to jewels. In exchange, as soon as I discover that jewel, I shall take custody of it and present it to Daijizaiten with my own hands. Is that agreeable? Once something has come from your mouth, you surely cannot swallow back the spittle you have spat.'

§ 5

Fixing her eyes upon her, she looks briefly at Mukadehime's face. Mukadehime deliberately turns her face away, and with an innocent expression, speaks:

Mukadehime: 'I shall leave everything to you, Anata.'

Onikumawake: 'Now look here, Mukadehime-sama, that deviates from our objective. You too stayed long at Magayanogatake, did you not, for the purpose of seeking the whereabouts of the jewel? Why do you speak with such a faint heart? No matter what Takahime-sama may say, Smart will not agree to it.'

Mukadehime: 'Everything resides within my own breast, so keep silent.'

Onikumawake: 'Resides in your breast, Mukadehime-sama—what is it that resides there? Are you not saying something exceedingly insidious? If so, then what you just told me must be a falsehood.'

Mukadehime: 'How could I, a concubine who serves the Kamisama even temporarily and the wife of Onikumawake, ever speak lies and falsehoods? If you despise me too much, even this Mukadehime will not let this matter rest as it is.'

Shrugging her shoulders, drawing her mouth into the shape of the character he, and gnashing her teeth, she glared fiercely at Takahime's face with a dreadful countenance.

Onikumawake: 'Ho-ho-ho-ho, do not make such a face like a Heikegani that has eaten Darasuke. Do you too get angry after all? What have you done with the treasure called patience?'

Mukadehime: 'That is your misunderstanding; I was suffering and writhing in agony as my stomach suddenly began to ache, and as a result, my expression became frightening. Ah, thanks to Kamisama, it has eased considerably. Now, now, everyone, let us get along and cross these rough waves in a single boat. With a sufficient supply of water for everyone...'

Each of them hugged whatever vessels they had, pushed their way through the forest overgrown with betel-palm trees, and guided by Kanshū, they sought a puddle in the valley, barely filled their water containers, finally loaded them onto the boat, and taking advantage of the moonlit night, hoisted their sails to a favorable wind, steered to the southwest, and proceeded onward, weaving among the small islands rising and falling upon the sea.
