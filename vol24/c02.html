Chapter 2: 唖呍 [732]

唖呍〔732〕

§ 1

Tomohiko enjoyed the ever-increasing trust of Onikumawake and his wife, and at last came to serve in the inner chambers as the chief of internal affairs. Koitohime, too, was won over morning and evening by Tomohiko's kindness, and though she might have thought him an unpleasing sight before she knew it, she ended up coming to rely upon him utterly as an incomparable source of strength.

Just as beans growing in the shade will burst open when the season arrives, the innocent maiden who had welcomed her fifteenth spring found her voice changing before she knew it, and the shape of her hips had grown considerably more mature. The primary essential point in binding the emotional intimacy of man and woman must surely be the heavy repetition of the degree of conversation, the frequency of meetings, and the great influence exerted by the matter of time.

Before she knew it, every time Koitohime saw Tomohiko's face, she would blush, hide in the shade of a sliding screen, and go so far as to peer at him through a stolen glance. Mukadehime had entrusted the care of Koitohime's personal affairs to Tomohiko, who enjoyed her deep trust; but since the path of love knows no separation between near and far, high and low, even Tomohiko—indecisive and flaccid of waist—before he knew it gave rise to strange thoughts, and wrapped in the flames of ambition, burning his heart day and night, he intended eventually to become the husband of Koitohime and seize actual control of Brahmanism.

Amidst Tomohiko's mounting path of love, even in the opening and closing of sliding screens, Koitohime saw them rubbing and tangling like young greens of pines standing side by side, and pine flowers blooming on a high peak where none could pluck them; at last the era of Tomohiko's triumph had arrived. Mukadehime, a dog that had eaten wild boar, shrewdly surmised the relation between the two; she exhausted words toward her husband Onikumawake, touched upon the matter on every occasion and handled every object, and circuitously employed various labors of mediation to realize her will of making Tomohiko the husband of Koitohime and causing him to become Onikumawake's successor.

However, Onikumawake's heart was pained by Tomohiko's base character and vulgar visage, and realizing that he was utterly unsuitable as a successor to the vice-leader, he always let Mukadehime's mediation—exhausting a thousand words and ten thousand phrases—go in one ear and out the other like the wind blowing in a horse's ear.

Tomohiko and Koitohime perceived the father's inner mind, and hiding from the eyes of others while worrying and fretting over the future of the two, they were absorbed in whispering talks.

At a certain time Tomohiko said,

Koitohime finally opened her mouth and with bashfulness said,

Tomohikosan: 'What kind of reason is that, pray? Did you not assert that no matter what happened you would devote your utmost strength for the sake of Koitohime and even offer up your life?'

Tomohiko: 'Yes, my heart has not changed in the least. Day by day, my cuteness and yearning for you increase more and more, and the flame of love comes blazing up to the point that if I cannot see your face for even a fleeting moment, I cannot remain still. However, you are the beloved only daughter of the esteemed vice-leader, and you cannot possibly enter into relations with a lowly person like myself. Your father's inner intention will by no means grant the desires of both of us. No matter how much your mother may act as an intermediary, I have understood that it is already no good. From now on, in order to forget this vexation, I shall journey far from your side, wander through the world, and undergo a hardship. Since this is the last time I shall look upon your face, please exhaust your filial piety toward your parents, possess a splendid husband, and devote yourself for the sake of Brahmanism.'

Koitohime was startled, fell weeping upon that place, and cried out,

Koitohime: 'Ah, what shall I do? Honorable Father, you do not hear!'

While she wept and shouted, Tomohiko said,

Tomohiko: 'Moshimoshi, Ojoyusama, regret cannot recover our mutual ties; please resign yourself as though you had seen a fleeting dream. I offer my apologies for the manifold sins of having committed discourtesies toward you with my truly lowly body, please forgive me... Farewell, I shall now part from you, my beloved.'

As he attempted to leave, she pulled at the hem of his garment and said,

Koitohime: 'Please wait a while. I too am a shred of a woman; even if heaven and earth should change, how could the path of a woman stand if I were to abandon you, with whom I once exchanged vows? The path of husband and wife is to share bitter and sweet together; no matter what you may say, I will not let you go anywhere. If we must part no matter what, then with your own hands you must stab and kill me, and go wherever you may choose.'

§ 2

Weeping and prostrating himself.

Tomohiko: 'Ah, a troublesome matter has arisen. If I say we must part, it is a matter of the young lady's strong resolve and an ichidaiji that touches upon life itself, leaving me with no excuse to offer to the deeply indebted husband and wife Onikumawake. Even so, if I were to take the precious young lady with me, it would be even less permissible; ah, there is no other way... Moshijōsama, I shall rip open my belly and perish right here. Please, anata, serve your parents and fulfill your filial duties, and when by chance you remember me, please offer even a single cup of water. I know not how joyful it would be beyond the chanting of a senmannin of sendenshi if a single drop of water were given by your own ote, Koitohimėsama. Farewell.'

Drawing his kuwaiken smoothly, just as he was about to press it against his belly, Koitohime clung to his arm,

Koitohime: 'Moshimoshi Yotahiko-san, please wait a moment. There is something I desire to ask.'

Tomohiko:

Tomohiko: 'Since I have already made up my mind, there is only death for the sake of an excuse. Please let me die properly.'

Koitohime: 'How could this ever be allowed as a death? Since it has come to this, there is no help for it. Whether to stick to one's parents or stick to one's husband, the path to settle upon is but one. Though it may cause my parents hardship for a while, if we but live long in this world hereafter, surely we shall be able to fulfill our filial piety toward my dear riyōshin. I beg of you, Tomohiko-san, will you not take me and flee?'

Tomohiko: 'This is astonishing, ojiyōsama; it is said that parents are for one world while spouses are for two, and surely there is nothing in this world as precious as one's parents. I am not the only man in existence; since there are plenty of much, much finer men, please put thoughts of me out of your mind from this moment onward, and practice kauyō toward your parents. Farewell, we part thus—'

And once again he attempted to thrust the kuwaiken forward. Gripped by unbearable sorrow, Koitohime bit into his arm, pouring her utmost strength into stopping Tomohiko, desperate to prevent his death. With an expression of overwhelming emotion,

Tomohiko: 'Ah, do you care for me even to that extent? If that is so, I shall follow your bidding, hide somewhere together with you for a while, and pass joyous days.'

Koitohime:

Koitohime: 'Ah, with that I am at ease.'

Entering the inner chambers, she secretly secreted a large sum of travel funds into her robes, and waiting for the night to deepen, the two left the mansion behind and vanished from Kenonkō into nowhere.

The two man and woman, differing in age like parent and child, walked hand in hand, wandering hither and thither through the land of Persia, and at last, crossing several lofty mountains, they advanced to the southern extremity of the land of India. Here there was a prosperous place called the Capital of Dew. The Brahmana sendenshi Ichihiko wielded considerable influence and made his name resound far and near. Deeming that wandering in such a place carried the risk of exposure, Tomohiko crossed the sea under the cover of a moonlit night, rowed across to the island of Ceylon, proceeded deep within, and built a dwelling in the valley of Mount Shiro, where the two came to live. The islanders, finding them a novelty, gazed upon Koitohime's appearance, which put flowers to shame, deemed her a heavenly maiden descended to earth, paid her reverence, and there was no end to those who visited and worshipped at this hermitage day and night. Koitohime outwardly made Tomohiko her servant, put on the airs of a queen, played the bat in a birdless island, and indulged in pleasures day and night together with Tomohiko.

The plating of Tomohiko's mitama, which he had hastily applied, peeled off day by day; blessed by the fact that the eyes of his mother and father parents were far away, he grew arrogant and came to order Koitohime about at his beck and call. Then, taking out the travel funds that Koitohime had brought along, he immersed himself in wine day and night, or began to dally foolishly with the island women. As her dream of love finally awakened, Koitohime came to loathe everything Tomohiko said and did as if it were venomous serpents and scorpions, and even the wind blowing from Tomohiko's direction felt as though it were cutting her flesh. The summer of love, infatuated beyond a hundred degrees, passed away before she knew it, and gradually the autumn wind began to blow; day by day the chill increased like a biting winter, and the fever of love that had yearned for Tomohiko cooled down completely, ending up like ice.

§ 3

Tomohiko grew exasperated as Koitohime's attitude grew colder day by day, occasionally swinging his fists, drinking despair-sake, and shouting with a hoarse voice, so that the relationship between the two grew increasingly discordant day and night.

One night, watching for the moment when Tomohiko guzzled heavy sake and collapsed in a drunken stupor, Koitohime left behind a single farewell letter, rowed a small boat moored to the beach, accompanied two islander negroes, and boldly fled toward the Pacific Ocean.

When Tomohiko's drunkenness wore off, he woke up and looked around to find that the night had cleared up completely and the voices of small birds were noisy.

Rubbing his sleepy eyes, Tomohiko cried out,

Koitohime: 'Water, water!'

Though he called and shouted, there was no response at all, whereupon Tomohiko said,

Tomohiko: 'Ah, she must have gone off to the mountain behind again to get some fruit. No matter what is said, since she is a young lady raised selfishly there is no help for it. However, even though she is such a person, she is still only sixteen so she is like a child. It is also pitiful to scold her so severely. I must cherish her a little from now on. If she stayed in Kenonkiyō, she would be revered as a young lady from both here and there, treated like a queen, and able to live in splendor and glory. That this Tomohiko, by means of an unexpected feat, used it as a trigger to skillfully coax her, and my skill at tricking an innocent novice girl—surely even the executive members of Brahmanism must have been astonished. Although my face is one that even to myself causes disgust, nevertheless thinking of me as the parent of her life, staying behind while sulking and leaping about is still rather endearing. Even if she thinks she hates me and wants to flee back, once she is brought across distant mountains and slopes to such a remote island, there is nothing a frail woman can do about it. When I think of it, how pitiful she is... Ah, my throat is parched. Let Tomohiko himself scoop up clear water and drink it to his heart's content.'

So saying, he thrust a bamboo ladle into the mountain stream flowing past the front gate, scooped up a single cup with a gulp, changed his voice, and said,

Tomohiko: 'Now then, my lord, please drink up. If you drink too much sake, it will not be good for your person. If you were to fall ill, what would I do? Say, my dear, if you think me dear, please do your best not to overdo the sake... is what she would usually spit out, but today of all days the mountain god has gone out on business somewhere. He will probably return home soon. I wonder if I should serve as the proxy for the mountain god until then.'

So saying to himself, he drank it down in one gulp,

Tomohiko: 'Ah, the deliciousness of hangover water is unknown to those who cannot drink. Ah, delicious, delicious, the love affair of two people through which not even water can leak—since I made free marriage the fashion without even a go-between, let us first have a cup assuming this ladle is the go-between. No matter how much of a ladle it is, this island is far away from Kenonkiyō, and there is hardly anyone who would pour water on the love affair of two people. However, as for Koitohime occasionally having fits of hysteria, even I am troubled for a moment... "Oh my husband, a fit of hysteria has pierced me. Please grant me a seppuku assistance"... making such truly bewitching sounds, it always gets on my nerves... no, it does not. Ah, delicious, delicious.'

Thus scooping and drinking, scooping and drinking, he amused himself alone.

To this place, one of the negroes appeared and came,

Negro: 'Excuse me, Tomohiko-sama, do you know that the queen got on a boat last night and went somewhere?'

Hearing this, Tomohiko turned pale,

Tomohiko: 'What, Koitohime got on a boat and left this place? Is that true?'

Negro: 'How could I tell a lie? I clearly witnessed in the moonlight that the two, Chanky and Monkey, operated the oars and set sail from the harbor. Not only me, four or five people all saw it.'

『さア、旦那様、御上り遊ばせ。あまり御酒を上りますと御身のためによろしく御座いませぬ。若しも貴方が御病気にでも御なり遊ばしたら、妾は何うしませう。ねー貴方、妾が可愛いと思召すなら、何うぞ御酒を余り過ごさない様にして頂戴……ナンテ吐しよるのだけれど、今日に限つて若山の神様は何処かへ御出張遊ばした。軈て御帰館になるだらう。それまで山の神の代理を勤めるのかなア』

と独語言ひ乍ら、グツト一杯飲み乾し、

『アヽ酔醒めの水の美味さは下戸知らずだ。アヽうまいうまい、水も漏らさぬ二人の恋仲、媒酌人も無しに自由結婚と洒落たのだから、此の杓を媒酌人と仮定して先づ一杯やりませう。何程しやくだと云つても、顕恩郷を遠く離れた此の島、二人の恋仲に水差す奴も滅多にあるまい。併し乍ら小糸姫が時々癪を起すのには、一寸俺も困る………「もしわが夫様、癪がさしこみました。どうぞ御介錯を願ひます」………なんて本当になまめかしい声を出しやがつて、俺は何時もそれが癪に障………らせぬワ。アヽうまいうまい』

と、汲んでは飲み汲くんでは飲み一人興がりゐる。

斯かる処へ黒ン坊の一人現はれ来り、

『モシモシ友彦様、女王様が夜前船に乗つて何処かへ往かれたのを、貴方御存知で御座いますか』

と聞くより友彦は真蒼になり、

『何ツ、小糸姫が船に乗つて此処を去つたとは、そりや本当か』

『何私が嘘を申しませう。チヤンキーとモンキーの二人が、櫓櫂を操り港を船出しましたのを、月夜の光に慥に見届けました。私ばかりでない、四五人のものがみんな見て居りますよ』

§ 4

Naze: 'If so, why did you not inform me at once?'

Servant: 'We came to inform you at once, but as you know, this steep slope prevents us from coming along steadily, sir.'

Naze: 'And do you know where Koitohime has gone?'

Servant: 'As to that, I do not know for certain, but she set out with her prow turned toward the direction of the country of Tsuki, so she has most likely gone to the capital of Dew.'

Tomohiko folded his arms, let out a deep groan of a sigh, and let coarse tears spill drop by drop from both eyes. After a while Tomohiko stood up,

Tomohiko: 'You wretched pair, Chiankiy and Monkī, having seduced my precious wife, where have you fled? Even if you possess the miraculous arts of soaring through the heavens or burrowing into the earth, I shall search for you even by parting the grass, and I will not rest until I meet my wife. At that time I shall offer the two of you, Chiankiy and Monkī, as a blood sacrifice!'

Raging like a madman, he threw pots, kettles, and the braziers, vented his grudge upon the sliding doors and paper screens, gave himself up to the utmost limits of violent rampage, smashed every piece of household goods into absolute smithereens, tore apart and shattered the garments and hand tools left behind by Koitohime, stamped his feet as he whirled round and round inside the room seven or eight times in a frenzy, rolled his eyes, and collapsed flat on the floor.

One of the blackamoors, startled, ran up to his side,

Blackamoor: 'My dear Tomohiko-san, have you gone mad? Come, calm your feelings; no matter how impatient you are, you cannot catch up with her now. In any case, there is a famous missionary named Ichihiko residing in the capital of Dew in the country of Tsuki, so she has most likely gone to his residence.'

Tomohiko came to his senses with a start at these words,

Tomohiko: 'What! What is that about Ichihiko?'

Blackamoor: 'Everyone was whispering that the Queen-sama has most likely gone to the residence of Ichihiko in the capital of Dew, I say.'

Tomohiko: 'That was well informed of you, you rascal. Come, I shall give you a tip.'

When he opened the cash box and looked inside, lo and behold, it was an empty chest, Kansaeimon, not a single zeni coin remaining. Quickly grasping and unfolding a folded paper left at the bottom of the box, he looked at it,

Tomohiko: 'Ah, I cannot make head or tail of this. In Spartan script... How malicious, knowing that I cannot read it, she must have left a will for me. Yet this is evidence for later. I must keep it carefully.'

Stowing it away as a precious treasure inside his amulet bag, he had the blackamoor guide him, and with all his might, shouting loudly as he hurried down the steep slope of Mount Shiro, he descended with great strides.

At last they rushed down to the port of Shiro. As a result of the blackamoor and the two of them engaging in a reckless marathon race, the moment he arrived at the port, his tension relaxed and he collapsed flat right there. Twenty or thirty blackamoors gathered around at the port, splashing water on him, pinching his nose, and doing various things until he finally came to.

Tomohiko, looking around on all sides in a daze,

Tomohiko: 'Oh, this is the port of Shiro. Come, you fellows, make ready a boat this very instant and send me to the country of Tsuki.'

Blackamoor: 'How much fare will you give us?'

Tomohiko: 'Eh, at a time like this, instead of talking about fare, we cannot delay even for a moment. The fare shall be given to you afterward as you desire. Come, be off quickly!'

He urged them on impatiently.

Tomohiko's pockets were in truth utterly empty. The eight blackamoors, rowing with eight oars and flying like arrows at Tomohiko's command, crossed the Indian Ocean and at last reached the shore of the country of Tsuki. This place is called Masago no Hama and has a shallow seabed. The boat anchored about ten cho offshore, and from there they were to tuck up their skirts and land on foot.

Blackamoor: 'My dear general-sama, please give us our fare.'

Tomohiko: 'Wait a moment; I will lavishly pay the fare for both round trips when we return to the port of Shiro. Since it is bothersome to pay in two installments, you had better float your boat here and wait.'

Blackamoor: 'Even if you say so... we cannot reach the capital of Dew in two or three days. Although it takes ten days for the round trip, do you expect us to wait in such a place?'

§ 5

If you hate waiting, you had better go back ahead and wait for me at the port of Shiro. On the way back I will take another ship anyway.........

Please take us across without saying such things. My wife is waiting and washing the pots.

The truth is, in my flurry, I left my money behind.

Hah, do not talk such nonsense! You were cleanly outwitted by the Jiyōu and fled with all your money stolen, I suppose. Until now, basking in the light of Jiyōusama, we respected you; but now that things have come to this, who on earth would follow you? If you have no money, it cannot be helped. Hand over everything you are wearing to me, without exception. If you hesitate and stall, shall we all gang up and give you a water burial right here in this sea?

Ah, it cannot be helped. Since it is a hot country anyway, and one can manage even naked, take these and be off.

And spinning round into a state of utter nakedness, he threw them into the boat, whereupon the blackamoor cried out,

My, this is unexpectedly fine clothing! Since it was made with an excess of money anyway......... Hey, hand over that amulet bag you are wearing around your neck to me.

If the likes of you touch this, your bodies will instantly grow numb. Come on, take it.

So saying, he thrust forward his neck. The eight blackamoors shouted,

Bah, we have no use for such a terrifying thing! Take it with you as you please.

So saying, they left him behind in the shallow sea, rowed their eight-oared sculls, and sped back like an arrow across the sea surface where the purple tides drifted.

Tomohiko sank his feet into the sand, perforce dangling the amulet bag from his neck, and in a style reminiscent of a kept hound, sluggishly proceeded on all fours through the shallow sea, making his way toward the shore.
