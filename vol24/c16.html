Chapter 16: 慈 Love の涙 [746]

慈愛の涙〔746〕

§ 1

Guided by the Kototama of the seventy-five sounds, the lake of Suha shines in clarity; from the distant age of the gods, three thousand years ago, unto the dawn of the Miroku divine administration, to manifest as the Mitama of Izanagi and firmly establish the divine nation, the precious treasure hidden deep within the lakebed of Suha was entrusted by Tamayorihime, the presiding deity of the dragon palace, with the divine commission that the plum blossoms of the three thousand worlds, the Mitama of the five petals, should open all at once in the age of the pine that remains evergreen; oh, the profound awe of that awaited time! Parting the surging waves, the goddess appearing to rival jewels holds five jewels in her hands, causing the actual manifestation before the five pillars of the Miko of teachings,

admonishing the future and solemnly swearing that when the divine work is completed, she will hand them over, the five Miko reverently receive these words, forgetting them neither in dreams nor slumbers, making the sincerity of the fluttering gods their heart, gentle as sheep, facing no enemy with drawn blades, steadfastly upholding the Sango path of absolute goodness, and cultivating and nurturing the virtuous nature of yielding to others; oh, the courageous devotion of Tamajiwake, Tamanohime, and Hatsuwakahime, the fresh and wiser ones, whose noble Mitama of the divine decree, together with Kyūsuke and Otami forming a band of five, prostrate themselves in worship at the lake of Suha, performing the ablutions of seven days and seven nights while purifying both body and soul,

proceeding briskly over Ōnogahara along path-edges strewn with shimmering golden and silver sands. From ahead, fifteen robust men come running, and upon meeting the party face-to-face, they single them out, clenching their fists and beating them regardless of the place, until together they are barely breathing, biting back tears of intense vexation, forcing smiles, and speaking thus: 'Though we with impure hearts proceed upon the pure earth strewn with golden and silver sands, even as we feel shame within our hearts, from whence we know not, wishing to purify our defiled Mitama, you have applied the benevolent fists of the deep-hearted Sumeōkami without omission to our impure bodies,

and we are choked with grateful tears. Ah, people of the world, people of the world, you are true men of profound grace who polish our Mitama; ah, how grateful, how thankful we are! From this now, reforming our hearts and making good our own deficient conduct, serving as officials of the Sango divine teachings, we shall polish our Mitama until there is no cause for shame before the gods of heaven and earth or the people of the world. Ah, Kannagara kamunagara! Rejoicing in the whip of benevolence, proclaiming the divine teachings of Sumeōkami to the countries of the four quarters, we shall use this as a bookmark to dedicate our true hearts for the sake of the people of the world. The grace of a father is higher than the mountains,

the grace of a mother is deeper than the sea; inexhaustible is the grace of parents, surpassing even their true hearts of cherishing their own children, a grace more precious than which we gratefully give thanks. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if the great earth should sink, even if our own lives should be lost, when shall we ever forget the boundless gratitude of this whip of divine grace? You are no ordinary person; you must be gods who appear with the heart of Sumeōkami manifested in the waters of Lake Suha. We gratefully give thanks; ah, Kannagara kamunagara, may your Mitama flourish prosperously! The Kami-naobi who created this world,

and the Ō-naobi of vast and expansive heart—truly, in all things in the human world, look upon them with Naobi, listen anew with Naobi, and re-proclaim the errors of one's body. We of the Sango teachings, whatever may befall, abiding in Kannagara, interpret all things in a spirit of pure goodness, and without uttering a single word of resentment, we rejoice in this whip of affection and offer our profound thanks. Today, from this very body which has received the present whip of the flawless and precious divine plan, we shall whip the steeds of our hearts, chasing and scattering evil thoughts that occasionally sprout upon the mountain ridges, driving them away at every river rapid, and thereby repaying the great heart of the great deity of great compassion and great mercy.

Advance, advance, come advance! Hasting swiftly to the mountain of endurance, what are even the mountains of swords to us? Even should we walk through fire or the depths of water, if it be for the sake of the great divine path, why should we feel reluctance? Arousing the Yamato spirit of Shikishima, dedicating wondrous achievements before the august presence of Kuninotokotachi, and returning to the blessed age of the gods, let us rejoice in the auspicious day when we shall voice our celebration. Ah, Kannagara kamunagara, may your Mitama flourish prosperously!'

Thus Tamajiwake finished chanting in a low voice, and facing the fifteen men who had beaten them, the company pressed their palms together and were choked with joyful tears. Even the most ferocious villains were utterly dumbfounded by the attitude of the five, and choking with sympathetic tears, they pressed their palms together, prostrated themselves flat upon the earth, and were left without words of apology. Tamajiwake rejoiced greatly, and delivering a spirited discourse on the spot, calmly departed from the place.

§ 2

Looking back behind, in the open plain free from any obstacles, the fifteen fierce men had vanished, leaving neither shadow nor shape to be seen anywhere. Hatsuwakahime:

Hatsuwakahime: 'Everyone, who do you suppose that person was just now?'

Tamaharuwake: 'I simply cannot make any sense of Tamaharuwake. Where on earth could he have gone?'

Tamaharuwake: 'Nay, nay, that person was indeed the physical manifestation of Konohanasakuyahime who appeared upon Mount Tenkyōzan, was he not?'

Hearing this, Tamanohime raised a voice of sheer exclamation, weeping tears of joy, and:

Tamanohime: 'Ah, Kamisama, I thank You ever so much. For protecting our souls no matter where we went, for bestowing upon us throughout this present divine work such heartfelt consideration beyond all expression of gratitude, we thank You from the bottom of our hearts. Words cannot possibly express our gratitude. Thanks to Your grace, we have received just enough divine power to finally cross the steep slope of endurance.'

So saying, she sniffled and wrung out her happy tears. Tamaharuwake, sobbing and unable to utter a single word, choked with emotion as he turned toward Jitentōjima, pressed his palms together, and let his tears stream down endlessly, entirely absorbed in gratitude; Kyūsuke and Ominu likewise merely pressed their palms together and wept with heavy sobs. Hatsuwakahime:

Hatsuwakahime: 'Everyone, do you now understand the true divine love of the Great Deity?'

The company:

The company: 'Yes.'

So they replied, their tears pouring down like waterfalls and dripping from their chins. Ah, Kannagara Tamashii Sakiwamase.

Having finished offering the congratulatory prayers of gratitude, the party once again soaked their clothes with sweat in the blazing, scorching wilderness, quickening their steps and marching onward while singing the propagation hymns.

Just then, before a small shrine, a single unsightly man was praying for something, whereupon Tamaharuwake strode briskly up to him and:

Tamaharuwake: 'Pardon me, from where might you be, honorable sir? To look at you, your frame appears afflicted with illness. Where are you heading?'

As he asked, the man abruptly raised his face at Tamaharuwake's words. Upon looking, his countenance was eaten away by leprosy, his entire body exuded an indescribable and overpowering stench, and foul pus was flowing from it. Contrary to his expectations, Tamaharuwake stood rigidly rooted to the spot, rolling his eyes and gazing in silence at the man.

Man: 'I am one who dwells in the valley over yonder, suffering from this hideous disease, with not a single person to keep me company. Since my youth, having exhausted to the utmost the principle that the body is subservient to the spirit, this is the heavenly punishment for rebelling against the gods. Look... behold, just as you see, I am made a public spectacle of the world. I can no longer walk a single step... You are missionary messengers who save people, so please heal this illness. I have heard that if a woman uses her lips to suck out this pus, the disease will be completely cured. I humbly beg you to take pity and save me.'

Hatsuwakahime, smiling cheerfully:

Hatsuwakahime: 'Uncle, if sucking it will cure you, let me suck it for you.'

Before she finished speaking, she began to suck the pus at her feet with slurping sounds, spitting it out, sucking and spitting again. Following the sequence from his head, face, shoulders, and hands, Tamanohime began to suck out the pus with slurping sounds and spit it out. Tamaharuwake and Kyūsuke could not even lift their faces for the extremity of the sight, focusing their prayers within their hearts that his illness might be healed as swiftly as possible. Ominu too drew close, targeted his abdomen, and began to suck out the pus with slurping sounds. In a short while, she had completely sucked out the pus from every corner of his whole body. The man, rejoicing, pressed his palms together, squatted on the road, and was lost in hot tears. The five of them placed the man in their midst all at once, rinsed their mouths and washed their hands in the flowing stream beside them, and offered up the Amatsu Norito. The man, suddenly wearing a joyful expression:

Man: 'Ah, thank you so much. Who would ever suck such filthy matter, even if I were their own child? My gratitude is beyond all words.'

With a single bow, he immediately stood up, walked with steps as robust as a normal person, and finally vanished far away from sight. Tamaharuwake, with a countenance of deep emotion:

Tamaharuwake: 'You three honorable persons have graciously helped him. I too would have gladly performed this service if I were a woman, but since the man stated that it had to be a woman, I was reluctantly forced to hold back. Ah, I am utterly overwhelmed by your peerless divine benevolence, a devotion equal to the hearts of Kuninotokotachi no Ōkami and Kusanagi no Ōkami; my gratitude knows no bounds.'

So saying, he was once again choked with hot tears. The three of them cheerfully accepted this divine virtue as a blessing and:

The three: 'Ah, Kamisama, today we have received wondrous divine grace.'

Pressing their palms together, they offered up the congratulatory prayers of gratitude, and the band of five proceeded westward and ever westward, marching onward and singing propagation hymns along a lovely rustic path that seemed paved with gold and silver sands.

By way of note, the soul of Hatsuwakahime is the divine vow destined to manifest three hundred thousand years later as Ōmoto Foundress Deguchi Nao; from here on, the stories of the five persons propagating throughout the western region, encountering various trials, eventually guiding the entire island of Australia to the teachings of Aonisayo, and fulfilling the divine work are, for reasons of space, slated to be detailed at a later date. Ah, Kannagara Tamashii Sakiwamase.
