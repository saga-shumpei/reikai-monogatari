Chapter 14: タールス Teaching [744]

タールス教〔744〕

§ 1

Tamaharuwake, unable to comprehend this, was pushed out into the adjacent room and looked into a natural rock mirror to examine his nose, only to find that his nose was swelling up in an unsightly manner and had turned a color like a ripe purple grape.

'Ah, now I understand. With this pitch-black nose, Lady Tērusu must have said... eppaeppa... It is only natural that she also said yes, yes... Oh, what a pity... If only this nose could somehow be restored to its original state, I would tease Tomohiko once more and warn him not to become arrogant, but even the Kamisama do not seem to hear me. In the end, alas, is this a great failure? Be that as it may, after all those numerous dojin had respected me with such devotion, how can I ever show this face again? Ah, Konohanasakuyahimenomikoto residing on Tenkyōzan, I beg you to heal the blackness of this nose. Kamunagara chihahe mase, Kamunagara chihahe mase. Cherry blossoms for the bush, warriors for men, that our names be not defiled in ages to come, Konohanasakuyahime, please, oh please, through Kamunaoobi and Ōnaoobi, look upon me again and listen to me anew, and grant this Tamaharuwake a red nose for a brief while.'

He prayed with all his heart. Mysteriously, the grape-like color of his nose vanished swiftly, and it ended up becoming an even more beautiful, vivid crimson nose.

'Kamisama, thank you for granting my prayer immediately. Nevertheless, I will no longer extract grease from Tomohiko. From now on, I shall leave this place, advance ever westward, and preach to the next tribe.'

So saying, he descended leisurely to the next room. Chīchiyābōru looked at this appearance once more in astonishment,

'Bōsu bōsu, chyūchiy chyūchiy, Chīrisutan, Pōrinsu, tēku tēku, otsuotsuotsu otsuotsuotsu'

and retreated fearfully while speaking. The meaning of this was,

'Is this person a god or a demon, an incomprehensible great monster. If one is careless, who knows what kind of divine punishment might befall us... Everyone, be cautious. And all of you, make your apologies.'

Such was the meaning. The whole assembly pressed their heads to the earth at the words of Chīchiyābōru,

'Pūsū pūsū'

and apologized in unison. To say "pūsū" meant "forgiveness."

Tamaharuwake spoke whatever came into his mouth,

'Tōrinbōsu tōrinbōsu.'

At this voice, everyone, one and all, began to walk with their heads down and feet up, moving sluggishly. Losing their center of gravity, every single one of them tumbled down haphazardly back and forth, left and right.

'Kūsu, riyā, pōru, suton'

he spoke whatever came to mind. This expression had become transformed into a kamunagara state, meaning,

'Everyone, I forgive you, so please be at ease.'

The assembly cried out "Uwā uwā" in unison while tossing Tamaharuwake in the air, shouted "Essā essā" in chorus to send him off up the mountain path for about a ri, escorted him across to the other side of a single valley as though seeing off the wind god himself, and fled back in desperate haste, raising war cries. Tamaharuwake watched them go from behind,

'What is this, I cannot make head or tail of it. They acted as if they were sending off even the spirit of smallpox. It seems I frightened them quite a bit, ahahahaha... By the way, where on earth have Tamanohime and Hatsuwakihime gone? This is no laughing matter. If I do not search for their whereabouts at once, I shall have no excuse to offer.'

Lost in thought, he folded his arms and cast his eyes downward. At that moment, something came approaching with a rustling sound of footsteps. When he looked, it was a large white fox. Tamaharuwake addressed the white fox,

'Ah, you are the divine retainer of Lord Onimuhiko, Tsukiyami Myōjin, you are most welcome indeed. Please inform me of the whereabouts of Hatsuwakihime and Tamanohime.'

The white fox, remaining silent, nodded, swung its tail to the left and right two or three times, and began to walk off with a sluggish gait. Tamaharuwake followed behind the white fox, parting the small brush to the right and left amidst the wooded mountain forest, stepping over or striding across the backs of giant serpents, and followed it toward the northwest for some two or three ri. The faint sound of the voices of the Heavenly Celebration Words echoed at the bottom of the valley; when he strained his ears to listen, they sounded precisely like the voices of Hatsuwakihime and Tamanohime. Just to be sure, he closed his eyes once again to ascertain the source of the voices, and when he opened his eyes, the figure of the white fox had vanished without a trace, leaving neither shadow nor form.

§ 2

Tamaharuwake pushed his way through the grass, guided by the voice from the bottom of the ravine, and went down. Strange to say, the voice that had been heard until now stopped completely, and only the sound of water dashing against the rocks of the mountain stream was heard roaring, scattering spray like snow, and not even a shadow of a person was to be found.

Though he searched the upper and lower reaches of the mountain stream at the top of his voice, crying out,

Tamanohime-sama, Hatsuwakahime-sama

there was no response whatsoever, and in addition to the sound of the mountain stream, the growls of fierce beasts could be heard growing fiercer moment by moment. When he looked across the mountain stream, a winding giant serpent raised its neck, stuck out its tongue which seemed to be three to four shaku long, licking it back and forth, and glared at Tamaharuwake.

My, what a thick fellow. There is no doubt that the voices of the two were right here, but the giant serpent fellow must have swallowed them up. Ah, what a regretful thing to have done. Was I delayed because of the heavenly punishment for teasing Tomohiko and others for fun? If I had been even one step quicker, it would not have been that the two were swallowed away into the serpent's belly in the dark, but the teachings of Izunome say... Carelessness is a great enemy, do not be careless for even a single moment, reform yourself and reform again, do not forget God for even the breath of a single respiration, arrogance is the root of grave injuries... so it is admonished. We too went about propagating to the world as if it were a daily catchphrase... Do not be arrogant, reform yourselves... but at last it came out mechanically like a phonograph, only the words were splendid and the soul had slipped away. That is why I played out such a failure. Ah, the hateful giant serpent fellow... No, no, it is by no means the giant serpent that is bad. Its business is to swallow local creatures, it is a creature that preserves its natural lifespan by swallowing living things, so to resent the giant serpent is our misdirection, and Tamanohime-sama and Hatsuwakahime-sama also evidently lacked caution. Must I return without avenging them while seeing before me the giant serpent that swallowed the two right before my eyes? Even if I were to kill the giant serpent now and clear away my vexation, it does not mean that the lives of the two will be saved, so rather than performing unnecessary slaughter, Tamaharuwake shall imprint this practical lesson in his heart, and from now on, with shattered bones and crushed bodies, let him make his life a target and act for the sake of three people. If I do so, I suppose I will be able to comfort the hearts of the two.

So he muttered to himself as he stood by the edge of the ravine, put his hands together, offered the Amatsu Norito, administered soul pacification toward the giant serpent, and prayed with tears,

Even one moment sooner... Ōmoto Sumeru Ōkamisama, please forgive the sins of the giant serpent, and quickly cause it to be born into the human world.

And he concentrated his prayer with tears. The giant serpent let tears fall drop by drop from both eyes, bowed its head toward Tamaharuwake, expressed its gratitude, leisurely climbed the steep rocky mountain, and finally hid its long and great figure.

Suddenly, a multitude of footsteps came to be heard toward the rear. When Tamaharuwake turned around and looked unexpectedly, dozens of apes, some of them holding babies and letting them suckle milk, came advancing toward the front of Tamaharuwake. Tamaharuwake offered the Amatsu Norito with all his might. Each of the apes knelt down, put their hands together, and while saying "Kyaa kyaa", it was as if they were giving thanks. Among the apes, one surpassing all others in greatness appeared, put its hands on the ground before Tamaharuwake while bowing its head, and while forcibly carrying him upon its back, climbed up the steep path as if shooting an arrow. The multitude of apes followed from behind where Tamaharuwake was carried. At this time, a great sound echoing as if the mountains themselves were collapsing was heard, and a giant serpent with the breadth of a sword, measuring about three to four jo in circumference and perhaps fifty to sixty ken in length, with a sharp sword shining on its tail, made the mountain stream where Tamaharuwake had been sitting squarely roar with the momentum of a thousand ri, and advanced toward Mount Nelson. If there had been no help from the apes, the life of Tamaharuwake would have fallen into a plight almost impossible to calculate what would have become of it.

Shiyōjiyō carried Tamaharuwake on its back, leaping across countless valleys, and led him to a somewhat flat point on the mountainside of a certain Kauzan, setting him down heavily.

Tamaharuwake turned to Shiyōjiyō and said,

'Ah, I know not of what Kamisama's Keshin you may be, but you have graciously saved me from a perilous place. As my expression of gratitude, I shall chant the Amatsunorito.'

So saying toward the flock of Shiyōjiyō, he chanted the Sendenka and the Norito, and shot forth a Reikuō from his Shitō. Those numerous Shiyōjiyō were instantly illuminated by the Reikuō, and vanished like smoke.

While one block of Hakuen was seen rising up from there, a beautiful Megami, smiling, approached close before Tamaharuwake, applied both hands to the ground, and said,

'I am the spirit of Shiyōjiyō. Although I was painstakingly born into the form of a Ningen, I was born as such a pitiful beast incapable of even understanding words, and I was lamenting the misfortune of my body. However, having been allowed to hear the voice of the revered and noble Amatsunorito, we are reborn as Ningens by this, and will make great activities for the sake of the country. And the Hatsuzakihime-sama and Tamanohime-sama whom you are searching for are safe, do not worry. There will surely be a time when you meet them. Whatever may happen from here on, please do not worry at all.'

As soon as she spoke, her figure vanished and the Hakuen also gradually grew faint, until at last neither shadow nor form could be seen.

Tamaharuwake had been earnestly chanting the Norito. Suddenly appearing from the forest behind, fourteen or fifteen tattooed adult men, upon seeing the figure of Tamaharuwake, said,

'Utsu potsupo utsu potsupo, Hōrensu, Sāchiraisu.'

While saying this, they joined their hands and worshipped him to the ground. Tamaharuwake thought within his heart,

'...Ha-ah, again... Sāchiraisu... is it. Since my nose has turned red, I wonder if they are going to make me Tomohiko's second generation. However, no matter what kind of belle there may be, for me, an excellent wife named Okatsu, the younger sister of Kuniyowake, is waiting at home, so I cannot do such a foolish thing, and it is a blessing in disguise. Muttering thus, he said,

'I am none other than the rare divine child of the deity Izanagi no Ōkami, who resides on Mount Amenokyo, Kamiyonohime no Mikoto.'

And he pressed his red nose with his finger to show them. The crowd, saying 'Wā-wā', treated Tamaharuwake as if carrying him in the palms of their hands, lifted his body carefully without even letting his feet touch the ground, swung open a nearby rock door wide, and led him deep inside with the air of an ant pulling an earthworm.

After being carried through a tunnel for some two or three cho, just when it suddenly became bright, a large skylight had formed. Carried further inward from there, after they had advanced another four or five cho, the crowd joined their voices together and said,

'Utsu potsupo, Jianjiahiēru, Utsupatsupa.'

Saying this, they reverently set Tamaharuwake down in a slightly wider place, and a man named Chiruteru, who seemed to be the boss among them, grasped Tamaharuwake's hand, pushed open a nearby rock door, and led him into the interior.

It was a grotto unexpectedly wide, and large leaves, seven or eight times the size of banana leaves, were thickly spread out in a scale-like pattern. The blue, withered fragrance of the tree leaves drifted with an indescribable mood.

'Hōrensu, Sāchiraisu, Utsutatsuta utsutatsuta, Kārisu Kārisu.'

Thales, with the utmost respect, guided Tamaharuwake to the deepest and most supreme chamber, brought forth rare fruits to entertain him, and from the bottom of his heart, gave thanks for the descent of an ikigami. The residents west of Mount Nelson had from ancient times firmly believed that a saviour would descend from heaven and save all people in both spirit and body. This was the same in the village of Jiana. This place was a village called Annahieru.

At this time Tamanohime and Hatsuwakahime came advancing into the village of Annahieru while singing propagation songs. Chirtel and several tens of villagers below him had gathered beneath the great tree of Anna, intending to harvest the fruits of the fruit trees. Gathering together, they forcibly made captives of the two maidens and carried them into the cave of Thales.

"What kind of woman are you? What do you think this place is? Thus, a fellow whose face was like a demon and tattooed pitch-black surrounded them from front, back, left, and right.

"Ho-ho-ho-ho! Just two delicate women, while several tens of big men gather together and carry us off like ants dragging a cicada's dead body into a hole, and you spoke so very kindly to us! So saying, they arched their eyebrows sharply.

Tamanohime-sama, no matter what is said, even if you were to be killed, you must never yield to such a thing. You already have a fine husband known as Wakahiko-sama.

Without needing to be told, even if my life were to be taken, I shall never yield to such a difficult demand, so please be at ease.

Hey you, little wretch, what kind of evil cunning are you plotting? Impudent in the extreme... Who do you think you are taking me for? So saying, aiming at Hatsuwakahime, he attempts to inflict a demon's bracken-root club from above her head.

Starting with Chiltel, everyone grew loud, threatening and teasing in various ways, saying, 'Become the wife of the Tars cult leader.' Chiltel, thinking this would never end, ran into the inner room, placed both hands on the floor before Tars, and spoke:

Just now, two lovely women have wandered into this village; they called themselves Tamanohime and Hatsuwakahime, and they are truly beautiful women. They are a match made in heaven to be your wives, and thinking it would be fine to let the younger one amuse herself as a concubine for now, I brought them into the cave. However, they are quite stubborn, not listening to a word we say, and replying with their chins—such wayward persons, how shall we deal with them?

Hearing this, Tamajiwake made his heart leap with a start, but he held back with an unconcerned demeanor.

What, two beautiful women have come? Bring them into this place at once. I shall examine whether there is a karmic connection, and that without a moment's delay.

He urged them on. Saying 'Hai,' Chiltel left this place and spoke:

Now Tamanohime, Hatsuwakahime, today is the descent of the deity Hanafakasama, and the leader is in a terrible good humor. That you have come thither must be some sort of karmic connection. At any rate, for the sake of an interview, follow behind Chiltel.

Hai, thank you. Then let us go. ...Hatsuwakahime-san, you too, together.

Taking Hatsuwakahime's hand, she followed behind Chiltel and was led into the leader's living room. The moment Tamajiwake saw the faces of the two, he was about to say 'Ah,' but restrained himself and stopped. Tamanohime and Hatsuwakahime saw the figure of Tamajiwake and rejoiced in their hearts as if they had met the Savior. Yet, pretending there was something amiss, they deliberately kept their faces downcast with feigned ignorance. Tars turned toward Tamajiwake and said:

Orens, Sārachiraisu, Atsutatuta, today you have graciously descended from afar out of the heavenly realms, and while the villagers of Anahieru are dancing with joy and blessing universal peace, once more such peerless beauties of this land, and two of them at that, have come hither. This must entirely be the matchmaking of the gods. How would it be if I made them my wives?

Īesu īesu, ētarusu ētarusu, etsuputsupa, pātsuku pātsuku, etsuputsupa.

He spoke thus. Tars scratched his head vigorously and once again said:

That may well be, but might there be some way you could grant your permission? If it is possible, I should like to make her my dwelling's wife. Moreover, I am resolved to raise the younger one carefully as my own daughter and finish her as a splendid priest of the Tars cult, so I earnestly beg for your permission.

This woman is a heavenly maiden who has descended from Takamanohara, and is not something for mortals to meddle with. Should there by any remote chance be an error that touches the divine will, your life shall be seized away immediately.

Putting strength into his voice, he rebuked him. Tars was struck by the awe of those stern words, involuntarily lowered his head, and supplicated:

Henceforth I shall never speak of such a thing again, so I beg of you, please forgive me.

Tamajiwake suppressed his inner amusement and stealthily glanced into the faces of the two women with an unseeing air. The eyes of both women were simultaneously fixed upon Tamajiwake's two eyes.

From this time onward, Tamajiwake thoroughly mastered the language of this village, expounded and instructed them in the teachings of Misoshiru-kyō, finished Tars into a splendid priest, and Chiltel likewise became a priest, causing every single person of the village of Anahieru to submit to the way of the Great Deity. After staying for two or three months, the three of them departed from this village, heading northwest and proceeding along the mountains while singing propaganda songs. For seven or eight ri, Chiltel and everyone else saw them off, and here they bade a sorrowful farewell amidst tears.
