Chapter 15: 諏訪 Lake [745]

諏訪湖〔745〕

§ 1

Tamaharuwake, together with Hatsuwakahime and Tamanohime, designated the Tars village of Ananai-hēru as a sacred site of Ananai-kyō, brought all the blackamoors into complete kijiyun, and being seen off by dozens of people led by Chirtuteru, parted sleeves at the entrance of the village of Iruna, and with cries of 'Uwā-uwā' disappeared in the east and west.

The three crossed valleys innumerable times, sat down upon a broad flat rock amidst the forest, and while resting, indulged in a retrospective chat. Tamaharuwake recounted, with amusing vividness, such episodes as his meeting with Terusu-hime, the high priestess of the Jianyanna religion, at the bottom of the Jianyanna valley, and his playful teasing of Tomohiko; then, as he set out from here to arrive at the hamlet of Ananai-hēru, he caught the sound of the voices of the two women reciting norito, descended into the valley, and while searching about for their traces on all sides, he encountered a giant serpent, was rescued by a troop of shōjō, and finally carried into the head temple of the Tars religion in Ananai-hēru, where, upon receiving unexpected hospitality, he told in broad outline the miraculous tale of his meeting with Hatsuwakahime and Tamanohime.

Tamanohime quietly spoke thus: 'When I was performing misogi in a certain valley and raising my voice in norito, Onimuhiko appeared from a nearby rock cave together with the white foxes Tsukiji and Asahi, and kindly took both our sleeves in their mouths and drew us into the depth of the cave. Wondering what on earth this meant, I plunged my body into the cave as I was being dragged, and while softly chanting the missionary hymn, a winding giant serpent appeared right opposite the valley stream in front of the cave where I was lurking, thrust out its long tongue which seemed to be three or four shaku long, and glared at the cave taking aim, yet overawed by the divine virtue of Onimuhiko and his followers, it could not even approach and glared for a while. At that time, you called out our names as your own voice. Before I could utter a single word, agonizingly thinking what a frustrating thing it was as I struggled, a sound arose as though the mountains themselves were collapsing, and two giant serpents, husband and wife as they seemed, with the circumference of their bodies reaching three or fourjō and a length of several dozen shaku, with their blade-like skin, making sharp swords flash at the tips of their tails, filled the valley stream as they passed by—the terror of that moment, even now when I think of it, makes my flesh crawl. Fortunately, the figures of the white foxes instantly vanished and the four quarters grew serene, so I crawled out of the cavern to meet you and searched the surroundings, but your figure was not to be seen at all, and feeling unbearably anxious, thinking whether something might have been done to you by that giant serpent, and wondering if perhaps you might be lurking somewhere nearby, just as I was passing by while purposely singing the missionary hymn in a loud voice, Chirtuteru and many other people of the Tars religion suddenly carried both of us away and brought us to that cavern, where I had a miraculous reunion with you, finally escaped danger, and furthermore, was able to preach the sacred Way of the Gods and bring everyone into kijiyun—for all of this, as the divine protection of the Supreme Deity of Ananai-kyō, I am now once again drowned in tears of gratitude... Ah, Kamunagara Tamachihauhase.' As she joined her palms thus, Hatsuwakahime also clasped her small hands and was drowned in tears of gratitude.

Just as they were talking thus, a piercing shriek that cut off one's breath was heard. Hearing this sound, the three involuntarily raised their hips and listened with strained ears; right at the bottom of the valley, a winding giant serpent was in the very midst of coiling tightly around a male and female pair and about to swallow them with its huge mouth wide open. The moment Tamaharuwake saw this, he dashed headlong through the thickets where wild grasses grew luxuriantly, and drawing near to look at this state of affairs, he immediately intoned the Amatsu Norito with swift lips, chanted aloud the Heavenly Counting Song, thrust out his index finger with a shout of 'Un', and emitted five-colored divine light, radiating it upon the giant serpent. The giant serpent instantly dissolved away into scattered fragments and stretched itself out in that place as if a log of timber had been felled. The two had already lost consciousness, and with the breath of a dying insect, were only faintly making their chest areas palpitate. In the meantime, Tamanohime and Hatsuwakahime came running in pursuit, and the three, combining their strength, fetched valley water, blew it upon their faces, made them rinse their mouths, administered various kinds of nursing, and chanted the Heavenly Counting Song to perform the divine work of soul-restoration; whereupon they instantly breathed back to life, and the two joined their palms—

'Whoever you may be, I know not, but you have saved us well from a perilous place. This favor of yours I shall never forget even if I die,' she said, and gave thanks together with tears. Tamaharuwake, raising a high-pitched voice, called out, 'Yay, are you not... Kiusuke-san, Otami-san? It was a dangerous matter.' The husband and wife lifted their faces with a start, and Kiusuke said, 'Yay, you are Tamaharuwake-sama, Tamanohime-sama, Hatsuwakahime-sama, you have come so well. Blown about by the fierce wind from the summit of Mount Nelson, each of us scattered in all directions, and until this very moment I have worried over what might have become of you. In this vast Riugūjima, even if we searched for three or five years, once separated, it should be utterly impossible to meet again; yet, by good fortune, to encounter you in such a place is entirely the divine arrangement of Kamisama. Ah, how grateful, how awe-inspiring!' Once again the five of them together chanted the Amatsu Norito with cool voices. The two giant snakes also slowly began to stir from their tails, gradually increasing their vigor, raising their proud necks, and shedding tears from both eyes as if apologizing to the five. Tamaharuwake addressed the snakes, 'Oi, oi, Great Serpent Teacher, by what karma were you born in such a form? How pitiful you seem, I shall chant the Kamigoto for you so that you may quickly be reborn as humans.' The male and female snakes aligned their heads, lowering them repeatedly to express their gratitude. The five chanted the Norito innumerable times. The snakes suddenly turned into white smoke, making for the vast sky, slender and winding, becoming clouds and vanishing into mid-air. This was entirely due to the virtue of Tamaharuwake and his party chanting the Amatsu Norito with complete sincerity and devotion, whereby the snakes were saved into the heavenly realm.

The party of five walked onward through the mountains of Iruna, singing propaganda songs while hurting their feet, tearing their straw sandals, and going barefoot, aiming to spread the message to the dwellings of the natives. Kiusuke, nursing Hatsuwakahime, carried her on his back and followed from the very rear.

From ahead, a group of several tens of rough men, their faces covered all over with unpleasant tattoos, appeared at this place, glaring with angry eyes and surrounding the five in a scattered manner. To the left was a sheer precipice, and in the abyss of a thousand fathoms, a blue-green torrent flowed, kicking up foam. At the moment when the five, whose advance and retreat were equally blocked, were troubled as to what they should do, Hatsuwakahime, carried on Kiusuke's back, said, 'Tamaharuwake-dono, step forward.' Tamaharuwake stepped to the front and strode right up before the rough men. The leader of the rough men was named Tamaru. Upon seeing Tamaharuwake's red nose, Tamaru was greatly astonished, abruptly altered his attitude, hurled his weapon away onto the ground, knelt down joining both hands, and expressed his respect by saying, 'Ōrensu, sāchiraisu, utsuhotsupo utsuhotsupo, atsutatsutā atsutatsutā.' At this even more reverent attitude, all of them threw down their implements, knelt upon the ground, and in one voice repeated, 'Ōrensu, sāchiraisu,' expressing their reverence.

Tamaharuwake proclaimed, 'āmē āmē, obeying the command of Kotobunwake no Mikoto, the leader of Mikkyō who has appeared in Jitentōshima, I have traveled far across the seas for the sole purpose of proclaiming the gospel of God to this island. From this time forward, believe in my path, become the beloved children of God, and flourish both spiritually and physically for ever and ever without end. The gates of heaven are opened, the time for the fulfillment of divine rule has arrived, repent!' Although these words were completely incomprehensible to Tamaru and the others because the languages did not match, believing wholeheartedly in the descent of the precious savior, they joyfully followed behind. Carrying the five on the backs of the big men along the precipitous path, they led them to a vast wilderness where golden sand was scattered all over the ground. This place, known as Andeo, was a vast and expansive plain, and numerous dwellings resembling human homes lined up, forming a small city. Setting the five down in front of the shrine of the Dragon God worshipped by the natives, they clapped their hands in joy, and the whole company offered up some kind of prayer deep in their hearts.

Behind the shrine, a boundless expanse of lake water appears in the shape of a lotus, brimming with deep blue waves. Waterbirds float, submerge, and playfully swim to and fro, sometimes flapping their wings as they run about wildly on the water surface in sheer delight. Having finished offering the Amatsunorito, the company stood entranced by the scenery of this lake, resting their breath for a short while. Tamaharuwake stood still in front of the small shrine,

Tamaharuwake: 'Setting forth from Onokorojima, wishing to transmit the teachings of Kami, we darted about through the Nanyōshiyotau, until we finally arrived at a single island of Riugū. Just as we advanced as far as the castle town of the capital of Izanagi, we found ourselves unable to gauge the hearts of the Princess of the Mukade and the Princess of the Ōriyō. As part of the divine Shigumi, ten pillar-like children of the teachings climbed up such cloud-veiled mountain slopes, stepped across the mountain ridges, paused upon the summit of Mount Nelson, and gazed out over the four quarters, feeling deeply moved by the spirit of grandeur. At that very moment, enveloped in black clouds rising from the mountain flank, the ten members of our party were surrounded, unable to discern even an inch ahead and falling into utter perplexity.

While we were in the midst of chanting the Amatsunorito at the top of our lungs, we were swept away by an unprecedented and violent gale, each of us whirled up into the midheaven like leaves of a tree, hurled down into a nameless deep valley, and brought to the verge of losing our breath. Yet, drenched in the dews of blessing of the Great Deity of Izanago, we gradually revived our breath. As for the swarms of giant serpents winding here and there, chanting the great spell of the Amatsunorito and the heavenly counting songs, we guided and pacified them until at last, escorted by numerous people, we came and beheld this place for the very first time. The deep blue surface of the lake suddenly parted in a cross shape, and the lakebed was clearly revealed. It was almost as if golden boards had been laid out, without a single pebble, without a speck of dust, without a single blade of waterweed. It was just like water contained in a golden pot, pure and radiating a brilliant radiance, of a solemn and exquisite beauty that dazzled the eyes. Through the gaps in the waves, the ridges of the golden palaces and jade towers dimly visible were truly yearning to behold, making hearts leap and souls fly away and scatter, while red coral trees stood in rows like a forest. Passing beneath the great trees of coral, appearing with serene steps, were numerous goddesses whose jade faces possessed the eyebrows of the moon, resembling the blossoming posture of plum blossoms, begonia blossoms, or simply peonies just beginning to bloom; draped in golden-colored garments and wearing golden dragon crowns, they thrust long-handled Tang fans in their left hands in place of scepter staves, while holding jade cups in their right hands, and causing five especially sublime goddesses to each hold the five-colored jade of heaven, fire, water, earth, and connection, Tamayorihime-no-Mikoto slowly ascended the lake, appeared before the five people, and spoke with tranquil words:

You are Hatsuwakihime, Tamanohime, Tamayoriwake, the believer Kyūsuke, and Otami—these five pillars. That you have bravely overcome trials and tribulations, endured hardships, and come from afar to this place for the fulfillment of the divine nation exceeds all praise. However, you, Hatsuwakihime, by the special will of ōkami, have been made to participate in the divine work of the wish-fulfilling jewel of adamant indestructibility, and Tamanohime has been charged with the divine service of the purple precious jewel, and now the Anana teachings as a whole have become the target of envy. Although Tamayorihime and the other two have not yet served in such momentous divine work, admiring your utmost sincerity and truth, we bestow upon you five the five kinds of treasures that are the divine treasures of the dragon palace. You shall further purify your body and mind above this, reverently carry them back to the Palace of Brocade, and hand them over to the Kyōshu Kotoyoriwake-no-Mikoto. 『汝は初稚姫、玉能姫、玉治別、信徒の久助、お民の五柱、よくも艱難を凌ぎ辛苦に堪へ、神国成就の為に遥々此処に来りしこと感賞するに余りあり。併し乍ら汝初稚姫は大神よりの特別の思召しを以て、金剛不壊の如意宝珠の神業に参加せしめられ、又玉能姫は紫の宝玉の御用を仰せ付けられ、今や三五教挙つて羨望の的となり居れり。玉治別外二人は未だ斯の如き重大なる神業には奉仕せざれども、汝等が至誠至実の行ひに賞で、竜宮の神宝たる五種の宝を汝等五人に授くれば、汝等尚も此上に心身を清らかにし、錦の宮に捧持し帰り、教主言依別命にお渡し申すべし。今汝に授くるは易けれど、未だ一つ島の宣伝を終へざれば、暫く我等が手に預りおかむ。華々しき功名手柄を現はし、重大なる神業を神より命ぜらるるは尤もなりと、一般人より承認さるる迄誠を尽せ。此一つ島はネルソン山を区域として東西に別れ、東部は三五教の宣伝使黄竜姫守護し居れども、未だ西部に宣伝する身魂なし。汝等五人は此処に七日七夜の御禊を修し、此島を宣伝して普く世人を救ひ、大蛇の霊を善道に蘇へらせ、且黄竜姫、梅子姫、蜈蚣姫其他一同の者を心の底より汝の誠に帰順せしめたる上にて改めて汝の手に渡さむ。初稚姫には紫の玉、玉治別には青色の玉、玉能姫には紅色の玉、久助には水色、お民には黄色の玉を相渡すべし。されど此神業を仕損じなば、今の妾の誓ひは取消すべければ、忍耐に忍耐を重ねて、人群万類愛善を命の綱と頼み、苟且にも妬み、そねみ、怒りの心を発するな。妾はこれにて暫く竜の宮居に帰り時を待たむ。いざさらば……』

と言ひ残し、数多の侍女神を随へ、忽ち巨大なる竜体となりて、一度にドツと飛び込み玉へば、十字形に割れたる湖面は元の如くに治まり、山岳の如き浪は立ち狂ひ、巨大の水柱は天に沖するかと許り思はれた。五人は感謝の涙に暮れつつも、恭しく拍手をなし、天津祝詞や神言を奏上し、天の数歌を十度唱へ、宣伝歌を声張り上げて歌ひ終り、再び拍手し、それより七日七夜湖水に御禊を修し、諏訪の湖面に向つて合掌し、皇神に暇を請ひ、宣伝歌を歌ひ乍ら、荊棘茂れる森林の、大蛇猛獣の群居る中を物ともせず、神を力に誠を杖に進み行くこそ雄々しけれ。あゝ惟神霊幸倍坐世。

玉依姫は空色の衣服にて、玉を持てる五人の女神の後に付添ひ玉ひしと聞く。
