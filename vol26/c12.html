Chapter 12: Autumn の色 [777]

秋の色〔777〕

§ 1

The foundation of the pine's age of the gods, so felicitously established, deep in the mountains of Ashibiki where deer trample the momiji leaves and cry with clear voices, forms the autumn sky woven into brocade by Sahohime; the sky of Kumoi is exceedingly high, and the stream of Wachi flows murmuringly, while beating the Kototamatsuzumi, how sublime is the celebration of the age of the gods!

From the very beginning of the opening of Tenchi, the two pillars of Kinriu and Ginriu solidified the muddy Unabara drifting like kurage, dividing and establishing the ocean and the land, bringing forth yamakahakusaki, and fully and minutely opening Utsushiyo, in close attendance upon the divine decree of the god Kuniharutachi, Toyokunihimenōomikami, while causing the brocade loom of Izuku and Mizu of Ananahi to be woven, felicitously and peacefully opened the age of the gods, when there appeared in the Garden of Eden Adaru no Hiko and Ebahime.

In that interval where their souls grew confused and day by day weakened unto exhaustion through the ugly deeds of body-first and spirit-following, Yamata no Orochi, ugly foxes, and crooked demons stole in; Tokoyohiko and Tokoyohime, the two pillars who beclouded and disturbed the Tenchi of the land of eternity, supporting Shiromanagahiko, strove with heart and body, with boundless stratagems, to grasp the Mizuhonokuni of Toyoashihara within the hands of the ugly demons, and without end in their ugly fury, of Hi no Shiranui.

The myriad deities born upon the earth, facing toward the divine government opened by the Great Deity of infinite benevolence and mercy, the Great Deity Kuniharutachi, turned the spears of the ugly ones, making Tokoyohiko their mastermind, and attacked with all their might, until it came to pass that Tenchi grew pitch-dark and it was an age where the Magatsu-hi raged; the Great Deity Kuniharutachi ascended to the Hinowakamiya of the divine land in the heavenly august skies, and having grandly proclaimed the rampant fury of the ugly ones that filled the great ocean expanse, atoned for all the hundred sins and transgressions without leaving one, and casting his body into the crater of the mountain of divine instruction, for the sake of mortals he wandered through Ne no Kuni and Soko no Kuni.

How sublime is he who, changing his name to Nodate no Hiko, stealthily and ever stealthily guarded the world! Toyokunihime also, yearning after the footsteps of her august spouse, sank deep beneath the waves of the Naruto of Awa, reached as far as Ne no Kuni and Soko no Kuni, changed her form to Nodate no Hime, and appearing once more upon the earth, uniting the fire and water of husband and wife, desiring to save a hundred divine beings within her heart of boundless benevolence and mercy, appeared beneath the golden mountain, and as various and sundry forms altering her shape, leaving behind the capital of pearls, upon the island of the gods floating on the waves, at the center of Jitentōjima.

Hiding her precious august form in the sacred site of the roots of the lower rocks where blue mountains encircle the four quarters, she endures ten thousand sufferings to wipe away the crookedness of this world, and awaits the peaceful age of the gods of the pine's age; upon the lotus stage of Okubusuyama, at the foot of the four-peaked mountain likened to Mount Carmel, changing his form to Kunitakehiko and appearing as Kotoyoriwake, at the time when he caused the precious loom of patterned brocade to be woven, beginning with the golden jewel sent forth from Seigunzan, even the island of Oki which becomes the wooden clogs of the Great Deity Kuniharutachi, the precious treasure kept hidden, the adamantine indestructible Nyoi Hōshu.

Appearing once again in the sacred domain, where divine virtue flourishes day by day, upon Takahurayama, enshrouded behind the shield of Alpine teachings, the purple jewel which Takaoirihime had guarded also gathered as the god willed, the august treasures of Takamanohara—the three august spirits of divine power, force, and body—gathering here, the opening of the precious governance of the divine realm, the gods of Tenchi leap up in joy, and the myriad common people, bathing in the grace of Izuku, the teachings established by the god of Ananahi flourish day by day; the palace of brocade glitters and shines with the beauty of the morning sun, and once more, deep within the lakebed of Suwa, the one island of Ryūgū, the Mani jewel kept hidden, through the arrangements of Tamayorihime, felicitously enshrined in the sacred domain, the four peaks where divine virtue shines add the color of gold, and the mechanism of the loom clearly appears, while the believers gathered in the eight-fathom halls lined up in the palace of brocade, beginning with the august decree of Kotoyoriwake, leap up in joy, and without distinction of old and young, men and women, line up in the sacred domain of Aya to welcome the jewel with such high-spirited valor; ah, how truly divine, how divine, the august arrangements of the precious gods, the Mani jewel safely guarded before and behind, left and right, by pure and righteous people, was built into the eight-fathom hall.

§ 2

Having entered the sacred Hōza, such a precedent, ever since the opening of the heavenly Ihato in times of Hisakata down to the present day, is indeed revered; it is known as the auspicious omen of the Kamikuni that governs the Sekai. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara, may your Mitama bless and prosper us.

The Kamuzukasa of the brocade palace, Tsukihiko and the austere Mikoto of Tamateruhiko, together with the auspicious Mikoto of Tamateruhime, guided joyfully by Otama no Kata, came forth into the eight-fathomed hall. A retinue including the noble Mikoto of the princess Umeko and Hatsuwakahime, having come from afar across the golden island to welcome and escort the jewel of Mani, stood radiant with exceptionally graceful sacred countenances to praise their achievements. Making their way through the throngs of men and women, old and young, standing shoulder to shoulder until the space was crowded, they stood side by side upon a stage elevated a step higher. How sublime is their divine form! The three sacred jewels and the five sacred jewels stand alongside those precious gems,

The austere Mikoto of Tamateruhiko, competing in brilliance with the auspicious Mikoto of Tamateruhime, as the Kamuzukasa, with Otama no Kata added hereto, now three mitama here, Tamajiwake and Tamanohime, adding hereto five mitama in this place, the divine teaching of the three-five moon shines clearly across every corner of the Sekai, dispersing the darkness of Tokoyo. The precious and austere divine work is felt as clear and tangible as the bright autumn sky of the eighth day of the ninth month. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara, may your Mitama bless and prosper us, like the loom of the warp and the woof, the sacred pillars of the brocade palace, the beautiful Tamateruhiko and the exceedingly pure Tamateruhime,

The austere Mitama shines across heaven and earth, and its crimson heart of autumn foliage illuminates without a shadow the auspicious land of Ashihara no Mizuho in its sublimity. The two Tamateruhide Kamuzukasa opened with a swift motion the jeweled palanquin they had escorted hither, and taking out one after another the willow boxes deeply wrapping the Mani jewel, handed them into the jade hands of Kotoeriwake. With what reverence they received them, and how sacredly they enshrined each jewel box in the inner sanctuary! The gods of heaven sprang forth with courage, hundreds of believers rejoiced together, the sky was high and the wind pure, and human hearts were bright and serene like the goddess of peace.

Now the ceremony of enshrining the jewels here was successfully concluded as well. Through the divine words of Kotoeriwake, starting with Tamateruhiko, needless to say the divine officers who served the Mani jewel, the Puritans of the Sanjukyō, old and young alike without distinction, without discrimination between men and women, offered to the Sumeru Ōkami rare divine sake, sacred food, and delicacies gathered from mountains, fields, seas, and rivers. Opening their hearts at the banquet mat of naorai, they celebrated this auspicious sign, rejoicing, singing, dancing, and making merry, until the autumn of the seichi became as though heaven itself had begun to open. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara, may your Mitama bless and prosper us, determined not to let this joy stray outward,

Opening their golden and silver fans wide, they raised the songs of the heavenly counting numbers, and their appearance of frolicking like golden and silver butterflies in a spring field was a scenery that could not be painted even in a picture.

From the residence of Akiyamahiko at the port of Yura, the five Mani jewels, which had been enshrined in a sacred boat and welcomed, were handed over to the Kyōshu with the assistance of Tamateruhiko, Tamateruhime, and Otama no Kata. Kotoeriwake no Mikoto respectfully revered them and came to enshrine them one by one in the inner sanctuary of the brocade palace. Thereafter, they partook of the delicious foods of the mountains, fields, rivers, and seas that had been offered as divine shinsen, prepared sake, side dishes, and various other treats, and everyone partook of them to exhaust their utmost joy, praising the divine virtues of Ōkami as each returned to their own dwelling.
