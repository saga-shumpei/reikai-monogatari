§ 4

Rejoicing in the grace bestowed upon me,

Not keeping it to myself, but placing it into my humble hands,

Showing the achievements passed over to me,

To build them up, as indicated by the divine will of Tamaharuwake,

In a world where jewels are fought over,

Without even a shadow of attachment in my heart,

Bright as the sun and moon,

Those very soul-spirits,

Tears of gratitude cannot be held back,

Gratitude suddenly stirs waves in the sea of my innermost heart,

The sea of love where it was hard to advance,

The sincere heart of Tamaharuwake,

Will surely be praised by the gods of heaven and earth.

I am the lowly Koitohime,

Blessed by the dew of grace,

Now a joyful sendenshi,

Though I have become a minister of the gods,

How can a person of impure mind

Fully exhaust their sincerity?

Without even reflecting upon such a soul-spirit,

Yielding to me without stint the precious divine work of the jewel,

Such a pure heart, wherever else in the world

One might search to the ends,

How could it be like the usual idle tears,

Drifting and floating on a solitary island,

A lonely person without a husband,

O Tamaharuwake, kamuzukasa!

I am in the painful darkness of love,

May the light of the jewel appear,

And illuminate the solemn path of vows between sister and brother,

Without regarding the sacred presences

Of Kamususanōnōokami

And Kunitakehikonōokami,

Pouring out the fullness of my heart,

I pray repeatedly and reverently.

The Mani jewel bestowed by Ōriyōhime

Not keeping it for myself alone,

Along with the divine ministers Takahime,

Takayamahiko, and Kurohime, who are the ministers of ananahi,

Letting all participate together in this unprecedented, sacred divine work,

Removing and casting away all barriers of the heart,

Illuminating the teachings of the Ananahi-kyō

Upon the earth where the sun and moon shine brightly,

Shine upon us, Izutama,

Before the presence of Mizutama,

Ōriyōhime offers her sincere heart

And prays with reverence.

Ah, kannagara kamunagara,

May our august spirits prosper and bless us,

As the divine Izanagi no Ōmikami

And the divine Izanami no Ōmikami

Turn around the pillar of heaven to the right and to the left

For a thousand and eight thousand reigns,

Just as they vowed,

Binding the covenant of sister and brother,

Matching the breath of husband and wife to spread the divine teachings in all lands,

We shall serve without bodily weariness.

Grant this, O sendenshi and kamuzukasa of Tamaharuwake,

Opening the depths of my heart,

I pray completely and in detail.

Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over,

Whether the moon waxes or wanes,

Even if the great earth should sink,

The path of sister and brother vowed before the presence of the gods

Shall remain unchanged forever, like a pine-tree age,

Imbuing eight thousand reigns into the divine will of the gods,

I pray reverently.

Ah, kannagara kamunagara,

May our august spirits prosper and bless us.'

Thus, blending celebration, delight, and romantic longing into one, she finished chanting the fullness of her heart and returned to her seat. Tamaharuwake was somewhat perplexed, and standing up immediately to respond to the song of Ōriyōhime, he once again opened his silver fan and began to chant quietly in words:

'Helped by the grace of the gods,

Bearing the name Tamaharuwake,

Now as a precious sendenshi,

Seeking to spread the teachings of Ananahi-kyō

To the four quarters of heaven and earth,

Crossing mountain ridges, crossing rivers,

Without wearying of the eight hundred paths of the tide,

Advancing and advancing, we landed on a solitary island in the Dragon Palace,

With a heart as pure as the waters of Lake Suwa,

Receiving the Mani jewel from the august deity Tamayorihime,

I govern the castle of earth's grace,

Handing it over to the jeweled hands of Ōriyōhime

To manifest the divine achievements before the presence of the gods,

Without distinction between high and low,

And to illuminate the path of the teachings,

Tamaharu, exerting his heart,

Wishes to offer his pure soul-spirit,

And how precious it is that you speak of binding the path of sister and brother!

Nevertheless, the separate august command of Tamaharu

Is that in former times,

Appearing in the village of Utsuyama,

He made Princess Okatsu, who is the younger sister of Kuniyoriwake, his wife,

As a husband and wife together, living in harmony,

Being persons who serve in the divine work of the gods.

Though the sincere heart of Ōriyōhime

Fills me, Tamaharuwake, with boundless gratitude,

The established august rule of the Imperial Ancestral Deities concerning monogamy

Is something I have no way to break.

When I think of the state of mind of my wife's august spirit,

Like the young grass left behind in the province,

It is all the more pitiful.

Even at the time when I was scorned as a clumsy peasant of Utsuyama village,

Without regarding my lowly person,

With the precious august lineage of the gods,

She served me—that precious wife.

Hear completely and in detail

That I have only one person in my own body,

And today only, regarding this matter alone,

Please release it from your heart.

Thinking of your person and thinking of the person of my wife,

Tamaharuwake no Kami,

Drinking in your pure heart,

You must surely not become angry.

Ah, kannagara kamunagara,

If I had two lives,

I would take you as my bride too, and live in harmony just as before

With Okatsu no Kata,

Serving together—please drink in this heart of mine, Ōriyōhime.

Before the presence of Kamususanōnōmikami

And Kunitakehiko,

Making my sincere heart clear,

Cruelly sweeping away the thoughts of your person

Into the winds of Shinato,

O kamuzukasa Ōriyōhime,

Tamaharuwake will not cast away your painful state of mind as if it were an enemy,

With a sincere heart that does not think of you as an enemy,

Looking upon it straight with naohi, listening to it straight,

Re-proclaiming it evermore to the furthest limits,

May you be granted a husband's august command far purer than mine.

Ah, kannagara kamunagara,

Tamaharu makes his sincere heart clear

Before the presence of the gods.'

Thus, since his wife Okatsu was in Utsuyama village and engaged in serving the divine work, he expressed before the gods that, although he appreciated the noble lady's heart, it was utterly impossible for them to become husband and wife. Thereupon, the clouds of romantic longing cleared away from Ōriyōhime, and she came to serve zealously in the divine work.
