Chapter 11: 言の Waves [776]

言の波〔776〕

§ 1

Akihiko found his ship at last approaching the sacred land, whereupon his vigor waxed ever more buoyant; bringing fully into play the inspiring mood of the vice-guardian deity, he began to chant:

The mountains of Yotsuo now come in sight, The stream of Wachi flows eternally, Unto the pure teachings along the Shirasegaha And the village of Ikuta he quickly crosses o'er. Without a single tidings, the waters of Otonase As well flow pure, the river of Yura; And famed in name upon the port of Yura Are Akiyamahiko and Momijihime. Akihiko, once called a deer, Following the lead of Kotoyoriwake, To welcome the Mani jewel, As he comes sailing down the current and looks about, Behold the unexpected Mizumitama, Taking solemn ukehi upon the riverbed of Yasu, Manifesting the pure and stainless heart, The Savior, Grand Deity Kami-Susanoo-no-Ōkami, Whose sacred countenance is surpassing fair, And the height of sublimity with tears of tender love Overflowing full upon His visage!

Deep within the mountains of Yotsuo, Enduring the trials of this mortal world the while, Awaiting the divine age here, The august soul of Kunitakehiko Appears like curling smoke, And hiding in the mansion of autumn mountains Where maple leaves shine bright, From the very beginning of distant antiquity, The secret homeland of the Isle of Gold, Deep within the bed of Lake Suwa, Hiding and waiting for the age of gods, The august spirit of Tamayorihime, With the Mani jewel safe and sound, Escorted by Yatagarasu, Purely crossing the heavenly expanse of sky, To the center of the land of Hizuma, Known as an outer enclosure, Descending like a dove upon the port of Yura, Firmly laying the foundation of the age of gods!

Ah, kannagara kamigami, Blessed by the good fortune of the august spirits, While washing away defiled souls in the pure waters of the Wachi, How noble it is to have participated In an unprecedented divine work! When I reflect upon that former time, Together with my elder brother Komahiko, Following Murasakihime, Leaving the flower capital far behind, Intending to worship at the precious throne Of Mizu-no-Manai, where Toyokunihime eternally Lies tranquil in settlement, Three masters and servants crossed the mountains, Advancing while parting the grass, Until reaching just this side of the Fuko Pass, At the moment when the three masters and servants advanced, Attending upon the Brahmin teachings, The guardian deity of the mountain of Mitake, Of fearsome name, the demon falcon,

Lacking all mercy, the rough falcon, Lured by the deity of crookedness Magatsu-no-Kami, Together with Murasakihime, Captured within the ugly cavern, Where progress became hopelessly blocked, At the very moment when they were troubled about their path ahead, The propaganda envoys of Izumo-Shinshū, Beginning with Princess Etsuko, Both Otoniko and Kamehiko, Charging right into the interior of the cavern, United their breath with the Tanshū of the divine avatar and rescued them, And how noble it was that the three there Smoothly and brightly threw open To the utmost the rock door of the heart of Izumo! Together with the people of Izumo-Shinshū, Traversing the mountain ridges of Mitake, Advancing toward Onigajō, Where Princess Mukade was secluded, Commencing the Kototama warfare,

Chasing and scattering the priests of the Brahmin teachings Beyond the clouds, Then hurrying onward to the sacred land, Intending to transmit the Great Way of the Gods, Advancing toward the mansion Of Princess Matsu of Mount Takagi, Had the flower of endurance bloomed at last And pleased the august eyes of the gods? Bestowed with the name Akihiko, An ever more noble propaganda envoy, West and east, north and south, Transmitting the divine teachings, The divine hall of Ikuta where Grand Deity Wakahimegimi-no-Ōkami is enshrined, At the very time when he was clearly transmitting The teachings of the three august spirits, Kuniyoriwake and Komahiko, To the people seeking the Way, Having wandered in search of jewels As far as the dragon palace isle of the Southern Seas,

The retinue led by Takahime-sama Came on a visit, and Kunisama, Komahiko, and Akihiko, all three, Faced yet again that tiresome jewel dispute; Judging it offhand and declaring out-of-bounds That deep beneath the shrine building On the isle of Chikubu in the Ōmi road The precious jewel was hidden away— Taking that falsehood for the absolute truth, Takahime-sama and her company, Beginning with Takayamahiko and Kurohime, Advanced without a moment's delay. Ah, kannagara kamigami, That Kuniyoriwake and Akihiko Should have proclaimed and transmitted such deceit Unbefitting their hearts as a crooked deed— Viewing it straightway in the direct light, listening anew, Proclaiming it an unavoidable matter, Pardon us, Great Deity who guards The Way of Izumo!

Before the august presence of the noble deities Haniyasuhiko and Haniyasuhime, We hold ourselves in awe, revere, and humbly apologize. Ah, kannagara kamigami, May the fortune of the august spirits prosper and flourish!'

Thus he finished chanting.

Amidst the cheers, the jeweled divine boat at last safely arrived and anchored at the southern shore of the beach of Yoshimi.

Led by Kotoyoriwake-no-Mikoto, followed in orderly sequence by Isokohime, Umekohime, Hatsuwakihime, Tamanohime, Tamajiwake, Kōryūhime, and Princess Mukade, they caused numerous believers who came forth to welcome them from the Yahiro-den of the Brocade Palace to bear the portable shrine, and keeping their ranks orderly and sedate, guarded before and behind by subtle music, they solemnly and quietly returned to the Brocade Palace.

§ 2

Bent with age, Natsuhiko's feet scarcely touched the ground for very joy, and like a plover he went his way to and fro, declaring the broad road too narrow, waving his hands and shaking his head, while he loudly hummed a song of celebration and returned homeward.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, May the divine spirits be blessed and prosper, To cleanse heaven and earth, the divine teachings Of the Ananahi sacred light Now is the time to shine in all four directions, Beginning with the three wish-granting jewels, And now once more the five Mani jewels, Weaving upon the loom of brocade According to the divine arrangement of warp and weft, Worshipping the true Kami anew In the Palace of Brocade, How truly reverend to be firmly established therein! With a heart pure and red, Akiyamahiko, By the true heart of the divine command, Shines brilliantly, and Momijihime Embodies the severe spirit of the Yamato heart; The Sumeōkami, examining in detail The hearts of husband and wife, Bestowing a peerless divine work forever, To spread the glory in all four directions, Kamususanōnōomikami, The severe spirit of Kunitakehiko Appears once more in the mansion, To firmly establish the great teachings of the Ananahi moon Upon a foundation enduring as rock; Upon the great divine road, Ikohime, With teaching flowers fragrant, Opening all at once, Umejikohime, Like the first tender buds of flowers, Princess of youth and maidenhood, Tamanohime, Whose jewel light waxes ever brighter, Manifesting as Tamajiwake, And the divine majesty of Terēsuhime, With the divine office of Kōryuhime, Even unto the nose-tip of the centipede maiden and Tomohiko, Crimson with a red-hearted palace service, Like Kusuke of Akashi lighting the dark night, Crossing the waves of ten thousand miles of ocean, Together with his wife Otami, Serving with heroic devotion In a peerless divine work! The flowering spring has quickly passed, And Natsuhiko of the fervent heart, Rejoicing and celebrating today's auspicious birthday, For a thousand and eight thousand ages, the foundation Of the divine teachings of Ananahi Remains ever firm and unshakable, Flourishing and prospering like eight mulberry branches Opening and extending day by day, Advance, O Kamunagara! Reverently before the divine presence, We celebrate today's joy: Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, May the divine spirits be blessed and prosper!

Thus he finished singing.

Urged on by Natsuhiko's song, Tsunehiko began to drone in a strange tone.

Weary of Kurohime of the Uranai teachings, Saved by the true path of Ananahi, Tsunehiko, who burns no agarwood and passes no wind, And knows no teachings, Serving close beside the Palace of Brocade, Morning and evening, Passing his days in quiet submission, Counting on his fingers the prosperity of the Ananahi teachings, Waiting for the age of pines as though it were slow to come, The wish-granting jewels of the three divine spirits Enshrined in the sacred land of Aya, The light of the teachings shining day by day In all four directions—how auspicious! Accumulating joy and piling up radiance Within the spacious Yahiropalace, Where nine kinds of flowers bloom and shed fragrance, And the ten beautiful worlds come round, Beyond all thought, from the Dragon Palace, The five divine spirits' Mani wish-granting jewels, Hatsuwakahime, Tamajiwake, And the train of divine messengers Working through pure spirits, Soaring high above Lake Suwa, Escorted by divine messengers, Pressing the clouds and moving majestically, Returning as they shine across the waters! Today's auspicious birthday indeed Sees the Miroku divine administration fulfilled, As though Heaven and Earth and the Pure Land Had appeared right before our eyes. All people, O all people, Atsujikami, Kunitsukami, And the deities of a hundred offices, With hearts red before their presences, Reverence and give thanks! The three jewels that appeared before, Awe-struck by the divine arrangement, Chosen by Kotoyoriwake, The instructions of the august spirit of Muzu, Serving before Tamanohime And Hatsuwakahime, Amidst the abundance of believers, Takahime, Takayamahiko, and Kurohime, Their fires of jealousy unquenched, Their hearts burning, east and west, To the very ends of the southern ocean, Searching for jewels with no destination known— If we hear that after their departure, The truly precious Mani wish-granting jewel of the Dragon Palace Appears and is enshrined At the seat of Kotoyoriwake's divine office, How troubled in their innermost hearts Takahime's feelings must be! Even now it is painful to imagine. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, May the divine spirits be blessed and prosper, And while the higher and lower ranks of Ananahi Entrust their hearts to Kami, Living in harmony and affection for ever and ever, Rejoicing before the great altar of Ito, With calm and peaceful minds, Blowing away the clouds and mists of the mind's sky With the precious divine water and fire, Build the foundation firm and enduring as rock, O Kamunagara! We pray before the divine presence.

Sadatahiko began to sing once more.

At the summit of Mount Chiyōjiyō, following the divine will, Sadahiko, who served in an unprecedented divine task, to save the perilous lives of Hatsuwakahime and Tamanohime, at the foot of the roaring waterfall, fought a fierce battle with the Brahman priestess, the centipede princess, and while rescuing the two divine messengers, Hatsuwakahime and Tamanohime, together with Haruhiko, returned once more to... and before the presence of... offered the three sacred jewels; here, finally, having been re-proclaimed as Sadahiko, the leader of the path at the vanguard, Takikō, although it is said to be the beginning of summer,

given the name Haruhiko, Hatsuwakahime, Tamanohime, and the party of four, with reverence, rowed their small boat to the island of... floating upon the waves of the sea of life and death in this world and the next, worshipped... and though they came, as they had earnestly wished, bound by the strict commandments of Kami, without knowing the hiding place of... they rowed back once more; yet, nevertheless, because... made... having clearly ascertained that it was indeed here, though they knew it, due to the exceedingly strict commandments of Sumekami, for three hundred thousand years into the future, to keep it as... Takahime-sama and Kurohime, with burning hearts, from near and far,

running about like demons to search for the whereabouts of the three sacred jewels, searching for jewels in a place they knew not, knowing it was a pity, they divided various reasons and arguments and spoke, but Takahime, taking the Hinode Kami as her shield, and subsequently Kurohime, championing the Otohime of Ryūgū, insisted that unless the lineage's mitama receive the three sacred jewels, the perfect and flawless Pine Age and the Miroku divine administration cannot be accomplished; if Sadahiko says he will not speak, well, it is fine even if he does not speak, Takahime, with the divine power of the Hinode Kami, will find them and show you, she boasted with fierce pride, crossing over the waves of the myriad leagues, to search and go wherever it may be, how pitiful her inner heart is.

Though she desperately wished to let them know where the whereabouts of the jewels were to give them peace of mind, the teachings of... could not be disobeyed after all; ah, wondrous Kami, wondrous Kami! Once more, through the divine arrangement of Kami, while Takahime-sama was away, the Maniboju of Ryūgū Island settled into the exalted Takama, and if one hears that, starting with Umekohime, Hatsuwakahime and Tamanohime were once again serving in the precious divine task, then Hinode Kami too and the Otohime of Ryūgū certainly must have been stunned, and struck dumb with astonishment; like an owl that missed its evening meal, with a perplexing look right before one's eyes, thought of as if seeing it now, it is a pitiful state of affairs.

If Takahime returns now, Hatsuwakahime and Tamanohime will surely turn to face her and a battle will undoubtedly begin; I wish for the restoration of peace to come to the sacred ground even for a moment; searching for the three sacred jewels or the five sacred jewels because she wants them, Takahime's heart is somehow like a deflated ramune drink, a blank, spiritless expression, not only lacking flavor or substance, but whoever drinks it finds it watery; after having made her suffer such a vague, dreary fate, grasping the teacher's lapels, squeezing out a piercing shriek, she will surely raise a commotion; Sadahiko is so worried about this that not even for a single night has he ever fallen peacefully asleep; compared to the three mitama,

there is a difference as vast as heaven and earth; as for the Mani jewel of Ryūgū Island, if at least one were shared with Takahime to give her a share of the credit, the matter would probably be settled peacefully; Ikamorowake-sama is young and lacks quick wit; if I were Ikamorowake, this time I would point out some credit for Takahime and Kurohime; if that were done, Takahime and Kurohime too, not knowing where to dance with their hands and tread with their feet, would untie the strings of their faces and turn into plump, smiling Otafuku masks; why is it that such karma always turns toward the worse path? When one thinks of this, the clouds of Takahime's attachment do not clear away, and she wanders astray into a dark path of her own accord, surely drifting away

from the most important of important divine tasks; with just the attitude of one's heart, one is summoned to the service of good or undertakes the service of evil; the trio of travelers utterly lost in the two paths of good and evil, right and wrong; even if they resigned themselves to the thought that the three mitama were inevitably a matter of karma, and having resolved themselves thus, after crossing far away and living for a long time on the Ryūgū Island where the Maniboju was hidden, without searching for the whereabouts of the jewels, and returning home thereafter, the appearance of the five jewels in the ironic providence of Kami will surely make them drop their jaws in astonishment; when one thinks and thinks on it, it is so pitiful; however it may be, without incident, if Takahime-sama should hear of it...
