Chapter 14: Great 変 Song [779]

大変歌〔779〕

§ 1

As a night storm blows from time to time, the surface of the lake has high waves, and from the roots of the old trees on the island, with a force to blow them down, the shrine built with ancient dignity begins to make a strange sound, creaking as it is shaken by the wind.

Taking this as good fortune, Kamehiko opens the doors of the shrine, slowly comes down the stairs, and approaching close to the side of Tamayatsuko, whose soul has been drawn out from her jewel, he covers her from the head with a white robe in the blowing wind, letting her long sleeves flutter.

Even the voice is that of a Megami, gently proclaiming with deep interest, 'I am Konohanahimenokami who appeared upon Tenkyōzan.'

Grasping that divine intent, I explain and point out the whereabouts of the jewel to you three deluded ones. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, with the Mitama thriving and flourishing, Ananai-kyō or Brahmanism, whichever it may be, sendenshi, three of you appearing here, taking the declaration of a Takagami, who does not even possess you, to be the truth, making a long journey with great effort, parting the darkness over the waves, rowing to Chikubushima where the three Mitama are pacified, and being entangled by a worthless stubbornness to search for hidden divine treasures—how foolish!

Hanatakahime, or the dark Kurohime of the pitch-black heart, or the Fukurokuju mask with a shining head—all assembled are great fools searching for the jewel under the shrine. Even if you dug a hundred zhang, down to the golden wheel abyss, there is probably no cause for that jewel to come out. You boasted that it was the living palace of Hinodeshin and Otohime of Ryūgū, but what an attitude! You may think a Megami utters harsh things, but do not think for a moment that the Megami is speaking; it is the fukusumori ghost possessing the three hearts that speaks.

Rather than engaging in needless trouble, reflect upon your own conduct, give up the quest for the jewel, and as soon as possible repent and serve the true path of the Ōkami in the world. Takahime was the first to arrive.

The Kurohime who appeared next, mistaking it for the jewel-digging kami sent by Kotoyoriwake, not wishing to be outdone, scraped with dark clouds to the very tips of her fingers, digging the sand like a tiny otyobo, and at last grinding miso in a mortar with the sand of false joy. No matter how long you dig, O-mae, nothing hidden will come out. The husband of Takayamahiko, trailing after the old grandmother's backside, bearing sixty masks, how easily you were deceived by the Takagami! Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, only incomprehensible fellows! If such persons sit in the executive posts of the teachings of Ananai, then indeed the sacred place will be in a great uproar of earthly and heavenly anomalies.

Kamehiko dotsukoi, riding upon the back of a turtle, floating amidst the waves, receiving the mikokoro of Konohanahime, I appear to protect the whereabouts of the jewel, for I am the true Megami. Since the three jewels are the divine administration of the Kami, no matter how much of a Hinodeshin Takahime may claim to be, she has no reason to appear. Abandon such rebellion, return to the sacred land of Ananai's ayame at the earliest moment, and offer apologies to the Kami.

On the autumn sky of September eighth, the treasure of Tamayori-hime in the single island of Lake Suwa where golden flowers bloom, the Mani jewel bound with heaven, fire, water, and earth, at Yura Harbor,

descending like a dove into the garden of Akiyamahiko, beginning with Kotoyoriwake, Umeko no Hime, Isago no Hime, Tamano Hime whom you dislike, and Hatsuwaka-hime all together, placed upon a mikoshi leisurely, ascending the rapids of the Yura River, joyous tidings in the moon of chrysanthemums, today on the ninth at the Yahiro-den at the foot of the mountains of Shio, it is indeed the day to be safely enshrined. If you too dawdle and delay, it will be the tenth-day chrysanthemum after the festival, and you will have to heap shame upon shame.

If you return straightway to the sacred ground from the mansion of Ikuta no Mori, you could have participated auspiciously in an unprecedented grand celebration, revered the Mani jewel of five colors,

and served at the margins of noble divine work; yet, driven by attachment, completely deceived by a Takagami who does not even possess you, coming from afar to search—what a pitiable state of blind kami. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara,

if you think that is impossible, return at once. Tamamori-hime, out of kindness, reveals a moment of sincerity. Gradually the wind has grown stronger; staying here forever, blown by the storm, is unbearable. Untoko dotsukoi Takahime-sama, yatotsuko dotsukoi Kurohime-sama, Takayamahiko no Fukurokuju-sama, in that case we take our leave. Dotsukoishiyo no dotsukoishiyo

Takayamahiko, growing sullen and letting steam rise from his Yakuwanatama-like head, raised his harsh voice incessantly: 'Taking Takahime-sama, Kurohime, the Hinodewake, and Otohime-sama of Riugū as your shields, you made a fool of a man who fastens a loincloth twelve feet long.

I had thought from the beginning that what you were saying was suspicious, but I never imagined you were such fools.

You have smeared mud on a man's face, caused a disgrace from which there is no turning back, and as for the Hinodewake, is there even such a thing? My buttocks are so astounded not even a fart comes out.

Though I hate to take an old hag like you as a wife, I heard that the precious Otohime of Riugū is a palace of flesh,

and through the mediation of Takahime-sama, coming all the way from Fusa aboard the heavenly ship of Torifune high across the sky was a foolish thing to do.

Covering your white heads thickly with black juice to deceive, like flowers blooming proudly upon a withered tree, had I known it would come to this, I would never have wedded you.

Lately, neither the Hinodewake nor Otohime-sama of Riugū can be relied upon at all.

Enticed by attachment, running across countries and islands to search for the whereabouts of the jewel, the foolishness of you two hags—

I have utterly lost all patience with you.

Driven into this plight by having your eyebrows shaved by the young men's possessions of Kuniyoriwake and Akihiko,

how can we ever return to the sacred ground?

Down to women and children, everyone who sees my face makes a fool of me.

That things have come to this is entirely the work of you two, Takahime and Kurohime.

Ah, kannagara kamukamu!

Let the inquiry into the jewel end today; completely abandon all thought, return to a sincere heart, serve well with reverence to the august words of the divine masters of the Izumo Shinpo, the divine beings of Tamateruhiko and Tamateruhime, and on no account bring forth your ego.'

Takayamahiko, putting his whole heart into it, cautions the two: 'Ah, kannagara kamukamu!

It is no use staying forever on this island where God resides.

Enduring the shame with a masked face,

at any rate, returning to the sacred ground, from the bottom of our hearts apologizing profoundly before the divine presence for all our misunderstandings and arrogance until now,

if we expose our utter shame, it may serve as at least some atonement for our sins.

If you hate that, Takahime, and Kurohime my wife, shall I cleanly write and hand you a divorce letter of three-and-a-half lines as of today?

Because you made a fool of a man until now, divine punishment instantly retaliated, and we have met with such misery.

It is good to reform; when the gates of Takamagahara open and one grows arrogant, things like this happen, and a state of affairs comes to pass where we cannot show our faces to the people of the world.

From today, completely washing our hearts clean, kannagara,

you should return to a pure and innocent heart.

Come, come, let us return, let us return!

Though the blowing wind be strong, though the high waves rage however fiercely,

taking the great and boundless benevolence of the Ōkami as our strength and relying upon it as our staff, let us return.

Ah, kannagara kamukamu!

Mitama sachihau mashimase yo.'
