Chapter 15: 諭詩の Song [780]

諭詩の歌〔780〕

§ 1

Takahime and Kurohime, both of them, together with Takayamahiko, prostrating themselves and worshiping the divine shrine, with flushed faces and in dejected spirits, went to the shore where the boat they had ridden in was moored, and when they looked out, the eastern sky was crimson, and its beauty flashed upon the waves.

Was it the work done by the Riujinsama lurking at the bottom of the lake? Mizukukimoji appeared here and there as 'a-o-u-e-i', 'ka-ko-ku-ke-ki', 'sa-so-su-se-shi'.

The three of them leaped into the boat, manipulated the oars, and gracefully floating upon the waves, came rowing back.

The lake water parted in two, and the appearance of the great dragon that emerged was especially solemn, with scales of gold and the surface of a sword.

Raising clouds, the awe-inspiring sight of it rising suddenly into the vast sky. While the three looked up, striking the sky, the appearance of the great dragon straightway vanished into the clouds.

The resonant echoes of cool breezes, and the distant, profound, and exquisite music came floating from nowhere.

The four quarters were suddenly enveloped in a sweet fragrance, and feeling their hearts revived and purified as they advanced the boat, at that very moment, lotus flowers fluttered down like snow, and before their eyes saw them accumulating thickly in the boat of the three, the lotus flowers somehow changed their forms and became the beautiful Goddess of Peace.

The three joined their palms here, wondering if it was the welcoming descent of Amitabha Buddha striving for the Pure Land, or the coming of Maitreya Bodhisattva, or the appearance of Konohanahime, or simply the Otohime of the Dragon Palace.

Reverencing her from the bottom of their awe-inspiring appearance and heartfelt core, they involuntarily lowered their heads, and the goddess, with gentle words, transmitted the divine oracle gracefully toward the three.

Precious and rare Onshigumi, Keeping the heart level and peaceful, Returning to the high Takama, Repenting and making the proof Of Magokoro, O Mitsumitama! The moon of Sango shines brightly, High rise the ridgepoles of the brocade palace, Sparkling across flash the emblems of Toeu, Worshiping it as the light of Kami, Do not let your heart be disturbed. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! May the Mitama be blessed and prosperous, Pleasantly parting the clouds and mist into the Ōzora, Before long, the noble Misugata of Kami Vanished away—oh, what a marvel! Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! May the Mitama be blessed and prosperous.

The lotus of Tenmeikaigo, Leaving Tenkyōzan behind, Wishing to illuminate the hearts of the three, The Onkami Konohanahime's Deeply invested Mioshihe— Yet to Takahime, whose clouds of Shifuchiyakushin Are not easily cleared away, What is Konohanahime so great about? Compared to the Hinode no Kami or the Otohime Of Riugū, She is not even worth counting. With contradictions overflowing like preaching sutras to Shiyaka, Though others may not know it, how could Takahime Ever listen to such a Norigoto? There is a limit to making a fool of someone. Konohanahime's words— That she, Takahime, a gentle Mitama, Could listen to them even now, Does she think her so? How pathetic!

As one of the lineage of Hensei Danshi, Takahime, who has revered the Hinode no Kami To the very depths of the golden nether hell, Is no such muddy spirit! The diamond-indestructible Nyoi Hōju, Hidden away wherever it may be, How could she fail to hunt it down and find it? Konohanahime, the male-and-female deity, Flew over from Mount Fuji, And with the three persons chattered endlessly, Spitting out words whose meaning nobody knew, And fled like clouds vanishing into mist. That unsightly posture was truly A pitiful sight to behold.

Kurohime-san, and Takayamahiko, Lord Fukrokuju with the long head, Do not lose your wits over such matters, And you must not change your hearts! Endure to the very bitter end.

Until the lineage of Hensei Danshi, Together with the Hinode no Kami, Gloriously comes forth into the open, Do not leave my side. Though whether you wish to hear or not The cheeky backtalk of Takahime the leader, I know not, Yet even the brushing of sleeves is a bond of many lives, And even a stumbling stone is the edge of affinity. Once you board the friendship boat of the same teaching, waves are bound to rise, Record-breaking winds will also blow, And sometimes you are bound to crash into reefs, Failing to escape shipwreck. If you are scared of that, can the teaching of the Sango Kami ever unfold? You must not lose your nerve And have your knees give out under you! Kurohime of the weak heart, And the friendship boat of Takayamahiko, Somehow show no enthusiasm at all.

While being blown by the Hiei mountain gale, Looking up at Mount Ibuki, Crossing the lake surface where wild waves rage, How brave to return! No matter what may happen, As long as the living palace of the Hinode no Kami Exists in this world, Even if demons appear, there is nothing to fear! Feeling as if you have an iron club for a demon And are stepping aboard a great ship, Follow along with me no matter where! Now, though Kotoyoriwake no Kami, Surrounded by the mob and myriad things, Boasts such a mighty momentum That he could even shoot down flying birds, The proud Heike shall not last long. Cherry blossoms are never things That remain forever on the twigs. Once a storm blows, Scattered blossoms lie like a flower mat.

Trampled and crushed under the feet of the elevated, Perishing away, Understanding this principle of the present, Though I am now but a winter tree, Spring will soon come when the flowers bloom. If you come putting your all into Magokoro, Looking forward to my rise to prominence, Takahime will not cast you aside as an enemy; She will use you heavily and rely upon you As her vital, indispensable right arm. No matter what anyone says, it's a matter of lineage; There is no one who can match Takahime-sama. How is it that such a clear-cut principle Cannot get through to clever you, my dear? It is a mystery to me. Within the depths of Kami, there are deeper depths, And within those depths, still further depths. How is it that the half-moon Appears in the fifteen skies? Though worldly mortals talk of all sorts of Rationalizations only,

The divine teaching of the moon of Sango— If you take away the living palace of the Hinode no Kami, it is nothing but a half-moon, you see! Wrapped deeply in clouds, Half of it is right here. Rejoicing in the half-moon, Starting with Kotoyoriwake, With Mokusuke and his daughter, Tamanohime, Kuniyoriwake, and Akihiko— That incomprehensible clique of executives— When Takahime returns before long, They will be struck dumb with astonishment, fall flat on their rear ends, And go 'Ah-hun!' in shock. Never let your spirits rot; Clasping hands happily as husband and wife, Whether it be the living palace of Riugū-sama Or whether it is different or not different at all, Stand firm by your initial words And do not lose your nerve halfway through.

Takayama-sama is a fine man, yet once again he whimurs, clouding the heart of Takahime like a weakling.

Kurohime-sama, from now on, saying yes and nodding to everything without heeding the old man's words—do offer a bit of counsel.

Thinking overmuch of men as precious, the path of the Gods has grown somewhat neglected.

As this Takahime looks on, it is naught but sights that weary the eyes, and at last it grows exasperating.

By a wife's single resolve, a man may be shaped into anything.

Because you show him a sweet face, Takayama-sama throws a tantrum and causes even Takahime distress.

Do pay heed just a little.

Can the path of the Gods be trodden so easily with mere trifles?

The Hinodewake takes heed.

My counsel and the eggplant's blossoms are never false in a thousand;

You must not hear them in vain.

Though flowers bloom in sevenfold, eightfold, and ninefold layers, into fruitless stray blossoms where the yamabuki bears no yield,

losing your wits, to Kotoyoriwake's high-collared-mikoto floral heart,

to the fleeting stray blossom whose prime is short,

you must not shift your heart.

The plum blossom of long toil, enduring well the winter's cold,

morning of snow or evening of frost,

enduring and waiting for spring,

surpassing all flowers, the fragrance of the blossom that has wafted forth

is surpassed by none, not even the words of Takahime, higher than the heavens.

O Kurohime-sama, the pine blossom blooming on Takayama's peak,

a high ambition with no means to pluck it,

give it up entirely, and to the living palace of Takahime's Hinodewake

entrust your heart and yield your body,

and foolish things like last night's

you must never speak of again.

A mood like preaching to a three-year-old child comes over me and I feel quite helpless.

O Kurohime, whom I counted upon, at Takayama-hiko's weak-kneedness

I too puffed out my breath for a moment.

Fortunately, here upon the lake, with no people about, seeing all is clear, I set forth the truth.

Ah, Kaminagara Kamigami, may your august spirits be blessed and prosperous.

At length, the boat safely arrived at the shores of Ōtsu. The two, Āru and Ēsu, gazing out over the lake shore, were waiting impatiently for Takayama-hiko's return.

Both Āru and Ēsu, having waited long upon the lake shore, stretching their necks for Takayama-hiko's return,

thither cutting through the raging waves, Takahime with a frightening face at last returns hither.

Both Āru and Ēsu instantly run up close to her side:

'Is it you, Takahime-sama? How auspicious!

Where have you placed the wish-granting jewel, adamantine and indestructible?

Fundamentally, there is not even a shadow of it anywhere.

In all likelihood, you must have swallowed the treasure whole.

No wonder your belly is bulging.

Come, come, let us return quickly.

I too have waited long and wearily.

Kurohime-sama, having obtained the precious jewel of gold,

where have you hidden it away in your wanderings?

Takayama-sama too, having successfully obtained the purple jewel, must have returned.

Come, come, everyone gathering together, crossing over Ausakayama,

sailing up the rapids of the Yodo River,

Arashiyama and the Hozu Valley,

the divine majesty's Oi River too,

crossing over Kannon Pass,

pointing toward the sacred ground, let us return.

Ah, Kaminagara Kamigami, with the blessing of the divine august spirit, there is no joy greater than this.

Attending upon Takayama-sama, the reward of having followed along is that even my shoulders grow broader.'

Both Āru and Ēsu, soaring up to the patterned high heaven, as the personal attendants of the Hinodewake,

praised as fine gods, blooming and fragrant as the flowers of the sacred ground—

thinking of it, thinking of it, how grateful, how unworthy, they prostrate themselves in worship.

The pathos of that appearance—

Takahime had no words to reply and remained silent, but Kurohime suddenly stepped forward:

'You are Āru and Ēru-sama,

within the divine depths there are yet deeper depths;

it is not something for you to know.

Do not trouble yourselves with meddling.

I cannot tell you the reasons.

If you persist in asking, it makes me angry.

Not speaking surpasses speaking by far.

Let us speak no words; because words were spoken, the father became the human pillar of Nagara.

Had the pheasant not cried, it would not have been shot.

You just keep silent.

When the Kami appears in the outer world,

establishing and separating good and evil,

the Kami Naobi who created this world,

the great Naobi whose heart is vast as well—

just let Kurohime be, like the cord of a haori, right at your breast.

Follow me without saying a single thing.

Ah, Kaminagara Kamigami, may your august spirits be blessed and prosperous.

§ 4

Floating upon the waves, Chikubujima, the temporary sanctuary where Kamususanōnōokami once concealed Himself, and Hidekohime who protects it, together with Kamehiko, the Kamuzukasa of Ananai-kyō, paid homage at the shrine of the goddess Benten, and desiring to head toward the sacred land of Aya, they manipulated the oars upon the boat.

While being blown by the Ibukioroshi wind, their hearts soared to Takamahara as they rowed across, gazing at Okinoshima on their left, while the waves of the lake of Biha ran high, and the wind suddenly blew fiercely, making the boat as if it were about to capsize, at that very moment, an entity of fire descending to illuminate the sky transformed into a magnificent deity, appearing before the two in the boat, and proclaimed the Kototama in a clear voice—

Upon doing so, oh wondrous marvel, the waves instantly calmed all around, the raging wind suddenly subsided, and the surface of the lake, like a mirror, gliding forward was truly courageous; the divine person who had now appeared was the avatar of Hinodewake, and no sooner had they arrived at the shore of Ōtsu than He vanished like smoke, returning to parts unknown; Hidekohime prostrated herself in worship, giving thanks for the divine grace, and accompanied by Kamehiko, while lamenting the parting from the pines of Karasaki, crossing the barrier road of Ōsaka to meet the deity of Miroku at Miii-dera temple, traversing Ōtani, and before they knew it, Fushimi and Momoyama were left behind as they advanced along the outskirts of the capital of flowers.

Crossing Mount Oeda past the barrier station of Katsuragihara where the two mitama had caused their floating reputations to be known, and their shoes worn on their feet, seeing Koyasu Jizō on the right, descending the pass of Nashinoki where sins and impurities are shed, crossing Mitsumazaka, the night dawned and grew bright yet passing through the place named Kuramiyagoe, passing Shinomura where green grasses thrive, Hiromichi, Umanohari, Kashiwabara, and gazing at the sacred mountain of Takakumayama on their left, as they walked softly along the current of the Ōi River, though lacking wings they reached Toba station, swiftly crossing the streams of clear Murogahara, Oyama, and Matsubara, and reaching the flower-filled Sonobe and Komugiyama, they paused to rest their breath at the summit of Kannon Pass, which bears the name of the thirty-three manifestations, as a pair of travelers.

Passing Shuchi, Gamounō, and Hiyama, crossing the pass of Enokikareki, a place linked to the deities, descending the valley paths of Ōhara, Taitō, and Shuchiwa, the two auspiciously arrived at Namimatsu of transcendent scenic beauty; the current of the Kogumogawa was clear, the ancient pines of the avenue deep in color casting their shadows upon the water, fish leaped in the treetops of the pines, and seeing the great bridge of Aya on the right, they finally arrived at the sacred land; ah, Kamagara, Kami, Kami, may your mitama prosper and be blessed.
