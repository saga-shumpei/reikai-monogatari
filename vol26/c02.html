Chapter 2: The Episode of Truth 心の Flower (Part I) [767]

真心の花（一）〔767〕

§ 1

The safe arrival of the Mani Jewel of the Riugū of Tenkuwasuichimusubi at the mansion of Akiyamahiko, along with the solemn festival combining joy and gratitude, concluded safely; the banquet of Nahorahi was grandly held, and it was finally decided that the five Shinpō should be sent forth toward the Seichi, ascending the Yuragaha in a vibrant manner. Regarding this, everyone went forth to the river mouth at the port of Yura to perform Misogiharahi, returned once more to the Shinzen to conduct the festival, constructed a magnificent Mikoshi, enshrined the jewel within it, and proceeded to head up toward the Seichi, stemming the current and causing the wind to fill sails of gold and silver.

Hereupon, everyone resolved to stand and sing and dance in celebration of the safe arrival of the jewel. First of all, Akiyamahiko stood up, bowed once to Kamususanōnomikoto and Kunitakehikonomikoto, obtained permission, spread golden folding fans in both hands, robed himself in the garments of a propaganda emissary, and leisurely began to sing and dance in the center of the hall.

Though years there are many, Though months there are many, Though days of birth and plenty abound, What auspicious day is today? The autumn sky of the eighth day of the ninth month, The mountains on all four sides putting on autumn foliage, The weaving of brocade by Sahohime, Before our eyes the mechanism of the loom, Erecting thick pillar-posts reaching the high heaven, Residing eternally and settled, The strict Mikoto of Kuninotachinotokotachi, The divine spirit of Toyokunihime, The living spirit of the auspicious Mikoto, Appearing as Kunitakehiko and Kototamawake, The honorable Mikoto, Revealing the workings of the inner and outer divine realms, Whereby the plan is faintly seen herein, The Mani Jewel of Riugū Island, Bound together with Tenkuwasuichimusubi, Has entered mine own mansion. Ah, Kannagara kamugamishi, May your august spirits prosper and bless us, Not once alone, but twice, The divine pillar of the three august spirits, Deep indeed is the supreme grace Of the Great Deity, Kami-Susanō no Ōkami, Staying within my mansion, The profound and immeasurable divine grand plan, The color of the heart is Momogaminohime, The scarlet Yamato spirit, Today's sky where it begins to shine, Ah, how grateful, how grateful, In the deep grace of the sea of Yura, At the river mouth of the pure current, Purifying a hundred sins and defilements, Entrusting oneself to the new august vessel, In which the precious jewel-box, ever purer, Has enshrined the five august jewels, While hoisting the golden true sails In the heart-refreshing divine wind.

How noble to send them to the Seichi, The plum blossoms of the three thousand worlds, Opening all at once, the age of the pine-tree gods drawing near, Without limit within and without the seas, Known is the auspicious sign Illumining firmly and enduringly the grace of the auspicious august spirit. Ah, Kannagara kamugamishi, May your august spirits prosper and bless us, Susanō, who descended from afar across the land of Persia, The Great Deity of the auspicious august spirit, Deeply hidden in the mountains of Shio, Waiting for the time, Before Kunitakehiko, Even the curtain of the heart is the sincere heart Of the humble man Akiyamahiko, Reverently celebrating. Ah, Kannagara kamugamishi, May your august spirits prosper and bless us.

Momogaminohime stood up once again,

The horses of moon and sun are exceedingly swift, When I reflect, full three years have passed, When on the eighth day of the chrysanthemum month of the Shin-yu year, Susanō no Ōkami came forth to my mansion, Reverently adoring his noble august shadow, Momogaminohime whose heart is scarlet, Exerting her body to the utmost of sincerity, There is worth in having served him, The joy filling heaven and earth Revolves once more on today's sky Of the ninth month and eighth day, the cyclical day of Kinoe-ne, Glad tidings of the chrysanthemum month, This festival whose fragrance is charming, Nyorai-hōshu, diamond-indestructible, From the ancient days of the divine age, Hidden deep within the rocks Of Kutsujima drifting on the waves, Akiyamahiko, the front entrance, who was entrusted With the mystic key,

Having had the golden key safely stolen away by Takahimetsume, And everyone having scorched their hearts with anxiety, Hastening at last to the island, Discovering the perilous place, And returning to my mansion in the company of Takahimetsume-dono, Swallowing it suddenly into her belly And escaping like clouds and mist, Recalling that old tale, Until this jewel of Mani Safely arrives at the Seichi, People, with all your hearts and minds, Be attentive, be watchful, and send it forth, As Takahimetsume, morning and evening, Is fascinated by the jewel, Should there be an opening in the fleeting pace of the running horse, Will she swallow it again into her belly And what sort of deed will she perpetrate?

An unfathomable, momentous affair. Ah, Kannagara kamugamishi, May your august spirits prosper and bless us, Deeply grasping and serving the sincere heart Distributed by Susanō no Ōkami To save and succor heaven and earth, Before the august presences of Hatsuwakahimetsume, Tamanohimetsume, Tamajitawake, and other One hundred officials, The old woman's heart of Momogaminohime Humbly lays bare a mere fraction. Ah, Kannagara kamugamishi, May your august spirits prosper and bless us.

Having thus sung and finished her dance, Hatsuwakahime stood up once more, spread out her golden fan, and sang while dancing herself.

'In the ancient days of the distant Kamiyo, from the holy water and fire of the Sun Ōkami, the divine Mikoto of Wakahimegimi was born into the world. Thinking to govern the lands of the four quarters under heaven as a peaceful country, with utmost devotion of heart and body, in the divine Miwaza, morning and evening, she served for a time, yet caught and broken by the vile spirits of the eighty Magatsu, she and her brother lost their right path, and touching upon the Mioshihe of the Ōkami, she concealed her Mitama in the nethermost land of Jigoku. Suffering a hundred trials like the scorching cauldrons of Jigoku, she ever renewed her heart, and awaiting the time, as Mokusuke, the divine Keshin of Tokiokashi, borrowing the womb of his wife Osugi, appeared as Hatsuwakahime and manifested as Kunitakehiko, to serve in the precious Kamuwaza of Kuniharutachinōokami, she applied her heart, though of tender age, and though her years were yet unripe, commanded by Kotoyoriwakenomikoto to serve in the sacred Kamuwaza, the divine treasures of the three thousand worlds, the adamantine, indestructible wish-granting jewel, at the roots of the trunk of the pine that stirs not through a thousand and eight thousand ages, she reverently concealed. To open up the Heavenly Rock Cave for the age of Miroku, her profound heart— would Takahime Tsukasa or Kurohime seek the whereabouts of the jewel, and crossing over the rough waves of the Seto Sea of the phenomenal and spiritual worlds, overriding the raging waves of the Pacific Ocean, landing on a certain island of Ryūgū, and seeking the hidden place, how lamentable are their anxious yearnings! Tamajiwake and Tamanohime, together with the divine ministers, feeling anxious for Takahime-sama, and rowing across the rough, raging ocean, now visible, now hidden, protecting her honorable person, I too landed on that same single island of Ryūgū, and in a desolate wilderness where human footprints were extinct, crossing over mountains and wading through valleys, I reached the hiding place of Tamayorihime, which brimmed with golden waves, and chanting the divine will, spread far and wide the Mioshihe of Mishinkyō, and having completed her propagation, riding upon the golden wings of the Yatagarasu, in the dragon's palace where the morning sun shines and the evening sun glows, she now abides. From Tamayorihimeno-mikoto, receiving in sacred silence the purple Mani jewel that governs and unifies heaven, fire, water, and earth, presenting it before the honorable presence of Umekohime— oh, the sheer joy of it! Looking up, high is the plain of heaven, cleaving the clouds and mists, to the central land of Hiezumanokuni in Jitentōshima, the honorable residence of Akiyamahiko, reputed to be the outer enclosure, how joyful was my descent! Ah, Kami nasake, nasake, with their august spirits prospering and blessing us, in a company of ten bearing the crest of the ten suns, rejoicing that we were permitted to serve in an unprecedented, peerless Kamuwaza, not keeping this joy to ourselves alone, but pressing through before the divine messengers Takahime Tsukasa and Kurohime, sharing this joy, joining hands, and serving the precious path of heaven and earth, the great sky of Mishinkyō— with sun and moon pure and clear, in the divine realm of strictness and mildness, weaving the brocade of twill, serving eternally in the radiant palace, rejoicing together, and beholding the prosperity of the teachings! Ah, Kami nasake, nasake, with their august spirits prospering and blessing us, o hear deeply and intimately the sincere heart of Hatsuwakahime, before the august presence of the divided Mitama of Kami Susanō-no-Ōmikoto and Kuniharutachinōokami, with awe and reverence we pray, with awe and reverence we supplicate.'

Umekohime stood up and began to sing and dance.

'By the divine words of the Father Ōkami, manifested in Kenonjō, the divine ministers of Brahmanism, Onimunehiko and all other twisted, warped people— in the Kamuwaza of guiding and pacifying them into the great path of the true Kami, the eight maidens disguised themselves, crossed the river of Eden, applied their utmost care in every diverse way, and exerted every possible best effort, yet all ended as bubbles in water. With the divine minister Omoinokano-mikoto superintending Kenonjō and opening up the teachings, we sisters, each leaving Kenonjō behind, at the very time of transmitting the path of Mishiku here and there, the vile men of Brahmanism, with neither pity nor mercy, like a storm-tossed boat drifting upon the waves near the shore, landed upon the treasure island of Ryūgū, protecting Princess Koito, accompanied by Princess Isago, Princess Imako, and Princess Uzume, in Jionkyō, concealing their light, making the noble Mikoto of Kōryūhime their front, clinging close to her shadow to preach and reveal the precious Mioshihe of the Ōkami, and indeed, not in vain was our deep concern, for Princess Koito, with a pure Mitama, cast off the foul path of Brahmanism like a worn-out sandal, and submitted in obedience to the true path— how inexpressible is that joy! Takayamahiko and Kurohime too, with utmost devotion of heart and body, unto the Mioshihe of Mishinkyō...'
