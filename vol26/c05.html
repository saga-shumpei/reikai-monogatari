Chapter 5: The Episode of Truth 心の Flower (Part IV) [770]

真心の花（四）〔770〕

§ 1

Tomohiko Sendenshi stood up, opened his silver fan, and danced and sang of his own accord. His song:

As a priest of the Brahman teachings, / serving as a deputy pillar to Onikumohiko, / and as a child of the house of Onikumawake, / Tomohiko, who served thus, / on the way back from a banquet of flower-viewing, / sought to cross the river of Eden, / but drunkenly collapsed from the wine of the feast. / While the moribundo were in an uproar and milling about, / behold, the beloved daughter of the Lord, / Koitohime, mistakenly / fell with a splash into the rapids of the river. / Rather than merely watching her fall, / leaping into the very river with his life at stake, / even while drowning, he rescued her, / and was cherished beyond measure by the husband and wife Onikumawake. / Promoted as a fortunate priest / of the precious teachings of Brahman, / rejoicing in his service,

Koitohime, raised in the deep inner chambers, / who had never even been touched by a stray breeze, / a single maiden, whom Tomohiko / deceived with his tongue like a sword, / and taking her hand by hand, down the Eden River, / drifting and drifting, he barely reached the island of Shiro, / hiding his body deep in the mountains, / making Koitohime the queen, / while he became the servant's god, / and thus he deceived and dwelt in this world. / Yet, driven by his inborn love of sake / and the limits of his reckless outrages, / he was at last abandoned by Koitohime. / His deep resentment led him to the sea of Shiro, / where he entrusted his body to a native boat, / rowing across as far as the country of India, / exhausting a myriad of hardships and penances, / until he drifted once more and landed, self-defeated, / upon the shores of Tsuruga.

Making night into day, he hid his body / in the village of Utsuyama along the Tanba road, / assuming the airs of a bat in a village without birds, / and here he passed himself off as a teacher of Brahman's doctrine, / artfully persuading the old and young, men and women of the village, / and just as he was opening his teachings, / the Sendenshi of Amanomafura / appeared and drew near, so pure / that, overwhelmed by those very Kototama, / he fled like clouds and mist. / Slipping into the villages of Yamashiro, Yamato, Kii, Izumi, / and the hamlet of Naniwa, / he deceived numerous men and women, / and though his heart was dark within, / he outwardly flaunted the kami, / reaching the house of Kusuke in the village of Akashi, / where he exhausted every kind of wicked deed, / and at the water's edge,

he wrongfully seized a moored boat / and rowed across with all his might. / Wandering through every nook and corner / of Awaji's single island of waves, / until at the very end of it, sniffing out the absence of Tōsuke, / he was smoothly invited into the mansion. / Placing Obayuri-no-kata before him, / performing a bogus, fraudulent spirit-possession, / going smoothly nine parts out of ten, / until his tail was suddenly caught. / Just as his predicament reached an impasse, / Tōsuke, whom he had thought dead, / returned here accompanied by three men: Kiyoshi, Takeshi, and Tsuru. / Crying out in dismay 'Namu Sanpō!', flustered, / he begged for a moment's grace and respite, / slipped stealthily into the privy, / and the end of his scheming was to straddle the pit hole, / crawling through it to escape and flee the house.

Entrusting his body to a roofless small boat / moored offshore, / saving his life by the skin of his teeth, / as he rowed out with all his might into the Seto Inland Sea, / by what sort of capricious wind's turning / was he driven ashore upon Shōodoshima? / While the wind died down into a calm, / wishing to tour this floating isle, / he let his feet carry him to the fortress of the mountain of Kuniki, / and upon going to see it, the hardship he felt / upon encountering the unexpected Odokohime, / Takahime of Misoshikyo, / and Kusuke of the village of Akashi, / made him feel as though he wished to vanish and sink away / if there were only a hole at the bottom of this earth, / troubling his heart. / Just as he was in this agony, / Tōsuke came once more, / bound by the arresting men, / and the misery of being dragged all the way back / to Sumoto in Awaji!

Softened by the compassionate words of Tōsuke, / who was like a Buddha in hell, / he managed at last to stroke his chest in relief. / Accompanied by the three men—Kiyoshi, Tsuru, and Takeshi— / he manned the boat to protect the perilous person of Takahime, / cruising and searching for her trail among the large and small islands of the South Seas, / until he encountered the divine commands / of Odokonohime and Hatsuwakahime. / Over the waves of ten thousand miles of ocean, / piling up months and days, they finally / arrived safely at a single island. / Leaving the port of Taka behind, / upon going to see the village of Jion, / the divine command of Kōryūhime, who called herself queen, / belonged to Koitohime. / Remembering times gone by, / just as his heart was troubled,

he was chased about by the servants of the castle of Jion, / abandoned in the woods outside the castle, / enduring a hundred adversities, / following mountain ridges and crossing peaks, / before he knew it, traveling a hundred or two hundred ri, / sustaining his hunger upon wild fruits, / climbing to the summit of Mount Nelson, / and resting his breath there, / gazing out at the scenery on all sides / to nourish his heart. / Scarcely had he done so when suddenly, / amid a pitch-black cloud on the mountainside / where neither black nor white could be distinguished, / he was enveloped, unable to discern even an inch before his eyes, / and while his heart ached, / swept away by a record-breaking fierce gale / right into the middle of the sky, / performing an aerial flight, / he fell headlong into the village of Jiana / far beyond numerous peaks.

Even when breath was about to cease, by the people of the Jiyannai teachings, life was barely saved, and happily blessed with a red nose, being revered by multitudes of people as Aurensu and Sāchiraisu, thought of by Princess Teerusu, disguising oneself as a Messiah here, and arranging irresponsible words to the villagers whose language was not even understood, making them worship; though it had been so,

revealing the true state of affairs without a single remaining nook to his wife no Mikoto, the husband and wife united their minds here, marched toward the Castle of Chion, and when he apologized for the discourtesy of former days to Ōriyōhime, she pleasantly forgot the resentment of the past, and taking each other by the hand from east to west, to govern Riugūjima,

oh the joy when it was thus proclaimed! At the arrangement of Ōriyōhime, in the horse-riding ground of the Castle of Chion, a garden party was held; just at that time, a mirage appeared high on the ridge of Mount Nerson, and Tomohiko became Sadahikogami, and the party of five, dressing themselves in the light attire of sedge hats and straw raincoats, stopped over at the village of Jiyanna, and being sent off by the divine children of the teachings,

crossing mountains and rivers, gradually arriving safely at Tamanogehara, and while purifying their souls here, having their sins reproclaimed by Princess Umeko, just at the opportune moment when they were rejoicing bravely and cheerfully, when looking upon a golden ship gliding and coming over the lake,

beginning with Kiyoshi-sama, who had appeared in the Castle of Chion and served as the Left Guardian God, Chiyankī, Monkī, and two others, silently waving toward this side upon the top of the ship; beginning with Princess Umeko, the party leaped onto the ship, and upon the waves swelling the true sails, gliding on, blown by the wind,

sent off by the music of Myōon Bosatsu and the drums of the waves, close to the side of the dragon's palace dwelling where Toyotamahime exists, laying the divine ship across, the ten divine children of the teachings, leisurely passing through the golden gates, at the moment of advancing with eager hearts,

welcomed in an unexpected place by the party of Hatsuwakahime, Tamanohime, and Tamajiwake, and once again guarded front and rear, left and right, by the twelve princess gods, invited to the inner wide hall of the water mansion where Toyotamahime tranquil forever dwells, at the moment of reverently serving and paying homage,

oh the sublimity of when she leisurely appeared, pushing open the jade doors of the upper seat, causing the attending goddess to hold five-colored jewels! The light of that white jade Mani jewel, pure even in heart, being bestowed with her own hands upon Kyūsuke of the village of Akashi, smiling silently, she caused him to stand in that place,

Kyūsuke, receiving the jewel, while handing it over to Tomohiko of the path of teachings into his hands, leisurely returned to his original seat and bowed in awe; though Tomohiko, whose heart was defiled, thought it shameful to his disappointment, bowing prostrate upon his face moistened by the dew-drop jewel of divine grace, while secretly concealing it deeply in the brocade bag granted by the handmaiden,

firmly tying it to his neck, giving thanks for divine grace, even at the times when he was plunged in grief and tears, Princess Umeko stood up from her seat and leisurely came forth from this place; the ten divine propagators of the gods, related to the crest of the ten suns, standing forth from the dragon's mansion,

riding upon the backs of Yata no Garasu, parting clouds and mists, crossing over the waves, oh the joy of finally coming down to the garden of the noble no Mikoto of Akiyamahiko,

Ah, what a wondrous deity, wondrous deity! from now on correcting his mind, purifying his soul, and even in dreams not forgetting divine grace, devoting his true heart to serve; the Great Ancestor God of Heaven and Earth who tranquil forever dwells in the brocade palace, the Great Deity of the Spirit of Mizu,

pitying a powerless body, causing Tomohiko to serve the divine work of the three thousand worlds without error and growing stoutly, with all the limits of his heart, all the limits of his body, all the limits of his strength, dedicating his true heart and praying in worship: Ah, wondrous deity, wondrous deity, may your divine spirit be blessed and prosper!'
