Chapter 7: 鈴の Sound [772]

鈴の音〔772〕

§ 1

Isokohime, with a look of utmost joy and a smile brimming upon her face, opened her golden fan, sang a celebratory song toward the assembled gathering, and gracefully danced with her long sleeves fluttering.

'Great Deity of Izunomitama, Great Deity of Mizunomitama, To subdue the Yamataorochi Coiling across the land of Yashima, And to sweep away the evils of this world, Kuniharutachinōokami Brooded with a thousand-fold shattered heart; Leaving the lofty seat Of the cloud-veiled sky, he concealed His precious divine body in the Nether-land, Desirous once again to protect this realm, And braving the hardships of water and fire From the crater of Mount Tenkefuzan, Roaming far and wide throughout Toyoashihara's Mizuhokuni, To blow away with the wind of Shinado The dark clouds enfolding the hearts of mortals, And to purify them in the rapids of Hayakaha, Wearying his body a myriad times, To guard the world where Akuma rages, How venerable and sublime he is! Upon the summit of Mount Ubusuna Stand the rows of the Isoyakata; Isokohime, revering her father god's imperial command, Descended to the village of Ken'on And made the path of Izanagi and Izanami's truth her shield, Approaching the divine officials Of Brahmanism, such as Onikumohiko and others, Thinking to transmit the path of salvation, Yet that too proved to be a bubble on the water; Helplessly departing from this place, Together with Umekohime, Crossing across the currents of Eden, A journey upon a pillow never trodden before, Exposing her body to rain and sleet, Combing her hair against the foul demonic winds, Just as she was opening the divine path hither and thither, Captured by the divine officials of Brahman, Her resentment is as deep as the plain of the sea; Like a half-rotted abandoned boat, The four master and servants, with lonely hearts in their breasts, Chanting the Great Norito before the divine presence of Heaven and Earth Along with their tears, Drifting over waves of unknown bounds, Sinking and floating, they arrive safely At the treasure isle of Ryūgū; The divine majesty reaches the port of Taka, And abandoning their divine boat they land ashore, Born of the noble child of Onikumawake Is the infant Koisohime, Who became the official of the path of teachings, Appearing in the village of Chi'on, Firmly and permanently erecting the great pillars of the palace in bustling prosperity, While the flowers of the garden of teachings fragrance give, And autumn skies where fruits are borne, Bidding farewell to Umekohime, Koisohime, And other officials, Together with Imokohime, Entrusting their bodies to a roofless boat Favored by the abundant grace of the gods, Threading through countless large and small islands Across the plain of the peaceful waves, At last arriving with joy here Upon the divine island of Hizuma of Jitendō; Galloping back and forth across the island of Jitendō West and East, North and South, Just as the great highway of Izanagi and Izanami's teachings was being spread to the four quarters, An unexpected secret village upon a single isle of Ryūgū, Where the Mani jewel is kept hidden Deep at the bottom of the golden lake, Appearing here, gazing up at the northern sky, Stepping down toward the brocade-like sacred spot, Together with Kotomuwake, Coming to look upon Akiyamahiko's residence, Oh, how wondrous is this! Beholding the severely yearning face of my father god, Susa-no-o no Ōkami, And the solemn, gentle countenance of Kunitakehiko no Ōkami, Bowing down in reverence, Within a breast of mingled joy and sorrow, Words fail, and there is nothing to do but weep; Ah, how wondrous, the divine will of the gods! May the divine spirit flourish and prosper, Fulfilling the great divine work of the father deity And the grand providence of Kunitakehiko, Bringing to complete and minute fruition An age of pines eternal and immovable, For a thousand, eight thousand, and ten thousand generations, Building it up, forever and ever In the divine land of the distant heavenly sky, Enthroned forever in the young sun palace Of the Sun Deity, Awe-struck and reverent before the august presence, We distantly pray: May the spirit of Mizunomitama shine brightly, And the shadow of Izunomitama be pure, And may the moon of Izanagi and Izanami forever Shine and glow, and never wane! Ah, how wondrous, the divine will of the gods! We pray before the presence of the gods.'

So singing to an end, she took her former seat.

Otohiko stands up, spreads out his silver fan, and himself sings and dances.

'The Ural teachings opened By Ural no Hiko and Ural hime, Armenia whose very name is venerable, Leaving behind the hall of teachings, To open the path of the Urals, Floating across the sea of Persia, Leading a mere four or five divine officials And advancing to a single isle, Spending three or four years exhausting body And exhausting heart, to no avail in opening the teachings of the imperial gods, Even this island where golden flowers bloom Like the fruitless yamabuki flowers, Which are the barren blossoms of my faith, Bringing no avail whatsoever, riding over the crest Of the rough waves, as I am returning, Just at that moment upon the sea of Persia, The wild wind rages and the waves roar fiercely, When my boat is about to sink swiftly Into the fathomless sea—'

原文（近代文語）

五十子姫はさも嬉し気に満面に笑を湛へ、金扇を開いて満座の中に向つて祝歌を歌ひ、長袖淑やかに舞はせ給うた。

『厳の御霊の大御神 瑞の御霊の大御神 八洲の国に蟠まる 八岐大蛇を言向けて 此世の曲を払はむと 国治立大神は 心を千々に砕かせつ 雲井の空の弥高き 位を捨てて根の国に 尊き御身をしのばせつ 又もや此世を守らむと 天教山の火口より 水火の艱苦を凌ぎつつ 豊葦原の瑞穂国 隈なく廻り神人の 心を包む村雲を 科戸の風に吹き払ひ 速川の瀬に浄めむと 百千万に身を窶し 悪魔の猛ぶ世の中を

梅子の姫や小糸姫 其他の司に暇乞ひ 今子の姫と諸共に 神の恵の著く 屋根無し船に身を任せ 浪太平の海原を 大小無数の島を縫ひ 漸う此処に自転倒の 秀妻の国の神島に 辿り着きたる嬉しさよ 自転倒島を西東 北や南と駆廻り 三五教の大道を 四方に伝ふる折もあれ 思ひも寄らぬ竜宮の 一つ島なる秘密郷 黄金の湖の底深く 秘め置かれたる麻邇の玉 此処に現はれ北の空 打ち眺めつつ綾錦

鎮まりいます日の神の 御前を慎み畏みて 遥に願ひ奉る 瑞の御霊の麗しく 厳の御霊の影清く 三五の月の何時迄も 照れよ光れよかくるなよ あゝ惟神々々 神の御前に願ぎまつる』

と歌ひ終つて旧の座に着き給うた。

音彦は立ち上つて銀扇を拡げ自ら歌ひ自ら舞ふ。

『ウラルの彦やウラル姫の 開き給ひしウラル教 名さへ尊きアーメニヤ 教の館を後にして ウラルの道を開かむと 波斯の海をば浮びつつ 僅に四五の神司 率ゐて進む一つ島 三歳四歳と身を尽し 心を尽し皇神の 教を開く甲斐もなく わが信仰の仇花に 実りもせない山吹の 黄金花咲く此島も 何の効果も荒浪の 上を辷つて帰り来る 時しもあれや波斯の海 荒風すさび浪猛り 千尋の海に吾船は 早沈まむとする時に

§ 2

Riding in the same sacred boat, the Kamuzukasa of Ananai-kyō and the Shinjin of Hinodewake were saved from perilous places, and for the first time awakened to the Way of Kami, landing at the port of Taru, trudging across the wilderness of Fusa, singing the Sendenka as they advanced bravely at the will of Kami, appointing both Yajihiko and Yotahiko as accompanying officials, and approaching Kojikatauge, when just at that moment, surrounded by the Metsuke kami of the Ural-kyō, their advance and retreat became blocked in a ravine, casting their bodies down a thousand-fathom ravine, remaining in a state of complete unconsciousness, exploring the lonely sights of the River Shōzu and Yachimata, called back to life and revived by the Shinjin of Hinodewake, they deeply awakened to the Way of Kami, determined to transmit the Mikokoro of Sumeōkami to the utmost limits of the eighty islands and eighty countries of the islands, without distinction between far and near, wandering round and round until they finally reached the island of self-revolution, right in the midst where the Kamuzukasa of Brahmanism relied upon the iron castle of Oeyama and Onigajō as immovable and everlasting, blowing them away with our Kototama, their hearts eager and cheerful, fifty children dividing to the right and left, just at the opportune moment when sincerity is devoted for the sake of Kami, for the Way, and for the people of the world, the precious treasures of the teachings of the Ananai kami, the three sacred jewels, were eternally enshrined at the will of Kami, the heavenly pillar of augustness growing ever thicker, the mechanism of the warp and woofing loom down to the bedrock drawing near, and in the autumn sky where the flowers of the teaching began to fragrance far and near, upon hearing that the five sacred jewels, coming once again and uniting with Heaven, Fire, Water, and Earth, the Mani jewels of the five sacred jewels, would gather safely within the sacred ground of Aya, the steeds of their hearts grew brave, and shedding tears of gratitude before the august presence of Nishikinomiya, the Kamuzukasa of Ananai-kyō, obeying the parting command, came and saw at the port of Yura that the august father of Tsuma-no-mikoto appeared as the Mizunomitama, Lord Susanoo-no-Ōkami, whose sacred countenance was especially radiant, and whose body was healthy, how joyful it was that He devoted Himself to the divine work, ah, kannagara kamunagara, may the august spirits prosper and bless, so that the three sacred jewels shine brightly and the five sacred jewels gleam purely, just like the sun and moon illuminating and shining brightly in the sky of the heart, without any clouds or mist, eternally illuminating the world with the light of Kami, ramming and solidifying the foundation of the divine age of Miroku down to the bedrock, and pounding and hardening it up to the upper bedrock, O Tianjin kami, Kunitsukasa, and Yaoyorozu, protect our divine work of guarding the august teachings of the Ananai moon, and before the august presences of the two pillars, Haniyasuhiko and Haniyasuhime, possessing pure august spirits, with reverence and awe we pray and supplicate, ah, kannagara kamunagara, may the august spirits prosper and bless!
