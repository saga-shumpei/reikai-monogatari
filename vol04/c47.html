The Universe of Divine Revelation (Part 2)

神示の宇宙　その二〔197〕

§ 1

In order to supplement what was stated in the previous section, I shall further add a few fragmentary explanations. However, as my view of the universe is entirely as revealed by divine revelation, whether or not it has any relation to modern astronomy is completely outside my concern.

For twenty-four years since receiving the divine revelations, I have been almost entirely cut off from the publications of the world themselves. Accordingly, I naturally do not know to what degree current astronomy has progressed and developed. Therefore, even if one confronts the view of the universe I describe immediately with modern astronomical knowledge, there will surely be not a few points difficult to accept readily.

Continuing from the previous section, I shall describe matters in order, beginning with the sun.

The sun is a dark body, and the reason its color appears as a red mixed with white is that water is shining. If one compares a red cloth and a white cloth in the dark night, the white cloth is seen more clearly. Judging from this as well, one can discern that the water is shining.

The respective microcosms within the macrocosm pull on one another, and likewise, that the sun maintains its position is due to the gravitational pull of the various stars. Therefore, the Lord of Heaven first created the various celestial bodies in order to support the sun. (Refer to the chapter "The Separation of Heaven and Earth" in Volume 1.)

前節で述べたところを補うために、さらに少し断片的に説明を加えておくことにする。しかし私の宇宙観はすべて神示のままであるので、現代の天文学といかなる交渉をもつか否かは全く私の関知するところではない。

私は神示に接してから二十四年間、ほとんど全く世界の出版物そのものから絶縁していた。したがって現在の天文学がいかなる程度にまで進歩発達しているかは言うまでもなく知らないのである。ゆえに私の述べる宇宙観に対して、直ちに現代の天文学的知識をもって臨んだとしても、にわかに首肯しがたい点が少なくないはずである。

前節に引き続いて太陽のことより順次述べることとする。

太陽は暗体であって、太陽の色が白色を加えたような赤色に見えるのは、水が光っているからである。暗夜に赤布と白布とを比較して見れば、白布のほうがはっきりと見えるものである。これによって見ても、水が光っていることが判断できるのである。

大宇宙の各小宇宙は互いに牽引し合っているものであり、それと同様に太陽がその位置を保持するのは諸星の牽引力によるものである。ゆえに天主は太陽を支持するために、まず諸星辰を造られた。（第一篇天地剖判の章参照）

§ 2

The distance between the sun and our earth corresponds to one-eighth of the microcosm's diameter of 5,670,000,000 ri, and all the stars of the firmament occupy positions above the height of the sun while emitting their own light. The light of the sun is by no means radiated toward the firmament; like a flashlight, it is radiated entirely toward the great earth alone.

Ordinarily, we regard the direction where the sun rises as east, but speaking originally from the universe itself, there is no distinction of east, west, south, and north. In the Buddhist teaching,

"Originally there is neither east nor west; whence could there be south or north?"

This saying is also based upon this principle. Now, if one establishes the distinction of east, west, south, and north, the earth, which is the center of the great earth, corresponds to the North Pole. Kita (north) signifies the dripping of spirit, the dripping of water and fire, and the dripping of breath. Minami (south) is a word-spirit meaning that all is visible.

As previously stated, the earth is not a sphere such as the scholars of the world believe, but a flat plane. What we call the earth is an exceedingly minute part at the center of the great earth, and as shown in the first figure, the great earth consists entirely of mountains of ice. Moreover, the farther those mountains of ice are separated from the so-called earth, the more precipitous they become. Ordinarily, the melting of icebergs refers only to the melting of those mountains of ice that draw near to the center of the earth, and the greater part of the mountains of ice never melt at all.

通常我々は太陽の昇る方角を東としているが、本来宇宙それ自体から言えば、東西南北の区別はない。仏説に、

『本来東西無く 何処にか南北有らん』

とあるのも、この理による。今、東西南北の区別を立てるならば、大地の中心である地球が北極に当たる。北とは気垂、水火垂、呼吸垂の意である。南とは皆見えるという意味の言霊である。

§ 3

As one proof of the spherical Earth theory, it may be cited that when a person stands on the seashore and watches a boat heading offshore, as the ship gradually moves farther out, it first conceals the hull and gradually conceals the mast, but that is because our eyeballs are already formed as spheres. Because a telescope consists of concave lenses, distant objects are visible in relation to the human pupil. Therefore, those who cling to the spherical Earth theory must first begin with the study of the human eyeball itself.

The Earth also possesses a kind of radiance and is not a dark body.

That which possesses the most grave role over the entire universe is the Great Yin, namely, the moon. Everyone knows that all things are generated and nurtured by the grace of the sun, but there is almost no one who knows the vast and boundless grace of the veiled moon.

All things in the universe possess a delicate and profound relationship with this motion of the moon. In a little over twenty-nine days, that is, an ordinary month, the moon makes one revolution through the mid-heavens. However, it does not move by axial rotation, but merely moves while maintaining the identical posture. That the position of the moon in the firmament changes sequentially, for instance from Sky A on the third day of the month to Sky B on the fourth day, is proof that the moon is not stationary, but is moving from west to east.

The moon is visible to our eyes when it travels along the upper line in the first diagram, and when it passes along the lower line, it is completely invisible to us. When the moon moves along the upper line, it is the activity of Tsukiyomi no Mikoto, and when it moves along the lower line, it is the activity of Susanoo no Mikoto.

§ 4

Next, the first question that arises when looking at the moon is that pattern on the lunar surface. That pattern, said from ancient times to be a monkey and a rabbit pounding rice cakes, is the shadow of our so-called five continents reflected upon the lunar surface. Therefore, the same pattern is always visible. The faint shadow visible on the darkened half of the moon is the shadow of the moon itself. That is to say, it is because the fireball portion that constitutes the other half of the moon is visible.

A lunar eclipse occurs when the moon is directly irradiated by the sun from behind. A solar eclipse occurs when the moon enters between the sun and the earth, obstructing the sun.

The Milky Way is where the sun's light radiates onto the icebergs of the earth and is in turn reflected into the sky, so that the myriad dark stars existing in the sky become visible to our eyes by that reflected light. The unevenness on the outer rim of the Milky Way is because of the unevenness in the height and depth of the icebergs.

Comets also travel through the great void, and at times are viewed from the earth. The great void refers to the realm outside this microcosm, and it exhibits a blue color. The color of the sky is green. However, because we look not only through the color of the sky but also through the blue great void together, it appears azure.

When viewing this microcosm from the outside, the sky is covered with spiritual robes of various colors, such as purple, red, and blue, far thinner than those of the earth, and the earth is wrapped in thick spiritual robes of various colors, such as yellow, light yellow, and white. And when viewing this universe as a whole, it exhibits a purple color. This is called the Sacred Jewel of the Manifest Realm (Utsushikuni no Mitama).

Our microcosm, with this at its center, is connected and communicates with the other various universes in all directions like a spider's web by means of spiritual lines, and in those universes as well, almost no beings resembling the humans, animals, and plants on our earth inhabit them. However, although gods dwell upon the celestial bodies other than the earth in this microcosm of ours, there are positively no living beings such as those inhabiting the earth. If the relationship between this microcosm and the other universes is shown by a diagram, it is as in Diagram 5.
