Borrowed Terror of the Tiger

§ 1

Miyamahiko and Kuniteruhime called themselves representatives of all living beings under heaven, and backed by the influence of Yatsuō-ōkami and Daijizaiten, wagging their tails like wild foxes borrowing the terror of a tiger, haughtily led numerous divine beings to the Grand Palace of Kuni-no-Koso in the holy land, and arrogantly proclaimed that they had been selected as advisory envoys, bearing the trust and hopes of the various divine officials at the great council of Tokoyo Castle, and immediately stepped before Kuni-no-Koso and stated:

"The Miyamahiko and Kuniteruhime of today are not the insignificant Miyamahiko and Kuniteruhime of yesterday; being the representatives of the dread Yatsuō-ōkami Tokoyohiko, whose influence and benevolence are peerless under heaven, and of Daijizaiten Ōkunihiko, whose divine power is matchless and whose authority overwhelms the divine beings under heaven, and being Miyamahiko and Kuniteruhime who bear the grave office of advisory envoys representing the eight million divine officials, even Kuni-no-Koso-ōkami must by no means treat us neglectfully,"

thus indulging in insolent words,

"First and foremost, for the peace of the divine realm and to respond to the holy will of Yatsuō-ōkami, banish from your side with the divine authority of Kuni-no-Koso those stubborn and obstinate gods from Ōyashimahiko-no-Mikoto on down, and command them to depart to Ne-no-Kuni and Soko-no-Kuni as disturbers of the divine realm,"

"If your words indeed represent the opinion of Yatsuō-ōkami, Daijizaiten, and the general assembly, ah, what more can I say? Even for deities of supreme righteousness and uprightness, must I swallow my tears and execute Ma Su for the peace of the realm?"

As His voice and tears fell together and His countenance showed boundless emotion, among both the deities serving near Him and the retainers of Miyamahiko, there was none who did not wring tears from their sleeves. Perhaps thinking that with a faint heart this mission could hardly be accomplished, Kuniteruhime, while encouraging Miyamahiko who was about to sink into melancholy, persistently pressed for the Kunisō's reply. The Kunisō, seeing that matters had come to this, had no room to do anything, and accepting their demand, approved the banishment of Ōyashimahiko-no-mikoto, Kotodamawake-no-mikoto, Kamikuniwake-no-mikoto, and Ōtarashihiko to Ne-no-kuni.

Here, by the strict command of the Kunisō, the aforementioned four divine ministers withdrew into divine retirement to the lunar realm of Yomi-no-kuni, combined their four souls into Kuniōtachi-no-mikoto, resonated with the spirit of Tsukinoōkami, descended once again to the earthly world, endured myriad hardships, and began foundational activities for the Miroku divine government; yet, among the deities of the faction of Yatsuō-ōkami and Daijizaiten, where body ruled spirit, not a single one knew of their situation during this time.

Next, Takateruhime-no-mikoto, Masumihime, Kotodamahime, and Ryūyohime withdrew into divine retirement deep beneath the earth into the world of the Earth-Moon, resonated with the spirit of the Earth-Moon and manifested as guardian deities within the great earth, combined their four souls to become Kinkatsukaname-no-kami, appeared in the surface world when the time arrived, and while devoting themselves to foundational divine work for the Miroku divine government, remained in the divine realm from ancient times to the present day, continuing their activities.

されど八王大神系の神司らも、大自在天系の神司らも、一柱としてこの神業を知了し居る者は絶対にあらざりしなり。神諭に、

"The divine age of old shall return, and it shall be reconstructed into the original divine age of old; the plum blossoms that open at once across the three thousand worlds."

One ought to fully savor such divine revelations.

Now, the two, Miyamahiko and Kuniteruhime, risked displeasure and strongly advised that the aforementioned deities should be banished by the divine authority of Kuni no Koso, and fortunately they achieved that objective, but in regard to the crucial recommendation of the voluntary retirement of Kuni no Koso, even these evil deities stammered and hesitated to speak. Ōkami asked in rapid succession,

"Is your advice finished with this?"

When He inquired thus, the two messengers, struck by the majesty of Ōkami, innocently,

"We have nothing more to say,"

spoke in reply without thinking. Taking the reply of the two messengers as a signal, Kuni no Koso Ōkami swiftly stood, closed the jeweled sliding doors with His own hands, and concealed Himself deep within the inner sanctuary. Feeling as though something were stuck between their back teeth and lacking vigor, the two messengers reported the result to Hachiō-ōkami. Hachiō-ōkami was angered by the cowardice of the two who were unable to recommend the crucial voluntary retirement to Kuni no Koso Ōkami, and immediately strictly ordered them into confinement, whereupon, pouting like owls that had missed their night meal, they dejectedly departed.
