§ 1

In the Grand Palace of the Holy Land of Jerusalem, a grand banquet was held combining the announcement of the new appointment of Archangel Momogamihiko and the announcement of the retirement of the divine officials below Hiromunehiko no Mikoto and Yukinarihiko; and Tokoyohiko, Tokoyohime, Ōtakawake, the Eight Kings and Eight Heads of various mountains and lands, as well as the other deities, were present at this auspicious and inauspicious banquet like a constellation of stars.

Momogamihiko stood and gave his inaugural address:

"Henceforth, I desire to receive the august will of Kuniso, strictly observe the laws of heaven and earth, and, together with the divine people, serve in united cooperation the great work of the administration of the world,"

he concluded briefly, and calmly took the seat of honor in the center. Hiromunehiko no Mikoto stood up from his seat, faced the divine people, and said,

"I express my gratitude for the kindness of the divine officials who have aided my ignorant self for many years, enabling me to retain the office of Archangel until this day,"

stating briefly in a sorrowful tone that it had become inevitable for him to retire this time; and vowing that henceforth he would commit himself to the clouds and water, wander throughout the realm, devote himself to cultivating soul and body, and serve the divine work from behind the scenes, he dispiritedly returned to his original seat.

"Repenting that, from the darkness and simple-minded folly of my heart, I unawares took actions that violated the heavenly laws at the Tokoyo Council, I personally assume the responsibility, resign my post, leave the sacred land just as my elder brother does, wander throughout the realm, accumulate discipline and self-cultivation, and hope there may come a time when I may once again follow in the footsteps of the various divine officials and serve in the divine work,"

he stated, and added:

"Regarding my future sacrificial actions, I hope to receive the kind and earnest teachings of the divine officials,"

having finished stating this, he returned to his original seat listlessly.

At this time, pushing open the jeweled sliding doors from the inner palace and accompanied by numerous attendant divine officials, the ancestral deity of the land, Kunitokotachi no Mikoto, appeared at this place and spoke quietly:

"Concerning the resignations of Hiromunehiko no Mikoto and those beneath him this time, my chest is as if swallowing molten iron, filled with immeasurable feelings. Yet, the laws of heaven and earth must not be violated. Now there is no room to do anything, and with profound regret, I cast away loyal divine officials who possess supreme benevolence, supreme love, and utmost sincerity that penetrates heaven and earth; empathize with my heart,"

he said, his august voice clouded, and one could behold that his tears even ran down along his cheeks.

§ 3

Among the assembly of divine beings, not a single one was able to raise their face at this proclamation of the Kuni no Koso; emotion pressed upon their chests, hot tears gushed forth, and the sound of sniffling could be heard from all around. The Kuni no Koso continued speaking further, and while wringing His sleeves wet with tears, said:

"God seeks to have all things in the universe dwell peacefully in the beautiful divine realm and have them courageously serve the great undertaking of the divine plan, striving and agonizing without distinction of day and night. You divine beings, take the heart of God as your own heart, face all things with a sincere heart of supreme benevolence and love, persevere with patience upon patience, and properly preserve your qualifications as divine beings,"

thus He explained and demonstrated, and furthermore declared again:

"God's benevolence widely loves and protects without distinguishing between friend and foe, and regardless of right and wrong, justice or injustice. All of you divine beings, beginning with Momogamihiko, look at this!"

When He rolled up the curtain of the seat of honor with His own hands, a great, pure mirror that illuminated all six directions hung there.

Following the proclamation of Kuni no Koso Ōkami, the divine beings all turned their faces at once toward the place of honor where the great pure mirror was enshrined, and involuntarily prostrated themselves; struck by an indescribable majesty, they fearfully raised their heads while shedding tears, and as they looked reverently upon the mirror's surface, what should they see—beginning with Makohiko, who had fallen from the heavenly bird-boat into the valley of Mount Sinai and had both body and soul pulverized, Takekuma, Onikuma, Kitsunehime, Onihime, Iwanagahime, Kuchikohime, Onigumohiko, Sagahime, Magokorohiko, the three white foxes who had been submerged in the Lake of Jewels, the five divine beings of the Dragon Palace who had presented the jewel in the Yahiro-dono, Shikoharahiko, Munenagahiko, Tsuruwaka, Kamewaka, Yasomagahiko, and all the other wicked persons who had perished under the previously mentioned divine punishment, each retained their physical bodies full of life, and were clearly perceived to be serving diligently and joyfully about the person of the Kuni no Koso.

"You must have realized through this the true love of God."

Having finished saying this, he turned his back toward the gods, and said,

"Gaze closely upon the back of my head."

When he said this, the gods and men looked up in sudden surprise to see that the hair on the back of the Kokusō's head had been entirely plucked out, blood was flowing, horribly festered beyond sight, and it appeared grievously painful to behold. The divine officials were moved all at once by that benevolence, and beholding this state, they dampened both sleeves with tears, creating a scene like an unseen shower falling from the sky, or wind blowing red autumn leaves to rot away and dye the earth. Not a single one among the divine beings could raise their face, and pressing their heads against the tatami, each gave thanks while acknowledging their sin of having hitherto remained ignorant of the deep benevolence of the Ōkami's heart.

In the divine revelation of the Ōkami,

"This God shall cast no one aside. Be they enemies, devils, ogres, serpents, or even tiny insects, this is a God that saves them,"

and every time Oni recalls the divine revelation thus shown, he is ever unable to hold back his tears.

When evil gods were sentenced to severe punishment for violating heavenly law, and their souls were about to perish, the Kokusō plucked out his own downy hair one by one as an atonement for them. The Foundress, having realized these deeds of boundless benevolence by the Kokusō, would always pull out her own hair for sinful believers, plucking out one, two or three, or dozens of strands, and

"Keep this as an amulet,"

bestowed them in this manner precisely because she embodied this supreme divine will.
