Volume 4 • Chapter 32

The Inescapable Path

免れぬ道〔182〕

§ 1

After a short while, Momogamihiko came in hurriedly, knelt before the two, and reported that it was a strict command from the awe-inspiring Kuniharutachi no Mikoto that their mother Kototaruhime, along with his elder brothers Hiromunehiko no Mikoto and Yukinarihiko, must present themselves with the utmost urgency.

Momogamihiko had been serving as the deputy to Chief Angel Hiromunehiko no Mikoto and assisting in the divine administration, but at last he forgot the love of his two elder brothers and schemed to take the sacred office of Chief Angel for himself instead. Taking Tokoyohime's arrival at the castle as an advantageous opportunity, he planned to join minds with her and eliminate his two elder brothers. When Kototaruhime, accompanied by her three sons, presented herself at the main hall of Kuniso Ōkami, the two divine officials Tokoyohime and Tokoyohiko were respectfully attending on the left and right by the side of the Ancestral Deity. Seeing this sight, Yukinarihiko was astonished almost to the point of fainting. At this time, Kuniso Ōkami said with solemn words,

"Bring Ōmichiwake before me."

Thus commanded, Yukinarihiko meekly complied, withdrew from the presence, and after a short while reappeared before Kuniso Ōkami bringing Ōmichiwake with him. Tokoyohiko turned toward Ōmichiwake and said,

"I too am impressed by your resourceful strategy."

§ 2

When He peered into his face with a smile, Ōmichiwake was anticipated and thrown into confusion. The Great God, the National Ancestor, turned toward Ōmichiwake and said,

"That you have tasted long years of hardships and privations for the sake of the divine realm, thereby fulfilling your divine calling as a god-man, fills Me too with feelings of gratitude beyond measure. However, you possess an excess of intellect yet lack virtue, and since becoming the false Great God of Hachiō, you abruptly changed your conduct; breaking that pact is not an act to be praised as a god-man. Furthermore, the conduct of the envoys under Yukinarihiko broke the laws of heaven and earth out of an excess of cherishing the sacred land. Although you are ones of utmost sincerity and truth, alas, in My duty as the National Ancestor, I cannot overlook it. Ah, must I cruelly abandon such a meritorious subject?"

So He choked with falling tears. Ōmichiwake, while feeling utterly abashed, gave a silent nod of deference to the Great God, the National Ancestor, bowed once to the Great God of Hachiō and the other divine officials, immediately withdrew from the divine presence, and threw himself into the Ryūgū Sea as he was. His nigimitama and sakimitama instantly transformed into the sea god. The National Ancestor bestowed upon this the name Kotohirawake-no-kami, causing him to protect the seas eternally, and bestowed the name Hinode upon his aramitama and kushimitama, commanding him to protect the land. The subsequent activities of Kotohirawake-no-kami and Hinode-no-kami were truly remarkable, becoming the foundational grand pillars of the Miroku Divine Government and possessing the affinity to appear in the world once again in later generations.

Hereupon, Hiromunehiko-no-mikoto respectfully perceived the divine feelings of the National Ancestor, took the responsibility, resigned from the sacred office of the Chief Angel, and yielded it to his younger brother, Momogamihiko. Incidentally, as stated in the previous volume, the manner of Momogamihiko's divine political governance ultimately opened the door to bringing harm even upon the National Ancestor.

The Great God of Hachiō, together with Tokoyohime, celebrated Momogamihiko's succession to the office. At this time, Onitakehiko of Mount Ōe, accompanied by Takakura and Asahi, stepped forward before the National Ancestor, and after offering the most respectful bow,

§ 3

"For what reason did You deign to command the retirement of the great meritorious persons, Yukinarihiko and those below him, at Tokoyo Castle on this occasion?"

When he timidly inquired thus, Kuni no Koso deigned to utter but a single word:

"Ask your own hearts."

Thus He deigned to answer. Onitakehiko somewhat changed his expression and said,

"When the deer and boars are exhausted, the hunting hounds are boiled. We merely slipped into Tokoyo Castle by command of the Noble God, troubled Hachiō-ōkami, and caused him to abandon his conspiracy. By no means did the envoy-retainers of the party, beginning with Yukinarihiko, disobey the Great God and take arbitrary action. Without a single speck of ambition, they acted purely out of the utmost sincerity to protect the sacred ground and assist the divine work. Furthermore, is it not a matter known to You that I acted loyally in accordance with the secret command?"

Thus he reported to the deity without the slightest sign of fear.

A look of astonishment somehow appeared upon the countenance of Kuni no Koso Ōkami. At the same time, an unpleasant smile flashed across the face of Hachiō-ōkami. Ah, what in the world does it tell that such a difference as ice and charcoal arose between the expression of Kuni no Koso Ōkami and the expression of Hachiō-ōkami? It is hoped that the readers will fully reflect upon this wondrous scene.
