Enlightenment of Heaven and Earth

天地開明〔186〕

§ 1

Momogamihiko no Mikoto, moved by the stern words of Kokuso Ōkami said to pierce the bone with their severity, finally resolved to resign, and immediately communicated this to Tokoyohime, the head of Ryūgū-jō. Tokoyohime did not offer even a single word advising him to remain in office; while inwardly leaping for joy, she assumed an indifferent air with an expression of sympathy, accepted the Mikoto's letter of resignation in silence, immediately proceeded to the Great Palace of the Holy Land of Jerusalem, and reverently reported that Momogamihiko no Mikoto, conscious of his responsibility, begged leave to resign.

Hereupon, the Holy Land of Takamanohara came to the unavoidable pass of presenting a disastrous state of complete disruption, as if the pivot pin had been removed from a folding fan; the Eight Kings and Eight Heads of various mountains and regions, together with the Kunitama and other divine officials, gathered from the ends of high mountains and low mountains, and the dragon gods stirred up violent storms from the five seas directed toward the Holy Land, riding upon dark clouds while churning the sea surface, and gathered in the Holy Land without exception. The number of divine personages who gathered in the Holy Land reached nearly three koku of millet grains. The Eight Kings of Mount Manju, who had maintained such an unyielding posture and flatly refused to attend the Tokoyo Council, cast aside all personal considerations upon hearing of the turmoil in the Holy Land and came to gather; Ōyashimahiko no Mikoto, Kototamawake no Mikoto, Kamikuniwake no Mikoto, and Ōtaruhiko, who had retired to Mount Ryōju, as well as Takateruhime no Mikoto, Masumihime, Kototamahime, and Ryūsehime, who had been ordered into confinement on the Plain of Eden, also broke the ban and came rushing together with numerous retainers, regarding this as a grave crisis for the Holy Land.

Both Daijizaiten Ōkunihiko and Tokoyohiko, who had hitherto maintained an attitude of utmost opposition toward the faction of Ōyashimahiko no Mikoto and the faction of Takateruhime no Mikoto, looked upon this disastrous state in which the Holy Land was on the verge of ruin, were overcome with sorrow, completely removed feelings of friend and foe from their hearts, and mutually gave rise to sincere devotion toward the restoration of the Holy Land. In particular, Daijizaiten was overcome with tears of sympathy, understanding the feelings of patient endurance and secluded living among the divine personages of the factions of Ōyashimahiko no Mikoto and Takateruhime no Mikoto. Because all were originally divine personages who revered Kunitokotachi no Mikoto as their ancestral founder, when it came to this moment of life and death, their petty feelings vanished like mist and clouds, and each divine official demonstrated the virtue of humility, showing mutual affection and love, without building the slightest barrier within their hearts. As the proverb says,

"Parents gather weeping; strangers gather feeding,"

so it is said. The divine personages who originally received the direct lineage of the righteous deity, or who were born branching off from that direct lineage, returned at this very moment to the original mind of kannagara, displayed utmost sincerity, and manifested their genuine devotion to realize the act of repaying the origin and returning to the beginning before the Great Deity.

"When one has fallen on hard times and tears wet the sleeves, the true depths of the human heart come to be known."

§ 2

Truly worthy of divine humans of the divine age, when they returned to their innate nature and restored the manifestation of their original guardian deity, there were neither enemies nor allies, nor was there any room for the evil minds of resentment, jealousy, discontent, or dissatisfaction to arise. Even though they were divine humans innately possessing such supremely good, supremely beautiful, and supremely upright divine minds, when their body-souls were deceived and possessed by the spirits of the six-faced, eight-armed ogre, the golden-haired, nine-tailed evil fox, or the eight-headed, eight-tailed giant serpent, which had manifested from the condensation of evil energies between heaven and earth, even their most pure and unadulterated body-souls, which were parted spirits of the Great Deity, suddenly transformed as if turning the palm of a hand. The swiftness of this was just like a shadow following form.

There is something I wish to state in passing, which concerns the nominal meanings of the three kinds of evil deities. A six-faced, eight-armed evil ogre does not mean that six heads or faces are attached to one body. It refers to one who at times transforms into an old person, at times changes into a child, becomes a beauty, transforms into an ugly person, feigns being a righteous deity, and at times alters their appearance into a purely evil deity, thereby performing miraculous and wondrous sorcery; nor does eight arms mean that there are eight arms on one body. Comparing this to humans of today, it means the capability of excelling over others in all techniques and skills with a single hand, such as being skilled at building elaborate machinery, skilled in calligraphy, proficient in painting, or skilled in music. It does not necessarily mean being skilled in eight kinds of things, but rather means being proficient in all one hundred kinds of skills.

Furthermore, regarding the golden-haired, nine-tailed, white-faced evil fox, the color gold is the most noble among colors and occupies the highest rank as a metal. As a fox it possesses stiff, needle-like hairs with a golden luster, but when manifesting through transformation, it appears in the body of a beautiful woman, attires itself in graceful and noble clothing, astounds all divine humans, and seeks to overawe them with its majesty. Moreover, nine tails does not mean that nine tails grow on a single fox; nine signifies the ultimate of numbers and exhaustion, which is to say, perfection.

The reason the island of Tsukushi is now called Kyūshū also arises from the meaning of nine as exhaustion. A tail signifies the power to govern all things. It is like commanders of the three armed forces in later ages waving their command batons to direct soldiers, or like the master of the purification place waving the grand purification wand left, right, left on the occasion of a festival to repel demons, summon righteous deities, and sweep away evil energy. Princess Masumi's waving of the golden wand to destroy the demon army was also, to use a negative analogy, of the same meaning as waving the tails of the golden-haired, nine-tailed fox. However, when a righteous deity uses it, it is said that they wave the golden wand left, right, left, whereas when an evil deity uses it, it is called waving nine tails. Where 'golden-haired, eight-tailed' or 'silver-haired, eight-tailed' appear in various places in this narrative, it means an evil deity whose function is somewhat inferior to the nine-tailed one.

Moreover, the eight-headed, eight-tailed giant serpent by no means signifies that eight heads and eight tails exist on a single serpent body. While octopuses, squids, and crabs have as many as eight legs, on a serpent body, even if the tip of the tail is occasionally split in two and hardened, none is ever divided into eight branches. Although Buddhist scriptures speak of things like the nine-headed dragon and indicate a dragon with nine heads, this too is an entirely symbolic term, meaning a dragon deity that performs miraculous and wondrous actions. From ancient times there has been the proverb, 'Yield to the long thing.' Compared to other animals, the giant serpent is the longest in body length, and rather than being frail like an earthworm, it protects its body with considerably hard scales and possesses numerous substitute legs on its belly. What appear to be scales on the belly all serve as substitutes for the serpent's legs. Legs signify that which is below. Therefore, one who stands above and commands those below is called a leader, and also called an elder. This spirit of the giant serpent distributes its parted spirits to various parts of the world, engages in endlessly changing activities, and means an evil spirit that uses the bodies of divine humans as vessels to bring about wickedness. Just as the twelve pillars of the Eight Kings and Eight Heads are not called the Twelve Kings and Twelve Heads but are called the Eight Kings and Eight Heads, the actions of this eight-headed, eight-tailed giant serpent are by no means limited to eight kinds. It means the action of evil force that perpetrates endlessly changing, immoral acts.

I, Ōni, had intended to describe the conditions of the confusion in the holy land, but before I knew it, the talk slid along, slippery and long like a serpent, crawled unnoticed deep into the mountains, and fell into a deep valley. Could this perhaps be what is called adding legs to a snake?

いま筑紫の島を九州といふのも、九は尽しの意味から出たるなり。尾といふは総てのものを支配する力をいふ。後世にいたり三軍の将が采配を振つて軍卒を指揮し、あるひは祭典にさいし祓戸主の役が大幣を左右左に振つて悪魔を退け、かつ正しき神を召集し、邪気を払拭するが如し。真澄姫が黄金の幣を打ち振りて魔軍を亡ぼしたまひしも、悪くたとへていへば金毛九尾の尾を振りたると同意味なり。されど正しき神の使用するときは金幣を左右左に振るといひ、邪神の使用する時は九尾を振ると称へたるなり。この物語のなかの所々に金毛八尾、銀毛八尾とあるは、九尾にやや劣りし働きをなす邪神といふ意味なり。

また八頭八尾の大蛇といふも、決して一つの蛇体に八つの頭があり、また尾が八ツあるにあらず。蛸や烏賊や、蟹には足が八つもあるが、蛇体には偶に尾の先二つに裂けて固まれるがありても、決して八つの岐になり居るものはなし。仏書に九頭竜などといひ、九つの頭のある竜のことが示しあれど、これも全く象徴的の語にして、神変不可思議の働きをなす竜神といふ意味なり。昔から「長いものには捲かれよ」といふ譬あり。大蛇は他の動物に比して身の丈もつとも長く、かつ蚯蚓のやうに軟弱ならず相当に堅き鱗をもちて身体を保護し、沢山の代用足を腹部に備へゐるなり。腹部の鱗と見ゆるは、みな蛇の足の代用をするものなり。足は下を意味す。ゆゑに上に立ちて下を指揮するものを長といひ、また長者ともいふなり。この大蛇の霊は世界の各地にその分霊を配り、千変万化の活動をなし、神人の身体を容器として邪悪を起さしむる悪霊の意なり。十二柱の八王八頭を十二王、十二頭と称へず、八王、八頭と称へらるるごとく、この八頭八尾の大蛇の働きも決して八種に限るにあらず。千変万化反道的行為を敢行する悪力の働きの意なり。

王仁は聖地の混乱の状況を述ぶる心算なりしが、つい談が蛇のごとくぬるぬると長く滑りて、知らず識らず山の奥に這ひ込み、深き谷間に陥りけり。これがいはゆる蛇足を添へるとでもいふならむか。
