第一章　常世会議〔151〕
Part 1: River Waves of the Eight Islands (第一篇 八洲の川浪)
§ 1
§ 1: Collapse of Prior Dispensations and the Summons to Tokoyo

In primeval antiquity, the sacred work of divine administration initially collapsed through the violation of celestial laws by Waka-sakurahime-no-Mikoto. Next came the divine governance of Kuninaohime-no-Mikoto; but she likewise abandoned the terrestrial realm and returned unto heaven, whereupon the governance was transferred unto Ōyashimahiko-no-Mikoto. All served the celestial plan under the supreme authority of Kunitokotachi-no-Mikoto. Thereafter, Takateruhime-no-Mikoto descended from heaven unto the earthly Takamanohara to serve in the divine governance, yet this too met with collapse. Next, Sawatadahiko-no-Mikoto descended from heaven to serve under Kunitokotachi-no-Mikoto, but this brought about great ruin, and he also abandoned the earth and returned unto heaven.

Though Kunitokotachi-no-Mikoto repeatedly changed the presiding deities, every effort ended in failure, leaving Him at a loss, as if the limbs had been torn from a crab. Nevertheless, being inherently upright and unyielding, Kunitokotachi-no-Mikoto strictly observed the laws of Heaven and Earth, yielding not in the least before any tribulation. Because He strictly commanded the deities under His authority that the laws must not be infringed by even a hair's breadth, the presiding chiefs of the earthly Takamanohara were repeatedly replaced.

Hachiō-Ōkami of the land of Tokoyo, resolving that this opportunity must not be lost, determined to assemble the Eight Kings and Eight Chiefs of every mountain and land across the world at Tokoyo Castle to hatch their plots. Dispatching heavenly bird-boats in all directions, he gathered the spirits in one place without regard to righteousness or wickedness, wisdom or folly, scheming to form a confederation of the Eight Kings and Eight Chiefs. Furthermore, having held secret consultations with Jizaiten Ōkunihiko, he sought to convene a council of national souls among the divine spirits of the world.

Namely, from the side of Hachiō-Ōkami, the deities who were to attend were Miyamahiko, Kuniteruhime, Magahiko, Magahime, Kiyokuma, Ryūzanwake, Sazaewake, Yasomagahiko, Asafure, Yūfure, Hifure, Yamaarashi, Hirowaka, Funakihime, Taitohime, Oniwaka, Saruhime, and Hiroyoriwake.

From the side of Daijizaiten Ōkunihiko, the deities who attended in stately splendor were Ōtakahiko, Nakayoriwake, Ushikumowake, Katoriwake, Kanikumowake, Fujitakawake, Takatoriwake, Tōyamawake, Shikokuniwake, Kuranami, Kakatora, Aratorawake, Kunihirowake, Izumowake, and Takahiko.

§ 2
§ 2: The Ultimatum to Mount Manju and Iwakusuhiko's Unyielding Resolve

Furthermore, among the twelve divine chiefs of the Eight Kings and Eight Chiefs, all were to attend except Iwakusuhiko and Mizuhowake of Mount Manju. Thereupon Hachiō-Ōkami of the land of Tokoyo, insisting that they must attend, sent Sazaewake as an urgent messenger unto Mount Manju at the head of numerous bird-boats, saying:

"If you do not attend this council for the unification of the spirit world and for peace, we shall, through our mighty united power, regard you as destroyers of both the spiritual and material realms, and it will become unavoidable to dispatch a force to subjugate you."

Having had this threatening missive delivered, Ōyashimahiko-no-Mikoto, Iwakusuhiko, and the deities of Mount Manju Castle hastily convened an emergency council.

Maintaining a staunch and resolute posture, Iwakusuhiko said:

"Even if all the divine spirits of the world unite as one and march against Mount Manju, I shall confront them through the power of the Kami of Mount Ryōju. With a single breath, I shall blow back and scatter an enemy host of hundreds, thousands, or millions, and in accord with the spirit of the laws of Heaven and Earth, I shall subdue and pacify all wicked spirits and demons beneath heaven, establishing the great divine age that fulfills the will of Kunitokotachi-no-Mikoto. Unto the divine power of the living and true Kami, no evil spirit or demon can ever stand as rival. Since this council in Tokoyo is the manifestation of a grand conspiracy by Tokoyohiko and Ōkunihiko, to take part in such a gathering and confer with them is more perilous than plunging into the sea while embracing a great boulder. Therefore, not a single divine chief of our mountain must attend."

When he had thus argued, Ōyashimahiko-no-Mikoto was the first to express agreement with the counsel of the Eight-King Kami Iwakusuhiko, maintaining that they must absolutely refuse to attend.

§ 3
§ 3: Mizuhowake's Proposal and the Walkout of the Righteous Deities

Hereupon Mizuhowake rose and said:

"The words spoken by Ōyashimahiko-no-Mikoto and Lord Iwakusuhiko are indeed entirely reasonable; yet one cannot resist the power of the times and seasons. I believe that conforming to the trends of the age is an indispensable requirement of divine governance. Let us promptly dispatch some divine chief from our mountain to take part in this grand council. If, by any chance, there are many chiefs who do not wish to attend, I pray that you dispatch me as the envoy. However great the dignity and virtue of the divine spirits of Mount Ryōju may be, and even if it touches upon some aspect of the laws enacted by Kunitokotachi-no-Mikoto, if we fail to attend this assembly of deities, we shall have no words of defense when the divine spirits of the world attack us all at once as wicked chiefs who destroy world peace. Mount Manju now stands at a critical hour of life or death. For the peace of both the spiritual and material realms, I cannot help but fervently desire that we decide to attend."

When he had thus argued, the divine chiefs, observing the scene, held their breath in silence to see how matters would unfold. At this moment, Kamikuniwake-no-Mikoto stood up and voiced fierce opposition to Mizuhowake's argument for attendance.

Mizuhowake was greatly infuriated and shouted:

"You are deities who broke the laws of Heaven and Earth, placed in custody upon this mountain by Kunitokotachi-no-Mikoto—nay, mere dependents! You possess no right to interfere in what we, the Eight-Chief Kami, discuss. Leave this chamber!"

His voice trembled and his face flushed red as fire as he shouted at them.

Hereupon, Ōyashimahiko-no-Mikoto, Kamikuniwake-no-Mikoto, Kototamawake-no-Mikoto, and Ōdaruhiko kicked back their seats and walked out of the council.

Left behind, Mizuhowake raised his shoulders, flared his elbows, and in a haughty posture pressed Iwakusuhiko with countless words, striving to expound the rightful reasons for attending. The divine chiefs in the fortress were split equally between approval and opposition, and no conclusion could be reached. Then Iwakusuhiko rose.

§ 4
§ 4: The Oracle of Kunitokotachi and the Rebuff of the Tokoyo Envoys

"There is now no other way than to abide by the divine oracle. You, Mizuhowake, shall kneel before the altar, beseech the divine decree yourself, and determine whether to attend or not based upon that divine revelation."

Leaving these words behind, Iwakusuhiko withdrew from the council. Thereupon Mizuhowake immediately went to pray at the Moon Palace (Gekkyūden) and reverently sought a divine revelation concerning this matter. Suddenly, Mizuhowake's body became paralyzed so that he could not move in the slightest. Mizuhohime, who had accompanied him, suddenly fell into divine possession, her body shaking up and down and from side to side, and the possessing Kami spoke through her lips:

"I am the Ara-mitama and Kushi-mitama of Kunitokotachi-no-Mikoto. Because this gathering of deities stems from the elaborate conspiracy of Tokoyohiko, Ōkunihiko, and their faction, not a single divine chief of this mountain must attend. No matter what hardships you may encounter hereafter, endure them well. Since this is the sacred sanctuary of the second divine administration, this fortress alone shall never be trampled by the enemy. I shall cause true and mighty divine chiefs and spirits to guard it firmly until the advent of the World of Miroku (567). Doubt this not in the least."

Having thus declared, the divine spirit departed from the maiden's body. At the same moment, the maiden's body was restored to normal. Having heard and witnessed this divine decree and its wondrous signs, Mizuhowake deeply repented of his past fault and apologized to the Great Deity in his heart; at that very instant, Mizuhowake's body was likewise restored to normal, regaining complete freedom of movement. Firmly resolved, he immediately gave thanks unto the Great Deity, conducted a solemn festival of returning to the origin and requiting the foundation (hōhon-hanshi), apologized to the Eight-King Kami and to the divine chiefs under Ōyashimahiko-no-Mikoto, and resolutely proclaimed to the envoy of the land of Tokoyo that not a single divine spirit of Mount Manju would attend. The envoy of Tokoyo, Sazaewake, looked utterly disappointed and glared at the assembled deities with malicious eyes, saying:

"Do as you please! But do not leave room for regret in the days to come!"

Leaving this parting threat behind, he boarded the heavenly bird-boat and returned unto the land of Tokoyo together with his many attendants.

現代日本語訳

「もはやこの上は、神示（しんじ）に従うほかに道はない。汝、瑞穂別よ、神殿に拝跪（はいき）し、自ら神勅を乞い、下された神示によって出席するか否かを決するがよい」

磐樟彦はそう一言を言い残して退席した。そこで瑞穂別は直ちに月宮殿（げっきゅうでん）に参拝し、今回の事件に対する神示をうやうやしく奉伺したところ、たちまち瑞穂別の身体は麻痺して微動だにできなくなった。瑞穂別に付き従ってきていた瑞穂姫（みずほひめ）がにわかに帰神（神がかり）となり、身体が上下左右に激しく震動し始め、早くも口を開いた憑神（つきがみ）は次のように告げた。

「我は国治立命の荒魂（あらみたま）・奇魂（くしみたま）である。今回の神集いは、常世彦や大国彦ら一派の周到なる陰謀から出たものであるから、当山の神司は一柱たりとも出席してはならない。今後いかなる難関に遭遇することがあろうとも、よく耐え忍ぶがよい。ここは第二の神界経綸の聖場であるから、この城だけは決して敵に蹂躙されるようなことはない。真正の力ある神司・神人たちに命じ、五六七（みろく）出現の世までは固く守護させよう。決して疑ってはならぬ」

このように宣言して、神霊は姫の体内より去られた。それと同時に姫の身体は元の状態へと復した。この神勅とその様子を目の当たりにした瑞穂別は、大いに以前の非を悔悟し、心の中で大神に謝罪すると同時に、瑞穂別の身体もまた元通りになって自由自在に動くようになった。そこで直ちに大神に感謝を捧げ、荘厳なる報本反始（ほうほんはんし）の祭典を挙行し、八王大神（八王神たる磐樟彦）および大八洲彦命以下の神司たちに陳謝し、万寿山の神人は一柱も出席しない旨を、常世の国の使者に向かって断固として宣示したのである。常世の国の使者である蠑螺別（さざえわけ）は拍子抜けしたような顔色となり、一同の神人をいかにも不快そうな目でにらみつけ、

「勝手にするがよい。後日に悔いを残されるなよ」

と捨て台詞を残して天の鳥舟に乗り込み、数多くの従者たちとともに常世の国へと帰っていった。
