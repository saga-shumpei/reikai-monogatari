Chapter 14: The Giant Monster [164]

大怪物〔164〕

§ 1

Here, Tamasumihiko, a subordinate retainer of Ōshimawake, obtained the permission of Yatsuō-ōkami and ascended the platform with great vigor, and while wearing a smile, proceeded to inspect the heads of all the assembled deities one by one, deliberately squaring both his shoulders, saying:

"Ah, deities of the whole assembly, execute the cleansing method of your earholes, make the ventilation good, and respectfully listen to the lofty argument and preeminent discourse that I deliver. As for me, the subordinate retainer of the foremost chief retainer of the Yatsuō God Ōshimawake—whose thunderous fame is unhidden in Mount Nankō and roars through heaven and earth—and moreover the subordinate retainer to that retainer, Tamasumihiko, is myself. Whether the sun shines or it clouds over, even if the Castle of Tokoyo burns down, so long as the famed castle of Mount Nankō is safe, I feel not the least pain or itch, and laughingly let it pass; such an exceedingly magnanimous great hero and bold man is Tamasumihiko! Even as the proverb says, 'Beneath a brave general there are no weak soldiers; beneath the navel there are no breasts; beneath the mouth there are no eyes; merely between the eyes and the mouth, there is only such a high nose.'"

While speaking, he clenched the fingers of his right hand into a fist, placed that fist upon his own nose, clenched the fingers of his left hand to form a fist as before, stacked it atop his right hand, and again withdrew his right hand to stack it atop his left hand, and again withdrew his left hand to stack it atop his right fist, alternately withdrawing and stacking his hands while extending his arms upward and forward, saying:

"Because I am a hero with such a high nose, praised as the leading actor of Mount Nankō, I am a noble being possessing both flower and fruit. The one extolled as the flower of the capital of flowers is myself, Tamasumihiko, who enjoys the renown of possessing wisdom, benevolence, and valor combined. Lest you lose your wits upon hearing my identity, tumble from the platform, and break or sprain your elbows, I give this warning to all the divine persons who have mounted the platform."

Thus, mimicking Hiroyoriwake, he assumed a thoroughly arrogant posture, and once again, as before, piling the fists of both hands high before his nose alternately, waving his hands and stomping his feet with a rumble, while dancing elegantly and amusingly, he improvised a song on the spot. That song:

"Kon-kon-chikichin, kon-chikichin, To begin with, the great monstrous assembly of this time, Tokoyohiko of the land of Tokoyo, By the fox-courage of Princess Tokoyohime, Finally opened this fox-like conference, For the sake of that monstrous peace; Eight hundred and eighty-eight deities, Gathered together, utterly foolish and obscure, A miserable gathering of dogs' tails, Terrified like Kishimojin of Iriya, Prayers muttered by ogres and evil serpents; The bell ringing 'no, no, hopeless,' Not the bell of the threefold assembly of Miroku, Is the signal pushing open The gates of hell, and heard by listening ears; The ears and tongue go to paradise, While the platform ascending is a mountain of needles; Steps painfully out of sync, A strangely foolish and weird assembly; Blowing conch-horns in mud paddies and under the open sky, The white-tailed tanuki drumming its belly, Mud smeared over the face of the gods, Everywhere nothing but mud-dwelling ghosts, Mud sticks, drumbeats, covered in mud, Like crucian carp intoxicated on mud, Forced to vomit mud and laughed at; Such an absurd blunder, Search where one may in Tokoyo, Could never occur again, sheer humiliation; Scratching his head in sheer astonishment, Looking up, there is the Milky Way in the heavens, The shadows of tens of millions of stars; Crying 'I want it, I want it!' for supreme command Over the divine realm, clearing their throats, Tokoyohiko playing the feigned innocent, Tokoyohime in the pitch dark of the night of Tokoyo; Surely their hearts, like the thin cord Of a loincloth, shift right and left, Their calculation missed by an order of magnitude; The old fox of the Kasuga woods, Fed upon and spurned, like Moscow's— The sheer gall of Michinukihiko; The dear, sweet Kasugahime, Having the rump-fur of his long tail Plucked out, saying he would forsake The sacred office of the Eight Kings, such a coward, Balding from the rear into the mud, With an expression as if on the verge of tears; Burned once by hot soup, Now blowing on cold sliced fish—how laughable! The Southern High Mountain bears the name Of an impregnable iron fortress, The rump-fur of Ōshimawake of the Eight Kings, Who boasted to the whole realm, Bewitched by the foxes of Yashima, Was completely plucked out, not a single hair remaining; Ahead of the castle, he himself foremost, Drowning in the love of that hussy princess, Kasugame, Pitifully and by his own doing, Forsaking the sacred office of the Eight Kings, The blind fortress of Tokoyo; Cast down into the mud paddies under the open sky, Did the mustard sting?—shedding tears From both eyes, how laughable! Though the bald head shines bright, The soul within has no light; Hasten to polish the soul, Become the god Tamasumihiko, And apologize to the deities of the holy land; If you refuse that, before me, Roll up your loincloth three or four times, And crying loudly, 'Bow-wow-wow!' Bark and run around, you bald Eight Kings! While talking of the times and seasons, The nose-god Imashirohiko, Rubbing his nose and rubbing his eyes, Chattering away the sleep-talking of a half-asleep face As if reciting a million dharani, A great kagura of mere lip service; A lion dance would be well and good, The empty swagger of the Eight Kings' god From beyond the skies of Mount Tenzan, The bottom of his heart thumping, Imashirohiko shook and trembled; Whether yuzu or mandarin oranges, Or measuring kumquats by the bushel, A fathomless, bottomless Crucian carp in a pond he might as well become; Coming to squeeze and suck fresh blood, The deity Katoriwake appears Upon the platform, and the posture Of an ash-colored cat washing its paws; A black face smoothly stroked, The bridge of the nose slapped sharply, Punishing himself with his own hand, Sparks flying from his eyes, elbow broken, Weeping unmanly tears, crying 'It hurts, it hurts!' A truly pitiful state of affairs; The bent waist of Yasomagatsuhiko, His heart, his nose, and the nape of his neck, A Magatsukami so thoroughly matched, A monster of a slant-faced grasshopper, Exposing his extraordinary, grotesque figure without omission Upon the high seat; Hiroyoriwake wandering aimlessly, Back and forth, left and right upon the platform, Blowing a great conch and dancing frantically, Repeating the folly of Katoriwake, Once again suffering sheer disgrace, Cast and thrown out of the hall, Such foolishness is pitiful even to the eyes of onlookers, Pitiful even to the eyes of onlookers; Kon-kon-chikichin, kon-chikichin."

The deities and officials filling the hall were intoxicated by the beautiful voice of Tamasumihiko singing in an amusing melody; forgetting that they were in the sacred assembly seats, they merely hung their mouths wide open, strained their ears, and stared in wonder.

When they suddenly raised their faces, it was a mistake that he had appeared to be Tamasumihiko until now; a fierce god with a robust frame resembling a Niō opened wide his mirror-like eyes, stood upright on the platform holding a thick iron club, and fixing his gaze toward the Great Deity Hachiō, thrust out a tongue like flames and steadily pressed forward; whereat even Tokoyohiko and the deities and officials filling the hall had their courage shattered by this sight, their complexions turned ashen like earth, and they began trembling violently as if visiting a fire after an earthquake. This fierce god gradually turned to smoke and vanished. The sun sank into the western mountains of Tokoyo, and together with the voice of the long-crowing cock heralding dusk, the grand assembly of the third day was once again closed in ambiguity. Throughout this time, Tamasumihiko remained in his seat, indulging in deep sleep. Therefore, he did not know of this spectacle even in his dreams. Which deity's incarnation was it indeed? The true identity of this monster will surely be resolved someday. As to what manner of deity is the Great Monster of the Three Thousand Worlds indicated in the divine oracle, it should be generally deducible.

満場の諸神司は玉純彦の面白き節にて謳ふその美声に酔はされ、神聖なる議席にあるを忘れて、ただ口のみ、あんぐりとし耳を澄まし、目を見張りゐたりける。

ふと面を上ぐれば、今まで玉純彦と見えしは謬りにて、仁王にまがふ骨格たくましき荒神は、鏡のごとき両眼をカツと見開き、太き鉄棒をひつさげ壇上に衝立ちながら、八王大神の方を見つめて火焔のごとき舌を吐き出しヂリヂリと攻めよるにぞ、さすがの常世彦も満座の諸神司もこの光景に荒胆をくじかれ、顔色土のごとくに変じ、わなわなと地震の孫の火事見舞のやうに震ひ出しける。この荒神は次第々々に煙のごとく成りて消えたまひける。日は常世の西山に舂きて、早くも黄昏つぐる長鳴鶏の声とともに、第三日目の大会議はまたもや有耶無耶に閉ぢられたりにけり。玉純彦は依然として此の間自席に眠りを貪りゐたるなり。そのため、この光景を夢にも知らざりける。はたして何神の化身なりしぞ。この怪物の正体はいつの日か氷解さるるならむ。神諭に示されたる三千世界の大化者とは如何なる神にましますか、たいてい推知し得べきなり。
