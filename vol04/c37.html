Volume 4 • Chapter 37

The Fullness of Time

時節到来〔187〕

§ 1

The holy ground of Jerusalem, the central point of the governance of the earthly divine realm, had lost its leader as mentioned above and bordered on utter ruin; while the multitude of deities lamented and cried out as though the Great Sun Deity had hidden within the Heavenly Rock Cave, Tokoyohime alone, as if cherishing some deep intention in her heart, showed grief on her face merely out of courtesy, yet beneath it lurked an unmistakable air of triumph.

In the great hall of the holy ground, beginning with the Eight Kings and Eight Chiefs, together with Ōyashimahiko no Mikoto, Takateruhime no Mikoto, and the other eight million deities, each straightened their posture and sat in rows, deliberating to swiftly designate a successor to the Archangel; and first of all, they decided to inquire into the divine will of the Ancestral Creator Great Deity and hold a ceremony. Consequently, recognizing that they must decide upon a deity to step forward before the Ancestral Creator Great Deity and present this inquiry, the assembly first selected Ōyashimahiko no Mikoto by a majority.

At this time, Ōyashimahiko no Mikoto stood up, faced all the assembled deities, and said,

"Having previously been sentenced to confinement on Mount Manju for the crime of violating heavenly law, even now as I assemble in this holy ground, I am seized by an inexplicable feeling of dread. However much it may be by the selection of all the deities, for one whose sins have not yet been pardoned to stand before the most austere and noble Ancestral Creator Great Deity would be an act of sheer shamelessness; therefore, this duty alone I must firmly decline. I pray that another deity be chosen in my stead."

Expressing true sincerity of humility upon his face, he finished speaking and returned to his seat. The deities throughout the hall also understood his feelings, and none attempted to compel him otherwise.

The one selected next was Takateruhime no Mikoto. However, she too, like Ōyashimahiko no Mikoto, said,

"I am one who was ordered into confinement in the fields of Eden for the crime of violating heavenly law—a person living in the shadows, so to speak. Even were I without sin, in this gathering of so many talented and distinguished figures, it is not fitting for a woman such as myself to obtrude and inquire into this sacred business. I earnestly desire that someone else be selected."

§ 2

Having spoken thus, he returned to his seat.

Hereupon, the divine beings had no reason to reject his words, and by unanimous consent, they selected Tokoyohiko. Since Tokoyohiko had now completely returned to the great spirit of supreme goodness and supreme beauty, without a single speck of desire in his heart, and had resolved solely to serve the deities with utmost sincerity and assist in a part of the divine work of the Ancestral God of the Land, being selected for this important divine duty filled him with great shame; he immediately stood up, faced the divine beings throughout the hall, and said:

"Unlike Ōyashimahiko no Mikoto and Takateruhime no Mikoto, I have never once held the office of Chief Angel; driven merely by idle ambition, I obstructed the divine work of the Great God, ultimately causing suffering to the divine beings of the holy land and further troubling the divine mind of the Ancestral Great God of the Land as a grave sinner, such that even now, coming to this holy land and turning my face to the divine beings brings me heartache, and I cannot cease my repentance day and night. Nevertheless, that someone such as myself should be selected for the duty of inquiring into the sacred mind of the Ancestral God is truly most troubling, and before the Ancestral Great God of the Land, I cannot bear the extremity of my dread. I hope that this decision will be promptly revoked,"

he said, and returned to his seat.

At this moment, Ōtakabewake stood straight up from a corner of the hall and addressed the divine beings:

"Regardless of ordinary times, at a critical juncture of life and death like today, it is not the time to vainly repeat words of modesty and dispute right and wrong. I believe that adapting to circumstances and responding to crises is that which divine beings should strive for most. Setting aside all personal feelings and not reproaching the past, I hope that Tokoyohiko will vigorously exert himself for the sake of the present and the future,"

and at this proposal, the divine beings unanimously selected Tokoyohiko with one voice. Tokoyohiko, too, deemed that under today's circumstances, to refuse would instead trouble the divine mind of the Great God and ignore the goodwill of the divine beings, so here he resolutely made up his mind and accepted the task of inquiring into the divine mind.

§ 3

All the deities and people present were elated as though the moon had risen in the dark night, clapping their hands in celebration, and their shouts of "Uro, uro" were so valiant as if to split heaven and earth asunder.

Hereupon Tokoyohiko, as the representative of all the deities and people, advanced into the inner sanctuary where the God-Ancestor resided and respectfully inquired after the divine will regarding the successor Chief Angel, to which the God-Ancestor uttered merely a single word:

"I appoint Tokoyohiko as Chief Angel."

So He declared. Tokoyohiko was overcome with awe, knowing not where to put himself, but raising his head, said:

"Toward the God-Ancestor, for us, who until this day have been ordered about by utterly evil, lawless wicked spirits and have committed grave sins, to receive this gracious decree is beyond my station. I humbly pray that, if Thou wouldst appoint Oyashimahiko no Mikoto as Chief Angel, it would be a matter of utmost gratitude."

Thus he offered his counsel with supreme sincerity visible upon his countenance, but the Great God-Ancestor said:

"There is no second word in the speech of God."

§ 4

Thus he spoke once more, closed the jeweled sliding doors, and entered the inner sanctuary.

Here at last Tokoyohiko became the Archangel, was appointed to a position above the Eight Kings and Eight Heads as the supreme governor of the earthly divine realm, and was granted the name Tokoyohiko-no-Mikoto.

"Wait for the appointed time; even the gods cannot contend against the appointed time. Once the time comes, even roasted beans will bloom."

That this was shown in the Divine Revelations surely refers entirely to such a matter.

Until now, Tokoyohiko-no-Mikoto, striving to become the Archangel of the Holy Land, had added hardship unto hardship, becoming the target of the divine mortals' curses and ridicule, and countless times at the very final stage had his purpose destroyed; yet now, casting away all desire, returning to wholehearted sincerity, and by the virtue of entrusting all things to the will of the gods, naturally, just as a single paulownia leaf falls in an autumn field when there is no wind, he received the personal confidence of the Great God, bore the trust and hopes of the divine mortals, and ascended to a position of high importance.

Thinking upon this, it must be said that the power of sincerity and repentance is truly great.

The time has ripened, and the abandoned hopes have blossomed into flower;
