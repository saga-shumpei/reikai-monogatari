Chapter 13: Spring Field の進行 [1969]

春野の進行〔1969〕

§ 1

The vast high plateau to the west of Nakanogaha had been scorched black by the power of the true fire unleashed by Asakahime no Kami, leaving not a single withered leaf upon the earth and becoming bright and clear; yet to the east, marked by the dividing line of Nakanogaha, it was a wilderness of wild grasses where great serpents inhabited and strange animals lurked, and as midnight approached, they harmed all the crops or injured the young and old of the Kunitsukami, so that it had not yet reached the realm of complete peace.

Here Ashiharahime no Kami, who is the deity of Mihishiro, built a sacred palace of purification on a small hill called Sakuragaoka at the foot of Mount Takashi, surrounded the perimeter of the hill with a moat to prevent the invasion of evil deities, and was protecting the Kunitsukami of the surrounding area. Therefore, even in this Shimagene, which has an expanse of five thousand squareri, the Kunitsukami and various living creatures who bathe in the grace of Mihishiro no Kami and live in peace number only about four or five squareri. In short, the sphere where the authority of Mihishiro no Kami reaches is but about one-thousandth of the entire island.

Having spent a night admiring the moon in the forest amid the fields where ever-verdant pines grew lushly, the eleven male and female deities together with the party of Naganozuchihiko, the elder of the Kunitsukami, set forth with the dawn, each on horseback, heading straight and at full speed toward the sacred place of Sakuragaoka far to the east.

Shinmaibiko no Kami stood at the very fore, and while on horseback, chanted thus:

'What a propitious day is this today! Setting forth from afar from the palace of Takachifu, whose name is exalted as the true supreme realm of Shibi-Tenkai, the eight deities who appeared manifest as the divine commanders of Asakahime, stirring up their stern and valiant hearts, crossing over myriad ri of mountain and field, traversing myriad ri of ocean waves, descending from heaven to this very island-root of the reed-plains where the earth was yet young—when you cut and brought forth the flint, wondrous in the world, flash by flash the sparks of fire scatter in all four directions, and at the moment they suddenly catch fire upon the roots of the grasses in the wild moor, fanned by the sea breeze crossing the ocean waves, they suddenly sweep and burn up the thickets of the wilderness in a single stretch of a thousand ri.

The Magatsumi of Guronosu and Gorosu, wrapped in smoke and burned by fire, with no means whatsoever of what to do, hid deep within the waters at the bottom of their eternal dwelling place, the Gross Swamp. Asakahime leisurely crossed the burned wilderness, made her night's lodging of dewdrops as she arrived at Oshigaoka at dusk, and waiting for the dawn, she entrusted four deities with the conquest of the Magatsu; whereupon the four deities, advancing valiantly, caused the Magatsu lurking in the depths of that deep swamp to face them, and as they respectfully recited the Amatsu-Norito and proclaimed the living Kototama, even those stubborn Magatsu faltered and retreated, though suffering yet persistently.

Suddenly a roaring voice was heard, "Uuuu-", so sharp that even heaven and earth seemed to split asunder; while the deities performed their divine exploits, the Magatsumi raised clouds, made rain fall, manifested their true forms as dragon-serpents, and fled away like a mist toward the valleys of Mount Takashi. Here the four deities, before the august presence of Asakahime who dwelt at Oshigaoka, proclaimed in every detail the full account of their exploits; and the Himegami, rejoicing at their achievements, together with Zuchihiko, secretly spun the threads of her divine providence as she intended to advance to the pure courtyard of Sakuragaoka.

Turning the wide stream of Nakanogaha that blocked the way into dry land by the light of the Kototama, and thinking to cross over to the east of the river, we respectfully went forth to meet them, following Ashiharahime no Kami, and offering our utmost gratitude for the immense grace by which she punished the Magatsumi of this Shimagene; and as dusk quickly drew near, spending a night of the moon in the shade of the pines in the forest amid the fields, talking together each as our heart fancied, the state in which we rejoiced and took pleasure was a joy and delight like unto the opening of the Heavenly Rock Cave. The eastern sky grew tinged with madder, and splitting the purple clouds, the Amatsu-Hi ascended in abundant prosperity; the singing voices of a hundred birds and a thousand birds chanted the eternal spring, while the white plum blossoms blooming and fragrant here and there, wonderfully escorted by Kalavinkas and with true cranes singing, the eleven deities, accompanied by Zuchihiko, traversed the wild moor, heading straight for the palace of Sakuragaoka—how valiant is their advance! Ah, kannagara kamukamu, reverently and deeply honoring the divine true words and grand providence of the Gods, morning and evening we perform purification, and through the blessing of the living Kototama, to go forth opening up the new land of Toyo-Ashihara—this indeed is a joy. As Sakuragaoka draws near, the blowing breeze is fragrant, and deep in the distant moor it burns bright—'

かく神々は馬上にて日長の退屈さに交々御歌詠ませつつ、其日の黄昏るる前、漸くにして桜ケ丘の聖所に着きたまひ、迎へまつる数多の国津神等の敬礼をうけ、新しく築かれし八尋殿に上りて、月を賞め夜桜を讃へながら、短き春の一夜を過させたまひける。
