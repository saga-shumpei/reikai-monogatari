Chapter 3: グロスの Island [1959]

グロスの島〔1959〕

§ 1

As Shibitenkai was still young in its land, and the shape of the land had not yet been completely determined in every nook and corner, the malevolent and eerie breath coagulated and solidified in various young land-masses, producing various fauna and flora. In particular, numerous bizarre animals inhabited there, causing the breath of the malevolent and eerie to scatter in all directions, and although it hindered the growth and development of all things in the universe, it was an unavoidable turn of events.

Here, when the Lord Ōkami was about to establish a complete and flawless country of the Gods, He entrusted the divine works of the spiritual world and the physical world to the two pillars of Amenomichitatsu no Kami and Ōmotoakitsuo no Kami. Amenomichitatsu no Kami proclaimed the Kannaagara no Daidō without growing weary day or night, and Akitsuo no Kami, wishing to create the ruling deities who should govern the land, wandered about here and there across the land. Meeting with the Mihishiro no Kami whom the Lord God had caused to be born, distributed, and placed, it became a divine decree that he should devote himself to the divine work of producing the Kunitama no Kami.

Among the evil gods, there were dragons with several heads, giant serpents, and tigers with wings, as well as wolves and bears, which inhabited the island. Some inhabited both land and water, and they easily refused to permit the daily governance of the righteous gods. Therefore, the Lord Ōkami dispatched gods possessing heroic qualities to the four quarters of Shibitenkai in order to fundamentally pacify, conquer, and totally annihilate these wicked demons.

Although the Mihishiro no Kami were all Goddesses, none of them resembled a graceful and beautiful appearance; rather, they were fierce, brave, and upright, and since only heroic gods of the age of the gods were chosen, that their actions were majestic and awe-inspiring was in accordance with the natural order of reason.

Fixing the foundation of the myriad-mile island-root for eternity, born as the eighth pillar deity of the Mihishiro, Asakahime no Kami, valiantly too, set forth on her long journey, enduring a hundred hardships, firmly and minutely solidifying the land-shape here and there. Having finished creating the island-root of Sano, making Nakachihiko the governor, and advancing further to solidify this young land of the myriad-mile island, she was welcomed by the Mihishiro no Kami, and at the sacred place known as the Hill of Myriad Miles, exchanged living Kototama, and bestowed upon the maiden of the Tadzoku tribe flint as a treasure of the land. Having finally finished her stay of seven days and seven nights, boarding a boat from the beach of Mikuraiya,

Lamenting the eternal parting, she advances as she divides the wave-paths across the tranquil sea-plain of myriad miles. Ah, Kannaagara Kamigami, as the Kototama of the Gods blesses, Asakahime no Kami safely, with her august spirit of Muzu making its appearance, the tale of her going forth into the land of the deep cloud-and-mist West, clearly and minutely without omission, Mizuki, sitting formally in the Sōunkaku, beseeches and prays with awe before the august presence of the Ōmoto Sumeru Ōkami for the blessing of the living Kototama. When I stand and dwell in the clear garden of the Sōunkaku where the pale winter sun shines, shaking the great sky, three airplanes come flying and dance madly,

making me deeply reflect upon the scenery of Japan in an emergency. Ah, Kannaagara Kamigami, the tale that I go on relating, blessing with the living Kototama, as it becomes a means to save the emergency Japan, as a blessing for the sake of the Dao and for the sake of the August Country, I recount it with reverence and awe. May my Kototama be blessed! May my Kototama have life!

When the Iwakusunobune on which Asakahime no Kami was riding wove through the large and small islands to the right and to the left, and as the sun was drawing toward dusk, she approached the island of Guros, which was rumored to be a gathering place of Magatsukami. The island of Magatsukami, true to its name, suddenly spewed black smoke in all directions, wrapping the sea surface in darkness so that even the august boat became invisible.

On this island of Guros, a fierce and wicked serpent god named Goros inhabited, and employing numerous ugly gods, he waited in readiness, intending to invade all the islands at the first opportunity. Now, when the august boat of the Mihishiro no Kami drew near to this island, the two giants of the island of Guros, the Magatsukami Guronos and Goros, called together all the Magatsukami, and frantically raged, determined to hinder the approach of the august boat.

§ 2

Asakahime no Kami chanted a miuta.

Crossing two hundred kairi, dusk has fallen, Approaching the Island of Guros as it seems. Hearing that Guronos and Gorosu, the wicked Magatsukami, Lurk within this island, I have brought the mifune near. Magatsumi make this their final stand and, spewing forth Kokuen, wrap the yomo in darkness. With the light of the water and fire of Kototama and the flint-stones Granted by the Kami, we shall make firm our resolve. However fierce the might of Magakami may be, If we burn them with fire, they will easily perish. However strong the power of Magakami may be, They are surely things devoid of the power of Makoto. In the hardship of fighting within the darkness of dusk, Floating upon the waves, let us wait for the morning.

Ubugamihiko no Kami chanted a miuta.

Reverend are the mikoto of Himegami; it is good to wait for Asahi and destroy Magakami. We shall eradicate the seeds of Magatsumi that obstruct Kamikuni And decree it a peaceful Mikuni. The darkness of dusk covers the entire Unabara, Yet I possess fire. Illuminating the mifune with mahi, let us wait quietly For akegata near the island of demons. Ah, a delightful journey upon the sea-routes, To advance while witnessing the hundred mischiefs of Magakami.

Tachihikohiko no Kami chanted a miuta.

Even the magami of the Island of Sano have gathered here, Fiercely striving to block our path. Though wrapped in curtains of black clouds, my ship Remains peaceful in the light of mahi. It is joyous to think of waiting for akegata and utterly Burning away the island of demons. Magakami, if you come submissively forth Before my landing, I shall pardon you. Begging Himegami for your lives, I shall save and succor you through the path of Makoto. Thinking of this as a life of but a single night, how truly pitiful The bodies of Magakami have become!

The black clouds surging from the island of Guros swelled up gradually like rising mountain-peaks, spreading out and enveloping the surrounding sea-surface in true darkness, while pale-white fireballs flew, raged, and circled around the mifune like a battle of fireflies, drifting together with an uncanny gloom.

Asakahime no Kami was not startled in the least; peacefully gazing upon the various maneuvers of Magakami, She chanted a miuta.

How fascinating these Magakami may be, Flying as pale-white fires in the sea of darkness! The fire of Magakami shines pale and white without heat, Are they ignis fatuus or nether fires, lacking warmth? Though seen as fireballs, they neither shine nor are they hot, Drifting merely like jellyfish. Tomorrow, I think to direct Guronos and Gorosu elsewhere And purify this island of demons. Wearied by the long journey of the yashioji, It is joyous to behold the sport of Magatsumi. Becoming fireballs in the hundreds of thousands, raging and frantic, Their appearance is wondrous to behold upon the ship. My ship floats upon the waves yet moves not, Tethered to the anchor of living Kototama.

Tachisehime no Kami chanted a miuta.

While fiercely concealing the moon of the mifukai, Black clouds rise from the roots of the demon-island's peaks. Magakami raise these black clouds from deep within, Lurking, trembling, and raging, it would seem. The Island of Guros, where countless Magakami gather, Today I behold strangely enshrouded in dusk. Those fireballs that do not reflect upon the sea of dusk Are truly the signs of nether fires. Were they true mahi, they would shine even to the depths of the waves, Yet they emit only a pale light without even a gleam. Illuminated by the living light of Kototama, Guronos and Gorosu too shall perish and vanish. With the advent of the Kami of the Mihidashiro, That demon-island shall be born as a pure and pristine land.

Ameharehime no Kami chanted a miuta.

I am the Himegami of Amehare serving in attendance, I shall clear the open skies. One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, Gather, O hundreds of thousands of Kami! Illuminating the moon of the vast sky, Shall I reveal the heads of the Magatsumi of the demon-island?

Even as She thus chanted, the black clouds enveloping the upper skies of the demon-island were gradually blown away by the winds of Shinato, and the clear, bright, perfectly full and serene moonlight of the heavens was made to float forth, shining deep down to the depths of the waves.

Hereupon, the Magatsumi of Guronos and Gorosu, striving with all their might to destroy the Kami and others of the mifune before the breaking of the night, manifested the forms of dragon-snakes over one hundred seasons old, and while each of their multiple heads brandished horns resembling swords, the spectacle of their incessantly blowing flames toward the mifune appeared utterly terrifying.

§ 3

Asakahime no Kami, with a serene smile, chanted a miuta:

'How valiant the war cries of Guronos and Gorosu, As if to solace the journey I take. Though they vomit fire, clash horns, and their eyes flash, I feel not the slightest hardship. When I ponder the hearts of the Magami, Who roar and rage with all their might, Pitiful indeed do they seem. Be that as it may, until dawn upon this boat Shall I rest in peace of heart.'

Ubugamihiko no Kami chanted a miuta:

'Thinking it a life of but a single night, the frantic rage Of the Magami at their last is pitiful indeed. Faintly illuminating the Tokoyami, the Magami Exude flames from their many mouths. Viewing them as dim-burning torches, it is amusing; Though the moon shines brightly in the Misora, The light of Tsukiyomi grows ever clearer, And the clouds over the demonic island of Kane have faded. Here and there, the Kurokumo spewed forth by the Magami Are growing faint little by little. Beholding such feeble and scheming war cries of the Magami, An adventurous spirit stirs within me. When tomorrow comes, this island of Kane shall be entirely Burned and purified; away with the Magami!'

Okitatsuhiko no Kami chanted a miuta:

'The clouds of the demonic island, floating eternally Amid the wadanaka, have cleared in the moonlight. Floating upon the vast, clear expanse of the unabara, The island of Gurosu has fully appeared. This island, too, is broader than I had thought, And numerous Magami are rushing about in tumult. This island, too, was brought forth by the Lord Ōkami, As an Ikushima, so shall we purify it. Serving day after day in the divine works of Kami, We welcome once again a joyful tomorrow.'

Tachiyohime no Kami chanted a miuta:

'Thick and high, the Kurokumo stand here and there Across the island, yet by tomorrow they shall leave no trace. Is this the island of the Magami, who untimely raise Kurokumo and muddy the water and fire of heaven and earth? Making the Magami of this island submit to words, To bring forth a youthful land is a joy indeed. Was it a dream that I heard the Mihishiro no Kami resides Upon this island? The Kurokumo rise. Does even the Mihishiro no Kami conceal His divine form For a while before the fierce war cries of the demons?'

Aparehime no Kami chanted a miuta:

'Let us illuminate both heaven and earth with the divine light Of the living Kototama, and bring forth a youthful land! Serving as an attendant to Aparehime no Kami, I shall clear away the clouds of this island of Kane.'

The various Kami, looking toward the island of Gurosu and anticipating tomorrow's campaign while chanting miuta, closed not an eye in sleep, but sat peacefully at ease upon the ship of rock-camphor, conversing merrily and cheerfully of various matters as they quietly awaited the dawn of day, wondrous indeed.
