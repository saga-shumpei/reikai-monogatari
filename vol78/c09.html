Chapter 9: 邪 Kami Conquest [1965]

邪神征服〔1965〕

§ 1

At the Honei in Shinobugaoka, Asakahime-no-Kami, with Nuzuchihiko as her companion for conversation, was waiting with eager anticipation for the triumphant return of the Yohashiragami, while bearing a somewhat anxious expression as she wondered how the day's battle situation had turned out; at that very moment, the sound of horse hooves echoing bravely and the sound of bells ringing chian-chian resounded through the air on all sides, and Ubugamihiko-no-Kami, who stood at the head of the four divine generals, galloped at full speed to the Honei in Shinobugaoka, and reported the battle situation minutely and in detail to Asakahime-no-Kami through a divine poem.

By the divine word of Mihishirogami Hiding in the swamp of Guros To crush the Maga army The Yohashiragami at Ōnohara With the sound of horse hooves high As they advanced, beneath the shade of the Tokihagi Single pine tree Finding it, they rested there Nourishing the horses' vigor While discussing the strategy of the Kototama warfare Passing time, they mounted their horses once more To the endless burnt field As they advanced, by the roadside An old woman, a Kunitsukami whose face was distorted Calling herself Pheasant The great serpent of the swamp had quickly Fled and disappeared, so for the Kami Even to advance was useless She spoke with all her might to stop them

Weeping, the old woman spoke thus: The evil Kami of Guronos and Gorosu Have wounded us all Taken the lives of parents and children And having exterminated all the Kunitsukami Fled toward the Takasayama region The Kami turned their fine horses back To Shinobugaoka And should stop their expedition against Maga Thus she spoke in detail and reason With all her might, but- Recognizing rightly that it was a falsehood of Maga I quickly understood If we strike out the living Kototama Maga will revert to a serpent And flee like clouds and mist Hiding its strange form deep at the bottom of the swamp of Guros At this, we grew spirited

Spurring our horses and approaching the edge of the swamp When we looked, it was yet wider The faintly muddy swamp water Bubbled here and there The sight of tens of thousands of Magatsumi lurking Was visible before our very eyes We, the four Kami, Set our positions in the east, west, south, and north of the swamp And took up our encampment Chanting the Amatsu Norito And striking out the Kototama of seventy-five sounds Ever more broadly Reciting the heavenly counting song, Even Maga, as expected, quailed At the moment when it appeared to falter Heard high from the open sky The Kototama of the sound U brought blessing And the great serpent of the swamp revealed its true form High above the water, Ah, the pity of its writhing about!

The sound U heard in the open sky Was it the achievement of the sharp-sounding Kami Who protects Mihishirogami? Ah, Kannagara Kamigami Through the blessing of the living Kototama, Without a trace, the evil Kami of the swamp Raised clouds and fled away From then on, we offered the Great Norito of gratitude To the Kami of Heaven and Earth Having finished chanting, each of us Tracing the road by which we originally came Gathered in the shade of the single pine tree Discussed the battle situation, and Once more mounted our horses Safely along the distant field road To this hill where Your Grace resides Raising shouts of victory, we returned home Now, from here, crossing the swift rapids Of Nakano River, to the sacred place where the Princess of Mihishiro resides

Leading the Kunitsukami in a train Advance, O Kannagara We pray before the presence of Kami

Thus she made her return report, and Asakahime-no-Kami, while highly praising and lauding the achievements of the Yohashiragami, recited a divine poem.

By the preciousness of the achievements of the four Kami Shinobugaoka has revived The Kami who accomplished achievements unheard of even in the age of the mighty gods In the desolate moorland- The Magatsumi, terrified and afraid of the living Kototama, Fled away like clouds and mist, did they not? From today, the Kunitsukami of the burnt moorland too Will likely spend their days with tranquil hearts The endless, broad plain of the country, without a single shadow, Will shine brightly across; the divine majesty of Kami The Lord of the army for whom we waited and waited has returned While I remained sitting, I released the Kototama I have deeply realized in today's battle That there is nothing that surpasses the light of the Kototama The Magatsumi, seeking to obstruct once more at the foot of Takasa Mountain, Have perhaps moved thither? From today, crossing right over the great river of Nakano, We shall advance aiming for Takasa Mountain The achievements of the four Kami shall forever Become a tale told on the island of Guros

と復命し給ひけるにぞ、朝香比女の神は四柱神の功績を甚く賞め讃へ給ひつつ御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『四柱の神の功の尊さに 忍ケ丘は蘇りたり 千早振る神世も聞かぬ功績を 荒野ケ原にたてし神はも 曲津見は生言霊に怖ぢ恐れ 雲を霞と逃げ去りしはや 今日よりは焼野ケ原の国津神も 心安らかに世を送るらむ はてしなき広き国原隈もなく 輝き渡らむ神の御稜威は 待ち待ちし軍の公は帰りけり 吾居ながらに言霊放ちつ 言霊の光りにまさるものなしと 今日の戦に深く悟りぬ 曲津見は再び鷹巣の山の根に さやらむとして移りけむかも 今日よりは中野大河を打ち渡り 鷹巣の山をさして進まむ 四柱の神の功は永久に グロスの島の語り草とならむ』

§ 2

Nuzuchihiko of the Kunitsukami sings:

Bathed in the precious grace of the Kamigami, Peaceful from this day forth shall the Kunitsukami be; For ten long years of bitter hardship under Magatsumi, The curses have vanished and they are revived at last. All the Kunitsukami, without exception, Shall dwell in expansive ease upon this land; Even the Kunitsukami who dug deep and lurked beneath the earth Shall live once more upon the soil of Aragane. Building their homesteads upon the earth, from this day forth They shall bathe in the grace of the light of Amatsubi. Come then, let us serve as attendants to Mihishirogami And advance together to the Sugado.

The Kami Asakahime chants a divine poem:

Accepting the words of Nuzuchihiko, I shall advance to the Sugado. O Momogami, make haste with the preparations of your horses! Come, let us set forth across yonder river.

The Kami Ubugamihiko chants a divine poem:

Awe-struck by the divine command of my lord, we four pillars Shall serve in the rear and hasten our advance. Amatsubi shines brightly across the heavens, And the vast sky is clear and radiant on this day of journeying. The burnt field where the shadow of Magatsumi was once concealed— How clear and bright is the shining Amatsubi! With horses aligned along the fields where white plum blossoms bloom, How clear and bright is the path we shall advance upon! Ume trees that seal the slopes of hills on right and left Have begun gently to unclose their buds. Escorted by the fragrance of white plum blossoms, How joyful is this day as we advance to the Sugado.

The Kami Okitatsuhiko chants a divine poem:

Having valiantly won complete victory over the host of Maga, Once again we shall advance to the precious Sugado. Do the Kariyōbinga twitter among the plum trees, As if to celebrate my lord's divine progression today? True cranes align their wings and dance in circles In the sacred sky above this hill; The voices of magpies are heard ringing with bravery— Oshinogaoka is indeed a sacred sanctuary. Leaving behind the lodging of a single night's dew at Oshinogaoka, With lingering reluctance, I now depart.

The Kami Tachiyohime chants a divine poem:

Serving as an attendant to Mihishirogami, How we stood firm against the host of Maga! Having realized the virtue of the majestic light of Kototama, I am one who fears nothing anymore. My swift horse shakes its mane and neighs, Eager perhaps to hasten our departure today. Truly, this day when the hardships of the Kunitsukami are cleared away Brings the feeling that heaven and earth have cleared at last. The everlasting sky is high, and the earth of Aragane Is vast; right through the midst of it I shall go.

Hereupon Mihishirogami, the Kami Asakahime, having completed all preparations to cross the turbulent current of the Nakano River traversing the island of Guros, with her four attendant deities and the Kunitsukami Nuzuchihiko as her guides, advanced with the momentum of a tiger crossing a river on foot, singing divine poems as she went.
