Chapter 23: 天の蒼 Cloud River [1979]

天の蒼雲河〔1979〕

§ 1

As the divine rock-camphor boat summoned by Asakahime-no-Kami drew closer to the shore of the Island of Lamentation, little by little it reduced its scale and completely restored its original form, whereupon she caused the divine boat to advance close to the water's edge, and together with her steed successfully achieved a safe landing.

When climbing up onto the Island of Lamentation to look, black smoke billowed densely so that one could not distinguish an inch before the eyes, and though it was said to be twilight, the moon in the august firmament hid its light, and even the shadows of the plants and trees growing underfoot became barely visible.

Hereupon Asakahime-no-Kami, immediately upon landing, chanted the Amatsukamigoto, and when she caused the seventy-five living kototama to resound forth, the black clouds of the august firmament parted north and south with precise contours, drawing lines of pale blue clouds east and west just like the Milky Way, while Tsukiyominokami, traversing the exact center thereof, cast brilliant rays upon the ground, whereupon Asakahime-no-Kami chanted a waka poem.

Far across the ocean wastes, at the twilight hour, To the Island of Lamentation have I come. The black clouds shroud heaven and earth, And amid the raven-feather darkness, only the voices of lamentation sound, where neither black nor white is told. When I lift up my voice to chant the divine words and release the seventy-five kototama, the clouds on all sides scatter. The black clouds of the great sky part to left and right, And the pale blue of the firmament streams away to the west. Crossing over the river of pale blue clouds in the great sky, How brightly shines the shadow of the moon-boat! Even the Island of Lamentation, where the eight-branched serpent lurked forever and raged, shall live from this day forth. Have the voices of the Kunitsukami's lamentation ceased? Did the Magatsuhi flee at my kototama? Tonight, too, we shall spend the night under the moon, And when tomorrow dawns, we shall sweep away the Magatsuhi. Appear, O sharp-sounding deity of resounding cries, That you may stay the lamentation of the people and give birth to the land.

Hatsuhikonokami chanted a waka poem.

By the blessing of the living kototama of our lord, The clouds that shrouded heaven and earth have parted. The moon-boat drifts along the river of pale blue clouds in the great sky, Bathed in brilliance. Because the power of the wind blowing across the sea is strong, The sound of the waves beating upon the rocky shore is loud. Our lord's summoned boat is firmly tied to the rocky shore, For the waves are high. How calm is the myriad-riled ocean through which our lord crosses, It must be according to the will of the gods. By the grace of the Great Deity, the Lord of All, The achievement of our lord who goes forth is illustrious. Achieving illustrious deeds in heaven and earth, O deity of the august miter who makest things shine, Grass and trees alike are lashed by the sea-wind, Leaning all to one side is this island foundation. O Tsukiyominokami, if you keep this in mind, Pray illuminate the whole of this night as well. Behold the august proceeding of our lord creating the nation's spirit, O moon of the firmament, do not let yourself be dimmed.

The voices of lamentation suddenly ceased, The august miter deity has ascended and gone. The crooked deity seems to be putting away his spear and making ready to flee, For a wind has arisen. The sound of insects crying all night at the roots of the grass Sounds sad upon the Island of Lamentation. The voice of the owl crying out amid the branches of the luxuriant trees at the opposing tail Was sorrowful indeed. Hanging the moon upon the treetops of the ever-verdant pines, Let us rest our lodging for tonight. Pushing aside a thousand folds of waves and beating back the evil deities, Though I have served my lord and come hither, Having departed from the island-roots of Ashihara, various Raging attacks of the Magatsuhi we must have encountered. Though I have come hither in peace to the light of our lord as an aid to the sharp-sounding deity, The Magatsuhi-mi flee and hide, and in their going Strive with all their might to bar the way. The black clouds of the great sky gradually part and go, And the cloud-river of heaven has grown vast.

In the cloud-river of the firmament across which the moon-boat crosses, Stars gleam like fine sand. Though it is said to be a lodging of a spring night, when I hear the sound of the wind Blowing through the treetops, it feels like winter. Do the Magatsuhi-mi still have strength remaining, That they shake the pine trees of our lord's lodging?

Tachikuhikonokami chanted a waka poem.

Tachikuhiko, I, attending by the august side, Gazed in wonder upon a hundred divine works. Though I am a deity lacking in power, to have come To the island-foundation of lamentation for the achievement of our lord, Raising clouds and mists, how strange indeed Is the state of the ugly Magatsuhi-mi striving to bar the way. When I think that the kototama is fragrant amid the black clouds Of the troubled ocean wastes, I feel at ease. Against the kototama brought forth by the Lord Deity, The opposing Magatsuhi are perishing away. More and more the face of the moon crossing the heavens Becomes bright and clear, while growing ever more brilliant, Until even the pine-crickets crying at the roots of the grass are visible, So strong is the radiance of the moon-boat. Both the myriad islands and the islands of Ashihara have been subdued By the light-giving water and fire of our lord. This island, too, shall surely be ruled in purity, Since our lord of light has made his august appearance, For upon this island the ugly Magatsuhi have gathered together, And seem to be distressing the Kunitsukami and others.

Though grass and trees, birds and beasts alike,

Shall revive in the light of the Lord,

Waiting for the dawn, I shall advance, serving

As the humble attendant of our Lord unto the depths of the island."

Tatsuyohime no Kami chanted her miuta:

"Though the night storm rising in the grassy plain is fierce,

What shall I fear, for I am of the Kototama?

I too am a small kami born forth

Into the power of the Kototama of the Lord Ōkami.

When I think how the clustered and congealed breath of eujiya

Became Magatsumi, it is indeed pitiful.

Since they are Magatsumi formed

From the congealed turbidity of the water and fire of the Lord of Kami,

They evoke our pity.

Yet if Magatsumi should spread throughout Ametsuchi,

Shibitenkai would surely become darkness.

Though there is no kejime of good and ill, the Magatsu

Who disturb Kamikuni must surely be swept away.

Though swept away, the Magatsumi who gush forth yet again

As foul kurokumo leave no semusube.

Behind good deeds wicked magawaza

Lurk indeed; such is this Ametsuchi.

In the Ōzora the clouds clear away shidaishidai,

The misora grows blue and the stars are filled.

The blowing wind too subsides shidaishidai,

And the dwelling of the kami of light is peaceful."

Ameharehime no Kami chanted her miuta:

"Aha, aha, by the Kototama of the kami of light,

The misora clears as if being wiped clean.

As far as the eye can see, gleaming beneath the moon,

The yamanu of the island of lamentation are pure.

The kurokumo of the Ōzora clear away and the root of the island,

While the sayo deepens, grows bright.

As evening falls, if it were not for the kami of Tsukiyomi,

Magatsu would rage wildly in this Ametsuchi."

As the clouds cleared away without a single nook in the misora, Asakahime no Kami chanted her miuta, giving thanks to the Lord Ōkami:

"How arigata through the grace of the Lord Ōkami,

Hath my Kototama shone forth brightly.

One by one the clouds and mists retreat, and both the Ōzora

And the earth have grown bright indeed.

Through the night the voices of small birds grow clear,

Blessing the island that is about to be born.

Do kunitsukami dwell in multitudes upon this island?

The voices of lamentation were heard hither and thither.

The voices of lamentation hither and thither have also subsided,

And the wind crossing the grassy plain is faint.

The moon now hangs

Upon the luxuriant branches of the everlasting pine, and the night grows chill.

Gradually the sayo deepens, and the moon of the Ōzora

Hath begun to decline.

When tomorrow comes, aligning our steeds, throughout

As much of the root of the island as exists we shall ride around.

We shall save the sufferings of kunitsukami and burn

The dwelling place of magagami with the power of true fire.

Both mountains and fields are overgrown with grass, and hands and feet

Seem to be an island root where none can enter.

Magatsumi will watch for an opening and attack;

Let the four pillars of kami keep watch without sleeping."

Hatsuhikohiko no Kami chanted his miuta:

"Revering the divine words of our Lord, through the entire night

I shall keep watch and serve, opening my eyes wide.

With my sharp eyes, wide-open like...

I shall glare at and drive away Magatsu.

Illumined by the light of my wondrously sharp and round eyes,

Magatsu shall perish.

Yet when compared

To the divine light of Mitomokami, it was but firefly light.

Watching all around the sacred body of our Lord,

I shall firmly guard against the assaults of Magatsu."

Kiritachihiko no Kami chanted his miuta:

"What a fascinating journey it is, coming all day long

Across the myriad leagues of sea, sweeping away Magatsu.

Though there is no light of Amatsuhi, it is an island made clear

By the pure light of Tsukiyomi.

When I think that tomorrow we shall sally forth to the Kototama battle,

Joyful am I and my eyes grow clear.

Aligning the bits of our spirited horses, the grassy plain

We shall burn down and advance once more.

The prospect of the grassy plain

Burning and spreading in flames was truly valiant indeed.

When I think that tomorrow too I shall behold the might

Of the blazing wild fire, my heart is emboldened."

Ameharehime no Kami chanted her miuta:

"O kami, beneath the moon upon the grassy plain,

If you kindle a fire it must be fascinating.

When I see the might of the burning fire it is fascinating,

And the steed of my heart is also emboldened.

Yet when it reaches the dwellings

Where kunitsukami dwell, it must be pitiful."

Tatsuyohime no Kami chanted her miuta:

"Though the burning fire is fascinating, because the kunitsukami

Have suffered, let us wait for the morrow.

This island too, here and there upon small hills,

Seems to rise steeply, dimly seen in the moonlight.

In the grassy field growing thick and overgrown,

Countless great serpents must be lurking.

Waiting and standing ready for the dawn of tonight,

My heart is emboldened and I utter a war cry."
