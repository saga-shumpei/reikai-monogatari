Chapter 19: Spring Field の御行 [1975]

春野の御行〔1975〕

§ 1

Hereupon, the Kami Unarizu, having concluded the sacred rites of the Kenkokusai, once again became light and, accompanied by numerous Jiushin, rode upon purple clouds and concealed His divine form in one quarter of the Uchiu, while making the Ikukototama resound freshly as 'u-u-u'. Gazing up at His majestic and stern divine appearance and overcome by Kangeki, the Kami Ashiharahime chanted a Miuta.

'Aha, aha, becoming light, the Kami of Unarizu has returned to the Misora; morning and evening to protect the land of Ashihara, how noble are the words He proclaimed! By the wondrous Kototama of the Kami of Unarizu, the ugly Magatsu have concealed their forms; the land born from the achievements of the Kami of Unarizu and the light of the Kami of Asakahime is also bright, with the moon traversing the Hisakata sky shining clearly, and the dews of blessing dripping upon the land of Ashihara; by the light of the Amatsu sun, Moromoro grow and flourish, and by the dew of Tsukiyomi, Inochi is nourished; with moon, sun, and stars all clear, how high is the Misora of the land of Ashihara! From this day forth, Amatsu Kami and Kunitsu Kami, leading and ruling, shall solidify the land; the divine soul of the Kami of Unarizu and the divine soul of the Kami of Asakahime shall be enshrined in the Myaiko; to the right of the palace of the Lord God, I shall serve by enshrining the living Kototama of Unarizu.

In the pure sanctuary to the left of the great hall, I shall enshrine the divine soul of Asakahime; the divine souls of the three pillars of Kami shall be enshrined forever and revered as the protectors of the land; in the shrine where the divine soul of Asakahime is enshrined, I shall store the flint stone, the treasure of the land; the foundation of the land of Ashihara has solidified, and the divine souls of the three pillars of Kami shine brightly; the ugly Kami whom I had troubled with for many years have also disappeared, and this is a joyful day; alas, because my power was insufficient, I left things to the violence of the ugly Kami; from this day forth, I shall enshrine the three pillars of Kami and make the light of the Ikukototama shine; let the Kami join their forces and make their hearts as one to devote themselves to the divine age; in the great palace of the new Tokiwagaoka, if I settle down and pioneer the land; truly, since the achievements of the Kami are noble, the land of Ashihara must be Tokoyo.

May it pioneer onward, ever more eternal and prosperous, through the noble protection of the three pillars of Kami; in the great palace, the Jūyō divine banner flutters, illuminating the prosperity of the divine age!'

The Kami Asakahime chanted a Miuta.

'By the achievements of the Kami, today when the land of Ashihara is born is indeed a joyous day; the Kami of Unarizu has returned to the Misora; come, I shall set forth upon the journey of birthing the land; since the prosperity of the land of Ashihara has also become visible, I shall henceforth part from you, Okimi; may the divine words of the Kami Ashiharahime eternally remain free from misfortune and flourish; the day when both heaven and earth are clear-skied makes my heart, as I set forth on my journey, leap with courage; though I regret the parting on this day we separate, the journey of birthing the land cannot be stayed; from now on once again through the raging seas of Magatsu, across the waves of the myriad-ri ocean I shall advance.'

The Kami Ashiharahime chanted a Miuta once more.

'I pray you, allow me, permit me to escort Okimi all the way to the beach; because You are the Kami who brought a perishing land to life, my sorrow at parting is doubly felt; O how sad is this day of parting from Okimi, who is so majestic, gentle, and pure.'

Hereupon, as part of the retinue of the company of the Kami Asakahime, the Kami Ashiharahime led ten pillars of Amatsu Kami and Kunitsu Kami to escort them, following along so as to send them off all the way to the beach of Tokiwa where the boat of the Kami Asakahime was moored.

The Kami Hatsuhikobiko stood at the head, proclaiming the Ikukototama aloud while chanting a Miuta.

'Crossing over the myriad-ri ocean, we landed upon the island of Gurosu; blowing away the ugly black clouds of malevolent kami blocking heaven and earth; chasing away Gronosu and Gorosu who lurked deep at the bottom of the swamp; here the new island of Gurosu shines clearly with sun and moon, and the spring of Tokoyo is born; a hundred flowers and a thousand flowers bloom and fragrance fills the air, little birds sing and butterflies dance; in the sanctuary of Sakuragaoka, plums, peaches, and cherries all at once bloom and fragrance fills the air, instantly manifesting the scenery of heaven; together with the Kami Asakahime, we admired the flowers at Sakuragaoka, sojourned for three days and three nights, retreated to Shinobugaoka; and here at last the raw foundation of the land of Ashihara was born; the Kami who have resided forever upon the roots of this island, celebrating the birth of the land, gathered together from here and there, the shouts of joyous acclamation filled the heavens, flowing across the earth without end; the shape of the land at last determined, protecting Okimi, we shall once again divide the clouds and mists of the myriad-ri ocean and advance; proclaiming that we send off today's auspicious departure; the leader Kami Ashiharahime, with the attendant deities, escorts the divine progress of Okimi; that true heart echoes and resonates across heaven and earth, the Amatsu sun shines genially, and the light of the midday moon remains clear and bright;'

§ 2

The spring breeze crossing the great field, soft like pure cotton, while the voices of a hundred birds, a thousand birds, and insects grow ever fresher and clearer, sings of the spring of Tokoyo. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! Let there be light in today's imperial progress, let there be happiness in today's imperial progress.

Serving before the august presence of Goddess Asakahime, even as I part from the land of Ashihara, bathed in the rare views of spring, how nostalgic is this land of Ashihara! Wrapped in the gentle heart of Mihishirogami, unconsciously the days have passed away. This is a sorrowful day, parting for the mountains and fields of the new land, where the sky is high and the earth is wide.

The god Okitatsuhiko chants an august poem.

Lining up our horses across the endless great plain, it is a joy to advance toward the shores of Tokiha. In the depths of the field, the heat haze burns softly, and the breeze blowing across our faces makes spring echo. Crossing the great plain of Yayohi in the azusa-bow spring, I stand together with the haze. Beyond the clouds, at the limits of the haze, may the god of Ashiharahime make the food-land clear and bright!

The goddess Ashiharahime chants an august poem.

How precious is the joy of today, following along in the rear of Goddess Asakahime! When I look back and gaze upon the rear figure of Goddess Asakahime, her august body is completely filled with light, and her divine form has become light, illuminating the heaven and earth of the land of Ashihara. Compared to the light of Goddess Asakahime, I am but a small firefly; yet the flint-stone she bestowed upon me is precisely what has granted light unto me. With my soul radiating light through the merit of the bestowed flint-stone, I shall govern the land. The Duke, who purely brought to life my disordered world, is alas about to return; yet resolving never to forget the grace of Goddess Asakahime, I shall build a shrine and enshrine her forever.

The god Nozuchihiko, richly seated upon his horse, chants an august poem.

Serving Ashiharahime of Mihishiro, the joy of sending off the Duke of Light! Will the god of Mihishiro not remain in the divine land, but travel far away—how lingering the regret! The wind blowing across the great plain is also gentle, quietly sending off the Duke of Light. Do a hundred birds lament the departure of the Duke as well, crying out piercingly among the branches of the trees? Even the voices of insects dwelling and crying at the roots of the grass sound lonely today. Both heaven and earth and all creation alike lament and grieve the Duke's departure.

The god Takahiko chants an august poem.

Lining up our horses, I am a sorrowful one seeing off the god of divine light who goes away. From the moment the god of divine light appeared, the land of Ashihara was newly born. Takahiko has become a god serving by his side through the grace of the god of light. The grace of the god of light is one that, even should heaven and earth perish, we must never forget. Though heaven should rend and earth should sink, how could we ever forget the grace of the god of light? The various kunitsukami as well, revived by the merit of the god of light, and all living things dwelling upon this island, none fail to be drenched in the divine grace.

The god Teruhiko chants an august poem.

With even the clouds and mist clearing away without a trace, I, Teruhiko, have become a god by his side. Having seen off the god of divine light, atop my horse I cannot stem the tears of parting. Since the god of divine light descended from heaven, the island of Gurosu has been revived ever more freshly. Though the way is distant and the footpace of our horses swift, when we reach the shore the sun will have grown dim in twilight. Here and there, soaring proudly across the great plain, the light upon the pines of Tokiha is fresh.

The god Kiyohiko chants an august poem.

Lining up our horses to send off the Duke across the great plain where both heaven and earth are utterly clear and bright, serving closely the goddess Ashiharahime, the joy of sending off the god of light! Across the sea of the sky, whose azure depths are unfathomable, the boat of the daytime moon shines brilliantly clear. The heavenly sun glows purely in the august sky, guarding the imperial progress of the god of light. The skies over the hawk-nest mountains of Adashino clear up, and I worship from afar the august progress of the Duke. As we advance through the spring field where the heat haze burns, even the sound of our horses' hooves is clear.

The god Haruhiko chants an august poem.

With the clouds and mist clearing away without a trace through the achievements of the god of light, how clear is Ashihara! Entrusted as a pillar of the land of Ashihara, today I send off the god of light. I am one who has no means to detain the god of divine light, who abounds in divine works. Sending off Goddess Asakahime and spurring our horses forward into the spring fields, the heat haze burns. A precious divine age has at last been born, ever since the appearance of Asaka no Hime.

The Kami Okitatsuhiko chants a miuta:

'Manipulated by the threads of the Lord Kami's shigumi,

We beheld the kunigata of the new kuni.

Behold the merits of the Kami Unarizu and our lord's

Noble light—how we were startled by it!

My soul revives with such a feeling,

Serving as an attendant on today's miyuki.

The tenchi of the Ashihara kuni are purified,

The light of the day-moon shines clear in the misora,

The light of the Amatsu-hi is pure, shining

Thoroughly and without exception across the Ashihara kuni.'

The Kami Tatsuyohime chants a miuta:

'In the wakakuni where plum, cherry, and peach flowers bloom,

My joy as well has revived completely.

O out-of-season plums, emit your fragrance; o peaches, bloom,

Cherry blossoms, fall not, and bless the mikuni.

Parting from the kamigamitachi whom we are loth to leave,

Lonely is today's journey across the hirono.

Thinking of the mirror marsh where the wicked tunes of Gronos and Goros have perished,

Frightening indeed it is.

The horses traveling across the island-roots where the shikogusa have been burnt away,

How peaceful their journey must be!'

The Kami Ameharehime chants a miuta:

'How clear and bright are the skies as we cross the nobe

Of the wakakuni and make our way back along the return path.

Thinking of the merits of our lord whom the Kami Unarizu protects,

They are earnestly noble.

While we are together with our lord, even the kuni

Where the ferocious Magatsumi rage is nothing to fear.

Feeling as though standing firm upon a bedrock,

Morning and evening I shall humbly serve as an attendant.'

Maihiko sings:

'I, Maihiko, following in the rear,

Escort and revere the Kami of Light.

Descending to earth to dwell together with the Kunitsukamigamitachi and others,

How peaceful my heart has truly become.

Even my body, which once dwelt above the clouds, descending to the harsh metal earth,

How I realize the joy of it!

Humble though my body may be, the awe of escorting

The miyuki of the Kami of the Divine Light.'

Nariyamahiko sings:

'I, Nariyamahiko, revering the divine words,

Escort our lord despite my humble body.

Becoming one whose body is intimate with the harsh metal earth,

I enjoy the peace of my soul-line.

Serving in the rear of the two pillars of the mihishiro-no-kami,

How joyful today's journey on the path is!

Not casting aside even the humble officials of the kunitsukami,

How noble is the Kami who summons us to attend!.

May it be the divine will that both heaven and earth embrace and love one another,

And that the Ashihara kuni be opened up.'

Ikuehiko sings:

'With my liver-piercing heart clouded as it was,

I ponder the awe of serving as an attendant.

As one who has become an official of the kunitsukami,

How joyful it is to escort today's miyuki.'

Sakaehime sings:

'Though I am but an official of the kami of the northern kuni,

How undeserving yet joyful it is to serve as an attendant.

The grace of the Kami Ashiharame is one that

I shall never forget; devoting my true heart,

Having become an official of the kunitsukami, my liver-piercing

Heart has gradually grown calm.'

Yaehime sings:

'As the chief of the nearby kuni surrounding Shinogigaoka,

Entrusted with this post, I cry out in joy.

Since everything is simply in accordance with the divine bestowal,

I shall joyfully serve.

Guarding the two pillars of the mihishiro-no-kami,

I weep with the joy of serving as an attendant.'

Thus, as the Amatsukami and Kunitsukami each chanted their miuta, as that day drew towards dusk, they finally approached the seashore of Tokiwa and arrived at the forest of camphor trees, where they determined a dew-lodging for a single night.

『梅桜桃の花咲く稚国土に 吾楽もしく蘇りけり 非時に梅も匂へよ桃も咲け 桜も散らで神国を祝へ 名残惜しき神々等に別れ行く 今日の広野の旅は淋しも グロノスやゴロスの曲の亡びたる 鏡の沼を思へば恐ろし 醜草を焼き払ひたる島ケ根を 旅行く駒は安かりにけり』

天晴比女の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『天晴れ天晴れ澄みきらひたる稚国土の 野辺を渉りて帰り路につくも 鋭敏鳴出の神の守らす吾公の 功思へばひたに尊き 吾公と共にしあれば曲津見の 伊猛る国土も恐るることなし 磐石の上に佇む心地して 吾は朝夕御供に仕へつ』

真以彦は歌ふ。

『真以彦吾も後方に従ひて 光の神を送り奉るも 地に降り国津神等と倶に住む 吾心安くなりにけらしな 雲の上にありし吾身も荒金の 地に降りて覚る楽しさ 卑しかる吾身なれども御光の 神の御行を送る畏さ』

成山彦は歌ふ。

『成山彦吾は神言を畏みて 卑しき身ながら公を送るも 荒金の地に親しむ身となりて 吾魂線の安きを楽しむ 二柱御樋代神の後辺に 仕へて道行く今日の嬉しさ 国津神の卑しき司も捨てまさず 御供に召さす神の尊さ 天も地も睦び親しみ葦原の 国土拓けとの御心なるかも』

霊生彦は歌ふ。

『村肝の心曇りし吾にして 御供に仕ふる畏さ思ふ 国津神の司となりし吾にして 今日の御行を送る嬉しさ』

栄春姫は歌ふ。

『北の国土の神の司の吾ながら 忝なくも御供に仕ふる 葦原比女神の恵は永久に 忘れざるべし真心尽して 国津神の司となりて村肝の 心はややに落付きにけり』

八栄姫は歌ふ。

『忍ケ丘を廻れる近き国土の長と 吾任けられて嬉しさに叫ぶ 何事も神の依さしの儘なれば 吾は嬉しく仕へ奉らむ 二柱御樋代神を守りつつ 御供に仕ふる嬉しさに泣く』

斯く天津神国津神等は各自御歌詠ませ給ひつつ、其日の黄昏るる頃、漸くにして常磐の浜辺に近づき楠の森に着かせ給ひ、茲に一夜の露の宿を定め給ひける。
