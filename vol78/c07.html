Chapter 7: 四 Kami 出陣 [1963]

四神出陣〔1963〕

§ 1

The eastern sky gradually drifts with the vapor of dawn, and the voices of small birds chirping in the forests of Shinonome bustle, singing of the spring of Tokoyo. Here, Asakahime-no-Kami builds a tent at the highest point of Shinonome, designates it as the grand headquarters for the subjugation of demons, seats Nuzuchihiko by Her side as an observation post, and causes four pillars of hero deities of fresh and sharp spirit—Ubugamihiko-no-Kami, Okitatsuhiko-no-Kami, Tatsuyohime-no-Kami, and Ameharehime-no-Kami—to set forth toward the conquest of the giant serpent of the marsh. The name of this marsh has been called the Gros Marsh since antiquity, and it is the stronghold of the evil deities of Gronos and Goros, who, making it their eternal dwelling, disturbed the heaven and earth of Gros Island and came to wield violent might.

Although the great plain where the serpent deeply lurks was burned away as if wiped clean by the divine majesty of the living Kototama and the power of the true fire, it is hard to calculate when the evil deities might again infiltrate this marsh and wield their violent might; thus is this a conquest war for sweeping away that anxiety and forever maintaining the peace of Gros Island, how awe-inspiring it is.

Although the surface of the marsh shining in the morning sun faintly enters the field of vision, since it is a view from the heights of Shinonome, the distance is not extremely close; here the four pillars of deities whipped their swift horses, and anticipating immediate battle and immediate victory, with high-spirited courage, caused their divine poems to be chanted and advanced in a straight line.

Though the morning sun shines or clouds, Though the moon fills or wanes, The power of the true word saves the world. The Kami appears in the front, Standing and separating the good and evil deities, Giving birth to the starry heaven of Ziwei, By the divine word of the Lord Ōkami, One hundred and eighty countries and eighty islands, All of the obstructing Magatsuhi, Directing words to them through the living Kototama, Those Magatsuhi who do not listen are smitten and punished, Crossing the wilderness and crossing the mountains, Crossing the rapids of rivers, and upon this land Of raw metal, not a single speck Of impurity shall be left behind, believing thus in Kannagara, According solely to the will of the Kami, The precious deities of the Mihishiro Are distributed hither and thither, How awe-inspiring it is! The pillar of Kami whom we serve, The Mihishiro of Asakahime Starting forth from the Takachihuno-miya, Without fearing even the clamor Of the ugly demons of evil, Crossing mountains and crossing wide rivers, At last crossing the marshy roads of myriad miles, Directing words and pacifying the crooked deities of Sano's island roots, Building the land, Having finished building, appointing the Amatsukami And Kunitsukami as rulers, Establishing the eternal foundation, Parting the crests of the waves Of the ocean enveloped once more in mist Where neither black nor white can be distinguished, We advance. At the emergence of the Face-Victorious Kami, There was none of the Magatsuhi that did not submit. Crossing from there to the island roots of myriad miles, To the sacred place at Manyogaoka Ruled by the Hime of the Yatohime And the Tanazoku, Directing words and bestowing As the treasures of the land for all ages Flintstones upon the Kami, While bidding a sorrowful farewell, Crossing the ocean once more, Arriving at the island of Gros, Driving away the crooked views of Gronos and Goros By the divine light of the living Kototama And the power of the true fire, Purifying and clearing the great plain, Line up our steeds and step by step Arriving at Shinonome, Directing words and pacifying the ugly deities of the marsh in the wild, To ward off the calamity of Magatsuhi in the young land, Taking up camp at Shinonome, You send us forth. Ah, Kannagara, Kami, Kami, With the sword of the living Kototama bestowed by The Lord Ōkami, All of the ugly Magatsuhi Directing words and pacifying, or else slaying, As the departure for the purpose of governing This land-plain as a peaceful country. Be bold, be bold, all ye Kami, Advance, advance to the marsh of the wild Where the crooked deities eternally lurk. Against the divine host of the living Kototama There shall be no enemy in the world to oppose. Be bold, be bold, advance, advance, We are together with the Kami. Ah, Kannagara, Kami, Kami, Let there be light in the living Kototama, Let there be fortune in our Kototama. Though I advance to punish the Magatsuhi lurking in the young land of Gros, Does the giant serpent lurk having made a marsh in the very center of the boundless and wide moor? The grassy borders where reeds and rushes grow densely have been burned, yet there is no way to dry the depths of the marsh. The heavenly sun shines purely in the beautiful sky, illuminating the surface of the marsh. Today indeed is the first battle going forth upon the sacred road by means of the divine word of the Mihishiro Kami. If I look back, Shinonome is already wrapped in a curtain of haze. Even the end of the body of the giant serpent lurking in the marsh bottom of the wild where heat waves burn, With nothing obstructing even a single eye, the ugly marsh shines within the burned great plain. Come now, let the four pillars make their power as one, Shall we shoot the arrow of conquest of the living Kototama? Appearing one after another close to the surface of the marsh, High is the heavy breathing of our swift horses

Thus chanting as They advanced, They beheld an ancient pine standing loftily in the middle of the genya, extending its branches in all directions as though subject to no constraint, sealing the very ten with an appearance just like the opening of a great umbrella, and thriving so densely in its twigs that even the light of the taiyō scarcely reached the ground; deeming this most fortunate, the yohashira no kami dismounted and mounted Their steeds, and while resting for a while, concentrated Their michikara upon various sakusen and keikuwaku.

Ubugamihiko no Kami chanted a miuta.
