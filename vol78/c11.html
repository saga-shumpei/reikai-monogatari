Chapter 11: 初対面 [1967]

初対面〔1967〕

§ 1

Just as Asakahimeno-kami was about to step across and traverse the new land where the Nakanogaha had suddenly transformed into solid earth, Ashiharahimeno-kami, who was one of the eighty Yasohimegami that had descended from of old as the guardian deities of this island, accompanied by three male and two female jiushin, in order to welcome Asakahimeno-kami, at last arrived at this kahagishi,

As one chosen as a Yasohashira Mihishirogami, I am Ashiharahimeno-kami. Having beaten back the ugly magawaza of the shikogami, The Lord of Light has descended. Aha, aha, by the miisao of the Kami of Light, The island of Gurosu has dawned bright. Twenty hatatose ago, I descended to this island, Intending to open up the kuni. Yet because the fierceness of the magatsumi was strong, I could not venture beyond the river of Nakano. The Ōnohara, overgrown with reeds, Even the Lord of Light who opened it up, Bowing low in reverence, wept tears. Through the light of Asakahime who descended from the palace of Takachiho, The kumokiri in the yomo have cleared away. The magatsumi of Guronosu and Gorosu crossed the river, And hid themselves in the mountains of Takashi. From kefu on, by the isaoshi of the Kami of Light, I shall kotomu to the magatsu of Guronosu and Gorosu.

Asakahimeno-kami chants a waka.

Hoping to meet the Yasohashira Mihishirogami, Though I came parting the wilderness, Ever since the emergence of Akitsuhikono-kami, for how long a time Did you wait, O valorous lord! Hoping also to meet Akitsuhikono-kami, Though I have journeyed far to advance hither, From kefu on, rename the island of Guronosu, And let it be called the Land of Ashihara. In the spring fields where heat haze flickers as far as the eye can see, Have I met the Mihishirogami at last? The himegami whom I sought and came from afar, Was truly earnest in her presence.

Maimaikuno-kami chants a waka.

Serving Ashiharahimeno-kami, twenty hatatose Have passed since I reached this kunibara. Being a kami without strength, I had no choice but to look upon the magatsumi from afar. With the turning of a good day of eternal time, to worship The noble light of Asakahime, By that isaoshi of having the wilderness burned with real fire, The maga have fled away. Though the magagami hide their forms for a while, As time passes, they will surely rage again. If you are indeed the Kami of Light, Grant us flint as a treasure of the kuni.

Asakahimeno-kami chants a waka.

Granting the words of Maimaikuno-kami, I shall bestow flint as a treasure of the kuni. What use would even the glowing night-illuminating jewel be? It cannot equal the power of living real fire.

Maimaikuno-kami chants a waka.

How precious is the kamigoto of the Kami of Light, I shall receive it upon my neck and never forget. The flint bestowed by Asakahimeno-kami, Must surely be a treasure to conquer the maga. Releasing real fire upon the mountains of Takashi where daylilies grow lush, I shall drive away the maga. If we do thus, the kuni of Ashihara shall eternally Prosper in peace, according to the will of the Kami.

Hatsuhikono-kami chants a waka.

Serving my lord and reaching the Nakanogaha, I have beheld the miraculous omen of the living kototama! Eager to meet the Mihishirogami, I galloped across the burnt field, Joining night to day, how swiftly I came! Imbued by the divine spirit of the mighty Kami, the Mihishirogami, Though one of the Yasohime, shines brightly indeed. Serving the eight Mihishirogami, Have I met the Yasohashira Hime at last?

Narayamahikono-kami chants a waka.

How can I lack the means to comfort the lord Who has come from afar, in the midst of the wilderness? At all events, make haste to the miya of Ashihara, And I shall serve you to the utmost of my true words. At last the spring air drifts As I stand in the great field, full of joy. Flocks of true cranes form lines in the heavenly sky, Drawing circles as they welcome the lord. The voices of a hundred birds have grown clear, Ever since the Kami of Light emerged. From kefu on, within this land of Ashihara, I pray no black clouds of the ugly ones shall rise. Until kefu, the hazy mists lingered and black clouds Covered the heavenly sky, feeling like winter. The ohonohara, covered by bloody winds, Now breathes the fragrance of spring with the scent of ume. Though the kalavinka sing all day long, until kefu, It was a damp grassland of the ugly ones.

§ 2

Sakaharuhimekami recited a sacred poem.

Miuta Even if the Mihishirogami were to appear and wish to welcome us, Ashiharahimekami is hurrying. Though our liver-like hearts are impatient, because the black clouds are deeply dark, we are troubled and distressed, alas. By the power of the true fire unleashed by Asakahihimekami, the fields have been opened up. When we look upon the state of the burning fire stirred by the reeds' wind, it is a sea of fire. The vast plain burned away by the swift wind did not leave behind even a single speck of dust. The road advancing to the palace of Ashihara is distant, and I am saddened as twilight approaches. I pray that beneath the pine shadows of this wilderness, you will deign to let us lodge for this single night. When tomorrow comes, arranging the bits of our steeds, I shall guide you to the noble sacred place.

Okitatsuhikokami recited a sacred poem.

Miuta Moved by the thick hearts of the gods, I too advance toward the palace of Ashihara. Nevertheless, tonight beneath the shade of the pine, I shall take shelter from the rain together with my lord.

Yasakahimekami recited a sacred poem.

Miuta As the heavenly sun leans to the other side of the sea, the shadow of the white midday moon appears in the majestic sky. Directly beneath the pale midday moon, the shadow of a single star shines as it is joined closely. In the upper right of the moon boat, there is a somewhat faint star; what sign might this be? When I think deeply, I am filled with joy; the coming forth of the Akitsuo deity must have drawn near. The single star shining beneath the moon must be the august figure of Asakahihime. The star's shadow shining faintly above the moon must be the august spirit of Ashiharahime. When evening comes, though there is no heavenly sunlight, the shadow of the moon boat must be shining brightly.

Tachiyomehimekami recited a sacred poem.

Miuta To firmly bind forever the island foundations, which are like immature land and floating fat, I came across. Wishing to meet the Akitsuo deity, I set forth and served as the attendant of Asakahihime. Even the Gross Island, where the warped deity raged fiercely and madly, is delightful today, oh land of Ashihara. Into the wild plain where reeds grow luxuriantly, tracking our way and parting the grass, I have come. Fighting against Magatsuhi while remaining in the body of a himekami, my manly courage has welled up within me. To speak with the Mihishirogami on the island roots of Ashihara, which is heard to be five thousand ri square, my liver-like heart grows brave and the wild beating of my breast does not stop, while my arms tremble.

Ikuhikokami recited a sacred poem.

Miuta Morning and evening I have served the Mihishirogami, but there has never been a joyful day like today. When the god of light, the Mihishirogami, appeared, my soul strings began to hum. By the Kototama proclaimed by the deity of sharp ringing emergence, the Magatsuhi of my Ashihara have vanished. The black clouds that untimely enveloped the majestic sky have cleared away without leaving a trace. I blush at myself, who have served for twenty years according to the promptings of the Lord God, yet accomplished no merit. Though the Mihishirogami is majestic and awe-inspiring, the power of the accompanying deities was lacking. This land of Ashihara, where the two pillar Mihishirogami descended from heaven, shall be eternal. The darkness proceeding to the eternal world has gradually cleared away through the noble merit of the god of light.

Ameharehimekami recited a sacred poem.

Miuta Serving as an attendant, I have never seen such a valiant battle as the one today. How pitiful was the state when we were smitten by the Kototama of the warped deities of Gronos and Goros. If we possess the flint stone and the severe Kototama bestowed by the Lord God, the warped deities will surely lurk in hiding. Come now, we must advance to the forest on the other side, seeking a lodging for a single night's rain.

Hereupon, the twelve male and female deities hastened forward along the twilight field path, heading toward the lush, clustering evergreen forest in the midst of the plain.
