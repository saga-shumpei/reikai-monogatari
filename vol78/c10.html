Chapter 10: 地異天変 [1966]

地異天変〔1966〕

§ 1

Born as Mihishirogami, Asakahime-no-kami, leading the myriad deities, left behind Shinogaoka where the pines grow lush, and hastened onward to the sacred place where Ashiharahime-no-kami dwells at the foot of Mount Takashi; aligning the bits of their steeds, they advanced bravely and steadily across ōnogahara. On the fields here and there, spring breezes fragrant, birds singing, and heat hazes burning in tranquil beauty, they gracefully appreciated the scene, and at last arrived around twilight at the riverbank of Nakanogaha, which crosses the island of Guros.

Kunitsukami Nuzuchihiko, pointing at the flow of the river, says:

'The upper rapids are swift, the lower rapids are lukewarm and deep; go by the middle rapids. The river water is turbid throughout, and the riverbed is all the more deep, making it hard to cross. With no means to cross over to the opposite bank, we know not even the sacred figure of Mihishirogami.'

Asakahime-no-kami, from horseback, glancing down at the turbid current of Nakanogaha, composes and chants a miuta:

'Is it the dripping of the drops of the breath of Magatsumi that makes this river water so exceedingly turbid? To cross straight through this river where even the legs of our steeds enter in defilement—how grievous a thing it is.'

Ubugamihiko-no-kami composes and chants a miuta:

'Compared to the flow of the eastern river, though turbid, its riverbed is narrow. By the kototama of the divine word of Asakahimekami, I desire to make them heavenly horses and cross over. Even that steed of mine which crossed the eastern river with mighty wings outspread—oh steed, oh steed, by the blessing of the living kototama, cause two wings straightway to sprout!

One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, hundreds, thousands, tens of thousands of living kototama's blessings, let the steed transform into an eagle, and let the river transform into earth! Though this current be deep, though the surface of the river be turbid, by the clear and radiant kototama bestowed by the Lord Great Deity, mountains, rivers, and fields alike are a land that must render obedience everywhere. The earth being young, still the land being young and unsettled, this island root is of the water and fire of kototama; by the light of kototama all is solidified, and as the mahoraba of the Shibi heavenly realm, flourish and prosper eternally! The two deities of Mihishirogami have descended to this island root; ah, how truly divine!

God is love, God is power; by the power of love and goodness, what turbidity or mountains and rivers alike shall be a national character that revives! If wings sprout not upon my steed, it cannot be helped; solidifying this wide river into unyielding earth, upon my departure to advance boldly to the opposite bank, I pray with reverence before the presence of the Lord God that You protect us. The heavenly sun shines, the moon waxes, spring departs to this floating island, a hundred grasses and a thousand grasses burst into flower, small birds sing, and butterflies dance. In the midst of such an auspicious land, the wide river whose turbidity is deep, obstructing the coming forth of the public—is it the fierce shouting of Yaso-Magatsumi? Is it the deceitful scheme of the ugly Magatsumi? Draw back, draw back, draw back, Nakanogaha!

Water too, freeze and become earth! The coming forth of Mihishirogami, who descended from the high and eminent mountains. The ugly deities of Guronos and Gorosu also, terrified and frightened by the light of the public, fled away taking clouds for mist. The wide river that hinders the divine progress of our noble public, by the blessing of my kototama, shall be made dry land and we shall advance. Iroha nihoheto chirinuru wo wakayotareso tsuenearamu uwi nokuyama kefu koye asaki yumemisi wehi mosezu. The good time of this good day, since the opening of this heaven and earth, is the divine progress of Asakahime, of a light unprecedented. River, draw back, draw back, become dry land; I am the child of God, the shrine of God!

Are there any mountains, rivers, grasses, or trees that do not render obedience to the living kototama of the child of God who is with God? Realize, realize the achievements of the living kototama, the light of the living kototama!'

As she thus chants the miuta, Nakanogaha, though so wide and surging with turbid current, gradually began to shallow its water.

Asakahime-no-kami, beholding this state of things, composes and chants a miuta:

'The kototama of Ubugamihiko-no-kami having blessed, the water of the wide river has begun to shallow. The wide river blocking the path along which I journey, I would make it dry land by the living kototama. In the kototama which Ubugamihiko-no-kami proclaimed, the river of Nakano shall become dry land. To the spirited horse, sprout wings, as was just proclaimed—in the living kototama there is no light; once wings are obtained, the lack of a second achievement is the very nature of a steed. My steed goes forth crossing over the unyielding earth, and has become a noble steed of true words. The deity ruler Ashiharahime of Mihishiro was about to set forth to welcome me.'

§ 2

Okitatsuhiko no Kami chants a miuta:

'Though I gaze as far as the eye can reach, the divine misugata of Ashiharahime no Kami is not seen, which is strange. A harugasumi rises in the distant moor, and even the blowing wind is exceedingly gentle, with no shadow of anything.'

Asakahime no Kami chants a miuta:

'Though she is not visibly seen before your eyes, Ashiharahime no Kami comes following her mitomo. As time passes, upon this kahagishi, the divine misugata of Ashiharahime no Kami shall shine. Nevertheless, the kahamizu of Nakanogaha has begun to recede more and more. Making the kahasoko into kugachi, when we cross to the opposite bank, then Himegami shall come. Because the sugado of Ashiharahime-no-mikoto is distant, unconsciously and unknowingly time must pass. Although you cause the hayakoma's hooves to hasten, the distant hirono is still uncertain.'

Tatsuyohime no Kami chants a miuta:

'According to the kototama proclaimed by Ubugamihiko no Kami, the kahamizu of Nakanogaha has indeed dried up. Kahamizu, quickly recede, for this is the miyuki of the Kami of Light of the mihishiro. To illuminate this floating island whose kuni is young, the Kami of Light stands here. Shall not the Nakanogaha, which arose from the iki of the shikogami, be transformed into the living kototama?'

Ameharehime no Kami chants a miuta:

'One by one the kahamizu have receded, and even the myriad clusters of rocks on the kahasoko have revealed their forms. What shall I do with the living lives of the fishes that have dwelt in great numbers upon the kahasoko?'

Asakahime no Kami chants a miuta:

'The fishes that dwelt in great numbers upon the kahasoko have fled away toward the upper rapids. Following the upper rapids, all the fishes have fled and entered the wide clear marsh. Even though the kahasoko dries up completely white, there is no concern for the lives of the fishes.'

'One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, a hundred thousand, eight million times, congealing the divine iki, we shall cross this kahase in peace. Ah, Kannagara, O Kami, Kami! Let there be light in the living kototama! Let there be life in our kototama!'

Just as they are causing these miuta to be chanted, the kahasoko swells up from deep beneath the earth with a loud acoustic resonance like a hundred thunders crashing all at once, and wondrously transforms into a flat plain without the slightest rise or fall.

At this moment, just as the retinue of Asakahime no Kami is about to cross the newly born land of the riverbed by aligning their horses, there are Kami appearing, riding on horses, now visible and now hidden in the field of daylilies. These Kami had set out from the Takakanosu Palace at the foot of Mount Takakanosu in order to welcome the retinue of Asakahime no Kami, and were none other than the Amatsukami of three sons and three daughters—led by Ashiharahime no Kami, who is one of the eighty Himegami who have gradually arrived here, along with Makaitahiko no Kami, Naruyamahiko no Kami, Eishunhime no Kami, Yaehime no Kami, and Tamahikohiko no Kami.

かく歌はせ給ふ折しも、河底は百雷の一時に轟く如き大音響とともに地底よりふくれ上り、少しの高低もなき平面地となり変りけるぞ不思議なれ。

ここに、朝香比女の神の一行は、新しく生れたる河跡の陸地を駒並めて渡り給はむとする折しもあれ、萱草の野に見えつかくれつ、駒に乗りて現はれ給ふ神々おはしけり。この神々は、朝香比女の神一行を迎へ奉るべく鷹巣山の麓なる鷹巣の宮居を立ち出で、ここにやうやう着かせ給ひたる八十比女神の一柱なる葦原比女の神を先頭に真以比古の神、成山比古の神、栄春比女の神、八栄比女の神、霊生比古の神の三男三女の天津神に在しましける。
