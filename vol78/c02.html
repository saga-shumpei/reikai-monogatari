Chapter 2: Waves 上の追懐 [1958]

波上の追懐〔1958〕

§ 1

The rock-camphor boat bearing Asakahime no Kami proceeded leisurely across the early summer ocean where thin mists trailed, and until her divine figure was hidden, the company of Takarahime no Kami bade her farewell with lingering reluctance and chanted a divine poem.

Aha, aha! The God of Light has gone forth, Illuminating the crests of the waves without a single shadow. Thinking that I am to part forever From the beloved God of Light, how sorrowful I am! It is with sorrow that I, beautiful, gentle, and valiant, Send forth the august boat of the Himegami. Raising your hands, you begrudged the parting— How gentle indeed is the heart of Hime! Experiencing the very feelings As when Akitsuo no Kami descended from heaven, is the departure of Hime. Painful is the body that parts from Akitsuo no Kami; Thinking upon this, I am filled with sorrow. Chosen as the parents of this vast divine land, I have encountered this sorrowful day. From now on, I will set straight the steed of my heart And strive to recompense the heart of Hime. Treading across the eight-fold paths of the tides, the eight-hundred-fold paths, the eight-fold paths of the tides, You set forth; how noble is your achievement. Building a palace eternally upon this island root, I will endeavor to comfort the august heart of Hime.

Quickly building an august dwelling to serve her, I shall enshrine and revere the august spirit of Hime. Even if her august form is no longer visible, I will enshrine her august spirit in the shrine And honor her great achievements. The august boat, growing distant moment by moment, Its divine figure has caused me to weep. Brimming with myriad measures of tears, upon the beach of Kuraya, Though sending forth the august boat is thought to be the decree of the Lord God, Ah, once more I yearn to meet you, O Ōmi.

Praying that the journey over the crests of the waves of the eight-fold paths of the tides be peaceful, I shall proclaim the divine words and pray to the august deities.

Mamoruhiko no Kami, gazing sidelong across the ocean expanse, chanted a divine poem.

Aha, aha! The august boat has grown far away, While my sorrow wells up ever more. For thousands of generations, the august form of Ōmi Shall shine within my breast and never be forgotten. Today the waves are calm and the heavenly sun Shines brilliantly; may the august boat be blessed. Looking back and looking back as you depart, How gentle is the form of the deity! Since you are a deity who descended from heaven from the peak of Takachihino-mine, Your august presence was all the more noble. Thinking that there is no way to see your august form again, I grieve this parting of today. Parting the waves of the endless ocean, I pray that the journey of Ōmi who advances is blessed.

Reisanhiko no Kami chanted a divine poem.

Out of an excess of reluctance in parting and out of sorrow, I was unable to offer the kototama. Why should I feel lonely after sending forth Himegami, who bestowed upon us the light of the myriad-ri island? At the wondrous departure of Asakahime no Kami, Wakakunohara has been revived. The august boat, growing smaller across the distant wave-paths, Is about to leave my field of vision.

Hosouhiko no Kami chanted a divine poem.

The pure form of Hakubakadake shall eternally Send forth the august progress of Ōmi. Since my eyes are small, the august boat In which Ōmi travels has already become invisible. How I envy the peak of Hakubakadake, as it eternally Sends forth the august progress of Himegami! Standing on the beach of Kuraya to send her forth, The august boat has already left the horizon. The God of Light, whom I prayed would remain eternally, has returned. From now on, devoting my sincere heart to the Omitoshi-no-Kami, I shall serve the land. If we protect the world with the light of the flint stones Bestowed by Hime as the treasure of this land...

Michimichihiko no Kami chanted a divine poem.

The deity who descended to heaven as a light in this land Has returned without mercy. Though meeting is a joyful thing, today I send forth the august boat in a sorrowful parting. You have become an Ōmi whom I shall never forget. Since you have bestowed light upon this young land, Morning and evening, serving in the young palace of fire, I shall praise the achievements of Ōmi. Pure of heart, gentle, and moreover valiant, Hime has truly proven to be a deity of true words.

Masamichihiko no Kami chanted a divine poem.

Even the sound of the waves striking against the gunwale Has grown ever more distant, step by step. Her shining, white, and gentle august face Has hidden itself high among the crests of the waves. It is the feeling of sinking into the waves of the heavenly sun; The God of Light has left the horizon. Thinking that I would serve her eternally, Asakahime is no longer in this land. Awed by the divine words of Takarahime no Kami, Morning and evening I shall serve her. Even the ugly Magatsuhi of Hakubakadake Must have fled in terror at the achievements of Himegami. As you look back, gazing upon The summits of Gōzugamine and Hakubakadake, Please remember, Asakahime no Kami, That there is a small me at the foot of Hakubakadake.

§ 2

Kumokahahiko no Kami chants a sacred song:

Now that it has come to this, even to mourn is in vain; / I shall merely offer my sincere heart and serve the divine spirit. / Even the shadow of the sacred boat is no longer seen, / And the tears of lamentation grow ever heavier. / All the myriad things of this island, / Each and all, shall weep while regretting the parting with you, O Lord. / The flowers of the hundred grasses wither, / And today's parting upon the beach seems to mourn. / The sunlight traversing the sacred sky grows dimly clouded; / Do ye lament this parting of the day.

Yamatohime no Kami chants a sacred song:

Illuminating both Heaven and Earth without a corner left unlit, the divine form of Himegami / Is now no longer to be seen. / The flint stones granted as a token of remembrance above all else / Must be the light of the divine country across ten thousand leagues. / However precious the light of the jewel-stone may be, / It cannot serve as the age to save the land. / Since there is nothing else to offer, it cannot be helped, / And so I have offered these humble treasures. / I gratefully think upon the tender heart of Himegami, / Who accepted them so graciously. / What shall I do? The God of Light has returned home, / Parting the waves of the distant ocean across ten thousand leagues, / And revering forever the public merits, / I shall serve at the young palace of fire.

Chikahihime no Kami chants a sacred song:

With myself upon the island root of ten thousand leagues where the land is yet young, / To encounter today's sorrowful parting— / To Himegami, so yearning, tender, and valiant, / My soul was bound by ties unbreakable. / My soul, entwining itself with your sacred person, / Shall guard you from afar across the ocean. / Since your divine merits are so noble, the heavenly raiment of Himegami / Illuminates the four quarters far and wide. / I have come to realize for the first time the preciousness of a sincere heart, / In the precious light overflowing from your sacred person; / When I think that both Heaven and Earth obey / The Kototama proclaimed by you, O Lord, I am filled with awe.

Yumusubihime no Kami chants a sacred song:

From today onwards, by the virtue of the flint stones bestowed by Himegami, / We shall draw pure water. / Morning and evening we shall serve at the young palace of fire, / Drawing sacred water to perform our purification. / The boundless ocean stretching far into the distance / Has become so wide that even the shadow of the sacred boat is no longer seen. / Now then, farewell, O my lord, Tadzukahihime no Kami; / Will you not return to the sacred sanctuary of ten thousand leagues? / Though you gaze upon the crests of the waves and yearn forever, / It is all in vain; return quickly.

Thereupon, the retinue of Tadzukahihime no Kami fixed their minds upon resignation as the sacred boat vanished from the horizon, and valiantly mounting the backs of spirited horses with the brave sound of hooves, by the time twilight fell upon that day, they safely returned to the sacred sanctuary on the hill of ten thousand leagues; without losing a moment, working night and day, they set about the construction of the young palace of fire, and before ten days had passed, the divine blessings grew even thicker, and a solemn young palace was built.

Hereupon, Yumusubihime no Kami morning and evening served at the young palace of fire, beginning with the principal deity and praising the fire deity, she boiled pure water and dipped bamboo leaves into it, sprinkling it left and right to purify the bodies and souls of morning after morning, and presenting the sacred hot water before the presence, she served faithfully. From this time down to the present world, in whatever shrine there is one who serves as a shrine maiden, boiling sacred water and offering it to the deities has thus become established.

Asakaihime no Kami set out from the beach of Koyara, and while regretting her parting from the retinue of Tadzukahihime no Kami from amidst the sacred boat, looking back again and again, she raised her sacred hands and chanted a sacred song:

How glorious, how glorious, the divine representation of the Mitashiro no Kami has manifested! / Though I am about to part upon the island root of ten thousand leagues, / Bound by the tender hearts of the deities, / Ah, how the days and months have unconsciously piled up! / Though I wish to dwell here forever, / I have no means to disobey the summons of the principal deity. / Arousing the heroic Yamato spirit, / I have come to announce this regretful parting. / If I did not possess a valiant and stalwart heart / That knows no end to parting forevermore, / It is not that I do not know the hearts of the deities, / But thinking upon the divine work, I have made my parting. / The deities stood upon the beach and looked upon my boat / With such tender hearts. / Until the very end of myriad ages I shall never forget: / The hearts of the deities, where the true word shines, / Are like meeting a friend of a hundred years; / I think of the deities with whom there was no barrier. / My boat has now become distant upon the wave-roads; / O deities of the island, dwell in peace.

The voices of the true cranes sounded mournful / As I thought of leaving the new land of ten thousand leagues.

§ 3

Ubugamihiko no Kami chants a sacred song.

Hearing the divine oracle of the two pillar Himegamitachi, / Tears well up within me even as I listen. / Of the living kototama of such gentle and pure Kamigami, / Alas, I had not heard them until now. / The Himegami should indeed be thus, whereas the hearts of the multitude / Are filled with envy and jealousy. / At the tender words exchanged between the Kami of the Mihishiro, / I was moved to tears by their grace. / Remembering the tender disposition of the Kami / Who bade us a distant farewell on the beach of Mikuriya, / When I think of the hardship of opening up the young Kuni, / My heart trembles with awe. / Exhausting the utmost of Magokoro and advancing upon the path of Aizen, / Magnificent are the Momogami! / As our boat departs far beyond the crests of the waves, / The Kami standing upon the beach are no longer visible. / O Kamigami, who grow ever closer and dearer in the ocean expanse / As our boat recedes further and further away! / The august forms of the Kamigami have faded from sight, / While the peaks of Mount Hakuba shine brightly.

The august forms of the Kamigami residing in the Kuni where Mount Hakuba towers are deeply nostalgic.

Okitatsuhiko no Kami chants a sacred song.

As the vast and youthful Kuni recedes from my field of vision, / Only the peaks of Mount Hakuba are shining. / May the myriad Ikushima floating upon the myriad-ri sea / Flourish forever alongside Heaven and Earth! / While yearning for the island roots that recede moment by moment, / I continue on my journey. / In the midst of this boundless ocean, / The island roots of the myriad-ri are a beloved Kuni.

Tachiyohime no Kami chants a sacred song.

Serving the Kami of the Mihishiro, purely I sported / In the sacred place of the island of Banri. / Grasses, trees, and hundreds and thousands of birds of the youthful Kuni / Sang of spring and were at peace. / The clouds and mists cleared away without a trace, and the august shadow of the heavenly sun / Was pure upon the Kuni of Banri! / The Kami of the Mihishiro in person bade public farewell to us / On the beach of Mikuriya. / Our boat, beating the drum of the waves, / Must have told its parting to Hime. / The gentle figure of Himegami floats before my eyes, / And she has truly become a lord whom I shall never forget. / Until the day we meet again with Akitsuwo no Kami, / I pray that she remains forever youthful and gentle.

Aharahime no Kami chants a sacred song.

How lonely it is to part publicly and go forth / Upon the ocean where both heaven and earth are cleared of mist! / Beginning with Himegami, the ten pillar Kamigami— / Their gentle hearts are indeed to be revered. / The Kamigami, who are both gentle and valiant, / Have driven away the ugly crooked kami. / Gradually the beach of Mikuriya has also grown distant, / And Mount Hakuba alone gleams brightly. / Our boat, tracing the waves of peace, / Protects you and advances ever further. / The mists that enshrouded the ocean have also cleared away completely, / Ah, what a delightful journey by boat this day is!

『天も地も晴れ渡りたる海原を 公に訣別れて行くは淋しも 比女神を始め十柱神等の 優しき心仰がるるかな 優しくて雄々しくいます神々は 醜の曲神を退ひ給ひし 漸くに御来矢の浜も遠くなりて 白馬ケ岳はひとりかがよふ 吾舟は太平の浪を辿りつつ 公を守りていや進むなり 海原を包みし霧も晴れ渡り 楽しき今日の舟の旅かも』
