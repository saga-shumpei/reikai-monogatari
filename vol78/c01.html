Chapter 1: 浜辺の訣別 [1957]

浜辺の訣別〔1957〕

§ 1

The myriad island roots floating upon the myriad great ocean plains measured approximately eight thousand ri in area, and because they were the birthplace of the land of luxuriant reed plains and lush rice ears, the soil was exceptionally fertile, the order of the four seasons of spring, summer, autumn, and winter was regular, and the growth of all things was likewise extremely favorable. Thus, just as harmful insects fondly cluster in great abundance upon delicious fruits and beautiful flowers, eighty crooked-crooked tsumi divinities had shaken their violent might and dwelt here as their dwelling of a thousand ages, yet Takarahime no Kami, who was one of the eighty pillars of mihishirogami, revered the divine decree of the great lord of all, led ten pillars of male and female divine generals, descended upon these island roots, drew and held the sword of living kototama, pursued and laid low the raging divinities along every mountain ridge, pursued and expelled them along every river rapid, rebuked and chastised them, and decreed this land to be a peaceful and joyful divine country. At that very time, among the eight pillars of mihishirogami who had intimately served at the palace heights of Takachihone, Asakahime no Kami—who was the most beautiful and the most brilliantly victorious divinity facing forth—followed by four pillars of male and female divinities, had momentarily stayed her divine footprints in this soil, whereupon the form of the country was suddenly renewed, and its divine radiance was added unto day by day and month by month, which was indeed a wondrous blessing. In addition, from the time when Asakahime no Kami, with her own divine hands, bestowed as a national treasure of this land the flint stones from which to strike forth the true fire that the crooked divinities most feared and abhorred, day by day the country became tranquil, all the kunitsukami bathed in that grace, and they flourished exceedingly in the way of cooked food. Among the eighty countries and eighty islands brought forth by the Lord Ōkami, though Sanono no Sato was the very earliest to begin the way of cooked food, opening the way of cooked food generally throughout the entire country took this myriad island as its initial source. Therefore, it was also called by the alternate name, the Land of Fire.

From this time, after a while, through the promptings of Asakahime no Kami, Taigen Akitsuo no Kami governed the western lands, left the nurturing of kunitamakami to Asakahime no Kami, caused Teruo no Kami to guard and protect the western lands, crossed the eight hundred roads of the tides, and descended from heaven to Manyo Island. Here, he caused Takarahime no Kami to join the divine water and fire, completed the great divine works of the left and right, brought forth kunitamakami, and seeing that the foundation of the country was firmly established, he proceeded once more to repair and solidify the young land of Wakanokuni on the northern face of Mount Takaharu. In this volume, we desire to briefly set forth the circumstances thereof.

As Asakahime no Kami and the four pillars of male and female divinities were about to leave Manyo Island, Takarahime no Kami led the ten pillars of divinities and respectfully escorted them on horseback to the beach of Karaya, where they mutually exchanged farewell divine poems.

Here, Asakahime no Kami dismounted from her horse as she was about to board the divine boat, and chanted a divine poem addressed to Takarahime no Kami.

Praying for the prosperity of the new land, We shall depart and go forth to the land of the west. O Takarahime Mihishirogami, dwell safely And peacefully as you bring forth the Kunitama. The clouds and mists of all eight directions clear away, and as the moon and sun are youthful, The prosperity of the land fills the heart. Since we have met with Akitsuo no Kami, your divine Merits shall be faithfully spoken and transmitted. The true heart of Takarahime, who dwells In such majestic and majestic beauty, shall be faithfully passed down. Though brief, by lingering in this new land, My divine threads were fully satisfied. The precious jewel bestowed by the Mihishirogami's own hand, As a pure divine spirit we look up to morning and night. If the Magatsumi divinities should rage and madden, Drive them away by the power of the true fire. The clouds and mists of the ocean plain clear away, and the crests of the waves Shine across the heavenly sunbeams. Though we lament the parting memories of this departing day, I am one who has no means to stay.

Tarahime no Kami chanted a responsive divine poem.

Filled with sorrow to think I am parting from Asakahime no Kami, Who dwells with such majesty and tenderness. I thank you for your public promise to report Every detail of my affairs to Akitsuo no Kami. I weep with joy at being given the flint stones, Which are the treasure for firmly establishing this land for a thousand ages. O flint stones, the most precious treasure above all, With the flint stones, there are no crooked divinities in the pacified land. Celebrating even your public progress beneath the heavenly sunbeams, You have cleared away the great ocean plain. Praying for your public good fortune morning and evening, I shall serve before the divine presence. Building a beautiful palace as a public monument to you on Manyoo-ga-oka to serve you, These island roots where the eight pillars of Mihishirogami descended Are especially venerable. Transmitting and transmitting them down to ten thousand generations, Asakahime's Divine spirit shall be enshrined and made our guardian divinity. Calling the divine name the Fire Deity, we shall make the great pillars of Asakahime's Palace thick and strong to serve.

'Though the sound of the waves echoes ever so loudly and softly, / The waves rage and churn within the sea of my heart; / Standing in lingering grief until Your Lordship's sacred boat is hidden from view, / There is nothing more we can do than watch you depart. / May the myriad paths of the surging tidal waves be safe and calm, / This alone is what I pray with my true heart; / Cherishing the flint-stone bestowed as a keepsake / Of the boundless, vast, young land of Marikajima, / Reverently accepting the sacred words of Takarahime no Kami, / I shall humbly serve Your Lordship's palace.'

Kumokahahiko no Kami chants this sacred poem.

'How distant indeed must be the journey of Your Lordship, / Who set forth to meet the deity Akitsuo no Kami! / May you proceed without a single mishap / Along the myriad tidal paths of the eight-hundred-fold sea routes, Asakahime no Kami! / Since the four pillars of attendant deities are with you, / My heart is at ease as I send off your sacred boat; / From time to time, please recall Marikajima / And let your pure spirits commune across the ten thousand leagues.'

Ubugamihiko no Kami chants this sacred poem.

'Though I cannot stem the tears of parting / In the sincerity of Takarahime no Kami's divine words, / Be at peace in your heart, for I shall advance ahead, / Guarding the vanguard of Asakahime no Kami's divine words. / Through various entertainments of the living Kototama, / My soul-threads are now being revived; / Leaving behind the dear island-roots of Banri, / I shall proceed across the myriad tidal paths of the ocean. / This island, as the supreme Mahoraba of Takaamahara, / Shall surely prosper for a thousand and eight thousand reigns. / Serving Asakahime no Kami, how beautiful it is / To gaze upon the land-shape of the roots of Marikajima! / Now, farewell! The sorrow of parting will never end as I guard the vanguard / Of my lord and part from the divine land.'

Tachihikohiko no Kami chants this sacred poem.

'For many years upon these island-roots, / I thought to dwell with a heart pure and liver-felt; / Yet being the attendant of my lord, / I am one whose heart no longer follows its own will. / I shall gaze upon and yearn for the clouds rising over Ushogamine and Shirumagatake / From the ship traveling far away. / I thought of the island-roots of Banri as the Takara-land / From the beginning of the divine age where spirits flourish; / Banri no Shima, having blown away the clouds and mists, / Is a radiant, brilliant land-plain of nobility! / I shall now leave behind this luminous land / And proceed across the ocean with the luminous lord. / Surpassing even a deity of light, may Takarahime no Kami / Illumine the new land of Banri!'

Tachisehime no Kami chants this sacred poem.

'While beholding the light of the new land, / I too shall go forth serving as an attendant; / Even down to the tips of birds, beasts, grasses, and trees, / The island-roots of Banri are held so dear. / Feeling all things in creation to be my friends and intimates, / I am now about to part from this new land; / If the Lord God grants permission, I too / Shall come again to this new land. / I shall protect the countenance of Takarahime no Kami's divine words / So that it remains youthful for ever and ever; / Facing the parting from this island, I shall protect / The gentleness and youth of Takarahime no Kami. / I pray that the sacred forms of the ten pillars of deities / May forever remain youthful.'

Ameharehime no Kami chants this sacred poem.

'Takarahime no Kami, you ten pillars of deities, now farewell! / Lamenting our lingering parting, we shall now take our leave. / May you remain youthful forever and ever, / Shining brightly as the guardian deities of Marikajima!'

Thus, mutually expressing words of farewell through poetry, Asakahime no Kami and the four pillars of deities lightly stepped aboard the rock-camphor boat together with their horses; thereupon, a pure wind filled with an indescribably spring-like or early-summer-like warmth suddenly began to blow, and without even using oars, the sacred boat began to move peacefully upon the waves.

『顕津男の神に会はすと出でたたす 公が旅路の遥けくもあるか 八潮路の潮の八百路も恙なく 進ませたまへ朝香比女の神 四柱の御供の神等おはしませば 心やすけく御舟を送るも をりをりは思ひ出して万里ケ島に 清き御魂を通はせたまはれ』

初頭比古の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『田族比女神の神言の真心に 別れの涙止めあへぬも 朝香比女神の神言の御尾前を 守り進まむ御心安かれ いろいろと生言霊のもてなしに わが魂線はよみがへりつつ なつかしき万里の島ケ根を後にして 潮の八百路を進みてゆかむ 此島は紫微天界の真秀良場と 千代に八千代に栄えますらむ 朝香比女の神に仕へて美はしき 万里ケ島根の国形見しはや いざさらば名残は尽きじ吾公の 御尾前守りて神国に別れむ』

起立比古の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『幾年もこの島ケ根に村肝の 心清けく住ままく思ひし 吾公の御供なれば村肝の 心に任せぬ吾なりにけり 牛頭ケ峰白馬ケ岳に立つ雲を 遠行く舟に仰ぎて偲ばむ 霊幸はふ神世の初めの田族国と 吾は思ひぬ万里の島根を 雲霧を吹き払ひたる万里ケ島は 光にみつる貴の国原よ 吾は今光の国土を後にして 光の公と海原進まむ 田族比女神は光の神とまして 万里の新国土を照らさせたまへ』

立世比女の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『新しき国土の光を見ながらに 吾は御供に仕へて行くも 鳥獣草木の端に至るまで なつかしく思ふ万里の島根は 森羅万象皆吾友と親しみし この新国土に別れむとすも 主の神の許しありせば吾も亦 この新国土に再び来らむ 田族比女神の神言の顔を いや永久に若く守らむ この島の別れにのぞみ田族比女の 神の優しさ若さを守らむ 十柱の神の御姿永久に いや若かれと吾は祈るも』

天晴比女の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『田族比女の神十柱の神いざさらば 名残を惜しみて今や別れむ 心若く永久にましませ万里ケ島の 守りの神と光らせたまひつ』

かく互に歌もて訣別の辞を述べたまひ、朝香比女の神初め四柱の神は駒諸共に磐楠舟にひらりと移らせたまへば、春とも初夏とも知れぬ陽気にみてる清しき風は忽ち吹き来り、艪櫂を用ひたまはぬに御舟は波上静に動き出でにける。
