Chapter 20: Tranquility Waves の Sound [1976]

静波の音〔1976〕

§ 1

Asakahime-no-Kami and her party, together with the retinue of Ashiharahime-no-Kami who had come to see them off, gradually arrived, around the twilight of that day, at the forest of Tokiwa near the sea of Tokiwa, where pines and camphor trees stood soaring and sealing the heavens, and spending the night there and letting dawn break, none of the gods could sleep for the fluttering of their hearts, but strolling about here and there in the vast forest, they indulged in composing poems and the like, and awaited the dawn.

The moon in the firmament was bright and clear, casting platinum light upon the pure garden where the trees were sparse here and there, the spring night breeze slowly blew through the treetops, and truly the spectacle of peace filled heaven and earth. From time to time, driven by the sea-breeze, only the quiet sound of the tide washing against the shore could be heard.

Ashiharahime-no-Kami, looking up at the moonlight shining clearly over the forest of Tokiwa, composed a poem with a tranquil heart.

As a sign of the new land, in the great sky, Does the platinum moon shine brightly clear? Having escorted the Kami of Divine Light afar, I grow familiar with the moon of Tokiwa's forest. Looking up, the treetops of the camphor trees in the gentle breeze Stroke the face of the moon in the firmament. When I see the moon in the tranquil sky of a spring night, My soul has indeed been moistened. Though the sound of the tide is heard, of a spring night Is the sea-blowing breeze tranquil? Seeing the moon in the shade of the forest's Camphor trees, lush and sealing the heavenly sky. With the camphor trees lushly and eternally growing, The crying sound of the nightjar in the forest rings clear, The owl rests its wings on the treetops of the camphor trees, And cries across the purity of the moon. Upon the sand where the shadows of the Tokiwa trees are sparse, Stands the shimmering, white moonlight. Having seen off the lord who goes far away across the ocean, It has grown twilight in the forest of Tokiwa.

Awaiting and waiting for the dawn when the heavenly sun rises, I shall send off the lord upon the beach. Like the clear moonlight traversing the firmament, How clear shall be the land of Ashihara! Descending from heaven through the high mountains of Hisakata, The parting from the Kami of Light is regrettable. The lord who wiped away the twenty years of distress from the shiko Sets forth toward the dawn.

Asakahime-no-Kami composed a poem.

Escorted by the heavenly gods and earthly gods and others, They have surely arrived at the moonlit forest. The purity of resting in this forest during the short midnight of a spring day, Illuminated by the moon. As for the land of reed-plains ruled by Ashiharahime-no-Kami, Tomorrow we shall set forth, according to the will of the Kami. When I go, will the vile Magatsumi once more Come to spy? Be mindful of this! Since the ferocity of Magatsumi is ever stronger, Pray, sweep them away with the true fire of the flint-stone. Deep in the treetops of the camphor trees lushly growing, How pure are the myriad birds singing in concealment! The white sands of the garden also shine brightly. Also with the moonlight protecting this new land, When tomorrow comes, parting the waves of the myriad-riled ocean, Steadily I shall advance toward the land of the western region. The joy of serving before the divine presence of Hinodewake Who governs the land of the western region. How many achievements you built under the protection of the Kami of Sharp-ringing Emergence And created the land!

This beach at the end of the land of Ashihara, Tomorrow we shall leave it in a rock-camphor boat. May the heavenly gods who serve the land of Ashihara And the earthly gods alike dwell in peace.

Nozuchihiko-no-Kami composed a poem.

Since the moonlight of the great sky is clear, Dark is the shade of the lushly growing camphor trees. Having escorted the Kami of Divine Light, I now Look up at the moon in the forest of Tokiwa. The purity of the moon-boat crossing the endless firmament Must be the heart of the lord. The Kami who shines even more than the moon Illuminating the ocean without exception and shining clearly! The night forest where Asakahime-no-Kami dwells Shines as if it were broad daylight. Having encountered such auspiciousness unheard of even in the age of the fierce gods, I am an earthly god! In the forest shade where the spring night breeze is soft, Lonely is the heart awaiting the dawn. Because I think of the lord who will return when tomorrow comes, I cherish this loneliness. In the twilight forest, too, myriad birds and thousand birds sing, Heard as if regretting tomorrow's imperial progress.

Hatsuhikohiko-no-Kami composed a poem.

Having finished crossing the vast fields of the land of Ashihara, The time to advance into the ocean draws near. Seeing the light of the moon shining in the great sky, What I thought of was the wondrous spirit. The sound of the tide also echoed quietly, And the sea-blowing breeze also grew calm. In this forest, my field of vision is opened afar, Eternally preventing the sea-blowing breeze. The moon traversing the firmament shall also for a while in this forest Rein its hooves and rest. Afar, following the Kami of Light, Day by day I saw wondrous divine works. I shall transmit the joy of the birth of the new land Through a thousand generations and celebrate it.

原文（近代文語）

朝香比女の神一行をはじめ、見送りまつりし葦原比女の神の主従は、漸くにして其日の黄昏るる頃、常磐の海辺に近き松と楠との天を封じてそそりたつ常磐の森に着かせ給ひ、一夜を此処に明かさせ給ひつつ、いづれの神々も心の時めきに眠り給はず、広大なる森を彼方此方と逍遥しつつ、御歌など詠みふけりて、明方を待たせ給ひける。

御空の月は皎々として冴え渡り、彼方此方の樹立まばらなる清庭に、白金の光を投げさせ給ひ、春の夜の風はおもむろに梢を吹き、実に平和の光景は天地に漲り渡れり。時々に海吹く風に煽られて、磯辺によする潮騒の音静かに聞ゆるのみなりき。

葦原比女の神は、常磐の森に冴え渡る月影を打ち仰ぎながら、心静かに御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『新しき国土の徴と大空に 白金の月冴え渡るかも 御光の神をはろばろ送り来て 常磐の森の月に親しむ 仰ぎ見れば楠の梢はそよ風に 御空の月の面を撫でつつ 春の夜の長閑けき空の月見れば わが魂線もうるほひにけり 潮騒の音聞ゆれど春の夜の 海吹く風は静かなるかも 天津空封じて茂る楠の 森の樹蔭に月を見るかな こんもりと永久に茂れる楠の 森に夜鷹の啼く音冴えつつ 梟は楠の梢に翼休め 月の清さに啼き渡るなり 常磐樹のかげまばらなる砂の上に かがよふ月の光は白しも 海原を遠行く公を送り来つ 常磐の森に黄昏れにけり

天津日の昇らす暁待ち待ちて 浜辺に吾は公を送らむ 御空ゆくさやけき月の光の如 晴れ渡るらむ葦原の国土は 久方の高地秀山ゆ天降りましし 光の神の別れ惜しまる 二十年の醜の艱みを払ひましし 公は暁かけて立たすも』

朝香比女の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『天津神国津神等に送られて 月照る森に着きにけらしな 春の日の短き夜半を此森に 月に照らされ休らふ清しさ 葦原比女神の守らす食国を 明日は立たなむ神のまにまに わが行かば又もや醜の曲津見は 窺ひ来らむ心し給はれ 曲津見の猛びいよいよ強ければ 燧石の真火に払はせ給へよ こんもりと茂れる楠の梢深く 潜みて鳴ける百鳥清しも 白々と庭の真砂も光るなり この新国土も月の守りに 明日されば万里の海原の浪分けて ひたに進まむ西方の国土へ 西方の国土をめぐらす顕津男の 神の御前に侍らふ嬉しさ 鋭敏鳴出の神の守りに数々の 功を建てて国土生みしはや

葦原の国土の果てなるこの浜辺を 明日は離れむ磐楠舟にて 葦原の国土に仕ふる天津神も 国津神々もまめやかにませよ』

野槌比古の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『大空の月の光のさやかなれば 茂らふ楠の樹蔭は暗しも 御光の神を送りて吾は今 常磐の森の月を仰ぐも 果しなき御空を渡る月舟の 清きは公の心なるかも 海原を隈なく照らして冴え渡る 月にもまして光らす神はも 朝香比女神のまします夜の森は 真昼の如く輝きて居り 千早振る神世もきかぬ目出度さに あひにけらしな国津神吾は 春の夜の風軟かき森蔭に 暁を待つ心は淋しも 明日されば帰らす公と思ふが故に この淋しさをわが抱くなり 黄昏の森にも百鳥千鳥なきて 明日の御行を惜しむがに聞ゆ』

初頭比古の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『葦原の国土の広野をわたり終へて 海原進む時は近めり 大空に輝く月の光見つつ 思ふは瑞の御霊なりけり 潮騒の音も静かにひびきけり 海吹く風も穏かにして この森はわが目路遠くひらかれて 海吹く風を永久に防ぐも 御空ゆく月も暫しはこの森に 蹄を止めて休らひ給はむ はろばろと光の神に従ひて 日々面白き神業見しかな 新しき国土の生れし喜びを 千代に伝へて寿ぎまつらむ』

§ 2

Kami Takahiko chants a sacred poem.

Mists enshrouding the boundless unabara, wishing to cross, The Kami of light has arrived here; The sound of shihozai echoes quietly too, Beneath the clear-spreading moon, Praising the moon all night long, drawing close to the shore, Hearing the sound of the waves, I shall await the dawn. The voices of momotori singing out, throughout the middle of the night, Are heard hither and thither in the treetops of the forest, Feeling as though both ame and tsuchi have been revived, I grow close to the moon of the eternal forest.

Kami Okitatsuhiko chants a sacred poem.

In the light of the Kami of mihishiro, the cloud-mists of ametsuchi Clear away, and the gleaming moonlight shines; The mists of the boundless sea shall also clear away, where the Kami Of mihikari emerges upon the myriad ocean routes; Spending a single night by the moon of this forest, Smiling and prospering, we shall proceed along the boundless unaji. Warmly, in the wind blowing at midnight, my sleeves Flutter like tefutefu; Beside the blossoms of shiraume, the iki of the night forest Grow pure and are filled with fragrance. The kuni of Ashihara is wondrous indeed, hither and thither As the fragrance of shiraume blossoms forth.

Kami Teruhiko chants a sacred poem.

The cloud-mists of the realm of Guros, which had grown utterly disordered, Has it cleared away, as the Kami of light stands forth? Following in the wake of the Kami of mihikari, Having advanced into the eternal forest, Thinking that from tomorrow the Kami of mihikari will no longer be with us, How lonely I feel, I, a Tianjin Kami; Crossing over the myriad ocean routes of the yashioji, The Kami of light will set forth tomorrow, alas. In my sorrow, having no means even to detain Him, Though I have escorted Him with all the sincerity of my heart, Brooding upon tomorrow's sad parting, Even my eyes gazing at the moon have grown dimmed. Gazing upon the serenity of the moon crossing the exalted sky, My dimming eyes' tears are burning hot as well.

Kami Tatsuehime chants a sacred poem.

Illuminated by the pure shinbutsu of the Kamis, Unexpectedly the days have piled up; Tomorrow we shall depart from the sacred kuni of Ashihara, And part the mists to advance across the boundless unabara. Repeating the journey of giving birth to a new kuni, Oh, the merits of the Kami Asakahime! Serving as an attendant to our Lord, whose Kototama fills the light with radiance, I shall go forth along the unaji. Tsukiyomi, high in the sky of the eternal forest, Shines pure and clear, illuminating us all. Do momotori also rejoice and take courage, singing beneath The shining moon, and remaining awake without sleep?

Kami Kiyohiko chants a sacred poem.

Scarcely has time passed since I was chosen among the ranks of the Tianjin Kami, Yet I have escorted the Lord of light hither; Though I am one devoid of strength, the awe of escorting And serving the Kami of mihishiro! The cloud-mists that eternally enshrouded ametsuchi Have also cleared away without a trace, and the shumi of the kuni is visible; The shumi of the abundant kuni is visible, and both moon and sun Are shining with the utmost limit of their light.

Kami Amaharehime chants a sacred poem.

On this bright and auspicious day, following the Kami Of mihikari, I have come hither too; I rest in the shadow of the dense forest Where ever-verdant pine and camphor trees grow thick. Late in the night, the moon in the exalted sky has tilted downward; Ah, the hamachidori crying out with noisy voices! To the distinct voices of the hamachidori calling back and forth to one another, The fleeting night has grown late. When tomorrow comes, serving my Lord, I shall go forth Along the mist-shrouded unaji—how joyous I am!

Kami Haruhiko chants a sacred poem.

May the unabara where my Lord goes tomorrow be clear; Guard the sacred vessel with gentle winds too. Straight on through the boundless unabara, the sacred vessel Shall proceed even amidst hardships. The waning moon has grown pale, and in the eastern Exalted sky, crimson clouds have welled up and risen; With the clear voice of magpies, dawn breaks, Singing to celebrate the blessings of the exalted sky.

At length, the single night in the eternal forest broke and cleared brightly, and while brightening the eastern exalted sky, the new sun shone brilliantly upon the lower world, rising quietly and ever so quietly.
