Chapter 14: Flower 見の宴 [1970]

花見の宴〔1970〕

§ 1

The sacred ground of Sakuragaoka, where Ashiharahime no Kami had been pacified for twenty years, was relatively expansive in area, extending one ri from east to west and two ri from north to south, with fruit trees such as plums, peaches, cherries, maples, figs, mandarins, camellias, and kerria roses planted appropriately here and there; around its periphery deep moats were drawn to prevent the incursions of Magatsukami, and at the front gate a bridge built with a single streak of rock was spanned, making it a wondrous realm of peerless scenic beauty.

The plums, peaches, and cherry blossoms, blooming in full glory, scented the surroundings with fragrance; small birds sang and butterflies danced, and the state in which red-crowned cranes, herons, and the like played, singing and rejoicing in the spring-like qi here and there in this sacred ground, was indeed a spectacle resembling the viewing of a painting by Hiroshige.

Ashiharahime no Kami, wishing to entertain the guest from afar to the utmost of her heart, commanded various deities to place and amply provide various rare fruits like mountains, and caused a grand banquet to be held with the five deities as honored guests.

At this time, numerous Kunitsukami, having heard the rumor that the saving deity would appear from the palace of Takachihino, vied with one another as they gathered, dancing wildly and leaping about outside the moat while crying out 'Uo Uo,' expressing the utmost sincerity of gratitude and joy.

At this time, Asakahime no Kami admired the state of the myriad flowers blooming in riotous profusion, and when she stepped out onto the surface of the garden, the numerous Kunitsukami, separating across the moat, reverently worshiped her radiant august figure, and as if the heavenly rock-door had opened, each and every one of them resounded with as much of the Kototama as there was, looking up and bowing down, and the voices of gratitude and joy were such that even heaven and earth seemed almost overturned thereby.

Asakahime no Kami gazed for a while upon the rare scenery of the surroundings and returned once more to the Yahiroden, and when she took her designated seat, Ashiharahime no Kami, to express her respect, exhausted the utmost of her splendid attire and served various delicious foods with her own august hands to Asakahime no Kami, and furthermore, opening up their hearts, conversed unreservedly about the current state of the land and the like without hiding anything; therefore Asakahime no Kami also rejoiced greatly at her unreserved sincerity and proclaimed the extent of her heart through an august poem.

Unreservedly Ashiharahime no Kami proclaims: Hearing this, even my soul lives purely; In the sacred ground of Sakuragaoka, Where the scenic beauty is exceptionally superb, I have prolonged my life. At the treatment by the warm-hearted deities, O tears of gratitude well up! Effusing a beautiful and pure attire, The preciousness of the deities who welcomed us, When shall I ever forget it? Playing for a day in the pure flower-garden, Though adorned beautifully, the yearning is That it lacks the ornamentation of luminous jewels.

Ashiharahime no Kami responds with an august poem: In the land of Ashihara, There is only the growth of reeds, rushes, and cogon grass, And there are no gems. It is said that the island-root of Banli, of which we hear by fame, Abounds in diamonds and other gems.

Asakahime no Kami chants an august poem: This glowing diamond— Was it not bestowed by Tazokuhime of the island of Banli? Since I myself have become a deity of light, The light of diamonds is unnecessary; I think to send this jewel, together with flint, Before the august presence of Ashiharahime no Kami. Come now, receive it, O diamond that glows even in the night of pitch darkness!

Ashiharahime no Kami, expressing a sense of gratitude, chants an august poem: Not only have you bestowed upon me the flint That pacifies the land of Ashihara, But you bestow a jewel as well? How grateful! This jewel bestowed by you Shall be handed down eternally as the treasure of the land. On nights without the moon, making this jewel our strength, We shall avoid the Magatsu and protect the land.

Asakahime no Kami chants an august poem: More than the light of the diamond that I bestow, Truly the true fire itself has been a jewel that shines in the night. By the efficacy of this true fire, Even the curtain of darkness in which we go through the eternal world Must clear away purely.

Ashiharahime no Kami chants an august poem: Revering your august teachings, which possess true words, I think to lead the Kunitsukami and others. From today onward, even the dragon, the great serpent, and the ugly Magatsukami Shall perish away in your light. Determining this land eternally as the Land of Mid-Reed-Plain of Abundant Ashihara, I shall give birth to the soul of the land. Since the land of five thousand square ri is vast, at the edges They are still lurking, the ugly Magatsukami; From today onward, joining our hearts With the myriad deities, we shall punish the Magatsu.

§ 2

Masamochihiko no Kami chants a miuta:

My lord has surely won the glowing gem of night, In the light of Asakahimegami's heart, As a flint to burn utterly the Magatsumi, Was not the God of light who bestowed this jewel? The divine light that illuminates the perpetual darkness without a shadow, Has appeared here upon Sakuragaoka. In the spring breeze the cherry petals flutter and fall, Drifting deep into the hall of eight fathoms; Floating and gleaming upon the waves of the wine cup, The cherry blossoms are truly the flowers of the land. As the emblem of the land of Ashihara, from this day forth, We shall stamp the cherry blossom upon our banners.

Nariyamahiko no Kami chants a miuta:

Are they not sitting face to face with the God of light, Who turned and subdued the Magatsumi of the land of Ashihara? They have been granted a jewel more precious than anything else, A true-fire flint and the glowing gem of night. If you hold it before the breast of my lord and cause him to stand Upon the hill of flowers, how beautiful he would be! Even the cherry flowers that adorn the hair of Himegami Cannot surpass the glowing gem of night.

Ubugamihiko no Kami chants a miuta:

Coming to this place, I feel the heart of the spring Yayohi, The voices of a hundred birds in the grove of flowers. Flying and crossing over the flowery branch-tips, the hundred birds Scatter their fragrance in the blowing wind. That the glory of flowers may last forevermore, It is not I alone who desire this. Though the islands of Wanmado were in early summer, coming here, We have seen the spring flowers grow youthful.

Sakaehirume no Kami chants a miuta:

Beholding the prosperity of the spring day before my very eyes, And viewing it in its purity upon Sakuragaoka, This hill where the God of mihikari has manifested, Has plums, peaches, and cherries smiling all at once. Rejoicing in the flower-smiling spring of Yayohi, Little birds sing and butterflies dance. Singing of the eternal spring of the land of Ashihara, Are the mountain bush warblers the voice of Kami? Both the bush warbler and the Kalavinka sing on and on Through the whole day, never tiring of the flowery spring.

Tachihikohiko no Kami chants a miuta:

Even in this desolate new land, I knew not That there was such a sacred place. Is this place in Ashihara the heavenly realm on earth? The voices of joy of the Kunitsukami are heard. Beneath the green shade of the ever-green pines, A hundred flowers and a thousand flowers bloom and gleam. It is praised as the achievement of the Kami of the mihashira, Who builds the heavenly realm upon the earth. I pray that until the end of countless myriads of years, Plums, peaches, and cherries may be handed down. Looking up amidst the Shibi celestial realm, Such a lovely divine garden I have never seen. Wandering through eighty lands and eighty islands, I now Play in the garden of the earthly heavenly realm. Looking up into the cloudy sky, standing loftily, Is it Mt. Takasu rising with the mood of spring? The spring appearance of Mt. Takasu bound with a sash of white clouds Is all the more clear. Until today, Mt. Takasu too was wrapped in dark clouds Out of season and caused rain to fall. As the cups of wine pile up, I now Feel my heart grow wildly enthusiastic and brave.

Yaehirume no Kami chants a miuta:

The surface of the widely winding moat grows pale blue, It is the spring when true carp and scarlet carp leap. Leaping up to the surface of the water, the true carp Illuminates its scales in the spring light.

Tachisehime no Kami chants a miuta:

Hearing of it, even the name of Gronos sounds dreadful, Yet it is hard to think of it as an island where Goros lurks. Upon such a pure and clear Sakuragaoka, We comfort the fatigue of our spring journey. Both spirit and divine soul have beheld the spring light, Is this not Sakuragaoka revived? Both mountains and fields are hazy with spring, and the gently blowing Wind contains the fragrance of a thousand flowers. How lovely, cherry blossoms are blooming on this hill, As we befriend spring with the Mihashira Kami.

Reiseihikohiko no Kami chants a miuta:

Though I suffered from the ugly rage of the Magatsumi, I have peacefully met today in the spring. If there had been no descent of the God of mihikari, Spring scented with cherry blossoms would never have come. For seventy or eighty years, plum, peach, and cherry flowers did not bloom, While it remained cold with wind and cloudy; Though there were many flower trees, they were dimmed by the malicious energy Of the Magatsumi, and spring was not there. Until yesterday, the cold winter of heaven and earth Suddenly became spring. Living in this island root for twenty years, I have never met a spring when flowers bloom. As the long day of spring inclines like the azusa bow, The curtain of twilight must have fallen. Though the sun of Amatsu hides, the light of the moon Crossing the great sky is so clear. Shall we see tonight upon Sakuragaoka The moon-boat that I gazed at in the forest of ever-green trees? The dew on the branch-tips of cherry trees shines, and Its beauty grows ever more surpassing.

§ 3

Ameharehime-no-Kami: 'Even the moon traversing the sacred skies must surely cherish / The height of the blossoms upon Sakuragaoka.'

Ameharehime-no-Kami chants a sacred miuta.

'Illuminated by the miraculous light of the moon, / The cherry blossoms at night are wondrous once again. / The weariness of a Kusamakura journey, too, / Has been forgotten amidst the moon and flowers of Sakuragaoka. / Behold us gazing upon the vernal forms / Of plums, peaches, and cherries blooming in brilliant profusion. / Surely there is no art that could compare the moon and the flowers. / In the Yahirodono newly and purely built / Upon the sacred Mahoraba of the land of Ashihara / By the light of Asakahime-no-Kami— / Setting forth at evening into the Yahirodono, lit by the blossoms, / I have caused my soul to live within the moon of the open sky. / Knowing that the flowers will not stay forever upon the branches, / But will scatter and pass away in the storm, how lamentable it is. / Nightingales, Kariyōbinga, and all a hundred birds, / Make merry and sport joyfully throughout this single day of Kefu. / Today indeed is the true height of the blossoms; when tomorrow comes, / We shall spread a magnificent Hanamushiro in this pure Suganiha.'

Thus the Kamigami, praising and glorifying the moon and the flowers, rested from the weariness of their journey throughout the day, and celebrated the eternal spring of Tokoyo.
