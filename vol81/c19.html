Chapter 19: Devil の滅亡 [2046]

悪魔の滅亡〔2046〕

§ 1

Kokuō Eerusu of the land of Saaru led a Taigun, crossed the steep passes of Ōsakayama, and attacked the castle of Idomu in an Itsukiyo. He scattered King Azumi and his other Jiushindomo, and becoming the master of Idomu Castle with Iki Yōyō, he ordered Narisu, the Umori, to lead Amata knights and return home, leaving behind the Gunshi and the Samori to supervise the land of Saaru. Narisu, with Iki Yōyō, led several hundred knights and sang with great spirit while on horseback.

'The Onaruji of the land of Saaru, / Following King Eerusu, / Leading Amata knights, / Crossing over Ōsakayama, / Attacking the village of Ningiyo, / Occupying this place without difficulty, / With momentum overflowing, Idomu Castle, / Guarded by an Amata army, / The Eugaikengo Tetsujiyō, / Occupied without any hardship, / Scattering King Azumi, / The Fūjin completely settled, / The neighing of horses and the sounds of Kachidoki, / Have become the wind blowing through the pines. / King Eerusu, with Kinzen, / Resides in the castle of Idomu, / While gazing at the Keshiki of Sanga, / Singing of the Miyo Taihei. / Thou, Narisu the Umori, / The land of Idomu is now pacified. / Thou, from here, with several hundred / Knights in tow, in a Daudau manner, / Cross over Ōsakayama, / And return to the land of Saaru, / Such was the command given; how awe-inspiring. / The Kachidoki of the King's army, / Acknowledging, I return. / The neighing of the steed is valiant, / The sound of hooves is crisp, / Dividing the mountain path as I advance. / The land of Idomu is finally / Pacified, yet the heart of the village, / What weighs on my mind is Saaru. / Left in the land of Saaru, / Crown Prince Eemusu is all alone, / Holding the politics of the land, / And must be worrying his heart. / The Amata captives must be filling / The prison of the castle of Kidayama. / This sanction, too, is by no means / An easy matter. / Hurry, advance, knights, / Until we reach the horse grounds / Of the castle of Kidayama as soon as possible.'

Singing thus, he traveled day and night, finally crossing the Kidagawa, and returned to the castle with flags and drums in a Daudau manner; his appearance was of such imposing dignity that it swept the surroundings, and the solemnity was beyond measure. Narisu, the Umori, not knowing that a consort had been decided for Crown Prince Eemusu after his departure for the campaign, slipped through the castle gate, appeared before the Prince, and sang to report the military situation in detail.

King Eemusu appeared on the throne, and while looking at Narisu with solemnity,

Eemusu: 'Art thou not Narisu the official, / Who served the Prince and headed for Idomu?'

At this song, Narisu lowered his head in surprise and sang in his report:

Narisu: 'The merit of the Prince is noble, and the royal army / Has destroyed the land of Idomu. / The royal army, while cutting down / The Amata enemies that came to attack, has advanced. / Idomu Castle, with its high stone walls, / Is difficult to attack and easy to defend. / Yet, due to the achievements of my Prince, / The enemy perished and vanished easily. / I, serving as the god of the Umori, / Have guarded thee close by thy side. / Due to the achievements of thy father, Idomu Castle / Has become a place of peace and safety today. / I have returned to convey / This situation in detail to the young King. / Leaving behind the Samori, the Gunshi, and other soldiers, / I led the knights and returned. / While gazing at the young King's diligent face, / I was moved to tears of joy. / The Mahoyoroba of the famous land of Idomu / Is a land suitable for the Prince to dwell in. / The young King remained in the land of Saaru / And was commanded to serve the land. / If it be the command of the Prince, the young King / Shall surely accept it.'

King Eemusu sang:

Eemusu: 'The joy of this day, / Hearing in detail the news of my Prince for whom I waited impatiently. / While the Prince was not here, I had no choice / But to take a wife. / Thinking the Prince was on the battlefield, / I took one for myself. / Please handle the matter before the Prince with care, / That I have taken a new wife.'

Narisu sang:

Narisu: 'How could the Prince ever hinder / The auspiciousness of the young King taking a wife? / Thinking that this land, too, is governed easily / By the royal authority of the young King, I feel joyful. / From this day forth, I, the Umori, shall serve / As the Samori of this land. / Following the command of the father King, / I shall serve as the Samori.'

§ 2

Chinryūhime, seeing the Samori Narisu for the first time, showed a look of surprise, yet being a cunning one, she remained calm and feigned ignorance.

Chinryūhime: 'I am the consort of King Eames. Are you the Samori? How well you have returned. Born between the two, Azumi and Muraji, I am indeed Chinryūhime.'

Narisu: 'How grateful I am to appear before the land of Sāru. The brave Mikokoro of the consort. From this day forth, offering my red heart, I shall serve the Wakagimi and the consort.'

Chinryūhime sang:

Hearing the words of Narisu the Samori,

The light of my soul shines bright.

Assisting the Seiji of my King, from this day,

Watch over all things in the land.

Though it is a governed land, I hear that here and there,

Namikaze arise, which is Yuyu.

That you have returned, I have waited long; this day,

Is like a Mauki meeting a Fuboku.'

Just then, the Uba Araragi appeared with a most Ōhei expression.

Araragi: 'I am the Uba Araragi who serves Chinryūhime. Lord Narisu, I am the one who protects the body of the Wakagimi, becoming his shadow and his sunshine. Understanding the heart of the Wakagimi, from this day forth, I shall plan matters with you.'

Narisu the Samori: 'What strange things I hear. Are you not the Uba of Chinryūhime? There is no point in discussing Seiji with a woman such as you; withdraw and be gone. As I am the Samoritsukasa, I have no need to listen to your words.'

Chinryūhime sang:

The words of Narisu are reasonable, yet,

Do not treat the words of Araragi lightly.

Araragi is the pillar of the land of Sāru,

You too, together, serve the country.

Though Araragi is a woman, she excels over men,

She is a wise and brave Masurame.'

Narisu sang:

Though I think the Mikotoba of the consort is reasonable,

It is ill for a woman to take the lead in matters.'

Araragi, Funzen, sang:

The Samori who despises Araragi, who recommended the consort to the Wakagimi,

Is an enemy of the land.

Can anything be governed without following the Kotoba of me, Araragi?'

King Eames sang:

The brave and wise soul of Araragi,

Cannot be reached even by a Samori.'

The Samori, Funzen, said:

I, the Samori, shall retire to the countryside, if you are to employ the lowly Araragi.'

So singing, he withdrew from the Mimahe with Ashibaya steps and vanished, no one knew where.

At that moment, the sound of war cries and shouts, enough to crumble the mountains, roared across the castle town. Numerous rioters, each carrying weapons, attacked the main castle like the madness of King Ashura. The inside of the castle became like a battlefield due to their force. Thinking it impossible for the few to oppose the many, the fake King Eames grabbed Chinryūhime under his arm, plunged into Shōbugaike pond, and the figures of the two turned into foam and vanished.

Just then, Yūzuki, the central figure of the rioters, entered deep into the palace while nocking an arrow to his bow. He advanced to the King's living quarters, but seeing that the shadows of the two were not there, he turned back. Just as he reached the entrance, Araragi came running with her hair dishevelled and her complexion changed, shouting in a loud voice:

Araragi: 'Hey, are you not Yūzuki? This is outrageous. To lure a crowd into this castle and plot a coup d'état is an outrageous method. Your crime deserves ten thousand deaths. Withdraw, withdraw!'

As she shouted, Yūzuki, while nocking an arrow to his bow, answered solemnly.

Yūzuki: 'You cunning Kusemono of evil wisdom, who wormed your way into the heart of the Wakagimi, made the true Chinryūhime-sama your own child, burdened her with a great crime and sentenced her to exile on a distant island, called your own child Chinryūhime-sama, deceived the eyes of the Wakagimi-sama, practiced tyrannical rule, and cast the Kunitsukami into the suffering of fire and water—all this is the work you have done. Now that this day has come, you cannot escape your destiny. Are you prepared to commit suicide, or will you become the rust of my bow and arrow? What is your resolve? I shall hear your answer.'

As he attacked, Araragi panicked and tried to flee, but Yūzuki drew his bow to a full moon and released the arrow with a sharp snap. Pierced by the powerful arrow, Araragi fell easily and vanished.

From this point on, there was no control within the castle, and no one knew where Narisu the Samori had gone. It was inevitable that Kidayama Castle was left to the rampant behavior of devils.

これより城内は統制機関なく、左守のナーリスも何処へ行きしか皆目分らず、木田山城はさながら悪魔の跳梁に任せけるこそ是非なけれ。
