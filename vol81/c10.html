Chapter 10: 復 Ancient 運動 [2037]

復古運動〔2037〕

§ 1

The crowd, a mixture of soldiers led by Mark and Raat, retook the Idumu castle without a single battle, as if walking through an uninhabited land; and as the military strategist Eeman, in the wake of his Shiushiyōraubai, leaped into a raging torrent and vanished, the Fūjin completely subsided, and a mood of Kausei drifted across the Tenchi.

Tāman, who had been serving as the Umori of King Azumi, was lurking in disguise among this crowd, and seeing this Kuōkei, he rejoiced greatly, stepped close to the side of Raat and Mark, exchanged a firm Akushiyu, and was overcome with tears of Iyahi.

Tāman sings to Mark and Raat:

The Lord who hath restored the Osukuni which my King had lost is a Kami. My King, secluded in Tsukimitsuyama, prayed morning and evening. The Mitama of the Lord God must have descended to add strength to you two Futahashira. If this be so, the land of Idumu shall surely return to the Chika of King Azumi. You must convey your merits in detail to the King; he will surely reward you. I have been lurking in the Jiyōka of Idumu, awaiting this Yokihi. How noble are you, who have exhausted your Magokoro, thinking of the King and the country.

Mark sings:

It is Arigata; even a powerless body like mine has accomplished this through the grace of the Lord God. I, who have served for the sake of the King and the country, shall not desire any reward. That King Eersu, who oppressed and devastated the people, has perished; this must be the will of Heaven. I, who attacked the unprepared castle of Idumu, have no power of my own. Through the time of Heaven, the principles of the earth, and the harmony of men, the beginning of the restoration has opened. The Magatsu, who oppressed the people, squeezed their blood, and grew arrogant, has perished and vanished. From this day forth, I intend to welcome King Azumi and bring peace to the hearts of the people.

Tāman sings:

It is Arigata; I shall quickly convey the words of the official Mark to my King. Thinking that my King will hear of your heart and rejoice, I am filled with joy.

Mark sings:

I, too, have been racking my heart for years to rectify the tilting country. Now that the time of Heaven has arrived, how pleasant is the feeling of this day, as my thoughts have finally cleared. Though I am powerless, the bowstring of my Magokoro did not break easily. The arrow released from the bowstring shall pierce through anywhere; such is the heart of a brave man. The black clouds that shrouded Idumu castle have cleared away, and the sound of insects chirping has become clear. The clouds that wrapped the vast sky have also cleared, and the moon in the Misono has begun to shine. The beginning of the restoration, which has taken years to build, has begun to open on this Yokihi. It was worth praying to the Kami morning and evening to see this new moon today. What more could I desire? I shall only follow the will of my King. I, who possess not a speck of impure heart, do not desire the rank of an official.

Seeing the Kausei of the land of Idumu, where the moon and sun shine, I have no further desires. The voices of the people's cheers are roaring so loudly that even the mountains seem to shake. The people feel as if they have been revived and will joyfully welcome King Azumi. I have been planning for the restoration for years, hiding from the eyes of the inspectors. I never thought I would encounter such a joyous day within a single year. King Eersu, who defiled the short days of a year, has vanished like a dream.

Tāman sings again:

The Magokoro of the people has gathered, and the clouds over the land of Idumu have cleared. I, too, have wandered through the alleys of life and death, awaiting this Yokihi. Starting with the brave Lord Mark, I intend to express my gratitude to Lord Raat. Secluded in the mountain of Tsukimitsuyama, my King has spent days of lamentation. It has become an Arigata era; the strict grace of the Lord God is ever-increasing. From today, I shall serve the Lord Ōkami and pray for the prosperity of the country. That I have encountered this unprecedented joy is due to the divine authority of the Kami and your efforts, and the nobility of you, who stand representing the Magokoro of the people.

Mark sings:

Whenever I hear the words of Lord Tāman, the Umori, I am moved to tears. It was worth exhausting my heart, deep within my soul, so that the reign of my King might be peaceful. Many times I was captured by the ugly inspectors and thrown into a dark prison. The heart of the inspectors, who tormented us without even giving us enough food, was that of a crooked demon. There were days when I was thrown into prison, beaten, and wept in misery. Seven or eight times I was locked in prison, lamenting the future of the world. Through the grace of the Lord God, I have finally reached this Yokihi. When I think of the days that have passed, tears of sorrow and anger flow down my cheeks of their own accord. There was not a single day of peace, as I was glared at by the officials of the inspectors. Called a 'blacklist' by the people of the world, how many days did I lament in tears of sorrow and anger.

§ 2

Tāman sings with a look of Buzen:

‘The tale of Lord Māku, so Ō and brave, Hearing it, tears of Hifun fall. Having cast aside his body to serve the King and the Mikuni, I weep for the heart of brave Māku. Enduring Kusagusa of sorrows, here and now, You have achieved the hope of Ishin.’

Rāto sings:

‘I, too, have endured the shame of the Nahame many times, And have served the Mikuni. The Inochi of my Chichihaha were taken, and the Yukuhe of my wife and children Are unknown, and I have suffered. For the sake of the King, for the sake of the Mikuni, for the sake of my Chichihaha, For the sake of my wife and children, I was encouraged. Where might my wife be, and what has become of my Miko, Thinking of this, I am filled with sorrow. Forgetting my Chichihaha and my Tsumako, until Kefumade, I have worked for the sake of the Mikuni.’

Tāman sings:

‘I have now heard the tale of Umoritsukasa Rāto, And have become filled with sorrow. My King also endured sufferings and hardships No less than yours. The Kimitami suffered with one heart, And have achieved the Taigefu of Ishin. Since it has come to this, I shall make haste to inform my King Of this Zuishiyō as soon as Hitohi passes. Mononofu, Lord Rāto, pray wait in this castle Until my King arrives. I shall now take Lord Māku with me And hasten back to Tsukimitsuyama.’

Rāto sings:

‘I accept the Onkotoba of Umoritsukasa Tāman, And I shall guard the castle.’

Tāman sings:

‘Then let us go, together with Lord Māku, And hasten back to the mountain of Tsukikage.’

Thereupon, Umori Tāman, together with the Yūshi Māku, mounted a chestnut horse, and sent off by the cheers of the crowd, they hastened toward Tsukimitsuyama, a hundred ri away, with the brave sound of hooves.

Tāman sings grandly from horseback:

‘Ah, how brave, how brave, By the grace of the Lord Ōkami, The black clouds of the ugly that shrouded the castle of Idomu Have cleared away. The castle of Idomu, from ancient times, Has been the territory of the King of Azumi, A true Mahoroba of the island of Isako. Intoxicated by the dream of supreme peace, Neglecting the preparations for war, Watching for the gap in our defense, The wicked and lawless country of Sāru, Surrounded us with the army of King Ērusu, And the ancient castle of the King fell, alas. Starting with the King of Azumi, The Umori, the Samori, and the military strategists, Gathered the remaining retainers, Crossed the fields, Crossed mountains and rivers, and hid themselves in the Steep mountains of Tsukikage.

Building a shrine to the gods of heaven and earth, While practicing the rites of Misogi, We waited for the time to arrive. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase, The divine majesty of the gods has appeared, The sound of war cries and the shouts of victory Have become the wind blowing through the reeds. Ah, how grateful, how joyful, Do the people still protect the country Without abandoning our King? The two heroes, Māku and Rāto, Having the courage of Tsukushi in their hearts, Suddenly, the black clouds of the ugly over the land of Idomu Have been swept away. The sun and moon are clear, the water is clear, The mountains and fields are turning a fresh green, And various fruits are ripening well. In the paradise of the land of Idomu, Welcoming the King once again, This day, enjoying the prosperity of a thousand generations,

Is a joy unheard of even in the age of the gods. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase, How precious is the grace of the gods. From now on, I shall turn my heart, Reform it and sharpen it, Morning and evening, I shall Revere and pray to the Lord Ōkami, And with all my heart, I shall plan for The prosperity of the land of Idomu And the happiness of my people. Though the road I travel is long, The horse of a thousand ri is swift, And as I advance, its mane flutters In the blowing wind. Is the horse a dragon of the earth? Without trouble on the winding paths, It runs without fearing the difficult terrain. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase, May there be happiness on the path ahead, May there be light in my Kototama.’

Māku sings grandly from horseback:

‘Following Umori Tāman, I go to Tsukimitsuyama, To tell the King of Azumi in detail Of the state of the country. Today’s journey is pleasant, The neighing of the horse and the sound of hooves Resound truly bravely. The autumn mountains are turning red, And the scent of the sleeves of Sahohime, Who weaves the brocade, is yearning. In the mountain gorges to the right and left, The voices of deer calling for their mates are clear, And the Tsukikage illuminating the valley Shines as clear as a mirror. As I advance along the lower road, I feel as if I am going to the land of Kasho, It is a brave feeling indeed. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase, Blessed by the divine majesty of the gods, Which has reached the extreme of tyranny and lawlessness,’

§ 3

King Aersu has perished. Princess Saxu has been slain. Starting with Samori Chikuta, all the mighty Naito have become mere Minawa due to the Keiriyaku of the Ningiyo officials, a miserable fate indeed. Just as Gunshi Eman, the only one remaining, was plotting to succeed them, we deemed the time ripe and gathered our Dōshira, filled with Iukokuhifun, in the suburbs of the castle of Idumu. As we loudly proclaimed the Saukiyo of the Ishin, the Gunshiu agreed in unison and surged like clouds and mist into the Jiyōnai where the ugly ones were hiding. Gunshi Eman, startled, suddenly plunged into the Gekiriu of Minano and lost his Inochi.

Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara. Within the castle, the shadows of the Tekishiyō have vanished completely, as if walking through a Mujin field. This is entirely the Kōhei and Mushi Onsaba of the Lord God. I humbly offer my gratitude. Now that it has come to this, I pray that you convey in detail the sincere heart of the people, who beg for nothing more than to welcome our King of Adzumi as quickly as possible, even if only a day sooner, and to have him establish a new reign. Oh, Migimori-kami. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara. Today's joy is boundless. The blowing wind is refreshing, and there is a resonance of the regeneration of heaven and earth. Even if Mount Gekkō is steep, and even if the valley streams are deep,

Why should a brave man falter? With a heart of great valor, I shall charge forward and report back before the King. How joyful that moment shall be. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara. May the divine spirits be blessed.
