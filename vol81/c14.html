Chapter 14: 鷺と烏 [2041]

鷺と烏〔2041〕

§ 1

Here, Chinriuhime, due to the earnest entreaty of her nurse Araragi who unraveled the situation, reluctantly consented to become the consort of Aemsu, the crown prince of the enemy commander Erusu, and it is truly pitiable that she sought to escape the immediate crisis.

King Aemsu, filled with joy and dancing with delight, commanded his subjects and strictly ordered that a grand wedding ceremony be held in the inner palace; thus, inside Kitayamajiyōnai, it was as if a cauldron were boiling, a great commotion from top to bottom, and there was none, high or low, who did not rejoice at the auspicious marriage of the young king.

Among them, Chinriuhime, as the star performer of the wedding, felt as if she had ascended from hell to heaven in contrast to her life in prison until this day, yet in her heart, she remained immersed in tears of sorrow.

King Aemsu invited Chinriuhime to the inner palace and, with his face beaming with a gentle expression, sang.

King Aemsu's song: 'In the garden where the Yufugaho blooms, I beheld thy form and fell into deep distress. Accepting that all things are a matter of time and season, thou who hast granted thyself to me art beloved. If thy heart were not in accord with mine, I would have suffered, fearing the thread of life might cease. Bathed in the dew of the blessings of the gods of heaven and earth, how happy I am to meet thee today. I shall not begrudge even the thread of life, for I am held in thy heart. Forgive the act committed without the rites of thy parents; soon I shall requite thy heart. I shall save thy father in peace and welcome him to the castle of Idumu. I lay my heart bare before thee and swear that I have no double heart.'

Chinriuhime sang: 'I thank the gods for my good fortune in having met the noble heart of the young king. While serving forever by the side of the king, I shall await the day I meet my parents. Though I unexpectedly hasten to the king, I do so while thinking of the sorrow in my parents' hearts.'

King Aemsu sang: 'Because of this love for which I staked my thread of life, I feel as if I am ascending to heaven. The morning moon, the evening moon, Araragi, and Senriu—their sincerity shines, and today is truly joyful.'

Araragi sang: 'Invited before the pure presence of the young king, my soul trembles with joy. I have consoled the heart of Chinriuhime, and on this auspicious day, I have come to meet you. Serving the young king forever, I too shall pray for the prosperity of the realm.'

Asazuki sang: 'Having revered the words of the young king, the Kototama has struck and broken through the various barriers. How I have racked my brain, wondering how to welcome the heart of the princess. Chinriuhime's heart has stirred, and my soul has begun to shine ever more brightly.'

Yufuzuki sang: 'I too was anxious, wondering what would become of it, but the heart of the princess has begun to stir. Through the aid of the living Kototama of Araragi, how happy I am to meet on this auspicious day.'

At last, the grand wedding ceremony was to be held, and the cheers of the Kunitsukami and others inside and outside the castle were enough to move heaven and earth. Inside the palace, a solemn wedding ceremony was underway. Araragi, acting as the intermediary, sang a celebratory song.

'Since the opening of heaven and earth, there has been no precedent; how auspicious it is to meet on this day of days.

The sun rises over Mount Daiei, and the moon floats on the surface of the Kidagawa. In the pure garden of Kitayamajiyōnai, the great palace pillars are firmly set, and in this castle with its sturdy defenses, the young king, King Aemsu, has welcomed Chinriuhime, the beloved daughter of the King of Azumi.

At the auspicious hour of this evening, the ceremony of the flowery candles is held; may they live in harmony as husband and wife, and may their bond be firm for a thousand generations forever.

As the King of the land of Saru, revered by the Kunitsukami and others, the time has come for him to rule over the vast and boundless plains of the country, as firm and eternal as the base of a rock.

The father, the Great King, has crossed the mountains of Daiei and is now the King of the land of Idumu, having ousted the King of Azumi and reigning in glory.

Yet, our King is possessed of infinite benevolence; he pities the Kunitsukami and others, and bathed in the dew of his grace, even the birds, beasts, insects, and fish submit to the King's virtue and sing of this auspicious day. The voice of the crane hidden in the branches of the dense forest of Kitayamajiyōnai is heard most clearly. The pine tree is deep in the color of a thousand years, manifesting the state of eternity. Chinriuhime is a wise woman, and also a refined woman; appearing as the consort of this land, she shall shine in all four directions. I have served by the side of the princess for twenty years.'

To have met with today's Yokihi Is indeed the dew of Kami's favor. Ah, arigata, medeta, thus I offer my blessings upon today's Yokihi.

King Ēmusu sings:

A joy unheard of from times of old, Such I seem to have encountered, taking a Hime as my bride. As heaven and earth clear in purity, my heart likewise Clears completely, like the moon in Misora. The Tsukikage clearing above the ridge of Ōsakayama Is today exceedingly pure. The sound of the wind blowing across Nobe Seems somehow to sing of today's joy. The Tsukikage gleaming on the ridge of Ōsakayama Floats upon the stream of Kita and celebrates. Upon the surface of the evening river where no Sazanami rises, The Tsukikage shines round and pure. If it be so, the King of Azumi is my father, And Ērusu has also become my father. Hand in hand, the land of Idomu and the land of Sāru Shall prosper forever upon the island of Isago.

Chinriuhime sings:

Forgetting everything completely, on this day I am one who enjoys being a bride. Waiting for the right time to return my father's Mikuni, Such has been the prayer of my heart. Becoming the consort of the compassionate King Ēmusu, I desire to fulfill my Kauyō to my parents. Looking at the evening moon illuminating Kitamajiyō, It shines with a smile, resembling the face of the King.

Asazuki sings:

The Kunitsukami gather like mountains, And their voices are heard singing of today's Yokihi. The battle cries of tens of thousands of Kunitsukamira Resound through Ame and earth.

Yufuzuki sings:

The light of Yufuzuki shines clear, Seeming to celebrate the joy of the Wakagimi today. I too am numbered among those on this mat, Overcome with joy, I have no words to utter. The Sama of mountains and rivers rejoicing can be seen, And the May rains have completely cleared.

Senriu sings:

By the blessing of Himegimi's valiant heart, Peacefully I have gazed upon the moon of a foreign land. It is far purer than the moon I gazed upon at the castle of Idomu in days past. Even my figure and features are said by people To resemble Himegimi, which is indeed strange.

Araragi sings:

It is because both Himegimi and you drank your fill of my milk And were nurtured by me. Though a maiden of humble birth, you are foster siblings, So beautiful as to be mistaken for Hime.

At long last, Chinriuhime finished the marriage ceremony and was about to proceed into the King's bedchamber, but her wet nurse, Araragi, with a troubled expression, secretly invited Chinriuhime into a room and spoke.

Araragi: 'Hime-sama, a terrible worry has come upon me. I am at a loss as to what should be done, but I beg your forgiveness. It is the greatest mistake of my life.'

Chinriuhime, her heart pounding at the unexpected words of her wet nurse, sang:

Do I hear strange words at this hour? Sorrow drifts upon your face.

The wet nurse, Araragi, lowered her voice even further.

Araragi: 'Hime-sama, how could I not be worried? According to what I heard from the handmaidens Takitsuse and Yamakaze, until now the King has welcomed beautiful consorts many times, but every single one of them lost her life after just one night. That rumor has spread far and wide, and for that reason, it is said that no one in this country will become the King's consort. No matter how noble a status one attains, it is meaningless without life! Now that it has come to this, even if we tried to escape, there is not even a crack for an ant to crawl out of.'

She whispered in her ear, out of breath.

Chinriuhime sings:

Dreadful things I hear; Thinking Araragi's words to be truth, I am terrified. How can I escape this place and prolong My eternal Inochi? If Araragi has good wisdom, please lend it to me; The Inochi of my precious life is heavy.

Araragi lowered her voice even further and spoke.

Araragi: 'Hime-sama, this King is a monstrous beast born from a bear and a tiger. Though he presents such a gentle appearance, when he embraces someone at night, the strength of his arms is so immense that a fragile Himegimi-sama would be strangled to death in a single breath and perish. Having served you for twenty years, I would have no excuse if my precious Himegimi-sama were killed in this place now. After thinking over various things, I squeezed out a piece of good wisdom. That is to say, my daughter Senriu is a foster sibling to you, so her face and figure do not differ from Hime-sama's by even a hair's breadth; it is said that one cannot distinguish between a sweet flag and an iris. Taking advantage of this, we shall have her exchange garments with Hime-sama, while Hime-sama wears Senriu's clothes and hides in the dark, and for just this one night we shall observe how things unfold. Since Senriu is my humble daughter, the difference between high and low birth is like that between heaven and earth. Therefore, if you permit her to act as your substitute tonight, I shall surely save Hime-sama from this peril.'

As Kotobataku persuaded her with clever words, Chinriuhime did not perceive the dark heart of her Uba Araragi in the slightest, so she cast off her Seisau and had Senriuhime Kika, while she herself donned the Kimono of the Senriu woman and waited, hiding in a Hitoma.

However, that night there was nothing particularly unusual, and the Senriu woman was Kinzen, strolling about the Asaniha.

Chinriuhime pulled on the sleeve of her Uba and, in a Kogoe, said:

Chinriuhime: 'Uba, it seems nothing happened last night, but what on earth do you suppose the Ōsama is thinking? Is it possible that he does not notice that a Kahedama has been used?'

As she spoke with a look of Shinbaige, Uba Araragi brought her mouth close to the ear of Chinriuhime and said:

Uba Araragi: 'It is said that if you take the Suishiyō Kuwabin decorated on this Saidan out to the garden and strike it quietly with a Koishi, you will immediately gain the Kuwanshin of the Ōsama, and he will surely love you, Hime-sama. It is said that the Ōsama believes my daughter Senriu to be the Makoto Hime-sama, and because he fears the Kahedama of last night, he cannot speak of it; therefore, please take this Kuwabin to the garden and strike it a little. It will produce a pure sound.'

As she Nengoro persuaded her, the innocent Chinriuhime, not knowing that there was a deep Keiriyaku, took the Suishiyō Kuwabin to the garden and struck it, whereupon the Suishiyō Kuwabin broke into two with a pop.

Upon seeing this, Uba Araragi grabbed Chinriuhime by the hair, Hikizu her about, and said:

Uba Araragi: 'Though you are a lady-in-waiting to Hime-sama, to strike and break a treasure of the house with a stone is beyond words; you are my child, yet you are not my child. Everyone, a great criminal has appeared!'

As she shouted in a loud voice, many officials gathered, surrounded her ten and twenty layers deep, and attacked with weapons in their hands, saying not to let the ruffian escape.

Chinriuhime was surprised by the unexpected turn of events and shouted to Uba Araragi:

Chinriuhime: 'Thou art not my daughter!'

Araragi, fearing that if it were discovered it would be a serious matter, stuffed cotton into the mouth of Hime to gag her, and beat her head and face until blood oozed out and she became a hideous figure, bearing no resemblance to her former self; thus, alas, Chinriuhime was sentenced to exile as a great criminal.

と呼ばはりければ、アララギは発覚しては大事と、姫の口に真綿を含ませ猿轡をかませ、頭部面部を打ち据ゑければ、血にじみ上り似ても似つかぬ醜悪なる面となりければ、茲に憐れや大罪人としてチンリウ姫は遠島の刑に処せられけり。
