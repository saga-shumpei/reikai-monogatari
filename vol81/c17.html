Chapter 17: 再生再会 [2044]

再生再会〔2044〕

§ 1

Queen Chinriuhime of King Ēmusu was an impostor. Asazuki, having realized that the handmaid Senriume had conspired with Araragi to beguile King Ēmusu and numerous jiushin, hinted at this matter at the banquet; whereupon she immediately incurred the gekido of Araragi, Senriew, and their faction, was instantly sentenced for a jiyūzai, and was banished to a desolate island drifting upon the aranami of kaiyōbanri. Overcome by kaugaihifun, Asazuki sang her jiyutsukuwai in song.

The surge of the tide resounded tautau, echoing among the ihama; the incoming waves shook like the manes of hakuba, a monosusama kuōkei as if biting into the rocks of the kishibe. Asazuki, like the ruler of this island, gathered shellfish and the like to stave off her hunger, and entrusting her fate to Ten, she gazed upon the heubeu unabara and sang.

Looking up, how high it is, The sky of the hisakata clouds is boundless; My soul, dissolving into the azure, Swells and expands to become the moon, And again separating, becomes the stars; Traversing the immense universe of unknown limits, As if it were my own domain. Am I the shadow of the morning moon? Cleaving through the waves, I ascend; Illuminated by the light of the morning sun, Though concealing my form by day, When evening comes, like the evening moon, Its light brightly illuminates the spaces between the waves; At the bottom of the thousand-fathom sea, How purely clear is the evening moon! In the swaying of the morning moon, How heroic is its floating upon the waves! Leaving the island of Isako behind, Traversing a thousand folds of wild waves, When I come to look from the single-log boat,

The famed desolate island of renown Has not a single tree nor a blade of grass; Blown by the wild winds and waves, It is an island of rock with no leisure to grow. Steadfast and eternal in the midst of the sea, How heroic is this island's root floating! Asazuki has been banished here, Unknowing of worldly dust, peacefully Thriving steadfastly and eternally. No matter how fiercely the wild waves rage, No matter if heat or cold should strike, What should a brave person fear? Could anything break my valiant spirit? Ah, how delightful, how delightful! Since this desolate island is vast, I shall establish it as my eternal dwelling, Taking a hundred species of fish as my companions, And be sung of as the King of the Dragon Palace. Be that as it may, how pitiful it is! Chinriuhime has fallen into the trap of a villain's craft,

Forced to yield to the completely unmatching substitute, The handmaid Senriew, Burdened with an unexpected crime, And banished to the island of Oki. The pitifulness of it presses upon my body; Attending the celebratory banquet Held at the castle of Kidayama, The violent arrows and bullets of Kototama Which were loosed filled them with fear and dread; The foul-hearted Araragi Interrupted my words, And while forcing upon me the crime of suspicion, Together with the youth king madly in love And her daughter, the woman Senriew, Out of their hatred for me, Bound my arms high and low, And banished me to this desolate island. Though I, a brave person, am prepared, Not even exposed to a draft of wind,

Reared deep within the imperial palace, Chinriuhime has been banished to the island of demons; How hateful this is! Be that as it may, bearing the name of the island of demons, The root of the island of Oki, When evening comes, beneath the wild waves Is said to conceal the entire island's form; To abandon her on such a dangerous island Is the conspiracy of Araragi and her faction To take the life of the Hime. When I think of it, when I think of it, how hateful it is! Now that it has come to this, Though I weep and regret, the Hime-gimi's Form is already swallowed into the belly of the wild waves, And no shadow of her remains. Should the blessing of the divine grace favor us, If she should still exist in this world, Submerging beneath the water's bottom, May she come to this island, kannagara! I pray to the gods of heaven and earth.

Ah, and yet, how wondrous it is! In last night's dream, Chinriuhime Was borne upon the back of a turtle, And reaching a certain seashore, Built a tiny hermitage In a forest of dense trees and dwelt there. Is it a dream, reality, or an illusion? What weighs upon my heart is the fate of the Hime; Though I wish to know her whereabouts Completely and in full detail, it cannot be helped. Ah, kannagara, kannagara, grant thy benevolence!

Upon this island where the sky is blue and the ocean is blue, Sighing in memory of the Hime, Living alone on this desolate island far removed from Isako Island, My lonely self finds it solitary. Be that as it may, without hearing the griefs of the world, I am happy alone today. The castle of Mount Kida shall soon perish; At the tyrannical hands of Araragi mother and child, Chinriuhime was banished to the island of Oki, And that she vanished like water bubbles was indeed fleeting. Be that as it may, that the Hime maintains her life Is what I heard upon the pillow of my dream; That the wicked should flourish and the good should perish Is an example that I think exists not even in the age of the gods. It was a crooked deed issuing from the vile heart of Araragi, For which any amount of hatred would still be insufficient.'

§ 2

Just at the moment he thus sang, the Kiyodai Shinki that had safely delivered Chinriuhime to the Masago beach surfaced with a plop at the water's edge, moving its neck Jiyōge as if beckoning Asazuki. Asazuki, joyfully taking this as the very Keshin of Kotohirawakenomikoto, the Shiyugojin of the sea, at once descended the hill, ran to the Migihabe, and sang while pressing his hands together in Gatsushiyō:

‘O how Arashikoto with Kotohirawake's Onmuka! Pray take me over to the island of Isago.’

As he leaped onto the back of the Shinki while pressing his hands together, the turtle lifted its great head above the Hajiyō and, parting the waves, sped straight toward the south.

Asazuki sang:

‘O how Arashikoto through the Mimegumi of the Kami of Tenchi! Kotohirawake hath verily manifested Himself. Upon a desolate island without Itsupon of grass or tree, I lived out my days in solitude; Rescued by the Keshin of the Kami Kotohirawake, This very Kefu I cross the Chihe of Namiji. The mountain of Ōsaka hazeth amid the Kumoma, And the light of the Amatsu sun appeareth dim; Borne by the wind blowing from the north, I now Return riding upon the back of the Shinki. Chinriuhime likewise upon this very turtle Must have been saved; how I long to hear thereof!’

Singing thus, he left himself to the course of the turtle. Akatsukigoro of the Akuruhi, when the morning moon turned pale in the sky and the sea breeze gently blew against his sleeves, the turtle arrived at the beach of Masago.

Asazuki leaped down from the back of the turtle to the strand, and facing the Shinki, sang while pressing his hands together in Gatsushiyō:

‘Even Asazuki, who lamented on the solitary island of rough waves, Hath been rescued through thy virtue. Never shall I forget thy grace; My grief hath been utterly dispelled. The high mountain hazing in the northeastern sky Is Mount Ōsaka, a dearly beloved mountain. Is this shore in the sacred land of Idomu, Since Mount Ōsaka is seen to the north?’

Hereupon Asazuki offered thanks to the turtle, bade it farewell, and crunching along the Masago of the strand, made his way toward the forest of Shigeki in the far distance, where ancient trees grew thick and lush.

As Asazuki trod aimlessly alone in the shade of the forest of Shigeki, he noticed a tiny hut bound together with miscanthus at the root of a strikingly thick zelkova tree. Stealing his footsteps, he approached and peeked at the conditions within. From inside the small hut, the faint voice of a woman singing echoed forth:

‘My land was stolen by the enemy, and my parents’ Whereabouts are unknown; how sorrowful it is! By the hideous chief of Ērusu my father’s Castle was stolen, and he must have passed away. I too, possessing a weak and fragile body, Was captured by the enemy army and sent far away. Captured in the castle of Kidayama of murky waters, I passed my days of lamentation weeping. Araragi, who served me for twenty years, Turned into a demon and rebelled against me. Though I know not what crime I have committed, My present self is filled with loneliness. To take away the thread of my life, Araragi Sent me off to the hidden island. Even at the moment when I was about to be swallowed by the rough waves, I was rescued by Kotohirawake. Mount Ōsaka riseth far away high in the northern sky; Seeing it hazing, it must indeed be my land. And yet the land of Idomu hath now already Fallen under the command of Sāru, alas the sorrow! Escaping at last from the hidden island, I now Hide in the forest of Shigeki and weep. Though I long to meet my parents but once, For my present self there is no avail. Brimming with ten thousand bushels of tears, I now Have no recourse but to weep. Thinking whether I shall rot uselessly in the shade of the forest trees, How sorrowful is my own plight!’

Hearing this song, Asazuki realized that she was truly Chinriuhime who had been exiled to the hidden island, and leaping for joy, he sang in a high voice:

‘I am the one who served in the castle of Kidayama, The remnant of the governor Asazuki. She who hideth within this house is surely none other than Chinriuhime, as I have now realized. Through the filthy-hearted plot of Araragi, I grieve for Hime who hath been brought to such a state. I too, for having declared Chinriuhime To be an impostor, was banished. Through the wrath of Araragi and Senriuhime, I was exiled to the desolate island. Worrying for the noble Hime from the desolate island, I looked up far away at the peak of the hidden island. Escorted by the Keshin of the Kami Kotohirawake, I have arrived at the beach of Masago.’

From within, the voice of Chinriuhime spoke:

'How suspicious, the sound of a human voice in the Shigeki forest.

It must be the work of a fox or a raccoon dog.

Though my sumika is an abaraya, the omotedo

must not be opened for a magami.

Asazuki is the Samorigami of Kitayamajiyō castle;

there should be no reason for him to come here.

The takumi of the fox and raccoon dog, weaving various words to deceive,

is truly shallow.

Together with Araragi and Senriuhime,

the maga of Asazuki plotted against me.

Even if you are truly Asazuki, I say,

I shall not meet you, even if I must die.'

Asazuki replied sorrowfully:

'Did I ever think that upon reaching the Shigeki forest,

I would hear words of anger from the Hime?

I am Asazuki, who has served with sincerity,

protecting the Himegimi both in shadow and in light.

It is sad that you think of me as one of the kind of Araragi and Senriuhime,

who only appear gentle.

For the words I spoke to protect the Himegimi, I was cursed,

and cast away to Arashima.

If it has come to this, I cannot return to the land of Saru.

I shall live in hiding in the Shigeki forest.

The castle of Kitayama shall perish, for in the behavior of Araragi,

which is devoid of humanity,

the officials within the jiyōnai are in a state of shibungoretsu,

and there is none who does not curse the mother and child of Araragi.

As a retribution for attacking the Idomu of the neighboring country,

the land of Saru shall soon perish.

The Onchichi, the King of Azumi, shall soon

take ushiha of the Shimane of Isago.

Please understand the pure heart of Asazuki,

for I have come longing to serve the Hime.'

Chinriuhime sang:

'By your various Kototama, the clouds in my breast

have cleared; enter quickly.

That you, a lone man, should come to visit this hermitage

of a lone woman is truly mysterious.

Were you also saved by Kotohirawake?

I, too, was sent here by a turtle.'

While singing thus, she suddenly pushed open the brushwood door from within. Asazuki prostrated himself on the ground, shedding tears, and said:

Hime-sama, I am so moved to see you. I felt pity for your situation, and at the grand celebration, I sang a satire that the current Chinriuhime-sama was an impostor and that this state of affairs was caused by the treacherous plot of Araragi. Because of this, I incurred the intense wrath of the mother and child of Araragi and King Ames, was judged by the officials of treacherous and cunning hearts, and was finally exiled to an uninhabited island called Arashima in the sea. While I was lamenting my solitude, the god Kotohirawake appeared in the form of a turtle and just a moment ago sent me to the shore of Masago. I surmised that you, Hime-sama, must have also been saved from the hidden island by the god Kotohirawake and were hiding in this vicinity. While wandering through the forest, this dwelling suddenly caught my eye. I approached with hushed footsteps, and when I peeked at the state of the interior, I heard the faint melody of your song and was overjoyed, convinced it was you, Hime-sama. I stood outside, fearing to intrude, and asked for you through song. If, by your benevolence, Hime-sama, you would use me as your servant, I would consider it a most grateful fortune. I have no intention of setting foot in the land of Saru again. Although this island is also the territory of your father, it is within the domain where King Aels of Saru wields his tyranny. If I am discovered by his underlings, it will be dangerous, so I shall take advantage of this forest, serve by your side, Hime-sama, and wait for the time to come. Although the King, your father, once abandoned the castle and retreated, I expect from now on that the wise King Azumi will surely prepare his military forces, return to power, and restore the Idomu castle to implement good governance. Furthermore, as the signs of the destruction of the land of Saru are already appearing, I believe that the island of Isago will entirely return to the rule of King Azumi. Hime-sama, please be at ease.'

With these words, exhausting all his eloquence, Asazuki comforted Chinriuhime and waited for the time to come, making this forest his sumika for a while.

『いろいろの汝が言霊にわが胸の 雲は晴れたりとく入りませよ なよ草の女一人のこの庵に 汝が訪ひ来しも不思議なるかな 汝も亦琴平別に救はれしか われも神亀に送られ来りぬ』

斯く歌ひながら、柴の戸を中よりパツと押開けば、朝月は大地にひれ伏し、ハラハラと落涙しながら、

『姫様、御懐かしう御座います。私は貴女の御身の上を気の毒に存じ、大祝賀会の席上に於て、今のチンリウ姫様は贋物にして、アララギの奸計より斯くなれるものとの諷刺を歌ひましたため、アララギ母子及びエームス王の激怒にふれ、奸佞邪智の心きたなき司どもに審判かれ、遂に海中の荒島という無人島に流され、孤独を託ちつつあるところへ、琴平別の神、亀と化して現はれ給ひ、たつた今の先、真砂の浜辺に私を送りとどけて下さつたのです。必ずや姫様も隠島より琴平別の神に救はれて、此の辺りにおしのびの事と察知致しまして、森林を彷徨ふうち、フツとこの御住居が目にとまり、足音をしのばせ近より、屋内の様子を窺へば、かすかに聞ゆる御歌のふしに、てつきり姫様と打ち喜び、畏れながら屋外に立ち、歌もて御尋ね致した次第で御座います。何卒姫様の御仁慈によりまして、私を僕として御使ひ下さらうならば、有難い仕合せと存じます。私は再びサールの国に足を踏み入れる考へは御座いませぬ。この島も御父の領分とは言ひながら、サールの国王エールスが暴威を振ふ領域内で御座いますれば、彼等が手下の奴輩に見つかつては危険で御座いますから、この森林を幸ひ、姫様の御側に仕へて時待つ事と致しませう。一時は御父王は城を捨てて退却されましたなれど、賢明なるアヅミ王様は必ず軍備を整へ、捲土重来して、イドム城を回復し、善政を敷き給ふものと、私は今より期待いたして居ります。次にサールの国は最早や滅亡の徴現はれ居りますれば、伊佐子の島は全部アヅミ王様の治下に復する事と存じます。姫様、御安心なさいませ』

と、いろいろと言葉を尽して、朝月はチンリウ姫を慰めながら、暫時この森林を住家として時を待ちゐたりける。
