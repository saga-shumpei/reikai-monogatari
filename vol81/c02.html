Chapter 2: Moon Light Mountain [2029]

月光山〔2029〕

§ 1

Idomu Castle was defeated by the Shifurai of the enemy, and King Azumi, along with his consort Muraji, Samori Naman, Umori Taman, the Gunshi Shiuran, and other surviving Ikusabitora, fled far to the south, seized the Kenshiyo of Tsukimitsuyama, built a Jiyōheki here, and while governing the Kunihara at the Nantan, resolved to await the time for a Saikiyo.

The King's only daughter, Chinriuhime, and her attendant Araragi, along with many Yūshi, became Horiyo of the enemy, were taken far north across Ōsakayama, and were bound in the Raugoku within the capital of Sāru, thus spending sad Tsukihi.

King Azumi, in his deep grief over the absence of his Saiai daughter Chinriuhime, sings a Jiyutsukuwai.

'From the distant Kamiyo of old, Wrapped in the winds of Heiwa, Living in peace and joy, The land of Idomu has, alas, Been seized by the military of Eērusu Of the land of Sāru, And now, retreated to the fleeting Nantan Of Tsukimitsuyama, How painful it is to plan a Saikiyo. Many of my Mikata have been Destroyed by the Tekigun, and my army Has been easily defeated. In the midst of such sorrow, My beloved daughter, who should inherit my reign, Chinriuhime is nowhere to be seen, And I can only weep as I seek her. Has she perhaps been slain by the enemy? As I think and think, how sad it is. By the grace of the gods of heaven and earth, Even in a dream, I pray that you might Let me know the whereabouts of the princess, But there is no sign, only the sound Of the rough wind blowing through the mountains and fields. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, I shall prepare my army once again, To take back the Idomu Castle granted by my ancestors, And to search for the whereabouts of the princess, I rack my heart in a thousand ways. As I think and think, how dreary it is. Even if Tsukimitsuyama is pure, Even if the flow of the river is clear, Without any joy, How fleeting it is to spend these Tsukihi. Though the moon shines in the sky, Though the stars twinkle like gold, My eyes are clouded, and there is only tears. Save me, Amatsukami, Before the Kunitsukami, For a rebirth in this fleeting world of mine, I pray with all my heart.'

'Having barely escaped to the mountain of moonlight, How painful it is for me to plan a Saikiyo. Where is my cousin Chinriuhime now? I am anxious, wondering if she has lost her life. When the time comes, I shall retake Idomu Castle, And make the merits of my ancestors shine. Defeated by the fierce army of Eērusu, I have come fleeing so easily. My army's formation broken by the Tekigun, Idomu Castle has been taken away. By any means, I will retake Idomu Castle, And let the Kunitsukami live in peace. The Kunitsukami must be lamenting morning and evening At the wickedness and lawlessness of Eērusu. The Kunitsukami have had their parents taken and children snatched, And their precious treasures must have been stolen too. The land of Idomu, where various fruits ripen abundantly, Has been devastated. Born as an ancestor of the Kunitsukami, How miserable it is to lament morning and evening.'

Murajihime sings.

'Has the peaceful land of Idomu, both high and low, Perished thus because of the sin of arrogance? Accustomed to the blessings of heaven and earth, day and night, This must be the retribution for forgetting those blessings. From today, let us fear the gods of heaven and earth, And serve them with deep reverence. The land of Idomu, which forgot the deep blessings of the gods, Has perished just like this. On the mountain of moonlight, we must worship the gods of heaven and earth, And open up the world. Because the land is clouded both high and low, It must have perished due to the divine censure. Without the protection of the Lord God, can the land be governed By the power of the Kunitsukami alone? Both high and low, let us realize the blessings of the gods, Purify our hearts, and strive in our duties. Shiuran, you who lead the army, take heed. Battles cannot be won by human power alone. Though we invoke the names of the Kunitsukami, we are but human, And human power has its limits. Receiving the limitless power of the gods into our bodies, There is no enemy god on the path we wish to tread. The enemy gods watch for openings and attack, Disturbing the land of Idomu.'

Shiuran sings.

'How awe-inspiring is the divine message of Murajihime. I can only tremble, thinking that I am indeed at fault. Now that it has come to this, having erred in the King's realm, I have no face to stand in this world. I shall apologize to my King for my sin of ignorance, And return the office of Gunshi. From today, I shall become an ordinary person and for the sake of the country, And for the sake of the King, I shall offer my sincerity. Thinking of how I was defeated by the Tekigun While holding a large army, I cannot show my face. I pray that you will take back the office of Gunshi, And cast me down among the ranks of ordinary men.'

Azumi-ō sings:

I wish to search for the Arika of Chinriu-hime; / Send the army as soon as Hitohi passes. / Send Mihashira of Bushi, and secretly / Seek out the Arika of the Hime. / Until the Onyukuhe of Chinriu-hime is known, / I think I shall not raise a war. / I am anxious whether she preserves her life / Together with the lady-in-waiting of Chinriu, Araragi. / Since Araragi is a wise woman, she must be protecting Chinriu-hime / And hiding somewhere. / Taking the sincerity of Araragi as my only reliance, / I pass my days and nights in solace.

Nāman, the Left Guardian, sings:

When I think of the Mikokoro of my King, I am filled with sorrow; / Lacking the strength in my own body, / And having had the castle of Idomu, where the King resides, taken away, / I do not feel as if I am alive. / Though I lament, there is no way to act, so I shall harden / My heart and plan for a second attempt. / Serving at Tsukimitsuyama, all through the night / I am drowned in tears for the sake of the Hime. / Wishing to bring the ruined country back to life again, / My heart has wandered into darkness.

Tāman, the Right Guardian, sings:

How shameful it is that while serving as the Right Guardian, / The country of Idomu has been taken away. / By any means, I must take back the Mototsu-Mikuni, / And illuminate the Mikosho of the King. / The retribution for the arrogance of the Kunitsukami has come, / And I have met with such a sorrowful fate. / Respecting and revering fire, water, and earth, / Perhaps I shall live in this world honoring the Kami. / As a retribution for making light of fire and water, / The land where I dwell has also been taken away. / If it be so, there is no way to act; I shall only / Harden my heart and attempt a second effort.

Azumi-ō sings:

From Kefu, upon the summit of Tsukimitsuyama, / I shall build a Miya-i for the Lord Ōkami. / Accustomed to the grace of the Lord Kami, until now / I have not performed the morning and evening services. / Bowing before the Kami in the morning and evening, / I shall pray for the prosperity of the country. / Call and gather the Kunitsukami, / And tell them to hasten to build the Miya-sha of the Lord Kami.

The Kami of the Left Guardian sings:

Revering the teachings of my King, from Kefu / I shall serve the Miya-sha of the Lord Ōkami.

From this time, the Kami of the Left Guardian issued orders to the nearby Kunitsukami; the Kunitsukami rejoiced greatly, and both old and young, men and women, gathered at Tsukimitsuyama. They felled the good timber from the large and small valleys, cutting the roots and trimming the tops, gathering pillars and beams, and thus it came to pass that the construction of the palace of the Lord Ōkami was finally underway.

The Kami of the Left Guardian first performed the Jichinsai and sang the song of the stone-pounding:

In the sacred ground of Tsukimitsuyama, / By the Mikoto of the King of Azumi, / The Miya-sha of the Lord Ōkami / Shall have its great palace pillars firmly set. / With the Chigi high in Takamagahara, / Here now, as we serve, / The Kunitsukami have gathered, / And with great courage, we perform / The precious ritual of grounding the earth. / Carrying the Iwakura from here and there / To this sacred ground, / Felling the great Zelkova trees / And designating them as the stone-pounding pillars, / Until the bottom of the great earth is reached, / We strike the stones with all our might. / May they remain unshaken for Chiyo and Yachiyo, / As the foundation of the country of Idomu, / And may these stones shine in the imperial reign, / From the mountain streams of Tsukimitsuyama, / Through the sincerity of the Kunitsukami.

Tsukimitsuyama shall prosper for eight thousand generations.
