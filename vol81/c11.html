Chapter 11: 五 Moon Gloom [2038]

五月闇〔2038〕

§ 1

King Ērusu of the country of Sāru, intending to occupy the land of Idomu, raised a great army, advanced upon Idomu Castle with the speed of swift wind and sudden thunder, occupied the royal castle in a single stroke, and caused King Azumi, along with Queen Muraji, as well as the Samori, the Umori, and the Gunshi, to flee together to Mount Tsukimitsu; whereupon he ordered his Naito to escort and transport the numerous enemy troops taken as prisoners to be bound in the Raugoku of the country of Sāru.

In the country of Sāru, there flows a slightly turbid stream called the Kitagaha, which cascades down from Mount Ōsaka. Because there are neither bridges nor boats here, all cross by means of the art of Suiba; across the Kitagaha, upon the eastern hill Kitayama, Ērusu erected castle walls, relying upon it as a position of Eugaikengo.

Taishi Ēmusu of King Ērusu was guarding Kitayamajiyō as the Rusushidanchiyō; when he saw the numerous enemy prisoners being sent in, he stood in the plaza within the castle, attended by his retainers Yūzuki and Asazuki, viewing the sight with delight; but recognizing among them a trio of beauties of noble, refined grace and lovely features, the unmarried Ēmusu resolved that even though they were women of an enemy realm, he would somehow make one his wife; returning thence to his mansion, he was instantly seized by the demon of love, spending night and day continuing naught but sighs of anguish and torment.

Needless to say, these three beautiful women were King Azumi's daughter Chinriuhime, the somewhat older serving-maid Araragi, and Senriu, the daughter of the wet nurse who was Chinriuhime's foster sister.

Asazuki and Yūzuki, their hearts pained by Ēmusu's extraordinary state night and day, tried every possible means desiring above all to see the Taishi's refreshing smiling face: they gathered small birds of sweet voice, made insects chirp, displayed various grain-bearing plants near the Taishi's eyes, and furthermore sang songs or danced, devoting all their heart and strength; yet because the Taishi's body only grew more emaciated day by day, one day Asazuki and Yūzuki, intending to recommend a pleasant excursion to Hanagaoka to the Taishi, stepped near his side and urged him with a song.

Asazuki sings: 'The light of the morning moon faintly whitens, Lodging dew upon the flower buds. All the hundred and thousand flowers of Hanagaoka Are drenched in the dew of the young king's love; Seeking to open the bud of the young king's heart, The morning moon sheds tears of dew.'

Ēmusu faintly heard Asazuki's song, and as if his heart were slightly moved, stepped forward two or three paces and sings: 'The light of the morning moon whitens and the vast sky Is hazy, resembling my own heart. My heart clears not morning nor evening; Wind, flowers, birds, and moon bring no delight. Even the chirping voices of a hundred birds, even The joint crying of the pine crickets bring me sorrow. My father fights in the crossroads of life and death, Yet it has no concern for me. My heart has somehow strayed from my father Who goes forth to battle, and suffers over a flower. The attire of the peaches fragrant on Hanagaoka Has also become a sad tidings unto me. Mountains and rivers are sad to me; the mansion of Mount Kita Is also lonely, as my thoughts clear not.'

Asazuki sings: 'I have faintly understood the heart of my young king; I, the morning moon, shall bring tidings of the flower.'

Ēmusu sings: 'Longing for the flower belonging to my father's enemy, I dream a painful dream. If it be so, honor, rank, and even My life of a string of pearls are not to be regretted. Loving a person I cannot have, I lament My uncontrollable life morning and evening. In the endless, vast country of Sāru, I have never seen such an auspicious flower.'

Asazuki sings: 'Is the flower desired by the young king Not a captive flower? Speak unto me.'

Ēmusu sings: 'Though I feel ashamed, I shall reveal My innermost heart; thy words have hit the mark. Because the figure of the captive woman is noble, She must surely be the daughter of Azumi. My father destroyed the land of Azumi And bought their hatred—how sorrowful it is! That flower which could have been plucked with a tranquil heart Is about to be scattered by my father's storm.'

Amesu sings.

'Gauging your shameful and sorrowful heart, I shall await your Kaherigoto as you depart.'

Thus, the master and servant exchanged songs and parted ways for a while. After Asazuki and Yufuzuki had their Tachii, Amesu, feeling as though a Hitotoki were a thousand autumns, looked down from the high hall at the murky flow of the Kitagaha below and sang his reflections quietly.

'Even if the flow of the Kitagaha is murky, how could my Magokoro be stained? The water of the Kitagaha, where both moon and sun float and flow, is sorrowful as its reflection breaks. Though my heart breaks into a hundred thousand pieces, I am like a flower that has no mouth. My father has traveled far across Mount Daiei and sown seeds of suffering. Both father and mother are far away in the land of Idumu, and I weep in loneliness for love. I am a sorrowful being with no means to pluck the flower I love, which remains beyond my reach. What has become of Asazuki, and where is Yufuzuki? The sky is clouding over. How shall I clear the clouds and mist from the sky of my heart? The rain of the fifth month falls. My sleeves are dampened by the rain of the fifth month, and the cuckoo sings without knowing it. Surely all the birds are crying out for love, just like my own heart in its solitude.

It is not like the deer on the mountain ridge longing for its mate, but the autumn leaves scattering upon my own face. Sighing deeply morning and evening, I have been longing for the love of my Inochi. Thinking that she is the daughter of the King of Azumi, who bought hatred from my father, I feel it all the more sorrowfully. Under the clouded sky of the fifth month, I can only look up at the sky and sigh. Though irises and Japanese irises bloom in the garden, I have no hope at all. Is the rain of the fifth month that falls softly not the tears that keep my sleeves from ever drying? I had not realized until this very day the sorrow of being born into such a world. Even if the water of the Kitagaha flows forever, there is no way to wash away my suffering. I was surprised to hear that the pure woman who was captured is the daughter of the King of Azumi. In any case, Asazuki and Yufuzuki shall be fragrant with the dew of Kototama, like the Asagaho and Yufugaho.'

§ 3

My soul has become darkness, captivated by the Yūgaho flower.

I have realized the sorrow of the heart that is said to be in love.

Having met the daughter of the King of Azumi, my soul was disturbed at a single glance.

I must have been captured by the demon of love.

Even if my life-thread should vanish, I cannot leave without having spoken with her for a single night.

I have forgotten my country, my castle, and my own self.

I yearn only for the Yūgaho flower.

Because it is a flower I gazed upon suddenly at twilight, I have named it my Yūgaho and yearn for it.

While pondering what the heart of Yūgaho might be, I also resent the heart of my Tarachine.

I sadly reflect upon my father's indulgence, which has pointlessly stirred waves upon the flat land.

The lady Yūgaho must not entrust her heart to the enemy who is the foe of my parents.

Just as he was thus expressing his reflections alone, the two handmaidens, Takitsuse and Yamakaze, each advanced respectfully before Ames, drawing tea and offering Kuwashi, while wondering at the state of the Hitsugi who was sunk in sorrow.

Takitsuse sings:

Takitsuse: 'Draw the clear water of the rapids and boil it; partake of the hot water, Lord Ames.'

Yamakaze sings:

Yamakaze: 'O fruit that ripened on the ridge of Mount Ōsaka; come, partake of the peach of life.'

Ames remains silent, not even attempting to touch the tea or the fruit offered by the handmaidens, but keeps his head bowed.

Takitsuse sings again:

Takitsuse: 'That the Wakagimi's countenance is not well, is it because you have an ailment in your body?'

Takitsuse: 'As I look upon the Wakagimi's appearance today, I, Takitsuse, become sad.'

Takitsuse: 'In a world where the moon and sun shine without shadow, why do you lament, Lord Hitsugi?'

Takitsuse: 'If it would comfort your Mikokoro, I would not hesitate to offer my own life to the Wakagimi.'

Takitsuse: 'Even I, Takitsuse, who serve the Wakagimi morning and evening, feel lonely today.'

Takitsuse: 'Seeing the Wakagimi's face that is not well, I feel as though I am breaking apart.'

Takitsuse: 'I wish for a single word of reply, for I have no means to do what I must.'

Yamakaze sings:

Yamakaze: 'The Wakagimi's countenance is heavily clouded; what manner of worry do you harbor?'

Yamakaze: 'I am the Yamakaze that heartlessly blows and scatters the blooming, fragrant flowers, and clears the treetops.'

Yamakaze: 'Though I know not what manner of worry you have, I, Yamakaze, shall blow it away.'

Yamakaze: 'I shall blow away even the black clouds on the ridge of the mountain of Ōsaka, as a night mountain wind.'

Yamakaze: 'To clear the clouds and mists of the Wakagimi's heart, I, Yamakaze, have exhausted my heart.'

Ames sings faintly:

Ames: 'Though I read the hearts of Takitsuse and Yamakaze, it is sad that I have no words to declare.'

Ames: 'When morning comes, I think of the morning glory; when evening comes, I think of the Yūgaho, and I am dampened in spirit.'

Ames: 'Though the water of the Kidagawa flows eternally, when will the darkness of my heart clear?'

Ames: 'Is my heart perhaps the sky where the cuckoo cries without distinguishing between morning and evening?'

Ames: 'I am not a cuckoo that cries in the May darkness where both moon and sun have hidden their light.'

Ames: 'Takitsuse, go to sleep early, and Yamakaze, leave my presence, for the night has deepened.'

Ames: 'I shall only sink into the abyss of my thoughts and spend the night listening to the sound of the dark water.'

Takitsuse sings:

Takitsuse: 'Revering the Mikoto of the Wakagimi, we shall now withdraw from your noble presence.'

Yamakaze sings:

Yamakaze: 'Though I understand the Wakagimi's sad heart, I am a person who has no means to act.'

Having sung thus, the two handmaidens return dejectedly to their own quarters.

The voice of the cuckoo crying across the deep night sky echoes incessantly from all directions, brushing past the forest of Kidayama Castle.

『滝津瀬や山風の心よみすれど 吾宣る言葉なきがかなしき 朝されば朝顔思ひ夕されば 夕顔思ひてしめらふ吾なり 木田川の水とこしへに流るれど いつか晴れなむ心の闇は ほととぎす朝夕べの分ちなく 鳴きつる空は吾心かも 月も日も光をかくせる五月闇に 鳴くほととぐす吾ならなくに 滝津瀬も早く寝よかし山風も 吾前を去れ小夜更けぬれば 吾は只思ひの淵に沈みつつ 闇の水音聞きて明さむ』

滝津瀬は歌ふ。

『若王の御言畏みいざさらば まかり退らむ貴の御前を』

山風は歌ふ。

『若王の悲しき心ははかれども せむすべもなき吾身なりけり』

斯く歌ひて二人の侍女は吾居間にすごすごと帰りゆく。

小夜更けの空に鳴き渡るほととぎすの声、四方八方よりしきりに木田山城の森をかすめて響き来る。
