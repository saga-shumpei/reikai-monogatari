Chapter 3: Moon 見の Pond [2030]

月見の池〔2030〕

§ 1

The Seijiyō of Tsukimitsuyama, by the initiation of King Azumi, hastened the construction process of one hundred days, and as the building of the beautiful Shinden was finally completed, King Azumi, together with the Samori, Umori, Gunshi, and other Tsukasatachi, gathered in the Imidono, finished the Shiubatsu of Nanukananayo, and arrived at the Dando to perform the Senzashiki of the Lord Ōkami.

On the Chiufuku of Tsukimitsuyama, a Seisen called the Tsukimi-no-ike gushed forth, reflecting the moon of the Saukū deep within its bottom. It appeared as if a Hakugin ball had been sunk into the Minasoko, and its elegance was boundless. The Shiubatsushiyugiyōshiya, including King Azumi, gathered at the Tsukimi-no-ike on the evening of the Nanukame, and while each of them poured the Seisen over their Zujiyō, they sang, purifying their Karatama.

King Azumi sings:

'My Karatama and soul have become pure, Having passed through the Shiubatsu of Nanukananayo. As I stand at the Tsukimi-no-ike of Tsukimitsuyama, The moon shines deep in the Minasoko. When I look up, it is the boat of Tsukuyomi; when I look down, The moon in the Minasoko shines like a jewel. Feeling as if I am standing between the moon and the moon, The evening after the Misogi is pure. Inviting the spirit of the Lord God to the Shinden, Tomorrow, at last, I shall serve the festival. Reflecting the blue of the endless sky, There is a moon even at the bottom of the Tsukimi-no-ike. Both moon and stars shine clearly in the Minasoko. Am I not a bird traversing the sky? Standing and gazing at the Tsukimi-no-ike, I feel as if I am traveling through the clouds. Spring has departed, and the purple azaleas and red azaleas Are fragrant at the shore of the Tsukimi-no-ike. White butterflies play among the flowers with a gentle air, Playing even at the bottom of the Tsukimi-no-ike. Beneath the shade of the evergreen pine trees, The azaleas bloom and shine red in the Minasoko. On this day when both heaven and earth are clear, I am joyful, having finished the Misogi. May the gods of heaven and earth, acknowledging my soul That has finished the Misogi, descend from heaven. Idomu Castle has been taken by the enemy, and now I Perform the Misogi at Tsukimitsuyama. Praying to the gods day and night, I temper my soul, And by strengthening my power, I shall protect the country. When I look up, the light of Tsukuyomi in the sky is clear, And the sands of the stars are twinkling. The spring breeze blowing on Tsukimitsuyama is soft, And the small birds are singing in the evening forest.'

Murajihime sings:

'Having piled up the number of my sighs, now here I see the moon reflected in the Minasoko. When I see the moon, where the Minasoko is perfectly clear, Indeed, it is well called the Tsukimi-no-ike. Sinking its shadow into the Minasoko, Tsukuyomi Shines as the guardian of the night. Receiving the protection of the day and the protection of the night, I shall protect the country at Tsukimitsuyama. Though the whereabouts of Chinriuhime are not yet known, When I see the moon, my heart softens. On this spring night, clear in the vast sky, The moon is hazy, and I think of my child. The vast sky is suddenly wrapped in mist, Blurring the light of the moon in the Minasoko. Perhaps the dew on the azaleas shines like jewels, Fragrant with the strength of the spring night moon. It is a quiet evening, and even the blowing wind Is very soft, and the mountain chickadees are singing. Though it is evening, this mountain where the mountain chickadees sing Is perhaps a manifestation of the grace of the gods. Until the number of sands in the Minasoko can be seen, The moon is clear, parting the mist. The spring mist that covered the sky in the blowing wind Suddenly clears, and the skin of the sky is visible. In this mountain that shall become the Shinden of the Lord God, I am pure, having purified my spirit. Since the moon in the vast sky is also a shadow of transience, I shall not lament this changing world. The moonlight, which sometimes wanes and sometimes waxes, Has brought my soul to life. Though I think of this world as one where light and darkness cross, I still yearn for Idomu Castle. I wish to search for the destination of my daughter, Whom I love morning and evening, in the moon. I have no way to see the moon through the trees Of Idomu Castle, which my ancestors bestowed upon me. Is the moon in the sky that I look up at The light that hung on the evergreen pine of Idomu Castle? Having come here, my heart has become pure, As the wind is fragrant morning and evening. Beneath the trees where the moon shines, the red azaleas Smile, filled with a heartless color.'

Shiuran sings:

'My King, rejoice, for in Idomu Castle The moon you gazed upon is shining. Thinking that I am looking up at the moon in the mountains of Tsukimitsuyama, Which I gazed upon in my hometown, it is dear to me. Since the moon and sun shine to the ends of every country, Why should I lament the dreams that have passed? Though I think of the present world as a dream, when I see The shadow of Tsukuyomi, I return to reality. It is joyful to think that the moonlight I look up at From over a hundred ri away is the same world. Perhaps the mermaids are rejoicing and looking up At the moon floating on the Pearl Lake. Compared to the salty lake of the mermaids, The Tsukimi-no-ike is all the more pure. My King, do not lament, for upon the earth The unchanging moon and sun are shining. By secluding yourself in such a pure mountain, The unity of ritual and government should be joyful. First, by reverently serving the gods, I shall govern This country, following the way of the moon and sun.'

月光山の中腹には月見の池と称する清泉涌出して、蒼空の月を底深く写せり。恰も白銀の玉を水底に沈めし如く見えて、その床しさ限りなし。アヅミ王以下の修祓修行者は、七日目の夕月見の池に集り来り、各自清泉を頭上より引きかぶりながら歌ふ。

§ 2

Revering the grace of the Amatsu Sun, may you govern the world with the dew of Tsukiyomi as your strength.

After seven days and seven nights of purifying the Mitama and Karatama, I feel close to the moon at the Tsukimi-no-ike.

Even the sound of the wind blowing through the treetops is serene.

The Mikokoro of the King is becoming manifest.

With a calm Murakimo, I shall wait for the time to retake the castle of Idumu.

Though the wicked officials of Eales may be strong, they shall not reach the power of the true Kami.

The Eales who have turned their blades against our King shall surely not meet a good end.

I wish to save the wicked souls of the Eales and purify them like the Tsukikage.

I, too, shall take the light of the moon as my Nori and advance with a pure Mitama and Karatama.

Even the Tokoyami shall be torn asunder by the power of light; it is a shining world, so I shall entrust it to Kami.

Appointed as a commander of my King's army, I am ashamed to think that I was so easily defeated.

I am ashamed even to look up at the face of the moon, thinking that I was defeated without protecting the sovereign of my King.

When I gaze at the moon in the Misora, feeling ashamed, it smiles upon me with a serene face.'

Samori no Naaman sings:

'Though I am one who laments, defeated in battle, I gaze upon the moon of this clear Koyohi.

When I look at the moon, my Chiji of sorrows clear away, and I feel as though I have been revived.

Though the Idumu castle is lost, I am happy to think that my King remains steadfast.

Though I know not the Yukuhe of the Himegimi, she must be alive under the protection of Kami.

The heart that thinks to Kita the wicked officials of Eales shall never clear for Toha.

I, Samori, have erred in the Matsuri of the country and have shown lamentation to the King.

My Murakimo cannot rest, standing or sitting, for having troubled the Mikokoro of my King.

Bound by the Kuwandai heart of the King, I have preserved my Inochi until this Kefu.

I thought I would die, as I had no Maushiwake to offer to my country and my King.

Yet, thinking that it is easy to die but difficult to live, I have endured.

Even as I live on to preserve my Inochi for the sake of the King and to destroy my enemies,

The heartless flowers bloom beautifully at the Migihabe, yet I am lonely.

When I see the light of the moon shining in the Ōzora, shame wells up within me for my own foolishness.

For the sake of the King, as long as this Inochi lasts, I shall clear my resentment and retake the castle.'

Azumi-ō sings:

'I know the sorrowful heart of Naaman.

Be at peace, waiting for the time.

It is not the sin of Naaman, but my own sin of having been separated from the Kami of Ametsuchi.'

Naaman sings:

'As I hear the gentle words of my King, my eyes are drenched in tears.

Praying to Kami morning and evening, I wonder when I shall clear the thoughts of my King.

The shrine of the Lord God is finally completed, and I await the auspicious day for the transfer of the spirit.'

Samori no Taaman sings:

'The words of my King are awe-inspiring; I weep at the heart of Naaman.

Entrusting my lamentations until now to the moon, I shall pray to the Great Kami to raise the kingdom.

There is nothing in the works upon the earth that is accomplished apart from the grace of the Lord God.

By worshipping the Lord God deeply, perhaps I shall receive the pure divine authority of the Kototama.

We were destroyed by the army of bows and arrows of this world, not using the army of Kototama.

Henceforth, I shall abandon the bows and arrows that wound people and fight with the living Kototama.

The purification of seven days and seven nights has ended, and my Murakimo has suddenly become clear.

Since flowers bloom naturally when spring comes, why should I be agitated in my own body this day?

Revering my King as the Lord of Glory, I shall wait for the time at Tsukikage-yama.

I, Samori, erred in the kingdom of the King and it was snatched away by the officials of crookedness.

Though my sin is heavy and deserves ten thousand deaths, I shall eventually compensate for it with the power of time.

Please wait calmly for a while; I shall see the King of Eales driven away.

Unless I Kita and defeat the officials of Eales, the sin of my own body shall not perish.

Even the Tsukikage that travels through the distant Misora has a precedent of waning and disappearing.

The world of darkness shall not last long; soon the clear moon shall shine again.

The Tsukikage grows fuller little by little, and then wanes little by little.

Though the Misora becomes dark as it wanes, it is a world where the Tsukikage shall emerge again.'

§ 3

Azumi-ō sings again.

Azumi-ō: 'By this pond where the moon is clear, I perform purification, and each of you has illuminated your heart. I have now heard your pure Kokorone closely and have been revived. It is an age where even the moon in the Ōzora hides itself. Why should I lament, when I have you as my strength? I am not Hitorimi, for I am a person who relies on you as my strength. Receiving the noble blessings of the Lord God, I think to clear my thoughts in tranquility. Rebuilding the sliding screens of my heart until now, I shall serve with awe before the Mimahe of Kami. I have realized whether my country can be governed while keeping the Lord God at a distance.'

Umori sings.

Umori: 'As I hear the awe-inspiring Kototama of my King, I think of the prosperity of the country from now on. The Mikoto of my King is righteous; can it be governed without the Isaoshi of the Lord God? The music of Ērusu must have watched for a Sukima and stolen the land of Idomu.'

Muraji-hime sings again.

Muraji-hime: 'That I grew arrogant in my heart and relied on my own power was a calamity. When Asu comes, I shall invite and worship the great Ōkami, and serve the Mimatsuri most beautifully.'
