Chapter 5: 心の禊 [2032]

心の禊〔2032〕

§ 1

Azumi-ō and the other Kunitsukamitachi, moved by the Mikotonori of Takahoko-no-Kami and Kamihoko-no-Kami, performed the purification of Nanukananayo and, intending to begin a hundred-day Shiubatsu, left the Seijiyō of Tsukimigaike and went to perform the Shiubatsushiki in the Seiriu of the Komaigaha flowing at the Sanroku. The water of the Komaigaha flowed blue and Tautau, and the Shibuki rising as it bit into the rocks in the Kahanaka shone in the Nitsukuō like mist, Sanagara as if scattering brocade of Hakugin, and its Saukuwan was enough to dazzle the eyes. Ichidō stood upon the Ōiha in the river, or sat in Tanza, and exhausting their Shinriyoku day and night, served in the Miwaza of purification.

Azumi-ō sings:

Even with the purification of Nanukananayo at Tsukimi-ike, The filth of my soul is not removed. When I reflect upon the great words of the Lord God, My body and soul are not yet purified. Hearing the Takitsuse of the Hayakawa, it is awe-inspiring, Resembling the voice of Takahoko-no-Kami, It feels as if my soul is being struck and beaten. The sound of the Takitsuse of the Komaigaha, As I stand upon the Iwane rising within the Hayakawa, Water vapor rises as I stand there, How valiant is the purification of washing my body Standing in the Hayase of the Komaigaha. Until the white sand of the riverbed is no longer visible, It is a deep flow where the water is blue. Even deeper than the bottom of the deep flow of the Komaigaha, Must be the filth of my own body. Building a castle at the Seisho of Gekkōzan, I lament my clouded heart. There should be no time for lamentation; I shall purify my soul And return the castle of Idomu.

Attracted by the treasures that have form, I fear the clouding of my soul. Yet, the Idomu castle granted by my parents, Should I let it pass into other hands so easily? In the water of the Komaigaha where moon and sun flow, I shall wash the filth of my soul. The valiant sound of the water of the Komaigaha Revives my soul. Wrapped between mountains, the Komaigaha flows, Its water clear and chillingly cold. In the mountain stream where many large and small fish gather, As I perform purification, my feet feel ticklish. The fish come and peck at my feet, Is the filth of my body still not removed?

Muraji-hime, standing in the shallows of the shore, immerses half her body and sings quietly:

How pleasant is this flow, my soul Has been washed by this sound of water. Though I wash, there is no way to completely and perfectly Remove the filth of body and soul. Your valiant figure, my King, standing in the Hayase And performing purification upon the Iwane. Seeing the Hayase of the Komaigaha, my heart Is emboldened and my body trembles. Standing for the hundred days and hundred nights of purification To fulfill the will of the Lord God, Perhaps the dew of the thickets of Momoki has gathered To become this Hayakawa. The river is wide and the water deep, The Takitsuse of the Komaigaha flows swiftly. Though the uguisu sings of spring in the treetops on the shore, My soul is cold. Garden azaleas bloom on the bank, reflecting their Red, white, and purple flowers on the riverbed. The sound of the Takitsuse echoes loudly all night long, Flowing with the moon.

When morning comes, the Amatsubi flows, and when evening comes, The water of the Komaigaha flows with the moon.

Shiuran sings:

The labor of the Nanukananayo purification was in vain, And now I perform the hundred-day purification here. Was my soul soiled ten or twenty times over, That it could not be washed even in the water of Tsukimi-ike? Bathed in the flow of the Hayakawa, would my soul Be able to serve as a commander of the army? From today, I wish to wash away my fierce heart And become a commander of the Kototama army. The voice of the kawazu singing clearly on the shore Trembles on the water surface and flows away. Oh, magnificent kawazu, speaking the Kototama, Clear without distinction between night and day. I am ashamed of my own body, which possesses Kototama inferior even to the kawazu. The kawazu must have washed its Kototama In the water of the Komaigaha, night and day. The spring, where the red flowers of peach and cherry bloom And reflect in the water, is tranquil. Where will the cherry blossoms flowing in the current of the Hayakawa Find their place of rest?

My heart is like the flowers flowing in the current, I have become unaware of the end. Purifying myself in this mountain stream where the water is cold, I shall revive my soul. It is a purification waiting for the Kami to descend Into the palace newly built on Gekkōzan. The Lord God, who once descended from heaven, Disliked the filth and returned. If there were no protection of the Kami in the world, I could not preserve my life even for a moment. The water of the Hayase, flowing through the valleys, Is chillingly cold. Even if the river water is cold, I will stand in this river For a hundred days and perform purification. Until I think the life of my Tama-no-o will vanish, The flow of the Komaigaha is chillingly cold.

§ 2

Samori of Naaman sings:

'Watching the wake of my King as I follow, the purification at Komai is chillingly cold. Though it be cold, why should I fear? For the sake of the King and the sake of the Mikuni, I feel at peace. It is not for the sake of the King or the country, but for the sake of purifying my own Mitama. If the defilement of my Mitama is completely cleansed, it shall become for the sake of the country and the King. Because my Mitama was clouded, I caused my King to hide in Tsukimitsuyama; when I think of it, what a fearful person I am. Because I troubled the King and lost the country, and forgot the Daidau of Saiseiitsuchi, the land of Idumu has been overturned. If one wishes to perform Matsuri, both the Karatama and the Mitama must be purified. In the Kunihara which the Lord God has brought forth, how can one maintain Inochi without purification? Thinking that the Inochi of the cord of jewels is a Tamamono of God, I devote myself to the work of purification.

If one wishes to perform Matsuri, one must first devote oneself to the purification of exorcism. Forgetting the grace of the Lord God, I mistakenly thought I could govern the country by my own power. Holding such a mistaken heart, how can Matsuri be performed and peace be attained? Matsuri is, first and foremost, to worship God. The Mikuni of God is according to the Mama of God. When the Hiyakunichi of purification is finished, I shall reform my Murakimo heart and serve the Ōji. Wielding the sword of Kototama in my right hand, I shall assist in the Matsuri of the King. The rapids of the water flowing Tautau, the Komai River knows not its Yukuhe. Did the dew of the Kigi leaves falling from the peak of Tsukimitsuyama gather to become a river? If the power of the dew of Hitori also accumulates, it shall eventually become a river of sincerity.'

Taaman sings:

'In the valley where the Harugasumi begins to Tanabi, I perform purification with reverence. The sound of the water flowing as it bites the rocks is loud, and I sing the living Kototama in the Toki. Even the sound of the rushing water striking the rocks weighs upon my heart regarding the future of the country. Thinking of the King and thinking of the country, I make my pilgrimage to the mountain of Tsukimitsuyama morning and evening. When I think of carrying my defiled Karatama before the Lord God, I am fearful. Even if there be an age when the mountains split and the seas dry up, I shall not step off the path of sincerity. When I immerse myself in the water of the rushing river, my Mitama feels purified of its own accord. Although I walk the path of sincerity of the Lord God, the work of purification was the beginning. One must sweep away the defilement of the clouds and mist of heaven and earth, if there be merit in the path of purification.'

While the gods and others were thus intent on their purification, a man came flowing down, his whole body stained crimson, screaming desperately for someone to save his Inochi from upstream, becoming a body half-dead and half-alive, while striking his head against the rocks here and there. Azumi-ō, who had been intent on his purification, looked sharply and saw, to his surprise, that it was the miserable figure of Eelsu-ō, whom he had considered his enemy day by day. Azumi-ō, forgetting the danger to his own body, leaped into the torrent, rescued the half-dead Eelsu-ō, carried him to the side, brought him to a place downstream where the rapids were slightly weaker, lifted him onto a sandbank of the river, and performed various kinds of care, such as making him vomit water. Shiuran, and beginning with Naaman, Taaman, and Muraji-hime, when they saw who it was and approached by crossing the rapids, they realized it was Eelsu who had attacked and captured their main castle. Thinking that this was the time to clear their grudge and take back the castle, they gathered around, becoming agitated and grabbing rough stones to beat him to death.

Muraji-hime sings in a loud voice:

'How pleasant is the death of the official of Eelsu, who has committed enmity against our country.'

Naaman sings:

'Since he is an enemy who troubled our King, it must be that he has met with the punishment of God. Now is the time for heaven's gift; let us strike off his head and take back the castle of Idumu.'

Taaman sings:

'As a reward for performing purification in the Arakawa, the enemy has fallen into my hands. How interesting it would be to pick up stones from the riverbed and strike and kill this enemy.'

Azumi-ō raised his right hand, acting as if he were pressing down on the air, and said:

'Wait a moment; Eelsu-ō is also a noble child of the Lord God; just forgive him. Since it is a sin that my Mitama has departed from God, there is no need to resent Eelsu-ō.'

Eelsu-ō regained his senses slightly, and while looking around in all directions, he saw Azumi-ō standing before him and caring for him, and he laughed and sang in a loud voice:

§ 3

Murajihime: 'Why did you save my life? Do you intend to hinder my Aragiyō? I am the King of Eales, and it is shameful for me to stand with a face saved by the likes of you, who are weak in the loins.'

Murajihime raises her eyes and sings:

'Are you truly my weak-hearted King? Do you forgive the enemy who seized the Idomu Castle? Having had his life saved, he turns against you and spews insults; do not forgive him.'

Azumi-ō sings:

'Though he is an enemy whom I have long thought to be evil, seeing him suffer makes me want to help him. In any case, to take advantage of an enemy's suffering and seek revenge is something to be ashamed of. I shall stand openly and fight, but we have no need for bows and arrows. By brandishing the Ikukototama of the Lord God, I intend to Kotomu and pacify the enemy.'

Naman sings:

'Though I think the words of my King are righteous, should we truly forgive such a wicked enemy? How can we forgive this enemy who, having had his precious life saved, spews forth insults?'

Taman sings:

'Is it not you whose precious life is in danger because of the wickedness of the Lord God? You who, having been saved, spew forth rough words, are a true Magatsukami. Come then, let us strike him with stones until the precious life of Eales is extinguished.'

At this, although Azumi-ō repeatedly gave Genmei to save the life of the King of Eales, the other Tsukasatachi, whose resentment had reached their Kotsuzui, picked up stones from Shihau and Hatsupau to throw at him, and miraculously, the figure of Eales turned into Mizukemuri and vanished into the Suichiu.

Azumi-ō and the rest of the Itsukau of the Misogi, seeing this state, could not bear the feeling of Fushigi and stood Bauzen, Mitsu the water, when a Kauriu, thought to be seven or eight Shichihachijiyō around its body, opened its great mouth and stuck out its red tongue, raising its neck above the heads of the Menmen and showing its intention to swallow them in one gulp.

At this, Azumi-ō remained composed and did not panic in the slightest, and while calmly watching the four who were flustered, he sang:

'One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, hundred, thousand, ten thousand.'

As he sang, the figure of the Kauriu gradually grew thinner, and finally, it became a small newt and crawled to the feet of Azumi-ō. Azumi-ō placed the newt on his palm and again recited the number song of heaven, whereupon smoke rose from his palm with a hissing sound, soaring into the sky before his eyes, and from within the smoke, a dragon appeared with a giant body superior to the one seen before. From nowhere, there was the voice of Kami, echoing like thunder.

'The Lord Ōkami has accepted the soul of the beautiful King of Azumi. Since your heart has been purified, your hundred-day Misogi is complete; return now. I am the Kamihoko-no-Kami who descended from the Takahi-no-Miya; be at peace in your heart.'

Azumi-ō sings respectfully:

'I am grateful; I have been revived by the words of Kami who watches over my soul.'

Again, there was the voice of Kami from the air:

'I shall keep the divine spirit of Kamihoko in the divine garden on the ridge of the Takamitsu mountain. While serving in the divine palace of Kami, King of Azumi, establish the foundation of the Idomu country. The heart of Murajihime is still impure; the merit of her hundred-day Misogi has vanished. The Misogi of the three pillars, Shiuran, Naman, and Taman, has become foam on the water. You must serve a hundred-day Misogi anew, for Gekkōzan is a sacred place.'

Thereupon, Azumi-ō was forgiven after a three-day Misogi and came to serve at the shrine of Gekkōzan and oversee the government of the country, while Murajihime and the others were ordered to perform the Aragiyō of one hundred days and one hundred nights anew, and were permitted to serve at the shrine of Gekkōzan and in government affairs.

茲にアヅミ王はエールス王の生命を救へよと頻りに厳命すれども、怨み骨髄に徹したる他の司等は、この機会に打殺さむと四方八方より石を拾つて投げつけければ、不思議やエールスの姿は水煙となりて水中に消えにける。アヅミ王を始め一行禊の面々は此の体を見て不思議の念に堪へやらず、茫然として水中を見詰めけるが、胴の廻り七八丈もあらむかと思はるる蛟竜、大口を開き紅き舌を吐き出しながら、一行の頭上に鎌首を立て、一呑みにせむず勢を示しける。

茲にアヅミ王は従容として少しも騒がず、四人の狼狽せる姿を静かに眺めながら、

『一二三四五六七八九十百千万』

と歌ひ行くにつれ、蛟竜の姿は次第々々に細り行きて、終には小さき蠑螈となり、アヅミ王の足許に這ひ寄り来る。アヅミ王は蠑螈を掌に載せ、再び天の数歌を宣りければ、掌よりシユーシユーと煙立ち昇り、見る見る天に冲し、煙の中より仄かに見ゆる竜の姿以前に優る巨体なりける。何処ともなく神の声あり、雷の如く響き来る。

『美しきアヅミの王の魂を 主の大神は諾ひ給へり 汝が心清まりぬれば百日の 禊は済みぬはや帰りませよ 吾こそは高日の宮より天降りたる 神鉾神ぞ心安かれ』

アヅミ王は恭しく歌ふ。

『有難し吾魂をみそなはす 神の言葉に蘇りたり』

空中より再び神の声あり。

『高光の山の尾の上の神苑に 神鉾の神御霊とどめむ アヅミ王は神の御殿に仕へつつ イドムの国の基を定めよ ムラジ姫の心は未だ汚れたり 百日の禊の功は消えたり シウランやナーマン、ターマン三柱の 禊は水の泡となりけり 改めて百日の禊に仕ふべし 月光山は聖所なりせば』

茲にアヅミ王は三日の禊にて許され、月光山の神殿に奉仕し、国政を見る事となり、ムラジ姫以下は改めて百日百夜の荒行を命ぜられ、月光山の神殿及び政務に仕ふることを許されにける。
