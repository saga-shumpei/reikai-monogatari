Chapter 8: 人 Fish の Victory [2035]

人魚の勝利〔2035〕

§ 1

On the mountainside of the southern face of Ōsakayama, there lies a wide plain, where a tide of water twenty ri from east to west and ten ri from north to south drifts, and it is called the Pearl Lake.

Around this lake, many Ningiyo dwell, living a life almost identical to that of the Kunitsukami, speaking well, and forming a Ningiyo-kyo; they call the one in the south Nankiyō, the one in the north Hokukiyō, the one in the east Tōkiyō, and the one in the west Seikiyō, and the groups of Ningiyo make this lake their Toha sumika, feeding on Giyokahi, preventing the Shinnifu of Kunitsukami from elsewhere, and enjoying the Ontaku of Ametsuchi.

Even in such a Heiwa Shinsen-kyo, the subordinates of King Idomu would occasionally come to attack and capture Ningiyo maidens, not just once or twice, so the King of the Ningiyo gathered the heads to deliberate, and to prepare for the Shinnifu of the Kunitsukami, they planted shells with sharp, pointed ends all over the open ground without leaving any space, setting up a defensive line to injure the feet of the attacking enemies.

At one time, the King of the Ningiyo gathered at the Pearl Island that protrudes into the center of this lake, and while gazing at the moon floating on the surface of the lake, they spent the night singing to one another.

Haruno, the chieftain of Tōkiyō, sings:

Born of the grace of the gods of Ametsuchi, This lake is an eternal garden. Floating the moon of the vast sky, the waves are calm, How wide is the shining lake. Like this, we have spent years Becoming Ningiyo in this clear lake. Though the wind blows and the rain falls, this lake Is so calm that not even the crests of waves rise. In this lake where the scenery of spring and summer is wondrous, The brocade of Ōsakayama is reflected. The clear water of the Ōsakayama valley does not enter this lake Even a little, but falls and surges. Does this lake connect to the Ryūgū sea? Though it is a lake, it is a tide of water. This wonder, that the tide wells up in such a high place, The lake must be a gift from the gods. Resting in this lake for a hundred years, How carefree is our life. From time to time, into this lake, which is a Shinsen-kyo, The Kunitsukami come to attack. Even if the Kunitsukami should come in tens of thousands, We shall fight to the end. Amatsu-hi shines all day, and the moon-boat Illuminates all night, O Pearl Lake.

Natsukusa, the chieftain of Nankiyō, sings:

Though we have human faces, our bodies are still Pitiful, covered in scales. Yet, held in the grace of the gods, It is joyful to live without suffering. Raised peacefully in the Pearl Lake, We have known no worries. Though the subordinates of the fearsome King Idomu Capture our kin and return, We rack our hearts morning and evening Thinking of how to prevent our enemies. Yet, because this lake is deep, We shall dive to the bottom and escape. While we sleep on land from time to time, We are captured by the enemies who sneak in. From tomorrow, the Ningiyo shall not sleep on the shore, But should rest floating in the lake. To float leisurely on the waves and eat Fish is a blessing. Only those who forget the grace of Ametsuchi Shall have their lives taken and suffer. As for us, the gift of the Lord Ōkami, The gods will surely protect us. I heard that the Idomu castle was destroyed by the appearance Of the King of Sāru, and our enemies Are fighting over our pearls, And sneaking into this lake. I feel uneasy hearing that King Eirus of the Sāru country Is a fierce god. Be that as it may, let us spend the night Gazing at the moonlight clear upon the waves. When I look at the moon, my sorrow fades, And I think of the flowers blooming in the spring fields. It is sad to think of our kin Who were captured when they went ashore to see the flowers. When spring comes, our friends, one after another, Have been captured due to our lack of vigilance.

Akizuki, the chieftain of Seikiyō, sings:

In the center where the sky is blue and the lake is also blue, We drink sake on the floating Pearl Island. Illuminated by the light of the moon traveling through the sky, This lake plain shines pure white. It is we who appear and disappear between the waves, Looking up at the moon in the sky. How great is the light of the moon That shines through even to the bottom of the lake eight fathoms deep. On dark nights, our kin gather on the shore, And spend the night in song and dance. The voices of the Ningiyo singing between the waves Echoed, and the night dawned. While looking up at the autumn leaves of the Ōsakayama, Autumn soaking in the lake is enjoyable. Akizuki shines and reflects on Ōsakayama, The Pearl Lake plain reflecting in brocade. While dyeing the color of the waves in vermilion, the autumn leaves Of the Ōsakayama mountain shine across.

或時人魚の王は、此の湖水の中央に突出せる真珠島に集りて、湖面に浮ぶ月を眺めながら一夜を明しつつ互に歌ふ。

§ 2

Fuyukaze, the Shiuchiyō of Hokukiyō, sings:

'Though it is winter, this Senkiyō is warm.

Ōsakayama towers to the north.

Because the mountain of Ōsaka is steep, few Kunitsukami come to this Senkiyō.

In the northern village where I dwell, no enemies come to take the Ningiyo, and we pass the time in peace.

If, by any chance, the enemy should come, gather all the Ningiyo into our village.'

Just as he was singing this, Jiyōu Sakkusu-hime of Idumu Castle, leading hundreds of Naito, came climbing up the steep slope of Ōsakayama with war cries, intending to surround and capture the flock of Ningiyo in one fell swoop.

At this sound, the four Shiuchiyō exclaimed, 'This is an Ichidaiji! We must gather all the Ningiyo in the northern village,' and they sent the swift-swimming Ningiyo to the three villages of Tōzainan to report the emergency; thus, tens of thousands of Ningiyo, fearing to be left behind, dived into the deep Minasoko and, without a single one remaining, gathered in Hokukiyō, all holding their breath and watching the enemy's Shifurai from afar.

The four Shiuchiyō stood on the Gantō of Shinjiyutau, watching the enemy's Shifurai with Iuzen composure.

Under the command of Jiyōu Sakkusu, the hundreds of Naito took positions in the three villages of Tōzainan, surrounded the lake, and while beating drums and gongs with a force that seemed enough to shatter the mountains, they attacked; however, disappointed to find not even a shadow of a Ningiyo on the surface of the lake, each of them jumped into the lake while on horseback and searched with the help of their horses, but not a single Ningiyo could be found near the three villages of Tōzainan, and eventually, as the horses grew tired and many drowned in the lake, even the formidable Jiyōu Sakkusu retreated to the shore, rested her weary horses, and she herself sat with a pale face, breathing heavily.

Jiyōu Sakkusu sings in a faint voice:

'Coming to the moonlit lake of pearls, I see only waves and no shadow of Ningiyo.

I wonder what has become of the Ningiyo who dwell in this lake forever.

Most of my Naito who jumped into the salt water and advanced have drowned and died.

The Ningiyo must be lurking deep in the Minasoko, pulling at the legs of my horses.

If this is the case, horses are of no use; I shall cut down trees and build dugout canoes to advance.'

Thereupon, the surviving Naito cut down many large trees standing by the lakeside to build dugout canoes, and after seven days and seven nights of diligent effort, they finally built several boats; intending to cross to Shinjiyutau, capture the Shiuchiyō, and force them to confess the whereabouts of the Ningiyo, dozens of Naito began to advance toward the somewhat wide Shinjiyutau while poling and rowing their dugout canoes; naturally, Jiyōu Sakkusu also sat comfortably in one of the boats.

The four Shiuchiyō watched the approaching boats from afar, looking on with composure, neither disturbed nor hurried.

Fuyukaze, the Shiuchiyō of Hokukiyō, having consulted with the other three, suddenly jumped into the lake, dived to the Minasoko, hurried back to Hokukiyō, informed the tens of thousands of Ningiyo of the emergency, and instructed them to unite their desperate strength against the enemy and annihilate them.

At the words of the Shiuchiyō, the tens of thousands of Ningiyo gained momentum, feeling that this was the time to avenge their long-standing grievances and cut off the root of their misfortune, and they waited with bated breath.

Jiyōu Sakkusu, with a triumphant look on her face, while having her dugout canoe rowed, looks out over the moonlit lake of pearls and sings:

'Ah, how pleasant it is; I am now about to occupy Shinjiyutau.

The island, where the Ningiyo have gathered their treasure of pearls, shines even in the night.

The light of tens of thousands of pearls seems to have made even the moonlight fade.

Where have the tens of thousands of Ningiyo fled?

They seem to have been startled by our might.

It is delightful to set out to take Shinjiyutau on this lake where the moon floats.'

Haruno watches this scene from afar and sings:

'Watching their departure, ignorant of the fragility of their lives, I am moved to pity.

It is pathetic to think that they throw away their lives, their eyes blinded by the greed for pearls.

They seem not to fear the power of the Ningiyo, who wait and wait in Hokukiyō, ready for battle.

In the silent, hushed lake, a rain of blood will soon fall.

Perhaps this is a pleasant evening; the time has come to avenge the grievances of our ancestors.

Sakkusu is a villain who seized the land of Idumu and took the life of her husband.

The demon Sakkusu is still not satisfied and seeks to steal our treasures.

How shallow it is to throw away the thread of life, lured by endless greed.'

Natsugusa no shigemi o wakete noborikuru / Naito wa shide no tabi o suru kamo / Wagahai kage naki o mite naito-ra wa / Hayakoma morotomo ni kochiu ni kakeirinu / Hayakoma wa tsukarehateken chikara naku / Hito morotomo ni oboreshi shitari / Tsugitsugi ni oboruru o mite Sakkusu wa / Riku ni mukatte nigeyuku okashisa / Hayakoma no inanaki o shirite Sakkusu wa / Migiwa ni namiki o kiritaoshitari / Nanukananayo maruki no fune o tsukuri oete / Watarikuru kamo inochi shirazu ni / Chikayoraba shinjiyu no iwa o nagetsukete / Ada kotogotoku uchikorosu beshi

Akizuki, the Shiuchiyō of Seikiyō, sings:

Omoshiroki yo to wa narikeri inagara ni / Ada o horobosu koyohi to omoeba / Suichiu ni chikara o tamotsu wagahai / Toraemu to suru orokusa o omou / Oroka naru Sakkusu-ō no teshitara o / Mizu no mokuzu to hōmurisaran / Omoshiroshi aa isamashishi waga teki wa / Shinjiyu no shimane chikaku yosetari

Just as they sang this, as the party of several dozen people of Queen Sakkusu attempted to approach the island, the three Shiuchiyō, taking this as their final stand, held the Shinjiyu rocks high above their heads and hurled them down as a Gansekioto toward the approaching enemy; how could they possibly withstand it, for their boats and all were sunk into the Kochiu without a trace, vanishing like bubbles in the lake.

The tens of thousands of merfolk gathered in Hokugō raised a cheer in unison, crying 'Wō wō,' and the very heavens and earth seemed to tremble because of it.

Queen Sakkusu, the ringleader of the atrocities who had occupied the castle of Idomu, murdered King Eerusu, flourished as the victor in love, and reached the heights of luxury, finally saw her heavenly fortune exhausted and became mere seaweed in the water; how fearful is the mandate of Heaven.

From this time on, those who would attack the schools of merfolk in the lake of Shinjiyu ceased to appear, and the schools of merfolk flourished as an eternal land of immortals.
