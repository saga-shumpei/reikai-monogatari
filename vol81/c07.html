Chapter 7: Moon Sound し [2034]

月音し〔2034〕

§ 1

The land of Idomu, famed as a paradise on earth, has seen autumn pass, and the mountains and fields in all directions are woven like brocade. The wind that blows is refreshing, and the sound of insects chirping is clear, evoking the spirit of Amatsumikuni. The Momokigi trees of Ōsakayama are adorned in robes of brocade, standing tall against the Amatsumisora.

The Minanogaha River, formed by the gathering of clear streams from the Momodanichidani of this beautiful Ōsaka, flows wide and blue. On the banks of the bottomless Fukabuchi, the towering rock faces are like a masterpiece carved by the axe of Kami. Above this, the beauty of the glowing Momiji is scattered with the green of pines, while small birds chirp and insects cry.

As evening falls, the moon dwells there, in the only Zetsushiyōchi of Idomu. The Adebito enjoying themselves here were none other than King Eales, who had newly become the Jiyōshiyu of Idomu, Princess Sakkusu, and Chikuta, with no other Tomobito present. Gazing endlessly at the Tsukikage reflected in the Fuchise, they drank sake together, exhausting their Kuwanki.

King Eales, in a tipsy mood, sings while gazing at the Tsukikage reflected on the water's surface.

King Eales: 'Drinking sake together every evening, the evening moon / The river water flows as I sober up / People say mermaids dwell in this deep pool / It is believable, for it is a Fukabuchi where the bottom cannot be seen / When evening comes, the Momiji glowing in crimson / Casts its dark shadow upon the water / Looking at the shadow of the rocks depicted in the moonlight / The pool and the Momiji are of the same color / I feel as if I am living in a beautiful heavenly pure land / Rejoicing in the blessings of heaven and earth / The taste of the sake I pour is especially fragrant / Gazing at the water's surface where the moon flows / The water flowing in bubbles is white / In the light of the moon where true jewels shimmer / Though the night deepens and the sound of insects grows faint / My heart does not wish to return to the mansion / Drink, drink, make merry, make merry, for the world / Is a world where light and darkness come and go / In the clarity of the moon's shadow reflected in the water / I have come to love this Minanogaha / The voices of the kajika frogs living on the shore / Of the Minanogaha falling from the mountains of Ōsaka / The Minanogaha, clear as it flows, reflecting the starlight / Its sands are white, illuminated by the moon.'

Princess Sakkusu sings.

Princess Sakkusu: 'Serving in the retinue of my King by the Minanogaha / I have seen the moon of the midnight flowing / Spring is good, and summer is good, but the autumn moon / Flowing is especially clear / The Tsukikage flowing along the rapids of the Minanogaha / Is interesting as it shatters into a thousand pieces / Though it is a quiet moon, the waves of the rapids / Shatter in the valley, what a reflection it is / The Tsukikage shimmering on the waves to the left and right / Is the proof of the various things of the world.'

Chikuta sings while holding up a sake cup.

Chikuta: 'Following the King, I sit on the towering rocks / And drink sake while viewing the moon / Insects chirp and chirp, the kajika frogs sing / The moon dances among the waves / The mountains are Ōsaka, the mermaids are pearls / The Minanogaha where the moon flows / Viewing the autumn moon above and below / Drinking sake on a night when the Momiji glows / The pines and Momiji cast dark shadows / Depicting the surface of the river / The moon clears over the treetops of the pines / My face is flushed red with sake / Though the moon shines brightly in the sky / My heart is clouded by love / To blow away the black clouds of love / I drink moon-viewing sake on the towering rock roots / Blow, river wind, sing, kajika frogs / There is a man drinking sake under the moon / The King is brave, crossing the high mountains / And dwells as the master of Idomu / On the shore of the river where the clear moonlight flows / I drink sake while the insects chirp.'

King Eales, in high spirits, lured by Chikuta's song, has his feet caught by the sake, and while staggering, places one hand on a pine tree and sings with a tongue that cannot articulate well.

King Eales: 'How pleasant is the castle of Idomu / A place to dwell among flowers and Momiji / Flowers bloom in thousands, but the fruit that forms is one / Be at ease, my wife / I am drunk, drunk on a shō of sake / I cannot even see the flow of the river / Though the moon shines, from where I see it / Everything is pitch black, true darkness / To the point where I cannot tell west from east / I am drunk and suffering, this moon-viewing sake / Drinking delicious sake as much as the dew of the moon / Suffering in my drunkenness by the side of the river / The night deepens and the sound of insects is faint / I want to return to the mansion quickly.'

Princess Sakkusu sings.

Princess Sakkusu: 'I cannot hear the King's words / This place is also the country you rule / The mansion is not the only home / The King longing for the mansion is amusing / Listening to the sound of the river rapids / Let us wait until tomorrow morning.'

サツクス姫は歌ふ。

『わが王の御供に仕へて水乃川 流るる夜半の月を見しかな 春もよし夏もよけれど秋月の 流るるさまは一入さやけし 水乃川瀬筋流るる月影は 千々に砕けて面白きかな 静かなる月にはあれど瀬の波の 谷間に砕けてうつろふかげかな 右左波にさゆるる月光は 世のさまざまのあかしなりけり』

チクターは盃を捧げながら歌ふ。

『王に従ひ壁立つ巌に 坐して月見の酒を酌む 虫は啼く啼く河鹿はうたふ 月は波間に舞踏する 山は大栄人魚は真珠 月の流るる水乃川 上と下とに秋月眺め 紅葉照る夜に酒を酌む 松も紅葉も影黒々と 川の面を描いてる 松の梢に月澄み渡り 酒に染まりし顔紅葉 月は皎々御空に澄めど 恋に曇りしわが心 恋の黒雲吹き払はむと 壁立つ巌根に月見酒 吹けよ川風うたへよ河鹿 月に酒酌む男あり 王は勇まし高山越えて イドムの主と住み給ふ

澄める月光流るる川の 岸に酒酌みや虫が啼く』

エールス王は機嫌斜ならず、チクターの歌に釣り出され、酒に足をとられ、よろよろしながら常磐樹の松に片手を掛け、ロレツも廻らぬ舌もて歌ふ。

『心地よきかなイドムの城は 花と紅葉のすみどころ 花は千咲く成る実は一つ 心もむなよわが妻よ 酒に酔た酔た一升の酒に 川の流れも目に入らぬ 月は照れどもわれより見れば 辺り真暗真の闇 西も東も分らぬまでに 酔ふて苦しき月見酒 月の露ほど美味酒飲んで 酔ふて苦しむ川の側 小夜更けて虫の啼く音も細々と 早く館へ帰りたい』

サツクス姫は歌ふ。

『王の言葉は聞えませぬよ 此処もあなたの治らす国 館ばかりが家ではないに 館こがるる王をかし 川の瀬音に耳すませつつ 明日の朝まで待ちませう』

Chikuta sings:

‘Without knowing what is before or behind, / Does the King get drunk? How amusing. / Princess, your daily takumi, now in this bashiyo, / Try it for the sake of love. / Though I know it is an evil thing, / For the sake of love, there is no zehi.’

King Eales, not dreaming that his wife, Princess Sax and his samori Chikuta were in a deep kohinaka, was lured out by the riyōnin, drank mushiyōyatara, and as he became unable to distinguish zengo, Chikuta gave a mekuba to Sax; the Princess, possessed by the akuma of love, thinking the time had arrived, stood in the haigo of King Eales, and putting all her zenshin strength into it, pushed him down with a great shove; how could he endure it, nandeu? King Eales was pushed down from the kabeta cliff with a splash, and he vanished, becoming minawa.

Princess Sax, wearing a disgusting smile, while gazing at the minomo, sings:

‘It feels as if the ametsuchi has opened all at once. / My adakumo has become minawa. / The person whom I revered and served as my King / Has truly become minawa. / When I look up at the tsukikage shining in the ōzora, / Why do I feel such fear? / Yet the moon shall not speak, and the kahamizu / Shall not convey the state of this koyohi. / The sound of insects and the voice of the kajika, somehow, / Have made me feel lonely. / Yet, thinking that from kefu I shall live intimately / With you, Lord Chikuta, I feel happy.’

Chikuta sings:

‘To see you smile after dropping your husband, / Who is greater than the fearsome Princess, into the river, / Makes me suddenly afraid, thinking / If I, too, am to be a second King Eales. / What shall I do? Now that it has come to this, should I let the world know / That my King’s whereabouts are unknown? / Or should I convey to the world for a while / That he is laid low by illness?’

Princess Sax sings:

‘Do not declare such weak-hearted things. Was it not you / Who turned my King into minawa? / Though you did not lay your hands on him directly, your heart / Killed him by borrowing my hands. / Banish the heart that thinks / It is fearful before the eyes of the gods of the ametsuchi.’

Chikuta sings:

‘We shall let the world know that my King, drunk on sake, / Fell into the abyss of the Mizuno River. / If we do this, no suspicion will fall upon us; / To hide it is, on the contrary, to reveal it. / Come then, let us hurry back to the castle / And report the drowning of the King.’

Princess Sax sings:

‘Our takumi has hit the mark perfectly, / And poor Eales has become river-scum. / Come then, let us hurry back to Idomu Castle; / Fear a long stay, even if there are no human eyes.’

Thus, the riyōnin returned to Idomu Castle with faces as if nothing had happened, feigned being overcome by drink, gathered the vassals in one room, and sang to convey the news of King Eales’s death.

Princess Sax:

‘While watching the moon flowing in the Mizuno River, / While drinking sake with my husband, / My husband became drunk on the moon-viewing sake, / Staggered, and fell into the deep pool. / Chikuta became stark naked and jumped into the abyss / To search, but could not see his shadow. / Fearing that if I delayed, he would perish, I too / Jumped into the water to search for the King. / Though the moon shines in the ōzora, it is night, / And there is no way to see the King’s shadow. / Fearing he would perish while I let you know, / We two searched at the risk of our lives. / Lamenting the fate of my King, with tears, / I hurried back to the mansion. / You must enter the Mizuno River, / Dive to the bottom, and search. / Even the peaceful Idomu Castle, like being wrapped / In black clouds, makes me sad.’

Chikuta sings:

‘As the Princess commanded, I dove into the river rapids / And searched, but there was no shadow. / The whereabouts of my King, who was more precious than life, / Have alas become invisible. / I have racked my heart wondering how / To console the heart of Princess Sax. / Thinking of the Princess’s grief, I too / Have felt that my life is unnecessary. / You officials must distribute many people to the upper and lower rapids / Of the Mizuno River and have them search.’

Although it was the middle of the night, the military strategist Eaman hurried to the castle, looked at the state of the two, tilted his head, and remained silent. The various officials, wishing to find the corpse of King Eales, beat gongs and drums, gathered a crowd, and as a result of searching the water thoroughly, discovered the King’s corpse in the abyss at the foot of the kabeta cliff, and held a grand burial ceremony as was the custom.

From this time on, Princess Sax reigned as Queen, Chikuta continued to serve as samori, and there was not one person who knew the secret of the hearts of the riyōnin.

『前も後も分らぬまでに 王は酔はすか面白や 姫様よ日頃の謀計今此の場所で やつて見なされ恋の為 悪い事とは知つては居れど 恋の為には是非もない』

エールス王は、妻のサツクス姫と左守のチクターとが深き恋仲となつてゐる事は夢にも知らず、両人におびき出され、無性矢鱈に酒を飲み、前後も分らずなれるを見澄まし、チクターはサツクスに目配せするや、恋の悪魔にとらはれしサツクス姫は、時こそ到れりと、エールス王の背後に立ち廻り、全身の力を籠めてウンとばかり突き落せば、何条以て堪るべき、エールス王は壁立つ崖よりザンブとばかり突き落され、水泡となりて消えにけり。

かくて両人は、何喰はぬ顔にてイドム城に帰り、酒に酔ひつぶれたる風を装ひ、群臣を一間に集めて、エールス王の訃音を伝へむと歌ふ。

夜中の事ながら、軍師のエーマンは急ぎ登城し、二人の様子を見て頭を傾け、無言のまま黙し居たりける。諸々の司等はエールス王の死体を求めむと、鉦や太鼓を打鳴らし、群集を集め水中隈なく捜索の結果、壁立つ巌根の深淵に、王の死体を発見し、型の如く盛大なる土葬式を行ひける。

これより、サツクス姫は女王として君臨し、チクターは依然として左守を勤め、両人が心の秘密は一人として知るもの無かりける。
