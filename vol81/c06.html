Chapter 6: Moon 見の宴 [2033]

月見の宴〔2033〕

§ 1

The castle of Idomu is a place of Fūkuōzetsuka, and the Minanogaha river flowing in the Tōhoku gathers the streams from the Tanidani of Ōsakayama, its Kahahaba being wide and its waters Sōsō.

King Eales of the country of Saru crossed over Ōsakayama, led a great Taihei, and suddenly occupied the castle of Idomu, dwelling there with many Jiushin; compared to the Fūkuō of the country of Saru on the northern face of Ōsakayama, it was more comfortable to live in, and feeling as if he were living in a Hanazono throughout the Shiyunkashiutō, he enjoyed a life of Tengoku on Chijiyō.

On an evening when the moon shone Kaukau in the Aozora and the sound of insects was clear, he pushed open the windows of the Ōtono facing the Minanogaha, and while Mioro the surface of the river, he held a moon-viewing banquet with his Gunshi, Samori, Umori, and other high-ranking retainers, and while praising the moon floating on the surface of the Minanogaha, he was lost in ecstasy, satiated with fine wine and food.

King Eales sings while looking at the night flow of the Minanogaha.

I have departed from the capital of the northern country of Saru, and I drink wine in the castle of Idomu. Spring is good, summer is also good, and autumn is also good; the country of Idomu is a Tengoku on Chijiyō. I have chased away and scattered Azumi, the lord of Idomu castle, and have revealed my martial valor to the world. My martial valor has reached the island of Isako, and even the trees and grasses of the four quarters have bowed to me. What weighs upon my heart, like the liver of a village, is King Azumi who hides in the mountains of Gekkō. Wait a while, for I shall prepare a hundred armies and destroy the fortress of the Gekkō mountains. I shall capture the mermaids of the Shinju-ko, which makes pearls into tears, and console myself. When I look at the light of the moon and sun flowing in the Minanogaha, it resembles the pearls themselves. The mountains and fields are green and clear, with the voices of birds and the sound of insects, in this castle of Idomu. The most enjoyable things in the world are the expansion of the country and the victory in the path of war. I now rule and protect the island of Isako, and have become the king of the Kunitsukami.

Princess Sax sings.

The schemes of my King have all gone according to plan, and the castle of Idomu is in our hands. The majesty of the King who has become the master of this country, where the views of spring and summer are exquisite. The shadows of the moon and sun float quietly on the Minanogaha, awakening the dreams of King Azumi. The wide river is visible from this high palace, where the manner of the fish swimming is clear. It would be interesting to capture the mermaids of the Shinju-ko and release them into this river. Even so, King Azumi must surely be watching the castle of Idomu. If we do not destroy the fortress of King Azumi and pursue him from the Gekkō mountains, my heart will not be at ease. If you wish to sleep peacefully with your pillow raised high when night falls, then destroy King Azumi. Black clouds wander over the fortress of the Gekkō mountains in the rich country of Idomu. The black clouds are the Gekkō mountains in the country of Idomu, where both moon and sun shine brightly.

Samori Chikuta sings.

The joy of drinking wine with the King tonight in the castle of Idomu, which possesses the scenery of all under heaven. The Minanogaha, where the moon and sun flow clearly, and this palace which I Mioro are magnificent. This castle, where the moon and sun flow directly below, seems to resemble the palace of Shibi. Even if the palace of Shibi were ever so clear, it could not reach the castle of Idomu. My King is the Lord God, and I, Samori, am the god of the high halberd, and Umori is the god of the divine halberd. The Lord God has brought down the two pillars of the high halberd and the divine halberd to the castle of Idomu. Having obtained such a magnificent country in such an age, the King who faces the four seas is the Lord God.

Umori Naris sings.

I do not understand the words of Samori Chikuta. It is terrifying to ignore the gods. It is fearful to be proud of seizing a country by human power and to liken oneself to a god. If we do not serve the Lord God with reverence morning and evening, the country will perish. If my King Eales, Chikuta, and the others do not change their hearts, there will be errors. Please accept my words and from today on, revere the Lord God. Although the castle of Idomu has entered the hands of the King, the time to be at ease has not yet come. The hearts of the Kunitsukami will not yet follow the will of my King. They have only submitted to the power of my King, and they do not submit from the bottom of their hearts. Do you not know that although this castle is beautiful, it has become the target of the resentment of the Kunitsukami?

King Ērus, filled with wrath and changing his expression, singeth:

‘Hearing Nāris’s disrespectful words, My heart hath begun to turn sharp. Since I am one who hath crossed the steepness of Mount Ōsakayama, Thy words must be a baseless anxiety. The castle of Idomu hath yielded unto me; I think to dwell forever in this scenic place. What hangeth upon my innermost heart is my native land, The realm of Sāru; I think upon my child. From tomorrow, return thou to the realm of Sāru; Fortify the castle and defend it firmly. Uniting all the various officers, Nāris, Guard thou the peace of the realm of Sāru. As for this realm, with Chikutā of the left guard and Ēman The military strategist, it shall surely be peaceful.’

Nāris of the right guard singeth:

‘Revering the august words of my King, from now I shall hasten back to the realm of Sāru. O my King, O Satsukuhime, without remissness Govern ye the realm of Idomu. Awe-struck by the King’s majesty, numerous officers Are staying obedient against their true hearts. If there be an opening, a hundred officers will rise up And conspire to attack the King; May my King not meet with unexpected affliction Through an arrogant heart.’

Ēman singeth:

‘Leading my King’s army, gradually I have brought the realm of Idomu into submission. By my King’s divine majesty and our military strategy, The castle of Idomu hath fallen. Because many servants of Azumi, the King of Idomu, Are in hiding, we must not let down our guard; Yet so long as Ēman the strategist is here, May my King reside in peace and tranquility. Even if tens of thousands of enemies attack at once, By my strategy I shall chastise and defeat them.’

King Ērus singeth with delight:

‘The words of Ēman have met my mind; Firmly defend ye the realm of Idomu. Beholding the Minanogaha river where the moon floateth night after night, I shall sip my sake.’

Satsukuhime singeth:

‘The stern Kototama of Ēman is valiant, The very reverse of the heart of the right guard. Nāris, who marreth the will of my King, Return to our realm and make the world secure.’

Nāris singeth:

‘Well then, I shall return to our realm, Departing from the castle of Idomu where my King resideth.’

Thus Nāris bade farewell to King Ērus and all the others, and whipping his horse across the moonlit plain together with four or five attendants, he hastened toward the summit of Mount Ōsakayama to return to his native realm of Sāru.

King Ērus detested the words of Nāris of the right guard, who was peerless in true loyalty, and rejoiced in the words of Chikutā and Ēman, the treacherous and deceitful false subjects; though his heart had never been at ease toward Nāris, it was decided to send the right guard back to defend the not very comfortable realm of Sāru and have him protect the native land. As the right guard departed far from his presence and returned over Mount Ōsakayama, the King’s mood became incomparably good; without distinguishing day from night, he gathered beautiful goddesses within the realm and abandoned himself to the pleasures of poetry, song, and music.

Both Ēman the strategist and Chikutā of the left guard likewise forgot state affairs and indulged in pleasure, so that the castle of Idomu took on the appearance of a brothel.

On an evening when the full moon shone in the sky, King Ērus assembled Satsukuhime, Chikutā of the left guard, Ēman the strategist, and other officers in a single hall, and while having numerous beauties pour sake, he sang with great pleasure:

‘I am the lord of the capital of Sāru, Now I become the King of Idomu. The moon bright and clear crosseth the blue sky; I leisurely sip my sake. Beholding the Minanogaha river where the moon floateth, I sip my sake in Idomu Castle. Having sent right guard Nāris back to the native realm, The clouds and mist in my breast are cleared away. Would it were well if there were no Mount Ōsakayama range Traversing the island of Isako in two! If there were no mountain, Sāru would be visible; Longing for Sāru, I think upon my princess. The seven maidens left behind in the realm Must surely be waiting, must surely be grieving. Leaving many subjects behind in Sāru, I dwell in the castle of Idomu. The sky is blue and fathomless; The sands of stars glisten brightly. King Azumi of the Idomu castle site Hath fled for his life to Mount Gekkōzan. Upon Mount Gekkōzan Azumi is secluded; In any case it will once be a rain of tears. Unto the Pearl Lake where mermaids are said to dwell Floateth the pearl moon’s light. The world-renowned castle of Idomu Is now our dwelling place. Though the island of Isako be said to be vast, We worship the light of the same sun and moon.’

Thus he was in an exceedingly high mood.

Satsukuhime sang once again:

'Taking the Ningiyo of the Shinjiyukosui Lake, I wish to play by having them spit forth Shinjiyu. Upon this Takadono where the full moon shines, Stars shine like unto Shinjiyu. The moon gradually ascends high into the Misora, And as it rises and rises, the night deepens. The voices of the Kajika in the Minanogaha River Sing of the royal Isei of the King. Before the royal Isei, the castle of Idomu Has fallen, and the cool moon shines upon it. Riding upon the wind of the Natsuyo blowing from the Nobe, The voices of insects come forth. The fragrance of flowers, blown by the Natsukaze, Blows even into the midst of the castle. In spring the flowers bloom, and when summer arrives, The moon shines crystal-clear over Idomu Castle. Along the Minano River where the moon flows, Insects cry in the morning and in the evening. The pure stream of the Minano River, No matter how many days one gazes upon it, one never tires. The Minano River on an autumn Tsukiyo Must surely be so cool, so pure.'

Chikutā of the Samori sang:

'Receiving the Mihika of the King's Miizu, Today I view the moon of Idomu. The mountain is Ōsaka, the stream is Minano, The castle is Idomu, and in the sky is the moon. The moon has come out, has come out over the eastern mountain, Yet the clouds, out of Rinki, conceal its shade. The face of the moon peeped through, Cleaving the clouds wrapped Tohe and Hatahe. Lightly pulling open the curtain of clouds, The moon peeped upon Idomu Castle. The water flows Sōsō as a Tokiha stream, And the King reigns over the world Riuriu. This Tengokuzuma living belonging to this world— Is it a dream or reality? How delightful! The thoughts planned Higoro have cleared away, And now I dwell in the castle of Idomu. Both the crying voices of insects and the voices of Kotori Sing of the King's thousand years. Upon the flower-like face of Satsukuhime of pure figure, The moon shines bright. Are the flowers cherry blossoms, peonies, or lilies? The flower by the King's side is the best. Both the singing voices of the cicadas and the voices of the Kajika Are the side dishes for today's sake.'

Ēman sang:

'Nārisi of the Ugan returned to Sāru, And now there are no demons in this world. The words spoken Higoro by the Ugan-tsukasa Always became a source of my irritation. Idomu Castle, having swept away the clouds, Is bright under the clear and pure autumn moon. The moon shines brightly and clearly in the Misora, Shedding its cold radiance over the Minano River. Gazings upon the moonlight above and below, I drink sake by the King's side. Rather than waging war, rather than working, How delightful it is to drink sake under the moon! Hearing that once dead, the flower of one's body does not bloom, I become intoxicated by the living, speaking flowers. Gazings upon the evening moonlight, drinking sake, Intoxicated, good, good night flowers! Plucking the night flowers and bringing them here, I am intoxicated by the sake of the Amatsu-mikuni. Mount Ōsaka towering into the sky— Above it rise the peaks of clouds. The peaks of clouds changing their shapes while I gaze upon them— Ah, are they like unto my own heart? So long as there is sake and speaking flowers, Even if the castle should perish, In the castle where Azumi and Muraji once dwelt, I shall remain steeped in sake while gazing at the moon.'

King Ērus stood up, glared intently at Ēman, and sang with dim, drunken eyes:

'The words of Ēman are devoid of propriety; to speak of this castle Perishing—such idle nonsense! To pray that it may flourish forever Is that not thy daily duty? To hold such a heart and yet be made The commander of my army must indeed be perilous.'

Ēman sang fearfully:

'O my King, grant me forgiveness! The words I uttered Were but the playful banter of sake. Having my heart stolen away by this fellow called sake, I let slide foolish things from my mouth; Yet morning and evening I exert myself in the business of war That my King's reign may be at peace. I beseech Thee, with a broad heart, re-declare Thy judgment And grant forgiveness for my impolite words.'

Satsukuhime mediated between them and sang:

'I humbly entreat my King: grant forgiveness To the impolite words of Ēman. Because Ēman is a man of bad sake habits, He spews forth things not in his heart. I have long known the pure heart of Ēman; Forgive my plea, O King.'

The banquet of moon-viewing, which had been so lively, was brought to an abrupt chill by Ēman's derailment in song, which aroused King Ērus's intense indignation; the entire company lost all mirth, and while everything remained utterly frozen and dull, the night deepened quietly, and the banquet was brought to a close.

Nevertheless, the anger of King Ērus gradually melted away, and Ēman suffered no blame whatsoever, remaining in his original post as military strategist without any reassignment.

『右守ナーリス、サールに帰り 今は此世の鬼はない 右守司の日頃の言葉 いつも私の癪となる 雲を払ふたイドムの城は 晴れて清しき秋の月 月は御空を水乃の川に 清く清しく冴え渡る 上と下とに月影ながめ 王の御側で酒を酌む 戦争するより働くよりも 月に酒酌むおもしろさ 死んで花身が咲かぬと聞けば 生きて物言ふ花に酔ふ 宵の月光ながめて酒に 酔ふてよいよい夜の花 夜の花をば手折りてここに 天津御国の酒に酔ふ 空に聳ゆる大栄山の 上に湧き立つ雲の峰

ながめゐる間に姿の変る 雲の峰かよ吾心 酒と物言ふ花さへあれば たとへお城は滅ぶとも アヅミ、ムラジの住ひし城に 月を見ながら酒びたり』

エールス王は立ち上り、エーマンをしつかと睨み、酔眼朦朧として歌ふ。

『エーマンの言葉礼なしこの城の 滅ぶと言ひしそのたはごとは とこしへに栄あれよと祈るこそ 汝が日頃の務めならずや 斯くの如き心を持ちて吾軍の 司とするは危かるべし』

エーマンは恐る恐る歌ふ。

『吾王よ許し給はれ吾宣りし 言葉は酒の戯言なりしよ 酒と言ふ奴に心を奪はれて あらぬ事をば口走りつつ 吾王の御代安かれと朝夕に 戦争の業をはげむ吾なり 願はくば広き心に宣り直し 許させ給へ礼なき言葉を』

サツクス姫は仲をとりて歌ふ。

『吾王にこひのみ申すエーマンが 礼なき言葉を許し給はれ エーマンは酒癖悪き男故 心にもなきことを吐くなり エーマンの清き心は予て知る 吾言わけを許しませ王』

さしも賑はひたる月見の宴席は、エーマンの脱線歌にエールス王の憤激となり、一座は興ざめ、しらけ切つたるまま夜は深々と更けわたり、宴席は閉ぢられにける。

さりながらエールス王の怒りは漸くにとけ、エーマンは何の咎めもなく、元の軍師の職に異動なかりける。
