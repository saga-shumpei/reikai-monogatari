Chapter 16: Turtle Kami の救ひ [2043]

亀神の救ひ〔2043〕

§ 1

The Yamakaha is clear and refreshing, and the fruits are ripening abundantly in the Mahoraba of the island of Isago. In the land of Idomu, famed as a Tengokurakudo, the Onaruji of the castle of Idomu, Azumi and Muraji, welcomed the birth of Princess Chinriu, who arrived with the rising sun. As a peerless treasure, her parents devoted their hearts day and night to her upbringing for twenty years. On a spring evening at the height of the blossoms, they were attacked and defeated by the bōgiyakubudau Maikusa of the Eels of the land of Saru. Her parents fled far away from the castle of Idomu and took refuge in Tsukimitsuyama, waiting for the time of Ichiyōraifuku.

Chinriu Hime: 'How can I, in my sorrow, be meeting with such misery? If the Kami of Tenchi exist, I pray with all my heart, in Kamunagara, that You may save us from our suffering this day.'

Having her warm dream of a spring night broken, she was captured by the enemy and suffered the shame of being bound. While the three of them, master and servants, were being carried far away on the backs of wild, rough horses, leaving behind their beloved homeland, they crossed the steep peaks of Mount Daiei. Surrounded by the Maikusa both before and behind, they spent days and nights of lamentation in the prison of the Kidayama castle in the land of Saru. Though she was troubled all the more by the infatuation of the enemy prince, she reluctantly accepted the wise counsel of her nurse and went to the inner chamber of Kidayama castle.

Before the ceremony of marriage with King Aems could be held, she was cleverly deceived by the treacherous plot of her nurse Araragi and was suddenly forced to be a great criminal. With a gag in her mouth and suffering from beatings, her face was torn and dripping with blood, leaving her a sight to behold. Scorned as a servant girl named Senriu, she was sent by many knights to the shore where the rough waves surge. From there, she was cast into a dugout canoe, left to the flow of the tide, and the small boat was suddenly abandoned to the surging waves, eventually drifting unknowingly to this lonely island of Kakure-jima.

Chinriu Hime: 'Ah, what shall I do now? Even if I were to ask the heavens, I have only tears to shed. If there were a shadow of a wild goose crossing the sky, I would wish to convey my misery to my dear parents, but my hopes are like bubbles on the water. It feels as if I am wandering through a dark path where the foam vanishes without a trace. It feels as if I am wandering through a dark path.'

A single hairy warrior knight who had steered the dugout canoe and brought her here landed the princess on Kakure-jima, and with a rough voice, he spoke:

Knight: 'Hey, you nun-like creature, you have been abandoned on this Kakure-jima as punishment for destroying the treasure of the country as a mere servant girl. Since it has come to this, your life ends today, so you had better be prepared. This island is called Kakure-jima; it floats on the surface of the water during the day, but as the sun begins to set, the tide rises, and this island sinks completely to the bottom of the waves. Once abandoned on this island, unless you are a fish, there is no way your life can be saved. It is truly pitiful. I would love to secretly save a beauty like you and make you my wife, but there are many inspectors and knights watching from the shore, so that is impossible. It is a pity, but you have only a little life left. Eventually, a shark will come and bury you in its belly. Well, you should be grateful. Even if you cry or scream, there is nothing to be done now that it has come to this. However, I cannot be forgiven if I do not have proof that I abandoned you on this island. I must either take back a handful of hair with flesh attached, or slice off your ear, or if not that, tear off your sleeve and bring back some shells unique to Kakure-jima as evidence to fulfill my duty today. It is pitiful, but your life ends right now. It would not be a loss to take one of your ears.'

と言ひながら石刀を懐より取り出し、姫を矢場に地上に打ち倒し、しきりと泣き叫ぶ姫に目もくれず、鋸引きにして左の耳を切りとり、血の滴る姫の顔を冷やかに打ち眺めながら、

Speaker: 'Ah, the Jikoku is pressing now; if I dally, I know not what will become even of my Marukibune.'

Saying this, he jumped quickly into the Marukibune, and plying the oar, he hurried back across the Unabara wrapped in Yufumoya.

Hime was at a loss for what to do, her Hitan knowing no bounds; leaving her fate to Ten, there was no other Tedate for her but to await her Shiki.

Hime stood upon the Chiyōjiyō of the island, which was sinking Kokukoku, and sang faintly:

The more I turn it over in my mind, Is there any in the world as sad as I? My Chichihaha, Having had their castle fallen by the enemy, Now have departed to a land Far beyond the Shirakumo. I, sent away by a Naito, To the enemy's Honjiyō in Kitayama, Enduring the shame of the Nahame, With tears that fall regardless of Chiuya, There were times I spent weeping. Because of the Renbo of King Ames, I was questioned Iroiro and Samazama, And as I could not stop it, I bent my Honshin, And intended to serve the hateful Ames, That was the Itsushiyō's mistake. Deceived skillfully by the Murakimo, And the heart-filthy Ubaoyako, Here I am now, A sad, abandoned small boat. Even the voices of birds have ceased, Abandoned on the island of Kakushi, Rather than waiting for my Shiki now, I have become a helpless being. Living in this world, bound in the prison Of the enemy, morning and evening, Rather than exposing the shame of the Nahame, I thought I would rather die. And yet, worrying over my Chichihaha, And hoping for a reunion, Was that also in vain? The waves rise one after another, And the island where I stand is being swallowed For the most part by the rough tide. Ah, how lonely, how sad, Even if it be a dream, I wish to let My Chichihaha know of this sorrow. My tears of sorrow have run dry, And now I only wait for my Shiki. The sound of the waves grows ever louder and approaches, It is the voice of a fierce beast That will snatch away my life, How terrifying this evening is.'

Just as she sang this song of sorrow, the water rose until it submerged the knees of Hime, who stood at the very Chiyōjiyō of Kakushijima. Just as Hime was bracing herself, thinking that this was the end, a great turtle appeared from somewhere, and floating its shell with a splash before Hime, it held up its head as if to say, 'Please ride upon my back.' Chinriuhime, thinking this was surely the help of God, climbed onto the back of the turtle in a hurry, and the turtle, passing through the rough waves, swam toward the south, ever toward the south.

Chinriuhime sang faintly while standing on the back of the turtle:

This turtle is the messenger of God, my life, It has saved me completely and in detail. Being carried on the back of a great sea turtle, I am happy thinking I am returning to my home. Having met with Samazama of troubles, I have been placed upon the boat of the turtle's help. Turtle, O turtle, do not approach the land of Saru, Send me to the rocky shore of Idumu. This turtle, greater than a Marukibune, Must make the sea journey peaceful. The Yufumoya that rose upon the Unabara has cleared, And the moon in the sky has begun to shine. Did the God of Ten and Earth also take pity on me? He has sent a boat of help. Leaving everything to the heart of God, I will cross the waves and return to my land. To the ugly land where the Magatsukami rages, I was sent and suffered much. Though the deep conspiracy of Araragi is hateful, I will forget it, starting today. I have understood the heart of a person without reliance. Because of the deeds done by the Uba Araragi, Senriu has completely impersonated me, And must be living there as the consort. Becoming the wife of a foreign enemy king, Senriu-hime is to be pitied. It was he who saved me at the brink Where my soul and body were both to be defiled. Thinking thus, even Araragi should not be hated. He who protected my chastity. How pitiful is the heart that follows the enemy, Thinking to tie a dream of temporary glory. I, too, in the weakness of my heart, Was about to entrust my body and soul to the enemy. How grateful I am that the grace of God is deep, And my body remained undefiled. Abandoned on the island root that sinks into the waves when evening comes, I, too, have been saved. This turtle is growing fatter little by little, And the Unabara has become peaceful.
