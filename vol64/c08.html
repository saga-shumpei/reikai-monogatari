Chapter 8: 擬侠心 [1814]

擬侠心〔1814〕

§ 1

'Where is my Jinsei to be found? From morning until night, I toil, Squeezing out Asemizu to Kanemau, Thinking to live like the Hitonami, Suffering, writhing, my Aseabura Almost exhausted in this Shintai. Squeezed like tallow, My Shintai suddenly becomes Kotsuritsu, And amidst the Himei I raise, I wonder if the Jinsei of you and me Lies hidden deep within. Think, oh think, my friend, This world of Shiyugi, If one chants of Capitalism, It is the yeast of Daizaiaku. Satsujin, Gautau, Gaukan, Sagi, Setsutau, Kefukatsu, And more, still more, Takusan, These too are surely Means that Wareware Must resort to in our Jinsei. That things have come to this Is the gift of Capitalism, The gift of Furaushiyotokushiya. Binding us hand and foot, The Hauhafu is the Keisatsu, The Saibanshiyo, the Keimushiyo, And even worse is the Kaushiyudai, To top it off, the Kenpei and the Guntai, And there are countless other means. More cunning than a spider's web, More stubborn than steel, With countless suckers, Capitalism Sucks our lifeblood and gnaws at our Aseabura. As long as this system exists in this world, Neither you nor we shall be saved. Because the place where we were set in Jinsei Was evil, our skin is cold, our bellies hungry, And in the end, we are bound and killed. We must do something, This system of wickedness and lawlessness. Strike, punish, the bourgeois class, Rise up, stand up, proletariat, The time to stand is now. What use are politics, religion, law, Ethics, or moral training? We stake our lives, And for the sake of our descendants, we must destroy The evil system, or the great duty Of Jinsei cannot be fulfilled. Strike, punish the bourgeois.'

So saying, four or five laborers carrying a red flag walk along the foot of Mount Olive. Several policemen who had been lying in wait, without a word, bound every one of them without a single exception.

Keisatsu: 'Hey you, what were you singing just now? I have arrested you because I deem it seditious. You must come to the Jerusalem station immediately, as there is an investigation to be conducted.'

One of them, while shaking his head vigorously, says:

Laborer: 'Hey, Police, don't make a fool of us. Since we have already cast aside our lives for our principles, I do not think of this arrest as even a fart in a teapot. However, you would do well to listen to the voice of the people and reflect. What are you? You receive a pittance of filthy money, become the source of the people's resentment, and serve as the underlings of the ringleaders of anachronism, living day by day—how truly pathetic you are. Though I may look like this, I am the world-famous Trotsky. How about it? At this moment, will you listen to what I say and untie everyone, or will you arrest us without waking up to the times? Calamity will befall your head faster than lightning. In this holy place, my subordinates should account for nearly seventy or eighty percent. Therefore, no matter how loudly you talk of laws or shout about religion, it is useless. If you are to awaken, it is now. How about it? I shall hear your answer. Until then, we will not move an inch.'

The policemen look at each other's faces, and hearing the name Trotsky, they are somewhat overcome with fear. The policemen put their mouths to each other's ears and consult on a course of action. There, Hinodewake and O-Hana appear, looking slightly tipsy.

Hinodewake: 'Oh, oh, if it isn't the police. Is that you? My, my, you have quite a haul there. Eh, however, I apologize for the unsolicited advice, but please, listen to what I have to say for a moment. I will not take much of your time. For what reason have you bound these laborers? Laborers are, after all, the foundation of the national production apparatus.'

Trotsky: 'Wait, you are the Hinodewake of Hinedeshima, who is famous in rumors, aren't you? And is that the famous O-Tora-sama over there?'

Hinodewake: 'No, O-Tora-sama has had some circumstances and has recently shut herself away in the spirit castle for the sake of the spirit world. I am a disciple of O-Tora-sama, and... eh... I am on my way to the mountain for the business of the spirit world, but seeing you all bound, I could not simply let it pass, and I am currently negotiating with the police. Again, for what reason did you meet with such an experience?'

警官は互に顔を見合せ、トロッキーと聞いて、稍恐怖心に懸られてゐる。警官は各耳に口を寄せ善後策について協議をやつてゐる。そこへ守宮別、お花の両人がホロ酔機嫌で現はれ来たり、

守宮『オ、これはこれは誰かと思へば警官、こなたで厶るか。さてもさても沢山な得物が厶つたものだな。エー、併し乍ら、御忠言で恐れ入りますが、一寸、私の言ふ事も聞いて下さい。永くお暇はとりません。何のために労働者をお縛りになつたのですか、労働者は抑も国家生産機関の基礎で厶いますよ』

トロッキー『イヤ、お前さまが噂に高い日出島の守宮別さまだな、そして、そこにゐるのは有名なお寅さまかい』

守宮『イヤ、お寅さまは、一寸様子があつて此頃霊城に神界のため立籠もつてゐられますよ。私はお寅さまのお弟子と、……エー……、神界の御用でお山に詣る途中ですが、貴方等が縛られとるのを見て、どうも、黙過する訳にも行かず、今警官とかけあつてゐる処ですよ。又何のために、こんな目にお会ひになつたのですか』

§ 2

Trotzky: 'Kefu is the day when farmers across the Zenkoku celebrate labor, and at the same time, to cast a protest in unison against the exploitation and Atsusei of the Baugiyaku-extreme Shihon-shiyugi, to show friendship for the Musan-kaikifu of the Sekai, and to swear that the determination and Danketsu of our Dantai shall become even more Kiyōko, we are holding a demonstration across the Zenkoku. Not a single Kumiahiin of the farmers' union was left behind; women, youth, and all joined in, and in the Saichiu of our movement to build a Musan-kaikifu Dantai, we were caught by the uncomprehending police. I do not know what the police will say, but since I shall have everyone sing the farmers' song, please listen to it and understand the plight of the farmers.'

Upon the command, 'Begin the farmers' song,' the six who were bound, as well as those standing in the vicinity, opened their mouths and began to sing in unison. The police were taken aback and listened in silence.

Born to farming, living by farming, I till the soil and die in the soil. Even in the scent of the thin soil, I seek to live in sweat and tears. The pathos of my life, Is a misery that the Bourgeois class, Cannot even perceive in their dreams. Ah, may there be power in my life, May there be power in our movement. ○ Though spring returns many times, That which is rich and arrogant, Is the fruit of the sweat and oil, Of us, the old and young, men and women. With sweat and oil in our cups, We scoop and play among the flowers, The Bourgeois class drinks wine, To comfort themselves with the autumn moon. They despise, mock, and whip, Their cold and starving brethren. Are they demons, great serpents, or wolves?

In this world where devils crawl, Should we leave it without a Tategae? May there be power in our lives, May there be power in our movement. ○ Even in the heat of midsummer, In the small fields that seem to boil, I work diligently. Even the sweat that flows like a waterfall, Is fertilizer for the rice harvest, Of the coming autumn. Enduring the bitter heat to weed the grass, While they revel in high towers with music. We stake our lives, To eradicate this evil wind, And save our future. The future belongs to us. May there be power in our activities, May there be power in our lives.

The dawn is white, the stars are cold, The autumn wind is like a blade. The grass in the mountains and fields has withered away, The earth is covered in frost. These fingertips that hold the sickles, These feet that tread upon the frost, Though they bleed from cracks and frostbite, There was not even a moment to rest. Yet the harvest is mostly, Stored in the granaries of the landowners. Like a lonely, weeping cold fox, Even the walls of the house where we live have fallen, A scene tragic to behold. Ah, is life to be like this, To pass one's life in misery? No, no, it is certainly not so. The crops given by Heaven, Are the property of those who work. Where is there even a point, Of power for those who live without labor? May there be power in our movement, The future belongs to us.

Offering sweat, tears, and blood, The pride of the art we paint, Upon the earth vanishes like a vain dream. The crystallization of our hardships, Stained with sweat and tears, Becomes a sacrifice for luxury and pleasure, And our children weep in hunger and cold. Ah, how can we, Continue to overlook this tragedy? May there be power in our lives, The future belongs to us. ○ Alas, whose treacherous scheme is this? Blood smokes upon the blade of justice. Standing with the sword of freedom in hand, Against the brave warriors, And their pitiful wives and children, The devil's hand of persecution has descended. Sharpening their claws, Like keen sickles, The Bourgeois class plots, To cut the precious thread of our lives. We must overthrow them, If we leave this world as it is, We will be destroyed by them, And our descendants will be cut off. May there be power in our lives, May there be power in our movement, The future belongs to us.

Though the walls fall and the eaves tilt, There is the pride of freedom, To rest this five-foot body. Though we are poor and weary, There is light in the truth we embrace. We have risen to rebel, Against long-standing exploitation and Atsusei. The future belongs to us, Strike them, punish them, Bourgeois class. ○ Do you not see the farmers, The unity that covers the whole land? Do you not hear the farmers, The voice calling, 'Come, friends'? Ah, if we do not rise now,

How shall we deal with this world of Konton? Arise, shake yourselves, rage, and go mad! The Mirai belongs to us.

Trotsky: 'First, our Shiyugi is as follows: for a long time, the Nōmin have had their Ikichi squeezed by Jinushi and Shihonka, and have become Yaseotoro. Because of this, the Nōmin, who should be the Taihon of the Kokuka, are Shintaikotsuritsu and cannot even perform their work with Manzoku. Contrary to this, the Bourgeoisie class, who are Furaushiyotokushiya, are fat like pigs and elephants. This is all the result of exploiting the Ikichi of the Hinmin. How can we Ningen, born as children of Kami, look at this Sanjiyō with Majime eyes? No matter how much Shiukyō may preach Rinri or preach of Tengoku, and no matter how noisily the Hafuritsu may regulate, people cannot live in this world without bread. Is it not the heaven-sent duty of us, as patriots, to annihilate the class of demons and great serpents who exploit the greater part of that bread, and to build a world of equal love? Of course, Shiukyō may save humanity spiritually, but if we neglect the urgent problems of food, clothing, and shelter, the authority and value of Shiukyō will be lost. I think that with such half-hearted measures, it is impossible to save the world of today.'

Morimiyawake: 'Indeed, that is perfectly reasonable; I give my full support. If you, Mr. Policeman, would please release this pitiful laborer. In his place, I shall become his substitute and be bound.'

Ohana: 'Hey, Morimiyawake-sama, what are you saying? Where in the world is there such a thing as taking on another person's sins? What do you intend to do with me?'

Morimiyawake: 'Even Jesus Christ of Nazareth was crucified for the sake of all the people of the world, was he not? The reason I am always drinking sake and living my life at a three-percent-five-rin pace is because I am ready to throw my life away for the sake of social humanity. Just talking about it like Burabasa or Otora-sama is useless if there is no sincerity. I intend to become the representative of the proletariat from here on and receive execution.'

Ohana: 'That may be so, but Morimiyawake-sama, after you have gone to such a place, what will become of me?'

Morimiyawake: 'You must work hard and bring me sake as a supply.'

Ohana: 'I will not be able to bring you sake.'

Morimiyawake: 'You will be able to. The officials of today are all holding their hungry bellies and strutting about wearing tin swords, so if you just give them money on the sly, they will listen to anything you say.'

At that moment, the surroundings suddenly became noisy, and hundreds of ruffians appeared, surrounding the policemen ten and twenty layers deep and beating them to a pulp. The man who called himself Trotsky and the others who had been bound took advantage of this gap to untie their ropes, raised a song of victory, and disappeared to parts unknown. Morimiyawake and Ohana, feeling triumphant, went on their way, humming a tune as they climbed toward the top of the Mount of Olives.
