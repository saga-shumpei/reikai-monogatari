Chapter 14: The Way of the Fierce Warrior [14]

§ 1

In a room deep within the American Colony, Subatsufōdo and Mariya sit with a tabakobon between them, engrossed in whispering.

Subatsufōdo: 'Mariya-sama, are you not feeling restless lately for some reason? The members of the group have been saying that, unlike your usual chinchiyaku self, your current yōsu is like that of a stray dog driven mad by love. If you do not exercise some kokoroe, will the tōitsu of the colony not be compromised? I am an old man, and I do not know when I might shiyōten. If that happens, you will have to carry the colony on your shoulders all by yourself. I hear rumors that you are deeply in love with the Seishi who has come from Hinodejima, but it is said that he has a wife and children in his home country. Even if you fall in love with a man who has a wife and children, your mokuteki will not be achieved. Contrary to your insistence on a dokushin seikuwatsu—claiming that until the shinsei is jiyōjiyu, you would not take a husband nor look at a man, no matter how good the match—everyone is whispering that it is strange. No matter how strong your words, a woman is, after all, a weak creature. It seems you cannot endure the loneliness of kodoku. It is only for a little while longer, so you must have a little patience. Surely, a suitable husband who pleases you will appear. Does the Lord God not say that those who endure to the end shall be saved?'

Mariya: 'Hai, I have endured to the end. To endure any longer would be a matter of life and death... It is a bit cruel of you to say such things. I have determined my husband through the providence of God, so I humbly ask for your consent.'

Subatsufōdo: 'If that is the case, then the rumors of people are not to be taken lightly. And who is this husband of yours? Surely, it cannot be the Seishi of Hinodejima who has a wife and children?'

Mariya: 'That... I... the Seishi... No, no, there is a person with whom I have promised to share life and death. However, I wish for a little time before I reveal the name.'

Subatsufōdo: 'Is this not a drastic change of heart? You are in love with Burabāsa-sama, are you not? No matter what you say, it is written on your face; if an old man's eyes see it, there can be little mistake. If you do such a thing, the American Colony will surely fall into ruin. Ah, what kind of devil has possessed you?'

Mariya: 'What are you saying? Where is the logic that a woman cannot have a husband? I am already thirty, and if I do not take a husband soon, how can I fulfill the divine work of bearing children? If I dally, time will leave me behind without a glance, and once I am old, no matter how much I seek a husband, even a beggar will not come to me. A flower has value while it is half-bloomed. My flower is already in full bloom, and it is at the point where it might scatter with a single gust of wind. If I do not take a husband before I scatter, how can I fulfill the true purpose of life?'

Subatsufōdo: 'It will not be much longer. Since the Second Coming of Christ will happen soon, waiting until then should not be too late. Look at Salome-sama. Born into a noble family, and despite having the freedom to marry any husband she wishes, she awaits the Second Coming of Christ and continues her dokushin seikuwatsu.'

Mariya: 'That is a different matter, as she is painting a fantasy with the returning Christ as her ideal husband. I have no such ambition, so I wish to have a suitable husband. Without saying such closed-minded things, please make an announcement to the members of the colony on my behalf, saying that due to the circumstances of the divine realm, things are as they are. If you do that, there will be no members of the group who will complain, whether they are Buddhist or otherwise.'

She set a defensive line and dashed out to the gate.

'The bitterness of exposing one's corpse, why should I loathe it for the sake of the Way? My wife and children left behind in the land, how might they be passing their days in this world? When is the day that the Master who dwells in the Seichi shall appear? Though I have heard of Jerusalem where the Kami gather as a holy city, for some reason, my Murakimo heart has become lonely. As I think and think, I have now realized before my very eyes the Haka-naki and weak Arisama of the human body. I pray with awe and reverence before the presence of the Sumeōkami of Ananahi, please bestow Your grace upon me.'

Startled by this voice, the three men abandoned Mariya on the spot and fled, vanishing like clouds and mist.

Mariya, due to the extreme shock and the momentum of being thrown onto the Daichi, fainted and lay sprawled out in the shape of a large character on the Sakamichi. Burabāsa took Seisui from a Mahofubin and poured it onto the face of the fallen woman. Looking closely, he saw that it was Mariya, who longed for him with love. Taking advantage of Mariya regaining consciousness, Burabāsa hid his face and ran off with Itsushiyōkenmei. Seeing his Ushirosugata, Mariya seemed to realize who it was; forgetting her Kutsū, she tucked up her skirts and pursued him like a Yashiya. Burabāsa hid his body in the thicket of the woods, and after Mariya had passed by, he breathed a sigh of relief, pressed his Riyōte together, and said, 'Ah, Kamunagara Chihahe Mase.'
