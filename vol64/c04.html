Chapter 4: 誤 Spirit Castle [1810]

誤霊城〔1810〕

§ 1

Ohana was all alone, performing Ankimokutau before a Kakejiku of the Hinomaru, while tears like rain streamed from her Riyōgan; just as she was sighing deeply, at the very moment when the Senden of the Seichi was not going as she had Yoki and not a single Shinto had been Deki, and furthermore, the Arika of Otora and Yamoriwake had become unknown, Yaku, the receptionist, came rushing back to that place.

Yaku: 'Hey, Ohana-sama, Ohana-sama!'

Though he called out many times in Yatsugibaya, Ohana looked at Yaku's face with a blank stare, Minaga, and acted as if she were Soshi.

Yaku: 'If you please, Ohana-sama, I have been calling you this much, so why will you not give me a Henji? Have you become deaf? Is this not too cruel?'

Ohana: 'There is no such person here.'

She turned her face away with a huff.

Yaku: 'Ha ha ha, my apologies, Yaku was in the wrong. Otohime-sama of the Riugū, Ikimiya-sama, Ayame no Hanako-dono, please turn this way for a Chiyotsuto.'

Ohana: 'Are you Yaku? What is your business?'

She remained composed.

Yaku: 'What is my business, you ask? What are you doing when the Arika of the Ikimiya-sama of the Hinodewake is unknown?'

Ohana: 'I am doing nothing. Since there was a Goshinji that he would return soon, there is no need to be so flustered. You, too, should calm down a little. It has been almost Ichinen since you became the receptionist here, but you only take your Getsukifu and have not made a single believer; I am troubled by this as well, so please be a little more active. Otohime of the Riugū will no longer deal with a Yakuza-like person such as you, who watches the Ikimiya-sama being kidnapped by evildoers and does not even go to his aid.'

Yaku: 'Since I became the receptionist here, I have had too much free time, and thinking that this would not do, I have been walking all over the town of Jerusalem, visiting gentlemen and merchants one by one, conducting the Senden of the Uranai faith, and loudly proclaiming the virtues of Ikimiya-sama. Everyone is impressed at first and says they would like to hear the story once and become a believer after that, agreeing for the sake of my face, but because Ikimiya-sama's derailments are so severe, it always ends in failure. If he were a god, he should act like a god, always staying shut up in the spirit castle and calling people without a voice, but because he walks around the city with Yamoriwake, who is a glutton and a drinker of dregs, looking like Atlas and with a backside like a dangling sake bottle, that style makes it impossible for a heart of respect to arise. And because what he says is not thorough, in short, rumors spread that he is a madman of Hinodejima, and no one will listen. Even if I sit here at the reception desk every single day like a stone guardian dog, there is no business, and even if I am allowed to copy the Fudesaki which looks like it was written by a madman, it is not very grateful. It is truly amazing that you, too, have come all the way here with such an Ikimiya-sama and have not become disgusted.'

Ohana: 'Hey, Yaku-sama, what kind of impudent things are you saying? Truly, those who have no connection to the spirit are hopeless. Do you, too, see such a magnificent Savior as bad, just like the rest of the world?'

Yaku: 'Yes, no matter how favorably I look at him, he looks like nothing more than an ordinary human. In the first place, what he says has no logic. The doctrine is incoherent and has no graspable point, and I cannot tell how much more magnificent the established religions are. I have been thinking about various job openings lately, but no one will hire me because I have been with the half-mad Ikimiya-sama. Therefore, I am forced to endure it, though it is unpleasant and unbearable. However, as they say, even a stumbling stone is a bridge of fate; thinking that it was because of fate that I was able to serve by Ikimiya-sama's side, last night, too, I devised a way to save Otora-sama by throwing out sixty yen of the accounting money to help her. If that sixty yen had not been there, Ikimiya-sama might have been beaten to a pulp on the spot and left half-dead. It is a little strange that he has not returned yet, even though he should have been back long ago.'

§ 2

Hmph, you can say that, but when Ikimiyasama was actually seized by the Arabs, you were just standing there watching, were you not? Even if you tell such lies, I have been watching you clearly.

Otohime-sama... but I was just standing there watching, was I not? How could a mere servant like me lay a hand on a ruffian whom even a Kamisama cannot touch? Please, listen to the circumstances of that time. If you do, you will surely understand my Chiukinburi a little.

Hmph, spare me that. Seeing your master in danger right before your eyes, yet you cannot even lift a finger, and you talk of Chiukinburi—it makes even a mouse laugh. If you have any more complaints, let me hear them.

I have plenty, so please listen with a serious mind. Tonight is the Seijitsu of Christ, and I had heard that a Daienzetsukuwai was to be held at the Sōin hotel, and that Ikimiyasama would also be delivering a Daidaiteki Shishiku. Fearing the worst, I was performing my duty of Keikai when Ikimiyasama, in front of the Shiujin Kuwanshi, began to berate Barabasa-sama and say things that were Gongodōdan. The believers who trust in Barabasa-sama daily became enraged, and without knowing I was there, they were whispering to each other, planning to go and beat up that crazy old woman. Thinking this was unbearable and that I must somehow save Ikimiyasama, I looked to the side and saw three Arabs. I secretly gave them money and told them to hide Ikimiyasama somewhere for a while. The Arabs immediately agreed, and that is how I saved the two of you from your Kinan. Because of the commotion last night, there are noisy rumors in the city and the police are active, so Ikimiyasama must have gone off somewhere to sightsee in a fit of pique. Even with this, can you not understand my Chiukinburi?

Goodness, what a clumsy thing to do. No matter how rough the believers of Barabasa-sama might intend to be, they cannot lay a finger on the Goshintoku of Ikimiyasama. Furthermore, there is a fine police force and the eyes of the public, so such worries are unnecessary. You have been by Ikimiyasama's side for a long time, yet you do not understand the extent of her Goshintoku? Truly, one who is blind and deaf is a hopeless case.

If I may, Ohanasama—no, wait, Otohime-sama of the Ryūgū, please do not call me blind and deaf so much. It is precisely because I know the degree of her Goshintoku that I was worried and took such measures. Otohime-sama, you are overestimating the Goshinriki of Hinodewake.

Do not concern yourself with that; how could children like you ever understand? To do something so outrageous as to give a large sum of sixty yen to the Arabs—who gave you permission to spend that? Neither Ikimiyasama nor I remember giving you permission. Return that money here; if you cannot return it, I will deduct it from this month's and next month's pay. You are the receptionist; you were never ordered to be the paymaster. Shall I report you for the crime of embezzling entrusted funds?

You say you will change the laws and make the police unnecessary, yet you report even a sneeze of a cat to the police? What a grand Kamisama you are. I heard you from inside the toilet just a moment ago, bragging to Barabasa and Mariya-sama that believers from all over the world were coming in a continuous chain, but not a single soul has visited in the past year, have they? You claim it is the path of divination that opens through sincerity, yet how could you tell such lies? Hearing it called a Reijō makes one dumbfounded.

Ho ho ho, what an ignorant creature you are. Can you not see that spirits from all over the world are coming in a continuous chain to worship and admire the Shintoku of Ikimiyasama? That is why it is not called a church or a propaganda center, but is written as a Goreijō.

Speaker: 'Never mind such talk of clocks; it is troublesome to have people's faces and clocks mixed up.'

Speaker: 'Oh, this is Otora-sama, thank you so much for returning. Otohime-sama has been waiting for you very much. Come, come, please pass through to the back quickly.'

Yak: 'Excuse me, Otohime-sama, no, let us stick with Ohana-sama. Please cut ties with such a stinking old hag. I shall become your chief of staff, oppose Otora-sama, and show you how to raise a banner splendidly. I know Ohana-sama's red heart well. Even listening from the side, she is such an unreasonable hag that I have lost all affection for her. Ohana-sama, it is a good time to cut ties, so please be prepared.'

Ohana said,

Ohana: 'Well, even so, nah.'

She tilted her head.

Otora-sama: 'Koriya, Yak, why are you interfering? When a master is giving an opinion to a disciple, how can a yakuza laborer say such things? Stay back; this is not your stage. Yaku: 'Ah, ah, there is no use in staying in a place where you do not understand. Ohana-sama, farewell, let us meet again. Otora-baba-sama, farewell, do your best to flirt with Yamoriwake. I shall, with my own thoughts, obstruct your objective to the very end, hee hee hee hee!'

With that, Yaku jerked his chin, shouldered a broom that was lying there, hitched up his skirts, and ran off somewhere. Thinking she would not let this one escape, Otora raised a shrill cry and, while swinging her large buttocks with a jiggle, chased after him through the dust-covered, crowded street without even being ashamed of the public eye.

Just as Otora caught up and tried to grab him by the nape of his neck, Yaku stirred up the dust of the main road with his broom, struck Otora in the face with it, and ran off again. Otora, bathed in the dirt and dust in both eyes, raised a hoarse voice and shouted, 'Oi, oi, anyone will do, catch that Yaku for me!'

The passersby surrounded Otora like a black mountain, and as if watching a spectacle, they were cursing her one after another.
