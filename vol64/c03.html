Chapter 3: Night at the Sacred Ground [3]

聖地夜〔3〕

§ 1

Burabasa parted from Baha'u'llah at the Teishiyajiyō in Jerusalem, left the platform, and began to walk along a somewhat wide Kaidau. The beautiful Yufuhi was painting the background of the nearby Yamayama, which were already approaching Tasogare, with lines of Goshiki clouds. Yet, a somewhat lonely Inshiyō was being etched upon his heart. While facing the castle of Zion, he proceeded toward the city of Jerusalem along the Ōdōri, where the traffic of Shiyaba was heavy, while longing for the olive trees whose calm Midoriiro leaves, shining on the Hataoka on both sides of the road, were turning into a Kuwaihakushiyoku color due to the Jinai, looking Itaita.

From behind, the voice of a Fujin calling out 'Moshimoshi' was heard. Burabasa looked back, stopped, and waited, as if without intention, for the Fujin to approach. When he looked, it was a Jewish Fujin covering her entire face with a thick veil featuring a Mandara Moyō, and she had an eerie appearance as if she had escaped from the land of the dead. Under the moonlight, Burabasa saw for the first time in this city the figure of a Jewish Fujin who had called out to him, and while startled, he made his round eyes shine in an unpleasant manner.

Maria: 'As a lowly woman unknown to you, I apologize for stopping you, a noble Seishi-sama. I am a woman from the American Colony, a Christian believer named Maria of Magdala. By the providence of the Lord God, I foresaw your arrival here, and I have come out in haste to offer my services as a guide to the sacred ground and to receive your noble teachings. I am by no means a suspicious woman, so would you please allow me to guide you to the sacred ground?'

She spoke as if pleading, with her sincerity revealed on her face.

Burabasa, being a stranger to this city, was delighted to hear the words of such an unexpectedly kind woman, and replied:

Burabasa: 'Yes, thank you very much. I have come from Takasago-shima all the way to the sacred ground by the command of the Lord God. Since this is my first time here and I am completely unfamiliar with the land, your offer to guide me must truly be a divine arrangement by the Lord God. However, as it is already night, I would like to stay at a hotel for the night and climb the Mount of Olives leisurely tomorrow morning. Could you please suggest a suitable hotel?'

Maria: 'Since you must surely be exhausted, it would be best for you to stay at a hotel tonight. There is a monastery with great spiritual power called the Hospice Notre-Dame de France, established for pilgrims to the sacred ground. Its facilities are no different from a hotel in any way, and they are very kind, and the cost is about one pound per night; shall I guide you there?'

Burabasa: 'Is it a Catholic monastery? That is a wonderful place beyond my expectations; please, I would like you to guide me there.'

Maria: 'Yes, please do so. I would also like to stay at the same place as you tonight and hear various rare stories about Takasago-shima.'

She stood at the lead and guided him to the Catholic monastery hotel, and it was decided that he would spend the night there. The two were shown to a room on the second floor, finished their evening meal, and when they looked far out the window, the full moon of the sixteenth night was shining brightly, illuminating the world below completely. Unlike a large monastery, there were only four or five guests, all of whom were monks of various sects.

Maria: 'Seishi-sama, tonight's moon is exceptionally beautiful, clearing the sky as if celebrating your arrival. Is it not a bit of a waste to spend such a beautiful moonlit night indoors? How about we take a walk in the moonlight before you go to sleep? I cannot bring myself to stay confined indoors when I see this moon.'

Speaker: 'Indeed, it is a fine moon. The moon I saw at Takasagojima and the moon I see now in this Seichi do not differ much, yet somehow I have come to feel a sense of nostalgia for it. It feels as though it would be a disservice to the Shinkai to spend the night in Mui. Shall I ask for a guide?'

Speaker: 'Yes, that would be fine.'

Mariya had already begun to descend the stairs of the second floor. Barabasa also followed Mariya, leaving the hotel as if sneaking out, and stepped outside the gate. The two walked along the western Jiyōheki on the outside of the Shigai, aiming for the Damascus Gate. This imposing Jiyōheki and gate, with their uneven Jiyōbu, were all built in the Chiusei, yet they give a sense of atmosphere that is somehow fitting for an old Shigai. A dauro leads from this gate to Damascus. Bathing in the Getsukuō, the two passed through the gate, crossed the Hokubu of the Shigai, and emerged at the Gate of Saint Stephen. The road of rough Shikiishi was covered in places by low, tunnel-like arcades, and under the shining Getsukuō, the deep, deep Ankokumen inside felt Kotosara lonely and eerie. Here and there on both Riyōgaha of the road, Arabs wearing red fezzes were drinking thick coffee from small bowls, or smoking strong tobacco with a carefree air, inhaling it through long rubber tubes after passing the water through large, elaborate flask-like devices, while glaring at the two with expressions that seemed to say, 'A suspicious fellow has come our way.'

Speaker: 'That man looked at us with strange, glowing eyes; is he here for some kind of faith?'

Speaker: 'Those people are those who advocate extreme atheism, and seeing me on a pilgrimage to the Seichi, they mock me, calling it Bolshevism. Since they have been tainted by material civilization and have become atheists, they are people who are difficult to lead to faith.'

Speaker: 'Do atheists still infiltrate even such a Seichi as this?'

Speaker: 'Atheists, to say nothing of socialists, communists, anarchists, and nihilists, have all come in great numbers. And they shower the believers who come to worship at this Seichi with various insults. I, too, wishing to save them through the noble path of the Lord God, stood in the Shigai of Jerusalem every day and every night, shouting until my voice grew hoarse, but even when they hear the voice of the power of God, they only grow angry. They adopt a very coercive attitude, and in the end, they rain down a storm of iron fists. It is said that even Shakuson of India said that sentient beings without affinity are difficult to save, but for those who have already placed their spirits' registry in hell from the Genkai, no gospel of God can ever reach their ears. Therefore, the people of my group, the American Colony, are treated as superstitious and are not treated like human beings. There is nothing left to do now but wait for the second coming of the Holy Messiah.'

Speaker: 'At Takasagojima, just as you have said, they treat the teachings of Rūtobahā that we believe in, and its followers, as superstitious, applying all manner of pressure and obstruction, and even treat the Great Sacred Lord as an evil god, with the people, both high and low, rising up in a rebellious attitude. However, I firmly believe that this, too, will be resolved by the power of the times. When the Messiah descends to the Seichi riding upon the clouds, all wise men, scholars, and evildoers alike will hide their shadows like stars before the sun, and will surely come to kneel at the feet of the Messiah. Have patience for a little while longer.'

Speaker: 'I wish to behold the advent of the Messiah as soon as possible. When will the true Messiah appear?'

Speaker: 'The Messiah has already been born in a certain Seichi and is making various preparations, so it will not be for a very long time. However, although the Messiah is currently suffering for the sake of all people, encountering the torments of the cross, He will soon appear as lightning flashes from the eastern sky to the western sky. I have come as a forerunner of the second coming of the Messiah.'

Speaker: 'That is the most welcome news; I should like to hear it at leisure upon Kanranzanjiyō.'

Speaker: 'You must by all means hear it.'

Speaker: 'Seishisama, this is the famous Saint Stephen's Gate.'

Speaker: 'Is this the Saint Stephen's Gate of legend, where the saint was dragged out and stoned to death? Indeed.'

He tilts his head and sinks into iushiu for a shibashi.

Speaker: 'Every time I pass through this gate, I recall the netsuretsu shinkauriyoku of the saint, and my shinkau netsudo increases all the more.'

She utsumui slightly and tears well up.

Speaker: 'Ah, Kamunagara chihahe mase. Grant this Burabāsa, whose shinkau is weak, infinite power. I, ni, u, san, i, yon, itsutsu, mutsu, nana, yatsu, kokonotsu, tō, hyaku, sen, man.'

He recites the Ama no Kazūta and for a while his kantan does not cease.

Upon passing through Saint Stephen's Gate, the path becomes a slight descent. Following Maria, he approaches the famous Garden of Gethsemane. Kanranzan draws near as if answering a call. The Garden of Gethsemane is enclosed by a thick, high, sekizau wall, kept in genjiu order. One can only see the tops of cypress trees peeking out here and there, and at first glance, it gives the impression of a graveyard from the outside. As it is night, the gate is firmly locked, and the interior cannot be seen. From there, one must climb the steep, rocky path strewn with round stones to ascend to the intended Kanranzan. The city of Jerusalem, adorned by the moonlight that sits upon the summit of the opposite mountain, the figure of the beautiful and noble daughter of Zion, lies before his eyes. Burabāsa's imagination runs through the entirety of world history in an instant, wondering if that beauty exists in reality, or if the associations accompanying Christ have created a phantom. It is just as it was when he stood before the ruins on the lotus pedestal of Mount Okehuse, the sacred ground of Takasago Island; yet, for Burabāsa, the two impressions were an indescribable contrast. The conflict between Christianity and Hellenism became a sorrowful key to unlocking the history of humanity over the past two thousand years.

Thinking of this Maria-fujin, the people of the colony, and the many pure-hearted seekers of the Way who, even until now, have sought God, perfected truth and goodness, and pained their pure hearts in yearning for beauty, he finds himself unable to stop the tears from overflowing. Ah, I wish to remove this sorrowful disharmony as soon as possible. Must Christianity continue to dye the present world gray forever? Is it impossible to make a pilgrimage to Jerusalem without turning one's heels to the Acropolis? He could not help but leak complaints and sighs, wondering why the Lord God did not create this world a little more harmoniously.

Burabāsa remains silent, lost in tsuisau for a long time.

Speaker: 'Seishisama, you seem to be lost in deep thought; is there something about my actions that does not please you? Please speak without reserve. I will correct any faults in any way.'

Speaker: 'No, no, how could there ever be any reason for me to be displeased with you? It is only that as I observe the state of this sacred ground, the history of old rises in my heart, and I have been spending my time in tears of immeasurable emotion.'

Maria says lightly,

Speaker: 'That is surely true; I myself have lost count of how many times I have wept while recalling the history of old since coming to this sacred ground. However, as the night has grown late, let us return to the hotel for now, and I shall guide you again at leisure tomorrow.'

She takes the lead and starts walking briskly. Here, Burabāsa and Maria cross the Kidron Valley under the moonlight toward the Jerusalem side, and following the city wall at the southeast corner, they advance toward the Dung Gate.

聖ステフアンの門を潜ると、少しく下り坂になつて居る。マリヤの後に従いてゲツセマネの有名な園に近づいた。橄欖山は呼べば答ふる様に近くなつて来た。分の厚い丈けの高い、石造の垣で厳重に囲まれて居るのがゲツセマネの園である。処々にサイブレスの木が頭を出して居るのが見えるばかりで、一見して外側からは墓地のやうな感じを与へる。夜の事とて門扉が固く鎖され、内部は見ることが出来ない。そこから団子石のゴロ付いて居る峻しい坂路を攀て、目的の橄欖山へ登るのである。反対側の山の頂に王座して居る月光に由つて装れたエルサレムの市街、美しい気高いシオンの娘の姿は眼前に横たはつて居る。その美しさは現実に存在して居るのか、夫れともキリストに伴ふ聯想が幻影を造り出したのかと、ブラバーサの想像は瞬間に世界歴史の全体を通つて走る。丁度、高砂島の聖地桶伏山の蓮華台上の廃墟の前に立つた時と同様に、然しその二つの感想は、ブラバーサに取つては名状しがたきコントラストであつた。キリスト教とヘレニズムの葛藤、夫れは過去二千年間の人類の歴史を解くための悲哀なる鍵となるのであつた。

そして此マリア婦人を始め、コロニーの人達や、純真なる数多の奉道者が今に至るまで神を求め、真善を極め美に焦がるる純な心を痛めて来た事を思ひ浮かべては、そぞろに涙の溢るるのも覚えなくなつて了つた。アヽこの悲哀なる不調和は一時も早く取り除きたいものだ。キリスト教は何処までも現世界を灰色に染なければ止まないであらうか。アクロポリスに踵を向ける事なしにエルサレムに巡礼する事には成らぬのであらうか。何故神様は、此の世をモウ少し調和的に造り玉はなかつたのであらうかと、今更の如く愚痴と歎息を漏らさざるを得なかつた。

§ 4

Dung Gate is said to be the place where filth was carried out from in ancient times. The village of Siloam unfolds below. Entering that gate, they passed between the Jewish quarter and the Mohammedan quarter, and emerged at the Jaffa Gate.

The city of Jerusalem in the present day is divided into three quarters inhabited by Arabs, Jews, and Armenians.

Near the dark Al-Qad, close to the site of the Temple, two or three Arabs stood and called out to the two, using gestures and words that could not be understood. The two, looking uneasy, pretended not to hear and quickened their pace.

Damascus, Saint Stephen, Gethsemane—such names give a profound impression to the hearts of fervent believers. Burabasa, with his head tilted, put strength into the tips of his toes with every step, and whispering to himself with a groan, followed after Mariya.

However, to the majority of modern Christians, religion is nothing more than a meaningless form and a dead tradition. How many believers are there among the carefree Christians who truly experience in themselves the heart of the Apostle Paul on the road to Damascus, or who can grasp even a fraction of the agony of the Savior in the Garden of Gethsemane of Kirisuto? Lost in tears of lamentation, he fell behind Mariya by about half a chō without even realizing it.

原文（近代文語）

ダング・ゲートは昔此門から汚物を運び去つた所と伝へられて居る。シロアムの村が眼下に展開して居る。その門を這入つてユダヤ人街とマホメツト教徒街との間を通過し、ジヤツフアの門へと出た。

現今のエルサレムの市街はアラブ、ユダヤ人、アルメニヤ人の住みて居る三ツの区域によつて仕切られて居る。

神殿の跡に近い暗いアルカードの傍に、二三人のアラブが立つて居て、手真似で訳の分らない言葉で両人を呼び止めた。両人は気味悪る相に聞かぬ風を装ひスタスタと足を早めた。

ダマスカス、聖ステフアン、ゲツセマネと斯う云ふ名は熱烈な信仰者の胸に深刻な感動を与へるものである。ブラバーサは傾首きながら一足一足指の尖に力を入れ、ウンウンと独り心に囁きながら、マリヤの後について行く。

然し現代の多数の基督教徒、それ等に対して宗教は無意味な形式、死し去つた伝統に過ぎない。呑気な基督教徒中に真にダマスカスの道にある使徒パウロの心を自身に体験し、キリストのゲツセマネの園における救世主の御悩みの一端だに汲み得る信徒が幾人あるであらうか、と慨歎の涙に暮れて知らず識らずにマリヤに半町ばかりも遅れてしまつた。
