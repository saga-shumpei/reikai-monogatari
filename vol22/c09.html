Chapter 9: Pure Spring [701]

清泉〔701〕

§ 1

The Ikukototama of one, two, and three, bestowed by Amatsumikami, appeared at the foot of the Yotsuō peak as the stern Mitama. To build the Miroku Miyo, Kamususanō no Ōkami appeared as the living Onko. Among the Yatariotome, the exceptionally noble Hidekohime, together with Yoshikohime, sought to secure the ninety-fold brocade palace and the hundred-thousand-fold Shinsaku for the end of Ikuokunen, making them Kakihatokiha. By the Mikoto of Amatsumikami, the Kongaufue Niyoihotsushiyu, the Ōgon jewel, and the purple precious treasures were hidden forever within the Okebuse mountain Ihato, waiting for the time to open.

Opening it, Kurohime and Takahime, with a pounding Dōki in her chest, suffered from the loss of the jewel. With no words of explanation to the gods of heaven and earth, Takahime, weeping, broke her heart into a thousand pieces. Under the cover of night, she departed from the foot of the Yotsuō mountain. She traveled along the ridges of the Rokkō mountains to Matanigadake, where the clan of Mukadehime had entrenched themselves. Though the whereabouts of the jewel were unknown, Takahime, in her hasty judgment, assumed it was the plan of the persistent Magami, the Yamata no Orochi. She built a small hermitage on Takatoriyama, and day and night, like a hawk or a cormorant, she watched for an opening.

Making the teachings of the three-five into a sutra here, she kept in her heart the desire to force open the rock-root, which was a breach of her own body that could not be woven as a warp. Accompanied by two or three believers, she waited for the time, though it was an agonizing wait.

The warmth of spring drifted about, and here and there among the mountain cherry trees—though they were not like Otafuku masks—the leaves appeared before the flowers. Two men came walking briskly along the valley path, sat down at the base of an old mountain cherry tree, threw their bamboo hats onto the ground, and indulged in whispering.

Kō: 'In this way, the mountains in all directions have put on new attire, filled with the vitality of spring. The buds on the trees are sprouting endlessly, moment by moment, celebrating the spring of Tokoshihe; the flowers are laughing, and the birds are singing. It has truly become an indescribable feeling. However, the Brahmanism we serve, compared to its former prosperity when Onikumohiko-sama of Ōeyama and Onikumawake of Onigajōyama responded to each other from north and south, sweeping through far and near, is now just like the arrival of winter. Since our fortress at Mikunigadake was torn down for the sake of the three-five teachings, we returned to our home country once, intending to wait for the time and make a comeback. I do not know how many times I tried to remonstrate with Mukadehime-sama, but she would not listen at all. And now, having come to this Matanigadake with Takayorihime of the Alps religion to assert the divine virtue of Daijizaiten-sama, three months have already passed. However, having been influenced since the fall of Takaharuyama, the believers who had gathered with such effort are melting away day by day like ice in the sun. The commander, Mukadehime-sama, is in a bad mood and vents her anger day and night, as if it were none of our business—it is truly troublesome, is it not? If we do not bring back some kind of souvenir today, that difficult face of hers will not return to its sheath. I wonder if there is any good idea.'

Otsu: 'Since this is a matter that has squeezed the very bottom of our wisdom, there is no fear of even a single louse being produced—or rather, falling out—no matter how much more you shake it upside down. In short, there is no other way but to use extraordinary measures. Takatorihime of Takatoriyama is of a considerable age, much like Mukadehime, but despite having appeared here only recently, she has tremendous influence. The number of men and women of all ages who visit the hermitage of Takatorihime, where she preaches the Way at the root of the rocks on Takatoriyama from the hour of the hare, is extraordinary. It is said that Takatorihime has swallowed a purple jewel, a diamond-like indestructible treasure, into her belly, so if we take direct action and do such-and-such to her, the world will be under the Brahman religion. Furthermore, there is a very beautiful woman named Tamanohime whom she relies upon as her right-hand man. If we can skillfully persuade that woman first and make her our own, we will gain an opportunity to approach Takatorihime. He who would fell a great tree must first cut its branches...'

'That is the strategy of shooting the horse first if you intend to shoot the general. But will things really go as smoothly as planned? As they say, even a halter on a cow can slip off from the other side; it is truly an uneasy business.'

'It is said that Tamanohime always comes to the valley stream to draw water. If we go to the front entrance, there are many pilgrims, so we would never be able to achieve our goal, but I have discovered that Tamanohime always descends to the clear spring in Tsubakidani on the back slope to draw the sacred water. That water must contain some extraordinary medicine. They say that Takatorihime gives it to many people, calling it sacred water, and all illnesses are strangely cured. That wretch Takatorihime claims it is divine power, and has Tamanohime, her trusted retainer, draw it secretly and offer it before the altar. First, if we skillfully get Tamanohime into our hands, and then rush to the main castle, success is undoubted. Come, the clear spring is only two or three chō away. Let us go quickly.'

With that, the two, Kananbōru and Sumātobōru, climbed up the rugged valley path, holding their hats in their hands.

Kanan and Sumāto finally arrived at the clear spring of Takatoriyama. Three women, identical in height, figure, and appearance, carrying thick bamboo buckets in both hands, appeared before the two with smiles on their faces.

The three women clutch their navels and collapse in laughter all at once.

'Strange.'

Looking suspicious, Sumāto tries to look into the Seisen to gaze at his reflection. As if to say that being seen would be taihen, Kanan picks up a tegoro stone and throws it into the spring before anyone can stop him. Instantly, the water begins to namita, and Sumāto's face is reflected as thin, long, flat, and wide, moving zuii, kuwatsudō, shinshiyuku, and jizai, appearing as a pitch-black figure.

Sumāto: 'I cannot see a thing. Is there not a spirit of a black man floating in this spring?'

Kanan: 'Aha-ha-ha, I find it funny too, Kanan-wai, eh, Sumāto?'

He laughs again. The three women laugh even harder. Not understanding the situation, Sumāto waits for the waves to settle and tries to peek again. Kanan throws a stone in.

Sumāto: 'Do not make a fool of me. Why do you interfere when I am trying to look into the Mizukagami?'

'If you look at your own face, I have a bit of a Kanan-koto, too. Aha-ha-ha, you are a very black person, indeed.'

'My face has felt gloomy since this morning and I cannot help it. I do not feel very smart. What has actually happened?'

'You are a simple-minded fellow. Mukade-hime-sama had ink painted on you so that you would not know which way she is headed. Your face is covered in ink. I find it amusing, Kanan-ball.'

'That is taihen. Perhaps I should use the chōzu here, Sumāto.'

'No, no, if you were to do such a thing, it would be taihen. Because you lack caution, Mukade-hime-sama is glaring at you for being the one who revealed the whereabouts of the jewel to Otama-no-kata at Mikuni-ga-take. She has given you a terrible disgrace... because you painted ink on Mukade-hime's face, she said that until the jewel is taken back, she would keep your face painted with ink... and so, while you were drunk and asleep, she played the plasterer and smeared it all over you.'
