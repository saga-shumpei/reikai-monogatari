Chapter 2: 探り合ひ [694]

探り合ひ〔694〕

§ 1

Kurohime, having lost the whereabouts of the Ōgon jewel entrusted to Kotoyoriwake, returned to her own mansion while harboring suspicions toward the two men, Teristan and Karlins, and having seated the two before her, she offered them tea to appease their mood, and while threatening and coaxing them, she questioned them; though the two, being innocent and having nothing to be ashamed of, confessed with tear-choked voices, Kurohime’s heart remained troubled and would not easily clear, and as they looked at each other’s faces, wondering what to do, they sat with arms folded, looking down and remaining silent, which was a pitiful sight.

Kurohime: 'Since humans are the living shrines of Kamisama, you must be honest to the very end. No matter what sins you may have committed, if you repent, Ōkamisama will look upon you with Kamunahohi and Ōnahohi, and will look again, listen again, and declare again to rectify you. Because this world is governed by Kamisama, who is as clear as the sun and moon from corner to corner, you cannot deceive the eyes of Kami even in the smallest matter. It would be a terrible disadvantage if you were to achieve your goals in this fleeting world only to suffer in the eternal and infinite spirit world, so I speak for the sake of Kamisama, for the sake of the people of the world, and for the happiness of your own souls, so tell me clearly where the jewel is. No matter how long you sit there with your arms folded in thought, evil will never return to good. The single act of stealing will never vanish throughout the ten thousand eons. Come, before it becomes widely known, confess every detail to me. It will become my failure and your sin, so if you only confess to me now, everything will be settled in peace and tranquility right here and now. Come, Teristan, Karlins, tell me honestly at once.'

Teristan: 'This is yet another inquiry, but no matter what you say, is there any other way than to answer that I do not know what I do not know?'

Kurohime: 'Eh, are you going to feign ignorance again? My, what incomprehensible people you are. Hey, hey, Karlins, you are an honest man. Since you have helped me, you have a virtuous face that seems to come from nowhere. You look just like the figure of Kamisama. Surely, because you are pure and clean in both spirit and body, you are hiding it because you think you must not break a firm promise to Teristan, but such concern is unnecessary. Think about it according to the gravity and scale of the matter. Even if you confess everything to me, it will by no means cause Teristan suffering or blame. In the end, it will be a great joy for Teristan, for me, and for you, as it was just a momentary impulse. Everyone has such things when they are young, so surely Kamisama will look again, listen again, and rectify you. No matter how bad you may have been, I will look again, listen again, and with a fresh heart, I will wash away everything that has happened into the river, and I will trust you and associate with you, so come, quickly, Karlins, speak up.'

§ 2

Karinsu, scratching his head, said:

Karinsu: 'Hey, Anata, I understand your Okotoba well.'

Kurohime: 'That is right, you understand, do you not? As expected, you are Terisutan's brother, so you are clever and great. Honesty is the treasure of this world. Hey, Karinsu.'

Karinsu: 'Moshi, Kurohime-sama, if I say I know something that I do not know, would that still be considered sincerity?'

Kurohime: 'It is bad to say you do not know what you do know. If you simply say, "I know, and I did such and such," even you, who have committed a monstrous Tamanusuto, will have your sins erased, and Ōkamisama will praise you as a Sunaho fellow.'

Karinsu: 'Oi, Terisutan, if we get involved with such a grandmother, it is like sticking our feet into a birdlime bucket. What should we do?'

Terisutan: 'Even if you say that, it would be a Gonangi for Kurohime-sama, who has always treated us with Taisetsu, so it is Shikata that she asks us so strictly and Shitsuko. If you put yourself in Kurohime-sama's Tachiba, it is not Muri. But having said that, it is truly Meiwaku for us two.'

Kurohime: 'Even though you Mahetachi understand things to that extent, why do you make me impatient? Nusubitotakedake is exactly what describes you. When others act Otona, you get carried away, and like a toothless person chewing an octopus, you only give vague answers... Eh, how irritating. You are troublesome thieves.'

She struck the round hibachi with her long pipe with a loud clanging sound, narrowed her eyes sharply, raised one knee, sat sideways, and panted. Just then, it appeared to be dawn at the high tower of Nishiki no Miya, and the Miroku drums, signaling the start of the Norito recitation, began to resound.

Terisutan: 'Oi, Karinsu, that is the sound of the Miroku drums; it is already time for the service. Shall we be excused for a moment and go to worship?'

Karinsu: 'Oh, that is right. Kurohime-sama, please calm your heart and think about our innocence. We are going to worship now.'

Kurohime: 'Ohohoho, the Miroku drums are a convenient rescue boat for you. However, whether it is Miroku or Shichigosan, you shall not worship. Until this matter is resolved, Kurohime will not permit you to worship. How could you go to worship the gods with such a thief's mentality and defile the magnificent shrine? You cursed fellows, this is a bit different from the teachings of the Alps religion.'

He clenched his fists, and a great struggle began in earnest while Kurohime was away.

『コリヤ、テーリスタン、盗人猛々しいとは貴様のことだ。覚えのない俺に自分の悪を塗りつけようとするのは怪しからぬぢやないか。俺が此間貴様の病気を苦にして親切に云つてやつたら、それを逆に取つて俺を盗人と誣るのか。さう云ふ貴様こそ怪しい点がある。此間の晩だつた、昨夜のやうに月は出てない、鼻を撮まれても分らぬやうな時、貴様は倒けたとか、道路が分らぬとか云つて大変に時間をとつた事があるだらう。サア何処へ隠した、有体に白状せ。もう斯うなる以上は兄弟の縁切れだ。併しさうは云ふものの、事実さへ白状すれば、矢張り元の兄弟だ、親友だ。鷹依姫様は既に改心なさつたのだから、玉を欲しがる道理はない。さすれば貴様は其の玉をもつて、三国ケ岳の蜈蚣姫様に献上し、バラモン教で羽振りを利かさうとする野心があるのだらう。サアサ、早く申さぬか、今迄のカーリンスとは訳が違ふぞ。閻魔が浄玻璃の鏡にかけて善悪を今に立別けて見せる。さア、如何ぢや』

と力任せに火鉢を殴つた途端、細い竹の羅宇はポクリと折れて、雁首はテーリスタンの額口に喰ひついた。

テーリスタンはムツと腹を立て、

『なに貴様、他に己の罪を塗りつけようとする大悪人奴』

と両手をひろげて武者振りついた。カーリンスは、

『何ツ、猪口才な』

と握り拳を固めて、無性矢鱈に黒姫の留守中に大格闘の幕が下りた。
