Takatorihime, accompanied by Tamanohime, climbed up with the light on the mountain as her objective, and afterwards, Wakahiko preached and expounded the doctrines of Mitsugokyō to Kinsuke and Ginkō; when suddenly, as if something possessed them, the two men, Kinsuke and Ginkō, let out a single scream of 'Cat!' and collapsed on the spot, falling unconscious. Wakahiko, startled, scooped up valley water and blew the mist of Ibuki upon their faces, and just as he was becoming impatient to revive them, startled a second time by the figure of Takatorihime descending from above, cutting through the wind, he drew near and looked: Takatorihime lay flat on her back upon the green moss before the courtyard, spread-eagled.

Having said just that, he twitched his hands and feet with a tingling sensation. Not knowing what to do in his panic and confusion, Wakahiko ran about to and fro carrying a water bucket, until he tripped on the foot of Takatorihime, and with a tumble he fell headlong upon her together with the water bucket. The water in the bucket was completely absorbed by the earth without a single drop remaining. Not knowing what to do, Wakahiko lost his wits and as a result became unable to stand up. He barely raised his body upon the moss before the courtyard, assumed a zazen posture, crossed both hands, and with sighs and gasps, sank into deep thought.

"Alas, as for the two, Kin and Gin, and Takatorihime-sama whom I relied upon as my strength, they are in this state; even if I wanted to tend to her, my waist will not support me, and to add to this, what could have happened to the whereabouts of my wife, Tamanohime? Since even Takatorihime-sama of such high divine virtue has encountered such a tragic plight, my wife may likewise have been cast away into some valley and smashed to infinitesimal pieces. Ah, do gods and buddhas not exist after all?"

Thus his grip on faith was about to slacken. Just at that moment, led by the Brahman smart ball and cannon ball, several dozen rough men including Iron, Bear, and Bee advanced pointing this way while brandishing bamboo spears; seeing this state, they could not even draw near for reasons unknown, and stood lingering in a row like a fence. Seven or eight male and female believers of young and old ages who had been worshiping at the shrine were terrified by this tragic scene and the fierce approaching force of the enemy, and sneaking out through the back door, they scrambled up the mountain in a flurry and vanished to parts unknown.

The mountain cherry blossoms, beaten by the mountain storm blowing at that very moment, scattered in fluttering disarray, vividly exposing the bleak and fleeting condition of the world. Wakahiko finally came to his senses and spoke to himself.

"Ah, if troops are strong they perish, if trees are hard they snap, if leather is stiff it tears. Teeth are harder than the tongue and yet break before it, so they say. We misunderstood the august mind of the Great Deity of Miroku, continued our reckless advance riding on victory, knowing only how to advance and forgetting to retreat, and neglected to look anew, listen anew, and rectify straightway through Naorobi. Even the sacred words of looking anew and rectifying straightway morning and night came to issue from our lips in a mechanical and unconscious manner—this is truly hopeless now. Ah, we have erred, we have erred.

The willow that bends beneath the piling snow yet does not break, Is truly the strength of its soft branches.

Such was the august revelation of Yorozubetsunomikoto. Did the deeply compassionate Great Deity cause us to receive the whip of grace, or did he refine anew our divine spirits that were about to become utterly desolate? Ah, how wondrous, how awe-inspiring, O God, please forgive me. Until this very moment I uttered words of resentment, asking whether gods and buddhas do not exist. Why did I not realize until now the truth that softness overcomes hardness? We have repeatedly committed the sin of speaking without acting while constantly chanting it with our mouths, and truly I have come to feel ashamed before heaven and earth. That Takatorihime-sama met with such a plight, that the whereabouts of Tamanohime are unknown, and that this Wakahiko has become unable to stand on his shin and waist—these are the sins of Wakahiko's misunderstanding, and truly God is impartial and selfless. To establish distinctions between friend and foe is the work of the deluded demons of us humans; when viewed from the merciful eyes of God, all are treated equally, and there are no barriers such as friend and foe. The wrongdoings up to now, I shall rectify straightway starting this very moment."

His voice clouded over, and forgetting even that he was before the faction of Brahmanism, he let out a voice of deep lamentation.

So solemnly did He pronounce the Shinji, and though His Misugata was unseen, that Kehai of His returning through the Kūchiu was felt as though visible to the eye. Takatorihime, as if awakening from a dream, nodded inwardly in her heart,

Takatorihime: 'Ah, I have been mistaken, I have been mistaken! Until now, we believed that we had exerted our utmost efforts for the sake of the Shinkai and for the tenkasu-banmin, continuing our great activities with fujiyakushinmiyō, and serving in the Goyō of the Miroku Shinsei; but could this have been the pride of our own hearts? O true God of deep compassion, please, I earnestly beg you, have pity on the ignorance of Takatorihime, and allow her to serve in a fraction of the divine work. So saying, she pressed her hands together, and choking with tears of gratitude, she dedicated her apology and prayers to the divine spirits of heaven and earth with a single, undisturbed mind. The white smoke that had enveloped her surroundings cleared away instantly, and when she looked around, how could this be—it was the mossy flower garden in front of Takatorihime's hermitage. From whence no one knew, three beautiful figures—Uedaehime, Nakadaehime, and Shitaehime—appeared with jewel-like countenances and attire like smiling plum blossoms, taking Takatorihime, Wakahiko, Kinsuke, and Ginkō by the hand to raise them up, taking nusa from their bosoms to brush away the dust from the four of them, tending and helping them as they proceeded into the hermitage. Smartball, Cannonball, and the rest of the company, whatever may have become of them, stood stiff-bodied, watching this scene disappear from view with dubious eyes.
