Chapter 21: Kami 判 [1628]

神判〔1628〕

§ 1

Blavatsky was still in the bloom of her youth at sixteen, having for the first time left the home of her beloved Fubo, and having followed Itahiko, with whom she had pledged herself as a Nise, as far as the port of El. However, due to the Kunkai of Hatsuwakahime, Itahiko was to set out on a journey of Senden alone. Like a bird whose wings have been clipped, or a crab whose legs have been severed, her heart was choked with loneliness and sorrow. With the faint Ichiru hope of at least meeting her beloved Itahiko in Jerusalem, she traversed the Daigenya, to which she was unaccustomed, crossed the steep Yamamichi, and had finally panted her way up to Koko.

Due to the Jiyaki that remained in her Tainai after crossing the Shisen, and the fatigue of her long Chiyōto journey, she had reached the end of her strength and was sinking into the abyss of Zetsubau. Just then, the voices of terrifying Maujiu began to attack from Kanata and Konata. Overcome by loneliness and fear, feeling as though her very soul might vanish, she collapsed by the Michibata. With a Ketsushi resolve, she prayed for the safety of her Fubo, Kiyōdai, and husband, unable to bear the sadness, and was crying out at the top of her voice when Michihiko, a Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō, suddenly appeared. Feeling as if she had met a Nyorai in hell, she lifted her heavy body and, in a somewhat composed manner, spoke.

Blavatsky: 'Michihiko-sama, it is truly kind of you to come to this place of hardship, which one might call my final moments, and to offer me such compassionate words; I feel as if I have almost been brought back to life. Now, regarding the command of the Kami that the journey be made alone, you and I are neither husband and wife, nor are we involved in any suspicious romance. Therefore, even if we were to walk together for three or five chō along the road, there should be no particular reproach from the Kami. In such a steep Yamamichi, would it not be possible for you to accompany me at least until we descend to the other side of this pass? As a request from a frail woman, I am sure you will consent. Surely, being stern is not the only duty of a Sendenshi.'

With this inescapable plea, Michihiko, seeing the desperate situation of the Hime, could only look down and sigh. The voices of the mysterious Maujiu sounded as if they were drawing closer to them every moment.

Michihiko was lost in thought, wondering what he should do.

Michihiko: 'Well, so be it. To save others is the duty of a Sendenshi. Even if I were to be judged for my sins and cast down into the land of the roots, could I abandon this pitiful woman? I have heard that the Lord God Susanoo-no-Ōkami bore the weight of a thousand offerings for the sake of the people of the world. As I am a Sendenshi who draws from the flow of the Ōkami to save the world, I shall willingly accept the burden of a thousand offerings. To fear for my own sins and fail to save others might, on the contrary, invite the wrath of the Kami. Perhaps the words of Hatsuwakahime-sama were a test of our hearts. How could we, who are not Kami, distinguish between right and wrong, good and evil? It is our duty to simply carry out what we believe in our hearts to be the best. For a man, the single character of determination is his greatest treasure. There is no need to be drawn into small matters and hesitate or waver. If I were to abandon this Blavatsky-hime and leave, she would surely become prey for the Maujiu. Even if there is a one-in-a-million chance that I fall into a group of sinners, I must save her.'

With this great surge of heroic courage, he stroked the back of Blavatsky-hime and spoke.

Michihiko: 'Hime-sama, please do not worry. It was said that the teaching of the Kami requires a solitary journey, but as a man, how could I abandon a frail woman to go on alone? Even if I incur the wrath of the Kami for saving you and am cast into the land of the roots, it is a matter of a man's pride and resolve. Come, I shall carry you on my back and help you cross this steep slope. Please, do not worry at all. Michihiko has already made his resolve.'

Blavatsky-hime, looking happy, replied.

Blavatsky-hime: 'Ah, they say there are no demons in this world; Michihiko-sama, thank you for saying that. You said you would save me even if it meant falling into the land of the roots. You are truly a kind Sendenshi; for your sake, I would not consider it a grudge even if I were to fall into the land of the roots myself. Your gentle words are worth more than all the joys of ten thousand years combined.'

§ 2

Suddenly feeling a strange emotion, Otomegokoro impulsively clung to Michihiko's chest and kissed his cheek.

Michihiko was startled and leaped backward.

Michihiko: 'This is quite something, Burawada-sama. If you do such things, it will truly be a violation of the heavenly laws, so I must ask you to refrain.'

Burawada: 'I am already in a state where my hands and feet are no longer under my control; since this body must die anyway, even if you were to carry me and cross this slope safely, I could never reach Jerusalem. Having already resolved to die, if I touch your body, my beloved, and pass away, I shall have no regrets left in this world.'

Such was her pitiful state as she wept and collapsed. Michihiko blinked his eyes in bewilderment.

Michihiko: 'Ah, you are truly a woman. You are still young, so it is understandable, but this is a troublesome situation I have encountered. Well, it cannot be helped. Burawada-sama, do as you please. I, Michihiko, am prepared for the consequences.'

Burawada was clinging tightly to the chest of her beloved and dear Michihiko, trembling.

A woman who had come walking briskly from below was, unfortunately, Debisuhime. Seeing this scene, Debisuhime stood with her eyebrows arched, glaring intensely. Because Michihiko and Burawada were clinging to each other and weeping with all their might, they did not notice that Debisuhime was standing before them.

Burawada, held in Michihiko's arms, spoke in a voice like the buzzing of a mosquito while stroking his cheek with both hands.

Burawada: 'Michihiko-sama, whose divine virtue is high, please cherish me forever. Somehow, the pain in my legs seems forgotten due to the joy of your kindness. Ah, suddenly I feel refreshed. Since you have a fine wife named Debisuhime-sama, we can never be together in the end, but if you would at least keep me in your heart, that would be enough for me.'

Michihiko: 'Burawada-sama, you are truly adorable. However, as you say, I have a wife who is not lacking, so I cannot possibly be with you forever. Furthermore, since you have become the wife of my friend Itahiko, how could I... be with you, considering my friend? I love you, but I love my friend Itahiko even more.'

Burawada: 'Yes, thank you for saying so. If that is the case, would you not become my husband and wife in spirit?'

Michihiko: 'Ah, what should I do? Hearing this makes me regret having Debisuhime as my wife. Why did I ever form a bond of friendship with Itahiko? Truly, the world is beyond one's control. If Debisuhime were to see this, even as good-hearted as she is, she would surely be furious. Well, I do not care anymore. Let Debisuhime or Itahiko come if they will; I, Michihiko, am prepared to become a sinner for the sake of this woman.'

Burawada: 'Even if it were the path of the dead, the mountain of needles, or the hell of the blood pond, I would not mind it in the least if I were with you.'

Michihiko felt his heartbeat intensify, and he began to feel suffocated. A flame of shame and joy burned across his face, and he suddenly felt hot, his tongue even becoming dry.

The two were so lost in their trance that their eyes were clouded, and just as they were about to be caught in the devil's hand of love,

'Abandoning the life of your wife, the young grass, You have turned your heart to the thistle flower. Do you make Debisuhime, whom no one dares to pluck, Despise you as a demon thistle? What a hateful voice.'

Michihiko was startled by this voice, and looking closely, he saw that it was none other than Debisuhime standing before him.

Before she could finish speaking, she took out a protective sword and was about to commit suicide when a great ball of fire, scorching the heavens, suddenly fell before the three of them, exploding with a thunderous sound and scattering sparks in all directions. Michihiko and Burawāda were both stunned and collapsed on the road. What had appeared to be Princess Debisu until now was a goddess of beautiful appearance. The goddess spoke quietly to the two of them,

Goddess: 'I am Konohanasakuya-hime-no-Mikoto who dwells on Mount Tenkyō. You two, Michihiko and Burawāda, had your consciences disturbed by the demons of Mount Harusei and were about to commit a great sin. I transformed into Princess Debisu to save you from your sins and to awaken you from your delusive dreams. From now on, you had better be careful. The gods will never hate you, but do not commit the same mistake twice.'

女神『妾こそは天教山に鎮まる木花咲耶姫命であるぞよ。汝三千彦、ブラワ゛ーダの両人、ハルセイ山の悪魔に良心を攪乱され、今や大罪を犯さむとせし所、汝等の罪を救ふべくデビス姫と化相して、汝の迷夢を覚まし与へしぞ。以後は必ず慎んだがよからう。神は決して汝等を憎みは致さぬ、過失を二度なす勿れ』

§ 4

He admonished them with solemn Kotoba.

The two prostrated themselves in surprise, and saying only, 'Yes, thank you,' they remained there, merely weeping.

Michihiko: 'The grace of the Ananahi Kami shall protect my soul forevermore. How noble is the Kami who appeared as my Wakakusa wife and taught me.'

Bravada-hime: 'The Kohigumo has now gradually cleared away, in the light that illuminates the Amatsumisora. How noble indeed is the Mikokoro of the Himegami who descended as a ball of fire. Why was I attacked by such strange clouds, and how I regret that I long for my husband.'

Michihiko: 'Even if my body falls to the land of Nesoko, I shall think to save thee. Bound by the rope of the Sumekami's law, I taste the bitterness of my body this Kefu. Well then, Bravada-hime, Michihiko shall part from thee and go alone.'

Bravada-hime: 'Shall I part from you, my beloved lord of Oshihe, and climb the Yamaji of love?'

Thus, while expressing their Jiyutsukuwai to each other, they parted ways, and Michihiko hurried up the slope so that Bravada-hime might not catch up to him. Bravada, fearing that if she were to catch up again she might only cause Meiwaku to Michihiko, deliberately slowed her Ashimoto and climbed upward while chanting a Shinka.
