Chapter 14: 嬉し涙 [1621]

嬉し涙〔1621〕

§ 1

Kokūn mōmō as they were, they enveloped the Tenchi and the Shihau, creating a Kuōkei where one could not distinguish between night and day. The wind that blew was somehow fishy and damp; its Heumen felt cold, yet it contained a certain Netsuki, a Kūki where sweat seeped from the body. Itahiko and his Itsukau followed their feet, without setting a Haukau, advancing as far as their Hizakurige would carry them, until they struck the foot of a mountain of Rotsukī that was Sautau high. It appeared to be a Sautau high mountain, but from around the Gogafume, Hahiiro clouds enveloped it, and they could not see the summit. There was no path for the Itsukau but to climb this mountain. They struggled with Iroiro Kushin, avoiding to the right and left between grass like needles and thorns, and climbed up a steep place like an Itakabe. From all Hatsupau, a Nakigoe that was somehow sad and unpleasant could be heard. It was not the voice of a monkey, nor was it the sound of insects on an autumn evening. It was truly a Kakochigoe like that of one sunk in the depths of Zetsubau. The Itsukau attempted to Sōjiyō the Amatsunorito, but their lips became Kiyōchiyoku, and they could not utter a sound. As they submerged themselves into the Hahiiro clouds, they were once again seized by a feeling of Fukuwai, like that experienced when crossing the Shisen of Mount Sūra. The group, leaving their fate to Heaven without a word, followed behind Itahiko as they climbed; the summit of the mountain was covered entirely with small stones that looked like broken oyster shells, as if they were climbing a mountain of swords. Itahiko sat down gently upon a rock shaped like a rose flower at the summit. The four who followed, lagging behind, were exhausted; with pale complexions, lips stained purple, and countenances as if sunk in the depths of Zetsubau, they arrived, gasping for breath and hanging their tongues out like summer dogs, and collapsed upon the oyster-shell-like stones while heaving their chests.

An old woman came climbing up from below with great vigor. Seeing the dejected figures of the group, the old woman opened her mouth wide and said:

Old Woman: 'Ohohoho. So, Itahiko, Amame, and you three pieces of junk, have you met your end? Now that you have come this far, there is no going forward or back; you shall cast away your dew-like lives here and fall into the eighty-thousand hells. But even so, do you not have the intention to apologize and be saved? Calling yourselves evangelists of the Sangokyō, you walk all over the world. Who do you think I am? I am the silver-haired, eight-tailed Inari-sama who protects Takahime. This will not make your open mouths close. If you have even a word to say, then speak. Because that fool of Asumagaruda threatened to strike this fleshly shrine of mine, this living shrine of mine finally ran away from home and its whereabouts became unknown. Wishing to clear my grudge, I led you astray to this mountain through my own schemes. Now, will you change your hearts and submit to the Uranai-kyō, or what? Give me a clear answer. No, no, you cannot answer. You are deaf, your mouths will not open, and no words come out; but surely you can hear a little. If you will do as I say, nod your heads. Oh, how disgusting, oh, how delightful, otshohohoho.'

Itahiko, in the misery of his speech organs being paralyzed, could not utter a single word and kept shaking his head from side to side. The other four, following Itahiko's lead, shook their heads from side to side like mechanical dolls.

Old Woman: 'Oh, how stubborn you are. Even in a situation of absolute despair, do you still not understand what I say? The spirits of copper scraps are such wretched things. Hey, Itahiko.'

So saying, she raised a whip of thorns and struck Itahiko's head twelve or thirteen times in succession. The pity of it, as blood flowed from his head in lines like the plumes of a firework. Itahiko, with his eyes closed, thought that even if he died, he would not abandon the teachings of the Sangokyō. No matter what torture he faced, he would not submit to the Uranai-kyō, and he shook his head from side to side all the more. The old woman applied the whip again and again. The base ones, seeing this, were frightened and began to nod their heads one by one. The evil hag, looking as if she were delighted, wore an unpleasant smile.

伊太彦は発言機関の止まつた悲しさに、一言も発する事を得ず、頻に首を横に振つて居る。外四人も伊太彦に做らつて機械人形のやうに首を横に振る。

婆『ても扨ても、ど渋太い奴だなア。絶対絶命の場合になつても、まだ俺の云ふ事が分らぬのか。銅屑の霊と云ふものは因果なものだなア。これや伊太彦』

と茨の笞をふり上げて、伊太彦の頭を続け打ちに十二三打ち続けた。頭部からは、花火の薄のやうに血がボトボトと線を劃して流れ出づる其痛ましさ。伊太彦は目をつぶつたまま、仮令死んでも三五教の教は捨てぬ。如何な責苦にあつても、ウラナイ教に帰順するものかと益々首を横に振る。婆は又々鞭を加へる。此体を見たベースは驚いて、そろそろ首を縦に振り出した。妖婆はさも嬉しさうに、忌やらしい笑を泛べて、

Old Woman: 'Ho ho ho. You are Base, are you not? Good, good, you are a fine one. Truly, it is a crystal ball. You are the only one among the five. Since it is said, "If you repent, from that day you shall be at ease," I shall grant you a taste of the joys of heaven right here as an example.'

As she said this, she took out something like a small ball from her bosom, placed it to her mouth, and blew into it with a puffing sound, whereupon seven or eight cushions of figured brocade appeared on the spot.

Old Woman: 'Ho ho ho, how is this? Such is the work of Ginmauhatsupi-sama. Now, Base, your legs must be aching. Sit upon this. Come, sit down quickly. And you must be hungry. If you but blow on this ball, the delicious food of your desire will appear.'

As she said this, she grabbed Base by the body and forced him to sit upon the seven or eight layers of soft cushions. Base sat down while feeling hesitant toward the other four. The old woman created various fruits and wine by blowing on the ball and gave them to Base. Asumagaruda, Burawada, and Kakusu were the same as Itahiko, still shaking their heads from side to side. Seeing this, the witch seemed to lament deeply.

Old Woman: 'What wretched karma these souls have. I try to help them by making things so comfortable, yet even when faced with such torment, they still insist on holding onto their own will. This one and that one, they all keep shaking their heads. Very well, I shall bring out the mirror of good and evil and show it to you. Everyone should just do as Base does. I myself do not wish to do such terrible things, but as it is the order from Yatsukamata-no-Orochi-sama, I have no choice but to do it.'

As she said this, she struck the three of them again with her thorn whip. The sight was too miserable to behold, with blood flowing profusely; the four of them entrusted their fate to heaven and prayed to God in their hearts. From nowhere, the sound of a dog's bark, enough to crumble the mountains, echoed:

'Woof, woof, woof.'

Upon hearing this voice, the witch instantly revealed her true form as Ginmauhatsupi and fled, tumbling and rolling, vanishing like clouds and mist. The moment this voice reached the ears of the four—Itahiko, Burawada, Asumagaruda, and Kakusu—they suddenly regained their strength and were able to use the Kototama freely. The blood that had been dripping until then left no trace, and they returned to their former healthy countenances, stood up straight, and recited the Amatsu-Norito. As for Base, he was found thrust into a thicket of sarutori-ibara, groaning in pain.

Itahiko: 'Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haemaese.'

The three of them also said in unison,

'Kannagara Tamachi Haemaese.'

Kakusu: 'If I may say, Itahiko-dono, the missionary, is this not a strange occurrence? A guardian spirit of Takahime-sama came all the way here to test us, but as soon as the dog's voice was heard, it revealed its true form and fled, did it not? Indeed, one must have faith in the gods.'

Itahiko, shedding tears of gratitude, said,

Itahiko: 'Ah, I am so grateful that words fail me. By the way, where has Base gone?'

Asumagaruda: 'He is suffering, stripped naked and covered in blood, inside the sarutori-ibara thicket here. I wish we could do something to help him.'

Itahiko: 'There is nothing we can do but pray to the gods to save him. Come, let us pray.'

Thereupon, the four of them recited the Amatsu-Norito in unison, apologized for Base's misunderstanding, and prayed intently for a while, drenched in sweat. Base only continued to groan. Suddenly, the one who appeared, leading the fierce dog Smart, was the spirit of Hatsuwakahime. The moment the four saw the princess's figure, they were struck with joy and astonishment, and for a while, they stood speechless, gazing at the princess's beautiful face.

Hatsuwaka: 'Itahiko-sama, you have passed the Shiken. Now, take the jewel of the Ubananda Riuō and proceed to the Seichi. Hearing that you had ascended Mount Sūraya, I hired a boat with Sumāto and climbed this Tauzan to protect your safety. You may now be at Anshin.'

As she said this, she intoned the Amatsunorito in a voice as beautiful as a Kariyōbinga. When he came to his senses with a start, Itahiko and the other four were in the Gankutsu of the Riuō, having collapsed after being struck by Jiyaki.

Ita: 'Ah, so this was indeed the Gankutsu of the Riuō. I have been to a terrible place. Thank you so much for saving us, I am truly Arigata.'

The other four, shedding tears of joy, pressed their Riyōte together and bowed prostrate before Hatsuwakahime. Just then, a Daikuwadan shining like a mirror appeared from the depths of the Gankutsu, and as it seemed to explode before them, a beautiful woman of indescribable elegance appeared, followed by twelve handmaidens, and bowed to Hatsuwakahime.

Riunyo: 'I am the Ubananda Riuō, who has been confined in this Gankutsu for the sake of repentance by Ōyashimahiko-no-Mikoto since the age of the gods, and I have been performing ascetic practices until now. As the time has come when all evil gods shall be forgiven regarding the fulfillment of the divine government, I have been protecting this precious jewel with great care until today, waiting for someone to come and save me. However, when you, the missionary Itahiko-sama, arrived with your four companions, if I may say so, my divine power was being drawn by the lady and had faded, so I could not attain liberation and was in trouble. Then, when Itahiko-sama and the others were struck by the poisonous air of the dragon god and their spirits escaped, leaving them like the dead, I was troubled. But then you, my lady of infinite divine power, appeared and let me hear the Kototama, so my sins of the past were dissolved, my attachments were removed, and my ugly form until now vanished, becoming this heavenly maiden. However, I shall bestow this jewel upon Itahiko-sama, so please go to Jerusalem and offer this jewel to perform a great service. I, with my twelve handmaidens, shall ascend to heaven and assist you from the shadows in the pacification of the capital of Haruna.'

So saying, she handed the night-shining jewel to Itahiko. Itahiko, trembling in his limbs, received it with reverence, wrapped it carefully in cloth, and placed it in his bosom.

Hatsuwaka: 'Riuō-dono, congratulations. The gods must be very satisfied as well.'

Riuō: 'Yes, thanks to you, I have been saved. I shall never forget this kindness.'

Riuō: 'Saved by the command of the Princess, who descended from the distant Amatsukuni. Now I shall ascend to the Amatsu-mikuni and serve the Moon God.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'The joy of saving your life, which has been hidden in darkness since ancient times. As you ascend to the distant land of the moon, pray convey my divine work.'

Riuō: 'I am grateful; I shall report these circumstances in detail to the Moon God.'

Itahiko: 'I, who was arrogant in heart, have been pacified by the Nāgarāja of Takshaka.'

Burawāda: 'I was a Magatsukami of ugliness, concealing the severe power of my husband. Yet, having changed my heart, from now on I shall serve the great path of the gods with devotion.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Revere the Sumeragami first and foremost, and cherish the Itahiko-tsukasa.'

Burawāda: 'I am grateful; I shall engrave the teachings of the Princess in my heart and never forget them.'

Asumagaruda: 'Following my younger sister, Itahiko, I have encountered unexpected blessings.'

Kākusu: 'While encountering the trials of various gods, I now see the joyful light.'

Bēsu: 'How sad I am, for I was deceived by a Magatsukami and went against the path. The shame of my body, which changed its heart after climbing to the ridge of the mountain in the dark land. The Sumeragami, whose mercy is boundless, has forgiven even this sinner.'

§ 4

Hatsuwakahime: 'Iza saraba Ubananda Riuō tokoshihe no / sumika o sutete Mikuni ni irimase'

Riuō: 'Arigatashi Hime no Mikoto no Mikotoba ni / amakakeri tsutsu Mikuni ni yukamu'

Thus they exchanged songs with each other and bade farewell to Riuō. Riuō, together with twelve jijiyo, raised clouds from the gankutsu and soared into the kūchiu, and in an instant, his form vanished like smoke. Hatsuwakahime followed the narrow hole of the gankutsu and emerged onto the isobata. Koko is a nanshiyo where the waves are usually rough, kiyogan are kitsuritsu, and it is impossible for ships to approach. Furthermore, once outside, the depth of the water is unfathomable, there is no bashiyo to moor a ship, and it is known as the dainanshiyo of the lake of Sūraya, a place that funabito feared and would not chikayo. When Hatsuwakahime followed behind Sumāto and the gonin crawled through the narrow hole and emerged, there were Tamakuniwake and Chidaukoji and their party waiting with their ship moored alongside. Itahiko was so overjoyed he could almost fly, leaped onto the ship, clung to Tamakuniwake, and wept tears of joy. Tamakuniwake also could only choke on tears of joy and shed tears. Ah, Kannagara Tamachihaemase.

初稚姫『いざさらばウバナンダ竜王永久の 住家を捨てて御国に入りませ』

竜王『ありがたし姫の命の御言葉に 天翔りつつ神国に往かむ』

かく互に歌を取り交し竜王に別れを告げた。竜王は十二人の侍女と共に岩窟より雲を起し空中に舞ひ上り、忽ち姿は煙の如く消えて仕舞つた。初稚姫は岩窟の細き穴を伝ふて磯端に出た。此処は平素波荒く巨巌屹立し船の近づく事の出来ぬ難所である。さうして外に出れば底ひも知れぬ水の深さに、船を置く場所もなく、スーラヤの湖の大難所と称へられ、船人の恐れて近寄らなかつた所である。初稚姫、スマートの後に従ひ五人は細い穴を潜つて出て見ると其処には玉国別、治道居士の一行が船を横付けにして待つて居る。伊太彦は飛び立つばかり喜んで船に飛び乗り、玉国別に獅噛みつき嬉し泣きに泣いて居る。玉国別も唯、嬉し涙に咽んで落涙する計りであつた。あゝ惟神霊幸倍坐世。
