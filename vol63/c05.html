Chapter 5: 宿縁 [1612]

宿縁〔1612〕

§ 1

Itahiko, Kākusu, and Bēsu, these three, passed through a mountain path from the foot of Mount Sutaruma and came out to the shore of Lake Sūraya. Here, fourteen or fifteen houses of boatmen who ferry across this lake were built. The three of them visited the boatmen's houses one by one, searching for a way to cross to Sūraya Island, which floats in the middle of the lake, but it appeared that all were away on fishing trips, and not a single boatman was to be found. Those who remained in the houses were only the elderly, wives, and children. Having visited every single house and reached the last one, they decided that if there were no boats left, it could not be helped, and they would wait here until the boatmen returned; thus, they had the old man and old woman serve them bitter tea, and eventually, they came to stay the night through the kindness of the elderly couple.

In the garden, the scent of the sandalwood tree drifted fragrantly, enveloping the small, humble cottage. The old man and old woman had two children, a boy and a girl. The elder brother was called Asumarugada, and the younger sister was called Burawāda. Both brother and sister possessed natural beauty and fine complexions, and the brother was named for his skin, which was as beautiful as agate. The name Asumarugada is the Sanskrit word for agate, and Burawāda is the Sanskrit word for coral. Itahiko and the other two were first treated to an evening meal, then they faced the garden and recited the Amatsu Norito, after which they returned to the house to talk of various things, and with hopes held high, they spoke enthusiastically and joyfully of all sorts of matters, saying, 'We must by all means climb Mount Sūraya tomorrow and retrieve the luminous orb.'

Itahiko had become suddenly youthful after being in a state of divine possession for a while at the foot of Mount Sutaruma, and from his physical features to the color of his face, he had become as beautiful as a jewel. This was because the spirit of Konohanasakuya-hime-no-Mikoto had come to protect Itahiko, as he was to fulfill a mission, and it was a task of great magnitude. However, Itahiko was unaware that his face and figure had become elegant and noble, and he still believed himself to be the same lizard-faced man as before. As the three were talking, a woman with a face like a coral tree quietly opened the sliding door of the dirt floor and peeked in with eyes that stole glances here and there. Itahiko muttered in the depths of his heart, 'For what purpose does the girl peek at us? Perhaps she finds our faces so strange that she is amused and is enjoying a free show of monsters. Ah, when it comes to this, I wish I had been born beautiful too. Why was I born as such a hyottoko?'

The old man, the old woman, Kākusu, and Bēsu all felt a sense of awe and reverence at the dignity that had come to possess Itahiko, and as if a savior had descended, they lined up all sorts of beautiful words and attended to his every need. Itahiko whispered in the depths of his heart, this time in gratitude:

Itahiko: 'What kind and good people they are. Being in such a remote place, perhaps the people are pure and kind. It is just like the Age of the Gods.'

The names of this elderly couple were Rūbuya (Silver) for the old man and Bazumarāka (Pearl) for the old woman. Although they were aged, a beautiful shadow remained here and there, much like that of Burawāda. The old man, Rūbuya, approached Itahiko joyfully, supported his hands, and said, 'I do not know who you honorable guests are, but thank you for visiting such a remote mountain region. I understand that you intend to cross to the island of Sūraya to retrieve the luminous orb, but since ancient times, not a single person who has crossed to that island to take the orb has returned alive. At night, it emits a truly magnificent light, so those who are blinded by greed steal across and lose their lives. However, looking at you, you do not appear to be an ordinary human. I believe you to be an incarnation of a god. If you could retrieve that orb and return, not only this village but the people of the country would never have their lives taken again. If it is you, you will surely be able to retrieve the orb and return. My son Asumarugada will likely return tomorrow, so I shall have him accompany you. I pray for your success. And as you know, my house is a wretched and narrow place, but I have built a small, new pavilion in the grove behind the house for emergencies, so please, I beg you, sleep there.'

伊太彦、カークス、ベースの三人はスダルマ山の麓より間道を通り抜け、スーラヤの湖辺に出た。ここには此湖を渡海する船頭の家が十四五軒建つて居る。三人は一々船頭の家を尋ねて、湖中に浮べるスーラヤ島に渡るべく探して見たが、何れも漁に出た留守と見えて一人も船頭は居なかつた。家に残つたものは爺婆か、嬶子供ばかりである。一軒も残らず尋ねて最後の家に至り、最早船がなければ仕方がない、船頭衆が帰つて来る迄ここに待つ事にしようと、爺さま、婆アさまに渋茶を汲んで貰ひ、遂に其夜は老人夫婦の親切によつて宿泊する事となつた。

庭先には栴檀の木が香ばしく薫つて小さき賤ケ屋の中を包んで居る。爺さま婆アさまの子には二人の男女があつた。兄をアスマガルダと云ひ妹をブラワ゛ーダと云つた。兄妹共に天稟の美貌でキメも細かく兄の方は瑪瑙の様な美しい肌をしてゐるのでそれを名としたのである。アスマガルダと云ふ事は瑪瑙の梵語であり、ブラワ゛ーダと云ふのは梵語の珊瑚である。伊太彦外二人は先づ夕餉を饗応され庭先に向つて天津祝詞を奏上し、再び家に帰つていろいろの話をしたり、「是非とも明日はスーラヤ山に登り夜光の球をとつて来ねばならぬ」と希望を抱いて勇ましく嬉しげに四方八方の話に耽つて居た。

伊太彦はスダルマ山の麓に於て暫らく神懸状態となつてより俄に若々しくなり、体の相好から顔の色迄玉の如く美しくなつて了つた。これは木花姫命の御霊が伊太彦に一つの使命を果さすべく、それに就いては大変な大事業であるから御守護になつたからである。併し乍ら伊太彦は自分の顔や姿の優美高尚になつた事は気がつかず、依然として元の蜴蜥面であると自ら信じてゐた。三人が話をして居ると土間の襖をソツと開けて珊瑚樹の様な顔をした女がチヨイチヨイ偸む様な目をして覗いて居た。伊太彦は「娘が何の意で自分等を覗くであらうか、余り珍妙な顔をして居るので面白がつて、チヨコチヨコと化物の無料見物をやつて居るのだらう。アヽ斯うなつて来ると人間も美しう生れたいものだ。何故俺はこんなヒヨツトコに生れて来たのだらう」と心の底で呟やいて居た。爺さまも婆アさまもカークスもベースも何となく伊太彦の威厳の備はりたるに畏敬尊信の念を起し恰も救世主の降臨の様にあらゆる美しい言葉を並べて、何呉れとなく世話をする。伊太彦は、

『何とまア親切な人もあるものだな。こんな僻地だから人間が純朴で親切なのであらう。まるで神代の様だなア』

と今度は感謝の意味に於て腹の底で囁いた。此老夫婦の名は、爺さまをルーブヤ（銀）と云ひ婆アさまをバヅマラーカ（真珠）と云つた。年はとつて居るものの、何処ともなしにブラワ゛ーダの様に美しい面影が残つて居る。爺さまのルーブヤは嬉しさうに伊太彦の前に進みよつて両手を支へ、

『これはこれは何処のお客さまか存じませぬが、よくもこんな山間僻地を訪ねて来て下さいました。承はりますればスーラヤの島に夜光の玉をおとりの為お渡りとの事ですが、昔からあの島へ渡つて玉を取りに行つたものは一人も生きて帰つたものは厶りませぬ。夜分になると、それはそれは立派な光が出ますので欲に目のない人間はソツと渡つて命をとられるのです。併し貴方はかう見た所で普通の人間と見えませぬ。神様の御化身と思はれます。何卒あの玉をとつてお帰りになれば此村中は申すに及ばず、国人が再び生命をとられる事がなくなります。貴方なれば屹度玉をとつて帰れるでせう。忰のアスマガルダが明日は帰るでせうからお伴を致させます。何卒御成功をお祈り致します。そして私の家は御存じの通り、かう云ふむさくるしい狭い所で厶いますが、まさかの時の用意に裏の林に狭い乍らも新しい亭が建ててありますから何卒それへお寐み下さいませ』

§ 2

Ita: 'Oh, oh, Jiisama, I am truly sorry to cause you such sudden trouble and to have you worry so much. Even a corner of the garden would be fine. It would be enough if I could just shelter from the night dew. As I am a Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō, I travel about performing my Shiugiyō, sleeping in mountains and fields, so it would be too much to ask to sleep in such a place.'

Rubuya: 'Do not say such things, I beg of you, for it is the wish of an old couple, so please, I ask you to go to the Shindachi and sleep there.'

Ita: 'Since you have gone so far as to say that, it would be rather rude of me to refuse, so I shall accept your kindness.'

Bazumaraka: 'Please, do so by all means. I have prepared the bedding perfectly for you.'

Ita: 'Then I shall accept your offer to sleep. Kākusu-sama, Bēsu-sama, come, let us go together.'

Kākusu and Bēsu both stand there fidgeting.

Rubuya: 'No, no, these two shall sleep at my house. Since you are a Kamisama, please, I beg you to sleep in the new place.'

Ita: 'If that is the case, I shall follow the Gomeirei of the Goshiyujin and accept your hospitality.'

He was guided by the old woman Bazumaraka to a clean and cool Shindachi.

This house of Rubuya served as the Rishiyō of the Kinpen, so it was relatively Fuyū. Therefore, everything was in order, and the furnishings in the room made for a dwelling with an indescribable, pleasant atmosphere.

Itahiko was very happy to be able to stay in such a beautiful room after a long time, guided by the old woman, and when he thought of his hopes for tomorrow, his spirits were so lifted that he could not sleep, and he lay on his side blinking his eyes.

At what he thought was the hour of the Rat, a young and beautiful woman, who seemed to float in the darkness, softly opened the front door and, hiding the sound of her footsteps, came close to Itahiko's bedside.

Ita: 'Well, this is a strange thing. It is night and I cannot see clearly, but she seems to be a wonderful beauty. There is no reason for a woman to be casting amorous glances at me, with my black, lizard-like face that even I find repulsive; perhaps this is a fox living in this forest that has transformed itself. I must stay sharp.'

Suppressing his pounding heart, and in a voice slightly trembling, he said:

Ita: 'Who is it? Who is this that invades a person's sleeping quarters in the middle of the night? Are you a specter or a demon, or perhaps a thief hiding from human eyes? Answer me.'

The shadow in the darkness replied in a faint, embarrassed voice:

'I am Burawāda.'

Ita: 'Burawāda-sama, for what purpose have you come to this Itahiko at such an hour? If you have business, I shall hear it tomorrow. Is it not a bit strange for a lady to come to a man's sleeping quarters in the middle of the night?'

Burawāda, while fidgeting, said:

'Yes, I have come to look for something I forgot in this room for a moment. I am truly sorry to have awakened you in the middle of the night.'

Ita: 'Well, that is a hard thing to understand. There is no reason why you would have forgotten something of yours in your own house. Why not look for it again tomorrow?'

Burawāda: 'No, no, I must catch it tonight, you see.'

Ita: 'And what is this thing that you say you must catch? If you like, shall I help you look for it?'

Burawāda: 'Yes, thank you. I beg you to help me.'

Ita: 'What is the item? I cannot get an idea of where to look unless I hear that. Is it a hairpin, a comb, or a bodkin?'

Burawāda: 'No, no, it is not such a small thing as that. It is my most precious, most important, lifelong treasure... the Ita...'

§ 3

Itahiko: 'That is a strange thing you are asking about. Are you planning to do some laundry? Why don't you wait until tomorrow at your leisure?'

Blavada: 'No, it is not a board. It is... Hiko-sama.'

Itahiko: 'That makes even less sense, does it not? Talking of boards and Hiko, you are looking for something that sounds just like my name.'

Blavada: 'It is that Itahiko-sama whom I have come to find.'

Itahiko: 'Haha, so you have come disguised as the young lady of this house, but you are likely Nāgarāja, are you not? You must be an envoy of the Riuō Upananda, who has foreseen that this Itahiko intends to retrieve the Yakuō jewel tomorrow and has come to do me harm.'

Blavada: 'No, no, I am certainly no such terrifying thing. I am the daughter of this house, the genuine Blavada. You are the husband whom the Kamisama has ordained for me.'

Itahiko: 'Now, young lady, you must not joke like that. It is troublesome to be teased by someone with such a dark, pockmarked, lizard-like face. With this face of mine, which even I have come to loathe, Itahiko cannot believe such things, no matter what you say.'

Blavada: 'You, how can you tell such obvious lies? Do you not see that I possess the appearance of a Megami endowed with the thirty-two marks? About a week ago, the Ananahi no Kamisama said that He would grant me a husband, and just now, the Kamisama whispered in my ear that my husband is the Sendenshi who is staying here tonight. I truly wish for you to become my husband. Nay, you are the husband ordained by the Kamisama.'

Itahiko: 'My, this is becoming more and more incomprehensible. Ah, what should I do? I feel a sense of joy, yet I also feel as if I am being pinched, and I even feel as though I am being unfaithful to the Kamisama. Haha, this must be a trial from the Kamisama. Oh, how dangerous, how dangerous. Kannagara Tamachi Haemase.'

Blavada: 'Oh, your words are so heartless; you should not act like that.'

Itahiko: 'Even so, is this not all too unexpected? Please, wait until tomorrow. Let me think about it at my leisure.'

Blavada: 'If I could wait until tomorrow, would a woman ever come to your room? Please be assured that I have not come with any impure intentions. If you would only say "Yes," that would be sufficient.'

Itahiko: 'Ah, in any case, I have my master. And you have your parents and your brother, so let us consult with both sides and then we can make whatever promise we shall.'

Blavada: 'What you say is reasonable, but the revelation of the Kamisama allows for not even a moment's delay. Please do not say such things and give me a favorable answer, I beseech you.'

Itahiko: 'My, what should I do? Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haemase.'

Blavada: 'Kannagara Tamachi Haemase.'

Thus, the two of them questioned and answered each other until the birds heralded the dawn, passing the night away. How, indeed, did the matter reach its conclusion?

Staying at an unexpected house, at an unexpected time, I met an unexpected person. Blavada, not waiting for the morrow, presses for an answer right here and now, how relentless.
