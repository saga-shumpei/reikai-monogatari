Chapter 19: 仕込杖 [1626]

仕込杖〔1626〕

§ 1

Iku and Sāru, seeing Itahiko sitting on a stone by the roadside with his head bowed, looked at him through the moonlight.

Iku: 'You appear to be a traveler, but I would like to ask you something. Have you seen a beautiful, beautiful Hime-sama like a Tenniyo passing by with a dog?'

Itahiko, thinking it a strange thing to ask, gazed intently at the faces of the two.

Itahiko: 'Well, the voice with which you ask sounds somewhat familiar. I am Itahiko, a Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō; I have not seen such a person pass by.'

Sāru: 'Oh, are you not Itahiko-sama? You certainly grumbled a lot in the Gankutsu of Kiyoharuyama. After that, you were supposed to have followed Tamakuniwake-sama to the capital of Haruna, yet you are still wandering about in a place like this?'

Itahiko: 'Indeed, you are Iku and Sāru. This is a rare place to meet. And are you thinking of following Hatsuwakahime-sama wherever she goes? It is surprising that Hatsuwakahime-sama has allowed you to accompany her.'

Sāru: 'Since she would not give us permission no matter what we said, we set out on a forced march, and by appearing and disappearing, following behind and going ahead, we have come this far. However, we lost sight of her at the port of Eru, and we are worried because we do not know if she is ahead of us or behind us.'

Itahiko: 'Ah, is that so? I, too, wish to meet Hatsuwakahime-sama once and express my gratitude, but as she is a deity, she is free to appear and vanish at will, and I have no idea where she has gone. Well, take a rest for a while. It seems there is still a long way to go on this slope, so there is no use in rushing. It is important for the human body to rest a little. It is much better for your body to rest and walk, rest and walk.'

Iku: 'Since we have met Itahiko-sama after such a long time, why don't we sit here and talk for a while?'

Sāru: 'You say it has been a long time, but were you not standing at the entrance like a guard when you were running an inn at the Suma barrier? You were, so to speak, a saving deity for Itahiko-tsukasa and the others.'

Iku: 'Indeed, I wonder if Itahiko-tsukasa was there at that time. There were so many Brahman soldiers that I must have overlooked him. And because I would be scolded by Hatsuwakahime-sama, I ran from the village of Suma as fast as I could, waiting for Hime-sama, and since I have been persuading the Brahman thieves here and there, it has come to this. Itahiko-sama, why don't the three of us go to the capital of Haruna together from here? For some reason, Hime-sama did not go to Haruna but turned her feet toward the Jerusalem road, so we followed her, but I wonder what will happen in the end.'

Itahiko: 'The things that the deities do are ultimately beyond our understanding. Even my master, Tamakuniwake-sama, intended to cross the Thames pass and go straight to Haruna, but various divine tasks arose and incidents broke out, and he was led this way as if by someone. This, too, must be for some divine reason. However, it is impossible for the three of us to go together. It is said that there is only one Sendenshi, so even Hatsuwakahime-sama cannot take companions. Because of that, I was cast off by my master Tamakuniwake midway and have been traveling alone, but while traveling alone is difficult, it is also convenient and carefree. In any case, since it is a divine command, I cannot go with you. Let us meet again in Jerusalem.'

Iku: 'Even so, Terukuniwake, Kunikuniwake, Koganehime-sama, and others did not go alone, did they? What will become of those people?'

§ 2

Ita: 'That must be for some reason of his own. We cannot understand it.'

Saru: 'Hey, Iku, saying things like that is just Yabo. The reason Hatsuwakahime-sama came all alone is because she has become a fully independent Sendenshi. The reason Ōgonhime and Kiyoteruhime went as a pair is because they went as two halves making one whole. The other Sendenshi are all in groups of three or four, so they are only about one-third or one-fourth of a person, ha-ha-ha-ha!'

Iku: 'If that is the case, then Itahiko-sama is quite something, is he not? It seems he has finally become a full-fledged person. Are we also two-in-one?'

Saru: 'That is a settled matter. Can you not tell by seeing that we have received one jewel for the two of us?'

Iku: 'Even so, Itahiko-sama is all alone and yet he has no jewel. What about that?'

Saru: 'Since the jewel of the heart shines for those who have achieved Kaishin, a jewel of form is not necessary. The reason one must walk around carrying a jewel is that there is still something lacking somewhere. It is like walking around carrying a Shikomizue because one is afraid of the night road. Right, Itahiko-sama, is that not so?'

Ita: 'It is an embarrassing story to be asked that, but in truth, I entered the Gankutsu of Mount Suraya, received the jewel of the Riuō Ubananda, and am keeping it here. I suppose I am also of the Shikomizue sort.'

Saru: 'Oh, that is mysterious. To think you climbed up Mount Suraya, known as the mountain of death where the most dangerous of the Eight Great Riuō reside, and came back with the jewel—that is quite heroic. And do you have that jewel with you now? I would like to see it for a moment.'

Ita: 'Oh, I appreciate the offer, but I cannot treat a divine treasure as my own. Since it is carefully wrapped and tucked away in my breast, I cannot let anyone gaze upon it until I go to Jerusalem and hand it over to the god Kototama-wake. And as for the jewel you possess, from whom did you receive it?'

Iku: 'We received it directly from Hinode-kami, though we are unworthy. Thanks to this jewel, we have encountered many thieves, crossed wildernesses of various fierce beasts, and traversed great rivers, and it is the divine virtue of this crystal jewel that we have arrived safely. If Itahiko-sama says that a jewel is important, then this one is also important. We absolutely cannot show it to you.'

Ita: 'Then it cannot be helped; it is a case of tit for tat. It is a fallacy on your part to hide your own jewel while telling others to show theirs. Now, let us part here. Since my teacher will be performing his training for ten or twenty days when we go to Jerusalem, we shall likely be reunited during that time. Farewell.'

With that, Itahiko walks away briskly.

The two follow Itahiko's words and do not pursue him; they sit down on a rock by the roadside and become absorbed in conversation.

Iku: 'Hey, Saru, when Itahiko was captured by Matsuhiko and brought to the Gankutsu of Mount Kiyoharu, he was quite an interesting fellow. I never expected that a man who was popular for having witty remarks always on the tip of his tongue would become a person of such high divine status. Is it not true that when people change, they really change? We two were not even permitted to accompany Hatsuwakahime-sama and have become outcasts; though we followed her scent like a male dog in heat searching for a female, we cannot meet her openly. If we go to the capital of Haruna and are scolded, saying, "You insolent fellows, what have you come for?", that would truly be like a hundred days of sermons resulting in nothing but a fart. We must clarify our position somehow; if we dither, saying "one who lacks a proper name cannot stand," we will be treated as thieves by the people around here, and on top of that, we will end up catching neither the horsefly nor the bee—would that not be futile?'

Saru: 'What are you saying? Since the gods bestow their blessings according to one's heart, where is there any reason for our sincerity not to reach the princess? Since the princess knows even things a thousand miles away, she knows that we are following her like this. The fact that she remains silent about this means that while she says nothing on the surface, she is in effect telling us to follow. Do not worry about such things. Now, shall we go?'

Iku: 'The two of us resting by the roadside are lifting our hips, intending to set off ahead once more.'

Saru: 'Where is Saru of this place to go? To the capital of Haruna where the lotus flowers bloom. Even now, Itahiko the Sendenshi has gone ahead, fleeing as if to carry away the jewel.'

Iku: 'The Itahiko-tsukasa who fled in fear of us, who wore faces like thieves.'

Saru: 'Do not speak such nonsense; those who dwell in this world are all but unfinished products of thieves.'

Iku: 'We two, who followed the lord of the Brahman army and earned our living as thieves.'

Saru: 'Do not speak of such things even in a dream. We are already the living shrines of Kami. Do you not know the example of the lotus flower blooming forth from the mud of the swamp?'

Iku: 'No matter how purely the lotus may bloom and scent, once it scatters, it becomes but buried grass in the mud. Though it was a lotus that once bloomed in fullness before the shrine, it is of no avail now. Each time I hear the way of Kami, I realize the depth of the grime upon my heart. Blow away the black clouds that obstruct my breast, and let it be illuminated by the light of the water. Taking the office of Itahiko-kami as my norm, I shall walk the path while polishing my soul.'

After about half an hour, the two again descend, chanting the Sendenka while keeping time with their feet.

'In the land of the moon, famed for its name, a hundred flowers bloom and scent. At the Ōtauge of Mount Harusei, having walked for three days and three nights, when we finally arrive here, we find the unexpected. The Itahiko-tsukasa of Ananahi is resting from the fatigue of his journey by the roadside, sleeping in such a mysterious way. When I think of it, when I think of it, it is embarrassing. In the Gankutsu of Takaharuyama, we too, who spent our days in a drunken dream, sharing sake with Itahiko-tsukasa, must now set our hearts straight. How joyful it is to serve the divine work of the palace of Chin, gathering in the forest of the shrine. When I come here, yearning for the wake of Hatsuwakahime-sama, the figure of the Hime is but clouds and mist, a journey into the unknown sky. When I look at the moon crossing the vast sky, she is riding upon a boat of clouds, advancing ever westward. Though I hear that Hime-sama is proceeding to the capital of Haruna, following in the wake of the moon, is it in accordance with the true path of heaven and earth to first proceed to the Jerusalem of Chin? When I think of it, when I think of it, the things that Kami plays at cannot be measured by our clouded souls. Just let everything be Kannagara. Walk the path of sincerity with single-mindedness, and go as far as you can go. Kami is with us, and man is the child of Kami, the shrine of Kami. How could the attacks of evil reach us? The divine declaration you taught us, we receive upon our necks and hasten. Guarding the crystal jewel, following the wake of Itahiko-tsukasa, come, let us advance to Jerusalem. We humbly pray before the presence of the Imperial Ōkami of heaven and earth. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if the great earth should sink, the path of sincerity saves the world. I, who walk the path of Ananahi of sincerity alone, am Kannagara. Following the wake of the Moon-no-Omikami, to Jerusalem where Kami gathers, to the mountain of Kami where the golden flowers bloom. Arriving at Mount Kogane, resting under the olive tree, I shall brush the dust from my heart with the cool breeze of divine grace. Advance, advance, come, advance; the capital of victory has drawn near. Purifying ourselves in the flow of the Jordan river with deep grace, we change into newborn infants. Receiving the permission of Hatsuwakahime-sama, while bowing down before the noble face of the Kami-tsukasa filled with glory, we ride the boat on the river of Eden and row out into the inlet of Fusa. With no worries upon the waves, we shall advance to the capital of Haruna. Take courage, take courage, take good courage; Kami is with us. Ah, Kannagara, Kami, Kami, grant us the divine favor of the spirit.'

Chanting thus, both Iku and Saru descend the western slope of Mount Harusei with great enthusiasm.
