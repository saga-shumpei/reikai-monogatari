Chapter 18: Night の Journey [1625]

夜の旅〔1625〕

§ 1

Itahiko, seeing his beloved Burawada-hime suffering before his eyes and seeking a final handshake, felt such pity for her heart that his chest tightened and he sobbed and wept for some time; then, lifting his head again and wiping away his tears, he spoke.

Itahiko: 'Burawada-hime, that you suffer in this way is because my own will was weak. Had I refused you properly at the village of Teru, your confusion would have cleared, and I would not have encountered such a divine commandment; please, forgive me. Although Itahiko is not such a heartless man as to deny a single handshake to the woman I vowed to share life and death with, this body has received a mission, and even if this flesh should rot away, how could I break this vow? It is because I love you from the bottom of my heart that I perform such a cruel act; never blame me as a heartless man. Itahiko's thoughts are immeasurable. I know not for what sin's retribution I have learned the suffering of love for the first time; you are also a daughter of Rubuya, a woman called Burawada, so there is no reason why you should not understand Itahiko's words.'

Burawada: 'Itahiko-sama, if that is the case, I shall take my leave now. May the Great Kami of the Hidden World protect and bless you.'

No sooner had she said this than she drew her dagger and sought to pierce her own throat. Itahiko was startled and tried to press her hand down, but due to his illness which grew heavier by the moment, his limbs would not obey him; he was anxious and wondered what to do, and in the instant he thought it a great crisis, the woodcutter leaped out, snatched the dagger from Burawada, and threw it into the nearby dense forest. The woodcutter instantly transformed into two beautiful women of exquisite appearance. Itahiko was startled and looked down. Burawada-hime also instantly transformed into a goddess of even higher nobility and beauty than her previous incarnation. When Itahiko finally raised his head to look, it was a great mystery; Burawada-hime and the woodcutter were nowhere to be seen, and three goddesses stood solemnly before him. Thinking that perhaps she had appeared before him as Konohanasakuya-hime while pretending to be Burawada, he folded his hands in prayer, unaware of what he was doing, feeling both grateful and humbled.

Suddenly, Itahiko's illness vanished as if wiped away, as if he had forgotten it, scattering to some unknown place, and filled with a most refreshed feeling, he sat upright, placed both hands on the ground, and spoke.

Itahiko: 'Ha-ha, how grateful and venerable, Konohana-hime no Mikoto-sama; I cannot express enough my gratitude for your teachings, which were filled with your heart to the very end. I humbly pray that you protect me so that I, Itahiko, may not fall into the temptations of the devil along the way. Also, Burawada-hime is a delicate woman traveling alone; I humbly pray for your protection for her as well.'

Konohana-hime: 'I have certainly heard your request. However, what will you do about the situation of Tamakuniwake?'

Itahiko: 'I am humbled. While encountering such trials, I prayed only for my own situation and that of my wife, and left the situation of my master behind. Please, I beg your forgiveness.'

Konohana-hime: 'You did not pray for them because you were at ease, believing that the propaganda envoys Tamakuniwake, Masumihiko, and Michihiko were endowed with divine virtue and had the thick protection of the Kami, did you not?'

At these words of compassion, which saw directly into his heart and heard his true intent, Itahiko was humbled, and with palms pressed together, he shed tears of gratitude like a waterfall. Suddenly, music was heard in the void, a fragrant scent filled the air, and surrounded by the auspicious birds of Kalavinka, they hid their forms in the clouds and mist. Looking back, Itahiko gazed up at the sky many times and recited:

Itahiko: 'The flowers of the trees bloom all at once, and the sky of Itahiko's heart has cleared. How venerable is the grace of Konohana-hime, who descended from the mountains of the Heavenly Teachings. My weary body has become healthy, and I know the depth of the Kami's grace. Where is the master, Tamakuniwake-tsukasa now? May the Heavenly Kami protect him. I know that the clear sky of Masumi I gaze upon is the manifestation of my friend's heart. I weep, longing for my friend and companion, Michihiko, and his pure spirit endowed with divine virtue. Michihiko, too, must be clouding his heart with thoughts of Debisu-hime about now. Debisu-hime and Burawada-hime must also be traveling the mountain path alone, following the teachings. One fears for those who walk the wild paths where demons, serpents, tigers, and wolves rage. Yet, if the venerable Kami are present, even a woman's journey shall proceed with ease.'

§ 2

Itahiko, having recovered his vigor, descends the Harusei Pass while singing the Sendenka, chanting to himself: 'Let us rise and advance to the capital of Chin, seeking the footprints of Kuniharutachi.'

Itahiko: 'When the plum blossoms of the Sanzensekai open all at once, the time has come. The Ikigami who saves this world gathers the Kami at Tenkyōzan. The palace of Iso, Jerusalem, the Caucasus Mountains, and Kenonkiyō; in the sacred ground of Onokorojima, the Ōkami Kamususanō distributes the severe Mitama. With the Mikoto of the severe Mitama, He sends forth the pillars of Kami in all directions to Kotomu and harmonize the ugly Orochi and Onigami that lurk within Toyoashihara and torment the Yobito, in order to Kensetsu the Tengoku upon the Chijiyō; how noble this is. Though I am but a small being, having received the Mikoto of Kami, I, with my teacher, Tamakuniwake-dono, am entrusted with the noble task of a Kami-tsukasa to conquer the capital of Haruna in the moon country where the Majin rage; how grateful I am. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if the earth should sink, the power of Makoto saves the world. At the foot of Mount Sudharma, I met with Karkus and Base, and upon hearing that a jewel existed on Mount Sūraya, my spirit was transformed. I, Itahiko of the fierce heart, begged for my teacher's permission, slipped through the secret path, and the three of us arrived safely at the shore of Teru. There, I met an unexpected woman, and while pledging our vows as husband and wife, I met Ubananda, known to the world as the foremost of the Eight Great Ryūō. We, a party of five, advanced into the rocky cave of Nāgarashā, and the terror we felt when we reached the boundary between the Yomi and the Genze was great. How noble it is to have been revived in the Genze, having been seen and corrected by Kamunao-hi and Ōnao-hi, who created this world and whose hearts are vast. Helped by the Senden-shi Hatsuwakahime who happened to come by, I crawled out toward the light in the crevice of the rock, and there was my teacher, Tamakuniwake-dono, waiting for us with his Iwakusufune laid out. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-tamachi. The divine plan is unfathomable. What is the thousand-fathom sea to me? The majesty is as high as Mount Sumeru, and what is the mountain to Itahiko? Rejoicing and emboldened, I boarded my teacher's ship for Eru Port, and as we hoisted the sails in a fair wind and ascended without incident, how could this be? Hatsuwakahime and her party stood at the pier, waiting most earnestly. Rejoicing in the safe reunion of my teacher's party and the celebration of our future, I received the teachings of Hatsuwakahime. With reverence, I parted from my beloved wife, and holding the light-emitting jewel, I ascended to the capital of Chin. My journey became a solitary one. When I came to the top of the pass of Mount Harusei, traveling day and night, my head ached, my chest was constricted, and my limbs would not move. In that suffering, I occupied a seat on the grass at the summit, and while concentrating on prayers of gratitude, I spent the time in tears of repentance. When I looked to see who the woman was who was being helped by two woodcutters and climbing up with great difficulty, how could this be? Knowing it was Burawāda, the sister whom I could not forget even in my dreams, I steeled my heart, determined to protect the mission of Kami, and as a result of my fierce struggle against the evil spirits within my heart, the darkness of my chest finally cleared. She who appeared as Princess Burawāda was the divine incarnation of the most venerable Konohana-hime. The two woodcutters who appeared were also the attendants of Konohana-hime. To transform such a lowly Itahiko into a true official, they rack their hearts in a thousand ways. The compassion of the Kanjizai of the thirty-three manifestations and the Tenson-sama is a matter of such reverence that I can only look up to it. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-tamachi. Now that my body has become healthy, I shall advance from here to Jerusalem, follow in the footsteps of my teacher, and proceed straight along the path of Makoto without looking aside. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if the earth should sink, and even if my body should decay, how could I allow this soul received from Kami to be defiled by the Magatsu-hi? While correcting my sight, my hearing, and my words, I continue to live in Kannagara.'

Advancing according to the teachings, the scenery around me gradually becomes filled with the colors of autumn. The trees and grasses of the mountains and fields rustle, fluttering in the wind blowing from the sky, and it has become most elegant. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-tamachi. I pray that the divine presence will appear as soon as possible, and that I may be allowed to advance before the great presence where Haniyasuhiko and the Mikoto of Haniyasuhime reside eternally in the divine palace beneath the Golden Mountain.

Singing thus, he descends the Kuwankōbai Yamamichi with a rhythmic tap-tap-tap. The sun tilts toward Seizan, and the evening breeze, Kotosara cool, blows against the face of Itahiko. Itahiko has finally descended to the middle of this high Ōtauge, where he sits upon a nearby rock and drifts into a doze. Just then, from the upper part of the pass,

Iku: 'Serving the Brahman Oshihe, Iku and Sāru, the officials of the ugly, In the Gankutsu of Kiyoharuyama, Instructed by the official Matsuhiko, To the Seidau of Ananai-kyō, We pledged Kijiyun and, following The official of the Kami Tamakuniwake, Together with the official Itahiko, We served in the Miyabushin Of the shrine forest, and while bidding A sorrowful farewell to our master, We served for a short while at the Uketsuke Of the Chin mansion, when suddenly Takahime of Ananai-kyō and Mokusuke of Eugenbau Came strolling along, How hateful their Baui was. Just then, appearing as a Tenniyo Of the Reigoku, Hatsuwakahime Stopped by and, having the Eujiya',

Warera two, yearning for the Divine virtue of the Himegimi, Resolved to serve as her attendants To the capital of Haruna, following Behind and before, guarding her person, And in the joy of the Zuihō Bestowed by the Hinode no Kami, The living Kami who illuminates this world, Though we had no permission from the Hime, Making sincerity our sole strength, We have advanced as far as this place. Where is Hatsuwakahime now? Even the voice of Sumāto-sama Has now completely faded from our ears, Becoming distant. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase, May the blessings of the Kami be upon us.'

'We pray that we may meet The Himegimi as soon as possible, And that we may hear the pure and noble Ryūsei of Sumāto.
