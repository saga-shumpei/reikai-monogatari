Chapter 6: テルの里 [1613]

テルの里〔1613〕

§ 1

The night broke open with the cry of a crow. The elderly couple, along with the beautiful Burawada, began tending to the flower garden from early morning, sweeping the gate, and waiting for the return of Asumagaruda's ship. They were filled with an impatience that felt like a thousand autumns, longing to tell the elder brother of the joy that Itahiko had been made the husband of Burawada by the command of Kami, and to see him rejoice. Itahiko, too, had no choice but to wait, though his heart was not in it, for the essential ship was missing. The six host and guests sat in a circle in the new house built within the cool forest, drinking fruit wine and waiting for the brother's return with joyful singing and dancing. Rubuya, the old man, first gave thanks to Kami and began to chant.

'In the land of the moon where the sky shines bright, At the southern foot of Mount Sudaruma, Like the spreading of the Mirror of Izu, Lies the vast Lake Sūraya. In the village of Teru nearby, Spring of Tokoyo has come To the house of Rubuya. I am a believer who has served and guarded The Oshi of the Ananai Kami Morning and evening from the beginning. This is a land where the Oshi of the Brahman, Famed by name, grows thick. If I were to declare it the Ananai-kyō, I would surely end up as rust on the blades Of the ugly officials. Knowing it is cowardly and weak, Yet unable to declare myself A believer of the Ananai-kyō, I have followed the ugly Oshi of the Brahman, Suffering through the time of waiting. Even the people of this village have long Devoted themselves to the Ananai-kyō And served it from ancient times, But because the ferocity of the ugly ones is great, They have served the ugly path against their will, Such is the pity of it. Therefore, even for my brother Asumagaruda, Though he is of age, I have not even let him take A wife, like a young blade of grass, But have prayed in a hushed voice Morning and evening before the presence of the Ananai Kami. While waiting for the divine plan of the Idumu Kami, Itahiko, an official of the Ananai Kami, Appeared here to our joy, And promised to cherish and love My daughter Burawada as his wife. Hearing this, I am filled with gratitude, Feeling as if flowers are blooming on a withered tree. My old tears have gradually Turned into tears of joy. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, The grace of Kami is ever deeper, The divine majesty ever higher, With a heart brimming with boundless joy, I serve and give thanks before the Great Presence. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, Whether the moon waxes or wanes, Whether the stars fall or the sea dries up, I shall pass the Oshi of the Ananai-kyō To my descendants and never let it go. My patience has borne fruit, Like plum blossoms opening all at once, In this age of fragrant scent, This day of birth is truly auspicious. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, May the spirits be blessed and prosperous.'

Itahiko also chants.

'I am the propagandist Itahiko, Having served the master Tamakuniwake-no-kimi From the forest of the shrine, I have manifested various merits. Coming to the foot of Mount Sudaruma, I looked at my own body, And its appearance was youthful, Shining with a green hue. While I wondered in surprise, I met the two, Kākusu and Bēsu, On the roadside where the trees grew thick. I heard that the night-shining jewel, Hidden by Nāgarashā, the Ubananda That had hidden in the mountain of Sūraya, Still existed. I brought forth the Kototama of Izu, Commanded the Dragon Kami, And was granted the night-shining jewel. I stood up with courage to offer it To the divine hall of the Golden Mountain, The sacred land of treasures, Jerusalem. Being taught the shortcut by the two, I came to the village of Teru, where green drips, To the shore of Lake Sūraya, And what I saw was a great mystery. Is it the return of Konohana-hime, Or perhaps Matsushiyo-hime of the age of Kami? Burawada, with her beautiful face, A princess of gentle appearance. When I was addressed by her jeweled voice, My heart throbbed in foolishness. Since it has come to this, Itahiko, Only Kannagara-kamu-nagara, Revering it as the divine economy, Shall take Princess Burawada as my wife, And tie a red bond like a jeweled camellia For a thousand and eight thousand generations, Serving the Great Path of Kami. If the master Tamakuniwake-no-kimi Learns of this news, He will surely rejoice. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, Swearing to Kami, Itahiko Pushes open the rock-door of his heart And states the full extent of his thoughts. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, Whether the moon waxes or wanes, Even if the earth should sink, I shall stand firm on sincerity alone, Serving Bazumarāka well, the father and mother Rubuya, Cherishing Burawada, And the Oshi of the Ananai-kyō, Not to mention the land of the moon, But to all the lands of the four directions without exception, I shall transmit it and report my triumph Before the Great Kami. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, Protect us, before the presence of the Great Imperial Kami of Ananai.'

伊太彦は又謡ふ。

『吾は伊太彦宣伝使 玉国別の師の君に 祠の森より仕へ来て 諸の功を現はしつ スダルマ山の麓迄 来りて見れば吾体 其面影も若々と 緑の色と輝きぬ はて訝かしやと思ふ間に カークス、ベースの両人に 夏木茂れる道の辺に 巡り遇ひてゆスーラヤの 山にかくれしウバナンダ ナーガラシャーのかくしたる 夜光の玉のありと聞き 伊都の言霊打ち出だし 竜神達を言向けて 夜光の玉を授かりつ 珍の聖地のエルサレム 黄金山の神館に

奉らむと勇み立ち 二人に間道教へられ 緑滴るテルの里 スーラヤ湖水の磯端に 来りて見れば摩訶不思議 木花姫の再来か 但しは神代を松代姫 容貌麗しきブラワ゛ーダ 姿やさしき姫君に 玉の御声をかけられて 胸轟きし愚かさよ かくなる上は伊太彦も 唯惟神々々 神の経綸と畏みて ブラワ゛ーダ姫を娶りつつ 千代に八千代に玉椿 赤き縁を結びつつ 神の大道に仕ふべし 玉国別の師の君も 此消息を知りまさば

§ 2

I humbly, humbly beseech Thee.

Bazumaraaka sings again.

The Fumizuki that I have waited for so long, / Today is the twelfth day. / By the grace of the divine Mitama / Of the Lord God Kamususanō, / The joy of a thousand generations has come. / Oh Itahiko of Ananai-kyō, / Though she be foolish, take pity on my daughter, / Burawaada-hime. / As a wife of the house that shall not change for a thousand generations, / Please take her as your bride, and together, / Like a pair of mandarin ducks, may you live in harmony. / Following the way of the Kami, in the Shingefu of pushing open / The Ihayado, may you serve with purity. / Though I am but an Oiki, / Because your Mikoto has come, / My heart is emboldened, and somehow / I have become happy and joyful. / Your Mikoto descends from / The Mototsukuni of the Amatsu-hi.

You resemble the Kami of Asahi well. / Spring has come to Teru-no-sato, / And it has become an age where the flowers of Toha bloom. / Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. / The deep blessings of the Sumekami / Of Ananai-kyō, I now once again / Rejoice in and offer my thanks. / Whether the Asahi shines or clouds over, / Whether the moon waxes or wanes, / Even if the Daichi should sink, / This Enishi shall never change. / Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. / I celebrate the Fuyu of the Mitama.

Burawaada sings again.

This truly Arigata day of today / Is like the feeling of the eternal spring / When the Ama-no-Iwato was opened. / Pushing the pole into the Ama-no-Kawahara, / I bow down and worship the dignified face / Of the Hikoboshi who has descended from heaven. / Even the budding flowers have received the dew, / And now the time to bloom has come. / Oh my parents, oh my lord, / Have pity on my sincere heart, / And bless me with Tokiwa and Kahiwa. / Serving my two parents well, / Without disobeying the words of my brother, / Together with my lord, / I shall stand against Mount Suraya, / And by speaking to Ubananda / Naagarashaa, who is known in the world / As the foremost of the Eight Great Dragon Kings, / Before the presence of the Sumekami, / I shall raise a song of victory and dedicate my achievements

To be handed down for a thousand and eight thousand generations. / Please bless me, Kamunagara. / I offer my thanks before the presence / Of the Lord God Kamususanō. / The Kaminaohi who created this world, / And the Ōnaohi whose heart is vast. / Truly, in all things, the world of man / Should be looked upon and listened to again by the Naohi. / The teachings of Ananai-kyō, / Together with my lord, the official Itahiko, / Combining our hearts and our strength / Into one, to the eighty countries / And the eighty islands, without leaving a corner, / Please protect our departure to open and spread them, Kamunagara. / I humbly, humbly beseech Thee.

Kaakusu sings again.

Kaakusu, who lives on the edge / Of the forests of Mount Sutaruma, / Protected by the blessings of the Kami, / Together with the official Itahiko, / In Teru-no-sato where the flowers of eternal life bloom, / Standing against the Ruubuya mansion, / I have received treatment I never expected; / How Arigata it is. / Not only that, but the divine official / Of Itahiko is Burawaada-hime, / And becoming the wife of her Mikoto, / To spread the most sacred teachings / To the world, she stands against Mount Suraya / And intends to establish merit. / We, who have served as the attendants / To the messenger of the infinite divine power, / The official of Itsu, / Feel as if we are ascending to heaven, / And we have offered our thanks for the divine majesty.

With all our hearts and all our bodies, / We have become those who are happy / To devote ourselves to this world. / Oh Great Kami of Ananai-kyō, / Though we are bodies stained with sin, / Look upon us with your great heart, / Forgive us, and use us / For the unprecedented Shingefu, Kamunagara. / Offering our thanks before your presence, / We celebrate the joy of the Ruubuya mansion / And beseech Thee. / Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. / May the Mitama be blessed.

Beesu sings again.

Joyous, joyous, most joyous. / Flowers have bloomed on the withered tree. / Even the fig, which was deeply wrapped / In the treetops, has now already / Begun to open like the Udumbara; / It has become a joyous spring. / Having obtained the jewel of Mount Suraya / As a protection from the Kami, / I thought I would spend my whole life / In comfort and ease, / But now, looking back / Deeply upon it, how shameful it is. / From now on, I shall follow / The precious official Itahiko, / And with a heart of red sincerity, / I shall serve the great Shingefu. / For the sake of the Kami and for the sake of the world, / If I receive the jewel of Naagarashaa, there will be no calamity. / Bound by the desires of my own body,

And trying to obtain that jewel, / The number of people who have had their precious lives / Taken is countless until now. / It is truly a regrettable state of affairs. / Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. / We, who have begun to realize / The great path of the Kami in our lives, / Are no longer the people of yesterday. / We are the precious children of the Kami, / Wrapped in the blessings of the Kami. / Ah, Arigata, Arigata. / Ruubuya-sama, Bazumaraaka, / And Princess Burawaada, / I humbly ask for your favor. / As soon as possible, may the Mikoto of my brother / Asumagaruda return, / And I shall tell him of this state of affairs,

And together, holding hands in joy, / While adorning the jewel-like ship, / Following the official Itahiko, / I count the days and wait in anticipation / For the day we climb Mount Suraya. / Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. / May the Mitama be blessed.

Ruubuya: 'The Ama-no-Iwato that was closed has opened, / And a joyous spring has come to my house.'

Bazumaraaka: 'This day of the Fumizuki, which I waited for so long, / Is filled with the blessings of the divine Mitama.'

Itahiko: 'Manipulated by the threads of the Sumekami's providence, / We have tied the threads of Enishi.'

Burawaada: 'I am happy that I have been given / The lord who is the official of the Kami, for whom I waited so long.'

Kaakusu: 'The joy of being a person who encounters such an age / Makes me feel as if it is the eternal spring.'

Beesu: 'The blessings of the Kami have become so precious / That there is no way to praise them enough.'
