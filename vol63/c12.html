Chapter 12: 五託宣 [1619]

五託宣〔1619〕

§ 1

Kākusu and Bēsu, both harboring feelings of suspicion, stood by a standing stone that rose straight up by the roadside, listening intently to the sound of the Sendenka drawing near.

Itahiko: 'A Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, I am Itahiko-tsukasa. Following Tamakuniwake, To the foot of Mount Sudaruma, As we advanced, Meeting by chance two people resting in the shade of a tree, And hearing that a Yakuō-no-tama exists on the mountain of Sūraya, My heart leaped with courage. Permitted by my master, I brought Kākusu and Bēsu along, Slipped through the mountain path, Stopped by the Rūbuya mansion in the village of Teru on the shores of Lake Sūraya, And having finished tying the Enishi with Hime-no-mikoto, Who is the Burawāda of the Kami's Shigumi, I boarded the ship of Asumagaruda, which acts as an elder brother, Drifted upon the waves, and at last, Rowed to Mount Sūraya. As we spent the night, A monster of unknown identity, Suddenly appeared here, And began a Mondafu of deep Kiyōmi, Neither winning nor losing, And as we scattered sparks, The monster turned into smoke and vanished. When the night had finally dawned, The five of us in the party, Broke through the dangerous death line of Sūraya, And with aching feet, climbed to the summit. Facing the ugly rock cave, Where Ubananda Nāgarashā lurks, I cut the wisteria vines growing thick on the mountain ridge, And made a rope ladder at last, Lowering it down. When the five of us slid down at once, To our surprise, A vast hole with no known end, Spread out without limit, And became a wide wilderness, Where mountains, rivers, grasses, and trees stood in rows. Ah, Kannagara-kami-kami, We who are led by the jewel-thread of the Kami's Shigumi, Wondering where to aim, Shall advance as far as the white clouds go. Having come this far and resting our breath, When I look back and gaze, How did it happen, Kākusu and Bēsu, Have fallen behind, And their figures are no longer to be seen. Ah, Kannagara-kami-kami, May the spirits be blessed, And may you grant the joy, That both of them may quickly, Follow in our footsteps, And that we may celebrate our safety together. Though the entrance to the ugly rock cave, Feels very narrow, How mysterious it is that this rock cave, Has spread out so widely. Though the sky is enclosed by the rock cave, And the light of the sun and moon cannot be seen, Yet somehow, the feeling is pleasant. Ah, Kannagara-kami-kami, I advance according to the will of the Kami.'

Saying this, he approached the two of them. Seeing the three figures, Kākusu was so happy he could have flown, and said,

Kākusu: 'Ah, was it you, Sensei? Burawāda-sama, Asumagaruda-sama, I cannot tell you how much we have been searching for you. How have you been?'

Itahiko: 'No, thank you. In truth, because I could not see the two of you, I thought perhaps you had hurt your feet again and were lagging behind, so I stopped by the roadside again and again, waiting and looking, and that is why I was so late. I must have kept you waiting quite a while.'

Kākusu: 'We waited quite a while. However, this is a strange place. Just a moment ago, a great river with surging muddy waters lay across our path, and a filthy old woman appeared and started spouting all sorts of threatening words, so Bēsu and I were negotiating with her with all our might, but that old woman turned into a rock, and the river became a field of pampas grass. I wonder if this is the afterlife.'

Itahiko: 'You have no memory of dying, so how could you have come to the afterlife? This is a large open space that has unfolded just like this from the rock cave of Mount Sūraya. Because it is such a wide hole, the grassland has unfolded to the point where the eye cannot reach. Is it not a mysterious thing?'

Kākusu: 'No, I am relieved to hear that. I was worried that Bēsu and I were on a journey to the afterlife, and I cannot tell you how much I fretted. Hey, Bēsu, that was quite unpleasant, wasn't it?'

Itahiko: 'Ah, was that so? Since this is inside a rocky cavern, the scenery of the surroundings is also different, and I suppose monsters will appear as well. Come, let us go deeper from here. Surely, Ubananda Riuō must be waiting, holding the jewel.'

As they sang this and proceeded, a house with a thatched roof stood at the foot of a bald mountain. The roof was leaking in places, forming many valleys where blue grass grew, and the bamboo base was exposing its bones. 'What a strange house this is,' the group stood still, tilting their heads in thought.

There, an old woman appeared suddenly, rattling the broken door, with a mottled face like Atlas,

Old Woman: 'Hey there, traveler, please stop by for a moment. I would like to offer you some bitter tea... You must be quite exhausted. It seems that parasites of mud and dust are swarming on your straw sandals. Your feet must be very heavy.'

Itahiko: 'Yes, thank you. However, I am truly sorry to decline your kindness, but as we are on a journey that requires some haste, I shall accept your hospitality on our way back.'

Old Woman: 'Hey there, you have poor judgment. When this old woman kindly offers to give you tea, why do you decline? Even if you say it is a hurried journey, you cannot walk for a whole day. Rest here and go. I shall let you hear the story of the Sango-kyō, which is excellent, truly excellent.'

Itahiko: 'Are you a person of the Sango-kyō? Actually, I am also a missionary of the Sango-kyō.'

Old Woman: 'How poor your eyesight is. Even looking at my face, you should be able to tell whether I am a divine minister or not. In truth, I am of the lineage of Takahime of the Sango-kyō, a hensei-danshi, and a living shrine of Hinodewake, but I have grown weary of the ignorance of Kotowake-wake and Tōsuke, and I am preaching the Way here, having opened the old Uranai-kyō. Well, well, listen and go. I will never tell you anything bad.'
