Chapter 11: Monster Way [1618]

怪道〔1618〕

§ 1

Karks and Base, the two of them, walk along trudgingly in silence through the middle of a vast plain where the scenery of the Atari has suddenly changed, as if pushed by someone or something. As they follow the foot of a small, bun-shaped hill with a bald top, they find pine and cherry trees standing together as if they were a single stock, looking intimate. It appears to be a winter scene, and sharp pine needles are shaken by the wind, falling one after another onto the heads of the two. The cherry tree has become completely naked, its branches trembling in the cold wind.

Karks: 'Hey, Base, we are certain that we crossed the death line of Suraya and entered the rock cave of Riuō together with Itahiko-kamutsukasa, yet it is strange that we have come to such a place before we knew it. And when I stood there, it was still the end of summer, but I wonder how winter has come so suddenly. It is a thing that does not make sense.'

Base: 'Hmm, that is so, it is a thing that makes no sense at all. Perhaps we were dreaming. It may be that while I was working as a woodcutter on the mountainside of Sudarma, I fell into a deep sleep, and winter came during that time.'

Karks: 'If that were the case, I still have clear memories of going to the village of Teru with the beautiful Kamutsukasa named Itahiko, staying at the mansion of Rūbuya-sama, receiving a magnificent feast, and then boarding a ship to cross the death line of Mount Suraya. Perhaps even now is a dream. A dream is something that shows you everything from birth to death in a mere five or six minutes; since a dream is an extension of thought, perhaps this state of being is also a dream. After all, they do say that this world is a floating dream.'

Base: 'Whatever one may say, this is a place I do not recognize. I wonder when we came here from Mount Suraya. And the scenery of the Atari is that of winter. Needle-like withered pine needles are falling from between the old pine trees. The cherry tree has become stark naked and is trembling. In any case, let us go as far as we can. Perhaps there will be something good yet.'

Base: 'Little did I think / That I would end up like this / Advancing into the rock cave / Of Mount Suraya. / If this be a dream / Then let it awaken quickly / Clearing the clouds / From the sky of my heart.'

Karks: 'The vast sky is all / Wrapped in black clouds / And I am sad / Not knowing the way ahead. / Is it because we met / The warnings of Uralhiko-no-Ōkami / That we are now lost / The two of us?'

Base: 'The teachings of Uralhiko-no-Ōkami / And the way of Sango / Are both teachings created by the Lord God. / I am now at my wit's end / And set out on a journey / In the lonely winter field. / Where are Itahiko and Burawāda-hime now? / Even the shadow of Asumagaruda cannot be seen.'

Karks: 'Wrapped in the darkness / Of the heart of the village, / We must be wandering / At the crossroads now. / O Great Ōkami of Heaven and Earth, have mercy / And illuminate the path for us two. / How lonely is our heart / Wandering in the winter field / Where there is neither moon, nor sun, nor starlight. / How shall we return / To my hometown / Where the flowers of the eternal spring bloom?'

Base: 'In this world where / Both sun and moon incline to the west, / I wander in a lonely wilderness. / Whether I have come west or east I know not, / But I shall go on, binding my sins. / Not distinguishing west, east, south, or north, / I have now become like a child.'

Karks: 'Well, it cannot be helped. There is a saying that even a dog will hit a stick if it walks. Come, let us continue to walk this path as long as our legs will carry us.'

Here, the two decided to sing songs as they pleased to dispel the loneliness of the field where the cold wind blew, and they proceeded trudgingly as their feet led them.

Karks: 'Ah, how mysterious, how mysterious / Is this the underworld or the crossroads? / Or is it the middle of the floating world? / When I look at the scenery of the Atari / The plants and trees of the mountains and fields have withered away / And there is a loneliness where even dew does not dwell. / I approach the shade of a pine tree / And look up to catch my breath / When I see the withered leaves falling with a rustle / Descending like needles / Piercing through my thin clothes / And the branches of the cherry tree are trembling / Shivering in the cold wind. / This journey that does not make sense / Is it a dream, reality, or an illusion? / With Itahiko of the Sango-kyō / I crossed the mountain path of Sudarma / And finally reached the village of Teru / Stopping at the Rūbuya mansion / Where Burawāda, like a celestial maiden...'

Hime-no-inochi treated me with hospitality, and from there I entrusted my body to the ship. That the five of us in the party thought to receive the Haugiyoku of Naganarasha, which rests in the mountains of Suraya, for the sake of the Kami and for the sake of the world, and to offer it to the sacred Seijiyō of Chin—was this but a dream? It is all a matter that defies Gaten. Where does the Adebito who appeared in my dream reside now? There is no way to inquire, and I can only weep. The sword of frost and the jewel of dew press upon my body. Is this truly the retribution for the sins I have committed until now? Or is it merely the Innen of a Zense? Truly, the sky of Konnichi is terrifying, and my Hizakurige cannot advance.

Wandering without a destination, am I advancing toward the village of Jigoku? Or am I ascending to the Tengoku Jiyōdo where the flowers of Tokoyo bloom? We, who are not Kami, have no Sensube but to shed tears. The suffering of being lost on a Yamiji! Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! May the light of the Kami illuminate our bodies, even for an Ichiji, as quickly as possible.

Base: 'The sun does not shine, the moon does not rise, and the sky is one where even the shadows of the stars cannot be seen. Like Maujiya, we follow a path we do not recognize, advancing without a destination. Is the place I am going to Tengoku? Or is it the Seichi of Jerusalem, Ōgonzan, or Yachimata? Wrapped in deep Nōmu, it is the feeling of a ship sailing on the vast ocean without knowing its destination. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! If the Kami exist in heaven and earth, have pity on the circumstances of us two. Whether this is the Genkai, the Reikai, or Tengoku, or perhaps Jigoku or Yachimata, make it known to us clearly. We have heard the teaching that man is the child of Kami and the palace of Kami, but my spirit, which has wandered so far, is like the darkness of the eternal night. I did not think the light of the moon and sun was so precious and pure. We, too, have at last begun to correctly realize the preciousness of the Izuno-omikari, the Mizu-mitama, and the light of the moon. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! O Sumegami who created us, sweep away the ugly horizontal clouds in our chests as quickly as an Ichiji, and illuminate our path completely and in detail. Kamunagara. We pray before the presence of the Kami.'

Singing thus, they finally arrived at the riverbank where the muddy current was surging.

Karks: 'Oi, there is no rain here, yet a terrible muddy current is flowing, is it not? If one were to try to cross such a large river, that would truly be selling one's life cheaply. There is no help for it now. It is not the twentieth century, but everything has come to a dead end. Shall we turn back?'

Base: 'Even if you think to turn back, there is no help for it, as someone or something is pushing us from behind. It is written in the scriptures of the Ananai-kyō that "if one becomes arrogant, one will encounter the trials of the Kami and be unable to either go or return," but after all, we have fallen into this plight as a result of slighting the Kami by talking of socialism and liberal egalitarianism. In any case, this cannot be thought of as the Genkai. We had our lives taken at the rock cave of the Ryūjin and came here. Now that it has come to this, there is no choice but to be prepared.'

Karks: 'That is right. No matter how I think about it, it does not seem like the Genkai. As you say, from here on, we must straighten the head of our horse, and since we must not be weak, even if we are to go to Jigoku, let us exert great horse-power, increase our speed, threaten the demons of Jigoku, make them bite their tongues, and build a republic, shall we? In any case, in the world of Konnichi, one cannot stand if one is weak.'

Base: 'Even if you say that, with only the two of us, we will not be able to conquer Jigoku. What will you do if there is a fellow called King Enma who flips through a ledger, reads out our crimes one by one, and tries to drop us into the Shōnetsu-jigoku? In any case, we have not lived in a way that would allow us to go to Tengoku.'

Karks: 'Do not worry. A place called Jigoku is a world where the strong win. Small villains receive severe punishments, but great villains become kings of Jigoku, use the many Maujiya as they please, and live a pleasant life. There is no such thing as an Enma. The Reikai and the Genkai are the same thing. Think of the state of the Genkai. It is a world of contradiction and darkness where, if you are below and cause disorder, you are punished, and if you are above and cause disorder, you are respected by the people. We must not be weak. From now on, let us tighten our loincloths, wear twisted headbands, put strength into our thin arms, cross this muddy current to the other side, and set out to conquer Jigoku. It is said that the human spirit resides in Tengoku or Jigoku depending on the love of its master, so what does it matter which Jigoku it is? It is like returning to one's own registered domicile. Let us wield violent power one after another, conquer the small groups around us, create a great union, and build the Karks-Base Kingdom. What, would I be intimidated by a mere Jigoku? No matter how painful they say Jigoku is, it is about the same as the Genkai. Since they say the Genkai is a reflection of what is called Jigoku, we have experience. In the Genkai, there is a general called Daikoku-nushi, so things do not go as we wish, but in Jigoku, we are free. If I have this one arm, I can do anything.'

Base: 'Well then. It seems to be the eighth ward of Jigoku. It is a matter of whether one takes it or sees it. Karkus: 'Ha ha ha ha ha. What a filthy old woman, is she not? It is disgusting even to speak to her. No matter what you say, I am Karkus Ōsama, the Gauketsu Hiyōrokudama of Tenka, you know.'

Karkus grabs the old woman's legs from behind and pushes and shoves with all his might, but she does not budge an inch, like a rock grown from the earth.

Karkus: 'Hey, what a strong-waisted, tough old woman this is.'

Base: 'Hey, Obaa-sama, will you not let go? I am running out of breath.'

Old Woman: 'That is a settled matter. I am grabbing you so that your breath will run out. I will make you run out of breath, beat you to a pulp like beating a gamecock, coat you in sand, make meat dumplings, and eat you up. Now that it has come to this, your time to pay the land tax in this world is over. You had better be prepared with a clean heart.'

The two are at a loss, and just as they are fretting over what to do, a Sendenka is heard from far behind. The moment they think 'Ha!', what had appeared to be an old woman until now was a giant rock by the river. What had appeared to be a river was an endless field of pampas grass, and the ears of the pampas grass were swaying in the wind, appearing as water.

原文（近代文語）

ベース『さうだなア。どうやら地獄の八丁目らしい。取つたか見たかだ。此濁流を横ぎり、其勢で地獄に侵入し、一つ脅喝的手段を弄して粟散鬼王を平げ、天晴地獄界の勇者となるも妙だ。ヤア勇ましくなつて来た。毒を喰へば皿迄だ。どうせ吾々は天国代物ぢやないからなア。アハヽヽヽ』

斯両人は河端に佇み泡沫の如き望みを抱いて雄健びして居る。傍の生へ茂つた茅の中の藁小屋から黒い痩こけた怪しい婆が破れた茣蓙を肩にかけ、ガサリガサリと萱草を揺りながら二人の前に出て左の手に榎の杖を携へた儘、

婆『誰だ誰だ、あた矢釜しい。そんな大きな声で喋り散らすと、俺の耳が蛸になるわい。貴様はどこの兵六玉だ。一寸こちらへ来い』

カークス『ハヽヽヽヽ。何とまア汚い婆もあつたものぢやないかい。物を言ふも汚らはしいわい。何と云つても天下の豪傑兵六玉のカークス王様だからなア』

婆『ヘン、人の見ぬ所でそつと猫婆を極め込み、欲な事計り致し、何も彼も人の前にカークス爺だらう。も一匹の奴は何と云ふ兵六玉だい』

ベール『此方は失敬ながら月の国にて名も高きベース様だよ』

婆『成程どいつも此奴も人気の悪い面つきだなア。ベースをカークスやうな其哀れつぽいスタイルは何だ。此処は三途の川の渡船場だ。サアこれから貴様の衣類万端剥取つてやらう。覚悟を致したがよいぞい。今の先、伊太彦、ブラワ゛ーダの若夫婦が嬉しさうに手を引いて此所を通りよつた。さうして馬鹿面をした、何でも兄貴と見えるが、アスマガルダと云ふ奴が妹や妹の婿の僕となつて通りよつたぞや』

カークス『何、伊太彦さまが此所を通られたと云ふのか。何ぞ立派な玉でも持つて居られただらうなア』

婆『玉は沢山持つて居つたよ。粟粒のやうな小つぽけな肝玉やら縮こまつた睾丸やらどん栗のやうな目の玉やらをぶらさげて、悄気かへつて此処を通りよつた。真裸にしてやらうと思つたが貴様等とは余程御霊がよいので、此婆も手をかける事が出来ず、此萱の中に隠れてそつと見て居つたら、綺麗なナイスに手を引かれ、あの川の真中を通りよつた。大方天国へ往くのだらう。併し乍ら貴様達は此婆の手を経て、三途の川を渡らずに一途の河を渡り、直様地獄へ突き落される代物だ。ても扨ても憐れなものぢやわいのう、オンオンオン』

ベース『ヤア此奴ア、グヅグヅしては居られない。此婆を突つ倒かして置いて此河を渡り、一つ地獄征服と出かけようか、カークス来れ』

と早くも尻引き捲り、濁流目蒐けて渡らうとする。婆は細い痩せこけた手を出して、ベースの胸座を取り、三つ四つ揺する。

ベース『これや婆、どうするのだい。失敬な、人の胸座を取りやがつて』

婆『取らいでかい取らいでかい、貴様の肝玉を引き抜いてやるのだ。こら其処な兵六玉、貴様も同様だから待つて居れ。この婆が此所で荒料理をして骨も肉も付け焼にして食つてやるのだ。大分腹が減つた所へよい餌が来たものだ』

カークスは後より婆の足をグツと掴み力限り突けども押せども、地から生えた岩のやうにビクとも動かない。

カークス『ヤア何と腰の強い、強太い婆だな』

婆『定つた事だよ。俺は地の底から生えたお岩と云ふ幽霊婆だ。兵六玉の十匹や二十匹集かつて来た所でビクとも動くものかい』

ベース『こら婆アさま、放さぬかい。俺の息が切れるぢやないか』

婆『定まつた事だい。息の切れるやうに掴んで居るのだ。息を切らして軍鶏を叩くやうに叩きつぶし、砂にまぶし、肉団子をこしらへて食つて仕舞ふのだ。こうなつたら貴様達ももう娑婆の年貢の納め時だ。潔う覚悟をして居るがよい』

二人は進退谷まり、如何はせむかと案じ煩ふ折柄、遥か後の方から、宣伝歌が聞えて来た。ハツと思ふ途端、今迄婆と見えたのは河の傍の巨巌であつた。川と見えたのは果しも知られぬ薄原で、其薄の穂が風に揺られて水と見えて居つたのであつた。
