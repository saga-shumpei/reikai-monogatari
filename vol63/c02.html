Chapter 2: 妙法 Mountain [1609]

妙法山〔1609〕

§ 1

At the entrance of the mountain path of Mount Sudaruma, where the summer trees grow thick and the greenery drips, two men, Ko and Otsu, were sitting and resting their hands from woodcutting, indulging in idle chatter.

Ko: 'Hey Aniki, I wish we could at least live a life like other people. We venture deep into the mountains from morning till night, and even though we do nothing but woodcutting, we only wring out our sweat; it is just like a hammer flowing down a river, and there is no way for us to get ahead, is there? People today consider a cultural life to be the supreme way of living, and I wish we could also enter into this cultural life that conquers nature and live a life of ease.'

Otsu: 'We intend to divert this thing called cultural life and apply it to the problem of personality. Brahmanists say such carefree things as "blind passions are enlightenment," but that is something said by people of high spiritual roots who have reached the state of enlightenment, and for ordinary people, it cannot be taken so lightly. It is impossible to polish one's personality and improve it that way. One must constantly introspect and reflect, and conquer one's physical instincts. It goes without saying that being natural in both spirit and body is ideal, but it is difficult to make these two run in parallel. In the sacred words of the Mizu no Mitama, it is taught: "It is difficult for those rich in bodily desires to enter the Kingdom of God. It would be easier to drain the great ocean with a clam than for a wealthy person to enter the Kingdom of God. A man cannot serve two masters; therefore, a man cannot serve both God and bodily desires." This is indeed a profound teaching, is it not?'

Ko: 'Is that not a bit hard to understand, even for God? Think about it carefully. Poor people like us—it might be disrespectful to say we study the scriptures, but we do not have the leisure to chant them and polish our souls. However, if one is a wealthy person, one has the time to live in leisure every day, and since one has the convenience of being able to chant the scriptures freely and master their secret meanings, is it not a natural conclusion that those who enter the Kingdom of God are the wealthy?'

Otsu: 'That may be so, but human beings do not go as we think. People in their prime of success are not the type to have such virtuous thoughts. As the saying goes, "When the house is poor, one thinks of one's parents," it is people like us who seek spiritual comfort and try to improve and cling to God; yet, even for poor people like us, such good thoughts do not easily arise in times of success. Of course, it is not that the Mizu no Mitama-sama ignores wealth itself in an absolute sense, but the reason such a teaching must be given is that the weakness of human beings is that they easily become slaves to material things. Even when seeking the same wealth, there must be a difference as vast as heaven and earth between seeking it to satisfy one's own desires and seeking it to walk the Great Way of God, both in its content and its spirit. There is absolutely no logic by which material things are unnecessary in a life as a human being. It is ultimately impossible to abandon the economic perspective entirely. Nevertheless, seeing that all the propagandists of the divine teachings are in unison in heightening asceticism and the doctrine of few desires, I think we must discover some deep meaning in that.'

Ko: 'There seems to be some truth in your argument. However, we must somehow make an effort to live at least a life like other people; as they say, "When the granaries are full, one knows etiquette; when food and clothing are sufficient, one walks the Way," so to live only a physical life is truly a pathetic story for a human being endowed with a body. For me, it is "Give me bread first, and then I shall walk the Great Way."'

Ko: 'There seems to be some truth in your argument. However, we must somehow make an effort to live at least a life like other people; as they say, "When the granaries are full, one knows etiquette; when food and clothing are sufficient, one walks the Way," so to live only a physical life is truly a pathetic story for a human being endowed with a body. For me, it is "Give me bread first, and then I shall walk the Great Way."'

甲『オイ兄貴、吾々もせめて人並の生活が為たいものだなア。朝から晩まで山深く分け入つて、杣ばかりやつて居つても汗を搾る許りで何時も金槌の川流れ同様、天窓の上りやうが無いぢや無いか。今日の人間は文化生活を以て最上の処世法としてゐるが、吾々も自然とやらを征服する文化生活に入つて安楽な生涯を送りたいものだなア』

乙『吾々は文化生活といふものを転用して人格問題に当てたいと思ふのだ。バラモン教徒は煩悩即菩提だなどと気楽さうな事を言つてゐるが、夫は悟道の境地に立至つた上根の人間の言ふことで普通の人間はソンナ軽々しい訳には行かぬ。迚も人格を磨いて向上する事は不可能事だよ。絶えず内観自省して、肉的本能を征服しておかねばならない。霊体共に自然であることは無論だがこの両者を並行さす事は困難だ。瑞の御霊の聖言には、「体欲に富める者は神の御国に入ること難し。富貴の人の神の国に入るよりは蛤を以て大海を替へ干す方却て易かるべし。人は二人の主人に仕ふること能はず、故に人も神と体欲とに兼仕ふることを得ず」と教へられてある。実に深遠なる教訓ではあるまいかなア』

甲『神さまもチト判らぬぢや無いかエーン。よく考へて見よ。吾々の様な貧乏人は聖典を研究いな研究と言つては勿体ないかも知らぬ、拝誦して心魂を磨く余裕がないが、富者となれば日々遊んで暮す暇が在るのだから、自由自在に聖典を拝誦したり、又その密意を極め得るの便宜があるから、神の国に入るものは、富者であることは当然の帰結ではないか』

乙『ソウ言へばさうだが人間と言ふものは吾々の考へ通りにゆくものではない。得意時代の人間は到底そんな殊勝な考への浮ぶものでは無いよ。「家貧しうして親を思ふ」とか謂つて、吾々の様なものこそ、精神上の慰安を求め、向上もし神に縋らむとするものだが、容易に得意時代には貧乏人の吾々だとて、そんな好い考へは起るものではないよ。勿論瑞の御魂様だとて、絶対的に富そのものを無視された訳では無からうが、斯んな教訓を与へなくては成らない所以は、人間の弱点といふものは凡て物質の奴隷となり易いからだ。同じ富を求むるにしても、我欲心を満足さすために求むるものと、神の大道を行はむがために行ふものとは、其内容に於てもその精神に於ても、天地霄壤の相違があるだらう。苟くも人間としての生活に、物質が不必用なる道理は絶対にない。何処までも経済観念を放擲することは所詮不可能だ。然るに凡ての神教の宣伝使が、口を揃へて禁欲主義や寡欲主義を高潮して居る所を見ると、其処に何等かの深意を発見せなくてはなるまいと思ふのだ』

甲『君の説にも一理あるやうだ。然し吾々は何とか努力して人並みの生活だけは為なくてはならないが、「倉廩充ちて礼節を知り、衣食足りて道を歩む」とか言ふから、肉的生活のみでは肉体を具へた人間としては実に腑甲斐ない話だ。吾に「先づパンを与へよ、然して後に大道を歩まむ」だからなア』

甲『君の説にも一理あるやうだ。然し吾々は何とか努力して人並みの生活だけはせなくてはならないが、「倉廩充ちて礼節を知り、衣食足りて道を歩む」とかいふから、肉的生活のみでは、肉体を具へた人間としては実に腑甲斐ない話だ。吾に「先づパンを与へよ、然して後に大道を歩まむ」だからなア』

§ 2

Otsu: 'We are starting to want to say that "man does not live by bread alone, nor does he live by spirit alone." However, Jikaku as a Ningen is necessary there.'

Kō: 'Jikaku is necessary, but I wonder if the Jikaku of the modern Ningen truly stands on a foundation higher than that of the average person. Does it stand on Reiteki Jikaku? That is what I cannot help but feel Kiiu about. The Jikaku that the Ningen of Konnichi advocate is, after all, merely of the Tsukinami-shiki Jikakusama.'

Otsu: 'If it is, as you say, an Iumeimujitsu Jikaku, a Tsukinami Jikaku, then Jikaku and Jikaku will immediately collide with each other, and Heiwa will be disturbed. The fact that there is no end to the Shijiu Tōsau in every Kakuhaumen, as in the Gendai, is likely due to the Futetsutei of Jikaku. However, since everything has a Jiyunjiyo and a Kaidan, there is also the point that at the Taushiyo of Jikaku, some degree of Dōeu and Tōsau are unavoidable.'

Kō: 'That is why I am Monota and cannot Haiki-katsugō toward the Jikakusama of the Gendai. While they make a clamor about the equality of personality and the respect for individuality, their attitude as a matter of fact is truly ugly and unbearable. Although there may be one or two Ningen with a splendid attitude among them, when viewed generally, they are all Ningen I cannot approve of.'

Otsu: 'Hmm, that is also true. The equality of personality that the Gendai-jin advocate is truly suspicious. I am one who recognizes that fact well. When they speak of the equality of personality, many of them try to pull those of high rank down to a lower place, saying, "You and I are of equal rank, we are both bunrei and bunshin of the same God," or worse, they show their backsides before those of the upper class or government officials and think that is what it means to act haughty. Yet, when they are treated in the same way by Ningen of lower rank than themselves, they are all people who get angry, saying, "Do not make a fool of me, you have insulted me." When they speak of freedom, they are all people who think that it means abandoning an unpleasant spouse to elope with a lover, or trampling upon law, morality, duty, and human feelings. And yet, if one were to ask if they grant freedom to others when they are in a position to do so, the fact is that they do exactly the opposite.'

Otsu: 'If they were to shout about anarchism, one would think they might even welcome a thief if one were to break into their house, but they are all people who go straight to the police station to report it. Because they are clamorously asserting that "the economic organization must be made into communism," when one says, "Then start by throwing away your own property," they are all people who make a face as if to say, "That is absolutely out of the question," and pretend to know nothing, trying to make others give up their property to achieve communism. There is a story that when two young socialism worshippers went to eat sukiyaki with chicken and were paying their respective shares, one of them said to the other, "I do not have enough money, so please pay for me using your money," and when the other refused, saying, "I cannot do such a thing," the first one retorted, "Does that not contradict your usual assertions?" to which the other replied, "No, that and this are separate issues," and ran away. In any case, Ningen are creatures who make various demands of others, but what would happen if those demands were made of themselves? I wonder if they have the preparedness to respond. It is not the real thing to shout for communism just because one's own position is in the Musan-kaikyu. With the tip of a pen or tongue, one can say anything splendidly, but can one execute it when the event actually falls upon oneself? It is a fact that in ten cases out of ten, they are all words without action, and it is quite common that they end up having to abolish their daily assertions.'

乙『「人はパンのみにて生くるものではないと共に霊のみにて生くるものにあらず」と吾々も言ひたくなつて来るのだ。併しそこは人間としての自覚が必要だ』

甲『自覚も必要だが、現代の人間の自覚なるものは果して人並以上に立脚して居るだらうか。霊的自覚に立つて居るだらうか。それが僕には杞憂されて成らないのだ。今日の人間の唱ふる自覚といふ奴は月並式の自覚様だからなア』

アナーキズムを叫ぶ位なら、自分の家に泥坊が這入つても歓待して行りさうなものだのに、真先に警察署に訴へに行く奴許りだ。「経済組織は、コンミュニズムに為なくては可けない」と言つて、八釜しく主張して居るから、「それなら先づ君の財産から放り出せ」と言ふと、『それは真平御免』と言ふやうな面付きで素知らぬ顔をして、他人に出させて共産にしようと言ふ奴計りだ。ある二人の青年ソシアリズム崇拝者が鶏肉のすき焼を食ひにいつて其割前を支払ふ時に相手の一人が「金が足りないから、君の金を出して支払つて済ませて呉れ」と言つたら、「ソンナ事は出来ない」と断つたので相手の一人が、「夫れは君の平素の主張に悖るでないか」と突込むと、「いや夫れと是とは別問題だ」と言つて逃げて仕舞つたといふ話だ。兎角人間といふ奴は人に対しては種々の要求を起すが、その要求を自分にされたら何うだらう。果して応ずるだけの覚悟を以て居るだらうか。自分の立場が無産階級にあるからと言つて共産主義を叫ぶのでは本当のもので無い。筆や舌の尖では何んな事でも立派に言はれるが、事実その事件が自分の身に降りかかつた時に実行することが出来るだらうか。十中の十まで有言不実行で、日頃の主張を撤廃せなくてはならぬやうになるのは、可なり沢山な事実だからなア』

§ 3

Kō: 'It is truly a world filled only with ninjiu of kiyogi and kiyoshiyoku. When I think about it this way, one might as well say that there is not a single person in the sekai who is a true ningen. The bundan meishi Kattedero was a well-known jiiushisauka in the seken, but while he was away on a riyokau to Armenia, his niyōbau, Fujin Kōru, took a young and handsome aijin named Uyurusu. When Kattedero discovered that they had been frequently exchanging tegami and asked for the shinsau, Fujin Kōru answered him clearly and calmly, "Since I no longer have love for you, I intend to practice and kiukau my daily jiiushisau and go to my aijin." After discussing it in various ways, Kattedero-shi allowed their romance. However, once Fujin Kōru was no longer in the house, he began to think of the children and other matters, and finally abandoned the jiiushisau he had made his principle, seeking repentance from his beloved wife Fujin Kōru from a humanitarian standpoint and asking her to return. Is there anyone as selfish as a ningen? Hahaha!'

Otsu: 'Hey, you, can you not hear the voice of a propaganda song coming from over there? Listen for a moment. It seems to be saying something like, "The Lord God appears in the world to distinguish between good and evil."'

Kō: 'Hmm, it is indeed the voice of a propaganda song. And it is a song of the Sangyōkyō. Even we, as followers of the Uraru-kyō, feel a certain comfort when we hear the propaganda songs of the Sangyōkyō. Shall we wait here and have them give us a solution regarding the problems of life?'

Otsu: 'No matter how much they are propagandists of the Sangyōkyō, it will likely be useless. One cannot shake a sleeve that is not there, after all. Rather than that, shall we not use divine power to obtain the treasure in the rock cavern of the great serpent on Mount Sūraya? No matter how many great serpents there are, they should be no match for divine power.'

Kō: 'Even without relying on the Sangyōkyō, we should be able to ask the Ōkami-sama of Uraruhiko, whom we worship in our daily lives.'

Otsu: 'I have prayed to the Ōkami of the Uraru-kyō morning and evening, but it seems that Uraruhiko-sama of late has fallen into poverty and does not grant me any fortune at all. I worshipped it thinking it was a religion where one could obtain (urareru) much wealth, but since I began my faith, it has become a religion of resentment (urameshi-kyō) where one must suffer (uraneba naranu), and I have fallen below the horizon of society and come down to being a mere woodcutter. Therefore, I have already quit the Uraru-kyō. However, if they let me obtain the rare treasure of Mount Sūraya, I would not mind continuing my faith. Once I stand in the position of a wealthy person, I would like to slowly advocate for communism and try living a life like a normal ningen.'

Kō: 'Stop such dangerous talk, and why not be content with your current circumstances for a while and wait for the right time? No matter how much rare treasure you obtain, it will be of no use if you do not have your life, will it?'

As they were discussing this, the party of Tamakuniwake approached, keeping pace with the sound of the propaganda song.

The party of six—Tamakuniwake, Masumihiko, Michihiko, Debisu-hime, Itahiko, and Chidō-kōshi—had finally reached the entrance to the ascent of Mount Sutaruma. Itahiko, as usual, was in the lead, singing the propaganda song in a loud voice. Two men were sitting in the shade of a tree at the entrance to the mountain, whispering about something. Itahiko, quick to notice this, looked back and said,

Itahiko: 'Sensei, we encountered a petty thief in front of the shrine last night, but look at that. Two more petty thieves have appeared over there. Unlike the fellow from yesterday, they have faces that look somewhat clever.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Hmm, it does indeed look like two fine gentlemen are resting there. Those men are certainly not thieves.'

§ 4

Ita: 'Is there not a precept, even if it is not a saying of Brahmanism, that says, "If you see a person, think of them as a thief"?'

Tamakuni: 'You must have been so shaken by last night's thief that you have become mentally deranged, and now everything that meets your eyes looks like a thief. Do not say such reckless things. It is better to think that everyone you see is a Kamisama. Even if, by some one-in-a-million chance, they were a thief, you should interpret it with good will, thinking they are a master who is polishing our Mitama.'

Ita: 'Even if you say that, look closely. Are they not holding weapons that are shining brightly? That fellow might be a robber with a weapon. If you gain some courage and determine that they are a thief, you should kick them aside and advance. For a human-beast whose Seishin is paralyzed, a half-hearted treatment method will not work. It is best to administer a morphine injection or perform major surgery.'
