Chapter 4: Mountain 上訓 [1611]

山上訓〔1611〕

§ 1

Tamakuniwake and his party, at the foot of Mount Sudaruma, parted ways with Itahiko and his disciples, and while sweating profusely, climbed the steep slope known for its sound, under a scorching summer sun, comforted by the singing of the Kariyōbinga.

As far as the eye could see, both fields and mountains were colored in green, a summer landscape; they finally reached the summit, where the evening sky was nearing twilight, and they were weary of gazing at the view.

Masumi: 'Thanks to the divine grace, we have finally managed to climb this steep slope safely. Tonight, let us use the moon as our pillow, the grass as our bedding, and the blue sky as our quilt, and weave peaceful dreams. The sky is vast and clear without a single dark cloud; the stars are sparse, and the moonlight shines upon our party, protecting us—how grateful and joyful I feel. It is only because we are on a journey that we can be drenched in such magnificent dew of grace.'

Michi: 'It is truly joyful. To sleep on the summit of the pass of Mount Sudaruma, bathed in the moonlight, fills one with a truly refreshing feeling. The mountains and fields in all directions spread out wide and far, and to the southwest, Lake Sūraya shines in the moonlight like a mirror, as if it were heaven itself. Teacher, from here on it is a descent; shall we decide to sleep here tonight?'

Tamakuni: 'It is truly a grateful thing. Let us shelter from the rain for one night here, bathe in the dew of grace, and advance in both spirit and body toward heaven. However, first and foremost, let us fulfill our duty, offer words of gratitude to the Lord God, and then exchange stories at our leisure before entering the land of Kasho.'

Michi: 'If that is your wish, I am truly grateful.'

With that, the whole party, with voices raised high, resonated the echoes of the valleys of Mount Sudaruma and recited the Amatsu Norito; upon finishing, they took lotus seeds from their bosoms to replace their evening meal, and spent their time in conversation about all things, while also enjoying the composition of poems.

Tamakuniwake: 'The moon shining in the vast sky, when it tilts peacefully, will soon bring the dawn. This scenery, is it the Imperial Land of Heaven or Paradise? I have no words to compare it to.'

Masumihiko: 'When I come to the ridge of Mount Sudaruma and look, the moon shines ever higher. Though the stars in the clear sky shine sparsely, the moon alone knows the world.'

Michihiko: 'The stars have filled the vast sky; the light of the moon of fifteen nights has also filled heaven and earth. The divine majesty that fills all things, I alone have received it in my heart, Michihiko. Yet, I shall never forget the grace of Tamakuniwake, which shines across the heavenly sky.'

Harumichi: 'Together with the ministers of the God of three-five, it is a joy to go to the capital of Itsu. The crooked gods that dwell in the human heart have also fled and scattered in the moonlight.'

Debisuhime: 'Following the footsteps of my teacher, I have finally climbed this mountain path of love with my husband. When I look out, my hometown has become hazy, and only the snow of Mount Terumon is visible.'

Harumichi: 'Where are the ministers of the Bell and Bat army now? They have already ended up as thieves.'

Michihiko: 'While I am asleep, they might watch for a gap, approach on tiptoe, and cut my throat, Bat. One must be careful and sleep tonight, for there are the evils of Bell and Bat.'

Tamakuniwake: 'No matter how strong the power of Bell and Bat may be, I have the protection of the Lord God. Rather than burning your heart over enemies outside your body, fear the enemy within your own body.'

Debisuhime: 'As long as the Imperial God and my teacher are present, what is there to fear, even in a lodging of dew at night?'

Masumihiko: 'Come, Bell, Bat, and Magatsumi; I shall try to make you submit to the living Kototama. When I look at the shadow of the moon shining in the vast sky, I feel ashamed of the roots of my own heart.'

Thus, they finished singing to one another, spread out their straw raincoats, and indulged in casual conversation. Harumichi: 'When I think of Bell, Bat, and the others who were my subordinates, leaving the army to become fierce thieves, wandering in all directions and causing suffering to many people, I feel such painful distress that I cannot stand or sit still. It seems that humans are indeed swayed by their circumstances. As for us, not to mention our own sins, in order to atone for the sins of all our subordinates, we have abandoned our positions as generals, become children of the teachings of the San-go-kyo under the instruction of Harumichiwake-sama, and have become monks, establishing our base on Mount Bikutoru and traveling the world in turns with my three colleagues; but when I think of it, it is a shameful thing before the Lord God. That such subordinates were created is entirely my own sin.'

§ 2

He expressed his Jiyutsukuwai, heaved a Toiki, and sank into tears. Tamakuniwake, with an Omomochi that could not bear the pity of it, spoke quietly,

Tamakuniwake: 'Chidaukoji-sama, please do not worry yourself. Even among parents and children in the Genzai, there is a saying that while one may give birth to the body, one cannot give birth to the soul. It is by no means your sin. It is because of the dust on the hearts of those people that they wander in various ways. The Seishin of us Ningen is not always Kenzen. Tokidoki, an Ichijiteki Henchō or Ijiyō occurs, and it seems there are five types of this Ijiyō.'

Tamakuniwake: 'First, it is when one is lost in Riyoku. When lost in Riyoku, it is easy for anyone to lose calm Handan and Shiumitsu Kauriyo, so one is often lured by profit. For example, in a case where one is keeping the Butsupin of another person, if a heart that thinks, "If only this were my own," suddenly arises, the sense of responsibility disappears, and one becomes dazzled by the state of having used it oneself and makes it one's own; or, if something one has always wanted is right before one's eyes, one loses the time to think about what comes before or after and ends up shoplifting. This is, needless to say, the activation of the Fukushu-sensei, and it is caused by the fact that one's intelligence is blocked for the sake of profit and one does not perform Kenzen work.'

Tamakuniwake: 'The second type is when one encounters a strong, strong stimulus. Even a person who would not disturb their heart by hearing simple backbiting may, when faced directly and severely abused, forget themselves and commit unexpected acts. Or, a person who can maintain a normal attitude toward the opposite sex may have their usual composure broken when encountering the seductive charms of a beautifully made-up person of the opposite sex. As such, even with the same stimulus, it can have an Ijiyō influence on the Seishin depending on the degree. Of course, it goes without saying that there are differences in degree depending on the innate nature and acquired education of the person, but it is most often caused by the activity of the Fukushu-sensei. Also, it is an undeniable fact that an abnormally intense stimulus makes the Seishin of a person Ijiyō.'

Tamakuniwake: 'The third type is the state of the Seishin that occurs when one is anxious or flustered. At such times, the activity of the Seishin lacks tranquility, so whether it is mental work or physical work, it is easy to invite mistakes and failures. It is a fact often seen in the world that by trying to hide a small mistake, one ends up making it larger, or as a result of being flustered by a small loss, one invites a great loss. At such times, it is when the Fukushu-sensei is continuing their greatest agony.'

Tamakuniwake: 'The fourth type is the time of despair and the time of triumph. In times of despair, the efficiency of the Seishin declines and becomes sluggish, leading to a tendency to become passive and dislike effort; in times of triumph, on the contrary, the efficiency of the Seishin increases and one becomes cheerful, active, and does not spare effort. Therefore, the influence on the Seishin is completely opposite when there is success in business and health and comfort of the body, and when there is not, or when one has received honor and when one has received shame. And when the Seishin is extremely depressed, as a result of becoming too passive, one gradually becomes a loser in social life, and when the Seishin is extremely exalted, as a result of advancing too actively, one comes to do work beyond one's real ability and runs toward adventure or dogmatism. This can also be called the result of the activity of the Fukushu-sensei.'

Tamakuniwake: 'The fifth is the state of the Seishin that occurs when one falls into superstition. A person who holds an un-Kenzen faith, despite not making mistakes in ordinary Handan regarding other matters, will come to have a remarkable misunderstanding when it comes to the aspect of faith. Therefore, even if it is a case concerning the healing of an incurable disease or a case concerning Riyoku, one loses the leeway to make calm Handan or think about what comes before or after, and finally, one ends up kidnapping a child, excavating a corpse, or stealing things from seven houses on the evening of Tanabata. Besides the above, it goes without saying that women temporarily experience Ijiyō in the Seishin based on physiological causes such as pregnancy and menstruation. That is why I said that all Ningen are unmanufactured goods or spare parts of madmen. It is not only the Kyōso of Ito or the Kyōshu of Mito who are sudden madmen. Ningen, who are the containers of the true Honsei-Fukushu-gami-sama, are truly mysterious.'

『治道居士様、必ず御心配なさいますな。現在親子の間でも、体は生んでも魂は生みつけぬと云ふ譬が厶います。決して貴方の罪では厶いませぬ。其人々の心の垢によつて種々と迷ふのですよ。吾々人間の精神といふものは、いつも健全なものでは無い。時々一時的の変調異常が起るものでこの異常には五つの型があるやうです。

先づ

第一は利欲に迷ふた時だ。利欲に迷ふた時は誰人も冷静な判断と周密な考慮を失ひ易いものだから、利を以て釣らるる事が多いものだ。たとへば他人の物品を預かつて居るやうな場合にフト「是が自分のもので在つたらなア」と云ふやうな心が浮ぶと、責任観念などが無くなり、それを自分が使つた場合の状態などに眩惑されて自分のものに為たり、また平生から欲しい欲しいと思つて居るものが眼の前にあると前後を考へる暇がなくなつて万引をしたりするやうに成る、これは言ふまでも無く副守先生の発動で、利益のために理智を塞がれ健全なる働きをせないといふ事に原因するものです。

第二の型は、強い強い刺戟に接した時だ。単純な蔭口位ゐ聞いても心を乱さない様な人間でも、面と向つて手酷しく痛罵されると、吾身を忘れて予期しなかつた行為をしたり、また普通の異性に対しては普通の態度が保たれ得る人間が、美しく化粧した異性の誘惑的な嬌態に接すると日頃の平静を破られやすいと云ふ様に同じ刺戟でもその程度によつて精神に異常な影響を与へる事がある。無論是等はその人間の先天的性質や後天的教養によつて程度の差が在ることは云ふまでもないが、副守先生の活動に原因する事が最も多いのである。又異常なる強烈な刺戟が人間の精神を異常ならしむると云ふ事は間違ひの無い事実だ。

第三の型は、焦心したり狼狽した時に起る精神の状態だ。こんな時には精神の活動が安静を欠いで居るので精神的の作業にしても、又肉体的の作業にしても過失や失敗を招き易いものだ。少々許りの失策を隠さうと為たために却て、その失策を大きくしたり、又少々の損失に狼狽した結果、大損失を招くやうな事をした事実は、能く世にあることだ。こんな時には副守先生の最も煩悶を続けて居た際である。

第四の型は、失意の時と得意の時だ。失意の時には精神の能率が減退して因循になり、消極的になつて努力を厭ふやうな傾きになり、得意の時には其反対に精神の能率が増進して快活になり、積極的になつて努力を惜まぬやうになるものです。従つて事業の成功と身体の健康慰安のある時と無い時、名誉を得た時と恥を受けた時とは其精神に及ぼす影響は全く正反対だ。そして精神が極端に沮喪した時は余り消極的になる結果、次第々々に社会生活の敗残者となり、極端に精神を発揚した時は積極に進み過ぎた結果、実力以上に仕事をするやうに成つて冒険的や独断的に走るやうに成るものだ。これも副守先生の活動の結果と云つても良い位なものです。

第五は迷信に陥つた時に起る精神状態だ。不健全なる信仰を持て居る人間は其他の方面の事物に就ては普通の判断を誤ることが無いにも拘らず、信仰の方面になると著しい誤解を来たすものです。従つてそれが難病治癒に関する場合であつても又利欲に関して居る場合であつても、冷静な判断や、前後の考へも廻らす余裕がなくなつて遂に、幼者を誘拐したり、死体を発掘したり、或は七夕の夕に七軒の家から物を盗む様になるのです。以上の外に婦人が妊娠、月経などの生理的原因に基いて、一時的に精神に異常を来すことは言ふまでも無いことです。それだから凡ての人間は狂人の未製品だ予備品だ、と言つたのだ。伊都の教祖や美都の教主而己が突発性狂人では無い。本正副守護神さまの容器たる人間は実に不可思議なものです』

Chidau: 'Arigatō gozaimasu. Thanks to your explanation, I have finally found peace of mind. Ningen are truly troublesome creatures, are they not?'

Sanzen: 'Chidau-sama, you too must have found peace of mind through the sensei’s explanation. I, too, have understood it a little. However, since we are sleeping under a ceiling of Aogumo without any shelter, I do not know when the moon might fall upon our heads and wake us, or if Bell and Bat might come and take our jewels, but let us leave that to Kamunagara and sleep. For a Biku-sama, the sutras are the most important thing, and for me, tomorrow is the most important thing.'

Chidau: 'Ahahaha. In that case, I shall beg your pardon and go to sleep.'

Thereupon, they all fell asleep, rowing a Shirakahayobune on the summit of the mountain pass of Mount Sutaruma, even though there was no river. As one saw a lump of Kurokumo rising in one corner of the Ten, it suddenly expanded with rapid force across the entire sky, swallowing up both the moon and the stars that had been shining brightly until then. To such a place, two men wearing masks and hoods came climbing up the pass. These men were, needless to say, the thieves Bell and Bat. Hearing the sound of snoring, the two lowered their voices.

Bell: 'Hey, Bat, isn't there something in the darkness that sounds like it is puffing, or bubbling like porridge being cooked? There is no reason for a basket seller to climb up to a place like this, and no reason for an old woman cooking porridge to be here either. What on earth could it be? It is not very bat, is it?'

Bat: 'Look here, Bell, do not shout. Not being bat is our prosperity. These fellows are definitely snoring like Ningen. Why don't we search their pillows quietly and see if we can make a profit? There will likely never be such a good opportunity again.'

Bell: 'Even if you say search their pillows, there is no one sleeping with a pillow on top of a mountain like this, is there? Even if I wanted to search, it is pitch black and I cannot see an inch in front of me. What should we do?'

Bat: 'Because we are searching in the pitch black, it is a pitch-black search. What kind of thief would I be if I could not work in the darkness?'

Chidau-koji had not slept a wink while lying down, as he was in charge of protecting the party of Tamakuniwake. Therefore, he heard every bit of Bell and Bat's whispering. Not knowing this, the two continued their whispering in low voices.

Bat: 'Hey, Onichiwake-shōgun has become quite senile, hasn't he? To throw away such an important position and become a wretched Biku, and to be sleeping in the forest of a shrine last night just as he did the night before. What a fool. Perhaps he has gone mad.'

Bell: 'It is boorish to just say such things. He traded his trumpet for a conch shell, abandoned his three thousand subordinates, and wanders about as a beggar, wrapped in black-dyed robes as if he were virtuous, so it is pretty much decided. He has had his spirit drained by that wicked propagandist called Kunichiwake, and has become senile.'

Chidau-koji, wanting to give them a start, put the conch shell to his mouth and blew it with a loud, booming sound. Startled by the sound of the conch shell, which came like water to their sleeping ears, the two fell backward onto their buttocks and trembled in fear. Chidau-koji, while making a thin, artificial voice from within the darkness, chanted:

'Shogyō mujō ze shōmetsu pō, shōmetsu metsu-i jakumetsu i-raku.'

Bat: 'Hey, Bell, that fellow is a monster of the conch shell. He is trying to play a trick on us by spitting out such things. Since we have weapons here too, we must counter him. In such times, it is best to chant a dharani to receive the help of Lord Ōjizaiten Ōkunihiko-no-Mikoto to ward off evil and disaster.'

Bell: 'I wonder if the gods will listen even if a thief chants a dharani.'

Bat: 'That is certain. From here on, I intend to counter the monster.'

The Divine Teachings of Lord Susanoo on the Mountain Top

1. The true God, who exists in the infinite, absolute, beginningless, and endless, and who appears as the Great Primal Spirit of spiritual power and body, is but one. This is called the true God or the Lord God of the universe.

Thou, treat this Lord God as thy true Father and Mother, and revere and love Him. Address Him as Lord Amenominakanushi, and also address Him as Lord Ōkunitokotachi.

1. The Lord God of the Sun, the spirit of strictness, and the Lord God of the Moon, the spirit of harmony, are the manifestations of the divine spirit of the Lord God, that is, Lord Ōkunitokotachi; in the heaven of Takamagahara, He appears as the Lord God of the Sun, and in the spirit realm of Takamagahara, He appears as the Lord God of the Moon.

1. Those who dwell in the virtue of love and goodness ascend to the heavenly realm, and those who dwell in the light of faith and truth ascend to the spirit realm.

1. The true God is Lord Amenominakanushi, one and only. Therefore, He is revered as the Yū no Yū (the Hidden of the Hidden).

1. The God who is a transformation of the true God is revered as the Yū no Ken (the Manifest of the Hidden); the Lord God of the Sun in the heavenly realm and the Lord God of the Moon in the spirit realm are both Yū no Ken deities.

1. Deities who once maintained human bodies and entered the spirit realm are revered as the Ken no Yū (the Hidden of the Manifest). This refers to Lord Ōkuninushi and various angels and heavenly beings.

1. Humans who maintain bodies in the manifest world, convey the great path of God, and preside over various affairs in the manifest world are revered as the Ken no Ken (the Manifest of the Manifest).

Thus, the fundamental Lord God who should be truly loved, respected, and trusted is the one Lord God who exists as the Yū no Yū. The other eight million deities each divide and manage their divine duties according to the command of the Lord God.

1.
