Chapter 1: Jewel の露 [1608]

玉の露〔1608〕

§ 1

The Lord God, the Great Ancestor of the nation, Kunitokotachi no Ōkami, having bestowed the three elements of the Reiriyokutai upon all things in the universe and created them, and having caused each to find its proper place, revered the noble Mikoto of Ōkuniharutachiōkami. Dwelling in the land beneath Ōgonsanka at the center of the Ōunabara, He watched over the hundred creatures of Ametsuchi with great peace and tranquility, and solemnly established the precious laws, setting forth the path for both Kami and men to tread. Yet, as days passed, months went by, and stars shifted, the world grew old, and from the evil Mitama of Adaru no Hiko and Ebahime, the world began to decline.

Due to the wicked deeds of Yamataorochi and the Shikogami, and with great reverence, He left the precious Seichi. While it is called the Shigumi of Kami, He hid Himself on Onokorojima, which floats in the midst of the Ōwadanaka. Changing His name to Kunitakehiko, He endured this world, waiting for the Miyo of the sunrise, the dawn. Mizumitama, who was a Megami, Toyokunihime no Ōkami, could not bear to see the suffering of her husband Kami while remaining still. She hid her divine form for a long time in the deep mountains of Mesopotamia in the Nakatsukuni of Toyoashihara, waiting for the Miyo of Miroku. Kunitokotachi no Ōkami appeared as the Itsukushimi no Mitama, and Takamutsuhime, who dwells in the eternal heavens of the four directions,

And Ōhirumemuchi no Mikoto, whose divine majesty was exceptional, was born between the two Kami, Izanagi and Izanami, as a Megami. Toyokunihime no Ōkami appeared as Kami Susanō no Ōkami, and they divided the Ametsuchi between them, each taking a portion to protect. Yet, even as they did so, the fury of the evil Kami grew strong, and the very roots of trees and rocks, and the hundred grasses, even to their single leaves, cried out and stirred in opposition. The Mizuho no Kuni of Toyoashihara once again became the darkness of the Tokoyo. Kami Susanō no Ōkami, wondering how to calm this tragic state, felt his heart break into a thousand pieces. He grieved morning and evening, and the mountains, rivers, grasses, and trees withered away, turning the land into a street of Shura.

Izanagi no Sumēōkami, who is the father, descended from the vast heavens and faced his precious child, Susanō. When he asked the reason for his grief and lamentation, Mizumitama spoke in full detail of the state of the world. 'I now think to depart for the land of the moon where my mother dwells, and I weep in parting from this world,' he replied. The father Kami, becoming greatly enraged, filled his chest with tears. 'Since you lack the authority to rule over the Ōunabara, you, my noble one, must go to the land of the roots,' he declared most sternly. Filling his chest with immeasurable sorrow, the father Kami

Returned to the Hi no Wakamiya. Kami Susanō no Ōkami, wishing to bid farewell to this world before the presence of the Itsukushimi no Mitama, Ōhirumemuchi, the Amaterasu no Ōkami, who was his elder sister Kami, ascended to her. As he did so, the mountains and rivers trembled at once, and the earth shook. The voices of the eighty Magatsu no Kami crying out echoed loudly before the Ōkami who dwells in the heavens. Amaterasu no Ōkami, observing this state of affairs, thought, 'My younger brother coming here must surely be with an impure heart, intending to seize my divine nation. There is no doubt he has come to attack.' 'Prepare yourselves!' she commanded, gathering the eight million Kami, and they adorned their swords, bows, and arrows in the firm courtyard.

They brandished their bows and roared with heroic fury. When she waited and questioned him, Kami Susanō no Ōkami of the Mizu no Mitama answered with calm words: 'I have no impure heart. By the Mikoto of the father Ōkami, I intend to go to the land of my mother. I have ascended only to bid a single word of farewell to my dear elder sister.' Before he could finish speaking, the elder sister Kami declared most sternly: 'I shall prove the purity of your heart here and now.' Saying this, she took the sword worn by her younger brother Susanō into her hands, and they performed the Ukehi, with the Asakawa river separating them. In this divine act, the noble Susanō no Ōkami, being of the Mizu no Mitama,

His spirit of purity and innocence became exceedingly clear. Kami Susanō no Ōkami took the jewels of the Misumaru entrusted by his sister into his hands, sprinkled them into the Ama no Manai, and shook them with a rattling sound. He crunched them in his mouth and blew them away. From the mist of Ibuki, five Mitama appeared, which was a fearful sight. Although the heart of the elder sister Kami was cleared of doubt for the first time, the Amatsukami and Kunitsukami could not easily settle their hearts. As Takamagahara suddenly became very tumultuous, the elder sister Kami was startled and hid her divine form deep within the Ama no Iwato. The universe instantly became darkness.

Akugami ran rampant, and the visitations of Orochi and Magahi swarmed and boiled like Sabahe. Here, the Kamigami gathered together, and as they performed Ongaku before the Ihato and proclaimed the Futonorito, the Ōkami appeared once more in this world. Rikugafu dawned here, and the Miyo of Sakae began. Alas, for the Tsumitoga of the Kami who initiated such a fearful disturbance, they caused Kamususanō no Ōkami to bear the Chikura no Okido, and Kamuyara from Takaamahara. Though the Ametsuchi seemed to brighten for an Ichiji and the Omote appeared to be governed in great tranquility, the Kuniguni of Toyoashihara—

With the Magami raging fiercely, the Sanjiyō of the world transformed once again into the Yachimata of Shiyura. Unable to bear the sight, Mizumitama, together with Kunitakehiko, opened the Sangyō. Like the hototogisu in the depths of the deep mountains wringing out blood with eight thousand and eight cries, they sought to construct the Miroku no Miyo, peaceful upon this earth. By directing the Kototama toward the Yamata no Orochi and the ugly Kami, and offering them to the elder Goddess to remove the calamities of the world, they erected a Kamiyakata upon the ridges of the Caucasus Mountains and Ubusuna, and how noble it was that they opened the teachings there. The bunrei of the Yamata no Orochi possessed the world and threw it into chaos.

Wishing to first direct the Kototama toward the Akugami of the capital of Haruna and sweep away the crookedness of this world, they dispatched many pure-hearted Senden-shi. How noble it was that they entrusted the task, wishing to illuminate the Mitama of the hitokusa with the blessings of the Kami, by sending to the capital of Haruna the Senden-shi of Tamakuniwake, along with the husband of Mizumitama's daughter, Isokohime, namely Otohiko, and Masumihiko, whose heart was as the clear sky, and Mitsuhiko, who transmitted the teachings, adding Itahiko-tsukasa to their number.

The Senden-shi of Tamakuniwake, together with the three Tsukasa, crossed over the Kajika Pass. While suffering from the yamazaru of Futokorotani, they were protected by divine power and stayed in the Kaminomori of a shrine to recuperate from illness. Having finished building a rare miyaiya, with voices of Norito raised valiantly, they stood before the presence and crossed mountains and rivers, finally entering the mansion of Terumon. They saved the princess Devis and her parents and sister from danger, raised the name of the Kami, and betrothed the princess Devis to Mitsuhiko. Crossing the lake of Terumon, they accomplished various rare divine works, stopped by the mansion of Bachiru on Mount Adsumos, and while saving the Dragon King Sagara, who had begun with the Dragon King Takshaka who had hidden in Mount Adsumos, and his wife-Kami, they received the Yako no Tama and the Nyoihoju from the hands of the Dragon King.

While rubbing their feet against their waraji on the summer path of the clear sky, they proceeded toward the holy land of Jerusalem, accompanied by the four pillars, Itahiko and Devis. As the sun set and they stopped before a shrine by the roadside, they encountered the unexpected conch shell, Kihunwake no Chidō-koji, and the Tsukasa of the Biku. Here, the party of six, while gazing to the right at the foothills of Mount Suddharma, proceeded, and with voices cool and clear, the Senden-ka caused the mountains and rivers of the surroundings to roar.

They proceeded, purifying the air.
