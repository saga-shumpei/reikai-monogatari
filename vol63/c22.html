Chapter 22: 蚯蚓の Voice [1629]

蚯蚓の声〔1629〕

§ 1

On the fourteenth day of the Uzuki month in the Inoto year, within a room on the second floor of the newly constructed Tenseishiya, I lay upon the Kōjiyutsudai and began to recount the Yumemonogatari of the Rokujifusankuwan, a tale of the distant Kamiyo.

The Misora was clear and the earth was green; the Shidareyanagi stood in Shiyukuzen silence, and in the Yufumagure, not a leaf stirred. The party of Tamakuniwake, the Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, having departed from the mansion of Iso, encountered various tribulations, yet they received the Zuihō of Nāgarashā, which rests upon Mount Sūraya, and entrusted it to Itahiko, the Miko of the Oshihe, before finally crossing the Unabara to reach the port of Eru.

Here, the party met in safety, their faces free of harm, and as they looked forward to the Kuōmiyō of their future, they prepared to depart for the Seichi. When Hatsuwakahime, the minister of the Lord, proclaimed the myriad declarations by the imperial command of Konohanahime, both Mitsuhiko and Itahiko bowed in reverence, parted from their sister, and each set forth alone toward the Seichi, and I shall now recount in detail the story of what occurred upon the road.

Jerusalem, which has been established as the sacred ground beneath the Great Sun since the distant days of the Kamiyo, is the center of Toyoashihara no Nakatsukuni. Nodatehiko and Nodatehime, who appeared upon the high-renowned Golden Mountain of this precious Seichi, are manifestations of the divine spirit; appearing in the world as Haniyasu-hiko and Haniyasu-hime, they have bestowed the precious Oshihe of the Three-Five. Awe-struck by that great divine will, the Lord Susanoo no Ōkami opened the garden of Oshihe in the sacred grounds of deep historical significance among the eighty islands of the land, educating many ministers and revealing the infinite benevolence of the divine teachings, thus protecting the Brahmanic faith.

To turn the hearts of the Yamata no Orochi and the ugly spirits, to rebuild the defiled earth into the land of the Lord, and to create a divine age of sincerity, free from envy, jealousy, and hatred, the Lord God endures ten million hardships, fearing neither distance nor proximity, and exerts Himself while shattering His own precious body. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds gather, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if the earth itself should sink, the Oshihe of Ananai-kyō shall never change, even unto the end of ten thousand kalpas, for it is one with the universe. Ah, Kannagara, how grateful is the divine majesty.

In the divine garden where even the young leaves tremble, the plum blossoms on the branches are lush and green, and as they stand side by side, they sing of the peaceful reign of Miroku. The fish swimming in the pond, filled with the dew of grace, play in the Kinryū-chi with ease and confidence, unaware of the clouded world. Though the moon shines brightly in the Misora and illuminates the entire divine garden, the ferocity of the crooked spirits still lurks in the darkness beneath the trees. Mizuki, who has offered both body and soul to the Lord, suffers as her very muscles and bones ache, yet she strives morning and evening to save the people of the world who are lost in darkness.

Though she breaks her heart in a thousand ways, the current world is one where few understand, and her means of salvation are like a boat drifting upon rough waves. Ah, Kannagara, may the divine spirit be blessed.

Breaking her body morning and evening, she seeks to explain and teach the living Oshihe left behind by the ancestors of the faith. Yet, many believers do not understand the awe-inspiring and precious heart of the Lord, even as she offers the Great Norito and pure prayers before the Lord; they are like the shadows beneath the young leaves of summer, restless and noisy, which is truly regrettable.

Though the waters of the Wachi River flow incessantly, forever filled with the dew of divine grace, the people of this current world who draw the pure water of the Jordan for the Zui no Mitama are few indeed. They slight and neglect the teachings of the pure and noble Imperial Gods, forgetting their daily assertions, and creating various miscellaneous excuses to escape, which is truly lamentable.

They treat the words taught to them—that the Great Moto of Takamagahara is the great bridge of the world that leads the three thousand worlds to the heavenly kingdom—as nothing more than a passing breeze. The fate of these ugly spirits, who cross the great bridge and wander further into the unknown, is pitiful.

Even if they awaken after encountering the trials of the Imperial Gods and repent to return, they are like chaff mixed with white rice; they are somehow unwelcome, and as they are no longer as they were at the beginning, the ugly, crooked spirits once again treat the Great Moto of Takamagahara as something to be used conveniently when necessary.

Once they are able to walk alone, they act as if they know nothing, criticizing the place as a den of extremism, while foaming at the mouth and blowing hot air, eventually blowing horns of unknown origin and vanishing like smoke.

These are the false believers lacking in sincerity, who force the teachings of the Lord into the laws of the phenomenal world, arguing whether it is truth, non-truth, or irrational.

§ 2

How ridiculous it is to chant of Guchi. No matter how superior one's Chishiki may be, even a Monoshiribito cannot perceive the Arisama of the world of Kami, which is invisible to the eye. It should be understood through the Mikokoro of the Zenchi Zennō Ōkami. There is a limit to one's Manshin. In all things in the world of men, if one proceeds by entrusting oneself to the Mikokoro of the Sumeōkami, there shall be no Kega. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara, I pray for the Fuyu of the Mitama.

It is truly laughable to see those who are Hanadaka, setting forth worthless Herikutsu, claiming that they will not acknowledge the existence and Keirin of Kami unless it is based on Kuwagaku, all while remaining ignorant of their own folly and posing as Kakushiya in the Sekai. Having frequented the house of learning and studied upon a desk, their Hatakesuiren and Namabiyōhafu can never suffice in actual practice. Even if they demonstrate everything with great brilliance in speech or writing, if they cannot perform the essential and vital deeds, it is as if it were but foam on the water. They are like the dead, lost in a state of dream, reality, or illusion. Though their physical eyes are open, their spiritual eyes are darkened, and while adorning themselves with the sole weapon of intellectual learning, they are pitiful spirits who advance.

The three volumes of Mountains, Rivers, Grass, and Trees are now finally brought to a conclusion here. There will likely be those here and there who will whisper behind my back, saying that Mizu-tsuki is again spouting nonsense. How could illiterate scholars or enemies, who do not know the taste of the labor of authorship, ever comprehend this toil? No matter how much the Kami of Heaven and Earth may assist my body, if the will and thoughts of the soul received from Kami do not shine, even a single word of oral dictation cannot be easily accomplished. The whispers of people whose hearts are as unsettled as floating weeds drifting upon the white waves are indeed deplorable, even regarding the toil of Kami. Though there are many authors in the world, there is no precedent since the creation of the world for one who dictates tens of thousands of words in a single day.

It might be forgivable to discuss the skill or clumsiness of the written text, but it is truly absurd that people who do not understand, who are within the teachings of Kami, should indiscriminately set forth worthless Herikutsu against this Mizu-tsuki, as if out of a hobby, and sing as if attributing their own accumulated inner frustration to Kami. Such filthy people, bound by the desires of their own bodies, bow down to Kami on the surface, waiting for a time when a rice cake might fall from a shelf. If I write that the Tatekae and Tatenaoshi of the world are now or soon to come, they will likely prick up their ears, widen their eyes, and pout their lips as they read.

To spend one's days waiting single-mindedly for such things is the way of a Magatsu-kami; it is to wait for the calamities of the world. The Daiji-hi no Ōkami distributes His Mikokoro morning and evening, wishing that there be no Nanigoto in the world, and the Oshie-no-Oyaso of Ōmoto prays with a sincere heart morning and evening to save the world from its distress. People who spend their days waiting for the great hopes of the world without even knowing that Mikokoro are truly like great serpents or evil spirits; they are people beyond compare. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara, prostrating myself before the presence of Kami, I pray single-mindedly, while my body aches, morning and evening, that He may grant true light to the souls of the believers who gather here at this sacred place, purifying their ears and illuminating their eyes.

I pray that He may cause the Heavenly Jeweled Spear to be polished brightly, and that He may protect us so that the divine power of the Kototama may shine in all four directions. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara, before the presence of the Ōkunitokotachi no Ōmikami, the Toyokunihime no Ōmikami, the Hi no Ōmikami who dwells eternally in the heavenly sky, and the Tsuki no Ōmikami, I lament the Arisama of the world and pray secretly alone. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara, may the Mitama be blessed.
