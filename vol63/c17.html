Chapter 17: 峠の涙 [1624]

峠の涙〔1624〕

§ 1

A man rests his fatigue upon an old tree stump at the summit of the pass on Mount Harusei, gazing at the sky of the past he has traversed, and speaks to himself:

Itahiko: 'Spring has passed and summer has departed; at last, the wind of early autumn has begun to blow. Even in the land of Tsuki, famous for its summer, now that I have climbed to this pass on the high mountain, the mood of autumn truly drifts about. Following my master, Tamakuniwake-sama, I departed from the mansion of Iso during the season when the winter winds howl. As a result of my severe austerities and penance, I have finally come this far, yet I suffer under the heavy burden of the sins accumulated upon my own body, and I can no longer walk even a single step. Ah, how can I be forgiven for my sins and fulfill the mission entrusted to me by Kami? Parting from my master at the foot of Mount Sudaruma, driven by a budding ambition to obtain the treasure jewel of Nagarasha in the rock cavern of Mount Suraya, I took Kaks and Bes as my guides. At last, I reached a corner of the lake of Sudaruma, and while taking shelter from the rain for one night at the house of Rubuya, I was unexpectedly captivated by Princess Burawada. Though I knew I was on my way to the capital of Haruna, I acted as a husband to my colleague Mitsuhiko, and without obtaining the permission of my master, I used the divine decree as a shield to settle a marriage of my own choosing. From then on, pretending to be a married couple, we were sent off by my brother and climbed Mount Suraya. On the way, I encountered a Kami who called himself something like the Five Great Powers, and while receiving various teachings, I was convinced he was but a specter or a goblin. Crossing the line of death, I snuck into the rock cavern, and our party of five advanced to the border between the spirit world and the physical world. I was subjected to the trial of the spirit of Takahime, was saved by an incarnation of Kami, and finally revived. Again, I was humiliated by the Dragon King, and thanks to the intercession of Hatsuwakahime-sama, I received this luminous jewel. Now, the pain in the body of this Itahiko, who is heading toward Jerusalem, and the agony of being poisoned at the line of death—do they still remain? My head aches, my chest is pained, and my feet are swollen like this; I can no longer advance even a single step. What kind of karma is this? Please forgive me, Amatsukami, Kunitsukami, and my master, Tamakuniwake-sama. Kannagara Tamachi Haemaese. Even so, Princess Burawada is a frail woman traveling alone; in what field might she be wandering? If even the sturdy feet of this Itahiko, who has trekked through mountains, fields, rivers, and seas, ache like this, how great must be the suffering of a frail woman unaccustomed to walking? Thinking and thinking, I have learned for the first time the agony of love. I cannot go against the words of the Great Imperial Kami and the words of my master, yet I cannot bring myself to give up this chest-paining feeling. Now that it has come to this, shall I not vanish like the morning dew at the summit of Mount Harusei? Even if it be but a pretense, I wish to meet Princess Burawada, with whom I pledged a thousand years, if only in a dream, and bid her farewell in this world. Ah, what shall I do with this thousand-autumn grudge and this myriad-koku of tears? Toward whom shall I pour them out?'

With his heart pounding and his breath failing, his tears fall like rain.

To this place, carried by two woodcutters, came Princess Burawada, his beloved wife whom he could never forget even in his dreams, looking pale and half-dead. Upon seeing her, joy and sorrow surged in Itahiko's chest, and squeezing out his tearful voice, he said only:

Itahiko: 'Ah, was that woman Princess Burawada? Your condition must be truly painful. It seems the agony from when I crossed the line of death still remains within my body, and now, in this moment between life and death, I had thought that if only I could meet you, I would like to make the journey to heaven. Ah, I never dreamed I would meet you in such a place. This, too, is the great mercy and compassion of Kami. Ah, how grateful I am, how grateful. Kannagara Tamachi Haemaese.'

He presses his palms together in prayer. Princess Burawada, raising a voice as thin as a thread and struggling for breath, says:

Burawada: 'Ah, how happy I am. Were you my husband, Itahiko-sama? I am but a woman of tender years, and I am filled with joy to meet you with my frail feet unaccustomed to travel. Crossing the precipices of Mount Suraya, entering and exiting the borders of life and death, fearing the commands of Kami, and parting from your party of great divine power, I was walking totteringly along a mountain path I had never trodden. Just then, my body became completely exhausted, my soul flew into the void, and when it seemed my end had come, I was finally saved and brought here by the kindness of these woodcutters. Please, Itahiko-sama, I am prepared for the fact that my life is now at its final moment. As a keepsake of this world, please lend me your hand once more. If I do so, even if I die as I am, I shall have no regrets left in this world. Ah, if my birth father, mother, and brother hear of our plight as a couple, how they will lament! That alone is the obstacle on the road to the underworld. Ah, what shall I do?'

と合掌する。ブラワ゛ーダ姫は糸の如き細き声を張り上げて息も苦しげに、

She threw herself down by the side of Itahiko and cried out. Such was her pain. Even the stout-hearted Itahiko, moved by the woman's affection, wrung his sleeves with tears of love and compassion, and said:

Itahiko: 'Ah, what you say is reasonable, but I am a man who has received a vital mission from Kami, and I must offer this jewel of Yakuō to the palace in Jerusalem and proceed to the capital of Haruna. Even if my Nikutai should perish, I must fulfill this mission as a Seirei; how else could I justify myself to the Shinkai? I must observe the order of the words of Ōkamisama spoken through Hatsuwakahime and the Gokyōkun of my master. As a messenger of Kami, how could I possibly take a wife's hand, no matter how dear she may be to me? If the Shinrei of this jewel were to escape from my bosom, that would be a failure for all eternity. Understand the Dauri of this, Burawāda-hime, and please forgive me for that alone. Even if our Unmei in this world ends and we reach the Reikai, the Aizen of our mutual longing will surely never be lost. Even if we were to live long in this world, it would only amount to twenty or thirty thousand days. If, in this short moment, I were to be captured by the demonic hands of love and cause an obstacle to a life of billions of years, both you and I would be forced to accumulate unforgivable sins. Truly, Itahiko, who loves you, wishes to grant you infinite life and bathe you in endless joy. Please, do not think ill of me.'

He spoke these words of admonition with a voice broken by gasping and suffering. Burawāda-hime shook her head from side to side and said:

Burawāda-hime: 'No, no, no matter what you say, is it not possible to allow just one handshake at the moment of death? My heart, burning with love, can only be saved from the suffering of the scorching hell by the hands of my husband. Even if we are to meet with any kind of torture in the future, is there any Dauri that says a husband and wife cannot care for, help, and save each other at the moment of death? You are too stubborn. Please, try to understand my heart a little.'

As she said this, she tried to cling to Itahiko. Itahiko, with a stern attitude as if brushing away a stinging bee, pushed Burawāda-hime away with the tip of his Kongo-zue, and said:

Itahiko: 'Now, Burawāda-hime, do not do anything so thoughtless. What do you think of the words of Ōkamisama and the Gokyōkun of my master? The bitterness of the present is the joy of the future; I did not think you were a person who lacked such discernment. Yet, as a loving couple with the same feelings, ah, how can I soothe this agony and suffering?'

He felt as if a red-hot iron were pressed against his chest, and he hung his head, lost in tears. The two woodcutters laughed aloud, and one of them said:

Woodcutter 1: 'Hahaha, what a stiff and old-fashioned man you are. Listening to the two of you from before, you seem to be a very happy couple, but life is short in the long passage of time. No matter what you say about the future, there is no Dauri for one who has died to live again; human life vanishes like foam on water. While you have a short life in this long floating world, why do you say such things that lead nowhere? Hey, you two, talking about the future and fearing Kami—don't say such foolish things. Moreover, in this state of your final moments, is there any reason why a couple who love each other cannot hold hands when they are about to die? That is why they say the propagandists are behind the times. Who are you being reserved for? Hey, you called Itahiko-sama, is there any man who would not eat a meal set before him when he is liked so nicely? Go on, be affectionate to her.'

Itahiko: 'You are woodcutters from around here; thank you very much for kindly helping Burawāda. And for your words just now, I am truly grateful for your kindness, but for us who believe in the future, how could we ever commit such a violation of the heavenly law?'

Soma no Ichi: 'Ha-ha-ha, what a weak-spirited man you are. If you are also a Sendenshi of the way of the Kami, why can you not be a little more manly? Ita: 'Indeed, your argument is reasonable, but it is the true Kamigokoro for a human to cry when sad, to be angry when irritated, and to laugh when happy; a human who does not reveal joy, anger, sorrow, and pleasure in their expression is a liar or a monster. We Sendenshi are advancing to the capital of Haruna while preaching along the way in order to rectify this.'

Soma no Ni: 'Indeed, that is reasonable for now. However, even a single sheet of paper has a front and a back. Is there any reason not to listen to the dying wish of one's beloved wife?
