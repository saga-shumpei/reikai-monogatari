Chapter 9: 超死線 [1616]

超死線〔1616〕

§ 1

The party of five finally climbed up to the middle of Mount Suraya, having ascended seven thousand three hundred shaku from the water's surface. Beyond this point lay the death-line zone of which the Eukuwai had spoken the night before. A Jiyakitai surrounded the mountainside in all directions, and all who had attempted to break through this death-line had been struck by the evil spirits, afflicted with Shintaisuishiyōbiyō, and fallen here one after another, with not a single person having climbed higher than this. No matter how brilliantly the Yakuō jewels shone or how precious the treasures hung before one's eyes, crossing this death-line was a feat that could never be accomplished by human effort. This zone was approximately seventy to eighty ken in width. Upon reaching the vicinity of the death-line, one could see white bones lying in heaps. Seeing this sight, Itahiko realized that it could not be broken through by ordinary means. Deciding that it was better to ascend by obtaining divine power, he acted as the guide, recited the Amatsu-Norito, and while finishing the chanting of the Ama-no-Kazauta, he rushed upward, entrusting himself to the momentum. At last, the party of five was able to break through the death-line.

Itahiko: 'Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haemaese. That we have safely crossed this dangerous zone, which even a Ryūō cannot break through and descend since the dawn of creation, is entirely due to the divine virtue of the Lord God.'

Wrapped in the robes of the Imperial God's grace, I have crossed the perilous death-line. If one entrusts oneself to the God of Kannagara, I believe there is nothing in this world to fear.

Bravada: 'Noble is the person who has safely crossed The gate of death, unheard of even in the age of the gods. Were it not for the protection of the God who grants spiritual happiness, How could one have crossed this hideous death-line?'

Asumagarda: 'The achievement of Itahiko, the divine official, Is a precedent unheard of even in the age of the gods. Even Ubananda-Nāgarashā must be astonished To see the martial spirit of Itahiko. How joyful it is to climb for the first time This mountain floating in the sea of Suraya.'

Karks: 'Having broken through the terrifying and hideous death-line, We have climbed up to the ridge of the mountain. This is Mount Suraya, where the believers who serve the Uraruhiko God Left their corpses to bleach.'

Base: 'Protected by the power of the 35 God, We have climbed easily up this mountain of treasure. Yet, my chest has become heavy and painful; Perhaps it is because I touched the hideous death-line.'

Karks: 'I, too, have become uneasy in my chest; Protect us, O 35 God.'

Bravada: 'My physical body has suddenly become heavy, And I struggle to walk this mountain path.'

Itahiko: 'If you brace your heart and rely on the God, There is a path to climb, no matter how steep.'

Asumagarda: 'My chest is somehow restless, and my hands and feet Will not move as my heart desires.'

Itahiko: 'For one who has broken through the famous death-line, a little suffering is unavoidable. Come then, let us whip the horses of our hearts, And proceed to the cavern of the Ryūō.'

Itahiko stood at the front, encouraging the hearts of the party while dragging his own heavy feet, and as he was blown by the wind on the mountain ridge, he sang a song and proceeded forward with vigor. The four followed behind with the slow pace of an ox, sweating profusely and panting as they trailed him.

Itahiko: 'The God appears on the surface To distinguish between good and evil. I, the propagandist of the 35-Kyo, And the official Itahiko, am here now. Having received the divine words of the God, I have come to the great front of the capital of Jerusalem, To offer the precious jewel That the Nāgarashā has protected for eternity, For the sake of the realization of the divine government. I speak to Ubananda, who guards this mountain: Your life, since the distant past of the age of the gods, Has been one of suffering, having been confined To the cavern of Mount Suraya Under the wrath of the God. The plum blossoms of the three thousand worlds Are now ready to bloom all at once. All the Eight Great Ryūō, through the grace and forgiveness of the God, Have been saved in the Amatsu-Mikuni, And the time has come for them to serve As the noble pillars of the God. Rejoice, Ubananda. I speak before the Ryūō: Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, Whether the moon waxes or wanes, Even if the earth itself should sink, There are no two words in the one truth of the 35 God's words. With a calm and peaceful heart, Accept the mission of this Itahiko, And quickly hand over the Yakuō jewel. From this moment on, manifest your spirit To the wide world, And become a pillar of the 567 divine government.'

Burawāda: 'My dear brother, please wait. / Having climbed the high mountain we are not used to treading, / In a single sweep, / My body is exhausted, / And my hands and feet have become heavy. / Your life is that of a messenger of propaganda, / And you do not fear the dwelling of any evil, yet, / My brother Asumagaruda, / And the two other companions, / Have been struck by the evil spirits of the Shisen, / And have become bodies of sorrow, / With hands, feet, and hearts beyond our control. / The Gankutsu of Nāgarashā, / Lies before my eyes, / And though the light of the glowing jewel, / Shines all around, / Our hearts are somehow, / Clouded, and we feel as if we are, / Following the path of Yomi. / Please let us rest, my brother, / I beg of you.'

Itahiko, hearing this song of Burawāda and pitying the four who were struggling in their walk, sat down on a rock resting on the mountain peak and decided to wait for a while for the stragglers to catch up.

Kākusu, breathless and crawling as if his life were fading, caught up.

Kākusu: 'Excuse me, Sensei, though you say this is the work of Kami, it is truly painful and I cannot bear it; please, pray to Kami for us.'

Itahiko: 'As for praying to Kami, it is better for you to pray with your own heart. Even I am bearing full responsibility, so I have endured the pain in my legs and the suffering of my body to come this far. Since we are doing the work of the spirit world, it is not something that can be done so easily. If you have the virtue of Kami, it is nothing, but even Nāgarashā could not escape by crossing the Shisen, and since we have come through a place of extreme danger where it has been in seclusion since the age of the gods, it is natural to be a little pained. If you rest here for a while and catch the mountain breeze, your energy will likely recover.'

Kākusu: 'Yes, thank you very much. If I think of everything as Shingefu, I will not hold any grudge even if I die.'

Itahiko: 'Do not say such weak-hearted things. As long as the Mizu-no-Mitama-sama, the source of eternal life, protects us, it is fine. In any case, there is nothing to do but believe in Kami and pray to Kami. Ah, Kamunagara, Reikō-haise.'

Bēsu: 'Sensei, I may be sounding weak, but I feel as if I am running out of breath.'

Itahiko: 'Eh, what a weak-hearted man you are. Just one more push. You must rely on the divine power of Kami and achieve your goal. What will you do if you fail when you have gone ninety-nine percent of the way? If you are such a coward, you will not be able to report back before the Kami who protect the unity of the manifest and the hidden worlds.'

Bēsu: 'Yes, as you say, but it is so painful that I can no longer think of greed or virtue.'

Ita: 'This is troublesome. In any case, Inori is Kanjin.'

Asumagaruda: 'When I gaze beyond the Kosui of Sūraya, I yearn for my Chichihaha.'

Itahiko: 'For one who advances upon the Ōji of the Chihaya Kami, it is best to forget the things of this world. No matter how high the blessings of Chichihaha may be, can they compare to the blessings of Kami?'

Burawāda: 'Embraced by the true parents of Ametsuchi, I feel as though I am ascending to the Mikuni.'

Bēsu: 'Thinking that Nanigoto is according to the Mikokoro of the Sumeōkami, what is there to resent? Remembering the strict Oshihe that joy comes after suffering, I Hohoe.'

Itahiko: 'Come then, let us advance toward the Arika of the Yakuō jewels; Morobito, stand up.'

Having thus finished expressing their Jiyutsukuwai to one another, Itahiko stood at the Sentō again and approached close to the side of the Gankutsu where the Riuō was said to be lurking.

This Gankutsu had a Tateana open like a deep Ido, and at the bottom of the Gankutsu, which seemed to be Ikujiyō deep, Yakuō jewels were shining in countless numbers, so brightly as to dazzle the eyes. Itahiko searched above the Te-no-o, cut some Fujitsuru, twisted them into a thick rope, tied one end to the Gankutsu at the entrance, and sliding down the rope, he reached the bottom. The others also drummed up their courage, followed after Itahiko, and while carving steps with their feet into the Fujitsuru rope ladder, they finally arrived safely at the bottom of the Gankutsu. Looking, they saw that another horizontal hole opened from there, and countless jewels were shining. In the depths, the Ubananda Riuō was coiled at full length with many of his kin; such was the terror of it. Itahiko put his hands together, clapped, and attempted to recite the Amatsu Norito, but for some reason, his tongue suddenly stiffened, he could not utter a single word, and he collapsed on the spot. Asumagaruda and all the others also collapsed on the spot, lying side by side. Ah, what will become of the fate of the five?
