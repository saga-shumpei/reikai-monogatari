Chapter 20: Way の苦 [1627]

道の苦〔1627〕

§ 1

Burawāda: 'Since the days of Tsukiteruhiko, I, Burawāda, born in the house of Rūbuya in the village of Teru, a believer of the Ananai-kyō which the Tohotsumioya have served, was persecuted morning and night for the sake of the teachings of the Shikogami of the Brahman religion, and thus, while outwardly casting aside the Ananai-kyō and serving the Brahman religion, I bored a hole in the pillar of my house, carved the sacred name of Kami therein, and worshipped in secret; as I waited for the time, Itahiko, the Kami-tsukasa of Ananahi, descended like a dove, and having obtained the permission of my father and mother, we bound the ties of Imose; my brother Mikoto also rejoiced, and to assist in the Shingefu of Itahiko-tsukasa, I crossed the waves of the lake of Sūraya, advanced three thousand and more shaku above sea level to the Ihaya where the Riuō lurks, endured various sufferings, and even advanced to the Iriguchi of Ne-no-kuni, where I was taught the Mioshihe of the grace of Kami most earnestly; saved by Hatsuwakahime, I escaped through a crevice in the Ihaya, was helped by the Mifune of Hime, and at the time I finally reached the port of Eru, I followed the teachings of Kami, parted in life from my beloved husband, and on this solitary journey, unaccustomed to walking, my feet were eaten by my waraji, I stained the small grasses of the road crimson, and with my staff as my strength, I have now reached the foot of Mount Harusei.'

'I am one who crosses this mountain, known by the name of the great Mount Takayama in the land of Tsuki, famous in song, to cross over to the holy land of Erusalem, but for some reason, my body is weary and my breath has become labored; I feel as though the air of the yōja from the time I crossed the line of death still lurks within my body; where are the master of Tamakuniwake and Itahiko-tsukasa now? Though I wish to hear of their state, the commandments of Kami are strict, and in this sky of travel, there is no way to meet them; how my parents and my brother Mikoto, whom I left in the country, must be worrying, and as I worry for the two of them all day and all night, I pray to Kami, and surely they too are praying that the awe-inspiring and severe grace of Kami be bestowed upon the two of us.'

'I, a frail woman, advance far along the cloud-path, and when I look back, I see an endless great field; though lotus flowers bloom and scent the distance and a hundred birds sing with cool voices, I have no way to ask, and I weep and sob; if there is a spirit in the ancient Shara trees standing in a row at this mountain pass, I wish it would tell me perfectly of the news of my parents, my brother, and Itahiko-tsukasa; ah, Kannagara-kami-kami, I leave this body to Kami, and keeping the words of the teaching engraved in my heart that worrying in advance is forbidden, I do not forget them, yet the longing for my home arises once more; oh, Kannagara, please wipe it away, I pray before Thee.'

'Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if the great earth should sink, I will not cast aside the teachings of the Ananai-kyō as long as my soul continues, let alone in this present world; though I, who advance on the one great path of sincerity, have no fear of being hindered by the Magatsukami, the evil one lurking in my heart raises its head once more, and leads the soul of a pure maiden into the dark path of love; oh, Kannagara, please save my body, which is filled with unclouded thoughts, I pray before Thee.'

While chanting thus, she finally climbed to the middle of the mountain pass of Mount Harusei, rested her breath here, pondered the past and the future, and shed tears on her sleeves at the loneliness of her solitary journey. The sun finally sank into the western mountains, and the surroundings began to look as if a thin ink curtain had been lowered. The flowers closed their doors and went to sleep, and the birds sought their nests, flapping their wings busily, aiming for the forests here and there. Burawāda-hime spoke to herself:

'Ah, what a dreary floating world this is; born as the daughter of a chieftain in the village of Teru, while secretly praying to the Kami of Ananai morning and night, I was persecuted by the Brahman priests time and again, and ended up becoming a believer of the Brahman religion against my heart; not only my family, but even the villagers were forced into a faith that was not in their hearts, and three times a month we were forced to perform fire-walking, diving to the bottom of the water, naked asceticism, and being thrown into a room of thorns; thinking this was the first duty to appease the heart of Kami, I endured the suffering of the Avici and the Garbha, and finally, this frail body of mine welcomed its sixteenth spring; the heavenly fortune circulated here, and I met the noble Kami-tsukasa of the Ananai-kyō, and with the consent of my parents and brother, we bound the ties of husband and wife; thinking that I would share any hardship or suffering with my husband, I parted from my parents and brother and followed him here, but now I can no longer take a single step.'

'Ah, how can I escape this suffering? Is this also because I deceived the surface, worshipped the Brahman god, and the divine punishment for enshrining the great merciful and compassionate Kami of the Ananai-kyō by boring a hole in a narrow, cramped pillar has come to pass? "One does not light a lamp and place it under the floor," is the saying of the saint; going against those words, the accumulated sins of having respected the evil gods of the Brahman religion have come around, and no matter what suffering my body may receive, I have decided that it is my fate, and I will never hold a grudge. Kami, please, I pray earnestly that You will forgive the sins of my parents, my brother, my husband, and the villagers, and use them for the glorious Shingefu.'

'Ah, now that it has come to this, I must resign myself. Ah, my chest has suddenly started to ache. I have no medicine with me. I can no longer ask Kami to forgive my sins. It must not be that my sins are forgiven and the admonition goes to my parents and my husband. Please, Kami, take my body and forgive the sins of all. Even so, I wish to see my beloved Itahiko-sama one last time at the moment of my death. Ah, how could I ever erase this agony?'

斯く謡ひながら、漸くにしてハルセイ山の峠を中程迄登りつき、茲に息を休めて来方行末の事を思ひ案じ、一人旅の淋しさに袖を霑して居る。日は漸く西山に没し、四辺は薄墨の幕を卸したやうになつて来た。花は扉をとぢて眠りにつき鳥は塒をもとめて彼方此方の森林目蒐けて忙しげに翅を早めて居る。ブラワ゛ーダ姫は独言、

§ 2

Hitori sits down on the grass by the roadside, lost in tears of sorrow. The voices of maujiu echoed from all shihau and hatsupau, so loud that the very sangaku seemed to tremble. Even the kijiyō Burawāda felt her hair stand on end at this terrifying roar, and she felt the pitiable waves of kiyōfu washing over her, even though she had already resigned herself to death. While crying and screaming as if she were about to perish, Burawāda throws herself down upon the grass by the robau and weeps bitterly.

Michihiko: 'As a Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō, following Tamakuniwake, crossing yamano and rivers, stopping by the Terumon mansion, breaking my heart and body with iroiro zatsuta and murakimo, saving the mansion from nangi, and as the fūjin settled here, taking the Hime of Debisu as my wife, together with my teacher, crossing the Kiyome kosui, and saving the lives of the Bachiru master and servant who built a wide mansion at the foot of Mount Azumosu through the grace of Kami, and while receiving the night-shining jewel and the wish-fulfilling gem by persuading the Takushaka Riuō, I proceed toward the capital of treasures with my teacher, crossing over the Suraya kosui.'

Michihiko: 'When we arrived at the port of Eru, the pillar of the Ananai-kyō, the glorious Hatsuwakahime, cleared away the clouds of confusion, and I parted ways with my teacher, and parting from the beloved sleeves of the Hime of Debisu, I proceed most bravely through the untrodden yamano. This is the entrance to the steep mountain pass of the famous Mount Harusei. The voices of the maujiu that I suddenly hear, are they an earthquake or thunder? My hair stands on end. Although I am a man who conveys the teachings of Kami to the world, I feel such terror. How could the Hime of Debisu or Burawāda possibly find a way to proceed along this mountain path?'

Michihiko: 'When I think of it, when I think of it, how pitiful it is. Though it is said to be for the sake of Kami, for the sake of the Way, and for the sake of the people of the world, this is such a painful grass-pillow journey. When I ponder the future of the women who have set out on this journey, I am moved to even greater pity, and my sleeves are soaked with tears. Ah, Kannagara-kami-kami, may your spirits be blessed, and I pray with reverence before you, protecting the two delicate women, the Hime of Debisu and Burawāda, with the divine authority of Kami, and allowing them to fulfill the divine work with ease, Kannagara. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if the great earth should sink.'

Michihiko: 'Because I am one who advances the teachings of the one true Ananai, I have nothing to fear, but how can weak women, who have not yet attained the understanding of the teachings of Kami, overcome this difficult barrier? I pray with reverence before the Great Imperial Kami of heaven and earth to protect them.'

While chanting this, Michihiko trudges up the mountain path where the unpleasant roaring sounds. As soon as the faint, sorrowful crying of a woman reaches his ears, Michihiko pulls himself together.

Michihiko: 'Now, that crying voice sounds exactly like a woman. There can hardly be anyone else passing through this mountain tonight. It must surely be the Hime of Debisu or the Hime of Burawāda. I shall run and see if it is true or not.'

He suddenly quickens his pace and climbs the uphill mountain path with vigor. Looking, he sees the figure of a woman lying sorrowfully on the grass by the side of the road. Michihiko approaches her in surprise.

Michihiko: 'Excuse me, Madam, who are you to be lying here all alone on this mountain path? As luck would have it, the moon is covered by black clouds, so I cannot see your face clearly, but I think you might be the Hime of Burawāda. Please forgive me if I am mistaken. I am by no means a suspicious person. I am one called Michihiko, a Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō. Come, please get up and tell me what has happened.'

The Hime of Burawāda rejoices at Michihiko's compassionate words as if she had encountered Noah's Ark, and she manages to raise her heavy body.

Burawāda: 'Yes, I am the wife of Itahiko. You are the highly virtuous Michihiko-sama; thank you for finding me. No matter what I say, this body of mine is full of sin; have I met with the admonition of Kami? I could not walk another step, and I was crying here on this grassy path, squeezing out my death-throes in shame.'

ブラワ゛ーダ姫は三千彦の情の籠もつた言葉にノアの方舟に出遇つたが如く喜び、重き身をやうやうに起き上り、

ブラワ゛ーダ『ハイ妾は伊太彦の妻で厶います。貴方は神徳高き三千彦様、ようまア尋ねて下さいました。何を云つても罪の多い此体、神様の戒めに遇ひましたか、モウ一足も歩けなくなつて、この草路に断末魔の声を絞つて恥しながら泣いて居りました』

§ 3

Michihiko, upon seeing this state, shed tears in torrents and, with his voice clouded, spoke.

Michi: 'Please be at ease. The Lord God will surely protect your body. Nay, He will protect even your soul for eternity. It is my deepest wish to take you with me and escort you to Jerusalem, but as you have heard, the command of the Lord God is most strict, and I cannot accompany you. However, for a person, the heart is what matters most. As long as the heart remains vibrant, the physical body is of no consequence. No matter how exhausted one may be, one will recover immediately upon resting. Please keep your spirits firm. Ah, Kamunagara Tamuchihahe Mase. ...Ah, Lord God of Ananai-kyō, I pray earnestly that You may save the frail woman, Blavatsky. Regarding this, Hime Davis is also a frail woman on a solitary journey; I pray earnestly that You may arrange for her to reach the holy land safely through the grace of Your divine favor. Ah, Kamunagara Tamuchihahe Mase.'

As he pressed his palms together in prayer, a curtain of silence fell between the two for a while. The voices of wild beasts grew even more intense, echoing from the valleys here and there as if a hundred thunders were striking at once.
