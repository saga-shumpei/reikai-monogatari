Chapter 16: 諒解 [1623]

諒解〔1623〕

§ 1

Hatsuwakahime had already arrived at the port of El, and together with Asumagaruda, Burawada, Kākusu, Bēsu, and Sumāto, she stood upon the pier, waiting for the ship of Tamakuniwake to approach. The ship finally arrived at the port of El. Tamakuniwake came ashore joyfully with his party, stood before Hatsuwakahime, finished a bow, and said:

Tamakuniwake: 'The clear lake of Sūraya, at last, / By the grace of Kami, I have crossed over. / How noble it is, Hatsuwakahime-chin-no-Mikoto, / That you have arrived so swiftly.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Gazing upon the surface of the lake, how many times / Have I waited impatiently for your ship, my lord.'

Masumihiko: 'Even this lake where gold and silver waves drift / Cannot compare to the radiance of Hatsuwakahime. / When I look upon the port of El from above the waves, / Your precious Misugata shines and burns.'

Michihiko: 'The moon is full, the tide is high, and the ship is full of people, / With hearts full, we have crossed the Namiji. / Having crossed safely by the grace of Kami, / I am now to part at this lake. / The pain of the parting road lies in the Namiji, / Please accompany me, Hatsuwakahime-no-kimi.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'I, who advance in awe of the Sumekami's Mikoto, / Being a Kami, shall not rely on companions.'

Debisuhime: 'One who walks the path of Kamunagara is but one person, / Knowing not the laws of advancement. / How shall I fulfill the Mimune of Kami, / Being one who is with my husband?'

Burawada: 'Even I, being a Kami, how could I fear / A solitary journey on the Kusamakura? / Yet, how could I fail to advance, / Separated from the husband whom Kami has permitted?'

Hatsuwakahime: 'How could I make a person my strength, / I who advance according to the will of the Ōkami? / The Minori of the Ananahi Kami is for one person alone, / To transmit the path is my duty.'

Chidau: 'How noble are the Okotoba of Hatsuwakahime, / They have cleared the darkness of my soul.'

Tamakuniwake: 'To me, who advances in awe of the Ōkami's Mikoto, / Please permit one person, Hatsuwaka-no-kimi.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'You are the Kamutsukasa sent by Kami, / Remain at ease together with Masumihiko.'

Itahiko: 'This is a fine mess, imitating Michihiko-sama, / I have lost my way in the darkness, unaware and unknowing.'

Itahiko sat upon a stone of the pier, folded his arms, and was lost in thought about something. Tears were even dripping from his eyes, as if he were unable to bear the feeling of repentance. Burawada, with a heavy heart, crept up before Itahiko and said:

Burawada: 'If it be you, my husband, you seem to be in a state of mind suddenly overcome with distress; has some worrying matter arisen? If it is no hindrance, please tell me. To be husband and wife means to share joys and sorrows to the end; that is the way of Heaven and Earth.'

Itahiko shook his head from side to side, his voice even growing hoarse, and said:

Itahiko: 'Burawada, please, consider it the bond until now, and forgive this Itahiko. It is the wish of a lifetime.'

Burawada: 'I do not know what has offended you, but perhaps it was my following the Okotoba of Hatsuwakahime-sama, leaving your ship, and coming here first that has offended your will. I have truly done a regrettable thing. From now on, I will surely protect your person, so please forgive me.'

She said this with tears in her eyes.

Itahiko: 'No, no, I am not thinking of such things at all. It was very good that you accompanied Hatsuwakahime-sama. If I can go to the capital of Haruna, fulfill the divine command, and then obtain the permission of Kami to become husband and wife with you, then so be it. If I am separated from you, this Itahiko intends to live a life of celibacy and serve the spirit world. You, from now on, leave me, return home, fulfill your filial piety to your parents, choose a suitable husband, and live in peace. However, even if we part once, if there is a bond, we may be able to be together again. With the words of Hatsuwakahime-sama and the words of the Ubananda Dragon King, this Itahiko can no longer stand or sit still.'

Burawada: 'If so, Tamakuniwake-sama, Hatsuwakahime-sama, what shall we do? Please, give us your command regarding us as a couple.'

§ 2

Tamakuniwake, having said 'Ah,' remains silent, wiping away tears while looking down and heaving a deep sigh.

Hatsuwakahime: 'Though we part, we shall meet again at the end of the sea, where the moonbeams float upon the waves of Onami and Menami.'

Tamakuniwake: 'According to the song you have just sung, Hatsuwakahime-sama, it would be best for you, Itahiko, though it is pitiful, to part ways with Burawada-hime here, and after the glorious achievement of the Shingefu, to bind your marriage vows anew. Burawada-hime, you are surely aware of this, are you not?'

Burawada: 'Indeed, your words are filled with compassion. If that is the case, I must not hinder my precious husband's Goshingefu, so let us part cleanly here. However, as I cannot return home as I am, please, I beg of you, let me also serve the needs of the capital of Haruna. Itahiko-sama, if that is so, I shall bid you farewell now. I pray that you may safely complete your glorious Goshingefu and return to report to the presence of the Sumeōkami.'

Tamakuniwake, with a gentle smile and looking quite cheerful,

Tamakuniwake: 'Burawada-hime-sama. Your resolve is truly magnificent. In that case, you shall go to Jerusalem alone, and from there, cross the river of Eden, emerge in the land of Fusa, and proceed to the capital of Haruna. Surely the gods will assist you. Ah, it is regrettable for me to part from the disciples who have helped me and whom I have helped to reach this point, but I cannot break the laws of the gods. However, although I was permitted by Lord Susanoo to take Masumihiko, Mitsuchihiko, and Itahiko with me, now that I think upon it, I cannot help but feel deep remorse, wondering if the Lord God must be looking down upon me in His heart, thinking, "Tamakuniwake is a spineless fellow." Nevertheless, with the permission of Hatsuwakahime-sama, I shall take only Masumihiko with me. Itahiko, it would be best for you to go alone from here to offer the jewel in Jerusalem, then cross the land of Fusa and proceed to the capital of Haruna. Mitsuchihiko, you too shall go alone.'

Itahiko, Mitsuchihiko, and Burawada all bow their heads at once, showing their consent while shedding tears.

Tamakuni: 'Ah, now Tamakuniwake is also at peace. Through the words of Hatsuwakahime-sama, who was possessed by the divine, our dreams of delusion have awakened. I am truly grateful.'

So saying, he joins his hands in prayer and weeps.

Debisu-hime: 'Farewell now, Mitsuchihiko, follower of the divine teachings; we shall meet again in the capital of Haruna. Hatsuwakahime and Tamakuniwake, ministers of the gods, may you be at peace, for I shall take my leave now.'

No sooner had she said this than she bowed to everyone and vanished into the town of Eru. From this point on, by the command of Hatsuwakahime-mikoto, Asumagaruda returned to her own home, while Burawada and Debisu-hime each set out to cross pathless mountains and valleys to proceed to Jerusalem. Itahiko and Mitsuchihiko also set out on solitary journeys, holding the jewels, to proceed to Jerusalem. Hatsuwakahime, together with Sumāto, vanished somewhere. Chidō-koji, accompanied by the four who were under his command—Batto, Beru, and Kākusu and Bēsu who had surrendered from the Uraru religion—each took the appearance of a bhikshu, purchased conch shells at the port of Eru, and set out to travel the great road to Jerusalem, leaning on their diamond staves. How will the actions of each of the propagandists unfold in the future?
