Chapter 16: 天降里 [1783]

天降里〔1783〕

§ 1

In the nine-foot-by-two-ken dwelling of the Hinminkutsu in Shigure-machi, Reiru and Māku suddenly welcomed four new and unusual guests: Teira, Harisu, Chiuin, and Chinrei, and a great vitality began to overflow from somewhere. The newly arrived guests were all clad in worn-out Raudōfuku, and they had so lowered their Minari that one could hardly tell if they were Taishi or Kifujin. Therefore, the Babakakaren of the neighboring Shichikennagaya had no way of knowing, even in their dreams, that they were Taishi and Ōjiyo in Hensau.

From early in the morning, the Onnagiin were holding an Idobata-Kuwaigi, carrying Teoke instead of bags, like the Oyakata of swallows.

Kō: 'Say, Oume-sama, strange, down-and-out people have been coming to Reiru-sama's place lately, haven't they? Oh, they might be Kifujin who have fallen from grace, but even so, they should follow the Nagaya Kisoku and distribute a bowl of Udon each, yet they have not even come out to offer Aisatsu.'

Otsu: 'Otake-sama, if something like receiving a bowl of Udon or Soba were to happen, that would be a real Taihen. It would be troublesome afterwards, you see.'

Otake: 'Even so, when I drifted into this Nagaya at the end of last year, you all took the lead and helped me with various things, and since you insisted that it was the Nagaya Kisoku to distribute a bowl of Udon or Soba to the three houses in front and the neighbors on both sides, I pawned my old man's happi coat and distributed a bowl of Udon to each.'

Oume: 'Well, that is true; if they were ordinary people, they would have to get along with each other and interact, but those two, Reiru-sama and the other one, Māku-sama, are people who are on what is called the blacklist of the Lama headquarters, and the Bansō-sama always comes around with a Nyoi-bō to investigate, doesn't he? They say that person is a Kōjōkaiin or a black radical. If one were to associate with such people, one would be targeted by the Bansō-sama, and no one would want to hire us for day labor. If that happens, our family's livelihood will dry up in an instant, won't it? My old man does construction work every day, and we help with the household budget by pasting matchboxes, but if it rains for three days, the construction work stops, and we are in a situation where the family would starve to death, so I cannot bear to be glared at by the likes of the Bansō-sama.'

Otake: 'What a terrifying person has crawled into this alleyway. Lately, because such people are wandering about, difficult laws like the Teian-ijihō, the Kokushidan, and... laws have been promulgated, and they say that even if three people gather and talk, they are immediately dragged away, so it is dangerous for five or six of us to be drawing water together like this.'

Oume: 'Are we not just women? Originally, no matter how many of the wives from the back alley gather and hold a sparrow-like meeting, nothing will come of it. No matter how blind the Bansō-sama may be, he would not do anything as unreasonable as dragging away women.'

Otake: 'What, there are some groups of women that are quite hard to handle, you know. Women these days have all received what is called higher-level prostitution education, so various Okyan and champions of women's rights and women's suffrage have appeared, causing headaches for the Lama headquarters; so these days, they say the Bansō-sama will immediately drag away even women if he sees they are slightly suspicious. Among those Kōjōkaiin-sama, there are three beautiful women who seem to be high-class prostitutes, so one never knows when the Bansō-sama might come. Never mind a treat of Soba, I cannot bear to be caught in the crossfire. Come, let us go home.'

With that, the five or six old women lowered their Teoke and disappeared, each slipping through their own small, broken doors.

Teira and Harisu are laughing with calm faces.

Rail: 'If Teira-sama, Harisu-sama, are you not bothered even when your fathers are spoken of?'

Teira: 'Yes, there is no child who does not grieve for the death of a father. However, if they were to become the object of the masses' resentment and meet a tragic end, that would be unbearable as a child, but they died fighting for the royal family and the national religion at a critical moment, so I am grateful, thinking it is entirely the blessing of the God of Uraru. Right, Harisu-sama, you think so too, do you not?'

番『イヤ、有難う。それでは一杯頂戴しようかな。僕だつて同じトルマン国の人民だ。如意棒をブラ下げて居る丈けの違ひだ。一つ上司の機嫌を損じたが最後、忽ち丸腰になつて労働者の仲間へ入れて貰はなくちやならないのだから、今の間に君等と懇親を結んでおかなくては、忽ち自分の前途が案じられて仕方がないからな。どうかレールさま、よろしく頼みますよ』

§ 4

Re: 'The Kaukifusōriyo of today, every one of them, are nothing but Gaki who take Wairo and Ketsutaku with Goyōshiyōnin, sucking up all the sweet nectar they can get. Among the Yakusō, the only ones who are relatively Ketsupaku are your group of Bansō. Even so, it is said that once you reach the rank of a minor Lama, you have a considerable amount of Yosanguwai Shiunifu. Is it not trivial for you to merely show off your Niyoibō in front of the Raudōshiya and Iba? We live in a world where Fusen will soon be Jitsukau. You should join our Nakama, take the lead in the Kaujiyōundō, get elected to the Eshiu, and carry out a Daikaikaku of both Kokusei and Shiusei. What is the point of working as a Bansō with a low salary? No matter how much you advance, the peak of a Bansō's career is a minor Lama. And you cannot even reach that point unless you serve for thirty years, ha ha ha.'

Ban: 'Hmm, that is true. If I could get into the Eshiu and work skillfully, I might be able to become a Banshoku Jōgyō. At worst, I might even be able to secure a seat as a Shudatō. If there were someone who would guarantee my livelihood, I would intend to resign from today and join you in your activities.'

Re: 'That is interesting. Even among the Bansō, it is said by your Nakama in the Kaujikai that you have something different about you. You should make up your mind and throw away your life as a Bansō. Your livelihood will surely be guaranteed by Bakushi-sama. And as long as you stay with Bakushi-sama, you will be as firm as a great rock. Teianijihō, Kokushidan, ... laws, and all that nonsense, will no longer matter.'

Chiu: 'This is an interesting Bansō-sama. Hey, you, to tell you the truth, I am Prince Chiuin. I have come here disguised as a scout for the Nakama of the Kaujikai in order to reform the Kyōsei. How about it? Do you not want to quit being a Bansō as of today and devote yourself to the Kaujiyōundō? I will surely make you a Jōgyō.'

Ban: 'Is that true? You must be teasing me, you have a bad sense of humor. There is no reason for a Prince-sama, as awe-inspiring as you are, to be in a place like this.'

Chiu: 'Modern people, trapped by convention, misunderstand that a Prince must have some special kind of authority, but even a Prince or a Kamibashira is a creature that eats white rice and excretes yellow waste, ha ha ha ha.'

Ban: 'No, I understand now, there can be no mistake. I felt from somewhere that you were a person of high refinement, and if that is the case, then these ladies must all be noblewomen who live above the clouds. I am a Bansō of the Komonzan named Teruman. Please, I ask for your guidance from now on. I will not spare any labor, like a dog or a horse, for whatever task you may have.'

Chiu: 'Ha, good, good, I have found one new person. I have had an immediate catch, ha ha ha.'

Five or six rays of sunlight fall through the gaps in the door onto the darkened tatami mats, and dust like smoke floats, drawing hazy outlines. The bell of the tofu seller is heard faintly. The bell of the newspaper delivery man rings out even louder.
