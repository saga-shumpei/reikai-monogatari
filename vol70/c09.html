Chapter 9: 針 Demon の Forest [1776]

針魔の森〔1776〕

§ 1

Surrounding the three directions of east, west, and south with the great ocean, the Moon Country stands prominent; it is the oldest civilized land in the world. The kings of over seven thousand countries, each sharpening their blades, contend for supremacy. With Brahmanism, Hinduism, Ananai-kyō, Ural-kyō, and hundreds of other religions, they vie with one another. Spreading various doctrines and assertions—such as liberation, nirvana, emptiness, void, Reishiyutaijiu, Taishiyureijiu, the realization of Miroku, and the restoration of Shinsei—the confusion of thought is ceaseless. Among them, Ōkuronushinokami, entrenched in the capital of Haruna, holds a sword in his right hand while preaching the Quran in his left.

The brutal state of forcing all manner of ascetic practices upon believers is an evil, wicked religion that neither heaven nor earth can permit. To reform this, remove the suffering of the people, and provide aid, Hinodewake, by the command of the Lord God, accompanied by Umekō and Terukōshi, crossed the Kajika Pass and headed toward the Aoi Marsh. Having cleansed his heart and spirit in the light of the fifteenth-night moon, he was attacked by a great storm of Dekatan and proceeded through the great highlands. The center of the Dekatan highlands, the country of Toruman, had from ancient times believed in the teachings of Ural and had governed according to the original teachings of God; however, as the moon passed and the stars shifted,

thought deteriorated day and night. As the teachings of Ural declined day by day and month by month, taking advantage of the void, Brahmanism, Soku-kyō, and others rampaged, causing the national character to split in three and the national fortune to become perilous. Seeing this, King Garden, whose faith was not overly strong, awakened, began to revere God, and thought to show an example to his people. At that moment, a wicked monk who called himself the founder of the Sokubutsuen sect conspired with Ōashiwake, who had been dispatched by Ōkuronushinokami, to crush Toruman Castle and solidify the foundation of Sokubutsuen by employing every possible means.

To take advantage of the state of the country with its sparse defenses and to wield tyrannical power is truly terrifying. King Garden, Princess Chigusa, and the old ministers, the Right Guardian and Left Guardian, were heartbroken and troubled by discussions of national defense. However, the loyal ministers, the Left Guardian and Right Guardian, became the rust of blades, and it was inevitable that the pillars of the Toruman country were lost. Protected by Hinodewake, the two thousand five hundred elite soldiers led by Prince Chiuin surrounded Toruman Castle ten and twenty-fold, and launched a total attack, striking the rear of the entire army of Ōashiwake that was threatening the royal castle. Upon seeing this state of affairs,

King Garden leaped for joy, and while commanding five hundred castle soldiers, struck the great army of Ōashiwake from the front, back, left, and right. The arrogant enemy army, in the face of the sudden arrival of reinforcements, panicked, abandoned their horses, and threw away their weapons, fleeing in all directions for their lives. While fleeing, they set fire to houses here and there, and hid themselves while howling like wild dogs. The ugly village clouds that had enveloped Toruman Castle finally cleared, and it became an age of the gods where the sun shone brightly. The cheers of the people, high and low, erupted at once, with a vigor that shook heaven and earth. The dust of the wind settled completely,

and here, Garden Kshatriya, first of all, consoled the heroic spirits of the Left Guardian and Right Guardian who had fallen for the sake of loyalty. Wishing to express his gratitude, he built a shrine deep in the forest of Harima and enshrined them, naming it the Palace of Harima. Now, this pure forest, said to have passed through several thousand years, is filled with moss-covered, dense old trees, standing in rows so thick that it feels dark even in the daytime. In the blowing wind, the branches roar, singing of the peace of the world. Here, Hinodewake-shi, leading King Garden and the Prince, became the head of the festival, and just as he was about to chant the Norito in a clear voice,

Kiyūbā, who had monopolized the affection of Princess Chigusa, walked with a swagger and appeared arrogantly before Hinodewake. He exhausted his mouth in criticism, claiming there were flaws in the ritual of the festival, and heaped insults and mockery upon it. Prince Chiuin, becoming angry, seized the wicked monk Kiyūbā, bound him, placed him in a rattan cage, and returned toward the castle. No sooner had Princess Chigusa heard of this action by Chiuin than she stood up in a rage with her hair standing on end. The Toruman Castle, which had once been settled in peace, was again covered by black clouds, and it was inevitable that a great disturbance within the house was once again provoked. Ah, Imi-kami-kami.

Following the will of Kami, Zuigetsu, while reclining upon the floating boat of the Kōjiyutsudai, narrates the story of the ancient Kamiyo while gazing upon the raging waves of the Nitsuponkai at Yuraminato. Praying before the Mimahe of the Lord God to protect this tale, 'Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, grant me the Fuyu of the Mitama.'

King Garden, as a result of the confusion and panic caused by the sudden attack of the Brahman army, mistakenly took the life of his Umorinokami, Sumandji, and the loyal old minister, the Samorinokami, fell in the midst of the camp. Although they had fortunately repelled the enemy and felt some Ando, he feared that if Ōkuronushi of the capital of Haruna heard of this report, it would be difficult to predict when he might attempt a Kendojiurai and slaughter his capital city. Although they had achieved a great victory through the divine protection of the propaganda envoy Terukuniwake, the wisdom of Prince Chiuin, and the activities of the brave general Djanku, it was impossible to establish a nation in such warring times by military force alone. First, he would worship the Ōkami, then purify the souls of loyal retainers and righteous warriors to show the people a model of faith... thus he begged Terukuniwake, and it was decided to build a shrine called 'Kunibashira-jinja' beside the shrine where Uralhiko of the Harima Forest was enshrined, and to enshrine the heroic spirits of the Samorinokami and Umorinokami.

As King Garden, Prince Chiuin, Djanku, and the senior vassals in the castle offered tamagushi and were about to finish the ceremony under the guidance of the chief priest Terukuniwake, Kiuba, extremely indignant that Terukuniwake, a priest of the San-iku-kyo, had become the chief priest, and emboldened by having gained the favor of Princess Chigusa, rushed up to the altar with extreme violence, knocked off Terukuniwake's crown, stood before the shrine, and shouted in a loud voice:

Kiuba: 'Atsuhahahaha! It is I, Kiuba, the founder of the Sukobutsuen sect, who filled the divine power of the Brahman deity, who saved Tolman Castle from crisis, and who devised divine strategies to save the royal family. In the first place, this shrine is dedicated to the Kami Uralhiko and the divine king Pangu. Yet, I cannot accept that a Demo propaganda envoy, who is a subordinate of the evil Kami Susanoo-no-Mikoto who disturbs the world, should be the chief priest; it is fearful before the Kami. What kind of fool is this, covering the wisdom of the Kshatriya? The Ural shrine should have a propaganda envoy of the Ural religion as its chief priest. If by any chance an infidel propaganda envoy is allowed to serve as chief priest, why was I, Kiuba, the hero of this occasion, excluded, and why was an illegal ritual performed against the divine will? Who is the person in charge of the festival? Appear here now and explain the reason. The fact that Terukuniwake's crown fell so easily to the ground is a symbol that the Kami do not permit this. If you think of this spiritually, it means danger to the person of the King, and it means the overthrow of the nation. The heavenly savior, Kiuba, gives you this advice here.'

King Garden and the senior vassals lined up were stunned by Kiuba's overly bold declaration, and waited with bated breath for Terukuniwake's reply.

Terukuniwake did not panic in the slightest, and while his crown remained knocked off, he offered his tamagushi with composure, led the ritual officials, and as he was about to return to Tolman Castle, Kiuba spread his arms to block his path and shouted:
