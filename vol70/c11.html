Chapter 11: 血臭姫 [1778]

血臭姫〔1778〕

§ 1

Chigusahime looked around the entire assembly, suppressing the fire of Shini burning in her breast, deliberately feigning an Egaho, and with particular Ingin, she placed both hands on the floor, shook her head two or three times from Saiu, and while remaining bowed, she lifted her face, keeping her forehead and chin almost in a Heikausen.

Chigusahime: 'Greetings to you all, beginning with my husband, Ōsama Garden, the Chibōzetsurin Yūshi Taishi Chiwin, the Nobushi of Banyū, Ojii-sama Jank, the Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, Kumokuniwake—no, I am mistaken, Pikakuniwake—no, that is also wrong, the Kamuzukasa called Derukuwake, and all other Rekireki Katagata; I offer my congratulations on this Goshiugi of Konnichi. I cannot help but rejoice that with this, the foundation of Tenkataihei and Bandaifueki for the nation of Tolman is firmly established. By the way, what has become of Kyūbā-dono, the Meisō of the Sukobutsuen sect and the savior of the nation of Tolman? I, his mother, dare to ask this question of Taishi Chiwin. No matter what, between parent and child, there is no need for reserve. Come, speak the truth quickly.'

At these words, Taishi Chiwin could not utter a single phrase, and having only said 'Haa, haa,' he hung his head.

Chigusahime: 'Ho, ho, ho, ho, ho, ho, my son, Chiwin-dono, as I said before, between parent and child, there must be no distance if the household is to be governed in harmony. You surely would not behave with impiety toward your mother, would you?'

Taishi Chiwin was even more at a loss for words, and having only said 'Ha, ha,' he hung his head. Ōsama Garden narrowed his eyes sharply and said,

Ōsama Garden: 'Hey, Chigusahime, there is no need to ask my son; I shall explain it in detail, so listen well. That man Kyūbā is a fearless villain; at the time of the sacred and incomparable festival of the Ōkami, he laid his hands upon the precious crown of Terukuniwake-sama and was a ruffian who attempted to bring shame upon him before the crowd. I thought to cut him down on the spot, but no matter what, it was in the presence of the Ōkami, and it would be fearful to defile the sacred grounds with blood, so I held back, intending to punish him when the time was right. Just then, Kyūbā’s fearless actions became even more extreme; he slandered the Sendenshi with the most abusive language, spread his arms to obstruct the procession, and committed such behavior that it defied description. Unable to watch any longer, Taishi Chiwin ordered Jank to capture Kyūbā and throw him into prison. I believe this was the most timely measure to maintain the peace and order within the castle.'

Chigusahime: 'Ho, ho, ho, ho, ho, ho, indeed, to bind a ruffian and throw him into prison is what the law of the land dictates, and regarding this, I, Chigusahime, have not a single objection; however, I believe that Kyūbā is not inherently a wicked man, nor is he a madman. There must be some deep cause that drove him to such violent behavior. I, Chigusahime, cannot understand how you could produce such an extremely insolent criminal in the presence of the Ōkami, looking only at his external actions without any preliminary investigation. Ksatria-sama, I ask that you explain until I, your foolish concubine, am satisfied.'

Ōsama Garden: 'Do not be noisy; this is not for you to know. I have my own way of thinking. This is no place for a woman to interfere. Now, go back to your living quarters at once.'

千草『ホヽヽヽヽヽ、如何にも乱暴者を縛り上げ、獄に投じ玉ふは国法の定むる所、之について千草姫一言も異存は御座いませぬが、彼れキユーバーだつて、もとより悪人でもなく、狂乱者でもなからうと存じます。彼としてかかる暴挙に出でしめたるに就いては、何か深き原因がなければなりませぬ。軽卒に外面的行動のみをみて、一応の取調もなく、所もあらうに大神の御前に於て、不遜極まる罪人を出し玉ふとは千草姫心得ませぬ。刹帝利様、愚鈍なる妾の得心が行く迄御説明を願ひませう』

王『喧しう云ふな、汝の知る所でない。余は余としての考へがある。女の出しやばる幕でない。サ、トツトと汝が居間に立返れよ』

§ 2

Chigusa: 'Ho ho ho ho ho ho, to say that it is not a time for a woman to step forward, Setsuteirisama, what an outrageous remark that is. The King is the father of the people, and the Queen is the mother of the people. Even in the ancient Kamiyo, did not the two pillars, Izanaginomikoto and Izanaminomikoto, circle the heavenly pillar and, by embracing and uniting the parts that were excessive and the parts that were insufficient, give birth to the Shinjin of the world? Men protect the outside, and women protect the inside; no matter what you say, the events within this castle are under the authority of Chigusahime. If you cast me aside, the nation of Toruman will be like a lamp before the wind; no matter how magnificent a King you may be, can the country survive even a single day without the inner assistance of the Queen? Pray, consider this most carefully. Nevertheless, for the festival of the God of Ural, you have not employed a single missionary of the Ural faith, and instead, you have promoted the missionaries of the Ananaikyo, who follow the teachings of the crooked gods that the Great God detests, to serve as chief priests; this is truly to disrupt the principles of heaven and earth. If I hear correctly, that Terukuniwake is a rebel against the Ural faith, one who has sunk into the evil teachings of Ananai in the midst of his path; the wrath of the gods will surely become all the more severe, and one cannot know when or what kind of calamity might erupt due to divine punishment. Furthermore, if there were an unavoidable case for appointing a non-believer of the Ural faith as chief priest, why did you not make heavy use of that Kyūbā who saved the nation from crisis and appoint him as chief priest? To reward evil ministers and to admonish loyal subjects is the secret of politics; even in the spirit world, there are heaven and hell. There is no room to think otherwise than that Kyūbā, having been excluded from the first honors in this recent war and having been shamed before the gods, has resorted to such a reckless act out of grief and indignation. No, it is certainly not the will of Kyūbā, but without a doubt, it is a warning from the Great God of the Ural faith, Panko Shin-ō, borrowing his body. To cast such a loyal and righteous man into prison is beyond the pale; Chigusahime cannot agree to this at all. Before casting Kyūbā into prison, why did you not imprison the faction of Terukuniwake? If you ignore such clear reasons and act with partiality as the ruler of a nation, the path of rewards and punishments will be disordered from here on, justice will not be carried out, and the nation will once again become a scene of chaos. Is Terukuniwake not merely a missionary of a weak sect, and is Kyūbā not the great patriarch of the Sukobutsuen sect, who enjoys the favor of Daikokunushi? If by any chance our national fortune declines and Daikokunushi, seeking to wash away the shame of this recent war, comes pressing upon us with tens of thousands of soldiers, what will you do? At that time, the most necessary person will be none other than Kyūbā. Therefore, I have been conciliating him to the end, buying his favor in ordinary times as a preparation for emergencies, and making arrangements to save the nation from crisis. Without knowing such profound divine strategies and devilish schemes of mine, to be swayed by the momentum, take the side of Terukuniwake, and cast Kyūbā into prison—what a clumsy way to act! Even if Kyūbā has minor faults or evil elements, there is no need to investigate such trivial points. If you keep him in the castle as a man in your pocket, treating him with the utmost favor, even Daikokunushi would not be able to attack the main castle so easily. Even if, by misfortune, a national crisis were to erupt, if you were to dispatch Kyūbā, who is favored by Daikokunushi, would it not be a principle that it would be settled peacefully without any difficulty? For the eternal peace of the Toruman nation, you must bring Kyūbā out of prison, have Setsuteiri and the others, including Prince Chiuin and Jiyanku, bow nine times and a hundred times to apologize for his sins, and bind Terukuniwake before his eyes to clear Kyūbā's grudge; otherwise, it will be a grave matter for our nation. Furthermore, the union of the Ural faith and the Ananaikyo is the most disadvantageous thing for national policy. If you quickly destroy this union and form an alliance with the Sukobutsuen sect, the anger of Daikokunushi of the capital of Haruna will be appeased, and our nation will be able to enjoy peaceful dreams with the entire populace. Does Chigusahime's words still meet with your objection?'

Ō: 'Silence, Chigusahime; those rude words of yours toward Terukuniwake and his party, who saved the nation from crisis, are no longer to be overlooked.'

王『だまれ、千草姫、国家の危急を救ひ玉ひし照国別御一行に対し、無礼極まる其雑言、最早聞捨ならぬぞ』

§ 3

Chigusa: 'Ho ho ho ho ho ho, lining up your dried-out blue heads like those of a bitter gourd, a bottle gourd, or a watermelon, and holding your consultations—I, Chigusahime, looking down from afar, find your grand actions to destroy the nation truly ridiculous. As Setsuteirisama is of advanced age, he suffers somewhat from mental distress, and it is no wonder that everything he says lacks the mark of truth; but as for a son like Chiuin, he is a hot-blooded youth, and without understanding such reason, how could he ever hope to preserve this nation? From this moment on, I shall request the King to retire, and if you will pardon me, I, Chigusahime, shall become the Empress and govern the nation of Tolman. A mere child like Chiuin, who still has the smell of milk on his lips, is utterly insufficient to be my partner in consultation. Ho ho ho ho ho ho.'

Chiuin: 'I speak to my mother. Is it not because of my father, King Garden, that you occupy the position of Queen and are able to participate in politics as the mother of the nation? If the King is to retire, you, Mother, have no right to participate in politics. Pray, consider that well.'

Chigusa: 'Ho ho ho ho ho ho, how clever, you brat! What kind of nonsense are you spouting? Who taught you that a woman cannot become the sovereign of politics? Think about it well. Is not Amaterasu Ōmikami a Goddess? Did not the Empress of England, known as Elizabeth, become the sovereign of a vast territory where the sun never sets? For a child to talk back to his mother is the height of unfilial conduct. If you dare to say one more word, I shall use my authority as your mother to cast you into prison.'

Saying this, she stood up abruptly and returned to her own room, her footsteps sounding harsh against the tatami mats.

Setsuteiri, due to his overwhelming anger and his sense of duty toward Terukuniwake, had steeled himself to the point of executing Chigusahime, but he reconsidered, clutched his chest tightly, and stood there trembling while gritting his teeth. Prince Chiuin, seeing his father's unusual state, had also steeled his heart, determined that if it came to the worst, he would cling to his father's arms to save Chigusahime. Setsuteiri finally opened his mouth:

Setsuteiri: 'Terukuniwake-sama, the missionary, that Chigusahime has been straining her brain in the wars since the other day, and as a result, her mind has become deranged. I humbly beg you to look upon her with the divine Naohi and Ōnaohi, and grant her your generous forgiveness. I feel as though I would like to crawl into a hole.'

He said this with a look of distress. Terukuniwake remained calm and replied:

Terukuniwake: 'Setsuteirisama, such concern is unnecessary. It was certainly not spoken from the true heart of Chigusahime-sama. There is a slight reason for this, but I cannot explain it today. I do not take her words to heart in the slightest. Please, set your mind at ease.'

King: 'Ah, hearing that, I am relieved. I humbly pray for your guidance for a long time to come. By the way, Terukuniwake-sama, what do you think? I am thinking of punishing Kyuba severely to cut off the root of the evil.'

Terukuniwake: 'I am a missionary who saves people; even if they are demons, snakes, or devils, I face them with a sincere heart, exhaust the limits of sincerity, and lead them to return to the path of truth—that is the way of the gods. I believe that punishments and the like are unnecessary. Every person harbors the divine spirit; for a human to judge another human is an act of extreme arrogance. I believe it would be best to save Kyuba as soon as possible. If you do so, the heart of Chigusahime-sama will also soften, and you will surely see the dawn of harmony in your household.'

King: 'Indeed, I think that is reasonable for the time being. Chiuin, what do you think?'

§ 4

Taishi: 'Yes, I have defied the will of my mother, which is irreplaceable in both heaven and earth, and have placed that Kyūbā under arrest before the eyes of the Kokumin. Regarding this, I hold a most firm Ketsushin. Should he enter the Jiyōnai again and conspire with my mother to wield Keni, our Ōshitsu would be like a Tomoshibi before the wind. Though these are the Okotoba of a Sendenshi, I cannot give a Sokutafu on this matter alone.'

Janku: 'With all due respect, I address the Ōsama and the Taishisama. Whatever the case may be, I believe it would be best to Kumito the Okokoro of Chigusahime-sama and release that Kyūbā. If by any Manmanichi he should take Datsusenteki Kaudō again, would it not be sufficient to show no Yōshiya and Tōgoku him once more? Though I am but humble, I, this Janku, intend to dedicate my Yosei to the Ōshitsu and stake my very life to protect the nation.'

Ōsama: 'No, hearing that puts my mind at ease. Both the left and right pillars of defense have passed away in the recent battles, and I was just lamenting in my heart the lack of a leading minister. If you, whose wisdom and courage are peerless, are willing to stake your life to remain in the capital and protect our nation, what more is there to say? I leave the handling of Kyūbā entirely to you. Manage it as you see fit.'

Janku: 'I am grateful for your immediate consent. Taishisama, this course of action by Janku may not be to your liking, but for now, I pray you leave it to this old retainer.'

Chiu: 'I shall say nothing regarding this problem. I have my own way of thinking.'

Terukuniwake stood up and began to sing in a clear, resonant voice.

Having finished the chant and concluded the dance, all those present were emboldened by this Kototama, and it was decided to leave all matters to the will of the Gods, bringing the banquet to a peaceful close.
