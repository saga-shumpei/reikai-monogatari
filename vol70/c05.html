Chapter 5: Flower Bird Mountain [1772]

花鳥山〔1772〕

§ 1

Amatsumisora is exceedingly pure, with clouds of Goshiki trailing, while Hōō, Kujiyaku, and Momodori are flying in the Teikū. The earth is a carpet of Aodatami, where purple, Asagi, white, and Kiiro flowers bloom and scent the air. The forms of Kotefu flutter, and while gazing most joyfully upon the Kuōkei of the Tengoku Jiyōdo, feeling as though blown by the wind, one proceeds with ease through the Kūchiu, three or four shaku above the Chijiyō. Gazing toward the Zenpau, the roofs of Kogane glitter, shining in the Amatsuhikage, manifesting a Saugonsekai. Gazing toward the Yunde, the Aōnabara is calm, and here and there, flickering like Kotefu dancing in the Kūchiu, are white-shining sails, full and partial. The birds of Goshiki are to the right and left, and the delight of running over the waves, with cool winds blowing eternally, sending an indescribable fragrance to the surroundings of those who walk the path, is truly endearing.

Here, a traveler, leaning on a spear with one hand, rests on the hillside of a round mountain where green grass grows thick. Looking back at the path he has traveled, he smokes tobacco and rests with a smile. To such a place, from the foot of the mountain, a woman comes climbing, calling out, 'Oi, oi!' Looking closely, how could it be—it is the unexpected Chigusahime.

Fluttering her cool, pure, white robes in the wind, she approaches the traveler's side, and with a face brimming with smiles, Chigusahime: 'You are Migimori-sama, the Sumandji. Oh, my, why have you arrived at such a peaceful mountain? It is a mystery.' When she asks this, the traveler nods and says, Migimori: 'You are a noble princess; why have you come here? I cannot comprehend it. In the inner chamber of Tolman Castle, while I was engaged in various war councils to resist the invading army of King Garden, the Sashimoji, and Ōashiwake, I, who could not reach an agreement, thought that I had lost my life at their hands for the sake of the noble Lord.'

Migimori: 'Is it a dream that I thought so? This body, which I cannot comprehend—what place is this? It is a sacred land like the Jiyōdo, where hundreds and thousands of flowers bloom, a place whose very name I do not know. Why have you come searching for us? Wonders upon wonders are piling up, and I do not know what to do.' As he speaks, Chigusa nods and says, Chigusahime: 'This is the so-called Jiyōdo of the third stage of the Tenkai. My life span in the world ended, and by the command of the Lord God, I was ordered to dwell in the Jiyōdo, so I came joyfully and briskly through the flower-blooming fields. It seems you, too, are in a position to dwell in the Tenkai.'

Chigusahime: 'I heard it clearly from Ibukido-nushi-no-Kami. Do not let your heart remain in the Genkai or such places; let us ascend quickly to the Tenkai, the place of divine guidance, together with me. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase. It is the noble divine arrangement. You have long been a bachelor, and although I have a husband, our worlds of existence are different, and today I am a single person. Because of the difference in will and thought, I cannot remain with King Garden forever in the Reikai. Your intellect is my intellect, and my will communicates entirely with your will. In this paradise of the Tenkai opened by God, let us two become husband and wife.'

Chigusahime: 'Let us serve the work of the Tengoku Jiyōdo with all our might for eternity. How is it, Migimori-sama?' When she says this, Migimori nods and says, Migimori: 'Ah, how grateful, how grateful. While I was in the Gensei, I was in love with you. Though I say that, the lowly ranks of the Genkai stood in the way, and I could not express the full measure of my heart even once. Your heart was the same, and I knew early on that you loved me, but considering the giri and ninjo of the reality-world, and its laws, I endured and suppressed it. Now, beyond this, it is the bond ordained by God.'

Migimori: 'There is no need to be reserved with anyone. The reward for the two of us, who exhausted all possible good deeds in the reality-world, has now ripened, and just like this, let us dwell in the garden of joy, transcending time and space, and live happily and joyfully for a thousand, eight thousand, and ten thousand generations. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase. I praise the divine favor of the spirit.'

§ 2

While they were singing to each other in this way, a fireball illuminated the sky, made a roaring sound, and fell before the two. Its radiance was like a diamond, like the light of platinum. The two were startled, and covering their eyes with both hands, they crouched down on the spot. The firelight instantly transformed into a beautiful divine being, and with a calm voice,

Angel: 'Sumandji-sama, Chigusahime-sama, I am an angel who has come from the Daiichireigoku to welcome you. Please, open your eyes.'

As the two replied with 'Hai' and slowly opened their eyes, a divine being clad in white robes and possessing great dignity stood before them seven or eight shaku away, smiling.

Angel: 'I am Kototamawakenomikoto. Sumandji-sama, Chigusahime-sama, you have done your best in the Genkai for the sake of the Toruman country and for the sake of the many people. And although you two were a true married couple whose intentions and thoughts were in harmony, you endured suffering everywhere, overcame the demon of love, and truly endured it throughout your lives. In the Shinkai, your good deeds in particular have been recorded. Now, I shall guide you to the Daini-reigoku.'

Sumandji: 'Hai, thank you very much. To meet a god in such an unexpected place, how grateful I am. My gratitude cannot be expressed in words.'

Kototama: 'It is the fruit that has ripened in the field you cultivated. I would be troubled if you were to thank me. Today's joy is the fruit of the joy that you yourself cultivated and nurtured. Chigusahime-sama, the same applies to you; you must never, ever offer thanks. Now, follow me.'

With these words, Kototamawakenomikoto stood up and went ahead, and the two, while caring for each other, stepped over and over the lush green hills that looked like clouds and waves, proceeding toward the east, ever toward the east.

Before they knew it, the sound of resonant music could be heard from all sides, and as they looked, they found they had already arrived upon a hill that looked as if it were paved with square rocks.

Kototama: 'This is the famous Kachōzan in the Daini-reigoku. Look, beautiful birds with green wings spread wide and red crowns upon their heads are soaring in all directions, emitting beautiful voices, and here too, beautiful flowers, such as do not exist in the world on earth, are blooming in profusion and emitting fragrance. This is your eternal home. Whatever you wish to eat, it will ripen instantly on the branches of these beautiful trees according to your desires, so please pick them and eat.'

Sumandji: 'I would like to ask the Angel-sama one thing. I heard you say Reigoku, but is the Reigoku not a paradise where the missionaries gather? I am a minor official of the Toruman country, and while I served the Ural religion in my daily life, I did not practice deep faith, and Chigusahime-sama is the same; as the queen of the Toruman country and as the mother of the people, she did her best, but if it were a missionary or a pastor, that would be one thing, but how is it that we, who were immersed in the secular world, have been able to come to the Reigoku? I do not understand this reason.'

Kototama: 'As you have asked, the Reigoku is indeed the eternal residence where the good spirits of missionaries and national leaders should come. In the present Genkai, not a single missionary, monk, priest, or pastor possesses the qualifications to ascend to the Reigoku. Furthermore, there is no one who ascends to heaven; all are registered in hell, and in the Jigoku-kai, they bear the fruits of confusion, contradiction, greed, derailment, and darkness, and are continuing to act recklessly, carving each other's flesh and sipping each other's blood. Although you were not a missionary during your lifetime, you did your best as a human being in the Genkai. In short, even if you did not believe in a superficial god, your true guardian deity was already suited to the Reigoku of the heavenly realm, and you were already registered in the divine records. The entire universe is established according to the principle of correspondence. This Kachōzan of the Daini-reigoku is yours. Your spirits had already created this beautiful sacred mountain while in the Genkai. You need not be reserved with anyone. Prosper forever, live happily as a married couple, and serve the work of the Shinkai. Farewell.'

As he was about to depart, Chigusahime hurriedly raised her white hand and said,

Chigusahime: 'Excuse me, Angel-sama, I have just suddenly thought of something. I recall that I joined hands with a person called Kyūbā of the Sukobutsuen sect, and that both of us fainted at the same time. What has become of that Kyūbā? I ask you for a moment.'

Kototama: 'Since his life still remains in the Genkai, he is now wandering in the Yachimata. However, you must not think of a person like him, whose virtue of Aizen is thin and whose light of Chie and Shiyōkaku is dull. Because your own Chie and Shiyōkaku will become dull, you must never think of the Genkai from now on. Your use in the Genkai is already finished. Sumandī-sama, please also never, ever think of the Genkai in the same way.'

Both of them bowed their heads suddenly and were overcome with Arigatanamida. Kototamawakenomikoto was wrapped in five-colored clouds, became a great fire-light, and returned while roaring through the air toward the eastern sky. Afterward, the two looked at each other, and

Sumandī: 'Hime-sama, is this not a mysterious thing? It is as if we are dreaming.'

Chigusahime: 'It is truly mysterious beyond words. There is no mistake that both you and I have died. Nevertheless, my consciousness is becoming clearer and clearer, and how can it be thought of as reality that I am permitted to be a married couple with you, my beloved, on the summit of such a beautiful mountain? It is truly mysterious beyond words.'

Sumandī: 'In the Genkai, I am your subject. And you are a person of nobility next to the King in the Toruman country. No matter how much it is by the permission of the God, it is truly too awe-inspiring for me to call you my wife.'

Chigusahime: 'Sumandī-sama, now that we have separated the Gen and the Yū, is everything not a clean slate? Coming to such a noble Reikoku, to still use such empty etiquette and formal language is like deceiving your own thoughts.'

Sumandī: 'Indeed, that is so. Then, I shall call you my wife anew. Please call me your husband. Although one daughter is left behind, I shall put that matter out of my mind as well.'

Chigusahime: 'Please do so. Now, let us two sing of this joy.'

Thereupon, the two joined hands and, like butterflies, raised their clear voices on the summit of Flower Bird Mountain and began to dance while singing.

Chigusahime and Sumandī: 'When I gaze at the Amatsu-mikara, / Like a hundred angels, like stars, / They shine and protect my body, / Sometimes far, sometimes near, / How grateful I am. / When I look down at my feet, / With rocks of Kataiwa and Tokiwa, / The mountain of God was built and solidified. / The birds I have never seen are beautiful, / Spreading their wings in the Tenkai, / The hundred flowers of auspicious omens, / Compete in luster and bloom fragrantly, / Comforting the eyes of us two, / To our heart's content. / Ah, Kannagara-kamu-kamu, / Human life is not limited, / To only about a hundred years of the Gense. / Until the end of many billions of years, / My spirit lives on, / Living and prospering, making flowers bloom.'

Chigusahime and Sumandī: 'Let us enjoy the true harvest, / Let us enjoy the true harvest. / God is with us, / And we are with God. / God and God are united, / The country of God is ever-widening, / Expanding, our marriage, / May it be spring forever, / May it prosper forever, / May summer come forever, / May we enjoy it forever. / The heaven becomes higher, / The color of the air is clearer, / The earth becomes wider, / All the grasses and Chigusa shine. / In the midst of the world of light, / You and I spend our lives. / Is it a dream, reality, or an illusion? / No, no, it is certainly not a dream. / Leaving the floating world of dreams,'

Chigusahime and Sumandī: 'Ascending to the true country, / Where the true God resides, / Plucking the true flowers, / Let us live the true life. / Ah, Kannagara-kamu-kamu, / May your spirits be blessed.'

Singing thus, the two became residents of the eternal Reikoku. Ah, Kannagara-reikō-saki-wa-se.

『天津御空を眺むれば 百のエンゼル星の如 輝き玉ひ吾身をば 或は遠く或は近く 守らせ玉ふ有難さ 脚下を伏して眺むれば 堅磐常磐の巌もて 造り固めし神の山 見なれぬ鳥は麗しき 翼拡げて天界の 瑞祥ふたひ百花は 艶を競ふて咲匂ひ 吾等二人の眼をば 心ゆく迄慰むる あゝ惟神々々 人の命は現世の 百年許りに限らない 幾億年の末までも 吾精霊は生通し 生て栄えて花咲かし

と歌ひ乍ら、二人は永久の霊国に住民となつた。あゝ惟神霊幸倍坐世。
