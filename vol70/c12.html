Chapter 12: Great 魅勒 [1779]

大魅勒〔1779〕

§ 1

In the shade of the trees in the Harima forest, two large men wearing masks and hoods sit on a bench, whispering to each other about something.

Reru: 'Hey, Mark, that fellow Kyūbā of the Sukobutsuen sect was thrown into prison thanks to the Eidan of Taishi-sama, which is quite satisfying, but can we really feel at ease hearing this? Rumor has it that he is a creature like Rasputin, who has bought the favor of Chigusahime and is doing all sorts of outrageous things. Since it is the Jisetsu when the rooster crows, the Keni of the Setsuteiri and the Keni of the wise Taishi will surely be Jiurin, and Chigusahime will undoubtedly rescue him. Once that happens, he will surely implement the Shihonshiyugi system more and more, treat us of the Kasōkaikifu with Atsupaku and exploitation, and enforce a wicked government that makes it impossible to live in this world. That is why, from our position as representatives of the Kōjō movement, if we do not do something about him as soon as possible, we cannot sleep with our minds at rest. I have come out here in this mask and hood to urge you on, but I have no intention of committing robbery. I lured you here with sweet talk of making easy money, with the intention of slaughtering that Rasputin while we still can.'

Mark: 'What is it? I was driven by the difficulties of life and could not make ends meet, so I jumped to the conclusion that you had finally revealed your true colors and were asking for my support to go out on your first night as a thief. I was prepared for a frightening experience, sneaking into houses or robbing passersby, not knowing if I could get the money I wanted or not, but since it was the word of a friend with whom I had promised to share life and death, I could not refuse, and I came here resigned to my fate, thinking, "I guess I am becoming a thief who will live a short and thick life from today on..." However, hearing your intentions, I am relieved. Exterminating the poisonous insects of the nation is truly the duty of a Kokushi. In a world where Betto is not practiced, there can be no reform or good government. We must offer Rasputin as a blood sacrifice, chill the hearts of the Bu-religionists, and turn the guts of the fake religious leaders inside out. Even if we do not do anything violent, the Rama-class fellows already fear us as violent men, so there is no telling when or what kind of plot they will use to trap us and have us arrested and taken to the ○○ head temple as suspects in a Betto attempt. If it comes to that, we must slaughter him while we can; otherwise, when he rises to power, he will surely issue useless laws like the Kōjōkai Bokumetsurei or the Bōryokudan Torishimari-rei to torment and kill us and our fellows more and more. Since he is currently imprisoned, let us take advantage of this, somehow snatch him away tonight, and finish him off in the dark like wringing a Shamo's neck.'

Reru: 'Ha, interesting, interesting. Come, it is time for the prison guards to be fast asleep. Come, let us go.'

The two men moved through the darkness of the tree shade and advanced toward the back gate of the prison outside Toruman Castle.

Reru: 'Hey, this is quite a problem; the wall is too high to jump over.'

Mark: 'Shall we look for a ladder under the eaves of some house and enter? And once we are inside, the first thing we must do is remove the bolt of the gate so that we can escape at any time. I will go look for a ladder under the eaves of the town houses around here, so you think about where Kyūbā is being held. He is a magician, so he is good at burning blue fire in the air. He uses that magic as a means of proselytizing. Also, he will surely use that magic to surprise the prison guards and spread the rumor that "this fellow is indeed an Ikigami-sama," and he is undoubtedly plotting to get out of prison as soon as possible.'

Re: 'Un, that is true. I shall think it over well.'

Thus, Mark hid his figure for a while, piercing through the darkness. Left behind, Re muttered to himself:

'Ah, it truly does not go well. We became representatives of the Kaujiyōkuwai to overthrow the Lama class and build a truly peaceful world; for ten years this year, we have forgotten sleep and food as if it were but a single day, and though our wives and children are weeping from cold and hunger, we have been working diligently day and night. Yet, no matter what one says, it is a world where the strong are strong and the weak are weak. Even if three of us gather to talk, we are immediately seized by the Bansō and thrown into Raugoku; it is such a dangerous world that we cannot even move our hands or feet. Despite that, that fellow Kyūbā, a spy for Ōkuronushi, came out to this Toruman country and acted as he pleased, looking down upon the Toruman Ōke and the Kokumin as if they were mere filth, praising the Shintoku of Ōkuronushi, opening a Jiyakyō, and trying to torment us more and more. But the joy and thrill I felt when he was bound before the eyes of the public, thanks to the wise decision of Prince Chiwin! When he was bound, it was not just him alone. It was as if an iron hammer had been brought down upon the heads of the Bansō and Lama monks who were bribed by him. Nevertheless, hearing that the Great Lady favors that evil monk, we can no longer remain silent. For the sake of the nation, we must execute this traitor within this very night, or there will be no turning back. Surely, a national crisis will break out again and torment us, the Kokumin. Ah, Mark, I wonder what has become of him. I wish he would come quickly. Somehow, I am so impatient I cannot help it. If we dawdle, the jailer might wake up, and it would be terrible if we ended up being thrown into Raugoku ourselves.'

He was muttering thus. Prince Chiwin, following the suggestion of Jiyanku, thought that if Kyūbā were released early tomorrow morning, he would surely return to the castle, break his mother's heart again, and throw the castle into chaos. He had come in disguise, intending to drag him out within this night and lock him away in a cave on Mount Arai with a jailer assigned to him. He had been hiding in the shade of a Sala tree, thinking, but since there were two suspicious figures in masks, he pricked up his ears, wondering if they were spies for Chigusahime. Finding that it was not the case, he felt somewhat relieved, stepped out from the shade of the tree, and said quietly:

Chiwin: 'Who are you? Hearing your conversation from earlier, your aspirations are truly admirable, and I am in full agreement. You appear to be members of the Kaujiyōkuwai, but surely you cannot drag that Kyūbā out with just one or two people. I am Prince Chiwin, and I intend to wake the gatekeeper, use my authority to make him open the gate, and drag Kyūbā out, so you should help me.'

Re: 'Yes, I am called Re, and I am a representative of the movement for improvement. Hearing the words of the Prince-sama just now, I am truly filled with joy for the sake of all the people. Please command us for whatever purpose you may have.'

As they were speaking, Mark came along, carrying a long ladder, panting for breath.

Mark: 'Hey, Re, I finally stole a ladder. Come, hurry, hurry!'

Re: 'Oh, that was hard work. But look, Prince Chiwin-sama is here.'

Mark: 'Eh, eh?'

Saying only that, he was startled and fell onto the ground on his backside.

Chiwin: 'Ha ha ha, well, are you also a member of the Kaujiyōkuwai? Do not worry at all. I am currently consulting with this man to take Kyūbā by force, so be at ease.'

Mark: 'Oh, wise Prince-sama, thank you very much.'

Re: 'If I may, Prince-sama, now that the ladder has arrived, there should be no need to wake the gatekeeper. If you do such a thing, it will naturally reach the ears of Chigusahime-sama that you have let Kyūbā escape, and I fear it will cause you trouble later. Therefore, please wait here, Prince-sama. We two will drag that devil out, even if we have to beat the jailer down should he resist.'

Taishi: 'Naruhodo, that is also a plan; please exert yourself and try it. In return, if this succeeds, I will surely give you a reward.'

Re: 'No, that is out of the question. I am sacrificing my life for the sake of the banmin; could I possibly do such a dangerous thing for the sake of wanting a reward? Rather than that, please lend your heart and listen to the cries of the banmin.'

Tai: 'No, I too have been trying to listen to the voices of the people from ordinary times, and I have been going in and out of the streets of the shisei in various disguises, so I know your kuwatsudōburi well. Please continue your kuwatsudō to the utmost. The baugai is currently very strong and you must be troubled, but before long, the capital of shiyōri will surely draw near.'

The futari climbed the ladder like monkeys and vaulted over the wall, opened the gate door from the inside quietly, and as they listened carefully to see in which raugoku Kyūbā was, something like a blue fireball was flickering and disappearing from the madoguchi... 'Ah, the tetsuki is here,' they thought, and as they approached, they saw two rauban snoring loudly, leaning against their isu. The futari quietly took the keys hanging from the rauban's waists, unlocked the lock, one entered inside, and while the other kept watch over the rauban, they pulled Kyūbā out and successfully brought him outside the gate quietly. When the rauban suddenly awoke, the door of the raugoku was open. The keys at their waists had been stolen and were nowhere to be found. Suddenly, they began to shout, 'Prison break! Prison break!' Hearing this voice, the guards of the raugoku all at once awoke, and carrying lanterns and pine torches, they ran about in all directions. Chiwin Taishi gave the futari strict instructions, threw Kyūbā into a cave on Mount Arai, and gave Rēru and Māku a large amount of money, deciding to have them guard him until a certain time. The futari followed the will of the Taishi, kept the secret, and did not even reveal it to their own wives and children.

Since it was said that once the middle of the night passes, it is the next day, Janku ordered four or five officials to release Kyūbā, but when they went to see, it was a great commotion of a jailbreak; thinking that he must report this to the King and Chigusahime by all means, he visited the King's living room, and already Chiwin Taishi was whispering with the King with their heads together.

Janku: 'I report. Yesterday, having received your permission, I sent minor officials to release that Kyūbā, but when they went to investigate, he had been stolen away by someone, and the guards of the raugoku are in a state of panic. If by any chance he escapes back to the capital of Haruna, appears before Daikokunushi, borrows tens of thousands of soldiers, and makes a comeback, it will be a terrible matter; therefore, I wish to send people in all eight directions right now to search for his whereabouts.'

The King and the Taishi remained calm and were not particularly surprised.

King: 'What, you say Kyūbā has broken out of prison and escaped? Let him be, let him be; there is no need to worry about it particularly.'

Janku: 'As you say, but we must determine his whereabouts right now and take measures so that he does not escape back to the capital of Haruna. I pray that you will entrust this matter to your old servant...'

Tai: 'Ah, Janku-dono, please do not worry at all. I have an idea. I will never, ever let him escape back to the capital of Haruna. In any case, please trust me.'

Janku: 'Since it is the words of the Taishi-sama, there will surely be no oversight. If so, this old servant shall withdraw now.'

Chigusahime was agitated and could not sleep a wink, and furthermore, her hearing had become extremely acute, to the point where even the buzzing of a mosquito could reach her ears. She pushed open the door and entered the King's room.

'Listen, my son Chiwin, if I listen to your words just now, were you not speaking as if you had some certainty regarding Kyūbā's situation? Now, with a mother's authority, I will question you to the end. Where did you hide him? Confess truthfully.'

千草姫は気が立つて一目も眠られず、且又聴覚が非常に鋭敏と為り、蚊の囁きでさへも、耳に入るやうになつてゐた。ドアを排して王の室に入り来り、

『コレ悴チウイン、今其方の言葉を聞けば、キユーバーの身の上につき、何か確信あるものの如く云つて居つたぢやないか。サ、母の権威を以て飽く迄も詮索する。何処へ隠したのだ。有体に白状しなさい』

§ 4

Taishi: 'Hahauhesama, is there any Dauri that I would know of such a thing? I have only just learned from the Chiushin of Jank that the figure of Kyūbā has vanished, and I was in the midst of being terribly Shinpai.'

Chigusahime: 'No, no, I shall not let you say such things. It is clearly revealed in the edge of your Kotoba. You are the Chiyōhonnin who hid Kyūbā, are you not?'

Taishi: 'This is outrageous. How could I, in the position of a Taishi, go to a place like a Raugoku in the middle of the Yoruyonaka?'

Chigusahime: 'Ho ho ho ho ho, it is strange because you are a person who can go there. Since you, while holding the position of Taishi, always hide from the eyes of the Ōsama, appear and disappear in the streets of the Shisei, associate with the Kasōkaikifu, and are rumored to dally with base women, going to a place like a Raugoku is Asameshimahe for you. Even if you try to Gomakuwa this Chigusahime, the Ikimiya of Dai-Miroku, by saying such things, it is Dame.'

Taishi: 'Hahauhesama, you are saying strange things. Who did you hear such a thing from—this Ikimiya of Dai-Miroku, of which I have never heard even once until now?'

Chigusahime: 'Hen, how could a Aonisai like you understand? This mother heard it from the Kamisama. From today, this Nikutai is renewed; I am the Ikimiya of the Dai-Miroku of Shimotsu-Iwane, the Savior of the three thousand worlds, the Hinodewake. If you think of me merely as the queen of a tiny Tolman country, this divine pillar will be a little troubled. Ho ho ho ho ho, oh my, speaking of the Ōsama, Jank, and the Taishi, what pathetic faces you all have. Are you surprised that this Chigusahime has suddenly become a divine pillar? I am the Ikimiya of the Hinodewake, to whom the three thousand worlds are visible at a glance.'

Taishi: 'Ah, is that so? Well, that is truly splendid. Through the divine virtue of Terukuniwake-sama having come to this castle, Hahauhesama has also suddenly become possessed by a deity, and has advanced to become a magnificent angel's Nikumiya, a Hinodewake-sama. In that case, you must surely know the whereabouts of Kyūbā.'

Chigusahime: 'That is a given. I am the heavenly being of the First Spirit World, the great pillar of the Dai-Miroku of Sokotsu-Iwane, the Savior of the three thousand worlds, the Nikumiya of the Hinodewake.'

Taishi: 'Indeed, that is a splendid Kamisama. Then please, let me know the whereabouts of Kyūbā. If only that is known, I shall serve you as the Ikimiya of the Hinodewake, and Chichiuhesama will also be pleased and entrust all matters of government to you.'

Chigusahime: 'Ho ho ho ho ho, how clever, you brat, you intend to corner your mother. Do you still doubt this mother? There is no mistake that I am the Hinodewake. The Kami never tells a lie. To ask the great pillar of the Dai-Miroku of Sokotsu-Iwane to find the whereabouts of Kyūbā as if it were some sort of fortune-telling, and to say "I will believe you are the Ikimiya of the Hinodewake if you do"... Hen, do not make a fool of me. By the appropriate Dauri, this Chigusahime resonates with the First Spirit World, and I shall never peer into places like a Raugoku, which should be compared to the land of roots and the bottom of the earth. If I were to use my divine eye for such a place, my hard-won wisdom and enlightenment would dull, and I would have to become a denizen of a devil's lair. To ask the spirit of a heavenly being of the great First Spirit World to reveal the whereabouts of someone thrown into a Raugoku... is there not a limit to your ignorance of the Dauri of things? Ho ho ho ho ho. No matter how wise you claim to be, while you may know of the manifest world, you surely cannot understand the news of the spirit world. Now, understand this: this Tolman country is determined as the root ground where the great pillar of the Dai-Miroku of Sokotsu-Iwane appears, takes hold of the national government, and turns the three thousand worlds into the world of Miroku. From the perspective of the Nikutai, the Ōsama is the husband of Chigusahime, but from the perspective of the Kami, he is no different from a slave; there is a difference as vast as heaven and earth. From now on, the Kami will conduct politics. If this Ikimiya does not judge good and evil, right and wrong, it will be Dame. Did the propaganda envoy of Terukuniwake not chant it yesterday? "The Kami appears on the surface to distinguish good and evil, right and wrong..." It is the Savior of the three thousand worlds, who has appeared as the Hinodewake, having made the Nikutai of Chigusahime the mechanism, and having lodged the spirit of the Dai-Miroku in the spirit of the heavenly being of the First Spirit World. Ho tsu ho ho ho ho, oh my, the stiff face of the Kshatriya-dono, the pathetic look of Chiwin, u tsu fu ho ho ho ho.'

She laughed until she collapsed.
