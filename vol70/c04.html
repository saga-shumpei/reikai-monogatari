Chapter 4: 共倒れ [1771]

共倒れ〔1771〕

§ 1

When Taishi's Chiuin first came to negotiate with Kyūbā, accompanied by his younger sister Chinrei and Umori's daughter Harisu, he secretly listened to the yōsu from the shadows of objects. Deeming it a daijiken of no small consequence, he conspired with Chinrei and Harisu during the yachiu, keeping it naimitsu from King Gāden and Samori. He issued orders to his fukushin subordinates to summon heiin from across the zenkoku, feigning that it was by Ōmei.

Meanwhile, King Gāden and Samori were busy arming five hundred soldiers within the jiyōnai, while exhorting their buka to crush the enemy in a single itsusen should they press forward, pouring all their energy into the kobu of their shiki. Shiyōgun Ōdaruwake had led three thousand troops to the foot of the castle, but upon learning from the dozens of cavalrymen guarding Kyūbā that Kyūbā had penetrated deep into the palace, he feared that opening hostilities in vain and causing the loss of Kyūbā's life would be a disaster. He worried about what kind of rebuke he might receive from the Great Lord. He reasoned that the hero Kyūbā, unparalleled in history, must have some profound strategy, having entered the castle alone to seek the dawn of an amicable resolution through diplomatic negotiation. He strictly warned his subordinates not to launch a general attack until Kyūbā's orders arrived, and remained encamped for three days and three nights to guard Kyūbā. King Gāden and Samori, thinking that Chigusa-hime had disappeared because she had heard of Umori's end and fled in fear of the calamity reaching her, focused their entire attention on military preparations, failing to notice the zen-sen zen-tō that Chigusa-hime was waging with all her secret arts.

After about half an hour, Kyūbā regained consciousness, rolled his round eyes, looked at Chigusa-hime's face, and said:

Kyūbā: 'Hey, aren't you Chigusa-hime? You grabbed my pulse point at such an inopportune moment with those delicate arms of yours, and you put me through a terrible ordeal, didn't you? I was away on a journey to the underworld for a while. Isn't it a bit too harsh to grab me like that?'

Chigusa-hime: 'Oh, you have no idea how worried I was. I followed your orders and gripped with all my might, but after you said "un" once, you wouldn't answer me no matter what I said. I thought you were terribly angry and refused to speak to me, so I immediately performed water ablutions and prayed to the Brahman gods, and only then did you finally speak. It is truly wicked of you to talk about journeys to the underworld and such. You wouldn't speak to me or show me anything but anger for half an hour.'

Kyūbā: 'No, I really must have fainted. I never lie. If you are going to hold my hand from now on, please hold the tips of my fingers. It is troublesome if you grab my pulse point.'

Chigusa: 'Where is the logic in the Savior of the world fainting because he was held by my slender arms? You only speak lies. Ho ho ho ho ho ho.'

Kyūbā: 'That is truly the case. Actually, I did not faint. I was doing that to test your true heart. No matter what is said, I am the Savior of the three thousand worlds. How could I be affected by such trivial things?'

Chigusa: 'Ho ho ho ho ho, you are truly a cruel person, making people worry so much.'

She then tries to grab his wrist again.

Kyūbā is startled and pulls his hand away, saying:

Kyūbā: 'Hey, once is enough for holding hands. Instead, I shall hold yours this time. Come, give me your hand, give me your hand.'

Chigusa: 'Please hold it until I am out of breath. Until I have seen the state of the crossroads... and until I have met the officials of the underworld and been told of the lotus seat where you and I shall live forever. I want to reserve a special seat before heaven becomes full.'

Kyūbā: 'Ha ha ha ha ha, hey Princess, if this hand of Kyūbā touches your hand, I will truly faint again. Is that alright with you?'

Chigusahime: 'I am quite content, for even if I were to be killed, this body is not my own. It is something I have dedicated to you, and your life is the same, is it not?'

Kiuba thought to himself... This woman is quite skilled with her hands. It seems she has reached the essence of Jiujiyutsu. If I do not grip her with all my might, make her faint, and go to peek into Yachimata, I will not be able to bear being bossed around in the future. My authority as a husband will be reduced to zero. Very well, I must grip her vital points with all my might once more to show her my prowess, or I might end up under the rule of a henpecked husband, perhaps even bound by the strings of a Yumaki. This is the tactical maneuvering of love, a one-in-a-thousand chance... With that, he thrust out his hairy hand and gripped the princess's hand, which was as white as a fresh bamboo shoot, so hard he meant to crush her bones. Chigusahime, however, could perceive the very depths of Kiuba's heart, so no matter how much strength Kiuba put into his grip, she felt no pain at all; it felt to her as if she were being touched by raw cotton. She was a woman of unexpected fortitude. Nevertheless, she feigned a faint, and the moment he gripped her, she groaned, twisted her face, and collapsed on the spot.

Kiuba: 'Ha ha ha ha ha ha, as expected of a woman. She has finally given up and collapsed. If I leave her to peek into the Iumeikai for about half an hour like this, she will surely be awestruck by my Shinriki when she wakes up and fall deeply in love with me. He he he he he he, since she is so enamored with me, I shall have the troops of Ōashiwake slaughter this castle for a time, conquer King Garden, Sashimori, and Migimori, punish the Crown Prince and other high-ranking officials with severe penalties, and I, Kiuba, shall take their place to become the Jōgyō and Kshatriya of the Tolman Kingdom. I shall make this peerless princess my wife, usurp tens of millions in assets, become the master of a magnificent castle, challenge Daikokunushi himself, and become the hegemon of over seven thousand countries. Ah, how amusing, how amusing! The season of good fortune has arrived. My wisdom is peerless, and my courage is like that of a god or a demon—that is me! He he he he he he. Hey, what is that terrible noise? Let me go outside and see what is happening.'

He tried to remove the door, but since the secret lock was engaged, it could not be opened unless it were Chigusahime. Even the formidable Kiuba was perplexed. Outside, there had been a skirmish between the castle and the enemy for a while, but the cautious Ōashiwake, having heard that Kiuba had infiltrated the castle, stopped the battle and was anxious to scout Kiuba's situation. Therefore, the battle ceased in less than half an hour. Ōashiwake had surrounded Tolman Castle with three thousand troops, layer upon layer. King Garden, too, gazed at the enemy's great force from afar, but lacking the courage to sally forth, he polished his spear and remained on guard, intending to hold out until reinforcements arrived. At this moment, an attendant to Prince Chiwin came to the King's side, bowed respectfully, and said:

Attendant: 'By the order of the Prince to present this to Your Highness, I have been keeping this letter; please accept it.'

King: 'What, you say the Prince gave this letter to me? In the midst of my great panic and confusion, I had forgotten about the Prince.'

As he said this, he hurriedly broke the seal and gazed at it, and the following message was written in vivid, bold ink:

One: The person named Kiuba who visited my father this evening is conspiring with General Ōashiwake to occupy this castle and overthrow our royal house; therefore, not a moment of delay can be permitted at this juncture. I, your humble son, thought that consulting with my father and Sashimori would be far too difficult, so I have conspired with my younger sister Chinrei and Harisu, the daughter of Migimori, to mobilize the entire nation. I am sending my retainers to various places, and I, too, shall leave the castle to prepare to attack Ōashiwake's army from the rear. Therefore, until I become the commander-in-chief, organize the troops, and return to the castle, you must never open hostilities with the enemy. I humbly implore you to delay for even a moment and wait for our army to arrive.
