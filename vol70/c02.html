Chapter 2: 折衝 Battle [1769]

折衝戦〔1769〕

§ 1

In the Kuwaigishitsu of Toruman Castle, the leadership including the King Garden, Ōhi Chigusa, Samorinokami Fūran, and Umorinokami Sumanjī are gathered, heads together, holding a whispered Jiueukuwaigi.

Garden: 'Well, Samori and Umori, because a grave Jiudaimondai has suddenly Botsupatsu, I have summoned you both via Kifushi, and I wish to borrow your Chie. For the Toruman nation, this is a matter of life and death for the Kokuka...'

Samori and Umori bow their heads at once, and waiting with reverence for the King's second statement. The King, while blinking his eyes, says,

Garden: 'It is nothing other than this: around the middle of last night, someone was knocking at the Monko incessantly. Following the Kifushi from the Monban Tama and Taru, I left my Shinjiyo and went to the Ōsetsuma, where I saw a wicked monk named Kyūbā of the Sukobutsuen sect, who claims to be an envoy of General Ōdaruwake and is currently propagating his heretical teachings within our country. Without even fearing my presence, he became overbearing and said... Your country has traditionally revered the Ural religion and used it to support the government; it must be dangerous in the extreme to try to win the hearts of the people with the Ural religion, whose lifeblood has already ceased. This time, General Ōdaruwake, acting under the great command of Ōkuninushi, has the imperial order to convert the seven thousand and more countries of India to the Sukobutsuen sect. Since it is his benevolent wish to save all people under heaven from the suffering of the mire and charcoal and to revive them in the Pure Land of peace, you must accept this humbly. If you should happen to disobey, the army of benevolence and righteousness will instantly reveal the claws and fangs of wolves, slaughter Toruman Castle, and awaken the King and the general populace. Depending on the King's reply, the safety or peril of the nation will be decided; give your answer immediately... Such was his strong demand, and his violent behavior was beyond words. I felt as if I would faint from anger, but I thought, "Wait, hold on for a moment," and somehow or other I muddied the words of the moment, telling Kyūbā that I would consult closely with you senior retainers and decide whether to accept or refuse, and ordered him to wait for three days. However, the momentum of that wicked monk was quite fierce; he shook his head left and right and gave a stern ultimatum, saying... "I will hear the answer right now"... There is no reason why the sovereign of a country, even for a moment, should be pressured by a single wicked monk. Nevertheless, if I were to rebuke him with a single word, he would not hesitate to lead the army of Ōdaruwake to surround this castle ten or twenty layers deep. Regrettably, I spent a thousand words and ten thousand phrases to request a one-day delay, and I have made it so that I must give an answer. Samorinokami-dono, Umorinokami-dono, what should be done?'

Samori: 'Wondering what had happened, I came to the castle without delay, and upon hearing this, it is a situation that cannot be taken lightly. To the King, who is the sovereign of the world, the insolent words of an unknown wicked monk are unforgivable. From here on, negotiation and reply are both useless. I wish to quickly gather the troops of the country, annihilate the army of Ōdaruwake, and remove this disaster from the nation.'

King: 'Indeed, your words meet my intentions. Now, make preparations for recruitment as quickly as possible. Issue strict orders to the soldiers within the castle and have them begin preparations for defense.'

Samori: 'Yes, I humbly accept your imperial order. Umorinokami-dono, you must send messengers throughout the country as quickly as possible and gather the army to save the nation from this crisis.'

Umori: 'I cannot believe these are the words of Samorinokami-dono. To wage such a reckless war and destroy the Toruman nation, which should be eternal as heaven and earth, is a poor strategy, Samorinokami-dono. Now that it has come to this, if we follow the opinion of Kyūbā for a while and the Royal Family and the general public submit to the sect he preaches, the world will be peaceful, the people will be spared from the suffering of the mire and charcoal, and you will be revered as a benevolent ruler. If, by any chance, we turn such a massive army, like clouds and mist, toward us and meet with the sorrow of total defeat, it will be a catastrophic failure that can never be undone for ten thousand generations. Your Highness, and Samorinokami-dono, please think about this carefully. For the sake of the Royal Family and for the sake of the nation, I, Umori, stake my life to offer this remonstrance.'

King: 'At this stage, Umori, what is the purpose of your Hikefumiren Ihideu, consuming the Kokudo and maintaining the Guntai in Heiso? Is it not for the purpose of preparation to act with Kiyokoku Itsuchiteki Kuwatsudō and defend against Guwaiteki in the face of such Kokunan? With such a Hikefumiren soul, in this Gendai of Iushiyōretsupai, especially when the Kokuō of Shichisenyokoku are all perfecting their Gunbi and, Koshitantan, are without Yonen in their Kokubau, to abandon the Sosendenrai Oshihe of the Kami of Ural is to damage and break the Igen of the Kami; it is the height of Goburei and will only hasten the Metsubō of the Kokuka. This Tolman country has the thick Shiyugo of the Kami of Ural; why should we suffer and bow our heads to such a demonic teaching? Steady your spirit and firm up your resolve.'

Umori: 'Though it is as you command, my Lord, at this time you must please consider this with great calmness. For it is a matter from which there is no return.'

King: 'Then what do you propose we do? Speak your mind without reservation.'

Umori: 'Yes, I speak with fear and trembling. The enemy is a massive army that exceeds the eye, and at this time when they are pressing near the castle, what use would it be to summon the Guntai in a delayed rush? The Shiyugo-hei within the castle are a mere five hundred, while the enemy's total force is three thousand cavalry; even if we gathered all the personnel in the country, would it not be at most two thousand five hundred? To face three thousand elite troops with five hundred soldiers is like a mantis waving its axe against a dragon carriage. It will take at least three days to carry out a total mobilization of the country and finally complete the preparations for battle. By then, the war will have already ended, and it would be a useless act, like offering flowers after the festival. If we ignore such obvious logic and fight, the Metsubō of the Kokuka will be more precarious than a candle in the wind. If you would please adopt the suggestion of Umori at this time, I believe it would be truly fortunate for the Kokuka.'

Chigusahime: 'Having heard the opinions of the King, as well as those of Samori-dono and Umori-dono, all are indeed most reasonable; however, I believe Umori's theory is the method most suited to the occasion. Your Highness, I pray you, please adopt the theory of Umori.'

King: 'Do not talk nonsense! Are you, too, trying to erase his theory? I have long thought that your usual behavior was not above board. The cause of the Metsubō of a Kokuka lies in women. The reason King Zhou of Shang lost his country was also due to the tyranny of women. This is not something for a girl to know; get out!'

With a roar of anger like a hundred thunders striking at once, Chigusahime shrank back, her face turned deathly pale, and she collapsed on the spot, trembling. The King, paying no heed to this sight, continued his words:

King: 'Hey, Samori, now that it has come to this, you and I shall combine our strength and annihilate the Guwaiteki. I shall stand at the head of the battle line from here on and command the three armies. Come, Samori, begin the preparations.'

Samori: 'Though I am aged, with these arms tempered by martial arts, even if the army of Ōashiwake were to press upon us with a million cavalry, what of it? Bearing the divine power of the Banko Shin-ō upon my head, I shall harass the army of Ōashiwake, which is protected by the demons of Yamata no Orochi, with the Hiju of Senpen-banka, and by unleashing surprise troops, I shall annihilate them. Come, Umori-dono, prepare yourself.'

Umori: 'This I cannot understand. Your words, both of you, are like pouring oil on firewood and embracing it to throw into the fire; it is a reckless resistance of extreme danger. How could it possibly succeed? I beg you to stop and think.'

King: 'Samori, do not deal with a traitor like Umori. I have known for over a year, through the reports of various people, that Chigusahime has been stealing glances at me and has been... with Umori. To keep such a traitor in the castle is like raising the cub of a tiger. Bind her up as quickly as possible!'

Chigusa: 'King, your heartless words—never, never did I dream that I would receive such suspicion. They say good medicine is bitter to the mouth and loyal advice is contrary to the ear. Umori-dono is dedicating his life for the sake of the royal house and the Kokuka. There is no one other than Umori who clearly understands the transition of the times and discerns the foundation of politics. Today's world has changed significantly. To blindly adhere to old conventions and try to build a Kokuka is the height of foolishness. I do not know whose slander this is, but to report to you that I have committed an act of infidelity is beyond words; it is the act of an unfaithful and treacherous villain. Please do not lend your ears to the words of such a rebellious subject, and please consider my suggestion calmly. Now that it has come to this, panic and confusion will have no effect. You must be calm, even more than calm, and devise a great plan for the hundred-year future of the Kokuka.'

Ō: 'Thou art the very Chiyōhonnin of this Baukoku, bewitched by the spirit of the Kinmaukiubi. The Ringen is like sweat; once it has emerged, it shall not return. I have no ears to listen to such Yomahigoto of a Baukoku as thine.'

So saying, he stood up, took his Yumiya, and attempted to depart with Samori. Just then, the Eusō Kiubā of the Sukobutsuen sect appeared leisurely, guarded by several dozen armed Heishi, and shouted:

Kiubā: 'Kiubā, the Daitōriyō of the Sukobutsuen sect, acting under the Mei of Ōkuronushi, has arrived leading the army of Ōdaruwake. Give your Hentafu immediately!'

Chigusahime and Umorinokami ran out to the Genkuwan in Yaniha, and Chigusa said:

Chigusa: 'Oh, oh, Kiuseishiyusama of such high Goshintoku, you have come to visit us. How could we ever defy the Oboshimeshi of Ōkuronushi, whose Jinjimugen is boundless? We shall abandon the Ural religion of our Sosen, follow your words, and the entire nation shall join the Sukobutsuen sect. Please, it is an unsettling act to advance with an army; pray, withdraw your troops. I shall stake my own life and guarantee this.'

Kiubā: 'Ahahahaha, has the stubborn King Gāden finally given up? How about you, Samori? You have no objections, do you? I wish for both of you to hand over a formal letter of surrender. Otherwise, I shall not be able to offer an excuse to Lord Ōkuronushi and Ōdaruwake. Come, hand over the letter of surrender quickly.'

While they were talking, King Gāden and Samori ran to the barracks, conveyed strict orders to many generals and soldiers, and began preparations to repel the enemy. Kiubā, overconfident and off his guard, believing that the King and Samori were trembling in the inner palace and hiding in their sleeping quarters like fleas or lice, sat down heavily in the reception room to receive the letter of surrender, sipping tea in a chair, and said:

Kiubā: 'Ahahahaha, now, now, Chigusahime-dono, Umori-dono, the power of Lord Ōkuronushi is truly something, is it not? Thanks to your arrangement, this Toruman Castle is saved, and the senile King Gāden and Samori’s Fūran can keep their heads on their shoulders; I congratulate you on this.'

Chigusahime, realizing that it would be a disaster if it were discovered that the King and Samori were advocates of war, prayed deeply in her heart that the two would somehow yield, and with an indifferent face, said:

Chigusa: 'Kiubā-sama, you are the savior of the Toruman nation; we shall never forget this grace even after billions of years. Now, Umori-dono, please convey this to the King, Samori, and the other senior retainers. I shall entertain Kiubā-sama myself...'

Umorinokami, guessing the heart of Chigusahime, thought to soothe the hearts of the King and Samori in the meantime, and at the very least, to deceive Kiubā in this situation, make it appear as if they had submitted, and then slowly devise a strategy. However, when he entered the King’s living room, it was empty like a cicada shell. Realizing the gravity of the situation, he rushed to the military office, only to find that the soldiers within the castle had already completed their arming, and the King, wearing armor and leaning on a spear, was on the verge of launching a total attack.

Umorinokami: 'Wait, wait, Highness, the matter has been settled smoothly. Please, wait a moment.'

Ō: 'Has Kiubā surrendered? How was it settled?'

Umorinokami: 'Yes, Kiubā is waiting solemnly with several dozen elite soldiers. Nevertheless, the great army of Ōdaruwake is now poised to slaughter this castle depending on your reply. I thought it the ultimate military tactic to deceive the enemy for a moment, catch them off guard, and in the meantime mobilize the entire nation to launch a pincer attack from inside and outside the castle; thus, I feigned surrender to the Sukobutsuen sect. Please, I beg you, lay down your arms and meet with Kiubā together with Samori-dono.'

The King flared up in anger and said:

Ō: 'You treacherous villain Umorinokami, to take such a national-humiliating action without my permission! You are no better than a beast, and I can no longer tolerate you. Prepare yourself!'

と呼はつてゐる。千草姫、右守司は矢庭に玄関に走り出で、

千草『これはこれは、御神徳高き救世主様、よくこそお越し下さいました。仁慈無限の大黒主の思召、何条以て反きませう。祖先以来のウラル教を、放擲し、貴僧のお言葉に従ひ、スコブツエン宗に国内挙つてなりませう。どうか軍隊を以て向はせらるるは穏かならぬ御仕打、兵を引上げ下さいませ。妾が身命を賭して、御請合申上げます』

キユ『アハヽヽヽヽヽ、流石頑強なガーデン王も往生致したか、左守はどうだ。異存は無からうか、両人の確なる降服状を渡して貰ひたい。さもなくば大黒主様大足別に対しても、愚僧の言訳が立ち申さぬ。サ、早く屈服状を御渡し召され』

斯く話してゐる内に、ガーデン王、左守は兵営に走り行き、数多の将士に厳命を伝へ、敵を撃退すべく準備に取かかつてゐた。キユーバーは王を始め左守は奥殿に戦慄し、蚤の如く虱の如く寝所に忍んでゐるものとのみ慢心して気を許し、降服状を受取らむと応接の間にどつかと尻を卸し、椅子にかかり茶を啜りつつ、

『アハヽヽヽ、これこれ千草姫殿、右守殿、大黒主の御威勢は大したもので御座らうがな。其方の計らひ一つによつて、此トルマン城も無事に助かり、耄碌爺のガーデン王も、左守のフーランも先づ之で首がつなげると云ふもの、まづまづお目出たう存ずる』

千草姫は王や左守の主戦論者たる事を悟られては一大事、何とかして二人の我が折れる様と、心中深く祈りつつ、素知らぬ顔にて、

『キユーバー様、貴方はトルマン国に対し、救世の恩人、億万年の後迄も此御恩は決して忘れませぬ。これこれ右守殿、王様始め左守其他の重臣に、此由を御伝へ下さい。キユーバー様は妾が御接待申して居るから……』

右守は千草姫の心を推知し、此間に王及左守の心を和らげ、後は兎も角此場合キユーバーを欺いて、帰順した如くに見せかけ、徐に策をめぐらさむと、王の居間に入つて見れば藻脱けの殻、コラ大変と、軍務署へかけつけて見れば、既に城内の兵士は武装を整へ、王も亦甲冑をよろひ、槍を杖について、今や、一斉に総攻撃に出でむとする間際であつた。

右守『若し若し殿下、話は甘く纒まりました。どうか暫く御待ち下さいませ』

王『キユーバーが降服致したのか、如何纒まつたのだ』

右守『ハイ、キユーバーは数十の精兵を引連れ、厳然と控へて居りまする。それにも拘らず、大足別の大軍は今や返答次第によつて、本城を屠らむとして居りまする。一時敵を欺いて、油断させ、其間に国内の総動員を行ひ、城の内外より挟撃にするのが、軍術の奥の手と存じ、詐つてスコブツエン宗に降伏致しておきました。何卒々々武装を解き、左守殿と共にキユーバーにお会ひ下さいませ』

王はクワツと怒り、

『不忠不義の曲者右守奴、吾許しも無く勝手に左様な国辱的行動をなすとは、鬼畜に等しき其方、最早勘忍ならぬ、覚悟せよ』

§ 4

Before the words were even out, he slid his spear and thrust it into the side of Umori, aiming to pierce even the bone; how could Umori endure such a blow, and he fell down flat on the spot, clutching at the empty air, and breathed his last.

Ō: 'Ahahahaha, it is a good omen to have offered the blood of a traitor as the first sacrifice of our departure. Come, from here on, we shall offer Chigusahime and Kyūbā as blood sacrifices.'

Saying this, he left Samori to supervise the troops, and leading several dozen picked soldiers himself, he marched fiercely toward the reception room.

Chigusahime, feeling somehow that the situation was ominous, sent a sidelong glance to Kyūbā and led him into a private room, where she locked the door from the inside; then, lowering her voice, she conveyed the hard-line intentions of Ō and Samori, and warned Kyūbā of the danger to his person. Chigusahime had by no means formed an ugly relationship with Umorinokami. She had merely trusted him deeply, as he was a man who understood the times, out of a single-minded devotion to the nation. Chigusahime, being wise, thought that even if Kyūbā were destroyed for a time, Ōtarawake was waiting in the wings, making the final victory uncertain. It would be better to win the favor of Kyūbā and protect the peace of the nation... and so, taking advantage of the fact that she was the peerless beauty of the land, she had made him a man within her own medicine chest. Even the violent and lawless Kyūbā had his bones softened upon meeting a single glance from Chigusahime, and whether it was his fortune or misfortune to have fallen completely into the princess's trap, it shall be decided by the judgment of God.

原文（近代文語）

と言ふより早く、槍をしごいて、右守の脇腹に骨も徹れとつつ込めば、何条以て堪るべき、右守は其場にドツと倒れ伏し、虚空を掴んで息絶えて了つた。

王『アハヽヽヽヽ、首途の血祭に国賊を誅したのは幸先よし。サ、之より千草姫、キユーバーの両人を血祭にせむ』

と云ひ乍ら、左守に軍隊を監督させおき、自ら数十名の精兵を従へ、応接間を指して勢猛く出でて行く。

千草姫は何となく、形勢不穏の気がしたので、キユーバーに対し、秋波を送り乍ら、密室に伴ひ、ドアの錠を中から卸し、声を忍ばせ乍ら、王始め左守の強硬なる意思を伝へ、キユーバーの身の危険なる事を告げた。千草姫は決して右守司と醜関係を結んでゐなかつた。只国家を思ふ一念より、時代を解する彼を厚く信じてゐたのみである。智慧深き千草姫は仮令一時キユーバーを亡ぼす共、後には大足別控へ居れば、最後の勝利は覚束なし。若かずキユーバーの歓心を買ひおき、国家の安泰を守らむには……と、自分が国内切つての絶世の美人たるを幸ひ、彼を薬籠中の者として了つたのである。暴悪無道のキユーバーも千草姫の一瞥に会ふて骨迄和らぎ、まんまと姫の術中に陥つたのは幸か不幸か、神の審判を以て処決さるるであらう。
