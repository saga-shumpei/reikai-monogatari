Chapter 3: 恋 Battle 連笑 [1770]

恋戦連笑〔1770〕

§ 1

Chigusahime accompanied Kyūbā into an itsushitsu, where she exerted every effort to entice him, aiming to melt his shintan and force him to spill all his himitsu. Seeing the bibau of Chigusahime, Kyūbā narrowed his eyes like acorns—wondering if she were an Amatsuotome, an angel, a Nelson-paté, Yōkihi, Ono no Komachi, Terutehime, or a goddess of heiwa—and lowered his eyebrows and mejiri past shichijinijifugofun, performing the geitau of letting a sticky substance drip from his kōkaku while wiping it away with a handkerchief, becoming like a yudetako as he mito her beauty. By this point, every itsupinitsushō of Chigusahime held the power to sway even the inochi of Kyūbā. Kyūbā completely forgot his own mokuteki and the ikisatsu with Ōdaruwake, and within the uchiukan, there was no longer any Kami, Buddha, Daikokunushi, heaven, or earth; all that caught his eyes was the enchanting Chigusahime, and all that reached his ears was the sound of her alluring, jewel-like voice.

Chigusahime: 'Excuse me, Savior-sama, what a magnificent person you are. No matter how much power it is said that Lord Banko Shin-ō possesses, or how great Lord Ōkunihiko is said to be, they are already Kami of the past. No matter how much one joins their hands in prayer, they will not say a word. And to think, what happiness it is for me to meet the Savior of heaven in person and hear the voice of heaven directly. When I gaze upon your figure spiritually, you shine brilliantly all over your body like a reirō jewel or a diamond. I feel as if my eyes are being dazzled. And your jewel-like voice—even hearing a single word of it becomes my flesh and strength. What a magnificent Kami has appeared. Please, Kyūbā-sama, do not let anyone other than me hear this exquisite jewel-like voice. It would be a problem if you let the eyes of the people of the world see this exquisite figure. Ah, if only it were possible, I would like to make all the people of the three thousand worlds blind. And I would like to turn the ears of all the people in the world into wood-ear mushrooms. Tell me, my beloved Kyūbā-sama.'

She poured out every possible form of flattery, attempting to melt the already melted fool even further. He became as limp as a boneless octopus sprinkled with buckwheat flour, drooling from his mouth and letting mucus drip from his nose; without even touching the jewel-like skin of Chigusahime, Kyūbā had his vital essence drained from his five orifices, becoming like a grasshopper caught in the frost of an autumn evening.

Kyūbā: 'Now, Chigusahime, what are you doing to me? I am the right arm of Daikokunushi, the founder of the Sukobutsuen sect, and a Mahatma, a holy hero who can see through the three thousand worlds at a single glance. To think you would turn my very bones and sinews into mush—is this not truly a formidable skill?'

Chigusa: 'Ho ho ho, oh my, Kyūbā-sama, what you say. You are not such a trivial spirit as to be merely the right arm of Daikokunushi or a savior. To think that you, with such a formidable skill—nay, such a magnificent, handsome man, a sinful lady-killer—could turn me, this Chigusahime who descended to the Toruman country as an incarnation of Tanabatatsume, steering a jewel-boat with a golden oar, into something like a mere konnyaku; I have lost all sense of day or night, west or east. Perhaps it is the weakness of being in love, but my life rises and falls depending on the way you breathe. If you think I am dear to you, please let me live long. I dislike a murderer who carries no blade. By the hōriki of the Sukobutsuen sect, I wish to live with you for a thousand or ten thousand years in eternal youth and immortality.'

Kiyū: 'Heh-heh-heh-heh-heh-heh, good, good. As long as you and I are happy, I do not care if the world falls into darkness or collapses. I thought you were a Tenkamusau Bijin, and it turns out I was right; were you an Amakuda of Tanabatahime? Indeed, you have a style of such high Kihin that it is beyond compare. Why should we not monopolize the Kaufuku of the Tenka between the two of us? Now that it has come to this, there is no need for Ōkuronushi or any such nonsense. My Ketsushin will not waver, so please be at Anshin. Chigusahime, you surely cannot hate me that much, heh-heh-heh-heh-heh-heh.'

Chigusa: 'Otsuho-ho-ho-ho-ho-ho.'

Laughing loudly, she continued:

'With this husband, there is this wife; the sun and the moon, the Tendau and the Tsuchi. Kiyūbā-sama and Chigusahime. Katsuobushi for a cat. I wonder if such a perfectly matched Fūfu exists anywhere in the Sanzensekai.'

Kiyū: 'Atsuha-ho-ho-ho-ho-ho, this Koitsu is Omoshiro. It is said that even a Ningen encounters Kaūn once in an Itsushiyō. This Kiyūbā has also attained his first Anshin-ritsumei through the Goriyaku of the Ōkami. You are a great savior to me. You are Miroku Nyorai, Messiah, Christ, and the Mizu no Mitama. Where else could there be a savior but you? You and I, the two pillars, shall dance high into the heavens, ride upon the Ama no Ukihashi, and save the people of the nations drifting in the great ocean, leading them to the Anyō Jōdo, shall we not? How about it, Hime? You surely have no objections.'

Chigusa: 'I do not like it. Did I not say so just before? I do not want you to show your form to anyone but me. I do not want you to let anyone but me hear your precious voice. You are a person of many fancies, so you likely intend to let the Sōsei of the Sanzensekai gaze upon this noble form, and let them hear that precious voice—which is so full of flavor that it makes one tremble, and which surpasses even the music of the Tenjin—but is it not our promise that I alone shall hear that voice?'

Kiyū: 'Now, Chigusahime, you are quite a formidable one. You have such a gentle face, yet are you not being too greedy? This Kiyūbā has come down to earth to save the ten thousand people of the Tenka. That would be going against my heavenly mission, you know.'

Chigusahime deliberately turned her back with a flounce and said:

'Hen, please do as you please. I am going to die now, (crying voice) Oon-oon-oon-oon-oon.'

Kiyū: 'Now, now, Chigusahime-dono, it is troublesome if you get that angry. I did not say anything bad about you; come now, listen calmly to what I have to say. It is not as if I intend to pour my love upon all the people of the world, you see.'

Chigusa: 'Eh, I do not know. An Otafuku like me will surely not be to your liking. Uoon-uoon-uoon.'

Kiyū: 'Atsuha-ho-ho-ho-ho-ho, you are just like an imomushi. You shake your head from right to left with such a flounce. Now, Hime-sama, you must not think so poorly of me. Come, listen calmly to the bottom of my heart.'

Chigusahime turned her body again with a flounce, collapsed flat onto the ground, and while covering her face with her left and right sleeves, said:

'Yes, I am an imomushi. An imomushi should just become a burden to Imosuke. There is such a thing as knowing one's place, you know, aan-aan-aan-aan.'

Kiyū: 'My, how hysterical you are. Did it offend you that much that I called you an imomushi?'

Chigusa: 'Yes, I must be an imomushi. From your eyes, I must look like a sechinmushi. Eh, how mortifying, aan-aan-aan-aan, I do not know, I do not know anymore. It would be better if someone like me had never existed in this world. Rather than being in love with a fickle person like you and being tormented to death, it would be better to just bite my tongue and die, uoon, uoon.'

Kyū: 'Hey, Hime-sama, listen carefully. If you think this Kyūbā is cute, then please do not worry yourself so much. It seems I am the one who is about to be captivated. Oh, I am starting to want to cry. I wanted to enjoy a good cry of love to my heart's content, but since you took the initiative, Hime, I have suffered a great loss. Chigusa: 'Kyūbā-sama, you are truly a cruel person. You intend to make me cry and cry until I die of impatience, do you not? Come, please kill me. Since I have entrusted myself to you from the top of my head to the tips of my toes, I no longer have the courage to even snap a finger, no matter what happens.'

Kyū: 'Even I, for your sake—though it is not like grilled eel—feel as if my spine has been split open. Can you not understand even a little of the sincerity I have shown you?'

Chigusa: 'Oh, what a pity, what a pity. It is you who do not understand my heart.'

Saying this, she extended her nails, which she had grown just a little, and rudely scratched him two or three times from his forehead down to his chest, aiming at Kyūbā's face.

Kyū: 'Ouch, ouch, ouch, ouch, ouch! Hey, Hime, do not do such unreasonable things. You are making earthworm-like welts all over my face, are you not? If I am treated like this, it will be bad for my reputation, and I will not be able to go out.'

Chigusa: 'That is exactly right. I have marked your precious, precious, cute face with the sign of a lovers' quarrel so that you cannot show it to other women. Can you still not understand the bottom of my heart even with this?'

Kyū: 'Ahaha, ouch, ouch! When I laugh, the muscles in my face pull, ahaha, ouch, ouch, ouch! You are a woman who does terrible things.'

Chigusa: 'That is to be expected. It is mutual. I have dedicated my life to you, so you will surely give your life to me as well. What does it matter if I peel off a little skin? Shall I take a little finger?'

Kyū: 'Well, for your sake, I would not hesitate to give a little finger, but is there any need to wound the perfect body given by the Lord God? Rather than that, please accept my soul. Because the soul is what matters most.'

Chigusa: 'You said you would give me your soul, but how will you do it?'

Kyū: 'My soul is my sincerity. It is the unity of word, heart, and action.'

Chigusa: 'Then, in order to show your sincerity, you will listen to everything I say, will you not?'

Kyū: 'Yes, I will listen. For your sake, I will give my life at any time.'

Chigusa, with a happy face and a sly grin, said:

Chigusa: 'Kyūbā-sama, I have understood your sincerity. I am so happy. The darkness has cleared because of this.'

With eyes brimming with dew and an indescribable charm, she gazed intently at Kyūbā. Kyūbā became even more dreamlike at the sight of Chigusa-hime's smiling face, and with drool flowing like a waterfall, he let his guard down completely, his face becoming like a poorly made Kiyomizu-yaki Hotei doll.

Chigusa: 'Now, Kyūbā-sama, you said just now that you would give me your life at any time, did you not?'

Kyū: 'Yes, I certainly said it. I am a man; if I say I will do it, I will do it. If it is for you, I will listen to anything. Even if I were to defy the command of Ōkuninushi-no-Kami, I would not defy your command.'

Chigusa: 'Ah, I am relieved to hear that. Now, let me receive your life immediately.'

So saying, she drew a dagger from her bosom and took a stance. Even the infatuated Kyūbā, upon seeing the short sword, turned pale at the thought that his life was truly about to be taken, and let out a trembling voice.

Kiyu: 'Wait, wait, you are too hasty; what is the point of giving your life to me? If I were to die, you would not be able to see my beautiful face, nor would you be able to hear my voice of jade, would you? It is fine to be infatuated with love, but you must not go that far; come now, why do you not calm yourself a little?'

Chigusa: 'The true flavor of love lies in the act of casting away one's life. True love is born from tears, yet while you speak of giving your life, why do you not put it into practice? You spoke of the unity of word, heart, and deed, but do you take me for a frail woman and are you merely mocking me? Oh, how vexing, how vexing; what a pity, what a pity.'

She throws the short sword to the ground and prostrates herself, weeping.

Kyuba finally feels relieved, and while stroking his chest, he says:

Kyuba: 'Hahahaha, how amusing, how amusing; unless love reaches this point, one cannot understand its sacred flavor. How adorable you are.'

Chigusa: 'Are you so amused by deceiving me? Well, I suppose that is to be expected. You are a man of such fickle heart, claiming you intend to save all the women of the three thousand worlds. The unity of word, heart, and deed is put to shame by you.'

Kiyu: 'Do not the people of today say things with their mouths that they do not think in their hearts? I, Kyuba, am the savior of the three thousand worlds. I never say things that are not in my heart. The people of today not only fail to align their mouths, hearts, and deeds, but their hearts and mouths do not even agree. I spoke what I thought in my heart to you, so it is the unity of word and heart; hahahahaha.'

Chigusa: 'I will not be deceived by such excuses as the unity of word and heart. Please show me the execution of the third, the deed.'

Kiyu: 'What a difficult request that is. You do not need to be so difficult, do you? Please buy my heart. It is precisely because I think of living for a thousand or ten thousand years to make you happy that I cherish my life. This, too, is for your sake.'

Chigusa smiles intentionally and says:

Chigusa: 'Ah, I understand now. Please, treat me with love eternally. You must not turn your heart toward others, you hear?'

Kiyu: 'Hahahaha, well, with this, peace is restored for now. With white hands like ivory carvings and agate nails, looking from the front or the side, you are truly none other than Tanabatatsume-hime. Hey, Princess, would you not shake my hand?'

Chigusa: 'Yes, that is an easy thing to do.'

She gently takes his jet-black hand, which looks like a hairy rock.

Kiyu: 'Hey, Princess, grip it a little more firmly. It feels so unreliable. With such a gentle grip, I cannot understand the degree of your love at all.'

Chigusa: 'Very well, if you say such things, I shall grip it until your hand shatters.'

Kiyu: 'Good, grip it until my breath stops; hahahahaha.'

He is again dripping thick, viscous threads from his mouth. Chigusa-hime uses a judo technique to grip his pulse point with all her might. Kyuba lets out a groan, turns pale, and collapses flat on the spot.

Chigusa-hime smiles and says:

Chigusa-hime: 'Hohoho, this devilish fellow, if I leave him like this, I shall be at ease for a while. It seems he has finally fainted; hoh-hohohoho.'

It appears that a fierce battle has begun inside and outside the castle, and the sounds of horses neighing, the clamor of the ranks, and the noise of projectile weapons grow louder with every passing moment.
