Chapter 18: 鳳恋 [1785]

鳳恋〔1785〕

§ 1

Chigusahime, acting with haughty airs as the Hinodewake, forced the Setsuteiri to kneel at her feet and spoke:

Chigusahime: 'The Savior of the Sanzensekai, the Futobashira of the Great Miroku of Sokotsuihane, the Tennin of the Daiichireigoku, and the Ikimiya of the Hinodewake, has matters to declare unto you, Garden. Steady your Shiyōne and listen well.'

Ō: 'Yes, whatever it may be, pray command me. I have sworn absolute Zetsutaifukujiyū to you.'

Chigusahime: 'Your words, Hinodewake, are Manzokumanzoku. You must now become the Hashiya of the Sanzensekai, and you bear the Daisekinin of engaging in the Shingefu of Sekaitōitsu. Regarding this, as a mere Ningen, there is nothing you can do on your own. Since the Hinodewake has descended from Ten this time, taking the physical body of Chigusahime as a dwelling, and with the miraculous Shingefu of the gods, I shall first purify the footing of the Toruman nation, eliminate the rebellious subjects, select the Suishōrei to serve the gods, and solidify the foundation for the fulfillment of divine government; therefore, even if it be but a single word or phrase, you must never disobey. You understand, do you not?'

Ō: 'Yes, I shall humbly accept your divine command.'

Chigusahime: 'That you do not utilize the divine command, but continue to allow the Junk of rural upbringing, possessed by the spirit of the Yamata-no-Orochi, to manage the national government, is a violation of the great decree of the divine realm, and the crime of rebellion is most grave. Summon your courage at once and banish that Junk.'

Ō: 'Yes, I have certainly understood your divine command, but he is a man of great popularity, the foremost in the Toruman nation. The thirty thousand citizens rely on him alone for their strength, and the three thousand soldiers respect him as their Great General; therefore, even if it is a divine command, I fear that to lay an axe upon his head would be most dangerous to the survival of the state, or rather, to the survival of the Setsuteiri house. I humbly request that you allow me to reserve this matter for a short time.'

Chigusahime: 'Ho, ho, ho, ho, ho, ho! How foolish, King Garden. To attempt to govern the Toruman divine nation with a wild warrior such as him is more dangerous than embracing a giant rock and throwing oneself into the sea. Now that the Hinodewake, peerless in divine power, has descended, what is there to hesitate or waver about? With a swift and resolute decision, banish that Junk.'

Ō: 'If that is the case, I have no choice. However, if I banish him, there will be no high-ranking priest to assist in the administration of the faith. Although there are many retainers, none are sufficient to hold the trust of the masses; they misappropriate the national treasury, each competing to build golden palaces and jade towers, living in luxury and becoming the target of the people's resentment, so I am troubled by the lack of a suitable person to replace the Junk.'

Chigusahime: 'If you summon Kyūbā, who is deeply trusted by the Daikokunushi of the capital of Haruna, and entrust him with the national government, the nation will prosper more and more, the world will be at peace, and the people will enjoy a golden age of prosperity; it is as clear as looking into a mirror.'

Ō: 'Even if I wish to summon that Kyūbā, his whereabouts are currently unknown. If you would kindly use your divine vision to inform me of his location, I shall promptly welcome him and make arrangements to fulfill your divine will.'

Chigusahime: 'Now that your resolve has been set, what more is there to say? Since the gods will draw him near, you must promptly relieve the Junk of his duties and send him back to his home province.'

Ō: 'Haha, I have certainly understood.'

Chigusahime: 'As expected, you are a wise ruler, one who fulfills the heart of the gods. Ya, Manzokumanzoku.'

At this moment, the one who appeared with great solemnity was the Superintendent of Religious Affairs, Junk.

Junk: 'I humbly inquire. Is there any hindrance?'

Chigusahime: 'There is no need for any reserve. The gods permit it; speak of whatever you wish.'

Junk: 'Pardon me, I speak to you, Your Highness. That you have cast the messengers of the San-Go-Kyō, Terukuniwake and Terukimi, who saved the Toruman nation from crisis, into the prison outside the castle without any crime, I, Junk, the Superintendent of Religious Affairs, cannot comprehend. I do not know what kind of misunderstanding this may be, but there is not a single word or deed of those two divine officials that should be doubted; it is entirely a false charge. I do not know who has slandered them, but with your wise consideration, Your Highness, if you do not release them promptly and apologize for the rudeness before the two, this land will likely not be preserved forever. I ask that you think this over carefully.'

千草『ハルナの都の大黒主が信任厚きキユーバーを召出し、彼に国政を任せなば、国家は益々栄え、天下は太平、民は鼓腹撃壤の聖代を来さむ事、鏡にかけて見る如くであるぞや』

王『其キユーバーを召出さむにも、今に於て行方分らず、何卒々々神様の御神眼にて、在所を御知らせ下さらば、速かに彼を迎へ取り、御神慮に叶ふ様取計らうで御座りませう』

千草『汝に於て其覚悟がきまつた上は、何をか云はむ。神が引寄せるに仍つて、速かにジヤンクの職を解き、国許へ追ひ返すべし』

王『ハハア、確に承知仕りました』

千草『流石は汝は名君、神の心に叶ひし者、ヤ、満足々々』

斯かる所へ恭々しく現はれ来たのは教務総監のジヤンクであつた。

千草『決して遠慮には及ばぬ。神が許す、何なりと申し上げて見よ』

ジヤ『恐れ乍ら、殿下に申し上げます。トルマン国の危急を救ひ給ひし、三五教の宣伝使、照国別、照公の神柱を、何の罪もなきに、城外の牢獄に投込み給ひしは、教務総監のジヤンク、合点が参り申しませぬ。如何なる事の間違ひかは存じませぬが、彼二柱の神司に於ては、一点の疑ふべき言行もなく、全く冤罪で御座いまする。何者が讒言致しましたか存じませぬが、賢明なる殿下の御考へを以て、速かに解放遊ばされ、二柱の前に其無礼を陳謝遊ばさねば、此国土は永遠に保たれますまい。此儀とくと御考へを願ひます』

§ 2

Ō: '...'

Chigusa: 'Foolish, Junk. From this day and this moment, you are dismissed from your post as Kyōmusōkan by the Ikimiya of Hinodewake. While the path is still clear, you would do well to prepare your travel gear and retire to your Kunimoto.'

Junk: 'This is an Okotoba of the Kamisama that I cannot comprehend. The Kyōsei of the Tolman nation is governed by Ōsama Garden, and it is the Ōhi-no-kimi who assists in that Kyōsei. Yet, how can a dismissal of the Kyōmusōkan, who represents the Kokuka, be executed by the command of Hinodewake, without using the name of the Ō or the Ōhi? Junk will absolutely not resign. Furthermore, what I cannot help but find suspicious is the matter of Taishisama Chiwin, who is in a position to govern the Kokudo of Tolman in the Shiyōrai, and Ōjiyo Chinrei-sama, who were both banished under the name of world inspection, claiming it was for training or by the command of the Kami. Is it not an outrageous state of affairs that such a grave matter was carried out arbitrarily without a single word of response to the Kyōmusōkan, Junk? Such actions will surely disrupt the discipline of the religious state and become the cause of the downfall of the royal house. I pray that you two wise ones will lend an ear to Junk's words and consider this calmly.'

Chigusa: 'Silence, Junk. There are no two words in the speech of the pillars of heaven and earth. Leave your post as quickly as possible and return to your home.'

Junk: 'Ha, ha, ha, ha, ha, this is outrageous. I have not yet received an order to return home from the Kyō-ō-denka. With all due respect, the Ōhi-no-kimi does not have the authority to appoint or dismiss this high official. Even if the Kyō-ō-denka were to strictly order my dismissal, in this time of national crisis, this Junk will not move a single step.'

Chigusa: 'Thinking that because the high officials Samori and Umori have passed away, there are no obstacles left, you dare to speak such insolence; I shall show you no more mercy. Prepare yourself.'

Junk: 'When you say you will show no mercy, what is it that you intend to do?'

Chigusa: 'The Savior of the three thousand worlds, the great pillar of Miroku of the Sokotsu-iwane, the celestial being of the First Spirit Kingdom, the Ikimiya of Hinodewake, shall immediately take your life, make your flesh food for crows, and cast your spirit into the lowest hell. Will you still not resign?'

Junk: 'Aha, ha, ha, I am already tired of hearing that long divine name, Junk. Has the Ōhi-no-kimi gone mad? If you have become insane, it is extremely dangerous; I shall build a barred room and keep you locked up until you recover from your illness.'

Chigusa: 'You unfaithful and unrighteous villain, speaking from the standpoint of the flesh, you address the Ōhi-no-kimi, but speaking from the spirit world, the Savior of the three thousand worlds, the great pillar of Miroku of the Sokotsu-iwane...'

Junk waved his hand and frowned as she began to speak, saying, 'That is quite enough. I have long been aware of the Ikimiya of Hinodewake, the celestial being of the First Spirit Kingdom. However, please listen well. An organization called the Mass Alliance has been formed, and they have informed me, with the consensus of the entire sect, that if Chigusa-hime-sama does not repent at this time, they will immediately carry out a coup d'état and reform the Kyōsei from its very foundation. This Junk has been doing his utmost to persuade and appease the leaders of the Mass Alliance to save the Ōhi-sama from danger, but the public sentiment has become so agitated that it is beyond my power, and one cannot know when a great storm or a great tidal wave will strike and overturn this palace from its very roots. In this truly critical situation of life and death, I pray that you will give it your deep consideration. I have nothing more to say. I shall now withdraw to the Kyōmusho. If that is all, I shall wait with bated breath for a favorable reply from both of you.'

Having said this, he left with heavy, angry footsteps, heading toward the Kyōmusho. Behind him, Chigusa-hime and Ōsama Garden remained silent for a while, as if a curtain had fallen.

と云ひ捨て、足音高く憤然として教務所指して出でて行く。後に千草姫、ガーデン王は少時無言の幕を下してゐた。

§ 3

Chigusa: 'Gāden-Ōdono, thou appearest to be greatly troubled in heart after hearing the words of Jank, but to allow such a devil as him to participate in the Sūki of Kyōsei is dangerous above all else. If thou considerest the Ichidaiji of the Kyōōke and the Zento of the Tenka, thou must follow the command of Kami and devise a Kufū to take his Inochi as quickly as possible.'

Ōdono: 'Yes, if he is indeed a devil who would absolutely inflict Kigai upon the Kyōōke and overthrow the Tenka, then I must use Hijiyōshiyudan and destroy him; however, as one who serves the Kami of Uraru, I cannot bring myself to commit such an act of Baugiyaku. I beseech you, as you mentioned just before, if you could use your Shintoku to cut off his Inochi Tachidokoro, that would truly be fortunate. For the Kyōō of a nation to use an assassin to destroy a high-ranking priest is an act of cruelty exceeding even that of King Zhou of Shang, and if such a thing were to reach the ears of the masses, they would surely not accept it. If you were to take the method of having the Kami summon his Inochi, there could be no better strategy than that.'

Chigusa: 'Indeed, there is reason in what you say. Now, I shall go to the living shrine of Hinode-no-Kami in the forest of Harima to worship, and after consulting with Uraruhiko-no-Mikoto, I shall have his Inochi summoned. Gāden-Ōdono, order your retainers to prepare the palanquin.'

Ōdono: 'I shall order it at once and begin the preparations.'

At the seventh hour of that day, Chigusa-hime again rode in her palanquin, escorted by many guard-monks, and visited the shrine of Harima; there, she muttered incomprehensible things within the sanctuary, performed like a madwoman, and then returned in her palanquin with a grand procession. During this procession, the passage of the masses was forbidden, and guard-monks were stationed at every interval to maintain a strict watch. Just then, a man appeared, singing a propaganda song in a loud voice, and as he attempted to cross in front of the palanquin, the guard-monks seized him without difficulty. Hearing this commotion, Chigusa-hime raised the bamboo blind of her palanquin and looked out to see a handsome man of refined features. Chigusa-hime was instantly overcome with feelings of love, and while agonizing over how she might bring this handsome man back to the castle, she made up her mind, raised the blind of the palanquin, revealed half of her body, and commanded strictly,'

Chigusa: 'Hey, you guard-monks, I have my own reasons for wanting to interrogate this culprit myself, so bring him along with me into the castle.'

The guard-monks accepted the words of the queen without a single word of objection, and it was decided that they would escort the bound culprit to the castle, following behind the palanquin.

と厳命する。番僧は王妃の言葉、一も二も無く承諾し、此犯人を縛つた儘、輿の後に従ひ城内に送り届ける事となりぬ。
