Chapter 1: 信人権 [1768]

信人権〔1768〕

§ 1

In the Ōko era, India, which had unified over seven thousand countries as the center of culture and the birthplace of the Butsuso, was established with various classes such as Jiyōgiyō, Setsuteiri, Shiyuda, and Bishiya. In particular, India was a country that could be called the root of the Brahman religion. Meanwhile, the Ural religion maintained considerable influence in a corner of the Dekatan Plateau, and there was a tendency for it to expand its religious lines toward Haruna, the capital and main castle of the Brahman religion, and to overturn the foundations of Ōkuronushi. Here, Ōkuronushi dispatched Senden generals in all directions, and in particular, he gave several thousand soldiers to General Ōdaruwake for this region, setting out with the sole objective of subjugation. Now, the Toruman country, which had the most fertile land within the Dekatan Plateau, was not a very large area, but it was inhabited by a considerable number of people. Furthermore, due to geographical relations, it followed the Ural religion. The name of the King of the Toruman country was Gāden. Gāden did not believe in the Ural religion, nor did he reject it; he held the view that it was merely to be used for funeral rites as a religion handed down from his ancestors. However, the majority of the people followed the Ural religion, a portion had entered the Brahman religion, and two or three percent had newly converted to the Sukobutsuen sect, and its momentum was like a fire burning across a plain. Ōkuronushi of the capital of Haruna sent missionaries of the Brahman religion to make the entire Toruman country a sphere of influence for the Brahmans, but as a result of various painstaking efforts, he realized that the name of Brahman would not appeal to the hearts of the people of this country. Thus, the cunning and ever-watchful Ōkuronushi ordered his favorite retainer, Kiyūbā, whom he had long kept at hand, to avoid the name of the Brahman religion and to build a religion with a different name but the same principles called the Sukobutsuen sect, and he exerted every effort to first bring the Toruman King to submission. King Gāden of Toruman had a queen named Chigusahime, the crown prince was Chiuin, and the princess was Chinrei. The official of the left guard was called Fūran, and he had an only daughter named Teira with his wife Mokuren. The official of the right guard was called Sumanjī, and his wife had already departed this world, and he had a daughter named Harisu. However, the King, as well as the officials of the left and right guards, did not convert to the Sukobutsuen sect, let alone the Brahman religion, no matter how much they were encouraged, and they were immersed only in the politics of the physical realm. Here, I would like to describe the outline of the circumstances under which the Brahman general Ōdaruwake suddenly began the attack on Toruman Castle.

More than ten ri away from Toruman Castle, in front of an old, dilapidated shrine in a small mountain village, two Shiyuda were whispering about something intently. Although it was the beginning of spring, the wind was still cold, and the buds of green grass were peeking narrowly at the sky from between the long, withered grass that served as a memento of the previous year.

Rēru: 'Seeing our rise, which has awakened us religiously, the beasts of the Brahman class seem to have been thrown into confusion and panic, and have felt no small amount of trembling and fear. They understand that they themselves are being overturned from the religious and economic positions of dominance they occupied, and most pitifully, they are drying their tear-stained faces and are now desperate in the face of the great conquest of the rising Sangō movement. Those who are about to drown will grasp even the tail of a poisonous snake for the sake of their lives; their mad behavior, just as the proverb says, is worth absolutely nothing but to provoke laughter.'

Māku: 'That is true. Even seeing that they have granted the treatment of a commissioned official to a Naniwabushi-reciting old fart, made the young ones wear monk's robes, sold off the promotion of folk customs to the old ones, and permitted the ideological guidance of the fake religious figures of the shit-making machine, it only further exposes their situation; when you think about it, is it not truly a pitiful state of affairs? Seeing this, for us Sangō believers who have been oppressed until now, it feels as if our bile has been cleared; it is extremely exhilarating, ha ha ha. However, in today's case, we cannot be careless in the slightest. It is a time of great distinction that requires even greater effort in this movement. We must not stand by with folded arms, viewing the destruction of the Brahman believers as a result of their own making. It is precisely because we recognize the inevitability of their own making, and because we realize the immense toxicity of the weasel's final fart, that we must choose the best tactics and struggle and strive to achieve victory in the religious war as soon as possible. This harvest of their own making is the result of the judgment of the greatest sin in human history, and there is not a single point to be forgiven. We must simply carry out and accelerate their destruction as soon as possible, which is, if anything, a mercy, a memorial service, and a farewell gift to them. Ha ha ha.'

§ 4

Now, the Sukobutsuen sect, a branch of the Brahmanism that Kyūbā has established under the inner command of Ōkuronushi in the capital of Haruna, is a religious order of asceticism that considers the offering of sincerity to Kami through difficult religious austerities and penance to be its foundation, and it is a sect of asceticism that is terrifying even to hear of. While it does not differ in the slightest from Brahmanism in the point of forcing hundreds and thousands of austerities upon its believers, the most extreme austerity is the training for the emasculation of women, that is, their masculinization, and it is considered of the utmost importance to make a vow of becoming a transformed male and to attempt to cast off being a woman. There are methods for this, such as the oophorectomy method and the mastectomy method; when it comes to the training of the oophorectomy method, it is a truly cruel practice that leads to ninety-nine out of one hundred people losing their lives, and as for the mastectomy method, they use white-hot fire tongs to burn off the breasts of the women. For those who have done this, the Kyōshiyu and the senior officials press a brand upon them to signify their service to the Great Brahman Kami. They call this the baptism of hot fire. The breasts thus cut off are offered to the altar, and the victim who has offered them is placed upon the sacred seat and revered as if they were Kami. Then, fragments of the breasts are mixed into the food of the sacred supper, and as soon as the entire congregation finishes eating it, a frenzied dance is performed around the victim. The scene is truly extremely gruesome, and one can see even by this that it is not the work of righteous Kami. After all, the breast is the symbol of woman, the symbol of beauty, and was even considered a part of the female reproductive organs. In the end, it is an evil religion established under the notion that what represents a woman is the breast. The doctrines of religions that arose in India generally speak of the world of the self as a world of entanglement, or they speak of the practice of escaping entanglement, and they speak of non-self, and they call it void, and they call it liberation, and they preach nirvana, and thus various religions that are devoted to what is called turning from delusion to enlightenment arise; perhaps because of the relationship between the land and the temperature, the country called India is a land wrapped in terrifyingly beautiful and yet monstrously magnificent nature, where hundreds of types of humans create hundreds of classes and use hundreds of languages, and it is a fermentation ground for superstitions, blind beliefs, foolish beliefs, and evil, wicked beliefs, such as standing or sitting on trees for three years or on stones for ten years, continuing to practice asceticism by standing on one's head in a hole for three months, living only by drinking water, or sitting in the ice and snow without food or drink to become a mummy, and while they shout noisily about keeping precepts, diligence, meditation, and patience, it is a national character that is lewd, impure, lazy, and beyond help. Therefore, as a natural result, something like the Sukobutsuen sect was able to arise.

Kyūbā, the founder of the sect, while rolling his terrifying eyes, keenly overheard the conversation between the two, Rēru and Māku, and fearing that they were those who would overthrow the state of Ōkuronushi, he began to fret with all his might, using his vajra staff as support, in order to pursue the figures of the two as they fled. However, the two of them quickly hid their figures in the mountain forest, and while scooping up water from a mountain stream to moisten their throats, Rēru said, 'Hey, Māku, we have encountered a terrible fellow, haven't we? Isn't that guy the ringleader of the Sukobutsuen sect, who used to frequent the residence of Ōkuronushi until a few years ago and was highly favored by Ōkuronushi? It was a dangerous situation where I would have been severely punished by Ōkuronushi if I had been careless. Why does such a monk swagger about so much? Why does such an extremely incomplete religion not perish?' Māku replied, 'There are hundreds of small religions in the seven thousand and more states of India, but it is certain that there is not a single one among them that is perfect. In particular, that religion of his is an extremely incomplete, unfinished sect, which is why it maintains its life force. Everything incomplete has room to develop in the future, and it has a future. Perfection signifies a dead end, and in the end, it is nothing but a synonym for destruction, ha ha ha ha.'
