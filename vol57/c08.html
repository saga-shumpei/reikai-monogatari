Chapter 8: 愚摺 [1458]

愚摺〔1458〕

§ 1

Wakkusu, together with Biruma, had successfully incited the chiyōmin, beaten Michihiko half to death, thrown him into the Anburakku River, and returned to his own mansion in high spirits.

His father, Ōrusuchin, attended by two kangofu, had regained consciousness and was repeatedly calling out the name of Wakkusu.

Stealing back quietly, Wakkusu approached his father's biyōshiyō with the attitude of a nusutoneko peering into an empty house, slinked in, turned to the two kangofu, and asked in a low voice,

Wakkusu: 'Pardon me, Kangofu-sama, day and night you suffer for this, but will the illness of Oyaji be cured, I wonder?'

One of the kangofu: 'Alas, I am sorry, but you must reconcile yourself to the end.'

Wakkusu: 'Indeed, that cannot be helped either. No matter how much one regrets it, nothing can be done about one's jiyumiyō.'

Biruma, in a low voice,

'If one counts on parent's zaisan, A yakuwanatama becomes a jiyama.'

he inadvertently blurted out. The sick man, Ōrusuchin, with sharp ears overheard these words, raised his painful body, thinking to himself... That scoundrel of a son Wakkusu, waiting eagerly for his precious parent to die... and forgetting his pain, raised his voice sharply,

Ōrusuchin: 'Oh you scoundrel, what unfilial things are you saying? Say once more what you just said.'

Wakkusu: 'Well, since his biyōki is so severe, I was worried and asking the kangofu. This fellow called Biruma who is here, not knowing my heart when I am this worried about his serious illness, blurted out such a thing.'

Ōrusuchin: 'That can hardly be. Since you constantly talk of such things from ordinary days, Biruma must have imitated you. You must take heed. The moment I close my eyes, your surroundings will suddenly become dangerous. That you have concealed the nyoi hōju jewel and plotted all sorts of conspiracies is clearly known to Michihiko-sama of the Izumo Shinji (Omoto). If these things come to light, the house of the steward will be severed, and you must prepare yourself for your life to be forfeit. Since this is the parting of a lifetime, even though you are a hateful son, I find myself wishing to protect you as the karma of a parent. For the present, hide yourself somewhere, go to a distant land, and endure hardships; that would be best.'

Wakkusu: 'Father, that is a mistake. A wizard called Michihiko had stolen it. As proof of that, the people of the town beat him to a pulp... mumbling mumbling.'

Ōrusuchin: 'What, you say the people of the town beat Michihiko to a pulp? You have done a terrible thing. You must apologize quickly, or a grave crisis may befall the mansion.'

Biruma: 'No matter what anyone says, since the whole neighborhood gathered, bound him hand and foot, and threw him into the Anburakku River, he must have died long ago. There is little fear of it becoming a calamity for Wakkusu-sama. Rather, he is a splendid savior who has subjugated the demons of the divine pavilion. And since the promise for you to become the adopted son of Oguchiwake-sama has been securely settled with Okusama, Ōrusuchin-sama, please rest easy.'

Ōrusuchin: 'To give an only son in adoption is something I can never do. If I do that, will not the bloodline of Ōrusuchin be extinguished?'

Wakkusu: 'Father, do not worry about such things and attain Buddhahood quickly. As a child, I cannot bear to watch your suffering. Once I am married to Debisuhime, three or five children will surely be born. It will be fine to make the eldest son the heir of the adoptive house, and the second son the successor to the Ōrusuchin family. As long as I, your bloodline, remain, everything is safe. If you keep grumbling, it will be fine to simply make the adoptive house into the Ōrusuchin family. (In a whisper) Ah, old as he is, making unnecessary meddling at the moment of death. I wish he would just drop dead already.'

He looked back and muttered in a low voice. Fortunately, it did not reach the ears of his father, who was suffering in his sickbed. Austine collapsed, limp and in agony, falling back onto the bed once more.

Kangofu: 'Excuse me, Wakadannasama, but if you speak so loudly, it will disturb his illness, so please, do go to the Hanare to rest.'

Wax: 'Otsuto, yoshi-yoshi, that is exactly what I have been waiting for. However, Kangofu-sama, I have my own circumstances to consider; in your estimation, do you think he will be alright for the day? I must also make preparations for the Saurei, you see.'

Kangofu: 'Do not worry yourself. I will surely see him restored to Honpuku. Even if he were to pass away, he would be fine for thirty or fifty days.'

Wax: 'Heh, in any case, I leave his Gokaihau to you.'

Saying this, he went to the room in the Hanare, and the two of them sat facing each other and began to drink Hiyazake greedily. Burma became dead drunk, and began to sing in a loud, Doragoe.

Burma: 'Old man Austine-sama / With his ribs broken, groaning / In such a pitiful state / Neither recovering nor dying / A most troublesome old man / Wax-sama must surely be / Troubled by this indeed / He must settle this somehow / And establish Wax-sama's / Purpose without delay / If things go well / I shall become the steward in one leap / Thinking of this, I wish / For the old man to die quickly / I want it, I want it, I want it / If the kettle-old man dies / Everyone will surely rejoice / Yoi-se, Yoi-se / Things do not go as one wishes / Truly, this floating world is frustrating / Yoito-se, Yoito-se'

Wax: 'How can you sing such things in front of me, no matter what? He is my own flesh and blood, so I do not feel... whether I am happy or sad about his dying. However, since all this property will become my possession once the old man dies, I feel somewhat happy, and since my only parent is dying, I feel somewhat sad; it is as if a wedding and a funeral have come at once, and I am shedding a tear of sympathy. You are quite a fool, are you not?'

Burma: 'A fool, am I? I do not know what that means, but this Burma hates telling lies or flattering. Wax-sama, your heart is exactly as I said. Stop that kind of hypocritical sympathy. It would be better to say clearly that you want the old man to die quickly. If you are a villain, why not act like a villain and be a man? Do you think a great conspiracy can be accomplished with such behavior? I thought you were a thorough villain, but you are a truly great villain. It is wicked of you to speak as if you are sad when you are not.'

He rambled on about things that made no sense.

Just then, Ekkusu and Heruman, both dead drunk, kicked the gate door recklessly.

Both: 'Hey, excuse us. The god of the demon gate has arrived. Is the master of Wax-sama's conspiracy at home? What, neither this one nor that one will answer. Haha, are you trying to ostracize me? Very well, I shall go to the mansion and expose the conspiracy... though I am one of them. I never know when I might be exposed and lose my head. Thinking of that, even the free liquor begins to taste bitter. Being tormented by the demon in my heart every single day like this is unbearable. I must ask that young fool Wax to cough up about three hundred ryō, and I cannot go on without drinking away my sorrows.'

They forced the door open, stomped into the sickroom, and said,

Ekkusu: 'Ha, Great General, so you are indeed ill. That is truly a pity. However, we two have come because there is something we wish to request. In this state, the steward will surely not survive. You cannot take your vast fortune to the underworld, so for the sake of good deeds, give us two three hundred ryō as a parting gift. Whether your precious son's neck remains attached or not depends on the wagging of our tongues. So that you may die without regrets, come, pay up three hundred, or rather, six hundred ryō. Steward-sama, if you think of it as saving your son's life, it is a small price to pay, is it not?'
