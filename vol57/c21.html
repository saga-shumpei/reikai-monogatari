Chapter 21: 言触 [1471]

言触〔1471〕

§ 1

Wakkusu was surprised and hurried home, only to find his father Ōrusuchin’s illness had grown increasingly severe, and he was barely breathing. Even the usually composed Wakkusu was startled; he rushed to his father’s bedside and, squeezing out his words through tears, asked:

Wakkusu: 'Father, how are you? Are you in much pain?'

He asked this with uncharacteristic gentleness. Ōrusuchin opened his eyes wide with a start, gave a grin, and then closed his eyes forever.

Wakkusu: 'Ah, at last, my most precious, most important father has passed away. Oh, what shall I do? Hey, Ōkusu, Biruma, is there not some way to make him come back to life once more? You nurses, what were you two doing while you were attending to him? I did not hire you to provide nursing that would let an old man die; I paid high wages to have him cured. You must have done something wicked while I was away. Get out at once!'

He began to show his true colors and say outrageous things. The nurses, dumbfounded and having no words to reply, puffed up their cheeks and began to gather their belongings, intending to flee.

Wakkusu: 'Hey, wait a moment. I shall search your luggage at the customs. You must be stuffing it with the old man’s gold coins that you pulled out from under the sofa.'

Nurse: 'Ho ho ho, it is insulting to be thought of as such cheap people. However, since this trunk is mine, try touching it if you dare.'

Wakkusu: 'Very well, I shall search everything. You great thieves!'

As he said this, he forcibly pried open the nurses' trunk. Instantly, white smoke rose with a hissing sound, and from within, Debisu-hime and Kerina-hime appeared, smiling. Wakkusu was struck with terror, lost his footing, and after crying out, 'Ba... a monster!', his throat tightened, and he sat trembling with his mouth twitching.

In the meantime, Ōkusu and Biruma removed the sofa, pried up the tatami mats, pulled out the gold and silver coins that Ōrusuchin had hidden, packed them into the nurses' trunk, and showed them to the fallen Wakkusu.

Ōkusu: 'Well, Wakkusu-sama, here is the extent of the spoils. There is not a single thing left behind. Biruma and I will gratefully accept these. You may live happily with your monster princesses. Since your back will likely not recover for three or four days, we two shall abscond from this holy land, go to the capital of Haruna, and live in glory and splendor. Farewell, ufu-fu-fu-fu-fu!'

They ran out to the front, laughing mockingly while tossing their chins. The figures of the two nurses had vanished completely, no one knew where. Wakkusu endured his frustration, gritted his teeth, and watched the two fleeing figures with resentment. Eru, a man of panic, having seen the beautiful women emerge from the trunk and Ōrusuchin pass away, quickly dashed out, appeared again at the crossroads, raised his voice, and began to spread the news.

Eru: 'Dondoko dondoko dondokodon / Oh, it is a crisis, a great crisis / Heaven becomes earth and earth becomes heaven / Dondoko dondoko dondokodon / Wakkusu-sama’s father / The chief retainer of the God’s mansion / Ōrusuchin has breathed his last / And has gone to the Pure Land / Dondoko dondoko dondokodon / Everyone, hurry and rush over / Help with all the funeral rites / And perform the final send-off / The president of the Akuyōkai / Wakkusu-sama has lost his footing / With his mouth wide open in a daze / He has collapsed without a word / Dondoko dondoko dondokodon / And furthermore, there is something strange / The two nurses have suddenly / Vanished like clouds and mist / In the midst of wondering at this mystery / Wakkusu-sama opened it with a snap / From within the trunk, with a hiss / White smoke rose up / Dondoko dondoko dondokodon / Oh my, how strange, how truly mysterious / In the mansion of the God / Debisu-hime and Kerina-hime / Appeared with smiles on their faces / Dondoko dondoko dondokodon / Thinking that this too must be / The work of the magic-user of three-five / I suddenly became afraid / And have come to report the death of the steward / The collapse of Wakkusu-sama / And the appearance of the monster women / Dondoko dondoko dondokodon / This time it is no lie / He is truly, truly dead.'

§ 2

Under the sofa, in a great heap, the Kodama of gold and silver are glaring with wide-open eyes and groaning. Now is the time to get your hands on them. All the Zaisan of this world lasts only for a Hitoichidai. If it is a treasure left behind by an Oyaji who died without passing it on to his son, it is all the same whoever picks it up. This is the Shiyoiuhin of the Tenka. You who desire money, fly out as quickly as you can, grab as much as you want, and live in Eieu and Eiguwa. Dondoko, dondoko, dondokodon. I do not intend to pick up the Kingin that is burdened with Shifunen. Nevertheless, Minasama, this is a Jisetsu when Ōgon speaks. Dondoko, dondoko, dondokodon.

It is better to use the Saurei of the Gauyokujiji as a pretext to rush out, and without any Enriyoeshiyaku, force your way in and make the treasures your own Menme. Before the Koshinu of Wax-sama returns to his original state, if you go quickly, you will be the one to gain. Those who go Hitoashisaki will surely receive more Kage. Dondoko, dondoko, dondokodon. Ah, e-eee, e-eee. How Haka is the Inochi of Ningen. The Gauyokujiji, who stretched his greed, left behind a great amount of Kingin, which were the solidified sins and impurities he had saved up by skimming off the top while serving as the Karei of the mansion of Okuninowake. How pleasant it is that he died leaving all that behind.

I, Eru-sama, have now realized that evil cannot last long. The Oyaji of Ōrusuchin always acted so haughty, calling me 'Eru, Eru' and ordering me around; now, seeing the retribution for that before my very eyes is amusing. Dondoko, dondoko, dondokodon. If a person does not perform good deeds and charity while alive, it is all for nothing. This Karei is a good example. Minasama, be firm. Dondoko, dondoko, dondokodon. Come, come, I shall guide you. Minasama, follow me. Dondoko, dondoko, dondokodon.

So saying, he ran off while beating his small drum. The crowd, blinded by greed, first sought to pick up even a single Kodama of gold or silver, and intending to join the funeral to console the spirit of the deceased, they stretched their greed on that sweltering summer day, running while dripping with sweat.
