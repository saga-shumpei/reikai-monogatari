Chapter 10: 転香 [1460]

転香〔1460〕

§ 1

At the crossroads where the cold wind blew in gusts, a man wearing tattered clothes like seaweed, with his lips stained purple, shivering and trembling as he stood, was Sharu.

Sharu leaned against the standing stone of Michiwake, shivering violently and muttering to himself.

Sharu: 'Ah, damn it, how utterly uninteresting. To be left standing here without a shred of guilt in this continuous blast of wind... as if I were a stone Jizō... Even we have blood circulating through our veins, you know. That old hag Takahime works people to the bone without rhyme or reason, making a complete fool of me. If I'm going to come and fetch the dying in such a place in this cold weather, I don't know how much more manly it would be to have just stayed a thief. The flow of water and the destiny of men—how things change when they change. I used to be a military man of the Brahmanism, publicly committing rapes, perpetrating robberies, blowing my own horn, and sounding trumpets, but now that I have fallen so low, there is nothing more to be done. My belly grows empty, my throat is parched, my clothes are torn, lice are clinging to me, and my body begins to tremble from nowhere—it is just like hell. An unrefined fellow wearing a twelve-foot loincloth is worked to the bone by an old hag with a face like Atlas, oh, how gut-wrenching and thoroughly uninteresting it is, what a bloody nuisance. On top of that, the really stinking thing is that Takahime's wretch of a woman spits out demands to clean up her excrement and urine. She really makes a complete fool of us, saying that even the Great Deity Kinsatsuyō was once dropped into a privy, so for you lot to clean out the privy is a splendid divine virtue indeed. It is truly the height of indignation. That being said, there is nowhere else to go, and I am at a loss where to place this eight-foot body of mine, so even if it is somewhat unpalatable, I have no choice but to cling to that old hag. Ah, damn it all, it's exasperating. If someone else would only come along who agrees with what I say, I would make them clean the privy, but everyone who comes picks a quarrel with Takahime and runs away, so it is just like pouring water into a basket. No matter how long it takes, not a single satisfactory believer is ever going to be made. Whether it's Uranaiism or whatever else she calls it, she serves as the founder, the executive officer, and the believers all by herself, so the old hag must be busy. The other day, when I asked her how many believers there were, she blurted out that there were forty-one. When I think about it carefully, how idiotic; the meaning of forty was meant to be always. She had the nerve to say she has been propagating the teachings for almost forty-four years by this year, but since three or four believers still haven't been made, she is really something else. Ranting and raving from morning till night about the same old eighteen complaints, a self-righteous and self-praising old hag who can neither rise nor fall. When I asked her how old she was, she spat out that she was forty-nine. From what I can see, she looks fifty-five or fifty-six no matter how you slice it, but it seems it's painful for her to be seen as an old person. I don't know what it is, but she's always spitting out stinking matters. Ah, how dreary—I'm already sick of it. I wonder if some good bloody fool won't show up and lend me a hand with my work.'

So he muttered to himself, becoming desperate and stomping his feet.

From the opposite direction came a pale, medium-height-and-build man with a chilly appearance, walking slightly head-down, wearing a broken sedate-hat and putting on foul-smelling garments. As soon as Sharu saw this man, he let out a single thunderous roar and shouted, 'Wait!'

The man was startled by this voice, stopped short with a jolt, stuck his rear out slightly, and placing both hands neatly atop his Kongōzō staff,

Man: 'What business might you have?'

Sharu: 'No particular business. There is just something I want to ask. What is your full name?'

Man: 'Yes, I was formerly a believer of Abunaiism and my name is Wani-no-kuchikyōfuyu, and now I am one who has entered the hypocritical group that humans despise above all else, leading a life of repentance. Might you please allow me to clean the urine pots, the privy pots, and the rubbish heap?'

と自暴糞になり四股を踏み乍ら一人呶鳴つて居る。そこへ向ふの方から寒さうな風姿をして稍俯向き気味に破れ笠を被り臭気紛々たる着物をつけ乍ら、やつて来た蒼白い中肉中背の男があつた。シャルは此男を見るより大喝一声『待てツ』と叫んだ。男は此声に驚いてハツと立止まり、少し尻を後へ出し、両手を金剛杖の上にキチンと載せ乍ら、

男『何用で厶いますかな』

シャル『何用でもない。一寸尋ねたい事があるのだ。貴様の姓名は何と云ふか』

男『ハイ、私は元はアブナイ教の信者で厶いまして鰐口曲冬と云ひ、今は人間の一等厭ふ一等厭と云ふ偽君子の団体へ這入つて懺悔の生活をやつてる者で厶います。どうか小便壺、雪隠壺、塵芥場の掃除をさして頂けませぬだらうかな』

Sharu: 'Ya, that is admirable. It may be said that you have fully grasped my intention. The truth of the matter is that for the past three months or so, not to mention urine and feces, filthy refuse has been piling up in the corners of my garden. How about it, would you not be willing to clean it up for me?'

Magafuyu: 'I shall humbly accept the task of cleaning. Since I will give it my utmost activity, I would kindly ask that you treat me to even a meal of barley rice.'

Sharu: 'Urine is called shishi, feces is called fun, and dust is called jinhō, so show me a shishi-funjin activity of cleaning. If you do that, perhaps even my master Takahime of Yakama will not fail to treat you to at least a cup of barley rice. At any rate, it depends entirely on your labor. They say that art assists the body, so surely you too will be prized by Takahime-sama. Come, from now on, taking as a souvenir the fact that you have made a believer out of Takahime-sama, it will also serve to assist my work—a double gain. Well, with this I too can breathe a little easier. Hey, you called Magafuyu, for a long time hence become my subordinate and take charge solely of cleaning the privy. My, what a treasured thing a life of repentance is, indeed.'

Magafuyu: 'Hatsukohime-sama once sucked out and aided the pus and blood of a leper, did she not? What is a mere cleaning of feces and urine? People all gleefully eat feces and urine, you know. The ones who eat it directly are dogs, but those who eat it indirectly are all human beings. They drop their feces and scatter it upon daikon radishes, turnips, rice, barley, and the like, and by that fertilizer vegetables grow and rice and barley ripen. So to speak, they are indirect feces-eaters and urine-drinkers. How could the mere cleaning of feces be something so filthy? If one does not attain the heart to joyfully clean filthy places, one cannot become truly good. For this is the true divine heart. If you do not practice what you desire for yourself upon others, and strive to carry out what you do not desire for yourself, it is not a life of repentance at all.'

Sharu: 'Ah, I am truly impressed. Come, please come along. Your tasks have piled up to no end, and they are quite good customers.'

Magafuyu: 'Yes, thank you very much.'

So saying, following behind Sharu and blown upon by the cold autumn wind, he was led to a thatched cottage at the foot of the rocky mountain.

Sharu rattled the slanted door, and with much ado pulled it open,

Sharu: 'Now, Magafuyu-sama, this here is the golden palace and jade tower of Great Miroku. Well, please step inside and drink a cup of cold tea. Ho there, Takahime-san, I have caught a fine bird alive. Come, with your Yamato spirit, please cook it to your liking. Perhaps this fellow will quite meet with your approval.'

Takahime: 'Now, now, Sharu, while guiding such a distinguished person, what a rude thing you are saying. Why do you not use good words and beautiful phrases? Have I not always told you that evil words and ugly phrases are forbidden?'

Sharu: 'Ah, dipping it in vinegar, dipping it in miso, you are a person who cannot be satisfied unless you utter some complaint or other. This person is a hypocrite who calls himself Magafuyu of the most despised and is living a life of repentance. Pray make him surrender with your eloquence. And he is saying to let him clean the toilet. My, what a fine person you have here, if ever there was one.'

Takahime: 'Ah, you who are called Magafuyu, that place is close to the edge; well, please draw near to the side of the hearth. It must have been exceedingly cold for you. This old woman, though she looks like this, is a gentle person who is not what she seems, so please be at ease. And as for you, you say you are living a life of repentance—did you commit some evil deed that required repentance?'

Magafuyu: 'Well, I do not think I have done anything particularly evil, but human beings create sins unknowingly, so for the sake of repentance, I am having the privilege of cleaning toilets and garbage dumps which people hate above all else, and wandering about these parts.'

Takahime: 'Well, well, this is a strange matter. However, try to think about it carefully. Is it not the duty of wives and womenfolk to clean the toilets? Men should have their own noble tasks as men. Besides, is it not a bit ridiculous for a man who carries his Kintama to be cleaning toilets in such a cowardly and lingering manner? Because you people do such things, the Jiyochiu of late have all become Zōchiyō, and have come to say, "I came to be a Gejiyo, but cleaning toilets is outside of my agreement," and even make the mistress of the house clean up what they themselves have excreted; this is all because you are to blame. What will you do if all the men of the Sekai enter the Itsutōen and start cleaning toilets? Where in the world is there such an unreasonable and Bachiata thing as having others clean the feces you have excreted, or walking around cleaning the feces of others? That is why people of the Seken say that the people of the Itsutōen are nothing but Kusoyatsuko. I thought there was some Ketai scent, and now I see that the scent of urine and feces is soaked into your clothes. Those who have registered themselves in hell are fond of places like piles of manure and toilet refuse heaps that sting the nose. It is troublesome that you think of that stench as if it were the heavenly fragrance of Takamagahara. If your nose is paralyzed to that extent, you may lose the ability to distinguish between good and evil, beauty and ugliness, and perhaps it is even comfortable for you. However, it is a waste for a human being, who has been granted a portion of the spirit of God, to lead a life of drunken dreams and dying sleep; you should clean your own feces yourself. You should not have others clean the feces you have excreted, nor should you do it for others. It is only during infancy that one has others clean one's feces. And those who clean such feces are the mothers carrying the babies or those whose job is child-minding. Try to think about it a little.'

Kyōtō: 'If I am scolded so bluntly, I have no words of defense. However, the Itsutōen has its own principles and platform as the Itsutōen. I would very much like to be allowed to clean the toilets.'

Takahime: 'No, no, that will not do. What do you think this is? The toilets here are different from ordinary toilets. They are the fertilizer of the great Miroku-sama, which has come out from his honorable rear. Although some dirty fellows of the Sharu are mixed in there, since there is a person called Sharu in this mansion... one cannot have people whose spirit and soul are not polished clean the toilets of the great Miroku-sama. It is not an ordinary matter to be allowed to clean the toilets of the great Miroku-sama. You cannot do it without receiving a great deal of divine virtue. You are probably saying such things thinking you might be treated to a bowl of barley rice, but in this harsh world, who would feed a bowl of rice to such a Kusoyatsuko? Rather than that, you should take to heart what the duty and heaven-ordained words of the Hinodewake say. If you do that, you can achieve a splendid success in life. Since you have been born as a splendid human being, is it not a shame before God to be doing toilet cleaning? It is fine that many people like you come along and take on the cleaning of toilets, but try to look a hundred years into the future. You will be treated by the Seken as a special buraku, and you will be given the name of the toilet tribe. If that happens, you will not be able to form marriage ties with ordinary people. It would be better for you to perform Tenkō to a reasonable degree. You probably intend to become the founder of a special buraku, but rather than doing such a thing, you should enter the Uranai-kyō that saves the three thousand worlds; you have no idea how splendid that would be.'

Kyōtō: 'That is also true. In truth, I cannot stand it, but because of the misery of not being able to eat, I am scraping by by cleaning the toilets that others dislike. Even so, since there seem to be many fools in the Seken, they praise a kind of well-mannered beggar as a saint or a gentleman. Since they write about it in newspapers and magazines and praise it, I have been able to endure it even if it is a little smelly. However, the founder of the Tenkō-shū is a very splendid person when seen from the outside, but he is quite skilled at buying and selling stocks, cornering the market on mines that seem profitable, and not returning what he has borrowed by making various excuses. Even so, he is highly praised by the upper classes and many of his books are sold, so the world is a strange place. Kojima Takanori wrote on a cherry tree, "Do not leave the Tenkō toilet empty; there are times when there is no lack of food offerings," and since he even predicted the affairs of my mad founder, I cannot be taken for too much of a fool.'
