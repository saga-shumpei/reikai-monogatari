Chapter 4: Phantom 子 [1454]

妖子〔1454〕

§ 1

The majordomo of the mansion, Ōrusuchin, whose aged and frail body had been trampled indiscriminately by a throng of rough men, had his breath restored once more thanks to the care of Wakkusu; yet, unable to bear the agony, he was carried back to his own mansion, running a high fever, and continued to suffer in torment day and night.

Meanwhile, within the mansion, Okuninowake had fallen into a state of suspended animation, speaking only in delirium. Furthermore, the whereabouts of the two daughters, Debisu and Kerina, remained easily unknown; the whereabouts of Michihiko, upon whom they relied as their strength, also became unknown, and Okunihime sank into an abyss of heartbreaking grief, trembling her body all over while lamenting the world, feeling as though she were scarcely alive. Yet, desiring in every way to prolong her husband's illness even for a fleeting moment and to let him meet his two daughters, she passed her days relying solely upon her husband. Inside the mansion, the two individuals, Ōkusu and Biruma, managed everything. Eru, the receptionist, with his testicles crushed by an ox, was suffering on a high pillow in the inner room of the cotton shop. Okunihime invited Ōkusu and Biruma into the living room and consulted with them in various ways. The two men, leaping in their hearts with the thought that the time had come to fulfill Wakkusu's ambition and to suck the sweet nectar, hung their heads with a feigned air of ignorance and worry.

Okunihime: 'Ōkusu, there is something I would earnestly like to consult with you about. My husband's physical condition is such that we know not when he might pass away, and besides, the majordomo Ōrusuchin is suffering from a severe illness; Wakkusu went out saying he was going on ascetic training for the healing of my husband's and Ōrusuchin's illnesses, and has not shown his face since, while my two daughters have not yet returned. If something should happen, what had best be done? I would like you, too, to think about it for a moment, you know.'

Ōkusu: 'It is truly a lamentable thing that has come to pass, indeed. It is not merely a calamity for the master's house, but a calamity for the entire town of Miyamachi. Although you are in the role of protecting the mansion of the Brahman religion, because you secretly brought in among the townspeople a magician of the Izumo religion whose origins are unknown, you have been made to suffer such a cruel fate. From now on, you must also listen a little to what we have to say. According to the rumors throughout the town, that fellow called Michihiko is a magician of the very first rank in the Izumo religion; by means of ninjutsu, he stole away the wish-granting jewel, which is of the utmost importance to the master's house, leaving the master's house with worries that beggar description. Having thoroughly thought out a situation where advancement and retreat are both blocked, he came crawling in disguised as a missionary of the Brahman religion. Therefore, even Princess Debisu, who had remained in the house until now, has become untraceable; all the precious sacred ritual implements in this town have been stolen, and there is a tremendous uproar. If the townspeople were to find out about such a thing, that would indeed be a matter of life and death for you, and you certainly could not remain in this mansion with such a bold front. If someone were to secretly send a swift horse to report this to the capital of Haruna, even supposing the master had passed away from illness, you would surely be subjected to reverse crucifixion. You have done a terrible thing. While I sympathize with your circumstances, I at the same time resent your handling of this matter. If such a thing should reach the ears of Lord Ōkuninushi, we ourselves know not what kind of punishments we might be subjected to. From now on, I would like you to listen a little to what Ōkusu says, too.'

Okunihime: 'That Michihiko-sama alone is not such a villainous person, you see; that must be some kind of mistake. It is certainly not Michihiko-sama who hid the diamond-indestructible wish-granting jewel; there is no doubt it was Wakkusu, the majordomo's son. It is clearly and distinctly known that he attempted various schemes using Ekisu, Heruman, and others in order to forcibly marry my daughter, Princess Debisu. How shameful, to cast such a crime upon a true missionary!'

Oaks: 'Okusama, husband is the first, but you are mistaken in your thinking. Whatever Wax-sama may say, he is the son of the karei; if this mansion cannot go on, his own house cannot go on either, so please think carefully and look, would you do such a disadvantageous thing? That is where the mahafutsukahi of Ananai-kyō are naive... Wax-sama is good-natured, so he was smeared. You must make them consider the innermost depths, for it is truly pitiful for Wax-sama. Please think about it carefully. A mahafutsukahi named Michihiko, startled by the war cries of the chiyōmin, fled like clouds and mist, and no matter how favorably one looks upon it, the dannasama will hardly be able to recover his health. And in this case, where even the karei, Ōrusuchin-sama, finds recovery difficult, who do you suppose is the one to become the strength of this mansion? Is it not other than Wax-sama? If you doubt Wax-sama, the chiyōmin will not forgive you for the sake of such a popular Wax-sama. If you do not lay your hand upon your chest and think well, it will be a matter of ichidaiji for the mansion.'

Okunihime: 'My, that is something I cannot comprehend; Burma, what do you think?'

Burma: 'Well, I investigated the rumors in the chiyōnai, and Wax-sama is truly a great person. Even the niyoihotsushiyu hidden by the mahafutsukahi of Ananai-kyō, he made them spit out by innocently saying he would bear the sin himself. Those who are truly chiushin and gishi are the likes of Wax-sama. Because he saved us from a situation where, if that treasure had not existed, not only the mansion but all the Miyamachi together would have had to suffer punishment from Ōkamisama, they are calling him the savior of the Terumon country. No matter what, they say we must make him the adopted son of Davis-hime-sama and govern this country, or the chiyōmin will not forgive us. Since I have no special interest in whether Wax-sama becomes the successor or not, I will not defend him for his own sake. Placing myself in a neutral zone, I will state my convictions without concealment or reservation.'

Okunihime: 'Since even the chiyōmin believe so, there is nothing that can be done. At any rate, in today's case, leaving aside whether to make him an adopted son or not for later, I would like to have Wax-sama come once.'

Oaks: 'That would be splendid, but since Wax-sama set out saying he would perform a life-or-risking task for the sake of the gods, for the mansion, and for the chiyōmin, his whereabouts are unknown, and there is no telling when he will return. Therefore, it would be inconvenient if there were no official to handle all domestic affairs. The karei has his chest trampled as you know, and there is no prospect of recovery; the two hime's whereabouts are unknown, and the dannasama is gravely ill; if someone is not newly appointed as karei, the administration of the mansion cannot be conducted for even a single day. As for myself, if I serve only as a gatekeeper, I cannot attend to the affairs of the Ōoku, you see.'

Okunihime: 'Oh, if that is so, I shall promote people in turn and ask for their help. Receptionist Eru shall be made temporary karei, and you shall become the receptionist; if we do so, everything will proceed conveniently.'

Oaks: 'Indeed, that is logical and extremely convenient. However, Eru-sama is such a flusterer, going around the town proclaiming that the dannasama passed away even while he still had life in him, causing a great commotion among the crowd, and on top of that, bumping into a cow's rear, having his testicles trampled flat, and suffering all the limits of agony in the detached room of the cotton shop. And because he is such a person that he does not notice a cow as huge as that tied by the roadside, they say he cannot even be a gatekeeper, and he has become the laughingstock of the whole town. If such a flusterer were to become karei, needless to say the prestige of the mansion, but even the divine majesty of the gods would fall, and there is strong opposition throughout the town. It would probably be for your own good to give up on that.'

Okunihime: 'Well then, how should we handle it?'

The hope of all the townsfolk is to make Wakkusu-san our adopted son, have Ōkusu-sama serve as karei, and then if you appoint ○○ as kafu, there is a rumor that the young lady will return and the younger sister Kerina-sama will also return safely. Biruma: 'The world these days consists entirely of blind men, so if I do not announce my own skills from myself, no matter when it gets to be, it will be like a hammer washed down a river, and the path to prosperity will not open up. Okunihime: 'If that is so, it cannot be helped. I shall appoint you as temporary kafu.'

Biruma: 'Moshi okusama, a temporary kafu is not the same as a furnace stoker, you know. It is the post next to karei, an important post, you see. Just to be sure, I will mention this for a moment.'

Okunihime: 'Would it not be splendid if a gatekeeper rose to the position of kafu? This mansion is stipulated by the order of Daikokunushi-no-Ōkami to have only one karei, and since we cannot place a kafu, I am sorry, but please bear with being the head gatekeeper.'

At a place like this, a nurse hurriedly enters, and says with a worried air,

'Okusama, please come quickly; the master appears to be at his final moments, and his condition has greatly changed.'

Okunihime strokes her chest and hurriedly rushes away to the master's sickroom. Ōkusu, together with Biruma, follows behind Okunihime and enters the sickroom. Okunowake suddenly sits straight up, letting his sunken eyes in his emaciated face gleam as he speaks,

Okunowake: 'Wife, where have you been going? From a while ago I have been waiting for you with great anticipation, and have those two daughters still not returned yet?'

Okunihime: 'Yes, they will likely return shortly. There is still no news of any kind.'

Okunowake: 'My, what a troubling matter, will I no longer be able to meet my daughters in this world? Ah, what a regret.'

Okunihime: 'Master, please somehow try not to lose heart; the gods will surely grant you a meeting with them by their grace.'

Okuniwake: 'Hmm, that is much trouble for him. I am sure he will let me meet them. Has the Karei, Aulstin, not come yet? What can he be doing?'

Okunihime: 'Yes, he has a little business, so he has fallen somewhat behind; he will be coming shortly.'

Oaks: 'I say, Dannasama, the Karei Aulstin had his chest broken by the chiyōmin, suffered near-death agony, and has returned to the yakata. And the machijiu is in a boiling-cauldron-like uproar, saying that you have let the sorcerer of Ananai-kyō into the okata. The two of us suppressed that matter, and now, upon consultation with the Okusama, I have just this moment become the Karei; therefore, I humbly ask for your kind favor. From now on, I will devote body and soul and achieve twelve-part results to show you, so please be at ease.'

Okuniwake: 'Aren't you Oaks, the monban? No matter how much popularity you claim to have, it won't do for a monban to become a Karei in such a single leap. Oku, did you permit such a thing?'

Okunihime: 'Yes... No.'

She fidgeted.

Okuniwake: 'To appoint a Karei, no matter what, one must obtain Aulstin's consent, and after he submits his resignation, inquire at the capital of Haruna, and not otherwise. It will not do to decide things so arbitrarily. Because this yakata is special, we must consult the Lord Daikokunushi about everything. Surely this cannot be the truth. Oku, you must have been telling a temporary joke.'

Fidgeting, Okunihime answered with a faint 'Yes' in a single word, casting her eyes downward.

Oaks: 'Even though you are the Okusama of the master of the yakata, there is no reason you should utter a joke. I believe that the words of the Okusama are heavier than gold and iron. No matter what you say, Oaks is the Karei of this house. I must investigate all affairs of the yakata and report to the capital of Haruna. If you reject this Oaks anywhere and everywhere, shall I report to the Lord Daikokunushi that you let the sorcerer of Ananai-kyō into the okata? Is that still not troublesome for you?'

Pressing her as life and death approached, he forced himself to persist as a wicked and lawless villain.

Okuniwake: 'Look here, Oku, as you see, this time I somehow do not seem to be recovering at all. Somehow, seek out Michihiko-sama as quickly as possible and have him become the strength of this yakata. If you have him protect us with that divine power, even if the God Daikokunushi should attack with tens of thousands of troops, there is no need to fear; never use such a wicked person after my death. Dismiss the monban from today. Ee, filthy!'

Containing wrath within his weakened body and mind, he shouted out. With that, he grew limp and weak once more, and suddenly fell into a comatose state.

Okunihime, while immersed in a grief too great for body or world, purposely hid her tears, changed her expression, and turning toward the two persons,

'Oaks and Biruma, both of you, since it is the master's command, I am sorry, but please leave this yakata as of this very moment. No matter what may happen, I have no intention of troubling myself with arrogant and impudent servants like you... I say, Dannasama, please be truly at ease.'

Drawing her mouth close to his ear, she declared it at the top of her voice amidst her tears. Okuniwake seemed to have faintly caught these words in his ear, and smiled powerlessly and wryly.

Oaks, exposing his arrogant face and growing fiercely haughty,

'I say, Okusama, Dannasama is suffering from a grave illness and has become senile. What he says is certainly not the truth. Words stirred by fever—if you take such things seriously, a great riot will break out in this yakata. Who else is there to shoulder this yakata on both shoulders today besides Wacks and the two of us? Think carefully. You said a monban cannot become a Karei, but what kind of class-conscious thinking are you trapped by? Long ago, didn't the monban of Tokoyo Castle get directly promoted to the office of Deputy Guard? That, too, must have been the result of failure. We are the men of distinguished service who saved the yakata from crisis. If it does not please you, it cannot be helped; we too have a certain thought of our own, so do not regret it later. The fact that the general machijiu had their precious treasure stolen is also because many people are suffering through Michihiko's sorcerer. If I may say so, Michihiko is the enemy of the machijiu. If you keep harboring that enemy until whensoever, then you two husband and wife will likewise be recognized as enemies of the country. To have this sacred ground opened by Daikokunushi trampled away plain as day by Ananai-kyō's lackey is something the general machijiu can hardly endure. If you will not use me as Karei, I will not beg further. I will report this state of affairs to the machijiu. Then the machijiu will come storming upon you as foes of Brahmanism and enemies of the gods. Be prepared.'

So he spat out, and dashed out to the front with fierce momentum.

El, whose testicles had been trampled flat by an ox and who had been recuperating in the cotton merchant's back room, had at last returned to the entrance, escorted by two or three children. Oaks and Biruma suddenly encountered each other at the entrance. El, feeling suspicious at the unusual looks of the two,

El: 'Hey, you two, changing your complexions, where are you going? There must be something behind this; first let me hear it. I will mediate somehow or other.'

Oaks and Biruma had come this far threateningly, but thinking it would not do to carelessly blurt out odd things to the machijiu and be troubled with the cleanup afterward, and just when they had been stopped by El, treating this as good fortune, the two sat down heavily at the reception desk and indulged in secret talk.
