Chapter 22: 天葬 [1472]

天葬〔1472〕

§ 1

Eru took the lead and rushed to Wakkusu's house. Korubusu of Ōrusuchin was sleeping peacefully upon the sofa. Close by, Wakkusu had fallen into a semi-upright posture with his hands touching the tatami and his legs thrust out, rolling eyes like a marsh snail with his mouth agape, as though glaring at the ceiling rafters. And he only kept rolling his eyeballs round and round. That ghastliness truly looked no different from a bakemono. The day was gradually drawing to a close. Somehow, a repulsiveness assailed from all directions. Numerous covetous fellows immediately burst into the inner room in a clamorous rush, fighting for the lead, and when they looked under the sofa, not a single ichimon remained... This is likely in the storehouse, no? ...finding the key, they forced their way into the storehouse, turned the household utensils there topsy-turvy, and were searching for the whereabouts of the gold.

Eru bowed politely with both hands on the floor before Wakkusu,

Eru: 'Well, Wakkusu-sama, such an unexpected and sorrowful thing has befallen your father, and you must be deeply worried. Yet we cannot simply leave him like this, so I, this Eru, straightway reported it to the town and have brought many people here just as you see. Please set your mind at ease. Regarding that, since it is the custom of this town that the gold and silver treasures stored up by your late father during his lifetime are made to be competed for and taken as a keepsake by the townspeople in general, you surely have no objection to that, do you? All the property of this house belongs to Ōrusuchin, and once its owner has returned to the nether world, this becomes public property, so the townspeople will leave it to their own freedom and carry away all the household utensils; thus it would be best for you to make up your mind to that. In exchange, the funeral expenses shall be met by selling off various tools and using a part of them. It would be best for you too to do some work and leave some property behind. Why did you not have a portion of the property granted to you during his lifetime? Truly, that was lacking in wisdom.'

Wakkusu finally opened his mouth, and letting fall tears from his white-eyed rolling gaze with a look of deep regret,

Wakkusu: 'Ah-ah, oi, Eru, what a regrettable thing I have done. Because I was one step late in returning, I was utterly unable to have the property transferred to me. A bakemono appeared right then, and while I was so paralyzed in my lower back that I could not move a limb, Oukusu and that fellow Biruma stuffed gold and silver into a large trunk and fled away with a chuckle. They cannot have gone far yet, so will someone not go and capture those fellows, distribute the loot, and return about three thousand ryo of it to me?'

Eru: 'Well now, that cannot be helped anymore, can it? Because there is a right of prior seizure.'

Wakkusu: 'Ah, what a regrettable thing. I must somehow wash away this grudge.'

Eru: 'That is unmanly. Do not harbor such an attachment. Rather than that, why not quickly get up, go to the shrine of the gods, report your parent's return to the nether world, carry out the procedures for a lavish burial, and become an adopted heir?'

Wakkusu: 'As long as the magicians of Izumo Shinpo do not perish, it is no use. Is there no scheme by which I can somehow flatten those fellows?'

Eru: 'That is easy. I have swallowed everything down completely.'

He spoke thus with a clever air. Thereupon, numerous old men and old women came along letting watery snot drip, wiping their eyes and blowing their hand-snot,

Together (weeping voices): 'Wān wān wān wān, ōn ōn ōn ōn, oh Wakkusu-sama. Pull yourself together. Is it not a sorrowful thing? Wān wān wān, ōn ōn ōn.'

And they began to put on a conventional, feigned cry. Both outside and inside the house, they spat upon their eyes and ritual weeping began. According to the custom of this place, no matter how hateful an enemy died, there was a rule that if one did not perform ritual weeping, one would be banished from the outskirts of the town. The fellows who had set their sights on the property inside the storehouse had also finished marking their respective name tags, and finally, with peace of mind, came before Wakkusu,

エル『ソリヤ、もう仕方が無いぢやないか。先取権があるのだからな』

ワックス『エー残念な事をした。此怨みを如何しても晴らさにやおかぬのだ』

エル『男らしくもない。そんな執着心を持つな。それよりも早く腰を上げて神館に参り親の帰幽を報告し、厚く葬る手続きをした上、御養子になつたら如何だ』

ワックス『三五教の魔法使が滅びぬ間は駄目だ。何とかして彼奴を平げる工夫はあるまいかな』

エル『あらいでかい。何も彼も俺がスツカリ呑み込んで居るのだ』

一同（泣声）『ワーンワーンワーンワーン、オーンオーンオーンオーン、これワックスさま。確りしなされや。悲しい事ぢやないかいな。ワーンワーンワーン、オーンオーンオーン』

と義理一遍の作り泣きを始め出した。家の外にも内にも目に唾をつけて義理泣きが始まつた。此処の習慣として何程憎らしい敵が死んでも、義理泣きをせなくば町外れをされる規則がある。倉の中の財産に目をつけた連中も各自に自分の名札を記け終り、ヤツト安心してワックスの前に来り、

Ichidō (Nakigoe): 'Waan waan waan waan, oon oon oon oon, waan waan waan, uun uun uun uun, Wakkusu-sama, you are truly to be pitied. Please give up now, I too shall give up. I am moved to tears of gratitude for receiving so many Katami. Waan waan waan waan, oon oon oon oon.'

After a while, the Saushikigakari of the Chiyōnai arrived. Then, the Biku, holding a bell reverently in his Yunde and winding Jiyuzu around his right hand, sat in Tanza before the Korubusu and began to chant mysterious Kiyōmon.

Biku: 'Chiin, chin chin chin, Shiyogiyōmujiyō, Zeshiyōmetsubofu, Shiyōmetsumetsuchi, Jiyakumetsuiraku, Namu Baramonsontenshi, Daijizaitenshi, Ōkunihikonomikoto, Kimeuchiyōrai, Reihōkaji. Reflecting upon the eighty-odd years of life in the Gense, during which time he served at the Kamuyakata of Mount Terumon, rising to the position of Karei, not to mention managing the Kuwaikei of the mansion and handling its Itsusai affairs, his efforts were not in vain; yet, because of the deep sins of greed, anger, and ignorance, his only son Wakkusu, bestowed upon him by the Brahman Heaven, became a reckless villain and was scorned as a fool and a delusional person, and having parted early from his wife and lived a lonely life in this floating world, it is entirely the result of Tenmei. However, because the Lord God resides in supreme benevolence and love, with this departure from the world, the external conditions shall be removed, all sins shall be erased at the crossroads, and he shall be made a pure and immaculate spirit, destined to be saved in heaven. You, spirit of Ōrusuchin, do not harbor attachments to the Gense; depart quickly to the immortal Reikai according to the laws of the Yūmeikai. Do not be lost. Attachment is the seed of hell. Kimeuchiyōrai, Namu Baramonsonten, protect him and grant him grace, chiin, chin chin chin chin chin.'

Biku: 'Now then, I have thoroughly provided the final guidance. Everyone, will you perform a burial in the earth, a burial in the water, or perhaps a burial in the sky? Which do you prefer? That is at your discretion, please decide.'

Wakkusu: 'Since I am the chief mourner, please let it be done according to my wishes. I beg you to perform a sky burial. If so, he shall surely go to heaven.'

Biku: 'Everyone, follow the wishes of Wakkusu-sama, and since we shall now perform a sky burial, I apologize for the trouble, but please make the preparations.'

Ichidō replied, 'We understand,' and having completed all preparations, they placed the Korubusu of Ōrusuchin upon a door panel and carried it toward the graveyard of Mount Terumon.

A sky burial is a ritual in which the Korubusu (corpse) is carried to the graveyard, cut into small pieces with stone knives or round stones, the bones are ground into powder without leaving a trace, mixed with roasted barley flour to make dumplings, and fed to a multitude of vultures. A water burial is a ritual in which the Korubusu is simply thrown into a river. Many men and women, young and old, used stone knives, stone fragments, and various wooden swords to cut the Korubusu into small pieces, and while singing pure songs, they began the preparations for the sky burial, sweating profusely. Vultures were soaring in the hundreds in the mid-air, waiting. The Biku sang a song. Ichidō kept time and broke down the Korubusu.

Biku's song:

Shiyogiyōmujiyō, Zeshiyōmetsubofu Shiyōmetsumetsuchi, Jiyakumetsuiraku is the way of the world This world is but a temporary one Buried in the belly of a vulture Without wings, in the mid-air Soaring to the noble heaven How joyous to ascend without difficulty Chin chin chin chin, chin chin chin Everyone, I rely on you firmly The Korubusu of Ōrusuchin Will be a bit of hard work Since there is so much skin and bone And not a bit of meat I do not know if the vultures will be pleased To eat it, but There, there, roasted barley Apply the powder generously To give it a good flavor A sky burial where not a single piece Must be left upon the earth

天葬と云へばコルブス（死骸）を墓地に運び石刀や丸石を以て体を細々にきざみ、骨も残らず粉にして了ひ、麦の煎粉をまぶして団子をつくり、沢山な禿鷲に喰はして了ふ儀式である。又水葬と云へばコルブスを其の儘川へ投げ込んで了ふ儀式である。数多の老若男女は石刀や石片や種々の木刀を以てコルブスを一寸刻み五分試しとなし、潔き歌を唄ひながら汗をタラタラ出して天葬の準備に着手した。禿鷲は中空に羽ばたきしながら幾百ともなく翺翔して待つてゐる。比丘は歌を歌ふ。一同は拍子をとつてコルブスを挫く。

Hagewashi-dono, pray exert yourself and consume every single bone without leaving a scrap. Chin-chin-chin-chin, chin-chin-chin. Shiyogiyōmujiyō, Zeshiyōmetsubofu. Shiyōmetsumetsuchi, Jiyakumetsuiraku. Leaving behind this transient Ukiyo, casting off all Shifuchiyakushin, may your body and Mitama ascend to Tengoku, riding upon wings of Kogane. Could there be anything more Medeta? Wakkusu-sama is also blessed with Shiawase. Compared to the Dosau, Suisau, or Kuwasau of the lowly commoners, this is the Saizen of all Hafushiki. Your Chichiuhe, saved and ascending to Tengoku, is truly a Ketsukō Mitama. Rejoice and celebrate, everyone. Chin-chin-chin-chin, chin-chin-chin. In the Jiyōdo of Tengoku, forevermore.

Given Hiyakumi Onjiki, seated upon a lotus pedestal, looking down from afar upon the Gekai, not to mention Mount Terumon, beginning with the mansion of Kami, may you protect the happiness of the Hitobito in this Chiyōnai and dispel their sorrows, becoming a true living Kami. Chin-chin-chin. Chin-chin-chin-chin, chin-chin-chin. Everyone, be grateful for this. The sutra chanting of the Brahmanism has been brought to a Medeta conclusion. Now, I shall return ahead of you. Since you have performed the first Tensōshiki, bring plenty of offerings later. Because there is a great inheritance, no matter how much money you spend,

it will not cause you any stomach pains or headaches, just as drawing water from the same bathhouse and giving it to the customer next to you is the same act. Please cherish the Bhikkhu. Take refuge in the Buddha, take refuge in the Dharma, take refuge in the Bhikkhu. If you err in this great law, be prepared to fall instantly into the hellish path when you die, so do not begrudge your offerings. Ah, if that is so, if that is so, I shall return ahead of you now. Chin-chin-chin-chin, chin-chin-chin.

So saying, while striking his bell, he led his two attendants and returned to his own hermitage.

The group finally finished the Tensōshiki, returned once more to the mansion of Wakkusu, and, preparing various feasts without stint, they lost themselves in gluttony and debauchery. Here, another great spectacle was performed. It was that Sumāto had burst into the middle of the banquet.
