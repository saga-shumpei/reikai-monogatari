Chapter 24: 空縛 [1474]

空縛〔1474〕

§ 1

Learning through the report of Tonku that Aursutin, the steward, had passed away in the Kamuyakata of Okuniwake, they proceeded directly to the Daishinden, offered the products of Sanyakakai, and with Michihiko acting as the saishiyu, while Kiudaukoji, Okunihime, Princess Davis, Princess Kerina, Hel, and other male and female servants participated, they performed the Kiiuhaukokusai for Aursutin and also held a grand saiten to pray for his meifuku.

Into such a place, Ōkuronushi's emissary from the capital of Haruna, Sendenshi Nicholas, leading six attendants named Polito, Batt, Lievena, Hanna, Malice, and Ruikin along with several tens of heisotsu, hurried into this mansion, took jindo in the ōsetsushitsu, and waited for the saiten to conclude.

While Michihiko and the rest of the ichidō, not knowing even in their dreams that Nicholas had come to this mansion leading several tens of soldiers, were concentrating on their prayers with single-minded devotion and, acting with leisurely composure, were about to return to the inner chambers to rest, Nicholas walked in with his long sword still hanging at his waist,

Nicholas: 'I am one who has been dispatched on a momentous mission by the Lord God, Ōkuronushi, of the capital of Haruna. Since the heisotsu I lead are fatigued from their long journey, pray grant them an appropriate resting place. Furthermore, what has become of Okuniwake-dono? I desire him to appear here swiftly.'

Okunihime: 'Oh my, oh my, it is from afar that the honored imperial envoy has come, and it would be my true intention to have Okuniwake come out to welcome you, but since he has been suffering from a serious illness bordering on life and death and has only now managed to cling to his life, I am discourteously keeping away, much against my will. By all means, pray forgive me.'

Nicholas: 'Okunihime, that must indeed be a great worry for you. If it is an illness, I will not insist on seeing you. However, I have heard rumors that this mansion harbors a magician calling himself the heretical sendenshi Michihiko; how is that? Since you are in a position entrusted with a precious sacred site of Brahmanism, especially the mansion of Ōkuronushi's origin, surely there can be no such misunderstanding. I ask to receive your reply promptly.'

Okunihime: 'Yes, at this stage what is there for me to hide? As you surmise, Michihiko-sama, the sendenshi of Izumimandara, and the holy man called Kiudau-sama have indeed arrived.'

Nicholas: 'To such a sacred holy place, with whose permission did they enter? I ask to hear the reason.'

Okunihime, her heart smitten with a start, resigned herself to the realization that there was no escape, wiped her tears with one hand,

Okunihime: 'It is truly a matter for which I have no excuse, but there are deep circumstances behind this. I beg you to hear me out once. In this mansion, wicked people swarmed; our entrusted sacred treasures from Ōkuronushi-sama were stolen, and we were at our wits' end. Just as the two of us were thinking of cutting our bellies open to offer an apology, Michihiko-sendenshi of Izumimandara appeared driftingly and taught us the whereabouts of the jewels. Moreover, he rescued me when I was drowning in tears of sorrow because my two daughters had been abducted by villains, and we have finally managed to have a parent-child reunion. Therefore, considering these two gentlemen to be the saviors of this mansion, I am forcibly detaining them even though they say they wish to return home quickly. There is certainly no guilt in Michihiko-sama or Kiudau-sama. Since I brought them in myself, I beg for your judgment, whatever it may be.'

Nicholas: 'Your judgment cannot be dealt with until I first hear the opinions of Ōkuronushi-sama. Until then, it would be best for you to restrain yourself quietly. However, there must be not a single moment of delay for the heretical sendenshi; come, bring him out before me as quickly as possible.'

Okunihime: 'Yes.'

So saying, she changed her complexion and fidgeted uneasily.

Michihiko: 'I am the very Michihiko you are inquiring after. Although I am called a sendenshi of Izumimandara today, since I am the husband of Princess Davis, the adopted daughter of this mansion, it will likely not go as you please. If you have any opinions, I am ready to hear them.'

Kiudaukoji: 'I am Kiudaukoji, a Shiyugenjiya of the Ananai-kyō, and the husband of Kerinahime, the daughter of this house. If there is any Futsugafu, you may do as you please.'

Devisuhime: 'Jiyōshisama, I am the wife of a Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō. I beg you to execute me in place of my husband.'

Kerinahime: 'I, too, shall accept execution as a substitute for my husband.'

Michihiko: 'Ahahahaha, Nicholas-dono, come, bind us quickly.'

Nicholas, contrary to the expectation that he might apologize, was taken aback by the composure of the four, yet he signaled to his six attendants. The six took out binding ropes from their bosoms and tied the four. The four were led to the Omotemon, remaining Shiyōyō as they were bound. Nicholas, as a Miseshime to the Tenka, drove stakes into the Hiroba before the gate, tied the four in a Ganjigara state, had them guarded strictly by Sūjifunin of Heisotsu, and taking the six with him, he returned to the inner room with his arms held high.

Okunihime was alone, leaning against an armrest, lost in sorrow.

Nicholas: 'Aiya, Okunihime-dono, what did you think of such cowards that you let them into your mansion? It is a method unbecoming of you to make even two daughters into criminals; it is a hasty way of doing things. I am sorry, but there is no longer any way to save them. You had better be prepared.'

Okunihime: 'The god of calamity who pursues me to the end; I am already prepared. Everyone, farewell.'

No sooner had she said this than she took out a dagger from her bosom and attempted to plunge it in, when in an instant, Smart flew through the air, rushed over, bit onto the dagger with a single bark, snatched it away, and ran off toward the front at the speed of Idaten.

From the next room, in the voice of Michihiko:

Chihayafuru kami no megumi ni dakareshi Ware karada o shibaru yoshi nashi. Ananai no kami no oshie no sendenshi Ima koko ni ari Nicholas no kimi

Nicholas, hearing this voice, his suspicion not cleared,

Nicholas: 'No, Okunihime-dono, it seems you have learned the magic of the Ananai-kyō. You are an even more suspicious creature. Since it has come to this, without waiting for the orders of Ōkuninushi-sama, I shall bind you and deal with you. Hanna, Maris, and the other four, bind this woman at once.'

The six answered, 'Hai,' and attempted to bind Okunihime without ceremony. At this moment, a cool voice came from the next room:

One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, hundred, thousand, ten thousand.

The heavenly number song was heard. From the body of Okunihime, golden light suddenly radiated, and Nicholas and the six others were instantly dazzled, staggering backward as they collapsed into the middle of the room.

Michihiko, Kiudaukoji, Devisu, and Kerina, the four, appeared leisurely from the next room while smiling, and took their seats before Okunihime. Upon seeing them, Okunihime was surprised twice,

Okunihime: 'Ah, you are the Sendenshi-sama, and, daughter, how did you untie those bonds and return?'

Michihiko: 'Truly, blades cannot stand against a single body. Even if you bind us, you cannot bind us. Please be at ease.'

Okunihime: 'Thank you. Ōkami of the Ananai-kyō, thank you for saving us. From this moment on, I shall abandon Brahmanism and remove the shrine, so please forgive me. Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haese.'

She said this while pressing her hands together in prayer.

Nicholas and the six others rose up again, and

Nicholas: 'Hey, how did you untie the bonds and return, you insolent wretches? Come, bind them quickly.'

The four all at once,

Michihiko, Kiudaukoji, Devisu, Kerina: 'Ahahahaha, Hohohohoho.'

They laughed aloud while turning their hands around. The six bound the four with great care, and this time, thinking it was surely safe, they bound them further with thin wire and led them once again to the gate.
