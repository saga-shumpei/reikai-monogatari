Chapter 11: Bird 逃し [1461]

鳥逃し〔1461〕

§ 1

With her hair, resembling a straw broom mixed with white, streaming wildly in the withered wind, she came running like a yasha, slapping the earth loudly with her feet until she reached the crossroads.

Looking, she saw that Magafuyu of Tenkaukyō, who had come just before, was running with his loincloth trailing black like a falling star, hitching up his shirihitsu and marching heavily with great strides.

No matter how much Takahime called and shouted, he would not even look back; unable to keep up with his darting momentum, Takahime gnashed her teeth at the crossroads and stamped the ground in frustration,

Takahime: 'Oh, what a shame! Sharu, because you are such a tonma, you let the bird that we went to all the trouble of bringing out escape. Why didn't you tie a rope around it while you had the chance? It is written in the Ofude, "If even the Kami ties a rope around it, no matter how much it wriggles and writhes in agony, it will never let it go."'

Sharu: 'I am a ningen, and you are a kamisama, aren't you? What good would it do for a ningen to tie a rope? Instead of complaining to me, why didn't the Hinode kamisama tie the rope? I will not listen to such complaints.'

Takahime: 'My, what a man who loves to quibble! Why won't you show zetsutaifukujiyū? Just who do you think you are treating me as?'

Sharu: 'Well, when I wondered who on earth you might be, lo and behold, you were right here. When I thought you were right here, lo and behold, you were Takahime-sama; when I thought you were Takahime-sama, you were the Ikumiya of Giritenjo Hinode no Kami; when I thought that, you were the Great Miroku of Sokotsu Iwane; when I thought that, you were the Heavenly Being of the First Spirit Realm; when I thought that, you were the Savior of the Manifest, Hidden, and Divine Worlds; when I thought that, you were the wife of Mokusuke; when I thought that, you were the august spirit of Tokoyohime; when I thought that, you were the august spirit of Tokoyohime, you were Takamiyahime-sama; and when I thought you were Takamiyahime-sama, you were Sadakohime-sama. Who is who, I haven't the slightest clue at all. Shall I just treat you as being right here?'

Takahime: 'Oh, what a useless thing to say, what kind of talk is that! Listing so many names, wouldn't it be fine to just say one? Verbose and tedious etiquette is not in fashion, you know. Without saying such complex names, why not simply say Ikumiya-sama? Won't the priest of Hōshōji come to collect the taxes?'

Sharu: 'Even so, with a simple title, it probably wouldn't please you. The longer you can make it, the better your mood is, after all. Saying it long and trailingly, like the drooping tail of the mountain bird trailing the foot of the mountain, seems to have some sort of value out of nowhere. Above all, it has an elegant taste, you see.'

Takahime: 'Good grief, what are you grumbling on about—simple, complex, elegant taste, and what not? Words like those of foreign barbarians are not used in the high Plain of High Heaven down here!'

Sharu: 'And yet, do you not occasionally use English, Anata?'

Takahime: 'That is not English; those are the pure and genuine words of the Age of the Gods! When you drone on with names for so long when I am this busy, it is not a very pleasant feeling. Shall I recite one for you, too? Then you will understand the flavor. When I wonder who on earth you are, you are the fellow right here. When I think you are a flag-bearing laborer of Brahmanism, you are a petty thief. When I think you are a petty thief, you are a river thief. When I think you are a river thief, you are a botched-suicide Yachimata laborer. When I think you are a Yachimata laborer, you are the soul of a damned ghost registered in hell. When I think you are a ghost's soul, you are an incorrigible scoundrel. When I think you are an incorrigible scoundrel, you are a spineless coward. You are a good-for-nothing, toothless, soul-vacant rascal! When I think you are a soul-vacant rascal, you are Takahime's arse-wiper. When I think you are an arse-wiper, you are Sharu after all! Ho, ho, ho, ho!'

Sharu: 'That is rather brutal, isn't it? To think you could manage to line up so many insults. After all, your claim of Giritenjo is a lie—you must be the vessel of the Golden-Haired Nine-Tailed Fox.'
