Kiudaukoji escorted Davis and Kelina as far as the pine forest, and was resting there for a shibashi with Heru. Suddenly, amidst the war cries of rauniyakunanniyo and the beating of taiko and gongs,

Dontokodontoko dokodokodon Chiyanchikichiyanchikichiyanchikichin!'

Thus shouting, wearing red sashes and red headbands, and carrying white flags with scrawled, messy characters, old and young with darkened faces came streaming down the slope. That the townsfolk as a whole had begun such erratic agitation was the doing of an evil fox constantly employed by Wax. This evil fox was a subordinate of Yōgenbō, a millennia-old ancient fox called Sankūbō, who from time to time lit thousands of fires at midnight on Mount Terumon, or caused huge characters to appear through letters, thereby deceiving the townspeople. This Sankūbō went in and out of Wax's body, always plotting ill deeds. When Michihiko of Ananai-kyō suddenly arrived there, fearing the exposure of its own true identity, and thinking that now was the very time to banish Michihiko and ensure that not a single envoy of Ananai-kyō could pry, it exerted its utmost strength, and first of all, to disrupt the minds of the townsfolk, it borrowed the mouth of Wax and began a street speech.

The townspeople, numbering around a hundred households, swallowed Wax's delusive words without a single doubt, and leaving their houses empty, set out to search for the princess. Every single house emptied itself, rushing outside, leaving not even a single cat behind to mind the home.

Meanwhile, Oksu and Biruma, under the secret orders of Wax, searched the deserted homes, stole every single article of value, and had them carried off to a certain cave on Mount Terumon.

Among all hundred households, there was not a single house whose most precious possessions were left un-stolen.

Wax, leading a multitude of people, pushed forward into the pine forest where the four were resting, and with a great voice,

Wakkusu: 'Hey there, you shaven-headed Buddhist monks, you use the magic of Ananai-kyō, you have kidnapped and hidden the two young ladies somewhere, you used the white foxes of Asahi and Takakura to make them impersonate Princess Debisu and Princess Kerina, and you are atrocious villains who advanced to get your hands on the treasure of the Niyoihotsushiyu once again; moreover, the other one is the ringleader of the major thieves who ravaged Miyamachi with drawn swords by threes a month ago, and even if you try to hide it, it will not be hidden. Now how is it, submit peacefully to your bonds!'

He shouted angrily. Kyudaukoji said in a gentle tone,

Kyudaukoji: 'I am by no means an evil god; originally I am one called Kernel, an Emishi of Brahminism. Following the command of Lord Ōkuronushi, obeying the two generals Oniharuwake and Kumehiko, on the way of marching to the mansion of Iso, I met the propaganda envoy of Ananai-kyō, Kāshyapa the Honored One, and Jikokuwake, who preached and explained the true way to me, and thus I became a monk. And these two honorable persons are Princess Debisu and Princess Kerina of the Terumon mansion. I have helped them in their hour of emergency and escorted them this far. Now dispel your doubts and guide us all.'

Wakkusu: 'Ha ha ha ha ha, what disgusting spewing, you shit-priest fellow! Even if you try to fool me, who possess peerless divine power, with deceptive eloquence, I am not the sort of person to be taken in by such things. Princess Debisu certainly does not have ears like those. It must be a malicious fox called Takakura; isn't blood still dripping as proof that its transformation failed and its ears were bitten by a dog? And isn't that other wretch-fox disguised as Princess Kerina also bearing a wound on the back of her neck? You too have traces of a wound around your ears. You must have certainly been bitten by a dog and come all the way here in disguise. Now everyone, there is no need for hesitation, by the command of Wakkusu, it would be best to bind them up and send them to the cave of Mount Terumon in order to make them confess the whereabouts of the princesses.'

Debisu: 'Hey, aren't you Norosaku's son Wakkusu, the steward? What a rude thing you are saying. Have you no sense to treat me, your benefactor who saved my life, with such discourtesy?'

Kerina: 'Oh, how rare, Lord Wakkusu, you are a man who continues to derail as usual. My younger sister and I are beyond doubt the daughters of Ogunowake, so hurry and get out of the way, for I am worried about our father's personal safety and we must return as quickly as possible.'

Wakkusu: 'Even if you try to chatter and deceive us, we will not fall for that trick. Now everyone, the fact that those ears are bitten is the proof of four-legged beasts. Bind them up quickly!'

When he gave the order, dozens of rough men bound the four tightly and boundlessly. Although Kyudaukoji and the other three were not persons to be easily bound by nameless urchins, they deliberately submitted to being bound so that they could return to the mansion bound, astonish Wakkusu and the others, and thus let them be. However, because Wakkusu had a scheme in his heart, by the command of the vice-guard of Sangubō, he divided them one by one into the rock caves in the ravine on the opposite side where Mikahiko had been thrown, sent them in together with the crowd, and firmly locked the bolts.

When the townspeople gave victory cheers and returned to their respective homes, they saw that the necessary gold and silver censers and water pots for worshipping the Brahmin god had vanished without a single one remaining. All of them, flustered and alarmed, came out to the street and recounted the circumstances of the robbery to one another. Those who came riding proudly on donkeys to that place were Wakkusu, Oukusu, and Biruma. The crowd gathered around the three and complained about the circumstances of the robbery.

Oukusu spoke in a loud voice from his horse:

Oukusu: 'The wicked propaganda envoys of Ananai-kyō sneaked into the mansion of Mount Terumon, swindled Princess Debisu, hid the Niyoihotsushiyu, cursed Ogunowake to take his life, and not being satisfied even with this, they snatched away the heirlooms of the houses in Miyamachi and every single essential utensil for worshipping the Brahmin god without leaving one. This is entirely the work of their magic, so do not be careless at all. If you dawdle, your precious daughters will be taken, your property plundered, and in the end even the lives of all of you will be taken. By now, at the divine mansion of Terumon, Lord Ogunowake is in a situation of nine deaths and one life, and his life is hardly likely to be saved. In addition, the whereabouts of the two young ladies are unknown because of the magicians, and you can hardly live in peace. Moreover, now that we have lost the most vital Niyoihotsushiyu, we are either to incur the wrath of Lord Ōkuronushi residing in the capital of Haruna and be banished from the village, or suffer extermination; yet because this Lord Wakkusu managed to get his hands on the Niyoihotsushiyu through his divine power, the crime of the hundred households of Miyamachi has been escaped. Therefore, from now on, let us revere Lord Wakkusu as the master of the mansion, and let the whole village recommend to both of them that they become husband and wife after the real Princess Debisu is found, and thus protect the house passed down from our ancestors, shall we not? Until today, Lord Wakkusu deliberately played the fool and showed himself as a prodigal son, and he is the true savior who has saved this national crisis.'

Thus he spun sweet lies and persuaded them. The assembled men and women, young and old, all resented suffering from the magic of the evil gods of Ananai-kyō, praised Wakkusu's divine power, and hurried back to their respective homes while chanting 'Long live Wakkusu!' three times. When they returned, since the ritual utensils and property passed down from their ancestors for generations were gone, they sank into sorrow once again, hated the propaganda envoys of Ananai-kyō all the more, and conversely came to respect Wakkusu like a god.
