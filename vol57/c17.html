Chapter 17: 涼窓 [1467]

涼窓〔1467〕

§ 1

Wax, the son of the steward, who was imprisoned by color and desire, sought to achieve his long-held ambition. He incited two of his wicked friends, stole the Niyoi Hotsushiyu that was enshrined in the Kami's mansion, and framed Okuninowake, who had been entrusted with its supervision. By forcing his will upon the beloved Davis-hime, he occupied the Kami's mansion and sought to live in luxury and splendor. Just as he was about to fulfill his akugiyakubudau scheme, Michihiko, a Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō and a teacher of the Kami's Oshihe, descended like the morning sun. His kiuaku was suddenly exposed, and with nowhere left to hide, he spun various zatsuta kankei.

He spread rumors that Michihiko was a mahafuzukahi, and slandered the Kiudaukoji who had saved the hime from kikifu, as well as the two pitiful otodoi, claiming they were incarnations of white foxes. He deceived many townspeople, bound them tightly, and threw them into the deep, ugly caverns of Mount Terumon. Watching for the right moment, he would sneak to the front of the cavern night after night, burning with the fire of love. Though he tried to woo them, the otodoi, whose souls were firmly set, showed no mercy and, in their anger, brushed him off without a second thought. Wax, the foolish son who had become a slave to love, found himself with no room left to act.

In the end, he became desperate, brandishing a sharp bamboo spear and preparing to strike Davis-hime down. Just then, parting the grass, Smart, the messenger of the Kami, flew in like a bird and bit his right arm. While letting out a cry of 'Ah!', he collapsed on the spot for a moment. Enduring the pain, he rose, and seeking to vent his love-born grudge, he incited the wild warriors of Akuyokai and brought them along. They swarmed in front of the cavern where Kiudaukoji was imprisoned, broke down the iron gate without difficulty, and dragged the two outside. Carrying spears and swords, they tormented the pair, who received dozens of wounds on their bodies.

Just as their dew-like lives were about to perish, Michihiko arrived, leading the fierce dog Smart, intending to save Kiudaukoji. Upon seeing the scene, he found exactly as expected: dozens of wild men were attacking, aiming at Michihiko. Michihiko, not the least bit flustered, recited the Amatsu Norito and was in the very midst of chanting the song of the living Kototama. The fierce dog Smart began a great activity, moving left and right with the force of a lion. Without any mercy, the wild men were knocked down one after another from the entrance like falling shogi pieces. Even the reckless young men, realizing they were no match, fled into the overgrown fields, each in their own way.

They crawled away on all fours, stumbling as they went. The sight was truly comical. Michihiko could not help but burst into laughter, and even while being blown by the storm of mercy, he approached Kiudaukoji, comforting him with gentle words. Carrying the wounded on his back, he returned to the Kami's mansion, avoiding the eyes of others. Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara, how grateful is the help of the Kami. Okuni-hime, followed by the otodoi Davis-hime and Kerina-hime, carried Kiudaukoji on their backs, and with Smart and Heru in tow, they returned to the back entrance of the Kami's mansion, arriving leisurely to await the return of Michihiko-tsukasa.

Okuni-hime: 'Sendenshi of the pillar of the Kami, your divine virtue is the salvation of Michihiko-san, the one and only sincerity. Thank you so very much. Come, come, please proceed to the inner chamber and take a rest. Please partake in this exquisite treat and soothe your fatigue. Kiudaukoji-san and Heru-sama, who have suffered many wounds, will be attended to by Kerina-hime, who will provide diligent care. First of all, please set your mind at ease. My two daughters have been taken care of with such great kindness. You are the saving Kami for both of them. Standing alongside Michihiko-sama, you are the divine pillars of the warp and woof. I revere and respect the height of your divine virtue.'

Okuni-hime: 'Even if the morning sun shines or clouds over, even if the moon waxes or wanes, even if the earth itself should sink, as long as the sincere Kami exists, how could we fear any attack of evil on this mansion of Mount Terumon? Stirring up our sincere hearts, brandishing the sword that breaks evil, and manifesting infinite divine virtue like a sharp blade cutting through tangled hemp, I shall not leave the hundred lost people unsaved by the teachings of the sincere Kami. My heart is set on this. Protect us, Kannagara, Kuninotokotachi-no-Ōkami, and all other hundred great Kami. Appear as the savior of the three thousand worlds, Michihiko-tsukasa and Kiudaukoji.'

Okuni-hime: 'Protect the fate of us, parent and child. Ah, Kannagara, Kannagara, manifesting a pure heart, I swear to the Kami and pray.'

So she sang, and clapping her hands together, she celebrated Michihiko's safe return and expressed her resolve for the future. Led by Okuni-hime, Michihiko passed into an inner room and spent time talking with Davis-hime about various matters. Kiudaukoji and Heru were carried into a room with a good view, where the windows were thrown open. Bathing in the cool night breeze, they forgot the pain of their wounds and lay down on a sofa under the kind care of Kerina-hime. Heru also lay down, lining up his pillow, and came to receive the kind care of Kerina-hime.
