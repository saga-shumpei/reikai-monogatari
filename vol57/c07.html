Chapter 7: Darkness Gloom [1457]

暗闇〔1457〕

§ 1

Wax was seized by the fierce dog Smart, carried out of the gate, and for a while, feeling faint, lay sprawling upon the summer grass and moaning. Into such a place came Biruma, praising the moon and humming a nose-song as he approached. Suddenly the entire heaven clouded over, the great sky like spilled ink, and swiftly wrapped up the moonlight.

Biruma: 'A single drop of darkness Between heaven and earth Falls heavily down, And darkness and silence Come scurrying in Amidst the swelling dark, The waves of roof-tiles echo, The forest of chimneys stands mute, The mountain ranges of the four directions lie prostrate, And all things Now at last Dissolve into an ash-gray hue From a single wall of darkness, A soot-stained, Reddened Moon Fringed its brow Reeling and swaying, The moon Begun to ascend Steep cloud-paths For a while, Before long, As the demon spreads its wings And shuts the moon away In a bedding of black clouds, From between its tooth-fallen gaps of darkness, Laughing aloud with a cackle, It hurried and plunged deep into the earth Next morning, A lukewarm rain Was falling patter-patter The moon hides within dreadful cloud-gaps, Officer Wax hides in the clouds, a black hound flies over, barking and raging "Oo, woof-woof!" Startled with a "Watsu," Wax drags it out by the sash, rat-tat-tap to outside the gate. At that terrifying demeanor, Biruma begins to tremble bily-bily, and Lord Oaks runs away. The fellow Eru who crushed his testicles hides away as swift as a cloud. Be that as it may, where on earth has Princess Davis hidden herself? With a lovely, exquisite countenance that might even be likened to the moon, snow, and flowers, even a brief glimpse makes one tremble. As clouds hide the moon, some wicked Kuratsu comes along, the lotus flower of the Telmon Mansion. Though I know not where it was hidden, Officer Wax is anxious. That princess whom he loved so much, the magician of Anago, was snatched away by Michimichihiko, leaving him biting his fingers agape. By now she must be drenched with the dew of the grass and tears. That being said, as for myself, I am a man not made of wood and stone; born a man, the urge arises to exchange a night's pillow with such a nice lady. Ah, sincerely do I pray, O deities! Led by the matchmaker of the moon, to find the princess's whereabouts and achieve the greatest feat of prowess is the plight that has fallen upon me. Finally slipping out of the mansion, relying upon the moonlight, Though I have come this far, Not an inch ahead can be seen or known. On the pitch-dark horse-road, Ouch, ouch, ouch! What on earth is this? Something nasty has touched me. Hey, hey, are you a demon? A thing beyond comprehension. The magician of Anago Showing a demon in such a place And trying to take my spirit—it's no use! Un, un, un, un! What is that? A fox, a badger, or a wolf? Or perhaps a tengu or an old tanuki? A groaning voice of unknown meaning. In this situation where, even if I try to run, My footing is unclear, Shall I steady my courage And investigate the true identity of this monster? If by chance it were the princess, That indeed would be an unexpected windfall. Ah, sincerely do I pray, O deities! May your divine spirits prosper and bless me!'

So trembling and shivering, he sang. Suddenly the black clouds parted with a snap, and the bright summer moon illuminated all sides as if it were day. Upon the jewel-like dew resting on the grass, the moonlight dwelt, shining like lapis lazuli. Biruma, seeing the black shadow at his feet and tilting his head to peer, perceived it to be truly the groaning of a human. Approaching timidly, he shook it two or three times, crying, 'Oii, oii!' The fallen shadow rose up heavily, peering into Biruma's face and staring intently. Since Biruma had the moon at his back, he could not clearly see the face, but upon the other's face, receiving the moonlight, even the texture of the skin was discernible.

Biruma: 'Hey, aren't you Wax? You've certainly suffered a terrible ordeal. When a mountain dog came out of nowhere and carried you off, the terror of it—not being able to abandon a comrade in distress, while Oaks and that fellow Eru were trembling quivering-shivering—I, this peerless and intrepid Biruma, thought to come and rescue you, only to run smack into you. My apologies, are you hurt anywhere?'

Wakkusu: 'Ah, arigata, you have come just in time. Because we are friends, you came, didn't you ah. When the yamainu came out, dragged me all the way here, and shook me three or four times at the very saigo, my eyes were spinning and I was frightened. But precisely because you are a shiniu, you came. If you had left me as I was, my inochi might have been gone. Ah, arigata, I offer you my thanks; kitsuto once I achieve my mokuteki, I will make you a karei, so wait and enjoy yourself.'

Biruma: 'How suddenly the kumoyuki has changed. You always used to call me monban, monban, but today of all days you said we are friends. Ah, arigata, I beg you to associate with me as a shiniu for a long time to come. Normally you would not even turn to look at wareware as if we were chiriakuta, but yatsupa in times of distress we turn to kamidano; it seems you are glad to have someone come in such a time. Yet if we stay in a place kayō this, who knows who might see us. Come, I will send you off to your mansion as quickly as ichiji possible.'

Wakkusu: 'Return home, you say? You might think whatever you like, but once again a magician of Ananai-kyō seems to have sneaked into the mansion. If that were not so, that dog could never have come out. Come, taking advantage of the ninki rising now, we shall beat gongs and taiko, begin tsujisetsupofu, lure that Michihiko monguwai, and defeat him, otherwise our standing may be ruined by the exposure of our plot. Even if your lower back hurts a bit, endure it and let us do a grand campaign tonight. Ouch ouch ouch. That yamainu fellow beat me up terribly. Hey, Biruma, go drag out a donkey from somewhere. And find and bring both a taiko and gongs. For this is the critical juncture of whether we ascend to heaven or plunge into hell.'

Biruma: 'Since the magician Michihiko has been locked away inside the rock cave and two sentries have been assigned to him, there is no way he should be returning to the mansion anytime soon. Omasama-san, have you perhaps lost your mind because you were shaken up by the yamainu?'

Wakkusu: 'Eh, don't talk nonsense, would I lose my mind over something like that? Come, if matters are delayed it will be a grave emergency, quickly, quickly!'

Urging him on thus, Biruma dashes off in a flash, straddles a donkey while leading another, helps Wakkusu mount, and while beating a small drum and surigane, dashes toward the four-way intersection of Miyamachi,

tonton chinchin tonton chinchin

and made a din in the middle of the night like a candy seller. Mokubei, Hachibei, and Tagosaku are also awakened from their dreams by this noise and rush out into the street in a panic. Wakkusu raises his voice loudly from atop the horse,

Wakkusu: 'Hey hey, people of Miyamachi-dono, once again the magician of Ananai-kyō has appeared at our mansion. Come, wake up every household, and those who can stand on their shins and loins, follow me and march upon the front gate of the mansion; if you are late it will be a grave emergency, the magician will steal our treasures as he did before, and in the end he will even take our inochi. The time to destroy the demons is right now, kefu!'

he shouted. One after another, panicked gossips spread the word, and in the blink of an eye two or three hundred men and women of all ages gathered at the four-way intersection.

Biruma, standing at the head while beating a rhythm from atop his horse,

Wakkusu: 'Out, out, out, out, the demon has appeared At the divine mansion of Mount Terumon In the inner chamber of Okuniwake Strike, strike, strike, strike now! Following the song and beat, the multitude of men and women bathed in the moonlight surged toward the front gate of the mansion. Hearing this commotion, Princess Okuni and Michihiko grew suspicious; Michihiko left Princess Okuni to tend to the sick person, and rushed out the gate all by himself. The moment Wakkusu sees Michihiko's figure,

Wakkusu: 'Hey hey, all of you, that fellow who has appeared right there is the enemy of the townspeople, the great thief and magician, do not let him escape!'

he commanded, and the ignorant men and women, like ants gathering upon an earthworm, struck him from all directions with pieces of wood, gathered together and bound him tightly, and with one accord shouting 'Washio washio!' threw him right into the rapids of the Umburaku River with a splash. Ah, what will become of Michihiko's fate?
