Chapter 20: 犬闘 [1470]

犬闘〔1470〕

§ 1

The sky was overcast, and patches of Nōtan clouds and Unkuwai clouds covered the Manten sky in places, revealing the skin of the Aokumo through a few breaks in the clouds in about seven or eight spots. There was no wind, the rustling of the tree leaves had ceased, and sweat oozed out over one's entire body. A humid Kūki filled the Tenchi.

The Akusuikuwaiin gathered in groups of three over there and five over here, blooming with tales of resentment about being bitten by dogs, their eyes upturned and blue veins standing out on their foreheads, and among them were some who gnashed their teeth and raged in fury. In their excessive irritation, all of them gulped down liquor to drown their Kutsū, raising their voices in barbaric tones like a Dora, and clamored in a Jibaujikiteki manner.

It was Wax who came along then, with white Hautai wrapped around his arms, straddling a Roba and followed by Oaks and Burma. Wax stood at the Jifujigaitō, and as if a pilgrim of Seiguwanji were beating a thin Mamedaiko, he appeared while playing it along with a Surigane, chanting, 'Don-don, don-don-don, chanchiki chanchiki chanchikichin,' and while standing straight up on the horse, he shouted loudly:

Wax: 'A great event has suddenly occurred. Needless to say for the Akusuikuwaiin, everyone who can stand on their own two feet must gather at Jongno.'

As he shouted this, the enraged old and young gathered in the blink of an eye by the hundreds, raising war cries of 'Wai, wai,' and clamoring to hear the speech of Wax. Oaks stopped playing the drum, stood upright on the horse, waved his arms left and right, opened his mouth wide, and began to deliver a street oration.

Oaks: 'Everyone, I am the head gatekeeper of the shrine and the future steward, Oaks. As you can see, Wax-sensei, who has appeared here, had his arm bitten by a mad dog used by the magician of the San-go-kyo, and we too have had our legs bitten through in this manner, suffering terrible fates. Nay, is it not true that not only we, but the majority of the Akusuikuwaiin have all been wounded by that mad dog? How can we, the chosen people of God who reside in the sacred land of Mount Terumon, let this pass unquestioned? Needless to say for the Akusuikuwaiin, as well as the members of the Suiheikai and the old and young of this town, do you not have the intention to stake your lives to protect this sacred land, crowd the mansion, capture the magician Mitsuhiko and the seeker Kyūdō-koji, beat the mad dog to death, and purify the sacred land? It is truly a matter of extreme regret. If this were a person of no consequence, that would be equivalent to wood or stone. Humans are animals of emotion. I pray that everyone will demonstrate the spirit of Brahman and follow us to support this grand undertaking.'

From among the Akusuikuwaiin, a man who was dead drunk appeared while wearing bandages, and said:

Man: 'Hey, gatekeeper, Oaks, thank you for your hard work. Even if you do not say such things, if I, Tonku-sama, do not take revenge even by myself, the face of the Akusuikuwaiin will not be saved. However, please wait for this attack until my legs heal for a while. I will finish them off with the strength of this Tonku alone. It is truly a bad state of affairs; how could one remain silent about this?'

From among the crowd, they shouted and cursed one after another:

'That is right, that is right. There must be no delay. Strike them down, strike them down.'

Wax, watching for the right moment, unwrapped his bandages to reveal his wounds, and said:

Wax: 'Everyone, I have met with such a miserable fate because of the magician. That I have engaged in self-sacrificial activities and suffered such injuries is all for the sake of you all. As a leader of the Akusuikuwaiin, even if I must cast away my precious life, I must sacrifice myself for your sake to crush the weak and help the strong. Come, if we are late, the enemy might escape, so please follow this Wax, and those who can stand on their feet, please push into the mansion and capture the enemy. As for those who are injured, it cannot be helped, so you may rest. However, the way a human holds their heart is essential; if you would observe my painstaking efforts, as I have managed to straddle a Roba and carry out activities while enduring the intense pain of having my arms bitten and my legs bitten like this, you should not have to worry about minor injuries. In this one-in-a-thousand chance, I pray for a great activity of united hearts and combined efforts.'

§ 2

Having finished speaking with such cunning words, the awatemono yajiuma each carried a konbō, or brandished a takeyari or chiyōken, and under the shiki of Wax, they advanced like the tide toward the gate of the mansion, keeping their ranks straight, raising their flags, beating their taiko, and sounding their surigane. Wax stood at the front and began to sing a shingunka.

Dōn dōn dōn Chiyanchiki chiyanchiki chiyanchikichin Wax: 'Everyone, everyone, tighten your Hachimaki firmly, tighten your obi, Tie the strings of your waraji well, Carry your emono in each hand, And those fellows who plot to overturn The seijiyō of Mount Terumon, Dōn dōn dōn Chiyanchiki chiyanchiki chiyanchikichin The mahafuzukahi of Ananai-kyō, Michihiko and the Kiudaukoji, And the konusuto who follow them, Together with the kiyōken Smart, Without leaving even one of them behind, Stake your inochi and face them, And send them to their ōjiyō, Dōn dōn dōn Chiyanchiki chiyanchiki chiyanchikichin For the heart is the daiichi in man. Rush forward against the weak, Flee away from the strong, If the mukafu is strong, Retreat for a while as per your yotei, Then prepare your battle equipment again, And advance with renewed strength, Dōn dōn dōn Chiyanchiki chiyanchiki chiyanchikichin All tactics of war, Like the ebb and flow of the tide, Are to exhaust the secret arts of Infinite change to face the enemy, That is what is called true strategy. Even if it takes three years, If we do not clear this grudge, The Akueikai and the Suiheikai, For the people living in the sacred land, The men will lose all face, And the women will have their faces ruined, Becoming a disgrace to heaven and earth, To be laughed at for generations to come, Now is the time to prevent that.' Dondoko dondoko dokodokodon Chiyanchiki chiyanchiki chiyanchikichin

As the taiko and surigane rang out busily, they quickened their pace and suddenly approached the gate, where Wax looked back and began to deliver another speech from his horse.

Wax: 'The mahafuzukahi of Ananai-kyō, the wicked and lawless Michihiko, the fallen priest Kiudaukoji, and the konusuto Heru, those three, are right here in this mansion, together with the kiyōken Smart. The rise and fall of the nation hangs in the balance. You warriors, all of you, with the force of a lion, break into this mansion which lacks military defense from both front and rear, first capture the wicked ones, take prisoner the Oguniwake-fūfu whose souls were stolen by the mahafuzukahi, and the two hime who are transformations of evil foxes, tie them to the laundry pole in this riding ground, and punish them. If you delay, there is a fear they will escape. Quickly, quickly!'

When he gave the order, the impatient crowd raised a war cry of 'Watsu-watsu' and rushed toward the gate like an avalanche, their momentum becoming terrifying, a scene like raging waves crashing against the shore. Smart, who had appeared from nowhere, began to growl as if this were his last stand, emitting a voice that shook even the mountains. Even the crowd and Wax were daunted by this growl and could not advance, and in that moment, Smart leaped into the crowd like an arrow, running about in all directions, targeting only the members of the Akueikai and biting them down one after another. The crowd was shocked and panicked, losing their color, and each fled for their inochi in their own way. Wax, followed by Ōkusu, Biruma, and Eru, whipped his donkey and fled at full speed toward his own mansion.
