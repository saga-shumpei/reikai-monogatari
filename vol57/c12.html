Chapter 12: 三狂 [1462]

三狂〔1462〕

§ 1

Michihiko, together with Sharu, proceeds while singing a Sendenkan in a low voice, advancing under the impression of passing through a road in Genkai without realizing it is the Yachimatakaidau.

At the Yachimata Sekishiyo, two guards—one with a red face and one with a white face—are standing by sternly.

Looking, a man was being questioned about something by the red-faced guard.

Akazura: 'What is your seimei?'

Magafuyu: 'Yes, I am called Waniguchimagafuyu.'

Akazura: 'Do you hold any shinkau?'

Magafuyu: 'Well, I do not have any particular shinkau to speak of, but I dabble a little in the three teachings of Shinjiyubutsu.'

Akazura: 'Among those, which kyō suited your heart best, or rather, which did you think you thoroughly understood?'

Magafuyu: 'Well, at first I studied Butsukyō with all my might.

Having done so, since there is nowhere to rely upon, I stopped.

In short, Butsukyō is like a lily bulb; if you peel the skin layer by layer and advance deep inside, nothing at all is left; so-called Butsukyō is nothingness, I think.

It just lines up a lot of fine-sounding phrases, and it looks just like an advertisement for a pharmacy.

If it were an advertisement for patent medicine, it would have a polite set of instructions attached: "This medicine should be used for stomachaches, hernia, or lung disease; also take so many times a day, drink with hot water, drink with water, it is good before meals, it is good after meals, adults take so many pills, children take so many pills, below such and such an age take so many pills." But the scriptures of Butsukyō only write that if you invoke Kannon you escape evil and calamity, or if you invoke Amida you are sent to Gokuraku, and because the eighty-four thousand scrolls of scriptures show their usage nowhere at all, I thought it was no good.'

Akazura: 'You throw away the feeling of respect and refuge toward Butsukyō, which leaked out the news of the spiritual world, and because you presumptuously approach it saying you study it and so forth, you cannot grasp anything.

Even if you study for ten thousand years based on the laws of the material world, the distant and subtle truth of the spiritual world has no reason to be resolved.

If you put aside your intellect for a while, make your will exclusive, and study, the light of God's love, Buddha's goodness, and faith and truth will shine forth.

That you feel Butsukyō is worthless is because your so-called spirit is worthless.

It is because the pure figure of the Buddha is not reflected in your cloudy mirror.'

Magafuyu: 'If I hear it put that way, perhaps it may be so, but it is somehow very difficult to understand.'

Akazura: 'It is a mistake to try to understand the spirit of the Buddha as a human limit.

The Buddha is compassion itself; once you understand the meaning of supreme benevolence and supreme love, all the sutras are understood.'

Magafuyu: 'Ah, is that so?

I had a terribly wrong idea.

From now on I will try doing a study.'

Akazura: 'No good.

For the second time, you say study, study as if it were a habit of speech, but the study you speak of is roasting a dog.

Barking fiercely is all it is good for.

You had better stop.

In such a psychological state, you can never truly comprehend the mind of Buddha.

Then what did you believe next?'

Magafuyu: 'Well, I do not particularly believe, but I certainly studied the Bible.'

Akazura: 'The Old Testament or the New?'

Magafuyu: 'Of course it is the Old Testament.'

Akazura: 'Did you gain anything from it?'

Magafuyu: 'Well, there is neither a place to sell nor a place to buy.

This too, as expected, did not suit my nature, and while wandering about in a fog of five leagues, upon the recommendation of a certain person I entered Ananaikyō, and when I studied it fairly seriously, somehow the words and deeds of Hensei-no-joshi did not sit well with me, so I cast it aside like a worn-out shoe and withdrew, and now I have entered a life of repentance.'

Akazura: 'Did you not even go so far as to copy the Reikai Monogatari?

While receiving direct teachings, not to understand—what a blind person you still are.

It is because you still approach it with a research-like attitude.

While copying the precious teachings of God, you were just used like a mere machine.

And did you have any point that you believed?'

赤『その方は霊界物語の筆写迄やつたぢやないか。直接に教示を受け乍ら、分らぬとは扨ても困つた盲だな。矢張研究的態度を以てかかつて居るからだ。結構な神の教を筆写し乍ら、ホンの機械に使はれたやうなものだ。さうして幾分か信ずる処があつたのか』

Magafuyu: 'Yes, I did believe in the Niyoshi side to some extent, but I considered this to be a mere half-baked swindle. More than that, I placed weight on the Shinyu of the Henjiyōnanshi, but upon seeing the original text, I was put off by the extreme clumsiness of the writing, and my faith gradually faded away.'

Aka: 'You are a fool. The Oshihe of Kami does not depend on the Kausetsu of the writing. Writing is a matter of Shieu; you must savor the Mitsui contained within the Kotoba. You have eyes but they are Dōzen to a Fushiana, ears but they are Dōzen to a Kikurage, a tongue but it is Dōzen to a kazunoko, a nose but it is Dōzen to a Fushikobuta; with such things, do you have the Shikaku to say whether the Ananai-kyō is good or bad, or what the Nanshi or Niyoshi are? You have become quite Manshin-filled.'

Magafuyu: 'I am not particularly Manshin-filled. If one intends to propagate to the people of the Sekai, faith is faith, but one must conduct thorough research; unless one investigates to the point of saying it is safe to apply to society, it will pour poison into society. In other words, it is faithful research for the sake of society.'

Aka: 'You are hopeless because you have not yet shed your Gashū-Gaken. You are what is called an Iken-gedō, Shizen-gedō, or Danken-gedō. With such an attitude, the Lord God will never let you realize the truth. The Lord God lets the foolish, the weak, and the small preach the path of sincerity. He will never show the Mitsui to a Manshin-filled person who adopts a research-oriented attitude. You have graduated from university and think of yourself as a scholar of some standing, but that learning has not the value of a single mon at the Yachimata or in hell. No, it is rather an obstacle and a cause of suffering. Your parents have also done a troublesome thing.'

Magafuyu: 'Though you are a gatekeeper, you speak condescendingly to a man of letters, but is it not outrageous to reject learning in an age of civilization where things progress day by day? If the citizens were all unlearned, would they not all be snatched away by other civilized nations? In order to plan for the development of human culture and the promotion of national prestige, it is impossible without learning at the university level. You people have only graduated from elementary school, so you are not thorough in the affairs of the world. That is why you are doing the work of a gatekeeper, a substitute for a police officer. You will never be able to stand against my arguments. I do not know what kind of qualifications you have to investigate, but please call someone more solid who can understand. Because the stages of knowledge are different, you would not understand.'

Aka: 'Do not talk like a Baka; this place is the Yachimata of the Reikai. It is a place where doctors and bachelors all come. How could this gatekeeper serve here if he were unlearned? You people are those who have let your mind and body be dissolved by the worthless theories of the natural world, who take falsehood as truth, and who consider the system of survival of the fittest and the law of the jungle as the best method; therefore, you will never understand the profound secrets of truth. When someone like you comes to the Reikai, you speak incomprehensible logic and defile the spirits, so I shall strip away all the worthless academic knowledge you have researched in the Genkai here.'

Magafuyu: 'Hey, Aka-sama, are you insane, or are you just drunk? What do you mean by saying this is the Yachimata of the Reikai? How can there be a Reikai, a Yachimata, or a hell? Once a human leaves descendants and dies, that is the end of it. You should cultivate a little philosophical knowledge. You will end up a dropout of society and eventually be unable to even serve as a gatekeeper.'

Aka: 'Do you think that being a gatekeeper is that lowly? How about cleaning toilets or cleaning garbage dumps? Are those still more noble?'

Magafuyu: 'That is correct; to gladly do what others dislike with a heart of great mercy and compassion is the reason for improving human character. If there were no people to clean toilets or clean up garbage, would the world not become a muddy, turbid world of urine, feces, and dust? That is why we are doing the service of the Kami, Ibukido-nushi. There can be no work as sacred as making dirty things beautiful. I do not think of it as lowly work or a dirty trade.'

Aka: 'Ah, is that so? Then I shall send you to the place you love most. In Jigoku, there are plenty of Gomisuteba and mounds of Taifun. The Maujiya from Shiyaba come there and sniff around with joy, just like flies gathering on Funiku. It would be best for you to go to the place that corresponds to your Mitamasauō, according to the love of your Shiyoshiyu when you were in Gense. Rather than serving all the Kami in their Goyō and bathing in Mugen Kuwanki in a Tengoku where there is neither night nor winter, you would surely be content to go to the Shiuki-filled Jigokudau. Come now, there is no need for Enriyo; you had best be off at once.'

Magafuyu: 'Well now, does that mean this is, after all, the Reikai?'

Aka: 'That is a certainty. What kind of Maujiya is it that cannot tell the difference between Reikai and Genkai? That is why you are called blind in heart.'

Magafuyu: 'If that is the case, would you please send me to Tengoku?'

Aka: 'Well, unless you pass through a certain Jikan here, a soul as stained as yours cannot be sent directly to Tengoku. First, you must completely strip away your Guwaibuteki elements. You must cast off all the lingering attachments you have accumulated in Gense—speaking things you did not mean in your heart, using flattery, debasing your body, and various other things—and enter the second, internal state to cross the gateway of internal life. The internal refers to your will and thoughts. If, indeed, that will is good and true, you will be able to ascend to Tengoku. However, once you enter the internal state, you will hear the teachings of the angels; if those teachings are able to enter your ears, you possess the qualifications to go to Tengoku, but if they cannot enter your ears no matter what, you are bound for Jigoku. This is called the third state, the time when the destination of the spirit is decided.'

Magafuyu: 'Hey, that is quite difficult, is it not? So, after all, both Tengoku and Jigoku do exist.'

As they were speaking, Takahime raised her hoarse voice and said,

Takahime: 'Hey, Mitsuhiko, Sharu, wait, wait! I have something I want to say.'

She came running while dragging her obi, which had become like dried kelp, and said,

Takahime: 'Hey, Sharu, you ungrateful wretch! While I was being knocked down by this Mitsuhiko's wickedness and suffering, you spoke ill of me and ran away, did you not? Hey, hey, Officer-sama, this fellow is a villain. Although it is a matter for the Giritenjō to punish directly, since the Goyō of the Kamikai is busy, I leave it to you, so please punish him severely and take him to task.'

Aka: 'Hey, are you not Takahime? Are you still making a racket even after coming to the Reikai? Why don't you leave the Guwaibuteki state already? It has been a year, and yet you are such a stubborn one.'

Takahime: 'Hen, you can certainly say that. Whether it has been a year or two, it is none of your business. You even hid Mokusuke-sama the other day; you have no sense of judgment, but because I am the Ikimiya of the great Dai-Miroku-sama of great mercy and compassion, I am letting it slide. If you keep grumbling, this Ikimiya will not stand for it.'

Aka: 'Shiro-sama, this old woman is becoming such a nuisance, so please push her out somewhere.'

Takahime: 'Hen, am I a nuisance? Well, I suppose that is so. The words of the true Kami must strike the ears of the wicked quite painfully. I am sorry, but for the sake of saving all the people of the world, I will say as much as I must, even if it hurts your ears a little. Do you know the Inen of Miroku-sama? Do you understand the Ichirin no Shikumi? Eh, I suppose you do not. Hen, you should not act so important when you do not even understand the Ichirin no Shikumi.'

Aka: 'Shiro-sama, please send her away somewhere quickly.'

Shiro: 'Hey, hey, Takahime-sama, this is Yatsumata, so please go somewhere else quickly. You are interfering with my duties.'

Takahime: 'Kōriya, Shirogitsune, are you going to listen to what Akagitsune says and try to cast out this Hinodewake? What a wicked intention that is. Try to think about it carefully. There is not a single place between heaven and earth that is not under the care of Miroku-sama. Do you know the grace of the soil, the water, and the fire? How do you perceive the great Dai-Miroku-sama of the Sokotsu-iwane who holds the root of all that? Truly, there is no one as troublesome as the blind. Hey, I thought there was a strange man standing there from before, but you are Kikuishihiko of the dangerous sect, are you not? I apologize for earlier; you certainly knocked me down. Hey, hey, Aka and Shiro, the Hinodewake commands you. Since this Kikuishihiko is the villain who knocked down this Ikimiya, make him suffer a severe punishment. I am giving you strict orders.'

§ 4

The white Shiyuei, having no other choice, swept out toward Sharu and Takahime with a Shiyurobauki. The two, startled, fled toward the south as if turning clouds into mist.

Michihiko: 'Excuse me, Monbansama, is this place the actual Reikai?'

Aka: 'Yes, that is correct. The moment you were bound by the Warumono and thrown into the Anburakku River, you lost consciousness, and so your Seirei has wandered here. Sumāto, a Giken who is a Keshin of Kami, leaped into the river at Yaniha, dragged your Shigai up to the bank, untied your bonds, and is now doing his utmost to provide Kaihau to your Nikutai. Sumāto will come to fetch you before long, so you should return together. It is not yet time for you to come here. Furthermore, as Warumono are Habiko on Mount Terumon, you must proceed with Jiubun Chiui.'

Michihiko: 'Now that you say so, it faintly comes back to my memory. So I did indeed drown, did I not? This place called Reikai is not the least bit different from the Genkai. One thing that is strange is that I had heard Takahime-sama had lost her life, yet she is behaving in such a wildly eccentric manner; is she also in the Reikai?'

Aka: 'She still has about thirty years of life remaining in the Genkai, but because she causes too much interference there, Tokiokishi-kami-sama appeared and, having requested it of Ibukido-nushi-no-Ōkami, has released her into the Chūyūkai for a period of three years. After three years, she will surely possess another Nikutai and be active in the Genkai once again. Since it would be unbearable if she went to the Genkai with her current state of mind, the plan is to have her undergo Jiubun training during the remaining two years before returning her to the Genkai.'

Michihiko: 'Indeed, everything that Kami-sama does is so thoroughly well-considered. However, after three years, the Nikutai of Takahime will surely be useless by then.'

Aka: 'There is a Nikutai whose life will end after three years and come to the Reikai, so the arrangement is to have the Seirei of Takahime dwell in that Nikutai and have her active in the Genkai for the remaining thirty years.'

Michihiko: 'Ah, is that so? So in three years' time, she will possess someone's Nikutai and carry out her eccentric proselytizing; that is troublesome, is it not?'

Aka: 'One year has already passed, so there are only two years left. Because we must somehow improve that self-attachment and self-view during these two years, we are having a very difficult time even in the Reikai. She is currently living alone in a small house built at the foot of a rocky mountain, but well, as long as she lives alone, she does not cause much harm, so Ōkami-sama is also overlooking it. It is troublesome because even when an Enzeru goes to her, she only talks back and will not listen. It is truly hopeless when a human Seirei becomes so hardened by self-attachment.'

Just as they were speaking, a fierce dog came leaping through the air from the south, crying out loudly two or three times, 'Woo-woo, uwatsu, uwatsu.' Startled by this voice, he looked around to find that the scene of the crossroads had vanished without a trace, and he was lying on the green grass of the Anburakku River bank. Beside him, the fierce dog Sumāto was sitting politely, looking at Michihiko's face with joy and wagging his tail. Looking toward Mount Terumon, black smoke was rising in thick clouds, sealing off the sky like a dark cloud. The moon appeared to be hiding and emerging amidst the black smoke, looking as if it were running swiftly.
