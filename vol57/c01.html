Chapter 1: Great Mountain [1451]

大山〔1451〕

§ 1

Rising from the depths of Konrinnaraku, piercing through Fūrin, Suirin, and Chirin, the great peak of Mt. Hauki, known as Daisen, is the keystone of the great land of Nihon.

Like a white fan hanging upside down in the sky, the Nihonkai sinks into the distance, while Ōkuronushi's Magatsumi—possessed by Yamataorochi—occupied the upper reaches of the Kahakami River, raising wind and rain, causing floods, and year after year afflicting Inadahime, who had grown up in Sada and Nagata, attempting to take human lives with lamentation.

In the spring of the twelfth year of Taishō, the year of Kinoe-i, in the month of Kisaragi, upon the moonlit night-shining Yomigahama, built as everlasting and firm as bedrock in the center of the grove of small pines, is the hot spring inn bestowed by the gods: the second-floor room of the Hamaya Ryokan.

Lying horizontally as usual, Mizuki writes with a running fountain pen facing the eastern window, striving not to leak a single word of the trials and tribulations of Mikoguniwake—who lives in the mountain mansion where the morning sun glitters upon Telmon, on the border of Persia and the Land of the Moon, chronicling the ninth volume of Shin-Zen-Bi-Ai (Truth, Goodness, Beauty, and Love), so that his achievements may endure until the end of three thousand years—while the howling Yomi sea breeze blows and the curtains flap back and forth like a beating rhythm, driving the Kototama chariot onward.

Ah, Kannagara kamugami-kamugami, blessing and favoring us with your spirits, you have completely and minutely narrated the fifty-seventh story, and I reverently pray before the great deity's presence that you cause it to become a household souvenir of the Ayano Seichi. Whether the morning sun shines or is cloudy, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even should the great earth sink, the principles of the teachings of Makoto-Hitotsu (Single Truth) and Azuma (Izumo/Three-Five) must be written down as the divine law; meanwhile, affected by the hot spring waters, Mizuki's stomach rumbles with diarrhea, yet he mixes in endless sophistries and blows a conch shell picked up on the beach ceaselessly and without end.

三五教は大神の直接内流を受け、愛の善と、信の真をもつて唯一の教理となし、智愛勇親の四魂を活用させ、善の為に善を行ひ、用の為に用を勤め、真の為に真を励む。故に其言行心は常に神に向ひ、神と共にあり、所謂神の生宮にして天地経綸の主宰者たるの実を挙げ、生き乍ら天国に籍を置き、恰も黄金時代の天人の如く、神の意志其儘を地上の蒼生に宣伝し実行し、以て衆生一切を済度するをもつて唯一の務めとして居たのである。故にバラモン教ウラル教其他数多の教派の如く、自愛又は世間愛に堕して知らず識らずに神に背き、虚偽を真理と信じ、悪を善と誤解するが如き行動は取らなかつたのである。神より来れる愛及び善並に信真の光に浴し、惟神の儘に其実を示すが故に、麻柱の教と神から称へられたのである。自愛及び世間愛に堕落せる教は所謂外道である。外道とは天地惟神の大道に外れたる教を云ふ。これ皆邪神界に精霊を蹂躙され、知らず知らずに地獄界及び兇党界に堕落したものである。外道には九十五の種類があつて、其重なるものは、カビラ・マハールシといふ。このカビラ・マハールシは、即ち大黒主の事であり、三五教の真善美の言霊に追ひ捲られて自転倒島の要と湧出したる伯耆の国のマハールシ（大山）に八岐大蛇の霊と共に割拠し、六師外道と云つて外道の中にても最も勝れたる悪魔を引き率れ天下を攪乱し、遂に素盞嗚尊のために言向け和されたのである。六師外道とは、ブランナーカーシャバ、マスカリー・ガーシャリーブトラ、サンジャイーヴィ・ラチャーブトラ、アザタケー・シャカムバラ、カクダカー・トヤーヤナ、ニルケラントー・ヂニヤー・ヂブトラの六大外道である。此外道は古今東西の区別なく今日と雖も尚天下を横行濶歩し、暴威を逞しうして居るのである。

§ 2

Purānakkāsyapa is like the materialist who belittles the paths of kunshin, fushi, fūfu, kiyōdai, and hōiu, ignores everything in the genkai, regards the struggle for existence and the survival of the fittest as the true meaning of jinsei, vigorously advocates the doctrine of making light of death and heavy of life, asserts that all things in uchiu are entirely void and nothingness, and that the nikutai of ningen vanish away like smoke the moment they shimetsu, with reikon and the like after death never remaining at all. If indeed there were reikon after death, for example, if one were to burn taugarashi to ashes and still after that the shinmi of taugarashi remained, it would certainly not exist; considering this, to argue about the life of ningen after death is the height of ugu and the very limit of meimau, thus passing such a danan.

Maskarī Gāśalīputra is the hein gedō who asserts that neither the suffering nor the joy of all sentient beings depends in any way upon the causal acts of ningen, and that joy and suffering both come naturally in every case. For example, when one sows a single seed here, the seed that is sown in fertile soil in a sunny place flourishes better than others, grows lush leaves, blooms beautiful flowers, bears beautiful fruits, and is loved by people; in contrast to this, when the very same seed is sown in barren soil or in a dark, shady place, it cannot receive sufficient sunlight, its growth is poor, its flowers are small, and it does not bear satisfactory fruit. Yet there is no good or evil whatsoever in that seed; it is the same seed taken from the same tree, and furthermore, that seed possesses neither good acts nor evil acts. It is simply due to the place where it is sown—that is, the environment—that one is either bathed in joy or immersed in suffering. Therefore, if the place where ningen is sown is bad, no matter how much one strives, one cannot surpass the seed sown in a good place. Therefore, there is no causal act whatsoever in the joy and suffering of ningen; thus he asserts, and this is also called the natural gedō.

Sanjāyi Belaṭṭhiputra is one who asserts that ningen never need to undergo any ascetic practices whatsoever. Just as looking at the plants and trees of heaven and earth, flowers naturally bloom when spring comes, fruits naturally form when autumn comes, and leaves naturally scatter when winter comes, so when the eighty thousand kalpas arrive, the suffering of ningen naturally exhausts itself and the Way is attained. In short, it is a gedō of desperation and Kannagara poisoning, and is made a kind of hein gedō. It seems that quite a few people of this kind are mixed into the Anagama-kyō of the twentieth century.

Ajātaśatru Śāmbara: this gedō makes it so that in the present world, no matter what, if one only subjects oneself to suffering, one will certainly be born in heaven in a future life, be bathed in infinite joy, be given food and drink of a hundred flavors, and send an eternal and infinite life of peace in glory, splendor, and prosperity; without performing the undertakings that ought to be carried out as ningen, hiding one's body in deep mountains and secluded valleys, cutting off fire, avoiding grains, chewing pine needles, digging up yams, inhaling air, going completely naked and barefoot in the dead of winter to endure the cold, being bitten by mosquitoes in the summer to experience every kind of suffering, and seeking to obtain the reward of that suffering in the world to come—this is the so-called ascetic gedō. Today this gedō also appears quite a bit here and there. And people of the present, who are dark to the truth, point to such an ascetic gedō, regard him as a true person, revere him as a saint, and respect him like gods and Buddhas. People who respect such an ascetic gedō are also lesser gedō who, by the principle of like seeking like, unconsciously register themselves in the path of hell.

Kakuda Kātyāyana: this gedō vigorously asserts opinions regarding various miscellaneous, selfish, formal, natural, and worldly loves, such as panlogism, spiritualistic panbasis, panpsychism, atheism, pluralism, monism, socialism, anarchism, nihilism, or communism, without casting a single glance at the formless spirit world, and while ignoring the spirit world and gods and Buddhas, yet being unable to be thorough even in the present world, and while neglecting the spirit world, at times acknowledging the existence of the spirit world a little bit, at times clinging only to the present world, losing the destination of the spirit, being a heterodox gedō of two unpersevering paths.

Nirukerantō, Jiniyā, Jibutra—this Gedau claims that human joy and suffering are predetermined from the origin by innen. For example, it argues that how the Sanpeki star influences one, how the Kiushi star influences one, whether one was born in the year of the rat or the year of the ox, or whether the mitama's innen is good or bad, thus falling into shiyukumeisetsu as a shiyukumeigedau. Such a shiyukumeigedau, no matter how much it may worship Shinbutsu, cannot transform its own fixed unmei. There is nothing to do other than resign oneself to everything as unmei and sacrifice oneself to that path. The tadpole resembles a catfish, but once it grows a little larger, hands and feet sprout and it ends up becoming a frog. It can by no means become a catfish. Therefore, even if someone with bad innen believes in Kami or exhausts sincerity, it is unlikely they will ever become truly magnificent. Asserting that everything is the result of zense innen and shiyōrai, it is a mumiyōankoku jiyōkengedau. Such gedaus are, in every case, views other than Kami or Buddha, each establishing its own school of thought. They claim that nothing can be achieved without spiritual research grounded in science, or they dislike and detest hoisting the names of Shinbutsu, attaching arbitrary names such as Taireidō, Nitōen, or various societies, attempting to research the spiritual world as so-called jiyōkengedaus. In the contemporary age, this gedau is most rampant; rather than invoking the names of Shinbutsu, it masks itself under scientific names such as Theosophy, divine spirit research, or spiritual study association, disguising itself as the attitude of civilized people. Gathering together like ants swarming to sweetness, it is a gedau that attempts to peer into the stars beyond the clouds. Such Nirukerantō, Jiniyā, and Jibutra occasionally arise even from within the Hinomoto teachings. All of them fall into such a gedau, unawares, out of arrogance born of self-importance.

By way of prelude, I will briefly explain the twelve innen. For human beings, there are:

1. Avidyā (ignorance)

2. Saṃskāra (formations)

3. Vijñāna (consciousness)

4. Nāmarūpa (name and form)

5. Ṣaḍāyatana (six sense bases)

6. Sparśa (contact)

7. Vedanā (feeling)

8. Taṇhā (craving)

9. Upādāna (clinging)

10. Bhava (becoming)

11. Jāti (birth)

12. Jarāmaraṇa (old age and death)

These constitute the twelve innen.

Ignorance refers to all past earthly desires. Formations refer to the creation of past earthly desires. Consciousness refers to the deluded consciousness placed within the mother's body in the present life. As for name and form, name consists of the four aggregates of mind, and form consists of the one aggregate of physicality. The six sense bases refer to the formation of the six roots within the mother's body. Contact refers to the state experienced by the age of three or four where one touches the root of external dusts. Craving refers to the condition from the age of five or six up to twelve or thirteen where one strongly receives external dust and gives rise to the discrimination of liking and disliking. Craving refers to the state from fourteen or fifteen to eighteen or nineteen where one gives rise to thoughts of coveting and loving external dust. Clinging refers to the condition after the age of twenty where one produces an even stronger sentiment of attachment to external dust. Becoming refers to the creation and accumulation of various karmic causes that will invite the fruits of the future three realms of existence. Birth refers to coming into existence within the future six paths or crossroads. Old age and death refer to the body of future craving and life finally decaying and breaking down. These twelve innen are things that human beings cannot possibly avoid. However, it is by passing through the barrier gates of these twelve innen that a human being for the first time enters the life of Kami, enters the true life of eternal infinity, and should lead a celestial human existence. Nevertheless, many numerous human beings have their minds and bodies clouded by the ninety-five kinds of gedau, promptly advance into the path of hell, turn their backs on Kami who is the Great Primal Spirit of the universe, and end up tasting infinite suffering. Therefore, Kami descended the Two Spirits of Rigor and Benevolence onto the earth to widely propagate the gospel of the heavenly kingdom, and in order to make every single person without exception a resident of the heavenly kingdom, filled with the Holy Spirit, caused prophets to come. No matter how much one is praised in the present world as a saint, a sage, or a virtuous person, those who are not conversant with the tidings of the spiritual world and who lightly dismiss the grace of Kami have their hearts already turned away from Kami, and thus it is utterly difficult for them to lead the life of the heavenly kingdom. Salvation without a covenant can never be sought. Therefore, Kami previously descended Śākyamuni Tathāgata to show the tidings of the spiritual world to the people of the world, and further descended Christ, Mahomet, and other true persons as prophets to earth, bestowing the covenant to save all people into the heavenly kingdom. Yet the rampant tyranny of the ninety-five kinds of gedau was extreme, to the point that almost no one believed in the covenant of Kami. Hence the world grew ever more dark, ending up as alleys of hungry ghosts, beasts, and asuras. At this, the supremely benevolent and loving Imperial Great Kami, in order to rescue this tragic state, descended the Two Spirits of Rigor and Benevolence to the earth and bestowed the divine covenant upon all people. Ah, yet among all sentient beings sunk within impenetrable darkness, there are few who incline their ears to the compassionate sound of salvation. Truly, when one reflects upon it, it is the height of tragedy. Ah, may the divine spirits bless us peacefully.

十二、老死、　　ヂャラー・マラナ

の十二因縁がある。

無明とは、過去一切の煩悩を云ひ、行とは過去煩悩の造作を云ひ、識とは現世母の体中に托する陰妄の意識を云ふ。名色の名とは心の四蘊であり、色とは形質の一蘊である。六入とは、母の体中にある中に於て六根を成ずるを云ふ。触とは三四才迄に外的の塵埃の根元に触るるを覚ゆる状態を云ふ。愛とは生れて五六才より十二三才迄の間に強く外部の塵埃を受けて、好悪の識別を起すを云ふ。愛とは十四五才より十八九才までの間に外塵を貪り愛する念慮を起すを云ふ。取とは二十才以後一層強く、外塵に執着の念を生ずるを云ふ。有とは、未来三有の果を招くべき種々の業因を造作し、積集するを云ふ。生とは未来六道又は八衢の中に生ずるを云ふ。老死とは未来愛生の身体、又遂に朽壊するを云ふ。この十二因縁はどうしても人間として避くべからざる事である。併し乍ら、此十二因縁の関門を通過して初めて人間は神の生涯に入り、永遠無窮の真の生命に入つて、天人的生治を送るべきものである。然るに総ての多くの人間は九十五種外道のために身心を曇らされ忽ち地獄道に進み入り、宇宙の大元霊たる神に背き、無限の苦を嘗むるに至るものが多い。故に神は、厳瑞二霊を地上に下し天国の福音を普く宣伝せしめ、一人も残らず天国の住民たらしめむと、聖霊を充して予言者に来らせ給ふたのである。如何に現世に於て聖人賢人、有徳者と称へらるる共、霊界の消息に通ぜず、神の恩恵を無みするものは、其心既に神に背けるが故に、到底天国の生涯を送る事は出来難いものである。約束なき救ひは決して求められないものである。故に神は前にシャキャームニ・タダーガタを下して霊界の消息を世人に示し給ひ、又ハリストスやマホメット其他の真人を予言者として地上に下し、万民を天国に救ふ約束を垂れさせられた。されど九十五種外道の跋扈甚だしく、神の約束を信ずるもの殆ど無きに至つた。それ故世は益々暗黒となり、餓鬼、畜生、修羅の巷となつて仕舞つた。茲に至仁至愛なる皇大神は、この惨状を救はむが為に、厳瑞二霊を地上に下し、万民に神約を垂れ給ふたのである。ああされど無明暗黒の中に沈める一切の衆生は救世の慈音に耳を傾くる者は少い。実に思うて見れば悲惨の極みである。ああ惟神霊幸倍坐世。
