Chapter 19: 抱 Moon [1469]

抱月〔1469〕

§ 1

Kiudaukoji strolled through the moonlit garden, guided by Kerinahime.

Kerinahime pointed far to the southwest.

Kerinahime: 'Kiudausama, do you see that object shining white like mist or a mirror in the distance? That is called the Terumon Kosui, a great lake said to be one hundred ri from east to west and two hundred ri from north to south, into which the Anburakku River flows. It is said from ancient times that its depth reaches the Riugūjiyō, and I wish to become as vast, deep, and pure as that lake. Moreover, when the moonlight reflects upon the water, it is a sight beyond description. The old Tokihakakiha pine trees surround the lake like a ring, creating a landscape of white sand and green pines that is truly indescribable. Once you have fully recovered, I would like to guide you there.'

Kiudaukoji: 'Yes, thank you. It must be a place of wonderful scenery.'

Kerinahime: 'Kiudausama, you will love me forever, will you not?'

Kiudaukoji: 'That is a strange thing to ask. I am a monk who conveys the love of the Lord God, not only to all people under heaven but even to the smallest blade of grass; therefore, I wish to bestow the virtue of love and goodness with all my might.'

Kerinahime: 'In this wide world, there must be one person to whom you pour your love in a special way.'

Kiudaukoji: 'The love of God is equal love, which is to say it is universal love, so there is no favoritism in my love. I intend to love everyone with all my strength, from the young and old to the birds, beasts, insects, and fish. If there is partiality in love, love itself is already incomplete.'

Kerinahime: 'Yes, I understand that. However, what is your understanding of love is best?'

Kiudaukoji: 'If I were a colonel of the Brahman army as I was until now, I would have enthusiastically chanted that love is best. But as you see, having become a monk with a shaven head and black robes, wearing the garment of patience, I have not even dreamed of such things as love.'

Kerinahime: 'Thinking of love, the one who loved, Is like a falcon, With powerful wings in the pine forest. I resent you, whom I prayed for, To have the heart of an evergreen pine.'

Kiudaukoji: 'How could I, who walk in awe of the path of the Imperial Gods, Ever let my heart be moved by a woman?'

Kerinahime: 'Kiudausama, will you truly not grant my wish? You are not a missionary like Mitsuhiko-sama, are you? And you are not on a journey bearing an important mission, so do you intend to let this pitiful woman die? Depending on your thoughts, I shall either play in heaven or fall to the bottom of hell. Can you fulfill your duties as a monk if you cast a lovely maiden into hell? I wish to hear your answer. My love is a nine-sun-five-bu blade, a great determination that can pierce even rock.'

Kiudaukoji: 'Ahahaha, Hime-sama, you must not say such joking things. Please stop teasing me.'

Kerinahime: 'They say that which moves is the true heart of man, And those who boast of not moving, Are they but stones or trees? There is such a poem; what do you think of that? The human body is surely not made of stone or wood. If the flame of affection does not burn within the heart, one cannot save the sentient beings. It is surely not your true intention to run only toward theory and maintain a cold attitude.'

Kiudaukoji: 'Ah, what a troublesome situation this has become. Another seed of agony has grown.'

Kerinahime: 'It must be very annoying to be loved by a base and foolish woman. I do not insist that you must have love for me.'

Kiudaukoji: 'Hime-sama, please do not tease me so, for Kiudau is truly troubled. How could I dislike a talented woman such as you? I am still a young man of feeling, after all. However, as I have once entrusted myself to the Lord God, I cannot simply absorb the taste of romance as I please.'

§ 2

Kerinahime: 'Well, Kiudau-sama, you are still caught by some thing, are you not? You cannot claim to have attained Gedatsu with such a state of mind. Moreover, are you not a Biku, and not a Sendenshi, and in the position of being Hanzokuhansei? Is not the path of the Kamisama entirely Kaihauteki? To be able to advance freely and at will along the Tantan Daidau, without being caught by Nanimono, is the path of the Kamisama of infinite Jinji, is it not? One who does not know passion can never be called a true Danshi.'

Kiudaukoji: 'To be attacked from both the Ōte and the Karamete so Tanpeikyu-ly leaves this Marubauzu with no escape route; please, I beg you, look upon me with Ōme and forgive me today.'

Kerinahime: 'Ho ho ho ho, you are a relatively Hikefu person, are you not? How can you perform Shiujiyōsaido with such an attitude? Are you thinking of corrupting the goddess of peace? If you remove the title of Biku, are you not just an ordinary human? The Ōkami-sama uses the art of transformation to perform Shiujiyōsaido, so do you not have the intention to become, for a while, in the state of mind of Kanjizaiten and save a pitiful woman? I say this with all due respect, but are you not imprisoned by the stubborn and narrow-minded thought that your spiritual power will decline if you become involved with a woman?'

Kiudaukoji: 'Since I am a product that my parents crudely manufactured in a single night, the thoughts of the new women of these times do not easily penetrate my head. I am truly an antique that is behind the times. I am a product that will eventually be displayed in a museum of antiquities, ha ha ha ha.'

Kerinahime: 'Oh, how stifling, how irritating! No matter what you say, I must carry out my original intention, or I will not be able to hold my head up before modern women. I cannot bear to smear mud on the face of womanhood. It is not just me alone who has been rejected by you; it is as if the representative of modern women has received an insult, so please be prepared for that.'

She said this with a sense of desperation, exhaling a fierce spirit.

Kiudaukoji: 'Ah, I wonder if this is a dream. When I was in the Brahmin army, I was disliked even by a woman who looked like a dried gourd with eyes and a nose, but now that I have become a Shugensha and thought I had renounced women, to be loved by a woman—something that has not happened since I was born—the world has truly changed, or rather, my circumstances have undergone a cataclysmic change. Oh, it cannot be helped; even if I suffer divine punishment and fall to the land of roots and the land of the bottom, I shall repay your ardent love.'

Kerinahime: 'Kiudau-sama, I shall never let you fall to the land of the bottom. Let us enjoy the pleasures of heaven, where there is no night and no winter, while still in this world, and serve the Ōkami-sama as a harmonious husband and wife; please be at ease. They say that a man's heart is like the autumn sky, so please swear one thing here.'

Kiudaukoji: 'Then I shall swear to the beautiful moon that clears the vast sky.'

Kerinahime: 'The moon has the changes of waxing and waning. I would be troubled if you were to change your mind midway, so please swear upon this Chibiki-iwa in the garden.'

Kiudaukoji: 'Chiyo yachiyo chibiki no iwa no ugoki naku / Kimi o medemu to chikau kefu kana'

Kerinahime: 'Chibiki-iwa osetedomo hikedomo ugokinaki / Wase no kimi to chiyo o chigiramu'

Having finished singing, she grasped Kiudau's hand tightly and shook it strongly two or three times up, down, left, and right. Kiudau also took the princess's hand, pressed their cheeks together, and sealed their bond for a thousand generations. For a while, the two stood upright in the shade of a pine tree, holding hands, their hearts rippling in silence. Hel, to relieve his boredom, appeared on the scene, strolling along while gazing at the moon.

Hel: 'Oh, my dear couple, I offer my congratulations.'

Kerinahime: 'Oh, was that you, Hel-sama? The moon is beautiful, so I was strolling with Kiudau-sama.'

Hel: 'Please, as it is a moonlit night every evening, do enjoy yourselves. I was being considerate and keeping my distance, ha ha ha ha.'

Kiudaukoji: '......'

Kerinahime: 'Ho ho ho ho, the moon is smiling at us as if he finds it amusing.'

Hel: 'You were also smiling as if you were happy, were you not?'
