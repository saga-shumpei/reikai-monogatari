Chapter 15: 化地蔵 [689]

化地蔵〔689〕

§ 1

One sect of Brahmanism, having established the Alps-kyō and built a den on Mount Takaharuyama, the Onibaba Takayorihime was committing evil deeds. To subdue and save her, the two, Takahime and Kurohime, whose names are renowned in Ananai-kyō, boarded the bird-like Ihakusubune. In high spirits, they ascended into the mid-heaven, pushing aside and parting the clouds. The two landed safely at the foot of Mount Takaharuyama, climbed the Munatsukizaka where golden grasses grew thick, and rested before a shrine in the forest of Ama where stone caves were lined up. While they were brandishing the heavenly Nuboko and in the midst of a Kototamasen, they were lured by the Magatsu, whom Takayorihime relied upon as her own arms, and were dragged into the stone cave. As no Tayori has been heard of them since, Kotoyoriwake, with the divine words of the God, together with the messengers of the gods, Ryūkokuwake and Tamajiwake, crossed over distant mountain paths. At last, they reached Lake Tsuda here. Ryūkokuwake advanced along the northern path, while Tamajiwake proceeded by crossing the lake waters. Kuniyoriwake, a missionary, seeing the Tsuzumi Waterfall to his right, headed toward Takarazuka, the treasure of sincerity where one devotes one's true heart to the God and the Sovereign, and climbed toward the peak, aiming for the sacred Mount Rokkō.

Kuniyoriwake, with his staff as his strength and in the spirited appearance of a pilgrim, climbed toward the summit of Mount Rokkō, when he found a stone Jizō standing upright in the withered grass by the side of the road.

Kuniyoriwake: 'Ah, my compass is also complaining of fatigue. I think I shall take a rest with this stone Jizō as my companion. ...Hey, Jizō-sama, you always show a beautiful face and look as if you are the incarnation of mercy, but you are quite cold-hearted, aren't you? No matter where I stroke you, there is not a bit of warmth. No matter how much you are called a Jizō, you are stuck to the stone like a kamaboko, and the way half of your body is sticking out, it is just like someone who has met with crucifixion. The current world's image of evil is exactly what you are a good representative of. The pattern of the world where the exterior is like a Jizō and the interior is like Enma is reflected in you.'

The Jizō, as if it had detached itself from the stone and emerged, walked out a distance of about one or two ken, tapping its shakujō staff with a hopping gait.

Jizō: 'Hey, Kuniyoriwake, or should I say, you woman-killer, widow-toppler, and pilgrim-turned-missionary of Munehiko, shall I let you meet the women you toyed with right here? You spoke ill of me, but you are quite a wicked fellow yourself. You are the one who harbors many demons in your heart, while on the outside you call yourself a missionary of Ananai-kyō; it is truly ridiculous. That Kotoyoriwake-no-Mikoto would trust such a person—indeed, a dark world is a dark world. It is a dark world full of the blind.'

Yotahiko: 'Hey there, Ishijizau-sama, or should I say Bakejizau-sama, your Kototama is a bit off, isn't it? I suppose you have been practicing usury in the Iukai. You are quite worldly and amusing, indeed.'

Bakejizau: 'The fellows in today's Shiyakuwai are getting stingy one by one, and my Shiyōbai is not adding up at all; I am short on money. They splash Shōben on me from here and from there; the fellows in this Sekai are just like dogs. When they borrow, they come wagging their tails, but when I tell them to pay it back, they act like Onigon or whatever and snap at me like Kamiinu. I had no Shikata, so I gave up usury completely, stirred up my Bodaishin, and became an Ishijizau, thinking I would help the Maujiya of this Sekai. I have been sitting stiffly at the crossroads with a Shiushiitsukuwan Fudō Seishin, but those who borrowed money from me show a Jizaugaho when they borrow, and an Enmagaho when it is time to pay. As a retribution for falling into the Akumadau, it is pathetic; they change their nature into dogs and appear in the Shiyaba again, and once more, they pass by and splash Shōben on me. Truly, the fellows called Ningen are hopeless. You did not suffer from usury, but you certainly made women suffer. I am sure that as a retribution, you will be reborn as a cat next time; it was properly written in Enma's ledger. Kuniyoriwake is the elegant name you used while in the Shiyaba. Your name is, after all, Take-ko, also known as Munehiko, and it is written that you have a plum blossom birthmark on your right arm. How is that? Is there any mistake?'

Yotahiko: 'There is not a single mistake. But, I wonder if there are many beautiful women when one goes to the Meido.'

Bakejizau: 'Are there? Of course there are. However, a Women's Alliance has been established, and it has been firmly decided that if a fellow called Munehiko appears, they will all gather together and throw him into the Blood Pond.'

Yotahiko: 'Are you, by any chance, a man or a woman?'

Bakejizau: 'What is the point of asking that? If you find out that I am a woman, you will probably show your true colors and place some order again, won't you?'

Yotahiko: 'Who would be such a fool as to send amorous glances to such a cold-hearted person?'

Bakejizau: 'The women in the Iukai all have bodies as cold as ice.'

Yotahiko: 'I heard that you are an alias of the Konjin of Hitsujisaru and are truly an incarnation of a kind deity; is that wrong?'

Bakejizau: 'There are various kinds of Jizau. I am a living Buddha who possesses both good and evil aspects, known as the Jizaugaho when borrowing and the Enmagaho when paying. Even the judgment of hell depends on money; whether I approach you gently and kindly or treat you harshly, it all depends on your own heart. Both good and evil are entirely within the heart of this Jizau. It has been said since ancient times that there is no law for a Jizau (landowner), and the world is free for the Jizau. You should not wander about as a sudden saint, talking about foolish honesty, good, or evil. Why do you not reveal your true self just as you are?'

Yotahiko: 'If you say that I am worth less than three mon, that is the end of it, but even so, I am a messenger of Kami who possesses the most excellent Mitama in this Shiyaba Sekai.'

Jizau: 'In any case, carry me on your back and take me to the Tetsupen of Rokukafuzan. It is not amusing for me to stand here in this Tanisoko forever. I have grown tired of the Keshiki around here. I want to see the Seken more broadly, you see...'

Yotahiko: 'Well, depending on the circumstances, it is not impossible for me to carry you. However, even the Sata of Jigoku depends on money; how much money will you pay?'

Jizau: 'As you have witnessed in Jitsuchi, I am a stone Jizau; there is no way I could have any money.'

Yotahiko: 'Then I shall stop. Ah, how heavy. I am already struggling with my own body on this Yamasaka, so I cannot bear to have extra Jiuriyō added on top of that.'

Jizau: 'You are quite a fellow to be complaining so easily. Do you think Takayorihime of Takaharuyama will Kijiyun because of such things? If you lack the courage to carry me to the mountain top, you will fail anyway. Your companion Tamajiwake was destroyed at the lake of Tsuda for the sake of Enshu, Sunshu, and Bushu, and Ryokokuwake was eaten by an ogre girl. You are the only one left. Since this Kototama battle is hopeless, if you cannot even carry me to the top, you had better take off your helmet and turn back from here.'

Yotahiko: 'What? Ryokokuwake was eaten by an ogre girl? Is that true?'

Jizau: 'It is true. It is Jizau (Jigō-jitoku).'

Yotahiko: 'Hey, stone Jizau, are you joking? That must be a lie.'

Jizau: 'What fool would tell a lie and let the wind catch his mouth? Tamajiwake has fallen into the fate of Jakumetsu-iraku, and wearing a triangular spiritual garment on his head, he is coming here right now. Well, you had better wait for a while.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Hmm,' he folded his arms and sat down heavily on the ground, practiced Chinkon, and focused his mental energy on judging the truth and falsehood.

Jizau: 'Ha ha ha ha, no matter how long you think, there is no chance that one who has changed his country will return. Will you not quickly accept my request?'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Do not be so noisy. You are a paralyzed Jizau who cannot even move your own body. Whether I carry you or not is entirely up to my own thinking. I am in the middle of gathering the eight million Kami at the Takamagahara of the earth in the Seika-tanden, and I am inviting the various Kami through Chinkon to hold a Kamu-hagari.'

Jizau had, before one knew it, transformed into the figure of a bewitching Bijin.

Jizau: 'Come now, Kunisama, I am Oichi, whom you tormented so much in the Shiyaba.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'What, Oichi? Do not talk such Baka. You are likely that Kinmaukiubi, that Dogitsune. You intend to deceive me, but do you think Kuniyoriwake is the sort to be deceived by such things?'

Jizau: 'I am already a Ningen of the Iukai, and though I know not what you think, this is not Rokukafuzan; it is the crossroads of the Rokudau. Try thinking about it well. The scenery around here must be entirely different from the Shiyaba, must it not?'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Do not talk such Baka. Where is there any difference? If you keep babbling, shall I make you reveal your Shiyōtai as a fox?'

Jizau: 'Ho ho ho ho, look at how that Sō-sama is Kiba. It seems you have become terrified from the bottom of your heart, sweating profusely, and are putting forth all your strength to Karaiba; with such an attitude, how could Takayorihime ever reach Ōjiyō?'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Whether I let her reach Ōjiyō or not is my own Jiiu and Kenri. Do you, a mere woman, have the Kenri to Yōkai our actions with such chatter?'

Jizau: 'Whether I have it or not, I am a Yōkai, so it is Atarimahe for me to Yōkai. Since the time you were in the Genkai, you kidnapped many women, did you not? That is why, having come to the Iukai, being Yōkai by a woman in return is the retribution for the sins you created in the past; ho ho ho ho, that face of yours looks so unpleasant...'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Agh, let me go!'

Jizau: 'Even if you tell me to let you go, a villain like you can never become a Buddha no matter how much you Kiba. You cannot even become an Oni; you are nothing more than a dog that walks around urinating on stone Jizau. However, in the Iukai, you are permitted to have the face of a Ningen. That is why you are called a human-dog. You have made me a horse until now, calling me the horse of the human-dog guard dog Saiō, but this time I shall make you the horse of Saiō. Come, crawl on all fours right there.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Will you continue to mock a man to the end? A man's arms have bones in them.'

Jizau: 'Women have bones too. Even if thin, they are like oak; your arms, though they look thick, are like fresh bamboo, hollow inside and limp. In the Shiyaba, you swung your arms around, and your bluffs were effective, were they not? You could strut about being called Mr. Fresh Bamboo, but in the Iukai, the situation is a bit different.'
