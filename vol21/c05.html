§ 1

Due to the prompt wit of Tamaharuwake, the six Konusuto began to realize their errors, joyfully surrendered to the way of Kami, and decided to follow the Sendenshi toward Takaharuyama. The sun was gradually setting, and the Tanimichi, surrounded by mountains and appearing as a Tsukishiro, began to brighten in some inexplicable way. However, the Tanimichi, burdened by high Kauzan to the east and west, did not reveal the Kaukau moonlight. After a while, a strange growling sound was heard ahead, and then, the sound of what seemed to be the footsteps of hundreds of people, if not more, began to be heard moment by moment.

Tamaharuwake: 'Yo, a strange Monōto has become audible. This appears to be the Gotsūkuwa of a Daishifudan of Sanzoku. Let us wait here, Kotomukeyaha them one by one, and become a splendid Ōoyabun. Ah, how interesting, how interesting. Has the season of Heaven arrived? Now, Ryū, Kuni, Enshū, and the other subordinates, make preparations without fail.'

Kuniyorawake: 'Haha, is it another change of business?'

Tamaharuwake: 'To face the occasion and respond to changes is the instinct of a hero and a man of valor.'

Enshū: 'Excuse me, those footsteps are not human. Those are fierce beasts called Senbiki-ōkami that occasionally pass through this road. No matter how much of a hero or man of valor one may be, one cannot prevail against Senbiki-ōkami. Come, come, everyone, scatter and hide yourselves in the forest. If we are too densely packed, it will be a disaster if we catch the eyes of the wolves.'

Tamaharuwake: 'What are you saying? I shall Kotomukeyaha every last one of those wolves with the Kototama of good words and beautiful phrases.'

Ryūkokubake: 'Is this a time to be saying such foolish things? Come, quickly, everyone prepare yourselves. We cannot afford to deal with beasts. If we get injured, it would be like being bitten by a dog... No, if we are bitten by wolves, we cannot even sue for damages, and there is no place to get money for medical expenses. Hey, Kuniyorawake, Enshū, Sunshū, all of you, there is no need to listen to what Tama-kō says. Today, I am the temporary commander. Come, quickly, quickly!'

He ran into the forest where trees grew thick nearby. Kuniyorawake and the other six acted in concert with Ryūkokubake. Tamaharuwake remained standing on the road.

Tamaharuwake: 'Ahahaha, everyone is such a weakling. What is there to fear from a few thousand four-legged beasts? Hey, you wolves, come out as many as you like!'

He shouted thus. The wolves came in a line up to five or six ken ahead, but perhaps daunted by the vigor of Tamaharuwake, who stood blocking the middle of the road and making a racket while wearing a twisted headband, they turned their path, aimed for the mountain on the opposite side of the valley, and passed by like the wind, making a rustling sound. He climbed the mountain pass, shouting in a loud voice on purpose so that it could be heard all around. While muttering this to himself, he began to feel a little sleepy and swayed his body unsteadily. Two rough men came walking up to that spot.

Man A: 'Hey, he is here, he is here.'

Man B: 'Is he not dozing off? It seems he has grown quite tired. Hey, Genshū, you take this and hand it over to Teristan. I am going to make preparations because many followers of the Sangōkyō propagandist are coming to Takaharuyama, so you must firmly hand over this, which contains money and the name list recording all the operational plans.'

Tamaharuwake did not make a sound on purpose, and received the package by nodding his head two or three times. The two rough men ran down the pass toward the south with a momentum as if a giant rock were falling from the mountaintop, making the ground tremble as if they were being chased by pursuers.

Tamaharuwake: 'Those fellows appear to be subordinates of the Alps-kyō. They mistook me for an ally and left important items in my care. It seems that some of the Alps-kyō fellows are also disguised as pilgrims. However, if I stay here, it will not be amusing if they come back and discover me. I wonder if I should change my position and take my time to examine the documents inside.'

Saying this in a low voice, he trod along the mountain ridge for about twenty or thirty ken, and began to walk while admiring the moonlight. A faint fire was flickering between the trees, hitting the bottom of the valley. Tamaharuwake, having gazed at this, said:

Tamaharuwake: 'Ah, it is strange that someone is lighting a fire in this deep mountain. Perhaps it is the den of mountain bandits, or maybe a woodcutter. In any case, I shall approach aiming for that firelight and try to peek at the situation. Traveling is indeed a very interesting thing.'

Aiming at the single point of fire, he descended toward the bottom of the valley through the steep, densely wooded mountain. Looking closely, it was a small woodcutter's hut with a roof covered in bamboo grass and tree bark. The coughing of a man could be heard from inside.

Tamaharuwake: 'Excuse me, I am a pilgrim, but I have lost my way and do not know the path ahead, so I have come here aiming for the faint fire. Please, I am not a suspicious person, so pray let me stay the night.'

A man's voice from inside replied:

Man: 'Yes, yes, as you can see, this is a filthy woodcutter's hut, but if you are a pilgrim, that is perfectly fine. Please, come inside.'

Saying this, he gladly opened the rough door, which rattled from the inside, and guided him to the back. Tamaharuwake was led by the guide into a beautiful room that did not seem to belong in such a deep mountain.

Tamaharuwake: 'Do you live here all alone in these deep mountains? When I was at the gate, I thought I heard a man crying; who on earth was crying?'

Man: 'I am Mokusuke the woodcutter. My wife, Osugi, passed away about two hours ago, and because of that, I was crying for my wife at the pillow of the dead, as a final farewell in this world. However, being suddenly widowed, there is nothing I can do, and having nothing to offer before the spirit, I made rice balls to offer to the spirit, and instead of a posthumous name, I shaved a piece of wood and enshrined her like this. I heard that you are a pilgrim, so would you please stay here and watch the house while I go to prepare offerings and inform my close friends and the temple priest? I will be back in a moment...'

Tamaharuwake: 'Well, that is truly a pitiful thing. Although I am on a hurried journey, seeing this, I cannot just abandon you. I will keep watch over the dead, so come, come, go quickly and return.'

He stepped over the threshold, and without knowing when, walked two or three chō, scooped up water from the stream in the valley, and took it into his mouth, vigorously, like a chicken drinking water; he would take a mouthful, lift his head, gargle 'gara-gara-gara' and spit 'bu-bu', then drink another mouthful and look upward, repeating the 'gara-gara-gara-gara' and 'bu-bu-bu' countless times.

Tatsukuniwake, Kuniyoriwake, Enshiu, Bushiu, and four others, who had been searching with the firelight as their target, caught sight of the figure of Tamaharuwake in the night, and stood still in the shadows of the trees, watching the situation, thinking him a suspicious person.

Enshiu: 'Excuse me, Sendenshi-sama, the gargling and spitting has appeared. This is a solitary woodcutter's hut; one never knows what might emerge. That must surely be a Bakeshiu.'

とガンヂガラミに括つた風呂敷包を解き開いて見れば、金色燦然たる金銀の小玉ザラザラと現はれて来た。さうして一冊の手帳が出て来た。開いて見れば、アルプス教の秘密書類である。三人はこれ幸ひと懐中に収め、後は杢助に返し、九人連れ此家を発つて津田の湖辺に向つて宣伝歌を歌ひ乍ら勢よく進み行く。
