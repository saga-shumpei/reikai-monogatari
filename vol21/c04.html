Chapter 4: 砂利喰 [678]

砂利喰〔678〕

§ 1

Umeteruhiko, who day and night proclaims the teachings of Kami, left the rare mansion behind. Here, three Sendenshi, having purified their hearts and souls in the Gankutsu of Takakumayama where Tamateruhiko was born, were escorted by Kamikunimori. They bade farewell at the gate of Kunadomeyakata and walked on, letting their feet lead them. They crossed the Sakahitauge, famous for the Tengu rock, and rolled up their skirts to cross the upper reaches of the Obata river to the opposite bank. Gazing left and right at Aonogahara, they proceeded through Hofukidani and arrived before Togakushiiha.

The three were climbing the path through the valley, where trees grew thick and lush along the mountain stream that roared with the sound of turbulent spray. Finally, they reached the foot of Togakushiiha, sat upon a rock by the roadside, and rested. About one chō away on the slope, the shadows of five or six suspicious men could be seen, whispering incessantly about something.

Tamajiwake: 'Ryūkokuwake, Kuniyoriwake-aniki, look, are those not strange fellows whispering over there? I wonder what they are doing on this mountain path.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Those are a band of thieves. A business of highway robbery has appeared, stripping passersby of their clothing and belongings. Just a while ago, a woman passed by crying, having been left completely naked. That must surely have been the work of those fellows. Because we are walking here wearing straw raincoats and hats, that woman must have taken us for their kind; did she not scream and flee in a panic?'

Ryūkokuwake: 'There is no mistake about that. We are surely going to be stripped as well. We must think of something to do here.'

Tamajiwake: 'What, you never know until you go as far as you can; it is the heart of the moment. There is no need for needless worry. If by any chance they are thieves, we shall take the lead in using the voice of thieves, join their ranks, and skillfully lead them to repentance. It is said that Konohanahime-mikoto-sama, when wishing to reform a thief, would manifest in one of thirty-three forms, become a thief herself, work alongside them, and say, "Hey, being a thief is a truly narrow and terrifying way to live in this world. Why don't we stop such useless things and take up an honest trade under the open sky?" and thus lead the thief to repentance. To reform a drinker, one drinks with them; to reform a gambler, one becomes a gambler oneself; that is the divine plan of Kami. If they are indeed thieves, we shall disguise ourselves as thieves, join hands with them, become their companions, and then lead them to repentance.'

§ 2

Kuniyoriwake: 'No matter how things go, I definitely want to stop being a thief.'

Tamaharuwake: 'What, I am not telling you to become a thief from your heart. It is a means to make the thieves stop, so what does it matter? That is the working of the Mitama of Kuwanjizaiten. If by any chance the other party is a thief, I, this Tamaharuwake, will threaten them by saying I am the boss of the thieves, so you all must pretend to be my kobun. And since names like Tatsukuniwake or Kuniyoriwake would be discovered by the other party if we recite them, let us change our names here for a while and go as Boss Tatsukō, Boss Kunikō, and Boss Tamakō. It would not be pleasant if the other party said to us, "Hey travelers, wait a moment, hand over all your belongings and go." He who strikes first controls the others. Once we identify them as thieves, I will be the one to light the fuse. Hey, follow Boss Tatsukō, Boss Kunikō, and Boss Tamakō.'

Tatsukuniwake: 'In the end, you have turned the propagandists into the kobun of thieves, haven't you.'

Kuniyoriwake: 'Yeah, this cannot be helped either. If I think of it as becoming an incarnation of Kuwanjizaiten, it is not impossible to endure. Come, Boss Tamakō, please go ahead.'

Tamaharuwake stood in the lead and, while swinging his arms, approached the place where five or six men were sitting in a circle blocking the road, and noticed that the clothes of a woman, which seemed to have just been stripped off, were nearby. Thinking that these fellows were definitely thieves, Tamaharuwake said in a deliberately loud voice,

Tamakō: 'Hey Tatsu, Kuni, come quickly. There are five or six men over there. We shall snatch their clothes and leave them stark naked.'

He advanced as he spoke. The five or six thieves were all taken aback upon hearing this voice.

Tamakō: 'Hey you small-time thieves, who do you think I am? I am Boss Tamakō, the great thief known as the right arm of the demon hag of Mikunigadake. Now, if you hand over all your belongings to me cleanly, it will be fine; if you hesitate to spit them out, I will pull the heads off every one of you and send you all to the same fate.'

A: 'Do not talk so noisily. I am the same as you. As a matter of professional courtesy, please spare at least our clothes.'

Tamakō: 'If you say "spare us," well, depending on the circumstances, I might not spare you, but how about this: won't you hand over just one piece to me? I will forgive you by turning a great disaster into a small one.'

B: 'Excuse me, Oyabun, please wait a moment. We are currently holding a meeting, becoming the founding committee of the Nusu Company, and are in the middle of discussing the recruitment of shares. Please, you too should hold many shares; if the goods are right, we might even recommend you as the president-sama.'

Tamaharuwake: 'I shall hold the shares, but as I am the Ichiban Oyakata, I will not pay the Kabudai. Present me with about eight million shares of Iusenkabu. If you do so, I shall grant Ninka for your Tetsudō-fusetsu and anything else you desire.'

Kō: 'Even if I were to receive such Ninka, I have no use for this Dorobau-guwaishiya. Thanks to your Tetsudō-ninka, Musendenwa, and Denshin, our business is being greatly hindered, so we have no need for such things.'

Tamaharuwake: 'You are indeed a Kosonusuto. What is the point of stripping the Kimono off a Tsūkaunin and tormenting them? Why not wear a Haorihakama or Yōfuku, and with the tip of a fountain pen, commit the theft of ten thousand hardships, ten thousand pressures, and ten thousand shipwrecks all at once? If you build a Tetsudō, you gnaw on the rails; if you open a road, you gnaw on the gravel; if you build a warship, you gnaw on the steel; if you contract for canned goods, you stuff them with stones—that is the kind of Ritsupa wisdom you must use to practice the Nūsū style. If you do that, you need not hide in these deep mountains and tremble; you can drive an automobile through the center of a great city in broad daylight, look down upon the fools of the world while carrying a prostitute, and puff on a cigar with a grand expression. Why not give up such hopeless petty thievery and switch to a business that acquires the greatest treasure in the world? There is a business far more magnificent than gnawing on warships, eating rails, or eating gravel, worth countless times more.'

Kō: 'Eh, Oyakata, where can such a business be found?'

Tamaharuwake: 'It is everywhere. Just follow me around for two or three days and see. I may be disguised as a beggar like this, but in truth, I am a Ritsupa person. In the current world, those who decorate their houses, decorate their clothes, and cover their bodies in gold are actually suffering within. It is raining fire inside their homes. Because we appear dirty on the surface like this, we do not have many expenses, we are very carefree, we obtain magnificent treasures that the people of the world do not know of, and we enjoy ourselves by making the flowers of joy bloom every single day. Why don't you become my foster child? Petty thievery must be quite painful, I suppose.'

Kō: 'As you have guessed, it is indeed quite painful. However, I have no choice but to engage in this kind of business.'

Tatenaho is before our eyes. Enshiu, Sunshiu, Kafushiu, Bushiu, Unshiu, and Sanshiu, all together. Come, come, now come! Even if there are tens of thousands of enemies, what is there to fear? Drawing the sword of Nahohi, attack and torment the swarming Yatsubara without limit with the true Kototama of Kami. Raise the Kachidoki, and as the Mitsukahi of the Kakihatokiha of Shinkai, lift your names for Chiyo and Yorozuyo. The way of Kami, which lets the inexhaustible Inochi live on forever. Whether the Asahi shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, take back the Hotsushiyu-no-tama held in the hands of Takayorihime, and as the Kami of salvation for this world, shall we not perform outstanding Kōmiyō and Tegara together with us? Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. May the Mitama be blessed, they say, as they advance, taking the lead over such steep mountain paths. With the three pillars of Mizu-no-mitama, adding the five Mitama, around the midnight when the three-five moon shines, they ride past Betsuin Village. Riding the Kuchiguruma that carries them through Ōtsukinami and the village of Nose, they have arrived at the shore of Lake Tsuda, where the waves are filled, in the village of Tada in the province of Settsu.

Although this story is long, enticed by the god of sleep, while lying down, one advances to Ne-no-kuni and Kasho-no-kuni. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. May the Mitama be blessed, they say, and looking back, the mist of the Toyama clears away. Waiting for the Asahi that illuminates Toyoni to rise upon the summit of Mount Takaharu, I shall relate the continuation of this in great detail.
