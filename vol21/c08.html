Chapter 8: 津田の Lake [682]

津田の湖〔682〕

§ 1

Starting with the three Sendenshi who appeared on the shores of Lake Tsuda, the six men from Enshiu, Sunshiu, Bushiu, Sanpau, Kōshū, and Unshū gazed far off at Mount Takaharuyama, and just as they were deciding on a plan to launch an attack from three directions, the six thieves began an internal quarrel; seeing that the men from Bushiu, Sunshiu, and Enshiu had been struck on their shins and had fallen to the ground, the three men from Sanpau, Kōshū, and Unshū abandoned the scene and fled back the way they had come.

Here, Tatsukuniwake decided to take the road to the north, making a detour to attack from Mount Ōtaniyama. Meanwhile, Kuniyoriwake decided on a plan to cross the Tsuzumi Waterfall, climb Mount Rokukafuzan, and head toward Mount Takaharuyama while subduing the Magami. Tamaharuwake entrusted himself to a boat moored at the lakeshore, intending to cross Lake Tsuda and rush straight to Mount Takaharuyama; he took the three men with injured legs onto the boat and, rowing the oars himself, set out toward the foothills in the west on a cold, windy, moonlit night.

When they had advanced almost to the center of the lake, the three men suddenly stood up.

Enshiu: 'Hey, you are a Sendenshi of the Sangokyō, and while you have the sacred duty to uphold the path of truth, it is truly a despicable way to act, taking advantage of having obtained secret documents to discern the enemy's defenses and attempt to attack from three directions. Why do you not proceed with nothing but sincerity?'

Sunshiu: 'The truth is, that notebook is a precious item for our group. Do you think we would let you take it from us? We noticed that you obtained this important document at Mokusuke's house, so the six of us communicated with each other along the way using secret signals, pretended to fight, and feigned leg injuries to get on this boat.'

Bushiu: 'Now, it is no use struggling anymore. Return it to us cleanly and completely. If you keep stalling, we will throw you into this lake.'

Tamaharuwake: 'Hahaha, what are you blathering about? To turn your blades against a Sendenshi of the peerless divine power of the Sangokyō—you have no regard for your lives. It is the same as a mantis waving its axe at a dragon chariot; if you repent quickly, I will forgive you, but if you persist in your evil intentions to the end, then there is no other choice—shall I bind your bodies with Kototama and throw you into this lake?'

Enshiu: 'If you have the weapon of Kototama, we have the weapon of Kototama as well. Besides, these iron arms of mine are waiting and roaring to go. Come on, will you hand it over to us quickly?'

Tamaharuwake: 'Even if you tell me to hand it over, it is not mine alone. After I have consulted with Tatsukuniwake and Kuniyoriwake, if they agree, I shall hand it over.'

Unknown Speaker: 'Do not talk like a fool. We have already arranged for our comrades to lie in wait on the way and finish off Tatsukuniwake and Kuniyoriwake. The moment you cross this lake to the other side, our allies are waiting, and the plan is set to torture you to death. Are you surprised? How magnificent our strategy is, is it not?'

Tamaharuwake: 'Even if three, five, or a hundred of you little brats come to attack, I am not a Tamaharuwake who would be startled. Do not underestimate me so much. So, Takayorihime is posing as the leader of the Alps Religion on the surface while actually being the great boss of mountain bandits, is she?'

Sunshiu: 'Do not talk nonsense. There is not a single thief in the Alps Religion. We are merely doing this job for a fee. In truth, we are not of the Alps Religion. We are a group of thieves, so look closely. You will find that our names are not written in that notebook. It is said that there are many names of those who sneaked into the holy land, but even if you know that now, the Alps Religion will feel no pain or itch. Instead, we just need to dispose of you three missionaries. ...Hey, what do you say? If we strip this guy naked, seize the secret documents, and bring them to Takaharuyama, we can receive a fine reward. Come, do not falter.'

They attacked from three sides with oars.

The missionaries of the Sangyō, who followed the principle of non-resistance, had no choice but to fold their hands and chant the Heavenly Number Song in self-defense. However, perhaps due to the impurity of having the secret documents of the enemy who defied the divine will in their pockets, the Heavenly Number Song and the Chinkon (spirit-pacifying prayer) had no effect at all, for this day only. The three men struck at them wildly from three sides.

Tamaharuwake had no choice but to grab an oar, and faster than the others, he waded into the midst of the three and fought back, moving like a flying bird. It was suspicious, but Enshiu fell into the lake with a splash. Cornered by the two, Tamaharuwake was again plunged headlong into the lake with a splash. When he tried to cling to the boat to climb up, the two tried to strike his head from above with their oars. In the meantime, Enshiu scrambled up into the boat.

Enshiu: 'Now, you wretch Tama, it is fine if you hand it over obediently; if you do not, your life is already over.'

Tamaharuwake swam with all his might to escape. The three men chased Tamaharuwake while maneuvering the boat. Tamaharuwake swam around, bobbing up and down. The secret documents slipped out of his pocket and floated on the surface of the water. The three men quickly picked them up, carefully hid them in the boat while they were still wet, and continued to chase Tamaharuwake as he bobbed up and down, trying to strike at his head. Thinking it would be a disaster to be struck, he held his breath in agony and dove to the bottom of the water; when he surfaced on one side to catch his breath, the three were chasing him in the boat once again.

Tamaharuwake was at his wit's end, and just as he faced a situation of nine deaths and one life, a boat arrived, rowing as if shooting an arrow, carrying one child. Tamaharuwake, rejoicing as if he had found a blind turtle meeting a floating log, clung to the boat. The boatman helped Tamaharuwake and brought him aboard. Tamaharuwake was gasping for breath. At this moment, the three thieves,

Thieves: 'Hey, do not get in our way!'

They came attacking, aiming at this boat.

Funabito: 'You must be the petty thieves of Enshiu, Sunshiu, and Bushiu. Now that I have come here, you too must resign yourselves to your fate. I shall crush you one by one.'

Startled by this voice, the three men rowed with all their might and fled toward the west, as if shooting an arrow. Their boat's bow collided with a large rock protruding into the lake, and the hull was smashed to pieces, sinking with a gurgling sound. The three men tried to swim away, each striking out with their hands.

The boatman steered the boat, aiming for the heads of the three men bobbing in the water. Tamaharuwake finally regained his senses. Looking, he saw that Mokusuke and his child were in the boat, chasing the shadows of the three thieves.

Tamaharuwake: 'Ah, you are Mokusuke-sama. Thank you for coming to save me from such a dangerous situation.'

Mokusuke: 'We shall hear the story later at our leisure. If we dawdle, the lives of the three thieves will be lost. Come, you must row this oar as well.'

Tamaharuwake immediately began to row the oar. At last, they rescued the three thieves. Then, they sent the three men onto the rock in the lake as before, turned the boat back to this side, maintained a distance of about ten ken, and facing the three, began to sing with a clear voice, intending to let them hear the Sendenka.

Tamaharuwake: 'Though the morning sun may shine or cloud over, Though the moon may wax or wane, Even if the great earth should sink, The power of Makoto shall save the world. Unknown: 'Ah, that is right. Children are Shiyōjiki. If I think these Koitsura are Kaai, it would be for the sake of their Shiyōrai to leave them on the rock for a while. Come, Mokusuke-sama, let us proceed over there.'

Leaving the rock island in the middle of the lake behind, they proceed as if shooting an arrow, pointing toward the eastern foot of Takaharuyama.

It is Fushigi, for the Kosui water increases in Mizukasa as one watches, and the high rock in the middle of the lake gradually sinks into the Suichiu, and already the waves have come rushing up to their Ashimoto. With the water increasing Kokukoku, the Sannin have already become submerged up to their necks.
