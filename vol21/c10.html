Chapter 10: 女権拡張 [684]

女権拡張〔684〕

§ 1

In the depths of the mountains where the blizzard rages, the Sendenshi of Tatsukuniwake, intending to head toward Takaharuyama, is struck by the sound of monkeys, and proceeds with an eager heart. On a valley path where Jinen is rare, buried in snow as the day darkens, he rests his breath for a Shibashi in the shadow of a rock standing by the Robau.

He draws his body into the shadow of a rock that protrudes from one Katahe of the valley, and it is decided that he will spend the Ichiya there. Tatsukuniwake falls into a doze, but what suddenly enters his ears is the charming voice of a woman; when he wakes in surprise, a Meurei Bijin stands before him, with seven or eight strands of loose hair from her temples hanging near her cheeks, looking slightly sorrowful, and holding one Akago.

Tatsukuniwake: 'On this snowy road in the middle of the Mayonaka, a woman alone, and furthermore holding a Chinomigo, where are you going?'

Bijin: 'Yes, I am a person from Naniha. I was abducted by the Onibaba of Takaharuyama, and have been suffering day and night, meeting with Semeku and Nanjifu; however, I was taken into the thoughts of a Buka of an old woman called Karinsu who has compassion, and was secretly rescued and have fled back to Kokomade. Yet, I do not know when the pursuers might come. Please, help me. As for me, I am freezing in this cold, and my body is stiffening so that I cannot take a single step. If you have fire, would you please let me warm myself?'

Tatsukuniwake: 'That must be a difficult situation. It is impossible to gather leaves here to burn, so I am troubled.'

Bijin: 'Would it not be possible for you to share the warmth of your warm body? Now that it has come to this, I cannot concern myself with shame or anything else. It feels as though the blood in my whole body is about to coagulate.'

Tatsukuniwake: 'Ah, this is a troublesome matter. I am currently on my way to conquer the Majin of Takaharuyama, and it is absolutely impossible for me to touch the body of a woman. Is there no good idea that can come to mind?'

As he looks around the surroundings, what catches his eye in the snow-light is a bundle of dry firewood, remaining dry, covered by the protruding rock.

Tatsukuniwake: 'Ah, here is some excellent firewood. I do not know who cut and left it, but this too is thanks to the grace of the Gods; how would it be if we burned this to get warm?'

Bijin: 'That would be convenient. Please, burn it. However, if we burn too large a fire, it will be troublesome if it becomes a target for the pursuers, so...'

Tatsukuniwake: 'Very well, very well, let us burn it small. However, the pursuers might come searching for our footprints in the snow.'

§ 2

Tatsukuniwake: 'Thanks to that, the falling snow has buried our Ashiato one after another, so it is Daijiyōbu.'

Tatsukuniwake strikes a flint to produce fire, lights the firewood to warm himself, and the woman also warms her hands with a look of joy.

Tatsukuniwake: 'According to your Ohanashi just now, you were held captive at Takaharuyama; if so, you must be well Gozon of the Uchimaku of the Alps Kyō.'

Woman: 'Yes, I am well Shiyōchi of it. Even if you three or five people go there, you are like insects flying into a flame; I think it would be for your own good to stop.'

Tatsukuniwake with a Fukigen face:

Tatsukuniwake: 'Even if there are Ikuman of Kiyōteki, once we have been Ninmei by the Kyōshiyu of Kotoyoriwake, we must fulfill this Shimei even if we lose a single Inochi, so we intend to go as far as we can.'

Woman: 'That is a Taihen Goketsukō, and it is Ketsukō. I also wish to join hands with an Okata of such strong spirit as you and clear the grudge of the cruelty that the demon hag has inflicted upon me until now, so please, will you not take me with you?'

Tatsukuniwake: 'No, that is out of the question. How could I travel with a woman? Besides, if it is said that I went to conquer the demon god while being helped by you, it would be a disgrace for generations to come, so I must flatly refuse that.'

Woman: 'You are a very stubborn person. I wish I could have a husband with such a firm spirit.'

Tatsukuniwake: 'Now, now, woman, stop your joking. Are you not holding a baby in your arms? There is no doubt that you must have a fine husband.'

Woman: 'No, no, I have never had a husband yet.'

Tatsukuniwake: 'Is it not a mystery that you have a child without a husband?'

Woman: 'Ho ho ho, you say things that do not befit a missionary of the Sangō Kyō. Did not Otama-sama, the birth mother of Tamateruhime-sama, conceive without a husband?'

Tatsukuniwake: 'That is so, but that is an exception. There is no reason for such a thing to happen to an ordinary woman.'

Woman: 'Did you look upon me as an ordinary woman?'

Tatsukuniwake: 'Well, looking at you like this, there is nothing unusual, and I cannot tell the difference.'

Woman: 'If you cannot even understand my true nature, your ability as a Saniwa is also hopeless. How could you possibly make the demon god of Takaharuyama submit?'

§ 3

Tatsukuniwake: 'This is a strange woman I have met. You are, in short, a Bakemono, are you not?'

Woman: 'There is no doubt that I am a Bakemono. However, there are both good and evil among Bakemono. Please act as my Saniha.'

Tatsukuniwake: 'Seeing you walking alone on a Yamamichi in this snowfall, carrying an Akago, you do not seem to be a Ritsupa person. Seeing that you have fled after being tormented by the Akugami Takayorihime, you are likely not a decent sort.'

Woman: 'Because I am a woman who has been tormented by Takayorihime, you say I am not a decent person, but did Otama-no-kata, who gave birth to Tamateruhime-sama, not suffer at the hands of Mukadehime at Mikuni-ga-take? Your Handan lacks Seikō. Is it not that your eyes are clouded by the Sennifushiyu that because Otama-sama is Ritsupa, but I am walking on a Yukimichi in the Yonaka, I must be suspicious?'

Tatsukuniwake folds his Riyōte across his chest, sighs deeply, and falls into thought. The woman pokes at the firewood and makes it smolder. The faces of the Futari become increasingly clear. When Tatsukuniwake suddenly looks at the woman's face, he notices that her two ears are twitching like those of a horse.

Tatsukuniwake: 'What is the matter with your ears? It is the Tsūrei that if one is a Ningen, the ears do not move. It seems that the Fuzuiikin of your ears are highly developed, and they move with Jiiujizai like a Chikushiyō. You are Kitsuto, without a doubt, a Mashiyō woman.'

Woman: 'Ohohoho, is the movement of my ears the only thing that bothers you? Are not your ears, or rather, your very soul, constantly wavering, and are not the raging waves and billows rising in your heart? That is far more ridiculous, hohohoho.'

Tatsukuniwake: 'Ha-tena. It has become increasingly incomprehensible.'

Woman: 'Truly, even I have never met a Senden-shi as dense as you. Do not the gods change their forms in various ways? Look at Konohanahime-sama. She becomes a dragon body, becomes a beast, appears in the Ritsupa form of a god, and even disguises herself as a beggar to save all living beings; yet, to treat me like a beast simply because my ears moved is a bit unheard of, is it not?'

Tatsukuniwake: 'Ha-tena.'

Tatsukuniwake says only this and looks down again.

Woman: 'No matter how much you say "hatena, hatena," if your Mitama is not polished, this Danpan will never end. What a slow-witted Senden-shi you are, hohohoho.'

This is the trend of the times, and one cannot push a cart up a slope. I believe that for men, keeping silent and adopting an attitude of obedience is the safest and best method for their future position, o ho ho ho.'

Natsuhiko: 'No, that is enough; I shall order a halt to this speech on the expansion of women's rights.'
