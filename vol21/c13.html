Chapter 13: Dream の女 [687]

夢の女〔687〕

§ 1

Tatsukuniwake appeared from beneath the shrine, covered in spider webs, and gathering the surrounding leaves and Kareeda, he lit a fire in the Hiroba beside the shrine. Illuminated by this Kuwakuō, the woman from Izen was Enrei beyond compare, her Rinkuwaku as clear as if she had suddenly emerged from the darkness, and she approached Tatsukuniwake Shizushizu.

Tatsukuniwake: 'Ya, I do not know what Jiyochiu you are, but that was a Taihen dangerous affair.'

Woman: 'Hai, I am a person of this village, and I am the Hitorimusume named Osaku.'

Tatsukuniwake: 'Anata, do you have no Gokiyōdai?'

Woman: 'I have two older brothers and one younger brother, and my Riyōshin are also living in a state of Sauken.'

Tatsukuniwake: 'That is a matter of Medeta above all else. However, why in the middle of this Mayonaka did such a Warumono try to Hitsusara you? It seems you are not like other women, given to Yoaruki; if you have parents and Gokiyōdai, there is Nanihodo no matter how reckless the fellow, he should not be able to break into your house.'

Woman: 'Hai, I am ashamed to say, but I had a wish, and I have been visiting this Miyasama at the foot of the mountain at the hour of the ox, without letting my parents or brothers know. Today marks the completion of the twenty-one days. Yet, I do not know how they learned of my shrine visits, but they were lying in wait at the entrance of this mountain, and they brought me here to the front of the shrine in Kannagara.'

Tatsukuniwake: 'And what kind of matter is that wish of yours? Is there some difficulty concerning your personal life?'

Woman: 'You just said from behind the shrine that you are Daijizaiten Ōkuniwake-no-Mikoto. Is that true? If you are Daijizaiten Ōkuniwake-no-Mikoto, you are the ancestral god of the Brahmanism and Alps religion that those Warumono respect daily. Despite that, is it not strange that they fled so easily? I suspect that I cannot accept your Kototama as being of the Daijizaiten lineage. Surely you are a missionary of the Sangōkyō...'

Tatsukuniwake: 'Iya, I am truly humbled by your keen insight. And as for the nature of your wish, is it not that some difficult matter has arisen?'

Woman: 'A matter of great importance to my life has suddenly occurred.'

Tatsukuniwake: 'And what is the reason for that?'

『それや又どういふ理由ですか』

§ 2

Woman: 'Yes, I have already reached the age of eighteen. From here and there, people have been pressing my parents and siblings daily to give me away in marriage. However, I have not yet found even one person who is my ideal husband. Therefore, I have been visiting the shrine for three weeks, starting today, to pray that I may be granted a suitable husband. In a divine dream, I was told that on the third week, I would be allowed to meet a man in front of the shrine, and that he is my true husband. You are the true husband permitted to me by the Kamisama; please, I beg you, cherish me.'

Man: 'This is quite something, young lady; your words are a bit troublesome.'

Woman: 'Ho ho ho, you say it is troublesome? Do you dislike women? I doubt there is any man in this wide world who hates women.'

Man: 'If it were a wife I had proposed to, that would be one thing, but when a woman approaches me like this, it is somehow frightening, and I cannot give you an answer immediately.'

Woman: 'You are single, are you not? If you had a wife, it would be different, but in your current situation, you must eventually take a wife, must you not? This is a bond tied by the gods; please, give me a favorable reply.'

Man: 'Excuse me, Osaku-san, or whatever your name is, you seem to be a very modern woman. It seems that as the world changes, women are beginning to negotiate directly with men. My, how things change.'

Woman: 'Do you truly not like a clumsy person like me?'

Man: 'No, that is not it at all. You are a splendid and beautiful woman, as if a celestial maiden had descended or Benzaiten had returned; if I were to compare you to a flower, you are like one half-bloomed and covered in beautiful dew. It is certainly not that I dislike you, but no matter what you say, I am on my way to Takaharuyama to conquer demons under divine command, so a promise of marriage is something I cannot even think of.'

Woman: 'Then you are saying that you will never cast your eyes upon a woman?'

Man: 'That is a matter of course. I am sorry, but I must decline today.'

Woman: 'Then when will you make a promise to me?'

Man: 'My heart is in the moment. Since I do not know what tomorrow will bring, I cannot make a promise.'

Woman: 'You intend to achieve military merit at Takaharuyama, and then select a splendid woman of the world to be your wife, so you have no intention of casting your eyes upon a woman like me, raised in a remote mountain village, do you?'

§ 3

Natsuhiko: 'No, no, never, never; such a thing does not even cross my mind. At any rate, until I have fulfilled my Shimei, I shall have no Kuwankei with women. It would be a great fear before the Shinkai.'

Woman: 'Did the Kamisama command that you must not meet a woman or make a promise until the conquest of Takaharuyama is finished? Did not Izanaginomikoto and Izanaminomikoto-sama unite as a Fūfu of Iki, and perform the Shingefu of Kuniu, Shimau, and Kamiu? Even the Kamisama could not perform their true Gokuwatsudō without being a Fūfu. It is by uniting the Iki of Inyō that all Banbutsu are first generated, is it not? And yet, to say that you will absolutely not make a promise because you are in the midst of an important Shingefu—that is something I cannot quite Gaten.'

Natsuhiko: 'Nay, I do not say absolutely, but just this once, Dōzo, please forgive me. Once I have successfully achieved my purpose, I shall consult with you again.'

Woman: 'Ohohoho, you selfish person. You even performed the funeral for Mokusuke-san's wife, did you not? Thinking of that, why should the mere act of making a promise of marriage with me now be displeasing to the Kamisama? Would it truly hinder your Shingefu?'

Natsuhiko: 'Ah, what should I do? Being pressed like this, I cannot help but be at a loss as to how to conduct myself.'

Woman: 'Is it not fine as it is? If you treat a woman's sincerity as nothing to this extent, you will eventually exhaust your woman-fortune and be forced to spend your life in a state of celibacy.'

Natsuhiko: 'Ah, a man's heart is fragile to emotion; if you speak so earnestly, I feel that it would not be right to let your kind intentions go to waste. In that case, shall we seal our promise here?'

At this moment, a voice from above the tree shouted, 'Fool!'

Natsuhiko: 'As you can see, Osaku-san, or whatever your name is, someone just shouted "Fool" at my words from above my head. As I thought, I shall withdraw this.'

Woman: 'Do you intend to irresponsibly retract words that a man has once let pass his teeth? If such a thing were possible, you might as well swallow the spittle you have spat out. I will not accept it unless you fulfill this promise, no matter what.'

Natsuhiko: 'I have not yet made a promise. I only said that I might make a promise.'

Woman: 'Before those words left your lips, you had already consented in your heart, had you not?'

§ 4

If people ask, you may answer that no demon exists; but if your heart asks, how will you answer?

Anata: 'Do you intend to deceive your own heart?'

Tatsukuniwake: 'Even if you say so, how could I possibly consent to this? Besides, I will be called a fool from above again.'

Woman: 'Ho ho ho ho, that was said by the Daitengu who always dwells in this forest. Although you are a splendid messenger of the Kamisama, are you really that afraid of one or two Tengu?'

Tatsukuniwake: 'If it were just any Tengu, I would not be afraid, but that Kotoba seems to resonate with my own thoughts, so I must obey it. Please, let this matter pass.'

Woman: 'Ah, men are such weak-spirited creatures. Since it has come to this, there is no other way.'

She suddenly reached out for a handshake.

Tatsukuniwake: 'No matter what you say, please leave this for later.'

Woman: 'No, no, no matter what you say, I will not let you go unless you grant my wish.'

Tatsukuniwake: 'If that is the case, then there is no help for it. Come, I shall proceed to shake your hand.'

Tatsukuniwake stretched out his Mete and tried to grasp Osaku's hand. Contrary to the expectation that Osaku would be pleased,

Osaku: 'Ugh, you filthy Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō, Tatsukuniwake!'

Before she had even finished speaking, she put all her strength into her body and pushed him down to the ground.

Tatsukuniwake: 'This is outrageous. What must I do to please you?'

Osaku: 'How could you, having received the Shinmei and being on your way to the Seifuku of the Magatsu, overturn your firm Ketsushin just because of the earnest plea of a woman? With such a weak soul as yours, how could you ever accomplish the Seifuku of the devil?'

Tatsukuniwake: 'What an incomprehensible way for a woman to behave.'

He folded his arms and sank into thought for a while. Suddenly, at the sound of a roaring thunderclap, he looked up, and the woman from before had vanished without a trace, and he found himself sleeping under the veranda of an old shrine. What he heard as thunder was the sound of the footsteps of an old rat living in the shrine running wild. Tatsukuniwake immediately prostrated himself before the shrine, gave thanks for the lesson of the dream, tempered his heart and soul, and proceeded toward the Seifuku of Takaharuyama.
