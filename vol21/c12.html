Chapter 12: 奇の女 [686]

奇の女〔686〕

§ 1

Tatsukuniwake enters deep into the valley of Ōtaniyama while singing a Sendenka in a low voice.

The sound of the bell announcing the evening rings out, echoing the Shiyogiyōmujiyō. In the sky, thousands of crows cry out languidly, cawing as they seek their roosts. The wind that pierces the body sways the treetops in a Shichigosan pattern. Tatsukuniwake stops by a grove of aged pine trees to shelter from the night's rain and dew, and upon looking to the side, he finds a small shrine.

Tatsukuniwake: 'Ah, how grateful I am; a shrine to some Kamisama stands here. In all likelihood, the Kamisama of the Yamaguchi is enshrined here.'

While murmuring this to himself, he respectfully claps his hands and bows before the shrine, recites the Amatsunorito, sings a Sendenka, and lies down behind the shrine. The song goes:

A Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō, Together with Tamaharuwake and Kuniyoriwake, As messengers of the Kami, Arrived at the shores of Lake Tsuda. As the strength and the staff, Upon which Takayorihime relies, I obtained the secret text by chance, And explored the enemy's positions, As if they were in the palm of my hand. While sneaking through secret paths here, And traveling over mountains and fields, The sun gradually set completely, And as the path ahead became invisible, I stopped quietly at the base of the Senbiki-no-iwa, And while spending the night sheltering from the falling snow, A young woman appeared, Surpassing even the flowers in beauty, Holding a baby to her breast, And as she was shut in by the cold, unable to move her hands or feet, She asked me to give her warmth for the night, And approached close to my side. I, a Sendenshi of the Kami's teachings, Who says I will save this world, Refused with a single word, Saying I must not touch a woman's skin, Until I have brought the Magakami of Takaharuyama to submission and peace. The woman then pressed her hands together again, And begged me to build a fire for her. When I suddenly cast my eyes nearby, I found withered brushwood, perhaps a gift from Heaven, I immediately struck a spark, And when I lit the fire, it blazed up, Making the surroundings as bright as midday. The woman's face became clearly visible, Even her true nature was fully revealed. With arguments of male dominance and female subordination, And debates of female dominance and male subordination, I sharpened my tongue like the Heavenly Nuboko, And fought, scattering sparks. The woman was no ordinary person, and quite formidable, She was not afraid of my Kototama. Just when things looked dangerous, A large man blowing a conch shell, Came advancing, making the echoes of the valleys here and there resound, He suddenly appeared at this place, And glared at the demonic woman. The woman was startled, and threw the child she was holding, Straight into the fire, And fled, vanishing like clouds and mist. I was attacked by sleepiness, And just as I was following the path of dreams, I was shaken awake and opened my eyes, And as I looked around the surroundings, there were, Onibukehiko's Byakkoshin who appeared on Mount Ōe, Followed by Yoriyorihime-kami.

§ 2

Appearing before my eyes, saving me in this emergency, and vanishing from this place along with the music of Meuonbosatsu, Tatsukuniwake remained all alone, dozing off with his back against a rock, waiting for the dawn. The snow fell incessantly, blocking the path and leaving him unable to move freely, trapped in a dead end. Regardless of the situation, he summoned his Aramitama and courage, trudging forward through the snow, when suddenly, he was led by the hand by a mysterious woman who appeared from within the snow, taken to a dilapidated hut, and troubled by being bombarded with various miscellaneous questions, wondering what he should do, when she peeled her eyes wide like umbrellas.

Thinking she had vanished in a flash, she suddenly transformed into an Ōbiyakuko and dashed off, aiming for the mountain path. When he looked closely, how strange it was; the mountain path, where snow had piled up more than five shaku, had vanished, leaving only a thin layer of snow. While holding a heart full of suspicion, he suddenly looked to his side, and to his surprise, he found himself at the foot of a rock, having been following a worthless dream path with his inner eyes closed. Bathed in the light of the Asahi, he stood up from that place and walked away, and just as he was searching for the footprints he had left in the snow, he encountered a Nojishi that came running, trampling through the snow, and for a while, he stood still on the path, gazing at it.

As a white-feathered arrow flew from somewhere, grazing the sky, and struck the head of the boar, the boar was startled, running wild to the right and left, back and forth, crossing over the ridge of the peak, and hiding its body in the valley. Even to the end of birds and beasts, the way of the Lord God is to save and assist; how could he leave it abandoned? Thinking he would help it, he searched for the footprints and went down into the valley. In the bottom of the valley, where reeds grew thick and human footprints were absent, he came following the trail of blood, and seeing a naturally hollowed rock cave, he pitied the corpse of the boar lying beside it, recited the Amatsu Norito, and just as he thought to revive and save it, an Onimusume emerged from the rock cave.

She immediately bit into the boar and drank its blood with a disgusting air. Wondering what this meant, he looked closely and saw that she looked just like O-Hikari, who was born as the daughter of O-Ryu in a village near Mount Takagi. O-Hikari finished drinking the blood, stared intently at his face, and said, 'Are you the uncle from next door?' He replied, 'Are you O-Hikari? Why are you living in hiding in these deep mountains? Go home quickly.' As he urged her, O-Hikari shook her head and said, 'I have grown tired of drinking the blood of the boar. You, who smell so delicious, let me eat you,' and she pressed him. Thinking this was a disaster, he was startled, and as he tried to soothe and appease her, teaching her about duty and human feelings...

Tatsukuniwake awoke, pricked up his ears, and held his breath, wondering who it could be. With the sound of many footsteps, several men brought a woman to this place.

『出遇つた事がない筈、世界の女はお前の姿を見ても、お前の方から風が吹いても、嫌がつて皆逃げて仕舞ふ。それもお前が強いとか、怖いとか云うて逃げるのではない。汚らはしくつて、怪体な臭がして鼻持ちがならないから、化物だと思つて逃げるのですよ』

『仕方のない女だなア。こんな女を連れて帰つて、カーリンスの奥さまにでもしようものなら、俺達の却つて迷惑になるかも知れやしないぞ』

『ナニ決して迷惑にやなりませぬ。キヨロキヨロ間誤ついて居ると、ちよいちよい長煙管がお前等のお頭にお見舞申す位なものだよ。けれども生命には別条はないから安心なさい、ホヽヽヽヽ』

『女子と小人は養ひ難し、到底弁舌では俺達は敗軍だ。不言実行に限る。サア各自に手足を取り、高春山に帰つてゆかう。これや女、何んぼ頤が達者でも直接行動には叶ふまい、男は口は下手だが実地の力は強いぞ』

『ホヽヽヽヽ、たつた一人の繊弱い女に対し見つともない、五人も六人も一丈の褌をかいた荒男が、蚯蚓を蟻が寄つて集つて巣へ引き込むやうにせねば、一人の女を引捉へて、その目的を達する事が出来ないとは、何と男程困つたものはないものだなア』

『偉さうに云ふない、男は裸百貫と云つて、体が一つあれば世の中に立派な一人前の大丈夫として通用するのだ。女は蔭ものだ。もつと女らしく淑やかにしたら如何だい。女の徳は柔順にあるのだぞ』

『私は柔順なんか大の嫌ひだ。私の女としての徳は柔術だ。一つ見本にお前達六人を、お月さまの世界迄も放りあげて見ようか』

『オイ皆の奴、如何しようかなア。こんな女を迂濶連れて帰らうものなら、何んな大騒動が勃発するか知れたものぢやないぞ』

『それだと云つて、連れて帰らねばカーリンスの親方に合す顔がなし、困つた事になつたものだなア』

『一つ柔術をお目にかけませうかな、オホヽヽヽ』

『エヽ、この上は直接行動だ。乙、丙、丁、戊、己、一度にかかれ』

『ヨシ、合点だ』

と六人の男は手取り足取り無理に女を担ぎ行かむとする。女は又もやキヤツ　キヤツと頻りに叫ぶ。祠の後より、

『暫く待てツ、大自在天大国別命これにあり、申し渡す仔細がある』

と雷の如く呶鳴りつけた。六人は女を其場に投げ捨て雲を霞と逃げ散つた。女は静々と神前に詣で、拍手しながら何事か暗祈黙祷をして居る。
