鬼娘〔685〕

§ 1

Hakusetsu gaigai to shimen no sanya, shironuno no shitone o kabutta gotoku manmokushōzen to shite itsuten no chiri o todomezu, michi tou hito mo ara kaze ni mukatte susumu Tatsukuniwake no sendenshi wa, kokukoku ni furitsumoru yuki o fumiwake, yōyaku tani to tani to no hosoki jifujirotō ni idetari.

Taiyō wa yukigumo ni shikira rete hikari o chiukū ni tsutsumi, tōzainanboku no haukau sae mo wakaranaku natte kita. Hi wa yōyaku yukigumo no seiten ni bosshita to miete, nan to wa nashi ni usugura samushi. Saredo yuki no hikari ni shimen wa tsukiyo no gotoku mōrō to hikatte iru. Tatsukuniwake wa goshiyaku iuyo no yuki ni tozasare shintai kiwamatte, mohaya tōshi sen bakari ni kurushimi tsutsu, Kototama no tsuzuku kagiri Amatsunorito o sōjiyō shi, yo no akuru o matsu no yamu naki hame to wa narinu.

Wazuka ichiri bakari no yamaji ni ichinichi o tsuiyashitaru o mireba, ikani tsūro no konnan narishi ka o sassuru ni amari ari. Masshiro no yuki no naka yori shiroki kage, mukumuku to fukuredashi, ikkai no shiroki tachisugata to natte Tatsukuniwake ga tatazumu mae ni araware kitari, mono o mo iwazu tsumetaki te o nobashite, Tatsukuniwake no te o tori susunde yuku.

Tatsukuniwake wa kokoro no naka ni ayashimi nagara, mohaya ikan tomo suru koto atawazu, kaibutsu ni te o hikaretaru mama ni fuan no nen ni kararete susumi yuku. Kaibutsu wa koyama no fumoto no araya no naka ni, Tatsukuniwake o izanōta.

Tatsukuniwake: 'Ikanaru kata ka zonjimasenu ga, yuki ni tozasare miugoki mo narazu, konnan no tokoro e oide kudasaimashite, sayō na anzen chitai e go-annai kudasaimashita no wa, makoto ni arigatō zonjimasu.'

Kaibutsu: 'Nanji wa Mukadehime no buka ka, mata wa Kotoyoriwake-no-Mikoto no buka naru ka, hentō shidai de wareware ni mo hitotsu no kangae ga aru kara, teki ka, mikata ka kikashite hoshii.'

Tatsukuniwake: 'Sayū iu anata wa, nan to iu gofujin de gozaimasu ka.'

Kaibutsu: 'Anata no hentō o kiku made mōsumai. Wareware no teki naraba ima koko de, nanji o seibatsu shinakereba narazu, moshi mikata naraba doko made mo tasukeru kakugo da yo. Saa, Baramon-kyō ka Sangyō-kyō ka, futatsu ni hitotsu no hentō o kikimashō.'

Tatsukuniwake: 'Tatoe Baramon-kyō demo, Sangyō-kyō demo makoto no michi ni kawari wa arimasenu. Watashi wa makoto no michi no sendenshi desu. Sangyō-kyō, Baramon-kyō, mata Arupusu-kyō to iu yō na kubun shita meishō ni, amari omoki o oite wa orimasenu.'

Kaibutsu: 'Sore wa dare shimo onaji koto. Shikashi ima nanji no zokushite iru kyōha wa, nan-kyō da to tazunete iru no da.'

Speaker: 'Are you not a Sendenshi who saves people? While you are in a Shiyokumu where you must abandon your own self to save others, you leave us who are currently starving to die, and as long as your own belly is full, is that enough? Do you still think you are a Sendenshi who saves people? You are a fake who deceives this world. You call us Bakemono, but you are the true Bakemono. Although you wear the skin of a human, your Mitama is no different from a four-legged beast.'

Speaker: 'Oi, I do not know if you are a fox or a Tanuki, but is there such an arrogant way of speaking when begging for food from others?'

Speaker: 'Who said I wanted the food possessed by a filthy human like you? Excuse me, but we do not act like beggars. There is only one thing I want. It will be fine if you give me that.'

Speaker: 'What on earth is it that you say you want?'

Speaker: 'It is nothing else. I want you to shave off the tip of that high nose of yours.'

Speaker: 'This nose is a precious item entrusted to me by my parents. This, at least, I cannot give you.'

Speaker: 'Then there is something I want to exchange. Since I am exchanging a large thing for a small thing, if anything, I am the one who loses quite a bit, but it is an unwritten law of modern humans that the one who makes the request provides two or three times the actual value, so although my thing is large, I want you to exchange it with me.'

Speaker: 'How could I possibly exchange it for an eight-mat size? It would be a hindrance to walking and I could not bear it.'

Speaker: 'Ahahaha, humans are filthy things, are they not? You immediately turn your thoughts to such places. What I am requesting is not such a thing. Your eyes are too small, so you are a Bakemono equivalent to a blind person who cannot see what lies ahead. Therefore, I am asking you to exchange my eyes for your eyes.'

§ 3

If you were to press such large eyes against my face, one would be enough to fill it entirely.

I hear there is a monster called a one-eyed Kozō. Your soul is perfectly suited for that. Since your spirit and body are in unity, let us exchange them.

Wouldn't one eye be too many?

You could attach the remaining one to the back of your head. That would be convenient, as you would be able to see both front and back clearly. Come, shall I forcefully pluck out your eyeballs and swap them?

I am a Ningensama. These eyes of mine are quite sufficient for my needs. I must decline this exchange.

I am one who does not retreat once I have spoken. In that case, there is no help for it. I shall take your rotten soul.

Do not talk nonsense. My soul is a Suishiyōdama. Where is it rotten? Are you blind, with those large eyes of yours wide open?

Are you not that creature who turned into a four-legged beast at the mansion of Matsuhime at the foot of Takashiroyama? You should know my true nature.

Well now, who on earth are you? You seem to know a great deal about me.

Ahahaha.

As she laughed and swiftly removed her head covering, it was none other than Matsuhime, beyond any doubt.

Oh, you are Matsuhime-sama, you certainly do play cruel pranks. My heart nearly jumped out of my chest.

Ohohoho, do I look like Matsuhime to you? That is precisely why I said I would exchange those eyeballs of yours.

Then who are you?

I am one called Yukihime, who has manifested from the spirit of snow.

Does snow have a spirit, I wonder?

As long as the sun of Truth does not shine across the sky of your heart, the mystery of this Yukihime cannot be solved. With a soul as clouded as today's sky, poor thing, you cannot even see properly.

Because you appeared with eyes like balls, I thought you were a Matokiri-tanuki monster. Can Yukihime really transform into such various forms?

When you are at a dead end and swing a stick in frustration, even you would likely open your eyes wide in distress, would you not? That is why I said you would encounter such large, harsh eyes. If you wish for one more, shall I open my large eyes and show you?

No, that is quite enough. It is a terrible nuisance, so I shall refrain from any more.

How about this?

Saying this, she opened her eyes wide, like a snake-eye umbrella, to show him. As Tatsukuniwake was startled and fell backward, Yukihime instantly turned into a white fox and ran off, dashing down the mountain path at full speed.

§ 4

Ah, what a great surprise that was. Now that the true form of that Dogitsune fellow is known, there is nothing to fear. If I do not pursue him and bring him to his Ōjiyō, I cannot fulfill my role as a Sendenshi. However, in this heavy Ōyuki, while a Yotsuashi might walk well, it is a bit difficult for a Ningen.

As he spoke this Dokugo and looked around, the snow, which he thought had piled up to over six Rokushiyaku, had only Itsusun of fox Ashikata upon it.

Tatsukuniwake folded his arms and sat down heavily.

Ah, what is this? That fox fellow has bewitched me. I thought I had spent Ichinichi breaking my bones and advancing along the deep Yukimichi, but it was just the same Ihakage as before. It seems something is wrong with me. Well, I shall now recite the Amatsunorito, receive the Goshinriki of the Ōkami, and proceed forward. I have had an unexpected dream in an unexpected place. It seems the night has broken. If I follow these fox footprints, I should be able to find the path.

Saying this, he relied on the footprints marked in the Hakusetsu and marched forward.

When he thought he had advanced about seven or eight chō, a wild boar appeared and crossed in front of him.

How strange. First I thought there were fox footprints, and now a boar. I might even encounter a real tanuki fellow next.

Just as he stood there, a stray arrow whistled through the air from nowhere, grazed over his head, and struck the face of the boar with a thud.

The boar panicked and rolled about, finally crossing a bald mountain and hiding its form in the valley beyond.

Ah, what a pitiful thing. It is our role to save even birds and beasts. Perhaps the Kami is trying to draw our attention. It would be terrible to abandon this. Fortunately, I must search for the boar's dwelling by following the footprints left in the Hakusetsu, perform Chinkon, and save it. Also, if I do not pull out that arrow, it will surely not survive. In any case, saving this creature is the priority.

He climbed up the bald mountain with a steady pace.

This place was the foot of Ōtani-yama, a grassy plain overgrown with pampas grass called Iwagaya, where no human had ever set foot. As he followed the bloodstains that had fallen here and there, he found a rock cave, and the aforementioned boar had already expired.

Tatsukuniwake: 'Haha, so this was the boar's dwelling. Still, it is fortunate for the boar that it did not die on the way but managed to return to its own home before collapsing. I shall bury it deeply somewhere here.'

§ 5

He picked up a stone of a moderate size and began to dig the earth with a clattering sound.

From within the rock cave, a woman of twenty-five or six, with horns growing from her head, came out smiling broadly and immediately clung to the carcass of the wild boar, beginning to suck its blood. As Tatsukuniwake watched this scene in silence, the Onimusume, having finished sucking every drop of blood from the body with a smacking sound as if it were delicious, stroked her belly with both hands. It appeared she had just noticed Tatsukuniwake standing there, for she widened her eyes, pursed her lips, and glared at him with a terrifying intensity, saying:

Onimusume: 'Ya! Are you not Tatsuwaka?'

Tatsukuniwake replied:

Tatsukuniwake: 'Oi, are you not Omitsu? Your parents did not know your whereabouts and were worried sick every single day. They even made pilgrimages to the mansion on Takashiroyama of the Uranai-kyo many times, but since they could not find you no matter what, they set the day you disappeared as the anniversary of your death and held a grand funeral, at which I even served as a ritualist. Come now, hurry home to your parents' house. It is unbearable for you to become an Onimusume in a place like this. I am on my way to Takaharuyama for a subjugation mission, and while it would be easy to take you along, I am not permitted to travel with a woman. Why do you not hurry home now?'

Onimusume: 'Look here, Tatsuwaka-san. When I was shaving my grandmother's eyebrows, I accidentally cut her face. So much blood flowed out that I was startled and licked it, and ever since then, I acquired a taste for raw blood. I began to crave the blood of humans and beasts, and in the end, horns grew on my head like this, and I became an Onimusume. How could I return to my home in such a state? Now that you know where I am, you will surely go back to the village and talk about it. If you do that, it will be the end of me, so for the sake of protecting my own body, I must, however regretfully, take your life. Prepare yourself.'

Tatsukuniwake: 'Do not say such unreasonable things. Surely you know something of gratitude. Do you not remember that when you were a child, this uncle of yours carried you on his back, held you in his arms, let you urinate, and even cleaned up your excrement? Can you really say you will eat me, even out of a sense of duty for that? One who does not know gratitude is not a human. Is it not said that even a crow has the filial piety to feed its parents, and a dog never forgets the master who fed it for three days for its entire life?'

§ 6

Onimusume: 'If you understand such things, how can you become an Onimusume? If you concern yourself with the Giri and Ninjiyō of the world, you cannot complete the Shiugiyō of an ogre. Come now, Oji-san, meeting me here is the end of your luck. Ah, the scent of delicious blood is in the air. I am sorry, but I shall partake of you.'

Tatsukuniwakenomikoto: 'Hey, hey, I am different from the Tatsuwaka of the past. I am Tatsukuniwakenomikoto, favored by Kotoyoriwakenomikoto-sama, the Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō with infinite Shinriki. If you do something foolish, you will be cast into the bottom of Jigoku and meet the Seibai of the ogres. Do you not fear the Mirai?'

Onimusume: 'Ho ho ho ho, if an Onimusume fears the ogres of Jigoku, what will become of her? Even if I go to Jigoku, they will say a splendid Onimusume has arrived and I will be Motehaya. My Guwanbau is to become an ogre. If I drink your Ikichi, I can become an even more splendid Onimusume. I have already grown tired of drinking the Ikichi of boars.'

Tatsukuniwakenomikoto: 'Are you saying that you will eat me no matter what? Or rather, are you saying you will drink my blood?'

Onimusume: 'If I do not drink it, my Karada will burn. I am suffering too, so I have no time to reflect on Giri and Ninjiyō. However, since you have taken such good care of me, I feel a sense of pity for you, though I know not why. I shall drink only a little and spare you.'

She then took a stone of a convenient size and struck the forehead of Tatsukuniwake with a sharp clatter. Tatsukuniwake lost consciousness and collapsed on the spot.

Onimusume: 'Ah, poor Oji-san. If I drink too much, your life will be in danger, so I shall drink only a sho or two and spare you.'

She placed her mouth to the wound and began to drink with a slurping sound.

Onimusume: 'Ah, what is this? This blood is acrid. It has quite a bitter taste. It seems your Mitama is clouded, and perhaps that is why even your flowing blood tastes bad. Come now, Oji-san, I shall spare you now. Wake up.'

Being shaken awake, Tatsukuniwake returned to his senses.

Tatsukuniwake: 'Ah, somehow I feel refreshed. My Karada has suddenly become light, and even my eyes have become clear.'

Onimusume: 'I have sucked out the evil spirits that were passing through your blood vessels, so you have now become nothing but fine, red, sincere blood. From here on, you are beyond my reach. You have become the pure essence of the Yamato-damashii. However, you must not tell anyone about this. If you do, my ears will hear it even from a thousand ri away; I will fly through the void and pull off your head, so be prepared for that. It would be easy to kill you here, but because I have the weakness of having been helped by you, even a wicked Onimusume like me cannot do anything. However, if you make a promise with me now and break it, then for the first time you will have committed the sin of breaking a vow, and at that time I will go to take your life with full justification, so please return with that resolve. With this, you should be able to carry out the conquest of Takaharu-yama splendidly. In human affairs, all is like the horse of the old man at the frontier. Because you were treated harshly by me, you will end up showing true merit as a result; so to speak, I am like your guardian deity. Be grateful.'

『そんな事が解つて居つて、如何して鬼娘になれますか。世の中の義理や、人情を構つて居つたら、鬼の修行は出来るものぢやない。サア小父さん、此処で逢うたがお前の運の尽きだ。アヽ美味さうな血の香がして居る。済まないけれどよばれませう』

『コラコラ俺は今迄の竜若とは違ふぞ。神力無限の三五教の宣伝使、言依別命様の御覚え芽出度き竜国別命ぢや。馬鹿な事を致すと地獄のどん底へ落されて、鬼の成敗に遇はねばならぬぞ。未来を怖れぬか』

『ホヽヽヽヽ、鬼娘が地獄の鬼が恐くて、如何なりませう。これでも地獄へ行つたら、立派な鬼娘が来たと云つて、持囃されるのだ。私は鬼になるのが願望だ。お前の生血を吸へば、もう一層立派な鬼娘になれる。猪の生血は最早呑み飽いたから』

と手頃の石を以て、竜国別の額をカツカツと打つた。竜国別は気が遠くなり、其の場に倒れた。

『アヽ気の毒な小父さんだ。沢山呑むと生命が危いから、一二升許り呑んでこらへて上げよう』

と傷口に口を当て、チウチウと呑み始めた。

『アヽ如何したものか、此の血はエグイ。なかなか苦味がある。矢張身魂が曇つて居ると見えて、流れる血まで味が悪いのかなア。サアサア小父さん、モーこらへて上げよう。起きなさい』

と揺り起されて竜国別は吾に帰り、

『アヽなんだか気分がスイツとした。俄に身体が軽くなり、目までハツキリして来たやうだ』

『お前の血管を通つて居る悪霊を薩張吸うて上げたのだから、モーお前さんは結構な赤い誠の血計りになつて了つたのだよ。此上は最早私の手に合はぬ。お前さんは愈大和魂の生粋になつて了つた。併し此事を誰にも話してはなりませぬぞや。話すが最後千里向かふからでも私の耳は聞えるから、宙を駆つて行き、お前の素首を引抜くから、其の覚悟でゐて下さいや。此処で殺すのは易いけれど、お前に助けられたと云ふ弱味があるので、如何に悪党な鬼娘でも如何する事も出来ない。併し私と今約束して、それを破れば、初めてお前さんに破約の罪が出来たのだから、其時は堂々と生命を取りに行くから、其の覚悟で帰つて下さい。これで高春山の征伐も立派に出来るだらう。人間万事塞翁の馬だ。私に酷い目に遇はされて、其結果誠の手柄を現はす様になるので、謂はば私はお前さんの守護神のやうなものだ。感謝しなさい』

'What a strange Rikutsu that is. To be struck on the head and have one's blood sucked, only to be grateful for it—I have never heard of such a thing since the Kaibiyaku. I wonder where the Kanjiyō went so wrong.'

Speaker: 'It is a settled matter. When the place changes, the goods change; when the house changes, the customs change; when you enter a village, you follow the village. In this Sekai, there are even countries that take a cut from Bakuchi to assist the national Kuwaikei, allowing the Kokumin to live in Anraku. On the other hand, there are countries where Bakuchi is considered a Zaiaku; thus, when the Shiyakuwai differs, the Heujiyun of Zenaku also differs. However, no matter where you go, breaking a Yakusoku is a Zaiaku. Did not Amaterasuōkami-sama and Susanōnomikoto-mikoto perform a Seiyaku with the Ama no Yasu no Kahara between them? In this world, without distinction between Genkai, Iukai, and Shinkai, keeping a Yakusoku is the most Taisetsu thing. To break it is the Kotsuchiyō of Zaiaku. You, too, must make a Seiyaku that you will not tell anyone of my Arika. If not, I shall take your life here and now.'

Ryūkokuwake: 'What, I am not the sort to have my life taken by the likes of you, but even if I were to take your life, it would be of no use. In short, it would only make me a criminal, so let us compromise here and make a Yakusoku that neither of us will speak of it.'

Omitsu: 'If that is the case, I shall spare you. Be off with you at once. You will surely not speak of it, will you?'

Ryūkokuwake: 'Un, I am a man. I will surely keep my Yakusoku, so rest assured.'

Omitsu: 'Sayōnara.'

With those words, she vanished deep into the shadows of the rocky cavern.

Ryūkokuwake, while stroking the wound on his forehead, returned to the path he had originally taken, and from there, turning to the left, he walked dejectedly up a narrow path overgrown with withered grass.
