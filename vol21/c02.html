Chapter 2: Dream の懸 Bridge [676]

夢の懸橋〔676〕

§ 1

Takahime and Kurohime, having departed the Seichi in high spirits aboard the Ama no Ihakusubune to bring Takayorihime, the Kyōshiyu of the Alps Kyō—a sect of Brahmanism entrenched in Takaharuyama—to submission and peace in accordance with the command of Kotoyoriwakenomikoto, have been missing for nearly three months without a single Shōsoku. Kotoyoriwakenomikoto secretly summoned three Sendenshi—Tatsukuniwake, Tamaharuwake, and Kuniyoriwake—and, without revealing it to anyone in the Seichi, ordered them to travel to Takaharuyama to investigate the whereabouts of the two. Tatsukuniwake is the new name of Tatsuwaka, who formerly served at the Matsuhimeyakata of Takashiroyama. Tamaharuwake is the new name of Tagosaku, and Kuniyoriwake is the new name of Munehiko.

The flowers of the teaching are fragrant, Blooming in the foothills of Okufuse, Bowing down before the Nishiki no Miya, That towers at the mountain's base, The three, secretly obeying, The command of Kotoyoriwake, Bathed in the light of the moon, Advance with courage to Ishihara Station, Through Nagatano and Haji in the night, Straddling chestnut horses, With the brave sound of hooves, In the morning breeze of Fukuchiyama, With sails set to the wind, pooting, While the lean horses pass gas, Looking left and right at Aonogahara, Running through Kuroimura, With hearts eager at Isō Station, Revering the sacred teaching, they cross, The rice fields of Kashiwabara and advance.

This place is the entrance to Shinchi-chiyama, the gateway to the ten-ri square area held under the influence of Takayorihime of the Alps Kyō. There is a natural rock bridge spanning from valley to valley, known as the Oni no Kakehashi. It is a perilous location, and one cannot possibly advance to Takaharuyama without passing through here.

A stone bridge naturally spanning the summit of a mountain of unknown height, the water of the valley stream flowing below echoes with a murmuring sound in all directions, striking one with a sense of desolation. As soon as Tamaharuwake arrived before this bridge, he let out a frantic cry:

Tamaharuwake: 'Yo, this is a fortress of impregnable defense. Those Alps Kyō fellows have certainly chosen a fine spot for a checkpoint. My love is the Oni no Kakehashi of the deep valley river; it is terrifying to cross, yet if I do not cross, I cannot meet the old hag Takayorihime, whom I yearn for.'

While he was spouting this nonsense, he had proceeded about halfway across. By some strange turn of events, the long stone bridge crumbled and broke easily from the middle, and along with the bridge, Tamaharuwake tumbled into the deep valley, falling with a splash into the foaming abyss.

Tatsukuniwake and Kuniyoriwake were terrified by this incident.

Tatsukuniwake and Kuniyoriwake: 'Ya, Kuni-san, what shall we do, what shall we do?'

They looked at each other's faces, struck by the waves of astonishment.

'I felt that today was a day of bad Kibun for no reason, so I let the horse go from the village of Iso, and the three of us have come walking like this, but it was Ketsukō after all. If I had been riding a horse, both the horse and Tamaharuwake would have perished.'

'What are you saying? I could resign myself if a horse died, but it would be useless if the essential Tamaharuwake were dropped into the Tanisoko. We must think of something. It would be pitiful for Tamaharuwake to be treated the same as a horse.'

'Ah, that is true. I was so surprised and Urota. Come, let us go downstream, for he must be Shiyukuhaku on some Ganseki somewhere, and we must search for his Nikutai.'

They turned back quickly. Tatsukuniwake followed behind, and after Hikikahe for about Shigochiyō, they began to Mekuba and Sōsaku here and there along the Tanigaha.

No matter how much they searched, there was neither shadow nor form. The Futari were at a loss, having no Tedate to take, and were spending their time in tears of regret. From about two or three Chō downstream, there was someone calling, 'Oi, oi.'

The Futari said, 'Strange, that is a Kototama I recognize.'

They ran toward the direction of the voice.

When they looked, Tamaharuwake was on the terrace of a large Ganseki hotel standing in the Tanigaha, wringing out his clothes with all his might.

'Oh, are you not Tamaharuwake? Has anything unusual happened?'

Tamaharuwake: 'Something very unusual has happened. Because a magnificent propagandist of the world passed by, that large stone bridge broke so easily, and suddenly the propagandist of Tamaharuwake performed a skillful aerial glide of several thousand Jō, landed safely in the water, was immediately carried by the water of the Tanigaha for about ten Chō downstream, and now I am a laundry-granny changing into a man, in the middle of pressing my wet clothes, ahahaha.'

'Oi, you cannot be the real Tamaharuwake. Falling from such a high stone bridge and plunging into the abyss of the Tanisoko, there is no way you could be looking so calm. You must be a transformation. Oi Tatsukuniwake, does this not seem suspicious to you?'

'Ah, that is right. That fellow must be some kind of transformation.'

They immediately began to spit on their eyebrows.

'In reality, Tamaharuwake died. I thought that along with the large Ganseki, I had fallen, my body had been smashed to pieces, and the flowing blood had dyed the valley water crimson, suddenly becoming a river of blood in an Inferno, but look, I am in such good health. Oi, you two of Ryu and Kuni, there is no bridge, but try jumping from the end of that bridge once; it is quite pleasant.'

§ 9

Upon hearing this song, Umeteruhiko was startled and rushed to the front gate, bowing his head to the sand.

Umeteruhiko: 'Was it you, Sendenshi-sama? Truly, this servant has been clumsy, and I have no excuse. Come, please, do come in.'

Tamaharuwake: 'No, thank you. If a beggar were to enter such a magnificent mansion, it would affect the honor of the house, so I shall take my leave for today.'

Umeteruhiko: 'Your anger is entirely justified, but I have been most rude... It is entirely the fault of this servant, so please, I beg you to forgive me. Come, come, please put aside your displeasure and step inside quickly. Hey, Umeteruhime, Harukō, will you not offer your apologies?'

He shouted this. The two were startled by this voice and, though they did not understand the situation, seeing Umeteruhiko prostrating himself, they likewise bowed their heads to the earth.

Tamaharuwake: 'Just now you said it was the servant who was at fault. It is by no means the servant. The responsibility lies with the master after all. It is the first error to have such a foolish man who lacks awareness as a gatekeeper.'

Umeteruhiko: 'Yes, no matter what you say, I have no words of excuse.'

Tamaharuwake: 'Well, it is fine now that you understand. Tamaharuwake, Kuniyorawake, shall we trouble them?'

Saying this, he took the lead and entered. The two also smiled and said,

Tamaharuwake and Kuniyorawake: 'Ah, we have caused you great worry. With this, all the circumstances are wiped clean. Come, Umeteruhiko-sama and your wife, and Haru-sama, please be at ease.'

Umeteruhiko, Umeteruhime, and Harukō: 'Thank you very much.'

With this, they stroked their chests in relief and followed the three into the interior together with his wife. Harukō stood by the gate and said,

Harukō: 'Ah, that was the greeting of Niwachō-sama. Thanks to that, it seems I have somehow avoided being dismissed.'
