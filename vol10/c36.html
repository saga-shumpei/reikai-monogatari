Chapter 36: Beyond All Expectation [466]

§ 1

Hebitsukami of Mount Altai; blown and scattered by the kototama of Umegakahime-no-Kami-no-Mikoto, blooming all at once, Magatsumi ran high into the sky, pointed southwest toward Armenia, and fled away like clouds and mist. Afterward, the two apostles returned, bringing along with them Tokikō, whose heart was pounding. When they came before the mansion of Kuroganehiko in Kuroganedani Village, Tokikō, who had been completely dispirited and utterly weakened until this very moment, squared his shoulders and stuck out his elbows, saying: "Behold my prowess! O people of Kuroganedani Village, I am Tokinogawa, the dog that ate wild boar, an old hand who still knows his trade! Helplessly bound by the times and seasons,

though I had demeaned myself as the gatekeeper of Kuroganehiko-sama, at last the revolving tide-wind blows and blows and swirls about. The truly powerful Hebitsukami, I grasped with one hand, and slap-slap dashed against a rock and tore to shreds; setting my right and left hands to the fellow's upper and lower jaws, when I exerted myself with a grunt, just as an eel is severed, it split left and right with a cracking rip! Do you know the proverb that says, 'Submit to the reign of the times'? Come now, from this moment on, Master Toki holds the realm of the times, is the lord! Even Kuroganehiko, head of Kuroganedani Village, is no match for me at all; and Kuroganehime cannot equal me! Without fail, from now on, toward this side,

you must not turn your back on Tokinogawa! The realm of the times, I shall rule. Magistrate of the times, governor of the day, it is a stroke of luck for Toki! Now from this moment on, Hebitsukami, the evil spirit of Mount Altai, as long as this Master Toki exists, has no precedent of appearing again. Look well upon this prowess of an unprecedented hero! The reign will be peaceful and tranquil, as Master Tokikō goes on to rule. 'Dokkoisho, dokkoisho!' 'Untoko dokkoi, dokkoisho!' 'Yoitoko yoitoko, yoitokosa!' 'Yoitasano, yōitosa!'"

So saying, waving his hands and treading in the roppō style, he chattered away, flung open Kuroganehiko's gate with a clatter, and entered.

Since the villagers had crowded into the chief's mansion today as well, waiting to see how matters stood on Mount Altai, they were seized with half-belief and half-doubt upon hearing this boastful talk, and many appeared who rejoiced, frowned, or stood blankly amazed. Kuroganehiko and his wife hurriedly appeared before the apostles together with their daughter Kiyohime, and said:

"Ah, you have had a hard journey. How did things go? All of us, beginning with ourselves, waited with craned necks and pale faces, wondering how you were faring. Please let us hear the situation quickly."

"Suddenly swaggering with broad shoulders, blowing large boasts and wrapping matters in baffling smoke with relentless retorts: Ah, Kanahiko, ah, Kanahime, and Kiyohime of pure heart! Set your minds at ease and peace. Kanatani Village, saved by the teachings of the Kami of Ananai, is still foolish. At this poem, Kanahiko and all the others finally felt relief, felt the divine virtue of the Ananai teaching, and deeply thanked the chivalrous spirit of the two missionaries; thereupon, the villagers gathered and held a lively and celebratory feast.

Presently, the parlor of Kanahiko was opened, a grand banquet was held, and Kanahiko stood up and recited a poem to express his gratitude:

"True missionaries of the Kami of Ananai who save this world! True-hearted divine ministers, firmer than rock, who save the people of the world! Aided by the grace of Ishikoridome-no-Mikoto and Umegakahime-no-Mikoto, higher than the peaks of Mount Altai and deeper than the sea, the great divine majesty has cleared away not only the life of one daughter, Kiyohime, but every misfortune of the people in all lands. Might you, Mikoto, be Kami who poled across the riverbed of heaven and descended? Ah, how grateful, how grateful! Striving to repay this deep grace, this modest mat is laid; jars of wine are piled high, and a hundred varieties of tree fruits are offered before you like a ridge of hills,

Accept here and now the full measure of our hearts, O missionaries. Drink the fruit wine quietly and deeply, as if reddening like ears of grain. Ah, all people, all people! The Kami of salvation are the two pillars of the true path of Ananai. Comparing them to heaven and earth, serve them day and night, revere them day and night. Ah, how noble is the grace of the Kami! Ah, how noble is the grace of my lords! Even if heaven and earth should change, even until the end of the flourishing Miroku, this divine grace that saved my daughter shall not be forgotten. Nevertheless, ah, I recall Teruhime, who was born three years ago as the elder sister of Kiyohime,

The sorrow of her having been cursed and ruined by an evil deity! If the two pillars had appeared here three years ago, ah, Teruhime too would have been safely saved like Kiyohime. Over and over it is terrifying, over and over it is sorrowful. O Ishikoridome-no-Kami, O Umegakahime-no-Kami, do not deem me a foolish parent for dwelling on what cannot be undone. In this world, there is no treasure that surpasses one's own child. Ah, nevertheless, nevertheless, that which is more noble than that is the teaching of the Kami, the divine path. Ah, from now on, let us take the teaching of the path of Ananai as our treasure and guide the people of all directions.

Pray, pray, simply pray! Pray well before the Kami of Ananai who save this world!"

Thus facing the two missionaries, he expressed his gratitude with tears. From this time, in order to repay the divine grace, Kanahiko became an attendant to Umegakahime and was to advance toward Armenia.
