Chapter 7: An Unexpected Honor [437]

第7章　思はぬ光栄〔437〕

§ 1

Down from the Rocky Mountains blew the mountain wind, and under the driving rain, hundreds of rivers surged, overflowed, and roared, flowing northwestward. Amidst the sound of that water like the Takano River, and the rough waves that stirred the hearts of the divine beings of Tokoyo-no-kuni, Tokoyo-shin'o at the head, followed by Takatoriwake of the crushed nose, and Miyamawake who had suffered intense distress, having returned, after that:

Nakayoriwake summoned the gatekeepers Kamihiko and Akaguma before the garden and said,

"Hear me, Kamihiko and Akaguma: as gatekeepers, for today's disgraceful failure and for the crime of letting Teruhiko escape, I dismiss you as of this moment. Depart from this place at once!"

Takayamahiko said, "Well, well, I cannot think these are the words of Lord Nakayoriwake. Today's mismanagement is not something of which petty gatekeepers could have known. Was it not you yourself, Nakayoriwake, charged with supervision, who allowed the precious jewel to escape? Taking out your anger on gatekeepers such as Kamihiko and Akaguma is utterly incomprehensible to us. If you have any conscience, you should first take responsibility and resign yourself."

Kamihiko said, "Hear, hear! Truly great! Splendid indeed, General Takayamahiko! It is precisely by standing upstream of the people that your generous, magnanimous virtue can govern them; Kamihiko is thoroughly awed. Look here, Nakayoriwake, if you have any conscience, you ought first to take responsibility and resign!"

Takayamahiko said, "Hold on, Kamihiko! For one of your lowly status as a gatekeeper, it is insolent to speak thus toward Nakayoriwake, a superior of renown."

Kamihiko said, "Well, well, General Takayamahiko, among the many superiors present in Tokoyo Castle, Nakayoriwake is, above all others, a fellow completely beyond reason, heedless of anything. I pray you render an impartial and upright judgment."

"What an entertaining creature you are, speaking in a way ill-befitting a mere gatekeeper. Yet until now, wagging your head and fawning with your tail like a dog of a house in mourning, you obeyed him submissively as your superior; toward Nakayoriwake, is this not far too opportunistic?"

"Well, they say that in everything one must follow the power of the day. Backing the side whose banners show favor is the way of the world. Now that, by a single word from Takayamahiko, the usually arrogant and insolent Nakayoriwake has been forced to resign, it is not merely I, Kamihiko; every single subordinate throughout the castle will clap their hands in joy. Indeed, it is most gratifying! As punishment for committing such harsh misdeeds, he panicked, and when you told him to leave at once, his face was not something one could bear to look at. Watching from beside him, I felt my heartburn slide down smoothly, as though I had swallowed a bowl of noodles. As the saying goes, the proud do not endure for long. This too is an example for the world: Nakayoriwake, a junk official sifted out from the very bottom, a dregs-official left over after husking rice from the grain! If I, Kamihiko, were to give one snort, he would blow away ten ken. Please, General Takayamahiko, I beg you to make your swift, resolute decision without delay. It is not just I who will rejoice; from now on, all the officials throughout the castle will leap with joy, tumble, fall over, slide, jump, land on their bottoms, drool, and..."

"Hey, hey, what are you chattering on about without end? Hold your tongue."

"Yes, yes, please forgive me. Becoming overly agitated, the crab blew a little foam. Speaking of foam, it reminds me. The one who panicked was Nakayoriwake; what a pitiable figure. The past, before suffering such harsh misfortune, was far better, yet that dim-witted, pitch-black, charred knave met with failure unexpectedly fast. A midday lantern, a bean-paste rice cake; what serves in the dark of night is the light of lanterns and oil lamps. In the castle of Tokoyo, there surely is not the slightest need for a midday lantern. Whether it be fate or karma, because of the karma of this fraud who hatched a gloomy, somber, insidious plot, he now has suffered a great hammer strike falling grievously upon his head, entirely unexpected even in dreams, completely resigning his former post, and twisting his face in an unprecedented grimace—how pitiful. Please send him home quickly. Neither in ancient times nor now has an evil person ever prospered. Nakayoriwake, that reckless warrior, had best return home as soon as possible and go into retirement. As punishment for meddling in needless affairs, he finds himself in an untenable position in this place—a vessel of Magatsu, black as soot, a creature of pure darkness."

"Ahahaha, what a fellow you are for blowing bubbles. Very well, very well; from this moment forth, in accordance with the regulations of Tokoyo Castle, Nakayoriwake is ordered to resign. Consequently, his subordinate Kanihiko shares the same guilt."

"Excuse me, is there not a slight mistake in that? Hey, Akaguma, if I get dismissed, you will meet the same fate."

Akaguma: "Tsk, what are you spouting? You do nothing but parrot others; even if you try to sell fakes, no one will buy them. Ooh, what an unlucky knave you are. The command leaves no room to move; do not dawdle, go home quickly. I alone am enough for the iron gate of Tokoyo Castle. If a bubble-blowing rascal like you hangs around, even I will end up being told to leave and will suffer the collateral damage. Ugh, how repulsive. You had best go home quickly."

Kanihiko: "Hey, if you speak so arrogantly, now that it has come to this, we are no longer friends; you are an absolute stranger. Bah, why should I show any reserve? I will gouge out your entrails and make you suffer terribly. You put on an arrogant face until now, but that will no longer serve. Go throw yourself into the Takano River and die."

Takayamahiko: "Hey, hey, you two, what do you take this place for? It is, with all reverence, the inner precinct of the everlasting darkness of Tokoyo Castle. Without uttering insolent words, lower your fox tails quickly, and crawl on all fours to apologize: 'Kon-kon, henceforth I will strictly behave myself; kwai-kwai, I will reform and improve.' If you do so, Takayamahiko will grant you a brief reprieve. During that time, you had best place your hands upon your chests and decide whether to leave or stay. Insolent rascals, shall I dismiss you from your posts as gatekeepers and set you in Nakayoriwake's place? Shall I make you attendants to Tokoyo Jin'ō?"

"Yes, yes, we stiffen in obedience; there is no excuse,"

At this time, from outside the gate, in a loud voice,

"Hear, hear! Toyamawake has returned, bringing Tsuki, Yuki, and Hana. Open this gate as quickly as possible!"

At this calling voice, the two said,

"We cannot hear what on earth you are saying at all. 'Open the door' or 'Toyama-ake' or whatever—what is this about? Long ago, my ears became Toyamawake."

Pounding on the gate,

"Open, open!"

The shouting voice grew more and more violent.

Kanihiko: "Whether it be a tofu seller, Toyama, or a plasterer, as if I would care! As if I would open this gate! We are, with reverence, the flanking attendants of Tokoyo Jin'ō. We are the successors of Nakayoriwake. What of Toyama? Having hips like tofu, do not speak so haughtily! Our offices are different. Do not fail in your duty!"

From the direction of the middle gate, the foolish official Kasukuma came running, and said,

"Ya, gyai! Aka, Kani! Master Takeyama says for Aka-Kani to come here a moment!"

The two: "Calling us 'Aka-Kani'—you make fools of us! As if we were two rolled into one! Hey, Kasukuma, you dregs of a fellow, what are you babbling? Toward the flanking attendants of Tokoyo Jin'ō, you dare say 'come here a moment' and 'Aka-Kani'—you must have been possessed by a fox!"

Just at that time, the sound of the bell of Tokoyo Castle striking six o'clock resounded: kōn, kōn, kōn, kon, kon, kon.
