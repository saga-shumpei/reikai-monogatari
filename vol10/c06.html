Chapter 6: Behind the Framed Plaque [436]

第6章　額の裏〔436〕

§ 1

Just as Takatoriwake, Nakayoriwake, and the other officials sitting in a row were taken aback, Teruyamahiko returned to this place and said:

"Well, strange things do happen. As you saw just now, this person Teruhiko appeared here, instantly concealed his form, and upon an urgent report that he was again running amok outside the gate against numerous attendants, I rushed outside in great haste to see the situation, but lo and behold, there was not even a human shadow, and only the yelping of a fox could be heard from yonder woods. Truly, it is a wondrous thing."

Takeyamahiko said, "Even speaking of wondrous things, could there be such a wonder? Nay, Lord Nakayoriwake, your travelling with immense hardship all the way to the Middle Land has completely come to naught; to be utterly flabbergasted must be precisely this."

Takatoriwake said, "Fugya, fugya, fugya."

Takeyamahiko said, "Alack, Lord Takatoriwake, can you still not speak clearly? Indeed, it is the height of pitifulness, indecency, misfortune, and 'fugya-fugya'."

From behind the framed plaque in the transom, a groaning sound came echoing forth. Puzzled, as the whole company turned their eyes toward the plaque and strained their ears, from behind the plaque came the words:

"Ahahaha! Unbeknownst to brother or sister, cast under an unfathomable and wondrous magic, confounded contrary to your expectations, your dumbfounded and utterly stunned countenances are truly too much. Clutching your heads, crying 'Ouch! It hurts!', having your heads struck and pressed down by the red ball that appeared from the darkness, having your noses squashed, a gathering of evil deities with red faces and darting eyes; whatever you plot with your shallow wisdom, blind gods whose feet cannot see, a bird will start up from right beneath your feet! However much you fret or brazen it out, like a thin cord loincloth slipping this way and that, the final outcome is just like this; ah, how comical! Disdaining the missionaries of the Ananai teachings and exhausting the limits of foolishness, so overwhelmed are you that pitiful rain falls from your two eyes. Straining your ears to the suspicious noise, whether it could be or could not be, the state of having the vital jewel taken from you, I sympathize with your unbearable anxiety; ahahaha, 'Fool! Fool!' caws the crow's condolence, ohohoho!"

Teruyamahiko said, "Hey, suspicious presence behind the plaque, what evil deity are you? I shall expose your true form!"

§ 2

Saying this and aiming at the plaque, no sooner had he seized a wooden sword that happened to be there and struck it sharply, than the eerie voice shifted direction once more, saying from nowhere:

"Ihihihi, how pathetic! No matter how much you search for my whereabouts, you will not discover my form until parched beans bloom into flowers. You spineless cowards, fretting with your breath failing over what will become of you, listen well to my counsel. Nakayoriwake, who returned here at last from a long journey with a triumphant and boastful face, without even a moment to rest and smoke, has met with the circumstances of this place, which is troublesome in the extreme; though stones fall or spears rain down, unless you search out Teruhiko's whereabouts and present him before the eyes of Tokoyo Shin'ō, your face will be utterly ruined, and you will not even be able to keep your superior post. Your power hitherto will fall completely to the ground. Nakayoriwake, who returned joyfully with a triumphant face—ah, how pitiable, how pitiable!—though he had a multitude of men guard a single Teruhiko and finally came back, Teruhiko has become an empty husk before he knew it; what a pitiable thing it is. Suffering a public humiliation unprecedented in ancient or modern times, your baseness is inferior even to that of a dog. Even a dog could sniff him out, yet what a troublesome matter it is. You useless lot, all of you, with those vexed expressions, shunned and hated like thorns by the people of the land of Tokoyo, wearing disgusting faces with no composure, fretfully exerting yourselves—you are fine vessels for Magatsu-no-kami; that which falls beyond one's reckoning is not limited to lustful passion alone. Ufufufu."

Teruyamahiko said, "It is truly a voice past comprehension. Everyone, what should we do? We must devise various means to dispel such an uncanny voice."

Once more, from nowhere:

"Ufufufu, you dazed fools, you flustered fools, what are you muttering so gloomily? Are you sad? Is it bitter? Carelessly falling into a trap and being subjected to such hardship, you have no chance of rising. Look at this helpless state of yours; the retribution for weaving lies is right before your eyes. Struck on the head, struck on the nose, wearing a dazed expression, you fools of Gensantarō, there is a limit to your lack of wits. Your naive schemes are mere bubbles on the water; what are you doing swarming like caterpillars? You flustered fools who uphold the teachings of Uraruhiko, you will not like what I say; it must be annoying. What is with that sorrowful face? Meeting with unprecedented folly, having your heads flattened, your noses broken, Teruhiko having escaped you—even in the eyes of onlookers, it is a pitiable state of affairs. Wahahaha."

§ 4

Takeyamahiko said, "Hey, what are you people babbling in such low voices? Why do you not speak in louder voices?"

Katatora said, "F-f-forgive me. I performed a brief imitation of a monster. Katatora's play, and all of you trembling and shaking—it is truly and exceedingly regrettable."

Teruyamahiko said in a loud voice,

"Fool!"

Katatora and Kanihiko spread both hands, stood up, and cried,

"Ah!"

Katatora said, "Hey, Kani-kō, what did you say?"

Kanihiko said, "Kata-kō, what did you say? I had no intention of saying anything, yet something suddenly rose from inside my belly, and I talked on without end."

"You too? A voice also came rising up from within my belly, and even though I tried to stop it, it would not stop. It is a path of voice that cannot be halted, try as one might."

"This is no joke; is this a commotion where one can make puns?"

At this time, the clamorous sound of men and horses was heard in front of the gate once more. While all stood up and pricked up their ears to see what it might be, Kanihiko immediately scrambled sideways and rushed to the front gate, whereupon someone shouted,

"Hear, hear! I am the man of merit who, obeying the command of the Tokoyo Shin'ō, was sent as an envoy to Hazama-no-kuni and took back the three women—Tsuki, Yuki, and Hana. Open this gate this instant!"

Kanihiko said, "What an incomprehensible thing! Even granting how fast Tōyamawake may run, having departed only this very night, Hazama-no-kuni is three hundred ri away. How could such an absurd thing possibly be? This fellow is another monster. As if I would open the gate!"

From outside the gate came the voice:

"Hey, hey, gatekeeper, what are you dawdling about? ... Open this gate at once!"

"Neither this gate nor that gate matters! Some incomprehensible fellow has come along—are you trying to stir up another trouble? Very well, I have a thought as well; though a gatekeeper, I am not to be made a fool of! Shall I crush you to pulp with the physical strength of Lord Kanihiko? Hey, hey, Akaguma, come quickly! It is another fox spirit. Such a bizarre night as tonight is peerless and uniquely strange throughout all ages. Tonight, no matter what happens, opening this gate is absolutely out of the question!"

—thus he was shouting at the top of his lungs.

Akaguma came running to this spot and said,

"Hey, hey, Kanihiko, pull yourself together! What nonsense are you spewing? The gate is already wide open, so how can there be any question of opening or not opening it? Dawn is just about to break. What are you sleepwalking about?"

As he clenched his fist and struck him across the side of the face with a smack, Kanihiko was startled, and while rubbing his eyes, looked carefully, only to see that the gate was flung wide open with no sign of a human figure; the moon had sunk into the western mountains, and only the wintry blast went whistling past like a flute, hyū-hyū.
