§ 1

The Chapter on the Purification in the Imperial Classics

"Thereupon, Izanagi-no-Ōkami proclaimed:"

"Thereupon" is an expression spoken in reception of the preceding main text, "having exchanged words of separation with Yomotsu-no-Ōkami," and so forth.

Regarding the meaning of the sacred name of Izanagi-no-Mikoto, when interpreted from the standpoint of Ōmoto Kototama, that is, the substance: I is spirit; Za is to invite; Na is to be paired; Gi is fire, which is the Sun Deity, the Yang Deity. The Mi of Izanami is water, the Yin Deity; having the sacred name called the Spirit-Inviting Paired Deities, the Yin and Yang of heaven and earth operate in pair, and the breath of human beings performs the paired respiration of inhaling and exhaling; therefore, the breath is the shrine where both deities reside. The first blowing of the breath within the placenta is called the Floating Bridge of Heaven. Its meaning is that A signifies "by oneself" and Me signifies "turning." Uki is a word that conjugates as uki and uku, and Hashi is a word that conjugates as hashi and hasu. U is water and is 〓__SP_018__. Ha is water and forms the horizontal, that is, 〓__SP_019__. The natural circulation of water and fire, floating and arising to form the vertical and horizontal, is called the Floating Bridge of Heaven. That the Ōmoto Shin'yu states, "This Ōmoto is the great bridge of the world; unless one crosses this bridge, one will not understand the matters of the world. The world shall be opened through the protection of warp and woof. The Great Deity of the Sun and the Deity of the Moon are the ancestors of this world," means nothing other than this.

Heaven and earth and human beings first releasing spirit—this is called "the two deities standing upon the Floating Bridge of Heaven." That which is conceived and first moves within the womb is the Floating Bridge of Heaven and the Great Bridge of Patterned Silk. In this manner, as the spirit of heaven and earth blows continuously, and the breath of human beings blows continuously, their extremity becomes wet and forms a drop like dew; this is the island formed by the accumulation of brine. Water and fire are shiho, and of shima, shi is water and ma signifies roundness; water and fire accumulate to form a circle of water, producing the moisture of breath, and that breath naturally solidifies; this is called Onogoroshima. In short, the two deities Izanagi and Izanami are the great deities who repair and solidify the Earth, thereby possessing and manifesting the divine virtue of ceaseless creation and transformation, and giving birth to the origin of all things. However, when Izanami-no-Ōkami once descended as a deity of the Land of Yomi, matters eventually reached the point where she declared that she would slay a thousand people in a single day. Furthermore, if one interprets the divine names of the two deities from the function of Japanese Kototama studies, Izanagi-no-Mikoto becomes the foundation and base of all creation, manifests great adamantine power, accomplishes the divine work of repairing and solidifying, embodies the heart of the Amatsukami to preserve the earth, combines all abilities and virtues to determine the origin of Su, lives through eternity without end, becomes the truth of the Heavenly Ancestor, and is the Deity of Fire and Water who invites toward the good path. Next, Izanami-no-Mikoto governs the three elements, forms the origin of substance, becomes the base and jewel of the physical body and the Earth to illuminate the dark realm; she is a deity possessing the function of the Great Moon, being the Great Deity of the Moon and the Mizu-no-Mitama. Even such an exalted, complete, and benevolent deity, when passing away to the Land of Yomi, could not but transform and emerge as a deity of body-primary and spirit-secondary. Here, in accordance with the main text, it is interpreted as the activity of the male deity alone.

§ 2

"I have arrived and stayed in a hideous, repulsive, and defiled land."

The kotodama of A is heaven, sea, nature, the circle, the general name of the seventy-five sounds, nothingness yet existence, and the water-spirit in the air. Considering this, "I" is a pronoun for all things in the universe. It means that hideousness and defilement have filled all things in this universe, and it has truly reached the state of the Land of Yomi. In the present age, heaven, earth, and all other things have truly ended up becoming a hideous, repulsive, and defiled land. Politics, diplomacy, education, industry, and morality have all utterly decayed, to the extent that people have come to doubt whether this is indeed the heaven and earth protected by the deities.

"Therefore, declaring, 'I will perform the purification of my body,' he arrived at Awagihara of the small gate of Tachibana in Himuka of Tsukushi, and performed the purification."

He declared that he would carry out a grand-scale purification of the universe and the nation. What was shown in the Divine Revelation, "By the command of the Three Great Deities of Heaven, the world has come where Ushitora no Konjin will effect the reconstruction and renewal through the great washing and great cleansing of the three thousand worlds," is precisely the great divine ritual of purification.

Tsu is the decisive power of true reality and tathata, and the power of correspondence.

Ku is the alternation of darkness, the origin of the three great calendars, and the ultimate of profound depths.

Shi is the present of the world, the North Pole of the Imperial land, and Amanohashidate.

No is as endowed by heaven, the seed of musubi, and freedom from hindrance.

Hi is penetration between the visible and invisible worlds, being without disorder, and consistency from beginning to end.

Mu is to settle firmly, to constitute the myriad people of the land, and the binding of the true body.

Ka is to see clearly, to change distinctively, and to shine and sparkle.

No is the word of continuation.

Ta is the power of contrast, the power of balance, and being abundant and surplus.

Chi is to overflow to the utmost, to accompany creation, and the rank of the parent.

Ha is the material of the sun, maintaining celestial bodies, and spring.

Na is fire and water, the entirety of vacuum, coming into harmony and completion, and the entirety of hydrogen.

A is the primal origin, the Great Mother, and the crowning touch of the circular form.

Ha is extending and opening, the color of heaven, tooth, and leaf.

Ha is the power of severance, the union of Fu and A, and beholding the boundary.

Ra is Takamimusubi, the fitting together of beginning and end, and the foundation of immeasurable life.

次に美曽岐の言霊を解釈すれば、

§ 4

"Mi" is water, the great yin, fullness, fruit, the Way, and becoming a jewel.

"So" is the seed of wind, the clothing of the body, and the wrapping of "Su."

"Gi" is to penetrate with vitality, to become white, to lose color, and to extend to all things.

In short, it refers to the great divine ritual of Grand Purification, which cleanses all impurities, sweeps away dust and dirt, manifests the spiritual virtues of wind and water, completes the pure and immaculate divine world, casts away vanity and ostentation, fulfills all matters, reveals the intensely vibrant divine authority, executes flawless divine governance, and leaves not a single speck of sinister evil within the universe. One must not forget that the current trend has met the opportune time when the great divine ritual of Misogi must be strictly performed universally throughout the world. The purpose of Ōmoto also lies in resolutely carrying out this Misogi of the realm.

"Therefore, the name of the deity that came into being from the sacred staff He threw away was Tsukitatsu-funado-no-kami."

Regarding the Kotodama of the sacred staff, "tsu" is the great adamantine power and decisive power, serving as the treasury of the jewel, and "e" signifies accomplishing in the midst, advancing forward, and preserving the jewel; that is, it is the role of assisting the divine power. Yet that the deity even cast away this staff shows how thoroughly it had become defiled. In modern terms, it refers to the high officials who assist in the administration of the realm.

The meaning of the name Tsukitatsu-funado-no-kami is an attendant deity who stands between the upper and the lower to block them, preventing the conditions of those below from reaching those above, and preventing the will of those above from being known to those below. The present age is a world in which this deity obstructs everything. Because the guardian deity who should have been a staff and a pillar proved instead to be of no help and an obstacle, Izanagi-no-Ōkami threw away His sacred staff as the very first act.

"Next, the name of the deity that came into being from the sacred sash He threw away was Michi-no-nagachiha-no-kami."

Regarding the Kotodama of the sacred sash, "o" signifies governing and cultivating the soul and mind, and is also the connecting band of the unity of God and man. "Bi" carries the meaning of radiant brilliance, illuminating the six directions. That is to say, when administering the governance of the manifest world, one must without fail spiritually preach and teach the Way of Heaven, Earth, and Man, thereby leading the myriad people to take refuge and causing them to submit and follow the politics of the manifest world willingly. This is the so-called sacred sash of the deity. The deity threw away this sacred sash as well because it had become defiled and unusable.

The meaning of Michi-no-nagachiha is the authorities such as religionists, educators, ethicists who preach the heavenly principle and human way, Shintoists who advocate reverence for the deities, respect for the Emperor, and love of country, propagators of the Imperial Way, orators, and preachers. That He cast away this sash signifies that He fundamentally reforms all doctrines of education, religion, and ethics.
