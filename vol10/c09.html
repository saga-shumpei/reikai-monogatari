Chapter 9: The Indigo Rump [439]

第9章　尻藍〔439〕

§ 1

In the flower-blooming spring, where every moment is worth a thousand pieces of gold, within the land of Tokoyo where Kototamahime-no-Mikoto, the divided spirit of Kin-katsukane-no-Ōkami, resides, the mountains and fields are green; Haruhime weaves countless looms, producing green, crimson, white, and yellow; flowers bloom in profusion, countless birds sing and dance in the treetops, the heavenly sunlight shines radiantly, and the shimmering heat rising over the fields is like the calmed waves of the great ocean. Rescued in the Dragon Palace and together with Hinode-no-Kami, riding the turtle of Kotohirawake, Odotsumatsumi, who surfaced on the coast of Chiri, crossed the Utsuyama Pass in the lands of Chiri and Hiru bathed in the morning sun; his footsteps passed the border of Karu, descending the mountain of Takateru where divine dignity was manifest, and, with abundant divine grace, bound together the land of Tokoyo; making a solitary journey to the land of "Ma" floating upon the great ocean where waves rage east and west, wearing a light-hearted straw raincoat and hat, and seeking to pacify Arayanohara in the land of "Me" where even the blind are opened, clad in straw sandals and gaiters, making no distinction between night and day, while drenched in the dew of grace, with a bed of grass, a pillow of stone, and wearing a quilt adorned with stars, he advanced singing pure-hearted missionary songs through the desolate land of mountains and rivers—ah, how heroic! The missionary maidens of Anaikyō—Matsushirohime awaiting the divine age of Miroku, the upright Takenohime, and Umegakahime—came advancing through night and day, drenched in dew. At a bustling crossroads, in the very center of the town of Kawada, face and face came directly together.

Odotsumatsumi spoke: "Ah, are you not the noble daughter of Masakatsumatsumi, the lord of the capital of Chin? While pursuing the lovely trace of the plum scent wafting in the wind and walking briskly up to the entrance of this town, a rumor among the townsfolk and the whispers of young girls said that a beautiful maiden missionary around nineteen or twenty years of age, who seemed neither sixteen nor eighteen, had passed through; believing that the time had surely come to meet you sisters, I whipped the horse of my heart and involuntarily dashed forward on foot. Truly, since landing at the port of Ataru last summer and parting from you at the foot of Mount Tamayama, there was no news, passing like summer insects; autumn gradually approached, adding sorrow to the loneliness of my heart. Even a stalwart man like Odotsumatsumi, who could crush demons, faced such a lonely autumn journey; as autumn leaves fell and the deer cried, there was no way to trace your whereabouts firmly; with the leaves of my heart's sorrow scattering, as I crossed the mountain paths of Chiri and came to the land of "Ma", I heard that you were captured by the inspectors of the arrogant Takatoriwake and sent to the land of Tokoyo; what sorrow struck my heart then! Like flower buds shielded even from a draft of wind, maiden missionaries, like the chirping of insects thronging in the autumn fields, evoked such deep sorrow that I wept tears of a man. Just then, hearing what people whispered, as I pricked up my ears, they spoke of a flower-like maiden missionary with a dazzlingly graceful and enchanting figure explaining the divine path in the land of "Me"; now meeting you right before my eyes, what joy, what grief, what deep pity I feel, seeing how worn away your noble form has become. Though it is said to be for the sake of the deity and for the path, facing the heroic resolve of the noble daughters of Momogamihiko-no-Mikoto, the archangel who grasped the divine governance in the holy ground of Jerusalem, we as men cannot bear our profound shame."

Thus, without even giving the three maidens time to reply, he merely poured out his heart tediously, repeating himself like thread unwound from a spool.

Matsuyohime said, "I wondered who it might be, and it is the missionary Odoyamatsumi; we meet in an unexpected place. We three sisters were rescued by Haruyamahiko, chieftain of the Land of Hazama, met the missionaries Teruhiko no Toyamatsumi and Komayamahiko, entered this Land of Me together with Haruyamahiko's three daughters, Tsuki, Yuki, and Hana, and at the foot of Mexico Pass, we parted ways—one going there and three coming here—and were just about to advance toward the Rocky Mountains while singing missionary hymns. Oh, how healthy and well you are! Beneath the sky of a foreign land, the joy of meeting you makes me feel as though I were ascending to heaven. This is the open road; shall we proceed to yonder forest, rest, and speak at our leisure?"

"That would also be fine. Then let us head toward that forest."

Hereupon, the missionaries, one man and three women, advanced toward the northeast while singing missionary hymns. The long spring day was already sinking in the west, the surroundings were enveloped in a mist-like haze, the curtain of darkness was drawn making all around dark, and a thousand crows blanketed the sky, crying kahai-kahai across the expanse. The sound of the bell tolling dusk struck the hearts of the four, and the loneliness of an autumn evening pressed upon them.

Exposing their blossoms in the fields of the Land of Me were weeping cherry trees, and they advanced threading through the rows of weeping willows that made their hearts faint. Suddenly, a clamorous sound was heard from ahead; as the four involuntarily halted and listened intently, slender bonfires began to flicker through the twilight sky, and immediately missionary hymns were heard clearly as if right at hand. When the company drew near, as if drawn toward the voice, and looked, they found someone surrounded by several hundred people, vehemently preaching something.

The four quietly muffled their footsteps, slipped into the crowd under cover of darkness, and looking closely, saw a man standing atop a small crag, earnestly expounding something to the gathering.

From among the crowd, a dark-complexioned large man with round eyes and a nose twisted to the left faced the missionary, saying:

"Hey, you must be a missionary of Anai-kyō! This is the domain of Tokoyo Shin'ō. Though this is a divine land where the Ural faith is revered to unite the hearts of the people, you are evil missionaries like your kind, missionaries who revere an evil deity that used sorcery to throw the Castle of Tokoyo into confusion and crushed the proud nose of the governor Takatoribake. I am Ushikumobake; having received the great command of Tokoyo Shin'ō to arrest you, I have come searching for missionaries of Anai-kyō. This Land of Me is, just as its name suggests, a land where Takatoribake's subordinates keep sharp watch with eyes like cormorants and hawks. Come down from that crag and meekly submit to your bonds! You have no chance left. Even if you struggle, now that you are surrounded by several dozen underlings like this, your end has come; surrender obediently!"

Thus he roared, raising a voice like thunder. With an easy and unhurried composure, as though hardly letting it enter his ears, the missionary on the rock said:

"Hold, Ushikumowake, or whatever your name may be; listen well. Truly, as you say, I am a missionary of Ananaikyō. However much you rely on superior numbers and surround me, I possess the deep protection of the deities. Cast away without delay the Ural religion that brings disorder to this world, and listen to my teaching, the Great Way of Kannagara that governs the country and brings peace to the realm. What manner of man is Tokoyo-jin'ō? What manner of man is Takatorihiko? How pitiful it is that, as the retribution for accumulated evils, divine punishment instantly fell upon him and broke his pride! If, while receiving such a divine chastisement, you still do not repent, I shall crush the bridge of the nose of every single one of you who are the retainers of Takatorihiko, without leaving even one behind. Come, my single word is the word of God. It is the voice of salvation. Will you hear it, or will you not? It is the dividing crest between good and evil, vice and virtue, heaven and hell. Will you follow my teaching with a faith as firm as this rock? If you refuse, through divine arts of infinite transformation, I shall inflict chastisement upon your heads. Whosoever among you has felt my words pierce their heart, let him step forward and state his name!"

Thus he growled like a roaring lion, producing a gong-like sound many times greater than Ushikumowake's thunderous voice. The tens of underlings were disheartened by these powerful kotodama, covered their ears, and involuntarily shrank and crouched down upon the ground, which was comical.

Ushikumowake cried,

"Ah, what a nuisance! Taste this!"

No sooner had he spoken than he ran up onto the rock with a countenance like a rakshasa, clenched his iron fist, aimed at the missionary's face, and attempted to strike with all his might as if to shatter his bones.

Then, in the twinkling of an eye, the missionary lightly dodged his body like a flying bird, and no sooner had he laid a hand on Ushikumowake's foot than Ushikumowake somersaulted; falling headlong with a crash from the exceedingly high rock onto the earth, by the very weight of his body he plunged his head deeply into the soft soil, turned his buttocks toward the sky, and was flailing his arms and legs in the manner of a flower vase.

Again, from among the crowd, someone cried,

"I am Kanikumowake; eat my iron fist!"

No sooner had he spoken than he clenched his fists and rushed to strike, but the missionary said,

"Ah, what a nuisance!"

And seizing him by the nape of the neck, plucked him up with one hand as though dangling a cat, and suspended him over Ushikumowake. Ushikumowake's two legs and Kanikumowake's two legs joined closely together, and a comical acrobatics was performed here. Head and head were toward heaven and earth, while buttocks and buttocks faced each other, becoming cheek-to-cheek. Indeed, the violent scheme of the two men had completely fallen to pieces.

"If you mistake the true way of God, you will have to plunge your head into the earth, turn your feet upward, and squirm,"

Just as this divine revelation foretold.

With their heads down, the two Kumowakes, Ox and Crab, had four hands and four feet; they fell heavily, went on all fours, and became the eight legs of Kanikumowake crawling about the earth; the ridiculousness of it was a pathetic sight to the eyes of onlookers.

と雷のような声を張り上げてどなっている。岩の上の宣伝使は、ほとんど耳に入れないかのような鷹揚な態度で、

「やあ、牛雲別とやら、よく聞け。私こそはそなたの言う通り三五教の宣伝使だ。いかに多勢を頼みとして私を取り囲もうとも、私には深い神の加護がある。一刻も早くこの世を乱すウラル教を捨てて、天下国家を治め平らげる随神の大道であるわが教えを聞け。常世神王、あれは何者か。鷹取別、彼はいったい何者か。積み重なった悪の報いとして、神罰がたちどころに下って鼻を折られたその哀れさよ。このような神の戒めを受けながら、なおも悔い改めないならば、鷹取別の家来であるお前たちの鼻柱を、一人も残さず粉砕してやろう。さあ、私の一言は神の言葉だ。救いの声だ。聞くか、聞かないか。善悪邪正、天国と地獄の分水嶺である。この岩のような堅い信仰をもってわが教えに従うか。拒むのであれば、私は千変万化の神術によって、お前たちの頭上に懲戒を加えよう。お前たちの中で、私の言葉が身に染みた者は名乗り出て参れ」

と、牛雲別の雷声の数倍もの銅鑼のような声を発し、獅子が吠えるように唸っている。数十人の手下は、この強い言霊に度肝を抜かれ、耳を塞ぎ、思わず地上に縮こまってうずくまるのは滑稽である。

牛雲別は、

「ええい、面倒だ、思い知れ」

と言うよりも早く、岩の上に羅刹のような形相で駆け上がり、鉄拳を固め、宣伝使の顔面を目がけて、骨も砕けよとばかりに力を込めて殴りかかろうとする。

この時遅く、かの時早く、宣伝使は飛ぶ鳥のようにひらりと体をかわし、牛雲別の足に手をかけるやいなや、牛雲別は宙返りを打ち、あれほど高い岩の上から、大地へとどっとばかりに転落する途端に、体の重みによって柔らかい土の中に頭部をぐさりと突き刺し、尻を天に向け、花立てのような恰好で手足を藻掻いている。

またしても群衆の中から、

「我は蟹雲別である、我が鉄拳を食らえ」

と言うよりも早く、拳を固めて打ちかかってくるのを、宣伝使は、

「ええい、面倒だ」

と首筋を掴んで、猫をぶら下げたように片手でつまみ上げ、牛雲別の上に向かって吊り下げた。牛雲別の両足と、蟹雲別の両足がぴたりと合わさり、ここに面白い軽業が演じられた。頭と頭とは天と地に、尻と尻とは向かい合って、尻合いとなった。さすがの二人の乱暴な計画も、支離滅裂となったのである。

「神の誠の道を取り違えると、頭を土に突っ込んで足を仰向けにし、のたうち回らねばならぬぞよ」

という神諭そのままである。

牛と蟹の両雲別は、頭を下にして牛の尻の、手四つ足四つで、どたりと倒れて四つ這いとなり、蟹雲別の八つ足となって大地を這い回る滑稽さは、他人の見る目にも哀れな有様であった。

原文（近代文語）

と雷の如き声を張り上げて呶鳴りゐる。巌上の宣伝使は、殆ど耳に入れざる如き鷹揚なる態度にて、

『アイヤ、牛雲別とやら、よつく聞けよ。吾こそは汝の言ふ如く三五教の宣伝使だ。如何に多勢を恃み吾を取り囲むとも、吾には深き神護あり。一時も早く此の世を乱すウラル教を捨てて、治国平天下の惟神の大道なるわが教を聞け。常世神王かれ何者ぞ。鷹取別かれ何者ぞ。積悪の報い、神罰立所に下つて鼻挫かれしその哀れさ。斯くの如き神の戒めを受けながら、なほ悔い改めずば、鷹取別が臣下たる汝等が鼻柱、一人も残らず粉砕し呉れむぞ。サア、わが一言は神の言葉だ。救ひの声だ。きくか、きかぬか、善悪邪正、天国地獄の分水嶺、この巌の如き堅き信仰を以てわが教に従ふか。否むに於ては吾は千変万化の神術によつて、汝等が頭上に懲戒を加へむ。汝等の中、わが言葉の身に沁みし者は名乗つて出よ』

と牛雲別の雷声に数倍せる銅鑼声して、獅子の咆ゆるが如く唸りゐる。数十人の手下は、この強き言霊に胆を挫がれ、耳を塞ぎ、思はず地上に縮み踞むぞをかしけれ。

牛雲別は、

『エヽ面倒なり、思ひ知れよ』

と言ふより早く、巌上に羅刹の如き相好にて駆け上り、鉄拳を固め、宣伝使の面部を目がけて、骨も砕けよとばかり力を籠めて殴りつけむとする。

この時遅く、かの時早く、宣伝使は飛鳥の如くヒラリと体をかはし、牛雲別の足に手をかくるや否や、牛雲別はモンドリうつて、さしもに高き巌上より、大地にドツと許り顛落する途端に、体の重みにて柔かき土の中に頭部をグサリと刺し、臀部を天にむけ、花立の如き調子にて手足を藻掻き居る。

又もや群集の中より、

『吾は蟹雲別なり、わが鉄拳を喰へ』

と云ふより早く、拳骨を固めて打つてかかるを、宣伝使は、

『エヽ面倒なり』

と首筋掴んで、猫を提げし如く片手に撮んで、牛雲別の上に向つて吊り下したり。牛雲別の両足と、蟹雲別の両足はピタツと合うて、ここに面白き軽業が演ぜられたり。頭と頭とは天と地に、尻と尻は向ひ合して、シリ合ひとなりぬ。流石両人の乱暴なる計画も、シリ滅裂となりにける。

『神の誠の道を取違いたすと、頭を土に突込んで足を仰向けにして、のたくらねばならぬぞよ』

との神諭そのままである。

牛と蟹との両雲別は、頭を下に牛のしりの、手四つ足四つ、ドタリと倒けて四つ這ひとなり、蟹雲別の八つ足となつて大地を這ひ廻る可笑しさ、外の見る目も哀れなりける次第なり。
