Chapter 1: The Gate of Tokoyo Castle [431]

第1章　常世城門〔431〕

§ 1

Floating upon the waves of the rough seas of the east and west is the land of Tokoyo-kuni; in the wintry wind that blows, the mountain downdraft from the Rocky Mountains, the magakami, resembling pitch-black darkness, conceals its dark heart, unfurls a banner dyed with the hollyhock crest on a white ground, and, seeking to firmly erect Ōkunihiko no Mikoto as a pillar of deity to deceive this world, breaking its heart and struggling with all its might in various ways, raising a battle cry of victory in the battle of the land of Yomi, drawing and brandishing the grim sword toward the surface of the wave-swept Takasago Island, the divine land of Kokoro-Tsukushi, and the Reed-Plain Land of Fresh Rice Ears, to make the divine power of the land of Tokoyo shine forth, of the great country...

Likening her husband no-Mikoto to the Deity of Sunrise, Ōkunihime manifested as the deity Izanami no Kami no Mikoto; holding back the haughty-hearted Takatoribiko for a time, she made him Prime Minister of the Tokoyo Shin'ō, entrusted the policy of body-master-spirit-subordinate to Hirokunibiko, and called him the Tokoyo Shin'ō who deceived the realm.

In the morning sun rising through the peaks of the Rockies, Teruyamahiko with his round head, and Takeyamahiko, acting valiantly, had the messengers of Matsu, Take, and Ume carried in palanquins and led numerous attendants, returning with shoulders squared in boastful, triumphant pride.

The two imperial envoys who appeared at the front gate of Tokoyo Castle raised their voices and shouted:

"Hear, hear, gatekeepers! Teruyamahiko and Takeyamahiko have returned to the castle. Open this great gate without a moment's delay!"

Just then, a wintry wind raged violently. The two gatekeepers, Kanihiko and Akaguma,

"Yes!"

replied and opened the front gate smoothly, whereupon Teruyamahiko and Takeyamahiko, though exhausted from their long journey, were stirred by a desire for fame and glared down arrogantly from atop their horses at the gatekeepers, saying:

"Hear, Kanihiko and Akaguma! Report our great deeds to the Tokoyo Shin'ō without a moment's delay!"

Leaving those words behind, they advanced toward the middle gate. Kanihiko, running in long strides toward the middle gate while walking sideways true to his name, came up and said:

"Well, well, Lord Teruyamahiko and Lord Takeyamahiko, please wait a moment. Allow us to report to the Tokoyo Shin'ō first and abide by his instructions."

Takeyamahiko shouted: "Bah! What are you dawdling about? Gatekeeper Kanihiko, so adept at walking sideways, must you make a nuisance of yourself at every turn? That grim countenance of yours like a Heike crab—are you weeping, angry, ashamed, or frightened, or perchance drunk on sake? You cowardly gatekeeper guarding the red gate, whose very face is as red as Akaguma! The descent of the celestial maidens of Matsu, Take, and Ume, peerless beauties—what need is there to consult the Tokoyo Shin'ō about anything? Open this gate at once!"

"Hold, Lord Takayamahiko, that is far too insolent. Lowly gatekeepers though we are, we bear the vital duty of strictly observing the rules within the castle; even were it the descent of a heavenly maiden, without the permission of the castle lord Tokoyoshin'ō, this middle gate must not heedlessly be opened."

So he spoke with a reluctant face. In the meantime, Kanihiko took a quick peek into the palanquin of Matsudayahime, fell heavily on his buttocks upon the ground, and said:

"Well, well, well, hee-hee-hee-hee, she shines, she shines! Such intense light—is she a diamond, the sun blazing in the heavens, a peony blossom, a violet, or an iris? That such a goddess should exist in the Land of Tokoyo where black and white cannot even be distinguished—the utterly astonished Kanihiko has frothed at the mouth and collapsed. Hey, hey, Chief Akaguma, rather than turning your black-bear-like face bright red with rage, take a quick peek into this palanquin. Shall I say she is white, or shall I say she is pure? Is she the plum or cherry blossom of spring's third month, or the blossom of a peach? With a straight nose and a firm mouth, vermilion lips, crescent eyebrows, clear eyes like the full moon, and hair the color of a crow's wet feathers—in a world with all sorts of women, if one were to take a mistress, well, well, well..."

The unyielding and dutiful Akaguma gave a bitter smile at seeing Kanihiko in this state, and said:

"While being charged with the duty of guarding the iron gate of Tokoyo Castle, what kind of shameful condition is this disgraceful display of yours? Pull yourself together!"

"Hey, hey, Akaguma, do not turn so red and become angry. It is the form of a heavenly maiden that smoothly melted even the stern knot of this crab-faced Kanihiko's countenance; being rigid is not one's only merit. You are ever like a withered tree, like a boulder, having neither savor nor color—a callous and cruel laborer. Just behold her face for a moment; spring blossoms will surely bloom even upon the withered tree of your heart. Even so, your nose makes you an exceptionally dark, big-nosed fellow, and wondrously enough, the bridge of your nose has been bright red lately; could it be a manifestation of your fierce breath? With a strange face like an asauri, a kamoshiri gourd, or a pumpkin, a dark-complexioned handsome man like an eggplant—when one gazes upon that tall nose, unfitting for a face like a gourd or an eggplant, the nose is quite red."

Akaguma raised his voice and said:

"In such a critical moment of one against a thousand, what nonsense do you utter?"

Thus he glared at him. Suddenly, a voice came from within the gate:

"Lord Teruyamahiko, Lord Takayamahiko, Tokoyoshin'ō is in the most delightful humor and stretches his neck waiting for you. Enter quickly."

As the middle gate smoothly swung open immediately following those words, Teruyamahiko said:

"Well, well, everyone, thank you for your labors on this long journey. Return to your respective rooms and take a leisurely rest. Come, Lord Takayamahiko, follow after me."

So saying, he took the lead, ordered the palanquin to be borne, and proceeded deep into the inner palace.
