Chapter 37: The Celebratory Banquet [467]

第37章　祝宴〔467〕

§ 1

Kanahiko and his wife, attributing it entirely to the grace of the Kami that their beloved only daughter Kiyohime had escaped great disaster and that the seeds of calamity in the land had been removed, chanted the Amatsu-norito, recited the propagation songs, and held a celebratory banquet; hundreds of men and women of all ages from throughout the village, without distinction of high and low, became dead drunk on the celebratory sake, and many appeared weeping with joy, laughing, or telling tall tales, from among whom four or five young men surrounded the gatekeeper Tokikō,

First Man: "Hey, Tokikō, you came back with tremendous force and were blowing extraordinary tall tales, but hearing the poem of the propagation messenger, what is this, you lost the use of your legs and showed a tear-stained face, did you not? Why do you put on such false bravado?"

Tokikō: "It is natural to show a tear-stained face. By the way, do they not say there is no dog that does not bark? Blowing tall tales and showing a tear-stained face are both charms for Tokikō."

"Witty again, are you? You used to say that in the past you were called Tokinogawa and wrestled as a minor sumo wrestler. Come, shall we try wrestling together once in this parlor?"

"Stop, stop, it is dangerous. For you, with unsteady hips like a green onion, to strike against the iron-like Lord Toki is like bringing about your own destruction. Rather than that, shall I tell you the firsthand account of when I went to Mount Altai?"

Second Man: "Hey, everyone, what this fellow says is always nothing but tall tales. Wet your eyebrows with spit and listen to him."

"Oh, are you showing respect to me by calling me a warrior? Now, from here Tokikō will narrate the outline of the subjugation of the evil deities on Mount Altai, so listen carefully. In the first place, Mount Altai is a deep mountain and ravine; those who advance into it are tigers and wolves, or wild dogs, or else bears, or Lord Tokikō..."

First Man: "Hey, hey, do not blow your horn right from the beginning. Are we not also the fellows who carried the Chinese chest and actually climbed it?"

"Ah, I made a blunder. From here begins the true story. In the first place, after parting from you village cowards in front of the fort, it was pitch dark, rain poured down like axle trees, and the wind groaned with such ferocity that rocks seemed ready to scatter. Caring nothing for that, Lord Tokikō, leading Ishikoridome, the propagation messenger of Sango-kyō, and carrying Umegakahime, pointed toward the cavern; with momentum as if to swallow heaven and earth, striding seven or eight feet in a single step, he appeared before the rock door and groaned fiercely, 'Un, un!' Even that tough fellow Hebitsukami shrank before my kotodama, transformed into a great fireball, and together with small fireballs soared into the sky, heading toward the southwestern sky and fleeing to Armenia—or so I thought, but it was his trick; he immediately possessed Lord Tokikō's body and roared, 'Ah, I am Hebitsukami, the chief deity of Mount Altai!' Even that propagation messenger trembled with fear and babbled, 'Please, please, spare only my life; look, my hip bone has relocated itself like this.' Thereupon, the fellow Hebitsukami who possessed this Lord Tokikō said, 'Ah, this Tokikō intends to forgive you, but the secondary guardian deity Hebitsukami will not forgive. Shall I sprinkle salt from your head and bite into you, munching you up?' Umegakahime clasped her white hands and pleaded with a cute face, 'Please, Lord Tokikō, please save the propagation messenger Ishikoridome,' so both Lord Tokikō and the secondary guardian deity suddenly felt pity and said, 'Tonight we were going to eat you to death, but out of regard for your gentle face, we shall forgive you.' Then the propagation messenger prostrated himself like a flat spider, and was so overjoyed that it was beyond all comparison."
