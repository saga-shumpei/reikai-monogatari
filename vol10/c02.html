Chapter 2: Darkness Over Heaven and Earth [432]

第2章　天地暗澹〔432〕

§ 1

The Castle of Tokoyo brushes the sky above the clouds, and through the trees far and near glitter the crest of the three-leaved mallow, the three-storied golden pavilion, and the keep shining in the morning sun. Renowned longleaf pines with their green three-needled leaves reach high into the view. Triumphant after bringing the missionaries of Pine, Bamboo, and Plum from the village of Hazama, Teruyamahiko appeared before the spacious courtyard of boundless artificial hills with mossy crags and hundreds of trees, and in a loud voice said:

"I report to Tokoyo-shin'o Hirokuniwake—ah, no, no, to Okunihiko-no-kami. Bearing the supreme command, we two traveled day and night to the mansion of Haruyamahiko, the chieftain of the land of Hazama, and having captured and brought back the three maidens, we humbly request your careful inspection."

Next, Takeyamahiko also said in a loud voice:

"In accordance with your command, we have at last accomplished our mission and returned. Their renown as supreme beauties exceeds what we had heard, being peerless figures of loveliness in the world; if you keep them permanently within this castle and dispatch them to the battlefield like the peaches of Yomo-tsu-hira-saka, any hero or gallant warrior will have his soul seized and his spirit scattered by a single glance of these beauties, and their surrender will be clearer than looking at a fire. Subduing the enemy by using the enemy's own resources is truly the divine calculation and ghostly strategy of the Six Secret Teachings and Three Strategies, and the great fortune of Tokoyo-shin'o shall shine ever more across the entire universe."

At this report, Tokoyo-shin'o smiled and said:

"I am deeply impressed by the actions of you two, which are nothing new today. At any rate, you must guide and bring the three missionaries to this place."

As he gave this strict command, Teruyamahiko turned to his subordinate deity, Katatora:

"Hey there, Katatora, guide the missionaries of Pine, Bamboo, and Plum here."

"Understood."

So saying, Katatora left the place, appeared before the palanquins of the three, and said:

"Hey there, three maidens, listen well. I am Katatorahiko-no-mikoto, the servant of Takatoriwake, retainer of Tokoyo-shin'o, chosen as the right hand of Teruyamahiko, firm of heart, hard of head, and with arms harder than iron. It is truly laughable that such formidable, stubborn female missionaries, who regard the Ural religion as an enemy, have dared to come to the land of Hazama as women to propagate the Anai religion. In the Castle of Tokoyo, firmly defended by iron gates, no matter how much you tremble, fret, or struggle, there is nothing to be done. Since matters have come to this pass, you cannot simply keep your obstinacy and maintain your stubbornness against the words of Tokoyo-shin'o. Come, come, I shall now lead you to the inner palace. Step into the footprints of this Katatora and come forth."

現代日本語訳

常世の城は雲の上に御空を摩し、遠近の樹の間を透して、三葉葵の紋所、黄金の色の三重の高殿、朝日に輝く天守閣がキラキラと光っている。見上げるばかりに名も高い、三葉の青い大王松。松、竹、梅の宣伝使を間の里から迎えて来て勝ち誇った照山彦。苔むす巌や幾百の樹、果てしなく広がる築山の広庭の前に立ち現れた照山彦は大音声で、

「常世神王広国別、あ、いやいや、大国彦神に申し上げます。吾等二人は、大命を奉じ夜を日に継いで間の国の酋長、春山彦の館へ罷り出で、三人の娘を召し捕らえて帰って参りましたので、とくと御実検を願い奉ります」

続いて竹山彦も大音声で、

「仰せに従い、ようやく使命を果たして立ち帰って参りました。聞いていた以上に並外れた国の美女の誉れ、例えるものもない天下の美形であり、永くこの城内に留め置かせられ、黄泉比良坂の桃の実として陣中に遣わされたならば、いかなる英雄豪傑も、美人の一瞥に魂を奪われ魄は散り、帰順することは火を見るよりも明らかでありましょう。敵の糧を以て敵を制するのは、これぞ六韜三略の神算鬼謀、常世神王の御盛運はいよいよますます六合に輝き渡ることでございましょう」

との報告に、常世神王はにっこりと笑って、

「今日に始まったことではない二人の働きに感じ入る。何はともあれ三人の宣伝使をこの場に誘い連れて来るがよい」

と厳命するのを、照山彦は従神の固虎に向かって、

「やや固虎、松、竹、梅の宣伝使をここへ案内せよ」

「承知いたしました」

と固虎はこの場を立ち去り、三人の輿の前に現れて、

「やや、三人の娘よ、よく聴け。吾こそは常世神王の御家来である鷹取別のその家来、照山彦の片腕として選ばれた、心も堅く、頭も固く、腕は鉄よりもまだ固い、固虎彦の命であるぞよ。何としても容易ならぬ、ウラル教を敵と睨む頑固者の女宣伝使が、恐れ多くも間の国へ、女の身でありながら三五教を宣伝に来るとは、まことに片腹痛い。鉄門によって堅く守られた常世の城、いかにガタガタ震えても、焦っても、もがいても、どうにもこうにも仕方があるまい。もうこうなった以上は、常世神王の御言葉を固く守り、片意地を張って頑固を通し続けるわけにはゆかない。さあさあ、これから奥殿へ連れて行く。この固虎の足跡を踏んで出て来い」

§ 2

Thus, squaring his shoulders with rough breath, he remained motionless like a petrified stone.

Having no recourse, the three maidens hid their tears with their sleeves and, smiling, delicately parted their flower-like lips:

"Well now, Sir Katatorahiko, thank you for your service as messenger. Say what you will, we are frail women and do not firmly know the rules of the castle, so we earnestly pray and request that you kindly intercede on our behalf."

Katatora said, "Ha! When this Katatora spoke with wordplays on 'kata', these fools proved to be exceedingly crafty. Like a fool and a pair of scissors, depending on how they are used, they might become the right arm of the Divine King of Tokoyo, or if things go wrong, they might become traitors from within, turning into enemies and rattling down this Tokoyo Castle that is harder than rock—demonic women indeed. Be that as it may, my duty is to bring these three maidens to the side of the Divine King and settle this matter. Hear, everyone! Guard the Propagators of Pine, Bamboo, and Plum firmly, and follow after Katatora!"

So saying, he squared his shoulders, pushed open the sliding door at his side, and proceeded deeper and deeper within. His figure soon vanished, leaving no news except one-sided rumors; like an abandoned boat on a desolate shore, Kanihiko, walking sideways on the path with a face that found no harbor, was utterly disheartened, frothing at the mouth, and said:

"Ah, ah, it is all completely over! When I briefly peered into the palanquin and beheld her, how beautiful was Lady Matsuyo's figure, and her younger sisters too, neither yielding to the other, like flowers and autumn leaves, whether peach or cherry, or plum blossoms—truly splendid beings they were. Ah, we too, under this cold sky where the winter gale blows fiercely, ordered to stand gate-guard without even a speck of fire, inquiring of the people coming and going from morning to night with this and that, had been spending painful and bitter days; but just as the moon appears in the dark of night, when I saw the faces of the three maidens, my heart cleared completely like a cloudless world, and in a way beyond all comparison, the sun and moon of ultimate truth shone brightly upon the ocean of my heart. Good grief! Just as I thought that even during our normally miserable gate-guarding duty we might occasionally behold such beautiful goddesses, I was so startled that my legs gave way and I tumbled over. Well, squids, octopuses, and crabs all have legs enough for four people! Encountering beauties said to topple castles, I too have toppled my body!"

Akaguma said, "Hey, hey, you fellow Kanihiko, how unsightly! How could someone whose heart is bewitched by women ever serve as a gatekeeper? You had better watch yourself."

"W-w-what are you spouting, you Akaguma? Making such an utterly sour face as though King Enma were inspecting the registers of the dead, flaunting the arrogant face of an official before people; yet at night you have your wife pour your drinks while guzzling sake, and I have seen your face at those times over and over—it was an unsightly mess not fit to be seen! What is that expression of yours, looking as if even glancing at a woman is filthy? Rather than continuing a life of falsehood, tormenting yourself and binding yourself in this world of absolute freedom, and leading a dull life, why not carry yourself frankly and innocently without the slightest affectation, like this Kanihiko? If you become too stiff, they will call you a second Katatora!"

"With a squashed face looking as if a monkey pelted you with an astringent persimmon, talking about toppling your waist or speaking of beauties is downright ridiculous!"

The heavenly sun, which until then had shone brightly, suddenly turned pitch black, and the universe became so dark that one could not discern things even an inch away; the wind roared in all directions as if a million fierce tigers were roaring wildly, creating a scene as if Tokoyo Castle itself would be shattered to smithereens like autumn leaves. Akaguma and Kanihiko covered their ears and closed their eyes, prostrating themselves upon the ground like flat spiders or crabs, merely awaiting the time when the heavens would brighten and the wind would cease.
