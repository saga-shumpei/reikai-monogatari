Chapter 5: Bizarre Phantoms of the Vulpine [435]

第5章　狐々怪々〔435〕

§ 1

After the two imperial envoys, Miyamawake and Kunitamahime, had leisurely departed, the inner palace was somehow drained of spirits, and only the sighs of one another could be heard.

"Wahahaha! Well now, how capricious this world is! After Teruyamahiko and I exerted all our strength and gave our whole hearts to accompany the three of them—Matsu, Take, and Ume—to this place, putting Lord Tokoyo-shin'ō in exceeding good humor, and barely after we were blessed with words of praise from the Divine King and rejoiced, even granting that this is a world of flux and change, how swiftly things alter! Turn the hand, and it becomes clouds; flip the hand, and it becomes rain. We brought them back with such joy, thinking we had added brocade upon flowers within Tokoyo Castle, but only for a brief moment; whether a dream, reality, or an illusion, there came the strict order from the Great Deity of the Rocky Mountains to capture the three—Matsu, Take, and Ume—alive without delay and send them over. How can we convey such a stern command to Lord Tokoyo-shin'ō, who was in such a splendid mood? It is as if one can already see him sinking into the depths of disappointment and despair, like having a jewel snatched from his palm. Well, well, by nature of duty, a subordinate like Takeyamahiko is quite convenient at times like this. Now, now, Lord Takatoriwake, you should report the purport of the imperial envoys to Tokoyo-shin'ō."

Takatoriwake, raising his face with his crushed nose, said:

"Fuga-fuga-honga... Such a thing, even without Takatoriwake having to say it, let Takeyamahiko, who is so articulate of mouth, report it."

"'Fuga-fuga-fuga, such a thing'—Takeyamahiko does not understand in the least what that means. I am utterly at a loss. Ah, it cannot be helped; it is Lord Tōyamawake's turn. Please make haste to report it."

Tōyamawake, making a face as if he had chewed a bitter worm, stood up heavily and, with lumbering steps toward the main hall, stepped out sluggishly.

"It is just like a hound that caught a boar at the risk of its life, only to be struck on the head by the hunter with the stock of his gun. It is a bizarre scene, neither amusing nor welcome, like crossing three ri over mountain slopes to the sake shop and five ri to the tofu shop, finally buying fried tofu and returning almost to the eaves of one's house, only to have a black kite from the sky peck one's bald head, and just as one collapsed in astonishment, having the fried tofu snatched away. Lord Tokoyo-shin'ō too has had a brief dream. Shortly after rejoicing over such a splendid night as this, as auspicious as a wedding, it has become utterly incomprehensible, as though bewitched by a fox. That is why they speak of the pillow of Handan's dream. Ah, kon-kon-chikichin, konchikichin! Takatoriwake snatched away the beautiful women whom Haruyamahiko had delightedly harbored, seizing them as a hawk seizes sparrows, and brought them back to receive praise from Tokoyo-shin'ō, intending to return home proudly tonight and boast to his wife; yet his nose is crushed into this state, and as for Lord Hirokuniwake, the Tokoyo-shin'ō, I fully sympathize with his astonishment tonight. Could there ever be such a contrast in this world again? Should one say having one's buttocks plucked by a snapping turtle, or having one's cauldron stolen on a moonlit night, this indescribable turn of events tonight is truly the height of distress and destitution. Kon-kon-chikichin, konchikichin!"

§ 2

Teruyamahiko: "Hey, Takeyamahiko, this is no time to be talking such nonsense. We must somehow devise a remedial measure. Is it not thanks to having successfully brought back the three maidens that you have finally come to stand shoulder to shoulder with Lord Takatoriwake? When one considers the feelings of Lord Shin'ō, how could you be saying such carefree things?"

"Well now, I am at a loss; will someone not lend some good wisdom to this Takeyamahiko?"

At this juncture, the false Tokoyo Shin'ō, Hirokuniwake, appeared at this place accompanied by Tōyamawake, wearing an unwell expression, and said:

"Ah, everyone, we must send the three maidens, Matsu, Take, and Ume, to the mansion on Mount Rocky as quickly as possible. Having said that, however..."

Takeyamahiko: "Whether you say 'having said that,' or say 'demon,' or say 'tiger,' there is simply nothing Takeyamahiko can do. Let us ask Lord Takatoriwake to lend us his wisdom. Hello, Lord Takatoriwake, what is your opinion?"

He deliberately spoke in a nasal voice. Takatoriwake only:

"Fuga, fuga, fuga,"

continued to utter incomprehensible language.

"Although it is contrary to my true wishes to hand over the three maidens whom I have taken a fancy to, I wish to keep them within this castle for a little longer. I hear that Haruyamahiko has three flower-like daughters named Tsuki, Yuki, and Hana; what if we were to use some means to bring the three daughters back and send them to Mount Rocky as substitutes?"

"Indeed, truly worthy of Lord Tokoyo Shin'ō, a splendid and clever plan; I, Tōyamawake, shall prepare my words, head for the Land of Aida from here, and have the three maidens brought back."

"I leave all the details to you. Manage it as you see fit."

"Wahahaha, a clever plan, a clever plan, myōchiki-chin, kon-kon-chiki-chin, kon-chiki-chin, Takeyamahiko, kan-kan-chiki-chin, kan-chiki-chin."

At this moment, the inspector Kariwaka rushed forward in haste and said:

"I have a report. Just now, Lord Nakayorihiko has returned to the castle from the Land of Aida, carrying in a wicker cage a formidable man named Teruhiko, a missionary of the Ananai Teachings. How shall we deal with him?"

"Ah, Tōyamawake and all the rest of you, drag this fellow Teruhiko whom Nakayoriwake brought back to this front garden, and interrogate him in detail."

All: "Understood!"

Tokoyo Shin'ō leisurely proceeded into the sleeping quarters.

§ 3

Straddling his horse, calmly, his spirit soaring to the heavens with fierce breaths, having captured Teruhiko, the missionary of the Ananai-kyō, Nakayoriwake returned to the front of the gate of Tokoyo Castle, pounded on the door of the gate, and cried in a loud voice.

"Hey, gatekeeper, it is Nakayoriwake. Open this gate quickly."

Kanihiko: "Well, what is this? Has another strange fellow come? Saying 'open from the inside' (naka-yori-ake)—what an obvious thing to say! It is only natural to open a bolted gate from the inside. If it were a gate that could be opened from the outside, he wouldn't need to shout from outside; he could just open it silently and come in."

Muttering this, he softly slid the bolt open to the left and right.

Nakayoriwake, while straddling his horse, said,

"Hey, Kanihiko, thank you for the trouble of opening the gate in the middle of the night. After this palanquin has passed through, shut the gate doors firmly and guard them. Now then, my retainers, you have done well. Return to your respective homes and rest."

Having tossed out these words, he proceeded deeper and deeper within.

Outside the middle gate, Nakayoriwake lightly leaped down from his horse, and stroking its mane, face, and neck, he said,

"Ah, bay horse, I have caused you much trouble for a long time. Go to the stable and rest at your ease."

Kanihiko, swaying his slender body that was bent sideways above the waist, came running, peered briefly inside the palanquin, and cried,

"Eeeek!"

and again, losing the use of his legs in fright, he fell flat upon the ground. The middle gate was quickly opened, and Nakayoriwake, having the palanquin carried along, proceeded deep into the interior, had the palanquin set down in the garden, and said:

"I have now returned to the castle without mishap. Please inspect carefully Teruhiko, the missionary of the Ananai-kyō."

Takeyamahiko: "Well, well, Lord Nakayoriwake! A great deed, a great deed! Surely it must be a beautiful woman."

"No, what are you saying, Lord Takeyamahiko? He is a stout man, sporting an eight-shaped mustache, capable of crushing even a demon with his robust muscles; do not let your guard down."

Takatoriwake, in a nasal voice, said,

"Horeha-horeha, Hakahakahiyoriwake-ka, hiyakume, haigi-haigi."

Takeyamahiko: "One hundred me, two hundred me, one kanme, a rough man of thirty kanme—surely he must have been heavy."

"Lord Takeyamahiko, jokes depend on the occasion. Lord Nakayoriwake has weathered wind and rain, braved hardships, fulfilled his mission, and at last returned; will you not treat him with courtesy? Teruyamahiko gives you this warning."

From inside the palanquin,

"Teruhiko, missionary of the Ananai-kyō, is but a temporary name; I, Toyamatsumi-no-Kami, bearing the name of the Heavenly Counting Song—one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten—present myself before you!"
