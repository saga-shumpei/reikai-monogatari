§ 1

In the inner palace of Tokoyo Castle, carpeted entirely with floral wool rugs, Tokoyoshin'ō appeared upon the elevated seat in the center, flanked by Takatoriwake and Tamayamahiko on the left and right, and sat arrogantly as though having formed a crane-wing battle formation. Teruyamahiko and Takeyamahiko retreated far back and waited in attendance at the lower seats, and when they recounted volubly the entire course of events of their mission to the Land of Aida, Tokoyoshin'ō was in exceedingly high spirits, saying:

"Splendid, splendid is your achievement! Even the renowned Hinodeshin will surely marvel at your unrestrained, ingenious stratagems. From now on, exert your strength even further and manifest preeminent deeds and achievements."

"Ah, these unexpected words of praise are far too gracious and beyond the station of Teruyamahiko. Henceforth, I am resolved to crush my bones and powder my body, sparing no toil of dog or horse, ever more for the sake of Tokoyoshin'ō."

Takatoriwake said, "Not contradicting my conjecture, both of you successfully accomplished today's mission, and Tokoyoshin'ō must surely be well pleased. Takatoriwake too is deeply impressed."

"What nonsense! Tokoyoshin'ō, or whatever—Hirokuniwake's Ōkunihiko, Ōkunihiko's Hirokuniwake, endlessly shifting and freely changing as the Great Sovereign God—how infuriating it was in the heart of Takeyamahiko when hearing such rumors on the road! The clear, upright heart of Takeyamahiko, like split bamboo, felt somehow enveloped in the dark clouds of the eternal world. No matter how many missionaries of the Anaikyō may come to the land of Tokoyo, as long as Takeyamahiko is here, I shall seek out their whereabouts with my heavenly clairvoyance, drag them out one by one before your presence, and present them to your eyes. Look at this Teruyamahiko with his shining head and his smug face of achievement; it is so ridiculous that it makes one boil tea on one's navel, wahahahaha!"

Thus Takeyamahiko's laughter echoed on all sides.

The sun had already tilted to the west, and the village crows announcing the dusk, crying kahai-kahai, were returning toward their roosts. The sound of the bell tolling impermanence rang out: ding, ding, ding, ding-ding-ding. Teruyamahiko, whose head shone so brightly in the silver candle illuminating the room, suddenly rose to his feet,

"Here, here, Katatora, Katatora, why are you dawdling? Bring the three to this place at once!"

When he called out thus, Katatora said immediately upon that voice:

"I shall bring the three maidens there right away. Please wait a moment."

Just as he spoke, all four sides suddenly turned pitch-black, a violent wind blew fiercely, the pillars of the inner palace began to sway back and forth and to the left and right, subterranean rumbles like a hundred thunders roaring at once resounded in succession, the brilliant light of the silver candles vanished abruptly, and a rock-door of pitch darkness in which black and white could not be distinguished was let down. "Ho, ho, servants! In this darkness where one can scarce distinguish close at hand, light the lanterns as quickly as possible!"

The calling voice, enveloped in a roar like a hundred peals of thunder resounding all at once, was fretfully inaudible. Tokoyo-shin'ō, losing his presence of mind, sat upright in the dark, waiting silently with a pounding heart, wondering what would become of it all. Threading through the darkness, a single ball like a toy handball suddenly appeared in the center of the room and rapidly began floating about the center of the room to the right and left, forward and back. However, it was merely red in color and emitted no radiance at all; the ball changed into various colors—red, white, yellow, and purple—and when it struck with a thud against the bald head of Teruyamahiko,

"Ouch, ouch, ouch!"

Teruyamahiko fell flat on his face. As the ball began bouncing—thud, thud, thud, thud—using Teruyamahiko's head as a starting point just like a child bouncing a handball, Takatoriwake, attempting to strike that ball, missed his footing from his seat and fell head over heels on his back, whereupon the ball shifted its location and again began bouncing like a handball—thud, thud, thud, thud—atop the head of Takatoriwake who had fallen on his back. Strangely, Takatoriwake's body stiffened and could not stir in the slightest, and the ball once again changed its position, came atop his nose, and again bounced like a handball. Takatoriwake,

"Ouch, ouch, ouch, my nose is breaking! I cannot bear it, I cannot bear it!"

strained out in a tearful voice. As the ball once again flew whining toward the forehead of Tokoyo-shin'ō and collided with it, Tokoyo-shin'ō, by that force, somersaulted backward on his back from the dais and tumbled down; and while he was groaning in agony with faint breath, Takeyamahiko shouted in a great voice from the darkness:

"Ah, utterly bizarre is the sight of this place! Hateful Yasomagatsukami, who has become a fireball to raise wind and rain, sound a whining roar, and once again perturb Tokoyo Castle, perish before the power of my kotodama!"

No sooner had he spoken than the fireball vanished without a trace, the sound of the wind that had been raging wildly until then ceased completely, and stars shone brightly across the whole sky. Takeyamahiko took out his fire striker, struck sparks with a click-click, and lit the silver candles, whereupon the surroundings shone as brightly as midday. At this time Katatora, who had brought the three daughters along, had lost the use of his legs and was crouching at the entrance.

"Ah, an unexpected assault by an evil god! What a dreadful state of affairs! Lord Tokoyo-shin'ō, are you injured? I, Takeyamahiko, am anxious for you. Nay, Lady Matsushirohime, please nurse the Shin'ō. What a dreadful state, Lord Takatoriwake, you too have an unusual complexion; I perceive that you have been struck by the evil fireball. Lady Takenohime, please nurse him. What has happened to Lord Takatoriwake's nose? Oh, it is utterly ruined!"

Just at that time, a sweet fragrance filled the room, and clear, ringing music became audible from nowhere in particular. Tokoyo-shin'ō was saved by Matsushirohime and was in exceedingly good humor; Takatoriwake and Teruyamahiko, too, were nursed by Takenohime and Umegakahime, and, forgetting the pain of their crushed heads and noses, were filled with delight. The sound of the music grew ever more serene and clear, and somehow the surroundings grew bustling.

The roaring sound of the Heavenly Rock-Boat and Bird-Boat echoing in the sky became audible as if right in hand. From this time forth, starting with the three maidens Matsu, Take, and Ume, Takeyamahiko gained the high favor of Tokoyo-shin'ō and came to participate in the councils for every single important matter.

鷹取別は、搗き立ての団子のやうな鼻をペコペコさせながら、何かフガフガ言つて居るばかり。

『貴殿の御言葉は判然いたさぬ。フガフガとは何の事でござるか。不甲斐ないことだとの御歎きか。ヤアヤア照山彦殿、貴下の頭は如何遊ばされた。実に妙な恰好でござる。梅ケ香姫殿、サア早く御介抱遊ばさるるがよからう』

『ヤアヤア、常世の国の雪起しか、城倒しか何だか知らないが、生れてから見たこともない天狗風が吹きよつて、この固虎も吃驚仰天、歯の根もガタガタガタ虎になつて了つた。皆の方々は美しい御介抱人が出来て結構だが、吾々は肩は抜け、腰は抜け、旁型の悪いものでござる。三人のお方は夫々御介抱人があつて結構だが、この固虎に限りて誰も世話する女がないとは、片手落にも程がある。何れの方か此の場に現はれて、わが身の介抱して呉れてもよささうなものだな』

竹山彦『オイ固虎、貴様は日頃から無信心で、おまけにヱルサレムの宮で昔から型もないやうな悪戯をいたしただらう。それが為に時節到来、神様が仇敵を御討ち遊ばしたのぢや。ガタ虎でなうてカタキとられだ。御気の毒様ながら、生命の失くなるまで、其処で辛抱なさるがよからう』

『ヤア竹山彦殿、そんなこと所ではない。本当に真面目になつて、誰か呼んで来て下さいな』

『常世神王様、お歴々の方々のこの大難を救はねばならぬ吾々の任務、汝が如きに介抱する暇があらうか』
