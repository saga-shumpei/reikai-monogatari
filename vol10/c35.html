Chapter 35: The Altai Cavern [465]

第35章　アルタイ窟〔465〕

§ 1

At the propagation songs of the two, Ishikoridome-no-kami and Umegakahime, all the people, beginning with the chieftain Kanahiko, finally stroked their chests in relief, accepted Umegakahime's opinion, made a substitute for Kiyohime, placed Umegakahime inside a Chinese-style chest, and Ishikoridome-no-kami and the gatekeeper Tokikō carried the chest on their shoulders; being seen off by several hundred men and women of all ages, as they reached the entrance of Mount Altai, suddenly the whole sky was colored in deep black, and a fierce mountain wind blew violently as if to blow away even rocks. While struggling against the wind, they at last reached the front of the mountain stronghold, quietly placed the chest containing Umegakahime before the cavern, and the villagers, vying with one another, stumbled and tumbled down the dark mountain path.

Ishikoridome-no-kami and Tokikō, the two of them, lay down within a nearby thicket and watched to see how matters were. After a while, over the whole surface of Mount Altai, blue firelights began to twinkle like the stars of the great sky, and from among them, a fire like an even larger pine torch made a buzzing roar and rushed around above the chest, back and forth, left and right, for nearly two hours, while from inside the chest a faint propagation song was resounding. Fearing this voice perhaps, the large firelight merely circled in the sky above and did not easily descend.

The hundreds and thousands of pale-blue fires of the mountain gradually vanished, turning into a pitch blackness where nothing could be discerned, and the light of the pine-torch fire grew thinner and smaller by degrees. From inside the chest, the propagation song came to be heard gradually louder. A clump of fire suddenly danced up into the sky and fled, trailing a band toward the southwestern sky.

Ishikoridome-no-kami, seeing this state, folded his arms and said,

"Hey, Tokikō, did you see the fire just now? It was quite splendid. One could by no means see such splendid fire unless it were Mount Altai."

Tokikō said, "Yes, it was indeed terrifying. Somehow my body felt as if it shrank, and neither my hands nor my feet would move."

"What a fainthearted fellow you are. Guided by her voice, though it is troublesome, could you not step over to the side of the chest and check how Umegakahime is doing?"

"Yes—no, out of the question! How could my legs possibly stand?"

"In that case I will go, so you stay hidden here."

As he said this and was about to stand up, Tokikō said,

"Wait, please take me along with you. It would be unbearable to be left behind all alone in such a place."

と言って立ち上がろうとするのを時公は、

「もしもし、私も一緒に連れて行ってください。こんな所に一人放っておかれてはたまりませんよ」

§ 2

Ishikoridome said, "Did you not say that neither your hands nor your feet could move? Even if you tell me to take you along, I cannot carry you on my back in this darkness, so it cannot be helped. Just wait here meekly."

"No, if that is the case, I will stand and accompany you."

"What a slack fellow, such a coward. Come on then!"

Taking his hand, Ishikoridome advanced gropingly toward the Chinese-style chest. The voices of the propagation songs were leaking out and could be heard outside the chest.

Ishikoridome said, "Oh, Lady Umegakahime, it seems the evil deity has fled."

As he said this, he swiftly removed the lid of the chest; Lady Umegakahime, still in her white garments with her hair disheveled, stood straight up, making her double-edged sword flash in the darkness, and cried,

"Ah, evil god Orochitsukami, who makes his nest in Mount Altai and takes human lives, learn your lesson!"

At her fierce demeanor as she pressed instantly toward the direction of the voice, Tokikō screamed out and fell prostrate upon the spot.

"Oh, Lady Umegakahime, calm yourself. I am Ishikoridome. The demon has already fled toward the southwestern sky as a ball of fire."

Umegakahime said, "Ah, Orochitsukami, fearing my propagation song, do you again use an artifice to falsely claim to be Ishikoridome-no-Kami, missionary of the Ananai Teachings, seeking to entice me? Learn your lesson!"

Drawing her sword, she swung it back and forth, left and right, pressing forward. Ishikoridome, stepping backward, cried,

"Hold, hold, wait, wait! I am the real one!"

Umegakahime said, "At this juncture, I will lend no ear to your clever excuses!"

Still clad in white robes, she slashed at Ishikoridome. Having no other choice, Ishikoridome took the lid of the white chest dimly visible in the darkness, parried Umegakahime's blade, and

repeatedly shouted, "I am Ishikoridome! I am Ishikoridome!"

Umegakahime stumbled over the corner of a rock and fell flat on the spot; however, as it was barely one foot beside where Tokikō had fallen, Tokikō once again raised a shriek, crying,

"H-Heh, Lord Orochitsukami, I-I am a man called Tokikō. I am the one who came to offer the prey you love; please, spare my life alone. I do not know if she will displease you, but if I confess the truth, she is not Kiyohime, but a wandering beggar-princess with some sour name. But you will not know the taste unless you try eating her. If she does not please you, I will bring the real thing again tomorrow night. If she is acceptable, please put up with her and save me!"

Ishikoridome, from within the darkness:

"Ho, that fellow Tokikō, exceedingly outrageous, you have brought a substitute for Kiyohime. If a substitute will suffice, I care not whether it be male or female. This Umegakahime is sour and does not suit my palate. Since your flesh is plumply fattened and looks delicious, shall I feast upon you now?"

Tokikō: "Th-th-th-th-that is wrong, that was not the promise, w-wait, please. Though I care not for this body of mine being eaten, my wife left at home would surely lament. Spare only my life, I beg you. Ah, had I known it would come to this, I would never have listened to what that fellow of the Ananai-kyō, Ishikoridome or whatever, who is like a beggar, said; I will devour that one from now on and avenge your resentment, so please grant me your mercy."

Umegakahime, from the darkness:

"Ho-ho-ho-ho-ho."

Tokikō: "What, ah, how disgusting! Is this a time for laughing ho-ho-ho? I am on the verge of being eaten right now. You are a stand-in, and though you may be glad to be spared because you do not suit the fancy of Lord Hedatsuka, you should try putting yourself in my shoes. While I am in a desperate plight where one rider faces a thousand, having no choice between back and belly, how can a missionary who saves people laugh? Do not make a fool of me. Now that it has come to this, I am desperate. Before I am eaten, I will take your life to vent my rage."

"Ho-ho-ho-ho-ho, Master Tokikō, you are eloquent in speech alone; can you move your hands and feet?"

"Ugh, as if they would not move! I will show you that they can move; appearances notwithstanding, I was once called Tokinogawa and took part in amateur sumo matches. What is a beggar woman, a vile wench like you, blabbering about? Even so, that rascal Ishikoridome, having blown only grand boasts, fled vanishing like clouds and mist. In any case, could there possibly be any decent fellow among the missionaries of Ananai-kyō? Truly, he has made me suffer a terrible plight!"

Ishikoridome, putting on a feigned voice:

"Hey, hey, Tokikō, your loose tongue goes too far. Shall I tear it out starting from your tongue?"

Tokikō: "There is neither from below nor from above. Even if you eat an insipid, hard-boned fellow like me, it will only make you sick to your stomach. My wife says that I am a stylish man, one degree more sour and refined than Umegakahime."

"It is precisely that sour fellow whom I wish to eat."

"After all, it is a lie; the sour one is Umegakahime."

Ishikoridome returned to her original voice and said,

"Hey, Tokikō, you did chirp quite a lot of ill of me. Long ago, Hebitsukami fled toward Armenia. That I said I was Hebitsukami earlier was because, taking advantage of the darkness, I was testing your courage and the good or evil of your heart. You are still far from having reformed."

"Yes, yes, are you the real one? If you are the real one, I will reform from now on, so please forgive me."

"Hebitsukami's physical body has fled, but his spirit possessed me and tells me to eat you. Be sure not to resent Ishikoridome as an ogre-like fellow. The secondary guardian spirit that possessed me is going to eat you from now on."

"You, please drive away such a merciless secondary guardian spirit."

Umegakahime: "Ho, ho, ho, ho."

Tokikō: "Come, come, Lady Umegaka, in traveling a companion, in life sympathy. That the three of us have come out into these deep mountains like this must be due to a deep karma. If you are a missionary, please drive away that thing called 'secondary' or 'guardian.'"

"Ho, ho, ho, ho."

Ishikoridome: "Ahahaha, it is a lie, a lie."

Tokikō: "Ugh, is that so?"

"This is no time for jokes. Dawn is already breaking; come, come, get ready, get ready. Lady Umegaka, since you are a woman, please get into this chest. Tokikō and I will carry it and return."

"Even if you tell me to carry it, my strength has left my legs and I cannot carry it."

While doing so, the sky at dawn was flushed with crimson, and the surroundings faintly broke into day. Looking around, rocks in the shapes of ogres, large and small, lay scattered all over the area. The deity Ishikoridome picked up a suitably sized, slender fragment of rock from the surroundings, blew her breath upon it to make a mallet, and struck and smashed the fossils of the ogres one after another, aiming at their heads; then, wondrously, like mist, bloody smoke burst forth vigorously from those stones. Decapitating every single one of the fossils of countless tens and hundreds of ogres, the three now descended the mountain with composure, and once again, heading for the residence of Tetsuhiko, the chieftain of Tetsudani Village, they returned triumphantly singing a song of victory.
