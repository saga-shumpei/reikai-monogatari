§ 1

In order to solidify the land drifting like unto jellyfish, Ishikoridome-no-Kami-no-Mikoto, the missionary of steadfast heart, spoke Kotodama, and at those words four or five natives of the riverside, bearing hard heads, crossed over precariously westward at last via the floating wooden bridge over the Uchi River;

A: "Ah, missionary of the Ananai teachings, thanks to you I have been able to return to my native village for the first time in three years. This is entirely thanks to you. I would dearly love to ask you to drop by my village, but there is a slight, well, a slight..."

Ishikoridome: "What is this, speaking so hesitantly? Speak clearly, will you?"

"Hey, Burikō, won't you speak on my behalf?"

Burikō: "Do not speak so naively. Even I, Burikō, have only just crossed the river after such a long time and finally felt relieved; if I were to say such a thing clearly, the evil deity of Mount Altai would fume with anger, and heaven knows what disaster would fall upon my head. Then my wife, who after three years had welcomed me home at last, looked upon my handsome face, and was feeling relieved and delighted, would say, 'You are a fool, babbling about such an important matter and bringing this upon yourself,' and get furiously angry at me, swinging her big backside angrily in my face, which would be terrible."

Ishikoridome: "Hey, you fellows make no sense. What is this 'buri-buri'?"

B: "No, every single day heavy rain pours fiercely down, and the Uchi River has suffered a tremendous flood. My wife too gets furiously angry, and it would be disastrous if a rain of tears should pour down, so please, forgive us this one thing."

"If you fellows dawdle and do not reveal the secret, I have plans of my own. Shall I treat you to a stone ball again?"

While saying this, she immediately grasped earth, formed a dumpling, and blew her breath upon it, whereupon:

A: "Please, please wait! If I am struck by such a stone ball, my head will be split open with a crack in an instant, and I cannot bear it. Even my liver of courage will be crushed, and my testicles will shrivel up."

Ishikoridome: "If so, will you not obediently confess your secret-sounding manner of speaking?"

"Yes, yes, it cannot be helped, I will speak cleanly. Well, the truth is, truly it is, really, really, truly, er, of Mount Altai, ah, the awkward Magatsukami is, well, well, well, you see..."

§ 2

"What you say makes less and less sense; speak seriously. Otherwise I shall give you another stone bullet."

"Hey, Burikō, instead of letting me do all the talking, why do you not share some of the responsibility?"

Burikō: "Well, it cannot be helped. Whatever happens, will the propagation missionary of Ananaikyō take charge of it for us?"

Ishikoridome: "I will take charge of anything; do not be alarmed, and speak the truth plainly."

"On Mount Altai dwells an exceedingly terrible evil deity named Hebizukami. That deity devours twelve large snakes day after day. When there are no snakes, he brings many henchmen to the villages around here and abducts and eats wives and daughters in their stead, so, unable to bear having our precious wives and daughters eaten, the people of the villages divide the work among themselves and catch twelve thick snakes every day to offer them at the cave of Mount Altai. In the summer there are many snakes and catching them is easy, but when it becomes cold like this, they all burrow into the ground, so attempting to catch them takes immense effort, and if we do not catch them, our wives and children are abducted and eaten before we know it; indeed, the people of this area risk their lives when it comes to catching giant snakes. If the fact that we have told you such a thing should reach the ears of Hebizukami, the evil god of Mount Altai, this village would surely suffer total destruction, so please, help us."

"Well, I wondered what it was, but is that all? All right, all right. I shall now climb Mount Altai and subdue that evil god Hebizukami. You guide the way."

All: "We will guide you, but having only just returned after three years, please allow us to return home once and meet our wives and children before guiding you."

"Since you have been away for three years, your wives and children may well have already been eaten."

How pitiful it was as all of them cried out in unison and wept prostrate.

Ishikoridome: "Oh, do not worry; such a thing is hardly likely to have happened. In any case, I shall stay in your village for a while and observe the situation."

Saying this, she proceeded toward their settlement together with all of them.

This settlement was called Tetsudanimura. By the time the party approached the entrance of this village, the pitch-black curtain of darkness had already fallen, and dark clouds covered the sky, turning it into a world of total darkness where one could not even discern what was close at hand.

§ 3

In Kanatani Village, which had seventy or eighty houses, for some reason not a single light was lit in any house. Faint sounds of sniffling and weeping could be heard. Burikō's party, owing to the extreme darkness, could not even distinguish their own homes, and advanced gropingly, step by step, with the gait of blind men without canes. In a somewhat elevated place, a pillar of firelight suddenly began to flicker. When Ishikoridome and the others of the party advanced steadily toward that fire and looked, this was the mansion of Kanehiko, the chieftain of Kanatani Village. It felt as if some secret lurked there. The deity Ishikoridome-no-kami stood before the gate and began to sing a proselytizing hymn in a clear voice. From within the gate, a thunderous voice was raised:

"Hey, who goes there, standing before this gate and singing a proselytizing hymn of the Ananai teachings? A grave crisis has befallen the chieftain's household; this is no time to be uttering such carefree things. I know not who or what sort of fellow you are, but if you play such pranks, your head will fly off!"

From outside the gate, Burikō raised his voice:

"Hey, is that voice not Tokikō the gatekeeper? I crossed over to the opposite bank of the Uchi River three years ago, and because the log bridge fell, I did not return until today and know nothing at all of the village's condition, but what is this grave crisis of the chieftain's house?"

Tokikō: "What do you mean, 'what is it?' Tonight we must offer the chieftain's daughter, Lady Kiyohime, as a human sacrifice at the fortress on Mount Altai to the evil deity Hebitsukami! When I think that such a beautiful, precious young girl will be made prey to the evil deity Hebitsukami, I am so filled with pity and regret that I can hardly bear it. Despite that, what do you mean by singing the proselytizing hymn that Hebitsukami hates? Your own daughter was eaten too, was it not this spring?"

Burikō,

"What!"

with only those words, collapsed heavily upon the spot and lost consciousness.

A: "Hey, I am Yoshikō, whom you know well; open the gate! It is guaranteed that the chieftain will surely rejoice. Because I have welcomed and brought the deity of salvation, Lady Kiyohime too will surely be saved. Won't you open it quickly?"

"Well, be that as it may, shall I open it?"

Tokikō said, and seized by slight anxiety, rattled open the plain wooden gate. The deity Ishikoridome-no-kami,

"Pardon me,"

said while passing through the gate and stepping inside.
