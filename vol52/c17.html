Chapter 17: 飴屋 [1353]

飴屋〔1353〕

§ 1

Reishiyutaijiyū refers to a state in which the inner aspect of a Ningen opens toward Kami, loving only Kami, understanding Kami, accumulating Zentoku, causing true Chie to shine, dwelling in the Shintoku of faith, and not being in the least bit caught up in Guwaiteki Jibutsu. Such a person is what is called a Tennin on earth, one who is registered in Tengoku while still alive, and this Seirei is called the Honshiyugojin. One cannot dwell in this Kiyōgū unless one dwells in the virtues of Shisui, Shijiyun, Shibi, Shizen, Shiai, and Shishin.

Furthermore, Taishiyureijiyū means that since a Ningen is, by necessity, something that exists between the Reikai and the Genkai, one opens Tengoku on one side and Jigoku on the other. Therefore, a Ningen must, by necessity, serve the divine work of the world while good and evil, beauty and ugliness are intermingled. However, this refers to a Ningen who is neither of ordinary general good nor of evil. Because a Ningen takes the physical body as a foundation and also as an ultimate point, it is called Taishiyureijiyū when viewed from the Guwaiteki aspect, but if one speaks of this subjectively, it is something that should be five parts spiritual and five parts physical, that is, five spiritual and five physical. If one comes to slight the spirit and value the body, it becomes five physical and five spiritual here. Even though both are five physical and five spiritual, it becomes five spiritual and five physical, or five physical and five spiritual, depending on whether the love that governs it is Guwaiteki or internal. Therefore, a Ningen of five spiritual and five physical has their inner aspect opened toward Tengoku, and a Ningen of five physical and five spiritual has their inner aspect opened toward Jigoku.

Generally, when one speaks of Taishiyureijiyū, it is considered evil in the explanation of spiritual studies, but Taishiyureijiyū refers to the Kiyōgū of a Ningen who is lost in the Chiukan while alive. Even if a Ningen does their utmost best and does not commit a single evil, they become either good or evil depending on the nature of their heart, nature, and emotions. Therefore, a Ningen cannot, by necessity, descend below five spiritual and five physical. If one descends below this, one must immediately fall into the Jigoku realm. No matter how much one thinks they have done their best, Tengoku and Jigoku are divided depending on whether that love is divine or natural, so if a Taishiyureijiyū Ningen commits even one evil deed in this world, it will inevitably become not five physical and five spiritual, but five physical and four spiritual, or seven physical and three spiritual, and one must immediately fall into the path of Jigoku.

Among believers, there seem to be some who take sacred words such as 'good and evil are non-dual' or 'right and wrong are one' as a shield and interpret them as they please, but this is said by Kami toward Ningen who are lost in the Chiukan, and it is a word spoken from the standpoint that Kami approaches with a heart of compassion regardless of good and evil. It is by no means a word that a Ningen should utter. As long as a Ningen maintains a physical body and is in this world, they cannot, by necessity, perform absolute good. However, if one can obtain registration in Tengoku in one's inner life, one can already be called a person of Reishiyutaijiyū.

The crossroads of the Chiukan are a court of judgment for good and evil, right and wrong, and the majority of today's Ningen are registered in this Chiukan and the Jigoku realm. However, when a Ningen crosses the barrier of the separation of spirit and flesh and goes to the Reikai, their Guwaiteki state is removed as time passes, so only their inner aspect exists, and here they come to lead a spiritual life. At this time, all the evil attached to the physical body is wiped away, and their pure spirit can harmonize with the group of Tengoku in accordance with the spirit. However, a Seirei with too strong a sense of selfishness leaves behind its attachment even after death and is not easily eradicated, so only the Guwaiteki aspect opens, and as the Guwaiteki aspect falls away with the passage of time, the evil at its inner bottom is immediately exposed, and it falls into the Jigoku realm, becoming a wretched appearance like a monster or a specter.

霊主体従とは、人間の内分が神に向つて開け、唯神を愛し、神を理解し、善徳を積み、真の智慧を輝かし、信の真徳に居り、外的の事物に些しも拘泥せざる状態を云ふのである。斯の如き人は所謂地上の天人にして、生きながら天国に籍を置いて居る者で、この精霊を称して本守護神と云ふのである。至粋、至純、至美、至善、至愛、至真の徳に居るものでなくては、此境遇に居る事は出来ぬ。

又体主霊従とは、人間はどうしても霊界と現界との中間に介在するものである以上は、一方に天国を開き一方に地獄を開いて居るものだ。故に人間はどうしても善悪混交美醜互に交はつて世の中の神業に奉仕せなくてはならない。併しこれは、普通一般の善にも非ず悪にも非ざる人間の事である。人間は肉体を基礎とし、又終極点とするが故に、外的方面より見て体主霊従と云ふのであるが、併しながら、之を主観的に云へば霊的五分、体的五分、即ち、霊五体五たるべきものである。若し霊を軽んじ体を重んずるに至らば、茲に、体五霊五となるのである。同じ体五分霊五分と雖も、其所主の愛が外的なると、内的なるとに依つて、霊五体五となり、又体五霊五となるのである。故に霊五体五の人間は、天国に向つて内分が開け、体五霊五の人間は、地獄に向つて其内分が開けて居るものである。

一般に体主霊従と云へば、霊学の説明上悪となつて居るが、併し体主霊従とは、生ながら中有界に迷つて居る人間の境遇を云ふのである。人間は最善を尽し、唯一つの悪をなさなくても其心性情動の如何に依りて、或は善となり或は悪となるものである。故に人間は、どうしても霊五体五より下る事は出来ない。これを下れば忽ち地獄界に堕ちねばならぬのである。何程善を尽したと思つて居ても、其愛が神的なると自然的なるとに依つて、天国地獄が分るるのであるから、体主霊従的人間が、現世に於て一つでも悪事をなしたならば、どうしても是は体五霊五所か体六霊四、体七霊三となりて、忽ち地獄道へ落ちねばならぬのである。

信者の中には善悪不二とか、正邪一如とか云ふ聖言を楯に取つて、自分の勝手のよいやうに解釈して居る人もあるやうだが、是は神が善悪不二と云はるるのは中有界に迷へる人間に対して云はれるのであり、且神は善悪に拘はらず慈愛の心をもつて臨ませらるる見地から仰せらるる言葉である。決して人間の云為すべき言葉ではない。どうしても人間が肉体を保つて現世にある間は、絶対的の善を為す事は出来ない。併しながら其内的生涯に於て天国に籍を置く事を得るならば、最早これを霊主体従の人と云ふ事が出来るのである。

中有界の八衢は善悪正邪の審判所であつて、今日の人間の大部分はこの中有界と地獄界に籍を置いて居るものである。されども人間が霊肉脱離の関門を越えて霊界に行つた時は、其外分の情態は時を経るに従つて除却さるるが故に、其内分のみ存在し、茲に霊的生涯を営む事となる。此時は肉体に附ける総ての悪は払拭され、其純潔なる霊は天国の団体に、霊相応に和合し得るものである。併しながら余り利己心の強い精霊は、死後に至るまで其執着を残し、容易に駆除されないが故に、外分のみ開け、且又外分が時を追うて脱離すると共に其内底の悪は忽ち暴露され、妖怪変化の如き浅ましき面貌となつて地獄界に堕ち往くものである。

§ 2

Bunsuke finally arrived at the Sekishiyo of Yachimata. Guided with relative politeness by two guards, one white and one red, he sat down on a stone bench beside the gate to catch his breath. About a Hanchiyō away, a noisy sound could be heard.

Tonchin tonton, chinchin tonton,

Chinton, chinton, chinchinton,

In the Yokochiyō where the Yufuhi is red,

The old man of the Ameya rings his gong.

Big Kodomo and small Kodomo,

Bring out an Itsusen and give me candy,

Bring out a Nisen and give me candy,

The old man's Riyōte move skillfully,

A Kingiyo here, a rabbit there,

By the time the next one is done, the day has turned to dusk,

The old man of the Ameya rings his gong.

A large group of Kodomo gathered and followed behind the Ameya. The Ameya, while making strange Miburi, chanted:

Tonchin tonton, chinchin tonton,

Chinton, chinton, chinchinton.

He arrived in front of the Sekishiyo of Yachimata. There, he set down his load and began to blow his toy Ratsupa incessantly, gathering a large crowd of Kodomo. The Kodomo gathered from all directions, bringing out red coins earned from drinking sake and white Ginkuwa coated in Oshiroi, pushing and shoving as they vied for position, crying, 'Give me candy, give me candy!' The Ameya moved his fingertips skillfully, twisting and crafting rabbits, chickens, and Daruma in an instant, puffing them up with a straw to increase their volume, and handing them to the approaching Kodomo in exchange for money. Then, at the request of the Kodomo, he began to sing again.

Tonchin tonton, chinchin tonton,

Chinton, chinton, chinchinton,

From within the candy, oh Otayan, if you do not like Otayan, then Kintoki,

If you do not like Kintoki, then Daruma-sama,

Even a rabbit pounds mochi, even a monkey,

In the mochi-pounding of the fifteen-night moon,

The old man of the Ameya looks just like it,

Hey, hey, you Kodomo around here,

If you want candy, I will give you as much as you like,

But it is in exchange for money,

Even the judgment of hell depends on money,

If you have no money, you cannot suck the sweet nectar,

No matter what you do, you cannot have it,

More than a mother's milk, more than sugar,

This candy is even sweeter,

The God of San-Ikkyō said that under the heavens,

There is no such thing as a stranger,

But no matter how much it is under the heavens,

If you have no money, you are a stranger.

It is a world where money is the enemy,

The gong this old man strikes now,

Is the bell of the dawn of the Miroku Sankai,

Those without money, do not come near,

Tonchin tonton, chinchin tonton,

Chinton, chinton, chinchinton.

He was working with all his might, making a fortune off the Kodomo.

The white and red guards were busy checking the identities of passersby, and since he had gathered so many Kodomo and started making a racket with his gong and drums, they were greatly bewildered. The white guard approached him and said:

White Guard: 'Hey, old man, can you not think about the location? If you make such a racket in front of a Sekishiyo like this, you are getting in our way. Why don't you show some consideration and go over there?'

Ameya: 'Do not bother me. I am a peddler, one who walks the roads to conduct business. Even I pay taxes to the government. Do not bother me about where I conduct my business. I am doing business for Kodomo in a place where many Kodomo have gathered. If you interfere with my business, shall I sue you for damages?'

White Guard: 'If this were the Genkai, it might be your own business, but this is the Sekishiyo of Yachimata in the Meido, so the time will come when you too will be investigated. Just go over there for a while.'

Ameya: 'No matter what you say, I am not moving from here. Hmph, do not make a fool of me with your talk of the Yachimata of the Meido. I have been singing until my voice is hoarse since earlier, gathering Kodomo, and I am in the middle of a thriving business. If your convenience is good, then my convenience is bad; if my business is in your way, why do you take such high taxes?'

『何と云つても此処は動かないのだ。ヘン冥途の八衢なんぞと馬鹿にしなさんな。最前から声が枯れる程歌つて子供を集め、商売繁昌の真最中だ。お前の勝手がよければ此方の勝手が悪い、私の商売が邪魔になるのなら、なぜ高い税金を取るのだ』

He shouted thus, and still beating the gong with a 'ton-chin-ton-ton, chin-chin-ton-ton' rhythm, he sang a song to keep the children in good spirits.

The Red Shiyuei, taken aback by the excessively stubborn attitude of the Ameya, glared at him.

Red Shiyuei: 'Hey, Ameya, even though I have explained things so clearly, do you not listen?'

Ameya: 'Even if you glare at me with a face as red as a Hinomaru flag, this Amerikaya-sama will not flinch in the slightest. Without saying Yankee-Monkey, just stay silent and withdraw.'

Ameya: 'Ton-chin-ton-ton, chin-chin-ton-ton, from inside the candy, Otaya-san and Kinta-san have jumped out.'

He danced wildly once more. The Red turned to the White Shiyuei.

Red Shiyuei: 'This is troublesome, what shall we do?'

White Shiyuei: 'Shall we leave him alone for a while? Even if we catch and scold an old man who does not understand the reason, it will not amount to anything. Eventually, when his Akinai runs out, he will likely leave, and the Kodomo will have no use for the Ameya once they have spent all their money.'

Red Shiyuei: 'But, is it not impossible to bear with such noise? We must drive this fellow away by any means. Hey, hey, Ameya, I cannot allow you to stay here, so you had better go somewhere else to conduct your Akinai. It is noisy and interferes with our Jimu.'

Ameya: 'Hmph, you say such naive things. What is so noisy about the Taiko of an Ameya? In the world of Konnichi, they talk of Raudōsaugi, Fusenmondai, Kosakusaugi, and Guwaikaumondai, and with all that noisy clamor, can you not hear those voices? It is unheard of to say that an Ameya is noisy. If something of this level hurts your ears, how can you serve as a Yakunin? The ears of a Yakunin are blocked so that they cannot hear even a bit of the Gaukifu of the Nanoku of Jinmin. Without that, one cannot be called a Daijinbutsu in the world of today. Even a petty official would not be able to serve. Becoming a kenzoku of Kōshin-sama and observing the 'see no evil, hear no evil, speak no evil' is the wisest way. If you keep making a fuss, you will have to suffer being a gatekeeper forever; you are truly a hopeless fellow.'

Ameya: 'Ton-chin-ton-ton, chin-ton-ton, chin-chin-ton. In the middle of the Yachimata-kaidō, I met the White and Red Shiyuei. Seeing as they do not know the taste of candy, they glare with bitter faces. Truly a fated nature. Chin-chin-ton-ton, chin-ton-ton.'

He danced wildly, playing the music noisily again. The Red, in a great rage, immediately twisted the Ameya's arms behind his back, quickly bound him, and hid his figure inside the gate.

Bunsuke felt as if he were wandering in a fog, and as he recalled the mountain path and swamp he had just crossed, the Kayanogahara, and meeting his parents, he kept tilting his head, wondering if it were a dream, reality, or perhaps the Genkai or the Yūkai.
