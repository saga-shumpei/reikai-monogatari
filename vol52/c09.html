Chapter 9: 黄 Spring 帰 [1345]

黄泉帰〔1345〕

§ 1

Okiku, raised as a chivalrous woman, was not like her age and was well-accustomed to people; she entertained the two men well, and until around midnight, they recommended sake to each other and recited poems together. Iku and Saru had requested Hatsuwakahime to allow them to accompany her, but judging by her demeanor, it seemed unlikely to be granted. Though they were happy to have received the Yakuō jewel, it was particularly painful that their purpose was being obstructed because of it. 'Hatsuwakahime-sama is trying to block our path by giving us treasures; her way of doing things is quite unkind,' they grumbled from time to time, while drinking sip by sip from the cups Okiku poured. However, their nerves were agitated, and they became either sad or lonely, thinking that once the day dawned again, they would ask the Hime with every possible method, and if she would not listen at all, they would take independent action, and one after another, they must go to the capital of Haruna. They spent their time in whispers, wondering if the Kamisama were testing their resolve, building up and tearing down their hopes.

Okiku already knew well that Hatsuwakahime had departed from this sacred ground. However, thinking it too pitiful for the two, she did not reveal the truth.

Iku: 'Silently folding my hands, I cannot sleep, / Watching the whiteness of my breath in the air.'

Saru: 'My sorrow turns to meditation and to song, / And circling me, it transforms into tears.'

Okiku: 'The steadfast heart of a brave man wavers, / How pathetic it is to see you turn back.'

Iku: 'I know not what circumstances may arise, / Yet I shall force my way to the capital of Haruna.'

Saru: 'It is sad to be rejected by a young woman, / And add shame upon shame to a brave man.'

Okiku: 'Since the Imperial Gods reside in every land, / You two should remain here.'

Iku: 'Though I have thought it over many times, / My initial resolve cannot be cut away.'

Saru: 'Do not spare this life, which is like a string of jewels, for the sake of the Way; / Please allow me to proceed. / Born into the Land of the Gods, we / Have none to serve but the Gods.'

Iku: 'How shall I break through this difficult barrier? / Ah, it is all a matter of the heart.'

Okiku: 'As I ponder your deep sincerity, / I too am drenched in tears. / If only Magahiko were the one in charge, / Perhaps you would not have to suffer so.'

Iku: 'In any case, once more, / I will stake my life and ask Hatsuwakahime.'

Saru: 'Though I push and pull the thousand-pulling rock, it does not move; / What shall I do with this stubborn heart?'

Okiku: 'If a storm were to blow in the middle of the night, / How would you two scatter?'

Iku: 'Blown and beaten by storms time and again, / The white plum blossoms finally bear fruit.'

Saru: 'The Yamato spirit of Shikishima is like the white plum, / Fragrant in the morning sun. / I will rouse my Yamato spirit and advance, / Crossing the wilderness of a thousand or ten thousand leagues.'

Iku: 'Trampling over roots of rocks and trees, / The Yamato spirit must advance to the Land of the Moon.'

Thus, the three did not sleep until late at night, reciting poems in a lonely manner, while worrying about how Hatsuwakahime would refuse them. Suddenly, there were noisy human voices and the sound of wooden clogs; as they strained their ears to hear what it might be, Ochiyo rushed in.

Ochiyo: 'Okiku-sama, the condition of Bunsuke-sama has changed. Please, come at once.'

Okiku: 'Oh, that is terrible. If you two, please wait here. I will go to Bunsuke-sama's room for a moment.'

She was about to leave quickly. The two were surprised and said,

Saru and Iku: 'We shall accompany you.'

They followed behind Okiku and rushed into Bunsuke's sickroom. When they looked, Matsuhime was in the midst of earnestly reciting a Norito for the return of the soul. Many officials and believers were flustered and noisy inside and outside the room, in a state of not knowing what to do. Iku-kō said,

Iku: 'Excuse me.'

Saying this, he approached Bunsuke's side, turned to Matsuhime, and said,

Iku: 'Thank you for your hard work.'

He greeted her lightly, took the Yakuō jewel from his pocket, pressed it against Bunsuke's forehead, and with a sincere heart, concentrated his prayers for about ten minutes. At this time, Bunsuke had already grown cold. Only the heartbeat in his chest remained faint.

Iku: 'Ah, this Koitsu might be hopeless; it is truly a troubled matter. Matsuhime-sama, I shall entrust this jewel to you. Please, do keep it pressed against his Zengakubu without letting it slip away. I am going to perform misogi in the Kajikagaha now.'

Iku, accompanied by Saru and Okiku, headed toward the Kahabe. Then, they cast off their clothing at the Nemoto of the Shinseimatsu, plunged in with a splash, kept their faces above the water, and the Sannin joined their voices in prayer for the Sosei of Bunsuke.

Okiku: 'Please save the life of the official who has served by offering his Magokoro to Kami. I am filled with sorrow, for I seek to stop one who follows the path of Kamunagara and the will of Kami.'

Iku: 'Wash away all matters into the rapids of the Hayakaha, and awaken his pure Mitama.'

Saru: 'It is the sincerity of a human to wish to preserve the life of one who is passing away. Grant him the strength to breathe once more and become a Mabito who devotes himself to the path.'

Okiku: 'Surely Kami will have mercy and save this Okina, who has held but one sincerity since his early days. By the power of Kami, save this Okina who devotes himself to the path and to the world. O Ametsuchi no Kami, look with mercy upon the heart that speaks only of the impossible before the Mimahe of Kami.'

With their hearts in a rush, they sang songs that came to their lips, floating and sinking in the torrent, risking danger as they began to pray. The Ōkami would surely forgive the Magokoro of these three. Even Iku and Saru, who are usually mischievous pranksters and ill-tongued fellows who do not appear very kind, and Okiku, the tomboy, reveal their Magokoro in the face of another's peril, forgetting their own danger to pray to Kami. This is truly the manifestation of beautiful human emotion, an act that can only be performed by those who constantly follow Kami, believe in Kami, and have realized the path of sincerity.

The three returned while worrying about the state of Bunsuke. Suddenly, Okiku became possessed by Kami, rushed into the sickroom, took the luminous jewel from the hands of Matsuhime, pressed it alternately against his left and right ears, and while chanting something in a low voice, prayed while shedding sweat. Both Iku and Saru, still naked, massaged Bunsuke's feet, blew breath into him, and exhausted every possible means. Bunsuke cried out 'Un!', opened his eyes wide, and sat up. Looking around restlessly and realizing that a large crowd had gathered, he said:

Bunsuke: 'Everyone, has something unusual happened? A great many of you have gathered here.'

Okiku: 'Have you regained consciousness? That is truly a happy thing. I was truly worried, Bunsuke-sama.'

Bunsuke: 'I had gone to play in a certain beautiful mountain. Somehow, my eyes suddenly began to see, and the lush scenery all around, the colors and scents of the blooming flowers—the joy of seeing with my own eyes for the first time in a long while and encountering the light of the world is not something that can be expressed in words. Ah, my eyes have become unable to see again.'

He said this with a lack of strength.

Matsuhime: 'Bunsuke-sama, you have been unconscious since the other day, and everyone was extremely worried. After the two, Hajime and Toku, struck you, they vanished, and you have had no consciousness since that time. Every single day you were only speaking delirious words. Well, it is fortunate that you have returned to your senses. I, too, feel as if I have been resurrected.'

Bunsuke: 'Indeed, now that you say so, I remember that vaguely. Just a moment ago, I met those two at the crossroads of a small village, and they were very kind to me.'

Okiku: 'Bunsuke-sama, you have been lying here; no such men have come. You must have had a dream. Please, pull yourself together. You were once dead, you know.'

Bunsuke: 'No, no, I have no memory of having died at all. I do not know who she was, but a beautiful young lady took my hand and led me to various places. And thanks to her healing my eyes, I was able to encounter the scenery of the manifest world that I had not seen for a long time, and I continued on a truly enjoyable journey. Then, about two or three ken in front of my face, a tremendous glowing object appeared. It was so dazzling I could not bear it, and as I kept my eyes closed for a while, this time I heard the sound of Norito. When I listened carefully and thought about it, it was the voices of Matsuhime-sama, Okiku-sama, and the others. When I thought "Ah!", I could no longer see again.'

§ 3

He says this with a look of regret.

Bunsuke had been knocked unconscious when he was struck on the skull during a struggle with the two young men, Hatsu and Toku, and had fallen into a state of suspended animation. Had the two, Iku and Saru, not been in possession of the luminous jewel at that time, he might perhaps never have been revived. The place where Bunsuke wandered after entering the Iumeikai was the vast wilderness of the Daisantengoku. Furthermore, the two he encountered at the crossroads of a certain village, Hatsu and Toku, were in fact their Seirei. Hatsu and Toku had originally held Bunsuke in high regard. However, driven by a momentary desire and misled by Takahime and Eugenbau, they had attempted to take the jewel of Eugenbau that Bunsuke had picked up; when Bunsuke refused, this disturbance broke out against their will. Nevertheless, the two Seirei, contrary to the intentions of their physical bodies, were extremely angry at having abused Bunsuke, and having temporarily departed from their bodies, they had come to this place to call Bunsuke back to the Genkai once more. It was through the power of the earnest prayers of Iku, Saru, Okiku, Matsuhime, and others that he was revived to fulfill the remaining duties of the present world. Although Bunsuke’s physical eyes were already blind and he was a most pitiable person, upon reaching the Reikai, he instantly shed his external state, crossed the second intermediate state, and advanced with great speed to the third internal state. Because of this, only the sincere heart that had loved and served the Lord God remained, and because his heart’s eyes were opened, he was able to behold the heavenly realm.

All those who are hard of hearing, those who lack the free use of their limbs, or those who suffer from various incurable diseases while in the Genkai are no longer the disabled persons they were in their physical bodies when they pass through the barrier of the separation of spirit and flesh and enter the Reikai. All their faculties function with increasing accuracy and clarity. However, even those who possess a perfect and complete physical body, if they had defects in their hearts, will become deaf, blind, demented, or disabled after the separation of spirit and flesh, and their appearance will instantly change to resemble that of a monster. Since the human countenance is an index of the heart, the true nature of one’s heart is immediately exposed in the Reikai. Even in the Genkai, those whose evil is most intense, no matter how magnificent their appearance may be, can be seen to have a monstrous aspect somewhere if one observes them closely. There are those who are perfect beauties in form but appear eerie or repulsive, and those who feel like ghosts in some way; this is proof that their Seirei are most inclined toward evil.

Bunsuke had suddenly arrived at the barrier of the heavenly crossroads. Yet, he himself did not think at all about the fact that he had once died, or by what procedure he had come to such an unfamiliar place. He had also completely forgotten about his wife and children left behind in the Genkai, as well as his acquaintances and friends. Only his knowledge concerning the Lord God became increasingly clear. He passed through the gate of the crossroads barrier without a second thought. When he looked back, he saw two guards, one with a white face and one with a red face, standing on either side of the gate.

Bunsuke: 'How strange a place this is. I wonder what the name of this land is. I would like to ask those guards.'

Turning on his heels and approaching them again, Bunsuke asked:

Bunsuke: 'What is this place called?'

The two guards replied firmly:

Guards: 'You will understand in due time. There is no need for you to ask. Furthermore, we are not permitted to say.'

This was because the guards knew that he still had the karma to return to the Genkai. If they had let him know that this was the crossroads of the Reikai, Bunsuke might have been startled, thought of his wife and children in the Genkai, and been unable to glimpse the beautiful barrier of the heavenly realm; they also knew that his soul would wander in the Chūūkai and would not easily be able to return to his physical body. Bunsuke was filled with a pleasant feeling for no apparent reason, and with no thought of Kobotoyama in his mind, he felt as though there were wonderful and beautiful places ahead of him, and he walked on with light steps. His heart was encouraged by the sudden opening of his eyes, and like a butterfly yearning for flowers, he fluttered from one place to the next. On the way, he frequently encountered friends and acquaintances who were in the Genkai, as well as people he knew who had already passed away. However, his heart at that time had no thought of distinguishing between those who had passed away and those who had not, and he thought only that they were all living and working with their physical bodies just as he was.

と惜しさうにいふ。

文助は初、徳の二人の若者と格闘した際、頭蓋骨を打たれて昏倒し、一旦仮死状態になつてゐたのである。此時若しもイク、サールの両人が夜光の玉を持つて居らなかつたなれば、或は蘇生しなかつたかも知れぬ。文助が幽冥界に入つて彷徨うたのは、第三天国の広大なる原野であつた。そして或村の十字街頭で初、徳の両人に出会つたのは、何れも其精霊であつた。初、徳の両人は元より文助を尊敬してゐた。併しながら一時の欲に駆られて、高姫や妖幻坊に誤られ、文助の拾うておいた妖幻坊の玉を受取つて帰らうとしたのを文助が拒んだので、止むを得ず、こんな騒動が突発したのである。併しながら二人の精霊は肉体の意思と反対で、文助を虐待したことを非常に怒り、暫く両人の体を脱出して、文助を現界に今一度呼戻さむと此処までやつて来たのである。そこへ熱心なるイク、サール、お菊、松姫等の祈祷の力に依つて、再び現世の残務を果すべく蘇生せしめられたのである。文助は肉体の眼は既に盲し、非常な不愍な者であつたが、霊界に到るや、忽ち外部的状態を脱出し、第二の中間状態を越えて、第三の内分的状態にまで急速度を以て進んだ。其為、神に親しみ神に仕へたる赤心のみ残存し、心の眼開け居りし為に、天界を見ることを得たのである。

すべて現界に在つて耳の遠き者、或は手足の自由の利かぬ者、其他種々の難病に苦んでゐた者も、霊肉脱離の関門を経て霊界に入る時は、肉体の時の如き不具者ではない。すべての官能は益々正確に明瞭に活動するものである。併しながら仮令円満具足せる肉体人と雖も、其心に欠陥ありし者は、霊肉脱離の後に聾者となり盲目となり、或は痴呆者となり不具者となり、其容貌は忽ち変化して妖怪の如くなるものである。総て人間の面貌は心の索引ともいふべきものなるが故に、其心性の如何は直に霊界に於ては暴露さるるものである。現界に於ても悪の最も濃厚なる者は、何程立派な容貌と雖も、之を熟視する時は、どこかに其妖怪的面相を認め得るものである。形体は申分なき美人にして、凄く或は厭らしく見える者もあり、又どことなくお化の様な気持のする人間は、其精霊の悪に向ふ事最も甚だしきを証するものである。

文助は先づ天の八衢の関所に突然着いてゐた。されど本人は自分の嘗て死去した事や、如何なる手続きによつて、こんな見ず知らずの所へ来たかなどと云ふ事は一向考へなかつた。そして現界に残してある妻子のことや、知己朋友の事などもスツカリ忘れてゐた。只神に関する知識のみ益々明瞭になつてゐた。彼は八衢の関所の門を何の気もなく潜つて行つた。後振り返つて見れば、白面赤面の守衛が二人、門の左右に立つてゐる。

『ハテ不思議な所だ、地名は何といふだらうか、あの守衛に尋ねて見たいものだ』

と再び踵を返して側に寄り、文助は、

『此処は何と云ふ所ですか』

と尋ねてみた。二人の守衛は、

『何れ後になつたら分るでせう。お尋ねには及びませぬ。又吾々も申し上げる事は出来ない』

とキツパリ答へた。これはまだ現界へ帰るべき因縁がある事を守衛が知つてゐたからである。もし此処は霊界の八衢であるといふ事を知らしたならば、或は文助が吃驚して、現界に於ける妻子のことを思ひ浮かべ、美はしき天国の関門を覗く事も出来ず、又其魂が中有界に彷徨うて、容易に肉体に還り得ない事を知つたからである。文助は何とはなしに愉快な気分に充たされ、小北山の事も念頭になく、只自分の行先に結構な処、美はしき所があるやうな思ひで、足も軽々と進むのであつた。そして俄に目の開いたのに心勇み、フラフラフラと花に憧憬れた蝶の如く、次へ次へと進んだのである。途中に現界に在る友人や知己並に自分等の知己にして、既に帰幽せし人間にも屡出会うた。されど其時の彼の心は帰幽せし者と帰幽せざる者とを判別する考へもなく、何れも自分と同様に肉身を以て生きて働いてゐることとのみ思うてゐたのである。

§ 4

In this way, even when a Ningen is in a state of Kashijiyōtai, or even after having entered the state of complete death, they never know that they have undergone Reinikudatsuri and have come to the Reikai. The reason for this is that there is no Heni in their Ishi, Saunen, or any other Jiyōdō, and furthermore, they are free from the various Hanzatsu Kihan that exist in the Genkai, placing themselves in a Jiyōtai like that of a Shōni. Considering this, as long as a Ningen does not turn against Kami in the Gense, ignore Shinri, or cause Taigai to the Shiyakuwai, the Yokubau and Kannen in their Nikutaijiyō—that is, the Akunen of Jiai—are wiped away after death, and because only the good belonging to their inner nature can freely become active, they are able to enjoy a peaceful life after death.

A certain saint once said that heaven is hard to ascend and hell is easy to fall into. However, as long as a Ningen possesses a physical body, they must inevitably remain in an intermediate boundary between their outer life and their inner life. Therefore, while one has a physical body, it is impossible to perform such perfect good as the celestial beings who reside in heaven. One must inevitably be content with an intermediate life where good and evil are mixed, and beauty and ugliness intersect. When a person dies, Kami immediately examines the evil and good of their life, removes the elements of evil, and seeks to save them to heaven as much as possible. For this reason, I would like to say that heaven is easy to ascend and hell is hard to fall into. However, this is the view of an ordinary Ningen, and since those who are registered in the hellish world filled with falsehood and sin as it is today are already inhabitants of hell, it is impossible to make their life after death peaceful unless they escape this hell while alive and are saved at least into the intermediate world. Yet, because Kami is of supreme benevolence and love, He desires to guide anyone, no matter who they are, to heaven by exhausting every method and means, to make them work for the Reikai as inhabitants of heaven, and to let them lead a joyful life.

As I have stated before, because Kami governs the universe by regarding it as a single personality, even the worst evildoer is a part of that one body. Just as a person, when a part of their body becomes filthy, painful, or develops a boil, wishes to heal it by exhausting various methods, it is natural that Kami is anxious to heal the boils and painful parts that occur in a part of His body—that is, those falling into the hellish world. From this, it should be clear how Kami loves Ningen and everything in the universe as if they were His own body. Kannagara Tamachi Haemase.

原文（近代文語）

斯の如く、人間は仮死状態の時も、又全く死の状態に入つた後も、決して自分は霊肉脱離して、霊界に来てゐるといふ事を知らないものである。何故ならば、意思想念其他の総ての情動に何等の変移なく、且現界に於けるが如き種々煩雑なる羈絆なく、恰も小児の如き情態に身を置くが故である。之を思へば人間は現世に於て神に背き、真理を無視し、社会に大害を与へざる限り、死後は肉体上に於ける欲望や感念即ち自愛の悪念は払拭され、其内分に属する善のみ自由に活躍することを得るが故に、死後の安逸なる生涯を楽しむ事が出来るのである。

天国は上り難く地獄は落ち易しと或聖人が云つた。併しながら人間は肉体のある限り、どうしても外的生涯と内的生涯との中間的境域に居らねばならぬ。故に肉体のある中には、どうしても天国に在る天人の如き円満なる善を行ふ事は出来ない。どうしても善悪混淆、美醜相交はる底の中有的生涯に甘んぜねばならぬ。人の死後に於けるや、神は直に生前の悪と善とを調べ、悪の分子を取り去つて、可成く天国へ救はむとなし給ふものである。故に吾々は天国は上り易く、地獄は落ち難しと言ひたくなるのである。併しながら之は普通の人間としての見解であつて、今日の如く虚偽と罪悪に充ちたる地獄界に籍をおける人間は、既に已に地獄の住民であるから、生前に於て此地獄を脱却し、せめて中有界なりと救はれておかねば、死後の生涯を安楽ならしむることは不可能である。されど神は至仁至愛にましますが故に、如何なる者と雖も、あらゆる方法手段を尽して、之を天国に導き、天国の住民として霊界の為に働かしめ且楽しき生涯を送らしめむと念じ給ふのである。

前にも述べたる如く、神は宇宙を一個の人格者と看做して之を統制し給ふが故に、如何なる悪人と雖も、一個人の身体の一部である。何程汚穢しい所でも、そこに痛みを生じ或は腫物などが出来た時は、其一個人たる人間は種々の方法を尽して之を癒さむ事を願ふやうに、神は地獄界に落ち行く……即ち吾肉体の一部分に発生する腫物や痛み所を治さむと焦慮し給ふは当然である。之を以ても神が如何に人間を始め宇宙一切を吾身の如くにして愛し給ふかが判明するであらう。惟神霊幸倍坐世。
