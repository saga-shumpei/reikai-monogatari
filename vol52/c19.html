Chapter 19: 屁口垂 [1355]

屁口垂〔1355〕

§ 1

Gariya, Case, Hatsu, and Toku, the four who had been blown away from the top of the Monomiyagura in the forest of Ukiki by the fart of the Eukuwai that called itself Onara, and who had tumbled down the Kaidan and severely suffered Fushiyō to their heads and shins, had become desperate and were striving to forget the Kutsū of their Karada by reciting Senriu to one another and turning their pain into laughter.

Gariya: 'It seems we have received a rebuke from the Kamisama, and have met with a Sanzan experience. It is truly a case of Heikōtonshiyu. We must hold a Senriukuwai or something to laugh, or else we cannot endure this. For when all Ningen laugh without Taai, it is the time when all Kutsū departs.'

Case: 'Since we were bewitched by a tanuki and blown away by a fart, why don't we set the theme as farts and compose some Daku?'

Gariya, Hatsu, and Toku, the Sannin, clapped their hands in Sansei.

Gariya: 'Deceived by a tanuki and made to smell a foul fart, where can a man stand?'

Case: 'Hekota by a foul fart, the Yoninzu are again Heikō at the Monomiyagura.'

Hatsu: 'Onara, a fellow who smells even just by hearing the name.'

Toku: 'Having the hair of my buttocks pulled out by a foul Magatsu, the awkwardness of my posture as I break wind.'

Case: 'No Moyō within the belly, spinning round and round. Appearing calm, I kill the fart with my heel.'

Hatsu: 'A Miyadaiku who descends from the Yane to break wind. What are you Kisamara laughing at, says the Inkiyo fart.'

Toku: 'Even a woman cries at the logic of a fart. A bride who is reluctant to leave the privy after breaking wind. What smells more than the fart one has broken is the Onara itself.'

Gariya: 'A fart in the bath is an accidental balloon. A kotatsu where one chants prayers and also breaks wind.'

Case: 'A fan used to waft the hem after breaking wind. A fart-breaking insect far more skilled than I.'

Hatsu: 'A long day, burning no incense and breaking no farts. A spring field where a horse goes along while breaking wind. The forest of Ukiki is noisy with farts that never cease.'

Toku: 'A master searching for the Chōtarō who broke wind. A loud laugh, with a fart mixed into the maid's sleep-talking. A jester breaking three farts that turn into money.'

Gariya: 'The mother-in-law broke wind, so my mind is eased. A single person who breaks wind and finds nothing funny. Rejoicing in the mother-in-law's fart is also important for the household.'

Case: 'The etiquette of a woman, killing neither insect nor fart. Four or five people involved in killing a fart.'

Hatsu: 'Blaming one's own fart on the child with an oh-oh-oh. Urging someone to leave the privy where they are breaking wind outside.'

Toku: 'Was the torpedo invented by a fart in the bath? Air entering a paper-covered fart balloon. Joking that it is already twelve o'clock after breaking wind. I too hold my nose at such fart-like logic.'

Gariya: 'Even the cat flies out of the kotatsu in amazement. The pride of having extinguished a candle flame with a fart. In a good mood, singing in the lavatory while breaking wind. A bride's fart defended by an automobile. The seeds of farts are now exhausted with this.'

'Ahahaha.'

The group forgot their Kutsū and laughed with delight. From then on, they engaged in Senriu without a theme, each speaking of whatever came to mind and losing themselves in laughter.

Gariya: 'The railway proceedings advance quickly. A child who is a handful is a burden on the parents' shins. The fortune withers and the bones show even to the walls. Hair tied in a ginkgo-leaf style that is bound to fall.'

Case: 'A secret relationship known only to the bamboo grove. The Jizō at the roadside shrine leers with a sidelong glance. A teetotaler lowers his head at the derailed troupe. The intimacy of passing farts to one another, saying it was you. A thin mosquito grumbles in the corner of the cupboard. A sudden shower and a large washbasin in the parlor. One with no talent beats a tea bowl at the lowest seat.'

Hatsu: 'If I tell of our first meeting, my wife scolds me with her eyes. A carefree person putting a rhythm to his yawning. The next morning, feigning that it was the influence of sake.'

Toku: 'A Takashimada hairstyle, and again putting a mosquito into the mosquito net. Calling someone lame because one leg is longer. The maid says the novel is the work of a rebel.'

Gariya: 'Daytime fireworks, a laundry pole giving chase. A derailed train lying down on the spring embankment.'

Hatsu: 'A cat sticks out a serious face from the cupboard. A foul Onara sticks out its face from the cupboard.'

Toku: 'The son, claiming he fell in love with the art, makes a poor excuse.'

Gariya: 'The eye doctor, making a face, takes the money.'

Kēsu: 'Fishing rods and carefree faces in conversation. Wearing Western clothes, the worker comes in wearing geta. In this world of geisha-talk, fools are ready to fight.'

Gariya: 'A dowry weighed against family status and body weight. A distant fire, pretended to be seen in the newspaper.'

Kēsu: 'Incense and moxibustion chase away stubbornness. Grasping goishi, one rushes to the dead face.'

Hatsu: 'A house full of children arguing again at the dining table. During a call, the receiver is hung up in frustration. From yawn to yawn, the length of the night. Giving an awkward greeting to a face that looks familiar.'

Toku: 'A woman just right, sulking at a tea stalk. The husband went out to the bath and never returned. Reaching a hand out from the kotatsu to hold a New Year's card. Sweating after being chased in a first dream of the year.'

Gariya: 'Fingers fly one after another where fleas jump. They will not give to a beggar because of democracy. A coward, scattered by a fart, falls to ruin.'

Kēsu: 'Making them laugh once, the orator vanishes into the dark. The yudōfu also falls to pieces and one gets dead drunk. There is a wireless telephone device in the tea stalk.'

Hatsu: 'The liveliness of washing each other's backs in gratitude for the bath. A New Year's card from a man who refuses to give up.'

Toku: 'The greengrocer sticks his sweaty neck out from the kitchen. Fleas appear and the conversation flies elsewhere. Catching a cold from the democracy of the final bath of the day.'

Hatsu: 'A coward who makes others stomp their feet in frustration. The orator is praised for making people cry at a tragedy.'

Toku: 'A blind man's chopsticks slip off the yudōfu plate. Both look as if they are tired of the debt collector. Stomping his feet, the wife economizes on toilet paper. The apron acts as a substitute for the toilet paper. The debt collector subjects the pipe to a cruel fate.'

Thus they twisted out these random verses and amused themselves with laughter. Just then, with a heavy thud-thud sound of footsteps, Takamiyahiko, the lord of the Kuruwa Castle, arrived. Seeing the four of them, Takamiyahiko kept his mouth twitching and glared at them in silence. The four felt slightly surprised by the figure of the enormous man and were inwardly trembling.
