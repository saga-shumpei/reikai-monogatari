Chapter 26: 姑根性 [1362]

姑根性〔1362〕

§ 1

The next to be called forward was Otoshi.

Aka: 'You are Bunsuke's daughter Otoshi, are you not?'

Otoshi: 'Yes, that is so.'

Aka: 'When did you come to the Reikai?'

Otoshi: 'Yes, I left the Genkai at the age of three, and I have been growing up in the world of Yachimata until this day.'

Aka: 'Is that your younger brother there?'

Otoshi: 'Yes, that is so. Both of us have been living a lonely life in Kayanogahara.'

Aka: 'Because you two siblings have undergone long training for the sins of your parents, in place of the place you should have gone to in Tengoku, I shall send you directly to Tengoku from here on. There is no longer any need to go to the Shinpantei. You may wait for a while.'

Having said this, he gave a signal to Shiro. Shiro immediately ran into the gate. After a while, indescribably beautiful Tennan and Tenniyo appeared from nowhere, carrying biha, kokiu, and tatebue. They gave beautiful irui to the two, wrapped them both in a mysterious reikuō, and while playing bimeu music, they rode upon the clouds heading east, and departed as they turned into light. The two guards watched their figures go, joined their hands in prayer, and wore expressions of joy upon their faces.

Aka: 'If only such pure spirits would come along, I would feel much better. When a half-dead hag like Takahime comes along, even a barrier guard like me cannot help but have a hard time. Furthermore, there is that Otsuyu and the fool, who are such extreme dotes that they have become slaves to love, and are so infatuated with romance that they can barely distinguish between right and wrong, so I am sure Lord Ibukido-nushi-no-kami must be very troubled indeed.'

Shiro: 'It is truly a troublesome matter. Well, I suppose I must go back to investigating bit by bit now.'

While saying this, Shiro took the hand of the old woman who was closest and made her stand before Aka.

Aka: 'Are you not Oteru from Hiiragi Village? Why have you come here?'

Oteru: 'Yes, please listen to me. I have only one son in heaven or on earth. That son is named Kōsuke, and he was truly filial to me. Abandoned by my husband when I was young, I remained a widow for a long time. I raised him with a parent's heart, thinking if he crawled, let him stand, and if he stood, let him walk. I never had a moment of rest, sleeping or waking. If he coughed even once, I worried he might have consumption, and if his breathing was heavy, I worried it might be heart disease. I worried so terribly, and finally, I raised him to adulthood and thought I would enjoy my old age by letting him have a gentle wife. However, there was a girl named Okiyo who was my niece, so I had him marry her. For two or three days, they both treated me with care, but after that, Kōsuke's heart changed completely. He would say Okiyo this and Okiyo that, and he would not even ask if his mother was there. Then, when night came, they would make this old woman sleep separately, and the two of them would embrace and sleep soundly, would they not? If I had known my precious son would be taken by Okiyo, I would never have let him take a wife, but it is too late to regret it now. So, I told my son Kōsuke to quickly drive out the wife who did not please his parent. But Kōsuke said to me, "Until now, I have listened to everything you have said, but Okiyo is my wife and not your wife, so you do not need to concern yourself with her. There is a saying that in old age, one should follow one's children. If you just stay quiet and rest, we two will work and support you, Mother." He would not even say he would drive out that hateful, hateful bride. How can I show my face when Kōsuke, whom I raised in my own bosom, is being controlled by Okiyo? Please, try to imagine my plight, an-an-an.'

Aka: 'Well, that is a troublesome matter.'

Oteru: 'It is truly a troublesome matter, is it not? However, my son was not such an unfilial person, but since the bride is a wicked woman, she whispers all sorts of evil wisdom into his ears, and so he is unfilial to this parent who is the only one he has. In my frustration, I hanged myself from the persimmon tree in the back. But it seems my death was ill-fated, and I have wandered to this place just the same. I cannot die even if I wish to, I am truly a wretched old woman, on-on-on.'

§ 2

Speaker: 'What exactly did your son and his wife do to be called unfilial?'

Speaker: 'Yes, they do nothing but things that displease a parent. Ever since Okiyo came, they have not slept with me even once. That makes me so angry I cannot bear it. Is it not unfilial to do things that displease a parent?'

Speaker: 'Is it not natural for a Fūfu to sleep together? Why does that count as being unfilial? You are letting your Shiutomekonjiyō get the better of you and are suffering from Hofukairinki, are you not?'

Speaker: 'Metsusau, why would I do such a thing? I am concerned for the welfare of Kausuke, and I spend my nights awake thinking of the life of Kausuke and his wife. Yet that wretch Okiyo makes my precious, precious son go through such hardships, abusing him and making him cry, so I am so angry I cannot bear it. How can a parent watch such things happen? Please, Gosuiriyō. There is no one as Fushiaha as I am. I lost my husband early, I am treated with Somatsu by my only child, and I am treated heartlessly by my daughter-in-law. How can I go on living? An-an-an.'

The Red Shiyuei kept his mouth pursed in the shape of the character 'he', opened a long ledger, looked at it, and smiled with a grin.

Speaker: 'Now, now, Otero, you have been quite cruel in your Iji of your daughter-in-law, haven't you?'

Speaker: 'Yes, I did Iji her. Her attitude was what it was, you see. It is what they call the needle-pricking of a Shiutomebaba; when I got too angry, I would prick the daughter-in-law's backside with a Momenbari. However, this is something that has remained in proverbs since olden times as the needle-pricking of a mother-in-law. If I do not make her suffer a little pain and discipline her, it will not be for the good of the house.'

Speaker: 'You are quite an Akutau of an old woman. It seems you are angry that your son is living in Shinmitsu with his Niyōbau.'

Speaker: 'Would I not be a little angry? You should try being in the Mibun of a mother-in-law yourself. You appear to be a Yakunin, so it would be nice if you showed a little Hiiki to a Raujin and scolded the daughter-in-law for me.'

Speaker: 'The daughter-in-law has done nothing wrong; it is you and your son who are at fault. I shall now proceed to pass judgment.'

Speaker: 'Metsusau, I have no memory of my son ever doing anything wrong, not even as much as a speck of dust. And as for me, Otero, I have kept my chastity since I was young, and I have never done anything like taking a man behind my husband's back. Please investigate thoroughly.'

Speaker: 'You claimed that Okiyo slept in, so you made her sit on the earthen floor of the garden, took all the ceramics you had from the cupboard, and for every word of complaint you uttered, you smashed one against the ground, and then another, and in the end, you even threw a teapot, a sake bottle, and a charcoal brazier, smashing them to pieces, did you not? Why did you commit such violence when Okiyo was bowing her head on the earthen floor and apologizing?'

Speaker: 'Yes, no matter what, they are the treasures of my own house, so I did not want to break them. At first, I threw the chipped tea bowls and the cracked small dishes. If she were a clever daughter-in-law, she would have grabbed my hand and cried, "Mother, please stop," to hold me back, but that Okiyo is a foolish woman who does not care for the house, so she did not stop me at all and just kept apologizing. So, I ended up breaking those ceramics that I regretted losing so much, due to the flow of events. It was truly a regrettable thing. It was never this old woman who broke them; it was the fact that that wretch Okiyo provoked me that became the driving force, and that is how such things came to pass. She is truly a woman of poor understanding. She did not admonish me, and in the end, she even brought a magnificent Nishikide bowl and said, "Mother, while you are at it, please break this too." So, I thought, "Eh, shall I break it?" but it was too precious, so I kept only the high-quality items. And when I thought about hanging myself, I thought that even if I died leaving such ceramics and money behind, it would be a pity for them all to go to such a hateful daughter-in-law, so I thought I would burn all the paper money and break all the ceramics. But I thought that my dear Kausuke would be troubled, so I left them unbroken. I also had about five hundred ryō in savings, but I left a note on this money: "This money is for Kausuke to use; Okiyo is not to touch it. If Okiyo uses this, I will haunt her." I wrote that, so no matter how much of an Akutau a daughter-in-law may be, she surely cannot use this, On-on-on.'

Guard: 'What a deeply sinful old woman you are. A foul-mouthed hag like you will surely be sent to Jigokuyu. Now, hurry up and pass through this gate.'

Old Woman: 'Even if I told a Wakarazuya like you, you would not understand the Seishin of an old person. Now, I must go where I must go, complain of my daughter-in-law's Akuji, and take revenge; I cannot just leave it at that. Namuamidabutsu, Namuamidabutsu, oh, how my back aches. I do not know what kind of Yakushiyo this is, but what could such a young Yakunin as you know? If one is born even one Ichinichi earlier, one is a Shishiyō of the world. Let me see, I shall meet someone who understands and have them hear my story. Excuse me, young Akashiro person, I have caused you a great Jiyama. Everyone, excuse me, Sayō-nara.'

Leaning on her staff of goosefoot, she bent her waist like an Ebi, her hair, which was only enough for a Jiyōmoku, tied back on her bald head, and she advanced into the Monnai with an 'echi-echi' sound.

The next person brought out was a rough man with Irezumi on his arms.

Guard: 'What is your name?'

Benzau: 'Hi, I am called Benzau the fireman, and I have made a bit of a name for myself in the world. No matter what kind of trouble arises, once this Benzau-sama strips to his Matsupadaka, tightens his Nejihachimaki, and says "wait, wait," that is the Saigo, the Hitokoe of the crane, and everything settles down at once as if water had been poured over it. What kind of place is this, anyway? Hmph, I cannot for the life of me understand why you people are checking my record.'

Guard: 'This is the checkpoint of Yachimata. Since you are a fellow who has done quite a lot of violent things in the present world, get on this scale. If the verdict for Jigokuyu comes down, I shall send you to Jigokuyu, and if the verdict for heaven comes down, I shall send you to heaven.'

Benzau: 'Ya, thank you. I am a man who would not flinch even at the bottom of the hellish cauldron, and since I am a chivalrous man and a boss of firemen by trade, Jigokuyu probably suits my nature. Please, do not do anything troublesome like using a scale, just send me to Jigokuyu immediately. Heaven does not suit my nature. In Jigokuyu, there must be fights, and there must be fires too. At such times, Benzau the fireman will strip to his Matsupadaka, jump in to mediate, and it will be convenient for drinking sweet sake. I am a true-blue brother who has been called a fighting fireman, a slow fireman, and a hoodlum fireman.'

He sat cross-legged, displaying his chivalrous spirit to the extreme.

Guard: 'In any case, it is the rule of the spirit world, so get on this scale, come, quickly.'

He urged him on.

Benzau: 'Very well. There is an example of Banzuiin Chōbei sitting on a willow cutting board and being cooked alive by the Shiro-saya group. We are those who idealize that Banzuiin. I do not care about anything, I will get on. Even if there is some good in me, do not ever send me to heaven.'

Saying this boastful remark, he stepped onto the scale. The scale remained horizontal, pointing neither to Jigokuyu nor to heaven, remaining perfectly still.

Guard: 'Haha, this one is a relatively good person. Although he spits out foul words with his mouth, half is good and half is evil; well, in today's world, this is a high-class sort. Hey Benzau, I am sorry, but I cannot send you to the Jigokuyu you desire. And yet, I cannot send you to heaven either; you are a Yachimata laborer. First, you had better train in the Chūyūkai for a while. Never wish for things like Jigokuyu. You do not need a judgment. Go ahead and head toward the northwest. Your corresponding partner is likely waiting for you there.'

Benzau made a disappointed face, like an owl that had missed its night meal, and muttered,

Benzau: 'Geez, Chūyūkai is not very exciting. Why won't you send me to Jigokuyu?'

He muttered this while walking slowly, swinging both arms, heading toward the wilderness.

After that, many spirits were asked their names one by one, their records were checked, and they were judged according to the love of the master of each place, some to heaven, some to the Chūyūkai, or some to Jigokuyu.

『ハイ俺ア、鳶の弁造と云つて世の中に些は男を売つたものでござんす。如何なる揉め事が起つても、此弁造さまが真裸となり、捻鉢巻をグツと締め「まつたまつた」とやつたが最後、鶴の一声、何でも彼でも水をうつた如く、一度に納まると云ふ男達でござんす。一体此処は何と云ふ所で厶んすか。ヘン、お前さま等にメモアルを調べらるると云ふのは根つから葉つから腑に落ちませぬワイ』

『此処は八衢の関所だ。随分お前も現世に於て乱暴な事をやつて来た奴だから、この衡にかかれ。さうして地獄行きの方が下れば地獄行き、天国行きの方が下れば天国にやつてやらう』

『ヤア、有難テエ、地獄の釜のどん底でもビクとも致さぬ某、根が侠客渡世兼鳶の親分だから、地獄行きが俺の性に合つて居るでせう。どうか衡なんか面倒くせえ事をせずに、すぐ地獄にやつて下せえな、天国なんか性に合はない、地獄には定めし喧嘩もあるであらう、又火事もあるであらう。其時は鳶の弁造が真裸となつて飛び込み仲裁をし、甘い酒でも飲むに便利がいい。喧嘩鳶の、グヅ鳶の、グレン鳶と云はれて来た、チヤキ　チヤキの兄イだ』

と胡坐をかき、侠客気分を極端に発揮して居る。

『兎も角霊界の規則だから、この衡に乗つて呉れ、サア早く』

とせき立てる。

『よし、幡随院長兵衛は柳の爼の上に坐つて、白鞘組から生きながら料理をされた例もある。俺達は其幡随院を理想とするものだ。何でも構はぬ乗つてやらう。些と位好い事があつても、決して天国へやつてはいけないぞ』

と業託を云ひながら衡にかかつた。衡は両方、水平になつて、地獄の方も指さず、天国の方も指さず、じつとして居る。

『ハハこいつは比較的善人だ。口で悪垂れを吐くが、善が半分、悪が半分、マアマアこれなら今日の娑婆では上等の部だ。オイ弁造、気の毒ながら其方の望む地獄にやる事は出来ぬ。さりとて天国にもやられず八衢人足だ。まづ暫し中有界で修業を致したがよからう。決して地獄行きなどを望むぢやないぞ。其方は審判の必要がない。これから西北の方をさして勝手に行け。又其方相当の相棒が待つて居るであらう』

弁造は梟鳥が夜食に外れたやうな詰らぬ顔をして、

『エエ中有界なんて気がきかない、なぜ俺を地獄にやらないのかなア』

と呟きながらノソリノソリと両腕を振り荒野をさして進み行く。

それから沢山の精霊は一々ネームを訊ねられ、メモアルを繰られ、或は天国へ、或は中有界へ、又は地獄へと各其所主の愛に依つて審かれて行く。
