Chapter 8: 巡拝 [1344]

巡拝〔1344〕

§ 1

Iku and Sāru, the two of them, finished their worship at the Shinden of the Ōhiromahe, and stood before the mansion where it is said Imoriwake was secluded.

Sāru: 'The Kosenjiyō of Imoriwake-sama, the old woman Otora, becomes Oka as I look upon it.'

Iku: 'This is the Kosenjiyō where the Dobin danced, the Tokuri whirled, and the sake cups were shattered.'

Sāru: 'This mansion is also the place where Magahiko and the old woman Otora performed their Kaishin.'

Iku: 'The drunken Kumakō-sama jumped in and Senriyō (occupied) the gold with threats. Climbing the Danbashigo built one stage higher, I go to visit the Hime.'

The two paid their respects at a small shrine where Konohanahime was enshrined and finished their Hairei.

Iku: 'The blessings of the Konohanahime Kami are before my eyes, beginning to bloom on the tips of the Kigi.'

Sāru: 'Iku exposes his Hanataka before the Mimahe of the Konohanahime Mikoto. The Sumekami who descended from the mountain of Tenkyō must be laughing at my Bakazura. Bewitched by the tricks of a tanuki, the fellow Iku has come to the Ōmahe without knowing shame.'

Having sung thus, they next proceeded before the shrine of Kanayamahiko-no-Kami and finished their Hairei.

Iku: 'Since I heard that this is a fearful Kami who ties bonds, I could not remain still and came to worship.'

Sāru: 'No matter how much you worship the Kami with that face, there should be someone who would become your wife.'

Iku: 'Rather than looking at my face and laughing, go and take a look at a water mirror for a moment.'

Sāru: 'Can a wife be made with just facial features? One must be a person with a beautiful soul. Even though I look like this, I am a trickster who is fawned upon by women here and there.'

Iku: 'Do not be so full of yourself, even though you are a person who has been made a fool of by maidens. A fellow who chirps such things before the Mimahe of the noble Kanayamahiko-no-Kami might be a widower bird. Come, let us go; it is not a face to be called a woman, which is embarrassing to the Ōkami.'

Iku: 'Ahahahaha.'

Laughing thus, they approached the shrine where Tamayorihime (the maiden of Ryūgū) was enshrined.

Iku: 'A greedy Kami who gathered various treasures and hid them at the bottom of the sea.'

Sāru: 'Do not say foolish things; the Otohime-sama is a Kami who has already detached herself from material desires.'

Iku: 'That was a mistake; I said something rude. Please listen again, O Otohime-no-Kami.'

Sāru: 'Although the Kami bestows treasures upon people, you are excluded.'

Iku: 'Excluded or unexpected, I do not know, but one cannot stand in the world without treasure.'

Sāru: 'What is the use of seeking material treasure? Pack your soul with treasures that do not decay.'

Iku: 'Do not say foolish things; a crystal ball is also material, yet it illuminated the dark night. If one has no money, the people of the world do not even treat one as a human being, asking what kind of person one is. Therefore, I am a person of good fortune who does not thoughtlessly discard gold, silver, and treasures.'

Sāru: 'As proof that the fellow Iku lacks Ikuchi, you are longing for treasures. No matter how much treasure the Kami has, would the Kami give it to a poverty-stricken face? Come, let us go; rather than feasting your eyes, forget the treasure and go to pick up treasure. The treasure I speak of is not gold, silver, or crystal, but the treasure of the teachings.'

Having finished singing thus, they next proceeded before the Chūdan no Miya. Here, the shrine of the Hi no Ōkami is built.

Iku: 'This shrine, where the Izanagi no Sumekami is enshrined, is especially beautiful.'

Sāru: 'That is to be expected, for it is the Kami who performed Misogi at Tachibana in Hyūga of Tsukushi.'

Iku: 'Wash the soul, which is drowned in the sins and impurities of Kokotaku, before the Mimahe of the Kami.'

Sāru: 'Apologize, Iku-kō, and ask to be forgiven for the foolishness of being deceived by the Magatsukami.'

Iku: 'Whether I am struck on the head or not, it is not a matter for you to know.'

Sāru: 'How painful it is to see a fellow traveler being struck by a tanuki and crying out. The shrine of the Hi no Kami, who descended to the mountain of Tenkyō, is especially high.'

Leaving this place again, they next proceeded before the shrine where the Tsuki no Ōkami was enshrined.

Iku: 'Before the shrine where the spirit of the Susanoo-no-Kami is enshrined, there is the Tsuki no Ōkami.'

Sāru: 'You have been made a fool of by the old tanuki and owl, and have even been laughed at by a maiden. The Tsuki no Ōkami, who is indifferent, will likely laugh at your face.'

Iku: 'The Kototama of the Kami is truly profound.'

Iku: 'Do not speak like a Baka. This is the Kamisama of Zengenbishi, and surely He shall look upon us with favor.'

Saru: 'Since this man is a Baka rare in this Sekai, I pray, O Moon Ōkami, that Thou mayest protect him.'

Iku: 'It is because Saru is the very proof of a Baka that he has been made to carry the Suishiyō ball by Iku.'

Saru: 'It is only because I knew that Iku had no guts that I let you hold the ball.'

Iku: 'Come, let us go. Illuminated by the light of the moon, it is a night that makes one feel bashful for no reason at all.'

This time, they paid their Sanpai before the shrine of Kunitokotachinomikoto on the Saijiyōdan.

Iku: 'Before the Onmahe of the Kami, whose name is fearful to utter, I have come as a Tsumibito. Yet, having repented, I am a Mitama of Iku who serves the path of the Ōkami.'

Saru: 'I am the Kami of the future Saru, who has received the Mimegumi of the Sumeōkami.'

Iku: 'With a face like that of a Bachiata Saru, how well you are called Kon. Even if the Yobito mock you as a mere imitation of Saru, you have no Shikata, do you?'

Saru: 'You are a Bakamono who does not know that this Kamuzukasa says there is a Saru-mono on the path of Ananahi.'

Iku: 'Who is the Bakamono whom people call the wolf of Kunidokoro-tachi-no-hiko?'

Thus the futari finished their Hairei, and then, while teasing each other, they came upon a stylish building, neither too wide nor too narrow, standing beneath a cluster of seven or eight well-branched pine trees. That was the so-called mansion of Matsuhime.

Iku: 'I think with nostalgia of the Matsuhime mansion, built in the shade of the evergreen pine trees.'

Saru: 'Since the Hatsuwakahime whom I adore is there, it is a mansion I yearn for all the more.'

Iku: 'This mansion, which has become the pivot of Kohokuzan, has been built like a folding fan.'

Saru: 'The green of the evergreen pine trees is lush and verdant, growing on without end.'

Iku: 'Hearing that the Kamuzukasa of Hatsuwakahime is there, my heart thunders and I can hardly advance.'

Saru: 'The wind of cowardice has blown violently again, exposing the fact that you, Iku, have no guts.'

Iku: 'If you say such things, shall I go first and show you my courage?'

Saru: 'How amusing. You will go before Hatsuwakahime, be scolded, and end up crying.'

Iku: 'Since I am the one holding the Suishiyō ball, Hatsuwakahime will surely praise me. At that time, you, Saru, had better watch with a look of resentment, sucking your finger.'

Saru: 'When we meet the Hime-sama, I shall reveal everything and report the whole story. You had better not turn your face red then; you are a fellow who would be mocked even by an owl.'

Iku: 'You may speak ill of me as much as you like, you wretch who failed even at hanging yourself.'

Saru: 'You are also one of the hanging-failed fellows, are you not? How could you ever show your face to the Hime?'

The futari, unable to bear the shame, came within four or five ken of the Matsuhime mansion, pushing each other and saying, 'You go first,' 'No, you go first.' Seeing their shadows, Smart ran up joyfully, leaping onto their chests, clinging to their backs, and licking their cheeks, full of spirit.

Iku: 'Oh, Su-chan! First of all, I am glad you are safe. I have finally followed your trail to this place. Please, I beg you to put in a good word for us to the Hime-sama.'

Saru: 'Hahahaha, what a Baka. Like a candidate for the House of Representatives these days, you are even fawning over a dog. Do you think a dog can speak?'

'When a master has influence, one feels as if one must bow even to a dog. That is what human nature dictates. They say that when one visits the home where one has married off a daughter, the parents will even bow to a stray dog. You are a fellow who does not understand the reason of things. With such an attitude, how do you think you can survive in today's world of universal falsehood? You are an out-of-date antique.'

'Leave me alone. No matter how grandly you speak, there is no authority in the words of a coward who is trembling for fear of being scolded by the Hime-sama. Well, just watch what I do. Ahem!'

Saying this, he made up his mind to approach the entrance, and peered inside with a look of trepidation. Hatsuwakahime and Matsuhime were in the midst of a conversation, smiling and earnest about something. Saru slid the door open with a rattle,

Saru: 'Heh, excuse me. Matsuhime-sama, I am meeting you for the first time. I am Saru from the Forest of the Shrine, and my other companion is called Iku. Oh, he is a spineless fellow, so please, I beg you to treat him kindly.'

Matsuhime: 'That is, that is, you have come all this way. Now, please, come on up.'

Saru: 'It is a great joy to see that Smart-sama is also in good health.'

Thinking perhaps to curry favor with Hatsuwakahime, he was fawning over the dog incessantly. Iku entered like a thief breaking into an empty house, trembling all over, and stepped inside without making a sound.

Hatsuwakahime looked at the two of them and said in a quiet voice,

Hatsuwakahime: 'Are you Iku-sama and Saru-sama? Have you come to pay respects to the Kami?'

The two of them replied,

Saru and Iku: 'Heh, well, that is...'

They scratched their heads, fidgeted, and squatted down on the earthen floor.

Hatsuwakahime: 'Did you have some business with me? I beg you, please tell me quickly.'

Iku made up his mind and said,

Iku: 'Well, the truth is, we thought we would like to accompany you, Hime-sama, wherever you may go, so we followed in your footsteps. Please understand the sincerity of us both; we truly wish to accompany you.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'You, was there anything unusual in the Forest of Yamaguchi?'

Iku: 'Yes, well, it was quite an interesting affair. We received excellent divine power and sent those two demons running. It was, truly, quite a delightful experience.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'That was a job well done. And you, you received something from the Kami, did you not?'

Iku: 'Yes, we received it.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Have you guarded it safely until you reached this place? Did nothing happen that would allow it to fall into the hands of others along the way?'

Iku: 'Heh, just as you see, I have it here. It is truly a magnificent crystal ball.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'That is called a luminous gem; it is not a crystal. It is a magnificent diamond that appeared from the island of Tsukushi.'

Iku: 'Heh, is that so? That was truly a grateful thing.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'You, you would not do anything as foolish as being bewitched by a specter along the way and having it snatched away, would you?'

Saru: 'Oh, I am truly humbled. The truth is, we were deceived by an old tanuki and it was taken, but thanks to Ochiyo-sama, it was returned to its original state.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Once that gem has entered the hands of a crooked spirit, it becomes very soiled. If you do not perform a purification now, it will become useless, you see.'

Iku: 'Shall we get some salt water and purify it?'

Hatsuwakahime: 'If you purify your own formless soul, the gem will naturally be purified. And you, you must have a sense of attachment to that gem, do you not? Why did you not hand it over to Saru-sama? Once it entered your hands and was taken by a demon, you effectively abandoned your right of supervision over the gem. From now on, it is better to let Saru-sama hold it. In truth, I asked the Hinodewake-kami on your behalf, and because He was moved by your earnestness, He granted one to the two of you; therefore, this gem is proof that your two spirits have become one. It is by no means something that one person should monopolize. That is to say, Iku-sama's heart is Saru-sama's heart, and Saru-sama's heart is Iku-sama's heart; it is arranged so that you become one body and act for the sake of the Divine World.'

Saru: 'Hey, Iku-shu, how about that? As expected, the monopolization of treasure is not permitted, is it? Because you acted as if it were your own thing, and did not even let me see it properly, but shoved it into your pocket, divine punishment struck, and it was taken by that tanuki fellow, you see.'

§ 4

Saru: 'Moshi Hime-sama, if that is the case, is this jewel something that should be handed over to Saru from now on?'

Hatsuwakahime: 'It is not a matter of whose it is. It would be best if you two-sama protect it by turns. And this treasure is a Goshinpau for the Sekaikiusai, not something that a Ningen should claim as their own. Consider it as something borrowed for a while, and preserve it with Taisetsu. And now that you have obtained the jewel, there is no Hitsueu for you to follow me. Please return to the forest of the shrine as quickly as you can, even if it is only for Ichiji. I am Arigata for your Goshinsetsu, but because Kamisama protect me Takusan, I shall never feel lonely, you see.'

Saru: 'If that is so, I shall return this jewel to you. Dōzo, I will perform any Goyō, so please do not Otsushiya such things, and permit me, even if it is just Saru Hitori, to accompany you to the capital of Haruna. Moshi, I am like this.'

He revealed his Netsusei on his face, and while shedding tears, he begged her.

Hatsuwakahime: 'You who have obtained from Kami the treasure that shines in the night, return to the forest of the shrine. This jewel is a rare Mitakara that illuminates the Yamiyo, bestowed by the Hinodewake. Should you carry such a treasure to the land of the moon, where the Magakami rage and go mad? It is the will of Kami that you, indeed, should protect this Mitakara. It is the stern command of Kami that you may not bring an attendant to Haruna.'

Iku: 'I cannot disobey those words of Hime-sama, yet how can I return just like this?'

Saru: 'No matter what command you may give me, please permit me, reconsidering your decision.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Though the flower of the heart of a Masurao may bloom, it is sad that there is no way to pluck it.'

Iku: 'Even so, can I return just like this? Even if I die, I shall serve Hime.'

Saru: 'If you will not permit me no matter what, I shall cut my stomach here.'

Matsuhime: 'Pitiful are the people who wander, not listening to the stern words of Himegimi. My tears fall as I measure the heart of the Masurao, from whom sincerity overflows. Yet, can I, a propagandist, disobey the will of the Sumeru-Ōkami?'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Iku, Saru, you two officials, calm your hearts and look back upon yourselves.'

Iku: 'I shall settle my thoughts for a while and then answer. In any case, I beseech you.'

Saru: 'It is not that I disobey the words of Himegimi, but I cannot suppress my intense heart. Nevertheless, I shall rest over there for a while and ask my own heart how to conduct myself.'

Thus, they answered Hime with poems, and withdrew to the former residence of the leader where Imoriwake and Otora had lived. The two of them, while sipping tea, crossed their arms, let out sighs, were at a loss for what to do, and spent their time in tears. From this point, Hatsuwakahime bade farewell to Matsuhime, watched for a gap in the two men's attention, and accompanied by Sumāto, quickly departed from the sacred ground and set out on her journey. Iku and Saru, both of them, remained convinced that Hatsuwakahime was still at the residence of Matsuhime, and spent the night being served sake by Okiku. Then, hearing of the critical condition of Bunsuke, they rushed out of the residence in the middle of the night, went down to the Kajika River to perform water ablutions, and prayed with all their might for his recovery.
