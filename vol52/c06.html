§ 2

Singing this, they hurried along the road.

Under an alder tree on one side of the road, a beautiful woman was standing with her head bowed, leading a black dog, looking pale and lost in deep thought. Iku and Saru, the two of them, caught sight of the woman standing beyond the rice field about ten ken away, and stopped in their tracks.

Iku: 'Hey, Saru, doesn't that woman look a lot like Hatsuwakahime-sama? And she even has a dog with her that looks just like Smart. Shall we go and ask her?'

Saru: 'Hmm, at a quick glance she does look like her, but isn't her face a bit long and isn't she too tall? And that dog, compared to Smart, seems to lack substance somehow. Perhaps that Magakami slave from last night is plotting a second strategy to torment us. It would be disadvantageous to get involved carelessly, so why don't we just pretend not to see her and keep going?'

Iku: 'That may be so, but she has such a worried look on her face. Since this is the riverside, I wonder if she intends to throw herself in.'

Saru: 'Well, I cannot tell from that appearance. Let us think about the situation here for a while.'

Iku: 'Hmm, that will do.'

With that, the two watched the woman's behavior for a while. Suddenly, the woman threw a thin sash over the alder tree and hung herself with a jerk. The black dog nearby let out a cry, turned toward the two, and scratched at the air with its front paws as if begging for help.

Iku: 'Hey, Saru, it seems we have successors, but they are doing it so painfully. What an unrefined sight; look at that, she is letting her snot run, and that posture is truly a sight to behold. When Hatsuwakahime-sama saw us hanging with snot running down, she must have thought what foolish, pathetic men we were. There is a saying that one should correct one's own appearance by looking at others.'

Saru: 'Hey, Iku, this is no time to be saying such carefree things. This is no time to be criticizing when seeing someone in danger. If we dawdle, she will die, so come on, let us two save her.'

Iku: 'Hatsuwakahime-sama, as a woman, saved two rough men who were hanging themselves; surely you alone are enough to save one woman hanging herself. I am here guarding the crystal ball... since touching dirty things will soil the ball, you go and save her.'

Saru: 'I feel so unwell that I cannot help it, Iku, won't you at least come with me to her side? If you do, I will save her by myself.'

Iku: 'Geez, what a spineless man you are. Come on, let us go.'

Urging Saru on, he hurried his steps to the foot of the alder tree. The woman had turned deathly pale, her limbs no longer moving, and she hung like a dangling persimmon. Saru quickly held the woman and said,

Saru: 'Ah, what a heavy woman, not at all what she looks like. This creature might be a demon from the underworld.'

He said this while helping her down. The woman seemed to have finally regained consciousness, looked around here and there, and said,

Woman: 'Ah, I do not know who you people are, but why do you interfere with such cruelty when I am in the middle of attempting a journey to heaven? You missionaries who save people should just stop your meddling.'

Thinking she would offer thanks, the woman instead flew into a rage with a sullen face.

Saru: 'Hey, I do not know who you are, but where is there anyone who complains after having their life saved? Tell me, Iku, if it were going to be like this, we should not have saved her. Phew, she is making a fool of us. Ha, this one is a lunatic.'

Woman: 'Do not concern yourself even if I am a lunatic. You are neither my friends nor my relatives, so there is no reason for you to save me, is there?'

Sāru: 'Even so, this dog, it was seeking salvation by turning toward me with itsu-shiyōkenmei, so I adjusted my busy dauchiu to come and save it.'

'Hmph, how ahau. Do dogs speak? You are quite baka despite how you look. You interfere with a person's mokuteki, and then you ask for thanks? It is beyond the pale. I called you baka for no other reason than this: in this world today, there are only fellows who think that by doing something life-risking to help others, they will be praised by the ningen of the sekai, or who expect to be thanked with even a single hitokuchi of gratitude. My eyes are called senriyōme; they possess the iriyoku to tilt a castle with one ichibetsu and blow away sanrin. If you understand, get down on your knees like a dog and offer an apology.'

'My, my, logic is something that can be twisted any way one likes. Aren't you spouting things just like Takahime or Kurohime, eh Iku?'

Iku, saying, 'Humph,' in utter amazement, stared at the woman's face, and while gazing intently, thought, 'While having such a gentle face, where must one press to make such abusive language come out?'

The woman struck Iku across the face with a single word: 'Baka.' Iku staggered and fell into the rice field. As soon as the dog grabbed the crystal ball from Iku's bosom, it dashed off with itsu-shiyōkenmei. Seeing this, Sāru shouted, 'Hey you beast, wait!'

The woman clapped her hands and laughed, 'Ho ho ho ho, you are baka. I am the monster that tried to scare you in the forest of Yamaguchi last night. It would be a baugai to us if we let you keep the crystal ball, so I took it from you using a trick. Bye-bye, ihihihihi.'

Showing her white teeth and twitching her chin, she revealed her true form as a great tanuki and fled toward the south, turning the clouds into mist. Sāru helped Iku up, and while grumbling complaints, he walked on sluggishly with a vacant look, intending to pay his respects at the sacred grounds of Kohokuzan regardless. An owl perched on the branch of a dense tree nearby was crying, 'Hoo, hoo, Hōsuke, Hōsuke, you had it snatched away in a flash, hoo, hoo, Hōsuke, appō, appō.'

『ホー、ホー、ホー助、ホー助、ころりと取られたなア、ホー、ホー、ホー助、アツポー　アツポー』

と啼いて居る。
