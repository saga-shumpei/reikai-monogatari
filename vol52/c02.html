Chapter 2: 哀別の Song [1338]

哀別の歌〔1338〕

§ 1

Ningen, regardless of whether it be the Takaamahara of Ten or the Takaamahara of the earth, upon ascending to its Reiiki, as one enters further within (that is to say, as one ascends higher), Shiyōkaku and Chie increase all the more, casting Kuōmiyō upon their Reikon, dwelling in Shinri, and becoming able to Ohiohi-Kantoku even those matters once thought to be Nankai Mondai. That all advance into such a Jiyōtai is due to the power of love that comes from Ōkami. This is because this love is a vessel capable of containing all things of Takaamahara. Ningen who receive the Goshinkaku of Ōkami into their Naibun to a great degree are called Tentekitennin. They are also referred to by the Betsushiyō of Naitekitennin or Kaushiyotennin. In Takaamahara, too, there is a distinction between Naiteki and Guwaiteki; the Naiteki Tenkai is called Kaushiyotenkai, and the Guwaiteki Tenkai is called Teishiyotenkai. Furthermore, the love where the Tennin in Tengoku reside is called Ten-ai, and the love where the Tennin in Reikoku reside is called Rei-ai. Moreover, in Tengoku, Ōkami appears as the sun, and in Reikoku, Ōkami appears as the moon. One such as Hatsuwakahime belongs, if one were to say, to the category of Tengoku Tennin, and being of the Iwa-no-Mitama, one may even say that she is the entirety of the sun's heat, that is to say, love. Again, Kotoyoriwake-no-Mikoto is a Tennin residing in Reikoku, who dwells in the truth of faith, and makes the light of the moon his entirety. Therefore, Hatsuwakahime is able to worship the Kami, offer Norito, and become a model for the Senden-shi and believers; and because Kotoyoriwake-no-Mikoto dwells primarily in the intellectual aspect, he is a spiritual Tennin who preaches and instructs in the Shinri of the universe, clarifies the truth of the three worlds of Gen-Yū-Shin, and acts as the motive power for all. One such as Konohana-hime-no-Mikoto belongs to the department of spiritual Tennin, and Hinodewake is a Kamibito who belongs to the category of Tentekitennin. Nevertheless, for any Kami, whether one is a spiritual Tennin and a Tentekitennin, or a Tentekitennin and a spiritual Tennin, can be realized according to the state of their daily activities.

Tengoku is entirely the priestly territory of Ōkami and the residence of Ōkami. Reikoku is the royal territory of Ōkami, and this is also called the throne or the precious seat of Mizu. Furthermore, the traffic between Tengoku and Reikoku is conducted in all cases by the hands of mediating Tennin organizations. This, too, is a traffic mechanism established according to the will of Ōkami. Hatsuwakahime also, by the hands of these mediating Tennin, would at times communicate with Tengoku, at times with Reikoku, or at times communicate with Tengoku and Reikoku simultaneously. Although it is considered that a Tennin of the earth such as Hatsuwakahime could communicate directly without relying on the hands of mediating organizations, while she has once descended to the earth and is in the circumstances of a physical human, there is an unavoidable necessity to go through a mediator. This is because she is a Tennin who intervenes within both the Naiteki and Guwaiteki aspects.

Hatsuwakahime departed from the forest of the shrine and sought to set out on her journey of expedition. At this time, Uzuhiko and his wife, as well as Kaede and the executive staff, and the righteous believers, lamented and grieved greatly at parting with this Kamibito whom they had revered like a parent and like a Kami, and they did not cease to implore from their sincere hearts that she might stay her feet for at least one more day. However, Hatsuwakahime had no room to accept the entreaties, no matter how tearfully they were made by people filled with such lovely faith. The reason was that she was in a position where she had to fulfill the great divine duty entrusted to her by Ōkami. And she had to complete the divine work as soon as possible, remove the harm to the world, and report back before Ōkami. Hatsuwakahime, too, was bound by the sentiment of the suffering of parting, and in her heart, she was very reluctant to leave this place. This was because there remained the anxiety that if, by any chance, she were to leave this place, Uzuhiko and the other staff and believers, whose virtue was not yet perfect and whose Chie and Shiyōkaku were thin, might once again be deceived by the intense Magatsukami and lose the faith they had gained with such effort. However, since it was a location not too far from the mansion of the Saien, when the time of Masakatsu came, the Senden-shi, who stood in rows like the glittering stars, would surely manage their affairs and come to support them. Thinking that if that were the case, even if she left this place, no regrettable failure would occur, she left a thread of hope, encouraged her heart, and resolved to stand outside of human sentiment and act for the sake of the Kami. Hatsuwakahime spoke words of farewell toward the shrine. The words were:

天国はすべて大神の祭司的国土にして大神の御住所である。霊国は大神の王土にして之を王座又は瑞の宝座とも云ふ。而して天国と霊国との交通の機関は、何れも媒介的天人団体の手によつて行はれてゐる。これも皆大神の思召に依つておかせ給うた所の交通機関である。初稚姫は又此媒介的天人の手によつて、或時は天国と交通し、或時は霊国と交通し、又は天国霊国一度に交通し給ふ事があつた。初稚姫の如き地上の天人は、媒介的団体の手に依らなくても、直に交通し得べきものと考へらるるなれども、一旦地上に降りて肉体人の境遇に居らるる間は、何うしても媒介者を通ずる必要があるのである。如何とならば、内的外的の両方面の中に介在し給ふ天人なるが故である。

初稚姫は祠の森を立出でて、愈遠征の途に就かむとした。此時珍彦夫婦を始め楓及び幹部役員を始め、正しき信徒は、親の如く神の如く慕うてゐた此神人に別るる事を非常に歎き悲しみ、せめてモウ一日なりとも足を留められむことをと赤心より懇願して止まなかつた。されど初稚姫は、如何に愛らしき信仰に充てる人々が熱涙を流しての哀願も、之を受入るる余地がなかつた。何故ならば、自分は大神より任されたる大神務を果さねばならぬ身の上である。そして一日も早く神業を完成し天下の害を除き大神の前に復命せなくてはならぬからであつた。初稚姫も哀別離苦の情に絆されて、其内心は、此処を立去る事を非常に惜しんだのである。万一吾此処を去らば、未だ徳全からず智慧証覚の薄き珍彦を始め其他の役員信者は、又もや強烈なる曲神の為に誑惑され、折角の信仰を失墜せざらむやとの案じが残つてゐたからである。併し乍ら斎苑の館に余り遠からざる地点なれば、正勝の時には綺羅星の如く立並ぶ宣伝使が、何とか事務を繰合して応援に来て呉れるであらう。さうすれば、自分はここを去つても、さまで憂ふべき失態は来さないであらうと一縷の望みを残して、いよいよ心を励まし、人情の外に立つて、神の為に活動せむことを決意した。初稚姫は神殿に向つて訣別の辞を述べられた。其詞、

Hatsuwakahime: 'To transfer the Reigoku of Takaamahara to the Chijiyō, and to establish the Seijiyō in the forest of the shrine as the precious Miyai; to set the Miyabashira firmly into the deep Ihane for Tokoshihe, beginning with the Sumeōkami of Ananahi who dwells in tranquility, and including the hundred Kami and Mitama who reside on the Saiu; I humbly present the Magokoro of Hatsuwakahime before the precious Mimahe, and serve. The Goshingefu of causing the plum blossoms of the Sanzensekai to bloom at once; the Kami who wander in the darkness of the Seireikai, the Jigokukai, and other strange Jiyashinkai, I shall cause to bow before the Shizen, Shiai, and Kautoku of the Ōkami, without leaving a single corner behind.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'I shall polish and purify the spirits of the foolish, bestowing upon them the light of Shinshin, turning them toward the light of the Kami of Tenkai, and for the sake of saving all existence in the universe into the paradise of Takaamahara, I set forth on my Kashimadachi. As I am one who advances, dividing the wild fields where the Majin rage in my youth, the Magatsu of ugliness will surely wait, watching for an opening to destroy and torment me without cease. Ah, Ōkami, Ōkami! No matter what kind of Magatsu may attack, or how the ugly demonic winds may rage, I pray you protect my Mitama with your holy and noble light and the heat of your love.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'I pray before the Mimahe, beseeching you to let me complete the Goshingefu which you have entrusted to me, peacefully and serenely, and to return to report to the Ōmae as soon as possible, Kannagara. Whether the sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if celestial anomalies and demonic apparitions assail me, this body which I have entrusted to the Kami transcends life and death. Making the virtue of love my sutra and the true virtue of faith my warp, I have sworn to protect my heavenly mission without wavering or yielding, no matter where I go; therefore, even if this body of mine should perish, the Mitama-body I have received from the Kami shall not decay, shall not perish, but shall remain active for thousands and tens of thousands of generations, for Tokoshihe.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Even after tens of thousands of years, I shall appear and disappear in both the manifest and hidden worlds; if I do not achieve this purpose, how could I ever stop, O Yamato-damashii? The courage to advance while opening the heavenly kingdom within my own body; I shall restore the Jigokukai to the heavenly kingdom instantly, according to the will of the Kami, and fulfill the heavenly portion of my Mitama completely and in every detail. Protect me, Kannagara; I pray before the Ōmae of the Sumeōkami, trembling and trembling in awe.'

Having finished this prayer, and having earnestly entreated that Uzuhiko and the servants of the Kami below him might not be deluded by demons, but might follow the great path of Kannagara and fulfill their missions, she withdrew from the presence of the Great Deity, entered the mansion of Uzuhiko, attended the farewell banquet, and it was decided that she would finally depart from this place.

Uzuhiko composes a poem as he faces the parting.

Uzuhiko: 'The sadness of this day, parting from you who descended from the distant heavenly skies. I pray, if only for a day, that you might spend it in this forest, in this living palace of rock.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'How could I disobey the words of the Kami, Uzuhiko? Ah, but yet, but yet. The words of the Kami, Kannagara, cannot be disobeyed; the pain of bidding this regrettable farewell. Even if my body advances far to the capital of Haruna, my soul shall remain with you.'

Uzuhiko: 'I am grateful; I shall take those words of yours as my strength and make them my pillar to serve the Kami. You are noble, for you advance, parting the ugly weeds that have grown wild in both field and mountain. I shall pray morning and evening before the Ōmae that all things may be blessed for you.'

Shizuko: 'My noble lord, you have come to this mansion and bestowed upon me holy treasures. You have given me a hundred invisible treasures; I shall polish them and offer them to the Kami.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Invisible treasures do not decay, nor are they eaten by insects; they shall become yours for Tokoshihe. If the virtue of faith is fulfilled, can even the Magatsu-mi or any enemy attack you?'

Shizuko: 'When a Magatsu-mi like Yokogenbō or Takahime comes, what shall I do? This alone weighs upon my heart, and I have been saved from a state where I could not sleep day or night. Yet, I wonder again if there is no precedent for the Magatsu-mi to come and attack.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Uzuhiko, Shizuko-dono, Kaedehime, be at peace, for this is the sacred ground of Uzu. If the Takaamahara opens within your bodies, could even great serpents or demons come to attack? Know that the enemy of my body to be feared is the sin of disobeying the Kami from within one's own heart.'

Uzuhiko: 'I am grateful; having received your holy teachings, I feel as though I have been revived.'

Kahedehime: 'If Hatsuwakahime-no-Mikoto returns, upon whom shall I rely to pass the days? Worshipping this noble one as the parent of my life, I have served her, and now I am filled with sorrow. Though we part, I shall wait, relying on the teachings of Kami that we shall meet again.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Kahedehime, the lingering attachment is endless, yet my tears shall be dried by the light of Kami as I go.'

Kahedehime: 'The rain of tears I shed now will soon clear, and the garden of the Kamikuni will be joyful once more.'

Iru: 'How I prayed to stop this departure, yet it was in vain, and now we are to be parted.'

Iku: 'I have prayed morning and night that I might serve as your Mitomo, no matter what.'

Saru: 'I shall follow in your Miato wherever you go, according to the will of Kami.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Though the heart of a warrior is a joy to behold, there is no way to go against the command of Kami.'

Saru: 'Even so, how can we part just like this? The pain in my chest is unbearable.'

Haru: 'You, who depart for the land of the moon, crossing the vast Arano, are truly Ō. My wish to serve as your Mitomo has also become a dream.'

Teru: 'How I thought to stop your Kadode, but all my efforts were in vain, and I part from you in tears.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Well then, Uzuhiko and the other officials, dwell in peace under the protection of Kami.'

Uzuhiko: 'If you depart, the forest of the shrine will be like the leaves scattered by the cold winter wind.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'Even if the leaves scatter, the Yōki of spring will be born, and the flowers of blessing will bloom in the Seijiyō. What would it avail me to remain here forever if it did not accord with the heart of Kami? For the Lord God has commanded me to go to Haruna with all haste.'

Thus, they exchanged songs of Ketsubetsu with one another, and while shedding tears of Aibetsu, Hatsuwakahime, accompanied by her Aiken Smart, and singing Sendenka, descended the Kiku Yamaji, clad in her Sendenshifuku and leaning on her staff. Smart, as if lamenting the parting from the people of the shrine forest, left behind two or three mournful cries, and following behind or leading before Hatsuwakahime, he shook his head and wagged his tail, following her with joy once again.
