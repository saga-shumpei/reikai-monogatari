Chapter 4: 俄狂言 [1340]

俄狂言〔1340〕

§ 1

Iku and Saru, the two of them, had completed their preparations for the Sanbasau and were waiting impatiently for the arrival of Hatsuwakahime. After about a hantoki had passed, the figure of Hatsuwakahime, accompanied by Sumato and singing a Sendenka in a clear voice, began to appear and disappear through the gaps in the trees as she descended. The two of them, while cautioning each other, saying, 'Now the shiyōnenba begins, we must not fail,' were practicing the shitageiko of the Sanbasau with various miburi and ashiburi.

Hatsuwakahime sings in a gentle voice.

Looking back upon the seijiyō of Ubusunayama, following the will of the Ōkami, I, the official Hatsuwakahime, proceed. Stopping by the forest of the shrine, I see the Magami who have possessed the Takahimetsukasa, and the Magatsukami of Eugenbau, who, feigning to be the will of my father, have carried out every kind of magatsu, trampled upon the precious seijiyō, and plotted to build a sumika for the Magatsu; how yuyu it is. Protected by the grace of the Kami, I entered the forest of the shrine, and with all my heart and body, through the kamuwaza of the Kami's guidance, I sought to kotomu the mitama of the magatsu and save them in the heavenly pure land, but that thought has become like bubbles on water.

Now it has come to naught. Praying for the fortune of the future before the Imperial Ōkami, while parting from the precious sleeves of the likes of Tatsuhiko and his wife, Kaedehime, the hundred officials, and the believers, I have made the Sumato given by the Kami the strength for my long journey, and the grace of the Kami my staff, and have finally arrived here. The flow of the valley is murmuring, playing the music of nature, and the young buds of the trees sway in the spring breeze, performing a dance of nature. Even in this valley where human footprints are rare, the voices of the birds are so lively.

The scent of the hundred flowers, green, red, blue, and yellow, and others, comforts my eyes; truly, it is a serene spring sky.

In the cry of the bush warbler, 'ho-hokekyo,' there is somehow the voice of the Kami's salvation. I am, from the beginning, on a journey alone. I am not permitted a long journey accompanied by an official of the teachings. Moreover, for me, who proceeds on the path of the Kami, there is no way to take a companion along. No matter what magatsu may come, as I am protected by the majesty of the absolute and infinite Ōkami, there is nothing in this world to fear. Ah, Kannagara-kamunagara, how grateful I am for the grace of the Kami. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if the ugly magatsu rage, it is a journey I have decided to take alone; how could I permit a companion?

Nevertheless, at the root of the oak tree in Yamaguchi, Iku and Saru, painting their faces with white and black, are waiting, pulling at their tegubane, wishing to follow me. Though I admire that sincerity, how could I, Hatsuwakahime, permit a companion whom the Kami does not permit?

Ah, Iku and Saru, you two, fathom my heart, abandon such desires, and return to the seijiyō as soon as possible to serve the Kami. The Kami is with you. Even if you follow us and come to the ends of the earth, without the permission of the Kami, how could your desires be fulfilled? Resign yourselves, you two pillars.

Hatsuwakahime, with caution, reveals the full extent of her heart to you without reservation, and I respectfully thank you for the depth of your sincerity. Ah, Kannagara-kamunagara, may your mitama be blessed.

Singing thus, she descends the steep slope, approaching step by step. The two of them, hearing this song and showing signs of disappointment and discouragement, say:

Iku: 'Oi, you black-faced jo-dono, did you hear that song? As expected of Hatsuwakahime-sama, she seems to know even that we are waiting here with our faces painted with white and black things, for did she not say, "painting faces white and black"? This might be a failure.'

Saru: 'No, you white-faced jo-dono, please, by all means, do not worry.'

Saying this, holding a fan in one hand and a tree branch in the other, with a strange posture and keeping time with their feet, Saru says, 'Toto-tarari, to-tarari, tarari-ri, to-tarariya, tarari, to-tarari, whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, on-ha, katta-kata, enya-ha, oi, katta-kata.'

『トートータラリ、トータラリ、タラリーリ、トータラリヤー、タラリ、トータラリ、朝日は照るとも曇るとも、オンハ、カッタカタ、エンヤハ、オイ、カッタカタ』

§ 2

While keeping the rhythm with his mouth, he began to sing. Iku was also drawn out, exposing his Matsushiro face.

Iku: 'Even if the moon waxes or wanes, even if the earth should sink,'

Saru: 'To serve in the retinue of Hatsuwakahime, Enya, kattakata, Onha, kattakata, how could we leave it undone? For the teaching of the Ananahi Kami is, whether it be an enemy, a Gaki, or a Mushikera, a path of salvation, a path of salvation.'

Iku: 'Therefore, no matter that you are the Kami Hatsuwakahime, how could it be that you would not permit this white-faced Jiyōdono and this black-faced Jiyōdono, who pray with Magokoro, to join the retinue to the capital of Haruna? How now, black-faced Jiyōdono, shall we two not perform the dance of the Amatsuotome to soothe the Mikokoro of Hatsuwakahime and insistently serve in the retinue that is so difficult to be granted?'

Saru: 'Indeed so, perform the Onma of the Amatsuotome quickly; when you dance thus, Hatsuwakahime, who is equal to a Tenniyo, shall return to her original Yashiki in the forest of the shrine. Onha, kattakata, Enha, kattakata, kattariko, kattariko, katta, katta, kattariko, enya, onha. Hatsuwakahime, who appears as the Mitsukahi of the noble Kami who created this world, the Kamunahohi no Kami, and the Ōnahohi no Kami whose Mikokoro is vast—the Kami who, in the world of men, corrects all things by seeing, hearing, and declaring them anew—since you appear as a Senden-shi to save the Yobito, how could there be any reason for you not to permit our earnest prayer? Even if the morning sun shines or clouds over, the water of the waterfall echoes all around; this waterfall water, falling from the foot of the Ubusunayama, is the dew of blessing that, after lurking for a while beneath the leaves, flows into the river of the river deer and has become a waterfall here. Indeed, this flow, when it welled up from Ubusunayama, was but a mere dew, but flowing and flowing, it gathered the water of the small rivers in the valleys into one, and thus it has become the waterfall of the Mizu no Mitama, so auspicious and beautiful.'

Iku: 'Hatsuwakahime is the same; when you departed from Ubusunayama, your appearance was like the dew on the leaves, a travel garment of a delicate maiden, and you had no friends to converse with. Yet, how noble is the blessing of the Kami, that even we, in the form of beasts, have served in your retinue, adding a touch of smartness as we set out on this journey. If one were to liken that state, just as this waterfall water increases in volume as it goes, gathering small rivers and growing larger, it must be the principle of heaven and earth that you should be followed by many Kami in your retinue. How grateful, how noble! Hatsuwakahime, who has now revealed your figure before my eyes, please let us serve in your retinue. The white-faced Jiyōdono and the black-faced Jiyōdono, for the sake of saving the world of darkness where even black and white cannot be distinguished, swear to the Kami of heaven and earth and pray before you, Princess. Onha, kattakata, Enha, kattakata, katta, katta, kattariko, kattari, kattari, kattariko, enya, onha.'

With these words, the two blocked the valley path and danced frantically with all their might. Even the composed Hatsuwakahime had no words to answer their logic, and for a while, she stood in a daze, watching the two dance. Smart, feeling suspicious about the change in their faces, tilted his head and pondered, but finally realizing that it was a prank by Iku and Saru, he went between the two, wagging his tail, and barked fiercely two or three times, 'Wan, wan, wan!' At this, Iku and Saru finally finished their Sanbasō dance. Because they had danced frantically as if their lives depended on it, they were drenched in sweat and had become monsters beyond description.

Hatsuwakahime: 'Ho ho ho ho ho, are you not Iku and Saru? When did you come to such a place? Uzuhiko-sama must be very worried. Now, return quickly and attend to the business of the Kami. And what have you done to your faces? It is truly unsightly, you look like monsters. Once the Sanbasō is finished, wash your faces quickly in this valley stream. You never know who might pass by. If travelers see your faces, they will surely be startled.'

Iku: 'Oh, this is a truly auspicious occasion. Whether it is because the Great God of Heaven commanded us to accompany you on your journey, I know not, but my body flew through the air to this place, and for the first time since I was born, I have been made to perform the Sanbasau. I humbly ask you to consider this as Kamunagara, and I beg you to take us with you wherever you go.'

Saru: 'Please, I too, just as Iku has said, wish by all means to be allowed to accompany you.'

'It pains me to disregard your kind intentions, but as I said before, I am not permitted to have companions, so please, I ask you to bear with me on that point alone.'

Iku: 'It is because I wish to implore you on that very point, and because I have no face to show you, that I have painted my face white and black like this to make my request. No matter what you say, I shall accompany you. Since I have come running out here on my own without receiving permission from Uzuhiko-sama, I cannot return as I am. Please, thinking of it as saving us, I beg you to grant your permission. Even if you say it is the way of the Gods, surely there must be such a thing as special treatment or an exceptional act of grace. If you look at it through Kamunahohi and Ōnahohi, is it not the role of a Sendenshi who saves people to grant us this permission?'

'This is truly troublesome. However, since you are a person who must be under the control of Uzuhiko-sama, the priest of the shrine forest, I cannot take you with me on my own authority. Do not say such unreasonable things, and please return home quickly. That is your primary duty to the Gods, you see.'

Iku: 'Ah, if you will not listen even after I have begged you this much, there is no other way. Hey, Saru, it is time for the suicide squad.'

They exchange a glance. Thereupon, both of them quickly hook the waistbands they had prepared in their bosoms onto the treetops of the oak, hang their chins, and their Sanbasau dance instantly turns into the movements of Sumiyoshi-odori puppets. Smart, seeing this, is startled, barks loudly, wanders around the person of Hatsuwakahime, tugs at her sleeve, and though he does not say it with his mouth, he expresses in his demeanor, 'Please save the two of them quickly,' and cries out, straining his sorrowful voice. Hatsuwakahime quickly grabs their hanging bodies, lifts them up by about five sun, and rescues them both. The two are dazed and stand there blankly. Hatsuwakahime, unable to grant the two's fervent request and yet unable to refuse it, spends a while in tears, lost in thought. Taking advantage of the moment they regain their senses, she suddenly prays to the Gods, transforms her body into a great bear, while Smart becomes a Chinese lion, and they glare with angry eyes, pawing the ground, and growl 'Woo, woo' at the two. The two are shocked, press their hands together, unable to utter a single word, and cower on the ground, trembling. Hatsuwakahime returns to her original form, Smart transforms into a gigantic lion, and with Hatsuwakahime on his back, they dash off across the wilderness.
