Chapter 25: Way Song [1681]

道歌〔1681〕

§ 1

Tamakuniwake left the shrine on Mount Sankao, and as he descended the northern bank of the Jordan River, his vigor was restored; feeling his courage surge spontaneously as the sacred place drew near, with a clear voice he began to chant a Sendenka.

Tamakuniwake: 'By the teaching of the Kami, along the borders of the Jordan, upon the northern road, how joyous it is to press forward! What is that which appears in the distance—is it the summit of the ridge of the olive mountains, or is it the sacred ground? The waters of the Jordan River are pure, and the sound of the stream flows in a murmuring chorus, sounding as though it echoes throughout heaven and earth. The lotus stage of the Reizanejiyō, dwelling upon the mountain of lotus flowers, by the divine protection of Konohanahime, obeying the precious messenger of Hatsuwakahime opening all at once, even the sky of the heart is Masumihiko. Shining within that bosom, Tamakuniwake's journey today feels valorous without knowing why, soaring even up to heaven, and his feet step ever more lightly as he advances along the stream.

Ah, Kamunagara! My destination is Gethsemane; in the flower garden where the moon shines brightly, with a hundred flowers in full bloom and competing in brilliance, the messengers of the Kami, us, will surely be waiting, offering sweet smiles. Mikihikotsukasa, Itahiko, and Oniharuwake's Jidōkoji, Davis's princess and Bravūda, and other stern divine messengers—have they already arrived at the sacred place? We are hurrying along the way, pressing onward with single-minded devotion, and if by any chance we should be behind them, this would be a disgrace for eternity. Though this is what I say, the grand designs of the Kamukai cannot be understood by our clouded spirits. The summer wind blowing across the face of the Jordan River gently and purely caresses my breast,

and arriving at the sacred mountain of olive fragrance, famed as the golden peak, Lord Haniyasuhiko, the avatar of Nodatenohiko who has eternally settled there, if he hears that our entire party has arrived safely, how overjoyed he must be! Receiving even the three jewels possessed by the Eight Great Dragon Kings, and finally offering the precious divine jewel of the achievement of the divine work, how joyous my own person is! Do not worry, Masumihiko; the sacred place has already come into view. Hinodewake no Kamitsukasa, leading numerous attendant followers, will surely rejoice to welcome the arrival of our party, appearing and waiting outside the gate.

When I think of it, when I think of it, how courageous it is! This too is entirely the divine spirit, the miraculous messenger of the great deity Kami Susanō no Ōkami, the encouragement of Hatsuwakahime, for which I humbly and gratefully give thanks. The capital of victory draws near; advance, advance, raise your courage! Ascending to the precious sacred place, personally granting the miraculous power to Haniyasuhiko, reorganizing our battle array once more, and commanding the Nārāshiyā lurking in Mount Zion to perform wondrous achievements eternally—this is a desire of mine that must be fulfilled. Grant me to achieve it, Kamunagara; before the august deities residing in the sacred place, I humbly pray.

Though the morning sun shines or clouds over, though the moon waxes or wanes, even if the great earth sinks, the power of truth saves the world. Through the august majesty of the Kototama of absolute truth, Tamakuniwake has gradually drawn near to the capital. When I think of it, when I think of it, the infinite benevolent grand designs of the august deities—overwhelmed with gratitude, tears spontaneously welled up and drifted across my cheeks. Ah, Kamunagara! Words fail me from excess of deep emotion and joy; with this, I conclude in silence.'

So chanting to an end, he looked back to see the face of Hatsuwakahime, and the princess's countenance added sublimity upon sublimity, brimming with indescribable joy and majesty. Tamakuniwake, unconsciously and before he knew it, let his head drop with a start, turned his body to the side of the road, and while rubbing his hands together,

Tamakuniwake: 'Nay, I have truly committed a rude thing. Come, please go ahead first.'

Hatsuwakahime: 'The Kami are in order, you see, ho-ho-ho.'

laughing as she spoke, she took the lead and advanced.

Smart followed close behind Hatsuwakahime, wagging his tail.

Masumihiko: 'I say, Sensei, you have failed again, haven't you? Just now Hatsuwakahime said in a single word that the Kami are in order, didn't she? Because you arrogantly went on ahead, you got scolded, didn't you? Have you forgotten that even at the foot of Mount Sankao she said, "The Kami are in order. If you break the order, discipline is disturbed"?'

Tamakuniwake: 'No, that was not what I was thinking. I went ahead with the intention of guiding the princess. After all, my thought was the thought of serving as the vanguard, you see.'

§ 4

The manner in which the leaves of the densely growing olive trees rustle in the wind has come to be visible. Now, getting closer and closer from here to Gethsemane, the flower garden where the Kami gather, moment by moment, we shall likely meet Michihiko, Itahiko, and other pillars of the Kami, the Kamutsukasa from whom we parted some time ago. When I think of it, how courageous it is! Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, may your Mitama be blessed.'

Chanting thus, they finally arrived at the ferry of the Jordan River. In ancient times, this was the place where a golden bridge called the Golden Bridge spanned. On the western bank of the river, Hinodewakenomikoto, accompanied by numerous Kamutsukasa, with countless purple, red, white, yellow, and light-blue flags fluttering in the river breeze, was awaiting the arrival of Hatsuwakahime and her party. By the order of Hinodewakenomikoto, a newly built roofless boat, with four boatmen handling the oars and sculls, was rowing toward this side. From across the river, the shouts of 'Ura-ura' could be heard to the extent of shaking the mountains. Hatsuwakahime and her party entrusted their bodies to the welcoming boat and leisurely crossed to the opposite bank, and upon reaching the riverbank, Hinodewakenomikoto stepped closely beside Hatsuwakahime and exchanged a firm handshake. Next, handshakes were exchanged with Tamakuniwake and Masumihiko, and guarded before and behind by hundreds of Kamutsukasa and believers, they proceeded to march toward the garden of Gethsemane, praised as the Riverbank of Peace.

と謡ひ乍ら漸くにしてヨルダン川の渡し場に着いた。昔はここに黄金橋と云ふ黄金の橋のかかつてゐた処である。川の西岸には日出別命数多の神司を従へ、幾百旒とも知れぬ紫赤白黄浅黄の旗を河風になびかせ、初稚姫一行の到着を待たせ玉ひつつあつた。日出別命の命によつて新造の無棚舟は、四人の水夫が櫓櫂を操り乍ら、此方に向つて漕ぎ来る。川の向ふには、ウラーウラーの声山岳も揺ぐ許りに聞えて来た。初稚姫一行は迎への舟に身を托し悠々として向ふ岸に渡り、川岸に着くや日出別命は初稚姫の側にツと寄り添ひ、固き握手を交換した。次いで玉国別、真純彦に握手を交はし、数百人の神司や信徒に前後を守られて、安の河原と称へられたるゲッセマネの園へと練り行く事となつた。
