Chapter 17: Mountain 颪 [1673]

山颪〔1673〕

§ 1

Bravāda: 'When Kami appears before our eyes, Separating good from evil, The Great-Straight-True Kami who created this world, The Great-Straight-True Kami of vast and boundless heart, Truly, whatever happens in the world of men, Seeing straight, looking straight again, listening straight again, Decreeing straight, walking on the path of Kami, Crossing over Lake Sudarma, Crossing mountains and at last, Climbing halfway up the pass of Mount Harusei, At the very moment when she was climbing, The weak-footed one, exhausted from the journey, Suddenly collapsed upon the great earth, With her advance and retreat blocked right here, And when her breath grew faint and gasping, Oh, the joy when the Great Kami of infinite benevolence, Michihiko, the guide of the Way, Appeared and mercifully saved her from peril, Being touched by that sincerity, Suddenly her vision grew dark, Burning fire of passion hard to be quenched, And just at the moment when her chastity Was about to be broken, Appearing as the avatar of Konohanahime Who dwells upon Mount Tenkyōzan, Manifesting as the princess of Debisu, Proclaiming deep teachings, Here the two awoke from their dreams, Parting from each other with reluctant sleeves, With a man's stride so swift, Michihiko the guide went forth. I, behind him, all alone, In a traveling sky whose paths I have not even trodden, The hardship of climbing up Steep slopes laboriously, The Great Ōmikami of Ananai-kyō, Pitying me, a weakling woman, Pray grant that I may gaze upon the honorable face Of my husband even a single day sooner. High and boundless is the august sky, Wide and limitless is the great earth. Even if the countless grass-people of these heavens and earth Should dwell in numbers without end, The living Kami who gave life to my body and soul And consoles me is none other than Itahiko alone. Ah, Kannagara, O Kami, O Kami, Protect me on today's journey. On the mountain path of Mount Harusei, Without distinction between day and night, Ugly thieves come out and lurk, Plundering and tormenting travelers, When I heard this, the shock Caused the bowels of a small woman To strike waves of wonder. That being so, we Are those who advance along the Great Way Of the Absolute and Infinite Kami Who created the heavens and the earth. If we press straight forward with sincere hearts, And advance upon the righteous path, How could any foes or deviant kami Ever dare to stain even a single finger upon us? Ah, Kannagara, O Kami, O Kami, I pray for the divine grace of the august spirits.'

Bravāda finally climbed up to the summit of the pass, and though it was a hazy moonlit night, she gazed down at the scenery where, floating from above the thin mists, the peaks of the mountain ranges standing side by side appeared here and there beyond,

Bravāda: 'When I stand and gaze from the pass of Harusei, The mountains of all four directions can be seen right below. The peaks of the mountains floating in the sea of mist, I imagine them to be homes built by Kami. Helped by the divine guide Michihiko, Have I at last arrived at the summit? Though the moon fills the great sky, it hides behind the clouds That hang upon the heart of my inmost soul. Itahiko, my beloved husband, how must he Have crossed these mountain paths? The sound of the hora conch blown by Harumichi Kyōshi Has also become inaudible; must he have gone far away? Loneliness comes pressing upon my own body, Above the ridges of the mountains where not a single shadow of man is seen. Though I am a body embraced by the blessings Of the Kami of heaven and earth, my heart is lonely. Come now, let us rest awhile on the grassy turf, Straighten our horses' packs, and descend as we proceed.'

Saying this, she sat down upon the grassy turf to rest her weary feet, chanted the Heavenly Number Song, and stood with her hands pressed together in prayer.

From the shade of the trees on all sides, three or four thieves suddenly emerged in a scatter, blocking Bravāda's path, and while the leader shook his face vertically and horizontally across and over, all over,

Leader (in a cowardly voice): 'Ko-ko-ko-ko, hey, woman tra-tra-traveler, whe-whe-whe-where are you going?'

Bravāda: 'Why, I am a woman on the business of Kami, going on a pilgrimage to Jerusalem, and proceeding to the capital of the Moon World.'

Leader: 'Na-na-na-na, what? Going to the Moon World? Heh, putting on the airs of a little girl, do you think you can go safely to such a place all alone? Hey there, who do you take me for? I am Dedabotchi in broad daylight... unt, dottsukoi, the great lord of highway obstruction, you see.'

Just at that moment, the mid-autumn moon pushed aside the clouds and illuminated the nether world, while at the same time, the leader's comical face unfolded with a start right before the princess.

Bravāda, unable to bear her amusement, burst out laughing with a 'Putsufu-ko-ko-ko'.

ブラワ゛ーダ姫『神が表に現はれて 善と悪とを立別ける 此世を造りし神直日 心も広き大直日 只何事も人の世は 直日に見直し聞直し 詔直し行く神の道 スダルマ湖を打渡り 山野を越えて漸くに ハルセイ山の峠をば 半ば登れる折もあれ 行き疲れたる足弱の 忽ち大地に打倒れ 進退ここに谷まりて 息たえだえになりし時 仁慈無限の大神の 道の司の三千彦が 現はれまして危難をば 救はせ給ひし嬉しさよ その真心にほだされて

忽ち眼相晦み 燃ゆる情火の消し難く 已に危く貞操の 破れむとするその時に 天教山にあれませる 木花姫の御化身 デビスの姫と現はれて 深き教を宣り給ひ ここに二人は夢覚めて 惜しき袂を分ちつつ 男の足のいと早く 三千彦司は出でましぬ 妾は後に只一人 踏みも習はぬ旅の空 険しき阪をやうやうに 攀登り行く苦しさよ 三五教の大御神 繊弱き妾を憐れみて 一日も早く吾夫の 御顔を拝ませ賜へかし

御空は高く限りなし 大地は広く極みなし これの天地の人草の 数限りなく住むとても 妾が身魂を生かしまし 慰め給ふ生神は たつた一人の伊太彦ぞ あゝ惟神々々 守らせ給へ今日の旅 ハルセイ山の阪道は 昼と夜との区別なく 醜の盗人出没し 旅人を掠めなやますと 聞いたる時の驚きは 小さき女の腸に 驚異の波を打たせけり さは去り乍ら吾々は 天地を造り給ひたる 絶対無限の神様の 大道を進み行くものぞ

誠心を立貫いて 正しき道を進むなら 如何なる仇も枉神も いかで一指を染め得むや あゝ惟神々々 御霊の恩頼を願ぎ奉る』

ブラワ゛ーダは峠の頂きに漸く登りつめ、朧月夜ながら眼下に並立する連峯の頂の彼方此方に、薄い霧の上から浮いて現はれてゐる光景を眺め、

ブラワ゛ーダ姫『ハルセイの峠に立ちて眺むれば 四方の山々真下に見えける。 霧の海に浮べる山の頂きは 神の造りし家かとぞ思ふ。 三千彦の神の司に助けられ 漸く頂上に着きにけるかな。 大空に月はませども村肝の 心にかかる雲にかくれつ。 伊太彦の吾背の君は如何にして これの山路を越えましにけむ。 治道居士吹き立て給ふ法螺の音も 聞えずなりぬ遠く行きけむ。 淋しさは吾身に迫り来るなり 人影もなき山の尾の上は。 天地の神の恵に包まれし 吾身なれども心淋しき。 いざさらば暫し芝生にやすらひて 駒立て直し下り進まむ』

と疲れた足を休むべく芝生の上に腰打掛け、天の数歌を歌ひ合掌して居る。

四辺の木蔭からバラバラバラと現はれし三四の泥棒、ブラワ゛ーダの前に立塞がり、甲は顔中を縦横十文字に振り動かせ乍ら、

甲（卑怯な声）『コヽヽヽコレ、女のタヽヽ旅人、ドヽヽヽどこへ行くのだ』

ブラワ゛『ハイ妾は神様の御用でエルサレムへ参拝を致し、月の国の都へ進む女で厶います』

甲『ナヽヽヽ何だ。月の国へ行く？　ヘン、小女ツちよの態をして、只一人、そんな処へ無事行けると思ふか。コレヤ、おれをドヽヽどなたと心得てる。天下晴れてのデーダラボッチ……ウント　ドツコイ、大道路妨様だぞ』

折から中秋の月は雲を押し分け下界を照らし給ふと共に、珍妙な甲の顔はパツと姫の前に展開した。

ブラワ゛ーダは可笑しさに堪へきれず、『プツフヽヽヽ』と吹き出した。

§ 2

First man: 'Hey, little girl Kome, what's so funny?'

Bravāda had finally lost her temper.

Bravāda: 'Ho, ho, ho, ho, ho! Just look at that—a face that you can't tell whether it's a horse, a monkey, or a Bakemono. If we captured her, took her to the capital of Haruna, and sold her to a Dōbutsuen, we could make some Kanemau. Ah, a good find has turned up. Hey, Eukuwai, I am going to Ikedo you right now, so you'd better fold your hands in Shinmeu submission!'

First man: 'Hey, Kiyo-Kiyōdai, this woman has pretty big Dokiyō. Ha, ha, ha, ha, ha! She couldn't be a Bakemono, could she?'

Second man: 'Kōriya! Hey! As a Bunzai of a woman, what do you mean by spitting out Baugen toward a Shichishiyaku Danshi? We are the oyabun of the Dorobausama. Throw away all your Mochimono neatly and become Matsupadaka. If you say why and wherefore and babble nonsense, I'll knock you down with my Tetsuwan, pluck off your head, and take your life entirely! Now then, this is your hundredth year. Even if you waste a thousand words and offer excuses, I won't lend an ear; you had better face your end obediently. If you do, I will spare at least your parent-given sack-length.'

Bravāda: 'Ho, ho, ho, ho, ho! What a spineless fellow. For a rough man to catch a single delicate woman and line up threatening phrases—what a gutless coward, not even worth talking about. You must have parents, too. I'd love to see the face of the parents who gave birth to such a useless nuisance. Just look at that—what a pathetic face. You're just putting on a big bluff and trembling all over, aren't you?'

First man: 'Na, na, na, na! Since he's a novice unaccustomed to the trade, chi, chi, chi, trembling a little is only natural. When you faced a man for the first time, you must have trembled, too. No matter what you say, this is our very first battle, so don't look down on us so much.'

Third man: 'Koriya, both of you, what are you saying... You are cowards. Now, I'm going to corner her one-on-one. You watch my technique.'

Before he finished speaking, he clenched his iron fist and tried to punch the woman fiercely right in the cheek. The moment Bravāda nimbly ducked her body, she swept his legs. How could he possibly endure it? He turned two or three somersaults, tumbled four or five ken down the steep slope, struck his forehead hard, gasped just a single 'Un,' and lay completely flat. Seeing this state, Bravāda's courage suddenly surged,

Bravāda: 'Ho, ho, ho, ho, ho! I don't know if you're the oyabun of thieves, but you're terribly weak. Hey, you little scoundrels over there, shall I pinch and toss you around, too?'

First man: 'Na, na, na, na! What are you spewing out? As if I'd let myself be pinched and tossed around in a place like this, like a cat or something! Hey, hold my hand. If you do that, no matter how strong a woman she is, there's hardly any chance of us being thrown.'

Second man: 'So, so, so, so! Running away is better than that.'

First man: 'Ni, ni, ni, ni! Even if you say run away, our transportation system isn't obeying our commands, is it?'

His body was rattling, his legs were trembling, and his lips were dyed purple as he shook.

Bravāda: 'Ho, ho, ho, ho! When I thought thieves would be a bit more quick-witted, they turned out to be so weak. Well, that stands to reason. Since they have countless tasks that should be done, yet are clumsy and spineless fellows incapable of a single thing, they are wretches who try to sustain their frail lives by pilfering others' goods without working; there is no reason on earth why such fellows would have any backbone. Still, how pitiful they look. Hey, you two thieves, reform yourselves... Though you probably can't do it, at least you'd better stop being thieves. If you keep doing things like this, in the end you'll lose your lives, too.'

Second man: 'Yes! We will stop being thieves starting from this very limit. Please excuse us, and do pass through here.'

Bravidahime: 'Setting out upon my very first journey, I encountered petty thieves for the very first time. Come now, farewell, petty thieves, let us part; Reform your hearts and return to goodness.'

First and second men, all at once:

'Though we had no original heart to steal, We were bewildered out of our love for life. From now on, even if we were to die, We shall not engage in theft down to our grandchildren's generation.'

From the shadow of the dense trees nearby, a man's voice echoed all around,

'O life of Bravidahime, quickly Go down, unto Sakamitsuhiko.'

Bravidahime: 'Down the three thousand steep mountain passes renowned in legend, Like Hiko and Hiko, I shall go descending.'

So replying, she once again chanted her propaganda song and gradually faded into the distance. The three thieves stumbled and staggered, hiding their figures within the forest.

Mitsuhiko: 'Aha, ha, ha, ha, ha! Evil is always weak before justice.'
