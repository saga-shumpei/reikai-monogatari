Chapter 3: Rock 侠 [1659]

岩侠〔1659〕

§ 1

There were two women held captive within the cavern of Torakumayama. Each was enclosed in a separate chamber, lamenting her tragic fate, while secretly conveying her thoughts to the other by means of song. One of these women was Davis-hime, and the other was Burawāda-hime.

Burawāda secretly sang:

'I am a mournful, sightless little bird; Though spring hath arrived, no flowers bloom, Nor can I hear the voices of the birds. Be it dawn or dusk, all is enveloped in darkness; I am a lonely, sightless little bird. Even the star of tears of love is invisible; Be it dawn or dusk, all is enveloped in darkness. Accompanied by the beloved man, Having at last left the bosom of my father and mother, As a young chick, I have no means to return to the old nest. Where is my beloved one now? Though I long to meet him but once, Deeply enclosed within this cavern of hideous intrigue, I see neither the light of the sun nor the moon— A sorrow of my mortal frame, Which I have no means to relate to anyone. Eternal tears burst forth; Oh, that they might eventually clear, That the gates of the Ōsaka barrier might open, And I might hear the warbling of the nightingale! When I ponder and ponder, I worry for the well-being of my father and mother; Whither shall I discharge The ten-thousand-fold volume of tears surging in my breast? Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! May the Mitama bestow your blessing upon us, That as swiftly as possible, Tamakuniwake, The minister of the Ananahi Kami, May perceive my peril And come forth to rescue me! Even the chirping of the matsumushi groweth faint inside this cavern Where happy tidings remain unheard— How sorrowful is my lot!'

Davis-hime, who had been thrown into the prison of the adjacent cavern, heard this song and realized that Burawāda was imprisoned in the next room.

Davis-hime: 'Where high crossbeams rise on Mount Terumon, Firmly establishing the great palace pillars, Serveth morning and evening The Ōkami who abideth in tranquility; Leaving the bosom of her father and mother, Rescued by the divine Michihiko, When at last she reached the banks of the Harusei marsh, Four or five hideous Magatsu Appeared from amidst the mist, Without letting her speak a word, seized her, Inserted a gag into her mouth, And while subjecting her to cruel tortures, Dragged her into this cavern; Morning and evening, the chiefs Of the robbers, Sēru and Hāru, Force an unwanted romance upon her, Causing her to writhe in agony— I am she, Davis-hime, the wife of Michihiko. Is the song I hear now sung By the noble wife of Itahiko? Not escaping the pattern of this sorrowful floating world, Hath your life also fallen into the evil schemes Of the wicked villains, Bringing you to this place? Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! If Kami exist in heaven and earth, Soften and purify the souls of the hideous masters, And deliver the two of us from our suffering! "God is with thee; Man is a child of God, a temple of God, And a being equal to God"— Though I have heard this stern teaching, What can a frail woman do? Tracing the dreampaths of this fleeting world, I pray morning and evening That the sorrow of my frame may become but a dream; Yet this terrible reality doth not clear away. The mountain wind of Torakumayama Rageth fiercely morning and evening, Tearing my heart and crushing my body; Having no longer any resort, my tears Have completely dried up—how sorrowful it is! Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! Bestow upon us the grace of the Mitama!'

Thus she was singing faintly. Hearing this voice, Burawāda in the adjacent room gained a little strength; considering the time when all was quiet in the middle of the night, they conversed with each other about the frailty of their lives in low voices through song.

Davis-hime: 'How came Burawāda-hime-no-mikoto to be imprisoned in this cavern?'

Burawāda-hime: 'Abducted by a villain, I wandered into this place in my desire to meet Itahiko.'

Davis-hime: 'Art thou also a person not outside the troubles of this floating world, suffering together with me?'

Burawāda-hime: 'How may I escape this suffering? Such is my present state that weeping is of no avail.'

Davis-hime: 'I too am a cuckoo of the same sentiment, my voice growing hoarse as I cry in secret.'

Burawāda-hime: 'Starting with Itahiko and Minister Michihiko, I have spent my days worrying for my master.'

Davis-hime: 'If our bodies are protected by the Sumera-ōkami, the time of our rescue shall surely come again.'

Thus they were expressing their inner feelings to each other. Arriving there with stealthy steps was Sub-Lieutenant Hāru, formerly a Brahman, who was known as the second-in-command of this cavern. Unlike a villain, Hāru was a handsome man of fair complexion and elegant features. He had fallen in love with Burawāda's graceful and captivating figure, and was breaking his heart with the torments of love, striving by any means to make her the jewel in his palm. Taking advantage of the moment when his chief, Sēru, had fallen asleep dead-drunk, he came to the entrance of this prison with quiet footsteps to achieve his romantic ambition.

§ 2

Burawāda: 'How strange! Who goes there in this midnight? Return quickly, vile magahito. I am the officer who serves Kami; How could evil ever attack me?'

Hāru, from outside the window: 'I am the officer of Hāru of the chest; I have come in secret to meet thee. Morning and evening I strove to save thee, Yet the officer Sēru permitted it not.'

Burawāda-hime: 'Born into this world as a danshi, How strange to become a nusubito! However fair and pure thy appearance may be, I shall offer no kotoba to a vile officer.'

Hāru: 'On the uhabe I may seem a vile officer, Dost thou not see the flower of my heart? I am a mononofu of both flower and fruit, Who hath come in secret now to save thee.'

Burawāda-hime: 'In a world full of falsehood, How could thy kotoba be accepted as truth? If thou too art a nusubito, Step not outside thy home on an ubatama night. How strange that a masurao should lose his mind to the scent of a frail woman and come pursuing her!'

Hāru stood chiyoritsu in the dark and uttered a hitorigoto: 'Well, well, it seems she won't listen to what I say, no matter what. Wait, wait, I must devise some kufū to make this woman say yes. Once a man utters words, he cannot pull back, or a danshi's menbokudama will be marutsubu. No matter what, though I hold the power of seisatsuyodatsu, it is only because I have the yowami of love that I am acting otona; yet there is nothing I cannot accomplish. I shall threaten her a bit and make her suffer an Ōjiyō.'

Nodding to himself, he deliberately raised his voice:

Hāru: 'Hey, woman! When I act otona, you get arrogant and bring shame upon a shichishiyaku danshi; you are an outrageously futodo kusemono! I hold the power of seisatsuyodatsu, so if you hate it, fine. Even if I have to force you into an Ōjiyō, I will splendidly make you yield. How about settling your mind and giving a favorable answer?'

Burawāda: 'Ho-ho-ho-ho, for someone of your standing as a greenhorn, what on earth are you saying? Have a little shame! Throwing a frail woman like me into a prison where lions are turned loose, and trying to take advantage of her weakness to satisfy your romantic lust—your innermost heart is utterly despicable. Do you think there is any fool of a woman who would yield to such a cowardly and miserable man, even if her body were to become food for crows? Put your hand on your chest and think about it! The man I love is none other than Itahiko-tsukasa, the propagation envoy of Divine Teaching!'

Hāru: 'What? Are you the wife of that fellow called Itahiko? Well, well, what a pitiable thing. That fellow Itahiko fell into my plot, dropped into a trap, and yesterday at dusk he passed away into silent peace. No matter how much you love him, you cannot live together with a ghost. Giving up is essential for human beings; you cannot live a single life forever either. You will have to take a husband eventually. How about it? As the fish desires water, water desires the fish. My face isn't entirely something to be discarded either. I am Second Lieutenant Hāru, who gained fame as a handsome man even in the capital of Haruna. Don't you feel like changing your mind and yielding to me?'

Burawāda: 'What? Is it true that Itahiko-sama fell into your evil hands, dropped into a trap, and passed away?'

Hāru: 'Ahahaha, you are a pitiable thing too! Itahiko had all his money stolen, his clerical robes stripped off, became stark naked, fell to the bottom of hell, and died! Wouldn't it be better for you to give it up already?'

Burawāda: 'Ho-ho-ho, it is useless even if you tell such lies! Itahiko-sama never wears clerical robes. He does not possess money; his master holds it, so he only has pocket change... Besides, as long as he possesses the Jewel of the Night Light, there is no way he would fall into your plots! Ho-ho, well, well, what a faint-hearted, weak danshi you are! I am so thoroughly disgusted by your extreme foolishness that my open mouth will not even close. Unluckily for you, my body has yet to be presented to Itahiko-sama, so I flatly refuse. Please go negotiate with someone else!'

§ 3

Growing somewhat irritable, the young, gentle woman began to utter words of reproach. When a woman has cast away her life, she utters things of indescribable strength.

Debisuhime held her breath and pricked up her ears, determined not to miss a single word of the exchange between the two.

At that moment, the Oyabun Sēru, who happened to wake up, felt suspicious upon noticing that Hāru was nowhere to be seen. As he walked toward the privy, he heard a whispering voice coming from the direction of the prison cell where the two women were confined. Wandering through the dark tunnel with steps still unsteady from drunkenness, he collided heavily against Hāru's shoulder. Hāru, caught by surprise, tumbled over, struck his skull slightly against the corner of a rock, and collapsed on the spot with a groan.

Sēru: 'W-Who is it? Whoever you are, you came here to woo them, thinking of whispering sweet words before my future wife and concubine, didn't you? Divine retribution is swift; you groaned and fell, but it must likely be that rascal Hāru. When he is around, he gets in the way of my love, and there is no helping it. With his pale, melon-seed face, he is the type to be doted on by women, so I was just thinking of doing something to him. Quite by chance, he bumped into me, cracked his head, and achieved Jiyakumetsuiraku. Until now, because he was somewhat clever, he contributed his strength to the organization of the Toransu Group, but now that the foundation has become so solid, he has become a hindrance instead. Oh my, oh my, good things come only when the time is right!'

Hāru, having struck his head, became faint and groaned, 'Un, un.' However, he suddenly snapped to his senses as Sēru laughed in a loud voice, and while groaning 'Un, un' in the darkness, he pondered the situation.

Sēru: 'Hey, you two women, you must have heard my soliloquy just now, didn't you? And that rascal Hāru must have said something to you. Tell me about it. The lives of you two are held in the single hand of this Sēru. If you do not obey submissively, it will be worse for you, eeh. Ah, I am drunk, I am drunk, how painful it is. It seems I am about to open a department store. At any rate, today my tongue is tangled and I cannot conduct full negotiations, so let us hold it off until tomorrow. Now that Hāru has fallen, you belong to me, ha ha ha ha. Though I may look like this, you two women, I am Captain Sēru, a man with blood and tears. You might think I am a merciless human being without blood or tears, but I know duty and human feeling very well, I have tasted love, and I understand affection. First, it would be best for you to enjoy tonight and wait for tomorrow.'

Arranging his convenient logic, he returned to his own living quarters. Hāru continued to groan 'Un, un' as before. Debisuhime and Burawāda, sharing the exact same feeling and behind the cover of darkness, stuck out their tongues and shook their chins, laughing in mockery.

セール『オイ、二人の女、最前から俺の独言を聞いたであらうのう。そして、ハールの野郎がお前達に何か云つたであらう。夫を一つ聞かして呉れ。お前達二人の生命は、このセールが片手の中に握つて居るのだ。素直に致さぬと為が悪いぞエーン。あゝ酔ふた酔ふた苦しいわい。どうやら百貨店を開店しさうだ。兎に角今日は舌が縺れて充分の交渉も出来ないから、明日迄保留して置かう。ハールが斃つた以上は、もう俺のものだハヽヽヽ。かう見えても二人の女、血も有り涙も有るセール大尉だ。お前達は血も涙もない悪棘の人間だと思ふであらうが、義理人情もよく知つて居る、恋も味はつて居れば愛も解して居る。まづ今晩は楽しんで明日を待つがよからう』

と勝手の理窟を並べ乍ら吾居間へ帰つて往く。ハールは相変らず『ウンウン』と唸つて居る。デビス姫、ブラワ゛ーダの二人は、同じ思ひの負ず劣らず、暗を幸ひ舌を出し腮をしやくつて嘲笑して居た。
