Chapter 2: Ancient 峡の Mountain [1658]

古峡の山〔1658〕

§ 1

The summit of Mount Torakumayama, which has just emerged from the clouds, is covered with snow despite being midsummer, and the smoke of the funkuwa rising above it is of a yellow-ochre color tinged with aomi, drifting further and further to the south.

The road gradually becomes a gentle slope, where the sound of Kayano is lonely, and two monbashira at the site of an ancient Brahman sekishiyoato lie fallen and leaning, looking sorrowfully up at the heavens in giyōten. The keshiki of the surrounding shinrin possesses a shinzanteki atmosphere in every respect, from the colors of the trees and grasses to the hues of the yamayama. Emerging from the mitsurin on the saiu, amidst only the sound of rain beating down and the sound of grass lying flat in the wind, seven people—walking amidst kayakusa taller than a man's height, with nothing but clouds to their right and left, stepping as their feet guide them and crossing the Hosotanigaha—are none other than Chidaukoji's itsukau. Chidaukoji climbs the sankei within the mitsurin, purifying the jiyaki of the sanya as he blows his accustomed horagahi. Ahead in their path, futari of toransu are seated once again on stones by the robau, engrossed in some zatsudan.

Kō: 'Hey, Tāru, isn't this pointless? On our way to conquer the mansion of Iso following the Brahman army, General Kishinwake suddenly had a change of heart and disbanded the army, so we have degenerated into toransu—something we have never done since we were born—and cannot return to our familiar native land, spending our days following Chief Sēru in search of aimless birds; isn't that truly senseless? I'm thinking of looking for an opportunity to return to my home country, somehow make a living, and try to lead my ideal life in my own native land, but what do you think?'

Tāru: 'Don't say such foolish things. What does it matter where we die? This entire heaven and earth is our native land. Someone like you who thinks their native land is merely the place where they were born does not yet know the true native land. The place of one's birth is merely like a single night's lodging on the journey of this world. Therefore, it is fine to die in the sea, fine to die in the sanya, fine to die in a river, in the capital, or in the countryside, and it is also fine to go on a journey and become fertilizer for the trees along the road. Wherever the sun, moon, and stars are harbored in the heavens and all things are borne upon the earth, there is nowhere that one goes that is not one's native land. Therefore, between this heaven and earth, it makes no difference in what kind of place one lives, sleeps, or dies. Once a human being has attained such enlightenment, there is no reason to long for the place where they were born or to begrudge their life. The ancestors of So-and-so of such-and-such a country, district, and village did not originally gush forth from that land like water, nor did they crawl out from the soil like mushrooms or bamboo shoots. Surely they migrated from outside and developed that land, did they not? If that is already true of our ancestors, why should we, their descendants, not boldly adopt the actions of our ancestors? Going to an unknown, new country, clearing new land to build a new village, or organizing a toransu group are all human freedom. Only when a human being reaches this point can it be said that they have displayed their true dignity. Taking up a single fishing rod and casually rowing a small boat, is that not exceedingly delightful? Yet when attempting to leave that land aboard a steamship of tens of thousands or thousands of tons, several hundreds or thousands of earthworm-like cowards shed tears as they regret their parting, but is this not truly our disgrace? While active in this very land of the Moon Country, to long for one's native land is the uttermost ugliness among ugliness, is it not? Leaving this place and arriving at that place, leaving one neighbor and reaching the next. Why should there be any need for the folly of regretting separation and tormenting one's heart and body? A person like you has reached the absolute extreme of dull-witted foolishness and pathetic posture. Your inner mind is so transparent that it has become amusing to me. Why don't you pull yourself together a bit; how can you carry on the toransu trade with such a mind? One wave comes and one wave leaves, ten thousand waves come and ten thousand waves leave. Such is the state of ten thousand miles of ocean. Raging waves and fierce billows are already ordinary matters. A person like you who foolishly fails to understand the true native land fears drifting away from their home for no good reason, even turning pale in complexion, a petty soul and faint heart whose chest burns with anguish. In the end, you will fall ill and shrivel up, becoming a pitiful object unable to make a move. A coward who traverses a thousand miles of sanya, wringing their intestines, only to shout for joy at a flower garden in a single small village is utterly unworthy to speak of human life. Instead of being a human being who laboriously tills fields in a small, cold village and spends their days weeding the paddies, a man should build a house in the vast, boundless fields of cold mountains and icy lands to form a great empire, fulfilling his duty as the ruler overseeing the domain of heaven and earth—this is what a man ought to strive for. This heaven and earth is truly our native land. Leaving the land of one's birth, which is equal to a single night's lodging, one must not commit the servile act of returning to the old nest again and seeking a home. This is the vital secret for living in today's human world.'

A wretch who forgets their birth Kokiyō will ultimately forget their nation, lose all remembrance of Jingi and Dautoku, become Fujin toward their Saishi, and multiply the sins of Fukau toward their Sosen. One who is not driven by the longing of Baukiyō—is that not a creature with Jinmen and Jiushin, who has forgotten the Ningen Reisei?'

If there were any good opportunity, I would love nothing more than to return to the so-called good of the world, live a life of empty manners and hypocrisy, and be praised by Shiyakuwai. It is precisely to gather the material for that purpose that I am engaging in this Toransu which I do not even like... We do not yet have enough leeway to preach Jingi and Dautoku. We have neither the material preparation for it, nor have we reached the point of boasting as the incarnation of hypocrisy that deceives the world. For the present, considering the Hatsutatsu of our business is safety first above all else. For as long as one has money, even a fool becomes a wise man, an unlettered person becomes a scholar, an Akunin becomes a good person, and a mere fly-insect fellow is called an influential person. You too had better repent and devote yourself heart and soul to the study of Toransu-gaku.'

Clamouring and thundering voices Resound from here and there, And the world is somehow filled with unrest. Ah, it is painful, it is agonizing; For what cause into such a fleeting world Was I born?'

Taru: 'Hey, M, what kind of despicable song are you singing? In the very height of summer, do not speak of such lonely things as winter or winter gales. While being a Baramon soldier, whining about your spirit shrinking and the like—ah, now I too am somehow feeling sick of the fleeting world. They say water conforms to the shape of the vessel, and a person follows good or evil companions. Working together with a bankrupt of evil like you, I too seem about to feel the impermanence of the world and fall into the so-called path of good of Shiyakuwai. Hey, the sound of a conch shell has come to be heard! Say, does this fellow not possess an authoritative voice from nowhere? Come, we must pull ourselves together here, exhaust the secret arts of Toransu, and skillfully draw that one into the rock cave. Come, here we shall erase such nouns as good or righteousness!'

Thus he suddenly put on a show of false courage.

With Chidō-koji at the head, the six repentant group members followed the narrow path through the dense forest and finally appeared before the two.

Taru: 'Hey, Yaku, Ēru, where is that personage heading?'

タール『これはこれは鬼春別将軍様で厶いましたか、お久しう厶います。私も、こんなトランスはし度くありませぬが、貴方から頂いたお手当金は、心の悪魔に皆使はれて了ひ、今は止むを得ずセールの世話になり、虎熊山の岩窟にトランスの乾児となつて居ります。然し貴方様が、そんな姿とお成り遊ばし、世界をお歩き遊ばすのを見て、懺悔の心が湧きました。只今限り私も改心致しますから、何卒岩窟にお越し下さいまして、セールの大親分を説き伏せ、善道へ復るやう御取計らひ下さいませ』

エム『将軍様、何分宜しくお願致します』

とエムは本当に涙を流してゐる。治道居士はタールの心の中からの改心でない、自分をたばかる為だ、とは直覚して居たが、兎も角岩窟の中に無事に到着して、セール、ハールの巨頭を改心させむと決心し、ワザとタールの言葉を信ずるものの如く装ひ、

治道『やア、それは何より結構だ。俺もお前の言葉を聞いて感謝に堪へない。サア案内して呉れ』

タール『ハイ、承知致しました。勝手覚えし此山道、近道も知つて居りますれば、お伴をさして貰ひませう』

エム『もしもし、治道居士様、何卒私を貴方のお弟子となし、どこかへ連れて行つて下さいませ。セール、ハールの大将を初め、此タールだつて到底改心の見込はありませぬ。あんな事云つて計略にかけて懐のものを盗らうと云ふ企みで厶いますよ』

タール『コリヤ、エム、何と云ふ事を吐すのだ。俺の心が貴様に分らうかい。仮令セール、ハールが悪人でも、もとの主人たる将軍様が、斯うなつて衆生済度にお歩き遊ばす姿を拝んだならば、屹度改心をなさるにきまつてる……。味方の裏切りする奴がどこにあるかい』

と小声で窘める。エムは大声を張り上げ乍ら、

エム『もし治道様、御一同様、険呑ですから御用心なさいませ。私は之にてお暇します。岩窟へでも帰らうものなら、私の今云つた言葉を大将に告げ、どんな目に合はすかも知れませぬから、貴方も何処へ逃げて下さい。さア早く早く、お逃げなさい』

と云ひ捨て、雲を霞と森林の中へ姿を隠した。

タール『ハヽヽヽ疑の深い奴だな。セールの親分だつて、元は真人間だ。治道居士様の懇篤な説示によつて改心するにきまつてる。此悪人の俺でさへも、将軍様のお姿を拝んだ丈けで、感涙に咽び、最はや悪魔は逃げ去つたのだもの、もし将軍様、何卒エムの云ふた事を信用遊ばさず、私が案内しますから、何卒岩窟へお越し下さいませ。さア、ヤク、エール、貴様は将軍様のお後からお伴し、随分抜かりなく用心して上るのだぞ』

と小声にて囁く。ヤク、エールは黙然としてニタリと笑ふ。

治道『アハヽヽヽ、随分人生と云ふものは面白いものだな。仮令悪魔に謀られようとも命を取られようとも、天に任した吾が身魂、何の恐るる事があらうぞ。之でも昔は三軍を叱咤した勇将だ。オイ、タール、心遣ひは無用だ。サア早く案内して呉れ。

虎熊の山は如何程峻しとも 安く上らむ神のまにまに。 セール、ハール、醜の司を言向けて 神の大道に靡かせて見む。 仰ぎ見れば山の尾の上は黒雲に 包まれにけり晴らしてや見む』

タール『サア御案内致しませう。之から段々坂が峻しくなりますから、足許に気をつけてお上り下さいませ。然らば私が先導致しませう』

と胸に一物、心に二物、罪の重荷を背負ひつつ、喘ぎ喘ぎ上り行く。
