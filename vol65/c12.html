Chapter 12: 天 Blessing [1668]

天恵〔1668〕

§ 1

Chidaukoji, accompanied by the five men—Bat, Karks, Base, Yaku, and Ale—blows the conch shell and advances ever westward toward Jerusalem.

Yaku sings along the way:

Yaku: 'The Kami have manifested in the outer world, Dividing the good kami from the evil. A truly revered age has now arrived. We too, originally of Brahman stock, Served well the lord of the army, And wishing to fulfill Ōkuronushi's divine will, Morning and evening we toiled, Grooming horses and performing various Menial chores, crossing steep mountains and rivers, And when we came as far as the Forest of Floating Wood, Before we knew it, an immense army Of our side, whose battle fortunes were ill-omened, Crossed the River Lion, Were driven out once more from Mount Victor and the peaks of Inokura, And here, meeting the bitter fate Of disbandment, utterly bewildered, Without a face to show as we returned straight home, Following Captain Sēru, We fulfilled our duty as thieves in the mountain stronghold of Torakumayama, Ranging far and near, And plundering travelers; Yet when the lord of the army, Chidaukoji, None other than our former master Oniharusewake, Assumed the guise of a bikhu And came as far as the banks of Lake Harusē, Upon that occasion, he expounded the principle of heaven and earth, Making us return to the true path, And knowing full well it was an unpardonable deed, We cleverly deceived our boss Sēru, Disguised him as a prison guard, Oh, the sheer joy of bringing today's grand play to a close! Nevertheless, as for this path ahead, How on earth shall we pass the days? Having no house to live in and for food, Destitute and poor, we ourselves, Even though burning to follow goodness, Somehow seem ready to slip into evil; For the nature of weak-hearted mortals, There is no other way than to entrust ourselves To the Kami, endowed with absolute and infinite divine power. Karks, Bat, Ale, Base, What do you think of this? I am anxious about the future And my heart is scorched with worry. No matter what divine power we have received, Being unlearned from the outset, we cannot even become bikhus; There is simply no way That the sacred messengers of the Kami Could ever be successfully fulfilled by us. If this fellow does not devise some scheme And ponder our course of life in the world, There is a danger that our mouths will be deprived of food. That storehouses being full leads to knowing ritual and propriety Is a golden saying of the ancients. No matter how the true teachings Are poured upon us like a waterfall And heard like the rolling thunder, No matter how much our hearts are opened, If our bellies are empty, there is no means To sustain the thread of our lives, which is like a jewel. How shall we escape this suffering? Hey, hey, listen, revered bikhu, Regarding the destination of us five, Concerning our course of life in detail, I beseech you, please teach us! The watershed between good and evil, In an instant depends upon the rudder of the heart; We must not capsize the boat. Ah, Kanagara Kamagami, O Chidaukoji, whose divine grace is profound, Before our master, with reverence, Earnestly we pray to you.'

Chidaukoji answers and sings in response:

Chidaukoji: 'The birds that soar through the heavenly expanse, And the beasts that dwell in the mountain and field, Are embraced by the grace of the Kami; Even without provisions for today, They splendidly maintain their lives. Man is the child of the Kami, the shrine of the Kami; How could Heaven ever create The people of the nation to go unfed? If you conform to the true path, Loving Kami will guard the spirits and souls Of us all in peace, Bestowing eternal prosperity and joy, And the food of life. Moreover, man shall not live in this world By bread alone; Once you have absorbed The food of the spirit in abundance, If you then seek Food to protect the physical body, If the Imperial Kami recognize it as necessary, They will surely grant it. To devote oneself to the great true path of the Kami Yet suffer and struggle in poverty regarding food Is a sign that one has not yet entirely Fulfilled the will of the Imperial Kami. Those people who have returned To the sincere heart of spirit-first and body-second Shall without fail be blessed abundantly with clothing, food, shelter, and occupation. First of all, polish the soul, Purify the heart completely, And walk the great path of the Kami. The Kami are with you; How could they ever abandon you? The Kami abundantly nourish Even birds and beasts; How much more so, in the case of human beings, Could there be any reason to starve and die? Simply Kanagara Kamagami, Entrust your body to the teachings of the Kami, Break your heart and break your body, And with all your might serve your own occupation Morning and evening. This is the primary and eternal, Unfailing secret of navigating human life. Ah, Kanagara Kamagami, Obeying the teachings of the Kami, I respond to the echoes of your wandering soul. Advance, advance upon the path of the Kami; Strive, strive in the labor of man. Man is the child of the Kami, the shrine of the Kami; The Kami dwell together with you.'

The five men, feeling their hearts cleansed and their burdens lifted, bow deeply before Chidaukoji.

Without a word, Chidaukoji turns his steps once more toward the horizon.

The journey continues through the vast and untamed wilderness, guided solely by the profound light of the divine will.

§ 2

So chanting, Chidaukoji led the way and advanced. They arrived at the foot of a small mountain. Looking, fragrant strawberries dyed the earth crimson, ripening as if awaiting people.

Chidaukoji: 'Come, everyone, let us rest here for a moment. Behold this. You are worrying variously, but on the path of following Kami, there are such heavenly blessings as this. They are not strawberries made in a field; are not strawberries ripening in the midst of the untamed wilderness waiting for us? No matter how voraciously you were to eat them with the force of fierce tigers, with just this much, the provisions for a hundred or two hundred people are fully assured. Furthermore, one after another, new fruits are attached in this manner toward the roots. Come, concentrating our gratitude and prayers, shall we partake of the delicious taste of heavenly blessings?'

The company, in unison,

': 'Yes, thank you very much.'

and settling themselves here, they gave thanks to Kami, picked strawberries that seemed to have water dripping from their tips, ate and ate, and gradually satisfied their stomachs.

Chidaukoji: 'How thankful, the divine grace of Sumeōkami, Filling to the utmost fields and mountains alike. Unto the children of men, who thirst and hunger, And find it hard to advance, Has been granted the taste of life. Departing from the mountain of Torakuma, now here, The joy of tasting the grace of Kami.'

Yaku: 'Who would have thought that in a grassy field where even the surroundings are dark, Red strawberries would be ripening.'

Yeru: 'Thinking it might be snake strawberries and drawing near to take them in hand, They are all the more delicious, eat-Yeru strawberries.'

Batto: 'This dim mountain path as well, into crimson Strawberries, Batto, has grown red.'

Kākusu: 'The true Kami has bestowed deep grace Upon Kākusu of sincerity.'

Bēsu: 'We who were thirsty and scratched our Bēsu Are now refreshed by the grace of the divine Kami.'

Yaku: 'Chidaukoji-sama, one need not worry about the world, need they? We have not properly done a single meal since yesterday, came down mountain slopes, and hurried all the way here, but already my stomach is empty, my throat is dry, and not a single drop of water was left—it was a place where progress and retreat were completely deadlocked. At the very moment when, at ninety-nine percent, we were about to breathe our last, to give us such precious strawberries—it is thankfulness beyond all expression. Thinking upon this, the Kami-sama will certainly never let us die in vain, will they?'

Chidaukoji: 'That is so. Human beings are safe as long as they simply receive and partake of what the Kami-sama gives. Present-day human beings eat on top of having eaten, drink on top of having drunk, indulge in every luxury, and since they do nothing but grumble that this is sweet and that is unpalatable, the cries of economic hardship arise. Because of that, prices soar all the more and the people suffer all the more. If ten thousand people out of ten thousand were to gratefully feel the benevolence of Kami and receive what is given, there would be no poverty in the world, no illness, and no conflicts would arise either. Therefore, we must walk the path of Kannagara no michi no matter what. By this, you must understand how thankful the Kami-sama are... Hey, Batto, ever since you left the Brahmin army, you had even sunk so low as to become a thief, but in the meantime, was there anything you thought pleasant? If so, I would like you to let me hear your impressions at that time.'

Batto: 'Yes, when I became your revered teacher's subordinate and lived a soldier's life, it was exceedingly painful. I was kept down by my superior officers. Moreover, if I worked faithfully trying to slightly win the favor of my superiors, I was hated by my colleagues. Unprincipled flatterers were promoted and immediately became my own superiors, keeping me down. It was so full of grievances, grievances, and not for a single day was my heart at peace. And never knowing when a formidable enemy might attack, we had to fight with our lives as stakes, and day and night we spent our days in trembling fear.'

Chidaukoji: 'Indeed, what you say is true. Even though I led three thousand subordinates, above me was the Kami Daikokunushi, and there were colleagues, and subordinates, and with a formidable enemy waiting on one side, the agony when setting out on the military expedition. Though I spoke grandly and shouted bravely with my mouth, deep within my heart I sowed the seeds of considerable anguish and suffering. And in a single battle, allies met with a tragic end. Also, even if they are called enemies, when I saw them meeting with a tragic end, my heart ached. Ah, it was truly like wandering in the alleys of hell, asuras, and beasts. The joy of heart when I was helped by Kuniwakete-sama and became a Buddhist monk—it felt as though I were born into a vast universe for the first time.'

Batto: 'I used to envy Shiyōgunsama, thinking he had such a comfortable position up to now, but it seems you found it to be the same way too, didn't you? If that is so, there is not a single person in this world living in peace of mind, is there?'

Chidau: 'Didn't the ancients say... Though the kinds of sorrows vary in this world, there is no one who lives with an easy heart... This poem truly sings of the painful conditions of human life. And when you were a thief before, did you never feel anything pleasant?'

Batto: 'Yes, somehow it was even more painful than during my military life. Even if you call it painful, if you are a soldier you receive a regular allowance every month, but a thing like a thief seems like a shoddy business requiring no capital, yet from morning till night, trembling with fear, tormenting the mind and aching the chest, and quite easily no income is obtained. Even when trying to get a little money, it is at the risk of your life just like a battle. Besides that, there is the fear that torite might appear at any time, and not once did food or sake pass smoothly down my throat. The only times I felt it pleasant to have become a thief were when I met Anata-sama and listened to the talk of the Kami in the forest, and when I acted as a substitute for Anata and played a role inside the prison; I have never felt anything as pleasant as those times. However, even more pleasant than that is the joy of rushing fiercely into the path of truth and making a pilgrimage together with Anata to the holy place Jerusalem—the stomach empty and the throat dry, when I became one facing nine deaths and gaining one life, this present joy of receiving the strawberries bestowed by Kami is something I have not tasted ever since I was born. Ah, Kanagara Tamachihai-mase.'

Chidau: 'No matter what, if a person does not labor in the path of truth, they will not understand the blessings of the Kami, nor will they understand the preciousness of food and drink. Come, everyone, just as Batto said just now, you too must have felt the present blessing with joy, didn't you?'

The four men all said 'Hai' at once, and were lost in tears of gratitude. Suddenly a roaring sound was heard from behind. Looking closely, Torikumayama began a great eruption, more than half of the mountain was enveloped in black smoke, and a great fire broke out. Everyone gazed at this spectacle and gave thanks with tears for the infinite benevolence of the Kami.

四人は一度に『ハイ』と云つたきり、感謝の涙にくれて居る。忽ち轟然たる響が後方に聞えた。よくよく見れば虎熊山は大爆発を初め、山半分以上は黒煙に包まれ、大火災を起して居る。一同は此の光景を眺め、神の無限の仁慈を涙と共に感謝した。
