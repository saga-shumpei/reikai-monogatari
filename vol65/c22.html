Chapter 22: 均霑 [1678]

均霑〔1678〕

§ 1

At the sudden eruption of Torakumayama, needless to say Senseizan, the mountains, rivers, trees, and grasses of thiskiyōdo trembled fiercely, and save for Michihiko, all turned matsusao and trembled in fear. The terrifying nature of the yōgan raining down without enriyo even to this chiten separated by seven or eight leagues. Yet, despite this large house, not itsuko struck them, which all felt to be the goshiyugo of Kamisama, arousing feelings of kanshiya. Even the ruthless Tēra, Kinguresu, and the subordinate konusubito suddenly grew frightened, involuntarily clasped their riyōte, and began itsushiyōkenmei kiguwan. That voice for ichiji made the kodama of the valleys of the urayama resound.

Sumanāhime: 'Minasama, it was a terrifying thing indeed. A gigantic yōgan like that... fell like rain for ichiji, but thanks to the divine virtue, not a single one hit our house, and nor did the house collapse from that tremor, and that everyone has safely saved your lives is entirely the precious goshiyugo of Kamisama.'

So saying, she opened the window and looked out at the houses of the village. However, thick smoke filled the heavens, creating a true darkness where black and white could not be distinguished. Indeed, since early this morning it had been gloomily dark everywhere, and everyone had thought it nighttime, but in truth, it was still the height of day. Feeling relieved that no fires had broken out in the village houses and there were no voices of agony, she stroked her chest in relief,

Sumanā: 'Minasama, my house is a place where one can look down upon the whole village at once, but in the village quarter no fires broke out from that commotion, and no cries can be heard, so every single house must have received the divine virtue of Kamisama. Now, let us hold a festival of thanksgiving to Kamisama, and have everyone receive the naorai.'

Michi: 'Nay, that is splendid. That such a great eruption and yōgan falling like rain should not strike itsuko in this wide house, and that the whole village is safe, is truly wondrous. This too must be the divine virtue of Kamisama. Now, you members of the Youth Corps, sorry to trouble you, but I ask you to prepare for the festival.'

Tāku followed the words of Michhiko, led the Youth Corps, made preparations for various offerings, and set about preparing for the festival. At last the preparations for the festival were completed. Here Michihiko and Sumanāhime put on new ritual garments, reverently offered prayers before the divine presence, and the festival safely concluded. Thereafter, the old and young, men and women of the village gathered in this wide house, and the subordinates of Kinguresu also lined up in the sacred precinct to partake of the naorai.

Sumanāhime stands joyfully in the center of the banquet hall, herself singing and herself dancing.

Sumanāhime: 'This place is the Secret Land of famed renown, With Senseizan guarding to the north, And Torakuma's mountain towering to the east, While white, blue, yellow, and purple Flowers emit their fragrant scents, Butterflies dance and play on high, Kalavinka release their cooling voices, Chanting of the Age of Gods. Truly precious is the joy Of the common people of Senseisato, Peerless in other lands, Central Japan's foremost auspicious sign. Though once it grew wild and raged Taking advantage of the time of Utsu no Magatsuhi, The precious divine messenger, Lord Michihiko Tsukasa being present, Caused our house and this village first of all To cast away the disasters of the ugly fiends, And now how joyful that it has completely returned To the ancient Age of Gods! High upon the peak of Senseizan, Clad in robes of five-colored clouds, Raising clouds and causing rain, The winds of five days and the rain Of ten days without missing the time, The thick blessings that fall continuously Are the joy of the land where I dwell, Bearing the name of Senseisato. Exhort yourselves, exhort yourselves, villagers! Dance, dance, all people! The joy of today's divine life I shall not let go elsewhere for thousands of reigns, May it continue on, to the Great Presence Our sincere hearts as we pray God will surely look upon And purely grant and accept. Ah, Kannagara, gods of gods! If we tread the true path Of human life, truly joyful, And keep the teachings of the gods, In this world there is no fear of distortion. Since I made a sad parting From my precious parents, siblings, and dear husband, With my heart clouded and my breast aching, With the thought that body and world were no more, Wondering if I should leave this world once and for all, From within the narrow heart of a woman I resolved and stood at the mountain Of Sensei, at the Hall of White Bones; Even as I prostrated myself before its presence, Just at the time when my life was about to end, By the grace of Michihiko Tsukasa, Whose very name is auspicious, My fragile life was saved, And when I returned to my house and peered about, How terrifying it was that the demonic hands Were already spread inside and out! Taking advantage of the darkness, standing at the back door, I peered at how things were,'

Sumanahime: 'Teirasama, who called himself my itoko, and the hitobito, who called themselves torite, and Turkesama and Intāsan, were engaged in some agetsurahi, and imagining that my inochi had perished, their hiyakusenman considerations, melted by tears of kanshiya, along with Michihiko-tsukasa, pulling open the inner fusuma, stood forth in that very place, and when they softly proclaimed the kototama, the darkness of the hitobito's hearts cleared away completely, and returning to the pure and revered kamunagara, to the wondrous mitama, how boundless was that joy! To the extent that even words of gratitude failed, I wept and entered into joy. Ah, kamunagara, kamunagara! Everlastingly receiving the blessings of Kami, and accepting this sakamori of nahorahi, may you partake of it pleasantly, thus she proclaims. Whether Asahi shines or clouds, whether the moon falls and stars are lost, even if Torakumayama should split, the body of a man entrusted to Kami—how should it fear? Now, before my very eyes, receiving the blessings of Kami, oh, the joy of smiling and flourishing! Ah, kamunagara, kamunagara! Before the mimahe of Kami, reverently, with awe, I offer my kanshiya.'

Michihiko: 'To sweep away various sins and defilements, the mountain of Torakuma must have erupted. The magakami lurking on the peaks of Torakuma have now completely fled and vanished. Coming to live in the pure village of Sensei, I hear only unexpected things. The true heart of Sumanahime—how the Kami of heaven and earth must cherish her! In the true heart of the lords, Intā and Turk, may the house of Bātara flourish on!'

Turk: 'Who could have imagined that into this village where demon-gods rage, the emissaries of Kami would come? You are truly Michihiko-tsukasa, who straightens the pillars of a leaning house.'

Intā: 'The darkness of the heart, like the liver of a village, has cleared away, having swept away the clouds of Bātara's house. Today's sky, which had grown dark even at midday—is it the sound of the eruption to make it bright? The magatsuhi lurking in my breast has also fled away at the mighty echo of that eruption. Both shishiguma and tōrō trembled and have grown calm at the sound of the eruption.'

Michihiko: 'All things are entirely the august heart of Sumerakami; look up and revere them, children of Kami. My master, who set out from the mountain of the tutelary deity—how does his august body fare, I worry and fret. Yet my master is a kami-hito; he shall dwell in utmost peace and ease.'

While Sumanahime offered a cup of sake to Michihiko,

Sumanahime: 'If you please, Lord Propaganda Envoy, regarding how I should direct my future path in life, what would be best for me to do? I earnestly beg you to show me the way.'

Michihiko: 'I will not give you orders saying you must do this or that... but how would it be if you were to carry it out by the best method you feel in your own heart?'

Sumanahime: 'Yes, thank you very much. If that is so, since thanks to you I was saved from a place where I had no inochi, and furthermore so many people have thus returned to the true way, I have no joy that can surpass this. I need neither mountain forests, nor fields, nor treasures. In this house I wish to enshrine the Kami of Hinodekyū and the Kami of Ural, console the souls of my ancestors and my husband, become a bikuni, and spend the rest of my life; how would that be?'

Michihiko: 'Indeed, that is a truly admirable thought. Michihiko raises both hands and agrees.'

Sumanahime: 'Thank you for your prompt consent. Regarding this, my house is a wealthy family of generations past... here in this neighborhood, but now that I am to become a bikuni and serve Kami, I recognize no need for property or the like. I earnestly desire to dedicate the entire property of the Bātara family for the sake of society and the public; how would that be?'

Michihiko: 'That is exceedingly splendid. Doubtless the villagers will also rejoice.'

Sumanahime: 'How would it be if I divided the entire property into four parts, dedicated a part of it to the shrine in Jerusalem, applied a part to the maintenance expenses of the kamidono, and bestowed the remaining two parts upon the villagers?'

Michihiko: 'That is an exceedingly good thought. Please do so. However, Kinguresu and the dozen or so others who have reformed right here just now have only just abandoned thievery, and in a case where they have no fields to do farming, nor capital to do business, how would it be if you were to grant even a little mountain forest or fields to these people as well, and have them engage in agriculture?'

Sumanah: 'Yes, thank you very much indeed. If Kinguresu-sama and the rest of you will only consent, I should like you to stay in this village and engage in honest work.'

Michihiko: 'Kinguresu-sama and everyone else, Sumanah-sama now says she wishes to distribute a fair share of property to you; what do you think of this? Thus saying, he joined his hands toward the heavens and was lost in tears of joy.

Michihiko: 'Though there are no hideous evils in the world, Do they not simply spring forth from the heart? If within the body there be no crookedness, what account Are even lions, bears, wolves, or tigers to us? Sumanako: 'At the critical time when the thread of my life was endangered, Was there not such a precious voice saying, "Wait"? Taku: 'From today, even the village of Sensei will become an ancient Garden where flowers bloom and fragrance fills the air. Through the blessings of the divine official Michihiko, We villagers are now being revived. I will serve well the nun-lord Sumanah, And guard the Way morning and evening.'

Intā: 'Even I, wet by the rain of the nun-lord's true heart, Wept in a stealthy voice while enduring. Teardrops of joy are filling and overflowing my breast, So that my body feels as if it were floating and rising with courage.'

Kinguresu: 'Even upon me, who have exhausted every possible kind of crooked deed, has God bestowed His blessing. If I had plotted evil in the mountains of tigers and bears, And were I still doing so now, I surely would have perished. Taylor came crawling haltingly and painfully to this place, lowering himself like a dog upon the earth of the garden,

Taylor: 'I, who have exhausted all the ugliness of the crooked gods, Will now repent from this time forth. All you hundred people, pray pardon my crimes; I will become a servant of the village and serve you.'

Thus the inheritance of the Bhadra family was distributed according to Sumanah's will, and without distinction of high and low, rich and poor, the villagers came to enjoy their work together, live in harmony with their neighbors, and pass their days in an atmosphere of gentle peace. Because Michihiko's journey of propagation was pressing, he could not afford to stay long; after lingering for two or three days to impart the teachings of God to the villagers, and entrusting Taku as the caretaker of the mansion, Sumanah desired to make a pilgrimage to Jerusalem, and as she would not be separated from Michihiko, she ended up following secretly behind him as he pressed forward.

ターク『仙聖の郷も今日より古の 花咲き匂ふ園となるらむ。 三千彦の神の司の御恵みに 吾里人は甦りつつ。 スマナーの比丘尼の君によく仕へ 朝な夕なに道を守らむ』

インター『吾とても比丘尼の君の真心の 雨にぬれつつ忍び音に泣きぬ。 嬉しさの涙は胸に充ち溢れ 身も浮く許り勇み立つかな』

キングレス『枉事のあらむ限りを尽したる 吾にも神の恵み賜ひぬ。 虎熊の山に悪事を企らみつ 今もありせば亡びしならむ。 此郷に現はれ来り爆発の なやみ逃れし事の嬉しき』

テーラはノソリノソリと足を痛めて此場に這ひ来り、庭の土間に犬突這となつて、

テーラ『枉神の醜の限りを尽したる 吾今よりは悔い改めなむ。 百人よ吾罪科を赦せかし 村の僕となりて仕へむ』

愈ここにバータラ家の遺産は、スマナーの意志に従ひそれぞれ分配されて、上下貧富の区別なく、郷民は互に業を楽しみ近隣相和し、和気靄々として世を送る事となつた。三千彦は宣伝の旅が急くので、永く留まる訳にも行かず、二三日逗留して里人に神の教を伝へ、タークを館の留守居と頼み置き、スマナーはエルサレムへ参拝せむと、三千彦の許しなければ、見え隠れに後を慕ふて進み行く事となつた。
