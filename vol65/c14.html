Chapter 14: Mountain River 動 Turmoil [1670]

山川動乱〔1670〕

§ 1

Debisuhime: 'The Kamutsukasa of Ananai-kyō, Michihiko of Izuno-oshihe, having become the husband of Debisuhime, by the shore of Lake Sudaruma, receiving the admonition of Hatsuwakahime, and here parting from Michihiko with sleeves separated, crossing mountain rivers, traversing wild paths, threading the valley ways wrapped in deep mist, at length reaching the dense forest at the foot of Mount Torakuma, caught by the subordinates of a thief who built his nest in a cavern, bound and thrown into the prison of the cavern, Sēru's crooked lust clinging morning and evening, like the wind of Shinadobe blowing away that annoyance, exhausting the utmost bounds of Izukototama to drive away the crookedness, keeping her pure chastity; realizing that Burawāda-hime no Mikoto also has ended up being a captive of the demon in the next room and is suffering, through mind-to-mind transmission communicating with each other with a song, while praying to Kami and waiting for the saving Kami to appear again Chidaukoji enters, hearing that he is inside the prison her heart leaps with courage, using the pure-hearted Yaku and Ēru as secret messengers, arranging the strategy and plan, making three substitutes, at last escaping from the cavern, measuring the time thinking to make Sēru reform, at the time when the inside of the cavern roars and the propaganda song echoes forth, carrying the luminous jewel, knowing that Itahiko of Chino-oshihe is the rescue corps, just as a horned dragon suddenly gains its time and ascends to the heavens, cooperating with each other in strength, directing and subduing Sēru and the others, setting fire to the ugly cavern, and again here bidding a fond farewell to Chidaukoji, Itahiko-kamutsukasa, and Burawāda while keeping the Oshihe of Kami, walking forward along the extremely lonely mountain path, I am a woman all alone, advancing with Kami as my strength; ah, Osubarashiki Kamunagara, Kami is with us. Heaven and earth are entirely the embrace protected by Sumeru-Ōkami; if we are fulfilled in the true way, even if a hundred thousand million ferocious wolves or Magatsuhi, or ugly giant serpents appear and obstruct our path, what is there to fear? As children of Kami possessing the Yamato spirit of Shikishima, whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wane, even if the great earth sinks, the power of truth saves the world; truth is the treasure of this world, the luminous jewel, the wish-granting jewel, the golden jewel—however wondrous they may be, is there any treasure to compare with the light of Naonohi no Mitama? Naonohi who created this world, Ootonohi of vast heart, taking the Mitama of Naonohi as the warp and the Mitama of Izu as the weft, transmitting the Oshihe of Chino of the Great Mitama of Mizuho no Mitama—we are pure propaganda messengers. Since I am the wife of Michihiko-kamutsukasa, how could I stray from the way? There is no blade that opposes righteousness, drawing and holding aloft the sword of benevolence and compassion, girding the shield of faith upon one's body, striking out living Kototama, directing and subduing the approaching crookedness, we shall advance to the noble capital where Kuninotokotachi no Ōkami is eternally tranquil; ah, Osubarashiki Kamunagara, protect me on my lonely journey, bestow grace upon my soul, I earnestly beseech you.'

Thus she chanted, and just as she advanced along the skirts of a certain mountain, together with a roaring sound of great volume, Mount Torakuma in her rear suddenly erupted, raining down lava and injuring the trees and birds and beasts of the mountain and plain. Debisuhime, unable to help herself at this sound of explosion, stopped and looked back, and the full heavens were as though ink had been spilled, and birds flying through the sky fell to the ground flapping heavily. Debisuhime with all her might chanted the divine words and began to pray that the rain of lava would cease as early as possible. From there the sorrowful voice of someone was heard. Looking closely, in the thicket nearby, a wild fox with about half its tail white, its hindquarters struck by a fragment of lava, was writhing and suffering. Debisuhime, upon seeing it, ran up before the wild fox, and with tears welling in both eyes, chanted the Tianjin Shinto prayers, and kindly dug up the earth all around, applied saliva, gouged out the rock fragment from its hindquarters, filled in the wound, and tended to it. The wild fox repeatedly bowed its head and faintly wagged its tail, expressing its gratitude. After a while the wild fox abruptly got up, and making its legs stagger, looking back and back again, hid itself in the cogon grass taller than its own stature.

斯く謡ひ、とある山の裾を進む折しも、轟然たる大音響と共に、背後にあたる虎熊山は俄に爆発し、熔岩を降らし、山野の樹木、禽獣を傷つけた。デビス姫は此爆音に思はず知らず立ち止まり、後ふり返り眺むれば、満天墨を流したる如く、空翔つ鳥はバタリバタリと地上に落ちて来た。デビス姫は一生懸命に神言を奏上し、一時も早く熔岩の雨の止まらむ事を祈願し始めた。そこへ何者かの悲しげな声が聞えて来た。よく見れば傍の叢に、尾の半分許り白い野狐が、熔岩の断片に臀部を打たれて、もだえ苦しんで居る。デビス姫は見るより野狐の前に走り寄り、両眼に涙を浮べながら、天津祝詞を奏上し、且つ親切に四辺の土を掘起し、唾をつけ、臀部の岩片をえぐり出し、傷痕を埋て、労はつた。野狐は頻りに頭をさげ、尾を幽かにふつて感謝の意を表はしてゐる。暫くすると野狐はむつくと起き上り、足をチガチガさせ乍ら、後ふりかへり　ふりかへり、身の丈にも余る茅草の中に隠れて行く。

§ 2

Debisuhime: 'The mountain of Torakuma has suddenly burst asunder, And bitter indeed is the plight even of the Nogitsune's bodies. Both grass and trees, birds and beasts alike, Tremble in terror at the fierce roaring of Torakuma. More terrifying than the lion, more terrifying than the tiger-bear, At that great detonation heaven and earth were shaken. As taught by the Sumekami, this must be the sign Of the Utsushiyo undergoing its great renewal. Fields and mountains alike, covered in molten rock and Kuwazanbahi, Tremble and quake; truly lonely is kefu. Though all men boast themselves to be the living palaces of the Sumeōkami, Now they have no color left in their faces. Amidst the raging fury of Torakuma's mountain, the tigers, bears, and lions Of the stormy fields also tremble and quake. Do not let the precious office of Michihiko meet with calamity In this time of hardship. How we ponder upon the person of Tamakuniwake Oshihetsukasa In the midst of this bakuhatsu! Since the life of Burawādahime is yet young, Pray, soothe her small breast. Should Chidaukoji hear this bakuhatsu, He would surely take courage, blowing his conch horn. Even though the sound of the conch horn were heard loud and clear, It could scarcely reach the mountain of Torakuma. How I yearn for the voice of the smart dog Attended by Hatsuwakahime as it bays in fierceness! Hearing this bakuhatsu of Torakuma, Lord Smart would surely be stirred to courage. Fields and mountains alike are enveloped in a strange mist, Which must be the breath arising from the mountain of Torakuma. More terrifying than the eruption of ancient Mount Etna Is this mountain of Torakuma. How it recalls to mind the hundred hardships Encountered by the life of Kototamawake no Kami! Mount Etna begins to quake, and upon the face of the earth Great waters overflow and winds blow fiercely. Even the voices of the people crying out and weeping for salvation Can no longer be heard amidst this bakuhatsu. When I gaze upon the august sky of the eastern mountains, He who is the sun sinks, and the moon shines brightly behind. The high mountain where the thieves have barricaded themselves Must have been shattered because the Kami is wrathful. The cavern where the living blood of Torakuma is squeezed Is purified, receiving the baptism of fire. Not only does it erupt, but a mountain tide of mud and water Surges down from the ridge of the mountain in a terrifying manner. Spouting forth countless amounts of lava in all directions, It seeks to destroy the lives of men. After the bakuhatsu, flattening the mountainous terrain, We shall build majestic and sturdy the palace of the Kami. Both sun and moon shall all be gathered into the august palace, And they shall illuminate the darkness of Tokoyo.'
