Chapter 15: 饅頭塚 [1671]

饅頭塚〔1671〕

§ 1

Bravāda: 'Following after my dear lord and husband, Parted in life from mother and father, Crossing over Sūraya Kosui, Arriving safely at the port of Eru, Admonished by the shinjin of Hatsuwakahime, Alone by myself, Parted in life from my beloved husband, Walking on unfamiliar skyways of travel, My feet being bitten by waraji, Day and night crossing over mountains, Crossing across ikuta of valleys, Untill reaching the shores of Lake Harusei, At the very moment of arriving, Captured effortlessly by a crowd of nusubito, Led away to the wicked gankutsu of Torakuma, Whose name is terrifying, Imprisoned inside the hitoya Of the gankutsu dark even in daytime, At the time of lamenting my own fūn, Realizing that Debisu Hime is imprisoned In the neighboring hitoya, Thinking how pitiable it is, Yet somehow my spirit also emboldened, As if meeting the Buddha in jigoku, My heart suddenly gleamed, To such a place Chidaukoji, Itahiko, unforgettable even in mubi, My dear lord and husband appeared, Subjugating every single one of my ugly mitama Unto the living ikukototama, Saving the lives of us futari, Without even casting a single word Of affectionate kotoba, Advancing us once again Onto the divine Ōmichi, Oh, the boldness of it! Once more I am all alone, To convey the divine oshi Beyond the clouds of Shirakumo's land, An extensive ōnohara where ennetsu is fierce, Bitten by horseflies, threatened by hornets, Coming at last to the mountain edges of Seru, Suddenly all around became gloomy, Wrapped in an unpleasant atmosphere, Praying with a single mind That this must be no ordinary matter, Chanting the sacred name of Kami At the very moment of advancing Along the mountain path fragrant with a thousand flowers, The great earth suddenly trembled, The roaring sound heard afterwards, Is it a lion-wolf or a Torakuma? Or perhaps the invasion of a giant serpent? Resembling millions of thunderclaps Falling all at once, Looking back at that sound, Raising my eyes to gaze at the sky, Who could ever have expected that until yesterday Captured by the ugly Magatsuhi, That mountain of clouds deeply impressive With the memory of suffering, As the explosion of Mount Torakuma, The terror of the moment I realized it, My hair standing on end, my legs growing weak, With the womanly weakness unable to advance, What could I do but shed tears, Nothing more than unable to stop them? At last my heart grew calm, My chest palpitations also subsided, And my feet at last becoming vigorous, Oh, the joy of my own body! If Kami did not exist in this world, In this terrifying roar, My life of the pure jewel string Would have vanished and disappeared, The benevolent grace of revered Kami Is something to look up to with awe, Ah, Kamnagara Kami Kami, May your mitama be prosperous and blessed! Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, Whether the moon fills or wanes, Even if separated from my husband, The true oshi of Kami Shall never be parted from even for thousands of ages, Nevertheless, Ōkamigami, Safely protecting the future course of my dear lord and husband, High in the sky of the capital of Haruna, With the face of the moon and the color of flowers, Grant us a safe reunion as husband and wife, Ōoomigami, I earnestly pray to you alone, Ah, Kamnagara Kami Kami, How revered is the oshi of Kami! How wondrous is the grace of Kami!'

To such a place appeared a wild fox with its feet staggering, catching hold of the hem of Bravāda's robe and attempting to pull her by force into the grassy field. Bravāda is startled and tries to escape, but the wild fox tenaciously bites her hem and no matter how much she struggles will not let go, so Bravāda, having no other choice and led along by the wild fox, follows into the grassy field that swallows up her stature. The wild fox dragged her into a considerably large hole near a mound shaped like a steamed bun situated within the wilderness.

After a while, a great roaring sound as if even the mountains were crumbling was heard. This was Manasuinnersarashiya, one of the Eight Great Dragon Kings who, having lurked since ancient times as the master of the mountain due to the explosion of Mount Torakuma, was continuously sending evil ki into the world. This Dragon King exceeds five million jo in body length, with a thickness appropriate to that, and is a terrifying dragon god of three million jo.

It is said that his physical body is gigantic, ascending above the nine heavens, or circling Mount Sumeru seven times, and he is a terrifying wicked dragon capable of stopping the waters of the great ocean, making mountains well up within the sea, or causing islands to submerge. Even now, a part of the Dragon King's body is passing right over the hole of the wild fox where Bravāda is hiding.

Thinking that suddenly the inside of the hole became pitch dark, a foul-smelling breath impossible to describe attacked her nose. The wild fox was crouching at the mouth of the hole, protecting the hime's safety. Thinking that suddenly the inside of the hole brightened again, the wild fox suddenly bit Bravāda's hem once more, attempting to pull her outside.

When Bravāda, as she is being pulled along, comes outside to look, the plants and trees on all sides have all fallen over, and a wide ditch of grass has been carved out to the extent that the eye cannot reach. Bravāda turns toward the wild fox and expresses her gratitude,

Buravu: 'Thy life that saved my life, When shall I ever forget thy grace? Hadst thou not been, I would have miserably perished, O parent of my life, the living god of the fox.'

As it sang thus, the fox wagged its tail, shook its head, showed a look of joy, and once again escorted her back to the original mountain path.

Burāvūdahime: 'Now farewell, I must part from my beloved lord, Rest peacefully through a thousand and eight thousand generations.'

The fox concealed its form once more in the grassy field. Burāvūda looked back over her shoulder, gazed at the smoke of Torakumayama soaring up to the heavens,

Buravu: 'When I look up, high in the sky of Torakumayama, How it is covered, the foul black cloud. In a world where even the heavenly sun is hidden and unseen, The foul evil spirit alone runs riot. The crookedness that wrings the lifeblood of the people of the world Must dwell in Torakumayama. Having incurred the wrath of the imperial deities, the high mountain Has erupted, how terrifying indeed. Gathering the resentment of the people, the volcanic crater Spits forth smoke that covers the sky. West, east, south, and north alike in black clouds Have been enveloped, today's great sky. If there were not the Mizu no Mitama of Izunitama, How could we clear away these black clouds? A flood overflows upon the land of rough iron, And black clouds block up the sky. Blowing away the black clouds, the majestic Ōkami Shows a crimson light. Flowing and purifying the muddy torrent, the auspicious Ōkami Restores the divine country to its antiquity. Through the grace of fire and water, even upon the surface of the earth It will likely open up in utter peace. The sun is hidden and the moon trembles at the mountain edge, A miserable reign where not even the stars can be seen. The great sky and the depths of the valley alike, all over Clouds and fog block the present world. If there were not the Izu no Mitama and the Mizu no Mitama, How could we solidify a shattered world? Though I, Burāvūda, am a frail body, I shall follow the Mizu no Mitama. Various trees growing on the high mountain, by the rough wind Tossed about, none become pillars. Deep in the foot-dragging deep mountains, at the bottom of the valley The giant trees that grow are the pillars of the world. The world created by the imperial deities Has none to rely upon other than the gods. Well, well, even if a person climbs to the heights, Sometime they will fall, like a monkey climbing a tree. The sound of water falling from a thousand-chō waterfall Just rumbles and echoes at the bottom. Even flying birds, as Torakumayama quakes, Fell to the earth like arrowheads.'

Singing thus, Princess Burāvūda once again softly chanted a propaganda song while, using her staff for strength, trudging step by step, parting the path overgrown with weeds, and proceeded ever westward.

Beyond the haze, on the other side of the clouds, the joy of seeing the golden palace of the imperial deities.
