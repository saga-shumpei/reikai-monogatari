Chapter 1: 感謝組 [1657]

感謝組〔1657〕

§ 1

Invaded by the strong light of the summer sun, the shadows of the boat fallen upon the water form into multi-layered stripes according to the swells of the moving water, swirling in dark green; whenever a pole is thrust in, circles are drawn, expanding larger and larger toward the shore. The color of the boat, weathered by wind and rain like that of ancient pottery, contacts the water in a sunken tone; reflecting upon the dark water the color of the setting sun tinged with a strong crimson that grazed the top of the cargo, and the light illuminating the half-surface of the slightly bent sail, it weaves an unseasonal pattern of colors.

By the shore of Lake Harusei, two men are indulging in idle talk while gazing at the water surface.

A: 'No matter what one may call the most difficult thing in the world, there is nothing so difficult as the path of conducting oneself in society. Though the Jitsugetsuseishin hang in the Ōzora and clouds wander to and fro in the Chiukū, a fellow called a Ningen cannot live day after day without eating and drinking. No matter how much Gokoku is piled in the storehouse of a rice merchant, if one has no Ōgon, one cannot buy even a Hitotsubu of Gokoku; and no matter how much sake there may be, without Ōgon, it cannot be had at will. Whether it be doing Nora work with cowering bodies while shivering in the mountain storm blowing down from Mount Harusei in the morning, or returning home carrying the stars on one's head at night, all is because one wishes to lead each day's life or to store up Ōgon. Because it is the present world where nothing can be helped without Ōgon, we became Trance out of sheer necessity. Feckless fellows hang themselves to perform a swinging departure, but we are full-fledged men. There is no way we could do such a cowardly thing. Our serving the Bara-mon army until now was, in short, to preserve the safety of our livelihood. Now that today has come, General Kiharuwake has ended up in that condition, so unless we, his followers, become Trance, we cannot manage today's livelihood.'

B: 'Indeed, your argument is most reasonable. Young men whose spirits pierce the sky, saying, "I will become an officer in either the army or the navy," "I will become a government official of such and such," or "I will undertake such and such a great enterprise," may speak splendidly on one side about thinking of the nation or loving the people, but on the other half of their minds, there is nothing other than the desire to take a large monthly salary and live a decent life. Generally on the surface, they speak of truly splendid things in name alone—such as honor, advancement, success, or social service—but in the final analysis, they still want to make money. Without Ōgon, in today's world, no matter how much of a saint, a scholar, or a old man from the countryside one may be, one still cannot be said to have gained honor or achieved success in life. A fellow without money is always driven away from society with mere remarks like, "That fellow is as feckless as ever; he is suffering, isn't he?" Even a single loincloth of a wife, if brought to the Rokuichi Bank, becomes money even if it is a bit dirty, but one cannot drive away bill collectors with beards or honor. No matter how much one boasts, "I am a bachelor of arts, an official of chokunin rank, a noble, or a policeman who controls society," if one lacks the essential money, when the critical moment comes, one cannot utter even a single groan. One would still have to bow one's head to the rice merchant, or rub miso for the miso merchant and walk back and forth a thousand times. Thinking of that, it is all about money. However, no matter how much one wants money, one must think carefully before going so far as to become Trance.'

A: 'What, modern humans operate on the principle of survival of the fittest, so there is not a single person who does not Trance. Without money, whether an official of chokunin rank or sonin rank, one is utterly useless. How much more so for hannin employees and petty officials. Even if they boast while discussing national politics at the court during the day, upon hearing the sound of the rice tub crying in the evening, what patriot or hero could fail to shed tears of broken heart? Therefore, in today's world, though I will not say all, there is likely no one who does not practice Trance, whether on a large or small scale. Even if one speaks of the moon, snow, flowers, romantic love, or physical pleasures, when it comes to not having even a single piece of sweet potato for tomorrow's food, will not even a three-year love cool down completely? Even if one wears brocade robes, glowers at the world with an imposing beard, and talks big with foam flying from the corners of one's mouth, when one has nothing in one's pocket, even a beggar woman will not pay attention to him. The more one boasts, the more painful one's stomach becomes; the more brocade one wears, the more painful one's chest becomes. In a world where even a three-year-old child of a wealthy family is revered through the power of money, and even Amida-sama gains value from gold leaf, no matter how much of a noble or a head priest one may be, one cannot raise one's head before a creditor, while even a shoemaker's son is welcomed as a fine young master as long as he has money. Even if by unreasonable means, as long as one has money, evil passes as good, so I am determined not to bend my original intention. When entering a brothel, even if welcomed with stylish words like "Welcome, please come in" or charming phrases like "Please step inside," once it comes to the bill and one does not have a single penny, no matter how beautiful a lady may be, she will instantly wrinkle her forehead and raise her eyes, and even a gentle, flattering clerk cannot help but lodge a complaint, can he? That face which was previously beautiful and gentle instantly changes drastically into a demon or King Enma, and the Kototama of "Welcome, please step inside" instantly turns into violent words and commands of "Get out, you coward," and in the end, one's hand, which was pulled in welcome, is heartlessly pushed out, and saliva is spat from the mouth that welcomed him.'

Otsu: 'Naruhodo, that is truly so. Without money, one cannot conduct Seikuwatsu in this present world. For Ningen, who can by no means abandon Ishiyokujiu, and as long as they are Dōbutsu who cannot live without eating, there is no Shakata but to be dominated by Kinsen. By all means, through Zenriyō methods, we must make a Sandan to store Kogane, buy rice, and sustain our Inochi.'

'"Come then, let us go to Hanami at Mount Harusei"'

'This too is a matter only possible after Ishiyoku is sufficient, a Sumai is secured, and Inochi exists. Therefore, making a Sandan to satisfy this Ishiyoku, establish a Sumai, and sustain Inochi is Kanjin, but this is exceedingly difficult; thus Eijiyoku and Rigai, Kuwafuku and Tokushitsu, Rinjiyō and Gakumon, Kyōiku and Sensō, all spring forth from this Hitsueu. There is no morality apart from this fundamental principle, and without this principle, there is no society and no nation. Much less politics, economy, industry, education, ethics, science, or religion. All things in the universe, every one of them, become the materials and elements of our Seikuwatsu, and as long as this Seikuwatsu does not exist, there is no Hitsueu for religion, priests, or Shinto priests. Much less is there any Hitsueu for saints and superior men, or even heaven and earth itself. The rays of the sun, the movements of the stars, wind, rain, frost, and snow, rivers, ports, and seas, mountains and forests—were these not all provided for the sake of our Seikuwatsu? It must be Ningen themselves who carry out this Seikuwatsu and continue to sustain Inochi. Since what is called human life refers to the existence of a thousand variations of states within Seikuwatsu, let us, as much as possible, stop being mere tramps and develop the resources of wealth bestowed by Heaven.'

Kō: 'We have no fields to be farmers, no plows or hoes or other attached tools; we have no capital to do business, and yet we cannot become beggars, so we have been forced to choose the life of a tramp, living thick and short in this world. You should not be blowing such weak notes at this late hour. Rather than that, if we do not quickly do some kind of job and return, we will not be able to make an excuse to the boss.'

As they spoke thus, the sound of a conch shell, "buu-buu," echoed from far and near, sometimes high and sometimes low.

The names of the two men, Kō and Otsu, were Yaku and Ēru. Becoming somewhat frightened by the sound of the conch which possessed an indefinable authority, they hid themselves in a thicket of nearby shrubs. Chidō-koji appeared at this place together with the reformed group of Beru, Batto, Kākussu, and Bēsu; he sat down on the lawn near where the two were hiding and rested his breath. Then, through inspiration, Chidō-koji intuitively perceived that the two tramps were hiding in the shade of these dense trees, and that they were soldiers of the Brahman army who had now fallen to the status of tramps. Chidō-koji intentionally spoke in a loud voice so that the two could hear him.

Chidō: 'Hey, Beru, Batto, and you other two, how is it? Which do you feel more at peace in: your hearts when you were acting as tramps until yesterday, or your hearts right now?'

Beru: 'Yes, even without you asking, from morning until night we were in constant trepidation, jumping at the sound of a person's voice and trembling at the sound of a conch shell; but now that we have returned to our true hearts even though we have nothing, and are allowed to accompany you and have realized the love of the Lord God, the world that until now was dark and closed has expanded infinitely, and a Heavenly Kingdom and Pure Land has been constructed within our hearts. I have never encountered such a joyful and happy thing even in my days as a Brahman soldier.'

Chidō: 'That must be so. Even for me, rather than when I was a Lieutenant General of the Brahman army, commanding the three services and boasting of authority, living without any want, there is nothing so pleasant as falling to the status of a bhikkhu and wandering the wide heaven and earth with a single straw cape and a single sedge hat for the sake of God and the world. Whether one lives in this wide and wonderful world narrowly, darkly, and in fear, or lives joyfully and courageously, it all depends on the state of one's heart. Surely you will not reverse back to being a tramp again?'

Beru: 'Yes, indeed, how could I do such a foolish thing? After tasting such pleasure, how could I return to a cramped and terrifying trans? There is no greater height of foolishness in the world than a human who performs a trans. It feels as though this world is so narrow that one cannot even cough loudly. Chidau: 'That is true. In this brief world, there can be no one so foolish as to commit Akuji and sow the seeds of calamity for Miraieigofu. Batto, Kākusu, Bēsu, you three surely agree with Beru, do you not?'

Sannin: 'No, we are beyond Kanshiya. We are shedding tears of joy.'

Beru: 'All right, then I understand. Now then, Chidaukoji-sama, shall we proceed by riding in the Tekushī once more?'

From the thicket of shrubs behind, a man's cry burst forth with the force of a collapsing embankment.

Beru looked back,

Beru: 'Oh, what is this? It seems another weeping Kanshiyagumi is hiding here as well... Hey, whoever you are, why are you howling in a place like that? Is there anyone who cries while being born into such a wonderful heaven? You too are likely a foolish person who has strayed from the grace of Kami-sama. There is no need for Enriyo. I, Beru the Propagator, shall deliver a sermon to you. Now, come out quickly, come out!'

At this voice, both Yaku and Ēru tumbled forth and appeared at the feet of Chidaukoji.

Beru: 'Oh, are you not Yaku and Ēru? I know not what makes you so sad in a place like this, but even if your weeping face dries, of what use will it be? You Yakuza fellow, were you not formerly a soldier? Well, you are Ēru, are you not? Were you not formerly a soldier wearing a sword? Why do you howl so blubberingly?'

Yaku: 'Ah, are you Beru? To confess the truth, after the disbandment of the Brahman army, I spent all the severance money received from General-sama on sake and women. Left like a deserted boat on the beach with no island to cling to, I wandered around, and while the two of us were performing trans, my conscience tormented me so much that I could not work. While in distress, I heard that Captain Sēru, together with Second Lieutenant Hāru, had set up a cavern on Mount Torakuma and were acting as bandits. I went there to seek them out, was made a subordinate, and under the chief's orders, was spreading a net by the edge of this marsh to see if there was any good work to be done. But hearing the conversation between Chidaukoji-sama and you from behind the trees, my own body became terrified, and feeling ashamed of my heart up until now, I wept spontaneously. Please, have this Biku-sama save me too. Are you not General Oniharuwake?'

治道『お前等三人は泣いてゐるぢやないか。今の境遇が苦しいのか……イヤ辛いのか。但しは私の云ふ事が気に容らぬのか』

ベル『オイ、バット、なアんだ。ほえ面かわいて……余りバットせぬぢやないか。オイ、カークス、御主人さまの前だ。何もカークスには及ばぬ。遠慮は要らぬから、自分の思つてる事を、ハキハキと申上げるがよからう。……これベース、汝もうつむいてベースをかいてるよりも、チツと、バットしたら何うだい』

三人一度に涙声になつて、

ヤク『ア、お前はベルか。俺は実の事を白状すれば、バラモン軍の解散よりゼネラルさまから頂いた涙金は酒色の為に使ひ果たし、よるべ渚の捨小舟、とりつく島もないので、そこら中をぶらつきトランスを両人がやつてゐた所、思ふやうに、良心が咎めて仕事も出来ず、困つてゐる所へ、セール大尉がハール少尉と共に、虎熊山に岩窟をかまへ、山賊をやつてると聞いたので、そこへ尋ねて行つて、児分にして頂き、親分の命令で、今何か可い仕事はなからうかと、此沼のふちで網をはつてゐた所だが、治道居士様とお前との話を木蔭で聞いて、自分の身が恐ろしくなり、何だか今迄の心が恥かしくて、思はず知らず泣いたのだ。どうぞ私も此比丘様に助けて貰うてくれ。鬼春別将軍様ではないか』

Beru: 'Oh, that is right. If you only undergo Kaishin, He will surely forgive you. Until just Nisannichimahe, I too, though I had no Oyabun like you, organized a Trans-dan in Dokuritsu and committed Zuibun Akugiyaku and Mudau acts; but Ketsukiyoku, even just feeding Jibun was difficult. Since He is your former Goshiyujinsama, He will surely forgive you.'

Chidau: 'You are Yaku and Ēru, are you not? Forgive me. It was all my fault that you were led into Daraku. To perform Tsumihoro for having led you all into Daraku, I have become a Biku of Itsusa-Ichiritsu, tasting Kannan-Shinku and walking in atonement for sin. There is no question of whether I forgive you or not; if you only perform Kaishin for me, there is nothing more joyful than this. Yaku: 'Yes, I am Arigata-u gozaimasu. I humbly request your favor in every way.'

Ēru: 'Zeneraru-sama, I humbly request your favor. I will surely perform Kaishin...'

The two persons, wiping away their tears while saying, 'Yes, we have understood,' stood at the front and came to lead the party of Chidau-koji toward the rocky cavern of Torakumayama.
