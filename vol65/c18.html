Chapter 18: 白骨堂 [1674]

白骨堂〔1674〕

§ 1

Michihiko traversed the mountain fields and crossed the valleys, and at last set foot upon the slope of Mount Senseizan.

This is one of the so-called Jitsupōzan mentioned by Buddhists.

Even Michihiko, being an alpinist, was exhausted by the long journey, and standing and gazing at the summit of Mount Senseizan, he let out a sigh.

Michihiko: 'Ah, though I have crossed the mountain fields thus far and come here, it seems I mistook the path somewhere and ended up coming toward Mount Senseizan.

There are no houses anywhere; the only sounds are the voices of birds and beasts.

Truly, it is a lonely thing indeed.

Tamakuniwake-sama praised me, saying Michihiko is a man of strong legs, but if I must traverse such terrible mountain slopes without any fixed route, I have no choice now but to utter words of weakness.

My two compasses seem somehow to have grown stiff.

If there is a good place to shelter from the rain somewhere around here, I shall rest my breath, leave my fortune to Heaven, traverse the famous mountains of the Land of the Moon, look down from the summit, and determine the direction of Jerusalem before proceeding.

As for that, Princess Davis and Princess Brauwāda have the delicate feet of women, so they must surely be experiencing hardships; however, the courage Princess Brauwāda showed when she encountered thieves on Mount Harusei was truly admirable.

If she has that much courage, she will surely proceed safely.

Rather than worrying about that, I must now cherish my own body and go as far as I can.

If there is some good shade of a tree, I shall rest; and since there is still time before nightfall, let me climb up and have a look.'

So he murmured to himself, and climbed up the narrow, nominal path, winding now to the right and now to the left.

The sun gradually drew near to the summit of the high mountains, and a great shadow began to assail him.

By the side of the path stood a single temple of white bones.

Michihiko stopped short,

Michihiko: 'Well, how strange.

Given that a temple of white bones stands in such a place, human houses must surely stand upon the top of this mountain.

Shall I first borrow the eaves of this temple and spend this night here?'

Saying thus, he suddenly plucked up his courage, climbed the narrow stairs of natural stone, and approached the white bone temple.

When he looked, a certain woman was uttering a thin voice and praying about something.

Michihiko, feeling suspicious, held his breath, concealed his body in the shade of the dense trees of the white bone temple, and listened to the woman's prayer.

'Alas, alas, my life has grown white and desolate

○

Within the breast of the grieving heretic

I weep, sorrowfully receiving the oppressive power of cruelty

The painfulness of resignation and sacrifice.

○

Both I and he are decaying amidst the pale whiteness

Ah, the vanity of that disillusionment

○

Without love and without friends

Sorrowfully gasping, I shall forget love and forget friends

Upon the plain of life where I walk alone

Only a black darkness lies spread out

Father, oh mother, and brothers

For the sake of my brother who lost his body

For the sake of all things in the world that perish

Be strong in the heavenly sky that encompasses the great expanse

○

White life is lonely

Vainly lonely

Alas, alas, the departed person, alas.'

Having thus chanted sorrowfully to the end, she leisurely took out a short sword from her bosom, smiled a lonely smile, and while intently gazing upon the blade where her face seemed to be reflected,

'_Oh, I pray you, the ancestral deities who created us.

Forgive a hundred sins and defilements, and lead my spirit to Sukawāna (the Pure Land of Peace and Rest)._

Even sooner than she spoke, she was now about to thrust the single blade into her throat.

Michihiko sprang out, beside himself, and instantly struck her arm, knocking the dagger down.

The woman was startled, gazed intently at Michihiko's face, and her lips were trembling violently.

Michihiko: 'Hey there, young lady, you must not give way to rashness.

For what reason are you about to abandon this splendid world?

First of all, calm your mind.

I am one called Michihiko, a missionary of the Izunome religion.

Receiving the command of God, I was on my way to Jerusalem when I lost my path and came out as far as this place.

Seeing that a temple of white bones was dimly visible, I came thinking to borrow a lodging for the night, only to find you in your present state.

How could I look on at this in silence and not stop you?—such is the reason I have intervened.

No matter how painful things may be said to be, there is no need to die.

First of all, compose yourself, I pray.'

原文（近代文語）

三千彦は、山野を渉り谷を越え、漸くにして仙聖山の阪道に取りかかつた。これは仏者の云ふ所謂十宝山の一つである。さすがアルピニストの三千彦も、長途の旅に疲れ果て、仙聖山の頂を眺めて吐息をついて居る。

三千『あゝ漸く此処迄山野を渡り、やつて来たものの、何処かで道を取り違へ、仙聖山の方へ来て仕舞つたやうだ。どこにも家は無し、声するものは鳥の声と獣の声ばかりだ。実に淋しい事だわい。三千彦は健脚家だと、玉国別様に褒められたが、かう酷い山阪を当途もなしに跋渉しては、もはや弱音を吹かねばならなくなつて来た。二つのコンパスは何だか硬化しさうだ。どこか此処辺でよい雨宿りがあれば息を休め運を天に任して、月の国の名山を跋渉し山頂から見下ろし、エルサレムの方向を定めて往く事にせう。それについてもデビス姫、ブラワ゛ーダ姫は繊弱き女の足、定めし困難して居るだらう、併し乍らブラワ゛ーダ姫はハルセイ山で泥棒に出逢つた時の度胸、実に見上げたものだつた。あれだけの勇気があれば、屹度無事に往くであらう。夫れよりも今は自分の体を大切にして、往く所迄往かねばなるまい。どこかよい木蔭があれば休む事にして、まだ日の暮に間もあれば一つ登つて見よう』

と独りごちつつ、形ばかりの細い道を、右に左に折れ曲りつつ登りゆく。

日は漸く高山の頂にさしかかり、大きな影が襲ふて来る。道の傍に一つの白骨堂が立つて居る。三千彦はつと立ち留まり、

三千『ハテ不思議だ。こんな所に白骨堂が立つて居る以上は、此の山の上に人の家が立つて居るだらう。先づこの堂のひさしを借りて、今宵一夜を過ごさうかなア』

と言ひつつ俄に勇気を鼓して、細い天然石の階段を登り白骨堂に近づいた。見れば一人の女が細い声を出して何事か祈つて居る。三千彦は訝かりながら足音を忍ばせ、白骨堂の密樹の蔭に身を潜ませ、女の祈りを聞いて居た。

『憐れ憐れ吾命白く荒廃せり

○

愁へる異端者の胸に

虐の力を悲しく受けて泣く

忍従と犠牲の痛ましさ。

○

蒼白き中に吾も彼も朽ちて行く

其幻滅の果敢なさよ

○

恋もなく友もなし

悲しくあえぎて恋も忘れ友も忘れむ

一人行く生命の原に

唯横たはる黒き暗闇

父よ母よ　オーそして兄弟よ

身失せたまひし吾背の為に

世の中のすべて滅行くものの為に

大空包む天の空に健かなれ

○

白き生淋し

果敢なく淋し

あはれあはれ亡き人あはれ』

斯く悲しげに謡ひ終り、徐に懐中より懐剣を取り出し、淋しげにニヤリと笑ひ、顔の写るやうな刃口をつくづく打ち眺めながら、

『オー、願はくは吾等を作りたまひし皇神よ。百の罪汚れを許し給ひて、吾身魂をスカーワナ（安養浄土）へ導きたまへ』

と云ふより早く、今や一刀を吾喉に突立てむとする。三千彦は吾を忘れて飛び出し、矢庭に腕を叩いて短刀を打ち落した。女は驚いて三千彦の顔をつくづく眺め、唇をびりびり慄はせて居る。

三千『これこれお女中、短気を出しちやいけませぬ。何の為めに此結構な世の中を見捨てようとなさるのか、まづまづ気を落着けなさい。吾は三五教の宣伝使三千彦と申すもの、神の御命令を受けてエルサレムに参る途中道踏み迷ひ、此処迄出て来た所幽かに白骨堂が見えるので、一夜の宿をからむものと来て見れば貴女の今の有様、これが何うして黙言て見て居られようかとお止め申た次第で厶います。何程辛いと云ふても死ぬには及びますまい。先づ先づお静まりなさいませ』

§ 2

Woman: 'Yes, thank you very much. I live in this deep mountain, and I am a woman from a small village named Sumanā. Having lost my parents, brothers, and husband to death, I have nowhere to lean upon. Moreover, since the young men of the village say various things and try to make me break a young widow's chastity, I have thought of following in the footsteps of my parents, brothers, and husband to go to the Anrakusekai. Just as I was about to commit suicide in front of this Hakukotsudau where the ashes of my ancestors are enshrined, it happened. Having no more joy even if I remain in this world, I have resolved to follow after my husband and make a pilgrimage to Gokuraku rather than fall into the temptation of Akuma and commit a sin. Please do not stop me.'

Michihiko wiped away his tears and, with a clouded voice,

Michihiko: 'While your words are plausible at first glance, that you alone were left behind must also be the divine arrangement of Shinkai. To say that you will commit suicide is a sin among sins. If your life had ended because it could not be helped, you might be able to go to Tengoku, but those who throw away their life by their own selfishness cannot go to Tengoku. Because you will surely go to Jigoku, I ask you to rethink it.'

Sumanā: 'If I commit suicide, can I not go to Tengoku by any means? Oh, what a troubling matter it is.'

Michihiko: 'As I heard from you just now, you said that your parents, brothers, and husband preceded you in death, but how on earth did such a thing come to be? If you were to die by your own hand now, there would surely be nobody to hold memorial services for the repose of your parents, brothers, and husband. If so, it would conversely become unfilial toward your parents and unchaste toward your husband.'

Sumanā: 'Yes, thank you very much for speaking so kindly. Through your teaching, my bewilderment has also awakened. I place myself entirely in your good hands. My house is in this small village, but my husband was named Bādara and served as the head of the village. Our homestead is reasonably wide, and our property is also considerable, but about half a month ago, a villain named Tāru, a major thief making a stronghold in Mount Torakuma, led a dozen or so subordinates, sneaked in in the middle of the night, slaughtered everyone in the household, and went home after seizing the treasures. At that time, fortunately hiding inside a closet by covering myself with a futon, I survived. Thereafter, taking care of the villagers, I had the corpses of my parents, brothers, and husband cremated, enshrined their white bones in this hall, and performed a fitting memorial service. Somehow, after that, my heart became lonely, and furthermore, various young men became a nuisance, which made me feel like dying.'

Sumanā's back was gently stroked three or four times by Michihiko while shedding tears, and with a soft voice,

Michihiko: 'Sumanā-sama, the more I hear, the more my sympathy knows no bounds. However, now that things have come to this, it is something that cannot be recovered even by regretting it. How about regaining your composure from now on and serving the Kami?'

Sumanā: 'Yes, thank you very much. However, though my village is a small settlement of fifty or sixty households, we have believed in the Ūraru teaching from generation to generation, so it will ultimately be impossible to suddenly enter your way. Despite your kind words, no matter how much I may believe, it will not do unless the three hundred villagers consent, you see.'

Sumanā: 'If that is the case, I leave everything to you. Will you not please come to my lonely, broken house once?'

Michihiko: 'That is a fortune I could not even ask for. Having gone astray in an unfamiliar mountain path with no house to lodge in, and feeling exhausted and troubled, I am indeed thick-skinned, but let me stay the night this evening.'

Sumanā: 'Thank you for your prompt consent. If so, I shall guide you.'

Descending the stairs of the Hakukotsudau, they once again went down the slope for four or five chō, turned to the right, followed the mountain path where trees grew thickly, and advanced deeper and deeper into the inner recesses.

Even though it was summer, because it was walking along the path by the bank of a valley stream covered by trees, they felt a coldness sufficient to make their bodies tremble.

原文（近代文語）

女『ハイ、有難う厶います。妾は此山奥に住まひして居りまする、小さき村の女でスマナーと申します。親兄弟夫には死に別れ、頼る所もなく、又村人の若い男等が種々様々の事を云つて、若後家の貞操を破らせうと致しますから、一層の事親兄弟、夫の後を追ふて安楽世界へ参らうと存じ、祖先の遺骨の納めてあるこの白骨堂の前で、自刃せむと致した所で厶います。もはや此世に在つても何の楽しみもなき妾、悪魔の誘惑にかかつて罪を作らうより、夫の後を慕ふて極楽参りをせうと覚悟を定めました。どうぞお止め下さいますな』

三千彦は涙を払ひ声を曇らせて、

三千『貴女のお言葉も一応尤もながら、貴女が一人残されたのも神界の御都合でせう。貴女が自殺すると云ふ事は罪悪中の罪悪ですよ。止むを得ずして命が終つたなら天国に往けませうが、吾身勝手に命を捨てたものは天国へは往けませぬ。屹度地獄に往きますから、お考へ直しを願ひます』

スマナー『自殺を致しましたら、どうしても天国へは行けませぬか、はて困つた事で厶いますなア』

三千『貴女は今承はれば、親兄弟、夫に先立たれたと仰有いましたが、それや又どうして左様な事になられたのですか。貴女が今自害して果てたなら、親兄弟、夫の菩提を弔ふものは誰も厶いますまい。さすれば却て親に対し不孝となり、夫に対して不貞となるでせう』

スマナー『ハイ、御親切によく言つて下さいました。貴方の御教訓によつて妾の迷ひも醒めました。何分宜しくお願ひ申します。妾の家は此小村では厶いますが、夫はバーダラと申し、村のたばねをして居りましたもので、家屋敷も可なりに広く、財産も相応に厶いますが、半月程以前に、虎熊山に山砦を作つて居る大泥棒の乾児タールと云う奴が、十数人の手下を引きつれ夜中に忍び込み、家内中を鏖殺に致し、宝を奪つて帰りました。其時妾は、押入の中に布団を被つて都合よく匿れましたので、生き残つたので厶います。其後は村人の世話になつて親兄弟の死骸を荼毘に附し、此堂に白骨を納めて、相当のとひ弔ひを致しましたが、何となく其後は心淋しくなり、又いろいろの若い男が煩さくて、死ぬ気になつたので厶います』

三千彦は涙を流し乍ら、スマナーの背を三つ四つ撫でさすり声も柔しく、

三千『スマナー様、承はれば承はる程同情に堪へませぬ。併し乍ら斯うなつた上は最早悔んでも帰らぬ事、これから一つ気を取り直し、神様にお仕へになつたらどうですか』

スマナー『ハイ有難う厶います。併し乍ら妾の村は五六十軒の小在所で厶いますが、先祖代々からウラル教を信じて居りますので、俄に貴方のお道に入る事は到底出来ますまい。折角のお言葉で厶いますが、何程妾が信じましても、三百人の村人が承知せなければ駄目で厶いますからなア』

三千『決して決して左様な事に御心配は要りませぬ。何れの教も誠に二つはありませぬ。又神様は元は一柱ですから、ウラル教でも宜しい。貴女が今死ぬる命を永らへて比丘尼となり、祖先を弔ひ、又村人を慰め、この山間に小天国をお造りになればよろしいでは厶いませぬか』

スマナー『左様ならば、何事も貴方にお任せ致します。どうぞ一度妾の淋しき破屋にお越し下さいますまいか』

三千『それは願うてもない仕合せで厶います。知らぬ山道に往き暮れて、宿るべき家もなし、体は疲れ、困つて居つた所で厶いますから、厚面しうは厶いますが、今晩は宿めて頂きませう』

スマナー『早速の御承知有難う厶います。左様ならば妾が御案内を致しませう』

と白骨堂の階段を下り、再び阪道を四五町下り、右に折れ、樹木茂れる山道を辿つて、奥へ奥へと進み入る。

夏とは云へど樹木覆へる谷川の畔の道を行く事とて、身も慄ふ許り寒さを感じた。
