§ 1

"Even though the flow of Cain may cease, The way of lust, though checked, does not stop; Sake and gambling still do not cease. 'Drink and make merry, for one step ahead is darkness,' So glibly speaks the missionary. We are naked like bagworms, Exposed to the rain, buffeted by fierce winds, Treading upon the deep mountain frost, Exposed to the dew of the eternal night. The sake we wish to drink, oh, we do not drink; The food we wish to eat, ah, we do not eat. Receiving the dregs and leftovers of others, We totter through each day, Crossing the log bridge of this fleeting world. Though we wear rags upon our bodies, The vital essence of our souls Is that of great men adorned in brocade. Oppressed by the iniquity of Ural no Hiko, We were in the mountains yesterday, and today again

Exposed to the tempest of the fields, Sniffed out by foul dogs, Captured, and on some day Carried off to the mountains of Ural, With tongues twisted and eyes gouged out, With limbs bound in a diamond shape, Made to listen to the carefree song 'Drink and make merry,' When we reflect upon our future ends like horned owls, Is this world an ogre, a great serpent, or a dark night? Our bodies vanish like the morning dew; Truly, our karma is fleeting. In what age will the God of Salvation Descend from heaven? On what day will the ship of rescue Rise from the bottom of the sea? We, sunken in sorrow, In what age will we ever rise? Ah, what a dreary world of darkness! Ah, what a dreary night of darkness!"

Thus singing, there was a band of wandering deities descending the bank of the Eden River. At this time, from up ahead:

"God has appeared on the surface To separate good from evil. Polish your souls, reform yourselves! Rectify the conduct of your bodies as well! The power of sincerity protects the world."

Thus with an interesting tune came a missionary singing. This was a divine person named Azumahiko, who was a missionary of the Great Deity named Haniyashiko, who had appeared at the foot of Mount Kogane to save this chaotic and entangled world and established a teaching called the Five Great Teachings. The party heard this voice and listened attentively. The missionary still advanced toward them while singing the propagation song.

Wearing a straw raincoat and woven hat, and clad lightly in straw sandals and gaiters, the missionary sang the propagation song aloud as he passed by the party and went on his way. They looked at each other's faces, and:

First: "Somehow, that song just now seemed easy for our ears to take in; isn't it strangely interesting?"

Second: "Yeah, now that you mention it, that's true. It even sounded like the voice of a deity."

Third: "At any rate, why don't we call out and stop him to hear more details?"

Fourth: "Even if we call out to stop him, he probably wouldn't turn around for people like us. Rather than embarrassing ourselves, why don't we drop it?"

Hei: "Do not talk nonsense; saving people is what an emissary does. Surely, if we call and stop him, he will wait for us."

All: "That sounds good. Hey! Hey!"

So all shouted in unison and raised their right hands, beckoning him. The emissary turned back and gazed toward them. There, a shabby man was selected from among the party and ran toward him. He then placed his hands on the ground before the emissary,

"We listened to your divine song, and we feel as if an indescribable strength has entered our hearts. It may be troublesome for you, but could you please grant us a detailed talk in order to save all of us?"

Thus, showing his sincerity on his face, he pleaded. The emissary said,

"In order to save the realm from confusion and destitution, I uphold the teachings of Haniyashihiko no Mikoto, who appeared at the foot of Mount Kogane, and propagate them throughout the realm. If you will listen to our propagation, I will gladly comply."

So he said. Meanwhile, everyone gathered near the emissary and bowed courteously one by one. The emissary also returned the courtesy politely, took a seat upon a beautiful flat rock nearby, and all sat around him, beginning their questions and answers.

Kō: "In your song just now, there were the words, 'God appears out in the open and separates good and evil,' but is there really a precious deity in this world who protects us? Will a true deity really appear who judges good and evil fairly and openly? This alone occupies our minds day and night without ceasing."

The emissary replied, saying:

"This world was created by the true God. And God created human beings so that they would perform divine service. God descends into humans as living shrines, anxious day and night to establish a splendid world. The physical bodies of all of you are also human beings created by being bestowed with the spirit and flesh of the precious God. As such, you are the noble lords of all creation who ought to serve as God's living shrines and labor. Yet, forgetting human duty and merely indulging in eating, drinking, and carnal lust is the deepest sin against God. In this world, there are good gods, and there are evil gods. And in the present world, the proportion has become one good god to nine hundred ninety-nine evil gods. Therefore, God intends to purify this world and purify the souls of humans who are God's living shrines to build a splendid divine nation, and thus has dispatched emissaries in all directions."

Thus, he recounted the outline.

Person A said, "There are truly many things that we cannot comprehend. Therefore, wishing to ask you, we called out and stopped you. In the first place, people today monopolize the vast mountains and fields, leaving us with no place to work, nor do they allow us to work. No matter how much it is said that poverty cannot overtake hard work, if there is no means to work, do we have any choice but to become beggars? Of course, we do not wish to idle about, eat and drink in comfort, or live in luxury. We would be satisfied if only we could work and, as parents, children, and spouses, somehow manage to pass each day. And yet, beneath this vast heaven, we do not possess even a place to set our feet. Everything has been monopolized by the strong and the mighty, leaving us nowhere to work; parents, children, and siblings are torn apart, and while suffering beneath the heaven, we barely pass the days as wanderers. If this world could be renewed so that, like the shining of the sun, the blessings of heaven and earth could moisten us all without exception, I think there could be nothing more grateful. And when might that splendid Kami appear?"

Thus tilting his head, he peered into the missionary's face. The missionary, while filling his eyes with tears, said:

"The birds soaring in the sky and the flowers blooming in the fields all receive the profound grace of Kami and continue their lives completely. How much more so is this true for human beings, who are the lord of all creation and living shrines of Kami! How could it be that the protection of Kami does not exist? If you simply entrust all things to the divine will of Kami and live this very day and moment with continuous gratitude, Kami will allow you to meet the flowering spring. The world is not composed solely of dark nights; even when there is a dark night, the dawn will eventually break. Just as after a winter of cold falling snow, a tranquil spring appears where flowers bloom and birds sing, joy will surely follow suffering. It is understandable that you wander all about because you have no place to work, but this entire world belongs to Kami. Among what humans possess, not even a single speck of dust clinging to the sole of the foot belongs to them. People of today monopolize vast mountains and fields and think of them as their own, but when their allotted lifespan ends and they reach the hidden world, they must abandon every immense treasure, wives, children, and kin, and trudge along entirely alone. The only companion one has is the heavy burden of deep sin. You, too, must believe in Kami, hold a heart of single-minded sincerity, and be active between this vast heaven and earth. Surely Kami will bestow happiness upon you. The tangible treasures upon this earth are treasures that perish. They are fleeting treasures washed away by water, consumed by fire, eaten by insects, and rusted away. Rather than that, human beings must strive to lay up in the Kingdom of Kami the single treasure of sincerity, which eternally and without end never decays, breaks, burns, or perishes."

Thus, as he earnestly taught and urged the doctrines of the Five Great Teachings, all held their breath and fervently listened to the missionary's instruction. At this moment, once again raising a voice high,

"Kaminaobi, who created this world, Ōnaobi, whose divine soul is vast; Let all people, in all matters, Look anew and listen anew with a straight spirit; Confess and correct the errors of oneself,"

chanting thus, there was a missionary advancing toward this direction.

と、大略を物語りける。

甲『吾々はどうしても、合点の行かぬことが沢山あります。それで貴神に御尋ねをしたいと思つて、呼び止めました。一体今日の人間は、広い山や野を独占し、さうして吾々の働く処もなく、また働かしてもくれない。何ほど働くに追ひ付く貧乏なしと云つても、働く種がなければ、吾々は乞食でもするより、仕方がないではありませぬか。勿論吾々は、遊んで楽に飲んだり食つたり、贅沢をしようとは思ひませぬ。唯働いて、親子夫婦が、その日をどうなりと、暮すことが出来ればそれで満足するのであります。然るに吾々は、この広い天が下に、脚踏み立てる場所も持つて居りませぬ。皆強い者、大きな者に、独占されて、働くに処なく、親子兄弟は、ちりぢりばらばらになり、天が下を苦しみながら、漂浪ひつつわづかにその日を暮してをります。こんな世の中を立替へて御日様の御照しの様に、万遍なく、吾々にも天地の恵が身に潤ふ事ができるならば、こんな有難いことはなからうと思ひます。さうしてその結構な神様は何時御現はれになりませうか』

と首を傾け、宣伝使の顔を覗き込む。宣伝使は両眼に涙を湛へながら、

『空翔つ鳥も、野辺に咲く花も、みな神様の厚き恵をうけて、完全に生活を続けてをります。况んや万物の霊長たり、神の生宮たる人間に於ておや。神様の御守りがどうして無いといふ事がありませうか。ただ何事も神様の御心に任せ、今日只今を、有難い有難いで暮して行けば、神様は花咲く春に会はして下さいます。世の中は暗夜ばかりではない、暗夜があつても何時かは夜が明ける。冷たい雪の降る冬があれば、また長閑な花咲き鳥唄ふ春が出て来る様に、きつと苦みの後には楽しみがあります。あなた方も働く場所がないからといつて、そこら中を漂浪ひなさるのも、無理はありませぬが、この世界は皆神様のものである。人間のものは、足の裏に附いて居る土埃一つだもありませぬ。今の人間は広大な山野を独占して、自分のもののやうに思つてゐるが、命数尽きて、幽界に至るときは、いかなる巨万の財宝も、妻子も、眷属も一切を捨てて、ただ独トボトボと行かねばならぬのである。唯自分の連れとなるものは深い罪の重荷ばかりである。あなた方も、神を信じ、誠一つの心を持つて、この広い天地の間に活動なさい。きつと神様が幸を与へて下さいます。この地の上の形ある宝は、亡ぶる宝であります。水に流れ火に焼かれ、虫に蝕はれ、錆朽ちる、果敢ない宝である。それよりも人間は、永遠無窮に朽ちず、壊れず、焼けず、亡びぬ誠といふ一つの宝を神の御国に積む事を努めねばなりませぬ』

と諄々として五大教の教理を説き勧めたるに、一同は呼吸を凝らして、熱心に宣伝使の教示を聞き入りぬ。この時またもや、声張り揚げて、

『この世を創造りし神直日 御魂も広き大直日 ただ何事も人は皆 直日に見直せ聞直せ 身の過は宣り直せ』

と謡ひつつ、此方に向つて進み来る宣伝使ありけり。
