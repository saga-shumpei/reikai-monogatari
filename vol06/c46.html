Chapter 46: The Deity Wakatoshi [296]

第46章　若年神〔296〕

§ 1

Amid the scorching heat of the dog days, which was so fierce that it was like sitting in a fiery chamber or being inside a cauldron, four or five farmers in the shade of trees beside the paddies of Mitose Village were engrossed in talk while looking out over the paddies, bathing their bodies in the cool breeze that blew up from time to time.

A said, "What a strange year this is. Ever since Ōtoshigami came to this village, the crop failures that had continued year after year ceased entirely, the rice bore well, and we peasants lived in a blessed world of peace and plenty; but ever since that kami returned to somewhere called Mount Kogane, crop failures have continued once again, and though we peasants grow rice, we cannot put even a single grain into our mouths, barely keeping ourselves alive by eating tree leaves and peeling bark. How miserable it is. Could there not be something about this that displeases the kami?"

B said, "Well, I wonder; it is a terrible thing, look at this rice, we cannot even bear to keep our eyes open and look at it, can we? Both last year and this year, no sooner did the stems grow lush and green and the leaves spread out, than rice insects infested the entire paddies, and before our eyes the rice leaves fell tumbling down one after another, and the green fields withered reddish-brown just like a winter wasteland. At this rate, this year too we will not be able to receive fine rice. With the rice plants, which could be called the parents of our lives, collapsing completely like this, we who are rice-eating vermin must eventually meet the same fate as this rice, ah."

Thus he sighed.

C said, "That is why I say so every time. The people of Mitose Village are obstinate and dull, and they understand nothing, which is the problem. Did not Ōtoshigami say so repeatedly, that fine rice grows entirely through the light of the Sun, the fine, pure water, and the favor of the soil? He surely told us that above all, if peasants forget the favor of this fire, water, and soil, or if they defile fire, defile water, or defile the soil, insects will attack the rice and we will not receive a single grain of rice, so be careful; I have never forgotten that for even a single day. And so I always keep that blessed teaching, though."

B said, "If that is so, the fields and paddies at your place should hardly be eaten by insects. Yet aren't insects eating your paddy as well?"

C said, "That is the times. For it is said that even the kami cannot prevail against the times."

§ 2

Otsu said, "If you please the gods by keeping fire, water, and earth undefiled, why would they not show an example—sparing only your rice fields from being eaten by insects—to demonstrate what happens to those who keep the teachings of our gods?"

Hei said, "No matter how much my single household purifies itself, insects migrate from the neighboring fields, so it cannot be helped. Unless the entire village repents together, even the pure will be dragged into it and ruined together. That is why the gods tell us that the whole village must unite in harmony and have faith."

Kō said, "Even if you say not to defile them, if one is farming, one must apply feces and urine to the fields; without fertilizer, the rice plants will not grow large, and the harvest will accordingly be small, so it is impossible not to defile them. That is asking the impossible, even for the gods."

Hei said, "Of course fertilizer must be applied, but that depends on the timing. Right now, at the vital time of planting the rice fields, if people scatter feces, scatter urine, relieve themselves in the fields, and do such foolish things, the gods will not protect them. When planting the fields, one must purify one's heart, keep one's body clean, worship the gods, and when there is menstruation or the like, no matter how busy it is, one must refrain from entering the fields just during the planting, for on that day the gods are protecting us. On ordinary days in between, even if one applies slightly unclean things, the earth absorbs them, and they reach the roots of the rice plants to become fertilizer. Furthermore, nowadays, people do not worship the gods at the time of rice planting, and claiming that feces and urine become fertilizer in the fields, they do as they please. In addition, because people eat the meat of cattle, which ought to be the treasure of farmers, and menstruating women enter the fields, Ōtoshi-no-Kami does not grant divine protection either. This is an affliction for the entire village, so I want each person to be mindful of it."

As they were conversing in this manner, a female missionary of neither tall nor short stature and with fair features appeared there.

"The field workers at the beginning of summer, Who plant the parent of life, Defiled the earth, defiled the fire, And even defiled the water, and the retribution For eating the meat of cattle is before our very eyes; As far as the eye can see across the wide fields, While the trees and grasses of the mountains Grow luxuriantly and green, the surface of the rice fields Is like a winter wasteland. Ah, Ōtoshi-no-Kami, Cause the conduct of the farmers To be renewed and purify the world, Make the year ripen abundantly, And make this the land of Toyouke. Divining the divine will of Haniyashihiko And Haniyashihime, O field workers who bear The name of farmers, amend your hearts! May the autumn harvest bend heavily, And may the fruits of the parent of life enter the storehouses."

§ 3

"Bestow until it swells, Bestow until it swells."

Singing thus in a low voice, she was about to pass before the field hands resting in the shade of the trees.

Kō said,

"Excuse me,"

When he thus called out and stopped her, the missionary halted,

"I take you to be peasants of this village, but what do you think of this rice being eaten by insects and about to be completely destroyed like this? Might it not be the wrath of Ōtoshi-no-kami? Surely there must be someone in this village who has killed and eaten the treasure of the peasants. Henceforth, it would be well for you to take care not to do such an unclean thing. I will now perform an incantation for you, so please never eat the treasure of the peasants in the future."

Saying this, she plucked the long grass nearby, fashioned it into the shape of a phallus, enshrined it at the water inlet of the field, and recited a norito. Thereupon, before their very eyes, the rice plants turned green; the surface of the paddies, which had resembled a frost-covered plain, suddenly became like the waves of the blue sea, the rice leaves rustled in the wind, and flourishing in moments, they came to make a murmuring sound. The peasants clapped their hands and rejoiced. When they looked beside them, the female missionary had gone somewhere, and her form had disappeared. This was a transformed deity of Wakatoshi-no-kami.
