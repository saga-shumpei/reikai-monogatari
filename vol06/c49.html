Chapter 49: A Journey on Shanks' Mare [299]

第49章　膝栗毛〔299〕

§ 1

Hiromichiwake-no-Kami and Izumohime, addressing the official from within the castle,

"We are grateful for your kindness. However, being missionaries entrusted by the Kami to save this world, to undergo hardships and toils is our true intent; therefore, if you have a need for us, we will gladly go anywhere, but as for the vehicle, we must beg to decline,"

refused thus. The official,

"What you say is indeed reasonable, yet we came to greet you with a palanquin by order of the lord of the castle, and if you do not by all means ride in it, we shall be scolded upon our return, so please do ride. We beg of you,"

entreated thus. Hiromichiwake-no-Kami said,

"Your words are quite reasonable, but when one considers that the peasants are weeding in paddy fields like boiling water despite this heat, it would be unworthy and we cannot commit such luxury. We possess splendid legs bestowed by the Kami, so we shall spur on this shank's mare and go. As for vehicles, we utterly beg to decline."

現代日本語訳

広道別天使と出雲姫は、城内の役人に向かって、

「ご親切はありがたく存じます。しかしながら、私たちはこの世を救う神の負託を受けた宣伝使の身の上であり、艱難苦労をすることこそが私たちの本意でありますから、ご用があれば喜んでどこへでも参りますが、乗り物だけはご容赦を被りたい」

と断った。役人は、

「仰せはごもっともですが、私たちは城主の命令で、駕籠を持ってお迎えに参った者であり、ぜひとも乗っていただかないと、帰ってから叱られますので、どうぞお乗りください。お願いいたします」

と懇願した。広道別天使は、

「あなたのお言葉はごもっともですが、この暑さのなか、農民は熱湯のような田の中で草取りをしていることを思えば、もったいなくてそのような贅沢なことはできません。私たちは神様からいただいた立派な脚を持っておりますから、この膝栗毛に鞭打って参ります。乗り物は真っ平ご免を被りたい」

原文（近代文語）

広道別天使、出雲姫は、城内の役人に向つて、

と断りける。役人は、

『御説は御尤もなれど、吾々は、城主の命令で、駕籠を持つて御迎ひに参つたもの、是非乗つて頂かぬと、帰つてから叱られますから、どうぞ御乗り下さい。お願ひです』

と頼み入る。広道別天使は、

『あなたの御言葉は御尤もなれど、この暑いのに百姓は熱湯のやうな田の中で、草除りをしてをることを思へば、勿体なくてそんな奢つたことはできませぬ。吾々は神様から頂いた立派な脚を持つて居りますから、この膝栗毛に鞭韃つて参ります。乗物は真平御免を蒙りたい』

§ 2

As they firmly declined and did not comply, the officials had no choice,

"Since we make such a request and you will not accept it, there is no help for it. Hey, palanquin bearers, you had better shoulder this palanquin and return at once. We will accompany this person and return on foot, so convey this matter to Umorinokami."

The palanquin bearers,

"Yes,"

answered, and shouldering the palanquin at once, ran off at top speed.

The two messengers of propagation, along with Iwahiko and the big man Kumako, trudged along the capital's main avenue together with four or five officials. And the angel Hiromichiwake and Izumohime proceeded, alternately singing propagation songs to each other.

現代日本語訳

と固く辞退して応じなかったので、役人はやむを得ず、

駕籠舁きは、

「はい」

と答えて、すぐに駕籠を担いで一目散に駆け出した。

二人の宣伝使と、岩彦および大男の熊公は、四、五人の役人と共に、都大路をとぼとぼと進んで行く。そして広道別天使および出雲姫は、代わる代わる互いに宣伝歌を歌いながら進んで行く。

§ 3

On both sides of the main road, three people stood over there, and five or ten over here, watching the figure of this party and offering their various rumors.

A: "Look at that! That fellow called a missionary hindered the passage of the Lord of the Castle, so he is being dragged away by the officials. Look, look! Two huge men like Nio are even following along. At any rate, that fellow will be dragged into the castle and meet with furitsuke."

B: "What? Is he going to teach acting? The Lord of the Castle must be quite eccentric. Even if they have a fellow like that do the choreography, they won't be able to put on a decent play."

C: "Choreography? Is it something so carefree? It means crucifixion!"

B: "Oh, is that so? Despite being a man, does he do sewing? The woman at my place also said recently that she was practicing needlework, and had cloth-pasting done over at Lady Otama's place."

A: "Is it something so carefree? He will be made to suffer a terrible ordeal!"

§ 4

Hei: "Not even knowing what a terrible ordeal he will be subjected to, he is singing a song so carefree; he must be an awfully easygoing fellow."

Otsu: "What nonsense, that is pure desperation. Like the petty song of a prisoner being dragged away, it looks disgraceful to walk down this main avenue despondently like sheep to the slaughterhouse, so he is putting on false bravery. Just listen to that voice; isn't he whining something about overlooking and turning a deaf ear?"

The party of the missionaries, while hearing this gossip, proceeded steadily forward together with the officials.

Furthermore, over on this side, there was a group of men and women who were whispering among themselves upon seeing the party.

Kō: "Today is the auspicious and splendid birthday of our Castle Lord, who, taking a large retinue and riding in a magnificent palanquin, is going to pay a visit of worship to a place called Hitotsushima or something; so all the townsfolk, for the sake of the celebration, were raising flags at each house, partaking of sacred sake, and dancing about, when some so-called missionary came along singing a stiff, tedious song, and supposedly obstructed the Castle Lord's procession, which is why he is now being dragged away. That fellow does not even have a face that looks drunk on sake, so why on earth would he do such a foolish thing? What a reckless fellow."

Otsu: "Like summer insects flying into the fire, is it? However, lately evil deities have been acting so domineering that tremendous uproars have broken out here and there, and the people have tasted misery and charcoal-balls of suffering and black spheres, rolling their eyes in distress, weeping, raging, and regretting here and there, so the Sun God took offense, rain continued to fall day after day, and finally, the Commander of this world, the deity of the Pillar of the Land, or someone called Izanami-no-Mikoto, became utterly disgusted with this world and departed for the Land of Yomi, a land of rubbish, or whatever filthy land it may be. Furthermore, today is the Castle Lord's birthday, and it happens to be wonderfully fine weather. Even the Sun God was deeply impressed by the Castle Lord's majestic virtue, making it such a splendidly clear day for the whole world. Yet, because he obstructed it by singing a gloomy song, retribution was immediate—striking off his own neck with his own sword; there certainly are fools in this world."

Thus they reviled them in various voices. Paying no heed to any details, the party advanced steadily and concealed their figures within Rōma Castle.

と口々にののしっている。一行はいっさい構わずどんどん進み、羅馬城内に姿を隠した。
