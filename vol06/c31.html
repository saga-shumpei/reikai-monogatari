Chapter 31: Brocade of Rags [281]

第31章　襤褸の錦〔281〕

§ 1

That so-called Banko Shin'ō, who had established a divine capital in the Ural Mountains and the plains of Armenia, propagated a body-primary, spirit-secondary theocracy throughout the realm, finally banished the gentle and solemn Banko Shin'ō, made himself the false Banko Shin'ō, and, instigated by the soul of the giant serpent, wielded tyrannical power for a time, was struck with the benevolent whip of the Great God's great compassion, was saved at Mount Altai, and suffered the torments of ants and insects; whereupon he completely repented of his past errors, returned once again to the Ural Mountains, established the divine capital in Armenia, well governed and showed benevolence to the divine people of all regions, and was most deeply revered by the people as the savior deity after the great cataclysm of heaven and earth; yet as the months and years passed, the two deities, husband and wife, grew somewhat weary of the divine administration, indulged in lust and gluttony, and once again,

"Drink and make merry, for darkness lies just ahead; After the darkness, the moon will rise; Let people drink, eat, sleep, and roll over,"

—singing thus, he was possessed once again by the soul of the giant serpent, and came to begin body-primary, spirit-secondary actions.

Even the souls of the giant serpent, who were so formidable in evil, as well as the souls of the golden foxes and ogres, hid themselves like newts and earthworms in the face of the great cataclysm of the universe, shrinking back before the blazing divine majesty and keeping themselves concealed; but as they grew somewhat accustomed to the peace of the world and carelessness arose in the hearts of the divine people, it once again became a disastrous situation where evil spirits and corrupt deities raised their heads and ran rampant.

As was shown also in the Divine Revelation:

"In this world, demons are watching for an opening to take advantage of, so do not loosen your sashes,"

—just as it was shown, if there is a moment of carelessness, the evil deities attack like the wind, corrupting their souls and attempting to cast them down to the Nether Country.

Banko Shin'ō (the assumed false name of Uralhiko) had his soul swayed by the soul of the giant serpent, and ultimately established a sect of teaching. This is called the Daichūkyō. The meaning of this teaching was, in short, a doctrine of extreme individualism, a principle centered upon oneself alone. "Dai" means one person. The meaning of centering upon one person was that Banko Shin'ō alone was the deity of this world, the king, and the supreme authority, and it was a way of establishing a teaching that demanded obedience to all commands with this one person at the center. Nevertheless, numerous proselytizers misunderstood the meaning of establishing the teaching, and, swayed by the evil spirits belonging to the kindred of the giant serpent and the golden fox, finally came to misunderstand that centering upon oneself alone was the principle of Daichūkyō. Indeed, it turned into the most abominable practice of egoism.

§ 2

This Daichūkyō spread far and wide throughout Ashihara no Mizuho no Kuni (the Earth), bringing about the rampant dominance of Ōyamagui-no-Kami, Koyamagui-no-Kami, Nozuchi-no-Kami, and Kayanuhime-no-Kami, and giving rise to a general trend in which body-ruling-spirit, unruly deities—such as Kanayamahiko, Kanayamahime, Honoyakihayawo-no-Kami, Kagutsuchi-no-Kami, Hinokagabino-no-Kami, Ōgetsuhime-no-Kami, Ame-no-Iwakusabune-no-Kami, and Ame-no-Torifune-no-Kami—manifested themselves in various places upon the earth.

Thereupon, Kamu-Izanami-no-Mikoto, the Pillar God of the Land, exhausted Her strength in governing the earthly deities and mortals and departed into the Underworld, as has already been recounted.

Separated southward from the sacred capital of Armenia by merely several tens of chō, a rural village had a fairly wide river flowing from east to west, which was called the Cain River. Basking in the spring sun, there was a group of four or five beggars engrossed in idle talk. Each was eagerly chattering about something or other.

Person A: "People in the world seem to say that if you live as a beggar for three days, you will never forget the taste of it. They seem to imagine that a beggar has no regular employment, merely wanders throughout the world bowing servilely to receive and eat people's leftovers, roams freely through scenic and historic spots, hums tunes, and passes through this world carefree. Not at all—a beggar's life is bitter indeed. The selfish people of this world know nothing of hardship, and that is why they utter such babble like sheltered brats, failing to see that if you beg for even three days, you will never forget that bitterness for ten thousand kalpas to the end of time. Born in the same era, born as beloved children of God with horizontal eyes and vertical noses—yet look at the difference: on the one hand are those fiends who possess numerous mountains and farmlands, erect houses and storehouses, keep many concubines and mistresses, wrap themselves in twill and brocade, get drunk on sake every single day, and strut about as if the entire realm belonged to them, pretending to be wealthy lords like Banko and singing, 'Drink and make merry, for what lies an inch ahead is darkness; drink, eat, sleep, and roll around!' And on the other hand are gentlemen like us, who every single day borrow the eaves of other people's houses, shelter from the rain and dew under trees, and clothe ourselves in splendid brocades of rags resembling a procession of seaweed or the shingle of a rag-dealer—how do they compare? Is it the moon to a snapping turtle, or heavenly clouds to the mud of a swamp? It is utterly absurd. When I reflect upon this, I have grown sick and tired of this world. What a fellow this Banko-Ōkami is—he is nothing less than the reincarnation of the Eight-Forked Serpent!"

Person B: "Hey, hey, do not speak so loudly. Look, another strange fellow is coming over there. He must be a petty official employed by those evil deities called Yamagui or Kawagui. Just the other day, when Don... was whispering about the conduct of Banko Shin'ō, the dogs of Yamagui and his ilk sniffed it out, forcefully bound Don..., dragged him deep into the Ural Mountains, and made sport of him, so it is told. How terrifying, how terrifying—it is a world of demons. Keep quiet, keep quiet. Better left unsaid than spoken, as they say."

§ 3

At this time, an inspector of dark reddish complexion, with rolling black eyes, stopped in front of the crowd of beggars and asked,

"Hey! What were you whispering just now?"

A said, "Yes, it is splendid weather, and how warm it is. Joyfully the mountains on all sides smile, and the birds are singing in the flowering trees. Truly it is a splendid spring in heaven. When I reflect that this, too, is entirely the gift of the benevolent rule of Banko-shin'ō, tears of joy spill forth. Yes, yes,"

thus speaking of unrelated matters. The inspector, slightly sharpening his voice, said,

"Fool! That is not what I am talking about. I am asking what you were whispering just now!"

A tilted his head, feigned deafness as if cupping his ear with his hand, and said,

"I am a little hard of hearing, so I cannot catch your words clearly, but I thought I heard you say something about whispering or drinking sake. If I am mistaken, please forgive me entirely. Ah, these days the days are long, my stomach grows empty, my throat becomes parched, and if there were someone who would treat me to even a cup of sweet sake, it would be truly splendid; but just now, having drunk a large amount of the water of this River Cain, I slapped my belly, going 'sasa yattokosei.' The belly sounds quite well. Since even my deafness allows me to hear it, it would surely be truly amusing if you were to listen. The belly-drum of the white-tailed tanuki, it has become amusing. Sasa yattokose, yōiyana. No matter how much I say yōiyana, with water the intoxication still does not come over me. Please treat me to a cup of good sake,"

and at this answer, seemingly drunk on gas, the inspector had no ground to press him, puffed up his cheeks, turned on his heel, and walked away.

『馬鹿ツ、そんなことを言つて居るのぢや無い。今何を囁いてゐたかといふのだ』

『私は一寸耳が遠いので、しつかり貴方の御言葉は聞きとれませぬが、何でも囁くとかささを呑んでゐるとか、仰有るやうに聴きました。間違ひましたら真平御免なさい。イヤもうこの頃は、日は長し腹は減るなり、喉は渇くなり、甘いささの一杯でも呑ましてくれる人があれば、本当に結構ですが、今このカイン河の水をどつさり呑んで、ささやつとこせいと腹を叩きました。腹はよう鳴りますよ。私の聾でさへ聞えるくらゐですから、貴方がたが御聴きになつたら、本当に面白いでせうよ。尾も白狸の腹鼓、面白うなつておいでたな。ささやつとこせー、よーいやな。なんぼよういやなと云つたつて、水では尚且酔がまはらぬ。よいささ一杯ふれまつて下さい』

と屁に酔うたやうな答へに、目付も取つくしまも無く、面ふくらし踵を返して帰り行く。
