Chapter 44: The Unwonted Husbandman [294]

第44章　俄百姓〔294〕

§ 1

In the narrow fields and long fields, the high fields and sunken fields, forming ranks in groups of threes and fives, how valiant were the figures of the men leveling the soil after the plows, and the young maiden rice-planters setting out the seedlings while singing in delightful voices.

The two missionaries were watching this scene, and Hiromichiwake said, as soon as he opened his mouth:

"Ah, how magnificent it is. Looking at this scene, it feels just like Heaven. If the people of the whole world were to work as hard as this, not a single complaint would arise in the world. We think that being missionaries is hard, but when we look at the work of these peasants, it feels as though no amount of hardship and tribulation on our part would ever be enough. We receive this rice, every single grain of which has been produced through hardship, and while basking in the benevolence of God and society and receiving generous protection, walking around putting on missionary faces while claiming it is for the sake of the Divine Way or for the nation, truly makes me feel ashamed before both the peasants and Heavenly God. For our part, out of gratitude, we should ask the peasants to let us help them."

Kumojiwake Tenshi also said,

"Indeed, that is fine. Perhaps I should try negotiating with them,"

and tilted his head dubiously.

Farmhand A: "Ah, an honorable peasant's life is quite painful. Oh, I have already grown tired of this. Ah, I hate it."

§ 2

...so he sighed.

Otsu: "What is this? Standing in the middle of the rice field like a crow-scarecrow, opening your big mouth, doing nothing but yawning, and saying you grew tired—what are you playing dumb about? Is there anyone who dreams while standing? When autumn comes, rice can be harvested, which is fine, but it is still the height of summer. Scoop up some muddy water or something and wash your face. If you stand like that, the kites in the sky will drop dung on you. You scarecrow rascal!"

Kō: "Saying that even a cat's paw is a human hand—no matter how busy you say you are, turning a human hand into a cat's paw..."

Otsu: "That is precisely why I tell you to wash your face. Yesterday, saying it would rain, my calico cat put saliva on its paws, stroked its own face, and washed itself. A cat's paw is good enough for the likes of you."

Kō grew angry, raised the hoe he held, and struck the muddy field with a slap using all his might. At that very moment, the muddy water splashed heavily onto Otsu's face. Otsu grew angry, and

"What are you doing!"

—so saying, he once again raised his hoe and struck the muddy field with a slap aimed toward Kō. When the muddy water flew onto Kō's face to an unbearable degree,

§ 3

Kō said, "Hey, a fight? If it is a fight, I like it better than a meal,"

and standing in the muddy paddy field, spitting on both his hands and stamping his feet,

"Come on!"

he spread his arms wide. Otsu became determined not to be outdone, and said,

"You, Tagosaku, do not mistake me. Just because a tiger closes its eyes and hides its claws, you take it for a cat; hey, I will show you the physical strength of this Master Tiger!"

No sooner had he spoken than, rolling up his knobby, tensed arms full of lumps like those of a Niō, he began wrestling in the muddy paddy field. The many peasants stopped their work all at once and cried,

"Hey, Tago, wait, wait! If it is a fight, do it in the mountains!"

§ 4

...they ran up from all directions. Tagosaku fled for his life. Tora gave chase. Hei grabbed Tora's straw raincoat, saying,

"There is no need to chase one who flees; forgive him who has surrendered, forgive him!"

Torakō said, "None of Mokubē's business! Crouching down and flicking frogs suits your nature. Who do you take me for? I am Master Tora, born in the Year of the Ox! I am the Ushitora no Konjin! If you cross me, I shall curse you!"

...he shouted, opening his eyes wide. The two missionaries, without thinking, plunged into the paddy field, saying,

"Now, now, Ushitora no Konjin, we pray you become peaceful and calm. We shall help in your place, so please rest for a while, as you must be tired."

Torakō said, "Who in the world are you? Tough work like farming is something missionaries like you could never do; you must be drifters walking carefree, blabbering things like 'Drink and make merry, for one step ahead is pitch darkness.' You had better learn the bitter taste of a farmer once. Come, I shall lend you this hoe; level the mud with this, understood? Ah, just when I was thinking I wanted to rest, strange fellows have dropped in. Hey, here is Tago's hoe as well. It suits the situation perfectly; do it dutifully. As a reward, I will treat you to at least a rice ball or half of barley rice."

The two missionaries simply said "Yes, yes" to everything and assisted with the rice planting, covered in sweat. And on the following day, and the day after that, until the rice planting was finished, they went about helping here and there.

This was appreciated by the fellow farmers, and they ended up continuing their life in the paddy fields until the Sanaburi Festival.

At the Sanaburi Festival, men and women of all ages throughout the village gathered to celebrate the safe completion of the rice planting, pounding dumplings and rice cakes to celebrate.

At this time, the two missionaries were also invited and joined this gathering, and as they expounded and taught the doctrines of the Ananaikyō, this entire village thereby came to partake of the divine grace.
