Chapter 10: The Parting of the Four Birds [260]

第10章　四鳥の別れ〔260〕

§ 1

Kasugahime approached the ship in a hurry. The name of the ship happened by chance to be Kasugamaru.

Before long, the ship loosed its moorings and was beginning to advance toward the east.

Kasugahime stood up at the bow of the ship, and in the instant she turned around to look at the Tokoyo country for the last time, face to face—who would have thought?—the missionary Takasumibiko was there, as though lost in thought while gazing at the ship.

Startled, Kasugahime looked closely and saw that, though his attire had changed and his skin was sunburnt, he was unmistakably her own husband.

The ship caught the spring breeze in its full sail and advanced toward the open sea, like the white waves of the two's painful sorrow. Takasumibiko stood up,

"The floating world changes like deep pools and shallows, Yesterday's clouds, today's clear sky; Though called a world without constancy, Walking along the same path, The missionary of Mount Tenkyō Is buffeted by the winds of the floating world. Even the towering waves—for Takasumibiko, Particularly long-standing like the paired pines of Aioi, Are of no avail in this spring of today. Though the mountains wear mists as sashes, Though the blossoms smile, and the voices of various birds Sing tranquilly, Changing like pools and shallows, like fleeting foam, This living parting upon vanishing waves: I go toward Tokoyo upon the receding tide, While you are upon the eastern waves. In the past when we had not met, my heart was at ease, Yet now my longing has grown ever more; May this heart of mine, grieving over our parting, Vanish like foam.

Those who live must perish, those who meet must part; Though we have met at last, Divided by the waves upon the surface of the sea, With a sinking heart aboard the ship, Floating through the floating world, by the blessing Of the god of Watarai, without misfortune, Cross over, cross over, Kasugahime! Upon the waves that grow faint, His voice too has become dim. The Kasugamaru pushes across the waves; Kasugahime pushes across the waves; The radiant rays of the morning sun rise in abundant prosperity, The waves shine and the sea breeze smells sweet, And the rain of mutual tears falls."

Thus reciting a sorrowful song filled with emotion, he saw Kasugahime off.

The ship advanced steadily farther and farther out to sea. Ah, how the hearts of these two must have been steeped in tears of grief!

Waving her veil until the figure of her husband standing on the land vanished, Kasugahime was brought to this inevitable parting between east and west.

While gazing out over the surface of the sea on all sides, Kasugahime recited a song of farewell in suppressed sorrow. That song:

"Separated by the waves of the floating world, Deep is my yearning upon the sea; Parted by waves that rise to west and to east, May this parting of today, in some future age, Lead to an age of paired pines of Aioi once more, When joyful to meet again, like the pines of Takasago Covered in deep snow, we grow white-haired together. Were this the proper age, extolled as the flower Of Moscow, his renown High in the clouds—Takasumibiko, The divine priest, and Kasugahime! § 2

"Already three years have passed since leaving the capital; The moon shining beyond the clouds, Rejoicing as the official of the God of Tsukiteru of pure heart, Crossing dew-drenched fields and traversing mountains and rivers, Reaching the crimson beach of long waiting, Upon seeing the manly form of my beloved lord, Like the full moon that never wanes, right before my eyes, Without even the leisure to exchange a single joyful word of greeting, Without any compassion, sinking into unexpected sorrow from the rough waves, I am returning to the east. O Takasumiwake, my husband! The land of Tokoyo, where raging gods make rampant havoc, is eternal darkness. Do not let your feet tread upon the oyster shells lining the beach; Take heed as you pass through, O Nagisahiko!

With the protection of Nagisa-no-Hime, May your body remain healthy as you go forth to Tokoyo-no-kuni, Paying heed to the front and rear, to the right and left! Even though seas and mountains may separate us, The soul of Kasuga-no-Hime Shall abide close beside your life To protect you for all eternity, Until the darkness of Tokoyo is cleared away, Until the spring of Tokoyo arrives! My thoughts, which have no leisure to clear away— Whenever I think of your person, Even the sun and moon in the sky of my heart grow hazy, Kasugahime, faintly bowing down in reverence to your noble visage, Faintly bowing down in reverence to your noble visage! Upon the waves of pure heart, As you depart for Tokoyo, Without ever being tormented By the morning dew or the evening frost,

May your body advance vigorously! May your body advance vigorously! O this parting, both joyful and sorrowful! In some world, like two companions accompanied by the endearing fragrance of white plum blossoms, Though the rough waves separate our bond as tightly joined as a water-tight camphor boat, By divine grace, thriving with the green of the paired pines, Kasugahime of burgeoning spring, Kasugahime of burgeoning spring! Joyful is today's sky as I wait, Joyful upon the waves as I wait! Utterly forgetting the weariness of the journey Of extraordinary propagation, Beholding each other's faces in the world of Miroku— Precious indeed is the reign of the pine! Precious indeed is the God of Miroku!'"

Thus singing, she reluctantly lingered over the unquenchable parting.
