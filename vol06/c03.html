Chapter 3: Quick Wit and Curious Wonders [253]

§ 1

Accepting the request of the father and son, Darumahiko, while somewhat enveloped in feelings of unease, leisurely mounted the back of the horse, and as the horse neighed valiantly, he advanced deep along the mountain path.

This was the hideout of Miyamahiko of Mount Kijōzan, and the one calling himself the father who had now lured the missionary back was Onikumahiko, a subordinate of Miyamahiko. The young one was an evil deity named Onitora. After proceeding several tens of chō, they arrived at this hideout.

Looking by the light of the moon leaking from the valleys of the high mountain, Darumahiko was secretly surprised in his heart that there was an unexpectedly spacious mansion, unlike what one would expect in such deep mountains and ravines. Onikumahiko raised his voice, knocked on the gate,

"Hey! Hey!"

and called out. In response to the voice, four or five men appeared from within the gate, and making a rattling sound, opened the black main gate,

"Well, is that Onikumahiko and Onitora?"

they shouted, whereupon the two,

"Shh!"

secretly hushed them, so the man, bowing low and prostrating himself, said,

"Oh, my, what a rude thing I have said. Being the middle of the night, I mistook you for the servants Onikumahiko and Onitora; I am truly sorry, Master,"

and muddled his words. Onikumahiko intentionally raised a loud voice,

"I forgive you today, but hereafter there must be no such carelessness,"

and without even a moment's pause, Onitora followed immediately after,

"Today is the third-year memorial service of my mother, so in deference to my mother's spirit, I forgive your recent rudeness,"

he added as his words.

The two men, rubbing their hands together, faced the missionary, and

"As this is deep in the mountains, everything is inadequate; moreover, there are only mountain monkeys who know neither manners nor etiquette. Pray, do not take it to heart, and we humbly beg you to stay at your leisure,"

they spoke politely.

While Taruhiko remained on horseback, advanced within the gate, and dismounted lightly before the hitching post, four or five men appeared from somewhere, and

"Onikumahiko is a great fellow, the first spear of today! Now that things have come to this, he is just like a bird in a cage, he belongs to us!"

they blurted out, whereat Onikumahiko was startled, and

"Hey, you madmen!"

while scolding them thus, he rubbed his hands together once again and said,

"In truth, for the memorial service of my wife today, I have gathered many persons fallen by the wayside and mad persons, performing as much of a memorial service as possible; therefore, such madmen gather and utter groundless ravings. By all means, without any reserve, slowly take off your robe, cast off your straw sandals and gaiters, and please rest in the inner chamber."

When he said this, the missionary grew increasingly suspicious, and here he made up his mind, suddenly disguising himself as a deaf person. The suddenly deaf missionary, following their guidance, took a seat in a somewhat beautiful room. At this time, the bald-headed man in question, bowing politely with his head to the floor, said:

Onikumahiko: "Ah, grateful missionary, you have kindly entered this thatched cottage. As it was a lean horse, you must surely have pained your body. First, without any hesitation, since a hot spring fortunately gushes forth, please take a bath at your leisure."

§ 3

As he urged him thus, the missionary, thinking it would be disastrous to become naked, feigned deafness and remained silent.

Onikumahiko urged him to bathe again and again. Yet the deaf missionary not only refrained from answering a single word, but deliberately addressed him of his own accord,

"Ah, there is a fine brazier here; what kind of wood was this made from?"

he asked. As soon as Onikumahiko heard these words, he tilted his head and muttered to himself,

"Aha, this fellow has become deaf! It is said that when a person has no life left, their eyes expire first, but with this fellow, it has expired from his ears first. In any case, his life is only for tonight. He was completely taken in by Miyamahiko's plot; sweet promises were lavished upon him, and he was a fool easily duped; everything is safe now,"

he was muttering in a low voice.

The missionary, pretending not to hear those abusive words at all, continued to smile brightly as if feeling truly pleasant. And once again, the missionary said,

"Hey, from which mountain and from what kind of wood was this brazier made?"

Thus he asked yet again, whereupon Onikumahiko said,

"Ugh, what a nuisance! Putting on airs of being deaf, what is the use of asking me? Asking people things is what those who can hear do. I shall startle him with a hand gesture,"

and instantly plucking out his own nostril hair, he burned it in the brazier to show him, whereupon the missionary,

"Ah, I see; was it made from the tall zelkova tree over there on Mount Hanayama?"

feigned ignorance in response, whereupon Onikumahiko...

"A deaf man's wit—what amusing things he spouts!"

Thus he laughed again.

The missionary thought to tease him once, saying,

"Hey, when was this matting cover made?"

Onikumahiko said,

"Ah, what a nuisance! Not even knowing that your own life ends tonight, you nonchalantly inquire even about the matting cover. Eat my backside!"

And turning abruptly around toward the missionary, he bared his pitch-black buttocks and slapped them twice, whereupon the missionary said,

"Yeah, is that so? It was made on the second day of next month, was it? No wonder it still gives off a strong, fresh scent."

Onikumahiko was utterly dumbfounded by that wit. The missionary began teasing him yet again.

"When was this sliding door made, I wonder?"

"Ah, what a nuisance! Meddling in everything from turnips to rapeseed, my backside is appalled. Do not butt in!"

Substituting gestures for these words, he turned toward the missionary once more, bared his backside, and thrust a measuring rule into it. While this signified "with a rule," the missionary, laughing again, said:

"Yeah, is that so? It was made for next month's provisions, was it? Ahahaha!"

Thus he rolled with laughter.

At this moment, a peerless beauty gracefully opened the sliding door and entered, glanced up at the missionary with a sidelong glance, and bowed politely, but as their faces met, an expression of sudden astonishment appeared upon both of their faces. Who, indeed, could this noble beauty be?
