Chapter 34: The Three Great Religions [284]

第34章　三大教〔284〕

§ 1

Just as Higashihiko, who was proclaiming to all under heaven the teachings of Haniyatsuhiko-no-Kami at the foot of Mount Ōgon, gathered the multitude, sat upright upon a rock, and was expounding the doctrine of the Five Great Teachings, far ahead there appeared yet another strange, white-haired messenger,

"Kamunaohi, who created this world, and Ōnaohi of vast spirit,

In all things of the human world, look anew and listen anew in uprightness,

And confess and correct the errors of your own person."

Chanting thus with an engaging melody, he advanced toward this direction.

His hair was thoroughly white, and his beard hung eight handbreadths down to his chest, this too being purely white and radiating a silver luster.

This messenger was the Angel Hokkō, who was proclaiming to all under heaven the Three Great Teachings, the doctrine of Mitsubahiko-no-Kami who had appeared in the village of Tamanoi at the foot of Mount Ryōju. Moreover, this Mount Ryōju was a high mountain towering on the border between India and Tibet, and Mount Ōgon was another name for the Mount of Olives towering beside the holy land of Jerusalem.

The strange, white-haired old messenger pricked up his ears and quietly listened to the Angel Higashihiko's preaching while standing by the roadside. The Angel Higashihiko, not dreaming in the least that this messenger had approached his side and was eavesdropping on the doctrine, was expounding the doctrine of the Five Great Teachings single-mindedly, without looking aside.

The Angel Hokkō, completely forgetting even to nod in greeting to the assembly, straightened his collar and listened to this doctrine in pure absorption. The Angel Higashihiko, closing his mouth to catch his breath for a moment, looked around and was surprised to see the strange, white-haired messenger standing beside the flat rock,

"Who are you?"

he asked with a slight nod, whereupon the strange, white-haired messenger politely returned the greeting and said:

"I am a messenger of Mitsubahiko-no-Kami who dwells at the foot of Mount Ryōju. Listening just now to your exposition, I could not contain my admiration and heard it with reverence. I pray you, would you not expound the profound depths of the doctrine to me as well?"

Thus he earnestly pleaded.

Hearing that he was a messenger, the Angel Higashihiko immediately stepped down from his seat on the rock, advanced before him, and politely bowing, said:

"I am of a dull nature, possessing no ability to expound the doctrine to persons such as you. I merely pass on orally, however dimly, the divine path to believers who have come after me,"

thus showing a modest countenance, he firmly declined, and added:

"What manner of doctrine do you proclaim? I wish to hear it."

When he said this, the Angel Hokkō replied:

"These are incomprehensible words of yours. In the first place, the divine path is not something to be conveyed through the knowledge or deliberation of a divine person. Does not God expound the profound and wondrous doctrine by borrowing the mouth of the messenger?"

§ 2

When he asked back thus, Azumahiko-no-kami submitted to that reason, and said:

"I was mistaken. Though being one who day and night propagates and spreads the divine revelations of the Great Deity, I had forgotten such grave significance. Ah, how shameful!"

Seeing him thus bow his head and hide his face in his sleeve, Hokkō-no-kami felt sorry for him, and said:

"Forgive my inadequate self for my overstated and discourteous words. Out of excessive concern for the divine man, it was the height of indiscretion for me to speak such insolent words to you, a propagator. Truly, it is shameful beyond measure."

Thus reflecting in his heart, he seemed greatly to feel ashamed.

Hereupon, the two propagators stood upon the rock, sang songs of propagation alternately, recited the Amatsu Norito, and praised the vast virtue of the Great Deity of heaven and earth. And combining the propagation songs of the two, they extended them into a single song:

"The Deity appears upon the surface, To set apart good and evil. Polish your soul, reform it, And right the conduct of your body. Kaminaobi who made this world, Ōnaobi of vast spirit; Merely in all things of the human world, Look again and listen again with Naobi; Confess anew the errors of your body."

Azumahiko-no-kami gave up his seat, had Hokkō-no-kami sit comfortably upon the flat rock, and listened intently to the divine teachings. At this time, all present pressed their palms together, praised the divine virtue, and sang the propagation song with one voice. Across the faces of all, unlike before, drifted a complexion full of cheerfulness.

Kō: "I would like to ask the propagator present. From what we have heard earlier from the propagator of Mount Kogane, in this world there is a reform of good and evil, and the Great Merciful and Great Compassionate Deity will appear to aid the good, destroy the wicked, crush the strong, save the weak, and rescue us, the fallen people, into heaven. Truly, it feels to us like being reborn, and our joy is boundless. However, your deity has appeared here once again and said, 'Merely in all things of the human world, look again and listen again with Naobi, and so forth,' but what on earth does this mean? I ask you to explain it in detail. Our ancestral mountains and fields have been occupied by wicked men, our wives taken away, our dwellings burned down, with no food to eat and no house to sleep in, and parents, children, husbands, and wives have been scattered in all directions; truly, we are living in a sad world too terrible to endure. Therefore, I have become a beggar like this, traveling about the four directions, searching for the villains who burned my house and took my wife, intending to take revenge, and enduring hardship and tribulation; but if a Deity exists in this world, why does He silently look on when there is such injustice? I doubt the existence of God in this world. If there is a Deity who reforms good and evil as the previous propagator said, I wish for Him to clear this resentment as soon as possible. Yet just now, in your words, you said, 'Merely in all things of the human world, look again and listen again with Naohi,' but is this not, in short, a teaching to give up on everything? I feel that the previous deity's teaching and your teaching somehow do not agree. Please, I beg you to instruct us in detail."

§ 3

Here, Kitaterunokami said:

"God is a being of supreme goodness, supreme beauty, supreme benevolence, and supreme love. Therefore, it goes without saying that He hates evil and detests mercilessness. However, no matter how noble and wise a human being may be, when compared with the wisdom of God, one is truly shameful. In today's world, filled with many disasters and infested with demons, we must by all means rely upon the power of God, who possesses infinite and absolute power. It is only natural in human sentiment that you, having had your house burned, your forests and fields plundered, and your wife taken, seek to dispel that resentment and search all over; and we too extend our utmost sympathy. However, human beings must endure precisely that and forgive their enemies. That is the noble aspect of human beings, and that is what accords with the divine will of God."

No sooner had he heard this than Kō rose abruptly,

"Fool!"

he shouted, and opening his eyes wide like a falcon, glared at Kitaterunokami, panting for breath:

"I-I do n-not like what such a propagator spouts. You cowardly rascal! My wife was taken, my house was burned, all my property was snatched away by villains, I have no house to sleep in and no food to eat, and parent and child have been scattered apart, suffering unbearable hardships and distress—yet you know nothing of suffering and nothing of human feelings! W-what do you mean, G-God? Forgive them? What nonsense! My backside is appalled. Do not make such a fool of people! Then, I-I will pour urine over your head right now, but even so, will you n-not get angry?"

So saying, he stood behind the propagator, aimed at the head of Kitaterunokami who sat upright, and began to urinate with a gushing sound. Higashihikotenshi and all the others shouted,

"Wait, wait!"

In the meantime, the urination was completely finished.

Kitaterunokami sat smiling pleasantly.

Kō, still foul-mouthed,

"Hey, coward, weakling, bedwetter, snot-nosed brat!"

reviled him. The group said,

"Hey, are you not the one who drips urine? You just dripped urine right now. Thinking it is about another, you are uttering about yourself. Hey, will you not wipe your nose? Running with snot, it is you who are the snot-nosed urine-dripper!"

When they said this, Kō became agitated:

"What? It is not that I dripped urine! It came out on its own from the urine's side. As for me, I did not run snot; it was the snot that came out of itself!"

he retorted insolently.

Kitaterunokami remained calm and composed, continuing his lecture while covered in urine.

「おい腰抜け、弱虫、小便垂れ、洟垂れ」

と罵る。一同は、

「おい貴様が小便垂れではないか。今現に小便を垂らしたであろう。他人のことだと思って自分のことを言っているぞ。おい、洟を拭かないか。水洟を垂らしやがって、洟垂れの小便垂れとは貴様のことだぞ」

と言うと、甲は躍起となり、

「なに、俺が小便を垂らしたのではない。小便の方から出たのだ。俺は、洟など垂らしはせぬ、洟の方から出て来やがったのだ」

と減らず口を叩く。

北光天使は泰然として、小便を浴びたまま講演を続ける。

原文（近代文語）

ここに北光天使は、

『神様は至善至美至仁至愛の御方である。故に悪を憎み、無慈悲を御嫌ひ遊ばすのは云ふ迄もない。しかし人間はいかに立派な賢い者でも、神様の御智慧に比べて見れば、実に耻かしいものであります。災多く、悪魔の蔓延る今日の世の中は、どうしても無限絶対力におはします神様の力に依頼らねばならぬ。あなたが家を焼かれ、山林田畑を掠奪され、女房を取られて、その怨みを晴らさうと思ひ、諸方を尋ね廻らるるのは人情として尤もであり、吾々も満腔の同情をよせますが、併し、そこを人間は忍耐して、敵を赦してやらねばならぬのです。そこが人間の尊い所であつて、神様の大御心に叶うといふものです』

と聞くや否や、甲はムツクと起ち上り、

『馬鹿』

と叫び、かつ隼の様な眼を剥いて北光天使を睨みつけ、息をはづませながら、

『オヽ俺はコヽ斯んな宣伝使の吐かす事は、キヽ気に食はぬ。腰抜野郎奴。嬶を奪られ、家を焼かれ、悪人に財産を全部ふんだくられ、寝る家もなく、食ふ物もなし、親子は散り散りばらばらになつて、在るにあられぬ艱難苦労をして居るのに、苦労知らずの人情知らず奴。ナヽ何が、カヽ神さまだ。赦してやれも糞もあつたものかい。尻が呆れらア。あまり人を馬鹿にするない。そんならお前の頭に、オヽ俺が今小便を引つかけてやるが、それでもお前はオヽ怒らぬか』

といひながら、宣伝使の背後に立ち、端坐せる北光天使の頭をめがけて、ジヤアジヤアと放りだした。東彦天使その他の一同は、

『待て待て』

と叫ぶ。その間に小便は全部放出し了りぬ。

北光天使は莞爾として坐りゐたり。

甲は尚も口汚なく、

『ヤイ腰抜、弱蟲、小便垂れ、洟垂れ』

と罵る。一同は、

『オイ貴様が小便垂れぢやないか。今現に小便を垂れたであらう。人のことだと思つて自分の事を吐かして居らア。オイ、洟を拭かぬかい。水洟垂らしやがつて、洟垂れの小便垂れとは貴様のことだよ』

といふに、甲は躍起となり、

『なに、俺が小便垂れたのぢやない。小便の方から出よつたのだ。俺や、洟垂れアせぬ、洟の方から出て来やがつたのだい』

と不減口を叩く。

北光天使は泰然として、小便を浴びたまま講演をつづける。
