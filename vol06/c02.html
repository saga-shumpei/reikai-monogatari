Chapter 2: Tears of the Waterfall [252]

第2章　瀑布の涙〔252〕

§ 1

Mount Kijōzan, fearful even in name, was renowned as the dwelling of evil, where Bōfurihiko and Takatora built fortresses of ugly men and ugly women, committed every atrocity, and slaughtered and devoured all living creatures; the breath blown morning and evening by these crooked deities made the wind damp and reeking of blood, and the Kijō River, so vast, was dyed crimson and utterly defiled, turned like a river of blood though not hell itself, without a trace of fresh, clear water; with a great waterfall on his right, his feet aching and his body drenched in the long rain, whether the tears bursting from both eyes of his sincere heart to save this world were rain or the surging rapids, the roaring sound of water echoing like the voices of demons, giant serpents, and Magatsu deities cursing this world, the missionary scrambling up the valley of the deep mountains, trudging along under the cold sky near dusk, the pitiful remnant of Ōtarashihiko—Tarumahiko, exhausted and barely able to stand, sat heavily upon the earth to catch his breath, not like Bodhidharma facing the wall for nine years, yet he looked up at the towering cliff with agonizing gasps, the sky of this god-man's heart clouding over as he gazed at the twilight sky where black and white could not be distinguished, murmuring to himself.

Tarumahiko said, "Alas, striving to illuminate the dark world, I whipped the steed of my heart and raced about on today's journey, yet having no means to seek the dwelling of Tsukiteruhiko no Kami no Mikoto, whose whereabouts are unknown, my heart rushes like a great waterfall, with no one to fathom my cascading tears as I weepingly advance through the Tokoyo Country; at last, here is Mount Kijōzan, and should my form be spotted by an evil deity, or should Miyamano-hiko appear wielding the Ame-no-sakahoko and advance upon me, what shall I do? Alas, in what age can that thousand-year resentment be cleared away? Such is the karma of my exhausted self; suffering from hunger, dried of tears, trudging alone along the mountain path, has the departure upon which I set my heart to illuminate the common people enshrouded in clouds of delusion now turned into vain sorrow? Having no means to report the years spent in the mountains and fields to Nodate no Kami and Konohana-hime no Kami no Mikoto who dwell on Mount Tenkyōzan, may the gods of heaven and earth divine these inexhaustible thoughts of my chest, which thunders like the sound of the waterfall resembling a roar of thunder."

Just as he was lamenting his karma in this manner, a clamorous sound was heard from far ahead.

Tarumahiko abruptly raised himself, strained his ears, and while he was lost in thought wondering who it might be, there came the crisp clatter of horse hooves drawing near.

Thinking it would be disastrous to be discovered, he braced his heart and moved his weary feet, attempting to descend toward the ravine path, when from behind came what appeared to be an aged deity, shouting in a hoarse voice.

足真彦は、つと身を起し、耳を傾け、何者ならむと思案に暮るる折しも、馬の蹄の音戞々と近より来るものありける。

見附けられては大変と、心を励まし疲れし足を運びながら、渓路さして下り行かむとする時しも、後方よりは老いたる神と見えて、嗄れ声を張揚げながら、

§ 2

"Oi! Oi!"

So called out the voice. The impurity of its kototama meant that it could not be an upright deity.

Thinking that he could not bear being attacked by an evil deity in his presently exhausted state, he forced his reluctant feet to run forward step by step.

The crooked deity leading a horse caught up without difficulty. Darumahiko, cornered with no way to advance or retreat, deliberately feigned vigor, placed his hand upon the hilt of his sword, and stood poised to strike should the other approach.

At this moment, the kettle-headed old man placed both hands to the ground facing the missionary, and

§ 3

"I perceive that you are an evangelist of the realm. I have one wish. I pray that, through your compassionate heart as an evangelist who saves all people, you might grant this lifelong wish of mine."

Thus he spoke, exceedingly polite. The evangelist,

"What is this wish?"

asked in return with a somewhat tense countenance, whereupon the bald-headed man merely wiped his tears with his sleeve and remained prostrate on the ground. Among them, a somewhat younger man with a dark face and an exceedingly protruding forehead resembling a Fukusuke head, mechanically shaking his face like the head of a puppet in a puppet play while pretending to wipe away tears, said:

"I have one request of the traveler. The one prostrating here now is my father. Having unfortunately parted from his wife three years ago, he now leads a lonely remainder of his life like an old tree; as today is the death anniversary marking the third year of his wife's passing, in order to comfort her spirit, we have been seeking someone who would allow us to make offerings to travelers passing along this difficult road, dispatching servants in all directions for her memorial, aiding travelers distressed by the journey, requesting a night's lodging at our wretched thatched hut, and chanting propagation hymns before her spirit to comfort it. Yet, by what bond of past lives, we beheld your presence as an evangelist, and unable to contain our joy, without regard for discourtesy, followed your tracks and arrived here. Though a wife to my father, to me it is the three-year memorial of my own birth mother; both father and son have been awaiting the passing of an evangelist. We beg of you, please grant us your lodging for one night."

§ 4

So he earnestly pleaded, plausibly sniffling and even letting his voice grow hoarse.

Thinking that he must not be off his guard, the missionary, somewhat at a loss in thought, stood silently and was suspicious of their words.

Father and son said in unison:

"Truly, to ask a noble no-Kami such as your honor to lodge in our humble hovel is a wish far beyond our station; yet as they say, even the brushing of sleeves is karma from a previous life, and on this day of the third-year memorial service for my wife and his mother, that we should encounter a missionary whose renown is so gracious to hear, we believe without doubt that it is entirely the protection of my wife's spirit. Since this is in such deep-grassed mountains, we have no feast to offer, but we have stored in abundance the figs, the fruits of Kagu, and the mountain yams that are the specialties of Mount Kishiro; so if you would kindly grant our wish and mount this lean horse, we shall attend upon you,"

so they earnestly begged.

Because Tarumahiko had been benighted on the road with no place to lodge, and moreover his stomach was empty and his feet were weary, having reached the height of pessimism, he slightly softened his facial expression, resolved in his heart, '...Ah, let happen what may. Since the divine decree of Konohana-hime stated, "Never think of this as a solitary journey; God shall abide behind your back and protect you," this too must be entirely God's arrangement...', and immediately indicated his consent.

Ah, what manner of beings might this father and son be?
