Chapter 48: Ame no Uzume no Mikoto [298]

第48章　鈿女命〔298〕

§ 1

Once scattered and fled, the crowd gathered again like a tide at the crossroads. And they began to argue with one another, finally turning into a carnage of fisticuffs and grappling. Without distinction between friend and foe, hitting, kicking, and striking at random, it instantly turned into a street of the Avīci hell of shrieking and screaming.

The Niō in question still stood stiff and motionless at the crossroads.

Meanwhile, the attendant of Mototeruwake held them back at the top of his voice. However, the struggle grew all the more violent.

At this time, the female missionary cast off her straw raincoat and hat among the crowd, and while drawing eyes to her floral face and willow waist, she serenely swung her long sleeves and began to dance, singing by herself:

"At the crossroads of the capital of Roma, A wave of people surging forward. With dark hearts and dark bodies, Uralhiko of the darkness of eternal night, Intermingling in the wicked deeds Of Uralhime, The heart clouded, The birthday of the indistinguishable life of Motokura. A promotional song to drink and revel: An inch ahead is pitch darkness. On a dark night, demons come out. The Niō, more fearful than demons, Lies in wait at the crossroads, Whether he will wring the bare neck Of Motokurawake or not, that I do not know. Ignorance is bliss for the townspeople, Deceived by Motokurawake, Their eyebrows counted and their bottoms plundered, The tightness of their bowels is just like this, And Lord Shibugami appears.

With a sour face and clenched fists, He stands, such terror. Fearful of the lord, cooking hard rice Fearfully with a tearful face, 'Congratulations, congratulations,' Making a grim face as if his eyes would pop out, A cuckoo with feelings of vomiting blood. Without even time to breathe a sigh of relief, A rough man appeared. At his raised fists, My heart is seized with fear, oh, how fearful! My heart is seized with fear, oh, how fearful! While saying it is fearful, it is fearful, Do you know what is truly fearful? No matter how mighty their power may be, The vile spirits of the dark-hearted Motokurawake And the Niō— More fearful than those is the skin of the heel. There are yet more fearful things. The One who made heaven and earth, and the sun and moon,

And made them to protect this world, The law of God is strict. If you break the law, from that very day, You will be cast down to the Root Country, The Scorching Hell and the Water Hell, Scorching in the cauldron of hell. Without even knowing this, the wretches of today, Nothing but blind fools gathering together, Saying 'Drink and revel,' what a wretched state! An inch ahead is the chariot of fire. By the retribution of revolving causality, Before our very eyes the capital of Roma Will burn, perish, and turn to pitch darkness. After glory, the moon rises. A moon it is, but a breath expiring. Fidgeting, faltering, and lying, The Castle of Roma hardened with lies. Polishing up the original mind Bestowed by the heavenly deities, Become the divine officiant of Mototeruwake"

§ 2

The divine law of Ananai-kyō, Clear your ears and listen well. Are your ears mere wood-ears? Drifting like jellyfish, What will you do with this surging wave of people? In the surging and agitated breast, Surely with such great regret, Even for conceit, there is a limit. Riding a palanquin through the capital of Rome, Strutting about as if owning the place. What happened to your legs, you scoundrel? With a heart like that of tigers, wolves, and jackals, There is no reason the world should be governed. Drifting upon the rough waves, How will you save the grass-like populace? Blind god who cannot distinguish the primeval darkness. Though I am a frail woman, Parting the clouds of the heavenly skies, I am Izumohime, who has descended.

As the crows of Izumo caw, Do not listen carelessly, deaf god. In this blind and deaf world, No matter how splendid the divine words may be, The voice of howsoever precious a Kami Cannot be heard, nor can the divine form Be seen; yet making the soul Received from the Kami shine bright, Become an angel of Mototeruwake, Return to the heart of old, And before Tsuki-no-mihashira-no-Ōkami, Ame-no-mihashira-no-Ōkami, And Kuni-no-mihashira-no-Ōkami, Apologize and prostrate yourself in worship. Born originally from the divided soul Of the precious Ōkami, Are you not Mototeruwake? Lingering on in this vain, floating world, And strutting about the world as if it were your own— I cannot understand the mind of such a person.

Yattokodokkoi, dokkoisho, Yoitosā, yoitosa, Yoi yoi yoi no, Yoitosassa!"

With an amusing rhythm and mysterious hand gestures, she danced wildly.

Both Iwahiko and the large man, who had been standing stiffly on the street like starched cotton laundry, were dissolved by this song and, before anyone knew it, had become completely limp like konjac.

The angel Hiromichiwake turned to the female missionary and,

"Are you indeed Izumohime, of whom I have heard rumors? Since I did not know, please forgive my rudeness,"

he greeted her politely.

Just as Izumohime was politely returning the greeting, four or five persons who appeared to be officials dressed in ceremonial robes appeared before them and, bowing politely,

Official: "I am a lower-ranking official serving in the castle of Rome. I have come now by the order of the lord of the castle. Please ride in this palanquin and come to the Roman castle,"

he earnestly requested.

For some reason, the palanquin of the angel Mototeruwake turned back and fled at full speed behind.

The large man who had emerged from this crowd, clenched his fists at the crossroads, and remained stiff with his mouth open, finally reformed, became a missionary, and stood alongside Tajikarao-no-Mikoto before the Heavenly Rock Cave, being the earlier incarnation of Iwatowake-no-Kami who opened the Rock Cave.

Another name for Tajikarao-no-Kami is Toyoiwamado-no-Kami, and another name for Iwatowake-no-Kami is Kushiiwamado-no-Kami. And Izumohime, who appeared just now, is the earlier incarnation of Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto of comical humor, who performed theatrical dances before the Rock Cave and made the Kami laugh till their jaws dropped.
