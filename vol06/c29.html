Chapter 29: Nakisawame, the Weeping Goddess [279]

第29章　泣沢女〔279〕

§ 1

Kamu-Izanagi-no-Ōmikami and Kamu-Izanami-no-Ōkami united the breath of light and shade of the pure and upright heaven and earth, and by the blessing of the kototama "Su," brought forth the heavenly pillar and the earthly pillar, finished giving birth even to the deities of mountains, rivers, grasses, and trees, and gave birth to and filled the world with mankind and all living things that have breath; yet while there was no time to rejoice and take pleasure in the prospering age of the gods, mankind multiplied in numbers like the stars in heaven or the grains of sand on the beach, and the defilement of the breath of kototama that did not sound in harmony spread throughout heaven and earth and the lands in all four directions, so that the pure and upright great breath grew utterly turbid, like gathering clouds,

becoming an obstructed world. As is the norm of an advancing world, the deities of Ame-no-Iwakusunobune and Ame-no-Torifune danced madly in the skies of heaven, casting dark shadows that covered and hid the firmament, while the hearts of people became clouded and defiled with each passing day and month, transforming into a strange age of eternal darkness; Kanayamahiko-no-Kami appeared, and in the deep mountains far and near people took steel, made weapons against each other, and fought, and with the breath of prioritizing body over spirit filling the land, it became an age of Ōgetsuhime where they mutually plundered goods. Ferocious beasts ran wild here and there in the fields and mountains, and taking the lives of mankind, made them their prey;

thereupon, the deity of fire appeared, becoming Yakihayao who burned at once the mountains and fields thick with trees and grass; the world transformed into that of Kagabiko, mountains spouted fire, the earth trembled, and it became an utterly terrifying, rampaging world of Kagutsuchi. The great pillar deity of the nation beheld this state of affairs and exerted all the might of her soul, exercising her power, yet being oppressed by the fury of the fierce evil spirits, she had no choice but to depart for the realm of Yomi. Haniyashiko-no-Mikoto and Haniyashihime-no-Mikoto, who were born from her feces, painfully sought to govern the world, while Waku-Musubi, born from her urine, and Mizuhanome, who purifies the world,

these deities exerted heart and body morning and evening, until at last the noble child was born, and it became the era of Toyoukehime-no-Mikoto who saves this world. Ah, how wondrous are the works of the deities!

Izanagi-no-Mikoto, deeply lamenting that Izanami-no-Mikoto, alarmed by the turmoil of the earthly world, had fled back to the realm of Yomi—namely, the world of the subterranean depths and earth tides—reminisced over her traces and composed an august poem.

That poem:

"By the pure and noble command of Taka-Musubi-no-Ōmikami and Kami-Musubi-no-Ōkami, who dwell as the divine ancestors, we, the two pillars of male and female, stood side by side, took hold of the Heavenly Jeweled Spear, lined up upon the Golden Bridge, stirred the depths of the ocean great waters that drifted like a jellyfish with a churning sound, descended onto Onogoro Island, gave birth to eighty islands and eighty lands, and to the deities of mountains, rivers, grasses, and trees; and despite our vow to bring peace under heaven and govern the divine land, crossing the manifold tidepaths of rough brine, you departed alone for the realm of Yomi, leaving me behind all alone."

§ 2

"How shall I finely make and rule this land so as to fit the divine heart? As I now ponder and reflect deeply: ever since standing upon the golden bridge, descending from heaven to Mount Hachisu, and walking around the pillar of Tsuki to the right and to the left, and swearing oaths— what can be done with the merit of those words? Even as I weep and weep, these night sleeves wring out tears. At your head and by your back, clear the heart of me who crawls and laments, O Nakisawame of the fleeting, transient world, O Nakisawame of the transient world."

Thus he sang, lamenting the parting, and sang of the goddess returning once more to Onogoro Island. From this time, Izanagi-no-Kami parted from the goddess, became downcast for a time, and lost his heart.

However, here he reflected upon Kaminaobi and Ōnaobi, roused the courage of his aramitama, dug the steel of Mount Ame-no-Kaguyama, forged many ten-span swords by himself, and planned to strike and punish the unruly gods by force of arms.

しかし、ここで神直日・大直日に省み、荒魂の勇気を奮い起こし、天の香具山の鋼鉄を掘り、自ら十握の剣を数多く造り、荒れ狂う神々を武力をもって討ち懲らしめようとお企てになった。
