Chapter 11: An Encounter in the Mountains [261]

第11章　山中の邂逅〔261〕

§ 1

Placing her frail feet firmly, a missionary of graceful appearance, a tender maiden who came trudging through the great forest of Mount Chōhaku, where trees grew dense and it was dark even in daytime, as she pushed her way through the thickets,

"Eek!"

cried out, and collapsed upon the spot. Looking closely, it was pitiful: her foot had been bitten by a venomous serpent and pained her, so that unable to walk even a single step, she sat upon the edge of a rock, closed her eyes, and firmly compressed her lips, yet unable to endure the agony, wept secretly with labored breath; though she called and cried out, there was not a single person's shadow all around, the pain grew increasingly severe, and just as the thread of her life was about to be cut, from far across, a graceful woman's voice sang:

"Though I hear the voice of the cuckoo, it shows not its form; Hiding my form in the mountain depths, Pushing through the thickets all alone, Explaining and teaching the divine revelation of Mount Tenkyō To the evil spirits that block the way Upon the mountain ridges and in the river shallows, At last here on the mountain path of Chōhaku I have advanced deeply."

Haruhime, pure of heart, fully revealing a face like the flowers of spring's third month, appeared here. Surprised to find a woman seated on a nearby rock, writhing in agony, and regarding the salvation of all divine beings under heaven as the sacred and noble vocation of a missionary, she ran to the woman's side, stroked her back, comforted her, and devoted herself single-mindedly to nursing her.

The woman appeared to have her pain somewhat alleviated, and at last lifted her face, saying:

"I know not who you are, but I am a woman traveling alone, and while pushing my way through the grass, I was bitten by a dread venomous snake; just when I, a frail woman, had no means whatsoever to help myself, this unexpected kindness has come—what god's salvation may this be? I am deeply grateful."

When she looked upon her, lo and behold, it was the missionary Haruhime, who had faithfully served in the castle of Moscow.

Kasugahime: "Ah, are you Haruhime?"

Haruhime: "Ah, were you Lady Kasugahime? To behold your face in an unexpected place, and to meet in such a grass-grown mountain, is indeed the arrangement of a merciful God; ah, how thankful, how awe-inspiring."

Saying so, forgetting all else, they grasped each other's hands, and their tears of joy were such as could be compared to a sudden evening shower.

At such a juncture, a rough man appeared, and no sooner did he see the two than he fled headlong, looking back again and again, and hid himself toward the forest yonder; but after a short while, that previous wicked man reappeared at this place together with four or five suspicious men.

Otsu grew furious like a raging fire, rolled his raccoon-dog-like eyes, and panted.

Hei: "Ahahaha! The ladle-tanuki with his rolling pin, black eyeballs like a grey starling, a brown-eared bulbul, an albatross—there is no medicine to cure a fool!"

Tei: "Oh, oh, that word 'medicine' reminded me. I happen to be carrying medicine for spasms right now. Shall I present this to that lady?"

Kō: "You are a man weak for women; why are you so tender-hearted whenever you see a woman? With a face like a yam like yours, no matter how much you flatter them trying to win the favor of the goddesses, it is useless. If you receive an outright rejection, that cheek of yours will truly be a fine laughingstock."

Tei: "Cheek or whatever, leave it alone. If I have her take this medicine, that woman will surely recover completely, break into a smile, and revealing snow-white teeth from those graceful lips, she will say, 'Though I know not who you are or whence you come, for this woman suffering and lost in these mountains, how could I ever forget this kindness in any lifetime? Ah, how joyful, how dear you are!' and extending her white arms, she will come clinging, never to let go. Then I will retort, 'Well, well, incomprehensible noble lady, what is the meaning of your embracing a rough fellow like me, who looks like a yam?' Then that woman, putting on a face like plum blossoms fragrant in the morning sun, will reply, 'No, no, even if your face is like a yam, you are the parent of my life; please cherish me for a thousand years, for ten thousand years.'"

Kō: "Fool!"

Thus he roared loudly.

Tei: "What do you mean, 'fool'? Captivated by the sight of a beautiful woman, you untied even the cords of your face covering, lowered your spatula-shaped eyebrows even further, and showed your octopus-like bald head; yet no matter how eccentric a woman might be, she would not give even a single glance to such an earthen-teapot octopus-bald head. Do not be so jealous; you had better consult your own visage. How utterly ridiculous!"

When he retorted thus,

Hei: "Hey, Kurokuma, you came rushing before us in a hurry saying there was fine prey, and made your report, but just what do you think this woman is? Is she not a terrible missionary? Just imagine if these women's illnesses were completely healed; she is the sort who would once again spout those head-aching, chest-oppressing words like 'three thousand worlds,' 'cuckoo,' 'shining,' or 'clouding.' You are a blind fool with your eyes wide open; someone wearing such a veil cloth is our enemy. If we were to help such a person, there is no telling what punishment we would receive from Lord Uraruhiko of Armenia. Rather, let us all gather together, beat her flat, and receive a reward from Lord Uraruhiko!"

All: "That sounds good, delightful, delightful!"

Exchanging glances and attacking from all four directions with broken sticks, Haruhime raised her clear voice,

"Kannagara tamachi haemase,"

chanting this, and,

"Plum blossoms opening all at once throughout the three thousand worlds,"

as she began to sing this, they all clutched their heads and crouched upon the earth.

Haruhime aimed her right index finger toward the heads of the four men, whereupon five-colored spiritual light radiated from the fingertip and pierced the entire bodies of the four.

All of them,

"Forgive us, forgive us!"

crying out thus, did nothing but fold their hands together and plead for mercy. At this time, once again from the depths of the mountains,

"Plum blossoms opening all at once throughout the three thousand worlds,"

with a voice singing this, echoing through the spirits of the trees, a man tall enough to pierce the clouds appeared. This was the miserable remnant of Ōmichiwake, the missionary of Hinode no Kami.

Kurokuma and the other demons were terrified out of their wits by the appearance of the male deity, and stumbling and tumbling, they fled like baby spiders scattering. Afterwards, the three looked at one another's faces, only wringing their sleeves with tears of joy.

黒熊以下の魔人は男神の出現に膽を潰して、転けつ輾びつ、蜘蛛の子を散らすがごとく逃げ去りぬ。後に三人は顔見合はせて、うれし涙に袖を絞るのみなりき。
