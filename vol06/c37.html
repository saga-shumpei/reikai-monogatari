Chapter 37: Grasping at Clouds [287]

第37章　雲掴み〔287〕

§ 1

High when looked up to, that which is praised as Toyoashihara no Mizuhochi, created by the Amatsu-no-Kami of the distant heavens, is the Five Continents drifting upon the waves of the vast sea. Handing down the teachings of Konohanahime of Tenkyōzan, born as the pivot of Hi-no-Moto in Nakatsu-no-Mikuni with divine authority, they weave the looms of damask and brocade with warp and woof. Haniyashiko and Mitsubahiko, appearing at the foot of the Golden Mountain and Mount Ryōju, and the purest gushing Mizu-no-Mitama flowing ever on to cleanse the world; consolidating the meritorious deeds of the Mizu-no-Mitama, here is Ananaikyō, the divine teachings whose fragrance of white plum blossoms opening all at once is so gracious.

Wishing to proclaim and spread them to the countries of the four directions, upon mountain ridges and in river shallows without leaving any corner, to the utmost where blue clouds hover and to the very edge where white clouds descend and crouch, to make rugged lands level and narrow lands ever wider, seeking to shine the divine light, the angel Aokumowake departs from the foot of the dazzlingly radiant Golden Mountain and advances, parting the blue clouds far away; having changed his name to Takahiko, an envoy of propagation in light attire, traversing rugged mountains and leaping across deep valleys, he comes singing in a cool and clear voice the song of propagation that awakens the people of the world. The world has turned to pitch-black darkness, and the clamor of the myriad deities

swarms and thrives like flies in the fifth month, to the extent that there is no means to govern; the mountains and rivers resound, the trees and grasses wither, and treading through mountains that have become just like a wasteland, he finally arrived at the foot of Mount Aokumo.

When the angel Takahiko finally arrived at the foot of Mount Aokumo, there were human voices of an unknown number of hundreds of people within the mountain. Wondering what was the matter, he sat down in the shade of a tree for a while and rested his breath without intentionally listening to the voices. A great number of people held handled tools in their hands and were in the middle of excavating a road that reached the mountain summit. The angel Takahiko came out from the shade of the tree and climbed up while singing the song of propagation once again. A great number of laborers raised their voices while engaging in earthworks, and,

"Drink and make merry, for an inch ahead is a dark night; after the darkness, the moon will rise. Let man drink and eat, and lie down and roll,"

singing thus, they were working while dripping with sweat. Through their midst, the angel Takahiko climbs upward while singing the song of propagation.

From among the multitude of people, one who appeared to be the headman appeared before the envoy of propagation, spreading both arms and blocking his way, and,

"Climbing this mountain is strictly forbidden,"

"Though I say there is no passage, why do you climb this mountain without permission, and what do you take this mountain for? In ancient times, Azumawake no Mikoto spread divine governance centered on this mountain as Yatsugashira-no-kami, but after the Great Deluge of the world, it was placed under the jurisdiction of Uralhiko-no-kami and Banko Shin'ō, and Azumawake no Mikoto guards this mountain as their subordinate, singing, 'Drink and make merry, one inch ahead is a dark night; after the darkness the moon appears,' letting the people of this world live carefree. And yet, what is this, singing that troublesome, stiff-necked propaganda song or whatever, babbling about overlooking and turning a deaf ear, what does that mean? Even if you say overlook it, even if you say pretend not to hear, we cannot overlook or turn a deaf ear. If you climb this mountain even an inch further, just try climbing it; I will tear off your living head!"

Thus he yelled. Although the Propagator had the scruff of his neck seized and was pinned down by this man, he was not weakened at all, feeling as if one or two mosquitoes or horseflies had settled on his shoulder. Seizing him and flinging him into midair was nothing to him, but since he could not break the precept of the divine revelation to 'look anew and listen anew,' he intentionally and submissively yielded himself to whatever the man did. Then, lowering his voice, he began to recite the divine words.

The large man's name was Kumotsukami. Kumotsukami's hand gradually became numb, and finally his entire body stiffened and hardened like a stone Jizō. Seeing this state, many laborers raised sickles, hoes, pickaxes, and rock-breaking hammers in their hands, attacking the Propagator from front, back, left, and right. The Propagator was not perturbed in the least, but sat upright on the road and vigorously recited the divine words. All the laborers who attacked and surrounded him from all directions became rigid and hardened like stone statues with their arms raised holding their hafted tools, unable to move at all, only rolling their two eyeballs black and white. The Propagator released the spiritual binding of Kumotsukami with the divine art of spirit-pacification. Kumotsukami immediately regained the freedom of his body, with tears welling in both eyes, prostrated himself upon the ground, and apologized for his insolence.

The Propagator changed his tone and said:

"According to your words just now, I have heard that Azumawake, the protector of this mountain, submitted to Uralhiko; is this indeed true? Recount it in detail, point by point."

Having been told this, Kumotsukami, clapping his large fan-like hands toward heaven so that they resounded, knelt upon the ground once again, and trembling with fright,

"I beg to speak: now that it has come to this, I will confess everything cleanly and completely without concealing anything, so please at least spare my life—"

Thus he wept bitterly as a man, so shameful was the sight. The numerous laborers all turned into stone Jizō statues, rolling only their eyes and watching this scene.

The missionary said,

"I am a missionary of Ananaikyō. Since our principle is to review and rehear all things, and to correct errors by speaking anew, I by no means consider you hateful. Each and every one of you is a beloved child of God. Since we too are children whom God loves, you and we are children of the same God, and so to speak, brothers. How could we ever oppress our brothers? God widely loves all creation. Since we are precious children of God, we must love one another and help one another. To discriminate among human beings is what God hates the most. It is the duty of human beings that you too amend your past hearts, return to your original true hearts, and as precious children of God, do good, help people, and perform deeds of supreme goodness in accordance with the divine will of God."

When he thus earnestly began to preach the human way, even Kumotsukami, who was so violent and lawless, was deeply moved by the missionary's words, and wiping away his tears, he recounted the whole story from beginning to end.
