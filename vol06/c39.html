Chapter 39: The Stone Buddha's Plunge into the Deep [289]

第39章　石仏の入水〔289〕

§ 1

The heavens were ever blocked with dark clouds, and from the continuous rain, the volume of water was increasing in the River of Shion; its bridge having been washed away, traffic to and from the village of Shionkyō was cut off, causing distress, so that the chieftain of this village, Torawaka, led many villagers and gathered a multitude of laborers like the stars in a clear sky, and they were now in the very midst of building a bridge.

The sound of water rushing down from Mount Seiun and converging from hundreds and thousands of valleys into one place was greater even than that of hundreds, thousands, and myriads of lions and tigers roaring and bellowing all together at once, and its dreadfulness was beyond compare.

Obeying the chieftain's command, many laborers stripped naked, jumped into the river, cut and carried many trees from the mountains here and there, and engaged single-mindedly in building the bridge.

The chieftain conveyed some orders to the head of the laborers and returned to his home.

From among the laborers, a large, stout man of remarkably robust frame, tall stature, and dark complexion stood up, and said:

"Hey, everyone, let us take a break, shall we?"

At these words, everyone vied to be first in gathering at the river embankment, and using grass leaves in place of tobacco, puffed out purple smoke and indulged in idle talk.

Man A: "Why on earth does this bridge collapse so often? They say it falls at least twice and at most five or six times a year, so for us people of Shionkyō, it is truly troublesome; calling it the Shion River—Four Graces—there is neither grace nor anything of the sort. It is a river of extreme hardship."

Man B: "If it is a river that does not die, it has a long life, so isn't that good?"

Man A: "You really make no sense. Look at this bridge; far from having a long life, doesn't it die once every two or three months? What a hypocritical river, calling itself the River of Four Graces. You would think the gods might be a bit more thoughtful."

§ 2

Otsu: "Do they not say, 'Change as the world will change'? With this recent rain, in this world that changes like the pools and shallows of the Asuka River of yesterday and today, that which changes not is the path of love."

Kō: "There, what nonsense are you spouting? Do not play dumb. Singing songs is no matter for singing now. If we do not finish building this bridge within ten days, we will get another terrible scolding from Azumawake no Mikoto."

Hei: "It is no use saying such unreasonable things. How on earth could such a long bridge be built over this muddy water in ten days or so?"

Otsu: "Whether it can be borne or not, it cannot be helped. We are working at it every day. The bridge was brought down by that so-called god of rain, and unexpectedly we suffer this hardship."

Hei: "This is no time for puns. The chieftain was just saying it now. Ururuhiko-no-kami of Armenia is coming to Mount Seiun. And I heard that if we do not have it built by then, there will be a terrible scolding. Day and night, even if it is crooked, we must finish building this bridge, or we will have no excuse before the chieftain."

Otsu: "What, what is this 'Armenia-Uralhiko'? Every single day, with Armenia-is-rained-upon-hiko, even our bridge was knocked down, to our great annoyance. For Armenia-is-rained-upon or raining to cross the bridge—it makes no sense at all, does it?"

Kō: "You senseless fellow. Be quiet; someone like you had better bite the rain and die."

Otsu: "Even if you say die, you spouted earlier about an immortal river, didn't you? Telling me to bite the rain and die—you are the one saying things that make no sense."

Tei: "As for the actual fact, this honorable person knows it well. All of you listen attentively and receive our oracle."

Otsu: "Oho, he has come out talking big!"

Tei: "Great or small, what does it matter? The reason it rains day after day like this is that God Uralhiko is said to have taken away the sacred golden jewel or whatever it was of Mount Seiun, and so God laments and sheds tears every day. Long ago, when Angel Kamuzumihiko provided divine protection, the weather was fine, Mount Seiun always towered into the blue clouds showing its magnificent form, purple clouds drifted over the mountain summit through the virtue of the jewel, the river water was pure and beautiful, fruit ripened, sheep thrived, and all under heaven was truly at peace; but ever since God Uralhiko of Armenia laid hands upon Mount Seiun, we have hardly been able to behold the sun, every single day the rain pours down in torrents, the sheep eat moisture-laden grass, fall sick, and die dropping one after another, the peaceful weather of wind every five days and rain every ten days has become an old dream, and there has never been such a wretched world. Whatever one may say, the essential leader has yielded to that evil fellow called Onizuka, so it is only natural that the sun is displeased. Until then, there was never a flood that broke bridges or washed away houses once in twenty or thirty years, was there? They say that Kuni-no-mihashira-no-kami, losing patience because the evil gods were so rampant, quickly departed for the realm of Yomi or some such place. It is said that Ame-no-mihashira-no-kami was left behind alone afterward to direct everything, but it is just the same as within a single house: just as the inside of a home is like darkness without a wife, so the world, too, gradually grows darker."

...he said sorrowfully.

Bo: "How can this world be governed in peace? How can this bridge be built?"

...he sang as he stood up and began to dance.

Kō said, "Fool!" and the moment he shoved Bo's shoulder with all his might, Bo plunged headlong into the river.

Bo quickly caught hold of a bridge pile, and turning once again toward the group,

Bo: "No matter what, I shall not be washed away. How can this bridge be built, how can this fellow Kō be struck down?"

...he sang while clinging to the pile and continuing his insolent chatter. At last Bo crawled up onto the river embankment like a drenched rat, stripped completely naked, and earnestly wrung out his clothes. And then, once more,

Bo: "How will my clothes dry? With it pouring this much, nothing can be done; what shall I do, oh what shall I do, a slip away from being a drowned corpse!"

...he began to dance in a carefree manner.

§ 4

This man was the reincarnation of a kappa and thought nothing of being in the water. He was a man who felt as though he had entered a hot spring in cold weather.

Bō walked briskly up to Kō's side,

"Thanks to you, I have received plenty of muddy water. There are no words to express my gratitude,"

saying this, he firmly seized Kō by the waist, dodged his own body, and leapt together into the river. Kō bubbled up like a stone Buddha cast in, sinking completely to the riverbed. The crowd of laborers was astonished and rushed about in confusion, wondering what to do. Bō clung once again to the bridge pier,

Bō: "How shall his life be saved? A stone Buddha sunk bubbling down, heigh-ho, heave-ho,"

and was singing so.

The crowd became angry, grasped the stones at hand, and hurled them aiming at Bō.

Bō instantly dove into the water, and after a short while he surfaced, holding up Kō's body in both hands. Stone missiles came falling like rain. Bō stopped the stones raining down using Kō's body. Kō,

"Ouch, ouch!"

unable to bear watching him clutching his head, making a grimace, and starting to cry, Bō rescued Kō onto the shallows, transformed into a giant turtle, floated leisurely upon the water, and once again concealed his form. What deity's incarnation might this turtle truly be?

戊『何うしたら生命が助からう、ぶくぶく沈んだ石仏、どつこいしよのしよ』

と唄ひ居る。

大勢は腹を立てて有り合ふ石を手に握り、戊を目がけて打ちつける。

戊はたちまち水中に潜り込み、しばらくすると甲の体を両手に捧げて浮き上つた。石の礫は雨のごとく降つて来る。戊は甲の体にて雨と降る石礫を受け止めた。甲は、

『あ痛、あ痛』

と頭をかかへて渋面を造つて泣き出すを見兼て、戊は甲を浅瀬に救ひ上げ、巨大なる亀と化し、悠々として水上に浮び、再び姿を隠したり。この亀は果して何神の化身ならむか。
