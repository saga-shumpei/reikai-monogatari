Chapter 8: 黒 Fox [1218]

黒狐〔1218〕

§ 1

Even past noon, Imoriwake did not return, so Otora gradually began to doubt the divine power of Kami, and rushed into Matsuhimeyakata.

Otora: 'Excuse me, are I interrupting? In the lower hall quite a rowdy uproar has broken out, but since your husband-and-wife harmony is so exceptionally fine, it seems it did not reach your ears. Well, that is quite understandable, oh my!'

Matsuhime: 'Ah, Otora-sama, thank you so much for coming. Has some urgent matter come up?'

Otora: 'Hey, you counterfeit Jiyōgihime-sama, how can you calmly say such things? What have you done with Imoriwake-sama? Did you not say that you would draw him back by daybreak at the earliest, or by noon at the latest? It is already nearly the hour of the horse, and yet there is not even a sign of Imoriwake-sama!'

Matsuhime: 'Ah, is that so. You must be feeling anxious. I say, Matsuhiko-sama, what could have happened to Imoriwake-sama?'

Matsuhiko: 'Well now, that depends on Otora-sama's repentance. If someone gets pinched a second time or has their nose twisted, anyone would become stubborn, you see.'

Otora: 'Hey, you counterfeit Matsudaisama, what did you say to me? Telling such lies, is it right for someone who serves the work of Kamisama? Until this very moment I deeply believed that while other people's shiyugojin might be false, Matsuhime-sama was Jiyōgihime and you were undoubtedly Matsudaisama, but if you say such things, I cannot help but doubt both Matsudaisama and Jiyōgihime!'

Matsuhime: 'Ho ho ho ho! What a difficult face you are making, Otora-sama. Although I say I am Matsuhime, since you people arbitrarily attached the character for pine to me, there is no doubt I am the spirit of Matsudaisama's wife. That being said, was it not you people who gathered around and elevated me as the living shrine of Jiyōgihime? I certainly never proclaimed myself to be Jiyōgihime from my side. Even if you now call me a fake or the real thing, it has no relation or responsibility to me whatsoever, does it?'

Otora: 'Such a matter can be heard afterward. Once and for all, what on earth has happened to Imoriwake-sama? Make a clear confession whether he is returning today or tomorrow.'

Matsuhime: 'Ho ho ho ho! To tell me to make a confession as though I had hidden him or something is quite amusing. Is it not fine even if such a drunken man does not show his face for two or three days? If he cannot fit in with worldly society anywhere and does nothing but drink, any eccentric person would lose affection and abandon him. In that case, he has no choice but to return here, does he not?'

Otora: 'If he went out without money he might come back, but no matter what anyone says, he had nine thousand ryo of gold with him, so with that money he is likely wandering about everywhere with that woman Otami, you know.'

Matsuhiko: 'They say that no matter how much you wander, if you live in idleness even mountains will disappear, so once the money is gone he will be able to return. No matter how lavishly he spends it, nine thousand ryo will last Otami and her husband twenty or thirty years without a problem, so well, why don't you wait until then?'

Matsuhiko: 'Heh heh heh heh!'

Otora: 'Hey, Matsudai-sama, what is so funny? When I am worrying this much, are you laughing at me? Is my sorrow funny to you?'

And she attempts to snap at him.

Matsuhime: 'Hearing the circumstances, it is regrettable, but after all, since this is rust arising from your own self, what can be done? Collecting money by having believers who seem to have a little gold shed tears of joy after bestowing upon them non-existent names such as Minister Rintōbiten, the Kamisama of Five-Six-Seven Fulfillment, Toyosaka Noborihime of the Morning Sun, Iwateruhime, and Kiso Yoshihime—why would such money ever become truly yours? Imoriwake-sama went away taking that gold because he intended to take away your sins. In short, Imoriwake-sama and Otami-sama are your sin-removers, rescuing boats, and benefactors of your life, so you should rejoice. Viewed from the standpoint of true faith, Otora-sama, you have received quite a wonderful blessing, have you not?'

Otora: 'How ridiculous, where on earth is there such divine grace? I shall hereafter pursue Imoriwake's footsteps, recover the gold, and I will not be satisfied unless I utter my grudges.'

Chōsaibau: 'Ohohoho, your grudges may not take effect well. If it were the words of pure and immaculate you, even nails might take effect, but as you are companions who know each other's weak points, it will end up in a husband-and-wife quarrel that even dogs would not eat. Besides, Otami-sama is a beautiful person in the prime of her youth, and you-sama are an old hag, if I may say so, who has made a fifty-year-old bottom; naturally, whoever is a fickle person would take the side of Otami-sama. Isn't it a bit too audacious of you, at your good age, to try to monopolize the love of Imoriwake-sama? You, what has happened to that nose of yours? Isn't it bursting? Most likely you tripped and hit it last night when you were chasing him. Doesn't the teaching of Kamisama also show that if you do not repent, a thing will come to pass where you must strike your nose? You have received practical education and obtained splendid divine grace, haven't you? I truly feel envious of you.'

Otora: 'Hmph, do not make a fool of me, leave off making a laughingstock of people at a reasonable point. Since even this Otora is not a stone Jizō or a doll, she has at least a bit of conscience. You have met a husband called Matsuhiko-sama and have been blessed with a child, so you are happy, aren't you, and you are also strong in spirit. That is why you can say such bold things. Just try putting yourself in my shoes.'

Matsuhiko: 'Moshi Otora-sama, how would it be if you gave up thinking about Imoriwake-sama at a reasonable point now? And if you simply must have a husband no matter what, I will arrange a suitable man for you.'

Otora: 'Hmph, do not make too much a fool of me, I am not making a fuss because I want a man. It is simply because Imoriwake-sama is one who must not be lacking for the sake of Kamisama and for the sake of the people of the world, that I am putting my mind on edge for the sake of all-under-heaven. Making a fifty-year-old bottom, would I ever need a man? If you take me to have such a weak soul, hmph, it makes my sides ache a little.'

Chōsaibau: 'Ah, is that so? That is why you occasionally perform various bizarre farces such as sake bottles dancing, wine cups shattering, twisting noses, and fainting.'

Otora: 'Heh, say whatever you please and go on. Enjoy yourselves cozily and harmoniously as husband and wife, farewell; it would not do for a long-dreary old hag to soil the scene of a joyful weeping of a long-awaited reunion. I have been a terrible nuisance. Since I am a dull-witted old hag, please forgive me.'

Just as she was venting her venom like this, the one who came up briskly was Oku.

Oku: 'Pardon me, Otora-sama, or rather, Mother-sama, is she here, I wonder?'

Otora: 'Hey Oku, what on earth are you coming to such a place for? Come, go back, go back; even though you are not of age, you are precocious, coming right away to listen to my private talk.'

Oku: 'It is not that I particularly wanted to come and listen, but since you are always getting drunk on sake with Imoriwake-sama and having a loud jealousy quarrel, I came along anxiously wondering if you had come here to burn with jealousy again. Mother-sama is good at jealous fits, after all.'

Otora: 'Go home quickly.'

Oku: 'The living shrine of the Gojifu-nijūshi-ōchi-sumera-no-mikoto and the living shrine of Hinodewake no Yoshikohime have dashed out of the great hall, and even Spring-sama has followed behind them, descending the Kototama-daradara Slope, going to the base of the Shinsei Pine, pulling up sixteen pine trees, and trying to shatter the rocks, or so I heard, so I came to let you know for a moment.'

Otora: 'Even if you pull up pine trees, you can just replant them. No matter how strong Ofuku-sama or Take-sama are, that rock will not budge an inch, so just leave it alone. More than that, go home quickly.'

The lover whom she forgets not even in her dreams,

At the very moment she had grown weary of waiting,

Hearing that Imoriwake had returned,

Otora leaped up in joy,

Her face, once like Enma,

Instantly transformed into Jizau,

Turning toward Matsuhiko and his wife,

She chattered on about rude things,

And apologized for her loose tongue,

'Now, now, Okiku, you stay here,

And be taken care of for a while,' she said,

While saying it would be unsightly,

To have their Naishiyōbanashi overheard,

Not knowing he was a fox spirit,

Believing him to be her truly beloved man,

She stepped over the narrow Kaidan,

Skipping two or three steps at once,

Dizzy and tumbling down with a crash,

Hitting her head in the Hiyōshi.

Saying 'Ouch, ouch, ouch,'

Having had her heart stolen by the man,

She paid no mind to the pain in her head or waist,

And went down as if rolling,

In any case, she went to the Kyōsoden,

And poured out all her Fusoku to the returned Imoriwake-sama,

Extorted money to this side,

Making it so he could not move,

Monopolizing the man's love,

Whispering that she must guard him,

So that this joy would not go elsewhere,

And luring him with sake,

So that he would not go outside at all.

When she returned, there was Imoriwake,

Sitting composedly in front of the brazier,

Still wearing his tanzen, how happy she was.

Otora: 'My, my, my, you have remembered your home and returned. I thought, who could this be? That calm face of yours.'

Imoriwake: 'I had not intended to return, but there was one thing I could not give up, so I turned back. Well, bring me some sake.'

'The thing you could not give up must be this warmed sake bottle and cup. Come, come, if you drink in a place like this, I am sorry if you get into a bad mood again, so hurry and go to the fox forest and have a good time with Otami.'

'It is troublesome for you to harbor such malice. That woman Otami, when crossing a single-log bridge, panicked and fell into the river, and that was the end of her.'

『思ひ切れないものとは、此燗徳利と猪口でせう。サアサアこんな所で飲んで貰ふと、又御機嫌をそこねるとすみませぬから、早く狐の森へでもいつて、お民とシツポリやつて来なさい』

『さう悪気をまはして貰つちや困るぢやないか。お民の奴、一本橋を渡る時、あわてて川へおち込み、それきりになつて了つたのだ』

§ 4

Otora: 'What, Omin... fell into the river and died, you say? Ah, how pleasing... no, no, how pitiful a thing indeed. You must have been so sad, too. Why did you not jump in and commit double suicide together, how terribly aloof and like a stranger you are acting.'

She spoke with an aloof and formal air.

Imoriwake: 'What can I say, Magahiko has done a regrettable thing. If Magahiko hears of this, he will surely regret it; what a pitiable fellow.'

Otora: 'The person who regrets it must be different, hey, what are you talking about. We are not some umisenyamasen who would be deceived by such a thing, you know. Even if you tried to deceive an old tanuki baba who has passed through far, far greater trials, it would be useless. By the way, Imoriwake-sama, what have you done with the money?'

Imoriwake: 'The money, you see, because I was in such a panic, I dropped it scattering down from the top of the itsuponbashi. I thought about trying to pick it up, but somehow with the force of a yashiya, though I know not what old hag she was, she came chasing after me, so I became suddenly frightened and fled all the way to the grove in the nonaka.'

Otora: 'It is useless to tell such a lie. Is it not right there in your bosom?'

Imoriwake: 'That is there, but this is money I picked up; your money was dropped once, and even if you say you want to pick up what was dropped, it is no use. Since Imoriwake has newly picked it up, the right of ownership has shifted to me. I will not even let you touch a single finger of it anymore, so do not harbor any lingering attachment to this money. Because it is the preparation money for that time, you see.'

Otora: 'Calling a thief bold is just like you. No matter what, no matter how, I cannot let it be unless I snatch it over here.'

Imoriwake: 'Ha ha ha ha, if you can take it with your power, try and take it. This Imoriwake is a bit different from how he has been until now, so if you carelessly even touch a single finger, you will meet with a terrible experience.'

Otora: 'Now, if you keep dilly-dallying, shall I twist your nose again? Without saying such things, honestly produce it. If you hand it over when I need it again, I will give it out without regret, so today do partake of some sake and rest soundly from early evening. Because you went flying out, a great commotion has broken out in this shinkan. The faith of the believers is beginning to waver, and it is a life-or-death situation where we do not know whether this castle can be held or not, so at the very least I cannot help feeling anxious unless I have this money. I will let you keep just a thousand ryo, so return the eight thousand ryo to this side.'

Imoriwake: 'If so, it is already a nuisance, so I will give you just one-tenth of it. Now, examine and receive it.'

What he thrust forward with a pop was a grand koban worth thirty thousand ryo. Grandmother Otora was startled and said,

Otora: 'Hey Imoriwake-sama, is this, oh my, thirty thousand ryo, is it not?'

Imoriwake: 'Un, it is thirty thousand ryo, and I still have twenty-seven thousand ryo in my bosom. If I have just this much, I can live at ease playing for a lifetime.'

Otora: 'That money, let us keep it in custody over here.'

Imoriwake: 'Your money was nine thousand ryo, and did I not add twenty-one thousand ryo in interest? You too should be satisfied with just that, as I have the right to use the twenty-seven thousand ryo, so I will by no means hand it over. If you were to take this by force, that truly would be robbery.'

Otora: 'I am by no means saying I will take it, I am saying we will keep it in custody. Since it is perilous to let you keep this money, I am saying we will keep it.'

Imoriwake: 'With this money, the truth of the matter is that I have left Omin in custody at a certain place. Omin has also been provided with twenty thousand ryo of money. Now, I will take my leave from here; Grandmother Otora-sama, please live in good health, farewell.'

As he tried to stand up, Otora was startled and stood up, spreading her arms wide,

Otora: 'Ah, hearing that, no matter what you say, I will not let you go. Even risking my life, I will never hand you over to Omin!'

At the very moment she lunged at him like a warrior, Imoriwake's body was covered in long hair. The instant she started in surprise and let go of her hands, Imoriwake's figure was nowhere to be seen, and a great fox resembling a black calf went scampering away, looking back over its shoulder toward the forest yonder. This was Grandmother Otora's fukushugoshin, and the ringleader who might be called the initiator of Shōkitayama. Overawed by the divine might of Matsuhiko, Matsuhime, and Gosankō, it had completely detached itself from Otora's physical body.
