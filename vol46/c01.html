Chapter 1: 榛並樹 [1211]

榛並樹〔1211〕

§ 1

Though the yamamizu destined eventually to become the sea may for a while flow hidden beneath tree leaves,

The teachings of Kuniharutachi no Ōkami, who has appeared as the Mioya of Ametsuchi that created and solidified this world, to save the yobito—

To the utmost limits where the aokumo tanabiki in the ama no misora, and where the shirakumo oriimukafu, the wind of heiwa sweeps furiously, and the rain of jinji falls incessantly.

Wishing to bestow the dew of blessing upon the hitogusa everywhere across the four quarters of Ametsuchi, down even to a single leaf of grass, the Mikage of the sun ascending in radiant glory, the shadow of the moon traversing the ōzora, and the countless glittering stars,

Dispatch the sendenshi to guide the yobito, and proclaim the kyō of ananahi throughout tenka.

Yet, because the spirits of magatsuhi are likewise the miko of Kami,

As they manifest from the miiki of darkness and light, if one views them from the vast and venerable eyes of the Sumeragami,

All living beings within the universe are the miko of Kami; how could the heart of jinji ever change? As days pass and the moon races onward, and as the stars shift, the Yamata no Orochi and ugly deities appear here and there, and deplorably propagate the teachings of light birth and heavy death in all directions.

Brahmanism, Uralism, and Uranai-ism each expand deviant teachings in accordance with the direction of souls under the sway of body-master and spirit-subservient, and it is truly detestable how they bewilder shinjin who are the miko of Kami.

Receiving the succession of Takahime's administration,

Leaving Kitayama Village, confining themselves to the mountain of Kokita, and preaching the incoherent kyō doctrines with an air of profound grace to foolish people ignorant of reason,

They gradually built shrines and kyō arenas here, establishing Imoriwake as the kyōshu, alongside Magahiko, Otora, Bunsuke, and a hundred other executives,

Driven by minds utterly lost in delusion, believing it to be for the sake of Kami and the world, they unremittingly spewed forth wild words like a cracked sake bottle pouring murky dregs, intoxicating, clouding, and muddying the souls of the yobito,

Believing and deluding themselves that this was the world's sole mioshihe, just like the blind and deaf, they advance recklessly without even caring for their own bodies.

Imoriwake, his soul shattered by the crooked deities, day and night boasting of being a fleshly temple for the entering and exiting of Kami, drank himself blind with sake,

And with a tongue so thick he could not articulate, he dragged countless men and women down to Ne no Kuni and Soko no Kuni.

The utterly ignorant followers, without realizing it was the blind leading the blind, turned themselves into the blind as well, and believing it to be a venerable path, amid tears of joy, unsuspectingly and wholeheartedly plunged downward into the dark realm.

Wishing to save this tragic state, revering the sacred words of Kami,

Led by Manpō and Gosanpō, who serve Kuniwakewake, the sendenshi of Ananahi Kyō,

And Matsuhiko, Aku, Taku, and Teku, four followers of Brahmanism,

Entangled by a mysterious cord at the foot of the single-plank bridge over Kajikagawa in accordance with the will of Kami,

When they climbed up to the shrine on Mount Kokita, the crooked deity felt an unaccountable dread and terror, and gradually fled away;

And the gold foil that adorned Imoriwake, Magahiko, and Obaa-san Otora instantly peeled off, how amusing that they staged such an unexpected and ugly spectacle!

Imoriwake could not forget Takahime for even a single moment,

His chest scorched by the flames of love, and in order to dispel his melancholy,

Knowing full well it was poison, he recklessly and unreasonably sought solace in sake—how pathetic!

Diligent in lifting his chopsticks,

He decided that Otami, who came from the village of Kinugasa once again, was the absolute ultimate nice person, and secretly from Obaa-san Otora,

Just as they began exchanging amorous glances with one another,

Matsuhiko-sama and Kumagō suddenly appeared here, and with nowhere left to turn, watching for a gap in Otora's vigilance,

They entrusted the money she had saved as dearer than life into their bosoms, took beloved Otami by the hand,

And vanishing secretly into the darkness toward Zuitokuji, disappeared like white waves.

Obaa-san Otora flew into a rage, disheveling her hair with the ferocious intensity of Asura-ō,

And with a croaking, toad-like voice that made the Kototama turbid, shouted at the top of her lungs:

'Tucking up her skirts along the slope, wherever that scoundrel Imoriwake may be lurking, I'll track him down, grab him by the nape of the neck, make him spit foam, twist his nose to my heart's content, and how can I fail to vent my grudge? And as for that scoundrel Otami, if I were to forgive her, it would be the ruin of my body; with both money and love utterly disturbing the depths of my liver and turning my wits upside down,'

Crossing the single-plank bridge over the clear waters of Kajikagawa, she fled into the grove amidst the fields.

Giving chase to the fleeing pair—as ill luck would have it, when Imoriwake had fled earlier, he had tied a rope to the tree trunk on one side of the road, which swept the feet right out from under the pursuing Obaa-san Otora.

Thinking to make her blow bubbles, in the very trap he had set, frail though it was she fell, and at the moment of her tumble she struck her nose, groaning "un, un, un," and fainted away, which could not be helped.

Matsuhiko, remaining behind, gazed upon the retreating figures of Otora-basama and Imoriwake, and dispatching three men, Isokō, Mankō, and another, caused them to pursue the pair.

Moreover, Magahiko, ever since the figure of Otami whom he loved had vanished, even should Otami scale the heavens or board the underground railway and hide at the ends of nowhere, declaring he would search her out without fail, became frantic, and with rough breath puffing "tonton," likewise pursued after them.

Such a bizarre commotion the unconcerned moon, upon the mountain edge, casting a light like a sharp sickle, looking down from afar upon the earth, watched with a broad smile.

Isokō, Mankō, and the other three, by the command of Matsuhiko-sama, likewise hitching up their skirts, sweating with might and main to search out the whereabouts of the three, causing the great earth to resound "dondon-don" as if shooting an arrow, making the single-plank bridge groan and bend under the crescent moon, crossed perilously over the vast moor, taking the grove amidst the fields as their mark, and while bathing in the moonlight, laughing with a "geragera-gerasa," marched forth and advanced—how comical! Ah, Kannagara kamigami!

This entertaining tale, without omitting or dropping a single detail, pray let us tell, Kannagara, before the august presence of Tsukiteruhiko, we humbly and reverently beseech.

The party of Isokō pursued the heels of the fleeing Otami, Imoriwake, Otora, and Magahiko, at last crossed the single-plank bridge, advanced northward for about two or three chō, and came as far as the shade of the trees standing in rows on both sides of the road of hazel trees. An uncanny voice was heard groaning "un, un."

Mankō: 'Hey, everyone, it seems Otora-basama is planting potatoes, and she is straining herself and groaning "un, un," isn't she? She certainly seems like an unlucky old hag. Why don't we stop the "un" exercise around here for a moment?'

Aku: 'In accordance with the words of Nakashita-sensei, let us halt here for the time being. Somehow, in the shade of the hazels, it is not quite clear, but there seem to be two people.'

Isokō: 'That's probably Imoriwake-sama coming along here with her, how convenient that is! Hey, hello there, master of the Urainai Teaching, though you have a splendid house, how is it that you have come out to such a place out of sheer eccentricity and are taking a sound sleep? Come on, get up, get up!'

And when they drew close and peered well into the face, it was Magahiko. Magahiko had stumbled over the fallen body of Otora-basama, caught his foot here, turned a somersault, struck his knee severely, endured the pain, and was only just letting out his breath with a faint "un, un." Otora-basama had also once fainted, but being stepped upon by Magahiko she came to with a start, and pressing her battered nose with both hands lay fallen upon the ground, so she had not noticed that Magahiko had collapsed beside her.

Taku: 'My, my, this is a mismatched couple. Now, Magahiko-sama, it's just me, but if Imoriwake-sama were to lay eyes on you, he would surely fly into a terrible rage! Lying pillow to pillow with Otora-sama, a bed of grass and a quilt of stars, there's a limit to such eccentricities! Come on, get up, get up!'

Magahiko had firmly believed Otora-basama to be Otami, and with all his might had been gripping her hem and stroking her knee with one hand, but hearing that it was Otora-basama, he uttered a somewhat crestfallen and disappointed voice,

Magahiko: 'Ah, what, mistaken identity? Where on earth has that wretch Otami gone off to? She must have eloped with Imoriwake. If I don't chase after her to the ends of the earth and grab her, my manhood is compromised! Now, Otora-sama, you grab Imoriwake, twist his nose thoroughly, and make him give up thinking about Otami! If you do that, it's fine for you and fine for me, and there will be no lingering grudges or hatreds—there is no such sensible plan as this!'
