Chapter 11: 変化 Kami [1221]

変化神〔1221〕

§ 1

Mankō: 'No sooner had Akukō-sama climbed onto the endai than the revealing of the secrets of the Kogitayama Hegure Jinshiya, unflinching and unblinking, in a tautau stream before the eyes of amata shinjiya, backfired and sparked a massive ōsaudō, turning things upside down; and as the ranchiki commotion began, Takekō, whom people and he himself believed to be the ikimiya of the Mirokujiyōjiyu, came rushing forward with the fierce momentum of a shishi, while Akukō, using Taku as a gisei, nimbly slipped out of that place, whereupon Mankō alone went around inspecting the Hegure Jinshiya ichiichi, and stood stock-still right in front of the shrine at the chiyōjiyō before the Moon Ōkami and Sun Mikami.'

Mankō: 'Imoriwake-sama being manipulated by a dogitsune—enshrining such a kami with all sorts of motsutai, there is a limit to acting like a baka. When you look at the faces of the believers who come out, deceived by foxes and tanuki, not a single one of them is any good; wretches missing an eye, the deaf, the crippled, haibiyō sufferers, yokone, kansau, bone aches, testicular swelling, and those suffering from ringworm—rubbish humans swarming out like hatching spider eggs, hitori of them of no use in this world, clumping together is utterly laughable. That is why, having built a shrine for such rubbish, an old fox, an old tanuki of an eight-mat spread, giving it a grand-sounding name, ancestral Hinodewake, Heavenly Kami,'

Mankō: 'Yoshikohime and Tokoyohime, reverently enshrined as commander-in-chief, how utterly ridiculous it is to worship them. Though they call it a world of darkness, I never knew there were so many fools in this world. Well, from now on, from now on, if we completely strip off the cat skin that Matsuhiko-sama and Matsuhime were wearing, expose the true nature of the magatsukami, and open the eyes of the people, how astonishing it would be! Thanks to the fact that my, Mankō's, mind is sound, it is a wonder that I am not deceived by the magatsukami of foxes and tanuki.'

Mankō: 'It is truly pitiable that people of the world—whether noble, wealthy, lowborn, or humble—let their hearts be dazzled by greed, unconsciously straying into delusion, and being made playthings by the magatsukami. When I think of this, unless we spread the Mikkyō teachings as quickly as a single day, make them the light of this dark world, illuminate the dark night, and save it, the three-thousandfold world will instantly turn into a desolate wilderness. Ah, immaculate Kami, grant us your divine blessings.'

At such a place, Okiku came walking briskly up.

Okiku: 'If it is you, Mankō-sama, you have no idea how long I have been searching for you since earlier. What on earth were you doing all by yourself in such a cold place?'

Mankō: 'Having been given the cold shoulder by you and scolded by Takekō-sama, I had nowhere to put myself, so I came all the way to the front of Yulahiko-sama's shrine to take refuge. Why on earth did you, too, climb such a steep mountain all by yourself?'

Okiku: 'I too have legs. Besides, since my sweethearted Mankō-sama is here, my legs felt unexpectedly light, and before I knew it, I climbed up here.'

Mankō: 'Don't be ridiculous; even though you're not of age yet, is there such a thing as flirting with a man? You certainly gave me a terribly rough time.'

Okiku: 'Well, that goes without saying. In front of my parents or other people, no matter how much I like you, can I make a fond face? Because I was embarrassed, I said I hated you.'

Mankō: 'And yet, didn't you put me to great shame last night? At that time, even though nobody was there, wasn't there far too much deliberate intent in it?'

Okiku: 'What on earth are you talking about? Even then, in the gloom, Aku, Teku, and Taku-sama were hiding and listening to our conversation standing face-to-face, you and I, were they not? That is why I said such a thing.'

Mankō: 'Indeed, that was so. You certainly pay attention to the tiniest details.'

Okiku: 'Of course I do; if we don't watch our step before and behind and get discovered by people, it would be a disaster, you see.'

Mankō: 'What of it even if we are discovered? We are going to become husband and wife anyway.'

Okiku: 'Even so, if people call us a wild-matched husband and wife, it would be a disgrace for generations to come.'
