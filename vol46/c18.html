Chapter 18: エンゼル [1228]

エンゼル〔1228〕

§ 1

While Otora and Magahiko, like a dog and a cat, were watching each other's gaps and scattering sparks in an intense and fierce debate, amid a dispute of humanity and justice that was at its height, a beauty like an angel descended. Needless to say, this was Ochiyo. Ochiyo quickly bounded before the two, joined her hands, uttered a sharp cry, leaped about three cubits into the air, and sat down properly and neatly before them. Both Otora and Magahiko, struck by the dignified and somehow graceful figure of the maiden, unconsciously bowed their heads, placed their hands on the ground, and showed reverence.

Chiyo: 'I am none other than Yurarihikonomikoto. Knowing that you two have been engaged in a debate for some time regarding the good and evil, right and wrong of the deity of Kogitayama, I have descended from Tenkiyokushibikiu to awaken you both from your deluded dreams; listen with reverence.'

She proclaimed this solemnly. Otora was struck with a sense of unexpectedness and, wondering whether it was true or false, awaited the divine decree. Magahiko thought to himself... Look here, Otora-san, please see; as I expected, the Yurarihikonomikoto whom I believe in is a true god, so please use the descent of this angel to awaken from all your deluded dreams... Although he did not say it aloud, he was expecting it in his heart.

Magahiko: 'This is truly Yurarihiko-sama, you have descended so graciously. The truth of the matter is that I came into conflict with Otora-san regarding matters of the gods and points of faith, and we were engaged in a debate with one another. I earnestly ask you to grant a clear and lucid proclamation.'

Angel: 'Magahiko, since I have descended to dispel your agony, you need no reserve or hesitation; ask whatever question you will.'

"Then, taking advantage of your words, I would like to ask: Otora-san says that the deity enshrined here at Kogitayama is a nominal existence without reality, but how is it in actuality? If it is a god, I want you to tell me that it exists. If it is not a god, if you tell me that it is not, I will make my decision on my future course based on that."

"I shall prove that the countless large and small divine spirits enshrined in this Kogitayama are indeed entities that exist in the universe. The Great Deity of Iba Shrine, the Great Deity of Rindōbiten, the God of Isokon Achievement, the Great Deity of Kiso Yoshihime, the Great Deity of Asahi no Toyosaka Noborihime, the Great Deity of the Earthly World, the Great Deity of Hinomaruhime, the Righteous Heavenly Hinode God, Tamasuruhime, Taishōgun, Tokoyohime, the Great Deity of Hegure Shrine, the Great Deity of the Everlasting Sun King of Heaven, the Great Deity of Kamiyoshihime, and various other miscellaneous enshrined deities are certainly existing gods—this I certify."

Magahiko, rejoicing wildly, shot a sidelong glance at Otora, and with a smug look on his face,

Magahiko: 'Well, Otora-san, how do you like that? Was my view mistaken after all?'

He showed off a somewhat triumphant expression.

Otora: 'Well, as long as they are enshrined, the divine spirits must be there.'

Magahiko: 'There, look at that! Then there is no harm in serving them morning and evening, is there?'

Angel: 'Though we call them all gods, do we think them alike? / Some are birds, and some are insects. / Good gods and crooked gods alike, / Throughout heaven and earth, we call them all gods.'

Otora: 'Thank you very much. Look here, Magahiko-san, there is no doubt they are gods, but among the gods there are one hundred and eighty-one grades, so we must consider that point.'

Magahiko: 'I ask the Angel once again. I have heard that the deities enshrined at Kogitayama are the principal gods who, from the First Heavenly Kingdom upward, protect the earthly world; there can be no mistake about that, can there?'

Angel: 'Though many a palace is built in Kogitayama, / The enshrined gods dwell at the crossroads. / The gods wandering at the crossroads are still foolish, / Being foul deities of the nethermost land. / Yet since man is the ruler of heaven and earth, / It is also proper to save the defiled gods. / The true god who protects the world / And safeguards the human soul is not this god. / This god is one who has enshrined / The crooked spirits that committed sins and defilements. / Rather than worship, save them, Kogitayama, / Pitying the plight of the enshrined gods. / Though I proclaimed myself the Prince of Yurari, / It is solely to save Magahiko. / The god called Yurarihiko God is a crooked spirit / Coiling about the Rocky Mountains in the Land of Tokoyo. / How foolish are the people who revere Matsuhiko / As the Prince of Yurari and serve him with respect. / Though Matsuhiko himself is aware of the truth, / He endured for a while in order to save you.'

Matsuhime: 'Matsuhime and Jiyōgihime are not naive shrines who do not know what a Magakami is. Yet, in order to save the lost people, they quietly bear an unseemly divine name.'

Magakiko: 'How strange! Did the very things I thought were gods saving the world turn out to be my foes? The regret of idly venerating groundless gods across the mountains and leading the world astray! From now on, I shall straighten the steed of my heart and serve the way of Sumeōkami.'

Otora: 'The strict Kototama of the angel shines and has illuminated the darkness of Magahiko. How grateful I am, rejoicing in the god who has swept away the gathering clouds hanging over my heart! Magahiko too, from now on, will surely gaze upon the moon in the open sky of his Murakimo heart.'

Magahiko: 'The sky of my long-confined heart has cleared up at last, at the voice of the angel, the messenger of Kami.'

Otora: 'How joyful it is that even Magahiko, who would not listen to my words, submits to the messenger of Kami! Since I am one whose body and soul lack sufficient light, I found it difficult to save the official Magahiko. The descent of the benevolent messenger of Kami has illuminated the chests of us two.'

Tenshi: 'An affectionate daughter born from the intertwined pines, you have built the eternal foundation right here. From now on, enshrine anew the gods of Mount Kogita according to the word of Kami.'

Otora: 'How could I ever turn against the divine words of Kami? With soaring courage, I shall serve.'

Magahiko: 'Now I leave everything solely to the will of Kami, for my body lacks both strength and wisdom. Oh, to the awe-inspiring grace of Kami, even my parried and thirsty soul has been refreshed. My soul, once pining, thirsty, suffering, and grieving, has been revived in the Mizu no Mitama. How I regret that I treated the divine pillars of Mizu no Mitama and Izu no Mitama so lightly! Please forgive my deep sins up to now, if they were merely the work of my heart's distortion.'

Otora: 'Magahiko, you must not attribute your sins to the demon of your heart; it is the rust upon your own body. A demon dwelt within your straying soul, causing you to serve unseemly works.'

Tenshi: 'How joyous that the clouds of delusion for you two pillars have melted away like spring ice! The divine realm where the Lord Kami eternally dwells blooms and fragrances with the spring of Tokoyo. Human bodies are indeed the seedbeds to become true persons of the divine realm in the heavenly skies. To think that the surface of the earth is something thoroughly defiled is nothing more than the delusion of the heart. If there is the realm of Kami in the Murakimo heart, this surface of the earth too becomes the realm of Kami. Establish and fulfill the divine realm of Kami upon the earth; if you do not, upon death there is no divine realm. If you dwell upon the earth and place your body in hell, after wandering you will likely become a demon. Even a world raging with demons, great serpents, and ugly crooked spirits becomes a flower garden of Kami if the heart is pure. How dreadful are the people who call this transient world hell or asura and spend their days in scorn. Every human being is a divine child born to ascend to the heavenly divine realm.

The Lord Kami saves the spiritual bodies of all ordinary people into the heavenly realm, leaving none behind and dropping none. Though His divine heart yearns anxiously to save them, people fall into darkness of their own accord. The great deity saves the souls falling into the dark world of Ne no Kuni and Soko no Kuni. This deity exists as the Mizu no Mitama and has opened the path of Misoso. Those who guard the sincerity of the path of Misoso, how could they ever be cast down to the root-country of the nether depths? Comprehending the love of Kami and the wisdom of Kami, if one dwells here, even the earth is the spring of the heavenly realm. The heavenly realm, knowing neither autumn, winter, nor night, is the paradise where humans ought to dwell. The divine realm, where eternal flowers bloom and fragrance and even tree fruits abound, is a joyous place. The Lord Kami sends numerous angels down to earth and causes them to guard the world so as to save it. The official of the teachings of Misoso is the angel; never doubt the words of Kami.'

Magahiko: 'Is not the official of the god of Uranai also the wondrous messenger of the supreme deity?'

Tenshi: 'The official of the god of Uranai is a true person who saves birds, beasts, insect tribes, and the like.'

Magahiko: 'Hearing that even the insect tribes were born from the divine water and fire of Kami, I think I wish to save them.'

Tenshi: 'You must save them by employing the heart of Kami; know that He is not a god whom humans love selfishly.'

Otora: 'The god enshrined in this mountain must be the god whom the insect tribes seek for salvation.'

Tenshi: 'Not so; it is the crooked god who even captures and eats the insect tribes, so do not let down your guard.'

Magahiko, through the admonition of the angel, cleared away the darkness of his heart, suddenly possessed a pure complexion, felt a hundredfold increase in vigor, and was able to experience infinite joy. Facing the angel, Magahiko expressed his gratitude for the divine grace with tears.

Maga: 'Thanks to the descent and grace of the noble and pure angel, my hitherto delusions have melted away like spring snow under the sun. Indeed, I float as if endowed with infinite effort and life, moistened with tears of joy. Henceforth, I intend to reform my foolish heart of the past, and devote my entire strength solely to the true Kamisama.'

Tenshi: 'Magahiko, you said just now that you will exert yourself for the sake of Kamisama and the world, but the power of Kami is vast and boundless, and there is not the slightest room for your strength to be added. Only, as a good people of Ten, you should make everything attached to your body shine brilliantly, and if by any chance you have a surplus, bestow it upon others. However, as a Ningen, how could you possibly save the world and save people? If you could see your own face and back with your own eyes, then for the first time you would be equipped with the power to save people, even if only to some small extent. Considering this, how could one in the body of a Ningen ever be able to save other people? While you hold such thoughts, the clouds of arrogance have not yet cleared away entirely.'

Yes, thank you truly and sincerely for your various teachings. However, we cannot save our own bodies and never be satisfied with just that. We wish to save the souls of our pitiful compatriots. Surely the necessity of the missionaries is not just for saving our own bodies. We would like you to give us clear instruction on this point.'

A missionary is, as the words read, a messenger who embodies the gratitude and nobility of Kami and proclaims it to the people of the world. They have absolutely no right to save even a single person. Saving the world and saving people is, in other words, the divine work of the Savior. Only, those who are missionaries are guides of the dead leading to the realm of Kami. These guides of the dead are apt to have their eyes blinded, wander at the crossroads of the eight paths, or lead guests to the root country and fall down themselves. Therefore, leaving everything to Kannagara is the best policy. No matter how much knowledge a Ningen may have, no matter what power they may have, are they not unable to produce even a single leaf of a tree? With a body incapable of producing even a single lump of soil, how could one have the power to save the people of the world? Only, those who are missionaries and believers should understand Kami, know the direction of the realm of Kami, and endeavor to guide the straying dead to the gates of Tenkoku; with this, their duty as a Ningen is fulfilled. You must not forget that any salvation beyond that lies in the hands of Kami.'

Yes, thank you for your kind instructions from beginning to end.'

A moment ago, I borrowed the mouth of Otora-dono to explain Kannagara, but because you despised Otora's physical body, even when I spoke the truth and let you hear it, you did not understand. Therefore, this time, borrowing the body of a pure and stainless young girl, Kami has given a disciplinary warning for the sake of Magahiko; do not harbor arrogance by any means.'

Magahiko choked back tears of joy, moistened the tatami mats, and prostrated himself. While Otora was overwhelmed with grateful tears, unable even to raise her face, she joined her palms and bowed low. As fragrant scents filled the surroundings and subtle music entered their ears, the Tenshi returned to the original seat, and the gentle figure of the lovely Ochiyo had completely reverted to that of an innocent twelve-year-old girl as before.

Magahiko for the first time repented of his past faults, was illuminated by the light of Kami, and following the command of Matsuhiko, gathered the deities of Kitakayama in one place, conducted a solemn purification ceremony, and sincerely consented to enshrine the true Kami. From then on, they finally made Matsuhiko the chief priest and Gosankō the master of the purification gate, and set about the solemn transfer ceremony.

Ah, Kannagara Tamashii Sachibeimase.

『宣伝使は読んで字の如く、神の有難き事、尊き事を体得して、之を世人に宣べ伝ふる使者である。決して一人なりとも救ふべき権利はない。世を救ひ、人を救ふは即ち救世主の神業である。只宣伝使たるものは、神の国に至る亡者引である。此亡者引は、ややもすれば眼くらみ、八衢にさまよひ、或は根底の国に客を導き、自らも落ち行くものである。それ故何事も惟神に任すが一等だ。何程人間が知識ありとて、力ありとて、木の葉一枚生み出す事も出来ないではないか。一塊の土たりとも産出する事の出来ない身を以て、いかでか世人を救ふ力あらむ。只宣伝使及び信者たるものは、神を理解し神の国の方向を知り、迷へる亡者をして天国の門に導く事を努むれば、これで人間としての職務は勤まつたのだ。それ以上の救ひは神の御手にあることを忘れてはなりませぬ』

『ハイ、何から何まで親切なる御教訓有難う存じます』

『最前お寅どのの口をかつて、惟神の説明を致しておいたが、其方はお寅の肉体を軽蔑して居るから、誠の事を云つて聞かしても其方は分らなかつた。そこで今度は清浄無垢の少女が体をかつて、神は魔我彦の為に訓戒を与へたのである、決して慢心致すでないぞや』

魔我彦は歓喜の涙をしやくり上げ、畳を潤はし蹲まる。お寅は有難涙にくれ、顔もえ上げず、合掌して伏拝む。四辺に芳香薫じ微妙の音楽耳に入るよと見る間に、エンゼルは元つ御座に帰り給ひ、可憐なるお千代の優しき姿は、依然として十二才のあどけなき少女と変つて了つた。

魔我彦は初めて前非を悔ひ、神の光に照らされ、松彦の指揮に従つて小北山の祭神を一所に集め、厳粛なる修祓式を行ひ、誠の神を鎮祭する事を心より承認したのである。いよいよこれより松彦を斎主とし、五三公を祓戸主となし、厳粛なる遷座式に着手することとなつた。

あゝ惟神霊幸倍坐世。
