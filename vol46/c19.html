怪しの森〔1229〕

§ 1

The heavy Yahekumo that shrouded the mountain of Kogita, the Tokitsukaze that blew it away without a trace, and the Kamikaze of Iso, elegant and refined, have swept away the dust and filth accumulated in the hearts of Yobito, and the garden of Heiwa has suddenly blossomed within the mansion of Kami.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. Even the Magabito, captured and suffering under the Shikotama of the eighty Magatsu, have finally awakened from their dream of slumber. Starting with Otora-baba-sama, the Matsuhikotsukasa, Bunsuke, and other officials and Mamehito of Kami, having received the Onkei of the true Kami from the depths of their hearts, raise voices of joy that fill the Ten, causing even the Chijiyō to tremble. Serving the Ananai-kyō,

starting with Matsuhikotsukasa, Isokō, Mankō, and other pure officials purify their bodies, cleanse their hearts, and re-enshrine the true Kami of Tenchi. Having enshrined and settled the former Kami in one place, they all bid a joyful farewell, and while singing the song of propaganda, they proceed, leaving the mansion behind. Otora-baba-sama follows behind Matsuhiko, thinking, 'I am now saved by the true Kami. If I do not invite Imoriwake-sama and Otami, who have ended up as captives of the devil, to the true path, awaken them, and save them, what excuse can I offer when I stand before the Kami of Tenchi?' She follows them wherever they go,

determined to convey the truth, and it is truly admirable to see her proceed, dragging her aged body step by step, following the party of Matsuhiko.

On Mount Kogita, Matsuhime, Magahiko, Okiku, and Ochiyo became overlapping pillars of Kami; Bunsuke faithfully continued his duties at the reception desk; and the other hundred officials and Mamehito joyfully followed the true teachings of Ananai, listening in detail to the gospel of the heavenly kingdom, and were moved to tears of ecstasy.

Meanwhile, the seven members of Matsuhiko's party bowed before the shrine on Mount Kogita, crossed the bridge over the Kajika River, and proceeded toward the Forest of Ukiki.

The story returns to an earlier point. About three ri before the Forest of Ukiki, there is a small forest. This is the fork where the main road of Kajika Pass and the side path diverge. When Kuniwake and Matsuhiko passed through, they had chosen the shortcut from the Forest of Yamaguchi and came by the side path. This forest was called the Forest of Ayashi, and it was said that mysterious events constantly occurred there. A rumor had spread that those who entered this forest could never return safely. Therefore, it had become a custom that when one encountered pursuers, if one only hid in this forest, the pursuers would usually not follow. For this reason, it was also called the Forest of Nanjoke. At the entrance of this forest, at the corner of the intersection where the main road of Kajika Pass and the side path divided, four or five rough men, appearing to be inspectors of the Brahman religion, sat in a circle and were indulging in idle chatter to pass the time.

Ko: 'Hey, Waku, even though it is this cold, we are ordered to serve as substitutes for the roadside Jizo on a path like this, day and night, without even a fire to warm ourselves; it is a dull business, is it not?'

Waku: 'I wonder what will become of this war, anyway.'

'What will become of it? The outcome is clearly evident. There is a saying that the many cannot defeat the few, and that a cornered rat will bite a cat. The many cannot defeat the so-called few. When it comes to the crunch, the small group, with their hearts united, will always achieve a great victory.'

'Even so, to say the many cannot defeat the few means that a large force cannot be defeated by one person. A large force and a small group are not equal in numbers, in benefits, or in any respect. It is the law of Tenchi that the strong win and the weak lose. That is why they say the many cannot defeat the few. Is your interpretation not contradictory?'

'To say the many cannot defeat the few means that the many cannot defeat the few. When the few cannot defeat the many, it is said that the few cannot defeat the many. However, I have rarely heard it said that the few cannot defeat the many. As proof, you can understand it by considering the Battle of Mount Kajika, can you not? Against an enemy of only four people, who did not even carry weapons, hundreds of brave warriors were easily routed. This is a practical example that the many cannot defeat the few.'

話は後へ戻る。浮木の森の三里ばかり手前に一寸した小さき森がある。ここは河鹿峠の本街道と間道との別れ道である。治国別、松彦が通過したのは、山口の森から近道を選んで間道を来たものであつた。此森は怪しの森と云つて絶えず不思議があると伝へられてゐた。此森へ入つたものは到底無事で帰れないと云ふ噂が立つてゐる。それだから追手に出会つた時等は、必ず此森へ隠れさへすれば追手も大抵の時は追及せないのが例となつてゐる。故に一名難除けの森とも称へられてゐた。此森の入口、河鹿峠の本道、間道と分れてゐる辻の角に四五人の荒男がバラモン教の目附と見えて車座となつて退屈ざましに雑談に耽つてゐた。

M: 'By the way, Kiyōdai, they say that at Kogitayama a tremendous number of believers have gathered for something called the Uranai-kyō; if it weren't for guard duties like this, I'd love to go over there just for research to see what kind of things they're up to.'

Kō: 'They say there are an awful lot of women. Virtually every single woman in the Village of Ukiki has fled to that Kogitayama place, so I hear. However, they say it's utterly impossible to go over there and snatch them away. They use all sorts of miraculous and mysterious arts, and with an influence where even Susanōnomikoto can do nothing about them, that's what they're like.'

M: 'If that's the case, it seems there are some pretty formidable fellows around. Our general was easily routed by just three or four disciples of Susanōnomikoto. I had thought the Ananai-kyō was mighty, but Kogitayama must be even beyond that. Truly, there is always someone superior to you no matter how high you go.'

Kō: 'That's a given. There's nothing stronger than reckless absurdity, after all.'

M: 'Well didn't General Katahiko and General Lunch go charging in with reckless absurdity? If reckless absurdity wins, wouldn't it be impossible for them to suffer such a disgraceful defeat?'

Waku: 'That's the part where human wisdom fails to understand. They say victory and defeat are matters of fortune's wheel. By the way, isn't it rather dull for the likes of us to keep going on with this simple, meaningless life day after day? Even if we have wives, they've been left behind in the capital of Haruna, and I really can't help feeling annoyed by this boredom in camp.'

Kō: 'If someone would just send a nice woman over here, it'd be interesting though.'

M: 'It would be nice if things went so swimmingly to order, but is there any chance a nice woman would pass through such a dangerous place?'

Waku: 'Even so, since they say lots of women have been flocking to Kogitayama, isn't this the only route they have to pass through if they come from there?'

M: 'Lately, because we are staked out in the Forest of Ukiki, every single one of them is terrified and won't come this side of the bridge, so it's a total dead end. The night has worn on quite a bit, it's freezing, and they make a huge fuss saying that if we build a fire, our whereabouts will be given away to the enemy according to military law, so this is truly a wretched trade.'

Just as they were talking thus, there was a woman running toward them with her hair disheveled and breathing heavily.

Kō: 'Hey, look over yonder. One has come just as ordered. Judging from her footfalls as she shows through the moonlit haze, she seems to be a woman. Shall we suddenly turn into robbers and try to scare her?'

Both: 'That sounds like fun.'

The one who came briskly walking into such a scene was Otami, who had fled from Kogitayama. Otami had intended to make her way toward the forest in the fields, but hearing human voices echoing into the forest for no apparent reason, she turned back to take the road this way, intending to come out onto the main highway.

Kō: 'Hey there, young lady, wait a minute. Where do you think this place is? As a woman, unauthorized passage is not permitted here.'

Otami: 'Ha, ha, ha, ha, ha! Why on earth can't one pass along the public highway? This road surely wasn't built by you lords. Even if you tell me not to pass, I will pass by my own right. Do not interfere with me.'

Kō: 'No matter what you say, if I say you shall not pass, you shall not pass by any means. Who do you think you are taking me for?'

Otami: 'How utterly absurd! As if there were any 'who do you think you are' about it. You were born a fine-looking man, aren't you, yet here you are made to stand guard at a road intersection like this? If you had skill and intellect, you would be in General Lunch's camp participating in the curtained headquarters and taking part in important consultations, but because you are scrap laborers for whom no place has any use, you are made to stand guard at an intersection like a stone jizō. Who on earth would be afraid of such empty blustering from a man like you? Step aside and stay put.'

Waku: 'What a stubborn nun-like wretch she is.'
