Chapter 5: 菊の薫 [1215]

菊の薫〔1215〕

§ 1

Okiku, seeing the commotion of the entire assembly, climbed onto the platform in a flurry and began to sing while striking the leg of the table with a white fan.

The Great Teacher of the Uranai Oshi, Imoriwake is Ōhiroki, calling himself Masamune-sama, following after Takahime-sama, Magahiko-sama's Giritenjiyō, a suspicious Hinode Kami-sama, having left Kitayamamura, having come on a business trip to such a place, and praising it as the one and only Seijiyō, O-Tora's Suzunohime, a hidden wife not spoken of to others, while joining the Iki of Fūfu, intending to raise a Daishingefu, drinking sake from morning till night, offering Miki to the Kami who has fallen into the world, saying so, from morning till night, drunk and muddled, that appearance of his was like a Hariko tiger.

Born in the land of Rokoku, the Himegami of the Ōunabara, having been reborn as Ōfune-sama, and that spirit having transformed, it is said that he became the Minister of Saketake of Yasaka. He taught that among the brothers of Ōfune-sama, there are two pillars, Ōiwa and Ōfuji. The child born between the couple, Motoshitanoritake and Hiyoshihime, is called Tokifumi, and his retainer, Yasaka Saketake, accompanying him, comes across to the land of Kami. That Hiyoshi-hime-sama, the other name of Ōunabara-hime, is a transformation of Hirano-hime, he would always let us hear such incomprehensible sermons. The leader of the land of Rokoku is Yamatake-sama, and his other name is said to be Kōryūhime.

Kōryūhime is again a Hegure, and became Ōtsuruhime. Tokoyo-hime is again a Hegure, Sarutako-hime and Hirano-hime, Ōunabara-hime and Hiyoshi-hime, Terumusu Mōshi Rōi emerge. What a strange, contradictory name of Kami. The other name of Terumusu-hime is said to be Tarata-hime. A horse is born from the mouth, a lion flies out, a peacock is born, a centipede, a snake, and a toad, and it is said that that toad and a fox-sama conspired to give birth to a person, only incomprehensible things, because he says them fluently while under the influence of sake, it is unbearable. Thinking of this, that Aku-sama's saying that there is no Kami without a name may not be entirely unreasonable.

No matter what the family register of the Kami may be, there is no need to worry at all. If you only receive the divine virtue and can live comfortably in this world, that should be fine for everyone. The Kami of heaven and earth, the Kami of Aomori Shirakijō, the Giritenjiyō of the Hinode Kami, Tokoyo-hime and Kōryūhime, Ōtaruhime and Kotouehime, Kanayamahime and Mi-hime, the Minister of the Earth, Chijōmaru, the Minister of Cultivation, Kitsukihime, Asahikohime and Minoruhime, Hayazatohime and Chijōhime, these sixteen pillars of Kami, this is the root of the root, of the long, long ago.

Even longer ago than that, the sixteen that become the origin of the spirit, it is said to be the soul of Okiku. Okiku is now in the prime of her life, having welcomed her sixteenth winter. Rather than grasping the name of the Kami and saying this and that, it would be much more beneficial to worship this chrysanthemum. The seven Kami that Giritenjiyō took charge of and raised, Amateruhiko-sama, Amenowakahiko and Hachiō-sama, the Minister of the Great Field, Ōhiroki, Masamune-sama, and the deep sympathy of Michigami Yoshinori, and Jūdō Yukinari, I heard that these Kami are worshipped together as twenty-three pillars.

Thinking that this Kami-sama is a parent becoming a child, or a child becoming a parent, or again becoming master and servant, there is no fixed judgment for this. When I thought it strange and asked Masamune about the origin of the divine name, Masamune-sama said, 'Is it not called a Hegure shrine, a Hegure of a Hegure of a Hegure warrior? No matter how much contradiction there is, if you say Hegure, can everything not be solved in a single word, Okiku-sama? Where is there a fool who worships Kami with blind honesty? You do not resemble a woman who has breathed the air of civilization, you are a fool of blind honesty,' he said while laughing.

Thinking of this, the Kami enshrined on this mountain are all suspicious. The Kami of the King of Heaven of the last generation, and the Hime of Jōgi are all lies. The meat palace of the 567 fulfillment, the meat palace of Rintōbiten, the Ikuba shrine and Iwateruhime, the Asahi no Toyosaka Noborihime, the meat palace of Kiso Yoshihime, Hinomaruhime and Tenjō-sama, Tamanorihime and the Great General, and Tokoyo-hime are all lies. Four wild foxes possess, and I remember them as things that speak of such trivial matters and lead people into darkness. Even if Okiku is made angry by everyone from now on and is blamed in various ways, the way of Kami that helps others, even while knowing it is a lie, this is again...

§ 2

How could I remain silent? I humbly beseech you, listen to what I say, with directness, reviewing it and re-examining it, and comprehend it thoroughly. Risking my very life, I lay bare the inner workings of every single detail before you all, offering it up here. Ah, Kamunagara, may the divine spirits bring peace and blessedness! Whether the morning sun shines brightly or clouds obscure it, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even though the great earth should sink, if it is not based upon truth alone, one cannot prosper and go forth in this world. I fear for the future. In this fleeting world, just to preserve my life, I told nothing but lies, leading people astray, and because I loathe sowing the seeds of eternal hellish suffering,

disregarding material gains and losses, I manifest the universal truth right here, awakening the eyes of those who are utterly deluded. Do not think ill of me; I earnestly implore you. Ah, Kamunagara, may the divine spirits bring peace and blessedness!

Teku began to sing:

Following Lord Matsuhiko, as I climbed up the slope of Mount Kogita and looked around, wooden shrines and stone shrines of various kinds, a great many of them, were standing there. Following Grandma Otora, when I inquired at the reception desk of Daimon Shrine, there was only one old man wearing white garments, diligently and wholeheartedly painting turnips and radishes. Thinking it a strange kind of shrine indeed, and wandering through the mountains with doubt in my heart, I finally realized it was the foul dwelling of the warped deity. Surely this place is none other than the crossroads leading to the netherworld, the intersection of the six paths, or else I was not mistaken in thinking it the entrance to the eight myriads of hells.

Now fully exposing the truth, the one who called himself the founder, Imoriwake-sama, started a marital quarrel, whispered secretly with Oand, slipped away swiftly into the gathering darkness with his tail tucked between his legs. When I gaze upon his retreating figure, whether it is a fox, a raccoon dog, or a wild dog, his appearance defies all understanding. As I watch Grandma Otora pursuing him from behind with a roaring growl like a fierce Yaksha, she is neither cow nor tiger; when I look closer, she is an old fox. Trapped in the darkness of love's path, heedlessly plunging forward with a steady trudge, she chases after the man she loves, pursuing him at the risk of her very life. If the temple on Mount Kogita were truly a place where genuine deities gather,

the founder himself, of vital and utmost importance, possessed by foul demons, stripped of his spirit, and fleeing from this place is something that should never happen. Having witnessed this state of affairs, no matter how stubborn the superstition, the dream would surely vanish at once. I walk the path of the gods, Teku of sole sincerity. Although I harbor no intent whatsoever to slander the teachings of others elsewhere, unable to bear watching and forgetting myself, I speak out with harsh words. O people gathered in this place, with directness, review it, re-examine it, and reflect upon it well! Ah, Kamunagara, may the divine spirits bring peace and blessedness!

As Teku finished singing, the vast grand hall once again became a scene of uproarious commotion, and people began to enact an indescribable, grotesque spectacle of a thousand varied forms—some raging, some weeping, some laughing, and some screaming. Raising her voice from the platform, Teku proclaimed:

Casting away my body for the sake of the Way and the people of the world, I have begun to proclaim the living Kototama. O people, with peace and tranquility in your hearts, calm yourselves before the great presence of the gods. Soon we shall enshrine the original and true deities of the beginning and unfold the world. The leaves of trees that are beaten by the winter wind and scatter away are those without the strength to remain upon the branches. Blown and beaten by the winter wind, whatever remains upon the treetops to the very end is the living mitama. Whatever events may come to pass, follow the god of the world's origin, all you people. Oh, what a day is today, illuminating the wandering hearts of Imoriwake, Magahiko, and Grandma Otora! Magahiko too must surely rejoice today, having left behind the darkness of utter delusion.
