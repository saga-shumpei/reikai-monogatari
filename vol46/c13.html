通夜話〔1223〕

§ 1

Yamataorochi and Shikogitsune, where the Magaoni reside, appearing in the Ural Mountains and Tokoyokuni, and upon the Rocky Mountains, the lineage of the Hime of Tokoyo, possessed by akuki and jiyashin, spreading the ways of the Urals and Brahmanism in all four directions, disturbing the realm beneath the heaven, and defiling it—how truly dreadful!

The Great Deity Kuniharutachi, by the grace of the Kami in the great Ōyashima, beginning with the Mikoto of Hiko, appointing the four pillars—Kototamawake, Ōdaruhiko, and Kamikuniwake— as the guardian deities of the realm, to protect without exception the myriad islands and myriad countries of the sea; yet the power of akuma being fierce, like the grass that is cut down, upon its tracks

sprouting once more and growing forth, how vexing that they are not eradicated! The Takahime of the Ural lineage, who served Brahmanism, mixing together the three teachings— Sangokyō and Ural-kyō— and building a truly strange training hall of the teaching of divination in Fusa no Kuni, at Kitayama Village, flourished in her influence,

but at last mastering herself and yielding to the path of the teaching of Sangu, and succeeding her in the aftermath, two men— Tōkarawake and Magahiko—conversed together, cutting into the rock on the mountainside of Sakatereyama, leveling the earth, and with names like 'Hegure-no-hegure-no-hegure warrior' and 'Hegure Shrine', attaching such strange shrine titles, while gathering the young and old, men and women of the neighborhood,

just as mice draw rice cakes, pinching them up little by little, now at last having obtained four or five hundred steadfast believers, here at length constructing a palace, serving therein, and with flourishing influence opening their teachings; yet, the Living Kami who protect heaven and earth— how could they ever permit such crooked deeds of the mud-slug?

Matsuhiko and his party appeared, passing themselves off as the Hiko of Yurari, meeting again with Matsuhime and my daughter, the gentle Ochiyo, from whom he had parted long ago, and even spending a night together; then the divine punishment of the Kami came around, Tōkarawake became drunk on sake, started a quarrel with Otora, and invited the fate of total collapse

by his own hand; calling himself Lord Masamune in the great expanse, Tōkarawake fled far away from this place in the cover of night, together with the believer Otomi; Otora, stamping her feet behind him and raging at his heartlessness, went running in pursuit to see, and catching her foot on a rope tied to the root of an alder tree, fell headlong upon the spot,

smashing her sacred-dance nose, and lay groaning, 'Un, un, un!'; and thither once more Magahiko, pursuing in the footsteps of Otora, came running with all his might, stumbled over Otora's body, fell headlong with a mere grunt, grazing the top of his knee, and as he writhed and suffered,

Gisaburō, Mankō, and three others came forth in pursuit, rescued the two and led them back to the Kokitayama Founder's Hall, and before they could even breathe a sigh of relief, various and sundry bizarre events broke out one after another, and the inner workings of the divination teaching were thrown into chaos like hemp.

Ah, how wondrous are the Kami, how wondrous! Though crookedness may prosper for a time, because it lacks the true divine power, it breaks apart of its own accord, fragilely. This is truly the proof of the unassailable divine majesty of the divine realm having been manifested. Ah, how wondrous are the Kami, how wondrous! No manner of crookedness can ever dim the eyes of the Kami of heaven and earth; into the very hole it dug for itself

it falls, inviting its own self-destruction. Is it only the path of divination? Of all things in the world, there is not one evil thing that does not perish. Considering this, the human body must uphold truth alone, never bending, never yielding, no matter where, and press forward for the sake of truth, never retreating a single step. Ah, how wondrous are the Kami, how wondrous! May your august spirits be blessed and at peace.

The few men and women remaining in the great forecourt indulged in various tales of the past until the hour grew late in the night.

Bunsuke: 'Now, now, everyone, what on earth are you chattering about until this late hour? I cannot sleep even a wink, you know. Come, hurry back to your sleeping quarters and rest, because everyone is troubled by it.'

Bunsuke: 'Ah, that truly cannot be helped. If so, please be as quiet as possible. Your loud voices pass right through to the reception desk, and I am troubled because I cannot sleep. Hatsuhiko: 'Yes, understood, farewell.'
