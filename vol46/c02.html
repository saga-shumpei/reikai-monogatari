Chapter 2: 慰労会 [1212]

慰労会〔1212〕

§ 1

Matsuhiko and Matsuhime, desiring that grandma Otora, Magahiko, Imoriwake, and others would return quickly, offered prayers in the grand hall and had just returned to the entranceway of the kyōshiyuyakata when a party of seven returned, whispering noisily.

Matsuhiko: 'Ah, Isokō-san, how are Imoriwake-san and the others?'

Iso: 'We missed capturing the ikimiya of Ōhirokimasamune, but in its place we have triumphantly welcomed and brought back the nikumiya of Madam Suzunohime. Since he who wishes to shoot a general must first shoot his horse, if we drag back the female-side horse and keep it, it will be all right; for even with whales, if you take the female the male is surely caught. And another by-product is that we picked up and brought along the ikimiya of the denokami of Giritenjiyōhi. Ha-ha-ha!'

'That was quite a hardship. Now, please come inside anyway.'

Matsuhime: 'Everyone, you must have had a hard time. Did you finally fail to capture Imoriwake-san and Otami-san? It is such a regrettable thing, isn't it?'

From Mankō's side,

'They say that the fish that got away is big; as expected, the fish that swallows boats broke the net and escaped. One shrimp and one sea scallop—quite a poor catch indeed, but even this may serve quite well as a relish for tonight's sake. Heh-heh-heh.'

Matsuhiko: 'Ah, well, anyway it is splendid. From here I am going back to Matsuhime-mansion to pray earnestly to the gods, so first of all please partake of some sacred sake in celebration of the discovery.'

Otora: 'Please pray that Imoriwake, through your pacification of the spirit, will be drawn and brought back here even within tonight, Matsuhime-sama, I earnestly beg of you as well.'

Matsuhime: 'Yes, I shall pray with all my might.'

Maga: 'I also ask you to pray so that Otami-san will be drawn and brought back.'

Matsuhime: 'Yes, I will pray.'

Mankō: 'No matter what anyone says, since it is the prayer of the eternal Hinonoo Ōkami-sama and Kamiyoshihime-sama, it will be all right. Now, shrimp and scallop, you may well feel at ease.'

Matsuhiko: 'Aha-ha, goodbye everyone, please take your time and hold a consolation party, and make it lively.'

Matsuhime: 'Please drink the sacred sake to your heart's content; no matter how intoxicated you may become, twisting noses is the one thing you must not do, ho-ho-ho.'

So saying, Matsuhiko and Matsuhime step up the two hundred steps with their feet. Mankō looks up at the two figures from below,

'Banzai to the couple, they match so well. Ouramabuki-sama, atsufa-ha-ha-ha.'

Matsuhiko and Matsuhime return toward the annex without even glancing back.

The group paid their respects in the grand hall, and afterwards held a celebratory feast of consolation at the kyōshozakata. Gradually the alcohol began to take effect, and casting aside all barriers, they started chattering about trivial matters. Being superstitious folks, Otora and Magahiko thought that the ikimiya of Yuruhiko and Kamiyoshihime would surely draw their two lovers back here, if early before dawn, or at the latest around noon tomorrow, and smiling with a feeling as secure as riding on a large ship, they drank sake recklessly without restraint.

Mankō: 'Ah-ah, we did some mighty hard labor. Unless I demand a fair reward, the balance won't hold. A reward as if it were just a hard-working day is utterly unappreciative, you know. Even when night work is forced upon us and we get some percentage of overtime pay, I just can't stand it.'

Iso: 'Hey Mankō, labor is sacred. Even I did the labor just like you. To demand as a right a reward corresponding merely to the amount of labor has no basis morally. If one were to regard only the reward of labor as a matter-of-course right, there would be no other way than for that very thing to bring forth a hundred evils in society and lead the world into confusion. What would you do if you said that the elderly, the sick, and children do not work, so do not give them bread? Being allowed to work is also, after all, thanks to the gods. The labor problem that has come to be clamorously brought up nowadays is not a problem between human groups or social classes, but a problem between God and man. Those of us who are propagandists or believers of Isokōkyō must stand upon eternal truth under any and all circumstances, transcending the times. To talk about labor problems while it is the sacred path of God is something we must restrain just a little bit.'

'Even so, labor is what develops the blessings of Heaven, and are not sendenshi also laborers after all, and without the right to demand remuneration, would it not be quite unbearable?'

'Do not sendenshi already receive instantly from Kami the remuneration of joy in faith, love, and righteous understanding? Granted, when people nowadays engage in the labor of material production and obtain appropriate remuneration, they say that they are earning it themselves, but they are by no means earning it—it is given by Kami. They are receiving okage. To think that you earn it yourself is a grave mistake. Human beings cannot live by themselves; they are permitted to live. That is why Kami says that man shall not live by bread alone. If the solution to human life were settled by the bread problem alone, the world would be like a bleak wilderness.'

'We, in short, are physical laborers. There is a difference as vast as heaven and earth in terms of suffering compared to indoor laborers who just sit still and use the tips of their mouths or pens, you see.'

'That is truly a shallow view. Physical laborers act in accordance with the natural principles of the human body, so even if they squeeze out sweat, it is a pleasant thing, and if they call it painful, that is only at the beginning of the evening. As for the suffering of the labor of those who hold pens to write, or sit in chairs to conduct investigations, it is a realm that physical laborers cannot even imagine. Everyone always thinks what other people are doing looks better, but whoever tries being in that position will find it quite painful. The higher one goes, the heavier the responsibility, and it is incomparable to simple physical labor. I too once buried myself in blue-covered books, rummaged through numerous reference books, and engaged in writing. Moreover, I also became a navvy, a farmer, and a cart driver, but the duty of holding a pen still looked the easiest yet was the most painful. Even the oral narrator of Reikai Monogatari and the scribes suffer. Even if you chase after Otora-baba-sama and experience grueling suffering that makes you gasp for breath, once you rest your body and drink a cup of sake, that is the end of it and it is over. Just try becoming a writer; there is not a single minute's slack in your mind, and you exhaust your body and soul to the extent that you cannot forget it even in your dreams. Well, let us put an end to such pedantic talk, and today let us cheerfully partake of the sacred sake and worship the new sun of tomorrow morning, shall we not? Hey Otora-sama, Maga-sama, shall we have one, please pour me a cup.'

Otora: 'You may not find it to your liking since I am an old hag, but I shall accept the honor.'

Smiling broadly, she handed the cup to Gosukō and poured it generously from the warmed sake bottle. Thus the cups went around and around, and the banquet grew more and more lively.

Mankō was unexpectedly drunk and collapsed, and acting as if he were on a solo stage, he began to launch kototama.

'It really is something, as they say that even at a hundred a sparrow does not change, and even as one grows old, romance seems never to die down, eh? Why, look at Otora-baba-sama herself, isn't that so? I for one am truly perplexed in the resolution of this problem.'

Gosukō: 'Romance is sacred; religious faith and righteous romance are things that elevate the human soul with grace. Righteous faith and perfect romance develop the human spirit and bestow eternal and infinite life. However, the shallow view of romance spoken of by modern scientists will not do. All romance is something that branched off from sexual desire. And romance is first contained within sexual desire in the form of potentiality. When the true Kami who created this world bestowed sexual desire upon human life, He made it contain romance from that very beginning. Faith and romance are the fountainhead of joy. Joy is that which preserves the spirit eternally and furthermore causes the graceful and perfect activity of the spirit to arise.'

Mankō: 'Hey Maga, what do you take this Mankō-sama for? In matters of love, I am number one in the whole world.'

Otora: 'Shut up! Does a bent-waist Magahiko have any qualifications to talk about love? He who stubbornly harbored a one-sided crush, letting out a himei [shriek] as he botched his ball-riding!'

Taku: 'Though there is a god at the shrine for Taku-san, he watches this uproar as if it were someone else's affair. This god is Fugiritenjiyō [Heaven of Unrighteousness], protecting against the calamities of husband-and-wife quarrels.'

Magahiko: 'Magahiko's face is like Aomorishirakijiyō, made to be a usurped princess by a newt's separate self.'

Magahiko: 'Not a Hanayori no Hime, but having become a Hanauchi no Baba-hime-sama [Old Hag Princess of Nose-Hitting], hasn't she? Hanayori Hime transformed in her mitama into Saruhiko Hime, a princess upon whom only crimson-shame persimmons bear fruit!'

Otora: 'Kinryūhime is taken away, leading to hardship for Ōashihime; has Masamune-sama escaped to Tokoyohime?'

Magahiko: 'Yurarihiko, Jōgi no Hime must be getting along so intimately by now.'

Thus singing derailed songs to one another, before they knew it, they had brightly cleared the night. Numerous pilgrims trudge in a long line toward the large front hall for worship, the sound of wooden clogs being heard in a chaotic din.
