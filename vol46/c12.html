§ 1

Mankō, feeling as though his rear hair were being pulled, forgot the vital and essential divine service, and drowning in Okiku's love, took the two paths of money and passion, and while treading upon the light snow on the ridge of the high mountain where the winter wind raged wildly, he gazed upon Okiku's gentle receding figure, untied even his facial cords, and began to sing along the road.

'When the Kami appear in the world front and center, To distinguish and separate good from evil, The Kami of Direct Rectification who created this world, And the Great Kami of Direct Rectification whose heart is vast, Truly, whatever happens in the world of men, Look again with directness, listen again with directness, And declare and rectify the errors of one's body. The Kamisama of Ananai-kyō Will surely grant forgiveness peacefully and calmly, With a peaceful heart, concerning the matter that I, Mankō, Bewildered by the darkness of love, Derailed and lost my way. Crossing over Kajikatauge, Together with Harukuniwake, I have come all the way to the forest of Nonaka. At that very time, Kamehiko, Abandoning his disciple Mankō, Vanished like clouds and mist. When I think of this, has Mankō's Goyō already come to an end? At the foot of Itsuponbashi, Meeting by chance with my beloved Okiku, And having bitter opinions sounded out to me In various ways by Grandmother Omitora, My heart was greatly reformed, And together with Matsuhiko-sama, To set out upon the sacred and demonic path of conquest, Though I was full of joyful courage, By what fate of my spirit and soul was it so, That drawn together by the Kami of Binding, I have become deeply adored by Okiku, That beloved and precious darling daughter, And landed in a predicament where, once drawn, I cannot draw back, So that, against my will, I am leaving the path of the Kami For a while and going forth. Kuninotokotachi no Ōkami, Toyokuninochu no Ōkami, The Great Pillars of Ananai-kyō, Kami Susanoo no Ōkami, Though truly it is an inexcusable thing, Grant me a brief leave of absence! From now on, the two of us, following and tracing The ridges of the mountains, to the Land of the Moon, Will hide our bodies at the end of some unknown place, And the two of us together will pass our days in harmony, After having done so, I will assuredly Perform the Goyō of the Kamisama. Saying that for a little while yet it cannot be helped, Please look upon us again with directness, And pray, forgive our love! That beloved daughter of only seventeen years, Whose shoulder-tucks cannot even be taken off yet, Such an adorable woman as that, No matter how much one speaks of the path of the Kami, How could I ever abandon her and cast her aside? That we should become husband and wife must also be A deep bond from a previous existence. Okiku's older sister is likewise A person of superior talent and appearance, Truly it was a regrettable thing. Surpassing even her home village, With round eyes like little bells, And lips like flowers, At the moment when she smiled broadly with a grin, Before I knew it, my soul Seemed to fly off into the empty sky. Eh-he-he-he-hetsu-he, what on earth, I wonder how such a fine-featured countenance could be produced! Just because her spirit and soul are good, The flower of her heart begins to bloom, And the god of good fortune descends, to ensure that the relationship between the two of us is harmonious, And will surely protect our sacred love to perfection. If I think and think, is there anyone in the world As fortunate as I am? Hey, hey, Okiku, wait a moment! You have a gait unsuited for a child, How remarkably fast your feet are, On mountain ridges where there are no paths! That you walk with such ease Must surely mean that the Kami of Binding Is minutely and profoundly protecting Your body. Will you not wait for me for a little while? My breath is about to give out.' When he called out, 'Wait, wait,' Okiku turned her head back and spoke: 'Hey, Manya-sama, how irritating you are! For a little while longer, to the limit of my body, To the very limit that my legs can hold, I must run onward! If by any chance I am discovered by the pursuers, And happen to be caught, That indeed will become a serious matter. As an eloping wretch of a arch-thief, I will not forgive you as you are, And who knows what kind of things Salamander-wake, flying into a rage, Will end up doing! That is what weighs upon my mind. It is just one more breath, Manya-sama! Come, come, let us hurry along.'

Leaving her words behind, Running and going, now visible now hidden, Along the mountain ridge where small pines grow lush, Mankō panted heavily and said: 'Hey, hey, wait, Okiku-sama! Do not run quite so fast! If your figure becomes no longer visible, What shall I do in this deep mountain? You and I, just the two of us, And other than us, to be a strength to us, There is not even a single cat's kitten! Please, please wait a little while! Aita-tata-tsuta, I stumbled! Striding purposefully through a place where there is no path, How on earth can you run to that extent! Even Mankō, great though he is, bites his tongue, And must tuck his tail between his legs and surrender. That is only natural, for whoever one might be, Having done a thing of the utmost boldness, How could anyone possibly walk leisurely?'

From now on, plucking out my kimodama, / once more with all hitokiba / I will follow after Okiku-sama / and try to go. / Somehow my kokifu / has grown painful, you see. / Will we not take itsupuku here? / Now that I have fled thus far, / surely no otsute will overtake me. / Ouch, ouch, ouch, ouch, it struck my eye! / That wretch of a pine needle (matsuba) pushed its way forward / and poked my precious, precious eye / without even showing enriyo! / Hey now, hey now, Okiku, where have you gone? / What shall I do if we are separated / with a pat-pat-pat like a mere kodomo? / There is a limit to being reckless! / Wait, wait, wait a while, wait a while."

So saying, with a vacant, dazed expression, he climbed up and down the narrow staircase, down and up, moving his mouth mumbling something and going up and down countless times.

Okiku and Ochiyo, holding hands, came down as far as the stone steps. When they looked, Mankō was muttering something incomprehensible, going down and up the stone steps with a tontonton.

Okiku: 'Ochiyo-sama, look at that Mankō-sama, making a blank face and performing a thousand-shrine pilgrimage (sendo), is he not? Why on earth would he do such a strange thing, aah?'

Ochiyo: 'Mankō-sama must be possessed by a fox. There are plenty of old fox dens here on Kogitayama, you see.'

'Shall I strike him even on the back to bring him to his senses?'

'At any rate, try calling out "Hey." If you do, he might realize it.'

Okiku stood upon the ascending staircase from about an itsuken distance ahead,

'Hey, Mankō-sama, won't you pull yourself together?'

and called out. Mankō, as if his mouth were heavy with suddenness,

'Ohey, Okiku, if you run like that, you cannot catch up. It will be terrible if you drop the twenty-seven myriad ryō (nijifushichimanriyō) of gold. Well, why not wait a little? Once we have escaped as far as here, it is safe already.'

'Ho ho ho ho, how unappealing. Could Mankō-sama be dreaming that he is eloping with me, I wonder, ah, Ochiyo-sama?'

'Surely it is so, just look at that face. That is because he is possessed by a fox. And so he is dreaming. What a troublesome, witless fellow he is, aah.'

Mankō turned downward again and went down with a tatatatara of flowing sweat, tontonton. As Magahiko, having had his fat thoroughly drained by the fox, was going up the staircase to visit the Matsuhiko Mansion, he collided directly with Mankō coming down with a blank face. Magahiko,

'Ha ha, this fellow too has been done in by a wicked fox, hasn't he. When I thought it was only me, lo, there is a companion as well. saying thus, the very moment he struck his back three or four times with all his might, Mankō came to his senses,

'Aa, where on earth is this place, where has Okiku gone, ohey, Okiku-ya-i, ohey!'

Maga: 'Aha ha ha ha, now, Man, do get a grip, they say you have been going up and down here quite a lot since a while ago, but you must have been possessed by a fox. Because you think of Okiku too much, you meet with such a plight, you see.'

Mankō: 'Where on earth is this place supposed to be?'

'What a dense fellow you are, it is the staircase of Kogitayama. You would not know if you keep your eyes shut, would you? What is that, opening your wide mouth like that?'

'E-e, you made a fool of me. Because I spoke too much ill of the great fox, that wretch has retaliated, hasn't he!'

'Aha ha ha ha, my second generation has been created, ufu ha ha!'

Okiku ran close by,

'Now, Man-sama, what have you been doing here since a while ago?'

Mankō firmly grasped Okiku by the back of her neck,

'You great fox, why are you making a fool of me! Come now, I will not let you off this time, prepare yourself!'
