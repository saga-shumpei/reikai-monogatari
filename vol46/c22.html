Chapter 22: 五三 Storm [1232]

五三嵐〔1232〕

§ 1

Isokō: 'Appearing upon mount Tenkyōzan, / the Hinode Kami, and also / Konohana no / Sakuhime no Mikoto, / changing her form in myriad ways, / entering the bodies of humble mortals, / altering her name to Isokō, / becoming a disciple of Harukuniwake, / to transmit the precious teachings of the gods / throughout lands in the four directions and eighty islands, / to guide, save, and rescue / the people of the world into Tengoku, / crossing over Kajikatauge, / lodging within the grove of the shrine or / the great primeval forest of Yamaguchi, / exposed to the winds and drenched by the rain, / driven ever onward by the blinding blizzards, / passing the dark beneath the trees in the grove of Nonaka / to spend a single night, and coming to the foot of the bridge / in mount Kajikayama, she beheld / Otora-san—whose soul was clouded / and troubled by the Uranai teachings— / and met with Okiku and her child, / and together with Matsuhikotsukasa, / spending one night and two in the headquarters of the Uranai teachings, / while the ugly Magatsukami, terrified instantly / by the divine power of the true deity, / concealed his form and fled away, / leading along a suspicious woman / and vanishing like a passing white wave. / From here, beyond even mentioning the land of Fusa / and the Moon's August Land, / crossing over the Ken'ongō of Mesopotamia / and the Garden of Eden, / marching to attack the Golden Mountain / in Jerusalem where the gods manifest, / utterly striking out with the living Kototama / against the ugly Magatsumagatsuhi, / leaving not a single one behind, / to guide them all away to the paradise / where the flowers of Tengoku bloom and are fragrant, / my heart— / when I think upon it, when I think upon it—is valiant indeed! / Whether the morning sun shines or is clouded, / whether the moon waxes or wanes, / even though the great earth should sink, / even though heaven should become earth and earth heaven / and a returning dark night should draw near, / how could I ever forget or neglect / the divine work of the gods' possession? / Matsuhikotsukasa and Mangō, / the trio of Aku, Taku, and Teku, / and Otora-san of the Uranai teachings— / once illuminated by the light of the gods, / having perfectly comprehended / both love and faith, there is no longer / anything in this world to fear! / General Ranch and Katahiko, gathered in the grove of Ukiki, / and Kumehiko, however valiant he may be, / striking out the Kototama of divine possession / ever so gently, / making them yield and harmonize, / letting them realize from the bottom of their hearts / the lofty virtue of the merciful gods of Misuso, / blowing away the ugly winds rustling on the earth / with the wind of Shinato, / establishing upon the earth a Tengoku / where flowers bloom and fragrance spreads and birds sing, / pure and cool—come now, let us go forward, / walking the path of the gods! / Be valiant, be valiant, be all of you valiant! / Proceed, proceed, be all of you proceeding! / Until the hosts of evil demons perish, / until the ugly devil-gods are lost to naught! / Ah, Mayowagami-kamigami, / may your august spirits prosper and bless us!'

Otora began to sing along the road:

'Of high renown in the village of Ukiki, / a female white-wave gambler, / praised as the Ushitora-baba, / nurturing a multitude of wicked brutes, / aiding the weak and crushing the strong— / so it was said upon the surface, / but the inner reality thereof was / bullying the weak while cowering and trembling before the strong, / a wretchedness manifesting / the ugly posture of the survival of the fittest. / The chivalrous men of the world today / all possess a front and a back; / they never truly aid the weak, / nor can they ever stand against the strong. / There is nothing that surpasses / the mere means of making one's way through the world! / Year by year old age creeps up, / wearing frost upon my head, / and wishing to change my trade / while the slang of the white waves no longer avails me, / even as I watched and waited for an opening, / hearing that the shooting range of the teachings / of Uranai had been opened in the northern mountains, / Otora leaped like a sparrow at the news. / Donning the mask of goodness, / making herself out to be a devout believer, / visiting day after day and night after night, / she insinuated herself into the favor of Salamander-wake, / managed to become the official in charge of internal affairs, / grasped all accounting in her own hands, / accumulated ten thousand yen of gold, / and while planning provisions for her old age, / that old geezer cast aside in days of yore, / the scoundrel Kumagō, came trampling in, / and sitting upon a camp stool / amidst a large and shameful crowd, / shouting aloud with a rattling tongue-twisting speech, / causing such troublesome agony / that she instantly devised a scheme / to take Kumagō into the inner room / and torment him with drink, / stringing together sweet words like larks / in ceaseless chatter, / drinking together and sharing cups, she took delight, / thinking to weaken Kumagō, speak severely and sternly of the way of the gods, / and drive him away— / but who could have ever expected it? Kumagō / hardened his belly and feigned innocence, / demanding that she hand over the ten thousand yen! / The shock of that moment / was a thought as though body and world were no more. / Though she blurred the mess of that very place / through the mediation of Isokō-sama, / with one thousand yen as severance money, / still the heart within her chest remains unsettled— / once the god of the Uranai teachings / surely grants his august protection, / that fellow Kumagō, upon his way, / shall have his body grow numb and his mouth twist awry...'

§ 2

I will reform completely,

accept my money,

and your face will surely dry of tears,

thought I, but it vanished like foam on the water.

Kumakō put the money deep into his bosom,

and with steady steps, slipping away aimlessly,

how hateful it was when he fled.

Neither gods nor Buddhas truly exist in this world,

if I had known things would come to this,

from morning till night I would have performed water ablutions,

subjected my body to freezing trials,

and never worshipped the gods.

I shall smash the countless shrines of all sizes,

and dispel this bitter regret,

just as I was thinking this, Biyakuko-sama,

appearing in the guise of Imoriwake,

gladly handed over a souvenir of thirty thousand yen,

and in my joy at receiving it,

thinking to worship the gods once more,

my wicked heart instantly turned over.

Before I could even rejoice, Imoriwake

swiftly transformed into a black fox.

The money I received was mere stone tile,

what a foolish dream I had seen,

and though I lamented, the darkness in my breast would not return,

when suddenly the voice of the gods cleared it away.

At last returning to my own body,

rectifying and seeing anew, hearing anew,

I prostrated myself before the gods of heaven and earth,

and when I offered my apologies, the murakimo

heart suddenly grew bright,

the moon of true reality shone within the shinten mind,

oh, the joy when it began to gleam.

We are saved by the gods,

this joy we monopolize,

is this a time to merely remain so?

With all our hearts, with all our bodies,

for the sake of the gods, for the sake of the world,

propagating the path of the teaching of ananahi with utmost sincerity,

awakening to the precious gods' true love

and true wisdom, to repay even a fraction of this lofty grace,

how joyful I am as I advance.

Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over,

whether the moon waxes or wanes,

whether the stars fall from the sky,

how could I ever turn back the heart

that I have once pledged to the gods?

May the gods of heaven and earth also witness this.

Fully and minutely accepting the reformation of Ushiba-sama,

making her serve as the precious divine messenger,

may it be so according to your will, O Kami.

Before the precious presence of the gods,

with reverence and awe, I humbly pray,

Ah, Kaminagara, Kami, Kami,

may your august spirits flourish and prosper.'
