Chapter 15: 黎 Bright [1225]

黎明〔1225〕

§ 1

As the sun of ichinichi quietly sank beneath the divine protection of kami, presently a peaceful and tranquil night visited the surface of the earth, the sound of the distant bell, the voices of birds seeking their roosts gradually subsided, and the surroundings grew as silent as death. The sound of the iron kettle's lid being rattled by the steam somehow resonated in the breast of old woman Otora-sama, evoking memories of her chivalrous kefukaku days in the village of Ukiki. Robbed of the thread of life's love and money by Imoriwake, unable even to sleep, and exhausted solely from suppressing the raging kiyōrandotau thundering in her breast, when she faced the kiyōdai in the next room and gazed intently at her own figure, each time she saw her hollowed cheeks, she thought it was no wonder that her own body was growing old and that Imoriwake's departure was likewise only natural, whereupon her both eyes welled up instantly with tears.

Otora: 'Ah, this will not do. I must pull myself together a little more and walk once again upon the path of kamisama.'

She tried thus to encourage her heart with her own hands, but the deep-seated grief of the previous night's vexation and mortification, soaking down to the very joints of her bones, suddenly burst forth, instantly threatening to shatter her adamantine faith and resolve.

Otora: 'Ah, if I were to describe my suffering tonight, it is like being made to embrace stones, seated upon an abacus, and undergoing false torture. The thread connecting people and people has snapped, and I have begun to feel a lonely yearning as I wander aimlessly through an unknown wilderness. Ah, what ever shall I do? I believed in kami, loved kami, and engaged in selfless activity in order to obtain peace of mind. And yet why on earth have I met with such a miserable plight? Unhappy people in this world cannot be limited to this Otora alone. Nevertheless, surely no one else has ever encountered such sorrowful and cruel misfortunes as this humble self. It has become so foolish, so humiliating, and so infuriating that I can no longer remain standing or sitting. Ah, what on earth am I to do?'

Thus she threw her aged body before the kiyōdai and grumbled.

Otora: 'Ah, yes, indeed, indeed. Man possesses three treasures. Those treasures are neither material treasures nor anomalous emotional desires, but rather the romantic love toward kamisama. First is love, second is faith, and third is hope. Without these three joys, one cannot stand even for a single day in a world of pitch darkness. Ah, I have erred, I have erred. True kamisama, Great Deity Susanoo, who guards the Misogikyō, up to this day while receiving the immeasurable and merciful grace of thee, without understanding it in the least, I followed the heretical doctrines of Imoriwake and uttered nothing but discourtesies. The sins of that heart must now have turned into demons to torment me. Ah, my enemy was lurking within my own body. Sweep me away, purify me, Great Deity Susanoo......'

So she clasped her hands, drowned in tears of remorse, and remained for a short while sunk in the abyss of silence. After a little while, a brilliant light shone forth from nowhere, and she felt as if it were enveloping Otora's entire body. Before she knew it, Otora was treading a dream path. At the bottom of her steadfastly sleeping eyes, a beautiful heavenly flower garden had unfolded. She felt a wondrous, indescribable emotion as if she were wandering abouting amidst peonies, herbaceous peonies, and dahlia flowers blooming in brocade-like splendor, together with various red and white butterflies. Otora awoke with a start and muttered to herself,

Otora: 'Ah, illuminated by the light of the deeply merciful Izumo Kamisama, the flower of turning from delusion and awakening to truth has blossomed within my breast. A fragrant breeze now passes, washing my heart. Until now, the desire to save people, to save people, boiled up moment by moment, and I know not how many times it set a fire in my breast; yet, far from saving all the people, I was made to thoroughly realize that I am a frail self who could not even save myself alone. The thorough salvation of oneself alone is precisely the salvation of all people, and the salvation of all people is nothing other than one's own self-awareness—that is, believing in kami, understanding kami, truly loving kami, and being encompassed within them—a truth which, through divine virtue, you have caused me to realize deeply and profoundly, and for this I am most humbly and thankfully grateful.'

§ 2

And the tears filled with sorrow suddenly changed into tears of joy; the sun and moon of Shinniyo shone across the high places of Shinten, and hundreds of thousands of stars came to be quickened by a lofty, graceful, pure, and sublime mood as if wrapping O-tora's body.

O-tora was suddenly choked with tears of rapturous joy, kicked off the bedding to rise, rinsed her mouth and washed her hands, and so as not to awaken anyone's eyes, she stepped with cautious and stealthy paces into the shrine to devoutly and happily chant the norito of gratitude and prayer for the first time from the bottom of her heart.

Truly, there is nothing quite as magnificent as the power of understanding and repentance.

That which keeps the shinrei alive for Eien and makes its nikutai full of seiriyoku and ōsei is truly nothing other than realizing true love, advancing into true shinkau, truly understanding God, and understanding oneself.

Ah, May the Divine Spirits Bless Us.

A storm having passed instantly, The black clouds that enshrouded my body Cleared away as if wiped clean, By the divine sacred light of Miroku, The soul that had been utterly lost Was illuminated like lapis lazuli light, And both my haggard body and soul Felt as though they were suddenly granted Infinite divine power. The moon of Shinniyo shone In the sky of the heart within the breast, Clear light brilliant and dazzling wrapped my body, The Milky Way lay stretched out long, East, west, north, and south, The heavenly august sky and the depths of the earth alike Without a single point of suspicious cloud, Hell was transformed into the realm Of supreme joy and supreme bliss, And I became one who joyfully plays. Ah, Ah, True Gods, True Gods,

The hitomitama of man Is the vast, boundless, supreme, holy, noble, Purer than pure, A fractional spirit of the Great Ōkami. Depending upon how My one and only soul Bears itself, this world May become the Heavenly Pure Land, Or may change into the Asura path of hell. How wretched it was that By the small desires of this earthly realm My soul had been defiled and my heart Disturbed and disordered. The Heavenly Pure Land is right before our eyes, Yet not knowing that it is open Within the very breast of my own body, O people of the world who drift and suffer In the desire-realm of selfish motives and selfish desires, When one peers into your circumstances, Truly it is the height of wretchedness.

I was saved by God first of all, Saved by God and tasted The supreme joy and supreme bliss of heaven. Ah, from now on, from now on, To crush my body to repay The benevolent virtue of the precious God, To dedicate my soul for the sake of the Way, And for the sake of the people of the world to the utmost, Should I not devote myself without fail? Kindness is not for the sake of others, It is a treasure that saves one's own body, Such is the joy of today's realization. Having received the benevolence and grace Of the Great Ōkami of infinite benevolence and compassion, I, who had strayed into hell and Asura, Was not saved at all. While leaving my own body in hell, How foolish it was of me to think That I would universally save the people of the world Who fall into the sorrowful waters and suffer. Thinking of this, Imoriwake-sama, Ominu no Kimi, Opened the gates of my heart for my sake, As the Savior of infinite benevolence and compassion.

When I proclaim this anew and look upon it thus, There is no enemy beneath the heavens, Resentment and jealousy have vanished away, And it is entirely the heart of God. When I reflect upon it, when I reflect upon it, how precious it is, To wash away the grime of my own body, The blessings of the Spirit of Mizu, Now I joyfully comprehend The august heart with which Great God Susanoo no Ōkami Casts the lingering rays of benevolence upon the earth To save the common people suffering in darkness.

The Uranai teachings praised by Takahime-tsukasa Seem on the surface Like words of salvation from a God of infinite benevolence, Yet the surface and the reverse are contradictory and unstable; Suddenly the weather completely changes, A fearful heart like that of thunder flashing And a violent storm blowing up Is made to arise, weakening the spirit, And I realized it to be the crooked teachings of Magatsuhi That forcibly draw one inward.

Saigendai Hinoo no Ten no Kami, His wife Kamuyoshihime no Kami, Lintōbiten, Kisoyoshihime, Ikuba Jinja, Iwateruhime, Nikutomiya of Miroku Fulfillment, Asahi no Toyosaka Noborihime, Giритеnyū of Hinode no Kami, Tamakoruhime, and of the earthly world, Great August Deity Hinomarumhime, Daishōgun, Tokoyohime, Hege-no hege-no hegemushiya, Great August Deity of Hege Shrine, Husband and wife deities of Tanebutsu Shrine, Spiritual deities of the sixteen pillars Of the divine tree at the great root,

Twisting out such strange teachings And falling into the superstition That these deceptive words Deceiving foolish people Were things than which nothing could be greater, Morning and evening they pained the heart within the breast, Wore away their bodies, And body and mind alike emaciated, The hell-road wherein they suffered When I reflect upon it from now on, It has become terrifying indeed.

The sun of the heavenly august sky walks, The moon travels, and the stars move on, While living in Takamanohara Upon the earth where the Milky Way flows, Unknowingly they fell away into Ne no Kuni And Soko no Kuni, Day and night continuing to kindle Asura, Seized by love and desire, How pitiable it is that they were suffering so.

The tears of repentance cannot be stemmed.

Tears flow in torrents down my cheeks. The darkness of mumiyō has broken open, and today is a joyful rain of tears, transmitting a sacred preciousness down my cheeks. Ah, these tears, these tears, washing away the souls of yobito, surely they are the dew of the pristine mitama. May they remain never-dried, always and forever. Flowing and flowing to the Kajikagaha, to any clear rapids whatsoever, without ever ceasing, may you wash the mitama of the magabito who are sunk in the dark sea.

Kami is with you. Man is the child of Kami, the shrine of Kami, being equal unto Kami. The seigen thus spoken, illuminated by the kuōmiyō of jinji, are realized through my own body where the flower of kuwaigo has blossomed,

Verily proven things. My own body stained with magu, saving itself and well enlivening, prospering and thereafter enlivening and rescuing the retsu of yobito of the world, constructing a tengoku of unparalleled saugon universally upon chijiyō, embodying the ōomikokoro of Kuniharutachinōomikami, Toyokuninushinōomikami, Kamususanōnokamubashira, and all the myriad other Kami besides, advancing ever more to proclaim it, and helping the ujiko of Kami. Make me a precious office. Utora, who until now wandered in darkness, reverently before the miomae, repenting, giving thanks, and trembling with awe, prays for the mitama-no-fuyu. Ah, kanagara kamukamu,

Mitama, prosper and bless us.'

Utora thus revived from within her spiritual realm, feeling as though she had resurrected into tengoku, intoxicated by a pleasant and refreshing mood for the first time since entering the faith, recited the norito of thanks and prayer before the miomae of the Infinite Kami of Jinji who protects Misoshiru-kyō, and returned joyfully to her own living room. At this time the night had cleared up completely, and she came to realize deeply within her body that the voices of myriad birds and magpies, flying over the ridges of the mountains and celebrating the eternal spring of tokoyo, were heard much more refreshingly and dependably than usual.
