Chapter 6: 千代心 [1216]

千代心〔1216〕

§ 1

Takekō stood up, climbed upon the endan, puffed out his cheeks, poured water from a tin dokuri into a cup, drank and drank again, exposed a face like a dokuri, thrust his chin diagonally forward with a sudden jerk, pressed firmly down upon the table with his riyōte, bent his waist like a bow, and began to deliver his jiyutsukuwai.

Takekō-sama born in the village of Ukiki, that is none other than myself; the fortune saved since my parents' generation is not excessively large, yet it was just enough for a husband and wife to live at leisure throughout their itsushiyō; just when we possessed such butsushitsuteki zaisan, Otora-san came flying in from early in the morning, chanted the norito of the Uranai Kyō in a booming voice, and said, 'Now, now, Take-sama, the tatekahe of this sekai has begun, the evil sekai will metsubō, and the sekai will diminish by sanbu; after that, to save the sekai, the Mirokubosatsu will appear, and build a ketsukō kamiyo—this the kyōso no kimi proclaimed.

Is it not a ketsukō thing? Born into such a jidai, to say nothing of myself, the mitama of you husband and wife are the original, original, distant-past ancestors of this sekai, belonging to the foundational root lineage of the Kamisama Kuniharutachi, and the spirit of the Ōkami of the Mirokubosatsu tatejiyō is dwelling within you! Would you not like to offer all your butsushitsuteki zaisan to the Kamisama, break up your household, proceed up to the Kohokuzan Taiseijō, and perform ketsukō goyō from morning till night? The Ofuku-sama of your household, you too are a ketsukō mitama, the Asahi no Toyosaka Noborihime, the noble divine okusan of the Mirokubosatsu shinsei tatejiyō!' Thus did she string together sweet words, speaking as if mochi were growing on a withered tree, all filled with good omens; and so, before I had even time to tilt my head in thought, Ofuku waved her hands, and suddenly a divine possession occurred.

'I am Ofuku of the mitama with the karmic bond to whom the Asahi no Toyosaka Noborihime has descended, O Takekō! You are the raw shrine of the imperial deities of the Mirokubosatsu tatejiyō; if you should doubt or disobey the shinshoku spoken by Asahi, a severe divine punishment will fall upon you, and it will haunt you even unto seven generations!' Thus she spoke, alternating between soothing and threatening through the mouth of my current wife; and so, having become thoroughly convinced that the kami never tells a lie, I fell hopelessly ill with it.

Not heeding the dissuasions of neighbors and relatives, I sold off our house and storehouses for a mere song, converted the whole into money, and thought: 'Since this sekai is changing any moment now, the butsushitsuteki zaihō are bound to collapse into ruin and scatter to the winds in a clatter of clatter and smash of smash before long; the fact that I heard the ketsukō divine teachings before anyone else is simply because my mi-tama is good; I cannot stay like this!' I accepted the words of grandmother Otora without a single objection or dual thought, and we husband and wife were chosen here as caretakers among the believers of the uranai, being fed nothing but worthless things from morning till night—turnips, radishes, potatoes, and burdock—taking these as our sole feast.

We have continued to serve thus far, but as I listen to the recent words of Taku, Teku-san, and Okiku-sama, my eyes seem to have begun to open; though I used to be arrogant, thinking myself the Ōkami of the Mirokubosatsu tatejiyō, it now appears that this fellow must be suspicious! An old tanuki of the Kohoku mountains has surely made my body its lodging and is nestling within; in the body of my wife Ofuku, too, an old tanuki is nesting, and while claiming to possess tennantsū, it blinds my wife's eyes, makes her see and hear strange things, and is surely making a fool of us. When I think of it, how shameful! Though I detest Otora-sama who deceived us, this too is doubtless the recompense for evil committed since ancient times,

which has now come forth to manifest itself; if by means of such a thing all the sins and defilements hitherto can be completely wiped away and purified, it is truly a cheap price to pay! Since matters have come to this, I shall devoutly obey the true divine teachings of Misoso, and dedicate my heart and soul for the sake of the Way and for the sake of mankind—Ame-no-mi-naka-nushi, praying humbly before the kami. O Ofuku, from now on you too must completely rebuild your heart, and such conceit as calling yourself the Asahi no Toyosaka Noborihime must never be entertained again—Ame-no-mi-naka-nushi! Awakened to the kami, Takekō will pay a little attention to you—ah, Ame-no-mi-naka-nushi, may your august spirits be prosperous and tranquil!'

§ 2

Chiyo climbed upon the dais, and with a smile playing upon her small face, began to chant.

Compared to the wisdom and power Of the noble and true Kamisama, The knowledge and power of human beings, Who are the living palaces of Kami, Are no more than a single drop of water In the vast ocean; Yet, for all that is said, man is nonetheless The lord of all creation. Since the noble Kami protect us And watch over us from above, There can surely be no reason Why evil should ever trespass against us. Just as you pray with all your heart, Filling your soul with sincerity And addressing the Kamisama of Tenchi, By the very power of that faith, Neither Yamataorochi, nor Shikōni, Nor Kinmaukiubi, however they might try, Could ever find a way to harm you. All the people gathered here upon this Miyama Are none other than righteous souls, Surpassing all others in the world. I had thought you to be persons of some understanding, And yet, how pitiful it is! Even to the eyes of a child like me, Deceptions that are plain as day Appear as pure truth To souls bewildered by greed. From morning till night, they slander The noble and true Kamisama, Enshrine nameless, deceitful kami Within magnificent shrines, While a bearded man, looking so pleased, Claps his hands together like a coal shovel, And prays with all his might. When I viewed such a sight from the side, I suddenly burst out laughing, Fell flat on my bottom and soiled my robes, And for that, I received a severe scolding From Matsuhime-sama.

Ah, truly, when human beings Are bewildered by worldly desires, Their two eyes grow utterly blinded, Their ears are stopped up, and evil deeds Resound in their ears as the merciful words of Kami. The functions of the five senses instantly Fall into complete disorder, Until even the heart and spirit, which matter most, Grow twisted and clouded, Making them spiritually blind, unable to see Before or behind, Leading them to live out Pitiful lives of woe—how sad it is! Matsuhime-sama, morning and evening, Wishes to sweep away everyone's superstitions And save them upon the Great Highway of Truth; She devotes her whole heart to this, Yearning to praise the true august name of Sumeragi, And yet— O Lord God Susanoo,

And Great August Goddess Toyokunihime, If one dares to utter such noble divine names openly, Tōmorokowake rolls his eyes in fury, And his mood becomes especially displeased. Even grannie joins in at his heels And utters such bitter complaints, That enduring and forbearing until this very day, You have made your residence apart from them, Praying to the true Kami with all your heart, Hiding it so that none might hear; And because of those earnest prayers, Today at last the clouds that shrouded Tenchi Have cleared away completely, Like the true Kamisama of Hinodewake Shining forth in brilliance. This is the auspicious opening of the Way, Which I reverently celebrate here. Without a doubt, all these things Have been manipulated by the deep designs of Kamisama,

And brought about by employing the hands Of the kami of Magatsuhi. If people whose hearts are clouded Were taught from the very beginning Nothing but straightforward truth, They would hardly ever draw near. Therefore, the Kamisama of Tenchi, Leaving things as the kami of Magatsuhi would have them, Close Their eyes awhile and let us await The coming of the dawn, Saving bewildered souls into the heavenly realm— How gracious indeed! When we reflect upon this, not a single thing That any of you have hitherto offered to Kami Has been in vain; It is all the divine work of Kamisama, And since you are those who have served with distinction, Do not let your spirits droop, nor your strength fail; Exert your courage ever more,

And from today, rebuild your shimandama, Waiting for the brilliant light of the true Kamisama To shine across the sanctuary of Kohigashiyama. Ah, Awa-no-Megamigami, May your august spirits be blessed and prosperous!

Kikukō climbs upon the dais and chants her reflections.

Following the divine office of Tōmorokowake and Magahiko, We set out from Kitayama Village, And when we gradually arrived here, There were a father and child Laboriously chiseling away At the steep slope of Sakateruyama, Making the sounds kochi-kochi-kochi. Feeling a strange wonder, I drew near their side And asked them with the utmost politeness, 'What kami might you be? I pray you, tell me your illustrious name.' Throwing down the pickaxe that had grown like a priest's staff Upon the bedrock, And wiping away the dripping sweat, 'I am the Golden Kami of Northeast, Fallen in the world and now reduced To humble, menial labor in the fields; In such spare moments, I climb to this mountain To shatter the rocks, Where the noble Kami is enshrined...'

Parent and child are piercing the foundation bedrock day and night; though I am but a lowly Shiyuda, I am the living shrine of Daishiyōgun. The spirit of this child is Chijiyōmaru; I know not why, but of itself, one's arms began to move, and such a hard rock mountain has smoothly become a place with many level areas. If we enshrine Kami here, how splendid it would surely become! Hearing these words, Imoriwake and Magahiko-sama clapped their hands, saying it is truly admirable indeed; if this were a mere human, how could it ever have been cleared thus? Surely this must be a sacred ground. Let us first inquire of Kami to search out and investigate the truth or falsehood.

Fixing my wife Oaku's consciousness as a living shrine possessed by Kami, I solemnly offered norito, and when I reverently inquired, my wife Oaku waved her hands, changing even the tone of her voice, and declared: 'Kikukō, listen well! Oaku is your wife, but she is the living shrine of Kisoyoshihime. From now on, as she becomes possessed by Kami, until a splendid shrine of Kami is built in this sacred ground, never think of her as a wife! Separate your night bedding, perform water ablutions in the Kajika River, and if you both undertake this to serve the divine work, I shall brilliantly manifest the guardian deity of Kikukō-sama,' she solemnly proclaimed thus.

Whether it be the guardian deity of Yamata no Orochi, or the mitama of the golden-furred nine-tailed fox—at the very time I was worrying and troubled, how joyful I felt when I heard that she was a living shrine deeply linked to Minister Rintōbiten! From then on, husband and wife took heed of all construction matters day and night, without even sleeping properly with our eyes closed, and served in the divine tasks. Tak, tek, if what Otora-sama says is the truth, we husband and wife are incomprehensibly foolish, having exhausted the height of stupidity; just as anger began to well up thinking to demolish the shrine of Kami from the entrance, little Ochiyo-sama, upon her pure and undefiled soul, was possessed by a noble Kami.

How joyful it was when she taught the principle of the non-duality of good and will! Now, from here on, in all things we shall follow the divine will of Sumeru Ōkami, and never utter a single word of complaint. Let us enjoy each and every day and firmly perform the divine service. All you who are seated here in a row, you must certainly have been thinking just like me. Following my example, from now on, in all things simply according to Kami's will, with reverence and awe we shall serve Kami, striving with our bodies laid bare. Ah, Kamunagara! Blow away the clouds and fog of confusion for Kikukō before the divine presence, return the title of Rintōbiten, and add us to the number of the people.

May You hear this peacefully and very tranquilly, as we exhaust ourselves to the utmost limits of our hearts and bodies, and we earnestly entreat You alone.'
