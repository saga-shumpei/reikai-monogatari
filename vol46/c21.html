Chapter 21: 民の Tiger Voice [1231]

民の虎声〔1231〕

§ 1

Fleeing under the cover of darkness from the sacred domain of Mount Kogita, wishing to fulfill her romantic desires, and talking with Imoriwake, she crossed the bridge of Kajika, and upon coming to the forest of Nonaka, heard an unexpected human voice, so numerous that they seemed like scores of people, murmuring and buzzing as it came to her ears.

Otaimi, startled and flustered, turned back the way she originally came, and out of breath and shuffling heavily, aiming once more for the forest of Nonaka, she advanced to take a detour back onto the main road of Kajika Pass.

In the shade of the eerie trees of the forest, four or five men sat upon folding camp stools, engaged in whispering chats, enduring the chill of a winter's night while trembling as they kept their vigil.

Such events being beyond the knowledge of a mortal body, weeping the while, with loneliness piercing her winter path, and her heart still drawn backward, the shadow of someone coming swiftly apace appeared.

The three men, Kō, Waku, and Emu, called out toward the woman, shouting that though they knew not what sort of wretch she might be, it was the checkpoint of the Brahman army, and passage was strictly forbidden; whereupon the woman laughed aloud, asking what wrong there was in traveling upon the great public highway free to all under heaven, and saying that they should clear away their obstruction, she attempted to press forward without paying any heed whatsoever.

When the men spread wide their arms and blustered that they would not let her go a single step, Otaimi raised her voice, declaring that she would make them repent for the sake of the future, you childish warriors, and as she spoke—

Pressing in upon them from front, rear, left, and right, she grabbed the necks of the men, struck them down to right and left, and even as she engaged in the fierce struggle, there was a man running along the narrow path overgrown with withered grass, making the earth resound with a thudding roar.

Upon seeing this state of affairs, you called out just 'Otaimi,' and your legs went out from under you so that you collapsed with a groan by the roadside—how comical it was!

Otaimi turned her head back and said, 'Are you Imoriwake-sama? How wonderful that you have come; I beg of you, please lend me your supporting sword.'

Imoriwake, remaining calm, let out a great laugh—'Ha ha ha ha ha ha ha ha, ha ha ha!'—and said, 'What business is it of yours to take fly-insects that are not even fit to serve as attendants and make opponents of them?'

I have come here at my leisure, exerting myself to be a spectator of such a valiant and lively action, so do show me an entertaining play—ah, how amusing, how amusing!

Uttering such words in a sudden display of sore defeat, how comical it was how he patched up the situation.

Both Otaimi and her opponents were utterly exhausted, resting their breath side by side by the roadside, and at length, upon recognizing the dawn of a reconciliation, Imoriwake threw out the large sum of money in his possession and began to act as if he were paying indemnities.

To such a place as this appeared a single Ekisu, chosen as a Brahman inspector, and once more it was amusing how he opened up negotiations here.

Imoriwake, who was free from desires and indifference, caring nothing for money, hurled away all the money he possessed in a lump,

and as a favorite of the Ekisu inspector, together with Otaimi, he was placed into a palanquin within the military camp, and being brought before the commander's curtained headquarters, with the single thought harbored in his breast of smashing the teachings of the Three-Five, he advanced toward the main camp.

While the three men, Kō, Waku, and Emu, were performing their guard duties in the eerie forest just as before,

a propaganda song was heard from afar, echoing like thunder.

Everyone pressed their ears with both hands, hid in the tree shadows, and trembled while waiting for the voice to pass.

Matsuhiko left the sacred ground of Mount Kogita, crossed the single-plank bridge, and straightway began to sing a propaganda song.

'The Kami have appeared in the manifest world, distinguishing between righteous Kami and evil Kami.

The Kami Naobi who created this world, the Great Naobi whose heart is vast.

Truly, whatever may happen in the world of man, look upon it again with Naobi and listen to it again with Naobi,

proclaiming anew the errors of one's body, the Way of the Kami of the Three-Five Teachings.

Awe-inspiring is the rigorous teaching of the Great Deity Kuninotokotachi,

and upon the high plateau of the native mountain, the Great Deity Kami Susanoo

has erected his precious august dwelling with its lofty chigi cross-posts,

and serving Him, forever dwelling in peace beneath the heavens,

He proclaims the path of salvation to the multitudes of countries in the four directions.

The propaganda emissaries who follow the teachings, the party of Kuninowake,

having ridden over Kajika Pass,

and at the descent of the southern slope,

just as they were advancing onward,

Katahiko of the Brahman teachings,

leading General Kumehiko,

serving as the vanguard of Lunch,

at the eighth station of Kajika Pass,

just as they were advancing, struck down and shattered by the Kototama of Kuninowake of the Three-Five,

scattering in utter rout like the falling leaves of an autumn tree—how pitiable they are!

I went to the curtained headquarters of General Katahiko, became his secretary, and just as I was heading toward the subjugation of the mansion of Saien,

touched by the compassionate light of the Imperial Kami, I was revived,

and by a wondrous bond, I met again my longing elder brother Kamehiko,

the story of the days that have passed since our parting—

here the brothers proclaim their names and lineage.'

§ 2

Bidding farewell to Tamakuniwake, they departed from the grove of the shrine, and as they descended the steep slope, they spent a night in the grove of Yamaguchi, their eyes wide with astonishment at the wondrous divine providence.

Coming to the grove of Nonaka, they beheld yet more wondrous things alone, and while passing the night there, they parted from Harukuniwake, knowing not what to do, yet weeping as they went.

Beginning with Isokō and Mankō, and taking the three—Aku, Taku, and Teku—in their train, as they advanced along the path through the fields, they came to the one-legged bridge on the opposite bank of the mountain of Kogita.

Drawn once again by a wondrous divine affinity, they climbed the mountain of Kogita, pacified the lurking Magatsu, and enshrined the righteous Kami.

Meeting unexpectedly with his wife and daughter, and rejoicing in the merciful love of Kami, he could not stem his tears of gratitude.

Departing once more from this place, he pursued the footsteps of Harukuniwake, advancing proudly with flying banners and beating drums toward the enemy camp in the grove of Ukiki.

Ah, how marvelous, how marvelous! There is no Kami that opposes righteousness.

Granted the infinite divine power of the Lord God Susanoo, may He safely fulfill this mission, Kannagara.

We pray before the awesome presence: whether the morning sun shines or is clouded, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even though the great earth should sink, this body is entrusted unto Kami.

Though we should die, though we should fall, there is never anything to regret.

Entrusting everything entirely to Kami-sama at the will of our hearts, the one who advances is fearless.

The Yamato spirit fills our breasts, our arms groan and our flesh leaps for joy.

Even an army of millions of enemies we shall not quail from, nor fear, but advance with a soul proud and majestic.

Already he had been transformed anew.

Ah, how courageous, how courageous! The power of Kami is beheld before our very eyes,

Abiding even with our lowly selves, advancing through the swarming enemy camp is like walking through an uninhabited field.

Ah, Kannagara, Kami, Kami, may your august spirits prosper and bless us, he prayed.'

Mankō sings again.

Departing from the mansion of Saien and arriving at length at this place,

Meeting unexpectedly with Otora-sama and even with Okiku,

At the very moment of wiping away the tears of an inescapable karmic bond,

Kept alive by the grace of Kami, the sacred place upon the mountain of Kogita

Is once more in the full glory of spring blossoms.

The darkness of Tokoyo clears away, the sun and moon shine purely across, the air grows exceedingly clear,

And the deep mountains, where demons hitherto had made their nests, are now the land of Kami,

Having become a heavenly pure land.

Following after Matsuhiko-tsukasa, ascending upon the military road of the demons,

Mankō, a servant of Kami, is a propagandist rare in the world,

Accompanying Harukuniwake,

To the capital of Haruna, let alone the Jerusalem where Kami abides,

Directing toward the Mountain of Gold, turning every single one of the army of Oniharubetsu

Toward the true path of three-five,

This departure to save and help them,

Ah, Kannagara, Kami, Kami, blessed by the grace of Kami,

As quickly as possible, even for a single moment, may you fulfill the divine work of the divine dwelling,

Before the august and precious presence of Heaven and Earth, Mankō reverently and awe-stricken prays.'
