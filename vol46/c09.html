Chapter 9: 文 Bright [1219]

文明〔1219〕

§ 1

From morning till night Bunsuke sat like a motionless statue, clad in Shiroshiyōzoku and Shirobakama, continuing to draw a black snake with a single horn entwined around a pine tree, along with turnips and daikon radishes. Okiku came stealthily up to him and spoke,

Okiku: 'I say, Bunsuke-sama, won't you please come over here for a moment? I will show you something most fascinating.'

Bunsuke: 'No matter how fascinating it may be, asking me to look at it is an impossible request. It is the same as watching a blind man's play or listening to a deaf man's jiyōruri recitation, so you had best stop.'

Okiku: 'What, if you are able to draw turnips and daikon radishes, you cannot possibly be unable to see. If you wish to see, surely you can see. Indeed, something truly strange is happening. Come quietly before anyone notices.'

Bunsuke: 'As the receptionist cannot simply leave his post and abandon this place, I shall ask you to let me see it another time when I am free.'

Okiku: 'It will not do if you speak of such a leisurely matter. You must come and see right now. The truth of the matter is that Imoriwake-sama has returned. And he is giving thirty thousand ryo in gold to our mother-sama.'

Bunsuke: 'What, he has given thirty thousand yen in money to mother-sama? As expected of Imoriwake-sama. Since he slipped away secretly last night, there has been a tremendous commotion turning heaven and earth upside down, and I was worried about how it would all turn out, but oh, how grateful and thankful I am! If he has returned, Kogitayama is safe. Thank you, O Kami, how grateful I am.'

Okiku: 'Please come and take a brief look. I simply cannot get over how wondrous it is.'

Bunsuke: 'What, money is something that humans possess, so there is nothing particularly wondrous about him bringing back some thirty thousand yen. Since it is he, there is no way he would steal someone's property. It must be that the Kami graciously caused the money to fall down. And what did mother-sama do with that money?'

Okiku: 'She immediately stuffed it right into her bosom.'

Bunsuke: 'Well, she has done a very good thing. It would not do to leave it in his keeping. Thank you, O Kami, how grateful I am.'

And he worshipped again.

Okiku: 'What, that is not all of it. When I asked about it afterwards, he said he still has another two hundred and seventy thousand ryo hidden in his bosom.'

Bunsuke: 'What, twenty-seven myriads of yen? Ha, ha, ha, that is most likely a mishearing. It must be twenty-seven sen.'

Okiku: 'How absurd! I am not such a fool as to mistake twenty-seven myriads of yen for twenty-seven sen. He certainly has twenty-seven hundred thousand ryo, and saying he would give a tenth, he tossed out thirty thousand ryo; and moreover, he said he had given twenty myriads of ryo to Otami-sama. Bunsuke-sama, please hurry and come. He is saying he will go right now to Otami-sama's place. Hearing that, I was beside myself with worry, so I secretly came to rely upon you. Imoriwake-sama will listen to what you say, but he will not listen to what our mother says.'

Bunsuke: 'That is a terrible affair. If such a person is left holding such a vast sum of money, one never knows what sort of things he might do. Because he is of a good nature, he might be straightway taken advantage of by others, and it would be a terrible thing if he brought the name of the Kami into disrepute. In that case, let us go.'

Okiku: 'Bunsuke-sama, please come quietly. It might not be convenient if too many people hear of it.'

The receptionist Bunsuke, hearing a tale he never expected, was stirred with eagerness he knew not why, and as he raised his heavy lower limbs, possessing eyes that could not see an inch before him, relying on his staff as he trudged along, following closely behind Okiku, making straight for the mansion where Imoriwake had his dwelling. Okiku, walking on ahead, said, 'Hark, hark, I pray you, Bunsuke-sama, here is the outer entrance. Come, come, let me take your hand and guide you to the revered chamber of the Kyōso.' Hearing this, Bunsuke nodded in assent, while wearing a gentle smile, showing a maturity far beyond his youth, praising her as a thoughtful maiden who noticed everything, from turnips down to the very tips of rapeseed greens.

§ 2

Stepping forward and entering the inner room,

Bunsuke: 'Hey there, hey there, Otora-sama! A wonderful thing has happened. The Kyōso-sama of Imoriwake has returned home safely, and I am so glad. Moreover, bringing a whole lot of money as a souvenir upon his return, I heard and leaped up, and straightway offered my thanks to the Ōkamisama. Ever since I heard last night that Imoriwake-sama had made a kakeochi, the upper and lower rooms of the mansion have been in a great uproar. The faith of numerous believers began to wobble, and even I have been racking my brains. On top of that, by the precious grace of the Kamisama, he is returning safely with three hundred thousand ryo of gold—there is nothing to compare it to, it is the height of joy! Hey there, hey there, Kyōso-sama, stealing the reception time of Bunsuke the receptionist, I have come here led by the hand of Okiku-sama, thinking I must offer my greetings. Please do let me hear your splendid story! It is truly a happy thing. Cranes dance and play for a thousand years, turtles sing and dance for ten thousand years—how on earth did such a happy, happy thing come about? This must surely be the virtue of the divine protection of Yurarihiko, the eternal Sun King, the God of Heaven, the Great Deity of Miroku Completion, Rintōbiten Ōkami, the righteous heavens of the Hinodewake God, the Great General, Tokoyahime, Asahi no Toyosaka Noborihime, and all the other precious Kamisama! Ah, marvelous are the gods, marvelous! No matter how much I say how happy I am, there is no way my mouth will close! Imoriwake-sama, Otora-sama, do not tease me too much and tell me quickly, my heart is getting impatient!'

As he shouted thus, Otora picked up her astonished and collapsed body abruptly, spread a cushion neatly in front of the brazier, sat down properly, placed both hands on her knees, and replied,

Otora: 'Bunsuke-sama, how well you have trudged along to visit with your poor eyesight! Today I have received wonderful divine virtue for the very first time. It feels as if the Heavenly Rock Cave has opened. Please rejoice with me, it is truly an auspicious day.'

Hearing this, Bunsuke sprang up in high spirits,

Bunsuke: 'Well now, that was a splendid thing! The Kyōso-sama of Imoriwake received great divine virtue and came home putting three hundred thousand ryo into his bosom, and gave one-tenth of it, thirty thousand ryo, clean to you, just as I heard from Okiku-sama! It is truly a happy thing. I feel as happy as if I had received it directly myself. And yet, is Imoriwake-sama's voice naturally inaudible, or is he sleeping soundly because he is utterly exhausted? If that is the case, it would truly be a regrettable matter of disturbing his sleep if I were to stay, so I will take my leave now. Hey there, hey there, Otora-sama, young mistress Okiku-sama, from now on, with your permission, after completely finishing the picture of the turnip I left half-drawn, receiving my leave to stand watch on the stage, I shall stretch out my legs and sleep soundly tonight of all nights! Goodbye to you all!'

As he stood up, Otora called out to him,

Otora: 'Bunsuke-sama, when I said just now that I had received splendid divine virtue and that the Heavenly Rock Cave had opened, I was not talking about money. A million ryo is still foolish; I have received divine virtue beyond all bounds, countless like tens of millions, that cannot be compared in any way! Just as Imoriwake-sama suddenly returned to this place and lined up thirty thousand ryo of koban dazzlingly right before my eyes, before I knew it, Imoriwake-sama suddenly changed his form, and crying 'Kuwai, kuwai, kuwai' like an ox resembling an old fox, hid his figure by heading toward the forest in the valley yonder. Perhaps because my greed was too great, and only thinking of foul things morning and evening became a sin, causing my pure and precious soul to naturally grow cloudy—rather than seeing this state, the lotus flower opening all at once, the darkness of my heart opening smoothly, there is nothing so happy as this! As you already know, the Kyōso-sama of Imoriwake lets his body and soul rot in alcohol, shakes his head in a daze before and after, and has become a mental patient. No matter how much you cherish such an incomprehensible man, the divine path will never expand anywhere. Moreover, that man is bewildered by a woman named Otami, yearns for Takahime who is on the path of Sanmi, and suffers in his chest morning and night, drinking sake to dispel his melancholy—knowing that no matter how much I cherish such a person, it is already no use, my attachments and everything else—'
