Chapter 8: 母と娘 [1073]

母と娘〔1073〕

§ 1

In the Moon Country where the moon shines across, protected by Bontenō, because it is a pure and noble land, it was called the Brahman Country. Originally, the Moon God Country took its places as its nations, numbering over seven thousand. It made the Setsuteirizoku its kings, and the Brahman tribe strove for Jiyōgiyō as their sole duty, but the old Fūshifu could no longer resist the power of time. The Setsuteirizoku met with Sanzan Ukime and were Heisoku, and now the order of the country has been disturbed to the point where the Brahman tribe completely Shiyōaku it. Within those over seven thousand countries, appearing in the Haruna Country, which is the widest, and in the capital of Haruna,

The Magatsukami who falsely claimed to be a descendant of Bontenō, Onikumohiko, left the Nakatsumikuni of Ashihara, crossed the Fusa Country, and crossed over to Onokorojima. He was wielding his Baui, but he was repelled by the Ananahi Oshihetsukasa protected by Kamususanōnōokami. He fled through the Chiukū like clouds and mist to the Indo Country. Appearing in the capital of Haruna, while enshrining the Jizaiten and Ōkuninushi of Bontenō, he advocated Reishiyutaijiu. It is truly detestable that he is spreading a cruel and lawless teaching. From the distant age of the gods, inheriting the throne,

The pure Setsuteirizoku who occupied the thrones of these countries were oppressed, and the noblemen of the country were persecuted. Ōkuninushi, who was known as the Brahman tribe, sent his subordinates to those countries. He had them grasp the full authority of both the manifest and the hidden, while he himself pretended to be the Great King of the Indo Country. At times, the Kōseishin who protects the Saienkan of Mount Ubusuna, leading many divine beings, gathered the gods here to drive away the Onikumo that fill heaven and earth morning and evening. With the words of Hinodewake, starting with the divine command of Yoshikohime and Kiyoteruhime, Terukuniwake and Tamakuniwake,

Entrusting the words to the divine official Tatsuwakihime, they advance the divine plan to proceed from three directions in the Indo Country. Ah, Kannagara, may the spirits be blessed. May this story run swiftly, and may the Kototama-guruma run well. I pray before the gods that you may tell the deep story of the age of the gods, which investigated the Moon Country that circles ten thousand miles without leaving a single corner, Kannagara.

Yoshikohime and Kiyoteruhime, disguised in pilgrim attire, wandered up and down the Kajika Pass, gazing at the magnificent scenery of the valley. Just as they were resting, they encountered Ham, Eel, and three other subordinates of Onikumawake. Finally, knowing it was an improper affair, yet unable to do otherwise for the sake of self-defense, they threw the three, Ham, Eel, and Joseph, toward the thousand-fathom valley. While coldly watching the other two flee like clouds and mist, the mother and daughter, while paying attention to their surroundings, sang as they proceeded along the rugged, winding mountain path.

The wife of Onikumawake, the vice-leader of the Brahman religion, appeared in the Egypt Country. While she was transmitting the teaching, she was working without knowing the evil deeds that violated the heavenly principles. But illuminated by the light of the gods of the infinite benevolence of Ananahi, she fled the capital of Iho. Following Onikumohiko, she hid herself in the Ken-on-kyō. While the days and months passed, and she was finally solidifying the foundation of the Brahman religion,

The propaganda envoy of Futodama-no-Mikoto appeared and emitted the Kototama that would shake heaven and earth. Starting with the great leader Onikumohiko, their entire clan like clouds and mist, led by the great snake of the vice-guardian, hid themselves in the Onigajo of the San-dake, the rocky cave of the Oeyama, where the fruit of the center of Onokorojima, known as the safe zone, stands. They determined their posts and propagated the Brahman teaching in all directions. Thinking that they would make the Mizuho Country of Toyoashihara and even the ends of the one hundred and eighty islands submit to the Brahman religion, and that they would illuminate the teachings and the divine majesty of Bontenō in all directions, it all became bubbles of water. Driven away by the strong men of the divine country who served Eiko-hime, Onikumohiko fled helplessly. My husband also followed after him and hid his figure.

Thinking that she would not leave her first intention to the end of a woman's heart, she abandoned Onigajo. She hid deeply in the rocky cave of Mikunidake, passing through the clouds, which was a hiding place in the safe zone. While she was using many subordinates and planning the revival of the Brahman religion, the propaganda envoy climbed the sky of white clouds, perhaps by the grace of the gods. The evil deeds were suddenly exposed, and in the sadness of having no place to live, her heart's horse ran wild. While hiding her body in the rocky cave of Shodoshima, in the strait between good and evil, and opening the Brahman teaching, she was in the midst of anxiously searching for the whereabouts of Koito-hime.

§ 2

Having unexpectedly encountered Tomohiko, whose heart was twisted, I grasped the tidings of my child. Steering a boat without a shelf, I joined forces with Takahime of Ananai-kyō and crossed the Unabara to reach the island of Hitotsu. Ascending to the village of Chion, I happily met the beloved daughter of Ōriyōhime. Guided by the Shinjin of Ananai-kyō, I worshipped at the sacred Reichi where Tamayorihime hides near Lake Suwa. Oh, the joy of serving in the Kamuwaza of the Hotsushiyu of Mani! Serving morning and evening in the Miyai of the Ōkami, who dwells in tranquility, having established the Ōmiyabashira at the foot of Mount Yotsuo.

By the Mikoto of Kotoyoriwake, I returned to the land of Fusa. While I was polishing my Shinkon at the Reijiyō of Mount Ubusuna, I appeared in the capital of Haruna in the land of the Moon, where I began to rectify the Magawaza of Ōkuronushi. Bathing in the blessings of the gods of Heaven and Earth, I desire to save and assist the people of over seven thousand nations, beginning with the ministers of ugliness. Fearing the Mikoto of the gods, I advance with joy. Just as I was traversing the mountain path of Kajika Pass, the evil spirits serving under the Brahmanism that my husband protects, with the hearts of tigers and wolves, persecuted us, mother and daughter.

As they unleashed their violence, knowing it was a violation of the heavenly laws, I could not help but use my hands in a moment of impulse. I grabbed the wretches who came forth and, without any mercy, threw them toward the valley of the Arakawa. The remaining evil spirits quickly fled, turning clouds into mist. Nevertheless, even they are the children of the gods, born from the sacred water and fire of the gods of Heaven and Earth. I pray that you repent, quickly purify your Shinkon, and while saving your spirits before the noble presence of the gods in the beautiful Tosotsuten of Takamagahara, reform your ugly deeds as quickly as possible.

Together with Kiyoteruhime, while wiping the dust of the heart on the mountain path of Kajika Pass, I serve and protect Ananai-kyō. Before the presence of the Sumē-Ōkami, I pray with awe and reverence. Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haemashimase. May the spirits be blessed.

Kiyoteruhime sings while following her mother down the slope.

The gods appear on the surface and distinguish between the good and evil spirits. My father, Onikumawake, who served the god-minister Onikumahiko of the Brahmanism, revered the divine spirit of Ōkunohiko—known as the Brahma-deva, the great leader of the cloud-power body, infinite, absolute, without beginning or end—as the Jizaitenshin. Leaving the land of Tokoyo, he crossed over to the land of Egypt. Dwelling in the land of Ken-on, he opened his teachings, and his influence flourished like the branches of a mulberry tree day by day. His power was unmatched as a god-minister, and he followed many attendant gods, finding peace of mind.

Appointed as vice-leader, he took my mother as his wife. How valiant they were, serving together for the sake of the Way! Born between the two, I, Koito-hime, was raised in luxury from childhood. Knowing nothing of the rough waves of the world, without discernment, I had my heart stolen by Tomohiko. Abandoning my parents, to whom I owed the debt of sea and mountain, I lost myself in the darkness of my heart. Led by the hand of the man I loved, I traveled along the river of Eden, crossed the Unabara of Fusa, and hid myself in the village of Matsuura on the island of Shiro, which drifts upon the waves. I stayed there for a year, but disgusted by the conduct of the minister Tomohiko, I fled in the night.

Leaving the mansion behind, together with the boatmen Chankī (Chōkichi) and Monkī (Mokichi), we pushed through a thousand waves and advanced far to the island of Ryūgū. I met the ministers of the gods who protect Ananai-kyō, such as Isago-hime and Umeko-hime. Saved in both Shinkon and body, and with the divine virtue of the gods, I headed toward the land of Chion. Protected by the shadow of the power of Takayamahiko and Kurohime, I was for a time a queen who shone with the influence of the sun and moon in all directions. However, the protection of the gods was not shallow; I met my beloved mother and served in the Kamuwaza of the Hotsushiyu of Mani, finally standing for the first time on the island of Jitentō.

I served with a sincere heart for a while in the Nishiki Palace, but by the order of the teacher Kotoyoriwake, my mother and I were sent to the mansion of Saien. Bathing in the dew of blessings morning and evening, I was granted the name Kiyoteruhime. As we, mother and daughter, served purely in the propagation of Ananai-kyō, we received the command of the god-minister Hinodewake to advance to the capital of the Moon, where my beloved father is said to be. How happy I am to have received that command! This body is entrusted to the gods; no matter what troubles may come, how could I fear the Way of the gods? With the Kototama of the noble majesty, I will appease all the Magatsuhi that obstruct our path.

To rebuild the land of the gods, this departure is to illuminate the capital of Haruna, where the moon shines over the land of the Moon. Ah, how valiant, how valiant! No matter how steep the mountain paths may be, as my heart's horse gallops, a journey of a thousand miles is not far. How joyful it is to advance! Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haemashimase. May the spirits be blessed, and may you protect the Kamuwaza of us, mother and child. With a heart as clear as the liver of a village, Kiyoteruhime prays with reverence. Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haemashimase. May the spirits be blessed.

Singing thus, with her face blown by the autumn wind, her straw raincoat fluttering, and leaning on her staff, she descends the Kajika Pass.
