Chapter 14: Pure Spring Mountain [1079]

清春山〔1079〕

§ 1

Deep in the mountains of Hanhageyama, which stands steeply on the border between Fusa and the Moon, a great cavern has been established as the headquarters for the Brahman religion; meanwhile, they blindly force the local people to offer up their produce through violence, and their arrogance grows daily while the voices of resentment fill the earth. The missionaries of the Brahman religion are limited to the subordinates of Kiyoharuyama, and they have been forcing faith through desperate, life-threatening means, carrying the Quran in their left hands and swords in their right.

Just as Terukuniwake, together with Terukō and Umekō, had crossed the Kajikatauge pass without difficulty and reached the foot of Mount Kiyoharuyama, they all stopped and tilted their heads for a moment at the sudden sound of a woman's scream echoing from the bottom of the valley. The screaming grew increasingly intense. Thinking this could not be a trivial matter, the party of Terukuniwake approached the bottom of the valley in search of the source of the cries, and upon looking, they saw four or five large men binding a woman with her hands behind her back and beating her. Thinking that missionaries who save people could not possibly leave this be, they rushed close to the scene while chanting a missionary song. Upon seeing the three figures, the five rough men panicked, left the woman there, and fled in every direction, scattering like dust.

Terukuniwake approached the side of the woman and said, 'We are missionaries of the Sangokyō. As we were passing along this road, we suddenly heard a woman's scream. We thought that some tragedy must be unfolding, and regardless of what it was, we felt we must save her, so we searched until we reached this place. Now that we have appeared, you are safe, so please set your mind at ease.'

When he comforted her kindly in this way, the woman blinked her eyes and replied, 'Yes, thank you very much. You have saved me from a situation of extreme peril. I am sure this is entirely due to the help of the Gods. To tell you the truth, I am a believer in the Sangokyō, a pilgrim searching for the whereabouts of my brother. As a woman traveling alone, when I arrived here, I was surrounded by the gang from the Brahman religion, bound hand and foot, and because I was so angry at their unreasonable demands, I cursed them with all my might. The five men became very angry and, wanting to kill me, they poked and prodded me all over my body with the sheaths of their swords from every direction. Unable to bear the pain, I cowardly let out a scream. Thank you so much for saving me.'

Terukuniwake: 'That is a dangerous matter. Now, please calm yourself. Hey, Terukō, Umekō, untie this lady.'

'Yes,' they answered, and the two men began to quickly untie the bonds.

Terukō: 'What a cruel way to tie someone up. They have used wisteria vines to bind her so tightly that they are cutting into her flesh.'

Saying this, he drew his short sword with a flourish, cut the vines, and finally set the woman free from her bonds.

The woman: 'Thanks to you, I feel relieved. You are missionaries of the Sangokyō. I became a believer in the Sangokyō after visiting Mount Caucasus for certain reasons. My brother is named Umehiko, and he went to the island of Ryūgū to preach the teachings of the Banko Shin-ō, but his whereabouts have been unknown ever since. My parents grieved over this and have already passed away, and I, who remain, am all alone and could not stay at home. When I heard rumors that there was a person named Umehiko among the missionaries of the Sangokyō, I disguised myself as a believer and went to Mount Caucasus to investigate the situation. In the process, the doctrines of the Sangokyō gradually became precious to me, and I finally became a true believer. I heard that my brother Umehiko had gone to a place called Jitentōtō as a missionary, and that he had become a disciple of the God Hinodewake. I was on my way to meet Hinodewake-sama at the mansion of Saien to ask for my brother's whereabouts, having come all the way from Armenia, when I met these villains and suffered this misfortune. Near here is a high mountain called Kiyoharuyama, and in its depths, a great general of the evil gods named Ōashiwake has built his lair. I was captured by his subordinates and suffered this misfortune because I was angry at their unreasonable demand that I become the wife of Ōashiwake. I am a pitiful woman searching for my only brother. I have heard that you are also missionaries of the Sangokyō; do you happen to know the whereabouts of Umehiko?'

§ 2

Terukō: 'Hey, this man called Umehiko and this man called Umekō, there is a pair of them right here.'

Woman: 'Oh, is that truly so?'

Terukō: 'The one called Umekō is this man, and the Sendenshi of Terukuniwake has been calling him Umehiko-sama until now. Now then, Sendenshi-sama, I remember you saying some time ago that you had a younger sister. Could it be that this person is she? With those crescent-moon eyebrows and the way her eyes are rounded, they look very much alike.'

Umekō: 'Truly, truly, they resemble each other, they are like two peas in a pod. No matter what one says, she must be the younger sister of Terukuniwake-sama.'

Terukuniwake gazes intently at the woman's face in silence. The woman also stares at the face of the Sendenshi, tilting her head as if to pierce through him. After a while, as if she had made up her mind, the woman said:

Woman: 'Are you not my older brother? I am so happy to see you.'

Terukuniwake: 'What was your childhood name?'

Woman: 'Yes, my childhood name was Ayame.'

Terukuniwake: 'Then you are indeed my irreplaceable younger sister; I am so glad you are safe. However, our parents, to whom we owe such deep gratitude, must have moved to another land because they were worried about this Umehiko. Ah, how regrettable. How can I ever apologize to my parents? I have been so devoted to the path of the Kamisama that I have forgotten about my parents and my sister until now. Sister, please forgive me.'

Ayame: 'You are too kind, Aniuhe-sama. There is no question of forgiveness or lack thereof; now that things have come to this, I withdraw what I said earlier. The truth is that our parents are being held captive in a rock cave on Mount Kiyoharu. In short, the Ashiwake of the Brahman religion sent messengers time and again to demand that I be given to him as his wife, but because our doctrines differ, my parents refused. When I said that I could not give an answer until I had met my brother, they took advantage of my absence while I was away at the mountain, kidnapped my parents, and returned to the rock cave on Mount Kiyoharu. They made a cruel and inhuman demand, saying that if I became his wife, he would spare my parents' lives, and if not, he would kill them. Since my parents had already become believers of the San-Go-Kyo, they stood firm, saying they would not surrender to the Brahman religion no matter how much torture they endured, so they must be suffering at the hands of the evil spirits by now. I was distraught, and thinking that I must somehow find where my brother was and that we should join forces to rescue our parents, I was on my way to the mansion of Saien.'

With that, she bursts into tears and collapses, her sorrow profound. Terukuniwake sighs and is lost in tears.

Terukō: 'Hey, Ayame-sama, everything has become clear from your story just now. Come, let us accompany the Sendenshi-sama and head out to conquer Mount Kiyoharu.'

Terukuniwake: 'Even though I have received an important mission from the Great God Susanoo-Ōkami, and even though it is to save my parents from danger, I cannot leave my mission unfulfilled to attend to such personal matters. Oh, what a dilemma. If I try to save my parents, the mission of the Great God will be delayed; if I follow the command of the God, there is no telling what will become of my parents. Oh, what a difficult situation this has become.'

Umekō: 'Now then, Sendenshi-sama, no matter how divine your mission may be, if there are people suffering at the hands of evildoers along the way, you cannot simply overlook them and pass by. That would surely be contrary to your duty as a Sendenshi who saves the world. It is the same as hearing a cry from the bottom of a valley and stopping by here; such hesitation is surely unnecessary. Come, let us quickly go to conquer Mount Kiyoharu.'

Terukuniwake: 'What you say is also reasonable. In that case, I am sorry, but I shall decide to save my parents from their danger.'

Ayame: 'Aniuhe-sama, thank you. Then I shall lead the way. If we go about three ri further from here, that is the enemy's rock cave, or rather, the place where our parents are being held captive. My heart is impatient even as we speak. Come, please go quickly.'
