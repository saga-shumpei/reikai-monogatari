Chapter 19: Jewel Mountain Storm [1084]

玉山嵐〔1084〕

§ 1

The Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, Beginning with the divine official Ōgonhime who colors the autumn fields, And the noble Mikoto Kiyoteruhime, Whose radiance shines in the four directions like the sun and moon, Only these two together, Mounted on steeds through mountains that pierce the clouds, At last crawled their way to the summit, And escorted by two Mago, While descending the Munatsukizaka, Continuing through night and day to the land of Fusa, They arrived at the Lion River.

Rēbu: 'Moshi Okusama, this is the famous Lion River of Fusa. There are no bridges; we must cross by horse no matter what. Since I am a Betsutō, I shall swim across to the other side. Please ride this horse and cross to the opposite bank. You may have the Kokoroe for Suiba, but you must loosen the horse's Haraobi, grip the Tazuna firmly with one hand, release the other hand, and as both horse and person float in the water, you must grasp the rope and the mane together to cross. Otherwise, there is a danger of Dekibotsu in the middle of the river; please cross with that in mind.'

Ōgonhime: 'I also remember practicing Suiba in the River of Eden. I shall be Daijiyōbu, but since Kiyoteruhime has no Keiken in Suiba yet, I am concerned. I wonder if there is some good Kufū.'

Kiyoteruhime: 'Please, never, ever worry. I too have the Keiken of galloping horses into the Eris River at Chion Castle and playing at Suiba.'

Ōgonhime: 'Ah, then I am relieved. Come, let us begin the preparations.'

Rēbu: 'Please stay as you are; I will loosen the Haraobi.'

Thus the preparations for the river crossing were completed, and Rēbu lashed the rumps of both horses with a whip with all his might. The spirited horses leaped up and plunged with a splash into the formidable torrent, showing only their necks, and without difficulty crossed the great river, about one ri in width, to the other side. The two Mago followed behind the horses and finally reached the opposite bank, where each wrung out their clothes and let the horses rest.

At that moment, arriving with dozens of subordinates, were Kugihiko and Kumehiko, whom Ōkuninushi relies upon as his right and left hands. Brandishing their batons of command, they came galloping at full speed toward the banks of the Lion River astride wild horses. Riding that momentum, the troop of dozens of cavalry plunged in—zaba-zaba-zaba—and without even glancing at the figures of Ōgonhime's party, they finished crossing to the opposite shore. Rēbu watched this and felt a sense of relief, saying:

Rēbu: 'Ah, it almost became a terrible situation, but thanks to the Kami, the unit of Kugihiko searching for you has crossed to the other side. However, there is no doubt that they are the vanguard. We must not let our guard down yet. They must have come leading a great army to recover from the failure at the Forest of Floating Wood; so to speak, those who just crossed are playing a role like scouts and the vanguard. From here, let us abandon the horses, disguise ourselves as beggars to pass through the difficult barriers safely, and proceed to the port of Taru.'

Kiyoteruhime: 'Rēbu, there is no need to be so worried. We have the protection of the precious Kami. With the profound protection of Kunitokotachi-no-Mikoto, who possesses the infinite, absolute, beginningless, and endless divine power, there is no need to fear any enemy whatsoever. To be seized by the wind of cowardice and be startled by the flapping of waterfowl is a folly that would make one's weight as a Sendenshi zero. Now, Mother-sama, rather than going in such filthy beggar's guise, shall we not proceed with dignity, singing the Sendenshi songs of Ananai-kyō and making the echoes resound in the surroundings?'

Ōgonhime: 'Just as you say, if one's heart is weak, one cannot cross the wilderness where wild beasts roar and rave. Rēbu, you may return now; or rather, it would be better for you to take independent action. If by any chance we encounter tens of thousands of enemies on the way, you would be a hindrance and it would be disadvantageous for our side...'

Rēbu: 'Moshi Okusama, is that not too cruel? After letting me accompany you this far, have you not forsaken me? No matter what you say, I shall accompany you as long as my legs can move.'

§ 2

Ōgonhime: 'In all warfare, it is of great advantage to face the enemy with a vast number of allies, or otherwise to charge in alone. To wield the courage of a lion in full fury, one must be alone; whoever you strike down indiscriminately is an enemy. If you are burdened by the worry of accidentally striking your own allies, you can never achieve full activity. Understand this logic and please, part ways with me. We shall meet again in the capital of Haruna...'

Reibu: 'You said that it is better to be alone, but what will become of Kiyoteruhime-sama?'

Ōgonhime: 'Kiyoteruhime is a woman, so she is easily noticed even in the midst of battle; there is little worry of her engaging in fratricide. But you two are men, so if you were to mistakenly strike one another, it would be a disaster. In a fight, being alone is the most advantageous. If it were said that Onikumawake's wife Mukadehime and their daughter Koitohime had their groom provide reinforcements against the attackers of the Brahmin faith, it would be a stain on their martial honor for generations to come. I beg of you, please part ways with me.'

Reibu: 'If that is the case, there is no help for it; let us part ways. However, I pray that the bond between master and servant shall not be severed.'

Ōgonhime: 'Since this parting is based on mutual understanding, please be at ease, for I shall never abandon you...'

Reibu: 'Yes, thank you. If that is so, I shall take my leave here. Please take great care along the way. Princess, if that is so, I pray you will look after your mother's well-being.'

Kiyoteruhime: 'Ha, very well. Do not worry; as long as I am with her, it is safe. No, no, the Lord God is with us, so it is as firm as a great rock. Now, Mother, let us go.'

She stood in the lead and walked on. Reibu watched them from behind until their figures disappeared. The one groom did not say a word, and perhaps moved by something, he quickly hid his figure in the nearby forest. Reibu folded his arms and spoke to himself.

'Ah, I have become alone once again. I am a man truly without luck in companions. However, I cannot help but be concerned about the Lady and the Princess. Oh, what should I do?'

He folded his arms, sat cross-legged on the ground, and with his head bowed, shook it from side to side.

While he was doing this, two or three men from the cavalry unit that had crossed to the other side earlier struck their horses with whips, plunged into the river again, and came crossing toward this side. Reibu thought...

'How strange. I thought they had crossed safely, but that fellow Kugihiko must have spotted the figures of the Lady and the young lady and turned back to capture them. There is no use in running; I am on foot and they are on horseback, so I will surely be caught. Rather than that, it is my duty to stand my ground here, hinder their advance, and buy time so that the two of them may get even a step further away from the enemy. So this is why the Lady told me to part ways with her; how truly noble she is. They are indeed different from ordinary people in some way...'

He muttered to himself while feeling a sense of admiration. Then, three mounted warriors came advancing, their hoofbeats echoing with a sharp clatter. Seeing Reibu, they said:

'Hey, who were the women you were just talking to here? State it in detail.'

Reibu, thinking to say something to buy time, feigned ignorance on purpose:

'Yes, they seemed to be human beings.'

Warrior: 'Fool, that is not what I am asking. I am telling you to let us know what the women were called.'

Reibu pondered for a while, then finally raised his face, exposing a blank, foolish expression:

'Haaai, a woman called Old Hag and a woman called Daughter, the two of them passed by.'

Rēbu: 'Yes, my Seimei? My Seimei is Seimei. Inochi is the Monodane of all things; you too should take an Itsupuku here. In the long Tsukihi, Inochi is short; Seimei is Kanjin. Are you not a Kamuzukasa of the Brahmanist Kyō who serves the path of the noble Kami of Seimeimuku?'

Bushi: 'Ugh, how irritating! You are a Tenkaitsupin Bakamono.'

Rēbu: 'Yes, when a deer is atop a horse, that is called a Kabamono. A Bakamono is a fellow where the horse is atop the deer.'

Bushi: 'Ugh, if we dally like this, it will be an Ichidaiji if we are late!'

As he whipped his steed to gallop off, Rēbu firmly grasped the bridle of the horse standing at the Sentō.

Rēbu: 'Chiyotsuto, please wait. On such a narrow path, it is dangerous to be in such a hurry. Why not leave your horse here and go up on foot? Rēbu: 'Hahahaha! Even if you say to go ahead, on such a winding path like a sheep's intestine, and a steep chest-striking slope at that, once the horse in front has stopped, there is no way to pass it. He muttered in a low voice.

Bushi: 'Ugh, what a helpless fellow. Everyone, shall we tie our horses here and give chase on foot?'

Rēbu: 'Ah, please do so; that is far safer and better. However, what is your intention in chasing such an old hag and a girl? Most likely you are chasing them thinking that old hag and girl are Mukade-hime and Koito-hime-sama. If so, you had better stop. Actually, I too am a retainer of Onikumawake-sama, and fortunately remembering the faces of the mother and daughter, I came this far to search for them, as my master said he would give any reward desired if I found his wife and daughter. However, thinking they were the very image of that mother and daughter, and believing the time for my promotion had come, I crossed the river with a dancing heart, but lo and behold, to my shock, they were but a filthy old hag crawling with lice and covered in chrysanthemum-stones with a beggar girl, a sight so base in character and frame it made me shudder. Never has there been such a useless thing. If that mother and daughter were truly Mukade-hime and Koito-hime-sama, it would be a great feat for me, so I would want to capture them together with your help; but I was so appalled that I gave the hag's head three or four blows, thrashed the beggar girl's backside, left them howling and sobbing, and now I am sinking here in the depths of disappointment and despair.'

Bushi: 'So you are a subordinate of Onikumawake-sama. As I thought, is Onikumawake-sama also searching for the whereabouts of his wife and child?'

Rēbu: 'That must be so. Without his only wife and daughter in heaven or earth, no matter how much he rises in the world, it would be a lonely life. It is only natural that he should yearn for his wife and child.'

Bushi: 'Indeed, it is so. If what you say is true, that mother and daughter cannot be the wife and child of Onikumawake-sama. Ugh, I have crossed the river for naught. Hey, I have troubled you. You two, let us turn back from here!'

He whipped his horse, and riding on the momentum, the three riders crossed the Lion River, heading straight ahead with their heads aligned.
