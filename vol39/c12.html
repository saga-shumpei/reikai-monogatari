Chapter 12: 種 Bright 志 [1077]

種明志〔1077〕

§ 1

Kunikō, Ham, and Tāru, the Sannin, breathed in the morning air at dawn, and through a mysterious occurrence, their spirits harmonized until they became like brothers, descending Kajikatauge while engaging in idle chatter along the way. Upon arriving at the Ihaya where Terukuniwake was said to be waiting, they saw no sign of Terukuniwake and his party, but only two men huddled in a small corner of the cave, trembling.

Ham jumped into the not-so-spacious cave without ceremony, and upon examining them closely, he found them to be the two men, Īru and Yosefu.

Ham: 'Hey, aren't you the ghosts Īru and Yosefu? How did you end up wandering into a place like this?'

Īru: 'Oh, is that you, Ham-sama? How fortunate that you survived safely. We two are also completely at a loss as to what is happening. Whether this is the Genkai or the Iukai, no matter how we think about it, it remains unclear. What do you think?'

Ham: 'Pull yourself together. This is the cave at the southern entrance of the Kajikasanroku.'

Yosefu: 'If that is so, then we have indeed returned to life. Last night, we fled here and hid ourselves, when three missionaries came by, chanting a propaganda song in voices that seemed to split our heads, and tried to enter this cave. Thinking it would be terrible if such fellows got in, we two braced the stone door from the inside and pushed with all our might, until they finally gave up and passed by. Since then, we two have been pushing against the stone door with all our strength, but as it seemed the missionaries had gone far away, and we were feeling quite suffocated, we were just letting in some fresh air.'

Ham: 'Hey, your saviors are here, two of them. Why don't you hurry up and offer your thanks? This is Tāru-sama and Kunikō-sama.'

Yosefu: 'What? The saviors who helped me were the four missionaries of the San-Go-Kyō. That fellow Tāru is a spineless man; he abandoned us when we were being tormented by the mother-and-daughter pilgrims and ran away, so he is a cowardly, unkind fellow, and it is useless to say such things. Hmph, thank you very much for nothing. Say, Īru, you know it too, don't you? It must be the missionary called Terukuniwake of the San-Go-Kyō. You surely have a clear memory of that, too.'

Īru: 'That is certainly true. The missionary who passed by here must indeed be Terukuniwake-sama, but his divine power was so strong that I became afraid instead, and thinking it better not to even approach him, I played a trick on the missionary who had helped me and hid here. Hey, Ham, how did you manage to survive?'

Ham: 'I was never dead to begin with. I was watching closely from the side as you two were buried waist-deep in the sand, rolling your eyes. However, for some reason, I could not stand on my own feet, so the three of us lined up our heads and waited for the time to come. Then, those two, Rēbu and Tāru, came along and, after spewing all sorts of insults about me, said they would give this Ham-sama a water burial in the valley stream, and because they were saying such wicked things, I said "You wretch!" and stood up. At that, Tāru here and that fellow Rēbu vanished like clouds and mist, and ended up sprawled out on the valley path. Divine punishment is a fearful thing. Just as I thought I would trample them to death, my waist went strange again, and while I was sprawled out on the valley path in the same fate, the missionary Terukuniwake happened to pass by, ordered this Kunikō-sama to care for me, and said he would go to this cave, but he walked straight down, and because you were interfering from the inside, he ended up leaving. One of them is this Kunikō-sama. Why don't you hurry up and offer your thanks?'

Īru and Yosefu, with an attitude that was both happy and fearful, bowed their waists excessively and bobbed their heads up and down five or six times.

I will say nothing more, Arigata, please, I beg you to Kannin.

Kunikō: 'You are a strange fellow. Why would the Tamago of the Sendenshi, whose Inochi I have spared, torment you? Come, you should be Anshin.'

Iru: 'In that case, will you forgive me for the Goburei I committed against the Jiyunrei of the Ananai-kyō?'

Kunikō: 'What sort of appearance did the Jiyunrei of the Ananai-kyō have? That is a bit of news. Tell me in detail.'

Iru: 'They were a Jiyunrei of two, an old woman and a daughter. They were quite strong, and in the end, they even threw the Taishiyō of Ham into the Tanisoko, so they are not to be trifled with by hand or foot.'

Kunikō pursed his lips on purpose,

Kunikō: 'That is outrageous. My mother and Niyōbau must have passed through Koko earlier in the guise of Jiyunrei. If that is the case, you are the one who committed Burei against my mother and Niyōbau. Having heard that, I can no longer show any Reuken.'

Iru: 'Moshi, Kuni-sama, it was not just me. I resisted under the Meirei of Ham, who is here. There was Yosefu, Taru, and a fellow called Rēbu outside; I, as an exception, and the others, totaling five, took Hantaiteki Kaudō, so please punish Ham. I will confess everything, so as a reward, please spare at least my Inochi. In return, if you order me to blow my nose, I will wipe it for you. If you order me to wipe your bottom, I will wipe it. Aahn, aahn, aahn!'

Hamu: 'Ahahaha, the mastermind of the evil is this Ham-sama. Here, Kuni-sama, please execute me first. To take the sins of one's subordinates upon oneself is the path that one who leads the masses must truly follow. Come, quickly, as you wish...'

He thrust his neck forward, as if to say, 'Take my head quickly.'

Kunikō: 'Very well, if you want me to take your head, I shall take it. However, at this final moment, confess your true origins one by one. Depending on the matter and the circumstances, I might not refrain from sparing you.'

Hamu, without any sign of being cowed and with a completely composed attitude, tells his story.

The moon country, where the light shines brightly across the heavenly sky,

Born in the southern edge of the Dekatan plateau,

As the child of the King of Tēmusu in Garanda, surrounded by blue mountains on all sides,

There were times when I succeeded to my parent's name and ruled the people in peace,

But then, the priests of the Brahman religion, the subordinates of Ōkuninushi,

Kugihiko and Katahiko, the two of them,

Slipped into the country before anyone knew,

And made all the people of the land submit to the Brahman religion,

Forming a faction, they approached the royal castle under the cover of night,

The ferocity of their force was such that my wife, children, and retainers,

Fled like clouds and mist, leaving not even a shadow or shape, and I wept myself to sleep.

Left behind, Ham alone,

With no means to resist,

Surrendered, clinging to my Inochi,

And was dragged before Ōkuninushi, and having no choice,

I abandoned the royal rank passed down through generations of ancestors,

And was driven out to the land of Fusa,

Wandering here and there, trudging along,

Searching for the whereabouts of my wife and children,

I unexpectedly encountered the deputy leader of the Brahman religion, the priest Onikumawake,

Before the capital of Taru,

And was strictly questioned about my origins,

And told the story of being tormented by the subordinates of Ōkuninushi,

Upon hearing which, the priest Onikumawake teared up,

Are you wandering in search of your wife and children?

You are a pitiful fellow.

I too am one who lives searching for the whereabouts of my wife and children.

I sympathize with your heart.

No matter what the Taishiyō, Ōkuninushi, may say,

I, Onikumawake, will intercede gently and save you.

The joy I felt when he said that!

Rejoicing and emboldened, this Ham,

Followed Onikumawake,

And headed toward the capital of Haruna,

Where I was promoted and became his subordinate,

And while searching for my wife and children, Mukade-hime and Koito-hime,

Wandering far and near, morning and evening,

I came to this place, and there,

I encountered a Jiyunrei of a mother and daughter who looked very much like Mukade-hime.

Wishing to hear the truth, I stood before the mother and daughter pilgrims with a loud voice. As I drew near and looked, Mukadehime, who struck my heart with a thud, was surely none other than Koitohime. Fearing that the secret might leak, I chased away the four ruffians. Hamu, who remained behind, had intended to convey the orders of Onikumawake to the mother and daughter, but that too became like bubbles on water. How regrettable it is to have been seized and thrown to the bottom of the valley, and to be parted from the mother and daughter whom I had gone to such lengths to meet. When Kami appears in the world, He shall distinguish the good and evil of the heart.

The Ōkami who created this world, the Kunikō who governs the land with the blessing of the Mitama, must surely understand the sincere heart of Hamu in detail. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, may the Mitama be blessed.

He answered with the song in a leisurely manner.

Kunikō: 'If that is so, Hamu-sama, you are searching for the whereabouts of Mukadehime and Koitohime-sama, and also looking for the whereabouts of your wife and child, under the orders of Onikumawake-sama, are you not? That is admirable. However, Mukadehime and Koitohime are by no means believers or evangelists of the Sangokyō, but it is certain that both mother and daughter are safe and sound. And the mother and daughter pilgrims who threw you earlier are splendid evangelists named Ōgonhime and Seishōhime, and they are by no means the persons you say they are.'

He spoke with a deliberate ambiguity before the three, as if implying something.

Hamu, quickly perceiving the inner thoughts of Kunikō, feigned ignorance on purpose.

Hamu: 'Ah, was that so? If I hear it that way, there are many points that do not match the physical description. ...Hey, Taru and the other two, what do you think?'

Taru: 'I am aware of the old woman and the girl, but because of the sheer terror, do you think I had the composure to examine their features?'

Hamu: 'Iru and Yosefu, what do you two think?'

Yosefu: 'If I may speak, I do not believe that such splendid persons could possibly be the wife and child of Onikumawake-sama. The Mitama of that person is already a precious vessel of flesh of a Kami who serves by the side of the Kami of Gesshōhiko in the Tusita Heaven...'

Iru: 'I think so too. No matter how much one says Mukadehime-sama and Koitohime-sama are heroes, there is no way they could possess such power. And if they were persons with such power, the martial prowess of the mother and daughter would surely be echoing throughout the world.'

Kunikō: 'That must be so. It is a great mistake to think of that mother and daughter as Mukadehime and Koitohime. Once you understand that, it is a waste of effort to pursue that mother and daughter any further. Rather than that, why don't you reveal your true identity like Hamu-sama?'

Iru: 'In that case, I shall lay everything out for you to see. I was born into a Shudra family in a small country called Sawara on the Dekatan Plateau. An evangelist named Tēgusu, a subordinate of Daikokunushi, came and began converting the people of the country to Brahmanism one after another. I could not bear the resentment and fought back, brandishing the doctrines of the Ural religion, but in the end, being outnumbered, I was thrown into the prison of Sawara. After suffering one hundred days and nights of torture, I finally overturned my original intentions and surrendered to Brahmanism from the emptiness of my heart to be saved.'

Hamu: 'Hey, hey, it is not from the emptiness of your heart, is it not from the bottom of your heart?'

Iru: 'That is right, it is from the bottom.'

Hamu: 'You are probably telling lies.'

Iru: 'Well, there is a bottom and a lid to everything. No matter what one says, the current situation is that one must follow the long and obey the strong, so my body is Brahmanism.'

Hamu: 'Your body is Brahmanism, and your spirit is the Ural religion. What a convenient evangelist you are.'

Iru: 'Hamu-sama, aren't you also just muddling through?'

Hamu: 'As a human being, how can one fathom the depths of another's heart? No power of law, military force, or oppression can ever achieve ideological coercion. Since it is a matter of the unseen world, it is all the more beyond the reach of the blind of today. You should not say such boorish things.'

§ 5

Kunikō heard the words of Tāru and sat with his hands clasped, lost in thought, but then he stood up abruptly, gazed at the nape of Tāru's neck, and cried out, 'Ah!', without meaning to. Tāru felt suspicious at this cry.

Tāru: 'Excuse me, Kunikō-sama, is there something possessing me?'

Kunikō: 'Were you not called Harukō when you were young?'

Tāru: 'Yes, my name is Harukō. And my older brother was called Kunikō, the same name as you. I do not know at all whether he is alive or dead now. Because it was such a great disturbance, parents and children were scattered in all directions...'

Kunikō: 'In any case, from now on, let us be like brothers and get along well. Hey, everyone, from now on you must remove all barriers and work together with me for the sake of Ananai-kyō. I will certainly meet with Terukuniwake-sama and arrange things well for you.'

Ichidō: 'Yes, thank you. In that case, Kunikō-sama, we leave it to you.'

Kunikō: 'Come, let us go quickly; Terukuniwake-sama must be waiting impatiently on the way.'

Saying this, he left the rocky cave behind, accompanied the four, and while singing a Sendenka, walked quickly down the rugged mountain path.
