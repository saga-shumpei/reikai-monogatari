Chapter 7: Capital 率天 [1072]

都率天〔1072〕

§ 1

Murasakiiro short-stemmed Shibakusa grows thick over the entire Daichi, and in the vast Daigenya where there are neither rocks nor tall trees, small flowers of red, white, yellow, and other colors are blooming like stars. Although the sky is filled with drifting Konpeki clouds, and neither the shadow of the Taiyō nor the form of the Taiin can be seen, two men walk through the Nohara where dew drops glisten, feeling an indescribable sense of Saukuwai Kibun. Feeling Fushin that the Atari Kuōkei is somehow different from the Genkai, they stop in their tracks, Bauzen.

Iiru: 'Oi, Yosefu, I wonder when we two came to such a place. I thought we met a pair of mother and daughter pilgrims on the Hosotanimichi of Kajikatauge and were thrown into the bottom of the valley, but after that it is all Yumeutsutsu; I cannot comprehend how or by what Tetsuzu we came to such a place. Do you have any memory of it?'

Yosefu: 'What remains in my memory is nothing but dreams. The thought that we met the mother and daughter pilgrims at Kajikatauge is the true dream; this is the true reality. Since the Genkai is called the floating world of dreams, everything that happened in the Genkai is a dream. We have finally returned to the reality that is the hometown of our souls. Is it not delightful to come out to such a splendid place, receiving the lingering breeze of Gokuraku, and traveling at will without having to be reserved before anyone? Unlike the Genkai, where one is restricted by petty boundaries such as whose territory this is or whose land that is, wandering in such a flower garden without any constraints is something that people of the Genkai cannot even know in their dreams. Ah, how grateful I am; even if it is a dream, I would not want this dream to leave for a thousand or ten thousand years.'

Iiru: 'Oi, look at that, look at the sky over there. Hasn't a strange cloud appeared?'

Iiru: 'In the span of a minute, just as I thought a spot like a mere ball appeared in the northwest sky, it expanded just like that, and the five-colored clouds became vivid, and the five-colored radiance began to shine even upon our faces, did it not?'

The five-colored clouds spread across the entire sky in the blink of an eye, and two goddesses wearing beautiful garments, one old and one young, adorned in five-colored robes, appeared to be descending toward them riding on the clouds. The Nohara of Murasakiiro where the two were standing felt as if it had risen to heaven, and in a state where one could not distinguish whether the clouds had descended or the ground had risen, the two goddesses appeared before the two men before they knew it. When they looked closely, they were the mother and daughter they had met at Kajikatauge and who had thrown them into the bottom of the valley. Iiru and Yosefu were startled by the unexpected encounter and lowered their heads.

Iiru: 'This is, this is Koganehime-sama, I have been truly rude. Please, I beg your forgiveness.'

Yosefu: 'You are Kiyoteruhime-sama; I did not know you were such a noble god and was rude to you. Please, forgive me.'

Koganehime: 'I am troubled by such an apology. It is I who, despite being a woman, threw rough men into the valley river; oh, I have only been rude.'

Kiyoteruhime: 'I, too, being a young woman, committed such a violent act as throwing rough men into the valley, but please, forgive me.'

Iiru: 'Hai, thank you very much. However, what place is this? I cannot comprehend it at all.'

Koganehime: 'This is the dreamland of the future. The reason you have come here is that you performed great activities for the sake of the gods in the Shaba, and through the grace of the gods, you have been saved into such a heavenly Gokuraku-Jōdo. There is nothing more splendid than this for both of us; I congratulate you.'

Iiru: 'We two have not done a single decent thing in the Genkai, and we have not done anything useful for the sake of the gods. I cannot comprehend being saved in such a place; could it be that you have mistaken us for someone else?'

Ōgonhime: 'As of today, you have not yet performed a single deed of merit. If anything, your bad deeds are more numerous, and if you were to face the judgment of a Kōhei Kami, you are not of the Mibun to come to such a place; even we are of the same mind. However, since the Kami Mitō the Kuwako, Genzai, and Mirai, you are being allowed to glimpse the Sekai that you ought to come to after you leave the Genkai, having performed deeds of goodness while in the Genkai from this point forward.'

Yosefu: 'That I should be brought to such a place in order to perform goodness from here on out, I cannot Gatsuten. If it were after I had achieved merit in the world, Atsupa, I would understand, but that defiled spirits like us should come to such a place, I cannot Gatsuten no matter what; could this perhaps be a dream?'

Ōgonhime: 'Far from a dream, it is Genjitsu. Even so, if you were to do evil things from here on out, you would surely not be able to come to such a Ketsukō place; you would have to go to the Hantai place. Now, I shall guide you through the Sekai of Tosotsuten.'

Riyōnin: 'Yes, Arigata.'

They bow their heads. The Chijiyō upon which they had been standing began to Ukiaga toward nowhere in particular, floating softly. And the party of Yonin, while still standing, ascended toward the blue-clouded sky.

Looking, they saw suddenly before their eyes a beautiful palace of vermilion railings and green tiles, surrounded by crimson jade fences, with gold and silver sand spread across the ground, mixed here and there with diamond sand, shining like the Milky Way. The two looked at each other as if in a dream, struck dumb with astonishment.

Seishōhime: 'Now, Riyōnin-sama, this is the palace of Tsukiteruhiko-sama of Tosotsuten. From here on, you must not say a word. Follow behind the two of us. No matter what the Kami says, you must not offer a reply. The rank of Kami and humans is different, so you must only listen to the will of the Kami and not utter a single word in response. If you wish to speak, do so before passing through this gate. Once you pass through this gate, even if you meet anyone, you need only bow your head and offer your respects.'

Iiru: 'Yes, I understand. I cannot believe this is real, no matter what I think. Will I truly be saved in the future, as they say?'

Ōgonhime: 'If you only receive the words of the Kami and obey them as they are, you will be able to visit such a Ketsukō place in the future. You must not say anything at all.'

Iiru: 'Yes, I shall say no more from this point on. Oi, Yosefu, you had better ask what you wish to ask while there is still time. Once we enter this gate, we will no longer be able to use our organs of speech.'

Yosefu, in awe, bowed his head quietly and did not utter a single word. Ōgonhime and Seishōhime, remaining silent, bowed to the gatekeepers and proceeded quietly deeper and deeper inside.

The sound of resonant music could be heard from nowhere, a fragrant scent permeated the four directions, white lotus flowers bloomed in profusion in the inner courtyard within the gate, and hedges of peonies, white plums, and roses vied in their splendor; small birds with beautiful feathers, such as one has never seen in the Genkai, sang with refreshing voices, chanting of the spring of heaven. Beautiful goddesses, holding golden jade cups and wearing golden-colored garments, dressed in white robes with crimson hakama, came out to welcome the four, and with quiet steps, they dipped their fingers into the purple-colored water from the jade cups and moistened the lips of each one. Both its taste and its fragrance were beyond compare. The four goddesses led the four people as they proceeded deep into the interior.

Advancing deep into the inner palace and gazing toward the front, there was a Kami sitting in solemn dignity, revealing a skin of purple-polished gold upon a Sumeru altar inlaid with gold and silver. Within the gentleness, there was an indescribable dignity, and as one faced the countenance, one felt a sense of being dazzled, while at the same time feeling an indescribable nostalgia. This Kami was Tsukiteruhiko-no-Mikoto. He looked at the four with a look of interest, extended his golden hand, and beckoned them to come to his knees. Many children waiting on the left and right held various flowers in their hands and danced gracefully before the Sumeru altar in silence. The fragrant aroma and the beautiful, subtle music constantly struck the nose and ears, and the brilliant light within the palace served only to illuminate the eyes anew.

Ōgonhime looks back and beckons to the three. As the three follow silently behind Ōgonhime, a round hole suffused with purple color is found bored obliquely and low from the back of the Dendau, with purple stairs attached. Ōgonhime descends the stairs with a brisk step. As the three follow her and descend without limit, a swamp overgrown with Zatsusau and reeds lies there.

The Futari had fallen into this swamp before they knew it. Although it is not very deep, the water reaches up to their mouths when they stand Chiyokuritsu. When the wind blows a little and the waves rise high, it attacks their noses and they become Ikiguru. Ōgonhime and Kiyoteruhime, wondering Dō, look around, but their figures are nowhere to be seen; the Dendau that had been beautiful until now has vanished like smoke, and there is only the loneliness of the Akikaze blowing over the swamp where reeds grow thick.

To such a place, from nowhere, the Riyōnin, Rebu and Tāru, come running in a hurry, stand by the edge of the swamp, call the names of the Futari, and beckon, saying,

Rebu and Tāru: 'Come here quickly.'

The Riyōnin, Iru and Yosefu, struggle and try to swim toward the two, but for some reason, their feet are stuck to the Numasoko like Shitsukuhi, and they cannot Miugo; lashed by the wind, high waves Tokidoki attack the area of their Bimoku, and the bitterness is beyond measure. As the two are suffering and writhing without being able to raise their voices, from nowhere, Hamu appears before Rebu and Tāru, and the Sannin begin a Kōron about something here. The Riyōnin, Iru and Yosefu, are watching the dispute of the Sannin languidly in the swamp. Behind Hamu, a Matsupadaka red demon with its mouth and ears torn open is following.

After a while, the Riyōnin, Rebu and Tāru, run away toward the southeast, heading for the embankment of the swamp at full speed. Seeing the two suffering in the swamp, Hamu tries to help them and attempts to jump into the swamp becoming Matsupadaka, but the red demon standing behind grabs his neck with a grunt and will not let go, so Hamu is struggling with all his might.

The Riyōnin, Iru and Yosefu, are gasping for breath and try to shout, 'Please help us quickly...', but for some reason, not a single word comes out. The voice of a Sendan-ka is heard in the sky from nowhere. Upon hearing this voice, the figure of the demon that had been attached to Hamu vanishes like smoke. Hamu chases after the path where Rebu and Tāru fled, and returns while making the ground shake.

As the two hear the voice of this Sendan-ka, their bodies float up lightly, and before they knew it, they were on the bank of the swamp. And their wet clothes have dried before they knew it. Thinking, 'What a strange thing this is...', as the two look at each other's faces and start running, chasing after the path Hamu ran, a large pine tree stands with well-shaped branches, hanging its branches over the swamp. When they look up at that pine tree, an unspeakably terrifying giant snake is stretching its neck about three ken long, looking down under the tree, opening its large mouth, and trying to swallow someone. The two open their mouths for the first time,

Iru: 'Oi, Yosefu, isn't this a disaster?'

Yosefu: 'Indeed, as Iru says, isn't there a strange fellow in this pine tree? Most likely, the Sannin from before have been swallowed by this giant snake. Look, we cannot afford to be dawdling.'

While saying this, when they look closely at the root of the pine, a head is growing out of the ground. A giant snake above and a severed head below; thinking, 'How disgusting,' they try to run away, but for some reason, their bodies have become stiff and will not move a bit.

The two compose themselves, and when they look closely at the head grown from the ground, to their surprise, it is the great leader of Brahmanism, Daikokunushi. The two are startled, change their complexions, and in a hurry,

Iru: 'Ah, are you not Daikokunushi-no-kami-sama? Why on earth are you sticking only your head out in a place like this? Please look there, a giant snake is coiled on the branch of this pine tree, and is it not about to swallow you in one bite? Come, let us flee from here quickly with me.'

二人は此宣伝歌の声を聞くと共に身体軽く浮き上り、いつの間にやら沼の畔についてゐた。そして濡れた着物は何時の間にか乾いてゐる。ハテ不思議なことがあるものだなア………と両人は顔を見合せつつ、ハムの走つた後を追うて駆出すと、一本の大きな松の木が枝振よく立つてゐて、沼の上に枝を垂れてゐる。其松の木を見上ぐれば、えもいはれぬ恐ろしき大蛇が三間ばかりの首を伸ばして樹下を眺め、大口を開いて何者か呑まむとしてゐる。二人は初めて口を開き、

イール『オイ、ヨセフ、大変ぢやないか』

ヨセフ『如何にもイールの云ふ通り、此松の木には妙な奴が居るではないか。大方最前の三人は此大蛇に呑まれて了うたのだろ。コリヤ、グヅグヅしてはゐられまいぞ』

と言ひ乍ら、松の根元をよくよく見れば、土の中から首が生えてゐる。上には大蛇下には生首、ハテ厭らしやと、逃げ出さうとすれ共、如何したものか、身体強直してビクともならぬやうになつてゐる。

二人は因果腰を定め、地中から生えた首をよく見れば、豈計らむや、バラモン教の大棟梁大黒主である。二人はビツクリして顔色をかへ乍らあわただしく、

イール『アヽ、あなたは大黒主の神さまぢや御座いませぬか。如何してマアこんな所へ首ばかり出してゐられます。あれ御覧なさいませ、此松の枝には大蛇が蟠つて、今や一口に呑まむとしてゐるぢや御座いませぬか、サア早くここを私と一緒に逃げませう』

§ 4

Ōkuronushi: 'You two, Iiru and Yosefu, should not have come to such a place. It would be best for you to turn back while there is still time.'

Yosefu: 'You say to turn back, but I do not understand where we ought to go. And look, blood is oozing from your neck; what on earth has happened to you?'

Ōkuronushi: 'I have received the punishment of the Ōkami of Tenchi. Here beneath this pine tree, my Teashi have been bound, and I have been buried in the Dochiu by the demon Haika I myself created. As you see, only my head is exposed above the Chijiyō, where hawks and crows peck at my head and Dokumushi bite my neck. I am suffering such agony. You, too, had better repent quickly and return to the path of truth; once you end up like me, it is already Dame. I have yet to face Takusan more Kurau, and I do not know if my sins will be forgiven or not. Quickly, recite the Shinmon of Ananai-kyō and escape this Kifuba.'

Iiru: 'This is a strange thing to hear. You are the Daikyōshiyu of the Brahman religion, yet why do you tell us to recite the Shinmon of Ananai-kyō? I cannot Gatsuten this at all.'

Ōkuronushi: 'Though I may be in my prime of power in the Genkai, my Reikon in the Mirai is destined to suffer Mugen Semeku beneath this pine tree just as you see now. Ananai-kyō is a teaching that has emerged from God, while all other teachings are merely branch-gods or teachings created by humans, so they do not understand the depth of the divine will. No, it is because I have been practicing a teaching that violates the divine will that I, as the representative of the Brahman religion, am meeting with such Semeku. That said, the physical body of mine is under the influence of a sub-guardian god whose power is quite fierce, and it will not easily undergo Kaishin. If only it would undergo Kaishin, I would be spared such agony, but because the physical body of Ōkuronushi will not repent, I, the true master, am suffering this Semeku. The fate of Ōkuronushi a hundred years from now is exactly this current state. Now, leave this place quickly.'

At that moment, the propagation song of Ananai-kyō was heard faintly once more, and the missionary appeared at the edge of the swamp with three attendants. As soon as this voice was heard, the body of Ōkuronushi surged up from the ground with a thud. The great serpent in the tree opened its mouth wide, swallowed Ōkuronushi in one gulp, summoned black clouds, and vanished into the mid-heaven in an instant.

Upon seeing the figures of the two missionaries, they suddenly regained their senses and looked around, realizing they had fallen into the valley bottom of Kajika Pass and were buried waist-deep in the dust of the dancing sand. The flow of the valley echoed loudly all around. When they calmed their minds and looked closely, they realized that the missionary Terukuniwake and his three companions, Ume-kō, Teru-kō, and Kuni-kō, were tending to their bodies and performing the divine work of calling back their souls with all their might.

Iiru and Yosefu, the two of them, explained in detail to the missionary party how they had been rude to the party of Yoshikohime and thrown into this valley bottom, and how they had since been hired by Onikumawake to search for the whereabouts of Mukadehime and Koitohime. Having suddenly attained enlightenment here, they followed the missionary, descended the valley, emerged onto the mountain path, and trudged along behind them. The two of them felt inexplicably struck by the dignity of the missionary and became fearful, and when the surroundings were enveloped in darkness, they seized an opportunity and fled.

Terukuniwake decided to spend the night with his three attendants in front of an old shrine by the roadside.
