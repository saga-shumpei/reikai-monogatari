Chapter 13: 浮 Tree の Forest [1078]

浮木の森〔1078〕

§ 1

On the border of Tsuki and Fusa, by the divine Miizu of the Tenchi Kami, in the shade of the forest of Ukigahara in the great plains of Afghanistan, stand the mother and daughter Sendenshi. Having departed the mansion of Iso, they face the capital of Haruna; their Misugata is truly Ō. The leaves of the Akino are turning color, and Ōgonhime and Kiyoteruhime, in the Onkeshiki of the Kami's Mikoto, are truly as radiant as the Terutahe of a mild Koharu.

It is well known that the mother and daughter Sendenshi are none other than Ōgonhime and Kiyoteruhime. As Kiyoteruhime gazes at the Yomo Fūkei, she speaks:

Kiyoteruhime: 'Mother-sama, your feet must be quite tired. It has been ten days since we encountered the Warumono at Kajikatauge, and we have traveled the Yamasaka to this point without Bunan. This must surely be due to the profound protection of the Kami.'

Ōgonhime: 'Having lived long, I have tasted the hardships of the world, so this much travel is no burden to me, but you, being young, must have felt great pain. My heart is full of the desire to have you meet your father soon, but since our faith is fundamentally different, we are, in terms of ideology, enemies. The teachings of San-iku-kyo teach that under heaven there are no strangers and no demons, and that all are precious children of the Kami. However, the teachings of Brahman do not understand the truth so broadly, so I do not know what the result will be even if we do meet as parent and child. Thinking of this, I am neither happy nor sad, and my heart cannot find peace.'

Kiyoteruhime: 'Mother-sama, you need not worry about such things. It is our heavenly duty to use the Kototama of the infinite benevolence of San-iku-kyo to convert even the most demonic, serpentine, or crooked spirits. Much more so with a father who shares our blood; no matter how stubborn he may be, if we, mother and daughter, plead with sincerity, he will surely repent.'

Ōgonhime: 'Since you have the skill to have become the Queen of an island, I believe it will be alright, but this problem of ideology cannot be moved so easily. In any case, I pray to the Kami that we may arrive at my husband's mansion as soon as possible, speak of the truth of Tenchi through divine power, and withdraw—no, reform—the evil and lawless Brahmanism. This is a very grave mission for me. The heart of Lord Susanoo-Ōkami is truly broad and benevolent; I am so grateful that tears fall. That He would entrust this great task to us, the wife and daughter of Onikumawake, shows a magnanimity that no ordinary Kami could ever conceive. Because He trusts us so much, I cannot betray the heart of this Ōkami, no matter what fate may befall us, mother and daughter.'

Kiyoteruhime: 'That is so. Even if Father-sama should be angry and kill you and me, we can never submit to Brahmanism. That is your resolve as well, is it not, Mother-sama?'

Ōgonhime: 'That is a matter of course. I cannot disobey the words of the infinitely benevolent Ōkami. We must uphold sincerity to the very end.'

Just as they were speaking, a Brahman Sendenshi, riding a horse and leading dozens of subordinates, advanced toward the treasure forest of Ukigahara. He jumped off his horse, led his men, marched boldly before the two who were resting, glared with anger, and shouted in a loud voice:

Ōashiwake: 'I am the Sendenshi called Ōashiwake, a retainer of the Daikokunushi of Brahmanism. Based on the report of Rebu, I have led my men here to capture you mother and daughter. Come, surrender yourselves at once!'

He stood there shouting in a loud voice. In an instant, dozens of subordinates surrounded the mother and daughter in the shape of a full moon. Kiyoteruhime took off her hat, revealing her flower-like face, and...

Kiyoteruhime: 'I am a Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, Kiyoteruhime, whose heart is clear and shining. As he gave this order, they replied with a shout of 'Oh!' and from all four directions, they aimed at the mother and daughter, brandishing their jitte and attacking. The mother and daughter responded, 'Understood,' and defended themselves with their kongō-tsue, making the air resound like waterwheels. The attackers were all daunted, and while circling them from a distance, they could only shout, 'Look there, look there!' one after another. Ōdaruwake, losing his patience, shouted:

Ōdaruwake: 'Eeh, you useless little brats of allies, you fly-like wretches who are not even worth having as attendants, stay back!'

He drew his long sword with a flourish and slashed at the mother and daughter. The two caught the blows with their hats to the right and left, slipped through, and aimed for an opening. Kiyoteruhime struck the enemy's leg with the tip of her staff with all her might. How could he possibly endure it? Ōdaruwake screamed, 'Ah!', collapsed on the spot, and lay there groaning with his eyes blinking. Seeing this state, the many subordinates, crying 'Disaster!', threw themselves into the fray, risking their lives. This side was a renowned warrior; it would be easy to stop the breath of every one of them without exception, but as they were in a position of serving the Lord God, they could not kill even a single insect-tribe creature. Thus, they were handling them with the tips of their kongō-tsue, like a cat playing with a snake, with light taps. Just then, due to the report of four or five subordinates, the army of the Brahman religion, having prepared their arms, came attacking in countless numbers, side by side, their sharp spears flashing in the sunlight. They surrounded the mother and daughter ten and twenty times over, and the ferocity of them thrusting their spears from all directions was extreme. Even the mother and daughter could not overcome the many with the few, and just as they were resolved that they would have to break the heavenly law and strike down the approaching warriors one by one, a roar that seemed to shake heaven and earth echoed. Dozens of wolves, appearing suddenly from the shade of the trees in the forest, glared at the enemy group, opened their large mouths, and attacked headlong. With that swift maneuver, General Yēru gathered his subordinates and fled like clouds and mist. At this time, the body of the fallen Ōdaruwake was also carried away, and not even a shadow of the enemy could be seen.

The two caught their breath and scooped up the clear water gushing from between the trees of the forest to moisten their throats.

The dozens of wolves chased the enemy in all directions, and then, lowering their heads and drooping their tails, they appeared before the mother and daughter, pretending to be in a state of reverence. They formed two lines, let out a single groan of 'Uu,' and vanished like smoke.

From beyond the forest, a cool voice, belonging to no one known, began to sing a Sendenka:

God appears in the world, To distinguish between good and evil. The way of the God of Ananai-kyō, We are the children of God, the palace of God. Yet even as we say this, How can a human body judge God? How much more so can the people of the world Understand good, evil, right, and wrong? The wicked officials who try to distinguish The karmic origins and nature of the soul, Appear here and there, Trampling upon the true path, And defiling the world into eternal darkness. Ah, Kannagara-kamigami, By the blessing of the spirit of God, Not to mention Ananai-kyō, The Brahman religion and the Ural religion, All the officials of God Who serve the path of the teachings, Rebuild the pillars of the soul, May you establish the reign of Miroku Forever, Kannagara. The lords of the land who serve God, Respectfully and reverently pray.
