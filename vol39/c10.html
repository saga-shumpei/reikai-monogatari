Chapter 10: 八の字 [1075]

八の字〔1075〕

§ 1

Rebu and Taaru were concentrating their efforts on reviving Iiru and Yosefu, who had been thrown into the bottom of the Tanisoko at Kajikatauge, by means of Jinkō Kokifu, when Hamu, whom they had thought to be in a state of Jinjifusei, suddenly stood up and, letting out a voice of anger, attempted to punish the two; finding this unbearable, they scrambled like monkeys through the Tanima where Kauho was Kannan, and finally hid themselves under an old shrine by the Michibata to catch their breath.

Hamu came along, and hearing him lamenting as he became unable to move his Sunekoshi, they suddenly regained their Genki; though they were not the Kami of wind to prey upon the Yowami of others, they used the Kowairo of an Onibaba and Onimusume to Kiyōkatsu Hamu, feeling Tokui.

Just then, the Nōmu that had wrapped the Atari in a Yamaoroshi that blew suddenly cleared away as if wiped clean, and as their faces Miaha, Hamu became Masumasu angry, stood up forgetting the Kutsū of his body, and:

Hamu: 'Bureimono, I shall punish you, know your place!'

The ferocity with which he chased them like Idaten was such that the two, against their will, fled headlong down the mountain path where leaves were scattered, making the ground shake with the sound of 'tonton-ton'.

Rebu began to sing while keeping time with his feet along the way:

In the land of Baramon, famed in name, Appeared in the capital of Haruna, Serving the great Ōkuninushi, Becoming a subordinate of Onikumawake, Princess Mukade and Princess Koito, Having received the Mikoto of the Lord, While searching for their whereabouts, Coming to Kajikatauge, Near the mansion of the Saien, Looking down the mountain path, Appeared a mother and daughter on pilgrimage, Hamu the official leapt up, Now that we five are together, The time has come to show our prowess, He waited, spitting on his hands, The mother and daughter, knowing naught of this, With their bodies braced in waraji and kyahan, In light attire of mino and kasa, Striking the earth with their kongōzue, How amusing their descent.

The mother and daughter on pilgrimage, Looking out at the scenery of the Tanima, Viewing the mountains of brocade, Shining with a thousand yellows and ten thousand reds, Seeing the moment they were lost in admiration, Together with Hamu's signal, Parting the thicket of leaves, When they stood up with arms spread wide, The mother and daughter pilgrims grew angry, And the rough men who had attacked them, Without a word, were grabbed by the eagles, And thrown into the deep Tanima.

Seeing the pitiful state of Yosefu, Iiru, and Hamu, The courage that had been brimming, Vanished before they knew it, Fanned by the wind of cowardice, Thinking that life is the seed of all things, They fled without looking back, Quickening their pace for seven or eight chō.

Appearing in the lower reaches, Descending by trailing wisteria vines, To the mountain stream with its splashing rapids, And going back up the river again, What is this that they see, Thrown into the dust of the dance here, Body and soul half-dead in that moment, The two, wishing to save them, Scooped the clear water of the Tani in their hands, And while making Iiru and Yosefu drink, Spent some time in nursing them with Jinkō Kokifu.

Hamu, whom they thought dead, With strong evil fortune, returned to life, And came chasing after them, targeting the two, Feeling as if they had escaped the tiger's mouth, The two fled for their lives, 'Untoko dottsukoi aitatsuta.'

Taaru, be careful, there are stones, This place is the habitat of a great serpent, Having finally descended the mountain stream, They hid deep under the floor of an old shrine, By the side of the road, While they were trembling, Hamu came again, Clenching his fists and chasing them, Overwhelmed by his momentum, We, startled for the second time, Felt as if our bodies were flying into the void, Such is the terror of descending this slope, Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase, Kami appears in the manifest world, To distinguish between good and evil, Kamunaohi who created this world, Ōnaohi with a heart so wide, By Naohi, seeing anew and hearing anew, While correcting the words, May the two be forgiven for their errors.

Though Hamu, whom we looked up to as our head, Has the courage of a desperate man, Manifest the power of Kami, And Kototama-mukae-yawase, Sweep away the calamities of our bodies, Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase, May the spirits be blessed.

原文（近代文語）

レーブ、タールは、河鹿峠の谷底に投げ込まれたるイール、ヨセフの両人を人工呼吸を以て助けむと丹精を凝らす折しも、人事不省に陥りしと思ひ居たるハムは俄に立ち上り、怒りの声を放ちて二人を懲さむとせしより、『こりや堪まらぬ』と行歩艱難の谷間を猿の如く伝ひて、漸く道傍の古き祠の下に身を潜め息を休めて居た。そこへハムがやつて来て脛腰たたぬ様になり、喞ち嘆くを聞いて俄かに元気づき、弱身につけ込む風の神ならねども、鬼婆鬼娘の声色を使つて、ハムを恐喝しつつ得意がつて居る。折しもあれやサツト吹き来る山颪に四辺を包みし濃霧は拭ふが如く晴れ渡り、互に見合す顔と顔、ハムは益々怒り、身の苦痛を打忘れて立ち上り、

『無礼者、懲して呉れむ、思ひ知れや』

と韋駄天走りに追駆け来る凄じさ。二人は心も心ならず一目散に木の葉散敷く山道を地響きさせて、トントントンと下り行く。

レーブは途々足拍子をとり乍ら唄ひ始めた。

『バラモン国に名も高き ハルナの都に現はれし 大黒主に仕へたる 鬼熊別の部下となり 蜈蚣の姫や小糸姫 君の御言を蒙りて 所在を探ぬる折柄に 斎苑の館に程近き 河鹿峠に来て見れば 山の小路を伝ひ来る 母娘二人の巡礼姿 ハムの司は躍り立ち 今や吾々五人連れ 手柄現はす時来ぬと 手に唾して待ち居たり かくとは知らぬ母娘連れ 草鞋脚絆に身を固め 扮装も軽き蓑笠の 金剛杖にて地を叩き 降り来れる面白さ

母娘二人の巡礼は 谷間の景色を打眺め 千黄万紅輝きし 錦の山を打眺め 感賞したる隙を見て ハムの目配せ諸共に 木の葉の茂みをかき分けて 大手を拡げ衝つ立てば 母娘の巡礼は腹を立て 武者振りついた荒男 物をも云はず鷲掴み 千尋の谷間に投げ棄てぬ ヨセフ　イールやハム三人 果敢なき姿を見るにつけ 張りきり居たる勇気まで 何時の間にやら消滅し 臆病風に煽られて 命あつての物種と 後をも見ずに逃げて行く 足を早めて七八丁

下手の方に現はれて 激潭飛沫の谷川に 藤蔓伝ひ下り立ち 又もや川を遡り 至りて見れば此は如何に 三人は此処に舞埃の 中に棄てられ身も魂も 半死半生の其の刹那 救ひやらむと両人が 谷の清水を手に掬ひ イール　ヨセフに呑ませつつ 人工呼吸の介抱に 暫し時をば移しける 死んだと思うたハムの奴 悪運強く生かへり 二人を目蒐けて追ひ来る こりや堪らぬと両人は 虎口を逃れし心地して 命からがら逃げて行く 「ウントコ　ドツコイ　アイタツタ」

タールよ気をつけ石がある 此処は大蛇の棲処ぞや 漸う谷川下り来て 道の傍の古祠 床下深く忍び込み 慄ひ居たりし折柄に 又もやハムがやつて来て 拳固をかためて追ひ来る 其勢ひに辟易し 二度吃驚の吾々は 躰も宙に飛ぶ如く 此坂降る恐ろしさ あゝ惟神々々 神が表に現はれて 善と悪とを立て別ける 此世を造りし神直日 心も広き大直日 直日に見直し聞直し 宣り直しつつ両人が 身の過ちを許せかし

吾等の頭と仰ぎたる ハムに決死の勇あるも 神の力を現はして 言向和せ吾々が 身の災を払ひませ あゝ惟神々々 御霊幸ひましませよ』

と歌ひ乍ら細き谷道を下り行く。

タールは又歌ひ出す。

『ガランダ国から現はれた ガラクタ男のハム公が チヨン猪口才な吾々を 追ひ駆けまはすは何の事 抜山蓋世の勇あるも バラモン教にて鍛へたる 吾言霊の神力に 木の葉の風に散る如く 吹き散らさむかと思へども やつぱり俺は弱い奴 『コラ』と一声云つたきり 頭の頂から足の尖 強き電気に打たれし如く ビリビリビリと震ひ出す 不思議な力を持つた奴 強い奴にはドツと逃げ 弱い奴には攻めて行く 之が孫呉の兵法だ 抑軍の掛引は 三十六計ありと云へ

§ 2

To treasure one's life and flee is the most clever way to do things. No matter how much one speaks of the way of Kami, it is unbearable to have one's life taken away. 'Untoko dottsukoi dottsukoishiyo.' Rebu's legs are slow. If you dawdle, Hamu will catch up from behind, and that fellow will strike my head, poka-poka, raising a demon's fern, and that will be the end, 'Untokoshiyo.' With one cry, 'Un,' though it be fleeting, it is a parting from this world, a journey to the land of the dead. There is nothing so miserable as this. Hey, hey, run faster! Shall I run ahead of you? It is dangerous for an Ashibeta like you to go first. At the very least, I must preserve my own life alone.

'Untoko, dottsukoi, how tiresome.' To have a Maurokuoyaji as a traveling companion truly agitates the heart. There, there, the approaching footsteps are heard, don-don-don. 'Untoko dottsukoi, wait, that was wrong.' It is the sound of water flowing through the valley. Nevertheless, we cannot afford to dawdle. On one side is a mountain stream, and on the other, we are surrounded by steep mountains; it is a Hitosujimichi that looks like a throat. If there were a thicket of leaves, I would like to hide for a moment, but unfortunately, this is a Hageyama. This is unbearable, what shall I do? I am beginning to feel a strange sensation, as if I am making a journey through hell. 'Aitatatsuta, aitatsuta.' Rebu, wait a moment, I have fallen.

My knee is scraped, and the red blood is flowing drip-drip. If he catches up to this place and strikes my head, poka-poka, there will be no end to the misery. Hey, hey, Rebu, wait a moment. You are a man with no loyalty to your friends. You abandon your friend in distress and run away like a white cloud behind. Your heartless behavior—ah, ah, it hurts, my leg is throbbing. If I had known it would come to this, I should have fled sooner. I wanted the two of us to help each other. We descended into a terrifying valley, and now we are suffering the bitterness of Taru, having fallen into a miserable state where we catch neither horsefly nor bee. Ah, Kannagara-kamukami-kamukami, may the spirits be blessed, will you stop Rebu's feet?

Hamu, do not lose your courage, I pray for one of two things. Please, by all means, forgive me. Although I know it was my own doing, in this case of life or death, I ask you, even if it may be unreasonable. Ah, ah, it is scary, it is terrifying. If I collapse in a place like this, even if the fellow Hamu does not come, wolves and tigers will surely appear and take my life to eat. When I think of it, it is a sad and helpless state of my current self. Pitying the heart of Taru, who has sunk into the abyss of despair, oh Kami of the Brahman religion, please help me. I ask you, clinging to my life. Ah, Kannagara-kamukami-kamukami, may the spirits be blessed.

So saying, he collapsed by the roadside, having sprained his knee joint, and was groaning in a weeping voice. The one who came rushing there, out of breath, was Hamu. When Rebu raised his head to look at what had become of him, he saw that he had fled far away and was running along the Hageyama path to a point where his figure looked as small as a three-year-old child. Hamu's footsteps were approaching moment by moment, don-don.

Hamu was coming down the slope with all his might. On the momentum of the descent, he could not stop his body, and he trampled over the body of Taru, who had collapsed on the narrow valley path, stumbled over his head, and with the excess momentum, he was thrown down as if cast away two or three ken down the slope. He too scraped his knee, and while saying 'Aitatatsuta,' he frowned and rubbed his knee. The pain in his lower back, which he had forgotten in his anger, had become intense once again. The two of them were collapsed on the slope in the shape of the character 'eight,' beginning a Kototama battle of 'Aitatsuta, un-un' in a Kannagara manner. A sudden gale blew, as if to make the rocks fly, and the hems of their clothes were blown up; Hamu and Taru were both exposing their thick, black buttocks to the wind. The unfeeling wind blew mercilessly, and in harmony with the groaning of the two, they were moaning, 'Uun, uun.' Listening to the voice of the propaganda song that could be heard on the wind, Taru and Hamu strained their ears, and saying 'This is unbearable,' they pressed their palms together and prayed with all their might.

命を大事に逃げ出すが 一番利巧なやり方だ 何程神の道ぢやとて 命をとられちや堪らない 「ウントコ　ドツコイ　ドツコイシヨ」 レーブの足は遅いぞよ 愚図々々してると追着いて ハムの奴めが後から 俺の頭をポカポカと 鬼の蕨をふりあげて 打すが最後「ウントコシヨ」 ウンと一声果敢なくも 此世の別れ死出の旅 こんなつまらぬ事はない こりやこりやもつと早う走れ 俺から先へ走らうか お前の様な足遅が 先へ行くのは物騒な せめて俺だけ一人なと 命を保たにやなるまいぞ

「ウントコ、ドツコイ辛気やな」 耄禄爺の道連れは ほんに心が揉めるわい それそれ近寄る足音が ドンドンドンと聞えてる 「ウントコ　ドツコイ　こら違うた」 谷間を流るる水の音 さはさり乍ら吾々は 愚図々々しては居られない 一方は谷川一方は 嶮しき山に囲まれし 喉首見た様な一筋道 木の葉の茂みがあるならば 一寸潜んで見たいけれど 生憎此処は禿山だ 此奴ア堪らぬ　どうしようぞ 地獄の旅をする様な 怪態な心になつて来る 「アイタヽタツタ　アイタツタ」 レーブ一寸待て俺や倒けた

擦り剥けよつた膝頭 タラタラ流れる赤い血が こんな処へ追ひついて 頭をポカポカやられたら おたまりこぼしは無い程に こらこらレーブ一寸待て 友達甲斐のない男 友の難儀をふり棄てて 後白雲と走り行く お前の薄情な其仕打 アーアー痛い足疼く こんな事だと知つたなら 早く逃げたらよかつたに 二人の奴が助けたさ 恐ろし谷間に下り立つて 虻蜂とらずの惨い目に 遭うたるタールの苦しさよ あゝ惟神々々 御霊幸ひましまして レーブの足を留めるか

ハムの脛腰抜かすなと 二つに一つの御願ひ 何卒許して下さんせ 勝手の事とは知り乍ら 九死一生の此場合 無理かは知らねど願ひます アーアー怖い恐ろしい こんな処に倒れたら 仮令ハム奴が来なくても 虎狼が現はれて 生命をとつて喰うだらう 思へば悲しき今の身の 詮術もなき有様よ 絶望の淵に沈みたる タールの心を憐れみて バラモン教の神様よ 何卒お助け下さんせ 命からがら願ひます あゝ惟神々々 御霊幸ひましませよ』

と道傍に倒れ膝頭の関節を挫き、泣き声となつて呻いてゐる。其処へ息せききつてやつて来たのはハムであつた。レーブは如何なりしと頭を上げて眺むれば、彼は遠くも逃げ去つて三歳の童子の如く其姿の小さく見ゆる処まで禿山道を走つて居る。ハムの足音はドンドンと刻々に近寄り来る。

ハムは一生懸命坂道を降り来る。勢づいた降り道、俄に身の留めやうもなく細谷道に倒れたるタールの躰をグサグサと踏越え、頭に躓き、勢あまつて二三間ばかり坂道の下に、投げつけられた様に打つ倒れ、これ又膝頭を擦り剥き『アイタヽタツタ』と云ひながら顔を顰め膝頭を撫でて居る。怒りに乗じて忘れて居た腰の痛みが又もや烈しくなつて来た。二人は坂道に八の字形に打倒れ、『アイタツタ、ウンウン』の言霊戦を惟神的に開始してゐる。岩も飛べよとばかり俄に吹き来る暴風に着物の裾を捲られ、ハム、タールの両人は揃ひも揃うて太い黒い臀部を風に丸曝にしてゐる。無心の風は容赦なく吹き荒み、両人の呻声と相和してウーン　ウーンと呻り立てて居る。風のまにまに聞え来る宣伝歌の声にタール、ハムは耳をすませ、『こりや堪らぬ』と両手を合せ一生懸命に祈つて居る。
