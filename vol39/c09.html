Chapter 9: 九死一生 [1074]

九死一生〔1074〕

§ 1

A Setsuteiri of the land of Garanda, who served under Onikumawake, had been stripped of his hereditary rank of Hamu by the subordinates of Ōkuronushi; although he had fallen to the lowly position of a Metsukeyaku under Onikumawake, dozens of his own subordinates secretly submitted to his command and served him with Chiujitsu, and as the remnant of the former Hamu, he received a Sautau amount of Sonkei from the Kokumin.

Now, while searching for the Arika of Mukadehime and Koitohime by the Mei of Onikumawake, he was waiting at this Kajikatauge with four Kogashirakabu under a Keiyaku that if he could capture and return with even one more Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, he would be restored as the king of his original land of Garanda. However, the four men did not know the Sujiyō of this Hamu; regarding him as a somehow Ōhei and disagreeable fellow, they watched him with eyes of Saigi, secretly conspiring to sniff out any Shitsupai he might make and report each one to Onikumawake, so as to cause the downfall of this Hamu who was like a lump above their eyes.

To such a place, the mother and daughter pilgrims came walking and encountered them; he gave the order to the four men to bind them with straw ropes without mercy and return to the capital of Haruna. The four men, striving not to be outdone by one another, rushed at the mother and daughter, only to be thrown into the valley without effort, and Hamu was also brittlely cast down to the bottom of the ravine. As expected of a direct descendant of the Setsuteiri, his soul was somehow firm; unlike Īru and Yosefu, he did not easily lose consciousness, and while buried in the sand at the bottom of the valley, enduring the pain and waiting for a natural recovery, Rēbu and Tāru crossed the valley and approached, pouring out various insults against Hamu; hearing their secret talk of helping the two women and throwing Hamu into the river at this time, his indignation made him forget the pain of his illness, and—

Hamu: 'You hateful pair!'

As he stood up, Rēbu and Tāru abandoned Īru and Yosefu and fled for their lives along the valley stream. While Hamu was gnashing his teeth in resentment and tending to Īru and Yosefu, the propagation song heard from above made him cry, 'This is unbearable!' and he leaped over rocks and waded through shallows with the speed of Idaten, finally climbing up to the mountain path; looking toward a forest on one side, he saw an old shrine there. Thinking to rest here for a while and then search for the Arika of Rēbu and Tāru to punish them, he was huffing with rage; but the moment he sat upon a stone in front of the shrine to rest, his tensed courage suddenly slackened, his waist pained and his legs throbbed again, and in a suffering that rendered him unable to move, he wept in resentment and grief over the treatment by Rēbu and Tāru. Suddenly, the voices of two pilgrims came from behind the shrine, and Hamu was startled once more,

Hamu: 'Ah! They were not ordinary pilgrims, but a demon-hag and demon-daughter who devour humans!'

Enveloped in thick mist, he did not know it was the feigned voices of Rēbu and Tāru, the targets of his resentment. Rēbu and Tāru, hearing Hamu's soliloquy and taking advantage of his inability to stand, were mocking and jeering at him with utter contempt; but suddenly the mountain wind blew, the thick mist cleared, and the truth was exposed. At that moment, his anger reached its peak, and with his hair standing on end, he rose up, forgetting his pained and ill body, and chased after the two fleeing men.

Hamu: 'I shall not let you escape! Realize your fate!'

While saying this, he clenched his fists and advanced along the steep mountain path with a thundering sound, 'ton-ton-ton,' like the raging of Ashura-ō; it was truly terrifying.

Hamu forgot his pain and began to sing, keeping time with each step.

Hamu: 'Though one speaks of the times and seasons I am the Setsuteiri of the land of Garanda The Hamu of generations of my fathers Having become a subordinate of Onikumawake Unaware that I am a noble being waiting for my time Those lowly fellows Rēbu and Tāru Their attitude of utter contempt Touches the core of my indignant heart Thinking that I must punish them once My heart was filled with such thoughts But until I achieve my objective Enduring with the thought that anger is a loss I have passed by with an indifferent face On the mountain path of Kajikatauge I met the mother and daughter pair I knew they were surely Mukadehime And the princess Koito But if I had said so, those fellows Like puppies over rotten meat Would have snarled at each other in dispute'

原文（近代文語）

鬼熊別の部下に仕へたるガランダ国の刹帝利、親重代のハムの位＠を大黒主の部下にとり剥がれ、僅かに鬼熊別の部下となり、卑しき目付役に成り下り居たれども、彼の部下は数十人密かに彼の頤使に甘んじて忠実に仕へ、昔のハムの果てとして、相当に尊敬を国民より払はれて居た。

今しも鬼熊別が命によつて蜈蚣姫、小糸姫の所在を索ねる一方、三五教の宣伝使を一人にても多く捕縛し帰らば、もとのガランダ国の王に復しやらむとの契約の下に四人の小頭株を引き率れ、此河鹿峠に待ちつつあつたのである。然し乍ら四人の男は此ハムの素姓を知らず、何となく横柄な奴、虫の好かない奴と猜疑の眼を怒らし、何か失敗ある時は、これを嗅出し一々鬼熊別に内報し、目の上の瘤なるハムを失墜せしめむと心密かに諜し合せつつあつた。

かかる処へ母娘の巡礼、進み来るに出会し、何の容赦も荒縄に、縛つてハルナの都まで、立ち帰らむと四人に下知を下した。四人は吾劣らじと母娘に向つて武者振りつき、苦もなく谷間に投げ捨てられ、ハムも亦脆くも谷底に捨てられて了つた。流石刹帝利の直系とて何処となく身魂堅固なりしかば、イール、ヨセフの如く容易に失神せず谷底の真砂に埋められて痛さを堪へて自然の恢復を待つ折しも、レーブ、タールの両人は谷を渡つて近寄り来り、散々にハムの悪口を並べ立て、此際二人を助けハムを谷川へ投げ捨てやらむとの密談を聞くより憤怒のあまり病の苦痛を忘れて、

ハム『おのれ憎くき両人』

と立ち上ればレーブ、タールはイール、ヨセフを捨て、谷川伝ひに生命辛々逃げて行く。ハムは無念の歯を喰ひしばり、イール、ヨセフを介抱し居る折しも、頭上に聞ゆる宣伝歌『こりや堪らぬ』と韋駄天走りに岩を飛び越え浅瀬を渉り、漸く山道に攀上り片方の森を眺むれば、此処に一つの古き祠がある。一先づ此処に息休め、レーブ、タール両人が所在を探ね、懲らしめ呉れむと息まきつつ、社前の石に腰うち掛け息を休めむとする時しも、張り詰めたる勇気は茲にガタリと弛み、再び腰痛み足うづき、身動きならぬ苦しさに、レーブ、タールの両人が仕打ちを憤慨し怨み涙に暮れてゐる。忽ち祠の後より二人の巡礼の声、ハムは又もや二度吃驚、

『アヽ彼は普通の巡礼ではなく、人を取り喰ふ鬼婆鬼娘であつたか』

と濃霧に包まれて怨みの的なるレーブ、タールの両人が作り声とは知らなかつた。レーブ、タールはハムの独言を聞き足腰立たぬにつけ込んで侮りきつて揶揄つて居たが、忽ち吹き来る山風に濃霧は晴れ其真相が暴露すると共に、怒り心頭に徹し、怒髪天を衝いて立ち上り苦しき病の身を忘れ、逃げゆく二人の後追うて、

『逃しはせじ、思ひ知れや』

と言ひ乍ら握り拳を固めつつ、さしも嶮しき坂道をトントントンと地響きさせ阿修羅王の荒れし如く進み行くこそすさまじき。

ハムは痛さを忘れ、一足々々拍子をとり乍ら歌ひ出した。

『時世時節と云ひ乍ら ガランダ国の刹帝利 親代々のハムの俺 鬼熊別の部下となり 時待つ尊き身と知らず 卑しきレーブやタール奴が 侮りきつたる其態度 小癪に触る俺の胸 一度は懲らしめやらむずと 思ひは胸に満ちぬれど 吾目的を遂ぐるまで 怒つちや損だと辛抱して 知らぬ顔にて過ぎて来た 河鹿峠の山道で テツキリ会うた母娘連 此奴あテツキリ蜈蚣姫 小糸の姫と知つたれど さう言つたなら彼奴め等は 腐つた肉を犬の子が 争ふ如くに啀み合ひ

§ 2

They will surely start to snatch each other's achievements, thinking that they will end up in a chaotic mess without gaining anything at all. With this plan in mind, I shall feign ignorance; if they are an old woman and a girl, I will take them back to the capital of Haruna and offer them to Onikumawake-dono. By manipulating them, I will deceive the four of them, and once my goal is successfully achieved, I will scatter them on the way. Then, I will reveal my true identity here, pretend to be a loyal retainer and a righteous warrior, and enjoy the fruits of my labor alone. All my thoughts have turned into bubbles of water. Untoko dottsukoi, aitata-tsuta! Because I schemed only for my own greed, I have received divine retribution.

Mukade no Hime and Koitohime were thrown into the bottom of the valley by the two officials with ruthless force, and how pathetic it was to see them exhausted. As regret welled up in their hearts and they spent their time in tears, the two men, whose bad luck was strong, escaped the jaws of death and came to the bottom of the valley. While they were whispering, I listened while pretending to be dead. They were cursing us with all their might, saying, 'Even if we save Iru and Yosefu, we cannot bear to save Hamu.' Because they were talking about giving us a water burial in this mountain stream while we were in a state of unconsciousness, I was so enraged by their rudeness that I forgot my pain and stood up. When I clenched my fists and glared at them, the cowardly and spineless pair...

They abandon their close friend in danger and run away. Hamu-ko, who remained behind, was in the middle of artificial respiration to save the lives of the two. The propaganda song of the San-iku-kyo began to be heard like thunder. My head ached, my chest felt tight, and my physical suffering was boundless. Leaving the two fellows to die, I followed after Rebu and Taaru, but when I arrived in front of the shrine, I felt limp and relaxed. My waist began to ache again, and my legs were so numb that I could not move. Without knowing that the two fellows were hiding under the floor, I began to grumble and complain. Just as I was lamenting, I heard the voice of the old woman, followed by the voice of the girl.

I am the demon hag and the demon girl, and this is my greeting that I will eat you up. Seeing Mukade no Hime and Koitohime as two people was a delusion of the eyes. We are the demon mother and daughter who devour people. I have gotten myself into a mess. Even the mighty Hamu-sama cannot bear to face demons with mysterious, magical powers. I must say something to escape this situation, and as I uttered various fabricated words, the demon hag became even more arrogant and began to speak of unreasonable things. Even though I have now fallen from grace and become a catcher and inspector, if you trace the source, I am a Kshatriya of the Garanda Kingdom, a person who was respected by the people of the country as Hamu-sama.

I cannot bear to be weak-hearted. Even if my legs and waist cannot stand, rather than meeting a cowardly end, I would rather shatter like a jewel and die. Just as I was making up my mind, a mountain storm suddenly blew in. The clouds and mist that had enveloped the surroundings finally cleared away here. When I looked closely, what is this? The two fellows, Rebu and Taaru, are taking advantage of my physical disability, looking down on me, and mocking me, Hamu-sama of the Kshatriya tribe. The sight of their attitude was so hateful that my anger suddenly reached the heavens. I forgot the pain in my waist, and although I chased them this far, my waist began to ache again, and my legs started to feel strange. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-kamu.

Receiving the blessings of the gods, I pray that Hamu's legs and waist may be healed swiftly and healthily. Kannagara. I humbly beseech Thee. Aitata-tsuta, aitatsuta! I cannot take even one more step. Is there no god among the hundred gods of the heaven and earth and the Brahmins who will listen to me? It is not that I am complaining, but is it not because I have been serving the Brahmin religion morning and evening that I want the gods to help me at such a time? When I think about it, it is regrettable. I cannot advance even an inch further. Am I to die in the fields? Must an unlucky person end up unlucky to the very end?

Am I to become prey for wolves, lions, and bears? Is this how the life of Hamu of the Garanda Kingdom is to end? If the clouds drifting in the sky have a heart, convey my message to my wife in the distant foreign land of Garanda. My rope of hope has run out, and is this miserable, tragic self of today appearing in the heart of the gods of heaven and earth as a mirror of evil? Must I die here, bearing the hundred sins that my distant ancestors committed? When I think about it, it is regrettable. Even though I pray to the gods this much, if there is no sign, there is nothing I can do. Now that I have made up my mind, I do not fear death.

I leave my body in the hands of the gods. Even if my corpse is exposed in the fields, I pray that my immortal soul may be saved before the noble gods in the Tosotsuten of Takamagahara. Kannagara. O Great God of the Brahmin religion, I pray before Thee.

With these words, he squeezed out a tearful voice, collapsed on the mountain path, closed his eyes in resignation, and waited for the moment of his death.

At this moment, subtle music was heard from nowhere, and as he watched white lotus petals fluttering down from the heavens, Hamu's body felt as if he had suddenly swallowed cool, refreshing water, and in an instant, he returned to his original healthy state. Overjoyed, Hamu thanked heaven and earth, apologized for the sin of his words, heart, and actions not being in harmony until now, and leisurely walked down the slope. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-haese.
