Chapter 11: 鼻摘 [1076]

鼻摘〔1076〕

§ 1

Clearing away the dark Eūn that blocked the Ametsuchi of the Brahman country, and spreading the Ananahi Oshihe of Kami, the Kunikō, who leads the three Sendenshi—Terukuniwake, Terukuniume, and the third—down the Sakamichi, proceeds while singing the Sendenka, illuminating the Moon Country with the light of Jitsugetsu and bringing the Ōkuronushi, whose heart is also dark, to Kotomukeyaha.

Kunikō: 'As Kami appears on the surface to Tatewa the good and the evil, I recall the Kajikatauge, the Kiusekichi where the Sendenshi Kotoyoriwake of old, riding upon a Kurige horse, was blown by a Retsupū that broke all records while crossing, fell into the Tanisoko, and explored the Tengoku. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! May the Mitama of Kami grant its blessing, stop the Retsupū now blowing, and allow our party to proceed easily to the capital of the Moon. Kamunagara! As a Kunikō serving in the retinue of the Sendenshi, the messenger of Kami, I pray with all my heart. Autumn is gradually deepening.'

Kunikō: 'The appearance of Sahohime, who weaves the brocade of a thousand yellows and ten thousand reds, is truly beautiful, like the autumn foliage of the eternal spring, "Dotsukoishiyo." Is the wind now blowing a wicked wind, or is it the noble Kamikaze? It is truly a dangerous wind. If I were to strike it with a "don" and slide through the air, only to fall into this Tanisoko, and if it were fated that I could make a journey to the Tengoku Jōdo, I would not be afraid in the least. But as for a Mitama as sinful as ours, how could we ever... "Untokosho, dotsukoi, dotsukoi, dotsukoishiyo." It is truly a dangerous Sakamichi. If I were to fall into the country of the roots and drop into the Hakan Jigoku...'

Kunikō: 'It is clear that I would open the gates of suffering for ten thousand kalpas and the end of the world. If I had lived in this world for a while and achieved even a little merit before Kami, I would never regret it. However, if such a thing were to happen now, how could I ever proceed before the presence of Kami? Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! Please stop this wind. I am so frightened I cannot bear it. I wonder where Ōganehime and Kiyoteruhime, who went ahead, are traveling now. Surely the mother and daughter are being troubled by this difficult wind, and are likely walking with their backs turned, faces flushed with frustration.'

Kunikō: 'Thinking of it as if I see it now, that is my primary concern. Setting aside Ōganehime, can I remain unconcerned about the figure of Kiyoteruhime? It is truly a poisonous wind. "Untokodotsukoi, Umekō-san!" "Yatsutokodotsukoi, Teru-sama!" Watch your footing carefully, for it is not just the wind. On such a steep Sakamichi, sharp stones are rising up one after another. Tigers, wolves, lions, and bears must also be crouching on the road, blown about by this Retsupū and crawling out of the valleys. You must not carelessly become their opponent, "Dotsukoishiyo," Teru-sama and Umekō-san. Excuse me, Sendenshi!'

Kunikō: 'You were originally known in the world as Umehiko, a Kami-tsukasa. If you are a person who has traveled around the countries in all directions, you must have experienced encountering Retsupū and heavy rain. Please, I beg you, tell me about it. Even though I am a rough man who cut my umbilical cord in the Moon Country and am now facing such a plight, and even though I speak strongly, my heart is pounding like a fast bell inside. Hey, excuse me, Sendenshi! It is greedy of you to remain silent when I am begging you this much. No matter how much you remain silent, this Retsupū will not easily subside. "Untokodotsukoi, aitata!"'

Kunikō: 'Oh, oh, this is a terrible situation. It seems my footing is becoming dangerous, "Dotsukoishiyo." An old shrine on the side of the road has been fanned by this Retsupū and has scattered into pieces, disappearing without a trace. If even a shrine dedicated to Kami is scattered so cruelly, how could the flesh-palace of Kunikō-sama, where the Bontennō resides, remain without being scattered? Truly, when I think of it, I am worried. "Untoko, dotsukoi, dotsukoishiyo." Look, look, there is a human shadow over there.'

Kunikō: 'That person, too, has been blown by the wind and has collapsed; whether they are crying or not, I do not know. They are stretched out in the shape of the character for eight, exposing their black backside.'

Thus he sang, and descended while being fanned by the fierce wind.

The mountain blast, which had been so violent, ceased abruptly, and the scenery of heaven and earth settled into a quiet calm, the rustling of the trees falling silent; the sky was colored in deep navy, while the earth was a field of brocade spread everywhere. The God of the Heavenly Sun sank faintly at the edge of the mountains, and the air of twilight gradually drew near. From nowhere, the sound of a bell echoed, proclaiming that all earthly things are impermanent. Birds, as if returning early to their roosts toward their dwellings, busy in the movement of their wings, flew in flocks of ten, twenty, or thirty toward the peak of the western mountain. Terukuniwake opened his mouth for the first time:

Terukuniwake: 'Ah, Kunikō-sama, you must feel relieved now. The wind raged quite a bit, but you also raged no less than it. You seemed considerably frightened; you are a man of small liver. A person's heart is entirely revealed in their words and deeds. If you do not adopt a slightly more composed attitude, you will never do as a Sendenshi of the realm.'

Kunikō: 'Absurd! I felt as though that wind was celebrating our future, and I was overcome with an invigorating mood that made it unbearably pleasant. Walking along dolefully like a dead person through a quiet autumn heaven and earth, unsure if one is dead or alive, is not very manly. My raging was a manifestation of so-called composure. It is the principle of movement within quietude; even so, both Terukō and Umekō turned pale and could not raise even a single squeak, so I felt unbearably sorry for them, and representing the fear of those two, I merely tried saying a little joke like that. I cannot bear to be thought a coward from your heart. Ahahaha!'

He shook his shoulders and put on a hero's laugh.

Terukuniwake spoke:

Terukuniwake: 'Well, whatever it may be, as long as you are energetic, it is fine. You must never be pessimistic. Starting with Kunikō-sama, the two of you were quite seized by fear.'

Terukō: 'Yes, as you say, my vital courage was taken away considerably.'

Umekō: 'I too was trembling, thinking, "A somewhat unusual wind is blowing..." However, it was not that I trembled out of fear. Because the wind blowing against my thinly clad skin was cold, I merely tried vibrating a little in response to the scenery.'

斯かる所へ山の尾を登り来る満月の光、ハムは手早く両人の髪をほどき、

『ヤア、タール、モウ許してやらう。以後はキツと慎んであの様な悪戯を致すでないぞ、そしてあのやうな水臭い事を思ふと、今度はモウ了見せぬからさう思へ。今日はこれきり忘れて遣はす』

タール『俺もお前がさう出れば、万更憎いとは思はない、只今限り忘れタールから、マア安心致すがよからう。ナア国さま、余り物をクニクニと苦にすると、病気になつて、しまひにや国替をせなくてはなりませぬからなア』

国公『国替なぞと縁起の悪い事をいつてくれるない』

ハム『アツハヽヽヽ』

タール『イツヒヽヽヽ』

国公『ウツフヽヽヽ、サアもう夜があけた。行かうぢやないか』

と三人は兄弟の如く親しくなつて、無駄口を叩き乍ら坂路さして降り行く。
