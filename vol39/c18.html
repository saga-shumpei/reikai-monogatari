§ 1

On the mountain top of the Thames Pass, a barrier of the Brahman religion has been established, where four or five people are examining the faith of those passing through. This is the ostensible reason, but in reality, acting under the command of Ōkuronushi, they have been keeping watch day and night with orders to search for the whereabouts of Mukadehime and Ōriyōhime, and to bind them and bring them back secretly to the mansion of Ōkuronushi the moment they are found. These barrier guards were five men named Harukō, Kiyokō, Michikō, Momiji, and Yukikō. From morning until night, at this barrier where sometimes not even a single person passed through, the five would open their mouths wide, stretch their hands up to the ceiling in the shape of an inverted 'hachi', and make a competition of yawning, saying 'Ah, ah, ah.' Having no other choice, they would pluck fruits from all around, brew fruit wine, and spend their days and nights drinking and drinking until they became complete drunkards. It goes without saying that even if there were a thousand such guards, they would be of no use at all. Harukō, beginning to feel the effects of the liquor, said,

Harukō: 'Hey, Yukikō, your name is 'Snow', yet it is truly beyond my understanding why your body gets hot and your face turns red when you drink sake. That is why they say this is a world full of monsters. Ge-gara-gara-gara.' Yukikō: 'What do you mean by monsters? The world is all like this. Do you know the song that goes, "A single bird is called a chicken, the morning glory blooms in red, and the character for snow is written with ink"? Is your name not Haru? Despite being Harukō, you go around with your nose in the air, singing humming tunes, and floating about from morning till night even though it is becoming autumn; I cannot understand the reason for it at all. You are the very monster with the price tag on it. No, you are a fool. Do you not know that if you slander others too much, it falls back upon yourself? It is just like spitting at the sky. Ah, I am drunk, I am drunk. (Dodoitsu) "In other places, a wife is like a distant mountain forest; who knows who is stealing her or who is taking her." It is a lie to say that humans do not need to be in contact with the scenery of nature. How much more pleasant it is than to be in a great city, which is the height of dust and worldly noise, inhaling the smoke of chimneys and smoldering like a Void Tathagata. Living in a place like this, it seems my three-hundred-year lifespan will extend by eight hundred years of lies. Delicious, delicious, this sake is delicious. Eighteenth-century people spit out, "Three ri to the sake shop, one ri to the tofu shop," but there is nothing as splendid as the life of the Age of the Gods, which is the height of governance and joy, where one tills the fields to eat, digs wells to drink, plucks fruits with one's own hands from trees heavy with them, and brews and enjoys sake with one's own hands. Rather than living in a dark world, lining up hypocritical words about benevolence, righteousness, and propriety, there is nothing as comfortable as continuing a simple life, looking out over the four directions from this mountain peak where the mountains are blue, the water is clear, and the sky is high, acting like a king. Whatever the case, rather than chirping about worthless logic like the spirit being the master and the body the follower, or about compassion, emotion, or morality, there is nothing as clever as transcending good and evil, passing through reason, enjoying the scenery of the Divine, and spending one's life in ease. No matter what, the future is dark; this moment is the golden age for us to carry out our free will. (Song) "Drink and eat, for the future is dark; if you drink sake, boil it in an earthenware pot." It is a good contrast to drink sake boiled in an earthenware pot and boil one's own kettle-head. Even if I were to say I would make myself a ham in the capital of Haruna, once I have learned such a life of freedom, I can never bring myself to leave this barrier guard post, rather than going to a complicated city and living a life of nothing but flattery and falsehood.'
