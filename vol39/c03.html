Chapter 3: 出師 [1068]

出師〔1068〕

§ 1

Terukuniwake no Umehiko was permitted by the Kyōshiyu of Hinodenowake to head toward the capital of Haruna in the land of the Moon, and with spirits high, he took his seat.

Umehiko: 'Arigata is it that I have fulfilled the will of Kami, / I go to the land of the Moon as Terukuniwake. / Now then, from here I shall depart, Ikuyohime, / guard the mansion purely hereafter.'

Ikuyohime sings:

Ikuyohime: 'My husband of Terukuniwake, / by the Kyōshiyu of Hinodenowake, / to the land of the Moon where lurks / Ōkuronushi no Kamutsukasa, / and the hundred other Shikogami, / according to the noble Mioshihe of Kami, / to Kotomukeyaha and save them, / you depart from the Iso mansion; / how valiant is this day of your departure. / I shall remain behind, / and while guarding the Kyō of Kami, / with all my strength, I shall guide / the people of the world to the Ōmichi of Sumeōkami, / and save them. / My dear husband, even for a Hitotoki, / leave the mansion quickly, / and the calamities that cause suffering and pain / to the hundred Shinjin of Tenchi, / please sweep them away, Kamunagara, / I pray before the Mimahe of Kami.'

As she finished singing, Terukuniwake said:

Terukuniwake: 'Now then, even if the fortune of Magatsu is in the land of the Moon, / I shall advance to the capital of Haruna. / How surprising this day's departure must be / to the Shingun of the Iso mansion of Ōkuronushi. / O officials who stand in rows in the Yahiro-dono, / I shall depart for the capital of Haruna.'

Tokiokishi-kami stands and sings:

Tokiokishi-kami: 'Terukuniwake-kami no Mikoto, go quickly, / I shall remain in the mansion and guard it.'

From this time, Kōryūhime and Mukadehime, having obtained the consent of Hinodenowake-no-Mikoto and being praised by many officials, changed their forms into ordinary travelers, crossed the land of Fusa, boarded a boat from the sea of Fusa, and decided to advance to the capital of Haruna. Also, Umehiko immediately prepared the attire of a missionary, and together with the retainers of Terukō, Kokukō, and Umekō, crossed the Kajika Pass, headed southeast through the land of Fusa, and decided to advance to the land of the Moon.

At this time, Tamakuniwake no Otohiko stood up and began to sing:

Otohiko: 'Serving the San-go-kyō, / the missionary of Tamakuniwake, / Otohiko, known by reputation, / preached in the land of Persia, / floated upon the sea, crossed to the island / of Jitentō, and while preaching the Kyō of Kami to far and near, / I stood and remained in the mountains of Ōe. / Toward the great leaders of Brahmanism, / Onikumohiko and Onikumawake, / the Majin, I opened a battle / of Kototama and scattered the Magatsu-kami, / I am a man of strength. / No matter how brave Umehiko may be, / can he compare to the official Otohiko? / The great Kyōshiyu of Hinodenowake, / sending Terukuniwake, / to Kotomukeyaha the Magatsu-kami of Ōkuronushi, / I wonder at his planning.

No matter how full of divine virtue, / or how one may possess the Kototama that shakes Tenchi, / the enemy also has sharp blades. / How can one or two men's strength / finish the task of Kotomukeyaha? / The heart of this Otohiko / is truly covered by clouds of anxiety, / and I am troubled for the future. / Ah, Kamunagara, Kami, Kami, / look back upon the heart of Kami, / and send one more, / Tamakuniwake, I pray with all my heart. / Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, / whether the moon waxes or wanes, / even if the earth should sink, / I shall never stain / the Mioshihe of the San-go-kyō.

Like the sun and moon, brightly, / revealing the light of the path, / I shall report again / before the Mimahe of Kami, Kamunagara. / Please permit me quickly.'

He expressed his hope through song. Hinodenowake-no-Kami answers him with a song:

Hinodenowake-no-Kami: 'The official of Tamakuniwake-kami, because your Kototama / is pure, you need not worry. / Yet, there are countless eighty lands, / and there is no way to do it with just one pillar. / O official of Tamakuniwake-kami, be mindful, / and go forth to the moon where Magatsu rages. / While revering Tsukiteruhiko-kami-no-Mikoto, / proceed through the night and into the day, my lord.'

Tamakuniwake stood up joyfully and answered again in song:

Tamakuniwake: 'The light of the Chihayafuru-kami appears, / and shall illuminate the jewel of Tamakuniwake. / By the light of Tsukiteruhiko-kami-no-Mikoto, / I think I shall cross the dark path in peace. / Iso-iso, I depart from the Iso mansion, / and I am happy and joyful as I advance. / I speak before the official Tōnowake, / please protect the person of my wife.'

Tōnowake answers this:

Tōnowake: 'If Isokohime remains pure and valiant, / you may go in peace even without word. / You are the beloved daughter of Susanoo-no-Kamison, / and the lord who is the Mizu-no-Mitama. / As long as this lord remains in this mansion, / the path shall prosper peacefully and joyfully. / Without leaving any heart of the village, / proceed quickly to the capital of Haruna in the land of India.'

Isokohime: 'I shall watch and guard the figure of my brave lord until it is hidden from sight. Without leaving behind a heart of attachment, proceed, my lord, according to the will of Kami.'

Tamakuniwake, while bowing to all present and singing the Sendenka, together with three attendants, crosses over Kajikatauge and traverses the Genya of the land of Fusa, intending to proceed toward Tsuki.

Kamehiko of Harukuniwake stands up and expresses his hope through a song.

Kamehiko: 'To Hinodenowake, who appears as the Oshihetsukasa of the Isoyakata where Kamususanōnōokami has hidden Himself, I speak. Though I am an official of the ugly ones who has followed the path of Uraru, I met you at the sea of Fusa, and having received the cultivation of your life, I became a person of sincerity. As a Senden-shi whose very name is auspicious, I rejoice and take courage, traveling across the lands of the four directions even to the self-revolving islands, and having subdued the ugly evil spirits, I have returned to this Isoyakata. Following the Sumeru-ōkami, my own spirit has achieved merit, yet you, without even looking my way, have promoted Umehiko and Otohiko, and entrusted them with the mission of admonishing Daikokunushi. Ah, how regrettable, how regrettable! I too am a child of Kami, of the palace of Kami; how could I be inferior to them? Please look upon me again, listen again, and rectify your view, Kamehiko. Send me to the land of Tsuki, and entrust me as a messenger of Kami to save and assist all the officials of the ugly ones who are possessed by Yamata-no-orochi, leading them to the great path of Sangokyō. I pray to you, having visited the presence of Kami, to let the divine power of my Kototama shine forth. Kannagara. Together with Kikukohime, I respectfully pray.'

He finished singing. Hinodenowake-no-mikoto, in response to this, says:

Hinodenowake: 'I find your brave declaration, Kamehiko of Harukuniwake, to be refreshing and reliable. Come then, you shall travel throughout the land of Tsuki and save it. O divine official of the land where the Kototama brings happiness, raise the shout of victory and return quickly.'

With this song, he permits the departure for the land of Tsuki. Kamehiko, emboldened, says:

Kamehiko: 'I shall proceed according to the words of the awesome and venerable Sumeru-kami. O Hinodewake-kami-no-mikoto, wait a while for my return. I speak before the official of Higashinowake. Please bestow your favor upon the hundred officials. I am now proceeding to the land of Tsuki; please guard Kikukohime. My wife, whom I leave behind, must also respectfully proclaim and convey the teachings of Kami.'

Higashinowake responds to this:

Higashinowake: 'Kamehiko, the fresh and brave warrior, shall surely have his name handed down for ten thousand generations. Will not the name of Kamehiko of Harukuniwake shine even through a thousand, eight thousand, and ten thousand generations? I shall look up to the achievements of Harukuniwake-kami-no-mikoto and wait in the skies of Karakuni.'

Kikukohime sings again.

Kikukohime: 'I, Kikukohime, shall wait for the brave and noble news, my neck long as that of a true crane. I shall not worry even for a single step of your journey; please proceed, my lord. Seeing you go forth, crossing mountains, traversing wildernesses, and drenched in rain, you are truly heroic.'

Hatsuwakahime stands up, opens her golden fan, and singing and dancing herself, celebrates the departure of the five divine officials.

Hatsuwakahime: 'In the Sangokyō teachings of the moon that shine limitlessly across the vast heavenly sky, the people of the four directions, the birds and beasts, and even the leaves of the grass are moistened by the dew of grace. Within the land of Ashihara-no-nakatsukuni, where all enjoy looking up at the venerable light of Kami, by what divine plan was the land of Tsuki left behind? In the seven thousand and more lands, the Kshatriya who appear as kings are deceived day and night by the ugly spirits of Yamata-no-orochi, and doing only evil things, the world is becoming a state of eternal darkness. At such a time, the divine official who serves the path of the Brahmins, Daikokunushi, has appeared. Making the capital of Haruna his base, while protecting the Brahmin tribe, he suppresses the Kshatriya tribe and wields violent power, which is truly deplorable. Consequently, the people of the three classes of Vaishya and Shudra are also suffering and writhing under the tyranny of the Brahmin tribe, and the land is filled with voices of resentment. Ah, Kannagara, Kami, Kami! By the grace of the divine spirit, Kamususanōnōokami has measured the time, and having manifested the Mizu-no-mitama, has planned to send the divine officials of Sangokyō to the capital of Haruna in the land of Tsuki to save the people of the world. How venerable is this plan! Ordered by the Kyōshu, Hinodewake, to perform this great work...'

Ōriyōhime and Mukadehime, starting with the noble officials, and the bright-hearted Terukuniwake, the Kami-ordained Tamakuniwake, and Harukuniwake—these three pillars, each entrusted with their duties, are sent forth for the sake of the Kami and for the sake of the world; how grateful I am. I, too, Hatsuwakahimenokami, though I am but young and inexperienced, shall emulate the pure works of the eight maidens sent forth by Kamususanōnōokami. I shall steal into the mansion of Ōkuronushi and persuade the Magakami to surrender to the path of truth, the Ananahi. Hinodewake and Azumanowake, before the presence of the Kami's officials, I pray with reverence and awe.

My father, Tokiokashi, you who are an official of the Kami, you must surely grant the wish of Hatsuwaka. If the Mikoto of the Kami blesses me and you send me forth, I shall endure any hardship. I shall explain and teach the Mikokoro of my Ōkami thoroughly and in detail. Waiting for the Jisetsu, I shall cause Ōkuronushi to Kijiyun to the path of truth, and I shall return to report before the presence of the Kami. Come, quickly, grant me this, Kannagara. I pray before the presence of the Kami.'

So she sang, and alone, she stole into the mansion of Ōkuronushi, petitioning to be allowed to depart on her mission to cause the Magakami to surrender. Hinodewake replied to this with a song:

'O Kototama of the brave Hatsuwakahime, every time I hear it, tears fall. Yet, Hatsuwakahime, you are an only child; how could I possibly send you to India?'

Hatsuwakahime replied to this:

'O Kototama of the mysterious Hinodewake, is my body not one dedicated to the Kami? My father, the Tokiokashi, must also cherish the sincere words of Hatsuwaka and grant my request.'

Tokiokashi-kami replied with a song:

'My daughter, who was born of the spirits of heaven and earth, serve according to the will of the Kami. The office of Tokiokashi-kami is but one; I think of you, who are departing, with great courage.'

Hinodewake-mikoto sang again:

'The Kami must surely be rejoicing at the spirit of this brave father and daughter. Come then, Hatsuwakahime, official of the Kami, proceed according to the will of the Kami.'

Azumanowake stood up and sang:

'Seeing the figure of the young Hatsuwakahime, I am moved to tears. Yet, since you are an official protected by the noble Kami, do not let your heart be troubled. Come, depart quickly according to the Mikoto, and return to report before the presence of the Kami.'

Hatsuwakahime sang again, looking joyful:

'Hinodewake, Azumanowake, and my father, the root of my life, your words have given me strength. While receiving the grateful blessings of the Kami, I shall proceed to the land of India.'

Having sung thus, she bade farewell to all, and like a drifting cloud, alone and without any attendants, she crossed the mountains and rivers of ten thousand leagues, proceeding toward the land of India.

Through the divine administration of the spirit world, the Kami official to be ordered to depart to persuade Ōkuronushi, who had appeared from the beginning in the capital of Haruna in the land of India, had already been largely decided. However, Kamususanōnōokami, considering that the husbands of his own daughters were among the three pillars, could not bring himself to speak of it openly; he ordered Hinodewake-kami to hold a consultation and proceed with the formalities of serving this mission as he saw fit. Furthermore, Hatsuwakahime had been moved by the noble and pure Mikokoro of Kamususanōnōokami, who had sent all eight of his maidens to the enemy's stronghold without leaving a single one behind, and she had been waiting for the time to come when she could engage in a divine service just as the eight maidens had devoted themselves. Even Mokusuke, the Tokiokashi-kami, was moved by the brave heart of Hatsuwakahime, and though she was his own child, he secretly choked back tears of gratitude, thinking what a magnificent person she was.

In this way, the preparations for the Kototama battle against Ōkuronushi in the land of India were completely finalized. Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haese.

黄竜姫や蜈蚣姫 尊き司を初めとし 心も明き照国別 神の命や玉国別 治国別の三柱を おのもおのもにこと任けて 神の御為世の為に 遣はし玉ふぞ有難き われも初稚姫神 年端も行かぬ身なれ共 神素盞嗚大神の 遣はし玉ひし八乙女が 清き御業に神傚ひ 大黒主の館まで 忍び参りて三五の 誠の道に曲神を 言向和させ玉へかし 日出別や東野別 神の司の御前に 謹み敬ひ祈ぎまつる

父とあれます時置師 神の司は初稚が 願を必ず許すべし 神の御言の幸はひて われを遣はし玉ひなば 如何なる悩みも堪へ忍び わが大神の御心を うまらにつばらに説きさとし 時節を待つて大黒主を 誠の道に帰順させ 神の御前に復言 申し奉らむいざ早く 許させ玉へ惟神 神の御前に祈ぎまつる』

と歌つて、自分も単独にて、大黒主の館に忍び込み、曲神を帰順せしむべく、出陣を許されむ事を請願した。日出別は歌を以てこれに答ふ。

『勇ましき初稚姫の言霊よ 聞く度毎に涙こぼるる。 さり乍ら初稚姫は独り御子 いかでか印度に遣はすを得む』

初稚姫はこれに答へて、

『いぶかしき日出別の言霊よ 神に捧げし吾身ならずや。 時置師父の命も初稚が 誠の言葉を愛で許しませ』

時置師神は歌もて答ふ。

『天地の御霊にあれし吾娘 神のまにまに仕へまつれよ。 時置師神の司は只一人 いでゆく汝を雄々しくぞ思ふ』

日出別命は又歌ふ。

『勇ましき親子二人の心根を 神は喜び玉ふなるらむ。 いざさらば初稚姫の神司 神のまにまに進み行きませ』

東野別は立上りて歌ふ。

『年若き初稚姫の御姿を 見るにつけても涙ぐまれつ。 さり乍ら尊き神の守ります 司にませば心痛めず。 いざ早く御言のままに出でまして 神の御前に復言せよ』

初稚姫は嬉しげに又歌ふ。

『日出別東野別や垂乳根の 父の言葉は妾を生かせり。 有難き神の恵を受け乍ら 印度の御国へ吾が進みゆかむ』

かく歌ひて、一同に別れを告げ、瓢然として只一人供人をもつれず、万里の山河を越えて、印度の国へ進み行く。

神界の御経綸にて最初より印度の国のハルナの都に現はれたる大黒主を言向和す為出張を命ぜらるべき神司は、略決定されてゐたのである。併し乍ら神素盞嗚大神は吾娘の夫が三柱迄も加はり居る事とて、明さまに言ひ出でかね玉ひ、日出別神に命じて、相談会を開かせ、随意に此使に奉仕する事の手続をとられたのであつた。又初稚姫は神素盞嗚大神が八人の乙女を一柱も残さず、敵の牙城に使はし玉ひし尊き清き大御心に感激し、如何にもして、八人乙女の尽し玉ひたる如き神務に従事せばやと、時の至るを待ちつつあつたのである。時置師神の杢助も、初稚姫の健気なる心に感じ、吾子乍らも天晴れなる者よと、ひそかに感涙に咽んでゐた。

斯くの如くにして、愈印度の国の大黒主に対する言霊戦の準備は全く整うたのである。あゝ惟神霊幸倍坐世。
