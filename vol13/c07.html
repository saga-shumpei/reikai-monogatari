Chapter 7: The Plain of Furu [533]

第7章　布留野原〔533〕

§ 1

'Through the Kototama of the True God, Hinodewake parts the clouds and mist! An emissary of the precious divine decree, Departing from Mount Tenkyō, Crossing the distant ocean waste, Arriving at last here upon the Sea of Fusa, Landing at the port of Taru; Resting at ease in the shade of Shizu Grove, Voices of men were heard in the dark; Waking from slumber, 'twas the emissaries Serving beneath Iwahiko: One, two, three, four, five, and six! Though bonded in intimate fellowship, Stormy billows arose at times, Fighting amidst murky night where black and white were indistinguishable; While they struggled, Hinodewake Disclosed the true path of God, And at last all in Shizu Grove, Softening the rock-hard heart—'

'Of Iwahiko, hard as a boulder, Submitting to the divine teachings Spreading fragrant as plum blossoms all at once; Advancing unto the renowned Plain of Furu, Pacifying the obstructing demons with Kototama, To establish an immovable divine age Lasting as long as the age of the tortoise! Spurring on the steeds of their hearts, Valiant as the rushing swoop of a hawk Descending upon a flock of sparrows, Across the vast moor overgrown with grass, Keeping the great Taru River on their right, Northward and northward they march!'

The party of seven strode deep into the Plain of Furu at their feet. Once more the curtain of pitch-black darkness descended, wrapping all four directions in gloomy desolation. The northeast wind whistled as it swept over the grassy plain, accompanied even by drizzling rain.

Takahiko: 'Last night in the Grove of Shizu we indulged in a wild spree, but through the mediation of the emissary Hinodewake we beheld the dawn of restored peace; and today brings a cosmic upheaval—clumsy Ural emissaries who regarded the Anai teachings as bitter foes are now seven companions walking together in the Anai faith! How quickly things change! Come, tonight let us take the grass of Furu Plain as our bedding and dream dreams of peace! Honored emissary Hinodewake, this Furu Plain plays tricks of raining one moment and clearing the next, so sound sleep may be difficult; shall we spend the night wearing our straw hats and raincoats?'

Hinodewake: 'Ah, that will be fine! First let us stretch our legs and sleep soundly tonight.'

Iwahiko: 'How grateful! However, this Furu Plain is a den of demons where monsters and shape-shifters constantly appear, so we may hardly enjoy peaceful slumber. Yet to rest our compasses—our legs—shall we lie down and have a staring match with the black clouds in the sky?'

Umehiko: 'Ah, exhausted! Walking along the banks of the Taru River, parasitic mud clung to our waraji so thickly that each step felt like ten! Nothing is more delightful than resting when weary. As the proverb says, "Send the beloved child on a journey"; truly, nothing is more agonizing than slogging along a dusty, storm-blown road without destination. The joy of stretching one's legs to soothe that fatigue makes a bit of light rain no trouble at all!'

Kamehiko: 'I too was rocked aboard the boat from Hitoshima so long that the spirit of my legs seems to have emigrated elsewhere; they feel like someone else's legs! Let us take a good rest here tonight.'

Hinodewake: 'Come, everyone, let us rest!'

Saying thus, Hinodewake lay down upon the weeds, his snoring rumbling like thunder as he instantly wandered into the land of dreams (Kasho).

Takahiko: 'Ah, what an innocent, fearless emissary! Placing us beside him who opposed him with all our might until yesterday, without the slightest anxiety or second thought, he takes the lead in falling fast asleep! His boundless magnanimity leaves us in speechless awe. Humans ought to be like this: when one harbors suspicion, jealousy, or doubt, one constantly worries about others and can never find peace. As they say, "Suspicion creates demons in the dark"; man tortures himself with his own thoughts! In the Ural faith, even when six men traveled together, fearing sudden nighttime attacks, they would post sentinels by turns. Compared to that, the Anai teachings are truly unattached and pure—a doctrine of universal love! You fellows had better sleep in peace as well.'

Komahiko: 'Indeed, yesterday's foe is today's friend! What sounded like the whisperings of demons and the roar of tigers has turned into the wind through the pines! Come, let all of us pull the corks from our hearts, share our karma, and lie side by side to perish... oops, to sleep together!'

Iwahiko: 'Go ahead and sleep, all of you; I, Iwahiko, must undertake the duty of guarding the emissary Hinodewake. For this is my true sincerity...'

Takahiko: 'True sincerity? Rather a pure red lie! The "ma" of sincerity (makokoro) must be the "ma" of demon (akuma)! You are still uneasy, suspicious that Hinodewake might seize your throat while you sleep, so you keep watch!'

Iwahiko: 'Nay, by no means! They say many giant serpents dwell in this Furu Plain. Sniffing human scent, who knows when they may arrive? Therefore I must stand guard!'

Takahiko: 'No need for such anxiety! You had far better go to sleep quickly!'

Umehiko: 'Hey, hey! Disaster, disaster!'

Takahiko: 'What is the disaster?'

Umehiko: 'The emissary Hinodewake is nowhere to be seen!'

Crying 'Ah!' at these words, they looked all around, but there was neither shadow nor sign of him.

Iwahiko: 'Look at that! As I told you, Furu Plain is a den of demons! A giant serpent probably used ninja arts and swallowed down the emissary Hinodewake without making a sound!'

Takahiko: 'Nonsense, how could such a thing be? He is an incarnation of the Divine, appearing and vanishing at will! Unlike lumps of sin like us, his spirit is so purified that he has probably become transparent to our sight!'

At that moment, an indescribably bloody, moist, and sultry breeze swept past them.

Otohiko: 'Ah, what wind is this? An eerie, sinister air—truly the trademark of Furu Plain!'

Takahiko: 'Overcome by terror, you are trembling all over—truly an encampment on "Buru-buru-buru-no-hara" (Shivering Plain)! Ahahaha! The coward's wind has begun to blow!'

Otohiko: 'Because that demon blows a fiendish wind from over yonder, we match him with the Kototama of numbers: One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, hundred, thousand, ten thousand, a hundred million coward's winds!'

Takahiko: 'What wordplay is this? Look, it rains again!'

Otohiko: 'Dwelling beneath the canopy of heaven, if we feared rain, how could we abide in this world? Far more terrifying than rain is Lord Uralhiko of Armenia! If it reaches his ears that we somersaulted in mid-air (switched allegiance) during our flight of propagation, disaster will strike! Takahiko: 'Are you still yearning for Armenia?'

Otohiko: 'Nay, I do not yearn for Armenia! But because the Lord Hinodewake on whom we placed all our reliance has vanished into mist, my heart has grown faint! Men of one to (all of us), let us search for his whereabouts one koku (ten times) faster! How should we deal with an emissary whose transformations and vanishings grow ever more unfathomable?'

At that moment, from amidst the dim, overgrown reeds, a colossal, soot-black face thrust itself forward:

Monster: 'Kie-hehehe! Kya-hahaha! Kyo-hohoho! Kyuuuu-!'

音彦『やや、怪奇なことがあったものだ。今出てきたお化けは、どうやら雪隠（便所）のお化けと見える。糞を垂れる最後の結末には、鼬の最後っ屁じゃないが、「ブスッ」と音をさせて屁を垂れおった』

岩彦『ワハハハ、妙なものだ。俺の腰ももう大丈夫だ。おいどうだ貴様、俺が腰を抜かしたと本当に思っていたのだろう。そんな弱いことで宣伝使が務まるかい。キャハハハ、キュフフフ、キョホホホ』

鷹彦『おいおい岩公、そんな言霊を使うと、第二の妖怪変化のご襲来だぞ』

岩彦『妖怪も神界もあったものかい。吾輩の勢力範囲内に立ち入って、何を容喙（ようかい）するのじゃ、権利侵害罪で起訴するぞ』

鷹彦『起訴するとは奇想天外だ。天涯万里の雨が降るフル野原、どうせ大した事はないから、まあまあ楽しんで、ゆっくりと夜を日に継いで旅行するのだな』

この時、何処からともなく、化物の声にて、

『神が表に現はれて 善と悪とを立別ける 化物此処に現はれて 嘘と糞とを立別ける 嘘で固めた岩公の 岩より固い頑固者 荒肝取られて腰打つて 骨を一々刻まれた 様な苦しい思ひして 涙をソツと押隠し 泰平楽の減らず口 此行先の荒屋に 又もや俺が待受けて どつと脂を搾つてやろか アハヽヽヽ、オホヽヽヽ、イヒヽヽヽ』

岩彦『ええい五月蠅い、またしてもまたしても。しかし今度の笑い方は正式だ。先刻のような「キューキューキュー」と言われると気味が悪い……おい化ちゃん、笑うなら今の流儀だよ』

再び中空より、化物の声、

化物『岩に松さえ生えるじゃないか、食って食われぬことはない。アハハハ、ウフフフ、マハハハ、イヒヒヒ』

鷹彦『おい岩公、喜べ、あの笑い声は何だと思う。「アー・ウ・マ・イ（美味い／上手い）」と笑っただろう、上手いことをぬかしおるなあ』

岩彦『ああ、うん……うん、まあまあ、いい、いいわい』
