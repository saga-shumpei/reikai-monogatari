Chapter 22: Pilgrimage to the Caucasus [548]

第22章　高加索詣〔548〕

§ 1

The hills on all four sides were verdant green; amid the green of young shoots fluttering like the sleeves of the Spring Maiden, the skies were fragrant with crimson blossoms. In the midst of the men and women thronging in an endless stream to revere the Living God Hinode-wake who had manifested upon Mount Caucasus, two singularly dark and hulking giants came marching along, chanting fragments of the sacred propagation hymns in broken snatches, and sitting down upon the wayside grass, fell into idle banter.

'Hey, Yotahiko, what thinkest thou? For so long a season there was neither light of sun nor moon, the grasses and trees were withered like dead leaves, and there was not the slightest sense of spring; yet since the Living God Hinode-wake manifested upon Mount Caucasus, the gold-rimmed Sun-Deity shineth brilliantly, the trees and herbs sprout tender shoots, flowers bloom, songbirds sing, the weather is fair, and indeed it seemeth as though hell hath turned into paradise in the twinkling of an eye!'

'Indeed it is so; I would fain worship at the sanctuary of Mount Caucasus as swiftly as possible! Yet with such a vast multitude of men and women, old and young, thronging together like beads upon a rosary, we cannot even stretch our legs and take a proper rest. If we loiter about, we shall surely be trampled underfoot. Tell me, friend Yaji, where shall we find lodging for the night?'

'Well, though it be spring, the nights are still biting cold, so sleeping in the open field is out of the question. Two leagues hence lieth a tiny hamlet named Tago, where should stand the dwelling of Otake, a maidservant who served long in mine abode; how would it be if we stretched our compass thither and begged a lodging for the night?'

'Hmm, the dwelling of Mistress Otake? That soundeth well enough. Yet wilt she grant us shelter? With such a multitude of pilgrims, she must have countless acquaintances; might not her roof be packed full to bursting ere we arrive?'

'There lies the bond between master and servant! No matter how unfeeling Otake might be, she could scarcely refuse a master who fed and kept her for ten long years. Come, let us make for Otake's house at all events. Ah, what an immense swarm of pilgrims!'

So conversing, the pair began to drag their weary, aching legs along. As the sun sank behind the mountain crests, they arrived at last in the hamlet of Tago.

'They say 'tis in this town. Since it is said to be no grand mansion, do thou search the right side, and I shall search along the left.'

§ 2

Yotahiko: 'Is there any shop-name or crest to know it by?'

Yajihiko: 'Thou knowest Otake's visage, dost thou not? We must seek her out by her face!'

Yotahiko: 'Even Otake would not stand out in the sunlight like a signboard from morn till eve; searching with so uncertain a target is utter folly! Rather let us knock at every single eaves on both sides of the road, asking "Is this the house of Mistress Otake, or is this?"—that were far the surest way!'

Yajihiko: 'Yea, that is well said! Behold, here is the hamlet of Tago. Take thou the right side, and I shall take the left.... Hello there! Might this be the honorable dwelling of Mistress Otake?'

From inside the house:

'Nay, 'tis not!'

Yajihiko: 'Could this not perchance be the residence of Mistress Otake?'

From inside the house:

'This is indeed the house of an Otake, but our Otake died fifteen years ago!'

'What! She was in my service ten years ago; to say she died fifteen years ago must surely be an error in thy reckoning! I speak of Otake who served in the house of Yajihiko of Takatori Village!'

From inside the house:

'We have never sent our daughter out to service!'

'Then 'tis the wrong Otake—a trouble to both of us! Bah, no help for it; struck out from the very start! Small though this hamlet be, there are many dwellings; if we inquire from door to door, we shall be taken for beggars and lose all dignity. Let us cast our net by skipping two or three houses at each stride!'

The two walked on, calling out here and there on either side of the road in search of Otake's abode.

From beneath the eaves of a tiny, ragged hovel, a voice called:

'Excuse me, sir! Might you not be the master from Takatori Village?'

'Aha, art thou Otake? Good heavens, what an ancient crone thou hast become! When thou didst abide in my house, thou wert a right handsome wench with peeling rustic skin; yet ten years make an era, and now thy wrinkles have multiplied to two-and-thirty! Gazing upon thy two-and-thirty years of wrinkles, one feeleth no desire to take on years; yet there is one thing I would fain take!'

'Oh-ho-ho-ho! Dear master, what is this thing you would fain take?'

'I would fain take lodging for the night! Wilt thou not grant us shelter beneath thy roof?'

'Heaven forbid! This is no house fit for a master to lodge in! It is so squalid and shameful that I pray you forgive me, but ask not for that!'

§ 3

'If so, wilt thou not guide us to some inn?'

'With such a multitude of pilgrims to the Caucasus, every house far and near is packed tight as sushi in a tub! Even in a miserable shanty like mine, strangers from who knows where have begged to lodge under the eaves, until the place is so crowded one cannot even stir!'

'Any place will do; is there no corner vacant? Were there a hammock, even the space under the rafters would suffice, so long as it shieldeth us from rain and dew!'

'How could so fine a thing be found in a destitute hovel like ours? The only open space we have is a brushwood loft choked with soot!'

'The brushwood loft will do admirably! Yet methinks it must be dreadful smoky.'

'Since we cook rice and boil tea down below, the rafters are thoroughly smoked indeed. If you sleep in such a place, dear master, people will mistake you for a smoked badger! Oh-ho-ho-ho!'

'Badger or fox, I care not a whit, so long as we pass the night! Aha, our lodging for tonight is settled! Hey, Yotahiko, here, here! The inn is decided! Where on earth hath he wandered off to, the rattlebrain? Lost in the crowd, he seemeth not to have noticed my bargain. Pray, Otake, call my comrade Yotahiko hither!'

'Since this hamlet is so tiny, when he reacheth the end of the road where the houses cease, he will surely retrace his steps along this side; so pray take a cup of coarse tea and wait in comfort.'

'Aha, Yota, what luck? Behold, this is the grand palace of Lady Otake! Here we lodge tonight!'

Yotahiko: 'Ho! What a wretched... nay, what a splendid mansion! Greetings, Mistress Otake; dost thou recognize my countenance? Ten years ago, when I sneaked in through the cattle shed, thou didst strike me with a lusty elbow blow—dost remember?'

'Oh-ho-ho-ho! People are listening; speak not of such disgraceful things! Pray rest yourselves leisurely around the back, until I clear the brushwood loft and prepare your bed!'

'Ah, as thou saidst, an amazing crowd of guests for so humble a cottage! And behold, is this thy mother, and an old grandfather with a shining bald pate? Splendid, splendid; to be hale and hearty in old age is the greatest blessing of all!'

§ 4

'Dear master, pray step this way and rest in the back!'

'Very good! Hey, Yotahiko, the front is a squalid shanty, but around the back stands a truly magnificent mansion! Come, let us take our ease here. Mmm! Look at the alcove ornaments, the carved transoms, the alcove shelves and writing desk—everything is many times finer than the guest room in my own house! That Otake must have amassed a monstrous fortune! Fearing heavy taxes if she displayed a grand facade, she put up a mean hovel in front, while keeping an elegant pavilion in the rear—a cunning rogue she is! 『ヤア怪体な事が有るものぢや、土瓶の奴俺の頭を蹴りよつて逸早く天上して仕舞つた。化物だらうかなア』

『爺さまの薬鑵頭を土瓶が蹴るのは当り前だよ。たとへ土瓶が天上位したつて何も不思議ぢやない。今朝も婆々が見て居れば門前を葱の浄瑠璃語りや、大根の役者が通つた。土瓶の天上位は別に不思議な事は無いわいなア』

又もや土瓶は柴屋からツルツルと下りて来た。

お竹『ヤア旦那様が大変喉を乾かして御座ると見える、充分入れて上げて下されな』

『ヨシヨシ』

下では禿頭の爺と婆、その他お竹の兄弟五六人が、大勢の客のために握り飯を一生懸命に拵えている。

弥次彦は鈎に土瓶を引っ掛け、ツルツルと下ろした途端に、禿頭の爺の額のあたりにコツンと当たった。弥次彦は吃驚して土瓶の綱をにわかに手繰り上げる。

『やや、怪奇な事があるものじゃ、土瓶の奴が俺の頭を蹴りおって素早く天上に登ってしまった。化け物だろうかなあ』

『爺さまの薬罐頭を土瓶が蹴るのは当たり前だよ。たとえ土瓶が天上に昇ったって何も不思議じゃない。今朝も婆が見ていれば門前を葱の浄瑠璃語りや大根の役者が通った。土瓶の天上に昇るくらいは別に不思議な事はないわいなあ』

またもや土瓶は柴屋からツルツルと下りて来た。

お竹『やあ旦那様が大変喉を渇かしていらっしゃると見える、十分注いであげて下されな』

『よしよし』
