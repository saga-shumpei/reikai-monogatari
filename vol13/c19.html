Chapter 19: The Feast in the Cavern [545]

第19章　馳走の幕〔545〕

§ 1

Guided by Izumo-hime, Otohiko, Kamehiko, and Komahiko quickened their pace and at last arrived at the foot of the Koshi Pass in the Takao mountain range. Here lay several colossal and flat stone platforms, while on either flank of the pass grew towering, ancient green pines of thousand-year antiquity, presenting a landscape of surpassing sublimity.

Otohiko: 'Aha! What an exquisitely scenic realm! Naught save pine and rock, like unto the very pristine dawn of the Age of the Gods! How sayest thou, friend Koma? Doth not thy spirit feel wondrously refreshed?'

Komahiko: ''Tis true! The union of pine and rock is divine indeed! The primordial Age of the Gods must surely have resembled such a place as this!'

Kamehiko: 'Hold, look upon yonder great rock! Someone is reclining at full length!'

Otohiko: 'Who can it be, slumbering in idle ease in such a spot?'

Izumo-hime: ''Tis Lord Hinode-wake no Mikoto! Wearied by the arduous exploration, he is taking a brief rest!'

Otohiko: 'Lo, 'tis the divine Lord Hinode-wake! Where then are Takahiko, Iwahiko, and Umehiko?'

Izumo-hime: 'They too are resting beneath the shadow of yonder pines!'

Otohiko: 'Aha, all are safe! When we were shaken loose and tumbled from the Heavenly Bird-Ship, we feared all was lost; yet that the whole company should be reunited thus is a blessing exceeding profound!'

Kamehiko: 'Verily! 'Tis as though encountering the Buddha in the depths of hell!'

Otohiko: ''Tis even as the sacred poem: "Upon the firm rocks and pines of the Age of the Gods is established / The divine protection of the rising sun!"'

Komahiko: 'Behold Lord Hinode-wake reclining upon the stone! 'Tis as though the eternal age of the pine were already established!'

Iwahiko: 'Ho! If it be not Oto, Kame, and Koma! How wondrous that ye arrived with whole skins!'

Takahiko: 'We were anxious when ye fell from the Bird-Ship, but 'tis a joy to see ye safe!'

Umehiko: 'Look at Master Oto's pale visage! Wast thou nearly swallowed by a frog in the ancient pond?'

Otohiko: 'What babblest thou? Master Oto enjoyed a leisurely excursion mounted upon a toad's back! 'Tis rather ye who were trembling within the Bird-Ship!'

Iwahiko: 'Cease thy boastful chatter! We soared through the heavens with Lord Hinode-wake and beheld the profound mysteries of the divine realm!'

Kamehiko: 'Ho, what exalted dignity! Whilst we were drinking muddy water on earth, enduring a march of bitter toil!'

Otohiko: 'High sound, high sound! While Lord Hinode-wake is resting his weary limbs from days of toil, 'twere unseemly to waken him! Make no sound (oto nashi), and compose yourselves in peace!'

Komahiko: 'Prithee keep the peace (oto nashi), and let this matter be settled amicably! Yet as for Lord Hinode-wake, he receiveth special reverence; but shall we not play some trick upon fellow Iwa?'

Iwahiko: 'Oto who leaped into the pond and was stripped of his pride, and Koma whose soul was crushed by the goblin balls—and Kame, have ye still not repented?'

Otohiko: 'What meanest thou by repentance? We have been thoroughly reformed from the start! 'Tis rather thou, Master Iwa, who wert smitten by a damsel and imprisoned in a stone cell; boast not so proudly!'

Iwahiko: 'What insolence! I am the peerless, valiant apostle of the Anai teachings! How could I ever be bewitched by a woman?'

Takahiko: 'Look, their brawl hath begun again! If ye must quarrel, leave the presence of the Deity and go fight in yonder ravine!'

Umehiko: 'Verily! 'Tis a crying shame to spoil so sublime a sacred atmosphere!'

Kamehiko: 'Yet Master Iwa, that we reached this place was due to the gracious guidance of Izumo-hime! We ought to offer thanks rather than spout abuse!'

Iwahiko: 'Humph! Izumo-hime or what not, this enchanted mountain swarmeth with treacherous goblins; one can never let down one's guard!'

Otohiko: 'Treating folk as monsters again! Thine own eyes are mere knot-holes!'

Iwahiko: 'Whether knot-holes or no, I will show thee hereafter!'

Otohiko: ''Tis a pun upon the firm eye (katame) of faith, firm as a rock!'

Kamehiko: 'An utterly bewildering affair! Shall I search around to see if an eye can be found? But ere then, I raise an urgent motion!'

Komahiko: 'What is thine urgent motion?'

Kamehiko: 'The deity Hokkō-shin had his eye pierced in a state of wakefulness! Unless it be pierced while sitting erect upon the rock expounding the doctrines of the Anai teachings, it profiteth naught!'

Otohiko: ''Tis true! Let us waken him!'

So saying, he pulled vigorously at Master Iwa's legs.

Iwahiko: 'Wh-wh-who is pulling at my legs? Doubtless 'tis Oto! Shall I make thee one-eyed like Hokkō-shin, eh Oto? Zzz, zzz...'

Otohiko: 'Lo, what an uncanny fellow! He is in a state half asleep and half awake! Falling under self-hypnosis, he appeareth to suffer hallucinations!'

Takahiko: 'Ha-ha-ha-ha!'

Otohiko: ''Tis no laughing matter! Unless we waken him swiftly, we too shall be dragged into the spell!'

§ 3

Umehiko: 'U-fu-fu-fu!'

Kamehiko: 'Lo, all have awakened into full consciousness at once!'

Takahiko: 'Hey, ye three, where have ye been blundering? What is the matter with your visages, covered all over with cobwebs? Looking as though ye have not washed your faces for ages—what a wretched spectacle!'

Otohiko: 'All groweth so vague and shadowy that I cannot comprehend!'

Takahiko: 'Dragged into a badger's hole, and now babbling sleep-talk? Where under heaven dost thou imagine this place to be?'

Otohiko: 'Where? I imagine 'tis right here!'

Takahiko: 'That 'tis here is certain; what is the name of the place (chimei)?'

Otohiko: 'Distinguished persons of fame (chimei no shi) are Master Taka, Master Ume, Master Iwa, and the apostle Hinode-wake!'

Takahiko: 'Fool! I ask what is the name of this spot (tokoro)?'

Otohiko: 'The name of a spot is assuredly a spot!'

Takahiko: 'What hour dost thou deem it to be?'

Otohiko: ''Tis a matter of course: 'tis later than this very hour yesterday by just the time it took to speak!'

Takahiko: 'Hey, Master Iwa, arise, awake! These three fellows were bewitched by a great badger, covered in cobwebs, spouting words as though under deep hypnosis! There is no prospect of their waking easily from so profound a trance; wherefore if I join with thee and gouge out one of their eyes, perchance they will come to their senses!'

Iwahiko: 'A capital idea! Those blind knaves were holding an assembly upon the rock, plotting to pluck out mine own eyeballs! Hey, Oto, Kame, Koma—bring forth your eyes (me)!'

Otohiko: 'In early spring, all the herbs and trees put forth myriad buds (me)! My head feeleth delightfully warm! Ha-ha!'

Takahiko stepped before the three, and blowing 'Poo, poo, poo!', sprayed water upon their faces. Startled at last, the three cried aloud:

All three: 'Lo, where is this? Is it not the interior of the cavern?'

And they looked around in bewilderment. The faint strains of the biwa lute echoed, and from the inner chamber, smiling gently and holding a lute, appeared a damsel the very image of the beauty who had emerged from the luminous orb. When the three looked about them, neither Hinode-wake nor the other three companions, nor pine nor rock was anywhere to be seen; 'twas the stone-paved mansion of the goddess deep within the cavern as before.

Otohiko: 'Whether dream or waking, I can comprehend naught! Verily, this is the peculiar wonder of the Cavern of Demons!'

Staring with wide eyes and tilted heads, struck with utter bewilderment, folding their arms in breathless despair, they kept dead silence. The beauty bearing the lute, gracefully clad in robes of state, quietly took her seat before the three, saying:

と今までの野卑な言葉を改め、心より清々として、三人は岩窟の探険に出かける事となりける。
