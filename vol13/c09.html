Chapter 9: The Fire-Rat [535]

第9章　火の鼠〔535〕

§ 1

The sun appeared to have sunk behind the western mountains, casting an even deeper gloom over the already dreary cosmos; the sound of the wind sweeping across the wilderness grew fiercer with each passing moment.

Otohiko: 'Hey, Iwahiko! Twilight has arrived at last! 'Tis the appointed hour! Where has the divine Lord Hinodewake gone? Surely he hasn't vanished into thin air again?'

Iwahiko: 'Do not utter rash words! He is a deity; he could never break his promise! Let us gather before the cavern and wait.'

Umehiko: 'Yet the wind grows ever colder, piercing to the bone.'

Kamehiko: 'Hey, will not something crawl forth from the dark? I feel an uneasy pounding in my chest that will not cease.'

Takahiko: 'Hey, Otohiko, do not become so feverish already! If I had some quinine, I would administer a dose, but unluckily I have none with me, so it cannot be helped!'

Otohiko: 'Who is feverish? An emissary beneath heaven of the Ural—nay, of the Anai teachings—could hardly flinch before a mere cold wind like this!'

Komahiko: 'Though your mouth boasts so, your knees are knocking together, are they not?'

Otohiko: 'This is the trembling of a warrior's martial ardour! When the moment arrives, I shall charge first into the cavern and snap the necks of the demons!'

Iwahiko: 'Hey, hey! Let us behold your skill first before you talk! Your bluster is a joke: wild and frolicsome in the morning, but before noon whining that your machine has run out of oil and you cannot march another step! Having such a weakling as a companion is no easy trial for the rest of us! Shamelessly spouting grand boasts like a volcano scorching heaven, yet spewing ill-omened panic all around—ah, the height of indignation, an utter absurdity!'

Takahiko: 'Hey, this is no time for silly chatter! Look yonder, through the reeds—the fellow from last night is...!'

Iwahiko: 'Ah, he has appeared again! This time that demon has shifted his position, assaulting us right up close! Hey, Mr. Monster, today's Iwahiko is quite different from yesterday's! Look well at this Cavern of Demons: my fists are ready! If you dawdle and play tricks, I shall have no choice but to take direct physical action, so prepare yourself!'

Monster: 'Ahahaha! 'Tis me, 'tis me! I merely disguised myself to test Iwahiko's courage!'

Iwahiko: 'Whom on earth might you be?'

Monster: ''Tis Komahiko (steed) who confounds men! Can you truly see my face in this darkness?'

Iwahiko: 'Hold on! Neither seeing nor not seeing—strange, eerie sounds tinged with ruinous sorrow reflect upon my ears! My ears are remarkably handy: I see with my ears and hear with my eyes, making me an emissary who outmatches even monsters! Do not play foolish tricks and end up weeping later! Crawling into the grass like a locust or grasshopper, leaping here and bounding there—that is why you are called a frivolous upstart! Pulling off stunts like a cheap showman to intimidate Iwahiko of peerless courage—that trick will never work on me!'

Komahiko: 'Ah, setting out to explore this cavern, who knows what fiends may emerge, so I merely conducted a little monster rehearsal! Please favor me with your continued patronage in the future as I continue my rehearsals!'

Iwahiko: 'A joke? Facing a battlefield, why speak with such careless levity? In such a state, you are certain to soil yourself along the way! Your failure is already officially registered in King Yama's registry, with revenue stamps duly affixed!'

Komahiko: 'Hey, what are you babbling about? "Registered"? "Registry"? Such terms are the jargon of twentieth-century mortals after the birth of Christ! Right now is five hundred thousand years before the Christian era!'

Iwahiko: 'This is the narrative of the Spirit World (Reikai Monogatari) transcending past, present, and future—of course such words apply! How could petty minds bound by small eras or vocabulary understand the infinite, absolute, beginningless, and endless divine plan of the gods? I am a new emissary of the Anai teachings whose learning surpasses ancient and modern times, whose knowledge pierces East and West! You too had better breathe the air of civilization!'

Komahiko: 'What nonsense does he chirp so eloquently! In this primitive age today, talking of "civilization"—even my rear is dumbfounded!'

Iwahiko: 'Do you not know the concept of a reverse voyage of civilization? Just wait and see: when an uncivilized world called the twentieth century emerges where mortals on earth have guts the size of beans, some strange fellow will surely appear somewhere and dictate our present deeds as bedtime tales! And then humans of that age will say, "History repeats itself," arguing whether this is uncivilized, untrue, or logical nonsense! You had far better keep silent and await the passage of time!'

§ 3

Once more the whole sky darkened suddenly, wrapped in swirling vortices of clouds and a tempest of rain driving like a bristling forest of spears.

Otohiko: 'Ah! Rain pours down in heaps like America and France! If that rat does not hurry and show us the cavern, we shall become drowned rats first!'

Komahiko: 'What posture is that? Cowering like a rat chased by a cat—what an unsightly, pitiful sight!'

Takahiko: 'Hey, everyone, chant the Kototama! When left idle, you start frolicking with unbearable clamor! In place of a gag order, I strictly command you to chant Norito and sing sacred hymns without ceasing!'

Iwahiko: 'Endless insolence when you are allowed to speak! Is there so much as a single rat between heaven and earth that would heed your commands? If you raise your steam too high, your boiler will burst!'

At that moment, Hinodewake-no-Mikoto suddenly reappeared atop the crags.

All: ''Tis the descent of the Divine! The manifestation of the living God! How grateful, how blessed!'

Iwahiko: 'Honored Lord Hinodewake, please exert your good offices so that the Fire-Rat may make its appearance swiftly!'

§ 4

Hinodewake: 'Very well, now is the moment!'

Saying thus, Hinodewake-no-Mikoto hewed down the shrubs growing thickly upon the rock, stripping trunk and branches, unbound the cord at his waist to fashion a bow, cut the reed stalks to make arrows, and loosed a swift arrow flying straight toward the rocky ridge extending east and west.

Hinodewake: 'Behold, the sky has grown red indeed! Go now and retrieve the arrow I have just shot forth! In doing so, the entrance will become clearly revealed!'

Takahiko: 'This is no ancient tale from the past, but an event of the future—like Lord Ōkuninushi going to fetch an arrow in the burning moor a hundred thousand years hence! We shall gladly go to retrieve it, but do not set fire to the moor to scorch us as was done to him!'

Hinodewake: 'Go and search according to my command!'

Iwahiko: 'Come, let us form the search party for the stray arrow! Since he is called Hinodewake (One who brings forth the sun/fire), he will undoubtedly bring forth fire to burn the thicket; and then, as the ancient rhyme goes, "The inside is hollow, the outside is smoking," the Master Rat will come running to our aid—such is the design! Forward, the whole brigade: One, two, three!'

They dashed into the murky darkness.

Hinodewake immediately drew forth his flint stones and struck fire into the roaring gale: instantly, with a thunderous roar, flames leaped and spread across all four quarters! Alas, what shall become of the fate of Takahiko and his companions?

鷹彦『これから十万年未来に於て、大国主神が矢を拾ひに原野に往つた様な古事ではない未来の事実だ。拾ひには行きませうが、其時のやうに原野に火をかけて焼かれては困りますぜ』

日の出別『マア吾々の命のまにまに探して来るのだよ』

岩彦『サアサ、是れから流れ矢の探索隊編成だ。何れ日の出別と云ふから、火を出して焼くには違ひない、さうすると、内はホラホラ外はスブスブと鼠の先生が遣つて来ると云ふ段取だナ。全隊進め、オ一二三』

と暗雲に駆け出したり。

日の出別は直ちに燧石を取り出し折柄吹き来る暴風に向つて火を放てば、忽ち轟々たる音を立て火は四方に燃え拡がりぬ。嗚呼鷹彦以下の運命は如何に成り行くならむか。
