Chapter 14: The Frog-Vessel [540]

第14章　蛙船〔540〕

§ 1

The august majesty of the Deity / And of Takateru-hime no Kami / Abideth eternally in peace; / And manifesting the sacred teachings / Of Konohana-sakuya-hime no Mikoto, / Transforming her form into three-and-thirty aspects, / Establishing the jewel foundation / Far firmer than the Cavern of Demons, / Striving to unlock the treasure-house / Of the divine governance in a world of gloom— / Even in that hour, the eighty-fold fierce demons / Stood strong across the way, / Obstructing even a single sacred word! / The Heavenly Rock-Ship hath vanished into the clouds, / And now, alas, helpless and forlorn, / We are enveloped in the dense mists / Of the moor of Furu! / Startled and awakened by thunder / That shook the vault of heaven, / Striking upon our breasts, we arose / And looked around—what marvel is this? / A sudden shower sweepeth across the moor,

Leaving us soaked to the skin, like drenched statues of the Buddha.

Otohiko: 'Hey, friend Kame, friend Koma! We were welcomed aboard by the apostle Hinode-wake and surely soared to the very heights of the ninth heaven; yet how can it be that, ere we knew it, we were sleeping exposed to wind and rain upon this vast and desolate grassland? It wholly passeth my comprehension! Yet what can have become of Takahiko, Iwahiko, and Umehiko?'

Kamehiko: 'When we cannot even understand our own plight, how should we have leisure to think upon others? We must gird up our loins with all our might!'

Komahiko: 'We three were shaken loose and cast down from the Heavenly Bird-Ship unawares! Yet is not Lord Hinode-wake mocking us beyond measure? This place lieth before the Takao mountain range. Beneath this region the Cavern of Demons must run; but having been thus cast out, all exploration is utterly confounded! Alas, it cannot be helped: if we set our course northwestward, steering by the starlight, we must at last reach the capital of Fusa. Since we passed this way once in our childhood, let us commit our fortune to heaven and journey on foot!'

Otohiko: ''Tis reported that nigh unto the Takao mountains dwell colossal toads. My legs are sorely weary; if a toad should appear, perchance I shall mount upon its back in place of an airplane, and steer for the land of Armenia, returning home upon a frog!'

Kamehiko: 'Lo, a great obstacle! Look ahead, Oto and Koma! Though we cannot see clearly in the darkness of night, doth it not appear that a marsh or lake lieth across our path, obstructing our advance?'

Komahiko: 'Well, let us continue our march so far as our feet will carry us!'

The company advanced northwestward across the dim and shadowy moor at the pleasure of their feet.

Otohiko: 'Hark, an eerie croaking resoundeth! Doubtless our honorable master toad hath manifested himself!'

Even as he spoke, an enormous toad as huge as an ox came lumbering slowly forth, blocking their way, opening a mouth as vast as a winnowing basket and gulping down the air.

Otohiko: 'Aha! Heaven never forsaketh a man! A capital steed hath presented itself! 'Tis a pleasant jest: transferring from the Bird-Ship unto the Frog-Ship (Kaeru-bune)! With this craft there is no need to worry about returning (kaeru), for from the very first 'tis a frog (kaeru)!'

Kamehiko: 'None can tell what demons may take disguise upon this enchanted moor! We ought first to chant the Kototama to unmask its true nature; if indeed it be a genuine toad, we might mount upon it, but if it should play us false, we should be sorely dismayed!'

Otohiko: 'What mattereth it? As the proverb saith, "Try riding the horse, try mounting the frog!"'

So saying, Otohiko leaped lightly upon the back of the toad, and cried:

Otohiko: 'Aha! 'Tis wondrously comfortable to ride! Kame, Koma, why do not ye mount as well?'

'Having no experience thereof, we pray for a stay of execution!'

Otohiko: 'What faint-hearted weaklings ye are! Then Master Oto shall take his leave and ride ahead! Hearken, Sir Toad: though my name be Otohiko, thou must by no means drop (otosu) me! Carry me tenderly without letting me fall! Like the reckless march of the frogs, whither we go none can tell, yet rocked upon thy back 'tis like entering paradise!'

Great Toad: 'Aye, aye, I understand! If ye ride upon my back, sitting at your ease, ye shall return (kaeru) straight to your homeland! Prithee enliven our journey by humming a carefree song! Art thou journeying in attendance upon Hinode-wake no Mikoto, crossing the Takao mountains unto the capital of Fusa?'

And with a gaping yawn, the toad lumbered steadily forward.

Kamehiko: 'Lo, Otohiko hath struck a fine bargain! Ho, Sir Toad, wait a moment, pause a while! If Oto alone hasteneth ahead while we two follow not, all will come to naught! Carry us two along as well!'

Great Toad: 'Beyond this place lieth an exceedingly broad and ancient pond. Shall I take you aboard after we have crossed it?'

Komahiko: 'Nay, 'tis precisely to cross that pond that we need thee; we would have thee serve as our vessel upon the water!'

Otohiko: 'Kame ought to be master of the pond! And Koma, bearing the name of water-steed, must surely swim with ease! The only one who dreadeth the water is Master Oto! Dost thou not know the verse: "The ancient pond / A frog leaps in / The sound of the water (mizu no Oto-san)"?'

Kamehiko: 'Ha-ha-ha! How glibly he playeth upon words! But hark, Koma, let us hope that fellow doth not blunder and meet with such a plight as befell friend Kame in the cavern!'

Exerting all its sinews, the toad began to hop with mighty bounds, and straightway plunged splash into the dark water, raising a cloud of spray.

Kamehiko: 'Lo, the toad hath leaped into the dark pond at last! 'Tis far deeper than I imagined! We thought the margin was all grass, but behold, 'tis hemmed in by sheer precipices! Hath friend Oto sunk beneath the waves together with the toad?'

Komahiko: 'Halloo, halloo! Master Oto, halloo!'

Kamehiko: 'Hey, Oto! 'Tis too early to settle down as sediment at the bottom of the pond!'

With Otohiko still perched upon its back, the toad popped buoyantly to the surface.

Kamehiko: 'Behold Master Oto in his pitiful disgrace! Wherefore is it said that the march of frogs is reckless! Things having come to this pass, we shall watch the spectacle from above!'

Otohiko: 'At such an hour of peril, when a man's very life hangeth in the balance, why stand ye idling? Prepare a derrick swiftly and haul Master Oto and Sir Toad up!'

Komahiko: 'What is a derrick? Thou meanest a crane! We are on dry land; on land one employeth a crane! How pitiful to overturn with a crash! What profiteth it to roll thy eyes round and round?'

Otohiko: 'Cease this idle quibbling! Derrick or crane, what care I? Thou art an utterly unbending and stiff-necked fellow!'

Kamehiko: 'Circumstances alter cases; manners change with mansions; wives alter with faces! The reeds of Naniwa become the shore-rushes of Ise; and a chattering fowl like thee, when taken to the capital, becometh an elegant city-bird! Take thy ease, therefore, and enjoy thy boating excursion upon the toad's back!'

Great Toad: 'Hark, friend Kame, friend Koma! Engage not in wanton strife, but in true fellowship stretch forth your hands and rescue him!'

Kamehiko: 'What a carefree fellow! Who ever heard of dancing when plunged into the bottom of hell? Oto, the hour of settlement for all thy past misdeeds hath arrived! Harbor no complaint, but accept it as thy karma; this patience is the very foundation of deliverance!'

Otohiko: 'Sir Toad, parleying with such fellows will never bring a solution! One standing high above upon the bank and one suffering in the depths of the pool—those on high are ever insolent and heedless! Swim swiftly to the opposite shore!'

Kamehiko and Komahiko: 'Ha-ha-ha! Might maketh right in this worldly realm! To tumble from above to below is swift and easy, but to climb from below to above is a grievous labor! Climb up if thou canst!'

Great Toad: 'I shall climb up splendidly! Be of good cheer!'

So saying, the toad swam across the wide surface of the water, sought out a landing place, and clambered rustling and safe onto the dry ground.

Kamehiko: 'Lo, Oto hath landed in safety after all! Friend Koma, how shall we two ever cross this ancient pond?'

Komahiko: 'Ah, indeed! 'Tis terrifying to cross, yet if we cross not, we cannot return to our wives' homeland! Hey, Master Oto, come back and fetch us, come back and fetch us!'

Rolling his eyes and thrusting out his tongue, Otohiko replied:

'Say "Aka"! If ye say "Aka", I will come and fetch you!'

Kamehiko: 'Alas, it cannot be helped! He mocketh us! Wherefore is it said that red-hot strangers (aka-no-tanin) are ever cold and distant! Yet we must swallow our pride; shall we say it?'

Komahiko: 'Aka!'

Kamehiko: 'Aka!'

Otohiko (making a face with fingers pulling down his eyes): 'Bleh!'

Komahiko: 'I suspected all along 'twould be thus! We uttered "Aka" wholly in vain! This is the bitter parting of red-hot strangers! It cannot be helped; let us beat our scheduled retreat!'

Otohiko: 'Hey, won't ye come quickly? How long will ye linger there idling?'

Komahiko: 'Lo, what is this? A smooth, broad avenue runneth unobstructed around the pond!'

Kamehiko: 'Aha! We were bewitched by the foxes of the plain of Furu! Ha-ha-ha!'
