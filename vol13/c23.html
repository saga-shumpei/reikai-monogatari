§ 1

Yajihiko and Yotahiko gulped down the vast pot of tea once more, draining it to the very last drop.

'Ugh, how vile! I fancied I was scouring my belly by drinking such a flood of tea, yet methinks my mouth is sticky still! Even after scouring, the crone's snot and mucus are lodged together in my intestines, wherefore my spirits are far from clean. Thou art a cruel knave: hadst thou warned me beforehand about the snot, I had not eaten! Thy heart is truly perverse!'

'My belly is sick from hunger, but thou, having stuffed thyself full, spuest forth such ungrateful grumbling—thy heart is black indeed! Alas, having swilled so monstrous an ocean of tea, I am bursting for a piss! Yet the ladder hath been removed, and we cannot descend; if I relieve myself here, it will leak through the floor below, and my bladder is ready to burst! What in heaven's name shall I do?'

Yotahiko: 'Here is a capital vessel! Why not piss into this large teapot? There is no fear of it leaking down below!'

So saying, they took turns discharging their frothing, amber urine into the vessel, until by dawn the teapot was half filled. Overcome with the fatigue of the road, the two fell fast asleep. Ere long, the crowing of cocks and chirping of birds began to be heard.

Yajihiko: 'Ah, how fleeting are the spring nights! No sooner did I drop down here than the cocks begin to crow; and no sooner had the smoke dispersed than they begin to kindle the fire below, choking us with smoke again! Cough, cough! Ah, how smoky, how smoky!'

The old crone called from below:

'Halloo, guests in the loft! Wilt thou lower the teapot?'

'Aye, aye!'

And Yajihiko caught the teapot upon the iron hook and lowered it smoothly down. After a little while, the crone cried:

'Here are your rice balls, guests! Lower the hook!'

Yajihiko slid the hook smoothly down. The old woman once more loaded rice balls upon the cross-tied basket and hooked it on.

'Hey, Yota, 'tis safe today; shall I eat my fill?'

'Nay, I am utterly stuffed! The night was so brief, yet I devoured two men's rations and swilled a whole teapot of tea besides, so that my belly is swollen and taut as a woman in her tenth month! Stand not on ceremony; eat them all thyself!'

'Somehow I have no appetite either; methinks I shall pass.'

'They say that even when hungry, the belly liketh it not; yet hunger maketh all food savory, and famished men care not for filth! Why not exert thyself and dispatch five or six of them, Yajihiko?'

§ 2

'Nay, my gorge heaveth at the very thought; I have no stomach for food! Rather let us beg a fresh brew of tea and fill our bellies therewith! Grandmother, wilt thou not grant us a plentiful brew of tea?'

The crone answered from below:

'Master from Takatori, the tea is boiled just to a turn—neither too hot nor too lukewarm, but fit for savoring! Lower the hook!'

'The hook hath been dangling down this long while!'

'Aha, so it hath! When one groweth old, what with rheum in the eyes and snivel in the nose, one becometh so doddering and filthy!'

So saying, she blew her nose with her fingers, seized the teapot handle with that selfsame hand, and hooked it on. Yajihiko hauled it briskly up:

'Again she hath handled the pot with snivelly fingers, yet surely the snot hath not slipped into the tea! Come, let us fill our bellies with tea at our ease!'

So speaking, they snatched the vessel between them and swilled it down greedily till seven or eight tenths were consumed; but suddenly a strange and pungent stench assailed their nostrils.

'Hey, this tea giveth forth a most uncanny odor! Deeming it a brew of pleasantly savory, salty tang, I drank it down; yet this stench is exceedingly foul! Dost thou not smell it, Yotahiko?'

Poking his head down through the hatch, he shouted:

'Hearken, grandmother! Hath this tea not turned putrid? It reeketh of a most bizarre stench!'

'How could it be putrid? 'Twas brewed fresh only yesternight! Yea, I simply added fresh leaves to what you left over from last night, and boiled it up anew! There is no cause to fear it hath spoiled!'

'Merciful heavens, what a horror! What shall we do, Yota?'

'No help for it! "What proceedeth from oneself returneth unto oneself!" 'Tis no stranger's piss, but our very own urine that we have drunk! Deem it a physic for the colic, and endure it with patience! Because thou art ever so proud, haughty, and rapacious, karma hath brought its retribution, and like a wolf thou hast been made to lap up thine own piss! Ptui! Ptui! Ptui!'

Thrusting their fingers deep down their throats, they retched with violent coughing, and the vomited swill, leaking through the slatted floor of the brushwood loft, cascaded down straight upon the bald pate of the old grandfather below!

'Curse it! That insolent rat hath pissed upon my crown—hold, 'tis not piss alone, but grains of rice mingled therein! Aha! This must surely be the guests in the loft—unruly rogues! Because he was the master who took our daughter into service, we gave them lodging in our loft, and now they shower vomit upon my head—the height of callous ingratitude! Very well, if they show such insolence, I have a rod in pickle for them! Hearken, Otake: thou shalt not set the ladder for them! Keep them enshrined in the rafters, smoking them until they turn into dried tinder! They have made an utter mockery of us! Halloo, ye scoundrels in the loft, what have ye to say for yourselves?'

'Pardon, grandfather! 'Twas not I; 'twas a rat! Even Yajihiko cannot bear to have a rat's mischief laid at his door!'

'Spout no foolish lies! Doth a rat spew forth human vomit? Despise me not because I am a rustic old man!'

Yajihiko: 'Hey, Otake, set the ladder for us forthwith!'

'Yea, I shall set it presently; wait a moment while I attend to my little business (relieve myself)!'

Yotahiko: '"I will set it after I attend to my little business"—she hath turned a merry pun upon us!'

The grandfather: 'Otake! How darest thou set the ladder without thy parents' leave?'

'Yet he is the master who showed me such kindness; say what you will, father, I will set it for him! Come, mount upon it quickly and descend!'

'Mount upon it, thou sayest? How can one tread upon so rickety, spindly a ladder? 'Tis perilous in the extreme!'

'If you fear to tread, hug both side-rails tight and straddle your legs down! If both straddle together, the ladder will snap; wherefore let the master descend first!'

'"The master first"—what an insolent mockery! Well, no help for it; they hold the handle of the sickle while we clutch the cutting blade! Until we make a safe landing from this brushwood loft, we had best play the quiet cat and keep our peace, Yota!'

At long last the two clambered down. Yajihiko puffed his cheeks with rage:

'Hark ye, crone! Make no fool of me by making us swill urine!'

'What sayest thou, fellow? Who made thee swill urine? Didst thou not discharge it of thine own accord and drink it thyself? Because thou didst act so shamefully wasteful, I knew full well it was urine, yet left it untouched to teach thee a lesson! Even if thou journeyest on pilgrimage to the Caucasus with such a profligate spirit, the Kami will avert Their faces from thee! An utter good-for-nothing rogue! Because thou hadst servants in thy household, I deemed thee a man of some breeding, but such villainy hath exhausted my patience! Poor as I am, I know the clean from the unclean! Heavens above, our ancestral, precious teapot, handed down through generations, defiled with urine—how can it ever be used again? Restore it to its pristine purity, or this old crone will never let thee go!'

§ 4

'What is a single earthenware pot? If it be so dear to thee, I will purchase another and give it back! What a monstrous fuss over a pot worth three or five copper sen!'

'This teapot cannot be bought for three or five sen—it is worth a full hundred ryō!'

'Even a teapot of solid gold could be had for a hundred ryō! Why dost thou demand so preposterous a sum for this soot-black octopus-pot? Taking advantage of our plight—what a grasping, covetous old crone thou art! With one foot in the grave, what good will thy greed do thee, thou withered vixen?'

'True, 'twas bought for three sen when first acquired, yet it hath been our sacred heirloom handed down since the days of my great-grandfather! Cast up the compound interest upon three sen over a hundred and fifty years, and thou wilt find a hundred ryō is cheap indeed!'

'What an exorbitant reckoning! This crone belongeth to the Ural Faith—the "Wont-Sell (Uran)" cult, Yotahiko!'

'Ural or Won't-Sell, what care I? For a hundred ryō or a thousand, who would ever part with a family heirloom?'

Yajihiko: 'Were it an octopus, it might have legs (interest); but this is a legless belly-pot (teapot), so how could it ever yield interest?'

Yotahiko: 'All compounded things are subject to decay, and those who meet must part—such is the way of the world! Thou hadst best resign thyself to the loss, for the salvation of thy soul in the world to come!'

The grandfather: 'What! Hearing your speech, did ye dare discharge vile urine into our ancestral heirloom? This passeth all endurance—ye have ruined our house's greatest treasure!'

Yajihiko: 'Cease thy petty squawking! If putting urine into thy teapot angereth thee so, thou who didst make us swill urine into our very bellies art the truly unpardonable scoundrel! Thou kettle-headed grandfather clutching thy belly-pot—shall I smite both thy pot and thy kettle together?'

The grandfather: 'After enjoying our hospitality, dost thou utter such brazen insolence? Though I be advanced in years, know that in my youth I was famed as "Wildcat Tora"!'

So shouting, he clenched his fist and struck a resounding blow.

Yotahiko cried:

'Thou doddering graybeard, what darest thou do?'

And clenching his iron fist, struck the grandfather's kettle-head with a resounding thwack! The old man screeched in fury, Otake wept, the old woman shrieked, and Yajihiko raged in a frenzy. The multitude of pilgrims passing before the house cried 'A brawl! A brawl!' and gathered in amusement like swarming black ants.
