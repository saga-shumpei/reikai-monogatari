§ 1

Hereupon, the six missionaries having pacified the tumult at Otake's lodging in the hamlet of Tago, Yajihiko and Yotahiko joined their retinue to journey unto Mount Caucasus. The six rode on horseback while the pair trudged along on foot, making their way toward the capital of Fusa. Ere long they arrived at the foot of Saruyama Pass, bordering the Takao mountain range.

The long spring day drew to a close, and the four quarters were veiled in nocturnal mists. The company of eight spread straw raincloaks within a certain grove and laid themselves down to sleep. Weary to the bone from their far journey, one and all sank into a slumber so deep they knew not left from right. In the dead of night, Yajihiko awoke and peered about: a hazy moon shone down through the branches overhead. To his astonishment, five of the six missionaries had vanished without a trace, and not a human sound stirred near or far.

Yajihiko: 'Halloo, Sir Missionary, awake! People have been lost!'

Otohiko: 'What? Human beings lost? How could such a thing be? Thou hast surely dreamed it!'

Yajihiko: 'Nay, 'tis no dream, never, never! Open thine eyes and behold: neither horses nor missionaries remain; they seem to have dissolved into thin air!'

Rubbing his eyes, Otohiko surveyed the surroundings:

Otohiko: 'Alas, what a cruel trick! They have abandoned us at last! Well, no help for it; in all things let us re-view and re-hear through the spirit of Kaminaobi and Ōnaobi! Deeming it well to spur our mettle, Sir Missionary Takahiko must have stolen away while we slept soundly. Yet what a grueling task! While I lay dreaming of midnight, the five missionaries march through the night dew—no light undertaking! Deeming that Otohiko was sorely spent, they doubtless said, "Let us suffer him to rest in peace," and marched ahead. They will surely await us this side of the capital of Fusa!'

Yajihiko: 'Sir Missionary, this is no hour for such easy optimism! Thy steed is missing too! A monstrous serpent prowleth these parts by night; might it not have gulped down the missionaries and steeds together, gorged its belly, and left us behind only because it was stuffed full?'

Otohiko: 'Fret not! Missionaries who proclaim the Way of God are guarded by the Kami; how could any serpent devour them? They have surely pressed forward and await our coming!'

Yajihiko: 'Why, 'tis only a dream! Even if it came to pass, what of the bond between thee and me? Are we not sworn brethren who share every crust between us? How darest thou withhold thy dream? What is thine is mine, and what is mine is mine still!'

Yotahiko: 'Well, no help for it; at Sir Missionary's word I must yield! Three noble falcons alighted at my feet, and gazing upon my countenance, bowed low, raised their heads, and bowed again, gazing upon me in rapt adoration as though saying, "Ah, what a magnificent hero is Master Yota!" Feeling exceedingly grand, as I stared back into the falcons' eyes, suddenly from heaven fell a mighty stalk of millet, bearing five or six measures of grain upon a single ear! Since millet is an emblem of tenfold thousandfold harvest, treasures shall multiply without end! Was it not a glorious dream? Tell me, Yajihiko, none but a holy sage could ever behold such a vision!'

So the three deliberately lumbered up the slope. From above, hundreds of armed foes descended; from behind, scores of captors closed in step by step. On one side rose a sheer cliff like a folding screen; on the other yawned a chasm of ten thousand fathoms. Hemmed in on all sides with no avenue of escape:

'No help for it; in such an hour, 'tis victory or death! Come on, let us fight!'

Clenching their fists, the three hurled themselves into the throng, smiting and striking down all who came within reach. As the horde closed in to seize them, with no path of retreat left, shouting with one voice "One, two, three!", they leaped resolutely into the abyss of the valley below.... In that very instant, starting in astonishment, Zuigetsu opened his eyes, and found himself lying within the cavern of Mount Takakuma. The sun of the fifteenth day of the second month shone brilliant and radiant high in the vaulted heavens.
