Chapter 5: Strife Among Comrades [531]

第5章　同志打〔531〕

§ 1

Pushing open the doors of heaven, the semicircular moon began to gleam faintly in the western sky. The faces of the companions became clearly visible enough to distinguish one from another.

Iwahiko: 'Oh, after such a long time we are able to gaze upon the countenance of the moon! That is precisely why I always say one must wait for the proper season!'

Umehiko: 'Today has been a strange day indeed: encountering the emissary Hinodewake aboard the Tsuruyamamaru by day, and beholding the face of the moon after so long by night! Ever since crossing over to Hitoshima of the Dragon Palace, it is rare to have beheld both sun and moon together!'

Iwahiko: 'Hey, somehow I feel an ominous pounding in my chest! Will not all of you sit up? I am convening an extraordinary assembly!'

Otohiko: 'What is Chairman Iwahiko's proposal? After floating upon the sea for so long and then forcing our march across the continent, our legs are stiff as sticks! Could you not open the session while we lie down?'

Iwahiko: 'Lying down is fine, but there is a particular circumstance to this! When the critical moment arrives, it will be to your own disadvantage! Taking advantage of the moon having risen at last, we shall become marathon runners and charge toward the capital of Fusa with doubled speed!'

Kamehiko: 'Look at that—what an outrageous urgent motion!'

Iwahiko: 'Do not speak so loudly! Peek quietly at that tree stump over yonder! There he is, there he is! If you shout and wake him up, disaster will strike! That fellow lying right there is the emissary Hinodewake we saw aboard the vessel! Before he opens his eyes, shall we hoist sail at our heels and flee at full speed, or shall we surround and assault him from all directions while he sleeps? Choose between these two motions: decide immediately!'

Takahiko: 'Such a motion is rejected instantly! And having rejected it, I shall propose a secret strategy against our true foe, so deliberate upon it with due care! Even if our half-dozen men were to open a volley of fire, it would be like opposing Krupp cannons with primitive matchlocks! The outcome is already decided: it is far safer and wiser to hoist a white flag and surrender! To lose precious life over such trivial vanity is the height of folly! In these dark times, not a single soul would praise you saying, "How admirable, he was faithful unto his duties!" The only ones who would chime in would be crows cawing to rob dumplings from your graves, and even that is no mourning cry, but joyful squawking at finding food—how utterly pointless!'

Rather than that, remember the dream all of you beheld just now in the Grove of Shizu!'

Kamehiko: 'Indeed, I too had that dream; it troubles me! But Chief Emissary Iwahiko was placed upon the monster's tongue, chewed and spat out—a truly wretched ordeal!'

All: 'Yes, we too had that exact dream! For four men to behold the selfsame dream at once must surely be a divine revelation! If we dawdle, an enemy may already be lurking amidst our own ranks!'

Iwahiko: 'Are you claiming a spy of the Anai teachings is mixed among our company of six? How could such an absurdity be true?'

Otohiko: 'Nay, one cannot say for sure!'

Takahiko: 'Quiet, not so loud! But they say a man whose name suggests wings and flying through the sky is an Anai spy who infiltrated our ranks! In the dream, did he not boast: "I am backing the monster like this!"?'

Three men: 'We heard the very same in our dreams! Come, let us beat him to a pulp right here, and then run our marathon!'

§ 4

In the confusion of pitch darkness, a rain of iron fists poured down. From several paces away, Takahiko called out:

Takahiko: 'Ahahaha! Blind men fighting among comrades! Beat each other soundly; I shall watch the spectacle from above!'

Umehiko: 'Hey, all of you! You are battering my head terribly; have you no sense? How can you beat your own ally?'

Otohiko: 'In the thick of ferocious combat where blades clash, who has time for mercy? Whoever we strike, we hit, knock down, and raise our victory shout in an instant!'

Takahiko: 'Ahahaha! Splendid, splendid! Everything evil spirits do is just like that! Hit harder, hit harder!'

Otohiko: 'Bah, no matter! I will take vengeance in a moment! Men, a voice sounded from behind! Charge, charge!'

Roaring a war cry, they charged toward the sound, smashing their foreheads right into the trunk of a great forest tree—smack!

'Ouch! Ouch, that hurts! Ah, that rascal Takahiko has an uncommonly hard body! ...Wait, what is this? 'Tis a tree trunk, a giant tree!'

Takahiko, crouching low and discerning the five men squirming in the dark, gently tapped what he took to be their heads with his walking staff.

Cease now, that is enough; Otohiko has fainted at last! Look here: I have transformed into a Tengu!'

Saying thus, he spread wings ten feet across and flapped them vigorously.

All: 'Ah, this creature is a Tengu monster! O Lord Tengu, we have committed a grave mistake! O Lord Tengu, please spare our lives!'

Takahiko: 'Will you sing the sacred hymns of the Anai teachings?'

All: 'Yes, yes! If you teach us, we shall sing!'

Takahiko: 'Will you quit the Ural faith?'

All: 'We quit, we quit!'

At that moment, from the darkness of the nearby thicket, the clear, refreshing notes of a sacred hymn echoed through the night from an unseen voice.
