Chapter 21: The True Guardian Spirits [547]

第21章　本霊〔547〕

§ 1

Even as the colossal firebomb exploded, suddenly the form of the goddess of before manifested Herself. The five companions involuntarily knelt prostrate upon the flat ground, offering their deepest reverence. In a voice serene and sweet, the goddess proclaimed:

'Takahiko, renowned in deeds of the Way; Umehiko, fragrant and noble as white plum blossoms bursting into bloom at once; Komahiko, whose spirit-steed leapeth valiant, marching straight and true along the path of sincerity; Kamehiko, steadfast in the divine doctrine of ten thousand ages; and Otohiko, pure in the sacred Kototama! Ye are henceforth the missionaries of the five souls who proclaim the divine teachings of the sacred pillar! Ah, how valiant, how heroic! The clouds upon your souls have cleared away; the sun and moon of ultimate reality illuminate the sea of your hearts without a shadow, and the turbulent waves within your breasts are stilled! Without a single day's delay, swiftly and speedfully set forth unto the capital of Fusa! Yet though the firm resolve of Iwahiko is commendable, as a missionary of the Anai teachings he still lacketh somewhat; wherefore let ye five souls soothe Iwahiko's heart, and cause him to stand and labor as a warrior of true strength! She who speaketh thus is none other than the Nigimitama (Gentle Spirit) of Konohana-sakuya-hime of Mount Tenkyō; doubt it not for a moment!'

No sooner had She finished proclaiming these words than the form of the goddess dissolved into fiery mist. Choking with tears of gratitude, crying 'Ah, how blessed, how awe-inspiring!', the five chanted the Amatsu-Norito with resonant, clear, and harmonious Kototama, their voices echoing far and wide throughout the vast recesses of the cavern.

From the darkness came chuckling: 'Ha-ha-ha, u-fu-fu!'

Iwahiko: 'Insolent serpent, art thou rejoiced? Laughing so merrily—come, swallow me down forthwith!'

Otohiko: 'Master Iwa, hast thou come to thy senses? That is most splendid indeed!'

Iwahiko: '"Master Iwa, hast thou come to thy senses? 'Tis most splendid"—cease such empty compliments, stand not on ceremony! Swallow me down, swallow me down quickly!'

Takahiko: 'Master Iwa, 'tis splendid indeed, yet thou art under a great misconception! Be not anxious for us; we are safe and sound!'

Iwahiko: 'Pah! Eat me from the feet up! Dying of starvation, and now half a dozen warm human bodies have arrived, wherefore thou criest "'Tis most splendid, thanks to you we are saved"—spouting such callous banter! Gulp me down quickly!'

Kamehiko: 'Master Iwa, 'tis I, Kame! Pray be at ease! Thy manifold loving cares pierce our bones with joy!'

Iwahiko: 'Thou callest thyself a tortoise? A wolf, rather! What meanest thou by "piercing bones and flesh"? Swallow me whole at a gulp! To have my bones and flesh crunched and munched together is somewhat inconvenient for me!'

§ 3

At that moment, six luminous orbs appeared from the darkness, and straightway five radiant goddesses and one fierce man with the countenance of a demon stood revealed. Struck with awe, Master Iwa watched this vision.

Each of the five goddesses bore a name-tablet, reading: "The True Guardian Spirit of Takahiko", "The True Guardian Spirit of Umehiko", "The True Guardian Spirit of Otohiko", "The True Guardian Spirit of Kamehiko", and "The True Guardian Spirit of Komahiko". And gazing upon the name of the demon, behold, upon a far broader tablet was inscribed:

'This demon is the Secondary Guardian Spirit (Fuku-shugo-shin) of Iwahiko. His True Guardian Spirit (Hon-shugo-shin), by reason of Iwahiko's haughty pride and stubborn arrogance, hath been unable to manifest Himself. Let him swiftly repent, and command this hideous secondary spirit to withdraw! —Nodatē-hiko no Mikoto'

So ran the inscription.

Iwahiko: 'Alack, what a revelation! When the five missionaries began speaking with gentle, maiden-like grace, 'twas because such exquisitely fair True Guardian Spirits had manifested in them! Yet that Master Iwa's guardian deity should be an ogre passeth my comprehension! Hearken, ogre, who art thou?'

The Ogre: 'I am no mighty ogre such as thou deemest me! I am a faint-hearted ogre (oni-miso), terrified by a single ball of fire or a clap of thunder! Though my outward aspect seemeth fierce and terrible, my inner soul is soft as bean-paste!'

Iwahiko: 'Why utterest thou such disordered, weeping words? I am not matched with so feeble a guardian spirit! Thou must surely be a false deity!'

The Ogre: 'Yet I am the exact copy of thine own self! An empty-swaggering, faint-hearted, good-for-nothing mock-demon!'

Iwahiko: 'Now that thou speakest thereof, thou dost indeed resemble me in some corners! Alas, is it truly so? Now will I become truly strong! From this day forth I dismiss thee from my service; never, never return to wander into Master Iwa's flesh! Begone forthwith!'

The Ogre: 'Aye, aye! Why should I tarry? From long ago I yearned and yearned to depart, yet thine own stubborn attachment bound me with iron chains and refused to release me; wherefore I was forced to abide with thee until now! Farewell, and give my humble regards unto thy True Guardian Spirit!'

Iwahiko: 'Ah! Now Master Iwa feeleth as though all around him hath grown bright and clear! Noble five missionaries, forgive my grievous toils! Henceforth I shall attend upon you as a true missionary and labor faithfully in the divine work!'

岩彦『何だ、不整頓な言霊の泣き声を出しおって、俺はそんな弱い守護神と違うぞ。貴様大方、偽神だろう』

鬼『はいはい、何しにまごまごとして居りましょう。疾うの昔から帰りたくて帰りたくて仕方が無かったけれども、お前の執着心が私を今まで鉄の鎖で縛ってどうしてもこうしても解放してくれなんだから、仕方なしに今までお前に附随して居たのだ。さあさようなら、本守護神様によろしく』

岩彦『ああ、これでこの岩公も何だか其の辺が明るくなったような心持ちが致しました。もしもし五人の宣伝使様、ご苦労でございました。さあさあこれから貴方がたのお供をして、本当の宣伝使として神業に尽くしまするぞ』

§ 4

All together: 'Ha-ha-ha! Felicitations, Master Iwa! Look yonder: in the place where the ogre departed, behold what a magnificent Guardian Deity hath manifested Himself!'

Iwahiko: 'Aha, wondrous, wondrous! What a glorious and sublime Guardian Deity of Master Iwa! How mercifully hast Thou protected me! I offer Thee my deepest thanksgiving!'

The True Guardian Spirit: 'Dost thou understand now, Master Iwa? Blessed art thou that thou hast dismissed the ogre! I too feel as though the Heavenly Rock-Portal had swung wide! Come, henceforth let thee and Me be united in perfect harmony of spirit and flesh, clinging close as glue and lacquer, devoting our single-hearted sincerity to God, and proclaiming the teachings of the Anai doctrine!'

Iwahiko: 'Most humble and awe-inspiring is Thy divine greeting! Hitherto I did naught but cause grief unto my True Guardian Spirit, stubborn in mine own willful ego! Henceforth let us be joined as branches intertwined and birds of a single wing, united throughout this life and all future ages forever, that I may stand steadfast in the true service of God!'

§ 5

Hereupon, the six True Guardian Spirits and the six missionaries advanced toward the spacious hall of the cavern, clapping their hands and singing the sacred missionary hymn, stamping the earth with joyful steps like butterflies dancing among spring blossoms:

'Opened, opened, the rape-blossoms have opened! / The lotus blossoms have opened! / The blossoms of the heart have opened! / The entanglements of the soul have opened! / Opened, opened! / The Heavenly Rock-Door, long plunged in eternal night, / Hath swung open smooth and clear!'

Presently, the True Guardian Spirits transformed into wondrous five-colored orbs of light and hovered above each man's head. As though absorbed into their very intellect, the orbs gradually diminished in size, until at last they permeated completely into the bodies of the missionaries. This was the ultimate realization of the unity of spirit and flesh, and the perfect harmony of deity and mortal.

Thereupon the company emerged from the cavern, crossed the moor, and marched valiantly toward the Koshi Pass.
