Chapter 10: The Cavern [536]

第10章　巌窟〔536〕

§ 1

Intent upon retrieving the arrow loosed by Hinode-wake no Mikoto, Takahiko, Iwahiko, and their companions parted the reed-plains, vying with one another to be foremost in renown.

Iwahiko: 'He loosed but a single reed-arrow into this boundless wilderness of reeds and bade us fetch it; but search as we may, if it cannot be found beneath heaven's stars, 'tis an utter impossibility! Sinking into the abyss of utter despair—surely 'tis such an affliction as this!'

Kamehiko: 'To search for a solitary reed-arrow across this limitless moor is a labor wholly impossible! 'Tis far more hopeless than striving to strike down the stars of the firmament with a bamboo pole!'

Takahiko: 'Ye blow faint-hearted whines again! Behold, this is the very testing of our spirits! No matter what betides, we must yield meek and unquestioning obedience to the word of the apostle Hinode-wake!'

Komahiko: 'Is not his bidding beyond all reason?'

Takahiko: 'We ought ever to consider that parents, masters, and teachers will enjoin things hard to bear. Yet never would parents or teachers cast us into the abyss merely to shout in cruel triumph. To the uttermost end, in perfect faith unto death, we must seek the arrow!'

Komahiko: 'What a vexing arrow-hunt! A plight wherein one falls into utter bewilderment! Hey, look! A great terror approacheth—fire is blazing toward us! With fire set to so dense a wilderness, we cannot endure! Verily, was not the apostle Hinode-wake utterly reckless? I suspected all along that he was deceitful!'

Takahiko: 'We have fallen into the snare of the evil deities who seek to cast us into the lowest hell! Nay, let us abandon despair; let us rage and storm and battle fiercely even until we be consumed by the flames!'

Even as he spoke, fire roared fiercely from all four quarters and all eight winds, and billows of dense black smoke enveloped them all. Umehiko and Otohiko cried aloud:

'Oh, the heat! Oh, the choking smoke! What anguish! What shall we do?'

Takahiko: 'Do not utter faint-hearted cries! Is it not said that if the mind be extinguished, even fire itself is cool? In such an hour, water must confront fire! We shall extinguish the raging inferno with the precious, inexhaustible water of God that never drieth! This is the crucible of our spiritual trial!'

Even as he uttered these words, the flames swept close beneath their very feet. Brought to bay and bereft of escape, they clustered together, embracing one another and stamping their feet in despair. Suddenly, with a thunderous crumble, the earth collapsed, and the whole company plunged deep into the soil. Above them, the voracious fire swept roaring past without mercy.

Iwahiko: 'Ah, now I understand! That the Deity will save us at the ninety-nine-percent mark of utter despair—this was the meaning thereof! When he spoke of manifesting the Fire-Rat to guide us, I thought a real scarlet rodent would appear, but behold, it was a colossal, wondrous fire-rat! Wherever the flame passeth, the burnt embers turn black as charcoal; hence did he call it the Fire-Rat!'

Takahiko: 'Ho, your spiritual perception is remarkably swift! Yet hark, does not the ground still sink beneath us? To plunge to the very bottom of hell would be a grievous plight; unless it ceaseth soon, excess will be as grievous as insufficiency!'

From an unseen quarter came the sound: 'Ku-ku-ku-ku-ku!'

All together: 'Behold, is not that the squeaking of a rat? Let us go toward that sound!'

Following the sound, the company advanced. Verily, an ancient rat whose fur bore a scarlet tinge appeared before them, bearing the reed-arrow in its mouth, and uttered:

'Within 'tis hollow, without 'tis narrow and choked!'

Having so cried, it vanished from sight. The moment the company stamped their feet, a resonant thud echoed, and they plunged into a deep pit. Looking round, they beheld six cavernous portals opening before them.

Takahiko: 'Behold, 'tis here, 'tis here! Hinode-wake no Mikoto is wondrously great! The spot where the arrow fell was indeed the entrance to this cavern! How exceedingly meticulous are the demons; having fashioned an entrance in such an obscure depth, none would ever divine it! Come, as promised, let each of us take a separate passage and set forth to explore!'

The six men each vanished into one of the passages. They were stone tunnels of lofty ceiling and ample breadth. Wondrous to behold, despite being subterranean, the interior of the cavern was exceedingly bright. Each of the six men hastened down his chosen portal.

Hinode-wake's Fire-Rat hath appeared upon the desolate moor, / Casting celestial light upon the hearts of men who wander astray.

Here and there, air vents were pierced through the stone walls connecting passage to passage. Having advanced some two or three chō along the six tunnels, they arrived at a spacious hall. Beyond this place, a massive stone portal was firmly shut, forbidding further progress. The six men, without prior accord, gathered together in one spot;

Takahiko: 'Well met, brethren! How did it fare with ye? Did anything strange occur?'

Kamehiko: 'It did, it did! Great marvels occurred!'

Takahiko: 'What was it that occurred?'

Kamehiko: 'There were myriad peepholes on both sides of the stone wall!'

Takahiko: 'What nonsense art thou babbling with such pompous gravity? Those are merely airholes common to all the caverns!'

Iwahiko: 'This is wholly devoid of satisfaction! A battle without an adversary is impossible! What, demons dwelling in the Cavern of Demons? 'Tis an utter mockery! Wherefore is it said that worldly rumors are never to be trusted!'

Takahiko: 'Nay, the decisive battle beginneth now! This is merely the prologue. Is it not reported to extend tens of leagues in depth? Let us push against this stone wall with all our might! Since 'tis said there are many doors, ah, indeed, let us push against them! Up, all of ye, charge in general assault!'

And they pushed with all their strength. The stone upon which Kamehiko thrust opened as effortlessly as a curtain; carried forward by his momentum, he plunged headlong into the cavernous opening and fell splash into a bottomless pit of unknown fathoms.

Kamehiko: 'Alack, a great disaster! Will ye not save me, fellows?'

The five men gingerly crossed the edge of the pit step by step, clutching firmly with their fingers, and said:

'Ah, an unforeseen disgrace! Verily, one can never let down one's guard for a single instant!'

Takahiko: 'We must somehow contrive to rescue friend Kame!'

Kamehiko, from the bottom of the well:

と、力一杯うんと押した。亀公の押した岩は暖簾を押すように手もなく開き、亀の勢い余って岩穴の中へ飛び込んだ途端、幾丈とも知れぬ落とし穴にザブンと音を立てて落ち込んだ。

亀彦『やや大変だ。皆の奴、俺を助けてくれないかい』

五人は穴の傍を一足、一足、指に力を入れながらそっと向こうに渡り、

『ああ、思わぬ不覚を取った。これだから油断は少しも出来ないと言うのだ』

鷹彦『何とかして亀さんを救い上げてやらねばなるまい』

亀、井戸の底より、

『おい、おい、何とかしてくれないか』

音彦『お前は名前からして亀さん、水の中は得意だろう。まあ悠々と水でも飲んで寛いでいたまえ。とっさの場合には良い知恵も出ないから、この井戸の傍で山の神じゃないが、井戸端会議を開会してお前を助けるか助けないかという決議をやるのだ。まあまあ、閉会になるまで辛抱してくれ』

亀彦『そんな気楽な事を言っていられるかい。一時も早く救い上げてくれ』

岩彦『ええい、やかましく言うな、手のつけようが無いじゃないか』

亀彦『手のつけようどころか、足も頭も体中、浸けているじゃないか』

鷹彦『さあさあ、臨時議会の開会だ。これから議長の選挙だ。院内総務は誰にしようかな』

亀彦『ああ辛気臭い、冗談どころじゃないわ、冷たくなって仕方がない、体も何もかも氷結してしまうわ』

岩彦『氷結したって仕方がない、こちらは多数決だ。五人を両派に分けて、三人と二人だ。鷹さんは議長という格だ』

鷹彦『ええ諸君、遠路のところよく出席下さいました。今回臨時議会を開会いたしましたにつきまては、最も急を要する事件でございまして、国家危急存亡の場合、何卒諸君の慎重なる御審議を願いたいのです。そもそも今回の議案提出の要件は、ご存知の通り、元ウラル教のへぼ宣伝使・亀公という男、醜の巌窟の探検に出掛け、実に短見浅慮にも思わぬ不覚を取り、深く井中に陥没致し、生命旦夕に迫る、という場合でございます。何卒諸君の秘蔵の知識を絞り出されて、彼の救済の策を講じ最善の努力を尽くされん事を希望いたします』

§ 4

'Aye, aye! Agreed, agreed!'

With this, the four greeted his words with simultaneous applause. From the bottom of the well, Kamehiko cried:

Kamehiko: 'Hey, won't ye rescue me quickly? Deliverance after I am dead will profit nothing!'

Takahiko: 'Silence! Cease thy clamor from the bottom of the well; dost thou dare desecrate the sanctity of the assembly? How say ye, gentlemen: as the prior motion, ought we to rescue friend Kame, or ought we to leave him to his doom? Indicate your will by rising!'

Otohiko: 'Are we not all standing already?'

Takahiko: 'Ah, my error! Honorable members, prithee be seated. Let those in favor rise: those who resolve to save Kamehiko, stand forth; those who judge he should not be rescued, remain seated. One, two, three!'

Takahiko: 'Lo, is Komahiko the solitary soul who hath risen? This is scandalous! Then the Speaker himself shall cast his vote and rise!'

Iwahiko: 'Tyranny of the chair! Majority rule, majority rule! Those in favor are two; those opposed are three!'

Takahiko: 'The tyranny of the majority hath reached its zenith! Shall I command the dissolution of the assembly?'

Kamehiko, weeping from the bottom of the well:

'Villains, mock me not! This is no time for banter! Pull me up quickly!'

Iwahiko: 'The perimeter of this well is lined with these very stones! Thou hast only to set hands and feet upon the rocks and clamber up!'

Kamehiko: 'Clamber up, sayest thou? There is neither handhold nor foothold for hands or feet!'

Iwahiko: 'Doubtless so! Thy limbs are stubby and the shell upon thy back blocketh thy way! Hullo tortoise, Master Tortoise, in all the world there is no creature so clumsy as thou, ha-ha-ha!'

Kamehiko: 'And what sayest thou, Master Hare?'

Takahiko: 'In an hour of peril, at the very brink of doom, if thou hast leisure enough to trade playful rhymes, haul thyself up forthwith!'

Kamehiko: 'I thank ye all for your great pains! Ye could not see from above in the dark, but a splendid flight of stairs hath been carved into the stone, ha-ha-ha!'

And chuckling merrily, he leisurely climbed up from the well.

Iwahiko: 'He hath made fools of us! Nay, that we convened an extraordinary assembly, raised a great uproar, and expended vast secret funds—'tis rather we who were cast into a pitfall! To squeeze secret funds from nonexistent brains is folly itself! For this assembly, the parliamentary stipends must be increased by fifty percent! Ha-ha-ha!'

Even as they laughed together, from the darkness ahead came strange, indescribable groans and monstrous cries, unceasingly striking upon their ears.

Wondrous to relate, as the sound reverberated, the bodies of the whole company trembled in every limb as though struck by an electric charge, and a creeping numbness began to overtake them.

Takahiko: 'Lo, this is a terrible peril! Up, brethren, gird up your loins! The moment to act with all our might is upon us! Above all else, the sacred words—the Kototama!'

With one voice, the whole company solemnly chanted the Amatsu-Norito.
