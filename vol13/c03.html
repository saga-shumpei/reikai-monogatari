Chapter 3: The Sound of the Waves [529]

第3章　波の音〔529〕

§ 1

The typhoon suddenly veered toward the northwest, and the Tsuruyamamaru, caught in the reverse current, drifted back upon the very sea route it had traversed.

Drenched by the furious raging spray, the passengers packed like sushi aboard the vessel were showered with brine over their heads, looking like newborn statues of the Buddha. Otohiko, an emissary of the Ural faith, gazed out upon the raving sea and cried:

Otohiko: 'Look at that! Because you, Iwahiko, strut about putting on airs as a mighty general and stubbornly hold out, you have once more provoked the displeasure of the Supreme Deity of Heaven, causing us to suffer this terrifying ordeal a second time! Because your reformation of heart is lacking, everyone else is dragged into suffering! If it were sharing a feast, it would be well, but fleeing for our lives like this, we are in a matter of life and death, not knowing when the ship may plunge into the abyss! Repent quickly and apologize unto Hinodewake-no-Mikoto! You are not the only one drenched with salt water and suffering bitter hardship from this tempest; it is a catastrophe for all of us!'

Iwahiko: 'Even if you tell me to repent, lately my pockets are destitute and I have no raw capital for repentance! Come what may, there is nothing to do but go wherever the ship is carried by the waves!'

Otohiko: 'This is the renowned wild Sea of Persia! If we are fortunate enough to drift ashore at some port, well and good; but suppose we are dragged down to the Dragon Palace at the bottom of the sea—we would be utterly helpless!'

Iwahiko: 'Do not borrow trouble in advance! Good comes by inches, evil by ells; this very instant is the moment to exercise our free will! How could anyone know what lies a single foot ahead? That is precisely why the Ural promotional song proclaims, "An inch ahead is pitch-darkness!"'

'Let the billows roar! An inch ahead is darkness; from amidst the billows the moon shall rise!'

Iwahiko: 'So the song goes! Even if the ship sinks and we go down to the Dragon Palace, if we lie low like Okitsuyamatsumi for a while, favorable winds will blow across the sea if one only waits, and then the Sun-Rising Deity will appear to rescue us—such is the design! How preposterous; why on earth should I sell this precious head of mine cheap to such a fraud as Hinodewake?'

Umehiko: 'Hey, Iwahiko! Your stubborn persistence is causing great hardship to everyone! Why not take off your helmet before the emissary Hinodewake who demonstrated immediate divine power, and ask him to rescue us once more?'

Iwahiko: 'As the sacred hymn of the Anai teachings itself declares:',

'Though the earth itself should sink away, the power of sincerity shall save the world!'

Iwahiko: 'We became emissaries of the Ural teachings and have upheld our single-minded sincerity throughout! Whether the Ural faith be good or evil, the offspring of a white egret is white, and the chick of a crow is black by nature. If the Ural teachings are a crow, then a crow is fine! Having been born a crow through karmic affinity of spirit and soul, how could one suddenly turn into an egret even if one wished? Rather than wallowing in petty anxiety, we had far better sing our promotional song!'

Otohiko: 'No matter how much we say, this commander is hopeless! Ah, it cannot be helped! Since an inch ahead is pitch-darkness, why not drink our stock of sake so we leave no regrets behind?'

Kamehiko: 'Drinking is greatly to my taste, and your counsel is splendid! Let us drink plenty of sake to forget everything and roll drunken banter for a fresh start!'

Otohiko: 'Ah, what an annoying fellow! With missionaries so completely incapable of understanding things, it is no wonder we could not pacify even Hitoshima of the Dragon Palace through Kotodama!'

Iwahiko: 'You have no composure! If you are terrified by a mere storm like this, how could you ever serve as an emissary beneath the heavens?'

Umehiko: 'Do you honestly think you served well yourself? Continually expanding tactical territorial ambitions and commanding us merely by the tip of your chin, retribution is right before our eyes! As the proverb says, "If the head does not turn, the tail cannot move." Think carefully: rather than returning home to be severely rebuked by King Pangu, it is far wiser to apologize directly to Hinodewake-no-Mikoto right here and be saved!'

Thus the company of Ural emissaries displayed about half a dozen faces of utter terror and despondency. The gale blew ever more furiously. The helmsman raised his voice and shouted:

§ 3

Helmsman: 'Hey, hey, honored passengers! All is lost! Prepare yourselves!'

Iwahiko: 'Hey, helmsman! You say prepare ourselves, but what on earth are we to prepare?'

Helmsman: 'When all human effort fails, turn to the gods! Fire Kotodama against this wind without yielding an inch! Come, follow after me and shout with all your might!'

Saying thus, the helmsman stopped rowing with oar and scull, drew breath deep into his lower abdomen, and roared:

'Ah, Oh, Oo, Eh, Ee!'

As he shouted this forth, all the passengers aboard, trembling with fright, united their voices in unison and fired Kotodama together: 'Ah, Oh, Oo, Eh, Ee!' Iwahiko exposed his sly face like a thief cat trapped under a washtub in the dim gloom, rolling his eyes nervously, twitching his limbs in restless anxiety.

Otohiko: 'Hey, Commander! What is that panic of yours? Though you talk big, when the critical moment arrives, you prove weak indeed!'

Iwahiko: 'At this extremity, our fleeting right in this transient moment consists solely of the freedom to agonize and suffer! While I am freely exercising my own rights to the full, what right have you to interfere? Hey, all of you, it is all over now, but I am fully resolved: even if I become seaweed of the ocean deep, I will never surrender to the Anai teachings! Consider carefully: if the Tsuruyamamaru sinks, Hinodewake of the Anai teachings is just as likely to drown together with us! Leave him alone, leave him alone! If he himself is terrified, he will surely pray to God to calm the wind and waves, and we need only quietly share in the benefit!'

Umehiko: 'Indeed! If the vessel sinks, the emissary of the Anai teachings cannot escape sinking either! Those words you just uttered are truly heavenly music unto our ears!'

Iwahiko: 'Whatever anyone says, it is useless! Look up at the sky; it wears a pitch-black countenance looking as though it were about to burst into tears at any moment—a truly gloomy spectacle! At the end of the world, it seems even the heavenly power is powerless! That rogue helmsman coercively urged us to fire Kotodama, abusing our vocal organs so terribly that our Kotodama suffered a total power outage, leaving my voice hoarse! Even the sake I had carefully stored in my stomach reversed its course, forcing me to open a whole greengrocery stall of vomiting! Never has there been such absolute chaos! You fellows were whimpering about the boat sinking and dying, but look now: are not the cosmic seasons truly marvelous? The wind god seems to have weakened considerably, falling silent, has he not? Worry no longer! That once-raging fierce god has said, "Ah, forgive me for troubling your hearts so long, emissaries of the Ural faith! If fate permits, let us meet again upon dry land—Adios!" and with sails hoisted at his heels, he has executed a scheduled retreat toward the capital of Armenia at top speed! How now, are you not dumbfounded by my instantaneous presence of mind?'

Kamehiko: 'Indeed! In the ferocious tempest, blind darkness misled our path, but now that things stand thus, what misery should drive us to surrender unto the Anai teachings? The Tsuruyamamaru was on the very brink of turning into a giant water tank and plunging to the ocean floor! Come, let all of us pluck up our courage! We are six, while he is but one! Shall we sing our promotional song? Like the tempest just now, we shall blow and blow and whirl about, evicting the very guts of the Anai emissary! With the sheer force of a seventy-five-meter hurricane!'

All shouted in chorus:

'Splendid, splendid!'

As the proverb says, "Danger past, God is forgotten"; exerting their impulsive spirits in strange ways, they began singing a chaotic, out-of-tune promotional song together.

岩彦『何を言ったところで仕方がない。空を仰いでみろ、真っ黒けな顔をして今にも泣き出しそうな暗澹至極のお面相だ。世の終わりというものは、天の力もどうすることもできないと見える。船頭の奴、我々にまで言霊の発射を強圧的に勧めおって、発声器官を酷使するものだから言霊の停電をきたして声も何もかすれてしまった。せっかく胃袋に格納しておいた酒までが逆流して、八百屋店を開店する始末だ。本当にこれくらい混乱を極めたことはありゃしない。貴様ら、それ船が沈むとか、死ぬとか、弱音を吐きおったがどうだ。時節というものは偉いものだろう。風の神、余程弱ったと見えて沈黙しかけたじゃないか。もう心配するな。あれほど猛り狂っていた荒神も「やあ長々とお気を揉ませました、ウラル教の宣伝使様、またご縁があったら陸上でお目にかかりましょう、アディオス」と言って、尻に帆をかけてスタコラサッサと、アーメニアの都を指して予定のご退却だ。どうだ、俺の刹那心には閉口しただろう』

亀彦『そうだ、あまりの激しい時化で咫尺暗澹、我が身の進路を誤っていたが、もうこうなる上は何を苦しんで三五教に降伏する必要があるか。すんでのところで鶴山丸が大タンクになって海底へ沈没するところだった。さあ皆の者、勇気を奮い起こせ。こちらは六人、向こうは一人だ。宣伝歌を歌ってやろうかい。今の風じゃないが、吹いて吹いて吹きまくり、三五教の宣伝使の肝っ玉を転宅させるのだよ。風力七十五メートルの勢いでな』

一同は、

『面白い、面白い』

と喉元過ぎれば熱さを忘れるとかで、妙な所へ刹那心を発揮して、調子も整わぬ複数人のしどろもどろな宣伝歌を歌い始めた。

§ 5

'Let the billows roar! An inch ahead is darkness; After darkness the moon shall rise! The moon is the moon, yet 'tis a wine cup; Drink and get drunk, drink and get drunk—when drunk, dance! Though we say get drunk, do not get seasick on the boat; Though we say dance, O ye mischievous waves, Do not dance in a way that capsizes the ship! When ye play such restless tricks, Even our very hearts begin to dance! Drink, drink on, drink comfortably! Though we say drink, 'tis not sea brine for the boat! Take that emissary Hinodewake, With raging sprays brandished like white wigs, And swallow him down in a single gulp! I am an emissary of the Ural faith; Even though the earth be wrapped in gloom, When we drink the sake that is parent of life, Even the complexion of our faces turns rosy red! Rather than the Sun Deity with a clouded brow Glaring down with a stern, solemn face, Taste a cup of this glorious sake That brings prosperity when drunken! Wine and women are the treasures of the world; Without wine the dawn shall never break, Without wine the dawn shall never break; From wine the sun rises, from wine the moon ascends, From wine the sun rises, from wine the moon ascends!'

Singing this garbled, discordant ditty, they completely degraded and ruined their promotional hymn.

Meanwhile, the vessel at last arrived safely at the port of Taru on the Persian coast.
