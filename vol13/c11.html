Chapter 11: The Mysterious Maiden [537]

第11章　怪しの女〔537〕

§ 1

Having concluded the solemn chanting of the Amatsu-Norito, the company resumed their resolute march toward the source of the uncanny sounds. The subterranean path grew ever narrower as they advanced.

Iwahiko: 'Lo, how narrow it waxeth! 'Tis winding like a sheep's intestine; watch your steps!'

Umehiko: 'Can ye not hear the enchanting, melodious voice of an exquisite maiden echoing yonder? Verily, our journey groweth exceedingly promising!'

Otohiko: 'What wanton folly is this? Coming to explore the cavern of demons, yet babbling of graceful maidens? To the very end, thou dost flaunt thine old Ural ways!'

Umehiko: 'As the spirit of a child of three persisteth unto a hundred years, and as the sparrow never forgetteth its dance, how much more shall Umehiko, whose blood burneth with youthful ardor, remain unchanged!'

Iwahiko: 'Ha-ha-ha! Yet what could that monstrous cry of a moment ago have been? I confess myself utterly at a loss to comprehend it!'

Kamehiko: 'That was the murmuring of the demon gods! Vanquished by the sacred Kototama of the Amatsu-Norito, they furled their banners, sheathed their halberds, and beat their retreat! But look, the vault descendeth so low that my head is striking against the rock!'

Takahiko: 'In the midst of the plain of Furu / Standeth the towering mountain of rock; / When we came thither to explore / The six dark caverns of the demons, / We encountered one another / As at the crossroads of the six realms! / Then pushing against the rocky portal / With all our might, lo, Kamehiko / Plunged straightway into a hidden well, / Falling splash with a mighty groan! / Weeping and pleading for compassion, / He besought the emissaries to spare his life! / How comical was his tearful visage / As we five straightway convened / An extraordinary session by the well, / And decreed by majority voice / That saving him was utterly impossible— / Even in that very hour,'

'Whilst we thought the sluggish sea-cucumber Kame / Was groaning in the watery depths, / He leisurely ascended the stone steps / And appeared before us with an unconcerned face! / How vastly diverting was his return! / Now upon this desolate moor of demons, / Seeking to subdue the wicked spirits through words / And deliver the world of men from harm, / We conversed together, when from afar / Resounded a discordant groan of corrupted words, / Wholly out of measure and tune! / Judging it to be the wanton mischief / Of the twisted deities of deceit, / We solemnly chanted the Amatsu-Norito, / Whereupon even the fiercest of the Magatsumi / Vanished away like fleeting smoke, / Melting like snow without a trace! / Thus did our company of six quicken our steps / And press forward into this winding maze,'

'Following the narrow subterranean tunnel, / Marching endlessly without bound, / Threading our eager hearts through the crevice, / Strung out like a long serpent, / Until at last the portal opened wide / And we entered this joyous expanse! / Yet looking round, behold what wonder: / Like a bush-warbler in the spring month of March, / Or crickets singing in an autumn field, / A divine missionary hymn far purer than they / Echoeth from an unknown quarter! / Ah, how passing strange, how wondrous! / The Deity hath appeared openly in the world / To sever good from evil asunder; / Kaminaho-bi who fashioned this cosmos / Possesseth a heart boundless and vast! / How wondrous to conquer the demons / In this gloomy cavern of stone! / Ah, how delightful, how joyous! / Advance, advance, press on with might, / Whatsoever demonic powers assail us!'

'Arousing the noble Yamato spirit, / Uttering the fierce war-shout, stamping our feet, / Subduing all with the divine Kototama, / Let no man retreat a single pace! / Advance, yea advance along the rocky road!'

§ 2

Even as he sang, from an aperture in the adjoining rock-wall, a maiden's face of snow-white, dazzling beauty peered stealthily toward them.

Umehiko swiftly caught sight of that countenance, and crying:

'Lo, a great marvel! It hath appeared, it hath appeared! Heavens above!'

fell backward flat upon his rump.

'Wait, hold! It hath appeared, it hath appeared!'

Takahiko: 'What meanest thou by "it hath appeared"? There is naught whatever here!'

Umehiko: 'Whether there be or be not, an extraordinary creature is dwelling there!'

Takahiko: 'What kind of creature dwelleth there?'

Umehiko pointed toward a crevice in the stone wall:

'Prithee look yonder!'

Takahiko: 'Why sittest thou upon the floor in such a place, friend Ume? Stand up forthwith!'

Umehiko: 'Nay, the day is long and the flowers are in exquisite bloom; wherefore I sit me down to behold the blossoms! Because ye chattered idly of graceful maidens, the demon transformed itself into a peerless beauty according to your order and hath manifested itself!'

Iwahiko: 'Ha-ha-ha! This fellow hath lost his wits! There is nothing whatsoever there! Who ever heard of a man dreaming whilst walking?'

Umehiko: ''Tis neither dream nor mundane waking nor mere phantom! Doubt it not in the least!'

Otohiko: 'Lo, this fellow is strange indeed! Friend Ume, hast thou turned into a goblin? Thou dost employ the very logic of the monsters of the plain of Furu!'

Umehiko: 'Nay, by no means! Far from a monster, only peer into that cleft in the rock! The fountainhead of that melodious voice is within that very hole!'

Clutching the rock and standing upon his tiptoes, Komahiko peered into the crevice indicated by Umehiko, cried 'Ah!', and straightway slid down with a heavy thud:

'Ouch! Nay, 'tis utterly monstrous, grotesque beyond measure, unprecedented throughout the ages, an incomparable wonder crowned with gold upon gold!'

Iwahiko: 'What meaneth this? Both of ye peer into that hole and utter extravagant babble! Let me conduct a strict inspection!'

So saying, he scrambled up the rock-face, peered within for an instant, and shouted:

'Verily she is there, she is there! A woman of demonic enchantment, wondrous and astounding!'

Takahiko: 'Let me see, let me inspect this demonic enchantress with mine own eyes!'

Iwahiko: 'Hold, hold! A beauty so dazzling that Ume and Koma lost the use of their hips! The only man who retained his composure is Master Iwa! The right of priority belongeth to me! If ye would look, I may grant you leave, but what commission wilt thou pay me?'

§ 3

Takahiko: 'Ha-ha-ha! Monster or what not, so long as it be a woman, ye are well content!'

Iwahiko: 'From the foundation of heaven and earth, 'tis a land where dawn breaketh not without woman! What a blessed fortune! Wherefore is it said that travel is both sweet and bitter!'

Takahiko: 'At all events, merely to look upon her ought to be free of charge! Even if I cast a glance with my discerning eye, she will not wither away thereby, nor will she melt like spring ice!'

Otohiko: 'Takahiko, why not form a Triple Alliance with friend Kame, shatter the strong fortress of these fellows, and conquer the beauty for ourselves?'

Iwahiko: 'Conquer her, sayest thou? She is a maiden of stone enclosed within the rock! We can do nothing whatsoever!'

At that moment, the maiden manifested her lovely countenance once more at the mouth of the cleft, smiled gently, and sang:

'The Deity hath appeared openly in the world / To sever good from evil asunder; / Kaminaho-bi who fashioned this cosmos, / And Ōnaho-bi whose heart is broad— / In all things throughout the mortal realm, / Restore and proclaim aright through Naho-bi!'

Iwahiko: 'Lo, this monster maketh merry! She is singing the very missionary hymn of the Anai teachings! This passeth my comprehension!'

Takahiko folded his arms and leaned back slightly, saying:

Takahiko: 'Hm, yea... Hearken, fellow apostles! Beyond all doubt, this must be a maiden apostle of the Anai teachings!'

Iwahiko: 'When there is no entrance, who but a goblin could pass in and out of such a narrow crevice? Verily, verily, she is a monster through and through!'

Shaking his head, Takahiko said:

Takahiko: 'Nay, hold! There is ample room for quiet deliberation! A courageous apostle who, like ourselves, came hither to explore the cavern might have fallen into the snare of the demon gods and been imprisoned within this rock!'

Otohiko: 'If we push with excessive violence, we might glide through the air like friend Kame and splash down into a deep well! 'Twere better to push gently!'

Iwahiko: 'What need is there for hesitation and delay?'

Exerting adamantine strength, he pushed mightily against the surrounding stone wall, saying:

'Ah, the entrance will not disclose itself! There must assuredly be some secret contrivance! Lo, a strange stone lieth fallen here; perchance this is the hidden mechanism!'

Reaching forth one hand, he wrenched the stone away. Straightway an opening broad enough to admit both hands appeared. Even as Iwahiko caught hold with both hands and heaved with all his might, a single stone slab four or five inches thick fell backward without resistance.

'Aha! Behold what they have wrought! The entrance must surely be nigh! Up, brethren, follow me!'

Crawling through the opening, he advanced about one ken, where another stone portal barred the way. Pushing against it with all his might, the portal swung open surprisingly light. Vying with one another, the whole company dashed into the chamber.
