Chapter 18: Opening the Stone Portal [544]

第18章　石門開〔544〕

§ 1

Before a magnificent mansion enclosed by massive stone walls such as ought never to exist within a cavern, the company of five arrived.

Otohiko: 'Aha, what a splendid structure! Encircled by ramparts of solid stone, even the portal doors are carved of rock! 'Twill be no easy matter to enter! The musician of the biwa lute dwelleth surely here! When I think that my beloved is concealed within, my heart beateth violently!'

Kamehiko: 'What babblest thou? Like a bashful youth or a green girl, at thy mature age to talk of violent heartbeats! Thou shameless rascal!'

Otohiko: 'Hey, Kame, consider the place and occasion! Standing before the hall of my beloved, thou oughtest not to shower such contempt upon a handsome youth!'

Komahiko: 'Ha-ha! What a hopeless fellow! His chronic ailment hath broken out again! Shall I administer a dose of my fist to restore his wits?'

Otohiko: 'Hearken! This dwelling fortress built high with stone walls—we deem it the very lair of the crooked spirits! We are missionaries of the Anai teachings, divine officers who proclaim the grand way of reverence toward God and love toward mankind, encouraging virtue and chastising vice! We have grave matters to inquire of you; unbar this gate without an instant's delay!'

Thus he cried with a mighty voice. Within the portal all was hushed in solemn silence, and no answer came, save that the faint strains of the biwa lute leaked softly forth.

Yajihiko: 'Hearken, Sir Missionary! The folk of this mansion are all deaf deities; though thou shoutest, the gate will never open! None but Master Yaji can ever unlock this mystical portal!'

Otohiko: 'How is it opened? Wilt thou not tell us?'

Yajihiko: 'Even as I told thee before: unless I gain my commission, I cannot enlighten thee!'

Otohiko: 'What vague and shifty words thou utterest! Dost thou mean thou wouldst have money?'

Yajihiko: 'Something of the sort! Thou art a cunning man: when I say I want the point (commission), 'tis plain as day! Feigning deafness, that is what is called true money-deafness!'

Otohiko: ''Tis too absurd; let us leave him be! By some device we shall surely contrive to open it! Since he spoke of deaf deities, they must be dragon-gods (Ryū-jin), for the character for deaf is written with the ears of a dragon; beyond all doubt this is the palace of a dragon-god!'

§ 2

At that moment, a thunderous voice resounded from within the portal:

'Who are ye that come whispering and muttering before my gates?'

Otohiko: 'Art thou the dragon-god of the deaf? If thine ears hear not, hear with thine eyes! We are the company of Otohiko, Kamehiko, and Komahiko, missionaries of the Anai teachings! Open the portal forthwith and welcome us!'

From within the portal:

'Ha-ha-ha! Bungling missionaries of the Ural teachings, who labored three years upon the solitary Isle of Ryūgū, whose painful toils through wind and rain came to naught, who set forth forlornly for Armenia, only to be scattered by the tempest and stranded upon this moor of Furu—ye clumsy, imperfect emissaries! How shamelessly ye boast!'

Otohiko: 'Hold thy noisy tongue! Unbar this gate without delay, else we have measures of our own!'

From within:

'Ha-ha-ha! Thou foolish fellow! The mystical portal is to be opened by thine own hands and thine own might! Sparing a little labor and seeking to have another unbar it for thee is the height of cunning deceit! Hast thou forgotten the mystical key?'

Otohiko: 'Aha, he uttereth a profound truth! Indeed, if we cannot open so simple a stone gate, how could we ever perform the divine work of opening the Heavenly Rock-Portal? In our distraction, we forgot the Amatsu-Norito! Kame, Koma, let us chant the sacred Kototama!'

So saying, they chanted the sacred words. No sooner had the prayer ended than the massive, impenetrable stone portal swung smoothly and noiselessly open to left and right.

Otohiko: 'Verily, 'tis even as the prayer declareth: "When ye thus proclaim, the Heavenly Deities shall push open the Heavenly Rock-Door, cleaving asunder the manifold clouds of heaven to hearken; and the Earthly Deities, ascending to the peaks of the high mountains and low mountains, shall part the mountain mists to hearken!" How say ye: behold the power of Master Oto's Kototama!'

§ 3

Kamehiko and Komahiko hung their heads in silence. Beholding a colossal man standing within the portal, Otohiko shouted:

Otohiko: 'Lo, art thou not Noro-kō the Boa Constrictor whom we beheld in our dream? How camest thou to be here?'

Noro-kō: 'Dream or waking, in this fleeting world all mortals are dreaming their vain dreams! There is scarce a steadfast soul enough to fashion eye-drops of! Yet ye fancy yourselves Anai missionaries—how astounding!'

Otohiko: 'At all events, announce unto Princess Garyō that Master Otohiko, the handsome youth whom she beheld a while ago, hath arrived!'

Noro-kō: 'Alas, it cannot be helped! I shall announce your presence forthwith; stand meekly here until the reply arriveth! Venture not a step within without leave!'

So saying, Noro-kō vanished into the interior.

Kamehiko: 'How long will that Noro linger? However deep the mansion be, he keepeth us in tedious suspense! Well, Yajihiko and Yotahiko, we thank you for your pains; thanks to you, the gate hath opened!'

Yajihiko: 'Though the gate hath opened, that which remaineth closed is thine own heart! How pitiful thou art!'

Kamehiko: 'Whether the formless portal of the spirit be open or shut, how couldst thou discern it? How could a crass materialist comprehend spiritual matters? Thou who at every turn demandest a commission—dare not meddle with the opening and shutting of spiritual portals!'

Yajihiko: 'Ha-ha-ha! What utterly ignorant missionaries! Whom dost thou imagine Master Yaji and Master Yota to be? Take heed lest ye foam at the mouth in astonishment hereafter!'

So saying, they transformed themselves in an instant into two spheres, one red and one white, and with a humming drone flew away into the unseen void.

§ 4

Otohiko: 'Lo, what was that? Those fellows were no ordinary mortals! Yet how long will Noro linger with his sluggish delay? Alas, if a man doth not act for himself, relying upon others bringeth nothing to pass! Kame, Koma, there is no need for hesitation! Let us enter of ourselves! We know not what secrets lie concealed; wherefore proceed with all vigilance! Whosoever unmasketh the true nature of Princess Garyō shall be awarded the Grand Order of the Golden Kite! Forward, forward, charge, charge!'

In step together, they dashed up toward the entrance. Advancing along an endless corridor winding in myriad turns, they strode forward with heavy footsteps.

Otohiko: 'Lo, there is no one here! We have run straight into a dead-end rock wall! Let us compose our spirits here and devise our second plan of battle!'

Otohiko (looking up toward the heavens):

'Aha! What an immensely lofty ceiling for a cavern! Look yonder: the apostle Hinode-wake, mounted upon the Heavenly Bird-Ship, is cruising through the sky with a roaring engine!'

At that moment, from ahead came the gentle strains of a woman's missionary hymn.

Kamehiko: 'Hey, Koma, look! We are under the open sky!'

Komahiko: 'Verily, verily! We are upon rocks by the wayside! How incomprehensible!'

A maiden missionary tripped lightly before the three men, and said:

The maiden: 'Are ye missionaries of the Anai teachings? Even now the apostle Hinode-wake, accompanied by three missionaries, men and women, awaiteth you at the foot of the Koshi Pass, having prepared horses for your journey!'

Otohiko: 'This passeth understanding! We thought ourselves deep within the cavern, yet behold, 'tis a boundless wilderness! Who art thou, fair lady?'

The maiden: 'I am from the holy land of Jerusalem, a missionary of the Anai teachings proclaiming the doctrine of Haniyasu-hiko no Kami of the Golden Mountain, named Izumo-hime! Well done on your arduous missionary journey! Prithee follow me thither!'

So saying, she walked ahead along the overgrown moor-path. Driven by profound wonder, the three followed after her.
