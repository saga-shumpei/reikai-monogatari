Chapter 12: The Pitfall [538]

第12章　陥穽〔538〕

§ 1

At the manifestation of the divine Lord Hinode-wake, Master Iwa, whose predicament had been exceeding perilous, was suddenly inspired with renewed vigor, and cried:

Iwahiko: 'Behold, thou demonic sorceress of shape-shifting deceit, plead as thou wilt, all is vain! Now that the supreme general of the Anai apostles hath appeared, thy fortune is utterly run out! How sayest thou: is not the divine providence of the Great Deity meticulously prepared in every detail?'

The maiden: 'Before so mighty a champion as thou, I must needs curl my tail and beat retreat! Wherefore I beg your leave to depart!'

Iwahiko: 'Serves thee right, thou goblin serpent! Didst thou not confess with thine own mouth that thou wouldst curl thy tail?'

The maiden: 'Ah, divine Lord Hinode-wake no Mikoto, how graciously hast thou come to receive me! Prithee, have no further dealings with an obstinate fool such as Master Iwa! For these are missionaries of false pretense—swayed by survival of the fittest, devouring the weak, utterly selfish, and inhumane...!'

Iwahiko: 'Villain, mock me not! Swelling with conceit... what outrageous arrogance for a woman to utter!'

The maiden: 'Fare ye well; rest at your leisure!'

So saying, she slipped through a tiny aperture in the stone wall as effortlessly as wisp of smoke.

Iwahiko: 'Lo, even as I foretold, she hath revealed the true nature of a shape-shifting goblin, escaping through a hole where even a man's neck could scarce enter! Hey, Taka, Oto, Ume, Kame, Koma! How say ye? Is not my clairvoyance wondrously grand? Master Taka was utterly duped, using polite phrases to her; but now the shallow bottom of thy discernment is laid bare!'

Takahiko: 'Ha-ha-ha! Cease thy hollow swaggering, like a wind-up puppet!'

Even as he spoke, a heavy, ominous clunk resounded.

Iwahiko: 'Lo, a strange sound! What can this mean?'

From without, the maiden suddenly thrust her pale face through the aperture in the rock-wall, saying:

The maiden: 'Master Iwa, prithee tarry in this cavern at your ease for ten days, twenty days, yea, a hundred days! I have snapped the portal shut, so there is no fear of wicked intruders entering from without, nor is there any danger of ye escaping from within! Having naught else to offer, lick the oil from the rocks... and quietly perform your spiritual austerities! As for me, holding hands with Lord Hinode-wake... O-ho-ho-ho, how joyous! Rest at your ease! Farewell!'

Iwahiko: 'A great disaster! That sound a moment ago was the shutting of the entrance portal! We cannot abide passively in such a plight!'

The maiden: 'Though ye cannot abide, ye must needs abide, for there is no help for it! Nay, there is no way of exit! Yet according as ye conduct yourselves, perchance I may contrive some measure for your relief!'

Iwahiko: 'Alas, what tyrannical coercion! Verily, men are never to be judged by appearances: wearing a gentle visage as though she would not harm a flea, yet her heart within is like that of a Yaksha! Hearken, companions, where there is an entrance, there must assuredly be an exit! Shall we not put our hands to this crevice and push and pull with all our might? That six-foot stalwart warriors should be locked away by a single frail damsel, pleading for pardon—'tis an unbearable disgrace! ...I pray thee, Lord Hinode-wake, wilt not thy divine self deliver us?'

'Well met, Master Iwa, rest at your leisure! ...Fair maiden, shall we now take our journey together unto heaven?'

The maiden: 'I thank thee with all my heart! If I may walk by thy side even for half an hour, I shall harbor not the slightest regret though I die! Ho-ho-ho!'

Iwahiko: 'Lo, hark at her shameless blather: "If I can walk by thy side even for half an hour, I shall be content to die"—flaunting such wanton romance before our very faces, making mock of us! Ah, they have already departed whither! What an uncanny, eerie business!'

§ 2

Takahiko: 'Lo, while I sought to take her for mine own, the apostle Hinode-wake came from without and utterly carried her off! Ah, 'tis reckless abandonment...! Yet it cannot be helped; let me too slip forth through this hole, even as the woman did!'

So saying, Takahiko, by wondrous divine arts, diminished his bodily stature, transformed himself into a small hawk, and swiftly glided through the aperture.

Iwahiko: 'Behold, he hath escaped as well! Hey, friend Taka, ah, thou art a wondrous fellow! Lift up the stone lid of a moment ago and welcome us back to freedom!'

Takahiko: 'Hark ye, Master Iwa and all your company! I shall henceforth journey unto heaven holding hands with the apostle Hinode-wake! Forgive me for displaying such romance before your eyes, but resignedly accept it as your karma! O-ho-ho-ho-ho, farewell!'

Iwahiko: 'Villain Taka! What meanest thou by mimicking that goblin woman and acting so outrageously puffed up? Put thyself in our shoes!'

Takahiko: '(In Gidayū puppet-theater cadence) "Thou art thou, and I am I! Take thy ease, take thy quiet rest for ten days, twenty days, a hundred days—yea, for a thousand, ten thousand years until ye turn to stone! Even as in the tale of Senmatsu: waiting a year, yet he appeareth not; waiting two years, yet he appeareth not; though they wait a thousand, ten thousand years, what tidings shall ever arrive? Doubtless the wives ye left behind in your homelands will weep and lament: 'When I think thereon, oh how pitiful! Are there neither gods nor buddhas in this world? Where and how fareth Master Iwa, upon whom I relied? Yearning and longing to behold him dawn and dusk, yet no tidings come save stony silence! Shedding tears like hail: in all the three thousand worlds, can there be any wretch so ill-fated as I? Ah, what shall I do, what shall I do...!'" Thus falleth the curtain on the tragedy—and ye are now sowing the seeds thereof! Prithee exert yourselves to sow your seeds well! A thousand years hence, someone may perchance come to worship with a votive bib, saying: 'In this cavern, Master Iwa, the stone-stubborn champion, turned to fossilized stone!' A man hath but one life, yet his name endureth forever—'tis a fine theme for future lore! Even as the Anai teachings declare: "When the seeds are sown and the seedlings sprout, then go ye forth; when harvest-time cometh, confer renown and restore them to their source!" Since even the Great Deity Kunitokotachi was imprisoned for long ages, 'twill be the glory of all ye missionaries to emulate the Deity and perform three thousand years of spiritual austerities! Ha-ha-ha, o-ho-ho-ho!'

Iwahiko: 'What meaneth this? A single fellow laughing both like a man and like a woman, as though he were a demon!'

Takahiko: ''Tis a matter of course! In the flesh a man, in the spirit a woman—the guardian deity of the henjō-nyoshi (transformed woman)!'

Iwahiko: 'Confound thy transformed women and men! Cease babbling sacred incantations and open the rock forthwith! ...Hearken, gatekeeper, if thou lingerest, I shall strip thee of thine office!'

Takahiko: 'Ho-ho-ho-ho! Master Iwa, whom wouldst thou dismiss? Imprisoned behind the rock-portal, thy face hath lost all authority! Thou art appointed an officer for life; prithee rest content in thy lifelong subterranean post!'

Iwahiko: 'Setting aside all intricate disputes, open this cavern portal without more ado!'

Takahiko: 'Nay, I am pressed for time; 'twas sport enough to tease ye thus far! It cannot be helped; I shall open it for you!'

Iwahiko: 'If thou keepest us enclosed in this stone cave forever, darkness will reign day and night! Bring forth the goblin woman and bid her perform the sacred dance of Ame-no-Uzume no Mikoto! Else shall we not easily emerge!'

Takahiko: 'Cease thine endless banter! Come forth swiftly, for the god Tajikarao shall now wrench open the rock-portal! Were it a radiant goddess of love like Amaterasu-Ōmikami, singing "Ah, how delightful, how clear and pure!", 'twould be worth opening; but to open upon a gallery of sour, wrinkled faces like a gathering of five hundred Arhats is wholly devoid of joy! Yet companions on the road must share mutual compassion; wherefore out of mercy shall I unbar the gate!'

Iwahiko: 'Hold, pause! Forget not to spread forth myriad delicacies of sea, river, mountain, and field upon the eight-legged offering tables! Dost thou imagine we shall come forth so lightly without mirror, without sword, and without the five-hundred jewel strings? Pile high the fragrant fruits upon wooden altars like towering pyramids, master the architecture of divine offerings, chant the Amatsu-Norito without blemish, and present the great sacred staff!'

Takahiko: 'What nonsense babblest thou? Can I alone perform the eightfold feats of the Thousand-Armed Kannon? If thou babblest further, remain imprisoned in the rock-chamber for thousands of ages forever!'

Iwahiko: 'Nay, we remit the offerings! Only open the portal! Left alone in this demon cavern, thou wouldst suffer bitter loneliness!'

Takahiko: 'It cannot be helped!'

Exerting all his might, he pushed violently against the stone wall. The stone portal proved lighter than anticipated; carried forward by his momentum, Takahiko tumbled headlong into the cavern, striking his knees and crying 'Ouch!' as he rubbed them. In that swift instant, the five men dashed out into the corridor and slammed the stone gate shut from without.
