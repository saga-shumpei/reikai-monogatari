Chapter 1: The Awakening of Kotodama [527]

第1章　言霊開〔527〕〔527〕

§ 1

[The Heavenly Rock-Door] Manifesting His pure and immaculate divine soul, the Great Deity Susanō-no-Ōkami brought forth gentle maidens through sacred covenant, rejoicing in triumph: 'I have won, indeed I have won!' Yet in the exuberance of His victory, His retainers broke down the ridges of the sacred paddy fields, filled up the irrigation ditches, and defiled the hall of the sacred harvest feast.

As Wakahirume-no-Mikoto sat in the pure weaving-hall weaving the sacred garments for the Gods, the roof of the hall was breached, and a dappled heavenly steed was flayed backward and hurled inside; startled by this intrusion, the divine weaving-maiden was struck by the weaving shuttle and perished. Grieving deeply over this tragic defilement, the Great Sun Deity Amaterasu-Ōmikami closed the great door of the Heavenly Rock-Cave and withdrew into seclusion.

Because the Solar Deity had hidden Herself, both heaven and earth were plunged into perpetual darkness; thereupon the eight hundred myriad deities gathered at the bed of the Tranquil River of Heaven to hold a divine council. Gathering the long-singing birds of the eternal night, they made them sing; taking hard stones from the river-bed and iron from Mount Ame-no-Kagu, they commissioned the divine smith Amatsumara and Ishikoridome-no-Mikoto to cast the sacred Yata Mirror.

They charged Futodama-no-Mikoto to bear the grand offerings, commanded Ame-no-Koyane-no-Mikoto to recite the grand liturgy, and Ame-no-Tajikarao-no-Kami stood concealed beside the rock-door. Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto, grasping heavenly bamboo foliage and stamping upon an upturned hollow tub, danced in ecstatic divine trance, whereupon the eight hundred myriad deities roared with laughter, filling the celestial sphere with joy.

Puzzled by the resounding laughter outside, Amaterasu-Ōmikami opened the rock-door a slit, inquiring from within: 'Because I have hidden myself, the High Plain of Heaven and the Central Land of Reed-Plains should be steeped in darkness; why then doth Uzume dance and sing, and why do all the deities laugh?' Uzume replied: 'We rejoice and laugh because a Deity far more noble than Thine Augustness hath appeared,' as Ame-no-Koyane-no-Mikoto and Futodama-no-Mikoto held up the Yata Mirror before Her opening.

Mistaking Her own radiant reflection for that supreme Deity, as Amaterasu-Ōmikami leaned forward in wonder, Ame-no-Tajikarao-no-Kami seized Her august hand and drew Her forth. Instantly Futodama-no-Mikoto stretched a sacred straw rope behind Her, declaring gently: 'Thou must not return within!' With the Great Deity's heart pacified, the High Plain of Heaven and the Central Land of Reed-Plains were illumined without a shadow, and the myriad deities rejoiced in boundless exultation. Thus was the Rock-Door gloriously opened—the virtue of visible worship and the blessing of invisible worship. Revere this, ye mortals born into the Divine Land!

By the decree of the Heavenly Deities, the Direct Spirit (Naohi) manifested, explaining the pristine divine wisdom and the sacred rites of visible worship, invisible worship, and Spirit-Pacification, illuminating the true path for all humanity. Remembering God's boundless grace and the Sovereign's benevolence, let every righteous soul steadfastly observe the sacred Divine Way.

[Spirit-Pacification (Chinkon)] Born into this divine garden of fertile reed-plains and everlasting evergreen pines, our seventy million brethren must deeply investigate and comprehend the peerless, profound essence of the Land of the Rising Sun. Established by the unchanging decree of the Heavenly Ancestor through ten thousand generations, the realm ruled by the Heavenly Sovereign is Japan alone. In the Divine Age, the dual deities Izanagi and Izanami appeared, executing the divine commission to repair and consolidate the earth, giving birth to the land, humanity, mountains, rivers, plants, and trees, and at last bringing forth Amaterasu-Ōmikami, Tsukiyomi-no-Mikoto, and Takehayasusanō-no-Mikoto.

Boundless was the joy of the Creator God, who rejoiced: 'Many children have I borne, yet none surpass these! Truly noble offspring have I attained!' Ascending straightway unto heaven, He inquired through the grand divination of the Musubi Deities, decreeing: 'Amaterasu-Ōmikami shall rule the High Plain of Heaven; Tsukiyomi-no-Mikoto shall guard the Realm of Night; and Hayasusanō-no-Mikoto shall govern the Sea-Plain,' apportioning unto each their sacred domain.

Thereupon Izanagi-no-Mikoto shook the sacred necklace of Yasaka curved jewels of five hundred beads about His neck so that it chimed melodiously, bestowing it upon the Great Sun Deity to rule Takamanohara; therefore this necklace is named Mikura-tana-no-Kami. This was the sacred rite anchoring the divine soul to establish Her as the supreme ruler of the Sun's land—the primordial inception of Spirit-Pacification and the cornerstone of national governance. Amaterasu-Ōmikami inherited this divine authority, conferring the Three Sacred Treasures upon Ninigi-no-Mikoto when entrusting Him with sovereignty over the earth below.

Successive Emperors through all generations have made this Divine Will their very heart, observing it as the sacred imperial institution upon ascending the throne; such is the virtue of Spirit-Pacification. Mortals born into this sacred land, from Taiwan to the ends of the Kuril Islands, must never neglect worship and reverence, looking up unto the divine majesty morn and eve. Spirit-Pacification is the cosmic law uniting visible and invisible realms; from the Sovereign's supreme way of unity between liturgy and government down to the moral self-cultivation of every family, it serves as the foundation for elucidating the formless and voiceless spiritual world.

Let those dwelling in God's realm discard the crooked ways resembling the sideways crawl of crabs; purifying their hearts and yielding their souls unto the divine art, let them attune their spirit unto the hidden realm and refine their spiritual faculties after the manner of Izunome. Walking this righteous path in these turbulent times, let Japanese men radiate Yamato-damashii across the earth and establish everlasting achievements. Taking the Direct Spirit as the warp and the Resolute Spirit as the weft, advance courageously until every army of Magatsuhi coiled throughout the eight islands is utterly annihilated alongside all foul demons!

[Mount Fuji] Since ancient times, Mount Fuji has been written as 'Inexhaustible Mountain' (Fujin-zan) or 'Peerless Mountain' (Fuji-san), hailed as the Lotus Peak, Mount Fukuchi, Mount Tenkyō, and Mount Fusō, revered as the sacred embodiment of Konohanasakuyahime-no-Mikoto, and variously called Mount Narusawa, Mount Hatachi, Mount Hoho, and Mount Yamagimidake. Diverse explanations have been offered regarding its name, yet all are mistaken. Japan has been hailed from primeval antiquity as the land blessed by Kotodama; even a small hill manifests its intrinsic spiritual function through its divine name.

In Kotodama spiritual science, the syllable Fu of Fuji signifies 'Force' (Riki)—the adamantine power spewing fire and smoke from the center of the earth. The syllable Ji denotes the crossroads (tsuji) of fire-veins, the Kotodama oozing vital spiritual energy from within the earth.

Furthermore, the spiritual inversion (Reikaeshi) of Fuji is Hi. Hi is Fire, Spirit, and the Sun. Thus it is called the volcano of Fuji and praised as a sacred peak, standing as the supreme representative of Japan. Though currently in a state of winter seclusion as a dormant volcano, it is a divine mountain that may activate and return to a living volcano at any appointed hour. Analyzing more deeply, the Kotodama Fu governs cosmic permanence in heaven, presiding over all manifestation: coming and going instantaneously, ascending and descending, emerging and entering, uniting fire and heat, radiating light eightfold.

The Kotodama Ji signifies resolute protection, firm consolidation, standing at the crossroads, and solemn pledging.

The Kotodama Hi embodies luminous penetration, the bond of the Sun, illuminating everywhere without exception, daytime, penetrating both manifest and hidden realms, the ultimate of boundless compassion, revealing the sacred form of Su, bringing all into harmonious unity, supreme majesty, and illuminating past, present, and future.

When we contemplate these operations of Kotodama, it becomes clear that Mount Fuji is the sublime emblem of Great Japan, manifesting the paramount divine vocation of the Divine Land and its people to teach and deliver all nations—the sacred peak where the Divine Spirit operates. Beginning with the celebrated verse 'A white folding fan hanging upside-down in the sky of the Eastern Sea', countless poems celebrate this mountain beyond all others.

§ 4

[Poetry of Mount Fuji and Commentary] The classic verse by Keichū sings: 'Mount Fuji is the Sovereign of peaks, like an imperial throne; draped in the sky hangs its scripture-hat of snow.' This is an exceedingly noble poem, portraying how Mount Fuji, crowning itself with purest white snow like the imperial canopy suspended above the Sovereign's throne, towers majestically above surrounding ranges as the monarch of all mountains.

Another celebrated waka sings: 'The white snow I sought by guesswork was but at the foot; in an unexpected quarter clears the peak of Fuji.' While gazing anxiously through mist wondering where the summit might be, the clouds suddenly disperse: 'Behold, towering unimaginably high above the cloud-seas, its peak thrusts boldly into the heavens!'—brilliantly capturing the awe and overwhelming reverence inspired by its sublime height.

The renga master Sōgi composed: 'On New Year's morn, let it be the vision I behold: Mount Fuji.' As a sacred sight for the dawn of the New Year, nothing surpasses Mount Fuji in sacred dignity. The Man'yōshū likewise contains numerous poems extolling Mount Fuji, each testifying that this sacred peak embodies the transcendent spiritual nobility of Japan.

A classical Chinese poem extols: 'In the Orient stands solitary the jade Lotus peak, through myriad ages unchanging its countenance; where pure ridges and radiant peaks bow in morning homage, amidst five-colored clouds towers this immortal mount.'

These several writings were published in the January 1921 issue of Shinreikai. Among them, the chapters on the Origin of the Sacred Flag, Spirit-Force-Matter, the Heavenly Rock-Door, and Spirit-Pacification were original compositions by Onisaburō dating from 1900, reprinted here in accordance with divine necessity.

富士がねは山の君にて高御座　　空にかけたる雪の経笠

実に上品な歌にして、天皇の高御座の上に釣るす経笠の如くにて然も天空高く、白皚々たる、純白の雪を戴き、群峰の上に屹として、一番高く峙え立ち居る富士山は実に山の中の君主なりといふ意味なり。

心あてに見し白雪はふもとにて　　思はぬかたに霽るる富士ケ嶺

あの辺が頂上かしら、雲に包まれて見えぬのかと、あせりあせり見る中に、雲が晴れると、ヤア何だアンナエライ高い雲表にニヨツキリと頂が現はれて居ると云つて茫然自失、今更にその高さに驚かされ、且つ崇高の感に撃たれて居る真境を写し出したる歌なり。

元朝に見るものにせむ富士の山

これは宗祇の作句なり。正月も近い目出度い元旦の見ものとして富士の山に越したものは無く、尊しと云ふの意味なり。万葉集にも随分富士を賞めたる和歌が沢山載せられあるが、凡て此の富士山は日本国の崇高なる意義を代顕したる神峰なり。

【富士山の名歌と解説】契沖の和歌に『富士がねは山の君にて高御座　空にかけたる雪の経笠』とあります。これは実に上品な歌であり、天皇の高御座の上に吊るす経笠のように天空高く白皚々たる純白の雪を戴き、群峰の上に屹立して一番高く聳え立つ富士山こそ、まさに山々の君主であるという意味であります。

また古歌に『心あてに見し白雪はふもとにて　思はぬかたに霽るる富士ケ嶺』とあります。「あの辺りが頂上だろうか、雲に包まれて見えないのか」と焦りながら見つめるうちに雲が晴れ渡り、「やあ何だ、あんな途方もなく高い雲の上にニヨッキリと頂が現れているではないか！」と我が目を疑い、今更ながらその高さに驚嘆し崇高の念に打たれる真境を見事に写し出した名歌であります。

連歌師宗祇の作句に『元朝に見るものにせむ富士の山』とあります。めでたい元旦の初景色として富士山に越すものはなく、その尊さを讃えた句であります。『万葉集』にも富士を賞美した和歌が数多く収められていますが、すべてこの富士山は日本国の崇高なる意義を具現した神峰であります。
