Chapter 6: The Great Reversal [532]

§ 1

God hath appeared at the forefront To stand and divide good from evil! § 2

'With the Kototama of single-hearted sincerity, I am the divine minister who pacifies all! Surmounting the Sea of Persia, Disembarking at the port of Taru, Mounting a steed and advancing quietly Into this Grove of Shizu; Stepping beneath the forest shade To rest awhile from travel's fatigue, Suddenly voices of men were heard! Straining my ears, what sight was this? The emissaries spreading the teachings Of the Ural gods unto all four winds— Iwahiko, Umehiko, Kamehiko, Komahiko, Otohiko, and Takahiko— Engaged in senseless strife among comrades! Listening at ease while relaxing, 'Twas as though carrion were cast before hounds: Clamoring with loud, contentious words, Striking, kicking, punching, weeping, and wailing, Until this very Grove of Shizu (Tranquility)—'

§ 3

'Has become a forest of turmoil and clamor! O emissaries of the Ural gods, Ye too are children of God, temples of God! The Great Deity who created this universe Is but One solitary Supreme Lord! Forsaking the Originating Deity, Revering mere branch-and-leaf spirits, Thrown into disorder by twisted demons: The retribution is right before your eyes! Swept away by the wind of majestic power Of the Great Deity Kami-Susanoo, The Ural Mountains and Armenia— Which ye thought an everlasting, rock-firm dwelling— Together with the barbed wire fences ye served, Have been pulverized into leaves of dust, Leaving behind mere traces of a transient dream! How pitiful the miserable lingering struggle Of the hideous demons Miyamahiko and Kuniteruhime! Both the High Plain of Heaven and the earthly realms—'

§ 4

'Have been shrouded in utter gloom in this age, And the teachings of Ural have reached their end! One day sooner, one moment faster, Repent swiftly, without delay; Recant and correct your foul and crooked words, And unto the glorious, auspicious teachings of Anai Offer up your sincere, true hearts in prayer, O ye six companions! The world shifts like hydrangeas of seven transformations; The land of Yashima is enveloped tenfold, twentyfold, Clouds and mist blocking all four quarters, An eternal night from which there is no escape! Take the spirit and soul of your innermost heart Bestowed upon your mortal frame, Polish it clear as the Heavenly Pure Mirror, And through Kaminaobi reform your souls into purity! I am Hinodewake, emissary of the Sun; Reverently obeying the command of the Sun Deity Dwelling in the heavenly celestial blue, unto the Reed Plains—'

Never from ancient days down to the present Hath any flourished who drew sword against the True God! 'Tis like crossing a dark sea pathway of raging waves— Its peril is boundless without end! Rejoicing in the boundless, deep grace of the Great Deity, Serve faithfully upon the wayside Of the sacred teachings of Anai, Of the sacred teachings of Anai!'

§ 6

At the sound of this singing voice, the companions felt as though they had been struck by lightning; they prostrated themselves upon the earth with a thud, holding their breath in trembling awe.

Takahiko: 'Ah, whom should it be but the emissary Hinodewake whom I beheld aboard the vessel today! I am in truth Takahiko, an emissary of the Anai teachings; masquerading as a missionary of the Ural faith, I probed the secrets of their wicked plots and returned here. Now the divine moment has ripened, and the hour has come to annihilate the evil strongholds of the twisted spirits! Seeing that the sky of murky clouds and mist will soon clear and the morning sun shine in the eastern heavens, the vast and boundless divine favor of the spirit is truly gracious, truly blessed! From this moment I wish to accompany Your Excellency and escort you unto Mount Caucasus; I pray thee grant me this permission!'

Hearing him speak thus in the darkness, Hinodewake answered:

Hinodewake: 'Oho! Art thou Takahiko of whom I have heard in rumors past? It is a blessed encounter to meet thee here! Nonetheless, unless we pacify these five emissaries through Kototama, we cannot fulfill our divine mission.'

Takahiko: 'Nay, have no anxiety over this matter! Having shared bed and board with these men for three years, I know their psychological state thoroughly, so I believe it is an easy task to bring them to surrender without great worry. Please leave these five men entirely unto me!'

Hinodewake signified his consent without words through the expression of his countenance.

日の出別は言外に承知の旨を顔色にて示された。

§ 7

Takahiko: 'Come now, Iwahiko! You alone are the most stubborn obstacle! If only you repent, the others are practically ninety-nine percent converted already! How now: will you surrender unto the Anai teachings?'

Iwahiko: 'Ah, it cannot be helped! Another voyage of reversal upon the Divine Way! In the course of time, even parents and children, masters and servants become foes and allies—such is the way of the world; inevitable it is, so I shall surrender!'

Takahiko: 'Is that truly in earnest?'

Iwahiko: 'What else could it be but earnest? 'Tis no wooden blade; 'tis real steel, a prized sword by Masamune!'

Takahiko: 'If only you had repented a little sooner! Holding out to the bitter end and capitulating at the very last moment is none too handsome! Nonetheless, 'tis far better than not repenting at all. Soon the dawn shall break; as a reward for your repentance, I shall allow you to sleep in peace. Tomorrow we must walk with all our might!'

Iwahiko: 'Nay, sleeping is out of the question! It is a cosmic upheaval within my very heart! A shock far fiercer than encountering earthquakes, thunder, or a rain of fire!'

Takahiko: 'Ah, so it must be! To make that firm resolve is the very hallmark of an emissary walking the Way of Sincerity!'

Just as they were speaking thus, the moon that had been veiled tenfold and twentyfold revealed its form beyond the Sea of Fusa, casting its pale, brilliant radiance slanting through this forest.

Once more the faces of the companions became faintly discernible. From this moment forth, the emissaries of the Ural faith underwent a swift conversion into believers of Hinodewake-no-Mikoto, and awaiting the break of dawn, they set out marching toward the capital of Fusa, chanting sacred hymns along the way.
