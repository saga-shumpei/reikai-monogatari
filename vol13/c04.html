Chapter 4: The Curtain of Dreams [530]

第4章　夢の幕〔530〕

§ 1

The Tsuruyamamaru put into the port of Taru upon the Persian Gulf. Hinodewake, the emissary of divine revelation, disembarked here and proceeded forward toward the capital of the land of Fusa, chanting sacred hymns.

The six emissaries of the Ural faith—Iwahiko, Umehiko, Otohiko, Kamehiko, Komahiko, and Takahiko—exchanged winks with one another, disembarked at the port of Taru, and hastened their steps forward.

Showered with the sand and dust blown by the raging gale until they looked like cakes of muddy ash, and shouting orders for maximum march speed, they dashed like lightning toward the Grove of Shizu.

Hinodewake-no-Mikoto, entirely unconcerned by tempest or torrential rain, leisurely mounted a wild horse grazing in the open wilderness, riding steadily forward. As dusk began to fall, darkness deepened intensely until it became impossible to proceed even a single inch.

Thereupon Hinodewake abandoned the horse, kicking up clouds of dust upon his ancestral means of transportation—his own two feet—as he advanced toward the Grove of Shizu, spreading his straw raincoat beneath the shade of the trees to pass the night in slumber.

The Ural emissaries—Iwa, Ume, Oto, Kame, Koma, and Taka—hastened their march after disembarking at Taru and arrived at the Grove of Shizu.

Umehiko: 'Ah, at last this is the Grove of Shizu! Come, roll call: One, two, three, four, five...'

Umehiko: 'Is there no six? Hey, who is missing? Someone seems to have fallen behind along the way!'

Otohiko: 'Because we forced our march through the pitch-black road, that rascal Iwahiko ended up falling behind! What an empty boaster! As the saying goes, "Empty vessels make the greatest sound"; he has no real substance at all! Many words, little dignity. While we were thinking of submitting to the Anai teachings, that rogue Iwahiko kept barking his endless retorts until he forced an adjournment of our council, failing at everything; and in the meantime, the emissary Hinodewake disembarked upon this vast land of Fusa!'

Kamehiko: 'Whatever anyone says about him, he is hopeless! Convinced that none is greater than himself, all advice is like wind in a horse's ears; unless he suffers some terrible disaster, being an immune, unfeeling blockhead, he will never wake up! He probably went gliding off the precipitous crags of the Taru River and crash-landed at the bottom of the ravine. By now, with his propeller snapped and stranded at the bottom of the gorge, Iwahiko is likely clutching a boulder and lamenting, "Alas, silence is golden; would that I had never uttered those needless words aboard the Tsuruyamamaru!"'

Komahiko: 'We too made the voyage, did we not? Regret after the fact is useless! However, this is the Grove of Shizu, a notorious haunted lair where monsters (bake) dwell! If we do not stay alert, who knows what dreadful ordeal we may encounter! We cannot just shrug Iwahiko off; unless we search with all our might and locate his whereabouts, we cannot fulfill our duties as emissaries. Returning to Armenia, we certainly cannot report that such a huge boulder of an Iwahiko was misplaced, lost along the road, or worn down to dust!'

Umehiko: 'What is that? Speaking of "lost" and "abraded" as though he were a piece of baggage! Mind your Kototama a little!'

Komahiko: 'Well, well, let us call it a night for now, and set forth early tomorrow morning for a combined search and exploration. In this pitch darkness where one cannot even see one's own nose if pinched, how could we ever locate his whereabouts? We cannot endure a tragedy where the mummy hunters turn into mummies themselves!'

Takahiko: 'What cold-hearted fellows you are! Human sentiment is indeed as flimsy as paper!'

Umehiko: 'Hear him! We are messengers of the gods, yet in this deep night enveloped in dark clouds where one cannot see an inch ahead, no matter how much you worry, this scene—nay, this dark-scape—is at an utter standstill! There is simply no room to employ any strategy!'

Takahiko: 'Though dark, we nonetheless stand upon the soil of God's land created by God! No matter how many monsters haunt the Grove of Shizu, this too is a fragment of the Divine Realm. Let one person wait here while the other four search for Iwahiko!'

Otohiko: 'How sinister! To utter the ill-omened phrase "four men" (shinin, homophonous with "corpse") in the dead of night! Why must you say "four men"?'

Komahiko: 'Kototama is a strange thing! If things went wrong, Iwahiko might truly have become a corpse (shinin)—a fine divination omen! Well, what does it matter? Tomorrow is another day!'

Takahiko: 'Who cares if a mere emissary or two goes missing? That fellow's pride was far too swollen, so he is receiving divine punishment! Always acting the grand saint and blustering as though he could swallow the universe, he has no true substance! Let us rest here tonight and search tomorrow morning.'

Just as they were speaking thus, a booming gong-like roar that threatened the very fate of their eardrums echoed from the darkness.

Umehiko: 'Ah, it groans! It groans! We are in terrible danger!'

Otohiko: 'Hey! Who goes there, groaning in the dead of night? I am an emissary of the Groaning (Unaru) faith, a retainer of Lord Unaruhiko! Blow your conch horns, beat your drums, put on all your horsepower and tear your throat as much as you like!'

From the darkness came an even louder roar:

'Ooo-ho! Otohiko! Far from bursting the eardrums of Komahiko (steed), I shall roar with a piercing screech like Takahiko (hawk), so take good care that your livers do not burst!'

Takahiko: 'What monstrous creature has leapt forth? Hey, master booming-voice, whom do you take me for?'

The loud voice in the darkness thundered:

'Ye spineless cowards of the Ural faith, listen well! The sacred capital of Armenia is reduced almost to utter ruin; now merely the crooked spirits Miyamahiko and Kuniteruhime desperately defend their solitary stronghold—a pitiful sight indeed! The fortress of Armenia is already like a candle before the wind! Surrender swiftly unto the Anai teachings! Ahahahaha, ahahahaha!'

Then came a grating crane-like laughter completely disconnected from the five musical scales.

§ 4

Kamehiko: 'Hey, everyone! We must completely replan our campaign strategy now! Turning over a new leaf, whatever we believe to be good, we must carry through to the end. Since Iwahiko has vanished somewhere, why not surrender unto Hinodewake of the Anai teachings right here to save our lives?'

Otohiko: 'What is that weak, ruinous wailing, that delirious nonsense tinged with despair? Have you forgotten the sacred calling of an emissary?'

Kamehiko: 'Boundless transformation in propagation, appearing and vanishing without limit—that is the very mystery an emissary must employ! How could anyone navigate this world with your rigid pedantry? When in Rome, do as the Romans do; one can never prevail against the power of the times!'

Just as they were absorbed in this chatter, a blazing flash of brilliance suddenly burst forth. Looking up at the sky in shock, all five collapsed onto the ground with a terrified cry.

From amidst the colossal luminous glow emerged a hideous monster with an indescribably terrifying vermilion face, staring with glaring eyes shining like arc lamps, bearing upon the tip of its five-to-six-foot tongue what looked like a human severed head, thrusting itself right over the five men's heads!

Umehiko: 'Alas! Iwa... Iwa... Iwahiko has been done in!'

Otohiko: 'Hey, excuse me, Mr. Red-Face White-Eyes! That is far too cruel! Is not that head Iwahiko's head? That is going too far!'

Monster: 'I was so terribly bored that a yawn came forth! Just when I was wishing to feast on some warm human flesh, here are four or five rolled out before me! Ah, sweet, sweet! The Way of Heaven spares human life, but monsters devour human flesh—such is the design! Come, which of you shall I gobble up first?'
