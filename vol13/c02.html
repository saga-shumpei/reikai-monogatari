Chapter 2: The Persian Sea [528]

第2章　波斯の海〔528〕〔528〕

§ 1

The world had become utter perpetual darkness; mountains and rivers groaned, plants and trees withered, dense clouds blocked all four directions, and through the murky night where black and white could not be distinguished, Hinodewake, the divine child of the morning sun shining without a shadow, set forth. In order to illuminate the skies of the capital where foul demons nested in famed Armenia of Asia Minor, where the twisted spirits raged, and bearing the teachings of Princess Konohana revealed upon Mount Tenkyō, he reverently obeyed the divine words of the deities in heaven and earth. Embarking upon the eye-less wicker boat from the River of Manai, he sailed ever westward across the waves of the Indian Ocean, leaving behind the Crane Island and the Crane Haven, arriving at last upon the Sea of Persia.

From the Crane Haven, several dozen passengers joined the voyage. Owing to the sinking of Yomijima, the water level across the Persian Sea had suddenly risen, and the low-lying coastal plains were said to have suffered near-complete submergence.

Aboard the Tsuruyamamaru, transferred from the Hinodemaru, were Hinodewake-no-Mikoto and several missionaries of the Ural faith. Across the dim waters where day and night were indistinguishable, the vessel crept along as sluggishly as a slug.

First Emissary: 'We have thus crossed over unto Hitoshima in Ryūgū as missionaries of the Ural teachings, and nearly three years have passed. However, since Ii-yorihiko, the guardian deity of Hitoshima, is an unshakeably pious missionary, our long-cherished goal has vanished like water foam, just like Yomijima. What report can we possibly make upon returning to Armenia? Anxious about that alone, as we head northwest toward home, we feel somewhat faint-hearted and dispirited, do we not?'

Second Emissary: 'Having exerted our utmost strength to do our best as missionaries of the Ural faith, whatever anyone may say now, it cannot be helped, can it?'

First Emissary: 'Even if you say it cannot be helped, to go on an embassy to an enemy land and disgrace our sovereign's commission is no great honor for a man. Much less when, having received a special commission as six men to surround and assault Hitoshima from north, south, east, and west, we roll up our banners and execute a planned retreat before the single solitary Ii-yorihiko—that can hardly be called a splendid success. If we do not bring home some achievement, how can we face Lord Uralhiko?'

Third Emissary: 'Hey, Iwahiko! What were you saying when departing Armenia? Boasting insolently that rock-hard Iwahiko would subdue Yomijima with Kototama in the blinking of an eye, you were talking immensely big, were you not? Do you not remember that I told you then: "Your failure is clearly visible through my clairvoyance"?'

Iwahiko: 'Do not grumble over things like counting the years of a dead child! Mourning over past hardships is strictly forbidden, even outside the Anai teachings. Consider carefully: for hundreds of days, the doors of heaven have been slammed shut, so dark day and night that one could hardly tell them apart. Neither sunrise nor sunset could be distinguished. When we set forth from Armenia, in the morning a fresh air drifted about, five-colored draperies adorned the eastern sky from which the gold-rimmed sun emerged, and at evening ranges of agate-like clouds shone in the western sky, making the distinction of day and night exceedingly clear. But ever since we landed upon Hitoshima, an indescribably gloomy gloom—neither cloud, mist, nor fog—has suffocated heaven and earth, the earth shakes incessantly, demons swarm forth, and in such a cruel world where even the Ural missionaries of matter-over-spirit must retreat one, nay, a hundred steps, how could complete propagation be possible? However resourceful, benevolent, and valiant a commander may be, he can never prevail against the power of cosmic seasons! What sayest thou, Umehiko...?'

Just as they were speaking thus, a sudden typhoon roared forth like roaring tigers and leaping dragons; furious raging billows brandished their white spray, biting into the vessel's hull. The ship groaned ominously from above, below, left, and right, rocking violently, pitching and rolling through mountain-like troughs of waves as it drifted northwest. The curtain of darkness descended, pitch-black without distinction between black and white, when suddenly from the darkness, overwhelming the clamor of the tempest, a sacred song rang forth:

'God hath appeared at the forefront to divide good from evil! Uralhiko and Uralhime appeared upon Mount Caucasus, establishing a counterfeit divine palace to deceive the world; that they once enshrined the Three Imperial Deities has turned into a faded dream of the past! Now merely taking the twisted spirits Miyamahiko and Kuniteruhime as their guardian deities, they crossed over unto the land of Tokoyo, sending their subordinate deities unto Hitoshima floating amidst the billows of the ocean—that treasure isle—searching every mountain ridge and river rapid to spread the Ural teachings; yet despite exhausting their innermost hearts, all was in vain!'

'How pitiful that they have vanished like foam, even as did the Isle of Yomi! Leaving behind the sacred ground of Mount Tenkyō, where the teachings of Karen-sō were opened in accordance with the divine words of the Great Deity Kami-Izanagi manifest in the High Plain of Heaven, embarking upon the ship of the Rising Sun, leaving behind the Tsugaru Strait, parting the waves of the Heavenly Well of Manai, arriving at last upon the Indian Ocean to receive deep divine grace; sailing out from the auspicious Crane Island and the Crane Haven linked to the pine age of the Gods, now advancing into the Persian Gulf, here unexpectedly encounter the missionaries of Uralhiko: Iwahiko, Umehiko, and four others!'

'By what divine dispensation have we become passengers aboard the Tsuruyamamaru, sharing a single lotus upon the waters? The sky of the Supreme Deity's heart is overcast! Ah, Iwahiko! Umehiko! Hinodewake, the divine minister of the rising morning sun, a branch spirit of the Rising Sun Deity spreading the teachings of Princess Konohana revealed upon Mount Tenkyō unto all four quarters, now points toward Persia; gazing upon the celestial sky as we set out upon white waves, it looks as though ink had been spilled; gazing upon the vast ocean, it looks as though mud had been poured forth; upon these very waves we meet!'

By the divine words of the Great Imperial Deities, in the breath of Kototama chanted in full detail by Hinodewake, cleanse your souls! No sooner had the song ended than the furious tempest and raging billows were calmed as if wiped away; the sea became flat as woven mats, shimmering with gentle ripples like fish scales.

The people aboard the ship were so astonished by this voice that they could not utter a single word, merely staring in awe at the divine virtue of Hinodewake-no-Mikoto. Iwahiko whispered in a low voice:

'Hey, Umehiko, Otohiko, Kamehiko! Are we not in terrible trouble? A formidable fellow is aboard, dealing us a crushing blow! He is but one, while we are half a dozen missionaries; to have such divine power displayed before the eyes of all passengers leaves the Ural faith completely stripped of face! If we do not exact revenge somehow, it will be disaster piled upon failure! If that Anai missionary named Hinodewake lands in Persia and advances with such momentum toward the sacred capital of Armenia, it will be catastrophic!'
