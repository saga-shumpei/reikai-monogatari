Chapter 15: The Lotus Blossoms Open [541]

第15章　蓮花開〔541〕

§ 1

Otohiko, Kamehiko, and Komahiko pressed forward northwestward across the desolate moor. Suddenly, from amidst the tall grasses as high as a man, five or six uncanny fellows emerged, spreading their arms wide and hemming them in on all sides, shouting:

'Villains! Whosoever ye be, what do ye mean by trespassing upon this ground? Throw down all your possessions upon this very spot and strip yourselves stark naked! If ye do so, we shall spare your lives!'

Otohiko: 'What insolence babblest thou, thou wretched thief! With so colossal a frame, that thou canst not earn a living without robbing others—what an ill-starred birth is thine! Who under heaven art thou?'

The man: 'I am Lord Noro-kō the Boa Constrictor!'

Otohiko: ''Tis small wonder thou art sluggish (noro-noro) as a snail! Harbor no such wicked intention as eating in idle ease without labor in these bitter times!'

Noro-kō: 'What antiquated nonsense art thou preaching? I am a modern man of the very latest fashion! In these times, is there a single creature without a thieving spirit? The world is wholly given over to ogres and brigands! And what art thou, swaggering as a missionary, the very acme of hypocrisy? Bearing a face long as a horse with a hammock slung beneath it... Nay, not only thy face, but thy hands and legs are long; thou art surely of the kin of Tenaga-hiko and Nagasune-hiko!'

Otohiko: 'Cease thy babble, and listen calmly to my missionary hymn!'

Noro-kō: 'Thou art a missionary who goeth about tyrannizing mankind, spouting of God, of the Way, of good, and of evil! Thy brain is hopelessly out of date! In an age wherein popular movements flourish so exceedingly, conservative babble will never pass current! We intend to coil up fellows like thee as with a capstan and sweep them away at a stroke! Deem us not common thieves; we are men of far superior standing!'

Otohiko: 'Alack, what a perilous moor! 'Tis as though a dreadnought battleship were navigating through a sea littered with drifting mines! ...Hearken, drifting mines! We too possess a minesweeper; shall we blow you to pieces?'

Noro-kō: 'Humph! What nonsense babblest thou? Bearing a nose like a ventilator, supremely grotesque; fierce of breath, yet faint-hearted as an empty oil-can that rattleth at the slightest wind! What, wast thou not plunged into the depths of a pond by a toad?'

Otohiko: 'What! Plunged into a pond by a toad? How dost thou know thereof?'

§ 2

Noro-kō: 'Ha-ha-ha-ha! Wherefore is it said that thy rating as a missionary is an absolute zero!'

Otohiko: 'If that be so, who in truth art thou?'

Noro-kō: 'The corporeal manifestation of that very toad is Lord Noro the Boa Constrictor!'

Otohiko: 'Is it so? If thou art a frog, thou oughtest to live naked! Why dost thou need our garments? ...Hey, friend Kame, friend Koma, wake up! Leaving me alone to parley while ye stand aloof—are ye content with that? What utterly cold-blooded fellows ye are!'

Kamehiko and Komahiko: 'Friend Oto, by the karma of thy soul thou wast born to confront such crises! As for us, the hindmost crow shall be foremost; watch our future deeds! We shall take part in the final divine work and display peerless glory!'

Otohiko: 'Spouting words like a twentieth-century Anai missionary—what sluggish idlers ye are! No rice-cake will ever fall from the shelf no matter how long ye wait! Gaze upon the true principle of heaven and earth: 'tis only by enduring frost and snow that the plum blossom bloometh in spring! And because it bloometh, it beareth fruit! Dost thou imagine that the flower of sincerity can bloom without labor?'

Noro-kō: 'Hold! Divert not your quarrel upon me! What will ye do with me? Settle the matter with me first!'

Otohiko: 'Hold thy tongue! Maintain strict neutrality for a while!'

Noro-kō: 'Our band mustereth six men, whilst your company is but three! Even if we stripped you three bare, 'twould be too short for a sash and too long for a cord! It cannot be helped; for this once we shall let you go! Henceforth take heed and repent!'

Otohiko: 'Ha-ha-ha! What insolent jesting! The thief is spouting the very words that we ought to say!'

Noro-kō: 'He who striketh first conquereth; thy guardian deity possessed me and uttered those words!'

Otohiko: 'An utterly incomprehensible fellow! In truth, what art thou?'

Noro-kō: 'What an obstinate interrogator! Noro is noro: I am the Noro who curseth (norou) such carefree fellows as thee—materialists who regard the cosmos as their private domain!'

Otohiko: 'All groweth utterly incomprehensible! At all events, supposing me to be capital and you labor, why not convene a Council for Capital-Labor Cooperation in the midst of this wilderness? Since 'tis a wild proposal on a wild moor, a capital scheme may emerge!'

§ 3

Noro-kō: 'Ha-ha-ha! Ye are missionaries wholly blind! If ye cannot even divine my true nature, all is in vain! Exploration within the Cavern of Demons is utterly beyond your power!'

Otohiko: 'Hearken, Noro! Doubtless thou art no ordinary mortal, but a wondrous monster; yet through unpreventable necessity our exploration of the cavern was interrupted, wherefore we altered our course toward the capital of Fusa! Why not lend us thy counsel and instruct us?'

Noro-kō: 'Instruct thee? Where under heaven dost thou imagine this place to be?'

Otohiko: ''Tis a matter of course: is it not the moor of Furu before the Takao mountains?'

Noro-kō: 'Behold thy folly! This is the very central hub of the Cavern of Demons!'

Rubbing his eyes and scrutinizing the surroundings, Otohiko beheld subterranean caverns radiating in all eight directions.

Otohiko: 'Hey, Kame, Koma! How appeareth it to you?'

Kamehiko: 'Indeed, it feeleth somewhat like the interior of a cavern!'

Komahiko: 'Verily, we seem to have been slumbering in a daze; can it be a dream?'

Noro-kō: 'Fare ye well...!'

Even as he spoke, Noro-kō and his five companions vanished, and the cavern was utterly enveloped in billows of white smoke. Straightway a luminous, radiant orb appeared.

Komahiko: 'Lo, a wondrous marvel hath manifested! Be on your guard! None can measure what strange occurrences may follow; let us firmly establish the Heavenly Pillar within our souls that our foundation be not shaken!'

Otohiko: 'Thou art over-sensitive, ever brooding upon distant perils! All is kannagara—spontaneous in the eternal now! We can know nothing until we reach the end; borrow not trouble!'

Komahiko: 'Behold, look yonder! Within that luminous orb a fair countenance is visible! 'Tis the very likeness of Konohana-sakuya-hime!'

Otohiko: 'Verily, she is wondrously fair, radiant as cherry blossoms in full splendor! Compared with Noro-kō of a moment ago, she refresheth the spirit!'

In an instant, the maiden's form was fully revealed; beckoning with her hand and casting a glance upon the three men, she fled swiftly into the gloom.

Otohiko: 'Aha, how wondrous! Though enveloped in white smoke we have lost her trace, we must search out her footsteps, meet her once more, and ascertain the truth of this marvel!'

Kamehiko: 'Imagining her to be a beauty, thou mayest find her an ogre with four eyes! When that hour arriveth, crying "Nay, she is no longer nice!" will profit thee nothing once thou art swallowed whole!'

Otohiko: '"Autumn waves" is the phrase of antiquated dotards! If that be not electric waves, what else could it be?'

Kamehiko: 'Ha-ha-ha! Having entered such an uncanny cavern of demons, this is no place to indulge in such wanton conceits! An inch of good is beset by a foot of evil; none knoweth what monsters may appear! Guard thy conduct!'

Otohiko: 'Ah, traveling with stubborn fools maketh me sick at heart: beriberi riseth to my head, hysteria descendeth to my feet, my feet ache with headache, and my head throbbeth with stomachache—'tis utterly wretched! Come, let us press on!'

Otohiko: 'That is baseless anxiety! Harmonizing severity and gentleness, flexible and adaptable, boundless in transformation—only thus can the divine mission be accomplished! Cold-blooded statues wearing scores of votive bibs can never redeem a corrupted society!'
