Chapter 20: Transformation of Speech [546]

第20章　宣替〔546〕

§ 1

Leaving the mansion of Princess Garyō behind, Otohiko, Kamehiko, and Komahiko set forth once more upon the exploration of the cavern. Chanting missionary hymns, they marched boldly along the winding subterranean path, now broad, now narrow, with ceilings now lofty and now low.

'The Deity hath appeared openly in the world / To sever good from evil asunder! / Kaminaho-bi who fashioned this cosmos, / And Ōnaho-bi whose heart is vast— / In all things throughout the mortal realm, / Restore by looking and listening aright through Naho-bi, / And amend every transgression through speech! / To subdue the crooked spirits of the Cavern of Demons / And establish the divine realm firm as rock, / We three missionaries advanced, / Yet ere we knew it, clouds of pride enveloped us, / Our hearts wandered down paths of gloom, / Straying from the way of true sincerity! / Dreaming dreams within a dream, / Puffed up in spirit, / We were taken aboard the Sacred Camphor Ship / And soared high unto the ninth heaven; / Yet scarce had we rejoiced,'

'Our joy dissolved, and in a dream-like flash / We were lost upon the desolate moor! / Encountering mysterious Noro upon the lonely plain, / We knew not that within his harsh words / Lay concealed the jewel-voice of divine grace! / Squaring our elbows in haughty pride, / How ashamed are we of our folly! / Dream or waking or fleeting phantom? / We whose hearts were darkened / Plunged once more into the black cavern / Where none could tell black from white! / Breaking through the countless goblin spirits / Swarming before and behind, to right and left, / We reached at last the bright three-forked road, / And collided unawares with a heavy blow! / Bitter was the pain in the dark chest! / Encountering mysterious Yajihiko / And sober yet foolish Yotahiko,'

'Before the stone portal that refused to open, / By the sacred Kototama of the Amatsu-Norito / It swung wide asunder! / Gazing within, an endless corridor stretched afar; / Dashed we headlong down the hall, / Marching endlessly without bound! / Even as our eager spirits hastened forward, / Brought to a sudden halt by a solid rock wall, / We looked up in astonishment—what marvel is this? / Unnumbered stars twinkled in the vault of heaven! / Even as we wondered in awe, / A figure surpassing jewels in beauty— / Whence came she, the maiden Izumo-hime? / Bidding us advance unto the capital of Fusa, / She walked ahead to guide our steps, / Leading us three missionaries / Unto the foot of the Koshi Pass!'

'Arriving there, behold what wonder: / The apostle Hinode-wake, / With Takahiko, Iwahiko, and Umehiko, / Lay upon the colossal boulder / Wrapped in deep and blissful slumber! / Yet scarce had we rejoiced, / Awakened by the roar of a sudden tempest, / Looking round, behold what horror: / Seated within the inner chamber of Princess Garyō, / Urged by the noble goddess to partake of a feast / Of lizards, earthworms, serpents, frogs, / And hideous slugs! / Even as we hesitated in revulsion, / Our bodies were bound fast as with iron fetters, / Bereft of freedom of hand and foot! / In that hour of utter desperation, / When we resigned our souls unto death, / Lo, Princess Garyō in an instant'

'Disclosed a radiant, gentle smile, / And proclaimed flawless virtuous words and beautiful speech! / In the boundless joy of her sacred counsel, / The dreams of pride dissolved away, / The pristine spirit of Naho-bi shone forth, / And emerging from the hall, / Transformed into the sacred soul of Izunome, / We missionaries of the Anai teachings / Cast off all slanderous speech! / Proclaiming the sacred, refined words of God, / How valiant our renewed march! / Whether the morning sun shine or be clouded, / Whether the moon wax or wane, / Yea, though the earth itself sink into the deep, / And though the cavern swarm with myriads of demons, / By the sacred Kototama bestowed by the Deity / We shall subdue them all with peace! / Weaving the horizontal and vertical threads / Of the Anai teachings like a brocade upon the loom, / We shall serve the divine mission!'

'Ah, how wondrous, how joyful! / Our hearts leap along the rocky path! / Arousing the Yamato soul, / Keener and firmer than rock, / Uttering the fierce war-shout, stamping our feet, / Let us march forward along the Way of the Gods, / Yea, forward along the Way of the Gods!'

§ 2

Even as they sang, they reached a crossroad within the cavern. At that moment, three men emerged from ahead:

Iwahiko: 'Ho! If it be not Oto, Kame, and Koma! Where have ye been idling, ye fools? We three have searched for your trace, circling these crossroads times without number! What in the world made ye linger so?'

Otohiko: 'Ah, is it indeed Master Iwahiko? We are deeply, deeply grieved to have caused you such anxious concern! We were taken aboard the Sacred Rock-Ship of Lord Hinode-wake together with you all, soaring high into the clouds in boundless joy; yet unawares, upon the overgrown, desolate moor we three were shaken down! Until this very instant we were in dire anxiety concerning the fate of you three noble gentlemen; but beholding your safe and sound countenances, there could be no greater joy! For this divine reunion brought about by the Deity of the Anai teachings, we offer our heartfelt thanks!'

Iwahiko: 'What is this? Adopting such exaggeratedly polite language, mock me not! Dost thou not know that excess of courtesy turneth into insolence? Thy sudden transformation—hast thou gone mad, or art thou a goblin? What an incomprehensible rogue! Say, Ume, this fellow is wondrously cracked!'

Umehiko: 'Aye, look upon the faces of the three! Malnourished, pale as ghosts, emaciated like hungry fiends! Intoxicated by the cavern gases, they must have lost their wits!'

Otohiko: 'Noble Master Iwa, Master Ume, pray give yourselves no concern! We suffer from no mental derangement whatsoever! We are missionaries of the Anai teachings; is it not so, friend Kame, friend Koma? We have not lost our senses in the slightest!'

Kamehiko: 'Truly, truly so! Master Iwa and Master Ume show exceeding kindness in their concern, but there is no abnormality whatever; pray be at ease!'

Iwahiko: 'Hey, Ume, Taka! 'Tis ever more unnatural! Like spoiled courtesans, they have suddenly turned sickeningly polite! Oto, Kame, Koma—are ye mocking us? Insolent knaves!'

Kamehiko: 'Nay, heaven forbid! How could we ever mock august missionaries of the Anai teachings and face the Deity? We are humble servants of God sent to proclaim the Anai teachings throughout the world; how could we behave with disrespect before your noble presence?'

Iwahiko: 'What comical fellows! Why hast thou turned so suddenly effeminate? Canst thou not display a vigorous, masculine spirit, using rough, hearty speech and shouting boldly? Bereft of martial courage, how canst thou ever subdue the demons?'

Otohiko: 'Yea, for grave reasons, we three have undergone sincere repentance!'

Iwahiko: 'Repentance doth not make a man so faint-hearted and weak! Under the divine protection of God, one must soar boldly across the world in vibrant vigor! Thou art intoxicated with kannagara: "kannagara" if it rain, "kannagara" if the wind blow, "kannagara" if a thief enter—can such a fellow ever serve as an Anai missionary?'

§ 3

So saying, he clenched his fists knotty as turban-shells and rained rapid blows upon their heads.

Otohiko: 'How gracious of thee to show such benevolent concern! We pray that thou wilt admonish us many times hereafter!'
