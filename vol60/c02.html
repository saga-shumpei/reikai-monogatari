Chapter 2: Kami Forest [1527]

神森〔1527〕

§ 1

Deep in the valleys of Mount Azumosu, the Sendenshi of Tamakuniwake, who leads many Kikori and Shiyōjiyō, travels here and there to perform the Ananahi. Selecting suitable Riyōzai, he worships Yamaguchigami, offers the roots and tops to the Sanrei, and fells the central trunk of the three-chestnut tree to serve as timber for a precious Miyai. The sound of saws goes 'gui-gui-gui,' and the voices of axes, 'chiyon-chiyon, kan-kan,' resound among the Kodama. The voices singing here and there, and the Kuwaisei of the spirited monkeys, make the scene appear as if it were a Senjiyō. Watching this Kuōkei, the couple Bachiru clap their hands, and how joyous it is to celebrate the Kensetsu of the Tengoku Jiyōdo.

Bachiru: 'How joyous it is, that from the days of the ancient Kamiyo, while serving as the lord of Rishiyō and concurrently holding the office of Kami, we have protected our home as it was in the Kamiyo, respecting the Brahman Kami, in the village of the Kami where the heart is also Suma, at the southern foot of Mount Azumosu. My late father, Bachiku, having become accustomed to the blessings of the noble Kami, thought lightly of the Kami who dwells in the forest of Tennō. Having taken the life of Shiyōjiyōhiko, the messenger of the Kami, he incurred divine punishment and finally, alas, lost his life before his natural span was complete. The soul of Shiyōjiyōhiko makes my body its palace, and strictly commands me to rebuild the shrine in the forest of Tennō, to build the palace pillars firmly upon the bottom rock, to repent for the sins committed by my late father, and to serve with firmness and constancy as a monk of Mount Azumosu. Having obtained the permission of Tamakuniwake, I joyfully fell the trees in the forest of the Kami, where no blade has ever entered since the Kamiyo, and serve by making them the pillars of the palace. How joyous is this day of my birth.'

The strong men of the Abāwikurāmin, who have each received the divine power of Ananwiikurā, appear from early in the morning, and seeing them work with twisted headbands in great activity, it is known to be Kannagara, the work of the Kami.

Anikushibutazura, working like a great wheel, sweating profusely as he exerts himself—Tamakuniwake is the Torairō-Kyabokurā, a Sendenshi rare in the world. The Mizumitama appears in the hall of the Ubuna to build the divine country of Maitorēya. Appearing on this sacred mountain where the Būruna-Chandora shines, he exerts himself in Nitteyōdeyukuta, and how valiant he is as he selects the pillars of the Kami one by one. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if the earth should sink, the way of the Kami is but one sincerity. How could one fear any kind of evil?

This palace, which the Kami has directed, is built like the Mahāwiyūba of the Tengoku Jiyōdo, and it shall surely save the people of the world for all eternity. From now on, morning and evening, I shall save and assist all, from the Aohitogusa to the birds, beasts, insects, and fish, with the heart of Maitorēya, receiving the Sudaruma of the teachings of the Kami and the Shikin purely into my body, and I vow and pray to the Kami. My soul was in Avudoyā, and I had been inclined to fish, forgetting and mistaking the true essence of the Four Noble Truths of suffering, accumulation, extinction, and the path, but having been given the trial of the Kami, how happy I am to have been reborn as a completely new person.

Purifying my heart to master the teachings of the Ananahi-kyō, I shall preach and instruct the people of the world in the Four Noble Truths—Samudaya, Jifuka, Nirōza, and Māru—and return all material treasures to society. Without distinction between rich and poor, noble and humble, we shall pass through this world happily and joyfully according to the will of the Kami. The pure and innocent Shiyōjiyōhiko and Shiyōjiyōhime, serving the Kami, keep only their bodies to protect one another; they do not possess the treasures of this world, but rejoice in the blessings of heaven and earth, living and prospering in this world. Even the birds that fly in the sky, the hundred beasts that dwell in the mountains, the scaly tribes, and even the grasses and trees of the fields never privately own treasure.

§ 2

Therefore, with a pure heart, following the laws of Ametsuchi and Shizen, rejoicing in the spring of Tokoshihe blessings, and living through this world with such Kenage spirit; when I think of this, we have privately owned Kiyoman wealth, monopolized the blessings of Ametsuchi, and becoming Rishiyō, have felt the bitterness of having boasted to the people of the world.

Now, from this moment on, I shall wipe away all Shifuchiyakushin, and while moving to build a Sauan on Mount Adsumos and living there in harmony with Saber-hime, I shall make the residence handed down by my Fuso a garden where the Satobito may rest their Shinshin from Tsukare.

At the very least, I shall atone for the Tsumitoga of my Fuso, and I pray, prostrating myself before the Mimahe of the Ōkami, that I may be allowed to serve the Shingefu of 'Saruvārō Katātsu-badaravuōdo vaēkaburachiyutsuchīru (relieving all the sufferings of the world) and live in Kamunagara, and I pray that the Ōkami may protect me.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, may the Mitama be blessed.

Teku put on a Nejihachimaki and began to sing while supervising the many Araotoko woodcutters.

Teku: 'The world is changing, destroying the despotic system, and becoming a new age of humanity; Socialism and Communism are rising both inside and outside the seas, the clouds of Ametsuchi have completely changed, and the time has come to build the heavenly pure land upon the earth.

The master of this house, Bachiru, having quickly realized the trend of world thought, has demonstrated Socialism and distributed Kiyoman wealth to the Satobito quite equally, and has demonstrated the utmost sincerity in living in the world with the upper and lower classes in harmony.

Is this truly the manifestation of the equal love of the Imperial Kami, or is it the awakening of man, or perhaps a gift of the times?

It is truly noble beyond measure; if one were to stubbornly continue to monopolize the treasures of the earth as in the past, one would end up as the target of the resentment of the four classes, and I cannot fathom what kind of disaster might visit; it is a critical moment that is difficult to measure.

You have reformed so well; this is truly my master; preventing the appearance of Anarchism, Nihilism, and a hundred other isms and factions, and while opening this beautiful Ametsuchi in complete safety, you have appeared as Shōjōhiko, the pioneer who enjoys and celebrates the age of the Maitreya Kami, and I, on behalf of the people of the world, respectfully thank you for the true heart of Shōjō-hime.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, I look up to, revere, and celebrate the preciousness of the grace of the Mitama.'

Mitsuhiko: 'All things born within the Ametsuchi of truth, goodness, beauty, and love, simply Kamunagara, Kamunagara, are immersed in the blessings of the noble Kami and flourish purely.

The forest of Mount Adsumos is a sacred place where no blade has been allowed to enter since the distant age of the Kami, a mountain of the Kami; the treetops are deep green, evergreen and firm, and it is a great pillar with both flowers and fruits, where eagles build their nests and hawks dwell.

On the branches of the pine, cranes dwell, and even the owls and scops owls that cannot see in the daylight have gathered and decided it as their home for a thousand generations.

Seeing the way they are surprised and flee in a frenzy at the momentum of the felling of trees, striking and swinging the forbidden hoes and forbidden axes at the trunks of trees here and there, I cannot help but shed tears of sympathy.

However, if the shrine of the Ōkami is built, the hundred birds and thousand birds will be encouraged, and as before, they will easily enjoy the heaven-sent blessings and spend their days peacefully in this heaven forever.

The descendants of the soul of Shōjōhiko, who had hidden their figures until now, the three hundred and thirty-three Kenzoku-sama, have returned to their home of a thousand generations, jumping and leaping across the treetops, and bravely raising their voices of joy, crying "kyatsu, kyatsu, kyatsu."

They have given birth to many children of the Chin, and if they meet the spring of Tokoshihe where the flower of joy blooms in the pure and innocent land of the Kami, then be brave, be brave, Satobito; celebrate, celebrate, Shōjō-sama.

May the Ananai-kyō, the masters of the Brahman teachings, and the believers all wipe away the dust of their hearts, live in harmony and intimacy forever, thank the blessings of the Kami, and from today, completely break away from the realm of asceticism and heresy, and may you spend this world happily and joyfully, Kamunagara.

I, Mitsuhiko of the teachings of the Chin, reveal my pure heart and, before the Mimahe of the Kami, respectfully and fearfully pray for the safety of the future of the world.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, how noble is the figure of the age of the Kami.'

Mount Adsumos is the noble Sumeru, and soon the countries of the four directions will shine.
