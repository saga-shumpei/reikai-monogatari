Chapter 10: Jewel 国 [1535]

玉国〔1535〕

§ 1

Guided by Itahiko-tsukasa, escaping from three thousand years of confinement, Takushaka-Riuō ascended from the darkness of the thousand-fathom deep earth. Using the art of Henge to take on a human form, with a face brimming with smiles, he climbed the stairs of the Ihato before the place where the Kami were enshrined at the sacred site of Mount Azumosu, appearing with a nobility that felt as if he were ascending to heaven.

The party of Tamakuniwake, while praising the achievements of Itahiko-tsukasa to the utmost, turned toward Takushaka-Riuō and spoke with gentle words.

Tamakuniwake: 'As a Sendenshi of the path of the Oshihe of Ananahi, which Kunitokotachi-no-Ōmikami and Toyokunihime-no-Ōkami have opened, I, Tamakuniwake-no-Kamuzukasa, having received the Mikoto of the Kami, have set out for the capital of Haruna. While on my way, manipulated by the threads of the Shigumi of the Sumekami, I came to Mount Azumosu in the village of Suma. When I looked upon this place, I realized that the time had come to save your spirit, which Tsukiteruhiko-no-Ōkami had hidden away three thousand years ago in order to govern this world in peace. I, too, having been forgiven for my own deep sins, which you so bravely bore, have appeared here in this form. Watching this scene, I could not hold back tears of joy, and before I knew it, I was wringing my sleeves. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Listen well, Takushaka-Riuō. Haniyasuhiko-no-Mikoto and Haniyasuhime, who appeared to cleanse the mud of this world, are the divine pillars of strictness and harmony. Since they began the management of the warp and woof of the universe, this has been a land of the Kami where not even water can leak through. From now on, you too must keep your heart pure and righteous. Serve the great divine work of the original Ancestor-Ōkami. I perceive the suffering you endured while lurking in the deep earth for those three thousand years.'

Takushaka-Riuō, having bathed in the light of the surface world after a long time, and seeing the rare faces of people and the lush, verdant scenery of the surrounding trees, could not contain his joy and replied to Tamakuniwake with a poem.

Takushaka-Riuō: 'I am Takushaka, known as the leader of the Eight Great Riuō, an evil Kami with nine heads and two tongues. Once I let my eyes shine, all trees and grasses wither; once I raise my voice, mountains, fields, rivers, and seas tremble. Even the noble Ōkami were troubled by me, but the Mikoto of the Kami, Tsukiteruhiko-no-Ōkami, who appeared as an angel of peerless divine power, descended from heaven. Without allowing for any argument, he bound me spiritually with the breath of Kototama and sealed me until now in the depths of the earth of Mount Azumosu. Now that it has come to this, how could I still love evil? I shall take the great heart of the infinitely merciful Ōkami as my own heart, and with all my heart and body, I shall protect everything, even down to the dew on a single leaf of the grasses and trees of the living world. The night-shining jewel that I had hidden as my treasure was tucked deep into the bosom of Itahiko, and now it has appeared here in this place. To manifest the sincerity of Takushaka-Riuō's repentance, I offer this jewel, which allowed me to freely control wind, water, and fire. Please, I beg you, accept it. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if the earth should break, the Kototama I have once sworn to the Kami shall never move. Please grant this, Kannagara, before the presence of Tamakuniwake, I humbly and respectfully pray.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Are you the Riuō whom I heard was the ancestor of the Yamata-no-Orochi that disturbs the world? It is interesting. You are a Kami who has repented from the bottom of your heart and returned the jewel. With words that hold not a drop of falsehood, I shall gladly accept the jewel. Itahiko-no-Oshihe-tsukasa has purely finished serving the divine work of the Ōkami.'

Itahiko: 'My spirit was weak, useless, and powerless, yet I have finished my service according to the will of the Kami.'

Wakkusu: 'Following after Itahiko-tsukasa, I have seen such a terrifying dream. Yet, seeing this current joy, my heart has become courageous before I knew it.'

Eru: 'How foolish of me to have been terrified, blocked by an unexpected ugly evil Kami. Yet, I am happy that we have returned safely together with Itahiko-tsukasa.'

Masumihiko: 'How shameful it is that I only thought of Itahiko as a person whose heart was calm.'

Sanzenhiko: 'I am reminded of the saying that even wood shavings have their time of use. Do not look down on me as a man whose Kototama is muddy; I, too, am one who has been mocked many times.'

§ 2

Itahiko: 'While it is said to be Kamunagara, I envy you for being able to proceed while taking your younger sister along.'

Debisuhime: 'The achievements of Itahiko-Oshihetsukasa surpass the work of opening the Ihato.'

Bachiru: 'The Ihato of this Miyama, which has been handed down as the place where the demons have hidden since ancient times, has been opened.'

Saberuhime: 'I never even dreamed in my dreams that such a pure-spirited Kami existed. How happy I am today to have welcomed you, having been taught by the Shiyōjiyōhime-Mikoto.'

Takushaka: 'From now on, I shall change my name to Shiyōjiyōokina and serve this Miyama for a long time.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Serve this palace peacefully until Chiyo, Yachiyo, and Yorozuyo.'

Chiruteru: 'How strange that the Shiyōjiyō-hiko and Shiyōjiyōhime should appear, and that Shiyōjiyōokina should manifest. Although I heard you were the ugly Magami of the Kuzuriu, your form is truly that of a Kami.'

As they sang this, the Daichi suddenly shook, the waves of the Kiyome Lake raged, and the lake split into two with a snap. From within, the truly terrifying Sagara-Riuō emerged, floating onto the Hamabe of Suma while carrying a girl of about seven or eight years old on his back. Suddenly, he transformed into the figure of an Ōna, holding a lovely Euji, and while wrapped in mist, he soared through the Chiukū and appeared before Takushaka-Riuō.

Sagara: 'Enduring three thousand years of suffering, the lord on my back has finally appeared in the world. This child was born from my own Mitama and is the incarnation of the Nyoi-no-Hōju.'

Takushaka: 'Meeting you, my Mikoto, whom I have longed for, fills my heart with joy on this day after three thousand years. Through the Oshihetsukasa of the Kami of Tamakuni and the people, I have been saved and received the forgiveness of the Kami.'

Sagara: 'Since you, my Mikoto, have appeared in the world, I too shall take the form of a human and serve. Oshihetsukasa of Tamakuniwake and all you people, please have mercy on this couple.'

Tamakuniwake: 'A couple with deep ties from long ago, may you protect the world forever.'

Sagara-Riuō set the girl of about seven or eight years old, whom he had been holding at his side, onto the ground, and the couple blew water and fire upon each other. Instantly, the girl transformed into the jewel of the Nyoi-Hōju. Sagara-Riuō held it up reverently,

Sagara: 'This jewel, which I held morning and evening, is the Nyoi-no-Hōju; I shall offer it to you, my lord.'

Tamakuniwake: 'I, who bear the name of Tamakuniwake-Kami-no-Mikoto, have obtained two jewels. If these two treasures are gathered in my hands, how could I fear even the Daikokunushi?'

Masumihiko: 'The name of my master is now known; please rename yourself Tamamoriwake.'

Sanchihiko: 'Rather than Tamamoriwake, please rename yourself Tamatoriwake-Kami, my master.'

Tamakuniwake: 'For one who has received the Kunitama on the right and the left, it is good to call myself Tamakuniwake.'

Itahiko: 'The essential jewel has become the property of my master, and I, Itahiko, have bitten my finger to seal it.'

Debisuhime: 'Why do you not call yourself Tamatoriwake? The name Itahiko-Oshihetsukasa is poor indeed.'

Itahiko: 'Now that it has come to this, there is no way to rename myself, as it is a title bestowed by the Kami.'

Masumihiko: 'It is the teaching of the Kami that we are to perform the work of the spirits of our connection. Even if I bear the name Masumi-no-Hiko of the Kototama, what would happen if my soul were to become clouded? I, too, shall polish the soul of my heart and try to emulate my master.'

From this time on, Takushaka-Riuō transformed into a human body and became Shiyōjiyōokina, while Sagara-Riuō became Shiyōjiyō-ōna. They made wine from rare fruits and offered it to the Kami every morning and evening to soothe the divine heart and apologize for their own sins. The Bachiru couple served as the priests of the two palaces forever, their descendants prospered, and they were respected in the world as pillars of the Kami. Furthermore, the Brahmin Chiruteru couple built a residence in a corner of Bachiru's mansion, became the village officials of the village of Suma, served the Kami devoutly, and cherished the villagers. Their subordinates, Kanna and Heru, were taken as house servants, and all others were added to the ranks of the villagers. They built a beautiful new village and spent the rest of their lives happily. Their spirits reached the heavenly realm, joined the ranks of the heavenly beings, and came to guard the sacred ground of Mount Azumosu.
