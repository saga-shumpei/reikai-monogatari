Chapter 1: Pure Clean 車 [1526]

清浄車〔1526〕

§ 1

The Kiyome Lake, measuring one hundred Hiyakuri from east to west and two hundred Hiyakuri from north to south, with a total area of twenty thousand square Hiyakuri, reflects the light like a mirror, floating twelve islands of various sizes. Mount Terumon, appearing as if blurred amidst the clouds in the northern direction, peers into the water mirror.

The great sun, resembling a Manjiugasa, begins to emerge from the waves in the east, and as it climbs the staircase of five-colored clouds, it gradually diminishes in size. The Satsusatsu morning breeze of summer cools the faces of the people, and the tranquility is such that the sails flutter back and forth. Sūman of the Chōzoku soar back and forth over the lake, and their voices, rejoicing and celebrating the sunrise, seem as if they might reach the Kiuten. On the Suma beach of white sand and green pines, Rauniyakunanniyo of all ages and genders are gathered in crowds like mountains, reflecting their shadows upside down in the lake.

The twenty Shōjō boats led by Itahiko approach the shore amidst cheers of Banzai. The crowd standing on the shore, wearing turmeric-colored headbands and red sashes, and holding drums, surigane, flutes, shō, hichiriki, kakko, and gekkin, display their respective exquisite skills to celebrate the safe return of Itahiko and his party.

The freshwater lake region lacks the unpleasant scent of the tide; the wind carries a fragrant aroma, and the unheard music purifies the ears of the people. The heaven is clear, the sea is blue, and the earth is also pure; heaven, fire, water, and earth are both very quiet and very lively, truly manifesting an ideal heaven and reaching the pinnacle of truth, goodness, beauty, and love. Not a single speck of dust remains between heaven and earth or in the hearts of men; the atmosphere is harmonious, and like parents and children, brothers, or husband and wife, even enemies and allies forget all barriers, and their intimacy is like that of a pair of mandarin ducks. Despite such a peaceful heaven and earth, the dark spirits of the heart, entangled in suspicion, were suddenly seized by fear and astonishment, and drove the Brahmin Yakkos and Sabor to cast themselves into the lake in their misery.

The people on the boats, the crowd on land, and the Shōjō corps, seeing this scene, spat on their hands, and the sincere desire to save the two of them erupted at once, their hearts burning with sympathy. Just then, Masumihiko and Mitsuhiko, who had been waiting with a small boat among the reeds by the order of Tamakuniwake-no-Mikoto in anticipation of such an event, realized this was a critical moment; they handled their oars and glided over the water surface like flying birds, sending their boat over the whirlpool where they had fallen, and were finally able to save the two. If by any chance there had been an ominous event where even one of these two had lost their life, it would have been as if a flaw had been marked upon the heaven and earth of supreme goodness, truth, and beauty, and a cloud had come over the light-jewel; that it ended without incident was truly a sign of peace, and the crowd rejoiced in unison, while applauding the chivalry of Masumihiko and Mitsuhiko.

Chiruteru, the guardian of the Suma barrier, built over ten Shōjō carts, decorated them with various flowers, and stood at the head of the line, waiting at the shore to welcome the Shōjō corps while having them pulled by many soldiers. Itahiko was the first to leave the boat and approach Tamakuniwake, and while brimming with tears of joy, he firmly grasped his hand and shook it two or three times. Tamakuniwake, choking with tears of gratitude, said in a slightly husky voice,

Tamakuniwake: 'Itahiko-dono, a magnificent achievement, you have worked hard. That you were able to return safely ahead of the scheduled time is entirely due to the grace of the Lord and the fruit of your sincerity. Now, let us return to the Bachiru mansion and have you tell us various rare stories.'

Itahiko: 'Yes, thank you very much. Then I shall accompany you.'

Bachiru and Sāberu-hime decorated beautiful floats, had Tamakuniwake, Masumihiko, Itahiko, Mitsuhiko, and Debisu-hime board them, and while standing at the front of the floats herself, she sang songs as the villagers pulled them with thick ropes. Three hundred and thirty-three members of the clan boarded the dozen or so carts equipped by Chiruteru, and while raising cheers of 'Kya-tsu, Kya-tsu,' they were pulled along with a 'Jiri-jiri' sound. Accompanied by bells, drums, wooden clappers, vertical flutes, horizontal flutes, kakko, gekkin, and various other music, they sang songs and paraded lively along the main road.

Bachiru: 'I have now suddenly obtained a Miko, a Miko not from the Mihara of a wife.'

Saberuhime: 'Even if the Karatama is not borrowed, thy Mitama has become intimate with me and given birth to him.'

Bachiru: 'Since he is a child born from the Wagafu of Bachiru and Shiyōjiyōhiko, he is not the child of a Hito.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Well then, let us cut short this Utage and proceed to worship at the Miyai of Kami.'

Teku: 'I, Teku-tsukasa, who guards the house of Bachiru, shall follow behind you, my lord.'

From this point on, Tamakuniwake, together with Ichidō, trekked through the Taniahi of Anata and Konata of Mount Azumosu, had the Somabito fell many trees in the Ōgahi and Ogahi, and began the Kizuku for the Fushin of the shrine with the brave sounds of Teono. Starting with the Amata of Satobito, the Buka of Chiruteru, and the Shiyōjiyōtai, they worked day and night with joy and courage, felling trees, carrying them, or shaving them, and they came to serve in the shrine construction, forgetting even the fatigue of their bodies.

之より玉国別は一同と共に、アヅモス山の彼方此方の谷間を跋渉し、大峡小峡の木を数多の杣人に伐採せしめ、手斧の音勇ましく宮の普請の木作りに着手する事となつた。数多の里人を始め、チルテルの部下並に猩々隊は昼夜の別なく喜び勇んで、木を伐り、或は運び、或は削り、身の疲れも打忘れて宮普請に奉仕する事となつた。
