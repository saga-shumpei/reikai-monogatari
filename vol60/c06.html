Chapter 6: 満悦 [1531]

満悦〔1531〕

§ 1

The Ōzora was without a single speck of Unei, and the Amatsu-hi no Kami shone brilliantly, shōrin upon the Reichi of Mount Adumos, while the Natsukaze crossing the treetops blew satsusatsu, sending forth an air of seiriyō. In the Chiukū, silent Ongaku could be heard, Haukau was fragrant, and upon the Chijiyō, the sound of various Gakuki ringing out at once, and the Bisei of Momodori in the treetops chirping of the Shiyōzui of Chiyo, this Zuishiyō of the Sansaigafuitsu of Tenchijin was manifested in the Meushiki of the Mandarage offered before the Shinzen. Chiruteru, who had served the Brahman army for a long time, held the position of captain, also served as the Sekimori of Kiyo, and had wielded considerable violent power, led his wife Chirunahime, as well as Kanna, Hēru, and other subordinates, bowed respectfully before the Ōmiya, held the Mandarage in his hands, and began to sing.

Chiruteru: 'Heaven is pure, the earth is pure / The flowers of repentance for the purification of the six senses bloom in abundance / Today's joy, forevermore / Before the presence of Kami, reverently / We all, with our very hearts / With all our hearts and all our bodies / Choked with tears of gratitude / When I look up and gaze at the sky / The Amatsu-mikora is deep blue / Boundless, quiet, wide, and ever higher / When I bow and gaze upon the Chijiyō / Peonies, peonies, dahlias, and the like / Mandarage bloom in profusion everywhere / Butterflies of subtle and varied colors dance / Ten thousand trees and grasses grow ever lush / Playing and frolicking in the summer fields / To see that Zuishiyō with my own eyes / How happy I am to behold it / When I look at the faces of the people / Gathering in this sacred ground,

Without distinction of old or young, man or woman / All their faces glow red / Just like heavenly maidens / This heaven and earth created by Heaven / The reality of truth, goodness, beauty, and love / How noble it is, as You show it / So broadly and so peacefully / The morning sun shines, the moon illuminates / The stars twinkle in the Amatsu-sora / The North Pole constellation does not move / The Seven Sword Stars are around it / Morning and evening, without slackening / Just as they revolve, the villagers / Gather at this palace / How happy and joyful it is to play / Circling around the palace / The Kami-tsukasa of Ōkuninushi / Who has appeared in the capital of Haruna / How You distribute Your heart / To build the Kami-no-kuni upon the earth,

Forever and ever, even though You are impatient / How can I reach the merits of Kami / That protect the San-go-kyō / Without distinction of far or near / Longing for the divine majesty of Kami / The number of people gathering / Hundreds of thousands is not enough / The momentum of the tide coming in one after another / And when I examine the Reikai / Starting with the heavenly men and heavenly maidens / The divine spirits of ten thousand people / And the spirits of birds, beasts, and insects / Competing to be first / Gathering, saying "I too, I too" / The scene is like the opening of the Ama-no-Iwato / Ah, how interesting, ah, how clear / Happiness and joy fill my chest / My body is also light, in the divine work,

It is grateful to serve / Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagam / Ōkunitokotachi-no-Ōmikami / I serve as a companion Kami / Before the presence of Ōkunihiko-no-Ōkami / Reverently and in awe / The captain of the Brahman army / Here, I lay bare my sincere heart / Reverently, reverently, I pray / The plum blossoms of the three thousand worlds / The Mandarage that bloom all at once / How happy is the person who beholds / The scene of the eternal spring / Even if the morning sun shines or clouds over / Even if the moon waxes or wanes / Even if the great earth sinks / Mount Terumon becomes the sea / The lake of Kiyome becomes a mountain / The sacred ground of Mount Adumos / High in the clouds, sticking out,

Even if it becomes high and steep / This body that I have sworn to Kami / How could I ever change my heart / Please bestow Your grace, Ōmikami / Before the presence of the thousands of / Tsukasa-no-Kami who follow You / Reverently, reverently, I pray / Reverently, reverently, I pray.'

Akisu then began to sing.

Akisu: 'Since my childhood at the Bāchiru mansion / I have served as a slave of the house / The Ama-no-Iwato is already Akisu / By the words of Shōjōhime / I have attended to Itahiko-tsukasa / Rowing across a thousand leagues of waves / Welcoming many children who are irreplaceable / In heaven and on earth / The uncle of Shōjō, now here / In place of the three hundred and thirty-three / Divine spirits, I proclaim / The life of my father, unexpectedly / Was taken by Bāchiku-sama, the thread of life / The mother and child who remained behind / In their sorrow, unable to do anything / In their confusion and panic / Gathered the boats on the beach / And under the cover of night, on the lake / Fled in fear.'

§ 2

On the Ukishima, feared as the Sumika of the Riuō Sāgara, we plied the unfamiliar Rokai, and having reached it with our lives in the balance, we spent sad days and months while barely sustaining our lives.

At that time, the Kamuzukasa of the Ananai-kyō, opened by the Izumitama and the Mizu no Mitama, appeared in Amata, and taking Bachiru-sama, whom we revered as our father, onto a ship, we departed from the shore to return.

My beloved mother, upon seeing this, lamented, and taking the rare child of Ninjiugafuitsu born between the two, she strangled it to death and hid herself deep in the Unazoko.

The Ichidō who remained behind, buzzing like the chirping of mosquitoes, called out and tried to bring them back, but they lamented the Hiun that they would never return to this world again.

Whether it was a message on the wind or the shadow of a ship seen beyond the Shiranami, it came approaching, kicking up the waves.

Thinking that this was truly the Mifune of our father's devotion, the children all stood on the Isobata, building a fence and gazing out.

Itahiko-tsukasa, who serves the Jinji no Kami, showed with Temane that he was leading Amata ships to welcome us all back to the mansion of Bachiru.

How great was that joy; feeling as if ascending to Ten, we vied to be the first to leap onto the ship of salvation.

When we boarded, we found unexpected Hannyatō, and while they treated us to its very fragrant scent and opened the lid of the barrel, we raised the Maho on the calm waves.

Protected on all sides by the fish tribes living in the lake, we arrived safely at the port of Suma and returned to our father's mansion.

While spending happy days and months, the joy of having returned to our original Sumika once again.

Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase, the Kami's palace was also built very purely and beautifully, and the Izumitama no Ōkami, Baramon Taishaku Jizaiten, settled there forever, and it became a world where they protect the people of the land of Izumi.

The kenzoku of us Ichidō, having offered our pure and innocent souls to serve, decided on the forests of Mount Adsumosu as our Chiyo no Sumika, to enjoy the spring of Tokoyo.

Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase, fearing the sacred ground of the Kami, the villagers, regardless of man or woman, live in this forest forever.

Love your brothers well, and never kill them.

If there is a mistake, pray to the Kami immediately, and the admonition shall be sent down to awaken the eyes of the people of the world strictly.

Be reverent, villagers, and you, the army lord of the Baramon religion.

We, the Ichidō who emerged from the water and fire of Shōjōhiko and Shōjōhime, here offer our Kototama.

Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase, may the Mitama be blessed.

Tamakuniwake: 'The vast heaven of Amatsu-mikora and the earth above are clear today, how auspicious.'

Masuhiko: 'In the clear sky I look up at, there is no dust; only the shadows of cranes dancing and playing can be seen.'

Mitsuhiko: 'The rare blessings of the Ōkami are in heaven and earth; how great is the joy of Mitsuhiko today.'

Itahiko: 'My heart, looking at the fields in all directions from the climb up Mount Adsumosu, is wide and peaceful.'

Debisuhime: 'The moon and the sun are high in the great sky, leaving the mansion of Terumon behind, I am Debisuhime.'

Bachiru: 'Changing from a human to a Shōjō, I shall serve the Kami with a pure and innocent heart.'

Saberuhime: 'The moon and the sun emerge from the waves and enter the waves; the lake of the Kami's deep blessings.'

Teku: 'Though I was one whose heart was clouded until now, it has become clear through the teachings of the Kami.'

Anchi: 'In the vast blessings of the Ōkami, on this remote island, I am one who has cast away sorrow today.'

Akisu: 'Spring has passed, the great summer sky is clear, and my soul is clear in the autumn.'

Karu: 'The joy of Karu-tsukasa today, who waited in vain for the Lord on the Isobata.'

Chiruteru: 'Flowers scatter, the moon shines in the sky of the land of light; I shall live purely as the gatekeeper of Suma.'

Chirunahime: 'Until when will the mandala flowers bloom, Chirunahime? Scatter quickly, scatter, dust and refuse.'

Kanna: 'Kannagara, the light of the Kami is so strong that the darkness of the eternal night has cleared away.'

Heru: 'Though the mountain of Terumon is hazy and cannot be seen, I see the blessings of the Kami before my eyes.'

Thus, having finished celebrating the festival of the Sengū-shiki, they returned, singing songs in their mouths along the way, pointing toward the vast mansion of Bachiru. Gradually the sun set behind the western mountains, and the twilight air pressed upon all sides. The whole sky suddenly appeared with gold and silver starlight like the sands of the Ganges, and it was looking down silently at the joyful figures of the party. The temple bell in the middle of Mount Haruna, which continues from the peaks of Mount Adsumosu, rang out, pressing upon the evening air with a deep toll.
