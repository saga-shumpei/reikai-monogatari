Chapter 11: 法螺貝 [1536]

法螺貝〔1536〕

§ 1

Tamakuniwake and his party bade farewell to Bachiru, Chiruteru, and the others, and set out toward the capital of Haruna, crossing mountains, fields, lakes, and rivers. Lamenting the parting, Bachiru and the rest clung to their sleeves, their eyes filled with tears, and sang a song of separation.

Bachiru: 'O Sendenshi entrusted by Kami, / Tamakuniwake of pure Mitama, / Amatsumikami and Kunitsukami, / and the hundred Kamigami rise in courage / to guard your side. / O noble and precious Kamuzukasa, / whom Masumihiko follows, / along with Michihiko, Itahiko, and Debisu-hime, / you whose Mitama are equal to Kami, / you have saved the parents and children from their sorrows, / governed this village well, / and built a precious Miyai. / Beginning with the Takushaka Riuō, / you have brought the Sagara Riuō to Kotomu, / dispelling the calamities of the Sekai, / constructing the Shōtengoku, / and bestowing the dew of grace / in all directions. / How Arigata it is that you have let it fall! / For a thousand generations and eight thousand generations, eternally, / at the foot of Mount Adsumosu, / may you dwell in peace / and guide us, we prayed / morning and evening. / Alas, how sorrowful it is, / to leave behind the child of the teachings, / and depart for the capital of Haruna, / the land of the moon where the evil Kami rage. / Watching your departure, / my heart is choked with grief, / and tears fall like a waterfall. / Ah, Kannagara Kamigami, / O Sendenshi entrusted by Kami, / no matter how much sincerity you show, / your body is one that cannot be relied upon in vain. / It is foolish to lament the parting, / yet, O my master, / once you have safely finished / the divine work in the capital of Haruna, / do not abandon this sacred ground, / but appear again / and convey to us your noble teachings / in complete detail. / I, Bachiru, the Kamuzukasa of the shrine, / on behalf of all the villagers, / humbly and respectfully pray. / Ah, Kannagara Kamigami, / may your Mitama be blessed.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Taking Bachiru's pure words as a memento, / let us now depart, according to the will of Kami.'

Debisu-hime: 'Now then, together with my master, / I shall bid farewell before the hundred people.'

Saberu-hime: 'Now then, may you go forth to Haruna in peace. / I shall pray to Kami and protect you.'

Chiruna-hime: 'Through the admonition of my master, my husband / has returned to the path of truth. / How shall I ever repay / the grace that is higher than the mountains? My heart is impatient.'

Teku: 'Now then, may you go forth in good health. / For there is no enemy before you.'

Kanna: 'Who would have thought I would meet a person I never expected, / and now face a parting I never expected?'

Heru: 'Since everything is as Kami entrusts, / it should not be a path for human questioning.'

Akisu: 'O Kami of salvation who saved my master, / tears fall as we are about to part.'

Karu: 'Resigning myself that everything is a floating world of dreams, / I shall not think of this heartless parting.'

Wakkusu: 'I, who have been cleansed of countless sins and impurities, / am now about to part from the Kamuzukasa of Kami. / Though I lament, regret, and weep, what can I do? / O teacher entrusted by Kami.'

Heruman: 'I have seen a mysterious dream in a world of dreams. / Do not forget the dream, for it is the grace of Kami.'

Ekisu: 'Wakkusu, I cannot hold back the tears of repentance / for having troubled my master. / O Tamakuniwake, Kamuzukasa of Kami, please pray to Kami / and have my sins washed away.'

Eru: 'We part at the foot of Mount Terumon, / and now I am to part from Kami again.'

Tamakuniwake: 'I speak to the old man and old woman of the Shōjō. / May you dwell in peace for all eternity.'

Old Man: 'I intend to remain here on this sacred mountain of Sumēru forever / and protect the reign.'

Old Woman: 'Standing alongside my husband, the Takushaka Riuō, / I shall protect this sacred ground forever.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Now then, I speak to all the people. / May you dwell in peace through the grace of Kami.'

§ 2

The sun had gradually begun to set. In the vast wilderness of Kuōbōsenri, where human traffic was sparse, they quickened their pace, hoping to find shelter for the night upon noticing a forest of Sarasaujiyu by the roadside.

In this forest stood a small, half-ruined shrine. The party of five decided to spend the night in front of the shrine, spreading out their straw rain capes and covering their faces with their hats. The night deepened, and it became the hour of the ox, when even the waters seem to sleep. Three men crept closer, observing the breathing of the five as they slept.

Kō: 'Hey, you underlings, it seems this lot might be worth something. If things go well, I might be able to live off the proceeds for the rest of my life, so you too must follow my command and carry out a desperate, grand operation.'

Otsu: 'Ha-ha-ha-ha, yes, ha-ha-ha-ha, understood. They are a group with quite loud snoring...'

Kō: 'You fool, do you think you can make a living in this trade if you are afraid of the snoring of sleeping people?'

Otsu: 'I am still a novice, so I do not yet understand the ropes at all...'

Hei: 'Excuse me, Boss Bell, this fellow is still inexperienced, so it cannot be helped. Why, a job like this is fine for me alone. No matter how much of a novice he is, in three months he will be a veteran.'

Otsu: 'Hey, what do you mean by saying I will be a veteran in three months? Are you talking about the rice market? There is not even a shop in a place like this.'

Kō: 'What a fool you are. You were not even a beggar, and now that I have brought you along to be a thief, what is wrong with your hips? You are acting like a leper or a syphilitic... it is unsightly.'

Otsu: 'He-he-he-he, I am suffering a little from leprosy. My syphilis is also just about to heal. Ouch, ouch, ouch, talking about leprosy has made it suddenly painful, I cannot move anymore. Ouch, ouch, ouch.'

He collapses.

Bell: 'Hey, Bat, it cannot be helped. No matter how many of them there are, cutting their throats while they sleep is an easy task. Come, leave such a spineless coward alone, and let you and I start the work from both sides.'

Bat: 'Yes, understood. This is the time for our luck to turn; how could we let this opportunity slip by and not rise in the world?'

Tamakuniwake had been listening to the secret conversation of the three from the beginning without missing a single word.

Just then, the sound of a conch shell, 'Boo-boo,' pierced the darkness, heard from behind the shrine. The thieves were startled and, leaving Otsu in front of the shrine, fled into the darkness. The sound of the conch shell rang out ever louder.

Tamakuniwake waited for the sound of the conch to subside, and said,

Tamakuniwake: 'The conch shell that sweeps away the impurities of the mountains and rivers, whose plaything might it be? Hearing it from near the side of this shrine, who is the master of this Kototama?'

From behind the shrine,

'I am the end result of Oniharawake, a practitioner serving as a monk, Chidō Kōji.'

Tamakuniwake: 'Were you the general of the Brahmin army who served under the administration of the Chikokuwake God? I am a missionary of Tamakuniwake; how unexpected it is to meet you in such a place.'

Chidō: 'How nostalgic, the renowned Tamakuniwake, a servant of the God; I am both happy and embarrassed.'

Mitsuhiko: 'The absurdity of the thieves trembling and shaking as they tried to search my pockets.'

Itahiko: 'The conch shell that drove away the thieves, surely it must be a God who blew it.'

Mitsuhiko: 'Is it you, the Chidō Kōji of whom I have heard? How unexpected it is to meet you in such a place.'

Chidō: 'I have now become a monk of Mount Victor, a wandering ascetic traveling in all directions. I am one who advances along the path of truth while suppressing the fierce, warrior-like heart.'

Davis-hime: 'The heart of a monk who advances solely along the path of God is truly valiant. I, too, am one who served the Brahmin God at the divine hall of Mount Telmon.'

Chidō: 'Crossing over the vast ocean of Telmon, I have finally arrived here. I was resting in the shade of the shrine to soothe the fatigue of my grass-pillow journey. Connected by the threads of fate of the Imperial Gods, how happy I am to meet a servant of the God.'

Mitsuhiko took out a flint, searched in the darkness, gathered some fallen leaves and dead branches, and lit a fire with a snap. Chidō Kōji appeared from behind the shrine, looked at the faces of the group, and appeared very happy.

原文（近代文語）

治道『貴方は噂に高き玉国別様の御一行で厶いましたか、これは不思議な処でお目にかかりました。私はバラモン教のゼネラルで厶いましたが、河鹿峠に於て吾部下の片彦、久米彦将軍が治国別様の言霊に打悩まされ、実に見苦しき敗をとりました。それに就いて私は到底武力を以て神力に勝つ事の不可能なるを悟りました。併し乍ら数千人の部下を引率れ大黒主の命を受けて征途に上るゼネラルの分際として、直ちに軍籍を捨て、尊き神の道に入らむとするも事情が許しませぬので止むを得ず、ランチ将軍と相談の上、浮木の森にて半永久的陣営を造り、戦ふ心もなく徒に光陰を濫費して居りましたが、遂に軍隊を二つに分ち三千余騎を率ゐてライオン川を横断し、ビクトル山の麓に陣営を構へ、ここにても亦思はぬ失敗をとり、再び猪倉山の山寨に立籠もり治国別様の言霊を浴びせられ、ここに全く菩提心を起し、至善至愛の大神に信従する証拠として頭を剃り落ち円頂緇衣の比丘姿となり、ビクトル山の傍に草庵を結び、吾々同志四人が交る交る神の教の宣伝に廻つて居ります。玉国別様御一行の事も治国別様より詳しく承はり、一度尊き謦咳に接し度きものと祈つて居りましたが、思はぬ処で面会を得まして何とも云へぬ嬉しさが漂ひました。何卒御見捨なく御懇意に願ひます』

玉国別『貴方がバラモン軍の鬼春別将軍様で厶いましたか、不思議の縁で不思議な処でお目にかかりました。これも全く大神様の御引合せで厶いませう。此四人は真純彦、三千彦、伊太彦、デビス姫で厶います。何卒今後は御入魂に願ひます』

治道『網笠一つ、蓑一つ、杖一本の修験者、何卒共に手を引き合うて神業に参加させて頂き度う厶います』

真純彦『初めてお目にかかります。いやもう何にも申上げませぬ。神様の御為世の為に互に力になり合つて進む事に致しませう』

伊太彦『私は狼狽者の名を売つた伊太彦で厶います』

三千彦『私は三千彦夫婦で厶います。いつも伊太彦殿に女を連れて居ると云つて揶揄はれ通しで閉口致して居ります』

治道『ハヽヽヽヽ何分若いお方は元気が宜しいから面白いでせう』

デビス姫『ゼネラル様、妾はテルモン山の神館の司小国別の娘デビス姫で厶います。不思議な縁で此三千彦様に命を助けられ、ハルナの都へお伴を致す所で厶います。して之から貴方は何方へおいでになりますか』

治道『これは又不思議な御縁で厶る。貴女が小国別様の御息女とは思ひも寄りませなンだ。かうなる上は何れも三五教のピュウリタンとして互に打解け御神業に奉仕させて頂きませう』

玉国別『最早体も余程、疲れも休まつた様ですから夜中なれども大明りがして居りますから、ボツボツ進みませう』

治道『どうか私も途中迄なりとお伴をさして頂きませう』

と立上る。側を見れば一人の泥棒が踞んで慄うて居る。

治道『オ、お前は何者だ。泥棒の片割ではないか』

乙『はい、私は乞食で厶いますが今日初めて泥棒のベルと云ふ男の家来となり、三人連れにて此人々の跡をつけ狙ひ、ここ迄参りましたが法螺貝の声で腰を抜かしました。二人は何処かへ風を喰つて逃げたで厶いませう』

治道『はてな、ベルが又泥棒をして居るのかな』

と頻りに首を傾けて居る。泥棒の乙はコソコソと闇に紛れて姿を隠した。一行六人は治道居士を先頭に法螺を吹き立て東南に道を転じて露おく野路を足許忙しく進み行く。
