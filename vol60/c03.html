Chapter 3: 瑞祥 [1528]

瑞祥〔1528〕

§ 1

The plum blossoms of the Sanzensekai, and the lotus flowers as well, have opened and become fragrant all at once in this age. The Ōmikami of the Izu-no-mitama and the Ōmikami of the Mizu-no-mitama appear upon the summit of Shiyumisenzan, governing all things in the Daiuchiu. It has become the age of Maitreya.

Now, the summit of Shiyumi is known as Meru-kuta in Bongo. It is translated as Meukauzan and is also called Sumeru. Its eastern side stores the treasures of Ōgon, the southern side is made of Hari, the western side is of Mizu-no-mitama and Hakuginhotsushiyu, and the northern side is made of the treasure of Menau, with ranges and peaks pressing against each other and protruding into the middle of the Ōwadanaka.

Rising above the clouds, its height is three hundred and thirty-six ri. It is the Tokihakakiha Miaraka of the Mototsuhashira Ōkami, who created the Tenchi, and is revered by both heaven and man, being called Anmei, Miōkō, Kongōzan, and Kōkōzan. When translated, these mean the lotus pedestal of the sacred mountain assembly and the holy hills. The mountain of Adsumosu also enshrines the noble gods of three and five; it is called Shiyumisenzan, and it is truly noble as the sacred ground where a hundred gods have gathered in high spirits.

Blessed by the grace of God, the hearts of the villagers here are pure and clean. Without a trace of turbidity or defilement, the heavenly beings appear and dance, having opened their spiritual ears. Hearing the voices of the Sanzensekai, they strain their ears to the subtle music of elephants, horses, cattle, carriages, bells, and chimes. The sounds of koto, se, flutes, and pipes are spirited, and the voices of the clear and cool songs, along with the cheers of a hundred people, are enough to shake the Tenchi. Many angels descend to play music, and the voices of their mysterious songs ring out clearly.

It is needless to speak of the young and old, men and women; the auspicious birds flying through the skies of seas, rivers, mountains, fields, and valleys—whether they be Kalavinka, phoenixes, peacocks, cranes, eagles, hawks, or nightingales—their cries harmonize with the Tenchi, as if heaven had been built upon the earth. There is no great agony as arises in hell, no voices of A-bi-kyō-kan, no cries of hungry ghosts for food, nor voices seeking drink. There is no shadow of the crooked Ashura living by the side of the great sea, whispering and cursing. All listen to the teachings of God with joy and courage, and it is truly noble that there is no distinction between man and beast.

Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara. The divine heart of truth, goodness, beauty, and love is manifested here. There is no one who does not look up to the divine virtue of Otohiko and Kuniwakewake, whose names thunder through the Tenchi, as the angels of the Sanzensekai. Whether the morning sun shines or is clouded, whether the moon waxes or wanes, how could the crooked ever attack this world that is illuminated by the pure and stainless spirit? The sacred ground of Mount Adsumosu perfectly and minutely manifests the scenery of Shiyumisenzan, and it is truly auspicious that it shall shine until the end of eight hundred million kalpas as the pacification of the Sanzensekai.

Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara. Having received the words of God, I leave the sacred ground of the lotus pedestal, which should be compared to Shiyumisenzan, and the sacred land of Aya, to describe the story of the living beings of the distant past while gazing at the great mountain, which has been handed down as the first sacred fortress of the divine land, while riding on the ship of salvation. At this very moment, from the sacred ground, Sumiko the Second and Ninsai-shi, the reappearance of Konohanahime, who are said to cleanse the mud of this world, are in the hot spring resort standing in the pine forest, which they reached from afar to protect the physical palace of the spirit. Sitting opposite each other on the second floor of the Hamaya with the officers and believers, they speak of their sincerity to illuminate the light of the three and five months—the Mizu-no-mitama of March 3rd and the Izu-no-mitama of May 5th—as perfectly as possible, and the auspiciousness of the vows they have made. Kato Akiko, who is also noble, copies the divine book that celebrates the grace of the sun and moon, which are ever bright and eternal. A thousand years of pine is still foolish; with a fountain pen in hand, she faces the rosewood desk and records the eternal age. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara. How noble is the grace of God. Whether the morning sun shines or is clouded, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if the great earth should sink, even if the stars should fall from the sky, even if the Sea of Japan should dry up, this sacred book handed down by God shall not move for a thousand or eight thousand generations. While gazing with interest and joy at the dance of the gods of Shinato and Mikumari, and beating the drum in my heart upon the ark that sails across the sky by the command of the Izu-no-mitama, my heart is pure and calm.

§ 2

Proceeding according to the will of Kami.

At the foot of Mount Sumeru, the Futahashira stand side by side, opening the world this Kefu.

An Onsenba to bring life to all people of the world, poling the ship of salvation forward.

The Monogata of the Masumi Hiko of Ametsuchi, Sumiko has come to govern this world.

Praying for the Miyo of Maitreya to come quickly, the smile of Masumi Hiko of Matsumura flourishes.

Waiting for the lofty light of the Tsukihi of Kitamura, praying for the world of Miroku to arrive even one Hitohi sooner.

The Kamumitama of the lofty Mount Fuji has illuminated the world of Tokoyami.

The Zuigetsu of Deguchi, the Kami who saves the world, shines across the Masumi sky.

Mahasthama-prapta, Manjushri, Avalokiteshvara, how noble.

Surya, Chandradevaputra, and Samantagan, protect the Mizu no Mitama.

Dhritarashtra-maharaja, Virudhaka, Virupaksha, Vaishravana, protect this Oshihe.

Fuki: Today, the Baufūu is violent, and being hindered by the sound of the Dotau, the Kōjiyutsu is Chiushi here.

原文（近代文語）

神のまにまに進み行く。
