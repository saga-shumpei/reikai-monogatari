Chapter 4: Tree 遣 [1529]

木遣〔1529〕

§ 1

Mount Sumeru of the Reizanejiyō, where old trees stand thick and dense, sealing the heavens; seeking them out in the great and small gorges, the Sendenshi of Tamakuniwake, with many Mamehito in tow, strive and exert themselves in felling them day and night without rest, a truly valiant sight.

Anchi waved his baton and sang the Kiyariuta, and from the gorges here and there, they began the transport toward the Sakujiba.

Antchi: 'Heaven is Shiyōjiyō, the earth is Shiyōjiyō, and the hearts of men shall also be Shiyōjiyō. To build the palace of the Shiyōjiyō, the villagers of Suma gather together, their hearts lifted by Miki, sweating as they carry the timber. Yōi yōi, yōitose. This place is the renowned Lake Kiyo, and from ages past, it has been known to the world as the sacred Reijiyō where the gods gather, the Shiyōjiyōmuku Chinjiyu of Sumeru, which connects to the south of Adumos. The morning sun shines Kirakira in the sky, traversing across to set in the west. In this forest of the gods where the morning sun shines directly, this mountain of wonders illuminated by the winter sun, upon this summit stands a camphor tree rare in the world, its circumference three hundred and thirty jō.'

Antchi: 'The height of the trunk is five hundred jō, and its branches spread in four directions, serving as the residence for three hundred and thirty-three Kenzoku-sama. Yōitose, yōitose. Hawks, owls, and wild eagles, along with a hundred other winged birds, dwell here in eternal stability, living in harmony and affection, never harming one another; a tranquility that has demonstrated the auspicious proof of peace since ancient times. Yōitose, yōitose.'

Antchi: 'The palace of the Shiyōjiyō, where the Brahman heaven was enshrined, has been exposed to rain and wind for many years; the ridgepole leaks, the beams are warped, and the pillars are eaten by insects, a truly miserable sight to behold. Whether it be the power of time or not, I lament its devastation. Baruchiru-sama has renewed his adherence to the teachings of the gods, and in this sacred place, he has auspiciously built anew the palace of wonders of the Sangokyō and the palace of the Brahman teachings, serving as the foundation for the three thousand worlds; how grateful we are to have it enshrined. Yōitose, yōitose.'

Antchi: 'Whether the morning sun shines or clouds gather, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if the great earth should sink, the Reijiyō of Mount Adumos shall remain eternal and unchanging. From the distant age of the gods, this is the birthplace of the Takushaka Dragon King, who appeared as the spiritual descendant of Amatsuhiko and Hōjōhime.'

Antchi: 'The fierce power of the nine-headed dragon, harsh to behold, would blind the eyes of those who saw it, spitting poison and devouring the lives of men. To punish such evil deeds, the Angel of Tsukiteruhiko appeared in Takamagahara, struck forth the Kototama of rigor, and it is said he sealed it at the rocky roots of this Reijiyō. It is a spot famous throughout the world. During those hundred thousand years, having tempered the heart and refined the soul, at the dawn of returning to a Shiyōjiyōmuku spiritual body, the Angel shall appear once more to save the Takushaka Dragon King, and he has vowed to protect the regulation of the five winds and ten rains. As long as this Dragon King remains hidden in the depths of the earth forever...'

Antchi: '...wild winds rage and heavy rains fall for ten or twenty days, and the people and livestock living on the earth, even down to the single leaf of grass or tree, will not be healed of this suffering. Yōitose, yōitose. Now that the Sendenshi of the Sangokyō, the Angel of the earth who has inherited the lineage of Tsukiteruhiko, has appeared here, we shall take this opportunity to offer the Amatsu-norito and the Kamigoto, which purify and clarify heaven and earth, to the spirits of the universe. If you would quickly save this Dragon King who has been sealed for a thousand and eight thousand generations, how the people of the land would rejoice. The five grains will ripen, the colors and scents of the flowers of grass and trees will be beautiful, and the harvest will be especially abundant. All living things on earth will enjoy the auspicious signs of truth, goodness, beauty, and love of the divine nation, beating their bellies and stamping the ground in joy, eternal and unchanging. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, I pray for the grace of the divine spirits. Yōitose, yōitose. The plum blossoms of the three thousand worlds, the lotus flowers blooming all at once, the highland of the self-rotating inverted island known as Mount Sumeru, the protection of Konohana-hime who appeared on Mount Tenkyō, all shall manifest here at last. The village of Suma, once wrapped in darkness, shall auspiciously become the era of the rising sun, a world where we enjoy and rejoice in the light of the gods morning and evening. Take courage, take courage, villagers!'

§ 2

Yoito-se, yoito-se. For thousands of years, this fine timber of the camphor tree, buried in the rough metal-bearing earth and turned to stone, is offered as a symbol of the Lord God's reign, eternal and unchanging. Is this not a sign of the grace of the Strict God? The weight of this camphor tree, surpassing all others in its majesty, is yet another rare sign of the eternal and unchanging Age of the Gods. When I think of it, when I think of it, how grateful I am. Villagers, pull with courage. Yoito-se, yoito-se. Those who serve the task of pulling out this great tree, because the grace of the Gods is deep, suffer not even a single injury to their bodies, and wonder of wonders, they do not even grow weary. The protection of the Gods is right before our eyes.

Pull with joy and courage. Yoito-se, yoito-se. If these palace pillars are made thick and stand piercing the clouds, the chigi and katsuogi will glitter, and the golden light will shine. The Land of the Moon, the Land of Fusa, Kara, and Yamato will all shine at once, like the sun and moon. Ah, how brave, how brave. Grandfathers and grandmothers, bringing their grandchildren, offer their labor by day, following the night, and offer their labor by night, never resting. In the hearts of the people, it is like the blooming spring of the Heavenly World, where there is no distinction between day and night. Yoito-se, yoito-se.

Itahiko: 'In the heavenly sky, a mysterious and subtle music comes to be heard. From the spirit land of Takamagahara, the angels and heavenly beings of the deep-joy paradise appear. To build the Takamagahara upon the earth, they flutter their feathered robes in the breeze to assist in this divine work, rejoicing and celebrating. The people living on the earth, saved by the Kototama of good words and beautiful language, without a shadow of evil, with one heart and one body, simply devote themselves to everything according to the heart of the Imperial Gods. Serving in the construction of the divine palace that shines in the world, the Jyōgyō-bisha, the Shuda, and the other twelve humble lineages, casting aside all various discriminations, stand together on the horizon. For the sake of the Gods and the sake of the world, in the San-go-kyō and the Brahman, how auspicious it is to devote one's sincere heart.'

Itahiko: 'Such an example is a peace unprecedented, not even heard of in dreams since ancient times. It goes without saying for the common people, but the Shōjōhime who had long lived in this sacred ground, and the hundred other children, met the spring of the heavenly paradise and returned safely to their original dwelling; this joy is a matter without precedent since the beginning of heaven and earth. Leaving the Ubusuna mountain, following the rare officials of the pure-hearted Tamakuniwake, I have finally come to this sacred ground of Adumos, famous in the Land of the Moon, and serve in this unprecedented divine work. The joy of my soul is like a sudden evening shower pouring down upon a drought-stricken rice field. Yoi-yoi, yoito-se. Align your strength, match your voices, pull with a yoi-yoi-yoi. Those who serve with sincere hearts in this divine work, unprecedented in all ages, are blessed by the grace of the Gods; their bodies are healthy, their homes prosper month by month, and their children and grandchildren, generation after generation, rejoice in the divine work of their ancestors, and will surely honor the divine virtue forever, making it a story for the ages to come.'

Itahiko: 'Take courage, all of you, take courage. Standing together in equality to achieve merit that replaces heaven and earth, let us build the sacred ground of Mount Adumos as the foundation of truth, goodness, beauty, and love. Kannagara. Swearing to the Gods, I, Itahiko, pray on behalf of all these Suma people. Yoito-se, yoito-se.'

Countless camphor, cypress, zelkova, pine, cedar, and oak trees, both large and small, were safely transported to the construction site in less than a month. That not a single person suffered even a scratch was entirely due to the deep protection of the Gods, and all were moved by a sense of gratitude; everyone, young and old, forgot to eat and sleep, and in a frenzy, poured their heart and strength solely into the palace construction. Through the devotion of the people, the magnificent palace was built with the chigi standing high, facing south, lined up from east to west. Ah, Kannagara, may the spirits be blessed.
