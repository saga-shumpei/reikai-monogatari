Chapter 5: 鎮 Festival [1530]

鎮祭〔1530〕

§ 1

The two palace buildings, which exhausted all beauty and goodness, were finally completed through the devoted efforts of Tamakuniwake and all the others; it was decided that Ōkunitokotachinōokami would be enshrined in the eastern palace, and Ōkunihikonomikoto would be enshrined in the western palace.

Tamakuniwake, serving as the chief priest, donned newly prepared ritual vestments and, together with the propagandists led by Masumihiko, the host couple Bachiru, and the villagers led by the Brahmin Chiruteru, performed a solemn ceremony of transferring the deities.

As the Goshintai for Ōkunitokotachinomikoto, the divine spirit was invoked into a precious agate jewel, three shaku and three sun in diameter, which had been passed down in Bachiru's family since ancient times; as the Goshintai for Ōkunihikonomikoto, the divine spirit was invoked into a crystal orb, approximately three sun in diameter, which Chiruteru had kept with great care, and it was decided that these would be enshrined.

Thus, Bachiru became the chief priest of the eastern palace, and Princess Saberu became the chief priest of the western palace, and they came to serve them by purifying their minds and bodies morning and evening.

Tamakuniwake, the propagandist, sang the ritual prayer for the ceremony of transferring the deities in the form of a song.

Tamakuniwake: 'In the forest of Tēwarājā, where the morning sun shines upon Adzumosu, we have felled the trees of the great and small gorges. The villagers of pure heart have built the palace pillars deep into the bedrock, constructing them sturdily, and have raised the chigi high toward Takamagahara. On this day, when we shall enshrine the Imperial Great Deities of the Three-Five Teachings and the divine teachers of the Brahmins, the Heavenly Rock Cave is opening; it is the time of the birth of life, the time of the birth of the sun. We pray to the Great Deity Ōkunitokotachi, who created and solidified this world, and receive the deep blessings of the Great Deity Ōkunihiko, who descends from the planet Uranus, along with the Brahma, Indra, and Jizaiten. How joyous it is that we have finally finished building these palace pillars.'

Tamakuniwake: 'The Sumeru mountain, which reflects the image of the spiritual land of Takamagahara, is the savior of the world. How joyous it is that the gods of heaven and earth have gathered here, coming to serve with the wish to protect the world forever and ever. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if heaven and earth were to overturn, as long as the Great Deity, the Original Ancestor, remains enshrined in this land, no calamity shall come. May the great three disasters of wind, water, and fire, and the small three disasters of famine, disease, and war, be blown away like smoke or mist by the morning or evening breeze, leaving no shadow behind. May You remain enshrined here forever as a sacred site of safety and peace, and protect the people of the world.'

Tamakuniwake: 'The God Naohi who created this world, and the Great Naohi of vast heart—in the world of men, let all things be seen anew and heard anew through Naohi, and let the errors of the body be corrected through proclamation. By chanting these divine, auspicious words of good and beautiful speech morning and evening, wiping away all evil spirits, and tasting the joy of the Divine Kingdom, may the people of this land enjoy it forever. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara. We pray that You remain enshrined in this divine bed forever, saving the world of eternal darkness, and that Your divine majesty may appear as Lashūzuda, Sahasrabali-Pūrṇadvadja, Sarvasattvapriyadarśana, and remain forever. We humbly pray.'

Tamakuniwake: 'May the various delicacies of the sea, rivers, mountains, and fields, which these villagers have offered with sincere hearts, be placed wide and high upon the eight-legged tables, like the shape of the Yokoyama mountains. We offer the Great Divine Sake and the Great Divine Food and Water with all our hearts. May You, the two Great Deities, listen completely and in detail to the sincere hearts of the common people, and may today's joy continue forever. Kannagara. Before the noble gods, I, Tamakuniwake, the divine official of the Three-Five Teachings, representing everyone with all my heart, humbly and fearfully pray. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara. May Your spirits be blessed.'

The festival ended safely, and the people gathered in every sacred space until there was no room left, holding a Naorai banquet with the offerings that had been removed from the altars; while partaking of the sacred sake, they each celebrated the day's grand event in their own way. I shall state one or two of the songs of the important people among them below.

Bachiru: 'Ah, how grateful, how grateful. The Heavenly Rock Cave has opened. The curtain of darkness has lifted. The air all around is somehow so refreshing, and the wind rustles. The treetops are playing natural music in elegance, and the branches are performing a dance, celebrating today's grand event. The lotus flowers blooming in the fields are blowing their fragrance high. Even the peonies, peonies, and dahlias are competing in their beauty and sending their scent. It feels as if I am looking at the Heavenly Pure Land right before my eyes. The palace that had rotted away is now beautifully renewed, and the scent of new wood strikes my nose. Everything I see and hear is, without exception, the blessing of the noble gods.'

原文（近代文語）

真善美を尽したる二棟の宮殿は玉国別以下一同の丹精によつて漸く完成し、東側の宮には大国常立大神を祀り、西の宮には大国彦命を鎮祭する事となつた。

玉国別は斎主として新調の祭服を身に着け、真純彦以下の宣伝使及び主人側のバーチル夫婦並にバラモンのチルテル以下里人一同と共に荘厳なる遷宮式を挙行した。

大国常立尊の御神体としてはバーチルの家に古くより伝はりし直径三尺三寸の瑪瑙の宝玉に神霊をとりかけ、大国彦命の御神体としてはチルテルが大切に保存せる直径三寸許りの水晶の玉に神霊をとりかけ、これを奉斎する事となつた。

さうしてバーチルは東の宮の神主となり、サーベル姫は西の宮の神主となり、朝夕心身を清めて之に奉仕する事となつた。

玉国別の宣伝使は遷宮式の祝詞を歌に代へて歌ふ。

祭典は無事に終了し、各聖地に処狭き迄群集り居て撤饌の供物により直会の宴を開き、神酒を頂き乍ら思ひ思ひに今日の盛事を祝した。其中重なる人の歌を一、二左に述べて置く。

There is nothing that He does not enclose. The Tsumitoga committed by my father has come around to my own body. While my heart aches day and night, I floated out to the lake of purification, and while gathering a hundred Urokuzu, I was comforting my heart. However, through the guidance of the grace of Kami, I encountered a Shike such as never seen before. Being saved by the island of Shiyōjiyō, through the encounter of Innen and Inguwa, I unexpectedly pledged the bond of Oshi with the Hime of Shiyōjiyō. At the dawn of passing Mitose, the Kami of salvation came, saved me, and sent me to the mansion of Izumi. Now, again, receiving the Mikotonori of Kami with gratitude, I have brought my Ichizoku to the office of Kami of Itahiko.

Being welcomed to this Miyama, the Oyako of Mitama rejoiced, and morning and evening they go to serve at the Shinden of Mount Sumeru. I have become a happy person. Since I have become a Biku from this point on and have become a pillar of Kami, I have returned the mansion of my Fuso, as well as the Sanya and Denpata, to the Kami of Tenchi. I have determined the Mochiba of each Satobito, and made all the property—not to mention rice, wheat, beans, millet, and sorghum, but also countless sheep and pigs—the possession of the Satobito. While building this Chijiyō Tengoku as it is for eternity, may you be bathed in that divine grace. Since you have come to serve Kami,

you should throw away material treasures without leaving even a speck of dust, and receiving the grace of Kami, the husband and wife and Oyako should serve joyfully at the sacred place. Please grant this, O Amatsukami, Kunitsukami, and the eight million Kami. Starting with the captain of the Brahman army who appears as its living palace, and including the hundred armies and the Satobito, please distribute my treasures fairly and selflessly, and spend this world peacefully. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if the earth should sink, the words of Bachiru of the office of Kami shall never change for eternity. With a peaceful heart and in tranquility,

please think of this, I swear before the presence of the Sumerakami and proclaim. Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haema-se, may the Mitama be blessed.'

The Satobito searched the wilderness and gathered four kinds of Mandarage, which they offered to the Shinden until there was no room left. Sabel-hime stood in the center of the flowers, holding the Mandarage in her hands, and danced wildly while singing a song in harmony with the subtle sounds of music from drums, kakko, shō, hichiriki, and harps.

Incidentally, the four kinds of Mandarage are:

1. Mandarava

2. Mahamandarava

3. Manjushaka

4. Mahamanjushaka

They are called so. Furthermore, Mandara is also translated as 'flower of heart's desire,' 'flower of fulfilled intent,' 'circular flower,' 'flower of joyful sound,' 'flower of various colors,' or 'wondrous heavenly flower.' Its colors are various and wondrous, such as red tinged with yellow, blue tinged with purple, or purple tinged with black. Mahamandarava is sometimes considered a white flower or a great white flower. Manjushaka is sometimes considered a soft grass, a wish-granting grass, or a red cluster flower.

Sabel-hime: 'The Mandarage of blue, red, white, yellow, and purple, which have united with the Ten-Ka-Sui-Chi, I offer to the Great Presence until there is no room left. The bouquet of Truth, Goodness, Beauty, and Love, which has fulfilled the water and fire of Tenchi, the Satobito offer with reverence and with sincere hearts. The Sumerakami has united the Ten-Ka-Sui-Chi of Kototama. The people of the earth stand in rows before the Great Shrine these various and wondrous flowers, the Mandarage which have united with the Ten-Ka-Sui-Chi, and offer their utmost sincerity. O Sumerakami, O Ōkami, we and the Satobito have purified our hearts, and at the sacred site of Mount Adsumos, having set the thick pillars of the Great Shrine,

serve forever as the officials of the Chin-no-Miya where You dwell in tranquility. Bachiru and his wife offer their sincere hearts here and pray. We, husband and wife, are moistened by the dew of the grace of Ōkami, and like the Mandarage blooming in fragrance, like the lotus flowers opening all at once, we build in the sky of our hearts a nation of peace and joy of the heavenly realm. For the sake of Kami and for the sake of the world, may You widely proclaim and transmit the unified and non-dual divine teachings of the Anabakyo and the Brahmans to the four directions, and save the people of the world. These three hundred and thirty-three adorable Shiyōjiyō are the Shiyōjiyō-hime who possessed my body, the precious children of the love of Tenchi born from the messenger of Kami.

Please have mercy and protect our Mitama forever, and let us pass through this world in peace. Please wipe away attachment and worldly defilement with the wind of Shinato, take purification at the river of Yasu, become a soul of purity and innocence, and let us serve You. Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haema-se, I pray for the blessings of the Mitama.'
