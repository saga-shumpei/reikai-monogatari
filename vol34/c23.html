Chapter 23: 動 Tranquility [964]

動静〔964〕

§ 1

Hearing that a detachment of Rokukō had returned, Sankō of the Orochi secretly led Rokukō into his drawing room. There Yosakō and Kankō, both persons, were waiting on either side.

Sankō: 'Oi Roku, things went smoothly, I suppose.'

Scratching his head a little and shaking his neck three or four times, Roku spake thus:

Roku: 'Heh, as for that, what a thing it is. When we arrived at the summit of the Takayamatauge in Mount Tsukushi, the five fellows instantly scattered like clouds and mist, and became nowhere to be found... such was the state of affairs. Truly, since we have gained a great victory, please be at ease.'

Sankō: 'You must have broken your bones quite a bit.'

Rokukō: 'No, how could that be, there is nothing preposterous like that. What is called the majestic power of the Great Boss is a tremendous thing, wow. When we, the whole party, went to Takayamatauge, Torakō's fellow sniffed our scent, fled away like clouds and mist, and it ended up with not a single cat's child being there.'

Yosa: 'Oi Rokukō, Torakō didn't run away. Wasn't he not there?'

Rokukō: 'That's right. Because he ran away, he is not there. Because he is not there, they say he ran away.'

Yosa: 'Kisama, what have you been doing until now?'

Rokukō: 'I, by the command of the Great Boss, sought the whereabouts of Torakō's gang, continuing night into day toward Mount Tsukushi, while shedding sweat in drops, climbed the rugged mountain path with untontokodokoi and yattokomakase—when I looked, it was a record-breaking great storm, rocks danced up into the mid-heaven, making a terrible sound, falling thump and bang wherever they pleased, large trees were blown down from the roots without a shred of regret, tree forks split, a rain of gravel fell, ah, that was a great disturbance since the creation of the world. The one who continued the march unperturbed through the midst of that was this party of Rokukō. Rokukō is great, but the boss's majesty is also a big deal, you know. I have never seen such an exhilarating sight since I was born. Torakō's rascal, blown by this gale, must have fallen down to the bottom of some valley with a thud and croaked, though I searched every nook and cranny of a thousand tails and a thousand valleys, whether he was eaten by wolves or done in by tigers, his shadow and shape became completely gone. How mysterious, pausing at the mountain summit, folding both hands, and trying to ponder, from the very root, no good thought came up, helplessly shouting "Oii Oii" to call together our comrades, once we did a headcount at the very top of Takayamatauge with "one, two, three...", and ran down the steep slope full of boulders with enyarayā, the whole party gathered in the shade of an oak forest, roughly Torakō's fellow is a believer of Misangyō, so he surely must have gone to Tatehi's mansion, because of this, leading a detachment, heading dashingly toward the mansion, trying a trial of arms, and thinking only to give them a start—yet wait a moment, if we advance rashly, rather the fight will be disadvantageous, pressing the rushing chest tightly, to the place where we waited holding our hands and legs, Torakō's fellow, without knowing it as a body that is not a god, appeared and manifested quite plainly in this place... that's it.'

Sankō: 'What happened after that, do you say? Why won't you hurry and tell the rest?'

Rokukō: 'Since there is a saying that unspoken is a flower, shall I be allowed to cut it off about here now? If you should get angry, saying Rokukō did a good-for-nothing thing, the feelings of both sides will go bad, so anyway, among the things Roku did, there is nothing good, right?'

Sankō: 'Oi Roku, won't you pull yourself together? What you say is disjointed, self-contradictory, isn't it that you don't understand the meaning of anything at all?'

Rokukō: 'Heh heh, all things have value precisely in the places where they are not understood...'

Sankō: 'What, do you say you gained victory in a place you didn't understand? What has become of Torakō?'

六公『言はぬが花と云ふ事が御座いますから、モウここらで打切りにさして頂きませうか、六公が六でもない事をしよつたと云つて、御立腹なせえましては、双方の気が悪うなりますから、何れ六のやつた事に六な事は御座いませぬワイ』

三公『オイ六、シツカリせぬか。貴様の云ふ事は支離滅裂、前後矛盾、何が何だか訳が分らぬだないか』

六公『ヘヽすべて物事は分らぬ所に価値が御座いますので……』

三公『ナニ、分らぬ所で、勝を得たと云ふのか。虎公は如何なつたのだ』

§ 2

Rokukō: 'I cannot quite make out the tiger. It will probably be settled somehow or other. I didn't have the leisure to investigate it in such detail... Regrettably, we gathered the remnants and returned here stealthily... that is how it is.'

Sankō swelled his face with anger,

Sankō: 'Hah, neither this fellow nor that fellow is any good! Hey Yosakō, Kankō, examine Roku thoroughly and report the details to me. I have some business with Toku right now...'

He spat out these words, left the place, and proceeded to the spacious banquet hall where a drinking party was currently underway.

Toku and Taka, the two of them, sat facing each other, endlessly whining and whispering about something. Sankō called out to them,

Sankō: 'Hey Toku, if you don't go quickly to the job I told you about, it will be too late and no good, you know.'

Tokukō: 'Heh, I will go if I have to go, but somehow it just won't go smoothly at all from root to branch. Today too much sake has gone to my head, and my body won't obey me, so please postpone it to tomorrow.'

Sankō: 'If it were something that could be postponed to tomorrow, would I tell you to do it? Come on, make preparations quickly.'

Tokukō: 'Even if you tell me to make preparations, once this much preparation has spun around, there is no further way to prepare. Observing that against such a dame, my own credentials promised little hope of success, I actually sat down with resolve and gulped down drinks to my heart's content.'

Sankō: 'Didn't you say you wouldn't drink sake until you accomplished this task? When the crucial moment arrives, how can you be so dead drunk? Come on, stand up quickly.'

Tokukō: 'Boss, no matter what you say, my lower back just won't stand up, my lower back... Rather than that, it might be quicker if you went directly yourself, Boss. Let's bury those two fellows just as they were, carry only Lady Aoi deep into the mountains, and there be sweet and get the hang of it with your own capability. That is the quickest way of all. As the saying of Omoto-kyō goes—though I shouldn't quote it—do not rely on people as canes, do not depend on subordinates, as the saying goes... Gegebu pūe, ah, how painful, how can a journey be made when it hurts like this? There is stillness within movement, and movement within stillness. Since it is going to be yours anyway, and since I cannot possibly go through with it, Boss, please go bravely and properly. I too will sit down with all my might, drink some sake, and pray for the Boss's success.'

Sankō: 'You really are a hopeless fellow.'

Tokukō: 'Truly, you are a hopeless fellow too. Coveting another man's wife—isn't that a cowardly and unmanly behavior unworthy of a boss who stands at the forefront of men? Egebu, uuu.'

Sankō uttered a shrill voice,

Sankō: 'Yosakō, Kankō!'

He called out. At this voice, both Yosakō and Kankō appeared staggering on their feet to this place,

Yosaku: 'Boss, is there some business you want with us?'

Sankō: 'Won't you take Tokukō to the valley stream in the back, make him drink water, sober him up, and see that he goes quickly to the place we discussed?'

Yosaku: 'That sounds like Yosakō's work. Hey Toku, won't you stand up? You, when the crucial time comes, what is the meaning of this? Do you think you can perform your duties as a boss's man with such an attitude?'

Tokukō: 'Now that I think about it, no matter what is said, it is that forest far away from human habitations. If Aoi's ghost, or something, should even stop breathing, it would be spooky... Not that it's scary or anything, but somehow the sake has drained out of my waist and I cannot move no matter what. Elder brother, I beg of you, since I will appoint you as my proxy today, won't you go briskly? Truly, the Boss has engineered such a grand farce up to this point, so it would be a pity if it all went down the drain like this, and since we also accepted the treat of sweet sake from the Boss, I feel terribly sorry for him.'

Yosaku: 'I am no good at it. If that's how it is, since it can't be helped—hey Kanshū, you, how about you go in Toku's place?'

Kankō: 'I am excused already. Today is the anniversary of my parent's death, you see.'

Just as this was happening, two or three henchmen came rushing in hastily,

Henchmen: 'Hey elder brother Yosaku, ta-ta-ta, it's a terrible emergency! The ghost of Aoi and the ghost of Tiger-kaku have come swarming here with a crowd of dead souls! Come on, prepare yourselves, prepare yourselves!'

Both Yosaku and Kankō, uttering a cry of 'Ah!', collapsed on the spot with their hips giving out.

Outside the mansion, the summer wind was blowing through with a roaring sound. The voices of evening cicadas could be heard from the trees at the edge of the mansion's garden, crying zemi-zemi, min-min-min so loudly that it hurt the ears.
