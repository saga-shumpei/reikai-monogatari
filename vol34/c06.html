When I think of it, when I think of it, how foolish, A thousand ri, two thousand ri, three thousand ri, Crossing rough waves, dotskoi shoyo, Guided all the way to the island of Tsukushi, Haa haa fū fū, ah, it is hot! Sweat blurs my vision so I cannot see, Things have come to such a pointless pass.'

Untokodotsukoi Kurohime-sama, let us take a short break. No matter how much one hurries, in two, three, or ten days, one cannot reach the capital of fire. Even if an aunt dies, it is time for a mealtime rest. Dokkoishō dokkoishō. In an age when summer vacations are all the rage, to have no rest is greedy. I too am somehow exhausted. Please let me rest a bit. Look, look, on yonder tree branch, sweet-looking fruits are growing. Once I see those, I cannot bear it. I cannot take another step. Ah, Kamunagara kamunagara, may your Mitama be blessed and prosperous. Oops, dangerous stone wheel, just a slip away from scratching my shins.

This too is surely the deep and precious divine protection of Kamisama. Let us rest here. Those of you with sturdy feet, please go on ahead. It cannot be bartered for greed or virtue; life is the seed of things. To reach the end and collapse is just burnt garbage. Haa haa fuu fuu, fuu suu suu. Somehow my breath is giving out. Sweat and fat have dried up completely, as if not a single drop remains, turning into a completely parched state. Untokodotsukoi, shall we rest? Saying this, she sits down heavily with a thump and lies down upon the green leaves.

Kurohime and Fūsō, both of them, rejoiced as if finding a boat in a time of need, approached the shade of the trees to rest, and wiped away their flowing sweat.

The three of them, while wiping their sweat, are resting in the tree shade where cicadas are singing. Huge blue wasps make a fierce attack. Glancing up suddenly at the sky, a large wasp's nest is hanging down. Crying out 'Goodness, this is terrible!', the three suddenly jump up and run away toward the slope. The swarm of blue wasps, appearing to mistake them for an enemy attack, put all their might into both pairs of wings, buzzing loudly and attacking straight for the heads of the three. Fortunately, the three were wearing sedge hats, so they escaped the sting of the wasps and, with their very lives, ran desperately up the slope for about two or three chō without knowing why. There, like no high mountain, beautiful pure water flows in a gurgling stream.

Yoshikō: 'Haa haa haa fuu fuu fuu... Ah, how thankful I am. I had no idea such magnificent pure water was gushing out here. It is just like receiving sweet rice cakes in a dream.'

So saying, he scoops the pure water in both hands and drinks it down cup after cup, looking delicious. Fūsō also drinks the water next.

Fūsō: 'Ah, how thankful, it is as if I have met a rescue boat. Pure Spirit Jun'yohime-sama, you have given us the water of life. Because of this, I have properly understood the grace of the Pure Spirit. Ah, Kamunagara mitama kawahibakomaseyo.'

Yoshikō: 'Kurohime-sama, would you not like to have a cup yourself? You must be quite thirsty.'

Kurohime, making a bitter face, spoke.

Kurohime: 'Do you know the distinction between the root and the branch, self and others, public and private? With your actions turning heaven and earth upside down, even if I were to thirst, I would never drink such water, why!'

Yoshikō: 'This is a strange thing to hear again. What is it that pleases you so little?'

Kurohime: 'Have you forgotten the rule that seniority has its order? Why did you not invite Kurohime, who is your elder, to drink first, and drink after her? For a youngster to drink first and drink afterwards, is that not quite discourteous?'

Fūsō: 'What a difficult old hag you are. Whether one drinks afterwards or drinks beforehand, the taste of water should be no different. To have rigid rules brandished about in a place like this is quite more than one can bear. Kurohime-sama, you are going against the supreme benevolence and love of cherishing your subordinates. If you say such things, we too have quite a bit to say for ourselves. Long ago, when there was a battle at Mount Zion, among the subordinates of Kototamawake no Mikoto there was a general named Kunijiwake. At that time, many subordinates suffered severe wounds from the enemy forces and were troubled by thirst. General Kunijiwake also suffered severe wounds and wanted to drink water, but since there was no water close at hand, a subordinate vassal managed to go down to a mountain stream, fill his cap with water, and bring it before Kunijiwake. At that time, many subordinate vassals looked at that water with envious expressions. General Kunijiwake saw this, and deciding not to drink the water he himself desired, told them to let his subordinate vassals drink it, whereupon he met his death in battle on the spot. If one intends to become a leader of men, without that degree of merciful love, subordinates will not grow. You have long been blowing your own horn and engaging in propaganda, but I wondered why not a single genuine subordinate was produced—yet after all, you have a major spiritual defect. Can you do propaganda with such egoism? Even if you chatter grandly about how God is love or how saving people is God, it is useless if facts do not accompany it. Since you are all words and no action, that is why we too end up getting sick of you. When people speak of being distant, they are talking about you. To make a fuss over a mere cup of water is more than I can take.'

Yoshikō: 'Hey Fūsō, you had better stop such distant talk. Since it would be inexcusable toward Mizu no Mitama-sama if it turned into a petty squabble over water, after all. We are not strangers to one another, so let us wash away this kind of conflict cleanly into the water.'

Kurohime: 'What a skillful man you are, always picking holes in every word! To say that my tongue slipped while I was off guard, and to attack so hastily—surely your own judgment is not very good either. If you are going to find fault with people, first reflect on your own body and consider your own actions.'

With that, she bared her white teeth, thrust her chin forward in a sullen pout, and grunted two or three times.

Fūsō: 'Ah, since we still have to climb this steep slope quite a bit from here on, shall we call it quits with the quarrel around here? Now, everyone, let us set off.'
