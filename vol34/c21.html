Chapter 21: Kami 護 [962]

神護〔962〕

§ 1

Torakō finally appeared along the Hinoe Highway, accompanied by a party consisting of Tamakō, Shinkō, Kiukō, and Yatsukō. This was the spot with a rock serving as a natural chair, where massive oak trees shrouded and blocked the rays of the sun, and where Kurohime had once been tormented by a long-armed ape. Rokukō, the henchman of Sankō the great serpent, leading several dozen subordinates, had been waiting under the oak tree while seeking the whereabouts of Torakō.

As Torakō casually came this far along the road while chanting a song, he saw Rokukō's party, hardened in quarrel attire, blocking the mountain path while carrying clubs and daggers.

Rokukō: 'Hey, you Toru rascal, I have been searching for your whereabouts since yesterday. Knowing for sure that you went to the summit of Mount Takayama, when I pursued you there, you quickly took wind, cowardly and unmanly concealing your figure. Since I have no excuse to give to the boss upon returning, I figured you must have gone to Takehi's mansion and waited for you at this mountain pass. Well, things being thus, it can no longer be helped. Make a clean break with Ai right here, and promise to dedicate yourself as the boss's wife, and that will be fine; but if you spew this and that and refuse to listen, I will make your torso and head into two, how about that? Pull your wits together and give a definite reply. No matter how much you fret, Ai is already in the boss's hands, so it is no use. Rather, how about gently becoming Sankō's henchman? Is it fine to have your life taken, or is it fine to become a henchman? Give me one of two replies.'

Torakō: 'Putting on an air like a crow's scarecrow, you self-deluded fly-bug. What kind of nonsense are you spewing? You are the very one whose head and torso I shall make into two, prepare yourself.'

Tamakō: 'Now, now, Torakō-sama, this is a minor matter before a major one, so you must not get angry now. Look at this. Gradually the crystal ball has become cloudy.'

Tamakō: 'Now, now, Torakō-sama, this is a minor matter before a major one, so you must not get angry now. Look at this. Gradually the crystal ball has become cloudy.'

Torakō: 'Bah, now that things have come to this, it is do-or-die. My manly pride will not let my spleen rest unless I go as far as I can and try whatever I can do. Tamakō, since you will only become a hindrance and get in the way of my actions if I rampage as much as I please now, you stay out of it. Hey, Shinkō, Kiukō, Yatsukō, you all run away somewhere too.'

So saying, quicker than picking up a weather-beaten, knotty piece of wood about a shaku long that had fallen there, Torakō dashed into the crowd of forty to fifty people while swinging it around. Rokukō and his men lost their nerve at this vigor and fled in utter disarray.

Torakō: 'Aha-ha-ha, what weaklings they turned out to be. Sankō cannot be much of anything either, if he keeps such good-for-nothings. Hey Tamakō, Shin, Kiu, Yatsu, it is safe now, come out.'

At this voice, the four of them came close to Torakō with their faces entirely covered in spiderwebs, looking pale and trembling in the legs.

Shinkō: 'My, Torakō-sama, what formidable horsepower came out of you.'

Torakō: 'I wonder why on earth my guts were so settled and my strength came out only today. Even to myself, it does not make sense.'

Tamakō: 'When I hid in the tree thicket and watched, seventy or eighty rough warriors, looking just like you, appeared from nowhere and swung around massive pieces of timber, so Rokurō's scoundrels and everyone else fled in that miserable state just like that. It truly was an incomprehensible and mysterious thing.'

Hearing this, Torakō shed tears, sat formally straight upon the great earth, clapped his hands, and finished chanting the Amatsu Norito.

Torakō: 'Lord Kami Susanoo no Ōkami, you have graciously helped me, thank you very much. Concerning this, given this situation, I cannot help but worry about Ai's circumstances, so please, once again, as you have helped me, please help Ai's circumstances. I beg of you.'

虎公は、玉公、新公、久公、八公の一行と共に火の国街道に漸く立ち現はれた。此処は樫の木の大木が太陽の光線を包んで遮つて居る、天然椅子の岩のある場所で、黒姫が手長猿に悩まされた処であつた。大蛇の三公の乾児六公は数十人の手下を引き連れ、樫の木の下に虎公の行方を求めつつ待つて居た。

虎公は道々歌を謡ひながら何気なくここ迄来て見れば、喧嘩装束で身をかためた六公の一行、棍棒匕首を携へながら谷道に立ち塞がり、

六公『オイ、虎の野郎、昨日から貴様の所在を探して居たのだ。高山峠の絶頂に往きよつたと確に知つた故、後追ひかけて往つてみれば貴様は早くも風を喰つて、卑怯未練にも姿を隠しやがつた。俺は帰つて親分に申訳が無いから、大方貴様が建日の館へ往きよつたのだと思つて此山口に待つて居たのだ。サアこうなつては最早叶ふまい。ここで綺麗薩張と、お愛の縁を切り、親分さまの女房に奉る、と約束を致せばよし、四の五の吐して聞かないと、胴と首とを二つにしてやるがどうだ。性念を据ゑて確り返答をせい。何程貴様が焦つたところで、お愛は最早親分の手に入つて居るのだから駄目だぞ。それより柔しく三公の乾児になつたらどうだ。命を取られるが好いか、乾児になるのが好いか、二つに一つの返答をしろ』

虎公『烏のおどしのやうな態をしやがつて、身の程知らずの蠅虫め。何劫託を吐きやがるのだ。貴様こそ首と胴とを二つにしてやらア、覚悟せい』

玉公『これこれ虎公さま、大事の前の小事だから、今怒つてはいけませぬ。これ見なさい。だんだん水晶玉が曇つて来ました』

虎公『ヤアもう斯うなつては破れかぶれだ。男の意地でどこ迄もやれるだけやつて見にや虫が納まらねえや。玉公、貴様は俺が今暴れ放題暴れて見るから足手纏ひになつては俺の活動の邪魔になる。オイ、新公、久公、八公も共にどつかへ逃げて了へ』

と云ひながら、虎公は其処に落ちてあつた一間許りの節だらけの、雨に曝された木片を拾ふより早く、四五十人の群に向つて振り廻しつつ暴れ込んだ。六公は此元気に肝を潰し散々ばらばらとなつて逃げ失せて仕舞つた。

虎公『アハヽヽヽ、何と弱い奴計り寄つたものだなあ。大蛇の三公もこれだけ穀潰しを抱へて居ては大抵ぢやあるまい。オイ玉公、新、久、八、もう大丈夫だ、出て来い』

この声に四人は顔一面蜘蛛の巣だらけになつて、真青の顔をしながら足もわなわな虎公の傍に寄つて来た。

新公『何と虎公さま、偉い馬力が出たものだなあ』

虎公『今日に限つて何故あれ程肝玉が据わり、力が出たのだらう。自分ながらに合点がゆかないのだ』

玉公『俺が木の茂みへ隠れて見て居たら、お前と同じ姿をした荒武者が七八十人どこからともなく現はれて、大きな材木を振り廻したものだから、六の野郎を初め、どいつもこいつもあの通り悲惨な態で逃げよつたのだ。本当に合点のゆかぬ不思議の事だつた』

虎公はこれを聞いて涙を流し大地に端坐し、拍手を打ち天津祝詞を奏上し終り、

虎公『神素盞嗚大神様、よくもお助け下さいました、有難う御座います。就きましては此様子では、お愛の身の上案じられてなりませぬから、どうぞも一度、私をお助け下さいましたやうに、お愛の身の上をお助け下さいませ。お願ひ申します』

§ 2

Shedding tears of gratitude, falling one after another, they bow their heads to the earth and pray.

Torakō, together with Tamakō and other kobun, once again beats time along the sloping path, singing as they set off running toward their home.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! / The Kami has appeared in the world, / Distinguishing clearly between good and evil. / Morning and evening, bowing low before the grand Mimahe of the Ōkami, / Pouring out our murakimo hearts, pouring out our bodies, / For the reward of having served devotedly, / When surrounded—untokosiyo— / By the six subordinates who are the kobun of Sankō of the Orochi, / At that very moment, / The boundless and merciful Mizumitama, / The Great Deity Kamususanōnōokami, / Displaying His majestic mitama, / Manifesting countless Kami, / And granting reinforcement / To Torakō, who stood entirely alone. / Truly, this is the grace of the Kami, wondrous and profound! / Ananai-kyō saves the world, / The one true path of the Kami. / Now more than ever—dotsukoisiyo—

It has grown so sacred and exalted! / With the divine virtue thus revealed— / Untokodotsuko! Upon this, / Though Sankō of the Orochi / Should lead countless subordinates / To the empty home of Torakō-sama, / And attack with all severity, / There is nothing whatsoever to fear! / My stubborn-minded wife, / By now, without a doubt, through the shintoku / Of the divine command of Susanō, / Is scattering the approaching enemies like flowers in a tempest, / Untokodotsuko! Driving them completely away, / Raising shouts of victory so that Torakō, / Untokodotsuko! In fine spirits, / May wait for his return, / Preparing warmed sake—dotsukoisiyo— / Making ready—untokosiyo— / Waiting for him, of that there is no doubt!

Though the morning sun shine or be clouded, / Though the moon wax or wane, / Though the earth itself sink into the abyss, / Entrusting one's body to the teachings of Ananai-kyō, / The one true path of the Kami, / And crossing this world morning and evening / With a sincere heart put forth to the utmost— / What are fierce lions and bears to this? / What are wolves and wild beasts? / Much less Sankō of the Orochi / And his weak band of subordinates! / Though hundreds of them should come, / Kicking them away one by one from the very edge, / Bringing them to repentance right before our eyes. / Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! / May the mitama grant us peace and prosperity, / And the teachings of Ananai-kyō— / Calling them the demons of this world, / Beginning with Sankō of the Orochi, / And all the rest of his followers,

Illuminating their hearts, Kamunagara, / Save them unto the true path, / We earnestly and solely pray, / Untokodotsuko, dotsukoisiyo! / And speaking of which, Kurohime and Fusayoshi, / By now, to the capital—dotsukoisiyo— / Have they safely arrived—untokose?— / Suddenly that weighs heavily on my mind. / Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! / O Kokutamakami's pure Yoshikohime, / O Tsukiteruhiko-no-kamisama, / Please watch over the future of these three / With gentle protection, I beseech you! / Untokodotsuko! Torakō, / From here returning straight to his home, / Settling the commotion in the household, / Heading onward to the capital of Hino-no-kuni, / Pursuing the footsteps of Kurohime-sama, / To hear the teachings of the sacred Kami,

Might I be granted the chance to listen—dotsukoisiyo— / That is a joy above all others! / Untokodotsuko, dotsukoisiyo! / Well now, what a blunder, before I knew it, / Taking the wrong path—dotsukoisiyo— / I have wandered unawares and astray / Into the valley path of the Hinode Pass. / My, oh my, whatever could this be? / There lies a perilous log bridge ahead; / Terrifying to cross, yet if I do not cross it, / There is no way whatsoever to return to my home.

If I turn back from here / And take the road by which I originally came, / Though it would be easy to return home, / If I were to do such a thing, / It would take too much time and be of no use. / To press on across the Hinode Pass / And return heading toward my home / Will likely be half an hour quicker. / Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara!

This too must be some divine arrangement / That has led me astray to this very place. / Indeed, whatever happens in the world of men / Must be looked at straight, heard straight, and spoken straight; / Rectify your own worldly transgressions. / The divine teachings of Ananai-kyō / Are now more profoundly understood than ever before. / We can never match the deep wisdom / Of the vast and boundless Kami.

How could we warped-minded ones ever fathom / The sacred heart of Heaven and Earth? / There is nothing left to do but entrust ourselves / Completely to the will of the Kami. / No matter how perilous this bridge may be, / Untokodotsuko! What time is there to hesitate? / Throwing open our fierce courage / Like taking a single bound over the caldron of hell—untokosiyo—

Shall we try crossing, Tamakō? / Arata, Hisashi, Yatsu—you three, / Follow this Torakō! / One, two, three!' So saying, / Like an arrow shot from a bow, across the log bridge, / Spreading both arms, making his body light, / He crosses over in a single dash. / Just at that moment, from the opposite side, / Led by Kurohime-tsukasa herself, / Along with his wife Io and even Ume, / Escorted by two men, / Pushing their way through the thicket of the woods, / They appear—what a startling surprise! / Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! / May the mitama grant us peace and prosperity.
