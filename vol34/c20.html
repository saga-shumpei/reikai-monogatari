玉卜〔961〕

§ 1

In the inner chamber of Takehi's mansion, the master Takekuniwake and his wife Takenohime sit facing one another, deeply absorbed in whispered conversation.

Takenohime: 'Goshiyujinsama, today we had an unexpected guest, and that person known as Kurohime-sama also seems to have gone through a great deal of hardship. Seeing how she looks somewhat worn out wherever she goes, it appears she has been worrying a great deal over the matter of your son, doesn't it?'

Takekuniwake: 'That is so, yet parents are truly precious things. That intent with which she went out of her way to visit us, wondering if perhaps I might not be her own child, is filled with truly pure, beautiful, and tender affection. If my own parents were still living healthily in this world, they too would surely be searching for me with such tender affection as Kurohime-sama possesses. When I think upon this, the grace of the Kamisama and of my parents fills my heart with gratitude until tears fall. Ah, where and how might my parents now be? With only the desire to meet my parents, I came to believe in the Kamisama, and today I have been shaped into such an excellent Sendenshi. If I indeed had undeniable parents and had been raised at a parent's knees since early childhood, I would have drifted into ease and comfort, and could never have possibly opened the splendid path of the Kamisama. When I reflect upon this, even the fact that the whereabouts of my parents remain unknown is rather a blessing for my own person and the deep, vast divine will of the Kamisama.'

Takenohime: 'That must indeed be so. The Kamisama look down upon and perceive all things without distinction of far and near, wide and narrow, large and small, light and dark, so their whereabouts must surely be perfectly clear to Them. Yet no matter when divine possession occurs, no matter how much we ask about the whereabouts of your parents, They seal Their lips and do not grant even a single word or half-sentence of revelation. In short, this must be the whip of affection, wishing to pity us as husband and wife and fashion us into splendid divine ministers. Once the Kamisama look upon us and think that we are now all right and that sincerity has been thoroughly carried out, They will surely let us know their whereabouts. Or perhaps, due to some circumstances, They may even draw your parents right here while they live. Please try not to indulge in needless worry.'

Takekuniwake: 'That is so. From this day forth, let us think no more of the matter of my parents. No matter how much we fret, as human beings there is nothing we can do about it; therefore, rather than that, devoting our utmost efforts as a Sendenshi for the sake of the Kamisama and for the sake of the people of the world will surely be the greatest thing of all.'

Takenohime: 'Ah, you have spoken well. Henceforth, leave everything to the heart of the Ōkami, and though it is not my place to say such things, the Kamisama will surely protect your parents in a good way, so please put it out of your mind. It is never because I think lightly of your parents that I say this; I speak solely for the sake of your happiness and for the sake of your parents...'

Takekuniwake: 'You must have found it quite unpleasant when I always wore a troubled expression while thinking of my parents, didn't you?'

Takenohime: 'Yes, I did not find it particularly unpleasant, but because the color of your worries and sufferings would time and again appear upon your face, I worried only lest it should harm your body. Please, show me your beautiful face just from this moment onward.'

Takenohime: 'Yes, I did not find it particularly unpleasant, but because the color of your worries and sufferings would time and again appear upon your face, I worried only lest it should harm your body. Please, show me your beautiful face just from this moment onward.'

Takekuniwake: 'I am truly sorry for having caused you worry. Leaving it to the Kamisama as of this day, I shall worry no more about the matter of my parents. Henceforth I shall not utter even a single word of lamentation, so please be at ease. One thing I wish to speak to you about as my final word is that the end of Kurohime-sama's words somehow felt as though they had some deep connection; how do you think of it?'

Torakō: 'Oh, indeed we have been treated to a grand feast, and as I am far too intoxicated, it would be discourteous, so I must ask to be excused here.'

Takekuniwake: 'Torakō-sama, please take your time. Today is my celebration, so you must let yourself drink your fill. Is it not far too early? Please take your time and stay the night this evening, and let us hear some interesting stories.'

Torakō: 'Yes, I am grateful for your kindness, but a mutation has appeared in the crystal ball which Tamakō, who has come here now, possesses and which he says was bestowed by Hinode-kami, and I cannot help feeling anxious about it. Judging from the cloudiness reflected in the ball, it seems that some unusual matter has come to pass at my unoccupied home, and since I cannot help feeling restless in my heart either, I will take my leave with this today.'

Takekuniwake: 'That must be a worry for you. I say, Tamakō, is it nothing of great consequence?'

Tamakō: 'Yes, based on my experience, I feel as though something tremendous is happening at the oyabun's home. Nevertheless, although an omen appears that in the end it will amount to nothing, there is a risk that if we dawdle, the incident will grow larger and more difficult, so I will take my leave with this.'

Takekuniwake: 'If you say so, there is nothing more to be done. So that there will be no untoward events at your unattended home, we two husband and wife will henceforth offer our prayers before the deities, so please return with peace of mind.'

Torakō: 'Yes, thank you very much.'

So saying while shedding tears, they bowed twice and the whole company together departed from this place, stepping briskly away.

Torakō's party came as far as the front gate. Ikukō, the gatekeeper, was dead drunk on celebratory sake, and with an unfluent tongue,

Ikukō: 'Hey, Torakō oyabun, is it not a bit early? Why not take a cup with me a little more leisurely? No matter how much you hurry, when the time comes for the sun to set, it sets just the same, you know.'

Torakō: 'Um, thank you. But today, for some reason or other, my heart is filled with foreboding, so I am going to return for the time being. I will come to play again later. You too, obtain a day's leave from Goshiyujinsama and come play; there is sake prepared no matter how much you want.'
