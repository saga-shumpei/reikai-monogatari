Chapter 13: 行進 Song [954]

行進歌〔954〕

§ 1

Fusakō and Yoshikō, accompanied by Torakō and three others, head toward the mansion of Takehi, proceeding along the road while chattering idly.

Fusakō raises his voice and begins to chant:

Ananai-kyō's Sendenshi, While Kurohime of pitch-black hue, Desired to seek her husband's arika, She brought along three companions three, Magokō, Fusa, and Yoshi, From Onokorojima away, To the ancient site of Takehi's wake, A short way before the stony ihaya, Magokō-sama chanced to stumble down. Fusa and Yoshi in sudden giyōten, Flustered and crying out for water and kitsuke, Though they rush to his kaihō, For some strange reason Kurohime-sama Stands smiling with a broad grin on her face. Thinking within their hearts with fungai, What a cold and hidau onibaba she is, Watch out, watch out, it is dangerous there, For there are sharp stones lying about, Let everyone and koitsu beware, Stumbling just like Magokō did, Would never do if you sustain a kega.

Watch out, watch out, toward the wakimichi, My humble song has derailed its track, And has grown quite impossible to make out. While Kurohime-sama for some reason keeps uttering abuse, She leaves behind Magokō, who suffers in pain, Without even turning her glance to see him off. We two, with no other choice left to us, Follow along closely after Kurohime-sama, And when we arrive to look before the ihaya, The curtain of darkness is instantly Pulled down so that neither eye nor nose, Nor even the arika of the mouth can be known. At this moment, the party of three Recite the Amatsu Norito in prayer, And just as they pause to rest a while, A booming voice echoes from within the ihaya, Wondering whatever kind of apparition this might be, Scarcely have they set their hearts on alert when From inside the ihaya someone shouts out loud at Kurohime, Calling her a wicked old hag.

Kurohime-sama grows furiously angry, And quarreling back and forth about this and that, She chants a song full of broken waka lines, And barely manages to gloss over the awkward spot, Escaping with her life by a narrow margin as she cries heave-ho, Waiting for daybreak as they make their escape, The steep slope stretches to right and left, Where weeds grow thick and rank and wild, As they push panting up the narrow Hozoya road, Their throats parched and dry in their distress, Beside them a sweet, pure spring bubbles up gushingly, And moistening their throats with the water, They are lectured on a thousand subjects by Kurohime-sama, And after making the water longer than an eon, They stretch out flat and heavy on their sides. What on earth is wrong with the two of them, Their lower bodies adhere firmly to the great earth, And in their agony of not being able to budge an inch, They are misunderstood by Kurohime-sama, And left entirely behind and abandoned to their fate. How utterly spiritless we two have been, As Kurohime-sama leaves us behind and climbs on ahead, The two of them gaze after her retreating figure, Murmuring that Kurohime is a wicked old hag, When instantly, like a record-breaking storm, A violent gale arises, Sweeping away giant boulders and felling trees, With a rain falling like showers of pebbles. Thinking this is truly more than they can bear, Just as they single-mindedly pray to the gods of heaven and earth, Suddenly a sendenka is heard, With the jewel voice of Tamajirōwake, Scarcely is it heard when the raging Wind and rain instantly grow calm, And clouds of peace in the mid-heaven Begin to tremble with a wondrous joy.

From then on, as we two pursue Kurohime's footsteps, Along the descending slope of the Takayama pass, On a steep and rugged path of jagged rocks, A party of four suddenly crosses our path. Thinking instantly that koitsu must surely be a thief, The two of them, jumping to rash conclusions, Decide to pretend to be the tengu's grandchildren, And while they are thinking of how to intimidate them, Unexpectedly Torakō speaks up, offering to give away his straw sandals, So they accept them against their better judgment, And though it is not to their liking, heave-ho, They have worn them all the way to this place. Chattering in an amusing and lively fashion About Yoshikō's lean-sumō Urushiyama, And the sumo tale of Otonigatake, The six of them now take on the airs of brothers, And set out toward Takehi's mansion in the land of fire.

Doubtless by now Kurohime has had an audience with Hinodewake, Saying, are you my child or are you my mother, I wanted to meet you, I wanted to see you, This is the destined meeting of noble deities, Could there ever be such a joyful thing as this? Come what may, first of all, They will recite the Amatsu Norito in prayer, Offering up their grateful devotions, While everyone in the mansion, high and low, gathers together, To celebrate with great abundance, Doubtless by now the divine mansion Is in an uproar from top to bottom, Rejoicing and taking heart in their elation. Ah, precious deities of Ame-no-Minakanushi, May your august spirits shine forth in peace, And may Kurohime-sama too abandon her selfish ego and stubborn pride, To become a true person of the gods who illuminates With the divine light of Ananai-kyō."

So chanting, Fusakō follows along behind Torakō. Led on by this song, Torakō once again raises his voice to chant:

Takeno's village Tamakō, / How is it that lately / The crystal ball, the family treasure, / Has developed a black spot, making him fret in anxiety? / This is surely none other than / The wicked deity called Kurohime / Who has come to this island, without a doubt. / Now, hurrying to the mountain peak / If I climb up and wait, Kurohime / Will surely climb up here, / Hey, Torakō, for my sake, / Although it is a steep slope, / Won't you please escort me? / Counting on you as a real man, / This is my earnest request as he poured out his words, / Thus making time amidst his busy schedule, / Dragging along his underlings like dry children, / At the summit of Takayamatauge, / Kurohime of Ananai-kyō / As she climbs up, Dokkoishiyō.

While waiting impatiently, / Prompted by Tamakō-sama's initiative, / Gathering white and black stones, / Just as they began the contest, / Before long, Kurohime / With her body braced in waraji and kiyahan, / Wearing a kongauzue and minokasa, / In light attire, with a swish, / Breathing heavily, she comes climbing up. / This fellow without a doubt must be Kurohime, Dokkoishiyō. / Everyone be careful, it is dangerous, / One slip and you are at the bottom of the ravine. / While listening with ears alert / To Kurohime-sama's tale, / Hearing that she was the wife / Of Takayamahiko, peerless in the capital of fire, / The five were startled, / Thinking this is a matter that does not make sense, / And that they cannot carelessly lay a hand on her.

Where on earth had his initial momentum gone? / Torakō, who had been full of high spirits, / Suddenly transformed into a cat in an instant. / Good grief, what kind of matter is this? / There are points that do not make sense. / Moreover, one more mysterious thing is, / When Kurohime-sama was young, / The abandoned child in Takeno village / At the divine mansion, Dokkoishiyō, / Might she not be Kenrokubake? / Thinking she might go and inquire, / Because he spoke as if with a purpose, / Tamakō-sama swallowed it down / And swiftly changed roles to become the guide, / Turning into the sole strength / Of Kurohime, whom they had thought a demon, / And guiding her to the divine mansion. / As Kurohime-sama says, / Fusa and Yoshiki, those two attendant fellows, / Must be coming from behind, without a doubt.

Torakō-sama, you fellows, / Wait here for both of them, / Catch them without allowing any yes or no, / And bring them to the mansion of Takehiko. / I earnestly entrust this to you, / Because he spoke with a dark face, / The men swallowed it down, / And Torakō-sama too, Dokkoishiyō, / Once asked by someone, / This disposition cannot pull back, / While waiting now and then / For those two weak maggots, Fusa-kō and Yoshiki-kō, / Fusa-kō-sama, running barefoot, / With blood flowing from his feet, / Bragging emptily that he is a tengu's grandson, / How amusing as he descends the slope! / Thereupon Torakō calls out, / And when he inquires, they are the two / Who came following after Kurohime's rear.

Thinking it was in the spirit of social service, / He threw out a pair of waraji / And said, 'Put these on!' / Whereupon the two fellows rejoiced, / Like rotten girls, / Flowing tears while sniffling, / 'Yes, yes, we gratefully receive them. / Though we know not who or what person you are, / This myself being a stranger, / To receive them without any price / Does not settle my heart, / Please take the price,' / Such an upright statement! / Thinking these fellows must be believers / Who uphold the path of the hemp pillar, / Torakō gave them spirit, / And the amusement of their empty boasting changing instantly, / As their shoulders and elbows puffed up in pride, / Yoshiki-kō-sama, carried away, / 'Even I, originally from Yoshinogawa, / Made a name for myself at Jitentōshima, / A sumo wrestler who has never been defeated, / Onigadake and Dokkoishiyō, / When we had a two-match contest, / At first I was defeated, / But afterwards the other side won,' / Making excuses one way or another, / Dokkoi dokkoi dokkoi-shiyō, / While rapping out a nonstop stream of talk, / In the very end, Fusa-kō / Exposed his actual circumstances, / 'Yoshinogawa, Dokkoishiyō, / A sumo wrestler living next door, / A fundoshi holder, Dokkoishiyō, / A fellow who eats rice balls, / A pot-lid you look up at when taken,' / Thus proclaiming his name as a skinny wrestler, / 'If you touch me, I lose, untokoshiyō,' / Hitting his name as Urushiyama,
