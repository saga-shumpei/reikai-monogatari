Chapter 8: 暴 Wind Rain [949]

暴風雨〔949〕

§ 1

Fusakō and Yoshikō, for reasons unknown, found their lower bodies sticking to the ground as if glued, and became completely unable to move a single inch.

Kurohime urged them on, saying, 'Hurry, hurry.'

Yet, in reality, their bodies refused to move even the slightest bit.

Kurohime, totally oblivious to this situation and exasperated beyond measure, sharpened her voice and spoke.

Kurohime: 'Hey, hey, you two! Coming to a place like this and intending to trouble this Kurohime—why won't you stand up when I've explained things so thoroughly?'

Fusakō: 'Kurohime-sama, no matter what you say, for some reason my legs won't stand at all, wow... Now look here, Yoshikō, how about you? Do you feel like you can move even a little?'

Yoshikō: 'I don't know what's wrong with me either, I can't move even a tiny bit. It's as if some soft-shelled turtle down at the bottom of the earth is clinging on and sucking us down; no matter how much I struggle, I can't get any movement. Ah, we've gotten ourselves into a terrible mess... Hello, Kurohime-sama, please perform chinkon for us one time!'

Kurohime: 'Hah! To think you were chattering away so much until just now, eating all those figs, and yet with such a perky face you can sit there and say your legs won't stand and your hips won't move! Move or no move, I'll take my leave first.'

With a sharp snap, she shook her body, and wearing a dissatisfied expression, she climbed away step by step.

In the blink of an eye, Kurohime's figure was hidden by the thicket of trees.

Yoshikō: 'Hey, Kurohime is a terribly cold-hearted wretch, isn't she... "When fallen into decline and tears stain one's sleeves, the depths of people's hearts are truly known"—there was an old poem like that, wasn't there? Because of Kurohime, haven't we been able to taste the meaning of this verse in actual practice?'

Fusakō: 'Oh, that's right... With hips gone numb and mountain paths blurred by tears, The heart of Kurohime-sama is truly known... Kurohime always prattling away with her glib tongue, The tail she deceitfully wagged has indeed become visible... That's it. Aha-ha-ha-ha!'

Yoshikō: 'With a body where hips and legs cannot stand like this, Instead of slandering the affairs of others... When the time truly comes that your hips and legs cannot stand, There is nothing for it but to die by the roadside...'

Fusakō: 'Hey, Yoshi, you shouldn't say such faint-hearted things. It may be that the Kamisama, for some divine convenience, has granted us a brief respite. Probably right around over here, there is a huge serpent lurking and waiting to swallow up our whole party, so the Kamisama of great mercy and great compassion purposely made it so our legs wouldn't stand in order to save us, who knows. No matter what happens, let's interpret things with good intentions, look anew and listen anew through Shinmidai-Naomidai, and as long as we maintain sheer sincerity—never forgetting Kamisama even when meeting with disasters, and never forgetting Kamisama even when meeting with joy—there is no doubt that Kamisama will save us. Come, from now on let's offer up divine words and pray for the healing of our illnesses!'

Yoshikō: 'That is so indeed. However, Kurohime-sama went on ahead by herself, and I wonder if she might be swallowed by that serpent. I can't help worrying about that. No matter how hateful an old hag's words can be, after all, I can't help feeling sorry for her.'

Fusakō: 'That is the true heart of a human being. Even for me, although I've been making a fuss and teasing Kurohime-sama, a woman who would travel all the way following behind her to come to a place like this is a rarity indeed. Truly, it is a noble aspiration for a wife. To tell the truth, I am already deeply moved by that heart of Kurohime's. First and foremost, let us pray with all our might that there be no disasters and no injuries along her way, and secondly, let us pray for the healing of our own illnesses, shall we?'

Yoshikō: 'So you think that way too after all? That is truly grateful; human nature is truly good after all.'

Fusakō: 'That is the very reason why human beings are the lords of all creation. It is the divine heart. Come now, even though our hips won't stand, nothing else is wrong with us, so this is still something of a blessing from Kamisama. First let us offer words of gratitude, and then make our prayers.'

§ 2

So saying, the two finished reciting the Amatsunorito and quietly began to concentrate their prayers.

Fusakō: 'Ah, beginning with Ōkuniharutachinōomikoto, the Great Primordial Spirit of the universe who created and solidified heaven and earth and lovingly nurtures all things, along with the heavenly gods, the earthly gods, and the myriad gods and goddesses: through your noble radiance and deep, warm grace, we were allowed to be born upon this precious earth and spent precious days without any inconveniences. And yet, unknowingly accumulating heavy sins and transgressions, failing to fulfill our heavenly vocation as human beings, you did not punish us, your inadequate servants, but cared for us, helped us, and allowed us to pass through this world peacefully and joyously; we gratefully thank you for your vast and warm grace. ...This time, accompanying Kurohime-sama, the missionary of Ananai-kyō, we crossed the boundless ocean, were safely allowed to cross to this island of Tsukushi, and arrived here safely, embraced within the bosom of the gods. Nevertheless, by whatever divine providence, as both of us rested our breath in the shade of this tree, miraculously our legs became unable to stand. This too is surely the retribution of our heavy sins coming home to us. We can never fathom the boundless and unfathomable divine heart of the gods, but since the gods are noble parents who love us human beings to the utmost, we gratefully thank you for binding our bodies thus for the sake of some attentive divine protection toward us. Accordingly, Kurohime-sama grew angered a step ahead and climbed this slope. We earnestly pray that no troubles or disasters arise along the way, and that she may safely reach the capital of the Land of Fire, and we ask that you grant her your special grace. Moreover, no matter what deep sins and transgressions we two may possess, please look upon us again, listen to us again, and rectify our declarations within your great, merciful, and compassionate divine heart, and we pray with awe and reverence that you grant freedom to these bodies of ours as quickly as possible, even for a moment; ah, Kamunagara sakiwahae mase.'

So saying, they clasped their hands and shed tears of gratitude in a steady stream. No matter how much they prayed, by some turn of events, the bodies of the two did not budge an inch.

The sun gradually slanted toward the western mountains, black clouds suddenly rose all at once, and rain like pebbles fell sporadically. Whether it was thunder, a storm, or the outbreak of an earthquake, an indescribable, desolate aura enveloped all four directions.

Without yielding or bending, and with all their might—may the Great Ancestral Goddess who protects Ananai-kyō protect both of us, as well as the person of Kurohime...—they concentrated their prayers with single-minded devotion. A gale, strong enough to seemingly knock down the ancient trees of the mountain, suddenly began to blow, making huge rocks fly in all directions like leaves, felling trees, splitting branches, and the terrifying sound thereof was enough to make anything weep.

At this time, from nowhere amidst the wind, a missionary song came floating in.

Tamajiwake: 'The god has appeared outwardly, Distinguishing good from evil. It is the direct-sun deity who created this world, With a heart as vast as the grand direct-sun deity. Whatever happens in the world of men, Look upon it directly, listen to it directly, Rectify the errors of your body by proclaiming them anew. The missionary of Ananai-kyō, I am Lord Tamajiwake. Hearing that Kurohime of Ananai-kyō Has crossed over to the island of Tsukushi, Wishing to seek out Takayamahiko, Entrusting her body to a boat without ribs, And that she has come across, I left the hall of the Saien, Struck across Mesopotamia, Stepped my oar into the Jordan River, Traversed the sea of Fusa, And when I finally came here and looked, A mountain storm beyond all expectation. Truly a desolate spectacle! Nevertheless, we are The missionaries of the True Way, How could we fear any manner of thing? Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, Whether the moon fills or wanes, Even if the great earth sinks, Even if rocks cause rain to fall, Even if Mount Tsukushigadake is torn apart, This body entrusted to the gods— With the sincere heart of Tamajiwake, Suppressing any sort of wind, Sweeping away the hundred disasters Of the common folk beneath the heavens, My heart is to help them on. Ah, divine spirits, gods upon gods, May the divine spirit flourish and Appear as the Kunitama deity, The divine pillar of the pure-world princess, Add strength unto me!'

I from this place unto the capital of the Land of Fire shall go forth, and Kurohime's dark path of straying Kohigumo, with Ibukiharahi sweeping away, making the true soul shine, at the Chiushinchi of Onokorojima, at the foothills of the mountain of Yotsuo, knowing well the Ōmiyabashira, before the calm-residing Kami, shall lead back and aid them, with the blessing of the Kami's Mitama, this Yamaarashi swiftly calming down, when Tamaharuwake's Oshihetsukasa will swiftly meet Kurohime of Ananai-kyō, and convey the Mikotoba of the Kami even a day the sooner, ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara, may the Mitama's blessings rest upon you.'

The voice singing thus came echoing into the ears of the two men.

Fusakō: 'Oi Yoshikō, did you hear that Sendenka? It seems the Sendenshi of Tamaharuwake has appeared close by; the Kamisama are truly wondrous things. We two who were abandoned by Kurohime-sama, with legs and loins unable to stand, just as we were suffering and writhing in agony, encountered a record-breaking violent storm, and though we believed in the protection of the Kamisama, trembling in fear and wondering what would come of it, we were anxious about our own bodies even now, but the grace of the Kamisama is truly venerable. To think we would meet Lord Tamaharuwake-no-Mikoto in such a place, something we, who are not Kami, could never have known. Ah, how thankful I am... Kamisama, you have immediately bestowed your divine virtue right before the eyes of us two. I have no words whatsoever to express my thanks.'

Thus he gave thanks with tears. Yoshikō also, sniffing and sniveling while weeping in hiccups, joined his hands and expressed his gratitude. When it seemed that the voice of the Sendenka had stopped completely, the violent storm that until now had raged as if blowing the mountains to pieces was swept away as if wiped clean, and in the sky, through the rents in the clouds, the skin of the blue clouds began to flicker into view. Opening the curtain of clouds, the sun of heaven at last began to shine obliquely upon the heads of the two.

Yoshikō: 'Kamisama, thank you very much. For this layer upon layer of grace, I beg of you, please let us meet the owner of that Sendenka just for a moment, I ask of you.'

Joining his hands, he was once again absorbed in prayer. Mysteriously, before they knew it, the loins of the two men had become able to move freely.

Fusakō: 'Ah, how thankful I am, my legs can stand, my loins are healed. Oi Yoshikō, how about you? Try standing up a bit, I am like this.'

Stamping his ground-shaking feet, he danced wildly with joy. Yoshikō also, with anxious hesitation, quietly raised his waist.

Yoshikō: 'Yay, before I knew it, the Kamisama have healed me too, ah, how thankful, how awe-inspiring... Now Fusakō, from here on, shall we search for the direction from which the voice of that Sendenka came?'

Fusakō: 'How wondrous it is. The voice of the Sendenka was heard quite close by, yet even when I look far out over the sloping path we climbed up in this manner, no shadow resembling a person is to be seen. Yet that voice seemed to have been heard from below this slope. What a wondrous thing. I certainly heard it sung as Tamaharuwake-tsukasa.'

Yoshikō: 'I certainly heard it that way too. If by any chance, that Sendenshi rested beneath the wasp nest just now, and was stung even in the eyes by those ferocious green wasps, perhaps he is suffering.'

Fusakō: 'Surely there is no cause to worry about such a clumsy thing happening, and besides, since he is a Sendenshi with that much divine power, he must surely possess the wasp cloth and the serpent fin. You need not worry needlessly like that.'

Yoshikō: 'Is that so. If so, let us slowly climb this slope from now on. Kurohime-sama must be terribly troubled by the violent storm just now, so we must surely catch up and offer her our condolences. The Sendenshi of Tamaharuwake may perhaps be upon this slope as well. It may be that the voice was heard as if coming from below due to the swirling of the wind or the echo of the trees. Now let us go.'

Saying thus, the two men, using their iron-shod staves as strength, climbed the steep slope once again. Kamunagara Mita Ma Sachi Haimasaseyo.

と四股ふみならし、嬉しげに踊り狂ふ。芳公も案じ案じソウと腰を上げてみた。

芳公『ヤア俺もいつの間にか、神様に直して貰つた、あゝ有難し勿体なし、……サア房公、是からあの宣伝歌の声のした方を捜してみようぢやないか』

房公『どうも不思議だなア。つい間近に聞えた宣伝歌の声、斯うして登つて来た坂路を遠く見はらしてみても、人らしい影は見えない。乍併あの声は此坂の下から聞えて来た様だ。不思議なことがあるものだなア。確に吾れは玉治別司と歌はれた様に聞えたがなア』

芳公『確に俺もさう聞いた。ヒヨツとしたら、あの宣伝使が最前の蜂の巣の下で、休息され、あの猛烈な青蜂に目でもさされて、苦んで御座るのだあろまいかな』

房公『ヨモヤそんなヘマなことはなさる気遣ひもあるまい、又あれ丈神力のある宣伝使のことだから、蜂の布も大蛇のヒレも持つて御座るに違ない。そんな取越苦労はせなくてもよからうぞよ』

芳公『そうだらうかなア。そんなら、これからボツボツ此坂を登ることとしよう。黒姫様も最前の暴風雨で、嘸お困りだらうから、一つ追つついて御慰問を申上げねばなろまいぞ。玉治別の宣伝使も、或は此坂の上に御座るのかも分らない。風の吹きまはしやら、木谺の反響で、下の方から声がしたやうに聞えたのだらうも知れぬ。サア行かう』

と言ひ乍ら、両人は金剛杖を力に急坂を又もや登り行く。惟神霊幸倍坐世。
