Chapter 9: Jewel の黒点 [950]

玉の黒点〔950〕

§ 1

At the center of the mountain range of Tsukushigadake, upon the summit of Takayamatauge, four or five men sat in a circle whispering about something, arranging black and white stones upon the sand, and contending in the game of go.

A: 'No matter what I do, this black stone is so stubborn that it gets in the way and I cannot help it. If I were only to kill off this one, the rest would be a sweeping victory, you see.'

B: 'Speak not foolishly; in this world of ours, hardship (black) is of the utmost essence. Do you think that things can be accomplished without hardship?'

C: 'This pure white stone, from which all defilements, obscurations, and dust have been cleared away, is just like the divine spirit of the Kamisama. Look well; is it not translucent to the center like a crystal? In days of old, when the Hinodewake Kamisama went forth to the land of Fire, my grandfather guided Him there, and as a token of gratitude, was given a crystal ball; to this day it is preserved with great care as our family treasure, and if one goes before that crystal ball and inquires about anything whatsoever, all the myriad phenomena of the universe are clearly reflected therein. Yet how is it of late, that from two or three days ago a black spot has formed in a part of the crystal ball, making it exceedingly unsightly; up to the point of ninety-nine percent everything can be made clearly discernible, but because of that one-percent black spot, I regret to say, it has come about that a complete judgment cannot be reached. Therefore black is unpleasing; I say let us kill it off. As if there were ever anything good about a black fellow! Some pitch-black hag called the Blackhime of Izanagi must have crossed over to this Tsukushijima without a doubt. Otherwise, a crystal ball that may even be likened to the kokudama could never display a black spot. Therefore, the very reason we fought a contest of white and black with you men here today was partly to divine what the state of the crystal ball might be, and whether Blackhime is indeed going to obstruct this country... such was the matter we divined. No matter what, this black stone stands in my way and I simply cannot bear it, alas!'

D: 'Even if you speak of killing this black stone, it is a mineral from its very origin. Unlike animals and plants, you cannot take its life, nor can you cut it down and let it wither, can you?'

C: 'Rather than that, has not Takenohime-sama of the shrine of Takenogahara acquired a splendid bridegroom of late? I heard that he is a splendid missionary called Takenodewake, or something of the sort.'

D: 'Takenohime-sama was the only daughter of Takenohiwakenomikoto who had established her dwelling in the cavern of Takenohi, and for a long time she continued to propagate the way of the Kamisama, but as countless men came to worship and ended up drunk on sake, they gave themselves over to her beauty and importuned her so that it became unbearably annoying; thus Takenohime-sama, who had upheld a principle of celibacy, at length made up her mind and moved her abode to Takenogahara.'

B: 'You too must be one of those who were given the cold shoulder by Takenohime... nay, not merely one, but rather an offender of indecency acts, ha ha ha ha ha.'

C: 'Speak no nonsense. From throughout the body of Takenohime-sama a light indescribable, like unto a crystal ball, is constantly radiated, so if we common mortals were even to approach her side, why, that indeed would cause our eyes to be blinded. They say there was a foolish fellow whose skin was black and whose nose was bent, who tucked a crystal ball into his bosom and went forth, and having had an audience with Takenohime-sama... "This is a precious treasure handed down in my house, and I present this unto you Milady,"... thus he produced it with a knowing look; whereupon Takenohime-sama, wearing a displeased expression, softly received it in her hands, turned it round and round to look at it, and said... "Ah ha, upon this crystal ball there has appeared the black spot of the clinging attachment called passionate love, and therefore, though I am much obliged, I return it unto you"... and she thrust it unceremoniously right back at him. And so that fellow cannot bear the worry of the black spot upon the ball, and harbors the ambition that if somehow this black spot should be removed, he might present it to Takenohime-sama, win her favor, and stealthily become her bridegroom. As long as that ambition is not removed, no matter how much the crystal ball was bestowed by Hinode no Kami, that black spot can never be removed.'

So saying, she looks up into Kō's face with a slightly sneering air. Struck as if by lightning, Kō lets his chest heave while blushing and falls silent.

Tei: 'So Tamakō is talking about Kurohime and whatnot. When I thought you looked as if the jewel in your palm had been snatched away the moment Shiroishi lost and Kuroishi won, could it be that such a profound scheme lay behind it? Ahahahaha... Although I hide it, it shows in my face, my love—until someone asks, "Are you grieving for someone?"—it is just like that Hyakunin Isshu poem.'

Otsu: 'Hey Tamakō, since such a cloudy crystal ball is an omen of ill fortune for your place, why don't you just throw it into the river in a proper fashion? They say that before a disaster strikes, a black shadow invariably falls across the surface of a treasure. Even on a human face, before a misfortune arrives, blackened spots appear from nowhere, you see.'

Kō says powerlessly,

Tamakō: 'Even if you tell me to throw it away, it is the injunction of my grandfather; no matter what happens, this jewel cannot be allowed to leave my family. If a noble Kamisama had appeared, it would be fine to offer it to Him, but my dead grandfather's last will was that it must never be sold, given away, or thrown away, so it is not within my freedom to decide. If Takenohime-sama will not accept it, there is nothing more to be done; I have no other method than to offer up prayers and chasten my body until the black spot on this jewel is removed. However, to my mind, Kurohime must somehow have come here to this Tsukushishima; truly a troublesome wretch has arrived. If someone like Hinode Kamisama were to come forth, this jewel would shine even more brilliantly, but the black spots have gradually spread and lately it has grown cloudy to a semitransparent state. Judging from the black spot on that jewel, I can see the sign that a wretch called Kurohime is drawing near the vicinity of this mountain pass about now. Therefore, to tell you the truth, I lured you up to this point under the pretext of gathering figs. On that occasion, I tried playing the match between the white and black stones. No matter how I look at it, black is bringing misfortune here. If we do not smash Kurohime—nay, Kuroishi—to pieces, this country will be utterly hopeless. I could not bear it if the Tsukushi island of the crystal ball were to turn completely into muddy water. The Kamisama of the Kunitama is Toyome-no-Mikoto, who makes this world pure and clear like crystal. If we do not somehow destroy anything named black, it will be a momentous affair for the nation.'

Tei: 'Does it not stretch the logic a bit to say that the boundless Kunitama Kamisama of Africa is dwelling within such a tiny crystal ball?'

Tamakō: 'Do not talk nonsense. To perform activities at will, expanding and contracting freely, is the so-called august virtue of Kamisama. Supreme and without exterior, infinitesimal and without interior, without far or near, without broad or narrow, without light or darkness, gathering the past, present, and future into a single lump of crystal ball, and handling it as freely as we roll a jewel in our palms—that is what Kamisama is. Since the august spirit of that Kamisama has become clouded like that, we mortals of Tsukushishima cannot afford to remain unconcerned. You immediately give vent to mortal passions in strange directions, as if I were in love with Takenohime, but this is not such a lighthearted matter. There is no telling when a terrestrial or celestial upheaval might suddenly break out. Even the violent storm a moment ago—is that not a raging the likes of which has not been heard of for twenty or thirty years? Huge boulders rain down crash upon crash like leaves, great trees are uprooted, tree branches are torn apart, rain falls like figs. Think carefully. This is by no means an ordinary occurrence.'

As he thus speaks, there comes an old woman climbing up the slope, tapping the path with her iron-shod staff, lightly attired in straw raincoat, straw sandals, and leggings. This is none other than Kurohime of the Omotokyo.

斯く話す所へ、蓑笠草鞋脚絆に金剛杖の軽き扮装にて、コツンコツンと、坂路を叩き乍ら登つて来る一人の中婆アがあつた。此れは言はずと知れた三五教の黒姫である。

Kurohime finally climbed up to the summit, and as though feeling a momentary sense of relief, she firmly grasped her iron-shod staff in her left hand, bracing her body solidly, clenched her right fist, and struck her lower back three or four times,

"Ah," she said, and at the very moment she gave a great stretch, her eyes caught sight of five men sitting in a circle and whispering about something, and Kurohime said,

"I say to you young gentlemen sitting there, I would like to ask you something for a moment. Have you not heard that an esteemed missionary named Takayamahiko has arrived in the Land of Fire?"

B: 'I know not what old hag you might be, but to make a solitary journey through this mountain path with such extreme boldness—what can you possibly mean? Inquiring after Lord Takayamahiko's person, what is your intention?'

C: 'Oi Torakō, you must not speak to her in such an overbearing manner. She might well be Lord Takayamahiko's mother, you see.'

Torakō: 'I say, madam, have you come all this way to find out the whereabouts of your son?'

Kurohime: 'No, no, I am Takayamahiko's wife. Longing for my husband, I have come all this way. Is Lord Takayamahiko getting along safely?'

Torakō: 'Safely indeed! As for the husband, the husband is in a tremendously powerful position. However, it is a matter that just doesn't add up at all, that you, madam, should be Lord Takayamahiko's wife. When there is such a splendid wife called Princess Aiko, for him to have a bent-backed old crone like you as his wife is rather ridiculous, isn't it? That must surely be a case of mistaken identity. Moreover, I have never heard since ancient times of such an aged woman coming along yearning for a man—isn't it some mistake about your son?'

Kurohime: 'I indeed once had a son, but when he was young, thinking it would bring shame upon our worldly reputation, I abandoned him at a four-way crossroads and had someone pick him up. As divine punishment for that, I am separated from my husband, the whereabouts of my own child are unknown, the years keep piling up upon me, I have no child of my own, and the only things I rely upon are God and my husband Takayamahiko. That there is a young wife belonging to that very Lord Takayamahiko—surely that cannot be a lie, can it?'

Torakō: 'I certainly tell no lies. If I were to utter even a single word of lie, the punishment of the Land's Soul Deity would strike me, and my mouth would instantly become twisted. The reason so many people in this land of Kumaso have twisted mouths is simply because they are all fellows who told lies and received divine punishment, isn't that so, Ninkō?'

Ninkō: 'Oh, absolutely, absolutely so. It is so terrifying that one cannot even utter the letter L of a lie.'

Kurohime: 'Ah, is that so. Without knowing the heart of Kurohime, who has come all this way crossing long seas and mountains, for even Takayamahiko to take a young woman as his wife is altogether too heartless. He might at least show a little consideration for my feelings as well. Ah, what shall I do now? Unable to advance and unable to retreat, things have come to a truly troubling pass.'

So saying, she let her tears fall in a stream, and stood immersed in lamentation.

Tamakō: 'Now, now, old lady, you just said a moment ago that you are Kurohime, didn't you?'

Kurohime: 'Yes, I am the true wife of the missionary Takayamahiko who dwells in the capital of the Land of Fire.'

Tamakō: 'Well now, a troublesome situation has arisen. I had come all this way and was waiting, guided by the warning of the crystal ball, thinking that unless I destroyed this Kurohime, this country would turn into a muddy sea; but if you are the wife of the highly virtuous Lord Takayamahiko, I can do nothing. Lord Takayamahiko is regarded by the entire populace as the living god and parent of the island of Tsukushi, a splendid personage, and I cannot mistreat his wife. That being the case, Kurohime, are you truly without a doubt the legitimate wife of Lord Takayamahiko?'

Kurohime: 'There is certainly no mistake; this so-called Princess Aiko is, in other words, Lord Takayamahiko's concubine. As a missionary of the Izumo Truth Religion where the rule of one husband and one wife is strict, there can be no reason for him to have two principal wives.'

黒姫『決して間違はありませぬ、愛子姫と云ふのは、つまり高山彦さまのお妾でせう。一夫一婦の掟の厳しい三五教の宣伝使が、二人も本妻を持つ道理は有りますまい』

§ 4

Tamakō: 'Well, it is something that defies all understanding. That a gentleman as splendid as Takayamahiko-sama should take a concubine like me—what a monstrous contradiction it is. Even considering that love operates outside the realm of reason, this is truly an excessive derailment. ...Excuse me, Kurohime-sama, were you not once divorced? It may be rude to say so, but for such a splendid sendenshi to become husband and wife with a dark person like you is just like the moon and a soft-shelled turtle, or a egret and a crow getting married. Therefore, did you not receive a mikudarihan at that place called Onokorojima? Otherwise, illuminated by the shinkaku of Takayamahiko-sama, there are simply too many points that refuse to make sense.'

Torakō: 'Say, Kurohime-sama, you said a moment ago that you abandoned a single child. If that child were alive today, about how old would he be?'

Kurohime: 'Yes, it was thirty-five years ago. If he were here now, he would be a splendid, high-spirited young man of thirty-five years of age. When I was young, having made a boy with a man my parents would not permit, and thinking it would look shameful to the world, I heartlessly abandoned him at a crossroads. But when I think of it now, I have truly done a regrettable thing.'

Saying this, she lets tears fall like falling leaves and sinks into sorrow.

Torakō: 'The one who appeared as the adopted son of Takenohime-sama at the sacred hall of Takenogahara is thirty-five years old this year, a splendid sendenshi named Kenokubetsu. According to the story, that person too was known as an abandoned baby when he was an infant, and because the whereabouts of his true parents remain unknown to this day, he believes in the god of Sanjikkyō, praying with a single-minded devotion, saying "Please let me meet my true parents even a single day sooner," and at last he has become an honorable sendenshi, so the story goes. It was exactly one year ago from now. Through the go-between services of Takayamahiko-sama of the mansion in Hinokuni, he became the adopted bridegroom of Takenohime-sama. Husband and wife are harmonious, their divine virtues grow higher day by day and night by night, and their momentum is simply tremendous, you know. Could it not possibly be the honorable child you abandoned?'

Kurohime: 'What! The sendenshi Kenokubetsu was an abandoned child? And his age is thirty-five—well, that is a matter that defies understanding.'
