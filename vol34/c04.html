Chapter 4: Song 垣 [954]

歌垣〔954〕

§ 1

Sannin sat in Tanza within the darkness, unable to sleep, and waited impatiently for the dawn to break.

Suddenly, a groaning voice began to sound from a nearby Gankutsu, 'Oo-oo.'

The night was already at the hour of Ushimitsu.

Shinrabanshiyō was silent without a sound, a stillness where even the crying voice of Mimizu could be heard.

Because a groaning voice was heard coming from inside the Gankutsu like oo-oo, it responded all the more harshly to the ears of Sannin.

Not knowing whose voice it was from inside the Gankutsu,

'Ah, what a disgusting hag! To abandon Magokō, who was the help that brought you all the way here, midway and take independent action—truly, that is a cruel, demon-like spirit. Shall the Enma of this world appear, grab you by the Erikubi, and hurl you down into the Senjin Taniahi?

Oh, an ogre, an Orochi, or a Magakami? A matchless Ninpinin, having lost your way in the dark paths of love, and growing old all the way to Afurikasangai, to come sneaking like a dog sniffing behind a Kojiki—what a utterly contemptible old hag you are!

Ha, ha, ha! Making a black face like a crow, no matter how much you cast amorous glances, Takayamahiko will not even look your way. You must think he is an irreplaceable, precious man, but Takayamahiko is broad-minded like the sky and sea, giving not even a single glance to a hag like you; everywhere there is a green mountain, and wherever he goes is his home, displaying an extreme philanthropic principle that all the women of the world are his wives—it is no use even if you think to monopolize the love of a fickle man. You had better quickly repent, ha, ha, ha, and return home.

Ki, ki, ki! Rather than spending the night in this eerie Taniahi and suffering terribly, having the grease squeezed out of you by Yoteruhiko-no-Mikoto, hurry back to Jitentōjima even a moment sooner. The gods do not tell lies.'

Kurohime tilts her head while... wondering vaguely... how it sounds remarkably like Magokō's voice from just before... driven by a half-believing, half-doubting thought.

Kurohime: 'Hey, hey, I do not know whose mischievous prank this is, but on such a dark night, you had better stop such worthless Kototama. How unamusing, even evil gods are teasing this Kurohime by saying Takayamahiko, Takayamahiko, Takayamahiko. What a terribly ill-mannered, nuisance of a god you are!'

Fusakō: 'My, Kurohime-sama, it is precisely because you are like that that they say you are no good. Is there such a thing as talking back to the gods?'

Kurohime: 'Keep quiet, this is no place for a child to pipe up. As for Kurohime, undeserving as she is, the Otohime-sama of Ryūgū is graciously granting her protection, so the only one to be feared between heaven and earth is Kuninotokotachi-no-Mikoto and no one else. All the other gods are merely branch gods. As for the Otohime-sama of Ryūgū, who works as the right arm of that Kuninotokotachi-no-Mikoto... Hmph, she is a living shrine! No matter how dark it is, surely you would not mistake her so much... right, Otohime-sama of Ryūgū?'

Fusakō: 'Hmph, I have not heard of the Otohime-sama of Ryūgū for a long time, but where on earth did you go?'

Kurohime: 'The physical body of Otohime-sama of Ryūgū is none other than Kurohime. The spirit of Kurohime is none other than Otohime-sama of Ryūgū. With such a lack of understanding, can you call yourself a believer of Hinomoto-no-Oshie?'

Hōkō: 'I, for my part, thought that Otohime-sama of Ryūgū was a much more magnificent personage, and that a hundred-millionth of her divine power possessed the spirit of Kurohime-sama; but to hear that Kurohime-sama's spirit is entirely Otohime of Ryūgū—already I have lost the feeling of respecting Otohime-sama. How foolish, if it were to come to this, I should not have abandoned my dear wife and child to follow all the way here. Magokō has met such a fate and kicked the bucket, Kurohime-sama's prestige has peeled off completely, a bizarre voice comes from inside the Gankutsu, and the night grows progressively later. Ah, could there ever be anything so disappointing... right, Fusakō, when day breaks, you and I will go to where Magokō is sitting, help him up, and shall the three of us return to the original sacred place? Truly, we have met with a most foolish experience.'

Kurohime: 'Hey, you two, you still do not understand this Kurohime, do you? You do not know the divine power of Otohime-sama of Riugū, whose transformations are countless and whose appearances and disappearances are limitless. Why, when the time comes, Otohime-sama spreads across the realm of Tenchi, stirs up Fūuraiden, shakes Jishin, and stands forth as the right arm of the divine work of Ōkuniharutachinomikoto. Today is the period of retirement for Otohime-dono. When the time is not yet ripe, she conceals her form like an imori or a mimizu, tastes the hardships of Arayuru Tenka, and serves the divine work of Kiuseisaimin.'

Yoshikō: 'Hmph, searching far and wide to hunt down the husband of Takayamahiko—do you call that the divine work of Kiuseisaimin? What a strange sort of Kiuseisaimin that is.'

Kurohime: 'Hah, silence! How could a mere hoyahoya believer possibly fathom the heart of a missionary? Takayamahiko-sama is a spirit of karmic destiny; unless this spirit and I are joined as husband and wife, the weaving of the warp and weft will not be accomplished. That is why Kurohime has come all this way seeking Takayamahiko-sama. In a world where men are not scarce, why would anyone search all the way to such a place for an old man like Takayamahiko-sama out of love or desire? Dark-skinned though Kurohime may be, a woman is still a woman. Since there are gods who abandon and gods who take in, if I wanted a man, there would be plenty of them even without Takayamahiko-sama. It is troublesome because ordinary people always let their minds wander to such matters. Cast away your mundane thoughts for a moment. The Ōkamisama is smiling at you. Kimi nara de dare o ka shiranu waga kokoro Takayamahiko no otto zo shitahashi... Otshohohoho.'

Fusakō: 'Hmph, you can make an argument for anything, can't you? Well, I am completely chilled by your eloquence. Ehehehe.'

From within the cave,

'Search though we may the vast islands of Tsukushi, Not even a shadow of Takayamahiko is to be found. Wandering aimlessly about this neighborhood, In the very end, one grasps only dung. Kurohime, longing and coming all this way, Collapses with a gasp and blows bubbles. Kurohime, who has spanned distant lands with her strides, Sheds tears out of sheer helplessness. Coming here, before a pitch-dark rock, A mysterious voice can be heard. In the darkness, someone unknown speaks, A Kurohime bewildered like a fox or raccoon dog. Exhibiting an outrageous and wicked heart, She leaves Magokō-sama to his doom. Tonight of all nights, I will strip away your fat, Two white and black eyes upon Kurohime's face. Now, how shall we clear away the dark love Of utterly bewildered Kurohime? A band of four comes pushing through the white waves, Guided by the glib tongue of Kurohime Tsukasa. Steadily climbing this mountain valley, Kurohime's complexion appears pale. Swift is the current of the mountain stream blocked by rocks, Thinking that once separated, they shall never meet at the end. Gazing at the clouds moving across the sky, she wonders, How fares the peak of Takayamahiko? Rebuilding and renewing is what she cried out all over, With Kurohime's rear end bustling about. Even in the age of the mighty gods never heard of, Kurohime's black heart showed upon her face. How terrifying is this rock cave Where the spirit of Getsushō no Mikoto is settled! Though Kurohime is unequal to the task, She goes forth, trampled under the feet of the cave's god. Until a thorough reformation can be achieved, Takayamahiko will not show his form. Nothing ever goes well through one's own ego, Witness the reformation of Takahime Tsukasa. Running about to the west, east, north, and south, How foolish was the heart that searched for the jewel! Would that I could illuminate the sky of the heart Of Kurohime, darker than the nut-black ink. Getsushōhiko earnestly exhorts and teaches, Revering the Way, keep it, you three. They say one forgets the heat once the throat has passed, How fragile is the heart of Kurohime Tsukasa. Spring passes, summer passes, the dusk of autumn, Dewdrops spill upon the sleeves of robes. Setting out from the distant Takamagahara, What do you wander seeking on the island of Tsukushi? Looking back at the mansion in Kitayama Village in Fusa Province, Kurohime's sky is desolate. Opening wide the storehouse of Kurohime's heart, And looking inside, a great serpent is writhing. Without knowing anything of the outrageous conduct Of the hen-turned-rooster, Kurohime speaks out.'

Spouting herikutsu morning and evening,

Hated by people, she leaves the country.

Dropping her mejiri, utterly fascinated by Takayamahiko,

Longing for the old days when she drooled.

If you think you will look like a masukagami, blow away the dust of Kurohime's heart.

Her body is here, but her heart is before Takayamahiko,

Forgetting Kami morning and evening.

What on earth is that awkward tsuratsuki?

So that she will not be hated by Takayamahiko.

Open your eyes and reflect on your own figure,

Even Takayama would lose aisau.

Vomit out all the sins of your heart,

And make a norinaoshi before the mimahe of Kami."

Kurohime, looking slightly keshiki, turned toward the direction of the voice,

"Uttering a voice from the obscure darkness,

How noisy, what are you spitting out?

Lining up various aras of people,

The fellow whose heart is satisfied by that is a magakami.

You must not listen carelessly, Fusakō,

Yoshikō, that is the place to settle your stomach.

Things as elusive as a yuurei,

The magakami spits out while hiding in the ihaya.

Imitating Tsukiteruhiko with a grand air,

The fellow chattering must be Magokō.

Since there is nothing more frightening in the world,

Know Kurohime, upon whom intimidation does not work.

Though the oni laughs if you speak of rainen,

You had better watch my future.

Even though the tongue that spits out only rikutsu is tatsushiya,

Magokō fails to match it with his bodily conduct.

Not knowing that his body is in the ruiran state of a pile of eggs,

A self-ignorant fellow teasing Kurohime-sama.

Only about Takayamahiko whom she renboed,

Magokō speaks with a bad and stubborn iji.

Stop that good-for-nothing takusen,

My ears are ringing and my stomach is getting angry."

From inside the ihaya, with an even louder voice,

"The one who does not understand is none other than Kurohime,

It is no wonder she strays in the dark.

Turning completely into a captive of love forever,

Has she come all the way here, what a pitiful old hag?

In reality and in dreams, Takayama, Takayama,

Hearing the words she spits out is utateki.

Chasing after the back of the man she cut ties with,

And with a gasp, to waste away in the fields of Africa.

Where oni and giant wolves prowl in Africa,

The captive of love wanders the wilderness.

Utufu huhu, itsuhi hihi,

From now on Tsukiteruhiko will also change his lodging,

And proceed toward the Capital of Fire.

Kurohime, put your hand on your chest and ponder,

Takayamahiko is not single.

Fusakō, quickly reform your heart,

And give up on Kurohime-sama.

Yoshikō, even if Heaven and Earth sink,

You must follow Kurohime-tsukasa.

Magokō is probably by the side of Takayamahiko

In the Land of Fire by this time.

Takayamahiko, the command of Kami, will surely not like a wife

Like Kurohime.

Embracing her softly swelling chest,

Takayamahiko lies down winding his jeweled arm around her.

If Kurohime were to gaze upon such a place,

She would surely turn her eyeballs black and white.

Narrowing her eyes, lifting her chin, and pointing her snout,

Her breath hot as she blows her fever.

Well then, even Tsukiteruhiko from now on

Leaves Kurohime-tsukasa behind and goes away.

The ihaya where Kojima-wake-kyōshi in days of old

Once appeared is nostalgic.

To leave behind the ancient footprints of Takehi-no-wake

In the end makes one sad.

Magokō is no doubt at this time delightfully

Singing funny songs.

Ha ha ha ha, oho ho ho, laughing heartily,

He he he, ihi hi, and leaves this place now.

Hearing the voice of the crow that does not cry in the dark night,

The Takayamahiko before lamenting is what I long for.

No matter how much Kurohime scorches her heart,

Being a flower on a high peak, she cannot even be plucked.

By this time Takayamahiko is at the sacred site,

Drinking sake with the daughter of the Iseya.

Not to mention the Nyoi-hōju and Shigyoku,

The ball of gold loses its aisau.

Giving Kurohime a stiff elbow blow,

He hid himself in Jitentō-jima."

So saying, stealing his footsteps, he went away to somewhere unknown.

ろくでない其託宣はやめてくれ 耳がいとなる腹が立つぞよ』

岩窟の中より、一層大きな声で、

『わからない奴は黒姫計りなり 暗に迷ふも無理であるまい。 ゐつまでも恋の虜となり果てて 此処迄来たか可哀相な婆。 うつつにも夢にも高山々々と 吐く言葉を聞くぞうたてき。 ゑん切つた男の尻を追ひまはし アフンとやせむアフリカの野で。 をに大蛇狼さやぐアフリカの 荒野さまよふ恋の虜が。

ウツフヽヽ、イツヒヽヽ

是れからは月照彦も宿を替へ 火の都へと進みゆかなむ。 黒姫よ胸に手をあて思案せよ 高山彦は独身でないぞよ。 房公よ早く心を改めて 黒姫さまを思ひ切るべし。 芳公よよしや天地は沈む共 黒姫司に従いちやならぬぞ。 孫公はモウ今頃は火の国の 高山彦の側にゐるだろ。 高山彦神の命は黒姫の 様な女房は好かれまいぞや。 淡雪の若やる胸をそだたきて 玉手さしまきいねます高山彦。 黒姫がこんな所を眺めたら さぞや目玉を白黒にせむ。 まなじりをつけ上げ口を尖らして 鼻息あらく熱を吹くらむ。 いざさらば月照彦もこれよりは 黒姫司をみすててぞゆく。

小島別教司の其昔 あらはれましし岩窟恋しき。 古の建日の別の御跡を 後にみすてて行くぞ悲しき。 孫公はさぞ今頃は面白く 可笑しき歌をうたひ居るらむ。 アハヽヽヽ、オホヽヽヽ、と笑ひこけ エヘヽヽ、イヒヽ、今ここを去る。 暗の夜に鳴かぬ烏の声きけば あはれぬ先の高山彦ぞ恋しき。 黒姫がいかに心を焦す共 高根の花よ手折られもせず。 今頃は高山彦は聖地にて 伊勢屋の娘と酒を呑むらむ。 如意宝珠紫玉は云ふも更 黄金の玉は愛想つかして。 黒姫に肱鉄砲をくはしつつ 自転倒島にかくれましけり』

と云つた限り、足音を忍ばせ、何処ともなく行きて了ひけり。
