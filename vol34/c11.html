Chapter 11: 富士咲 [952]

富士咲〔952〕

§ 1

Itsupau backed a towering Kauzan, in front of it flowed a picturesque ravine where a Seiriu rushed, and in a scenic spot overlooking Daigenya, a Betsuden of Takehiyakata was built, where three guests sat in a Teiza and whispered deeply together.

Takekuniwake: 'Gorautai no mi o motte, you have honored me by visiting. I hear that you too are searching for the yukuhe of your goshisoku and traveling around, but dōzo, if it is no sashitsukahe, would you mind making that jijiyō clear to me in a kantan manner?'

Kurohime: 'Hai, watashi is one called Kurohime of Ananai-kyō. Tadaima I am a propagandist serving at the Palace of Brocade in Onokorojima, but for certain reasons I have come all the way to this island of Chikushi with three companions in order to search for the whereabouts of my husband. Having come thus, when I heard five young men talking variously at the summit of the Kauzan Pass, I learned that the propagandist Takekuniwake of the Hall of Takehi is thirty-five years old this year, and that he does not know the yukuhe of his parents and is searching for them very desperately; hearing this, my heart stirred without knowing why, and since the child I abandoned is also thirty-five years old this year, I thought he might possibly be that son, and disregarding even your busy circumstances, I committed an intrusion.'

Takekuniwake: 'What kind of name is it that you call your husband?'

Kurohime: 'Hai, he is called Takayamahiko. Having heard recently in the capital of Hinokuni that he is carrying out the propaganda of Ananai-kyō, I am on my way to search for him there. Such being the case, prompted by the talk of the five men, I recalled my child, and thinking that you might perhaps be the child I abandoned in my youth, I have made bold to inquire without regard for discourtesy.'

Takekuniwake: 'Eh, what are you saying? That Takayamahiko-sama is your husband is something I hear that does not make sense. Takayamahiko-sama is in truth my master, but he took as his bride Aikohime, the eldest daughter of the Great God Susanō, and now they serve devotedly in the divine work in marital harmony, their divine virtue shining brightly in all four directions, with a momentum that would shoot down a flying bird. How could Takayamahiko-sama have taken another wife when he had a legal wife like you? Takayamahiko-sama is not a person who would commit such acts that violate heavenly law, so is this not some kind of mistake?'

Kurohime: 'At the sacred site of Onokorojima, we had a husband-and-wife quarrel over a trifling matter, and taking that opportunity, my husband Takayamahiko threw me aside, saying he was going off to the island of Chikushi, and from then until now there has been no news whatsoever. When I rushed to this island and listened to the rumors of the people, as you said, he is living with a young wife; even if an old hag like me were to go, they would surely not pay attention to me. However, since I have come this far, I intend even if only for a glance to meet him, to say what I wish to say, and depending on his attitude, since I too am a propagandist of the divine way, to have him grant a divorce so that nothing remains behind. At the same time, hearing your rumors on the way, thinking he might possibly be my own child, rather than a fickle husband, I would wish to meet the son who caused my own womb pain and have him care for my old age, and so disregarding discourtesy I have asked you. However, if I am mistaken, please forgive me.'

Takekuniwake: 'Does that goshisoku have any identifying mark?'

Kurohime: 'Yes, because he was an infant I cannot tell for certain, but there is definitely a white birthmark in the exact center of his back, and since it resembles the shape of Mount Fuji, I thought this might possibly be a reincarnation of Konohanahime of Mount Fuji, and so I gave him the name Fujisaki... and since I had certain circumstances and would leave this child abandoned here, I prayed that some person of deep compassion would take him into their hands and raise him... saying this, I added a little money, wrote his name, and abandoned him. Ever since then I know not what became of my son; in my youth I forgot about my son, distracted by various things, but growing old like this makes those parts lonely, and day and night I have never forgotten the child I abandoned. However, if I may be so bold, do you have any such mark?'

§ 2

Takekuniwake: 'I was also abandoned by my parents when I was an infant, so as I cannot see my own back, I know nothing about it.'

Takenohime: 'I always wash your back, and it is a truly beautiful body, without a single mole or even a single moxa scar. Moreover, there are no birthmarks anywhere.'

Kurohime: 'Ah, is that so. Then you are surely not the Fujisaku I am searching for.'

Takekuniwake: 'Was there nothing, perhaps, added as a token at that time, such as an article of property?'

Kurohime: 'I added nothing else at all. I merely put the sacred divine name of Konohanasakuyahime into a protective amulet bag, along with the name of the child called Fujisaku.'

Takekuniwake: 'If that is so, I am probably not your son. When I was abandoned, a single protective sword was included, and upon that protective sword, the character for ten was clearly marked with pearls. I still possess that protective sword to this day. Furthermore, the characters for East and High faintly appear at the base of the sword's hilt. Seeking my parents with this as proof, from around my fifteenth or sixteenth year I ran all about those parts, crossed from the land of Fusa to Onokorojima, and finally came here to Tsukushijima, where I became a disciple of Lord Takayamahiko, was trained as a missionary, and on this very day last year became the adopted son of this household.'

Kurohime folds her arms and remains lost in thought for a while.

Takekuniwake: 'Have you perhaps any inkling of this, madam?'

Kurohime: 'Yes, a protective sword marked with the character for ten using pearls, and upon it the marks for East and High—well now, there is something that does not quite add up.'

Pressing her closely, Takekuniwake speaks:

Takekuniwake: 'Since you have traveled about preaching to the world, do you not have some inkling of this? Even if there is a mistake it does not matter, so if there is even the slightest clue, please speak of it. Today I could not help feeling that someone connected to my parents was bound to appear. When I heard you were coming, I rejoiced, thinking you might perhaps be my longed-for mother, so this is truly a regrettable matter. Yet this too must be some bond of fate. Since this is the carefree household of a young couple, please stay here without any reserve for as long as you like. For I cannot help feeling that you are someone with whom I share a bond...'

Kurohime: 'Yes, thank you very much. Still, since I must meet Lord Takayamahiko at least once no matter what, if there is another opportunity I shall avail myself of your kindness. However, I also feel as though I have some slight inkling, but since I cannot recall it right away, let me ponder it leisurely and give you my answer.'

Kurohime had on a certain occasion heard Takahime's reminiscences of old times.

At the edge of those words,

...she seemed to recall hearing something to the effect that... she too, in her youth, had taken a man her parents did not allow, conceived a child, and made it an abandoned child... Takahime had reportedly worn the mark of the character for ten until now, but upon entering Omoto, she changed it to the divine crest of the ten luminaries. Might he not be the child of Takahime? No, no, I must not let slip a careless word and cause trouble; where on earth is Takahime now, her whereabouts are unknown. If I were to say a careless thing, search for a person whose whereabouts are unknown, and Takekuniwake-sama were to go to all the trouble, only for us to be mistaken as we are now, it would not do; it would probably be safer for the sake of both of us to say I know nothing...

Nodding to herself with these thoughts, Kurohime changes her tone,

Kurohime: 'I truly have no recollection. However, having heard your story, I shall keep it well in mind and ponder it. If I should meet someone who appears undoubtedly to be your parents, I shall inform you immediately.'

§ 3

Takekuniwake: 'Yes, thank you very much. I kindly ask for your favor.'

Takenohime: 'As you know in any case, he is an unfortunate husband, so Kurohime-sama, if any clue should occur to you, please let us know. I beg of you.'

Kurohime is deeply moved, has not even a word to reply, and hangs her head down.

Thus she thinks within her heart,

"Ah, what is called the affection of parent and child is the same thing wherever one goes in the world. Just as Takekuniwake-sama yearns for his parents and gives his mind to them morning and evening, if my child is also living in this world, he must surely be longing for his parents. A child who has no parents, if he collapses there, it means there is no one to raise him up. Ah, when I think of it, though it is said to be the excess of youthful folly, I have done a sinful thing. To become a sendenshi of Ananai-kyō with such a deeply sinful body and try to perform the business of the kogane no tama—truly, that was a misjudgment. It is no wonder that the jewel was hidden from me by Kamisama."

Thus she did not speak it with her mouth, but allowed the waves of zange to drift within her heart.

To such a place, Takehiko leads numerous executives and appears noisily into this scene, politely placing both hands on the ground,

Takehiko: 'Great Master and Wife-sama, congratulations on this day. I do not know how we should express our joy; we, beginning with the people of the mansion, are truly in a state of dancing with joy and leaping like sparrows. It is said that the mother whom you always yearn for has come today, and there is nothing more joyous than this. On behalf of all, I offer my congratulations.'

So saying, this time he turns his head toward Kurohime, bows deeply twice,

Takehiko: 'Were you the birth mother of the Great Master? Oh, welcome indeed. We are all people who day and night rely upon the patronage of the Great Master. Please do not forsake us and cherish us for a long time to come.'

Takekuniwake: 'Hey Takehiko, my mother has not appeared. A sendenshi named Kurohime has appeared.'

Takehiko: 'Eh, what are you saying? You must not conceal it. All of us know for certain that she is your mother. Since it is now the joy of all the people in the mansion without a doubt, please do not say such mean things and state it clearly.'

Takekuniwake: 'Who did you hear such a thing from?'

Takehiko: 'Yes, the gatekeeper Ikou said without a doubt that he heard your story, and he did the job of a postman without charging a tip, so soon congratulations will come from this village too.'

Takekuniwake: 'Hey Takehiko, that is terrible. Please retract it, saying it was a complete mistake. Because it will be troublesome if many villagers come to congratulate us, you see.'

Takehiko: 'Since we have already made a public announcement and people are coming to congratulate us one after another, we cannot retract it on this occasion. If it is said that the mansion of Kamisama spread around a mistaken thing, that truly affects our credibility. Without saying such things, please leave her as your mother even if it is a mistake. In any case, she is probably someone's mother...'

Takekuniwake: 'You are a troublesome fellow. Call Ikou here for a moment.'

Ikou lifts up the head that had been cowering among the large crowd with a thrust,

Ikou: 'Yes, Ikou is here. Congratulations for a long time. If I have made a mistake, please forgive me in every way. Mother, who came here searching for the footsteps of her child whose whereabouts are unknown, please cherish her dearly. No matter how much you try to conceal it, judging from your appearance, there is no doubt it is a relationship between parent and child.'

Takekuniwake: 'Well, a troublesome thing has happened... Kurohime-sama, what shall we do?'

Kurohime: 'It is a truly regrettable matter that a person like me has suddenly come and caused trouble for the mansion... Hello, you who are called Ikou-sama, I am one called Kurohime. When I investigate very thoroughly, it turns out not to be my son, so actually we were looking at each other's faces and feeling disappointed. Please revoke it immediately so that it does not become a trouble to the mansion.'

Ikukō: 'My, how the parent-child hearts have united and firmly formed a bond! No matter what is said, it is mind-to-mind transmission, transmission outside the teachings, and not establishing words.
