Chapter 1: 筑紫上陸 [942]

筑紫上陸〔942〕

§ 1

Searching the four quarters for the whereabouts of the golden ball, Kurohime of Ananai-kyō finally cast aside her attachment to the ball and returned from the center of the land of the gods, the inverted land, back to the sacred land of Aya. While secretly lurking in her own home for a while, she clashed with her husband Takayamahiko, with whom she had pledged a second life over the error of Mani's sacred jewel, and a scandal of separation arose. Takayamahiko left the sacred land to go to the island of Tsukushi, throwing away his stubbornly clinging wife Kurohime, and vanished like a puff of smoke. Abandoned by her beloved husband, Kurohime could no longer bear it, becoming desperate.

Making the search for the ball her second priority, and wishing to track down the whereabouts of her husband, the wrinkled old hag, whipped by monkey-like mind and horse-like will, crossed over the waves of ten thousand ri. Exerting her heart and body to the utmost, as far as the bottom of the sea of Tsukushi, the boundary of Shiranui, she resolved to search even to the ocean floor. Taking three companions—Mago, Fusō, and Yoshis—with her, she swelled her true sails and rowed out into the sea of Yura. Leaving the Sea of Japan far behind, crossing the Pacific Ocean, passing beyond the offing of Ryūgū Island, and viewing the Indian Ocean to her right, she finally crossed over and arrived at the eastern shore of Tsukushi Island.

This place is called the Port of Tatehi, and it was a historically profound port where, in ancient days, the missionary of the Sun God, Omenanagi no Tsukasa, and Iwahime no Tsukasa landed.

When Kurohime, together with her three attendants, claimed to be searching for the whereabouts of Mani's jewel and the golden jewel, that was merely a superficial reason; in truth, she had already almost discarded her attachment to the jewels. Only her mortification at having her marriage severed by Takayamahiko before the eyes of the gathered public, her desire to wash away that shame, and her lingering attachment to Takayamahiko had merged into one. Driven by her monkey-like mind and horse-like will, she had lifted up her aged body, left the splendid sacred land behind, and been brought once again to such a barbarian country. Even if Takayamahiko were to revert to a giant serpent, turn into a demon, or hide inside a stone chest, Kurohime felt a woman's stubborn pride that she must meet him at least once, clear away the dust of resentment piled high in her heart, and, if fortune favored, warm their old relations once more, become husband and wife again, and return hand in hand to the sacred land. She felt that if she did not show Takahime and all the others that her stubborn pride was just like this, all the efforts she had made hitherto would come to nothing, and her prestige would be completely shattered—thus straying off into such a strange realm, she was seized by the demon called love, and almost like a semi-madwoman, her eyes turned upward, her cheeks hollowed, her complexion turned pale, and she had assumed a truly dreadful countenance.

Although Mago, Fusō, and Yoshis had been manipulated by Kurohime's various sweet words and had followed her this far, they had no purpose of missionizing and no enjoyment whatsoever; their hearts were simply filled with the desire to seek out Takayamahiko's whereabouts as soon as possible and return together with Kurohime to the Island of Onokoro. Kurohime herself, despite being a missionary, had her heart and soul stolen by the search for Takayamahiko, and merely prayed morning and evening that she might be allowed to meet her husband as soon as possible, almost completely forgetting her mission to propagate the Way. There is nothing more unmanageable than an old woman crazed with love. By the time Kurohime arrived at this Port of Tatehi, she had stopped over at that island and called at this island countless times, engaging in strict searches, and thus she had expended a great deal of days, taking almost a year.

Thus, their boat suffered shipwreck two or three times, and while buying boats by expedient methods or picking them up, they had finally managed to trace their way here. In the meantime, they had encountered miserable hardships where their backs could not be substituted for their stomachs, continued actions that violated divine laws, and on occasion, they had quietly stolen boats belonging to various people tied up at islands and ridden in them.

Because the three attendants, accompanying Kurohime, had instead witnessed as missionaries numerous contradictions in Kurohime, the trust they had in Kurohime when they left the sacred land and their attitude toward Kurohime now had changed completely. The notion had unexpectedly dawned in the hearts of the three that Kurohime was, in short, a person of mere talk, a stubborn and malicious old hag whose deeds did not match her words. Therefore, day by day they grew to despise Kurohime, and now they had reached a state where they no longer readily obeyed Kurohime's commands. Not only that, but touching upon matters and encountering things, they would tease her and watch Kurohime's grief and joy, sorrow and pleasure, and the desires of love and hatred appear on the surface of her face, making it at least a solace for their journey. Nevertheless, having boarded the boat, they could not turn back midway, and thanks to some of the teachings of the gods having soaked into the hearts of all three, from around the time they came out into the middle of the Pacific Ocean, the three whispered secretly together, and Mago even once initiated a consultation as to whether they might throw Kurohime into the middle of the sea and return to Onokoro Island with a feigned innocent face. Yet, thinking that if they did such a reckless thing, they would instantly accumulate grave sins of violating divine laws and it would be impossible to tell what severe punishments might be inflicted upon them from the divine realm, they corrected their thoughts and re-enunciated them by the flash of the Naobi spirit, and though reluctantly, they had followed this far through the hardships and sufferings of the waves of ten thousand ri.

三人の従者は黒姫に随従して却て宣伝使として数多の黒姫の矛盾を目撃してゐるので、聖地を出た時の黒姫に対する信用と、今の黒姫に対する態度とは、ガラツと変つてゐる。黒姫は要するに口計りの人間で、行ひの伴はざる執念深き悪垂れ婆アと云ふ観念が、三人の胸に期せずして兆してゐる。それ故日を逐うて黒姫を軽蔑し、今は容易に黒姫の命令に服しない様になつて居る。のみならず却て事に触れ物に接し、からかつて見ては、黒姫が喜怒哀楽、愛悪欲の面部の色に現はるるを見て、せめてもの旅の慰みとしてゐた位である。併し乍ら乗り掛けた舟、途中に引返す訳にも行かず、幾分か神様の教が三人共腹に浸み渡つてゐるお蔭で、太平洋の真ん中へ出た時分から、三人はヒソビソと囁き合ひ、一層の事黒姫を海の中へ放り込んで、素知らぬ顔で自転倒島へ帰らうかと迄、孫公が発起で相談した事もあつた。されどそんな無茶な事をすれば、忽ち天則違反の大罪を重ね、如何なる厳罰に神界から処せらるるやも計り難しと、直日の魂の閃きに見直し宣り直し、厭々乍ら、万里の波濤を艱難辛苦してここ迄従いて来たのである。

§ 2

Kurohime and her party disembarked from the boat, landed at the port of Takehi, ascended the mountain stream of Gekitanhimatsu, and while gazing at the scenery in all directions, climbed up the narrow valley path parting the grasses. As it appeared to have relatively frequent human traffic, the sheep-gut-like path shone white in zigzag folds.

Magokō: 'Thanks to Kurohime-sama, I have been shown an unexpected magnificent view. Roasted by the sun upon the boundless ocean, exposed to the sea breeze, lashed by the rain, I have completely turned into something like Shibugami-sama. Since Kurohime-sama is inherently a crow-like person, she does not stand out too much, but if we return to Onokorojima and show this face to our Oyasu, I wonder how much she would regret it. When I think of that, it is unbearably frustrating, oh. If you trace the origin of this, it is because Kurohime-sama has had her soul extracted too much by her husband, so it has ended up in this result... right, Yoshikō, Fusakō, your faces have also become quite dark. You and this Otaki and Otetsu will surely regret it. Just imagining it from now on, how pitiful, oh.'

Yoshikō: 'Come now, my Otaki and Fusakō's Otetsu as well, they must have been prepared from the beginning. Otaki, that girl, when I was setting out, gave a reluctant pat on my back... Look here, my master, since you are going as an attendant to Kurohime-sama, you must not come back with your facial skin infected by Kurohime-sama's influence even to the color of your face. She spat out that I should even come back with the inside of my heart blackened. But as for the inside of my heart, I most certainly decline. Ha ha ha ha.'

Magokō: 'That the inside of your heart, too, your wife told you to come back blackened like Kurohime-sama is a riddle. Since you are always a diligent fellow, the two of you, you and Otaki, from morning till night, do nothing but petty quarrels that even dogs would not eat, with raw wounds never ceasing, a nuisance to the neighbors all around. Therefore, just as Kurohime-sama yearns for Takayamahiko, you should become single-minded toward this Otaki, and for Otaki's sake, even if you cross mountain slopes a thousand or ten thousandri away, become a man of ardent and deep affection who does not dare to shun it, learning Kurohime-sama's chastity and practicing it identically for your wife... that is a convenient riddle. Your wife, too, is quite a resourceful one. She is surely far superior in wisdom to you. Ha ha ha ha.'

Yoshikō: 'When I hear such things already, I have come to yearn for my wife. If I had wings, I would want to fly back just like this, oh.'

Magokō: 'No matter what is said, once it has come to this, there is already no help for it. It is as if we have become the adopted children of the Kurohime thief, so if you eat the poison, lick the dish as well. At any rate, we shall meet Takayamahiko's husband, be shown a tumultuous riot of hey-hey and ho-ho for one or two acts, and after that, we have a binding karma that we cannot part until we ourselves take the labor of mediation in residence, and the husband and wife exercise the principle of equal opportunity and make a joyous triumphant return to Onokorojima. We have only just arrived at the port of Takehi. It will not do to be driven by the yearning for home at a time like this. The people of Onokorojima always have a strong homesickness, so grand enterprises can ultimately never succeed. Now that it has come to this, isn't it fine whatever happens to a wife or two? If things are favorable, a firm resolve to permanently reside in this Tsukushishima, launch a grand enterprise, and never return to Onokorojima for the rest of one's life is crucial.'

Yoshikō: 'Since you have detested your wife Oyasu from the beginning, you probably have no attachment to Onokorojima. Because such a kind and devoted wife of mine is holding her kneecap and praying to the gods, waiting for my return now and then, I cannot do such a coldhearted thing. To return a single day early and see my wife's rejoicing face is my only pleasure. There is nothing in the world as important as husband and wife. No matter how much success one has achieved in a place like this, what is interesting in a place where one cannot spend even a lifetime with one's wife and children?'

Magokō: 'Aha-ha-ha-ha! While grumbling and fighting every single day as if she were the most hateful creature on earth, you still miss such a wayward chiyakaka, do you not? Love is indeed an inscrutable thing, is it not!'

Fusakō: 'That is only natural. Even Kurohime-sama—pardon me for saying so—has come all the way to such a place to seek out an old bald-pated geezer as misshapen as the shadow-drying of the setting sun; so it is entirely natural that a husband should yearn for his wife. My own Otetsu is also truly, truly kind. The reason Magokō's Anayasu is not overly kind is simply because Magokō is to blame. Women are creatures who will become however you wish if only the husband treats them with kindness and sincerity from the bottom of his heart. If you treat your wife abusively by calling her a household daugu, a kikai, or a sanjiki as you do, even a blind wife would not think of her husband with deep affection. Why don't you follow Kurohime-sama's example and cherish your wife a little? As a souvenir from coming to such a distant place, taking back your kindness toward your wife with a manifold, intensified devotion is the greatest souvenir you could ever bring her... isn't that right, Kurohime-sama...'

With that, he thrust out his tongue, pushed forward his chin, and gazed at her with a somewhat chōrōteki look. Kurohime opened her lips for the first time,

Kurohime: 'When you three people left Jitentōjima, you were truly sincere and earnest believers. How on earth did it come to this? Day by day your sincerity has waned, until finally you have come to look upon even me with eyes of contempt. For what purpose did you come all this way on a shugyō? From here on there is a rocky cave in the valley of the Chikushi Pass where Tatehinowake no Mikoto once had his oil extracted, so if you do not straighten your hearts right now, your oil will be extracted just like Kojimawake of old, and you will become utterly exhausted. Mend your ways while there is still time.'

Magokō: 'Aha-ha-ha-ha! Kurohime-sama, are we three the only ones who need to mend their ways? Have you forgotten that there is still one more person, an old hag who must mend her ways above all others?'

Kurohime: 'How could someone who has completely mended her ways have any room left to mend them? If you observe the conduct of this Kurohime, you will understand well enough.'

Magokō: 'Kannagara, a gift from heaven... you say that, but is it sincerity to silently pilfer another person's boat and ride away in it? If such a thing can be considered the conduct of someone who has completely mended her ways, then thieves are far more reformed than the likes of us, are they not?'

Kurohime: 'Oh, do not sit there quibbling and arguing about petty sophistries. When our boat was broken along the way and we found ourselves at a total impasse, the fact that a boat with no master drifted right over there was what is called a gift from heaven. Does not the proverb say that if you do not take what heaven bestows, misfortune will instead visit your own body? There is a teaching of the sages that God grants to human beings what is indispensable to them. Which is more precious, a single boat, or the lives of the four of us? Think carefully upon the lightness and heaviness, the greatness and smallness of matters. To act according to the occasion and respond to changing circumstances is precisely the Great Way of Kannagara. Is it because you are incapable of understanding even such things that you, who call yourselves believers of Misoshikikyō and eggs of propagation commissioners, have followed me all the way to such a place? O-tsu-ho-ho-ho!'

Magokō: 'I am so astonished I am at a loss for words. And yet, I truly admire your steadfastness in yearning for Takayamahiko-sama—who completely forgot even his precious divine service for the sake of his husband, pushed propagation to the background, utterly forgot about that golden jewel which had driven him half-mad, and dealt you a stunning elbow-blow right before a great crowd. There are oh-so-many points here for us to learn from. Aha-ha-ha-ha, oho-ho-ho, utsu-fu-fu-fu, ehe-he-he-he, ihi-hi-hi!'

Kurohime: 'Now see here, Magokō, are you mocking this old woman?'

Magokō: 'The god of the rocky dwelling has slowly possessed Magokō-sama and begun to utter kototama, you see. U-fu-fu-fu!'

So he laughed aloud. At that very moment, he struck his lower back against a sharp stone by the roadside, uttered a sharp cry of 'Ouch,' turned deathly pale, scowled, closed his eyes, and fell entirely unconscious.

原文（近代文語）

孫公『アハヽヽヽ、毎日日日あれ丈憎相に云うて喧嘩をし乍ら、矢張あんなやん茶嬶が恋しいのか。恋といふものは分らぬものだなア』

房公『ソリヤ其筈だ。黒姫さまでさへも、云うと済まぬが、夕日の影干しのやうな無恰好な禿頭の爺を、こんな所迄はるばる尋ねて来やつしやるのだもの、夫が女房を慕ふのは当然だ。俺ン所のお鉄でもそれはそれは親切なものだよ。孫公ン所のお安は、余り親切にないのは、つまり孫公が悪いのだ。女と云ふものは、男の方から親切に真心を以て可愛がつてやれば、どうでもなるものだ。貴様の様に、女房を家の道具だとか、器械だとか、産児機だとか云つて、虐待するやうな事では、目つかちの女房だつて、夫を親身になつて思つては呉れないよ。チツと黒姫さまに倣つて、お前も女房を大切にしたら如何だい。こんな遠方迄来た土産として、女房に対する親切を益々濃厚に持ち直して帰るが、何よりの女房への土産だよ……なア黒姫さま……』

と舌をニユツと出し、頤をつき出して、稍嘲弄的に目を注ぐ。黒姫は始めて口を開き、

黒姫『お前さま達三人は自転倒島を出た時は、随分誠実な熱心な信者であつた。それが如何したものか、一日々々と誠がうすらぎ、遂には妾に迄、軽侮の目を以て見るやうになつたぢやないか。何の為にお前さまは遥々と修業に出て来たのだい。これから先は建日別命が昔脂を取られた筑紫峠の谷間の岩窟があるから、今の中に心を直しておかぬと、昔の小島別のやうに脂をとられて、ヘトヘトになりますぞえ。今の間に改心をしなされ』

孫公『アハヽヽヽ黒姫さま、改心する人は吾々三人計りですか？　まだ外に一人、第一に改心をせなくてはならぬ婆んつがある事をお忘れになりましたか？』

黒姫『改心し切つた者が、何うして改心する余地がありませうか。お前さまは此黒姫の行ひを見なさつたら、大抵分るだらう』

孫公『惟神だ、天の与へだ……と云つて、人の舟を黙つてチヨロまかし、それに乗つて来るのが誠ですか。あんな事が、改心し切つた人の行ひとすれば、吾々よりも泥棒の方が余程改心しとるぢやありませぬか』

黒姫『エヽ、ツベコベと小理屈を言ひなさんな。途中に船が破れて、進退これ谷まつた時に、主のない船がそこへ流れて来たのは、所謂天の与へだよ。諺にも天の与ふるを取らざれば、災却て身に及ぶと云ふ事があるぢやないか。神は人間になくてならぬものを与へ給ふと云ふ聖者の教がある。船一艘が大切か、吾々四人の生命が大切か、能く事の軽重大小を考へて御覧なさい。機に臨み変に応ずるは、即ち惟神の大道だよ。こんな事が分らぬ様な事で、能うお前さまも、三五教の信者ぢや、宣伝使の卵ぢやと云つて、こんな所迄従いて来ましたな、オツホヽヽヽ』

孫公『呆れて物が言へませぬワイ。併し乍ら、お前さまが夫の為には大切な神務も忘れ、宣伝を次にし、あれ程気違のやうになつて居つた黄金の玉の事をケロリと忘れて、大勢の前で肱鉄砲をかましてくれた高山彦さまを慕ふ其貞節には実に感心だ。大に学ぶべき点がオホアリ大根だ。アハヽヽヽ、オホヽヽヽ、ウツフヽヽヽ、エヘヽヽヽ、イヒヽヽヽ』

黒姫『コレ孫公、お前は此年老を嘲弄するのかい』

孫公『岩屋の神さまがソロソロ孫公さまに憑つて、言霊を始めかけたのだよ。ウフヽヽヽ』

と笑ひこける。途端に路傍の尖つた石に腰を打つけ『アイタタ』と云つたきり、真青な色になり、顔をしかめ、目を塞いで、人事不省になつて了つた。
