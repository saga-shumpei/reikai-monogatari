Torakō: 'That's interesting. Won't a kenkuwa start soon, I wonder?'

If Kurohime-sama is the mother of that taishiō there, that truly is a big deal—a grand commotion of an earthquake and strange phenomena. Nay, it is a great celebration. Na Tamakō, what do you think?'

Torakō, while stretching on tiptoe from the small warrior's window of the dividing fence, briefly gazed at the banquet seat in the front garden of the upper mansion,

Torakō: 'Eh, every single one of them is an ill-mannered fellow. Stripping stark naked, getting drunk on sake, and carrying out bureikō to the utmost heart's content. I suddenly feel like drinking sake too. The fellows here are all witless fellows, it seems. Not a single person comes bringing a single tokkuri even though Torakō has come. Un, there are lots of figs right here. I'll pluck these and hurl them into the banquet seat. It will surely become quiteomoshiro.'

Murmuring to himself like this, he plucked thirty or forty of them and hurled them over the high fence. Numerous heavily intoxicated people, without knowing in their dreams that Torakō was playing such a prank from outside the fence,

A: 'Yai kora, don't make a fool of me. Was it you who made a fig clunk against my head?'

B: 'What are you spitting out? From saizen, if I had kept silent, you would have made it bump against my forehead and said that I hit it—how could you possibly say such a thing. No matter what you say, you intend to buy a kenkuwa with me, don't you. Well, even though I look like this, I am Torakō's number one monban. Shall I show you the arm of Kumakō?'

Saying this, he brandished a tokkuri and swung it in all directions, and at his fierce momentum, all the old and young, men and women, raised screams of 'kyatsu kyatsu' and fled running back and forth, front and rear. Kumakō, carried away by the momentum of the sake, threw bowls and brandished a tokkuri at random; the chaotic, drunken uproar of clattering and tumbling could be heard as clearly as if it were right outside the fence. Torakō perceived that his own monban Kumakō was acting violently, immediately put both hands on the high fence, plunged headlong, and leaped into the garden,

Torakō: 'Hey hey, wait, what kind of violence is Kumakō's fellow doing?'

Kumakō was dead drunk, and his eyes could scarcely see anymore.

Kumakō: 'Na na na na what. Who do you think you are treating me as? I am Kumakō, who has Torakō-sama, the peerless giant chivalrous man of the village of Takeno, as his oyabun. If you blurt out hesitatingly, shall I pull out and extract the raw heads of you both and everyone? Hen, you damn fool, why are you thrusting an opposing spear into what Kuma-sama is doing? It's not the least bit interesting. Ge na na na na garagara garagara uwaputsuputsuputsu.'

Torakō promptly struck Kumakō's cheek with an open hand, as if to make even his head fly, with a sharp slap. Taken by surprise, Kumakō staggered back five or six steps and fell flat on his back with a heavy thud.

Torakō: 'Oi Kuma, won't you pull yourself together? Why are you acting so violently at such a medeta seat?'

Kumakō: 'Well, well, was it you, Oyakata? I am truly sorry. I did not mean to act so violently, but that fellow Hachikō rudely hurled a fig right at Kumakō-sama's head... Torakō supported Kumakō in his arms, made a detour from the front gate of the upper mansion, and brought him to the entrance. Torakō, Oyabun of Takenomura, I understand clearly. Please wait just a moment.'

With these words, she concealed her figure inside the middle gate before the foot soldiers.

After a short while, Kurohime, escorted by Kenokuniwake, Kenonohime, Takehiko, and Ikishikō, pushed open the middle gate with a swift motion and gradually approached the scene.

Torakō: 'Well, well, Kenokuniwake-sama, Kenonohime-sama, how terribly you must have troubled yourselves caring for Kurohime-sama! How has it gone; has the matter of your karmic ties been resolved?'

Torakō: 'Ha, thank you very much. At the same time, Fusakō and Yoshikō, the two who came along escorting Kurohime-sama, are waiting here. And was not Kurohime-sama a relative of yours?'

Kenokuniwake: 'Yes, she was completely unrelated.'

Torakō: 'That, that is truly a regrettable thing. Hey Fusakō, Yoshikō, it's completely no use, resign yourselves to your fate. Since things have come to this, there is no help for it. Fusakō: 'I am Fusakō, an attendant of Kurohime, and this is Yoshikō. We are truly sorry to have intruded for so long and troubled you during your busy time.'

Torakō: 'Hey Fusakō, Yoshikō, since they are speaking so kindly just as you heard, and it is their kind intention, it wouldn't do to let it go to waste, so let us go and accept a cup.'

虎公『いえいえ、御主人のお許しある迄は此処に控へて居りませう。然し此処に黒姫さまと言ふ方が見えて居る筈だ。其黒姫さまのお伴の芳公、房公と云ふ二人の者が門口に待つて居ると云ふ事を、一寸奥へ知らして下さい』

お種『武野村の親分虎公さま、確に申し上げます。何卒待つて居て下さい』

と足軽に中門の中へ姿を隠して仕舞つた。

暫くすると黒姫は建国別、建能姫、建彦、幾公に送られ、中門をサツと開いて此場に徐々とやつて来た。

虎公『ヤアこれはこれは建国別様、建能姫様、黒姫様をいかいお世話で御座いましたらう。如何で御座いましたな、御因縁の解決がつきましたか』

建国別『ア、貴方は虎公の親分さま、能う来て下さいました。さア何卒奥へお這入り下さいませ。此処は門の前、今日は幸ひ私が此処に参つた一周年の祝、何卒皆さま、何もありませぬがお酒なつと飲つて下さいませ』

虎公『ハイ有難う御座います。乍併此処に黒姫様のお伴をして参つた房、芳の両人が見えて居ります。そして黒姫様は貴方の御縁類の方ではありませぬでしたかな』

建国別『はい、全く違ひました』

虎公『それはそれは残念な事で御座います。オイ房公、芳公、薩張駄目だ、観念せい。もう斯うなりや仕方がないわ。矢張火の国都迄お伴して高山彦、黒姫の戦争を観戦するより道はないわ』

房公『私は黒姫の従者房公、芳公と申す者で御座います。黒姫様が永らくお邪魔しましてお忙しい中を誠に済みませぬ』

建国別『いえいえ如何致しまして……黒姫様が御親切に御訪問下さいまして吾々夫婦の者は感涙に咽んで居ります。何卒そんな事言はずに奥へお這入り下さつて、お酒でも一つお飲りなさつて下さいませ』

虎公『オイ房公、芳公あの通り親切に仰有るのだから、折角のお志だ、無にするのも済まないから一杯よばれて来ようぢやないか』

房公『さうだ。一杯頂戴したいものだね……なア芳公、貴様もあまり下戸でもあるまい、一つ御馳走にならうか』

建能姫『何卒皆さま揃うて奥へお這入り下さいませ。差上げる物とては別に何も御座いませぬが、お酒が沢山に用意してありますから、早く此方へお這入り下さいませ。サア吾夫様、奥へ参りませう。黒姫様も余りお急ぎでなくば、お伴の方と一緒に奥の方へ引返して下さつたら如何でせうか』

Kurohime: 'Yes, thank you for your kindness, but my heart is somehow in a hurry, so I will take my leave now. If we have the affinity, we shall meet again.'

Takekuniwake: 'Please, when you meet Sensei, convey to him that the two of us, husband and wife, send our warm regards. To tell the truth, it was our intention as husband and wife to accompany you all the way to Hinokuni, but as you know, things are as they are today, so we must be excused. If you would only stay with us for two or three days, the two of us, husband and wife, would accompany you, but since you are in such a great hurry, it cannot be helped. Please forgive us for our rudeness.'

Kurohime: 'Yes, thank you. Thank you for your care over so long. Fusakō, Yoshikō, the two of you leisurely take your drink and go wherever you will.'

Becoming somewhat desperate, she bowed to the couple and walked away briskly down the narrow path before the gate. Tamakō and the other three persons, urged on by Takehiko and Ikukō, who served as the stewards of the mansion, proceeded toward the inner room.

Left behind, the two persons Fusakō and Yoshikō looked at each other's faces, bit their tongues, tilted their heads slightly, and looked bewildered...

Fusakō: 'Hey Yoshikō, what shall we do? I want to drink wine too, but with Kurohime-sama's mood like that, our calculations failed, and she looked like she would jump into a ravine and die out of utter discouragement. We cannot afford to dawdle. Come, let us chase after her now so that no harm comes to Kurohime-sama, shall we not?'

Yoshikō: 'Hey, that is right. Just as you say, we cannot afford to dawdle. Come, let us go.'

With that, the two men pulled their twisted headbands tight, tucked up the skirts of their garments, and ran off in search of Kurohime, making the ground rumble as they retraced the road they had come.
