§ 1

Having visited the mansion of Takehi, Kurohime of Ananai-kyō, firmly believing Takekuniwake to be her true-born child, had come all the way to visit him; but overcome by the sorrow of an unexpected disappointment, she hastily rushed out of the mansion, leaving her two attendants behind, with her hair disheveled and facing the blowing wind, as she descended the steep mountain path, letting her feet carry her away.

The heart of this Kurohime, whose strength and reliance had completely failed her, was a most merciless and tragic plight to hear of.

Having crossed over ten thousand miles of rough waves, her yearning and adored husband, Takayamahiko, resided in the divine mansion of the Land of Fire, and had taken as his second wife Aikohime, who put flowers to shame.

Rather than hearing that they spent their days in sweet harmony and reigned in prosperity, Kurohime's heart somehow became desolate and ruined, and even her going footsteps appeared weary and forlorn as she trudged along.

"Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara," she cried aloud while descending, "may the divine spirits bestow their blessing, that I may encounter Takayamahiko residing in the capital of the Land of Fire, and live in harmony with Aikohime, opening the depths of my heart to one another, taking each other's hands to spread the divine teachings of Ananai, and save us, O Kamunagara; I pray before the presence of the pure Yoshikohime,"—thus she declaimed as she descended the rugged and perilous rocky path with a rapid clattering tread.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, may the divine spirits bestow their grace.

So as not to lose sight of the figure of Kurohime rushing forth, the two persons, Fusakō and Yoshikō, followed along the ninety-nine winding mountain paths, keeping time with their feet and chanting as they went.

Yoshikō: 'Untokodokuoi dokuoishiyo, earth becoming heaven, untokodokuio, heaven becoming earth, untokodokuio; a strange thing has come to pass. That old man Takayamahiko, taking a young wife thirty years his junior, while Kurohime, a wrinkled old hag of fifty with a sagging rear, crossed over ten thousand miles of stormy seas, untokodokuo in the scorching heat, letting sweat flow drop by drop, tailing after a heartless old man to plead the full measure of her heart—she comes running, untokodokui! My, what on earth is this? Is that not Aikohime as well, untokodokuio? Aikohime, with one foot already plunged into the coffin, dokuoi! An old man with no use in this world, a jurōjin face with a kettle-head, like the shadow of the setting sun dokuoishiyo, or a drying pole, a loach-like monster—to be sending amorous glances and serving him with devotion as her own husband, what a marvel it is! I know not why one would think a world without men is hard to bear, but if one is to be with such an old man, there are far more splendid people about. This Yoshikō, untokodokuio, no matter how filthy a fellow I may be, am young in years, dokuoishiyo, and have a certain charm about me around those parts. If one is to have a man at all, even without holding onto a dried mummy-like skeleton of skin and bones, one should be able to do better; yet I am simply astounded, untokodokuio! My tongue is growing numb, untokodokuio, it is dangerous! Look, look, there is a stone right there; if you cut your waraji sandals, there will be no enduring it. If Torakō were here alone, he might bring out extra sandals, but unfortunately Torakō is at the banquet cup. Ah, from here on, if Kurohime barges into the capital of the Land of Fire, untokodokuio, it will be a terrible affair; surely she will not settle down peacefully, untokodokuio, right, Fusakō? I am worried beyond endurance. Come now, let us make haste! Driven by the single-mindedness of a woman's heart, consumed by vexation and regret, if she loses her temper and casts herself down into a ravine to commit suicide, dokuoi, how on earth will any excuse ever stand when we return to the sacred land? Kurohime-sama is also a Kurohime indeed; what on earth does she mean by shaking us two off and running ahead? Where on earth could that fellow Magokō be hiding, untokodokuio? That fellow's business also weighs on my mind. Distracted hither and thither, my head is in a whirl, why! Untokodokuio, it is dangerous! Look, look, there is a rolling stone right there too; watch your toes as you come. If you happen to slip and injure yourself, there will be no way on earth to excuse it to your missus, O-Tetsu, dokuoishiyo. When we left Jitentōshima, my missus, that O-Taki, said to me: "I say, I say, you there, your body is not yours alone, untokodokuio, but your body belongs to me!"'

My body is untokoshiyo / after all, it is yours, you see / if you are full of conceit, thinking only of yourself / and forget me and get hurt / I will hold a grudge and turn into a vengeful ghost / even if I die, dokkoishyo / you cannot go to Takaamahara / the face of you when you blurted that out / now flickers right before my eyes / and my wife has come to feel so dear to me / your wife is also just the same / wherever you may go / human feelings do not change / please take good care of yourself / I will mind things in place of O-Tetsu / ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara / may Mitama be blessed and prosper']

Moving his hands and stepping in a chidori pattern while taking the beat step by step, he goes down this steep slope.

Kurohime at last arrived at the bank of Takayamagaha. Here, well-suited natural sitting rocks are lined up as if waiting for someone. For a while she rests her breath, worries over things past and future, and falls into shedding tears.

There, one or two large oak trees seal the sky. Kurohime closes her eyes and is lost in thought, when dozens of long-armed monkeys look at this figure from the branches of the oak tree, and one monkey lets itself down from a branch. One after another, joining hands successively, seven or eight fellows become like a chain, and as if a spider came down from the sky, they come down right above Kurohime's head with a 'chiu-chiu', snatch Kurohime's sedge hat with a jerk, and smoothly and gradually carried it up onto the top of the tree. Kurohime has her precious headgear—which might be called the label of a missionary—robbed, and gazing at the flock of long-armed monkeys in the tree, she gets angry in her eyes and glares at them with a look of chagrin. The monkeys feel as if they have played a triumphal song and are teasing Kurohime mockingly with a 'kyatsukyatsu'. Kurohime, having her inauspicious headgear made into a plaything, with her regret knowing no bounds, picked up stones at hand and hurled them toward the group of monkeys in the tree. The monkeys raise their voices in a 'kii-kii-kyā-kyā' and call together their kind from all four directions. In the twinkling of an eye, monkeys gathered on top of the oak tree as thick as gumi fruit. Then they rain down a shower of urine from the tree top, drop dung, and pluck oak nuts to hurl them straight at Kurohime. Kurohime meets with the double attack of oak nuts and urine, unable even to move her body, and stood looking resentful. If Kurohime were to move even a single step, the flock of monkeys would instantly gather around, scratch and tear at her, and it has come to a situation where there is no knowing what might happen.

Monkeys, as a rule, once they see a sign of weakness, ride the momentum to the utmost, pursue you, and commit violence. Kurohime seems to have grasped that breathing pattern to some extent, and even putting on a brave front, without moving her body so much as a tree grown from the earth, she was glaring eyeball to eyeball with the flock of monkeys. Moment by moment the flock of monkeys gathers. Once again, a monkey's arm comes down over her head, and this time wraps tightly around her hair with its hand, trying to pull her up. Kurohime also can no longer bear it, and interlocking her hands, adopts the posture of chinkon while saying, 'One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, hundred, thousand, ten thousand!' The flock of long-armed monkeys sees this, interlocking their hands respectively, and while saying 'kyatsukyatsu', glare at Kurohime without leaving a single one. Kurohime spread her legs, leaped up with a ton, and showed herself lying flat in the shape of the character for 'big' (dai) on the great earth. The fellows of long-armed monkeys, imitating this again, paying no heed even to being atop the tree, leaped up all at once, and the very moment they became shaped like the character for 'dai', tumbled down in an avalanche onto the earth with a stendo, raising shrieks of 'kyatsukyatsu', and run away in a sorry plight—how comical it is. Kurohime at last stroked her chest in relief, took a handkerchief out from her bosom, and wiped away her sweat.

Five or six large fellows, who might be called the ringleaders of the monkeys, were watching Kurohime's state from atop the oak tree without even running away, but seeing Kurohime wipe her sweat, similarly with both hands, while making the pretense of taking a handkerchief out from the bosom, smoothly stroked their faces. The large monkey in the tree once more plucked oak nuts and began hurling them like driving rain and hail toward Kurohime. Kurohime spread both hands, held one leg straight up, and pounded the earth with her left foot, making a tonto-tonto-ton. The large monkeys in the tree all at once spread both hands, held one leg straight up, and the very moment they imitated the earth-pounding on top of the tree with a tonto-tonto-ton, they crashed down to the ground without a single one remaining with a dosu-dosu-dosun, raised shrieks of 'kyatsukyatsu', stumbling and tumbling, and hid their figures God knows where. At that nick of time, in a considerably fierce wind blowing briskly, the sedge hat Kurohime had been wearing fell down right before Kurohime noiselessly, fluttering softly like the falling paulownia leaves of early autumn.

Kurohime entertained thoughts of revival, crouched directly upon the ground, clapped her hands, and began to offer the Amatsunorito. However, the voice of the norito was coming from nowhere in particular, and carried a feeble tremor.

To such a place, both Fusakō and Yoshikō came running up, panting while making the earth rumble with heavy footsteps, and caught up to this spot.

Fusakō: 'Ha ha ha, ah, my breath is painful. Well, well, Kurohime-sama, I am so glad you were here. You have no idea how worried we were.'

Yoshikō: 'Kurohime-sama, is the hair on your head not terribly disheveled?'

Kurohime: 'You have come to a good place. Until just now, those scoundrels of tenagazaru came coming in numbers too many to count, and they even messed up my hair like this. But thanks to Kamisama, every single one of them has retreated, so please be at ease. You fellows, you were awfully fast, were you not? Did you have time to receive your sake?'

Fusakō: 'Absurd, is this a time for such things? Because Kurohime-sama went back with a changed complexion, we were beside ourselves with worry, thinking that if something untoward were to happen it would not do, and so both of us flew through the air and ran all the way here.'

Kurohime: 'Ah, that was a regrettable thing indeed. And how are Torakō-sama and Tamakō doing?'

Yoshikō: 'By now they are probably dead drunk on sweet sake, playing the fool. When things come to this, those without a master have it easy.'

Kurohime: 'Since there is no need for such hesitation, you would have done well to take your time and receive some sake; what a truly regrettable miss of a drink you have made.'

Fusakō: 'Yes, thanks to you, we have missed our drink, and we are grateful. But Kurohime-sama, you too have had your purpose completely turned upside down, have you not? Since the general is meeting with an upside-down outcome, there is no reason we attendants should not miss out as well, ha ha ha ha, truly, it is the height of pity for us all, ho tso ho tso ho tso.'

So saying with a pout, he shook his shoulders and mocked her. Kurohime,

Kurohime: 'Hey, are you making such a joke again? Hey, what a gloomy fellow.'

Cursing foully, she abruptly snatched up her bamboo hat, leaned on her Kongō staff, quickened her pace, and dashed off briskly, paying no heed to the two. Raising a loud voice, Fusakō said,

Fusakō: 'I say, I say, Kurohime-sama, please wait a moment, that is not how things are.'

Kurohime: 'Hey, you two are young and so your legs are strong, take your time and rest; this Kurohime is old and her legs are heavy, so I shall go on ahead step by step. Catch up to me from behind.'

Yoshikō: 'I say, I say, Kurohime-sama, if you let your stubbornness show and travel alone, the monkey fellows will attack you again. Please wait a moment, I am advising you out of concern for your welfare.'

Without even lending an ear and without looking back, wiggling her bottom jigglingly, using her staff for strength, Kurohime hurried onward toward the capital of the Land of Fire, making a clacking sound with her staff upon the rain-washed, stone-strewn slope.

と口汚く罵り乍ら、矢庭に笠を引つかぶり、金剛杖をつき、足を早めて、二人に構はずスタスタと駆出した。房公は大声を張り上げて、

房公『モシモシ黒姫さま、一寸待つて下さいな、さうしたものぢやありませぬぞや』

黒姫『エヽお前達は若いから、足が達者だ、ゆつくり休んでお出で、此黒姫は年が老つて、足が重いから、ボツボツ先へ行きます。後から追ひ付いておくれよ』

芳公『モシモシ黒姫さま、我を出して一人旅をなさると、又猿の奴が襲撃しますぞや。暫く待つて下さいな、私はお前さまの身の上を案じて忠告するのだよ』

黒姫は耳にもかけず、後ふり向きもせず、尻をプリンプリン振り乍ら、杖を力に雨に洗ひさらされた石だらけの坂路を、コツリコツリと杖に音させつつ、火の国の都を指して急ぎ行く。
