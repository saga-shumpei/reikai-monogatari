Chapter 19: 生命の親 [960]

生命の親〔960〕

§ 1

Kurohime is making her way in haste along the valley path filled with stone blocks toward the capital of Hinokuni.

A dangerous single log bridge is thrown across the deep valley river along the way.

Kurohime had become a cautious human being who would even tap a stone bridge before crossing it.

This was because she had learned her lesson from her foolish mistake of visiting the propagandist of Takekuniwake at the mansion of Takehi, thinking foolishly that he might be her own child.

When Kurohime peered beneath the single log bridge, the single log bridge, exposed to wind and rain for many years, was rotted away at the bridgehead by some seventy or eighty percent.

Wondering what on earth she should do about this, she stands hesitating at the bridgehead and lets out a sigh.

At that very moment, a small child of about three shaku appears abruptly, looks up into Kurohime's face, and smiles a grin,

My love is the single log bridge of the deep valley river

To cross it is terrifying, if not crossed

I cannot meet the one I yearn for

Having chanted this, the child vanishes instantly with a puff along with white smoke.

Stricken by this bizarre phenomenon and enveloped in clouds of anxiety, Kurohime devotes herself with all her heart to prayer, chanting, 'Kaminagara Tamashii Sakihamaseyo.'

This place was just before the pass of Mukōtōge.

To go to the capital of Hinokuni, the proper route is to pass through Hinokunisaki.

However, Kurohime, failing to notice the wide Hinokuni highway to her left, ended up heading blindly to the right and straying into this mountain path.

At this time, once again and quite suddenly, seven or eight small children appear at the foot of the bridge, holding hands with one another,

Children: 'Out it came, behold it came, it has appeared

At the foot of Mukōtōge, in the shade of the camphor tree, a demon appeared

Thinking it a demon, how terrifying

The three lords of the great snake have appeared

Binding Lady Aai securely

A man named Takahimewhich is Takahiko

And even Kanekō bound tightly

They dug a hole and buried him

A great boulder rests upon it'

Having said just that, the figures of the children vanish with a puff once more, and in their wake, white smoke sways faintly.

Kurohime clasps both hands and tilts her head,

Kurohime: 'Oh dear, this makes no sense at all. Whether the children who just appeared are demons or gods, I know not, but they spoke of something that weighs heavily on my mind without reason. They said a man named Takahiko was bound tight and buried, and that Lady Aai was buried as well. Could it perhaps be about my beloved husband, Takahiko-sama? And Lady Aai must surely be Lady Aiko. Speaking of the foot of Mukōtōge, I know not how much distance remains from here, but come what may, I can no longer remain sitting idly. Ah, what on earth should I do? ... Is there anyone in this world as burdened with karma as I am? How dreadful, it must be that the heavenly retribution for abandoning the baby boy I received with such pains from the gods in the folly of my youth has come home to roost, causing everything I do and attempt to be as mismatched as the beak of a crossbill. Now that I think on it, truly this body of mine is deep in sin.'

So she murmurs to herself, drooping her head powerlessly accompanied by falling tears.

At this moment, from somewhere unknown, a propaganda song is heard coming forth.

The morning sun, whether it shines or clouds over

The moon, whether it waxes or wanes

Even if the great earth should sink

No matter what disasters may come

A propagandist entrusted unto the gods

Uphold and carry through sincerity alone

The gods are together with you

Your sincerity becoming manifest

The divine way that saves you

The living gods who save this world

Are not limited to the mountains of Tenkyō

Gods dwell in every place

Embracing the blessings of the gods

Go forth to the very end, three-five

Propagandist of Kurohimeuji

The single log bridge of the deep valley river

However perilous it may appear

If the flower of sincerity of faith

Should bloom within your heart

You shall easily cross the divine bridge

Advance, advance, cross over swiftly

I am Tamajiwakeuji

Attaching myself close to your spirit

Guarding your future path

I have advanced all the way hither

Ah, Kaminagara kamigami

May your august spirits be blessed and prosper'

Having sung just that, the voice of the propaganda song stops abruptly.

Kurohime, gaining strength from hearing this propaganda song nearby and feeling strongly encouraged that Tamajiwake is in this vicinity, pulls herself together from her dejected state and waits with eager anticipation.

Nevertheless, far from the figure of Tamajiwake, not even a single beast shows its form.

Kurohime makes up her mind to cross this single log bridge with mincing steps, crosses over to the far side, and strokes her chest in relief,

Kurohime: 'Ah, what a dangerous moment it was. To think that such a decayed bridge could be crossed safely! This too is surely the blessing of Kamisama, and must be proof that Tendausama has not yet forsaken Kurohime. Ah, how thankful, how grateful, how awe-inspiring!'

With these words, she puts her hands together and, amid tears, chants a norito of thanksgiving and prayer.

There appears a girl of some fourteen or fifteen years, appearing frail and languid. When looked upon, her eyes are swollen, her complexion is pale, her hair is disheveled, and the hem of her kimono is covered in soil. Seeing this shōjiyo, Kurohime immediately addresses her:

Kurohime: 'Hey, hey, as a young girl, what have you come to do on such a terrifying yamamichi? As I see, your eyes are swollen. Your hair is disheveled, your facial complexion has turned pale, and the hem of your kimono is covered in red soil, is it not? There must be some circumstance behind this. If there is no sashitsukahe, please tell this to obasama. I am a sendenshi of Ananai-kyō. To the extent that my strength reaches, I shall surely become your help.'

When she speaks thus with kind consolation, the girl bends her waist and expresses her thanks withing in:

Shōjiyo: 'Obasama, arigatō gozaimasu. Dōzo, please help me. I am a girl named Oume. An elder sister named Oai was captured by warumono, killed, and buried beneath the earth. And together with two gentlemen…'

Having spoken thus far, she bursts into tears with a loud cry. The girl's spirit, which had been tightly strung until now, was melted by Kurohime's affectionate words, and the moment she finally felt relieved, her spirit slackened and she collapsed powerlessly. Kurohime descends with great difficulty to the Fukayadani River, draws water into a water canteen, makes the girl drink it, sprinkles it upon her face, and tends to her with utmost diligence. Not allowing the efficacy of Kurohime's earnest nursing to be in vain, the girl regains her breath, strokes her chest with a suffering look, and says:

Oume: 'Ah, how frightening, how frightening, obasama! Dōzo, please help me. I beg of you.'

Kurohime: 'You said in your words just moments ago that your elder sister named Oai was killed by warumono and buried beneath the earth.'

Oume: 'Yes, they bound her with tight cords and buried her in the earth beneath the forest. And together with a person named Takayamahiko and a scoundrel named Kanehiko, they were buried in a deep hole, and after placing many, many large stones upon it, they went away.'

Upon hearing the name Takayamahiko, Kurohime turns her face pale and points her mouth:

Kurohime: 'What did you say? What has become of the person called Takayamahiko?'

Oume: 'Yes, my elder sister Oai and I were bound, and while we were being tormented by a warumono named Hebizawa no Sankō, he chanted the propaganda songs of Ananai-kyō and came to help us—he is that honorable person. They threw dust into his eyes, knocked him down, bound him with rough rope, and buried him together with my elder sister. Uwa-ts……'

With that, she weeps and prostrates herself again. Kurohime, flustered and alarmed:

Kurohime: 'Hey, hey, Oume-sama, hold yourself firmly. Where is Takayamahiko-sama buried? Come, we must go quickly and save him. Is not the one called Oai-sama none other than Yoshikohime of the Hinodewake capital? Come, let us go.'

When she urges her, the girl replies:

'Yes, because it was so frightening, my wits went blank, and I cannot clearly understand what obasama is saying, but I will guide you, so dōzo please help them.'

Kurohime: 'Ah, that must be so, it cannot be helped. How pitiful, it is only natural to be afraid. Even so, how miraculously you managed to escape. Come, we must not dawdle even for a single moment. Once life departs, there is no recovering it, you see.'

Oume: 'Obasama, I shall guide you. Dōzo, please follow closely after me.'

And she leads the way ahead. However, Oume's spirit had been drained by the commotion of the previous night, and moreover, as a result of straining herself with all her might to try and remove the piled-up stones, her body had become exceedingly fatigued, and even her footsteps were wobbly.

Therefore, her feet would not move as she wished, and out of extreme exasperation Kurohime grew anxiously impatient and could not bear it.

Kurohime: 'Oume-sama, or whatever you are called, I shall carry this for you. You must guide me from my back. Not a single moment can be delayed...'

Speaking thus, she hoisted Oume firmly onto her back, and using her staff for support, she rushed on, forgetting herself, along the mountain path overgrown with weeds.

Just when she thought they had come nearly ten chō, Oume spoke in a faint voice from her back,

'Oba-sama, right over there at the roots of that camphor tree, aren't there a great many stones piled up? Over there, Anesa-ma and the two persons are buried. Wān wān.'

And once again she burst into weeping. Console-ling the crying and wailing Oume, Kurohime hurriedly hastened to the front of the mound, took Oume down from her back, summoned her utmost Diamond-like strength, chanted the Shingau with her mouth, and laid her hands upon the huge stone. Finding that push or thrust it would not budge even an inch, she felt discouraged, and letting tears stream down her face, she began fervently chanting the Amatsunorito with all her might.

At this time, appearing at the foot of the round-log bridge, eight children about three shaku in height emerged from nowhere, and with the lightness of throwing balls, effortlessly tossed away the huge stone, flinging it four or five ken ahead. Then, turning once again into white smoke, the form of the children vanished. Suffocating with tears of gratitude, Kurohime frantically pushed aside the earth and, covered in sweat, dug them out.

When she looked, the three males and females were buried together side by side with their pillows aligned. Praying for divine assistance within her heart, Kurohime dug out the bodies of the three, laid them upon the green grass, swiftly untied the binding ropes one by one, chanted the Heaven's Number Song, and prayed for the resuscitation of the three.

In the meantime, Oume took Kurohime's water flask, drew valley water, and made the three take it into their mouths. Crying out 'Un' with a single shout, Oai abruptly sat up, saw Oume's form, and with a joyful look,

'Ah, were you my younger sister Oume? How wonderfully you have remained safe,'

she made as if to leap upon her. Oume said joyfully,

'Anesa-ma, I am so happy, the Oba-sama of Aosatokyō has saved us. Offer your thanks.'

Comparing the faces of the two men, Kurohime had been intently examining whether they might be Takayamahiko,

'Ya, this was Magokō after all. Now what would be best to do?'

and touched her hand to his body. There was still warmth somewhere about. Paying no heed to Oai's words of gratitude, Kurohime performed soul-calming for the two men with all her might and chanted the Heaven's Number Song.

The two finally groaned 'Ūn', sat up, and looked around in all directions restlessly.

Magokō: 'Oh, is it Kurohime-sama? How wonderfully you have saved us.'

Having said just that, tears streamed down, and he lowered his head to the great earth in gratitude. Kanekō looked around in all directions restlessly,

'Ya, Oai-sama, it was truly a perilous affair.'

Oai: 'Kanekō, the missionary-sama of Aosatokyō has saved the lives of all of us. Offer your thanks.'

Kanekō: 'This, this, though I know not who you may be, you have well and truly picked up our lives. Because of villains, we were buried alive in such a place. If your coming had been even a little bit later, our lives could not have been saved. I am Kanekō of the village of Yakata, and I am not a very good person. That it has come to this is entirely divine punishment. Please make apologies to the gods.'

Kurohime: 'Well, it was more wonderful than anything. I too have received wonderful divine virtues, and there is nothing that feels so good. If you thank me in that way, my hard-earned virtuous act will vanish like smoke. Everything is simply what the god has saved. Thanks to that, we were able to save all of you, so please offer your thanks to the god.'

Beginning with Magokō, the four of Oai, Kanekō, and Oume sat correctly behind Kurohime, chanted the Amatsunorito, concluded the prayer of gratitude for saving their lives, and the party of five turned back the way they had originally come, making their way toward the mountain pass of Mukou-tōge.

『ヤア、之は孫公ぢやつた。まア如何したら良からう』

と身体に手を触れて見た。まだ何処ともなしに温味がある。黒姫はお愛の感謝の言葉を耳にもかけずに、二人の男に一生懸命に鎮魂をなし、天の数歌を謡ひ上げて居る。二人は漸く『ウーン』と呻いて起き上り四辺をキヨロキヨロ見廻して居る。

孫公『やあ、黒姫さまか。ようまあ助けて下さいました』

と云つたきり涙をタラタラと流し、大地に頭を下げて感謝して居る。兼公は四辺をキヨロキヨロ見廻し、

『ヤア、お愛様、誠に危い事で御座いました』

お愛『兼公、三五教の宣伝使様が、妾達一同の生命を助けて下さつたのですよ。お礼を申しなさい』

兼公『之は之は、誰方か存じませぬが、能くもまあ生命を拾つて下さいました。悪者の為めにこんな処に、生埋めにされて居りました。モ少し貴女のお出でが遅かつたら、生命は助かりませぬでした。私は矢方の村の兼公と申して、あまり良くない人物で御座います。斯うなつたのも全く天罰で御座いませう。何卒神様にお詫をして下さいませ』

黒姫『まあ、何よりも結構で御座いました。妾も結構なお神徳を頂きまして、斯んな気持の良い事は御座いませぬ。さうお礼を云つて貰ひましては、妾の折角の善行が煙となつて消えて了ひます。何事も皆神様が助けて下さつたのです。大きな岩石で圧へつけてあつた此塚は婆アの力に及ばず、苦しみ悶えて居る矢先、木花咲耶姫様の御化身が現はれて、岩を取除けて下さいました。其お蔭で皆さまをお助けする事が出来ましたのですから、何卒神様にお礼を申上げて下さい』

孫公初めお愛、兼公、お梅の四人は黒姫の後に端坐し、天津祝詞を奏上し救命謝恩の祝詞を終つて一行五人はもと来し道へ引返し、向日峠の山道指して辿り行く。

あゝ惟神霊幸倍坐世。
