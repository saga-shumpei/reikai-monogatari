Chapter 2: 孫甦 [943]

孫甦〔943〕

§ 1

Magokō, the moment he rolled over laughing, struck his hipbone violently against a rock corner and, uttering a mere 'Uhn,' fell completely unconscious. Fusakō and Yoshikō, both beside themselves with panic, fetched valley water to splash on his face, forced his mouth open to make him drink water, and earnestly continued various ministrations. However, Magokō's body grew progressively colder, and no matter how much they called or shouted, he gave no response whatsoever. Kurohime looked coldly down with a sideways glance at Magokō's fallen body.

Fusakō: 'Now, Kurohime-sama, Magokō is in such a plight. Why will you not pray to the Kamisama for him? Please hurry and chant the kazūta to perform the tamagahe. If you dawdle and do things half-heartedly, he will no longer be among us, you know.'

Kurohime smiled a wry smile,

Kurohime: 'The admonitions of the Kamisama are terrifying things indeed. All of you, look upon this and make kaishin. To revere your superiors, it says... What on earth do you take the heavenly gokisoku to be? Ever since crossing the Pacific Ocean, this Magokō has talked back to every single thing Kurohime said and has insulted his superiors; since the sin of violating divine law has naturally returned to requite him, no matter how much you beg or how much you pray, it is already hopeless. ...Now, Fusakō, Yoshikō, you too must have talked back to this Kurohime just like Magokō, and spoken ill of her as well. Not knowing which of you shall be the second candidate, ohohoho... Ah, what a pleasing thing. If not for such things, it would be too foolish to practice faith in the Kamisama. The propaganda song of the Misoshiru-kyō, which says that the Kamisama shall manifest in the open to separate good from evil, is surely not a lie, is it? As for the saying that the Kamisama rewards the good and destroys the evil, has not this Kurohime's mouth been teaching it until it grew sour? That is why they say the Kamisama is fearsome. Ah, Kamunagara Tamae Masase.'

Fusakō: 'Even so, Kurohime-sama, are you not far too cruel? The Kamisama is the Kamisama, but if this Magokō were Takayamahiko-sama, Kurohime-sama, could you-sama remain with such an indifferent face?'

Kurohime: 'Confining myself strictly to Takayamahiko-sama, he would never commit such incomprehensible acts of violating divine law. Have no idle worries, so pray do not trouble yourselves, ufuhoho.'

Yoshikō: 'Oi Fusakō, it looks as though Magatsuhahi has possessed Kurohime. Were it not so, even the most heartless and cruel human being could not possibly assume such an attitude when their crucial disciple has perished. Shall the two of us from both sides use chinkon-zeme to cast out Kurohime's evil spirit?'

Fusakō: 'I shall attend to Magokō's nursing. There is still a little warmth in him, so he might yet revive. You take charge of subduing Kurohime's Magatsuhahi.'

So saying, Fusakō directed himself toward Magokō's fallen body, performed chinkon with all his might, and began chanting the heavenly kazūta. Yoshikō clasped both hands and directed his spiritual energy toward Kurohime, uttering 'Uhn, uhn.'

Kurohime: 'Ohohoho, as it is said that the enemy is in Honnōji and that our true enemy lies within our own heart, the reason this Kurohime appears evil is precisely because a demon dwells within your own heart. Rather than performing such foolish tricks, you should apologize quickly to the Kamisama. Until this Kurohime's anger subsides, Fusakō or you, it is all the same as Magokō. Oh, what a truly pitiful sight. Unless your kaishin stems from the root of your heart, no matter how much you pray to the Kamisama, it will avail you nothing. From now on, you shall place the Kamisama first and Kurohime second in all things.'

Yoshikō: 'When you become husband and wife once again with Takayamahiko-sama, how will things stand? Will Takayamahiko be third, or perhaps second? It would be inconvenient for us unless you let us hear about that first.'

Kurohime: 'This is hardly the time to be talking about such matters. Magokō has grown cold just like that, and yet do you feel nothing at all?'
