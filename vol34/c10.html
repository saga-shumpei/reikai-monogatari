Chapter 10: 空縁 [951]

空縁〔951〕

§ 1

The Kamuyakata of Takenogahara is newly built as a Kozatsupari upon a hill of beautiful Fūkei. Chitose-old trees, Utsusau, wrap around the Keidai, making it a truly Kaugau Chiten. Before it, the Fukatanigaha, which sends forth Gekitanhimatsu, flows across. From morning until night, the Shinto who gather there follow one after another, and the Shintoku of Kami shines across the Yomo.

In the inner room of the hall, the Sendenshi of Takekuniwake is leaning against a Kefusoku, heaving a deep Toiki. A Zetsusei Bijin, who had entered gracefully carrying hot water on a tray, was Takenohime, having opened the sliding door softly.

Takenohime: 'Tsumasama, good morning. The hot water has boiled; please, have some.'

She offers it. Seeing that he does not even notice the voice of Takenohime, he keeps his eyes closed and is lost in Meisau, as if in Mokunen. Takenohime raises her voice slightly.

Takenohime: 'Moshi-moshi, Tsumasama, the hot water has boiled; please, have some.'

With an Omomochi as if startled by this voice,

Takekuniwake: 'Oh, you are Takenohime. Has the water boiled? Thank you, I shall partake.'

Takenohime: 'Tsumasama, it has been exactly one full year today since you came to my house. Yet, not even once have you shown me a cheerful face. At first, I thought perhaps I was lacking and not to your liking, so I worried much, but since you are always kind to me, I could not understand it, and thinking that perhaps you had some deep secret, I have always been observing your state, though I felt sorry to do so. However, in your sleep one night... I want to meet my father and mother just once... those words you spoke remain in my ears even now. Please, as I am your wife, you need not hold back anything from me; I beg you to tell me clearly.'

As she asked this with trepidation, Takekuniwake replied,

Takekuniwake: 'I am truly sorry for hiding it from you, my wife. Do not resent me as a distant husband. You are the daughter of the noble Takebiwake-no-Mikoto, but this Takekuniwake does not even know the whereabouts of his parents, let alone have any clue as to his own origins. Now, I have succeeded Takebiwake-no-Mikoto, received the splendid name of Takekuniwake, and serve the noble Kami, but in my childhood, I was called Kintaro, knew not my surname, and having been picked up by others, I have managed to grow to this day at thirty-five through the compassion of others. Whether my parents are alive in this world at this time, or whether they have become people of the other world, I know not, but an attachment of wanting to meet my parents, wanting to meet them, rises from the bottom of my belly, and always, without knowing it, my face would swell, and I showed you a sullen face. Please, do not take it ill.'

Takenohime: 'How unworthy, what are you saying? Today is the auspicious day of one full year since you entered my home as my husband; please, restore your mood, and let us both offer thanks to Kami, and perhaps treat all the officials and Shinto to sacred sake in celebration of our hearts. Through the grace of Kami, you will surely meet your parents someday. Please, do not be so discouraged, and live with a pure heart.'

Takekuniwake: 'Yes, thank you. Then, today, let us cheerfully offer thanks to Kami. And let us have the officials and Shinto partake of the sacred sake.'

Takenohime, looking happy, left the place briskly to order the preparations for the banquet to the official, Takehiko.

Afterward, Takekuniwake clasps his hands together, and unable to stop the tears of gratitude for his parents' circumstances and the kind words of Takenohime, he lets fall the rain of an unseasonable evening shower onto the sleeves of his teaching robes.

Takenohime opens the sliding door quietly, bows respectfully with both hands on the floor, and says in a quiet voice,

Takenohime: 'Tsumasama, I have ordered everything for today's celebratory banquet to Takehiko. Come, let us go before Kami to offer our thanks together.'

Takekuniwake advanced deep into the Shinden while lighting his face with a look of satisfaction at Takenohime's gentle Kotoba, and offered his Norito of thanksgiving and Kiguwan. The voice of Takenohime like rolling jewels, and the voice of Takekuniwake's Norito of clear and sonorous Ontoraurau harmonized together with the Kinshitsu, and an indescribable Fūin echoed thoroughly throughout the Keidai, overflowing with a divine Kuōkei.

Starting with Takehiko and other Kanbu and Yakuin, countless Rauniyakunanniyo crowded in from Sauchō, being surrounded by a harmonious atmosphere to participate in Kefu's Shiyukuen, forming groups of Sansangogo in various places of the Keidai, and praising the high virtues of the Fūfu Takekuniwake on every side. The superiors and inferiors were in complete harmony and joy, and the mood of the heavenly pure land overflowed to the absolute limit inside and outside the mansion. To such a place, there were a man and a woman of Michisha who knocked on the front gate and entered.

Woman: 'Moshimoshi, won't you open this gate for a moment? I am a person called Kurohime who has come from Jitentōjima. If you tell them that she is the wife of the missionary of Takayamahiko of the Land of Fire, Takekuniwake-sama will surely meet me...'

The gatekeeper Ikukō was surprised and panicked at the voice calling herself the wife of Takayamahiko, and quickly opened the front gate. The gate through which countless worshippers went in and out was located on the side. This gate was solely the gate for Takekuniwake's private residence. Kurohime said,

Kurohime: 'Gokurōsama'

saying thus, she hurriedly advanced into this gate. Ikukō looked at the face of a single man,

Ikukō: 'Ah, you are Tamasama, aren't you? Why on earth did you guide this person here again?'

Tamakō: 'There is something I cannot quite understand. If by any chance, this person might be Takekuniwake-sama's mother-sama. That is why I guided her here anyway.'

whispering thus, drawing his mouth close to the ear with a countenance as if minding being overheard by others.

Ikukō: 'That is terrible. Today is the place where the Shiyukuen of the full first year since Takekuniwake-sama's awakening is being held. That the mother-sama should arrive at such a joyous place is a matter more and more joyful. Hey Tamakō, please act as gatekeeper in my place for a while. I am going from this moment to report this good news to Takekuniwake-sama...'

saying this behind and running to catch up with Kurohime.

Ikukō: 'Moshimoshi, mother-sama of Takekuniwake, please come along steadily. I will first report to the master and come to welcome you. By all means, please have the trouble of standing and waiting for a while near this middle gate.'

and already the hasty Ikukō, firmly believing her to be Takekuniwake's mother, rushed indiscreetly toward the inner room in a flurry.

In the inner room, the Fūfu Takekuniwake sat facing each other, scooping and exchanging the sake of celebration.

Takenohime: 'My husband-sama, is there ever a time when I feel so unaccountably happy as today? In regard to that as well, if your parents-sama were to come to this seat, and parents and Fūfu like this intimately received the Naorai sacred sake, how very happy it would be.'

Takekuniwake: 'Ah, that is so. However, just now in the midst of my prayer before the kami, a strange thought suddenly arose. I had a feeling that my parents are surely living in this world and acting as splendid missionaries for the kami. And today, a feeling somehow floated up as though a clue to meet my parents might be formed, and the taste of the sake became even better.'
