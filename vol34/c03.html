§ 1

Magokō, without minding the details, remains in a state of divine possession and continues to chant.

'Ufu-fu-fu-fu-fū, ufu-fu-fu-fu-fu! / The Ushitorakonjin has appeared, / sorting out the uzaumuzau. / Kurohime, who lost her senses, / threw away her ukiyo mood, / and came wandering all the way hither, / chasing after a noisy man, / with a norokekata that makes one sick to death. / Though they say it is the way of the ukiyo, / it is the darkness of love where one wastes one's ukimi. / Setting up a sign of the Uranai teachings, / for a time, brilliantly and spectacularly, / even Kurohime, who made her haburi felt, / because of carelessly taking / Takayamahiko as her husband, / came to know the taste of the ukiyo, / and her empty heart wanders rōro, / under a traveling sky with no fixed destination. / Meeting with eye-blinding hardships, / she left behind the sacred ground of a marvel, / crossing the rolling, churning unabara, / coming over hither yōyō, / wandering along pathetically. / A blunderer's sendenshi, / sweetly deceived by Kurohime-sama, / we were like cows led to Zenkuōji, / following behind a visiting old woman, / and have moved to Tsukushi Island, far from the ukiyo of Africa. / An old man with a head like a melon, slender and long, / she chases after him, wearing away her ukimi. / A noisy woman, all alone, / with a soul like a maggot, / coming hither rōro thus— / however enthusiastic about women / Takayama might be, must it not be annoying? / Rather than lingering in a world of annoyance / and seeing miserable eyes, / you had better reform more quickly! / With a backside like a grinding mill, / wandering about is of no use. / Ah, kami-nagara-kamigami! / Is it not an annoying thing? / If you have the leisure to dawdlingly / act like this in such a place, / speed your feet and go away to the Land of Fire / even an instant sooner! / Though her face may be different, who knows, / Takayamahiko is indeed there! / And that very Takayamahiko-sama, / as for one of the eight maidens / of the Great Deity Kami-Susanoo, / a person called the maiden Yoshikohime / waits upon him morning and evening; / needless to say regarding all household affairs, / with service reaching to the places where it itches, / leaving no drop of water untouched— / the God Takayamahiko / is bursting with laughter and letting his fat drool, / in a most excellent mood! / Ah, kami-nagara-kamigami! / How would it be to show Kurohime-sama / to such a place for even a single glance? / Instantly casting her two eyes upward, / wrinkling her nose and twisting her mouth, / the fire of resentment would surely / scorch even the clouds of heaven! / Takayamahiko is a formidable fellow! / Unlike that gossiping old hag / who has tied fifty hips, / with a white face that puts snow to shame, / and plump, nourished skin— / a maiden with no fault to be found anywhere! / Taking her as a wife, morning and evening, / he loves her, calling her Yoshiko, Yoshiko. / How envious it is to the eyes of others! / Truly a splendid couple! / Ehe-he-he-he-hetsu-he, he-he-he-he-he!'

Kurohime: 'Hey, hey, Magokō, is that true of yours? That that Takayama-sama has as his wife that maiden called Yoshikohime—the daughter of Susanoo-no-Mikoto who closed the Rock Cave of Heaven—and is residing in the Land of Fire, is a story that defies comprehension. It cannot possibly be so, limited to Takayama-sama though he is, but please tell me the true thing. No matter how carefree Kurohime may be, upon hearing such a thing, I cannot let it pass by no matter what. Come, please quickly make clear and answer the false and the true.'

Fusakō: 'Kurohime-sama, Magokō is saying such a thing and teasing you. You must not take it seriously. ... Say, Yoshikō, is it not somehow suspicious?'

Yoshikō: 'No, I by no means think it suspicious; try thinking about it well. The manner since a moment ago—is it not by no matter what means something beyond human mischief? Without a doubt, there can be no mistake in the divine revelation.'

Fusakō: 'Even so, are not the things he says contradictory? One moment he says Takayamahiko is living with the daughter of Iseya in the Sacred Land of Aya, and the next he says he is now in the Land of Fire with Yoshikohime, letting his fat drool—what on earth is what, I do not understand the meaning of it in the least.'

Yoshikō: 'That is indeed so. Most likely a Magatsu-kami has possessed him. ... Hey, Magokō, will you not pull yourself together? You are rolling your eyes and have evidently lost your senses again, speaking such contradictory things. Will you not pull yourself together a little bit? We too would feel disheartened if you were to go mad in a place like this.'

§ 2

Kurohime: 'Indeed, just as Fusakō says, Magokō's words are completely inconsistent from start to finish; a thing called Magatsuhi seems clever, yet what a foolish being he is. Just when one thinks he is saying Takayama-sama is in Onokorojima, he says he is in the Land of Fire; I haven't the faintest idea what is what... There is a teaching that says: Do not make men your strength, do not make the master your staff... To be deceived by such a man's divine possession means the Ananai-kyō sendenshi are completely useless. Come, come Fusakō, Yoshikō, let us abandon such a man right here and go all the way to the grotto of Tsukushi. If we go that far, there is no doubt the god of the grotto will let us hear accurate things. Come, let us go.'

Fusakō: 'If we are subjected to the fivefold rhyme Kototamazeme by the god of the grotto like Kojimawake of old, we will not be able to endure it. Since we are fellows with scarred feet, it is wiser not to draw near to such a dangerous regional area.'

Kurohime: 'Do you adopt a weak-spirited defeatist attitude again? Ananai-kyō is a progressivist doctrine. Retreat is absolutely out of the question. Didn't even the god of Kojimawake eventually become a splendid deity called Takehinowake no Mikoto? Since bad times are surely followed by good ones, come, let us go quickly.'

Yoshikō: 'Kurohime-sama, will you not take Magokō with you?'

Kurohime: 'We refuse no one who comes, we chase away no one who goes; shall we leave it to Magokō's free will?'

Yoshikō: 'Hey Magokō, why don't you come with us and follow behind Kurohime-sama? No matter how long you keep doing mock-Kototama like a, o, u in a place like this, it is thoroughly full of derailing, so even the august Kurohime-sama has gotten utterly sick of it, and nobody will listen to you. Come, go with me.'

While saying so, he grabbed Magokō's left and right hands firmly and tried to pull him up. Magokō, like a rock born from the earth, no matter how much he was shaken or pulled, did not budge an inch, and with just a single word,

Magokō: 'Leave it to my free will.'

saying only that, he closed his eyes and sat in silence. Without paying any attention, Kurohime chants a turbid Kototama sendenka while briskly climbing up the valley road where the wind blows favorably.

Fusa and Yoshi, while feeling their hearts drawn toward Magokō, look back over and over again and reluctantly follow behind Kurohime.

At last, at twilight that day, Kurohime reached the old sacred site of Takehinowake at the grotto of Tsukushi.

Kurohime: 'Now, this is the auspicious place where the famous Kojimawake no Mikoto had his fat extracted by the divine spirit of the god Tsukerahiko and achieved success. Everyone, together let us offer the Amatsu Norito.'

Fusakō: 'Shall we pray first for the safety of Takayama-hiko-sama, second for the repentance of Kurohime-sama, and third for the success of Magokō-sama?'

Kurohime: 'Ah, again and again, do not keep saying Takayama-sama, Takayama-sama. Takayama-sama is my husband. When his name is called by you fellows, it doesn't feel very pleasant, you see.'

Yoshikō: 'Since he is the second husband, you probably feel as though it wouldn't diminish you even if his name is spoken.'

Kurohime: 'As of today, you must not say a word about Takayama-hiko-sama. More than that, speak of the gods first. If your mindset is bad, you will meet the same fate as Magokō right here, ending up as monsters whose shins and loins cannot stand and whose mouths alone are fluent. Everyone who has opposed Kurohime has without exception been just like that. Come, come everyone, finish your Norito and sleep quietly here tonight. From now on I must make inquiries of the god. If you fellows stay awake, evil spirits will cross-talk and clear divine messages cannot be received, you see.'

Fusakō: 'Kurohime-sama, unbefitting a sendenshi, you are truly a cold-hearted person. When crossing the Pacific Ocean, you needed the three of us, so no matter what was said you quietly humored us, but the moment we arrived at this island, thinking Takayama-hiko-sama is here, your imposing manner suddenly became fierce, and it has become apparent that you treat us as nuisances, hasn't it?'

Yoshikō: 'Water pining for love, in Akita it becomes rejected water... that's it. It seems cold water is no longer needed now that the autumn wind has begun to blow. They call it the cold water of the aged, and bit by bit this old hag herself has almost turned into cold water. That is why they say it is useless to depend upon human beings. Fusakō and Yoshikō, both of us are fellows unworthy of friendship, leaving behind my danger and heading off to a distant, foreign land... he is probably cursing us bitterly, I imagine. Come, calm your spirits leisurely, let us pass the night right here until tomorrow morning, and only after our footing has become clear shall we go forth as messengers of consolation, shall we not?'

Kurohime: 'Hey, hey, both of you, what are you muttering about in the darkness? Why won't you go to sleep quickly?'

Fusakō: 'For some reason, the golden jewel and the Mani treasure bead flicker before my heavenly eye, and the state in which Takahime-sama is embracing and kissing Airohime-sama reflects upon the fundus of my eyes in a panoramic style, so my mind is agitated and I cannot sleep at all. Yoshikō: 'Now, Takahime-sama, are you not also going a bit too far? To make a rump of sixty years and take as your wife an Airohime or whatever her name is who has not even reached thirty yet—does this not show a slight difference in your judgment? Taking a wife whose age differs even more than Ohan and Chōzaemon, what honor is there in that for you? Fusakō, putting on a feigned voice, spoke:

'Aiya Kurohime, though I perceive your heart, they say a sparrow never forgets its hen even at a hundred, and compared to a white-haired, wrinkled old hag with one foot in the grave, by the fragrance of Airohime in the very prime of her bloom and dripping with water, this Takahime's mind is completely overthrown, and how could I ever possibly unite with a shit-hag whose stench resembles that of a dung-fly compared to the scent of musk? Rather than harbor unexpected hopes, it would be best for you to abandon them and return to your home province. A warrior has no second word. Take away that hand... and starting up abruptly, he stepped right into a room. Chiyachiya, chiyanchiyanchiyanjiya.'

Yoshikō: 'That is unacceptable, Takahime-san, though I suppose your words may not be unreasonable, from that very day we first met—with your long head like Jūrōjin, shining brightly like a pumpkin, wrinkled since your youth, and that facial appearance looking as though eyes and a nose had been stuck onto a testicle—hoping only that if I were to match with such a man, the color of your face would never change throughout the years, going against the doctrines of the Urunai Teachings, I took you as my husband, and surely you cannot have forgotten that. When I think of it, when I think of it, it is so regrettable, so vexing... and clinging closely to him, with tear-laden words of entreaty, ohohoho!'
