無花果〔948〕

§ 1

Fusakō climbed the slope and began to sing once more.

When I think of days of yore, long ago / Hinodewake and Hafurihime / Tsuranagihiko having passed / Along the mountain road of Tsukushi-ga-dake / Thanks to Kurohime-sama / How delightful to climb step by step / Fusakō-sama isheave-ho / Bearing the guise of Hinodewake / Yoshikō-sama isheave-ho / Playing the role of Tsuranagihiko / For the sake of Kurohime-sama / Making me climb such mountain slopes / Truly and sincerely, it is as though / I have stripped the skin right off my face / Kurohime-sama is Hafurihime / Even if I think of her as an emissary / Of the divine Mikoto, heave-ho / It hardly befitting for a wrinkled old hag / If she were a young maiden / Like a princess of celebration, heave-ho,

Even if a little grumbling is spoken / It is not so very painful / Yet for some reason unknown, anger wells up / Kurohime-sama, heave-ho / The wind blowing from that direction is also hateful / Reluctantly and against my will, I have followed along / Hah-hah, hū-hū, hū-hū, sū-sū, sū-sū / What sort of karma is mine / To be climbing such mountain slopes / If my Otetsu at home were to see this / Heave-ho, heave-ho, she would surely regret it / So surely would she lament / That even Tendau-sama, heave-ho / Clinging on and declaring it cannot be heard / Heave-ho, heave-ho, she would surely weep / Thinking and thinking, it is so pitiful / Growing anxious over Otetsu's state / My feet scarcely move forward / Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara / May the divine Mitama grant blessing,

That Otetsu's body remains free from ill / That the children grow sturdy and straight / Until Fusakō safely achieves his triumphant return / Please, I beseech you, protect them / Hah-hah, sū-sū, sweat pours out / The fresh spring water I drank a moment ago / Has risen all the way to the top of my head / My kettle-head has swelled up / Sweat flows and my eyes smart / Heave-ho, heave-ho, my kneecaps / Are laughing at me with a creak-creak / What is so funny that you laugh / That Kurohime-sama with single-minded devotion / Is climbing the Takayama Pass / Heave-ho, is that funny to you / On the soles of my feet, heave-ho / When I think they hurt, heave-ho / It seems a massive blister has formed / No matter how good it is to be diligent,

Blisters on my feet are utterly out of the question / Could it be that Kurohime-sama and a badger / Have performed protective rites and brought me to this pass / Hey now, Hey now, Yoshikō, pull yourself together / What has happened to your feet / No matter what, I cannot walk / If there were a single joy awaiting ahead / In the heart of Kurohime-sama, heave-ho / Then even with aching feet, dragging them along / Would make pressing onward worthwhile / But with tears that have no destination / Wringing out sweat and fat / How ever can I endure this / Heave-ho, heave-ho, heave-ho / There is a jagged stone right there / Pray be careful, Kurohime-sama / If by any chance you should suffer an injury / Upon Mount Takayama, heave-ho,

I should have no face to meet anyone with / Take care while you are yet unhurt / And proceed gently on your way / That being so, where might Magonkō be / And how does he fare / A monkey's piss falls upon me / As I reach such a place, in leisurely fashion / A company of five appears before me / Catching a single glance of the three figures / They cry out that this is unbearable and flee / Wondering at this, when I gaze upon their tracks / A shining object has been left behind / When I instantly open it and peer inside / It is what Kurohime-sama morning and night / Searched everywhere for, heave-ho / Filled with a ball of gold / Not only that, but a great quantity / Of coins are jingle-jangling about / Even sweet-looking fruits whose very names are unknown,

Are laid out as if to say with every detail / Heave-ho, heave-ho, three honorable ones / Please partake and ascend / Seeing this, the three rejoice / Rushing ahead and seizing them with eagle claws / Gulping them down, how sweet they are / Ah, ah, it was a dream after all / To have seen such a dream while walking, heave-ho / Saliva wells up from my throat / Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara / Even if it be a dream, once more / Heave-ho, heave-ho, into such a plight / I beg you to plunge me, I entreat you / Feeling as though dreaming within a dream / That husband whose whereabouts remain unknown / Wondering if he resides in the capital of the Land of Fire / Kurohime cleared her throat and went along, heave-ho / Her calculations went utterly wide of the mark

If it were the person of a double, that indeed would wake one from a dream; could the dream seen just now be forming a warning for Kurohime's future? Come now, we cannot stay like this; what are you doing before Kurohime, making a difficult face like an owl that missed its night meal, and snapping at us without reason? The dream seen while walking somehow seems to have told about your future; truly, it is a bizarre dream. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! While calling it a dream of the floating world, without letting us see such a dream, grant Kurohime to Takayamahiko with mercy, dokkoishyo, and let them meet; this is my prayer.

Yoshikō too, it is just like that; you surely do not think of Kurohime like an enemy, do you? Making a regretful face and giving us leftover figs—such kindness, surely we have not forgotten it. This Fusakō too, even though he drank water ahead of Kurohime and said that the matter of being scolded was let flow into the water, still remembers it; untokodotsukoi, how aloof! We deeply and gratefully bow to Kurohime-sama's true heart. Untokodotsukoidotsukoishyo, hah hah hah hah hu su su su! Truly, it is a steep slope; clouds peak in the heavenly expanse, shining faintly yellow.

With the heat in the earth earthworms are jumping out, getting scorched by the sun, and becoming dried strips. Reckless earthworm insects, untokodotsukoidotsukoishyo, reckless untokoshyo. Not just earthworms, there are one or two here as well. Once again my stomach has emptied; looking carefully at the branches of the trees to see if any fruits are growing hereabouts, nothing edible is to be found. A troublesome thing has come to pass; our vital stomach is empty, how can this journey go on? Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! May the divine spirits bless us, and grant delicious fruits to our party of three.

We pray with our lives at stake; hah hah hu fuh, ah, how exhausting! Sweat seeps into both eyes, and the surroundings have become invisible. Nothing can be traded for greed or gain; let us take a short rest here. Yoshikō, shall you rest too? Unlike the case with Kurohime, the two of us have no joy in the future; how can we push ourselves any further?', saying this, he collapses and lies down sideways. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! May the divine spirits bless us.

While lying down heavily by the side of the road, Fusakō gazes at the clouds drifting in the sky, and with a resentful face intensely stares at Kurohime. Yoshikō too lies down beside him, and while twitching his feet cheerfully gazes at the drifting clouds, whispering something in a low voice.

Kurohime: 'Everyone, wouldn't it be better to rest after going just a little further? Didn't we just finish resting by the clear spring a moment ago? If we carry on like this, day will fall upon us deep in the mountains.'

Fusakō: 'Hmph, you have addressed us so politely as "everyone" only for this time. That trick won't work on us, you see. Since the oil in our engine has already run out, further operation is impossible for us. As for you, your heart's monkey and horse called "monkeyish mind and unruly horse" are setting their hopes on the capital of the Land of Fire, so you must be feeling impatient. If both of us are such a nuisance to you, please go on ahead, step forward and go first. Life is the seed of all things; if we throw away even our precious lives for the sake of your love, the calculation doesn't add up at all, so we will leisurely take our rest right here. Heh, it must be terribly inconvenient for you, but please resign yourself to the way things are and pardon us. No matter what you say, since you are in the physical body of the Otohime of Ryūgū, you are quite something. A weakling in legs like me cannot possibly go on the same way as you. When I press my back firmly against the great earth like this, somehow the feeling is wonderful... As the saying goes, "There is no comfort equal to lying down in this world; the fools and idiots who get up and work are..."; folks who work recklessly without fearing for their lives are probably only one or two people like me, ah-ha-ha-ha, u-tsu-fu-ha-ha!'

Kurohime: 'Hey, you two, if I speak politely, you complain that I am speaking politely; just how can I please you, Oimasama? Truly, you are a hopeless lot, aren't you!'

Yoshikō: 'Whether they are white things or black things, we simply cannot hold out. First of all, the hangar of our stomachs has become utterly empty, so there is no means left to be taken. If you would bestow upon us even that fig you are carrying in your bosom, if it were even half-ripe, our engines would run just a tiny bit, you know.'

Kurohime: 'Oh, what weak men you are. If that is so, I will give you this one, so split it in half and stow it away. If you do that, a tiny bit of horsepower will come forth, I suppose.'

Fusakō: 'Yes, since we are getting it from Baasan-sama anyway, it is only natural that horse-power—or rather, idiot-power—comes out. However, if we are given a half-ripe fig, there is a risk of getting diarrhea, so please give us two or three figs.'

Kurohime: 'Oh, what an impudent man you are. If that is the case, it cannot be helped; it is a treasured fig, but I shall give it to you. Since they say there is no fruit until we cross this mountain, I plucked and brought this heavy one. Because you two lack caution, you meet with such hardships. That is why Kamisama says, "Do not treat food shabbily, and preserve everything." The Kamisama of great mercy and great compassion always gives us warnings so that we will not utter cowardly groans and fall into trouble when times like this arrive, so you had better be careful from now on.'

Fusakō: 'Every single word is most reasonable. From now on, I shall ponder your words one by one so that I do not fall into such a plight again.'

Taking them out from her bosom, Kurohime tosses them one by one in front of the two men.

Catching them quickly in their hands, the two men say,

Fusakō and Yoshikō: 'One fig, two figs... oops, three figs, oops, four figs...'

while counting and receiving them.

Kurohime: 'My, what ill-mannered men you are, you look completely like four-legged vessels. At least when eating, sit up properly and receive them with dignity.'

Fusakō: 'Yes, I am humbled, thank you for the warning. ...Come, Yoshikō, let us receive the bounty of Kurohime-sama.'

Yoshikō: 'Thanks to Kurohime-sama, one fig—no, my very life has been saved.'

Kurohime: 'Now you understand how precious Kurohime is, don't you.'

Yoshikō: 'As expected of Kurohime-sama. Thanks to your advanced years, your mind is capable of noticing everything from head to toe, ohohoho!'

Shrugging their shoulders up and down, as if leaping upon it, they instantly crammed it into their mouths. Seeing this sight, Kurohime bursts into laughter,

Kurohime: 'Hohohohoho!'

and holding her stomach, she laughs.
