§ 1

Magokō: 'Opening all at once the path of the gods / the living gods who save this world / are Kuniharutachinōokami / and Kamususanōnōomikami / guarding the island of Tsukushi / Kunitamagami's Sumiyohime / the divine glory of the gods appears / and even the mud of Tokoyo clears completely / whether the morning sun shines or clouds over / whether the moon waxes or wanes / however fiercely Magatsuhi may rage / the power of sincerity saves the world / where are the party of Kurohime wandering now? / longing to hear, I came and looked / voices of people in the Katakata forest / an atmosphere far from ordinary / ah, Kannagara, Kamugami-kamugami / may the august spirits multiply and bless us / people here in this world'

Magokō: 'serving each other in sincerity / loving and being loved, endlessly / in the age of the gods created by the gods / enjoying the spring of Tokoyo / bathed in the rain of prosperity, light, and joy / grant it to us, I pray / ah, Kannagara, Kamugami-kamugami / may your august spirits multiply and bless us!'

Chanting this prophetic song while catching the sound of strange human voices, suspecting something out of the ordinary, it was Magokō who had come running up. Seeing the miserable scene of dozens of the Great Serpent Sankō's underlings gathered together, binding a man and a woman, beating, kicking, and trampling them, even sooner than he could register it, blessing the twilight, he stood still in the shade of a tree and shouted in a great voice,

Magokō: 'Hey, hey! This here is Takayamahiko-mikoto of the capital in the Land of Fire. You villains who have brought a young woman into this forest and committed outrages, now Takayamahiko will strike you down and destroy you by his divine power! What do you say? If you repent, save those two men and women, and leave this place, well and good; if you refuse to listen, by the miraculous and mysterious divine power, I shall make every single one of you set out on the journey to the Netherworld without a single survivor. What do you say? Let me hear your answer!'

At this time, from the top of the camphor tree, five or six voices shouted,

'Hey, whoever you think you are, Takayamahiko is nothing but a glaring lie, I suppose. Show your face!'

cried out in a roar. Along with this voice, the very instant Magokō looked up into the tree, from above they grabbed sand and hurled it straight at Magokō's both eyes. Having sand thrown into both eyes, Magokō cried out 'Ah!' and squatted down on the spot, rubbing his eyes. Taking advantage of that opening, Yosankō promptly pulled a rope tight around his neck and dragged him about two or three paces away. Magokō lost his wits at the utter suddenness of the attack, thrashing about with his hands and feet. Gathering together one after another, the numerous underlings tightly bound Magokō with their hands and arms tied behind his back.

The sun gradually sank below the horizon, and all around was enveloped in the pitch-black darkness of a plover's feather. Slipping away into the darkness, Ume managed to escape from the spot and hid her figure, no one knew where.

Sankō: 'Ha ha ha ha, what sort of nonsense is this foolishness, sticking your nose into needless interference? Talking about being Takayamahiko of the capital in the Land of Fire—what on earth are you spewing? Who do you think you are dealing with? If you call me Sankō of the Great Serpent, I am a chivalrous boss whose name echoes far and wide across the Land of Fire. Causing needless trouble, a moth flying into the fire, what a truly pitiful fellow you are... Oi, Yosankō, fold this fellow up and finish him!'

Yosank: 'Yes, if we tie him up like this, there is no fear of him running away, so shall we leisurely torture him to death? More than that, first about Lady Ai, let us have one more negotiation, and how would it be to make him follow the boss's terms?'

Sankō: 'Since it is a bit awkward for me to say such a thing directly, I leave it to you, who are good with words. Do it well. In exchange, I will give you any reward you like...'

Yosa: 'Hey, I understand well. No matter what anyone says, I will make them say yes. But since it has become this dark and I can hardly even see faces... Hey Kanshiu, light the lamp... Oyabun, please set your mind at rest.'

This Oai, battered and bruised though she may be, is the Niyōbau of Torakō-san... You are persistent in asking if it is yes or no; once no, always no... It is not like the words of that Dodoitsu, but this aversion has soaked to my very entrails, how could it ever be washed away? Rather than haggling over such a thing forever, kill me quickly. Yosa: 'Kanekō, have you come to your senses? Oh, splendid, splendid. If you are not steady, you will be possessed by Torakō's spirit again. Now, Oyabun-sama, shall we untie Kanekō's ropes?'

Sankō: 'Now, wait, wait, there is no need to be in such a hurry. Rather than that, make Oai conclude the final discussion quickly, for that is our primary objective...'

Kanekō: 'Hey Yosakō, you alone are unreliable. I will also lend you my support, so untie these ropes quickly.'

Sankō: 'Hey Yosa, until my order is given, you must never untie them. Since this fellow is two-faced no matter what, I cannot let my guard down even a little.'

Yosa: 'Hey Kanekō, it is just as you hear, just as Oyabun says, and I am the same way. It cannot be helped, so please endure it there for a while.'

Oume, who had been hiding in the darkness from the very beginning and watching this scene while trembling with fear, made sure there was no one around in any direction, and timidly approached the place. The firewood was still burning. Because of that, the mound where the three were buried was clearly visible. Oume tried with all her might to push aside the stones and rescue them, but they were heavy stones that four or five rough men had carried together; no matter how much she pushed or shoved, they would not budge an inch, and finally, her strength exhausted, she collapsed in tears right there.
