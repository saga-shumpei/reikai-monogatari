Chapter 20: 困客 [1809]

困客〔1809〕

§ 1

The Great God of Mizumitama, revering the Mikoto, from the sacred Reijiyō of Mount Ubusuna, to save and assist in the divine Misono of Tengoku, by Kotomukeyaha, the evil Mitama of Ōkuronushi who dwells in the lunar Mikuni, the Sendenshi party of four, led by Terukuniwake, while being blown by the fierce winds of Kajikayama, crossed over the forest of the shrine, the Yamaguchi, and the mysterious forest, running about here and there, preaching and transmitting the true Mioshihe of Kami to the Kunibitotachi, healing the sick, saving and assisting the poor, and now here, while performing a hundred Kamuwaza, they have saved the Kinan of the Turman country and arrived at this place.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, may the Mitama of Kami be blessed, and I humbly pray that Thou grant us to fulfill our Shimei in Matsubusa. Even if the Great Sun shines or clouds over, even if the Moon waxes or wanes, even if the earth should sink, even if the Akumitama should rage, once I have bathed in the power of the true Kami, what manner of evil should I fear? Advance, advance, now advance! We are the children of Kami, the palace of Kami; even if the evil Kami, such as tigers, wolves, lions, bears, demons, or great serpents, should rage and run wild, what is there to fear? Until the Sendenshi of the Yamato men, of Shikishima, reach the capital of victory, their brave hearts shall never falter.

Awakening the Yamato-gokoro, let us advance through the great plains. I shall re-proclaim the hells as Tengoku instantly through my Kototama. From the kings, nobles, and aristocrats above, to the Chandala below, I shall extend the hand of Kami's salvation, saving all in one lotus-born destiny, and manifest the divine power of the Mizu-no-Mitama; I humbly pray that Thou grant me to fulfill my Shimei, Kamunagara, before the Great Ōkami, I humbly and reverently pray. The plum blossoms of the three thousand worlds, the Mikuni of Kami that opens all at once, opening, scattering, and bearing fruit. The Great Sun God, the Moon God, and Mizumitama, who dwells as the administrator of the Arakane that protects the earth.

I shall never forget the deep grace of the Great God Kami-Susanō, nor shall I forget His noble merits. Advance, advance, now advance! Facing the fortress of the devil, drawing the sword of Shōju, to bring about Kotomukeyaha is within our plan. Ah, how brave, how brave! The Sendenshi, whose very name is noble, having received the Shimei of Kami in their bodies, have no enemies wherever they arrive. Even if the Brahmanism or Ural religion should turn their blades against us, with the light of the strict Kototama granted by the Great Ōkami, I shall illuminate the dark night, make the divine virtue shine in the lunar Mikuni, and I humbly pray that Thou grant me to fulfill my Shimei, which I must illuminate. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, may the spirit be blessed.

While singing thus, it was Terukuniwake, Terukō, and Umekōwake, these three, who had come as far as the rice field path near the village of Irie.

Terukō: 'Excuse me, Sensei, the sun is already setting, but shall we not take lodging in the village of Irie tonight, rest at our leisure, and go to the port of Suga by boat tomorrow?'

Terukuniwake: 'Indeed, you seem quite tired; let us first take a rest here. It should not be far to that village now.'

Umekōwake: 'Sensei, let us by all means stay in Irie village tonight. There is an inn with a fine view called Hamaya, so let us stay there and decide to arrive at the port of Suga tomorrow. At the port of Suga, there is a wealthy man who is a medicine merchant named Alice, and his son, Irku, and daughter, Princess Dariya, are enthusiastic believers of San-go-kyō. They are surely waiting for us, without a doubt.'

Terukuni: 'That is right, since Umekōwake-sama has stayed there once before, it should be familiar and comfortable.'

Teru: 'Looking far out at the scenery in all directions, the distant mountains are veiled in cobalt blue.'

Terukuni: 'The mountain shadows, as if blurred in light ink, must be the high mountains of the village of Suga.'

Ume: 'The vast plains where summer grasses grow thick, how joyful it is for one who advances. The day is already coming to an end, and with a grass pillow, I shall heal the fatigue of the journey at an inn.'

From the grass behind where the three were resting, a groaning sound of 'un, un' could be heard. Umekōwake, with sharp ears, heard this, and walking briskly, approached the groaning in the thicket to find a monk with an ugly, base face collapsed in a state of being half-dead. As soon as Umekōwake recited the heavenly number song, the collapsed man stood up, rising with a start.

梅『どうやら千草の高姫の相客は人間ぢやないらしいぞ。先生、之から私が正体を見届けて来ます。何卒暫く此処に待つて居て下さいや』

照国『人さまの居間へ飛び込んで調べると云ふ失礼な事はないぢやないか、そんな事せずとも自然に分つて来るよ』

斯く話す時しも二階の障子をサツと開けて離れ座敷を覗いたのは千草の高姫であつた、梅公の顔と高姫の顔はピツタリと合つた。千草の高姫は梅公の顔をパツと見るより、恋しいやら怖ろしいやら、顔を真蒼にしてピリピリと慄ふた。妖幻坊の杢助は高姫の様子の只ならぬに不審を起し、

『オイ、高チヤン、お前は様子が変ぢやないか、何をオヂオヂして居るのだ』

千『杢助さま、あれ御覧なさいませ、三五教の照国別、照公、梅公別の三宣伝使が離棟の居間に泊つて居ます。そして玄真坊が横にゐますのを見れば、三万両の金を取り返す為に、息をふきかへして来たものと思はれます』

杢『やアそりや大変だ、三五教の奴と聞けば俺もチツト虫が好かない、何とかしてお前と二人、此所を逃げ出さうぢやないか』

千『杢助さま、貴方も気の弱い事を仰有いますな、斎苑の館で彼奴等を家来扱をして居つたぢやありませぬか。一つ貴方の大きな声で、呶鳴つて下されば照国別等と云ふへぼ宣伝使は、一たまりもなく逃げ出すぢやありませぬか』

杢『ウン、それもさうだが、今荒立てては事が面倒になる。俺にも一つの考へがあるからのう』

千『智謀絶倫と聞えた貴方の事ですから、滅多に如才はありますまい。それで何も彼も貴方にお任せ致して置きます』

杢『ウン、今夜の処置は俺に任しておけ。俺に計略があるから、さア千草の高姫、此方へおじや』

と云ひ乍ら二階の段梯子をトントントンと下り、表へ出て番頭に小判を一枚握らせ、

杢『一寸月を賞して半時ばかり経てば帰つて来るから、表戸開けて置いてくれよ』

と、巧く誤魔化し高姫と共に浜辺に駆け出し、一艘の舟を盗んで一生懸命にハルの湖の波を分けてスガの港へ向け漕いで行く。

照国別の一行は一夜を此所に明かし、あくる日の朝早くより一艘の船を誂へ、之亦スガの港をさして進み行く事となつた。あゝ惟神霊幸倍坐世。
