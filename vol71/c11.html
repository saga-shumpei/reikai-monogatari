Chapter 11: 異志仏 [1800]

異志仏〔1800〕

§ 1

Genshinbau, having been separated from the two, Koburai and Kōro, in his confusion and fear of the pursuers, ran westward under the moonlight without any destination; yet, his body was exhausted almost to the point of collapse from hunger, and his steps were far from steady.

As he walked with uncertain feet, looking up at the sky in desperation and searching for smoke somewhere in the distance, hoping to find a farmhouse and get some bread, a voice calling out, 'Oi, ōi,' was heard from the forest nearby.

Genshinbau thought, 'How strange; there should be no one in a place like this to call out and stop me. Judging by the situation, it must be those pursuers from last night who have come this far and gotten ahead of us. I cannot afford to be careless; the best of the thirty-six stratagems is to flee,' and he spurred on his tired legs, running with all his might through the beautiful field where flowers grew in abundance.

From behind, the two pursuers came chasing after him, shouting at the top of their lungs while brandishing their jitsute: 'Oi, ōi!'

Coming to a sudden stop before a steep mountain that blocked his path, and realizing he could no longer escape, he spotted a roadside wayside shrine and crawled inside to hide for a while, only to find a life-sized stone Buddha standing there.

Genshinbau immediately gathered all his strength and pushed hard against the neck area, and the stone Buddha fell over without any difficulty.

Genshinbau stood gracefully upon the pedestal where the stone Buddha had been, exposing his awkward Rakan-like face, raised his left hand, lowered his right hand to the level of his knee, and attempted to disguise himself as the stone Buddha to evade the eyes of his pursuers; such was his quick wit.

The two pursuers, who had finally arrived, spotted the wayside shrine and said:

Kō: 'Oi, where did that thief go? It seems he must have sunk into the grass around here, for he has vanished without a trace. Searching for a fellow like that is like trying to grasp at clouds; he might even be a sorcerer. Anyway, since there is this wayside shrine, why don't we order our legs to take a rest? My stomach has become quite empty, and even if we catch him after such life-risking activity, we will only receive a pittance as a reward. Once we spend it, that will be the end of it.' Otsu: 'That is true. Even though you and I have become respectable men and are serving as pursuers, if you look at our origins, we are decent human beings, are we not? If we dawdle, we will go hungry; it is a thankless role as a pursuer. Well, since this spring day is long, catching one thief will not do much for the world, and one's own body is what matters most. Ah, let us take a smoke break at this wayside shrine.' Kō: 'There is a strange stone Buddha standing here. I do not know who the stonemason was, but it looks just like a living Buddha. Let us have a smoke.' As they said this, they stingily struck their flint, filled the pipe bowl with tobacco, lit it, and the two began to puff away. Every time they took a puff, they tapped the ashes onto the instep of the stone Buddha, and then refilled it to smoke again. Genshinbau, unable to bear the heat, rolled his white eyes from the center of his black face and even shed tears. Just as the two looked up, the eyes of the stone Buddha rolled around, and since he was even shedding tears, they cried out, 'Ya, this fellow is a monster!' and in their surprise, they fell backward, saying, 'Ah, Rakan-sama, please forgive us. We have been terribly rude. We cannot stand up at all. Please say you will forgive us just this once. If you do, we will be able to return to the homes of our beloved wives. In exchange, we will never serve as pursuers again, even unto our grandchildren's generation,' and they pressed their hands together, begging with all their might. Genshinbau thought to himself, 'Ha, what foolish fellows. They seem to think I am the real thing. If they have lost the use of their legs, then I am safe. Shall I slowly return to my true form?' and he jumped down from the pedestal.

Genshinbau: 'Hey, you Kotsupayakunin, you must be surprised by my Shinpenfushigi and my Mariyoku, right? I am the mastermind of thieves, Genshinbau-sama. This Ishibotoke here, I pushed it over just like this with one Koyubi, and I stood in its place. If your legs have given out, there is nothing you can do. You must have at least a Shōshō amount of money on you. Hand over all your Arigane to me... What, you say you have none? What is that you are hanging from your waist?'

Kō: 'Oh, this is just the leftovers of my Bentau, sir.'

Genshinbau: 'Good, I understand. I am hungry too. Even if it is your leftovers, I cannot trade it for my Inochi, so hand it over here.'

Saying this, he snatched it away by force, devoured the Bentau of one man, and then snatched the Bentau from the waist of the next man, eating it greedily until not a single Hitotsubu remained, and he even washed the Bentaubako from the Koguchi with his tongue.

Kō: 'Excuse me, Genshin-sama, you are a person with tremendous Shinriki, aren't you? We are Tautei no match for you. Even just looking at your face has made our legs give out like this.'

Genshinbau: 'Wahahaha, you were quite surprised by my Goshinriki, weren't you? What kind of fellow are you, anyway?'

Kō: 'I am not anyone of note or cleverness. However, whether my parents gave it to me or someone else did, I do not know, but I am called Tonbi. The other man is called Karasu.'

Genshinbau: 'Indeed, Tonbi and Karasu, this is interesting. Since my two servants got lost on the way, I shall make you two my servants. How about it? Will you repent and quit being pursuers?'

Tonbi: 'Yes, yes, we will quit. Being your henchmen is far better than being pursuers, I am sure. Hey Karasu, you think so too, right?'

Karasu: 'I agree with your opinion. Excuse me, Genshin-sama, I humbly ask for your favor.'

Genshinbau: 'Good, I understand. Then from now on, you will do as I say. You must obey my orders no matter what. Come, let us go.'

Tonbi: 'Excuse me, Genshin-sama, you say let us go, but our legs will not stand. Could you please use your Shinriki to fix them for us?'

Genshinbau: 'Eh, it cannot be helped, I will fix them. In return, once these legs are fixed, you will acknowledge my Shinriki, right?'

Tonbi: 'Yes, yes, it is not a matter of acknowledging it; ever since our legs gave out, we have been acknowledging your Shinriki and begging you to let us be your servants.'

Genshinbau: 'Hmm, indeed, that must be true.'

Saying this, he struck the area around both their waists repeatedly with his clenched fists. Because of the extreme pain, the two stood up before they knew it, ran away about one ken, and then collapsed again with a thud.

Genshinbau: 'Hahaha, you are legless cowards. No matter how many thousands of such people I take with me, you would only be a burden. When your legs are fixed again, follow me. I will surely make you my servants. I am busy with the work of governing the world, so I will excuse myself. Still, what pathetic creatures you are.'

He jerked his chin three or four times and went up the slope, humming a tune in high spirits.

From this time on, Genshinbau spent over a hundred days sleeping in the mountains and fields by day and stealing by night. Thinking of Dariya-hime again, he could not bear it and wanted to possess her by some sweet means. Since his pockets had become a bit warmer, he disguised himself and snuck into the city of Tarahan, and checked into the Maruta Hotel, which was known as the first-class inn in Tarahan. Genshinbau settled into a two-room suite in the back and pondered various ways to overthrow the Tarahan castle. Then, a maid came out to serve tea and said,

'Excuse me, guest, my master asked me to ask for your name, so please write it in this guest book.'

Gen: 'How strange, the way a person's Shiyutsuse goes is beyond understanding. Hmm, is that so? That old man has returned to being the Samori again. Very well, hearing that makes me so irritated I cannot stand it. How ridiculous that the old man Shiyakanna should be the Tōriyō of the Itsukoku. But you two, you must have earned quite a Daibun, right?'

Kobu: 'We tried to earn, but with that fellow involved, it is only enough for the two of us to pay our Yadochin. However, we have thoroughly spotted the Arika of the money. It seems that without borrowing the Taishiyō's Chie, it will not be a match for our hands.'

Gen: 'Hmph, is that so? Then tonight, shall we try to snatch the treasure from somewhere?'

After that, the Sannin used the Yu, finished their Yufushiyoku, and once again entered a Hitoma to whisper and consult on the Shitajiyunbi for the execution of their Taimau.

The Sannin finally snuck into the Yashiki of the Samorizukasa, and after grabbing a large amount of money, they slipped out quietly from the back door in the guise of Fukumen and Zukin, and proceeded to sneak toward the mansion of the Samori, following the narrow paths behind the town. Just then, with the sound of a bell ringing 'chan, chan, chan, chan,' in the blink of an eye, flames scorched the heavens, and the Hirokōji, known as the main thoroughfare of Tarahan City, began to burn. The starry night, which had been so silent that the mountains, rivers, plants, and trees seemed to be dozing, suddenly seemed to wake up, and the surroundings became noisy; every house here and there rushed out carrying fire hooks in firefighting attire, and finding it unbearable to be in danger, the Sannin hid themselves under the eaves of a house and began to consult in whispers once again.

Gen: 'Hey, tonight might be no good. Since every house here and there has woken up at once and rushed out carrying fire hooks, the congestion on the streets is terrible. Even if we do not work on a night like this, is there not tomorrow night?'

Kobu: 'It is the perfect night for a thief's trade. Since everyone is distracted by the fire, we call it fire-thievery; we can jump in disguised as firefighters here and there. That kind of thievery is limited to times like this, is it not, Kōro?'

Kōro: 'Hmm, that is right. The one who currently has the most cash is the Samorizukasa. Usually, there would be thirty or forty guards at his house, but since it is such a big fire, they have all gone out to fight the fire, so the house is completely empty. Come, shall we go? Now, if we snatch ten million ryō of gold, and next we bribe the populace, it is the perfect time to plot the overthrow of Tarahan Castle. Genshin-sama, there is no better opportunity than this. I have thoroughly investigated the Yashiki of the Samori, so please let me guide you.'

Genshin: 'Indeed, I must follow your orders. Ah, if it is an order like this, I shall obey.'

Saying this, having no way of knowing as a mortal that disaster would befall him, he snuck in with the other two from the back gate of the Samori, taking advantage of the commotion of the fire.
