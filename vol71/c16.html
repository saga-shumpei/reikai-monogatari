Chapter 16: Phantom 魅返 [1805]

妖魅返〔1805〕

§ 1

About thirty ri away to the west of the Jiyōshi of Tarahan, there is a small village called Ihatakimura. In this place, there is a fairly wide, deep-blue pool called Uogafuchi, where a great number of fish gather throughout the four seasons. As it is the custom of the land of Indo not to kill living things wantonly, the fish merely multiplied day by day. There were Shiyuda who would occasionally go fishing at this Uogafuchi, which was said to be as full of fish as it was of water. While the Jiyōgiyō, Setsuteiri, and Bishiya would never fish or commit the act of killing, it was generally believed that since the Shiyuda were of low status and hardly treated as human beings, they would incur no blame from the Shinbutsu no matter how much they killed. However, even the Jiyōgiyō, Setsuteiri, and Bishiya did not feel that it was killing to purchase fish sold at shops and serve them at their meals, provided they were not eating living creatures.

As four or five Shiyuda were preparing to fish while sitting in the shade of trees along the riverbank where the summer trees grew thick and indulging in idle chatter, three corvus came drifting into the pool. The Shiyuda first cast their nets from the rocks toward these corvus, and when they finally managed to retrieve them to the roadside, they found that one appeared to be a mountain ascetic, while the other two had the faces of thieves from goodness knows where, so it was decided to call a priest from an old temple to hold a funeral. While they would leave the corpses of thieves to be prey for wolves and tigers without a second thought, they felt they could not simply abandon a mountain ascetic, so they consulted the passages of a scripture called Jurokai, which records fortune and misfortune, divination, and the like, to determine which of the five-element funerals should be performed. The divination indicated that this ascetic must be buried in the earth, so the villagers gathered together, buried the body in the ground just as it was, and instead of erecting a marker, left the top of the head exposed from the ears up. Among the five-element funerals, there are five methods: field burial, tree burial, cremation, earth burial, and water burial. Tree burial is a method of funeral where the corvus is hung upon a tree and exposed to the wind. Those who did not fall under the divination of this Jurokai were to be left as corpses on the roadside as people without the divine protection of the Lord God.

To such a place, a beautiful woman of about forty approached while singing a promotional song, and as she gazed at the two corpses abandoned on the roadside, Woman: 'Ah, I know not who you are or where you are from, but you poor souls. Oh, you must have been pulled up by someone after becoming drowned corpses; your clothes are still soaking wet. If left in a place like this, you will surely become food for dogs and crows. I must do something to save these people and make them my followers. May the Lord God of the Uranai faith protect and bless you.'

Saying this, she placed her white, slender hands, which looked like tortoiseshell work, upon the foreheads of the two and began to pray with all her might. However, no matter how much she prayed to the Lord God of the Uranai faith, there was no effect, so this time, as a trial, she changed the divine name to the Lord God of the San-Go faith and prayed with single-minded devotion. Gradually, warmth returned to the bodies of the two, and after about half an hour, they finally breathed again, rose up with a start, thanked the Lord God for the great grace of saving their lives, and expressed their gratitude with tears. This woman is Chigusa no Takahime, who escaped from Turman Castle. Since fleeing the castle, Chigusa had chosen to travel through mountains and fields where there were few people until she reached this place, and having revived two dead people for the first time, she reached the pinnacle of her triumph, Takahime: 'Now, now, I know not what kind of thieves you are, but had this Chigusa no Takahime not passed this way, your precious lives would have already gone to the place called the ten million billion lands, and you would never have been able to return to this world again. Tell me, what are your names? I must hear them, for otherwise, I cannot offer my thanks to the great Miroku-sama of the living palace of the Hinodewake.'

Having boasted grandly before the two, the Takahime of Chigusa felt she must revive this fellow no matter what, and became even more desperate. Suddenly, warmth returned to his forehead, and his blue-black face took on a bright red hue. Gen: 'Hey, Niyōbau, is there such a thing as running away from home without even sharing a pillow for a single night? At least wait for this one night.'

She struggles, trying to shake him off and escape.
