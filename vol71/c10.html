Chapter 10: 荒添 [1799]

荒添〔1799〕

§ 1

Genshinbau, Koburai, and Kōro are running with all their might along a muddy path where Mizugusa grows thick across the entire plain, their feet sinking into the mire as they struggle through their walk, fearing the danger of their pursuers. On both sides of the path, shallow water has collected here and there, reflecting the moon and stars in silence. After traveling about three chō, they find an old well by the roadside, and there is an air as if someone has fallen into it. Koburai, though fearful, relies on the moonlight to peer into the old well, and sees someone who looks very much like the beauty he saw earlier at Kanko's house, struggling to climb up with all her might, grabbing the Mizugusa at the edge of the well only to fall back in, again and again. The three, though thieves by trade, cannot overlook the danger to a human life, and Koburai unties his obi and lowers it into the old well. Just as one who is drowning might even grasp the tail of a poisonous snake, Linjian, unaware that they are a group of thieves who would do her harm, clings firmly to the obi. Koburai, Kōro, and Genshinbau, dripping with sweat, finally manage to pull her up, and when they look closely, they see it is the very beauty Genshinbau had set his heart on digging up. The beauty is almost in a state of delirium, not even knowing who it was that saved her. Genshinbau, while signaling to the other two, takes the woman, whose clothes are soaked like a drowned rat, and as she is, they squeeze her clothes here and there to wring out the drops, then carry her between them, hurrying westward along the difficult path where they sink to their shins. After traveling nearly one and a half ri, a forest, looking as if it were floating in the moonlit night, lies ahead about a few chō away. The three, thinking they should at least go to that forest to rest, pant as they walk, dripping with sweat. When they finally approach the front of the forest, an old, dilapidated hall stands there in a lonely manner.

Koburai: 'Ah, that was quite a back-breaking effort. Say, Genshin-sama, shall we spend the night in this forest? And then, by nursing this beauty with sincere, polite, and kind care, and using every trick of human sentiment and duty to make her say yes, why don't we make her a temporary comfort for you? No, not just for you, but since we were the ones who picked her up and carried her, shall we keep her as common property? She is a woman you fell for, after all, so she is not something to be discarded. Even this Koburai finds his appetite slightly whetted, you know.'

Genshinbau: 'Whatever the case, whatever the circumstances, we must first bring this woman to her senses; if we leave her like this, won't things go wrong?'

Koburai: 'No, there is no need for such worry. She has swallowed a little water, but her life is not in danger. We must settle the fight that will happen later right now—whether to make her common property or to make her Koburai's exclusive property—for it would be unsightly to have this beauty hear such a discussion once she regains consciousness. Genshin-sama, will you give her to me as my exclusive property... Well, how about it? You seem to want to make her your own, but it won't go so easily, you know.'

Genshinbau: 'No matter what you say, I am the one who first fell in love with her. My spirit is truly possessing this woman. Furthermore, a bond has already been formed with this woman through a carnal relationship. In a sense, I am a relative, and you are a total stranger, are you not? Such things are settled even without saying them; she is definitely Genshin-sama's.'

Koburai: 'Whether you are a relative or have a carnal relationship, I know nothing of that, but if this Koburai had not found and saved her, this woman would already be dead. If so, the parent of her life is this Koburai-sama; even if I concede a bit, I shall use her for two nights, and Genshin-sama, you use her for one night. I think that is only fair, even from the standpoint of rights.'

And I, Kōro, who hold the power of command over you both, will absolutely not give her to you two. I intend to make her my wife at the Kōro residence and have her as my companion on the long journey ahead. He-he-he-he!'

While the three were wasting their time with this exchange, Rinjan recovered her energy and, finding it all too ridiculous, burst out laughing, 'Fu-fu-fu-fu!'

Kōro: 'Oh, Princess-sama, have you regained consciousness? Oh, how auspicious, how auspicious! What on earth is the matter with you? You were floundering like a crucian carp drunk on mud after falling into an old well, but perhaps it is because you were connected by the thread of fate. It is because a deep bond has been formed that your figure appeared before my eyes after I followed Genshin and Kōro. So, thinking that your life was already at an end, please entrust your whole life to this Koburai. After all, it is Koburai who is your savior, is it not?'

Rinjan: 'Ah, was that so? I thought I would be happy to have my life saved, but how filthy! To have my life saved by a petty thief is a disgrace to my ancestors. Ah, what a regrettable thing I have done. Here, thief-sama, please lend me that sword. If I have been saved by the likes of you, I cannot face my brother. I shall commit suicide here and now before your eyes.'

Kōro: 'Now, now, young lady, that is a bad attitude. Life is the material for all things, is it not? It is precisely because humans are alive in this world that there are flowers, fruits, and joy. Do you think flowers and fruits will bloom if you are dead? The yakuza and laborers who are obsessed with traditional religions might be superstitious enough to believe there is a future, but for modern people who have awakened to science, such things as a future do not hold water. Furthermore, to throw away a precious, irreplaceable life, bound by unnecessary theories of honor—such as bringing shame to one's ancestors, staining one's brother's name, or having been saved by a thief—is far too outdated. In any case, a person should just be happy for themselves. How about it? Why not reconsider and become my wife? I am not a thief by birth, nor am I a bad person. I am merely practicing the trade of a thief for my daily living.'

Rinjan: 'No matter what you say, I can never entrust myself to a man who bears the name of a thief.'

Kōro: 'Well, well, what a stubborn woman you are. Try to think about it carefully. Look at the state of the world today. From the high officials of the Left Guard down to the lowest petty bureaucrats, is there anyone who does not steal, changing their methods and tricks? Those who bite into gravel, those who eat up revenue stamps, those who sell Buddha to make a living, those who bite the feet of the gods—from top to bottom, from corner to corner, it is a world of thieves. If you say you will not take a man because you hate thieves, and you keep on with such rigid talk, you will never encounter a man who is perfectly pure and innocent for the rest of your life. You would do well to think that over carefully.'

Kōro: 'As long as you Shinmeu-ly Jiyunpō my Meirei, I shall never Hengahe. Now, from here on is my Hitoributai. Now then, Rinjiyan-sama, regarding the matter of becoming my wife, you surely have no Goizon, do you?'

'Ho, ho, ho, ho, Kōro-sama or whatever you are called, how self-serving. I dislike a person who holds another’s Kintama and asserts his own Kenri without letting them say Iyaō. A person who is truly Kamunagarateki, possessing Keni and Tokubau, is respected by the world without resorting to such Kenbōjiyutsusū. To be looked down upon as such a cheap woman is a nuisance to me, Rinjiyan. Isn't that right, Mr. Thief?'

Just then, dozens of footsteps, brandishing lanterns and torches, came rushing forward, trampling through the marshy wilderness with a crunching sound. Genshinbō, Koburai, and Kōro, not knowing what to do in their panic and confusion, hid in the thickets of grass and vanished, no one knew where.
