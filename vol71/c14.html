Chapter 14: 障路 [1803]

障路〔1803〕

§ 1

Genshinbau, Koburai, and Kōro, the three of them, thanks to the mercy of Samori, barely escaped the death penalty. They stuffed all the gold they possessed into their waistbands, and while shaking their heavy hips, they searched through the forest where human footprints were rare, and began to run westward, ever westward, for a day and a night. The three of them were exhausted, their bodies as limp as ropes, and they could no longer walk even a single step.

Koburai: 'Listen, Genshin-sama, no matter how much gold we receive, it does not make our bodies healthy, nor does it fill our stomachs. Now that it has come to this, gold and everything else are of no use. If we continue this for three days with nothing but this heavy burden, we will surely lose our lives.'

Genshinbau: 'Do not talk like a fool. If you have money, you can eat any sweet thing you desire, and you can buy any beauty you wish. Since this is an age where gold is almighty, even if you want to become a Chiyakutan-giin, you need fifty or a hundred thousand in gold. Even if you think of becoming the Prime Minister of a Tanmeinaikaku, you need twenty or thirty million ryō of gold.'

Koburai: 'That may be so, but wandering through these mountains and forests, we cannot find any beauties, and whether we want to eat sweet things or tasteless things, there are no shops at all, are there? I think a measure of rice is more precious than a bag of ten million yen. Ah, I wish I could somehow get my hands on some food.'

Genshinbau: 'Do not whine like that. If we run for just one more day, there is a safe zone. If we go there, there are women, and they sell all kinds of beautiful clothes. A hundred kinds of food and drink are also waiting. Well, you should be patient until then; if you are so hungry that you cannot help it, just lick your own thumbnails. If you do that, you might be able to endure the hunger a little. Look at that octopus; when an octopus has nothing to eat, it eats all of its own legs.'

Koburai: 'It is unbearable to be compared to an octopus, you know. You are the one who is an octopus-priest, so you should lick your own legs. Koburai is a full-fledged human being, even if I am thin and withered. I cannot imitate an octopus. Hey, Kōro, you cannot walk even one more step, can you?'

Kōro: 'I am also suffering and cannot bear it, but when I think that I can use this money to buy sweet things and sleep while holding a beauty, my energy returns, and I am able to walk a little more. No matter what anyone says, humans depend on their hearts. It is only for a little while longer, so why don't we be patient and follow Genshinbau-sama as he says? It is no use dying in a field in a place like this.'

Koburai: 'Well, it cannot be helped. I suppose I will have to trouble my compass again.'

As they advanced reluctantly for only a block or two, more than ten pursuers with ten fingers and toes appeared from within the tall grass that was enough to bury a person, and surrounded the three of them. To their right was a valley a thousand fathoms deep, and on the three other sides, they were surrounded by pursuers. With no way to advance or retreat and believing this was the end, the three of them threw themselves toward the blue abyss all at once, hoping to survive by a miracle, and threw away their lives cheaply. The pursuers watched them, saying 'Oh, oh,' but they could not approach the famous sheer cliffs, and they went back while grumbling and leaving their enemies to their fate.

In the middle of a vast and boundless wilderness, a single old man is walking along, singing a song in a voice as faint as the buzzing of a mosquito.

'The flow of the river and the life of a man, How mysterious is the path that lies ahead. Serving at Tarahan Castle, I am a retainer of Samori. I know not when it happened, In this vast field with no known limits, I wander in bewilderment. With no one else traveling the road, I have no one to ask for the way. Ah, what shall I do, leaving behind My thousand-year grudge upon the field, Must I meet my end so helplessly? Ah, how miserable, how miserable. In which part of the sky Does the direction of Tarahan Castle lie? I have wandered in a dream for a hundred leagues, How sad is my own fate. Though a thousand grasses grow in the wilderness, It is a withered field with neither flowers nor fruit. Even the wind that blows has no sound.'

Not even the voice of a crow can be heard. Whether it be the Jiyakukuōjiyōdo or not, the scene of this place, as if the Tenchi had fallen asleep all at once, has nothing to compare to its loneliness. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. O Ōmikami of the Ananai-kyō, lead us to the Sumika determined for eternity, to the Samorike built in the Jiyōka of Tarahan. When I think of it, when I think of it, the Yukusue of a human life is indeed Haka. The Kamunahohi who created this world, and the Ōnahohi of vast heart; in the world of men, whatever happens, one must look back, listen back, and correct back with Nahohi, and the Mioshihe of the Ananai-kyō, as taught by the master Umekōwake, I have heard in Umara and Tsubara, yet—

Without the means to look back, in this Arenohara where even the names are unknown, for what sins have Warera fallen to such a place? It is a world that defies Gaten. Have mercy, Ōmikami. Lead us to our home. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. May the Mitama be blessed. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, Tatohe the earth may sink, the power of truth saves the world. When God appears on the surface and distinguishes between good and evil, the Kamunahohi who created this world, and the Ōnahohi of vast heart; in the world of men, whatever happens, it is said to be the path of looking back with Nahohi. The thing called the power of truth—

It must be the power of the true God who created this world. Placed in the human realm, how could the truth emerge? O God of the power of truth, I pray to you earnestly, please save our lonely circumstances. The plum blossoms of the three thousand worlds, the land of God that opens all at once; know the grace of the sun, moon, and earth as they open, scatter, and bear fruit. The living God who saves this world, the gods gathering in Takamagahara—though I engrave such noble propaganda songs upon my heart and do not forget, this field where not even a single plum blossom has opened is the eighth street of the path to hell, surely a continuation of the Yatsumata highway.

I, who wander into such a lonely wilderness, must be polishing my Mitama while receiving the punishment of God, having piled up infinite sins in both the previous life and the present world. Not having the sense of having died, I cannot even think of this as the Yūmeikai. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. If God is in this world, I pray to you earnestly, please guide my body and return me to my beloved home.

While singing thus, as he followed the narrow path that clung to the great wilderness like a crawling earthworm, something was moving in the soil, its head rising up. Shiyakanna could not understand why a head like a truffle was moving in the center of this lonely wilderness, so when he poked it two or three times with the staff he held in his hand, saying 'Ouch, ouch,' it emerged from the soil bit by bit, like the moon rising over the edge of a mountain, until it had revealed itself up to its shoulders. When he looked closely, it was the exact figure of Genshinbō. Shiyakanna was surprised and said:

Shiyakanna: 'Hey, you, aren't you Genshinbō? What are you doing in a place like this?'

Genshinbō: 'Ah, I have something I must apologize to you for. I do not remember it clearly, but as divine punishment for going to your mansion, forcing unreasonable demands upon you, and returning after snatching up a great deal of money, the God who governs the Yūmeikai has bound a vast amount of gold to my body as a penalty. Because of its weight, my body has sunk into the earth, and now, as you see, I have only managed to reveal my head above ground. Please, say the word "forgive" just once. If you do that, my sins will likely become lighter...'

Shiyakanna: 'My legs feel wobbly, but my body is so light that I feel in danger of my feet leaving the ground, yet it is a strange thing that your body is heavy. In that case, how about I chop off your head and leave your torso in the soil? After all, it is a body that will enter the soil sooner or later anyway.'

Genshinbō: 'Hey, brother, don't say such unreasonable things. If you were a magician like Ten-ichi, you might be able to reattach a head even if you chopped it off, but mine won't work like that. Please, brother, can't you exert your diamond-like strength and pull my body up with a tug?'

Shiyakanna: 'Even if you tell me to pull your body up, your body is buried from the neck down, so there is nowhere to even get a grip, is there? Still, if you insist that I pull you up, I suppose I shall try grabbing both your ears and see if that works.'

Genshin: 'I do not care how you do it, but at least get my body out onto the ground somehow. It is so painful I cannot bear it; it feels as if I am about to be dragged down into the hell at the bottom of the earth.'

Saying 'Very well,' Shiyakanna tried with all his might to grab the cold ears with his cold hands, but they did not budge an inch, as if they were solid rock. While this was going on, two more men came trudging along powerlessly, wrapped in wet garments. Shiyakanna looked back and said,

Shiyakanna: 'Hey, are you two, Koburai and Kōro? Why have you come to such a desolate place?'

Koburai: 'Oh, is that you, Oyakata? First of all, congratulations on your good health. I do not remember clearly, but I recall that while I was on my way home after receiving money at your place, I encountered pursuers and jumped into a mountain stream; before I knew it, I had arrived at this place. However, when I jumped in, I threw away all the gold coins I had worked so hard to receive at the bottom of the valley, and now I do not even have a single coin. Could you please, Oyakata, spare me a little money?'

Shiyakanna: 'It is the same for me. I do not have a single coin on me. When traveling through a place like this, there are no houses and no shops, so why would I need money? If you are hungry, you have no choice but to pluck some grass and eat it as you go, do you not?'

Koburai: 'Oh, is that not Genshin-sama over there? What is this, you have only your head sticking out... Come now, get up, get up!'

Genshin began to rotate his eyes wildly and excessively like a gyrocompass, and he began to move his facial muscles frequently, concentrating his mouth, eyes, and nose all in one place.

Shiyakanna: 'Hey, this fellow seems to have fallen into hell. He still seems to have an attachment to gold. Hey, Genshin, give up on that money once and for all. If you do that, you might be saved.'

Genshin: 'Hey, no matter how much I want money, I cannot trade it for my life when I am suffering like this. I have had enough of money. I do not need a single coin. Hey, you gold, I am giving you your leave starting today, so go wherever you like.'

No sooner had he said this than he flew up about three ken from the ground with a pop, as if a child's toy monkey had been flicked by a bowstring, and fell back down to the same spot with a thud.

Shiyakanna: 'Hey, Genshin-bō, that thing called greed is a terrifying thing, is it not?'

Genshin: 'It truly is. I have had enough of money now. But you, you abandoned the magnificent mansion of Tarahan Castle; why have you come to a place like this again? It does not quite make sense to me... Ha-ha, you must have regretted losing my money and came chasing after me. That is why you were dragging me into the earth with some kind of mechanical device, perhaps using electricity, were you not?'

Shiyakanna: 'Do not talk nonsense. I am tired of even looking at money. However, I just remembered now that after I let you escape, I certainly intended to commit seppuku before the gods and die, so why have I come to a place like this and am still alive? It is as if I have been bewitched by a fox; I do not understand at all whether this is the manifest world or the spirit world. Where do you think this place is, anyway?'

Genshin: 'Well, it is truly mysterious and I cannot bear it. If I listen to your story, assuming you died before me, you should have arrived here first. I have a feeling that the three of us ran through mountains and valleys for a day and a night and jumped into a mountain stream. It is impossible that we should have arrived here first. It is truly something I cannot comprehend.'

Koburai: 'This must be it, Genshin-sama; there is no doubt that we are on a journey through the spirit world. We think we are alive like this, but our physical bodies have long since died, and only our spirit bodies have wandered here. It is said that in the spirit world there is no distinction between time and space, and there is no far or near, so I do not know if those who died earlier come later, or if those who died later come earlier. Well, let us just assume we are dead; it will be better not to be surprised later.'

§ 4

Koo: 'Hey, Koburai, it seems this is the eighth block of the Jigokukaidau. Has this not become a troublesome affair?'

Gen: 'Indeed, I am a bit taken aback, but since we have managed to find four companions to travel with, the loneliness has thinned out somewhat. In any case, I do not care if it is Jigoku or anywhere else; shall we not go as far as we can? For we would rather go to Jigoku and engage in the activity of the wheel than suffer from boredom in Gokuraku, would we not?'

Kobu: 'I heard that opinion of yours on the rocks of the Haru Yamatauge; come, let us go. Since it is quite lonely, shall we not sing a Kaushinka?'

Gen: 'That sounds amusing; I shall sing first.

The vast Ōnohara with no known limit,

This place is the eighth block of Jigoku.

Whether it is the Yachimatakaidau or not,

Leading three Kerai along,

In this Aranohara of unknown name,

How brave it is to march forward.

If there be a Tengoku in this world,

I shall try to go there.

If not, then it must be the Jigokudau,

Shall we advance with shoulders and elbows squared?

Yet, are there really such things as Jigoku,

Or Gokuraku in this world?

No matter how far one goes, it is the same,

A cold wind blows with a whistling sound,

While stroking the Hazue of the grass,

Passing through without any Enriyo.

This must surely be Jigoku.

No matter how frightening Jigoku may be,

If it is like this, it is as easy as a fart.

Dokkoi, dokkoi, dokkoishiyo.

Hey, hey, you three Kerai,

Follow me closely from behind.

I shall not be responsible if you fall behind.

Oh, how strange, oh, how strange,

A mysterious building in the distance,

Has begun to flicker before my eyes.

Is this, after all, the Genkai?

If it is the Genkai, then all the more,

I shall frolic with all my might,

And proceed with vigor.

I thought to occupy the Tarahan Castle,

And become a magnificent king,

To wield my influence,

But I know not when it happened,

That I wandered into such a place,

Where even the cardinal directions,

Are unknown in this strange day.

The building seen in the distance,

Must be the dwelling of an Oni or a demon.

Come, let us go, come, let us go.

Why are you trembling?

If there is a Jigoku,

I shall capture the Oni of Jigoku,

Line them up on bamboo skewers like locusts,

Roast them over a fire,

And eat them one by one.

Ah, how amusing, how amusing,

The departure of the King of Jigoku,

Let Oni or snakes come forth.

Dokkoi, dokkoi, dokkoishiyo.'

Singing with all his might in such a manner, he thrust his head forward and advanced with a trotting gait. The three were left behind by about four or five chō, following along while singing the Kaushinka in weak, trembling voices.

The moment Genshinbō, staring only at his feet, rushed forward, he struck his head against a standing stone at the crossroads, and with a cry of 'Kyaa, uun,' he collapsed on the spot, stretched out like a frog that had been slammed down.
