Chapter 9: 踏違ひ [1798]

踏違ひ〔1798〕

§ 1

At the southern foot of Haruyamatauge lies a small hamlet called Haruyamamura, consisting of some seven or eight households scattered here and there. In every house, the eaves are slanted and the walls have crumbled; even without chimneys, blackish-red smoke rises morning and evening through the holes in the Gobangata wall-lath where the plaster has fallen away.

In a single house occupying the most scenic position in the village, a man named Kanko lies on a tattered futon, breathing heavily with an unnamed illness, having nothing to do day after day. Around the time when the sun seems to have descended seven levels, Kanko's friend Kinsu arrives, and while sliding open the slanted door with a rattle, he speaks.

Kinsu: 'Hey, Aniki, eh, I heard rumors that lately you have not been feeling well, but what on earth has happened to you? I returned from Tarahan City yesterday, so I knew nothing of your condition and failed to visit you; I am truly sorry.'

Kanko (in a weak voice): 'Oh, you are my friend Kinsu, thank you for coming. Well, well, well, please boil some tea and drink it. And while you are at it, I would like you to let me have a cup of that delicious stuff too.'

Kinsu: 'Very well, if it is for you, I shall boil some tea. Living as a widower, you must be having trouble with your cooking; you had better find yourself a good wife soon. Living a bachelor's life may seem fine, but one never knows when a change might come to one's body. I am thinking of finding a woman, even if she is a beggar's child, and arranging it for you, but women these days, every one of them, have become so impudent; they are not just lying around here and there like wind blowing. According to my old man, in the old days, they said a woman had no home in the three worlds and truly could not hold her head up before a man; they were treated almost like slaves or toys, but women these days, those who are physically fit become factory girls and are employed by large companies. They are hired as telephone operators, chauffeurs, office clerks at the town hall, and even police officers; not only do they walk side by side with full-grown men, but even when they do the most pleasurable things as human beings, they take money from men, becoming high-class courtesans or streetwalkers. There are so many occupations for women that there is hardly any waste, so I too am continuing my bachelor life. When I think of that, men these days cannot hold their heads up to women at all. It is entirely a woman's world. I peeked into the Patriotic Women's Association the other day, and it was nothing but women.'

Kanko: 'Well, that may be so, but I have already become, somehow, blank.'

Kinsu: 'You say you have become blank, but where exactly have you become blank? Do you mean an illness has started?'

Kanko: 'Yeah, just, already, it is nothing, I am blank, and from the root, I do not feel well.'

Kinsu: 'Hmm, if so, it is an illness then.'

Kanko: 'Whether it is an illness or something else, I do not know, but I am sick.'

Kinsu: 'Are illness and sickness not the same thing?'

Kanko: 'That is the blank illness.'

Kinsu: 'How did this blankness begin?'

Kanko: 'The beginning of the blank illness is a healthy body.'

Kinsu: 'A healthy body is a given, is it not? I am asking for the cause of your blankness.'

Kanko: 'The cause, yeah, after all, it is lust; it started from lust and became blank.'

Kinsu: 'It started from lust and became blank? It is not blank at all from the root. Since it is between you and me, there is nothing to hide. I am prepared to do anything for your sake; are we not childhood friends? It is likely a mental anguish; tell me without hiding or concealing anything.'

When I asked her to cut a loincloth of one Ichijiyō, Injin-sama listened closely to my name and said, "Are you Kanko-sama of Haruyamamura? The money is unnecessary, so please, just take it home." Then, with hands as beautiful as Shirauo, she wrapped the loincloth Goteinei and tucked it into my bosom. After that, with a gentle, indescribable Metsuki, she said, "Please come again." The moment I saw her face, I felt a thrill and nearly lost my senses. I managed to return home, but I thought it would be Motsutai to use this loincloth for my own loins, and Motsutai to use it as a Tenuguhi, so I hung it in the Tokonoma and worshipped it Mainichi, Hinichi. Whenever I did so, the gentle figure of Injin-sama would appear hazily from within the loincloth, and each time, my body would feel that thrill. Then, when the Getsumatsu arrived, I would hear the sound of Komageta, "Karan-koron, karan-koron," and a gentle voice would say, "Is this the home of Kanko-sama? I am Injin of the Mitsumaruya in Tarahan City. Please open up for a moment." Thinking it might be a dream, reality, or an illusion, I opened the gate to see her, and she had brought Otokoshiu who looked like demons. She said in a gentle voice, "Ah, Kanko-sama, Maido Gohiiki," and gave me something written. It was not very long, about the length of a Mikuda, so this was Fushigi. Since I am not yet married, I thought it could not be a divorce letter, and wondered if it was a marriage proposal. The moment I thought that, I felt a thrill and collapsed right there. When I finally regained consciousness, Injin-sama was nowhere to be seen, and Yabui Chikuan-sama from the neighboring village was there, nursing me. Since then, my body has been wasting away Mainichi, Hinichi. Look, my body has become nothing but skin and bones, just like a bamboo craft covered in wet paper, reduced to nothing but sinews.'

Kinsuke: 'Hey, show me that document. I shall read it for you, since you are uneducated.'

Kamehiko: 'I cannot count how many times I thought of asking Chikuan-sama to read it, but I was too embarrassed, so I kept it hidden. Do not laugh, and please keep this a secret.'

He pulls the document from his bosom as if it were precious. Kinsuke takes the document quickly and reads aloud:

Memorandum

One, White cotton, one Ichijiyō,

Balance due: 48 sen, Mitsumaruya,

To Kanko-sama of Haruyamamura

Kinsuke: 'Ha! This is just a delusion over a loincloth. She came to collect 48 sen. It is neither a love letter nor a marriage proposal. Will you not wake up from your dream? What is this, acting lovesick? It is not so thrilling after all, is it?'

Kamehiko: 'A debt is a debt, and love is love. Even if Injin-sama is not in love with me, I am fully in love with her. Therefore, no matter what, I must be with Injin-sama, or else this thrilling illness will not be cured.'

Kinsu: 'Hey, you are a troublesome fellow. However, since your father once saved my life when I had none, I must help you. With that, the two siblings hurried to Kanko's dilapidated house, and when they arrived, they found Kanko lying flat on his back on a torn tatami mat. Seeing the critical state of his friend, Kinsu could not bear to hesitate, and with tears in his eyes, he turned to his sister.

Kinsu: 'Hey, sister Linjian, I heard from our mother that you are a woman born in the year of the Monkey, the month of the Monkey, the day of the Monkey, and at the hour of the Monkey. It is said that if I take your ikigimo and have Kanko drink it immediately, his biyōki will be cured. So, please, save your brother's face and give me your inochi.'

Linjian: 'Look, brother, is that not unreasonable? No matter what, there must be a way to cure the biyōki without taking my liver. Please, at least spare my inochi.'

Kinsu: 'No, I am a man. Once I have said I would help my friend's biyōki, I cannot turn back. I must take your liver and have Kanko drink it.'

Linjian: 'Look, brother, this is too much. Please, I beg you to forgive me.'

While they were talking, the three men, Genshinbō, Koburai, and Kōro, having drawn their three-foot blades of autumn water from somewhere, kicked down the flimsy door and burst in. Seeing this, Linjian was startled and cried out.

Linjian: 'Brother, please help me! Oh, I am terrified, my liver is crushed!'

Kinsu: 'Hey, you spineless creature! If your liver is crushed, I have no more use for you. Hey, I do not know what kind of thieves you are, but you come at an unnecessary time... Koburai: 'Entering a house with drawn swords is our trade. If you keep spouting nonsense, your head will fly off. We do not think there is money in a house as small as this, but cook some rice and serve it. These thieves are different from petty thieves; we never bully the poor. Cook about five or six shō of rice, and we will pay for it.'

Koburai: 'Hmph, do not underestimate me so much. I am the future Samorinokamisama of Tarahan Castle.'

While saying this, he and Kōro measured out about two shō of hulled millet with a bucket, set up a pot, and began to prepare the evening meal. Kanko was still lying there, breathing faintly. Genshinbau gazed intently at the beautiful face of Rinjan, drooling, narrowing his eyes, and being led by his Shineniba, he began to suffer from his chronic ailment once again. Kanko, having gradually recovered due to the attentive nursing of Kinsuke, sat up on the tatami mats, and seeing an unfamiliar man present, he was terrified once more.

Kanko: 'Hey, Kinsuke, I do not know who this person is, but it seems I am about to faint again.'

Genshinbau: 'Now, now, young woman, why are you trembling? Please do not worry at all. These two are thieves as you see them. It is a lie that they broke into this house and asked for food. To tell you the truth, they sniffed out that you had come to this house and intended to force themselves upon you. I heard them talking about it while I was resting in the shade of a tree, unaware that I was there, so I came to save you from your crisis. However, because these fellows confronted me with deadly weapons, I drew my sword and fought back. These two are gold-plated thieves, but I am the heaven-sent savior, a high-ranking monk and sage named Genshinbau, the incarnation of the Heavenly Emperor. Entrust your body and soul to this Genshinbau. If you do so, through the protection of the gods and Buddhas, your good fortune, virtue, and longevity are guaranteed.'

Rinjan: 'Yes, I humbly request your protection in every way, for there is nothing in this world as terrifying as a thief. Thieves are more frightening than earthquakes, thunder, fires, or even one's own father.'

While washing the millet, Koburai glanced at Genshinbau's face.

Koburai: 'Tsk, Genshinbau-sama, are you not being a bit too excessive?'

Genshinbau shouted in a loud voice, 'Fool, you are a witless fellow.'

Kōro: 'I command it. Genshinbau shall take care of the young lady, and Koburai shall diligently prepare the meal.'

Koburai: 'Tsk, they treat me like a fool.'

Saying this, he adjusted his sash in a cross pattern, tied his headband to the side, and began to diligently prepare to cook the rice. Rinjan stared intently at Genshinbau's face, was suddenly taken aback, and recoiled.

Rinjan: 'Hey, you are that corrupt monk who holed up on Mount Ōra, performed the trick of bringing down the stars, and forced yourself upon my younger sister.'

Genshinbau: 'Ha ha ha ha! Even if I forced myself upon her, I would never take her life. That she was so infatuated that she would even give her life for me must also be a bond tied by the gods. You, who are connected to her, are also destined by the gods to become my wife.'

Rinjan: 'Ugh, you filthy corrupt monk! I shall report you to the authorities and avenge my sister. Prepare yourself!'

Saying this, she jumped out from the back door and ran off into the unknown along the rice field paths she had learned.

Genshinbau, Koburai, and Kōro, the three of them, said, 'We cannot stay here like this,' and once again, they fled out from the back door into the moonlit field, hungry and heading to parts unknown.
