Chapter 7: Dream の Way [1796]

夢の道〔1796〕

§ 1

The gray clouds that filled the entire sky were torn in places, revealing patches of red clouds that looked as if they might fall. At the eaves of a dilapidated temple at the foot of the mountain, a large zelkova tree was blown by the cold wind, shivering as it shed its leaves one by one like feathered robes. Two or three black-and-white spotted crows were cawing with ominous, hoarse voices. A high tower with reddish-brown tiles and fallen walls dominated the entire landscape of the area.

The sound of the temple bell, which tolls the impermanence of all things, echoed from far, far away, though it did not seem to come from this temple. A man walked sluggishly across a half-rotted wooden bridge where frost pillars stood, leaning on a green bamboo staff and bowing his waist, calling out feebly, 'Is anyone there? Is anyone there?'

A nun with a fierce countenance suddenly slid open the torn shoji screen and thrust her face out. The nun frowned with a look of disgust and said,

Nun: 'Where are you from, and for what purpose have you come wandering here? This is no place for you to come; please leave at once.'

Barugii: 'I am called Barugii, a man of some small reputation. I have come this far because there is someone I wish to inquire about. Is a priest named Genshinbau staying at this temple?'

Nun: 'I know no such person. Please leave at once. Do you have any idea where you think this place is? This is a temple where only nuns reside, a place forbidden to men. Do you not notice the signpost that says men are not allowed to enter?'

Barugii: 'Ah, is that so? I was in such a hurry as the day was turning to dusk that I did not notice it, and I have been rude. However, as the day has completely ended and there are no other houses in the vicinity, I do not know if it is forbidden to men or not, but I would like to ask for lodging for the night, even if it is just in a corner of your garden.'

Nun: 'That is absolutely impossible. Even speaking to a man brings down the divine punishment of the Buddha. I do not know how it looks to your eyes, but this is a magnificent temple called Gokuraku-Jōdoji. Now, now, please go home quickly.'

Saying this, she slammed the torn shoji screen shut and hid herself away, looking thoroughly annoyed. Barugii crossed the bridge again and, with no destination in mind, walked on feebly, staggering along. When he had proceeded north for about half an hour, an old woman of about eighty with a black face and hair disheveled like a wild tangle came flying from behind, shouting in a cracked voice, 'Hey! Hey!' Barugii stopped abruptly and, with a puzzled look, said,

Barugii: 'Are you the one calling me? For what purpose have you stopped me?'

Old Woman: 'I am an old woman named Okuma who runs a low-class inn by the edge of that thicket. Would it not be possible for you to come and stay at my place tonight?'

Barugii: 'Oh, that is grateful. But, old woman, even if you say it is small, as long as you are running an inn, you must have two or three rooms, right?'

Old Woman: 'Do not worry. Even if I say it is small, it is a cheap hotel. Whether it is you alone or two people, I can put you up in any corner.'

Barugii: 'How much is the room charge?'

Old Woman: 'Anything is fine, so please just give me what you think you want to pay. I am not greedy, so do not worry about that.'

Barugii: 'Oh, in that case, I was in trouble without a place to stay, so I will let you put me up.'

Following the old woman, he walked about four or five chō through a field of weeds where the grass grew thick, and around the house, cow and horse dung was piled high, the foul odor piercing his nose.

Barugii: 'Old woman, this is a very smelly house. There are no cows or horses here, so why is there so much cow and horse dung piled up like this?'

Old Woman: 'Because the guests who stay here every day leave behind heaps of cow and horse dung when they return, so it is just like this... as they say, even dust, when piled up, becomes a mountain... and so a mountain of dung has been formed.'

On the night of the twenty-third night moon-waiting, just as I was resisting with all my might to keep my daughter from being taken away by you and a few other petty thieves, you drew an ox-knife, stabbed my stomach, and went home after mocking me while I suffered, did you not? Well, you have come to a good place. From now on, in order to take revenge for that, I intend to torture you to death and then pound your flesh and bones to bury them in this ox-belly. Even if you struggle, there is no escaping now. You had better prepare yourself by chanting a prayer or something. Still, this is truly satisfying. Aha ha ha ha.'

§ 3

The monsters of Gozu and Mezu, with one accord, raised their voices in a war cry that seemed to rend the very heaven and earth.

Balugie, finding himself in a desperate plight, strained his voice to the utmost to chant the Kazūta of the Ananai-kyō that he had learned from Dariyahime, reciting 'One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, hundred, thousand, ten thousand' with great difficulty. The monsters, including Gozu, Mezu, and Okuma, gradually faded in their shadows and finally vanished without a trace. Looking around, he found himself lying with his clothes covered in mud beside a narrow path overgrown with withered grass.

Balugie finally stood up and muttered, 'Oh, what a terrible dream I have had. Now, where on earth am I? It is dark, and I cannot sleep in such a desolate field, nor is there anyone passing by; this is truly troublesome. Well, there is no help for it; I shall walk as far as my compass leads me. It is useless to lie down in such a place and have such frightening dreams again.'

As he muttered this, he began to walk, operating his compass with a tottering gait like a sheep being led to the slaughterhouse. Beside the road, the nun he had met earlier at the old temple appeared, thrusting her sallow face out from the withered grass, and called out, 'Excuse me.'

Balugie was startled and said, 'Oh, are you not the nun I met earlier? What are you doing in a place like this?'

Nun: 'Is it I, you ask? You surely know me well; I am Dariyahime.'

Balugie: 'Hey, do not make a fool of me. Dariyahime does not have such a sallow, withered face. You must be a tanuki, and you are only disguised as the nun from before, are you not?'

Nun: 'No, no, never, I am neither a tanuki nor anything of the sort. I am the spirit of the deputy guardian who was lured out by Genshinbō to the thieves' cave in the Taniguku Valley. To escape that crisis, I acted with a demonic heart, using my own beauty as a shield, pretending to be in love with you, and attempting to have you sent to my home. I am filled with evil thoughts. Please, I beg you to say that you forgive me. If you do not, I cannot be saved. In the world of the Gods, there is not the slightest bit of unfairness; I must atone for the sin of deceiving you, and that is why I am wandering in such a place.'

Balugie: 'Indeed, now that I look closely, there is a certain resemblance. Oh, I have spoken truly rude and inexcusable things to you as well. However, there is no question of forgiving or not forgiving; please, set your mind at ease.'

Nun: 'I deceived you so easily, and despite being a sinful being, I dared to strike the head where the Gods reside three times, intending to offer you my opinion. Because of that sin, my head has become bald like this, and I am wandering in such a place. It was a grave sin to strike a head when I had no right to do so.'

Balugie: 'My, the rules of the Gods are indeed difficult. In that case, if I may, I shall forgive the sin of the rudeness you committed against me.'

Nun: 'Yes, thank you very much. Please, take this fan in your hand and strike my head three times.'

Balugie: 'Oh, this is truly a matter of equality; if that is the case, I shall follow your instructions and accept your request.'

Saying this, he lightly tapped her three times with the fan. With that, the nun's figure vanished instantly like smoke. From the edge of the mountain in the distance, someone was calling his name, saying, 'Hey, hey.' Because the voice sounded somewhat familiar, Balugie felt as if he were being pulled, and he walked toward the sound without realizing it. Suddenly, a great fire appeared from the east, scorching the sky, and fell before Balugie, exploding with a thud that shook the earth. The moment it did, he realized he was lying bound hand and foot in the meadow at the foot of Mount Haru pass. While still bound, Balugie managed to raise his body, sat cross-legged on the grass, and as he gazed at the clouds drifting across the sky, Dariyahime, Tamakiyowake, and several villagers arrived on the scene.

バル『何とマア、神様の規則といふ者は難しいものですな、そんなら畏れ乍ら、私に加へた無礼の罪を、更めて赦しませう』

尼『ハイ有難う御座います。どうぞ貴下のお手で此扇子を以て私の頭を三つ打つて下さい』

バル『ヤア、これはこれは御均等さまに、左様ならば仰せに従ひ御免を蒙りませう』

といひ乍ら、軽くポンポンポンと扇子の胸で三度打つた。これつきり尼僧の姿はパツと煙の如く消えて了つた。「オーイオーイ」と向方の山の端から吾名を呼とめる者がある。其声に何となく聞覚があるので、バルギーは引ずらるる如き心地し乍ら、声する方に何時とはなしに進んで行つた。忽ち天を焦して東方より一大火光が現はれ、バルギーの面前に落下し、ドンと地響うつて爆発した途端に気がつけば、自分はハル山峠の麓の草原に雁字搦に縛られて倒れてゐた事が分つた。バルギーは縛められた儘、漸くにして身を起し、草の上に胡座をかき、空ゆく雲を眺めてゐると、そこへスタスタとやつて来たのは、ダリヤ姫、玉清別、及び数人の村人であつた。

§ 4

Dari: 'Oh, Barugii-sama, how pitiful, by whom have you been bound in such a way? Come, come, everyone, please untie the bonds quickly.'

Barugii: 'Yes, thank you very much. I had a dispute with an unexpected fellow, and as I had struck my lower back and was weakened, I was easily bound by the enemy and seem to have lost consciousness. I am so glad you have come.'

Dari: 'Barugii-sama, you are truly a person of firm righteousness. When the Lord Kamususanōnōokami descended upon the Kamuzukasa of Tamakiyowake, and you were pressed by Genshinbau and others at the foot of Haru-yamatauge, and while being interrogated about my whereabouts, you hid them at the risk of your life. That sense of chivalry has been greatly praised by the Kamisama as well, and having received the Gosenji to go and save you as quickly as possible, I have come to your aid without a moment's delay. Please, be at ease.'

Barugii: 'Nay, this is, this is truly humbling. I have no words to express my gratitude. I can only do this.'

He says this while shedding tears and pressing his hands together in prayer.

Tamakiyowake: 'Barugii-sama, I have been impressed by your manliness. How would it be if you returned to my house and recuperated until your back injury heals? A palanquin will arrive shortly.'

Barugii: 'Do you think so highly of a villain like me? Ah, I shall never do evil things again from this moment on. I intend to return to my natural goodness and dedicate my life to society and to the Way. I humbly place myself in your hands.'

From this time on, Barugii was carried by the villagers and stayed at the shrine of Tamakiyowake together with Dariyahime-sama to nurse his illness, remaining there for about a month while receiving the devoted care of Dariyahime-sama. Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haemase.
