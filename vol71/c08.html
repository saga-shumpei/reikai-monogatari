Chapter 8: 無遊 Monster [1797]

無遊怪〔1797〕

§ 1

Genshinbau, who had entrenched himself in Mount Aura, performed the art of bringing down stars, gathered three thousand petty thieves, and sought to grasp the seven thousand and more countries of Indo in his own hands, was like one clutching at clouds or chasing bubbles; he drew up fantasies like trying to knock down the stars shining in the heavens with a bamboo pole, and while he was puffing up his awkwardly formed nose, he was intruded upon by Umekōwake of the Ananai-kyō, cast aside by his beloved wife Yoriko, disowned by his sworn brother Shīgō, and his painstaking strategic plans all came to naught like a painted cake. Leading only three hundred scoundrels, and wandering here and there like a woman of the latch who loves men again, in order to execute his second strategic plan, he finally lost his head over Princess Dariya of the port of Suga. Parting from his many subordinates, he sneaked into the rocky cavern of Shiyakanna on Mount Taniguku, disguised as a magnificent savior, but had his wild spirit crushed by Shiyakanna, had his face scribbled upon by Dariya with great care, and was furthermore treated to the indignity of having sand kicked at him by her heels before she vanished into thin air. In his frustration, he sought the support of Shiyakanna's subordinates, and together with Koburai, he scoured the mountain slopes, discovered that Princess Dariya was hiding in the mansion of Tamakiyowake, and with the tenacity of a snake eyeing a frog, he stood guard with Koburai before the locked gate, determined to snatch the princess. There, he was overwhelmed by the Kototama of the Child of God who had manifested in the form of Tamakiyowake's son, and believing that Princess Dariya had already escaped the mansion of Tamakiyowake, he made his way to the foot of the Haru Mountain pass, where he encountered Kōro, who had also been a subordinate of Shiyakanna, and struck down Barugī. Here, the three joined hands, and regardless of the matter of Dariya, they tightened their headbands, resolved to make a great effort to carry out a grand undertaking that would astonish the ears and eyes of the world, and convinced themselves that if they were hailed as magnificent heroes and great men, beauties like Dariya would flock to them like falling rain without even being sought. His face, with his ugly features concentrated in one place, his protruding teeth bared, and steam rising from his bald head as he grinned, was a style that would make even a man who usually looked as if he had bitten into a bitter worm burst into laughter in spite of himself.

Genshinbau, with Koburai and Kōro following behind him, reached the summit of the Haru Mountain pass, and looking down toward the southeast, he saw the Tarahan Castle with its high roof tiles and white walls shining in the evening sun, which filled him with a strangely magnificent feeling. Genshinbau sat heavily upon a large rock on the right side of the summit, opened and closed his mouth in a rhythm like a toad catching mosquitoes on a summer evening, and while twitching his chin, he muttered to himself: 'Ah, the mountain ranges as far as the eye can see are covered in dense forests, the fertile plains stretch out without limit, and the city of Tarahan appears to my eyes as if it were reaching the height of prosperity. A man of great spirit should live forever in this world and rule over all under heaven. Shiyakanna, that fellow who flourished as the Left Guardian of that Tarahan Castle—if a man of that caliber can become a Left Guardian, then what time or effort would it take for me to take Tarahan Castle into my own hands if I approach it with my magic and wisdom? Oh, yes, that is it, that is it; from now on, I shall remodel my head and begin a grand plan. To do so, I cannot succeed without a chief minister; I want to have good retainers somehow, but this Genshinbau, who leads three thousand subordinates, is in a truly miserable state today. Although it is said that the path of life is such that prosperity and decay are not constant, it is simply not good enough that Koburai, who was a petty thief leader, and Kōro, a petty thief, are my Left and Right Guardians. Alas, I must somehow gather Shiyakanna's subordinates, collect the three hundred men I pulled from Mount Aura, provide them with various training, and if I do not conquer the world, the worm in my belly will not be quieted. Princess Dariya is hard to abandon, but Tarahan Castle is also hard to abandon; well, where there is a will, there is a way.'

§ 2

Standing abruptly upon the rock, his gaze, as he looked down upon the southeastern sky, appeared somehow ominous.

Koburai, observing this state of affairs, burst into laughter and said,

Koburai: 'Ufufufu, Genshinbau-sama, I do not know what it is, but you are in a terribly fierce mood. I overheard a little of your plan, which seems as unlikely as flowers blooming on a withered tree. What exactly is your plan? A place like Tarahan Castle is surely impossible. Since brave warriors of wisdom, like stars in the sky, are lined up there like a forest, even if you, Genshinbau-sama, are a heaven-sent savior, it will not go well for you. It is fine to indulge in fantasies, but if you do not consider the appropriate logic here, you may invite your own ruin. As a loyal subject, I offer you this advice for the sake of your future.'

Genshinbau: 'What! As a mere thief, how could you understand the heart of a hero? First of all, craftsmanship lies in the details; just watch what I do.'

Koburai: 'You speak of me as a mere thief, but are you not a boss of thieves yourself? You pretend to be virtuous, fingering your prayer beads, leaning on your vajra staff, and masquerading as a savior, but in your heart, you are a great robber no different from a tiger or a wolf. I do not know if this offends you, but a sign of misfortune is appearing on your face. There is truth even in the words of a commoner, and I would like you to lend an ear to what we have to say.'

Genshinbau: 'Well, I suppose I must listen to what you say. However, I do not intend to leap to the top of Mount Himalaya in a single bound. I intend to increase my allies step by step, cultivate my strength, and then finally set about the execution. After all, I am Genshinbau, a man with the proven skill of having led three thousand subordinates.'

Koburai: 'Indeed, that must be so. Even Koburai does not doubt your capability in the slightest. However, money is required for everything one does, is it not? You must have that money hidden somewhere, must you not?'

Genshinbau: 'It is indeed hidden. Look at that Tarahan Castle; you can see the white walls of storehouses here and there in the town, can you not? All of my wealth is tucked away in those.'

Koburai: 'Ahahaha, no matter how much wealth is tucked away, as long as you are not the owner, you cannot pull it out and use it openly. What is your plan for that?'

Genshinbau: 'That is where the incarnation of the Heavenly Emperor, the heaven-sent savior, comes in. All the wealth existing on this land is something created by the gods, and I intend to use means to extract and utilize it.'

Koburai: 'Ah, if that is the case, it appears your plan is to shed the mask of the heaven-sent savior and return to being the original boss of thieves.'

Genshinbau: 'To change and transform, appearing and disappearing without limit, is the true essence of a great hero. Did Christ not say it... that one cannot serve both God and money... We live in a world where the head of a great religion issues dishonored bills, and members of the House of Peers run fraudulent advertisements to squeeze money from the poor. No matter what, money is the foundation. If my goal is achieved, I will surely employ you in a high position; until then, you must obey whatever I say, whether it be good or evil. Above all, if you settle your mind, I, Genshinbau, intend to exhaust every divine calculation and demonic strategy to launch this great ambition.'

Koburai: 'Indeed, this will be interesting. However, when you take the world, you will surely make this Koburai either the Minister of the Left or the Minister of the Right, will you not?'

Genshinbau: 'Hmm, well, the wind will blow as it will at that time.'

Koburai: 'Depending on how the wind blows at that time, I might fall into any kind of fate. I cannot make such an unreliable promise.'

Genshinbau: 'Rather than counting your chickens before they hatch, Jitsukauriyoku is what matters most. If you only put it into practice, even if you say you dislike it, I will ask you to become Samori; Ronkōkaushiyō is a matter for after we succeed. Now, from here on, I shall cast away my Jiyuzu, snap my Kongauzue in two, and becoming a reincarnation of the great thief Ishikaha Gōemon, I shall attempt a Daikuwatsudō.'

So saying, he tore the Jiyuzu to pieces and smashed it against the rock, and the one hundred and eight Bodaijiyu beads, representing the Hiyakuhachibonnau, scattered in all directions like Amearare, while the Kongauzue was snapped into three, lying fallen at the foot of the Ōiha with a resentful look.

Genshinbau, perhaps because some attachment to the Jiyuzu remained after all, gazed at the scattering beads and said:

Genshinbau: 'Namu Hiyakuhachibonnau Jiyuzu Daibosatsu, Namu Daikyūseishu Henshō Kongauzue Nyorai, Namu Amidabutsu Namu Amidabutsu.'

Leaving these words of dedication, he descended from the rock and emerged onto the narrow red-clay road of the mountain pass. Kōro, exhausted from the journey, was lying flat and snoring like thunder. Watching this, Genshinbau said:

Genshinbau: 'Hey, Koburai, this fellow Kōro is a carefree one, is he not? Even though we do not know when pursuers might come to such a place, he is sleeping and snoring like thunder. How about we shake him awake?'

Koburai: 'Genshinbau-sama, I cannot tell what this fellow Kōro is thinking. He might be feigning sleep like a tanuki on purpose, and when he has heard all of your and my plans, he might go to Tarahan Castle and, begging your pardon, betray us. I have thought his behavior strange and have never once let my guard down, but is it not all the more suspicious that he is snoring in a place like this? To prevent disaster before it happens is the duty of the wise; I believe that cutting off the root of evil while it is but two leaves is the policy for future safety. Once it becomes a situation where even using an axe and halberd is of no avail, there will be nothing more that can be done.'

Genshinbau: 'Do not worry so deeply; what could he possibly do with that face? Well, do not worry, leave it to me.'

Koburai: 'Hmph, is that so? Then please do not speak of my proposal to this Kōro.'

Genshinbau: 'Hmm, very well. I will not do anything so clumsy as to blabber about unnecessary things and cause internal strife among colleagues. Well, you may rest easy.'

Kōro stretched his limbs with a groan, intentionally shook himself three or four times, continued to yawn, then rubbed his eyes, opened his chestnut eyes wide, and said:

Kōro: 'Ah, I slept well, I slept well. I was just about to become the King of the Land of Kasyo, but what a pity that the dream has ended. ...That which is more precious than life itself, is the awakening from an unfinished dream... that is it. Oh, Genshinbau-sama, ah, Koburai-san, my elder brother in song, I have been terribly rude.'

Genshinbau: 'You slept well indeed. However, you say you dreamed of becoming the King of the Land of Kasyo; that is a fine fortune-telling. I shall buy that dream from you.'

Kōro: 'Yes, thank you very much. However, I cannot sell such a dream for money. Since it was a dream I saw at the summit of Haruyama Pass, at the roots of the Tokiwakaniwa rock, it will surely come true. I absolutely cannot sell it.'

Genshinbau: 'No matter what you say, it is just a dream, is it not? It cost you nothing to begin with, so I will give you one hundred ryō, please sell it to me.'

Kōro: '...'

Koburai: 'Excuse me, Genshin-sama, why would you do such a foolish thing? It would be no good if our hard-planned scheme turned into a dream. You are a bit slow-witted to try to buy an intangible dream with tangible treasure.'

Genshinbau: 'Do not talk nonsense. A dream is nothingness, and nothingness is nothingness; from nothingness, existence is born; it is existence yet it is nothingness, and it is nothingness yet it is existence. This is precisely the application of the Kototama of A in Kototama studies. A is Heaven, it is Father, it is the Great Universe, it is the Great Authority. I must obtain this spiritual dream seen during sleep, or my purpose will not be achieved. This must surely be something the gods showed to Kōro. Since you and I have planned to occupy the Tarahan country and become kings, and this dream was seen at the very feet of that plan, it is the unity of the manifest and the hidden; there could be no better omen than this. Therefore, if Kōro says he will not sell it, then I, Genshin, shall take that dream. Since robbing others is my trade to begin with, aha ha ha!'

§ 4

Kobu: 'Even if you say that thievery is your Shiyōbai, is it not going a bit too far to snatch away even a person's dreams?'

Gen: 'Is this not what they call a Shigumi that lets not even water leak through? To be thorough, to enter from the minute into the subtle, and to exercise Chiui is exactly what a Chishiya should do. The Kuwatsuyō of Shidaimuguwai and Shishōmunai is the very Shikaku of one who is a Kami.'

Kobu: 'You have now discarded your Jiyuzu, snapped your Kongauzue in two, returned to being a Dorobau, and have retired from being a Keshin of the Tentei, have you not? Now that it has come to this, calling yourself a Kamisama will no longer pass as valid.'

Gen: 'Ahahaha, there are various kinds of Kami. I have resigned from the Seishinkai, but from now on I shall become a Kami of the Jiyashinkai. There are even Kamisama for Dorobau, you know. From now on, I am the Kami of Dorobau, the Kami of Boronsu.'

Kobu: 'Heh, humph. In that case, do you not desire the Anjiu of the afterlife in the Tengoku and Jiyōdo?'

Gen: 'Oi, as a person of my Bunzai, while harboring the heart of a wolf that would occupy another's land, how could there be such a thing as Tengoku or Jiyōdo for me? I shall become the champion of hell, both in spirit and flesh, and act accordingly. Rather than going to Gokuraku Jiyōdo to be served a hundred flavors of food, living a life of ease on a lotus pedestal, and suffering from boredom, how much more enjoyable it is to rush into the streets of hell and engage in activities where life itself is the target! Gokuraku is a place that does not suit our nature.'

Kobu: 'Un, indeed... Chigiru akinasu... Hell is quite right. If that is the case, I too shall become a foster child of the Akki Rasetsu of Genshinbō, rush into the streets of Shura, and with the force of a lion in fury or a man crossing a river without a boat, I shall overturn the Tarahan Castle from its very foundations and show it to you, itsuhihini!'

Gen: 'Oi, Kōro, will you not sell it to me after all?'

Kōro: 'Hai, I shall sell it. In return, I have one request. I do not need money; please grant me the power of command.'

Gen: 'Very well, I shall grant it to you. In return, I have certainly received the dream here.'

Kōro turns to the side, sticks out his tongue for a moment, and with an indifferent face,

Kōro: 'Ya, how grateful I am. Now, whether it be Genshinbō or Koburai, you shall obey my commands.'

He says this, shrugs his shoulders, and suddenly becomes energized.

Gen: 'Hahaha, what a hopeless fellow.'

Kobu: 'Hen, as if I would listen to the commands of such a guy. It is utterly ridiculous, enough to make one's navel tea laugh. However, Genshin-sama, no matter how long we stay on top of a mountain like this, there is no food. Shall we not raid some house somewhere to fill our stomachs? The sun has tilted toward the western mountains, and it is still three ri to the village at the foot of this mountain. Come, let us go.'

Saying this, Koburai takes the lead on the mountain path he knows by heart, and the three of them descend the steep slope.
