Chapter 12: Mud 壁 [1801]

泥壁〔1801〕

§ 1

The internal affairs of the Tarahan nation, which had been in a state of chaotic disorder, underwent a complete transformation following the accession of Prince Sudarman. With the Left Guardian and Right Guardian cultivating and nurturing the governance of the state, the peace of the people, and the wealth of the nation from morning until night, the domestic situation finally escaped the suffering of the depths of misery. As all matters were set on the right path, it was hailed as the Miyo of Miroku, and the common people, one and all, respected and adored the new Shinōdenka as if he were their parent, their master, or their teacher.

Yet, as the long spring days waned and the flowers in the fields and mountains scattered, the light of the fields dripped with fresh greenery. While the Hototogisu sang of the peace of the world here and there, the proverb that good deeds have many demons proved true. In the main thoroughfare of the Tarahan castle town, even the road one walked upon was a wide avenue, yet grand buildings and high towers were suddenly engulfed by tongues of flame. Before one's eyes, they collapsed, and dozens of wealthy merchants fell like a row of dominoes.

Taking advantage of this chaos, Genshinbō, along with the two men Koburai and Kōro, conspired to sneak into the Shiyakanna mansion of the Left Guardian. They intended to steal gold, silver, and treasures to fulfill their long-held ambitions. In the sadness of a body that was not divine, unaware that danger was approaching him, he slipped through the white smoke and pushed open the wicket gate of the back entrance of the Left Guardian's mansion. Looking out into the city from afar,

the moonlight, which had been shining just moments before, was now like ink, engulfed by the flames, losing its light and trembling. The spectacle was truly dreadful. Taking advantage of this opportunity, the three men crept deep into the inner chambers, and just as they were about to break the lock of the treasure house and leap inside, they were discovered by the guards. The three were rounded up, bound hand and foot, and thrown into the prison within the main castle; it was a miserable fate.

Genshinbō and the other two, taking advantage of the commotion of the fire, snuck into the Left Guardian's mansion, but just as they were working to break open the treasure house and steal a mountain of treasures, they were easily captured by a dozen guards who had been hiding in the shadows. They were escorted to the guardhouse within Tarahan Castle and thrown into individual solitary cells.

The fire finally subsided, and with the donations and sympathy money gathered from all directions, plans were made to manage the great shops as they had been before. As the atmosphere of reconstruction began to drift in, they began to investigate the three thieves. First, Genshinbō was brought out, and Arina, of the Right Guardian's office, was assigned to examine him.

Arina sat solemnly on the high seat. Genshinbō was brought out to the white gravel courtyard with his hands tied behind his back, yet he sat down on a chair with a haughty air. Leaning back slightly, he glared at the Right Guardian, thinking to himself, 'This greenhorn, how insolent. He still has milk on his lips. What is he capable of?' He pursed his lips into the shape of the character 'he' and waited for Arina's interrogation.

Arina: 'What is your name?'

Genshinbō: '....'

Arina: 'State your address and name clearly.'

Genshinbō: 'My current address is the first prison within Tarahan Castle.'

Arina: 'What do you say your name is?'

Genshinbō: 'What will you do if you hear my name? If you insist that I give my name, I do not mind telling you. Do not be surprised when you hear it. To begin with, I am the celestial being of the First Spirit Kingdom, the incarnation of the Heavenly Emperor, the savior sent from heaven, Tenshinbō-sama, and I am a sworn brother of Shiyakanna, the Left Guardian. I rode into Shiyakanna's mansion to offer my condolences for the fire, fearing the danger of the spreading flames, and just as I was about to break the lock to move the treasures of the storehouse to a safe zone, those ignorant foot soldiers jumped out from the side, treated the savior sent from heaven like a criminal without rhyme or reason, and shoved me into a place like this. A greenhorn like you is not even a match for me, even if you are an official of the Right Guardian. If you have any doubts, call Shiyakanna here; I will explain the principles of heaven and earth to him thoroughly, ah... Hey, you greenhorn, will you not untie my bonds? Will you not let me have a smoke? Is there anyone who treats the sworn brother of the Left Guardian with such abuse? This is the height of thoughtlessness. How do you think you can serve as the Right Guardian of a nation if you cannot distinguish between a guest offering condolences for a fire and a thief? You had better pull yourself together.'

§ 4

Ari: 'I have no ears to listen to such Yomaigoto. I shall examine you once more at the Shirasu, for the Tekikaku Shiyōko has already been brought forth.'

Saying this, he left the place with loud Kutsuoto. Genshinbau opened his large mouth once again, gathered his Bukatsukau Mehana together, and in desperation began to perform a Dodoitsu.

'Fled, fled, fled away again, Fearing the Goikuō of Genshin-sama, Umori-no-Ari-na has hoisted sails on his backside, And fled away with a Stakora-yoiyasa. What a pitiful Shiromono it is indeed. Samori-Shyakanna must be at this moment, Sighing deeply at his desk, Recalling the Furukizu of the past, With a Zutsū-Hachimaki and sweat dripping down, Likely leaking from his Yakan. Ah, Korashiyo, Korashiyo, It is the Daiku-sama who builds the house, And Shyakanna who plasters the walls. With the old Furukizu all plastered over, Shyakanna hides them with mud. Shining brightly with his daughter's light, Like a firefly he emits a glow. He himself is a thief who torments others, While accusing me of being a thief and tormenting me. If you are going to be a thief, do it properly. Over the moon country of Shichisenyokoku, As if holding the Riyōbun of Ōkuronushi, In the palm of one hand, For a Bantō-sama of a mere Tarahan-Itsukoku, You lack wit. Whether you are Samori or Imori, I know not, But you are a narrow-minded Oyaji-dono, Ah, Korasa, Korasa.'

Thus he chattered on like a mentally deranged person. From between the lattice windows, a swarm of mosquitoes attacked without any reserve or hesitation.
