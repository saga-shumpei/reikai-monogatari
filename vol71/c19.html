Chapter 19: 角兵衛 Lion [1808]

角兵衛獅子〔1808〕

§ 1

In the back room of the Hamayariyokan in the village of Irie, the aforementioned Genshinbau and Chigusa no Takahime have been staying for about ten days, doing nothing and chatting idly.

Takahime: 'Excuse me, Genshin-sama, it has been about ten days since we stayed at this inn, but is it not terribly boring? The Hidakayama, famous for the lake of Haru, can no longer be seen, and the coming and going of the full-sailed and half-sailed boats seems much lonelier than in the past. Shall we not compose a poem or something to enjoy ourselves?'

Genshin: 'Even without suffering from boredom, if only you and I were here together, we could have any kind of pleasure, but because you complain about guests and the moon, you do not respond, and thus you are throwing away a pleasure that costs nothing and suffering by your own hand. I am truly at my wit's end with this situation.'

Takahime: 'Ho ho ho ho ho, what is this, Genshin-sama is quite a refined person, is he not? Truly, what is hateful is the guest. If only there were no guests, I could satisfy Genshin-sama's mood completely.'

Genshin: 'Generally speaking, I have heard that the moon lasts at longest a week and at shortest three days, but for you, it has already been ten days, has it not? That is a somewhat strange condition.'

Takahime: 'That is certainly true, for I am a celestial being of the Spirit Realm. An ordinary human would only have seven days of impurity in a month, but because I clear away the whole year's worth at once, I have eighty-four days of menstruation, combining twelve months.'

Genshin: 'I cannot wait for such a long time. How about this, let us take the plunge and commit adultery? That is what is called menstrual adultery, ah-an.'

Takahime: 'Ho ho ho, what a lecherous fellow. It is said that even if one violates a dragon woman, one will not be saved for seven lives; how much more so is there anyone in the world foolish enough to commit menstrual adultery with a celestial being? You will be punished not only for seven or eight lives, but for a million lives.'

Genshin: 'Can you not ask for a reduction? It seems you could endure about half of the eighty-four days. Since the world is full of charms, there should be no harm in that. Even the gods would not say such boorish things, would they?'

Takahime: 'Genshin-sama, please stop such talk now. I feel as if I am going to fall into hell. Rather than that, shall we not simply compose a poem...'

Takahime: 'I wish to lean on your arm, my lord, but I cannot, for the moon's barrier stands in the way. The stars shine in the sky as if they desire to be together, but their shadows fade due to the moon's barrier. The moon shines upon the head of Genshin-no-kimi, and shadows fall into the valley of the sacred mountain.'

Genshin: 'Hey, do not tease me. The valley of the sacred mountain is bright red, is it not? The autumn leaves are shining, and I would like to go viewing the autumn leaves.'

Takahime: 'Genshin-sama, no, that is disgusting.'

While saying this, she slapped his bald head with her slender hand. Genshin gathered his eyes, nose, and mouth all into one place as if he had tightened a drawstring bag,

Genshin: 'Etsu-he he he he, hey, Chigusa, do not do such unreasonable things. Blood flows through my head too, you know.'

Takahime: 'The kettle was boiling so much that I burned my palm. Please, Genshin-sama, go and draw some water, for I must cool my hand...'

Genshin: 'It should be fine that the warmth of my head remains on your hand; well, enjoy waiting, for the warmth will not stop for so long.'

Takahime: 'Have some moderation with your vanity. The sweat and grease from your kettle-head have stuck to my hand, and it is so unpleasant that I am asking you to go and draw some water.'

Genshin: 'Eh, what a hopeless mountain goddess you are.'

Saying this, he stood up himself and drew water from the well,

Genshin: 'Now, mountain goddess-sama, no, no, Miroku no Futobashira-sama, please wash your hands.'

Chigusa: 'Zenzai zenzai.'

Saying this, she washed her hands in the water of the Kanadarahi.

Chigusa: 'Ya, Genshinbau, you have worked hard. As a hōbi, I shall give you this water, so you had best drink every last drop before me. It is certainly not the teaka of Chigusahime-sama, but the aseabura of your own yakuwanatama, so please accept it with joy.'

Genshinbau: 'Oi, kakan, niyōbau... no, Chigusa no Futobashira, do not make a baka of me. Who do you think I am? Am I not your husband?'

Chigusa: 'You are a husband who forced his way in by relying on your status as a husband, and whether viewed from the front or the side, you have such a husbandly style, indeed.'

While they were flirting in this manner, the Kakubee Jishi came along the street in front, making a clamor and beating a taiko.

The master of the yadoya came out to their ima with trepidation and said,

'Moshi kiyakusama, you seem to be very bored, but just now, as you can see, the Kakubee Jishi has arrived at the gate. How would it be if you had them perform a dance? It would be very entertaining for your amusement.'

Genshinbau: 'Ya, that is welcome. Please let them perform for the viewing of the Himegami-sama... And, Miroku no Futobashira-sama, what do you think of the Kakubee Jishi?'

Chigusahime-sama, with a deliberately composed face, said,

'Zenzai zenzai, I desire it, I desire it.'

Teishiyu: 'Hai, as you command, I shall bring them here at once.'

Saying this, he went out to the front. After a short while, a man wearing a small lion mask and a taiko player wearing a deep straw hat arrived.

Genshinbau: 'Yaa, gokurau gokurau gokurau, there is no need for reserve. Come up to this zashiki and perform a dance for us. Lately, I am troubled because the mood of Miroku-sama is poor. We must perform a kagura dance or something to bring about the Iwato-biraki; I myself am truly troubled by the peak of the autumn leaves.'

The Kakubee Jishi bowed lightly, jumped up into the zashiki, and one began to sing while the other began to dance.

'Tetenkoten tetenkoten Tetenko tenko tenko tenten Tetenko tenten tetenkoten Kakubee Jishi When the first day of the first month dawns The elder brother is eleven and the younger is seven In this town where we danced last year This year too, as always, the Kakubee Jishi Tetenkoten tetenkoten Tetenko tenko tenko tenten Tetenko tenten tetenkoten Kakubee Jishi When the first day of the first month has dawned The elder brother plays the taiko and the younger dances Longing for our home, the Kakubee Jishi The day ends with the sound of the taiko Tetenkoten tetenkoten Tetenko tenko tenko tenten Tetenko tenten tetenkoten Kakubee Jishi'

Genshinbau: 'Ah, myōmyō, sa, I shall give you this as a hōbi.'

Saying this, he tossed out a koban. The two Kakubee Jishi were delighted, and when they removed the lion mask and straw hat they were wearing on their heads, to their surprise, Genshinbau and Chigusahime-sama were sitting there on many zabuton like a pair of monsters.

Kakubee Jishi: 'Ya, you are Genshinbau, are you not? I have found you in a good place. You are the villains who tricked the two of us and buried us alive. I had received the teachings of the Kami in a dream and had already abandoned the thought of revenge against you, but seeing the two of you sitting there so composed like a married couple, I cannot forgive this. Oi, Kōro, you go quickly and report this to the office. I will stand guard so that you do not escape...'

Kōro: 'Yōshi, I have understood.'

And Kōro flew out to the front immediately. Genshinbau began to tremble violently,

'Ya, Chigusahime-sama, what shall we do? We cannot remain like this, both you and I will have our heads flown off...'

Kōro: 'Kora, it is only natural. Genshinbau, have you realized it now? Soon you will be bound by the arresting officers and your head will fly. Watching that, drinking a cup is the least I can do to vent my anger. Itsuihihi utsuhuhi, it is truly a pleasant feeling.'

Genshin: 'Hey, Koburai, I will give you ten thousand ryō in gold, so will you not withdraw your complaint? Even if you walk around as a Kakubee Jishi, you will not easily earn ten thousand ryō.'

Koburai: 'Do not talk like a fool; how could such a thing be done? Kōro has already filed the complaint, so you must resign yourself to it; there is no other way.'

Chigusahime: 'Ho ho ho ho, the comical sight of that Genshin-sama trembling; what on earth is that attitude of yours? Now, now, you fellow, it was this Genshin-sama who buried you alive, so please think that Chigusahime knows nothing of it at all.'

Koburai: 'I hold not a shred of resentment toward the Hime-sama who is my savior. And I shall never do anything like filing a complaint against you, so please, I beg you, be at ease.'

Genshinbau turned pale and was trembling violently. Chigusahime drew close to his side.

Chigusahime: 'Now, Genshin-sama, will you not pull yourself together? You, who make such a bold plan as to grasp the Moon Kingdom in your hands, how can it be that you tremble at mere Torite? Would it not be fine to blow them away with your Shinriki?'

While saying this, she gripped with all her might the two lifeless orbs hanging beneath his crotch. Genshinbau grasped at the empty air, let out a single 'Uun,' and collapsed on the spot. Outside, the sound of footsteps could be heard, bustling as if the Torite officials had arrived. Koburai, feeling as though he were welcoming the officials, hurriedly slipped out to the front. In the meantime, Chigusahime completely removed the Dōmaki from Genshinbau, wrapped it around her own waist, and settled herself in the room on the second floor of the front building, feigning ignorance.

A dozen or so Torite officials, along with Kōro, Koburai, and the innkeeper acting as a guide, came into the room and, seeing Genshinbau collapsed, said:

Torite: 'Ha, this fellow, it seems he bit his tongue and committed suicide, for he is already dead. There is nothing to be done with such a person. Innkeeper, I leave this corpse to you; it would be best to throw it away in some field somewhere.'

Having said this, they quickly departed.

Chigusahime entered a room and, looking through a tear in the shoji screen on the second floor, gazed at the detached guest room. She saw the innkeeper and the others loading Genshinbau's corpse onto a door panel, shouting as they carried it off somewhere. Chigusahime stroked her chest in relief and said:

'Namu Tonshō Genshinbau, for the sake of your soul, I offer my prayers and humble bows. Ho ho ho ho, this too is a parting gift of my own small intention. Genshinbau, you spirit, you may as well attain Buddhahood in peace. In the end, without letting the thirty thousand ryō of gold slip through my fingers, I have swindled it away. Now, with this, I am safe. I shall choose some place of scenic beauty, erect a great building, astonish the eyes of the people, build the headquarters of the Uranai religion, overturn the San-go-kyō from its very roots, and make the fame of the great pillar of Miroku shine throughout the world. Truly, when things are convenient, they are indeed convenient.'

She was smirking to herself. From outside the shoji, a voice like a cracked bell rang out:

Mokusuke: 'Watsuha ha utsuhu ha, splendid, splendid! I, Mokusuke, have clearly witnessed the skill of Chigusa no Takahime.'

Chigusa no Takahime was startled by Mokusuke's voice. 'Has Mokusuke-sama, whom I long for daily, been staying at this inn? Oh, how embarrassing. I must apply powder, I must apply rouge, I must redo my hair, I must tidy myself up.'

Chigusahime: 'Moshi moshi, Mokusuke-sama, as you have guessed, it is Chigusa no Takahime. Please, do not open this for a little while. I will tidy myself up for a moment, and then I shall see you.'

Yōgenbau Mokusuke, with a deliberately sulky tone, replied:

'Ah, is that so? If you say you will not see me, I shall not insist on being seen. Farewell. I shall ride the clouds back to the Kuruwa Castle.'

千草の高姫は杢助の声に打驚き、日頃恋慕ふ杢助様が此宿に泊つて御座つたか。おゝ恥しや、白粉も付けねばならうまい、紅もささねばなるまい、髪も結ひ直し、身繕ひせにやならぬと、

千『モシモシ杢助さま、お察しの通り千草の高姫で御座います。どうぞ少時ここを開けない如うにして下さいませ。一寸身だしなみをして、それからお目にかかりますから』

妖幻坊の杢助はワザとに、すねた如うな口振で、

『あ、左様で御座るか、会つてやらぬと仰有れば、たつて会つて貰ひたいとは思はぬ。さよなら。拙者は曲輪城へ雲に乗り立ち帰るで御座らう』

§ 4

Chigusa: 'Excuse me, Mokusuke-sama, is it not too cruel of you to abandon and return without even glancing at the wife who has been pining for you? Since parting from you, I have lived my days thinking only of how I wish to see you, whether awake or asleep. Please, I beg you to forgive my previous rudeness and look upon me just once.'

Eugenbau: 'If that is the case, then with your permission, I shall take this opportunity to see your beautiful face, Takachan, after all this time.'

Saying this, he made the floor of the second story creak, pushed open the shoji screen without ceremony, entered with a heavy, lumbering gait, and sat down firmly before Chigusa. With his round eyes bulging and a wide grin on his face, he spoke.

Eugenbau: 'Well now, Takachan, you have become quite young, have you not? You look three or four years younger than you did in your Takamiyahimejidai days. First of all, it is a joyous thing to see you in such good health.'

Chigusa: 'Excuse me, Mokusuke-sama, where on earth have you been wandering? You cannot imagine how much I have been searching for you. Have we not been apart for nearly three years now?'

Eugenbau: 'I, too, have been searching for your whereabouts and have come all this way. Thanks to the god of divination, it is the greatest fortune that I have met you at this inn by chance. Ah, I am so happy. Come, let us spend this night resting and talking of old times.'

Chigusa: 'Such a happy occasion is rare indeed. What would you like, Anata? I wish to offer you something...'

Eugenbau: 'Ah, I have no particular preference for anything. The only thing I like is your face, ha ha ha ha!'

Chigusa: 'Indeed, you always said that what you liked best was my face, and what you hated most was dogs.'

Eugenbau: 'Now, now, Takamiyahime, do not speak of dogs anymore. In truth, when I arrived at the village of Irie, I was barked at by many dogs, and I felt so ill that I could not bear it; I have been staying at this inn for about three days now. It seems you have been seducing some handsome man in the back room, and thinking of that, I cannot help but feel jealous.'

Chigusa: 'Ho ho ho ho, that fellow was a corrupt monk named Genshinbō, who had built a fortress on a mountain called Ōrayama and was acting as the ringleader of thieves. Knowing he had a great deal of money in his robe, I sweet-talked him into coming to this inn, and I successfully swindled him out of thirty thousand ryō. Who would ever be a partner to such a bald octopus-like pot? Please, think carefully.'

Eugenbau: 'That must be so. I know well that you, having a handsome man like Mokuchan for a husband, would not be a partner to such a hyottoko... yet, having been apart for three years, my heart grows suspicious and I feel strange. Ah, I am sorry for casting false accusations upon you; here, I offer my apologies.'

He pressed his palms together and bowed his head to the floor. Chigusa-hime said,

Chigusa: 'Excuse me, Mokuchan, stop it, please. Your teasing has gone far enough. Your behavior is so distant and hateful, it makes me miserable.'

Eugenbau: 'No matter how much you say you hate me, we cannot break the bond that connects us. I, too, have to yield a hundred steps to you because of the weakness of my love for you, ha ha ha! A man is a fragile thing when it comes to a woman, ah ha ha!'

Chigusa: 'Oh, how hateful, that you can say such jokes. I feel rather resentful. Come, let us sleep peacefully tonight after such a long time.'

Eugenbau: 'Are you not the heavenly being of the First Spirit Kingdom, the spirit of the great Miroku-sama of the bedrock of the earth, who is said to have a menstrual cycle of eighty-four days? I must respectfully decline to sleep with you.'

Chigusa: 'Oh, how hateful! You must have heard about that corrupt monk from somewhere. You are a spiteful person; where were you listening?'

Eugenbau: 'Hmm, in the privy... well, no, I was just passing by when I thought of going to the privy, and since the voice sounded so much like yours, I eavesdropped and heard you saying such things. I did not realize it was you, so I returned to my own room... and I could not help but envy the man who had such a beautiful woman. Well, it is fine that it was you.'

Chigusa: 'I have no such menstrual cycle. Please, rest assured and sleep.'

Eugenbau: 'Very well, how interesting. Then, shall I let you serve as my companion in the bedchamber after all this time?'

Chigusa-hime turned her back with a huff,

Chigusa: 'I do not know; do as you please.'

She was acting somewhat sulky, like a child. The sound of fierce dogs barking, 'wan wan wan,' could be heard from all directions.
