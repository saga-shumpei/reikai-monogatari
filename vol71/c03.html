Chapter 3: Gate 外漢 [1792]

門外漢〔1792〕

§ 1

A red-crowned crane that maintains a life of a thousand years does not build its nest in a withered tree; even birds that fly in the sky do not return to a nest once it has been struck, and beasts that burrow in the earth do not approach a hole once they have been targeted—such is the Dauri. Having had their stronghold overturned by the Akugami of the Brahman religion, the remnants who escaped the Talahan castle town gathered, and in the plains of Kamitani, along the peaks of Tanigukuyama where human footprints are rare, they built over thirty households. They remained determined to follow the Ananahi path handed down by their ancestors, working diligently at their family trade day and night, hoping that when the time came, they would once again build the Hafujiyō of Ananahi and soar across Tenka. Tamakiyowake, who served the Sendenshi of Hinodewake, settled his home here for a thousand generations, harboring a grand ambition and waiting for the time to arrive.

Tamakiyowake had two sons named Kamino-ko and Tamano-ko. Kamino-ko was called a child prodigy from his youth, and his divine name echoed throughout the village. Just as Tamakiyowake's wife, Tamakohime-sama, went out to the edge of the well in front of the gate to prepare the evening meal and was about to draw water with her hand on the bucket, a strange mendicant monk appeared with a haughty attitude, clattering his khakkhara staff and followed by three ruffians. He gazed at Tamakohime-sama's beauty while smiling, his poorly formed features bunched together, and blew his nose with a sharp sound.

Tenshinbō: 'I am a high-ranking priest and man of knowledge, the incarnation of the Heavenly Emperor and the heaven-sent savior, known as Tenshinbō.'

He spoke with a haughty air, baring his protruding teeth. Upon seeing him, Tamakohime-sama tried to suppress the pain of laughter that threatened to burst forth, but the more she looked, the more she realized he was the ugliest of ugly men, a man so thoroughly and meticulously crafted that even if one were to set him upon the female dogs in the alley, the courtship would surely fail. Much less, to think that he would brazenly come to pursue a human beauty and the hidden Dariyahime-sama—it was so absurd that she could not help but laugh, 'Ho-ho-ho-ho-tsu.' Tenshinbō became even more overbearing.

Tenshinbō: 'This is outrageous. For one who appears to be the mistress of this house to look at our face and laugh coldly without a single word of greeting—what is the meaning of this? I cannot fathom your heart, madam.'

Tamakohime-sama: 'Well, well, you who call yourself the incarnation of the Heavenly Emperor, for what purpose have you come? If you have business, please state it quickly. Judging by your appearance, you seem to be of another sect, and since our house follows a different faith, I humbly ask you to leave.'

Tenshinbō thought to himself... 'Haha, the little monk from before said this village follows the Ananahi-kyō, so I must pretend to be of the Ananahi-kyō...' and while feigning ignorance, he spoke.

Tenshinbō: 'You just said that our faiths differ, but in essence, such things as sects are merely matters of branches and leaves. God is originally one root; depending on the convenience of the era and the country, He manifests as a god or as a Buddha, and it is the true God who performs activities with complete freedom. Although I am dressed as a monk, in truth, I am a believer in the Ananahi-kyō. Among my disciples are Sendenshi such as Terukuniwake, Umekō, and Terukō, so although I do not know what sect you belong to, I ask you to listen to what I have to say for a moment.'

Tamakohime-sama: 'Ah, is that so? Since you claim to be the master of the famous Sendenshi Terukuniwake, what is your name? Once I have heard that, depending on the circumstances, I might listen to your story.'

Tenshinbō: 'As I stated before, I am the heaven-sent savior, the incarnation of the Heavenly Emperor, Shīgō-Yorikowake-no-Mikoto.'

Tamakohime-sama: 'Ho-ho-ho-ho. That is a name that sounds exactly like a bandit from Mount Ōra.'

Tenshinbō: 'This is inexcusable. I am a man of courage who confronted the bandits of Mount Ōra, brought the ringleaders Shīgō and Yoriko to their knees with my words, blew them away, and removed the calamities of Tenka, taking their names as my own in commemoration.'

§ 2

Tamakohime: 'Is that so? Oh, I have heard rumors that in Mount Aura there lived a mountain priest called Genshinbau, who was said to be the savior of Tenrai, an incarnation of Tentei. What has become of that Genshinbau? Surely he must have been destroyed by your Goshinriki, must he not?'

Koburai, while pulling repeatedly on the sleeve of Tenshinbau, said:

Koburai: 'If you please, Tenshinbau-sama, this will not do. Let us return quickly while the footing is still clear. This woman is no ordinary woman. If we dally, her true form will appear.'

Tenshinbau said in a low voice:

Tenshinbau: 'Do not talk nonsense. Since it was said that Dariya-hime is hiding in this house, I intend not to move from this spot until I have used one means or another to drag her out. If you wish to leave, you may leave as you please, Kisama.'

Tamakohime, having keenly overheard the whispering of the two, said:

Tamakohime: 'Ho ho ho, so you are indeed Genshinbau-sama of Mount Aura. In truth, Dariya-hime-sama, whom you are seeking, is resting in the inner parlor with a clever man named Barugi. They are a truly affectionate couple. Since they have repeatedly begged me to ensure that if a mountain priest called Genshinbau should come, I must not let him in, I must ask you to please return home. I am extremely busy with the preparations for dinner.'

Tenshinbau: 'Indeed, I am Genshinbau, the incarnation of Tentei. Though once, while on Mount Aura, I was incited by evil spirits and committed some misdeeds, there is a principle that one who is strong in evil is also strong in good; the Genshinbau of today is pure and undefiled, and is no longer the Genshinbau of the past. Therefore, having been granted the divine name Tenshinbau by Tentei, if you would allow me to stay for one night, it would serve as a prayer for your house, and there is no doubt that your descendants will prosper and your fortune will be perfect. I humbly beg for a night's lodging.'

Tamakohime: 'If that is the case, please wait a moment. I cannot decide this on my own; I will go and consult with my master.'

Saying this, she ran quickly into the back.

No matter how long they waited, she had gone in with a water bucket, bolted the door shut from the inside, and not even a single cat showed its face. Genshinbau stood at the gate and, in order to clear the master's doubts, raised his withered voice and began to sing in a solemn manner, chanting the propaganda song of the San-go-kyo:

Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, Whether the moon waxes or wanes, Even if the earth should sink, The power of Truth shall save the world. That which is called the power of Truth, From the very beginning of the creation of heaven and earth, Until the end of the ages, Protects this world alone. It is the power of the Chin of the one and only God-man in the universe. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-kamu, May the spirits be blessed, The one and only savior of Tenrai, The Mizutama appearing, In the sacred grounds of the Ubusunayama, Genshinbau, who was the incarnation, Of the Great God Kami-Susanoo-no-Ōkami, Let the master of this house understand in detail, That I am the incarnation of Tentei, Kannagara.

I humbly pray with reverence, That God may appear before you, And distinguish between good and evil. What, then, is God? The Creator who made, The infinite great universe, This is the true God. Now, God is formless, And as long as He remains without voice, No matter how much power He may possess, To protect this world just as it is, Is a task that is ultimately difficult. Therefore, the one prophet on earth, Chosen by God, Is determined to be the instrument of God, And in the spirit of Kami-Susanoo-no-Ōkami, He harbors the sacred spirit of Chin, The fleshly palace that has descended, This is the great pillar of the world, The pillar of God is Tenshinbau.

There is no mistake in this at all, Clear your doubts quickly, God is knocking at the gate, Wishing to bestow blessings upon this house, And to save you, both in spirit and body, Into the heavenly pure land. People with darkened hearts, Misjudge the pillar of God, And open the gate to welcome Him in, What foolishness, not knowing the etiquette. Regret later will be of no avail, Change your hearts quickly, And look with your two eyes, Take off the blue, red, and black glasses, And look at the face of my own body, Then your doubts will surely clear. For all incarnations, Are not faces like those of ordinary humans, They have the appearance of the five hundred Arhats or Fudo-sama, Or the faces of evil demons and Rakshasa.

To entice the hearts of men, it is that which manifests. No matter how beautiful the face may be, if you do not pay attention to the Magakami lurking within the heart, you will meet with a failure that will make you bite your navel in regret. Reflect upon this, Tamakohime. For the sake of the master of this house, Tamakiyowake, the Keshin makes this Senji. Ah, Kamunagara, Ahau. May the spirit be blessed and prosper.

The child of Kami peered at the figures of the two from the window, and hearing this song, burst into laughter, clapping small hands and singing along with the younger brother, Tamako.

A monk like a ghost has come, bringing a thief's Kobun along. Saying things like 'truth,' 'lies,' and 'Keshin,' he strings together sweet words. He deceives the father of this house, and stays overnight in this home. Sleeping or waking, in a dream or reality, he plots to take away the unforgettable Dariya-sama. He lines up a thousand lies, rolls his eyes, and points his beak with reckless abandon. While exhaling foul Iki, he pollutes the Kūki of the Yashiki. You had better resign yourself now. For Mangofumatsudai, the Monguchi shall not open for you. Coming here disguised as Ananai-kyō, what is this about doing sweet things? You are a Kuwanon that eats its own tail.

It would be better to go home quickly. Shall I draw water with a ladle and pour it over your head? Tuck your Shitsupo between your legs and leave as soon as possible. You do not have a face that should be chasing women. Go away quickly, you Binbōgami. Aha, aha, aha, aha, aha, Mother-sama, please take a look at that face for a moment. A frog in the rice paddy that failed to croak, an owl that missed its midnight meal. It is a style that cannot be described. Truly, a bizarre selling monk. Leave the mansion of Kami quickly, set sail with your tail, and be gone. After you have left, I must scatter three bales of salt and perform a grand cleaning from corner to corner. What a troublesome mountain monk has come.

Ihi, ihi, ihi, ihi, ihi.

With that, he thrust his chin out, poked his head from the window, and while tapping his small feet, withdrew into the back.

Koburai could not bear the absurdity, clicked his teeth with a 'Tsk,' and while glaring, walked out of the Monguchi at an angle. Tenshinbō also clicked his tongue with a 'Tsk' and ran out; the manservant who had been waiting quickly fastened the bolt of the gate.

The evening sun tilted, and the doors of darkness began to expand from all directions. Genshinbō, deep in his obsession, having heard that Dariya, whom he currently loves, is in this mansion, felt that even if he had to risk his life, he could not leave without achieving his goal. He sat down on a stone in front of the gate, folded his arms, and was lost in thought. Evening crows seeking their nests were crying out from above his head from the forest in the neighborhood, as if to mock him with their 'Ahau, ahau'.
