Chapter 4: 琴の綾 [1793]

琴の綾〔1793〕

§ 1

Tenshinbau, having been unceremoniously cast out beyond the gate of the Kamuyakata of Tamakiyowake, which was surrounded by a solid and impenetrable Takabei, felt his heart burn with uncontrollable jealousy at the thought of his beloved Dariya-hime, whom he currently adored, dreaming warmly and whispering intimately with the petty thief Balgie. Desperate to find a way to scale this wall, should he but have wings, to leap into their living quarters, claw at Balgie's face, drag him by his topknot, and vent his pent-up frustration, he groaned repeatedly, clenching his fists and striking his own thighs with reckless abandon. Seeing this state, Koburai found it impossibly comical.

Koburai: 'My, Tenshinbausama, Gokeshinsama, that is a most magnificent display of ferocity. It is no wonder, for your indignation is entirely justified. To have a beauty worth a thousand or two thousand ryō, even if sold for scrap, snatched away by the likes of Balgie, while you are cast out of the gate and left to watch with your fingers in your mouth—that is hardly a pleasant tale. Even I, were the times different, would not be without the desire to make that Dariya-hime my wife. There is something about the image of Dariya-hime that is gentle and endearing. I, too, feel as though I would like to hold those white hands once and speak with her of the sentiments of mountain clouds and sea moons. Whether viewed from the front or the side, that pure and simple heart, rich in religious fervor, elegant, noble, and enchanting, is manifest in those full, drooping cheeks of hers.'

Tenshinbau, letting out a heavy sigh, replied:

Tenshinbau: 'My appearance is naturally as you see, so it is not exactly what one would call love, but in truth, this did not arise from my own heart. All love comes from the gods, and marriage is the work of humans. Since I felt this sacred love commanded by the Lord God, my change of heart and this pilgrimage have been like lifting a foot in surprise after stepping on a flower; to the eyes of others, it must surely look like the height of idiocy, but this love commanded by the Lord must succeed no matter what. As for Genshinbau himself, even if the moon in the water cannot be grasped, I would be content to merely accept this situation with a single thought upon the shore, but no matter what I say, it is painful because the Lord God, my principal object of worship, does not permit it. My current feeling is exactly like a precious jewel shattering into dust and vanishing. Ah, is even a heavenly savior utterly useless when it comes to love?'

Koburai: 'Ha ha ha ha, Tenshinbausama, what a profound lamentation. ...Like a dragon or tiger at the start, like an escaping hare at the end... I truly perceive your heart and your circumstances, hee hee hee.'

Tenshinbau: 'Hey, Koburai, do not make a fool of me. The world is not only night; there is also day. No matter how deep one sinks into the abyss of unrequited love, there is a time to rise again, so do not look down on me so.'

Koburai: 'Rather than that, wait for the dawn, and when Dariya begins to stroll in the garden, you can at least catch a glimpse of her face through a hole in the fence and extinguish your smoldering passion.'

The mischievous child of the gods peeked stealthily through a knot-hole in the gate and saw two shadows in the dim light, keeping a distance of about one ken, absorbed in a conversation of sorrow.

The child of the gods: 'Hey, Tenshin-sama, what are you saying? Dariya-sama was startled to hear that you had come here to look for her, and just a moment ago, she fled toward the east with an old man through the back gate. I came to let you know because I felt sorry for you, keeping it a secret from her mother. If you want to meet Dariya, you had better go quickly; by now, she must be near the foot of Agoyama.'

Ten: 'What, you say Dariya has escaped? That is a serious matter, Soitsu.'

Kami no Ko: 'For your sake, it would be more convenient if Dariya had jumped outside rather than remaining surrounded by the Kinjiyōtetsupeki.'

Ten: 'Is that really true? You are not lying, are you?'

Kami no Ko: 'If it is a lie, then let it be a lie. I am kindly informing you despite my sleepiness, so you may do as you please, hihihi.'

Laughing as he spoke, he hid deep within the interior of the building. Afterwards, Tenshinbau folded his arms and remained lost in thought for a short while, then:

Ten: 'Hey, Koburai, what do you think? Could it be true? Perhaps he is just saying such things to drive us out because we are a nuisance.'

Koburai: 'Children are honest, you know. Even Dariya would not be able to remain at ease while Tenshinbau-sama is here. If I were Dariya, I would certainly run away.'

Ten: 'That is indeed reasonable. Come, Koburai, we cannot afford even half a moment of delay. Let us go.'

With these words, they rushed along the dimly lit mountain path, stumbling and falling, heading toward the Agoyama region.

Dariya-hime remained in the detached room, as calm as could be, determined to deceive this Balky until she could return to the port of Suga, and she was doing her best to win his favor by pouring him sake.

Dariya: 'My most beloved Balky-sama, is that fellow called Tenshinbau not a truly dull-witted monk? While you and I and Kiyowake-sama are here in this mansion, enjoying ourselves by drinking sake and savoring the future of our love, he has been driven out by the mistress of this house and is weeping outside the gate—does that not truly make one want to shed a tear of sympathy for him?'

Balky was flustered and said:

'W-what are you saying? Do you mean to say that you, Hime-sama, feel like shedding a tear of sympathy for Tenshinbau? That is a serious matter. If that is how you feel, Hime-sama, how can I be at ease? It is my resolve that I would not regret losing my life for your sake, Hime-sama...'

He turned pale.

Dariya: 'Hohoho, I am joking. It is merely a polite formality for the sake of the world. Even if my heart feels otherwise, I am a woman, so I cannot speak as bluntly as you men, can I?'

Balky: 'Hmm, I see, I understand. Eh, that makes me feel a little relieved. However, Hime-sama, is your vow to me not the same? If you do not like me, you must tell me bluntly while there is still time; otherwise, if we go as far as the final Dan-no-ura and you suddenly cast me aside, I shall be at a loss. No matter what, my face is not one suited for romance, so I cannot help but feel anxious.'

Dariya: 'Hohoho, please do not have such concerns. No matter how good a man's appearance is, if he lacks charm, he is useless. In this world, it is first rank, second money, and third appearance. No matter how bad one's looks are, or if one has no money, if one has charm, women will cling to him. I did not fall for you, Balky-sama, because of your looks or your money; even if I had desired money or looks, it would have been utterly useless. I fell for you because of the most essential beauty a lover must possess—your charm.'

Balky: 'D-do I really seem that charming? It seems you are quite fond of me, ehehehe.'

Dariya: 'Money and looks do not matter, / But I have fallen for his charm. / It is something like that, hohoho.'

Balky: 'Indeed, charm is everything, charm is everything. If I were to abandon a woman who has fallen for me to this extent, it would be a waste of my good fortune as a woman. I swear by Bonten, Taishaku, Jizaiten, and—oops, wait—the Great Kami of the San-go-kyo, I will never abandon you, Dariya-no-kimi, for ten thousand eons. Please, rest assured, my On-kaka-daimyōjin-sama.'

Baru feels as though she is already his wife, yet at the same time, he feels as though she is a noble young lady of another, untouchable and beyond his reach; he is caught in a strange psychological state, using coarse language one moment and polite language the next. Dariyahime takes the clavichord nearby, and while retuning the strings, she begins to sing a song she learned in childhood, which requires no particular preparation, to console her boredom.

Barugī, waving his hands in a flurry, said, 'No, no, no, that is not it, that is not it. I was just saying it as a Chiyotsuto joke. Even I know what a clavichord is; I am, after all, the one who has earned a reputation as a Meushiyu of Tenka.'

Dari: 'Naruhodo... they say that a cat that catches mice hides its claws, and one never knows what kind of Kakushigei a person might possess. The grace of pretending to know nothing while truly being a Meijin of the koto—that is the first thing I find charming about you. People in this world today are so eager to talk as if they know things even when they do not.'

Baru: 'Un, that is right. I understand well. No, I truly understand. It is elegant to pretend not to know even when one does. That is where the value of a Danshi lies, fully Fukuzai within. It is what is called the Bitoku of Kenson. The Bitoku of Kenson is the Yuen that constitutes one's Jinkaku. Though the Jisetsu and Tokiyo have led me to join a group of Dorobau, my origins are what they are.'

Dari: 'Ho ho ho, I do not know about that, but as a Kogashira of Dorobau, I suppose you cannot really discuss Jinkakumondai. That aside, as a return for my performance, I ask you to sing a song and play the chords, and let me hear a Hodoyo tone.'

Baru: 'Uun, depending on the koto and the quality, it is not that I cannot play, but I shall reserve that until the time I sing the waves of the four seas with you. Otherwise, it would not be called a Kakushigei. It is a scheme to make your parents and siblings say "Ah!"'

Dari: 'Ho ho ho, what a Hodoyo greeting that is. Now, at this moment, you look as if you are dead drunk and about to fall asleep—is that your ambition? Looking at your fingertips, there is no trace of you having handled chords, is there? Look at my fingers; I have hard calluses just like this.'

Baru: 'Mine, you see, is called Sugaki, where I play only with the tips of my nails. That has become my specialty. Since my nails grow, I cut them often, so there are no calluses on my fingertips now. Well, wait without doubt. Mine is something I perform before the Lord God or such, so... shiyatsuchin, shiyatsuchin, shiyatsuchin-chin... see, it is a truly fine tone.'

Dari: 'Playing shiyatsuchin, shiyatsuchin before the Lord God, that must be Yakumo, surely.'

Baru: 'Whether it is Yakumo or Kogumo, it is still a koto, is it not?'

Dari: 'Ah, in that case, please borrow this Yakumo-koto, assume that I am a goddess of peace, and try playing it once.'

Barugī, scratching his head incessantly, said, 'Ya, Dari-dono, the truth is, it is a lie, a lie. I have never even held a koto. For this alone, I must offer my humble apologies.'

Dari: 'Ho ho, hearing that, I am relieved. A koto is something for women to toy with. It is only the musicians who play the koto. Musicians are always poor, and during festivals, they are given a seat off to the side, shivering with runny noses. There is nothing good about such people. A Danshi is best when he prefers rougher things; I cannot say how much more splendid that is.'

Baru: 'He he he, Naruhodo, quite right. I understand. That is so. Just as you say and as I hear, when it comes to rough things, I climb over high walls, draw my long sword, and leap into a wealthy house. The moment I shout "Hey!", everyone, this one and that one, shrinks in fear, tosses out the gold they saved more dearly than their own lives, and joins their hands in prayer. That is quite impressive, is it not?'

Dari: 'You were a Dorobau after all, were you not? I do not like having a husband who is a Dorobau.'
