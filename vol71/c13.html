詰腹〔1802〕

§ 1

In the detached room of the Samori-no-Tsukasa mansion, the sliding doors are tightly shut, and Umori and Samori are deeply engrossed in a secret conversation.

Umori: 'Samorisama, I was greatly concerned about you following the great fire on Hirokōji the other day; have there been any changes since then? I am very worried that thieves might have taken advantage of the confusion to sneak into your mansion.'

Samori: 'No, thank you for your kindness. Ah, I do not wish to grow any older. I am here as the Samori-no-Tsukasa, like an ornament in an alcove, but I am merely fretting in my heart and it is all to no avail. When I was in the valleys of Tanigukuyama, I spent ten years racking my brains, thinking how I could become the Samori again and reform the national government, doing this and doing that, but when it comes to actual practice, things do not go as smoothly as one might think. I feel as though I am still sharp in my own mind, but it seems I have become senile without realizing it.'

Umori: 'Samorisama, what are you saying? Your reputation is immensely popular. I, Umori, am also allowed to serve my duties thanks to your prestige, even if I am somewhat crooked in my ways, and I am sure it must be tiresome for you to see only my shortcomings. The King is still young, and if we do not have Samorisama exerting yourself, the country of Tarahan will surely not be able to hold up.'

Samori: 'With a wise King and a brilliant person like you, the pillars of the country of Tarahan will surely not waver. I am old, my spirit is willing but my body does not move as I wish; it is a troublesome thing. I feel truly sorry to leave all government affairs to you, but they say that one should buy the hardships of youth, so please, do your best. I am just an ornament in the alcove.'

Umori: 'What are you saying? What could we green youths do? Everything is being managed one way or another under your command, Samorisama. By the way, Samorisama, regarding the thief who snuck into the mansion on the night of the great fire at Hirokōji, I interrogated him yesterday, but he said such extraordinary things that I suspended the interrogation and locked him in prison; however, he keeps bringing up your name, Samorisama, and I am truly troubled in front of the retainers. What should I do?'

Samori: 'Who on earth is this thief who brings up my name, Samori?'

Umori: 'He claims to be a savior sent from heaven, an incarnation of the Heavenly Emperor, a celestial being from the First Spirit Kingdom, named Tenshinbō. And since he insists that he is a sworn brother of yours, Samorisama, I thought I would ask you before proceeding with the interrogation, which is why I have come to see you specifically.'

Samori looks troubled and says,

Samori: 'Umori-dono, that is likely a madman. In any case, I shall interrogate him tomorrow. Please ensure that no one else comes.'

Umori: 'Yes, I understand. Furthermore, two other thieves are also using your name, Samori-dono, just like Tenshinbō, and are demanding to see their boss, Samori.'

Samori: 'What were the names of those two thieves?'

Umori: 'Yes, one is called Koburai, and the other is called Kōro.'

Samori: 'Strange, in the rocky cave of Tanigukuyama...'

He starts to say, then suddenly cuts his words short,

Samori: 'I have been hiding from the world deep in the mountains of Tanigukuyama for a long time, so I do not know the situation, and I cannot tell who might have seen my face and is now impersonating me. In any case, Umori-dono, could you quietly bring those three thieves to my mansion? There are things I wish to investigate in private...'

Umori: 'If it is your command, Samorisama, I shall bring them even if it means going against the law.'

Hidari: 'Although it is the rule to investigate at the Shirasu, this Samori is, as you know, an old man, and my legs will not hold out, so I wish to conduct the investigation at my own mansion. As Samori is the Saishiyō of the country, it should make no difference whether I am investigated at my house or at the Shirasu. Such precedents have occurred many times since the Jidai of the Senō.'

Migi: 'I have made a terrible Shitsugen. In that case, I shall bring him here tomorrow, so I ask that you investigate him thoroughly. Sayō nara.'

He spoke these words with Ingin Aisatsu and returned to his own mansion. Afterwards, Samori leaned against his Kefusoku, and while heaving a Toiki, he spoke in Hitorigoto.

'Ah, there is nothing more Haka than the Yukusue of a Ningen. I endured the Kanku of Guwashinshiyōtan for Jifunen to achieve my Mokuteki with him, became Samori again to reform the Kokusei, and thought I had reached a position to participate in the political center of the new Prince's government; yet in this world of constant strife, Genshinbō, who is skilled in cunning and evil, has entered as a thief and must have even laid out my old secrets to Migimori. Ah, how pathetic. How can I possibly maintain my position today? This has become a troublesome matter. I never intended to become the leader of thieves from my heart, but it was my own lack of judgment that I chose any means necessary out of concern for the Tarahan country. And regarding this reform of the Kokusei, my two hundred subordinates have only been a hindrance; not one of them has been of any help. Ah, if one does not walk the path of justice and public morality, the end of the world cannot be achieved.'

Samori thought of his past and his Yukusue, and he spent the night without getting a wink of sleep until dawn.

The crows left their nests and sang of peace happily, and the sparrows chirped pleasantly on the eaves. Samori watched them and again spoke in Hitorigoto,

'Ah, why was I not born as that crow? The way it soars freely through the vast sky, back and forth, left and right, without any hesitation, is just like a heavenly being. The sparrow releases its innocent voice and sings of the eternal ages. Despite that, why is it that I, a Ningen born as a living shrine of God, have such deep suffering?'

Just as he was letting out a Toiki, Genshinbō, Koburai, and Kōro, guarded by prison officers, came swinging their arms through the back gate to the garden in front of Samori's living room. Upon seeing the figure of Genshinbō, Samori was so shocked that he nearly fainted, but he pulled himself together and said,

'Oh, prison officers, thank you for your hard work. I, Samori, will take charge of these three. Please return quickly.'

Prison Officer: 'Hai.'

Saying this, the prison officers quickly left the scene. Seeing that there was no one nearby, Genshinbō, without any hesitation or reserve, jumped up onto the floor, sat cross-legged in front of Samori, and stared silently at Samori's face. Both Koburai and Kōro also sat cross-legged on either side of Genshinbō, waiting in a casual manner.

Samori: 'Hey, aren't you Genshinbō? Where have you been wandering? And did you get your hands on Dariya-hime? Tell me how things have progressed since then.'

Genshinbō: 'Ha-ha-ha-ha, Dariya does not matter, but hey brother, your intuition was quite sharp, wasn't it? Thanks to having a beautiful daughter, you have risen to become the Saishiyō of a country, so it would be fine to treat me a little. When I heard about the fire in Tarahan City the other day, I thought your house was in danger, so I headed there to help with these two, but those ignorant soldiers mistook us for thieves and threw us into prison. You must have heard of my predicament, so isn't it a bit too selfish of you to act as if you know nothing? And that young fool Migimori dared to speak rudely to me. How about it, brother? For the sake of our brotherhood, won't you listen to what I have to say?'

Samori: 'What on earth do you expect me to do?'

Genshinbau: 'It is nothing else; I want you to dismiss that Umori and replace him with this Genshinbau, or if that is not possible, I want you to become the Umori and recommend me as the Samori; I want you to grant one of these two requests.'

Samori: 'I would grant you anything else, but to recommend you, who have been a thief on Mount Ōra, to the office of Samori would be utterly impossible. It is certain that His Highness would never permit it.'

Genshinbau opened his mouth wide and laughed loudly,

Genshinbau: 'Ha ha ha ha, hey brother, what are you saying? I am the great boss of three thousand thieves on Mount Ōra, and you are merely the boss of two hundred small-time thieves, are you not? Is there any reason why the boss of two hundred can become the Samori, while the great boss of three thousand cannot? Is that not a rather selfish piece of logic?'

Samori said 'Hmm,' and remained silent with his head hanging down. Koburai inched his knees forward,

Koburai: 'Look here, Oyabun, you said that if you achieved your goal, you would use me as a senior retainer, did you not? Hey, Kōro, you are the same, aren't you? We have been told this like a daily routine every single day. I do not particularly want to be a thief, but since I have faithfully followed the orders of my boss, is it not natural that when the boss succeeds, we also succeed?'

Kōro: 'Hmm, that is exactly right. Look here, Oyabun, or rather, Samori-dono, you will buy this thin-skinned excuse, will you not?'

Samori: 'I certainly should have made that promise to you all. However, I regret that today I cannot carry out that promise. Even though you came to support my mansion, you hid from the eyes of the guards, snuck in through the back gate, and tried to break the locks of the treasure house; therefore, in anyone's eyes, you can only be recognized as thieves. Today, I recognize you all as criminals. Peace of mind is a common thing, but the laws of the Tarahan country cannot be bent. Although the law dictates that all three of you should be sentenced to death, I will overlook it out of brotherhood and the bond between master and servant. Now, you had better hide yourselves from the back gate as soon as possible. If you linger in Tarahan Castle any longer, you will be captured again, and if that happens, it will be beyond my power to help you.'

Genshinbau: 'Eh, it cannot be helped, I will go home quietly today, but Samori, you must have saved up quite a lot of money, could you not give me some as a souvenir? I cannot go anywhere without money, you see.'

Samori: 'If that is the case, it cannot be helped; I will give you all the money in that storehouse you tried to sneak into, so take it and hide yourselves quickly. I will take care of the rest somehow.'

Genshinbau: 'Ha, to tell you the truth, as you suspected, that was the money I wanted. You are indeed my brother.'

Koburai: 'Ha, as expected of our boss... if we only have money, we do not need names or ranks, do we?'

Kōro: 'Oyabun, thank you. In that case, we will divide it among the three of us without reserve and return home.'

From this point, the three of them stuffed their bodies with yellow-gold coins and, sewing through the dense forest where the leaves grew thick from the back gate, hid themselves somewhere.

Later, Samori took out some writing paper and left a note addressed to the King and Queen, as well as to the Umori, in beautiful calligraphy; he then dressed in white, and in front of the shrine where the Great God of Sango is worshipped, he cut his abdomen and met a magnificent end.

The Umori, who knew nothing of such matters, visited Samori's mansion again to see how things were, and when he went to the detached room without announcement, he found Samori soaked in red and lying dead.

And there, three suicide notes were written. Arina, without waiting to take anything else, broke the seal of the one addressed to him and read it down, which was as follows:

1. Regarding myself, having been favored by His Highness the King and having achieved my long-held desire to participate in the government, today the ruffians Genshinbau, Koburai, and Kōro spoke rudely to me, the Samori, but I had no authority to punish them, and I was forced to give them money and let them escape. Such an action by the Samori is a great crime of ignoring and trampling upon the laws issued by His Highness the King, and I have no qualification to remain in this position any longer; as I am a great criminal against the state, I have cut my abdomen and ended my life to apologize to both Their Highnesses, the Umori-dono, and the general public. I pray that from now on, you will assist His Highness and strive with all your might to make the foundation of the Tarahan country ever more solid.

Great Criminal of the State, Shiyakanna

To Umori-dono

It was written thus.

Arina, upon seeing this, was shocked, but feigned ignorance, went to the castle to report the details of the circumstances to both Their Highnesses, and presented the two suicide notes. Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haemaese.

コオ『親分有難う、そんなら遠慮なしに三人分配して帰ります』

これより三人は山吹色の小判をしこたま身につけ裏門より木の葉茂れる密林を縫ふて、何処ともなく姿を隠した。後に左守は料紙を取りよせ、筆の跡も麗々しく国王、王妃両殿下を初め右守に当てたる書置を残し、自分は白装束となつて、三五の大神の祭りある神前にて腹掻き切り立派な最後を遂げた。

かかる事とは夢露知らぬ右守の司は、様子如何にと再び左守の館を訪ひ、案内もなく離棟の座敷に行つて見れば左守は紅に染つて縡れて居た。そして其処に三通の遺書が認めてあつた。アリナは取るものも取りあへず自分宛のを封押し切つて読み下せば左の通りであつた。

一、拙者事、国王殿下のお見出しに預り日頃の願望を達し、国政に参与の栄を担ひ居り候処、今日玄真坊、コブライ、コオロの無頼漢、左守たる拙者に向ひ無礼の言を吐き候も、これを咎むるの権威なく、止むを得ず金銭を与へて逃げ帰らし申候。斯の如き左守の処置は国王殿下の発布されたる法律を無視し、且蹂躙せる大罪にして、到底此儘職に留まるべき資格なく、国家の大罪人なれば、両殿下を初め、右守殿其他国民一般に対し謝罪のため、皺腹切つて相果て申候。今後は何卒々々殿下を輔け奉り、タラハン国の基礎を益々鞏固ならしむべく、奮励努力あらむ事を願ひ申候

国家の大罪人　シヤカンナより

右守殿　参る

と認めてあつた。アリナは之を見るより驚き乍らもわざと素知らぬ顔を装ひ、城中に参内して両殿下に事の顛末を詳細に言上し、二通の遺書を捧呈した。あゝ惟神霊幸倍坐世。
