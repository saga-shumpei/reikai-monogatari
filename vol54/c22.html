Chapter 22: 凱旋 [1408]

凱旋〔1408〕

§ 1

At the sound of the door being knocked incessantly, the two servants of Temes thought it must be that the Brahmin foot soldiers had come to requisition something again, and exchanging glances, determined not to open it carelessly; no matter how much it was knocked, they uttered not a peep, and carefully braced the great gate from the inside beneath the crossbar.

Harukuniwake, having no other choice, began to chant a Sendenka in a loud voice audible within the estate.

Harukuniwake: 'The Sumeōkami who appeared in Takaamahara, while causing His Mikokoro to grieve in a thousand ways, created Tenchi; yet because Adaru no Hiko and Ebahime disobeyed the teachings of Kami, their tsumitoga turned into jiyaki, coagulated and hardened to become demons, and ended up as Yamataorochi, shikogitsune, and a hundred mamono, which dwell upon Chijiyō, clouding and defiling the spiritual nature of hitobito, and making the bodies of the precious persons born as the divine children into the dwelling places of Magatsu; the divine pillars of the Brahmin religion, the crooked deities of Ōkuninushi, obeying the command of the chin, attack the precious sacred land of the Ubusuna, and the great army of the Brahmin religion,

at the strategic stronghold of Kajika Pass, by the Kototama of Harukuniwake, the proselytizing envoy of the Anankyō, smashed and put to flight, pitched camp at the foot of the forests of Ukiki or Mount Victor, and having exhausted every conceivable atrocity and lawlessness, just at that very moment, the proselytizing envoy who is the messenger of Kami, the party of Harukuniwake, having appeared once again in this very place, scattered the ugly army and saved the nation of Biku;

Onibaruwake and Kumihiko, following more than three thousand horsemen, scattered like clouds and mist, established their dwelling in the caverns of Mount Ikura, pillaging the houses of near and far, seizing people, and performing deeds of lawlessness and cruelty—

hearing that such things were done, we, wishing to remove the harms of the people of the world, by the divine oracle of Konohanahime, came hurrying all this way in order to save the two daughters of this house and Michiharuwake, yet the careless gatekeepers mistook us for the enemy, and refusing with all their might, how lamentable it is that they do not open the gate;

O Temes, master of this house, you are without doubt Harukuniwake, the proselytizing envoy of the Anankyō; pray open this gate swiftly, even within a single breath;

Ah, trulying Kami, Kami, pledging to Kami, we proclaim and transmit the Kototama free from falsehood.'

At this song, the two gatekeepers exchanged glances, and while wrapped in a cloud of half-belief, leaving one to guard the gate, the other proceeded toward the living room of Temes.

Although three days had passed since Michiharuwake of Anankyō, together with the head clerk Shiina, had gone forth resolutely to rescue the two daughters, Temes and his wife Berishina had received no news whatsoever, and thus they had been worried, incessantly praying to Kami or drawing sacred lots; just then, Biku, the gatekeeper, came running in in a flurry,

Biku: 'I report to my master. At the front gate, I know not whether they be friend or foe, but they are chanting in a loud voice, saying they are of the Anankyō or Harukuniwake, and have come to save the daughters of this place; what shall we do? Thinking it would be terrible if we opened it carelessly and they turned out to be the fellows of the Brahmin, I tried refusing them as much as I could refuse, but by myself I cannot tell good from evil at all. Pray, master, although it is troublesome for you, would you not investigate?'

Temes: 'What, do you say that Harukuniwake-sama of the Anankyō has appeared? There can be no mistake about that. Come what may, let us go right up to the gateway and consider it.'

Putting Biku before him, holding a long sword tucked at his waist, Temes appeared with brisk steps and in a loud voice:

Temes: 'What person from where are you, O honored one who lingers at my gate just now and causes us to chant? If you let me hear your honorable name, I shall open this gate.'

Mankō, having caught this sound, in a loud voice:

Mankō: 'I indeed am Mankō, a retainer of Harukuniwake, the proselytizing envoy of the Anankyō. Open this gate for us as quickly as a single moment.'

Harukuni: 'I am one called Harukuniwake, and am by no means a suspicious person. By the divine oracle of Yoshikohime-sama, my disciple Michiharuwake set out toward Mount Ikura in order to recover the two young ladies of this household who were seized by the Brahmin army; and I, in order to save the lives of all, have come all this way without a moment's delay. Open this gate for us as quickly as a single moment.'

Temes hearing the voice of Harukuniwake from within the gate, and hearing a voice of dignity from nowhere in particular, and feeling that it could by no means be a deception, quietly unfastened the bolt, threw the gate wide open, and peeking out with fear and trembling, saw four Sendenshi standing there.

Temes joyfully bowed politely, and

Temes: 'Are you indeed Harukuniwake-sama whom I have been longing for? Please excuse my rudeness. Now, please come right in.'

Harukuniwake: 'Yes, thank you. Then I shall take the liberty.'

So saying, the party of four passed through the great gate.

Temes: 'Hey Biku, since the Brahman's fellows might come at any time, close this gate securely. And you, guard this place.'

So ordering, he took the lead of the party and guided them to the interior. Harukuniwake was invited into the couple's living room, and while being offered tea, with brief greetings,

Harukuniwake: 'I hear that the two young ladies of your honorable house have both been carried off to the Brahman's stronghold on Mount Inokura.'

Temes: 'Yes, thank you very much. The truth of the matter is that three days ago, a splendid Sendenshi named Michiharuwake, saying he was a disciple of Harukuniwake, came along, disguised himself in military uniform together with Shiina the head clerk, and said he would bring back the two of them, but up to now there has been no news at all, so the two of us as husband and wife are worried. Would it not be possible for you to save them by your divine power?'

Harukuniwake: 'Ah, I have come in haste concerning that very matter. If we dally too much, they are said to be thrown into some deep pit, so their lives would be in danger. I intend from here on, without a moment's delay, to head for Mount Inokura, launch the Kototama of a thousand horsemen, make the enemy surrender, and successfully rescue the four and return. Since I have firm conviction, please do not worry at all, absolutely not.'

Both Temes and Berisina,

'Yes, thank you very much.'

Joining their hands, unable even to raise their voices for tears, they wept and prostrated themselves.

From this point, the party of Harukuniwake left the gate of Temes, abandoning themselves to their feet, and as the sun approached the western mountains, they quickened their pace and advanced.

Flying across valleys and traversing among rocks, protected by the grace of the gods, they finally reached a dark forest even by day, along a path of pitch darkness where black and white could not be distinguished, yet taking this as good fortune and holding their breath, they finally arrived in front of the cave. As it was nighttime, numerous soldiers had all cast off their armor and were sprawled inside tents or temporary huts. Horses were tethered to trees here and there and neighing vigorously. The party of Harukuniwake quickly put on the military uniforms discarded here and there, blessed by the darkness, and with innocent faces, suppressing their footsteps, paying attention to all sides, proceeded along a long tunnel. Miraculously, just at this hour, by divine protection, the path in the pitch-dark night was clearly visible to their eyes.

Since some human voice was coming from a cave nearby, as they pressed their ears against the rock wall and listened, Kishunwake was gathering Kumehiko and other staff members and holding a whispered consultation.

Kishun: 'Kumehiko-dono, is there such a thing as casually throwing precious beauties into a place like that? Is there no way to somehow save them?'

Kumehiko: 'It is utterly hopeless, I suppose. Since today is the second day, they are surely dead.'

Kishun: 'Are you not doing a cruel thing unsuited to you? While you are one who was lectured by Princess Karna, saying that the future is terrifying, and even saved Queen Victoria, why do you do such a cruel thing as to take people's lives?'

Kumehiko: 'The truth of the matter is that I threw them in with iron plates built around the bodies of the four, so they should not have suffered any injuries. And those iron plates are shaped like tubs, with thick ropes running through them. No matter how deep the bottom of a well is, as long as one handles the rope, they can be easily rescued. Fortunately, there is a restorative medicine here gushing from the rock, so if they are made to take this, even if their breath has stopped for two or three days, recovery should be all right.'

久米『ヤア其方は番兵ではないか、最前から吾々の話を立ち聞き致して居つたのだな。不都合千万の奴だ。サア一時も早く彼方に立去れ』

治国『拙者は三五教の宣伝使治国別の一行で厶る。拙者の徒弟道晴別を初めシーナ、及スミエル、スガールが大変なお世話になつたさうだ。一言お礼を申さなくてはならないと思ひ、態々お尋ね申しましたのだ。アハハハハハ』

久米『イヤ、これはこれは治国別様で厶いましたか。何卒言霊は一寸暫くお見合せを願ひます。唯今直にお渡し申しますから、一寸此処に待つて居て下さいませ』

治国『イヤイヤ決して決して左様な御心配は入り申さぬ。拙者が自ら救ひ出さねばならぬ義務が厶る。貴方方はマア悠りと御休息をなされませ。四人の所在はビクトル山から既に霊眼で見て置きました』

久米『イヤどうも治国別様の慧眼には恐れ入りました。今日限りバラモンの将軍職をやめますから、どうぞ命計りは御救助を願ひます』

万公『先生、こんな事云つて又計略にかけるのですよ。四人放り込みやがつた穴へ鬼春別も久米彦も何奴も此奴も放り込んでやりませうか、アハハハハ、面白い面白い、エヘン。どんなものだ、鬼春別、久米彦両将軍、この万公が現はれた以上は到底駄目だぞ』

松彦『万公、何を云ふのだ。お前は篏口令を布かれて居るぢやないか』

万公『万公末代云はない心算だつたが、あまりむかづくのでつい口が辷りました。それよりも早く四人を救ひ出さうぢやありませぬか……これや久米彦、貴様が放り込んだのだから貴様が救ひ出して来い。万一一人でも命がなくなつて居たら、先生が何と仰有つても、この万公が承知せぬぞ。ヘン馬鹿にして居やがる、将軍も何もあつたものか。先生の前に来たら猫に出会うた鼠のやうな塩梅式ぢやないか。醜態を見やがれ、イヒヒヒヒ』

松彦『万公さま又忘れたのか、エエ久米彦殿早く案内なされ』

万公『これや早く案内を致さぬか、何を愚図々々して居るのか、そして鬼春別はどこに居るのか』

と虎の威をかる糞喰ひの狐のやうに無暗矢鱈に噪いで居る。竜彦は此の間に天眼通により四人の所在を知り、手早く一人々々を穴の底から引き上げ、鎮魂を施し、漸く四人共息を吹きかへさせた。

茲に鬼春別、久米彦両将軍は土下座をしながら、慄ひ慄ひ治国別に罪を謝した。治国別はいろいろと誠の教を説き諭し、且つ鬼春別、久米彦、スパール、エミシの高級武官に一人づつ態と背負はしめ、凱歌を奏しつつ一先づ玉木村のテームスの家をさして神恩を感謝しながら帰り行く。
