Chapter 13: Rock 情 [1399]

岩情〔1399〕

§ 1

On the summit of Mount Inokurayama, there is a gigantic wild boar-shaped rock formation known as Ihakura. It was from this that the name Inokurayama originated. Above the fifth station, the mountain is entirely fortified with rock, while below the fifth station lies a dense, eerie jungle. Furthermore, this rock has entrances to grottoes here and there, and it was rumored that their interiors extended for several ri, with large bats dwelling within in great numbers.

Inside these grottoes, pure water gushes forth in various places, leaving no shortage of water whatsoever. Moreover, an oil resembling sweet dew oozes from the rocks here and there, and as long as one merely tastes this without performing excessive labor, one's physical strength will not decline for two or three months—such is this divinely bestowed grotto.

The two generals Oniharuwake and Kumehiko had their subordinate soldiers advance deep into the grotto for exploration, and as a result of the investigation, they learned that no particularly terrifying ferocious beasts dwelt there. Therefore, they firmly determined this place to be their stronghold, constructed hastily made barracks below the fifth station, and barricaded themselves with a mountain stream as their boundary.

As long as they remained within this grotto, no matter how much divine power Kunikubewake possessed, it would surely be impossible for him to overthrow it; it might well be many times more secure, and also wider, than the grotto of Mount Daiumuzan.

Pouring their full effort into making this place their eternal dwelling, the generals had rock cut and expanded, engaged in various miscellaneous tasks, and selected those among the three thousand soldiers who were dexterous with chisels to excavate the grotto day and night.

In front of the cave entrance stood a hastily made office, firmly guarded by the Spāru and Emishi colonels.

In a somewhat spacious living room near the center of the grotto, Generals Oniharuwake and Kumehiko were tilting cups of wine collected from around the region and indulging in reminiscences.

Oniharuwake: 'Kumehiko-dono, now that we have taken up positions in such an impregnable fortress, we are safe at last; yet what I consider a thousand-year regret is that we let both Hiruna and Karuna escape. Is there not some scheme by which we might somehow win them back?'

Kumehiko: 'Well, if we were to stake our lives on it, it might not be impossible to win them back, but since a lion appears to be protecting that woman just as before, it will probably be somewhat difficult.'

Oniharuwake: 'No matter what is said, she is a dazzling beauty, so I naturally thought she was no ordinary person. For the most part, she must have been the incarnation of some deity. Ah, what a foolish sight I made. Kumehiko, because you lacked alertness, the jewel in our palm was taken away. My nose was twisted, my face was scratched, ah, and my dignity as a general is zero.'

Kumehiko: 'No matter what is said, since you took the lead and had your soul stolen by a beauty, for me to pine away is, so to speak, tantamount to following the education of Your Excellency, so there is nothing that can be done about it.'

Oniharuwake: 'Do not talk nonsense. Was it not Your Lordship who first dragged Karuna into the camp?'

Kumehiko: 'It is needless to speak of things that have passed; rather, what about those two, Sumieru and Sugaru, whom we plundered this time—how would it be to somehow persuade them and temporarily make them substitutes for Hiruna and Karuna?'

Oniharuwake: 'Ah, I have had enough of women. It is fine to just make them cook rice.'

Kumehiko: 'If so, let us have both of them cook rice, and since you have had enough of women, I shall decide to take both of them.'

Oniharuwake: 'Ah, that will not do. If Your Lordship is to do as you please, I shall also do as I please.'

Oniharuwake: 'Sugaru is a beauty second only to Princess Karuna, while Sumieru is a relatively ugly woman. Such selfish behavior will not do, surely.'

Kumehiko: 'If so, how would it be to leave it to the freedom of both persons and let them make their choice?'

Oniharu: 'Ah, that would be good. Then let us call Sugaru and ask her... which of us she likes best. And whichever one Sugaru says she likes, that one shall set her free. To force her would not be amusing, nor is it manly.'

Kume: 'That would indeed be amusing. Kumehiko nodded in assent, left this living room, reached the next cave, and strictly ordered a man named Lieutenant Sam to drag Sugaru to the general's living room. The lieutenant answered 'Yes' and hurried his steps to the single room of the cave where Sugaru was locked away. The two generals stripped off their military uniforms, changed into civilian clothes, tidied up their faces, and so on, engaging in a competition of handsome men, waiting with eager impatience.

After a while, Sugaru was timidly escorted by the lieutenant, came to the general's living room, and was trembling violently. Oniharuwasu broke into a wide grin,

Oniharu: 'Hey, Sugaru, you must be finding things quite inconvenient too. This here is the general who commands the whole army, and the man here is likewise a general. It seems a bad fellow among our subordinates brought you to such a place, and you are truly a pitiable person. Both of us are going to great lengths trying to find some way to send you back to your parents' home, but no matter what, at the foot of this mountain the forces of the Anankyō are surging by the tens of thousands, so it is perilous and we cannot manage to send you off yet. For the time being, it would be best to wait for the opportune time right here. And if there is anything you find inconvenient, we will listen to any request at all, so you should speak without reserve.'

Sugaru: 'Yes, such unexpected kindness—I am deeply grateful.'

Kumehiko had all the things most likely to please a woman said to her first by Oniharuwasu, leaving nothing for himself to say, and he fell into deep thought with a painful heart, wondering what on earth he should do. It seemed to him that Oniharuwasu had designs on Sugaru, and he was beside himself with anxiety,

Kume: 'Ah, you, Sugaru, a famous beauty even in the village of Tamaki. To truly fall into the hands of villains and come to such a place—how pitiable you are! I too am drowning in tears of sympathy. Somehow, I want to send you back to the village of Tamaki at the earliest possible moment, but as the general just said, enemy troops are surrounding us, so you must bear with it here for a little while. If you were not captured by the Brahman army, you would be captured by the Anan Army. Thinking of that, you are truly fortunate. I fully intend to drive away the enemy, so believe whatever we say and wait happily. Right, Sugaru? Even though I look like this, I am a quite kind man.'

Sugaru: 'Yes, both of you lords have spoken so kindly to me. Please treat me favorably.'

Oniharu: 'Hey, Sugaru, which of us generals do you think is the kinder man? That is something I would like to hear.'

Sugaru: 'Yes, both of your lordships are deeply compassionate gentlemen. However, I know not why, but the gentleman who spoke kind words to me first is the one who makes me happy.'

Oniharu: 'Ahahaha, does that mean you have taken a liking to this bearded face?'
