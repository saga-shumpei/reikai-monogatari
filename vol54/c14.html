Chapter 14: Darkness Cavern [1400]

暗窟〔1400〕

§ 1

Oniharuwake crossed his arms, a look of disappointment and discouragement floating upon his face, and was sunk in some meditation. It was Kumehiko who came cheerfully thither.

Kume: 'General-dono, General-dono.'

Roused with a start by the calling voice,

Oniharu: 'Ya, Kumehiko-dono, how was it?'

Kume: 'Well now, she is a shrew of a woman with whom nothing can be done either this way or that, and I was truly put to it. Having no other choice, I cast her into the deepest dark cavern. Assuredly by now she must have perished.'

Oniharu: 'That is a regrettable thing to have done. And what have you made of her sister, Sumiel?'

Kume: 'That wretch too I bound with rough hempen ropes and cast together into the dark cavern. Still, it was a most comforting business, ha, ha, ha, ha.'

Oniharu: 'Ya, that is a regrettable thing to have done. Yet somehow I feel ill at ease here. I was just thinking of calling once at your private apartment. Since I have some consultation to make from now on, let us go as far as your room.'

Kumehiko, while keeping the two sisters concealed in his own room, had lied that he had cast them into a dark chasm and put them to death; therefore, if Oniharuwake were to come, there was fear of an immediate exposure. Alas, a troublesome business has come to pass... he thought, but as he was a hardened rogue, he purposely put on an unconcerned face,

Kume: 'For you to condescend to come to the filthy house of such persons as ourselves is truly overwhelming. Might it not be possible to have you hear of it in your own private apartment?'

Oniharu: 'Nay, my room is filled only with men, and it is inconvenient in every way. Should I go to your room, you have even two women's hands ready, so I thought it would be truly most convenient, and therefore I said I would borrow your room.'

Kumehiko flushed scarlet with a start... Oniharuwake has somehow perceived that the two are hidden in my room, hasn't he; this is terrible... while making his heart leap, he purposely feigned ignorance,

Kume: 'Ha, ha, ha, ha, General-dono is a person who is exceedingly suspicious. Since we too are leaders of the Brahman army, we do nothing of such cowardly sorts. I would like you by all means not to misjudge character.'

Oniharu: 'Ha, ha, ha, ha, up to now I had been misjudging your character. Today I have at length come to understand the degree of your character. Since you speak thus, in order to clear away my suspicion, let us have your room opened and shown once.'

Kume: 'My room is within my rights. However superior an officer you may be, it will not do to search it. For this alone I flatly refuse.'

Oniharu: 'Nay, whatever may be said, by virtue of my rights I will carry out a search of the room.'

So saying, he steadily slipped through the tunnel and advanced toward Kumehiko's room.

Kumehiko... if it is exposed now of all times, either a disturbance will arise, or else I shall lose my head. Better yet, shall I dispatch Oniharuwake from behind at a single stroke? No, no, the General also has numerous stalwart retainers. I could hardly lay hands on him carelessly; yet if my room is peered into, it will be exposed in an instant. Alas, what would be best to do... suppressing the anguish of his heart pressing upon him moment by moment, he followed stealthily in concealment.

Oniharuwake had already arrived at the entrance of the door. Then, pressing his ear against the door, he was pondering the state of affairs within. The sisters Sumiel and Sugaru, knowing not a whit of such matters, while wetting their sleeves with tears as they lamented the fate of their parents and their own misfortune, were praying with all their might: 'Ban-gu Ōkami, save us, help us.'

Since Oniharuwake did not possess the key, he could not very well open it and enter; moreover, to order his subordinates to open it would run the risk of lowering his own character and reputation, so he, having become a slave to love, was listening with all his might, tilting his head to catch the state of things inside. However, there was only a buzzing sound echoing vaguely, and he could not catch a single word.

鬼春『いや拙者の居間は男ばかりで、何かにつけて不都合で厶る。貴殿の居間へ参れば女手が二人も揃うてゐるのだから、誠に以て都合が好いと存じ、それで貴殿の居間を拝借しようと申したのだ』

久米彦はハツと顔を赤らめ、……鬼春別は何時の間にか自分の居間に二人が隠してあるのを悟つたのかな、こいつア大変だ……と胸を躍らせ乍ら故意と空恍けて、

久米『ハハハハハ将軍殿は随分疑の深い方で厶るな。吾々もバラモン軍の統率者、左様な卑怯な事は致しませぬ。何卒人格を見損はない様にして頂き度いものですな』

鬼春『ハハハハハ今迄貴殿の人格を見損つてゐたのだ。今日愈人格の程度が分つたので厶る。さう仰せらるるなれば拙者の疑を晴らすために、一度貴殿の居間を明けて見せて貰ひませう』

久米『拙者の居間は拙者の権利の中で厶る。如何に上官だつて捜索する訳には参りますまい。こればかりは平にお断り申します』

鬼春『いや、何と云はれても拙者の権利を以て室内捜索を致す』

と云ひ乍らスタスタと隧道を潜つて久米彦の居間に進み行く。

久米彦は……今露顕れたが最後、一悶錯が起るか、但は自分は首にならねばなるまい。一層の事、鬼春別を後から一思ひにやつつけて了はうか。いやいや将軍にも股肱の家来が沢山ある。うつかり手出しも出来まい、ぢやと云つて吾居間を覗かれたが最後、忽ち露顕するのだ。はて、如何したら宜からうか……と刻々に迫る胸の苦みを抑へて、見え隠れに跟いて行く。

鬼春別は已に已にドアーの入口に着いた。そしてドアーに耳を寄せて中の様子を考へてゐる。スミエル、スガールの姉妹は、そんな事とは夢にも知らず、両親のことや、自分の身の不運を歎いて涙に袖を霑し乍ら、一生懸命に盤古大神救ひ玉へ、助け玉へと祈つてゐる。

鬼春別は鍵を持つてゐないので、開けて這入る訳にも行かず、又部下に命じて開けさしては却て自分の人格や声望を落す虞れがあるので、恋の奴となつた彼は、一生懸命に首を傾けて室内の様子を聞いてゐる。されど何だかワンワンと響きがするばかりで少しも聞きとれなかつた。

§ 2

General Kumehiko finally appeared here,

Kumehiko: 'Oniharuwake-san, it seems there is something suspicious inside my quarters.'

Oniharuwake: 'Indeed it is most suspicious. Come, quickly bring out the key and open this place. If you do so, your suspicion will be cleared, and the estrangement between us two will be resolved, which would be excellent.'

Kumehiko: 'Indeed, that would be excellent, but unfortunately I have lost the key, so I cannot even enter.'

Oniharuwake: 'Even without a key, you can just smash it open. Bring a hammer or something.'

Kumehiko: 'This is outrageous. To smash open my living quarters with a hammer is by no means the path a samurai should take. Leaving aside the case of an actual battlefield, smashing open another person's living quarters in ordinary times is truly called violent and disorderly conduct; for this alone, no matter how much it is the order of a superior officer, Kumehiko can never consent.'

Oniharuwake: 'If you say so, it appears you are engaging in some suspicious and sluggish business. If you do not listen to my command, from this moment on I shall dismiss you from the post of general by virtue of my authority as a superior officer, so prepare yourself accordingly.'

Kumehiko: 'I did not become a general by your command; I was appointed general by receiving the command of Daikoku-nushi-sama, so your concern is quite uncalled for. Leaving matters of official duty aside, there is no reason to drag a superior officer into private affairs; Kumehiko will definitely not allow this room to be opened.'

As the two were thus arguing, the one who came running in a flurry was Martha the colonel.

Martha: 'General-sama, a terrible thing has happened.'

Oniharuwake: 'What do you mean by terrible?'

Martha: 'Yes, three thousand soldiers have all without a single exception stripped completely naked, saying things that make no sense at all, smashing up the office, throwing away spears and swords, throwing stones, and engaging in violent and disorderly conduct. If we dawdle, they might even enter this room, so I earnestly ask you to suppress them with the majestic power of both generals; our strength is utterly no match for them. I think that those scoundrels of the Sansekkyō are using magic to torment our army.'

Upon receiving this report, General Oniharuwake and General Kumehiko completely forgot their private argument, darted away at full speed to the entrance of the cavern, and when they looked around, eight or nine tenths of the three thousand troops had stripped completely naked, and chattering away about things that made no sense, were smashing up the semi-permanent building from the edges with the sounds of crash, smash, bang, crack, clatter, thud. The two generals, letting out a great roar of 'Hey!', leaped right into the midst of the crowd. The multitude, infected by the intoxicating demon-misting wine, seeing the figures of the two generals, rushed up each trying to be first, and crying 'Yoisho! Yoisho!', tossed them in the air, threw them onto the ground, committed every imaginable act of violent and disorderly conduct, and finally the two generals were trampled all over their bodies by the multitude, coming to the verge of having their very breath cut off.

Thereupon Deku and Shīna, both attired in tunics with swords tightly slung, appeared leisurely, slipped into the cavern without any hesitation or reserve, and were searching everywhere for the whereabouts of Sumieru and Sugāru. The numerous Brahman troops lurking within the cavern, upon seeing the attire of the two, felt no particular suspicion and were engaged in their respective military duties.

Once again the semi-frantic naked troops came tumbling in a crowd into the cavern, raging and running wild wherever they chanced to strike. At length the intoxication of the demon-misting wine wore off, all the soldiers returned to their senses, picked up the swords they had discarded, or picked up the clothing that had been hanging here and there along the mountain stream, dried it in the sunlight, and rescuing the two generals, escorted them back to their original quarters. The numerous troops who had acted out a state of madness by the power of the demon-misting wine for a time now fully awakened, and it was decided to enforce military discipline even more strictly.

Deku and Shīna of Haruharawake at last found the whereabouts of Sumieru and Sugāru, smashed open the door, forced their way inside, and just as they were about to rescue the two and escape from the room, they were feebly bound by the Brahman troops who had gathered from the tunnels before and behind, left and right, and all four were dropped separately into the dark cavern.

§ 3

Oniharuwake, Kumehiko, and the leading members of Subaru, Emishi, Shamu, and Maruta gathered in the general's office, the largest room within the cavern, to investigate the causes of the recent strange incident from various angles.

Oniharu: 'That nearly ninety-nine percent of a three-thousand-strong army became completely naked and acted out such a state of madness is surely no ordinary occurrence. There must be some cause for this. You had better investigate thoroughly and be careful so that such a blunder does not happen again.'

Subaru: 'Indeed so. It is utterly incomprehensible; I suspect that the Harukuniwake faction of the Ananai-kyō is perhaps using some sort of sorcery to throw our camp into confusion and planning to capture the general alive.'

Emishi: 'At first, there were only a few madmen, but it gradually spread and became like that. There are no such things as sorcery in the Ananai-kyō. It is likely the work of a faction of Yōgenbō living on this mountain. First of all, unless we enshrine the Brahman god and offer fervent prayers, such a thing might happen again, which would be dangerous.'

Kume: 'Those two suspicious soldiers—the fellows we threw into prison—might they not be spies of the Ananai-kyō?'

Oniharu: 'Hahahaha! What could one or two spies accomplish if they crawled into a place fortified with such a large army? As I surmise, those two women, Sumieru and Sugaru, who are said to have been abducted from the wealthy farmer Temusu's house in Tamaki Village, are the truly suspicious ones. As proof of that, did not the madness of our friendly soldiers recover right after we thrust them into prison?'

Emishi: 'Indeed, hearing it put that way, there is no mistaking it. Bringing women into the military camp is something not permitted by the gods, so perhaps Lord Daijizaiten took such measures as a warning to make all of us take heed. Regarding that as well, those two soldiers are wearing our army's uniforms, but they too seem somewhat suspicious. They might even be the master of this mountain in disguise.'

Oniharu: 'You must on no account deal with those four. If we shut them away like that, they will not play any more tricks. What do you think, Kumehiko-dono? I would like to hear your opinion.'

Kume: 'Indeed, however one thinks of it, it is an incomprehensible matter. As the general has said, we shall leave them alone, and at any rate, we shall strive to tighten military discipline. No matter what kind of enemy approaches, by guarding this heavenly stronghold and with just this many allies, we will be all right, so let us give instructions to the army in general.'

Now, discussing various matters back and forth, as a result of their deliberations, they mutually warned each other and resolved not to let anything unusual approach, thus closing the meeting for the time being. From then on, they mutually admonished each other, strictly enforced military regulations, and determined to defend this stronghold to the death with the upper and lower ranks united.
