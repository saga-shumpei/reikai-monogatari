§ 1

Samori, with a pale face and tilting his head incessantly, visited the mansion of Harukuniwake. Harukuniwake had already departed, accompanied by Tatsuhiko and Matsuhiko, to inspect the progress of the Shinden construction on Mount Victor while acting as a supervisor. While Mankō was leaning against a desk all alone in the entrance room dozing off, it was Samori's Cupid who entered with the words, 'Pardon me.' Mankō was startled by this voice and—

Mankō: 'Ah, Samoridono, you have come at a good time. What is your business? Most likely you have come regarding a marriage proposal, ehehehehe.'

Samori: 'As you have surmised, I have come to consult with Harukuniwake-sama regarding a marriage proposal. Is Harukuniwake-sama at home?'

Mankō: 'Yes, he has just gone out for a moment to supervise the Gofushin of Mount Victor, but he should return soon, so please wait. However, during his absence, I, Mankō, am authorized to exercise full authority; in short, my opinion is the opinion of the Sensei. Please, do tell me. You have quite a pale face.'

Samori: 'No, a marriage affair has arisen that is like a cataclysm of heaven and earth, beyond all reason and logic, and I am unable to judge it myself. I have come to trouble Harukuniwake-sama in hopes of receiving his judgment.'

Mankō quickly thought... Since Princess Daiya and I exchanged glances frequently when we were in charge of cooking and serving meals, the Princess must have started acting stubborn, and the rigid Samori, unable to handle it, has come to consult Harukuniwake-sama. Very well, taking advantage of his absence, I must remodel this stubborn old man's brain now, or else the Princess's precious intentions will end up as a mere pie in the sky... Taking it as his own affair, he said with a deliberately composed face—

Mankō: 'A marriage proposal like a cataclysm of heaven and earth? What on earth is that? When a man and woman who love each other get married, there should be no such thing as status, lineage, or any such nonsense.'

Samori: 'No, that may be true if one speaks of logic, but whatever one says, one side belongs to a Kshatriya family, so it must be considered very carefully. I have not yet informed the King, and have come secretly to first seek the opinion of Harukuniwake-sama.'

Mankō: 'I see, that is a lot of trouble for you. If it concerns a Kshatriya family, ha, I understand almost everything. You are worried because there is a vast disparity in the status of the man and the woman. To begin with, love is by no means a base passion as old-fashioned people with antiquated heads used to think.'

Samori: 'I know that well. Even Samori understands that a marriage problem does not arise without love. However, even if one says love is an essential element of marriage, one cannot recklessly carry it out without considering status. Marriage can be perfectly established even without love. If one were of the Vaisya or Sudra rank, the theory of the supremacy of love might pass, but whatever one says, this is a grave matter affecting the dignity of a lord of a country, so it cannot be as easy and simple as it is for the common people.'

Mankō: 'Is that not a somewhat prejudiced view? Please think carefully. You seem to be separating the issue of marriage from the issue of love, but such a lack of understanding is something a person who has received today's education cannot even conceive. For example, a human would still be a human without a brain, would they not? If elements other than the brain are present, they would indeed be a human. However, one could never call that a truly splendid, human-like human. In the same way, a legal marriage might formally and splendidly be established without love. However, we can never believe that to be a complete marriage with spiritual significance that respects the personality and freedom of those who are truly men and women.'

According to the words of Hime-sama, one must first consider human beings and life essentially, fundamentally, and primordially. Without any prejudice and without being caught by anything, one must correctly realize the true flavor of life. A marriage without mutual understanding is truly the source of sin in life... such was the conclusion. Truly, Samori-sama, is it not exactly as Hime-sama said? I resonated extremely with that opinion.'

Such is the cunning of his speaking with a face as if he had heard it, though it was a word Hime never uttered. Samori... thinking that if it is about Yoshikohime-sama, she is surely sophisticated, so even if she is young, she might say such things... without doubting in the least, leaned his knees forward, thrust his neck out, and began to listen intently, saying, 'Yes, yes.' Mankō, as if thinking "now is the time,"

Mankō: 'Thereupon Yoshikohime-sama Otsushiya... if she be a Fujin as a human being, she must see all defects and imperfections and strive to exclude the evils that can be avoided. A Fujin who grudges this effort is a coward. If not a coward, she is a sluggard; if not a sluggard, she is a fool... so she was saying. While such cowards, fools, and sluggards do not cease, the world cannot advance even a single step; since it is a time when everything is being reconstructed, we wish to take the lead in everything and abolish the distinction between upper and lower classes... so, unlike her age, she rattled on with the tip of her tongue spitting fire. I also completely resonated with that opinion and eloquence; truly, do not the words of Hime-sama have the weight of a thousand pieces of gold? Where there is no reconstruction, there is neither improvement nor progress. In such a state, no matter how much time passes, the gates of Heaven will not open eternally. And the light of Truth, Goodness, and Beauty will ultimately be unable to shine upon the earth.'

Samori: 'Indeed, that is reasonable for the most part, but no matter how much you say you resonated with Hime-sama's theory, since you are a religious man, I believe you are not one to listen to such things from the bottom of your heart; how is it?'

Mankō: 'Being a religious man, how could I bend the truth of the universe? To begin with, love is the fundamental requirement of human life, and the source of all religion also comes from love. That being so, that which can govern the human heart and guide it correctly is, of course, not something like ascetic Brahmanism or Uranai-kyō. Anago-kyō does not say such old things. Anago-kyō is neither a self-denying old morality nor a formality, but a clear teaching truly adapted to the Jidai. I cannot by any means think that the Kingdom of Heaven, the Paradise, can be constructed on earth by denying human-like desires. Rather, I think one must affirm and emphasize human desires, and yet that must simultaneously unite entirely with the spirit of self-denial and self-sacrifice, so that there is no disharmony between the self and the non-self. Is it not that such a state of mind must be found in the world of love after all? For love is a sacred and inviolable thing that allows no interference from hypothetical things such as the separation of high and low, classes, formalities, property, or laws. You probably intend to ask the Master to make the view of love between me and Hime-sama unsuccessful. No matter how much those other than the persons themselves build a fence, the water of a dam that overflows if dammed will burst out somewhere, so you had better consider that well.'
