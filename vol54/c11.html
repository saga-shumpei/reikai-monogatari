道晴別〔1397〕

§ 1

Michiharuwake: 'Revering the divine words of the Great Deity Kamususanō, leaving the noble seichi of Ubusuna, becoming a follower of Harukuniwake, crossing over Kajikatauge, and passing the night in the forest of Yamaguchi where the magami fiercely rave—just when the hour of the ox arrived and I wondered at the shiyutsugen of eukuwaihenge, looking closely with the tanriyoku of Matsuhikotsukasa, I saw unexpectedly my younger sister Kahedehime. Saying how joyful it is, at the moment of raising the nanori of kiyōdai, the divine blessings smiled upon us, and we encountered our two parents. Returning to the forest of the shrine, together with Tamakuniwake, we finished building the miaraka of the deities.'

Michiharuwake: 'Being bestowed the name Michiharuwake, pursuing the footsteps of my master, I have finally arrived here. Crossing the wide kahase of the Lion with clear flowing waters, seeing Mount Victor on the right, walking across the grassy field path, now right here I have arrived at Shimeji Pass. Ah, O Kami, O Kami! How is my master, and to the edge of what place has he gone? With a heart anxious on this solitary journey, O Great Deity, grant that I meet him even a single day sooner, O Kami! I pray before the divine presence.'

Chanting thus, it was Michiharuwake who had come as far as the climbing entrance of Shimeji Pass. Gazing up at the steep slope of Shimeji Pass, Michiharuwake rested his breath for a while. It was neither quite raining nor clear, the May rain sky hung low, and the voices of cuckoos could be heard echoing from forests here and there. Both mountains and fields were draped entirely in a fresh green attire, feeling somehow muggy and stifling, to the point where his outer garment felt like a nuisance. Looking up sharply at the steep slope, Michiharuwake spoke to himself.

Michiharuwake: 'Ah, what a tiring slope this is! Have my master, along with Matsuhiko, Tatsuhiko, and Mankō, already passed through here? If the rocks by this roadside could speak, they would surely let me know. Ah, there is nothing to be done. First, let me sit calmly in meditation upon this rock and try to divine whether the master has passed or not. However, since my soul is not yet polished, I cannot tell anything clearly—how troublesome this is.'

So murmuring, two or three men came walking down the slope briskly and in a hurry. Seeing Michiharuwake upon the rock, the three men spoke.

First Man: 'Allow me to ask you a brief question. Are you not a propagation emissary-sama of Izumo Shinshū?'

Michiharuwake: 'Yes, as you might guess, I am a propagation emissary named Michiharuwake. What business might you have with me?'

First Man: 'Though it is daunting to speak to you in such a place, the truth is, hearing that a noble propagation emissary has appeared on Mount Victor and is relieving the sufferings of the people, and ordered by our master, we three servants have braved dangers to come this far to meet that personage.'

Michiharuwake: 'Is there a rumor that a propagation emissary of Izumo Shinshū is on Mount Victor? Indeed?'

Crossing his arms, Michiharuwake muttered to himself in a low voice.

Michiharuwake: 'Ah, what a blunder! If things were like this, I should have stopped by for just a moment. My master might still have been behind me. Because I thought I was too late and hurried along, I must have gone right past him.'

First Man: 'If that is so, are you not of the party of the propagation emissaries of Izumo Shinshū? We heard that there are about four of you in all.'

Michiharuwake: 'Ah, those four people are my master or friends. Do you know for certain that they are on Mount Victor?'

First Man: 'No, no, whether they have already set out or are still there—that we do not know for certain.'

Michiharuwake: 'And what sort of business do you have in going to meet the propagation emissary?'

First Man: 'Well, the truth of the matter is, I am a servant of a wealthy farmer in Tamaki Village. Two of our young mistresses were snatched away by the subordinate of a Brahman general called Kiharuwake, who has built his stronghold in the mountain fortress of Ikurayama. Our master and his wife, worrying variously about the plight of the young mistresses, practiced water austerities day and night and prayed to the Kami. In a dream revelation, we were told that a propagation emissary of Izumo Shinshū has come to Mount Victor, and if we pray to him, he will surely take them back. Relying on those words, we have come this far to make our plea.'

At the summit of Shimeji Pass, there is a somewhat level spot. There, beaten by the wind, stood four or five warped and withered-looking pine trees in a row. Two men in tunic attire, one called Betto, the other called Fueru. Sitting upon small stones, they were absorbed in idle talk while looking down upon the scenery in all directions.

Betto: 'Hey, isn't this region an absolute spectacle? Every single mountain, just like that, has its upper part become like the head of a person with favus, and the manner in which green trees are dotted among them is just like looking at a picture. The flow of the Lion River is visible as if it had drawn a faint sash, but we too certainly had our hardships when we crossed over there.'

Fueru: 'Indeed, midway your horse gave out, you were swept away for about four or five cho, and the sight of you when you were saved by the natives and gave out is just as if I see it right now before my eyes. If I hadn't been there at that time, you might have been snatched away by the natives and met with a torturous death, who knows. Because you are an incomparable, supreme scoundrel, you see.'

Betto: 'Hmph, don't talk so grandly; even this person is a Gledinar of the Brahmin army. Above all, it was this very Betto who smoothly hoodwinked both Sumieru and Sugaru, daughters of the wealthy farmer of Tamuki Village, and brought them back to the main camp of Ikurayama, so I must be a great person, mustn't I? If evil is not thoroughgoing to this extent, you can by no means become a soldier like you. Without making a fuss, you are to obey my command. Moreover, I will smoothly manage it with the General and have you made into at least a corporal, you know.'

Fueru: 'Hmph, don't make a fool of me; though I look like this, I am a warrant officer, a second lieutenant candidate. Aren't you still a sergeant? Don't you know that I am disguised like this?'

Betto: 'Hmph, don't say such naive things. Can't you see quite well if you look at your own shoulder straps?'

Fueru: 'Now, it is just because I am entrusted with a secret mission that the mark of Ebbō Reppo is written thereon. In another month, I shall be a fine Junker. If it becomes so, you see, I will use you by the chin, so if you do not win my favor while it is still time, it will become an obstacle to your success. If you think it's a lie, look at this.'

saying which, he turned back his collar to show him. When Betto peered closely at it, the mark of the Junker candidate was attached to it. Suddenly flattening himself flat upon the ground,

Since there is only one person here on this summit, no matter how many tens of thousands may come out, it is not possible to attack all at once, so you are to diligently perform your duty until the relief soldiers arrive, you know.'
