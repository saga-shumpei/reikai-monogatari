Samori no Tsukasa no Chiyōshi: 'The song of Haruna.'

Haruna: 'As a replica of Takaamahara, in the sacred precinct of Mount Viktor, knowing well the great pillar of the palace, you dwell in peace, O Great Kami. Before your august presence we offer our reverent prayers. The presiding deity of Takaamahara, manifesting as the majestic spirit, one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, a hundred, a thousand, ten thousand things' primal parent, Great Ōkunitokotachi Ōmikami, Great Takamimusubi Ōmikami, and the Great Kami Kamumimusubi, with your awe-inspiring power, creating endlessly myriad things, intending that the divine people beneath the heavens shall dwell forever peacefully and serenely in a blessed august realm, you created the sun, moon, and earth, and granted unto each pure spirits, bringing forth eight hundred myriads of noble kami, protecting all creation in heaven and earth, with boundless and vast grace, which we revere and worship. In the luxuriant reed-field land of Mizuhokuni, where the living kototama prospers, you bestowed a hundred treasures. The sovereign's throne—ah, for a thousand and eight thousand reigns—shall never move nor change, across the eighty islands and eighty countries, to the very limits where the heavenly walls stand erect, to the uttermost bounds where the lands soar up, to the very ends of the trailing clouds, your resplendent and penetrating great divine majesty—this truly is the day's protection, the kami's protection! Rejoicing thus, morning and evening we shall serve. The moon-reading kami's light, which illuminates the everlasting night, is the night's protection.'

Haruna: 'Cherishing the mortals, the ancestor of Ame-katsukuni and Kuni-katsukuni, the Great Kami Kuninotokotachi, from the very time heaven and earth were formed, remained hidden and dwelt there. By the spiritual virtue of Tamadomaru no Mitama, he created and solidified the drifting land which was like a jellyfish, and separated the earth's seas and lands. The Great Kami Toyokuninushi, by the spiritual virtue of Ashitamaru no Mitama, brought forth and protected plants. The Great Kami Ashimehikochi, by the spiritual virtue of Ikudamaru no Mitama, cherished and nurtured all animals. The moving power is Ōtoji, the calming power is Ōtobe, the dissolving power is Uhijineshinokami, the coagulating power is Suhijineshinokami, the power protecting attraction is Ikuchihikiakami.'

Haruna: 'The power protecting laxity is Tsunuchihikakami, the power protecting union is Omodashinokami, and the dividing power, through the majestic authority of Umakashinokami, is bestowed upon all things in the world. Making the spirits of heaven and earth accord with the great path of the kami, the divine Izanagi no Ōmikami, who rules the sun's divine land, and the divine Izanami no Ōmikami, who rules the moon's divine land, by the divine decree of the heavenly august kami, taking and holding the heavenly jeweled spear, completely and thoroughly created the thousand-five-hundred autumns' Mizuhokuni as a land of a thousand riches and a land of peaceful shores. In the lands far and near, they brought forth the land-spirit kami, appointed the tutelary local kami, and made the common folk their ujiko.'

Haruna: 'Distributing them each and every one, they caused them to be guarded intimately. We humbly revere and joyfully praise this great grace. Ah, Kami-nagara, O august spirits, prosper! Though it is said that man is the child of kami and the palace of kami, yet unawares, mixing with the crooked deeds of the crooked-spirit kami, day and night sinking into sins and defilements, sinking into the sorrowful rapids, you pity this wretchedness, O stern spirit, O Great Kami of the august spirit of Mizu! In the Aya sacred land, to say nothing of Mount Koganeyama and the precious Mahuraba of Ubusuna, in this mountain surrounded by green fences at the roots of the lower rocks, manifesting yourself, to teach and guide the people of the world, ruling the countries of the four quarters beneath the heaven peacefully and ever so serenely—though we have no means to recompense even one ten-thousandth part of that grace, we shall devote our utmost sincere heart to the utmost, for the sake of the kami and the sovereign, and serve as long as our lives shall last. Have mercy, Kami-nagara!'

Haruna: 'Prostrating ourselves before the august presence of the kami, reverently and respectfully we pray. From the very time when heaven and earth first opened, the ancestor of the land who remained hidden, the Great Kami Ōkunitokotachi, before your august presence Haruna reverently, with awe and trembling, makes this prayer. Upon the spirits of the common folk born into the pure and noble lands beneath the heaven in the countries of the four quarters, grant your august divided spirits.'

Haruna: 'Illuminating the spirit of Naohi, make it shine ever more brightly and beautifully as the spirit of Izunome. May it never happen that we mistakenly stain or shatter our pure spirits against the ugly crookedness of Magatsuhi. Through the perfection of the four souls and five emotions, enable us to serve the heavenly enterprise of the Imperial Great Kami with utmost peace and tranquility. Come what calamities may, may we endure them well, maintaining the noble dignity of being human, and may the thread-like life bestowed by the kami be long, our family occupations grow ever more prosperous, and may we manifest as the flowers and lights of the beautiful heaven and earth, fulfilling the innate essence of our being as the august children of heaven...'

Haruna: '...'

May Thou deign to purify and lift us up. With reverence and awe, I pray that we may fulfill the Mikokoro of Sumeōkami, that in this Utsushiyo, our spirits may be free of sin and defilement, and that we may be spared from grievous errors. May Thou protect the Seirei bestowed by Kami, and may we live in Kamunagara. In all the Narihai we conduct, may we be blessed with Mitamanofuyu, and may our Masawaza be truly virtuous. May we rouse the courage of Hachiku, and may we advance ever forward until we reach the realm of Umara. Morning and evening, may we revere the Kami, honor our Lord, and without deviating from the Mikoto, may we fully perform the duties of the Kunitami and the Yobito, and may we live in harmony with all people of the world.

May we never engage in strife or madness, but correct the errors of our bodies, and with Miyabinoyogoto as our shield, may we reconcile Kami and humanity. May we establish Isao that replaces Tenchi, and may it stand as Kakiha and Tokiha. May the Sachimitama of deep love protect all Banbutsu that live and breathe, without harming or destroying them. May we revere and serve the Daidau of Seiseikuwaiku, and through the light of Kushitama, may we fully and thoroughly comprehend the truth of the Oshihe of Magakami that leads to madness. May we be blessed with the spirit of Naohi, and reflecting upon right and wrong, may we strive for a faith of pure sincerity. May Thou deign to help us realize the Mikokoro of Kami through the aid of Kototama, and may we refine and temper our hearts.

May Thou deign to purify and cast away the myriad sins and defilements that touch my body, and the delusions that dwell within my heart. Just as the eternal Mount Victor never wavers, and just as the flow of the Lion River remains forever pure, may Thou deign to make us steadfast, unchanging, and long-lived, and may Thou make us truly magnificent. As long-lived people of the world, may we preserve our lives in health, and while observing the Five Constant Virtues, may we fulfill our duties as children of Tenchi, passing on our beautiful achievements for all generations to come. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! All my gratitude and my prayers, from the ancient days of the age of Kami in Takama.

Thou who didst bear the thousand offerings, who didst retreat to the single island of Ōwadahara, and who didst purify the myriad sins and transgressions of the Amatsutsumi and Kunitsutsumi; Thou, the guardian Kami of Utsushiyo and Yūshiyo, Kuninotokotachi no Ōmikami, Toyokuninu no Ōmikami, Iwa no Mitama no Ōmikami, and Mizu no Mitama no Ōkami. I pray that Thou wilt hear these words blessed by Thy name, that Thou wilt grant Thy consent, and that Thou wilt bless us with the protection of the night and the protection of the shining sun. Prostrating myself before the presence of Kami, I bow my head and pray. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! May Thy Mitama be blessed.

Karunahime sings again: 'Born as the younger sister of the Migimoritsukasa, I, Karunahime, on this auspicious day and at this auspicious hour, revere and celebrate the festival Thou hast conducted. The land of Biku, protected by Kami, is suddenly attacked by the unexpected forces of the Brahmin Oniharawake and Kumehiko, who lead a great army and strike with sudden ferocity. My elder brother, the Migimoritsukasa, in his cowardice and lack of resolve, has lost his composure, and it is a source of deep regret that the main castle has been trampled by the enemy before our very eyes. Together with my lord, I followed Hirunahime and faced the approaching enemy, attempting to fight with the strength of a lion, but what could we do against an enemy as vast as clouds and mist?'

'With no way to hold them back, I suddenly devised a plan. Disguised as a pilgrim, I intentionally allowed myself to be captured by the enemy and was sent to the camp of the two generals. When I reflect upon the feelings of that moment, it feels as if I were crossing a sword. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara! That Thou didst protect me in such a dangerous feat and allow me to achieve such outstanding Isao, Sumeōkami, how noble Thou art! Though the enemy retreated for a time, before we could even rejoice, the rebel army of Migimori, leading over three thousand horsemen, raised the banner of rebellion once again and attacked with great pomp and ceremony.'

'One danger passed, only for another to arise. The commanders in the castle worried and fretted over what to do. My lord commanded the entire army and fought in defense, but the rebels, emboldened by their victory, showed no sign of retreating. At such a moment, parting the distant clouds of heaven, the three-five messengers of Kami, the Senden-shi, descended. Through the blessing of the living Kototama, the impure-hearted Migimoritsukasa, starting with Bertz and ending with Ciel, were struck by the power and collapsed, unable to move in their wretched state. Following Hirunahime, we mounted our horses and left the mountain pass of Ikura behind, the sound of our hooves echoing in haste.'

Returning and looking, the interior of the castle has completely become a lane of Shiyura. At the Omotemon are the Sendenshi, and from the Uramon, Hirunahime together with me pressed forward to attack, chasing and scattering the enemy without leaving one. Oh, the gratitude of looking up once more at the Shiyokuō of Tenkataihei! Even if the morning sun shines or clouds over, even if the moon waxes or wanes, and even if Ametsuchi were to overturn, the divine grace of the true Kami—we shall not forget it until thousands of generations. By the prayer of Hirunahime, thick Miyabashira were erected in this Seijiyō, and the joy of reverently worshiping the Ōkami is the feeling of Tengoku and Jiyōdo opening before one's very eyes right now. Ah, Sumekami! Oh, Sumekami!

Hanging down thy Mitamanofuyu, the setsuteiri of the land of Biku, not to mention the hundreds of officials, may you protect the myriad people peacefully and exceedingly tranquilly unto Tokoshihe, Kamunagara. Before your presence, with reverence, we humbly pray.'

Tatsuhiko: 'The thick Miyabashira of the Ōkami of Ametsuchi standing firmly—today indeed is joyful. The Kami of Ametsuchi too surely grant the sincere hearts of the hundreds of officials. As in the ancient age of the Kami, the land of Biku restored straight—today indeed is joyful.'

Yorogimi: 'I pray for a thousand, eight thousand, and ten thousand generations that the land of Biku may prosper more and more. Following the command of the Mikoto of Kuninokuniwakeshinokami, meeting in today's festival is joyful. You who govern the land of Biku, the setsuteiri, the ruler's sincerity the Kami too must admire. Although you now cross the slope of the seventies, I, Yorogimi, pray unto ten thousand generations. Preserving a ten-thousand-year life, may you reign over the land of Biku, O ruler of the setsuteiri.'

Kuninokuniwake: 'With the lofty divine majesty of the chihayaburu Kami, we have served and completed the jeweled palace dwelling. The land of Biku, which Hirunahime has aided, shall surely prosper as a land without night. Even in the dark night, the divine official Kuninokuniwake prays for the myriad generations of the land of Biku.'

Thus each offered congratulatory poems, successfully concluding the rite of transfer, and thereupon it was decided that a great banquet of Naorai would be held in Hofusu.
