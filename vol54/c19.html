§ 1

Harukuniwake chants.

Harukuniwake (in the style of a Noh chant): 'Pushing aside the eightfold clouds of the distant Hisakata sky, To the province of Fusa whose very name is auspicious, Upon the summit of Mount Viktor, Ramming down the upper bedrock, Tamping it firm against the lower bedrock, Laying and arranging the foundations securely, Gold, silver, agate, ruri, shiyako, To be likened to amber or glass, The pure sands of the Lion River Spread thickly over the upper bedrock, Cutting the trunks of the great and small ravines, Root and tip alike offered up to the Sumeōkami Of Yamaguchi, With the divine miizu, like a three-part chestnut, The middle trunk carved with devout heart Using the sacred axe and sacred hoe, With the skill of the Hida artisans brilliantly displayed, The true pillars revealing the light of the imperial reign, Erected straight, With the framework joined in exquisite detail, As the heavenly shade and the solar shade, The great roof is set up, Thickly thatched with hinoki bark, Ah, in such an auspicious sacred age, Extending far and wide to the end of the fan's reign, Appearing as the pivot of the nation, Equal to the kami, the sage sovereign, With the sequence of five winds and ten rains favorable, Mountains and rivers pure and clear, All kinds of crops bearing rich fruit, The state of supreme happiness and ultimate bliss Where the myriad people beat their bellies and stamp the earth, All as the august virtue of the kami and the sovereign king, Oh, the sublimity of today's grand rite as we look up in awe! Though the morning sun shines or clouds over, Though the moonlight crossing the vast sky Either waxes or wanes, Like the number of stars in the heavenly riverbed Spread with golden and silver sands, Or the countless grains of sand on the beach, Bestow your grace upon the lives of the multitude, Sumeōkami. To bring forth the age of Miroku, Serving morning and evening, Even in the dark night clear, Harukuniwake's Divine messenger, Matsuhiko of the ever-verdant pine, Not to mention the joy of official Tatsuhiko for all eternity, With the crane's longevity, Mankō-san From the bottom of his heart Rejoices, celebrates, and venerates today's solemn festival. Ah, profoundly revering the kami, Praying for the blessings of the august spirits, Praying for the blessings of the august spirits.'

So chanting to the end, he returned to his original seat. Taruman had been a propagandist of the Former Ural Religion, but now having become a disciple of Harukuniwake, instructed in the teachings and rituals of Izutsu-no-miya, and having become a chief priest to serve for a long time, he came to hold the official duty of praying for the peace and security of the royal family and the nation. Taruman stood up to express his celebratory sentiments as chief priest and began to chant.

Taruman (in the style of a Noh chant): 'Though the red jewel shines with brilliant threads, how precious indeed Is the attire of you, the white jewel! Now then, from the very beginning of the creation of heaven and earth, The remote ancestors of the Victoria family Appeared as the sovereigns of the nation, Above, revering the kami, Below, showing compassion to the myriad people, Granting winds every five days and rain every ten days In due measure, With the nation flourishing and the people prosperous, That state of the heavenly paradise and pure land, Transmitted ever onward eternally, This wondrous country too, having its times, When the ugly winds of deviation blew violently, And the Castle of Victoria, fixed as a dwelling for a thousand ages, Was already on the verge of leaning to its fall, Pushing aside the eightfold clouds of heaven, A divine priest descended descending, To rescue the darkness of this world straight away, Harikuniwake the divine person, And the three divine priests at the beginning, Oh, the sublimity of their descent! Not to mention Taruman the priest, The sovereign king and the queen's palace, The chief priests of the Left and Right Guardians as well, Blow away the clouds of bewilderment, Illuminated by the light of the sun and moon shining in the sky, Like unto the teachings of Izutsu, Illuminated upon the path of the teachings, Deeply comprehending the path of truth, Dwelling in the virtue of love and goodness, Bathing in the light of faith and truth, Erecting stout great shrine pillars upon the lower bedrock of Mount Viktor, Worshipping and serving the Sumeōkami, Concentrating earnest prayers for universal peace, the accomplishment of the realm, and the peace of the myriad people, Without regard for his own lowly person,'

As entrusted by our master and the king, though presumptuous it is, serving as the kamuzukasa of this palace of jewels, purifying my body morning and evening, avoiding defilement, and solely devoting my sincerity, O Taruman, please grant and accept my heart.

Though the light of Amatsumisora may sometimes shine and sometimes cloud, though there be nights when the moon waxes or wanes, making the teaching of the single, sincere Ananahi God our strength, as long as there remains a sincerity wherein the waters of the Lion River never cease nor run dry, crushing our bones and grinding our bodies to dust, we shall devote ourselves for the sake of the Gods, for the sake of the Sovereign, and for the sake of the Mikuni.

Ah, awe-inspiringly, beginning with the Great Deity Ōkunitokotachi who rules and guards this world, before the august presences of the eight million deities of heaven and earth and the myriad deities who follow them, with reverence and awe we pray.

Ah, Kamunagara, kamunagara, May your mitama flourish and bless us! May your mitama flourish and bless us!

Matsuhiko sings again.

Matsuhiko: 'Prostrate before the august presence of the Ōkami, praying morning and evening with sincerity poured forth, wishing that Matsuhiko of the ever-verdant pines—whose colors change not even through a thousand and myriads of reigns—may flourish forever and ever in the eternal land of Biku, how great is our joy that the Sumeōkami quickly heard the prayers of us all, and within a hundred days and a hundred nights caused such a magnificent august sanctuary to be built!'

Verily, the divine land of Biku, being a land where the divine protection is ever profound and favored, like the boulders of Mount Victor, shall never be moved for all eternity.

The king of such a felicitous divine land, having comprehended the teaching of Ananahi,

More and more the foundation of the country shall be purified and solidified like bedrock, and like the lush green of the pine trees, it shall flourish unto the unknown bounds of the end.

Verily, as for this country, the mountains of the four quarters, as far as the eye can see, are pine forests; what may be seen flickering between the branches of the trees—might it be the great oaks, or might it be the sacred camphor trees?

Throughout a thousand and eight thousand reigns, steadfastly, with lives long and never rotting, never showing a sign of withering, those sacred trees—drawing upon this, the land of Biku as a portent of unshakeable peace—gazing far out over the four quarters, whatever floated into his heart, transcribing it here, in accordance with Kamunagara, we celebrate and serve the august rituals of the divine shrine.

Ah, Kamunagara, kamunagara,

May your mitama flourish and bless us!'

Samori raises his aged body and sings with joy.

Samori: 'Ah, how wondrous, how wondrous! The blessing of the Gods is right before our eyes.

The breath of the Gods sets right the castle of Biku, which had been leaning toward ruin.

Protecting the Ananahi teachings, the rigorous mitama and the auspicious mitama—to say nothing of the Sumeōkami—leaving the hall of the saien, the kamuzukasa and the retinue of Chikokuwake descended like doves.

The great blessings bestowed to save the sufferings of our great sovereign first of all, as well as the hundred officials and the people of the land—in what age could we ever forget them?

Even if the countless starlight of the boundless firmament should suddenly fall, or the sands of the beach should be exhausted, the blessings of the true God

We shall never forget throughout all eternity.

Verily, to govern a country, first and foremost, is to praise and serve the revered Gods of heaven and earth, to follow the teachings of the Gods, to approach the lower subjects, and to manifest oneself as an official of the land, showing a model in detail, softening the hearts of the people, and governing the world eternally.

I have comprehended the path of sincerity.

Until now, the office of Samori also had its spiritual light darkened, scorching its heart in politics, leaning its heart toward the manifest world, and placing the God of our ancestral origins second—how foolish it was!

Without knowing it, before the presence of the Gods, accumulating countless sins,

not even punishing me, but looking upon me anew with a vast heart, not only forgiving me, but also commanding me to hold the official duties of Samori as before, and entrusting me with so grave a mission—how wondrous it is!

Words of gratitude cannot be exhausted.

Now from this time forth, Kyūbitto also shall polish his heart and purify his body, first of all praying to the Ōkami, and for the sake of the Sovereign, assisting in the exceedingly magnificent government—such is my heart.

Please grant it, Kamunagara.

Before your august presence, with reverence, we pray.'

So singing, he took his seat. Ekusu, the Yoshu, sings again.

Ekusu: 'The Kshatriya of the divine land of Biku, serving as a chief retainer of Queen Victoria,

the Yoshu official Ekusu here reverently offers up and expresses gratitude with a sincere heart, holding in awe the lofty grace of the Sumeōkami and the blessings of Chikokuwake.

Kami Naobi who created this world, O Naobi of vast heart,

truly, in human life whatever may be, look straight, listen straight, and rectify your transgressions.

Now that we have been bestowed with such revered divine teachings,

down to the generations of our descendants, with awe and caution, obeying the sincere teachings of Ananahi,

guarding the official duties of the Yoshu official with a single mind and undisturbed spirit,

we shall devote the utmost of our hearts for the sake of the Gods and the Sovereign.

Ah, Kamunagara, kamunagara,

Once surrounded by the ugly demon army of the Brahmin forces,

the altars of the state appeared endangered, but the Ōkami of infinite benevolence and compassion, pitying the critical situation of our sovereign of lofty virtue,

saved us—how wondrous it is!

Truly, in this world, whatever may be, following the divine will, no matter how small a matter it may be, never asserting our own ego, acting according to the Gods, we shall go securely as if sliding a box into place—this is the path of the Gods which I have understood for the first time.

Ah, Kamunagara, kamunagara,

O Sumeōkami, eternally

guard this sacred era,

and cause our mikuni to flourish!

Ah, Kamunagara, kamunagara,

Before the presence of the Gods, we pray.'

So singing to a close, he calmly took his seat.
