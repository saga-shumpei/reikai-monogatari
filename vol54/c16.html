Chapter 16: 百円 [1402]

百円〔1402〕

§ 1

Because my pocket is cold, the quack doctor Made even his medicine catch a cold.

'Ah, I have searched around to see if there are any savory patients somewhere, but there are only those who have collapsed on the road or dropped dead, and not a single fellow likely to give money from the bottom of his heart; yet I have wandered into such a place. This is why my wife at home always tells me not to drink sake, not to drink sake, but as they say a doctor knows no regimen, somehow I simply cannot keep to my temperance. Lately, people of the world have grown too clever and have begun paying attention to hygiene, exercise, and the like, so everyone has become robust, and the pocket of Eusen-sama grows ever more weak and debile—what a troublesome affair this is. The medicine laid in stock last year is catching a cold, they say, but since the principal cost has been incurred, unless I make patients swallow it and turn it into money the accounts will not balance; yet no matter how much they swallow it will not cure them, so people say that fellow is a bamboo shoot, a quack bamboo-hut doctor, and spread such hopeless rumors that the gate is as deserted as a sparrow trap—truly it is a pathetic state of affairs, if there is a good patient anywhere, I should like to grab hold of one.'

So he muttered to himself as a Chiyabinoyaji tried to slip through the gate of Yachimata.

Aka: 'Hey there, hey there, wait a moment; what kind of fellow are you?'

Doctor: 'Well, as for me, I am a national physician whose business is the benevolent art. Do you have some business with me? If it is a disease, perhaps I shall feel your pulse.'

Aka: 'I am extremely healthy, and have no use for the likes of a doctor.'

Doctor: 'Ah, is that so? In that case, you are a person who has no business with me. The only people who have close intercourse with me are diseased households. Lately, some heterodox teaching or other called Omotoism has been spreading, and everyone cures their diseases, causing tremendous interference to my business, so I am somewhat troubled. Ah, is it the end of the world even for doctors, I wonder.'

Doctor: 'Well, I am also called Biyō Eusen, and I am also called Yabuichikuan.'

Aka: 'Both of those are your nicknames; what is your real name?'

Eusen: 'My business has been so busy that I have forgotten my real name. Everyone calls me Sensei, Sensei, before me, so perhaps Sensei is my true name.'

Aka: 'How many sick people have you saved in the course of your life?'

Eusen: 'I have saved a great many, but as for those who have no span of life left, even the gods or doctors cannot help them... However, it is said that doctors and pumpkins are better when they are withered, and if one intends to become a full-fledged doctor, one has no choice but to kill about a thousand people through experience, you see.'

Aka: 'Do you truly understand the cause of illnesses and medical methods thoroughly even when you examine patients?'

Eusen: 'As the standing of a human being, there is no such thing as being able to understand clearly, but anyhow, I pull out the medical books prepared by my predecessors and blend my medicines. Then, calling it inquiry, I ask the attendants or family members about the course of the disease, and question them on the presence or absence of appetite; and calling it observation, I look at the condition of the patient—diagnosing a patient with a yellow face as having jaundice, and a red fellow as being drunk on sake, and giving them medicine appropriate to that. Furthermore, in order to carefully examine the state of blood circulation, respiratory organs, nervous system, and so on, I exhaust all sorts of means such as percussion, auscultation, and palpation, and when I still cannot understand, I just give it some plausible name and gloss it over, you see. There are plenty of doctors, but there is probably not a single one who has grasped actual illnesses. The world is simply like this.'

Aka: 'You unprincipled quack doctor, who knows how many people have thrown away lives that should have been theirs due to your wrong medicines, you heinous criminal of a murderer!'

Eusen: 'Even if doctors kill people, it does not touch the law in particular. That is the privilege of doctors. If an ordinary human being kills people, they would no doubt immediately receive the punishment of capital punishment. Whether a doctor performs an abortion or cuts off hands and feet, it does not become the crime of abortion, nor does it become the crime of injury; since it is a kind of privileged class, there is no reason for you people to call me a great criminal, so please do not mind it. Hey, your face is red; you are slightly flush with blood, are you not? Although it is a bit old, shall I prescribe some senega as well? Tangerine peel, Poria cocos, gentian powder, linseed oil, baking soda, and oyster shell—how would it be if you condescend to take a dose?'

Aka: 'Fool, do you peddle medicines?'

Eusen: 'Indeed I do. In a world where the seizonkiyōsō is fierce, if I simply sat still as a doctor, nobody would come. Unless I put up advertisements in the daishinbun, have reporters carry chiyōchin, pay to acquire a hakase shiyōgau, and dress up the teisai with all sorts of zatsuta things, there isn't a person who would even take the hand of a kojiki, you know.'

Aka: 'When I examine this chiyōmen carefully, it appears that you mixed water into wine, claimed it was a very kauki medicine, made a biyōnin drink it, and ripped them off outrageously.'

Eusen: 'That is true enough, but the way we rip people off is by doing it in small amounts like five or ten gosen a dose. I have no idea how many wretches there are in the sekai who rip people off by the thousands and ten-thousands of senen in a single hit. Among the shiyakai-shibiyō there are diseases that can be cured and diseases that absolutely cannot be cured, but still, if a biyōnin asks for medicine, I cannot well refuse to give it, and since I cannot rip them off heavily either, knowing full well that it is wrong, I mix water into wine and let them drink it for the sake of comfort. Since this is the very code of ethics that a doctor ought to fulfill, you should not disparage it in a single breath like that.'

Aka: 'At any rate, you are a difficult case. You probably won't come to your senses with just a morphine injection, but since I am going to subject you to some rough treatment presently, well, go to the back and wait until your turn comes.'

Eusen: 'You said just now that you were going to subject me to rough treatment, but that must mean major surgical operations. Do you possess a doctor's license? If you do such a thing, I will charge you with a violation of the Medical Practitioners Act. Come, what is your full name? Let me hear it. Since I have my own plans as well, if our turf is invaded by amateurs like you, we will never be able to stand as doctors. Lately, plenty of our enemies such as spiritual healing, prayer, and psychotherapy have appeared and interfered with our business, so we have decided at a bamboo-shoot conference to hold a national doctors' convention, lodge a protest with the government, and annihilate their kind. For an amateur like you to say to me, a doctor, that you will perform a major surgery and so on is truly outrageous as a citizen of a constitutional state. Come, let your name be heard.'

So saying, he took out a notebook from his breast pocket, licked a pencil, and tried to write down the name. The Shuei of White firmly gripped Eusen's arm, and ignoring his protests, dragged him away by force and hid him within the gates.

A man with round eyes, horns on his nose, and about a shaku of cheek-whiskers, looking like a fellow who had some peculiarity, swaggered down this highway with broad strides as if to say the road belonged to him. The Shuei of Red shouted,

Aka: 'Hey, wait a moment, there is something to investigate.'

At this shouted voice, the man stopped, glared at Aka's face with a loathsome look, and said,

Man: 'What is this? Freely striding across the great highway of the realm is our right of freedom; what do you mean by telling me to wait? If you interfere with the rights of others and make me waste even a moment of time, I will demand damages for that much loss. Do you understand the rights and duties of human beings, eh?'

Aka: 'This is the barrier of Yachimata. It is the watershed where we examine your memorial of past life and decide whether to send you to heaven or cast you into hell... What is your name?'

Man: 'I am a famous attorney by the name of Hio Ishiro. Furthermore, I also serve as a patented patent attorney. Even so, I am a graduate of a university faculty of law. What authority do you have as a mere shuei to investigate us?'

Aka: 'Indeed, you are a villain who squeezes the lifeblood of human beings. You certainly have managed to squeeze out plenty of money, haven't you?'

Hio: 'When people are sunk in the tears of sorrow and cannot even turn their necks, providing them with a little solace is my official duty. Demanding a labor fee as compensation for that is a business right, isn't it? What is wrong with demanding what ought to be demanded?'

Pledged for a second world in front of you, The severed threads of the samisen that fret my heart. Severed, severed, so goes the rumor of the world, Like the roots of duckweed in water, they will never be severed…… no matter what anyone says, Toku is Toku, and to Eusuke-sama, no matter what anyone says…… they will never be severed…… she breathes out, so she is quite something.

So saying while putting his hand inside his kimono bosom, with a thump he ran straight into the gate, and at the moment when his head grew dizzy and wobbly, he tumbled flat down right here.

The guard beat his back with all his might, three or four times in succession.

Eusuke came to with a start, the drunkenness of sake also wearing off, turned pale in the face, and was staring fiercely at the faces of the two.

Aka: 'Who are you?'

Eusuke: 'Hehe, I am known as the famous hedonist Eusuke. Since you have stopped me, is there something Eusuke can do for you? It is better not to inquire too deeply into matters that do no good for Toku. For if Toku turns into a loss, it will be a mutual nuisance, you see.'

Aka: 'What woman of where is this Toku you speak of?'

Eusuke: 'Ehehehe, it is a bit difficult to put into words, but she is Bekosuke's wife.'

Aka: 'Did you have relations with Bekosuke's wife?'

Eusuke: 'If you say there are relations, there are. If you say there are none, it is somewhat like none, but……'

Aka: 'Didn't you get the cold shoulder from Toku? And didn't you have a large amount of money taken from you?'

Aka: 'Even if you did not commit adultery physically, haven't you already committed adultery in your heart?'

Eusuke: 'Well, since that is not clearly known, on my part, I am already about to commit adultery in heart, but since there is no time to have a hearty chat, the other party's intentions are not fully understood.
