Chapter 21: 鼻向 [1407]

鼻向〔1407〕

§ 1

Harukuniwake and his party attended the banquet of the naorahi of Hōfusu hosted by Setsuteiri, finished the grand shiyuen, and spent the night here.

From asamadaki, the King and all members made hatsumairi at the shinden of Mount Vikutoru.

Tatsuhiko suddenly became kamugakari and spoke as follows.

Tatsuhiko: 'I am Konohanahimenomikoto of Tenkyōzan. You Harukuniwake, since the fūjin of the land of Biku has now calmed down and there is no kōko to worry about, set forth toward the west at the earliest possible moment. Your totei Michiharuwake is attempting to rescue Sumieru and Sugāru, the daughters of Rishiyō Teemusu of Tamakimura, who have been captured by the faction of the Brahmin general Oniharuwake and Kumehiko, but instead he is about to be cast down into the deep hole within the gankutsunai, so you must head for Mount Ikura even a moment sooner. If time is delayed it will be a serious matter; Matsuhiko, Tatsuhiko, and Mankō must also head forth to the rescue together.'

Thus he declared and ascended back to heaven.

Hearing this divine oracle, Harukuniwake realized that his disciple Harukō had become Michiharuwake and had fallen into the enemy's trap for the sake of rescuing the pitiful woman, and telling this circumstance to Setsuteiri, it was decided to set forth without losing a moment.

Harukuniwake: 'As you have just heard, the divine oracle has descended, so although I have been under your care for a long time, I will take my leave with this.'

Setsuteiri: 'I have been under your care for a long time, and I have no words to express my gratitude. However, since it is truly regrettable to part ways just like this, I would by all means like you to return to Hōfusu once and exchange the parting cup. Since it is not too much of a detour, please drop by. Moreover, if you are in a hurry, we must also make preparations for horses.'

Harukuniwake: 'If so, at such a kind offer, rather than hurrying on foot as we are, it would be extremely convenient if we might borrow your horses. In that case, we shall trouble you once more.'

With that, the party of four hurried back to Hōfusu together with Setsuteiri and other officials. The Samorishi ordered his subordinates to select four fine horses, and the party hurried their preparations for departure. Setsuteiri, lamenting the parting and shedding tears, himself took up the cup and handed it to Harukuniwake, brimming the wine and holding the ceremony of parting.

Setsuteiri: 'What I thought to gaze upon Your august shadow for evermore, Has turned into bubbles upon the water. Yet the meritorious deeds you have left behind Shall never decay even unto the myriad ages. Knowing beforehand that it would come to this, How sorrowful it feels all the more now.'

Harukuniwake: 'Though I am a body that has passed the age of seventy, It rejoices me to see your august form abiding in health. Leaving the deep feelings of my heart behind, I shall press forward upon the great highway of the gods. Hirunahime, Harukuniwake is going now, About to part from you and set out upon a journey. I pray that you will morning and evening Care for and protect the King, our master.'

Hirunahime: 'Dear divine preceptor Harukuniwake, As we are about to part, my tears overflow. Attaching myself to the body of my lord, morning and evening, I shall guard and protect him by the grace of the gods. O preceptor of Harukuni, O Matsuhiko, May Tatsuhiko and Mankō abide in health.'

Matsuhiko: 'O pillar of the land of Biku that shall not move for a thousand and eight thousand generations, Praying to the gods, cause it to be erected. Now come, together with my master, Let us whip our steeds and ride forth on our parting way.'

Taruman: 'Receiving the power of the gods of heaven and earth into your body, Advance to the august land of the moon. Since the august command of Harukuni is in the hands of the gods, No manner of crookedness shall likely obstruct it. Needless to say concerning my lord, How the preceptors and people of this land must grieve! Yet think upon it with an easy mind, For we keep the teachings of our lord and master profoundly.'

Harukuniwake: 'How difficult it is; facing this parting, even a single word Will not come forth, so great is the sadness. Yet the soul bestowed by the gods Shall remain in this august land and protect it.'

Samori: 'O august command of Harukuni, take heed And advance through the plain of Uchinogahara. You are setting forth to drive away Once again the ugly soldiers of the Brahmin. Seeing off the departure Of the brave preceptor of the teachings, my tears overflow.'

Harukuniwake: 'Embraced by the profound grace of the Great Deity, Let us press forward with an easy mind.'

Umori: 'How valiant indeed it is to hear The words of you, O official of the Kami, Tatsuhiko!'

Mankō: 'To pray that you may remain in utmost health throughout the ages, Is truly a sincerity of the heart.'

Haruna: 'How precious are the people who warded off the hardships of our land And aided our sovereign Lord. Alas, it is in vain that I thought to serve by your side for endless ages, For now we are about to part. I beseech you, O Kamuzukasa of Ananai-kyō, Do not forget the land of Viku.'

Harukuniwake: 'How could a Kamuzukasa ever forget The divine land where the Kami dwell?'

Princess Karu: 'Who would not mourn the multitude of people Moistened by the dew of the great Kami's grace? I too find myself strangely brought to tears By today's parting, so filled with sorrow.'

Thus they exchanged poems with one another, and mounting the fine horses bestowed upon them by the Kshatriya without delay, the four disciples lined their stirrups, raised their whips, and galloped off as one arrow toward Ōharano. Watching them depart from behind, the company pressed their palms together, their voices clouded with tears of gratitude.

The party of Harukuniwake, Reverencing the divine words, Bade farewell to the King and officials of Viku, And with pure hearts Mounted their willing steeds, Lining their stirrups and clattering along, Down the dewy path where green leaves flourished, Their faces caressed By the early summer breeze as they advanced. In the twinkling of an eye, They trod across fifty ri of moorland, And right up to the foot of Shimeji Pass, A continuous range of peaks Where the demons of Ikura lurked, They arrived safely and swiftly.

Here the four reined in their hooves And leaped down upon the earth, Turning the heads of their horses toward the east, And with a single stroke of the whip, Those famed horses of renown Turned back along the road whence they had come, And neighed as if loath to leave.

Returning swift as an arrow, The party of Harukuniwake Scaled the steep slope of Shimeji Pass With shouts of 'Enyarayatsu!', And while wiping away their sweat for a while, They gazed out at the scenery in all directions, Healings their travel-weariness Amidst a landscape resembling a painting.

After a short while, the party, Walking down the steep path lined like a standing board While chanting a marching song, Proceeded downwards with utmost care.

Mankō took the lead, stepping forward pace by pace, and began to sing in rhythm.

Mankō: 'The two who swaggered about As generals of the Brahman army, Having been driven out of the land of Viku, And escaping with their lives by a hair's breadth—dotsukoishiyo! Cowardly and lacking in valor, they retreated at last To the mountains of Ikura, Living in hole-dwellings like moles, Leading a force of over three thousand horsemen, Putting on such a mighty front. Harukunibake's party, alternately singing propaganda songs and marching songs, finally arrived at the Mansion of Thames in the village of Tamaki. Inside the estate, ancient trees grew lushly and boundlessly wide, and many splendid buildings stood in rows. Yet, somehow, an atmosphere as though sorrow were wrapped within this mansion seemed to linger.

Mankō: 'If I may say so, Teacher, this appears to be the Mansion of Thames in the village of Tamaki, which Konohanahime-sama indicated.
