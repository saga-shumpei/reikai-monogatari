Chapter 18: Truth 信 [1404]

真信〔1404〕

§ 1

In the early summer season when the green leaves drip with dew, the trees of the mountains and fields form a natural kabuotto, and the wind plays the natural Kōdo. As far as the eye can see across the wilderness, the Shiyuda and the Kaudo, in groups of three and five, straighten their lines and plant the seedlings of Inochi. The Arisama is as if the Tengoku had been moved to the Chijiyō. Looking from the Chiyōjiyō of Mount Victor, the beautiful, Tatohegata-less furiisu appears as if it were tanabi.

How does Minsinger see it? It is a great Daiguwatefu painted by Ten. Who are the people in the Guwachiu? It is a pastoral scene of chasing cattle, singing a cantabile song, or perhaps the contrast of an intermingled presto, which is Omoshiro.

The Tansei of one hundred days and one hundred nights has finally appeared here. The Miaraka built by choosing the Shiyōchi of Mount Victor is a most Saugon festival of the gods. Its norito resounds through heaven and earth, and the elegance of the rondo played by the celestial beings of the Reikai and Tengoku realms is profound. With the sōhō or light-hearted style, the clavichord is arranged in the mid-air to play adagios, melodies, motifs, maggiore, atissimo, fanzett, and tumblr. The living Kototama also follows the order, exhausting the limits of the phrases, and with correct rhythm, it consoles the heart of the God of heaven and earth.

Seeing that Arisama with one's own eyes, it sounds most refreshing to the ears of those who have awakened to the spirit. Chikokuwake serves as the chief priest, and the Kshatriya of the King of Biku, the Princess Hiruna, the Lord of Aaru, and the hundred other officials straighten their seats and line up for the ceremony of the Sengū; there was no one who did not praise it as the most prosperous event since the creation of the world.

On the summit of Mount Victor, a magnificent shrine with a cypress bark roof was completed. Dedicated to the Lord God Ōkunitokotachi no Ōkami, and including Amaterasu Sumera Ōmikami, Kami Izanagi no Ōkami, Kami Izanami no Ōkami, Kami Susanoo no Ōkami, Toyokuni-hime no Ōkami, Wakasakura-hime no Ōkami, Konohana-hime no Ōkami, and the Hinodewake, with Banko Shin-ō enshrined in a separate hall, the Saugon ceremony was concluded without incident. Victoria, the Kshatriya King, recognizing the dawn of the nation's peace and the stability of the royal family, and wishing to give thanks for the completion of the shrine, respectfully presented a celebratory song to the shrine.

Kshatriya (in the style of a Noh chant): 'O Great Lord God Ōkunitokotachi, who appears as the Mototsu-mioya no Kami of heaven and earth, who dwells eternally in the vast, distant heavenly sky; O Great Lord God Toyokuni-nushi; O Great Lord God Kami Izanagi, who rules over the heavenly land of the sun; O Great Lord God Kami Izanami, who rules over the land of the moon; O Great Lord God Kunitokotachi, who appears as the Iwa no Mitama; O Great Lord God Kami Susanoo, who appears as the Mizu no Mitama; O Great Lord God Kankatsuyō, who appears as the soul of the great earth; O Great God Ōwatsumi, who rules and protects the bottom of the sea without limit; O Great God Konohana-hime, who appears on Mount Amakyo.'

'Starting with the Hinodewake, the hundred divine pillars of officials who protect the Miroku-kyō, the Banko Shin-ō Shionagahiko no Inochi who appears in the land of Tokoyo, and all the other hundred gods—before their great presence, I, the Kshatriya who serves the King and protects the nation of Biku, which the God has granted to be as eternal as heaven and earth, a servant of the God Victoria, in this noble and pure presence, respectfully and reverently rejoice in the high grace of heaven and earth. Offering various delicious things from the sea, rivers, mountains, and fields, I perform today's festival, wishing to repay even one layer of the thousand layers of your deep grace.'

'O eight million heavenly gods, O eight million earthly gods, have pity on my heart. Protect the nation of Biku, and our lives, eternally. Make it an era where the people, as soon as possible, may dwell in peace, immersed in the grace of the God, with homes prospering and enjoying their livelihoods with joy. Protect us so that we may flourish eternally with the sun and moon. Kannagara, I pray before the presence of the God. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if this world changes, the grace of the Imperial Great God—Chikokuwake's missionary, Matsuhiko Ryūhiko, the divine priest. The merit of saving the nation of Biku—in what age shall I ever forget it? Kannagara, I swear with a sincere heart before the presence of the God. The nation of Biku has until now governed the world with the teachings of the God of Ural as its pillar, cherishing the living souls and serving them, but now that we have awakened to the noble teachings of the God Susanoo, who has appeared as the pioneer of the era of Miroku, the hundred officials of Victoria Castle and all the people shall revere the divine grace and protect the precious teachings eternally. The divine priest of Chikokuwake, respectfully before the presence, rebirth...'

§ 2

We humbly offer our gratitude for your divine grace.

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara.

May your Mitama flourish ever more.

Hirunahime wears a pure white Shiroshiyōzoku and a Murasakiiro skirt, unfurls a Ginsen, has an accompaniment arranged while playing a Kurabuikōdo, and sings a Shiyukuka herself.

Hirunahime: 'On Mount Victor, with Chigi soaring high,

and the Ōmiyabashira standing thick and firm,

the Sumekami enshrined and dwelling here,

before Your rare Mimahe, with reverence deep,

we offer words of gratitude and praise.

Now, the Kunigara of Biku,

since the distant days of Kamiyo of yore,

passed down along with Tsukihi evermore,

maintaining rightly the Kejime true

between lord and vassal, a land of Kami indeed.

Both in the skies of Kumoi and upon the earth below,

living in harmony, affection, and grace, like parent and child,

thus had it peacefully governed come;

yet the days of Amatsumisora flowed away,

the moon drifted and the stars shifted,

and the foul Makaze blew in wild storms,

striking the branches of the Kigi along the Yomo yamabe,

as if stripped of their Hagoromo,

in such a most lamentable Kunigara,

it suddenly fell into disorder and desolation.

Appearing as the pillar of the imperial land,

my lord and husband, the Kshatriya,

deeply afflicted his heart,

and praying morning and evening

to the great deity Shionagahiko

who appeared in the land of Tokoyo,

desiring to govern all under heaven peacefully

and with utmost tranquility,

even those sincere prayers vanished like bubbles,

and the age became one where Magatsukami,

Yamata no Orochi, and ugly demons raged fiercely.

Though the River Lion flows on endlessly

with waters forever running,

the Kshatriya house, on the verge of perishing,

to rescue it from its collapse and reverse its fall,

and to comfort and soothe the divine will of the lord,

from the frail heart of a woman,

while casting false glances

at the wicked and lawless Magokami,

and muddying the blood of my own body,

repenting of those transgressions,

recanting them before the Mimahe

of the deeply compassionate Mikatsuhi no Kami,

and having them heard anew to be restored as before,

governing successfully as an arch-duchess,

is truly a boundless blessing.

As a token of a sincere heart

to repay such precious divine grace,

requesting permission from the Kshatriya lord

to build the great palace here,

and at last granted by the divine personage Chigunowake,

the joy of finishing the service at the rare palace,

ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara,

may the Sumerookami live forever,

not to mention the imperial land of Biku,

may my lord, the hundred officials,

and the people of the four quarters remain free from harm,

preserving their lives in this lovely manifest world,

diligently applying themselves to their daily livelihoods,

and advancing the prosperity of the country

into a sacred era as everlasting and firm

as the pine tree's green,

we pray to You, Kamunagara,

with reverence before Your Mimahe.'

So singing to the end, she took her seat.
