Chapter 1: 子 Treasure [1387]

子宝〔1387〕

§ 1

Victoria, ravaged by the Hanshiyō Belz, Was in a state beyond recognition. The Sumika of the King was at last By the Sendenshi Harukuniwake And his Itsukau rescued, As if gaining a new life Amidst nine deaths under the early summer sky. Dust and refuse from the very Nesoko Were blown away, and Taihei Became the Miyo once again. Hirunahime was restored To her former rank, and Mameyaka While serving the Archduke, She enshrined the Kami, and within the Jiyōnai, Needless to say, and throughout the Kuninaka, It was governed in peace and tranquility, Which was indeed Medetaki.

The Hanshiyō Belz, Schier, and the rest of their Itsupa were to be sentenced to Jiukei for Hangiyakuzai according to the Kokuhafu; however, through the Atsusen of Harukuniwake, Matsuhiko, Tatsuhiko, and Mankō, a Taishiya was granted. The Riyōnin were ordered to Hiyakukanichi of confinement, and it was decided that four guards would strictly watch the gate.

Kshatriya (the Taikō) was gradually reaching old age, and as the world became peaceful, he recalled the future and was pained at heart for not having a single heir. King Victoria had five sons and one daughter with the Archduchess (the Taikō-hi). There were children named Earl, East, Wales, Erinan, Oaks, and the girl Daiya, but one night the King had a dream... a terrifying dream where the five sons banished him, divided the land of Vic into five, each asserting their own supremacy and throwing the country into disorder. Thus, he plotted with Victoriahime in the middle of the night to secretly kill his five biological sons. He even went so far as to say, 'If the child currently in your womb is a boy, I shall kill him as well; if it is a girl, I shall spare her.' Hearing this, Victoriahime was greatly shocked, yet she deliberately feigned ignorance, worrying deeply, 'King Victoria, whose temperament is such that once he speaks he never retracts no matter how much he is admonished, will surely not spare the five children; and what shall I do if the child in my womb is a boy?' Nevertheless, she feigned ignorance, her heart overflowing with ten thousand koku of tears. Then, she secretly whispered her father's resolution to the five boys and commanded, 'Escape with your lives as quickly as possible.' The five brothers were greatly shocked, and having been given various items by Victoriahime, they slipped out of the Jiyōnai under the cover of night, bored a hole in the mountain valleys of Terukunigadake, which continues from the peaks of Mount Victor, to avoid disaster, and maintained their lives by becoming hunters.

Victoriahime gave birth when her months were full; when she hurriedly examined the infant, it was a girl. Feeling relieved at last, she named her Daiya. When Daiyahime turned seven, she lamented to her mother about her lack of siblings. Thereupon, Victoriahime secretly told her the story of how she had five older brothers who were hiding as hunters in Terukunigadake. Upon hearing this, Daiyahime could not help but want to meet her five brothers, and when she turned ten, she secretly escaped the castle at night, followed the peaks with her delicate feet, and finally arrived at the valley of Terukunigadake. When she looked, there was a fairly large earthen cave where animal skins and such were being dried. Four of the brothers were out hunting and absent, but the fifth brother, Oaks, was keeping watch alone. Fearing discovery, the five had determined that they would strike down and never let return any human who came to this place. As Daiya arrived there utterly exhausted, Oaks, with his eyes rolling, spoke.

Oaks: 'Where have you come from?'

Daiya: 'Yes, I am called Daiya, the daughter of King Victoria. When I heard from my mother... she said that in secret from my father, for about ten years, my five brothers have been hiding as hunters in this Mount Terukuni. She said she has kept you hidden, saying that since my father is also aged, you should return when the land is transferred. Because I could not help but want to meet my brothers, I have come to find you in secret from my parents.'

So saying, she fell prostrate and wept aloud. Ōkusu carefully examined Daiya's face and found that she bore a resemblance to his elder brothers in some way, and also to his mother. However, he worried greatly, wondering what his four elder brothers would say when they returned; even though they were parents and siblings, since it had been decided to take their lives, would they not kill this Karen sister... and with a clouded voice, he asked.

Ōkusu: 'You are indeed my sister without a doubt; you have done well to come. Our Guwankoitsupen Chichiō, saying he had a dream, intended to kill us five brothers. By our mother's compassion, we have managed to preserve our Inochi, but I wonder if our father is still Otatsushiya.'

Daiya replied amidst her tears.

Daiya: 'Yes, Father is extremely Otatsushiya. And as for Mother, in my seventh year, she became distressed over the matter of my brothers, which led to her falling into Biyōki, and she passed away. Afterwards, a Komazukahi named Hirunahime became Father's consort, and it has been Ichinen this Kotoshi. With Mother gone and my brothers away, I felt no desire to remain Jiyōnai, so I have come seeking after you. Since I no longer wish to return Jiyōnai, Dōzo let me stay Koko forever.'

She joined her Riyōte and pleaded through her tears.

Ōkusu: 'Ah, you have done well to seek us out. However, until my brothers return, please hide inside this Tsuzura. After I have heard my brothers' feelings, if they say they will spare you, I shall have you Kōzen declare yourself as our Kiyōdai; but if they say they will strike you dead, though it is a pity, I will keep you in this Tsuzura and send you away somewhere after my brothers have gone...'

Daiya: 'I leave everything to your kindness; I shall be satisfied even if I meet my brothers and am killed.'

She sobbed and wept.

Ōkusu: 'It is almost time for my elder brothers to return, so come, get in here.'

He placed Daiya inside the Tsuzura and assumed an air of indifference. There, his four elder brothers—Āru, Īsu, Werusu, and Erinan—returned lightheartedly, having captured rabbits and badgers. Ōkusu went out to meet them.

Ōkusu: 'Brothers, you are Taihen early today.'

Āru: 'Yes, as we were able to catch rabbits and badgers so conveniently, for some reason I felt hurried today, feeling as though some abnormality had occurred regarding you, so I returned in haste.'

So saying, he removed his footwear and entered the spacious cave to sit down.

Āru: 'I wonder if anything changed during my absence; I simply could not help feeling hurried.'

Ōkusu: 'Ah, was that so? To tell the truth, our sister came seeking us. However, though I thought to strike her dead in accordance with our pact, she was so pitiable that I mimicked a monster and chased her away.'

Thus he spoke, testing his brother's opinion.

Āru: 'That we have a sister—well, I cannot comprehend it; if so, she must be a child born between our parents after I departed.'

Ōkusu: 'Mother was pregnant when we fled, and the one born was a girl, a sister named Daiya.'

Āru: 'Why did you drive such a person away? I too wished to meet her once; well, you have done a regrettable thing.'

Īsu: 'If Father were to discover such a place as this, he might come to attack us again, bringing a large army. If you were going to let her return, why did you not kill her, however pitiable it might be?'

Āru: 'Nay, there is no need to kill her, but why did you not stop our sister? She would know the state of affairs Jiyōnai; Father cannot be expected to live forever, and if only Mother were Otatsushiya, we could return later and rule the land of Biku. But if our sister has returned, this will cause Taihen trouble; we must flee this place as quickly as possible and hide ourselves somewhere.'

So he spoke with an anxious look.

Oaks: 'According to my younger sister's words, our mother passed away three years ago, and our father is exceedingly robust, having made the lady-in-waiting Hirnahime his archduchess and being in tremendous spirits; thus the wish of us brothers will surely never be realized.'

Upon hearing that their affectionate mother had passed away, the four elder brothers raised their voices and wept aloud all at once.

Oaks: 'Brother, if our younger sister should come here seeking us, will you cherish her, or is it your intention to kill her?'

Thus he peered into the faces of the four elder brothers, and the four spoke in unison,

Brothers: 'Since we hold no grudge against our younger sister, if things have come to this, let all six of us brothers and sister become as one to the very end, live in harmony, and await the opportune time to accomplish our objective, shall we not?'

At these words Oaks was relieved at last, and

Oaks: 'Truth be told, I have hidden my younger sister inside this tsuzura.'

When he so spoke, Earl immediately opened the tsuzura, took tender care of his younger sister, brought her outside, and the five gathered around her, stroking her head, rubbing her back, and all six brothers and sister clung together, wept tears of joy. And so the brothers and sister continued their lonely life in the mountain dwelling at this place.

Meanwhile, in the inner chamber of Setsuteiri, Hirnahime, Kuniwake, Taruman, Kyūbitto, and Ekusu held a small banquet, indulging in talks on various topics. Kyūbitto turned toward Kuniwake,

Rashu: 'Thanks to the grace of the Ōkami of Sango and the exertive efforts of you masterly teachers, the rebel general Berutsu has at last surrendered and been ordered to undergo house arrest as you see, ah, with which I feel relieved for the moment; yet Setsuteiri-sama is advanced in age, and possessing no heir, he is exceedingly worried. Might there be no method by which he could be blessed with a child?'

So he asked with an anxious look. Kuniwake, seeing his chance, leaned forward,

Kuniwake: 'Does not Setsuteiri-sama have five sons and one daughter, namely Āru, Īsu, Uerusu, Erinan, Ōku, and Daiya-sama? If you were to welcome them back with abundant courtesy, you would surely be able to secure a splendid heir.'

Rashu: 'Yes, as you say, there were six children, but at present their whereabouts are unknown, so in truth Setsuteiri-sama has grown old and longs for his children, searching for them in secret. Nevertheless, their whereabouts remain utterly unknown, and even if their whereabouts were known, it is unlikely they would return.'

Kuniwake: 'Setsuteiri-dono, if I were to deliver the six children to you, what would you do? Would you harbor your former thoughts and intend to slaughter them all?'

Setsuteiri, while wiping his tears,

Setsuteiri: 'In truth, being possessed by an evil demon, I suffered bad dreams continuously for a week, and believing that my enemies had been born as my own children, I conceived the cruel intention to beat to death every single one of the five sons without exception; but somehow sensing this, they fled the castle grounds during the night and hid somewhere to plot, so that not knowing when they might come to destroy me, I have scarcely slept a wink even through the night watches.'

Kuniwake: 'That is a total misconception on your part; your children are truly gentle and good persons who, while currently leading a wretched life, think of the nation, think of the royal house, and bear not the slightest grudge. If you now reform your heart and invite the six children into the castle, they will surely fulfill their filial piety to the utmost.'

Setsuteiri: 'Are they still alive in this world? Or might they be alive and plotting something evil... I cannot help but worry.'

Kuniwake: 'Do not worry in the least. I shall take full responsibility.'

Setsuteiri: 'If it is the word of Kuniwake-sama, there can be no mistake. If they are indeed among the living in this world, I would dearly wish to meet them at least once.'

Kuniwake: 'Very well, leave it to me for two or three days. I shall surely arrange for you to meet them.'

Setsuteiri, displaying an attitude half of joy and half of anxiety, relied upon the words of none other than Kuniwake as his strength, and entrusted everything to him. Rashu, Ushushin, Taruman, and Hirnahime all bowed their heads together, and spoke to Kuniwake,

Retainers: 'We earnestly entreat you to kindly handle this matter.'

Thus with tears they made their plea. Kuniwake returned to the beautiful mansion granted to him, and after consulting with Katsuhiko, Tatsuhiko, and Bankō, planned the welcome and return of the six children.

Thereupon, the three men, in accordance with Kuniwake's command, prepared their travel attire to cross Mount Bikutoru and head toward the mountain valleys of Terukunigatake, bound themselves in straw sandals and gaiters to welcome the six children, and holding aloft hand-flags inscribed with the word 'Welcome', set forth secretly without letting anyone know to seek them out.

治国『それは貴方の御心得違ひといふもの、貴方のお子様は実に温良な方で、今はみじめな生活をし乍らも、国を思ひ、王家を思ひ、少しも恨んではゐられませぬよ。貴方が今改心して、六人のお子様を城内へお招きになれば、キツと孝養を尽されるでせう』

刹帝『まだ此世に生きて居るでせうか。但は生きて何か悪い事を企んで居りは致しますまいか……と心配でなりませぬ』

治国『決して御心配なさいますな。私が引受けませう』

刹帝『治国別様のお言葉なれば、決して間違はありますまい。何卒此世に居ります者なれば、一度会はして頂き度いもので厶います』

刹帝『宜しい、二三日私にお任せ下さい。キツとお会はせ致しませう』

刹帝利は半喜び、半不安の態乍ら、外ならぬ治国別の言葉を力とし、一切を任して了つた。左守、右守を始めタルマン、ヒルナ姫も一同に頭を下げ、治国別に、

『何分宜しく頼みます』

と涙と共に頼み入る。治国別は自分の与へられた美しい館へ帰り、松彦、竜彦、万公と相談の上、六人の子女を迎へ帰る事を謀つた。

茲に三人は治国別の命に依つて、ビクトル山を越え、照国ケ岳の山谷を指して旅装を整へ、六人の子女を迎ふべく、草鞋脚絆に身を固め、奉迎といふ各手旗を翳し乍ら、誰にも知らさず秘に尋ね行く事となりぬ。
