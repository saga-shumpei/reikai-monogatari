§ 1

Samori advanced into the Ima of Harukuniwake, and having tightly closed the sliding doors, he related the Tenmatsu of what had occurred, intending to lower his voice and seek an Iken together with Matsuhiko and Tatsuhiko regarding the Ketsukonmondai of Āru.

Harukuni: 'Naruhodo, that must indeed be a difficulty, and we must think of something. However, regarding this Renaikuwankei alone, it can by no means be Seishi by any Keni whatsoever. Since all things that are love are asserting the self by means of the very Hauki of the self, when one reaches the Shiukiyoku of love, one does not care for one's Chii or property; it is truly a fierce thing. When that love increases and reaches the extreme of intensity, one comes to joyfully cast away even one's own life without regret; therefore, no matter how much one is a Propaganda Minister, one's power will not suffice regarding this problem. It is not a rare example in the world that there are those who cast away their lives without looking back at anything else for the sake of loving their nation, for the sake of loving their lord, or for the sake of loving money. Especially those like the martyrs who offer their lives upon the altar of God's love for the sake of a burning religious conviction are almost the same as romantic love. One cannot criticize this intense love by labeling it merely as a narrow and selfish act. Compared to the intensity of the degree of love, it cannot escape becoming increasingly concentrated. Accordingly, that it appears most narrow in romantic love merely proves that romantic love is more intensely concentrated than any other love and reaches the highest degree of incandescent heat. Since the love of these two people stands in the same state of mind as a martyr who dies for the faith without regret, I believe it is the best policy to settle it as peacefully as possible.'

Mankō was standing outside the sliding doors, inclining his ear to hear how the situation was, and upon hearing the response of Harukuniwake, he thought, '...Somehow these are fragrant words. And although I do not understand clearly, since I heard words like Taimen or Shiyuda, it must surely be about myself. Harukuniwake-sama is also great; as I thought, he is favoring me...' and while rejoicing, he continued to listen with strained ears. The conversation proceeded with the voices gradually becoming lower. Mankō eventually forgot that he was standing outside the sliding doors and, opening the doors about five inches, he thrust his face in. However, as the four people had their heads huddled together and were desperately racking their brains over this problem, they did not notice Mankō peeping in. Mankō softly edged up behind Tatsuhiko and, looking down while feigning his voice, said in a gentle womanly tone:

Mankō: 'Only in the state of mind of romantic love can a human being be the most perfect person. Furthermore, in a human being who has never once tasted love in their whole life, or a human being who never associates with the opposite sex throughout their life, there is surely some great defect as a human. Isn't it so, Harukuniwake-sama? That the source of all human love lies in sexual desire is surely the divine law since the beginning of creation; if there is no sexual desire, there is no romantic love. If there is no romantic love, there are no things that are love at all.'

When Harukuniwake and the others suddenly heard a strange voice and raised their faces all at once, they saw Mankō shrinking behind Tatsuhiko and trembling. Samori, quickly seeing the figure of Mankō before his eyes, said:

Samori: 'Ohohohoho, Mankō-sama has appeared at the seat of the secret meeting. Come now, Mankō-sama, do not be in Shinpai. For it is certainly not about you!'

万公『ナアニ、別に遠い事もありませぬ、雪隠の窓から覗いて居つたら、鳶と烏がつるんでをつたので、此奴、妙な事だなア、大方刹帝利の娘と首陀の息子とが婚礼をする前兆だと思つたものですから、突止めやうとここ迄やつて来た所、たうとうここでパツと放れ、あの通り中空を翔つてをるのですよ。何とマア不思議な事があるものですなア』

§ 4

Matsuhiko: 'Don't talk nonsense; is there ever such a thing as a kite and a crow mating?'

Mankō: 'Well, since that is why it is strange, I am watching like this. Since Ten is showing us such a heuhon, it may well be that the daughter of a Setsuteiri will apply for a ketsukon to the son of a Shiyuda, and they will auspiciously hold the ceremony of gafukin. Matsuhiko-sama, since you have a ritsupa wife, you are an outsider concerning ketsukonmondai. As I am currently in the kenkiuchiu, please do not make a jiyama. It would be troublesome to treat a kikonshiya and a mikonshiya dōitsu, you see.'

Matsuhiko: 'Oh, what a troublesome fellow. Saying such things, you chased after Samori and had yourself put to shame, didn't you? You are a fine tsurayogo for our party of sendenshi. I will give you leave from now on, so go back to Kogitayama or wherever, and become a plaything for Okiku-sama or something. Harukuniwake-sama says he is cutting all ties of master and pupil as of this very moment, and is terribly angry, you know.'

Mankō: 'Ah, how uninteresting it is to be attached to a teacher who lacks tact. However, if I am cast aside in a place like this, my pride as a man will not stand either; well, I suppose I shall endure it and let you accompany me as far as Koganeeyama.'

Matsuhiko: 'That will not do, no matter what. Since it was once uttered that you would cut the ties of master and pupil, it is no use whatever you say. Come, I have brought traveling expenses entrusted to me, so take these and return to Kogitayama.'

Mankō: 'Tatsuhiko's fellow has slyly crept right under the teacher's throat and is plotting to seize my lover, isn't he? That must be it. Because it is somewhat inconvenient for Manko-sama to be here...'

Matsuhiko: 'Don't talk foolishness; Tatsuhiko is not such a man. He is a pure and undefiled sendenshi. He is not a rotten man who casts his eyes on women while on official business. When you say such things, I feel so terribly sorry for Tatsuhiko. To surmise people's hearts based on your own base mind—is there no limit to how senseless that is?'

Mankō: 'Hmph, you say so, but you, Harukuniwake, are the younger brother, and Tatsuhiko-sama is the brother-in-law; the three brothers have joined hearts with the intention of neatly ostracizing poor outsider Mankō-sama, haven't you? Even if you say ritsupa things with your mouth, it seems you still practice favoritism after all. Well then, I will return from here to the mansion of Saien and petition for every single one of your three unfair treatments, so be prepared for that. I don't need traveling expenses like this!'

So saying, he flung the money Matsuhiko had handed him down onto the lawn. Matsuhiko was greatly disconcerted at being accused of favoritism, and resolved to ask Harukuniwake once more to forgive Mankō's offense and let him accompany them on official business as far as Koganeeyama; thus, he decided to soothe Mankō in various ways, coaxing and wheedling him, and lead him back to Harukuniwake's mansion. And by means of Harukuniwake's earnest admonitions, Mankō finally succeeded in severing his thought of attachment toward Princess Daiya.
