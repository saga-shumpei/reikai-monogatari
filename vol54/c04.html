Chapter 4: 理妻 [1390]

理妻〔1390〕

§ 1

Starting with Setsuteiri Victoria-Ō, the Arch-Duchess Hirnahime felt kiyōki at the return of Earl, offering various gentle kotoba and celebrating his buji; furthermore, Setsuteiri firmly grasped Earl's hand and, with tears, apologized for his fumei in being deceived by an akugami and attempting to commit satsugai upon all of his saiai no kodomo. Earl realized that his father's heart had unexpectedly softened and that his previous hatred had been instigated by an akugami; harboring no resentment toward his parent, he rejoiced in their long-awaited menkuwai, thanked his father for his kauon, and minutely related the monogata of the hardships of his sanchiuseikuwatsu, his sister's visit one year ago, and the ikisatsu of how the sannin no Sendenshi had come to welcome him, becoming overwhelmed by tears of joy. Samori, Umori, and the Naijitsukasa Tarman also rejoiced as if a dead man had returned, and they satsuoku performed hairei at the Shinden, partook of the miki of the nahorahi, and prayed for the banzai of the Ōke. Then, numerous retainers were dispatched to the manor of Kuninakawake to welcome the five siblings into the castle, and inviting Kuninakawake and his entire party, they held a banquet and offered thanks for the divine grace. Compared to Earl, Hirnahime was different in age by as much as a parent and child. Nevertheless, Earl respected Hirnahime as if she were his true mother, and Hirnahime also cared for the Earl siblings as if they were her own children, showing them kindness in both shadow and light. Setsuteiri, realizing he was aged and his remaining life was short, had Earl succeed the house, and for the five younger siblings, he divided the land of Vic into six parts, determining each of their domains to serve as the bulwark of Earl's Ōke and protect the nation. He bestowed Prince upon East, Kings upon Wales, Count upon Erinan, Viscount upon Oaks, and gave Baron to Dayahime, dividing the territories in the four directions of the land to protect the nation for eternity.

First of all, the necessity of welcoming a consort for the elder brother Earl became pressing. Samori and Umori hurried in all directions seeking a suitable spouse, but there was no woman who met Earl's favor. Although they selected candidates many times and showed them to Earl, Earl merely shook his head left and right and rejected them. Furthermore, Earl suddenly experienced a physical disorder due to the change in his environment and food, and although he had returned to his former eminent status, he could not bear the stifling aristocratic life, continuing to agonize, thinking that if this were the case, he should not have returned, and that he wished the six siblings could live harmoniously in the mountains, hunting as they pleased and leading a simple life. Eventually, appearing to have developed a slight abnormality in his spirit, he made it his pleasure to slip out of the castle at every opportunity and wander alone through the countryside. No matter how much Setsuteiri, Samori, or Umori admonished him, he would not listen at all. Finally, Setsuteiri was at his wit's end and sought the counsel of Kuninakawake. Kuninakawake replied to the effect that it would be best to leave Earl to his own devices for a while. Since these were the kotoba of none other than Kuninakawake, the high-ranking officials from Setsuteiri downward left Earl to do as he pleased. Earl cast off his splendid clothes, placed a forage cap upon his head, donned a pelisse, slipped out of the castle, and was resting alone in the shade of the pine forest at the foot of Mount Victor. While gazing at the clouds passing through the sky and sighing 'Ah, ah,' thinking he would like to catch a rabbit if one should pass by, a sturdy, clumsy woman of about twenty years old came there, carrying a rake and a basket on her back to rake up withered pine needles. The woman was a daughter of the Shudra named Hannah. Seeing Earl's figure and not knowing he was a noble prince, she placed her basket beside him and busily gathered the withered pine needles, stuffing them into the basket. Earl: 'Hey, I do not know what woman you are, but will you not let me help you a little? I would like you to lend me that Kumade for a moment.'

While Hanna thought, ...He is a man of high Kihin from somewhere... and, filled with Dōjiyōshin, thought, ...If what this person says is Hontau, it is truly a pity; is there not some Kufū to save him somehow?... she threw the Matsubaka aside, approached Earl's side, and:

Hanna: 'Moshi, I do not know where you are from, but if you have made such a Goketsushin, how about it? Will you stop the Kubitsu and live Itsushiyo with me? I am the daughter of a lowly Shiyuda, but since my brother is taking over the family line, I am not in a position to succeed my parents. Hanna: 'I dislike Futsū men. With only young masters who know neither the taste of profligacy nor the taste of the world, one can never create a harmonious home. Rather than that, a person who has fallen sufficiently and made a Goketsushin to the point of throwing away their life must surely know both the sour and the sweet of the world. How about it? Do you not have the thought to prolong the life you were going to throw away and live a lifetime with me? Since my body is strong like this, I will work for two people. Even if you were to fall ill, I would not be troubled. You may laugh at me as a shameless creature for a woman to propose marriage, but I have prayed to God morning and evening that I wanted to become a couple with someone who has fallen to the very bottom like you. Since I have received a fair amount of property divided from my parents, I will not let you suffer hardship easily.'

Earl: 'Indeed, you are an admirable woman. However, your appearance is quite bad.'

Hanna: 'If my appearance is bad and not to your liking, it cannot be helped. However, please think well yourself.'

Earl: 'No, I never care for appearance. I want a woman who possesses a splendid heart like yours. To tell the truth, I have been asked to take many beautiful women as brides until now, but somehow they did not please me; it was not interesting even staying inside, and being unable to endure the annoyance of more talk of brides, I came all the way Koko. However, I have completely used up my parents' fortune, and what is more, I suffer from a venereal disease and have Botamochi-scabies all over my body, so no one will come near my side. Despite that, I am troubled because there are Takusan Maoshiko for Yomeiri. Coming here, I am again proposed to by you; meeting with marriage attacks day after day, I have no place to set myself in this wide heaven and earth. Please, say no more. Even if you take someone like me as a husband, the end cannot be accomplished, and I must cause you hardship.'

Arl was struck with deep emotion, shaking his head at Hanna's Kotoba, thinking what a truly Shinsetsu and beautiful-hearted woman she was. The woman again took the Kumade in her hand, and while gathering Karematsuba,

Hanna: 'Moshi, Anata, can you by no means listen to what I say? Thus she continued her Netsushin Kudo. Arl, while deciding in his heart that this was indeed the one he should make his Niyōbau, yet wishing to test her heart further just to be sure, suddenly opened his large mouth, and while intentionally letting saliva drip, began to perform a Mane of a mute, going 'Aaaah, aaaah.' Seeing this, Hanna's Shinki became suddenly excited, and while worrying whether he had become a mute, she thought... I know not from where he comes, but he is a truly pitiful person. All the more, I must save this person even if I sacrifice my own life... and she went behind him, stroking his back and praying to God, wishing for his illness to reach full recovery as quickly as possible. In the meantime, Arl untied his sash and threw it over a pine branch. The woman, startled, tried to catch and stop him. Arl, with the limit of his voice,

Arl: 'Ya, young lady, my body is covered in scabies. It will be terrible if it infects you!'

Hanna: 'No, no, no matter Nanihodo the scabies may infect me, how could I overlook the critical moment of your life?'

Entrusting herself to her great strength, she hugged him firmly around the waist and would not let go. No matter how much Arl struggled, he could do nothing against the woman's great strength. Arl, thinking to reveal his lineage for the first time,

Arl: 'Nay, the truth is, I am the one called Arl, the eldest son of King Victoria. Dōzo, release me. Your beautiful heart has touched me to the bone. In truth, I am neither a sufferer of scabies nor of syphilis.'

When he stated the facts, Hanna was astonished and leapt back about two or three ken, and while rubbing her head against the ground,

Hanna: 'I thought you were a person unusual in every way, but I did not know you were such a noble person; I have been truly rude. Dōzo, I pray for your forgiveness of my sins many times over. I am sorry for saying such improper things as asking you to make me your Niyōbau, which is a thing to be feared.'

She thus fearfully offered her apology. Arl changed his tone and said,

Arl: 'Ya, though you are said to be the daughter of a Shudra, you are a truly admirable woman. Will you not Dōzo become my Niyōbau? If it is with you, I shall likely be able to spend my life in happiness.'

At these Kotoba, Hanna, while trembling in her body,

Hanna: 'How could a lowly person such as Jibun do such an unworthy thing? Dōzo, I pray for your forgiveness in this matter alone.'

She thus repeatedly shook her head and apologized. Arl spent a thousand words and ten thousand phrases, finally persuading Hanna, and taking her by the hand, they returned happily to Hofus.

Samori-no-tsukasa, seeing this, was greatly surprised, and while pointing his mouth and rounding his eyes,

Samori-no-tsukasa: 'Moshi, your lordship always flies out of the castle at will, and while your parents are in great Shinpai, do you not notice it at all? Furthermore, what is this? To return to the castle pulling the hand of such a lowly woman—have you not lost your mind?'

Earl: 'Between my Guwanko father and the Guwanko Samori, you are truly troublesome people. You Mahetachi, who are obsessed with worthless Keishiki such as Kizoku or Heimin and do not think of the Kokuka no Daiji, are truly Fuchiyūfugi. Why will you not listen to what I say?'

Samori: 'Even so, it affects the Meiyo of the House of Victoria; moreover, if you make such a filthy woman your consort, first of all, Anatasama's Gokeni will fall to the ground, leading to the contempt of the Yakunin, and how then shall the Kokuka be governed? I beg you to please reconsider that alone. Is this not entirely a Kichiga no Sata?'

Earl: 'Hmph, I am Kichiga, and you are Torichiga. A nightingale to the bamboo, a sparrow to the plum—is that not called Kichiga or Torichiga? While being in the position of a Kshatriya who must consider the Jidai no Sūsei and perform the Tenka no Keirin through Shiyōkaitsuchi, how can the Kokuka be governed if you regard Kizoku marriage as your sole accomplishment while being obsessed with worthless Keishiki? Think on it a little.'

Samori: 'Ah, ah, a troublesome problem has erupted. If I were to report such a thing to the Kshatriya-sama, there is no telling how he would scold me. Serving at court is something that should never have been done. What should I do?'

Thus he spoke, folding his hands together while tears dripped from both eyes.

Earl: 'Guwanko fellows like Chichiuhe and you cannot understand my spirit. At any rate, let us request a judgment from the Propagandist Kunioshiwake; if it be the words of Kunioshiwake-sama, even my Guwanko father will surely listen.'

Samori: 'Indeed, then I shall follow your command; since I would be flatly rejected even if I spoke to Chichiuhe, I shall go from here to the manor of Kunioshiwake-sama to inquire. And if Kunioshiwake-sama says it is well, I shall have him negotiate with the Kshatriya-sama. I pray you, until then, please remain hidden in your private room, the two of you.'

While saying this, he hurriedly left the entrance and rushed toward the manor of Kunioshiwake; the two of them hid themselves in Earl's private room.
