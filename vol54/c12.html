Chapter 12: Phantom 瞑酒 [1398]

妖瞑酒〔1398〕

§ 1

Kō, Otsu, and Hei shoved the two men, Bet and Fuel, into a storehouse, and took turns keeping watch at the entrance with the lock. This estate belonged to a wealthy farmer who had served as the village headman of Tamaki for generations, and storehouses lined up there to the number of some twenty. Once shut inside here, there was no way anyone would ever notice; dropping water and food from the window, they decided to confine the two here until the daughter returned. Then, the tunics of the two were completely stripped off, and appropriate clothing was provided. The name of Kō was Shīna. This man was a hereditary retainer of the Thames family and managed all the household affairs of the Thames family.

Now Michiharuwake, guided by Shīna, appeared before the married couple Thames and Berisina, and recounted in detail how he had met Bet and Fuel at the summit of Mount Shimeji, and how, struck by their divine power, he had been dragged all the way here. The Thames couple were extremely overjoyed and brought out tea and confectionery, had hot water boiled to show them to the bath, made them change into missionary garments, invited them into the guest room, and recounted the circumstances of their daughter's dangerous plight.

Thames: 'You are the globally renowned missionary-sama of the Ananaikyō; thank you very much for coming. However, since the two daughters have been carried off into the midst of an army of many thousands, even you, sir, with such mighty divine power, will likely find it difficult to easily retrieve them, won't you?'

He peered into his face. Michiharuwake folded both hands for a moment as if in thought, but smiling as though with some certainty,

Michiharuwake: 'Ah, please do not worry at all. It will hardly go smoothly with just one or two ways, but I shall devise some scheme, slip into the midst of the enemy, and bring your young lady back. However, even if we bring her back, it will all come to nothing if they come to take her back once again; this fellow must either thoroughly reform the enemy or simply drive them away, or else it will no longer do.'

Berisina: 'We earnestly beg you, since we, an old couple, draw our necks in and worry day and night, to please save us through the divine power of God.'

Michiharuwake: 'Your house appears to be of the Ural religion, but do you have no inclination to believe in the Ananaikyō?'

Berisina: 'Yes, God is originally of one stock; to have descendants suddenly change the God worshipped by their ancestors feels somehow as though it would be a wrong to our ancestors. If we enshrine the God of Ananaike that you practice in faith, will divine punishment not befall us?'

Michiharuwake: 'Since God is of one stock, whether one follows the Ural religion or the Ananaikyō, God is not one to speak of such petty matters, and to believe in the teachings of utmost grace, devoid of falsehood and solely of sincerity, is truly filial piety toward one's ancestors. First of all, let us enshrine the God of Ananaike, and pray through that divine virtue that the lives of you two may be saved. Or else, if you say that you are by all means reluctant to abandon the Ural religion, then that is fine; I shall never force you to do so...'

Thames: 'I know not what the old woman's opinion may be, but since we suffer such agonizing trials while thus believing in the God of Ural from morning till evening all this while, I have begun to doubt whether the God of the Ural religion has also somehow gone astray lately. In fact, even King Victoria of the country of Biku is of the Ural religion, yet he met with such a great disaster, and rumors are spreading that he was saved by the God of Ananaike. Please, I beseech you favorably. Berisina, surely you too have no objection, do you?'

Berisina: 'If you say so, I, your wife, shall raise no objection whatsoever. Please have it enshrined without fail.'

§ 2

Michiharu: 'If so, let us enshrine together the Kamisama of Ananai-kyō and Bankoshinnōsama, who protects the Ural-kyō. For the Kamisama are fundamentally one, you see.'

Thames: 'Just as you say, truly impartial and selfless kotoba; let us first of all enshrine the Kamisama, and upon that pray to have our daughter saved.'

And now, the resolution of the husband and wife having been finally settled, Michiharuwake suddenly constructed a Shinden, finished a kantan ritual, and prepared to advance toward Mount Inokurayama to take back the sisters Sumieru and Sugaru. Fortunately, as there were the military uniforms of Betto and Fueru, both Michiharuwake and Shīna donned them, and they were to slip away into the night and advance into the encampment.

Shīna, knowing the locality well as it was nearby, thoroughly understood the geography of Mount Inokurayama. Leaping across the rapid current of the valley river to the right, crossing to the left, they gradually arrived at a wide valley mouth enclosed on three sides—east, north, and west—by deep mountains. Having slipped in among some three thousand soldiers wearing the same military uniforms, they would catch the eye of high officers, but in the attire of sergeants and common privates, there was no fear of being easily seen through.

The moon was peeking over the edge of the eastern mountain, casting its light upon the valley river. From among the young leaves here and there, the voices of cuckoos could be heard delightfully. At the foot of a giant rock that seemed to reach up to the sky, four or five Brahmin soldiers were sitting cross-legged on the ground and keeping night watch. All of them seemed to be intoxicated with alcohol.

Soldier A: 'Hey, even though there are no enemies, to be made to do night watch day after day, isn't it dull? The night watch these days has turned to sheer desperation; unless we drink desperate liquor, we can't get through it. That sour, spoiled-like sake, the colonel fellow... saying, "This is for the night watches to drink,"... and spitting it out, just passing around what they left unfinished drinking, isn't it truly sickening?'

Soldier B: 'Still, it's better than not drinking. It's not as though he gave a strict command saying that unless one drinks this, it violates military regulations; since you'd probably be bored, how about drinking even this if you don't mind—it was given down by the colonel-sama, isn't getting even a slightly tainted sake better than getting none?'

Soldier A: 'Even if you say so, they themselves eat and drink their fill of mellow liquor, holding idle councils about nothing at all, calling it Hofukū and Gesurato, making Sumieru and Sugaru, who are exceptionally beautiful, pour drinks for them, and drooling over them; we common foot soldiers are treated almost as if we aren't human at all.'

Soldier B: 'Don't talk nonsense, that is where endurance comes in. As long as you endure, flowers will bloom when the time comes. From now on, according to the general's orders, once the fortress of Mount Inokurayama is completed, we will ravage and go around neighboring countries, trample them under horsehoofs, and construct a great republic. When that happens, people will be needed no matter what; no matter how much of a common foot soldier you are, even you will be promoted to the rank of captain.'

Soldier A: 'Henh, even if I were to become a lieutenant, what is so wonderful about it? Don't they say a poverty-stricken sublieutenant, a make-do lieutenant, and a barely-scraping-by captain? How on earth can a wife be supported with such a thing? If you could make me at least a Junker, I would be willing to break my bones, but a general who holes up in a place like this out of fear of three or four propagandists of Ananai-kyō has already shown his future. No matter what anyone says, both generals, Oniharuhwake and Kumehiko, are fools.'

Soldier B: 'Hey, don't speak in such a loud voice. Hei, Tei, and Bo are listening with faces as if they had dozed off. A human heart is not something you can easily figure out. You never know when they might turn the tables on us and, looking all awe-struck, secretly report us before the general.'

Soldier A: 'Nonsense, if a fellow like that dares to do such a thing, he's done for in an instant.'

Incidentally, this wine contained a drug that induced a terribly caustic frenzy. At the very first sip, it had an indescribable texture upon the tongue. Oto:

Oto: 'Hah, Gunsaudono, you can really talk! As befits a superior officer of the thief army, you are full of wits.'

Deku: 'Calling a superior officer a thief, what do you mean?'

Oto: 'Still, going into people's houses and threatening them to get things is being a thief, isn't it? Hehehehe.'

Deku: 'Come, drink a cup and see. Bring out a cup.'

Oto: 'As for cups, we have nothing so clever. Since we have a temporary bamboo cup right here, please pour it into this. Wine poured into a bamboo tube is doubly and exceptionally sweet, you know.'

So saying, he thrust out a bamboo tube. Deku poured it with a gurgling sound from his gourd and gave it to him.

Deku: 'Hey, you must not drink it all by yourself. This is called Eumeishiyu; if you drink one mouthful, your life is extended by thirty years. If you drink two mouthfuls, your lifespan is shortened by thirty years. Therefore, you are to pass this around for five people to drink.'

Oto: 'How difficult, what a nerve-wracking wine this is.'

So saying, claiming he could not even take a single sip, he held a full mouthful in his mouth. It had an indescribably good taste, and though he could not help wanting to drink another mouthful, perhaps thinking it a pity to shorten his lifespan by thirty years, he reluctantly passed it to Kō. Kō drank a sip, was struck by its flavor, and reluctantly passed it around to Hei, Tei, and Bo. By the time it reached Bo's mouth, there was barely enough even to wet the tongue. The five of them suddenly began to dance, grew short of breath, jumped into the river, crawled back out, began babbling things that made no sense, and dashed headlong back into the camp. A fiercely pungent odor emanated from it; those who smelled this odor were instantly infected, discarded their uniforms, became stark naked, and threw themselves into the middle of the river—that was the characteristic. Spreading one after another, the majority of the three-thousand-man army threw their swords into the mountain stream, took off their tunics, tossed those too into the mountain stream, became stark naked, and began chattering wildly about things that made no sense. Spreading on and on, with everyone stripping stark naked one after another, the colonel, Malta, saw this in astonishment and, resolving at all costs to report to the general, dashed headlong toward the main camp. A great multitude of naked soldiers, chattering in a gaggling manner about things that made no sense, formed ranks and charged toward the general's encampment.
