§ 1

The dawn sky is tinged with madder, and the voices of the Momodori bird sing 'Chiyochiyo', as if celebrating the prosperity of the Kokuka of Biku, and congratulating King Victoria upon the joyous event of the Oyako Taimen; thus, this morning even the chirping of the birds sounds somehow brave and inspiring.

In accordance with a Shinji, Harukuniwake realized before Mimei this morning that Matsuhiko and his group would return; he threw open the doors at the Kadoguchi, boiled hot water, arranged the Zabuton, and waited for them. Thereupon, the eight sons and one daughter returned in high spirits. The faces of the five Otokokiyōdai were covered all over with thick, bushy beards like bears. At first glance, they did not look like Ningen at all. Moreover, having lived in the mountains for a long time, grime had accumulated upon them to such a degree that one might suspect moss had grown all over their bodies.

Matsuhiko: 'Senseisama, thanks to your grace, we have at last brought back the six Gokiyōdai to welcome them.'

Harukuni: 'Ah, all three of you have worked hard. Now, now, Rokuninsama, please step up this way. I have boiled hot water, so please take a hot bath in order, starting from the eldest brother.'

Earl: 'No, I have no words to express my gratitude. I hear that my father was immensely indebted to you. On top of that, to rescue us brothers and sister as well—we regard you as if you were gods.'

Thus speaking, unlike a rough man, he shed tears of joy in quick succession.

Harukuni: 'I am humbled that you should express your thanks. In all things, I have merely been permitted to perform service for the sake of the Lord; therefore, please do not worry and enjoy the hot bath. Hey, Manko, guide them to the bathplace. And scrub off their grime.'

Manko: 'Yes, I understand. Come, Earl-sama, please enter first. I shall wash your back.'

So saying, he guided him to the bathroom. Matsuhiko and Tatsuhiko took turns letting the six brothers and sister bathe, kindly washing them; then they shaved off their beards entirely and had them change into the clothing for six people entrusted to them by Hōfusu. When they were transformed like this, every one of them appeared as noble as a refined young aristocrat. As for Daiyahime, being a woman, a man could not wash her back, so she bathed all by herself, carefully removing the grime from her body, tidied her lovelocks, wrapped herself in a beautiful Kosode, and with a smile, placed both hands on the floor before Harukuniwake, expressing her gratitude for her salvation.

Manko, seeing Daiyahime's appearance, was utterly astonished:

Manko: 'My, oh my, this is like Yoshinoyama in full bloom! I heard that women are like monsters, but hey, what on earth has happened? Compared to Okiku of Komokitayama, the difference is as night and day. What a grand and stylish beauty she is, ehehehe.'

Matsuhiko: 'Hey, Manko, are you still longing for Okiku? What a troublesome fellow you are. In that case, even if you become a monk, it will be no use.'

Manko: 'No, I have no complaints or anything anymore. I have fallen utterly and tremendously in love; ah, I am in love, I am in love. Even I am surprised at how deeply I have fallen in love.'

Matsuhiko: 'Ahahaha, you speak like a sculptor or a inspector of well-digging. What a troublesome fellow you are. In that case, you will have to remain as Manko forever.'

Manko: 'Because I brought back this Himesama by extending Manko's sympathy, well, since she has elder brothers, she won't succeed the Kshatriya house, and she is of a status where she will be sent off to marry somewhere, isn't she? Hey, Matsuhiko-sama, I'm giving up on Okiku now, ehehehehe.'

Matsuhiko: 'Ahahaha, do you know what it means to be beyond your social standing? You really are a troublesome fellow.'

Manko: 'Family lineage, wealth, status, fame—what do such things matter? I cannot bear to have sacred love restrained by such things! She shall surely be mine. Even for you, it might be a waste to give her to Manko, but with a divine heart that loves subordinates, you will act as a mediator for me, won't you? No, I firmly believe that out of a compassionate heart that loves disciples, you will surely make the arrangements for me. Even for the Kshatriya-sama, since she was once considered no longer among the living, in short, she is a windfall. That being so, as a reward for our having gone to fetch her, depending on your mediation, he will surely say he grants her to Manko. Please make no mistake, Sensei, I'm counting on you...'

Daiya: 'Hohakaha, that Mankō-sama is indeed pleased to speak with such great kindness. However, I already possess a husband. Though I am but jifuitsusai in years, I hold the kokoroe of a full-fledged woman.'

Mankō: 'Well now, who might this husband of anata be? Surely you would not commit such a baka thing as between kiyōdai...'

Daiya: 'Yes, I intend to ask Senseisama to negotiate with my father, that he might request my ketsukon with Haruna-sama, the musuko of Samorinokami, after nisannen. For Haruna-sama is the one I like ichiban.'

Mankō shook his head repeatedly,

Mankō: 'Despite your setsukaku efforts, Haruna-sama is dame. Already he has acquired a ritsupa dekidai wife named Karunahime. Furthermore, as your age differs from his, it were best that you give up such thoughts.'

Daiya: 'Oh dear me! That Haruna-sama should have taken a wife within a single ichinen! Whatever shall I do?'

Mankō: 'Hahaha, that is precisely why I say it is dame to love a man of such a different age.'

Daiya: 'Though you say Haruna-sama and my age differ, it is merely ten jifunen or so. Do you not differ by as much as sanjifunen? If I were to become fūfu with such a person, the people of the seken would laugh, calling us Hanchiyōuemon and O-Han. Hohakaha, oh dear, what an thoroughly unpleasant face!'

With that, she pertly turned her senaka.

Mankō: 'Ah, I have failed at this. How might I become younger in age? Why was I not born twenty years later?'

Matsuhiko: 'Ahahaha!'

Tatsuhiko: 'Hey, Mankō, speak no such baka things, but prepare the guests' meal at once. From now on, I command you to act as boy. Go to the kitchen quickly, gird up your sleeves, and get to work!'

Mankō: 'Yes, I have understood. However, with so very many guests, Mankō alone cannot manage it all. Since a woman is indispensable for cooking, shall we have Daiya-sama lend a hand? If that be disagreeable, then let Tatsuhiko-sama also draw water or the like.'

Harukuni: 'Nay, Matsuhiko and Tatsuhiko have important duties. Henceforth they must make various preparations, proceed to Hōfusu, and go to consult with Ksatriya-sama on various matters. Regrettable though it is, Mankō, pray manage by yourself for today alone. As Daiya-sama is a woman, if she were to assist for a short while it would suit us well, and you too would be pleased; yet as it is perilous with Mankō, we cannot do so, which leaves us in quite a plight.'

Mankō: 'Sensei, no such anxiety is needed; I too am a man. I shall rarely commit any clumsy blunder, so I pray you, even if only for half an hour, let us work together. After pushing through thorns up and down steep mountain slopes, and just when I thought I had returned here at last, I am made to act as a bath-house attendant, and now even commanded to cook... Thus, it seems you might show a little consideration for me.'

Daiya: 'While living an inconvenient life in the mountains, I have gained experience in cooking for six persons. Then I alone shall take charge of the kitchen. Mankō-sama, pray take your rest.'

Mankō: 'Heaven forbid! If we let a young hime of tender years act as cooker master, it would ill become a man. Nor would it be excusable should it reach the ears of Ksatriya-sama; therefore, let us serve as cooker master through fūfu... oops, I mean a man and woman working together. Well, Sensei, there can be no hindrance in that, can there?'

Harukuni: 'If even Daiya-sama consenteth, it shall be well. However, thou shalt be the rice cooker, and we shall have Daiya-sama become the butler.'

Mankō: 'Ah, it cannot be helped. I shall obey the command of my lord.'

And so Mankō gathered various Shiyokureuhin and, tucking up his sleeves with a cord for Suiji, set to work. Daiya kindled the fire and boiled the tea; once the Jiyunbi was perfectly complete, he carried in the Zenbu, and after performing Dokumi for each and every dish, he arranged them in the Zashiki. From this point, the entire group partook of Asahan, leisurely exchanging stories of Sanchiuseikuwatsu, the Kuraubanashi of the Sendenshi, and other Yukuwai tales; they then began Sensōdan regarding the interior of Victoria Castle, passing about Itsutoki in an Omoshirōka manner.

In accordance with the Naimei of Harukuniwake, both Matsuhiko and Tatsuhiko adjusted their Emon and made their Sannifu to the Hofus. Talman, the Naijitsukasa, received the Riyōnin with Teinei, showed them into the inner room, and while entertaining them with tea and sweets, began to inquire after the news of the six children as if he could no longer wait.

Talman: 'I have been waiting for you most anxiously; what was the news regarding the children?'

Tatsuhiko thought it would not be very interesting to say immediately that he had brought the six back safely; intending to tease the Naijitsukasa for a moment, the mischief-loving Tatsuhiko intentionally assumed a worried expression. While wiping the saliva from his lips with a napkin, he let out seven or eight successive coughs, and acting as if it were difficult to speak, he fidgeted and rubbed his hands together.

Tatsuhiko: 'Eh, though we went to great lengths, it was by no means an easy matter. It was, indeed, an unexpectedly remote place of deep mountains and dark valleys, overgrown with brambles, a difficult spot where even birds do not pass.'

Talman: 'Heh, indeed, you must have been greatly troubled. And did the children return safely?'

Tatsuhiko: 'Well, that is, yes, what shall I say. Truly, I have already broken my bones with effort.

The companions of moon and flowers gradually vanish like snow,

The light shines after the three-day moon,

The quack doctor shows a change rather than a cure.'

Talman: 'Eh, what are you saying? Were the children not there?'

Tatsuhiko: 'Heh, as for being there, they were there. But you see, they would not easily say "Yes," and with charging spears they surrounded the three of us in ten and twenty folds; they attacked with such ferocity that there was not even a gap for an ant to crawl out. We barely managed to cut a path through and fled back like clouds and mist... such was the state of affairs. Ah, ah, it could not be helped.'

Talman: 'The other side was six people, yet you say ten or twenty folds, or no gap for an ant to crawl out... please do not tell such jokes, but let me hear the good news quickly.'

Matsuhiko was smiling, silently listening to the dialogue between the two. And from time to time, he let out a "pu-pu" burst of laughter. Talman grew impatient and, sliding his knees closer, said:

Talman: 'Eh, do not tease me, tell me quickly. Surely you have gained the victory.'

Tatsuhiko: 'As a result of the struggle by the three of us, it was simply impossible to bring them back alive, so we chopped off their heads and have finally made our triumphal return. Presently we shall submit them for the inspection of the heads. Since hair grows all over their faces like bears, I pray you inspect them carefully so there is no mistake. Right now at Harukuniwake-sama's residence, as we cannot treat even just the heads neglectfully, we are making a palanquin to carry them here, so please inspect them well.'

Talman: 'Who requested such a thing? This is outrageous violence! What use is it to bring back only the heads? You are a great criminal of murder; from now on, no matter how high a Monkū of divine virtue you may be called, I cannot forgive you. Prepare yourself!'

So he spoke, his complexion changing as he flew into a rage. Tatsuhiko remained composed.

Tatsuhiko: 'Ahahaha! Since it was impossible to return lugging such large bodies, I thought it would be very convenient to bring back only the heads. In accordance with the will of the Kshatriya-sama, we have destroyed the rebellious children who sought to disturb the nation of Biku and have returned; what is it that dissatisfies you? You said that you only wanted the six children to show their faces, did you not? You did not specifically say to show the torsos, nor did you say to show the limbs. We have no ears to listen to such complaints, ahahahaha!'

竜彦『吾々三人がヤツと格闘の結果、到底生捕にして帰る訳にも行かず、首をチヨン切つて、漸く凱旋致しました。やがて首実検に供へませう。毛は熊の如くに顔一面に生えて居りますから、御見違ひのない様にお検めを願ひます。今治国別さまの館で仮令首丈でも疎には出来ませぬから、輿を作つて舁いで参りますからよくお検め下さいませ』

タルマン『左様な事を誰が御願申しましたか、以ての外の乱暴、首計り持つて帰つて何になりますか。貴方は人殺の大罪人、これから、何程神徳の高きモンクだと云つても、許す訳には参りませぬ、覚悟をなされ』

と顔色を変へて怒り出した。竜彦は平然として、

竜彦『アハハハ、大きな体を引抱へて帰る訳にも行かず、首丈持つて帰れば大変軽便だと思ひ、刹帝利様の意志に従つて、ビクの国家を乱さむとする、悪逆無道の御子女を亡ぼし帰つたのが、何がお不足で厶るか。六人の子供が顔さへ見せてくれさへすれやよいと仰有つたでせう。別に胴体を見せいとも、手足を見せいとも仰有らなかつたでせう。さやうな不足は、吾々は聞く耳は持ちませぬ、アハハハハ』

§ 4

Taruman was utterly dumbfounded by the excessiveness of the situation; his face turned Matsusao, and while his lips trembled, he glared resentfully at the face of Tatsuhiko. At this moment, Samori and Umori, who had been at Rēbu-an-Rūmu waiting for Harukuniwake's Henji, thought they heard the voice of Tatsuhiko; they pushed open the door, followed the Rauka, and appeared at this spot. Seeing Taruman, his Kahoiro was like earth and he was shivering. The Futari were smiling cheerfully. Samori's Kyūbitto, seeing this state, thought to himself, 'Haha, that fellow Taruman, because he struts about acting like a Yogenshiya and a Sendenshi, he is being taken to task as a Miseshime; this is Omoshiro...' and thus thinking, he appeared before the Sannin.

Samori: 'Hearing of the Yōsu just now in the Kitsuenshitsu, it seems that things have ended in Fuseikō, but no, anything is Ketsukō. As long as you bring Āru-sama back with you, the Jiyōchiu of Biku will be as firm as a Banjiyaku. Ya, you have truly exerted yourself; Dōzo, please convey my regards and thanks to Harukuniwake-sama.'

Matsuhiko: 'At long last, after explaining various Jijiyō, I have arranged to bring back Hitori only. Presently Harukuniwake-sama shall place him in a Kago and send him; Dōzo, please perform the Ōketo.'

Samori: 'Thank you. Surely Satteri-sama will be satisfied. And who might that Hitori be?'

Before Matsuhiko could give a Henji,

Tatsuhiko: 'Aiya, because hair grows all over the face, I cannot tell if it is a man or a woman, an elder brother or a younger brother. I do not know what it is, but eh, I have finally managed to pull Hitori back. Dōzo, please examine him well.'

As they were speaking, led by Mankō and carried in a Kago, the one who arrived was the eldest son, Āru. Matsuhiko said,

Matsuhiko: 'Ya, he has returned just now. Come, let us all welcome him.'

And they went to the entrance to meet him. The Kago was brought alongside the entrance. Emerging abruptly from within, a noble youth with his beard shaven off and wearing splendid clothes was the eldest son, Āru. Taruman, as well as Samori and Umori, were so astonished they could only utter a cry, and for a while, no words would come.

Harukuni: 'Everyone, this is the eldest son, Āru-sama.'

Samori and Umori were suddenly overcome by tears of joy and could not utter a single word; taking his left and right hands, they guided him to Satteri's living room. Taruman also finally stroked his chest in relief,

Taruman: 'Tatsuhiko-sama, remember this. I shall surely return the favor...'

While saying this, he followed behind Āru and disappeared into the inner room. Harukuniwake, accompanied by Mankō, quickly departed without meeting Satteri, as he was concerned about the five people he had left at his own dwelling.
