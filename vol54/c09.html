Chapter 9: Flower 祝 [1395]

花祝〔1395〕

§ 1

As the bridegroom, Prince Āru, opened his golden fan and stood up, he began to chant and dance by himself.

Āru (Chanting): 'In Takaamahara the Yahoyorozu gather, Kamuizanaginomikoto and Kamuizanaminomikoto, descending to Aokigahara of Tachibana in Himuka of Tsukushi, beheld and established the heavenly pillar and the earthly pillar, spinning around and meeting to the left and right, revolving and revolving, ah, what a lovely maiden! Thus decreed the ancient matters, now coming round and manifesting before our very eyes, today's joy is the Senshiubanzairaku. Born in the house of Shiyuda, taking gentle Hanna as my bride, we exchange the nuptial cups as wife and husband, uniting the sacred breath of heaven and earth; needless to speak of the realm of Biku, the royal house of Victoria that has appeared and manifested as the ruler, to guard it for a thousand generations and ten thousand generations, the decree of my spouse bestowed to protect it, how auspicious it is here where the green of the paired pines is deep, an eternal and unwithering marvelous realm, the common folk also peacefully, looking up to the august reign of the sage, enjoying day by day their occupations with gladness, mountains and rivers pure and clear, fields yielding grain and fruit, human hearts peaceful, serene and ever serene, the evening sky of tonight, the dew of grace forever, bestowing and letting fall, Miroku Ōkami, the face of the moon, of the full moon night, changing not on and on, the heavenly riverbed forever and ever, without ever drying up, with the time coming, letting sweet dew fall upon the earth, needless to speak of the five grains and tree fruits, bestowing the dew of loving-kindness upon all things, such deep and precious divine grace, how joyful am I who receive it! How joyful am I who receive it! Though the sun shines or clouds, though the moon waxes or wanes, even though the great earth should sink, pledged with sincerity, the way of wife and husband shall forever remain unchanged, shall forever remain immovable, ah, Kamuni, Kamuni, rejoicing and celebrating today's longevity, Senshiubanzairaku.'

Having finished his chant, he took his seat. The sound of applause, like a sudden shower of hail, echoed through the wide hall. Hanna-hime opened her chūkei fan, and with modest, graceful sleeves, she began to chant and dance by herself.

Hanna: 'Ah, how grateful, how grateful! Born in the house of Sāfu, I am a lowly maiden, Hanna-hime. Guided by the wondrous drawing-together of the precious gods, shining brightly in the cloudy sky, the royal son of the ruler of the realm of Biku, discovered by Prince Āru, under the shade of the pine forest, carrying a basket and a rake, a pair of us together amidst withered pine needles, the many things we had gathered, while we were busy packing them into the basket, parting the eight-fold clouds of heaven, the single marvelous royal son who descended, from the moment I saw him, wondrous though it was, he took the hand of a lowly maiden, treating her with utmost tenderness and care, and to the famed Victoria Castle, the awe of being led back! My heart trembling within me,

wondering what on earth would become of me, worrying and troubled as I was, yet guided by the tying gods, the salamander turned into a dragon, and seated in the ninety-fifth august position, you became the husband of my lordly spouse, and today at last the wedding ceremony has been performed. Ah, how grateful, how grateful! All those called women, without lineage and mounting a jeweled palanquin, as I once heard from an old man in the village, was not a falsehood, and now it has descended upon my own body, bearing the body of a delicate woman, raised to a heavy and exalted position, wondering whether my divine blessings might perchance be exhausted, though my heart is far from calm, yet captivated by the profound affection in my lord's heart, unable even to refuse, and feeling myself to be a woman who does not even understand her own station,

without minding the mockery of the people of the world, I humbly obeyed my lord's august will. Ah, my lord, my lord! Pitiful though I am, imperfect as I am, please forever have compassion on me and let me serve by your side. O Minister of the Left, O Minister of the Right, O Prince Taruman, Minister of Domestic Affairs, look with pity upon my foolish self, and forever watch over my shortcomings. Needless to speak of household matters, but the essentials of the nation's rituals, teach them to me, I pray, Kamuni! Before the august presence of the gods, we humbly pray, especially to serve the precious thirty-five-fold path of teachings, the propagation emissaries of Kuniwake, Matsuhiko, Tatsuhiko, and Mankō...'
