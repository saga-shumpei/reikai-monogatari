Chapter 18: Jewel 則姫 [1208]

玉則姫〔1208〕

§ 1

Imoriwake was dead drunk, helpless, and lay stretched out alongside the sake flask.

Otora quietly placed a night-garment over him from above, and stepping softly with hushed footsteps, she sat down by a square charcoal brazier in the four-and-a-half-mat room and muttered to herself,

Otora: 'Truly, Imoriwake-sama is also such a troublesome man; the more kindly I treat him, the colder he becomes, somewhere along the way. I thought he was a somewhat warmer person, but I grossly overestimated him. Had I known he was this cold, I would not have become so infatuated from the very beginning. Every single coin of the fortune I took such pains to save has gone entirely into the construction of the shrine, and then the thousand gold pieces that I had pinched and scraped to accumulate were taken by Kumakō; whatever shall I do from here on out? It truly is a worrisome matter. Sake must be offered from morning till night, and sake is naturally not free either; a single sho of sake requires as much as twenty-five sen in taxes. Having to pay indirect national taxes like this is simply unbearable. Even so, as a woman of pride, I cannot cast him aside now, nor can I be cast aside by him; what a difficult predicament I have fallen into.

A great many superstitious people come to visit, but because Bunsuke is a fool, he thinks this deity is satisfied with mere turnips and daikon radishes, and not a single believer offers anything of monetary value; shouting 'It is the Dragon God!' they do little more than come and release black snakes up on Mount Kitayama. Can the maintenance of the shrine—no, can a business be run on things like this? Oh, I am caught in a terrible dilemma.'

While she was murmuring thus to herself, the one who came stomping noisily over the floorboards of the corridor, making heavy footsteps, was Magahiko.

Magahiko: 'Otora-sama, are you still awake?'

Otora: 'How could I possibly sleep, even if I tried? Because you went and said such useless things, didn't the fellow Kumakō end up making off with a thousand gold pieces?'

Magahiko: 'Still, one's life cannot be compared to a thousand gold pieces; since there are still nine thousand gold pieces remaining before the sacred altar, you do not need to be so pessimistic. More than that, Otora-sama, you always said you would make Kamigihime and Giritenjō husband and wife, but because the living shrine of the Eternal Hinode no Ten no Kami has arrived, haven't I completely become like an octopus in a fish-trap? Your divine pronouncements are sometimes wrong, aren't they?'

Otora: 'Well, even if one or two things turn out wrong, there is nothing that can be done about it, so please do not be so vindictive and argue with me when I am already this worried.'

Magahiko: 'Otora-sama, you said it doesn't matter if one or two things were wrong, but the marriage issue between Kamigihime and myself being mistaken even once is a terrible agony for me; nay, it is practically tantamount to ruin.'

Otora: 'Can't it be helped? The deity says, "We shall make it suit the occasion," so no matter how great Giritenjō Hinode no Kami may be, he surely cannot rival the Eternal-sama. Rather than that, why don't you set your sights on a younger, prettier woman? Such a used-up article wouldn't find a buyer even if you displayed it at a second-hand dealer's shop. Instead of buying a coat that someone else has already worn out, how much more refreshing it must be to buy an unwashed garment whose basting threads have not yet even been removed.'

Magahiko: 'Actually, I thought so too and made up my mind. Just yesterday I looked around among the believers, and I would like to make Ominu-san, who always comes to visit, my wife; would there be any chance you could arrange this for me?'

Otora: 'What, you want to make that Ominu your wife? Hmm, that is a good intention; I shall try negotiating it for you one of these days, though I don't know if it will go well.'

お寅『何、あのお民を女房にしたいと云ふのか、ウンそれやよい了見だ、一つ私が懸け合つて見よう、併し旨く行くか知らぬがな』

Maga: 'Please manage to persuade Otermin's mitama there so that she will believe herself to be Tamanorihime, the mitama of the wife of Giritenjiyō-sama; if it is the command of Kamisama, she will surely listen because she is such an enthusiastic shinjiya.'

Otora smiled with a grin,

Otora: 'Hey Maga-yan, do you think your own mitama is Giritenjiyō all the way? Isn't that auspicious? This Otora is a baka, but yesterday, through Isokō-sama's uta, I saw right through it completely; because Imoriwake-sama is trained by Takahime and his seishin is strange, he says whatever comes out of his mouth, such as whose mitama is what and whose mitama is that. However, when I think that this is also shiyōbai, there are things I cannot overlook, but truly, if you think he is Giritenjiyō, you are ōatechiga; your mitama is a tanuki, you see.'

Maga: 'Hey, that is outrageous; if I am a tanuki, then Otora-sama must be a toraneko.'

Otora: 'My shiyugojin is not such a hedoro-like thing. When I listened to Isokō-sama's uta, my eyes opened, and when I secretly examined the shiyugojin in the pit of my stomach, what it said from the pit of my stomach was: "I am a hanko, though they call it banko. I am a monster who cannot be told whether I am a tiger, an ox, or a fox, but after all, I am the boss of the old foxes, and I am swinging my weight around the Kitayama neighborhood." Truly, I have become completely astonished. When I think of this, each and every one of them is just a fox or a tanuki, isn't it?'

Maga: 'If so, what is Imoriwake-sama's shiyugojin?'

Otora: 'Though it cannot be said in a loud voice, he is a very large tanuki. When you hear that, he sometimes points his mouth or rolls his eyes wide, doesn't he? But even if they are tanuki or hanko, they are not too baka. Maga-yan, you too should act with that intention, becoming something like a fox; a fox is better than being called a tanuki. A fox is called Inari-sama, and everyone reveres it, you know.'

Maga: 'Let us put aside the talk of foxes and tanuki for a while; my marriage problem is at hand. Somehow or other, please arrange things for Otermin-sama; if you dally, Imoriwake-sama will snatch her away.'

Otora: 'Hmm, that is right. I was just thinking that Otermin's gaze seemed to be focused entirely on Imoriwake-sama and I could do nothing about it. By any chance, that amattyo might be casting amakyu at Imoriwake-sama. What a hateful fellow! Well, well, shall I peel off the skin of his face before long?'

Maga: 'Instead of doing such a thing, why don't you arrange it for me so that I will cherish her and protect her so that she won't even cast a glance toward Imoriwake-sama? If you do that, Otora-sama, you will be safe first of all.'

Otora: 'That is true. There is no need to make a fuss, so let us negotiate with her so that she will become your wife whether she says yes or no.'

Maga: 'Yay, that is most grateful! As expected of Otora-sama, you have taken it up so well. That is why they call you a toriagewabasama. Ufufufufu.'

Otora: 'What kind of pun is that? Hearing this, if we do not settle it somehow this very moment, Imoriwake-sama will be in danger and I cannot help it. Hasn't Otermin returned today, you say?'

Maga: 'How could she return? That woman's visit to Kamisama is merely on the surface, and in truth, she is infatuated with Imoriwake-sama more than a hundred times over.'

So he incites Otora-baba-sama and schemes to have her mediate his matchmaking.

Otora became hot with anger,

Otora: 'Hey Maga-san, where is that Otermin-sama staying?'

Maga: 'She is sleeping next to the cookhouse, but in truth, I also tried sounding her out this evening and I got beaten. It is truly, truly ridiculous.'

Otora: 'Ahahaha! How witless! You were given an etsuppatsu, weren't you? Is that why you came to ask this Otora-sama for backup? But Maga-yan, I like you a great deal. You must meddle from time to time and be careful, otherwise Imoriwake-sama will be in grave danger and we cannot help it. You are a suitable man for a watchdog.'

You must meddle from time to time and be careful, otherwise Imoriwake-sama will be in grave danger and we cannot help it. You are a suitable man for a watchdog.'

Maga: 'Aren't you being a bit too harsh by calling me a guard dog?'

Otora: 'What's wrong with being a guard dog? I'm going to make you a splendid master before long, so hey Maganyan, you served as a guard dog in the past as Chiyokonan for Imoriwake-sama so that evil wouldn't enter.'

As she was about to step forward and leave, Okiku, who had been prowling around that vicinity, came over.

Otora: 'Aren't you Okiku? Where is a young woman wandering about in the middle of the night?'

Okiku: 'I ran into the Ikimiya of that Cultivating Daijin, and I really gave it to him good, taking all his grease. Truly, that fellow Mankō is being hoodwinked by a badger and is confident that he is the Cultivating Daijin, so it's so funny I can't stand it. Even this Okiku isn't Chijiyōhime or anything like that, so how could a maiden whose nose tip isn't even split endure having such foolish passion?'

Otora: 'You're a tomboy who knows everything inside and out, aren't you? I'm going over there for just a moment, so you wait here with Magahiko-sama, you hear.'

Okiku: 'Mother, it simply won't do for a woman to be walking around alone stealthily at night.'

She shot back immediately with a tit-for-tat retort.

Otora: 'I have business for Shinkai, so you stay here and wait obediently with Maganyan. And if you get sleepy, go on and rest however you like.'

Okiku: 'Mother, how unpleasant. If I have to look at Maganyan's face, I'd rather just go into the bedroom and rest.'

Otora: 'Oh, do as you please!'

Uttering these words, she abruptly turned and walked out, pointing toward the dark room next to the kitchen and proceeding step by step by feeling her way in the dark.
