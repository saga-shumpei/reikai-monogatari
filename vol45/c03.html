大根蕪〔1193〕

§ 1

Invited by Ushitoraba,

Matsuhiko, of Matsudai-sama,

Together with Mankō, Isokō, and the others,

Along with three others, up the steep slope,

Reluctantly climbing onward,

At the foot of the pine tree by the river,

Enveloped by the thousand-drawing rock,

Clutching the secret key,

Determined not to be careless, with hardened liver-heart,

Firming his belly and resolute,

Feigning an indifferent demeanor,

Up the narrow staircase step by step

He ascends before the gate.

Otora-baba-sama halts in her steps,

Hark, hark, I say, O reception officer Bunsuke-dono,

Starting with the living shrine of Matsudai Kami,

Five ragamuffin gods

Are at last making their appearance here,

Hurry quickly to the inner chambers,

And announce them promptly to the kyōso-sama of Imoriwake.

The divine grace is vast and broad,

Masamune-sama and Giritenjiyō,

And the living shrine of Hinodewake too

Will surely be well pleased, won't they?

The Otohime of Riugūkai

Is the fleshly shrine she possessed,

And through the greeting of Ushitoraba-sama

We have managed to eat our way this far,

So if you dawdle, they will go back,

And it will be the same old story again,

Quickly, quickly, she whispers close to his ear,

While Bunsuke-jijii shakes his head

Three or four times vertically,

Like one tracing up the river's flow,

With a plodding gait

He pushes open the fusuma and conceals his white form in the inner room.

After a short while, Magahiko,

With his face brimming with smiles,

And eager at heart, comes out to welcome them,

You are the living shrine of the heavenly Great Deity, the Sun King of Matsudai.

How very good of you to come down to us.

Masamune-sama in the inner room

Has lined up bottles of port wine

Alongside rare delicacies from mountains, fields, rivers, and seas,

And waits with a smiling face.

You need have no reserve whatsoever.

You are the living shrine of Kami.

Since things have come to this, let us mutually,

Without any separation between foe and friend,

Join our bellies and devote the utmost of our strength to the divine work.

This is not the time to create petty divisions

And bicker in a muddled way.

Now that the auspicious time for the fulfillment of divine government

Has finally drawn imminent,

O fleshly shrine of Matsudai-sama,

By all means, come what may, upon this mountain

You must remain.

The wicked deity, Kami Susanoo,

Sunk in the teachings he established,

Blowing trivial fervor,

Far and near across the wide world,

I have heard that there are many fools

Propagating them.

When I learned that you, Honorable Sir,

Had entered the Omotokyō,

I was startled for a moment.

Be that as it may, when I listen closely,

Because of the joy at meeting your elder brother again

At Kajika Pass,

It was merely a passing impulse of the moment

That you entered your faith in Omotokyō,

And since that has become known,

At last this has a pulse.

Such a magnificent fleshly shrine

Must not be allowed to go back empty-handed,

So saying, the fleshly shrine of Masamune-sama,

In great anxiety, caused Otora-sama

To detain you purposely—for this discourtesy,

Please look upon it with renewed eyes, listen to it with renewed ears,

And proclaim it anew, as Magahiko,

Acting as the representative of Imoriwake,

Here offers his greetings.

Come, come, quickly, without reserve,

Pass on through to the inner rooms.

The thread of the fulfillment of divine government

Opens up here at Kobitoyama.

Could there be anything so felicitous as this?

Ah, Iwakoto Kami Kami,

We humbly pray before the presence of Kami.

How could a man possibly stand?

Aku, Teku, Taku, yo, Isokō, yo!

What on earth are you thinking?

If you have any opinions, right here and now,

No hesitation is needed, quite plainly,

Won't you pour it all out to me?

The worms in my belly are grumbling and growling,

They are so angry and furious there is nothing I can do.'

Isokō: 'Whatever Isokōtsukasa thinks,

Without a single moment of reserve or hesitation,

If I am to display it, it is as follows:

You had better clear out your ears and listen well.

The deity of Mount Kogita

Is possessed by the princess of Tokoyo,

And both Takahime and Kurohime,

Enveloped in the clouds of delusion,

Opened up and left behind this Shikomichi.

That most vital and essential Takahime,

And Kurohime have repented, Surrendered to Ananai-kyō,

And are now splendid Kamutsukasa.

What is the use of believing in the teachings

Of Urayai, which people do not even care to look at?

That most vital and essential Kyōso,

Whom Takahime-sama and Kurohime

Have themselves utterly tired of and cast aside—

Surely there cannot possibly be any truth

In such a Urayai teaching, can there?

Even if you hear only this much, you must understand.

Come to think of it, there is no value in researching it.

Now, now, I say, Matsuhiko-sama,

I have already grown sick of this.

Before we sink too deeply into it,

Let us leave this place in a brisk haste,

And ascend upon the sacred path of the demons.

To spend our time dallying

With such worthless wretches,

If we fall behind in the vital and essential Kamuwaza,

What shall we ever do?

It will end up as an inexcusable excuse

Before the deity of the Saien mansion.

Mankō, Aku, Taku, Teku-sama, yo!

What do you fellows think?

I want you to let Isokō hear your opinions first;

I beg of you, do so.'

Aku: 'Using the sacred names of the deities of heaven as a pretext,

How hateful are these crooked ones who go forth to disturb the world.

With Giri-Tenjō, the deity of Hinode, and Magahiko,

With what aim do they utter such scandalous rumors?

They skillfully hook Matsuhiko, calling him the lord of all ages, the sun king,

Saying he is the deity of heaven.

Saying things so well that they sound not unpleasant,

Matsuhiko-sama is beginning to be led astray.'

Matsuhiko: 'For a little while longer, leave things as I make them be;

Good and evil shall be judged by the gods.'

Taku: 'Building strange shrines all in a row,

And uttering bizarre prophecies—how absurd it is.

Taku has nothing coming to mind right now,

He only wants to slip away from this place quickly.'

Teku: 'Making us climb a steep mountain step by step,

We have been bewitched by a tight-fisted fox.

Tightly, from the name of the princess to the guardian deity of the fox,

As if there were any such things as Giri or Tenjō.'

Bunsuke: 'If you have been sitting here listening silently from the very beginning, you fellows seem to greatly doubt the head temple of this Urayai teaching and are grumbling on and on with petty complaints, but if you say such things, divine punishment will surely strike you. Just leave everything to the gods; while you are in a state where you cannot even pick clean the lice clinging to the lining of your own clothes, is there any reason you should be arguing this and that about the vast and boundless divine power? While remaining in the status of ordinary human beings who cannot see beyond a single paper sliding door, why do you mutter and grumble about the teachings established by Great Ohiro Masamune-sama? You would do well to exercise a little self-restraint; oh, what truly pitiful people you are.'

Mankō: 'Drawing taro-radishes, giant radishes, and snakes,

He has been pushed around and made to dance by Bunsuke-sama.

With a face like a taro, pumpkin, or eggplant,

Drawing radish and turnip—how ridiculous it is.

Bunsuke lines up nothing but sophistries,

Glaring with his hips sagging like a wet diaper.'

Bunsuke: 'Now, now, young fellows, whether I draw giant radishes or draw snakes is none of your big business. Leave me alone; how could things be understood by mere luffa dregs like you? Out of a gourd comes a horse; out of a sake bottle comes sake. If you do not quickly reform, radishes from which there is no going or returning will come pressing upon you. You are probably just spreading falsehoods, playing with yam potatoes, and doing nothing but mountain yams. Truly, you are such good-for-nothing little potato-helpers. Muttering nothing but petty logic that isn't even worth a fart's worth of pressure, what is the meaning of all this?'

Mankō: 'Grandfather, I spoke truly rude words.'

Bunsuke: 'Have you understood that it was rude? If you understand, that is fine; since the gods see everything afresh, hear everything afresh, and proclaim everything afresh, keep this well in mind from now on. You make a fool of me thinking my eyes cannot see, but in this world there are those who see yet are blind, and those who are blind yet see, so if you play too many sly tricks, something irremediable will come to pass.'

Mankō: 'Coming to such a demon's den, if our spirits and souls are clouded as well, there will be no undoing it. Ufu-fu-fu-fu.'

Bunsuke: 'Ah, what an impossible fellow. If I keep company with such a muddle-headed man, I won't even be able to draw a single dragon god.
