Chapter 8: Small 蝶 [1198]

小蝶〔1198〕

§ 1

Matsuhiko and Matsuhime, both of them / are our beloved daughters in the prime of youth; / while they stood together at the Utagaki / and spoke to each other of the sincerity of their hearts, / suddenly a burst of laughter arose / like tiles being smashed apart, / sounding out with an unpleasant rattle / and a creepy tone, / mixed with jealousy and mockery, / echoing with the utmost ominousness.

Chiyo, the daughter, opened the doorway / and gazed outside, / when, who could have expected it, / Magahiko, holding both ears with his hands / while bending his waist into the shape of the kana character for ku, / was running away with cautious steps. / Chiyo, looking back at his retreating figure, / strained her gentle voice / and waved her hand like a maple leaf,

laughing out loud with a 'Ho ho ho ho ho.' / Startled by Chiyo's voice, / Magahiko, looking back over his shoulder, / with a face turning crimson and eyes starting out of his head / like popped acorns, / thrusting out his tongue and jerking his chin, / 'Ihi hi hi hi, ihi hi hi,' / puffing with his own arbitrary heat, / 'It would serve you right to have cried your heart out; / from now on, I, Imoriwake, / will go before grandmother O-Tora, / tell the whole story down to the last detail, / and I will never stop obstructing / the love of the two of you no matter what, / so you had better remember this,' / muttering thus, / he glared at Chiyo and hurried away toward the mansion.

Chiyo laughed derisively once again, / 'Ho ho ho ho ho, Magahiko's / love garment of a twisted heart / has now broken in vain; / a broken and tattered sore loser! / To try to make a woman of someone who has a fine husband, / even while professing the teachings of the gods—what is the meaning of this? / Cross-love has its limits too! / Magahiko, possessed by a delusion, / blinded his eyes to love / and stepped off the great highway of good and evil, right and wrong—how pitiful! / Father and mother have been a publicly acknowledged husband-and-wife pair from ancient times; / whom should they hesitate before? / If they want to laugh, let them laugh; if they want to slander, / let them slander as much as they please. / As long as I exist, / even if Lord Imoriwake says whatever he may choose, / I do not care at all. / Spoken from the path of the Urani teachings, / father is Yurarihiko, / the eternal sun-king and god of heaven, / and mother is Kamigihime; / if spoken of from the path of truth, / it is a playful story indeed, / but as they joyfully attach the names of gods / under the guise of the path of the Urani teachings, / what is wrong with it / even if father Matsuhiko passes himself off as the god Yurarihiko, / and mother Kamigihime says, "We are a couple of gods and gods," / now that you mention it? / Are not Imoriwake and O-Tora-sama / well aware of this from long ago? / No matter how grandly Magahiko may come swaggering along / with his twisted ways, / can anything ever stand in the way of the two of them? / Ho ho ho ho ho, how funny! / Eavesdropping from the outside / upon the sweet words of my father and mother exchanged since days of old, / a jealousy has arisen, / wrapped in the flames of envy, / and he bursts out laughing with a harsh "Ka ka ka ka katsu" / at the doorway where appearances are so bad! / Is that what a man wearing a loincloth twelve feet long should do? / There is a limit to shamelessness. / When I thought that such a person / would become the vice-prelate and right-hand man of Imoriwake, / it is not like the bottom of a Buddhist altar cracking, / but Amida himself is spilling out, isn't he? / O-ho ho ho ho, o-ho ho ho! / What would be a good comparison / for Magahiko-sama's style? / A thin rat that has fallen into a drain, / a chicken that has fallen into a privy, / its tail feathers battered and its waist bent, / barking like a dog from afar—the cowardice of running away with a laugh, how pitiful!

Ottodotsukoi, Kannagara! / Even while walking the divine path of the gods, / carried away by the heat of anger, / I have uttered ill words against Magahiko unawares. / As a child, / the foolish rambling words spoken by a young girl / who knows nothing of Misumaru no Ōkami, / the great Naohi who created this world / with a heart as vast as can be— / looking upon them with directness, correcting and listening anew, / I beg of you to forgive the sin and crime of my foul language and violent words! / Pitying the maidenly heart / that displayed an eccentric flight of fancy, / carried away by thoughts of my father and mother's situation, / please forgive me, / as I bow down in reverence and apologize before the august presence of the thirty-five supreme deities.'

Matsuhiko: 'Chiyo went outside and has stayed there; she seems to be chanting something or other. We must not be careless and say something that might displease Magahiko-sama, though.'

Matsuhime: 'Chiyo was raised by a famously chivalrous man after all, and has been brought up as a hardened scoundrel since she was little, so her nerve is big and she talks about remarkably grand things in a precocious manner unbefitting her years. From time to time she pulls off such eccentric stunts that she leaves Imoriwake-sama and Magahiko-sama dumbfounded, monopolizing the name of a mischievous girl. That is why I wanted to reveal myself to them too, but I deliberately kept it hidden.'

Matsuhiko: 'By what means did you come to meet Chiyo?'

Matsuhime: 'That Otora-sama brought her. It seems they were close as fellow kefukaku, and since she is a girl with neither parents nor kiyōdai, she brought her here kindly, saying she wished to raise her properly. When I considered the yōsu, I realized she was indeed my own daughter, and I was so overcome with joy that I could not contain myself; yet, thinking it would not be for the child's good to reveal myself now, I have kept it hidden until this very kefu. Truly, the kyōiku of a kodomo is taisetsu. It is a common custom in the seken for children without parents to become dorobau or daiakunin, so I must be sufficiently careful from now on and provide her with an education. It is troublesome that she says things like an old woman at the age of twelve or thirteen.'

Matsuhiko: 'That is so. For a kodomo, kyōiku is kanjin. Since a kodomo possesses mohausei, they wish to immediately jitsukau what they have mikiki. A kodomo likes to play by making a mane of their parents, and adults in turn like to make a mane of children by lining up white and black stones. This too must be ingō, I suppose. Aha!'

Matsuhime: 'Even I, and you too, are now serving the path of the Kami and are revered by others, acting like a teacher, but at the time that child was conceived, we were truly not much to speak of. Since she is a child conceived with the soul of that time, it is no wonder she was born as she is. It is thanks to the blessing of the Kami that she was not born with a disability, at the very least.'

Matsuhiko: 'However, Chiyo is still standing outside, chanting about Magahiko and the like, is she not? It is a troublesome matter. Well then, I shall go and call Chiyo.'

Saying this, Matsuhiko stood up, opened the door at the entrance, and peered outside. Chiyo was making 'iin-iin' sounds, rolling her eyes, clenching her fists, and acting as if she were punching someone's head, striking at the empty air.

Matsuhiko: 'Hey there, Chiyo, what are you doing?'

Chiyo: 'Yes, this is the kagura that I, the princess, am performing outside the rock door to celebrate the reunion of the Lord God of the End-Age Sun and Yoshikohime-sama. No matter that I am a daughter, I am being considerate, knowing that I must not disturb the long-awaited reunion of the husband and wife. Please, go ahead and cry and laugh to your heart's content with Mother-sama, and act out your play with all your might. I must not disturb the enjoyment of Father-sama and Mother-sama, you see.'

Matsuhiko: 'What an astonishing tomboy you are. Here, Chiyo-san, such reserve is unnecessary; come inside at once.'

Chiyo: 'Please let me play here a little longer.'

Matsuhiko: 'Playing is fine, but you must not speak ill of Magahiko or say such things.'

Chiyo: 'But Father-sama, Magahiko-sama is a man beyond help. If I do not make him feel a little shame, it will not be for his own good later. Even though he is a man, he comes to Mother-sama's room at every opportunity and tries to curry favor; it is disgusting. I am so angry I cannot bear it. I have endured until this kefu, but now that I know Father-sama and Mother-sama, I am safe. Even if Magahiko and his ilk come in a bunch, I am fine. Is it not said that the light of one's parents shines for seven ri? For a long time, I was despised for being without parents, and I have endured the regret and bitterness until now. Among them, Maga was the one who despised me the most. That is why my long-held resentment has burst forth, and abuse pours from my mouth. Please, let me say just a little. Even saying this much is only the beginning.'

Matsuhiko: 'If you look at it from your own heart, it may be unreasonable, but to endure that is the way of Kamisama. You should not speak out so bluntly what you wish to say and be hated by people. A Kodomo should just behave like a Kodomo.'

Chiyo: 'Even if I am hated by Magahiko, does it matter at all? As long as my father and my mother are willing to cherish me, that is all that is fine, right?'

Matsuhiko: 'At any rate, mother is waiting, so come inside.'

Ochiyo came following behind Matsuhiko with a smile.

Matsuhiko: 'Ochiyo has become quite a slick and hardened girl. This is a troublesome matter.'

Matsuhime: 'Truly it is troublesome. It is because I cannot say with a loud voice that this is my daughter. Truly I am quite troubled. When I think that if a child like this grows up, she might even become the boss of Bakuchi gamblers, the future is frightening, indeed.'

Chiyo: 'Mother, I intend to become a Kefukaku knight-errant. To help the weak, crush the strong, order huge Araotoko men around with my chin, play the Jiyōu queen, and have people praise me as Aneki, Aneki, echoing my name far and near—that is my first desire in Jinsei. Look at Grandma Otora. Thanks to being a Kefukaku, are you not becoming a favorite of Imoriwake-sama?'

Matsuhime: 'Now look here, Ochiyo, is it that you want to become like Grandma Otora?'

Chiyo: 'I dislike a half-way Onnakefukaku like Grandma Otora. I am thinking of becoming the grand boss of the country of Fusa and the country of the Moon.'

Matsuhiko: 'How troublesome, to have brought forth such a grand thing. After all, seeds are things that cannot be disputed, are they?'

Chiyo: 'Ha, ha, ha, ha, ha! The seed of a Nasu eggplant is an eggplant, and if you sow melon seeds, melon sprouts will come forth. I was born into this world from father and mother's mischievous spirits, and on top of that, brought up in the hands of a Kefukaku; therefore, it is only natural for me to have such a heart.'

Matsuhiko: 'Is it better for you to become a missionary of Kamisama, or is it better to become a Kefukaku?'

Chiyo: 'Would not becoming a missionary of Kamisama be uninteresting? Incomprehensible old crones, old men behind the times, greedy humans, the blind, the mute, the deformed, and the sickly—do even single satisfying ones come close to the place of Kamisama? If by chance you think healthy men and women have come, they are only humans with mental defects; what is so amusing even if one is revered by such people? If one were revered on the basis of understanding, it would be delightful, but what glory is there in being made a fuss over by the uncomprehending? It makes me truly want to vanish as if it were stupid. Rather than that, try becoming a Kefukaku. Araotoko men with a hundredkan of bare flesh, with no defects in spirit and body, men among men gather together, take courage in righteousness, establish sincerity, punish bad people, and are treated by everybody as Aneki and Aneki—just like Kamisama, without greed, using no money carried over from the night before, and being pure and neat—is there anything as delightful as passing this world like that? My desire is to become an Onnakefukaku no matter where.'

Matsuhiko: 'Ha, ha, ha, ha, ha! How troublesome. The parent is a missionary, the child is an Onnakefukaku; somehow our grains do not seem to match.'

Matsuhiko: 'Father, there is probably no rule saying that the child of a carpenter must practice carpentry, and the child of a doctor must practice medicine no matter what. In each human, there is a corresponding genius, and there are suitabilities and unsuitabilities for all enterprises. The spirit of education is to make one's own genius manifest to the twelfth degree. With current education which applies oppressive education to injure the instincts of children, and only creates humans who are like acorns compared in height measured by a box, great figures cannot be made, you know. Even plants, if you apply various interference educations such as bending branches, cutting them, or binding them with wire, will they not become mere ornaments for the alcove? Trees that have grown freely in the valleys of mountains will grow up to become fine pillars. That being the case, humans must somehow be educated so that they can make their genius manifest completely.'

Matsuhiko: 'Matsuhime, just as you said, what a cheeky daughter she is. It looks like she has received quite a bit of social education.'

Matsuhime: 'She is a daughter who is completely beyond my hands.'

Matsuhiko: 'That is true. No, perhaps it is better not to interfere after all. It is a one-in-six chance. Whether she becomes a terrible good person or a bad person, we will not know unless we keep watching. In the end, with the power of parents, it is no good. Leaving it to Kamisama is the best.'

Chiyo: 'That is so-called Kannagara education. You yourself always say Kannagara, Kannagara, after all.'

Matsuhiko: 'Aha, ha, ha, ha, ha!'

Matsuhime: 'Oho, ho, ho, ho, ho!'

Chiyo: 'If we leave it to the Kannagara god himself, The green of the pine will flourish through Chiyo; The morning dew under the twin-mated pines receives the sun, And the good-tasting mushrooms have sprouted forth.'

千代『神様の宣伝使なんて気が利かぬじやありませぬか。訳の分らぬ婆嬶や時代遅れの老爺さまや、剛欲の人間や、盲や唖に、不具に病身者、一人だつて満足のものが神様の処へ寄つて来ますか。たまたま体の丈夫な男女が来たと思へば精神上に欠陥のある人間ばつかり、そんな人に崇められたとて何が面白う厶りませう。理解の上に崇められたのなら愉快ですが、無理解者から持て囃されたつて何が光栄ですか。本当に馬鹿らしく消え度くなつて了ひますわ。それよりも侠客になつて御覧なさい。裸百貫の荒男、霊肉ともに欠陥のない、男の中の男が集まつて来て義に勇み、誠を立て、悪人を懲し、まるで神様の様な欲のない、宵越しの銭を使はぬ綺麗薩張りした人間ばかりに姐貴々々とたてられて、此世を送るほど愉快な事がありますか。あたいは何処迄も女侠客になるのが望みです』

松彦『ハヽヽヽヽ困つたな。親は宣伝使、子は女侠客、どうも反が合はぬ様だ』

千代『お父さま、大工の子は大工を営み、医者の子は何処迄も医者をやらねばならぬと云ふ規則はありますまい。各自に人間には、それ相応の天才があつて凡ての事業に適不適があるものです。自分の天才を十二分に発揮するのが教育の精神でせう。圧迫教育を施して児童の本能を傷つけ、桝できり揃へた様な団栗の背競べの様な人間ばかり作り上げる様な現在の教育では大人物は出来ませぬぜ。植物だつて、枝を曲げたり、切つたり、針金で括つたり、いろいろと干渉教育を施すと、床の間の置物よりなりますまい。山の谷で自由自在に成育した樹木は成人して立派な柱になりませう。さうだから人間は如何しても天才を完全に発揮させる様に教育させなくては駄目ですわ』

松彦『松姫、お前の云つた通り、何とまアこましやくれた娘だな。随分社会教育を受けたと見えるな』

松姫『到底私の手には合はない娘ですよ』

松彦『さうだな。いや却て干渉せない方がよいかも知れない。一六ものだ。大変な善人になるか、悪人になるか、先を見て居らねば分るまい。到底親の力では駄目だ。神様にお任せするが一等だ』

千代『それが所謂惟神教育ですよ。貴方だつて、いつも惟神々々と仰有るのですもの』

松彦『アハヽヽヽヽ』

松姫『オホヽヽヽヽ』

千代『惟神神に任せば自ら 松の緑は千代に栄ゑむ 相生の松の下露日を受けて 生え出でにけり味良き茸は』
