Chapter 9: 賞詞 [1199]

賞詞〔1199〕

§ 1

A snake swallows a man though it is but an inch long, and the sendan tree is fragrant even from its tender shoots—how true this is!

An otomego of twelve springs, born and raised among the kefukaku, like a kosuzume of Shūgakuin chirping endlessly through the chiyochiyo of the Mōgiu, her learned shiranami kotoba, though suppressed and unspoken now, is nonetheless unfailingly clever; even the raujin seasoned in the winds of this world can only bite their tongues at her flawless kotoba, promising yet terrifying, impossible to fathom as she appears.

Intent on visiting the eight-hiro hall, O-chiyo obtains her parents' permission, and with a cheerful smile and an eager heart, trips down the kizahashi.

Watching her go from behind, Matsuhiko turns to his wife Matsuhime and says, 'What manner of person will our fearsome daughter ever become?

Though her physical body was begotten by us, the mitama alone is not born of human strength; all are formed into human beings by solidifying the miiki of the gods of Ametsuchi, and once born into this world, they should grow according to the will of the gods, advancing wherever their heart's mitama directs, and nothing surpasses leaving them to themselves. Even if we fret, wishing to train her in worldly crafts unsuited to her nature and make her a splendid pillar, the mitama is not something human power can sway. Ah, Kamunagara kamunagara! May the mitama sachihahe, and while warmly guarding O-chiyo's body and mitama, manifest grand achievements for the sake of the gods and the world, becoming the warmth of this world, its light, its salt, and its flowers to save the world! May you abundantly bear the ripening fruits of the divine teachings, Kamunagara! Before the august presence of the great ancestral deity who protects the Izanagi-Izanami teachings, we two husband and wife reverently, with awe and trembling, pray. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even should the Great Earth sink, in all the three-thousand worlds there is no treasure superior to a child. Much less is it unnatural for parents to fret over the future of their beloved only child. Ah, Matsuhiko and Matsuhime! You and I, holding fast in awe to the august words of the gods, without worrying or troubling our hearts over our child's affairs, entrust everything before the august presence of the gods, uniting the breath of husband and wife, striving with all our heart and the utmost of our bodies to save and protect the people of the world, upholding sincerity alone, praised as the flower of this world, illuminating the august name of the gods in the world, and letting that name shine through all eternity. When I think of it, how truly blessed it is—this meeting as parents and spouses! When we heard that Magakami dwelt upon the mountains of Kohoku and came to see, today's outcome is beyond all expectation, and we now fully realize and understand the state of a world where good and evil are non-dual. To the eyes of the gods, even Tōmorokowake and Magahiko, imprisoned by the ugly gods, are likewise the august children of us all; how could there be distinctions of love and hatred? For human beings to despise, judge, and hurl abuses at their fellow countrymen is an ugly deed that troubles the precious august heart of the gods of the heavenly realm. Come, from now on, we shall explain and clarify the principles of Ametsuchi to Tōmorokowake-jinbashira, Magahiko-sama, O-tora-sama, and all the hundred officials, exhausting our kotoba and our very bodies, most gently encouraging the righteous path, rescuing the ugly mitama into the divine grace, and fulfilling our mission to help them! Receiving the divine august might, with a calm heart, fully and completely tasting the divine power of the living Kototama among the officials, my heart shall serve the divine teachings of infinite benevolence and compassion. Grant this, O awe-inspiring gods of Ametsuchi, before whose august presence we reverently and respectfully pray. Ah, Kamunagara kamunagara, may the mitama sachihahe!'

Thus he prays with all his heart—such is Matsuhiko. As O-chiyo descends the kizahashi, she begins to sing once more.

'Everlasting and firm as Tokiwa and Kiwani, Matsuhiko of the flourishing pines, and Chiyo no Matsu born amidst the greenery of Matsuhime—brought forth by the banks of the River Lion, how charming is her first cry upon birth! Born by the riverside, bearing the name of the lion reputed to be the king among beasts, surely there is a deep divine plan in her raising her birth cry.

Be that as it may, leading the way in the world, illuminating the light of the heart, universally saving the people of the world—though man has a hundred crafts, no divine craft is more exalted than rescuing pitiful humans, birds, and beasts who have fallen into the sufferings of the floating world.'

§ 2

Now before my chichihaha, Wishing to become a shiranamionna, When I ask and probe Into my father's mikokoro, oh how arigata. 'According to the promptings of thy heart, Cross this world,' so gladly Didst thou declare in words, While the rain of emotion fell incessantly, And my sleeves in tears of joy Became as though wrung out. Born thus amidst the union Of open-hearted chichihaha, truly I am Within the sanzensekai of this world An exceedingly kaufukushiya. The grace of kami and the benevolence of my father, How could I ever forget them, through chiyo and yachiyo, Even to the end of the age of Miroku, Tricolored higher than the mountains and deeper than the sea, Rejoicing in such blessings, Serving kami and parents well, To fulfill the mimune Of the great kami of Mizu no Mitama, Grant my praying heart even in matsubusa, O Kamunagara. I pray before the presence of kami, Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, May your mitama sachihahe. Whether the asahi shines or clouds over, Whether the moon waxes or wanes, Whether the stars fall from the sky, Never clouding my heart with crookedness, My soul forever Is the moon dwelling in the pine tree's tips, A crane building its nest in the age of chiyo, Such a kedaka figure before the presence of kami, And before the presence of my father and mother, Shall be illumined, O Kamunagara. May your mitama sachihahe.'

Thus she sang as she went down. Matsuhiko and Matsuhime, hearing this cool song from within the window, for the first time understood their daughter's honshin, and the fūfu, looking into each other's faces, were lost in tears of joy.

Matsuhiko: 'Did I think her a kimama daughter? Ah, she has realized makotogokoro.'

Matsuhime: ' moistened by the dew of the sumekami's grace, Even the green of the pines will flourish on.'

Matsuhiko: 'If she is a child born of the confluent pines, She will surely flourish for chiyo and yachiyo.'

Matsuhime: 'The evergreen tree flourishing on the ridge of Kohokuyama, Catches the asahi and grows ever more brilliant in hue.'

Matsuhime: 'On the branches of the pine, a thousand-year true crane builds its nest, How felicitous to celebrate the spring of yachiyo.'

Matsuhime: 'Bathed in the dew of the grace of heaven and earth, It bends not to the wind, nor drowns in the water.'

Matsuhiko: 'Wet by the rain and blown by the wind, the evergreen's Hues grow ever more refined on the branches of the chiyo pine.'

Matsuhime: 'To my younger sister and elder brother, my dear husband, meeting as confluent pines, Days and months pile up within Matsuhime's breast. Without even announcing herself as my child, she left herself to the storm Upon the peak of Kohokuyama. Yet, somehow, to this pitiful child's Breast does it reflect, she who longed for me?'

Matsuhiko: 'Meeting again with her weeping chichihaha, Her heart was eager, smiling and flourishing. The age of the pine where she flourishes and goes is chiyo and yachiyo, Fluttering grasses in the wind of grace. In the office of Magahiko, always pitied as a child without parents, How pitiful is such a child. Inside her breast, enduring the years and months, She broke through and rode upon the kototama of Maga. Calling herself Chiyoko, morning and evening, She cast her heart upon her father and mother. The depths of the heart of the child Who wandered seeking the whereabouts of her chichihaha were truly pitiful.'

Matsuhime: 'How many times did she think of revealing who she was, Yet weighed what came before and after. Every time I saw the deeds performed By a wise daughter unlike her years, my heart wavered.'

Matsuhiko: 'Though she is an innocent maiden, her thoughts of kami And her thoughts of her parents are truly admirable. To endure and bear the ugly revling of Magahiko, And to have come thus, was truly valiant. Though people say that praising one's own child is the height of foolishness, I cannot help but want to praise her.'

Matsuhime: 'Thinking of her as a single grain of seed, all the more So nostalgic and lovely is she. If they hear this song of both parents, Her heart of chiyo will smile and flourish. To become a parent and to be born as a child is also known To have mysterious ties from a previous world. Entrusted with the child of the kami of heaven and earth, How joyous it is to raise them. Though she is a child born of my own body, A rare and sacred shrine wherein the spirits of heaven and earth reside.'

Matsuhime: 'The pine breeze of the mountain of Kohoku Blows up ever so gently, Not to mention the land of Fusa, Without a corner left untouched, Sending forth the rain of grace. Nevertheless, as the pillar of kami Who exists as the tutelary kami, It is for the purpose of sending forth The sacred breath of the great sumekami. Though this teaching opened by the office of Takahime is clever, Cutting away strange branches and leaves, Letting fresh buds grow, and inhaling the air Of the eternal spring of yore, If left to the mood of spring, The spring flowers will bloom once more, And it must become a sacred place of wonder. Striking out the kototama of rigor Of the crooked kami lurking within The hearts of Salamanderwake or Magahiko,

Joining the hearts of the fūfu together, Calling upon the name of the princess of the supreme righteousness, Of the Sun King of the last age and the heavenly kami, Expounding and preaching the true teachings, Rescuing this spirit up into the heavenly kingdom Firmly and immovably, Living to become the kami of this world, The righteous work of spirit-first-matter-follower, Propagating it throughout the land of Toyoashihara, Making the august majesty of the Sanghō shine in the four directions, We will surely establish the heavenly kingdom upon this land, O Kamunagara. Before the presence of kami, the pine fūfu, With reverence, pray for chiyo and yachiyo. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, May your mitama sachihahe.'
