Chapter 13: 五三の Moon [1203]

五三の月〔1203〕

§ 1

Otora followed at Okiku's heels, raising a shrivelled voice in a tone that seemed to muddy the surrounding air.

Her voice struck unpleasantly upon the ears of everyone, harsh and jarring like a cracked shield, and intensely stimulated their eardrums.

Morning and evening, solemnly revering and respecting the presence of the Kamisama, offering up various rare things from the mountains and rivers, throwing out even the ten thousand ryō that had been stored away, and building shrines in great numbers in this world—yet the Ōten no Kami of the matsu-daihi, the great deity of the moon, the daishiōgun, the Asahi no Toyosakanobori Hime, Giritenjiyō ya Kitsuku Hime, the ploughing great deity Chijiyōhime, Amawakahiko ya Sadakohime, Ōriyōhime ya Kinriuhime, not to mention Kanayamahime, the Tanemonojinjiya Ōmikami, and even lining up hegure-no-hegure-no-hegure warriors and hegure shrines; while she was placing such faith in them, what did the Kamisama think, as she watched that villain come along, fleece her of a thousand ryō in gold, puff up his shoulders, and march sturdily away, remaining with an indifferent face—you Kamisama, you are not listening!

Though I think in my heart that it is a pity to give away a thousand ryō, through the divine punishment of the precious Kami, right in the middle of this slope, retribution would strike him and freeze him in his tracks so he could not move a step forward or backward, and Kumakō, repenting his past misdeeds from the bottom of his heart, would say that not a single mon, let alone a thousand ryō, was entering his hands, and that he was returning this to the Kamisama, asking for her help—she thought he would come making a crying face, but that expectation missed the mark.

To let off safely a man who had flatly taken a thousand ryō of gold—have the Miroku-jōjū no Kamisama and the Tokoyo no Hime grown blind and deaf of late? I am so astonished that words fail me.

When I think of it, when I think of it, thinking them powerless junk Kami, a sudden anger welled up inside me.

If things were to come to this, I should not have served them from ordinary times with all sorts of miscellaneous cares; I am completely disgusted, you Yurihiko! The Ōten no Kami of the matsu-daihi and Matsuhime, the princess of the upper righteousness, are not to be relied upon at all.

If you think them precious Kami, they are wolves you never expected—the scoundrel Kumakō came to this mountain where wolves dwell, performed actions similar to four-footed beasts, and tormented Otora.

The wild wolf and the bear, ending up in a three-way deadlock, quarreling about this and that as the winter sky began to darken—is it from anger or from the cold, but my body began to tremble with a shiver?

Because it is unbearable, because it is unbearable, it is truly, genuinely intolerable—a Kami with neither power nor virtue!

Now, now, Imoriwake-sama, no matter how much you serve a Kamisama who does not even speak, is it not utterly useless?

How could one remain unconcernedly at ease with a blank face afterwards, when the golden Kamisama who would grant your requests on the spot has been snatched away?

Otora has bones in her arms!

From now on, pursuing Kumakō's tracks with the momentum of a fierce deity, grabbing that fellow firmly by the chest, how could I fail to drag back the golden Kamisama once seized?

It is because I think I will be saved in an emergency that I serve the Kami morning and evening.

No matter how much you try to implore a blind and deaf Kamisama, there is no chance that she will listen.

No matter how grand a Kami she may be, she does not possess even a single sen.

They are nothing but impoverished Kamis.

Eating the sacred wine and cooked rice prepared from our sweat and fat from morning till night, receiving all sorts of hundred-flavored foods and drinks from seas, rivers, mountains, and fields while sitting at ease, yet not saying a single word or making any sound—what is the use of worshiping such a fellow?

From now on, I will resolutely cast aside the junk Kamis, search out the true and fundamental teachings of the Kami, and show you how to receive superhuman divine virtue!

When I think of it, when I think of it, it is foolish and a bizarre thing.

The more I think of it, the angrier I grow.

Thank you for your trouble, everyone.

Whether you worship this Kamisama, abandon her, or throw her away, do as you please.

Since I hear that belief is free, I will never obstruct you, but there is no point in enshrining a Kamisama whom I, the principal and essential person, have grown disgusted with.

It is not worth even a fart's worth of worth.

§ 2

If it were a fart, it would make a sound, but this fellow does not even go 'but' or 'sut.' I have been lost until now, and I have grown weary of my own self, bit by bit. Realizing that I have been a fool, I feel like digging a hole in the earth and hiding away. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara. Are there really gods and Buddhas? The gods are with us; humans are the children of God, the shrines of God. Knowing such a clear Dauri, why have I been losing my senses over the junk god privately manufactured by Takahime-sama? It is regrettable. Come now, from here on it is Yakekuso. Let us break the Dote, drink sake, show the Iji of a Shiranamionna, and make a Don-chan uproar.

Drink and make merry, for the path ahead is Yami. After the darkness, the moon will rise. The light of the moon clearly illuminates my own body. The god enshrined here—rather than illuminating, if I do not light an Akari or attach a Rafusoku, I cannot even see, for it is a blind god. How troublesome. Aita-ta-ta-ta, aita-ta-ta! My mouth slipped too much, and I bit my tongue with my Okuba. Is it a god with Shiyōne after all? If so, let us worship from now on. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara. May the Mitama Sachihahe.

Isokō began to sing little by little.

The god appears on the surface and distinguishes between good and evil. Within the gods, there is good, and there are also Akugami. They label the good on the surface, and as Ikigami who save this world, they make their Shinkau strong. The foxes, badgers, and crooked spirits have built nests in the bodies of believers who cannot Rikai this, and they use the names of noble gods to deceive the people. If I were to speak of the true nature of this god enshrined on the mountain of the small north, without hiding or concealing it, the believers who have offered their lives and souls would be astonished. I know that. Being deceived by such demons and losing one's senses, falling into the land of roots, the land of the bottom, or the path of beasts—seeing this, I feel pity. Without regard for my own busy self, I am wasting precious time and staying here, for the sake of saving and helping the Mitama of you all onto the righteous path of God. Think, think, and reflect. This god's name is a divine possession full of derailments by Takahime. When the Mitama fell into hell, it was possessed by an ugly demon, made the palace of flesh its dwelling, and is of the lineage of the transformed male. It blindly believed itself to be the living palace of the god of Hinodewake, and thus established its teachings.

How can there be sincerity in the god of the fortune-telling sect, which the essential Takahime and Kurohime-tsukasa have abandoned of their own accord? Think of this Dauri, purify all the shrines, sweep them clean, and enshrine the true God of heaven and earth. If you do not, Imoriwake-dono, the body of the Tsukasa will become a nest of crookedness, and your body will be shattered in an instant; your soul will become clouded and fall into the path of hell, the land of the roots. Magahiko-sama and Otora-sama, you too should pull yourselves together. What is the point of giving a name to a nameless god and worshipping it? There is no path other than becoming a plaything for foxes and badgers.

While calling itself a living palace born from the breath of the God of heaven and earth, it has its spirit defiled by crooked gods, performs the recitation of polluted Kototama morning and evening, and disturbs the world—what is the meaning of this? This is the matter that Isokō, swearing to the God of heaven and earth, lays out before your life without fear or hesitation, as a warning. If this god truly has a sincere spirit, then even if it takes my life for the rude things I, Isokō, have just uttered before its eyes, I have no regrets. If it cannot do this, then this god is a Magatsu with neither spirit nor power. If you do not wake up here,

you will incur the wrath of the true God, and not to mention your body, even your soul will be smashed to pieces, and you will suffer infinite pain for all eternity. Reflect, Imoriwake-dono. Before the hundred Tsukasa, I state this, swearing to God. Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara. May the Mitama Sachihahe.
