Chapter 7: 相生の Pine [1197]

相生の松〔1197〕

§ 1

Of the lineage of the Hime of Ural, and meeting at birth Takahime, who mixed together the teachings of Brahmanism, Uralism, and Ananai-kyō hither and thither, self-styling herself of the lineage of Henjiyōnanshi while building her dwelling in Kitayamamura of Fusa's land, relying upon Imoriwake, Magahiko, Takayamahiko, and Kurohime as her sole kokō, establishing the honzan of the Uranai-kyō, propagating the teachings throughout the lands of yomo, touched by the jinji of the Ananahi no Kami, entirely repenting her zenpi for the sake of the Kami and the sake of the world, while striving in shiyashin no kuwatsudō, now completely masquerading as the director of the teachings of Ananahi, and becoming the rare director of the kamuyakata in the forest of Ikuta.

Magahiko, remaining behind, made Imoriwake the kyōso, left Kitayamamura, secluded himself upon Mount Sakateruyama, and here once again newly established the teachings of Uranai, proclaiming them as the sanctuary of the mountains of Kohikita, and spreading the teachings to neighboring countries—how valiant indeed! Imoriwake and Magahiko wielded the eloquence instilled by Takahime with absolute freedom, preaching to the foolish men and women here and there until they collapsed like falling dominos, causing them to shed tears of joy as the true teachings peerless in heaven and earth, and causing a light like that of fireflies to faintly shine even in the valleys of the mountains of Kohikita.

Nevertheless, in the world of eternal darkness where even black and white cannot be distinguished, there were neither wise men to investigate right and wrong regarding the twisted and warped teachings of Imoriwake and Magahiko, nor anyone else, and fools and hags, entangled in greed and wishing to ascend to heaven to spend their post-mortem days in peace, gathered one after another. Otora-sama, the Hakurōnyō whose name was high in the village of Ukigi, by what mechanism or at what time began to frequent the mountains of Kohikita, piling visit upon visit with great frequency, being exceptionally loved by Imoriwake, assuming the airs of a wife, paying attention to everything from this to that and every single matter of personal care with added attention upon attention, exhausting the utmost limits of kindness to finally win the joy of the kyōso.

The hag of Ushitora smiled with delight and stood waiting with the feeling as if she alone were carrying the mountains of Kohikita upon both her shoulders. Imoriwake, his soul extracted by crooked deities, drank nothing but sake day and night without distinction, comforting the anguish of his heart, yet from time to time the crooked ghosts lurking in his heart came leaping forth, yearning for the color and fragrance of Takahime and letting slip words that wounded Otora's heart—how many times could such ugly behavior be counted to the end?

While Otora suppressed her mortification, tightly fastened her pouch of forbearance, and remained stiffened and packed tight, a great flood came attacking, and as if a thousand-ri embankment had burst at once, the muddy waters of jealousy overflowed, and forgetting even human eyes and the before and after, she seized the collar of the kyōso—the dreadfulness of it! To hide such a drunken uproar from the group of directors of Matsuhiko waiting outside, she agonized her heart and tried in various ways to fix up the appearance of this place, but she could not finish hiding the warm sake bottle, the earthen vessel's posture, the broken sake cup, and the shrill voice that could be heard outside—there was no help for it, for since old hag Otora had come to this mountain and begun her service, there had never yet been a great quarrel so angry even once.

By what cue of a gourd, or what unthought-of scandalous state was exposed before the eyes of the distinguished guests—to call it a divine punishment is also rather foolish. Ah, Toyosaka Kami-kami, may your mitama be prosperous and tranquil.

○

Hiding in the annex of the mountains of Kohikita, grasping the actual power of the teachings morning and evening, respectfully wearing the great august heart of Kami Susanoo-no-Ōkami, directing the crooked deities of the Uranai-kyō into ten thousand directions day by day, fundamentally improving them, replacing and rebuilding the mitama of Imoriwake and Magahiko, making them understand the true essence of Ananai-kyō, and wishing to make the divine virtue shine for the sake of the Way and for the sake of the people of the world, Matsuhime was called the Hime of Kamigiyoshi just as Imoriwake said, and though against her heart, the season when the flowers of spring bloom and fragrance fill the air she prayed to Kami for—deep and yearning indeed is the interior of Matsuhime's heart.

The other august name of Yurai no Hiko enshrined in the mountains of Kohikita, the august deity of the King of the Sun of the Last Age, and the hundred other divine names—none of them are correct things. Deceived by the divine spirits of foxes and raccoon dogs, Magahiko believed them to be true kami, grew proud, and set up rows of Hegure shrines—hegure no hegure no heguremushiha—and wandered about in bewilderment—how lamentable indeed! Matsuhime of Ananai-kyō is not a person to be deceived by such things and worship them. Nevertheless, if she were to perform extremely strict saniwa right now, Imoriwake and the hundred other divine directors would come forth all at once like boiling water in a cauldron...

Growing furious and realizing that Matsuhime's personal safety was suddenly in peril, Matsuhime feigned ignorance, and before anyone knew it, she had seized actual control of the teachings of the divination, so that the temple on Mount Kogita came to be a place she could move almost entirely under her own single command.

Now that things had come to this, Matsuhime felt she had no need for any hesitation whatsoever, thinking she would soon display her true nature bit by bit; yet just as she thought this, her husband of days gone by, Matsuhiko-sama, having disguised himself as the deity of the three-and-five, and being guided by old woman Otora to this unimaginable mountain, climbed up and appeared before her.

Peering out from the window of the living room, her heart leapt with a start, and an intense longing suddenly pressed upon her; unable to bear it any longer, she bamboozled old woman Otora by calling it a divine decree, beckoned her over, and said, 'The person who has just come is Yurarihiko, the divine living palace of the Ōten deity of the final days; if you let that deity return, the design for the fulfillment of the Miroku divine administration will surely never be established. Though it is a trouble, make a single dash, pursue him from behind, and by all means lead the final lord back once again to this mansion, treating him most cordially, so that he will be enshrined upon this mountain for ever and ever, and we can establish the purpose of the divination's divine teachings, Otorasama. If you accomplish this mission of mine, I will praise you from now on as the foremost hero of this mountain.'

As she coaxed her thus, old woman Otora suddenly cast aside her energy, grasped a fan painted with the crest of the ten luminaries in one hand, flew out of the Matsuhime mansion, and called out, 'Ohoi, ohoi,' calling back Matsuhiko—such was the extraordinary skill of the old woman; ah, sovereign gods, let your august spirits be blessed.

Allowing both himself and others to call him the right arm of Imoriwake, Magahiko the vice-prelate swallowed the oracles of Imoriwake whole from one to ten, without any distinction between good and evil, right and wrong.

Simply thinking with joy from the bottom of his heart that surely there could be no other true god in the wide world than this, and serving most zealously day and night at the divine presence without sparing his bone and body, completely unaware that the teachings which disturbed the truth were such, completely deluded Magahiko regarded whatever Imoriwake did, regardless of good and evil, right and wrong, as nothing less than the righteous work of the gods—how foolish it was to be thus superstitious about the teachings.

Even this simple-minded and single-hearted Magahiko, who was thus superstitious about the teachings, was not omitted from the selection of young men, and while his heart was disturbed by love, he worried his head morning and evening, wondering whether to make this woman his wife, or perhaps that one, or maybe the third or fourth, without a single moment spared in the election of his wife, and feeling troubled in his heart when—though somewhat advanced in years—Matsuhime, who could deceive the flowers, came to this mansion.

Determining in his own license that Matsuhime would be his wife for two existences, and while engaged in divine service, paying attention to all matters and devoting his whole body solely to purchasing Matsuhime-sama's favor, he had been looking forward to the arrival of an auspicious day and hour, turning up the sleeves of joint sleep, and raising the nuptial ceremony, when all those joys turned into bubbles of water upon discovering that Matsuhiko, whom he had never expected, appeared in this divine hall, and when he inquired about the other august name of Yurarihiko—whom the believers of the divination relied upon as their sole principal deity—he appeared as the Ōten deity of the final days in a wondrous palace, having suddenly descended here; and from the moment he heard that Matsuhime, the princess of superior righteousness, was his spiritual spouse, Magahiko had been palpitating with a restless heart.

When Chiyo, the maidservant of Matsuhime, appeared at such a place and said to Magahiko-sama that Princess Superior, his master, had business with him, he left his seat upon hearing this, wiggling his nose and stretching his elbows, and went to hear the good news; but lo and behold, Matsuhime's demeanor had completely changed, and she appeared difficult to approach.

When Magahiko, self-proclaiming himself as righteousness itself, faced the princess and tried to express the fullness of his thoughts at length, Matsuhime, with an immovable momentum, spoke to Magahiko-sama, saying, 'From today, I have something to ask of you; Matsuhiko-sama is my husband, so from now on, stop making nasty scrutinizing looks and saying stupid things; I have a husband of two existences, and I am greatly troubled by this; Matsuhiko-sama is Yurarihiko, the Ōten deity of the final days...'

I am your wife Jiyōgihime, from the distant days of Kamiyo, bound by an unbreakable Innen, a Hegure-no-Hegure-no-Hegure warrior wandering the ends Sekai, dwelling there until in the spring when Udonge blossoms, the deep green pines of Ahiohi, binding a vow for Chiyo and kelp, that relationship between you and me is a state of utmost purity and cleanliness, two divine ministers neither defiling nor defiled, born as the spiritual head of all things, more than anything the single character of severing is precious, to cast away romantic attachment utterly and so abruptly thrusting an elbow cannon like a harsh surprise, struck dumb without a word of reply.

Though she exhausts every word and does her utmost, Matsuhime will not consent, mocked even by Ochiyo, with a gush of regretful tears while harboring a grudge against Matsuhikotsukasa, she glumly rises and returns to her original seat, making the color of her face pale as she returns only to find Mankō and Gōsankō along with the rest of the gang mocking her with all their might, Magahiko-sama grows angry and gnashes his teeth yet before others he can neither show anger nor weep, holding down his distressed and suffering breast as he looks down piteously, guided by the maiden Chiyo, Matsuhiko-sama proceeds to the annex and upon looking, what is this, Matsuhime whom he has longed for day in and day out, arrayed in full attire and smilingly, brimming with smiles as Matsuhiko takes her hand and calls her into the inner chamber.

Even Matsuhiko, seasoned as he is, is left dumbfounded, unable to utter a word while gazing upon Matsuhime's face, yet looking around Matsuhime softly grasps that hand and says, 'My beloved husband Matsuhiko, lured away by the night storm, ten years have already passed since we parted, I have spent an excess of days, mornings of rain and evenings of wind, whenever I remember I weep, whenever I remember I sigh once more, without even the milestone of the sun and moon's steed, praying day after day that you, my husband, might have your whereabouts sought out and be allowed to meet me by the pile up like seas and mountains, even their threads are utterly tangled, please try to imagine the state of my heart which I cannot untangle, well now, safe and in good health, I too am happy and With sincere heart and utmost devotion, pitying the two bodies who purely guarded the teachings of the gods, meeting in this unthought-of mountain, giving thanks before the deities of Heaven and Earth, from this journey onward especially, sparing not one's life for the sake of the Way, with all one's heart and all one's body serving and repaying a ten-thousandth part of the divine grace, Ah, Kototama, may your noble spirits prosper.
