From morning till night, a feast is held; the priest of Imoriwake, while saying that he offers sake to the coming and going of numerous kami, turns his cheeks red, blows foul breath wildly, distorts the noses of the attendants, and polluting the surrounding air with the pungent stench of body odor, while rolling the kototama of the Amatsunorito upon the roots of his tongue where his articulation fails, during the morning he sweats profusely as he chants the divine words of the Urayai teaching, and once again offers delicious sake to the Kamisama; having become a sloppy drunkard, when midday arrives he advances unsteadily toward the shinzen and speaks: Thou who art in heaven, hallowed be thy name, thy kingdom come, thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven; setting before himself a heap of appetizers consisting of Amen, somen, tokoroten, udon, soba, and yakiimo, he welcomes the divine advent of the Magatsu-no-kami with utmost politeness, and while thoroughly carrying out the principle of absolute philanthropy, when evening comes he turns not to the sake of Masamune, but to the fleshly palace; Imoriwake rubs his juzu beads, chanting Namu Amida Butsu, Namu Amida, the Hannya Shingyō, and the Haramitsu Kyō in an interesting rhythm, succeeding the position of Tokonami-sama, the principal icon of the Sanakyōgō, and masquerading as a splendid teacher of the doctrine.

Raising a hoarse voice in the mood of sake and groaning is Otora-sama, who stretches out her wrinkled hand, fiercely grips the warmed sake flask, thrusts the morning-glory-shaped sake cup forward, narrows her eyes, and exclaims, "The virtue of sake is also vast and wide; O Masamune-sama, hey, just a moment, please drink up and pass it on!" When she thus extends it, Masamune of the sake tank, in high good spirits, replies, "Otora, you are a splendid fellow; though you are aged and a withered cherry tree, the fragrance of flowers still seems to linger somewhere; that gentle look in your eyes—oops, oops, it is spilling over! "Be that as it may, if I look back and see that this too has been soiled by the august sake of Masamune-sama, I feel all the more grateful; since it is the dew imbued with the spirit favored by the dear person, how could I think it unsatisfactory? Hey, Takahimé, Takahimé, while opening your big mouth wide, why do you not utter a single word saying 'I am here'? "Hey, hey, Ushitora Baba-sama, as for you, my mind is from the very beginning not at ease; call forth Jōgihimé who resides in the Great Interior, and let her keep me company in drinking sake; somehow I feel lonely, and my surroundings have grown cold; after all, human beings cannot pass through this world interestingly and amusingly without the opposite sex; come, come, quickly call Jōgihimé and bring her here," he whines. Ushitora Baba-sama grows furious, foaming at the mouth, rolling her chestnut-like eyes wide, and retorts, "Lord Imoriwake, how on earth can you say such things in front of me? Have you already forgotten the loving whispers of our past tryst? Truly, you are a heartless man; though I am now aged and have become a wrinkled old hag, I am a chivalrous woman of the village of Ukiki who took the alias of Ushitora!"

"To make a fool of me has its limits; no matter how many kami may come and go, I will not tolerate having my love garments torn off like a cat's eyes changing hue as you shamelessly demand; since I also have my own resolve, remember this!" Saying this, completely forgetting that she had kept Matsuhiko waiting at the reception desk, she forcefully grabs the lapels of Imoriwake, and shouting, "Hey there, Masamune the Great Vastness, Imoriwake, do not make a fool of me; Otora's arms have bones in them, and I will not let it end just like this; how is this, how is this?" she crushes his chest with all her might. Imoriwake foams at the mouth and his face turns instantly pale.
