Chapter 16: Rain 露 Moon [1206]

雨露月〔1206〕

§ 1

In the garden of Oshihe, the great Ōhiroki, in the fleshly palace of Masamune-sama, the gods who come and go are offered Miki, and as they drink and revel, chanting the Magatsunorito and sharing cups of sake, their hearts are lifted in high spirits, playing in the garden of Tengoku.

Watching for the opportune moment, Otora-sama appears before the Ōhiromahe, intending to offer her evening Orei, and with Okiku-san in attendance to serve as a companion for the sake, she wraps her body in solemn Shiyōzoku, clutches a Chiukei with a look of great solemnity, and departs from the Kyōso's mansion, accompanied by Isokō, Mankō, and three others, proceeding leisurely to enter the Daishinden.

Reverently clapping her hands in Kashihade, she causes the air in the Atari to become even more turbid, chants the Magatsunorito, and after stating her own selfish wishes in a stream of a million words, she bids farewell to the five and hurriedly returns to the room where Imoriwake-sama lies in hiding.

Afterward, Mankō and Isokō pull open the sliding doors of the cupboard, take out bedding and pillows, spread them out before the Ōhiromahe, and lie down stretching out their legs, and all of them, one after another, chant the San-go no Amatsu Norito, and upon finishing, they whisper and laugh together about the circumstances of Takahime-sama, Kurohime-tsukasa, and Otora-baba-sama, and the romance of Imoriwake-sama, finally falling asleep.

Mankō-sama wakes up, observes the breathing of the four, slides open the storm shutters at the entrance without making a sound, and begins to wander about the garden, thinking, 'Is Okiku-sama perhaps outside? I must have a direct confrontation, or I will not be satisfied.'

Thinking of Isokō-sama and the four others who are fast asleep, he muses, 'Shall I too board the boat of Shirakawa, which they do not even dream of? The sound of oars seems to be creaking as it approaches the harbor of Otora. If I ask in which direction to go to reach the pier, it is truly worrisome. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase. I pray earnestly for a joyful reception through the grace of the god of connection. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, whether the stars fall from heaven, or even if a weasel lets out a final fart, Otora-baba-sama said that Mankō is the living palace of the Cultivating Great God, and her daughter Okiku is the Earthly Princess, and because they are a fated couple, if their spirits and bodies unite here and participate in the Great Divine Work, the mountain of Kohoku will be prosperous.'

He continues, 'I do not trust in the gods of fortune-telling, but Otora-baba-sama's words pleased Mankō-sama. For now, I shall play the hypocrite, and once I have successfully made Okiku my wife, I will consider the Shihodoki, lead her to submit to the path of San-go, and perhaps we shall take each other's hands and follow Matsuhiko-sama on the path of conquering the demons. Even the patient Otora-sama will surely believe once her beloved daughter has faith in the path of San-go. If that happens, Mankō's marriage will not end in vain. If I stake my life on the two paths of God and love and join the Great Divine Work of the spirit world, it is truly a convenient matter. If one waits, the wind of the sea route will blow. Matsuhiko-sama has met his beloved wife after a long time, and I have met my sister-in-law in an unexpected place. That we have come to hold our wedding ceremony here must be some divine arrangement. There is nothing more profitable than this. Thinking of this, I feel sorry for Isokō, Aku, Taku, and Teku. Truly, this floating world does not go as one wishes. The gods appear in the open to distinguish between good and evil. Isokō-sama's power of discernment is truly astonishing. The skill with which he solved the difficult Sanmai-kyō without effort is that of an extraordinary person. If Matsuhiko heard that, he would surely be impressed. I never dreamed until now that Isokō was such a great person. Is he an incarnation of Konohana-hime appearing on Mount Tenkyō? He is different from the ordinary. When I peeked at Isokō-sama's sleeping figure, it seemed as if an indescribable spiritual light was enveloping his surroundings. This fellow is a monster to whom one must not speak careless nonsense. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase. May the spirits be blessed, and may Imoriwake-sama, Magahiko-sama, and Otora-baba-sama wake up to the truth of the San-go.'

§ 2

To comprehend the Way and open the teaching of Shinsei to the four quarters, may You protect it, the great deity who guards the Way of Ananahi, before the august presence of Kuniharutachi, we humbly and reverently pray.

It is Mankō who is singing this while sitting on the Roha platform of the garden.

Mankō: 'Ah, what a dark night! A great many stars are appearing, but as expected, it is no good unless it is the moonlight. Nevertheless, for young men and women burning with the blood of youth, the deity of binding is after all a dark night. If Okiku and I were to be peered at by a round face from the sky right in the middle of our emotional union, it would hardly look decent.'

Mankō: 'Just take a look at that, over there, that person is reading a love letter on the railing of the bridge, while the clouds out of jealousy hide the moon.'

Mankō: 'They say that even the moon, upon seeing the romance of young men and women, becomes happy and smiles. No, perhaps it is not out of happiness, but laughing because it is funny. Right then, that rascal of a cloud gets jealous and makes the love letter impossible to see, so a thing called a cloud is a spiteful fellow. Onikumohiko is also a cloud of this world after all.'

So he mutters to himself. To that place, there came a shadow approaching with stealthy footsteps. Mankō's heart leaped involuntarily. Who on earth was this human shadow?
