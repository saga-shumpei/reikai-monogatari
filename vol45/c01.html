Chapter 1: Pine Wind [1191]

松風〔1191〕

§ 1

Both fields and mountains are dyed in brocade by the autumn wind, becoming the very beginning of winter, with the withered leaves of the trees, O how ruthlessly, scattering mournfully on Mount Kogitayama, while morning and evening the believers wring out their sweat and strain their voices, and even the Kototama being proclaimed somehow has become clouded and murky in the state of the world— such is the Kamuyakata which has exposed this truth, where the blind who wander in endless delusion and the deaf in spirit gather together, rejoicing in perverted teachings to bring about heaven and pure land, struggling and sweating until Magatsumi, looking as though it has found its moment, leaps and bounds on high, while the Imoriwake who presides over the Urai-kyo teaching turns his physical body (karatama) into a dwelling place where evil spirits nest.

Without distinction between night and day, into cheap liquor that melts the heart, the tongue becomes tangled and the words of the divine oracle are utterly incomprehensible, while the twisted-hearted Magahiko serves haughtily by his side, flattering him with endless chatter, and even while having his eyebrows plucked and his rear hairs pulled out, with the heart's soul of Murakimo supposedly having been polished— from eyes utterly steeped in delusion, he summons together the old hags, and preaches with a solemn face a jumbled, disjointed divine teaching, while senseless superstitious believers, flocking like flies to a privy, sensing a foul odor, cling to it as if it were precious aloeswood and haiko, rejoicing as though it were musk, and treasure up teachings resembling mere flatulence.

To blow the foul demon winds in all directions— how vexing indeed! Bunsuke, who cannot see with his eyes, at the reception desk of Daimon Shrine, attired in white ceremonial robes and white hakama, rolling and glaring his whites of his eyes, strives within the ink-stone's ocean where his brush's tip reflects his heart, as a propitious omen of the pine's age of the gods, while a black snake coils inextricably around the thousand-year-old aged pine, with no distinction between backbone and belly, clinging resolutely into a single-horned sacred body (goshintai), shaking its head incessantly from morning till night writing away,causing the superstitious old hags to shed tears of joy and sniffle their noses, as it is mounted on hanging scrolls and picture frames,

and made to be revered by clapping hands reverently— how amusing it is! Not only that, but speaking as though it were an offering to the gods, he wields his brush stroke by stroke to paint ink sketches of sweet herbs and bitter greens, not that it is any proof of having been bewitched by strange teachings, but painting crude pictures of turnips and radishes together with their tops, in a Kogita mansion where from root to leaf the Kototama does not function at all, from top to bottom there is nothing but derailments— the deluded and deaf false divine arts, and the rising smoke like that of an ink-drawn dragon in the pitch-black sky, winding and twisting toward the empty heavens, while people's hearts wander in empty space— a sight gloomy indeed to behold. Matsuhiko, Mankō, and Gosankō,

along with Aku, Taku, and Teku, muttering complaints as "aku, taku, teku," took their leave out of sheer obligation, and descended the steep slope full of discontent, heading back leaving the mansion behind. Just as they walked briskly as far as the foot of the single-plank bridge spanning across Kajika Riverbed, far back up on the slope, stretching out his withered voice, opening a fan marked with the crest of the Ten Suns, beckoning to them and shouting at the top of his lungs, "Oi, oi!" he stopped them like Kumagai of old. The perplexed party halted in their tracks, and looking back behind them, they saw that the one they had met at the foot of the bridge was none other than Grandmother Otora, walking briskly down the sloping path like an arrow shot from a bow, with her hair streaming in disorder.

Knowing this was no ordinary matter, the party sat down at the foot of the bridge, and waited while resting their breath. Ah, great kami, manifestations of divine presence! May your august spirits be peaceful and auspicious, while the eighty magatsumi and ugly officials lurking in the mountains of Kogita distort truth and defile the world— pray, let them describe minutely, without a single omission or flaw, that very reality in all its details, Mizuki prayed and beseeched with trembling reverence before the august presence of the Great Deity, the Heavenly Susanoo.

When Matsuhiko and his party took their leave of the Kamuyakata in Mount Kogitayama, descended the steep slope, and returned as far as the foot of the single-plank bridge, someone from behind called out to them, shouting "Oi, oi!" Though they thought it unpleasant, surely, if she ran with that momentum, she would catch up with them somewhere; and wondering what on earth she might say, for the sake of reference, hearing it out could hardly be a loss, so they sat down here, spread out their straw mats, and decided to wait a while. The one who came running in a flurry was none other than former Grandmother Otora. Grandmother Otora panted and wheezed, drawing her breath in deep gasps...
