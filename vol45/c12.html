Chapter 12: 喜苔 Song [1202]

喜苔歌〔1202〕

§ 1

The sacred ground of Mount Kogita had already concluded its Tsukinamisai ritual, and Magahiko, with his bent waist, mounted the platform first, finished delivering a fluent speech on divine virtue with a composed expression, and descended in high spirits.

Otora-sama, who climbed up after him, rattled on like a torrent with eloquence as swift as a rushing river and arrows flying from a bow, entrusting the conduct of Imoriwake to Kami without offering any defense. Wishing to dispel the doubts of the Mamehito with all her heart and soul, she smoothly slipped the wheel of Kototama forward and leisurely descended the stage.

When one looked up, upon following after her, the dignified figure of Otomego stood abruptly on the platform, and unexpected Chiyo-sama clapped her hands like autumn maple leaves, offering prayers to Kami.

Having detailed at length how the divine grace of revered Kami is by no means trivial, she declared smoothly that no matter how wicked the conduct of Imoriwake, Magahiko, or Otora might be, one must never abandon the Great Way of Kami, keeping Kami and humans strictly separate and distinct in faith. Striking forth a living Kototama, she descended the platform amidst a storm of applause from the numerous believers who clapped their hands, hiding her small figure.

At this moment, in the very middle of the believers, there was a man standing stark upright like a Nio, shouting at the top of his voice to lay bare the inner workings of the Uranai faith.

His voice reached the ears of the Kyōso-dono Imoriwake, and Otora also rushed over in alarm, taking the raging rough man by the hand with careful attention and pulling him back to her own living room. When she lined up a wealth of sake and side dishes before him and treated him, the man instantly narrowed his eyes, struck his forehead with his right hand, and drained his cup gulp after gulp.

As the alcohol went to his head and he began to speak smoothly with the rolled tongue of a thief's slang, he blurted out, 'Hey, hey, Otora, who do you take me for? I am Kumakō, renowned far and wide. You shook me off, hid yourself away in the village of floating driftwood, became a chivalrous person unsuited to your nature, opened a gambling den, gathered numerous followers, and threw your weight around as a thief named Otora, didn't you? If you make too much a fool of me, shall I expose your inside secrets? I am fully entitled to make you pour my drinks.'

Holding hands with Imoriwake, you wicked deity are putting on a grand display of pomp and luxury in broad daylight while squeezing the lifeblood out of people. Now from here on, Kumakō will not budge an inch no matter what; unless you offer a reasonably good greeting, I will not be appeased.'

When he pressed her insistently, demanding to know what she would do, Imoriwake was struck with utter astonishment and stood trembling and shivering.

Otora arched her willow eyebrows in anger and retorted, 'My, my, Kumakō, what are you blathering about? Who would deign to be a partner to a sake thief like you? I grew disgusted with a dissipated, unreliable man like you and ran away. Fancying yourself with the audacity to show up here without feeling the least bit ashamed that a man was abandoned by a woman! Come now, hurry up and be off with you; you are getting in the way of the divine path. Quick, make haste and leave!'

When she urged him thus, Kumakō straightened his knees and shouted, 'Hand over ten thousand ryo of gold! If you refuse, this grudge of Kumakō's will never be healed as long as I live. You’ve built such a splendid house; you cannot possibly tell me you don’t have ten thousand or five thousand ryo of gold! Hand it over quickly, or else become my wife!'

Just as Otora was clamoring to know what her answer should be, Mankō, Gosankō, Aku, Teku, and Taku—the five of them—startled by the noise, hurried over with bustling footsteps, shouting, 'I know not who any of you are, but I am Gosankō, a thief who has made a name for myself lately. Leave this place to me!' They spouted thief's slang and pushed themselves forward, quite amusingly.

Kumakō glared with blazing eyes and brushed them aside coldly, snapping, 'I know not whose fellows you might be, but this is no stage for the likes of you to step out onto. Be gone from this place at once!'

At this moment, Aku stood up and declared, 'I am General Katahiko, a retainer of Ōkuninushi, highly renowned in the capital of Haruna. Leading tens of thousands of troops, I am heading toward the mansion of Saien. Hearing that we happened to pass Mount Kogita on our way to pray for victory before the divine presence, only to hear the unseemly sounds of your quarreling voices—such improper noises cannot be overlooked as they are! Brahmā, Indra, and Jizaiten, grant me the binding spell of *kinshibari*! Bind Kumakō hand and foot, and return with him to my camp!' As he issued this command to Taku, Teku, and Mankō, even the mighty Kumakō was cowed with fear, weeping and begging for mercy with tears in his hands, while Aku turned to face Gosankō.

Are you the Kefukaku Isokō Oyabun who has been selling yourself lately? I shall leave this place to you. If you do not listen, I shall report this immediately. Saying that he would go to the Shinzen to offer a Kiguwan, he stood up and left the place.

Afterward, Isokō, with a composed face, lined up his Shiranami Kotoba, took out a thousand Koban that Otora-baba-sama had hidden, and handed them to Kumakō as Tegi money. Kumakō received them and said, 'This is truly an Arigata Gochiyūsai. If I have this much money, I can drink sweet sake and play for five or ten years. How grateful I am.' Fearing to stay too long, he stood up, tucked up his skirts, and went up the Sakamichi.

He returned home Ichimokusan. Afterward, Otora sighed, 'Thanks to Isokō-sama, I was saved from a dangerous situation. If it can be settled for a thousand Ryo, it is cheap. I have caused you great Gokurau.'

Mankō, Aku, Teku, and Taku are also quick-witted people. Now, Imoriwake-sama, that fellow Kumakō has left. Magahiko-sama, why do you not appear here quickly? Please drink the celebratory sake, Isokō-sama. Starting with that fellow Mankō, and you guests Aku, Taku, and Teku, let us drink in an Omoshiro manner.

When she called out in a loud voice, the two people who had been hiding, having snuck into the next Hitoma, appeared suddenly in this place.

They exposed faces that looked displeased for no particular reason and were trembling. The comicality of their style was something.

Otora: 'Now Imoriwake-sama, you are truly a spineless person. If one were to speak of your merits, it is that from morning until night, you drink sake continuously without stopping and lose yourself in it; that is your merit. Magahiko: 'Hey, since the Kyōso-sama entered the chest, I, respecting the authority of the Vice-Kyōso, was clinging to your side.'

Otora: 'My, what a fine pair of cowards have gathered. Even if Imoriwake-sama is beyond help, why are you not more firm? Can you serve the Hinodewake God of justice and heaven with such an attitude?'

Magahiko: 'Since Ōhiroki Masamune-sama was performing the hiding in the heavenly rock cave, I, as his attendant, thought I must do the same, and since it was not that I was not afraid, I hid in the rock cave for just a short while.'

Mankō: 'Aha-ha-ha-ha.'

Teku, Taku: 'Ufu-fu-fu. Pupu-tsupu-pu.'

Okiku appeared in this place and said:

Okiku: 'Now, excuse me, Mother. Matsuhime-sama was surprised and asked if some strange event had occurred. She said that although it would be fine for her to go, it might be inconvenient, so she asked if I would mind going for a run to see the situation. Because she said that, I returned to the gate and stood there to listen. Though I do not know who the men were, they were raising their voices in a great quarrel that echoed all around. Thinking I could not bear this, I returned and told Matsuhiko-sama and Matsuhime the whole story from beginning to end.'

Okiku: 'I joined my hands and begged them to help my mother quickly. When I asked, the two of them smiled and played the Norito toward the Shinzen, saying, "Do not worry at all.
