Chapter 14: 三昧経 [1204]

三昧経〔1204〕

§ 1

Imoriwake, having been pinched by Torabaa-sama and having his nose twisted into a faint, completely forgot the dogs-wouldnt-eat-it jealous quarrel, and in the back room began to smack his lips over hanniyatau once again, served by Torabaa. As for Tora, through some evil karmic entanglement, the thousand-ryō koban she held dearer than the gods was trampled upon by her husband of fifteen years whom she had cast aside—the drunken Kumakō—and after having her booze heavily drunk up and being stomped on, she boiled with vexation, began to exhibit the wobbling in religious faith common among superstitious people, and after calling down the gods to the utmost as not even being worth a fart's tissue paper, she bit her own tongue, came to with a start, and returned once again to a heart that worshipped the gods. Isokō explained it through a waka poem, intending to wake her from her deluded dream. Namely, that the enshrined deity of Kogitayama was a false and nameless god; that because of the sins Takahime had accumulated in her own heart, she had once fallen into the nethermost country, wandered at the crossroad of heaven, and finally recovered her breath, and just as her senses were fading, an old fox that had been watching for that opening crept in, imitated a deity, fabricated various absurd divine names, and made her blindly believe that she was the living shrine of Hinodewake, stubbornly asserting that she was of the lineage of the sex-changing male and possessed a pure Yamato spirit; that matters of the three thousand worlds could not be understood unless through this Hinodewake, and that it was her mission to quietly let the sex-changing female know of it—thus being deceived by the evil fox, she grew ever more stubbornly confident in her secondary guardian spirit, and being extremely indignant that the spirits of the sex-changing female and of Katsukatsukanokami did not take her up, she cajoled Kurohime, Imoriwake, Magahiko, and other demo gentlemen and old hags with grave mental defects, collected donations from the believers of Mitsugokyō, and established her base in Kitayama village, but as her imposture was finally wearing thin, she ordered Imoriwake to transfer the head temple to Kogitayama. Meanwhile, Takahime and Kurohime themselves, deeply moved by the merciful virtue of the Great Deity Kami Susanoo whom they had thought to be a god of supreme wickedness and lawlessness, cast away the Uranai teachings like worn-out sandals, converted to Mitsugokyō, and were at last added to the number of propagators. Yet their attachment remained strong to the end, and the notion that she was the living shrine of the rightful heavenly Hinodewake could not easily be removed. Moreover, Kurohime for her part firmly believed herself to be the living shrine of the Otohime of Ryūgū, putting the propagators of Mitsugokyō through a great deal of trouble. Nevertheless, through the zealous and varied cultivation by Yotorihiko-no-Mikoto and others, day by day, like peeling off thin paper, the clouds of delusion were removed from the skies of Takahime and Kurohime's hearts. Now the two of them had entirely awakened from their deluded dreams, reflected upon their past words and deeds, and were tormented by shame. Yet Imoriwake, remaining as before, succeeded to Takahime's legacy and stubbornly supported the Uranai teachings. The reason was that the various demons who had hitherto plotted to disrupt the true teachings of Mitsusu by using the bodies of Takahime and Kurohime as instruments had no place to remain within their bodies upon the conversion of Takahime and Kurohime, and one by one had completely shifted their abode into the bodies of Imoriwake, Magahiko, and Torabaa-sama in Kogitayama. Therefore, the fierce superstition of these three persons was in no way inferior to, and indeed surpassed, that of the former Takahime and Kurohime. Furthermore, Imoriwake had formerly been a soldier, possessed a fair amount of social education, had dabbled slightly in philosophy, and half-knowingly grazed the scriptures of various sects, thus broadening his knowledge somewhat; hence, for crooked gods to use as a tool, he was far more convenient than Takahime or Kurohime. Yet no matter how much common sense or learning one possessed, once one's core conscience was violated and a major mental defect incurred, even the so-called wise men and scholars of the world would become no more manageable than foolish men and women. Imoriwake was not an illiterate person who was unable to judge the disjointed divine names and doctrines revealed by Takahime, but by now he had entirely lost any fair capacity for understanding. Therefore, in the morning he prayed to the god of Uranai, at midday he shouted 'Amen', and when evening came he rubbed his prayer beads, rang bells, and earnestly chanted Buddhist scriptures, believing it to be his sole good deed.

The sutra that Imoriwake chanted with reverence was always the Kanbutsu Zanmai Kyō. This scriptural text was a forgery by the disciples of Gautama Buddha, created as a expedient for the propagation of Buddhism in order to defend Buddha. In short, anyone with a modicum of thought would be able to perceive that this scripture was a case of partiality defeating its own purpose toward Buddha. Evening having come, Imoriwake, as was his wont, began to chant the Kanbutsu Zanmai Kyō while rubbing his prayer beads and ringing his bells.
