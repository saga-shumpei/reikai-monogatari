The worms of the throat could not help demanding it with a croaking sound. Like a cat pouncing upon a dried bonito, where had the initial fierce bearing gone? Suddenly becoming beaming-faced, they began to do it with a drinking-at-first-sight manner. The fact that Kumakō had raised a loud voice in the crowd and attacked Imoriwake's sake was, at the bottom of his heart, a calculation that he wanted to somehow reach the sweet sake; therefore, it was a fair wind for a crossing ship and a sail for a favored hand—a most convenient circumstance. 蠑螈別は、小さい声で舌をもつらせ乍ら、

蠑螈『おいお寅、煩いから有る丈け持つて帰なしたらどうだ。そして今後は文句は云はないと書付けをとつておくのだな』

お寅『これ蠑螈別さま、何と云ふ気の弱い事を仰有るのだ。生命と懸替の、あの一万両を渡す位なら死んだがましぢやないか。何うしてお前さまと私と此先やつて行くのだい。黙つて居なさい。溝壺へ捨てる金が有つても熊さまなんかへ渡す金はありませぬぞ。こんな処へヌツケリコとやつて来て思はぬ苦労をかけやがつて、これ熊公、此お寅さまを何と心得てる。浮木の森の女侠客丑寅サンと云つたら、ヘン、憚り乍ら此姐さまだ。お前達の様な一羽鶏に脅かされて屁古垂れる様な姐さまぢやありませぬぞや』

と棄鉢気分になり、入墨のした腕をグツと捲り、一方の足を立膝し乍ら泡を吹き飛ばし呶鳴りつけた。熊公は猛り狂うてお寅婆の髻を引つ掴み力限り引張りまはす。蠑螈別、魔我彦は此権幕に肝を潰し、奥の間の長持の中へ身を隠し、慄ひ戦いてゐる。此声を聞きつけて万公、五三公、アク、タク、テクの五人はドヤドヤと走り来り、

§ 4

Mankō: 'Wait, wait, if I tell you to wait, you had better wait!'

Iso: 'I know not what entanglement this may be, but the arbitration of this place shall be undertaken by me, Isokō.'

Saying this, he deliberately adopts the language of the shiranami to begin intimidating them.

Kumakō: 'Aha-ha-ha-ha! For a mere kowatsupa yarou to leap out into such a place and arbitrate our kenkuwa is kataharaite indeed! This is mere karaiba of a Yanosukeningiyō! Do you think to make a hekotare at such a thing, and make the face of Kumakō the yohidore stand? This is no stage for kowatsupamushiya! Get on back and clear out!'

Aku: 'Ho there, you called the drunken rogue Kumakō, you had best wait a while. I am none other than Katahiko Shiyōgun, the great inspector of Brahmanism. What are you chimayo to rush into such a place and make such a ranbau? An outrageous shiromono indeed! Ho, my kerai, bind this ranbausha soundly with the wondrous art of kanashiba bestowed upon us by Daijizaiten!'

Iso: 'Now, now, Katahiko Shiyōgun-sama, your anger is natural enough, but to bind a man without even listening to the circumstances is what they call merciless. Might I ask if you would be so kind as to leave this matter to me, Iso-san, the chivalrous hero of Iruna?'

Aku: 'Though he is a most hateful fellow, yet since you, a chivalrous hero currently making a name, say so, I suppose I cannot refuse you flatly. If that is so, I entrust the resolution of this place entirely to you. Should you fail to settle it satisfactorily, there shall be no forgiveness!'

Iso: 'Hai, I understand the details completely. As a chivalrous hero currently making a name, even at the cost of my life I shall bring about a settlement here and show it to you. If by any chance it does not go well, I shall perform seppuku right here and show you. Then you may punish me as you please, honorable sir.'

Aku: 'If that is so, I shall wait a while in the inner room. Mankō, Taku, Teku, follow behind me. Oyabun Isokō, farewell then.'

Sticking out his elbows, he strides leisurely toward the grand hall while suppressing a laugh.

Otora: 'My, my, Katahiko Shiyōgun-sama, that you should grace us with your noble person! This too must be the divine virtue of the gods; oh, I am deeply grateful.'

Iso: 'Katahiko Shiyōgun, famed as the minister of the right guard of Ōkuninushi who reigns flourishingly in the land of Haruna, is now leading tens of thousands of troops on his march toward the mansion of Saien, having heard of the divine virtue of Kobakayama—nay, hearing whispers of it, and in order to pray for victory, he halted his troops in the forest of Ukiki, and accompanied by a small number of retainers, came to worship. According to the divine revelation, he heard that a debauched man named Kumakō attempted unlawful coercion against the divine minister Imoriwake-dono and others, as well as Otora-dono. Without a moment's delay, upon coming here, to find this unacceptable state of affairs—thus Katahiko Shiyōgun-sama conveyed it through the spirit to the spirit of this Iso-san. Even if this Iso-san, possessed by the august spirit of Katahiko Shiyōgun, stands as mediator, surely it will not be found lacking, eh, you called Kumakō?'

Kumakō: 'Hai, that a noble personage like yourself should trouble yourself with the labor of arbitration is an absolute honor. Oh, from this day forth I shall cast aside my old heart completely, and hereafter I shall never set foot in this mansion again. Please be completely at ease.'

Iso: 'A prompt acquiescence! Ah, the divine spirit of Katahiko Shiyōgun and the older brother of Iso-san are both thoroughly satisfied. Regarding this, Otora-dono, Imoriwake-dono, I too am a chivalrous hero. I cannot do things by halves. Consider this as straw-sandal money for the nonce, and hand over one thousand ryo of gold flatly to this Kuma-san. If Imoriwake-sama can have this resolved with just this, it must be a cheap bargain. It is merely pinch-money. Aha-ha-ha-ha!'

Otora: 'How could I possibly disobey the honorable oyabun's arbitration? I too was formerly a female chivalrous hero, and I understand the pride of a chivalrous hero well. If that is so, out of regard for your honorable face, I will hand over the one thousand ryo of gold, so please make sure that Kumakō-sama never comes saying anything further hereafter.'

Iso: 'Ah, I understand completely. As expected of an elder sister! You are truly decisive. Ho, Kumakō, how is this? You should have no complaints now, surely?'

§ 5

Kumakō: 'Why, I thank you truly. With as much as a thousand ryō of gold, I can have five or ten years of sweet saké. Ah, I thank you indeed.'

Otora brought out a thousand koban from the next room,

Otora: 'Now, Oyabun Isokō-sama, please take this and keep the bond in exchange.'

Iso: 'Ahahaha. Taking a bond is something for the people of the future. A man once making a promise is like the everlasting bedrock, never to be shaken at all. How is that, Kumakō?'

Kumakō: 'Yes, yes, I too am a man. How could I say a word against it? Hey, Otora, rest assured. Thankful I am; with you, there being this and that or not, such fine saké can be drunk. Chiyoi, chiyoi, chiyoi, I receive it.'

And joining both hands, he turned his palms upward and shook them up and down.

Iso: 'There, it is a thousand ryō. Receive it securely.'

Kumakō: 'Yes, thank you. For all eternity, I will not forget your great favor. And as for Otora, I forget her from this very moment.'

Sooner said than done, he shoved them into his bosom, and as if saying that lingering was to be feared, he hurried away down the slope like an arrow shot from a bow.
