Chapter 5: 肱鉄 [1195]

肱鉄〔1195〕

§ 1

Unwittingly drunk on sake, he babbled on without knowing his own state, and at the end of his derailment, he carelessly blurted out that he longed for Takahime. Hearing these words, Otora-sama, who was serving the sake, flared up like a raging fire, grabbed him by the lapels, and pinned him down. She struck the flesh-shrine of Masamune, who knew neither front nor back, and taking the hand of Giriten-sama, she dragged him into the inner room, laid him upon the futon, and mended the situation with such absurdity. Matsuhiko and his party, who revered him as Matsudai-sama, gazed at the scene of the room in total disarray, knitted their brows in suspicion, and stood still at the entrance.

While Magahiko-sama was finishing the cleaning with a palm-fiber broom, waiting for the fool, he finally entered the room and took a seat. From the next room, a groaning voice could be heard: 'Otora-baba-sama is a terrible wretch, she keeps listing nonsense about longing for Takahime-dono.' As he groaned in a trance, Otora, with her eyes glaring and her face puffed up, was interrupted by Mankō-sama, who folded his hands and suddenly feigned a divine possession. 'Un, un, un, dosu-dosun!' He leaped up about a foot, clasped his hands neatly to his chest, and whispered, 'I am the Great God of Agriculture,' as he seized the moment. Otora was struck with astonishment, and the flesh-shrine of Mankō-sama...

Thinking, 'Is he indeed the Great God of Agriculture? Then, by the providence of the spirit, he is the person who will become the bridegroom of Okiku. Ah, how grateful, how grateful,' she folded her hands repeatedly, with an innocence that made her forget the anger of Imoriwake. Matsuhiko and the others, watching the state of this scene, were struck dumb, thinking it truly a gathering of superstition. Just then, a young girl appeared and said, 'Excuse me, Giriten-jō, the living shrine of Hinodewake, Kamiyoshihime-sama, has suddenly found a need for you. She says to excuse yourself from the guests and come, even if only for a moment,' and she urged him to come quickly, holding out her hands.

When she spoke, Magahiko stood up and said, 'Everyone, please excuse me. Otora-baba-sama, Okiku-sama, Matsudai-sama, and everyone else, please treat them with great care. I shall return here again after a while,' and saying this, he walked briskly toward the detached mansion together with the messenger girl, his shoulders squared.

In another building was the room of Matsuhime; though the room was narrow, it was a three-ken structure, a house that was neat and simple, made of plain wood without decorations. Matsuhime lived in seclusion here with a young girl of twelve or thirteen named Chiyo as her maid, holding the real power of the Uranai religion. On the surface, Imoriwake was the founder, but the actual power lay with this Matsuhime. Therefore, both Imoriwake and Otora-baba-sama held her in awe, and internally, they submitted to all her dictates. Naturally, this Matsuhime was a strong woman who had been an intermediary of the Uranai religion and had served as the leader of the faith at Takashiro-yama. She was a person of merit who had submitted to the Sangō-kyō and welcomed Tamateruhiko back. Because the Imoriwake faction had gathered the remnants of the Uranai religion at Kobotoyama, opened a sacred site, propagated a heretical faith, and were actively displaying the Uranai style, Kototawake-no-Mikoto had specifically ordered Matsuhime to be sent to the Uranai religion to fundamentally reform its doctrines. For this reason, Matsuhime was endowed with special divine power, and even the formidable Imoriwake yielded a step to her, contenting himself with the empty name of founder, while from morning until night, he immersed himself in sake under the pretext of divine comings and goings, seeking the whereabouts of Takahime and drowning his pent-up feelings in intoxication.

Magahiko: 'Kamiyoshihime-sama, for what business have you called for me through Ochiyo-sama just now?'

Matsuhime: 'There is no particular urgent business, but I have asked you to come for a moment because I am troubled unless you promise not to speak of me from this day forward.'

Magahiko: 'Have I said anything to you that would be an obstacle?'

Matsuhime: 'You are always saying unpleasant things to me, are you not? Until today, I have avoided the sharp edge of your love by putting things off day by day, but today I must tell you clearly. The guest you have now is a person called Matsuhiko-sama, but who do you think Matsuhiko-sama is the living shrine of?'

Maga: 'Is this not the living shrine of the Lord God of Matsudaihi?'

Matsuhime: 'That may well be. Because it is so, there is a karmic bond by which I must somehow become the husband and wife with Matsudai-sama, and Giritenjiyō-sama, I wish for you to give up on me cleanly and once and for all as of this very moment.'

Maga: 'The gods of ancient times were husband and wife with Matsudai-sama and Jiyōgihime-sama, were they not? However, today your spirit has ascended and your physical shrine is different, so even if you and I were to become husband and wife, punishment would hardly fall upon us. No matter what you say, I have worked hard for you as both your shadow and your sun until now, so if you suddenly come out of nowhere and say such a thing, I will by no means agree to it.'

Matsuhime: 'Now, Giritenjiyō-sama, what kind of things have you done when you say you worked hard for me as my shadow and my sun? I must express my thanks, so let me hear about it for a moment.'

Maga: 'Just as one would expect from the living shrine of Giritenjiyō, I am a man of strong principles. Though you were of Ananai-kyō, I am fully aware, by the hundreds and thousands, that you successfully disguised yourself and entered here. Imoriwake-sama also once held a quiet consultation among the three of us—including old mother Tora and myself—saying, "That person is suspicious; perhaps she has come as a bomb, so shall we get her dead drunk and finish her off?" Even so, because this Magahiko holds affection for you, I thought that if I said this and that to help you, you would inevitably say yes; thus, I made various excuses and convinced Imoriwake and that ghastly old mother Tora. Now, you have become the foremost authority of the Uranai teachings, and both Imoriwake and Tora revere you secretly as their teacher, handling everything by receiving your divine oracles. That too is entirely the fruit of Magahiko's mediation. If this Magahiko were not here, your life would have vanished long ago. Now, do you still say no even after knowing this? Matsuhiko-sama may indeed be the King of Matsudaihi. However, that is a marriage of spirits; you and I must forge the matrimonial bond of our physical bodies, or else this Magahiko's manhood cannot be upheld. Now, let me hear your decisive reply. Depending on your answer, this Magahiko has thoughts of his own.'

Matsuhime: 'Ho ho ho ho! When you say you have thoughts, what on earth do you mean? If you have thoughts, I too have thoughts. Now, let me hear those thoughts.'

Magahiko was at a loss for words, and

Maga: 'Er... those thoughts I speak of simply mean that I am filled with overwhelming emotion.'

Matsuhime: 'Ho ho ho ho! What is so great about being filled with overwhelming emotion? Are you not saying something ridiculous?'

Maga: 'Please leave aside such wordplay and let us swiftly bring the treaty to conclusion.'

Matsuhime: 'What treaty is this? In an age when extraterritoriality, mixed residence in the interior, treaty revision, and equal opportunity are all the rage, I have no desire to conclude such a stifling treaty. Treaties are generally concluded only when nations face mutual danger. Whether it be the Sino-Japanese Treaty or the treaties of the Washington Conference, they are by no means concluded for the sake of universal peace. There are no particular elements of danger contained between you and me; for what purpose is there a treaty? And what sort of matters are at issue in its clauses? Unless I hear about those first, can we so easily conclude, ratify, and exchange a treaty?'

Maga: 'The article [jō] in the treaty [jōyaku] you speak of and the emotion [jō] in the affective pact [jōyaku] I speak of differ as much as heaven and earth in their essence. Your jō is the character for rule [suji], while my jō is the feeling of a blue heart.'

Matsuhime: 'That is different from the jiyō [upper/superior] of Jiyōgihime, is it not?'

Maga: 'That is entirely the exact opposite.'

魔我『ふり釣瓶いかにピンピン覆るとも 汲んで見ようぞ天上車井』

松姫『義理天上車に釣瓶はかかるとも 片方は汲めど片方からから。 並べては少しも汲めぬ山の井の 釣瓶を如何に濡らす由なし』

千代『義理天上恋の破れた悲しさに 首をつる瓶とおなり遊ばせ。 ホヽヽヽヽ釣瓶おろしにかけられて 沈み給へり恋の深井に』

魔我『まだ年も行かぬ癖して魔我彦に 何をつるつる水臭い事云ふ』

千代『如何しても末代さまの御前に 行かねばならぬ魔我左様なら』

魔我『まて暫し、そんな事なら俺が行く 子供の飛び出す幕でないぞや』

松姫『義理天上日の出の神の生宮に 今日は改め一言申す。 松彦は此松姫がその昔 相知り合うた珍の恋人。 恋人と聞いて驚き給ふまじ 神の許せし夫婦なりせば』

魔我『何とまア悪性な事になつて来た こんな事なら救ふぢやなかつたに』

松姫『村肝の心の底ぞ知られける 枉のすくひし魔我彦司を』

魔我彦は双手を組み、

『エーエ、雪隠の火事だ』

松姫『オホヽヽヽ』

千代『イヒヽヽヽ、阿呆阿呆阿呆』

魔我『エー、コメツチヨの癖に八釜しいわい。キヽヽヽヽ気色が悪いわい。サツパリ杓子だ。源助だ、アーア』
