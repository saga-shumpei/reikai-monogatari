Chapter 19: 邪 Kami 全滅 [1951]

邪神全滅〔1951〕

§ 1

Here, the five male Kami, overcoming countless hardships through steep valleys and barely breaking through, stood around the hills surrounding Masukedani Valley, and as they each let fly the conquering arrows of the living Kototama to the limit of their strength, even the mighty Magatsumi could not endure, showing desperate strength with Koko as their last resort; the hundreds and thousands of Magakami turned into clouds and mist, rocks and fireballs, swirling madly back and forth, left and right, attacking the vicinity of the Itsuhashiragami time and time again as danger approached moment by moment, until the Kami Tamayamahiko, feeling that this was truly the end, gathered strength in his Seikatanden,

'A O U E I'

and as he chanted this over and over again, in the grove of Kuwa trees near the small Sasakehara at the foot of the mountain, the three Hikome, who had been waiting with bowstrings drawn (Tegusune), turned toward the steed they had ridden in upon and chanted with all their might,

'Ta To Tsu Te Chi

Ha Ho Fu He Hi'

whereupon the horse instantly sprouted great wings and transformed into a gigantic eagle, and the three Hikome, thinking this truly to be a divine bestowal, straddled the back of the eagle-horse, soaring high up into midair straight toward the stronghold of the Magakami in Masukedani Valley; and when they attacked and fought with the momentum of a falling boulder from the heavens, striking the heads of the dragon Kami with the beaks of the great eagles or wounding their sword-skins with the spirit of fierce valor, even the mighty Magatsumi could not withstand them, and the mountain streams instantly turned into rivers of blood, the shadows of the Magakami being purified without a trace left behind. Seeing this great victory, the three Hikome soared through midair as they were, flying straight back in a single bound to the fountain grove where Mitashiro-no-Kami was stationed, reporting their mission and receiving the words of praise and admiration from Mitashiro-no-Kami.

Now, as the five Hikogami advanced into the stronghold of Masukedani Valley, where the swarms of giant serpents had dwelt for eternity, chanting the living Kototama, the traces of panic and confusion were vividly visible, and numerous jewel-treasures scattered here and there, dazzling to the eyes; thus the five male Kami unanimously resolved in council to pick them all up as spoils of war and offer them to the Kami of Tanazokuhime, wrapping countless jewel-treasures—gold, agate, lapis lazuli, giant clam shells, platinum, diamonds, and the like—into five bundles, and raising shouts of victory, they turned back for the time being toward the fountain grove.

All the Amatsukami of the true word, being Kami born from the water and fire of various voices born from the Kototama of Su, shone with light throughout their entire bodies, existing as transparent entities just like crystal; thus, even without requiring decorative objects such as diamonds, gold, silver, pearls, or gems, their brilliance was wondrously supreme. In contrast to this, the Magakami, whose bodies were filled with turbidity, attached various jewel-treasures to their entire bodies to be wrapped in light, attempting to imitate the Kami of the true word. For example, the Kami of the true word are like peacocks, whereas the Magatsumi are like crows; just as a crow envies the beauty of the peacock's wings, picks up its fallen feathers, inserts them between its own wings, and boasts of that beauty to many other crows, the Magatsumi vied with one another to gather jewel-treasures and boast of their light to their peers, and therefore, the stronger the Magami, the more jewel-treasures they had attached to their bodies. By virtue of this Kototama warfare, even the sword-skinned dragon Kami, the gigantic serpents, and the hundred dragon Kami had no leisure to gather and wrap up the treasures they should have adorned themselves with, and flurriedly fled away to one side of the heavens, illuminated by the light of the sun, gradually perishing and disappearing—a most felicitous thing indeed.

However, the Kami of antiquity polished natural stones devoid of light to create the jewels of Izu-no-Osumaru, using them as necklaces, bracelets, or ornaments around their waists, yet they utterly disdained wearing anything that emitted light like diamonds upon their bodies. Why was this? Because since the divine bodies of the Kami are entirely composed of light, wrapping oneself in luminous jewel-treasures would serve as a reason indicating the weakness of the Kami's own light, which they abhorred as being looked down upon by other Kami. Even in the present world, setting aside those called noble ladies or young misses, for all women to vie with one another in yearning for the brilliance of diamonds, sparing no expense of gold, buying them in competition, decorating various parts of their bodies to boast of their luxury, beauty, and light—this is no different from a crow boasting by inserting the fallen feathers of a peacock between its own wings. To wrap one's entire body in diamonds of intense brilliance would be one thing, but to attach small lights to a single localized part and boast of it is truly an act of baseness that exposes a contemptible mentality.

原文（近代文語）

茲に五柱の男神は、谷間の嶮を千辛万苦を重ねつつ辛うじて突破し、魔棲ケ谷を囲める丘の廻りに佇み給ひて、各自力限りに生言霊の征矢を間断なく放ち給ひければ、遉の曲津見も居耐らず此処を先途と必死の力を現はし、百千の邪神は雲霧となり岩となり火の玉となり、前後左右に狂ひ廻り、幾度となく五柱神の身辺を襲ひ危険刻々に迫りければ、霊山比古の神はもはやこれ迄なりと臍下丹田に力を籠め、

『ア　オ　ウ　エ　イ』

と繰り返し繰り返し宣らせ給ひければ、山麓の小笹ケ原の傍なる楠の森に、手具脛ひいて待ち給ひける三柱の比女神は、わが乗り来りし駿馬に向ひ、

『タ　ト　ツ　テ　チ

ハ　ホ　フ　ヘ　ヒ』

と力限りに言霊を宣り給ひけるにぞ、駒は忽ち大なる翼を生し、長大なる鷲と化しけるにぞ、三柱比女神はこれぞ全く神の賜なりと、鷲馬の背に跨り、一目散に魔棲ケ谷の邪神の巣窟さして中空高く翔りつき給ひ、天上より大なる鷲の嘴もて、竜神の頭を啄き、或は太刀膚を傷り、獅子奮迅の勢をもて挑み戦ひ給へば、遉の曲津見も敵し得ず、谷川は忽ち血の川となりて邪神の影は跡もなく清まりける。この大勝利を見るよりも、三柱の比女神は、其儘中空を翔り、御樋代神の屯し給ふ泉の森をさして、一目散に復命申し給ひ、御樋代神の感賞の言葉を頂き給ひける。

扨て五柱の比古神は、大蛇の群の永久に棲みし魔棲ケ谷の巣窟に、生言霊を宣りつつ進み給へば、周章狼狽のあとありありと見えて、数多の宝玉は彼方此方に飛び散りて、目も眩ゆきばかりなりければ、五柱の男神は戦利品として悉く拾ひ帰り、田族比女の神に奉らむと評議一決し、金銀、瑪瑙、瑠璃、硨磲、白金、金剛石なぞ、数限りなき宝玉を五つの苞に包み、勝鬨あげて一先づ泉の森に引き返し給ふ事とはなりぬ。

総て真言の天津神はスの言霊より生れたるさまざまの声の水火より生れませる神にましませば、全身悉く光に輝き、恰も水晶の如く透明体にましませば、ダイヤモンドまたは金銀珠玉の装飾物を要せずとも其光彩妙にましましにけり。之に反して曲神は、身体曇りに満ちぬれば種々の宝玉を全身に附着して光に包まれ、真言の神を真似むとするものなり。例へば真言の神は孔雀の如く曲津神は烏の如し、烏は孔雀の翼の美しきを羨みて、其落ちし羽根を拾ひ吾翼の間に挿み置きて数多の烏に其美しさを誇るが如く、曲津神は競ひて宝玉を集め、其輩に対して光を誇るものなれば、曲神の強きもの程数多の宝玉を身に附着し居りしものなり。此度の言霊戦によりて太刀膚の竜神も、長大身大蛇も、百の竜神も、装ふべき宝を取り纒むる暇もあらず、倉皇として天の一方に逃げ去り、天日の光に照らされて、次第々々に亡び失せけるこそ目出度き限りなりけれ。

併し乍ら太古の神々は、光なき天然の石を琢磨きて五百津御須麻琉の珠をつくり首飾、腕飾又は腰の辺りの飾となし給ひしかども、決して金剛石の如き光を放つものを身に帯ぶることを卑しめ給ひしものなり。何故なれば、神の御身体はすべて光にましませば、光の宝玉を身に纒ふ時は神自身の光の弱きを示す理由となりて、他の神々に卑しめらるるを忌み給ひたればなり。今の世にも貴婦人とか称するもの、令嬢とか言へるものはさておき、すべての婦人等が競ひてダイヤモンドの光に憧憬れ、千金を惜しまず競ひ購ひ、身体の各部に飾りつけて其豪奢を誇り、美を誇り、光を誇れるは、恰も烏が孔雀の落羽根を吾翼の間にさして誇れるのと何の選ぶところなかるべし。全身を光強き金剛石につつむなればまだしも、唯一局部に小さき光を附着して誇るが如きは、実に卑劣なる心性を暴露せる卑しき業と言ふべし。

§ 2

If one is filled with the virtue of Aizen and added with the light of Shinshin, even without attaching jewels to the body, manifold lights surge throughout the entire body, and unconsciously one comes to be respected.

While gazing upon the dull light of various jewels inlaid about the fingers or necks of women and such, Gojin is constantly prompted to nausea with the thought of how shallow and vulgar they are, and is made to feel the ugliness of those people all the more.

The Itsunohashira completely exterminated the ugly Kami of Magumokayadani from the root, and unable to contain their joy, while looking down upon the dwelling place of Magatsumi, which constantly vomited black smoke and troubled the Kuni, they stood upon a slightly elevated hill, singing Miuta and dancing in a frenzy.

Their Miuta:

Ahareahare, the Yomo Kunihara has cleared up!

On the upper reaches of the Ōtani River, where the southern side of Mt. Shirahumagatake has gathered a hundred valleys and a thousand valleys,

lurking in hiding, the violent and raving hideout of the ugly Magakami,

the Mototsuba that clouded the world,

the ugly mountain that raised clouds,

the Mototsuboride, which caused fog to well up and hindered the growth of all things while raging, has perished.

From today onward, on the island roots of Myriad Miles,

may both the black clouds of the ugly raging and the welling up of the cold fog disappear without a trace,

deep within the azure high-distant skies,

the light of Amatsuhi shines brightly,

and the Kami of Tsukiyomi stays brilliant light in the cloud-wells,

raining down the dew of blessings upon the earth.

May He protect the lives of all things for a thousand years and eight thousand years,

and this divine Kuni shall forever have flowers blooming and bearing grain,

becoming a divine age where crops grow abundant and cattle and horses wax fat day by day to flourish.

This Kunihara is still youthful, and there are not yet even the shadows of the Kunitsukami.

The tribe of frogs and rats till the fields and raise crops, preserving their lives,

thus resting forever and ever along with the moon and sun.

Ah, how truly wondrous it is, Kami, Kami!

Blessing with the living Kototama, Mitsuhashira Himenomiko swiftly crossed over upon the backs of the eagle-horses,

how valiant it was that they came soaring high across the great sky to assist us in the battle where we suffered!

Though we desire not the hundred treasures hidden away by the Kami of Magatsu,

we shall gather them as a token and wrapping of today's victory shout,

pile them high upon the backs of the steed, and offer them before the presence of Mitishironokami—how brave it is!

Since ancient times when Heaven and Earth were first created, how noble such a precedent is!

We who served the divine works of Kami's reliance with utmost sincerity and detailed care shall be transmitted for a thousand years and eight thousand years,

becoming the talk of the world.

When I think of it, I am joyful and valiant;

when I think of it, I am filled with awe—it is the light of the living Kototama of the Lord Kami,

it is the precious power of fire and water.

O heavenly messenger of the high-distant sky, let this be the spoken word of the matter.

The night of black-disguised wings draws near, come now, let us descend and return as far as the forest of Izumi.

As the Itsunohashira made numerous jewels be borne upon the backs as spoils of war, while chanting Kotoshigoto at the perilous places where the rocks of Mutsuishimura stood in rows, and at length arrived at the Kusunoki forest of Sasagahara at the foot of the mountain, the five divine steeds, waiting anxiously for the return of their lord, stood aligned side by side under the trees.

Reizanbuhiko-no-kami, seeing this, recites a Miuta:

Our horses wait valiantly and rightly,

did it even reach the ears of the living Kototama?

Having driven away the raging Kami of Mt. Shirahumagatake, we raised a shout of victory and have come back;

my core-liver heart is cleared, and surely my soul has surged forth, turning into a fleet steed.

In the joy of reporting our restored command accurately, even this twilight is bright in heart.

Yasamunebuhiko-no-kami recites a Miuta:

The Magatsumi that blocked the path where I went vanished like smoke, and today's shout of victory—

by the power of Himenomiko who came soaring through mid-air, Magatsu was broken without difficulty.

From today onward, the clouds rising up to Mt. Shirahumagatake shall all reflect in crimson.

May even the grass and trees of the youthful earth and youthful Kunihara flourish luxuriantly, making today their limit.

Thinking of the fortune of my own body that served such valiant and righteous divine work,

Magumokayadani of Mt. Shirahumagatake, where black clouds rose out of season, is clearing up completely.

How fascinating, having smashed the fortress of Magatsu, tomorrow I shall report my restored command before the august presence.

Michimichibuhiko-no-kami recites a Miuta:

§ 3

'Stepping and crushing roots and rocks, as we climb upward, How perilous indeed is the path of the valley! Magatsumi has become a goddess and upon my path Seeks to obstruct me, though shallow-minded. The Itsuhashira, uniting water and fire, proclaim on high, And at the living Kototama, Maga clamors aloud. Becoming rocks, becoming balls of fire, in manifold ways, Exhausting their strength, they came forth to shoot and oppose us. No sooner do I see it as perilous than Tamayamahikogami, Concentrating water and fire, proclaims the Kototama. Hardly has the Kototama ended when Himegamis Come flying, straddling eagles and horses. Let us hand down in speech to later ages of this day of ours, The wondrous Kamuwaza of the living Kototama.'

The Kami Masamichihiko chants a Miuta.

'Even the Kamuwaza that weighed upon the Murakimo heart, Without hardship has concluded and the skies have cleared completely. The Amatsu sun tilts toward Hakubagatake, And a great shadow has come casting forth. The mountain shades fall sideways and from the Misora, From the earth, darkness seems to press close upon us. When I look back, how valiant I am with the Momogami, Having united water and fire to drive back Maga. Upon the myriad-riled island troubled by Kurokumo and fog, Tenchi has grown purely and brightly clear.'

The Kami Kumokahahiko chants a Miuta.

'The achievements of the Momogami, for a thousand ages and ten thousand generations, Shall shine brilliantly, even as a tale to be told. Come now, let us whip our steeds and across the vast plain, Hurry back as far as the grove of Izumi.'

Hereupon the Kamis straddled the backs of their steeds, and urging them on side by side along the twilight field road, they hastened toward the grove of Izumi.
