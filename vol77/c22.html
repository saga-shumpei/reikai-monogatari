Chapter 22: The Episode of 歓 Voice 満天 (Part I) [1954]

歓声満天（一）〔1954〕

§ 1

The eleven pillars of Kamigami, who had returned in triumph to the sanctuary of Marigaooka, desiring to hold a banquet out of the abundance of their joy, sent forth swift messengers to all living creatures in the boundless plains of Mari; and as they waited for the appointed day to come, all living creatures throughout the lands spanning eight thousand hōri vied with one another in rushing forward to gather like clouds and mists, and the voices singing and celebrating the triumph with one accord were such that even Tenchi seemed about to crumble.

Millions upon millions of horses, cattle, sheep, mice, and frogs vied with one another to surround the sanctuary of Marigaooka ten and twentyfold, and there being not even enough space to stick an awl was truly an unprecedented, momentous celebration.

Hereupon Takarahime no Kami ascended to the newly completed Takadono, gazed afar at the crowds rejoicing and making merry, and with her heart overflowing with joy, she chanted a miuta.

All the living creatures gathered together followed the miuta of Mihishirogami by clapping their hands, stamping their feet, and swaying their hips strangely as they danced wildly with great valor.

The miuta of Takarahime no Kami.

The dark clouds of the ugly fiends / That strayed into the distant, heavenly august sky / Have blown away and scattered, / The heavenly sunlight shines serene and clear, / The boat of Tsukiyomi crossing the august sky / Grows ever chill and bright, while all / The stars gleam brilliantly in the firmament.

The wind of Shinato blows gently too, / Caressing the spring of Tokoyo, / And grasses and trees in their hundreds and thousands / Smile upon this spring as a paradise of a thousand years, / Abundant, gentle, and fragrant, / Scattering their sweet scents to the four quarters.

Mount Ushogamine soaring into the clouds, / And Mount Hakubagatake high in the august sky, / With waters and fires shining clear and pure, / Entwine and girdle themselves in purple hues of cloud.

As one gazes out upon the vast seas of Mari, / It is as though one is celebrating and praising / The auspiciousness of the eternal spring of Tokoyo.

The twittering of hundreds and thousands of birds / Resembles the voice of Kalavinka, / A tone clear and pure even to hear.

The true cranes flying high in the august sky / Align their wings in purity, / And resting their wings above the forest of the sanctuary of Mari, / Sing for a thousand ages.

This spring of glorious blossoms / Attracts white and yellow butterflies; / Pine crickets, bell crickets, and grasshoppers, / Though autumn has not yet come, gather close together in this garden / To sing clearly of ten thousand ages—how auspicious it is!

At the foot of Mount Hakubagatake / White clouds play, while the deep crimson cloud color / That shrouds the peak of Mount Ushogamine / Is so bright one might almost doubt / Whether it is the resting place of the heavenly sunlight.

The horses shake their manes, / Toss their tails to right and left, / And beat back the ugly crooked fiends.

The joy of this day shall be handed down / Through eternity to become a model / For the gods of future ages.

The true cattle with black hair like nukaboshi / Toss their two horns on high, / To the right, to the left, before and behind, / Lifting their front thick legs straight up, / Standing erect and dancing wildly / Like human hands—how fascinating!

The sheep dance with valor, while the white hares / Curl themselves into the shape of the moon, / Transforming into balls to roll and tumble / And dance in all four directions.

The mice dance bravely, and the hundreds of frogs / Clap their palms and dance in a frenzy.

When one gazes at the blue firmament, / Kites, crows, and falcons alike / Align their wings and, drawing and erasing rings of the moon, / Sing and dance.

Ah, Yimakamigami! / Are the islands of Mari spanning eight thousand hōri / Truly the heavenly realm where the Lord God eternally dwells? / Could even the solemnity of the palace of Shiwei / Surpass the bustle of this good day? Yimakamigami!

Henceforth, we must transfer and plant countless Kunitsukami, / Plow and firm up the true fertile land of Wakakunihara minutely and thoroughly, / Plant the seeds of trees and grasses, / Sow such kinds as rice, barley, beans, millet, and panicum until the land is packed full, / And making the peaceful land tranquil, / Decree every living creature to dwell and live in the joy of the heavenly paradise / As the eternal divine nation.

Only, as for myself, being Mihishirogami, / I deem it a single regret of this joyous day's happiness / That I have not yet brought forth the Kunitamanokami / Who should descend as the ruler of the land.

Ah, Yimakamigami! / Awaiting the time of the divine descent, / Waiting upon the august progress of Taigen-Atsuonokami, / We shall wash our hearts, purify our souls, / Maintain the purity of the sun and moon shining in the august sky, / And await one another.

Ah, Yimakamigami! / We humbly and reverently pray, / We respectfully and earnestly beseech / Before the august presence of the Lord Great Deity / Who dwells in the distant heavenly high palace, / Together with the countless awe-inspiring deities whom He commands, / To protect and guard the island roots of Mari for eternity.

§ 2

The Kami Yamatohime chants a divine miuta.

Aha, aha, how auspicious is today, the day of birth!

When all living creatures gather here from far and near,

Awe-inspiring is the august miizu of our lord; throughout the land,

All lift up their voices in the august presence to celebrate and offer kotoba.

Upon the summit of Mount Hakubagatake, piercing the clouds,

Purple clouds trail, and the heavenly sun shines bright.

The light of the midday moon ascends into the eastern sky,

Shining and glowing white as it rises.

Do the moon and sun alike celebrate today's joyful event?

Side by side, they have ascended.

Such a day of celebration, unheard of even in the age of the gods of old Chihayabu,

Fills the land with words of rejoicing.

Though it is called a young land of earth, to see it thus,

With so many living creatures, is truly joyful.

Even the frogs, coming forth into the fields to till the tahata,

Have gathered in this iminiha to dance.

High and low alike, joining hearts, make this a banquet

To celebrate the day of the destruction of Magakami.

Mountains and fields alike are all universally revitalized,

Illuminating the dewdrops of inochi, without end.

Floating upon the boundless unabara of myriad miles,

The prosperity of this young land knows no end.

I, a goddess, also stood forth upon the marching path against Magakami,

And have met in today's joyful banquet.

The Kami Chikaihime chants a divine miuta.

Filled to overflowing with boundless joy,

I know not where my hands dance nor where my feet tread.

Both moon and sun lavishly cast forth their light,

Illuminating the dew on the myriad grasses.

All things that live and breathe upon this island

Fail not to celebrate this auspicious day.

The young land, which the true crane peacefully ruled for eternity,

Has now become the age of the gods of the Mihishiro Kami.

The red-crested crane shall adorn the ever-green pine of a thousand years and sing of a thousand ages.

If it be so, you must create the Kamunagara Kami

And appoint them as the rulers of the land.

Does the softly blowing wind also celebrate, emitting a clear

Tone of sound as it passes through the forest?

The Mihishiro Kami ascends to the lofty hall,

And the voice of the miuta sung is bright and clear.

Through the brightly polished kototama of our lord,

All things are revived.

If we look up, the sea of the August Sky is boundless,

Growing ever deeper and bluer.

Crossing the blue unabara of the great sky,

The boat of Tsukiyomi cuts through the waves and advances.

When we look upon the fish-scale clouds drifting in the great sky,

They are precisely like the waves of the sea.

The Kami Yumusubihime chants a divine miuta.

Since the opening of heaven and earth, like this,

Such an auspicious precedent has never been heard.

Magakami, which vexed and afflicted all things day and night,

Has perished today—how auspicious it is!

Far and wide, around the edges of the distant hills of myriad miles,

The voices of joy gatherers from far and near.

What sounded until yesterday like voices of lamentation

Has surely become the wind softly blowing through.

Voices of joy fill heaven and earth,

Narrating the prosperity of the land.

Through the achievements of the Mihishiro Kami, the young land of the

Myriad-mile kunihara has revived.

All things that live and breathe upon this island

Sing of the achievements of the Mihishiro Kami.

Our lord, raising his noble body,

Has set forth upon the marching path against Magakami.

Encouraged by the brave and mighty valor of our lord,

I, too, a goddess, have turned toward the marching path.

In the august light of the living kototama of our lord,

We have driven away and expelled the army of Magakami.

From today onward, auspicious events shall multiply,

And the myriad-mile kunihara shall prosper without end.

From today for the very first time, horses and cattle shall drink fresh

Water and eat fresh grass.

Even birds, beasts, and tiny insects shall be completely clear and pure,

Reviving through water and fire for ten thousand generations.

Serving the Mihishiro Kami, I now

Behold this joy right before my eyes.

Just as they chanted thus, the joyful voices of the multitude rose higher moment by moment,

Displaying a scene that made one think the heaven and earth of the island roots of myriad miles might be overturned—truly an auspicious sight.

This was the evening of December 16, the eighth year of Shōwa, when the dictation of Chapter Twenty-Two of this volume was concluded. Cold rain fell in torrents upon the spacious garden of the Osaka Branch, and unseasonable thunder rumbled across the midnight sky at two o'clock, presenting a scene so fierce that one might have suspected a great earthquake had broken out.

Thus records the dictater.

かく歌ひ給ふ折しも、群衆の歓ぎ喜ぶ声は刻々に高まり、万里の島根の天地は覆へらむかと思ふばかりの有様を現出したるこそ目出度けれ。

本巻第二十二章を口述し終りたる昭和八年十二月十六日の夕刻なりき。冷雨は大阪分院の広庭に沛然として臻り、時ならぬ雷鳴は深夜の二時轟き渡りて、大地震の勃発せしかと疑ふばかり凄まじき光景を現じたりける。
