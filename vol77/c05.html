Chapter 5: Kotodama 生 Island [1937]

言霊生島〔1937〕

§ 1

Born as Mihishirogami, Asakahimegami as Kamutsukasa directed the islands of Magami, opening up the divine land of Sanu, leaving Amenakahikogami and Sanuhiko behind, she proceeded accompanied by four deities while pushing aside the waves of Unabara.

In Misora, Amatsu shines across, while the shadow of Hiruzuki glows white, right in the middle floating upon the waves, proclaiming Ikukototama, how awe-inspiring it is as she proceeds.

Now, the sea of fog, flowing down from Takachihoyama as the great current of the eastern river, and gushing down from Takateruyama as the pure current of the western moon river, meeting where they pour into the great ocean,

It is called the sea of ten thousand miles, with numerous islands standing like a game of Go, where mists rise and clouds seethe, fitting indeed as the dwelling place of Magatsumi.

The divine boat upon which Asakahime no Kami rides beats the drum of waves against its gunwales, slipping through the islands to the right and left without even oars or poles, and the island of Sanu, measuring over a hundredri in circumference, has unconsciously faded from the horizon.

Asakahime no Kami looks back, gazes up at Misora, and chants a divine poem.

Asakahime no Kami's divine poem: 'When I look up, the mountains of the land of food of Sanu have faded far away from my field of vision. Amenakahiko Sanuhiko must now be sighing, thinking of the boat in which I go. Though I too feel it hard to leave behind, caught up in the press of divine work, O hundred wings traversing the sky, if you have hearts, convey my heart to the bedrock of Sanu. Floating upon the sea filled with waves eight fathoms deep, I think of the island of Sanu. Mount Sakae and the bedrock of Sanu have become for me a sanctuary hard to forget. Across the vast sky without a single cloud-wisp, as the wings of the eagle flutter, cloud and fog clearing purely while a hundred birds stand gazing at the shadow of sun and moon, the wings of a hundred birds all shine, glittering as they are reflected in the waves. Today, crossing this sea without even a single rough wave, my heart is serene.' 'The land in the west where Akitsuwo no Kami dwells is far away as I float upon the boat. Crossing this sea southeastward, I shall avoid the flow of the moon's great river. Though the water of the moon's river gushing down from Takateruyama pours torrential into this sea, the shadow of a hundred birds taking to the sky is reflected on the water's bottom like swimming fish. The fish swarming and dwelling on the water's bottom must have revived in the light of the heavenly sun. On the moonlit night, the hundred fish of the sea bottom will come floating out, doubly bold.'

Hatsuhikohiko no Kami chants a divine poem.

Hatsuhikohiko no Kami's divine poem: 'Serving in the retinue of Asakahimegami, I have seen such wondrous divine work. The island of Sanu created by Hikohime no Kami fades afar in shadow as purple clouds trail over it. From today onward, the bedrock of Sanu too, living and living, shall trail purple clouds and become a heavenly land. How peaceful is the boat proceeding, rocked leisurely upon the waves. The water and fire of heaven and earth are completely purified, and the breath of living life is refreshing. We are deities who, breathing the pure water and fire, should dwell eternally in the heavenly realm. If the water and fire of heaven and earth grow cloudy, there is not even food to sustain the life of Amatsukami. From today onward, blowing away the cloud and fog of this infantile land, I shall purify water and fire. Floating a boat upon this sea where water and fire are pure, I shall proceed in the retinue of Akitsuwo no Kami. In the midst of the calm great ocean, I joyfully converse with Hikohimegami.' 'Hikohimegami's divine water and fire are entirely light; I, of dark heart, am suffering. Serving Mihishirogami morning and evening, I think to purify the water and fire of Kototama. A hundred birds crossing from island bedrock to island also have voices growing clear in the pure wind. It is a joy to hear the voices of small birds in a sea where the blowing wind is exceedingly pure. Looking out, Takachihoyama peeps stealthily, covered in purple clouds above the clouds. Looking up at the eastern sky, the mountain of Takateru faintly reveals its shadow. How vast is this sea crossing between mountain and mountain. This sea, having gathered the eastern river and the moon's great river, has grown vast indeed. Remembering how I shall serve in the retinue no matter where, it felt joyful. Floating easily upon the Iwakusubune, I serve in the creation of the land of the Shibi heavenly realm.'

§ 2

Both heaven and earth and the wind are purified, and this is the journey of Kuniu to solidify the lands of Kamikuni. To give birth to the kami of Kunitama, Asakahimegami sets forth with a brave heart. Truly, the most precious thing in Kamikuni is the divine work of purely giving birth to Kunitama. It is indeed the birth of kami for the sake of solidifying the foundation even unto the ages of ikumannen.

Tatsuyohime no Kami chants a miuta.

In all directions, morning and evening, the clouds and mists clear away completely, matching the heart of Tatsuyohime no Kami. Serving as a sobame to the kami of Mihishiro, I cross the wide and pure Unabara, leaving behind the sacred shrine of Sakakiyama. Now I am filled with joy, serving publicly. Serving the kami of Mihishiro, morning and evening, living while smiling and prospering, living on and on without knowing destruction, how many blessings there are on today's journey in Kamikuni! The island of demons rose up in the fierceness of Magatsumi, spewing out turbid kototama. The kototama proclaimed by Magatsumi, whose eyes, mouth, and nose are not even complete, are like thunder. Even the thunderous voices that shake Ametsuchi came to a stop before the ikukototama. The figure of Magakami suddenly turns into rock, giving birth to the island kene of Kakihatokiha. It seems that Magakami, unknowingly and unconsciously for my sake, is serving in the miwaza of the kami.

If the divine words of Asakahimegami were not present, this island of demons would surely not prosper. The island of demons instantly transforms into a divine island through ikukototama, with water and fire prospering. In the divine light of Asakahime's ikukototama, the clouds and mists of the four directions clear away without a trace. Thinking that I serve in the retinue of Himegami, whose kototama is thus pure, fills me with joy. Even the Magatsumi that obstructs my path will perish in the divine water and fire of Asakahime no Kami. Because such a brave, strong, and beautiful kimi of mine is seated here, my heart is at ease. Looking up, the mountain hazy in the distant Unabara is surely none other than Shiroumadake. High on its peak, white snow piles up, an island that blows down cold wind eternally. Looking up from above the ridge of Shiroumadake, black smoke spews forth. Shiroumadake enters my eyes, and the island of Sano must be growing ever more distant now.

Gradually the sun tilts, yet the shimane of Shiroumadake is distant, while the waves dance.

Ameharehime no Kami chants a miuta.

The vast sky clears as the true clear sky, and the sun tilts toward the high land and secluded mountain. The heavenly sun, emerging from the mountain of high radiance, draws close to the ridge of the high land and secluded mountain. Fan-tossed by the sea wind, rough waves begin to rise, shaking the Iwanokusafune from side to side. This wind is likely the ugly work plotted by Yaso Magatsumi, rocking our august boat. No matter how fiercely Yaso Magatsumi may rage, what are they against the journey of kototama? Magakami, fearing the light of kototama, raises the wind and opposes kimi.

Just as this song is chanted, the waves of the great Unabara rise higher moment by moment, almost swallowing the august boat. The boat drifts in the midst of the rough waves, tossed about like a leaf in the sea.

Asakahime no Kami calmly chants a miuta.

Magatsumi once again changes hands and alters tactics, attempting to obstruct the path we go. Though the waves of a thousand fathoms rage, what of it? With my kototama, I shall solidify them into rock.

Waves, oh waves, become rocks! Waves, oh waves, become an island! Kamikuni is a country aided by ikukototama, a country where ikukototama shines like the sun, a country blessed by ikukototama, a country that lives. Quickly become rocks, quickly become an island, without a moment of hesitation, solidify!

As this is chanted, the violently raging waves, untouched by the blowing wind, instantly transform into a steep, rocky mountain like the teeth of a saw, and the foaming ripples turn into fine sand, thus bringing forth a single living island—how awe-inspiring!

Kiritsuhikohiko no Kami, astonished, chants a miuta.

Now once more I am astonished at the wondrous efficacy of the kototama of Hime no Kami's divine words. I now perceive the principle that Kamikuni is the land of kototama and the land of water and fire. Having given birth to the island of Sano, and now once more bringing forth an island of rock, how wondrous is this efficacy! Name this island the island of waves, and if it was born from the water and fire of the Mihishiro Kami, proclaim this mountain to be Saw Mountain, since all its peaks are pointed.

Asakahime no Kami chanted a miuta:

'O islands born from the living kototama I proclaimed, / I shall name you Kototamaikushima; / Since the crests of the waves are jagged like a saw, / I shall also name you Nokogiriyama.'

Ubugamihiko no Kami was astonished once more and chanted a miuta:

'Aha, aha! The waves instantly became mountains, / And the seafoam instantly became masago; / How profound indeed are the water and fire of kototama, / I, Ubugamihiko, now perceive this with clear understanding; / Serving such a Himegami of august merits, / How my soul has swelled with awe.'

Tatsuyohime no Kami chanted a miuta:

'Descending from the distant Amatsutakamiya, / This must be the august aid of Unarizu no Kami; / Unarizu no Kami has visibly manifested / His pure august shadow in the misora; / To assist and protect the miwaza of Himegami, / Hidden in the shadows is Unarizu no Kami.'

Asakahime no Kami chanted a miuta:

'Protected by the miiki of Unarizu no Kami, / My kototama grows ever more crystal clear; / Though His misugata is not plainly seen, / How manifest are the virtues of Unarizu no Kami.'

Ameharehime no Kami chanted a miuta:

'Serving as a mitomo upon the unabara, / How wondrous it is to witness this divine miwaza; / In the unabara where both ame and tsuchi are clear and bright, / How moving are the Ihashima gushing forth; / Through the virtue of Unarizu no Kami, within the wadanaka, / Another Ikushima has now been brought forth.'

Thus the Kami, each chanting their miuta, turned the prows of their boats toward the direction of Mount Hakuba, hazily visible in the distant sky.
