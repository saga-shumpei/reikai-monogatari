Chapter 11: 望 Moon の Shadow [1943]

望月の影〔1943〕

§ 1

Mihishirogami Hitohashira, Takara no Hime no Kamutsukasa, descended upon the island roots of Banri, and having subdued the rampaging kami, forever dwelling hundreds of frogs and mouse kindreds rescuing, extending along the great river of Banri, at the scenery-wondrous Banrioka, determined their eternal dwelling place.

On the southern side of Hakubagatake, lurking and blowing scattering the evil vapor, all living things without exception damaging and destroying, with such malice the ten pillars of male and female kamitachi having caused to follow, and lurking in the valley of demon dwellings to cause the mischief-doing wicked kami to be pacified and harmonized, they set forth, crossing the plains of Senri upon the backs of fleet horses, and gradually the sky also grew dusk.

Arriving at the spring forest where the great camphor trees are luxuriant, here all the kamitachi, desiring to borrow a lodging of a single night's dew, made their horses descend, and in the moon-shining midnight forest shadows, each and every one composed poems and rested with awe.

Now these island roots of Banri, if the earth was yet young and the kokudo not yet completely solidified, any great rivers, small rivers, and pond waters were turbid and polluted, unfit for drinking; but now descending here into the spring forest, seeing the clear springs down to their bottoms, gushing forth unceasingly, the kamitachi rejoiced and took courage, their bravery hundredfold increased beyond usual. This forest was of an extent scarcely to be reached by the eye, with pure springs welling forth here and there, the ground one surface of fine sand, a sacred place refreshing even by night.

Here Takara no Hime no Kami composes and recites an august poem:

Beneath the shade of the evergreen camphor's great tree / The pure waters of jewel-like life gush forth / Having crossed and come to the island of Banri, like this / Such pure springs I had not seen before / The moonlight is intercepted by the camphor tree's twigs / Though no shadows are cast, O clear true-clear water! / Purifying myself in the shadowless jewel-spring / I will lodge the moon's light within my breast

Having thus caused the poem to be chanted, when she stood beside the pure spring welling forth amidst the sparse-treeed fine sand, the moon shone brightly and brilliantly upon the spring's surface.

Takara no Hime no Kami composes and recites an august poem:

Looking up I see Tsukiyomi's boat, looking down I see / Tsukiyomi's boat floating upon the spring / Gazing up at Akitsuo no Kami's august spirit / I gaze upon the moon in the spring's waves / The Lord God's divine words holding in awe, I now / Create the kokudo upon the island roots of Banri / Where does Akitsuo no Kami now abide? / Though the moon shines, there is no way to converse / Floating in the true-clear water the pure moonlight / Gazing up at it as my dear brother-spouse / The moss-grown, divine-aging evergreen trees / Are surely mementos of the age of the gods / From the very beginning when the Lord God was born / Already ten thousand years have passed away / The island of Banri where eightfold clouds rise and eightfold mists mingle / Behold, today for the first time I see the moon! / The dew resting upon every single leaf of the camphor tree's twigs / Illumining everywhere, the clear moonlight / Such a heart-pure eve / There has not yet been, upon the island of Banri / Crunching and treading the fine sand, the fleet horse's / Hoofbeats also, living kototama! / Such a brightly clear moonlit night / How regrettable it would be to spend it sleeping! / Tsukiyomi's shadow I have seen for the first time / The day to meet my dear spouse will also be near at hand / Subduing the Magatsumi lurking in Hakubagatake / I will peacefully guard this country's plain / Protected by the power of the ten pillars of kami / I ascend upon the expedition path of Magatsu / The white dew resting damply upon the leaves of the trees / Wondrous is the radiance beneath the moonlight / Looking up, the stars in the august sky are like fine sand / Shining upon my head / Come now, let us make the great camphor tree's lower shade / A lodging of a single night and rest

Momogamihiko no Kami composes and recites an august poem:

Serving the Mihirokami, grass-pillow / How delightful is this evening of travel! / The cloud-mist that deeply wrapped the four directions / Has cleared, and the moon has come forth in the august sky / The horse I rode also neighed with courage / Beneath the breeze-clarified moonlight / The clear springs where the moon floats here and there / Are surely a divine oracle to wash the soul! / My liver-root heart has become pure / The tree leaves whispering in the softly blowing wind / Upon the island of Banri, turbid and cloudy throughout / I knew not that such a sacred place existed / The sound of insects is also heard ever more clearly / Little birds return to their nests and whisper / The hundred birds also, in a night when the moon is clear for the first time / Rejoicing, sleepless, must be singing! / My heart also is courageous and my breast leaps / My two arms' throbbing does not cease / Heaven and earth alike clarifying, Tsukiyomi / Has illuminated our heads
