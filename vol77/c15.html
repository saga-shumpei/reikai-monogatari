Chapter 15: 笹 Plain の邂逅 [1947]

笹原の邂逅〔1947〕

§ 1

Tamayamahiko no Kami easily eluded the treacherous schemes of Magatsumi in the grassy plain of Ozasa and passed the night in safety, yet gradually, in the light of the Amatsu sun ascending to illuminate the eastern sky, breathed forth the breath of resuscitation.

At that very moment, the four deities—Mochimunehiko no Kami, Nahomichihiko no Kami, Masamichihiko no Kami, and Kumokahahiko no Kami—appeared and came riding into this place at leisure, holding their horse reins; dismounting lightly from their horses, Mochimunehiko no Kami chanted a divine poem.

Tamayamahiko no Kami, is it well that you are safe? / In the pitch-dark night of the hideous demons, advancing through the gloom, / As for me, my way forward was blocked, / And not being able to tell a hand's breadth, I lodged on the way, / Rising forth in my strength into the dawn's sky, / I urged my horse and have come hither.

Tamayamahiko no Kami chanted a responsive divine poem in reply.

I saw the long-awaited figures of the Yohashirahiko! / My soul-line has been resuscitated, / Passing the night in the Ozasa plain of deep darkness, / How the hideous Magatsu spoke with me! / The ugly females and Sagume, while illuminating the radiant jewel of the night, / Tried to lure me into the demon's cave, / Transforming their shapes and bodies into those of three female deities, / How gently they tempted me! / The dragon god flashed its eyes before me, / And feigned that it was the jewel of the night, / Whatever I might do, there was no way to advance, / Enveloped by the curtain of black clouds where a hand's breadth is indistinguishable, / Surrounded by their grassy-smelling breath, I feared for my fragile life-string. / From this time forth, assigning our posts, each and every one, / I think we should advance into the demon-haunted valley.

Mochimunehiko no Kami chanted a divine poem with astonishment.

I too, sheltered within a certain small grove, / While resting, saw the radiant jewel of the night, / Three female deities appeared to me as well, / And tempted me with the jewel of the night, / Thinking whatever happened that they were strange things, / I drove them away with my Kototama, / Though in the appearance of the three female deities, / I thought it slightly strange, / Be it as it may, or however it might be, / I had resolved in my heart to wait for the breaking of the night.

Nahomichihiko no Kami chanted a divine poem.

I too encountered the three female deities, / Who are the incarnations of the hideous Magatsu, / Since the fierceness of Magatsumi was intolerable, they urged me, / Saying, 'Come to my place,' / When I looked closely, their two ears moved, / So I realized truly they were incarnations of the wicked deity, / By restraining the water and fire of Kototama and blowing the wicked deity away, / They vanished and disappeared, / With various means and devices, / The wicked deities seek to block our path of conquest.

Masamichihiko no Kami chanted a divine poem.

Though the three female deities were not seen before me, / The radiant jewel of the night had fallen down upon the ground, / Illuminating the darkness around the place where I am going, / The radiant jewel of the night shone brilliantly, / Regarding it with suspicion and not touching it with my hand, but taking up my whip, / When I struck it, the radiant jewel of the night moved, / What seemed to be a true jewel illuminating the dark night, / Was proven to be none other than the eye of a dragon, / Making a sound of great commotion, / The radiant jewel of the night shattered into a thousand pieces, / As the eye of the dragon god broke and shattered, / Did it escape as a one-eyed dragon?

Kumokahahiko no Kami chanted a divine poem.

In the wild plain, I too grew belated as twilight fell, / And met with a gentle woman, / All the Hiko deities who went before me, she declared, / Would perish entirely, so do not advance, / The strange goddess, casting amorous glances, / Sprang upon the neck of my horse, / When I applied the whip to the horse, it instantly leaped up, / And casting off the goddess, galloped away, / Coming hither, I learned for the first time in a stalwart form, / That Hiko no Kami was safe and unharmed, / Now then, as the light of the Amatsu sun ascends, / Let us assign our posts and set forth upon our path of conquest, / The hideous wicked plots of the wicked deity, / Must be waiting in weary anticipation with minute preparations, / Be that as it may, prior to today's departure, / Shall we proclaim the divine words upon this Sasahara plain?

Tamayamahiko no Kami and the other various deities expressed their sympathy with the proposal of Kumokahahiko no Kami, raised their Kototama so loud as to split even heaven and earth, and chanted the precious divine words. When Tamayamahiko no Kami performed ritual purification in the clear waters of Hosoyagawa flowing through the Ozasakehara, the four deities, not to be outdone, also performed the vigorous ritual purification, and each chanted their respective divine poems of departure.

霊山比古の神は、小笹の芝生に曲津見の計略も難なく逃れて一夜を明し給ひけるが、漸く東の空を照して昇らせ給ふ天津日の光に、蘇生の息を吐き給ひける。

折しも保宗比古の神、直道比古の神、正道比古の神、雲川比古の神の四柱は、この場に悠々と駒の手綱をかいくりながら現はれ来り、駒をひらりと飛び下り、保宗比古の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『霊山比古の神は事無くおはせしか 夜半を進みし醜の常闇に 吾こそは道の行手を塞がれて 咫尺弁ぜず途中に宿りし 東雲の空を力に立ち出でて 駒を急がせここに来つるも』

霊山比古の神は答の御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『待ち待ちし四柱比古の姿見つ わが魂線は蘇りたり 常闇の小笹ケ原に夜をこめて 醜の曲津と言問ひしはや 醜女探女も夜光の玉を照らしつつ 吾を魔窟に誘はむとせし 三柱の比女神の姿と体を変へて やさしく吾を誘ひしはや 竜神は眼を光らし吾前に 夜光の玉と偽りにける いかにして進まむ由もなかりけり 咫尺弁ぜぬ黒雲の幕に 青臭き息に囲まれ玉の緒の 生きの生命を危ぶみにけり これよりは部署を定めて各も各も 魔棲ケ谷に進まむと思ふ』

保宗比古の神は驚きながら御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『吾も亦とある小さき森蔭に やすらひにつつ夜光の玉見し 三柱の比女神吾にも現はれて 夜光の玉に誘ひにけり 如何にしても怪しきものと思ひしゆ 吾言霊に逐ひやりにけり 三柱の比女神等の面ざしに 似たれど少しは怪しと思へり 兎にもあれ角にもあれや夜の明くるを 待たむと心定めたりしよ』

直道比古の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『吾も亦醜の曲津の化身なる 三柱比女の神に逢ひける 曲津見の猛び忌々しければ吾許に 来れと彼等は誘ひにけり よく見れば二つの耳は動きたれば 正しく曲神の化身と悟りき 言霊の水火をこらして曲神を 伊吹き払へば消え失せにけり 色々と手段を持ちて曲神は 吾等が征途を防がむとすも』

正道比古の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『吾前に三柱比女は見えねども 夜光の玉の地に落ちゐたるよ 吾伊行くあたりの闇を射照らして 夜光の玉はかがやきにけり 怪しみて吾手にふれず鞭もちて 打てば夜光の玉は動けり 闇の夜を照らす真玉と見えけるは 正しく竜の眼なりけむ 大いなる騒ぎの音を立てながら 夜光の玉は千々に砕けぬ 竜神の眼は砕け破れつつ 独眼竜となりて逃げしか』

雲川比古の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『荒野ケ原に吾も漸く黄昏れて やさしき女に出会ひけるかも 先に立たす比古神等は悉く 滅び給へば進ますなと宣りし 怪しかる女神は秋波をよせにつつ 吾駒の首に飛びつきにけり 駒に鞭あつれば忽ちをどり上り 女神を捨てて駆け去りにけり ここに来て始めて知りぬ比古神の 事なく在せしを雄々しき姿に いざさらば天津日の光昇りませば 部署を定めて征途に上らむ 曲神の醜の奸計はこまやかに 手筈極めて待ちあぐむらむ 兎も角も今日の首途に先立ちて この笹原に神言宣らむか』

霊山比古の神其他の諸神は、雲川比古の神の提言に賛意を表し、天地も割るる許りの言霊をはり上げて、貴の神言を宣らせ給ひぬ。霊山比古の神は小笹ケ原を流るる細谷川の清水に禊し給へば、四柱の神も吾後れじと健びの禊を修し給ひ、各自首途の御歌詠ませ給ふ。

§ 2

The divine miuta of the Kami Tamayamahiko:

Aha, aha, having purified ourselves in the Hosotanigaha,

How purified indeed have our Kototama become!

That we had not realized until now the awe

Of the waza of purification, how foolish am I!

Now that I have performed the misogi and purified both my karatama and tamashihi,

There is nothing whatsoever to fear.

My soul, growing ever more brilliant, resounds aloud,

And the power of the Ikukototama is now completely fulfilled.

The vital inochi of the thread of jewels, bright and clear,

Grew ever purer and increased its radiance.

Shall we illuminate the soul of the Magami, blacker than the darkness of nubatama,

And raise the kachidoki?

With the boundless power of the Ikukototama,

How joyous is our setting forth upon our march today!

The hideous fortress of the Magami draws near as well;

Come, let us advance, illuminating it with the Kototama.

Though the Yasomagatsu hide deep within the valleys,

We shall make them manifest in the light of the Kototama.

This valley of the shinrin where the eagles dwell,

Shall we peacefully open up and advance.

The Kami Mochimunehiko chants a miuta:

Having performed the misogi, my wagami has become bright red;

Come, let us advance into the valley where the demons dwell.

The time for the Magami, which for made upon the island of eternity had obstructed us,

To perish has now truly come.

Thinking that the Magami, who rouse the clouds and summon mists and rage, shall perish,

Fills my heart with joy.

The hideous fortress, constructed with countless chibikiiha aligned side by side,

What is there to fear?

Hiding amidst the kurokumo and breathing forth jiyaki,

This surely is the end of the Yaso Magatsu.

The strict Kototama bestowed by the Lord God,

We shall purify in today's misogi and advance.

We shall breach the fortress of the demon-haunted valley,

Which has grown wild, veiling both moon and sun.

The Kami Nahomichihiko chants a miuta:

Raising kurokumo even to the summit of Mount Hakubakedake,

The Magatsu stand waiting in readiness.

In the hundred and thousand valleys of Mount Hakubakedake, surging forth kurokumo,

The Magatsu have blocked our path, Warera.

In the Ikukototama of a-o-u-e-i, do the raging and frantic

Dragons and great serpents fold up their inochi?

If they come in submissive obedience, I too

Shall rouse a heart of love and save them.

To purify the island of eternity where the Mihashiro no Kami descended

Is likewise our task, Warera.

The Goddess Tazukuhinome in the shade of the spring's grove

Shall surely guard our combat.

Though the takitsuse falling down the mountain stream is high,

The water is turbid with red without exception.

When we look upon the flow of the mountain stream, it is the turbidity of water and fire

In which the Magatsumi are concealed.

Where this water flows and where it soaks,

Trees and grasses do not grow, and grains do not ripen.

We shall shatter the hideous fortress of the Magami,

And make it a takitsuse of pure, clear water.

Nevertheless, this single stream of the Hosotanigaha

Has grown perfectly clear for the sake of the misogi.

Thinking it to be a brook created by the Lord God that we might perform misogi,

How venerable it is.

The Kami Masamichihiko chants a miuta:

Feeling as though I bear a heavy burden upon both shoulders,

Awe-struck by the divine words of the Kami, I advance.

Yet when evening falls, the wildness of the Magatsu must surely grow fierce;

In the broad daylight, let us fight well.

As if last night, the avatar of the Magatsu appears,

And because it is hateful how they lead us astray,

The Magami fear the broad daylight, and making the dead of night

Their own world, they rage and storm fiercely;

Yet when evening comes, pausing from combat without a moment's rest,

Let us chant the Ikukototama and brighten the darkness until dawn.

The Kami Kumocahiko chants a miuta:

Having turned into clouds and mist to block heaven and earth,

The Magatsumi are now on the verge of perishing.

Along the path trodden by the brave Kami of the five sons and three daughters,

There can be no means by which any Magatsu can obstruct us.

Come what may, we shall fight the hideous Magatsu;

While there is sunlight, victory must surely be ours.

To attack the Magatsu in the dead of night as they rage at their own wild pleasure

Can be of no avail.

The Magatsumi, fearing the light of broad daylight,

Turn into clouds and mist and envelop the land.

Just as these miuta are being chanted, the three Himegami bring their horses' bits abreast and appear leisurely upon the scene, and the Kami Yamatohime chants a miuta from horseback:

Were the five Hikogami present here?

I came hither worrying over the darkness of last night.

I myself, by the arrangement of the Mihashiro no Kami,

Set forth late upon this sacred journey.

Thinking that the Magami were imitating the forms of us three Himegami,

I tarried behind.

If we obey the words of the Mihashiro no Kami,

We hear that the Magatsu have changed their shapes into ours.

The Kami Tamayamahiko chants a miuta:

At the flawless plans of the Mihashiro no Kami,

I am nothing less than astonished and struck with awe.

The words chanted by the Kami Yamatohime

Possess something that presses upon my wagami with awe.

The Magatsumi transformed themselves into the three Himegami,

And devised a plan to lure me.

Nevertheless, my tamashihi whispered to me:

Do not let your guard down against the avatars of the Magami!

Obeying the whispering words of my soul,

I escaped the snare of the crooked stratagem.

§ 3

Chikahihime no Kami chanted this miuta:

'That I came lagging behind the five male Hikogamitachi / Was because I thought upon the wicked takumi of the crooked / Struck by the sharp mikoto of Takarahime / I too have been startled now at this late hour / The path by which I came grew faintly bright / And became a field where the moon hung / In the ozasahara and shibafu the five Itsuhashirakami dwell / Thus did the Kami of the mihishiro declare / Just as the mikotoba foretold, the five male Hikogami / Were waiting here in the ozasasahara / Since the protection of the Kami is thus mighty / What is there for the ugly Magami to fear?'

Yumusubihime no Kami chanted this miuta:

'The three Himegami came across the great field of night / Illuminated by the faint moon / Upon the great field of night where I came, the night has broken / And the steps of the horses have grown swift / In the age of the Kami where the kototama shines in heaven and aids us / How could the ugly Magami prosper? / Come now, let us all, the Momogamitachi, unite the kototama / Of iki and advance ever onward!'

Tamayamahiko no Kami chanted this miuta:

'Since the kamubashira of the gonansanjiyo have thus gathered / Let us press forward with speed / Yet, you the Momogamitachi, each and every one / Let us determine our bushiyo and march upward to attack!'

Hereupon, the Kami of the gonansanjiyo determined their respective bushiyo, and it came to pass that they advanced toward Mashikegatani, which stood soaring and majestic in the distant sky.
