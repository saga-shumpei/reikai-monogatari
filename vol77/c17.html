Chapter 17: Sword 槍の Rain [1949]

剣槍の雨〔1949〕

§ 1

Born and manifested by the Lord's Kototama, within the midst of Shibitenkai, floating upon the seas of Made, the island roots of Made were yet tender, and the land within was not yet settled; clouds and mists closed in on all four quarters, ceaseless wicked spirits rampaged, and with the dreadful ill of impairing and shattering the life of Subetenomono, born as the Mihishirogami, the divine pillar of the Hime of Takara, residing as the ruler of this land, desiring to protect Subetenomono, built a temporary Miaraka at the sacred place of Marigao, and obeying the Tohashiragami, morning and evening did she proclaim the Futonorito, yet what shall be done? The wicked mass of malignant energy born at the beginning of the country, the sword-blade-like hideous Tatsugami and Orochi and the like, rampaged with an air of absolute ownership, and the island roots of Made became a lonely world of Tokoyami.

The divine ruler, the Hime of Takara, desiring to eliminate this tragic state, finally followed the Tohashiragami and established her headquarters in the forest of Izumi. Setting up her main camp in Mashikedani on the southern side of Mt. Shirubake, she unleashed the arrows of Kototama upon the hideous Magatsuhi lurking within Mashikedani, illuminating them. We, the eight divine rulers, advancing today deep into the recesses of Mashikedani, this very day is a divine work unprecedented since the beginning of the opening of Heaven and Earth. Ah, true deities, may the Kototama of the Lord Ōkami, clearly manifesting the blessings of water and fire, completely pacify and harmonize the eighty Magatsuhi. May you sweep away the clouds and mists of this land without a single corner left, and to protect forever the life of all living things with a peaceful mind and peaceful country, grant unto my Kototama endless life and light.

The stream of the valley murmurs, echoing through the tree spirits, and the mountain wind blows wildly against our path. The eighty Magatsuhi, dismayed by today's Kototama warfare, panic-stricken as a result, variously changed their forms into all sorts of shapes, employing every kind of artifice, and desperately defending themselves. How delightful is this departure! Though the craft of the Magatsuhi be deep, though Mashikedani be dark, though the stream of the valley be muddy, though the perilous spots of the path be many, how could we fear? Advancing ever more through the power of the water and fire of Kototama, facing the fortress of Magatsuhi, with the light of the living Kototama, we shall fundamentally overthrow it and remove the evils of this world. Since we are the children of God and the palaces of God, having appeared by borrowing the water and fire of the living Kototama, all the Magatsuhi that create obstacles on the path we advance shall utterly vanish. Ah, how delightful, how valiant! Today's journey, entrusted by God, we pray with reverence before the presence, entreating that you protect us. We pray with reverence before the presence, entreating that you protect us.

Even as he sang thus, just at the moment when Hosomuhikohiko no Kami was ascending along the valley, by the divine power of Ryozanhiko no Kami, the rock villages, which were the incarnations of Magatsuhi solidified as boulders, rushed headlong down like shooting arrows toward the valley floor like rain and hail, falling before and behind, to the left and to the right. Resembling a hundred thunders roaring in unison, resembling a hundred wolves howling and raging in unison, the sparks flying as stone struck stone at the riverbed flashed like lightning, reaching a state of unnamable misery. Amidst the rain of flying rock gravel, there was a goddess crying out with a shrill scream. When Hosomuhikohiko no Kami turned his eyes toward the direction of the voice, to his utter astonishment, the Hime of Takara, who is the Mihishirogami, had encountered the hardship of nine deaths and one life, crushed beneath a giant boulder.

Hosomuhikohiko no Kami, thinking this a grave emergency and that even if he gambled his life and body he would rescue the person of the Mihishirogami, at the very moment he was about to leap down toward the valley, not knowing whose divine voice it might be, a single roar of "Wait!" echoed from the sky into his ears. Hosomuhikohiko no Kami, starting in surprise, thought that since the Mihishirogami dwelt in the headquarters of the Izumi forest and would not advance even a step, she had no reason to be in such a valley, and that it must be entirely a malicious trick of the wicked deities designed to torment them. Thereupon, his courage suddenly increased a hundredfold, and while gazing intently upon the rain of boulders falling ceaselessly like hail upon the valley floor, he composed and chanted his divine poem with perfect composure.

How delightful! This must be the utmost performance of the Magatsuhi in Mashikedani. What appeared to be the falling of a thousand-drawing boulder was the death agony of the hideous crew of wicked deities. By the Kototama proclaimed by Ryozanhiko no Kami, the Magatsuhi fell, turned into stone. If it be so, along this valley, most of the Magatsuhi visions seem to have perished. The living Kototama proclaimed by the three Hime goddesses makes the surroundings bright. If I strain my ears and listen to the sound of the waterfall rapids, the divine voices of the three deities are echoing. The water of this great valley, gathering the streams of a hundred valleys, is muddy. The mist surging up from the valley floor swells and swells, rising to take the shape of a dragon. The mist in the valley, which changes its shape so delightfully, gradually grows thin.

斯く歌ひつつ、保宗比古の神は谷間を伝うて登らせ給ふ折もあれ、霊山比古の神の神力に、巌と固まりし曲津の化身なる石村は、雨霰と谷底に向つて矢を射る如く急転直下し来り、前後左右に落下するさま、百雷の一時に轟く如く、百狼の一時に吠え猛るが如く、川底の石と石と相打ちて迸る火光は、恰も電光の閃けるが如くにして、其凄惨のさま、形容すべからざるに到りける。岩飛礫の雨の中に悲鳴をあげて号泣する女神あり。保宗比古の神は、声する方に眼を注がせ給へば、豈計らむや、御樋代神にまします田族比女の神は、巨巌に圧せられ九死一生の苦難に遇ひ給ふにぞありける。

保宗比古の神は、素破一大事、身命を賭しても御樋代神の御身を救ひ奉らむと、今や谷間に向つて飛び込まむとし給ひし刹那、何神の声とも知らず、空より一口「待て」との大喝一声耳に響きければ、保宗比古の神は、はつと気が付き、御樋代神は泉の森の本営におはしまして、一歩も進ませ給はざれば、かかる谷間におはす筈なし、全く醜神の吾等を苦しめむとする奸計なるべしと思ほすより、俄に勇気百倍し、谷底に雨霰と時じく落ちくだつ巌の雨を打ち見やりつつ、平然として御歌詠ませ給ふ。

§ 2

Just as He thus deigned to chant the divine poems, the sky suddenly gathered black clouds, plunging everything into utter darkness where even an inch ahead could not be discerned; thunder roared, lightning flashed, and a torrential downpour arrived. The wind from the foot of Hakuba blew away even the heavy, thousand-draw boulders like mere leaves, while a rain of spears and a rain of swords poured down incessantly and with extraordinary denseness upon the person of the Kami Mochimunehiko. That danger reached a pitch utterly beyond the power of language to express. The Magatsukami regarded this as the critical juncture, exerting their full strength to block the march of the Kami Mochimunehiko, and aiming to destroy Him, thus concentrating their desperate might right here.

The Kami Mochimunehiko, surrounded by the fierce evil vapors of the wicked kami, suffered from choked breathing and a suffocating chest; His head began to ache, the functioning of His hands and feet completely stopped, and His tongue—the heavenly spear of jewels which should have been used for the living Kototama—grew stiff with no way to help it. With His advance and retreat thus brought to an extremity, He had no other means left than to leave matters entirely to the will of the Magatsumi and await death.

When it came to such a pass, from beyond the forest of Izumi, a gigantic blaze made a roaring sound, shaking heaven and earth, and shining high above the head of the Kami Mochimunehiko, dancing wildly back and forth and right and left. Thereupon, the evil vapors in the valley vanished without a trace, the rain of boulders and the violent rain of swords and spears hid their shadows, and heaven and earth grew serene while the sunlight shone brilliantly and completely without a shade. Hereupon, for the first time, the living Kototama of the Kami Mochimunehiko gained freedom of movement, His body and mind suddenly refreshed, and He was wondrously revived into a great, courageous heart that would not yield to tens of thousands of enemies, how awe-inspiring it was.

How fascinating is the sport of the Magatsukami, commencing the theatrical performance of ugly Kototama, while causing the rain of swords, spears, and boulders to fall, startling the surroundings of My own body. I too, surrounded by the ugly Maga, was unable to move my body and suffered greatly, and the Magatsumi also, possessing power hard to despise, afflicted the warfare of the Kami. At an untimely hour, the path to chant the living Kototama was forgotten, as I grew flustered in heart; when the progress and retreat of My divine soul reached this extremity, the Kami of the Mihishiro aided Me. Unwittingly and unknowingly, my heart grew arrogant, and I fell into the ugly stratagem of the Magatsumi. Even though I now regret the slackening of the thread of my soul, it is of no avail and merely brings shame. From now on, drawing tight the reins of my heart, I shall untimely proclaim the precious divine words of the Kami. The shadow of the Magatsumi vanished without a trace, and the sound of the wind crossing the valley is clear. The sound of the water even of the waterfall rapids, falling and flowing with a gushing sound, grew limpid. Has the Magatsumi suffered defeat in the first battle and hidden deep within the Valley of the Demons' Den? I shall continue the pursuit to the very end, and accomplish the divine work of the divine dwelling. Upon the boulder of the opposite shore, releasing the light of Kototama, Ryōzan-hiko stands forth, does he? Upon the prayer of the Kami Ryōzan-hiko, the Mihishiro no Kami must have issued forth as light. Compared to the striking divine power of the Kami Ryōzan-hiko, I am a small thing. Was it that my heart grew arrogant in trying to provoke the Magakami unknowingly, that I met with such mournful experiences? I had not imagined that the strategy of the first Magatsu would be something so fierce. One after another, the ugly Magatsumi will press upon us, exhausting their full strength. This divine work of opposing the many with the few is an extraordinary Kototama warfare. Gradually the figure of the Kami Ryōzan-hiko appeared smaller and smaller as He ascended on high. I too, by the light of the living Kototama, would ascend and advance through this valley. The shadow of the black clouds that deeply enveloped the intervals of the valley has vanished away, and the light of the shining sun beams forth.

While chanting this reflective poem, the Kami Mochimunehiko, treading and tearing apart the roots of rocks and trees, continually proclaimed the divine words and caused Himself to ascend toward the forest of the Valley of the Demons' Den.
