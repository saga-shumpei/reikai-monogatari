Chapter 1: 天 Horse 行空 [1933]

天馬行空〔1933〕

§ 1

At the sacred ground of Takachihoyama, serving as the eight pillars of deities, Asakahime alone, paying no heed to the remonstrances of the various deities, valiantly pushed through the hazy cloud-fog over the raging wild plain of Magatsu, not knowing where it would end, crossing the slopes of various hardships, climbing to the ridge of the deeply historic Sakakiyama, recalling the divine ruins of Akitsuo, and proclaiming the divine words in the great shrine of the Lord Ōkami, bade farewell to the various deities serving on Mount Sakaki, and once again alone upon the wild plain, urged her steed forward and set forth; when the day had become twilight, the eighty Magatsumi, astonished, thinking to obstruct the hime's path ahead, exerted their crooked powers to the utmost to become a wide marsh lying across the way—how detestable it is!

Asakahime, seeing this, chanted the Amatsunorito, emitted the living Kototama, confined the Magatsumi transformed into a marsh unto eternity and bound them, instantly caused the great boulder that was the incarnation of crookedness to become a boat, and together with her steed, at leisure, reached the shore of the night lake where the moon shone and glittered; once again the august boat became a boulder, and the august boat-boulder, whose very name is auspicious, stood loftily on the southern shore. As luck would have it, in the eastern sky the dawn gradually broke, and the far-off crowing of house-roosters was heard; feeling emboldened that the homes where the Kunitsugamitachi dwelt were drawing near, transforming the marsh into a lake and naming it the waters of Lake Maga, she whipped her steed along the shore and advanced.

Her heroic figure was reflected upside down on the water's surface together with her steed, and her elegant august figure made one think that even a famous painting could scarcely match it; Asakahime, feeling emboldened, made her steed strike the hoofs at the foot of a hill veiled in mist deep within the wild plain, and when she hurried, it was the village of Sano. The various Kunitsugamitachi dwelling here, rejoicing that the august hime of the deities was descending from the heavenly skies, and exhausting the utmost of their sincerity, welcomed her with utmost cordiality.

Asakahime, alighting from the back of the steed, transmitted the way of livelihood to the Kunitsugamitachi, striking fire and burning all things to eat them, transmitting the way of fire-cooked food; accompanied by only one person, Sanohiko, who was the chieftain of the Kunitsugamitachi, when she once again set forth, parting the cloud-fog of the wide wilderness, and when she easily traced her way at eventide to the banks of the eastern river lying across and blocking her path ahead, the light of the new moon shone and glittered brilliantly across the sky, guarding the path ahead of the august hime. Ah, how awe-inspiring and wondrous are the divine blessings of Kannagara!

The eighty Magatsumi, defeated in their stratagem at Lake Maga, with their subordinate Magatsumi sealed away by the divine living Kototama of Asakahime, the majority of them having transformed their bodies into fish and shellfish and being granted a dwelling place at the bottom of the lake forever, and being designated as the daily food of the Kunitsugamitachi, were filled with the utmost indignation and bone-deep resentment; through a thousand thoughts and myriad considerations as to how they might somehow intercept the path ahead of the august hime and inflict disaster upon her, they had assumed the form of countless great serpents from the banks of the eastern river falling from the peak of Takachihoyama, holding their grips ready and waiting for her.

The torrent of the eastern river, illuminated by the light of the shining new moon at that very moment, was a beautiful stream where the crests of the waves shone and glittered like sparkling stars flowing along.

When the august hime peered closely through, the great serpents lay stretched all across the water's surface of the eastern river, and what appeared to be the shining of stars on the wave crests were all the scales of the great serpents. On each and every scale the moonlight shone, forming an indescribably beautiful flow of light. Sanohiko, not dreaming at all that the great serpents were lying across and that their scales were shining, admired it as such a beautiful stream and composed a poem:

How beautiful is the flow of the eastern river! / As the waves drift, the moon shines across it. / Serving in the retinue of the august hime, / to see such a beautiful night river, / the nighttime view of the great eastern river flowing majestically / has no equal in the world; / straddling the back of a steed, thinking to cross / this stream, my heart is purified.

"Now then, Sanohiko, let us make the crossing of the rapids," he said as he whipped his steed to set forth, but the deity Asakahime sternly stopped him and let him know by means of an august poem:

'The eyes of Sanuhiko are gleaming like the moon upon the wide waves of the river, so it appears; what seem to be river waves are all the scales of the monstrous serpents, the transformations of Magatsumi. Do you not know that the light of the moon in the vast sky shines upon the countless scales of these serpents? If you enter your horse into this river, it will instantly become the prey of the grand serpents and perish. One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, Among millions, And tens of millions, The Kamitachi come forth here, Blow away and scatter the eightyfold evil deeds of Maga, With the wind of Shinado, Drive them away, O Kamunagara. Asakahime offers her Magokoro, And prays devotedly.'

As she chanted these waka poems thus, from all the shihau and hatsupau, the kototama of the 'u-u-u' wailing voice echoed and resounded; the millions and tens of millions of grand serpents swarming and blocking the surface of the mighty river gradually diminished their forms, and before one's eyes their shadows and shapes vanished completely, leaving the bluish water flowing deeply and widely, illuminated by the moon in a continuous, roaring torrent. Sanuhiko, astonished, spoke:

'The grand serpents were seen through by the wondrous teri of the divine speech of Asakahime-no-Kami. Without knowing that she was such a noble deity, I shamefully served her as an attendant. Magatsumi transformed its body into tens of thousands of grand serpents, and lay in wait to bring about calamity, did it not? Now, as twilight falls upon this riverbank, I have witnessed the eightyfold evil deeds of Maga. Clever though the eightyfold wiles of the crooked deity may be, They could not prevail against the deity of the true words. What I took to be the flow of the mighty river was the shape of Magatsumi transformed into grand serpents. If it be so, I shall not fear, even if I follow the command of Asakahime and cross the river. No matter how strong the momentum of the swift steed may be, Without the divine majesty (omiki), one cannot cross this river.'

Asakahime-no-Kami chanted a waka poem:

'The waters of the Eastern River falling from the peaks of Takachise are strong, and the horses advance not. No matter how strong the divine power may be, at this eastern mighty river, the horses struggle. I now think to soar through the vast sky by the light of the living kototama and cross the river.'

Sanuhiko chanted:

'How will you soar through the noble sky and cross? We, the Kunitsukami, are utterly helpless. You indeed are an Amatsukami; to cross over the vast sky must be so easy for you. How shall I, Sanuhiko, a Kunitsukami burdened with a heavy physical body, cross through the void?'

'Guarding the sacred rear of Asakahime-no-Kami, secretly, secretly, I serve as her attendant. The pure kototama of the 'u' sound chanted just now was the sportive spirit of the deity Tsubinamidasu.'

Scarcely had these voices been heard in the air when, from amidst the mist, straddling a white horse, there appeared a deity descending leisurely before the presence of Asakahime-no-Kami. Looking closely, it was none to other than the majestic figure of the heroic deity Tsubinamidasu-no-Kami, who had been appointed as the divine custodian of the Takachise Palace, exactly as the kototama had foretold.

Asakahime-no-Kami, from the very first glance, spoke:

'You are indeed the divine custodian of the Takachise Palace; I rejoice in the achievement of guarding me. The manifest merit of bringing the distress of the Maga of Sanasigawa under subjection must have been guarded by your divine power. The ugly stratagems of the crooked deity have been shattered. Tsubinamidasu, upon your living kototama, Come now, let us advance across this wide river to the opposite shore and proceed under the moonlight.'

Tsubinamidasu-no-Kami chanted a waka poem:

'Realizing the peril of the divine speech of Asakahime-no-Kami, I pursued her in haste. Awe-struck by the divine speech of the Lord God, I remained hiding and guarding the sacred rear. The lands of the western region are distant; right before us, there are numerous fortresses of Maga. Go forth and sweep away every single ugly fortress of the crooked deity toward the lands of the western region. Come now, I shall conceal my form. May your journey along the folds of the road be peaceful.'

As soon as these waka poems were chanted, the figure of Tsubinamidasu-no-Kami suddenly turned into smoke and vanished away.

Sanuhiko, astonished, spoke:

'Though I think of the heavenly realm as a land where wondrous things pile one upon another, I am truly astonished. Beholding the activities of the distant Amatsukami, My soul has begun to tremble.'

Asakahime-no-Kami chanted a waka poem:

'The astonishment of Sanuhiko is natural; a land of divine works unknown even in the dreams of the Kunitsukami, Where deities bring forth lands and engender the deities of the national soul, Such divine works of the deities are wondrous above all. Seen through the eyes of a Kunitsukami, I too Must surely appear among a throng of wondrous deities.'

Having finished chanting this waka,

'O steed that I ride, O steed of Sanuhiko, / Grow wings, grow mighty wings, / Grow, grow, mighty wings, / That this fleet horse may become a heavenly steed / And soar through the skies,'

repeated he time and again, and when he proclaimed with a clear voice, 'Tatotsutete, hahofuhehi,' wondrously, this horse caused mighty wings to grow.

When Himegami mounted the horse together with Sanuhiko, the heavenly steed beat its gigantic wings in the air, gazing far down below upon the torrent of the wide river whose bounds could not be seen, its wings glistening sparklingly in the moonlight, filled with an indescribable joy, and it arrived safely on the opposite bank. Sanuhiko could not restrain his astonishment and sang:

'My horse grew wings and became a bird, / Soaring through the august sky to cross the river; / By virtue of the living kototama of Himegami, / The horse I ride has turned into a bird. / The horse of Himegami transformed into a heavenly steed, / Shining purely in the august sky; / The joy of my journey in Tengoku, / I shall tell and hand down to the Kunitsukamitachi. / The deity Unarizu appeared and on the rapids / Repelled the flooding Orochi; / How noble is the merit of the deity Unarizu / Who closely attends the shadow of Himegami!'

Asakahime no Kami recited an august poem:

'Protected by the merit of the deity Unarizu, / I have safely come hither; / That wings grew upon my steed was also the hidden merit / Of the deity Unarizu. / Realizing now anew the merit of the deity Unarizu, / I feel ashamed within myself; / From now on, making the mikotonori of the Lord Ōkami / My strength and reliance, I shall advance through the arano. / Accompanying the Kunitsukami Sanuhiko, I go, / Anxious about the path of our journey; / Though the Amatsukami travel through the august sky, the Kunitsukami / Are beings who tread and walk the rugged earth. / The wings of my steed before long faded and vanished, / And it began to neigh amidst the grass blades of the field. / The horse of Sanuhiko also concealing its wings, / Rested its breath and grazed upon the grass. / Though we have at last crossed the eastern river, / An ocean lies sprawling before my path ahead. / This sea is praised as the Great Sea of Demons, / And I hear that hordes of Yosomatsumitachi gather there.

All the Matsumi of the road that I tread, / I shall cause to submit and harmonize, and Kimiko shall advance. / Though the early summer wind blows, somehow or other / This land of the plains is faintly chilly.'

Sanuhiko sang:

'Resolved to serve as the attendant of Himegami unto the very end, / How the steed of my heart leaps with valor! / Whatever calamity the Matsumi may send forth, / I shall fear nothing, thanks to the merit of Himegami.'

Thus praising the divine virtue of Asakahime no Kami, Sanuhiko mounted his steed and, following behind her, proceeded through the Wakakunihara where mist endlessly enshrouded the landscape.
