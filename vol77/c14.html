Chapter 14: Night Light の眼球 [1946]

夜光の眼球〔1946〕

§ 1

At this, Tamayamahiko no Kami departed from the headquarters in the forest of the spring where Mihishirogami resided, and proceeded while urging his steed southward and ever southward across Ōnogahara; but suddenly the black smoke exhaled from the direction of Masumegaya blocked the heavens and crawled upon the earth, so that a shaku or a sun could not be distinguished, and the progress of the steed was also far from smooth.

As he struggled onward, around the time the day was growing dark into twilight, he finally arrived at the slightly flat Sasagahara at the foot of the mountain; yet as it was already dark even by day, coupled with the closing in of the evening gloom, even the white steed upon which he rode became hard to completely distinguish, whereupon even the formidable Tamayamahiko no Kami was brought to a sheer deadlock, and in a bewildered state, chanted aloud the Ikukototama to dispel the malignant vapor.

His miuta:

Ao-u-e-i, Lord of Amatsutakamiya, Our journey of divine mission—drive away the clouds and mist! Ka-ko-ku-ke-ki, the shining sun and moon, Forever brightly illuming the divine land of Kami! Though the ferocious fury of the hateful Magami be strong, With the Ikukototama, we shall clear away the clouds and mist! Sa-so-su-se-shi, The shadows of the brightly gleaming sun and moon, the Magami Seek to conceal—how vexing indeed! Illumining the fortress of the fly-like Magatsu no Toride, I shall advance into Masumegaya! Ta-to-tsu-te-chi, As long as the water-and-fire life of the string of jewels remains, I am one who shall illuminate the island-roots of Banri! Hiding in the valleys of the lofty mountains, the Magatsumi, Wiping away water and fire, I shall shine the heavenly sun! For as long as the living life of the soul-line endures, Devoting all, I shall surely fight with the Magami! Na-no-nu-ne-ni, Sevenfold, eightfold, tenfold, twentyfold, what is wrapped— Disperse, clouds and mist, by the Ikukototama! For a long time, the island-roots of Banri have been closed in— Those clouds and mist I shall sweep away with the water-and-fire life! The sound of the waterfall cascading down has also grown muddy, This valley stream where the great serpent dwells— Amidst the trouble-free Shibi-tenkai, I pity and think upon the rampaging Magami. Ha-ho-hfe-hi, Until even the white figure of the steed cannot be seen, The water and fire of the Magami have wrapped it in black! By the endless power of the Ikukototama, I shall dispel the black clouds of the ugly fiends! By the divine water and fire of the great Lord Ōkami, I am Tamayamahiko born from the sound of O! Ma-mo-mu-me-mi, The fortresses of the ugly Magami, one and all, I have come to guide in speech and pacify! The islands of Banri are the mihishiro of the Lord Ōkami— Render submission, you ugly dragon deities! Maka-fushigi, at the mountain skirts of Hakubagatake, That ugly black clouds linger and swirl— Verily, these are the clouds wrapped by the ugly dragon deities and great serpents In order to plot against me! Wa-wo-u-e-wi, I now, by the divine words of Mihishirogami, Stand facing the predetermined path of the Magami! I shall strike down, cut away, and sweep aside every wicked stratagem of the evil deities, And advance valiantly! I now rest in these bamboo-grass fields, Thinking to wait until the break of night. Though I earnestly think to advance, I cannot distinguish a shaku or a sun; For this perpetual darkness, there is no remedy.

At the very moment when He thus chanted, there were three Goddesses appearing leisurely, hanging a nocturnal luminous jewel upon their breasts. Glancing suddenly, they were the three Goddesses: Yamatohime no Kami, Chikaihime no Kami, and Yuihime no Kami; and while bowing slightly in greeting before the divine words, they illuminated all around with the night-luminous jewel, and the figures of the Himegami appeared ever surpassing in beauty, godliness, and gentleness.

Tamayamahiko no Kami thought it made no sense that the three Goddesses should have proceeded after Him yet arrived here so early, and with crossed hands remained lost in thought for a while, then spake:

Yamato-hime no Kami laughed gently with a voice of 'ho-ho-ho-ho-ho-ho', and spake:

'O foolish words of Tamayamahiko! The night-luminous jewel is my very god-soul. In times of emergency, my god-soul Becomes like this a light and illuminates the darkness. Though the perpetual darkness be ever so deep, with the night-luminous jewel I hold I shall illuminate the mountain path. Ascend, following the light of my soul, O Tamayamahiko no Kami, along the dark mountain path!'

Tamayamahiko no Kami chanted a miuta.

'No matter how I try, my heart cannot settle; your figure I deem to be the incarnation of the ugly Magami. Be that as it may, while other deities may be misled By the light that illuminates the darkness, I shall not acknowledge it!'

霊山比古の神は、三女神は吾より後に進みたる筈なるに、早くも先着したるは合点ゆかずと双手を組み暫し思案に暮れ居給ひけるが、

『審かしも汝は三柱比女神に 面ざし偽せし曲津見なるらむ 三柱の比女神は夜光の珍の玉 持たせしことの未だ無きものを』

山跡比女の神は「ホホホホホホ」と優しき御声に打笑ひながら、

『愚かなる霊山比古の言の葉よ 夜光の玉はわが神魂ぞや 吾神魂まさかの時には斯の如 光となりて闇を照らすも 常闇はいや深くとも吾持てる 夜光の玉に山路を照らさむ 吾魂の光に従ひ登りませ 闇の山路を霊山比古の神よ』

霊山比古の神は御歌詠ませ給ふ。

『如何にしても心落ちゐぬ汝の姿 醜の曲神の化身とおもふ よしやよし他の神々は闇を照らす 光に迷はむも吾は認めじ』

§ 2

Chikahihime no Kami, smiling gently,

Chikahihime: 'Ah, thou who utterest foolish words, my very soul that shone within the living Kototama thou didst proclaim! Were there no light from the living Kototama thou hast declared, I should never obtain the jewel of night-light. Be that as it may, come, follow after me and ascend this path of darkness, that we may brighten the fore of Tamayamahiko no Kami's august steps; thus I illuminate the jewel of night-light, and the three Himegami likewise all shine with the jewel of night-light to await thee for thy sake.'

Tamayamahiko no Kami, unable to endure the dubiety, chants an august poem.

Tamayamahiko: 'When I gaze upon thy countenance, Tomikaumi, I cannot think thee to be the Kami of the three Himegami. Be that as it may, until the break of night, I think to nurture the living Kototama here.'

Megami: 'Though thou utterest foolish Kototama, must the eyes of Tamayamahiko no Kami have been straying! Illumined thus by the jewel of night-light, my countenance indeed appears transformed; the form of the Megami seen in broad daylight and the form of the Megami seen in the light of the night-light are ever shifting things, yet here at the mountain skirt the bamboo grass is thick with dew. Pray, advance unto the hermitage where I dwell.'

Tamayamahiko no Kami, growing ever more suspicious,

Tamayamahiko: 'In this mountain of the hermitage of the three Himegami, I deem it cannot be; utter no deceitful words.'

Megami: 'Since it is a land where Kototama shines forth and prospers, even in the space of a moment a hermitage is built. Do you not consider that all things in this universe live by the water and fire of Kototama?'

Yumusubihime no Kami, smiling,

Yumusubihime: 'How strange and laughable is the contention between Tamayamahiko no Kami, Yamatohime, and Chikahihime! The clouds and mists of doubt cross to and fro, and in this bamboo-grass plain of darkness where neither black nor white can be distinguished, I too hold the jewel of night-light to illuminate the darkness of the myriad-mile island ridges. Though it is right that you doubt to the utmost, are you not also a Kami of Kototama? To dispel the doubts of Tamayamahiko no Kami, come, let us conceal the jewel of night-light.'

Tamahiko: 'Yamatohime and Chikahihime, do you too conceal the jewel of night-light you hold!'

Even as she thus chants, the forms of the three Himegami and the jewel of night-light vanished entirely, and the surroundings became a true darkness where one could not discern an inch before one's face; only the sound of the storm blowing through the dwarf bamboo was audible, a desolation to which naught could be compared.

Hereupon, Tamayamahiko no Kami, in the lonely dwarf-bamboo plain where only the sound of the mountain wind sweeping through the dwarf bamboo and the breathing of a fine horse were heard, folding his arms to await the break of night and prepare for battle, chants an august poem.

Tamayamahiko: 'The darkness pressing upon the utterly desolate dwarf-bamboo plain is surely the water and fire of Magakami. By virtue of the awesome Kototama of the Kami, wondrous to name, the night-abiding Magatsuhi has vanished away. A mountain wind rises over the bamboo-grass, chilling my skin; how shall I pass this single night? Benighted upon the journey where confronting Magakami bars the way, perforce I chant the Kototama poem. Whatever may betide, following the whisper of my soul's thread, I shall advance upon the journey of Magatsuhi. The Himegami who unexpectedly appeared here—is she an incarnation of Magatsuhi, with glaring eyes? Making her eyes, which shine almost painfully, flash brightly, a wicked deity masquerades as the night-light jewel. The countless ugly Magakami gathered along this mountain path were awe-inspiring indeed. Since it is yet an immature land of the country's plains, the watchers of Magatsuhi commit arbitrary deeds as well. Changing her form in various ways, she presses close; this wicked deity at the foot of the mountain is detestable indeed. Making the soul of my courage-fronted heart shine, I resolve to accomplish my divine work. This incessant pouring rain—can it be the work of Ryūjin? It will not endure for long. Born from the water and fire of the mighty Kami, I, who am righteous, shall only press forward. The sudden rain fell and suddenly ceased; the power of Magakami is frail indeed. Enduring for a long time while fighting and pressing on, I shall try to address words of conviction to the ugly Magakami. The dragon-snake deities gather in this valley, raising unseasonable clouds—how hateful they are! Since the blessings of the water and fire of the living Kototama are manifest, what should I fear from the eighty Magatsuhi? Breaking the fragile stratagem of the fickle watchers of Magatsuhi, I shall press on. Though black clouds enwrap me tenfold and twentyfold, I will clear them away with the living Kototama. Did they fear my resplendent Kototama? The dragon-snakes became Hime and watched in secret. One after another, making them appear as the jewel of night-light, the ugly females sought to deceive me. Though the jet-black darkness presses close, the light of the divine spirit I hold is brighter and brighter. The wild-blowing storm of the wicked ones, too, which was in the water and fire of Magakami, has suddenly ceased. Transforming their bodies into the forms of affectionate Megami, those ugly females and Sasara-detectives, falsely taking the names of Himegami with whom we have ties, have illuminated the darkness and lured me. The wicked plot entices me by manifesting the form of a beautiful Himegami before my eyes, seeking to dazzle my sight with a charming, exquisite grace that cannot even be painted. The valiant three Himegami would never utter such uncanny Kototama. By the side of the valley stream where only the murmur of the flowing water is heard, the bamboo-grass plain is quiet with dew. Both heaven and earth are overcast as if in everlasting darkness; do the wicked Magatsuhi scheme with all their might?

Without sleeping, sitting upright in this bamboo thicket, shall I wait until the break of night? Guarded by the undivided grace of the Kami, I pray that I may conquer the ugly Magatsuhi. What seems like closed eyes—is the eternal darkness of this mountain skirt the entrance of Magatsuhi?

Thus chanting august poems alone upon the grassy darkness, he passed the night here. The sky of dawn grew faintly bright with purple clouds trailing across it, as if to celebrate today's journey.

Tamayamahiko: 'The eastern sky at last grows like the dawn, and purple clouds trail across the heavens. The voices of a hundred birds are heard sounding clearly. The light of the Heavenly Sun that shall soon rise on high—amidst the reverberating sound of the waterfalls in a hundred thousand valleys, the night has broken.'
