Chapter 6: 田族 Island 着陸 [1938]

田族島着陸〔1938〕

§ 1

Fearless against the raging, roaring waves of Itake, Himegami transformed them instantly into an island of rock by the divine light of Ikukototama.

The foaming waves were instantly turned into the fine sand of the island, accomplishing wondrous feats and astonishing Momogamitachi.

Illuminating the light of Iki, she pushed through the waves of the boundless Made, and proceeding bravely, ever so leisurely, with Shiroumagatake as her mark, while chanting the divine poem of Kototama in a clear voice, she advanced valiantly.

Amatsubi crossing the misora grew dim upon the peak of Takachihoyama, and its yamakage came sweeping over the unabara of Made.

The cold evening unakaze came sweeping upon the mifune of Megami.

Asakahime no Kami chants a divine poem.

'Crossing the unabara of boundless Made with high waves, the sun has sunk upon Takachihoyama. The yamakage of Takachiho falls far and dark upon the surface of the boundless sea. Countless momodori seek their roosts and fly toward the shigeki treetops of the shimajima. The evening crows with nubatama black wings hurry toward the shimane of the west. As the sound of the waves echoes ever higher, the boat in which I ride trembles gently. The boat that came weaving through countless island-mountains was safe through divine grace. Amatsubi has sunk into the mountains, and the light of Tsukiyomi shines ever more brilliantly. Gazing upon the pure figure of Tsukiyomi, I cannot help but yearn for my dear spouse. When I look at the moon crossing the misora, I cannot help but yearn for the pure figure of my dear spouse. Though Amatsubi has hidden, the mifune of Tsukiyomi illuminates the Iwanakusabune.'

'Crossing the great ocean where the moon shines bright, to give birth to the kami of our national soul, the voices of the momodori have grown unheard, leaving only the sound of the ocean surf. The waves striking the shores of the shimajima are splashing whitely, at the mercy of the wind. The sound of the waves surging and crashing heavily against the rock bases has grown doubly high. Unblooming flowers bloom in full upon the face of the sea, and clear is the boat of our midnight journey.'

Shodohiko no Kami chants a divine poem.

'The boundless sea has already grown dusky, and the sound of the surf echoes loudly. Do the momodori take shelter in the shigeki of the island? Not even a single bird shows its shadow. To which island shall I too draw my boat, and sheltered from the rain, weave dreams? The deep snow of Shiroumagatake shines in the moonlight, casting its shadow upon the misty unabara. The Ikushima of Shiroumagatake, famed in song, is said to have many white horses gathering and dwelling there. My horses have been kept aboard the boat all day long, and must be suffering, for they are given neither water. Is there no way to water them? The water of the boundless sea has turned entirely to salt.'

Kiritachihiko no Kami chants a divine poem.

'Serving as an attendant to Himegami on the boundless sea, how weary we have grown as dusk falls. In the mid-ocean where Magakami Itake runs wild, a boat sent adrift at dusk is lonely indeed.'

Tachiyohime no Kami chants a divine poem.

'Strange are the words of Kiritachi no Kami. Is this not a journey of Ikukototama? If you speak of loneliness, it is lonely; if you speak of joy, it is a divine age of joy. In the realm where Kototama aids and blesses, utter no weak sighs, O Kiritachihiko no Kami.'

Kiritachihiko no Kami chants a divine poem.

'Tachiyohimegami, your words of Kototama are most true, most true. I have erred in my Kototama. The unabara on a night lit by the distant moon shines brilliantly and most lively. The crests of the waves sparkle as flowers and strike the shore, and the spray of the tide shines like jewels. Living on and on, I shall flourish eternally, with neither heart nor soul growing weary. Compared to the valor of Asakahimegami, I am but a small and weak kami.'

Asakahime no Kami chants a divine poem.

'At the words of Kototama spoken by Kiritachi no Kami, the unabara has revived. The stars of the great sky have descended and shine brightly upon the water's bed. Gazing upon the moon and stars above and below, the boat in which I go is a heavenly bird-boat. To ride upon the waves of such a beautiful sea, illuminated by the moon, is a joy indeed.'

Ameharehime no Kami chants a divine poem.

'The shadow of Shiroumagatake has gradually drawn near. Crossing far over the path of waves a thousandfold, the crests of the waves upon the unabara flickered in hundreds and thousands in the moonlight. Though shimane are lined up on right and left in great numbers, there is not even a place where a kami should dwell. Revealing their bare rock bones, the islands cast the shadow of the moon traversing the sky in a ruddy glow. Shiroumagatake, which we look up to, has trees growing lush and green; would that we might go and clear it!'

§ 2

Thus chanting, at length the august vessel reached the foot of Mount Hakuba. This island was called the Island of Made, and among the islands floating upon this ocean, it was the widest and most fertile noble island-root. On the Island of Made, countless wild horses and sheep inhabited, and it was truly an island of the Takara where not even a single pillar of the Kunitsukami had yet dwelt.

As the divine retinue of Asakahime no Kami bound the boat to the rocky shore and ascended quietly, the multitude of horses and sheep vied with each other in leading the way and fled toward the foot of Mount Hakuba. While resting beneath the shade of the camphor giant trees that stood densely sealing the heavens, the members each chanted august poems.

The ocean of Made is crossed at last in the midnight of the moon, Could it be that we have arrived at the island of the Takara? Dense and towering to seal the sky, Under the shade of the camphor tree the moonlight cannot be seen. Coming up to this island, I have heard the insects Singing at the roots of the grasses of Ikushima. The tones of the insects grow ever clearer As I continue to chant the glory of the Amatsubi for eternity. The snow upon the ridge of Mount Hakuba shines white and clear, Radiant even to the night eyes. How many tens of thousands of horses and sheep That swarmed and inhabited this island must have fled and vanished? The soil of this island-root is fertile, and a hundred grasses Grow luxuriantly with endless prosperity. An island where the sprouting grasses are sufficient To nourish tens of thousands of horses and sheep. Here upon this island, the Kunitsukami shall plant their seeds, And we desire to open it for a thousand and eight thousand reigns.

Hatsumatehiko no Kami chanted an august poem.

When I look up, Mount Hakuba is covered with snow Above the clouds and shines brightly. The sound of waves is heard loud, and in the distant field, The neighing of white horses echoes across. The neighing of the white steed is loud, and tens of millions Of voices echo together as one. At the thought that our August Mihishiro Kami dwells upon this island, In the neighing of the steeds, When I hear the neighing of the white steed, I perceive The light of the living Kototama of the Amatsukami.

Asakahime no Kami chanted an august poem.

Though not even a single pillar of the Kunitsukami dwells here, The eighty Mihishiro no Kami must be present. When we look at the sprouting of grains in the opened fields far and near, The Kami surely dwell here. It must be the dwelling place of Takarahime no Kami, Who is one of the eighty pillars of the Mihishiro Kami. Tomorrow, we shall advance deep into this island, And make inquiry of the Mihishiro Kami.

Tachisehime no Kami chanted an august poem.

Hearing that the Mihishiro Kami dwells upon this island, It is awe-inspiring, and we shall offer our inquiry. Awaiting the day when the Mihishiro Kami dwells upon this Takara Island And gives birth to the Kunimitama no Kami.

Kirichihiko no Kami chanted an august poem.

Though the mountains and fields of the Island of Made look pure, Black clouds rise in the intervals of the valleys. These black clouds must be the water and fire of the eighty Magatsumi. Tomorrow we shall approach and pacify them with words.

Asakahime no Kami chanted an august poem.

Revering the divine words of the Lord Kami, Takarahime no Kami should dwell in this land. Awaiting the emergence of the Akitsuo no Kami, She shall hide herself within this island-root.

Ameharehime no Kami chanted an august poem.

Does the eighty-pillared Mihishiro Kami dwell All alone upon this wide island? Takarahime no Kami, dwelling all alone Upon this wide island-root, must be lonely.

Asakahime no Kami chanted an august poem.

Accompanied by the ten pillars of noble Kami, The Mihishiro Kami who shall dwell in tranquility, Come what may, until the breaking of night in this forest, Shall sleep in peace, listening to the roaring of the tides.

At the very moment when they were thus chanting, whence did two pillars, Wamoriwake no Kami and Wakaharuhiko no Kami, come riding upon white horses, stop their steeds in the shade of this forest,

Awe-inspiring though it is, we shall offer our words of inquiry; does not There dwell Asakahime no Kami in this forest? Revering the divine words of Takarahime no Kami, We two pillars come forth to welcome you.

Asakahime no Kami replied with an august poem:

We shall speak to the two pillars of Kami, that I indeed am Asakahime, the Mihishiro Kami. Clearing away the mist-enshrouded ocean of Made, We have now come to this island-root.

Wakaharuhiko no Kami chanted an august poem:

Awe-inspiring is this evening when we gaze upon The august figure of Asakahime, for whom we waited and waited! Come now, O Mihishiro Kami, O all Kami, We shall guide you to the mansion of the Hime. I am indeed the divine official Wakaharuhiko, Who serves the deity Takarahime no Kami. And the single pillar who has now appeared here Is the divine official Wamoriwake no Kami. Come now, we shall guide you to the mansion. Arise quickly, O Hime no Kami and all Kami.

Asakahime no Kami,

Come now, according to the words proclaimed by Wakaharuhiko, We shall advance to the august mansion.

Scarcely had she spoken when she mounted a white horse, whereupon the four pillars of Kami—Hatsumatehiko no Kami, Kirichihiko no Kami, Tachisehime no Kami, and Ameharehime no Kami—lightly leapt upon their steeds and advanced with bits aligned along the field path of the moonlit midnight, which was awe-inspiring indeed.
