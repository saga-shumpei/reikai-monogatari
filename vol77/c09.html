Chapter 9: Horse 上征誦 [1941]

馬上征誦〔1941〕

§ 1

As Wamorihiko no Kami, who serves at the head of the procession of eleven deities led by Takarahimeno Kami, celebrates with generous grace the outset of the expedition upon his horse, he chants a sacred miuta:

In the distant Amatsutakamiya / eternally ruling over Shibitenkai, / by the divine word of the Lord Ōkami, / welling forth from the sea of mud, / the myriad-riled island chain is the land of Kami. / For a thousand years, for eight thousand years, unto all eternity, / in the boundless, flourishing land of Kami, / the swarming and dwelling wicked eagles / and the fierce beasts' wild rampages— / to pacify and harmonize them, the Mihishiro / Takaranohime descended. / We, the ten divine pillars, / have likewise followed the Hime and descended from heaven. / For that great achievement, upon the myriad-riled island, / even the strife of birds and beasts / is now completely pacified; / frogs and mice dwell in peace, / emerging into the fields morning and evening, / tilling the paddy and dry fields, sowing grain seeds, / and tending them carefully.

Yet the Magatsumi have not yet faded away, / and hidden in the thousand gorges and eight hundred ravines / of Mount Shirahumagatake soaring to the skies, / the vile Magatsumi's sword-fleshed / dragon-gods and giant serpents still even now, / day and night, spewing poisonous breath, / cloud, shatter, and ravage this living land's heaven and earth, / while their disasters, month by month and day by day, / piling one upon another, cause the Age of Eternal Darkness / to threaten once again to visit this land— / beholding this with pity, the Mihishiro / who appears as a deity, Takarahimeno Kami, / taking us as her companions, toward the valley of the dragon's hiding place, / the Demon-Inhabited Ravine (Masegatani), / proceeds with valorous might. / Ah, oakamigami, oakamigami, / by the blessing of the living Kototama, / the vile Magatsumi shall hide their shadows,

and the clouds and mists clogging heaven and earth / shall clear away without a trace, while the light of moon and sun traversing the heavens / shines brightly. All things that exist upon the earth, / all living creatures, every single one, / leaving none behind, granted the sacred radiance, / do we pray that forever / they shall guard and protect us. / Though Masegatani be deep, / though the dragon's fury be fierce, / I shall not fear; drawing the stern sword of Kototama, / cleaving and sweeping away, completely purging / the Magatsumi of the heavenly realm, / to make it a paradise of divine indwelling, / to cause it to live and protect it—oh, what joy! / The neighing of the steed is valiant, / the sound of the hooves goes katsu-katsu, / and through the mountain foothills where clouds and mists bewilder, / we advance with courage.

We advance with courage.

Reizanmikoto no Kami, swayed upon his steed, quietly chants a miuta:

Serving before the presence of the Mihishiro no Kami, / into the ravines of Mount Shirahumagatake, / to pacify and harmonize the deeply hidden Magatsumi / do I advance. / Even if the light of the heavenly sun be clouded, / even if the form of the moon be waning, / whatever demonic winds may rage, / whatever clouds and mists may shroud our path, / how could I ever fear? / Born and separated from the sacred Water and Fire / of the Lord Ōkami of Kototama is this very body of mine. / For surely the heavenly realm, being a realm born / from the Water and Fire of Kototama, / there can be nothing in the world / that surpasses the aid of the living Kototama. / Advance, advance, O deities of the living Kototama, / ten pillars strong! / I am Reizanmikoto no Kami, / who has resounded and come forth into birth / from the Kototama of the 'O' sound.

Mount Shirahumagatake too shall be transformed, by Kototama, / into a divine mountain of the spirits, most pure and pristine. / Even the vile dragon-gods and giant serpents / lurking within Masegatani / shall I pacify and harmonize, or else cut down, / or else destroy, washing and purifying the myriad-riled island, / and Mount Shirahumagatake as well as Ushuzugamine soaring to the east, / shall I shape and solidify into pure, fresh, spiritual mountains— / pondering and turning over this great achievement anew, / my chest beats high, my arms flex, / and power fills and overflows my entire body. / While filling myself with this vigor / and proclaiming the living Kototama, / any fierce dragon-god whatsoever, / and every serpent, shall utterly vanish away. / Ah, what a joyful journey of this day! / Ah, what a valiant service in the public cause!

Wakaharuhikono Kami chants a miuta:

Are my horses valiant upon today's journey of conquest? / Prick up your ears, snort with your noses, / shake your manes, and tossing your eight-tasselled / long tails to left and right, / even the sound of the bells ringing shyan-shyan, / and the sound of the hooves valiant, / a thousand and ten thousand leagues what do we care? / With the vigor to trample and shatter them, / through the mountain-foot paths where grasses grow a hundredfold, / with fierce momentum we advance. / Now, this very island chain / was a paradise for frogs and mice; / born into being through the Kototama of SU, / the crane, upon the pine branches of Tokiwa, / acting as governor of the Myriad-riled Island, / employing a hundred monkeys, / had guarded the livelihood of the countless frogs— / a land of Kami. / As stars shifted and years passed by degrees, / the atmosphere of sorcery and evil in heaven and earth...
