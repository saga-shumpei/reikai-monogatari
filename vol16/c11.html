Chapter 11: Treasure 庫の鍵 [601]

宝庫の鍵〔601〕

§ 1

The Mizumitama of Kamususanō, the Izumitama of Kunitakehiko, and the Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, together with Kamehiko, whose name is auspicious, and Hidekohime, who illuminates the darkness, and the Hime Yoshiko, under the protection of Onitakehiko, subdued the fierce Magatsukami and the clan of Onikumohiko; the many demons who would not submit fled in all directions like clouds and mist, and Onikumohiko, riding upon the clouds, escaped toward the direction of Mount Ibuki; such were the various rumors that arose as the Sango moon of the ninth month ascended, illuminating the sky, and at the twilight hour, two man and woman appeared before the gate of Akiyamahiko.

Disguised in hoods and veils, fearing the surroundings, they knocked upon the gate in low voices, calling out to be let in; at the sound, Kamekō and Ginkō emerged, answering with a start, and peering through the gap in the door, they grew suspicious of the two figures and asked who they were; the voice replied with grace, saying, 'I am the Hinode Ōkami, and this is the shrine of the god Yukunarihiko; pray, open the gate with haste, for there is a matter of detail to be reported to the god-official Akiyamahiko,' and they urged them on with such haste and an unsettling air that Kamekō, pouting and shouting, replied that he did not recognize any god of sunrise.

As the Sango moon began to rise in its brilliance at twilight, he said, 'Is it not the god of sunset who knocks at the gate? Yukunarihiko is but a mask of lies; are you not the ugly Magatsukami, the god of the dead-end, who has lost his dwelling? Even if the gate is closed, this is the mansion of the god-official Akiyamahiko; the ugly vessels of the Magatsumi that have not yet opened within your hearts shall be shattered; without saying that this is a gate of friction, it would be better for you to return quickly; a fellow who knocks at the gate at sunset cannot be a decent one; if you have business, come tomorrow, for this great gate is one that we guard night and day without sleep, and the opening of the great gate is at the hour of sunrise.'

The gatekeeper who lives by the day also said, 'The gate of sunset shall not open; return, return,' and urged them to depart.

Takahime raised a shrill voice, tinged with trembling, and stood with a fierce countenance: 'Ten ri in every direction is the inner sanctum of the palace; I am the Hinode-gami of the great gate opening; convey at once to the great commander Akiyamahiko the arrival of the Hinode-gami, the god Yukunarihiko; as a mere gatekeeper, you cannot refuse the opening and closing of the gate; do not dally, lest you regret it later; what do you take the Hinode-gami to be, who has appeared as a saving god against the great calamity approaching this house tonight?'

Kamekō said, 'Oi, Ginkō, take a look; there is one woman with her face plastered in white powder, looking quite unpleasant, and one man with a face like a pale gourd; they say they have come to inform us of some great event at the mansion, and that if we do not open this gate, we shall regret it tomorrow; what should we do?'

Ginkō said, 'No matter what you say, it is the strict command of Goshiyujinsama that once the hour of Kuremu has passed, we shall open the gate for no Nanibito whatsoever; leave them be, leave them be.'

Kamekō replied, 'Yet they are no Futsū Ningen; they claim to be Kami.'

Ginkō answered, 'There are Iroiro of Kami; there are wolves that devour men, there are Magatsukami, and there are even Hanakami, Chirikami, and Shirifu. One must distinguish them well and exercise Handan, or else a Shiyutsutai will arise that shall leave one gnashing one's teeth in regret later. Let me see, I shall peer through and examine their Yōsu.'

Ginkō closed one eye and pressed the other against the Sukima of the gate, peering through.

Ginkō exclaimed, 'Ha, ha, ha! That fellow is certainly a Kami, but he is a mere ginger; he would be fit only as a remedy for Seki and phlegm. Look, he is pressing his mouth to the other's ear and engaging in Hisohisobanashi. That man with the pale Kahoiro must be the husband of that woman; what a Kiraku fellow he is. To stand before another's Monzen and act in such an Ichiya manner—is he not ashamed before the moon?'

Aohiko said, 'Excuse me, a grave matter shall erupt against the Oyakata within this Konya. The night is dark where one cannot see an inch ahead; we are the messengers of propaganda to save the world, so please, open the gate.'

Ginkō retorted, 'What are you spouting? To speak of a night where one cannot see an inch ahead under such a bright moon—that must be the state of your own heart. If you have business, come tomorrow. Even if some emergency were to erupt in this mansion, it is not for you to interfere. Be off with you at once.'

Takahime said, 'That may be so, but by the urgent divine decree of the Hinode no Kami, whatever the case may be, please convey this matter to the master, Akiyama-hiko.'

Ginkō said, 'Ah, there is no help for it. I shall at least report this to Goshiyujinsama, so until then, you must wait here. Hey, Kamekō, you must not open the gate for any reason until I return.'

So saying, he cast the words aside and ran off toward the inner chambers.

Aohiko said, 'Excuse me, gatekeeper, will you not open the gate quickly? If you dally, your own fate shall be in peril. It is by the divine decree of the Kami that I have come, wishing to warn you, for it would be pitiful if you were cast headlong into the Ne no Kuni and Soko no Kuni.'

Kamekō replied, 'Even by divine decree, I cannot open it without the permission of my master. I know not if I shall fall into the hell called Ne no Kuni and Soko no Kuni, but even the judgments of hell depend upon gold. If I open this gate and fall into hell, it would be a trouble; therefore, you too must produce something. If you do so, I shall open it for you. It is said that if one has gold, one can even open the lid of the cauldron of hell; one must give sake money to the demons to find the wisdom to escape hell, so come, produce it, produce it! When one asks a newt if there is anything other than a love potion, it shows its finger in a circle; even a newt is like that, so you, Kamekō who lives in the same water, produce something round, and I shall open it for you quietly.'

Takahime said, 'See, this is why it is said that the teachings of the Spirit of Mizu are the ways of evil; even the Monban is governed by the Shiyugi of Kanetori. Look here, Aohiko-sama, does not this single Ichiji show how the Ananai-kyō is a matter of Genkin-shiyugi, Rikoshiyugi, and the Yarikata of self-interest? If you do not awaken to a reasonable Kagen, you shall be plucked clean by the Spirit of Mizu, even to the last hair upon your backside.'

Aohiko replied, 'Indeed, I had thought you to be one who was sharp in every corner, yet the Ananai-kyō is truly an Aniki.'

Kamekō exclaimed, 'Hey, what a fellow to be so Guzuguzu and stingy! I know not from Doko you are Sendenshi, but the Ananai-kyō is generous with Zenibara; even for a Yakuza-like Monban such as Wareware, they would slip two or three Koban into one's Futokoro without us saying a word. This fellow appears to be of the Uranai-kyō, for even when we make a Rokotsu Seikiu, he is a miser who will not bring it forth. That is why he thinks only of snatching away the believers of the Ananai-kyō without any labor of his own, to bring them into the Uranai-kyō. Hey, you two Sendenshi, have you no forgotten items? Surely you have forgotten something; do you not need small change even to board a ferry? Is there any law that says one may pass through a gate without such things? What witless Sendenshi you are! I had intended to take advantage of the situation while that fellow Ginkō was away, but since the other party is a witless Donbei, success is uncertain.'

At such a moment, Ginkō came running and said,

'Hey, Kamekō, it is the order of our master; open the gate at once and let them pass.'

'Ah, is that so?'

So saying, he removed the bolt, opened the gates to the left and right, and changing his tone, Kamekō said,

'Ah, these are indeed two Sendenshi-sama who seem to possess most magnificent divine virtues. I was away in the back on urgent business, and it seems my servant, that fellow, spoke nonsense about the gate being closed and demanded money, acting in a stingy manner. By no means; as this house consists of believers in the Ananai-kyō, from the highest to the lowest, we are pure and clean, and we consider even touching money to be a defilement. We have recently hired a Monban, and as that fellow has hitherto been a believer in the Brahmanism, he spoke of money, which is a source of great shame. It was by no means I who said such things; pray, do not take it ill. When you meet our master, I beg you to explain that it was not Kamekō who spoke thus. In this world where misunderstandings are so frequent, it is not a pleasant gate where even the pure and clean Kamekō must suffer the side-blows of a Monban and have his belly searched for a crime he did not commit.'

Unwittingly, the initial feigned voice was forgotten, and it returned to the original Jigoe.

Takahime said, 'Ho ho ho, even so, your voice resembles it well. At first, I thought it was someone else, but as expected, you are the owner of the voice from before. My, what a transformation you play; being the Kobun of the Ōbakemono Mizumitama, your ability to transform is indeed Kimeu. Ho ho ho ho ho.'

Kamekō, returning once again to a feigned voice, replied:

'Nay, nay, never, never. In the beginning, it was my own Jigoe. In the midst of it, the Ikiriyō of a Shinmai Monban possessed me and spoke; thus, the voice of the Shinmai Monban emerged just as it was. Aha ha ha ha.'

He attempted to gloss over it with laughter.

Ginkō said, 'Aha ha ha ha, the Sata of Jigoku depends upon Kame, indeed.'

Kamekō replied, 'The Sata of Jigoku is a matter that can be settled by Kame and Ginkō in this world. Aha ha ha ha. Come, come, you two, pray enter quickly.'

As if to say it was a settled matter, the two swung their arms wide and, with Ōmata, marched forward in high spirits, sauntering along slowly.

The two stood abruptly at the Genkuwan, listening intently with an air of peering into the state of the house, or perhaps not peering at all.

A man appeared, suddenly sliding open the shoji of the Genkuwan:

'Oh, you are Takahime-sama and Aohiko-sama. You suffered a most pitiable disaster some time ago. I have thought of coming to offer my sympathies since then, but I hear you were severely burned—the water torture, the fire torture, the smoke torture, the God of fire emerging from the eyes, and you, Aohiko-sama, with a face pale as death and sighing in despair. My, how you have taken the trouble to visit. Well, well, if you have such reservations, do not enter so hastily without business.'

No sooner had he spoken than his figure vanished entirely.

As the two stood at the Genkuwan, Takahime said, 'This is why they call it the Bakemono-kyō. Aohiko-sama, this is why I say it is useless unless you follow me and receive practical training. The insight of the Hinode-no-Kami cannot be mistaken, can it?'

Aohiko replied, 'It is truly so. Alas, I am struck with awe.'

Takahime said, 'You must not speak such frivolous things. It appears that you, too, have been possessed by an evil spirit of the Mizumitama. Will you not compose yourself?'

At this moment, Momijihime appeared gracefully from the inner chambers to this place:

'Oh, you two. Is it not because of some unusual matter that you have come in the middle of the night? In any case, pray come up and rest, and then let me hear the nature of your business.'

Takahime said, 'If that be the case, I shall accept your offer without reserve; come now, Aohiko, you must follow me, and be sure to keep your eyes cast in all directions, that you may not be caught off your guard.'

Momijihime replied, 'We are followers of the Ananai-kyō, who abide by the path of righteousness; pray, do not trouble yourself, for there are no traps to be found here, nor have we constructed any hidden dens like the Makutsugahara in the depths of the earth; pray, be at ease and settle your heart in peace.'

Takahime said, 'It was a most pitiable fate that befell your master the other day; I pray you, let me at least pay my respects to his spirit, for it seems that even by believing in the Ananai-kyō, one cannot prevail against the Akuma; I heard that he was captured by the subordinates of Onikumohiko of Ōeyama and put to a cruel death, and the very hearing of it brings tears to my eyes, let alone for you, his Niyōbau, whose sorrow I can well imagine.'

With these words, she secretly moistened her eyes with saliva and began to wail with a hollow, feigned grief. Aohiko stood agape, staring fixedly at the face of Momijihime. Takahime replied, 'The Ananai-kyō was good in its origin, but now it is utterly worthless; the true and essential foundation of the Ananai-kyō is the living shrine of the Hinode-no-Kami, and I, this Takahime, know everything from the very beginning of the opening of this world unto the end of eternity, for such is the Uranai-kyō; therefore, without asking anyone, I am well aware of the calamity that befell your master, Akiyama-hiko; is the Uranai-kyō not a magnificent teaching?'

Momijihime said, 'To treat my husband Akiyamahiko, who is not even dead, as a Shinin is indeed the act of a most wise and great Kamisama; Akiyamahiko is alive and well.'

Takahime replied, 'You speak such Rikutsu because you do not know the Innen of your own Mitama, but after he was once captured at Ōeyama and died, I, this Takahime, cast the Iki of the Denokami from an Enkaku place and administered an injection of Shinrei, by which he was Ikikahe; now, change your heart and convert to the Uranai-kyō.'

From the inner chamber, the voice of Akiyamahiko was heard, saying, 'Momijihime, Momijihime.'

Answering 'Hai,' Momijihime bowed lightly to the two and proceeded into the inner chamber.

As the two watched the back of Momijihime as she departed, they turned their eyes to a framed tablet and saw the edge of a key appearing from behind it. Takahime stood up, took it in her hand, and saw it inscribed as the key to the treasure house of Kanmurijima and Kutsujima. Takahime smiled with a grin, thinking that with this, her great vow would be fulfilled, and she quickly tucked it into her bosom with an indifferent face, while Aohiko began to tremble violently.

Aohiko said, 'Excuse me, Takahime-sama, wh-what are you doing? I cannot comprehend why you would place the heirlooms of this house into your own bosom.'

Takahime replied, 'Shh, what a man lacking in flexibility you are; it is the command of the Hinodewake, and I have clearly perceived with my divine vision that this house possessed the key to Kanmurijima and Kutsujima, and I have come specifically to unearth it; come, come, while it is still night, let us depart from this place, prepare a boat, and cross to Kanmurijima.'

She then attempted to lead the way.

Aohiko said, 'We must at least offer our greetings to the people of this house; I feel somehow uneasy about this.'

Takahime replied, 'Oh, you are slow to understand; this is no time for dawdling, for the time is pressing and brooks no delay; a great achievement does not regard minor details, and a wife does not regard her husband; for the sake of the fulfillment of the divine nation, bathe in the rain and comb with the wind, and come quickly with the great activity of a lion in fury.'

With these words, they slipped out of the house through the back gate. Not a single person within the residence of Akiyamahiko noticed that the two had fled. The two rushed to the port of Yura, secured a small boat, and while Aohiko handled the scull and Takahime rowed with the oar, they rowed with all their might out onto the moonlit sea.
