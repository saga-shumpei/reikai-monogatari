Chapter 8: 衣懸 Pine [598]

衣懸松〔598〕

§ 1

Two man and woman appeared near Dōjigafuchi, which had become close to the main castle of Ōeyama, and crawling out from the earth once more, they gazed upon the entrance of the Ihato.

Aohiko said, 'Ah, Takahime-sama, it appears that before we knew it, our entrance has been blocked by such a Chibiki rock. Fortunately, we have emerged through this escape hole, but had this hole not existed, we would have been forced to meet our end, trapped like spirits in a bottle, together with the wretches of the Onihiko faction whose souls were extracted by the Ananai-kyō. I wish to find some way to remove this rock.'

Takahime replied, 'Ho ho ho, this is surely the Goshiyugo of the Kamisama of the Uranai-kyō. When the Jisetsu arrives in due time, this rock shall surely Shōmetsu like ice melting on a spring day. There is no doubt that the Henjiyōniyoshi of the Mizu no Mitama has committed this Itazura. You need not worry at all.'

Aohiko said, 'Even if you say so, how could this massive rock possibly melt? Whether we push or pull, it would not budge an inch, even with the strength of a hundred or a thousand men.'

Takahime said, 'Oh, look at Aohiko-sama’s pale face; if you worry over such a trifle, the Shinsei-jōju cannot be accomplished. Now that you have transformed into Yukinarihiko-no-Mikoto who appeared in the holy land of Jerusalem, please make your heart a little more courageous.'

Aohiko said, 'Even so, if we do not remove this rock, we shall never be able to enter or exit the rocky cavern of the earth again. Getting out was a struggle, and I emerged with the thin skin of my chest scraped away, but entering is utterly impossible. Is this not a matter of immediate urgency? If the great forces of Onikumohiko were to appear here at any moment, what would become of us? Alas, how uncertain is our present state.'

He returned to a state of dejection. Takahime laughed heartily and said,

'Ho ho ho, oh, you foolishly honest Aohiko-sama, are you trembling with your face and neck turned deathly pale? That is why it cannot be helped if the world calls you Aokubi. Will you not be a little more resolute? What is there to fear from Onikumohiko?'

Aohiko said, 'Yet Onikumohiko is the great leader of the Brahmanism; was it not for fear of that fellow that we dug this rocky cavern here as a preparation for such an emergency?'

Takahime said, 'I once thought so, but now that the day has come, with the strategies in my heart, Takahime has already made most of Onikumohiko’s men our own; do not worry so much. You, too, should reform your heart a little and become of an Oni-spirit.'

Aohiko: 'Nay, if we are to side with such an Akuma, we shall have none of it; we shall take our leave from this day forth.'

Takahime: 'Ho, ho, ho! Now that it has come to this, think not that you can escape, for I shall not let you flee, not even to the Konrin Naraku. Unbeknownst to you, I have bound both your body and your Mitama with the cord of a Yumaki. Though you may speak of fleeing, you shall find it impossible; if you wish to flee, pray, do so as you please. The Hosohimo I have cast is stronger even than a chain of iron; is it not said that a single strand of a woman’s hair can tether a Taizau? Since I have bound you with the Shitahimo, it is of no avail to struggle. Ho, ho, ho!'

Aohiko: 'Until now, I had believed your Kyō to be greater than the Ananai-kyō, and I had intended to thoroughly chastise the Mitama of the Henjiyōniyoshi, to make every one of your Buka the captives of the Uranai-kyō, and to guide them toward good and save them; yet this is a grave Atechiga. I wish to hear your Honshin: are you of good or of evil?'

Takahime: 'There are Kami who appear as good while working evil, and there are Kami who appear as evil while working good. That you have followed me until now without understanding the Zenakujiyasei... yours is a Mitama for which I have lost all patience. Ho, ho, ho, ho!'

Aohiko: 'If that be so, does the Uranai-kyō appear as good while working evil, or does it appear as evil while working good? Which is the truth?'

Takahime: 'Ah, how dull of understanding you are! To speak of evil is to speak of those who settle matters with precision in all things. To speak of good is to gain profit through the toil of others; in short, it is to have a feast set before one and to take up the chopsticks without having endured any labor.'

Aohiko: 'I understand even less; your words...'

Takahime: 'If one is strong in good, one is also strong in evil; as for me, whether it be good or otherwise, I care not for such things. When the authorities come upon a band of thieves, to those thieves, the authorities are the greatest of villains; when the police break into a place where men are gambling in secret, to the gamblers, those police are the greatest of villains. When you and I are whispering at the foot of a bridge in the dark of night, and the moon of Ananai opens the gates of the clouds to peer down, that moon is the greatest Akuma to us. If you cannot understand even this much, how can the Uranai-kyō ever be opened? You are thinking entirely like a person of the seventeenth or eighteenth century, hundreds of thousands of years in the future. At the very least, reform yourself into a human of the late nineteenth or early twentieth century, to whom good and evil are unfathomable. Ah, how dull of understanding you are! It has been a laborious task to forge even a single pillar of a Kami. Though I, this Takahime, who has been called both man and woman since my youth, long to tell you of the one-rin mechanism hidden in my heart, it cannot yet be revealed to you. Ah, what a troublesome matter this has become.'

Aohiko clasped his hands together and remained for a while lost in Shian.

Takahime said, 'Ah, it is Shikata ga nai; to deal with such an incomprehensible Kamubashira as you makes my shoulders stiff. Ah, it is Shikata ga nai. Come, come, let us return to my hidden dwelling at the foot of the Kinukakematsu, and by drinking sake to restore my Kigen, I shall, in the course of whispering, let you know the truth. If I do so, perhaps you too will Kaiaku a little and your heart will find Ochitsu. The thing called Kaishin is a riddle: it is to say that the true Kyō of Kamususanōnomikoto is a lie, a lie, to devour its Oshihego like Shiramigoro, and then, sticking out one's tongue in secret, to let escape a smile of Kuwaishin. You are not yet evil enough; it is best that you remain to the end in Manshin... indeed... in Manshin. The reverse of Manshin is Kaishin, and the reverse of Kaishin is Manshin; the reverse of Omotekyō is Ural-kyō, and it is when the front and the back become one that the Keirin of Tenchi is carried out.'

Aohiko replied, 'Ah, it has become Masumasu impossible to understand. If that is so, then you are founding a Meishinkyō.'

Takahime said, 'That is so; Meishin is the character for rice with the character for [36789] multiplied upon it. The character for rice is the shape of Ōyashima; because we invade the fortress of Ōyashimahiko Mikoto and fleece the believers, it is what is called Meishinkyō. Ho, ho, ho, the word "to be lost" (mayōta) means to summon the demon of evil. Furthermore, those fellows of the Anabako-kyō are fools, so they say things that make no sense, such as that "to be lost" means to be intoxicated with the true Ma, ah, how Meishin it is, how Meishin it is.'

Aohiko said, 'I have entered Masumasu deeper into the labyrinth.'

Takahime said, 'That is a settled matter. It is natural that we, who have barricaded ourselves in a shrine built in a place with a connection to the character for rice, should be in a labyrinth. The Susanōnomikoto of the Anabako-kyō is a remarkably foolish and honest fellow; he bears the thousand-fold penalty for the sake of the world, exhausting all goodness and exhausting all beauty, and while being called a demon and a heretic by the world, he accepts that bearing the cross is his own heaven-sent duty. Is there any other such fool in the world? If one were to speak of the mirrors of fools in the world, the grand champions would be the fellow who gets carried away and climbs a tree, the fellow who takes upon himself the thousand-fold penalty, and the fellow who walks along the wide road under the eaves of houses, hits his head on a signboard, makes a lump, and barks. ... Onigumohiko is also a great fool. Even if one thinks to proclaim evil from the beginning and commit evil, what of it? Can such a thing succeed? The deeds of those without wisdom are almost all entirely absurd. It is a world of fools who do not know the truth that good and evil are not two, and that right and wrong have the same root. Aohiko, you too have been quite infected by Susanōnomikoto; in this world, everything has a front and a back. To be as Gonbe-rel as Gonbe, to be as Boro-rel as Boro, and after that, to become white and pure is the wise path. You are also quite a fine green gourd, are you not.'

With that, he struck his forehead with a sharp slap.

Aohiko replied, 'Ah, I am truly humbled by your Imishinchiyō Gosetsumei. Now that it has come to this, I shall leave everything to you, no matter what may come.'

Takahime said, 'Ah, indeed, indeed; without such a spirit, Shinkau cannot be attained. Shinkau is one and the same with the heart of Renbo; it is the Renai between man and woman expanded to its Kiyokudo and spread to the scale of the Uchiudai. In love, there is no distinction of Jiyōge, Bishiu, or Zenaku. Is that clear? Do you understand?'

Aohiko replied, 'Yes, from the very root... I understand well.'

Takahime exclaimed, 'Oh, what a Ketsutai reply! It is as if you are chewing on old Furuwata, or as if a Hanu octopus were sucking on something—so lacking in Hagi. Ohohoho! Be that as it may, let us proceed to the hiding place of Kinukakematsu.'

So saying, she took the lead and began to walk with the swiftness of a rotating compass. Aohiko followed her in a state of Fushiyōbushiyō.

The Magami, messengers of Onikumohiko who had appeared Saizen, five men in all, took the lead and, drawing a multitude of the Magun behind them, advanced toward this place. Suddenly, cries were heard; though one peered to see if they came from the right, the left, behind, or before, no figures were visible, only voices, echoing from beneath one's very feet. Onikumohiko, riding a chestnut horse that trotted with a tinkling, mincing gait, reined in his steed and cried out in a great voice:

'Ho, ho, you men! Remove this rock. In the depths of this earth lies a vast cavern. It appears that the two propagandists of the Uranai faith, Takahime and Aohiko, are hiding there in secret with a multitude of people. Quickly, remove this rock!'

As he bellowed this command, the multitude of Magami swarmed toward the giant boulder from all directions, like ants gathering upon a rice cake. Yet, against the rock, which was like a small mountain and weighed, one knew not how many millions of kan, they could do nothing at all.

Onikumohiko, growing impatient, leapt down from his horse himself and brought forth a thick rope woven from human hair; he hooked it onto the crag, and all together, with voices joined in unison, they pulled with cries of 'Eiya, Eiya!' Though they pulled and pulled, the rock would not budge; it did not lift its waist even by the breadth of an ant's courier. From within, the voices of many people were heard drawing nearer with every passing moment. Just then, there appeared three propagandists, one man and two women, advancing while waving flags of the Jūyō and singing the song of propaganda, raising voices so great that they seemed to shake heaven and earth.

Kamehiko cried out, 'Ho, ho, you scoundrels of Onikumohiko's faction! Your fortune is spent. I am Kamehiko, who celebrates the ten thousand generations, and these are Princess Hanako and Princess Etsuko, who illuminate the dark night; we are the three propagandists of the Sangokyō. Taste the pure Kototama unique to the Sangokyō, which shakes heaven and earth with a single word and commands the wind, rain, thunder, and lightning with a single cry!'

No sooner had they spoken than they chanted the Kazūta of Ama, and the Sannin simultaneously extended their Migite, emitting from the tips of their Hitosashiyubi a Goshiki Reikuō, which they directed upon the assembly like a searchlight; whereupon even the formidable Onikumohiko abandoned his horse, and tumbling and rolling in his Itsushiyōkenmei, fled toward the Honjiyō of Ōeyama, vanishing like clouds and mist.
