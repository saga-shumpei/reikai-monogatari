Chapter 12: 捜索隊 [602]

捜索隊〔602〕

§ 1

Akiyamahiko, the administrator of the Hitogo of Yura port, gathered many of his household retainers in a secluded room with a fine view, and while gazing at the full moon rising at that moment, held a celebratory feast for the subjugation of Onikumohiko of Ōeyama.

In the deep azure sky, there was not a single shadow of a cloud; the stars were sparse, and the moon was clear and cool, as if a silver mirror were suspended in the heavens.

Akiyamahiko spoke: 'Ah, what a fine moon. Though there are many moons in every month, the moon of this night is the moon to behold; it is a more beautiful and pleasant feeling than even the moon of the mid-autumn festival. In the joy of subjugating the Akuma, the countenance of Miroku-sama appears to be smiling. To gaze upon such a beautiful and noble moon and hold a moon-viewing banquet seems truly extravagant. Yet, this is the Kuōdaimuhen Gojikuō of the Miroku-Nokami. Without distinction of Kisen or age, there is no one who does not feel pleasure in gazing at the moon. No matter how magnificent one may say the Nichirin-sama is, there is no one who drinks sake while looking at the Nichirin-sama in the midst of the day, nor is it possible to perform moon-viewing of the sun. If one were to gaze at the sun in the mid-heaven for a while, one's eyes would instantly be dazzled. Even seeing this, the distinction in the workings of the moon and sun is clear. I have heard that Susanoo-Ōkami is the spirit of the moon; truly a noble, beautiful, and benevolent countenance. Where is Momijihime? I wish to let her behold this magnificent figure.'

So saying, he rose from his seat, and from the room next to the entrance, he called out, 'Momijihime, Momijihime, Momijihime.'

Momijihime, at the voice of her husband, left the two guests waiting and appeared at the moon-viewing seat, and upon informing Akiyamahiko of the visit of the propagandists of the Uranai religion, Akiyamahiko's complexion changed instantly.

'What, the visit of the propagandists of the Uranai religion? That is a serious matter; it would be best to greet them politely and send them away safely. No, Momijihime, you must refuse the guests at the entrance as quickly as possible.'

Momijihime felt caught between the two propagandists and Akiyamahiko, and when she finally appeared at the entrance, the figures of the two were nowhere to be seen. Wondering at this, as she looked around the surroundings, she noticed that the tassel of the jewel-key, which had been hidden behind the lintel, was not visible; startled, she examined the back of the lintel, and behold, how could this be, the key to the treasure house of Kaburashima and Kutsujima, which had been kept there until just before, had been stolen by someone. Momijihime, surprised and flustered, entered the inner palace and fearfully informed her husband, Akiyamahiko, of the loss of the jewel-key, to which Akiyamahiko...

"Lo, a great calamity! Is this not the doing of the two Sendenshi? Ho, you men, this is no time for a Shiyuen! Let the women search every corner of the Keidai, and let the men rush outside the Monguwai to inquire after the Arika of the Sendenshi and investigate the Umu of the key."

Upon this Gechi, the Amata Danjiyo began to search for the whereabouts of the key, while arranging themselves both within and without the gate.

Akiyamahiko summoned the Monban, Ginkō and Kamekō, to his side and asked, "While you were charged with guarding the Omotemon, did you not notice the Datsushiyutsu of the two Danjiyo? Tell me the Yōsu."

Ginkō replied, "We two, holding our Yakume in Taisetsu regard, have kept watch with eyes as keen as the Niō of the Sanmon, so that we would be aware even of the coming and going of a mouse. Yet, the two Danjiyo who entered earlier have not passed through the Omotemon. They must surely be lurking within the residence; I pray you, conduct a thorough investigation."

Akiyamahiko asked, "What of the back gate?"

Kamekō, scratching his head, answered, "Indeed, of that back gate, I know nothing from root to leaf."

Akiyamahiko said, "To be a Monban means not only the front, but the back gate is also within your charge; is that not why two Monban are stationed there? In all likelihood, they have slipped out through the back gate."

Kamekō replied, "The Omotemon is for all things entering, while the back gate is for all things that are but dregs to pass out."

Akiyamahiko shouted, his face changing color, "Fool! Make haste and search the direction of the back gate!"

As the two approached the entrance of the back gate, they saw something black lying at the threshold; picking it up quickly and gazing upon it, they found it to be the tassel of the jewel key.

Ginkō exclaimed, "Ah, with this, all is well! We were on the verge of letting the two thieves escape and being dismissed from our posts; with this, we may at last offer an excuse."

So saying, he shut the back gate firmly and, in high spirits, proceeded to the living quarters of Akiyamahiko.

Akiyamahiko was leaning against a side pillar, his eyes closed, sunk in deep meditation.

Ginkō, placing the tassel of the jewel key into his bosom and letting a small portion of it show, said, "My Lord, do not be troubled. It is certain that the thieves have fled, but the item they had stolen was left behind at the entrance of the back gate. I, Ginkō, though it was a moonlit night, perceived it with sharp eyes and picked it up; I have brought it here now. Pray, examine it."

He spoke with a spirited air, and Akiyamahiko raised his face, opened his eyes, and filled his countenance with a smile.

What, has the Tamakagi been lost? That is indeed Chiyōdeu; it is a Deka, a Deka! Come, bring it before me at once.

Ginkō pointed with his fingertip to the tassel in his bosom.

Heh, heh, heh, heh! It is exactly as you see; it is a magnificent tassel, made of pure silk, so heavy and lustrous that it feels substantial. With this in hand, there shall be no need for you to be so alarmed.

Akiyamahiko replied, That is Arigata; bring it before me.

Ginkō, shrugging his shoulders, said,

Here it is, pray examine it well.

So saying, he presented it with a Motsutai air.

Akiyamahiko exclaimed, Ah, this is the tassel of the Tamakagi, but where is the key?

Ginkō replied, Dannasama, such a rusted key is of no consequence; pray do not feel Shinpai. If I have but a Hitokire of iron, I shall forge it anew for you. Since you have obtained this magnificent tassel, pray do not concern yourself with such a filthy object.

Akiyamahiko cried, Ah, a Shima! I cannot remain here like this. Ho, Ginkō, Kamekō, make preparations for the ship.

We have understood the Isai, they replied, and departed from that place.

Momijihime searched the Shitsunai thoroughly, but as the Arika of the key remained unknown, she appeared in that place, exhaling a sigh of Tauwaku.

Dannasama, Ikaga shall we do? I have no excuse to offer to Susanōnōokami and Kunitakehikonomikoto.

Akiyamahiko replied, Now that it has come to this, it is of no avail to repeat such complaints. There is no doubt they have crossed to Kammurijima and Kutsujima by ship. We must prepare a ship as quickly as possible, pursue them, and reclaim the key.

At that moment, the front of the house suddenly became clamorous once more. The husband and wife looked at each other, and straining their ears, they listened intently to the state of affairs outside.
