Chapter 9: 法螺の貝 [599]

法螺の貝〔599〕

§ 1

The Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, Kamehiko, who celebrates the Yorozuyo, was fanned by the breath of Kototama, and Onikumohiko, who had fled and scattered like clouds and mist, returned to the Honjiyō with his Inochi Karagara, running with the speed of Idaten.

He gathered the red, white, and blue demons into one Hitoma, and held a Daiheujiyō to seek the whereabouts of Onihiko, Onitora, Ishikuma, and Kumataka.

The altar of the Brahman Kyō remained half-offered, and on the ninth day of the ninth month, a day of suffering and Moga, came unpleasant tidings.

Though the half-moon shone in the Misora, with no recourse for the evening, the sky of the heart was overcast; pitiful indeed was the heart of Onikumo, who was tormented by demons and enveloped in Murakumo.

Onikumohiko opened the inner Hitoma, sat cross-legged on the Jiyōdan, thrust his ivory-like horns upward, opened his crocodile-mouth, and addressed the assembly: 'Today is truly a joyous Kikuzuki viewing banquet; where have Onihiko and the others, whom I sent to gather offerings for the sacrifice on the day of the Brahman Kyō festival, hidden themselves? That they have not returned by now suggests some emergency has occurred. In such circumstances, negligence is a great enemy; prepare your arms one and all, and even if any enemy should come, do not falter, do not yield, but fight well and defend well, scatter the enemy a thousand leagues away, and manifest the divine power of the Brahman Kyō to the world.'

As he gave this command, a dwarf appeared from the midst of the assembly, and while wobbling his misshapen head like a Fukusuke, he approached Onikumohiko with unsteady, tottering steps. The dwarf spoke: 'I report that Onihiko and the other brave generals have had a change of heart and defected to Ananai-kyō, and having rushed up to the Aya no Takama, they intend to lead a great army before many days pass, surround this mountain ten and twenty-fold, attack and crush every one of you, including the commander Onikumohiko, into splinters, and make Ōeyama the stronghold of Ananai-kyō; this is the enemy's strategy. You must leave this place as soon as possible, or lead the entire allied army and advance toward the holy land; if the time is delayed, it will be a great disaster. He who strikes first controls the man; decide your course of action at once.' As he spoke, Onikumohiko folded his arms and was intent upon the continuous emission of his blue-breath sighs. Just then, there was a clamor of voices at the front gate; Onikumohiko stood up himself and appeared at the gate, and when he fixed his eyes and gazed, behold, what was this? The four heavenly kings, Onihiko, Onitora, Kumataka, and Ishikuma, were returning in high spirits, having many followers carry their Ajiro palanquins. Onikumohiko felt a sudden relief in his heart.

と述べ立つるにぞ、鬼雲彦は両手を組み青息吐息の連続的発射に余念なかりき。時しもあれや、表門にガヤガヤとさざめく人声、鬼雲彦は自ら立つて表門に立ち現はれ、屹と目をすゑ眺むれば、こは抑如何に、鬼彦、鬼虎、熊鷹、石熊の四天王は数多の従卒に網代の駕籠を舁つがせながら意気揚々と帰り来る。鬼雲彦はハツと胸を撫で下ろし、

"Ha! Atsupa, Atsupa! Thou, Onihiko, Onitora, Kumataka, and Ishikuma, well hast thou returned in safety. What of the prey?"

Onihiko, shrugging his shoulders and twitching his nose, replied: "To the Commander Onikumohiko I make report. In the first place, we led the subordinates and confronted the mansion of Akiyamahiko at the port of Yura. Even that gate, so stoutly built, I reached out my right hand and pushed with a single cry, and with a crash, the strength being excessive, I leaped some four or five ken into the interior of the gate at the very moment of pushing—what peril, nay, what delight! Following me, the many subordinates entered, dividing themselves to the four quarters, and from the entrance, the storehouse, the water gate, the shed, and the firewood room, they raised a war cry and burst in. Susanoo-no-Mikoto, that peerless hero, and Kunitakehiko and the others who followed him, seemed at first to be struck with terror and fled in confusion, but suddenly they regained their power. Taking the thousand-pulling rock as a plaything, they caused the very earth to seem as if it would split, and with a thud, a thud, a rain of rocks fell, and in an instant, they built a mountain of rock, said to be a ri in diameter, before the gate of Akiyamahiko. Yet, our warriors, led by Onihiko, who were not in the least daunted, stood at the forefront, and when I lifted one foot and kicked that solid rock mountain, it crumbled with a crash; and the moment I lifted my left foot and kicked it again, the mansion of Akiyamahiko danced up into the mid-heaven. I, not content with this, grasped a great rock of tens of thousands of kan as if it were a handball, and hurled it toward the sea like rain and hail; the deep sea of a thousand fathoms resounded with a splash, the volume of water increased, and at last, a great island appeared. Ha! Since the creation of heaven and earth, has there ever been such a hero in heaven or on earth? How can such a mighty hero blindly follow a commander like Onikumohiko? When I reflect upon my own self, it becomes utterly ridiculous. Nevertheless, today is the festival day of the Brahman religion; however heroic one may be, one cannot prevail against the Gods, and so I have returned just now to offer my respects."

Kumataka, again waving his great hands and stamping his feet upon the earth, said: "I, without being surprised by the absolute and infinite divine power of Onihiko, crossed swords with the God Susanoo-no-Mikoto. As we challenged and fought each other with the thousand-pulling rock, the Mikoto was daunted by my fierce power, and watching for the moment he staggered two or three steps, I flew in like a bird, and without letting him speak, I wound my arms, harder than iron, around his back and bound him fast. I thrust him into a wicker palanquin and had the guards keep strict watch over him. While I was inquiring as to the whereabouts of the peerless and powerful Kunitakehiko, a great man appeared; this was surely Kunitakehiko. Before I could show him the power of Kumataka, I clenched my fist and struck out in all directions, and as I aimed at the side of Kunitakehiko's face and struck him one blow, his head danced up into the mid-heaven, and with a splash that resounded far across the Japan Sea, he vanished into water and spray."

§ 3

Onitora once again stamped his feet in the Shiko, and spoke: 'I proceeded toward the mansion of Akiyamahiko, and as I observed the Yōsu, I saw that Onihiko, one of the Shitenō, and Kumataka were facing Kamususanō no Ōkami and Kunitakehiko; their Shishi-Funjin vigor was fierce, and they exhausted their Hijiyutsu in Chiyōchiyōhatsushi, striking at the Jiyōdan and Gedan; when they swept the lower, they leapt into the Chiuten, and when they swept the upper, they concealed their bodies in the Nesoko no Kuni, flying about the Tenchi and Shihau with Jiiujizai, their Denkuōsekikuwa Daikuwatsudō being truly Mezama. I, being one of the Shitenō myself, thought to show them what I could do, and no sooner had I bared my Shiri and fired off an Itsupatsu, than thick clouds and smoke enveloped the surroundings, creating a pitch darkness where black and white could not be distinguished; they were caught in their own trap, and thinking this unbearable, I tightened my breath from below my navel and blew it out with a sudden puff; and behold, what is this? The enemies and allies who had been fighting so magnificently here until now had vanished without a trace, and pointing toward this side of Ōeyama, the palanquins, the men, and everything else were scattering as they raced through the void; ah, how grateful, how grateful, is the divine power of the Brahman religion so noble as this? I shall make use of this momentum to return to the main castle of Ōeyama, and if I bare my backside to the commander Onikumohiko and release a fart, the mansion together with him shall dance up into the Chiuten and fall headlong into the Wadano-hara, instantly transforming into a ship; there is no need for the trouble of moving house, come now, shall I bare it once and see, Great Commander Onikumohiko?'

He shrugged his shoulders and let out a roar of triumph. Ishikuma, who was known as one of the Shitenō, once again swung his arms and struck his chest with a thud, saying: 'I received orders from the Great Commander from this castle, and leading many minor warriors, I arrived at the mansion of Akiyamahiko, and found it in the midst of the very same chaotic revelry that the three have just described; thinking it uninteresting to follow in the footsteps of others' achievements, I spread my legs and flew in a single leap to the country of Chōsen, and when I set one foot upon Danzan in Kyōshō-dō, the hiding place of Kamususanō no Ōkami, the mansion and all were reduced to ruins, and the guardian deities, terrified by this, scattered like clouds and mist; I took a step across and set my foot upon the palace in the Saien of the Ubusuna mountain range, trampled the Caucasus mountains, and as I stretched my back, I felt a sharp pain in my forehead as it struck something with a thud; looking closely, it was the great sun shining in the heavens, and thinking this convenient, I forcibly stuffed it into my bosom and returned, only to find, whether it be a dream, reality, or an illusion, a scene in this place that I cannot comprehend, and it was amusing to see the minor warriors gathered together and deliberating upon our whereabouts, ah ha ha ha.'

と肩を怒らし雄猛びをする。四天王の一人と聞えたる石熊は、又もや腕を振り胸をドンドンと打ちながら、

§ 4

He blew the Hora, and shrouded the assembly in smoke.

Onikumohiko gazed intently at the faces of the four, and said, 'Yea, yea, have you four gone mad? Your report is as vague and empty as grasping at clouds; be that as it may, bring the Ajiro-kago to this place, for I shall inspect the heads of the enemies one by one.'

Onihiko replied, 'Nay, it is by no means vague or empty; all is the Gotakusen of the Fukushiyugojin, and the Kanjinkaname Gohonzon has already submitted to the Ananai-kyō.'

'What? You say they have submitted to the Ananai-kyō? For what reason is that?'

Onihiko said, 'This was indeed a Shitsugen. It was a slip of the tongue; they have not submitted to the Ananai-kyō, but rather the Ananai-kyō has submitted to them. Aha, ha, ha, ha!'

Onikumohiko, his countenance overflowing with Tokui, pulled open the door of the Ajiro-kago roughly and peered inside; and lo, how could this be? His Saiai wife, Onikumohime, lay there, her Gotai slashed into pieces, covered in blood, and dead in her nakedness.

Again, from the four Kago appeared blood-stained men and women; looking closely, they were his own Saiai son and daughter.

The son and daughter, bearing dozens of wounds upon their bodies, squeezed out voices like the chirping of insects, and saying, 'Father, it is a great pity,' they fell flat upon that spot, and their whole bodies grew cold, becoming as ice.

The moon began to shine brightly, and the surroundings were as luminous as day; the sleepy crows flew about in the mid-heaven, crying out 'Aho, aho'.
