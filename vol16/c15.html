Chapter 15: Valley 間の祈 [605]

谷間の祈〔605〕

§ 1

Kamehiko, Hidekohime, and Yoshikohime, these Sannin, ascended the current of Yura and arrived at Kaumorieki, where they sat upon a stone by the Michibata to rest their breath. On both right and left, Utsusau Raujiyu grew in Hanmo, and in the bottom of the valley, dark even at midday, a multitude of Rauniyakunanniyo proceeded toward the Yamaoku, Rakueki like ants gathering to sweetness.

Kamehiko seized one among them and spoke: 'That so many people should form a Giyōretsu and journey toward the north, is there some unusual occurrence? I desire to hear of the Yōsu.'

The man replied: 'Yes, yes, I know not what it may be, but it is said that since Nisannichi Izen, a magnificent Kamisama has appeared in the Taniahi of Kensakiyama at the foot of Ōeyama, and all are making pilgrimages with petitions for the Heiyu of Biyōki or the prosperity of Shiyōbai. I myself have no particular petition, but as so many people are visiting and the reputation is great, I am going to see what manner of being it is.'

Kamehiko asked: 'What manner of Kamisama is that, in truth?'

The man answered: 'It is said to be a Kamisama with a name like a tatami-maker, containing the words Ura or Omote. Furthermore, there are intermediaries with names containing the word Ao, such as Aohiko, Aogaeru, or Aotatami, and it is said that if they strike the pilgrims one by one with tree branches, Biyōki is healed instantly and desires are fulfilled; it is, indeed, of great popularity. It is said that for a fashionable Kamisama, if one does not visit quickly there is no divine virtue, so everyone carries a bento and makes a pilgrimage toward the foot of Kensakiyama. Well, you also have a strange appearance; are you not perhaps an intermediary of the Kamisama?'

Kamehiko replied: 'That is so; we also are intermediaries of the way of the Kami.'

The man laughed: 'Ah, even if you say you are intermediaries, you must be raw-smelling intermediaries. The intermediary with the blue name who has appeared in the valley stream in these depths is, they say, a great ascetic who practices purification and will not even allow women to approach. Moreover, you are in a group of three like three birds; who would listen to what you say? It is truly laughable. Hahaha!'

Kamehiko said: 'By no means, by no means are they my wives or anything of the sort. Each is an independent missionary of the way of the teachings. You are troubled because you immediately assume such things whenever a man and woman walk together. Those called ordinary mortals are shallow and wretched creatures.'

The man retorted: 'Hee, you speak well indeed. It is a world where there are sages among ordinary mortals, and ordinary mortals who appear to be sages. Do not call me an ordinary mortal so much. You, a missionary of the ordinary mortal, a fool. Well, you three birds, stay calmly by the Michibata and play at being intimate. Ah, having become involved with such false missionaries, my companions have already gone off somewhere ahead.'

§ 2

They ran off at full speed, Ichimokusan, and proceeded deeper and deeper into the interior.

Kamehiko said, 'Hidekohime, judging from the words of the man just now, it seems to be about Aohiko of the Uranai-kyō. If he were to spread the Uranai-kyō once again, leading the Sejin astray and spreading Gaidoku, we, as the Sendenshi, would fail in our duty toward the Shinkai; shall we therefore go and conduct a Jitsuchi Chōsa?'

Hidekohime replied, 'Indeed, it is not a journey of great haste, so let us investigate. This, too, may be some Oshigumi of the Kamisama.'

Yoshikohime said, 'That would be most Omoshiro. Having kept such silence since Senjitsu, I feel as though insects are breeding in my mouth, and it is Fukuwai and unbearable. Dōzo, let me handle the Kausefu this Kondo. I shall show you my Funtō with all the power of the Kototama.'

Hidekohime said, 'Ah, that too shall be Omoshiro.'

Kamehiko said, 'Come, let us go. Let us see the Yūben and the Funsen of Yoshikohime.'

With these words, the Sannin mingled with the Gunshifu and proceeded northwards along the Yamamichi, where it was dark even in the daytime.

The party mingled with the Gunshifu and at last arrived at the bank of the valley stream flowing at the foot of Mount Kensen. Upon the rock walls of this valley stream were natural water vessels filled with beautiful water, called the Ubugama and the Ubutarai. Aohiko, the Sendenshi of the Uranai-kyō, stood by the side of this waterfall basin in his austere white garments, scooping up the water of the valley stream with a ladle and sprinkling it upon branches of brushwood, healing the illnesses of many men and women, young and old, performing various forms of divine divination, and deceiving the multitude of men and women.

Yoshikohime turned to Kamehiko and Hidekohime and said, 'Now, as promised, I shall take the stage alone; you, taking advantage of the coming dusk, hide in the shade of the trees and observe my activities.'

Having said this, she vanished into the deep forest, leaving them behind.

Aohiko stood sternly by the side of the water vessel, imparting his teachings to the many people.

One man, clapping his hands, knelt timidly before Aohiko and said, 'I have one request for you, O Living Kamisama. I suffer all year round from the pain of colic. I beseech you, by your divine virtue, to save me. If it can be cured by medicine, what medicine would be best? I desire your instruction on this as well.'

Aohiko replied, 'There is no ailment, whether it be colic or otherwise, that cannot be cured. It depends entirely upon your repentance. Abandon the San'iku-kyō, which is popular these days, as quickly as possible, and become a believer in the Kamisama of the Uranai-kyō. From that day forth, your colic will be completely cured, as if it were a lie.'

So saying, he took out a flint and struck fire, gathering the withered brushwood nearby and kindling a vigorous blaze.

The faces of all were illuminated as if it were day, yet the figure of Etsuko-hime, hidden in the thicket of trees, remained unseen. Etsuko-hime, still unyielding, cried out,

Startled by the light of the bonfire, a swarm of hornets that had built their nest nearby, unable to endure the flames licking their nest, began to attack with all their might, numbering in the hundreds and thousands, aiming for the glowing eyes of Aohiko.

Aohiko cried, 'Aita!'

And he fell to the ground. The swarm of bees bit into every part of Aohiko's body, leaving no space untouched.

The Ichidō were astonished by this voice, and at the same time filled with joy; while clapping their hands toward the direction of the voice, fearing to make a Nagai, they departed Ichimokusan along the river. Ah, what is the fate of Aohiko?

『ヤアヤア此処に集まる老若男女、穢らはしき肉体を持ち乍ら、此の聖場を汚すこと不届千万な、一時も早く神界に謝罪をせよ。三五教の宣伝歌を唱へ奉り、蜂の災禍を払ひ与へむ。惟神霊幸倍坐世』

と唱ふる声につれ、一同は一生懸命になりて、

『惟神霊幸倍坐世、惟神霊幸倍坐世』

と唱へ始めける。

此の声は四辺の山岳をも揺がす許りなり。暗中より、

『ヤアヤ、汝等蜂に刺された目は、これで全快したであらう。聖場を汚してはならないから、一刻も早く此の場を立去り、綾の高天へ御礼のために時を移さず参詣致せよ。夢々疑ふな。惟神霊幸倍坐世』

一同は此の声に驚き、且つ歓び、声する方に向つて拍手し乍ら、長居は恐れと一目散に河伝ひに帰り往く。アヽ青彦の運命は如何。
