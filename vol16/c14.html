Chapter 14: 鵜呑鷹 [604]

鵜呑鷹〔604〕

§ 1

Kamehiko, while rowing the oar, faced the sea breeze and sang a Funauta with delight:

Kamehiko: 'If I cross Matsubara to see Tanabe, the fog that hides Tanabe gathers.'

He finally passed Takeshima, Bakuchigamisaki, and the Shirokuroiha before his eyes, gazed at Matsubara on his Mete, and viewed Jiyajima and Hiroshima on his Yunde, until at last, as the twilight of the Jifushichiya passed, he arrived safely at the port of Tanabe.

In the darkness of the evening where one could not distinguish even a Shiseki, Takahime and Aohiko, unable to wait for the boat to be moored in a Yokozu manner, leaped up nimbly and hid their figures, blending into the darkness.

Kamehiko: 'Ya, is Takahime not here? Where is Aohiko... Onitakehiko-sama, what shall we do?'

From within the darkness, the voices of Aohiko and Takahime rang out:

'Aha, hoho, many thanks to you; do you think we shall hand over this jewel?... Everyone, farewell, adieu.'

With such Reichōteki Kuwaisei, they vanished, blending into the darkness, no one knew where.

Onitakehiko and Kamehiko immediately leaped from the boat and lamented:

'Ah, we have had a Shitsupai. Had we but moored at the port of Yura, this would not have happened... It was my own Fukaku to have followed the words of Takahime and moored at Tanabe... Eh, there is no Shikata. Even if we were to pursue them, it is pitch black; let us first return to the mansion of Akiyama-hiko and consult with him.'

Speaking thus with no strength, they pointed toward the port of Yura and continued their journey on foot; just as the party returned to a point slightly before the port of Yura, the moon of the Jifushichiya, rising to illuminate the eastern sky, revealed its elliptical form between the pine trees, appearing as if it were mocking the entire group.

A cold wind, resembling a wintry blast, struck their faces without mercy, and the trees on all sides blew like flutes out of season, sounding as if they were jeering at the party's failure.

Kamehiko: 'Ah, this is ominous; it is as if I have merely served as an attendant to Takahime. Even if Takahime should soar through the heavens or burrow into the earth, shall I not seek her whereabouts and retrieve the Nyoi-hōju jewel?'

So saying, he stamped his feet in the middle of the main road, and finally sat cross-legged, refusing to move.

Onitakehiko said: 'Ya, Kamehiko-dono, now that it has come to this, it is a matter that will not return even if we repent. There is no other path but to part the grass, search for their whereabouts, and retrieve the jewel. However, I have no face to meet Akiyama-hiko; I shall take my leave from here.'

Before he had finished speaking, he turned into white smoke and hid his figure.

The dozen or so retainers of Akiyama-hiko stood in a state of bewilderment, wondering what to do, each folding their arms, and the sound of their sighs did not cease for a while.

§ 2

Kō said, 'Pray, Kamehiko-sama, if you remain so indifferent, what are we to do? How shall we return to the mansion and offer our apologies to our Shiyujin? We have no way to make our Haukoku.'

Kamehiko replied, 'Is it not sufficient to report the Aritei as it is? I shall return to the mansion of Akiyamahiko no more. Return with haste and convey this to the Shiyujin and his Fūfu, as well as to Hidekohime and Yoshikohime.'

Kō replied, 'That is too much... The Shiyujinsama and the two Onnasendenshi are waiting with necks stretched long in anticipation. Regardless of what follows, pray return just this once.'

Kamehiko remained silent.

Otsu said, 'That is why I always say that the Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō are Koshinuke. Have you seen the Subashiko nature of the Sendenshi of the Uranai-kyō? Even when confined within the rocks, they conduct such Danpan. While having a blade pressed against their Nodomoto, did they not employ a Meuan and Kisaku to cut the throat of the one who held them with that very blade? Because we followed such a Garakuta Sendenshi as Kamehiko, we have been made to expose such unprecedented shame to heaven and earth. Ah, how can we ever face our Shiyujin again?'

Hei said, 'It is Shikata that you say such things, but it is like counting the age of a dead child. In all things, resignation is essential. It is a world where the wicked prosper and the good decline; it is no wonder that the good are deceived. We are all the more impressed by the righteousness of the Ananai-kyō. Come, Kamehiko-sama, do not say such things and let us return with haste.'

Kamehiko said, 'Come, let us go. I merely wished to probe what opinions you held. What of it? Even if the jewel is taken, it is hidden somewhere. It is unlikely they have hidden it to the very depths of hell. I shall ride into the headquarters of the Uranai-kyō and, without allowing them a word of protest, I shall seize it back. In any case, it is a matter of time. All of you, leave everything to Kamehiko. Do not worry. Come, let us go.'

Leading the way with vigor, while performing Naohi, Minaoshi, Kikinaoshi, and Norinaoshi, the party of fifteen or sixteen set out, pointing toward the mansion of Akiyamahiko at the Yura no Minato, and arrived back with swift steps. As Kamehiko stood at the front and attempted to pass through the main gate with a weary air, the gatekeeper Ginkō appeared upon the scene.

'Hail, Sendenshi-sama Kamehiko! A great achievement, a great achievement! Thanks to you, the Nyoi-hōju jewel and the key, which were once snatched by the enemy, have been successfully recovered; surely our Shiyujinsama must be overjoyed. The Shiyujin rejoices, the Okusama rejoices, and above all, you rejoice, and those who followed you rejoice; I, too, rejoice with you. As expected of a Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō, I am truly struck with admiration. Within, to celebrate your success, a feast of various delicacies from sea and mountain has been prepared, and the Danna-sama is in high spirits, awaiting your arrival. We, too, cannot contain our joy for you.'

§ 3

With eager haste and shoulders swaying, he was in high spirits. Kamehiko offered a slight bow, and with slow, heavy steps, proceeded toward the inner chambers.

Ginkō: 'Hey, Ihakō, Ichikō, how did it go? It must have been quite amusing, I suppose.'

Ihakō shrugged his shoulders and replied:

'It is a settled matter. Since Ihakō, the peerless man of great strength, made his appearance, capturing one or two Takahime is as easy as passing wind. Yet, the Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō are also quite something. They have gone and had the jewel snatched away, you see...'

Ginkō: 'What? Snatched away? Is that truly the case?'

Ihakō: 'Well, it is not exactly that it was snatched... rather, well... it was taken.'

Ginkō: 'Which side took it?'

Ihakō: 'Well, the one who took it, took it. It is as if to say the one who had it taken from them, had it taken... is that not so?'

Ginkō: 'Takahime must have made a strange face when the jewel she had gone to such lengths to seize was snatched back, did she not?'

Ihakō: 'Indeed, that is so. ...As it was in the darkness of the first degree, where one could not tell even if one's nose were pinched, I know not what face she made; yet, one side was in high spirits, while the other was crestfallen, like a sheep being led to the Toshiyo. It was a pitiable state of affairs, indeed.'

Ginkō: 'Well, that is excellent. Now that the Niyoihotsushiyu and the key have returned, I wonder if we might partake in plenty of Ihahizake this evening.'

Ichikō: 'I cannot speak too loudly, but it is a total failure.'

Ginkō: 'What do you mean by a total failure?'

Ichikō: 'In any case, it is like an octopus eating a pot; it is a difficult matter where one makes a face like a Fukurodori that has missed its midnight meal, saying "Afun"... the curtain rises from here on.'

The group hurried forward, pointing toward the inner chambers. Akiyama-hiko and his wife, along with Princess Eiko and Princess Etsuko, came out to the entrance to welcome Kamehiko.

Akiyama-hiko: 'We have caused you a great deal of concern. How do matters stand?'

Kamehiko: 'Yes, indeed. I shall report the details to you now.'

Princess Eiko: 'Please, let us hear the joyful news as quickly as possible. We have been waiting for the time to pass, feeling as if each moment were a thousand autumns. Knowing you, Kamehiko-sama, who are never remiss in anything, and with the deity Onitakehiko accompanying you, there surely could be no untoward clumsiness. ...A great victory... a great banzai... come, pray tell us the interesting outcome quickly.'

Kamehiko: 'I shall now report the details to you.'

§ 4

Before the words were even uttered, he stripped to the waist, and with a hand so swift it defied the eye, he drew his double-edged Tantau and plunged it deep into his left Wakibara, beginning to gouge the flesh. Hidekohime, struck with terror, clung to his hand and cried out, 'Alas, Kamehiko-dono, do not be so hasty!'

From beneath the strain of agonizing breath, Kamehiko replied, 'To bid me not be hasty is a cruelty; for I have allowed the sole Otakara of Kamususanōnōokami to be deceived and stolen by Takahime of the Uranai Kyō, and I have no face to present myself before Him. I have already resolved upon death; do not hinder me in vain and cause me further suffering. As for the Isai, pray inquire of Ihakō, Ichikō, and Isokō. I, Soregashi, shall cut open my belly to apologize for this Shitsupai. Farewell to you all.'

No sooner had he spoken than he exerted his strength for one final Hitoegu, and in an instant, his breath departed. Hidekohime and Yoshikohime could only shriek, 'Waa!' as they threw themselves upon the Shigai of Kamehiko, weeping prostrate, oblivious to all Zengo. Akayamahiko and his wife blinked in shock, standing in Mokunen, wringing their sleeves with tears of Hitan.

At this moment, a man appeared from the Omotemon, racing toward this scene with the speed of Idaten. Upon looking, it was Onitakehiko, who emerged holding Takahime and Aohiko in his left and right hands, carrying them in the manner of one holding a cat, and declared, 'Hark, everyone, I have captured both Takahime and Aohiko. Since the jewel is surely in the bosom of Takahime, I shall conduct an inquiry from here and retrieve it for you. Everyone, pray be at ease...'

Akayamahiko and his wife were startled a second time and exclaimed, 'Hark, is it Onitakehiko-sama? How well you have come to us. That is truly grateful, yet, even so, Kamehiko-sama, who was in such good spirits until just now, has cut his belly and met his end.' As they wept prostrate, Onitakehiko laughed heartily and said, 'Hark, everyone, do not be concerned; the Senden-shi of Kamehiko shall soon appear in this place.'

'Eh!' cried the assembly in astonishment. The Shigai of Kamehiko rose up, and before their eyes, hair like that of a shaggy dog grew over his entire body; he became a strange beast, neither resembling a gray tiger nor a bear, and began to crawl out slowly, heading toward the Omotemon. The assembly felt as though they were dreaming within a dream, unable to utter a single word, and for a while, they could only gaze at one another's faces.

To such a place appeared the true Kamehiko, who said with a smile, 'Hark, Onitakehiko-sama, I have caused you great worry. Given that it was a dark night, I was for a time in a state of panic and confusion, wondering where they had lurked and hidden, but thanks to the grace of the seventeen-day moon shining in the eastern sky, and thanks to the grace of Miroku-sama, I have obtained the object of my purpose, and there is nothing more grateful than this. ...Ah, Akayamahiko and his wife, Hidekohime, and Yoshikohime-dono, pray be at ease.'

§ 5

『如何に立派な神でも、無茶には叶はぬ。……サアサア青彦、一先づ此場を逃げたり逃げたり』

§ 9

He urges them on. Aohiko, in a state of Urota, loses his Nigemichi and circles the same spot in a Kuwaiten. Kamehiko presses the attack with ever-increasing intensity. Akiyamahiko commands:

"Ah, if it has come to this, it is a desperate gamble. Momijihime, take up the Naginata!"

At this Hitokoe, before Momijihime could even grasp the Naginata from the Nageshi, she cried out,

"Takahime of Akugiyakubudau, prepare thyself!"

As she struck, Takahime dodged to the right and to the left, parrying for a time with her fan; yet, unable to withstand the Shiukuwa, she suddenly transformed into Hakuen and fled with Ichimokusan through the Tenjiyōmado, aiming for the Seihoku Ten, trailing a belt of clouds across the Chiukū, and vanished Ichihaya from sight. Thereafter, Aohiko stood trembling violently, like Aona withered by salt.

Kamehiko declared, "Ah, there is no point in dealing with such a Yowamushi. I shall spare thee. Come, come, depart from this place at once!"

The feast, prepared with such Setsukaku, was trampled and crushed underfoot, ending in total ruin. Onimukehiko suddenly transformed into Hakuen and, once again through the window in the ceiling, trailing a belt of clouds, aimed for the Seihoku Ten and vanished into the mid-heavens, pursuing the trail of Takahime.

Beginning with Akiyamahiko and his wife, Kamehiko, Eikohime, and Etsukohime reverently recited the Amatsu Norito before the altar, finished chanting the propaganda songs, and here bade farewell; thus, the three propagandists ascended the Yuragawa and set forth toward the sacred ground.
