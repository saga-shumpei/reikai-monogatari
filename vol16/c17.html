Chapter 17: Valley の水 [607]

谷の水〔607〕

§ 1

Aohiko, having appeared at the foot of Kensakiyama, sought to establish the Oshihe of divination; while performing ablutions in a Seisen, a clear spring with deep Enyu, in a Ubudarahi and Ubugama dug into the bank of the Tanigaha flowing at the base of the mountain, he saved the Rauniyakunanniyo of Ochikochi and preached the Oshihe of divination.

At that very time, as the sun set and the sky grew dark, Kamehiko pushed through the people and entered; as he stood in the thicket of the forest, listening to the Yōsu of the situation, Yoshikohime, hiding her form, preached the Ananahi Kami no Oshihe from within the darkness, while the Rauniyakunanniyo, startled, bewildered, and suspicious of the Kotosara-turbid Kototama of Aohiko, were anxious to hear the Yurai.

At that moment, the Sumeōkami descended in Kamugaka, and with a pure and cool Kototama, even Aohiko was startled; he took out a Hiuchi, struck fire, and groping about, gathered Kareki and Kareha, and when he ignited the fire, the Atari instantly became as bright as midday; while he was in the midst of groaning and agony, being stung by many bees that had gathered, drawn by the light of the fire, Eiko-hime appeared.

Together with Kamehiko, she broke off small branches of an evergreen pine, and using them as a substitute for an improvised Ōnusa, she put her heart into it and waved them left and right, driving the fierce swarm of hornets into the darkness beyond, thus saving Aohiko from his peril; Aohiko, at last, raised his face and looked around at the Atari, and it was admirable how he wiped away tears of gratitude for the appearance of Kamehiko, whose very name is auspicious, and Eiko-hime, and repenting his past wrongs, he apologized earnestly.

Yoshikohime also appeared, and together they chanted the heavenly number song: One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, hundred, thousand; praising the divine work of Kamehiko, which surpassed that of men, and celebrating the ten thousand generations, they revered the words of the Sumeōkami and, to serve in a shrine of beauty upon the rock, they pounded and solidified the upper foundations and pounded and hardened the lower foundations, brought forth sacred hoes and axes, and felled trees from the great and small gorges; for one hundred days and one hundred nights, they performed ablutions in this Tanigaha, and it was a joyous ridge-raising to save this world, a pillar-standing; gathering various grasses and reeds to make a roof, with Chigi and Katsuogi standing bravely, they served with reverence, which was truly auspicious; appearing in the Makutsu-gahara, they pushed open the Iwato of the heart, and Onikumohiko, who had submitted to the path of the one sincerity, the Ananahi, along with Onitora, who was known as the sword in his bosom, and Onihiko, Ishikuma, and Kumataka, those heroes and men of valor, together with the one hundred men who followed them, repented their past wrongs and appeared bravely in this valley; looking up to Aohiko, who had surrendered, as the Kami of craftsmen, they worked day and night without cease, most vigorously, pounding and solidifying the foundation of a thousand generations, the foundation of Miroku of ten thousand generations, and inviting the divine spirit of the Sumeōkami, they served with solemnity, which is truly noble; it is a joyous tale of the Reikai, the noble age of the Kami, reflecting in the water mirror of the Ama no Manai, the Isuzugawa flowing through the valley of the Moto-Ise, where the light shines, the pure divine form for future ages, as the four spirits are gathered and the sixteen are...

§ 2

Reclining my body within the pavilion of the Makimonogatari Zuishiyō, I contemplate the Nahohi, the Minaho, and the Kikinaho; thus, I penetrate the Genzai, the Kami, and the Iugen, transcending the Kuwako, the Mirai, and the Genzai. These words of the Chōetsu, the Fukakai, and the Iugenbimeu Kototama are like the Shiraume that bloom all at once; in the fragrant Harukaze, they scatter, and thereafter bear fruit—a Monogatari that possesses both flower and fruit. The Gokeirin of the distant Kamiyo, concerning the Manai-ga-take, the Sogabegau, the Toyuke no Kami, and the Susanō, unfolds in the Yayohizora of the eleventh year. The light of the moon clears the Yohiyami of the sky, and as the Tama rises, illuminated by its radiance, the Kuchiguruma emerges from the belly.

While grasping the rudder of the brush, I push the cart straight ahead, knowing not the destination. At the Jifujigai where the vertical and the horizontal intersect, I heed not the hundred slanders of the people of the world who say the Tsujitsuma does not meet. As the Yamataorochi stretches out long, winding to the right and to the left, leaping hither and thither, the procession of frogs moves on without looking ahead. I peel back one layer of skin to reveal the true nature of the Zuishiyō no Rei—a Monogatari that is truly incomprehensible. Ah, Kannagara Kami-Kami, may the spirits be blessed.
