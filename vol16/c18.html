Chapter 18: 遷宅婆 [608]

遷宅婆〔608〕

§ 1

As a result of the bitter toil and arduous suffering of the assembly for one hundred days and one hundred nights, the palace of white wood was finally erected, and the Chinsaishiki having been completed in peace, the assembly proceeded to the feast of Nahorahi. It was the fifteenth day of Shiyōguwatsu; snow fell thick as Gamau, and as far as the eye could reach, the world was one of silver; the shadow of the Amatsu sun cast its light upon the earth, and all was Reirō, with not a single speck of Jinai remaining in Kenkon, such that one might well imagine this to be the beautiful Misono of Tengoku.

Hidekohime, having served as Saishiyu for the Shinrei Chinsai, descended the stairs with composure, when suddenly, moved by the Shinrei, her divine form became endowed with even greater majesty, and opening her mouth slowly, she deigned to proclaim: 'I am the Nigimitama of Amaterasu Ōmikami. Verily, this place is a sacred spiritual ground second only to the holy land of Aya, a field of Keirin for the fire and water of the Shinkai where the Tenjin Chigi gather; it is the source of the river of heavenly spirits in the Shinkai, a most severe and holy divine domain that washes and purifies the evil spirits of the universe and saves the hundred Mitama for the Shinkoku. Furthermore, to the northeast lies Ōeyama; this is called the Akutagawa of the Shinkai, and as it is the source where evil spirits gather and well forth, it is the land with the mission to purify the muddy, evil, and defiled waters that seek to inundate the world with the spiritual springs of the river of spirits. Beyond this turbulent stream lies the mountain of Amano Manai-ga-take, where the spirits of the gods of the Mizu-no-Mitama, who govern the Keirin of heaven and earth that swallows both the pure and the impure, gather as a source. A Bunrei of Toyokunihime has descended to Manai-ga-take and is currently engaged in the Keirin of the Miroku Shinsei; yet, the power of the Magakami is vigorous, and they are attempting to obstruct the Keirin of Toyokunihime with their thousand-fold transformations of sorcery. Thou, Etsukohime, cross the turbulent stream of Ōeyama from here, proceed toward Manai-ga-take, and by Kototama, harmonize and drive away, and blow clean the hundred Magatsubi. Furthermore, Kamehiko and Hidekohime, for ye have a special mission in the Shinkai, proceed from here to the sacred land; thereafter, I shall grant thee a special mission anew.'

Thus she deigned to speak with solemn words, and together with the subtle music that was suddenly heard, she withdrew. Ah, how noble is the divine decree of the Sumerōmikami.

Hereupon, Kamehiko and Hidekohime, having received the divine decree, set out for the sacred land together with dozens of attendants, including Kumataka and Ishikuma. Furthermore, Etsukohime and Aohiko, accompanied by Onihiko and Onitora, and four or five followers, performed Misogi in the mountain stream and, while chanting the Sendenka, crossed over the Makutsu-ga-hara of Ōeyama and proceeded toward Manai-ga-take.

Etsukohime ascended the mountain stream of the Miyagawa, leaping to the right and wading to the left through the steep valleys, and at last advanced into the center of Makutsu-ga-hara; when she stood by the Kinukake-matsu and looked, the retreat of Takahime, which had burned away one hundred days prior, had again been bound with vines and creepers, and a temporary hut had been newly built in the same place.

Yoshikohime said, 'When I was invited by Takahime to come beneath this pine tree, before long, smoke and flames rose in thick clouds, and from all four quarters of the hut, fierce tongues of crimson fire burst forth, consuming everything in an instant; the sight of Takahime and this Aohiko leaping like fire-rats from that log bridge into the blue abyss was truly piteous. At that time, when I saw Takahime struggling and writhing in the water, I stripped myself bare to rescue her, yet Takahime was exceedingly angered, saying, "It is outrageous that you should fly at me naked, seize my hand, and pull me up while I was enjoying a swim of my own accord," and thus, in reverse, I was rebuked for saving her life; seeing that single-log bridge now, the scene of that time appears before my eyes as if it were happening this very moment,' she recounted.

Aohiko replied, 'Indeed it was so; had Kamehiko not been there at that time, I would have become a drowned corpse. I truly regarded him as the parent of my life and felt gratitude from the bottom of my heart. Moreover, whenever I attempted to offer my thanks to Takahime, she would glare at me with vertical eyes, so I could never express my gratitude and remained troubled in my heart, thinking it a pity; she is truly one who hates to admit defeat.'

Onihiko said, 'The fellows of the Uranai faith are all such creatures; since only those who are headstrong and loathe to admit defeat gather there, they are people who know not of defeat or weakness. They know not even the meaning of evil; it is a truly stubborn doctrine. To those who know not defeat, there is no victory or loss, and to those who know not shame, there is no shame; when a human being becomes like that, they are strong—nay, they are carefree. Since they have determined that everything they do is good, there is no way to reconstruct their spirit and soul.'

Aohiko said, 'Ah, I too am so astonished by Takahime’s obstinacy that I cannot find words to speak. When we were sealed under the stone lid at Kutsujima, I felt as though I were fading away, and though I could not even weep, I trembled; yet Takahime is of a stout heart. Conversely, she engaged in negotiations like a cornered rat biting a cat, so how could I not be astonished? Thinking we had finally arrived at Tanabe, she vanished into the darkness, only to be discovered by the moonlight, seized by the neck by Onitakehiko, and carried along the long journey to the mansion of Akiyama-hiko; it was so painful, yet she would not admit it, and I felt as though my breath would cease. Even so, she is a spiteful woman who would wag her tongue and spout nonsense, so one never knows how far her stubbornness extends. She swallowed the Nyoi-hōju jewel before the eyes of many as if it were nothing, and in the end, she performed the feat of escaping through the skylight like smoke; whether she is a monster, a deity, or a demon, she is a fool of unknown origin. Furthermore, her glibness is like that of a master of swallows or sparrows. One cannot know a person until one has associated with them, but when confronted with that brazen attitude and flowery speech, nine out of ten men and women are undone; she is truly a cunning one. To make matters worse, there is that Kurohime, who is also skilled in eloquence, constantly following behind to support her, and thus they have fashioned her into a person of great power, adept in mouth, hand, and evil. However, a new hut has been built upon this burnt ruin; perhaps that wretch Kurohime has followed after us, built a hut upon the ashes, and is hiding there. Being a missionary of the Uranai faith, she is persistent to the end, is she not?'

Onitora said, 'Shall I go and investigate for you?'

Onihiko replied, 'If Kurohime should be there, what would you do? Would you not be licked by the tip of her tongue and become limp and spineless?'

Onitora answered, 'What, it is Daijiyōbu. Onitora possesses the scroll of the tiger of Onitora; I shall show you my Jifuichishichiban, so watch the Kenbutsu at your leisure.'

The party sat upon a convenient stone by the roadside, resting and indulging in Zatsudan.

Onitora descended the slightly sloping path for about Shichihachiken and peered stealthily from the outside into a Waragoya at the foot of the Kinukake pine.

Onihiko cried, 'Ah, they are there, they are there! One old woman and two men. Hey, old woman, what are you? Are you of the Brahman Kyō, the Uranai Kyō, or the Ural Kyō? Give me a Hentafu!'

From within the Koya, a voice replied, 'Oh, how Yakama! I know not what kind of Gokutsubu you are, but I was drinking sake heartily in celebration of the Shintaku and dreaming a warm dream. To awaken me with such a loud voice—do you possess no sense of Ninjiyō? I shall report you for disturbing my Anmin.'

Onitora retorted, 'Ha, you are quite witty. You are a creature who moves her mouth incessantly while lying down, grumbling like a cow. You are a hungry ghost just like Takahime or Kurohime. If you do not repent, you shall again be licked by that tongue of crimson lotus; the Waragoya shall be visited by Shukuyūshi and return entirely to nothingness, and the flames shall spread to your hair and garments, forcing you to cast yourself from a single-log bridge to reach Jakumetsuraku, and you shall be compelled to depart on a journey to the Jūmanokudo.'

From within the Koya, the voice answered, 'I know not who you are, but I am the very Kurohime of whom you speak. Though my name is Kurohime, my face is as white as the snow falling there, and I am a missionary of the Uranai Kyō with a heart as pure as a snow-hag. You who wander the cold, cold mountain paths, parting these many drops of rain, appear to be a fellow in dire straits. It is the way of the homeless to wander on a day such as this. Even the pond snail or the snail possesses a house; you homeless beggars, thinking to ask for lodging at another's house while saying this and that... it shall not go so easily. However, if there is a heart for fish, there is a heart for water; if you will listen to what I say, it is not that I will not let you stay. Rather than shivering so miserably that your teeth do not meet, clattering and trembling, would it not be better to listen to my splendid tale, warm yourself by the fire, and drink your fill of delicious cloudy sake? The world is full of fools; even if you sing worthless propaganda songs mixed with tears while the snow falls and the wind blows so hard it would peel the skin from one's face, who would gather to listen? You are only being chilled, one after another, like this snow. It would be well to think calmly, with a cup of warmed sake.'

Onitora exclaimed, 'Hahaha, hey Onihiko, come here for a moment; this fellow is spouting quite a lot of nonsense, and the second generation of Takahime is dwelling there. It is a middle-aged crone who calls herself Shirohime or Akahime; it would be amusing if we were to subdue this creature and make her our vanguard on the way to Mount Manai.'

Onihiko replied, 'Oh, is that so? I thought it was a straw hut where some crone might be lurking. Very well, shall I, Onihiko, go forth to provide support?'

Crunching through the snow, he drew near to the side of the hut and peered softly inside.

Onihiko said, 'Ah, she is there, she is there; this is a creature I have seen sometime before. She is a truly clamorous crone, a creature that looks like a monster of a bell.'

Onitora said, 'Whether she be a bell or soot, she is in any case a black-named crone, a crone of propaganda. Hey, crone, how about you begin a battle of tongues that will divide the world, wielding that eloquence of yours in which you take such pride? It would be amusing.'

The crone shouted, 'Hey, Oto, Kan, how long will you sleep, drunk on sake? Outside, there has come a hyottoko-man who would break a carrying pole if he were to match or suit you, and he is chirping like a shrike; will you not go out and engage in a battle of tongues?'

Oto and Kan groaned, 'Hmm, munya munya munya, ah, ah.' (in a sleepy voice)

The crone cried, 'Oh, how irritating! Are you only yawning and dreaming of the middle of the night? It is already noon; will you not wake up quickly?'

Oto-kō said, 'Whether it be noon or cat-time, I know not, but just as the two of us were soundly fishing for cats and dreaming of eating a great heap of sweets, ah, what a terrible loss! A most excellent beauty of seventeen or eighteen appeared, and with thin, white, soft hands, she narrowed her eyes and offered me a cup, saying, "Oto-sama, one cup." Just as she was doing so, I was awakened. Oh, how dreadful, how ill I feel; it is an irreparable loss for my entire life. To describe the feeling of being served by a beauty such as I have never seen since I was born—even if one were to go to the Heavenly Pure Land, it is unlikely to be anything but a dream. Ah, how happy I was, how happy I was!'

The crone said, 'Hey, Oto, what are you doting about? Will you not pull yourself together? Open the door and look outside; many senile fools have come, and they are in the midst of being pierced by the tongue-blade of this Kurohime, preparing to surrender to the Uranai faith. Come, come, Kan-kō, wake up, wake up.'

The crone rose slowly and lumbered out of the hut.

§ 5

"Ah, if it be not Aohiko, and thou art there as well? Come now, what has become of thee? When didst thou enter the Ananai-kyō? Is not the garment of the Sendenshi changed? Come, cast it off quickly and exchange it for the Kyōfuku of the Uranai-kyō."

Aohiko replied, "Well, well, Kurohime-Sensei, with all due respect, the Aohiko of this Konnichi is no longer the same as the Aohiko of Hiyakunichizen; unless thou speakest to me with that understanding, I shall find it a matter of great Meiwaku."

The old woman laughed, "Ho, ho, ho, ho! Thou art a Hahineko-Yarau who changest as swiftly as the pupils of a cat's eyes. That Onnasendenshi standing there is that ruined Sendenshi called Yoshikohime, is she not? What profit is there in following such a one? Thou wouldst do well to consider the Dauri of things and discern the Rigaitokushitsu, ho, ho, ho, ho!"

Kankō said, "Everyone, do not sit there; pray, enter quickly. Within it is warm, and without it is drafty; there is a very spacious room inside."

The old woman retorted, "Hey, Kankō, canst thou not think well before thou speakest? Without answering thy mistress Kurohime, to invite them to enter at thine own discretion—what is this thou sayest? To let such people enter together is like packing a Bakudan; one knows not from where it might explode."

Kankō replied, "Whether it be a Bakudan or what else, does it matter? To quietly seize the enemy's Bakudan and use it is a brilliant strategy; it is the art of war of the Rikuto-Sanryaku, to control the enemy with the enemy's own provisions, ha, ha, ha, ha!"

The old woman said, "Thy strategy is, after all, like a fart; it has neither scent nor sound, and like Onkō, thou art a person of three parts spirit and seven parts illusion, with no substance to grasp."

Onkō exclaimed, "Here, here, Kurohime-cha-cha-sama, upon what evidence dost thou speak of one like Onkō? If thou speakest such nonsense, I shall not be lenient. Thou boastest day and night as if it were a habit, saying, 'I am the Princess of the Ryūgū, visible to all the world,' yet who dost thou think this Onkō-sama standing at thy feet is? Thou art truly blind; I am the Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō, Otohikotsukasa."

The old woman laughed, "Art thou the Sendenshi Otohikotsukasa, famed in sound? Oh, wait, hold on! Thou art a spineless fellow, far inferior to what I have heard. Thou art a man like a fart released in the water. That such a piece of junk should be a Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō is truly pathetic. Thou hast a foolish face, as if thou hadst dropped the essential, invisible thing in thy mother's womb at birth; to call thyself a Sendenshi—the very dung-beetles would be astonished. Thou art no Sendenshi, but a dung-beetle, ho, ho, ho, ho!"

§ 6

Otohiko said, 'Ah, there is nothing to be done, for only stubborn old hags gather in the Uranai-kyō.'

The old woman replied, 'It is a settled matter; you are also a fool of a laborer to such an extent, showing a face as if your ague had just subsided at this late hour, and saying such things as, "Only stubborn fellows gather in the Uranai-kyō." With such dull-wittedness, what success could you possibly achieve even if you entered the Uranai-kyō as a spy? I, this Kurohime, thought you were a fool with a peculiar habit, and because I remained with a feigned ignorance, you became conceited and dare to say such things? Look at your own face, you greatest fool in the world, you spineless coward of the Ananai-kyō; while you were sleeping, I, Kurohimetsukasa, wrote it upon you in deep black ink. Do not speak with such an arrogant air without even knowing that, you who have a face like the bottom of a pot. It seems you are also quite fond of hardship. The saying, "Leaving my native village from the heart, suffering in a strange land unknown," exposes the circumstances of a fool like you without any remainder. Ho, ho, ho, ho! And while we are at it, what is the attitude of that fellow Aohiko? With a face like a gourd grown in the shade, has he let a fart upon the magnificent God of the Uranai-kyō, or has he not? ...Exposing that out-of-tune, foolish face to this cold sky, listening with a look of gratitude to the teachings of a fellow who bears the cold name of Mizu no Rei, you are a monster of earthquakes who trembles and walks about like a konnyaku specter. Place your hand upon your chest for a moment and reflect upon your own heart.'

Aohiko replied, 'I am greatly humbled, but in any case, since Aohiko and Kurohime differ in color from our very names, we shall never get along. Upon that black, black face, like a weasel in a lime kiln, you have plastered a heap of white powder; it is exactly as if snow had piled upon a charred stake standing here. Even if you make a fifty-year-old backside and dye your gray hair or paint your face, you cannot hide the wrinkles. Even if you try to imitate the young and appear youthful, it is but feces floating in the flood of a privy; it is merely filthy. Will you not repent in due measure?'

The old woman retorted, 'What is so funny about me wearing white powder on my face? I wear it as a sign of a teaching that is flawless, paying attention to everything from corner to corner, and taking care even of the "oshiroi" (face powder) so that nothing is overlooked. You are wandering about, bewitched by a white fox, are you not? You are a failed ghost of this world.'

Aohiko replied, 'The one who is a failed ghost of this world is you. Life is but fifty years; even if you cross the slope of fifty and apply white powder and act coquettishly, the demons of hell will not fall in love with you. It is as certain as a stamped seal that you will be mistaken for a sister of the old hag of the Sanzu River, and even if you go to the underworld, you will be subjected to great rejection once again. You are truly a troubled old woman, a thing like a cattle tick that has a strong attachment, is deeply adhesive, and will not let go once it has stuck. I have been thinking that I would like to save you somehow into the magnificent Ananai-kyō, but now that it has come to this, is it hopeless? Your ears have become like octopuses, your eyes have hardened like knots in wood, and your mouth only speaks of being healthy and sound; therefore, there is no prospect for you at all.'

The old woman said, 'Ah, what a man who prattles such Yoma-like nonsense; at first, he wept tears and even let his nose run, declaring that the Ananai-kyō was Ketsukō and expressing how Arigata it was, yet next he claimed that the Ananai-kyō was Satsupa Dame, that the Fukakai Kaudō of the Mizu-no-Mitama did not sit well with him, and that he was utterly Aisau-tsukita, saying that even hearing the name of the Ananai-kyō made him feel ill; then, having been picked up by the Uranai-kyō through the Shōkai of this Kurohime, just when one might think he had become able to walk alone, he again manifested his Henshinbiyō, saying, "Yatsupa, the Uranai-kyō is Dame; my former wife did not lie, the Goshinriki of the Ananai-kyō is strong," becoming a heart like duckweed, a Heuchiyakumon who drifts to the west when the wind blows from the east, and drifts to the eastern shore when it blows from the west; can one receive the Okage of Kamisama with such a disposition? To serve Kami with a strong Shinkau that is Shiushiitsukuwan, Fuhen, Fudō, like an Azusayumi that pierces even rock, and which goes forth never to return, is the spirit of man, who is the Reichiyō of all Banbutsu; what a Hyōrokudama who wavers so easily; ah, what a pitiable creature; ah, what a sorrowful thing; ho ho ho.'

Aohiko said, 'What are you saying? Look here, Kurohime, you too said that the Ananai-kyō was Ketsukō, asking if there could be such a Makoto-no-Kyō in the wide world, and you abandoned the Brahmanism you had believed in until then from your mind as if casting away worn-out sandals, and now that I see you have become a staff officer for Takahime of the Uranai-kyō, do not speak with such an air of greatness. The saying about a monkey laughing at its own backside refers to you; oh, that reminds me of a monkey, for a monkey is a creature skilled at scratching; you, claiming to be the Fudesaki of Takahime, copy daily things that look like a procession of bent nails or persimmon calyxes, and relying on them as your sole weapon, you feel as if you have scraped off the head of an oni with a spatula, and you say you will stir up the Shinkau of the true believers throughout the world; what a troublesome thing, a monkey indeed; there is a saying that when a monkey pounds mochi, Okame mixes it; look here, monkey-crone, since the propagandists of Aohiko will from now on stir up the positions of the believers whom you have led and reconciled by breathing fire from your tongue, well then, you had better exert yourself and be active; you are a forerunner of the Ananai-kyō; nay, your task is already one of great toil; when I think that you have been turned to the service of evil according to the innen of your soul, I cannot help but feel pity for you; ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haese, because it cannot be fulfilled, Tamachi Haese; aha ha ha.'
