Chapter 21: 御礼参詣 [611]

御礼参詣〔611〕

§ 1

Heisuke, a man of sour countenance who lived with his old wife in this world, lamenting their fate and passing his days in constant complaint after their only granddaughter—a girl without peer in heaven or on earth—had been abducted, was struck with joy and astonishment when his granddaughter Osetsu unexpectedly returned; he knew not where to place his hands or feet, and with his old wife Onara, who was unseen but whose presence was felt, he shook his bent waist and leaped about in a manner most comical, for there had been no tidings of the girl.

Heisuke said, 'Now, now, Osetsu, where have you been and how have you fared until now? Day in and day out, the old woman and I have done nothing but speak of you and weep. Truly, you have returned to us; now that you have come back, I, Heisuke, shall have no regrets whenever I must depart this world. Come, come, tell me a little of your circumstances.'

Osetsu replied, 'Yes, yes.'

Unable to raise her voice for tears of joy, she said only this:

'I was confined within a rock cavern deep in Mount Hijiyama. A personage like unto a Kami appeared there and rescued me; even now, this person has kindly escorted me to our very gate. I pray you, Grandfather, Grandmother, offer your thanks most earnestly.'

Heisuke exclaimed, 'What, what is this you say? Is the person who saved you standing at our gate? I cannot remain thus; I must offer a word of thanks, or I shall not be at peace. Here, old woman, will you not also offer your thanks?'

The old woman, smiling broadly, could not hear him, so Onara said:

'Grandfather, is this not splendid? Let us hasten to offer our thanks to the Kami.'

Heisuke said, 'The Kami is indeed a Kami, but if we dally, the person who saved her may depart.'

So saying, he lit a lantern and stood at the gate, and said:

'I know not who you may be, but I thank you for saving my granddaughter. As you see, this is but a lowly, dilapidated hut, yet I pray you, enter. Outside, the snow has piled up as you see; you must be terribly cold. I shall kindle a fire in the garden.'

Etsuko-hime replied, 'Ah, are you the grandfather of Osetsu-dono?'

Heisuke answered, 'Yes, yes, I am an old man named Heisuke. My young son and his wife passed away before me, and I raised my only granddaughter as my own daughter, hoping to see her grow; yet last winter, the evil ones under the command of Onigumohiko of Mount Oe—those wicked men called Onihiko and Onitora, who are of most malicious disposition—abducted my precious granddaughter, and I have spent my days and nights weeping and suffering over this alone. Both the old woman and I have had our lives shortened by some twenty years because of this; that I am able to meet my granddaughter again is entirely due to you, my lord. I pray you, enter and rest at your ease, for the old woman also says she wishes to offer her thanks.'

§ 2

Yoshikohime said, 'Ah, your Goshinsetsu is Arigata, yet I have urgent Goyō in the Shinkai, and thus I must beg your Gomen. Regarding this, I have an earnest Onega to make of you. Will you not hear me?'

Heisuke replied, 'You, who are the parent of my Inochi, pray tell me whatever you desire; if it be within the power of this old man, I would even offer my life.'

Yoshikohime said, 'I thank you for your Satsusoku Goshiyōchi. These who are here are my Michizu; I beseech you, would you not permit these four or five persons to lodge here for this Konban alone, even if it be but in a corner of the garden?'

Heisuke replied, 'Hei, hei, I Shiyōchi; pray lodge even a Hiyakunin or a Sennin.'

Yoshikohime said, 'There is likely no place to lodge a Hiyakunin; it shall suffice if you but lodge five or six persons.'

Heisuke replied, 'This was a Shitsurei; I was so overjoyed that I fell into a Datsusen. Come, everyone, pray enter without Goenriyo. However, if you enter in a Muchiyakuchiya manner, there are pits for warding off the demons of Ōeyama, so pray follow behind me as you pass.'

He stood at the lead.

Yoshikohime said, 'Farewell; pray give my regards to Osetsu-dono. If there be a bond of fate, we shall meet again. Otohiko-sama, Kame-kō-sama, you must be weary this Konban, but pray follow me. There is a matter upon which I wish to consult you briefly.'

'We Shiyōchi all details; we shall follow your command,' they replied.

Yoshikohime, accompanied by the two, departed from that place in haste. Iwakō, Onihiko, Onitora, Kan, and Kunugi, these five, could not enter and stood wandering at the gate.

Heisuke said, 'Come, come, everyone, pass through here and enter straightaway.'

Iwakō, Kan, and Kunugi followed Heisuke and entered into the inner quarters.

Onara said, 'Oh, oh, everyone, you have done well to escort the young lady in this cold; pray lodge here at your ease for this Konban.'

Iwakō replied, 'Hei, how could that be? As Osetsu-sama knows, I am a retainer of Yoshikohime-sama; if you offer me thanks, I am rather troubled. I would be Arigata if you would but permit us to lodge for this Konban alone. Yaa, Onihiko, Onitora, you fellows, will you not enter? Why are you lingering so?'

Heisuke said, 'Yaa, you are of the same kind as the Onigumohiko of Ōeyama, are you not? How could I permit Onihiko and Onitora to enter? I am sorry, but pray return, pray return.'

§ 3

Ihakō said, 'Pray, Grandfather, those fellows called Onihiko and Onitora are the retainers of Princess Yoshikohime, who saved Osetsu-sama. The two of them finally repented of their evil and became retainers to Princess Yoshikohime, and because they made known the whereabouts of Osetsu-sama, the maiden was saved. Let the past enmity flow away like water, and for the sake of Princess Yoshikohime, I pray you, let them lodge here.'

Heisuke replied, 'Say what you will, I shall let you three lodge here, but those two gaki cannot be lodged. If they wish to enter, they may enter as they please, but they shall likely fall into a pitfall, not knowing the ways of this house.'

Ihakō said, 'This is a surprise; Grandfather, though you are aged, you are a man of strong tekigaishin. You must let the past flow away like water.'

Heisuke replied, 'Even if you tell me to let it flow like water, can this urami be so easily washed away? It would be well if you would put yourself in my place for a moment.'

Kankō said, 'That is indeed so, it is most reasonable. It is just as Grandfather says, and as I have heard, is it not so, Ichikō?'

Ichikō said, 'Oh, indeed, indeed. As a parent whose beloved daughter was tormented, even for a year, who could remain silent? What Grandfather says is most reasonable. As for those fellows Onihiko and Onitora, their innen has come to requite them, so there is no help for it. Perhaps it would be better for them to stand tachiban outside tonight, as it might serve to atone for their sins until now.'

Heisuke replied, 'Ah, you three gentlemen, you have spoken well. This old man is greatly pleased. Come, come, please lodge here; no matter what anyone says, I cannot lodge those two gaki.'

Outside the house,

Onihiko said, 'Hey brother, no matter how much they say they will let us lodge, it is too embarrassing to enter, is it not?'

Onitora said, 'It is so. The karma of the past has come around, and my heart is tormented by the oni within; I cannot calmly accept their hospitality, nor can I face the old man and old woman with a bold countenance. Ah, there is no help for it. Even Otohikosama and Kame-kō walked this snowy road; if we push ourselves, it is not impossible to go on. This is not the only house. Let the three of them lodge here in peace, and shall we two proceed a little further?'

Onihiko said, 'Ah, that is a wise judgment. Hey, Ihakō, Kankō, Ichikō, I shall go on ahead and wait at the foot of Hijiyama, so when the night breaks, you three come out. Farewell, excuse me for going ahead. You three may look upon the face of Osetsu-sama and drool to your hearts' content.'

§ 4

Leaving behind this Sutezerifu, he walked away briskly from this place, running toward the Haumen of Hijiyama.

Heisuke said, 'Now, you three, there is a Kotatsu prepared in the inner room; it must be cold, so pray enter. As for me, I am so filled with joy this Konban that I cannot sleep. Since I shall be speaking with my daughter for the first time in a long while, do eat some Chiyazuke and retire early. Tomorrow, I shall prepare a Gochisō to celebrate with you.'

Ihakō replied, 'Oh, Grandfather, Grandmother, and beautiful daughter, we thank you. In that case, we shall beg your pardon and go ahead. We have Takusan of Bentau, so pray do not feel Goshinpai for us. As we came along the Michimichi, we were Hohoba-ing rice balls, so we are not very hungry. It is Ketsukō if we may simply sleep.'

Osetsu said, 'Now, everyone, pray retire to sleep; I shall provide Goannai for you.'

So saying, she led them to the next room for Annai.

Ihakō said, 'Ah, how grateful I am. Kankō, Ichikō, there are no demons in this Sekai, are there? Let us sleep peacefully now.'

Kankō said, 'For some reason, my eyes are blinking and I cannot sleep.'

Ihakō said, 'Even if you cannot sleep, it is well if you enter this warm Kotatsu and take Kiusoku until the morning of tomorrow.'

In the next room, the three members of the Kanai were whispering about something.

Heisuke said, 'Oh, what a joyous thing this is. Now, Onara, even if I were to die now, I would be Tokushin.'

Onara said, 'Master, what is it that you are saying? You speak of dying again and again; you should not say such inauspicious things. Now that our daughter has returned, you should summon your vitality, and would it not be better to resolve to live for a thousand or ten thousand years?'

Heisuke said, 'Oh, Onara, have you not become able to hear well? This is a mystery; how has this come to pass? The daughter whom I thought had been killed has returned, and the ears of the old woman, who had resigned herself to being deaf for a lifetime, have begun to hear. Ah, could there be such a grateful thing? This is entirely due to the divine grace of Toyokuni-hime-no-Mikoto, who has recently appeared at Manai-ga-hara. When the snow has melted a little, shall we three, parent and child, go to pay our respects?'

Onara said, 'We shall go, we shall surely go. Even if the road is difficult, I would wish to go even this Konban, but since we have three guests, let us go to worship together with the three guests when the night breaks, even if the weather is foul. Now, Osetsu, shall we not do so?'

§ 5

Osetsu said, 'Yes, yes, I shall act as your guide, so please perform your pilgrimage of gratitude; however, we must needs consider the feast for tomorrow's guests, must we not, Grandfather?'

Heisuke replied, 'Oh, that is true; what manner of feast shall we provide for them? Shall we serve them rice mixed with sand, or shall we make it a feast of rice mixed with gravel? Which would be the better, eh, Onara?'

Onara said, 'Since our daughter has returned home in safety, shall we celebrate by making a feast of half-slaughter, or perhaps it would be better to make it a feast of total slaughter, eh?'

Osetsu said, 'To make it a total slaughter would be too much, so Grandfather, a half-slaughter would be better, would it not?'

Heisuke said, 'Ah, that is so; a half-slaughter requires less labor and is therefore good. Then let us decide upon a half-slaughter. Come, Grandmother, shall we begin the preparations now?'

Ihakō, who was sleeping in the adjacent room, turned pale of face and spoke in a low voice,

'Hey, Kankō, Ichikō, did you hear that?'

'Oh, I heard it. What a terrifying house this is! Did they not speak of feeding us rice mixed with sand, or making a feast containing gravel? What on earth can this mean?'

Ihakō said, 'Is it merely such base talk? If you listen to the three of them whispering even now, were they not saying whether to half-slaughter us or to slaughter us all? If we remain here idling, our lives will be forfeit. Is there no scheme by which we might escape? They have dug a pitfall at the gate, and the rear is a precipice; this is truly a situation where one is trapped and cannot advance or retreat. Well, there is no help for it; shall we attempt to flee? I remember the path the old man walked, so if we follow that way, it should be well. Everyone, prepare yourselves. Kan, Ichi, are you all here? It is the final resort of the thirty-six stratagems.'

So saying, they rose and, creeping stealthily, crawled out onto the surface of the garden, avoiding the flickering light of the lantern. Just as Heisuke glanced at the garden, he saw dark figures crawling about.

Heisuke cried, 'Hey, who goes there? Are you thieves?'

Ihakō replied, 'Yes, we are neither thieves nor anything of the sort; we are the three guests from last night.'

Heisuke said, 'Have you lost your wits? That is the garden. Come, quickly, go back into the kotatsu and sleep.'

Ihakō shouted, 'Hold, hey, you demon grandfather, demon grandmother, and demon daughter! I know your schemes perfectly well. You demons may eat rice mixed with sand or gravel, but human beings do not eat sand or gravel. You spoke of half-slaughtering us or slaughtering us all; what is the meaning of this, you senile old fool?'

§ 6

Heisuke laughed, 'Ha, ha, ha, ha! Have you misheard me? By rice mixed with sand, I meant rice mixed with chestnuts; by mixing in Kuriishi, I meant rice mixed with barley.'

Ihakō replied, 'But did you not say you would hangoro (half-kill) us, or minagoro (all-kill) us?'

Heisuke laughed, 'Ha, ha, ha, ha! When I said hangoro, I meant Botamochi; when I said minagoro, I meant rice cakes that have been pounded and pounded until fully crushed. Do not be anxious.'

Ihakō said, 'What, was it only that? Oh, this is a kind Oboshimeshi from our grandfather; whether it be hangoro or minagoro, it is all Ketsukō. Please, prepare them with haste. Hey, Kunugi, Kan, do not worry; it was a feast of Botamochi and Ankoromochi, ha, ha, ha, ha!'

Kunugi and Kan replied, 'Ah, we are relieved. You cannot imagine how much our hearts were crushed; before we could even eat the Dango or the rice cakes, three or four lumps of Ankoromochi had already formed in our chests, ha, ha, ha, ha!'

Onara said, 'Come now, everyone, please sleep without Goshinpai. I shall now prepare the minagoro.'

Ihahiko said, 'Nanibun, I Otano you well. If it is all the same, I would like to partake of both the hangoro and the minagoro.'

Osetsu laughed, 'Ho, ho, ho, ho!'

The Sannin, finally at ease, fell into a Taai slumber. Suddenly, they awoke to the sound of a Chiyabo.

Ihakō said, 'Oh, I slept soundly, and already it is Yoa. Hey, everyone, let us rise quickly and Chiyōdai the Gochisō.'

Osetsu appeared on the scene and said, 'Come now, everyone, please use the washbasin. Since the hangoro and minagoro are ready, please eat your fill. Today, we shall go to offer thanks at the place where the deity Toyokuni-hime of Manaigahara appears, so I pray you will accompany me.'

Ihahiko and the other two answered, 'Hai,' and leapt to their feet. They washed their hands, stored a great quantity of Botamochi and Ankoromochi into their stomachs, and the six of them set out together, proceeding toward Manaigahara while singing songs of propagation.
