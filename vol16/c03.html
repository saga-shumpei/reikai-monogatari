Chapter 3: Gate 番の Dream [593]

門番の夢〔593〕

§ 1

The night deepens in profound stillness. At the hour of the Ox, even the waters are void of sound. A mass of immense light, shining with a piercing brilliance, comes rushing toward the pine tree of the Riutō, emitting a low moan.

The light of fire once circles the perimeter of the pine, then rests and glows with a radiant luster upon the treetops. The five men who had fallen beneath the tree awaken in sudden terror and astonishment, gazing at the sky with mouths agape, as if receiving a Botamochi fallen from the heavens. Since even a Botamochi does not easily fall from a shelf, they are like monkeys fallen from a tree, their legs giving way beneath the old tree, a sight most comical as the night begins to break.

As they turn their eyes toward the sound of dozens of footsteps approaching from the east, which seem to sap the soul, they behold in the dim light near the Shinonome the subordinates of Onikumohiko, fleeing for their lives while pursued by a single man. The four or five who lead the group trip and fall before the five men whose legs have failed them, and those who emerge one after another pile upon one another; without mutual enmity, like the blossoming of a socialist or popular movement, they are all but fellows who fall and eat the dirt.

Kamehiko: 'Ho! You are the henchmen of Onikumohiko, who establishes his stronghold at Ōeyama. I shall cut you down one and all; prepare yourselves.'

So saying, he holds his double-edged sword in a reverse grip and brandishes it high above his head.

Ishikuma: 'Hey! What have you done with the two daughters who are of vital importance? This is no place for you to have wandered into by mistake. Will you not hasten to save the two women? If you do, we too shall be saved by your grace. Ahahaha!'

Kamehiko: 'Oh, that is true. Carried away by the momentum, I had lost sight of Princess Eiko from my mind. This is intolerable. If I dally, it is hard to say whether she might meet with the calamity of annihilation. Hey, you enemy scoundrels, you petty warriors, you have given me good counsel. In consideration of your merit, I shall spare you for today.'

No sooner had he spoken than he turned on his heel and retreated along the path he had come, as swift as an arrow in flight.

Ishikuma: 'Hey, Onitora, elder brother Kumataka, how is this? This wisdom of Monju of mine—it is of no use to one who possesses no wisdom like the Amanohashidate of yours; at such a time, it is of no avail. With presence of mind and peerless strategy, even Monju Bosatsu would turn tail before the infinite wisdom of Boss Ishikuma and flee in haste, crying out in joy. Truly, one must possess knowledge. Ahahaha, Ohohoho, Ehehehe.'

§ 2

How wondrous it is; all who have come beneath this tree, without a single exception, have shared the same fate, their loins utterly drained away.

The assembled Magami, in their prayer to Kami when all else fails, and holding that even evil possesses its three parts of Rikutsu, pressed their hands together like Jiunō to seek the salvation of Kami, and chanted:

Anokutarasanmiyakusanbodai, Namuyobutsūenyobutsu, Uenbutsupofusō, Enjiyōrakugachiyō, Chōnenkuwanzeon, Bonenkuwanzeon, Nennenjiushinki, Nennenfurishin.

Thus, with their hands and mouths granted the Jiiuken, they fired their Kototama in succession, equally and without distinction of Kafuotsu.

Changing the subject, Hidekohime, possessing both Kiriyō and charm, stood before the gate of Akiyama-hiko, the provincial governor of Yura harbor, awaiting the breaking of the dawn, when suddenly the front gate opened with a clatter; the gatekeeper, upon seeing the two figures, changed his countenance and fled at full speed, pointing toward the distance as he hid himself.

Instantly, four or five rough men, brandishing their Jutte, blocked the path before the two, and without allowing a word of assent or denial, seized their hands and feet, and placed gags in their mouths; though they struggled, there was no mercy, and like a cat in the ashes beneath a hearth carrying off a small mouse, they carried the two into the depths.

Once again, a man appeared before this gate, knocking upon the door as if to break it, and shouting, 'Open, open!'

The gatekeeper, with great reluctance, muttered: 'Ah, what a strange day this is; from the very break of dawn, a beauty like Benzaiten came, and before I could rejoice at having had my way with her, the great commander of the mansion took her away to the depths without allowing a word of protest. Ah, thinking of this, I wish I were a commander. I know not what kind of blockhead this is, but he lets out a strange voice and knocks at the gate enough to make one angry; well, it cannot be helped, this too is the duty of a gatekeeper. He has been drinking and shouting since the break of dawn, but perhaps there is still a little left in his gourd; I shall take it to compensate myself. Hey, Kame-kō, why are you dawdling? Will you not wake up properly? That is why I tell you not to carouse at night. If you must carouse at night, do so in a way that you are not awakened by others, and wake up in time to perform your duties.'

The sound of knocking at the gate became increasingly fierce.

Gin-kō said, 'Hey, Kame-kō, wake up quickly and open it for him.'

Kamehiko, at the gate, said: 'Hey, what is this? This fellow is suspicious; there should be no one in such a distant land who knows our names, but in any case, let us parley with him... Kame-kō, wait outside the gate; even if you tell me to open it at your own will, I cannot enter unless you open it from your side.'

門を敲く声、益々猛烈になつて来た。

銀公『オイ、加米公、早う起きて貴様開けてやれ』

亀彦、門前にて、

『ヤア、何だ、此奴は怪しいぞ、コンナ遠国に吾々の名を知つてる奴は無い筈だが、何は兎もあれ、一つ掛合うて見よう……………………亀公は門外に待つて居るのだ、勝手に開けと言うた所で、其方から開けて呉れなくちや這入れないワイ』

§ 3

Ginkō exclaimed, 'You, Kame, what insolence is this to speak so to your superior? I possess the authority of command; you are the one tasked with opening the gate. What is this talk of asking me to open it? For what purpose do you receive such fine provisions?'

Kamekō replied, 'Ah, ah, ah, do not scold me so. I have not yet uttered a single word.'

Ginkō retorted, 'And yet you just said "Kame," did you not? My ears have not yet retired from their duty.'

Kamekō advanced before the bolt of the gate and lamented, 'Ah, General Ginkō speaks only of unreasonable things. As the saying goes, "To speak is to chill the lips in the autumn wind." Even when I say nothing, he claims I have spoken and fastens a grievance upon me. It is truly absurd; I wish I could open my heart and show it to him.'

From outside, Kamehiko cried, 'Cease your bickering and complaining! It is the arrival of Lord Kamekō; will you not open it quickly?'

Ginkō shouted, 'Do you still presume to command this Ginkōtsukasa? "I wish I could open it," you say? That is a matter of course! Will you not open the gate quickly?'

With that, he clenched his fist and struck him upon the head three or four times.

'Ouch, ouch, ouch, ouch! What a wretched thing. To be struck three or four times by such a fellow, and to be told that something is "weak"—it is hardly worth the trouble.'

Ginkō grumbled, his tongue thick with the intoxication of wine, 'You say "ouch, ouch," yet the gate remains unopened, does it not? And what is this talk of it being "weak"? This fine front gate, which our Goshiyujinsama refined his Shinkon to select the most excellent timber to construct—how could such a thing be weak?'

Weeping, Kamekō removed the bolt and said, 'Now, I know not who you may be, but cease your dawdling and get in at once.'

Kamehiko laughed, 'Hahaha, my apologies, Gatekeeper. I have disturbed you since early this morning. You are very polite at first, but your words grow rough later on.'

'It is a settled matter. The first half was spoken by the primary guardian deity of Kame, and the latter part was spoken by the deputy guardian deity; it is the entanglement of the gate and the bolt.'

Kamehiko laughed, 'Hahaha, so you are also called Kame-san. It is very troublesome to have the same name both inside and outside the gate.'

'Since you are Kame-sama, I had thought to let you stand long outside the gate for a thousand or ten thousand years, but lately, the winds of economic depression blow throughout the world. With prices falling, I thought to sell out and opened it cheaply for you. The world has truly reached a dead end, has it not?'

§ 4

Kamehiko said, 'Ah, ha, ha, ha! You are indeed quite the wit, are you not? Let us cease this mon-dafu here; have two women not appeared in this place?'

'Ha! You are the one who is this to that woman, are you not?'

So saying, he thrust his thumb forward and displayed it.

'What is that? Is it not merely a finger?'

Kame said, 'With your countenance that seems to imply some hidden meaning, I wonder what you intend to do hereafter, having not forgotten even the finger. As for the two women, long ago such-and-such did such-and-such, and by now they are the such-and-such of such-and-such; we too desired to do such-and-such, but because such-and-such came and did such-and-such and did such-and-such, it is utterly Dame.'

'What you say is such-and-such and such-and-such; do you not see that I cannot understand the meaning at all? Will you not speak more clearly and Uchia?'

Ginkō said, 'Hey, you two, Kame and Kame, if you wish to hear such-and-such, then produce such-and-such; if you do so, I shall do such-and-such to such-and-such and do such-and-such to the such-and-such of such-and-such. Whatever may be said, the Ginkau, nay, Ginkō, is Kanjin.'

Kamehiko said, 'You are a fellow who becomes Masumasu incomprehensible. Ah, it is no Muri; it is my own Fukaku to attempt to inquire of a Monban.'

So saying, he attempted to advance into the interior.

Ginkō said, 'What! To advance so deeply is Burei. Wait Shibaraku; I shall meet with such-and-such and do such-and-such regarding the Yōsu of such-and-such, for even the Sata of Jigoku depends upon such-and-such, you know.'

So saying, he folded his hands and thrust them out before him.

Kamehiko said, 'Ha, ha, ha, ha! You are materialistic to the very end, are you not? Does the evil wind of the doctrine of gold-omnipotence blow even unto the sacred Jitentō Island? Alas, the world is at its end; it is Owari-daikon. Though the form alone is splendid, it lacks both taste and substance; it has become a watery world indeed.'

Kamehiko, heedless of all else, attempted to proceed into the interior. The two men clung fast to both of Kamehiko's feet,

'It is forbidden to pass until such-and-such is done,'

they said, biting at him. While his feet were held by the two men, Kamehiko moved slowly and heavily, dragging the two of them along as he aimed for the interior.
