Chapter 16: Kami 定の地 [606]

神定の地〔606〕

§ 1

Aohiko was beset by Kumabachi upon his entire Karada, and he writhed in agony, unable to endure the pain. Upon beholding this state, Kamehiko and Hidekohime broke off small branches of the Tokihagi pine, advanced before Aohiko, and recited the Amatsunorito; as they chanted the Ama no Kazūta and waved the branches in the manner of Saiusa, the bees vanished instantly to parts unknown, and the Kutsū of Aohiko’s Karada was suddenly stilled.

Aohiko gradually raised his head and, illuminated by the Kagaribi, beheld to his astonishment that the Futari, Kamehiko and Hidekohime, were seated in Tanza before him, concentrating their Kiguwan for his sake with Itsushinfuran; Aohiko immediately placed his Riyōte upon the Daichi and said:

"Were you, Anata, the Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō, Kamehiko-sama and Hidekohime-sama? I have no words to express my gratitude for saving me from such peril."

So saying, he wept with joy. From the thicket of trees behind, a woman’s voice spoke once more:

"Yea, Aohiko, thou wert led astray by the bewitching words of Takahime, who is possessed by the Kinmaukiubi Akuko, and thou, a weak-hearted Sendenshi, didst forsake the teachings of Ananai to fall into the Uranai-kyō; the true Kami hath now appeared here to restore thy heart and hath dealt thee a whip of admonition; shouldst thou fail to repent further, it is impossible to fathom what calamities may yet befall thy body; yea, Kamehiko, Hidekohime, well done, well done; that thou hast saved Aohiko from his peril through the sincerity and truth of thy Kototama is a glorious achievement, and I shall report this to the Ōkami."

Kamehiko replied, "Yea, I know not which Kami thou art, but to receive such excessive praise directed toward unworthy ones such as we is an honor beyond our station; henceforth, we shall strive with all our might and body for the fulfillment of the Shinkoku, and we humbly beseech thee to bestow upon us thy deep and vast protection."

Hidekohime said, "Ah, how grateful we are for the divine revelation of the Ōkami; we shall observe it morning and evening with reverence, strive for the unity of Kototama, heart, and deed, and labor to the utmost for the Shinkai; we humbly pray that thou wilt continue to grant us the whip of thy benevolence, that our weak faith may be strengthened and manifested to the scale of the universe."

So saying, they pressed their palms together in prayer. Aohiko, drowned in tears, could say nothing, and only prostrated himself upon the Daichi once more, overwhelmed by gratitude and humility. From the darkness, the woman’s voice called out again:

"Thou, Aohiko, dost thou repent from the bottom of thy heart and wilt thou follow the teachings of the Ananai-kyō? I shall hear thy reply."

At this voice, Aohiko sat upright and replied:

"I know not which Kami thou art, but now that it has come to this, I shall abandon the Uranai-kyō completely and clearly; I humbly beseech thee to use me once more in the path of Ananai as before."

原文（近代文語）

青彦は身体一面に熊蜂に取つかれ痛みに堪えず、苦しみ悶えつつありき。此体を見るより亀彦、英子姫は常磐木の松の小枝を手折り、青彦が前に進み出で天津祝詞を奏上し、天の数歌を唱へながら左右左と打ち振れば、蜂は忽ち何処ともなく姿を隠し、青彦が身体の苦痛も俄に静まりける。

青彦は漸く頭を上げ篝火に照し見れば、豈図らむや亀彦、英子姫の二人、吾前に端坐し、一心不乱に吾がために祈願を凝らし居るにぞ、青彦は忽ち大地に両手をつき、

『貴方は三五教の宣伝使、亀彦様、英子姫様で御座いましたか、危き所をお助け下さいまして、お礼の申しやうも御座いませぬ』

と嬉し泣に泣き入る。後の木の茂みより又もや女の声、

『ヤア青彦、汝は金毛九尾の悪狐に魅せられたる高姫の妖言に迷ひ、三五の教を捨ててウラナイ教に陥没したる心弱きデモ宣伝使、汝が心を立直さむと誠の神は今此処に現はれ、汝に誡めの鞭を与へたるぞ、尚改めざるに於ては、今後如何なる災禍汝の身に降らむも計り難し、ヤア亀彦、英子姫大儀々々。汝が至誠至実の言霊に依つて、青彦が危難を救ひたるは天晴功名手柄、此由大神に奏上致さむ』

亀彦『ヤア何れの神様か存じませぬが足はぬ吾々に向つて過分の賞詞、身に余る光栄と存じます、此上は益々粉骨砕身、神国成就の為に努力致しますれば、何卒厚き広き御保護を垂れさせ給はむ事を偏に願ひ奉る』

英子姫『アヽ有難き大神の神示、朝な夕なに慎みて、言心行一致を励み神界のために能ふ限りの活動を致しませう、何卒何卒仁慈の鞭を御加へ下さいまして、妾が弱き信仰を益々強く宇宙大に発揮せしめたまへ』

と合掌する。青彦は涙にくれながら唯何事も得云はず、あな有難し忝なしと又もや大地に平伏するのみ。暗中より又もや女の声、

『汝青彦、心の底より悔い改めて三五教の教を遵奉するや、返答聞かむ』

と呼ばはる声に青彦は起き直り、

『何れの神様か存じませぬが、もう斯うなる上は綺麗薩張とウラナイ教を諦めます。何卒元の如く三五の道にお使ひ下さいますやうに』

§ 2

From the darkness, the voice of a Megami spoke once more:

I am Amaterasusumeōkami. In days of old, I appeared upon this Miyama, and at the Ame no Manai, commonly called the Ubugama and Ubudarahi, I performed Misogi and established my Shinkaku; this is my Kiuseki. Nanjira, you shall Yoroshi build a Kiuden here and enshrine my Mitama. Borrowing the Nikutai of Yoshikohime, I make this Senji; Yumeyume, do not doubt it.

Kamehiko replied, Isaishiyōchi. From this moment, I shall purify my body in this Tanigaha, serve the Miaraka of the Ōkami Mizu, Hōsai the Shinrei, and establish this as a place of prayer for the Tenka Taihei and the peace of the land.

As he thus answered, Amaterasuōmikami smiled with joy and said:

Kamehiko, Eikohime, and Yoshikohime, I entrust the construction of the Kiuden to you three pillars of Kami; farewell.

No sooner had she spoken than she returned to the Mototsu-Miza. Yoshikohime returned to her original Nikutai and appeared before the three. Reverencing the divine decree of the Ōkami, they performed Misogi in the Tanigaha anew, recited the Amatsu-Norito, fashioned the Imi-suki and Imi-ono, and devoted their body and soul to the construction of the Kiuden. After one hundred days and one hundred nights, they brought the work to completion, invited the Shinrei of Amaterasuōmikami, and performed the ceremony of enshrinement with great solemnity. This was the beginning of the construction of the Ise-Jingū Kiuden. It is now called the Moto-Ise of Tango, and this Tanigaha has been called the Miyagawa ever since.

By reason of this connection, it is perceived that the deepest divine providence of the Shinkai was at work when the founder of Ōmoto, on the eighth day of the third month of the old calendar in the thirty-fourth year of Meiji, led many followers and, accompanied by Kinoshita Kamekichi of Uesugi, who shared the name of Kamehiko, was commanded to perform the divine work of Misogi. Furthermore, it is perceived that the pouring of the clear water of this Ubudarahi and Ubugama into the Kinmeisui of the Ryūgū-kan, and the subsequent act of the founder, who led many followers on the eighth day of the sixth month of the old calendar in the thirty-fourth year of Meiji and poured it from the mountain top of Kutsujima toward the vast ocean, was a part of the great divine work of the purification of the world. Anakashiko, anakashiko.

原文（近代文語）

暗中より又もや女神の声、

『吾は天照皇大神なるぞ、其昔此御山に現はれ、産釜、産盥と俗に称する天の真名井に御禊して、神格を作り上げたる我旧蹟なり、汝等宜敷く此処に宮殿を造り、我御霊を祀れ、悦子姫の肉体を借りて此由宣示し置く、夢々疑ふなかれ』

亀彦『委細承知仕りました。之より此谷川に身を清め、大神の美頭の御舎仕へ奉り、神霊を奉斎し、天下太平国土安穏の祈願所と定めまつらむ』

と答ふれば天照大御神嬉しげに打ち笑はせ給ひ、

『亀彦、英子姫、悦子姫三人の神柱に宮殿の造営を一任し置く、サラバ』

と云ふより早く元津御座に帰り給へば、悦子姫は元の肉体に復し三人が前に現はれ、大神の神勅を畏み、改めて谷川に禊し天津祝詞を奏上し、忌鋤、忌斧を造りて宮殿の造営に身心を傾注し、百日百夜を経て全く工を終へ、茲に天照大御神の神霊を招ぎ奉り、鄭重に祭神の鎮座式を奉仕したりける。これ伊勢神宮宮殿造営の嚆矢なり。今は丹後の元伊勢と云ふ、この谷川は是より宮川と称へられたり。

此因縁により、大本開祖は明治三十四年旧三月の八日、数多の教子を引き連れ、亀彦の名に因みたる上杉の木下亀吉を率ゐ、禊の神業を仰せつけられたるは、最も深き神界の御経綸の在します事と察せらるるなり。又此産盥、産釜の清水は竜宮館の金明水に注ぎ込まれ、次で開祖は数多の教子を率ゐ、明治三十四年旧六月八日、沓島の山上より大海原に向つて打注ぎ給ひたるも、天下修斎の大神業の一端と察し奉るなり。穴賢、穴賢。
