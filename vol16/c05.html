Chapter 5: Autumn Mountain 館 [595]

秋山館〔595〕

§ 1

Driven forth from Takaamahara, bearing the burden of the Chikura no Okido, the Great Kami Kamususanō set out upon a journey of Sasurahi through the lands of Yashima. Anxious as to the Yukue of the Great Kami, one gazed toward the eastern sky, where the Murakimo of clouds, swirling in the wind, cleared away on this joyful morning of Kefu.

On the day of the chrysanthemum banquet, the ninth day of Kikuzuki, parent and child, lord and servant met again. The Ihato of the heart was opened by the Magokoro of Akiyamahiko, the official of the Kami, who weaves the loom of twill and brocade. The red heart is Momijihime; Kamehiko celebrates the Yorozuyo; Hidekohime illuminates the darkness; and Yoshikohime shares in the feeling.

At the very moment of their meeting, the sound of a battle cry was heard at the Omotemon. Suddenly the gate opened, and Akiyamahiko stepped forth to face the approaching demonic host. As he recited the heavenly numbers with courage and all his might, the enemy forces—the subordinates of Onikumohiko of Ōeyama—fell heavily to the earth. Their state of writhing in agony was truly a sight to behold. With red complexions and blue eyes, their palm-fiber red hair disheveled, they stood their ground for about six shaku, approaching with a slow, heavy gait. Onikumohiko’s trusted sword, Onihiko, wearing a loincloth of tiger skin, stood with his ox-like horns erect.

Opening his great mouth, he laughed aloud. Onihiko cried out: 'Hahaha! How insolent! Do you think Onihiko would flinch at such a defense of Kototama by Akiyamahiko? We have countless Janjahieru here, thick as clouds and mist. Even if you possess the power of a lion king, the moment I swing my arm, your body shall be shattered into splinters. Today is the ninth day of the ninth month, and at the main castle of Ōeyama, the great commander Onikumohiko is holding the grand festival of Brahman. As an offering, we must present warm human flesh and offer wine of blood before the Kami. I have heard that the ringleaders of the Sangyō, who target the Brahman religion as their enemy, and the kin of Kamususanō are hiding in your mansion. Without further ado, drag your master before me immediately. If you babble on, I shall step in myself, twist your arms, break your legs, reduce your stature, stuff you into this wicker basket, and offer you together with the others as a divine sacrifice to the Kami.'

No sooner had he spoken than he gripped Akiyamahiko by the collar and tightened his hold. While Akiyamahiko was being pinned down by the peerless, powerful Onihiko, he attempted to chant the Kototama regardless of the circumstances; but the demon picked up a stone of convenient size, thrust it into Akiyamahiko’s mouth, and forced a gag upon him.

No sooner had he spoken than the Buka Magami, who had been in Kumon, struck by the Kototama, began at last to rise; and with pale faces, they feigned Karagenki, and with voices of Karaiba trembling and shaking, they raised a war cry, 'Uwaa, Uwaa,' and began their Jii Undō in earnest, which was truly Okashi.

Within the inner chambers, the sounds of Shichikukuwangen and the voices of Nodoka songs resounded, as if oblivious to the Kuōkei of this place. The Magun raised their voices in a war cry with all their might and continued to Dona.

In the Okuden of this place, the Shiyuen was in its Matsusaichiu, the guests letting these sounds drift by as if they were but the noise of a storm upon the peaks, when the Monban, Ginkō and Kamekō, came rushing in haste, and pressing both hands firmly to the floor, they rubbed their heads against the tatami and said, 'We humbly report, the front gate is in a state of great emergency.'

Momijihime replied, 'Ho, ye two, Kamekō and Ginkō, what is this matter of an emergency? Relate the details to me.'

Kamekō said, 'Yes, yes, I shall report. That... well... what shall I say? It is a matter most difficult to relate... well, a great event has occurred... if I speak thus, you shall surely be able to judge the situation without my further explanation.'

Momijihime commanded, 'Speak clearly and at once.'

Kamekō said, 'Ho, Ginkō, thou art the superior officer. Thou shouldst know the details. The condition of our master...'

Ginkō replied, 'Ho, I was unfortunately away at the privy, so I do not know the actual circumstances. Kamekō, thou didst witness the scene in person. Wilt thou not report it at once?'

Kamekō retorted, 'As a superior officer, didst thou not hide in the privy, trembling, while our master was in a state of crisis? I, for my part, was struck with terror by the great number of the attacking force, my eyes were dazed, and my observation of the scene was insufficient; yet thou didst hide thyself in a safe zone and peer out from the window of the privy. Ginkō said, 'Ha, I shall report. Just as Kamekō has said, it is not a whit different. If you do not do such and such quickly, Onihiko may do such and such to our master, and do such and such to such and such a place. I beseech you, go to the front gate without a moment's delay and save our master.'

As he rushed to attack, Hidekohime and Yoshikohime stood before him to block his path.

Even I, Kamehiko, a mere stranger, find it so regrettable and unbearable. Such human sentiment is as thin as paper; truly, I am utterly astonished. Come, Lady Momijihime, without regard for such fellows, let us rush out to the front with me, Kamehiko, and wage a battle of mourning for Akiyamahiko. As long as these slender arms endure, and as long as the mekugi of my sword holds, I shall strike and mow them down in all directions, leaving not a single one of the enemy's wretches alive. Like scattering autumn leaves, I shall dye the earth with blood and perform a great activity of blood-rivers and corpse-mountains. Lady Momijihime, come, follow Kamehiko!"

So saying, he pointed toward the front and attempted to depart. Hidekohime, faster than one could take the sash from her waist, cast it nimbly around Kamehiko's neck and pulled him back sharply. Kamehiko, his throat constricted by the thin cord, fell helplessly backward onto the spot.

The human voices heard outside approached the depths of the mansion moment by moment.

Momijihime thought:

To the Great Deity, the Divine Susanō,

To whom I have offered my heart and soul,

Shall I serve with all my strength and all my being,

Or shall I abandon this place,

And go to save my husband, Akiyamahiko?

The divine command is heavy, yet as a wife, she could not bear to sit and watch the fate of her husband. With a thousand thoughts and ten thousand considerations, wavering back and forth, the crimson blood surged into Momijihime's face, reflecting the turmoil within. Her heart pounded, and as the enemy's forces drew nearer by the moment, the Princess's heart was truly pitiable.

The voices that had been heard approaching this place vanished before one knew it into the Indian summer sky. Akiyamahiko returned, pointing toward the inner chamber, composed and without haste or agitation. Kamehiko and Momijihime, in their overwhelming joy, felt their chests tighten; unable to speak, they collapsed on the spot, doubting their own minds as to whether it was a dream, reality, or an illusion, and spent the time in contemplation. Kunitakehiko stood up.
