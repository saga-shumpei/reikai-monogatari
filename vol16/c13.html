Chapter 13: Kami 集の Jewel [603]

神集の玉〔603〕

§ 1

Ginkō and Kamekō, the two gatekeepers of Akiyamayakata, set out toward the port of Yura to make preparations for departure, and ran toward the Omotemon.

At that moment, a male and two female Sendenshi appeared, and while singing Sendenka, they attempted to enter the Monnai in a leisurely manner.

Ginkō and Kamekō spread their arms wide and said, 'You must be the Sendenshi of Kamehiko, the kin of the Kaginusubito. I am already sick of hearing even the mere mention of the word Sendenshi; the two Sendenshi who came just before stole the Taisetsu key and escaped through the Uramon. Because of that, the inside of our Tauyakata is in a state of great Ōsaudō, and you are also finished; the Kanjinkaname key was taken away by the previous Sendenshi, and having had the Shinpi key stolen, our Tauyakata is in unprecedented chaos, and from now on we shall gather many guard dogs to search for it, so you had better go home.'

Kamehiko replied, 'These words of yours are incomprehensible; do you treat us as thieves?'

Kamekō said, 'It is a settled matter; to speak of Sendenshi is synonymous with Kaginusubito, so you shall not pass; if we were to let someone like you enter the inner quarters, it would be a great disaster.'

Kamehiko said, 'Your words are not acceptable; the two Sendenshi you speak of must be the Sendenshi of the Uranai faith, but we are the Sendenshi of the Sangokyō, and we are those who bestow the Shinpi key.'

Ginkō said, 'What, you say you bestow the Shinpi key? Come, show me that key quickly; if you hand over the key, I shall permit your passage, for I feel that every Sendenshi is like a Kaginusubito; where did you steal that so-called Shinpi key from? Come, hand it over quickly.'

Kamehiko laughed, 'Ha ha ha, what an ignorant Monban you are; come, Princess Eiko and Princess Etsuko, let us proceed.'

As they attempted to go, Ginkō and Kamekō raised their voices and shouted. Hearing this voice, Akayamahiko wondered what had happened and rushed out to the front, and upon seeing them, he realized it was the party of Sendenshi led by Kamehiko.

Akayamahiko said, 'Ah, this is indeed Kamehiko-sama, Princess Eiko, and Princess Etsuko, you have done well to come. We have been in a bit of a turmoil and are in a great uproar, for the Taisetsu tama-kagi entrusted to us by Susanoo-no-Ōkami has been stolen by someone, and we have just now sent our servants in all directions and are in the midst of a search.'

§ 2

Akiyamahiko said, 'A short while ago, two Sendenshi of the Uranai Kyō, named Takahime and Aohiko, came to the Genkuwan and then vanished from sight. Upon checking, I find that the Taisetsu Tamakagi, which I had hidden behind the tablet in the Genkuwan chamber, has suffered Funshitsu. Though it is never a good thing to suspect others, I cannot help but harbor such thoughts within my heart, and thus I am creating sin.'

Kamehiko replied, 'Ah, I understand your Goshinpai and sympathize with you. Let us, together, provide Chikarazohe and search for the Arika of the key.'

Akiyamahiko said, 'That key is the treasure key of Kamurijima and Kutsujima; should it happen that, by some Manmanichi, they use that key to cross over to both islands and steal the Niyoihotsushiyu, the very Kiso of our hard-won Shinsei Jiyōjiyu would be rendered Mechiyamechiya. Even at the cost of our lives, we must protect this key and the jewel.'

Kamehiko said, 'Ah, indeed. It is at such a time as this that we must seek the aid of Onitakehiko-dono.'

So saying, he turned toward Mount Ōe and recited the Amatsu Norito to seek succor, and at his very word, a masked giant appeared in response, crying, 'Ou!' and upon closer inspection, it was indeed Onitakehiko.

Kamehiko said, 'Ah, you are Onitakehiko-sama! How fortunate that you have come. The matter I request of you is thus and so.'

As he recounted in detail how the key had suffered Funshitsu, Onitakehiko tilted his head and closed his eyes for a moment, but suddenly his countenance brightened and he said, 'Ahahaha! The plunderers of this key are those fellows, the Sendenshi of the Uranai Kyō named Takahime and Aohiko. Even now, they have boarded a ship from the port of Yura and are rowing toward Bakuchigamisaki. Come, I shall accompany you. Prepare the ships! Akiyamahiko-dono, have no Goshinpai.'

Akiyamahiko said, 'I am grateful. I humbly and earnestly beseech your favor.'

Onitakehiko said, 'I shall now prepare a ship together with Kamehiko and head toward Kamurijima and Kutsujima. Akiyamahiko, along with Eiko-hime and Etsuko-hime, shall remain at this hall and await our return. Kamehiko, come.'

No sooner had he spoken than he led Kame-kō and more than ten of Akiyamahiko’s retainers, outfitted three small boats, and rowed out into the moonlit sea of Yura port, the sound of the oars ringing out valiantly. The moon, in its fifteenth night, cast its reflection deep into the seabed, and as the boats swayed, the image stretched and shifted, appearing as a silver dragon upon the ocean floor; leaving the waves of Bakuchigamisaki behind, they gazed upon the spray of the tide, viewed Kaburaiwa and Kyōgamisaki to the left, looked toward Takakumoyama to the right, and pursued Takahime as swiftly as an arrow in flight.

§ 3

Takahime had landed upon Kamurijima two hours prior, and using the Tamakagi, she retrieved the Niyoihotsushiyu which Susanōnomikoto had kept hidden; she buried it secretly at the root of a great mulberry tree upon the mountain as a marker, and once more, together with Aohiko, she boarded the boat and set forth toward Kutsujima.

Countless giant crocodiles surrounded the perimeter of Kutsujima, keeping strict guard; pressed by the swarm of crocodiles, the boat could no longer advance even a single shaku, so Takahime tied the boat's rope about her waist and, crossing over the backs of the crocodiles, finally climbed up the cliff together with Aohiko. This interval required nearly two hours. The party of Onitakehiko and Kamehiko suddenly caught up to this place. The many crocodiles parted to the left and right, opening a passage for the boat. All of them immediately rushed up the island and ascended toward the rock cavern at the summit. At the very peak of the Tsuriganeiha stood a rock cavern approximately one jō in height. Therein was concealed a diamond-hard jewel, emitting various radiances of yellow, crimson, blue, red, purple, and others. The two, with undivided attention, leaped into the rock cavern and, working until they were covered in sweat and mud, sought to take the jewel. The key had been placed with great care upon a bed of leaves at the edge of the hole. Kamehiko quickly took up the key and thrust it into his bosom. This diamond-hard jewel had protruded from the world beneath the earth and was so firmly adhered to the tip of the crag that it could not be easily taken; Onitakehiko secretly pulled over a large boulder from nearby and dropped it with a thud upon the rock hole. The two, their exit blocked, could do nothing and raised cries of lamentation.

Onitakehiko and the entire party, acting with composure, recited the Amatsu-Norito and sang the Sendenka, and furthermore, gathering in the vicinity, they rested and indulged in idle conversation. From the gap between the rocks, the struggling figures of the two were clearly visible. When Kamehiko peered into the gap, Takahime looked up from within the hole at the face of Kamehiko, and said: 'Ha! You are a missionary of the Sangokyō; we have come to welcome this jewel in obedience to the divine decree, and if you obstruct the divine work, you shall be cast headlong into the cauldron of hell; cease your mischief quickly, return to the path of sincerity, remove this rock, and offer your apologies to the Hinodewake, you insolent wretch!'

Kamehiko replied: 'Hahaha, do not wag your tongue while cast into a rock hole from which you shall never emerge in this final age; this rock is a giant Senbiki-iwa, a thing that would not move easily even if a hundred or a thousand men were to come; pray, remain here at your ease and engage in deep contemplation, for we shall now take our leave and head toward the sacred land.'

§ 4

Takahime said, 'If you will not take the Ganseki, then so be it; but in return, you shall not know the Arika of the jewel of Kamurijima. If you desire to know the Arika of the jewel, remove this rock, rescue us two, place us upon a ship, and send us back to the port of Tanabe with Teichiyō; do you not desire the Niyoihotsushiyu jewel?'

Kamehiko replied, 'Ah, what a cunning creature. Onitakehiko-sama, how shall we proceed? With your Tenganriki, will you not search for the Arika of the jewel?'

Onitakehiko said, 'Once the Niyoihotsushiyu has passed into the hands of an Akugami, its Guwaibu becomes defiled and clouded, and it emits no Reiki whatsoever. If one wishes to use that jewel again, it must be purified and polished in the Seisen of Wachi for the duration of Nanukananayo. Nevertheless, if the Arika remains unknown, I shall have no excuse to offer before Susanō no Ōkami. Ah, there is no Shikata; there is no other way but to make Takahime and Aohiko, the Riyōnin, make a Hakujiyō.'

Kamehiko said, 'This is troublesome. I had intended to seal them in this rock cave for Mangafumatsudai; it is a pity. Hey, Takahime and Aohiko, you two are truly fortunate. Tell me the Arika of the jewel in detail, and I shall remove this rock for you.'

Takahime replied, 'Not so fast; it shall not go that way. Unless you remove the Ganseki and escort us to Kamurijima, we shall by no means make a Hakujiyō. Should we carelessly reveal the Arika, we would have no standing left if we were to be abandoned here. The only way to save ourselves is to keep the Arika of the jewel hidden. Ho ho ho ho.'

Kamehiko muttered, 'Ah, you are a stubborn one, neither vinegar nor konjac will move you. Shall I yield one step, remove this rock, and grant you salvation? I shall think of it as losing a bet I never made and endure it.'

As he muttered this, he exchanged a glance with Onitakehiko, and Onitakehiko gave a single grunt, kicked the rock with all his might, and the Ganseki, with a thunderous clatter and roar, plunged into the purple sea below, sending up a pillar of water with a splash. Takahime and Aohiko finally crawled up and said, 'Oh, everyone, we have caused you concern. Thanks to you, we have been saved. Come, let us return.'

Kamehiko said, 'Hey, hey, it shall not go that way. Where have you hidden it? Will you not make a Hakujiyō?'

Takahime replied, 'The Niyoihotsushiyu jewel is hidden on Kamurijima. It is not here. Quickly, set sail; if you dally, a gale will blow, and we shall be unable to return.'

The party of Onitakehiko managed to descend from the Tsurigane-iwa and boarded the ship. They placed Takahime and Aohiko on the ships of Onitakehiko and Kamehiko, and placed the servant of Akiyama-hiko on the ship they had arrived in, and they rowed back toward Kamurijima with the valiant sound of oars. No sooner had Takahime arrived at Kamurijima than she scrambled up the mountain like a monkey, swiftly dug out the jewel, and thrust it into her bosom.

"Behold, here is the Niyoihotsushiyu. If I were to hand it over now, it might be convenient for you, but it is somewhat inconvenient for me; should I be cast into the sea while aboard the ship, it would be a calamity. Kamehiko replied, "You are a person of meticulous caution to the very end. We have no intention of causing you any suffering; pray, hand it over to us at once. We shall surely see you safely to Tanabe."

Takahime answered, "Absurd. Depending on how you conduct yourselves, I have not yet decided whether I shall dash this jewel against the Ganseki to shatter it, mar it, or cast it into the sea. Whether I hand it over or not is a matter to be determined only after we have arrived at Tanabe. Ho, ho, ho!"

Takahime retorted, "Absurd. Like the two wheels of a cart, two legs, a pair of sake decanters, or a chisel and a hammer, nothing can be accomplished alone; one cannot walk with but a single leg. You must take Aohiko along with us as well."

Kamehiko remarked, "You are an impudent one to the very end. Without such a spirit, one could never hope to dismantle the Sangokyo. Ah, how admirable, how admirable; it is the crawling through the legs of Han Xin. Ha, ha, ha!"

Onitakehiko urged, "Come, Lord Kamehiko, pray continue your discourse at your leisure aboard the ship. A strange cloud has appeared in the northeastern heavens. The onslaught of a tempest approaches moment by moment. Come, quickly, quickly!"

Thus he urged them on. Kamehiko, Takahime, and the others divided themselves among four ships, and with the valiant sound of oars, they set their course for Tanabe. Ah, what manner of thing shall this precious jewel prove to be?

In passing, it is said that this Niyoihotsushiyu is also known as the Kototama, and is called the Jewel of the Gathering of Kami; it is a mysterious living jewel that emits speech. It is a precious stone that functions much like the cylinder of a phonograph popular in modern times, and it is said that it is now preserved in the hands of evil kami, the descendants of the Uranai-kyo, and is deeply hidden in a certain location in Germany.

因に言ふ、此如意宝珠の玉は一名言霊と称し又神集の玉とも言ひ言語を発する不可思議の生玉である。丁度近代流行の蓄音器の玉の様な活動をする宝玉にして今はウラナイ教の末流たる悪神の手に保存せられ独逸の或地点に深く秘蔵されありと言ふ。
