Chapter 6: Stone 槍の Rain [596]

石槍の雨〔596〕

§ 1

The Ōzora is clear and serene, and the Yamakaha are pure and bright. The Wachi River flows in stillness, and the Otonase, where not even a branch stirs, moves with a gentle current. Like the flowing of Aoginu for Ikusenjiyō, the Yorudan River, with its deep Minase, sways in its course. The Kamiyo turns, and the north wind, swelling the Maho, comes ascending. Bestowing deep grace upon Kaumorieki, the river passes the stone Sekishiyo standing in the Nakaba of the stream, and at last, the Hase River flows with swift Ashimoto. At the Ochiahi of the waters, to the right and to the left, one takes the helm toward the Yunde, and the Kahaji ascending is a Nagasuna. Crossing the rapids of Ikuta villages, this place is the Seichi, and passing beneath the Shirasebashi, one ascends.

In the Kahamizu of the Guwariyō pine, branches are submerged and fish leap, while the moon shines clear above the treetops. What is seen in the Mukau is perhaps Ineyama, known as the Fuji of Tanba. The Misen mountain towers into the clouds, standing with such majesty. With neither enemy nor ally, the boat is moored at Mikata-daira. When one gazes upon the configuration of the lands in the four directions, the Ryūgū-kan of the Shimotsuiwane, surrounded by the Aogaki mountains, appears. This place is the renowned Koajia, known as the Takama of the earth, the Seichi of old, Jerusalem. It is a Seichi that surpasses the scenery of the Kanransan and the Yorudan.

Kami-Susanō-no-Ōkami, Kunitakehiko-no-Mikoto, and the other three ascended upon the lotus pedestal of Okebuseyama, and when they gathered the eight million deities of heaven and earth in divine assembly, the hundred deities, vying to be first, drew near to the pure Kototama of the Mikoto. They gathered until there was no space left, and the manner in which they celebrated and rejoiced at the appearance of the Sumer-Ōkami was as if a lotus had blossomed all at once.

Kami-Susanō-no-Ōkami whispered some matter in secret to Kunitakehiko-no-Mikoto, promising to meet again after thirty-five myriads of years until the dawn of the Miroku divine government, and suddenly, he mounted a red-crowned crane that arrived and flew away, pointing toward the east high in the Nakaba of the sky. Kunitakehiko-no-Mikoto, while whispering some matter to Kamehiko, Eiko-hime, and Etsuko-hime, gave a strict divine revelation toward Manji, and parting here, he alone hid his majestic figure beyond the peaks of the Shiō.

The one man and two women, the propagandists left behind, kept the divine words of the two deities hidden in the depths of their hearts, and once again departing from this place, they set their sights on the mountains of Ōe and advanced with joyful hearts. Alas, what divine strategy was proposed by these five pillars upon the mountain? The secrets of the divine realm cannot be easily fathomed. Though the moon may wax or wane, and even if the great earth should sink, the power of Makoto shall save the world. Until the divine age arrives when the god of Makoto appears, the era of Miroku returns, and the whole world rests in its proper place, celebrating, rejoicing, and taking courage in the grace of the gods of heaven and earth, this is the way of the gods that must not be spoken, this is the mechanism that cannot be told. Whether it be a monk or a bald head, hiding the head and revealing only the tip of the tail, I set it down. From the beginning, for Onisaburō who tells the tale, for the one who holds the brush, and for those who listen, it is a story that feels somewhat hollow and heavy upon the heart; thus, for now, I wrap it up and speak it so.

§ 2

Onihiko, having appeared before the gate of Akiyamahiko leading a multitude of Mabito, did first thrust a stone into the mouth of Akiyamahiko, bound him with a gag, and secured his hands and feet behind his back; then, advancing further into the depths of the Okuden, he bound Kamususanō Ōkami, Kunitakehiko, Momijihime, Hidekohime, and Kamehiko, all together, hand and foot, and raising a cry of victory, returned with valor, proceeding leisurely toward the main castle of Ōeyama.

While carrying the Ajiro palanquins along a mountain path where ancient pines of a thousand years grew thick, leaping over rivers and traversing the rocky Ihama, they at last emerged at Makutsugahara; there, the company set down the Ajiro palanquins, sat upon the surrounding rocks, and indulged in idle chatter while resting their breath.

Kō said, 'Hey, Onitora, at the base of the Ryūtō pine, you were toyed with and made a fool of by those two formidable women, and having received a glare from the great commander Onikumohiko that made fire flash from your eyes, you turned pale and cowered; how is it now, can you return with a proud face, or will you not have to treat us when we return?'

Onitora replied, 'Oh, indeed, Kumataka, the same may be said of you; the sight of you at that time was truly a thing to behold. It was because we did not obey the orders of our superior as commanded, and lacked harmonious action, that we performed such an unexpected failure. Nevertheless, is it not wine that we should abstain from? Had we not been drinking wine at that time, we would not have performed such a failure.'

Kumataka said, 'What, it was by no means a failure; because we let the two women escape, we conversely discovered the whereabouts of Kamususanō no Mikoto, and thus misfortune was turned into fortune. In all things in this world, human affairs are like the horse of the old man of the frontier; however, today, because Onihiko’s command was well-directed, such results were brought about. In all warfare, it is useless unless there is normal activity with unity between superiors and inferiors; no matter how many Jiyanjiyahieru are gathered, if all actions lack unity, failure is imminent. Everything depends upon the commander’s careful and thorough command and our own utmost loyalty and sincerity toward the commander. Now, Commander Onihiko, I congratulate you on today’s success; with this, Commander Onikumohiko may be at ease, you may be at ease, and all of us may be at ease; we are all at ease together, ha ha ha ha.'

At this moment, from the thicket of pines overhead, stone dumplings fell like rain, pattering down upon the bodies of Onihiko, Onitora, Kumataka, and all the others. Though they cried, 'Ouch, oh, it hurts,' and attempted to flee, they were blocked by the wall of stone spears and could not move their bodies in the slightest, receiving countless lumps upon their heads and faces. As Ishikuma looked up, a stone the size of a fist struck him squarely on the bridge of his nose, crushing it; blood flowed in streams, he grimaced, groaned, and collapsed upon the spot. The deities imprisoned within the Ajiro palanquins remained in perfect safety, as if behind walls of gold and iron, and beholding this spectacle, they could not help but laugh aloud in unison, 'Ha ha ha ha, ho ho ho ho.'

The rain of stones ceased abruptly. As Kamususanōnomikoto and the seven others appeared suddenly upon this place, it was seen that their gags and binding ropes had, ere they knew it, been undone.

Yoshikohime spoke: 'Oh, Minasama, a most regrettable matter has come to pass. These men, who possess boundless benevolence and have carried us in kago through this steep and difficult path, risking their lives and dripping with sweat and water to bring us hither, are now engaged in the business of selling dango, having opened branch shops, and are being encouraged to flourish in their trade. Surely you all must be hungry; I pray you, purchase one dango-lump from those stalls for each of them. Ah-ha-ha-ha.'

Kamehiko replied: 'We, too, are terribly hungry. The goods sold at the branch shops are uninteresting; shall we not rather tear off and partake of the dango-lumps from the backs of the main shop, those that possess eyes and noses? Ah-ha-ha-ha.'

The group responded: 'Ho-ho-ho-ho.'

Onitora, while knitting his brows, said: 'Hey, hey, all of you, will you not pull yourselves together? Even if a rain of stones has fallen, it is no cause to be so disheartened. I am an exception, but you lot must quickly regain your spirits and bind this fellow fast, or there is no telling what may come to pass. Aie, everyone here is nothing but a coward. Onitsukami has mistaken friend for foe and rained stone-bullets upon the heads of his own allies. If it were said that we were struck by the stone-bullets of an enemy, it might be tolerable, but to think of vanishing like the dew of this valley, struck by the stone-bullets of one's own side—I cannot die, even if I were to die. Ah, somehow my breath seems to be failing. Oi, you lot, go and call my wife; at this final moment, I wish to see her face once more before I die, that alone is the lingering attachment of my soul in Yomi. An-an-an.'

Kumataka said: 'Hey, hey, will none of you pull yourselves together? What is this, wearing such pale faces as if you had come to fetch fire from hell? With such weakness, how can you fulfill this duty? Will you not pull yourselves together? Aita-ta-ta, I too am in pain. In times of distress, one must rely on the demons; O Namu Onikumohiko Daimyōjin-sama, have pity upon our peerless, loyal hearts, and stay this pain with all haste. In return, I pray you rain a shower of steel spears upon the heads of the Kamususanō faction and destroy them. It is for your sake as well; it is a double benefit, for if I am saved, my retainers are saved also. Where else could such a good thing be found? Eh, no matter how much I beseech you, you are a god of the Brahman religion who will not listen to reason.'

At this moment, arrows with sharp Kitsusaki fell like rain, piercing the bodies of Onihiko and the other Magami without any Enriyoeshiyaku.

"Ah, once again! Has Ōkamisama made a Kanchigahi? The enemy remains Buji as they are, while the Mikata are subjected to a concentrated barrage of fierce Soya; what a grievous Machiga to make. Ouch, ouch, ouch! This Honto is unbearable, and my breath is failing me. There is no Shikata; when I reach my Saigo and die, I shall become a demon of Jigoku and lie in wait for these Koitsu to arrive, that I may exact my Henpau. Hey, Susanōnomikoto and the rest of you Yatsura, mark my words: when you die, I shall see to it that your eyes are blinded, your mouths are rendered speechless, and your Karada are left unable to stir even an inch."

Kamehiko replied, "Hahaha! Do not vomit, do not vomit. To say you shall make our eyes blind, our mouths speechless, and our bodies unable to move—what a thing to say! It is truly a Tenkaitsupin of Chingenmeugo. Pray, Hidekohime-sama, Yoshikohime-sama, would it not be fitting to perform a dance? Such an Omoshiro Kuōkei is rarely seen, save here at Ōeyama."

"Hohohoho."

Akiyama-hiko clasped his hands together, and with a voice clear and cool, he chanted the heavenly number song, "Hifumi-yo." No sooner had he done so than the wounds of all the Magami were healed as if wiped away, and voices of joy filled the air, both here and there.

"Ah, I am saved."

"It is strange."

"It is a mystery."

"What a bizarre thing this is!"

Thus they began to whisper among themselves. Akiyama-hiko turned toward them all, and with a voice clear and cool, he sang the song of propagation:

"Though the morning sun may shine or be clouded, / Though the moon may wax or wane, / Though Onigumohiko may be mighty, / Though the mountains of Ōe may be deep, / Though you may have many subordinates, / You are but weaklings like lice, / Who morning and evening seek and devour / The living blood of men. / The work of God is to make you vomit forth / The living blood stored within your bodies; / The rain of stones that crushes the head, / The tips of the Soya that squeeze out the blood, / It flows forth like the tide. / I am the one who shall save this world, / The minister of the children of men, the true man / Of the teachings of the three-five God. / You who have become demons and devils, / Purify your spirits in the clear flow / Of the valley stream.

§ 5

Return, O Momobito, to the one true Oshihe of Ananahi; for originally, ye are the Wakemitama of Tenchi. There is neither Zen nor Aku, for one must Chōetsu the Zenakujiyasei. Become as the spirit of a newborn Akago, and if ye follow the Nori of Tenchi, then even Makutsugahara, where the Oni and the Orochi rage, shall instantly become the Kenonkiyō of Mesopotamiya. The flowers of prosperity shall bloom for Tokoshihe, and the fruits of the trees shall ripen with sweet savor. There is no need to break one's heart to torment and afflict the people of the world, for in doing so, one but suffers oneself. Come, O Morobito, O Morobito, if ye reform from the depths of your hearts and return to the true path, Kami shall extend the Mite of salvation, and shall save you in the Takama of Tokoshihe, filling you with glory. How shall it be? Come, how shall it be? Fix your hearts and return your words; proclaim them with a voice clear and cool. Kami shall attend upon your bodies and protect you abundantly. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara, Sachihahe Mashimase.

When the chanting ended, Onihiko and all the others prostrated themselves upon the Daichi, and while choking with tears of Kanshiya, they cried out with voices that shook even the Sangaku.
