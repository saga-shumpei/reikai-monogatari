Chapter 12: 心の反映 [1096]

心の反映〔1096〕

§ 1

The autumn wind blows fiercely in the valley of Tamayamatauge, where Karu-shi, who served under General Ranchi—the great leader of the Brahman Kyō who ascended to conquer the mansion of Iso—clashed with Rebu and others, who were renowned for serving as children of the house of Onikumawake; as a result, they both fell into the valley and fell into a state of unconsciousness, and while walking through the withered fields of the Iukai, they rested upon a rock in the middle of the field, when the eight men who were subordinates of Karu were led away by two red and black demons, heading toward the Ichizu River through a field overgrown with withered grass, while Rebu and Karu, being led by blue demon slaves, finally arrived at the crossing of the Shōzu River, and when they looked, it was a vast river with no end in sight, its clear current flowing tautau while spitting out white foam, appearing like a writhing Orochi, and a single house on the riverbank was a Giyokurō sparkling with Konkuō, dazzling to the eyes.

The blue demon opened the golden gate and hid himself inside the mansion, and after a while, hearing a voice saying that they had finally lured the two men here and that they should be accepted, the former demon bowed and vanished to parts unknown; as the two stood on the riverbank, they whispered about the strangeness of this beautiful house that did not suit the land, when suddenly, a clear female voice, as if shaking a golden bell, called out for them to come quickly, and as they stared with eyes of wonder and approached, they thought they had been mistaken to think her an old demon hag, for she was a beautiful woman of peerless light, appearing to be sixteen or seventeen years of age, with a graceful figure that would make even flowers shy away, smiling very pleasantly; the two were surprised and exchanged words with the woman at the river's edge for a while, but when they entered into the inner room, the inner room was a field of grass, and the Shōzu River was roaring tautau just as before.

Led into a crystal mansion, they lost the exit and were perplexed, and as they stood there exchanging many words and begging for salvation, the beauty from the riverbank took the hands of the two, appeared before that ugly hut, and spoke: 'The house you have entered until now is but a model of the dwellings of the Shaba and the Shinkai; this thatched hut is the checkpoint of Datsueba-sama, where the old demon hag dwells for eternity, stripping the skin of the dead who have piled up heavy sins in the Shaba and are setting out on their journey to the Jūman-okudo.' No sooner had she said this than the maiden suddenly turned into an ugly old hag, stretched out her withered, shriveled hands, and grabbed the two by their bare necks; because of the nastiness and coldness of it, the two leaped into the middle shallows of the Shōzu River, plunged in with a splash, and swimming with powerful strokes, they finally managed to reach the opposite shore.

The two, still in their clothes, had crossed the limitlessly wide river with unexpected ease and safety, and feeling as if they had performed a great feat, they could not contain their sense of exhilaration; gazing at the surface of the river, they looked into the deep blue waves.

Rebu: 'Though I was grabbed by the neck by the old demon hag and jumped into this river at the risk of my life, I thought in the middle of this vast river that I could never cross it safely, but contrary to this torrent, it is a mystery beyond knowing that we crossed so easily and swiftly in a straight line, just as an arrow flies.'

Karu: 'That is the point where the Genkai and the Shinkai differ. Look there. Before we knew it, the river has sunk somewhere, and beautiful flowers are blooming in full glory, are they not? Ah, a fragrance beyond knowing strikes my nose. Look there. It is not a river. It is like the Garden of Eden.'

Rebu: 'Yes, indeed, indeed, what a mysterious thing this is. White plum blossoms are blooming here and there on the branches of large trees. Roses, peonies, clover, white lotuses, and various other flowers are blooming until there is no room left. Truly, the state of heaven is different. Now that we have come to such a place, I never want to return to the Genkai, where life is nothing but falsehoods, for ten thousand eons. Say, Karu-san, you and I, out of a little bit of stubbornness, were grinding our teeth against each other for the sake of our masters, calling it loyalty or duty, trying to boast of our honor, snarling at each other like shaggy dogs set upon by hunters, and killing each other though we had no grudges; but like dragons and tigers whose power was not equal, we finally met our end at the bottom of the valley of Tamayamatauge, and it became our departure for the Shinkai. When I think of this, there is no one more pitiful than the fellows in the Genkai.'

However, thanks to the fact that you and I engaged in a competition of hypocrisy, we have escaped the sufferings of Shiyaba and have been able to come to such a Tengoku Jiyōdo; who is to say what might turn out to be for our Gotsugafu? The enemy of Kinofu is the ally of Kefu, and even the roar of the tiger and wolf has become the floral Kunpū passing through the flower garden of Gokuraku. Now that we have come to Shinkai, there is no need for Meiijiyufuku, nor is there any Yochi for us to quarrel. We wander through the Kuōdaimuhen flower garden as we please, pick and eat the fruits of the trees in Jiiujizai, and abandon all Keirui to travel through Tengoku alone; is there anything more Yukuwai than this? However, since it is said that there is Zeninzenguwa and Akuinakukuwa, it must be that to be able to come to such a place, one must have exhausted all good in Genkai. If we look back on our past, there is no reason why we should ever be able to come to such a place. Perhaps Ōkami has made a mistake in the person, or perhaps there has been a misunderstanding. If that were the case, we would be in great trouble. This beautiful and joyful situation might suddenly change, and we might be cast into the hellish path of supreme ugliness and suffering. Thinking of this, a sense of attachment arises after all. No matter how much one says to remove attachment, how could attachment not remain in this Tengoku? Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haemase. Please, Ōkami, even if it is but a dream, I pray that You will keep us in this state of being forever.

He is joining his hands and worshipping heaven and earth with all his might. Before they knew it, the ground where the two were standing had risen by about twenty ken, and a level, broad road had appeared in the low-lying areas to the left and right, where various kinds of people and birds and beasts could be seen coming and going to and fro.

Rebu: 'Hey, the situation has suddenly changed again. Oi, Karu, if you are not careful, you do not know what might happen, so you cannot let your guard down even for a moment.'

Just as he was speaking, a scream like that of a monkey being strangled was heard from about two or three chō ahead. The two did not say a word, and thinking that if someone were being persecuted by an Akuma, they must save them, they dashed off in silence. When they drew near, they saw a round-bellied man with a pale face, wearing a white robe hanging loosely, holding a blood-stained sword in his right hand, and gripping the nape of a beautiful child of about four or five years old with his left hand, just about to plunge a short sword into the child's chest.

Rebu and Karu, forgetting themselves, leaped at the man, trying to snatch away the blood-stained sword and save the child, but although they struggled with all their might, the man in the white robe did not move an inch, as if he were a rock grown from the earth, no matter how much they pushed or struck him. In the blink of an eye, he cruelly stabbed the child to death.

Rebu: 'You Akuma, do you know where this place is? How can you perform such a brutal act in such a noble Tengoku?'

Man: 'Aha! How ridiculous. For you to call me an Akuma, when you are but a vessel of an Akuma yourself, what is the meaning of this? That a dung beetle does not know the stench of dung is a description of you. Now, it is your turn; do not move from there. Ihi! What a lovely thing this is. You fake, fraudulent ghost, because of the Karma of your soul, I, this Tenrai Bosatsu, shall now defeat you, so you had better be happy to receive my blade.'

Rebu: 'Aha! What are you spouting, calling yourself Tenrai Bosatsu? Even if one is a Bosatsu, is there such a thing as brandishing a lethal weapon and committing murder on the streets of Tengoku? Furthermore, to murder an innocent child is an outrageous act. Hey, Akuma, or rather Tenrai, listen well. We are Karu and Rebu, the masters of martial arts who were sung of in the world as heroes and great men in Brahmanism. Even if small fry like you were to gather in the millions and attack us with martial spirit, it would be like a swarm of mosquitoes attacking a Senbiki-no-iwa. Now, I shall show you our martial prowess, snatch your sword away, and hack you to pieces; you had better be prepared. You had better recite some divine text as a memento of Shinkai.'

男『イツヒヽヽヽ其方は現界に於て一つの善事もなさず、まぐれ当りに神界へふみ迷うて来よつて、一角善人面をさらして、ツベコベと理屈を囀つてゐやがるが、此悪魔も此血刀も、皆貴様の心の反映だ。貴様は八岐大蛇の悪魔の憑いた大黒主の部下に仕ふる鬼春別の乾児の乾児の其乾児たる小悪人で居ながら、三才の童子に等しき天の下の青人草の生血を吸ひ、少しの武勇を鼻にかけ、修羅の戦場に疾駆した其罪が今ここに顕現してゐるのだ。要するに此方は貴様の罪が生んだ悪魔だから、貴様が本当に神直日大直日に見直し宣直し、発ごんと改心を致したならば、かかる尊き神界の大道に如何して俺が現はれる事が出来ようか。俺が亡ぼしたくば、貴様の心から改心したがよからう。人が悪魔だと思うて居れば、みんな自分の事だぞ。コリヤ、レーブ、其方は今の先黄金姫に出会ひ、三五教の教理を聞いたであらう。人が悪いと思うてゐると皆われの事ぢやぞよ………と玉山峠の岩蔭で聞かされたぢやないか』

レーブ『成程さうすると、お前は俺の言はば副守護神だなア。何と悪い副守が居やがつたものだなア』

男『アハヽヽヽ都合のよい勝手な事をいふな。副守護神所か、貴様の本守護神の断片だ。トコトン改心致さぬと、まだまだ此先で貴様の生んだ鬼が貴様に肉迫して、どんな目に会はすか知れぬぞ。己が刀で己が首切るやうなことが出来致すから、早く改心致したがよからう。レーブばかりでない、カルも其通りだ、此童子はヤツパリ、カルの身魂の化身だ。どうだ判つたか』

レーブ『ヤア判つた、斯うして二人仲よくして神界の旅行をやつてゐるものの、本当のことを言へば、おれも淋しくて仕方がないから、道伴れにしようと思ひ、表面こそ親切に打解けたらしくしてゐるものの、行く所まで行つたならば斯様な悪人は此下に見ゆる地獄道へつき落してやらうと、心の端に思うてゐたのだ。ヤア悪かつた、オイ、カル公、俺は本当に済まなかつた。心の罪を赦してくれ』

§ 4

Karu: 'Ah, is that so? Though I have been walking with you in an open manner, I was inwardly trembling with fear, thinking that I knew not when you might pull off my head. If that is the case, then that Dōji was the manifestation of my own Kiyōfushin. Since you have had such a Kaishin, I shall no longer fear you. Let us open our hearts to one another, be friends from the bottom of our hearts, and wander through this Tengoku together. Ah, Kamunagara Chihahe Mase.'

Joining their Riyōte, the Riyōnin closed their eyes and offered a Kiguwan to the Tenchi. After a while, they opened their eyes and looked around, and there was neither the shadow of the man nor the shadow of the Dōji; what had appeared to be blood flowing upon the Daichi were flowers of crimson, blooming and fragrant in Funpun, and butterflies with beautiful wings of white, yellow, purple, and blue were dancing and playing, Henpon, aiming for the flowers. The Riyōnin awakened for the first time from the illusions of their hearts, and while reciting the Amatsunorito, they proceeded, holding hands, ever more intimately toward the north, and yet further north.
