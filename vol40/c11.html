Chapter 11: 三途館 [1095]

三途館〔1095〕

§ 1

The four directions are desolate, without even the sound of insects, and one proceeds through a boundless withered field, buffeted by a demonic wind that defies description.

On one side of the path, there is a man resting, leaning against a square rock that looks as if it has been stained with fresh blood. Toward him, seven or eight men come walking, their footsteps unsteady and relying on their staves, while crying out in strange voices, 'Hoi, hoi.' Looking at any of their faces, they are all as pale as earth, and they wear triangular spirit-garments upon their heads. This was a group of the dead traveling through the Iukai. The one who was resting against the rock earlier is Rebu, who had been thrown into the valley bottom of Tamayamatauge by Haruzau and rendered unconscious. Those coming from behind were Karu and seven or eight of his Brahmanist retainers. Karu had been thrown by Ōgonhime and rendered unconscious, and the other dead were all members of the group who had been dealt with by Rebu.

Seeing the figure of Rebu, Karu says,

Karu: 'Yo, good morning. So you have also come to a place like this? You are a man of good company. If we are to die, let us die together on the journey to the Sanzu River; let us join hands and go to the Jūman-okudo, through the Blood Pool Hell, the Mountain of Needles, and the Eight Cold Hells. Now that it has come to this, unlike in the Genkai, there is no need for honor or property in the Iukai. Consequently, strife and resentment are also unnecessary. My only regret is that, despite my expectation that the merit of believing in the Brahman God during my lifetime would earn me a place in the heaven of supreme happiness and joy, that hope has been completely dashed, and I am left only to wander through this lonely, withered field. But this cannot be helped. Come, Rebu-sama, let us go together.'

Rebu: 'Ya, everyone, you are all here. Karu-sama, you may have no memory of me, but as for the others, it is as if I were the one who sent you on this journey to the afterlife. However, I am not yet dead, so I decline to be treated as one of the dead. Rebu-sama, the hero and man of valor who continued to fight a fierce battle upon the Chibiki-no-iwa, is still young; how could I die at a time like this? Since this Rebu-sama has the protection of the living God, there is no fear of me dying so easily. You all came to drag me to the afterlife by saying sweet things, did you not? My, what a wicked man you are. Will you not clear away your worldly attachments already? If you wander into a place like this, you will not be able to go to a splendid heaven. Namu Karu Tonshō Bodai, I pray that you may be saved to heaven. Kannagara Tamachi Haese.'

He puts his hands together in prayer.

Karu: 'Oi, Rebu, what are you dazed about? This is not the Shaba. It is the gateway to the Iumeikai, the middle of the withered field. Come, let us advance from here. Various demons may appear and throw all sorts of difficult questions at us, but that too is of our own making; since each of us has things in our hearts that we remember, it cannot be helped whatever may appear. Since we are all being tormented by the demons of our own kin that we created within our hearts, there is no path but to resign ourselves.'

Rebu: 'Ha ha ha ha, what are you spouting, you who are one of the dead? If the blue, red, and black demons come with iron clubs, you will be the one to lose your nerve, lose your sight, and have to undergo a second journey through the Iukai. No matter what you say, this Rebu-sama has no memory of having died.'

Karu: 'Well, it does not matter. If we go as far as we can, we will know well whether we are dead or alive.'

Just as they were speaking, a naked demon with a rough-hewn appearance like a Nio, with bright red horns, appeared suddenly from the withered grass, wearing a tiger-skin loincloth pulled tight, with pale, ox-like horns protruding from its forehead, and shouted in a loud voice,

Demon: 'Oi, you dead ones.'

Rebu realized for the first time that he had indeed come to the afterlife. However, since he had died in the very midst of transmitting the path of the true God, he firmly believed that he would never be persecuted or oppressed by such demons. He firmly believed that if he only used the Kototama of good words and beautiful phrases, they would vanish instantly, so he was not as surprised as the other dead, and he looked at the faces of the demons with composure. The demon gave a slight nod to the two of them, Rebu and Karu, and with a relatively gentle face,

'Hey, Goriyōninsama, since I will guide you from here on, come to the bank of the Shōzu River. As for the other Yatsura... it would be best if you follow the black and red Riyōoni, take a right here, and go. Now, go!'

As he made as if to chase them while brandishing an Iboibo-covered Kanabō, the eight Maujiya were led away by the Akakuro-oni and vanished into the distance of the Baubau withered moor.

The Aōni sent Rebu and Karu ahead, and they finally arrived at a wide riverbank where the Minaoto could be heard Sōsō. On the Kahabe stood a neat, small house of Ōgonzuku, which one could not tell what it was. The Aōni opened the Kanado with a clatter, went inside, and said,

'I have just brought two Maujiya from the Shiyaba. Please accept them.'

He greeted them with Teinei. Rebu and Karu looked at each other's faces and whispered in low voices,

Rebu: 'Hey, this has turned into a strange affair, hasn't it? If we are told to cross this great river, that would be a real disaster. Didn't the Oni just say... he brought two Maujiya and asked them to accept them? Since even a fierce-looking, hateful Oni is greeting them with such Teinei, there must be a much stronger great Oni here. Should I try to escape back the way we came while there is still time?'

Karu: 'Even if you say we should escape, we do not know the geography, and in this withered moor where there is not a single obstacle, we would be found immediately. It might be more profitable to act humbly, complete the negotiations smoothly, and have them send us to a good place.'

Just as they were talking, the Aōni turned toward the two and bowed his head repeatedly in a Teinei manner,

'I shall take my leave now. I have told the master of the mansion everything from beginning to end, so please go in as you please and talk at your leisure.'

Saying this, he strode away with long steps, swinging his Kanabō lightly as he returned the way he had come.

Afterward, the two looked at each other with puzzled faces,

Rebu: 'Hey, it seems this is the Shōzu River, but what a strange river it is. The waves are divided and flowing in three stages. It is a river where one cannot even tell if it is flowing vertically or horizontally.'

Karu: 'Hey, what kind of river is that? This mansion might surely be the guard station of the Onibaba of the Shōzu River. Here, we will have our clothes stripped off completely. If that happens, we will be left naked as the future turns toward the winter sky, and the curtain may rise on the tragedy of setting out for the Hachikan-jigoku. We have gotten ourselves into a troublesome situation.'

Just as they were speaking, the door of the mansion was pushed open, and what appeared was a beautiful girl of twelve or thirteen years old.

Rebu: 'Well, I was completely mistaken. When one speaks of the Datsue-ba of the Shōzu River, I thought there would be a starving demon with a hideous face, looking as if it would eat people, waiting there. But instead, two girls of twelve or thirteen, still with their shoulder tucks, come out with gentle faces. They say that in the Yūkai everything is the reverse of the Genkai, so perhaps these so-called young girls of the Genkai are the hags of the Yūkai. If one says girl, in the Yūkai it must mean hag. If one says hag, in the Yūkai it must mean girl. If one says girl...'

Karu: 'Hey, hey, you keep saying the same thing over and over, what are you mumbling about? It will be bad if the girls hear you.'

'It was so mysterious that I just happened to say such things.'

The two girls placed their hands on the floor in a Teinei manner,

'Are you Rebu-sama and Karu-sama? Please, the Princess has been waiting for you for some time. We have also prepared a meal, so please rest and eat at your leisure.'

Rebu: 'Oh, how stylish they are. If that is the case, then this is still the Genkai after all. On this beautiful riverbank, I do not know whose servants they are, but they have gathered many little girls to beg for tips, prepare feasts, and demand high prices, all to strip us bare. Hey, if we are not careful, it might be fine if it is just our clothes, but our souls might be extracted by these women. Because more than any Onibaba, the most terrifying thing is a beautiful woman.'

カル『コリヤ鬼婆、俺の部下がそんな所へ行つているのに、何だ気味がよささうに、其笑ひ態は…貴様こそよい悪垂婆だ。何故一途の川をこんな婆が渡らぬのだらうかな、のうレーブ』

Baba: 'How could a hag like me, who guards the Sekishiyo of the Iukai, possess such things as Jihi or Dōjiyō? I am the Watashimori of the Shōzu River precisely because I am an Akunin. When a Zennin comes, I transform into a girl like the one you saw Saizen; when a fellow from the Genkai comes, I transform into a girl with a Kirei Uhakaha but a filthy Nakatsura. When an Akunin comes, I become this terrifying hag. I am a hag who transforms according to the Kokoroshidai of those who come here.'

Reibu: 'In that case, since the demons have not yet dragged me off to the Ichizu River, there must be some hope for me somewhere. Very well, then I shall make a Funpatsu and set out on a Shinkai Riyokau. Hey, Karu, you follow me and cross the Nakatsuse.'

Karu: 'Very well, I shall be your Michizu no matter how far we go.'

Riyōnin: 'No, Baba-sama, we have caused you a Taihen Jiyama. If there is a Goen, we shall meet again. Sayō, stay healthy, stay Gobuji, stay Otatsushiya... no, I hope you drop dead soon, ho ho ho!'

Baba: 'Hey, you! Even after coming to the Reikai, you still speak with such lack of discipline and spout such abuse! Even if you say you are going to the Shinkai, I will not let you go!'

She raised her thorn staff and chased after them with such ferocity. The two of them plunged into the Nakatsuse with a splash, and swimming with all their might, they finally reached the opposite shore.
