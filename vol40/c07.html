Chapter 7: 文珠 [1091]

文珠〔1091〕

§ 1

Terukuniwake, while resting in the forest of Kuru with the Sendenshi Terukō, Umekō, and Ihahiko, encountered a troop led by Katahiko, a Bushiyō under Oniharuwake, advancing toward the Iso mansion. They hid secretly in the thicket of trees to observe the Yōsu. The troop of several dozen cavalrymen led by Katahiko crossed the Lion River, galloped for over a hundred chō, and finally arrived at the forest of Kuru. Some dismounted to rest their Jinba, while others wandered through the forest or lay down to indulge in Zatsudan.

This troop was a formation engaged in a Jiyūeu Ninmu that could be called either the vanguard or the scouting party of the Kōgekigun heading toward the Iso mansion.

After resting for a while, Katahiko nimbly leaped back onto his horse, performed a Tenko of his Jinin, and shouted in a loud voice from his Bajiyō to issue orders:

Katahiko: 'From this point forward is a location that can be called the sphere of influence of the Sangokyō. As General Ōashiwake of Mount Kiyoharu is currently absent due to the march to the Karmata country, the defenses are sparse and hardly sufficient to be a force. Although General Ranch of the main unit shall advance from behind, we have our own mission. Keep your hearts alert to the surroundings, watch the left and right, and from here to the north, the utmost caution is required.'

While issuing these orders, the party of Terukuniwake, hidden in the shade of the trees, heard that this was the vanguard marching on the Iso mansion. Even if they were few in number, they could not let them pass as they were. They had to find a way to drive this vanguard back. Terukuniwake was lost in thought with his arms crossed, fearing that if he let them pass while encountering the enemy, he would be told that he had allowed them to approach Mount Ubusuna, and thus he would have failed in his duties toward Tamakuniwake who was coming from behind.

Ihahiko, feeling anxious, spoke:

Ihahiko: 'Terukuniwake-sama, a grave situation has arisen. It appears to be the troop of Katahiko. We must not let them advance further into the interior, so shall we not devise some method here? According to your words earlier, it was said that the Sangokyō is a doctrine of non-resistance everywhere, but the enemy advances with military force. Even with the miraculous use of Kototama, it will not be an easy task to fight against an enemy ten times our number. It will surely be impossible without resorting to military force, so please sing the Sendenka and awe the spirits of the demons. I, this Ihahiko, shall use my specialty staff, leap into the very midst of the enemy, and ensure they do not advance even one step further. I shall never do anything to kill or injure the enemy. It is merely to intimidate the enemy and drive them back to where they came from...'

Terukuniwake: 'Kōganhime and Kiyoteruhime are supposed to have gone as the vanguard. If we drive them back, it would be like causing both of them to be caught in a pincer attack by the enemy force coming from behind. Alas, a troublesome situation has occurred. Our mission is to bring the Ōkuninushi of the capital of Haruna to submission, and we should not be dealing with such petty warriors as them. Yet, knowing they are a troop advancing on the Iso mansion, to fail to prevent them would be to fail in our duties. At any rate, let us begin a battle against Katahiko's troop using Kototama. If that does not work, there will be no choice but to adopt the method of using the staff as Ihahiko suggests to scatter the enemy. First of all, let us borrow the power of the Lord God and fight well. Terukō and Umekō, you should also prepare yourselves.'

Terukō: 'This is the first time I have encountered an enemy army, and there is nothing more exhilarating than this. This must be the time to test the divine power of my Kototama.'

Though he said this boldly, his voice trembled for some reason.

Umekō: 'Sendenshi-sama, I ask that you be aware that if, by any chance, we are trampled by the enemy's hooves and our lives are in danger, we may have to resist. I am not skilled at using weapons like Ihahiko-sama, but I must find a way to defend myself and protect my life.'

§ 2

He seemed to be forgetting his important Shimei and was only worrying about his own Anzen. Ihahiko was already gripping his staff, and like an arrow at the moment of leaving the string, he stood with his body slanted, waiting impatiently for the command of Terukuniwake. At this moment, the enemy could be seen advancing northward with their horses' heads aligned.

Terukuniwake sang a Sendenka with a clear voice.

The Jizaiten of the land of Tokoyo, / Worshipping Ōkunihiko, / The Kamuyakata of the Brahman Kyō, / In the land of the moon where the sky shines through, / Appearing in the capital of Haruna, / The other Mina of Onikumohiko, / Gathering the Rautau of Ōkuronushi, / Day by day and month by month, / Regaining their strength once again, / Arrogant and proud, now already, / With noses held high in Jikaujiman, / Advancing to attack the Iso-yakata, / Where Kamususanōnōokami resides in peace, / How brave is their Idetachi, / Even if Katahiko is brave, / Can they not oppose the Kototama of the Sango-kyō, / Which shakes the Ametsuchi and commands the Raiden and Fūu at will?

Ah, Kannagara, / Look back upon the heart of the Kami, / Rather than starting a reckless war, / Return to sincerity as soon as possible, / O Katahiko, / I am a child of the Kami and you are also, / A precious child of the noble Kami, / Let the child and the child harmonize, / Fulfilling the great path of the Kami of the Ametsuchi of one sincerity, / Saving and helping the Kamibito under heaven, / Becoming the pillars of the Kamikuni, Kannagara, / Vowing sincerity to the Kami, / Facing your army, / Pulling out the Kototama-guruma, / I am the Kami Terukuniwake, / Terukō who illuminates this world, / Umekō who opens and blooms with the divine authority of the Kami at once.

The Senden-shi with firm hearts, / The four, including Ihahiko-tsukasa, / Left the Iso-yakata, / And advanced to this Kurusu-mori, / Hiding and lying in the shade of the trees, / Having heard the story of your troop, / Completely and in detail, / Now that I have understood, how could I, / Let you pass through as you are?

O General Katahiko, who serves as a subordinate of Oniharubake, / Look into the mirror of your heart, / At the words of Terukuniwake of Sango, / Where the divine army of the Kototama-tai appeared as brave warriors, / Very peacefully and calmly, / Reflect upon it, Kannagara, / I swear to the Kami and preach it to you.

At the sound of the Sendenka suddenly heard from within the forest, Katahiko and all his men were taken by surprise; they stopped their horses for a while, and a look of hesitation began to appear. The four or five knights waiting behind were struck by the Kototama, and for some reason, they became fearful; it even appeared as if they were already turning their horses' heads, ready to flee. Seeing this state, Katahiko grew impatient, thinking that if he hesitated, it would lead to a lack of unity among his allies and would be nothing but disadvantageous, so he raised his voice.

Katahiko: 'Hey, all you knights! A group of Senden-shi of the Sango-kyō has appeared. We, the brave warriors of the divine army who have received the divine power of the Great Jizaiten Ōkunihiko-kami in our bodies, should not hesitate before them; trample them to death under your horses' hooves!'

As he gave this strict command, he spurred his horse, and while whipping the back of his horse severely toward Terukuniwake, he advanced to crush him. Terukuniwake remained calm and composed, reciting the heavenly number-song, and began to sing the Sendenka once again. However, for the Magami whose hearts were completely clouded, the power of the Sendenka did not penetrate sufficiently, and the enemy attacked with all their might. Ihahiko, who had been waiting with his arms ready for their fierce onslaught, said, 'Terukuniwake-dono, please forgive me,' and like an arrow released from a string, he charged at the enemy while swinging his Kongō-zue with a roar in all directions, and in an instant, he kicked the horses' legs one after another. The horses were startled and stood up, the knights on horseback fell to the ground headlong, and abandoning their horses, they fled in all directions. Katahiko, still on horseback, dashed off toward the south at full speed, while Ihahiko mounted an enemy horse and, whipping the horse's belly with his staff, chased after Katahiko at full speed.

Terukuniwake remained calm and composed, still singing the Sendenka. Many knights fell and rolled about in all directions as they pleased. However, not a single person fled toward the north, perhaps out of fear. There were seven or eight horses lying on the ground, having been struck on the knees by Ihahiko, and they were groaning here and there. Terukō and Umekō divided the work and cared for two enemies who, when falling from their horses, had thrust their heads into the ground, dislocated their shoulder bones, and were groaning in the agony of a narrow escape from death. Terukuniwake gave Chinkon to the wounded enemies with all his might. Gradually, the neck bones returned to their original state through the care of the two, and the dislocated shoulder blades were also healed as they were before.

The two knights, having gradually recovered under the care of the three, were greatly surprised to see the faces of the three Ananai-kyō Sendenshi and believers, as none of their own allies were to be found in the vicinity.

Keris and Taakusu: 'We are two men who have been under the patronage of General Katahiko and have become Sendenshi of the Brahman religion. Please, from this day and this moment, we shall surrender to Ananai-kyō, so we beg you to spare our lives.'

They pleaded, shedding tears in torrents. Terukuniwake changed his tone, and while tending to them with great courtesy, said:

Terukuniwake: 'So you were indeed Sendenshi of the Brahman religion. The world is a place of mutual aid; it is a world of helping and being helped, of give and take. Ananai-kyō will never commit such inhumane acts as killing or wounding enemies like the Brahman religion does, so you may rest assured. Now, there is something I must entrust to you two. Would you not stop by Kiyoharuyama and go as messengers to the Iso Mansion?'

Keris and Taakusu: 'Yes. Now that we have become your disciples, we shall accept any task. However, the mere thought of going to the Iso Mansion fills me with a strange sense of dread.'

Terukuniwake: 'Ananai-kyō is a faith that saves even its enemies, so there is no reason for you to be tormented. Furthermore, if you say you are disciples of Terukuniwake, they will surely treat you with great care. I shall write a letter now; take it and stop by Kiyoharuyama, and then go to the Iso Mansion to meet Hinodewake-kami, and I shall arrange for you to practice the path of Ananai at the Iso Mansion for a while.'

The two replied:

Keris and Taakusu: 'Yes.'

Having said that, they were overcome with tears of gratitude, mounted their horses again, and set off toward the north. One letter was addressed to Poolo at Kiyoharuyama, urging him to surrender, and the other was a report from Terukuniwake to Hinodewake-kami regarding all the details he had encountered during his expedition, requesting that he let these two learn the doctrines of Ananai-kyō, noting that if he were to employ them as Sendenshi in the future, they would likely achieve commendable results, and asking for his kind consideration. The two were moved from the bottom of their hearts by the compassion of Terukuniwake, and eventually stopped by Kiyoharuyama to deliver the letter to Poolo, then proceeded to the Iso Mansion to learn the doctrines, and furthermore, they revealed and recounted every detail of the operational plan for the Brahman religion's Iso Mansion to attack, thereby providing great convenience.

The details of the two stopping by Kiyoharuyama, causing Poolo and others to surrender, and other interesting developments will be described in a later chapter.

Returning to the story, Iwahiko mounted his steed and galloped toward the southwest, chasing after the fleeing Katahiko with all his might. When he arrived near the Lion River, a troop of dozens of cavalrymen under General Kugihiko once again shot arrows at Iwahiko, aiming at him alone along with Katahiko, and attacked. Iwahiko became a target for the entire troop, his body pierced by arrows until he looked like a hedgehog. However, Iwahiko, having transcended the boundary between life and death, moved with the force of a lion, galloping back and forth, attempting to trample the troop with his horse's hooves, but exhausted by his severe wounds, he fell heavily from his horse to the ground and lost consciousness. Just as General Katahiko and General Kugihiko thought this was their moment and dismounted to behead Iwahiko, dozens of Chinese lions appeared from nowhere with a roar so loud it seemed to shake the mountains. A large man was mounted on the back of the most gigantic lion among them, and while emitting a powerful divine light from between his brows, he charged toward Kugihiko's troop. Daunted by that force, they threw away their weapons, abandoned their horses, and scattered in all directions. At the roar of the lion, Iwahiko started and regained consciousness, and when he looked around, a divine person, emitting spiritual light from between his brows, was sitting nearby on the back of the giant lion, smiling. Iwahiko forgot the pain in his body, stood up, knelt, and thanked him for the grace of saving his life. When he looked closely, to his surprise, it was Tokiokishi-kami, a hero of great renown in Ananai-kyō. Overwhelmed by surprise and joy, Iwahiko...

清春山に二人が立寄り、ポーロ其外を帰順せしめたる一条や其他の面白き経路は項を改めて述ぶることとする。

話は元へ返つて、岩彦は駿馬に跨り、逃げゆく片彦の後を、己も馬に跨つて一目散に西南指して駆け行く。ライオン河の近くまでやつて来ると、釘彦将軍の一隊又もや数十騎、片彦と共に岩彦一人を目がけて弓を射かけ、攻めかくる。岩彦は一隊の的となり、身体一面矢に刺され、蝟の如くなつて了つた。されど生死の境を超越したる岩彦は獅子奮迅の勢を以て、馬の蹄にて一隊を踏み躙らむと、前後左右をかけ巡りつつあつたが、身体の重傷に疲れ果て、ドツと馬上より地上に転落し、人事不省となつて了つた。片彦、釘彦将軍は今此時と、馬を飛び下り、岩彦の首を刎ねむとする時、何処ともなく山岳も崩るるばかりの大音響と共に数十頭の唐獅子現はれ来り、其中にて最も巨大なる獅子の背に大の男跨り、眉間より強烈なる神光を発射しながら、釘彦の一隊に向つて突込み来る、其勢に辟易し、得物を投げ棄て、或は馬を棄て、四方八方に散乱して了つた。獅子の唸り声に岩彦はハツと気が付きあたりを見れば、巨大なる獅子の背に跨り、眉間より霊光を発射する神人が側近く莞爾として控へてゐる。岩彦は体の痛みを忘れ起直り、跪いて救命の恩を謝した。よくよく見れば嵩計らむや、三五教にて名も高き英雄豪傑の時置師神であつた。岩彦は驚きと喜びの余り、

Ihahiko: 'Ah, Anata is Mokusukesama, how did you know of my saunan? It is truly wonderful that you have saved me.'

Mokusuke laughed heartily, Mokusuke: 'Nay, Ihahiko, you must never again engage in ranbau. To resort to bauriyoku and trouble your enemies, while being a sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō, is an action contrary to Goshinriyo. You must fight the good fight to the end, unleash the Shinriki of Kototama, and if that does not suffice, it is never against Shinriyo to watch for an opening and make an ichiji taikiyaku. From here, you shall mount this shishi, cross the Lion River, and save Ōgonhime and Kiyoteruhime from their saunan; farewell.'

No sooner had he spoken than the figure of Mokusuke vanished like smoke, and the shadows of the amata shishi were gone, leaving only itsutō of a kiyodai karajishi, which sat with its riyōashi together, behaving with perfect decorum. The one who had appeared as Mokusuke was, in truth, Konohana-hime-no-Mikoto, who, by the command of the Miroku Ōkami, had temporarily assumed the form of Mokusuke to save Ihahiko from his peril. From this moment, Ihahiko, alone, mounted the karajishi, crossed the Lion River, and set off in haste to pursue them, intending to save Ōgonhime from her critical danger.

At this time, the form of Ihahiko had become transparent, as if it were made of tortoiseshell. The one whom the Buddhists call Monju Bosatsu is the spirit of Ihahiko the sendenshi. From this point on, with the aid of the shishi, Ihahiko traversed the Moon Country in every direction, appearing and disappearing in various places, and came to save and protect the divine army of the Three-Five whenever they were in critical danger.

『ヤア貴神は杢助様、如何して私の遭難が分りましたか、よくマア助けて下さいました』

杢助はカラカラと打笑ひ、

『イヤ岩彦、今後は決して乱暴なことは致してはなりませぬぞ。苟くも三五教の宣伝使たる身を以て暴力に訴へ敵を悩まさむとするは御神慮に反する行動である。飽迄善戦善闘し、言霊の神力を発射し、それにしても行かなければ、隙を覗つて一時退却するも、決して神慮に背くものではない。汝は之より此獅子に跨り、ライオン河を渡り、黄金姫、清照姫の遭難を救ふべし、さらば』

といふより早く杢助の姿は煙と消え、数多の獅子の影もなく、只一頭の巨大なる唐獅子のみ両足を揃へ、行儀よく坐つてゐた。今杢助と現はれたのは、其実は五六七大神の命に依り、木花姫命が仮りに杢助の姿を現はし、岩彦の危難を救はれたのである。岩彦は之より只一人唐獅子に跨り、ライオン河を打渡り、黄金姫の危急を救ふべく、急ぎ後を追ふこととなつた。

此時、岩彦の姿は何時の間にやら透き通り、恰も鼈甲の如くなつてゐた。仏者の所謂文珠菩薩は岩彦の宣伝使の霊である。之より岩彦は月の国を縦横無尽に獅子の助けに依りて、所々に変幻出没し、三五の神軍を、危急の場合に現はれて救ひ守ることとなつたのである。
