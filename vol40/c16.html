Chapter 16: Spring 駒 [1100]

春駒〔1100〕

§ 1

Harukō, having been saved from a severe illness by the Sendenshi, felt gratitude toward them as his saviors of life. He now surrendered himself completely to the Shintoku of Ananahi, and it was decided that he would proceed as a guide for the party, following them toward the Capital of the Moon. While descending the steep slope of the Thames Pass, Harukō began to sing, keeping time with his feet.

'My birthplace is Armenia. The Mioshihe of the Hiko of Ural, I have revered since the days of my parents; it is a noble and pure lineage. Both my parents have departed this world, and the brothers who remained behind, tossed by the winds of Ukiyo, have become scattered and separated. The whereabouts of my elder brother are still unknown. To open the Mioshihe of the Kami of Ural on Riugūjima, which drifts in the middle of Ōwadanaka, I was appointed as a Kamuzukasa and set forth, I, Ihahiko. Seeking the whereabouts of my brother, I came to the land of Fusa, and just as I was about to cross Wadanaka, steering a small boat, the Kamuzukasa Ōdaruwake appeared at the port of Taru.

He preached the Mioshihe of the Brahman Kyō in great detail. As I mingled with the crowd and listened to the Mioshihe, half-attentively, I became convinced that there was a flavor to it from somewhere unknown, and I became addicted. Untokodotsukoi, what a steep slope! Everyone, please be careful. Slipping into the Brahman Kyō, I eventually became a person serving under the Great Lord God, Daikokunushi-no-Kami. Using my original name, Harukō, I was promoted to be a barrier guard here. I became a messenger sent to search for the whereabouts of the wife and children of Onikumabetsu of the Brahman sect, and to capture all the Kyōshi of Ananahi and send them to the Capital of the Moon. Mount Thames has high peaks.

The wind that blows is fierce, and it is a difficult pass where people do not tread. Nevertheless, to proceed to the Iso Mansion and Mount Ubusuna, this is the primary shortcut. The Kamuzukasa of the Ananahi Kyō will likely cross here for the most part. Obeying the order to build a barrier station on the pass and wait, I spent my mornings and evenings drowning my heart in sake. The essential duty of the barrier guard was, in short, my side job; my main profession was drinking sake. While I was living like that, the Mukade-hime of the Brahman Kyō, Koito-hime, came riding on a horse, climbing up with a 'hi-hi-hi'. When I looked closely, there was a dignity in her presence that was somehow...

Feeling overwhelmed, I feigned ignorance and, displaying a great magnanimity, let her pass through the barrier station with ease. When Mokusuke of the Ananahi Kyō path came riding on a truly terrifying lion, leading a great multitude, and climbed up, my heart trembled and my body shook; I felt as if I were not alive. Aitata-tatsuta, I stumbled. Untokodotsukoi, look at this. Since then, everyone became desperate. On such a dangerous mountain, how could we serve while sober? To deceive our fear, if only for a moment, by drowning it in the intoxication of sake, we drank gulp after gulp of fruit wine until we were dead drunk, and collapsed in the shape of a large character on the wind-swept pass.

As a result of that, the Kami of Colds came along, and—untokodotsukoi, dotsukoishiyo—invaded my body. Suddenly, a headache arose. With a pain as if a hammer were crushing my brain—kan-kan-kan—I was writhing in agony. Just then, the living Kami, Terukunibetsu, the Kamuzukasa of the Ananahi Kyō, appeared and saved my life—dotsukoi. How could such a noble path of Kami exist anywhere else? No matter how noble the Brahman Kyō or the Ural Kyō may be, praying with sincerity from morning till night without a single effect appearing is ridiculous. I have come to hate it.

O Kami Terukunibetsu, although it is said that there is a river ahead where the current of the lion is fierce, I know of one place where it can be crossed with ease. The flow is gentle and the shallows are shallow; it is a perfect spot. Although the path is a little roundabout, it is the only safe zone. Following the proverb 'more haste, less speed,' let us choose that place to cross. Beyond that, there is a small slope of Mount Tama. If Harukō, who is well-versed in the geography, serves at the head, we shall be able to go to the Imperial Capital of the Moon easily, without even realizing it. Please trust me. Untokodotsukoi, dotsukoishiyo.'

§ 2

Mukadehime, who departed just now, and Koito-hime, I wonder where they are and what they are doing at this moment. Thinking that Rebu-sama, who served as their attendant, must surely be lost in the wilderness and suffering a terrible shock, my heart grows anxious. Whipping my heart's steed, I set off at once on my Hizakurige. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. May the Mitama of the Kami grant us blessings, and may this party be led safely and with ease to the capital of victory. Before the presence of the Kami of Ametsuchi, Harukō prays with all his heart. Untokodotsukoi, dotsukoishiyo. There lies a flat road. Terukuniwake-dono, the Sendenshi, shall we take a rest?

Sweet-looking fruits are growing. Because the Sakamichi is so steep, my throat began to play a flute. The intoxication of the sake has faded, and the condition of my head feels somehow hazy. Untokodotsukoi, yatsutokoshiyo.

Singing with such vigor, the party of four descends the Thames Pass with grace.

At a point about seventy or eighty percent down the slope, there is a wide road with a gentle Kuwankōbai. There, the Ichijiku are bursting open like pomegranates, as if waiting for someone.

Harukō: 'Moshi, Sendenshi-sama, these Ichijiku here are famous; when a good person passes by, they grow large like pomegranates just like that, turn a purple-red color, and offer hospitality to the traveler. When a bad person passes, they become small, tight buds, hiding themselves so as not to catch anyone's eye—they are strange Ichijiku. I have heard rumors of this before, but never have I seen them open their mouths and look so sweet as they do today. Surely, it is because a true Ikigami is passing by that they have appeared in such a form. How would it be if we took a break here, prepared our stomachs, moistened our throats, and availed ourselves of the kindness of the Ichijiku-sama?'

Terukuni: 'Since we have come quite a distance, let us decide to take a rest. Haru-sama, I am sorry to trouble you, but would you mind gathering a few of those Ichijiku for me?'

Harukō: 'Hai, I understand. Moshi-moshi, you two attendants, let us go together.'

Umekō: 'That sounds interesting.'

Terukō: 'I will also go with Haru-sama. Sendenshi-sama, please wait here.'

Terukuni: 'Un, yoshi-yoshi, I will wait alone, so please go and bring them back quickly.'

Here, the three went eagerly, managed to cross a narrow valley stream, plucked the sweet-looking Ichijiku until their pockets were full, and returned. The four of them, clicking their throats, received them as a blessing from heaven and filled their stomachs.

Terukuni: 'Haru-sama, according to your song along the way earlier, it seems you have an older brother, and that brother was called Iwahiko, was he not?'

Harukō: 'Hai, my brother is named Iwahiko, and he is a man with a slightly stooped back. By the command of the Kami Uralhiko, he crossed to Ryūgū-jima with the Kamitsukasa known as Oto-hiko and Ume-hiko, and since then, there has been no word from him. The headquarters in Armenia is now like a lonely castle in the setting sun; it has lost its former power, and only a few remaining believers guard the Kamiyakata. It is said that the Kamiyakata has moved to the capital of a fairly large country at the southwestern foot of Chikyō-zan, which they call the Karmata country of the Moon Kingdom. And it is said that Tokoyami-hiko, a descendant of Uralhiko-sama, has become the leader and is restoring the power of the old days. I suddenly longed to meet my brother Iwahiko, and when I was about to boldly board a small boat to cross to one of the islands of Ryūgū, I heard the Kamitsukasa Ōashiwake of the Brahman religion preaching at the port of Taru. I was suddenly moved with gratitude and eventually converted to the Brahman religion. However, as the days and months passed, the gold leaf of the Brahman religion peeled off, its true nature became apparent, and it became uninteresting. Eventually, I became desperate, turned into a heavy drinker, and while I was entrusted as the chief guard of the Thames Mountain barrier, I caught a nasty cold. I was possessed by the spirit of a centipede, and in a situation where I had nine deaths and one life, I was saved by the divine guidance of the Kami and by your hand.'

Umehiko: 'If that is so, were you the younger brother of Ihahiko? I am that very Umehiko. I parted from Ihahiko in the Forest of Kuru, and he leapt into the midst of the Brahman cavalry, and I have not seen his face since; yet I have a feeling that we shall meet before long, so come along and enjoy the journey.'

Haruhiko: 'Are you, then, Umehiko-sama? Now that you say so, I feel as though I have seen you somewhere before. Has my brother Ihahiko been active together with you until this day?'

Umehiko: 'Ihahiko-sama is a truly splendid missionary. Until this day, he has been disguised in the rocky cave of Mount Kiyoharu, the stronghold of the Brahman Ōdaruwake, and has been a man calling himself Yatsukosu.'

Haruhiko: 'Was that Yatsukosu my brother Ihahiko? I had heard the rumors, but I have never met him. Being within the same Brahman sect, we were so separated by place that I remained unaware of it. Ah, how grateful I am; that I have discovered the whereabouts of my brother must be entirely due to the divine guidance of the Kamisama. Ah, Kamunagara chihahe mase.'

He joins his hands in prayer and is choked with tears of gratitude.

Terukō: 'Haru-san, the time has come for your long-cherished wish to be fulfilled as well; the Kamisama of the Sangokyō must be grateful, are they not? You will not be recklessly turning back to the Ural religion or regressing to the Brahman religion anymore, will you?'

Harukō: 'How could I, how could I ever do such a thing? I only pray that, through the grace of the Kamisama, I may be allowed to meet Ihahiko as soon as possible. And we brothers wish to devote ourselves to the teachings of the Sango.'

Umekō: 'Will you give up your Ural-style heavy drinking now?'

Harukō: 'Although it is not a drink I particularly like, the world was so lonely that I could not bear it, and I was drinking out of desperation; from now on, I shall not drink a single drop. In the first place, it is not a drink I am very fond of.'

Terukuniwake sits down on a stone by the roadside and sings:

Through the grace of the Heavenly Gods and Earthly Gods, Today I have learned the whereabouts of my brother. Blessed by the teachings of the Sango God, I shall cross the Lion River in peace. The barrier official who guards the Thames Pass, Has healed the illness of Harukō-sama. Not only healing the illness of the body, but the path of Sango, Heals the illness of the soul. If the wind blows, how cold it must be at the Thames Pass, Where the barrier guard of spring stands. While my spirit was buffeted by the stormy winds, Now I have become a person of sincerity.

As he finishes singing, Harukō replies to this:

Had the messenger of the Sango God not come, I would have departed for the other world. My life and my soul, like a string of jewels, have been saved, How could I ever turn my back on the path of Sango? Drinking heavily morning and evening, I became a dwelling for crooked gods, How detestable I was. In my heart, which rushed to capture the ugly official Mukade-hime, the centipede dwelt. Bound by the divine thread of Koito-hime, I have awakened to the path of the Sangokyō. When I heard of the whereabouts of my brother Ihahiko, I felt as though I had been reborn. Kamunagara, for the sake of the Gods and for the sake of the world, My soul of spring shall not cease to serve.

Having finished singing, they follow Terukuniwake, and the party of four descends the Thames Pass, pointing toward the southwest while singing missionary songs once again.
