§ 4

Speaker: 'No matter how kindly you may speak to me, it is somewhat difficult for me to be helped by a person of the Ananai-kyō.'

Speaker: 'Do you not wish to save your colleague in a case of life and death?'

Speaker: 'With the rosy face of the morning becoming the white bones of the evening, the path of life is one that we must all tread toward death at least once; as this is a world of dreams, it might even be more convenient for Harukō to die here for his own sake. Since your kindness would only go to waste, I humbly beg you to excuse me from performing Chinkon.'

Speaker: 'How remarkably cold-hearted the Brahmanism is, even toward its own friends. That it is even more ruthless toward all living beings can be observed from this single incident; hearing such things, I feel I must by all means reform Brahmanism.'

Speaker: 'In truth, four out of the five gatekeepers are currently unable to take a break, so we are in a difficult spot. As you can see, we are quite busy, so if you have business, please come again tomorrow.'

Speaker: 'Hahaha, they are treating us as if we were beggars. However, even if it is Brahmanism, I cannot simply pass by and not save a person in distress. Terukō-sama, Umekō-sama, you two go inside and perform Chinkon for the sick person sitting there.'

Speaker: 'No, no, that is preposterous; the only sick person is Harukō. The others have only caught a slight nasal catarrh even if they say they have a cold, and they are sleeping soundly after driving away the god of wind, so please do not trouble yourselves.'

Terukō and Umekō, ignoring the details, entered the back and sang the Heavenly Number Song from both sides. Yukikō was startled, lifted his buttocks which had been resting on Harukō's head, and crouched in the corner of the guard hut, trembling. When they had chanted the Heavenly Number Song two or three times, Harukō coughed loudly twice. At that moment, two small centipedes flew out. In the blink of an eye, they grew into large centipedes five or six shaku long and fled down the Thames Pass like an arrow. Harukō's fever broke for the first time, his body returned to its former state, and while wiping away his sweat, he said,

Speaker: 'I do not know who you are, but you have truly saved me in a case of life and death; I am most grateful.'

With a voice of gratitude, he suddenly looked up and saw that it was the three members of the Ananai-kyō missionary group led by Terukuniwake. Harukō, overjoyed and emboldened to find the missionaries who were his saviors, left the four others behind, repented from the bottom of his heart, and followed Terukuniwake as a guide toward the Land of the Moon.
