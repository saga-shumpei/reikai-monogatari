Chapter 4: 珍客 [1088]

珍客〔1088〕

§ 1

At the hour of the horse, a young and beautiful woman wearing a deep-brimmed hat and covering her face appeared at the gate of Onikumawake's mansion. It is an undeniable fact to the reader that this is the new Isonohime. Isonohime said in a cool, slender voice,

Isonohime: 'Excuse me, Monban-sama, please open this gate for a moment. Quickly, quickly.'

She urged them impatiently. The gatekeeper Asanebau had just managed to wake up, and was still rubbing his sleepy eyes without even having performed his morning ablutions.

Asanebau: 'Eh, what is this? To the house of a widowed master, a seductive woman's voice saying, "Excuse me, Monban-sama, please open this gate. Quickly, quickly"—what a fool she takes me for.'

Saying this, he peeked outside for a moment through a knot-hole in the gate, and continued,

Asanebau: 'Oh, what is this? Since she is wearing a deep-brimmed hat, I cannot see what her face looks like, but that figure—this is truly what is meant by a flower-like face and willow-like waist. It is a scene where a "graceful and beautiful woman knocks at the gate to visit her beloved." I thought the master of the house was quite a rigid man, but for such a creature to come visiting, one cannot be too careful. They say the Ananai-kyō will change the world and so on, but truly, if things like this happen, the world might indeed change. Well then, shall I open it?'

As he tried to get up, another gatekeeper, while still lying down, said,

Gonkō: 'Hey, Sutekō, you must not open this gate recklessly. Last night, the chief retainer Kumahiko-sama called me and told me to keep watch at this gate without sleeping. He especially ordered me to be careful for these next two or three days. You must not open it recklessly until we have investigated thoroughly. Sutekō, leave it alone.'

Sutekō: 'Even so, Gon-sama, such a splendid lady is asking with a voice like a bell; how can I stay here without opening it? If it were a man, one might need to worry about danger, but for a delicate woman like that, what danger could there be in letting her through the gate? I have become unable to bear it, even without worrying too much. I will open it.'

Gonkō: 'Did I not tell you to wait? Will you not obey the orders of your superior?'

Sutekō: 'You were so obedient to the orders of the "superior" (hot sake) last night that you got quite drunk, didn't you? You, who had someone warm up such "superior" sake while saying, "This is just slightly warm," and then complained arrogantly, forgetting your essential duties due to a hangover—why do you interfere with my authority when you cannot even hold your head up? I will open the gate by my own authority.'

As he tried to shake him off and go, Gonkō pulled him back firmly and said,

Gonkō: 'Wait, wait, I will investigate it myself.'

Sutekō: 'Hey, you are tearing my clothes, aren't you? Even if my sleeves are pulled by rough men like you, the accounts will never add up. If I am to be pulled, I would rather have that lady pull and tear them... Hehehe, oh, it is tattered, it is tattered. This is why they say one cannot quit being a gatekeeper. Judging by that style, she must be quite a beauty. She is a creature whose elegance of appearance and speech cannot be exhausted by words. Ehehe.'

Gonkō, ignoring all this, looked outside through the knot-hole in the gate and said,

Gonkō: 'Hey, this is serious. I cannot open it carelessly. It is a figure that I cannot quite make sense of. And it is a figure I have seen somewhere before. In any case, wait here until I go and ask the chief retainer. Until then, you must never open it, no matter how much she requests from the outside.'

Having said this, Gonkō ran off toward the inner part of the house. From outside the gate,

Isonohime: 'Excuse me, Monban-sama, it is frustrating, please open it quickly. I have urgent business with Onikumawake-sama. Come, quickly, quickly, it will be terrible if I am seen by people.'

With these parting words, he rattled the gate open. Ishionohime did not even offer a nod, and the moment she stepped over the gate, Sutekō grabbed her sleeve tightly.

Sutekō: 'W-w-wait, please. I do not know what kind of woman you are, but to enter without a nod or a word of thanks after causing this gatekeeper so much trouble and worry is rather impolite, is it not? You may look down on me thinking I am a lowly gatekeeper, but in the eyes of the gods, there is no distinction of high or low among humans.'

Ishionohime: 'Oh, my, this is the gatekeeper-sama, I am sorry. Please forgive me, now hurry and guide me to the side of Onikumawake.'

Sutekō: 'I would love to guide you, but my role is only to guard this gate, and I do not have the authority to guide you to the Ōoku.'

Ishionohime: 'My, what a stubborn and narrow-minded family tradition in a world where equality of the races is proclaimed...'

Sutekō: 'Now, now, Jiyochiu-sama, what are you saying? Is it not rude to start criticizing the family tradition the moment you step into the entrance? Come, come, go inside freely. The Karau Kumahiko should be there, so after negotiating with him, you may have a long and deep talk with the Goshiyujin about the mountains and the seas, and have a good cry to your heart's content after all this time.'
