Chapter 6: 仁 Love の Truth 相 [1090]

仁愛の真相〔1090〕

§ 1

Terukuniwake, accompanied by Ihahiko, Terukō, and Umekō, departed from the grotto of Kiyoharuyama and, while traversing the wilderness of the southwest, finally advanced to the forest of Kurusu, two or three ri before the Raion River; there, the party rested their feet and passed the time in conversation regarding the divine virtues, and Terukuniwake, rising to answer the questions of Terukō and Umekō, began to chant solemnly of the truth of Miroku, the supreme benevolence and supreme love.

The song:

The Savior of the Sanzensekai, / The truth of Mirokunokami, / Is the Great Saint of great compassion and great mercy. / Without stain, without defilement, the heart of attachment / Is not even as much as the dew on a rabbit's fur. / He is the Chōgiyoshi of Tenninshiyōba. / The Daufū and Tokukau, / Perfuming all Baniu, leave no corner untouched. / Wisdom is serene, emotion is serene, / And the meditation grows ever more tranquil. / Evil thoughts are extinguished, consciousness perishes, / The heart is pure and clear, / And the thoughts of dreams and illusions / Are severed, like water.

○

The body is not existence, nor is it non-existence; / It is not cause, nor is it condition. / It is not self or other, nor is it direction; / It is not short or long, nor is it circular. / It is not emerging, nor is it submerging; / It is not birth and death, nor is it creation. / It is not action, nor is it arising; / It is not sitting, nor is it lying down. / It is not walking or staying, nor is it moving; / It is not leisure, nor is it turning. / It is not advancing, nor is it retreating; / It is not safety or danger, nor is it this. / It is not 'not', nor is there any confusion / In the realm of gain and loss. / It is not that, nor is it this; / It is not coming and going, nor is it blue. / It is not red or white, nor is it yellow; / It is not crimson, nor is it purple. / It is not any of the various colors; / The essence of the Suishō-Mitama / Having fully embodied, the Restorer / Is the manifestation of Miroku, / The true reality illuminating the world. / Looking up at the Great God, so high, / The absolute and infinite divine virtue / That we receive—how joyful is the age of the gods.

○

The divine power of the precepts, meditation, wisdom, and liberation / Is generated from the virtue of knowledge and vision; / Samadhi and the six supernatural powers arise from the path, / And compassion arises from fearlessness in the ten directions. / Sentient beings emerge from the cause of good deeds; / Showing this, the sixteen-foot golden body / Radiates infinite brilliance, / Shining and illuminating in perfect order, / Its light reaching far and clear. / Like the shape of the moon, the tuft of hair / Swirls, and there is sunlight at the nape of the neck. / The color of the swirling hair is deep blue, / And a fleshy protuberance emerges at the crown. / The eyes are pure, like a bright mirror, / Shining and blinking up and down. / The color of the eyebrows stretches in deep blue, / The mouth and cheeks are well-proportioned, and the lips and tongue / Are red and beautiful like cinnabar flowers. / The forty teeth are white, / And pure like snow and crystal.

The forehead is wide, the nose is long, / The face is open, and the chest / Is the lion's chest, marked with the swastika. / The hands and feet are pure and soft, / Endowed with the signs of a thousand spokes. / There is webbed skin between the armpits and palms, / Gripping inside and out, the arms are long, / And the elbows and fingers are slender and long. / The skin is fine and soft, / And all the hair swirls to the right. / The ankles and knees are clearly revealed, / Hidden like those of a horse. / The fine muscles and the bones of the joints / Are like the legs of a deer. / The surface and interior are transparent and very pure, / Without stain, without filth, / Not dyed by muddy water, and receiving no dust. / The thirty-two signs and eighty secondary marks / Are the spiritual appearance of the most solemn and most holy. / There is no color of sign or non-sign,

And for all things in Baniu that have signs, / There is no power of the eye that can oppose it. / Mirokunokami is the sign of no-sign, / Yet sits in a body with signs. / Just as the physical signs of sentient beings are, / All sentient beings rejoice and bow, / Casting their hearts and expressing / Their reverence to accomplish their tasks. / This is the result of having / Pride and arrogance exorcised; / Such a noble and wonderful-colored / Body has been achieved. / All sentient beings, without exception, / Respectfully submit to that divine virtue, / Taking refuge and offering faith, / Rejoicing, celebrating, and dancing wildly at the divine government / Of peace and tranquility, / Looking up at the age of the gods that will flourish / For a thousand, eight thousand, and ten thousand generations— / The great pillar of the motive force.

It is a matter of awe and reverence. / The Sangokyō is the way of God; / There is no distinction between it and the way of Buddha. / Because it is a way to save the people of the world, / Using only truth as a shield, / I shall here roughly describe / The truth of Miroku, the God, / Which is revealed in the teachings of God, / According to the Dharma preached by the Buddha. / Ah, Kannagara, Kami-Kami, / May your spirits be blessed, / And may the teachings of the Sangokyō, / Regardless of past or present, / Without distinguishing between East and West, / For the sake of the world, / Be polished and researched; / Realizing the true essence of Shinto, Confucianism, Buddhism, / And other religions, / For the sake of the world and for the sake of people, / Fulfill your sincerity. / This is for the leaders of the Sangokyō, / And goes without saying, / Even for the believers.

§ 2

Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. May the Mitama be blessed. I pray before the solemn presence of Kamususanōnōomikami.

Terukō: 'Sendenshi-sama, that song just now does not seem to be the Goshinsau of Mirokunōokami, but rather that of the goddess Konohanahime, does it not?'

Terukuni: 'The goddess Konohanahime also manifests the activities of Mirokunōokami, either in part or in whole. She may manifest as Amaterasuōmikami, appear as Tanabatahime, or reveal herself as Konohanasakuyahime, become Kuwanjizaiten, become Kuwanzeonbosatsu, or manifest as Katoriwake, Kagatora, Karl, Tanshiu, and so forth; she may even become Susanōnomikoto. It is the Goshinsau of Mirokunōokami to manifest not only as gods, but also as humans, beasts, Mushikera, and Kusaki to save all existence. In short, Mirokunōokami is a deity who may be called the root deity of the Yamato-damashii.'

'What kind of spirit do you call Yamato-damashii? Is it the spirit of a god, or the spirit of a Buddha?'

'It is neither the spirit of a god nor the spirit of a Buddha, but a spirit and soul that has synthesized and unified all truth, goodness, and beauty, which is even more magnificent. To explain this in detail would be endless, but what is called Yamato-damashii is what is referred to as the Bodhicitta in the way of the Buddha.'

'Where can one distinguish between a god and a Buddha?'

'What is called a god is a general term for the infinite power formed by the union of the essence, the original spirit, and the original force of the universe, which we call the True God. What is called a Buddha is a term for an enlightened one; in short, it is another name for a great saint, a great person of virtue, or a great truth-seeker.'

'Please tell me the outline of Yamato-damashii.'

'Yamato-damashii is what is called the Bodhicitta in the way of the Buddha. This Bodhicitta is formed by the gathering of three hearts. The first is called the heart of a god, the heart of a Buddha, or the heart of enlightenment, which refers to the emotion that works toward goodness. In short, it is what is called a heart of great compassion or a heart of sympathy. The second is called the heart of supreme meaning, which is reason. It goes without saying that reason has various levels, such as passive and active. Since the levels of reason cannot be exhausted in a day, I will omit them and speak of the third, which is called the Samadhi heart. The Samadhi heart is what is called the will. When good emotion, good will, and good reason are in complete agreement, it produces a great determination and a great courageous heart of immovable diamond; this is the Samadhi heart. The union of these three becomes the Bodhicitta and also becomes Yamato-damashii. No matter how superior one's reason may be, or how much knowledge one may have attained, knowledge alone cannot save all sentient beings. Only those with knowledge and academic ability can understand it, and it cannot save their spirits and souls in a general sense. Conversely, the heart of enlightenment, or what is called the heart of a god or the heart of a Buddha, is an emotion, so a heart of great compassion and a heart of sympathy arise and work well. This heart of compassion and sympathy can reach everyone from the wise and the learned down to birds and beasts. There is nothing as great as this. The Ural religion is a teaching that prioritizes intellect, and the Brahmin religion is a teaching that prioritizes reason. That is why they cannot save the general public. Because the San-iku-kyo is a religion of emotion, it adopts the principle of non-resistance in all things and advances while holding up the banner of universal brotherhood, philanthropy, and compassion; therefore, there is nothing, even down to a single blade of grass, that does not submit to its virtue. In a world like today, where military power and academic ability are flourishing, trying to open the way with only a heart of compassion may seem like something weak and unreliable, but that is by no means the case. The final victory is determined by good emotion, that is, the heart of great compassion and sympathy. That is why, when I went to the cave of Mount Kiyoharu, I forgave the wicked people of the Brahmin religion. From here on, I do not know how many times we will clash with the followers of the Ural and Brahmin religions, but you must never do anything violent. Since both sects hold scriptures in their left hands and swords in their right, combining martial power with their teachings, you will not achieve this goal safely unless you are very firm in your resolve.'

§ 3

Ihahiko: 'Hey Umehiko, Terukuniwake-sama, things have certainly changed since the days of our exploration of the ugly caves. You seem to have been close to the side of Kotoyoriwake-sama, as you have become able to speak in such a truly splendid manner. While I am at it, I would like to ask one thing. There is a question that is always going back and forth in my mind, Ihahiko. People call it the Brahman sect, and at times they call it the Brahman religion, or perhaps the Brahman treasury, or a vehicle, or a division; how should one explain this distinction?'

Terukuni: 'Whether one calls it a religion, a sect, a vehicle, a treasury, or a division, it all carries the same meaning of teaching. No matter how you say it, it is the same thing.'

Ihahiko: 'Oh, thank you. I understand now. However, the scriptures of Buddhism are called sutras; is the sutra in that context different from the teaching of the religion?'

Terukuni: 'That meaning is slightly different. The character for sutra is the character for warp. All teachings until now have been nothing but warp. Without the weft, one cannot weave a perfect brocade. That is why established religions are inevitably of no use to society. With only the warp, one cannot apply things freely and at will. In Ananaikyō, the spiritual lineage of the Lord God Kuninotokotachi-no-Mikoto becomes the warp, and the spiritual lineage of Toyokunihime-no-Mikoto becomes the weft; because the warp and weft are thus aligned, a perfect and flawless teaching has been opened. Therefore, no matter what, without this teaching, the affairs of society cannot be settled. In short, all teachings until now are unfinished products; one might even say they are unfinished products. For this reason, in Ananaikyō, the scriptures are not called sutras or the Quran, but are referred to as Divine Oracles.'

Ihahiko: 'Ah, with that, the clouds in my chest have cleared away completely, and I feel as if the sun and moon of true reality are shining upon me. As expected of one who has received the name Terukuniwake, you are truly remarkable.'

Just as they were speaking, the shadows of several dozen horsemen appeared from the other side, galloping toward them at full speed with the brave sound of hooves. Terukuniwake signaled to the three of them, and they hid their figures in the thicket of trees, holding their breath to observe who the cavalry might be. The general on horseback leading the vanguard was Katahiko, who was quite well-known in the Brahman religion. Their troop was in the midst of crossing the torrent of the Lion River and was attempting to advance rapidly toward the Iso mansion on Mount Ubusuna. To rest their weary bodies, they dismounted their horses in this forest where the four were hiding, and sat down for a while to indulge in idle chatter.
