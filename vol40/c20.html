Chapter 20: 入那の Forest [1104]

入那の森〔1104〕

§ 1

Ōgonhime parted ways with the party of Terukuniwake at the banks of Afuhinuma, heading west and further west. The day gradually turned to dusk, and the Momodori sought their roosts, returning to the forests here and there, the sound of their wings noisy. The party of four arrived at a fairly wide riverbank they had suddenly struck. The water appeared relatively shallow, and it seemed one would not feel much difficulty in wading across. The party tucked up their hems in the dim light, crossed the stream, and reached their destination, a dense forest shade about two or three chō to the west.

It was the twilight hour before the moon of the night had yet revealed its form. Inside the forest stood a weathered, fairly large shrine.

Ōgonhime: 'The autumn day is quick to fade, and it has come to the point where we must rest again before our feet grow too weary. Fortunately, let us take a short rest inside this shrine in the forest.'

Kiyoteru: 'Mother-sama, let us not rest in such a place this evening; the moon will rise soon, so let us wait for the moon here and proceed. If we walk until around midnight, we shall be able to make considerable progress on our journey...'

Ōgonhime: 'It is a long journey, so let us walk when the day breaks, and when the day fades, no matter how easy it may be, let us make it our rule to stop and stay.'

Kiyoteru: 'Even so, I cannot help but feel impatient. I feel as if some incident has occurred to Father-sama, who resides in the capital of Haruna, and I am beside myself with worry.'

Ōgonhime: 'No matter how much you fret, it is a long distance; let us leave everything to the Lord God and proceed at a steady pace. If we were to grow weary and collapse on the road, it would be troublesome in this world where evil spirits run rampant.'

Rebu: 'Two-sama, how would it be if you stayed the night here? We two shall take turns standing guard, so...'

Ōgonhime: 'In that case, I know not which god this shrine belongs to, but let us first recite the Amatsu Norito and borrow this shrine. Kiyoteru-hime... would that not be good?'

Kiyoteru: 'Regarding the matter of Father-sama, let us pray to the god here with all our hearts, and then let us sleep.'

Ōgonhime nodded lightly, recited the prayer as was the custom, entered the shrine, spread out her straw mat, and the mother and daughter laid their heads down to sleep. Rebu and Karu were lying down under the floor of the shrine, but before they knew it, they had fallen into a doze.

Two or three men came walking up with a steady gait, stood near the shrine while hiding their footsteps, and spoke.

Aruma: 'Hey, Teku, it seems to be inside the shrine around here.'

Teku: 'Hey, Aruma, what could possibly be in a place like this?'

Aruma: 'Even so, a strange sound is coming from there, is it not? It is surely not the snoring of a mouse. I heard from the Kshatriya-sama of Iruna that he said the party of Ōgonhime would certainly pass through here. Come now, why not keep quiet and observe the situation? If we catch her, we shall receive a splendid reward. It is my desire to receive even one small country and become a lord to boast of my power.'

Teku: 'However, that person called Ōgonhime is quite a hero, and she is beyond our reach. If we only know her whereabouts, we should just report it silently and have someone stronger capture her. That is a far wiser course of action. Hey, Temu, which will you choose?'

Temu: 'If I had to say, I am neutral. However, if it is all the same, I would like to capture her alive. Hey, hey, it seems the snoring of a real human has started to come from there.'

Rebu and Karu were listening to the conversation of the three men from under the floor, holding their breath. The three men, not dreaming that two people were hiding under the floor, climbed the stairs with heavy footsteps.

Aruma: 'Yeah, this veranda seems to be quite dilapidated due to the ravages of wind and rain over several hundred years. If you are not careful, the floor will fall through, and if you scrape your shin, you will end up crying like you did last time, whining about being carried and the like. Be careful, be careful.'

Rebu picked up some stones from under the floor and struck the old board from below with all his might, again and again. The three were startled by this sound, and the moment they jumped up, they fell headlong from the stairs in front of the shrine,

'Ouch, ouch, ugh, ugh.'

They began to groan.

Teku: 'Hey, can you not pull yourselves together? To be startled by such a sound, lose your composure, and tumble down from a place like this—do you think you can perform our duties with such cowardice?'

Aruma: 'Teku, did you not fall just the same? It is a bit too brazen of you to be so quick to blame others while feigning ignorance of your own failure, like Hanbee who ignores his own faults.'

Teku: 'Because you two tumbled, I was dragged down along with you. If anything, I am the victim. Since you two are the culprits with sufficient evidence, I shall report this to Setsuteirisama and have you dealt with accordingly, so keep that in mind.'

Aruma: 'Hahaha, you upstart Sendara, do not talk so haughtily. Even though I look like this, I am of the genuine Shudra lineage. Our races are different, you know.'

Teku: 'Hey, do not say such rude things as calling me a Sendara; I am, however unworthy, of the Brahmin caste.'

Aruma: 'A Brahmin? That makes me laugh. Say, Temu, did this fellow not meet a Sendara in the middle of the road today and whisper something to him looking quite familiar? Anyone who approaches that fellow and speaks to him must be of the same lineage, for otherwise, no one would go near him because he is so filthy.'

Teku: 'Hey you two, what kind of rudeness is this toward your superior? As officials of the police, whether they are Sendara or Shudra, we must examine the faces of everyone we meet, and we cannot know the degree of a person without making them speak, so I spoke to them out of necessity to prioritize my duties. Can this duty be performed with such cold indifference? There is a one-in-a-million chance that Mukade-hime might realize how strict this search is and pass by disguised as a Sendara, whom people despise. That is why I was performing my duties faithfully. You fools. I am dismissing you as of this moment for such lack of diligence, so keep that in mind.'

Temu: 'Hey boss, you talk big with your mouth, but can you even stand on your own waist?'

Teku: 'As you well know, I have come all this way precisely because I can stand on my own waist. Do not spout nonsense. Now, I am dismissing you as of this moment; go wherever you like, you are free to roam the world. You had better disappear.'

Aruma: 'No matter where you tell us to go, both of us have had our waists knocked out by the shock, so please hold off on the dismissal for a while. If we are to be dismissed, it must be in the form of a voluntary resignation, otherwise, it will be troublesome for our future employment. I will not stand for being dismissed from this splendid—well, not so splendid—job just because I called you a Sendara. Right, Temu-kō?'

Teku: 'A warrior has no second word. You know the temperament of Teku-sama, who never retreats once he has spoken.'

Aruma: 'Hmph, Teku-teku, what a bad Teku you are, you thief-turned-official. Now that it has come to this, I am ready for anything. Hey, Temu-kō, since you are Temu-kō, if that fellow Teku comes at you, you shall be the one to resist and fight him. If by any chance the situation looks dangerous, I will provide assistance, but it will be no good until a little more time passes. My waist that was knocked out has not yet settled back into its original sheath. Still, Teku must have hit his waist hard too; look at the color of his voice, it sounds quite painful. It is because a Sendara climbed up to such a sacred place with his shoes on that divine punishment has struck, and even we have been caught in the crossfire.'

Just as they were speaking, an even louder and more mysterious sound was heard from under the floor. The previous sound had been caused by Rebu alone tapping the floorboards with a stone, but this time, since both of them had struck the space under the floor with all their might using stones, it sounded four or five times louder. The three of them let out a shriek and tried to flee, struggling only with their hands, but they could not stand up at all. While this was happening, the moon shone mercilessly upon the lower world, and strong light poured through the gaps in the forest, illuminating the bodies of the three.

Rebu and Karu crawled out from under the floor with a grin, strode up the stairs, and began to sing in a loud voice that echoed all around.
