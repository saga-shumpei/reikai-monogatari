Chapter 17: 天幽 Cavern [1101]

天幽窟〔1101〕

§ 1

The four men, Itsukau and the others, finally descended the Thames Pass and arrived at the banks of the Lion River.

Harukō: 'My feet are quite tired. If we cross the middle of the river as it is, my feet will only grow more weary. You all have horses, so it may not be a problem for you, but surely even horses must be tired. As I sang earlier, it will be a bit of a detour, but if we go about fourteen or fifteen chō downstream, there is a shallow place where the river is wide and the current is gentle. It is a secret spot that even the members of the Brahman sect do not know much about. How about it? It would be very safe to cross from there.'

Terukuni: 'That is true. There is no need to risk danger and cross the rapids when there is a safe crossing place. In that case, let us make a slight detour and cross downstream.'

Just as they were talking, a horse that had been abandoned by a Brahman priest came along, moving slowly while plucking grass by the roadside with its eyes closed.

Harukō: 'Oh, how kind the gods are. The three of you have horses, and I have been following along on my own inherited legs, but as luck would have it, a horse has been left behind. Now we can all four be mounted. Lord Sendenshi, may I ride it?'

Terukuni: 'Since it is a stray horse, it should not be considered stealing. If the owner is found, you can return it then, so you should ride it without hesitation.'

At these words, Harukō was emboldened; he approached the side of the horse, stroked its neck, and said,

Harukō: 'Hey, horse, I am sorry to trouble you, but I am counting on you. From today, I am your temporary master.'

He leaped onto the horse as he spoke. It was a relatively docile horse and a rare treasure. This was the famous horse that Kugihiko of the Brahman sect had been riding. It was a beast that would cross any torrent or great ocean without the slightest hesitation. Here, the four men lined up their bridles and, with the sound of their hooves, arrived at the shallow place downstream. The depth of the water was a shallow ford of about four or five sun to one shaku. In return, the width of the river was about three times as wide as in other places. The four men crossed leisurely, heads of the four horses aligned, while indulging in conversation about all sorts of things.

Harukō: 'Lord Sendenshi, I heard that a man named Yatsukosu, who served in the rock cavern of Mount Kiyoharu of the Brahman sect, is my older brother Iwahiko. Since we are crossing this river, I have an interesting story regarding this Yatsukosu, so shall I tell it?'

Terukuni: 'By all means, please tell me. It is a rare tale... knowing that man, I am sure there is one.'

Harukō: 'Hearing that Yatsukosu is my brother has made me feel particularly attached to this river. This river was named the Lion River because upstream there is a great secret realm called the Tenyū Cavern, overgrown with trees and inaccessible to humans, where countless hundreds and thousands of lions reside. Therefore, that Tenyū Cavern is also called the Lion Cavern, and consequently, this river was named the Lion River. Around the spring of last year, when a man named Yatsukosu was crossing this river, two cubs of the Chinese lion that lived upstream came to the river to play, accidentally fell into the rapids, and came floating along, bobbing in the water. Yatsukosu happened to pass by, grabbed the two lion cubs that were about to drown, rescued them to the riverbank, nursed them back to health in various ways, made them spit out the water, and carried them on his back, stepping boldly into the Tenyū Cavern to deliver them to the lions' den. Since then, it is said that the lions have protected Yatsukosu, and whenever danger approaches him, many lions appear from nowhere to save him from crisis—that is the common reputation... no, it is said to be a fact. Hearing this, Ōashiwake of Mount Kiyoharu thought that if he kept such a man, he would be safe in an emergency, so he made him his retainer. If that Yatsukosu is indeed my brother Iwahiko, I would be truly happy. I heard yesterday that Manjushrī Bodhisattva crossed the checkpoint of Thames Pass riding a lion, and my brother is just like a Manjushrī Bodhisattva.'

'What a strange story I have heard. To hear a story about a lion at the Lion River, this too must be some providence of the Shinkai.'

While speaking thus, they finally crossed the wide river without difficulty, took the road north for about fourteen or fifteen chō, approached the foot of Tamayamatauge, and began to climb toward the summit while being blown by the Akikaze, saying 'Hi, hi, hi.'

The party of four dismounted from their horses at the summit of Tamayamatauge, and while each led their horse by the bridle, they descended about seven or eight tenths of the way, urging the horses with 'Haa, haa, hi, hi.' Suddenly, a fairly large wolf appeared, and the strangeness of its behavior was such that it caught the hem of Harukō, who was in the lead, and tried to pull him away by force.

Harukō: 'You beast, biting the hem of a Ningensama, are you not a wolf? Listen here, you beast, even if you think you can eat me, you are no match for me. This person here is the messenger of the golden line of the Ananai-kyō, which opens the path of the Daijizaiten... or rather, the Kuniharutachinōokami who created this world, and I am Harukōwake, who serves the honorable Terukuniwake-sama. Do not make a mistake about who we are. If you want to eat a human, there is a gatekeeper at the summit of Thames Pass who has collapsed, half-dead and half-alive, drunk on sake. You had better go and gobble him up to fill your belly. Shoo, get away!'

Although he waved his right hand to drive it away, the wolf did not seem particularly angry, and while wagging its tail left and right like a dog, it kept biting and pulling his hem and would not let go.

Harukō: 'Hey, hey, I am on an urgent journey. If you obstruct the traffic, I will report you to the police box for the crime of obstructing traffic. What is this, wagging your tail like a pet dog? Haha, this fellow is a mountain dog. You were likely kept in a human home, but due to your master's downfall, you too wandered, finally fled into the mountains, became a mountain dog, and evolved into a wolf, didn't you? They say that a person's physiognomy changes according to their circumstances, but it seems that even a beast is no exception to that principle. Hey, wolf-dog beast, will you not let go? If I were being pulled by a seventeen or eighteen-year-old nice girl, I would feel a bit better, but being pulled by the likes of you does not give me a very good feeling. Eh, you beast, you are a stubborn one. If you are a wolf-dog, you are at least the king among beasts, so you should be able to understand the Kototama of a Ningensama. If you dawdle, I will have my horse trample you to death.'

The wolf wagged its tail incessantly and tried to pull him by the hem into the thicket of trees by the side of the road.

Harukō: 'If I may, Terukuniwake-sama, this beast is a fancy fellow; he is mocking us, a mere four-legged creature acting above his station. Would it not be better to kick him to death?'

Terukuniwake: 'A wolf is a righteous beast, so you must not do such a violent thing. It must be trying to inform us of some emergency. Why don't we follow where the wolf is pulling us? Perhaps it intends to help us all by leading us to a hideout, thinking that a thousand wolves are coming.'

'Even if you say so, being dragged along by a wolf is not a very pleasant feeling.'

'Hey wolf, we will follow where you go, so please let go of Harukō's hem.'

At this voice, the wolf suddenly released the hem it had been biting, came up to the front of Terukuniwake, bowed its head slightly to greet him, and then went down toward the mountain stream with a rustling sound. The party of Terukuniwake followed the wolf and descended to the mountain stream where water was trickling. Looking there, about ten humans were lying unconscious with their faces scraped and their shoulders dislocated.

'Oh, so many injured people. This is likely the result of a clash between the group of the Brahmanism followers and the party of Yoshikohime-sama. Now, Teru, Ume, and Haru, the three of you, scoop up the valley water in your hands, let them drink it, and spray it on their faces. I will recite the Amatsu Norito here and perform the soul-calling.'

'Yes.'

Answering thus, the three of them exerted their strength to care for each in their own way. At last, every one of them was revived without exception. There was a man whose shoulder had been dislocated; they set his bone back into place while he was unaware, and then performed Chinkon. The one who had finally breathed back to life was Rebu, who had brought Ōgonhime and Kiyoteruhime this far. The man with the terrible scratch on his face was Karu, who had stood at the head of the Brahman army. These two, along with the rest of the group, thanked them for saving their lives, and while expressing gratitude for the Jitoku of the Ōkami of Ananai-kyō, they followed behind the Sendenshi, descending Tamayamatauge, and proceeded to guard the Daigenya where the Magami were raging, keeping watch both in front and behind.

Terukuniwake and the other four rode on horses, while the eight, including Rebu and Karu, proceeded through the wilderness while guarding the front and rear. Ahead, they came to a shallow, wide swamp that blocked their path. The day was finally drawing to a close. Terukuniwake commanded the group to spend the night there. Under the guidance of Terukuniwake, they recited the Amatsu-norito and chanted the Kamigoto, and each of them, exhausted, fell into a deep sleep. The moon of the desired night began to rise from the peak of Tamayamatauge, shining brightly. The figures of the group became as clear as if they could be touched by hand. Two men named Sugaru and Chiru, while feigning deep sleep, were considering the breathing of the group. At the hour of midnight, when the moon shone directly overhead, the two men, Sugaru and Chiru, rose quietly, took out ropes from their bosoms, and one by one, tied up the six people: Terukuniwake, Terukō, Umekō, Harukō, Rebu, and Karu, in a chain. Then, they quietly shook the other six awake. A man named Kiru, his deep sleep broken, sprang up while saying, 'Un, un, un,'

Kiru: 'Da, da, da, who is it? People are sleeping soundly, so stop pinching noses and tickling, and just let me sleep, will you? You are a troublesome fellow.'

Chiru brought his mouth close to the edge of his ear and said in a small voice,

Chiru: 'Hey, Kiru-kō, do not speak in a loud voice. I have just tied up the Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō and the turncoats, so will you wake up and smash their heads in, or shall we drag them to the side of the horse of Oniharawake-sama and earn some merit?'

Kiru said in a voice like a cracked gong,

Kiru: 'Are you not Chiru? Where is there any sense in tying up the Sendenshi who went to the trouble of saving our lives? You will be punished for doing such a thing.'

Chiru: 'Eh, what a troublesome fellow you are. There is a limit to being dense. How could I ever let such people go?'

Kiru: 'No matter what you say, I am indebted to that Sendenshi. It is not what a human should do to return evil for good. Try to calm down and think with a sincere heart.'

Sugaru, while saying 'Oi, oi' to the next man, tried to wake him by pinching his nose and tickling his armpits.

Seru: 'Da, da, da, da, who is it? Who is pinching the nose of Seru-sama and tickling my armpits? Hey, when it is time to sleep, sleep quickly, and when it is time to work, work with all your might. I will not forgive you for disturbing my sleep, so keep that in mind.'

Sugaru brought his mouth close to his ear and said in a small voice,

Sugaru: 'Hey Seru, do not speak in a loud voice. It will be a disaster if the Sendenshi wakes up. I have tied up everyone, this one and that one, so let us all gather, wake up, and beat them down, or else I intend to drag them before the Shōgun-sama. If we do that, it will be to your credit.'

Seru, rubbing his sleepy eyes, said,

Seru: 'What, are you saying you will drag me before the Shōgun-sama? That is outrageous. When did I ever do such a bad thing? Victory or defeat is a matter of fortune. To say that it is a crime to have been defeated and fallen into a life-or-death situation—does that not mean one cannot even go to war? That is a bit unreasonable. (In a loud voice) Hey, everyone, wake up! Sugaru is saying he will drag us one by one and take us before the Shōgun-sama!'

スガルは又次の男を小声で、

『オイオイ』

と云ひながら、鼻をつまんだり腋の下をこそばかして目を醒まさうとしてゐる。

セル『だゝゝゝ誰ぢやい、セルさまの鼻をつまみやがつたり腋の下をくすぐる奴は。こら寝る時はトツトと寝て働く時には精出して働くのだぞ。安眠の妨害をさらすと俺や了簡せぬから、さう思へ』

スガルは小声で耳に口を寄せ、

『おいセル、大きな声で云ふな。宣伝使が目を覚ましちや大変だ。何奴も此奴も皆俺が引括つておいたのだから、之から皆寄つて目を覚まして彼奴を叩き伏せるか、但は将軍様の前へ引張つて行くつもりだ。さうすれば貴様達の手柄になるのだから』

セルは寝ぶた目を擦りながら、

『何、俺を何か、将軍様の前へ引張つて行くと云ふのか。そりや怪しからぬ。俺ヤ何時そんな悪い事をしたかい。勝負は時の運だ。俺が負けて九死一生の場合に陥つたと云つて、それが罪になると云つては戦に行く事も出来ぬぢやないか。そりや一寸無理だよ。（大きい声で）おい皆の奴、起きてくれ、スガルの奴、俺達を片つ端から引張つて将軍様の前へつれて行くと吐しよるわいのう』

§ 4

Terukuni: 'Atsuhahahaha!'

Rebu: 'Utsufuhahaha!'

Terukuni: 'To think that the Heihafu of Rikutausanriyaku would be broken from within our own Mikata, how Omoshiro this is. Guu guu guu.'

And he snored again.

Rebu: 'So, you two, Sugaru and Chiru, you took out your Torinaha from your bosoms while I was pretending to be asleep, and tried to bind us six, did you? Hen, don't make a Baka of me. I, Rebu-sama, using my Shinpen Fushigi Kamuwaza, pretended to be bound while firmly gripping your Torinaha, and before you knew it, I had tied all eight of you up. The moment I pull this rope, the necks of you eight will be tightened all at once, and your breath will be cut off.'

While saying this, he pulled and tugged at it. Fushigi, the necks of the eight men became tied in a Totsukurimusu, and suddenly their breath was choked off; they began to groan, rolling their eyes and thrashing their Teashi in agony.

Rebu: 'Ahahahaha, Omoshiro, Omoshiro! Moshi-moshi, Sendenshi-sama, Ume-sama, Teru-sama, Karu-sama, wake up, wake up! Now, it is time for a Sarumaha Geitau.'

Terukuni: 'Ahahahaha, you did that well.'

Rebu: 'Just as per your Gonaimei, I have bound every one of these fellows Nainai with my specialty Torinaha. Shall I pull the rope?'

Terukuni: 'It would be better to untie them one by one.'

Rebu: 'If I untie them so quickly, wouldn't the interest be lost entirely? Teru-sama, Ume-sama, Haru-sama, Karu-sama, I shall show you something Omoshiro, and even then, it will hardly be too late, will it?'

Terukuni: 'If you dawdle, won't their breath expire?'

Rebu: 'What does that matter? These fellows are the ones who just traveled the journey to the Meido with us. It would be fine to make them cross the river of death once more.'

The eight men began to groan.

Terukuni: 'Hey, Teru, Ume, Haru, Karu, you four, loosen the rope quickly.'

At this command, the four hurriedly loosened the Totsukurimusu bit by bit. This was because if they untied them suddenly, their breath would expire again. The eight men rose up with a start, and crawling like frogs, they trembled.

Rebu: 'Hey, you two, Sugaru and Chiru, you are dog-beasts who tried to repay your life's benefactor with enmity. Now, leaving the other six aside, I shall give you two a jolt of life. Your souls are dead. Nay, they are rotten. Unless I apply a branding iron to you, you will never return to your original right mind.'

Sugaru and Chiru said, 'Hai, hai,' and while doing so, they inched backward step by step, and watching for an opening, they dove into the swamp with all their might, splashing and fleeing.

Seru, Kiru, and the other four fellows followed the two, and with all their might, they fled into the swamp, splashing and shattering the full moon reflected in the water.

Rebu: 'Ahahahaha, in the end, they crawled away like frogs and gave up the ghost. They are exactly like frogs. The frog procession that looks not ahead—that is this very thing. In the end, they were saved by the water.'

The heartless moon shines brightly, looking down upon this lively drama in secret.
