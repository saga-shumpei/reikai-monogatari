Chapter 13: 試の果実 [1097]

試の果実〔1097〕

§ 1

Fragrance wafts and flowers bloom, butterflies dance and small birds sing. The earth is covered in a carpet of flowers, spread out without a single empty space. It is a sight of the Gokurakujiyōdo, and it is a comfort to gaze upon it as one passes through. In the azure sky where clouds drift, the sun and moon stand side by side. Their brilliance shines in seven colors, and the climate is neither too hot nor too cold. In the center, where perfect harmony is attained, Karu and Rēbu walk on, leaving it to their feet. Though it is called a journey through the Jiyōdo, their feet have grown somewhat weary, and their stomachs cry out in emptiness. The courage of their bodies has faded before they knew it, and they sit down with a thud by the roadside to rest their breath.

Even on the journey through the Tengoku Jiyōdo, it is no different from the world of Shiyaba; is there not the suffering of hunger and thirst? The teaching that there is suffering within pleasure and pleasure within suffering, as explained in the divine Mifumi, is now realized before their very eyes. Is it that heaven and earth are things where suffering and pleasure pass back and forth to each other? Such states of supreme joy and supreme pleasure cannot be attained even by Kami. Is this the truth of heaven and earth? Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara, may your spirits be blessed, and as you distribute the paths of suffering and pleasure in moderation, please protect us in peace, they pray deeply in their hearts, occupying a seat by the roadside, looking up at the vast sky and prostrating themselves upon the earth.

They were lost in tears of repentance.

To this place, a beautiful woman of prime youth, elegantly dressed in flowing thin silk of five colors, suddenly appeared. Carrying two beautiful, nameless fruits in both hands, she faced the two and spoke in a quiet voice,

Megami: 'You must be the two, Rēbu and Karu. Since you have walked a distance of ten thousand ri since crossing the Sanzu River, you must be very hungry. I have appeared here, having received the command of Tsukiteruhiko-kami-sama from the Tosotsuten, and I am called Ikutamahime-mikoto. I have brought these fruits all the way here, wishing to offer them for your sustenance. Please, do eat them.'

Rēbu: 'Ha!'

Rēbu: 'The mercy of the Lord God is vast and boundless. Beautiful flowers bloom and scent the sides of the road, but there is not a single fruit. Driven by hunger, we two were suffering and writhing, and as we could not take another step, we were resting here. They say the way of heaven does not kill men; I am truly grateful.'

Karu: 'I have no words to express my gratitude for your kindness; I shall humbly accept your offer.'

Rēbu: 'Ha, thank you very much. In that case, I will take the fruit of Tamatome-tama. Please, by all means, give the fruit of Ashi-tama to Karu.'

Karu: 'Excuse me, Megami-sama, I will take the fruit of Tamatome-tama, so please, by all means, bestow the fruit of Ashi-tama upon Rēbu.'

Megami: 'Ohohoho, both of you are quite alike in your preference for this bitter, unpleasant, and hard fruit.'

Rēbu: 'Yes, it is not that I dislike it, but even if something is sweet, it is only sweet while passing through the throat, and even if it is tasteless, it is the same. As we observe the teaching to bestow upon others what one desires for oneself, and not to bestow upon others what one does not desire, how could I possibly pass the tasteless one to Karu?'

Karu: 'I, too, am actually of the same opinion as Rebu.'

Megami: 'Ohohoho, what a great hypocrite you are. You have become such a good-hearted person through the teachings of Kami, have you? That is still not enough. It will not do unless it is a sincerity that arises from the heart of Tennen Shizen Kamunagara. As long as you have a heart that thinks you have done a great good deed by patiently eating tasteless things yourself while giving delicious things to others, it is still not a true Zenshin. You likely have an ambition to go to Tengoku Jiyōdo by performing such Kiyogiteki Zenji and receiving the reward for it. Why do you not manifest your Honnō and request your own preferences in a Sekirara manner? You are still actively exercising a heart that adorns the Uwabe.'

Rebu: 'Ah, I am humbled. You have seen through me to the very bottom of my stomach with your Kuōsen. Indeed, a spirit of falsehood still lurks somewhere within me. Thank you for your kind warning.'

Karu: 'I, too, was of the same mind as Rebu.'

Megami: 'In that case, which of these fruits will you take here and now?'

Rebu, while scratching his head, said:

Rebu: 'Yes, I truly cannot decide. Since it is impossible to divide them as you have instructed, I would rather not take either of them.'

Megami: 'Do you remember the saying that if one does not take what Heaven gives, disaster will befall one's own body?'

Rebu: 'Yes, I am certainly aware of that as well.'

Megami: 'Then why do you not accept this fruit?'

Rebu: 'Ah, I am lost in the path of good and evil, right and wrong, and I cannot grasp what I should do.'

Megami: 'Now, Karu-sama, what do you think?'

Karu: 'Yes, I will honestly accept the fruit of Ashitama, which tastes good. I feel sorry for Rebu-sama, but as an individual soul, we each have our own responsibility. At this moment, when we are pressed by hunger and thirst, I shall accept the Ashitama that my own true heart desires.'

Megami: 'Ohohoho, then is it acceptable if I give the fruit of Tamayurime-tama, which Karu-sama does not desire, to Rebu-sama? Will you be satisfied with that?'

Karu: 'It has become even more difficult. Now that it has come to this, I have nothing more to say. Human judgment is useless. From here on, I leave it to Kami. I shall accept whatever you give me. I will never request this or that, saying I like this or dislike that.'

Megami: 'Ah, with that, you too have finally qualified for a journey through the Kamikai. Nothing can be solved by human morality or ethics. Leaving it to Kami is the first priority. Now, Karu-sama, on behalf of Kami, I shall present the fruit of Ashitama to you.'

Karu: 'I gratefully accept this blessing of Heaven.'

And receiving it from the hands of Megami, he began to eat it with a crunch, as if leaping for joy,

Karu: 'Ah, delicious, it tastes good, what a magnificent fruit this is.'

He praised it incessantly and finished it in an instant.

Megami suddenly threw the fruit of Tamayurime-tama onto the ground, whereupon it emitted a five-colored light and turned into many beautiful Megami, who returned to the heavens. The two men watched this scene, and without thinking, they clasped their hands together and bowed down. Megami took another fruit of Ashitama from her bosom and said,

Megami: 'Now, Rebu-sama, so that there is no unfairness, I shall present this fruit of Ikutamahime to you on behalf of Kami; eat it at once.'

As she held it out, he pressed it to his forehead with both hands clasped together,

Rebu: 'Yes, thank you.'

And while shedding tears of joy, he also ate it as if leaping for it, and finished it in an instant.

The many Megami who had appeared from the fruit of Tamayurime-tama, which the Kami Ikutamahime had thrown onto the earth, ascended once to the heavens, then descended again to this place in full attire, surrounded Ikutamahime, and while she was seated in a carriage, they soared into the mid-air with the sound of resonant music, and as the heavenly robes fluttered in the soft breeze, they opened a path like a rainbow and ascended to the heavens. Watching them go, the two men looked at each other's faces, and their hearts were troubled once again by this resolution.

They began to walk along again while talking. From the main road about twenty or thirty ken below on the right side, the voices of agony and lamentation were heard. The two looked down at this unexpectedly, and there were carriages, automobiles, rickshaws, and various other people coming and going. This was the state of human life in the Genkai. If one looked closely, inside the automobiles, ogres with horns and women like yasha with mouths split to their ears were smoking cigarettes, running along the main road with a triumphant air as if they owned the place. Pitiful, honest humans were being run over by automobiles and carriages, or having their flesh scraped away, their blood squeezed out, or becoming gaki, carrying heavy burdens, and going back and forth barely holding onto their lives.

The miserable state was more than one could bear to look at. As they were doing so, the voices of agony and lamentation were heard again from the main road twenty or thirty ken to the right. The two again drew their bodies toward the direction of this voice and ran over, and when they looked down at their feet, they saw General Ranchi of the Brahman religion encountering Koganehime and Kiyoteruhime, surrounding them ten and twenty layers deep, shooting arrows, brandishing spears, and wielding blood-stained swords, breathing heavily as they sought to take the lives of the two. As the mother and daughter prayed with all their might, chanting the Kototama, countless wolves appeared, heading toward the army led by General Ranchi, raging and biting them down in every direction, and the scene of them fighting and going mad while shedding blood became clearly visible. This was the middle of the hell realm of the Yūkai, and a terrifying tragedy was being repeated.

Karu: 'Yes, I have no choice but to leave all things to Kamunagara. No matter how much a ningen may fret, there is nothing that can be done...'

Megami: 'Like the believers of the Ananai-kyō in the Nijitsuseiki, you too are quite afflicted with Kamunagarachiudoku. Without exhausting the shiyudan that should be exhausted, avoiding difficulties, protecting your own anzen, and watching the kunan of the sekai jinrui from the sidelines... to say that it is beyond the reach of jinriyoku and that there is no shikata but to leave all things to Kamunagara... is this truly musekinin, or munō, or hikefu, or should I call you jinchiku? Your soul is utterly appalling; how could you ever achieve shiyujiyōsaido with such an attitude? Although you riyōnin have approached the gateway to Takamagahara through the megumi of Kami, with such a selfish heart, the situation will change in an instant, and you will fall this very moment to the bottom of the Hachiman Jigoku. Today it is another's affair, tomorrow it is your own; the karma revolves like a small cart, and where will you place the heavy burden of your sins? How could you possibly continue your leisurely journey through heaven forever? You are truly a pitiable person. Try consulting your own conscience for a little while. With such a state of mind, it is amazing that you have been able to walk the world claiming to be of the Brahmanism or the Ananai-kyō. I shall not meet with a heartless person like you ever again. If that is the case, mind your step and fare you well.'

No sooner had she spoken than she turned into white smoke, and the whereabouts of the Megami became invisible.
