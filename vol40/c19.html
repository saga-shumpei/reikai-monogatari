Chapter 19: Moon 会 [1103]

月会〔1103〕

§ 1

On the southern shore of the Aoi-no-numa, Ōgonhime and Kiyoteruhime were gazing at the surface of the marsh, watching the retreating figures of the eight evildoers until they were hidden from sight.

Ōgonhime: 'The Shikogami, fleeing while shattering the moonlight that glitters across the surface of the marsh.'

Kiyoteruhime: 'Even the moonlight, so clear and serene, is trampled by the Magahi once it falls upon the water.'

'Will the moonlight on the clear waters of the Aoi-no-numa ever return to its original form?'

'The shattered form of the moon shall shine across the waters once more, once the waves have calmed.'

'The Shikobito have stirred up this marsh, where both the sky and the depths are clear.'

'It must be a sign that the life of Ōkuronushi of the Moon Capital of the Brahman is perishing.'

'The moonlight on the night of the full moon is different from the moon of the Brahman religion.'

'Seeing this moon shine, I recall the days of Kiyoteruhime-sama. The fact that the Shikotsukasa of the Brahman trample this moonlight is truly a sign of failing fortune. Though one calls it a failure of fortune, it is not the moon-shadow of the path of the Ananahi Oshihe. It is the moon of Ōkuronushi, who cast the light of a firefly upon the Moon Capital of the Brahman. The moon of Ōkuro has become invisible, hidden by the clouds in the Ōmisora. Though the moon and the sun shine in the Amatsumisora, Ōkuronushi has been hidden by the clouds.'

'Once again, I see a human shadow coming this way, shattering the moon on the marsh.'

'It must be that the Magahi of the Shikogami are coming again, for the clouds in the Amatsumisora are deep.'

'The Magahito, who cross the marsh like black clouds hiding the moonlight of the full moon, are detestable. The human shadow approaching moment by moment must be the eight Shikobito from before. Terukuniwake-mikoto must be residing where they crossed to the opposite shore.'

'The eight Shikobito, the remnants of the Shikogami, appearing and attacking at the mountain pass of Tamayama.'

While they were thus leisurely singing these thirty-one-syllable poems, the six individuals including Kiru and Eru came fleeing again, flapping about as if chased by someone, their appearance quite comical. The black clouds that had been blocking the sky until now cleared away, and the bright moonlight began to illuminate the surface of the marsh once more. Seeing the mother and daughter still standing there, the party of Kiru and Eru were startled; unable to climb onto the shore, they turned left, heading toward the east, and dashed through the water at full speed. Following them, there was a shadow of someone cutting through the water with vigor, mounted on a horse. When illuminated by the moonlight, it appeared to be the party of Terukuniwake. Four were on horseback and two were on foot, gradually approaching the shore. Ōgonhime called out and began to sing.

Ōgonhime: 'The figure of Terukuniwake, crossing the Aoi-no-numa where heaven and earth meet, is heroic.'

Kiyoteruhime: 'The moonlight of the full moon is bright, for the Tsukasa, Terukuniwake, has arrived.'

Terukuniwake was surprised by this voice, and wondering if Ōgonhime and her daughter were here, he raised his voice from horseback and sang in reply.

Terukuniwake: 'Are you here, Kiyoteruhime-sama, whose moon-shadow resembles the light of gold? The moon of the full moon is the god Terukuniwake, who has come across as the god wills.'

While they were singing thus, the horses reached the shore. The party of Terukuniwake leaped down from their horses and, while bowing to Ōgonhime,

Terukuniwake: 'You are Ōgonhime-sama and Kiyoteruhime-sama; we have met in a mysterious place. Many difficulties must have occurred along the way, I presume.'

Ōgonhime: 'Terukuniwake-sama, you have arrived very early. You were mounted on horseback, while we, mother and daughter, are but weak women who traveled over mountains and slopes; though we started out ahead, we were eventually overtaken. This is truly a case of the crow behind becoming the one in front. Ho ho ho ho.'

Terukuniwake: 'It must have been a great hardship for you, who stood at the vanguard in the wilderness where the Majin rage. In truth, thanks to you, we have arrived here without much obstruction. It is good to see that Kiyoteruhime-sama is also well.'

Kiyoteruhime: 'Yes, thank you. There were many times when we could hardly bear the frustration of inaction, but following the teachings of the gods, we adopted a policy of non-resistance and have come this far. There were truly many regrettable things that happened.'

§ 2

Terukuni: 'Indeed, had I been of the Brahman faith, I would have rampaged quite a bit, but it is a truly regrettable matter. However, this is rather the Gokeirin of the Kamisama; showing my military power to fifty or a hundred Kowatsupa Mushiya would be of no avail, you see.'

Kiyoteru: 'Terukuniwake-sama, as the two of us, mother and daughter, were attempting to spend the night here while bathing in the light of the full moon, the Brahman fellows came fleeing in a rush across this swamp, and since they offered a bit of resistance, I thought that as long as I did not take their Inochi, it would not go against the Goshinriyo of the Kamisama; so, to wake myself from my sleepiness, I took the eight of them and threw them into this swamp. They were weaker fellows than I had thought, and with a splash-splash-splash, they headed due north and retreated back along the path they had come. The way they were in a state of Urotahe at that time was quite a Mimono. After a while, those fellows again came fleeing and scrambling as if chased by a whale, and the moment they saw the two of us, they immediately turned east through the swamp and have just now scattered in flight. They are completely like water birds. Ho ho ho ho!'

Terukuni: 'There are no fellows as troublesome as those. When I was passing through Tamayama Pass, a wolf appeared and tugged at the sleeve of Haruko-san, refusing to let go, so I listened well to what the wolf had to say and followed after it, only to find that at the bottom of the valley, this Rebu, Karu, and eight other Brahman Kowatsupa Mushiya had fallen into a state of unconsciousness. I nursed them in various ways and saved their lives, but when we reached the north bank of this swamp, the sun had completely set, so we all spread our straw mats there and went to sleep. Then, in the middle of the night, the eight fellows tried to calculate our breathing and bind us, so I took their ropes with a feigned ignorance, and while they were unaware, I had them loop the ropes around their own necks like a string of ground cherries, and when I gave a little tug, they suddenly began to groan in agony and rampage about, thrashing and tumbling. Thinking it was too pitiful, I untied the ropes for them, and as they apologized while crawling like frogs, they jumped into this swamp in a group of eight, and just when I thought they had fled headlong toward the south, they came back to the same place in terror again. They started running toward the south once more the moment they saw my figure. It appears those fellows are evolved water birds. Aha ha ha ha!'

Kogane: 'Ho ho ho ho!'

Kiyoteru: 'Rebu, were you also unconscious at the bottom of the valley? I had been wondering where you had fled to. Well, it is fortunate that you were saved by the grace of the Kamisama.'

Rebu: 'Yes, thank you very much. I had finally lost consciousness and was crossing the Sanzu River, making my way toward the heavenly realm, when there was someone calling "Rebu, Rebu" from above the lush green mountain over there. Just as I was about to run headlong toward the direction of that voice, together with Karu, the subordinate of the Brahman who had been cast aside for your sake, I came to my senses and found myself lying with my shoulder bone dislocated at the foot of Tamayama Pass, where I was saved by the party of Terukuniwake-sama. Karu was saved at the same time, and those eight fellows who just fled, despite having their lives saved, have forgotten that great debt and committed such outrageous acts. Truly, there is nothing as wicked as the human heart.'

Kogane: 'Terukuniwake-sama, you did well to save Rebu. Since this man is a loyal man who serves Onikumawake-sama, I do not know if it differs from the Omoishiro of the Kamisama, but I am thinking of taking him along on our travels as a servant; what do you think?'

Terukuni: 'That is truly convenient. It must be very inconvenient for you two women to be alone. When I asked Hinodewake-sama about that matter, wondering if you might order someone to accompany you, Hinodewake-sama shook his head from side to side and said that you should never worry, for the two of you would surely find two good companions on your journey. Now that I think about it, I am truly struck with wonder at the Tengantsu of the Hinodewake Kamisama.'

Ōgon: 'Which path will you take from here?'

Terukuni: 'I shall follow the edge of this marsh to emerge at the Kaugen Plateau, pass through the land of Idomu to the capital of Yasu, stop at Riyōshiuzan, and since it is the Gomeirei of Hinodewake-sama to bring the Kamuzukasa of various places of the Brahmins to Kotomukeyaha, I cannot go straight to the capital of Haruna.'

Ōgon: 'Ah, is that so. I intend to turn right from here, cross the land of Fusa, emerge at the port of Taru, and proceed by sea to the capital of Haruna. Please take care of yourself.'

Terukuni: 'If that is the case, let us part ways here. I humbly ask for your favor regarding the two, Rebu and Karu.'

Ōgon: 'Farewell now, Kamuzukasa Terukuniwake, though it is with lingering regret that we part here.'

Kiyoteruhime: 'Beginning with the Tsukasa of Terukuniwake, I shall part from the Tsukasa of Teru, Haru, and Ume.'

Terukuni: 'O Mei of Hime Ōgon and Hime Kiyoteruhime, may you go in peace. Since the blessing of the Kami is deep, you shall cross the Unabara of Fusa in safety.'

Terukō: 'The color of the moon shines in Ōgonshiyoku, may you go along the Nobe of Hime Kiyoteruhime.'

Umekō: 'Bathing in the light of the moon on the great plain, may you mother and daughter go in peace.'

Harukō: 'Farewell, Hime Ōgon and Hime Kiyoteruhime, and Rebu and Karu-sama. May you reach the capital of Haruna as soon as possible, safely, according to the will of the Kami.'

Here, the party of four of Terukuniwake and the party of four of Hime Ōgon part ways to the east and west, and each ascends the road of their journey, following the paths they were commanded to take.
