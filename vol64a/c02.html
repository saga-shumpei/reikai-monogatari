Chapter 2: The Heralds of Truth [1631]

宣伝使〔1631〕

§ 1

There was one Tōyōjin who boarded a military train bound for Jerusalem, crossing the Suez Unga from Kantara Station. The Kishiya was racing through the center of the vast Daisabaku with the momentum of Itsushiyasenri. Outside the windows, there was a sense of Sekireu where neither Shinrin, Tahata, Kasen, Sonraku, nor even the shadow of a person could be seen. Here and there, desolate Teishiyajiyō, looking like Koya, stood in Mokumoku. Mount Sinai was hazily obscured in the far Enpau. It seemed they had reached the Kauchi of Judea, as the hills became steeply Kifukōbai moment by moment, and Kanran trees began to appear one after another outside the windows. A propagandist, appearing to be a Tōyōjin of about Shijitsusai, with round eyes and a dark complexion, and a dignified air from somewhere unknown, was a gentleman named Barabāsa, who had been dispatched from Takasago-shima, crossed the ocean for several dozen days, and had come ahead of Uzunbara Chiyandā, the leader of Rūtobahā, who had traveled from afar by the command of God as a forerunner of the second coming of the Messiah.

Barabāsa was well-versed in the languages of various countries around the world and was also proficient in the recently popular Esperanto. Therefore, he was specifically selected as a propagandist for Rūtobahā, crossed the vast ocean, sought sacred sites in foreign skies, and was assigned as a vanguard for the second coming of the Messiah. This Barabāsa had left behind one wife and one beloved daughter in his home country of Takasago-shima. Gazing at the infinitely wide desert unfolding outside the window, Barabāsa pondered the ancient deeds of the saints and let out a sigh with a heart full of deep emotion.

An elderly gentleman with white hair, who was sitting in the adjacent seat smoking a cigar, moved closer to Barabāsa and sought a handshake in a very familiar manner. Barabāsa could not help but be struck by an unexpected joy at being asked for a handshake on the same vehicle in a country where he was a stranger, thousands of miles across the ocean. The moment Barabāsa stood up and exchanged a handshake with the elderly gentleman, he felt a sense of nostalgia as if he had met an old friend of a hundred years.

The elderly gentleman said familiarly, 'I am one called Bahāurā of the Baháʼí Faith, and having heard from the Lord God that the second coming of the Messiah is near, I have carried my aged body and arrived here safely today, having finally come from the Tokoyo-no-kuni. From where have you come, sir? Even a brief look at your face tells me you are no ordinary person. I perceive you to be a saint, and if it is no trouble, I would like to speak with you.'

Barabāsa replied, 'Yes, thank you very much. It is an unexpected fortune for me to meet a saint like you in a foreign land and on the same train, and to exchange talk of our paths. In fact, I am a propagandist serving under the Great Lord God of Kuninotokotachi, who dwells by erecting the palace pillars at the central point of Takasago-shima, and I am called Barabāsa. I have come from afar, following the divine command, wishing to touch the spiritual atmosphere of Jerusalem for a while and serve a part of the divine administration of the spirit world. Since there are no detestable barriers such as nationality or race in religion and faith, I pray for our friendship as brothers.'

The elderly gentleman said, 'Indeed, let us seek a close friendship with each other. I am one who is not satisfied with the attitude of modern religious figures. We human beings, who grow under the light of the same sun, must love and help each other as the most beloved children of the Lord God in every way. And a religion that has broken away from all superstition; a religion freed from the moldy forms, not to mention the dead gods and dead Buddhas of the past; a religion that has transcended sectarianism and is thoroughly based on truth, goodness, beauty, and love; a religion that has broken away from mandala-like scriptures woven into complicated doctrines and commentaries mixed with various legends, forced associations, and superstitions; a religion that realizes the unity of name and reality, the unity of spirit and flesh, and the unity of God and man, and the non-duality of the sacred and the mundane; a religion of vast and boundless scope that dissolves the shackles of narrow-minded, prison-like faith that makes the founder who arose out of necessity for that era the sole savior and makes the founder's teachings the immutable sacred words of all ages, and brings about the Great Assembly of all saints, that is, the opening of the World Parliament; a religion that clearly indicates the great path of one husband and one wife; a religion that removes the barriers between the secular and the transcendental and teaches the true living path of one reality; a religion of true salvation that looks at all humanity as the object of love from the bottom of the heart, breaking away from narrow religions that sow seeds of contention by distinguishing between good and evil, believers and non-believers, salvation and sin, and heaven and hell; a religion with authority that solves everything including problems of national language, labor, and international relations, as well as problems of academics and religion, and makes all world humanity equal residents of the world of light—I have been fighting against all kinds of persecution and hardship for several decades because I am pressed by the necessity of such a religion. Therefore, since you have also traveled to the Holy Land as a divine messenger of Rūtobahā, I wish for a friendship of mutual support as brothers who are children of God, in order to save the people of all nations to the heavenly realm.'

Bahā'u'llāh-sama, I am truly impressed by your Okotoba just now. As for the Lūt-Bahā that I uphold, I cannot find even a hair's breadth of Sauiten in its Shiyugi and Seishin. The teachings of Lūt-Bahā in Takasagojima and your Kikyō are like the two halves of a tally matching in Tōzai. I sincerely hope that from now on, we may enjoy an Eien Shinkau as sister religions.

Nanitozo, I humbly request the same. Tell me, Burabāsa-sama, what is the Arisama of the Genkon Sekai? Has it not reached a state where the Ten no Kamisama is about to bring down a disciplinary iron hammer upon us, the Jinrui?

I read the London Times on the ship yesterday, and it contained an article that makes it impossible for us to remain calm. It was exactly the same as the teachings of Lūt-Bahā.

What kind of article was it?

The headline was written in size two type, grandly stating, 'The spirit of a deceased newspaper king whispered to the detective novelist Conan Doyle that a great catastrophe is coming to the world.' I have it in my pocket now, so I shall read it aloud.

Saying this, he took out a finely folded newspaper from his pocket, opened it, and read:

'Mr. Conan Doyle, currently in the United States, made the following eccentric announcement in San Francisco on the 31st. He said, "The spirit of the late Lord Northcliffe, the newspaper king, whispered to me: 'A great catastrophe will come to this world during your lifetime. Mr. Doyle is making the above statement quite seriously and earnestly,' and so on.

Having finished reading, he continued:

'The Lūt-Bahā that we believe in has also been asserting similar divine revelations for thirty years, but the scholars of the world, who are only obsessed with material research, and other fellow men not only refuse to believe us easily, but they have also spread rumors and called us deceivers, causing the Holy Lord and the believers to suffer oppression from all levels of society. The second coming of the Messiah can no longer be very far off.
