Chapter 20: The Mandate of Prosperity [1649]

福命〔1649〕

§ 1

Torashima Torako, seeing Yamoriwake, whom she had been longing for, return in a carefree manner, danced about the room with joy.

Torako: 'Come, Ohana-sama, prepare the sake. Magahiko-sama, prepare the side dishes. Hey, Hisanosuke-sama, why are you dawdling? Ōhirokimasamune-sama has returned. Men are such thoughtless creatures.'

She gave orders in a sharp, nagging tone. With a clatter, a bang, and a jingle, the sake and side dishes were finally prepared. Yamoriwake cast off his Western clothes, changed into a lined kimono for laundry, sat cross-legged with a thud, and while narrowing his upturned eyes even further and making a squeaking sound in his throat, he began to drink with great gusto. Yamoriwake is a troublesome man who forgets everything once he is drunk. As the intoxication took hold, he began to sing.

Ohana of Ayame, Torako, and Magahiko formed a circle and began to sing, starting with 'Yatokosei, yoiyanā.'

Yamoriwake: 'At the request of the Hinode-no-Kami, I sought to explore the ends of the world. Dressed like a beggar, I snatched away a woman and wandered through Honkon, Shiyanhai, and Pekin, but though I searched, I could not find a Hinode-sama with a physical body as Torakohime-sama described. My pockets grew empty, and I could not even drink the sake I craved, so I had no choice but to return to Shiyanhai in Shina with the black-faced beauty, a Christian I had fallen for. I listened to the stories of people from various countries, but no matter what, I could not understand. I decided that this was hopeless.'

Yamoriwake: 'I boarded the Tenpukumaru and returned to the island of Hinode, but I was accused of having received ten thousand ryō from a radical group. I was told to come out, thrown into a dark place with a lovely woman, and interrogated about various things. Now, I have returned as a man cleared of all suspicion. Truly, going abroad is a back-breaking affair. I will not go abroad again, even if Hinode-no-Kami-sama tells me to. I have truly seen what a fool I am. Today, I must drink plenty of sake to recover from the fatigue of the journey; otherwise, the worm in my belly will not be satisfied. Yatotokosa no untokoshiyo.'

Yamoriwake: 'Untoko, dokkoi, O-Tora-sama. Yatoko, dokkoi, O-Hana-sama. Even if the world were to be overturned, as long as there is sake, Yamoriwake finds the world truly peaceful. Drink and make merry, for just ahead lies darkness. After darkness, the moon will rise. The moon is the moon, but it is the end of my luck. The Mizu-no-Tsuki that has lost its charm. While you were unaware, I saw a newspaper article in Shiyanhai stating that the second coming of Hinode-no-Kami is near in Jerusalem, the capital of the West, at the foot of the Mount of Olives, and that Burabāsa has gone there under the secret orders of Chandā-sama from the holy ground of Okebuseyama.'

Yamoriwake: 'If you dawdle, Chandā will surely destroy the plan that was so painstakingly laid out. Everyone, please be careful. Untoko, dokkoi, dokkoishiyo. Dokkoi, slipping into the ash shed, tumbling and falling, covered in ash. It cannot be helped if one looks foolish. Because I wanted to warn you not to make a stern face like an owl that has missed its night meal, I crossed the rough seas and returned all the way here. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara. Do not let your eyeballs pop out. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if the earth sinks, if Hinode-no-Kami does not know of this magnificent plan, he will not be able to show his face to the world. Hurry and prepare it. Aha-ha-ha-hatsuha, how foolish. Ihi-hi-hi-hitsuhi, to a foreign land. Ufu-ufu-futsufu, wandering about. Ehe-ehe-hetsuhe, being chosen. Oho-oho-hotsuho, how terrifying. Kaka-kaka-katsuka, the radicals. Kiki-kiki-kitsuki, the danger. Kuku-kuku-kutsuku, slipping through. Keke-keke-ketsuke, the strange and evil. Koko-koko-kotsuko, in poverty. Sasa-sasa-satsusa, searching. Shishi-shishi-shitsushi, deeply. Susu-susu-sutsusu, disgusting. Sese-sese-setsu-se, being attacked. Soso-soso-sotsuso, the bottomless. Tata-tata-tatsuta, the fool. Chichi-chichi-chitsuchi, with all my might. Tsutsu-tsutsu-tsutsutsu, I have exhausted myself. Tete-tete-tetsute, in a frantic dance.'

§ 2

Toto-toto-totsu-to, in a far-off land, Nana-nana-natsuna, suffering great Nangi, Nini-nini-nitsuni, for Ninen, Nunu-nunu-nutsunu, the Nusubito, Nene-nene-netsune, peeking and listening to the Neiki, Nono-nono-notsuno, an Akushiyō like strangling the throat, Haha-haha-hatsuha, a desperate feat, Hihi-hihi-hitsuhi, Hiruyoru, Fufu-fufu-futsufu, Fudan Kuwatsudō I have carried out, Hehe-hehe-hetsuhe, Heko-tarete, Hoho-hoho-hotsuho, horo-horo-to, Mama-mama-matsuma, while hesitating, Mimi-mimi-mitsumi, the suffering of the body too, Mumu-mumu-mutsumu, Muri-ni enduring, Meme-meme-metsume, for the sake of Meiyo, Momo-momo-motsumo, to the skies of Morokoshi, Yaya-yaya-yatsuya, with great difficulty, Ii-ii-itsui, I have gone, Yuyu-yuyu-yutsuyu, even in a dream, Ee-ee-etsue, an Etoku-no-dekinai Shigumi.

Yoyo-yoyo-yotsuyo, searching well, the muddy waters of Ra-ri-ru-re-ro, Wawa-wawa-watsuwa, crossing and passing over, Ii-ii-itsui, now here, Uu-uu-utsuu, joyfully, Ee-ee-etsue, Enyara-yatsuto, Wo-wo-wo-wotsuwo, you have returned, Yamoriwake-sama, if we do not treat you with Daiji, the Meuga will be bad, Ah, Kanzake, it is Kanzake, Kanzake is better than Hiyazake, rather than serving Shihokarazakana, go and buy a Hitoshiho of Guji, and serve it to him lightly, how about that, Untoko-dokkoi-dokkoisho.

Yamoriwake-sama, as the drunkenness slowly circled, stood up and began to sing, trampled on the Tokuri and Sarahachizen, collapsed with a thud on the spot, and fell asleep snoring loudly.

Torako and Ohana, both of them, with Itsushiyōkenmei, carried Yamoriwake-sama into the Betsushitsu, stroking his feet, fanning him with an Uchiha, and exhausted all their sincerity in nursing him.

From the lush branches of the pine tree on Kobokuyama, an owl is crying, 'Gu-u-u-u-u-u-u-u-u-u-u, Aho-aho-aho.'
