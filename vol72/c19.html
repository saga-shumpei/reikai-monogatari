Chapter 19: 旧場皈 [1828]

旧場皈〔1828〕

§ 1

Chigusa no Takahime and Kyūbā, the pair, were in high spirits, their breath as fierce as a wildfire sweeping across a plain; they had driven out Tamakiyowake and every single person related to the Suga no Miya, and in their joy over this windfall that seemed to have fallen from heaven, they became like Utsutsusantarau, forgetting even that Mokusuke was sleeping at the Uranai headquarters in Kitamachi; though they did not particularly like him, they praised Kyūbā of Tennenbau—the driving force who had enabled their great victory in the Kototamasen—as being beyond compare, and they occupied the sacred grounds, indulging in dreams of swallowing up the world.

Kyūbā: 'Excuse me, Ikimiyasama, the work of Kyūbā shows a bit of skill, does it not? It surely cannot be the achievement of Ikimiyasama alone.'

Takahime: 'Well, that is true; just as a cart cannot run without two wheels, and a human cannot walk without two legs, that is the logic of it.'

Kyūbā: 'Indeed, that is so; even when eating, one hand will not suffice. Without two chopsticks, one cannot even pick up the pickled vegetables. In the age of the gods, the two deities Izanagi and Izanami stood upon the Heavenly Floating Bridge and, by harmonizing the breath of Yin and Yang, created various gods. How about it? Let us renew our old friendship here; I shall become Izanagi no Mikoto, and you, Ikimiyasama, shall become Izanami no Mikoto. Starting with the land of Toruman, not to mention the seven thousand and more countries of India, shall we not spread our wings as far as this land reaches? You once played the role of the queen of the Toruman nation, so you are skilled; you must be thinking of such things, are you not?'

Takahime: 'Such things, Kyūbā-sama, are mere trifles to speak of. Am I not the Savior of the three thousand worlds, the Great Miroku of the deep bedrock? This Ikimiyasama governs both heaven and earth, a divine pillar sent from heaven to unify even the fifty-six billion seven hundred million small universes; to unify this tiny earth is, compared to the vast and boundless universe, like the dung of an insect born from the eyebrow of a louse, or the dung of an insect born from that dung.'

Kyūbā: 'To think you would speak of such great things, only to preach of such small ones.'

Takahime: 'It is a settled matter; I am the Savior equipped with the divine authority of Miroku, the greatest without outside, the smallest without inside.'

Kyūbā: 'At Ikimiyasama's vast and boundless ambition, even this Kyūbā is left speechless. Even this Kyūbā is the great leader of seven thousand and more countries, with power unrivaled in the capital of Haruna, the right-hand man of Daikokunushi-sama.'

Takahime: 'Now, now, Kyūbā-sama, please stop such petty talk in front of the Great Miroku. This god dislikes small things. I am a god who performs great deeds, a god who performs deeds that have never existed in this world since ancient times.'

Kyūbā: 'Is that not exactly like the Ofudesaki of the San-kyō? Heh heh heh heh. By the way, Ikimiyasama, what do you intend to do with that second one called Mokusuke?'

Takahime: 'Ah, I was so happy that I had completely lost the god of time-keeping from my mind. Oh, this will not do; Kyūbā-sama, you must wait here. I shall go to Kitamachi for a moment to let my husband hear of this success and make him happy.'

Kyūbā: 'Excuse me, Takahime-sama, is it not a bit too cruel to me? To leave the first one behind and send amorous glances to the second one is a bit unheard of. No matter how much you say you will go, this Kyūbā will not let you.'

Takahime: 'You, keep your vanity within reasonable limits; it is not just the first one, there are eight of them; in short, they are all just Tempo coins, you see.'

Kyūbā: 'This is terrible; are you saying I am two mon short? In your eyes, does this Kyūbā look that much like a fool?'

Takahime: 'Now, do not worry. In recognition of your service this time, I shall wipe your buttocks for you, just a little. If one wishes to wipe the buttocks of the Great Miroku-sama, it cannot be done with ordinary means. It is said that you, as the Empress Yoriko, used to clean buttocks, but compared to cleaning such filthy buttocks, how much more honorable and successful it is to be allowed to clean the divine fertilizer of the Great Miroku-sama, ho ho ho ho.'

Kyūbā: 'Hey, you are making a fool of me, are you not?'

While they were talking like this, Mokusuke’s Yōgenbō, finding Takahime’s return delayed, came up the Torotoro Slope of Mount Suga and arrived in front of the mansion.

Standing at the entrance and listening to the situation, the precincts were silent with no sign of people, and it was so quiet that one could hear the cry of a cuckoo. However, as he could hear voices whispering furtively in the back of the mansion, he quietly went around to the back of the mansion and looked inside through the window, where he saw Takahime and Kyūbā sitting with sake and side dishes in the center, flirting and teasing each other, talking happily. Yōgenbō could not contain his anger, and letting out a voice like thunder from outside the window, he shouted with a single cry:

'Hey!'

The moment he shouted, Kyūbā was startled and jumped up about a ken, hit his bald head against the ceiling, and fell back onto the wooden floor as if a frog had been thrown, his limbs trembling, and he fainted.

As expected, Takahime did not move an inch, but looked quietly out the window and said, 'Ho ho ho ho, what is it, Moku-chan? Even if you shout with such a loud voice, there are no deaf people here. Takahime’s ears can hear even the cry of an earthworm, so please do not make a fuss. I have just successfully tricked this monk and have received this divine mansion, which the San-go-kyō spent a hundred days and a hundred nights of painstaking effort to build, completely with a deed, so please come in. It is unsightly if people see you.'

§ 4

Yō: 'Look here, Takahime, if what Kyūbā says is without error, I shall forget about this for today. However, it is a little inconvenient to have this fellow here. Since the headquarters in Kitamachi will fortunately be becoming vacant, how about we give that to Kyūbā?'

Takahime: 'If Mokusuke-sama is in agreement, let us give it to him; for it is said that the mediation and efforts of Kyūbā-sama played a part in the occupation of this mansion as well.'

Mokusuke: 'Hey, Kyūbā, in consideration of your services, I shall grant you the Kamuyakata in Kitamachi, so you had best return home at once and take a rest. I shall give you the Shinden and all the Shiyodaugu as well, so receive them with gratitude.'

Kyūbā: 'Yes, thank you very much; then I shall gratefully accept. I will clean it thoroughly with great care, so please, do come and visit from time to time.'

Yō: 'No, now that such a magnificent Kamuyakata has been obtained, I no longer recognize any need for it, nor is there any need for me to go there; you may do as you please with it.'

As this was said, Kyūbā, rejoicing, while bowing his head repeatedly, left behind these words: 'The Kamuyakata of the Uranai-kyō, I gratefully accept. If that is the case, you two, please enjoy yourselves at your leisure later,' and while saying this, he headed toward Kitamachi, returning home with a jaunty stride.
