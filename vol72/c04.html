Chapter 4: 銀杏姫 [1813]

銀杏姫〔1813〕

§ 1

Eugenbau, who had disguised himself as Mokusuke, and Chigusa no Takahime, fearing that they would lose their lives in the destruction and sinking of the Takasagomaru, both quickly stripped off their clothing and, becoming completely naked, leaped into the sea; at that moment, Eugenbau revealed his true form, transforming into a terrifying creature that was a hybrid of a lion and a tiger. Takahime, also stark naked, clung to the neck of the fur-covered Eugenbau, and while drifting with the waves, they washed ashore on a remote island where old trees grew thick.

Takahime, catching her breath, said, 'Ah, Mokusuke-sama, we encountered such a terrible storm, and I thought for sure my life was at an end; yet, thanks to your art of transformation, we crossed this rough sea, and thanks to you, my life was saved. My, you possess such marvelous hidden talents, do you not?'

Eugenbau, having been discovered in his true form by Takahime, was deeply pained at heart and was at the very moment wondering what excuse he might use to deceive her; however, receiving words of admiration from Takahime instead, he realized that she had interpreted his actions from the bottom of her heart as good intentions, so he intentionally put on an air of triumph and said, 'Hey, Chigusa no Takahime, my magic is truly magnificent, is it not? When the moment of crisis comes, I transform into this strange and monstrous form, neither lion nor tiger, so there is nothing in the world I fear. Since I have taken such a beauty as my wife, if I do not use some mysterious art to gain your trust, I am placed in a dangerous zone where I do not know when I might be replaced by that fellow Tōsuke; therefore, to save you, I transformed into this ugly body and exerted my loyal service to the Empress Chigusa no Takahime. Hahaha!'

Takahime: 'Ah, you hateful Mokusuke-sama. Secondly, I wish you would stop with that old-fashioned talk about Tōwake or Tōsuke. That Tōsuke is a man who worked as a boatman in Sumoto of Awaji, a man with a face like stiff paper, dark-skinned, and looking like a giant udo plant. He knows not a single extraordinary feat, and he is the sort of creature who finds joy in brushing the dust from the beard of Yashima no Wake and sniffing his backside; even if he were to pile up ten million ryō of gold and walk about on his head, I, Chigusa no Takahime, am not one to be swayed by him. It is too good to even call him Tōsuke; he is better suited as Tōfu-no-suke. With just one finger of this Takahime, I can crush him or tear him apart as I please. Ohohoho!'

Eugenbau: 'Hey, Taka-chan, you are blowing quite a lot of hot air, are you not? At the mansion in Saien, you were struck down by Tōsuke's elbow-cannon and defeated easily, and you fled weeping across Kajika Pass to hide in the forest of the shrine, lamenting the world, did you not? Your words are a bit too bold, are they not?'

Takahime: 'Wearing wooden clogs is only suitable for a fool. No, even simple wooden clogs are too good for Tōsuke. Since I, Chigusa no Takahime, am the Empress of the Tolman country, paulownia or agarwood clogs are what suit my nature. Hmph, do not go around belittling people by saying that wooden clogs are too good for them.'

Eugenbau: 'Hey, hey, Taka-chan, I am a bit troubled if you misunderstand me like that. The conversation has gone off the rails; when I said your words were too bold, I meant that your mouth was running too much.'

Takahime: 'Hmph, do not make a fool of me by saying my mouth runs too much; I can make a perfectly fine living with my mouth, and I can even afford to keep one or two men as playthings.'

Eugenbau: 'Hahaha, that makes even less sense, does it not?'

Takahime: 'Less sense, not parting, what are you talking about? You are thinking of parting from this Takahime, are you not? Seeing me with my hair elaborately styled and wearing face powder, you must have been infatuated with me. Now that we have been shipwrecked, my protective clothing has been snatched away by the waves, my hair is disheveled, and my private areas are exposed, you have likely lost your affection for me. Hmph, even like this...'

(Dodoitsu) Even if you dislike me, there are those who love me; if there were none, my own body would not stand.

As this Zokueu says, while there may be men who are discarded, there is hardly any place in the Sanzensekai where one can find a woman who is discarded. Hmph, if you dislike me, please say so clearly, for I have my own thoughts on the matter as well.

Yōkai: 'Ah, are you not saying something even more troublesome? Ha ha, I understand! I have read it! This Mokusuke has used too much Eujiyutsu, and the form I transformed into caught the eye of the Niyotei, and thus the Akikaze has blown. Very well, if that is the case, there should be plenty of room for me to think about it as well.'

Takahime: 'Once again, are you trying to make me feel helpless with your arts? Oh, what a disagreeable person you are. And what have you done with the jewel of the Magawa? Surely you have not dropped it into the lake, have you?'

Yōkai: 'Hmm, do not worry about that. When I dove into the lake, I swallowed it into my belly, so it is Daijiyōbu.'

Takahime: 'My, my, you swallowed it? Hmph, why did you not let me swallow it? You are truly a Mizukusa person, are you not?'

Yōkai: 'I have every intention of letting you swallow it, but in such a Totsusa, how could I have had such Yoyū? If you will excuse me, I have safely stored it in Mokusuke's Takaamahara, so there will surely be a time when I spit it out again. There is no need to be so Shinpai.'

Takahime: 'Indeed, what a Nukeme-less Mokusuke-sama you are. That is why you are Takahime's most beloved husband and my Danna-sama for generations to come. However, Mokusuke-sama, we have arrived at this island, but being naked like this, I cannot travel, and I wonder if you could not use the laws of the Magawa to fashion a fine garment for me? Even you, in that hairy transformed state, cannot go out among people.'

Yōkai: 'Indeed, just as you say. Just as I hear. Just as the tree frog stops on a tree and croaks, the beginning is as stated. I am also just like this, bowing down with both hands on the ground to apologize to the noble Takahime. Ha ha ha!'

Takahime: 'Now, Moku-chan, this is no time to be laughing. No matter how much you say it is spring, it is unbearable to be this cold, is it not? Is there not some way to devise a solution?'

Yōkai: 'Oh, there is a boat moored here. Thinking from this, there must be someone who has landed on this island. Shall we not peel off that fellow's skin and have you and I wrap ourselves in it?'

Takahime: 'Are you going to do something like a common highwayman? If you do that, you will not be called a messenger of the Tokiokishi. No matter how cold I am, I hate to wear stolen clothes. If you do such a thing, the divinity of the Hinodewake will fall to the very ground.'

Yōkai: 'What a foolishly honest Niyotei you are. As the old proverb says, one cannot choose between one's back and one's belly. If one intends to perform a great good, a small evil is unavoidable depending on the time and Baahi. Ah, it is cold, it is cold. With such a strong wind blowing suddenly, I cannot endure it. If there were a good bamboo grove somewhere, I would like to hide in it and avoid the wind.'

Saying this, he left behind the words, 'Takahime, follow me,' and hid himself within a bamboo grove where thin bamboo grew sparsely. In truth, although he had barely managed to transform his face to look human, he was suffering because he was not wearing a single piece of cloth on his body, and he feared that even his face would revert to its original Yōkai form; thinking that if he showed such a hideous face, even the noble Takahime would lose interest, he jumped into this bamboo grove to perform a second transformation art. This grove had an area of about a hundred tsubo, and as soon as one entered it, countless ants like white sesame seeds would swarm up, and no matter who the human was, they would bite and tear at the body, causing it to swell up and become unbearably painful and itchy. While this was happening, countless mysterious spiders with legs a foot long would come, emitting highly adhesive silk from their tails, binding the body tightly—it was a terrifying forest of demons. Not knowing this, Yōgenbō had jumped in, and it was unbearable. With the pain of countless ants biting into his coarse hair, even the great Yōgenbō let out a scream, begging for Takahime's salvation with a groan like a roaring tiger. Hearing that voice, Takahime approached the side of the grove and peered inside to find Yōgenbō in the midst of the agony of being tormented by ants, writhing in pain. Takahime, startled to the point of losing her composure, went around to the back of the bamboo grove and found a small, slightly elevated mound, upon which stood a great ginkgo tree with a circumference of three jō, reaching toward the heavens. At the base of the ginkgo tree stood a small shrine, where a young man and woman were praying while sobbing about something. The quick-witted Takahime, intending to steal the clothes of the two and save Tōsuke from his plight while also wearing them herself, hid behind the ginkgo tree and listened to the conversation of the two.

Takahime: 'Ho ho ho ho, now Moku-chan, what has become of the Shinriki of the Magawa gem? Surely you have not let those Shiroari fellows steal it, have you? It does not seem quite in Tsuriahi that you, who wield the Meujiyutsu of Shinpenfushigi, should be outdone by mere ants.'

Yōkai: 'They say that ants gather around sweet things, and do not gather around bitter fellows. At any rate, I am of the Saijiyōtō as a Ningen, and I am sweet to women, am I not? My Ketsueki is also Hitoichibai sweet, and because my Mitama is exceptionally good, those ants are Arigata and once they latch on, they will not let go. Even you, if you were to latch onto me, would not easily let me go, would you?'

Takahime: 'Indeed, hearing your Dauri, it is Gomotsuto Senban. I must ensure that Moku-chan does not go where there are ants from now on. I am sure that my body would also be swarmed by ants, would it not?'

Yōkai: 'That may well be, for you always let me hear your Tefutefunannan sweet whispers. However, those two Danjiyo who saved me are Kaai, so shall we not save them? No matter what, they are the parents of my Inochi.'

Takahime: 'Moku-chan, what are you saying? Now that your Shiyōtai has been discovered, would leaving such people not become a hindrance in the Gojitsu? If we leave them to be eaten by the ants as they are, it will not be the crime of Hitogoroshi, and the ants will be happy to fill their bellies; for the sake of the ants, we are saviors. Ants and Ningen are the same thing; if we save the lives of millions of ants in exchange for the lives of just two Ningen, who knows how much merit that will be. Give up such benevolence of Song Xiang. The ants who are so happy will be troubled. Come, come, since we have swindled and stripped the clothing of the two, you put on the man's. I dislike it, but I will wear the woman's for a while; what in the world is more precious than wisdom? Mokusuke-sama, have you understood the beauty of Chigusa no Takahime a little?'

Yōkai: 'Whether squid, crab, or octopus, the legs are four servings worth. Ah, impressive, impressive; even Mokusuke, the master of time, is humbled by your skill. It is like water on the face of a stunned frog, ufu ho ho ho.'

Fukue: 'Excuse me, please save me; is this not too cruel?'

Takahime: (While clicking her chin) 'Hmph, you stupid fool. What are you saying? Did I not give you the final rites so carefully just a moment ago? Well, it would be best if both of you just lay there and were eaten; be grateful and give thanks. Your body will soon become an ant tower; in a world where you cannot eat even if you work from morning till night, to be able to lie down and be eaten is to be a happy Ningen, oho ho ho.'

With a hateful look, she thrusts out her chin, slaps her buttocks three or four times, and runs off at full speed with Mokusuke to the boat landing, entrusts herself to the small boat the two had tied up, and rows away toward nowhere, singing a nasal song and continuing her sweet whispers across the calm, spring lake surface.
