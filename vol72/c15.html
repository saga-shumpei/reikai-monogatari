Chapter 15: 災会 [1824]

災会〔1824〕

§ 1

Borrowing the body of Chigusa-hime, the Ōhi of Toruman-koku, she revived in the Shiyaba once more. Changing her name to Chigusa-no-Takahime, she permeated the Seirei of Magatsu. She raged, vowing that she would never leave the Uranai-kyō unchallenged. She reunited with Eugenbau, the Kobun of the Orochi who dwells in the Horaana of Taiunzan, with whom she had formed a bond in a Zense. Once again, they tied the knot of Fūfu, and wandering here and there, they finally purchased a home in the Kitamachi of Suga-no-minato. They built a magnificent Shinden to rival the Seichi of Ananai-kyō that had been erected in Suga, and she raged, declaring she would compare their Seiriyoku.

Hiring two or three temporary Yakuin, she sat before a four-legged desk, facing the Zennan and Zenniyo who approached with solemn Kuchō. Sitting on a chair with an air of composure, she sipped water from a cup, cleared her throat with a loud 'Ahem,' and holding a fan in one hand, she fixed her eyes upon the believers. With a beauty that would put flowers to shame, she spoke in a cool voice: 'Welcome, everyone, to this pilgrimage. Though I may appear to be a human, I am by no means a common person. I am the living shrine of Hinodewake, born by receiving the spirit of the Tenjin of the First Reikoku; I am the great Miroku of the Sokotsu-iwane, the Savior of the three thousand worlds.'

Takahime: 'I am called Chigusa-no-Takahime. The teaching I preach is called the Uranai-kyō, a teaching without equal in the world. The blind shall have their eyes opened, and the deaf shall hear. The lame shall stand and walk. Lung disease, kidney disease, heart disease, stomach ailments, not to mention duodenal ulcers, appendicitis, neuralgia, bronchial catarrh, pulmonary catarrh, influenza, cholera, scarlet fever, paratyphoid, swollen glands, venereal sores, aching bones, pubic lice, and scabies—any illness whatsoever, if you are a person whose heart is acceptable to Takahime, I shall heal you immediately. However, I will not grant divine grace to those who do not understand the sincerity of the Uranai-kyō.'

Takahime: 'To those believers who engage in comparative research, asking which is greater, the gods of Ananai-kyō or mine, punishment shall befall you, not divine grace. Understand the logic here and strive, everyone, for a faith of absolute obedience. Mokusuke-sama, who served as the Sōmu at the mansion of the Saien, was called one of the three crows of the great god Susanoo-no-Ōkami and was the only evangelist in the world. He grew tired of Ananai-kyō, sympathized with the path of Takahime’s teaching, and finally went so far as to become my husband. That this has become a rare phenomenon where he is opening the teaching to the world is by no means a strange thing.'

Takahime: 'The plating will peel off soon enough. The authentic Uranai-kyō shines the more you polish it. Mokusuke-sama, a man of sharp intellect, quickly perceived the sincerity of the Uranai-kyō and abandoned the Magatsu teaching of Ananai. Even looking at this alone, you should understand. As for the person called Tamakiyowake, the priest of the Suga-no-miya, I know not if he is some horse-bone or cow-bone from nowhere, or perhaps a vessel for a fox or a transformation of a tanuki, but how can the sacred grounds of the gods be maintained with such a style? Just watch; within a month, it will be shattered to pieces before your very eyes, as clear as if reflected in a mirror.'

Takahime: 'There is no god of true salvation other than the Uranai-kyō. I know not what women like Yoriko-hime, Hanako-hime, or Dariya-hime are doing, but how could the gods be pleased to have three women who have their seven days of impurity each month staying in a pure and immaculate Shinden? If there is a god who would be pleased by such a thing, it must surely be a Magatsu. I am already forty-three; though I keep a youthful appearance, I am not a person who has guests staying for seven days a month. Even considering this, the teaching of the god of Uranai is the truth. Take heed, all you people. Do not let yourselves be led astray by the Magatsu teaching and become the vanguard of hell.'

Takahime: 'I warn you so that you do not do so. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara. Great God of the Uranai-kyō, Hegure-no-Hegure-no-Hegure-musha, Great God of the Hegure-jinja, Great God of Rintōbiten, Chijō-daijin, Chijō-hime, Chijōmaru-sama, Yukinari-sama, Sadako-hime-sama, Kizuki-hime, Great God of Miroku-jōju, Great God of Sadahiko-hime, Kotoyage-hime-sama, Haruko-sama, and a hundred other gods, before you, I, Takahime, appearing as Hinodewake, command the protection of the people gathered in this place. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara. May your spirits be blessed.' Just as this was happening, a Shugenja...

§ 2

Holding a Shiyakujiyō in his Yunde, hiding his face with a Fukaamigasa, and raising his voice in a most solemn Kototama, he entered and said, 'I request an audience with the Kyōso of the Daihonbu of the Uranai Kyō.' As he spoke, he pushed through the Gunshifu, made his way toward the Endan, and stood before Takahimetsukasa.

'I am the chief priest of the Sukobutsuen sect, a trusted retainer of Ōkuronushi who appeared in the capital of Haruna in the land of the Moon, a priest known as Kyūbā. I pray you, permit me to deliver a speech.' When he called out thus, Takahime knitted her brows in Fushin. Though his face was hidden by the hat, the tone of his voice sounded familiar somewhere.

Could it be, perhaps, the Kyūbā-sama whom she loved and longed for? 'Whatever the case, please ascend the Endan. I look forward to your excellent talk. Now, all you Goshinjiya, I shall descend from the Endan and rest in a room in the back. This person is a Shiugenjiya; he will surely give us a precious talk. Listen to him with reverence,' she said, while pushing through the people, hiding herself in the adjacent room, and watching to see how things would unfold.

Kyūbā, too, was astonished the moment he saw the face of Chigusa no Takahime. Though waves surged in his heart, he feigned ignorance and began his speech of approval. Kyūbā poured water into a cup.

He cleared his throat with a loud 'Ahem,' adjusted his Shinkan fan diagonally, threw off his hat, looked around at the Gunshifu, and said in a loud voice, 'I am a pilgrim who has suffered for many years on the path of Uranai. The Kyōshu-sama, Takahime, who has appeared now, is no mere human. She is a living god who descended from the heavenly skies, riding the clouds, to the sacred grounds of the mountains of Kohoku. Everyone, please rejoice. If you believe in this god, your longevity, good fortune, and prosperity will be assured; there is no doubt of a bountiful harvest and the extension of your life.

In the first place, among gods, there is a distinction between the true god and the evil god. If you are to worship a god, you must revere the true god.

And cast aside the evil god. The true god grants life, heals illnesses, and bestows good fortune; such is the way of infinite benevolence. The Magatsu no Kami causes illness, brings poverty, shortens lives, and even causes heavy rains, strong winds, and earthquakes; it does nothing but things that people dislike. The Miroku Kyō, built on the mountains of Suga, is just the same. While wearing a mask of goodness, it gathers evil demons and prays morning and evening to disturb this world. Everyone, please be careful. Do not go to the mountains of Suga. Warn your neighbors and relatives, and receive the blessings of the god of the Uranai Kyō.

Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase. I, too, have business with the Kyōshu. For now, excuse me,' he said, and while leisurely descending the Endan, he headed toward the room where Takahimetsukasa was hiding.

The many believers had expressions of bewilderment. Muttering complaints to one another that they did not understand what was what, they slowly began to return home. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase. The excitement was enough to make one's eyes pop out.

Takahime was hiding in a room and listening to Kyūbā's speech, and she thought, 'Judging by his build, the shape of his face, and the tone of his voice, he is not in the slightest different from the Kyūbā I met at Toruman Castle. My, this has become a strange situation. Mokusuke-sama, who is prone to jealousy, is sleeping in the annex, so that is fine, but I do not know when he might wake up. If it is indeed Kyūbā-sama, what shall I do?' as she held her chest in thought. There, Kyūbā entered with stealthy footsteps and said in a low voice, 'Hey, Chigusa-hime-dono, do you remember this face?'

Takahime: 'Yes, how could I ever forget that face? It is a countenance without equal in the world. And you, are you not being terrible? Where have you been wandering?'

Kyūbā: 'Now, Chigusa-dono, at the divine hall on Mount Suga, a woman who rose from being a mountain bandit named Yoriko-hime has taken a haughty stance, hung a large sign reading "Religious Debate Hall," and has brazenly posted, "I shall hand over the duties of this hall to the person who can defeat me in debate." Tell me, do you have the heart to go and engage in a debate?'

Takahime: 'What, Yorikohime is writing such things? How foolish she is; to cut one's own neck with one's own sword, this is truly like a moth flying into the fire. Ah, how interesting. In that case, I shall make preparations from today, and we shall set out tomorrow.'

Kiyū: 'Ah, I am grateful for your prompt consent. Though I may be of little use, I shall accompany you. However, Chigusahime-sama, do you have this, you?'

He thrusts out his thumb.

Takahime, hesitating slightly, then speaking with resolve:

Takahime: 'Yes, I have a splendid husband named Mokusuke-sama, a Kami of the Tokiokashi. If you speak in a loud voice, he will wake up, so please, I beg you, speak in a low voice.'

Kiyū: 'Even though a true husband is here, why should I, the true husband, have any need for reserve? That fellow called Mokusuke is a scoundrel who snatched away my wife. Very well, I shall force my way into the inner room and have a showdown with him.'

As he attempts to stand up, Takahime suddenly grabs him by the collar, strangles his throat, delivers a blow to his body, and quietly throws him under the floor. Ah, what will be the fate of Kiyūbā?
