§ 1

The direct-action-style struggle between Genshinbau, Koburai, and Kōro aboard the Tokihamaru ended safely, and the ship glided quietly over the water, causing the waves to beat against the hull like drums.

To soothe the boredom aboard the ship, interesting folk songs from various lands were heard from here and there. Among them, the most prominent was the funauta of the Ebisumatsuri. The boatman stood at the bow and began to sing with a refreshing voice accustomed to the sea, in an interesting rhythm.

‘In the first dream of the first and second days of the New Year, I fashion a boat from the camphor tree of Kisaragiyama, And launch it now. I set up the Shiroganebashira, Loading it with the wealth of Kogane, Spreading sails of figured brocade, Sailing toward the island of treasure, Loading it with amata treasures, Leaving it to the following wind, Racing to the port I desire, And storing it in this storehouse. Yōn, dē, yāru. Two Kami of the ancient days, This island, which shook forth from the Konrinzai, They say is called Onokorojima. On the mountains are Tokiha colors, Kogane and Shirogane flowers blooming, Even though the mountain winds blow, In the season that does not scatter, the lands, Even to the bays, are prosperous. Five grains, grasses, and trees are without lack, Seven treasures and ten thousand jewels fill the storehouses, From the blessings of the age that is never closed, Since we hear of long life and no illness, Ships gather from the lands of the four directions. From the undergarments of figured brocade, To the striped cotton and the crimson, Mixing the Tang and the Yamato, How bustling the shops of trade are. The villages of hunting and fishing, Have pheasants, ducks, and cranes in the mountains. Along the rocky shores of the back ports, As the morning tide of Ebisu opens, Yansa, how auspicious, the bowl of water, Yōn, dē, yāru.’

Genshinbau, hearing this song, jumped up, and feeling that he would not be outdone, the funauta that spilled out from his poor head was something a bit bizarre.

Genshinbau: 'The spring sea surface shines well, With many large and small islands, Lined up like Go stones, Pirate ships go back and forth to the right and left, Moving sideways here and there, When they see a ship loaded with treasure, They come at once, And without letting you say yes or no, they plunder, If you talk back, they even take your life, Such a terrifying ship. If you encounter such a group, Yōn, dē, yāru. Like a hammer-headed shark drifting in the river, You will never be able to hold your head high for a lifetime. I, too, in the past, joined hands with the great leader of the mountain bandits, And descended to the mountain of Ōra, Using the tips of the cedars as a trick, Making many firelight shadows shine, Stars descended from heaven. I spread the word that you would hear the sermon, Of the incarnation of the Heavenly Emperor, the Savior, Genshin Nyorai, And deceived the good men and women, From village to village here and there. Just when I thought I had one more breath, Umekōwake of the San-go-kyō, Came out with his wife, And together with two women, I was made to eat the octopus-trapping pot, Truly a worthless octopus monk. When I think of it now, it is terrifying. Yōma, the Kami of heaven and earth, When I think that they kept this evil monk alive, Until now, it seems my merit is exhausted. Yōn, dē, yāru. I wandered here and there, Planning only evil things, Three hundred bad elements. Turning here and there, I was alone, and when I took Dariya-hime into the rock cave of the mountain, To make her do as I wished, It was the foolishness of a cicada shell. Since then, I have become desperate, And at the village estate of Kamiya-mura, I tried to sneak into the mansion of Tamakiyowake, To snatch back Dariya and force her to be my wife, But that too ended in bubbles. When I remember all the evil things, I have done so far, Cold sweat wells up over my whole body, Like a sudden evening shower. Yōn, dē, yāru. The ship I am on now is the Tokihamaru, I am a propagandist serving the business, Of the Kami of the mansion of Saien. Helped in a dangerous place, By the master, Terukuniwake, I have rebuilt my heart from the bottom, And am going to serve as an attendant. Yōn, dē, yāru. Now, from here on, from here on, I will rebuild the foundation of my heart, If there is an enemy who opposes the Kami, I do not care if it is a demon or a snake. I will jump in with my life as the target, And I must make up for, The evil I have exhausted until now. Ah, how interesting, how interesting, The belly drum of the interesting tanuki, Moving briskly over the beating waves, Tanuki monk, octopus monk, Whether people laugh or slander, I do not care about such things. From now on, I will expose my shame to the world, And make atonement for my own sins.'

Tokihamaru finally arrived safely at the port of Suga around midday the following day. In this port, bonito fishing is flourishing. Just as Tokihamaru arrived, the net-hauling had begun. To soothe the weariness of their journey, the party asked the fishermen to let them join in the net-hauling, and they decided to sing songs together in an Omoshirōkashi manner.

Several boats gathered around the net, pulling it up toward the shore while keeping time with a chant. When the lead boat sang the beginning of each verse, the fishermen of the other boats would join in and continue, thereby equalizing the tempo of their strength; the rhythm was just like that of a Kiyari-bushi.

'If only this child were a boy, I would let him hold the oar. Hora, hōo, satsusā, yatchin-ee. Iyanho, satsusā, yatchin-ee. Suga is shining, shining, the island of Tama is cloudy. Rain falls on the high mountains of Ai. The great rock of O-iwa splits in two, and when it splits, the world is good, yoyahā satsusa. Pull, young men, beautiful assistance, let the twelve boat-spirits be brave. The master is Daikoku, the mistress is Ebisu, and the children in the middle are the boat-spirits. Perch a nightingale on the stern of the boat, and I want to make it cry for a big catch tomorrow. Even if the boat is new and the oar is new wood, it will not run if there is no boatman. Hora, hōo, satsusa, yatchin-ee satsusa. Yatchin-ee, yoiyahā satsusa.'

Terukuniwake: 'Now, Terukō-dono, is this not an interesting net-hauling? The many boatmen are showing their black buttocks, barefoot and naked, with black headbands tied askew, spreading a large net across the entire surface of the sea, and chanting the Kototama in unison to bring up the bonito—is this not a spectacle that strikes one with an indescribable sense of grandeur?'

Terukōwake: 'Indeed, as you, my master, say, it is the height of the magnificent and the sublime. If we Sendenshi were to imitate that net-hauling, we could draw in at least tens of thousands of believers at once; it would be interesting if we could perform our mission well. How about it, Sensei? If we go to the town of Suga from here, perhaps we could rent a large public hall and let tens of thousands of townspeople hear us at once, and many believers might be able to bathe in the divine virtues of the Ōkami. With little labor and much effect, is this not the most civilized method?'

Terukuni: 'No, no, that is not so. Public halls are absolutely unsuitable for the propagation of the path of the Kami. Public halls are places where politicians and ideologues indulge their private interests; even if one were to teach the sacred path of the Kamisama in such a place, it would not correspond to the spirit and soul of the people, so there would be much labor and no merit.'

Terukō: 'Then, Sensei, how about a theater?'

Terukuni: 'That is even more impossible. A theater is a place where pleasure-seekers gather. It is a place for Kabuki, Jōruri, Naniwabushi, magicians, and motion pictures; even if many listeners were to come, they would come in a mood for sightseeing, so not a word would enter their ears. It would be more like damaging the name of the Kami.'

Terukō: 'Indeed, when you put it that way, there is nothing to be done. Then, how about a school auditorium?'

Terukuni: 'A school auditorium is a place for academic research. Profound, subtle, metaphysical truths and faith cannot be conveyed at all by speaking in a school auditorium. Since everyone listens with a foundation of research, no one can enter into true faith. Youth halls, clubs, public halls—all places where the masses gather are useless. Fish caught by night-footing or fish caught by net, even if they are the same fish, taste bad. Just as fish caught one by one by attaching bait to the tip of a fishing line taste good, the propagation of the path of the Kami is in accordance with the principle of one-to-one. If it cannot be helped, five or six people might be unavoidable, but that would actually lead to failure.'

照国『大なる悪事を為したる者は悔い改むる心も亦深い。真剣味がある。それ故身魂相応の理によつて直に掌をかへす如く地獄は化して天国となるのだ。沈香も焚かず庇も放らずと云ふ人間に限つて、自分は善人だ、決して悪い事はせないから天国に上れるだらう等と慢心して居ると、知らず識らずに魂が堕落して地獄に向ふものだ。悪い事をせないのは人間として当然の所業だ。人間は凡て天地経綸の主宰者だから此世に生れて来た以上は、何なりと天地の為に神に代る丈けの御用を勤め上げねばならない責任をもつてゐるのだ。その責任を果す事の出来ない人間は、仮令悪事をせなくとも、神の生宮として地上に産みおとされた職責が果されて居ない。それだから、身魂の故郷たる天国に帰ることが出来ないのだ』

照公『天国に吾魂在りと思ひしに 地獄に向へる事の歎てさ。 今よりは心の駒を立直し 神の任さしの神業励まむ』

玄真『身は仮令根底の国に沈むとも 神の恵みは忘れざるらむ』

照国『千早振る神の恵みは世の人の 夢にも知らぬ処にひそむ。 暗の夜を照り明さむと宣伝使 よさし玉ひぬ瑞の大神』
