Chapter 3: 厳の欵乃 [1812]

厳の欵乃〔1812〕

§ 1

Toyosaka rises the morning sun, and the light of rigor also illuminates the path of Terukuniwake. The party of the ministry, while it is still early morning, awakens and sets sail from the port of Irie, on the shore of the clear Lake Haru where the waters are calm. The teachings of the rare, Terukō-sama, and Umekōwake, who opens them all at once, together with Genshinbau, board the Tokihamaru, a name that is itself auspicious.

The party of the teachings of the pine manipulates the oars and moves leisurely, sliding through the port of Haru. The sea of Haru, filled with waves like fish scales, reflects the shadows clearly, and here and there, albatrosses and seagulls fly about in a beautiful manner. Terukuniwake stands up, bows down to worship the Amatsuhikage, and with a clear voice, chants the Futonorito.

The chanting voice startles Kaijiyaku, and while striking the waves like drums at the side of the ship and shouting of the coming of the divine nation, their progress is truly valiant. Terukuniwake: 'The Mototsukami who appears on the mountain of Tenkyō in the days of Kamiyo, by the divine decree of Konohanahime, with the spirit of rigor and the spirit of the divine, intends to open the teachings to the four quarters. Entrusting many angels, to the limit where white clouds trail and blue clouds descend, I convey the noble teachings of the Imperial God, the limit of which is unknown. Following those divine instructions, the divine pillar of the spirit of the divine, the Great God Lord Susanoo, who appeared in the hall of the Saien, while bearing a thousand offering shelves, his body is soaked and wet in the rain, his hair is combed by the wind, and his hands and feet are chapped by the frost.'

'Lacking food and clothing, even on cold evenings that cut the body, or on summer days that seem to burn, he does not falter or yield for the sake of the Way. To save the troubles of the people of the world, he strives, and how noble he is. Building the hall of the Saien in the sacred ground of Mount Ubusuna, while purifying the hearts of people who have been defiled by the souls of evil, he causes them to be reborn as true humans. To us who are deep in sin and guilt, he has even bestowed the title of missionary, a name that is itself auspicious. To save the people of the world who have fallen into the floating rapids and are suffering, he is planning to subdue the Magatsunokami who dwell in the capital of Haruna and build the divine nation forever.'

'While he is planning this, he sends divine ministers to the four quarters. It is grateful that he causes the spirits of the heavenly men and heavenly women, who descend from the high Takamagahara where the blue clouds are high, to descend into our souls and protect us. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase. When the message entrusted by the God is fulfilled, I wait with joy for the dawn when I shall again worship the most beautiful noble countenance of the Great God at the divine hall of the Saien in the sacred ground of Mount Ubusuna, and receive the voice of the God. It is joyful to tread the path of the God.'

'The path I walk is protected thickly by the Imperial God, so even if any evil should attack, how could I fear? The brave men of the divine nation of Shikishima, even if heaven and earth were to overturn, even if the moon were to fall and the stars were to sink, how could they falter? Like the catalpa bow that does not shake, my true heart is unwavering. May there be good fortune for our party crossing the sea of Haru. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase. The grace of the God is to be feared. The grace of the God is to be feared.'

Terukō: 'Having received one character of the name of my teacher, Terukuniwake, I, who bear the name Terukōwake, am noble, for the divine majesty of the God also shines upon me. Since leaving Mount Ubusuna, together with my teacher, while overcoming all kinds of troubles, subduing and harmonizing the evil gods here and there, and saving the hardships of all people, the journey of the mission that has come this far is truly joyful. This is the famous Lake Haru; the waves are fine and the wind is clear. The Tokihamaru, which advances with its true sails filled, is a divine ship. It is a divine ship that leads and carries all the people of the world to the heavenly kingdom. It is a divine ship that takes on all sins and defilements and lets them wander and disappear into the sea of a thousand leagues. Ah, how valiant, how valiant. Let the waves rise, let the wind blow. With the momentum of a thousand leagues in a single rush, may the ship I ride run swiftly to the port of Suga. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase. The journey of the teachings of the God has joys that the people of the world do not even know in their dreams. Bowing down, I pray for happiness on the earth, and looking up at the heavens, I pray for the peace of the people of the nations. With grass for a mat and a stone for a pillow, with the branches of trees as a roof, and gazing at the moonlit sky, how many nights of sleeping in the fields, it is truly a joyful journey. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase. May the spirits be blessed.' Umekō: 'Following my teacher, and according to the instructions of the Great God Lord Susanoo, I leave, looking back at the hall of the Saien. Our party is without incident.'

§ 2

Having crossed the difficult pass of Kajika, / Through the forest of the shrine and Kogitayama, / Without even casting a glance at the forest of Ukiki, / Crossing over the Thames Pass, / Arriving at the Aoi Marsh, / Where even the moonlight is of Ōgon, / The Sendenshi of the Hime-no-Inochi, / With the Kiyoteru of the marsh, / Parting the sleeves of the Hime-no-Inochi, / To the right and to the left, / Saving the crisis of the Tolman country, / Through the grace of Kami, / Reunited with my teacher, / At last I have come to this place. / Ah, Kamunagara Kamunagara, / The grace of Kami is awe-inspiring, / The grace of Kami is awe-inspiring.

Genshinbau sings:

Entrenched in the mountain of Aura, / The one who carried out, / The plot of Akugiyakubudau, / Am I not the Kataude of Yamataorochi? / A Ninpinin beyond compare, / Deceiving the Hime of Yoriko, / Making the mountain of Aura my base, / Regarding the Dorobōgashira Seego, / As my Bōshiyu, I set up on the branches, / Of the great Ōsugi towering high on Aura Mountain, / A Hoshikuda, / Kidnapping the Fujiyo of Riyōka, / Bewildering the good men and women, / Plundering gold, grain, and goods, / Plotting to usurp, / The Moon Country of over seven thousand nations. / Just as the time arrived, / The Kamisukasa of the path of the teachings of the Three-Five, / Was intruded upon by Umekōwake, / And my plan became a painted cake.

Without a place to turn, I selected three hundred, / Bad elements, / Crossed the lake plain of Haru, / Slipped into Tarahan Castle, / And hearing the situation, the Sahamori, / Shiyakanna, who possesses both wisdom and courage, / Was banished ten years ago, / And hiding his form deep in the mountains, / Was planning a comeback. / Hearing this rumor, I leaped for joy, / Disguising myself as an incarnation of the Tentei, / To make Dariya, whom I never forget even in dreams, / My companion for the night, / I stepped deep into the mountains, / Risking the two paths of lust and desire. / In a rock cave of Mount Taniguku, / Guided by petty thieves, / Just as I was about to spend the night, / Dariya-hime, whom I rely on as my life, / Watched for my moment of drunkenness,

And from the moment she vanished like sea foam, / What cared I for Shiyakanna or anything else? / Borrowing two hundred subordinates, / While searching for the whereabouts of Dariya, / I pinned down that she was hiding, / In the house of the village head of Kamiyamura. / The joy of that dawn, / Felt as if I were ascending to heaven. / Clouds gather in the sky, wind blows on the flowers, / The world is such that there is no help for it. / Like the moon on the water that cannot be grasped, / I left my heart behind and proceeded reluctantly, / Following the dark path. / Sitting on the rock of the pass of Tarahan, / Which is high when climbed, / Looking out far ahead, / The castle built in the field, / Is suitable for my dwelling, / I thought, and while roaring with bravado, I, a petty thief, / Proceeded with two others.

The path ahead was various, / Wrapped in the clouds of love's journey. / In the futility of never finishing, / My heart grew impatient, and while I waited, / Sneaking into the castle town of Tarahan, / A great fire broke out in Tarahan Castle. / Thinking the time of heaven had arrived, / I broke into Tarahan Castle, / Slipped into the treasure warehouse, / And just as I was rushing to seize the military funds, / I was captured. / Though I was imprisoned for a time, / And saw a moment of misery, / As soon as I heard that the Dorobōgashira Shiyakanna, / Had usurped the position of Sahamori, / And was seizing the reins of government, / I spewed forth slander and hatred, / And finally made the Sahamori commit suicide, / Forced him to tribute tens of thousands of Ōgon, / And on a narrow path along a mountain stream, / Which I was not used to treading,

I was chased by pursuers and had no choice, / But to leave my fate to heaven, / And with a splash, I leaped, / Into the mountain stream. / Having become unconscious, / I suddenly found a pillow on a journey in the Yūkai. / While meeting with a hundred torments, / I was saved by human compassion, / And plotted evil once again. / Staying at an inn on the beach of the inlet, / Just as I was in a tipsy mood, / The beauty Chigusa no Takahime, who lies to flowers, / I was bewitched by her charms and drooled, / Even my purse was tightened, / And all my money was stolen. / Just as my life was in danger, / By the teacher Terukuniwake, / I was finally saved and am now here.

The Dharma's friend-ship, the Tokiwamaru, / Restored with the heart of a pine, / Umekōwake, who scents the heart, / Together with the Kamisukasa, / Of Terukuni, who shines brightly, / While bathing in the cool breeze, / We advance on the great lake plain, / Where the ripples of crepe-like waves drift. / How happy is my own fortune. / Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, / Whether the moon waxes or wanes, / Even if heaven and earth should overturn, / The sincerity I have sworn to Kami, / Shall not falter even through thousands of generations. / Until the end of the Miroku of the pine, / Please protect me, Kamunagara. / Reverently before the presence of Kami, / I pray for the future of my own life.

§ 3

Terukuniwake: 'As I sail upon the spring Unabara of Azusayumi, the Momodori and Chidori fly in the Ōzora. How fortunate am I to sail upon the spring lake, which resembles the spring of Tengoku.'

Terukōwake: 'The sun shines brightly in the Ōzora, and the joy of advancing quietly along the Unaji of Terukō. The wind is clear and the waves are calm; the Funabata of the ship I ride upon beats like a drum against the waves.'

Umekōwake: 'The plum blossoms, which open at once by the Mioshihe, must be fragrant throughout the Sanzensekai. While intoxicated by the Keshiki of spring where the plum blossoms scent the air, I cross the spring lake.'

Genshinbau: 'Recalling the wrongs I committed in the past, even the Kami-no-mifune feels all the more painful. From now on, I shall advance upon the Ōmichi of the true Kami, even if I should die.'

While each was reciting their poems in this manner, they proceeded across the calm Komen of the spring lake. Just then, the Itsuten suddenly clouded over, a Baufū blew violently, and the Gekirau and Dotau surged forth like Sangaku; the Tokihamaru became in a truly perilous Kuōkei, as if it were a leaf scattered by the wind. Looking far out across the distant sea surface, the hull of the Takasagamaru, famous upon the spring lake, was smashed to pieces, and all the passengers were tossed about by the Gekirau and Dotau, their voices crying out for rescue with their last breath, as if the state of the Kyōkan Jigoku had manifested. Forgetting the peril of his own body, Terukuniwake encouraged the boatman to save the distressed Takasagamaru, and while rowing with eight oars, he rowed with all his might toward the site of the Takasagamaru's shipwreck. Ah, Kannagara Tamachi Haemase.
