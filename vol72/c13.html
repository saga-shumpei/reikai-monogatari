Chapter 13: 捨台演 [1822]

捨台演〔1822〕

§ 1

Kyūbā, who had undertaken the Setsuchinsauji, was a Nisekaishin. Seeing the Kamutsukasa of Tamakiyowake, Dariya-hime, standing side by side and reciting the Norito, his Shineniba began to run wild. Observing their intimate Yōsu, he could not bear the jealousy and stomped his feet in Jidanda. Yet, after Shibaraku, he considered the Yōsu of the mansion and thought of what else he might do. He was truly attentive to every Zatsuta detail, for within the mansion of the Suga-no-Miyai, there were items of Kennonshigoku. As five or six days passed, Kyūbā burned with the flames of love in his heart, thinking of how he might make Dariya-hime his own.

During the Habakarisauji, Dariya-hime entered the privy. Thinking this was a fortunate and Kutsukiyō Baahi, he stood before the Tearahibachi. While drawing water with a Hishiyaku and pouring it over Dariya's Riyōte, he watched for an opening and firmly grasped her Shirauo-like Senshiyu. Kyūbā: 'Listen here, Dariya-sama. I am Shinken about you. If you do not listen to my request once more, I shall die. A man with Kintama, a brave fellow, joins his hands before a delicate woman; this request is purely because of love. There is no distinction of high or low in love. Though names may change, such as master or servant, all humans are the divided spirits of the Tentei. Is there really such a distinction as noble or lowly?'

Kyūbā: 'Is there such a distinction? If you despise me because of the Setsuchinsauji and will not listen to what I say, I shall also consider a plan. So that you do not bite your navel in regret later, settle your mind and give me an answer. Be brave and do it, Kyūbā is at the crossroads of life or death. Whether to let one man live or to kill him depends entirely on your heart. Even if you say I am an ugly man, I am not a tiger or a bear; I am a man with eyes, nose, mouth, ears, and eyebrows properly attached. I have five fingers on my hands and feet. Even if you are called a beauty, your tools are surely no different. Settle your mind quickly. It is a man's pride, and I will not pull back once I have said it. Come, come, your answer!' As he pressed her, Dariya-hime laughed.

Dariya-hime: 'Listen here, Grandpa Kyūbā. Are you saying this in earnest? You must be saying this because you are drunk on sake. No wonder your face is a little red. If you have time to say such nonsense, you had better clean the mansion quickly. If Yoriko-hime-sama hears of this, she will surely give you a terrible scolding. I will never say anything bad. Please let go of that hand.' When she said this, Kyūbā's eyes bulged.

Kyūbā: 'Why, why should I let go? If I let go of this hand, you will immediately fly out before the Empress Yoriko-hime and tell her what I have done, will you not? If that happens, I will not be able to keep my seat in this mansion. Once an arrow has left the bow, it does not return to its origin. A good answer!' As he pressed her, Dariya screamed in a high-pitched voice.

Dariya-hime: 'Oh, oh, help me, I am scared!' As she called out with all her breath, Hanaka was surprised and came running. Seeing this state of affairs, she looked on. Hanaka: 'If it is not Kyūbā-dono. Do not do such foolish things. This is a noble divine mansion. It would be wise for you to understand that. With a face like wrinkled paper, you grasp the hand of a beauty of the world and talk of love and nonsense. Consult with your own face, and then let go of that hand. You are truly an astonishing fake monk. Pretending to have reformed, you snuck into the mansion for a while, and while appearing diligent, you are a wolf in sheep's clothing plotting to satisfy your lust for love. A villain like you should leave as quickly as possible. If you do not let go of that hand immediately, I will tell the Empress Yoriko.'

When she said this, Kyūbā steadied his torso, opened his mouth wide, and laughed loudly. Kyūbā: 'Atsuhahaha! Hatsu! Hey, hey, you little octopus-woman. Who do you think I am? I am the Kyūbā-no-Kimi, the great founder of the Sukobutsuen sect, the savior most trusted by Daikoku-nushi-no-Ōkami. With the body of the only daughter of a mere medicine seller or the daughter of a Mokubei, you dare to slap my cheek? What is there to fear from Yoriko-hime? If I hole up in the mountains of Aura and act as a mountain bandit, or if I present myself to the Brahmin station and say, "I beg your pardon," you too will be regarded as my accomplices and cohorts. You will be thrown into a dark prison, groaning without seeing the light of day, and though you may lament, it will be of no use. You will become criminals in the shadows before your very eyes. Even so, do you refuse the demands of Kyūbā, Hanaka-hime? And you too, Dariya-hime? If it comes to this, I am desperate. I will turn the Suga-no-Goten upside down from its foundations, and the priest, that old fool Tamakiyowake...'

§ 2

'I shall show you a Hoezura (a face of defeat)!' With such Baujiyakubujin (arrogant) words from Kyūbā, Hanaka was left dumbfounded. She immediately rushed into the inner chamber and appeared before Yoriko, reporting the Baujiyō (outrageous behavior) of Kyūbā in Chikuichi (every detail). Hearing this, Yoriko smiled, adjusted her Emon (garments), and with an Iuiu (leisurely) air, approached the vicinity of the Benjiyo (latrine).

Yoriko: 'Hotsu-ho-ho-ho, Kyūbā-sama. Thank you for your truly Goshinsetsu (kindness). Not only did you clean my bottom, but you even took my hand to wash it. My, what a thorough effort; I am deeply moved by your Goshinsetsu. Now, now, Dariyahime-dono over there, please listen kindly to what Kyūbā-sama has to say.'

Upon her words, Dariyahime spoke in a Namidagoe (tearful voice): 'No matter how much of a Subeta (lowly woman) I may be, or how humble my Mibun (status) might be, I feel as though I am being defiled even by speaking to an old man who performs Habakarisauji (latrine cleaning). Much less, to ask me to become your Niyōbau (wife) is simply too much. My Harada (anger) and tears have dried up, and I am so stunned that I cannot find words. Nanitozo (please), I beg you to forgive me.'

As she burst into tears, even the persistent Kyūbā let go of her hand, turned toward Yoriko, and said: 'Now, now, Yoriko-niyotei-sama. I had been wearing a cat's mask, but now that I have revealed myself, it is a matter of breaking the pot. Shall I take your Kubitama (head) as a prize? If that is not to your liking, I shall Sugusama (immediately) rush to the Brahman station, Sutsupanu (expose) your Sujiyō (true identity), and make you suffer the shame of the Nahame (bonds). Ika (how) is that?' As he sniffled and approached with his Katahiji (shoulders and elbows) raised in a Tsumeyo (threatening) manner, Yoriko-hime smiled.

Yoriko: 'Where is the proof that I have committed evil deeds until now? Since you speak of things I do not understand, shall I, on the contrary, report you to the Brahman office, Kyūbā-sama? Ika, ika (how, how)?' When she countered him with this, Kyūbā hesitated for a moment, held his breath, and sank into silence.

Ah, Imikami-kami-kami. How this situation will be resolved and what the future holds is truly interesting.

Kyūbā, enveloped in the flames of love and unable to endure, had grabbed the hand of Dariyahime and tried to woo her with threats and deceits. However, frustrated by Dariyahime’s stubbornness, which would not budge even for a lever or a club, he grew desperate and attempted to intimidate her by threatening to report her to the Brahman station. Nevertheless, for a corrupt monk like Kyūbā, who was also a meticulously crafted ugly man, his appearance was such that even a stray dog in an alley would not jump at him even if provoked. Much less was there any reason for a peerless beauty to cast amorous glances at him. Humiliated by the three beauties who had gathered together, his resentment pierced to the marrow. '...Now, it is a matter of breaking the pot. I shall Sutsupanu the Sujiyō of Yoriko, report her to the Brahman station, and I will not rest until I have cleared this grudge...' Saying this, he glared at the signboard of the consultation office, spat upon it, kicked sand with his hind legs, and revealed a countenance like a Yaksha. Intending to meet Ilk, the brother of Dariyahime, to achieve his purpose, he left the place, leaving behind a nasty parting remark without a single word of greeting.

Kyūbā immediately slipped through the front gate of the medicine shop, blocked the entrance, and shouted in a booming voice: 'I am the Lord Kyūbā, founder of the Sukobutsuen sect, who has been favored by the guidance of the God of Daikokunushi, who governs the Moon Kingdom of over seven thousand nations. I wish to have an audience with the master without fail.' At the voice calling out, Aru, the head clerk, came out politely to welcome him.

Aru: 'Ya, who could it be? Why, you are the cleaning attendant of the mansion, are you not? My, you startled me. Since you were blowing such a great horn about being Daikokunushi or the founder of the Sukobutsuen sect, I thought some great noble gentleman had arrived. What is this nonsense? Please stop your joking at a reasonable point. Come, go back to the mansion quickly, go back.'

Kyūbā: 'Silence, clerk! I am the great savior known as Kyūbā, the right arm of Daikokunushi, the Tennenbō. I had entered as a cleaning attendant to spy on the state of the Suga mansion. Just you wait and see. I will tie up your master, the old hag, and you as well, and throw you all into a dark, dark prison. Lead me to a splendid parlor immediately; I have things to tell the old hag and the master.'

Aru: 'I do not know if it is a lie or Honma, but I shall convey this matter to the Shiyujin, so please wait.'

Kiyū: 'If you dawdle, I will not Shiyōchi, so go to the inner room quickly.'

He scolds him. While clicking his tongue, Aru mutters a Kogoto:

Aru: 'Che, that Maisubauzu.'

He runs into the Shiyujin's Ima. After a Shibaraku, Iruku enters briskly:

Iruku: 'Ya, if it is not the Saujiban Kiyūbā, what is your business? I am busy, so long-winded talk is a Mendau, tell me quickly.'

Kiyū: 'Listen here Iruku, is it not a Shitsurei to stand in the way and speak to me, the favorite Chiyōshin of Lord Ōkuronushi, Kiyūbā-kun? Stay back.'

Iru: 'What are you talking about? I do not understand at all. Oi Aru, go to the Yokochiyō Seishinbiyōin and ask the Inchiyō-sama to come.'

Kiyū: 'Do not speak such Baka. If you dawdle, this Tauke will meet the Ukime of Danzetsu. Until now, by the Mei of Lord Ōkuronushi, I have been sneaking in to investigate the Uchimaku of the Ananai-kyō. The more I investigate, the more suspicious things they are doing. They do not pay the slightest Keii toward Lord Ōkuronushi. If I were to Gonjiyō these Shidai in detail, it would be a Taihen.'

I: 'Hai, there may be all sorts of bad things, but faith is naturally free. Even if it is Kiyūbā-sama or Lord Ōkuronushi, as long as there is nothing bad, I do not fear them even a little, so please do not concern yourself with us.'

Kiyū: 'Very well, you said do not concern myself, do not cry later.'

Saying this, with a rough gait, he smashes the surroundings with his Kongō-zue, swings his arms wide, passes through the front gate, and hides his figure somewhere.
