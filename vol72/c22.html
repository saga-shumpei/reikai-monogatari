Chapter 22: Phantom 魅帰 [1831]

妖魅帰〔1831〕

§ 1

The rumor that the three beauties, who looked like Tenniyo, including the Kamutsukasa Tamakiyowake of the Suga Shrine, had been banished as a result of a Mondafu with Chikusa no Takahime, spread suddenly like lightning throughout the town of Suga and even to the neighboring villages and districts. Consequently, the Kamuyakata of Suga became exceedingly prosperous, and Sankeishiya gathered until there was not even enough space to stand. The signboard for the Shiukyōmondafushiyo remained posted just as it had been before, and the only difference was that the name Yorikohime had been rewritten to Chikusa no Takahime. Because it was rumored among the Machibito that Chikusa no Takahime was someone who could talk down even Yorikohime, who had been reputed to possess the Santoku of Chijinyū, no one dared to engage in a Mondafu, even though the signboard was there. Eugenbau, as usual, was sleeping during the day in a room in the Hanare with the door locked tight.

Kōro and Koburai, the two, arrived as scouts from early in the morning, blocked the entrance, and called out, 'Is anyone there? Is anyone there?' As Chikusa no Takahime appeared with a composed air, she said,

'You who call out at the entrance, I wish to hear by whose command you do so.'

Kōro: 'I have come to worship at the Suga Shrine, and seeing the signboard, I intend to engage in a Mondafu.'

Koburai: 'I, too, have come to visit you, for my anger was stirred upon seeing the signboard of the Shiukyōmondafushiyo.'

Takahime: 'How amusing. To wake me from my sleep, I shall strike you two down with my Kototama.'

Kōro: 'Do not speak so arrogantly; there is a precedent for black clouds covering the bright moon.'

Koburai: 'No matter how wise you may be, how can a woman hope to win against a stout man?'

Takahime: 'You foolish woman wearing the robes of a man, what does it matter? Come at me at once.'

Kōro: 'Wait a little while longer; a strange event that will dazzle you is about to break out.'

Koburai: 'Spit out whatever you have to say for a little while longer, if your own end is near.'

Takahime: 'How ridiculous it is for a withered leaf, without a shadow to see, to stand at the entrance and spit out nonsense.'

Kōro: 'Wait a moment, Takahime; I shall make you look like you have no shadow to see.'

Koburai: 'Even if you speak arrogantly, you cannot see what lies an inch ahead; how pitiful is the blindness of Magatsu.'

Takahime: 'Do not open your mouth with such ill-omened words upon invading the Kamuyakata early in the morning.'

Kōro: 'How pitiful is the fool who does not know the pain of others while pinching his own tail.'

Koburai: 'The Magatsu body, whose body and soul are completely numbed, must be unable to endure even the touch of a blade.'

Takahime: 'Instead of spitting out such senseless things, you should at least clean the latrine.'

Kōro: 'I shall begin the great cleaning to sweep away the dust of Mount Suga while the sun is still up.'

Koburai: 'The gods will finally appear in the open, and I shall reveal the tail of the tanuki.'

Takahime: 'What are you saying? Do you not fear the true Lord God, you soul of a fox or raccoon?'

Kōro: 'Whether you are an adulterer or a true god, I do not know, but there is a foul smell of excrement somewhere.'

Koburai: 'It must smell, for are you not called Chikusa no Hime? You, Hanataka-hime, prepare to have your nose broken.'

Takahime: 'I am one who has descended from Takamagahara; it is honorable that I am called Takahime.'

Kōro: 'What are you spitting out? How amusing it is to see you striking your cheeks and acting arrogant without knowing the reason.'

Koburai: 'Perhaps this woman is a foolish girl who is infatuated with the specter of Eugenbau.'

Takahime: 'How noisy you are, babbling at the entrance. Do you not know shame, you withered-leaf warriors? You who do not understand the meaning of the Kamuyakata have come to try and defile my soul.'

Saying this, she turned on her heel and walked into the back.

Kōro: 'Hey, woman, are you afraid of me and running away? I will follow you wherever you go.'

Koburai: 'How interesting. She has finally tucked her tail and run; she must be trembling in a room in the back.'

The two, with deep persistence, stepped up into the entrance and entered the Mondafu room, only to find Takahime waiting there with a look of bewilderment. Upon seeing the figures of the two, she said,

'You foolish men who know no etiquette, to enter the back without even obtaining permission.'

Kōro: 'To be in a sacred place so brazenly without knowing the path of the gods of heaven and earth. I am shocked, truly shocked, Chikusa-hime; seeing you is vastly different from what I had heard.'

Takahime: 'You may blow your own hot air as you please; I shall not engage with those who do not understand.'

Kooroo: 'Do you think you can escape by saying such sweet things, Chigusahime? I shall not cease my investigation until the very end.'

Koo: 'How reliable it is that you shall reveal the true Shiyōtai of the Kinmaukiubi in the midst of this day.'

Takahime: 'You wretches, return quickly from the Kamuyakata; if you defile it, you shall receive the Meibatsu of Kami.'

Kooroo: 'Do you think you can threaten me with such sweet words, Chigusahime? The Senchi is dancing, and my backside is astonished.'

Koo: 'Though you are a Kusobaba, you are a Kinmaukiubi wretch, disguised with Shiroi plastered all over.'

Takahime: 'You Kisama have come to defile the mansion, have you not? There is a foul scent that I cannot even describe.'

Koo: 'That is obvious; I have begun this Daujiyōyabu, and I shall not cease the battle until you show your Shitsupo.'

Takahime: 'What a troublesome fellow has come here; shall I set up a Senkau for you, you Aodaishiyō?'

Kooroo: 'Just as a snake eyes a frog, I shall not return until I have swallowed you whole.'

Koo: 'It is amusing to see Takahime trembling with a face like a Yamabato that has been struck by a Mamedetsupau.'

Takahime: 'You may spout your Akukōzafugon as you please, not knowing that this is a land where the Kototama flourishes.'

Kooroo: 'It is precisely because I know it is a land where the Kototama flourishes that I shall shatter your evil Kototama.'

Koo: 'While striking out the Kototama, I shall pierce the ugly Kimodama of Takahime and see.'

Takahime: 'You make me laugh; with arms as thin as Senkau, do you think you can strike or not?'

Kooroo: 'I have no Yōshiya; I shall turn into the smoke of Senkau and smoke you out.'

Koo: 'Chigusahime, you tremble with a smoky face; you are being moxibusted and sweating, trembling all over.'

Takahime: 'A fellow who looks like a Goma-no-hae has come, breathing hard as if to demand a Sakate.'

Kooroo: 'Thou art the very Kusemono who used a Sakate to usurp the Seijiyō.'

Koo: 'The two men shall not move until the point where you turn upside down and apologize.'

Takahime: 'I have grown weary of arguing with such incomprehensible objects as these.'

Kooroo: 'Without letting you say yes or no, I have crowded into the mansion; do you think I shall return without taking your rough Kimodama?'

Koboo: 'That is right, Kooroo, just as you say; I shall extract your fat and teach you a lesson.'

Takahime: 'An Aburamushi has crawled here early in the morning, attempting to extinguish the Tōmyō of Kami.'

Kooroo: 'It is you who are the wretch extinguishing the Tōmyō of Kami, you ugly Kami-tsukasa with a dark heart.'

Koo: 'Shall we leave now without engaging with such an incomprehensible Yōbaba?'

Takahime: 'Look, the wretch has finally given up, frightened by the authority of Takahime-sama.'

Kooroo: 'Just as I thought of returning, you started your nagging again, so I shall fight one more battle.'

Koo: 'You are like a man trying to hold down a catfish with a gourd; there will be no end no matter how long we stay.'

Takahime: 'The wretch has slowly begun to show weakness; the bottom of your bag of wisdom is now visible.'

Kooroo: 'What are you spouting? I have plenty of wisdom, but you lack the power to receive it.'

Koo: 'According to the appropriate logic, there is no other way for a fool than to speak like a fool.'

Takahime: 'There is a limit even to being a sore loser; even Gaki and Chishō would be stunned and flee.'

Thus, while they were engaged in such trivial exchanges, a large crowd of old and young, men and women, wearing twisted headbands and singing songs, came climbing up, pulling a float under the pretext of offering it to the Kami-mae. Takahime, seeing this scene, twitched her nose and was looking out at the front with an air of supreme triumph, when Yorikohime, Tamakiyowake, Hanaka, Dariya, Aru, Esu, and Iruku, whom she had kicked out the day before yesterday, along with the rest of the Sango-kyō missionary group, dressed in magnificent clothing and wearing yellow twisted headbands, stopped their float at the wide garden of the questioning place and came up to the entrance one after another.

Yorikohime: 'This is indeed Takahime-sama of Chigusa; the day before yesterday, you bestowed upon me a splendid lesson that I shall never forget for the rest of my life, and like a lotus flower opening, I have been made to realize the logic of heaven and earth. Though I am a person of defiled body, I have come with this float fully loaded with offerings to express my gratitude. Hanaka and Dariya also asked me to convey their best regards to you.'

Takahime said with arrogance, 'Well, well, repentance is more splendid than anything else. You have gone to such lengths to build this Seijiyō only to be cast out, and you must be very disappointed, but once a body has been wounded, it cannot serve the Lord God who is supreme and pure; I feel sorry for you, but this must also be the Karma of your previous life.'

Yotahiko: 'I thank you for your repeated Gokyōkun. I ask you a rather strange question, but since you have become the Kamutsukasa of this Seichi, there should not be even a single speck of cloud upon your person, should there?'

Takahime: 'There is no need to ask such a thing. If there were even an Akuji or Ketsuten in the person of this Takahime, even as small as a rabbit's hair, I could not be serving in this Seichi in Ankan. That is the rule of Tenchi, you see.'

Yotahiko: 'I beg your pardon for saying this, but human beings are creatures who commit sins without knowing it. If I were to discover a Ketsuten in you, would you leave this Seijiyō?'

Takahime: 'There are no two words in the Kotoba of Kami. If there is indeed a Ketsuten in my Sujiyō, please investigate it. I shall leave this Seijiyō at any time.'

Yotahiko: 'Hearing those words, I feel as if I have gained a million allies. Ho-ho-ho-ho, Hanaka, Dariya-sama, Tamakiyowake-sama, Aru-sama, Esu-sama, Iruku-sama, you must all serve in this mansion once again, O-ho-ho-ho-ho.'

Thus she shows an attitude of utter fearlessness.

Terukuniwake approaches Takahime with a brisk step.

Terukuniwake: 'Oh, Takahime-sama, I have not seen you for a while. I am a missionary of Terukuniwake.'

Takahime: 'What? A missionary of Terukuniwake? Oh, you are Umehiko-sama, the incompetent missionary who deserted from the Ural religion, are you not? My, my, my, you have certainly risen in the world. Even a rotten rope becomes useful after three years, and even a beggar's child becomes three years old after three years. It must be thirteen or fourteen years since I last parted from you. Well, that is fine, fine. From now on, repent and serve with sincerity. It is not as if I, Takahime, would not use you as a disciple.'

Umako: 'Oh, Chigusa no Takahime-sama, I am the one you met at Toruman Castle.'

Takahime: 'Yes, indeed, you were the person called Umakowake, were you not? You look as beautiful as ever. Please, do not go anywhere else and serve as an official of this Kami-mansion.'

Umako: 'Yes, I am grateful for your thoughts. I humbly ask for your kind favor. Where is your husband, Mokusuke-sama, whom I met on the lake? I would like to see him for a moment.'

Takahime: 'Yes, Mokusuke-sama is a little tired and is sleeping in the annex of the detached building.'

Umako: 'Actually, I have come to celebrate your success and have brought gifts. These many boxes are souvenirs for Mokusuke-sama, and this basket is a gift for Takahime-sama, so please accept them.'

Umako: 'Oh, not at all. Now, Iruku-sama, Tamakiyowake-sama, please have everyone help carry all these boxes to the front of Mokusuke-sama's annex. And when I give the signal, you must all open the lids at once.'

The villagers: 'Yes, we understand.'

With that, more than ten young men from the village, under the supervision of Iruku, carried them away to the annex. Umakowake placed the basket in front of Takahime.

Umako: 'Now, Takahime-sama, I shall open this basket for you to see. It may have a very significant meaning for you.'

Saying this, he laughs with a meaningful look and suddenly removes the lid, and Fukue and Kishiko, both of whom had been stripped naked on the island of Taima, robbed, and thrown into an ant's nest, stood straight up wearing white burial robes. Takahime was so startled she almost collapsed, but as expected of a cunning person, she regained her composure, steeled her nerves, and said:

Takahime: 'Oh, I wondered what it was, but a white heron is the best. For me, this is a gift beyond compare. I shall have it prepared as a side dish for tonight's sake.'

Umako becomes stern.

Umako: 'Now, Takahime-dono, do not play the fool. These women are the ones you robbed after calling yourself Ginkohime when you offered prayers to the ginkgo tree on the island of Taima, are they not? Not only that, but you also used schemes to drive them into the Ant Forest to be eaten alive, did you not? Furthermore, you even deceived me, this Umako, and tried to have me killed by ants, did you not? Can you still say that you have no Ketsuten in your person? Now, I shall have your answer.'

Takahime has no Gonku to reply, and suddenly her Ganshiyoku turns Matsusao, and even her lips are trembling.

Yotahiko: 'Moshi Takahime-sama, you also have indeed committed Ohihagigautau, plotted Bōsatsu, and done things that are Zuibun not good, have you not? Come, Ikaga is it? Even with this, do you still claim to be a Kuwanzenmuketsu Mitama?'

When Umekō blows a whistle as an Ahizu, dozens of Mauken, from where they came it is unknown, appear and emerge, and with the sound of dogs like the Hiyakurai roaring all at Ichiji, 'Watsuu, Watsuu, Watsuu, Watsuu,' Eugenbau no Mokusuke cannot endure it, reveals his Shiyōtai, and vanishes into the clouds and mist, Izuko it is unknown. Takahime, finding her Shintai at a standstill, no sooner does she cast off her Hakui than she instantly reverts to a Kinmaukiubi Hakumen evil fox, calls the clouds, raises the rain, and aiming toward the direction of Ōtakayama, she gallops through the mid-air like lightning and vanishes from sight. 高姫は答ふる言句もなく忽ち顔色蒼白となり、唇迄もふるはせて居る。

ヨ『モシ高姫さま、貴方も矢張追剥強盗をなし、謀殺を企らみ、随分善からぬ事をなさいましたね、サア如何です、之でも貴女は完全無欠の身霊と仰有いますか』

梅公は合図の口笛を吹けば、如何はしけむ数十頭の猛犬現はれ出で、ワツウ　ワツウ　ワツウ　ワツウと百雷の一時に轟く如き犬の声、妖幻坊の杢助は耐り兼ね、正体を現はし、何処ともなく雲を霞と消え去つて仕舞つた。高姫は進退これ谷まり、白衣をパツと脱ぐや否や、忽ち金毛九尾白面の悪狐と還元し、雲を呼び雨を起し、大高山の方を目がけ電の如く中空を駆り姿を消して仕舞つた。あゝ惟神霊幸倍坐世。

因に云ふ。玉清別は元の如くスガの宮の神司を勤め、ダリヤ姫は大道場の司となり、アル、エスの両人を掃除番となし置き、ヨリコ姫、花香姫は、照国別一行と共に聖地を去つて宣伝の旅に赴く事となりける。
