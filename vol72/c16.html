Chapter 16: 東西奔走 [1825]

東西奔走〔1825〕

§ 1

Eugenbau opened the door of the Betsukuwan, and appeared while making the floor resound with heavy thuds.

Eugenbau: 'Hey, Takahime, that was a Gokurau, but it seems the Shinjiya have all gone home by now.'

Takahime: 'Yes, they have all returned. From now on, it is a Butai for just you and me. Shall I warm some sake for you?'

Eugenbau: 'Hmm, I would not mind if you poured me an Itsupai, but somehow there is a strange scent, is there not? It smells of a man from nowhere, and I cannot help but be bothered by it.'

Takahime looked as if she knew nothing and said,

Takahime: 'Yes, that must be so. There is an Itsupiki of a wild boar slaughtered here. Please take a look; I threw it under the floor.'

Eugenbau: 'What is this? Is this not a Ningen? You have done a terrible thing, have you not?'

Takahime: 'It is a Ningen-boar (a corpse). This fellow, you see, ever since I was at Toruman Castle, has been boasting about being the Kyōshiyu of the Sukobutsuen sect, leaning on Ōkuronushi, and on the other hand, carrying Ōdaruwake on his shoulders. Since there was simply no other Shikata, I took advantage of my Bibau to trick this fellow and saved Toruman Castle from its Kikifu.'

Eugenbau: 'Naruhodo. However, to speak of the Kyōso of the Sukobutsuen sect is to speak of the right arm of Lord Ōkuronushi. He is a sworn brother of the Great Serpent... Hmm, he is a splendid propagandist with the power of a serpent. How about this, Takahime? Let us revive this priest by bringing him back to life, and you treat him kindly with your charms. By using him as a bridge, we shall worm our way into Ōkuronushi’s favor and seize the political power of Toruman. If we do that, whether we crush Suga no Miya or do whatever else, it is our own business, is it not?'

Takahime: 'As expected of Mokusuke-sama, you have noticed a good point. I do not know how much wisdom you possess. In that case, is it alright to save this Kyūbā?'

Eugenbau: 'Ah, it is fine, it is fine. However, while it is fine to trick him with your charms, you must not let him get the upper hand. I get a little jealous, you know.'

Takahime: 'Please do not worry about such things. Hmph, it is insulting to think of me as such a cheap Takahime.'

Eugenbau: 'It might be difficult to talk if I am here, so I will go into another room to rest. You handle him well with your own power.'

Takahime: 'You will never get angry no matter how sweet things I say to him, will you?'

Eugenbau: 'As long as it is only lip service, you may say anything. In short, you just need to manipulate him sweetly and use him as a substitute for a servant.'

Saying this, he hid his figure in the Betsukuwan. Takahime pulled Kyūbā up from under the floor, brought him back to life, and recited the song of the numbers of heaven. With a grunt, he regained his breath, and while looking around the surroundings, he said,

Kyūbā: 'Hey, are you not Chigusa? Is it not terrible to strangle a person and make them faint?'

Takahime: 'That is only natural. Please try to think about it. You came out to negotiate while that hateful, hateful old man who is jealous was sleeping in the back. I thought it would be bad for the neighborhood if you fought, so I just put my hand on your throat for a moment. I do not remember doing anything as exaggerated as stopping your breath or killing you.'

Kyūbā: 'Are you truly tired of your current husband?'

Takahime: 'That is certainly so. If I liked him, why would I have deceived your eyes and revived you after you had fainted? My current husband is terrible when he gets angry, but he is also quick to get back into a good mood; he is a very refreshing person. So, even now, when I consulted my husband, he said that I need not worry, and that I should be kind to Kyūbā-sama. How open-minded is my current man?'

Kyūbā: 'I do not know which one is open-minded, or if I am being toyed with. However, seeing that you revived me after I had fainted, I can trust you a little. In that case, although I feel sorry for your current husband, is it alright if I ask for your affection from time to time? I am counting on you then.'

§ 2

Takahime: 'That is indeed so; since you are saying it with your own mouth, it is your own gojiiu. That aside, I am thinking of storming the Suga Shrine tomorrow to engage in a once-in-a-lifetime mondafu with Yorikohime, but I cannot help but feel a little uneasy about it; I would like to practice during this evening, you see.'

Kyūbā: 'Well, you are quite the yūbenka yourself, but that woman called Yoriko is also a kitai yūbenka. When she begins to unleash her kenga eloquence in a tautau manner, any yūbenka will fold their flags, sheathe their spears, and flee. It would be well to practice while doing some nighttime senden; why not go out to Honmachi and try it? I shall follow you, keeping out of sight.'

Upon hearing this, Takahime was kōdori, smoothed up her hair, applied usugeshiyō to her face, wrapped herself in a hade hitohe, donned her ebichiyakama, put her geta on her feet, and holding her shinkuwanafugi in her hand, she stepped out of the gate.

Moving with the grace of a taifu on a dauchiu, swaying her shoulders and hips, she assumed a haughty posture and arrived at the crowded jifujigai of Honmachi.

Bathed in the light of the moon, with Kyūbā following behind, she appeared with a dignified air, stood by the side of the road, and straining her voice as if ringing a bell, she spoke:

Takahime: 'Listen here, everyone, the great leader of the Uranai religion, Chigusahime, is about to give a speech.'

Takahime: 'Please listen to me for a moment. I was originally a person who served as the queen of the Toruman country. For the sake of saving all sentient beings, I pushed aside the clouds and descended from heaven, coming and going through the streets of the city, to proclaim to the people of the world the divine virtue of the true Parent who created heaven and earth, the infinite, absolute, beginningless, and endless, and the origin of His deep grace.'

Takahime: 'To save you from the sufferings of the eight-fold hell and to guide you to the paradise of Takamagahara, where the Lord God resides eternally in peace, and to bestow upon you the joy that remains unchanged and unmoved forever—this is the purpose of this journey. You must not think ill of me or doubt me, nor must you make light of the Lord God.'

Takahime: 'As I am a queen, I have no lack or inconvenience in this world. My heart of great mercy and compassion cannot bear to see the suffering of the people of the world, and so, despite my delicate body as a woman, without regard for cold or heat, I am performing difficult austerities for the sake of the world and for the sake of the way of the Lord God.'

Takahime: 'You may have heard that at the Suga mountain, where a shrine was recently built, a training ground for the Sango religion has been established. The priest who guards it, a person called Tamakewake, I know not what kind of horse-bone he may be, but compared to Chigusahime, he still lacks sufficient hardship.'

Takahime: 'Without hardship, the fragrant flower of truth will not bloom. Not only that, but the woman who manages the great training hall built beside the Suga shrine is Yorikohime. A woman called Dariya, who is like the scent of a flower, has shamelessly hung up a sign for a mondafu hall and is deceiving the people of the world.'

Takahime: 'In the first place, human beings are creatures who cannot see even an inch ahead. How could there be any reason for them to understand the profound truth of religion? The living soul that descended from heaven, the fleshly palace where the Hinodewake resides eternally, is something that only Takahime can understand.'

Takahime: 'From now on, everyone, just watch. Tomorrow I will storm the shrine, take on Yorikohime as my opponent, and begin a religious mondafu.'

Takahime: 'I will make her return to the path of truth, and I will show you a magnificent victory. No matter how grandly she speaks, she is just a creature who holed up in the mountains of Ōra, was called elder sister by the subordinates of thieves, and acted haughty; how could she possibly explain the path of the true Lord God completely and in detail?'

Takahime: 'Everyone, I tell you now. No matter how busy your work may be, set aside tomorrow for the whole day, and after listening carefully to the mondafu between this one and Yoriko, to see which is better, which is false or true, please make a good judgment. I am giving you notice from now.'

Takahime: 'Ah, Kannagara-kamu-nagara. The Lord God will appear in the open and distinguish between good and evil.'

Takahime: 'Yorikohime must also be facing her final moments tomorrow. When I think of it, I feel sorry for her; as an individual, I cannot bear it, but for the sake of the Way, for the sake of the people, for the sake of the Lord God, and for the sake of the country, this is my resolve that I must go through with. Everyone, please understand.'

Takahime: 'I do not wish to engage in arguments by choice, but this sense of chivalry that will not allow me to leave the weak unaided and the strong unchecked—how could I remain silent?'

Takahime: 'If my argument wins, I will drive out Yorikohime, and Chigusahime will take her place, become the priest of the Lord God, propagate the true religion, and change the Suga shrine to be dedicated to the Hegure Shrine.'

Hegure, Hegure, Hegure Mushiya! The Ōkami of Hegure Jinjiya, whose Sanjifusansau is yet unfathomable, is now engaged in the Kuwatsudō of the Gojifurokuoku Shichisenman Miroku, orchestrating the Shigumi to transform this world into a Rakuen of Tokihakakiha, a Tengoku. Rejoice, O Hitobito! There is no falsehood in the Kotoba of Kami; it shall surely be brought to Jiyōjiyu.

Kamunahohi, who created this world, and Ōnahohi, of boundless heart—in the world of men, all things must be viewed and heard through Nahohi, and the transgressions of the world must be rectified. Reverently following the Kyō of Kami, I shall turn and convert Yorikohime, who leads the Hitobito of this world astray and throws it into chaos, along with the Yatsupara who follow her, one and all. Ah, how valiant, how valiant! The auspicious day of tomorrow is awaited.

Kyūbā, emitting a voice like a toad catching a cold from the rear, sang: 'Kami appears in the open to distinguish between good and evil. Kamunahohi, who created this world, and Ōnahohi, of boundless heart—in the world of men, all things are viewed and heard through Nahohi. Cleanse your ears, everyone, for the Onoshihe of the Uranai Kyō! Listen well to the Takusen of the Ikimiya-sama, without missing a single word or half-phrase.'

'The great Miroku of the foundation of the lower rocks, and Chigusahime, who has appeared as the Ikimiya of the Hinodewake, have descended here as the Ōkami of Hegure Jinjiya. With a heart of great mercy and compassion, they have appeared to save the pitiable Hitobito who are afflicted and possessed by the spirits of demons and great serpents.'

'How different this is from Yoriko, who lives stubbornly in the Kamudachi of the Suga no Miya! Such an Ikigami rarely descends again. The time has come; the time is now. Grandfathers and grandmothers, bring your grandchildren, invite your neighbors, and come to hear the important Danpan of tomorrow. It will be a good lesson for you.'

'Not only that, but you shall form a precious bond with Kami, and for ten thousand eons, forever and ever, you shall be immersed in the fountain of blessings, and you shall obtain the right to live in this world just as it is, in Tengoku. Do not doubt it, I pray you.'

'Even I, Kyūbā, the Great Kyōshu of the Sukobutsuen sect, have tucked my tail and follow behind the Ikimiya-sama, serving as an attendant. Seeing this alone, everyone, you must understand that the divine virtue of the Ikimiya-sama is no ordinary thing. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-kamu, may your spirits be blessed.'
