問答所〔1820〕

§ 1

In a corner of the vast Keidai of Suga-no-miya, a building called the Mondafudokoro was newly constructed, where Yorikohime, Hanaka, and Dariyahime, these three, were on duty day and night. They wrote in thick brush strokes on a large Ōkanban at the front, 'A Mondafudokoro for all religious matters,' and beside it, in fine characters, they had written:

'No matter what kind of Sendenshi or Shiugenjiya you may be, I shall accept your challenge. If by any chance I am defeated, I shall resign from my service at Suga-no-miya and yield my position to the person who defeats me. The uncrowned Empress, Yorikohime.'

Yorikohime of extreme boldness / Like a dog that has eaten a boar / Thinking nothing of anyone / That heart of hers is clearly / Revealed on the large Ōkanban. / The Sōsamidare sky is low / The mountain cuckoo cries across / In the twilight where even the young leaves have aged / A Shiugenjiya clad in strange attire / Leaning on a shakujō / Wearing a deep-brimmed amigasa / Standing and blocking the entrance / Of the Mondafudokoro, he asks aloud / 'I seek a challenge, I seek a challenge.' / Hanaka-hime steps out / And bows with great courtesy / 'Seeing you are a Shiugenjiya / Though I know not who you are / The Empress Yoriko awaits you / Surely to engage in a debate / You have come, there is no doubt. / You, the first to arrive / Please do your best. / I shall wait by your side / And listen to your high arguments and excellent theories one by one. / That will be my first / Great training. / Please, come up quickly.' / She draws fresh water into a basin / And takes off his straw sandals and even his gaiters / Washing his feet / And when she hands him the garden clogs, the Shiugenjiya / With an expression contrary to expectations / Smiles broadly and slips his feet / Into the garden clogs, and leisurely / Wanders around the entire Keidai / Tilting his head, wondering what kind of question / He should pose, / Not missing a moment to waste time.

Yorikohime opens the window / And gazes at the strange figure and bizarre appearance / Of the Shiugenjiya who has just arrived / Without thinking, she bursts into laughter / 'Ho-ho-ho-ho' / Falling over and rising again / Peering out, she is truly innocent. / The Shiugenjiya thinks in his heart / 'That bold and fearless woman / While displaying such a large Ōkanban / Is wrapping people in smoke. / I know not what kind of person she is / But the woman who washed my feet / Has a bit of refinement. / A fragrance, from somewhere / Hits my nose with a puff. / No matter what, I must make that woman / My wife. / However, if I should lose / In this evening's debate / I would be put to red-faced shame and lose my standing as a man / And would have to return in dejection / Without having achieved anything. / Entering a mountain of treasures / And returning empty-handed is not clever. / I must devise some plan / And crush that woman called Yorikohime / Into smithereens / And after forcing her to submit, I, Kiūbā, / Will make my prestige shine brilliantly / And after successfully occupying / The sacred site of the Sangokyo / I will replace the headquarters of the Sukobutsuen sect, and that will be fine. / In the land of Toruman, I made a blunder / And was separated from Chikusahime / After that, having lowered my standing as a man / I was made to suffer the beating of green bamboo / Like chasing the God of Wind / The humiliation of being driven away / By Tagosaku, Mokubei, Okame, and the others / I must raise my flag here / And wash away the shame of Aikei / Otherwise, I will not be able to stand / Before the Lord Ōkuninushi / And offer my excuses. / The general Ōashiwake / Must surely be angry too. / If I do not show one feat of merit / The light of the Savior / And the founder will be under dark clouds.' / Thinking such selfish and egoistic / Thoughts of his own / He straightens his collar and fixes his eyes / And returns toward the entrance. / At the comical nature of his style / Yorikohime, from inside the window / Laughing and falling over again / Enters a room and, facing the mirror / Adjusts her hair / And after grooming herself, she wraps her body / In a long, white garment / And steps out to the debate seat.

She sits, dazzling the four directions. / The Shiugenjiya, following the guidance / Of Hanaka-hime, comes out. / The moment he sees Yoriko / His eyes are dazzled and his heart leaps / He loses the freedom of his tongue / And the Kototama he tries to utter stammers / The earthquake in his body / Breaks out at an untimely moment, how pathetic. / 'This must not be,' the Shiugenjiya / Pulls himself together / And with his spirit firmly set / In his lower abdomen, he leans back slightly / Glaring at the Empress Yoriko / He offers a light bow / And opening his awkwardly formed mouth / With a snap, he says, 'I am / The right arm of the great Lord Ōkuninushi / Renowned in the world / As Kiūbā!'

§ 2

The Lord Ōkuronushi is the Seiyū who holds the moon-realm of over seven thousand nations in one hand. You, who dwell within that domain, why do you call yourself an Empress? If it comes to matters and affairs, I shall use the Hafuriki of the Sukobutsuen sect to bind you strictly and send you to the capital of Haruna. I cannot fathom what kind of Keshin of an Akuma you are, but you are indeed a Sagume who deceives this world. You mimic the appearance of Tennin and Tenniyo, and using your beauty as a shield, you extract the hearts of Iujiyōdanshi in this world, pretending to be an Empress, with the secret scheme to Shiyōaku the moon-realm of over seven thousand nations.

The Shiugenjiya who realized this pressed her, saying, 'I wish to hear your Hentafu.' Yorikohime laughed aloud, 'Ho-ho-ho-ho-hotsuho, ho-ho-ho-ho-ho! I know not what kind of Bauzu you are, but I have heard the name Kyūbā. Seeing and hearing are an Ōchiga. To think you have managed to traverse the Sekai with such a face! When I think of this, the Sekai is truly vast. People of the world, following the example of the blind, do not notice your ugly face and vulgar appearance, but instead rejoice and adore you, calling you a Kyōso-sama or a Kyūseishu. That heart of theirs is pitiful. Your sect, called the Sukobutsuen sect, gouges out the breasts of women, hollows out even the fortified zones, and offers them before the Kami. It is a false religion of ignorance and stubbornness. Seeing your face for one moment, I have understood the whole character of your sect. There is no point in debating with a person like you who is not worth taking into account.

Perhaps it is because you wish to tuck your tail between your legs and flee as quickly as possible. If you dawdle, the tails of a wild fox will appear. In the palace of Suga, there are many dogs with keen noses. Ho-ho-ho-ho, ho-ho-ho! It is so funny that my intestines are twisting,' she mocked. Kyūbā, with his chestnut eyes, bristled with anger, squared his shoulders, breathed heavily, and rolled up his sleeves. Clenching his fists, he struck the table with all his might, making the water in the cup dance. He took a sip, caught his breath, and glared up at Yoriko's face nastily from below.

'You are truly a magnificent harlot. I have traveled through many countries and met many women, but I have never seen a shrew like you. If you have that much courage, instead of serving the Kamisama and the like, you should fly off to Mount Ōra, meet the real Yoriko, become her disciple, and work as a cook for the thieves. I am truly astonished and speechless. Is there such a fierce harlot in the moon-realm of over seven thousand nations? Ha-ha-ha-ha-ha!' he laughed bitterly. Yoriko became sharp and retorted, 'Yoriko, who used this chin to command the three thousand thieves of Genshinbō and Sīgō, is this very sister here. Do not be surprised, do not be surprised. I have disbanded the mountain of Ōra, abandoned the path of the Akuma, and in the lake of Haru, where even the water is clear, I have purified my soul in the blowing wind. I am Yoriko-hime, who has instantly transformed into a true intermediary of the Kami at Mount Suga. No matter what kind of wicked person one may be, the soul received from the Kami is a mirror of supreme goodness and beauty. If you polish it, the human soul will shine. Do not despise it too much.'

Upon hearing her reveal her true identity for the first time, Kyūbā was astonished. Stunned, he fell from his chair with a thud, bruised his hips, and cried, 'Ouch, ouch, ouch! Ah, it hurts! Medicine, water, bandages!' He feigned a tantrum, plotting to somehow spend a night in the room where this beauty lived. He was a bold and fearless scoundrel. Yoriko-hime, having probed the very depths of Kyūbā's heart, feigned ignorance and puffed rings of smoke from her cigarette.

'Hey, hey, Hanaka, Dariya-sama. There is a fallen traveler, a useless Bauzu, here. Bring a torn straw mat, wrap him well from head to toe, take him to the side of the privy, and lay him there to sleep. The urine-user of the Sukobutsuen sect, a dung-priest who swindles the world, belongs by the privy. Ho-ho-hotsuho, ho-ho-ho-ho!' Leaving that laughter behind, she fanned her cheek with her fan and entered her own room.

Seeing this attitude, Kyūbā was so enraged that he could not bear it. He tried to rise abruptly and grab her by the collar to punish her, but the hinge of his hip had dislocated. Swallowing his frustration, he rolled his eyes and soaked in an untimely rain of tears. The night deepened quietly, and the sound of the drum announcing midnight could be heard as seven-five-three.

Hanaka and Dariya, the two of them, reluctantly took out bedding, covered Kyūbā with it, and entered their respective bedrooms. Ah, Imikami-kami-kami. The workings of the Kami are indeed interesting.
