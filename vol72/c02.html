Chapter 2: 時化の Lake [1811]

時化の湖〔1811〕

§ 1

Eugenbau's Mokusuke and Kinmaukiubi's Takahime, fearing the Shintoku of Hatsuwakahime and the might of the Mauken Smart, fled the forest of the shrine as if turning clouds into mist. They ran about here and there in the Ōnohara, where the pampas grass grows thick, and with Uro, Mago, Shiga, and Ichiya, they were plagued by Kenkuwa, leaving them with no means to act, as if under a moonless sky.

Hearing that Imoriwake, the Magahikotsukasa, was at the Reijiyō of the far-shining Kogitayama, Eugenbau, while wearing the guise of the Sōmushiyoku of the Iso mansion, and claiming to be the Daikyōso of the Oshihe of Uranai and the Takahimetsukasa, the two held hands and caused the Itsuponbashi to sway.

Crossing the flow of the Kajika, they came to the Imori mansion where Makaze and Koikaze were blowing fiercely. Seeing Bun-suke, who could not see the world of the eyes, sitting at the reception desk while wearing white robes, Takahime was startled and said: 'Hey, hey, are you Bun-suke? This sacred place is the Imoriwake, which transmits the Oshihe of Takahime, the mansion of the Kami-tsukasa. It is presumptuous of you to preach the Oshihe while setting aside the Takahimetsukasa, the ancestor of the Oshihe of Imoriwake. You must understand this well. This personage is the one who serves the Oshihe of the Lord Susanoo, who dwells with the Chigi standing high on the mountain terrace of the Ubusuna, and with the Ōmiya-bashira made thick.'

'I am Mokusuke, the Sōmushiyoku, one of the Sanba-garasu. Greet me quickly, treat me with great courtesy, and revere me as if I were a Kami.' So saying, he blew a great conch, shook his hips, and rushed into the Matsuhime mansion. Though he was teased by Ochiyo and Okiku, he suppressed his anger and, having cleverly won over the minor officials Hatsu and Toku, he exhausted every means to seize the Kami mansion of Kogitayama. Just as he was doing so, the rumbling of the palace on the summit shattered his wild courage, and he fled. As he descended the two hundred steps in a rush, he unexpectedly collided with Bun-suke, lost the Kuruma-no-tama, and together with Takahime, Hatsu, and Toku,

they fled as if turning clouds into mist. Just as they were nearing the forest of mystery, Eugenbau, finding the Kuruma-no-tama he had hidden in his bosom was gone, turned pale and looked thoughtful. He sat down heavily on the grass, and seeing his withered appearance, Takahime grew suspicious and asked what had happened. Eugenbau said that he had inadvertently dropped the Kuruma-no-tama, which was superior to the Nyoi-no-hōju, on the mountain of Kogita. He ordered Hatsu and Toku to take his life and go to the mountain of Kogita to retrieve the Kuruma-no-tama. The two, with aching hips, climbed the Chiga-chiga slope and knocked the Bun-suke-tsukasa unconscious.

Having finally seized the Kuruma-no-tama, they ran back to the shadows of the forest of mystery. Takahime and the other were delighted, snatched the jewel away, and shoved it into their bosoms. As the curtain of darkness descended, the two took this as an opportunity to aim at the heads of Hatsu and Toku, who were lurking in the dark, and threw stones at them. Eugenbau, who could see in the night, and the pair of Kinmaukiubi fled as if turning clouds into mist, and while hiding for a while in a tanuki hole in the Ukiki forest, they applied the Kuruma-no-tama. The castle they built in a single night towered, scraping the heavens. Kinmaukiubi's Takahime,

convinced it was a real castle, praised the Myōjutsu of Mokusuke-tsukasa to the skies. Takamiyahiko became Eugenbau, and she herself became Takamiyahime. While she had the handmaidens of Takako and Miyako serve her, not knowing they were tanuki, and was lost in love, she was broken into by Hatsuwakahime, a missionary of the San-go-kyō. Her sorcery was exposed, and Eugenbau, unable to bear the situation, grabbed the Takahimetsukasa. As their fate was now in the moon country, and they were flying through the sky of the highlands of the Dekatan country, a sudden fierce wind blew. Unable to endure it, he loosened his arms holding Takahime, and performing the acrobatics of aerial gliding,

they fell to the ground, and Takahime's breath ceased. Yet, Takahime, not knowing in her dreams that her physical body had perished, wandered aimlessly here and there on the Yachimata-kaidō of the Yūkai. Searching for the whereabouts of Mokusuke-tsukasa for three years, she built a dilapidated hut at the foot of a rocky mountain where the sun and moon did not shine. She caught the spirits passing by and blew a conch, saying, 'Realize and know the Savior, the living palace of the Hinode-no-kami, the great Miroku of the deep rock roots, who dwells here!' It was amusing to see her making such a fuss. At the dawn of the third year, Chigusa-hime, the queen of the Toruman country, passed away.

§ 2

Making her Nikutai her dwelling, she was reborn into Konoyo once more. Assuming the guise of Chigusa no Takahime, she held even the Kokuō under her thumb, and while she performed every manner of Kiyōtai day and night, she was plotted against by Umekōtsukasa, the Keshin of Kototamawake. With no way to conceal it, she suddenly reverted to Kinmaukiubi, left Toruman Castle behind, and fled like clouds and mist. Just as she was seeking the Yukue of the Eusō Kyūbā, she encountered Eugenbau, who had successfully disguised himself as Mokusuke, in a room at the Hamayariyokuwan in Iriekau. There, the two joined hands and set out on the night road, emerged onto the Hamabe, and entrusted themselves to the Noriahi Takasagomaru, heading toward the port of Suga. This Haranchiyōdefu and boundless, truly Omoshiro Monogatari, I shall now relate in Umara and Tsubara detail. Ah, Kannagara-kamu-kamu, grant us thy divine grace.

The fate of Magatsu in the land of the moon, the Eugenbau known to the world as the severed arm of the Yamata no Orochi coiled upon Mount Ōun. Fearing and trembling at the light of the pure and bright Great Path of the Sangokyō Imperial Gods, he gathered many demons, crushed his heart and scorched his body to destroy the Great Path of God. The terror of Takahime, whose heart was twisted after being cast out by the Sangokyō, and Mokusuke, who had disguised himself to probe her bowels. Even if the body be ground to powder or shattered, the path of Sango, which shall not be left in ruins, is a strong Azusayumi. Setting sail upon the sea of Haru, she met Takahime again, and entrusted herself to the ship that bears the name Takasago, a companion ship of the faith. Like oil poured upon the water, she hums a tune and proceeds toward the port of Suga upon the calm sea.

The Takasagomaru, boarded by Eugenbau and Takahime, was a considerably dilapidated ship. Since its construction, this ship had retained a strange custom known as Takasago-warai. Those who boarded the Takasagomaru were permitted, as an unwritten rule, to criticize, abuse, and mock anyone from the highest political affairs down to the actions of minor officials, including masters, servants, friends, acquaintances, and all other persons, without reservation. As the ship's progress was slow, flowers of various interesting stories bloomed to pass the time.

Kō: 'Hey, Koburai, isn't that peddler-priest called Genshinbō truly a hopeless little devil? He blows great boasts about being a Keshin of the Heavenly Emperor or a savior sent from heaven, but he holed up in Mount Ōra, had his true colors seen through by Umekōtsukasa of the Sangokyō, was driven out after his cave was purged, and then planned to repeat his performance at Mount Ōra by leading three hundred petty thieves. He fell in love with Princess Dariya at the port of Suga, was skillfully shot down by her rejection, and in the end, had the Lord of the Left Guard of Tarahan Castle cut his belly, had his gold plundered, was fed fried pots by us, stayed at the Hamayariyokuwan in Iriekau, drooled over that witch called Chigusa no Takahime, had his eyebrows plucked and his testicles squeezed, and was stripped of all his money. It is said he was almost killed, but isn't that truly a satisfying feeling? The way he panicked when we disguised ourselves as Echigo-jishi and exposed his face was priceless, wasn't it? Truly, just thinking about it settles my stomach. Heh heh heh.'

Koburai: 'Even if you say Genshinbō is bad, it is well known what he is; he is the sort of thing that can be handled as long as you dangle a woman in front of him. He has a bit of ambition, but since he is a fool at heart, such a guy is not worth being surprised by. However, lately, if I hear the stories of the Sangokyō missionaries, it is said that a great demon, a hybrid of a lion and a tiger called Eugenbau of Mount Ōun, is rampaging through the world and tormenting the people. They say he is trying to usurp even the sacred grounds of the Sangokyō, but was sternly warned by an angel called Hatsuwakahime, the Keshin of the Heavenly Being of the First Spirit Kingdom, and is now crossing Lake Haru hand-in-hand with that lewd hag called Takahime. I heard people whispering about it at the pier this morning, saying it was the talk of the living god called Terukuniwake of the Sangokyō. Since we gave Genshinbō such a hard time and turned his guts upside down, if we ever encounter Eugenbau, we will twist off his neck, tear it apart, and treat him like a child crushing a doll, thereby removing the worries of the world and becoming saviors ourselves. I have been rolling my eyes, looking to see if any suspicious characters are on this Takasagomaru, but I see no one who looks like a demon, and I am a bit bewildered by the misdirection. If by any chance one is hiding in the hold, I will whistle, so I am counting on your support. The honor will be split between us, you know. Ahem.'

妖幻坊、高姫の乗り込んだ高砂丸は余程の老朽船であつた。此船には建造以来、高砂笑と云つて一種の妙な習慣が残つて居た。高砂丸に乗り込んだ者は、大は政治の善悪より下は小役人の行動をはじめ、主人や下僕、朋友知己、其外所有人物を捉へて忌憚なく批評し、悪罵し、嘲笑することが不文律として許されて居た。遅々として進まぬ船の脚、退屈まぎれに種々の面白き話の花が咲いて来た。

Kōro: 'Do not blow your horn so pompously, you Uchibenkei who is bold at home but timid abroad. With that face of yours, there is no such thing as Eumitaiji or anything of the sort. The Ten has a mouth, and even walls have ears. Since the fellow called Eugenbau is a Taishiyō of the Mashin, it is said that he can hear our whispers even from Senri away. They say the mouth is the gate of misfortune, so it would be best to keep silent first.'

Kobu: 'Do not talk like a Baka. If I were afraid of Eugenbau, could I live in this world? No matter how strong he is, he is a Shiromono who turns like a Konniyaku when he sees a woman's face, so he is easily understood. There is a proverb that says seeing and hearing are an Ōchiga, so if we meet the Jitsubutsu, he might be an Anguwai insignificant person, ahahaha.'

Eugenbau and Chigusa no Takahime were cowering in the bottom of the boat, turning pale as they listened to the conversation of the Futari. Although they were furious and could not bear it, they were on the lake, and since the Riyōnin were weak on water, they swallowed their tears of frustration and remained there with a Soshi face. Just as the Tōhoku sky, which had been clear, saw a black cloud appear, it instantly expanded to the Tōzainanboku. The Manten became as if ink had been poured over it, making it dark even in the daytime. A Baufū blew in, rain poured down in a Haizen manner, and the Harau raged like mountains. The Takasago-maru, which had already returned to a state of semi-ruin, made strange creaking sounds and suddenly met the disaster of capsizing. All the passengers called for help at the top of their voices, floating and sinking. At that moment, there was a newly built ship advancing vigorously, rowing with eight oars while braving the raging waves and billows. Alas, what will be the fate of all the passengers?
