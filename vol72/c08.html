Chapter 8: 街宣 [1817]

街宣〔1817〕

§ 1

In the port of Suga, famed for its name, lived Arisu, the master of a medicinal herb wholesale house, known far and wide as a millionaire. Abandoning himself to greed and hot-bloodedness, he exhausted every means of avarice and injustice, committing such wicked acts that he would squeeze the very lifeblood from the people. Regardless of distance, near or far, young or old, men and women alike, all cried out in one voice, calling him an ogre, a great serpent, and a demon; there was not a soul who did not revile him. As the proverb goes that gold and dust, when piled up, become filthy, he would not even move a hand or tongue for free, such was his greed. If it meant profit, he would not even hesitate to take the bones of a cow or a dog. Mountains of dust, formed by the gold of people's resentment, piled up high. The sins of the father visited the child.

In the end, the whereabouts of Dariya became unknown, and even he, in his heart, could not forget the deep affection of parent and child. Overcome with agony, he spent his days on his sickbed, a body writhing in groans and sighs. Just as his second son, Iruku, was searching far and near for his sister, he encountered the Kamuzukasa of the Ananai-kyō, Umekōwake, on a ship. Assisted by him, he heard the way of Kami for the first time. Leading his sister, he returned home, eagerly pointing the way, accompanied by the Sendenshi. Arisu, the father who had waited and waited, upon hearing of his daughter's safety, was not merely joyful but danced and raved in a frenzy, his hands dancing and his feet stamping.

Unaware of his white hair, he shook his head, his demeanor a mixture of sorrow and joy. Through the earnest propagation of the way of teachings by Umekōwake, the ogre Arisu also repented. He offered all his wealth to the Ōkami and, at the sacred site where old trees grew thick on Mount Suga, he built a great shrine pillar where the Kami of Heaven and Earth reside. He made this an atonement for the sins he had committed until now. Furthermore, he became determined to save the people of the world who were suffering, lost in the misery of being deceived by the teachings of Akuma. How admirable his resolve was. Just as Umekōwake was finishing his evening meal, intending to take temporary lodging for the night,

Seeing the pattern of a great fire burning high in the sky above Tarahan Castle, scorching the clouds, he left the rest to Yoriko and Hanakahime. While leaving the two in charge, he saddled his chestnut horse. With majestic air, he rode across the great field. The neighing of the horse and the sound of bells, 'Hin-hin, shyan-shyan, dō-dō,' rang out as he galloped through the clouds and mist. Ah, Kannagara-kamunagara. May the spirits be blessed. Yoriko-hime, following the teachings of Kami, and Hanakahime, whose flowers scent with the spirit of Mizu, the two companions, acting upon the instructions of Umekōwake, wrapped themselves in white garments and rode upon their dappled horses, blowing conch shells to gather the people in the streets and corners of Suga.

Opening their gentle, flower-like lips quietly, they raised their hands and stood upright upon their saddles. Yoriko: 'All you young and old, men and women, who dwell in the port of Suga! I, though a woman, am here to convey the teachings of the spirit of Mizu of the great Ōkami, the Savior of the three thousand worlds, the great Kami Susanoo. Please listen with a calm heart, you who are the marrow of the village.'

Yoriko: 'In the first place, this world is where the living Kami, the original ancestor of Heaven and Earth, exists as the one and only pillar. He created the sun, moon, fire, water, wood, metal, and earth, and all things in the universe. Furthermore, He created human beings in the same form as Kami, and He has bestowed the spirits of Gen and Mizu upon each one.'

Yoriko: 'He has made us serve in the governance of Heaven and Earth. The human body is truly a noble thing. Yet, humans do not know this. Once born into this world, they think there is no purpose to life other than to drink, sing, sleep, and wake; if they have money, they drink sake and play with music and dance. How pathetic it is to misunderstand that this is all there is to life. If one says that the duty of life is fulfilled by this, then humans are the same as beasts. How could they be called the primates of all things? Since humans are children of Kami and temples of Kami, created as dwellings for the noble Kami, there must be noble duties beyond just food, clothing, and shelter. If you ask what those noble divine works are, it is for humans to exhaust their utmost sincerity for the sake of the Kami of Heaven and Earth, to polish and open their souls to plan for the perfection of the Heavenly Pure Land, and to serve as Sendenshi, guiding their fellow brothers and sisters living in this world out of the circumstances of the eight-forked hell and leading them to the eternal, unchanging flower garden of Heaven. To serve in a part of that divine work while attending to the mission is the greatest duty as a human. Ah, Kannagara-kamunagara.'

§ 2

May the Mitama be blessed and prosper. The Kami has appeared on the surface, to distinguish between good and evil. The Kamunahohi who created this world, and the Ōnahohi of vast heart. In this human world, whatever may occur, look back and listen back to the Nahohi. Correct the transgressions of the world. This is truly the Mikotoba of the Ananahi Oshihe of Kami. Revere it, O hundred people. Submit yourselves, O Kamunagara. There is no falsehood in the Oshihe of Kami. One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, hundred, thousand. Leading the Tamigusa of ten thousand lands, without leaving even one person behind, to the Ananahi Oshihe of Kami, the Miko of Kami who shall not cease from fulfilling the Taigefu that changes the Tenchi.

Yoriko-hime and Hanakahime. As a part of the atonement for the Tsumitoga committed until now, this Sendenka is for the sake of serving. Listen with a peaceful and calm heart, O Hitobito. I pray for this earnestly.

Shyanko, shyanko, shyanshyan. Shyanko, shyanko, shyanshyan. The horse's hooves are katsukatsu, pulling the Tazuna tight and applying the whip, they head toward the neighboring town. The Sendenshi of the plum blossom fragrance, saved by Umekōwake. Although there is no Shiyōgau yet, the heart that thinks to guide and save the people of the world is like the glowing of red autumn leaves. Following her elder sister Yoriko, they ride through the town of Suga in abundance.

Wearing beautiful Senden-fuku of watae from top to bottom, at the crossroads of the main street, they stop their horses. Standing straight upon the saddle, the style is the living figure of the Holy Kannon, endowed with the thirty-two marks. Unknowingly, the townspeople press their palms together and bow down, rejoicing at the appearance of a living Kami and the descent of a Nyorai. Gathering before the two, so close that not even an ant could crawl between them, they build a mountain of people with great spirit. Hanakahime raises her gentle voice, and lifting her delicate right hand, white as a whitebait, she speaks.

Hanakahime: 'Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. The Kami has appeared on the surface, and preaches the path of sincerity. Whether the morning sun shines or clouds over, whether the moon waxes or wanes, even if the great earth sinks, even if the Magatsu-kami rages, the power of sincerity saves the world. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. May the Mitama be blessed and prosper. We are the Sendenshi of the Ananahi Oshihe, the Kamitsukasa of Umekōwake. Descending like clouds, you have transmitted the most precious gospel to us two sisters. In that joy, our clouded spirits have cleared, and our souls, which had lost their center, have been revived in Takamagahara. Today's journey feels as if we have met the spring where flowers bloom once again. There are no words to express this joy.'

Hanakahime: 'Not making such a precious teaching a thing for one person alone, I distribute it to the many gathered here, and I pray that we may share in that joy and pleasure, and I humbly declare that you should listen with peace and tranquility. While the army of Ōtatewake, famous in the Brahmin army, is pressing upon the castle town of Toruman and attempting to cast the people into the suffering of fire and water, unable to watch it any longer, the Sendenshi of Umekōwake, the lord of my back, whipped his horse and headed for the great plains. In his absence, though I am unworthy, I have borrowed a woman's body, and without minding the rain or wind, for the sake of the people, I have entered the path of Senden. If you wish to hear the details, come to the house of Alice, the pharmacy in the center of Suga. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. May you bestow your divine favor.'

Hai-hai, dōdō, hin-hin, shyanko, shyanko. The neighing of the horses and the sound of bells, very bravely, they walk slowly through the main street, through the town of Suga. Pointing toward the front of the pharmacy of the millionaire, which is known as the center of the town, they return. Ah, Kamunagara, Kamunagara. May the Mitama be blessed and prosper.
