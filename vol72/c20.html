Chapter 20: 九官 Bird [1829]

九官鳥〔1829〕

§ 1

Kyūbā, having been scolded by Mokusuke and mocked by Takahime, had indeed received the house in Kitamachi which he had bought for five hundred yen under false pretenses, but knowing Mokusuke and Takahime, he could not tell when they might go back on their word.

Kyūbā: 'Ah, this is truly miserable. I have been acting as a lantern-bearer for Takahime-sama, walking from town to town, while that fellow Mokusuke occupies a magnificent Kamuyakata without lifting a finger, and now he is flirting and whispering with Chigusahime. I cannot bear this rage. Wait, wait, this is a moment for patience. I shall wait for the right time to kick Mokusuke out, make Takahime completely my own, and become the Kamuzukasa of the Suga Kamuyakata to show off my power.'

So saying, he walked away dejectedly, exposing a face that looked like a poorly made Daruma doll of Imariyaki pottery. When he returned to the Kamuyakata in Kitamachi, the lock was firmly fastened, and no matter how much he picked or twisted it, it would not open in the slightest.

Kyū: 'Ah, that fellow Mokusuke is making a fool of me. Wait, wait, perhaps he left the key with the original landlord next door.'

Muttering this, he stood in front of the Taruya, and said,

Kyū: 'Excuse me, I am Kyūbā, a high-ranking official of the Uranai religious headquarters. I wonder if Mokusuke-sama might have left a key with you? I would like to ask you for a moment.'

The master, Kyūsuke, heard a strange voice like the croaking of a frog, and when he came to the front, he saw a wicked monk standing there.

Kyūsuke: 'Oh, do you have some business with me?'

Kyū: 'There is no particular business, but I intend to live in that Kamuyakata from today as it has become my property. Did Mokusuke-sama not leave a key with you?'

Kyū: 'I certainly have it in my keeping, but... I was told not to hand it over to anyone who comes... Even if you bought it, I cannot easily hand it over to you.'

Kyū: 'Originally, I paid three hundred yen and Mokusuke-sama paid three hundred yen to buy this house, so naturally half of it is mine. However, as you may have heard, the Kamuyakata of the Suga Shrine became Mokusuke-sama's possession as a result of a discussion, and since this Kamuyakata was no longer needed, he asked me to buy it from him, so I threw in the remaining three hundred yen and bought it just now. If you find it suspicious, it is not too far, so please go to the Suga Kamuyakata and verify it.'

Kyū: 'Oh, so you paid half of that money? I see.'

Kyū: 'That is correct. I am a powerful monk who could be called the right-hand man of the Great Lord Ōkuninushi of the Sukobutsuen sect, and I always have money bulging in my pocket. Mokusuke is a traveler who has been training in various provinces, so there is no way he could have money. From what I heard, he boarded the Takasagomaru at Lake Haru, and since Takahime encountered a storm and the ship sank, they both became stark naked and barely managed to reach the port of Suga with their lives, so there is no reason for him to have even a single coin. That three hundred yen is actually suspicious, too. Who knows what he might have done somewhere to get it.'

Kyū: 'Ah, is that so? In that case, there should be no problem, so I will hand over the key.'

He took it out from his bosom and handed it to Kyūbā. Kyūbā, feeling better, squared his shoulders and walked away, striding through the alleys of Kitamachi.

Kyū: 'Ah, at last my nest is built. No, it is not a nest, it is my main castle. From today, I am the lord of Kitamachi Castle, the noble Kyūbā-tennenbō. Now that this has happened, the first thing I need is a wife, or rather, an empress. Beautiful women will surely come to this Kamuyakata sooner or later; I shall scout for one within four or five days, pick the best one, and drag her in to satisfy my immediate desires. In the meantime, I will see what I can do about Chigusahime.'

§ 2

Muttering such things to himself, he pulled the bedding out from the closet, slapped his thigh, and went to sleep.

After a while, someone knocked on the front door, and O-San from next door arrived.

O-San: 'Excuse me, is Kyūbā-sama at home?'

The child of a bushi wakes at the sound of a bridle, the child of a beggar wakes at the sound of a rice bowl, and Kyūbā wakes at the voice of a woman. Rubbing his sleepy face, with a cracked, hoarse voice, Kyū: 'Hi, hi, hi, hi, welcome. What business have you come for? Let me hear the purpose of your visit.' He stands up from his bedding, stands squarely before the hibachi at the entrance, and glares at O-San's face. O-San, while startled, rubs her hands together and, speaking politely in a voice like ringing bells, O-San: 'This is, this is Kyūbā-sama, the master of this house. I am truly sorry to have disturbed your sleep. I have come to inquire on behalf of my master. Since my master will be here soon, please do not go anywhere.' When she said this, Kyūbā shook his bald head vertically and drooled, Kyū: 'My, what a beautiful woman you are. As you know, I have become the master of this place starting today, but I have no woman to cook my rice. It is a pity and a waste to keep a woman like you, with the rough skin peeled away, as a maid at an inn forever. Perhaps Kyūsuke-sama is coming to mediate, asking me to take you as my wife. If you were to be with me, you would be the mistress of this house from today. This house and estate would be shared between you and me. It is a sudden promotion, as if a newt had become a dragon and ascended to heaven. Kyūbā, the savior of the office, is a god of fortune for you. I should not be too hateful, should I?' He blurted this out from his crooked mouth. O-San turned red in the face, O-San: 'Now, now, Kyūbā-sama, that is not the story. Although I do not know the deep details, the money that Mokusuke-sama handed over turned into tree leaves during the night. My master is furious. We cannot just let things be like this. He is consulting about reporting you and the Mokusuke couple to the officials and having you bound. I could not bear to hear it, so I said, "Oh, Master, it is natural for you to be angry, but they say that impatience is a loss. It would be better to report it after first confirming the truth with Kyūbā-sama next door." When I said that, my master said, "Then I leave it to you; go and check whether Kyūbā is there or not." There should be no mistake, but if you use counterfeit money, you will be judged by the rules of the authorities and will have to eat smelly rice. Because I thought that was pitiful, I stopped my master's sharp blade and came here out of kindness.' When she said this, Kyūbā was surprised, Kyū: 'Could there be such a strange thing? Was it not a genuine hundred-yen note, a splendid piece of money so new it could cut your hand? Perhaps a thief broke into your house during the night, snatched all the money, and replaced it with tree leaves. Is there such a ridiculous event in the three thousand worlds? Anyway, bring Kyūsuke here. I, Kyūbā, will explain the principles of heaven and earth and clear up your doubts. Ha, ha, ha, ha, you come saying such meaningless and absurd things.' He blustered and would not take her seriously. O-San had no choice but to return, knelt before her master with both hands on the floor, and explained the details exactly as Kyūbā had said. Kyūsuke, while shaking his head repeatedly, headed toward the Kyūbā mansion. Kyūbā was again slapping his thigh and humming a tune, and just as he was lost in meditation, the front door was pulled open with a clatter, and the master of the barrel shop, Kyūsuke, entered with a fierce look. With a harsh tone, not even saying excuse me, he stood straight in the garden, Kyūsuke: 'Kyūbā-sama, you mountain priest, you are quite the villain. Colluding with the Mokusuke couple, you used magic to make fallen leaves look like money, and you have usurped my house, which is the most important of all. I can no longer tolerate this. No matter what excuses you make, I will never lend an ear. I will report you to the Brahmin office, and whether I am white or you are...'

Mokusuke: 'I will not let this pass unless you clearly distinguish whether your heart is black or not. Be prepared for what is to come. I have already had the Bantō summoned to the Yakushiyo. Before long, you shall suffer the shame of the Nahame, be carried throughout the Chiyōnai in a Tōmarukago, and after being paraded about as a Zainin guilty of Sagi and Ōriyō to become the laughingstock of the Machibito, your Inochi shall become like a Tōkuwa before the wind and vanish. Namuamidabutsu, Amidabutsu. Tonshiyōbodai, Kamunagara. Do not let your Medama pop out.' So saying, while shaking his body, he kicked the threshold and departed. Left behind, Kyūbā folded his arms.

Kyū: 'Because I blew a Hora, claiming money that was not my own as if it were my own, the Tenbatsu has come to requite me. Must I now share in the Tsumitoga of the Mokusuke Fūfu? Truly, when I think of it, it is Kuchio. Even in the Oshihe of the Seijin of old, they say the mouth is the gate of misfortune. From now on, I shall keep this Kokoroe and never tell a lie. Yet, even though I say this, how can I make this proof clear?' Sighing with blue breath, he slammed the front door shut, barricaded himself in the detached mansion, locked it from the inside, opened a Nagamochi, climbed inside, and covered himself with a futon, trembling. The state of Kyūbā is truly pitiful.
