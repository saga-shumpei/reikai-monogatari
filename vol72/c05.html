Chapter 5: 蛸 Ship [1814]

蛸船〔1814〕

§ 1

Seeing the sinking of the Takasagomaru, the Tokihamaru, carrying Terukuniwake and his party, rushed to the scene to save them from the danger, threw out rescue ropes, and pulled everyone aboard their ship without leaving a single soul behind. The passengers, having escaped death by a hair's breadth, thanked and respected Terukuniwake as if he were a Kami, and the voices of joy seemed to well up within the narrow confines of the ship.

The storm, which had been so fierce, stopped abruptly, and the surface of the sea suddenly became calm, creating a scene as if gliding over tatami mats. Among the rescued people were Koburai and Kōro, the minor leaders of the thieves who had once shared affairs with Genshinbau. Seeing the figure of Genshinbau sitting calmly in the ship that had saved them, the two, Koburai and Kōro, lowered their heads in a corner of the ship and began to whisper.

Koburai: 'Hey, Kōro, isn't that man sitting there by the side of the Sendenshi with his mouth pursed like the character for 'he' and looking like a Kami, none other than Genshinbau?'

Kōro: 'I have been thinking the same thing since a while ago, thinking he looks just like him, but since he is clinging to the side of the noble Sendenshi like a louse egg and will not leave, I could not bring myself to call out to him, thinking perhaps it was my own poor eyesight or that I might be mistaking him for someone else; yet, he does look very much like him. Looking at him from the front, from the side, observing him from anywhere, staring at him, or investigating him, does it not seem that he is the genuine Genshinbau? Shall we not devise a way to lure him out here by singing a song?'

Koburai: 'Even if he is an enemy who once caused us hardship, he is a companion who saved my life, so let us forget what has passed. The Sangyōkyō teaches that worrying over the past is forbidden, after all.'

Kōro: 'What, if that fellow is Genshinbau, do you think he would save us for fear of his old crimes being exposed? Surely, we were drinking water and were in a dream-like state, not knowing who saved us, but there is no reason to think he saved us at all. If Genshinbau had seen us, he would surely have pretended to save us only to throw us into the water. In any case, we two are a great nuisance and a thorn in the side to Genshinbau.'

Koburai: 'Indeed, that is true. If it is not him who saved us, then there is nothing to fear. Shall we not sing of his old crimes here and appeal to the Sendenshi-sama?'

Kōro: 'Then I shall say one part, and you sing the next; if we sing alternately, there will be no lingering resentment, and it should be fine. Now, I shall begin.'

So saying, he sat up slightly on his knees, glared at Genshinbau's face, and began to sing.

Koburai: 'This is the sea of Haru, famous in name.' Kōro: 'Yōi-yōi, yoitosa-yoitosa.' Koburai: 'Ships coming and going are many, And the poles and rudders are never left to dry.' Kōro: 'Yōi-yōi, yoitosa-yoitosa.' Koburai: 'Boarding the Takasagomaru, We met with a terrible storm, The ship capsized in an instant, And we were washed into the water, bubbling, Already on the verge of becoming a corpse.' Kōro: 'Yōi-yōi, yoitosa-yoitosa.'

Koburai: 'Hey, the rest is your turn, I am the accompanist.'

Kōro: 'Very well, this is the climax, so accompany me firmly. Heaven does not kill men, A rescue ship appeared, And everyone, without exception, the Tokihamaru, Saved us, how grateful we are.' Koburai: 'Yōi-yōi, yoitosa-yoitosa.'

Kōro: 'Among them, one mystery is, A large man superior to others, With a peerless noble lady, Jumped into the sea and instantly, Appearing in a strange form, Like a tiger or a lion, Pushing aside the rough waves, bubbling, It is interesting that he ran away. In the joy of being saved, let us sing a song of gratitude. The Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, the person called Terukuniwake, without regard for his own danger, rode through the raging, violent waves, and saved the lives of those in peril. Is he an Ikigami, or perhaps a Gokeshin of Kami? My gratitude cannot be expressed in words; I offer my thanks a thousand times over.

Koburai: 'Yoi-yoi, yoitosa yoitosa.'

Kōro: 'Furthermore, there is a strange matter. The Nisekeshin of the Nisetentei, the sole Nisekiushiyu in the world, who hides in the mountains of Aura and deceives the Yobito by performing strange Hoshikuda and bizarre Geitau, that scoundrel Genshinbau is sitting there with a composed face. If such a filthy Mitama-slave crosses the Unabara of Haru, the Riujin will surely become enraged, stir up Arakaze and waves, and capsize the ship. When I think of it, I am terrified. That Genshinbau, the incarnation of Akuma, failed at the mountains of Aura and wandered into the mountains of Tanimama. He breathed threats to force himself upon Dariya-hime of the port of Suga, but after being dealt a sharp blow to the elbow, he continues to stalk her without shame. Like water off a frog's face, the shameless monk went to the castle of Tarahan, threw a tantrum before the officials of the Left Guard, made unreasonable demands, and snatched up a great deal of money.

Chased by pursuers, he plunged into the Fukaya River and even made a trip to the underworld, that rogue. He has returned to this world once more, deceived the two of us, and I hear he was squeezed to death by a woman called Chigusa-hime, upon whom he had set his heart. That piece of filth is sitting calmly on the Tokiwa-maru, wearing an arrogant face.'

Koburai: 'Yoi-yoi, yoitosa yoitosa.'

Kōro: 'The hateful way he acts so composed! Listen, listen, Sendenshi, there is a strange fellow here. You must not let your guard down with that one. If he sees a woman, he does not care if she is a maiden or someone else's wife; he lowers his eyes and drools, a fellow who will not be satisfied until he has his way. As long as he is on this ship, everyone on board, please be careful with your belongings. I myself once joined a group of thieves, but since I have repented, I will never return to that path. Look, look, I am dressed as a Shugensha, wearing a straw cape and hat. While standing at people's gates, I make my living by chanting sutras. We of the repentant group have absolutely nothing to worry about.'

Koburai: 'Yoi-yoi, yoitosa yoitosa.'

Kōro: 'That piece of filth over there, masquerading as an incarnation of the Tentei, is a great thief who deceives people. Once again, please be careful with your belongings. You saved our lives, and in return, I shall expose the past evil deeds of Genshinbau, the Sendenshi, from root to leaf, and humbly submit my complaint. The Kami of heaven and earth are witnesses. What Koburai and Kōro say is never a lie. I will be careful in return. Ah, Kannagara-tamachi-hae-mase.'

Koburai: 'Yoi-yoi, yoitosa yoitosa.'

Unable to bear it any longer, Genshinbau walked up to the two of them with quick steps, drew close, and said in a low, polite tone: 'Well now, Kōro-sama, Koburai-sama, it is a pleasure to see you both in such good health. Oh my, I have been thoroughly roasted before everyone. I would like to ask for your forgiveness at this point.'

Koburai: 'Hmph... you want to be forgiven after just this much... Look, what nonsense are you spouting? You, you remember the terrible time you gave me, don't you? Now, I am going to pull off your head, and though it may be a bit unsightly, I will make it into a netsuke for my tobacco pouch, so prepare yourself for your karma. Tell me, Kōro, if we don't do that, won't the worms in our bellies refuse to be satisfied?'

Kōro: 'Yes, that is true. How could I ever let such a fellow be saved? I became a Shugensha just to track down his whereabouts and settle this grudge, so meeting him today is like seeing the Udumbara flower bloom in spring. Now, Genshinbau, what is your answer?'

He struck his chest three or four times with his clenched fist, and the way he roared was truly comical. It was a style reminiscent of a newt thrown up from the bottom of a well, dancing about in its distress.

Genshinbau: 'Ah, I was truly wrong, and I am sorry. However, seeing this as a matter of fate, I have reconsidered and listened anew; please, forgive my sins. You see, I have fully repented and become a disciple of the Sendenshi of Ananai-kyō, so even if you touch me with a single finger, it would be an act of disrespect toward the Sendenshi-sama.'

Ko: 'Hmph, you are nothing but a stray fox borrowing the tiger's authority. Even if you try to slip away, that toy wakizashi of yours will not be easy to draw. Now, it is time for some rough cooking. Hey, Kōro, to console ourselves from the boredom in this boat, why don't we cook up an octopus, even if it is a bit old, and show it to everyone?'

Kōro: 'To cook this octopus and offer it to the Riujin-sama to pray for the safety of our voyage is also a form of Shinjin. It would be troublesome if another Shike were to come, wouldn't it? Itsuhihihi, how delightful this is.'

Gen: 'Hey, you two, Koburai and Kōro, do you really intend to cook me in earnest? Eh? If that is the case, I have my own resolve as well. Although I have joined Ananai-kyō, which follows the principle of non-resistance, self-defense is permitted; it would take no effort at all for me to finish off one or two petty thieves. Come, if you want to face me, then try it.'

He glared with his acorn-like eyes, stood like a Nio, and stamped his feet on the bottom of the boat. Before Koburai and Kōro could even say, 'What, you impudent, corrupt monk,' one of them grabbed him by the neck, the other snatched his legs, and they began to perform a lively scene of action, tumbling and rolling within the narrow confines of the boat.

Seeing this spectacle, Terukuniwake said:

Strife is the work of the Magatsu-no-kami,

To be tranquil is the heart of the Lord God.

To be hated and to hate in return is the ugly deed

Of the evil spirits; fear and be cautious.

Good and evil deeds cross paths,

And thus this world unfolds.

Upon hearing this song, the two, Kōro and Koburai, let go of their hands at once, and Genshinbau, sniffling his nose, managed to stand up with great difficulty and said:

Grateful am I to the teacher, Terukuniwake-sama,

My life is saved by your living Kototama.

Being struck by you, Koburai and Kōro,

I am happy to think my sins are being erased.

Ko: 'There is no way to defy the stern Kototama of the teacher who saved my life.'

Ko: 'Though I think of you as a hateful fellow, my strength has left me at the voice of the Sendenshi telling me to wait.'

Terukuni: 'The clouds of strife are gradually clearing away, and the mirror of truth shines; how auspicious.'

Terukō: 'Even while encountering such miraculous divine aid, how human hearts still persist in strife.'

Umekō: 'The hearts of ordinary people are vast and uniform, and now there are three figures before my eyes. How they resemble the sky of May, clearing and then clouding over again; such is the human heart.'

Umekōwake asked Terukuniwake for a moment of time, and declaring that there was a person to be saved, he brought the boat alongside the beach town, landed once, and then, borrowing a small boat, rowed away toward the island of Tama floating in the lake.

The Tokiwamaru raised its sails to the favorable wind and proceeded toward the port of Suga.
