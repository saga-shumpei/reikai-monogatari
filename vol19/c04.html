Chapter 4: Goodness か悪か [649]

善か悪か〔649〕

§ 1

In the Mahoraba of the land of Mizuho, surrounded by the Aogakiyama, lies the Shinsakuchi, a fortress of Kengo known as the Shitatsuihane. There, the Yotsuō, who knows naught even in dreams of the Kibiyōsen of the Shōsansai nor the Fūsuikuwa of the Daisansai, appeared at the foot of the mountain. By the Onmiizu of Tamateruhime, whose light shines in Yomo, she appeared as Tanabatahime, clad in Terutahe robes, weaving the loom of Tateyoko, brocade and damask. Attended by Murasakihime, as days and months passed and years were crossed, her name resounded in Yomo.

Yoshikohime, accompanied by Natsuhiko, Tsunehiko, Kamehiko, Taki, and Ita, departed from the dwelling at the foot of the mountain of Yotsuō without revealing her Shimei, setting forth for the sake of Shingefu to parts unknown. Otohiko and Ikohime, having received a different Shimei, likewise departed from the dwelling of Yotsuō, leaving for parts unknown without revealing their destination, in accordance with Kannagara.

Remaining behind, Murasakihime, Wakahiko, Osetsu, Otama, Bakō, and Kakō devoted their full strength day and night to the upbringing of Tamateruhime.

Summer drew to its close ere one knew it; the heavens were high, the winds were clear, the ears of rice in the fields beat like golden waves, and the autumn foliage, woven into brocade by Sahohime, reached the height of the season.

At that very time, in the dead of night, there was a man knocking at the door. Bakō and Kakō were startled by this sound and awoke.

Bakō said, 'Hey, Kakō, is there not a sound of someone knocking at the front door? I hate to trouble you, but would you mind going to investigate?'

'What, that is but the sound of the cold wind of the autumn night knocking at the door as it scatters the maple leaves. You have become quite hypersensitive, have you not? It is no wonder, for we are charged with the duty of protecting the living spirit of the Miroku Shinsei, Tamateruhime-sama; it is natural to pay heed to the sound of rain and the echo of the wind. However, it would be a pity if you were to fall ill from overthinking; we must leave all things to the Kami, and as for us, it is sufficient that we do our best and serve with loyalty.'

'That may be as you say, but the sound just now was by no means the sound of rain or wind; it seems as though there is someone visiting at the gate.'

Kakō replied, 'Is it the storm on the peak or the wind in the pines? Or perhaps, if I am mistaken, the mischief of foxes and badgers, trying to threaten us by knocking on the rain shutters with their tails. It seems that spies from the Uranai-kyō have come time and again of late, attempting to snatch back Tamateruhime-sama; it would be a disaster if we were careless and opened the door in the middle of the night. We would have no excuse to offer Eikohime-sama or Yoshikohime-sama. For now, the safest course is to adopt the principle of the three monkeys: see no evil, hear no evil, speak no evil. With my deer and your horse, we shall guard this place firmly and well.'

§ 2

The sound of knocking upon the door grew ever more violent.

"Is it not knocking with ever greater violence? How would it be to inquire of Murasakihime?"

"That is indeed so; yet, as she is sleeping so soundly, it would be a pity to awaken her in the middle of the night."

The sound of knocking upon the front grew ever more violent. Shikakō, in a voice that seemed sullen, spoke:

"Who is it that knocks so recklessly upon a house where people have fallen asleep?"

"I am one who has come all the way by solitary travel from Chikubushima in Gōshiu, by the Gomeirei of Hidekohime. Is Murasakihime at home? Is Wakahiko present?"

Shikakō replied: "Since you know the names of Murasakihime and Wakahiko, you must be something or other; thinking thus, I cannot open the door easily. We sleep by day and serve as Fushinban by night. During the night, the authority is ours, and no matter who tells us to open, it cannot be opened until the Honshiyugojin of this Shikakō issues the command to open. Well now, it is a trouble for you, but as your Shiyōtai is unknown, it would be best to wait until it is Shizen to open. You will likely reveal your thick fur over your whole body when illuminated by Nitsukuō. As we are Ningen who cannot see during the Yabun, I must humbly decline."

From outside:

"Is that voice not Shikakō? I have not come today for any other matter. By the heart of Kami-Susanoo-no-Ōkami, I have come as a direct messenger, bearing the great command of Hidekohime; I am Kamehiko, a missionary of the Sangyō."

"What, is it Kamehiko-sama? If so, there is no reason not to open, but might this be a feigned voice?"

"What, is there any need to feign a voice? I have details to convey to Murasakihime and all others. It would be best to open without a moment's delay."

"Somehow, Kamehiko-sama, only on this Kefu, is even your tone of speech solemn; regarding this, whether it be good or evil, auspicious or ominous, this is no ordinary messenger, is it, Bakakō? What should we do?"

"It is a grave tone of voice. It appears he has come bearing the proxy authority of the Ōkami, and the resonance of his Kototama is somehow more solemn than usual."

"What, he is merely saying such things to jest. He intends to intimidate us with a tone of voice as if there were some grave business. What, there is no need to worry; it is like the mountain groaning and bringing forth a single mouse. Hahaha."

§ 3

Kamehiko said, 'Open the door quickly; why do you linger?'

Shikakō replied, 'You have arrived with such a haughty and exaggerated manner; this is a matter that we two cannot easily resolve, so perhaps we should consult with the commander Wakahiko.'

Umakō said, 'That would be well.'

Saying this, he entered the living quarters of Wakahiko and shook his shoulders, saying, 'Pardon me, is it Wakahiko-sama or Aohiko-sama? I know not which I should address, but pray, arise for a moment. A formidable personage has appeared at the entrance. Come, come, arise and wake at once.'

'If it be not Umakō,' said Wakahiko. 'Why do you make such a clamor in the middle of the night?'

'Nay, a sudden incident has occurred. By the Mikokoro of Susanōnōokami, a messenger of the Sendenshi of Kamehiko has arrived as an Ojikishi from Hidekohime-sama.'

Wakahiko replied, 'What, a messenger of the Sendenshi of Kamehiko has arrived? How late you have come! We, including Murasakihime and all of us, have been counting the days, stretching our necks in anticipation, wondering when the words of praise from Hidekohime-sama would descend. Rejoice, Umakō, for surely we shall receive a Gohōbi.'

'That is indeed grateful; shall we then open the door?'

'Wait a moment; we must tidy the sleeping quarters and cleanse the surroundings before we invite them to enter, for it would be disrespectful to the Ojikishi to receive them in such disarray. Pray, Murasakihime-sama, Otama-sama, arise quickly; a messenger of the Sendenshi of Kamehiko has just arrived as an envoy of Hidekohime-sama.'

Murasakihime said, 'Ah, is that so? That is a serious matter; we are in a predicament, are we not?'

'We have endured such bitter toil and hardship to welcome Tamateruhime-sama into the Sangōkyō, so there is no reason why we should be rebuked, rather than praised. Do not be anxious; no matter how magnificent a divine personage they may be, a woman is still a woman. You should not worry so needlessly.'

'Even so, I cannot help but feel uneasy. Whatever the case may be, let us quickly tidy the room and have them enter.'

The group cleared away their bedding and finished cleaning the room neatly.

Wakahiko said, 'Now, the preparations are complete. Umakō, Shikakō, it would be well to open the front door and guide Kamehiko-sama inside.'

The two, Umakō and Shikakō, replied that they understood, and they slid open the front door. The two were astonished, for the messenger of the Sendenshi of Kamehiko stood with solemn dignity, wearing a golden crown, with a luminous jewel illuminating the surroundings, clad in a white robe with long, thin silk sleeves. The messenger advanced with composure as if the entrance were too narrow, bowed once to the two, walked straight into the inner room, sat formally before Tamateruhime, performed clapping and two bows, finished reciting the divine words, and sat in a formal posture.

Murasakihime placed her hands upon the floor and spoke: 'Oh, this is indeed the Sendenshi Kamehiko, nay, the Jikishi of Hidekohime. Despite the darkness of night and the great distance, you have come to visit us. I pray you, let us hear the nature of your mission.'

Kamehiko composed his demeanor and replied: 'My arrival at this mansion this very day is for no trivial matter. Regarding the conduct of you, Murasakihime, and Wakahiko, Kamususanōnōokami has expressed extreme displeasure, and having received a Goshinji from Hidekohime, I have come here purposely as the Jikishi of the Mikoto of Hidekohime.'

Murasakihime and Wakahiko started in surprise, placed their hands upon the floor, and said: 'We are deeply grateful for your trouble, Jikishi-sama. We pray you, let us hear the nature of your mission with haste.'

'It is the strict command that, as you have forgotten the laws of Tenchi regarding Tamateruhime of the Shinkaikeirin, and have employed the secret schemes of Kenbōjiyutsusū, and have attained your objective through the strategy of Hankankuniku, such acts do not accord with the divine will. The deity of Tamateruhime must be surrendered for the time being to Kurohime of the Uranai faith. You are to send Tamateruhime-sama and Otama to the hands of Kurohime without a moment's delay, and you are to bear this responsibility and depart from your office of Sendenshi.'

Thus he declared with solemnity.

Murasakihime flushed with shame and said: 'Truly, your words as Jikishi are clear in their reason. It is the result of my own lack of virtue, and now there are no words of excuse left to offer. I accept this with reverence.'

'Wait, wait, Murasakihime-sama. To speak so bluntly, setting aside this Wakahiko, will leave us with no resolution for the future. Even if it were a strategy of Kenbōjiyutsusū, the welcoming of the living spirit of Tamateruhime, the precious treasure of the Miroku Shinsei, into the Sangyō is an outstanding achievement of merit. We should be receiving words of commendation; that we should instead be dismissed from our posts and, furthermore, told to hand over Tamateruhime-sama to Kurohime, does not seem to be the words of Ōkami or Hidekohime. Hey, you, Kamehiko, you must be jealous of our success and are saying such things. Nay, this must be the rambling of your own primary guardian deity; it is surely the mischief of your secondary guardian deity. I, Wakahiko, shall now perform a spiritual injection and reveal the demon possessing you.'

As he quickly clasped his hands and let out a cry to apply a spiritual binding, a goddess appeared suddenly from behind Kamehiko like smoke, her brilliance illuminating the universe, and she cast her gaze upon the face of Wakahiko. Murasakihime and Wakahiko felt their bodies wither, prostrated themselves on the spot, trembled in fear, and their tears came to moisten the tatami mats. Kamehiko softened his expression.

Bakō said, (in a low voice),

"Hey, Shikakō, the night plot of the owl, thou art making a difficult face because thou hast missed thy supper. Dost thou know the divine oracle of the Sangyō which says, 'Even if it be a good deed, if it be not done with sincerity, and if evil be mixed in even to the width of a hair, the matter shall not be accomplished'?"

"Hmm, I think I heard that sometime. What a difficult thing the Kami say. If the Ōkami, who governs the three thousand worlds at will, speaks of such small things in such a fussy manner, the accomplishment of the divine government is uncertain. However, since even Wakahiko-sama and Murasakihime, whom we revere as our master and teacher, are retiring, it is the same for us. If we do not think of something, we shall meet with a foolish fate."

"Excuse me, Wakahiko-sama, Murasakihime-sama, congratulations, I offer my felicitations."

Shikakō hurriedly placed his hand over Bakō's mouth and said,

"Hey, hey, Bakō, what art thou saying? Is that not somewhat rude?"

"Bakō, well hast thou spoken. Truly, there is no greater cause for celebration than this. This very day and hour, the Heavenly Rock Cave of the lower abdomen has opened. From now on, the true sun and moon of suchness shall appear. I wish you congratulations, one and all."

Wakahiko clapped his hands and said,

"Ōkami, I thank Thee. At last, the dark clouds of the heart and heaven have cleared for me as well."

Thus he expressed words of gratitude together with his tears.

Tamateruhime, with a countenance of beauty that defies description, began to smile, her Gokigen no longer askew, and Otama wept with joy.

Bakō and Shikakō looked at each other's faces and said,

"Well, I do not understand. How will this turn out, I wonder?"

What manner of actions will Murasakihime and Wakahiko henceforth truly undertake?
