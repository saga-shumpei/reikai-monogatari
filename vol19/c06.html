Chapter 6: 和合と謝罪 [651]

和合と謝罪〔651〕

§ 1

It is said that when a single leaf falls, one knows the autumn of Tenka. In the land of Fusa, where the teachings of Kami are unsuited, Takahime, the head of the Honzan Uranai-kyō and whose heart is filled with pride, dwells in the darkness of her heart, consumed by Shifuchiyakushin; meanwhile, Hanatakayamahiko and Kurohime remain secluded in an inner Hitoma, whispering in consultation regarding the Zento of the Uranai-kyō.

Takahime said, 'Though it is said that Eikoseisui and Eshiyajiyōri are the common lot of Jinsei, how deeply has the Uranai-kyō fallen, like Momiji in the garden scattered by the wind. Hinodewake, who was once so active and zealous, has vanished from this Honzan like smoke, and many of our Buka and Shinto have scattered to the Shihau; the Uranai-kyō is now a defeated remnant, like a crab stripped of its Teashi, and we must surely devise a path to its restoration.'

Kurohime replied, 'Might it be that even the Hinode-no-Kami-sama is, at this juncture, somewhat troubled?'

Takahime said, 'The Hinode-no-Kami-sama is away, for he is engaged in protection in foreign lands and has gone to investigate even the corners of the world. It is certain that when he returns, he will provide the protection of the sunrise, but if we leave things as they are, this Honzan shall face the misery of a lonely castle at sunset, crumbling like earth and tiles. More importantly, Kurohime-sama, how fares the Ryūgū-no-Otohime-sama these days? She is a Kami of little wit, is she not? It would be well if she would exert herself in activity at such a time as this.'

Kurohime replied, 'As you know from the Ofudesaki, is it not certain that the Ryūgū-no-Otohime-sama is accompanying the Hinode-no-Kami-sama and is protecting together with him in foreign lands? Takahime-sama, ever since the affair of Tamateruhime, you have become quite vacant, have you not?'

Takahime said, 'Are you not the same, Kurohime-sama? It is said that ever since Takayamahiko-sama departed for that place, you have become increasingly absent-minded day by day. You may not perceive your own state, but Torawaka spoke of it so.'

Kurohime replied, 'What are you saying? It troubles me to be so misjudged. Since the time Takayamahiko-sama departed, I have been all the more active. More than that, Takahime-sama, though it may offend you to hear it, ever since Hinodewake-sama hid his form from this Honzan, you have seemed all the more spiritless, like sake left to grow cold for ten days and then drunk—a most wretched state. Shall we not pull ourselves together and act with resolve? Look upon that Sangyō-kyō. Its power is like the rising sun, and it is not even to be compared with the likes of the Uranai-kyō.'

§ 2

Takahime said, 'It is said that the hated child shall prevail in the world, and thus it is an age where evil prospers; as this teaching flourishes in such an age of evil, I understand it well enough. Yet, though the Mitama of evil may prevail in the world to the extent of ninety-nine percent, the Hinode-no-Kami declares that even if the Mitama of good remains fallen, it shall turn completely with the remaining one percent. No matter how many may gather in the Ananai-kyō, they are but a disorderly crowd. The fate of the collapse of the Naikaku from the Uchiura is already beginning to sprout. Though the Uranai-kyō may be few in number, it is a solid organization of faith, possessing the sincere, pure Yamatodamashihi of the one path of good. It is said that ten thousand soldiers are easy to obtain, but one general is difficult to find; therefore, it is well that they are few. If too many Garakuta gather in a clatter, insects will eventually breed, the crystal water will become foul, mosquito larvae will swarm and emit an unbearable stench, and in the end, these larvae will sprout wings, scatter, and eventually settle upon the heads of men to drain their living blood. Do not, I pray you, be anxious. The divine plan is so ordained that once the protection of Hinode is attained, it shall turn completely in an instant.'

Kurohime replied, 'Does that mean it is the divine policy to select only the good, and allow not a single person whose Mitama is clouded to draw near?'

Takahime answered, 'That is Kannagara. Even if one were to forcibly pull them in, if they do not draw near of their own accord, there is naught to be done. Furthermore, no matter how much one may attempt to detain them, one cannot bind them with ropes and hold them fast; thus, those who withdraw are also left to Kannagara, and are permitted to take free action. To refuse none who come and to pursue none who depart is the divine will. Since it is declared, "Do not go to pull them in by force; when the season arrives, God shall draw the sincere ones near and cause them to perform the true service," there is no need for you to be so fretful and anxious.'

Kurohime said, 'Yet, did you not command that Tamateruhime-sama be brought here by force?'

Takahime replied, 'That is a misunderstanding on your part. It is because you attempted to pull her in by force that you let her escape. Because you did not manage it skillfully so that she would come from the other side and say, "Please take charge of Tamateruhime-sama," did not Susanoo-no-Mikoto, who is usually so shrewd, act with such laxity? You are indeed a wise person, but as they say, even the wisest may have a lapse in judgment; in that particular matter, it was your failure, Kurohime-sama.'

Kurohime retorted, 'Even so, if I had remained sluggish, it would have come to pass that eight or nine tenths would have been taken by the Ananai-kyō; thinking that if I engaged in such roundabout ways I would be left with the regret of the six irises and ten chrysanthemums, I merely took extraordinary measures. Victory and defeat are the fortune of the times; it is useless to count the years of a dead child now. Do not you, too, become so complaining and speak of such irredeemable, foolish things.'

§ 3

Takahime arched her brows sharply, stamped her feet with a heavy thud, caused the tatami mats to resound, turned her back with a sudden motion, and concealed herself in the adjoining chamber.

Takayamahiko exclaimed, 'Hearken, Kurohime, what insolence is this that you utter? Though I have ever instructed my subordinates to regard the unreasonable demands of generals and masters as inevitable, why do you persist in offering retort and silencing them at every turn? As the pillar standing between you, I am left with no manner of greeting or apology to offer.'

Kurohime, with eyes flashing in fury, replied,

'Listen, my lord, did you not declare when we crossed the River Yura in times past that you would yield absolute obedience, and did you not say that you would even serve as a bearer of sandals? Do you now assume the countenance of a husband and interfere in every matter spoken by the Ikimiya of the Otohime of Riugū? Remain silent and withdraw; this is no stage for you to thrust your head forward and intervene. Should you persist in such meddling, I shall enforce a Kankōrei upon you.'

Takayamahiko replied, 'Very well, very well; I shall henceforth observe silence before the imperial wrath of the Lady of Riugū.'

Kurohime said, 'No matter how many shells Kurohime may fire, the Takayama battery must maintain silence and never return fire. We are at the critical juncture of the 203-Metre Hill; how could I endure the enemy receiving reinforcements from the sidelines?'

Takayamahiko replied, 'Very well, very well; there is no help for it. I shall submerge my vessel in the depths of the Gulf of Bohai and observe the situation. Yet, seeing the black ship suffering under enemy fire, I cannot possibly maintain a neutral stance. I feel a desire to render some manner of support.'

Kurohime, her mood somewhat improved, said,

'Ah, is that so? That is what is called the manifestation of a sincere heart. You are concerned after all, are you? It seems that when two become husband and wife, they cannot help but be concerned for one another. You do indeed possess the proper qualifications as the husband of Kurohime. Though we were strangers at first, the affection of a married couple is truly something exceptional.'

How droll it was to see her suddenly break into a smile.

Just as it seemed that dark clouds would fill the heavens, a tempest would arise, and thunder would roar, the breath of Takayamahiko’s smooth Kototama caused the black clouds to scatter instantly in all directions, and the weather transformed into a scene where the bright, clear light of the full moon shone, and the stars glittered in the mid-heaven.

Kurohime said, 'Hearken, Takayamahiko-sama, you were a man of kindness beyond what your appearance suggested. Please reveal that kindness and restore the good humor of Takahime. However, even in showing kindness, there are limits.'

Takayamahiko said, 'That is a request of considerable difficulty. I do not grasp the degree of it; pray, grant me a limitation as to how far I must go.'

Kurohime replied, 'Ah, what a boorish man you are. It is a matter of standing in the midst of the inseparable and the non-separate, and contriving an amicable resolution. Sending radio waves is strictly forbidden, you see.'

Takayamahiko answered, 'Who would ever send radio waves to such an old crone? Rest assured.'

Kurohime said, 'You stated that you would not send radio waves to the old crone, but if Takahime is an old crone, then I am yet another old crone; thus, you are a most perilous man. It is for this reason that you used clever words to cast Otami, who had come all this way with such effort, out to Takashiroyama. Now, Lord Takayama, I am not an amateur who would be remiss in such matters. Being the world's most experienced in this path, I am unlike a fledgling such as you; perform your task with firmness. From now on, I shall have you refrain from using the word "old crone." Though the years may advance, my heart is in the flower-bloom of sixteen; it is the mechanism of Reishiyutaijiu, which places weight upon the heart.'

Takayamahiko replied, 'Indeed, old crone or otherwise, even if you were a maiden of old, you are exceptional; there is something about you that is truly fine.'

Kurohime said, 'That is likely so; such is the skill of Kurohime, who can manipulate a man of seven feet as if he were a hand-ball. No matter how much you, Lord Takayama, may stamp your feet in frustration, you shall not even be able to approach my footing. Nevertheless, take great care to ingratiate yourself well with Takahime, the maiden of old, and restore her good humor. Above all, you must not send them.'

Takayamahiko visited the living quarters of Takahime with a look of vexation. Takahime lay there, having pulled her night-garment completely over her head, in a state of abandonment.

Takayamahiko said, 'Pardon me, Takahime, it is Takayama.'

Takahime replied, 'Now, you there, are you perhaps confused? I am not Kurohime. The place you ought to be is in a different direction. Come, come, be off with you at once; it would be a mutual nuisance if Kurohime were to probe a belly that has no pain.'

Takayamahiko said, 'No, no, pray do not worry in the least. I have come having obtained the public authorization of the Mountain Deity. In truth, Kurohime intends to come and offer her apologies, but as she spoke many rude things in a moment of impulse and has no face to show you, she commanded me to go in her stead, to manage your good humor, and to apologize until you are fully satisfied. I beseech you, through Kami-naobi and Ō-naobi, to look again and listen again, and to rectify the impropriety of Kurohime's derailment.'

§ 5

Takahime said, 'Hey, hey, you have become a Shitadori before I knew it. Why do you, being the master, use Keigo toward your Okusama to such an extent?'

Takayamahiko replied, 'Yes, yes, for this, there is an Innen that exists.'

Takahime said, 'Whether there is an Innen or not, I do not know, but when you use such Goteinei Kototama, the Fūfunaka naturally becomes good and it is Medeta. That is why people say that I am Kakaadaimiyōjin and that Takayamahiko-sama is Medeta. Ho, ho, ho, ho.'

Takayamahiko said, 'Anything is Ketsukō. Nanitozo, please restore your Gokigen as well. If you do so, this Medeta man will become even more Medeta, so please Wagafu with me.'

Takahime said, 'What are you saying when you ask me to Wagafu? Wagafu is to bring peace to the conflict between the Taishiyō of one side and the Taishiyō of the other, but no matter what is said, are not Kurohime-sama and I in the Ahidagara of Shitei? Is it not a bit Senetsu to ask me, your Shishiyō, to Wagafu? It is natural to apologize by saying, "Please forgive me for whatever has offended you Imamade," but with such an arrogant and insolent attitude, the worms in Takahime's stomach will not easily and readily consent.'

Takayamahiko said, 'Your opinion is most reasonable; I was confusing the relationship of husband and wife with that of Shitei. This is a word to be used between Kurohime and myself. Takahime's Chachya-sama, nanitozo, please look again, listen again with a magnanimous heart, and forgive Kurohime for her rudeness.'

Takahime said, 'Ah, is that so? If you understand things that way, it is a quarrel without roots from the beginning. Since both are disputes out of thoughts for God and the path, I have not even a little bit of personal resentment. Please have Kurohime-sama come here quickly.'

Takayamahiko said, 'I have understood. Kurohime-sama will surely be very happy as well.'

Takahime said, 'There, again, you are using Keigo toward your Okusama. It is not very becoming; please refrain from it.'
