Chapter 11: 変態動物 [656]

変態動物〔656〕

§ 1

Within a Shiyoinzuku building of substantial construction, lacking excessive Saushiyoku and possessing a Shōshiya air, a woman who has passed her Misoji and a gentle woman of about Hatachi are whispering together, with a paulownia Maruhibachi placed between them.

Osetsu said, 'Matsuhime-sama, the spring Keshiki is indeed Yoroshi, but the beauty of the scenery in all directions where the Usuyuki has gathered, when viewed from this moss-covered garden, is truly Kakubetsu. As the Manmoku is spread as if with silver mats, and the Ohikari of Nichirinsama dwells upon it and reflects with a glittering light, it is as if jewels were spread out. The Ōmikokoro of Kamususanōnōokami, who Goshiyugo the Aragane earth, must be like this scenery, with not Itsuten of dust or defilement, a truly Mizumizu Mitama.'

Matsuhime replied, 'Sayō, when I gaze upon this snowy Nobe, even our hearts become refreshed. Your home country differs from these parts, for the snow is deep, and at this time it must be truly beautiful. It is said that all things are a mirror reflecting heaven and earth; where the spirit of the land is pure, a correspondingly pure and beautiful Keshiki is drawn by the nature of heaven and earth. I, too, wish to pay homage at the precious throne of Manai in Hinu, but as I am burdened with a great duty, I cannot leave this mansion even for a day. Truly, the way of the gods seems vast, yet it is restrictive. Osetsu-sama, it is said that since you visited Kurohime-sama of Makutsugahara at the beginning of this summer, your heart has suddenly changed and you have returned to Ananaikyo. How does Ananaikyo differ from Uranai-kyo?'

Osetsu replied, 'Yes, it is truly a shameful matter. Because my heart lacked any firm foundation, I was toyed with by the winds and wandered here and there. However, I believe that both teachings are truly excellent. The gods are originally of one stock; although the names are divided into Ananaikyo and Uranai-kyo, there is not the slightest difference in the true gods we revere. There is only a distinction in the depth, shallowness, breadth, and narrowness of the interpretations held by those who transmit the teachings. Nevertheless, as humans, we have no choice but to entrust all things to the gods. We need only to pray to the gods who created this world and protect the people without distinction between day and night. To speak of a teaching as profound or shallow depends upon human interpretation, and I believe it has no relation to the gods themselves.'

§ 2

Matsuhime said, 'If that be your thought, why have you come to the Uranai-kyō? Is your Goshiyujin not still serving as a Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō? There is a Dauka which says, "Though a Meoto may pledge their troth for two lives, if the paths they tread diverge, they shall come to hate and contend." A manner of Shinkau where the husband goes east and the wife goes west is something to ponder. When the paths of Shinkau differ, the Gokatei shall surely not be at peace. Since it is said that a woman should follow her husband, as in Fushiyōfujiyū, would it not be for the good of your Gokatei to Shinpō the Ananai-kyō which Aohiko-sama serves? Or, perhaps you might cause Aohiko-sama to Kijiyun to the Uranai-kyō which you serve; how would it be to settle upon one path or the other?'

Osetsu said, 'In truth, the reason I have come here is that there is a matter I wish for you to hear. What points of the Ananai-kyō do you Oboshime to be evil?'

Matsuhime said, 'For my part, I have neither the Kenri nor the Chishiki to Maoshia Karekore. However, as the Mitama of Henjiyōniyoshi have entered into the current Ananai-kyō, and various things not present in the teachings of Henjiyōnanshi have been imported, if left as it is, the painstaking efforts of the men will come to naught. Because Takahime-sama of the male lineage is concerned that this is a grave matter for the sake of the realm and the nation, she has been compelled to establish the Uranai-kyō. Therefore, if it be possible, I would wish for those of the Ananai-kyō to reconsider and establish the true Oshihe.'

Osetsu said, 'Do you not favor the teachings of Susanoo-no-Mikoto, the Mitama of Henjiyōniyoshi?'

Matsuhime said, 'I know not why, but it does not sit well with me; it seems there is something lacking somewhere. It is said that there is affinity in all things, and in the path of Shinkau, there are those to whom it is suited and those to whom it is not.'

Osetsu said, 'If that is so, do you not understand the intentions of Takahime-sama and Kurohime-sama of late?'

Matsuhime said, 'Nay, I am dimly aware of them. It seems that the two of them are becoming strange, and before long they shall return to the Ananai-kyō. However, as for me, I cannot so lightly, as if turning over one's palm, make a plaything of my own spirit.'

Osetsu said, 'If that is so, do you intend not to heed even the commands of Takahime-sama and Kurohime-sama, whom you look up to as your masters?'

§ 3

Matsuhime said, 'Even if Takahime-sama should suffer a downfall, I shall uphold the Uranai-Oshihe until the very last one of us remains. The Shishiyō is precious, yet the Path is also precious; pray, consider well which is of greater importance, the Path or the Shishiyō. I say this not with the slightest intent to turn against my Shishiyō, but in unavoidable circumstances, I am resolved to defend the solitary fortress of the Uranai-Oshihe to the death, in order to clarify the distinction between the root and the branch, the self and the other, and the public and the private.'

Osetsu replied, 'Hearing you speak thus, I perceive that you are unyielding to the very end.'

Matsuhime answered, 'There is a proverb that even upon a rock, a Himematsu may grow. It is said that the sincerity of a single heart can pierce even stone. My Tetsusekishin shall not be moved by any fire of cannons or any display of power; as this is my sole life, no matter what anyone may say, I shall not be shaken in the least. My soul is not so fragile as to be moved by spears, guns, levers, or staves, nor shall it ever be moved. If one's faith is such that it would waver, it were better not to have held it from the beginning. No matter how earnestly or circuitously you may advise me, Osetsu-sama, it shall be in vain; therefore, while I am grateful for your kindness, I pray you speak of it no more. If a person thoroughly maintains obedience, patience, and sincerity, the Kami shall surely protect them. It is not a matter that depends upon the nature of the sect.'

As the two were thus conversing, the gatekeepers Kumahiko and Torahiko came rushing in with great haste.

Kuma and Tora exclaimed, 'We report to Matsuhime-sama, a most momentous event has occurred; it is, indeed, a most gratifying and great victory, and we pray you to rejoice.'

Matsuhime said, 'Oh, you are Kumakō and Torakō-sama; what is the matter that you come in such a frantic state with such a look of blood upon your faces?'

Torahiko replied, 'As you are aware, those thorns in the side of the Uranai-Oshihe, our mortal enemies, the petty warriors of the Anau-Kiyō who serve the evil Kami of Susanoo-no-Mikoto—two fellows with foolish faces named Uma and Shika—came hither. Without so much as a word of permission, they opened the wicket gate and attempted to pass through to the inner sanctum without our consent as gatekeepers. You have said until now that if the followers of the Anau-Kiyō should come, we must not let even one pass, but must drive them away, and that we must not suffer a foolish fate like that of Kurohime-sama of Makutsugahara. We have never forgotten those words even in our dreams, and have guarded the front gate strictly until this day. As they came sauntering up and mocked us, saying that the gate was four-legged and that we were four-legged spirits, we thought, "How insolent, you beasts who know no etiquette; if you are beasts, we shall administer a punishment befitting beasts." We both took our six-foot staves and struck their heads three or four times with a thud. The spineless cowards, lacking the courage their mouths boasted, had their spirits crushed and are now crouching like frogs upon the earth, weeping and wailing. Since they passed through this sacred gate without permission, and furthermore vomited that the gate was four-legged, and then shed tears upon the earth to defile this sacred ground, we were moved by a spirit of valor and took decisive action.'

§ 4

Matsuhime said, 'That was a violent deed you have committed; who told you to do such a thing? Tell me, Kumakō, did you truly do as Torakō says?'

Kumahiko replied, 'Indeed, indeed, is that all? I was so filled with Gofubara that I rolled up my skirts, and together with Torakō, we clenched our fists—the fists of a master of the hundredth degree of Jiudau—and struck their Denbu until they turned blue; the sight of them at that moment was truly Kotsukei.'

Matsuhime asked, 'And what became of those two persons?'

Kumahiko answered, 'Indeed, those two—or rather, those two beasts—were cast out of the gate, where they clung to each other like a corrupted woman, performing a scene of Shiutanba. When I peered through the Fushiana of the door, it was truly a pitiful sight; nay, it was a pleasant feeling, as if my Riuin had been settled. Ah, that we were able to prevent the Bakuretsudan of the Uranai-kyō from being Mihatsu with such great effort is, needless to say, entirely due to the Kuōdaimuhen Goshinriki of Ōkamisama, and the manifestation of the great spirit of the Ai-kyō by us two. If you would be so kind as to do such-and-such for us, we shall be all the more grateful for the divine grace and serve the cause with even greater strictness.'

Matsuhime asked, 'Did the receptionist for Tatsuwaka just watch in silence?'

Kumakō replied, 'He neither gave commands nor refrained from them; to speak ill of it, it was in the manner of catching a catfish with a gourd. However, since he gave us a measure of support, I believe it would be acceptable to view his contribution as equal; indeed, indeed, we have performed a most grand activity.'

Matsuhime places her hands upon her knees, bows her head, and ponders something.

Osetsu said, 'Kumakō, Torakō, I have just heard that the horse and the deer of the Sangokyō have appeared; have you truly committed such rough deeds? People who perform such Ranbau appear in the Uranai-kyō from time to time; surely Ōkamisama cannot be pleased by this.'

Torahiko exclaimed, 'Hey, Osetsu, what are you spewing? You are a creature like a cat, deceiving Matsuhime-sama with sweet words, and furthermore, you are conspiring with the beasts of the horse and deer to overthrow this mansion from its very foundations, are you not? I have clearly seen through such things since the moment you arrived; shall I take hold of your testicles as well and cast you out of the gate?'

Kumahiko laughed, 'Hahaha, a woman's testicles—this is the first time I have heard of such a thing; Torakō, is that not a mistake for one's liver?'

§ 5

Torahiko said, 'Though it be but a slight difference, cease your grumbling; it is but a mistake between the testicles and the liver. Since the matter originated from the gate, I have corrected the liver to the Kototama of the testicles. Is it not for this reason that I have always called the Shiyugojin of Osetsu the evil Kiubi, the nine-tailed fox? Aha, ha, ha, ha!'

Matsuhime said, 'Hearken, Torakō, Kumakō, go and offer your apologies to those called Umakō and Shikakō, and bring them to my Ima. Truly, you are a man beyond all remedy.'

Torahiko said, 'Hee, what is it you say? Do you command me to bring such Bakuretsudan here? I have thought of late that you, Anata, were somewhat changed, but it seems you have indeed suffered a Datsusen.'

Matsuhime said, 'Cease your grumbling and it shall be well; if I say go and welcome them, then go and welcome them. Kumakō shall go with you as well.'

The Riyōnin cast a reluctant 'Hee' upon the spot and approached the Omotemon with a listless air.

Tatsuwaka said, 'Hey, you two, is not your Kahoiro somewhat strange? What has happened to you, Itsutai?'

Torahiko said, 'What has happened, you ask? A great problem has arisen from the gate, and we are in the very midst of agony and suffering. We have truly met with a foolish fate.'

Kumahiko said, 'As a result of the friction and the dispute, this Kumakō finds the feelings of agony unbearable this day.'

Tatsuwaka said, 'It is because your manner of approach was poor that you have received a counter-blow.'

Kumahiko said, 'What? My approach was perfectly flawless, without a single defect; yet no matter what is said, that fellow Osetsu is constantly beguiling Matsuhime at every opportunity. Consequently, the character of Matsuhime-sama has changed entirely; whereas she would usually leap with joy as if shown something wondrous by a nun, she does not stir in the least, no matter what excellent report I bring. I am filled with rage even to look upon the face of Osetsu. Ah, how grotesque! My guts are churning with such revulsion. Furthermore, what makes my karma boil all the more is that she commands me to politely welcome the two, Uma and Shika, whom we had gone to such lengths to cast out. It is utterly beyond discourse.'
