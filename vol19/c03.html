Chapter 3: 千騎一騎 [648]

千騎一騎〔648〕

§ 1

Takayamahiko departed from the Gankutsu of Makutsugahara and proceeded with Itsushiyōkenmei, aiming for the Seichi. Upon reaching the banks of the Shirasegaha, the Kasui had overflowed due to the incessant Samidare, and the Tosen had become Zetsutai Fukanō.

Takayamahiko paced up and down the riverbank, stamping his feet in frustration; gazing at the Seichi Yotsuōzan before his eyes, he was driven by a Dōkei that the Gozashiyo of Tamateruhime lay Kanata, and he appeared as one in a state of Kiyōki. To this place, breathless and Hasekita, came his wife, Kurohime.

"Ah, art thou Kurohime? For what purpose hast thou come forth?"

"Takayama-sama, alas, what is it that thou sayest? It cannot be left as it is. Yonder, visible on the other side, is the Shinzan of Yotsuō, the hidden Bashiyo of the Ananai-kyō; Tamateruhime-sama must be in the thicket of that forest. Come, come, cross quickly."

"Though thou sayest to cross, how could one cross this Gekiriu?"

"Cross it by staking thy Inochi. It is for this reason that men are said to be unable to act in the hour of crisis. Thou, too, shouldst seek the aid of the guardian deity of Hanataka and cross high through the mid-air."

"Even if thou sayest such things, one cannot so easily return to one's original body."

"What reason is there to say it cannot be returned? It is because thy faith is insufficient. Whether I become fire or a serpent, I shall not leave this river uncrossed."

No sooner had she spoken than she transformed into the figure of a serpent, fearful to behold, and aiming for the very center of the raging Gekiriu and surging waves, she plunged in with a splash and at last reached the opposite shore.

Takayamahiko trembled at this sight, and while he was shivering, a black cloud arose from the serpent's body, forming a mass that crossed the sky above the river toward him; in the moment it enveloped the body of Takayamahiko, he fell suddenly upon the opposite bank of the river. The serpent body instantly changed back into the original Kurohime.

"Come, Takayama-sama, such a feat can be performed but once in a lifetime, but in this instance of Senki-ikki, the power of Kurohime's faith has manifested. Come, come, fear not this, and follow me, for there shall be no such thing hereafter."

Takayamahiko said in a trembling voice,

"How fearful a thing is a woman."

"Hearken, Takayama-sama, thou art surely disgusted with me now. If thou wouldst be disgusted, then be so; I, too, have a thought of my own."

§ 2

She laughs coldly. Takayamahiko blinks his eyes, and while rubbing his high nose with the back of his hand, he says, 'Nay, I leave all matters to you, Kurohime; henceforth I shall not concern myself with anything. Pray, use me as you please, as you desire.'

'You have attained a great degree of Kaishin, have you not? That is indeed what makes you my fine husband. Come, let us go. Oh, what a way to walk! Can you not be firm? Once we cross this river, it is the territory of an enemy against whom one cannot afford to be negligent.'

'Even if you say so, my legs are trembling somehow, and I cannot walk.'

'Oh, what a cowardly man you are. Who is there to fear? It is but the likes of Murasakihime and Aohiko and their Renchiu, is it not?'

'I fear neither Aohiko nor Murasakihime. What I fear is your Shiyōnen.'

'Takayama-sama, though I may appear thus, a woman is still a woman. Do not Goshinpai. Even so, I shall continue to cherish you as something Daiji.'

Takayamahiko, trembling violently, says, 'Nay, I have no need to be cherished further. For one such as I, to be near you is Motsutai. It is too fearful. I pray you, make me your Zaurimochi instead.'

'Oh, what a cowardly thing this man says again. Ah, I have become completely exhausted of Aisau; I am becoming sick of it.'

'I pray you, do become sick of me. If you would but grow to dislike me, it would be Taihen Kautsugafu.'

Kurohime sharpens her voice and says, 'What is that you say? Even if you tell me to grow to dislike you, who would do such a rash thing now? Consider yourself a frog targeted by a snake, and resign yourself to your fate.'

'Yes, I shall resign myself. Thinking that all things are bound by Innen, I shall endure even this evil affinity. For the evil Innen of a previous life has come to bear fruit.'

'What do you mean by evil affinity? Your heart, as a man, is like the autumn sky, and perhaps it is a fickle affinity, but I am the autumn sky to the very end, and I love you, love you, and am transparently clear in my love, ho ho ho ho.'

'Pray, Kurohime-sama, I beseech you to save one person and forgive my sins.'

'What is it you say now? Once things have come to this, how could I ever forgive you? I shall take you to the bottom of the Ryūgū sea, and I shall swallow you, bite you, and lick you, and cherish you dearly.'

'I have no need to be cherished further. I pray you, make such concern unnecessary. I have no way to return the favor.'

§ 3

"Ah, what an incomprehensible man you are. I shall speak with you at leisure later. Come, we must depart without a moment's delay. The sun has just begun to set."

With these words, he set Takayamahiko in the lead, and they journeyed toward the mountain foot of Yotsuō, traversing the wild fields where the summer grasses grew thick.

The moon of the thirteenth night of the fifth month illuminated the sky like an elliptical mirror. Umakō and Shikakō gazed upon the moonlight.

"Ah, is that not a magnificent moon, Shikakō?"

"That is a settled matter, Umakō. It is a moon of commemoration, for on the evening of the fifth day of the fifth month, the Tamateruhime deigned to arrive. Throughout all ages, past and present, has there ever been such a fine moon? Yet, in regard to this, is not Kurohime to be pitied? Behold, the Tamateruhime shines like a crystal in the firmament, and here within this dwelling is the Lady Otama, and the Tamateruhime; one might well call these three combined the three Mitama. Ah,

As if emerging from the dew, the moon after the rain,

what think you of this?"

"Well, Shikakō, do you possess knowledge of Haiku?"

"Yes, whether it be verses, songs, or aught else, there is nothing I do not know. Name whatever you wish. I, Shikakō, shall compose a verse with wit and immediacy for your eyes to behold."

"Then, look at the moon yonder; black clouds are encroaching, and it seems they are about to obscure it. Compose one upon that."

"Enveloped is Tamateruhime by Kurohime; in the sky of Aohiko, one shall witness a fool."

"What an ill-omened poem you recite. Will you not recite it anew, you knave Shikakō?"

"I had thought you would say as much, Umakō. This time, it is in earnest.

In the vast sky of Aohiko and Murasakihime, the moon, Tamateruhime, shines bright.

What think you of this?"

"Very well. Give me one more."

"I can provide as many as you wish; if the moon is the theme, then the moon is the ancestor. The Great Deity of the Moon is the ancestor of this world. Listen well, Umakō. Ah,

Clusters of clouds upon the moon, storms upon the flowers,

Flag-clouds in the east, and a comet,

The riverbed of heaven lies north and south,

The flow of stars, from the long-enduring,

Falls into the land of Fusa.

Takayamahiko and the moonbeams rising,

From the peaks of the short mountains,

Today is the auspicious thirteenth night.

Even if Kurohime should obscure it,

Blown away by the mist of Ibuki,

The great sky changes in an instant,

Becoming the pure forms of Murasakihime and Aohiko.

What think you of this?"

"That is a remarkably long poem, Shikakō."

"Long it is, indeed long; soon a long fellow with a dark face shall arrive. A fellow long in breadth, and Takayamahiko, the long green gourd, in height. If we do not act with skill, we shall be the fools. What the Hensei Danshi declares shall not differ by even a fraction of a hair."

§ 4

What are you spouting? I would have you cease this at once. Hey, look there, are there not two shadows stirring? I cannot tell if they be deer.

Shikakō said, 'Oh, they have come. One is a thick, short fellow, and the other is a thin, long one. Ah, this fellow is Takayamahiko, and that is Kurohime. If we dally, like the moon in the sky, we shall be swallowed by Kurohime, and it will be an Ichidaiji for Tamateruhime-sama. Come, come, bar the door!'

Before he had finished speaking, Shikakō leaped inside and slammed the bolt shut.

'Hey, will you not let me in as well?'

'Eh, what a nuisance. You, go and hide somewhere in the thicket; are you not a horse? I shall defend this Sekishiyo from within.'

The two shadows drew nearer and nearer. Shikakō would not open the door under any circumstances. Umakō, having no choice, hid his body in the dense reeds and trembled.

The two shadows appeared at the Toguchi. One was a woman, the other a man.

'Excuse me, pray open this place for a moment.'

'What is this? Is there anyone who visits another's house after Kuremu has passed? The night is the Sekai of demons; if you have business, come tomorrow. Shikakō shall absolutely not open this Kadoguchi.'

'That may be so, but just a little will suffice; please open it but a Shaku. There is an Ichidaiji I wish to speak of.'

Shikakō, standing at the Toguchi, said, 'Even if you have an Ichidaiji, it is also an Ichidaiji for us. No matter what you say, I will not open it. Excuse me, Aohiko-sama, please come here for a moment. It seems that Kurohime has arrived.'

Aohiko, from the inner chamber, said, 'No matter what anyone says, it shall not be opened.'

'That is what I say, but Umakō is outside, having failed to enter, and is hiding.'

Kurohime heard this voice, searched the surrounding thicket, and said, 'Ah, you are Umakō. Come, it is safe now. Here, here, Takayamasama, bring out the rope we prepared. Eh, why are you trembling like an earthquake? You are a faint-hearted beast.'

Before she had finished speaking, she untied her own narrow sash and bound Umakō, saying, 'Now Umakō, come this way. Until this door is opened, you are a hostage. If it is not opened, I, this Kurohime, shall reveal my true form and swallow you in one gulp.'

'Ah, I thought it would come to this. That is why the gods say to be cautious with Kototama, yet that fellow Shikakō would keep saying this and that about Kurohime, and so we have fallen into this predicament. Hey, Umakō has been bound; Shikakō, will you not open the door?'

You are the one to be bound, while I am the one to rest soundly within. Pray, stand in Tachiōjiyō there until the dawn breaks. Since you are in the company of the gentle Kurohime and the Irōtoko Takayama, you shall not be overly lonely, I trow.

Do not speak such Reikoku words; surely you possess some understanding of the path of a Hōiu.

Wait a while, for now Murasakihime is about to unleash her Jifuhachiban Kototama. When that is done, both Kurohime and Takayamahiko shall meet a Hisan fate and perish like leaves scattered by the wind.

What then shall become of me?

I have not yet made any Kenkiu regarding your fate. Ho, Kurohime, you are truly to be pitied, and I offer my sympathy for your Goshinchiu. You must have received many a Kogoto from Takahime. However, no matter how much you may wish to welcome Tamateruhime, it is now Dame; so while there is yet light at your Ashimoto, return with haste. There is an Itsupiki of a Shikakō there; mount it and return home.

Hey, Shikakō, cease your Muchiya talk; I am by no means the horse of Kurohime.

Kurohime said, 'Will you not open it, no matter what? If you will not open it, very well. Though I am not the Tsurigane of Daujiyōji, I shall become a great serpent, wrap myself ten or twenty times around this house, and turn it into boiling water.'

Pray, Murasakihime and Aohiko, hold yourselves firm. She speaks of impossible things.

Murasakihime spoke with quiet words, 'Ho, ho, ho, do not be concerned. Shikakō, keep the door firmly fastened. Pray, Kurohime, it is truly a pity for you, but for the sake of the Divine Realm and for the sake of the world, it is unavoidable that we treat you with unkindness. I beseech you to return.'

No matter what you say, I shall not return. I will pluck the heads of Aohiko and Murasakihime, present them to Takahime of the land of Fusa, and I shall not rest until I have welcomed Tamateruhime.

Aohiko said, 'What a tenacious old woman you are; even I am astonished. Would it not be better to abandon your attachment?'

It is you who possess the attachment. It would be better if you were to cast it off first. Now, hand over Tamateruhime and Otama to me.

Aohiko said, 'This attachment alone I shall never release. It is not something that should be possessed by any individual, but a precious treasure for the fulfillment of Divine Governance. Even if heaven and earth were to be overturned, I cannot consent to this; I pray you, return home at once.'

§ 6

"Whatever may be said, Kurohime shall not return."

Murasakihime spoke: "Tamateruhime is a magnificent Kamisama, indispensable even to the Ananai-kyō. She is also a necessary Kamisama for the Uranai-kyō. Since it is impossible to satisfy the desires of both, why do you not undergo Gokaishin and enter the Ananai-kyō? Once you have undergone Gokaishin, Takahime shall naturally undergo Gokaishin as well; pray, convey to Takahime that Murasaki has said so."

"It is you who should undergo Kaishin, for you have employed Kenbōjiyutsusū and, through Keiriyaku, have Ōriyō the Tamateruhime whom I have long desired. Look into the mirror of your heart and see which of us is good and which is evil. Is your conduct not a method that destroys the Seishin of the Ananai-kyō—in short, is it not the Iushiyōretsupai of Wareyoshi?"

Shikakō replied: "Hey, cease your Yakamashi clamor; is it not you, Kurohime, who are the one of Wareyoshi? This Tamateruhime has an Inen, for the Kamisama of the Ananai-kyō have orchestrated the divine plan, and Etsukohime has even taken her up. Whatever may be said, it is justice, and the right of priority exists. Rather than coveting the treasures of others, inciting unnecessary rebellion, and suffering in agony, why not abandon all and find peace of mind? Shikakō is filled with wrath."

Kurohime declared: "Whatever may be said, I shall not let this matter rest without seeing it through to the end. Even if it takes a thousand or ten thousand years, I shall pray and pray, and I shall surely prevail through prayer. Ha! Taking this Umakō as a hostage serves no purpose. Come, Umakō, you are free to roam the world. Go wherever you please."

As she untied the bonds, Umakō exclaimed:

"Hey, hey, Kurohime-sama, I thank you. Hey, wait a moment; I was bound by you and released by you, so there is no reason for me to offer thanks. Ah, what an irreparable blunder I have committed. How ridiculous."

At this moment, the voice of Murasakihime, clear and cool, caused the song of the heavenly numbers to resound. Instantly, the head of Kurohime turned pure white, and taking the hand of Takayamahiko, she fled, pointing toward the northwest, vanishing like clouds and mist.

Umakō said: "Hey, Shikakō, the couple Kurohime has already fled. Will you not please open the door for me?"

"Very well, very well."

So saying, he pulled the door open with a rattle, and asked:

"Hey, Umakō, how was it? You were bound, were you not?"

"Yes, I was bound. However, it did not hurt in the slightest. When that wretch Kurohime was binding me, she was praying with all her might in a low voice: 'Ōkami-sama, I am sorry. Please forgive me. I bind the innocent Umakō, but this too is for the sake of the divine path; please look upon this, listen to this, and rectify it with Kaminaobi and Ōnaobi.' Truly, it is well said that the nature of man is good."

Aohiko, hearing this, folded his hands, bowed his head, and sank into deep contemplation. Murasakihime immediately offered a Norito of gratitude before the divine altar. Tamateruhime suddenly began to weep as if she were being crushed. Otama, surprised and flustered, stroked the back of Tamateruhime and comforted her.

In the sky, the moon shone dimly through the rifts of clouds filled with white, fish-scale waves, and the sorrowful voice of a mountain cuckoo could be heard beyond the peaks.
