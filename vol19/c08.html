Chapter 8: Great 悟徹底 [653]

大悟徹底〔653〕

§ 1

Murasakihime, Wakahiko, and Otama, having concluded their Kiguwan at the Shinden of Motoise, were attending to Tamateruhime while awaiting the return of the two, Umakō and Shikakō, to hear their Fukumei.

As the hour of Tasogare passed, two shadows approached the Shinden in haste.

"Pray, Murasakihime, is Lord Wakahiko present? It is we, Umakō and Shikakō."

Wakahiko replied, "Ah, Umakō and Shikakō, you have labored well. How is the Yōsu?"

Umakō answered, "Indeed, it went passably well. We were told that Kurohime had returned to the land of Fusa and was Fuzaichiyū; the remaining Jifushigonin of the Renchiu were in the Matsusaichiu of a great Ōsawa, drunken and gluttonous. We two were eventually drawn in and overcome by wine, and while we were in the Saichiu of enjoying ourselves without Tekimikata or Kubetsu, there arrived Takahime from the Honzan of the land of Fusa, and Tsurukō and Kamekō as envoys of Kurohime. Thereupon, we conveyed your opinions to them, and Tsurukō and Kamekō consented without a moment's hesitation. However, they stated that they would first visit the land of Fusa and provide a definitive reply later."

Murasakihime said, "Ah, you have labored greatly. If that be the case, shall we return to the sacred ground until their reply arrives?"

Wakahiko replied, "That would be well. If we consider that we have accompanied Tamateruhime on her pilgrimage to Motoise, it shall not have been in vain. Come, let us return with haste."

Thus the party of five, bearing Tamateruhime with reverence, returned once more to the mansion at the foot of Mount Yotsugi. Scattered by the storm on the peaks, the clouds that filled the heavens vanished into the void, and the waxing moon began to shine in the eastern sky. It was a time not long before the break of dawn.

Some four or five days later, around the middle of the night, several men and women appeared at the hermitage of Tamateruhime at the foot of Mount Yotsugi. In the dead of night, with the cold wind raging, Murasakihime and all her household had retired to sleep. A man's voice knocked at the gate of the door.

Tsurukō said, "Pray, forgive me for coming in the middle of the night, but I am Tsurukō, who has come from the Honzan of the Uranai faith in the land of Fusa, as you know. When I immediately conveyed to Takahime and Kurohime what I had heard from Lord Umakō and Lord Shikakō the other day, they were exceedingly pleased and have now come here, bringing a great company with them."

Hearing this voice, Umakō spoke from within the house,

"Ah, the voice of the incomparable Lord Tsurukō, pray wait a moment. I shall report this to Murasakihime at once. Hey, Shikakō, will you not wake? It is a great matter, a great matter!"

Shikakō rose abruptly and said, 'What is this Taihen? It must be that Kurohime-sama and Takahime-sama have come from the land of Fusa in great Oyoroko.'

Umakō replied, 'You lazy fellow, so you were listening, were you?'

Shikakō said, 'Aha, listen, Murasakihime-sama, Wakahiko-sama, Kurohime-sama and Takahime-sama, the Goriyōnin have arrived.'

Murasakihime said, 'Ah, they have come from such a Goenpō. Umakō, pray open the front door quickly. Then, serve them some Ochiya at the Uketsuke and ask them to wait there until the inner room is Katazu.'

With these words, she set about clearing the Nedoko with Isoiso and began the Sauji. Wakahiko changed his Nemaki, rushed out to the front, and said,

Wakahiko said, 'Ya, Kurohime-sama, Takahime-sama, you have come from so far. Come, this is but a Karigoya, and compared to the Honzan where Anata Oide, it is nothing but a Shibagoya, but pray, Ohai.'

Takahime said, 'Aohiko-sama, all things are by the convenience of the divine realm, so let us wash away all that has passed and be on good terms.'

Wakahiko said, 'Yes, yes, as you command; there is nothing more excellent than being on good terms.'

Kurohime said, 'You are, as I expected, just as I perceived; I was certain you would bring about such a good result. My eyes are indeed black. Takahime-sama, what do you think? There can be no mistake, can there?'

Takahime said, 'Indeed, I am humbled. Come, let us be excused.'

With this, the group one after another stepped over the threshold and advanced into the inner room.

Murasakihime said, 'This is indeed a great honor that you have all come. Every day, I have been stretching my neck in anticipation, waiting to hear what your reply might be. I had intended to inquire as soon as I received a reply from your side, but for you to come yourselves is truly a matter of gratitude. Pray, forgive my rudeness until now.'

Kurohime said, 'Now that it has come to this, we are as parent and child. Do not trouble yourself at all.'

With these words, the party of seven took their seats in the inner room, sitting in a row like cloth bags on a shelf.

Murasakihime, joyful from the bottom of her heart, politely entertained the guests who had come from afar. Wakahiko, Umakō, and Shikakō suddenly donned their tasuki and devoted their full strength to the menu of the feast. Otama did not leave the side of Tamateruhime and protected her with great care.

Kurohime said, 'Now, Murasakihime-sama, you are truly an admirable person. Even I, Kurohime, did not notice your profound and miraculous secret plan. One who accomplishes great things must be as you are. I am truly impressed by your skill in orchestrating the play so cleverly that even I, your superior, did not know of it. Listen, Takahime-sama, is that not why I told you when I showed her to you that I had found a treasure? My insight, Kurohime's, is not entirely to be discarded. Ehehe.'

She shakes her shoulders.

Murasakihime replied, 'Nay, as I am one whose Chie is insufficient, the truth is that Wakahiko, originally named Aohiko, and I, following the Osashizu of Yoshikohime, did deceive Kurohime-sama, though we knew it to be wrong. In short, when we caused you to eat the fried pot and brought Tamateruhime-sama here, I felt a sensation that I cannot describe. I felt glad, yet somehow ill at ease; I felt pity for you, and it was as if there were a black shadow in the very depths of my heart. Now, however, it has become as if there is not a single cloud, and there is no greater joy than this.'

Kurohime rounded her eyes and pursed her lips.

Kurohime said, 'So, it was indeed you two who conspired together to lead me astray. Truly, you are princesses with black hearts, beyond measure and not to be trusted. Ohohoho.'

Takahime said, 'Now, Kurohime-sama, is that not enough? If you but repent, there is nothing more to be said. Rather than speaking of what has passed and making each other feel ill, it is filial piety toward Kamisama to perform our Goyō with courage. Shall we not put an end to such matters, and with hearts Uchito, join in the divine work from here on?'

Murasakihime said, 'I thank you. Regarding this, there are various deep reasons, but as the preparations for the meal will soon be ready, please eat at your leisure, and afterwards, we shall have you listen to our tale of repentance.'

At this moment, Wakahiko appeared.

'Everyone, the meal is prepared. As the night is already beginning to break, pray use the water for purification and partake of the meal.'

Kurohime said, 'Come, everyone, let us cleanse our bodies, offer our thanks to Kamisama, partake of the meal, and then listen to the story at our leisure.'

At these words, they all rinsed their mouths and washed their hands in the clear spring of the valley behind, offered Norito before the altar, and upon finishing, sat at the breakfast table.

Kurohime said, 'I have partaken of a magnificent feast into which you have put your hearts in every way. The sincerity of Aohiko-sama has permeated it, and I have found it delicious beyond measure. Now, I ask Aohiko-sama, or rather Murasakihime-sama, once more: having plotted so much to deceive this old woman and having succeeded thus far, for what purpose do you now say you will hand Tamateruhime-sama over to me? Is it not that you have failed to meet the wishes of Tamateruhime-sama, and being tormented by some terrible dream that you two are shown every night, you have reached an impasse and are now surrendering? As one who serves the Goyō of the evil gods of Susanoo-no-Mikoto, I find this strange beyond endurance. Come, speak plainly and reveal all. Depending on the circumstances, I may yet accept Tamateruhime-sama.'

Wakahiko replied, 'Nay, nay, that is far from the truth. Tamateruhime-sama is ever in a most excellent mood, and her Goshintoku shines day by day; it is because of this Okata that the Ananai-kyō has attained such great Seiriyoku.'

Kurohime asked, 'Why then, having exhausted all your Hijiyutsu to obtain such a splendid Tamateruhime-sama, have you come to beg that I accept her into the Uranai-kyō?'

Wakahiko answered, 'In truth, when I beheld you at the valley river at the foot of Kensakiyama, we conspired with Murasakihime-sama in an Ishindenshin manner, feigning Kijiyun to the Uranai-kyō to fully Tanchi your Keiriyaku, and succeeded in ingratiating ourselves until we were entrusted with a Jiunin. Thinking this a stroke of fortune, we brought the couple Ayahiko to the house of Toyohiko, received Tamateruhime-sama and Otama-sama, and thought that Kurohime-sama must be yawning and waiting for us even now. We returned with hearts full of joy, thinking, "What a fine deed we have done, how thrilling," and served her day and night. Thanks to that, the Ananai-kyō grew in power like the rising sun, and I believed that Susanoo-no-Ōkami-sama would surely be pleased. However, one night, Eiko-hime-sama, the daughter of the Ōkami, conveyed to us a most extraordinary reprimand from the Ōkami, saying, "To obtain a noble deity by employing deceptive stratagems of political intrigue is a most grievous impropriety. Since Tamateruhime is most essential to the Ananai-kyō, she must also be necessary to the Uranai-kyō. Why did you take such a predatory action, mercilessly seizing that which Kurohime is striving with all her might to obtain? Do you not know the heart of God, which teaches that you should do unto others what you desire for yourself? Return Tamateruhime to Kurohime without a moment's delay and offer your apologies. Furthermore, cease your roles as missionaries of the Ananai-kyō." Such was the unexpected displeasure and the stern command. Because of this, Murasakihime-sama and I were struck with dread. The spirit of the Ananai-kyō is not such a thing. Moreover, the great heart of Susanoo-no-Ōkami-sama is not a soul of half-purity and half-impurity like our own. Feeling and trembling before the sincerity of his crystal-like spirit, and fearing that you were still residing in the cavern of Makutsugahara, we took advantage of the occasion when Tamateruhime-sama went to worship at Moto-Ise-sama to send the two, Uma and Shika, to offer an apology. As luck would have it, you were away at the main mountain, and fortunately, the two, Tsuru and Kame, arrived from the main mountain; thus, Uma and Shika conveyed our intentions and made the request of you as you have seen. It is by no means that we two are offering her back out of a sincere change of heart. It is entirely in accordance with the command of the Ōkami.'

Murasakihime said, 'Just as Wakahiko has spoken, there is not a sunbun of saui. I pray you, oyuru our akushin until now, and pray, ōketo Tamateruhime-sama into the Uranai-kyō. We, ichidō, shall serve as otomo to the land of Fusa. And since we have mohaya been jiyomei from Ananai-kyō, I pray you, anata, to use us under your bakuka as your otsuka.'

Kurohime replied, 'Very well, very well, I shall make you into splendid sendenshi of my Uranai-kyō. Do not have any goshinpai. Furthermore, I shall certainly ōketo both Tamateruhime-sama and Otama-sama.'

The divine teaching that great good resembles great evil, true filial piety resembles impiety, and the true religion resembles a false religion, has struck my heart as if driving in a nail. Ah, Mizu-no-Mitama-sama, I pray you, forgive my misunderstandings and my rudeness until now.'

So saying, she pressed her hands together, let tears fall in torrents, and writhed as if throwing her body against the tatami in apology.

Kurohime, with a face as if bewitched by a fox, did not utter a single word, but merely rolled her eyes and gazed at them all. Whether one might say she had missed her midnight meal like an owl, or had been struck by a bean-gun like a pigeon, she was exposing a style beyond all description.

Murasakihime clung to Takahime, and amidst her tears, said, 'Moshi-moshi, Takahime-sama, I pray you, forgive me.
