Chapter 13: 混線 [658]

混線〔658〕

§ 1

From the Misora where the moon shines across, scattered snow falls fluttering, as if in shame. In the Kojitayami where Jiyumoku grow thick, the Sendenshi of the Ural Kyō, the Riyōnin named Terujī and Koronbo, having left the land of Tokoyo behind, and wishing to open the path of the Ural, crossed over Umikahayamanu and came to Onokorojima. Seeing the purple clouds rising in the distant sky, they wondered at the mystery, thinking that this must surely be the Shiyutsugen of a Shinjin; at any rate, they decided to go toward the clouds as their Mea, and while bathed in the moonlight, the Futari proceeded, walking step by step along the ridge of Takakumayama and to the Sanroku of Ōharayama.

In the forest on one Katahe, there was the voice of a strange person; wondering what Nanigoto it might be, the Riyōnin, with stealthy steps, Suriyo and watched the Dedokoro of the voice.

Tanimaru said, 'Oi, Onimaru, your efforts were great. The Ontaishiyō Onikumohiko was Gekitai by the Sendenshi of the Ananai-kyō, and subsequently, the vice-general Onikumawake, who had constructed a Kenjiyōtetsupeki at Onigajō and intended to sweep across the world, also fell into such a miserable state and fled back to the land of Fusa. We, who were left behind, have sunk into a tragic plight as if our bird wings were taken away. We thought that if we could somehow recover Ōeyama and Onigajō once more, and if we two could barricade ourselves in both mountains, take up our positions grandly again, and restore our former prosperity, we would achieve our purpose after a thousand hardships. Now that we have welcomed the deity Tamateruhiko, who is said to have been born of Kototeruhime of Takakumayama, no matter how great the Ananai-kyō may be, they will be able to do nothing. The wretches Takahime and Kurohime of the Uranai Kyō worked with all their might, and as a result, the essential Tamateruhime was snatched away by the Ananai-kyō; at present, is it not the case that the winds of decline are blowing gradually upon the Uranai Kyō as well? In contrast, the Ananai-kyō is prosperous as you see, by the divine power of Tamateruhime. As for the Brahmanism we serve, if only we could get our hands on Tamateruhiko, we would destroy both the Ananai-kyō and the Uranai Kyō with a single kick, but since our general has met such a fate, there is nothing we can do. However, even if our generals Onikumohiko and Onikumawake have lost heart, the true God never loses heart, so I thought that even if I were left alone, I must uphold this path. They say that if you wait, the sea route will bring a favorable wind, and truly, today has been a splendid day. Regarding that, I have caused you a great deal of trouble.'

§ 2

Onimaru said, 'Brother Tanimaru, there is no need for you to offer me such thanks. Your deeds were truly magnificent. When we reached Kunadome of Yamaguchi, Kunadomenokami was guarding it firmly, leading a multitude of Teshita. I was filled with anxiety, wondering how I might break through this Nankuwan, but thanks to the Kiyōkau Danpan you conducted with Kunadomenokami, we managed to pass that place and reach the foot of Takakumayama as if swimming. Just as I was feeling Anshin, thinking that the situation was now in our hands, the Kami Mikunimori appeared on the scene, bringing with him his Niyōbau, known as Kuniyorihime. The ferocity of their Giyōsau when they glared at Oretachi makes me shudder even now.'

Tanimaru replied, 'However, by employing your customary Benzetsu, we skillfully slipped through this Sekishiyo, and when the Riyōnin arrived before the Gankutsu, neither the object of our quest, Kototeruhime, nor Tamateruhiko-sama were to be seen. While we were exploring the interior of the Gankutsu, the joy I felt when the cry of an infant reached my ears—never since the cutting of the umbilical cord have I known a moment so delightful.'

Onimaru said, 'Thanks to that, we were able to welcome Tamateruhiko-sama and return, but Kototeruhime, whom we had heard was a mother with a figure as magnificent as a goddess, did not show her shadow at all, did she?'

Tanimaru replied, 'She must have been terrified by our might and fled. Yet, the womb is but a borrowed vessel, and Tamateruhiko-sama is no different from the cast-off shell of a cicada. Since we have returned having obtained the essential Honzon, does it not matter little what becomes of Kototeruhime?'

Thus, while carefully resting Tamateruhiko by their side, unaware that there was one watching from the other side, they whispered in high voices, overflowing with joy. Two figures lay hidden in the shade of the trees nearby.

Colombo said, 'Look here, Terujī, we have come all the way from the land of Tokoyo to manifest our merit and restore the Ural religion to its former power, yet we have been outstripped by the fellows of the Brahman religion; is this not vexing? Is there no means to snatch him away to our side?'

Terujī replied, 'Indeed, it seems there are two of them over there. We, too, are two. We must devise some scheme to threaten them and skillfully obtain Tamateruhiko-sama. Since the essential jewel is shining right before our eyes, five or six ken away, we cannot return as we are, regardless of whether we succeed or fail.'

§ 3

Colombo said, 'Yet, hearing the words of Aitsu just now, since he is a fellow who has skillfully broken through the Yamaguchi Sekishiyo, which Kunadomenokami was guarding with a Takusan number of Buka, as well as the Mikunimori Sekishiyo further within, he must surely be a man of great strength. It is Tautei Dame for us to confront him with our Nikudan, exhausted as we are from a Chiyōto journey. There is no Shikata but to devise some Kikei. O, Anii Teruji, does no good thought arise in you?'

Teruji replied, 'Since they must return by this path, shall we not lie in wait along the Chiuto and do something? It would be an Ichidaiji if we spoke too softly and were perceived by the enemy. Come, follow me.'

With that, they muffled their Ashioto, bent their waists, and crawling, left the place, Hikikahe along the path they had come, and arrived back at the Sanroku of Sakahitauge.

Teruji said, 'As I know not Nandoki the fellows of the Brahman Kyō shall return, I must quickly devise a Keiriyaku; I shall climb this Rauboku and shake the pine branches with a rustling sound, mimicking the voice of a Tengu, while you hide your body in the thicket of shrubs, and watching for the moment the two fellows are startled and lose their composure, you shall softly lift up Tamateruhiko-sama and flee to the side of the Tengu Rock at Sakahitauge; if you do so, success is surely without doubt.'

Colombo said, 'Anii's plan sounds interesting at first hearing, but it is a dangerous performance, as they say the ox's halter slips off from the beginning; if things go wrong, the end may be that I slip from the high tree and lose my composure or break the bones of my legs.'

Teruji said, 'Hey, before I have even climbed the pine tree, do not speak such useless things about falling or not falling. Has there ever been a flaw in any of my Keiriyaku until now?'

Colombo said, 'It is a funeral for the Heavenly Way, an empty burial.'

Teruji said, 'Eh, you are a fellow who speaks of ominous things again. I am about to climb a high tree, and you speak of an empty burial; you are a fellow who speaks of ill-omened things once more. Nevertheless, I know not when they shall return. Let us quickly begin the plan. I shall refrain today, for you have used a strange Kototama upon me. As a penalty, you shall take the role of the tree-climber. Use the Kototama of the Tengu well.'

Colombo said, 'Anii, you know well from always that my tree-climbing is clumsy. Without saying such things, as you are the superior, please perform the superior role as expected. I shall serve as the subordinate. If I, with a body as heavy as a millstone, were to climb the tree and slip, and go tumbling to the ground, that would indeed be a disaster. Come, come, one, two, three.'

§ 4

Terujī said, 'Alas, it cannot be helped. Under a brave general, there are weak soldiers. This, too, is because my own form is poor.'

So saying, like a monkey, he embraced the trunk of the great tree and climbed high into the branches.

Koronbo cried, 'Hey, Aniki, where are you?'

Terujī replied, 'You fool! If you speak in such a loud voice, it will be a grave matter if those two fellows approaching nearby hear you. Will you not be quiet for a moment?'

Koronbo said, 'But because you did not teach me the Sakusen Keikuwaku sufficiently, I am in a total state of Yamikumo as to what Haushin I should take.'

Terujī said, 'It is Ketsukō precisely because it is Yamikumo. Fortunately, under a sky of Yukigumo, the round moon is not visible, and as the surroundings are dimly dark, this Geitau can be performed. If you dally, we shall be Hatsukaku. Be silent now, in good Kagen.'

Koronbo said, 'Ah, how lonely it is. Look, a white hand is being extended and beckoning. How detestable it is... Hey, some loathsome fellow is extending a thin white hand and beckoning to me. Shall I try to climb up to Aniki's side somehow?'

Terujī said, 'Alas, there is no Shikata for such an Okubiyōmono, a fellow who lacks wit. Though you speak as if you were Zuibun strong and offer Ritsupa Chie when you are by my side, the moment you are Hitori, you immediately become terrified... If you keep dallying, they will return. That which appeared to be a white hand beckoning is but Kareobana. From ancient times, it is said: "Seeing the true form of a ghost, it is but Kareobana." Settle your soul in your Seika Tanden... It is a case of one against a thousand. It will be troublesome if you do not struggle valiantly.'

Koronbo said, 'Alas, it cannot be helped. Shall I hide myself in the thicket of trees?'

So saying, he hid his body within the Kareobana, trembling in fear. To this place, Onimaru and Tanimaru both advanced, respectfully carrying Tamateruhiko.

Onimaru said, 'Hey Tanimaru, was there not some strange voice just now? Could it be that the fellows of the Sangyō-kyō have detected our movements and come to usurp Lord Tamateruhiko?'

Tanimaru replied, 'It is so; it sounded like a human voice. I would not mind if one or Futari came, but if there are many, it is a bit troublesome. We must protect Lord Tamateruhiko, and if we do so, there is only Hitori to struggle against the enemy. Quietly... forgive me... shall we ask Lord Tamateruhiko to rest in this thicket, and consider the situation between the two of us?'

Onimaru said, 'Even so, it would be a dire matter should Tamateruhiko-sama cry out, "Hogyar, hogyar."'

Tanimaru replied, 'Tamateruhiko-sama, I offer my sincerest apologies, but I pray you rest here for a short while. I beseech you, by all means, do not raise your voice.'

With that, he respectfully spread a straw raincoat, covered it with a sedge hat, and placed broken tree branches upon it.

Tanimaru said, 'There, now it is secure. As the situation is urgent, we must flee for a time, but it would be troublesome if the two of us were to become scattered in this mountain; let us therefore determine a place to settle. ...It shall be before the Tenguiha at Sakahitauge. Do you understand?'

Onimaru replied, 'Yes, yes, I understand perfectly.'

The two men, while peering about their surroundings, advanced slowly with clenched fists toward the base of a great tree. Colombo, from within the grass, gazed up into the tree and emitted a strange sound:

Colombo cried, 'I... ma... jiya...'

Tanimaru said, 'Hah, what a strange sound that is. It is not a deer, nor an insect, nor the cry of a bird; it is a bizarre, uncanny sound, bearing a mournful tone of national ruin.'

Onimaru muttered, 'I... ya... ra... shii...'

Tanimaru said, 'Hey, Onimaru, even you are saying "I..."—what are you talking about? Pull yourself together. As long as I am with you, I possess the strength of a million men. Steady your resolve.'

Suddenly, from above the tree:

'Zaa, zaa, zaa, ootsu, you are surely the remnants of the Akugami of Ōeyama. You wicked scoundrels, who have trespassed upon the sacred mountain of Takakumayama and stolen Tamateruhiko-sama away, the Great Tengu of Takakumayama shall now pluck your heads from your shoulders, rend you from your crotches, and hang you upon the branches of a pine tree. If that cannot be accomplished, then set down the Tamateruhiko-sama you hold in your bosom beneath this tree, and depart from this place with all haste!'

Tanimaru said, 'What is this? To think there exists such a bizarre Tengu. A Tengu should know even of things a thousand leagues away. He dares to command me to take Tamateruhiko-sama from my bosom. This fellow must be a leaf-Tengu or a wild Tengu. ...Hey, you wild Tengu or leaf-Tengu in the tree, if you continue such foolish behavior, shall I, in turn, rend you asunder from your crotch?'

Onimaru said, 'Excuse me, Tanimaru-sama, you must not say such preposterous things. How could we possibly contend with a Tengu dwelling in such a great tree? ...Excuse me, Tengu-sama in the tree, my commander is a little intoxicated with sake, so I pray you, overlook this. It was the sake that spoke.'

§ 11

Tanimaru said, 'Teruji, you go ahead first. I shall follow slowly and search thereafter.'

Teruji replied, 'If you say that, and go ahead of me, do you not see that I shall not know the direction in which it has been dropped? Tanimaru, have no reserve. Come, go you ahead first; the final ichibeukan is the shiyōbu.'

Tanimaru laughed, 'Ah, ha, ha, ha, how cleverly you speak. If you do not move, I cannot move even for futsuka or mitsuka. You think to place me at the sentō to search for the arika... truly, the sendenshi of the Ural-kyō are zuru.'

Onimaru said, 'Oi, oi, Tanimaru, if you dally, the fellows of the Ananai-kyō will pass by, pick it up, and take it away. Come, let us go quickly.'

Tanimaru retorted, 'Eh, what a dull-witted fellow you are. While I am saying this and wasting time, you already know the bashiyo; why do you not naninani it secretly? You are a tonma yarau.'

Teruji said, 'Oi, Colombo, you must not leave the side of Onimaru-kun for even a moment. Grasp his sleeve firmly and follow him wherever he goes, it matters not. And then, you shall naninani the naninani. Is that understood?'

Colombo replied, 'I shiyōchi. But if Onimaru’s pace is swift, what shall I do?'

Teruji said, 'Whether by his obi or by his feet, you must cling to him. What a dull-witted fellow you are.'

Tanimaru said, 'Ah, now that it has come to this, it has become tautei impossible to dokusen it. If so, there is no shikata; let us proceed slowly, treating it as kiyōiubutsu.'

So saying, they began to walk slowly. Teruji, while gripping the sleeve of Tanimaru firmly, said,

Teruji said, 'Oi, Colombo, you must not let go of Onimaru.'

Tanimaru said, 'Eh, it is as if I have thrust my feet into a torimochioke. A tick has fastened itself upon me in such a terrible place... Ah, this too must be some form of meguri.'

With these words and a sullen face, the two pairs of four legs hastened down, pointing toward the foot of the Sakai Pass.
