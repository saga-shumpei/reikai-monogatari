Chapter 9: Body and Soul の Clean 化 [654]

身魂の浄化〔654〕

§ 1

The darkness of the heart clears, and in the world, the demons are like the pear tree; upon the crag of the mountain pass, two men sat with their Uchika, gazing at the sky of Yukigumo, and indulging in Zatsudan.

Arataka spoke: 'Ah, when I reflect upon the beginning of spring this Kotoshi, I became a Buka of Onikumawake, leading Amata Teshita into the Gankutsu of Mitakeyama, taking pride only in evil deeds, convinced that self-preservation was the Honriyō of Jinsei, and holding the Kaudō of Rikoshiyugi as my Kinkuwagiyokudeu. Yet, it seems Tendausama had not yet forsaken Wareware; we were saved by the Itsukau of Otohiko, Kamehiko, and Yoshikohime, and having Honzen attained enlightenment, we were permitted to enter the Ananai-kyō. We exerted our mental strength to launch a counter-attack upon the main castle of Onikumawake and to Kotomuke-yawasu him, but it appeared that the great commander Onikumawake had not yet been reached by the divine rope of salvation. He let our sincere Kototama warnings pass by like wind to a horse's ear, and in the end, he followed in the footsteps of Onikumahiko and fled to parts unknown. Even though we shared the rice from the same pot for but three days, as for us, we must save him to the path of truth wherever he may be; yet, his whereabouts are unknown, and since entering the Ananai-kyō, we have been unable to serve the Kami in any notable way, which is a source of distress. When we went to Chikubushima, Eikohime had already completed the divine work and, together with Susanoo-no-Ōkami, had returned to the residence in the Saien of Fusa-no-kuni. The members of the Ananai-kyō have scattered and parted, and in our present state, we are almost lost in our direction; at the very least, we must Kotomuke-yawasu Matsuhime of Takashiro-yama and achieve some merit... is that not so, Onitaka?'

Onitaka replied: 'Oh, that is so. Though the place is different, this is indeed Ōe-yama. That which is visible over there is certainly Takashiro-yama. Always, due to the evil spirits of the wicked Kami, black clouds had enshrouded the summit of the mountain, but today, what has come to pass? Are there not magnificent clouds trailing in a way unlike any other time? It may be that someone of the Ananai-kyō has conquered it, enshrined a splendid Kami, and the divine virtue is manifesting. If that should be the case, while it is indeed splendid, it is as if there is no longer any place for Wareware to act upon this Jitentō-tō. In any event, let us once survey Takashiro-yama to ascertain the truth, and if by any chance they have submitted to the Ananai-kyō, then there is no help for it. Shall we not press on, cross this sea, go to even the single island of Ryūgū, and perform one great deed?'

Arataka said, 'Oh, that is Jiyōfunbetsu. However, as our first undertaking, shall we not set out to explore Takashiroyama?'

Onitaka replied, 'Splendid clouds are trailing over Takashiroyama, but look there, toward the true west, are not purple clouds again hanging in layers as they do at the foot of Misenzan? Might it be that a male deity, who rivals Tamateruhime, has made his appearance? Nevertheless, in any case, let us head toward Takashiroyama, and next, let us investigate the source of those purple clouds. Come, let us go.'

So saying, they descended the steep, board-like slope and ran toward the west, and further west. The view was desolate, and the earth was covered in a single sheet of white cloth of thin snow. Leaving footprints like those of a Niō, they arrived at the foot of Takashiroyama, the village of Chiyokaha, and the side of Nakiishi.

Arataka said, 'On one side, upon a small, miniature-garden-like hill where the trees are dense, white snow rests upon the treetops as if the Unohana had bloomed, causing flowers to blossom out of season; and before us flows the Ōigawa, a stream of indescribable beauty. Since crossing the slope of Ōeda, I have never seen such a fine landscape. Let us rest here for a while, and then proceed slowly toward Takashiroyama.'

Onitaka said, 'Has not a strange voice begun to sound? There is no one who appears to be human, nor does there seem to be any beast. Footprints of foxes and tanuki remain upon the thin snow, yet it is neither the voice of a fox nor the voice of a human; it is a sound that I cannot comprehend. In any case, there is a large rock here. Everywhere else is covered in thin snow, but this rock alone has not a single flake of snow accumulated upon it, and furthermore, it is bone-dry. Fortunately, upon this rock—though I am no Yōryū Kannon—why do we not close our eyes, sit in meditation, and temper our hearts and spirits?'

Arataka nodded in agreement and sat down heavily upon the flat crag.

Arataka said, 'Hey brother, this rock is very warm. You too, sit here for a moment and see.'

Onitaka said, 'Indeed, it is a truly warm rock. While the entire surface of the earth is covered in falling cold snow, and the heavens and earth are holding up a mirror to show us that the human sentiments of this cold-hearted world are just like this, what can this mean? Only upon this rock, a mere tsubo in size, the cold-hearted snow does not accumulate, and it retains a warmth like that of spring. Even seeing this, it must be a divine hint that somewhere, a few warm-hearted humans still remain.'

§ 3

Suddenly, the flat rock at their Shitsuka began to Meidō, and Kokukoku, the Onkiyō increased in Kiyōdai and Mauretsu degrees. The Futari, startled, leaped down with Ashibaya, retreated Shichihachiken to Konata, and remained gazing at the Ganseki. Instantly, the Ganseki exhaled Hakuen. Following this, a purple cloud, thin and long, rose toward Ten as if a pole were set within the Hakuen, taking the form of a fern frond with a clenched fist, vanishing in the Chiukū at a distance of Nisanjitsuken, then appearing and vanishing again in the same manner; Ikukuwai, the purple Enchiyū continued Tachinobo, in a state of Seiseimetsumetsu. The Futari could only raise their voices in astonishment, crying, 'Ya, ya!' The fierce Daibakuon gradually became Teisei, and at last, ceased entirely. The Hakuen, however, remained Izen, rising vigorously.

At this moment, a goddess with a fair face and full cheeks appeared, wearing a golden crown, adorned all over her body with Yōraku made of Shiyujiyu Hōgiyoku, and clad in white, thin garments; the placement of her brows and eyes, the set of her nose, the crimson hue of her lips, and her teeth like snow, which she revealed slightly as she smiled, were all manifest as she appeared. Arataka said, 'Ya, was this the famed Nariishi of Kawase? Not knowing this, we have committed a thousandfold rudeness, trampling upon it with our defiled bodies; truly, there is no excuse for what we have done. Surely, the spirit of the Nariishi has appeared to deliver some stern oracle unto us. In any case, there is nothing to do but to offer our apologies.'

So saying, Arataka prostrated himself flat upon the earth where thin snow lay, expressing his contrition. Onitaka likewise lay prostrate upon the earth, trembling. Suddenly, music was heard in the void, and large petals, like those of a lotus, fell in showers. The surroundings were enveloped in an indescribable fragrance. Arataka, while keeping his head to the ground, tilted his neck slightly and, with one eye, peered fearfully at the goddess upon the rock. The goddess, attended by two beautiful children on her left and right, stood manifest within the aforementioned Hakuen, smiling, and extending her delicate hands, which were white with a slight tinge of peach, she beckoned toward the two persons at Konata.

Arataka said, 'Oi Onitaka, lift your head and behold that goddess. It seems she has some business with the two of us.' At this voice, Onitaka, though fearful, cast his eyes toward the goddess; the instant he did so, Onitaka cried, 'Ah!' and again pressed his head to the earth. Before they knew it, the two felt as if they were being dragged by some power, and they were safely arrived before the flat rock as before. The goddess, with grace, said:

Arataka-dono, Onitaka-dono, it has been a while.

At this voice, both men raised their heads at once, gazed intently upon the figure of the Megami, and scratched their heads with countenances of bewilderment.

The Megami caused the two Chigo to hold beautiful jewels from her bosom and signaled them with a glance.

The two Chigo advanced before the two men, pressed the small purple jewels against their foreheads, and struck them in with a tapping sound.

Before the two could even utter a cry of pain, the agony ceased.

The two Chigo instantly returned to the sides of the Megami and laughed as if in great delight.

From this moment, a sense of calm and a spring-like atmosphere drifted into the hearts of Arataka and Onitaka.

The Megami spoke quietly: From this moment forth, you shall no longer be Arataka and Onitaka; I bestow upon you the names Takayasuhiko and Takateruhiko.

I pray that henceforth you shall become true Shinjin and participate in the Shingefu.

Do you remember my face?

Arataka, at last in a state of relief, replied: To be bestowed the name Takayasuhiko is a grace for which I am deeply grateful, and an honor for my person.

Takateruhiko said: For a Mitama as clouded as mine, to be given the Onna of Takateruhiko leaves me with no words to express my gratitude.

If I may be so bold, are you not the deity of Tan-shu who resided with us in the rocky cavern of Mount Mitake?

The Megami smiled and nodded.

Takayasuhiko said: Ah, now the world has cleared for me.

I feel as though I have been fully granted the divine power to bring the Matsuhime of Mount Takagi to submission and harmony, to serve the Shingefu of the Great Deity of Mizu-no-Mitama, and to bring the Yamata-no-Orochi that coils about heaven and earth to submission and harmony.

I thank you.

Takateruhiko also remained silent, bowing his head to express his gratitude.

Takayasuhiko said: You have hitherto transformed your body into that of Tan-shu and endured various hardships to polish our Mitama; I pray you, reveal your name.

The Megami replied: Now is not the time to reveal my name.

Please polish your Mitama until you can perceive it naturally.

When my identity becomes clearly known to you, that shall be the time when your Mitama have become true Shinjin.

Until then, I shall reserve it as a pending matter for your sake.

No sooner had she spoken than the figures of the three pillars vanished like smoke.

The Naru-ishi continued to emit its small, low rumble as before.

Takayasuhiko said: What a mysterious event has occurred.

A mysterious Megami appeared before us and bestowed upon us divine names beyond our station, such as Takayasuhiko and Takateruhiko, yet I feel a new anxiety growing as to whether we shall truly be able to fulfill the responsibilities thereof.

Takateruhiko said, 'Indeed, I share that sentiment. Yet, that Megamisama resembled Tanshiu-sama in some indefinable way. You must have thought so as well, did you not?'

Takayasuhiko replied, 'Ah, I felt such profound awe that I could not gaze upon her countenance in reverence. My eyes were dazzled, and I could not face her. I felt a shame rising from the depths of my heart, as if my sins committed until now were being laid bare, and it was truly agonizing. Could this perhaps be but a dream?'

Takateruhiko said, 'Nay, it is no dream, but a true manifestation. Now that this has come to pass, we must be all the more circumspect in our words and deeds, and become noble Sendenshi, lest we have no excuse before the Megamisama we have just beheld. Yet, does this Nakiishi not continue to moan as before? We know not what manner of Kamisama may appear again. Would it not be well to remain here a while longer, offering Norito and waiting?'

Takayasuhiko replied, 'Ah, how could I endure the appearance of any more noble Kamisama? This is sufficient. I am so overcome with shame that I know not what to do. Come, let us hasten to Takashiroyama.'

The two bowed reverently to the Nakiishi and proceeded with quickened steps along the embankment of the great river. Suddenly, they collided with two men, and in their astonishment, the two cried out,

The two said, 'Ah, this is truly a grave discourtesy. We were walking with our heads bowed, hastening on our way, and not knowing that a Megamisama was passing, we have collided with you. We have no excuse. We pray you, forgive us.'

Takayasuhiko said, 'Ah, are you not Umakō and Shikakō? With such great haste, where do you intend to go?'

Umakō replied, 'Yes, our commanders, Murasakihime and Aohiko-sama, have committed a great failure and are suffering, finding themselves in a position where they can no longer remain in the sacred land. We two, finding it unbearable to watch their plight, stole away from the mansion and have come to Chikubushima in Gōshiu, hoping to meet Eikohime-sama and, through her compassion, have her intercede with Susanoo-Ōkami on behalf of the two. We have come in such haste that we have brought nothing with us. We beseech you, Tanshiu-sama, will you not lend us your aid? We implore you.'

Takayasuhiko replied, 'I am not Tanshiu-sama. I am Arataka, who was a great evildoer that went with you to the Kototama battle at Onigajō.'

Umakō replied, 'Pray, Tanshiu-sama, what are you saying? With your features clear and noble, and your countenance solemn and inviolable, though you are disguised in the form of a Megami, you are surely none other than the incomparable Tanshiu-sama. 馬公『モシモシ最前のお歌に依つて、私達もスツカリと信仰の妙味と効果が、心底から諒解出来ました。併し乍ら吾々両人は、紫姫様のお供を致し、比沼の真奈井の貴の宝座へ参拝の途中、あなた方に拐はかされ、其お蔭にて尊き三五教の信者となり、御主人の紫姫様は、世にも尊き宣伝使とまでお成り遊ばし、吾々両人は昼夜感涙に咽び……アーア吾々主従は何とした果報者だ……と喜んで居りましたが、計らずも紫姫様は若彦さまと共に、大神様の御不興を蒙り、少しの取違より、今は三五教を除名され、神様に対しては申訳なく、其罪万死に値すると言つて、日夜紫姫様のお歎き、家来の吾々両人、これがどうして見て居られませう。そこで吾々は紫姫様にお暇を願ひ、一つの功名を立て、大神様に誠を現はし、其功に依りて御主人の罪を赦して戴かうと思ひ、それとはなしにお願致しましたが、どうしても紫姫様は吾々に暇を下さらないので、血を吐くやうな思ひをして、心にも有らぬ主人に対し罵詈雑言を逞しうし、ヤツと勘当されて此処までやつて来ました。モウ斯うなる上は、仮令失敗を致さうとも、御主人の御身には何の関係も及ぼさないなり、万々一吾々が功名手柄を現はした時には御主人様に帰参をお願致し、さうして紫姫様の名誉を回復したいばつかりで、両人申合せ、何とか良い御用をして見たいと思つて、此処まで参りました。併し乍らあなたは既に高城山を言向け和さむと御決心なされた以上は、吾々はお供の身の上、仮令成功を致しましても、それが御主人様のお詫の材料にはなりませぬ。あなたは既に其れだけの御神徳をお頂きになつたのだから、此言霊戦はどうぞ、私に譲つて下さいますまいか』

§ 9

Shikakō said, 'It is as Umakō has just stated. I beseech you to discern our earnest kiyōchiu, and for this konkuwai, leave the matter unto us. Should we, manmanichi, meet with shitsupai, would you two, as the dainigun, undertake the tomurahiikusa?'

So saying, he let tears fall in torrents, revealing his magokoro upon his countenance as he made his entreaty.

Takayasuhiko replied, 'Since our nifushin, we have achieved no kōrau; we have striven to kotomu and reconcile the guwankiyō Matsuhime, that we might present her before the Kamisama, yet among bushi, it is ahimitagahi. Having heard such jijiyō, I cannot find cause to refuse you firmly. Now, Takateruhiko-sama, shall we yield the glory of this kototamasen to Umakō and Shikakō? Recalling the goshinchiyoku that one should bestow upon others what one desires for oneself, I cannot find it in my heart to reject you with muge.'

Takateruhiko said, 'It is as you have otsushiya. Umakō, Shikakō, though it be a labor, I pray you perform this with splendor. I shall observe your mushaburi from that place by the riverbank where the scenery is fair.'

Umakō and Shikakō replied, 'We thank you for your prompt consent. As our kototamasen is that of those whose mitama have yet to be polished, we humbly beseech your protection from the shadows.'

Takayasuhiko said, 'Pray, manifest a glorious tegara of merit.'

Takateruhiko said, 'I pray for your great victory.'

The two, Umakō and Shikakō, expressed their gratitude, parted from the two, and proceeded with haste, pointing their way toward the mansion of Matsuhime.
