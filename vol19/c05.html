Chapter 5: 零敗の苦 [650]

零敗の苦〔650〕

§ 1

As if sitting within the Ennetsu-Kuwabau, or dwelling in the Fuchiu, the air was of such Kokushiyo that a single Hikausen, parting the Kumoji, descended and landed safely in the Hironiha of the Honzan of the Uranai-Kyō in Kitayamamura of the land of Fusa.

Magahiko and Imoriwake, the two, were startled by this sound, and thinking it to be the Gokikuwan of Takahime, they rushed out to the front without Toriahe, only to behold Takahime, with eyes upturned and a Giyōsau that could not be described, emerging from the vessel accompanied by the two, Tsuru and Kame.

"Ah, Imoriwake-sama, it was a Taigi during my Rusuchiu; has there been anything unusual?"

"Yes, there has been no great change, but since Nisannichi Izen, a Kuōgiyoku, emitting a light like the Taihakusei, has frequently fallen before the Teizen at the hour of Yahan with a great Daionkiyō."

"That is a most auspicious sign; but what have you done with that jewel?"

Magahiko lowered his head with great respect and said, "From early every morning, I have searched with great care, yet nothing that could be called such a thing has fallen, nor has any trace remained."

"That is a strange matter; in any case, some excellent thing shall surely come to pass."

Imoriwake said, "Have you discovered the source of the purple clouds? You must have surely obtained a good result; as you are one who leaves nothing to chance, all in the mansion, doubting not a great success, have been waiting for your return with necks stretched long. Pray, enter the inner chambers quickly and tell us all the excellent tales of your journey."

Takahime remained silent.

Magahiko said, "Come, come, Imoriwake-sama, is it proper to inquire about the important divine administration of the Shinkai at the very entrance? It is only natural that Takahime-sama remains silent."

Imoriwake said, "Ah, that is so; Takahime-sama, come, let us guide you to the inner chambers."

Takahime entered the inner chambers, and all were suddenly in a flurry, busy to the utmost with preparations for a welcome.

Takahime said, "Since I have returned from afar today without falling, offer much Omiki before the divine altar, present various feasts, and hold a leisurely Naorai banquet; as I have returned with such great speed, I have a slight headache, so I shall go to the inner chambers and rest quietly for Nisannichi."

Hinodewake said, 'Ah, that may be so. Yet, if you retire to rest, I shall have no means to inquire of you; I pray you, speak but a word, even if it be but a small beginning.'

Takahime replied, 'The Oshigumi of Shinkai, the Himitsu, must remain Himitsu unto the end. For the present, by Goshinmei, I cannot speak of it... Here, Tsuru, Kame, you also must rest. Do not speak of various matters.'

Tsurukō said, 'I am not in the least Hirau. There is no Hitsueu for me to rest, so shall I, in your stead, leisurely take turns with Kame to deliver an Enzetsu upon the entire Daishitsupai... hum... the Tenmatsu of the whole affair?'

Takahime said, 'Now, now, Tsuru, Kame; is it not said that the crane lives for Sennen and the tortoise for Mannen? Upon the crane, I lay a Kankōrei for the duration of Sennen. Upon the tortoise, I lay a Kankōrei for the duration of Ichimannen...'

Tsurukō said, 'Pray, Takahime-sama, if a Kankōrei is laid upon me for Sennen, I shall be as one mute; I beseech you to grant a Torikeshi for that alone.'

Takahime said, 'Nay, I lay the Kankōrei only regarding this Kondo matter. As for other things, you are free. You must not speak only of the secret concerning the purple clouds. When the season arrives, I, Takahime, shall reveal all to everyone. Come, Tsuru, Kame, you have long attended me, and it must have been arduous. Go to a place where no one is present, and play at your leisure for two or three days; for if you chatter of various things, it will be troublesome. ...Hinodewake-sama, Magahiko-sama, I shall take my leave. Pray, drink sake at your leisure, live in harmony with all, and offer gratitude for the divine grace. I feel a headache, and I shall speak no more for a while.'

So saying, she slid open the sliding door, advanced into the inner chamber with a weary air, and locked it firmly from within.

Hinodewake said, 'Now, everyone, we shall henceforth conduct the festival, and upon its conclusion, we shall hold the Naorai banquet. Today, we shall drink our fill of sake. Yet, while it is well to drink sake, I pray you, take heed that you are not swallowed by the sake.'

Kō said, 'General Hinodewake, it is you who are always swallowed by the sake. Today, we pray for your utmost vigilance. Since Takahime-sama has a headache and has retired to rest, we must not raise our voices, lest we cause harm to her health.'

Eisabetsu said, 'It is a settled matter; do you think me one to be remiss in such affairs?'

The Saiten was concluded with the customary Genshiyuku, and the Ichidō proceeded into the Betsuden, where they abandoned themselves to the banquet of Nahorahi, and as the intoxication of the wine began to circulate, the Kensau reached its zenith.

A said, 'Hey, Tsurukō, it must have been Zuibun Yukuwai. I envy you. We, too, would wish to accompany you on such a journey.'

Tsurukō replied, 'To ride upon the Daihikausen, to look down upon the Shinrabanshiyō of the Chijiyō from Ganka, and to become a hero of Kūchiu Seifuku, crossing over to Onokorojima—it was truly Yuyukuwaikuwai, beyond what Hitsushi could ever exhaust.'

Tsurukō replied, 'Mochiron, there was no Tsuiraku on the Tochiu, and I reached the Mokutekichi in a Ritsupa manner.'

A said, 'That is a given, but what of the Kanjinkaname, the purple cloud? What became of that?'

Tsurukō replied, 'Regarding the purple cloud, a Kankōrei of Sennenkan has been proclaimed, so I pray you, do not ask of the purple. In its stead, I might report on the matter of Tamateruhime, should circumstances permit. However, until the wine circulates a little more, the Kototama will not function with dexterity; one must pour oil upon the pulley, as it were.'

Kamekō said, 'Hold, hold, Tsurukō; if you speak of the matter of the purple cloud, can it not be said that you are speaking of Tamateruhime? Will you not strictly observe the Kankōrei?'

Tsurukō replied, 'Nay, is it not sufficient that I refrain from speaking of the purple cloud? All of you, my companions, are witnesses; is that not so, Eisabetsu-sama, and did Takahi-hime-sama not say as much?'

Eisabetsu said, 'In any event, pray tell us of your success. All have been waiting in great anticipation.'

Tsurukō replied, 'Because you do not partake of wine, you are hopelessly earnest. You are a fellow without flexibility. Since such things could by no means be uttered from the lips of Takahi-hime-sama, her command that we should not speak was, in essence, a command to speak. It is not as if I require capital to speak with my own mouth, nor need I worry about paying national taxes... I shall exercise my own right of freedom.'

Kamekō said, 'Then you may do as you please. As for me, I shall maintain silence to the end...'
