Chapter 14: Voice の在所 [659]

声の在所〔659〕

§ 1

Tanimaru, Onimaru, Terujī, and Colombo, these four, leaving behind the Tenguiha of Sakahitauge, passed beyond the roots of the old pine tree at the Sanroku and arrived running at the place where the Euji of Tamateruhiko had been hidden. Tanimaru, with eyes wide, searched here and there, and the three, desiring to achieve the Kōmiyō of Wareichi, became Chimanako and, following in the wake of Tanimaru, began their Sōsaku. Suddenly, the crying voice of an Akago was heard; Tanimaru stood at a Tachido, folded his arms, and pondered from whence the crying voice might come.

Tanimaru said, 'I am certain that I had laid him down to sleep here; not only is there no Keiseki remaining, but though the Okoe is heard, I cannot discern the Haugaku at all. It seems to be heard in the east, and it seems to be in the west; when I think it is in the west, it is heard in the south, and when I think it is in the south, it seems to be heard in the north. What is this? That fox fellow must have seized Tamateruhiko-sama and is wandering Sokorajiu, leading us on a Uro. Hey, I shall search the east, so Onimaru, you Kisama, search the direction of the west. And you two, Terujī and Colombo, divide your Tewa and search the south and north. However, whoever finds him, it shall be Kiyōiu, so please keep that in mind.'

Terujī said, 'That agreement shall not be mistaken, shall it? Indeed, how interesting. Come, Colombo, you go to the south, and I shall try searching toward the north.'

Strangely, the crying voice of the Euji was heard most loudly to the ears of Tanimaru in the east. To Onimaru, it was heard in the direction of the west. To the ears of Colombo, it was heard in the south. To the ears of Terujī, it was heard certainly from the direction of the north.

The four, in the east, west, south, and north, rushed out in haste, seeking the voice. The fierce crying voice heard by the ears of the four resounded from their respective front, back, left, and right. The four, as if their ears were being pulled by that voice, spun their bodies in a whirl, and, dizzy, all four fell down. For a time, the voices of the four calling out and the sound of the wind were stilled, and a curtain of silence descended. The night gradually broke, and illuminated by the Omikage of the Amatsuhi rising as it pushed aside the clouds in the eastern sky, when each awoke at once, how could they have anticipated it, the four were sleeping, drenched, at the roots of the Tenguiha.

Tanimaru said, 'Ah, what is this, were we dreaming? As expected, because we were near the Tenguiha, those long-nosed fellows must have deceived us for a moment. Even so, where could the essential Tamateruhiko-sama have gone? Ah, he was here; grateful, grateful. Tamateruhiko-sama, please forgive us. We laid you, alone, to sleep upon such a rock, and we fell into a deep sleep, oblivious to all.'

§ 2

Saying this, he drew near and attempted to lift him, but the Zenshin of Tamateruhiko was Hieki, cold as ice.

Tanimaru said, 'Hey, Onimaru, Tamateruhiko-sama has become cold; what on earth should we do now?'

Onimaru replied, 'Is he not stone, just as we saw in the dream?'

Tanimaru said, 'Ah, indeed, this fellow was stone after all, just as in the dream.'

Teruji and Colombo spoke at once:

Teruji and Colombo said, 'Hahaha, truly, truly, there is no way to offer a Goaisatsu; now that it has come to this, no matter how much one weeps or regrets, there is no precedent for stone to speak. Please, perform the funeral rites with Teichiyō. Come, Colombo, we go to the place of the dream.'

They Kakeda. Tanimaru and Onimaru followed them and Kakeda.

Just as they were descending to the Nakahodo of the slope, they encountered a Hitori Bijin, dripping like water, coming up while carrying Tamateruhiko.

Teruji said, 'Excuse me, are you not Kototeruhime-sama?'

The Bijin replied, 'Yes, that is Sayō. I have protected the Kamisama Tamateruhiko and have come Kokomade.'

Teruji said, 'I say a strange thing, but Dōzo, as he is the Kamisama of the Ural Kyō, we shall treat him with Taisetsu; will you not give him to Wareware?'

Kototeruhime replied, 'Yes, since I must have someone receive him, if it is your desire, I shall do as you wish.'

At this juncture, Tanimaru and Onimaru came in pursuit.

Tanimaru and Onimaru said, 'Ah, was it Tamateruhiko-sama? We have been searching for him with great longing. Come, come, Dōzo, please come to Tanimaru. I shall carry you.'

Kototeruhime said, 'Are you not Tanimaru-sama? During my absence, you stole him from the rocky cave, and is that how you treat him with Taisetsu? You left him like an abandoned child, spreading a straw mat in such a thorny room, did you not? How could I entrust this noble Tamateruhiko-sama to you with peace of mind?'

Tanimaru said, 'No, I am truly sorry. No matter what I say, since Teruji of the Ural Kyō is aiming for him, I feared it would be a disaster if he were taken; not knowing it was among thorns, and because it was a hazy moonlit night, I made a mistake and laid him to sleep. Dōzo, please give him to me.'

Kototeruhime said, 'Being entreated by both sides in this manner, if I favor one, I cannot satisfy the other, and I am troubled as to how to act. Then, let us do this. Although Tamateruhiko-sama has not yet reached one hundred days since his birth, he speaks from time to time and even walks; therefore, Teruji of the Ural Kyō and Tanimaru of the Brahman Kyō, you two shall take hold of his hands and pull Tamateruhiko-sama between you. I shall give him to the one who wins the tug.'

§ 3

The Yonin spoke at once, 'If you so desire, it is Kōhei and Ketsukō.'

Kototeruhime set Tamateruhiko down in the Mannaka of the Sakamichi.

Tamateruhiko extended his hands to the Saiu, and said, 'Come, pull the hands of this boy; I shall go to the side of the one who wins, but pray, pull me gently.'

'Shiyōchi,' replied Tanimaru and Terujī, the Futari appearing on the Saiu, and bending their waists, they grasped the hands of the short-statured Tamateruhiko and pulled with all their might, saying, 'Come, Tamateruhiko-sama, come unto us.'

With Itsushiyōkenmei, they pulled until his arms were nearly wrenched from their sockets.

Tamateruhiko cried out, 'Ah, it hurts, it hurts, it is painful!' and, frowning, he began to weep.

Terujī, startled by this cry, let go of his hand involuntarily.

Tanimaru said, 'Come, he is now ours; Tamateruhiko-sama, though it be a Gokurau, pray become from Kefu the god of the Brahman Kyō.'

Tamateruhiko shook his head and said, 'No, no, I shall be under the Osewa of Terujī.'

Tanimaru replied, 'Is that not a violation of our Yakusoku?'

Tamateruhiko answered, 'Anata, you pulled me without regard even as I raised a Himei in pain; had Terujī not released me at that moment, my body would have been Chigi into two; I shall be under the Osewa of the deep-hearted Terujī.'

Tanimaru said, 'Do not speak of such Komutsu matters; would it not have been well to endure it for a little while? Moshimoshi, Kototeruhime-sama, pray, do you, as his birth Okaasama, speak to him well.'

When they turned to look, behold, how strange, the figure of Kototeruhime was already nowhere to be seen, neither shadow nor form remaining.

Tamateruhiko said, 'Since it has come to this, I shall cease going to either side; now that Matsuhime-sama of the Uranai Kyō has come to welcome me, I shall go with her.'

At this moment, there was a woman walking up the slope with a weary gait, and Tamateruhiko, looking joyful, said, 'Yea, are you Matsuhime? You have come well to welcome me; come, come, take me with you.'

Matsuhime replied, 'This is indeed Tamateruhiko-sama, I have come longing for you; come, I shall carry you upon my back,' and she thrust forth her back.

The Yonin, exchanging glances, surrounded Matsuhime from the front, back, and sides, and struck her with iron fists; seeing her fall while raising a Himei, they snatched away Tamateruhiko, and the Yonin hid their forms in the thicket of the forest.

§ 4

Matsuhime, having been struck and beaten by the Baukan, fell into a swoon upon the Sakamichi, yet as the Yufugure of that day drew near, she drew breath once more; and looking upon the Atari, she beheld two beautiful Megami standing before her in a state of Genzen.

The first Megami spoke: 'Thou art Matsuhime of Takashiroyama, art thou not? Come, follow us, and let us proceed to the Gankutsu of Takakumayama.'

Matsuhime replied: 'I know not which Kamisama ye may be, but I am beholden to you for your salvation. I was oppressed by the Warumono and fell into a state of Kizetsu, and I had been traveling in the far, far world beyond. Yet, as two Megamisama appeared and said, "Ho, Matsuhime, dost thou know where thou art? This is the Iriguchi of the Iukai. It is not yet time for thee to emerge into the Iukai; come, come, we shall send thee back," I regained my senses. When I look upon you, you are the very same Ofutakatasama whom I beheld in the Iukai, and by your grace, my Inochi has been spared.'

So saying, she pressed her hands together, lost in tears of Kanshiya.

The second Megami said: 'Come, Matsuhime, let us go to welcome Tamateruhiko of Takakumayama.'

Matsuhime replied: 'That Tamateruhiko was but a moment ago snatched away by the Warumono. Surely, he can no longer be at Takakumayama.'

The first Megami laughed, 'Ohohoho, the propagandists of the Ural and Brahman faiths came this morning, did they not? They, being entangled in the heart of greed and blinded in their sight, mistook a mere stone for Tamateruhiko and fled home in joy. Come, from this moment, compose thy spirit, advance alone into the Gankutsu, meet with Kototeruhime, and bring Tamateruhiko back with thee. I shall escort thee as far as Kurumado. Beyond that, the work is thine alone. For us, the Futahashira, to assist thee would be an easy matter, yet that would not redound to thy credit; therefore, go forth with a stout heart.'

Matsuhime replied: 'For all things, I offer my gratitude. I shall accept your kindness and be escorted as far as Kurumado. And pray, by what divine name are you called?'

The two Megami smiled, and said, 'The season shall come when thou shalt know. Here, we cannot yet speak of it.'

So saying, they took the lead and proceeded with swift steps toward the depths of the mountains. Matsuhime followed in the wake of the two Megami, walking forth with a heart full of eagerness.

The two Megami said: 'A few cho further lies the barrier of Kurumado. Here we shall part from thee. We shall surely meet again in time. Farewell.'

§ 5

Scarcely had these words been uttered when the forms of the Nijiyoshin vanished as if they had been suddenly extinguished. Matsuhime, feeling as a blind person who has lost their staff, or as one from whom a lantern has been snatched in the Yamiyo, dragged her heavy feet, borne upon the carriage of Kibau.

The sun had already reached Tasoga, and in that hour of darkness before the Jifushichiya moon had yet risen, she arrived at the Sekishiyo of the Kami of Kunadome. Here, a Genkaku Kuwanmon had been constructed.

Matsuhime said, 'Pardon me, I am one who journeys to Reizan. I beseech you, pray let me pass through this gate.'

One of the Monban, Kō, pushed open the Yokomon and stepped forth.

Kō said, 'I know not who you are, but one who asks to open this gate in such darkness is surely no person of good standing. As you are always being deceived by foxes and tanuki, and are accustomed to being a Baka, I shall not open it today, nor shall I permit your Tsūkuwa. Denaho and come again on the morning of Asu.'

Matsuhime said, 'That may be so, yet I am surely no suspicious person. I pray you, let me pass. I must hasten to the birthplace of Tamateruhiko-sama.'

Otsu, hearing this voice, said,

Otsu said, 'Hey, Kō-kō, in this darkness—ah, how loathsome—why do you dally with a woman dressed in such white garments? Will you not enter quickly? It is surely that same one again.'

Kō-kō said, 'Even so, this person pleads that she must by all means pay homage to Tamateruhiko-sama and asks to be allowed to pass; I cannot refuse her without mercy, can I?'

Otsu said, 'What is this? You again, catching a woman in the twilight and lingering in idle talk? You are a most self-willed fellow. Though you are wont to treat men who inquire with cold indifference, as if brushing their noses with wood, today you come forth with such gentle speech; your own face must surely be crumbling. It is fortunate that it is Yamiyo. If it were day, you would be a fine monster.'

Kō-kō said, 'If my face is that of a monster, what then is yours? With a countenance like a catfish scalded by boiling tea, what right have you to criticize the physiognomy of others?'

Otsu replied, 'So you have finally revealed your true colors, ha ha ha, you are truly spineless when it comes to women.'

Takekimi replied, 'No matter what you say, once a man has spoken his word that he shall not let one pass, he shall not let one pass.'

Katsukō said, 'Excuse me, Jiyochiu, as you have just heard, my Ahibōyaku is a Guwankomono of that sort; therefore, even if I were to force your passage, I know not what kind of Nandai he might cast upon us both later. Katsukō said, 'In truth, the Monban have not been this strict until now, but two or three days ago, a fellow from the Brahman Kyō, called Tani or Oni, came and deceived Kunadomenokami and us, and finally stole away the Taisetsu Tamateruhiko.
