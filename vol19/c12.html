Chapter 12: 言照姫 [657]

言照姫〔657〕

§ 1

Tatsuwaka, the Uketsukeyaku who also served as the guardian of the Omotemon of Matsuhimeyakata, was lost in deep Shian with his hands clasped when he gazed upon four figures creeping slowly through the small gate and was struck with astonishment. Two Sendenshi in the guise of goddesses vanished like smoke outside the gate.

Tatsuwaka, beholding the suspicious forms of the four, exclaimed:

"Ho, you who go there, are you not a bear and a tiger? Yea, Umakō and Shikakō, to pass upon this cold Chijiyō crawling on all fours, what manner of Wake is this?"

Kumahiko replied, "It is the Kengen of the Honshiyugojin of the bear and the tiger."

Tatsuwaka said, "Thou art a Zainin who hast Ikatsu and Ōda against Umakō and Shikakō, so this is but a Tauzen consequence, yet what has become of Umakō and Shikakō?"

Umakō replied, "Aye, the Honshiyugojin has appeared unto me also. I pray you, strike my backside and drive me in."

Tatsuwaka muttered, "How strange."

After a Shibashi of Shian, he himself also began to crawl on all fours and followed them; the forms of the Gonin crept deep into the Okufuka of the mansion. Within, Matsuhime and Osetsu were seated, with a paulownia charcoal brazier between them, engrossed in tales of divine grace. The five figures crawling on all fours appeared unexpectedly before the moss-covered garden, yet the two women were so absorbed in their conversation that they did not notice this strange and grotesque spectacle in the least. The five, having become beasts, were unable to utter human speech, and waited impatiently for the two to naturally cast their eyes upon them. Perhaps weary of waiting, Kumahiko, like a bear, let out a roar:

"Un, uun."

Following this, Torakō cried out with all his might:

"Uwaa, uwaaar."

Umakō neighed:

"Hin, hin, hin."

Shikakō cried:

"Kairō, kairō."

Tatsuwaka maintained his silence. Startled by these sounds, the two looked toward the garden and beheld the five men crawling on all fours; Matsuhime rose from her seat to flee, saying:

"Ah, how loathsome; what can this be, Osetsu-sama?"

Osetsu said, "Pray, Matsuhime-sama, there is no need to be so startled. It is nothing at all; it is Tatsuwaka-sama, Kumahiko and Torahiko-sama, and furthermore Umakō and Shikakō-sama of the Sangōkyō. Ho ho ho, how well it becomes them."

Matsuhime, with a look of relief, said:

"Come, come, Tatsuwaka, Kumahiko, Torahiko, let there be an end to this jest. Do you mock me because I am the mistress of this house? What a disgraceful sight. While serving the work of the Kami, to mimic filthy beasts—what manner of conduct is this? Have you no sense of propriety?"

§ 2

Torahiko: 'Waa, waa.'

Kumahiko: 'Oo, oo.'

Matsuhime: 'Ah, it has come to a troubled state. Have they all lost their minds? O Osetsu-sama, what shall we do?'

Osetsu: 'Indeed, it is a troubled matter; however much we may ask what to do, is there any Shikata?'

Osetsu: 'Hey, hey, Umakō, Shikakō, it is I, Osetsu. Is this not too much of a Goburei?'

Umakō: 'Hin, hin, hin.'

Shikakō: 'Kairo, kairo.'

Osetsu: 'Ah, that there may be no mutual resentment, both of you have become Ketsutai.'

Matsuhime: 'In such times, there is none other than Kamisama who can provide a Kaiketsu. Ah, how Kahaisau, have they fallen into the Chikushiyōdau while yet alive? I have heard of Jinmenjiushin, but it seems these Koitsu have become Jiutaijiushin as well.'

Matsuhime: 'It appears that in this world, there exist Jigoku, Gakidau, and Chikushiyōdau. Ah, how terrifying, how terrifying. Hey, hey, everyone, try to stand. Can you truly not stand at all?'

Matsuhime: 'Has the rank of Ningen already vanished? The character for rank is written as a standing person, but these are complete Yotsuashi. Ah, how Kahaisau, and this is because my, Matsuhime's, ego is strong.'

Matsuhime: 'Come, let us offer an apology to Kamisama. Osetsu-sama, Anata, please keep watch over these Gonin of men. I shall now receive some water and perform Gokinen with Itsushiyōkenmei.'

With a Matsusao face, she proceeds toward the room of the divine altar. The Gonin are shouting at the top of their voices, 'Oo, oo,' 'Waa,' 'Hin, hin,' and 'Kairo, kairo,' none yielding to the others.

After a while, Matsuhime appears at this place.

Matsuhime: 'Ah, Osetsu-sama, I have prayed with Itsushiyōkenmei, but have the people not yet been healed?'

Osetsu: 'Yes, just as before, they remain huddled and crawling in the thicket of trees in the garden. It seems that at last, only their groaning has ceased.'

Matsuhime: 'What shall I do? I have no Shikata; shall I, in atonement for my sins, go down on all fours and crawl?'

Osetsu: 'Heaven forbid, what are you saying? Born as a Ningen capable of standing and walking, if you despise the living shrine of Kamisama and imitate the Yotsuashi, you will be struck by divine punishment and become a true Yotsuashi, just like the Gonin-sama now.'

Matsuhime: 'Even if you say that, does my responsibility not remain unfulfilled? I do not care if I fall into the Chikushiyōdau; it is proper to share both suffering and joy.'

§ 3

Osetsu said, 'To be born as a splendid Ikimiya of Kami and yet commit such filthy acts is to insult the Honshiyugojin; the Honshiyugojin will lose all affection, depart from your Nikutai, and leave you with only the Fukushiyugojin. Should that come to pass, you shall inevitably fall into such an Asama state. Ningen bears a responsibility for their own Jishin before Kamisama. To hold one's own self in low regard is the same as holding the Ōkamisama in low regard; there is no sin of Manshin as profound as this. I beseech you, pray, refrain from such conduct.'

Matsuhime replied, 'Even if you say so, can I, as I am, stand by and witness this Sanjiyō?'

Osetsu answered, 'If it is the course of events, there is no Shikata. The overturning of the Zenshiya is a warning to the Kōshiya; you must, you must never imitate such things.'

With these words, she infused strength into her voice, and, changing from her usual manner, she spoke with a slightly stern Keshiki, as if to scold her. Matsuhime remained in Mokunen, hanging her head, and was lost in tears of Hitan.

Osetsu continued, 'For Ningen, conduct is of the utmost importance. If one mimics a stammerer, one naturally becomes a stammerer; if one mimics a mute, one naturally becomes a mute; if one mimics the deaf, one instantly becomes deaf; and if one mimics a cripple, one shall suffer the immediate divine punishment of becoming a cripple—this is the undeniable truth of Heaven and Earth. Furthermore, having been granted life as Ningen, is it ever permissible, regardless of the circumstances, to treat one's precious and Ketsukō Nikutai as if it were a four-legged beast? Look there, the five of them are gradually taking on the appearance of beasts, are they not? Moreover, because they are born as Ningen yet bear filthy names—calling them horses, deer, bears, tigers, or dragons—they instantly fall into degradation just as their names suggest. It is said that this is a land where Kototama brings fortune, but it is not only Kototama; it is a world where conduct brings both fortune and calamity. By all means, Ningen must purify their names, purify their hearts, and rectify their conduct. Ah, how pitiful; though I am inadequate, I shall attempt to rectify this through the power of Kototama. Then, the great merciful and compassionate Ōkamisama will surely grant forgiveness once more.'

Matsuhime said, 'I am truly astonished. I beseech you, pray, offer an apology to Kamisama on my behalf.'

Osetsu replied, 'I shall do so with reverence.'

Osetsu stood up, advanced before the altar, recited the Amatsu Norito, and upon finishing, appeared once again in this place.

Osetsu said, 'Hear me, Ryūwaka-sama, Kumahiko-sama, and Torahiko-sama; Kamisama has granted you forgiveness. Come, arise. One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, hundred, thousand, ten thousand.'

§ 4

Tatsuwaka suddenly rose to his feet.

Tatsuwaka said, 'Ah, I thank you.'

Next, the four, Kumahiko, Torahiko, Umakō, and Shikakō, rose to their feet once more.

'Oh, oh, Lady Osetsu, you have truly saved us. We have committed a grave error in our understanding. Henceforth, we shall never again commit such foolish acts.'

Matsuhime said, 'Now, you three, Tatsu, Kuma, and Tora, did you not trouble me by acting in such a foolish manner?'

Tatsuwaka replied, 'Nay, nay, heaven forbid. As I was standing guard, I saw the figures of Kumahiko and Torahiko crawling slowly through the small gate. Thinking this strange, I looked closely and saw Umakō and Shikakō, all four together, crawling on all fours and proceeding toward the inner chambers. I thought, "Ah, these fellows, Kuma and Tora, are crawling out of excessive humility to apologize for their previous rudeness. Furthermore, Umakō and Shikakō cannot bear to walk upright, and are crawling along to keep them company." Ah, moved by the sight of sincerity meeting sincerity and the upholding of duty, I, who bear great responsibility, could not bring myself to walk upright like a human, and out of sheer shame of heart, I followed them crawling on all fours. Having done so, within a few ken of walking, I became a true quadruped, unable to stand or even to speak. It is truly a terrifying thing. Tell me, Kumahiko, Torahiko, how was it for you?'

Kuma and Tora spoke at once, 'We felt as if we had truly become beasts, and we were worried that we might never be able to stand and walk again. Thanks to Lady Osetsu, we have been saved from the suffering of the path of beasts. We thank you.'

They were shedding tears of joy from the bottom of their hearts.

Osetsu said, 'Ah, that would have been a terrible thing. Henceforth, I pray you be prudent. Shikakō, Umakō, why did you even engage in such foolish mimicry? I am ashamed before the Ōkami.'

'Nay, we have no excuse. From now on, we shall surely take heed.'

Osetsu said, 'Umakō, Shikakō, did you not meet a magnificent Goddess on your way?'

Umakō replied, 'Yes, we met her.'

Shikakō replied, 'She escorted us to the gate. However, from that point on, her form vanished.'

Osetsu said, 'That is to be expected. Because you yourselves lowered your character and performed such foolish acts, even the compassionate Goddess was astonished and departed. Offer your apologies.'

§ 5

Umakō said, 'I thank you.'

Shikakō said, 'This time, truly, I have received practical education in various ways from Kamisama.'

Kumahiko said, 'I have inflicted upon Umakō and Shikakō insults that truly should not have been, and I have added physical violence. Yet, you two, who are so patient, did not offer even a whit of resistance, and instead, you offered us gratitude. We, of shallow Chie, thought you were Baka or Kichiga, and thus we Masumasu Giyakutai. Therefore, I am Hajii from the bottom of my heart; ah, my Seishin was that of a Yotsuashi, and how could I, as a Ningen, stand and walk upon the earth? In the excess of my Zange, I mimicked the Yotsuashi for the sake of Shiyazai.' So saying, he teared up.

Matsuhime said, 'Ah, was it so? If you have attained Kaishin to that extent, there is nothing more splendid than this. However, in the teachings of Kamisama, it is said that one must revere Kamisama, revere others, and revere one's own self. Please, think of the human body as a splendid shrine of Kamisama, and even if it is your own body, you must not treat it poorly. Had Osetsu-sama not stopped me, I too would have fallen into the path of beasts just as you have. Come, everyone, let us gather together and offer our thanks to Kamisama. In truth, orders had descended from the head temple in the land of Fusa from Takahime-sama and Kurohime-sama, and my heart had already submitted to the Sangyō; however, there was no reason for my subordinates to listen if I were to suddenly say such things, and I was troubled as to what I should do. Yet, Kamisama, without a single oversight, showed the model of Makoto and urged the Kaishin of everyone. Since some time ago, Osetsu-sama has come and, exhausting various words, showed us the way to return to the Sangyō, but I thought that if I submitted too easily, the sincerity of Osetsu-sama's heart, which thinks of her husband, would be difficult to manifest, so I intentionally said things that were not in my heart and held firm. Thus, it was only because I wished to give credit to the two, Umakō and Shikakō, who worry for the person of Murasakihime-sama and wish to save her, that I have held firm until now. That I have attained Kaishin from the bottom of my heart is, needless to say, due to the mercy of Ōkami-sama, and also due to the power of Osetsu-sama, and the power of the sincere heart of Umakō and Shikakō who think of their master. There is nothing in this world as splendid as Makoto. From this day forth, I intend to vacate this mansion, go for a while to pursue Shugyō, and polish my Mitama. Please, Osetsu-sama, together with Umakō and Shikakō, guard this mansion and preach the path of Makoto to the many believers. Just as you hold your husbands and masters dear, I too must serve my teachers, Takahime-sama and Kurohime-sama. I humbly ask for your favor. Ryu, Kuma, Tora, and all others, revere Osetsu-sama as my proxy—nay, as my teacher—and look up to Shikakō and Umakō as senior disciples, and work together in harmony for the sake of the path.'
