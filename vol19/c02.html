Chapter 2: The Beak of the Crossbill (Isuka no Hashi) [647]

鶍の嘴〔647〕

§ 1

In the Makutsu-ga-hara, where Natsukusa grows thick leaving no space for the tread of feet, and within the Ihaya where the Yamahototogisu cries in sorrow as if spitting blood, the three, Takahime, Takayamahiko, and Kurohime, sat in Teiza in an inner Hitoma, awaiting news of how Murasakihime and Aohiko fared. At that very moment, Umekō returned in haste, breathless, accompanied by the two, Tatsu and Tobi.

Torawaka said, 'Yo, is that not Umekō? Where have you been? Your complexion is not at all bright; it is unlike you, who are always Kuwaikuwatsu, to appear so pale and thin, and I cannot help but notice it.'

Umekō replied, 'Eh, it is nothing; this is no stage for you, so remain Otona and wait.'

Torawaka smiled with a grin.

Torawaka said, 'Hen, you have been bested, have you not? Shall I say a turtle has bitten your backside? It is a Teitaraku like having one's pot stolen on a moonlit night, or perhaps, if I am wrong again, it is an Anbaishiki where the Fukurodori has missed its Yashiyoku. Kurohime-sama is indeed fortunate to have such a Kerai, hi-hi-hi.'

Umekō said, 'Eh, do not speak so noisily. Move aside; if you stand in such a narrow entrance, I cannot even enter.'

Torawaka said, 'Ha-ha-ha, it would be better if you did not enter. Should I report the situation, or should a great earthquake erupt, this Ihaya would tremble. This Torawaka is of no use as I lack trust, but Murasakihime-sama, Aohiko-sama, and then you, Umekō, are truly formidable. Shall you let me hear of this recent failure—no, this tale of your great achievement? You always say that Kurohime’s eyes are black, and there can be no mistake, for you say that if she glares with these eyes, not a single thing will be amiss, a-ha-ha-ha.'

So saying, he thrust his chin forward, blocked the entrance, and laughed in a twisted manner. In the inner Hitoma, the three, Takahime, Takayamahiko, and Kurohime, were in the midst of a secret consultation.

Takahime said, 'Now, Kurohime-sama, has it not already been a week since Murasakihime and Aohiko departed? And even now, there is no Tayori, not even a word that a cat has sneezed or a festering nose has burst. They were not only chosen by your discernment but were selected and sent by you, so there should be no oversight, yet I cannot help but worry that if something were to happen, it would be a great calamity.'

Kurohime, with a somewhat anxious expression, replied,

'Because I sent them on such an abrupt negotiation, they are likely spending their days and nights in endless deliberation, as if holding a family council and wasting time in an Odawara-hyōjō, with problems arising one after another. That which is quickly formed is easily broken, and that which is delayed is difficult to break; as they say, great vessels are long in the making, and the more time it takes, the more promise it holds. Plants that grow straight up in a year and bloom will wither when autumn comes. When it comes to tall trees like the plum, cherry, peach, and camellia, they do not bloom in two or three years, but in exchange, their trunks grow to pierce the heavens, and they bloom every single year. As for Murasakihime and Aohiko, who met my discernment, there is no possibility that they would turn against us, is there not, Takayamahiko-sama?'

黒姫は稍不安の面持にて、

§ 2

Takayamahiko said, 'Come now, I cannot offer any guarantee for this.'

Kurohime, with horns of wrath rising in her eyes, replied, 'Oh, what are you saying, Takayamahiko? You must not underestimate Murasakihime and Aohiko so much. Your Mitama once dwelt upon Mount Kijiyōzan and taught evil things to Kotsunehime; this time, in the quest for the treasure of Nankauzan, you performed a great Daishitsupai because of Michihiko; and now, being a Mitama that has returned to the Uranai-Kyō, such thoughts arise within you. To measure the hearts of Aohiko and Murasakihime using your own heart as the Heujiyun is somewhat Zankoku.'

Takayamahiko raised his voice slightly and said, 'The past is the past, and the present is the present; as I have accumulated Keiken in my Mitama, I understand the depths of most people's hearts well. I am always Otona and adopt the Fugenjitsukau-Shiyugi, yet you always step forward and stir everything up, and if I say one Hitokoto, you immediately raise your eyebrows in a Sakada, breathe with a rough Hanaiki, and foam at the mouth. I am always silent because you are annoying. Today, fortunately, as we are before Takahime-sama, I have spoken my thoughts without reserve.'

Kurohime replied, 'What are you saying? Are you not the master of this house? Are you a Mitama so obedient as to listen to what I say? You are a fated Mitama who must find fault with everything, one by one.'

Takayamahiko said, 'The dispatching of Aohiko and Murasakihime this time was your proposal, was it not? At that time, I told you that it was dangerous and suggested that you secretly send Torawaka with them. At that time, you shook your head and were in a terrible rage; I thought, "Ah, your usual illness has appeared again," and endured it. These fellows will surely meet with failure; nay, not only failure, but Aohiko and Murasakihime must have been spies for the Sangokyō.'

Kurohime said, 'What are you saying? Just wait and see; it will surely be understood soon. Aohiko will return bringing Tamateruhime with him. If he does not bring her back, I shall never see you or Takahime-sama again.'

So saying, she thrust out her chin, clenched her upper and lower teeth firmly, and thrust them forward. Takayamahiko, feeling sullen, clenched his fist like a conch shell and attempted to strike Kurohime on the side of her face. Seeing that a great crisis was at hand, Takahime stepped in between them and said, 'Wait, wait! Is there not a saying that even dogs will not eat the quarrels of others? Have some sense. Are you two not the pillars and important figures of the Uranai-Kyō? Can you provide a lesson to everyone with such behavior?'

§ 3

Kurohime: 'Yes, yes, that is so; for, in any case, my husband is a Hyottoko.'

Takayamahiko: 'Look here, Kuro, what is this about a Hyottoko? If I am a Hyottoko, then you are a Bettoko.'

Takahime: 'Come, come, both of you, speak with rectitude, speak with rectitude; will you not be mindful of the Kototama?'

At this juncture, with Torawaka in the lead, the three, Ume, Tatsu, and Tobi, appeared.

Torawaka: 'Kurohime, the three, having kept their mission from me, have returned without any great vigor; I pray you, inquire of them in detail.'

Kurohime: 'You, Umekō, Tatsukō, Tobikō, how did the matter conclude? You dealt with Murasakihime and Aohiko well, did you not?'

Umekō: 'Indeed, indeed, they are truly Aohiko and Murasakihime; they performed a clever feat, and though it is not for us three, they played the victor and departed toward such and such a place, just as Tobi and Tatsu did.'

Kurohime: 'Ah, is that so, is that so? Then you have labored well. Now, quickly perform the cleaning of Tamateruhime’s chamber, and make preparations for the meals and sake of everyone.'

Umekō: 'Yes, though I do not see the necessity for it at all.'

Kurohime: 'Now, Umekō, what is this you are saying? Why do you not listen to what I say, arguing whether you acknowledge the necessity or not? Come, come, Tatsukō, Tobikō, you also have labored well. I pray you, tell us in detail before Takahime of the glorious achievements of Aohiko and Murasakihime.'

Tobikō: 'Ah, it is such an overwhelming matter that I cannot even speak of it.'

Tatsukō: 'Whatever one may say, it is like the iris on the sixth day or the chrysanthemum on the tenth; I have returned having received the convenience of the Gods, whatever that may be.'

Torawaka: 'Ha, ha, ha, this fellow is truly weakened. To speak of "convenience" is the fitting excuse of a coward. I say, Kurohime, this one surely accomplished nothing; it appears as if he has merely returned after eating the octopus trap.'

Kurohime: 'Now, Torawaka, who asked you for your opinion? You are a man who went to Mount Misen and returned having failed, so you have no qualification to speak of this matter one way or the other; step down one level, go to the garden, and clean that place. Come, come, Umekō, speak quickly.'

Umekō, while stroking his head with his left hand some three times, said,

Umekō: 'Yes, I followed and watched the rear of Murasakihime, Aohiko, and their party. Then, the truly remarkable Aohiko-sama brought forth both Ayahiko and Otami, and caused the old man Toyohiko to exclaim in surprise, "Oh, were you Ayahiko? Or were you Otami? Oh, are you my father? My mother? My younger sister? My elder brother?" and a scene of tragicomedy unfolded. Thereupon, Murasakihime, appearing as a goddess of peace, opened her small mouth and said, "Everything is the work of the Gods; now that the young couple has returned in this manner, must you not, Toyohiko-sama, offer Otama-sama and Tamateruhime to the Gods in return for their divine grace?" When she negotiated with such fluent eloquence, the old man Toyohiko, whether in joy or not, shook his head wildly and incessantly, nodding until I thought it might fall off, and in the end, he sat down heavily on his heels and his eyes grew dizzy. Well, the old man said, "Ah, this is a wonderful thing; they say that in times of great joy one dances and leaps, not knowing where one’s hands and feet are, but I am so happy that I am dizzy, the house is dizzy, and I do not know where my body is," and he rejoiced exceedingly. I have never seen or heard of such great joy since I was born.'

§ 4

Kurohime said, 'Ah, it is so, it is so, you must be rejoicing; tell me, Takayama-sama, how is this, do you still have complaints? Takahime-sama, with this, it is my desire to have you return to Honzan with a proud countenance, ho ho ho. Come, Umekō, speak of what follows; I feel as though I cannot wait even for a twinkling of an eye.'

Umekō replied, 'Well, from here on it is a matter of Jikan; perhaps it would be better not to speak, for the anticipation is the pleasure. Hey, Tobi, Tatsu, will you not say something as well?'

Tatsukō retorted, 'Hmph, it is useless to think that you can eat all the good parts and leave only the Kasu for others to consume; after you have made your Haukoku... come, come, speak of what follows, Jiyunjiyun and without exaggeration, so that you may receive the Medama.'

Umekō said, 'Ah, when one becomes a Taishiyō, the Sekinin is heavy; well, well, there is no Shikata, then I shall speak; Kurohime-sama, do not be Bitsukuri.'

Kurohime replied, 'Why should I be Bitsukuri? It is Takayama-sama who shall be Bitsukuri; there may be those who are Bitsukuri from excessive joy, and those who are Bitsukuri from being too Ahau and having no face to show.'

Umekō said, 'Ah, Murasakihime and Aohiko have taken Tamateruhime-sama and, with Iki Yōyō, have done such-and-such to us, and have done such-and-such at such-and-such a place.'

Kurohime said, 'Now, Umekō, how frustrating, will you not speak quickly? How long will you keep me in suspense?'

Umekō replied, 'No, no, I am by no means keeping you in suspense; I am informing you. Unknowingly, there is rudeness and offense; there is no curse from a god one does not know. Please, for today, let me be an exception to the account.'

Kurohime said, 'Are you not saying something strange? And when will Aohiko and his party return?'

Umekō replied, 'As to when that shall be, I cannot give you a definitive answer; this, too, is surely the power of time.'

Takahime said, 'Kurohime-sama, Aohiko and the others, including Murasakihime, have returned to Sangokyō.'

Umekō said, 'Well, well, as you have observed with your divine vision, Takahime-sama, it is truly a thousand pities; Aohiko, Murasakihime, and the others have all gone to Guren. By now, they are likely drinking celebratory sake at the foot of Yotsugi-Ōyama.'

So saying, he held his head and cowered in a small corner.

Kurohime said, 'Ah, that is not the Aohiko I know, and Murasakihime is likewise; to say they have gone to Sangokyō, they have surely gone to release the deputy guardian deity; I am certain they will return.'

§ 5

Takahime said, 'Kurohime, it is now Dame. Lord Takayamahiko, you must also be Gomanzoku to have such a Ritsupa wife; I have Tōriu here for so long and caused Jiyama during this busy time. You have shown me a great Baka, ah, but this too must be some Gotsugafu. Sayō, I shall return.'

Takayamahiko said, 'Please, take me with you as well.'

Takahime said, 'Do as you please; it would be a virtue to protect Kurohime with Taisetsu. Sayō.'

So saying, she heeded not the Ōzei who sought to restrain her, and with Aosuji standing out upon her brow and in a terrible Keshiki, she rushed out to the front, and accompanied by the Futari, Tsuru and Kame, she fled Matsushigura from Makutsugahara, running toward the port of Yura.

Takayamahiko cried, 'This is Taihen!'

With a Nejihachimaki, his skirts tucked up in Shichibusanbu, and having applied oil to his long, thin compass, he sought to leap forth. Kurohime gripped his sleeve firmly and asked,

Kurohime said, 'Lord Takayama, with such a Ketsusau, where are you going?'

Takayamahiko replied, 'It is a settled matter. Not to mention the Kanjin Tamateruhime, even Aohiko and Murasakihime have been taken by the Sangokyō; how could I ever justify myself to Takahime? From here, I, this Takayamahiko, shall rush into the mansion of Princess Etsuko of Mount Yotsugi, and if I do not snatch Tamateruhime under my arm and return, I shall not be myself. If I dally, Takahime will board an airplane and return to the land of Fusa; before that happens, I must obtain Tamateruhime and offer my apologies. Do not Jiyama!'

With that, he kicked and shoved her aside, and ran out with all his might. Kurohime also cried out at the top of her voice, 'Oi! Oi!', and with her hair disheveled and her obi trailing, she pursued Takayamahiko, running as her feet would carry her.
