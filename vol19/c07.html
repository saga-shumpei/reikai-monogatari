Chapter 7: Ox 飲 Horse 食 [652]

牛飲馬食〔652〕

§ 1

Before the Great Ōmikami of the Izu no Mitama, where the Nigimitama is enshrined, at the Motoise of the light of Kami, there were three sons and two daughters, Mamehito, who knelt before the Ōmimahe, offered the Amatsunorito, and prayed with sweat streaming down, beseeching that the clouds and mists of their hearts be cleared away.

Wakahiko said, 'Listen, Umakō and Shikakō, though it be a labor for you, would you go and visit the Gankutsuyakata of Kurohime-sama in Makutsugahara? I shall remain to provide Gohogo for Tamateruhime-sama, and together with Murasakihime-sama and Otama-sama, I shall concentrate my Kiguwan for an Enman Kaiketsu within this Goshinden and await your return; I desire that you meet with Kurohime-sama and convey our Magokoro to her in detail.'

Umakō replied, 'Very well, if it has come to this, I shall cast all caution to the winds. I do not understand the reason for what Kamisama Otsushiya, but I shall go.'

Wakahiko said, 'Perhaps the wonder lies in that which is not understood; unless we go as far as we must go, our limited knowledge can never fathom the divine will. This time, you must humble yourselves completely, and no matter what Kurohime-sama may say, you must not offer a single word of contradiction.'

Shikakō said, 'In that case, Umakō, let us go for the sake of reconnaissance at least. I feel as though there is no spirit in this. However, what shall we do if Kurohime is not there?'

Wakahiko said, 'Should it happen that she has returned to the land of Fusa, there will surely be some representative left behind, so you need only negotiate with that representative.'

Shikakō said, 'What shall we do if there is no representative? What will happen if she is away?'

Wakahiko said, 'Nay, there is no need to inquire into such matters. You must act according to the circumstances as they arise.'

Both the horse and the deer replied at once, 'We have understood the details perfectly. Come, brother, at a gallop!'

As they were about to tuck up their skirts and leap forth, Wakahiko said, 'Wait, wait, you two, do you know the nature of your errand?'

Umakō said, 'Yes, I know it. We are to see whether Kurohime is present or not, are we not?'

Shikakō said, 'If Kurohime is not there, we are to see the representative. If there is no representative, we are to act according to the circumstances, and if there is any sake, we shall drink a cup, shall we not?'

Murasakihime laughed, 'Ho, ho, ho, ho.'

Wakahiko laughed, 'Ha, ha, ha, ha, what flustered fellows you are. If Kurohime-sama is not present, you two shall say: We have come to offer an apology on behalf of Murasakihime-sama and Wakahiko. Since we shall present Tamateruhime-sama, we pray that you will cast your past anger into the river; we beseech you to treat us with favor. You must say this and listen for her reply.'

Umakō said, 'I am not such an unperceptive Umakō as that. Ever since the arrival of the Jikishi of Kamehiko, the Sujigaki has been clearly understood. Is that not so, Shikakō?'

Shikakō replied, 'Indeed, indeed. Come, let us depart. You three, I pray you cherish and provide Gohogo for Tamateruhime-sama. Though I feel that to think of her being handed over to the hands of Kurohime even now makes the Sewa seem devoid of Shigahi, this too is Nariyuki. Resign yourselves to the fact that it is a matter of Innen; I beseech you to look after her well.'

Having said this, they cast the words aside and, following the Tanigaha, ran Matsushigura toward Makutsugahara along the Kiku Shōkei.

The scene shifts to the Gankutsu of Makutsugahara, where the Shiyujin is Rusu. There, Manabe, the Rusu Shidanchiyō, along with Ushi, Tora, Tatsu, Taka, Tobi, and seven or eight others, eat and sleep, wake and sleep, spending their days like Muguramochi dwelling in holes from morn till night. They go not forth for Senden, but bring out the stored sake and rice in abundance, and like white ants devouring a pillar, they display to the full the true nature of the insects within the lion's body.

Torawaka said, 'Hey there, General Umekō, it was about this time last year, was it not? When the sudden incident at Fukō-tōge became known to Kurohime-sama, were we not in a state of great trepidation?'

Umekō replied, 'One should not speak of things that have passed. That was but the prologue; the incident of Tamateruhime occurred, and that became the cause of failure, leading the stubborn couple Takayamahiko to surrender the castle to us and return to the Honzan of Fusa-no-kuni. Thanks to that, the thorn in our side is removed, and though it is not the Ural-kyō, we can continue our ox-drinking and horse-eating, saying, "Drink and make merry, for darkness lies but an inch ahead." After all, this too has emerged from the Hōsun of Umekō. Is not the plan of Umekō, foreseen from a year ago, a magnificent thing? It is a formidable skill to have laid the foundation to cast even Kurohime aside; say what you will, I am the elder brother of the song.'

Torawaka said, 'I pray you, set aside such boasting. In this world that craves the new, there are no buyers for moldy, year-old tales of pride. Be that as it may, it is those fellows of the Sangokyō who have seized the fisherman's profit. It is Kurohime who has been utterly defeated and played the fool. She displayed such trust in Murasakihime and Aohiko, treating them with special favor as if they were precious, but who could have foreseen it? Who could have expected it? Is that not a pathetic state? Such a one might yet be cast off by the Sangokyō, and like a ghost after the Bon festival, come wagging her tail to return, saying that the Uranai-kyō was the truth after all and that she has repented. Whatever may happen, we must not deal with her again.'

Umekō said, 'No matter how brazen Aohiko may be, he would not come to offer apologies so many times. I am convinced that such a thing is absolutely impossible; pray, drink at your leisure and make merry. The world is dark but a step ahead. Unless we consume and drink while we have the means, the remnants of the Brahman-kyō, if not the Ananai-kyō, may descend upon us and seize our stores. In any case, there is no loss in filling one's belly; though our numbers have dwindled, we have provisions and sake that have lasted two years. You must exert yourselves to keep your stomachs working, and every single day, you must each consume the portions of five or six men, or else you shall not be worthy before Kamisama.'

Thus he spoke, foolishly and intoxicated with sake, chirping his own arbitrary logic.

At this moment, there was a man at the threshold who peered through the stone door and called out, 'Oi, oi!'

Torawaka said, 'Oi, Takakō, Tobikō, is there not someone at the entrance calling out "oi, oi"? I know not who the fellow is, but whether he be friend or foe, it matters not; drag him in, feed him until he is full, let him drink his fill of sake, and set him to the task of being a consumer of grain. Go quickly and bring him in. From here on, it shall be a siege of sake, a siege of rice, and a siege of fine feasts.'

'Oi, understood,' replied the two, Tobi and Taka, as they came to the entrance of the cave, each holding a rice ball in one hand and a sake bottle in the other.

Bakō said, 'Excuse me, I am a horse. Would you not be so kind as to let me pass?'

Tobikō said, 'Hmm, whether you be a pack horse, a riding horse, a wooden horse, or a horse that follows another's lead, I know not; but come, drink this fine sake and eat a rice ball. By doing so, you shall manifest your sincerity.'

Bakō said, 'Oh, I could not possibly; how could I accept your sake? In truth, I have come to offer an apology. I beseech you, I beg for your favor.'

Tobikō said, 'Bah, how foul-smelling; is there anyone who apologizes to the face of a sake bottle? If you were to gulp down two or three shō and then apologize, it would be logical, but where is there anyone who apologizes to the face of a bottle?'

Bakō said, 'No, no, I am a subordinate of Aohiko, who opposed Kurohime-sama, and of Murasakihime. It is truly regrettable, and I have come to offer an apology to Kurohime-sama.'

Tobikō said, 'Hmm, that Kurohime? Thanks to you lot, she has been shriveled up. Long ago she withdrew to the main mountain in the land of Fusa; since then, we have been eating and drinking every single day, living in ease. We owe it all to Aohiko-sama. If you are his retainer, then it is all the better. Come, drink a cup in celebration.'

Tobikō said, 'Ah, you are Shikakō, are you not? Yes, very well, you can speak a little; you are a man of our party. Now, this is the feast of the Sake-torture, the Rice-torture, and the Botamochi-torture. It is the repayment for last year. Do not be timid, but drink like a cow and eat like a horse in a manly fashion. Since Kurohime is away, the headquarters of the Giuinbashiyoku-kuwai has been established with Umekō as the chairman. Depending on your performance, it is not impossible that I shall make you an executive, nor is it limited that I shall not recommend you as a special member.

Spring has come even to the rocky cavern, with flowers of sake.

Ha ha ha, now come in, come in!'

The two, Taka and Tobikō, forcibly pulled the hands of Uma and Shika and brought them before the multitude.

Umekō said, 'Ah, are you not of the Sangokyō faction?'

Umakō replied, 'We wish to join the Giuinbashiyoku-kuwai from today.'

Umekō said, 'Ah, the two of you. Since the founding of this society, you are the first to apply for membership outside of the founders. Ordinarily, we would determine the grade of a member after examining their manner of drinking and eating, but today, to express our celebration, I shall recommend you as special members. Therefore, to uphold the honor of special members, eat until your bellies burst, and drink sake until the heaven becomes the earth and the earth becomes the heaven. Is that understood?'

Umakō replied, 'For this special consideration...'

Shikakō replied, 'And to be ranked as special members, we are grateful. We shall partake to the very end.'

Umekō said, 'Ah, with this, our comrades have increased by two. This is far faster than Kurohime's recruitment of believers. Nay, it is what one calls achieving results.'

Torawaka said, 'Hey, everyone, shall we give three cheers for the chairman?'

All replied, 'Oh, that is well, that is well. Long live the Giuinbashiyoku-kuwai, long live the chairman, long live the two, Uma and Shika, long live all the members, wa ha ha ha!'

They laughed so boisterously that the rocky cavern seemed as if it would collapse. At this time, Tsurukō and Kamekō arrived outside the rocky cavern, accompanied by four or five attendants.

Tsurukō said, 'Look here, Kamekō, is there not a very lively sound coming from within?'

Kamekō replied, 'Oh, indeed. Perhaps there is someone inside with the power of the heavenly eye, and they are holding a welcome party for us to celebrate in advance.'

Tsurukō said, 'If there be one whose Tengan is so keen, why does he not come to welcome us?'

Kamekō replied, 'Perhaps he is so overjoyed that he has become intoxicated and forgotten. Yet, the spirit must surely have come to meet us. It is safest to interpret all things with Zeni.'

Tsurukō said, 'Yet it is somewhat Henteko. I wonder if they are performing Sentaku of the body and Kintama Shiwano while the demon is away. In any case, shall we not try to Dona at them?'

As they spoke, the two, Tobi and Taka, appeared at the Iriguchi, walking with Kauhomansan.

'Ha! Dare is it? Peering from the Iriguchi as if with Kena, there is no need for Enriyo. Come, drink your fill, eat your fill, and dance. Kefu, there have been two Nifukuwaishiya from the Ananai-kyō. Oh, they have brought Shichihachinin with them. The Riusei of the Giuinbashiyokukuwai is like Kiyokujitsushiyōten. To express our Shiyukui for this day, I shall recommend you as Tokubetsu members. Do not stand at the Iriguchi like beggars; slide in at once.'

Tsurukō said, 'Are you not Taka-kō and Tobi-kō? What is Ume-kō, the caretaker in the absence of Kurohime, doing? We are Tsuru and Kame, both dispatched from the Honzan of Fusa-no-kuni. Report to that fellow Ume-kō as quickly as possible.'

Hearing this voice, Taka and Tobi were instantly sobered, and trembling violently, they cried, 'Oh, this is Tsurukō and Kamekō, Taka-kō and Tobi-kō, Uma-kō and Shika-kō, Tsurukō and Kamekō, Taka-kō and Tobi-kō, Uma-kō and Shika-kō!'
